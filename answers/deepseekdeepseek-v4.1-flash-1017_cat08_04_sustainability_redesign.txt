# 1. System boundary and assumptions

**Boundary used:** order-fulfilment operational environmental impact from “Receive order” through “Close/END” for spare-part orders, including stock picking, emergency/planned production, CNC and heat-treatment energy, inspection and rework loops, packaging material and packaging rework, outbound route choice, partial shipments/backorders, and delivery exceptions. I use the logged attributes `energy_kwh`, `transport_kgco2e`, `material_scrap_kg`, `package_material_kg`, `rework_flag`, `expedite_flag`, `batch_size`, `route_mode`, and `promised_lead_time_hours` as the core evidence.

**Inside boundary:** distributor-controlled and contracted fulfilment activities represented in the event log, including the effects of batching, machine choice, inspection/rework, packaging-route compatibility, consolidation waits, air/ground/dedicated-van choice, and cancellations due to delay where they affect fulfilment impact.

**Outside boundary:** upstream embodied emissions of purchased stock parts, supplier manufacturing not represented by machine events, facility overhead not allocated by event attributes, end-of-life packaging/part disposal beyond logged scrap, customer-use emissions, and macro-level carrier network changes. These are material but not directly estimable from the supplied log.

**Assumptions and uncertainty:** batch energy can be monitored per batch and allocated to orders, but per-order allocation may be imprecise. The discovered model has low precision because emergency production and partial shipments combine in disruption scenarios, so decisions should be evaluated by segment/path and monitored with conformance checks rather than relying on a single “optimal” path. Service-contract and true Critical-downtime orders constrain emission reductions; I do not treat all air or dedicated-van use as unjustified. Rebound risks include forecast-error obsolescence, displaced expediting, and changed customer ordering behaviour.

- **Transport:** `transport_kgco2e/order` by `route_mode`, customer type, promised lead time, and available slack; share of air, dedicated van, ground, and consolidated truck; empty/return-load risk for dedicated vans; SP1 conformance rate.

# 3. Intervention options

1. **S1. Green shipping recommender using promised lead time, route reliability, customer segment, and slack**
   - **Target path/segment:** all shipments, especially Standard-stock Service-contract and non-critical expedites; directly addresses SP1 violations.
   - **Mechanism:** at package/ship decision, compare route options and select ground/consolidated/air according to predicted probability of meeting `promised_lead_time_hours` and service classification. Default to lower-emission route when probability is above the SP1 threshold; allow air only when slack is insufficient or Critical justification exists.
   - **Service/quality/resilience effect:** reduces transport emissions without automatic service loss if slack is validated. Quality should be unchanged. Keeps alternative-route choice for resilience; risk arises if route-reliability estimates are poor.
   - **Evidence to monitor:** route decision context, promised lead time, realized slack, route mode, late delivery, SP1 override rate, carrier disruption events, and alternative-route availability.

2. **S2. Dynamic batching for CNC and heat treatment with maximum waiting-time caps by segment**
   - **Target path/segment:** Made-to-order and heat-treatment paths, especially MTO batch wait -> CNC_A -> heat treatment.
   - **Mechanism:** form larger batches only when energy-per-item benefit is real and due-date slack allows, with caps by segment. Critical-downtime and short-promise Service-contract orders should have tight or zero waiting caps; Regular MTO can accept longer promise or capped wait.
   - **Service/quality/resilience effect:** lowers CNC/heat energy per item at batch sizes such as 12–20, but increases median batch wait from roughly 4h to 38h in the evidence if uncapped. Caps protect service and reduce late-delivery risk. Larger batches can worsen obsolescence.
   - **Evidence to monitor:** `batch_id`, `batch_size`, batch wait, heat-treatment wait, late delivery, 14-day non-shipment/obsolescence, energy per item, due-date slack, and emergency expedites caused by waiting.

3. **S3. Shift Made-to-order production from CNC_B to CNC_A when queue and due-date slack allow**
   - **Target path/segment:** MTO cases currently on CNC_B, especially those with inspection -> rework production loops.
   - **Mechanism:** CNC_B is faster but has higher rework rate (18% vs 9% on CNC_A). Use CNC_A where it reduces scrap and rework loops without consuming so much slack that delivery or downstream inspection is worsened.
   - **Service/quality/resilience effect:** likely reduces rework, scrap, and hidden material waste; may increase cycle time if CNC_A queues grow. Do not move Critical-downtime or very tight Service-contract orders if it risks promised lead time.
   - **Evidence to monitor:** `machine_group`, queue time, due-date slack, rework rate, scrap kg, inspection loops, cycle p90, late delivery, and downstream packaging returns.

4. **S4. Replace air with priority pick + ground for Standard-stock Service-contract when slack is sufficient**
   - **Target path/segment:** Standard-stock Service-contract air shipments, which are a known SP1 violation source.
   - **Mechanism:** prioritize picking and earlier carrier cutoff, then select ground/consolidated truck if the promised lead time can still be met with high probability. Retain air for genuinely short slack or disruption recovery.
   - **Service/quality/resilience effect:** materially reduces transport emissions because air is roughly 4–6x more emitting than ground per prompt evidence. Service should remain protected if SP1 threshold is enforced; quality unaffected. Need alternate ground/carrier route for R1.
   - **Evidence to monitor:** promised lead time, pick-release time, route, late delivery, SP1 conformance, air override reasons, and carrier-recovery cases.

5. **S5. Consolidate truck departures twice daily for non-urgent stock orders**
   - **Target path/segment:** non-urgent Standard-stock Regular orders and some Service-contract orders with sufficient slack.
   - **Mechanism:** hold eligible parcels to scheduled consolidated truck departures, reducing individual parcel movements. Evidence suggests consolidated trucks can reduce transport kgCO2e by about 35–55% versus individual ground parcels but require 12–24h consolidation wait.
   - **Service/quality/resilience effect:** reduces transport emissions and vehicle movements; increases cycle time by the consolidation wait. Only use when promised lead time and service class allow. Fewer departures can reduce flexibility, so keep an alternate carrier route for Service-contract resilience.
   - **Evidence to monitor:** consolidation eligibility, wait time, route mode, load/build rate, late delivery, SP1 conformance, and disruption-switch availability.

6. **S6. Improve packaging-route compatibility checks before shipment**
   - **Target path/segment:** all packaging choices, especially Made-to-order heavy parts, reinforced packaging, and packaging rework loops.
   - **Mechanism:** check part geometry, weight, `route_mode`, and Q1 requirements before packaging. Use reinforced packaging when required or when inspection certifies low damage risk; avoid incompatible packaging that triggers `Rework packaging`.
   - **Service/quality/resilience effect:** reduces packaging rework, damage-related returns, and material waste. Reinforced packaging doubles package material, so apply selectively. Quality improves; cycle time can fall by avoiding rework loops.
   - **Evidence to monitor:** package material kg, rework-packaging events, Q1 compliance, return/damage reasons, route-mode/package compatibility, and packaging material per shipped unit.

7. **S7. Add an inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations**
   - **Target path/segment:** MTO inspection paths, CNC_B-heavy combinations, and cases with repeated inspection -> rework production loops.
   - **Mechanism:** risk-based inspection before packaging, especially for known defect-prone machine/part combinations; reduce rushed inspections that miss defects and later cause packaging rework or returns.
   - **Service/quality/resilience effect:** may add modest inspection time and energy but reduces expensive rework loops, scrap, and late delivery from late defect discovery. Helps quality and reduces hidden material waste.
   - **Evidence to monitor:** inspection queue, rework flag, rework-production loops, scrap kg, first-pass yield by machine/part, and returns after packaging.

8. **S8. Replace some dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes**
   - **Target path/segment:** Critical-downtime Service-contract cases that are not true downtime-critical, and dedicated-van cases lacking explicit justification.
   - **Mechanism:** use shared emergency courier lanes with service-level guarantees for justified urgent orders; keep dedicated vans for true downtime where empty return or premium service is justified. Use the SP2 justification event and triage.
   - **Service/quality/resilience effect:** lowers transport emissions and empty running, but can slightly increase response time. It must not be applied to true downtime without a contracted service guarantee and alternate route.
   - **Evidence to monitor:** dedicated-van justification, criticality category, response time, late delivery, transport kgCO2e, carrier concentration, and fallback-route availability.

9. **S9. Split Critical-downtime into true downtime and precautionary urgent categories**
   - **Target path/segment:** Critical-downtime Service-contract; emergency production -> reinforced pack -> dedicated van path.
   - **Mechanism:** triage at order receipt/availability check. True downtime gets emergency production/dedicated or fastest justified route; precautionary urgent uses stock/ground/shared emergency if promised service allows.
   - **Service/quality/resilience effect:** reduces unnecessary emergency production, air, and dedicated vans while preserving true critical service. Risk is misclassification, so require documented justification and monitor outcomes.
   - **Evidence to monitor:** criticality category, justification event, route, emergency-production use, service outcome, SP2 compliance, and true-downtime late-delivery exceptions.

10. **S10. Introduce carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular Made-to-order customers**
    - **Target path/segment:** Made-to-order Regular customers, not Service-contract or true Critical-downtime.
    - **Mechanism:** offer slightly longer promised lead times when it enables dynamic batching, CNC_A use, and ground/consolidated transport. Align promise date with lower-impact production and route plans.
    - **Service/quality/resilience effect:** can reduce energy and transport emissions while decreasing rushed inspection/rework and expedites. Cycle time may rise but is communicated; late-delivery risk falls if promises are realistic. Customer acceptance and order-lost risk are key.
    - **Evidence to monitor:** offered versus accepted lead time, route, batch size, late delivery, cancellations/lost sales if available, rework, and obsolescence.

11. **S11. Reduce avoidable partial shipments by changing availability-check rules**
    - **Target path/segment:** Stock paths with partial shipment -> ground + backorder, which show high late delivery and second-shipment impact.
    - **Mechanism:** during availability check, combine line items or wait for complete shipment when customer segment and promised date allow; avoid second shipments unless service-critical. Preserve partial shipment for genuine critical/urgent exceptions.
    - **Service/quality/resilience effect:** reduces packaging and transport from multiple shipments and reduces customer contact; may delay first shipment. Risk is lower responsiveness, so apply only with slack and segment rules.
    - **Evidence to monitor:** partial-shipment rate, backorder wait, number of shipments per order, package material, transport kgCO2e, late delivery, cancellation, and customer segment.

12. **S12. Create a sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides**
    - **Target path/segment:** all policy-relevant route, dedicated-van, and packaging decisions.
    - **Mechanism:** require structured reason codes and approval for air, dedicated van, or Q1 packaging override. The log enables governance rather than hidden non-conformance.
    - **Service/quality/resilience effect:** not a direct emission reduction, but prevents unjustified exceptions from becoming normal. Poorly governed exceptions could legitimize higher-impact choices, so review them against service outcomes.
    - **Evidence to monitor:** exception reason, policy, segment, approver, route/package outcome, late delivery, rework/returns, and repeated root causes.

# 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching rebound:** Larger CNC/heat-treatment batches reduce energy per item but increase waiting and can push late deliveries, which may then trigger emergency production, air, or dedicated vans. The log already shows heat-treatment batch waiting has 1.7x higher late-delivery risk, and large forecasts can leave 6% of batch-produced items unshipped within 14 days.
2. **Consolidation rebound:** Consolidating non-urgent trucks lowers transport emissions, but if promise dates are not adjusted, missed service can displace emissions into expedited parcels or dedicated vans.
3. **Air-to-ground rebound:** Replacing air with ground for Standard-stock Service-contract orders can backfire if slack estimates are wrong; recovery air shipments may erase savings and damage service-contract trust.
4. **Packaging rebound:** Reinforced packaging reduces MTO damage returns, but applying it indiscriminately doubles package material and may increase weight/volume, raising transport emissions. Compatibility checks must distinguish Q1-required parts from low-risk parts.
5. **Inspection rebound:** Extra inspection can reduce rework and returns, but if it creates inspection_cell queues, it may cause late delivery and rush packaging or expediting that displaces environmental burden.
6. **CNC_B-to-CNC_A displacement:** Moving work to CNC_A reduces rework but may create a queue at a more efficient machine, delaying orders and causing air/expedite elsewhere.
7. **Critical-van rebound:** Replacing dedicated vans with shared emergency lanes can reduce empty running, but if true downtime is misclassified or the shared lane lacks resilience, an urgent failure may require more costly emergency transport later.
8. **Customer-behaviour rebound:** Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular MTO may reduce batching/transport impact, but some customers may respond by ordering earlier, holding larger inventories, or cancelling and sourcing elsewhere, shifting emissions outside the boundary.
9. **Partial-shipment rebound:** Combining shipments reduces transport and packaging, but delaying the first available item can increase backorders or cancellations; if customers reorder through a different supplier, emissions and service risk are displaced.
10. **Exception-log rebound:** A sustainability exception log improves transparency, but if overrides become easy, air and dedicated vans may be normalized. Exceptions need reason codes, approval, and outcome review rather than blanket tolerance.

# 6. Balanced recommendation

Adopt a **segmented, conformance-governed redesign package** rather than isolated “green” switches:

1. **Define service-criticality first:** implement S9 triage for Critical-downtime into true downtime versus precautionary urgent, and enforce S12 exception logging. This prevents emission reductions from damaging true service-critical orders while allowing lower-impact choices for precautionary urgency.
2. **For Standard-stock Service-contract:** implement S1 + S4 + S5. Use priority picking and ground/consolidated transport whenever promised lead time can be met with high probability under SP1; keep air for genuine short slack or disruption recovery. Consolidate only non-urgent stock with 12–24h slack. This directly attacks the 22% SP1 violation area and the high transport impact of air, but does not eliminate all air indiscriminately.
3. **For Made-to-order:** combine S2 + S3 + S6 + S7 + S10. Use dynamic batching with tight waiting caps by segment, shift work from CNC_B to CNC_A only when queue and due-date slack allow, require packaging-route compatibility and Q1 checks, and use risk-based inspection to reduce rework loops. For Regular MTO only, negotiate carbon-aware promise dates that permit batching and ground/consolidation. Do not use longer promise-date negotiation for Service-contract or true Critical orders.
4. **For Critical-downtime:** protect true downtime with fastest justified route and explicit SP2 justification. Use S8 shared emergency courier only for non-true-critical cases or where a contracted shared lane still preserves R1 alternate-route resilience. Keep dedicated vans where downtime risk genuinely outweighs transport emissions.
5. **Reduce hidden material waste:** prioritize S6 and S7 because rework and packaging loops generate scrap, returns, and extra transport. Reinforced packaging should be mandatory for MTO heavy parts under Q1 unless inspection certifies low damage risk, but should not be applied indiscriminately.
6. **Reduce avoidable partial shipments:** apply S11 selectively where combining shipments does not delay critical orders. Use it mainly for Standard-stock Regular or Service-contract orders with sufficient slack, preserving partial shipment for genuine urgency.

This package balances environmental impact, service, quality, and resilience because transport reductions are tied to promised lead-time slack, batching is capped by segment and due-date risk, quality interventions target rework/scrap rather than just energy, and critical service retains dedicated/emergency options with justification. It explicitly avoids choosing the lowest-emission route when service or resilience constraints are violated.

# 7. Validation and governance plan

**Validation design:** use a phased or stepped-wedge rollout by segment/path, with pre/post comparison against the 12,000-order baseline and path-level evidence. Because the discovered model has low precision for combined emergency/partial paths, analyze at segment level and drill into event paths rather than only global averages.

**Core validation measures:**
- Energy: `energy_kwh/order` by segment, machine, and batch band; energy per item for CNC_A/CNC_B and heat treatment.
- Transport: `transport_kgco2e/order` by route mode, segment, and slack; air share, dedicated-van share, consolidated-truck share, and empty/return risk for vans.
- Materials: `material_scrap_kg/order`, `package_material_kg/order`, packaging-rework rate, Q1 violations, and damage-related returns.
- Batching: batch size, batch wait, heat-treatment wait, obsolescence/non-shipment within 14 days, and late delivery after batch wait.
- Service: median and p90 cycle time, late delivery, cancellations due to delay, partial-shipment/backorder rate, and emergency-production rate by segment.
- Quality: rework flag, rework-production loops, first-pass yield by machine/part, and packaging/customer returns.
- Resilience: alternative-route availability for Service-contract customers, dedicated-van justification completeness, and carrier disruption recovery.

**Conformance monitoring:**
- **SP1:** for every shipment, record available slack, promised lead time, route reliability estimate, chosen route, and whether ground/consolidated could have met the promise with probability above 85%. Treat air use outside that condition as an exception. Monitor the Standard-stock Service-contract baseline violation rate and require an SP1 reason code where air is retained.
- **SP2:** require a dedicated-van justification event with criticality category, customer/service rationale, and alternate-route check. Monitor the current missing-justification pattern and block or escalate cases without justification.
- **Q1:** for Made-to-order parts over 8 kg, require either reinforced packaging or an inspection certification of low damage risk. Monitor violations and their link to rework/return loops.

**Segment-level service guardrails:** set non-inferiority thresholds with service owners for each segment: Standard-stock Regular, Standard-stock Service-contract, Made-to-order Regular, Made-to-order Service-contract, and Critical-downtime Service-contract. Track late delivery, p90 cycle time, cancellations, and emergency expedites. Critical and Service-contract segments should have tighter tolerances; if a route or batching change causes late delivery or rework to rise beyond tolerance, roll back or tighten caps.

**Exception governance:** use S12 as a controlled register, not a blanket approval. Require reason codes, approver, policy affected, segment, and outcome for every SP1/SP2/Q1 override. Review weekly for repeated root causes, e.g., carrier cutoffs, packaging incompatibility, forecast error, or inspection backlog. Feed findings back into route thresholds, batch caps, packaging rules, and carrier contracts.

**Closed-loop management:** use process-mining conformance checks, path-level dashboards, and control charts or before/after comparisons to verify that emission reductions are not achieved by displacing impact into expedites, rework, obsolescence, or cancellations. Update the redesign package only when both environmental indicators improve and service/quality/resilience guardrails remain within agreed tolerances.
