## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

### Facts (from materials)
- GPIP-4 is a board-approved policy effective 2026-01-01, scope all Northbridge entities.
- DE-AAC-PROC-17 is a controlled local SOP effective 2025-03-01, scope maintenance/production-support purchases at Aachen.
- ECD-2 is an incident directive signed by COO and Incident Commander, effective 2026-07-14 09:00–2026-07-18 18:00, scope “critical sterilization-line lots.”
- CFO email is an ordinary email, not in controlled repository.
- ERP outage 2026-07-14 08:30–13:00; ERP timestamps may lag real actions by up to 12 minutes.
- Chief Risk Officer was unavailable during the incident and did not ratify ECD-2.
- Incident Command members are not explicitly designated as budget owners.
- CFO owns Finance control framework and is on executive committee.
- COO can issue operational directives, but GPIP-4 reserves formal exceptions to CRO.
- Organization has treated signed incident directives as temporary policy overlays in the past, but not in cases involving new-supplier Compliance clearance.

### Assumptions (explicitly stated)
- A “written exception” under GPIP-4 A7 must be issued by the CRO and recorded in the policy repository; a jointly signed incident directive does not automatically qualify.
- “Ordinary workflow steps” in DE-AAC-PROC-17 B7 refers to local procedural steps not expressly suspended by ECD-2.
- “Critical sterilization-line lots” in ECD-2 is not defined; Operations’ interpretation (backup-line components) is plausible but not authoritative.
- The CFO’s email constitutes an interpretation of Finance controls but does not waive Compliance or segregation-of-duties controls unless explicitly stated.
- Post-hoc approvals do not cure missing pre-commitment approvals under GPIP-4 A6.
- The burden of proof lies on the actor to demonstrate compliance with all applicable controls at the time of each act.

### Decision Method
- Apply rules as they existed at the time of each act (not at case closure).
- Treat normative sources as dimension-specific: global policy (GPIP-4) governs integrity and thresholds; local SOP (DE-AAC-PROC-17) governs timing and site-specific rules; incident directive (ECD-2) governs emergency scope and overrides ordinary timing/supplier rules; CFO email governs Finance interpretation within its stated scope.
- Prefer contemporaneous evidence (telephone recordings, signed PDFs, access logs) over ERP timestamps when the latter are unreliable due to outage.
- Classify cases based on the strongest defensible interpretation; where ambiguity persists, classify as INDETERMINATE and state what evidence would resolve it.
- Do not treat emergency necessity as a blanket justification for all deviations; evaluate necessity against stated scope and thresholds.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

### Case P-501
**Alternative:** COMPLIANT under DE-B4 (emergency) and ECD-C4 (overrides timing).
**Premise change:** ECD-2’s scope includes any action taken to prevent imminent sterilizer stoppage, even if ECD-2’s effective window began after the commitment.
**Why weak:** ECD-2 effective window starts at 09:00; commitment at 08:50 predates it. DE-B4 allows Shift Lead to substitute Plant Manager but does not waive segregation of duties (GPIP-A1) or PO-before-commitment (DE-B1).

### Case P-503
**Alternative:** COMPLIANT under CFO-D1 (Finance approval not required) and ECD-C4 (overrides segregation).
**Premise change:** CFO’s authority to interpret Finance controls extends to waiving segregation of duties during ECD-2.
**Why weak:** CFO-D3 explicitly states that essential integrity controls remain in place; GPIP-A1 is an integrity control. No document grants CFO authority to waive A1.

### Case P-505
**Alternative:** NONCOMPLIANT (Finance approval after commitment).
**Premise change:** Finance approval must precede commitment regardless of ECD-2.
**Why weak:** CFO-D1 allows joint Incident Command authorization to suffice for operational approvals up to EUR 150k; Finance approval at 19:00 is after commitment but within the same day. However, GPIP-A2 requires Finance approval before commitment; CFO-D1 does not explicitly waive this.

### Case P-507
**Alternative:** NONCOMPLIANT (no Finance approval).
**Premise change:** CFO-D1 does not cover >EUR 100k; Finance approval is required by GPIP-A2.
**Why weak:** CFO-D1 applies to “operational approvals” up to EUR 150k; Finance approval is a Finance control, not an operational approval. GPIP-A2 is not waived by CFO-D1.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

### Policy Breach
- **P-501:** Segregation of duties (GPIP-A1) and PO-before-commitment (DE-B1) breached. Remediate by retraining on segregation and PO workflow.
- **P-503:** Segregation of duties (GPIP-A1) and Finance approval (GPIP-A2) breached. Remediate by reviewing Incident Command authorizer independence and Finance approval process.
- **P-504:** Unapproved supplier (DE-B3) and lack of Incident Command authorization. Remediate by restricting emergency purchases to defined scope and requiring Incident Command sign-off.
- **P-506:** PO-before-commitment (DE-B1) and Finance/Plant approvals (DE-B2) breached. Remediate by enforcing pre-commitment approvals and documenting extensions.

### Logging Defect
- **P-503:** No Finance or Compliance events recorded. Implement mandatory logging for all approvals with real-time timestamping.
- **P-507:** ERP authorization timestamp delayed; signed PDF queued during outage. Implement backup logging (e.g., email queue timestamps) and reconcile during outages.

### Late Evidence of Timely Act
- **P-502, P-505, P-508:** Verbal authorizations recorded late; ERP timestamps suspect. Require contemporaneous recording (e.g., voice memo timestamped by phone) and reconcile with ERP on recovery.

### Inability to Prove Compliance
- **P-503, P-506:** Missing Finance/Compliance approvals. Require pre-commitment approvals with evidence before commitment; post-hoc approvals are not curative.

### Monitoring Actions
- Audit all emergency purchases during ECD-2 for compliance with ECD-C3 (joint authorization within 4h) and CFO-D1/D2 (thresholds).
- Review Incident Command authorizer independence and training.
- Implement a real-time dashboard for emergency purchases with alerts for missing approvals.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Replacement Clause (draft):**

> **Emergency Procurement Exception Hierarchy**
> 1. **Scope of Emergency:** An emergency is an imminent safety harm or production stoppage expected within six hours, limited to critical sterilization-line components or backup-line components essential to prevent line stoppage within 24 hours.
> 2. **Authority to Act:** During an emergency declared by the Incident Commander, the Incident Command group may authorize purchases up to EUR 150,000 with joint signatures. For amounts above EUR 150,000, Legal clearance is required.
> 3. **Overrides:** Emergency authorization overrides ordinary purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding requirements but does not waive:
>    - Segregation of duties (requester/committer  final approver),
>    - Finance approval for amounts  EUR 50,000,
>    - Compliance clearance for amounts > EUR 100,000 or new suppliers.
> 4. **Formal Exception:** A written, time-limited exception from the Chief Risk Officer is required to waive any of the above controls. A jointly signed incident directive does not constitute a formal exception unless explicitly ratified by the CRO in writing.
> 5. **Evidence:** All emergency authorizations must be recorded within four hours of commitment, with contemporaneous evidence (e.g., voice memo, signed PDF) preserved. ERP timestamps are secondary to contemporaneous records.
> 6. **Safeguard:** No emergency purchase may normalize deviations; all post-incident purchases must return to standard controls within 48 hours of emergency expiry.

**Rationale:** Resolves authority (CRO vs. COO), scope (definition of critical), thresholds (EUR 150k), segregation of duties, new-supplier rules, retrospective records, and expiry. Adds safeguard against emergency normalization.
