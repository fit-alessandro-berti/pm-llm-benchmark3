## 1. What is observed

At the branch-month level, `automation=1` records system availability, beginning in April for Alpha and Beta and in July for Gamma; Delta and Epsilon remain unavailable through August. Availability is not case treatment. Realized automated-fast-path share rose gradually—from 4–6% before formal adoption to 47–49% in mature July–August observations for Alpha/Beta and 28.5% for Gamma—so implementation intensity and learning vary over time. The nonzero “automated” share before availability also indicates pilot exposure, legacy routing, or measurement contamination that the panel cannot distinguish.

Rollout was endogenous. Alpha and Beta were chosen for high volume and poor historical cycle times; Gamma followed a readiness pilot. The same periods include a centralized fraud desk, adopter-specific arrival increases, branch-specific FTE changes, a May product/case-mix shift, and unconstrained local work-practice changes. Thus adopter status, demand, capacity, routing, and complexity move together.

The outcomes are aggregate branch-month summaries rather than case-level records. Monthly medians and p90s do not reveal within-month distributions, waiting-versus-service decomposition, censoring, eligibility, overrides, or route-specific performance. Rework and escalation are likewise unstratified. CSAT is a respondent mean with 20–45% response rates but no response counts, preventing precision weighting and adjustment for survey selection. These are observations and measurement constraints only; they do not by themselves assign any performance change to automation.

| **Primary: cohort-specific group-time DiD/event study**                                           | Separate April cohort, July cohort, and relative months; estimate launch and mature-use effects without using already-treated branches as controls | For Alpha/Beta, Gamma while not yet treated plus Delta/Epsilon; for Gamma, Delta/Epsilon | Pre-treatment outcome levels and slopes, baseline arrivals/FTE, baseline complexity; calendar effects; contemporaneous load/mix only in separate sensitivity specifications | E1, E2, E4, E5 and short-run E7                             | Conditional parallel trends; no anticipation; stable outcome construction; no material cross-branch interference; rollout versions sufficiently comparable | Event-study leads, cohort-specific plots, leave-one-control-out estimates, placebo dates, alternative windows, comparison of total versus covariate-adjusted specifications | Only five branches, short pre/post support, endogenous timing, and shared fraud-desk spillovers                    |

The primary design fits the rollout structure better than a pooled two-way fixed-effects coefficient. It estimates cohort-by-calendar-time contrasts, uses Gamma only while it is not yet treated, never uses already-treated Alpha/Beta as controls for Gamma, and can display separate launch, ramp-up, and mature-use effects. That matters because fast-path use rises gradually and a single post indicator averages different treatment versions. It also avoids the negative-weighting and contamination problems that conventional staggered TWFE can produce when effects vary by cohort or event time.

The design remains fragile. Alpha/Beta have only three clean pre-months before April, Gamma has only two post-months, there are two never-treated branches, and the fraud desk creates plausible cross-branch interference. Baseline matching should therefore use levels and slopes, while contemporaneous arrivals, FTE, and complexity should be handled in paired sensitivity analyses: excluding them targets the total rollout package; including them estimates a more controlled contrast but may condition on treatment-induced variables. Event-study leads, leave-one-control-out estimates, placebo adoption dates, and raw case-level balance checks are essential. The strategy yields an assumption-dependent short-run effect, not a transportable universal percentage. Prospective randomization should dominate the decision for the next phase.

For Gamma, the immediate pre-window is May–June and the post-window is July–August. Each month receives equal weight. These are averages of monthly medians and p90s, not pooled case-level quantiles.

The raw experience is therefore not “cycle time fell everywhere.” Alpha improves at the median but not the tail; Beta improves neither raw duration measure in this window; Gamma improves both.

**Comparison with never-treated branches.** The following subtracts the contemporaneous equal-weight change in Delta and Epsilon from each adopter’s change. Negative values are favorable for duration and rework. A positive escalation contrast only means more referral, not necessarily worse quality.

| adopter | control calendar window          | median contrast | p90 contrast | rework contrast | escalation contrast | CSAT contrast |
| ------- | -------------------------------- | --------------: | -----------: | --------------: | ------------------: | ------------: |
| Alpha   | Delta/Epsilon, Jan–Mar → Jul–Aug |     **−6.94 h** |  **−7.78 h** |    **−1.15 pp** |        **+1.15 pp** |    **+0.125** |
| Beta    | Delta/Epsilon, Jan–Mar → Jul–Aug |     **−2.79 h** |  **−5.25 h** |    **−2.55 pp** |        **+0.65 pp** |    **+0.058** |

For context, Delta/Epsilon themselves deteriorated from January–March to July–August by 3.64 hours at the median, 14.72 hours at p90, and 2.45 points in rework, while CSAT fell 0.058. During Gamma’s May–June to July–August comparison, the same controls were already recovering: median fell 4.28 hours and p90 fell 7.95 hours. Thus Gamma’s raw improvement substantially overstates its relative improvement.

Using the broader not-yet-treated comparison for the April cohort—Gamma, Delta, and Epsilon through June—gives April–June relative contrasts of −4.70 and −3.70 median hours for Alpha and Beta, and −5.41 and −6.84 p90 hours. This is broadly consistent with the never-treated comparison but does not remove the identifying assumptions.

**Treatment-intensity analysis.** A descriptive branch-and-month fixed-effects association was estimated per 10-percentage-point increase in completed automated-fast-path share. The adjusted specification also includes arrivals/FTE and high-complexity share.

| outcome          | branch/month FE only, per +10 pp | plus load and complexity adjustment, per +10 pp |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------: | ----------------------------------------------: |
| `median_cycle_h` |                          −1.30 h |                                         −0.62 h |
| `p90_cycle_h`    |                          −1.84 h |                                         −1.01 h |
| `rework_pct`     |                         −0.53 pp |                                        −0.24 pp |
| `escalation_pct` |                         +0.25 pp |                                        +0.12 pp |
| `csat_1to5`      |                           +0.026 |                                          +0.010 |

These are dose associations, not dose effects. Actual completed fast-path share depends on eligibility, complexity, overrides, learning, and successful downstream completion. It is consequently a potentially post-treatment, outcome-related variable. End-to-end within the treated periods, p90 rises from April to August in Alpha and Beta despite higher use, but falls from July to August in Gamma.

**Pre-trends and regression to the mean.** January–March median slopes are −0.5 hour/month for Alpha and +0.5 for Beta; corresponding p90 slopes are −0.95 and +0.85. The three comparison branches range from −0.6 to +1.6 median hours/month, so three pre-points do not convincingly establish or refute parallel trends. Gamma’s January–June slopes—+2.12 median and +5.76 p90 hours/month—are close to Delta’s +2.08/+5.69 and Epsilon’s +1.82/+5.20, which is more encouraging. Nevertheless, Alpha/Beta were selected because of weak historical performance, and Gamma was activated directly after its worst May–June observations. Regression to the mean and common recovery from the May–June shock could therefore produce part of the apparent improvement.

**Sensitivity and bounded interpretation.** Across alternative transparent windows, the control-adjusted descriptive contrasts span approximately:

* median: **−2.8 to −6.9 hours**, about −6% to −14% of adopter baselines;
* p90: **−5.3 to −7.8 hours**;
* rework: **−1.15 to −2.55 percentage points**;
* escalation: **+0.65 to +1.15 points**;
* respondent CSAT: **+0.058 to +0.125**.

Arrival-weighting rather than equal-month weighting changes the controlled duration contrasts by at most about 0.34 hour, so weighting is not the main uncertainty. Confounding is. A conservative scenario range is therefore much wider: roughly −7 to +1 hour for the median and −10 to +10 hours for p90.

CSAT is even less bounded. With a respondent mean near 4 and response rates between 20% and 45%, assigning all nonrespondents the lowest or highest possible score produces population-mean bounds ranging approximately from `[1.6, 4.8]` to `[2.35, 4.55]`. Those bounds dwarf the observed changes.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

`expand with an embedded evaluation`

Make both Delta and Epsilon the next rollout sites, but activate only randomized eligible subsets initially. Stratify eligible low- and medium-complexity cases by branch, week, complexity class, and arrival-load band, then randomly assign team-week cells to immediate automated triage or delayed activation. Keep high-complexity and safety-sensitive cases on manual triage during the first phase. This produces within-branch contemporaneous controls and avoids again choosing the currently weakest branch as the sole treated unit. Analyze assignment as the primary intention-to-treat contrast; treat actual automated routing as noncompliance, not as the primary exposure.

Reconstruct at least 12 pre-rollout weeks from case-level logs and collect a four-week implementation/ramp period plus at least 12 evaluable post weeks before crossover. Record registration, triage start/end, system recommendation and version, eligibility, randomized assignment, actual route, override and reason, specialist/fraud referral, queue entry/start/end, rework loops, resolution, closure, staffing by skill and shift, WIP and queue length at each transition, and CSAT invitations, responses, counts, timing, and customer strata. Report route-specific and overall median and p90 cycle time, waiting/service decomposition, escalation, rework, throughput, unresolved backlog, and fairness by complexity and customer segment.

Monitor fraud-desk and specialist-queue waiting for treated cases, manual controls, Alpha/Beta/Gamma, and the organization overall. This detects displacement rather than genuine capacity creation. Pause a cell if its p90 rises by more than 10% or five hours versus its randomized control for two consecutive weeks, rework rises by more than 1 percentage point, serious misrouting occurs, or adjusted CSAT falls by more than 0.10; require human override throughout.

Scale after the evaluable period only if the randomized ITT shows a practically meaningful median improvement—at least three hours or 7%—while the upper uncertainty bound excludes more than three hours of p90 harm, rework is noninferior within 1 point, no material fraud-desk spillover appears, and no prespecified subgroup shows customer or quality harm. Otherwise revise routing thresholds, staffing, or escalation rules before another randomized phase.

    group = panel.loc[panel["branch"].eq(branch)].set_index("month_label")

    post = group.loc[post_months, CONTRAST_COLUMNS].mean()

## 10. Final claim ladder

* **Observed:** Formal availability was followed by gradually increasing automated-fast-path use, consistently higher escalation, and heterogeneous absolute changes in median and p90 performance.
* **Suggestive:** Relative to Delta/Epsilon over matched calendar windows, adopter changes were 2.8–6.9 hours more favorable at the median, 5.3–7.8 hours more favorable at p90, and 1.15–2.55 points more favorable for recorded rework, while escalation rose by 0.65–1.15 points.
* **Conditionally causal:** Under conditional parallel trends, stable treatment versions, valid measurement, no anticipation, and negligible shared-queue interference, short-run availability may improve cycle-time and rework outcomes relative to delayed rollout; those assumptions are not established by this panel.
* **Not supported:** A universal statement that “automation reduces cycle time by X%,” an individual-case treatment effect, guaranteed absolute p90 improvement, or a population-wide CSAT benefit.
