## 1. What is observed

We observe an eight-month branch-month panel (five branches, Jan–Aug 2026) with staggered, non-random automation. **Availability**: `automation=1` for Alpha/Beta from April, Gamma from July, and never for Delta/Epsilon. Availability is a facility-level switch, not a case-level treatment. **Actual use / intensity**: `automated_fast_path_pct` rises gradually after switch-on (Alpha 25→48, Beta 27→50, Gamma 25→32), so treatment is dose-varying and confounded with a learning curve; the binary flag overstates uniform exposure. **Selection**: adopters were chosen because they had high volume and poor historical cycle times (Alpha/Beta), i.e., deliberate targeting of weak units, not random assignment; Gamma passed a readiness pilot. **Co-occurring changes**: a March centralized fraud desk (unmeasured waiting), April+ routing that added ~10% arrivals to Alpha/Beta and ~5% to Gamma, FTE shocks (Alpha −2 in May, Beta +1 April, Gamma −1 June, Delta +1 June), and a May product change raising `high_complexity_pct` broadly. These are entangled with treatment timing. **Measurement limits**: `csat_1to5` is a survey mean with 20–45% response and no counts (selection + no weighting/uncertainty); medians and p90 are pre-aggregated, so within-branch distributions are hidden; fraud-desk delay is invisible; FTE is productive-equivalent, not headcount. Treatment, dose, demand, staffing, and case-mix all move together.

Interference: routing reallocates arrivals *between* branches and the shared fraud desk couples branches, so no branch is a clean control (SUTVA fails). Treatment-version: "automation" in April differs from "automation" in August because dose and learning differ; E1 and E2 are not interchangeable.

## 3. Identification strategy

**Primary — staggered-adoption event study with not-yet-treated controls (Callaway–Sant'Anna / Sun–Abraham style group-time ATT), estimated descriptively given 5 clusters.**

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Staggered event-study (group-time ATT) | cohort × relative-month (τ) with April cohort {Alpha,Beta}, July cohort {Gamma} | not-yet-treated (Gamma pre-July) + never-treated {Delta,Epsilon} | high_complexity_pct, fte, arrivals; branch & calendar-month FE | E1/E3/E4/E7 | parallel trends conditional on covariates; no anticipation; controls unaffected by treatment (no spillover) | plot pre-τ coefficients; placebo on Delta/Epsilon; leave-one-out | fraud-desk/routing spillover contaminates controls; too few clusters |
| Interrupted time series per branch (segmented) | level + slope break at own switch month | branch as own control (within-series) | seasonal/complexity covariate; deseasonalized via never-treated | E3/E4 within branch | stable pre-trend absent intervention; no coincident shock at break | pre-slope fit; compare break to Delta/Epsilon same month | May complexity spike & May FTE loss coincide with Alpha post-window |
| Synthetic control (weighted donor pool) | switch month | weighted combo of {Delta,Epsilon, pre-Gamma} | pre-period outcomes + high_complexity_pct | E3/E4 | donor pool unexposed; good pre-fit; convex counterfactual | pre-period RMSPE; in-space placebo | only 2–3 donors → unstable weights, wide inference |
| Dose model (intensity) | continuous `automated_fast_path_pct` | within adopters + controls | complexity, fte, arrivals, branch/month FE | E2 | dose exogenous conditional on covariates | correlate dose with complexity/FTE shocks | dose endogenous to mix/learning |

**Why the primary is preferable (150–250 words):** The design is fundamentally staggered (April vs July cohorts) with a never-treated stratum, which is exactly the setting where naïve two-way fixed effects is biased: already-treated Alpha/Beta would act as "controls" for Gamma, and with heterogeneous, time-varying dose effects the TWFE weights can be negative and even flip sign. A group-time ATT framework estimates each cohort's effect against clean not-yet-treated units at each relative time, then aggregates transparently, and it yields the event-time (τ) curve needed to separate the learning ramp from a level shift (E7). It also exposes pre-trends directly: I can inspect whether adopters were already diverging before switch-on, which matters because they were *selected on poor historical performance* (regression-to-the-mean risk). Critically, it lets me use Gamma as a not-yet-treated control for Alpha/Beta through June, and Delta/Epsilon throughout, giving a secular-trend benchmark. Its honest limitation—which I foreground rather than hide—is that with five branches inference is descriptive, not inferential: clustered standard errors are unreliable and I will report ranges, leave-one-out sensitivity, and placebos instead of p-values. ITS and synthetic control are retained as cross-checks because they rely on different assumptions (within-series stability; convex donor fit), so agreement across them is more persuasive than any single estimator here.

## 5. Quantitative evidence

**Windows (defined):** pre = all months before own switch; post = all months from switch onward. Adjacent-quarter and secular benchmarks added because a broad May complexity spike inflates any naïve pre/post.

**(1) Adopter pre/post contrasts (arithmetic, not causal):**

| branch | median pre→post | Δ% median | p90 pre→post | Δ% p90 | rework pre→post | escalation pre→post |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alpha | 48.9→48.5 | −0.8% | 98.5→108.1 | +9.8% | 13.2→13.7 | 8.5→11.4 |
| Beta | 45.9→47.8 | +4.1% | 92.9→102.7 | +10.6% | 12.8→12.2 | 8.2→10.7 |
| Gamma | 40.6→37.2 | −8.3% | 85.8→84.9 | −1.0% | 13.0→12.6 | 8.4→10.7 |

**(2) Never/not-yet-treated benchmark (secular trend):** Delta and Epsilon never adopt, yet both improve from the May–June complexity peak into August: Delta median 42.6(Jun)→39.0(Aug); Epsilon 49.4(Jun)→40.8(Aug); Epsilon p90 107.5→91.4 (−16). This June→August recovery is *untreated* and large. Gamma's July–August improvement (median −8.3% vs its own inflated pre) is therefore substantially attributable to the same secular rebound, not automation. Against a not-yet-treated benchmark, Gamma's "effect" shrinks toward zero.

**(3) Intensity analysis:** Regressing median on `automated_fast_path_pct` *within adopters, controlling for high_complexity_pct*: raw dose–median correlation is near zero/positive for Alpha/Beta (dose climbed while median stayed ~flat and complexity rose), and negative for Gamma but collinear with the secular rebound. Conclusion: no clean monotone dose–benefit signal on the median; dose is confounded by complexity and season.

**(4) Pre-trends & regression-to-the-mean:** Adopters were selected on *poor historical* cycle times (Alpha/Beta highest baselines). Selecting the worst units guarantees expected improvement by mean-reversion even with a null treatment. Pre-period slopes for Alpha/Beta are noisy/flat, so parallel-trends is not clearly satisfied; any post improvement is confounded with reversion + the secular rebound seen in controls.

**(5) Sensitivity / bounded conclusions (two outcomes):**
- **median_cycle_h:** Under "no secular adjustment," Gamma looks like −8%; after subtracting the never-treated June→August improvement (~−7% to −16%), the *automation-attributable* median effect brackets roughly **−3% to +2%** (i.e., indistinguishable from zero). Alpha/Beta bracket **−2% to +5%**.
- **p90_cycle_h:** Alpha/Beta show +10–11% post, but this window contains the May complexity spike and Alpha's FTE loss. Bounding by removing May–June, residual tail effect is **−2% to +8%**—consistent with *no improvement and plausible tail worsening*, not a reduction.

Arithmetic facts (the Δ% above) are separated from interpretation: the numbers are real; the causal reading is that they are largely explained by selection, mix, staffing, and secular rebound.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**Decision: `expand with an embedded evaluation`.**

The data neither support the requested "automation reduces cycle time by X%" statement nor justify halting a system that is operationally live and shows no clear customer harm. Given endogenous selection, a broad secular rebound in never-treated branches, entangled staffing/mix/routing shocks, and only five clusters, the honest position is that no defensible causal effect is currently identified—so expansion must be structured to *create* identification.

**Next subset:** Delta and Epsilon are the two never-treated branches and should be the evaluation cohort, but **do not switch both simultaneously**. Assign the *phase-in* by a pre-committed rule that breaks the selection-on-weakness pattern: randomize (or if leadership refuses randomization, use a matched, coin-flip-tie-broken assignment) which of Delta/Epsilon goes first, with the second following after a fixed lag. This turns staggered timing into a designed stepped-wedge rather than an endogenous accident, giving each unit a clean not-yet-treated control.

**Assignment / phase-in:** stepped-wedge — Delta at month T, Epsilon at T+2 (or reverse, by the randomized draw), holding routing weights fixed during the pre/post windows so demand shocks don't confound.

**Minimum measurement:** ≥3 clean pre-period months per branch (no concurrent product/routing change) and ≥4 post months to let the dose curve mature (adopters took ~4 months to reach ~50% fast-path).

**Process-mining / queue data to collect:** case-level timestamps to reconstruct real variant flows (not branch-month aggregates), per-stage waiting times **including the fraud desk**, specialist-queue length/utilization, and cycle-time deciles so median and tail are tracked separately.

**Spillover monitoring:** track fraud-desk load and cross-branch routing volumes weekly; hold routing constant or measure it as a covariate so Alpha/Beta contamination is quantified.

**Guardrails:** freeze/roll back if p90 rises >15% for two consecutive months, if escalation into the specialist queue causes measured wait growth, or if CSAT (with newly-required response counts) drops ≥0.15 with adequate response. **Scale-up rule:** expand system-wide only if the stepped-wedge shows a median or p90 reduction that survives adjustment for complexity/FTE/arrivals *and* exceeds the never-treated secular trend by a pre-registered margin (e.g., ≥5%) with leave-one-out robustness.

## 10. Final claim ladder

- `Observed:` Automation availability was staggered (Alpha/Beta April, Gamma July, Delta/Epsilon never), realized fast-path use ramped gradually to ~32–50%, and adopters' median cycle times were roughly flat while their p90 rose ~10% during the ramp.
- `Suggestive:` Once the untreated Jun→Aug secular rebound and the May complexity spike are accounted for, automation shows no clear net reduction in median cycle time and may transiently worsen tail latency during the learning ramp.
- `Conditionally causal:` If a designed stepped-wedge at Delta/Epsilon with fixed routing and fraud-desk timing were run, a modest median reduction could plausibly be attributed to automation—only under conditional parallel trends, no anticipation, and no cross-branch spillover.
- `Not supported:` The blanket statement "automation reduces cycle time by X%," any claimed CSAT improvement, and any escalation effect attributed to automation (escalation rose org-wide with the March fraud desk) are not defensible from these data.
