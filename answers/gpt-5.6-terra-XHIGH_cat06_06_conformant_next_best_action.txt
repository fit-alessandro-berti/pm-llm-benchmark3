## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

The decision should be lexicographic rather than a simple expected-value optimization:

1. **First, exclude actions that violate mandatory controls.**  
   Actions A and B do not become acceptable because their apparent deadline benefit is €20k–€35k. Their expected operational benefit cannot offset a known breach of a mandatory approval path, sanctions control, and, for B, the four-eyes rule.

2. **Then compare conformant alternatives using conditional ranges.**  
   The current deadline value is €20k–€35k, but it is realized only if there is both:
   - a valid approval; and
   - execution before the deadline.

   If the observed 0.55–0.75 ultimate-approval prediction were transportable and timing were guaranteed, it would imply an illustrative gross benefit of roughly:

   \[
   €20k \times 0.55 = €11k
   \quad \text{to} \quad
   €35k \times 0.75 = €26.25k
   \]

   That is not a full expected value because the probability of completing the intervention within 90 minutes is unknown and may correlate with decision outcome.

3. **Do not treat the 0.46 fuzzy-match confidence as a probability of unlawful execution.**  
   It is match confidence, not a calibrated probability of sanctions exposure. Nevertheless, the process explicitly says that any unresolved fuzzy match requires second-officer review before approval or execution. The unresolved status matters more than the numerical confidence for conformance.

4. **Regulatory harm is asymmetric and partly non-monetary.**  
   A false clear could plausibly generate €100k–€500k+ in direct regulatory/compliance harm, but this understates possible licence, enforcement, correspondent-banking, reputational, and remediation effects. Without a defensible probability of unlawful execution, assigning a precise expected compliance loss would create false precision. The appropriate dominance argument is that a valid second-review route exists and can plausibly still meet the deadline; therefore knowingly bypassing it is not admissible.

5. **Customer harm and deadline harm should not be mechanically added.**  
   The €15k–€80k false-rejection/prolonged-hold range and the €20k–€35k deadline-loss range may overlap. A late or rejected payment could cause both, but the problem does not establish whether they are independent losses. They should be tracked separately and only combined after confirming the cost definitions.

6. **Rank 1 dominates rank 2 on case information if it does not delay the review.**  
   The alternate screen takes 15–30 minutes while the S2 review takes 35–75 minutes. If S2 can begin reviewing the validated evidence immediately and waits only to make the final sign-off until the new screen result arrives, screening is not necessarily on the critical path. Its incremental value is uncertainty reduction, not substitution for the mandatory second review.

7. **Observed predictions are not intervention guarantees.**  
   The 0.48–0.64 SLA-breach prediction describes “continue normal processing,” not “interrupt P-901, allocate S2, use a scarce API call, and prioritize P-884.” Likewise, prioritization may alter reviewer quality, queues, and case mix. The ranking therefore favors robust process-valid options over fine-grained expected-value claims.

## 5. Resource spillovers

**S2 versus S4**

- **S2 now:** the stated review duration is 35–75 minutes, plus 5–15 minutes for recording and execution. This creates a roughly t+40–90 execution window. It is the only currently available route that plausibly preserves P-884’s deadline across much of the stated range.
- **Cost of S2:** P-901 loses approximately 45 minutes of attention, raising its breach probability by 0.12–0.28. Because no monetary or compliance impact for P-901 is supplied, that probability increase cannot be fairly converted into a net-loss comparison. It must be treated as a material unresolved cross-case externality, not ignored.

**If S4 becomes available at 35 minutes**

Using the S2 review duration as a planning proxy for S4, the best-case S4 route is approximately:

\[
35 \text{ min availability} + 35 \text{ min review} + 5 \text{ min execution} = 75 \text{ min}
\]

That leaves only 15 minutes of deadline slack in the best case and no slack under slower review outcomes. Still, a confirmed t+35 S4 availability can make a `D + S4` plan competitive because it avoids the quantified P-901 breach increase.

Accordingly:

- If P-901’s 70-minute deadline is highly consequential or cannot absorb the 45-minute interruption, and S4 is genuinely confirmed for t+35 rather than merely forecast, promote `D + S4` to rank 1 or co-rank 1.
- If P-901 can absorb the interruption or S4’s actual review capacity is uncertain, retain `D + C with S2 now` as rank 1 because it provides a materially better P-884 timing distribution.

**If S4 becomes available at 65 minutes**

Again using comparable review duration, S4’s fastest plausible payment execution becomes roughly:

\[
65 + 35 + 5 = 105 \text{ minutes}
\]

That misses P-884’s 90-minute deadline even in the fast case. In that scenario, waiting for S4 should fall materially in the ranking for P-884’s deadline objective. Immediate S2 use becomes more strongly favored if P-884’s deadline remains a priority. Waiting until t+65 and then switching to S2 is also too late to preserve the best S2 timing advantage.

**Alternate-screening API quota**

- One of three remaining expedited calls is justified for P-884 only if it is started immediately and its result is used in the independent review.
- It should not delay S2’s evidence reading or final decision unnecessarily.
- The call has opportunity cost: another case might be more screen-sensitive later in the day. That opportunity cost is unquantified, but P-884’s high amount, sanctioned-border destination, score of 72, and unresolved match make the call decision-relevant rather than merely exploratory.
- A re-screen result does not erase the need for a new risk-score assessment or replace second-officer review while the triggering conditions remain.

**Waiting for an exception**

Action G is especially risky as a time-consuming distraction. A 20–60 minute Director response does not include the time to establish and justify a genuine incident or to complete the exception workflow. It should not reserve S2, delay the re-screen, or postpone normal review. It may be pursued in parallel only if an independent, genuine emergency basis appears; it should not be manufactured as an SLA-management device.

## 6. Final recommendation and confidence

**Primary recommendation:** execute the rank-1 conformant composite plan now:

1. Use one expedited alternate-screening API call immediately.
2. Have R17 complete the required post-evidence Analyst Review and document a refreshed/as-of risk score.
3. Interrupt P-901 and assign S2 immediately for an independent second-officer review, with S2 allowed to pre-read the file but not give final approval until the alternate-screen result and R17’s refreshed review are in the case file.
4. If approved, record approval and execute; if rejected or suspicious, reject or hold as documented. A SAR, if warranted, must not be disclosed and does not authorize payment.

This is preferable to a raw S2-only route because the alternate screen can reduce decision uncertainty without extending the critical path under the stated time ranges.

**Fallback:** if S2 cannot be released without unacceptable protected harm to P-901, preserve the alternate-screen result, route to a confirmed S4 as soon as available, and accept that P-884 may miss its deadline. If S4 or the analyst identifies insufficient evidence, move into the Action E evidence loop or a documented rejection under Action F’s actual threshold; do not use A or B to recover the deadline.

**Confidence:** Moderate overall. Confidence is high that A and B are prohibited and that an independent second review is currently required. Confidence is lower on timing, approval probability, alternate-screen diagnostic value, and the net priority trade-off against P-901 because those are observational or unquantified.

**Immediate reranking trigger:** the alternate-screen result together with R17’s refreshed risk score and documented fuzzy-match status. A credible resolution of the match and material score reduction could change the required review path; a persistent unresolved match or score at least 70 confirms that S2/S4 review remains mandatory.
