# 1. Objectives and constraints

The policy should deliver cases safely and on time while maintaining capable, rested teams. Throughput is one objective, alongside low error and rework rates, fair use of available capacity, continuity of ownership, documented learning opportunities, and coverage when specialists are absent. These objectives conflict: protecting ownership can retain a slower worker; training consumes mentor time; and using the fastest specialist for every case can create tomorrow's bottleneck.

**Safety and compliance constraints**

- A skill score of **0 means not allowed**, including under supervision. It does not become eligible because the worker has a training priority. In particular, Eva and Jonas cannot perform Security Review yet, and David cannot prepare or finalize Approval as an assigned Approval worker.
- Urgent and high-priority work requires skill 2 or 3, unless a named, qualified supervisor is explicitly assigned and has capacity. This policy also requires supervision for other skill-1 execution, rather than treating normal priority as permission for unsupported trainee work.
- Security Review requires skill 3, or skill 1–2 with explicit supervision by Gita or Hugo. C001's specialist requirement means the specialist must lead the substantive security judgement and sign-off; merely naming an unavailable supervisor is insufficient.
- Final Approval must be finalized by a worker at skill 2–3. A skill-1 worker can prepare material under supervision, but cannot exercise approval authority.
- No worker receives more than four assignments in this decision batch. Conservatively, count each distinct case in which a worker executes, prepares, supervises, or provides required ownership support. Count retained-owner assignments too; batching does not turn several cases into one assignment.
- Reserve execution and supervision time against actual availability. Do not double-book mentors or rely on overtime to make an assignment fit.
- Exclude workers whose overtime risk is high from additional work unless the case is urgent and no safer feasible alternative exists. Record the exhausted alternatives and reason for any exception.
- Batch Quality Check only for normal/low-priority cases with more than 24 hours of SLA time remaining. Each case retains its own deadline and review record.

**Preferences and conditional requirements**

- Prefer continuity strongly for high-complexity cases with high handover cost. Retain a competent owner, or provide feasible supervision to a weak owner. An owner at skill 0 cannot execute the activity; an owner at skill 1 cannot be retained as an unsupported performer. Preserve case knowledge through a structured handover if reassignment is necessary.
- Penalize additional reassignments, especially when a case is already at or above two. The historical 1.8× rework and 1.4× appeal associations justify caution, but are not proof that reassignment itself caused those outcomes.
- Balance projected effort relative to available hours, including complexity and mentoring. Equal numbers of cases would not necessarily be fair.
- At least 20% of normal/low-priority **case assignments** should advance an eligible worker's documented training priority when SLA risk remains acceptable. Count a supervised pair once in this case-level denominator; count both workers separately for capacity and assignment limits. Preparation can qualify as training, but observation alone, a skill-0 assignment, or an unrelated trainee activity cannot.
- Preserve capacity in scarce skills and build future substitutes. Do not require specialist execution when competent execution is safe and adequate.

For this example, use complexity at least 0.75 as a provisional “high complexity” threshold. This is a stated policy choice, not a threshold supplied by the data. A history of loops or rework can trigger enhanced review even below that threshold.

The snapshot does not supply processing durations, remaining downstream work, shift calendars, or mentor availability. Therefore, it supports a defensible staffing recommendation, but does not establish that all work fits today's hours or that every SLA can be met. The release schedule must pass a capacity and deadline check using those additional inputs.

# 2. Assignment policy

**Use case bundles rather than selecting a performer in isolation.** A bundle specifies the executor, any preparer and supervisor, the accountable case owner, and reserved time for every participant. This makes the cost of supervision visible and allows ownership to continue when specialist assistance is needed.

For each case and bundle, estimate:

- Processing and review effort conditional on activity, complexity, skill, supervision, and previous loops.
- Earliest completion under the workers' existing commitments and calendars.
- Coordination or handover time, distinguishing a change of case owner from assistance by another performer.
- Remaining time to the contractual SLA endpoint, including downstream activities. Finishing the next activity is not necessarily finishing the case.

Use an upper completion-time quantile appropriate to the service, with more conservative estimates for urgent or complex work. Let `slack(c,b)` be the deadline minus that estimated case-completion time, and let `miss_risk(c,b)` be the estimated probability of missing the deadline. Neither estimate should assume that mentor time is free.

An illustrative urgency score is:

```text
U(c) = priority_component(c)
       + clip(1 - best_feasible_slack_hours(c) / 24, 0, 2)
       + estimated_breach_probability(c)

priority_component: low = 0, normal = 1, high = 2, urgent = 3
```

The 24-hour scale and component weights are tunable, disclosed policy parameters. Urgency orders the cases and increases the weight given to SLA risk. It should not be added as an identical constant to all candidate scores and then presented as influencing which worker wins. If a case has no feasible bundle, escalate it rather than assigning it a reassuring score.

Score eligible bundles with named components:

```text
score(c,b) = - w_sla(U(c)) * miss_risk(c,b)
             - w_time * expected_total_effort(c,b)
             + w_skill * skill_match(c,b)
             + w_continuity * ownership_and_context_retention(c,b)
             - w_handover * transfer_and_coordination_cost(c,b)
             - w_load * projected_effort_per_available_hour(b)
             - w_overtime * permissible_overtime_exposure(b)
             + w_training * documented_learning_value(c,b)
             + w_resilience * remaining_scarce_skill_coverage(c,b)
```

Normalize components to disclosed scales before weighting them. Skill match rewards adequate expertise for the actual role, with extra value for specialist-level judgement on complex work. It does not automatically reward skill 3 enough to defeat every competent worker. A supervised trainee bundle has its own effort and risk estimates; it must not inherit the specialist's predicted speed.

Continuity rewards retaining the accountable owner and relevant knowledge. Handover cost reflects the cost band, complexity, cross-team coordination, and reassignment history. The observed 2.7-hour median waiting penalty for changing owners on complex cases and 3.3-hour service penalty for retaining weak skill are starting evidence for estimation, not fixed surcharges or quantities that can simply be subtracted from each other. Supervised work may behave differently from either historical group.

Load measures include existing committed effort, new work, and supervisory effort. Resilience measures include how much Security and Approval coverage remains after allocation, how many qualified substitutes are available, and whether training develops a useful future substitute. Weights and thresholds should be reviewed with workers and service managers, versioned, and checked for sensitivity; different defensible weights can produce different allocations.

```text
INPUT: queue, versioned skill matrix, roster, existing reservations,
       case histories, duration/risk estimates, policy version

1. Validate skill and availability data; retrieve decision-time snapshots.
   For normal/low cases, set the safe training target to ceil(0.20 * case_count).

2. Generate execution/supervision/preparation bundles for each case.
   Reject skill-0 execution, unauthorized final approval, missing required
   supervision, and prohibited Quality batches.
   Reject nonurgent additional work for high-overtime-risk participants.
   An urgent high-overtime exception is considered only after showing that
   no safer feasible bundle can serve the case.

3. Predict effort, completion, SLA risk, and handover cost for each bundle.
   Reject bundles that exceed available participant hours or lack review slots.
   Apply the strong continuity preference to complex, costly handovers.
   Mark a training bundle only when role, training target, and SLA checks match.

4. Order cases by urgency; reserve critical execution and review slots first.
   Within each priority class, compare predicted slack and breach risk.

5. Select a joint batch allocation using the component scores.
   Enforce the four-case limit for every participant and safe training quota.
   Account for supervisor commitments, queues, and scarce-skill reserves.
   Repair earlier choices through swaps/backtracking when a later case,
   mentor booking, or training requirement would otherwise be infeasible.

6. Break otherwise equivalent bundle ties by:
   lower SLA risk; fewer costly ownership changes; lower normalized load;
   suitable documented training; then a rotating resource order.
   Break case-order ties by earlier deadline and then case ID for reproducibility.

7. If no safe complete allocation exists:
   keep unsafe work queued; escalate urgent infeasibility immediately;
   seek another qualified worker, move flexible reservations, or reschedule
   work within its SLA. Do not relax skill-0 or approval-authority rules.
   Record any training shortfall caused by unacceptable SLA risk.

8. Log the chosen bundle, component scores, rejected alternatives,
   participant reservations, ownership decision, and exception reason.
   Release work only after required workers confirm actual availability.

9. Re-evaluate on completion, absence, new urgent work, or material risk change.
   Keep started work stable unless intervention is necessary; require a
   meaningful predicted benefit before changing an otherwise sound allocation.
```

A worker can flag missing documents, unrecorded commitments, or an unsuitable training task. The dispatcher can make a reasoned override, which is recorded and reviewed. Repeated overrides are evidence to improve the policy or data, rather than automatic evidence of worker noncompliance. Do not repeatedly split the same decision batch to evade its four-case limit.

The stated baselines are routing heuristics, not complete compliant schedulers. Raw first-available routing excludes only skill 0; it can therefore choose an unauthorized Final Approval worker or an unsupervised Security trainee. Both raw baselines can ignore overtime and batch limits. Evaluate those violations explicitly, and also compare versions using the **same hard-constraint filter** as the proposed policy. Otherwise, faster results may simply reflect weaker safeguards.

# 5. Explanation to workers

“We assign work using the skills the task requires, its deadline, the knowledge already held by its owner, and the time each person actually has available. Open-case counts help us understand workload, but they do not tell the whole story: a difficult case and time spent supervising also count.

“We try to keep complex cases with the people who know them. Celine remains involved in C001, with Hugo leading the specialist Security judgement. Eva keeps C016 because she is qualified and a transfer would be costly. For some less costly transfers, such as work currently with David or Iris, we move the next task to someone with capacity so existing commitments can be completed within working hours.

“Training is planned work. Amal's Diagnosis and Hugo's Approval preparation come with named supervisors and protected review time. Hugo will not finalize Approval until authorized to do so. Eva and Jonas need prerequisite Security development before they can perform a Security Review; their training priorities do not override that requirement.

“If we have missed a commitment, a case needs skills beyond its recorded description, or a learning task is unsuitable, please raise it. We will explain and record changes. The indicators help us improve staffing, support, and the process. We will interpret them with case complexity and available time, rather than ranking people by raw speed or case counts.”

Workers should be able to see their commitments, the reason for each assignment, the supervision arrangement, and how to request a review. Explain an exception in ordinary language, such as “specialist sign-off required” or “owner's available hours are already committed.” Do not expose unnecessary personal information about colleagues or present score decimals as unquestionable judgements.

# uncertainty rather than pretending timestamps identify service effort.
time_model, risk_model = fit_or_load_validated_time_and_risk_models(
    interval_log=intervals,
    enriched_history=join_decisions_to_outcomes(df, decisions),
    temporal_validation=True,
    retain_uncertainty=True,
)

def assign_batch(queue, snapshot):
    normal_low_cases = queue[
        queue['case_priority'].isin(['normal', 'low'])
    ]
    training_target = math.ceil(0.20 * len(normal_low_cases))
    candidates = {}

    for case in queue.to_dict('records'):
        bundles = generate_executor_and_supervisor_bundles(
            case, skills, roster, snapshot
        )
        candidates[case['case:concept:name']] = []
        for bundle in bundles:
            if not satisfies_skill_and_authority_rules(case, bundle):
                continue
            if not permitted_overtime_exposure(case, bundle, snapshot):
                continue
            estimate = estimate_effort_finish_and_risk(
                case, bundle, snapshot, time_model, risk_model
            )
            if not reservable_for_all_participants(bundle, estimate, snapshot):
                continue
            components = explainable_score_components(
                case, bundle, estimate, snapshot, training_targets
            )
            candidates[case['case:concept:name']].append(
                (bundle, estimate, components)
            )

    # Joint selection/repair is needed: choosing each case independently
    # can double-book a mentor or consume another case's only safe resource.
    allocation = select_and_repair_batch(
        candidates,
        urgency_order=compute_urgency_order(queue, candidates),
        participant_case_cap=4,
        safe_case_training_target=training_target,
        reserve_scarce_skill_capacity=True,
    )
    validate_joint_capacity_and_rules(allocation, snapshot)
    record_decision_snapshots_scores_and_exceptions(allocation, snapshot)
    return allocation

# Audit actual participation, not just planned assignments.
participant_cases = actual_participant_case_rows(decisions)
batch_counts = participant_cases.drop_duplicates(
    ['batch_id', 'resource', 'case:concept:name']
).groupby(['batch_id', 'resource']).size()
cap_violations = batch_counts[batch_counts > 4]

normal_low_assignments = case_assignments[
    case_assignments['priority_at_assignment'].isin(['normal', 'low'])

training_share_by_batch = normal_low_assignments.groupby('batch_id')[
    'eligible_documented_training'
].mean()

# Use a designated terminal event; include overdue open cases separately.
case_outcomes = derive_case_outcomes_with_deadline_history(df)
case_outcomes['sla_miss'] = (
    case_outcomes['case_completion_time'] >
    case_outcomes['contractual_deadline']
)
overdue_open_cases = find_overdue_unfinished_cases(case_outcomes)

# Rule replay uses original candidate states, calendars, skills, scores,
# and policy version; it checks batch constraints and approved overrides.
policy_deviations = replay_and_compare_decisions(decisions, skills, roster)
quality_report = stratified_rework_and_appeal_report(

The resource networks answer different questions. Handover-of-work reveals performer transitions and potential cross-team coordination. Working-together shows resources appearing in the same cases; it does not prove simultaneous collaboration or supervision. Activity-based similarity and organizational roles suggest overlap in observed work, but not legal or current competence. Display them with `view_sna` or save visualizations with `save_vis_sna`, then validate interpretations with workers.

`discover_batches` can identify candidate batching patterns, but the priority, SLA, and individual-review rules must still be checked. Performance DFG edges and variants can expose waiting, Diagnosis loops, and Rework paths. Inter-event performance includes more than pure resource waiting and should not be labelled as service time. `get_service_time` and `insert_case_service_waiting_time` require appropriate interval information for meaningful interpretation; overlapping work and administrative delays still need attention.

For deployment, keep three separate measures: case elapsed time, activity execution effort, and queue-ready-to-start waiting. Validate PM4Py's returned columns and aggregation conventions against these definitions, especially for overlapping activities. Use learned durations and networks to inform estimates and audits, retain uncertainty, and preserve the authorized skill rules and human review of exceptions.
