## 1. Materiality Framework

Severity is the harm of the interpreted business story if that story is right. It is not edit distance. Rank each case by the **control objective** that failed or is unproven, then scale by **exposure**, **reversibility**, **timing**, **customer or regulatory harm**, **recurrence**, **evidence reliability**, and **whether the case is even in the payable-claim model**.

| Score | Meaning | Typical signals in this log |
|---|---|---|
| 5 Critical | Irreversible payment-authorization or fraud-gate failure on a high-risk or high-value path, or pay-before-approve (P3). | Missing documented Fraud Screen on a P2 claim; bank accepted funds before approval. |
| 4 Severe | Clear data-aware policy bypass (P1/P2) or a categorical fraud-relevant gate skipped, even if residual loss is smaller. | Fitting Auto Approve after a recent beneficiary-bank change or above the EUR 5,000 cap. |
| 3 Material uncertainty | A required control is not reliably evidenced; exposure is material; the activity may have happened off-system. | Notification only via a mutable CRM link on a large payment; uncorroborated verbal control. |
| 2 Assurance / model | Monitoring, labeling, or scope error that impairs detection but does not demonstrate an operational bypass. | Synonym activity name; external assessor document; denial/withdrawal forced onto a payable model. |
| 1 Negligible | No control objective is threatened. | UI telemetry, transport retry, fitting silent routing, in-policy Auto Approve. |

**How the overlay interacts with the tree.** The tree only constrains order and the XOR between `(Fraud Screen → Manual Approve)` and `Auto Approve`. P1–P3 are data-aware authorization rules the tree cannot see. P4 accepts multi-channel notification if durable evidence is retained. P5 says UI, autosave, retry, and transport events are not business activities unless they expose a control issue. P6 says denied and withdrawn cases may be out of M0’s intended population.

**Confidence is not severity.** Confidence is how strongly the evidence supports the interpreted story.

- High: independent timestamps (bank acceptance vs approval), shared message IDs plus transport logs, procedure/UI naming, explicit attribute values (amount, risk, bank-change flag).
- Medium: contemporaneous external documents whose case binding is incomplete; participant recollection without an artifact.
- Low: mutable free-text links, no corroboration, or a live scope dispute that changes what “should” have been logged.

A score of 5 with medium confidence still outranks a score of 1 with high confidence. A score of 3 with low confidence is an evidence-preservation and investigation item, not a closed finding that the activity never occurred. A missing event is a missing record, not proof of non-performance.

**Do not rank by `raw_cost`.** Costs are uniform unit weights on every visible log/model move. That function cannot express P3 irreversibility, P1/P2 data conditions, P4 alternative channels, P5 noise, or P6 scope.

## 3. Repeated Deviation Patterns

**A. Retry-duplicate notification (`S(Notify)` then `L(Notify)` same message ID)**  
Frequency: CL-603 plus 74 cases in the full month, one connector.  
Business risk: Low for double payment or missed notice; moderate for metric pollution and false “extra notify” exceptions.  
Cause: Transport retry written as a second business event.  
Confidence: High.  
Owner: Integration / claims-notification platform.

**B. Data-aware Auto Approve that the tree accepts (P1/P2 failures with `raw_cost` 0)**  
Frequency: CL-609, CL-610, and two further fully synchronous cases in the month (four total).  
Business risk: High. The fraud and delegation design is bypassed without a conformance red flag. Bank-change Auto Approve (CL-610) is the sharper fraud vector; amount/risk breaches (CL-609) break the EUR 5,000 / LOW-only rule.  
Cause: Auto Approve implemented as an unconstrained XOR branch; amount, risk, and bank-change predicates not evaluated at the service or in the model.  
Confidence: High on the two detailed cases.  
Owner: Claims authorization product owner + payment controls.

**C. External-adjuster assessment absent from the core log (`M(Assess Loss)` + document)**  
Frequency: CL-606 and nine high-value cases; two lack a stable document-to-case identifier.  
Business risk: Medium. Assessment may have been done, but high-value files are not reconstructible if the binding is weak; a true skip would be a P2-path integrity issue.

Confidence: Medium (document exists in the typed case; binding quality unknown for two of nine).  
Owner: Loss-adjusting operations + document-management / data model.

**D. Activity-label / mapping mismatch**  
Frequency: CL-611 in sample; mapping table changed after the fact implies a known class.  
Business risk: Low operational, medium detective (false “skipped Assess Loss” plus false extra activity).  
Cause: `Assess Damage` vs `Assess Loss` synonym; late dictionary update.  
Confidence: High for CL-611.  
Owner: Claims data governance / event taxonomy.

**E. Out-of-scope or unmodeled non-pay outcomes (deny, withdraw)**  
Frequency: CL-607, CL-612; denied and withdrawn cases account for most alignments with `raw_cost` ≥ 4.  
Business risk: Low for cash leakage; high for governance and alert fatigue. Analysts will chase cost-4 ghosts while zero-cost P1/P2 breaches sit green.

Confidence: High.  
Owner: Process owner and model owner jointly — P6 must be decided.

Frequency: CL-604 in sample; the repair pattern `L(Pay Claim)` … `M(Pay Claim)` is the diagnostic signature.  
Business risk: Critical. Cash has already moved. EUR 90,000 accepted 17 minutes before recorded approval; Fraud Screen also missing.  
Cause: Payment API not gated on the required approval (and fraud) token.  
Confidence: High on order; medium on the missing fraud step.

Frequency: CL-602 (and the fraud-screen hole inside CL-604).  
Business risk: Critical when amount, HIGH risk, or bank change is present. Verbal confirmation is not an audit artifact.  
Cause: Offline/verbal practice, skipped step, or screen recorded in a system that is not in the event log.  
Confidence: Medium.  
Owner: Financial crime / SIU and the manual-approval authority.

**H. UI telemetry mixed into the business log**

Business risk: Negligible control risk; moderate noise risk (CL-601 is cost 3 for no business reason).

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

A missing log event is not proof the activity did not occur. Each test below is meant to discriminate, not to decorate the alignment.

**CL-602 — `M(Fraud Screen)` on EUR 480,000 HIGH + bank change**  
H1: Fraud Screen was never performed; approver rubber-stamped a verbal impression.  
H2: A screen was performed in SIU/email/phone and never written to the case log.  
H3: A screen exists under another label or case id.  
Discriminators: SIU case file, call recordings, email with timestamps before Manual Approve, user access logs on the fraud workspace, whether the bank-change flag was visible to the approver. If nothing predates approval, treat H1 as the working hypothesis. If an artifact predates approval but is unlabeled, that is H2/H3 (evidence gap), not a clean operational miss.

**CL-604 — bank accept 10:03, approval 10:20**  
H1: Payment service executed without reading the approval state (true P3 break).  
H2: Approval existed earlier in another system and was only copied into the core log at 10:20.  
H3: Clock skew or booking-date vs authorization-date confusion.  
Discriminators: Payment-gateway request payload (approval token present or absent), approval-system immutable timestamp, NTP/clock comparison, maker-checker ids. A gateway call with no approval token confirms H1. An earlier immutable approval id supports H2 and would downgrade operational severity while leaving a logging defect.

**CL-606 / nine-case external-adjuster pattern**  
H1: Assessment was done by the licensed adjuster (document at 10:05) and never interfaced.  
H2: The document is loosely associated and belongs to another file (especially the two cases without a stable id).  
H3: Assessment was skipped and a later report was attached for appearance.  
Discriminators: Immutable document-to-case identifier, hash, and signature time; whether fraud screeners cited the report; whether the report amount and peril match the payment. A signed, bound, pre-screen report supports H1. Unbound or post-payment documents keep H2/H3 open.

**CL-608 — `M(Notify Claimant)` plus mutable CRM link at +3 hours**  
H1: Claimant was notified in the portal at +3 hours; only the core event is missing (P4 satisfied, evidence weak).  
H2: Someone typed a CRM reference after the fact.  
H3: No customer notification occurred.  
Discriminators: Portal immutable message id, delivery/read receipt, outbound mail/SMS gateway log, CRM field audit history (created-at, last-modified-by). A gateway record at +3 hours with an immutable id supports H1. A CRM field created or edited after the query date supports H2. Nothing in portal or gateway supports H3.

**CL-609 and CL-610 — zero-cost Auto Approve**  
H1: Auto Approve service does not evaluate amount, risk, or bank-change (systematic rule gap).  
H2: Attributes were wrong at decision time (risk not yet HIGH; bank-change flag late).  
H3: A manual user invoked an “auto” function as a shortcut.  
Discriminators: Decision-service logs and rule version at the approval timestamp; as-of values of amount, risk, and beneficiary account; identity of the calling principal (system vs user). CL-610’s bank change one hour before payment is especially informative: if the flag was already on the payment record, H1 is favored over H2.

**CL-611 — Assess Damage vs Assess Loss**  
H1: Same activity, different label (pure mapping).  
H2: Assess Damage is a narrower engineering step and Assess Loss never happened.  
H3: Both happened and one is unlogged.  
Discriminators: Payload fields (reserve, cause of loss, quantum), the post-case mapping table, UI screenshot/procedure showing the user-facing name, whether any second assessment artifact exists. Procedure + UI + later mapping update strongly support H1.

**CL-607 / CL-612 — high cost on deny/withdraw**  
H1: Model-scope incompleteness only (P6); operational handling was correct.  
H2: Denied/withdrawn cases sometimes still pay (leakage hidden because we only inspected two).  
H3: Customer was left without a decision notice (especially CL-612, where the aligner inserts `M(Notify Claimant)`).  
Discriminators: Finance ledger for these case ids (any cash out?), denial/withdrawal letters, P6 written RACI. Zero ledger amount plus a denial/withdrawal notice confirms H1. Any payment confirms H2 and would jump severity to 5. No customer-facing artifact supports a limited H3 communications fix without treating the invented Pay/Approve moves as real.

**CL-603 / 74 retries**  
H1: Connector retry of one message.  
H2: Two genuine notifications (customer contacted twice).  
H3: Retry after a failed first send, so only the second reached the customer.  
Discriminators: Shared message id (already present), SMTP/gateway success codes, claimant-visible duplicates. Same id + retry log confirms H1 and keeps severity at 1.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

The supplied `raw_cost` is the sum of a **uniform unit cost** on every visible log or model move. That is an edit distance under one cost function. It is not compliance risk, financial exposure, customer harm, or confidence.

**Cost-function design.** Every `L` and `M` costs 1. CL-601’s three workbench events therefore equal CL-604’s combination of an out-of-order EUR 90,000 payment, a missing fraud screen, and a repaired pay. CL-602’s missing Fraud Screen on EUR 480,000 HIGH risk with a one-day bank change costs 1 — the same as CL-603’s connector retry. Uniform weights flatten P3 irreversibility and P2 fraud gates into the same currency as autosave.

**Scope.** CL-607 and CL-612 produce cost 4 because M0 cannot represent Deny or Withdraw, so the aligner inserts Assess/Auto Approve/Pay (and, for CL-612, Notify). Those inserted moves are **model completions**, not evidence that staff auto-approved or paid. The note that denied and withdrawn cases dominate cost ≥ 4 follows mechanically. Using cost to rank “worst cases” would put a correctly denied pre-screen and a EUR 2,000 withdrawal above a EUR 480,000 undocumented fraud gate.

**Hidden moves.** CL-605’s `T(tau,c=0)` is fitting. The converted net’s silent routing before Auto Approve is not a skipped control and must not be narrated as one. Cost 0 is right here; the same 0 is wrong for CL-609 and CL-610.

**Data conditions.** CL-609 and CL-610 are fully synchronous. The tree’s XOR allows Auto Approve, so cost is 0, yet P1 fails on both and P2 fails on CL-610. Four monthly cases share this shape. Control-flow fitness is silent on amount, risk, and beneficiary-bank change. **Zero-cost policy failure is the central demonstration that alignment cost is not business risk.**

**Activity granularity and duplicated telemetry.** CL-601 (UI) and CL-603 (retry) inflate cost with events P5 says are not business activities. Month-wide, 74 identical notify retries would dominate any unfiltered mean cost. Conversely, CL-611 splits one assessment into `L(Assess Damage)+M(Assess Loss)` (cost 2) because the log and the tree use different names for the same step.

**Missing-but-external evidence.** CL-606’s cost 1 and CL-608’s cost 1 both mean “model event not in the core log.” In CL-606 an external licensed report exists at 10:05; in CL-608 a CRM portal message at +3 hours may satisfy P4. Cost cannot say “done elsewhere with weak binding” versus “never done.” Treating `M(*)` as non-performance overstates operational failure and understates records risk.

**Irreversibility and exposure.** CL-604’s payment was accepted by the bank before approval. P3 says that is severe even when the alignment repairs it cheaply — and it did (cost 3, including an invented second Pay Claim). CL-602’s residual exposure is EUR 480,000 with every P2 red flag and no fraud artifact. CL-610’s residual exposure is EUR 4,500 but the same P2 bank-change gate was bypassed at cost 0. A risk measure that cannot see cash-out timing or amount cannot be used as the priority key.

Keep **technical alignment cost** and **business severity** in separate columns. Reweighting the cost function can improve a queue; it cannot replace the materiality framework in Section 1.

## 7. PM4Py Reproduction and Aggregation Code

```python
"""
Reproduce tree→net conversion and alignments; aggregate the AUTHORITATIVE
supplied move summaries. Alternative optimal alignments and PM4Py move
formatting may differ from the supplied notation — treat the table in
the prompt as diagnostic ground truth for business interpretation.
Technical raw_cost and business_severity are kept in separate columns.
No external files are read.
"""

import pm4py
import pandas as pd

# 1) Parse the normative process tree
tree_string = (
    "->('Register Claim','Validate Coverage','Assess Loss',"
    "X(->('Fraud Screen','Manual Approve'),'Auto Approve'),"
    "'Pay Claim','Notify Claimant')"
)
process_tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_string)

# 2) Convert to an accepting Petri net (introduces silent routing transitions)
net, initial_marking, final_marking = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(process_tree)
```
