# 2. Scenario forecasts
## 2.1 Optimistic
• Handover pattern – local loops 18%, cross-team 50%; tech handovers centralised but processed within SLA  
• Queue/wait – Tech_SS median 2.5 h, 90th pct 7 h; approval queue median 1.8 h  
• Cycle-time – median -0.8 d (6 %), 90th pct -1.5 d; East converges toward North  
• Rework/quality – rework 2 pp due to consistent specialist advice; quality check failures unchanged  
• Role clarity – clear RACI in shared queues, ownership logged in system  
• Fairness – SLA-miss rates converge (North 5.5 %, South 5.8 %, East 6 %); priority attribute drives order, not team  
• Early warning signs – Tech_SS utilisation >85 % for >5 consecutive days

## 2.2 Central (most probable)
• Handover – nominal count -10 %, but “caseworker  Tech_SS  caseworker” replaces local loop, so net touches 3 %  
• Queue – Tech_SS median 4 h, 90th pct 16 h; long tail visible for high-complexity  
• Cycle-time – median flat (±0 d); 90th pct +0.9 d; tail driven by shared queues  
• Rework – overall 0.5 pp; high-complexity +1 pp (lost early advice)  
• Role clarity – caseworkers unclear on who approves borderline cases; duplicated escalations seen in event log (subcontracting patterns )  
• Fairness – East improves; North/South slight SLA drift; customer_segment “Rural” experiences +1 d wait (language/regional notes lost)  
• Early warnings – increase in reassignment_count after Tech_SS answer (>1.4 mean), duplicate approvals logged

## 2.3 Adverse
• Handover – total +7 % (extra approval handover outweighs loop removal); tech node becomes super-hub  
• Queue – Tech_SS median 6 h, 90th pct 30 h; approval queue median 5 h  
• Cycle-time – median +1.4 d; 90th pct +4.0 d; high-complexity +6 d  
• Rework – +3 pp overall, especially legal/risk (+5 pp) due to rushed reviews  
• Role clarity – parallel approvals observed; working_together network shows fragmented collaboration  
• Fairness – North/South perceive loss: SLA-miss +3 pp; customer satisfaction gap widens to 0.6 score points  
• Early warnings – backlog growth rate in Tech_SS >15 % week-on-week; spike in cross-team “exception handler” handovers

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time
1. Tail latency: 90th/95th percentile may rise even if median falls, harming urgent outliers.  
2. High-complexity starvation: these cases may queue behind many low-complexity ones; fairness issue.  
3. Local knowledge erosion: early informal fixes removed  rework later; appears months later.  
4. Skill stagnation: local staff stop learning; detection via shrinking activity diversity per resource.  
5. Accountability gaps: duplicated or missing approvals; hidden until audit finds conflicting entries.  
6. Exception handling resilience: central node outage (flu outbreak, system downtime) stalls whole flow; average of sunny-day data misses this.

# 5. Pilot design
• Scope – run shared Technical & Approval Service for Team East only + 20 % random sample of North/South high-complexity cases.  
• Comparison – remaining North/South cases keep old model (control).  
• Duration – 2-month pre-measurement, 3-month pilot, 1-month post-stabilisation review.  
• Segmentation – tag cases by team, complexity, specialty, customer_segment.  
• Guardrail metrics (stop-loss triggers):  
  – Tech_SS 90th-pct wait >24 h for >2 weeks  
  – SLA-miss of pilot cohort >2 pp vs control  
  – Customer satisfaction drop >0.3 points in pilot vs baseline.  
• Staff feedback – weekly pulse survey + qualitative interviews; add “local_expertise_flag lost?” check boxes.  
• Rollback/adjustment – if any guardrail breached, revert East to embedded specialist within 10 days or add resources; iterate routing rules.

# 6. Recommendation
Pilot first, then scale adaptively.

Because:  
• Benefits (specialist utilisation, East relief, decision standardisation) are plausible but bottleneck, fairness, and learning risks are non-linear and only visible under load.  
• Early averages could look good if backlog is low; do not infer long-run success until tail metrics, rework, and customer satisfaction are stable.  
• Require guardrails and continued local up-skilling initiatives to avoid capability erosion.
