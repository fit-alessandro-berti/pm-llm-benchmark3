# 1. Risk indicators

Overall, the procurement-to-payment event log evidence is most consistent with a **mixture of mechanisms** rather than a single explanation:
1. **Formal and informal role specialization** explains the high-volume routine pairs (`Buyer_Aline → Approver_Elena`, `Buyer_Boris → Approver_Felix`), standard vendor onboarding (`VendorMaster_Marc → AP_Iris`), and automated match-exception routing (`Bot_Match → AP_Kim`).
2. **Operational emergency handling** accounts for the majority of rapid turnarounds and repeated interactions in medical supplies (`Buyer_Carla + Approver_Gita`) and weekend facilities repairs (`Buyer_Dan + Manager_Hugo`), though accompanied by control-compliance erosion (late retrospective justifications and unflagged rush cases).
3. **Data-quality, identity, and ingestion artifacts** (`legacy_erp` bot-to-human mapping errors, `Approver_Gita` shared-account usage, `quarter_close_upload` batch spreadsheet timestamps, and missing maintenance-ticket links for `V-77x`) substantially inflate apparent segregation-of-duties (SoD) and timing anomalies.
4. **Concentrated residual control risk requiring forensic verification** is highest in the `VendorMaster_Lena + AP_Kim` cluster (and secondarily in non-batch self-justifications and non-emergency rush approvals), where multiple risk indicators converge without an emergency justification.

# 2. Benign alternative explanations

### Pattern A: `VendorMaster_Lena + AP_Kim` concentration on `V-77x`, 17–19 min median approval times, 83% `manual_upload`, 9 four-eyes violations, and 11 subcontracting loops
* **Benign Explanation 1 — Unlinked Corporate Banking Migration & Batch Maintenance**: Vendor family `V-77x` recently changed its banking platform across multiple regional entities. Because the standard ERP interface could not update bank routing across multi-region open payables simultaneously, `VendorMaster_Lena` (bank-changes specialist) executed pre-authorized mass updates via `source_system="manual_upload"`, and `AP_Kim` (designated approver for higher-risk suppliers) immediately approved the queued payments once the maintenance batch was loaded. In the 9 four-eyes violation cases, the `manual_upload` ingestion template may have populated `org:resource` with the batch uploader's ID (`VendorMaster_Lena` or `AP_Kim`) on both events even though the underlying actions were split.
* **Benign Explanation 2 — Offline Pre-Verification & Post-Approval Formatting Corrections**: Complex bank changes for high-value suppliers (`amount_band="high"` in 68% of cases) often undergo out-of-band verification (signed letterhead review and treasury callback) *before* data entry. Thus, the 17-minute interval in the event log measures only the lag between final master-data entry and queue release, not the true verification duration. Similarly, the 11 `VendorMaster_Lena → AP_Kim → VendorMaster_Lena` loops may reflect benign correction of regional SWIFT/IBAN formatting rejections encountered during payment file generation.
* **Evidence to Support vs. Refute**:
  * *Supports benign*: Authenticated IT/master-data maintenance tickets for `V-77x` pre-dating the events; out-of-band bank callback logs signed prior to `Change Vendor Bank Details`; distinct user IDs in `entered_by` vs. `approved_by` on the 9 violating cases; bank account changes matching verified corporate treasury accounts of `V-77x`.
  * *Refutes benign (increases concern)*: Absence of maintenance tickets or callback documentation for specific `V-77x` cases; `entered_by == approved_by` showing a single user manually uploaded and approved both events; before/after audit trails on the 11 loops showing bank details changed to an unrecognized account prior to `Release Payment` and reverted to the legitimate `V-77x` account afterward.

### Pattern B: Fast approvals (8–14 min medians), high emergency co-working, and $n=2$ subcontracting loops in `Buyer_Carla + Approver_Gita` and `Buyer_Dan + Manager_Hugo`
* **Benign Explanation 1 — Dedicated Urgent Operational Escalation Channels**: `Buyer_Carla` specializes in emergency medical supplies (88% `emergency_flag=True`) paired with `Approver_Gita` (delegated urgent/high-value approver), while `Buyer_Dan` handles facilities repairs (79% `emergency_flag=True`, frequently on weekends) paired with `Manager_Hugo` (emergency overrides). In clinical shortages or weekend facility breakdowns, buyer and approver coordinate synchronously by phone or messaging before logging the PO, resulting in 8–10 minute in-system approval medians and frequent `Approver → Buyer → Approver` clarification loops (36 and 29 cases) as vendor availability or repair scope updates in real time.
* **Benign Explanation 2 — Clerical Backlog Transcription & Assistant Proxy Entry**: `Temp_Nina → Buyer_Carla` (65 co-cases, 4 min median) reflects end-of-quarter emergency backlog entry, and 23 emergency approvals under `org:resource="Approver_Gita"` have assistant IDs in `entered_by`. When already-executed emergency orders are transcribed into the ERP after the fact by support staff, sequential steps (`Create Purchase Order`, `Emergency Override`, `Approve Purchase Order`) are logged minutes apart within a single data-entry session.
* **Evidence to Support vs. Refute**:
  * *Supports benign*: Matching hospital/clinical urgency tickets or weekend facility incident reports; timestamps on external vendor quotes (`Receive Quote`) or delivery notes (`Goods Receipt`) confirming genuine time pressure; `entered_by` confirming assistant or `Temp_Nina` transcription.
  * *Refutes benign (increases concern)*: Use of the 8–10 minute fast-track channel on the 12% (`Carla + Gita`) and 21% (`Dan + Hugo`) of cases where `emergency_flag=False` without valid urgent justification; repeat awards to favored high-margin vendors bypassing `Request Quote` / `Receive Quote`; PO modifications within the 36/29 subcontracting loops that artificially keep values just beneath higher approval thresholds.

### Pattern C: 42 Four-eyes violations on `Create Purchase Order + Approve Purchase Order` (`Buyer_Carla`, `Buyer_Dan`, `Approver_Gita`)
* **Benign Explanation 1 — `legacy_erp` Bot-to-Human Mapping Defect**: In `source_system="legacy_erp"`, `Bot_PO` generates the purchase order while storing the designated human approver in `approved_by`. Due to a known mapping table defect, the ETL pipeline overwrites `org:resource` on `Create Purchase Order` with the human approver's identifier instead of `Bot_PO`, creating synthetic self-approval violations whenever that human subsequently performs `Approve Purchase Order`.
* **Benign Explanation 2 — Shared Account Usage Under `Approver_Gita`**: In 23 emergency approvals, assistants logged in under the shared `org:resource="Approver_Gita"` account while their distinct personal identifiers were captured in `entered_by`. If an assistant created or modified the PO under the shared session and `Approver_Gita` approved it (or vice versa), `org:resource` falsely indicates a single-person violation.
* **Evidence to Support vs. Refute**:
  * *Supports benign*: The violating cases have `source_system="legacy_erp"` with `entered_by="Bot_PO"` on the creation event, or involve `org:resource="Approver_Gita"` where `entered_by != approved_by` across the creation and approval events.
  * *Refutes benign (increases concern)*: Cases occurring in native modern ERP source systems (not `legacy_erp`) where `entered_by == approved_by == org:resource` across both `Create Purchase Order` and `Approve Purchase Order`, proving a single individual created and self-approved the order.

### Pattern D: 17 Same-person `Emergency Override + Retrospective Justification` violations (9 min median) and Late Retrospective Justifications (18%–21%)
* **Benign Explanation 1 — Quarter-Close Spreadsheet Migration Artifact**: 12 of the 17 same-person violations share identical minute-level timestamps and originate from `source_system="quarter_close_upload"`. Independent retrospective reviews may have been properly completed offline on paper or email during the quarter, but when consolidated into a tracking spreadsheet and bulk-uploaded after quarter close, the import script stamped the override owner (`Manager_Hugo` or `Approver_Gita`) into `org:resource` and assigned the batch-import timestamp to the justification events.
* **Benign Explanation 2 — Clerical Upload by Override Owner & Weekend/Calendar SLA Distortion**: For the remaining 5 cases, the manager who performed the override may have uploaded a counter-signed justification document into the system (`org:resource` capturing the uploader while `approved_by` records the independent counter-signer). For the 18% (`Carla + Gita`) and 21% (`Dan + Hugo`) late justifications (>3 working days), weekend repairs (`Buyer_Dan`) or quarter-close clerical bottlenecks (`Temp_Nina`) may have delayed system logging even though substantive managerial sign-off occurred within 3 business days.
* **Evidence to Support vs. Refute**:
  * *Supports benign*: Raw `quarter_close_upload` spreadsheet columns and attached PDF/email approvals showing a different human reviewer in `approved_by` signed within 3 business days of the override.
  * *Refutes benign (increases concern)*: `entered_by == approved_by` for the 5 non-batch cases (and within the original spreadsheet for the 12 batch cases), or absence of any independent reviewer signature/documentation supporting the emergency override.

### Pattern E: High-frequency co-working in `Buyer_Aline → Approver_Elena` (510 cases) / `Buyer_Boris → Approver_Felix` (495 cases) and 31 `Vendor Check + Change Vendor Bank Details` same-person cases
* **Benign Explanation 1 — Structural Team Alignment**: `Approver_Elena` is the designated Team A approver and `Approver_Felix` is the Team B approver. Their high co-case counts with `Buyer_Aline` and `Buyer_Boris`—paired with normal 2.4h–2.6h median approval times, 4–5% emergency rates, 1% late justifications, and low vendor concentration—reflect standard organizational workflow routing.
* **Benign Explanation 2 — Intended Vendor Master Role Design**: Having `VendorMaster_Lena` or `VendorMaster_Marc` execute both `Vendor Check` and `Change Vendor Bank Details` (31 cases) reflects consolidated master-data vetting within the Vendor Master team, where the formal control boundary is between master-data maintenance and AP payment approval (`AP_Iris`/`AP_Jonas`/`AP_Kim`), not between two master-data steps.
* **Evidence to Support vs. Refute**:
  * *Supports benign*: ERP workflow routing rules mapping Team A/B buyers to their respective approvers; corporate SoD matrix confirming `Vendor Check` and `Change Vendor Bank Details` belong to the same authorized role profile when `Payment Approval` is segregated.
  * *Refutes benign (increases concern)*: Any subset of the 31 cases where the same vendor-master resource also executed `Payment Approval` (overlapping with the 9 `Lena`/`Kim` violations) or skipped mandatory onboarding verification documents.

# 3. Case prioritization

### High-Priority Case Clusters (Immediate Substantive & Forensic Review)

1. **Cluster H1 — `VendorMaster_Lena + AP_Kim` Bank-Change & Payment Approval Cluster (44 co-cases; prioritizing the 9 four-eyes violations, 16 fast 17-min `manual_upload` approvals, and 11 `Lena → Kim → Lena` subcontracting loops)**
   * **Reason for priority**: Represents the single sharpest convergence of fraud/control-failure indicators in the log: direct breach of a normative disbursement SoD rule (9 cases), 83% `manual_upload` share, 72% concentration in vendor family `V-77x` split across regions, 68% high-amount share, 17–19 minute approval medians (vs. 7.8h global median), post-approval bank-modification loops (11 cases), and low emergency justification (16% `emergency_flag=True`).
   * **Documents and audit-trail fields to inspect first**:
     * Event log fields: `entered_by`, `approved_by`, `org:resource`, `source_system`, `vendor_id`, `region`, `amount_band`, `time:timestamp`, and `lifecycle:transition`.
     * Master-data change history: exact before-and-after bank account/IBAN values across all 11 `VendorMaster_Lena → AP_Kim → VendorMaster_Lena` loops (checking whether bank details were altered before payment and restored afterward).
     * Supporting documents: the corporate banking-platform migration ticket for `V-77x`, independent bank callback verification logs, signed vendor remittance change requests, and actual bank wire beneficiary settlement records.

2. **Cluster H2 — Non-Batch Same-Person `Emergency Override + Retrospective Justification` Violations (5 of 17 cases involving `Manager_Hugo` and `Approver_Gita` not from `quarter_close_upload`)**
   * **Reason for priority**: These 5 violations cannot be attributed to the quarter-close spreadsheet upload artifact and indicate potential direct self-review of emergency overrides within minutes (9 min fast subgroup median vs. 1.5 days global median).
   * **Documents and audit-trail fields to inspect first**:
     * Event log fields: `entered_by`, `approved_by`, `org:resource`, `source_system`, `amount_band`, `vendor_id`, and `purchase_category`.
     * Supporting documents: attached retrospective justification forms, secondary managerial sign-off emails, and underlying emergency work orders or delivery receipts (`Goods Receipt`).

3. **Cluster H3 — Residual True-Human `Create Purchase Order + Approve Purchase Order` Four-Eyes Violations (Subset of the 42 cases remaining after filtering out `legacy_erp` bot mappings and `Approver_Gita` assistant proxy entries)**
   * **Reason for priority**: Isolates genuine procurement self-approvals (`entered_by == approved_by` in native ERP systems) from known logging defects, focusing especially on high-value or non-emergency orders among `Buyer_Carla`, `Buyer_Dan`, and `Approver_Gita`.
   * **Documents and audit-trail fields to inspect first**:
     * Event log fields: `source_system` (filtering out `"legacy_erp"` where `entered_by == "Bot_PO"`), `entered_by` vs. `approved_by` on both events, `emergency_flag`, and `amount_band`.
     * Supporting documents: user session/IP access logs for the shared `Approver_Gita` account, purchase requisitions (`Create Purchase Request`), vendor quotes (`Request Quote` / `Receive Quote`), and three-way match records (`Goods Receipt`, `Invoice Match`).

### Medium-Priority Case Clusters (Targeted Control Compliance & Process Audit)

1. **Cluster M1 — Non-Emergency Fast-Track Approvals in Emergency Pairs (~15 cases [12% of 126] in `Buyer_Carla + Approver_Gita` and ~22 cases [21% of 104] in `Buyer_Dan + Manager_Hugo` where `emergency_flag=False`)**
   * **Reason for priority**: Fast approvals (8–14 min) are operationally understandable for genuine emergencies, but routing non-emergency purchases through urgent override channels risks circumventing competitive bidding (`Request Quote` / `Negotiate Terms`).
   * **Documents and audit-trail fields to inspect first**: `emergency_flag`, `contract_type`, `purchase_category`, `amount_band`, presence/omission of quoting activities, and written justification for expedited handling.

2. **Cluster M2 — Batch-Migrated Same-Person Justifications (12 cases in `source_system="quarter_close_upload"`) and Late Retrospective Justifications (>3 working days: 18% of `Carla + Gita` [~23 cases] and 21% of `Dan + Hugo` [~22 cases])**
   * **Reason for priority**: Primarily reflects administrative backlog (`Temp_Nina`) and batch spreadsheet ingestion, but requires audit verification that a distinct authorized person substantively reviewed each override and that SLA breaches are remediated.
   * **Documents and audit-trail fields to inspect first**: Original pre-upload spreadsheet file (reviewing original approver columns and sign-off dates), `entered_by`, `approved_by`, and business-calendar elapsed time between `Emergency Override` and `Retrospective Justification`.

3. **Cluster M3 — High-Value Emergency Subcontracting Loops (`Approver_Gita → Buyer_Carla → Approver_Gita` [36 cases] and `Manager_Hugo → Buyer_Dan → Manager_Hugo` [29 cases] where `amount_band="high"` [42% and 31% shares])**
   * **Reason for priority**: Verifies whether repeated buyer–approver ping-ponging on high-value urgent cases reflects legitimate specification changes or improper order splitting/threshold manipulation.
   * **Documents and audit-trail fields to inspect first**: PO version history across `lifecycle:transition`, line-item price/quantity changes between loop iterations, `vendor_id`, and physical `Goods Receipt` verification.

### Low-Priority or Monitoring-Only Clusters (Routine Baselines & Expected Role Patterns)

1. **Cluster L1 — Routine High-Volume Buyer–Approver Pairs (`Buyer_Aline → Approver_Elena` [510 cases, 2.4h median, 33 loops] and `Buyer_Boris → Approver_Felix` [495 cases, 2.6h median, 30 loops])**
   * **Reason for priority**: Metrics align with healthy routine operations (low emergency share of 4–5%, low high-amount share of 17–18%, 1% late justification, 5–6% manual share, low vendor concentration).
   * **Audit-trail fields to monitor**: Automated continuous monitoring on the small 4–5% `emergency_flag=True` and 5–6% `manual_upload` slices only.

2. **Cluster L2 — Standard Onboarding, Vendor Master Dual-Step, Automated Exception Handling, and Backlog Intake (`VendorMaster_Marc + AP_Iris` [41 cases, 1.8h median], `Vendor Check + Change Vendor Bank Details` [31 cases] with independent AP approval, `Bot_Match → AP_Kim` [210 cases, 6 min median, 52 loops], and `Temp_Nina → Buyer_Carla` [65 cases, 4 min median])**
   * **Reason for priority**: Reflects intended process architecture (automated invoice match exceptions routed to `AP_Kim`, routine vendor onboarding reviewed after 1.8h by `AP_Iris`, and temporary quarter-end intake support).
   * **Audit-trail fields to monitor**: Periodic confirmation that none of the 31 `Vendor Check + Change Vendor Bank Details` cases share a payment approver with the vendor-master specialist.

# 4. Network and conformance analyses

The following Python / PM4Py pipeline operationalizes all six analytical tasks while correcting for source-system and shared-account confounders:

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 0. Load and format the event log, plus construct a de-confounded actor field
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
df = pd.read_csv("procurement_event_log.csv")
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    timest_format="%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S",
)

# Resolve true actor to separate data-quality artifacts from genuine human actions:
# - For creation/entry steps, prefer 'entered_by' (captures Bot_PO in legacy_erp
#   and assistants using the shared 'Approver_Gita' account).
# - For approval steps, prefer 'approved_by' when populated.
approval_acts = {
    "Approve Purchase Order",
    "Payment Approval",
    "Emergency Override",
    "Retrospective Justification",
}
df["org:true_actor"] = df["org:resource"]
is_approval = df["concept:name"].isin(approval_acts)
df.loc[is_approval & df["approved_by"].notna(), "org:true_actor"] = df["approved_by"]
df.loc[~is_approval & df["entered_by"].notna(), "org:true_actor"] = df["entered_by"]


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Filtering four-eyes violations using pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
sod_pairs = [
    ("Create Purchase Order", "Approve Purchase Order"),
    ("Change Vendor Bank Details", "Payment Approval"),
    ("Emergency Override", "Retrospective Justification"),
    ("Vendor Check", "Change Vendor Bank Details"),
]

for act1, act2 in sod_pairs:
    # Raw check on org:resource
    raw_viol = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
        df, act1, act2, resource_key="org:resource", case_id_key="case:concept:name"
    )
    # De-confounded check on org:true_actor (after fixing legacy_erp & shared logins)
    clean_viol = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
        df, act1, act2, resource_key="org:true_actor", case_id_key="case:concept:name"
    )
    print(
        f"SoD Pair ({act1} | {act2}): "
        f"raw cases={raw_viol['case:concept:name'].nunique()}, "
        f"true-actor cases={clean_viol['case:concept:name'].nunique()}"
    )


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Discovering handover, working-together, and subcontracting networks
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
how_net = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
    df, resource_key="org:resource", case_id_key="case:concept:name"
)
wt_net = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(
    df, resource_key="org:resource", case_id_key="case:concept:name"
)
sub_net = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(
    df, n=2, resource_key="org:resource", case_id_key="case:concept:name"
)

# Multi-dimensional network analysis grouped by resource and activity family
net_analysis = pm4py.discover_network_analysis(
    df,
    out_column="case:concept:name",
    in_column="case:concept:name",
    node_column_source="org:resource",
    node_column_target="org:resource",
    edge_column="concept:name",
    performance=True,
)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Comparing emergency and non-emergency cases
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
df_emerg = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df, "emergency_flag", [True, "True"], level="case", retain=True
)
df_non_emerg = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df, "emergency_flag", [True, "True"], level="case", retain=False
)

variants_emerg = pm4py.get_variants(df_emerg)
variants_non_emerg = pm4py.get_variants(df_non_emerg)

# Identify non-emergency cases routed through emergency resource pairs (Cluster M1)
rush_resources = ["Buyer_Carla", "Approver_Gita", "Buyer_Dan", "Manager_Hugo"]
non_emerg_rush = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df_non_emerg, "org:resource", rush_resources, level="case", retain=True
)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Checking timing with path-performance filters & service/waiting times
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Isolate unusually fast PO approvals (< 15 minutes = 900 seconds)
fast_po_approvals = pm4py.filter_paths_performance(
    df,
    path=("Create Purchase Order", "Approve Purchase Order"),
    min_performance=0,
    max_performance=900,
)

# Isolate unusually fast Bank Change -> Payment Approval (< 30 minutes = 1800 seconds)
fast_bank_to_pay = pm4py.filter_paths_performance(
    df,
    path=("Change Vendor Bank Details", "Payment Approval"),
    min_performance=0,
    max_performance=1800,
)

# Extract exact sub-traces between Bank Change and Payment Approval (e.g., Lena->Kim->Lena loops)
subtraces_bank_pay = pm4py.filter_between(
    df, "Change Vendor Bank Details", "Payment Approval"
)

# Compute activity service times and case-level waiting/service times
service_times = pm4py.get_service_time(df, start_timestamp_key="time:timestamp")
df_with_timings = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Declarative rule & log-skeleton check for Retrospective Justification
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Discover and check log-skeleton and DECLARE constraints (learned from compliant baseline)
df_compliant_emerg = pm4py.filter_paths_performance(
    df_emerg,
    path=("Emergency Override", "Retrospective Justification"),
```

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

Process mining analysis of last quarter's procurement-to-payment event log identified several segregation-of-duties (SoD) exceptions, compressed approval intervals, and concentrated resource-handover patterns. Importantly, process mining indicators reflect workflow anomalies and control exceptions rather than conclusive proof of fraud, collusion, or intentional misconduct. Most high-frequency pairings—including routine buyer–approver teams (`Buyer_Aline`/`Approver_Elena` and `Buyer_Boris`/`Approver_Felix`) and dedicated urgent escalation channels (`Buyer_Carla`/`Approver_Gita` and `Buyer_Dan`/`Manager_Hugo`)—appear largely consistent with formal role specialization and operational demands for emergency medical supplies and weekend facility repairs.

Furthermore, several apparent control breaches are heavily influenced by data-quality and logging limitations. Specifically, a mapping table defect in `legacy_erp` conflates automated `Bot_PO` creation with human approvers under `org:resource`, shared credentials under `Approver_Gita` mask assistant data entry recorded in `entered_by`, and twelve of seventeen same-person retrospective justifications stem from a single `quarter_close_upload` spreadsheet migration with identical minute-level timestamps.

After accounting for these operational and technical factors, the most concerning subgroup requiring priority audit review is the interaction between `VendorMaster_Lena` and `AP_Kim` (44 co-cases). This cluster exhibits nine direct four-eyes violations combining `Change Vendor Bank Details` and `Payment Approval`, rapid approval turnaround (17-minute median across 16 fast cases), 83% reliance on `manual_upload`, 68% high-value transactions, eleven subcontracting loops, and 72% concentration in vendor family `V-77x` despite low emergency incidence (16%). While a legitimate banking-platform migration for `V-77x` lacking ticket linkage in the log may explain part of this pattern, the convergence of manual uploads, rapid disbursements, and post-approval bank-detail touches presents elevated control risk.

Internal Audit recommends targeted follow-up before drawing conclusions: reconciling all `V-77x` bank changes against authenticated maintenance tickets and callback logs, inspecting `entered_by` and `approved_by` fields to separate genuine SoD breaches from `legacy_erp` and batch-upload artifacts, and enforcing individual user credentials.
