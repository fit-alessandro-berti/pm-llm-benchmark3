# Automation Portfolio Prioritization for Small-Business Loan Origination

The headline finding is that the most frequent activities (Receive/Register/Classify) are cheap, low-variance, and already partly digital, whereas end-to-end performance is dominated by a small set of low-frequency, high-waiting-time synchronization and control activities: **Resolve entity mismatch** (31.0h wait, 38% rework, blocks both KYC and signing), **Wait for external valuation** (58.0h wait), **Customer signing** (44.0h wait, 21% reminder loop), **Financial document review / Request missing documents** (18.0h + 8.0h waits, 52% repeated), and **Committee packaging** (26.0h wait, synchronization of four upstream artifacts). Automating frequency-leaders first would "expose classification, KYC, and document-review bottlenecks" (A1's own stated risk) and push clean inflow onto the 9-FTE credit-analyst pool that the working-together network already flags as central and overloadable. The recommendation therefore optimizes flow, synchronization, and control integrity rather than raw transaction count.

## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

**Upstream data-quality dependencies.** Everything downstream inherits the quality of intake and classification. A2 (classification) governs the control path — its 8% error rate misroutes cases into wrong KYC/collateral/committee handling, so A2 is a *quality* prerequisite for A3, A7, A8, and A9. A6 (normalization) is an explicit prerequisite of the credit model ("needed before credit model", DFG `Normalize -> Credit analysis: 5000`) and of A8 (committee packaging waits on normalized statements). A5 improves the input quality that A6 and A8 depend on by reducing the 52%-repeated missing-document loop. Consequently, **A1 alone is a benefit only if A2/A5/A6 keep pace** — otherwise A1 just enlarges downstream queues.

**Legal/compliance controls.** KYC clearance (A3) must precede Customer signing (A10) — provisional signing occurs in 6.5% of cases, 1.1% needing remediation, so A10 must hard-block on a verified KYC flag from A3. Contract terms must match the Committee decision — A9 must consume decision data directly and enforce terms-match, feeding A12 when mismatches arise (contract-remediation loops 8.5%, 14% for Credit-line renewal, DFG `Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue: 650`). Four-eyes: A8/A9 must not let the same identity that performed Credit analysis complete Committee packaging for High-exposure SME (violations 5.8% at month end). A11 (activation) must verify the full control set is closed before acting.

**Human escalation capacity.** A3 and A4 shift work rather than removing it: the KYC pre-classifier escalates residual hits to the **3-FTE compliance specialist** pool that the handover network already shows as a two-person dependency. A3 without A4 (and without added specialist capacity or triage) simply concentrates the hard 38%-rework entity-mismatch cases onto three people. Similarly, A1/A6 increase clean inflow to the **9-FTE analyst** pool; A6 must land *before or with* any inflow accelerator so normalization relief offsets the added volume.

**External valuation dependency.** A7 addresses the largest single wait (58.0h, `Wait for external valuation`), but the valuers are outside direct control. A7 can only triage/parallelize/route; it cannot compress the intrinsic partner SLA, and 19%-late deliveries feed A8's synchronization. A7's value is realized only for the case mix that needs it (71% of Equipment finance, 18% of Working-capital), which A2 must identify correctly.

**Core-banking integration constraints.** IT can integrate only **two modules deeply** in the first six months. The two deep integrations should go to modules that must write authoritative records into core banking and enforce controls at the source: strongest candidates are **A3** (KYC status is the gate for signing/packaging/activation) and **A9** (contract generation writing terms bound to the committee decision). A1, A5, A10 can run as lighter portal/RPA-style integrations; A11 should be deferred until its control preconditions (A3/A9/A12) are deeply integrated.

## 3. Portfolio alternatives

Each portfolio is at most four modules and satisfies the compliance rule that at least one first-wave module strengthens controls.

**Portfolio Q — Quick-win (politically attractive).** A1, A2, A5, A10.
- Optimizes: visible labor savings on high-frequency intake, cleaner routing, fewer document loops, faster signing adoption. Easy, lighter integrations; RM-friendly if A5 nudges are flexible.
- Sacrifices: does **not** relieve the true critical-path constraints (entity mismatch, external valuation, committee synchronization, analyst normalization). A1 risks flooding KYC/doc-review/analysts. No deep control gate at KYC, so provisional-signing and four-eyes exposures persist. Compliance rule is only weakly met (A2 reduces misroute but doesn't harden a legal control).

**Portfolio B — Bottleneck / synchronization.** A4, A6, A7, A8.
- Optimizes: the dominant waiting-time and synchronization drivers — entity-mismatch (31.0h, critical path 82%), analyst normalization (20.0h) and analyst overload relief, external valuation wait (58.0h), and committee-pack assembly (26.0h). Directly compresses end-to-end cycle time.
- Sacrifices: fewer headline "transactions automated" numbers; A8 is fragile without upstream A3 KYC status, and this portfolio lacks a hard KYC/signing/contract control module, so it under-delivers on the compliance-first-wave requirement unless A4's audit trail is treated as control-strengthening. Heavier reliance on the strained 3-FTE specialist and analyst pools during transition.

**Portfolio C — Compliance-first (recommended core).** A3, A4, A6, A9.
- Optimizes: the two hardest controls plus the two heaviest internal bottlenecks. A3 hardens the KYC gate (feeds A4, A10 precondition, packaging), A4 dissolves the worst critical-path wait, A6 relieves analyst load and unblocks credit analysis and packaging, A9 enforces terms-match-decision and cuts the 8.5%/14% remediation loops. Meets the compliance mandate with two genuine control modules (A3, A9) and fits the two-deep-integration limit exactly (A3 + A9).
- Sacrifices: the politically attractive high-frequency win (A1) is deferred; some external-wait relief (A7) and signing-loop relief (A10) wait until phase 2; upfront specialist-capacity planning is required so A3 escalations don't swamp the 3-FTE pool before A4 lands.

## 4. Expected process-level effects

**Portfolio Q (A1, A2, A5, A10).**
- Cycle time: modest net gain; signing wait (44.0h) shrinks via A10, document loop (`FDR -> Request missing documents -> FDR`, 3300/2800) shortens via A5, but critical-path waits at Resolve-entity-mismatch and Wait-for-external-valuation are untouched, capping improvement.
- Waiting time: A1 lowers the 1.5h register wait (small); A5 attacks the 8.0h missing-docs wait; A10 attacks 44.0h signing wait. No effect on the 31.0h/58.0h/26.0h synchronization waits.
- Rework: A2 reduces the 8% misroute and A5 reduces the 52% repeated missing-doc rate; entity-mismatch 38% and contract 8.5%/14% loops persist.
- Handovers: A1/A2 reduce back-office touches; A10 reduces RM reminder handovers.
- Resource demand: **risk of overload** — A1 accelerates inflow (`Receive -> Register -> Classify -> KYC/FDR`) into Compliance ops and the 9-FTE analysts without A6 relief.
- Control violations: minimal improvement; four-eyes (5.8%) and provisional-signing (6.5%) largely unaddressed since no hard KYC or contract gate.

**Portfolio B (A4, A6, A7, A8).**
- Cycle time: **large reduction** on the critical path — A4 attacks the 31.0h/38% entity-mismatch step (`KYC -> Resolve entity mismatch -> KYC`, 1750/1160), A7 parallelizes the 58.0h valuation wait, A6 shortens the 20.0h normalization step, A8 compresses the 26.0h committee-packaging synchronization.
- Waiting time: strongest of the three on the dominant queues; A8 shrinks the reconciliation wait once its inputs are ready.
- Rework: A6 cuts normalization defects (16%) feeding credit analysis; A4 cuts entity-mismatch reloops. A8 rework risk if upstream KYC/valuation inconsistent (GIGO).
- Handovers: A8 collapses multi-party artifact assembly (RM + analyst) into fewer handovers; A4 reduces specialist-to-KYC ping-pong.
- Resource demand: A6 protects the analyst pool; A4 concentrates residual hard cases on 3-FTE specialists — **capacity planning required**. Legal ops unaffected (still month-end spiky).
- Control violations: indirect only; without A3/A9 the KYC-precedes-signing and terms-match controls are not hardened. A8 must embed a four-eyes check to avoid the 5.8% High-exposure violation.

**Portfolio C (A3, A4, A6, A9) — recommended.**
- Cycle time: **large and durable reduction** — A4 removes the worst critical-path wait; A6 shortens normalization and unblocks credit analysis (`Normalize -> Credit analysis`, 5000) and packaging; A9 removes contract-remediation reloops (`Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue -> Contract preparation`, 650/510). A3 shortens KYC resolution and de-risks the signing gate.
- Waiting time: A3 reduces the 4.0h KYC wait and enables faster, safer signing; A4 attacks 31.0h; A6 attacks 20.0h; A9 attacks 22.0h. External 58.0h wait deferred to A7 in phase 2.
- Rework: A6 cuts 16% normalization defects and 24% doc-review loops indirectly; A9 cuts 8.5%/14% contract remediation; A3 cleaner KYC reduces downstream entity churn feeding A4.
- Handovers: A3 reduces manual KYC hit resolution; A9 reduces legal-ops rework handovers; A4 reduces specialist reloops.
- Resource demand: A6 shields the 9-FTE analysts; A3 shifts residual work to 3-FTE specialists — mitigated because A4 (in same wave) automates the hardest downstream mismatch cases. Legal ops smoothed away from month-end spikes by A9.
- Control violations: **strongest** — A3 provides the auditable KYC flag that hard-blocks provisional signing (6.5%→target near-zero), A9 enforces terms-match-decision and preserves the model-suggestion/override/final-decision audit trail; four-eyes enforceable via A9/packaging role checks.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

- **Auditability.** Every model (A2, A3, A6, A9) must log suggestion → human override → final decision (explicit control finding). Compensating control: immutable audit log integrated with core banking for A3/A9; periodic sampling review by compliance.
- **False negatives (A3 KYC).** Severe regulatory exposure. Compensating control: conservative thresholds, mandatory human escalation of ambiguous hits, no auto-clear on borderline scores, and a monitored false-negative rate with a hard rollback gate.
- **False positives (A2, A3, A5).** Misroutes and customer friction. Compensating control: human-in-the-loop confirmation for low-confidence classifications; A5 nudges kept flexible to avoid RM/customer resistance.
- **Model drift (A2, A6, A9 rule library).** Compensating control: scheduled revalidation, drift dashboards on classification accuracy and extraction quality, and a legal-owned change process for A9's rule library (stale rules = legal risk).
- **Exception handling / shifted workload.** Automating clean paths concentrates hard exceptions on scarce humans (3-FTE specialists, 9-FTE analysts). Compensating control: sequence A6 before/with any inflow accelerator; add specialist triage/capacity before scaling A3; keep A1 throttled until downstream can absorb volume.
- **Four-eyes (5.8% High-exposure, month-end).** Compensating control: A9/A8 enforce distinct-identity checks for Credit analysis vs Committee packaging on High-exposure SME; block completion on violation.
- **Provisional signing (6.5%) and terms mismatch (8.5%/14%).** Compensating control: A10 hard-blocks on A3 KYC flag; A9 blocks contracts whose terms diverge from the committee decision, routing to A12.
- **GIGO in committee pack (A8) and activation (A11).** Compensating control: A8 only assembles when required artifacts (KYC, normalized statements, valuation-if-required, analysis notes) pass validation; A11 refuses to activate unless all controls are closed.
- **External valuer quality disputes (A7).** Compensating control: keep human valuation sign-off; A7 routes/parallelizes only, with SLA and quality scorecards per valuer.

## 6. Recommended phased rollout (first year)

The two deep core-banking integrations in the first six months go to **A3** and **A9**.

**Phase 1 (months 1–6): control-and-bottleneck core — A3, A9 (deep), A6, A4 (lighter).**
- Order: (1) A6 first — relieves analysts and improves inputs so later inflow changes are safe; (2) A3 deep — establishes the auditable KYC gate; (3) A4 — attacks the worst critical-path wait, absorbing the exceptions A3 escalates; (4) A9 deep — enforces terms-match and cuts remediation loops.
- Monitoring metrics: KYC false-negative rate; entity-mismatch waiting time and 38% rework rate; normalization defect rate and analyst utilization; contract-remediation loop rate (target <8.5%, watch 14% renewals); provisional-signing rate; audit-log completeness.
- Gate criteria to proceed: A3 false-negative rate at/below agreed threshold with full audit trail; A6 extraction accuracy above threshold on real PDF mix; analyst utilization not exceeding capacity; A9 rule-library sign-off by legal.

**Phase 2 (months 7–12): flow and adoption — A10, A7, A5, then A1 (throttled).**
- Order: (1) A10 (hard-gated on A3 KYC flag) to cut the 44.0h signing wait; (2) A7 to parallelize the 58.0h external valuation wait; (3) A5 to reduce the 52% missing-doc loop with flexible nudges; (4) A1 last and throttled, only after confirming downstream (KYC, doc review, analysts) can absorb accelerated inflow.
- Monitoring metrics: signing wait and reminder-loop rate; external-valuation lateness (19%) and end-to-end time for collateral cases; missing-doc repeat rate; downstream queue lengths after A1 ramp.
- Gate criteria: A10 shows zero KYC-bypass signings; A7 shows reduced synchronization wait without quality-dispute spikes; A1 enabled only if downstream queue metrics stay within SLA (throttle/kill switch retained).

**Deferred beyond conditions met: A2 (as accuracy improves), A8 (once A3/A6/A7 stable), A12 (as A9 surfaces remediation volume), A11 (only after full control chain is deeply integrated).**

## 7. Final recommendation

**Recommend Portfolio C (A3, A4, A6, A9) as the first-year core**, rolled out per the phased plan, with Phase-2 flow modules (A10, A7, A5, A1-throttled) layered on.

Rationale and trade-off: the process-mining evidence shows end-to-end performance is governed by synchronization and control activities, not frequency. The largest waits and rework live at Resolve-entity-mismatch (31.0h, 38%, critical path 82%), Wait-for-external-valuation (58.0h), Customer-signing (44.0h), and Committee-packaging (26.0h), while the frequency leaders (Register 8 min/1.5h, Classify 12 min/2.0h) are cheap and low-wait. Portfolio C attacks the dominant internal bottleneck (A4), protects and unblocks the central analyst pool (A6), and hardens the two controls with real regulatory and legal exposure (A3 KYC gate, A9 terms-match) — satisfying the compliance mandate that a first-wave module strengthen controls, and fitting the two-deep-integration limit exactly (A3, A9).

**The highest-frequency activity (A1 auto-registration) should not be automated first.** Its own stated risk is that it exposes classification, KYC, and document-review bottlenecks; automating it first would accelerate inflow into the 3-FTE compliance-specialist and 9-FTE analyst constraints (both flagged in the social/working-together networks) and enlarge the very queues that dominate cycle time — a locally attractive but process-negative move. A1 is still worthwhile, but only in Phase 2, throttled, and after A6/A3/A4 make the downstream able to absorb it. This sequencing delivers a defensible process-level improvement (shorter critical path, fewer control violations, protected scarce resources) rather than a cosmetic labor-count win, and it keeps compliance responsibility with humans while giving them an auditable model-suggestion/override/final-decision trail.
