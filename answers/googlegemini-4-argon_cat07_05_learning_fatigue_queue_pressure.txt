# 2. Integrated explanation

A simplistic reading of the aggregate data suggests two seductively false narratives: first, that workers take an hour to "warm up" (driving service times down from 18.8 minutes to 12.9 minutes by mid-shift), and second, that workers become "fatigued" in the final two hours (causing error rates to triple from 2.5% to 7.9% and rework to hit 13.7%). Both conclusions fail when we examine the underlying operational dynamics. Notice the fundamental paradox in the aggregate table: in sequence positions 51 to 76+, service time *falls* to its fastest level of the day (10.8 minutes) while case complexity drops to its lowest level (0.43), yet errors and rework explode. Pure physiological fatigue typically slows workers down or degrades accuracy at constant load; it does not make workers process cases 40% faster while failing on easy cases.

Instead, the work period is governed by three distinct operational regimes driven by shifting case mix, resource selection, and queue dynamics:

**Phase 1: The Carryover & Triage Regime (Positions 1–15, Minutes 0–90)**
At the start of a shift—particularly the Early shift (clearing night exceptions, 48% high complexity) and Night shift (56% high complexity)—the queue is loaded with complex, high-priority carryover cases (mean complexity 0.72, priority 41%). Furthermore, Senior resources intentionally select these difficult backlog cases early in the shift. Because complex cases require nearly double the processing time of simple ones (22.4 min vs. 12.7 min at baseline) and carry double the intrinsic error risk (4.2% vs. 2.1%), early-shift aggregate service times (18.8 min) and error rates (3.4%) are artificially elevated. As this initial backlog is cleared across positions 6–30, complexity falls to 0.54. What looks like "cognitive learning/warm-up" in the aggregate data is primarily **case-mix normalization**, supplemented by a modest genuine warm-up among Seniors and Mid-level staff. Crucially, for Juniors, early exposure to complex cases causes high early-shift error rates (5.1%).

**Phase 2: The Steady-State Baseline (Positions 16–50, Minutes 90–300)**
In the middle of the shift, queue length stabilizes at low-to-moderate levels (31–33), and incoming cases reflect an average complexity mix (0.50–0.54). Under these unconstrained conditions, workers operate near their true capability baseline. Importantly, resource-level data proves that when WIP remains low (<30) later in the shift, **fatigue has almost zero impact on quality**: Senior error rates remain flat (2.1% early vs. 2.2% late low-WIP), Mid-level error rates are unchanged (2.8% vs. 2.9%), and Junior error rates actually *decline* (5.1% early vs. 4.4% late low-WIP) once they are no longer overwhelmed by early-shift complex carryover cases.

**Phase 3: The Queue-Pressure, Batching, and Automation-Strain Regime (Positions 51+, Minutes 300+)**
During the final third of the shift—most acutely in the Day shift (where end-of-shift queues swell to 75) and Late shift (inheriting a backlog of 70)—arrivals outpace capacity and mean queue length surges from 31 to 88. This triggers four compounding mechanisms:
1. **Queue-Pressure Speed-Up (Corner-Cutting):** Confronted with high WIP (>70), workers across all experience tiers accelerate to clear the queue. In low-complexity cases, service time is compressed from 12.7 minutes to 9.4 minutes; in high-complexity cases, it is slashed from 22.4 to 16.8 minutes. This rushing induces a severe speed-quality tradeoff: error rates in high-WIP, high-complexity cases reach 10.9%, and Junior error rates under high WIP surge to 12.4%. Even easy cases suffer anearly tripled error rate (5.8%) when rushed.
2. **Opportunistic Triage and Batching:** To survive the late-shift queue surge, workers cherry-pick easier cases (driving processed complexity down to 0.43 and high-priority share down to 19%) and adopt workarounds. Juniors (`Junior_H`, `Junior_I`) batch 9–18 consecutive `Document Review` events together late in the Day shift, logging ultra-short service times that pull down the late-shift average duration while increasing superficial review errors.
3. **Automation Interaction:** Under high queue lengths (>70), `Bot_Risk` continues instantaneous `Risk Classification`, but likely misclassifies edge-case claims or passes unvetted automated triage downstream, forcing hurried human agents to execute more frequent overrides and corrections precisely when human cognitive bandwidth is lowest.
4. **Measurement Artifacts from Deferred Quality Checks:** Because `Quality Check` activities are systematically deferred until the final 45 minutes of the shift, errors committed earlier in the afternoon are discovered and logged in a compressed end-of-shift burst, artificially inflating the apparent error and rework rate of the 76+ sequence bin.

# 3. Proposed analyses

To disentangle these mechanisms rigorously, we combine process mining in `pm4py` with econometric panel regressions.

### Analytical Design

1. **Within-Resource Comparisons & Within-Shift Position Effects:** Use resource fixed effects ($\alpha_r$) so each worker acts as their own control, eliminating confounding from baseline skill differences (`resource_experience_months`) or shift assignment (`Night_L` vs. `Senior_A`). Model `sequence_position_within_shift` using splines or categorical bins.
2. **WIP/Queue Stratification & Complexity Adjustment:** Explicitly interact `wip_at_start` bands (<30, 30–70, >70) with `case_complexity_score` and `priority` to separate intrinsic task difficulty from workload-induced rushing.
3. **Shift and Time-of-Day Controls:** Include both `shift_id` and wall-clock hour-of-day fixed effects alongside `minutes_since_shift_start`. Because Early (06:00–14:00), Day (09:00–17:00), and Late (14:00–22:00) shifts overlap, we can observe the exact same clock hour (e.g., 13:00 or 15:00) at different shift elapsed times (e.g., hour 7 of Early vs. hour 4 of Day), cleanly separating circadian effects from shift fatigue.
4. **Batching Detection:** Use `pm4py.discover_batches` to identify sequential, concurrent, and parallel batching per resource and activity. Flag batched events (`is_batched = 1`) and re-estimate late-shift speed and error models with and without batched `Document Review` and deferred `Quality Check` events.
5. **Resource-Experience Interactions:** Interact `resource_experience_months` (or tier: Senior/Mid/Junior) with `sequence_position_within_shift` and `wip_at_start` to test how experience buffers against early complexity and late queue pressure.
6. **Rework/Error Downstream Analysis:** Use `pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity` and performance DFGs (`pm4py.discover_performance_dfg`) to trace whether `error_flag` at a given sequence position leads to `Rework Correction` within 7 days, and trace the `Bot_Risk` $\rightarrow$ human correction pathway under high vs. low queues.

### PM4Py and Statistical Implementation

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
import statsmodels.api as sm
import statsmodels.formula.api as smf

# ---------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Ingestion, Formatting & Temporal Feature Extraction
# ---------------------------------------------------------
df = pd.read_csv("claims_event_log.csv")

# Format dataframe for PM4Py standard column conventions
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
)

# Compute event/case service and waiting times, plus arrival/finish rates
df = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df)
df = pm4py.insert_case_arrival_finish_rate(df)
temporal_features = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(df)

# Derive event-level service time in minutes and wall-clock hour
df["service_time_min"] = (
    df["time:timestamp"] - df["start_timestamp"]
).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
df["clock_hour"] = df["start_timestamp"].dt.hour

# Define categorical bands matching operational regimes
df["seq_bin"] = pd.cut(
    df["sequence_position_within_shift"],
    bins=[0, 5, 15, 30, 50, 75, np.inf],
    labels=["1-5", "6-15", "16-30", "31-50", "51-75", "76+"],
)
df["wip_band"] = pd.cut(
    df["wip_at_start"],
    bins=[-1, 29, 70, np.inf],
    labels=["Low (<30)", "Med (30-70)", "High (>70)"],
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Batching Detection & Visual Diagnostics
# ---------------------------------------------------------
# Discover batching behavior (e.g., Junior_H / Junior_I in Document Review)
batches = pm4py.discover_batches(df)
for activity_res, batch_info in batches[:10]:
    print(f"Batch detected: {activity_res} -> {batch_info}")

# Export Dotted Chart (reveals batching stripes and end-of-shift Quality Check bursts)
pm4py.save_vis_dotted_chart(
    df,
    "dotted_chart_shifts.png",
    attributes=["start_timestamp", "org:resource", "concept:name"],
)

# Export Events Distribution over wall-clock hours and day of week
pm4py.save_vis_events_distribution_graph(
    df, "dist_hour.png", distr_type="hours"
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Rework, Automation Interaction & Path Performance
# ---------------------------------------------------------
# Quantify rework cases per activity
rework_dict = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(df)
print("Rework cases per activity:", rework_dict)

# Compare Performance DFG under Low Queue vs High Queue to isolate Bot_Risk overrides
df_low_q = df[df["queue_length_at_start"] <= 30]
df_high_q = df[df["queue_length_at_start"] > 70]

dfg_low_perf, sa_low, ea_low = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(df_low_q)
dfg_high_perf, sa_high, ea_high = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(df_high_q)

print(
    "Low Queue Risk->Rework transition:",
    dfg_low_perf.get(("Risk Classification", "Rework Correction")),
)
print(
    "High Queue Risk->Rework transition:",
    dfg_high_perf.get(("Risk Classification", "Rework Correction")),
)

# Filter specific high-risk paths or slow cases for root-cause inspection
bot_cases = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df, "org:resource", ["Bot_Risk"], level="case"
)
slow_cases = pm4py.filter_case_performance(df, min_performance=3600, max_performance=86400)

# Cluster log to identify distinct process variants (e.g., complex referrals vs fast-track)
clusters = pm4py.cluster_log(df)

# ---------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Econometric Disentanglement (Human Resources Only)
# ---------------------------------------------------------
df_human = df[df["org:resource"] != "Bot_Risk"].copy()

# Flag suspicious sequential batching (same resource, same activity, gap < 30s, service_time < 2m)
df_human = df_human.sort_values(["org:resource", "start_timestamp"])
df_human["prev_act"] = df_human.groupby("org:resource")["concept:name"].shift(1)
df_human["gap_sec"] = (
    df_human["start_timestamp"]
    - df_human.groupby("org:resource")["time:timestamp"].shift(1)
).dt.total_seconds()
df_human["is_batched"] = (
    (df_human["concept:name"] == df_human["prev_act"])
    & (df_human["gap_sec"].between(0, 30))
    & (df_human["service_time_min"] < 2.0)
).astype(int)

# Model A: Log Service Time (Within-Resource FE, Shift/Clock Controls, WIP x Complexity, Batching)
mod_speed = smf.ols(
    formula="np.log(service_time_min) ~ C(seq_bin) * C(wip_band) "
    "+ case_complexity_score * C(wip_band) + C(priority) + is_batched "
    "+ C(concept_name) + C(shift_id) + C(clock_hour) + C(org_resource)",
    data=df_human.rename(
        columns={"concept:name": "concept_name", "org:resource": "org_resource"}
    ),
).fit(
    cov_type="cluster",
    cov_kwds={"groups": df_human["org:resource"]},
)
print(mod_speed.summary())

# Model B: Error Rate (Logistic Regression testing Fatigue vs Queue Pressure by Experience)
mod_error = smf.glm(
    formula="error_flag ~ C(seq_bin) * C(wip_band) * resource_experience_months "
    "+ case_complexity_score + C(priority) + is_batched "
    "+ C(concept_name) + C(shift_id) + C(clock_hour)",
    data=df_human.rename(columns={"concept:name": "concept_name"}),
    family=sm.families.Binomial(),
).fit()
print(mod_error.summary())
```

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

Using raw event-log metrics to evaluate, rank, or discipline individual workers in this claims center is both statistically invalid and operationally destructive for five reasons:

1. **Endogenous Selection Bias and Role Specialization:** Work is not randomly assigned. Seniors proactively pull complex carryover cases at the start of shifts (`case_complexity_score` = 0.72), while Night staff handle urgent exceptions (56% high-complexity share). Conversely, late-shift workers facing massive queues select simpler tasks or batch routine reviews to survive the backlog. Comparing raw service times or error rates across workers without conditioning on `case_complexity_score`, `priority`, and `activity_family` punishes workers who take on the hardest cases.
2. **Measurement Error and Attribution Lag:** The event log contains severe timing and attribution artifacts. Because `Quality Check` is systematically delayed until the last 45 minutes of the shift, errors committed at 11:00 or 13:00 may be flagged and logged at 16:30, creating an artificial spike in late-shift error rates (`sequence_position_within_shift` 76+). Furthermore, when `Junior_H` and `Junior_I` batch 9–18 `Document Review` events, `start_timestamp` and `time:timestamp` reflect system logging bursts rather than true cognitive processing time.
3. **Management-Induced Queue Pressure:** Workers do not control claim arrival rates or shift staffing levels. The surge in error rates in the final two hours of the Day shift (7.5%) and Late shift (6.8%) is driven by `wip_at_start` exceeding 70—a systemic capacity deficit created by management scheduling. Holding workers punitively accountable for errors committed when management fails to staff for predictable afternoon demand peaks is profoundly unfair.
4. **Automation-Induced Vulnerability (`Bot_Risk`):** `Bot_Risk` logs zero service time and zero "human" fatigue, yet its classifications require substantially more human correction when `queue_length_at_start > 70`. When a human agent has to override or fix a flawed `Bot_Risk` classification under heavy queue pressure, the resulting longer duration or downstream `Rework Correction` is logged against the human agent's case record, masking automation degradation as human failure.
5. **Perverse Incentives from Punitive Error Metrics:** If management penalizes raw late-shift error rates (Intervention 8) or pressures workers on raw service time, agents will game the system: they will avoid complex/high-priority claims, defer difficult `Eligibility Decision` events to the next shift, under-report exceptions during `Quality Check`, or cherry-pick easy documents. Quality and throughput metrics must be used as **systemic diagnostic signals** to fix staffing and routing, never as raw punitive weapons.

# 6. Monitoring dashboard specification

To manage the claims center without falling prey to aggregation traps, the process-mining dashboard must report Complexity- and WIP-Adjusted metrics across **10 core indicators**:

| # | Indicator Name | Definition & PM4Py / Event-Log Computation | Granularity & Stratification | Action Threshold / Alert Rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Complexity-Adjusted Service Time** | Mean and median event duration (`pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time`: `time:timestamp - start_timestamp`) divided by expected duration for its `case_complexity_score` decile and `activity_family`. | By `shift_id`, `sequence_position_within_shift` bin, and `wip_band`. | **Speed-Up Alert:** Ratio < 0.75 when `wip_at_start > 70` (signals corner-cutting/rushing). |
| **2** | **Priority-Stratified Waiting Time** | Queue waiting time prior to `Initial Triage` and `Eligibility Decision` (`start_timestamp` of event minus previous event `time:timestamp` or case arrival). | By `priority` (High vs. Normal) and `shift_id`. | **SLA Breach Risk:** High-priority waiting time > 30 min during shift handover windows. |
| **3** | **Real-Time WIP & Queue Pressure Index** | Active `wip_at_start` and `queue_length_at_start` juxtaposed against active human capacity (`pm4py.insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` arrival-to-finish ratio). | Real-time 15-minute rolling window by `shift_id`. | **Amber Alert:** WIP > 50. **Red Capacity Trigger:** WIP > 70 (deploys floating Senior reviewer). |
| **4** | **True Error Rate (Origin-Timestamped)** | Share of events with `error_flag == 1`, attributed to the *timestamp and sequence position of the originating decision activity*, not the timestamp of the late `Quality Check`. | By `wip_band` x `case_complexity_score` x experience tier. | **Quality Alert:** Origin-adjusted error rate > 4.0% in Low/Med WIP or > 6.0% in High WIP. |
| **5** | **7-Day Downstream Rework Rate** | Percentage of cases entering `Rework Correction` or repeating an activity within 7 days (`pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity`). | By originating `activity_family`, `shift_id`, and `wip_band` at decision time. | **Systemic Rework Alert:** 7-day rework > 8.0% for any shift or > 10% for High-WIP periods. |
| **6** | **Case-Mix & Carryover Complexity Index** | Rolling mean of `case_complexity_score` and share of `priority == 'High'` entering the queue vs. being selected by workers. | By hour of shift (Hour 1 vs. Hours 2–6 vs. Hours 7–8) and resource tier. | **Backlog Composition Alert:** First-hour high-complexity share > 45% (triggers Senior triage pairing). |
| **7** | **Sequential Batching & Deferred QC Index** | Share of events flagged by `pm4py.discover_batches` (e.g., $\ge 5$ consecutive identical activities with service time < 2 min) plus the ratio of `Quality Check` volume in the last 45 minutes vs. shift average. | By `org:resource`, `concept:name` (`Document Review`, `Quality Check`), and shift hour. | **Workaround Alert:** Consecutive batch run $\ge 8$ events or > 35% of shift's `Quality Check` events logged in final 45 minutes. |
| **8** | **Cross-Shift Handover & Queue Ownership Churn** | Net queue delta across shift boundaries ($\text{Queue}_{\text{end}} - \text{Queue}_{\text{start}}$) and count of cases touched by $\ge 2$ shifts or handed back across resources. | By shift transition (Night$\rightarrow$Early, Early$\rightarrow$Day, Day$\rightarrow$Late, Late$\rightarrow$Night). | **Handover Bottleneck:** End-of-shift queue > 60 or > 25% of carryover cases having `case_complexity_score > 0.70`. |
| **9** | **Automation Override & Exception Rate (`Bot_Risk`)** | Frequency with which a case processed by `Bot_Risk` (`Risk Classification`) requires subsequent human reclassification, `Specialist Referral`, or `Rework Correction` (`pm4py.discover_performance_dfg`). | Stratified by `queue_length_at_start` (<30, 30–70, >70) and `case_complexity_score`. | **Bot Degradation Alert:** Human override rate of `Bot_Risk` increases by > 25% when `queue_length_at_start > 70`. |
| **10** | **Complexity-Weighted Worker Load Fairness** | Cumulative complexity-weighted work units ($\sum \text{service\_time}_{\text{expected}}(\text{complexity}, \text{activity})$) assigned per resource per shift, plus exposure time to High WIP (>70). | By `org:resource` and experience group (Senior, Mid, Junior, Night). | **Injustice / Burnout Alert:** Any Junior resource absorbing > 35% high-complexity cases in Hour 1, or any worker spending > 120 minutes/shift in WIP > 70. |
