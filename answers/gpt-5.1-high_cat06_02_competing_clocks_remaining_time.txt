## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

### Scenario 1 – Smooth path (no rerun, no contamination, no QA rework)

- **Chemistry waiting and service**
  - At now (Fri 18 Sep 15:30) there are one urgent and three ordinary samples ahead of B‑417, with two analysts working until 18:00.
  - Remaining work on current samples: ≈0.4–0.9 h for one analyst and ≈1.2–2.0 h for the other. Even if B‑417 is treated as high priority after the urgent sample, total remaining Friday capacity after clearing current work is only about 2.5–3 analyst‑hours.
  - Given a triangular chemistry service time of 1.5–4.5 h with mode 2.5 h, it is very unlikely that B‑417 both starts and finishes before 18:00; realistically it either does not start or receives only partial processing.
  - Over the weekend the chemistry lab is closed. Any unfinished work for B‑417 resumes **Mon 21 Sep at 08:00**.
  - Assuming B‑417 is prioritized on Monday among the remaining backlog, it likely starts between **08:00 and 09:30**, finishes service between roughly **10:00 and 12:30**, and has some short reporting/validation lag. Chemistry result availability in this smooth scenario is therefore **Mon ~11:00–13:00**, with an optimistic edge case around **09:30–10:30** if much of the work was already done Friday.

- **Microbiology incubation and readout**
  - Primary incubation started Fri 14:00 and becomes technically ready 46–54 h later, i.e. between **Sun ~12:00 and Sun ~20:00**.
  - Without weekend on‑call (the modal case), no microbiologist is available to read until **Mon 08:00**. The first readout plus recording then takes **0.5–1.5 h**, giving microbiology completion around **Mon 08:30–09:30**, perhaps up to **≈10:00–10:30** if there is some internal queue.
  - If management authorizes on‑call over the weekend (≈0.25–0.45 probability), a Sunday readout 1–3 h after technical readiness would give a microbiology result somewhere between **Sun ~13:00 and Sun ~23:00**. In that subcase, microbiology finishes *before* chemistry, so the join is chemistry‑dominated.

- **Parallel join time**
  - Parallel completion time is the **maximum** of chemistry and microbiology finish times, not the sum.
  - Under the no‑contamination, no‑on‑call baseline, microbiology completes around **Mon 08:30–09:30**, while chemistry completes around **Mon 11:00–13:00**. The join is thus governed by chemistry: **Mon ~11:00–13:00**.
  - In on‑call micro scenarios, microbiology finishes Sunday; the maximum is then chemistry on Monday, so the join is still **Mon late morning to early afternoon**.

- **QA waiting and service**
  - QA operates Mon–Fri 08:00–20:00 with two reviewers from Monday 08:00 onward. At the join time (late Monday morning/lunchtime), B‑417 joins a queue whose size is uncertain due to weekend backlog and arrivals at ≈0.35 batches/h.
  - With median QA service ≈1.5 h and an 80th percentile ≈3.5 h, two reviewers have capacity ≈1 batch/h, leaving moderate but not extreme congestion.
  - For a high‑priority sterile batch, a reasonable central assumption is QA waiting of **≈0.5–2 h**, with a conservative tail up to **≈3–4 h** on a busy day.
  - QA service itself then takes **≈1.5–3 h** in this smooth path (no query or investigation), so typical QA start around **Mon 12:00–15:00** gives QA completion around **Mon 14:00–18:00**.

- **Optional rework/investigation**
  - In this scenario, we condition on **no documentation query** and **no deviation investigation**, recognizing that this covers only a subset of paths.
  - Under those conditions, QA does not send the batch out for rework; the first QA pass ends in acceptance.

- **Final release**
  - Electronic Release is available continuously and takes **0.25–0.75 h** after QA acceptance.
  - With QA acceptance around **Mon 14:00–18:00**, Electronic Release occurs around **Mon 14:30–18:45**, with a central window **Mon 15:00–18:00**.

### Scenario 2 – Chemistry rerun dominant

- **Chemistry waiting and first-run service**
  - The initial waiting and first run follow the same pattern as Scenario 1, giving a first chemistry result (borderline) around **Mon ~11:00–14:00**.
  - In borderline cases (≈0.12–0.22 overall, higher if the temperature excursion is confirmed), a **rerun** is required. Reruns are prioritized and have triangular service time 1.0–3.0 h with mode 1.8 h.
  - If the first run completes by **≈14:00–15:00** on Monday, the rerun can often be started almost immediately and has a good chance of finishing before the **18:00** lab close; if the first run completes later (e.g. **≈16:00–17:00**), part of the rerun is pushed to **Tue 08:00 onward**.

- **Chemistry rerun completion**
  - **Favorable subcase:** First run ends ≈12:00, rerun starts ≈12:30, finishes ≈14:00–15:30, with result consolidation shortly afterward. Chemistry is then complete **Mon ~15:00–16:30**.
  - **Less favorable subcase:** First run ends ≈15:30–16:30, rerun starts late afternoon, reaches closure with ≈0.5–1.5 h remaining, and resumes Tue 08:00. Final result then appears **Tue ~09:00–11:00**.

- **Microbiology branch**
  - In this scenario we assume **no contamination indication**; the microbiology path behaves as in Scenario 1, completing no later than early Monday.
  - Therefore, microbiology is **not** the dominant clock; chemistry rerun is.

- **Parallel join time**
  - The join is at the **maximum** of microbiology and (possibly rerun) chemistry completion times.
  - In favorable subcases, chemistry completes **Mon 15:00–16:30**, so the join is late Monday afternoon.
  - In less favorable subcases, the join is delayed until chemistry finishes on Tuesday morning, i.e. **Tue 09:00–11:00**.

- **QA waiting, service, and rework**
  - B‑417 reaches QA either late Monday or Tuesday morning.
  - **If late Monday:** QA is open until 20:00 with two reviewers. A high‑priority batch arriving ≈16:00 can still be started the same day. Waiting time ≈0.5–2 h and service ≈1.5–3 h give QA completion **Mon 18:00–20:00**, with Electronic Release spilling slightly beyond that (to ≈20:30 at the latest).
  - **If early Tuesday:** queueing conditions are similar to Monday, but with an extra day of arrivals; waiting ≈1–3 h and service ≈1.5–3 h put QA completion roughly **Tue 13:00–17:00**.
  - For this scenario we still condition on no documentation query or deviation investigation; those paths fall under Scenario 4 or Scenario 5.

- **Final release**
  - Favorable path: **Mon 21 Sep ~19:00–20:30**.
  - Central rerun‑dominated path: **Tue 22 Sep ~14:00–18:00**.
  - Slow‑queue tail: **Wed 23 Sep morning to early afternoon**, if chemistry finishes late Tuesday and QA is congested.

### Scenario 3 – Microbiology contamination / confirmation dominant

- **Chemistry branch**
  - Chemistry behaves similarly to Scenario 1: first run finishes Monday late morning to early afternoon, with reruns **not** triggered in this scenario (we are focusing on cases where microbiology is the primary driver).
  - Chemistry therefore typically completes **by Mon ~13:00**, and is not on the critical path.

- **Primary microbiology incubation and first readout**
  - Primary incubation completes technically on Sunday between **12:00 and 20:00**, but without weekend on‑call the first readout occurs **Mon 08:00–10:00**, as in Scenario 1.
  - At that first readout, a contamination indication is observed (probability roughly 0.08–0.12 overall, higher if the temperature excursion is later confirmed).

- **Confirmatory incubation and second readout**
  - The confirmation phase adds **20–30 h** of continuous incubation plus another **0.5–1.5 h** of readout and recording once a microbiologist is again available.
  - If contamination is seen early Monday (≈08:30–09:30), additional incubation runs until **Tue ~04:30–15:30**.
  - Microbiologists are staffed Mon–Fri 08:00–18:00; thus:
    - If confirmatory readiness occurs in the early hours (e.g. **Tue 04:30–08:00**), readout waits until **Tue 08:00**, finishing ≈**08:30–09:30**.
    - If readiness occurs during the day (e.g. **Tue 09:00–15:30**), readout can happen the same day, completing ≈**Tue 09:30–17:00**.
  - With weekend on‑call and an earlier first readout (e.g. Sunday), confirmatory incubation and second readout may shift forward by ≈12–24 h, but in many of those cases the join is still at or after Tuesday because chemistry and QA calendars constrain earlier steps.

- **Parallel join time**
  - Chemistry is complete by late Monday, so the join is determined by the **maximum** of chemistry and the confirmatory microbiology timeline, which is now microbiology.
  - Central join time is therefore **Tue 08:30–16:00**, with an optimistic edge around **Tue ~08:30–10:00** and a pessimistic edge around **Tue ~16:00–17:00**.

- **QA waiting and service**
  - QA sees B‑417 on Tuesday daytime.
  - With two reviewers and ongoing arrivals, a central waiting time of **≈1–3 h** is reasonable, with longer waits (4–5 h) in busier states.
  - QA service remains ≈**1.5–3 h**. Thus, QA acceptance typically falls between **Tue 14:00 and 20:00**, with earlier cases (short wait, short service) around **Tue 12:00–14:00**.

- **Optional rework/investigation**
  - This scenario is defined without additional QA documentation queries or investigations, though in practice contamination and/or a confirmed temperature excursion would **increase** investigation probabilities and push the case toward Scenario 5.

- **Final release**
  - Adding **0.25–0.75 h** for Electronic Release, terminal times concentrate around **Tue 22 Sep ~16:00–21:00**, with plausible early completions around **Tue ~12:30–14:30** and late-but-still-typical ones around **Wed 23 Sep ~10:00–12:00** if QA is delayed.

### Scenario 4 – QA documentation query or documentation-only investigation tail

Here the parallel branches are broadly smooth (no contamination and no major chemistry delay), but QA rework dominates.

- **Chemistry and microbiology**
  - Chemistry and microbiology behave as in Scenario 1, giving a parallel join around **Mon 11:00–13:00**.
  - QA receives initial results Monday afternoon.

- **Initial QA waiting and first pass**
  - B‑417 joins QA Monday lunchtime or early afternoon.
  - Initial wait plus service are similar to Scenario 1: QA may begin work on the batch **Mon ~13:00–16:00** and complete the **first pass** within ≈**1.5–3 h**, i.e. by **Mon ~15:00–18:00**.

- **Documentation query path**
  - With probability ≈0.15–0.30, QA issues a documentation query, adding **4–12 working hours** before QA can resume.
  - Working hours are Mon–Fri 08:00–20:00 and Sat 09:00–13:00.
  - Example timing:
    - If the query is raised **Mon 16:00**, the remaining Monday working window is 4 h (16:00–20:00). A short query (4–6 h) might be resolved by **Tue mid‑morning**; a long one (10–12 h) might require **Tue plus part of Wed**.
    - After documentation is supplied, B‑417 re‑enters QA’s active workload and receives another ≈**1.5–3 h** of QA review before acceptance.
  - Typical query-driven completions therefore fall between **Tue 22 Sep afternoon** and **Wed 23 Sep late afternoon**, depending on how the 4–12 working hours map into the QA calendar and how much extra waiting occurs when B‑417 re‑joins the queue.

- **Documentation-only deviation investigation path**
  - If a deviation investigation is opened (probability ≈0.04–0.10 when both test branches are normal, higher when anomalies exist), ≈60–80% are documentation‑only, adding **8–20 working hours**.
  - For an investigation opened late Monday:
    - 4 h of Monday working time (16:00–20:00) count toward the 8–20 h.
    - The remaining 4–16 h spill into **Tue and Wed**, potentially into **Thu** at the upper end.
  - Following completion of the documentation work, QA needs a fresh review slot (1.5–3 h), so final acceptance commonly occurs **Wed–Thu**, with tails into **Fri**.

- **Final release**
  - Adding **0.25–0.75 h** for Electronic Release, documentation‑driven tails produce terminal events mostly between **Tue afternoon and Fri evening**, with the central mass around **Wed 23 Sep**.

### Scenario 5 – Coupled worst-case (excursion confirmed, rerun, contamination, lab investigation)

- **Chemistry and microbiology**
  - A confirmed temperature excursion simultaneously raises the chance of a borderline chemistry result and a microbiology contamination indication.
  - It is therefore plausible that **both** a chemistry rerun and a microbiology confirmatory incubation occur, with both branches already delayed into Tuesday.

- **Deviation investigation and lab work**
  - Under these conditions, the probability of opening a deviation investigation rises to ≈0.30–0.55.
  - A sizable minority (≈20–40%) of investigations become **lab‑based**, adding **3–7 calendar days** of additional laboratory work and analysis, often with intermittent QA involvement.
  - Because lab investigations are not restricted to QA working hours, the added days are roughly calendar days, but QA’s acceptance and final decision are again constrained by its opening hours.

- **Join, QA, and terminal event**
  - By the time the deviation investigation is opened, both primary test branches are already complete; the investigation becomes the **dominant clock**.
  - Adding 3–7 calendar days on top of a base completion around Tue–Wed yields terminal events from approximately **Fri 25 Sep** through the following week (early October), depending on investigation complexity and QA queueing.
  - Some of these paths end in **Electronic Release** with a well-documented deviation; a small fraction may end in **Batch Rejected**, which adds only a modest extra 0.5–1.0 h after the critical defect is confirmed but is embedded in a very long preceding timeline.

## 4. Forecast summary

- **Plausible earliest completion**
  - Requires: chemistry largely completed on Friday or early Monday, relatively fast microbiology (possibly with weekend on‑call but not needed), no reruns or contamination, light QA queue, and no QA rework.
  - A defensible earliest window for Electronic Release is **Mon 21 Sep ~11:00–13:00**, corresponding to roughly **≈67–69 h** remaining from Friday 15:30.

- **Central / P50-like remaining-time range**
  - Combining the relatively high probability of the smooth path with moderate chances of either a chemistry rerun **or** a short QA query, a central “most likely” completion window is **Mon 21 Sep ~15:00 through Tue 22 Sep ~12:00**.
  - This corresponds to approximately **≈72–93 h** of remaining time.

- **P80-like remaining-time range**
  - To capture ≈80% of probability mass, we include common rerun cases, microbiology contamination with efficient handling, and typical QA documentation queries or doc-only investigations.
  - An 80%-style band is **Mon 21 Sep ~15:00 through Wed 23 Sep ~18:00**, i.e. about **≈72–123 h** from now.

- **P90-like / severe-tail range**
  - The 90% and more extreme tails are driven by (a) microbiology contamination plus confirmatory incubation with slow calendars, (b) longer QA documentation or deviation investigations, especially lab-based ones.
  - A conservative severe-tail window is **Wed 23 Sep through Fri 25 Sep** for documentation-driven tails, extending to **≈1.5–2 weeks from now** (late Sep to early Oct) when a full lab investigation is opened and proceeds slowly; this corresponds to roughly **≈120–340 h** of remaining time.

- **Most plausible terminal event(s)**
  - Across all scenarios, the **most plausible terminal event** is **Electronic Release of B‑417**, with the bulk of its probability mass between **late Monday and late Wednesday**.
  - **Batch Rejected** is a low-probability but non-negligible tail outcome, concentrated in the same very long paths that involve both confirmed temperature excursion and a lab-based deviation investigation.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

1. **Outcome of the temperature-excursion flag**  
   - **Type:** Dependence uncertainty / path uncertainty.  
   - **Role:** Whether the excursion is confirmed strongly modulates both borderline chemistry probability and microbiology contamination probability, and therefore the likelihood of chem reruns, extended incubations, and deviation investigations. It also couples these events, so that “both bad things happen” is materially more likely than under naive independence.

2. **Microbiology contamination and confirmatory incubation**  
   - **Type:** Path uncertainty + calendar uncertainty.  
   - **Role:** Contamination indications (especially under a confirmed excursion) trigger 20–30 h of extra incubation plus a second readout subject to weekday staffing, easily shifting completion from late Monday to late Tuesday or Wednesday. This is one of the primary drivers of multi-day delays.

3. **QA documentation queries and documentation-only investigations**  
   - **Type:** Path uncertainty + working-calendar uncertainty.  
   - **Role:** Queries (4–12 working hours) and doc-only investigations (8–20 working hours) can add 1–3 QA working days, especially when triggered late in the day. Because they are bound to QA’s opening hours, their impact on calendar time is highly sensitive to when the first QA pass occurs.

4. **Chemistry reruns and queue discipline**  
   - **Type:** Path uncertainty + queue uncertainty + service-time uncertainty.  
   - **Role:** A borderline chemistry result under uncertain queue discipline can push completion from Monday midday into late Monday or Tuesday. Whether the rerun fits before the 18:00 closure or spills into Tuesday morning depends on both service times and how aggressively B‑417 is prioritized.

5. **QA queue length and arrival overdispersion at the join time**  
   - **Type:** Queue uncertainty.  
   - **Role:** The number and priority of other batches at the moment both test results are available determine whether B‑417 sees ≈0.5–1 h of QA waiting or something closer to 3–5 h. Overdispersed arrivals around weekends and daytime peaks mean that two batches with identical branch timings can finish several hours apart purely because of QA congestion.

## 6. Most forecast-changing next event

The **single most forecast-changing next event** would be clarification of the **temperature-excursion flag**—specifically, whether engineering confirms or rules out the excursion.

- **If the excursion is ruled out (flag cleared):**
  - Borderline chemistry and microbiology contamination probabilities drop toward the lower ends of their bands.
  - The likelihood of needing chemistry reruns, extended microbiology incubation, and deviation investigations decreases substantially.
  - The forecast would shift mass toward the **smooth path and short QA-query paths**, tightening the central completion window around **late Monday to Tuesday morning** and reducing the probability of completions beyond mid‑week.

- **If the excursion is confirmed:**
  - Both borderline chemistry and contamination probabilities move toward the top of their bands, and the events become materially more correlated.
  - The chance that at least one branch requires rework (and that QA opens a deviation investigation) increases, pushing probability mass from Monday–Tuesday into **Tuesday–Thursday and beyond**, and thickening the tail scenarios up to and including full lab investigations and possible rejection.

A **secondary update trigger** would be a firm decision on **weekend microbiology on‑call authorization for B‑417**.  
If on‑call is explicitly authorized, scenarios with Sunday microbiology readouts become more likely, making chemistry and QA the dominant clocks and shifting mass modestly toward **earlier Monday–Tuesday completions**; if on‑call is explicitly declined, paths with delayed initial readouts and microbiology‑driven joins gain probability, particularly in contamination scenarios.
