## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Small randomized/quasi-random routing pilot: assign 15–20% of eligible Medium/High cases to Orion or Nova, overriding standard routing, stratified by `shift` and `compliance_required` | Direct causal effect of `resource_group` isolated from selection | Individual case (eligible pool defined by `complexity`, `shift`, `compliance`) | Maintain certified reviewer minimum in each group; exclude emergency/legal-hold cases; cap pilot volume; monitor queue depth in real time | Group-specific DFGs and variant comparison; `wait_before_review_h`, `review_service_h`, `rework_count`, `cycle_time_h` distributions; conformance rates | Produces unbiased conditional contrast of group assignment, separating routing selection from performance | Roll back if mean `queue_wait` in pilot pool exceeds baseline by >20% for 2 consecutive weeks, `rework` rate exceeds 35%, or certified overtime exceeds 10% |
| 2 | Queue and compliance capacity intervention: add temporary compliance-check reviewer during high-load night shifts; adjust queue priority for compliance-required cases; do not change group assignment rules | Queue wait and compliance bottleneck (mediator), not the group label | Shift-block or compliance-step (night/high-load periods) | Use existing staff pool; no change to certification requirements; maintain audit trail | Queue-state reconstruction (`wait_before_review_h` by `load`/`shift`); throughput per shift; trace variant frequency with/without `Request Info`; DFG comparison pre/post | Tests whether observed group differences shrink when capacity is equalized; distinguishes capacity effects from reviewer skill | Halt if `cycle_time` in target shift-block increases relative to control block, or staff utilization exceeds safe threshold |
| 3 | Observational selection correction and quality linkage: expand data collection to include open/abandoned cases and reviewer IDs; link 30-day outcomes to full population | Selection bias (`case_completed` collider) and quality-outcome pathway | All incoming cases (observational, no routing change) | No routing change; enforce data governance/privacy controls | Conformance including incomplete traces; standardized quality/appeal rates by `complexity`/`group`; abandonment-rate analysis by `group`/`complexity`; reviewer-level service distributions | Quantifies bias from conditioning on completed cases; validates whether 30-day appeal is influenced by group independent of case characteristics | Halt data integration if quality issues, missing fields >10%, or privacy breaches occur |

**Final recommendation (120–200 words)**

Do not approve the proposed 50% reassignment of Orion cases to Nova. The decision relies on a crude aggregate comparison that reverses after accounting for complexity, compliance, and shift. Standardized analysis shows Orion is actually faster within comparable case strata, likely because its certified staff and night-shift capacity reduce queue waits despite handling more complex, compliance-heavy work. Moving half of this workload to Nova—without addressing capacity, certification mix, or shift dynamics—risks bottlenecks, longer queues for high-complexity cases, and increased rework.

Instead, proceed in three phases. First, run Phase 1: a small, stratified routing pilot that randomly assigns eligible medium and high-complexity cases across groups, measuring wait, service, rework, and trace conformance. This isolates the direct group effect from selection. Second, implement Phase 2: augment compliance-check staffing and queue management during high-load night periods without changing group labels. If wait differences shrink, the mechanism is capacity, not group identity. Third, launch Phase 3: collect open/abandoned case registries and reviewer-level identifiers to correct selection bias and link quality outcomes properly. Only if the pilot consistently estimates a direct harmful effect—after controlling for shift, load, and compliance—should structural reassignment be considered. Until then, report standardized, not crude, metrics to the committee, and treat resource group as a bundled operational mechanism, not a standalone treatment.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Selection into the completed-case sample.** We observe only finished cases; if `resource_group` or `complexity` affects abandonment or open-case duration, all comparisons are biased. *Most valuable reduction:* A full registry of open, abandoned, and completed cases with `resource_group`, `complexity`, and `shift`, enabling inverse-probability weighting or selection models.

2. **Reviewer-level skill and assignment heterogeneity.** `resource_group` bundles certification levels, individual reviewers, and shift coverage; without reviewer IDs, we cannot separate skill from group label. *Most valuable reduction:* Reviewer-level identifiers and certification flags linked to each case.

3. **Dynamic queue-state and load conditions.** `arrival_load` is measured only at arrival; actual queue dynamics, staffing changes, and night-shift queue behavior are unobserved. *Most valuable reduction:* Timestamped event logs with precise queue-entry and queue-exit events (not just case-level aggregates).

4. **Compliance-check execution quality and duration.** We only observe whether compliance was required, not its actual duration, errors, or repetitions. *Most valuable reduction:* Event-level compliance-step timestamps with pass/fail/repeat outcomes.

5. **Causal pathway from review actions to the 30-day quality outcome.** The appeal indicator is coarse, post-process, and sparse; it may be influenced by customer behavior or external factors rather than review quality alone. *Most valuable reduction:* Structured follow-up data linking specific review actions (`rework`, `Request Info` content) to appeal reasons, collected prospectively for all cases including open ones.
