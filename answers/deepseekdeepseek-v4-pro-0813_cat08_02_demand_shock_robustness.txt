## 3. Robust redesign package

The robust package is not “add capacity everywhere.” It uses reallocation, rework reduction, arrival smoothing, and conditional surge policies.

### Core package

#### A. Front-end smoothing and selective registration automation
- Adopt **I7**: shift courier pickup slots to smooth 10:00 and 17:00 spikes.
- Adopt **I8**: automate registration only for orders with complete digital metadata.
- Maintain an unscheduled STAT lane for true urgent samples.

**Why robust:**  
It reduces front-end queue spikes and manual registration workload without creating a false picture of downstream capacity.

**Downstream risk:**  
More complete orders reach pre-analytical faster; pre-analytical and assay may become exposed. Monitor pre-analytical queue and assay utilization immediately after automation rollout.

**Assumptions:**  
Courier routes can be renegotiated. Complete digital metadata is frequent enough to matter. Exception handling for incomplete orders remains separate.

**May fail if:**  
Emergency samples still arrive unscheduled, or incomplete metadata is common and manual registration remains overloaded.

---

#### B. Early pre-analytical defect triage
- Adopt **I6**: reject or flag poor samples within 2 hours.
- Defect criteria should be explicit: hemolysis, clot, wrong tube, inadequate volume, labeling mismatch.
- Auto-flag cases needing accession correction rather than letting them wait.

**Why robust:**  
This reduces recollection loops and accession-correction rework. Process-mining shows peak pre-analytical -> request recollection is 780, and request recollection -> receive sample takes 60h median. Preventing poor samples from proceeding protects assay, technical validation, and medical validation capacity.

**Downstream risk:**  
Triage itself adds pre-analytical and accession correction workload. Too strict triage can increase recollections and cancellations.

**Assumptions:**  
Defect criteria can be standardized. Pre-analytical staff can perform triage without delaying release of clearly good samples.

**May fail if:**  
Pre-analytical staff are already saturated; this is why triage should be paired with conditional accession support from cross-trained validators.

---

#### C. Tiered dispatch with WIP caps, not strict urgent-first
- Modify **I1**: use priority classes: Urgent > Complex-panel > Routine.
- But enforce WIP caps to prevent routine starvation:
  - Urgent queue age > 8h: escalate.
  - Complex queue age > 48h: escalate.
  - Routine queue age > 60h: protect from insertion of non-urgent cuts.
- Reserve minimum routine capacity during normal working hours unless urgent breach risk is imminent.

**Why robust:**  
Strict urgent-first helps urgent cases but starves routine and complex cases. WIP caps prevent one class from consuming the entire line.

**Downstream risk:**  
Releasing urgent/complex faster from assay increases load on medical validation and specialist review. This is why I5 and I10 are triggered conditionally.

**Assumptions:**  
Real-time queue age by case type is available. Dispatch staff can follow priority rules without overriding every day.

**May fail if:**  
All WIP caps breach simultaneously. No scheduling rule can prioritize all case types at once.

---

#### D. Conditional flexible staff reallocation
- Cross-train two technical validators to accession correction (**I4**) in advance, but activate only when:
  - Accession correction queue > 14h, and
  - Technical validation utilization < 95%.
- Add temporary evening medical validation shift (**I5**) when:
  - Medical validation queue > 22h, or
  - Urgent p90 > 15h.

**Why robust:**  
It creates surge capacity without permanent staffing and avoids overloading one station under all conditions.

**Downstream risk:**  
I4 can create a technical validation shortage. I5 can empty medical validation but expose specialist review and release integration.

**Assumptions:**  
Cross-training occurs during a stable period, not during the peak. Overtime/evening shift is feasible.

**May fail if:**  
Both accession correction and technical validation are saturated simultaneously; then I4 should not be activated unless I6 reduces correction volume enough.

- Do not adopt **I2** as a blanket policy. Consider 24 only if:
  - Routine prep queue > 15h,

  - Batch wait < 14h,

**Why robust:**

**Downstream risk:**

**Assumptions:**

**May fail if:**

- **I7** courier slot smoothing.
- **I8** automation for complete digital metadata.
- **I6** early triage but with a maximum 2-hour triage-to-decision limit.
- Stagger pre-analytical shifts to match new arrival profile.

Avoid:
- **I2** large batches because arrival spikes already create batchy downstream flow.
- Strict urgent-first at pre-analytical if it starves routine intake too severely.

Switch off:
- Courier smoothing changes if they create pickup delays that threaten urgent TAT.

## 6. Residual vulnerabilities

- **Tiered dispatch can still starve some class.** If urgent, complex, and routine queues all breach simultaneously, no priority rule can satisfy all targets.
- **Early defect triage may over-reject borderline samples**, increasing recollections, cancellations, and provider friction. This is a redesign-induced risk.
- **Cross-training can shift the bottleneck to technical validation.** If I4 is active during high technical validation demand, the tech validation queue can exceed its peak 16h median.
- **Evening medical validation may expose specialist review.** Medical validation can release faster, but Complex-panel and selected urgent cases still wait on one specialist.
- **Courier smoothing depends on logistics cooperation.** If routes cannot be changed, or if emergency samples arrive unscheduled, front-end spikes persist.
- **M1/M2 routing can concentrate failure risk.** If all routine volume goes to M2 and M2 fails or repeats rise, the repeat loop becomes self-feeding.
- **Automation may create a digital divide.** Orders with incomplete metadata may be displaced by complete digital orders, delaying manual registration.
- **Complex-panel outsourcing adds transport delay and split-record complexity.** Traceability and result integration may create new manual work.

## 7. Minimum monitoring dashboard

### Leading indicators to monitor daily during peak periods

| Leading indicator | Warning band | Critical band | Why it matters |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Courier intake queue | > 5h median or spike > 2h at 10:00/17:00 | > 7.5h | Front-end bottleneck that creates downstream waves |
| Registration queue | > 2.8h | > 5h | Manual registration delay before pre-analytical |
| Pre-analytical queue | > 9.5h | > 14h or utilization > 96% | Gate before assay; also generates recollections |
| Triage reject/recollection request rate | > 8% of received samples | > 12% | Signals sample quality problems and future rework |
| Accession correction queue | > 14h | > 20h | Small specialist pool; corrections unblock many cases |
| Preparation queue / batch wait | Prep > 11h or batch wait > 14h | Prep > 16h | Large batches may help efficiency but delay urgent cases |
| Assay queue | > 18h | > 24h or utilization > 98% | Core capacity bottleneck in most peak scenarios |
| Repeat assay rate | > 12% | > 18% | Repeats consume assay capacity twice |
| M2 repeat rate | Rising above baseline | > 2x normal M2 repeat rate | Early warning of M2 degradation or assay failure |
| Technical validation queue | > 16h | > 24h or utilization > 100% | Post-assay gate; worsened by cross-training |
| Medical validation queue | > 22h | > 36h | Needed for urgent and complex cases |
| Specialist review queue | > 36h | > 60h | Single-specialist fragility |
| Urgent WIP age | > 8h from receipt | > 12h | Leading indicator of likely 18h TAT breach |
| Routine WIP age | > 48h | > 60h | Leading indicator of routine 72h target risk |

### Lagging outcome metrics

These should be reviewed daily but are outcomes, not leading signals:

- Median and p90 cycle time by case type: Routine, Urgent, Complex-panel, Repeat-after-failure.
- Urgent 18-hour breach rate.
- Routine 72-hour breach rate.
- Overall rework rate.
- Release result count and cancellation rate.
- Outsourced Complex-panel TAT, if I10 is active.

The daily operating rule is: act on queue and WIP thresholds, not only on median historical cycle time.
