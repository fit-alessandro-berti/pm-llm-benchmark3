## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

### Simplified dependency graph (process gating, not log order)

- **Intake/data foundation**: A1, A2 improve case identity + routing, which reduces downstream rework and wrong control paths.
- **Parallel “clearance” streams (must stay parallel in reality)**:
  - **Compliance/KYC stream**: A3 and A4 improve KYC throughput and exception resolution. **KYC clearance gates** Contract + Signing legally.
  - **Document/financial stream**: A5 improves completeness and reduces loops; A6 depends on doc quality and feeds credit analysis.
- **Risk/synchronization stream**:
  - **Valuation**: A7 improves the external synchronization point `Collateral valuation request -> Wait for external valuation` (2350) and reduces late valuations (19%).
- **Decision packaging and downstream**:
  - A8 depends on (KYC status) + (normalized statements) + (valuation if required) + (credit notes); otherwise it just repackages missing artifacts.
  - A9 depends on (committee decision) + (product terms) + (KYC clearance) and should enforce **decision–contract consistency** and **KYC-before-signing**.
  - A10 depends on (contract ready) + (KYC clearance) + (identity/consent).
  - A11 depends on (signed contract) + (controls complete); unsafe otherwise.
  - A12 is cross-cutting: structured exception routing + remediation evidence, feeding back into rule updates.

### Upstream data-quality dependencies

- **Case identity + duplicates** (A1) and **correct product/exposure routing** (A2) materially affect:
  - Whether collateral is requested (Equipment finance: 71% need valuation),
  - Whether the correct committee cadence/controls apply (High-exposure SME),
  - Whether KYC and document requests are sent with correct entity/product context.
- **Document completeness** (A5) is a practical prerequisite for A6 (normalization) and for reducing `Financial doc review -> Request missing docs` rework.

### Legal/compliance control dependencies

- **KYC clearance before signing** must be enforced in A9/A10: signing UX must be *gated* by KYC status (not “provisional then remediate”).
- **Decision–contract matching** is primarily addressed by A9 (and supported by A12), reducing the 8.5% contract-remediation loop (14% for renewals).
- **Four-eyes for High-exposure**: A8/A9/A12 should embed separation-of-duties checks (Credit analysis vs Committee packaging) and block/route exceptions.
- **Audit trail** is mandatory across A2/A3/A4/A6/A9/A12: store model suggestion, confidence, human override, final action.

### Human escalation capacity (exception handling)

- **Compliance specialists are 3 FTE** and already a dependency bottleneck; any A3/A4 solution must:
  - Triage to reduce needless escalations,
  - Provide “assistive” suggestions, not auto-clearance,
  - Monitor specialist queue length and aging (to avoid shifting delay from KYC ops to specialists).
- **Credit analysts are central**; if A5 reduces rework and increases “clean inflow,” analyst capacity must be monitored to avoid growing `Credit analysis` waiting (28h median).

### External valuation dependency

- A7 cannot “automate away” external waiting; it can:
  - Improve request completeness (fewer back-and-forth),
  - Route to valuers with capacity/skill fit,
  - Enforce SLA tracking and escalation,
  - Enable parallel valuation for high-risk/high-exposure cases.

### Core-banking integration constraints (two deep integrations in first 6 months)

- Likely **deep integrations**: **A9** (product terms + contract artifacts + decision feed) and **A11** (activation posting).
- Most others are **workflow/portal/document** integrations (shallower). Plan first 6 months with **A9 as the single deep integration**, avoid adding A11 until upstream controls stabilize.

## 3. Portfolio alternatives

### Quick-win portfolio (politically attractive, “front-to-back”)

- **Modules**: **A1 + A2 + A9 + A10**
- **Optimizes**:
  - Visible automation at high frequency (A1),
  - Early routing correctness (A2),
  - Contract throughput + control checks (A9),
  - Signing delay reduction (A10).
- **Sacrifices / risks**:
  - Leaves major rework loops untouched (`Doc review <-> Missing docs`) and low-frequency critical-path blocks (A4, A7),
  - Can **expose downstream bottlenecks** by releasing more cases into Compliance/Credit without reducing their exception work,
  - Change-management risk with customers (A10).

### Bottleneck / synchronization portfolio (targets dominant waits and critical-path blockers)

- **Modules**: **A4 + A5 + A7 + A9**
- **Optimizes**:
  - KYC blocking exceptions (A4) that are on the critical path in 82% of impacted cases,
  - Rework loops driven by missing documents (A5),
  - External valuation synchronization (A7),
  - Contract-control correctness and reduced remediation (A9).
- **Sacrifices / risks**:
  - Less “headline” labor saving in the visible back-office queue (A1),
  - Does not directly automate credit judgment work (still reliant on analysts),
  - Requires disciplined exception workflows (specialist capacity; partner SLAs).

### Compliance-first portfolio (minimize control violations and audit risk)

- **Modules**: **A2 + A3 + A9 + A12**
- **Optimizes**:
  - Correct routing to the right control path (A2),
  - Safer KYC hit triage with mandatory escalation/audit (A3),
  - Decision–contract matching + KYC gate before signing (A9),
  - Structured remediation and reporting (A12).
- **Sacrifices / risks**:
  - Smaller immediate cycle-time gains on the “waiting monsters” (external valuation, customer response) unless paired later with A5/A7/A10,
  - May increase flagged exceptions initially (more work before less work).

## 4. Expected process-level effects

### Quick-win portfolio (A1+A2+A9+A10)

- **Cycle time**:
  - Some reduction at the front (`Receive -> Register` (11880) and `Register -> Classify` (11680)) via A1/A2,
  - Downstream improvement via A9/A10 on `Contract prep -> Signing` (7300) and signing delays (44h median),
  - **Limited impact** on cases dominated by `KYC -> Resolve mismatch` (1750) and `Wait for external valuation` (2350) because those remain.
- **Waiting time**:
  - Cuts visible back-office waiting (Register 1.5h; Classify 2.0h),
  - Potentially reduces `Customer signing` waiting (44h) if adoption is strong,
  - Risk: faster intake can increase waiting in `KYC check` (4h) and `Credit analysis` (28h) if downstream capacity is unchanged.
- **Rework**:
  - Reduces misrouting-driven rework (A2),
  - Reduces contract remediation loops (`Contract prep -> Remediate control issue` (650)) with A9,
  - **Does not reduce** the dominant doc loop (`Doc review -> Request missing docs` (3300) and back (2800)).
- **Handovers**:
  - Fewer back-office touches at intake,
  - More automated handoff to legal/e-sign, but customer exceptions still create RM handovers.
- **Resource demand**:
  - Back office demand drops; Compliance/Credit may see earlier, more continuous inflow,
  - Legal ops may see higher throughput earlier in the month (risking month-end peaks unless smoothed).
- **Control violations**:
  - Improves decision–contract match (A9),
  - If A10 is gated by KYC status, reduces provisional signing (6.5%),
  - Four-eyes violations may persist unless explicitly embedded in workflow controls (A9/A12 logic needed).

### Bottleneck / synchronization portfolio (A4+A5+A7+A9)

- **Cycle time**:
  - Targets the “long waits that gate downstream”: **entity mismatch (31h median wait)**, **external valuation (58h)**, and **missing-doc loops**.
  - Improves readiness for `Committee packaging` by reducing prerequisite latency (KYC cleared, docs complete/consistent, valuation delivered).
- **Waiting time**:
  - Reduces time spent between `KYC check -> Resolve entity mismatch` and re-clearance (`Resolve mismatch -> KYC` (1160)),
  - Reduces time trapped in the customer-document loop (`Financial doc review -> Request missing docs` (3300) ↔ back (2800)),
  - Reduces `Wait for external valuation -> Committee packaging` readiness delay (1980) by routing/triage + SLA enforcement,
  - A9 reduces `Contract preparation` waiting and rework (22h median wait; remediation loops).
- **Rework**:
  - A5 reduces repeated missing-doc requests (52% repeated) and doc-review exceptions (24%),
  - A4 reduces repeated mismatch cycles and late-stage KYC surprises,
  - A9 reduces contract remediation (8.5% overall; higher on renewals).
- **Handovers**:
  - Fewer “ping-pong” handovers among KYC ops ↔ specialists, and analysts/RMs ↔ customers for documents,
  - Fewer handovers in valuation (standardized request + routing reduces valuer back-and-forth),
  - More straight-through handoffs into contract generation once prerequisites are met.
- **Resource demand**:
  - **Compliance specialists**: likely fewer long-running cases but a risk of short-term increase in escalations during rollout; must monitor queue aging,
  - **Relationship managers**: fewer manual chases if A5 nudges work; but need playbooks for exceptions to avoid hidden work,
  - **Legal ops**: more even flow if upstream blocking decreases; mitigate month-end overload with WIP limits and scheduling.
- **Control violations**:
  - A9 directly reduces decision–contract mismatch and ensures KYC gate before signing,
  - A4 reduces the incentive for “provisional signing then remediate” by making KYC exceptions resolve faster/cleaner,
  - Four-eyes violations still need explicit workflow enforcement (pair A9 with assignment checks for High-exposure cases).

### Compliance-first portfolio (A2+A3+A9+A12)

- **Cycle time**:
  - Moderate improvements; main value is preventing rework and late-stage remediation, not eliminating external/customer waits.
- **Waiting time**:
  - A3 can reduce KYC hit-handling latency if escalation is well-tuned, but may also increase flagged queues initially.
- **Rework**:
  - Fewer wrong-path cases (A2),
  - Reduced contract remediation loops and clearer remediation pathways (A9 + A12).
- **Handovers**:
  - More structured handovers for exceptions (A12), reducing ad hoc “who owns this?” churn.
- **Resource demand**:
  - More compliance/QA work upfront (intentional), less post-hoc cleanup later.
- **Control violations**:
  - Strongest reduction in KYC-before-signing, decision–contract mismatch, and improved auditability,
  - Best platform to enforce four-eyes via assignment rules + exception logging.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

- **Model/automation error causes wrong control path (A2)**:
  - **Controls**: conservative thresholds for High-exposure SME; human confirm for low confidence; periodic misrouting audit; “reason codes” and override logging.
- **KYC false negatives (A3/A4) are severe**:
  - **Controls**: never auto-clear hits; assistive triage only; mandatory escalation for ambiguous matches; dual-run/shadow mode before enforcement; regulator-ready audit trail.
- **OCR/extraction errors in financials (A6)**:
  - **Controls**: field-level confidence + reconciliation checks; require analyst validation when confidence low; store extracted evidence links; monitor drift by document type/quality.
- **Customer friction and channel conflict (A5/A10) with RM resistance**:
  - **Controls**: progressive disclosure (only ask for missing items); allow “RM-assisted upload”; measure abandonment; A/B test nudges; provide RM templates to reduce incomplete instructions.
- **External partner dependency and quality disputes (A7)**:
  - **Controls**: SLAs and scorecards; routing by valuer capacity/quality; standardized request package; dispute resolution workflow; second valuation for high-risk/high-exposure triggers.
- **Rule library/template drift creates legal risk (A9/A12)**:
  - **Controls**: versioned rule governance with legal sign-off; automated regression tests on template outputs; change windows; mandatory review for renewals (higher remediation rate).
- **Automation shifts bottlenecks (general)**:
  - **Controls**: WIP limits and throttling at case-release points; monitor specialist/analyst queue aging; time-of-day load balancing (back office utilization <65% after 15:00 suggests scheduling leverage).
- **Four-eyes violations (Credit analysis vs Committee packaging)**:
  - **Controls**: separation-of-duties enforced in task assignment; hard blocks for High-exposure SME; exception justification captured in A12; month-end surge monitoring.
- **Auditability gaps across modules**:
  - **Controls**: centralized “decision ledger” capturing model suggestion, confidence, human action, final outcome; immutable logs; periodic sampling reviews.
- **Security/privacy**:
  - **Controls**: least-privilege access, encryption at rest/in transit, PII minimization in models, retention policies aligned to regulation.

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

Recommended portfolio to implement in year 1: **Bottleneck / synchronization (A4 + A5 + A7 + A9)**, sequenced to respect dependencies and the **two-deep-integration limit**.

### Phase 0 (Weeks 0–4): foundations and baselines

- **Prereqs**:
  - Standardize case identifiers, entity master data hygiene, and document taxonomy.
  - Add/validate event attributes needed for controls: exposure class, product, KYC status timestamps, committee decision ID, preparer/approver IDs.
  - Define “critical-path” metrics recognizing that KYC and doc review are effectively concurrent (avoid enforcing sequential gating).
- **Baseline metrics**:
  - Median waiting at: Resolve mismatch (31h), Wait for valuation (58h), Customer signing (44h), Contract prep (22h).
  - Rework edges: `Doc review -> Request missing docs` (3300), `Request missing docs -> Doc review` (2800), contract remediation loop (650/510).
  - Control KPIs: four-eyes violation rate (5.8% in High-exposure at month end), provisional signing (6.5%), decision–contract mismatch proxy (remediation 8.5% overall; 14% renewals).

### Phase 1 (Months 1–3): reduce rework and external synchronization (low core integration)

- **Deploy A5** (checklist + portal nudges):
  - Start with Working-capital + renewals (highest volume), then expand.
  - Include RM playbooks to prevent incomplete instructions (explicitly targets the observed RM-driven loop trigger).
- **Deploy A7 (MVP)** (triage + routing + SLA dashboard):
  - Standardize valuation request packages to reduce back-and-forth; route to valuers by capacity/quality.
- **Monitoring & gates**:
  - A5 gate: reduce repeat loop rate (`Request missing docs -> Doc review` / `Request missing docs`) and average “time to docs complete.”
  - A7 gate: reduce late valuations from 19% and reduce time from `Collateral valuation request -> Wait for valuation` completion.

### Phase 2 (Months 3–6): unblock KYC exceptions + strengthen downstream controls (one deep integration)

- **Deploy A4 in assistive mode first**, then enforce:
  - Provide suggested match resolutions + required evidence links; specialists approve/override.
  - Add queue triage to protect 3 specialist FTE capacity (limit escalations, prioritize aging cases).
- **Deploy A9 (deep integration #1)**:
  - Start with template generation + **control-rule checks** for renewals (highest contract-remediation rate) and one additional product.
  - Enforce decision–contract matching and hard-gate signing on KYC clearance.
- **Monitoring & gates**:
  - A4 gate: reduce median waiting for mismatch cases; maintain low “wrong match” defect rate via QA sampling.
  - A9 gate: near-zero critical decision–contract mismatches in pilot; reduction in `Contract prep -> Remediate control issue` (650) frequency; complete audit trail coverage.

### Phase 3 (Months 6–12): scale, stabilize, and prevent bottleneck shifts

- Expand A4 coverage (more mismatch patterns) with strict escalation thresholds.
- Expand A7 to Equipment finance-heavy segments; add dispute workflow and secondary valuation triggers for high exposure.
- Expand A9 rule library and templates; add automated regression tests and monthly rule governance.
- **System-level monitoring**:
  - Watch for shifted bottlenecks: growth in `Credit analysis` waiting (28h) and `Committee packaging` waiting (26h) as upstream blockers fall.
  - Manage peaks: legal ops month-end overload—use WIP limits and earlier scheduling where possible.

## 7. Final recommendation

**Recommend the Bottleneck / synchronization portfolio: A4 + A5 + A7 + A9.**

- **Why this portfolio**: It targets the **dominant end-to-end delays and synchronization points** that process mining surfaced as critical-path drivers:
  - **Low-frequency but high-impact blockers** (entity mismatch at 31h median wait; external valuation at 58h) that gate downstream work,
  - **High rework loops** (missing documents: 3300 requests with 2800 returns) that inflate workload and waiting,
  - **Downstream control-risk and remediation** (contract remediation loops 650/510; decision–contract mismatch; KYC-before-signing).
- **Why not “automate the highest-frequency activity first” (e.g., A1 Register)**:
  - The back-office queue is visible, but utilization drops below 65% after 15:00, suggesting the wait is largely scheduling/batching—not true capacity shortage.
  - Automating A1 first **risks releasing more cases into the real bottlenecks** (3 compliance specialists; long KYC/exception path; analyst-heavy steps) and can worsen end-to-end cycle time even if local service time drops.
  - Frequency ignores the process reality that **exceptions and synchronization (mismatch, valuation, missing docs)** dominate completion time and risk exposure.
- **Trade-off**: This recommendation sacrifices a “headline” high-frequency labor win in exchange for **measurable cycle-time reduction, fewer rework loops, and stronger control compliance**. Once these prerequisites stabilize and downstream queues don’t spike, A1 (and later A10/A6) becomes safer and more valuable as a follow-on.
