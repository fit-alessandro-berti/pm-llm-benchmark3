## 1. Feasibility model

```text
Partial-Order Control-Flow Relations (for case c):
1. Strict Entry Sequence:
   Register(c) < Triage(c)

2. Verification Prerequisites:
   Triage(c) < Identity_Check(c)
   IF originals_required(c) = no:
       Triage(c) < Document_Check_1(c)
       [Identity_Check(c) || Document_Check_1(c)]   (concurrent execution permitted)
   IF originals_required(c) = yes:
       Triage(c) < Originals_Receipt(c) < Document_Check_1(c)
       Triage(c) < Identity_Check(c)
       [Identity_Check(c) || (Originals_Receipt(c) < Document_Check_1(c))]

3. Assessment Join:
   {Identity_Check(c), Document_Check_k(c)} < Assessment_k(c)   (for rework iteration k >= 1)

4. Advisory & Preliminary Work (optional parallel branches):
   Triage(c) < Escalation_Advice(c)        (may overlap Identity/Document/Assessment)
   Triage(c) < Preliminary_Compliance(c)   (may overlap Identity/Document checks;
                                            does not satisfy final Compliance_Review)

5. Rework Loop (when triggered at step k):
   (Assessment_k(c) OR Compliance_Review_k(c)) < Request_More_Info_k(c)
   Request_More_Info_k(c) < Document_Check_{k+1}(c) < Assessment_{k+1}(c)

6. Decision Gate & Exit:
   IF high_risk(c) OR high_value(c):
       latest(Assessment_k(c)) < Compliance_Review_final(c) < Decision(c) < Notify(c)
   ELSE:
       latest(Assessment_k(c)) < Decision(c) < Notify(c)
```

```text
Conditional and Declarative Constraints:
C1 (No-Self-Overlap / Capacity):
   For any resource r and distinct service intervals i != j assigned to r:
   complete(i) <= start(j) OR complete(j) <= start(i).
   (Exception: S1 advisory work may run concurrently with checks performed by other resources.)

C2 (Four-Eyes Separation):
   If Compliance_Review_final(c) is required:
   resource(latest(Assessment_k(c))) != resource(Compliance_Review_final(c)).

C3 (Compliance Non-Substitutability):
   Preliminary_Compliance(c) or Escalation_Advice(c) cannot replace
   Compliance_Review_final(c) after the latest relevant Assessment_k(c).

C4 (Rework Completeness):
   Every Request_More_Info_k(c) obligates both Document_Check_{k+1}(c) and
   Assessment_{k+1}(c); if high_risk(c) OR high_value(c), it obligates a new
   or explicitly reaffirmed Compliance_Review before Decision(c).

C5 (Evidence-Based Decision Policy):
   Unless decision policy is explicitly altered, unresolved material discrepancies
   trigger Request_More_Info rather than unconditioned approval.

C6 (Queue-Forecast Non-Determinism):
   Observed queue snapshots are point-in-time forecasts, not guaranteed waiting times;
   arrivals, shift boundaries, and urgent preemptions shift actual start times.
```

- **Routing and timing/reservation (K-219, K-104):** K-219 alters resource routing (`D1 -> D2`) to trade D1 queue wait against D2 service time and handwriting-rework probability. K-104 alters routing and timing by triggering early `S1` escalation and parallel `C1` preparation plus a provisional `C1` capacity reservation while keeping final compliance and four-eyes rules intact.
- **Allowed decision policy (K-337):** Prohibiting `Request More Info 2` after `Assessment 2` alters the admissible decision policy—forcing conditional approval or rejection on incomplete evidence rather than iterating until certainty—which simultaneously truncates control flow and shifts quality/compliance risk.

**Factual mechanism:** At Triage (Tue 09:35), legacy case-locking created only `Identity Check` (`I2`, Tue 11:30–13:20). `Document Check` (`D2`) was not queued until `I2` finished at Tue 13:20, running Tue 15:00–19:30 (4.5 h) and pushing `Assessment` (`A2`) into Wednesday morning (Wed 09:30–12:00) and `Decision` to Wed 14:30 (29.5 h total).

**Minimal intervention:** At Triage (Tue 09:35), create both `Identity Check` and `Document Check` tasks concurrently using a controlled lock workaround with mandatory real-time task timestamps.

**Temporal propagation:** `I2` still runs Tue 11:30–13:20. However, `D2` enters the Tuesday queue at Tue 09:35 instead of Tue 13:20 (3.75 h earlier). Facing `D2`'s Tue 08:00 queue of ~4 h plus arrivals, `D2` starts around Tue 12:30–13:30 and finishes around Tue 17:00–18:00 (1.5–2.5 h earlier than Tue 19:30). Because `I2` already finished at Tue 13:20, the `AND-join` completes when `D2` finishes (Tue 17:00–18:00). Yet Tue's `A2` queue (6 h at Tue 08:00 plus intraday arrivals) means earlier readiness often still queues until Wed morning (`A2` starting Wed 08:00–08:30 rather than Wed 09:30), compressing final completion far less than the upstream parallel savings.

**Process-feasibility check:** `Originals Receipt` is not required, so `I2` and `D2` may run concurrently after `Triage` (Rule 3). Both complete before `A2` starts (Rule 2). Low risk/value requires no `Compliance Review`. Real-time timestamping prevents artificial concurrency artifacts.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best case: `D2` starts Tue 12:00, finishes Tue 16:30; `A2` catches a late-Tuesday slot (Tue 18:00–20:30); `M1` approves Wed 08:30–09:00 (~24.0 h total, -5.5 h). Base case: `D2` finishes Tue 17:30; `A2` queue absorbs early arrival, starting Wed 08:00–10:30; `M1` finishes Wed 11:30–13:00 (~26.5–28.0 h, -1.5 to -3.0 h). Worst case: workaround friction or missing lock release causes `D2` delay/rework or `A2` hold, yielding Wed 14:30–15:30 (~29.5–30.5 h, 0 to +1.0 h).

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Event-log validation showing `D2` actually already starts informally before `I2` finishes (making factual serialization a logging artifact), or capacity data showing `A2` has unutilized Tuesday evening slots.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 (early escalation + C1 slot) | Adds `S1` advisory work + `C1` prep; may save 1–3 h final `C1` if early packet is complete, or increase total `C1` hours if re-read. | None reliably (target `C1` wait drops, net `C1` work ambiguous). | `C1` effective queue (via reserved/idle slot fragmentation); `S1` advisory queue. | Urgent Mon/Tue compliance cases displaced or delayed by provisional reservations. | **Negative at scale** (beneficial only selectively when `C1` has slack). | Singleton `C1` utilization near 100%: idle reserved slots cause exponential waiting-time blowup. |
| K-219 (route handwritten docs to D2) | Transfers 4 h `D1` work to 5–8 h `D2` work; adds `D1/D2` + `A2` demand when rework occurs. | `D1` queue (especially Mon–Tue peak). | `D2` queue; `A2` queue (earlier arrivals + rework second passes). | Standard digital cases behind `D2` and `A2`; complex corporate cases benefit from shorter `D1` queue. | **Mixed / U-shaped** (positive for moderate diversion, negative if `D2` saturates or rework spikes). | Rework multiplier: each failed check generates a 2nd `Document Check` and 2nd `Assessment`. |
| K-337 (suppress RMI 2) | Eliminates `D1` (3–4 h) and `A1` (2–3 h) third-pass work; increases `C1` scrutiny (+0.5–2 h) and `M1`/appeal effort. | `D1` and `A1` queues. | `C1` queue (harder reviews); post-decision appeal/re-application inflow. | High-risk cases waiting behind longer `C1` reviews; customers facing stricter rejections. | **Ambiguous-to-negative** (faster internal throughput offset by shadow rework and risk loss). | Risk/appeal threshold: cutting verification shifts load from `D1/A1` into legal/compliance tail risk. |
| K-411 (parallelize verification) | Zero net service-hour change (unless workaround overhead adds minutes). | Post-`I2` handoff delay (virtual wait disappears). | `D2` early-day queue and `A2` afternoon queue (earlier arrival synchronization). | Cases routed to `D2` and `A2` arriving later in the day. | **Slightly positive to neutral** (bottleneck migrates to `A2`). | Join-synchronization & bottleneck migration: reducing `I2–D2` lag moves congestion into `A2`. |

Scaling any single-case intervention to 100 similar cases within one week shifts behavior from local slack exploitation to coupled queueing dynamics, where queue displacement and bottleneck migration dominate.

First, **reservation of scarce compliance capacity (K-104)** does not scale linearly because `C1` is a singleton resource already facing a 16–20 h Monday–Tuesday backlog. Across 100 high-risk cases, reserving provisional `C1` slots creates phantom utilization: when early packets are incomplete or upstream Document Checks slip, reserved `C1` windows sit idle or force preemption of urgent arrivals. Rather than clearing backlogs, premature reservation fragments `C1`'s schedule and increases variance for non-target urgent compliance cases.

Second, **substitution between D1 and D2 (K-219)** redistributes document-check load. Locally, shifting a case from `D1` (11–13 h queue) to `D2` (2–5 h queue) exploits unused generalist capacity. Across 100 cases, however, `D2`'s longer service times on difficult documents (6–14 h vs. `D1`'s 4–9 h) rapidly exhaust `D2`'s slack, causing `D2`'s queue to balloon while `D1`'s queue only partially shrinks—especially if `D2` still loses complex corporate work to `D1`.

Third, **additional or avoided rework** introduces sharp nonlinearities. If K-219 increases handwriting clarification loops, each rework event adds 8–36 h externally plus a *second* Document Check and Assessment, multiplying internal demand on both pools. Conversely, K-337 avoids `D1` and `A1` rework loops across 100 cases, freeing substantial capacity, but transfers failure into `C1` scrutiny, post-decision appeals, and re-submissions—creating hidden shadow rework.

Fourth, **assessment-queue bottlenecks** absorb upstream gains. Under K-411 and K-219, earlier completion of verification pushes batches into `A1`/`A2` simultaneously, creating wave arrivals that inflate Assessment wait times unless Assessment capacity expands.

Fifth, **weekend and external-response effects** create step-function discontinuities: missing a Friday cutoff (K-337, or late K-219 rework) adds ~48–60 h of calendar delay regardless of marginal service-time savings.

Finally, **timestamp behavior versus true concurrency (K-411)** matters at scale: if workers batch-log completions or hold informal locks, event logs show illusory parallelism while real handoff waits and simultaneous resource contention still degrade throughput.

## 7. Final synthesis

Across the four counterfactuals, **parallelizing verification with real-time logging (K-411)** appears most robust. Although its final cycle-time gain is modest when the Assessment queue absorbs earlier readiness, it preserves every normative control-flow, quality, and four-eyes constraint, introduces no rework risk, and is trivial to reverse. Its downside is largely limited to administrative lock coordination and revealing downstream bottlenecks.

Conversely, **suppressing the second Request More Info loop (K-337)** is the most dangerous intervention despite offering the largest apparent speed-up (~66–135 h saved by avoiding a weekend wait and a third cycle). Because it alters the underlying decision policy rather than mere scheduling, it forces high-risk, high-value decisions under unresolved discrepancies—trading visible cycle-time reductions for compliance indefensibility, erroneous rejections, or severe credit and sanctions loss exposure.

Local counterfactuals are **least transportable to a system-wide policy in K-104 (C1 reservation) and K-411/K-219 (upstream acceleration)**. Locally, reserving a `C1` slot looks cleanly beneficial; across 100 cases, `C1` is a single-server bottleneck with a 16–20 h queue, so provisional reservations cause severe displacement and idle-slot waste. Similarly, accelerating `Document Check` locally assumes stationary Assessment queues, whereas system-wide rollout pushes synchronized waves into `A1`/`A2` and migrates the bottleneck downstream.

To reduce uncertainty before scaling, the most valuable additional data are: (1) **resource-level shift calendars and overtime/after-hours rules** (to separate true queue wait from off-shift gaps); (2) **true task-start versus batch-commit timestamps** (to audit real concurrency); (3) **granular queue compositions by case priority and complexity** (to forecast displacement); and (4) **downstream rework, appeal, and audit-exception rates linked to specific document handlers and incomplete-evidence decisions**.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **structural:** Advisory work by `S1` and preliminary `C1` screening can occur concurrently with `Identity Check` and `Document Check` without creating an invalid duplicate `Compliance Review` state, provided a final `C1` review follows `Assessment`.
2. **temporal:** Overnight gaps observed in the factual logs (e.g., `D1` running Mon 21:40–Tue 05:40 while `M1` signs primarily after 08:30) reflect resource-specific shift calendars or batch cutoffs rather than unlogged active work.
3. **resource:** Single-case interventions do not trigger overtime, cross-pool reallocation (e.g., `A1` helping `D1`), or temporary certification of a second compliance reviewer to relieve `C1`.
4. **behavioral:** Under K-411, workers comply with real-time task-start/complete logging and do not hold informal record locks that preserve hidden serialization.
5. **quality:** `D2` has a meaningfully higher handwriting error and `Request More Info` probability than `D1` on difficult handwritten documents, creating an asymmetric rework tail despite faster queue entry.
6. **quality:** In K-337, prohibiting `Request More Info 2` binds both `A1` and `C1` (preventing `C1` from issuing an informal `RMI` workaround), forcing `M1` to either reject or approve with enforceable conditions.
7. **interference:** At the single-case level, non-target queue arrivals remain approximately stationary; at the 100-case weekly level, earlier verification arrivals create synchronized congestion waves at `A1`/`A2` and `C1`.
8. **interference:** Provisional `C1` slot reservations in K-104 cannot be costlessly backfilled on short notice when upstream `D1` or `A1` steps slip, resulting in dead capacity and displacement of urgent compliance arrivals.
