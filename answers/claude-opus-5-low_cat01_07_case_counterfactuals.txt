## 1. Feasibility model

```
PARTIAL ORDER (per case; "<" means "must complete before start of")

Common backbone (all cases):
  Register < Triage
  Triage   < Identity Check
  Triage   < Document Check          (task-creation permitting)
  Identity Check   < Assessment_final
  Document Check_k < Assessment_k     (each assessment reads a completed doc check)
  Assessment_final < Decision
  Decision < Notify

Concurrency (unordered pairs, i.e. genuinely parallel-able):
  Identity Check  ||  Document Check          [only when originals not required]
  Identity Check  ||  Originals Receipt
  Escalation Advice (S1)  ||  {Identity Check, Document Check, Assessment}
  Preliminary Compliance Screen  ||  {Identity Check, Document Check}

Originals branch (K-219, K-337):
  Triage < Originals Receipt < Document Check_1
  Identity Check unordered w.r.t. Originals Receipt

High-risk / high-value branch (K-104, K-337):
  Assessment_final < Compliance Review_final < Decision
  Preliminary Compliance Screen < Compliance Review_final   (does NOT replace it)

Rework loop (K-337; k = 1..n):
  Assessment_k --[Request More Info_k]--> Document Check_{k+1} < Assessment_{k+1}
  Compliance Review may also emit Request More Info, re-entering the same loop
  Only Assessment_n (the last, non-RMI assessment) satisfies Assessment_final

Legacy-lock branch (K-411, factual only):
  Identity Check < Document-task creation < Document Check   [an artefact, not a norm]
```

```
CONDITIONAL AND DECLARATIVE CONSTRAINTS

C1  originals_required = true  =>  Originals Receipt precedes every Document Check
C2  originals_required = false =>  Identity Check and Document Check may overlap freely
C3  (high_risk OR high_value) => exists Compliance Review after Assessment_final
                                  and before Decision; no Decision without it
C4  after any Request More Info: NEW Document Check AND NEW Assessment required;
    if high_risk/high_value, ALSO a new or explicitly reaffirmed Compliance Review
C5  four-eyes: resource(Assessment_final) != resource(Compliance Review_final).
    With A1/A2 and only C1 certified, this binds only if C1 were ever to assess;
    it also forbids "the assessor signs off their own compliance conclusion" via
    S1 advice being recorded as the compliance review. S1 advice != Compliance Review.
C6  no resource performs two service intervals with overlapping [start, complete)
    -> I1, I2, D1, D2, A1, A2, C1, M1 are each serialised; S1 advisory MAY overlap
       other resources' work but not its own second interval
C7  C1 is a single point of failure: every high-risk/high-value case is serialised
    through one calendar. Reserved capacity is capacity removed from other cases.
C8  queue figures in the snapshots are forecasts; realised waiting may differ by
    roughly +/-50% and grows super-linearly as utilisation approaches 1
C9  a Decision may be "approve", "approve with conditions", or "reject"; it may not
    be issued while a known discrepancy is neither resolved nor explicitly carried
    as a documented, priced condition
C10 timestamps must reflect real service intervals; batch-entered timestamps that
    merely look concurrent do not create concurrency and invalidate C6 checking
C11 M1 (decision) and, in practice, A1/A2/C1 have bounded shift availability;
    readiness outside a shift converts into waiting, not into completion
```

**Which lever each intervention actually pulls.**

*Timing/sequencing only (no change to who does what or to the decision rule):* **K-411** — it deletes an artificial precedence (the case lock) and lets two already-assigned resources run concurrently; the activity set, resources and decision policy are unchanged. **K-104** is mostly timing too, but with an added *resource-reservation* component: it front-loads S1 advisory work and pre-books C1, changing when C1's capacity is consumed rather than whether compliance happens. It does not remove the mandatory final compliance review (rule 10).

*Routing (resource substitution):* **K-219** — the same activity, a different server (D2 instead of D1), with a different service-time distribution *and* a different rework-hazard. This is the only intervention whose effect is genuinely two-sided at the case level.

*Decision policy:* **K-337** — prohibiting the second `Request More Info` does not merely delete waiting time; it changes the evidentiary standard under which `Decision` may be taken. The case must now be closed on incomplete evidence, so the outcome distribution (approve / approve-with-conditions / reject) and the downstream appeal, loss and audit-defensibility profile all move. Treating this as a "save 2 days" intervention is the central modelling error to avoid.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

**Factual mechanism:** The binding constraint was not service time but *when information became actionable*. Triage saw the sanctions ambiguity at Mon 08:50 and dropped it; it re-entered the process only when A1 rediscovered it at Tue 19:10. Every compliance-related activity therefore started from a cold file: S1 gave advice Tue 19:20-21:20, and C1, entering an 18 h backlog, only began Wed 13:10 and spent 4.5 h — the upper end of the 3-6 h band — because "most evidence had to be read from scratch". The 58.5 h cycle decomposes into ~2 h of triage/identity, an 8 h complex document check that itself waited ~9 h behind D1's 11-13 h queue, ~9 h of assessment queue, and ~18 h of compliance queue plus a lengthened review.

**Minimal intervention:** Change exactly one decision at Triage — emit the escalation packet and request a provisional C1 slot. Nothing about the activity set, the resource assignments for identity/document/assessment, or the mandatory ordering `Assessment_final < Compliance Review_final < Decision` is altered. The preliminary screen is added as a *new, non-substituting* activity (rule 10).

**Temporal propagation:** Two distinct channels. (i) *Queue-position channel*: the provisional reservation moves this case forward in C1's calendar, so the ~18 h compliance wait shrinks to whatever gap remains between Assessment completion and the reserved slot — plausibly 2-13 h instead of 18 h. (ii) *Service-time channel*: because C1 has already read the file and S1's advice is on record, the final review needs verification rather than reconstruction, so 4.5 h becomes 2.5-3.5 h (the guidance allows a 1-3 h saving). A third, smaller channel: A1 assesses a case whose sanctions question is already framed, plausibly 3-3.5 h rather than 4 h. Crucially, the upstream path is untouched — D1 still finishes Tue 05:40 and A1 still starts Tue 15:10 — so the earliest conceivable compliance start is Tue ~18:40. That floor is why the saving is single-digit-to-mid-teens hours, not "two days".

**Process-feasibility check:** Preliminary screen ∥ identity/document checks is explicitly permitted (rule 10) and violates no precedence. Final `Compliance Review` still strictly follows the final `Assessment`. Four-eyes holds: A1 assesses, C1 reviews, S1 only advises — S1's advice is *not* recorded as a compliance review. C1 performs two non-overlapping intervals (Mon 14:00-16:00 prelim, then the final review), respecting rule 11. `Decision < Notify` preserved.

**Best/base/worst case:** *Best* — packet complete, C1 takes the reserved Tue-evening slot 21:00-00:00, M1 signs Wed 08:30: cycle ~42 h, saving ~16 h. *Base* — prelim work partially useful, C1 reviews Wed 08:00-11:00, decision Wed 11:30: cycle ~51 h, saving ~7-9 h. *Worst* — the early packet is incomplete, C1's prelim 2 h is wasted, the final review still takes 4.5 h and the reserved slot lapses because Assessment ran late: cycle ~57 h, saving ~1 h, and two urgent cases have been delayed for nothing. A small tail (~10%) exists where the early screen finds a real hit and the case becomes *longer* but correctly so.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Logs showing that C1's realised review duration is insensitive to prior familiarity (no measurable prelim effect); a high historical rate of incomplete Triage escalation packets; evidence that reserved C1 slots are frequently forfeited because upstream assessment overruns; or data showing that the compliance queue is scheduled by strict priority already, so a "reservation" buys nothing that a high-risk flag did not.

### K-219

**Factual mechanism:** Service was cheap (4 h) and waiting was expensive. Originals arrived Mon 17:30; D1 was unavailable until Tue 09:30 — roughly 16 h of idle case time, most of it overnight. Routing to D1 was a *quality* decision (handwriting) that imported a *capacity* cost. Downstream, A2 waited until Tue 20:00 and M1 signed the next morning.

**Minimal intervention:** Change only the server for `Document Check` (D1 → D2), triggered on `Originals Receipt`. Activity set, ordering, and decision rule unchanged.

**Temporal propagation:** D2's queue was 5 h at Mon 16:00, so D2 plausibly frees around Mon 21:00-22:00 and can start almost immediately after originals land. But D2 is slower on handwriting: 6-8 h rather than D1's 4 h, so the check completes Tue 04:00-06:00 instead of Tue 13:30 — about 8-9 h of readiness gained, not 16, because slower service eats half the queueing advantage. That gain then hits two absorbers. First, A2's Tuesday queue (6 h at 08:00) means assessment starts ~Tue 10:00-14:00 rather than instantly. Second, and decisively, `Decision` is M1's, and in the factual trace M1 signed at Wed 08:30 after an assessment that ended Tue 22:30 — consistent with a morning signing batch. If that batch is the real constraint, an assessment finishing Tue 12:30 still yields `Notify` at Wed 09:00 and the *observable* cycle time is unchanged. This is the clearest case in the set where **earlier readiness ≠ earlier completion**.

**Process-feasibility check:** `Originals Receipt < Document Check` respected. Identity Check (I2, Mon 14:20-16:20) is unordered w.r.t. originals and unaffected. Not high-risk/high-value, so no compliance review is required and none is added. Four-eyes is not engaged (A2 assesses, M1 decides — distinct roles). D2 performing the check overnight requires D2 to be genuinely available; if D2's 5 h queue is measured in *business* hours, D2 starts Tue 08:00 instead and the whole advantage collapses to ~1.5 h.

**Best/base/worst case:** *Best* — D2 works Mon evening, finishes Tue 05:00, A2 assesses Tue 10:00-12:30, M1 has an afternoon signing slot: cycle ~28 h vs 24 h factual… in practice a 4-6 h saving. *Base* — everything shifts earlier but `Notify` still lands Wed 09:00: **saving ≈ 0 h**, with the only real benefit being reduced exposure to a D1 disruption. *Worst* — D2 raises a handwriting clarification (25-35% likely): external round trip 8-36 h, a second D2 check and a second A2 assessment, `Notify` slipping to Thu afternoon: **+18 to +36 h**. Expected value across these is approximately neutral, with a fat negative tail. This is the intervention whose case-level appeal is most illusory.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** A measured D2-vs-D1 rework differential near zero on handwritten standard documents; evidence that M1 signs continuously rather than in batches (which would convert readiness into completion); D2 shift data showing genuine evening capacity; or, conversely, a discovered D1 accuracy premium that shows up as post-decision error rates rather than as rework.

### K-337

**Factual mechanism:** The cycle time is dominated by two external round trips and a calendar accident. Loop 1 cost ~20.5 h of supplier latency plus a 5 h re-check plus a 2.5 h re-assessment. Loop 2 cost ~70.5 h because Assessment 2 ended Fri 10:30 and the supplier reply only arrived Mon 09:00 — the weekend, not the supplier, is the largest single term. Internal service across the whole case is roughly 24 h; the case ran ~9 days.

**Minimal intervention:** Delete exactly one edge — the `Request More Info 2` transition — and require that Assessment 2 terminate the evidence-gathering phase. Everything before Assessment 2 is held fixed, which is the correct minimal-change discipline: the intervention is specified as post-Assessment-2, so Document Check 1/2 and Assessment 1 and RMI-1 all stand exactly as observed.

**Temporal propagation:** Assessment 2 now runs Fri 08:00-11:00 — slightly longer than the factual 2.5 h, because instead of writing an information request the assessor must characterise, bound and price the residual discrepancy. `Compliance Review` becomes available on Friday rather than the following Tuesday. C1's Friday queue is not given in the snapshots; the Thu 08:00 snapshot shows 5 h and clearing, so a Friday-afternoon start (14:00-16:00) is plausible. The review itself lengthens to 5-6 h, above the 3-6 h band's midpoint, because signing off on incomplete evidence demands an explicit residual-risk rationale. Decision then falls either Friday evening (if M1 is reachable) or Monday morning. The whole ~70 h weekend loop and the ~28 h of the Mon-Tue re-check/re-assessment disappear. Note the asymmetry: the *time* saved is mostly latency that the organisation never controlled, while the *risk* absorbed is entirely the organisation's.

**Process-feasibility check:** Rule 7 is satisfied vacuously — no new RMI is issued, so no new Document Check is owed. Rule 5 is satisfied: Assessment 2 is now `Assessment_final`, and the compliance review follows it and precedes `Decision`. Four-eyes holds (A1 assesses, C1 reviews). Rule C9 is respected only if the discrepancy is carried as an explicit, documented condition — a silent approval would breach the intervention's own terms and the compliance rule. If C1 declines to sign, the case escalates rather than shortens, which is a feasible and important branch.

**Best/base/worst case:** *Best* — C1 signs Friday 14:00-19:30, M1 approves-with-conditions Friday evening: `Notify` Fri ~20:15, saving ~118 h of calendar time versus Wed 09:10 the following week; the supplier's later confirmation (which factually arrived) proves the conditions were adequate. *Base* — C1 reviews Friday afternoon, M1 signs Mon 08:30: `Notify` Mon ~09:10, saving ~48 h; outcome approve-with-conditions, ongoing monitoring obligation created. *Worst* — C1 refuses to certify on incomplete evidence and escalates to S1, or M1 rejects to avoid retained risk: the applicant appeals, the file is reopened, and total elapsed time and cost *exceed* the factual path while the relationship is damaged; alternatively the approval stands and the unresolved inconsistency later proves material, producing a loss and a supervisory finding.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Historical data showing that second RMI loops on this supplier type resolve materially — i.e. that Assessment 3 frequently changes the decision, not just confirms it (factually here, Assessment 3 merely "accepted" the evidence, which *supports* the intervention). Conversely, a nontrivial base rate of second loops uncovering fraud, or supervisory guidance forbidding conditional approval with open discrepancies on high-risk files, would reverse it. Evidence that assessors respond to the ban by front-loading RMI-1 into an omnibus request (loop-shifting) would also nullify the measured benefit.

### K-411

**Factual mechanism:** A pure system artefact. `Triage` finished Tue 09:35 and, since originals were not required, rule 3 permitted `Identity Check ∥ Document Check` immediately. The legacy system created only the identity task, and a case lock prevented D2 from taking ownership until identity completed at Tue 13:20; D2 then started at 15:00 after its own queue. So the document check was delayed by roughly 3-5 h for no normative reason, and the delay then compounded into an overnight assessment wait.

**Minimal intervention:** Create both tasks at Triage and lift the lock for concurrent read-only ownership; mandate real-time timestamping. No resource, activity or decision change.

**Temporal propagation:** D2's Tuesday queue was 4 h at 08:00, so D2 realistically frees around 12:00 and runs 12:00-16:30 (4.5 h, standard digital) concurrently with I2's 11:30-13:20. Both prerequisites for `Assessment` are therefore satisfied at Tue 16:30 instead of Tue 19:30 — a 3 h readiness gain. What happens next depends entirely on assessment availability, which is why this case is the cleanest illustration of gain absorption. Factually A2 started Wed 09:30 with a note that "the assessment queue had grown"; the Tue 08:00 snapshot puts A2 at 6 h. If A2 has evening capacity, assessment runs Tue 17:00-19:30 and M1 signs in the Wednesday morning batch — a ~6 h saving. If not, assessment simply starts earlier in Wednesday's sequence (say 08:00-10:30 instead of 09:30-12:00) and the decision moves from 14:00 to ~11:00 — a ~3.5 h saving. On a congested Wednesday where A2's queue is FIFO over many cases, arriving 3 h earlier may change nothing at all.

**Process-feasibility check:** `Triage < {Identity Check, Document Check}` and `{Identity Check, Document Check} < Assessment` hold. Originals not required, so C2 licenses the overlap. Not high-risk/high-value, so no compliance review — correctly absent. Rule 11 is respected because I2 and D2 are *different* resources; the concurrency is across resources, not within one. The real feasibility hazard is C10: if the workaround is logged by batch entry, the event log may show one resource with overlapping intervals or show a document check "starting" before its task existed, which is false concurrency and breaks any later conformance or capacity analysis.

**Best/base/worst case:** *Best* — D2 starts at 11:00, both checks done 15:30, A2 assesses Tue evening, decision Wed 08:30: cycle ~23.5 h, saving ~6 h. *Base* — decision Wed 11:00: cycle ~26 h, saving ~3.5 h. *Worst* — the assessment queue absorbs everything and the decision still lands Wed 14:00 (saving 0 h), while the workaround produces unreliable timestamps and D2's other cases are pushed back; net negative on information quality.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Evidence that the case lock exists for a substantive reason — e.g. that identity outcomes legitimately scope the document check, so parallel document work is sometimes wasted or must be redone. Also: assessment-queue data showing utilisation near capacity (in which case the intervention only moves waiting from the document stage to the assessment stage), or an audit finding that workaround-created tasks are systematically timestamped by batch.

**100 similar cases in one week.**

Applied at scale, the four interventions behave very differently from their single-case counterfactuals, and in three of four the case-level estimate is an upper bound.

*Reservation of scarce compliance capacity.* C1 is the only certified reviewer in the window and is the hard constraint on every high-risk/high-value case. Reserving slots for 100 cases does not create capacity; it converts an implicit FIFO into an explicit priority scheme in which everyone is priority. The genuine, volume-robust benefit of K-104 is not the reservation but the *preliminary work*: if a 2 h early screen reliably removes 1-3 h from the final review, total C1 demand per case falls and the system gains real throughput. If the packets are frequently incomplete, C1 spends 2 h with no downstream saving, total C1 load rises ~30%, and the compliance queue — already 20 h on Monday — grows rather than clears. The rollout must therefore be gated on packet-completeness, not on reservation discipline.

*Substitution between D1 and D2.* At one case, moving work from a 13 h queue to a 2-5 h queue is obviously attractive. At 100 cases, the queues equalise: D2's short queue was short precisely because work was being routed to D1. Blanket substitution simply relocates the document bottleneck, while paying D2's slower service on every case (6-8 h vs 4 h here) — a net *loss* of document-pool throughput of roughly 30-50% on handwritten work. Worse, the rework hazard is paid 100 times: at a 30% rate, 30 cases re-enter document *and* assessment queues and incur external round trips. The defensible policy is load-balancing with a quality filter — send handwritten and complex-corporate work to D1, send standard digital work to D2 — rather than a rule keyed on "whoever is free first".

*Additional or avoided rework.* K-337 is the only intervention that removes work from the system rather than moving it. Across 100 cases it would free roughly 100 external round trips' worth of elapsed time and a substantial block of D1/A1 re-check capacity, which in turn shortens every other case's wait — a genuine positive externality. That capacity gain is purchased with retained risk that is invisible in the event log and materialises months later in a different queue (conditions monitoring, appeals, supervisory review).

*Assessment-queue bottlenecks.* K-411 at scale is the textbook bottleneck migration. A1/A2 queues run 4-12 h all week and one assessor is in training on Tuesday. Delivering 100 cases to that queue 3 h earlier does not create assessment capacity; it front-loads arrivals, raises peak queue length, and converts document-stage waiting into assessment-stage waiting. Realised cycle-time gains will be a fraction of the per-case 3-6 h — plausibly 0-2 h on average — unless assessment capacity is added in the same step. The intervention remains worth doing (it removes a defect and improves flow-time variance), but it should be scaled *together* with assessment staffing.

*Weekend and external-response effects.* External latency (8-36 h) plus weekend boundaries is the dominant term for originals/rework cases; K-337's headline saving is largely a weekend that was never internal capacity. This creates a strong day-of-week interaction: a case reaching Assessment on Thursday afternoon has a far higher chance of a weekend-straddling loop than one reaching it on Monday. Any rollout should be reported by arrival day, and a cheap complementary policy — issue RMIs before Thursday noon, or batch supplier chasing — may capture much of K-337's benefit without changing decision policy at all.

*Timestamp behaviour versus true concurrency.* K-411's workaround is the main measurement hazard. If tasks are created in advance but completed intervals are batch-entered, the log will show overlaps that never happened, making the intervention look successful while nothing changed, and simultaneously destroying the ability to verify rule 11 and the four-eyes rule. Real-time timestamping is not a reporting nicety here — it is the precondition for evaluating every other intervention on this list.

## 7. Final synthesis

**Most robust: K-104's preliminary escalation and compliance preparation.** It attacks the mechanism that actually produced the 58.5 h cycle — information discovered at Triage and acted on only 34 h later — rather than attacking a queue. Because it converts serial discovery into parallel preparation, it helps under most queue assumptions, and its benefit is partly a *quality* benefit (a better-prepared compliance file, earlier detection of genuine issues) that survives even when the timing gain is small. Its weak component is the C1 reservation, which redistributes rather than creates capacity; the packet, not the booking, is the part worth scaling. K-411 ranks a close second: it removes an artefact with no normative justification, so it is right to fix regardless of the modest and easily-absorbed time gain.

**Most dangerous despite its speed: K-337.** It offers by far the largest number — 48-118 hours — and that number is seductive precisely because most of it is weekend and external latency the organisation never controlled. What the organisation actually buys with that time is retained risk on an unresolved discrepancy, on a high-risk, high-value file. It is not a scheduling change but a change to the evidentiary standard for `Decision`; the costs surface later, elsewhere, and in a heavy-tailed distribution that no cycle-time dashboard will show. Note too that factually the second loop *did* resolve the issue, so this case is not evidence that second loops are wasteful.

**Least transportable to system-wide policy: K-219, with K-411 close behind.** K-219's single-case appeal rests entirely on D2's queue being short — a condition that the policy itself destroys once 100 cases are rerouted, while the slower service and higher rework hazard are paid every time. K-411's gain is similarly self-limiting: it releases work onto the assessment queue, which is where the constraint then sits.

**Most valuable additional data:** (1) resource-level shift calendars and true service-vs-waiting decomposition, especially for M1's signing batches and C1's availability — without these, "earlier readiness" cannot be distinguished from "earlier completion"; (2) D1-vs-D2 rework and post-decision defect rates by document type; (3) the historical base rate at which second RMI loops actually change a decision; (4) reliable, real-time, resource-level start/complete timestamps, since every claim above depends on distinguishing genuine concurrency from batch-entered overlap.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **structural** — The event log is complete for the modelled activities: no unrecorded handovers, informal checks, or off-system waiting exist between the listed intervals, so observed gaps are genuine queueing rather than hidden work.
2. **temporal** — Queue figures in the snapshot table are means of forecast distributions with roughly ±50% dispersion, and they interpolate smoothly between snapshot timestamps; realised waiting for any individual case may deviate substantially.
3. **resource** — D1, D2, I1, I2 and A2 have some evening/overnight availability (implied by the factual Mon 21:40 and Tue 20:00 starts), whereas M1 signs decisions in bounded morning/afternoon batches; this asymmetry is what caps the K-219 and K-411 gains.
4. **behavioral** — Triage staff, once prompted, will author escalation packets that capture the same substantive concern a senior assessor would later identify; packet completeness is assumed to reach 70-85% after a short learning period, not immediately.
5. **quality** — D2's rework probability on difficult handwriting is materially higher than D1's, assumed 25-35% versus roughly 5%; this parameter is unmeasured and is the single largest determinant of K-219's sign.
6. **quality** — Preliminary compliance familiarity genuinely reduces final review time by 1-3 h (per the service-time guidance) and does not merely relocate the same reading effort into an earlier, additional sitting.
7. **interference** — Reserving C1 capacity is zero-sum within the review window: hours booked for a pilot case are hours unavailable to the urgent non-target cases, with no overtime, no second certified reviewer, and no cross-window borrowing available.
8. **behavioral / interference** — Prohibiting the second RMI loop does not induce compensating behaviour: assessors do not pre-emptively broaden RMI-1 into an omnibus request, and they do not shift toward defensive rejections. If this assumption fails, K-337's measured cycle-time benefit largely disappears while its risk profile remains.
