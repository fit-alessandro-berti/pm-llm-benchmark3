# 1. Model scope and state variables

## Digital twin boundary
The digital twin covers the municipal building-permit process from **portal submission** through **permit issuance or rejection**, including municipal review activities, applicant/thirdparty responses, inspector travel, and crosscase coordination. It excludes upstream zoning policy design and downstream construction compliance. Interventions are represented as switches on arrival quality, routing rules, resource capacity, and batching logic.

## Level of detail
A discrete-event simulation (DES) at the **activity level** is used. The simplified process tree is treated as a control-flow skeleton; data-driven routing tables augment it. Cases are not independent: shared resource pools, architect submission bursts, and district inspection batching create passive and active dependencies.

## Entities and state variables
**Case entity (permit application)** stores:
- `case_id`, `case_type` (Simple renovation, New residential, Commercial, Complex variance)
- `district`, `architect_id`, `application_value`
- observed/imputed flags: `requires_fire`, `requires_environment`, `requires_public_notice`
- `submission_time`, `current_activity`, `queue_entry_time`, `activity_start`, `activity_end`
- `rework_count`, `bundle_id` (for inspection batching), `objection_flag` (latent), `violation_history`
- `SLA_deadline` by case type

**Resource entities**: clerks, planners, fire reviewers, environmental reviewers, inspectors, legal officers, system. Each has capacity, calendar, and current task list.

**Batch entity** for district inspection grouping: `batch_id`, `members`, `earliest_ready`, `scheduled_time`.

## Queues and calendars
- Per-activity queues with priority by `SLA_deadline` and aging.
- Municipal calendars: clerk/planner/fire/env/inspector/legal as described; system tasks unlimited.
- External calendars: applicant response calendar (workdays, no municipal control), public-holiday calendar, council-meeting weeks affecting Legal check.

## Cross-case dependencies
- **Architect bursts**: same `architect_id` submitting multiple cases in a week increases shared queue pressure and enables template reuse.
- **District inspection batching**: cases in same district and architect cluster can be bundled, reducing travel but increasing waiting for first-ready case.
- **Public-notice objections**: latent objection on one case raises objection probability for related neighborhood cases.
- **Shared resource pools**: congestion in Planning review or Environmental review affects all dependent case types.

# 2. Parameter-estimation plan

## Log and model handling with PM4Py
1. Load the excerpt or full log into a pandas DataFrame and use `pm4py.format_dataframe` with the specified keys.
2. Convert to an event log via `pm4py.convert_to_event_log`.
3. Use `pm4py.get_variants` to enumerate variants and their frequency by case type.
4. Use `pm4py.discover_dfg` for control-flow frequency and `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg` for transition durations.
5. Use `pm4py.filtering.filter_log_by_event_attribute_values` (or pandas) to split by `case_type`, `district`, and `architect_id`.

## Arrivals
- Aggregate `Submit application` events by hour/weekday/month.
- Fit a non-homogeneous Poisson process (NHPP) with seasonal multipliers (Mar–May, Sep–Nov = 1.7×; Monday = 2.2× Wednesday).
- Model architect bursts as a secondary cluster process conditioned on `architect_id` weekly counts.

## Service and waiting times
- Where both start and completion exist, fit service-time distributions (lognormal/gamma) to medians and p90s.
- Where only completion timestamps exist, use `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg` transition gaps as **proxies**, then separate waiting by inferring queue entry from previous activity end and comparing to industrial-engineering service estimates.
- Explicitly retain a `service_time` vs `waiting_time` split only for activities with partial start data; otherwise report the limitation.

## Branching probabilities and routing
- Estimate `P(requires_fire)`, `P(requires_environment)`, `P(requires_public_notice)` from case attributes and variant frequencies.
- For incomplete environmental sensitivity, use a logistic imputation on district and case type, validated against observed env-review variants.
- Rework probabilities are estimated from loop variants detected in the DFG (e.g., return to Completeness check, Inspection rework).

## Resource calendars and dependencies
- Calendars are taken from HR data and validated by comparing observed activity timestamps against available days.
- Dependencies: group by `architect_id` + week and by `district` to estimate batching propensity and travel-time reduction; objection correlation estimated from prolonged public-notice cases.

# 3. Assumptions

## Arrivals
- Submissions follow an NHPP with periodic intensity; architect bursts are additive clusters.
- Applicants do not strategically delay submission in response to backlog.

## Service times
- Service times are conditionally independent given case attributes.
- Fire and Environmental reviews follow part-time calendars; inspectors include deterministic district travel plus stochastic no-show risk.
- System tasks have near-zero service time and unlimited capacity.

## Routing
- The simplified process tree is a control-flow skeleton, not a complete executable model; data-driven routing overrides or augments it.
- Missing attribute thresholds (e.g., building area) are imputed and treated as uncertain.

## Resources/calendars
- FTE counts are effective capacities; no unplanned absenteeism beyond calendar exceptions.
- Legal officers are unavailable during council-meeting weeks except urgent rejects/permits.
- One clerk is dedicated to portal support on Mondays.

## Rework
- Rework loops are explicit and capped (e.g., max 3 repetitions) to avoid infinite cycles.
- Rework probabilities depend on case type and review outcome, not on unstructured text reasons.

## Applicant/third-party response
- “Receive missing documents” and objection extensions are exogenous, heavy-tailed, and modeled separately from municipal service.
- Applicants do not adapt optimally to process changes except through intervention D1/D7 levers.

## Cross-case dependencies
- Inspection batching reduces travel but increases waiting for the first case in the bundle.
- Same-architect cases share template quality, affecting missing-document probability.
- Public-notice objection risk is correlated within district/neighborhood.

## Non-stationary demand
- Seasonal factors and Monday bursts are fixed; no long-term trend or external shock unless explicitly added in stress scenarios.

## Missing data
- Missing start timestamps are inferred, not observed.
- Objection events are missing; public-notice duration is used as a **proxy**, not as observed objection counts.
- Emergency cases removed from log imply right-tail underestimation.

# 5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty treatment

## Warm-up and run length
- Because demand is non-stationary, initialize the simulation with an empty system and run a **6-month warm-up** (one seasonal shoulder) before collecting; alternatively, seed in-progress cases from a log snapshot to reduce warm-up to 1 month.
- Collect statistics over **24 simulated months** to cover two full seasonal cycles.

## Replications and seeds
- Use **30 replications** minimum (50 for heavy-tailed tail metrics).
- Use common random numbers across scenarios for the same arrival and response streams.
- Document base seeds per scenario (e.g., `seed = 1000 + scenario_id * 100 + rep`).

## Uncertainty treatment
- Report 95% confidence intervals for means and percentile-based intervals for median/p90.
- For Bayesian-style credible intervals, use empirical priors on service-time parameters and posterior updating from log.
- Sensitivity analysis: one-at-a-time on arrival intensity, rework probability, objection probability, and inspector travel; global Sobol on top four parameters.
- Heavy-tailed applicant response: use truncated lognormal with practical cap (e.g., 365 days) and report robust statistics; use antithetic variates to reduce variance.

# 6. Validation strategy

## Face validation
Walk through the augmented model with process owners; confirm routing rules, calendars, and batching logic match municipal reality.

## Event-log replay validation
Use PM4Py conformance checking (token-based replay or alignments) to confirm the model can replay historical variants with acceptable fitness (>0.95). The simplified tree is augmented with routing tables to achieve this.

## Distributional validation
Compare simulated activity service/waiting and case-cycle distributions against empirical medians and p90s by case type and district using KS/AD tests.

## Resource-utilization validation
Compare simulated utilization per pool against inferred busy fractions from timestamps with known starts; investigate discrepancies as calibration gaps.

## Bottleneck validation
Confirm that the highest simulated waiting times align with performance-DBG bottlenecks (Planning review, Environmental review, Legal check).

## Out-of-sample validation
Hold out the last 3 months of the 18-month log; calibrate on the first 15 months; compare predicted arrivals, SLA misses, and backlog to observed outcomes.

# 7. Decision criteria

## Metrics and thresholds
- **Cycle time**: median and p90 by case type; target reduction vs baseline with CI excluding zero.
- **SLA compliance**: Simple <20d, New residential <60d, Commercial <90d, Complex <180d; desired 95% per type.
- **Fairness**: district median-duration ratio (D3/D5 vs others) 1.2; architect-concentration penalty <5% difference.
- **Resource utilization**: steady-state 0.60–0.85; chronic >0.95 considered infeasible.
- **Rework rate**: reduction in missing-document and inspection-rework loops.
- **Backlog**: end-of-peak backlog below baseline by a specified margin.
- **Robustness**: under S1–S3, metric degradation 20% relative to baseline or improved vs baseline.

## Selection rule
An intervention (or combination) is shortlisted if it improves target metrics with confidence intervals excluding the baseline and does not violate fairness or utilization thresholds. The digital twin supports **relative comparison and robustness screening**, not proof of exact optimality, because the model remains partially calibrated and subject to missing-data bias.
