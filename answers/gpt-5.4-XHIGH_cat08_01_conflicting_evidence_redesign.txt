## 1. Current-process diagnosis

The core contradiction is real, not noise: the modal path is attractive on headline speed and apparent cost, but it is being used beyond the situations where it is safe. The dominant flow is roughly `Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close`, with about 2,300 dispatches after fast approval out of 5,000 total cases. On its face this is why the process looks efficient: `Intake -> Auto screen` is 0.8 mean hours, `Auto screen -> Fast approval` 1.0, `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement` 3.0, and `Dispatch replacement -> Close` 5.0. That is why Premium low-risk accessories can complete in a median 8 hours on the fast path and why fast approval is 31% cheaper before rework.

The problem is that the same fast path is also the main generator of downstream pain. Reopen/complaint happens in 14.1% of fast-approval cases versus only 3.3% of the repair-quote/repair path. The long-tail is especially damaging: `Fast approval -> Customer complaint/reopen` averages 72 hours and `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` averages 96 hours with a 180-hour p90. For high-value devices the segment split is stark: Premium high-value has 22% complaints on the fast path versus 5% on the reviewed/repair path; Standard high-value is 18% versus 6%. Fast approval is therefore not truly “cheap” for those segments, which is consistent with the prompt’s finding that its net cost advantage shrinks from 31% pre-rework to only 9% after complaints, reshipments, and service credits.

Conformance evidence says the fast lane is not just overused but often noncompliant. Rule R1 requires claims above EUR 400 to go through Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review before Fast approval. Rule R2 forbids dispatch before evidence completeness except for Premium low-risk accessory claims. Rule R3 requires any reopened claim to pass Manual eligibility review before final close or denial. Yet the alignment diagnostics show frequent move-on-log deviations of `Fast approval before Evidence completeness`, `Customer complaint/reopen after close`, and `Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices`. Token-based replay fitness is 0.91, but only 68% of traces fit, which is a warning that deviations are common enough to matter operationally. Low precision in the discovered model reinforces that there are many low-frequency but policy-breaking combinations, not just one bad edge.

The evidence-completion loop is a second major source of delay and abandonment. `Request missing evidence` is entered 1,430 times from auto or manual review, and 26% of cases that enter it get at least two such requests. The single largest delay jump in the prompt is `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` at 34 mean hours and 88-hour p90. This matches the segment result for unclear defect/missing evidence: 22% of all cases, 140-hour median cycle time, highest abandonment, and repeated evidence requests.

The slower reviewed paths are not uniformly “good,” either. `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote` takes 22 mean hours and is explicitly flagged as too slow for Premium cases. Fraud/risk review is also a constrained specialist bottleneck, especially on Mondays and after campaigns. So the right diagnosis is not “make everything manual” or “make everything fast.” It is that the process needs sharper segmentation: the fast path is excellent for a narrow subset, while the reviewed and repair-oriented paths are operationally clumsy but materially better for high-risk and high-value outcomes.

Downstream quality also matters independently of the front-end decision. The temporal profile links `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` to incorrect replacement part selection, and wrong replacement is expensive: expected error cost is EUR 95 for low-risk accessories and EUR 410 for high-value devices. On the repair side, `Rework repair` is 12.5% for repaired high-value devices versus 4.0% for repaired low-risk devices. Quality-check capacity is underused except after repair campaigns, which suggests some downstream control could be added without redesigning the whole process around inspection.

## 2. Redesign A: Segment-Gated Straight-Through Processing

- **Redesigned flow.** Keep straight-through processing, but only for claims that are both low-risk and evidence-complete. At intake, classify by customer segment, item value, defect type clarity, and evidence completeness. Premium low-risk accessories retain the current R2 exception and can still go directly from auto screen to dispatch. Standard low-risk accessories can also remain mostly auto-routed, but only after explicit completeness confirmation. Any claim above EUR 400, any high-value device, and any unclear-defect case is blocked from direct fast approval and routed to Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review before approval. Any reopened claim is hard-routed to Manual eligibility review before close or denial.

- **Targets.** This mainly targets the overuse of fast approval on Premium and Standard high-value devices, plus the conformance violations around R1, R2, and R3.

- **Mechanism of improvement.** The redesign uses the process-mining contradiction directly: it protects the fast lane where the data says it is defensible and closes it where the complaint and compliance evidence says it is dangerous. Because auto-screen rules are outdated and not segment-aware, a rules refresh is the concrete lever. The likely benefit is fewer bad replacements, fewer reopened claims on high-value cases, and materially better conformance, even if some cases become slower upfront.

- **What it sacrifices or leaves unresolved.** It deliberately sacrifices average cycle time for high-value claims and pushes more work into scarce Manual eligibility review and Fraud/risk review capacity. It does not by itself solve repeated evidence loops, the slow `Manual review -> Repair quote` handoff for Premium cases, or incorrect part selection after an otherwise correct approval.

## 3. Redesign B: Evidence-First, Single-Touch Intake

- **Redesigned flow.** Move evidence resolution to the front as a dedicated stage instead of allowing repeated bouncing between review and evidence request. After intake, any case with unclear defect signals or missing documents goes to an evidence-completion lane with a guided portal, defect-specific checklist, and upload validation. If the first digital request fails, or if the customer shows low digital literacy, the case switches to assisted evidence capture by agent rather than sending a second generic request. Manual eligibility review starts only after a completeness checkpoint is met, and Fraud/risk review can be triggered from that checkpoint when needed. The operating rule is one outbound evidence request before human intervention.

- **Targets.** This targets the 22% unclear-defect/missing-evidence segment, the 1,430 entries into `Request missing evidence`, repeated evidence requests, and abandonment.

- **Mechanism of improvement.** The redesign attacks the biggest avoidable loop in the log. If the new portal cuts repeated evidence requests by the stated 30-45%, it should reduce the 34-hour average delay from evidence request back to manual review and improve both compliance and decision quality because reviewers receive cleaner cases. It also reduces the chance of violating R2 by making completeness explicit before dispatch for everyone except the narrow Premium low-risk exception.

- **What it sacrifices or leaves unresolved.** It adds friction at the front for some cases that might otherwise have gone through quickly. It also creates a real abandonment risk for customers with low digital literacy if the assisted path is weak. This redesign does not directly solve wrong replacement selection or the premium SLA problem on reviewed high-value cases.

## 4. Redesign C: Decision-Fast, Fulfillment-Safe Exception Cell

- **Redesigned flow.** Do not slow every approval decision; instead, slow only risky fulfillment. Keep approval fast where it is justified, but add a lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate for high-value replacements and any case with ambiguous part mapping. Use the underused quality/compliance capability to verify replacement selection and evidence-completeness signoff before shipment. In parallel, replace the once-daily escalation board model with a small event-driven exception cell for Premium and high-value cases: one reviewer, one technician, and risk access on demand, plus pre-priced common parts to reduce `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote` waiting. Reopened cases that meet defined severity criteria go straight to this cell rather than re-entering the generic queue first.

- **Targets.** This targets the long-tail `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` problem linked to wrong part selection, the high expected cost of wrong replacements on high-value devices, the poor scalability of the current escalation board, and the temporal-profile problem that reviewed premium cases wait too long for repair quotes.

- **Mechanism of improvement.** The redesign accepts that some fast approvals are correct, but their fulfillment execution is not. A 6-18 hour pre-dispatch gate is much cheaper than a EUR 410 expected replacement error on high-value items or a 96-hour average delay to a complaint. The exception cell also compresses the most expensive and customer-visible failures instead of letting them circulate through ordinary queues.

- **What it sacrifices or leaves unresolved.** It deliberately adds delay to targeted dispatches and can reduce the apparent speed advantage of replacement-heavy flows. It does not fix upstream overuse of fast approval by itself, and if the part catalog/master-data issue is deeper than selection checking, inspection can become expensive containment rather than a durable cure.

## 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

- **Redesign A failures.** Manual-review inflow rises toward the top of the expected 15-35% range, reviewer utilization crosses the 82% queue cliff, and Premium reviewed cases become even slower. Fraud/risk review becomes the new bottleneck on Mondays and after campaigns, causing compliance-safe but operationally unacceptable delays. Rules may also be too blunt, pushing low-risk cases out of the fast lane without enough downstream benefit.

- **Redesign A bottleneck migration.** The most likely migration is from auto screen into Manual eligibility review first, and then into Fraud/risk review for high-value edge cases.

- **Redesign B failures.** The portal reduces repeated requests but increases abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers, so the process looks faster only because some customers drop out. A second failure mode is that evidence taxonomies and checklists are poorly designed, so customers upload more files but reviewers still send them back, digitizing the loop rather than removing it.

- **Redesign B bottleneck migration.** The likely migration is from repeated `Request missing evidence` loops into an assisted evidence team first, and then into a larger but cleaner Manual eligibility review queue.

- **Redesign C failures.** The pre-dispatch gate expands beyond the intended high-risk cases and destroys the speed advantage for low-risk replacements. A second failure mode is that the gate catches some wrong parts but does not fix the underlying part-mapping or price-confirmation problem, so cost rises without enough permanent quality gain. A third risk is that the exception cell becomes an informal VIP lane that starves standard cases.

- **Redesign C bottleneck migration.** The most likely migration is into Quality/compliance inspectors and technician/part-price confirmation capacity, with the exception cell becoming the next constrained resource after the current escalation board.

## 7. Recommended experiment sequence

- **First pilot.** Start with Redesign A, but only for high-value devices and claims above EUR 400 in one region or channel. Keep the current fast exception for Premium low-risk accessories during the pilot. This should be first because the rules change is deployable in two weeks, directly addresses the clearest R1/R2 violations, and has the cleanest segment-level success criterion.

- **Metrics to monitor.** Track segment-level median and p90 cycle time; 30-day and 60-day complaint/reopen rate; R1/R2/R3 deviation counts; percentage of fitting traces; wrong-replacement reshipments; service-credit exposure; abandonment; repeat evidence requests; and queue/utilization measures for Manual eligibility review, Fraud/risk review, and quality gate resources.

- **Stop/go thresholds.** For Redesign A, go if high-value complaint/reopen drops by roughly one-third to one-half, R1/R2 deviations fall sharply, and reviewer/fraud queues remain below the point where Premium SLAs break. Stop or narrow scope if manual inflow approaches the top of the expected 15-35% increase without clear downstream benefit, or if Premium low-risk cases drift above the 24-hour service-credit threshold. For Redesign B, go if repeated evidence requests fall roughly in the promised 30-45% range while abandonment in low-digital-literacy cohorts stays roughly flat or rises only marginally. Stop if the cycle-time gain is achieved mainly through abandonment. For Redesign C, go if targeted high-value reshipments and complaint/reopen rates fall materially while added dispatch delay stays within the intended 6-18 hour band for those targeted cases.

- **Sequencing logic.** Run Redesign B second, after the Redesign A pilot stabilizes, because sharper routing will otherwise just feed more borderline cases into the existing evidence loop. Run Redesign C third, after A and B, so the organization can test whether a targeted fulfillment gate reduces downstream complaints in a cleaner population instead of masking upstream misrouting or evidence defects.

- **How to learn whether improvements are real rather than shifted downstream.** Use stepped-wedge or matched holdout cohorts by segment, value band, and channel. Evaluate from intake through at least 30-60 days after close, not just to initial close. A redesign counts as an improvement only if its apparent cycle-time gain is not offset by later reopen, reshipment, service-credit, denial reversal, or compliance-exception movement. In other words, measure the full loop, not just the first pass through the process.
