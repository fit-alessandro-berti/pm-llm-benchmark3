**Queueing quantities and units**

**Opening-hours convention**
All counts are bounded to 07:00–19:00. Cases can carry over overnight, but no service occurs when closed, so the “system” includes an overnight inventory that is not served and not observed in completion counts until the next opening block.

**At least five threats to naive inference**
1. **Service time  flow time:** Median service  25–35 min, but WIP is 20–50 cases; implied sojourn times are 2–5 h. Replacing flow time with service time would understate waiting by 4–10×.

   - **Effect:** A single service-time distribution at “33% complex” is not observed in any regime; it is a mixture of a 25-min median and a 35-min median, which underestimates tail risk.

3. **Staffing / capacity / WIP (Simpson’s-like reversal)**
   - Pooled WIP  29.9; pooled staff  5.3 FTE; ratio  5.6 cases/FTE.
   - Regime A: 20 WIP / 6 FTE = 3.3.
   - Regime D: 43 WIP / 6 FTE = 7.2.
   - **Effect:** Pooled data show *higher staff correlates with higher WIP* (Apr 16 staff 7  WIP 52; Apr 29 staff 7  WIP 43). Segmented view shows staff is a **reaction** to mix/backlog, not a cause of congestion; interpreting the pooled correlation literally would misallocate staff.

4. **Outage / recovery hidden in averages**
   - Pooled mean completion  86/day < arrivals  91/day, suggesting mild growth.
   - Actual pattern: 58 on Apr 15 (deficit 47), 129 on Apr 16 (surplus 17), 112 on Apr 17 (surplus 13).
   - **Effect:** Pooling does not reveal the acute crisis or the rapid recovery; it flattens both into a mild drift.

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

**Representative hourly vectors (12 bins, 07–19)**

| Day type | Mean/bin | Variance (sample) | Fano (var/mean) | CV (std/mean) | Lag-1 autocorr |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary (2026-04-07) | 8.42 | ~28 | ~3.3 | ~0.63 | moderate positive |
| Outage (2026-04-15) | 8.75 | ~26 | ~3.0 | ~0.58 | moderate |
| Bursty (2026-04-21) | 9.58 | ~85 | ~8.9 | ~0.96 | high (spike carries) |
| Weekend (2026-04-25) | 4.25 | ~5 | ~1.2 | ~0.52 | low-moderate |

**Interpretation**
- **Overdispersion:** Fano >> 1 on all but the weekend; Poisson assumptions are rejected. The bursty day (Apr 21) has Fano  9, indicating clustering far beyond random arrival.
- **Coefficient of variation:** CV  1 on Apr 21 vs ~0.5–0.6 on ordinary days; the process is not just “more arrivals” but “more variable arrivals.”
- **Lag dependence:** Positive serial correlation within the hourly sequence (especially Apr 21) means a high-arrival bin is followed by elevated bins; this violates independent-increment assumptions for simple M/M/c approximations.
- **Daily sequence:** Lag-1 autocorrelation of daily arrivals is positive because regimes persist (baseline weeks stay low; complex weeks stay high). Change-point evidence: the rolling 3-day mean of `complex_pct` jumps sharply at 2026-04-20 (index 14); this is a structural break, not random fluctuation.
- **Stationarity:** Rejected. Mean, variance, and service-time distribution shift at Apr 20; staff and backlog shift at Apr 29.

**Limit of four representatives:** They are selected, not sampled; they do not cover the intervention (Apr 29+), they do not include a “post-clearance” stable day, and they are presented as “representative” rather than stochastic draws. One cannot build a full stochastic model (inter-arrival distribution, batch sizes, correlation structure) from four aggregated profiles.

**Boundary conditions for the defensible case (stable baseline):** The 7-day window is long enough that overnight carry-over averages out because arrivals  completions; we use the **arrival count divided by 720 minutes** as  because arrivals occur only during opening hours; WIP is averaged over 15-minute intervals within those same hours. The result is an **implied mean sojourn time in minutes**, not a median, and not equal to service time (mean service is unknown; median  25 min).

# 7. Operational implications

**Regime-based actions**
- **Stable baseline:** 6 FTE is adequate for ~100 arrivals/day at 25 min service. Peak smoothing (28–33% in 08–10) is manageable with current scheduling.
- **Outage / recovery:** After 180 min disruption, temporary overcapacity (7 FTE, 129 completions) is justified as **clearance**, not as a new normal. Staff should return to 6 FTE once WIP falls below ~25, or risk sustaining high cost for transient backlog.
- **Complex / burst:** The jump to 35-min median service with 45% complex cases reduces effective capacity ~30% even at constant staff. Peak 40–50% in 08–10 compounds this. Recommendation: either add 1 FTE permanently (as intervention shows) or reduce case complexity (coding/rework) rather than assuming “same staff = same output.”
- **Intervention:** Completions exceeding arrivals (121 vs 116) is the right signal for backlog reduction, but only the first 3 days are observed; do not declare “problem solved” at 32 WIP if mix remains 43%.

**Data-collection recommendations**
- Log exact intervention start time; the “during 2026-04-29” ambiguity prevents precise change-point timing.
- Capture 07:00 backlog explicitly for every day.
- Record case-level arrival timestamp, service start/end, variant flag, and staff assignment; only with this can PM4Py methods (`extract_temporal_features_dataframe`, `get_case_arrival_average`, `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate`) be applied correctly.
- Record hourly completions at case level (not just aggregate) to distinguish backlog clearance from new demand.

**Policy that looks beneficial pooled but is risky segmented**
- **Pooled view:** Average WIP is 30, average staff 5.3, so “reduce staff to 5.0 and rely on efficiency” seems plausible because the mean suggests moderate load.
- **Segmented view:** During Regime D, 6 FTE already yields 7.2 WIP/FTE and backlog growth; during Regime E, 7 FTE is needed just to drive WIP down. Reducing staff based on the pooled mean would hit the complex/burst period hardest and prevent recovery, causing a rebound backlog into the next week. **The safe path is regime-conditioned staffing, not mean-conditioned staffing.**

# 8. Reproducible Python

- Pooled and regime summaries calculated in-memory.

"""

# ------------------------------------------------------------------

    "completed": [94,99,100,103,95,41,38,101,105,58,129,112,46,41,94,

    "median_service_min": [24,25,24,25,26,29,28,25,26,31,33,29,30,29,
                           34,35,36,34,35,36,35,36,35,33,32,33,35,34],
    "p90_service_min": [44,46,45,47,49,55,53,48,50,68,72,60,58,56,78,

    "staff_fte": [6.0,6.0,6.0,6.0,5.5,2.5,2.5,6.0,6.0,6.0,7.0,6.5,2.5,

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Pooled and regime summaries (heuristic segmentation)
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
def regime_heuristic(row):
    # Transparent heuristic; not definitive ground truth
    if row["outage_min"] > 0:
        return "outage"
    if row["date"].date() >= pd.Timestamp("2026-04-29").date():
        return "intervention"
    if row["complex_pct"] > 35:
        return "high_complex"
    return "baseline"

daily["regime"] = daily.apply(regime_heuristic, axis=1)

pooled = daily[["arrivals","completed","mean_wip","staff_fte","complex_pct"]].mean()

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Burstiness / dispersion indicators (on representative hourly vectors)
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
def burst_metrics(vec):
    arr = np.array(vec, dtype=float)
    mu = arr.mean()
    var = arr.var(ddof=1)
    fano = var / mu if mu > 1e-6 else np.nan
    cv = arr.std(ddof=1) / mu if mu > 1e-6 else np.nan
    # simple lag-1 autocorrelation
    if len(arr) > 1:
        lag1 = np.corrcoef(arr[:-1], arr[1:])[0, 1]
    else:
        lag1 = np.nan
    return fano, cv, lag1

burst_results = []

    burst_results.append({
        "day": r["representative_day"],
        "fano": round(fano, 2),
        "cv": round(cv, 2),
        "lag1_autocorr": round(lag1, 2)
    })

burst_df = pd.DataFrame(burst_results)
print("\n=== BURSTINESS / DISPERSION ===")
print(burst_df.to_string(index=False))

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
arrivals = daily["arrivals"].values
lag1_daily = np.corrcoef(arrivals[:-1], arrivals[1:])[0, 1]
print(f"\nDaily arrival lag-1 autocorrelation = {lag1_daily:.3f}")

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Transparent change-point / segmentation heuristic
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
daily["complex_roll3"] = daily["complex_pct"].rolling(window=3, min_periods=3).mean()

print(f"\n=== CHANGE-POINT HEURISTIC ===")
print("Largest rolling (3-day) complex_pct step at index:", max_idx)
print("Date:", max_row["date"].date(), "| roll_diff:", round(max_row["roll_diff"], 2))
print("This is a heuristic; it detects the Apr 20 mix jump but is not causal proof.")

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Little's Law for explicitly chosen windows
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Window definitions match regime assignments above.
# Units: L = mean WIP (cases); lambda = arrivals per minute over 720 min opening.
# We use arrivals as throughput proxy where arrivals  completed (baseline,
# intervention); we flag recovery/complex as approximate/descriptive only.
OPEN_MIN = 12 * 60  # 720

def ll_window(subdf, label, defensible_note):
    L = subdf["mean_wip"].mean()
    lam = subdf["arrivals"].mean() / OPEN_MIN  # per minute
    W = L / lam if lam > 0 else np.nan
    # Also show using completions for comparison where valid
    lam_c = subdf["completed"].mean() / OPEN_MIN
    W_c = L / lam_c if lam_c > 0 else np.nan
    print(f"\n--- {label} ({defensible_note}) ---")
    print(f"  Mean WIP L = {L:.1f} cases")
    print(f"  Arrivals lambda = {lam:.4f}/min  => W = {W:.0f} min ({W/60:.1f} h)")
    print(f"  Completions lambda = {lam_c:.4f}/min => W = {W_c:.0f} min ({W_c/60:.1f} h)")

# Explicit windows: baseline, outage (not defensible but shown for contrast),
# high_complex, intervention.
ll_window(daily[daily["regime"]=="baseline"], "STABLE BASELINE", "Applicable (arr  comp, low carry-over)")
ll_window(daily[daily["regime"]=="outage"], "OUTAGE DAY", "NOT DEFENSIBLE (disrupted boundary)")
ll_window(daily[daily["regime"]=="high_complex"], "COMPLEX/BURST", "Approximate descriptive only")
ll_window(daily[daily["regime"]=="intervention"], "INTERVENTION", "Approximate only (clearance component)")
