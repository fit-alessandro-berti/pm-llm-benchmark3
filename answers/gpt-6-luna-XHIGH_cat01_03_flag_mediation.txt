## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

I treat the flag as an element of a routing policy bundle, generated from an imperfect risk score, rather than as a freely manipulable exposure. The primary estimands are policy effects and interventional mediation analogues. I do not use natural direct or indirect effects: their cross-world mediator assumptions are especially implausible when review and escalation are chosen after triage, can be affected by earlier policy, and alter shared queues. A controlled direct effect can be defined for a tightly specified hypothetical setting (for example, force the same review and escalation decisions and queue regime under both display conditions), but it is not the main policy question and may have poor support.

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | P2 versus P0, retaining priority triage and replacing mandatory review with specified human discretion | All arrivals in a rollout period, including unflagged cases exposed to queue spillovers | Mean or restricted mean arrival-to-close time; also close-by-24-hours | Total system policy effect at a stated rollout saturation, including direct and spillover effects | Yes, once eligibility, discretion rule, saturation, and capacity are specified | Nonrandom policy adoption, queue interference, and time-varying case mix |
| E2 | P1 versus P0, hide only the displayed flag while score, human information, and automated flag routing remain unchanged | Flagged cases; separately report all arrivals for displaced effects | Arrival-to-triage-start and arrival-to-triage-done | Total effect of display suppression; a controlled direct effect only if routing and queue assignment are held fixed by design | Conditionally: must specify which systems and staff still receive the flag | P1 as written leaves ambiguity about machine routing; workload and triage resources may change |
| E3 | Under a fixed priority policy, replace the flag-dependent probability of manual-review occurrence with a common stochastic review rule | Eligible flagged cases, with effects also measured over all arrivals | Cycle time and downstream decision measures | Interventional indirect analogue through review occurrence, with review-wait distribution standardized separately | Yes, if the stochastic rule and eligible population are specified | Review occurrence is confounded by risk, complexity, triage findings, and reviewer judgment; conditioning on reviewed cases selects |
| E4 | Keep review occurrence governed by a specified rule, but draw manual queue waits from the P0-like versus P3-like distribution for the same measured queue history | Cases routed to review under the specified common occurrence rule; report the population-level stochastic effect | Manual start time and total cycle time | Interventional indirect analogue through manual-review waiting, not a natural indirect effect | Conditionally; queue intervention must specify capacity, priority, and congestion saturation | Wait is post-policy and affected by other cases; queue state may be treatment-induced and shared |
| E5 | Hold review policy fixed and compare specified escalation rules or capacities, with escalation occurrence and senior waiting generated under each rule | Cases eligible for escalation and all arrivals in the relevant queue system | Escalation start, close time, and adjudicated decision quality | Interventional analogue through escalation occurrence and escalation queue delay; report them separately | Yes only after criteria, capacity, and eligibility are operationally defined | Escalation is selected by post-review findings and risk; scarce senior capacity creates interference |
| E6 | P3 versus P0: add incremental, protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases | Flagged eligible cases and all cases sharing review resources | Arrival-to-close, review waiting, and unflagged waiting | Total policy effect; queue-mediated components are secondary interventional analogues | Yes if “protected” means specified incremental staffing and priority rules | Capacity may not be truly incremental; staffing assignment and queue spillovers can confound comparisons |
| E7 | P2 versus P0 (or P4 versus P0 under a precise rule) | Eligible flagged cases plus concurrent unflagged cases | Error against blinded independent adjudication, missed high-risk cases, rework, and final recorded outcome | Total effect on decision quality; recorded approve/reject alone is only a process outcome, not accuracy | Yes with a fixed audit protocol and outcome horizon | No gold standard in supplied data; differential verification, hidden risk, and policy-induced selection |
| E8 | P1 versus P0 while forcing the same priority, review, escalation, staffing, and queue-state paths | Cases in a common-support subpopulation | Triage and cycle time | Hypothetical controlled direct effect of display alone | Mathematically definable; often not operationally feasible | Treatment-induced mediators cannot generally be fixed without changing the system; limited positivity and cross-world interpretation |

For E3–E5, “interventional” means draw mediator values from an explicitly specified policy distribution conditional on pre-mediator and time-updated history, then compare resulting outcomes. This avoids requiring each case’s mutually incompatible mediator values under two policies. It still needs sequential exchangeability, positivity, adequate history measurement, and an interference model; it is not identified by this case table.

Time-ordered process-mechanism diagram:

```text
intake: underlying_risk, risk_score, complexity, arrival_load, U_unmeasured_case_features
      -> flag (policy assignment) -> priority_lane
arrival + Q_triage_t + priority_lane -> triage_wait -> triage_start
triage_start -> triage_service -> triage_done
triage findings + risk + complexity + human judgment + flag
      -> manual_review occurrence

              -> manual_service -> manual_done
manual_done + review findings + human judgment -> escalation occurrence
escalation=1 -> Q_senior_t -> escalation_queue_wait -> escalation_start
             -> senior_service -> escalation_done
last observed stage completion -> residual_to_close -> close
risk + complexity + reviewer skill + all findings + policy -> decision_outcome

Across cases: each assigned lane/review/escalation contributes workload to later
Q_triage_t, Q_manual_t, and Q_senior_t; those queue states affect other cases' waits.
```

The observed timestamps split stage service from the wait immediately before a stage, but do not observe queue state, decision time outside named stages, review findings, or the underlying risk fully. Conditioning on manual review or escalation can create selection because these are post-flag decisions influenced by risk and triage findings. Manual occurrence, earlier queue exposure, or reviewer assignment can also become treatment-induced confounders of later mediator-outcome relations. A case’s flag changes shared workload, so the usual no-interference assumption fails: its policy can delay other cases, including unflagged ones. The diagram is acyclic only when queue state and decisions are indexed in time; a static case-level graph would hide this feedback.

The flagged cases begin triage much sooner: median arrival-to-triage-start is 12 minutes versus 150 for unflagged cases, and median triage service is 36 versus 57 minutes. The pattern is consistent with priority-lane acceleration. Yet all 10 flagged cases receive manual review, with a median 405-minute interval from triage completion to review start; among the four reviewed unflagged cases that wait is 105 minutes. ## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Does crossing the flag threshold change the policy outcome locally? | If a deterministic score cutoff exists, run a fuzzy or sharp regression-discontinuity analysis near that cutoff. It estimates the local effect of the assignment bundle induced by the cutoff, not a universal biological effect of “flag.” | Centered risk_score, separate smooth functions by side, intake time/load, complexity, policy era, and pre-cutoff covariates | No precise score manipulation, continuity of potential outcomes, no other rule changes at the same cutoff, adequate local support | Density and covariate continuity checks; placebo cutoffs and pre-flag outcomes | Discontinuities in complexity, staffing, or pre-policy outcomes, or no first-stage change in routing, would undermine the design |
| Are wait differences explained by queue state? | Reconstruct queue length, workload, server availability, and position immediately before each stage; estimate interval-specific outcomes with time-varying queue histories, then report both case-level and system-level effects | Risk, complexity, arrival time, queue state just before routing, resources and shift; avoid adjusting away downstream queues when estimating total policy effects | Queue logs are accurate, arrival ordering and priority rules known, and concurrent demand captured | Check predicted waits against observed starts; test whether apparent flag effects remain within comparable queue states | Near-zero adjusted manual-wait contrast with queue-state balance would weaken a flag-specific delay mechanism; a residual congestion response would strengthen it |
| What is the effect of P2 or P3 as an operational policy? | Randomize eligible cases or queue-time blocks to a clearly specified routing/capacity rule; an encouragement version estimates assignment effect, and uptake can support an IV complier effect if exclusion and monotonicity hold | Block on risk/complexity, arrival-load band, and calendar/shift; record assignment and adherence | Ethical equipoise, adherence captured, no cross-arm contamination or modeled spillover, sufficient sample | Verify first-stage change in review occurrence or wait; examine pre-treatment balance and protocol overrides | No routing/wait first stage means the tested contrast was not delivered; excess audited misses or unflagged queue harm reverses favorable interpretation |
| How much of cycle time operates through review occurrence versus review waiting? | Longitudinal g-computation or a marginal structural approach with stochastic interventions: standardize occurrence first, then waiting conditional on queue history, then escalation; estimate each interventional analogue and their joint effect | Baseline risk/complexity/load plus time-updated triage findings, queue state, reviewer, and prior stages; model post-treatment confounders rather than ordinary regression conditioning | Sequential exchangeability, positivity, correct models, consistent interventions, and explicit interference handling | Compare estimated mediator distributions to observed support; truncate/examine extreme weights; simulate observed policy outcomes | Poor overlap, unstable weights, or large unmeasured-confounding sensitivity would make component estimates too fragile to guide policy |
| Is a noncausal association appearing before the flag can act? | Use a negative-control outcome measured entirely before flag display/routing, such as intake documentation duration or pre-triage processing time; test association with later flag after case-mix adjustment | Pre-flag case characteristics and arrival context, without post-treatment variables | The flag cannot affect the chosen pre-treatment outcome and shared queue cannot causally reach backward in time | Repeat across placebo outcomes and policy eras; confirm the timestamp truly precedes flag availability | A strong residual association suggests hidden case mix or timing structure; a null result is reassuring but does not prove exchangeability |
| Could omitted risk account for observed effects? | Validate risk_score and flag against a blinded risk audit; use quantitative bias analysis varying prevalence and effects of hidden risk, plus bounds for misclassification | Risk-score band, complexity, outcome history, triage findings and measured load | Audit sample representative and quality label reliable; sensitivity parameters cover plausible omissions | Tipping-point plot; compare conclusions with and without audited cases; test calibration by complexity/load | Plausible hidden-risk values that erase the P2 benefit or reveal substantial missed high-risk cases would reverse the recommendation |
| Do cases affect other cases through shared queues? | Randomize queue pods or time blocks to different rollout saturations, with washout/crossover where feasible; estimate direct effects and spillover effects on unassigned/unflagged cases | Pod, block, time, workload, case mix, staffing; define exposure as own assignment plus peer treatment saturation | Partial interference within chosen queue clusters, manageable carryover, stable measurement and enough clusters | Test effects on unassigned cases and later arrivals; inspect cross-pod borrowing and washout | Material unflagged delays or cross-cluster spillover requires system-level policy evaluation rather than an isolated case effect |
| Are timestamp contrasts robust to their semantics? | Reconcile event logs with operational definitions and independently sampled audit trails; repeat using local-time corrected and rounded intervals | Stage, system version, daylight/time-zone handling, overnight shift | Start/completion times reflect actual work and wait, not batch entry or retrospective documentation | Ordering checks, impossible negative durations, duplicate timestamps, and reconciliation with queue logs | If start times are documentation times or clocks differ, the stage decomposition and mechanism interpretation must be rebuilt |

The supplied 20 records are descriptive support for hypotheses, not enough to execute these designs. In particular, risk_score, complexity, and arrival_load alone cannot satisfy exchangeability for mediation or reconstruct shared queues.

## 6. Policy comparison

**Limited pilot: P2.** Eligibility: flagged medium-complexity cases in a prespecified risk-score band with genuine clinical/operational equipoise; keep high-risk red-line cases, statutory review triggers, and clinician overrides under current review. Randomize queue-time blocks within shift and arrival-load strata to current routing or discretionary review, so shared-queue spillovers can be estimated; log actual assignment and overrides. Set a review-backlog cap and protect unflagged cases with a maximum wait threshold; suspend P2 blocks if either limit is exceeded. Primary outcomes: restricted mean arrival-to-close time and review wait among eligible cases. Secondary outcomes: review occurrence, escalation, close-by-24-hours, unflagged waits in the same queue, rework, and blinded audit misses/decision quality. Minimum data: exact flag availability and policy version, risk score, complexity, rationale/findings, stage start and completion timestamps, queue length and staff availability over time, resource IDs, assignment/overrides, close, and independent outcome audit. Stop or pause for a prespecified rise in audited serious misses, unflagged wait harm, protocol violations, or sustained backlog breach. Analyze assignment-to-policy as primary, with uptake as a secondary mechanism measure.

# Parse local clock times as supplied; do not silently infer a time zone.
wide = pd.read_csv(StringIO(CSV), na_values=["-"])
time_cols = [
    "arrival", "triage_start", "triage_done", "manual_start", "manual_done",
    "escalation_start", "escalation_done", "close",
]
for col in time_cols:
    wide[col] = pd.to_datetime(wide[col], errors="coerce")

def minutes_between(start, end):
    return (end - start).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0

wide["arrival_to_triage_start_min"] = minutes_between(wide["arrival"], wide["triage_start"])
wide["triage_service_min"] = minutes_between(wide["triage_start"], wide["triage_done"])
wide["triage_done_to_manual_start_min"] = minutes_between(
    wide["triage_done"], wide["manual_start"]
)
wide["manual_service_min"] = minutes_between(wide["manual_start"], wide["manual_done"])
wide["manual_done_to_escalation_start_min"] = minutes_between(
    wide["manual_done"], wide["escalation_start"]
)
wide["escalation_service_min"] = minutes_between(
    wide["escalation_start"], wide["escalation_done"]
)

    wide["manual_done"].combine_first(wide["triage_done"])
)

wide["cycle_time_min"] = minutes_between(wide["arrival"], wide["close"])

    "arrival_to_triage_start_min", "triage_service_min",
    "triage_done_to_manual_start_min", "manual_service_min",
    "manual_done_to_escalation_start_min", "escalation_service_min",
    "residual_to_close_min", "cycle_time_min",
]

def print_stage_summary(frame, group_name):
    print(f"\nStage summary by {group_name} (median minutes; observed support):")
    for value, group in frame.groupby(group_name, dropna=False):
        n = len(group)
        if n < 5:
            warnings.warn(f"Small subgroup {group_name}={value!r}: n={n}")
        print(f"  {group_name}={value!r}, cases={n}")
        for col in stage_cols:
            observed = group[col].dropna()
            if len(observed) < 5:
                warnings.warn(
                    f"Small interval support for {group_name}={value!r}, "
                    f"{col}: {len(observed)}/{n}"

            median = observed.median() if len(observed) else float("nan")

    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",

    resource_key="org:resource",

# according to the library's performance definition. They are not causal effects,
# and they are not identical to the explicit stage wait/service intervals above.

        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",

        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",

    print("  end activities:", freq_end)

for flag_value, group in wide.groupby("flag"):
    observed = group["cycle_time_min"].dropna()
    print(
        f"  flag={flag_value}: n={len(group)}, "
        f"cycle median={observed.median()} min, support={len(observed)}/{len(group)}"
    )

    "\nInterpret absent stages as not occurring, not as zero-duration observed "
    "stages. DFG differences are descriptive: case mix, policy selection, and "
    "shared-queue interference prevent a causal reading."
)

The event log intentionally contains only stages present in the source. PM4Py DFG edges describe observed event sequence and its library-defined performance measure, not the separate queue/service decomposition or a causal mediation effect. The explicit interval summaries retain NA and support counts for missing stages.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk:** If an audited risk measure shows eligible flagged cases have substantial unobserved severe risk and discretionary review under P2 misses it, retain mandatory review for that stratum or reverse the P2 recommendation.
2. **Queue interference:** If randomized saturation shows P2 or P3 materially delays unflagged or later-arriving cases, or apparent case-level gains are displacement, prefer the policy with the best queue-wide outcome.
3. **Decision quality:** If blinded adjudication finds a clinically meaningful increase in serious errors, rework, or unequal error rates under P2, stop it even if cycle time improves.
4. **Timestamp semantics:** If audit shows local timestamps are batch-entry times, mixed clocks, or not actual stage starts/completions, withdraw the stage-delay interpretation until corrected event data reproduce it.
5. **Positivity or overlap:** If risk/complexity bands contain no comparable cases under alternative policies, do not extrapolate a mediation or subgroup effect there; restrict the pilot or choose a different design.
6. **Reviewer behavior change:** If hiding the flag or changing routing changes reviewer diligence, escalation thresholds, or override behavior in ways not captured by current measures, revise the mechanism and policy comparison using measured behavior and audited quality.

## 9. Final synthesis

Directly observed in these 20 records, flagged cases enter triage much sooner: median arrival-to-triage-start is 12 minutes versus 150 minutes for unflagged cases, and triage service is 36 versus 57 minutes. At the same time, all flagged cases undergo manual review; their median triage-done-to-review-start interval is 405 minutes. Six proceed to escalation, where the median wait to escalation start is 420 minutes. Median arrival-to-close is 987 minutes for flagged and 345 for unflagged cases. Missing stages are absent events, not zero-time stages.

A plausible process explanation is a policy bundle: priority triage accelerates the front end, while mandatory reviews increase demand on a shared queue and some cases then wait for scarce senior capacity. Queue spillovers can affect unflagged cases too. This is consistent with the intervals, but risk_score is imperfect, complexity and latent risk affect routing and duration, and review/escalation are selected after treatment. With only a coarse arrival-load snapshot and no queue histories, reviewer rationale, or truth-labeled decisions, the data do not identify direct effects, mediation proportions, or decision quality.

The most useful next evidence is a limited P2 policy pilot among prespecified medium-risk cases in equipoise, retaining red-line review and clinician overrides. Randomize queue-time blocks, measure contemporaneous queue states and outcomes for all cases sharing the queue, and audit decisions blindly. That design can estimate a policy effect while showing whether shorter cycle times come from fewer reviews, shorter waits, or displacement—and whether quality remains acceptable.
