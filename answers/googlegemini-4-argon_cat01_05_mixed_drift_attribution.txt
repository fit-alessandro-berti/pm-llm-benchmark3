## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The divergence between `raw_model_fitness` (collapsing from 0.93 to 0.55 in March) and `mapped_model_fitness` (holding at 0.90) demonstrates that the March discontinuity is predominantly a **semantic and data-engineering artifact**, not a sudden breakdown in business compliance. Because conformance checking penalizes unexpected activity labels (`Eligibility Screen`) and extra technical events (`Open Case`), raw alignments register massive move-on-log penalties. However, recovering 0.90 fitness via label mapping and deduplication **does not prove the underlying business process remained identical**. Mapping assumes functional equivalence; if `Eligibility Screen` executes different automated business rules than `Validate Eligibility`, or if the residual 0.02–0.03 fitness drop (from 0.925 baseline to 0.90) reflects real edge-case routing glitches introduced by the migration, simple synonym replacement masks behavioral drift.

Different artifacts corrupt downstream analyses asymmetrically. Duplicated `Open Case` events distort trace length, prefix-based predictive monitoring, and DFG start-activity frequencies (`Open Case -> Register` appearing 26 times in March), yet hardly affect median cycle times if timestamps are identical. Conversely, the jump in `missing_resource_pct` (1.0% to 4.8–6.5%, concentrated in migrated steps) leaves control-flow DFGs intact but severely biases organizational mining, handover-of-work networks, and individual workload/service-time estimation due to informative missingness.

Crucially, the post-May shifts cannot be dismissed as logging drift. After normalization, `mapped_model_fitness` erodes further to 0.86–0.87 in July and November, normalized `Assess -> Request Info` rates more than double (12 to 29 per 100 cases), `Assess -> Senior Assessment` quadruples in October (5 to 22), and median/p90 cycle times surge by +58%/+78%. These changes occur under a stable post-March schema (11 distinct labels) and reflect genuine control-flow rework, routing adaptation, and queueing congestion.

### 2. Normalized DFG-distance analysis
Let $\mathbf{r}_t \in \mathbb{R}^7$ be the vector of normalized relation rates per 100 cases in month $t$ across the 7 reported edges. We compute the **$L_1$ (Manhattan) distance** $D_1(t, t_0) = \sum_{i=1}^7 |r_{t,i} - r_{t_0,i}|$ and **$L_2$ (Euclidean) distance** $D_2(t, t_0) = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^7 (r_{t,i} - r_{t_0,i})^2}$ relative to baseline $t_0 = \text{2026-01}$, plus consecutive step distances $D_1(t, t_{\text{prev}})$.

*Assumptions about omitted edges:* The 7 edges omit early dropout transitions (e.g., `Eligibility -> Close/Reject` accounting for the ~2–5 per 100 drop between `Register->Eligibility` and `CollectDocs->Assess`), direct `SeniorAssessment -> RequestInfo` edges, split decisions (`Approve` vs `Reject`), and terminal `Decision -> Close` edges. We assume omitted terminal edges scale deterministically with case completion, meaning $D_1$ on this 7-edge core captures the primary internal routing variance (rework and escalation) but underbounds total graph edit distance.

### 3. Decomposition of cycle-time deterioration
We decompose the median cycle time increase from Baseline (**31.15h**) to two peak regimes—**Summer Peak (July–August avg: 44.6h, $\Delta = +13.45\text{h}$)** and **Renewal Peak (October–November avg: 48.4h, $\Delta = +17.25\text{h}$)**—and address the P90 tail (**+39h** in Jul–Aug; **+50.5h** in Oct–Nov):
* **Direct Service Time (`Assess` execution): ~5% of $\Delta$ (+0.5h to +0.8h).** Observational notes show median `Assess` service time grew only ~0.6h (4.8h to 5.4h). Even accounting for modest service time in other steps, active task execution explains well under 1 hour of the 13–17h median shift.
* **Control-Flow / Rework Loops (`Request Info` + re-collection + re-assessment): ~30–40% of $\Delta$ in Jul–Aug (+4.0h to +5.5h); ~25–35% in Oct–Nov (+4.5h to +6.0h).** In May (before staffing cuts), a +12.5 pp rework jump raised median cycle time by +5.35h (~0.43h of median shift per +1 pp of rework, though partly confounded with +4.5 pp complexity). Applying this empirical sensitivity to July–August (+16.5 pp rework) yields ~5h attributable to extra loop iterations and external document turnaround.
* **Queueing and Resource Contention (Staffing loss + Priority re-ordering): ~45–55% of $\Delta$ in Jul–Aug (+6.0h to +7.5h); ~35–45% in Oct–Nov (+6.0h to +8.0h).** Between May and August, rework stabilized/declined (24%->29%->27%) and Supplier Z fell (43%->38%), yet median cycle time rose another +8.5h (36.5h->45.0h) and p90 surged +26h (84h->110h). Furthermore, in September, when rework dropped to 20%, median cycle time remained 41.5h (+10.35h over baseline). This persistent floor and heavy tail expansion (P90/Median ratio rising from 2.18 in Jan to 2.44 in Aug and 2.47 in Nov) are classic queueing signatures of the June assessor loss and July priority rule starving standard cases.
* **Population / Case Mix (High-complexity share & `Senior Assessment` routing): ~10–15% of $\Delta$ in Jul–Aug (+1.0h to +2.0h); ~25–35% in Oct–Nov (+4.5h to +6.0h).** In July–August, high complexity was only +8 pp above baseline. In October–November, complexity jumped +22.5 pp (to 41–45%) and volume jumped +26%, adding a second assessment stage (`Assess -> Senior Assessment` jumping from 7 to 22 per 100 cases) and compounding specialist queue congestion.

### 4. Candidate change points by drift dimension
* **Logging / Semantic Drift:**
  1. **2026-03** (Primary): Sudden step change in `raw_model_fitness` (-0.38), `distinct_activity_labels` (9->11), `missing_resource_pct` (+3.75 pp), and `duplicate_open_case_pct` (+4 pp).
  2. **2026-10** (Secondary / Potential): Surge in `Assess -> Senior Assessment` (7->22 per 100 cases). Given the baseline note that `Senior Assessment` was *"historically recorded inconsistently,"* part of the October spike may reflect improved logging compliance alongside genuine seasonal complexity.
* **Behavioral / Control-Flow Drift:**
  1. **2026-05** (Primary): Sudden shift in `rework_variant_pct` (13%->24%), `fast_variant_pct` (55%->45%), and normalized DFG step distance ($D_1 = 37$).
  2. **2026-10** (Secondary): Second structural DFG shift ($D_1 = 37$ from Sept), driven by `Assess -> Senior Assessment` (+15 per 100 cases) and renewed rework (+6 pp). *(Note: 2026-09 also acts as a recovery inflection point).*
* **Performance Drift:**
  1. **2026-05** (Primary onset): Median cycle time breaks out of the 30.8–32.0h band to 36.5h (+17.2%); p90 jumps from 71h to 84h.
  2. **2026-06 / 2026-07** (Regime intensification & tail divergence): Median crosses 40.8h->44.2h and p90 hits 95h->104h despite Supplier Z plateauing, marking the shift from rework-driven delay to capacity/priority-queueing delay.
  3. **2026-10** (Seasonal performance shock): Median jumps from 41.5h to 47.8h (p90 from 96h to 116h) on arrival of renewal volume.

### 5. Global vs. mechanism-specific change points
The data **strongly refute a single global change point** and support **multiple mechanism-specific change points**. A univariate change-point detector on `raw_model_fitness` or schema counts flags **March 2026**, when cycle times ($\sim 31.2\text{h}$) and normalized DFG distances ($D_1=3$) were completely invariant. Conversely, a detector on rework rates or normalized DFGs flags **May 2026** and **October 2026**, whereas tail performance (p90 minus median) accelerates across **June–August 2026**. Conflating these into one global timestamp would misattribute summer queueing delays to the March IT migration or misclassify the March schema shift as an operational failure.

### Primary Monitoring Population
Treat the **Level 1 segment: `Other Suppliers` $\times$ `Standard Complexity`** as the **primary baseline monitoring population** (complemented by `Other Suppliers` $\times$ `High Complexity`).
* **Why:** In any month, `Other Suppliers` represent 54% to 86% of cases, and `Standard Complexity` represents 55% to 80%, giving this cell robust monthly support ($\sim 210$ to $430$ cases/month). Crucially, it is insulated from the exogenous Supplier Z defect shock and from high-complexity `Senior Assessment` routing ambiguities, making it the cleanest "canary" for internal capacity degradation, general assessor behavioral drift, and the negative externality (starvation) of the July priority queue rule.

### Handling Seasonality and Multiple Testing
1. **Seasonality:** Do not compare October–November against trailing summer windows for volume, complexity, or pooled cycle times. Instead, use **1) Covariate-stratified monitoring** (evaluating monitors *within* Level 1 `Complexity` $\times$ `Supplier` cells, since cell-conditional physics should remain stable even when cell weights shift), and **2) Year-over-Year (YoY) seasonal reference windows** (comparing Oct–Nov 2026 against Oct–Nov 2025 arrival and complexity distributions) paired with dynamic capacity-adjusted SLA bands.
2. **Multiple Testing:** Running 11 monitors across 4 Level-1 strata each week/month generates $\ge 44$ hypothesis tests per cycle, guaranteeing false alarms under naive $\alpha = 0.05$. We control this via a **hierarchical two-stage gate**:
   * *Stage 1 (Family-wise / FDR control):* Group monitors into three families (Data/Schema [#1–3], Population/Control-Flow [#4–6, #10–11], and Performance/Queueing [#7–9]). Apply the **Benjamini-Hochberg False Discovery Rate (FDR) procedure at $q = 0.05$** within each family across strata.
   * *Stage 2 (Minimum Effect Size + Persistence):* Require both statistical significance *and* a domain-meaningful minimum effect size (e.g., $D_1 > 15$, wait shift $> 15\%$, or persistence across 2 consecutive weekly windows) before triggering an operational alert, except for Monitor #1 (hard schema errors).

def run_part_a_analysis():

    df_dfg = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(DFG_RATES_CSV))

    # --- Step 1: Validate the Data ---
    assert len(df_sum) == 12, "Expected 12 monthly rows in summary."
    assert df_sum.isna().sum().sum() == 0, "Missing values detected in summary."
    pct_cols = [c for c in df_sum.columns if c.endswith("_pct")]
    assert (
        (df_sum[pct_cols] >= 0).all().all()
        and (df_sum[pct_cols] <= 100).all().all()
    ), "Invalid percentages."
    assert (
        df_sum["p90_cycle_h"] >= df_sum["median_cycle_h"]
    ).all(), "p90 must be >= median."
    assert (
        df_sum["mapped_model_fitness"] >= df_sum["raw_model_fitness"]
    ).all(), "Mapped fitness < raw fitness."

    # --- Step 2 & 4: Baseline-Relative Changes & Raw-to-Mapped Fitness Recovery ---
    # Define Baseline as Jan-Feb 2026 mean
    base = df_sum.iloc[0:2].mean(numeric_only=True)
    analysis = df_sum[["month", "cases"]].copy()
    analysis["cases_rel_pct"] = (
        (df_sum["cases"] - base["cases"]) / base["cases"] * 100
    ).round(1)
    analysis["high_comp_diff_pp"] = (
        df_sum["high_complexity_pct"] - base["high_complexity_pct"]
    ).round(1)
    analysis["supp_z_diff_pp"] = (
        df_sum["supplier_z_pct"] - base["supplier_z_pct"]
    ).round(1)
    analysis["rework_diff_pp"] = (
        df_sum["rework_variant_pct"] - base["rework_variant_pct"]
    ).round(1)
    analysis["median_cycle_diff_h"] = (
        df_sum["median_cycle_h"] - base["median_cycle_h"]
    ).round(2)
    analysis["p90_cycle_diff_h"] = (
        df_sum["p90_cycle_h"] - base["p90_cycle_h"]
    ).round(1)
    analysis["p90_to_median_ratio"] = (
        df_sum["p90_cycle_h"] / df_sum["median_cycle_h"]
    ).round(2)

    # Fitness recovery metrics
    analysis["fitness_recovery_abs"] = (
        df_sum["mapped_model_fitness"] - df_sum["raw_model_fitness"]
    ).round(3)
    raw_loss = base["raw_model_fitness"] - df_sum["raw_model_fitness"]
    analysis["pct_loss_recovered_by_mapping"] = np.where(
        raw_loss > 0.01,
        ((df_sum["mapped_model_fitness"] - df_sum["raw_model_fitness"]) / raw_loss * 100).round(1),
        0.0,
    )

    print("=== BASELINE-RELATIVE CHANGES & FITNESS RECOVERY ===")
    print(analysis.to_string(index=False))

    # --- Step 3: Transparent Distances Between Monthly DFG Fragments ---
    edge_cols = [c for c in df_dfg.columns if c != "month"]
    R = df_dfg[edge_cols].to_numpy(dtype=float)
    r_base = R[0]  # 2026-01 baseline

    df_dist = pd.DataFrame({"month": df_dfg["month"]})
    df_dist["L1_to_Jan"] = np.sum(np.abs(R - r_base), axis=1)
    df_dist["L2_to_Jan"] = np.sqrt(np.sum((R - r_base) ** 2, axis=1)).round(2)
    df_dist["Linf_to_Jan"] = np.max(np.abs(R - r_base), axis=1)
    df_dist["Step_L1_prev"] = [0.0] + [
        np.sum(np.abs(R[i] - R[i - 1])) for i in range(1, len(R))
    ]

    print("\n=== NORMALIZED DFG FRAGMENT DISTANCES ===")
    print(df_dist.to_string(index=False))

    # --- Step 5: Flag Candidate Change Points Using Simple, Explainable Rules ---
    # Rule: Flag month t if month-over-month step change exceeds domain-calibrated thresholds:
    # - Logging drift: |delta(raw_fitness - mapped_fitness)| > 0.15 OR delta(missing_resource_pct) > 2.0 pp
    # - Behavioral drift: |delta(rework_variant_pct)| >= 6.0 pp OR DFG Step_L1_prev >= 25

    df_sum["d_fitness_gap"] = analysis["fitness_recovery_abs"].diff().fillna(0.0)

    df_sum["d_p90_h"] = df_sum["p90_cycle_h"].diff().fillna(0.0)

    caveats = """

2. Ecological Fallacy & Aggregation: Monthly means/medians pool Supplier Z with Other suppliers and High with

   Standard cases) cancel out in monthly medians.
3. Non-Normalized Operational Metrics: Only 'mapped_model_fitness' and the 7 DFG rates had label normalization

# PART B: REUSABLE PM4PY FUNCTIONS FOR FUTURE EVENT-LEVEL DATAFRAME ANALYSIS

from typing import Dict, Optional, Tuple
import pm4py


def prepare_event_log(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    case_id_col: str = "case:concept:name",
    activity_col: str = "concept:name",
    timestamp_col: str = "time:timestamp",
     timest_format: Optional[str] = None,
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    1. Formats a raw pandas DataFrame into a canonical PM4Py event log DataFrame
    without mutating the caller's original DataFrame.
    """
    df_copy = df.copy(deep=True)
    formatted_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df_copy,
        case_id=case_id_col,
        activity_key=activity_col,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_col,
        timest_format=timest_format,
    )
    return formatted_df


def slice_monthly_window(
    formatted_df: pd.DataFrame,
    dt_start: str,
    dt_end: str,
    mode: str = "cases_contained",
    case_id_col: str = "case:concept:name",
    timestamp_col: str = "time:timestamp",
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    2. Slices a time window using pm4py.filter_time_range(...).
    Valid modes in PM4Py: 'events', 'traces_contained', 'traces_intersecting'.
    """
    pm4py_mode = "traces_contained" if mode == "cases_contained" else mode
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(
        formatted_df.copy(deep=True),
        dt1=dt_start,
        dt2=dt_end,
        mode=pm4py_mode,
        case_id_key=case_id_col,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_col,
    )


def normalize_event_log_labels(
    formatted_df: pd.DataFrame,
    label_mapping: Optional[Dict[str, str]] = None,
    deduplicate_activity: str = "Open Case",
    case_id_col: str = "case:concept:name",
    activity_col: str = "concept:name",
    timestamp_col: str = "time:timestamp",
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    7 (Helper). Applies synonym mapping (e.g., 'Eligibility Screen' -> 'Validate Eligibility')
    and removes consecutive/duplicated technical 'Open Case' events per case on a deep copy.
    """
    if label_mapping is None:
        label_mapping = {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"}

    norm_df = formatted_df.copy(deep=True)
    norm_df[activity_col] = norm_df[activity_col].replace(label_mapping)

    # Sort deterministically by case and timestamp, then drop duplicate 'Open Case' per case
    norm_df = norm_df.sort_values([case_id_col, timestamp_col], kind="mergesort")
    is_target_act = norm_df[activity_col] == deduplicate_activity
    is_dup_within_case = norm_df.duplicated(subset=[case_id_col, activity_col], keep="first")
    norm_df = norm_df.loc[~(is_target_act & is_dup_within_case)].reset_index(drop=True)
    return norm_df


def compute_monthly_process_artifacts(
    window_df: pd.DataFrame,
    discover_constraints: bool = True,
    case_id_col: str = "case:concept:name",
    activity_col: str = "concept:name",
    timestamp_col: str = "time:timestamp",
) -> Dict[str, object]:
    """
    3, 4, 5, 6. Computes monthly DFG, variants, temporal profile, and optional
    log skeleton / DECLARE models using canonical PM4Py discovery functions.
    """
    # 3. Frequency and Performance DFGs: returns (dfg_dict, start_activities, end_activities)
    freq_dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(
        window_df,
        case_id_key=case_id_col,
        activity_key=activity_col,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_col,
    )
    perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
        window_df,
        case_id_key=case_id_col,
        activity_key=activity_col,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_col,
    )

    # 4. Trace Variants: returns dict mapping variant tuple/string -> count or list of traces
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(
        window_df,
        case_id_key=case_id_col,
        activity_key=activity_col,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_col,
    )

    # 5. Temporal Profile: returns dict mapping (act_a, act_b) -> (mean_sec, std_sec)
    temporal_profile = pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(
        window_df,
        case_id_key=case_id_col,
        activity_key=activity_col,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_col,
    )

    artifacts = {
        "frequency_dfg": freq_dfg,
        "performance_dfg": perf_dfg,
        "start_activities": start_acts,
        "end_activities": end_acts,
        "variants": variants,
        "temporal_profile": temporal_profile,
    }

    # 6. Optional Declarative / Constraint Discovery (Log Skeleton & DECLARE)
    if discover_constraints:
        if hasattr(pm4py, "discover_log_skeleton"):
            artifacts["log_skeleton"] = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(
                window_df,
                case_id_key=case_id_col,
                activity_key=activity_col,
                timestamp_key=timestamp_col,
            )
        if hasattr(pm4py, "discover_declare"):
            artifacts["declare_model"] = pm4py.discover_declare(
                window_df,
                case_id_key=case_id_col,
                activity_key=activity_col,
                timestamp_key=timestamp_col,
            )

    return artifacts


def compare_raw_vs_normalized_window(
    raw_window_df: pd.DataFrame,
    normative_net=None,
    initial_marking=None,
    final_marking=None,
    label_mapping: Optional[Dict[str, str]] = None,
) -> Tuple[Dict[str, object], Dict[str, object], Dict[str, float]]:
    """
    7. Compares raw and normalized label mappings on a time slice without overwriting
    the original log, optionally computing token-based replay or alignment fitness.
    """
    raw_copy = raw_window_df.copy(deep=True)
    norm_copy = normalize_event_log_labels(raw_copy, label_mapping=label_mapping)

    raw_artifacts = compute_monthly_process_artifacts(raw_copy, discover_constraints=False)
    norm_artifacts = compute_monthly_process_artifacts(norm_copy, discover_constraints=False)

    comparison_summary = {
        "raw_distinct_labels": float(raw_copy["concept:name"].nunique()),
        "norm_distinct_labels": float(norm_copy["concept:name"].nunique()),
        "raw_variant_count": float(len(raw_artifacts["variants"])),
        "norm_variant_count": float(len(norm_artifacts["variants"])),
        "events_removed_by_dedup": float(len(raw_copy) - len(norm_copy)),
    }

    if normative_net is not None and initial_marking is not None and final_marking is not None:
        raw_fit = pm4py.fitness_alignments(raw_copy, normative_net, initial_marking, final_marking)
        norm_fit = pm4py.fitness_alignments(norm_copy, normative_net, initial_marking, final_marking)
        comparison_summary["raw_log_fitness"] = float(raw_fit.get("log_fitness", np.nan))
        comparison_summary["norm_log_fitness"] = float(norm_fit.get("log_fitness", np.nan))
        comparison_summary["fitness_recovery"] = (
            comparison_summary["norm_log_fitness"] - comparison_summary["raw_log_fitness"]
        )

    return raw_artifacts, norm_artifacts, comparison_summary

## 9. Provisional attribution

We assign the observed drift across 2026 to five distinct mechanisms with graded confidence levels:

1. **March Conformance & Data-Quality Shift $\rightarrow$ System Migration (Confidence: High, 95%).** The sudden March drop in `raw_model_fitness` (0.93 to 0.55), expansion from 9 to 11 activity labels, appearance of `Open Case -> Register`, and jump in `missing_resource_pct` (1.0% to 4.8%) while `mapped_model_fitness` held at 0.90 and median cycle time stayed at 31.2h is overwhelmingly explained by the March 1 system migration.
2. **May–June Rework Surge $\rightarrow$ Supplier Z Disruption (Confidence: High, 85%).** The May doubling of `rework_variant_pct` (13% to 24%) and normalized `Assess -> Request Info` rate (12 to 24 per 100 cases) coincides exactly with the May 5 Supplier Z disruption and a jump in `supplier_z_pct` (18% to 43%), and reverses in September (`supplier_z_pct` down to 24%, rework down to 20%) upon Supplier Z's recovery.
3. **June–September Persistent Delay & P90 Expansion $\rightarrow$ Assessor Staffing Loss Mediated by Rework Load and Priority Queueing (Confidence: Moderate-High, 75–80%).** While Supplier Z initiated the May cycle-time rise (36.5h), the continued climb to 45.0h (median) and 110h (p90) in August—and the 41.5h floor in September after Supplier Z recovered—is best explained by the June 15 departure of four assessors, compounded by the July 1 priority rule inflating standard-case tail delays.
4. **October–November Second Peak $\rightarrow$ Seasonal Renewal Population Shift Interacting with Constrained Capacity (Confidence: High, 85%).** The October surge in volume (760–790 cases) and `high_complexity_pct` (26% to 41–45%) drove `Assess -> Senior Assessment` from 7 to 22 per 100 cases and pushed cycle times to 49h/121h. However, whether the `Senior Assessment` spike is purely complexity-driven or partly logging-habit change remains only **plausible (Confidence: Moderate, 60%)** given historical logging inconsistency.
5. **December Moderation $\rightarrow$ Seasonal Decay + Overtime/Quality Campaign (Confidence: Moderate, 55%).** Both lower renewal arrivals (690 cases, 34% complexity) and the December 5 interventions occurred simultaneously, preventing clean separation in monthly aggregates.

**Mediation and Interaction Obstacles:** Clean additive attribution fails in summer and autumn because Supplier Z rework loops *multiplied the effective arrival rate* of `Assess` tasks into an already depleted assessor pool (non-linear queuing interaction), while Supplier Z cases also carried higher baseline complexity.

**What Would Revise This Account:** I would revise these attributions if event-level stratification revealed that: (a) May–August rework rates rose equally inside non-Supplier Z cases (implicating internal assessor policy/training rather than Supplier Z); (b) post-July 1 pre-`Assess` waiting times grew equally for high-complexity and standard cases (refuting priority-rule starvation); or (c) `Assess -> Senior Assessment` rates in October surged inside standard-complexity cases (proving a logging or policy shift rather than seasonal case mix).
