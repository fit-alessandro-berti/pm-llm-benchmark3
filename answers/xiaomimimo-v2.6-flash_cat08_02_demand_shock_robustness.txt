The arithmetic is unforgiving: **the peak median wait at Medical validation (22 h) already exceeds the entire Urgent contractual window (18 h).** No upstream dispatch rule can recover an 18 h SLA while that queue stands.

### S2 — Assay degradation / outage

**Sequence:** Assay is instantaneously  > 100% (one machine must carry ~2× load). Upstream backs up in push order: Preparation benches  Pre-analytical  Registration/Courier, while Tech-val, Med-val and Specialist review **drain and idle — classic constraint starvation**. Urgent aging then triggers I1, starving Routine and Complex. Repeat cases (clustered after night shifts and M2) compete with Urgent for the surviving machine.

**Migration:** the moment the machine is repaired, a catch-up wave hits **Technical validation, already at 101%** — it becomes the binding stop within one shift. This is the textbook pattern: *fix the visible constraint, discover the invisible one.* I3 during an outage is actively harmful: dedicating M1 to Urgent+Complex puts multi-assay panels and urgent work on one machine with head-of-line blocking, while all Routine volume on the already-unreliable M2 raises repeats — and repeats consume assay capacity twice, feeding back into the same shortage.

### S3 — Validator / specialist absence

**Sequence A (specialist):** Specialist review (87%, 36 h, structural Friday gap)  Hold-for-specialist backs up (holdmed-val edge 1848 h)  Tech-val must retain held cases (already 101%)  assay backlog. Complex-panel is 2.4× more likely to enter Hold, so **a single specialist’s Friday absence is effectively a Complex-panel outage**.

**Sequence B (medical validator):** Med-val (93%, 22 h)  mandatory node for ~41–49% of cases  Tech-val cannot release  Tech-val fills past 101%  Assay blocked.

**Key principle illustrated:** *a station can be the bottleneck at 58% utilisation if it is a non-divertible routing node for a protected class.* Med-val’s normal utilisation looks cheap; its queue at peak is 22 h. Utilisation alone is a misleading steering metric for mandatory nodes.

### S4 — Courier compression / delay

**Sequence:** Courier (88%, 7.5 h, 10:00/17:00 spikes)  Registration (85%, overtime >9 h raises error rate)  Pre-analytical (96%)  **Accession correction (91%, 14 h)** and **recollection loop (60 h)**  back to Courier.

This is a *closed positive-feedback loop*, unique among the scenarios: delayed/compressed intake raises tube age  pre-analytical rejects rise (4.5%9.0% already)  recollection rises  clerks spend time explaining errors to physicians  clerk capacity falls while courier demand rises  tube age rises again. Meanwhile the 10:00 burst hits Preparation, forcing either bigger batches (I2) or urgent-bypass collapse (already 74%58%), and the whole maturing cohort lands on Assay at a fixed hour while Med-val sits idle earlier in the day.

**Migration:** **I7 alone does not fix a throughput delay** — smoothing slots redistributes tubes but if pick-up is later, specimen age increases and the reject/recollection loop gets *worse*, exporting load from Courier to Pre-analytical (96%). I7 needs tube-stability rules; I6 without I7 exports a 2-hour rejection burst onto the courier.

### S5 — Compound

Interactions are super-additive: S1 raises Med-val demand 2.58× while S3 removes Med-val supply; S1 raises Complex volume 2.85× while S3 removes the specialist those cases need; S2 raises repeat incidence while S1 already pushed Assay to 98%. Realistic projections: rework >15% (from 11.8%), Urgent p90 >40 h, Complex p90 >300 h, cancellation >8%.

### S6 — Catch-up burst

A step arrival of 600–900 orders instantaneously adds WIP equal to ~50% of normal-period WIP. Under push scheduling this is absorbed as queue everywhere simultaneously; under WIP caps it is absorbed as *held* orders at the front, which is recoverable and visible. This scenario separates a redesign that bounds WIP from one that merely resizes queues.

# 3. Robust redesign package

**Design principles (derived from the evidence, not from volume):**
1. **Reduce arrivals and work content before adding capacity** — three stations are already 96% at peak, so adding a bench simply moves the stop.
2. **Bound WIP instead of pushing it** — WIP grew 2.4× while throughput grew 1.43×; the queue is the problem, not the service time.
3. **Reserve capacity at non-divertible nodes** (Medical validation, Specialist review) rather than discovering the conflict at 22 h and 36 h.
4. **Make the scarcity trade-off explicit and agreed**, because not all classes can be protected simultaneously.

### R1 (structural): Three-lane routing with capacity reservation at order entry
Convert the single shared path with ad-hoc priority exceptions into three explicit lanes established at **Register order**, where class is already known:
- **Lane A — Urgent:** reserved time-slots on Assay, pre-booked Medical-validator slot, direct (non-batched) preparation path.
- **Lane B — Routine:** pull-based batching with a hard maximum-wait timer.
- **Lane C — Complex-panel / Repeat:** *scheduled assay blocks*, because Complex needs multiple assays and is 3.1× more likely to need Med-val and 2.4× more likely to hit Specialist hold. Scheduling converts a queue surprise into an appointment and removes the preemption tax.

*Why robust:* it addresses the mix shift (S1), the validator/specialist conflict (S3) and the burst (S6) simultaneously, because reservation is set from the **class mix, not the average volume**. *Assumptions:* class is known correctly at registration (it is — case type is an order attribute); reservation percentages can be re-scaled within one shift. *Failure mode:* reservations sized on normal-period mix (31% Urgent+Complex) will be wrong on day one of a wave — they must be re-derived from a rolling mix trigger, not fixed.

### R2 (structural): Pull / WIP caps at Assay and Technical validation, with bounded-priority aging dispatch
Upstream stations may not push into Assay when Assay WIP exceeds its cap; Urgent may exceed the cap by a bounded amount only. Aging rule: `priority = contractual-breach-risk + age`, with a **hard promotion threshold** so no class can be starved indefinitely.

*Why robust:* it is the only mechanism that keeps  away from 100% at the two stations the consistency check identifies as already saturated-but-masked (98%, 101%). It works identically in S1, S2, S4 and S6. *Assumptions:* WIP can be measured at the station in near-real time; supervisors accept holding orders (physicians may call). *Failure mode:* **the wait is not removed, it is relocated to Registration/Pre-analytical** — see §6, this is a vulnerability created by the redesign itself.

**Explicit scarcity policy (must be agreed, not discovered):** under genuine shortage the standing rule is Urgent > Complex-panel > Routine for Med-val and Assay slots, with the declared trade *“Routine absorbs the delay: Routine p90 may reach 144 h so that Urgent p90 stays 18 h.”* This is stated openly because I1’s conflict hint is correct — priority cannot be granted to everyone.

### R3 (load reduction): Front-gate defect-prevention bundle = I6 + I7 + I8-metadata-completeness + M2/night-shift QC
- **I6** early pre-analytical defect triage (<2 h). Moves rejection from a 42–60 h loop to 2 h — **~20× sooner** — for the 3.1%5.5% of cases that are cancelled anyway, releasing registration/pre-analytical/courier capacity. Recollection incidence 4.5%9.0% is the largest internal amplifier in the log.
- **I7** courier slot smoothing, paired with tube-stability rules (do not trade slot smoothness for specimen age).
- **I8 split into two parts:** retain *metadata-completeness validation at order entry* (attacks the source of the 2.8%4.3% accession-correction rate) and gate *full auto-registration* behind pre-analytical headroom.
- **M2/night-shift QC programme:** repeat incidence 6.7%14.8%, and repeats consume assay capacity twice (13 h normal / 34 h peak loop). Cutting peak repeat incidence to ~9% releases ~6% of machine time at a 98%-utilised station **without adding a machine**.

*Why robust:* load reduction helps whichever station happens to fail — S1 through S5. *Assumptions:* triage thresholds are calibrated; M2 failure is a controllable (maintenance/QC) cause, not a design property. *Failure mode:* an aggressive 2 h reject rule creates a **new** false-reject loop (physician re-order  double courier trip) and may push borderline specimens through, exporting defect into Assay and raising repeats.

### R4 (capability, activated conditionally): Validator and specialist coverage
- **I5 as a standing rostered capability** (evening/staggered medical-validation shift), deliberately aligned with the arrival profile: Tech-val at 101% delays Med-val-ready work into the evening, and 17:00 courier spikes mature in the afternoon. Downstream of Med-val is only **Release result (2 h/5 h)** — and that resource **does not appear anywhere in the capacity table**, so it must be instrumented before I5 is activated.
- **Specialist cross-cover:** eliminate the structural every-second-Friday absence with a paired-deputy arrangement and by moving Complex-panel reviews off Friday. Specialist demand already grows 5.6×/week — the steepest curve in the dataset.

*Why robust:* needed in S1, S2 (more repeats/holds), S3 and S4 (evening maturation). *Assumptions:* specialists accept evening/Friday cover; work actually arrives in the evening. *Failure mode:* if upstream remains day-shift aligned the evening shift sits idle — **I5 must be paired with staggered assay/tech-validation shifts or it is capacity in the wrong place.**

### R5 (front-end redeployment): Tier-1 accession corrections by trained registration clerks — replacing I4
I4 as stated is unsafe: it drains Technical validation, which is at **101%**. Instead, create a tier-1 correction role for registration clerks (85%, and further freed by the I8 metadata gate), with QC sampling on tier-1 corrections. Corrections unblock 430+310 cases/period at a 14 h queue with only 2 specialists.

*Why robust:* it relieves a 91%-utilised, 2-person pool without touching the 101% pool, and its upstream feeder (registration) is where the defect originates. *Assumptions:* clerk corrections can be quality-controlled. *Failure mode:* sample-identity errors are the worst failure mode in a laboratory, and **the DFG ends at Release result  END, so post-release errors are invisible** — quality migration here is undetectable with the current log.

# 6. Residual vulnerabilities

1. **Created by the redesign — waiting migrates to the physician-facing front end.** WIP caps and pull rules (R2) deliberately hold cases at Registration/Pre-analytical. The 2.8 h registration queue and 9.5 h pre-analytical queue become the *visible* complaint surface, physician telephone chasing rises, and clerk time is consumed there — partially offsetting the I8 automation gain. The delay is not removed, it is re-placed where it is most politically visible and least contractually covered.

2. **Created by the redesign — bounded-priority aging weakens the contractual Urgent guarantee.** Aging exists to prevent Routine/Complex starvation (correctly, per the I1 conflict hint), but it means that under sustained compound scarcity Urgent can be overtaken by aged cases. We consciously trade a ~45%-breach rate down but not to zero, in exchange for WIP stability. This trade must be written into the operating agreement; otherwise it will be discovered as a penalty invoice.

3. **Created by the redesign — defect triage can export defects downstream.** A hard 2 h reject threshold either false-rejects good samples (new recollection loop, double courier trip) or lets borderline specimens through to Assay, raising repeats at a 98%-utilised station. The guardrail (false-reject 2%) is only as good as the rejection-reason data, which must be newly instrumented.

4. **Created by the redesign — quality migration in accession correction.** Tier-1 clerk corrections shift the most dangerous error type (sample identity) to a less-experienced pool, and the DFG **ends at `Release result  END`** — there is no post-release loop (amendments, corrected reports, complaints) in the log. Reported “rework 7.5% / 11.8%” therefore counts **only pre-release rework**; any error created by R5 is structurally invisible.

5. **Structural, unaddressed:** only 2 accession specialists with no absence cover; one specialist unavailable every second Friday; M2’s inherently higher failure rate; Medical-validation and Specialist-review peak utilisation (93%/87%) is already absorbing ~60–160% unstated capacity stretch that cannot be repeated.

6. **Structural, unmeasured:** **Release result has no resource row, no queue metric and no utilisation figure**, yet it is the direct downstream of the intervention with the largest capacity change (I5) and the subject of I9. Any redesign that accelerates validation will discover this station only by overflowing it.

7. **Correlated external failure of I10:** the partner lab’s 12 h transport benefit is available precisely during winter waves — the same conditions that degrade courier performance. Outsourcing relieves Specialist review *by adding a transport dependency to a system whose transport leg is already at 88% utilisation*. If winter storms hit, I10 fails exactly when its trigger fires.

8. **Measurement fragility:** the peak DFG does not balance at Technical validation (7,240 in vs 8,080 out) or Assay (+1,750), partly from window truncation of multi-tube, order-flattened traces. WIP cannot be safely inferred from DFG counts; if trace completeness degrades further, every dashboard threshold below becomes untrustworthy.
