## 1. Conformance semantics

Evaluating an object-centric event log against an intentionally incomplete specification requires separating normative intent, logging fidelity, and graph-projection mechanics. Because a summary edge set compresses multi-object lifecycles, raw structural differences between observed and normative graphs cannot be treated uniformly as violations. We establish seven semantic classes:

- **Forbidden behavior**: An observed execution, lifecycle coupling, or object-event association matches a rule explicitly marked `forbidden`, and available evidence indicates the event genuinely occurred in the business domain rather than through corrupted extraction.
- **Missing mandatory behavior**: Within the exact precondition and population scope of a `mandatory` rule, a required causal step, object interaction, or event-to-object link never occurred in reality.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior**: An observed edge or object coupling is absent from the norm (or explicitly marked `unspecified`) and operates in a domain where the specification makes no prohibitive claim. Rarity does not make unregulated behavior noncompliant.
- **Typical-pattern absence**: An execution diverges from a `typical` expectation via recognized or plausible business exceptions (such as write-offs, credit terms, or intervening fulfilment steps) without violating any mandatory or forbidden rule.
- **Likely data-quality error**: The business process likely behaved permissibly—or never linked the entities at all—but identifier reuse, missing foreign keys, unstandardized event typing, or incomplete ETL logic produced a spurious forbidden edge or suppressed a mandatory edge.
- **Abstraction mismatch**: Underlying process execution satisfies the business intent of a norm, but the formal representation used to check it (for example, checking an *eventually-follows* requirement using a *directly-follows* projection, or ignoring multi-object cardinality) generates a false discrepancy.
- **Insufficient evidence**: Summary graph counts are mathematically compatible with both compliant execution (such as many-to-one object multiplicity or censoring at the observation window boundary) and genuine noncompliance or data loss, requiring instance-level inspection.

**Open-world versus closed-world assumptions** govern the default burden of proof. Closed-world semantics apply strictly to `forbidden` rules (any authentic occurrence is noncompliant) and to `mandatory` rules *only after* verifying that the scope precondition holds and the graph abstraction faithfully tests the underlying obligation. Everywhere else, the specification is open-world: unmodeled activities (`Write Off`), unmodeled lifecycle couplings (`object_codeath`), and `unspecified` policies (`Customer`, `RiskCase`, `Reopen Order`) are presumptively permissible unless instance-level evidence shows they bypass a mandatory control or trigger a forbidden state.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG
An Object-Centric Directly-Follows Graph projects the log onto per-object-type sequential traces and aggregates immediate event-pair transitions.
- **What it can establish**: Whether specific direct transitions ever occurred along an object's lifecycle (for instance, proving that 2 `Order` traces contain `Cancel Order` immediately followed by `Dispatch Package`, implicating N3 unless an intervening event masked even *more* violations) and the count of objects exhibiting a direct step (`98 of 100` for N1; `198 of 200` for N4).
- **What it cannot establish**: Long-range temporal precedence (*eventually-follows*), multi-step causality, whether missing successors reflect window censoring (`14 Invoices` with no successor under N5), or whether two events are separated by a benign unmodeled event. Critically, **interpreting an "eventually expected" business rule as a directly-follows rule is dangerous in both directions**:
  1. *False positives on mandatory rules*: N1 requires every newly created standard `Order` to *eventually* enter validation. If an order executes `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review -> Validate Order`, the business obligation is 100% satisfied, yet the directly-follows edge `Create Order -> Validate Order` disappears! Similarly, under N2, `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice -> Close Order` (72 Orders) breaks `Validate Order -> Close Order` without any noncompliance, and under N4 an intermediate `Stage Package` or `Inspect Package` event would suppress `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package` even when every package was packed prior to dispatch.
  2. *False negatives on forbidden rules*: N3 forbids dispatching *any* package *after* cancellation (`Cancel Order ... -> Dispatch Package`). Checking only direct succession (`Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`, 2 Orders) would miss a cancelled order where `Reopen Inquiry` or `Update Address` occurred between `Cancel Order` and `Dispatch Package`.

### OTG
An Object Type Graph summarizes structural relationships (`object_interaction`, `object_cobirth`, `object_codeath`) aggregated across entire object lifecycles.
- **What it can establish**: Existence of prohibited lifecycle couplings—such as `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)` with weight `1` violating T6—and macro-level presence of unregulated structural patterns like `(Package, object_codeath, Delivery)` (`7`), `(Order, object_interaction, Customer)` (`100`, T4), and `(Order, object_interaction, RiskCase)` (`3`, T5).
- **What it cannot establish**: *Which* event type created an interaction edge, *when* in the lifecycle the interaction occurred, or per-event completeness.
- **Counts, scopes, and cross-abstraction tension**: T2 states `(Package, object_interaction, Delivery)` is mandatory *specifically for a `Dispatch Package` interaction*. In the observed OTG, `(Package, object_interaction, Delivery)` has weight `200`, looking 100% compliant for 200 shipped packages! Yet ET–OT reveals `Dispatch Package -> Delivery` has weight `198`. How can a relation be conforming in OTG and suspicious in ET–OT? Because OTG aggregates interactions across *all* shared events (such as the delivery-confirmation events that also generated the `7` `object_codeath` edges), masking the missing `Delivery` link at the exact moment of `Dispatch Package`—or counting multi-package interactions that offset the 2 unlinked dispatches. Conversely, OTG shows `(Order, object_interaction, RiskCase)` (`3`), which is suspicious when compared to ET–OT, where `RiskCase` appears *only* in `Manual Compliance Review` (`3`) and `Manual Compliance Review` has no `Order` edge.

### ET–OT
An Event-Type to Object-Type matrix counts associations between event classes and object classes.
- **What it can establish**: Forbidden cross-domain bindings at the event level (`Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice`, weight `1`, triggering E10) and discrepancies in relative attachment frequencies (`Dispatch Package -> Package` `200` vs. `Delivery` `198` under E5/E6; `Record Payment -> Payment` `93` vs. `Invoice` `92` under E7/E8).
- **What it cannot establish**: Exact per-event multiplicity when relationship cardinality exceeds 1-to-1, temporal order, or whether a link reflects genuine business action versus ETL key collision.
- **Counts, scopes, and cross-abstraction tension**: Consider `Add Item`: ET–OT shows `Add Item -> Item` = `1450` (E3) and `Add Item -> Order` = `1448` (E2), while OTG shows `(Order, object_interaction, Item)` = `1450` (T1). Under a naive 1-item-per-event assumption, 2 `Add Item` events violate mandatory rule E2. However, ET–OT counts *event–object pairs*, not distinct events. If 1,448 `Add Item` events occurred, each linked to exactly 1 `Order` (`1448`), and two of those events bundled 2 `Item` objects (`1446*1 + 2*2 = 1450`), then **every** `Add Item` event linked to both `Order` and `Item` (100% compliant with E2, E3, and T1!). Thus, an apparent deficiency in ET–OT ratio counts may be completely conforming at the event-instance level. Conversely, `Pack Package -> Package` (`200`, E4) and `Dispatch Package -> Package` (`200`, E5) appear perfectly symmetric in ET–OT, hiding the 2-package temporal gap exposed in OC-DFG (`198 of 200` under N4).

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **Temporal operator conflation in Order validation and cancellation (N1, N3, N4)**
   - *Operational risk*: Expressing eventually-follows obligations as directly-follows edges triggers false alarms whenever compliance reviews or staging steps intervene (N1, N4), while checking N3 only via direct succession misses post-cancellation shipments cushioned by an intermediate status event.
   - *Proposed clarification*: Replace directly-follows syntax with object-lifecycle temporal constraints: `Create Order` must *eventually be followed by* `Validate Order` unless `Cancel Order` intervenes (`mandatory`), and `Cancel Order` must *never eventually be followed by* `Dispatch Package` on any linked `Package` (`forbidden`).
2. **Undefined "standard Order" and pre-validation cancellation scope (N1)**
   - *Operational risk*: If a customer cancels an order 30 seconds after creation, penalizing it for never entering `Validate Order` conflates normal customer attrition with broken order-intake controls; likewise, "standard Order" lacks an attribute predicate.
   - *Proposed clarification*: Define the exact `Order` attribute identifying standard orders and exclude orders cancelled prior to fulfilment eligibility from N1 (`mandatory` within refined scope).
3. **Unapplied-cash classification mechanism (E8, T3)**
   - *Operational risk*: T3 recognizes unapplied cash as normal, whereas E8 requires an explicit event type/label not consistently populated by finance Systems/clerks, turning routine treasury timing differences into conformance violations.
   - *Proposed clarification*: Require every `Record Payment` lacking an `Invoice` link to bind either to an `UnappliedCashAccount`/`Customer` credit object or carry `payment_category = 'UNAPPLIED'` (`mandatory`).
4. **Cardinality bounds on multi-object events (E2, E3, T1)**
   - *Operational risk*: Ratio checks on ET–OT totals (`1448` vs. `1450`) cannot distinguish valid multi-item cart additions from orphaned line items lacking parent `Order` links.
   - *Proposed clarification*: Specify instance-level cardinality per `Add Item` event: `count(Order) == 1` and `count(Item) >= 1` (`mandatory`).
5. **Cross-object propagation of `Manual Compliance Review` (E9, T5, N1)**
   - *Operational risk*: `Manual Compliance Review` links to `RiskCase` and `Customer` in ET–OT yet surfaces on `Order` traces in OC-DFG/OTG, creating ambiguity over whether compliance holds legally block a single `Order` or an entire `Customer` account.
   - *Proposed clarification*: Define whether `Manual Compliance Review` must directly reference affected `Order` objects when triggered during order intake (`mandatory` when order-triggered) while keeping `Customer` attachment (`typical`) and external `RiskCase` adjudication (`explicitly unspecified`).
6. **Object codeath and delivery-confirmation lifecycle closure (OTG, T2)**
   - *Operational risk*: Analysts may misread `(Package, object_codeath, Delivery)` (`7`) as premature object destruction, or allow late confirmation events to mask missing `Delivery` objects at `Dispatch Package` (T2/E6).
   - *Proposed clarification*: Document synchronous lifecycle termination on final confirmation as (`explicitly unspecified` or `typical`), while anchoring T2 strictly to event-instance co-occurrence at `Dispatch Package` (`mandatory`).
7. **Absence of aging horizons for open invoices (N5)**
   - *Operational risk*: Without a time bound, permanently stalled or uncollected invoices hide indefinitely inside the "open credit terms" bucket (`14 Invoices` with no successor).
   - *Proposed clarification*: Keep `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment` (`typical`), but define a separate SLA monitoring rule requiring either `Record Payment`, `Issue Credit Note`, `Write Off`, or `Open Dunning Case` within `due_date + grace_period` (`mandatory` once past grace window).
