# 1. Data audit and estimands

**Opening-hours convention and units.** I treat 07:00–19:00 as 12 opening hours = 720 minutes per full-time-equivalent server. Therefore a rough daily service supply is `staff_fte × 720` staff-minutes, reduced on an outage day by the no-service interval.

**Assumptions used, explicitly labelled.**

- **A1 — Rate denominator:** arrivals/day and completions/day are converted to per-hour opening-time rates by dividing by 12. This is a convention, not proof that work occurred uniformly.
- **A2 — Outage timing:** the 2026-04-15 hourly vector has zero completions in the 11:00–14:00 bins, so I treat the 180-minute outage as approximately 11:00–14:00 for diagnostic load calculations.
- **A3 — Intervention timing:** the staffing intervention began sometime during 2026-04-29; exact time is unavailable. Therefore 2026-04-29 is treated as a transition day, not as a clean before/after observation.
- **A4 — Load proxy:** `50  arrivals × median_service_min / staff_minutes` is only a pressure proxy. True utilization would require mean service time and effective parallel-server behavior. `90` is a tail-stress indicator, not utilization.

**Queueing quantities.**

- `arrivals`: number of case arrivals during opening hours. Arrival intensity: `arrivals / 12` cases/hour.
- `completed`: number of cases completed during opening hours. Completion intensity: `completed / 12` cases/hour. These need not be the same cases that arrived that day.
- `mean_wip`: mean number of cases in the system from 15-minute open-hours snapshots. This is the closest available analogue to Little’s Law `L`, but only if boundary conditions are handled carefully.
- `median_service_min`, `p90_service_min`: active handling-time summaries, excluding waiting. They are **not** mean flow time and must not be substituted into `L = W`.
- `net = arrivals - completed`: a crude backlog-change proxy only if no other inventory adjustments occur and boundary WIP is known.
- `complex_pct`, `peak_08_10_pct`: mix and intraday-shape covariates. They are descriptive indicators, not proof of causal mechanisms.

**At least five threats to naive inference.**

1. **Unknown boundary backlog.** WIP at 07:00 and 19:00 is missing. Arrivals minus completions cannot be interpreted as exact backlog change.
2. **Overnight carry-over.** Cases can wait overnight while no service occurs. A 12-hour WIP average ignores closed-hours waiting time.
3. **Completions are not same-day flows.** A case completed on April 16 may have arrived on April 15 or earlier.
4. **Service-time aggregation error.** Medians and p90s are not means; using them as mean service time biases utilization and flow-time reasoning.
5. **Non-stationarity.** At least four distinct disturbances are visible: the April 15 outage, April 16–17 recovery, the April 20 complex-mix/peak-share jump, and the April 29 staffing intervention.
6. **Weekday/weekend composition.** Pooling 20 weekdays with 8 weekend days produces an average day that represents neither.
7. **Serial dependence.** Daily rows are ordered; weekly cycles and regime persistence make observations non-independent.
8. **Burstiness.** Representative hourly vectors show strong intraday clustering, especially April 21, so daily averages hide queueing peaks.
9. **Possible coding change.** `complex_pct` could reflect a real case-mix shift, a coding-practice change, or both. The data cannot distinguish them.
10. **Peak-window definition caution.** The daily `peak_08_10_pct` values do not exactly match a literal reading of the representative hourly bins. For example, April 7 has 17% in literal 08:00–10:00 bins but 32% in 09:00–11:00 bins, close to the reported 31%. The timestamp convention should be reconciled before using this covariate mechanistically.

| Apr 20–May 3 pooled | 1,335 | 1,247 | +88 | Hides the post-Apr-29 reversal |

A pooled model over the whole high-complex period would estimate persistent overload, even though the final five days show completion surplus and falling WIP.

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

Using the representative hourly vectors, I compute population-variance Fano factor `var/mean`. A Poisson-like count process with equal mean and variance would have Fano  1. Values above 1 indicate overdispersion relative to that benchmark.

| Representative day | Arrivals mean/bin | Arrival Fano | Arrival CV | Completion Fano | Completion CV | Reading |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Apr 7 ordinary weekday | 8.42 | 2.96 | 0.59 | 1.56 | 0.44 | Ordinary weekday is already more bursty than Poisson-like |
| Apr 15 outage | 8.75 | 3.79 | 0.66 | 2.06 | 0.65 | Arrivals continue; completions structurally interrupted |
| Apr 21 bursty post-mix weekday | 9.58 | 9.28 | 0.98 | 0.90 | 0.33 | Extreme arrival burstiness; completions are much smoother, so the queue absorbs shocks |
| Apr 25 weekend | 4.25 | 1.18 | 0.53 | 0.72 | 0.45 | Lower-volume day, less overdispersed arrivals |

The April 21 vector is especially important: arrivals include hourly counts of 34 and 24, while completions remain comparatively smooth. A daily model using only 115 arrivals/day misses the queueing effect of those spikes.

**Serial dependence indicators.**

- Lag-1 autocorrelation of representative hourly arrivals:
  - Apr 7: about **+0.68**, largely reflecting a smooth intraday ramp and decline.
  - Apr 21: about **0.13**, consistent with isolated spikes rather than persistent hour-to-hour growth.
  - Apr 25: about **+0.80**, consistent with a smooth weekend ramp.
- Lag-1 autocorrelation across the daily sequence:
  - Daily arrivals: about **+0.41**.
  - Daily completions: about **+0.30**.

Thus daily rows should not be treated as independent. Weekly seasonality, regime persistence, and backlog dynamics induce serial structure.

**Daily overdispersion.** Across all 28 days, arrivals have Fano  9.7 and completions  10.3. This does **not** mean the underlying arrival process is Poisson-overdispersed in a single stable regime. It mostly reflects mixing weekdays, weekends, outages, recovery, the Apr 20 mix shift, and the Apr 29 intervention. Within narrower weekday regimes, daily counts are much more regular. Structural non-stationarity can masquerade as stochastic overdispersion.

**Change-point evidence.**

- **Apr 20:** complex share jumps from the prior 21–27% range to 41%, then remains 39–47%; p90 service jumps from mostly 44–50 baseline levels to 78–88 on weekdays; peak share also rises sharply. This is the strongest persistent change point.
- **Apr 15:** outage starts; one-day exogenous shock.
- **Apr 16–17:** recovery state.
- **Apr 29:** staffing increases and completion surplus appears; timing is known only at day granularity.

**Why four representative days cannot establish a full stochastic model.**

They cover only one ordinary weekday, one outage day, one bursty post-mix weekday, and one weekend pre-intervention. There is no representative hourly vector for the intervention period, no repeated sample within each regime, no raw timestamps, no activity sequences, and no pairing of arrivals to completions. They are useful diagnostics, not enough to fit a full queueing process model.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Little’s Law requires consistent boundaries: `L = W`, where `L` is average number in system, `` is arrival throughput into that same system, and `W` is average time in system for those arrivals. In a stable window, arrival and departure rates coincide. Here they often do not.

All implied times below use opening-hour rates. They are in hours. They are not service times and must not be compared directly with median active handling minutes.

| Window / regime | Boundary stability | Throughput proxy | WIP comparability | Carry-over risk | Stationarity assessment | Implied flow-time calculation if defensible | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline weekdays, Apr 6–14 | Arrivals 701 vs completions 697; plausible but not measured 07:00/19:00 equality | Arrivals 8.35/h; completions 8.30/h; either is similar | WIP 18–23, mean 20; open-hours snapshots only | Moderate overnight risk, but net flow near zero | Plausibly quasi-stationary within weekday pattern | Approx. `20 / 8.35  2.4 h`; using completions `20 / 8.30  2.4 h` | Approximate only |
| Early low-complex weekends, Apr 11–12 & Apr 18–19 | Arrivals 167 vs completions 166 | Arrivals 3.48/h; completions 3.46/h | WIP 12–16, mean 13.5 | Moderate | Plausibly quasi-stationary for weekends | Approx. `13.5 / 3.48  3.9 h`; completions give about 3.9 h | Approximate only |
| Outage day, Apr 15 | Unstable: outage plus large WIP increase | Arrivals 8.75/h; completions 4.83/h over open day | WIP 47 but boundary change unknown | High | Not stationary; service capacity interrupted | No defensible `W`. Diagnostics conflict: `47/8.75  5.4 h` vs `47/4.83  9.7 h` | Not defensible |
| Recovery, Apr 16–17 | Unstable: WIP falls 52  35 | Arrivals 8.79/h; completions 10.04/h | Mean WIP 43.5, but declining | Very high; completing prior-day or older work | Not stationary; burn-down state | No defensible steady-state `W`. Diagnostics: about 5.0 h via arrivals, 4.3 h via completions | Not defensible |
| High-complex pre-intervention weekdays, Apr 20–28 | Unstable: arrivals exceed completions by 94 | Arrivals 9.50/h; completions 8.38/h | WIP 37–50, mean 44.3 | High | Not stationary; backlog-building | No defensible `W`. Diagnostics: about 4.7 h via arrivals, 5.3 h via completions | Not defensible |
| Intervention weekdays, Apr 29–May 1 | Unstable: WIP declines 43  36  32 | Arrivals 9.58/h; completions 10.08/h | Mean WIP 37, falling | High; active backlog burn-down | Not stationary, though direction is clear | No steady-state `W`. Diagnostics: about 3.9 h via arrivals, 3.7 h via completions | Approximate only for burn-down description, not for steady flow time |
| Full 28-day pool | Unstable: net +110 arrivals over completions | Arrivals 7.50/h; completions 7.17/h | Mean WIP 29.9 across incomparable regimes | Very high | Strongly non-stationary | Pooled diagnostics about 4.0 h via arrivals, 4.2 h via completions; not meaningful as flow time | Not defensible |

**Key caveat.** If cases wait overnight, the relevant `W` should include calendar elapsed time while the case remains in system, including closed hours. The supplied WIP snapshots are only during opening hours, so even the “approximate only” values are open-hours diagnostics, not full end-to-end flow times.

# 7. Operational implications

- **Staffing:** Do not set capacity from the pooled average of 90 arrivals/day. Weekdays run around 100–115 arrivals/day depending on regime; weekends around 42–49. The high-complex weekdays Apr 20–28 show `50` near 0.85–1.01 and p90 stress far higher, so they likely needed more than baseline capacity or better peak smoothing.
- **Outage recovery:** April 16–17 completion surges should be budgeted as recovery capacity, not treated as a permanent productivity benchmark. After an outage, measure success by 19:00 backlog and WIP decline, not completions alone.
- **Peak smoothing:** The April 21 hourly vector suggests bursts at specific hours while completions remain smooth. Appointment rules, staggered complex cases, or protected complex-case capacity could reduce queueing without increasing total staff.
- **Backlog recovery:** The intervention period shows the desired direction: completion surplus and WIP decline. However, because the intervention began during April 29 and backlog state was already elevated, the causal staffing effect is not identifiable from these aggregates.
- **Weekend protection:** A pooled WIP comparison might suggest moving staff from weekends to weekdays because weekend WIP is lower. That is risky: weekend staffing is only 2.5–3.0 FTE, and some weekends have `50` near or above 1 with high p90 stress. Cutting weekend capacity could create carry-over that then contaminates Monday.
- **Policy that looks good pooled but is risky segmented:** Setting a single daily completion target near the pooled weekday average could make intervention-period completion rates look “too high” and justify staff reduction. But those completions were likely burning down backlog accumulated Apr 20–28. Cutting staff then, while complex mix remains around 40–45%, could recreate the backlog.
- **Data collection:** Capture case IDs, arrival timestamps, service start/end timestamps, waiting time, activity sequence, resource, 07:00 and 19:00 backlog, exact outage start/end, exact intervention time, and a flag for coding-practice changes. Also reconcile the `peak_08_10_pct` timestamp definition with hourly bins.

def rng(s):
    return f"{s.min():g}-{s.max():g}"

def summarize_one(x, label):
    return {

        "days": len(x),
        "A/day": round(x["arrivals"].mean(), 1),
        "A/hr": round(x["arrivals"].mean() / OPEN_HOURS, 2),

        "S50 med(range)": f"{x['median_service_min'].median():g} ({rng(x['median_service_min'])})",
        "S90 med(range)": f"{x['p90_service_min'].median():g} ({rng(x['p90_service_min'])})",

        "staff med(range)": f"{x['staff_fte'].median():g} ({rng(x['staff_fte'])})",
        "complex med(range)": f"{x['complex_pct'].median():g} ({rng(x['complex_pct'])})",
        "rho50 med(range)": f"{x['rho50_full_open'].median():.2f} ({rng(x['rho50_full_open'])})",
    }

def dispersion_stats(x):
    x = np.asarray(x, dtype=float)
    mu = x.mean()
    var = x.var(ddof=0)
    return {
        "mean": round(mu, 3),
        "pop_var": round(var, 3),
        "Fano_var_over_mean": round(var / mu, 3) if mu else np.nan,
        "CV": round(np.sqrt(var) / mu, 3) if mu else np.nan,
    }

def lag1_acf(x):
    x = np.asarray(x, dtype=float)
    d = x - x.mean()
    denom = np.sqrt(np.sum(d[1:] ** 2) * np.sum(d[:-1] ** 2))
    return round(float(np.sum(d[1:] * d[:-1]) / denom), 3) if denom else np.nan
