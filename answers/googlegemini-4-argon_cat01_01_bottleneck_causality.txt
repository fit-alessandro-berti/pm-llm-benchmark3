## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional Verdict:** `mainly confounded` (with structural non-identifiability across shift and load regimes)  
**Confidence Level:** 75/100

While Orion’s crude mean cycle time (21.96h) is 6.72h longer than Nova’s (15.24h), this association reverses within every observed complexity stratum—a classic Simpson’s paradox driven by case-mix routing. Within Low, Medium, and High complexity strata, Orion’s mean cycle times are lower by 2.14h, 2.21h, and 3.43h, respectively. 

**Strongest Evidence For:** Routing policy explicitly assigns difficult cases to Orion (42.9% High complexity vs. Nova’s 12.5%) and concentrates Orion on Night shifts (92.9% vs. 25.0%) under High arrival loads (92.9% vs. 50.0%). Adjusting for observed complexity alone reverses the direction of the effect, showing Orion has both shorter pre-review waits and slightly faster review service times within strata.

**Strongest Evidence Against:** Stratification by coarse complexity does not achieve causal identification. There is near-zero positivity (overlap) across shift and arrival load (only one Daytime Orion case exists: C02). Unobserved case risk within complexity bands, survivorship bias from analyzing only completed cases, or thoroughness-induced rework could still mask localized structural delays.

**Why the 50% Reassignment May Backfire:** Blindly shifting 50% of Orion’s workload to Nova confuses a *routing policy proxy* with a *resource performance treatment*. Because Orion handles the toughest night-shift claims and appears faster within every complexity tier (likely due to having more certified reviewers), diverting its cases to Nova risks overwhelming Nova’s less-certified staff with complex claims. This would inflate both service times and queue waits, potentially increasing overall cycle times and degrading 30-day appeal rates. Conversely, the reassignment would only succeed if Orion’s night queue is currently at a non-linear tipping point where relieving arrival load dramatically collapses wait times—a dynamic not supported by the current within-stratum wait numbers.

| **Phase 3: Stratified Micro-Randomized Routing Pilot (Weeks 7–10)** | Conduct a bounded A/B routing experiment on **Medium-complexity** (and borderline Low/Medium) cases across both Day and Night shifts, randomly assigning 20% of eligible cases between Orion and Nova (50/50 split within the pilot pool). | Direct and mediated effects of `resource_group` (reviewer skill bundle vs. group-specific rework/service speed) while fixing positivity gaps. | Individual case (`case_id`) immediately post-`Classify`, stratified by `shift` and `compliance_required`. | Exclude High-complexity cases requiring specialized Orion certification; cap total volume shifted to Nova at 10% of overall workload to prevent queue tipping. | Intention-to-treat (ITT) differences in `review_service_h`, `wait_before_review_h`, `rework_count`, and 30-day appeal rates across randomized arms. | Provides gold-standard experimental identification of the Orion vs. Nova treatment effect across both shifts, eliminating unobserved case-risk confounding. | Immediately halt pilot if Nova’s queue wait on Medium cases exceeds 14.0h, rework exceeds 35%, or 30-day preliminary quality complaints spike by >5%. |

### Final Recommendation
Management must **reject the proposal to blindly reassign 50% of Orion’s cases to Nova**. The proposal is based on a textbook ecological fallacy (Simpson’s paradox): Orion’s higher overall average (21.96h vs. 15.24h) is an artifact of routing rules that send 75% of High-complexity cases and 81% of Night-shift cases to Orion. Within every individual complexity tier, Orion is actually **2.1 to 3.4 hours faster** than Nova—driven primarily by shorter pre-review waits and reinforced by faster review service from its certified staff. Dumping half of Orion’s complex, night-heavy workload onto Nova’s less-certified daytime reviewers risks severe queue congestion, longer service times, and increased appeals. Instead, operations should execute the three-phase learning plan: first, audit open cases and split stage timestamps to rule out survivorship bias; second, relieve High-load night queues and compliance bottlenecks directly; and third, run a safe, stratified 20% randomized routing pilot on Medium-complexity claims to establish true causal performance before altering core routing rules.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Right-Censoring and Truncation Bias from Unobserved Open/Abandoned Cases:** Because the dataset contains only completed cases (`case_completed = 1`), we cannot rule out that Orion’s slower or more problematic High/Medium cases are still sitting open in the queue.  
   *Single most valuable data field/change:* Add an **entry-cohort status indicator and censoring timestamp** (`case_status` $\in \{\text{Completed, Open, Abandoned}\}$ for all cases passing `Classify` within the observation window).
2. **Conflation of Queueing and Compliance Execution inside `wait_before_review_h`:** We cannot separate how much of pre-review time is spent waiting for or executing the `Compliance Check` versus waiting in the resource group’s own review queue after compliance is finished.  
   *Single most valuable data field/change:* Record **activity-level lifecycle timestamps** (`compliance_start_ts`, `compliance_end_ts`, and `review_queue_enter_ts`).
3. **Unobserved Case Risk Within Coarse Complexity Strata:** The 3-tier `complexity` label (`Low`, `Medium`, `High`) may hide continuous claim severity that the routing engine or human dispatchers use to assign harder within-tier cases to one group.  
   *Single most valuable data field/change:* Log the **continuous routing risk score or exact claim-feature vector** (`routing_propensity_score` / claim dollar value) used by the routing engine at `Classify`.
4. **Structural Collinearity Between Group Assignment, Shift, and Arrival Load:** With only a single Daytime Orion case (C02) and 100% overlap between Night shift and High arrival load, observational adjustment cannot separate Orion’s reviewer capability from night-shift congestion dynamics.  
   *Single most valuable data field/change:* Implement a **randomized routing flag** (`pilot_random_assignment`) on a 20% subset of eligible Medium-complexity cases across both Day and Night shifts.
5. **Cross-Case Interference and Individual Reviewer Heterogeneity:** Aggregating `review_service_h` across initial and repeated reviews and hiding reviewer IDs obscures whether performance differences stem from a few senior specialists or dynamic queue starvation when rework re-enters the pool.  
   *Single most valuable data field/change:* Record the **anonymized `reviewer_id` and certification level** alongside **instantaneous active queue depth (`wip_count_at_routing`)** for each `Review` event.
