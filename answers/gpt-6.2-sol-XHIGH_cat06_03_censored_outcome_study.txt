## 1. Target definition

The primary target is **90-day default conditional on lender activation within 60 days of scoring**. Score each original lender application once, 24 hours after its first recorded Application Started event, at the close of the timestamp batch. The scoring population consists of applications still in pre-decision review at that landmark. Applications already accepted, rejected, withdrawn, transferred, or otherwise past review are outside this scoring rule. The 24-hour landmark and 60-day activation window are study design choices.

Let L be the landmark, X(L) the information available at that point, A the first lender activation in the original application episode, and D the default time for that account. The estimand is

**Pr(A < D ≤ A + 90 days | X(L) = x, L < A ≤ L + 60 days, eligible at L, observed policy regime).**

The conditioning event is a future outcome used to define the estimand and identify training examples. At scoring time an eligible case can receive a forecast interpreted as risk **if it joins the group activated in that window under the operating policy**. Report the fraction scored and the fraction subsequently in that group. The horizon starts at activation, and feed delay affects when the answer is known. The bounded activation window gives a common interpretation and a finite follow-up requirement.

An all-application alternative would estimate transitions from review to lender activation, rejection, withdrawal, or transfer, and then from activation to default. A quantity from that model is the probability of following the lender-activation-then-default path within the specified windows. It incorporates both the chance of exposure and the risk after exposure. Applications resolved before the landmark enter the all-application accounting in their observed state. Estimating default risk under a new offer policy would instead require counterfactual assumptions about selection, take-up, and credit terms.

## 2. Cohort construction

Start with the full application registry or an event extract reconciled to it. Use the original lender episode as the unit and include starts from 1 January 2024 through 31 December 2025. Require a genuine observed start and a usable point-in-time prefix for the modeling cohort. Carry unscorable cases in the cohort flow with a reason, including fast exits, incomplete or ambiguous histories, and insufficient observation to reach the landmark. Reconcile IDs and activity names, and quarantine unresolved timeline or label conflicts from their affected analyses. A linked downstream transfer ID supports follow-up audits and remains associated with the original application. Identify lender activation within the original episode. Partner-originated accounts that activate with the lender remain eligible.

For any as-of date, use the earlier of the as-of date and the event observation boundary for process observations. Use actual receipt or commit time alongside event time when available. Find the first lender activation and the time-stamped absorbing dispositions in that view. Later activation stays in the cohort flow and falls outside the primary conditioning window. Cases still open at the event boundary have censored application follow-up. If the 60-day window was fully observed with no qualifying activation, membership in the primary activated cohort is resolved even if the application remains open. Use a separate release-dated view for outcomes. Reports can arrive after the application event window if the independent feed covers the relevant outcome horizon.

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

- **Ordinary right censoring.** For an open application, dispositions beyond its observation boundary are unknown. In a post-activation survival analysis, right censor at a verified last outcome observation before day 90 if the account is known default-free through that date. A silent feed or the end of the application log gives no evidence of event-free follow-up.
- **Delayed labels.** An activation can complete day 90 while its report is still pending. Require the horizon to have ended and the outcome to have been released by the as-of cutoff. Apply the maturity rule to both classes. The 7–35 day lag describes common delays and provides no guarantee of completeness.
- **Activation selection.** Approval, take-up, and activation determine who becomes exposed. The primary model conditions on that group. Adjusting for outcome reporting within it provides no outcomes for rejected or withdrawn applicants.
- **Transfer.** Treat transfer out as a competing disposition for the original lender episode. For a cross-platform endpoint, transfer can instead initiate missing follow-up. Audit linked and unlinked transfers separately. The roughly 60% with links may differ systematically from the rest.
- **Permanently missing feeds.** A mature activated account can have no report, particularly for partner-originated cases. Use delivery records, source and activation vintage, and later releases where available to characterize missingness. A single extract can leave long delays and permanent loss indistinguishable.

Use a fixed-horizon analysis with a common opportunity for observation. Include starts whose landmark plus 60 days is observable in the process log and whose landmark plus 60, 90, and another 35 days falls before the outcome cutoff. This allows the same minimum follow-up even for the last permitted activation. Keep every qualifying activation in the denominator, including those with unresolved outcomes. Fit a binary risk model on reported outcomes with inverse probabilities of label observation. Estimate those probabilities in the full qualifying activated cohort using observed reporting predictors, such as partner/source, activation date, feed vintage, and relevant history. Post-landmark variables can enter the observation model if measured across that cohort. The score itself uses only information available at the landmark. Use temporal fitting or cross-fitting for the observation model, inspect overlap and effective sample size, and disclose any trimming or support restrictions.

The analysis assumes that, conditional on the measured reporting predictors, reported and unreported outcomes have the same risk, and that relevant strata have positive reporting probability. Check recorded chronology, report lags among observed outcomes, missing-label frequencies, and empirical overlap by source and period. Repeated snapshots can partially test latency and show which gaps eventually resolve. Risk dependence among never-reported accounts and outcomes under hypothetical activation are untestable from this export. A post-activation survival analysis would also require defensible censoring assumptions and verified event and last-contact times.

Compare with the mature reported-only estimate and with results using a longer reporting buffer. To assess informative missingness, vary default odds among unreported accounts relative to comparable reported accounts, especially in partner strata. Show how these assumptions affect calibration and the intended decision. Without a missing-at-random assumption, if N qualifying activations include d recorded defaults and u unresolved labels, cohort prevalence lies between d/N and (d + u)/N. Give bounds by source or score band where denominators permit. In strata without usable reports, the data support bounds and coverage reporting.

## 4. Prefix extraction

For the primary elapsed-time prefix, take canonical intake and review events from the genuine start through the 24-hour landmark that were available when its timestamp batch closed. Evaluate eligibility using dispositions known by that same point. A short case still in review contributes every eligible event seen so far, even if only the start has been observed. A case that exited review before or at the landmark has no primary score.

For the fixed-count comparison, score immediately after the third eligible event if it arrives by day one and before review exit. Apply the same target timing rules relative to that trigger. Applications that never reach it in time have no score under this rule. Report coverage and conditional performance for each trigger, and state which cases and horizons are shared in any paired comparison. Use a real event sequence if available. With timestamp batches and no sequence key, omit the exact-count score if the third event falls inside a larger simultaneous batch. Use order-insensitive elapsed-time features within tied batches.

Map legacy and migrated names to common business activities with a versioned, source-aware map. Audit unmapped activity and migration duplicate rates by period. Remove confirmed duplicates using an immutable event identifier or a validated replay signature. Preserve genuine repeated document requests and returns to review. With the supplied schema alone, flag ambiguous repeats and assess their influence in a sensitivity run. Compute loop counts and outstanding requests from the observed prefix.

## 5. Process-aware features

| Feature family | Examples and availability |
|---|---|
| Static case attributes | Channel, region, and requested amount as recorded at intake, or verified versions at scoring. Establish that case-level fields preserve historical values. |
| Prefix control flow and rework | Activity counts, KYC or review observed, document requests, returns to review, and completed request-to-review loops. Use ordered transitions and last activity only with established event order. |
| Prefix timing | Time since the last observed event, elapsed time to first review if reached, and time since an outstanding document request. Indicate stages still underway at scoring. |
| Resource and queue | Distinct observed handlers and handoffs when order is known. Current review queue, recent assignment load, and resource experience require historical reference data available at the landmark. |
| Calendar and cohort | Day and hour of intake, policy and migration regime known at scoring, and intake source where historically populated. Fit reference distributions and encoders on permitted prior data. |
| Versioned scores | Last score released by the landmark, change from the first observable update, and absence of any observable score. Require reproducible update and release history. |

Compute queue and resource aggregates from records already visible to the operational system, including work still open. Such features need a shared historical clock that captures when assignments, closures, and staff records became available.

## 6. Leakage audit

| Feature or data element | Leakage or bias risk | Valid availability rule | Keep/repair/drop | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `final_limit` | Reveals final decision and credit terms. | A separately recorded provisional quote would require its own pre-landmark release. | Drop this field. | The warehouse limit is finalized after the decision. |
| `decision_reason` | Encodes acceptance or rejection and may be repeated on earlier rows. | Use the verified decision event time solely for eligibility and outcome accounting. | Drop from features. | The reason arrives at or after the end of review. |
| `collection_status` | Can reveal the target directly. | Use in an outcome workspace only, with appropriate timestamps. | Drop from features. | This status arises after activation. |
| Future events | Signing, activation, collections, default, and future loop completion reveal dispositions or outcomes. | Enforce event and receipt cutoffs before computing aggregates or process representations. | Drop from feature inputs. | Later events may inform outcome accounting under its separate cutoff. |
| `documents_total` | A nightly snapshot can count later same-day uploads. | Rebuild from unique uploads received by scoring, or use a snapshot actually published before scoring with known coverage. | Repair or drop. | A same-day join allows future documents into a prefix. |
| Updated `risk_score` | A later or backfilled score may appear on an early row. | Require an immutable or versioned score and its release time. Use the last unambiguous available update. | Repair, then keep if verified. | Event time alone does not establish score availability. |
| Resource experience | Extraction-time tenure and volumes include later work and staff changes. | Join a historical staff version or aggregate from prior visible events. | Repair or drop. | Historical experience must be reproducible at every landmark. |
| `default_90d` and `label_available_at` | Repeated labels and release timestamps can leak outcomes or selection. | Reconcile binary labels by case in the outcome workspace using horizon end and actual release time. | Keep for outcome and ascertainment only. | Reports can be attached to old rows after scoring. |
| `data_extract_time`, case ID, and activity aliases | Extraction vintage and IDs can encode period or outcomes. Aliases can create false process shifts. | Use extraction time for provenance, IDs for joins, and approved aliases before feature derivation. | Drop raw fields as predictors and repair activity names. | Predictors should reflect the observed application history and calendar information available at scoring. |

## 7. Validation design

Use separate historical as-of views. One feasible schedule uses the following case-start blocks, with the common maturity allowance enforced in each block.

| Role | Case starts, UTC | Outcome information allowed |
|---|---|---|
| Train | 1 January–31 March 2024 | Released before 7 October 2024. |
| Validate and calibrate | 7 October–20 December 2024 | Released before 1 July 2025. Freeze preprocessing, model choices, calibration, and any decision threshold before test scoring. |
| Post-policy test | 1 July–12 August 2025 | Released by the end of 15 February 2026. Replay scoring at each case’s landmark. |

Use older rolling folds for model selection where support permits. Keep all prefixes for a case in its block, use confirmed entity links to guard against duplicate episodes, and fit encoders and reference distributions on fold-appropriate history. Calendar gaps give outcomes time to mature before each subsequent block. Verify that historical event and label versions and their availability are reconstructable. With occurrence time alone, historical replay relies on immediate event availability and immutable event versions. Check those assumptions against operational logs or archived snapshots if available.

The July test evaluates transport across the credit-policy change. Report eligibility and activation rates, selected case mix, score distributions, label coverage, and calibration by regime. Use policy at scoring as known then and the actual policy applied at decision for retrospective analyses of boundary-crossing cases. Audit the October migration by source and date using alias coverage, duplicate rates, prefix lengths, and feature drift. Replay post-migration scoring to assess coverage and input drift. Post-migration prefixes need a later extract for primary performance evaluation under the same 60+90+35 day design. The July test can already include post-migration reporting, so report feed changes separately from prefix changes.

For eligible activations report observation-weighted Brier score, log loss, calibration curves, calibration intercept and slope, AUROC, and average precision. Include outcome prevalence, label coverage, effective sample size, and uncertainty intervals. For example, resample calendar blocks and account for repeat applicants when reliable links exist. Stratify by channel, region, partner/source, and policy period where sample size and overlap support inference. Show mature reported-only results and missingness sensitivity alongside the weighted metrics. Report landmark coverage and the observed fraction subsequently activated within the window in the underlying application cohort, with eligibility and follow-up denominators. For an all-application transition model, evaluate event-specific cumulative-incidence calibration and time-dependent Brier scores with a suitable censoring adjustment.

Random case splits mix policies, software versions, and calendar-dependent label availability. They can train on future conditions and place linked episodes on both sides, giving a performance estimate that poorly represents deployment.

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

The result describes predictive associations with default among applicants selected into timely lender activation under the observed policies, subject to assumptions about outcome ascertainment. Differences in estimated risk may reflect eligibility rules, take-up, terms, or feed coverage as well as repayment behavior. The forecast applies to eligible review-stage applicants with adequate support. A counterfactual estimate for otherwise unexposed applicants requires a specified offer and servicing policy, a definition of take-up under that policy, and assumptions such as consistency, exchangeability, and positivity, or an appropriate intervention study.

Deployment can change who and what the study observes in several ways:

- Reviewers may deny, approve, or escalate different applications, changing who activates and whose default is observable.
- Adjustments to limits, terms, document requirements, or activation delays may affect take-up and default among exposed accounts.
- Score-driven queue ordering and routing may change waiting times, rework, partner transfers, and feed coverage.
- Extra outreach and servicing for high-risk accounts may prevent defaults or change when they are recorded.

Use the score initially in shadow evaluation or review planning under its conditional interpretation. Decisions that change access, terms, or servicing require evaluation under the proposed workflow with exposures, interventions, reports, and outcomes tracked.

## 9. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation skeleton

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

C, A, T = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
Y, RELEASE = "default_90d", "label_available_at"
day = pd.Timedelta(days=1)
utc = lambda s: pd.Timestamp(s, tz="UTC")
BEGIN, EVENT_STOP, EXTRACT_STOP = map(utc, ["2024-01-01", "2026-01-01", "2026-02-16"])
MIGRATION = utc("2025-10-15")
COMPETING = ["Rejected", "Withdrawn", "Transferred"]
ACTIVATION = "Account Activated"
EXIT = COMPETING + ["Accepted", "Contract Signed", ACTIVATION,
                    "Collections Contact", "Default Recorded"]
# Confirm canonical document names and populate (source, migrated name) aliases.
INTAKE = {"Application Started", "Identity/KYC Check", "Credit Review",
          "Documents Requested", "Document Uploaded"}
ALIASES = {}
EVENT_COLUMNS = [C, A, T]

# Input is the full original-episode extract, with IDs already reconciled.
raw = pd.read_csv("applications_events.csv", dtype={C: "string"})
for field in [T, RELEASE, "data_extract_time"]:
    raw[field] = pd.to_datetime(raw[field], utc=True, errors="raise")
raw[Y] = pd.to_numeric(raw[Y].astype("string").str.lower()
                       .replace({"true": "1", "false": "0"}), errors="raise")
assert raw[[C, A, T]].notna().all().all()
events = raw.loc[raw[T].ge(BEGIN) & raw[T].lt(EVENT_STOP),
                 EVENT_COLUMNS + ["source_system", "org:resource"]].copy()
migrated = events[T].ge(MIGRATION)
events.loc[migrated, A] = [
    ALIASES.get((source, name), name)
    for source, name in zip(events.loc[migrated, "source_system"], events.loc[migrated, A])
]
# Remove confirmed replay duplicates upstream. Identical rows without IDs are ambiguous.
print("Suspected repeated event rows", events.duplicated(keep=False).sum())
print("Observed activity inventory", events.groupby(A).size().to_dict())

def snapshot(cutoff):
    assert cutoff <= EXTRACT_STOP
    # Occurrence-time replay assumes immediate availability and immutable versions.
    # With ingestion timestamps, also filter by receipt time at each scoring instant.
    stop = min(cutoff, EVENT_STOP)
    seen = events.loc[events[T].lt(stop), EVENT_COLUMNS]
    starts = (seen.loc[seen[A].eq("Application Started")].groupby(C)[T]
              .min().rename("start"))
    seen = seen.join(starts, on=C)
    seen = seen.loc[seen[T].ge(seen["start"])].sort_values([C, T], kind="stable")
    first = (seen.loc[seen[A].isin(EXIT)].groupby([C, A])[T].min().unstack()
             .reindex(index=starts.index, columns=EXIT))
    for activity in EXIT:
        first[activity] = pd.to_datetime(first[activity], utc=True)
    m = starts.to_frame()
    m["review_exit"] = first.min(axis=1)
    m["competing_at"] = first[COMPETING].min(axis=1)
    a = first[ACTIVATION]
    m["activation"] = a.where(m["competing_at"].isna() | a.lt(m["competing_at"]))
    m["timeline_conflict"] = ((a.notna() & m["activation"].isna()) |
                              first[COMPETING].eq(m["competing_at"], axis=0).sum(axis=1).gt(1))
    m["terminal_at"] = m[["activation", "competing_at"]].min(axis=1)
    m["application_censored_at"] = pd.Series(stop, index=m.index).where(
        m["terminal_at"].isna())
    m["landmark"] = m["start"] + day
    m["eligible"] = m["landmark"].lt(stop) & (
        m["review_exit"].isna() | m["landmark"].lt(m["review_exit"]))
    m["activation_deadline"] = m["landmark"] + 60 * day
    m["activation_window_observed"] = m["activation_deadline"].lt(stop)
    m["calendar_ready"] = (m["activation_window_observed"] &
                           (m["activation_deadline"] + (90 + 35) * day).lt(cutoff))
    m["horizon_end"] = m["activation"] + 90 * day
    m["horizon_complete"] = m["horizon_end"].lt(cutoff)

    # Use release timestamps on the replicated annotations, even after the event window.
    valid = raw[RELEASE].lt(cutoff) & raw[RELEASE].le(raw["data_extract_time"])
    valid &= raw[Y].isin([0, 1])
    reports = raw.loc[valid].groupby(C)[Y].agg(["min", "max"]).reindex(m.index)
    m["label_conflict"] = reports["min"].notna() & reports["min"].ne(reports["max"])
    m["y"] = reports["min"].where(m["horizon_complete"] & ~m["label_conflict"] &
                                  ~m["timeline_conflict"])
    m["observed_label"] = m["y"].notna()
    m["analysis_activation"] = (m["eligible"] & m["calendar_ready"] &
                                m["activation"].gt(m["landmark"]) &
                                m["activation"].le(m["activation_deadline"]) &
                                ~m["timeline_conflict"])
    m["status"] = "open_or_other_unresolved"
    for disposition in COMPETING:
        m.loc[first[disposition].eq(m["competing_at"]), "status"] = disposition
    m.loc[m["activation"].notna(), "status"] = "activated_horizon_open"
    m.loc[m["horizon_complete"], "status"] = "activated_report_pending_or_missing"
    m.loc[m["observed_label"] & m["y"].eq(0), "status"] = "observed_non_default"
    m.loc[m["observed_label"] & m["y"].eq(1), "status"] = "observed_default"
    m.loc[m["timeline_conflict"] | m["label_conflict"], "status"] = "audit_conflict"
    return seen, m

def pm_frame(rows):
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(rows[EVENT_COLUMNS].copy(), case_id=C,
                                  activity_key=A, timestamp_key=T)

def prefixes(seen, m, k=3):
    joined = seen.join(m[["landmark", "review_exit"]], on=C)
    safe = joined.loc[joined[T].le(joined["landmark"]) & joined[A].isin(INTAKE) &
                      (joined["review_exit"].isna() | joined[T].lt(joined["review_exit"]))]
    eligible_rows = safe.loc[safe[C].isin(m.index[m["eligible"]])]
    elapsed = eligible_rows[EVENT_COLUMNS].copy()
    assert set(elapsed.columns) == set(EVENT_COLUMNS)
    assert elapsed[T].le(eligible_rows["landmark"]).all()
    exits = eligible_rows["review_exit"]
    assert (exits.isna() | elapsed[T].lt(exits)).all()

    kth = safe.loc[safe.groupby(C).cumcount().eq(k - 1)].set_index(C)[T].rename("kth_at")
    with_kth = safe.join(kth, on=C)
    at_k = with_kth.loc[with_kth[T].le(with_kth["kth_at"])].groupby(C).size()
    fixed_input = safe.loc[safe[C].isin(at_k.index[at_k.eq(k)]), EVENT_COLUMNS]
    fixed = fixed_input.copy()
    if not fixed_input.empty:
        fixed = pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(pm_frame(fixed_input), length=k)[EVENT_COLUMNS]
        assert fixed.groupby(C).size().eq(k).all()
        assert set(fixed[C]) == set(fixed_input[C])
        assert fixed[T].le(fixed[C].map(kth)).all()
    return elapsed, fixed

_, full_cohort = snapshot(EXTRACT_STOP)
print("Snapshot cohort", len(full_cohort), full_cohort["status"].value_counts().to_dict())
print("Visible IDs with no observed start", events[C].nunique() - len(full_cohort))
folds = {
    "train": ("2024-01-01", "2024-04-01", "2024-10-07"),
    "validation": ("2024-10-07", "2024-12-21", "2025-07-01"),
    "test": ("2025-07-01", "2025-08-13", "2026-02-16"),
}
study = {}
for name, dates in folds.items():
    lo, hi, cutoff = map(utc, dates)
    seen, cohort = snapshot(cutoff)
    cohort = cohort.loc[cohort["start"].ge(lo) & cohort["start"].lt(hi)]
    elapsed, fixed = prefixes(seen.loc[seen[C].isin(cohort.index)], cohort)
    counts = elapsed.assign(doc_request=elapsed[A].eq("Documents Requested"),
                            review=elapsed[A].eq("Credit Review")).groupby(C).agg(
        event_count=(A, "size"), doc_requests=("doc_request", "sum"),
        reviews=("review", "sum"), last_event=(T, "max"))
    landmarks = cohort.loc[cohort["eligible"], "landmark"]
    X = counts.reindex(landmarks.index)
    X["idle_hours"] = (landmarks - X.pop("last_event")).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    assert X["event_count"].notna().all()
    assert set(X.columns) == {"event_count", "doc_requests", "reviews", "idle_hours"}
    outcome = cohort.loc[cohort["analysis_activation"],
                         ["activation", "horizon_end", "observed_label", "y"]]
    study[name] = (X, outcome)
    print(name, {"starts": len(cohort), "elapsed_scores": len(X),
                 "fixed_scores": fixed[C].nunique(),
                 "eligible_and_calendar_ready": int((cohort["eligible"] & cohort["calendar_ready"]).sum()),
                 "eligible_activations": len(outcome), "labels": int(outcome["observed_label"].sum())})
    if name == "train":
        # Descriptive prefix DFG only. Omit cases with ambiguous within-batch order.
        tied = elapsed.loc[elapsed.duplicated([C, T], keep=False), C]
        ordered = elapsed.loc[~elapsed[C].isin(tied)]
        if not ordered.empty:
            dfg, starts, ends = pm4py.discover_dfg(pm_frame(ordered))

# Join verified feed/source metadata to the full activated denominator for
# observation weights. Train on observed-outcome IDs, selecting inputs only from X.
# Survival needs verified default and last-event-free follow-up times. An application
# competing-risk model uses disposition times and process censoring from the cohort.
```
