## 1. Materiality Framework

To translate raw alignment costs into a business severity assessment, we evaluate each deviation against multiple dimensions of materiality. Severity is scored from 1 (negligible) to 5 (critical).

- **Control Objective (Severity Driver):** Did the deviation compromise a preventive control (e.g., approval before payment, fraud screening) or merely create a logging inconsistency?
- **Amount / Financial Exposure:** Cases involving large payouts (e.g., > EUR 50,000) or funds already disbursed irreversibly carry higher severity.
- **Reversibility:** If the deviation occurred before payment (e.g., a withdrawal or denial), the financial risk is limited. If it occurred after payment, the funds are likely irrecoverable.
- **Timing / Sequencing:** Payments executed before the corresponding approval (P3) are critical control failures regardless of technical cost.
- **Customer / Regulatory Harm:** Missing notifications (P4) or payments to recently changed bank accounts expose the organization to fraud liability and customer harm.
- **Recurrence:** Systemic issues (e.g., label mismatches, retry duplicates) are more severe collectively than individually.
- **Evidence Reliability:** A missing event in the core system does not automatically mean the activity was skipped; external evidence (e.g., adjuster reports, CRM messages) may prove the activity occurred. The quality and immutability of this evidence affect confidence.
- **Model Scope:** Deviations arising because the model does not capture a valid business path (e.g., denials, withdrawals) are model mismatches, not operational control failures.

**Confidence vs. Severity:** Severity measures the potential business impact if the interpreted deviation is true. Confidence measures the degree of certainty that the deviation actually represents a real-world business issue rather than a logging artifact, label mapping issue, or model scope limitation. High severity can coexist with low confidence (e.g., a missing notification on a EUR 250,000 claim where a CRM record exists but is mutable).

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

The supplied alignments demonstrate that raw alignment cost—computed as a uniform sum of log and model moves—is a poor proxy for business risk for several reasons:

1. **Cost-function design (CL-602 vs. CL-604):** Every visible move costs 1. CL-602 has cost 1 because only `Fraud Screen` is missing, yet it involves a EUR 480,000 high-risk claim with a bank change. CL-604 has cost 3 due to sequencing deviations, but both represent severe control failures. Uniform cost cannot distinguish a missing EUR 480,000 fraud screen from a missing EUR 800 notification.

2. **Scope (CL-607, CL-612):** Denied and withdrawn cases produce raw costs of 4 because the model forces a payable-claims path. These are not operational failures—the payment was never intended. High cost here reflects model scope mismatch, not risk.

3. **Hidden moves (CL-605):** The `T(tau)` silent move is correctly costed at 0. It represents a Petri net conversion artifact, not a business event. Treating it as a deviation would be misleading; ignoring it is correct.

4. **Data conditions (CL-609, CL-610):** Both have raw cost 0, meaning perfect control-flow conformance. However, CL-609 violates the EUR 5,000 Auto Approve limit, and CL-610 violates the bank-change rule. Alignment cost is blind to data-aware policies, making zero-cost alignments potentially the most dangerous.

5. **Activity granularity and duplicated telemetry (CL-601, CL-603):** CL-601 has cost 3 purely from UI telemetry (Open Workbench, Autosave, Close Workbench) that P5 explicitly excludes from business activities. CL-603 has cost 1 from a duplicate notification retry. These costs reflect technical noise, not business harm.

6. **Missing-but-external evidence (CL-606, CL-608):** CL-606 has cost 1 for `M(Assess Loss)`, but an external adjuster document exists. CL-608 has cost 1 for `M(Notify Claimant)`, but a CRM message exists (albeit mutable). Alignment cost treats these as missing activities, but the activities likely occurred; the issue is evidence reliability, not operational omission.

7. **Irreversibility (CL-604):** A payment made before approval is irreversible. The alignment cost of 3 does not reflect that funds have left the organization. Severity must account for whether the deviation affected a reversible pre-payment step or an irreversible disbursement.
