## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation
**Distinguish categories (per case):**  
- **Policy breach:** Missing mandatory precommitment finance approval (RULE_A2) or compliance clearance (RULE_A3) for amounts/suppliers that trigger those rules; using an unapproved supplier when not overridden by C2.  
- **Logging defect:** Failure to record incidentcommand joint authorization within four hours for critical purchases where the written PDF was queued during the ERP outage (e.g., P503, P507, P508). The defect is not fatal if the true verbal authorization occurred within four hours (phone log supports this), but the written record appears late in ERP, causing uncertainty.  
- **Late evidence of a timely act:** Some ERP timestamps may be up to 12minutes later; however, contemporaneous phone logs or supplier acknowledgments are available and reliable for determining whether an approval (e.g., finance or compliance) occurred before Commit. No case relies solely on an inaccurate ERP timestamp without corroborating phone evidence.  
- **Inability to prove compliance:** Not applicable; sufficient contemporaneous evidence exists for each purchase (phone logs, acknowledgments, signed PDFs, clear finance/compliance timestamps). The only minor uncertainty is the exact timing of the written PDF appearance due to outage, but phone logs resolve the precommitment window for IC authorization.

**Immediate caselevel remediation (per purchase type):**  
1. **Issue a breach note** for each noncompliant purchase, documenting the missing financeapproval (or complianceclearance) precommitment step and the observed timing.  
2. **Require a written criticallot declaration** at request time if a purchase is intended to restore a sterilization line within 24h. The declaration must include the phrase “critical” or “critical contingency” and be signed/approved by at least one IncidentCommand member. This satisfies the explicitlabel assumption and avoids ambiguity.  
3. **Implement a twostep emergency checklist** (to be used only when an emergency is recognized under B4 or ECD2):  
   - Step1 (within 30min of decision): obtain written or recorded joint IncidentCommand authorization (verbal OK if recorded within 4h). Use a simple form or Teams message that timestamps the decision and names the two IC members; the form must be saved outside ERP (e.g., shared drive) to avoid outage delays.  
   - Step2 (before Commit): confirm that finance approval (if amount50k) and compliance clearance (if >100k or new supplier) are already obtained **or** place a hold on Commit until those approvals are logged. If finance approval cannot be obtained instantly, the checklist must log the expected time and trigger a followup reminder.  
   - Step3: after Commit, record the Emergency Form within 24h and attach the ICauthorization record.  
4. **Audit the ERP outage handling:** train staff to capture phonecall timestamps (e.g., callrecording metadata) and supplier acknowledgment times immediately; these should be entered into a temporary “Outage Evidence” sheet that is later merged into the normal ERP record. This ensures evidence weight is correctly applied.  
5. **Local SOP amendment (draft):** add a bullet under B4: “If a purchase is designated ‘critical’ by the Incident Commander, the timing rule ‘CreatePObeforeCommit’ may be relaxed; however, finance approval (A2) and compliance clearance (A3) must still be obtained before Commit. Use the Emergency Checklist (see above).” Also add: “Do not use the phrase ‘critical’ loosely; a written declaration with the word ‘critical’ or synonymous phrase (e.g., ‘critical contingency’) is required for the timing override.”  
6. **Monitoring actions:** weekly internal audit of any emergencyrelated purchases during simulated incidents; verify that the checklist was used, that criticallot declarations exist, and that finance/compliance approvals are logged before Commit. Flag any purchase where the written ICauthorization PDF appears after Commit in the ERP (due to outage) and require a supplemental note confirming the true verbal authorization time from the phone log.

**Remediation by role:**  
- Procurement: follow checklist at request; record criticallot declaration if applicable; do not commit before finance/compliance approvals unless the checklist explicitly allows timing relaxation for critical lots.  
- Plant/Incident Command: ensure written ICauthorization record is created promptly (within 30min of decision) and stored outside ERP; label purchases as critical only when truly needed within 24h for sterilization line restoration.  
- Finance: provide finance approval within the checklist deadline; if delay >2h, raise a hold flag visible to procurement.  
- Compliance: perform compliance clearance before Commit for >100k or new suppliers; document clearance timestamp.  
- IT/ERP team: improve outageevidence capture (autolog call start/end times, supplier email timestamps) and provide a temporary offline form that syncs after outage.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification
> **Emergency Purchasing Procedure (effective immediately, superseding parts of GPIP4 and DEAACPROC17 for the Aachen site).**  
> 
> 1. **CriticalLot Definition** – A purchase is designated a *Critical SterilizationLine Lot* only when the Incident Commander **writes** (in the request or purchase note) a short statement containing the word “critical” (or an exact synonym such as “critical contingency”) and explicitly states that the part is needed **within the next 24hours** to restore or continue production of a sterilization line. The statement must be signed or approved by at least one IncidentCommand member and retained with the purchase record. *No purchase automatically qualifies as critical simply because it occurs during an incident.*  
> 2. **Timing Override** – For Critical SterilizationLine Lots that occur **within the ECD2 time window** (09:00CEST14Jul2026through18:00CEST18Jul2026), the local rule “CreatePObeforeCommit” (B1) may be relaxed; *Commit_to_Supplier may occur before PO creation.*  
> 3. **SupplierRegister Override** – For Critical SterilizationLine Lots within the ECD2 window, suppliers outside the approvedsupplier register may be used. The purchase record must include the IncidentCommand criticallot statement and a note that the supplierregister exception has been applied.  
> 4. **IncidentCommand Joint Authorization** – Before Commit_to_Supplier for a Critical SterilizationLine Lot, **two distinct IncidentCommand members** must jointly authorize the purchase. Authorization may be verbal at the moment of decision, but a written record (e.g., a onesentence note in a shared Teams channel or a signed PDF) must be created **within four hours** of the commitment decision. The written record must reference the criticallot statement. If the ERP is unavailable during the outage, the written record should be saved outside the ERP (e.g., in a shared drive) and later entered; its timestamp is not used to determine compliance with the fourhour rule.  
> 5. **Mandatory Baseline Controls (never overridden by the timing or supplier overrides)** –  
>    a. **Segregation of duties (A1):** The person who requests or commits to a supplier must not also be the final business approver (Plant Manager or Finance Controller) for that purchase.  
>    b. **Finance approval (A2):** For any purchase EUR50000, an independent budgetowner approval **and** a Finance Controller approval must be obtained **before** Commit_to_Supplier. This applies even for Critical SterilizationLine Lots. If the CFO email guidance (D1) is used, it may inform daytoday judgment but cannot replace the mandatory financeapproval step.  
>    c. **Compliance clearance (A3):** For purchases >EUR100000 or from a new supplier, Compliance_Clearance must be obtained **before** Commit_to_Supplier. This applies even for Critical SterilizationLine Lots. Legal clearance is required for purchases >EUR150000 (as per CFO guidance) and must also be obtained before Commit.  
>    d. **Plant approval (B2):** Purchases EUR25000 require Plant_Approve (or delegated Shift Lead when the Plant Manager is absent in an emergency). Plant approval must be obtained before Commit.  
> 6. **CRException (A7) safeguard** – A written, scoped, timelimited exception from the Chief Risk Officer may override the timing relaxation (Rule2) and the supplierregister override (Rule3) **but shall never override Rulesb,c, ord** (finance, compliance, segregation). The exception document must specify the exact scope (e.g., “Critical SterilizationLine Lots of type X”) and the start/end times. The exception must be signed by the CRO and circulated to Procurement, Finance, and Incident Command before use.  
> 7. **Evidence handling during outages** – When the ERP is unavailable, staff should record the decision time using the phonecall log, supplier acknowledgment email timestamp, or a manual sheet. These contemporaneous records are considered **equally reliable** (or more reliable) than ERP timestamps that may be delayed up to 12minutes. After the outage, enter the recorded time into the ERP and annotate with “outageadjusted”. Audit logs should capture the source of the timestamp.  
> 8. **Emergency Form** – Record_Emergency_Form must be completed within 24hours of Commit_to_Supplier, regardless of timing override. The form should include a reference to the criticallot statement (if applicable) and a brief note on which baseline controls were satisfied before Commit.  
> 9. **Safeguard against emergency normalization** – The phrase “critical” may **only** be used with the explicit 24hour restoration purpose described in Rule1. No postincident “retroactive” labeling is permitted. All criticallot decisions must be documented at the request stage, not after Commit.  

*This clause replaces the ambiguous references in GPIP4 (A4A7), DEAACPROC17 (B1B5), and the CFO email guidance while preserving all mandatory integrity controls. It provides clear, timebound conditions for the two temporary relaxations (timing and supplier register) and explicitly prevents those relaxations from removing finance, compliance, or segregation requirements.*
