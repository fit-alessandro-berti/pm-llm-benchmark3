# 4. Network and conformance analyses

The analysis should preserve original identifiers and compare `org:resource` with `entered_by` and `approved_by`. Bot identities should be separated from human identities before applying person-level segregation-of-duties tests. Function signatures can vary slightly by PM4Py release, so the implementation should be validated against the installed version.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

df = pd.read_csv("procure_to_pay.csv")
df["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"], utc=True, errors="coerce")

# Retain relevant fields and format as a PM4Py event dataframe.
elog = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
)

# Useful actor field: do not overwrite source evidence; create a review field.
elog["actor_for_review"] = elog["org:resource"].astype(str)

# Flag known data-quality populations for separate reporting.
elog["is_manual_upload"] = elog["source_system"].eq("manual_upload")
elog["is_legacy_erp"] = elog["source_system"].eq("legacy_erp")
elog["is_quarter_close_upload"] = elog["source_system"].eq("quarter_close_upload")
```

**Four-eyes filtering**

PM4Py’s four-eyes filter is commonly used to identify cases where the same resource executes both specified activities. Run it separately for the two normative rules, then reconcile results to raw authentication and actor fields.

human_clean = elog[

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

**Evidence that would increase concern**

- Vendor bank-account changes for V-77x lacking vendor-signed confirmation, callback verification, approved maintenance ticket, or migration authorization.
- Bank accounts newly associated with V-77x that are also associated with unrelated vendors, employees, former employees, or unusual beneficiary addresses.
- Payment approvals occurring before the bank-change control completed, or payments released to changed accounts without required independent validation.
- Authentication, session, IP-address, device, or privileged-access logs showing the same individual performed bank-change entry and payment approval despite differing event-log names.
- Workflow evidence showing that `entered_by`, `approved_by`, and `org:resource` all support a confirmed same-person approval rather than a shared-account or mapping explanation.
- Emergency cases with no work order, clinical urgency record, incident ticket, delegated authority, quote/availability evidence, or retrospective justification.
- Repeated emergency flags for transactions that do not meet defined emergency criteria, particularly high-value transactions processed during ordinary working hours.
- Original documents proving that the same person both authorized the override and approved the retrospective justification, with no independent review.
- Evidence that `manual_upload` bypassed standard validations or approval checkpoints without compensating controls.
- Unusual payment characteristics: amounts inconsistent with historical vendor patterns, fragmented purchases near approval thresholds, duplicate invoices, payment reversals followed by reissue to a changed account, or unusual timing relative to bank changes.

**Evidence that would reduce concern**

- Approved emergency-policy documentation, on-call schedules, delegation records, and contemporaneous emergency tickets supporting Carla/Gita and Dan/Hugo fast paths.
- Independent retrospective justification approvals completed within three working days, evidenced in original workflow records even if migration timestamps are distorted.
- A reconciled vendor-platform migration register linking each V-77x bank-detail change to an approved ticket, vendor confirmation, verified bank account, regional rollout date, and payment record.
- Payment-control records showing independent AP review, dual authorization, sanctions/bank-account validation, and successful post-payment reconciliation.
- Raw source-system audit trails demonstrating that the shared `Approver_Gita` account reflects assistant data entry while an authorized individual separately approved the action.
- Corrected `legacy_erp` mapping tables showing that apparent bot/human self-approval cases were generated by identity-mapping defects.
- Role descriptions and access-control matrices demonstrating that vendor checks and bank-detail maintenance are intentionally combined, while payment approval remains independently controlled.
- Stable, policy-compliant outcomes in a stratified sample of routine repeated pairs, supporting specialization or workload allocation rather than inappropriate coordination.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

Review of the procurement-to-payment event log identified several control exceptions and workflow patterns requiring targeted follow-up. The evidence does not establish fraud, collusion, or improper conduct. Several observed patterns are plausibly consistent with legitimate operational specialization, emergency handling, system migration, shared-account use, and event-log mapping limitations.

The most concerning subgroup is the VendorMaster_Lena–AP_Kim population involving vendor bank-detail changes followed by payment approval. This subgroup combines a sensitive activity sequence, short elapsed times, concentration in vendor family V-77x, a high proportion of manual-upload records, and repeated correction loops. The pattern may be explained by the recent banking-platform migration and missing linkage between maintenance tickets and procurement cases; however, the available event log does not provide sufficient evidence to confirm that explanation for individual cases.

Emergency medical-supply and weekend-repair approval patterns involving Buyer_Carla/Approver_Gita and Buyer_Dan/Manager_Hugo show unusually short approval times and some segregation-of-duties exceptions. Their high emergency rates and stated specialist roles provide a credible operational explanation. Follow-up should therefore focus on cases lacking emergency documentation, delegated authority, or timely independent retrospective justification.

The same-person emergency override and retrospective-justification records represent a control-design or control-operation exception, but their interpretation is constrained by quarter-close spreadsheet migration and shared-account attribution issues. Audit should obtain original workflow records, source-system access logs, vendor bank-change evidence, migration tickets, and approval metadata before reaching conclusions. Data-quality remediation should precede person-level attribution or trend reporting.
