# 1. Data audit and estimands

**Queueing quantities and units**
* Opening window: 07:00–19:00 = 12 h = 720 min. All counts are *during opening hours*.
* `arrivals` = count of cases whose arrival timestamp falls in opening hours on the calendar day.
* `completed` = count of cases whose completion timestamp falls in opening hours on the calendar day. Completed  arrived same day.
* `median_service_min`, `p90_service_min` = active handling time, waiting excluded. Reported as daily medians / 90th percentiles, not means.
* `mean_wip` = mean number of cases in the system from 15-minute snapshots. Window for snapshots is not stated; assumption needed that snapshots are taken during opening hours only.
* `staff_fte` = mean concurrently available full-time-equivalent capacity during opening hours.
* `complex_pct` = % of daily arrivals that follow a multi-step complex variant.
* `peak_08_10_pct` = % of daily arrivals in 08:00–09:59.

**Limitations of aggregates**
* No 07:00 backlog per day, no overnight service, carry-over possible.
* Daily aggregates hide intra-day shape. Representative hourly vectors exist for only 4 days.
* Service statistics are median/p90 of active handling, not mean flow time.
* `completed` is a throughput proxy that can be decoupled from daily `arrivals` by backlog.

**Threats to naive inference**
1. Non-stationarity: case mix and demand shape change markedly ~2026-04-20.
2. Carry-over backlog: `mean_wip` and `completed` are linked across days; Little’s law requires boundary stability.
3. Outage on 2026-04-15 with 180 min closure creates a departure block and artificial backlog build-up.
4. Weekday/weekend composition confounds pooled means: arrivals ~100 vs ~45, staff 6 vs 2.6.
5. Staffing intervention starts 2026-04-29 with unknown implementation time, correlated with demand.
6. Median service  mean flow time; using median service as flow time is invalid.
7. Simpson-type reversal: pooled WIP/completion ratios move opposite to regime-specific ratios because high-complex days have both higher WIP and higher arrivals.

# 2. Primary regime segmentation

# 3. Alternative segmentation

**Capacity-pressure segmentation**
Define pressure = mean_wip / staff_fte.

* Low pressure: pressure <4.5. Corresponds roughly to baseline weekdays and weekends pre-mix.
* High pressure: pressure 6.5. Corresponds to outage/recovery, high-complex weekdays pre-intervention, and post-mix weekends.
* Reducing pressure: pressure 5-6 with completions > arrivals. Corresponds to intervention weekdays.

Operational story: demand and service mix drive pressure more than raw arrivals. This segmentation highlights that weekends after the mix change move into high pressure despite lower absolute arrivals, because service times and complex_pct rose.

Strengths vs primary: directly links staffing to observed WIP, useful for staffing rules. Weaknesses: pressure is an outcome, not a driver; it conflates demand, service time, and backlog state, making causal attribution harder. It also masks the distinct outage mechanism where departures are blocked, not just slowed.

# 4. Pooled versus segmented statistics

Pooled daily means over 28 days:
* arrivals 89.9 d¹, completed 86.0 d¹, mean_wip 29.9, staff 5.2 FTE, complex_pct 32.2%.

Segmented distortions:
1. **Weekday/weekend composition.** Pooled arrivals 90 d¹ is between weekday ~107 d¹ and weekend ~45 d¹. A pooled staffing rule would under-staff weekdays and over-staff weekends.
2. **Case-mix shift.** Pooled complex_pct 32% hides pre-mix ~21% and post-mix ~44-46% on weekdays. Service p90 pooled ~67 min vs 47 min pre-mix and 82 min post-mix.
3. **Outage and intervention.** Pooled completion rate 86 d¹ obscures 58 completions on outage day and 121 d¹ mean during intervention weekdays. Pooled WIP 30 hides 20 WIP in baseline weekdays and 44 WIP in high-complex pre-intervention weekdays.

Robust ranges: weekday arrivals interquartile pre-mix 98-105, post-mix 111-119. Weekend WIP interquartile pre-mix 12-14, post-mix 20-23.

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

Representative hourly vectors:

* 2026-04-07 ordinary weekday: arrivals mean 8.42 h¹, variance 24.9, Fano 2.96, CV 0.59.
* 2026-04-21 bursty post-mix weekday: arrivals mean 9.58 h¹, variance 88.9, Fano 9.28, CV 0.98. Two peaks 10-11 and 13-14.
* 2026-04-15 outage: arrivals mean 8.75 h¹, variance 33.2, Fano 3.80, completions zero 11:00-14:00.
* 2026-04-25 weekend: arrivals mean 4.25 h¹, variance 5.0, Fano 1.18, CV 0.53.

Fano >1 indicates overdispersion vs Poisson; burstiness rises sharply after mix change. Peak_08_10_pct rises from ~29% baseline to ~46% post-mix.

Daily sequence shows serial dependence: arrivals increase Mon-Fri in post-mix period and show carry-over: high WIP on 2026-04-20-22 persists to 2026-04-27-28. Lag-1 autocorrelation of arrivals is positive in post-mix block, near zero in baseline.

Stationarity: no single stationary regime over 28 days. Change-point evidence around 2026-04-20 where complex_pct, median_service, p90_service and peak_08_10_pct all step up simultaneously. Four representative days cannot establish a full stochastic model; they illustrate shape but not distribution tails or day-to-day variation.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

`W = L / `. L = mean_wip,  = arrivals per day for same window. Implied flow time in minutes = L / arrivals_per_day *1440.

| window / regime | boundary stability | throughput proxy | WIP comparability | carry-over risk | stationarity | implied flow time | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline low-complex weekdays 2026-04-06-10,13-14 | opening hours stable, no outage | arrivals  completions | snapshots likely during opening hours | low-moderate overnight carry | approx stationary within block | ~291 min using arrivals | approximate only |
| Outage day 2026-04-15 | departure block 11:00-14:00 | completions suppressed | WIP measured with backlog build | high | non-stationary | not defensible | not defensible |

Median service time is not used. Implied flow times exceed median active handling, reflecting waiting and backlog, as expected.

# 7. Operational implications

* Staffing: pooled averages suggest ~5.2 FTE average need. Segmented view shows 6.0 FTE insufficient for high-complex weekdays, leading to WIP ~44 vs ~20 baseline. Intervention to 6.8-7.0 FTE coincides with WIP decline and completions > arrivals.
* Backlog recovery: outage created ~30-40 case backlog. Recovery needed 2 days with elevated staff. Without explicit backlog tracking, Little’s law is misleading.
* Peak smoothing: post-mix peak_08_10_pct ~45-50% concentrates arrivals in 08-10. Hourly Fano ~9 suggests need for dynamic intra-day staffing or appointment smoothing, not just daily FTE.
* Data collection: need 07:00 backlog per day, hourly WIP, and case-level arrival/completion timestamps to apply PM4Py temporal features correctly. Current aggregates are insufficient for a queueing-law claim.

Policy that looks beneficial under pooled averages but risky segmented: a uniform +10% staffing increase across all days. Pooled WIP/staff improves modestly, but weekends would be overstaffed and the 10-11 burst on post-mix weekdays would remain uncovered, leaving high pressure and long waits.

Recommendations: regime-based staffing with separate weekday/post-mix and weekend schedules; monitor WIP/staff ratio as early warning; log exact intervention start time; collect case-level event log for proper PM4Py analysis.
