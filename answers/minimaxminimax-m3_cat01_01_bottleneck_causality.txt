## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional verdict:** `partly causal through mechanisms` — confidence **55/100**.

The crude gap (Orion 21.96 h vs. Nova 15.24 h;   +6.7 h) reverses or disappears inside every complexity stratum: High Nova 35.0 h vs. Orion 31.6 h (  3.4 h), Medium Nova 19.4 h vs. Orion 17.2 h (  2.2 h), Low Nova 9.6 h vs. Orion 7.5 h (  2.1 h). Across strata, Orion is *faster* on comparable work. This is the canonical signature of case-mix confounding plus routing-policy confounding: Orion is preferentially selected for harder cases and night/peak-load conditions.

Mechanism-level causation is plausible: Orion still shows longer *queue* waits within strata (e.g., High wait 19.5 h vs. 22.8 h; Medium 9.3 h vs. 11.1 h; Low 3.5 h vs. 5.1 h), so some operational effect on waiting time may exist. But cycle time is not meaningfully worse, and rework rates (50% vs. 50% on High) are identical. Service-time differences are within rounding.

**Why a 50% blanket reassignment may backfire:** it discards information the routing rule uses, concentrates difficult and night work in one group, likely inflates Nova's queue, and would degrade both speed and quality if Orion's apparent "slowness" was actually selection. It cannot improve performance over the crude baseline and may worsen stratified performance.

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 3 | **Beneficial Orion effect masked by selection.** Orion's certified reviewers actually accelerate hard cases. | More certified reviewers; better triage of compliance; fewer abandoned hard cases. | Orion High cycle 31.6 h < Nova High 35.0 h; Orion Med 17.2 h < Nova 19.4 h. | Crude gap +6.7 h contradicts; the apparent advantage could be selection (Orion takes cases it can finish faster). | Compare within-reviewer outcomes across groups if reviewers ever move between groups; otherwise run a randomized pilot routing Med cases 50/50. | Beneficial hypothesis predicts that in a randomized Med/High pilot, Orion-assigned cases finish faster than Nova-assigned ones at matched complexity. ## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1 — Queue/compliance capacity** | Temporarily add 1 reviewer to Orion night shift *and* parallelize compliance check with reviewer notification (start review prep while compliance runs). | Reduce `queue_wait` driven by night staffing and compliance dwell; this is the only mediator that Orion could plausibly worsen. | Operational (no case-level reassignment). | Pre-registered metrics; run 2 weeks; rollback if night-shift review_service degrades or appeals spike. | Variant frequencies (with/without compliance), DFG for Orion night, median `queue_wait` and `cycle_time` per complexity; bottleneck analysis on `wait_before_review_h`. | Isolates the queue-mediator hypothesis from the group-label hypothesis without changing assignment. | Rollback if cycle_time does not improve 5% within the same complexity mix, or if 30-day appeals > baseline +1 case. |
| **Phase 2 — Quasi-random routing pilot** | For 4 weeks, route a randomized 20% slice of incoming Med-complexity cases to Nova (treatment) vs. Orion (control), stratified by shift and load. | Identify direct effect of resource group at fixed complexity/shift/load. | Case-level randomization within Med stratum. | Stop if Nova's queue saturates (>2× baseline wait); exclude High from randomization initially (safety); pre-register analysis plan. | Per-case cycle, queue, service, rework, appeal; reviewer-level fixed effects; DFG per arm; conformance to intended route. | Provides an unbiased causal contrast on the largest balanced cell (Med); reveals reviewer identity contribution. | Rollback if Nova's queue wait exceeds +50% over baseline in Med stratum, or if any quality incident links to a rerouted case. |
| **Phase 3 — Targeted expand & matched evaluation** | If Phase 2 shows Orion  Nova on cycle at matched complexity, *narrow* the routing rule to send only a calibrated share of High+Night to Orion and route more Medium-day to Nova. Update the routing engine with explicit cost weights. | Replace group-label heuristic with mechanism-aware policy. | Policy change in the routing engine. | Maintain a holdout 10% of cases on the old rule for A/B comparison; track abandonment proxies (rework_count spikes, time-to-close variance). | Standardized cycle, queue, rework, appeals; PSI/drift on case mix; counterfactual simulation using replay. | Identifies which mechanism (compliance dwell vs. night staffing vs. reviewer mix) drove any change. | Rollback the policy if standardized cycle regresses by 3% on the holdout, or if any stratum shows worsening. |

**Final recommendation (156 words):** Do not implement the proposed 50% reassignment. The crude cycle-time gap is a Simpson-paradox artifact: within every complexity stratum Orion is at least as fast as Nova, and Orion's longer overall mean is driven by being routed 93% night and 43% High-complexity cases. A blanket reassignment would destroy the routing rule's information value, push harder work onto Nova's day shift, and likely inflate Nova's queue without improving aggregate cycle time — a probable net loss. Instead, run a three-phase plan: (1) add night-shift capacity and parallelize compliance to test the queue-mediator hypothesis; (2) launch a randomized Med-complexity routing pilot to obtain an unbiased group contrast; (3) update the routing engine only after evidence shows which mechanism (queue, compliance, reviewer skill) actually drives delays. This sequence preserves operations, yields publishable causal evidence, and avoids the high-risk 50% cut. The decision under consideration conflates a label with a mechanism; the answer is to study the mechanism.

def warn_small(cell_df, label):
    if len(cell_df) < 3:
        warnings.warn(f"Small cell ({len(cell_df)} cases) for {label}: estimates unstable.", RuntimeWarning)

def compare(df, label):
    g = df.groupby('group')['cycle_time_h'].agg(['count', 'mean', 'median'])
    print(f"\n[{label}] crude cycle_time by group:")
    print(g)
    if (g['count'] < 3).any():
        warnings.warn(f"Group with <3 cases in {label}; means are not reliable.", RuntimeWarning)

def stratified(df):
    print("\n[Stratified by complexity] mean cycle_time, mean wait, n:")
    out = (df.groupby(['complexity', 'group'])
             .agg(n=('case_id', 'count'),
                  mean_cycle=('cycle_time_h', 'mean'),
                  mean_wait=('wait_before_review_h', 'mean'),
                  rework_rate=('rework_count', 'mean'))
             .round(2))
    print(out)
    for (cx, gp), sub in df.groupby(['complexity', 'group']):
        warn_small(sub, f"stratum complexity={cx}, group={gp}")

def standardized(df, weights):
    print("\n[Complexity-standardized mean cycle_time]")
    rows = []
    for cx, w in weights.items():
        for gp in ['Orion', 'Nova']:
            m = df[(df['complexity'] == cx) & (df['group'] == gp)]['cycle_time_h'].mean()
            n = len(df[(df['complexity'] == cx) & (df['group'] == gp)])
            rows.append((cx, gp, n, m, w))
    sdf = pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=['complexity', 'group', 'n', 'mean', 'weight'])
    print(sdf)
    out = (sdf.assign(contrib=sdf['mean'] * sdf['weight'])
              .groupby('group')['contrib'].sum().round(2))
    print("Standardized mean cycle_time (common weights):")
    print(out)
    return sdf

def overlap(df):
    print("\n[Overlap counts by (complexity, shift, resource_group)]")

    print(piv)

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Whether unobserved case risk differs systematically between Orion and Nova within the same complexity stratum** — most valuable addition: a case-level `risk_score` (e.g., claim amount band, prior-claim count, fraud flags) logged at Classify.
2. **Whether Orion's longer queue wait within strata reflects staffing geometry, reviewer skill mix, or random load fluctuation** — most valuable addition: per-event timestamps and `resource_id` for every activity, enabling queue-state reconstruction and within-reviewer comparisons.
3. **Whether the completed-case sample is selection-biased (i.e., cases that Orion finished but Nova would have abandoned)** — most valuable addition: a parallel `case_status` field (Open / Abandoned / Completed) plus abandonment timestamps, to model truncation explicitly.
4. **What the routing rule actually conditions on** — most valuable addition: logged `routing_decision_features` and the rule's version, plus a counterfactual log of the *other* group the engine considered, enabling propensity-score analysis on the assignment mechanism itself.
5. **Whether the 30-day appeal is a meaningful quality signal or post-process noise** — most valuable addition: a confirmed `appeal_upheld` flag with reason codes, separating policy-side from review-side quality.
