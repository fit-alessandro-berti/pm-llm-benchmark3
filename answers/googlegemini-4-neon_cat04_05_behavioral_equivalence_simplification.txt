# 1. Equivalence Notions

Equivalence between two process models is only well-defined relative to an explicit **observation boundary** (which events are visible vs. hidden) and **abstraction function** (which aspects of traces are retained vs. collapsed).

Let $\Sigma = \{\text{'Open Case'}, \text{'Analyze Revision'}, \text{'Check Revision'}, \text{'Approve Revision'}, \text{'Rework Case'}, \text{'Close Case'}\}$ be the visible alphabet, and let $\tau \notin \Sigma$ denote silent transitions. For any complete execution sequence $\rho \in (\Sigma \cup \{\tau\})^*$ from initial marking to final marking, let $\pi_{\Sigma}(\rho) \in \Sigma^*$ denote its projection onto $\Sigma$ (removing all $\tau$). The **visible language** of a model $M$ is $L(M) = \{\pi_{\Sigma}(\rho) \mid \rho \text{ is a complete execution of } M\}$.

### Treatment of Repeated Labels
In both Model A and Model B, every visible label appears on **at most one leaf** (zero duplicate-label nodes in either tree). However, loop unfolding produces multiple occurrences of the same visible label within a single trace. Across our equivalence notions:
- **Transition identity is never observed**: two distinct internal transitions carrying the same label are indistinguishable outside the model.
- **Visible trace semantics (unbounded, P1, P3)** distinguish repeated occurrences of a label strictly by **sequence position** (so both total multiplicity and relative ordering matter).
- **Dashboard abstraction (P2)** discards sequence position for internal activities, collapsing `Check Revision` and `Rework Case` to **Booleans**, `Analyze Revision` to **multiplicity (count) only**, hiding `Approve Revision`, and retaining boundary positions only for `Open Case` and `Close Case`.

### Formal Definitions

1. **Visible trace equivalence after hiding $\tau$ (unbounded):**
   Models $M_1$ and $M_2$ are visible-trace equivalent, written $M_1 \equiv_{\text{vis}} M_2$, iff
   $$L(M_1) = L(M_2).$$

2. **Bounded visible-language equivalence under P3:**
   Define the P3 bounding predicate on visible traces $\sigma \in \Sigma^*$:
   $$B_{\text{P3}}(\sigma) \iff |\sigma| \le 12 \;\land\; \#_{\text{'Analyze Revision'}}(\sigma) \le 3.$$
   Let $L_{\text{P3}}(M) = \{\sigma \in L(M) \mid B_{\text{P3}}(\sigma)\}$. Models $M_1$ and $M_2$ are P3-equivalent, written $M_1 \equiv_{\text{P3}} M_2$, iff
   $$L_{\text{P3}}(M_1) = L_{\text{P3}}(M_2).$$
   Because $L_{\text{P3}}(M) \subseteq L(M)$, $M_1 \not\equiv_{\text{P3}} M_2$ **proves** $M_1 \not\equiv_{\text{vis}} M_2$, whereas $M_1 \equiv_{\text{P3}} M_2$ provides only bounded empirical evidence, never a proof of unbounded equivalence.

3. **Business-relevant equivalence under P1 (operational compliance view):**
   Under P1, all visible labels in $\Sigma$ remain observable (including the count and placement of `Approve Revision`), silent $\tau$ is hidden, and every complete trace must satisfy the compliance predicate $\Phi_{\text{P1}}(\sigma)$:
   - $\sigma$ starts with `'Open Case'` and ends with `'Close Case'`;
   - Splitting the interior of $\sigma$ at each `'Rework Case'` yields $k \ge 1$ revision segments $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_k$;
   - Every segment $\sigma_i$ contains exactly one `'Analyze Revision'`, exactly one `'Check Revision'` (occurring before the next `'Rework Case'` or `'Close Case'`), and at most one `'Approve Revision'`.
   
   Because P1 retains the number and placement of all visible activities (including approvals), two models are P1-equivalent iff they admit the same visible behavior under this observation boundary ($L(M_1) = L(M_2)$) and satisfy the same compliance invariants—at minimum, a model violating $\Phi_{\text{P1}}$ cannot be P1-equivalent to a compliant model.

4. **Abstracted dashboard equivalence under P2 (executive dashboard view):**
   Define the dashboard abstraction $\alpha_{\text{P2}}: \Sigma^* \to \mathcal{D}$ mapping any non-empty complete trace $\sigma$ to the summary tuple
   $$\alpha_{\text{P2}}(\sigma) = \Big(\text{first}(\sigma),\; \text{last}(\sigma),\; \#_{\text{'Check Revision'}}(\sigma) \ge 1,\; \#_{\text{'Analyze Revision'}}(\sigma),\; \#_{\text{'Rework Case'}}(\sigma) \ge 1\Big).$$
   Notice that $\alpha_{\text{P2}}$ hides `'Approve Revision'` and $\tau$, collapses `'Check Revision'` multiplicity to a Boolean, ignores interleaving between `'Analyze Revision'` and `'Check Revision'`, records the exact count of `'Analyze Revision'`, and records whether any `'Rework Case'` occurred. Models $M_1$ and $M_2$ are P2-equivalent, written $M_1 \equiv_{\text{P2}} M_2$, iff
   $$\{\alpha_{\text{P2}}(\sigma) \mid \sigma \in L(M_1)\} = \{\alpha_{\text{P2}}(\sigma) \mid \sigma \in L(M_2)\}.$$

| **Loop scope** | `*` encloses `->(+('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision'), X('Approve Revision', tau))` with redo `'Rework Case'`. | `*` encloses only `->('Analyze Revision', X('Approve Revision', tau))` with redo `'Rework Case'`; `'Check Revision'` sits *outside* the loop. | In Model A, checking is a per-revision obligation repeated on every rework cycle. In Model B, checking is a case-level activity detached from rework iterations. |
| **Min / max `Check Revision` occurrences** | Minimum $1$, maximum unbounded ($\infty$); strictly equal to $k = \#_{\text{'Analyze Revision'}}$. | Minimum $1$, maximum $1$; strictly invariant regardless of $k$. | For all traces with $k \ge 2$ analysis attempts, $L(A)$ and $L(B)$ are **completely disjoint** ($\#_{\text{Check}} = k \ge 2$ in A vs. $\#_{\text{Check}} = 1$ in B). |
| **Possible placement of `Check Revision`** | Exactly once inside each revision segment, concurrently with that segment's `'Analyze Revision'`, and **strictly before** that segment's optional `'Approve Revision'`, the next `'Rework Case'`, and `'Close Case'`. | Anywhere between `'Open Case'` and `'Close Case'`: before the first analysis, between analysis and approval, **after** an approval, before or after any `'Rework Case'`, or immediately before `'Close Case'`. | Model B allows approvals before checking (even at $k=1$) and allows revisions to enter rework or reach closure without ever being checked. |
| **Optional approval occurrences** | $0 \le \#_{\text{'Approve Revision'}} \le k$; each approval is gated on completion of **both** `'Analyze Revision'` and `'Check Revision'` for that iteration. | $0 \le \#_{\text{'Approve Revision'}} \le k$; each approval is gated **only** on `'Analyze Revision'` for that iteration, independent of `'Check Revision'`. | When `Approve Revision` fires (instead of `tau`), it acts as an observable synchronization barrier in Model A that does not exist in Model B, separating the two models even at $k=1$. |
| **Concurrency (`+`)** | Local concurrency inside each loop iteration between `'Analyze Revision'` and `'Check Revision'` only. | Global concurrency between a single `'Check Revision'` and the entire unbounded revision loop `*`. | Model A restricts interleaving to pairs of `(Analyze, Check)` within one revision; Model B allows `'Check Revision'` to interleave with arbitrarily many analyses, approvals, and reworks. |
| **Join before `Close Case`** | The loop `*` exits after completing the final iteration's `X('Approve Revision', tau)` (which itself follows the `+` join of the $k$-th `Analyze` and $k$-th `Check`), then `'Close Case'` executes. | The top-level `+` joins the single `'Check Revision'` branch with the exited loop `*` branch before `'Close Case'` executes. | Both models guarantee at least one `'Analyze Revision'` and at least one `'Check Revision'` before `'Close Case'`, which is why boundary/existence checks under P2 match even though operational compliance fails. |

# 3. Verdicts

| View | Verdict | Confidence | Decisive reason | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P1 (Operational compliance)** | `not equivalent` | High | Model A enforces one `Check Revision` per revision before optional `Approve Revision`, `Rework Case`, or `Close Case`. Model B executes `Check Revision` only once per case (leaving $k-1$ revisions unchecked for $k \ge 2$) and allows `Approve Revision` before `Check Revision` even at $k=1$. | Note that Model A itself allows `Check Revision` to precede `Analyze Revision` within a single revision due to `+`; if business policy requires strict analyze-then-check order, Model A would also need refinement. |
| **P2 (Executive dashboard)** | `equivalent only under stated abstraction` | High | For every $k \ge 1$, both models yield the exact same set of abstracted dashboard tuples: $\big\{(\text{'Open Case'}, \text{'Close Case'}, \text{True}, k, k \ge 2) \mid k \ge 1\big\}$. | Equivalence breaks immediately if the dashboard adds check counts, approval rates, check-to-rework timing, or stochastic case-mix frequencies (unless branching probabilities match). |
| **P3 (Bounded testing)** | `not equivalent` | High | Exhaustive analysis within $|\sigma| \le 12$ and $\#_{\text{Analyze}} \le 3$ yields $|L_{\text{P3}}(A)| = 76$ and $|L_{\text{P3}}(B)| = 85$, with only **4 shared traces** (all at $k=1$); 72 traces are unique to A and 81 are unique to B. | Bounded testing suffices here to *refute* equivalence via explicit witnesses; had a tighter bound been misconfigured (e.g., $k=1$ with approvals hidden), bounded testing would have missed the divergence. |
| **Unbounded visible trace equivalence ($\equiv_{\text{vis}}$)** | `not equivalent` | High | Follows directly from P3 counterexamples (and analytically: $L(A) \cap L(B)$ consists solely of the 4 traces at $k=1$ where `Check Revision` precedes any `Approve Revision`; for all $k \ge 2$, $L(A) \cap L(B) = \emptyset$). | None; this is an exact formal proof over the complete infinite languages. |

# 4. Counterexample Traces

In fact, neither language includes the other ($L(A) \not\subseteq L(B)$ and $L(B) \not\subseteq L(A)$): their intersection contains **only 4 traces** (all with $k=1$). Below are five complete visible traces—two admitted only by Model A and three admitted only by Model B—all falling within the P3 bound ($|\sigma| \le 12$, $\#_{\text{Analyze}} \le 3$).

| Trace ID | Trace | Model A | Model B | First decisive semantic difference | Relevant view |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CE-A1** | `['Open Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision', 'Rework Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision', 'Close Case']` | Admitted | Rejected | At event 6, a **second** `'Check Revision'` occurs after `'Rework Case'`; Model B executes `'Check Revision'` strictly once per case. | P1, P3, $\equiv_{\text{vis}}$ |
| **CE-A2** | `['Open Case', 'Check Revision', 'Analyze Revision', 'Approve Revision', 'Rework Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision', 'Approve Revision', 'Close Case']` | Admitted | Rejected | At event 7, a **second** `'Check Revision'` occurs inside the second revision before the second `'Approve Revision'`; Model B cannot repeat `'Check Revision'`. | P1, P3, $\equiv_{\text{vis}}$ |
| **CE-B1** | `['Open Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Approve Revision', 'Check Revision', 'Close Case']` | Rejected | Admitted | At event 3, `'Approve Revision'` occurs **before** `'Check Revision'` has completed; in Model A, `+('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision')` must join before `X('Approve Revision', tau)` is enabled. | P1, P3, $\equiv_{\text{vis}}$ |
| **CE-B2** | `['Open Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Rework Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision', 'Close Case']` | Rejected | Admitted | At event 3, `'Rework Case'` occurs with **zero** preceding `'Check Revision'` events; in Model A, the first iteration cannot exit into `'Rework Case'` without completing `'Check Revision'`. | P1, P3, $\equiv_{\text{vis}}$ |
| **CE-B3** | `['Open Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision', 'Approve Revision', 'Rework Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Approve Revision', 'Close Case']` | Rejected | Admitted | At event 7, the second revision is approved (and at event 8 the case closes) without a second `'Check Revision'`; Model A requires a fresh `'Check Revision'` after `'Rework Case'` before approval or closure. | P1, P3, $\equiv_{\text{vis}}$ |

### Effect of Optional `Approve Revision` and Hidden `tau`
- **Why optional `Approve Revision` sharpens the separation:** When `Approve Revision` is executed (rather than `tau`), it serves as an observable synchronization checkpoint after `+('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision')` in Model A. That single branch produces the shortest counterexample (**CE-B1**, length 5, only $k=1$ analysis attempt), where Model B approves a revision *before* checking it.
- **What happens when `tau` is chosen and hidden:** If every iteration chooses `tau` (auto-approval, so `Approve Revision` never appears), the $k=1$ subsets of Model A and Model B become **identical** (both admit only `['Open Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision', 'Close Case']` and `['Open Case', 'Check Revision', 'Analyze Revision', 'Close Case']`). Crucially, however, hiding `tau` does **not** make the models equivalent: as **CE-A1** and **CE-B2** (both containing zero approvals and $k=2$ analysis attempts) demonstrate, `'Rework Case'` and `'Close Case'` still expose the loop-scope mismatch between $\#_{\text{Check}} = k$ (Model A) and $\#_{\text{Check}} = 1$ (Model B).
- **Why P2 collapses all five counterexamples:** Applying $\alpha_{\text{P2}}$ maps **CE-B1** to `('Open Case', 'Close Case', True, 1, False)` and maps **CE-A1**, **CE-A2**, **CE-B2**, and **CE-B3** all to `('Open Case', 'Close Case', True, 2, True)`—every one of which is realizable in both models.

# 5. Bounded Comparison Code

The script below performs **exhaustive bounded breadth-first search (BFS)** over the Petri net state space `(marking, visible_trace)` using `pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(...)`.

### Exhaustive Bounded BFS vs. Playout (`pm4py.play_out`)
- **Exhaustive bounded BFS (implemented below):** Because sound process-tree WF-nets are 1-safe (finitely many reachable markings) and visible length is monotonically non-decreasing up to `max_visible_len = 12`, deduplicating visited states on `(marking_key, visible_trace)` terminates silent $\tau$-cycles and explores **every** complete trace within the P3 bound. Critically, when `len(trace) == max_visible_len`, silent transitions are *still* allowed to fire so trailing structural `tau` transitions can reach the final marking `fm`.
- **Random/heuristic playout (`pm4py.play_out`):** Playout samples a finite number of simulated runs stochastically. It is **not exhaustive**: it can miss low-probability interleavings or deeper rework paths (false equivalence), cannot guarantee exact language cardinality, and depends on unstated random branching choices.
- **Scope limit:** Even exhaustive bounded BFS only proves or refutes equivalence *within the P3 bound*. Here it refutes equivalence (finding 76 traces for Model A, 85 for Model B, 4 shared, 72 A-only, and 81 B-only).

```python
from collections import deque
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import Marking

TREE_A_STR = (
    "->('Open Case', "
    "*(->(+('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision'), X('Approve Revision', tau)), 'Rework Case'), "
    "'Close Case')"
)

TREE_B_STR = (
    "->('Open Case', "
    "+('Check Revision', *(->('Analyze Revision', X('Approve Revision', tau)), 'Rework Case')), "
    "'Close Case')"
)


def fire_transition(marking: Marking, transition) -> Marking:
    """Apply standard Petri net firing rule, pruning zero-token places."""
    new_marking = Marking(marking)
    for arc in transition.in_arcs:
        new_marking[arc.source] -= arc.weight
        if new_marking[arc.source] == 0:
            del new_marking[arc.source]
    for arc in transition.out_arcs:
        new_marking[arc.target] = new_marking.get(arc.target, 0) + arc.weight
    return new_marking


def marking_key(marking: Marking) -> frozenset:
    """Canonical hashable representation of a marking."""
    return frozenset((place, count) for place, count in marking.items() if count > 0)


def check_net_soundness(name: str, net, im: Marking, fm: Marking) -> bool:
    result = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=False)
    is_sound = result[0] if isinstance(result, tuple) else bool(result)
    print(f"[{name}] Soundness: {is_sound}")
    return is_sound


def exhaustive_bounded_traces(
    net,
    im: Marking,
    fm: Marking,
    max_visible_len: int = 12,
    max_analyze: int = 3,
    analyze_label: str = "Analyze Revision",
) -> set[tuple[str, ...]]:
    """
    Exhaustive BFS over (marking, visible_trace) within the P3 bound.
    - Handles silent transitions (t.label is None) without incrementing visible length.
    - Prevents silent-cycle non-termination via visited set on (marking_key, visible_trace).
    - Allows trailing silent transitions even when len(trace) == max_visible_len.
    - Records a trace as complete iff marking_key(curr_m) == marking_key(fm).
    """
    fm_key = marking_key(fm)
    start_key = marking_key(im)
    visited = {(start_key, ())}
    queue = deque([(im, (), 0)])  # (marking, visible_trace, analyze_count)
    complete_traces: set[tuple[str, ...]] = set()

    while queue:
        curr_m, trace, analyze_cnt = queue.popleft()
        if marking_key(curr_m) == fm_key:
            complete_traces.add(trace)

        for t in pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, curr_m):
            if t.label is None:
                next_trace = trace
                next_analyze = analyze_cnt
            else:
                if len(trace) + 1 > max_visible_len:
                    continue
                next_analyze = analyze_cnt + (1 if t.label == analyze_label else 0)
                if next_analyze > max_analyze:
                    continue
                next_trace = trace + (t.label,)

            next_m = fire_transition(curr_m, t)
            state = (marking_key(next_m), next_trace)
            if state not in visited:
                visited.add(state)
                queue.append((next_m, next_trace, next_analyze))

    return complete_traces


def dashboard_abstraction_p2(trace: tuple[str, ...]) -> tuple:
    """Exact P2 executive dashboard projection."""
    return (
        trace[0],
        trace[-1],
        "Check Revision" in trace,
        trace.count("Analyze Revision"),
        "Rework Case" in trace,
    )


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # 1. Parse both process trees
    tree_a = pm4py.parse_process_tree(TREE_A_STR)
    tree_b = pm4py.parse_process_tree(TREE_B_STR)

    # 2. Convert to accepting Petri nets
    net_a, im_a, fm_a = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_a)
    net_b, im_b, fm_b = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_b)

    # 3. Check soundness
    assert check_net_soundness("Model A", net_a, im_a, fm_a)
    assert check_net_soundness("Model B", net_b, im_b, fm_b)

    # 4. Exhaustive bounded exploration under P3 (<= 12 visible events, <= 3 Analyze Revision)
    traces_a = exhaustive_bounded_traces(net_a, im_a, fm_a, max_visible_len=12, max_analyze=3)
    traces_b = exhaustive_bounded_traces(net_b, im_b, fm_b, max_visible_len=12, max_analyze=3)

    # 5. Compare visible trace sets under P3
    shared = traces_a & traces_b
    only_a = sorted(traces_a - traces_b, key=lambda t: (len(t), t))
    only_b = sorted(traces_b - traces_a, key=lambda t: (len(t), t))

    print(f"\nP3 Exhaustive Bounded Results (|trace| <= 12, #Analyze <= 3):")
    print(f"  |L_P3(A)|             = {len(traces_a)}")   # Analytical expectation: 76
    print(f"  |L_P3(B)|             = {len(traces_b)}")   # Analytical expectation: 85
    print(f"  |L_P3(A) & L_P3(B)|   = {len(shared)}")     # Analytical expectation: 4
    print(f"  |L_P3(A) \\ L_P3(B)|  = {len(only_a)}")     # Analytical expectation: 72
    print(f"  |L_P3(B) \\ L_P3(A)|  = {len(only_b)}")     # Analytical expectation: 81
    print(f"  P3 equivalent?        = {traces_a == traces_b}")

    # 6. Print sample traces unique to each model
    print("\nShortest traces unique to Model A (first 5):")
    for t in only_a[:5]:
        print("  A-only:", list(t))

    print("\nShortest traces unique to Model B (first 5):")
    for t in only_b[:5]:
        print("  B-only:", list(t))

    # Bonus sanity check: P2 abstraction on the bounded languages
    p2_a = {dashboard_abstraction_p2(t) for t in traces_a}
    p2_b = {dashboard_abstraction_p2(t) for t in traces_b}
    print(f"\nP2 abstracted sets equal within P3 bound? {p2_a == p2_b}")
    print("  P2 tuples:", sorted(p2_a))
```

### Analytical Verification of Exact P3 Cardinalities
We can verify the BFS counts independently by combinatorics ($k = \#_{\text{Analyze}} \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, $a = \#_{\text{Approve}} \in \{0, \dots, k\}$):
- **Model A:** Each iteration chooses `(Analyze, Check)` or `(Check, Analyze)` ($2^k$ ways) and which $a$ iterations include `Approve` ($\binom{k}{a}$ ways), giving length $3k + 1 + a$:
  - $k=1$: $2 + 2 = 4$ (lengths 4–5);
  - $k=2$: $4 + 8 + 4 = 16$ (lengths 7–9);
  - $k=3$: $8 + 24 + 24 = 56$ (lengths 10–12; $a=3$ has length $13 > 12$ and is excluded).
  - Total: $|L_{\text{P3}}(A)| = 4 + 16 + 56 = \mathbf{76}$.
- **Model B:** The loop produces $\binom{k}{a}$ base sequences of length $2k - 1 + a$; inserting one `'Check Revision'` at any of the $2k + a$ positions produces distinct traces of length $2k + 2 + a \le 11 \le 12$:
  - $k=1$: $2 + 3 = 5$;
  - $k=2$: $4 + 10 + 6 = 20$;
  - $k=3$: $6 + 21 + 24 + 9 = 60$.
  - Total: $|L_{\text{P3}}(B)| = 5 + 20 + 60 = \mathbf{85}$.
- **Intersection:** Only the $4$ traces of Model A at $k=1$ also belong to Model B, leaving $72$ A-only and $81$ B-only traces.

# 6. Stochastic and Footprint Comparison

### 1. Behavioral Footprints (`pm4py.discover_footprints`)
Behavioral footprints summarize a model by its **directly-follows (order-1) relations**: causal sequence (`->`), parallelism (`||`), and non-directly-follows (`#`), plus start/end activities.
- **What footprints actually detect between Model A and Model B:**
  - Start activities (`{'Open Case'}`), end activities (`{'Close Case'}`), and activity sets are identical.
  - Both models exhibit `Analyze || Check`, `Analyze || Rework`, `Check || Rework`, `Analyze -> Approve`, and `Approve -> Rework`.
  - **Exact footprint difference:** In Model A, `'Check Revision'` can directly precede `'Approve Revision'` (`Check -> Approve`), but `'Approve Revision'` can **never** directly precede `'Check Revision'` (any later check is separated by `'Rework Case'`). Thus Model A classifies the pair as **causal sequence** (`Check -> Approve`). In Model B, `'Approve Revision'` *can* directly precede `'Check Revision'` (as in **CE-B1**), so Model B classifies the pair as **parallel** (`Check || Approve`).
- **Why equal footprints would still not establish trace equivalence:**
  Footprints are strictly local pairwise projections. If `Approve Revision` were absent or hidden, Model A and Model B would have **100% identical footprint matrices**—both having `Check || Rework`, `Analyze || Rework`, and `Analyze || Check`—even though their languages remain disjoint for every trace with $k \ge 2$! Footprints cannot express loop scope, global occurrence counts ($\#_{\text{Check}} = \#_{\text{Analyze}}$ vs. $\#_{\text{Check}} = 1$), or long-range synchronization.

### 2. Stochastic Languages and Earth Mover's Distance (`pm4py.get_stochastic_language`, `pm4py.compute_emd`)
Process trees A and B contain **no transition weights or probabilities**. To compute a stochastic language or Earth Mover's Distance (EMD), one must either:
1. derive empirical trace distributions from an observed event log, or
2. **explicitly declare** a stochastic parameterization (e.g., uniform branching via playout, or declared loop-continuation probability $p_{\text{rework}}$ and approval probability $p_{\text{approve}}$).

- **How EMD adds evidence:** Under declared probabilities (or against a production log), `pm4py.compute_emd` quantifies how much probability mass must be moved (weighted by trace edit distance) to align two stochastic behaviors. A non-zero EMD under identical branching assumptions highlights behavioral divergence weighted by operational likelihood.
- **Why a low EMD does NOT establish trace equivalence:**
  Suppose we declare $p_{\text{rework}} = 0.02$ and $p_{\text{approve}} = 0.05$. Then roughly $(1 - 0.02)(1 - 0.05) = 93.1\%$ of probability mass in *both* Model A and Model B falls on the 4 shared $k=1$ traces, pushing EMD close to $0$ even though $L(A)$ and $L(B)$ are disjoint for every rework case ($k \ge 2$) and Model B violates P1 compliance on 100% of rework cases! Conversely, two *trace-equivalent* models with different branching probabilities yield a high EMD.

# 7. Complexity Comparison

### Exact Structural Derivation from the Process Trees
Using the convention that **root depth = 0** (so tree depth equals the maximum number of edges from root to a leaf; add 1 if counting node levels):

- **Model A nodes (12 total):**
  - Depth 0: `->`
  - Depth 1: `'Open Case'`, `*`, `'Close Case'`
  - Depth 2: `->`, `'Rework Case'`

  - Depth 1: `'Open Case'`, `+`, `'Close Case'`
  - Depth 2: `'Check Revision'`, `*`
  - Depth 3: `->`, `'Rework Case'`
  - Depth 4: `'Analyze Revision'`, `X`
  - Depth 5: `'Approve Revision'`, `tau`

|---|---|---|---|
| **Process-tree node count** | 12 (5 operators, 7 leaves) | 12 (5 operators, 7 leaves) | Identical |
| **Operator multiset** | `->` (2), `*` (1), `+` (1), `X` (1) | `->` (2), `*` (1), `+` (1), `X` (1) | Identical |

**Key lesson:** Syntactic complexity metrics are almost indistinguishable between Model A and Model B—identical node counts, identical operator multisets, identical leaf sets, zero duplicate labels, and nearly identical Petri net arc-degree simplicity (`1 / (1 + max(mean_degree - k, 0))` with default $k = 2$). Structural similarity provides **zero guarantee** of behavioral equivalence.

### Self-Contained Structural & Petri Net Simplicity Code

```python
from collections import Counter
import pm4py

TREE_A_STR = (
    "->('Open Case', "
    "*(->(+('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision'), X('Approve Revision', tau)), 'Rework Case'), "
    "'Close Case')"
)
TREE_B_STR = (
    "->('Open Case', "
    "+('Check Revision', *(->('Analyze Revision', X('Approve Revision', tau)), 'Rework Case')), "
    "'Close Case')"
)


def process_tree_metrics(tree) -> dict:
    """Compute exact structural metrics recursively on a PM4Py ProcessTree."""
    visible_labels = []
    silent_leaves = 0
    operator_nodes = 0

    def walk(node, depth: int) -> tuple[int, int]:
        nonlocal silent_leaves, operator_nodes
        if not node.children:
            if node.label is None:
                silent_leaves += 1
            else:
                visible_labels.append(node.label)
            return 1, depth
        operator_nodes += 1
        total_nodes = 1
        max_d = depth
        for child in node.children:
            c_nodes, c_depth = walk(child, depth + 1)
            total_nodes += c_nodes
            max_d = max(max_d, c_depth)
        return total_nodes, max_d

    node_count, max_depth = walk(tree, 0)
    label_counts = Counter(visible_labels)
    duplicate_label_occurrences = sum(c - 1 for c in label_counts.values() if c > 1)
    duplicate_label_types = sum(1 for c in label_counts.values() if c > 1)

    return {
        "node_count": node_count,
        "operator_count": operator_nodes,
        "depth_edges": max_depth,
        "visible_leaves": len(visible_labels),
        "silent_leaves": silent_leaves,
        "duplicate_label_types": duplicate_label_types,
        "duplicate_label_occurrences": duplicate_label_occurrences,
    }


for name, tree_str in [("Model A", TREE_A_STR), ("Model B", TREE_B_STR)]:
    tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_str)
    metrics = process_tree_metrics(tree)
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)
    simplicity = pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm)
    print(
        f"{name}: {metrics} | "
        f"Petri net: |P|={len(net.places)}, |T|={len(net.transitions)}, |F|={len(net.arcs)}, "
        f"simplicity={simplicity:.4f}"
    )
```

# 8. Simplified Model

### Target Semantics Choice
**No single simplification can preserve P1, P2, and P3 simultaneously:**
- Preserving **P3** (exact visible language within bounds) requires keeping `Approve Revision`, `tau`, and all concurrency interleavings of Model A (or B), leaving no meaningful reduction—Model A is already minimal in process-tree form for its exact language.
- Preserving **P1** (operational compliance with visible optional approvals) likewise requires keeping the loop-scoped check and optional approval choice; at most one could drop concurrency (`+` $\to$ `->`), which saves only 1 node (12 $\to$ 11) while **changing** Model A's visible language.

Accordingly, we target **P2 (executive dashboard view)**, where a dramatic, **exact** semantics-preserving simplification exists.

### 1. Compact Process-Tree Expression

```text
->(
  'Open Case',
  *('Analyze Revision', 'Rework Case'),
  'Check Revision',
  'Close Case'
)
```

For every $k \ge 1$, this model generates exactly one canonical trace:
$$\sigma_k = \text{'Open Case'},\; \text{'Analyze Revision'},\; (\text{'Rework Case'}, \text{'Analyze Revision'})^{k-1},\; \text{'Check Revision'},\; \text{'Close Case'}.$$
Its P2 dashboard abstraction is $\big\{(\text{'Open Case'}, \text{'Close Case'}, \text{True}, k, k \ge 2) \mid k \ge 1\big\}$, which is **identical** to both $\alpha_{\text{P2}}(L(A))$ and $\alpha_{\text{P2}}(L(B))$.

### Structural Comparison Against Both Originals
- Nodes: root `->` (depth 0); children `'Open Case'`, `*`, `'Check Revision'`, `'Close Case'` (depth 1); children of `*`: `'Analyze Revision'`, `'Rework Case'` (depth 2).

| Metric | Model A | Model B | `simplified_model` | Reduction vs. Originals |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Total nodes** | 12 | 12 | **7** | $-41.7\%$ vs. A & B |
| **Operator nodes** | 5 (`->`, `*`, `->`, `+`, `X`) | 5 (`->`, `+`, `*`, `->`, `X`) | **2** (`->`, `*`) | $-60.0\%$ vs. A & B |
| **Depth (edges)** | 4 | 5 | **2** | $-50.0\%$ vs. A; $-60.0\%$ vs. B |
| **Visible leaves** | 6 | 6 | **5** | $-1$ (`Approve Revision` abstracted) |
| **Silent (`tau`) leaves** | 1 | 1 | **0** | $-100\%$ |
| **Concurrency (`+`) / Choice (`X`)** | 1 / 1 | 1 / 1 | **0 / 0** | Eliminated |
| **Duplicate labels** | 0 | 0 | **0** | Unchanged |
| **P3 trace count** | 76 | 85 | **3** | 1 canonical trace per $k \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ |

```python
# Regression & negative trace validation against exact bounded traces (k <= 3)
def accepts_via_exact_pattern(trace: tuple[str, ...]) -> bool:
    """Exact language membership for simplified_model."""
    if len(trace) < 4 or trace[0] != "Open Case" or trace[-2:] != ("Check Revision", "Close Case"):

    middle = trace[1:-2]
    if len(middle) % 2 == 0:
        return False
    return all(
        middle[i] == ("Analyze Revision" if i % 2 == 0 else "Rework Case")
        for i in range(len(middle))
    )

regression_traces = [

        "Open Case", "Analyze Revision", "Rework Case",

        "Check Revision", "Close Case",

]

negative_traces = [
    ("Open Case", "Analyze Revision", "Close Case"),

assert all(not accepts_via_exact_pattern(t) for t in negative_traces), "Negative trace accepted!"
print("All 3 regression traces accepted and all 4 negative traces rejected.")
```

### 3. Property Preservation Table

| Property | Status | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| `Open Case` first and `Close Case` last | **Exact** | Preserved strictly by the top-level sequence `->`. |
| At least one `Analyze Revision` and at least one `Check Revision` | **Exact** | Loop executes `Analyze Revision` $\ge 1$ times; `Check Revision` executes once. |
| Exact count of `Analyze Revision` ($k \ge 1$) | **Exact** | Unbounded positive integer range $\{1, 2, 3, \dots\}$ preserved. |
| Exact count of `Rework Case` ($k - 1$) and Boolean `rework_occurred` ($k \ge 2$) | **Exact** | `Rework Case` strictly separates consecutive analysis attempts. |
| Dashboard abstraction $\alpha_{\text{P2}}$ | **Exact** | $\alpha_{\text{P2}}(L(\text{simplified\_model})) = \alpha_{\text{P2}}(L(A)) = \alpha_{\text{P2}}(L(B))$. |
| Multiplicity of `Check Revision` | **Abstracted** | Collapsed to a single occurrence (`Boolean = True`), matching P2 rather than Model A's $k$ occurrences. |
| Interleaving of `Analyze Revision` and `Check Revision` | **Weakened** | Concurrency `+` is restricted to one deterministic placement of `Check Revision` after the analysis loop. |
| Per-revision compliance check before `Rework Case` (P1) | **Lost** | Intentionally omitted because P2 does not track per-revision checks; therefore unsuitable for P1. |
| Optional `Approve Revision` count and placement (P1, P3) | **Lost** | Removed completely as P2 hides approvals and silent choices. |

### 4. Regression Traces and Negative Traces

**Regression traces (must be accepted; verify exact P2 preservation across $k = 1, 2, 3$):**
1. **REG-1 ($k=1$, no rework):** `['Open Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision', 'Close Case']`
   - P2 output: `('Open Case', 'Close Case', True, 1, False)`. Also belongs to $L(A) \cap L(B)$.
2. **REG-2 ($k=2$, one rework):** `['Open Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Rework Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision', 'Close Case']`

**Negative traces (must be rejected):**

3. **NEG-3 (dangling rework without follow-up analysis):** `['Open Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Rework Case', 'Check Revision', 'Close Case']` — violates loop semantics (`Rework Case` must separate two analysis attempts).
4. **NEG-4 (unabstracted approval event):** `['Open Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision', 'Approve Revision', 'Close Case']` — rejected because `Approve Revision` is intentionally removed from the P2 simplified model.

# 9. Final Recommendation

### Suitability by Business View
- **P1 (Operational compliance):** Use **Model A** exclusively. Model B is non-compliant because it permits unchecked rework iterations ($k \ge 2$) and approvals prior to checking (even at $k=1$). *(Secondary governance check: confirm with compliance owners whether Model A's local parallelism `+('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision')` is truly intended, or whether `Check Revision` should strictly follow `Analyze Revision` via `->`.)*
- **P2 (Executive dashboard):** **Model A**, **Model B**, and **`simplified_model`** all induce the exact same executive dashboard abstraction. Use **`simplified_model`** as the clean conceptual/reporting specification for the dashboard layer, while continuing to execute and audit operations against **Model A**.
- **P3 (Bounded testing):** Do **not** treat Model A and Model B as interchangeable. Under P3, they share only 4 traces (at $k=1$) and diverge immediately at length 5 (**CE-B1**) and lengths 6–7 (**CE-B2**, **CE-A1**). Test operational systems against **Model A**.

### Should `simplified_model` Replace Either Original Model?
- **Do NOT replace Model A** in workflow execution, conformance checking, or compliance auditing (P1/P3): `simplified_model` intentionally drops per-revision checking and approval visibility.
- **Do NOT use Model B** for operational compliance; if Model B currently exists only to feed a dashboard or illustrate a high-level case summary, replacing Model B's *reporting role* with **`simplified_model`** is strictly clearer (42% fewer nodes, depth 2 vs. 5, zero concurrency, zero silent transitions) while preserving P2 semantics identically.

### Warning on the Limits of Bounded Comparison
Bounded exploration (such as P3) is **asymmetric as formal evidence**:
- Finding a counterexample within the bound **proves** unbounded non-equivalence (as occurred here).
- Finding **zero** counterexamples within a bound **never proves** unbounded equivalence. Had a test harness restricted traces to $k=1$ with `Approve Revision` hidden (or relied on sparse random `play_out` sampling under low rework probability), Models A and B would have appeared indistinguishable—even though their languages are completely disjoint for every trace with two or more analysis attempts ($k \ge 2$). Always pair bounded automated checks with structural/inductive reasoning over loop scopes and invariants before certifying equivalence.
