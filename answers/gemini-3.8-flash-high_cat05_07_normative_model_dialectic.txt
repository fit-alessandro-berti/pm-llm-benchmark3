## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### Stipulated Facts (Established Beyond Dispute)
The documentary, log, and architectural evidence establishes the following facts without material dispute:
1. **Normative Baseline (M0 & CS-9)**: Compliance Standard CS-9 is formally approved and effective from 2025-01-01. It mandates: (a) `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` prior to supplier activation; (b) `Manual_Procurement_Approval` prior to `Activate_Supplier`; and (c) a strict resource segregation rule ("four-eyes" principle) requiring the requester and manual approver to be distinct human individuals. Model M0 encodes control-flow sequence as a Strict Partial Order containing concurrent checks (`Sanctions_Check`, `Quality_Review`), an exclusive decision choice between an approved branch (`Manual_Procurement_Approval` $\rightarrow$ `Activate_Supplier`) and `Reject_Supplier`, followed by `Notify_Requester` and `Close_Case`. M0 does not encode resource constraints or data attributes.
2. **Pilot Directive PD-3**: Steering-committee minutes and follow-up emails established a pilot from 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31 for LOW-risk German (DE) suppliers within approved corporate groups, permitting `Auto_Approve` and provisional activation prior to `Quality_Review` (which must occur within two business days). PD-3 was never formally incorporated into CS-9 or M0. The Compliance observer recorded that the pilot could proceed as a "monitored exception," while later stating she had not approved weakening CS-9.
3. **Emergency Directive FD-7**: In response to acute flooding, FD-7 was promulgated with an effective window of 2026-07-10 00:00 through 2026-07-12 23:59. It permitted provisional activation prior to quality review and manual procurement approval for critical flood-relief suppliers, subject to: (a) mandatory `Sanctions_Check` prior to activation; (b) an initial spend cap of EUR 20,000; and (c) compensating controls of daily transaction monitoring and retrospective quality review/approval within two business days. FD-7 contained an automatic sunset clause and expired at 2026-07-12 23:59 without written extension.
4. **CRM Notification Migration**: Effective 2026-07-01, `Notify_Requester` was migrated to an external CRM service. While ~70% of notification events are mirrored into the onboarding event log, log-level notification missingness increased from 2% to 29%. Older CRM links rely on mutable text fields rather than immutable foreign keys.
5. **Aggregate Figures**: During June and July 2026, 312 cases executed `Auto_Approve`: 289 met PD-3 criteria, 14 were clearly ineligible, and 9 lacked recorded eligibility attributes. Six cases used `Auto_Approve` in August 2026 post-expiration. Forty-seven cases executed `Request_More_Info`, none of which activated before evidence arrived.
6. **Specific Trace Realities**:
   - SUP-701 executed the complete M0 sequence with verified human segregation of duties.
   - SUP-702 met all PD-3 eligibility criteria during its active window, and its CRM notification was verified at +2 hours via a stable ID.
   - SUP-703 involved a HIGH-risk foreign supplier with an initial spend of EUR 180,000 using `Auto_Approve` via operator override.
   - SUP-704 conformed to all FD-7 conditions (EUR 18,000 spend, daily monitoring, next-day checks) during the emergency window.
   - SUP-705 executed the FD-7 sequence on 2026-07-13, after FD-7 expired, with EUR 12,000 spend and retrospective checks.
   - SUP-706 lacks an onboarding log notification; CRM shows a message at +3 hours, but the case link is a mutable text field entered two days later.
   - SUP-707 executed two cycles of `Request_More_Info` to obtain missing certificates prior to approval.
   - SUP-708 activated a HIGH-risk supplier 40 minutes prior to its `Sanctions_Check`.
   - SUP-709 was rejected immediately upon failing a sanctions pre-screen, omitting `Quality_Review`.
   - SUP-710 was a reactivation of a supplier closed six months earlier with valid quality certifications, omitting registration and quality review.
   - SUP-711 contains duplicate `Register_Supplier` entries bearing identical source transaction IDs from an ETL migration error.
   - SUP-712 matches M0 control flow, but requester and manual approver resolve to the same individual via dual aliases.
   - SUP-713 executed `QA_Clearance` instead of `Quality_Review` following a UI redesign; audit confirmed identical substantive scope, and formal mapping was approved one week later.
   - SUP-714 executed `Auto_Approve` for an eligible supplier on 2026-08-02, two days after PD-3 expired.
   - SUP-715 matches M0 control flow, but the quality reviewer and manual approver are spouses in the same reporting line with no conflict-of-interest (COI) disclosure on file.

### Disputed Propositions
- **Disputed Proposition 1 (Legal Hierarchy & Validity of PD-3)**: *Whether steering-committee minutes and email decisions constitute legally binding amendments or authorized operational exceptions to CS-9.* Compliance asserts CS-9 is the sole controlled standard and cannot be overridden informally. Operations asserts that the executive steering committee possesses inherent operational authority to authorize monitored pilot variances.
- **Disputed Proposition 2 (Ultra Vires Continuation of Emergency Powers in SUP-705)**: *Whether continuing factual disaster conditions legally justify extending emergency procedures post-expiration without a written renewal.* Operations asserts necessity and continuity of recovery; Compliance asserts that an expired directive is legally void and unilateral invocation is ultra vires.
- **Disputed Proposition 3 (Scope Boundaries of M0 for Pre-Screening and Reactivations)**: *Whether M0's boundary begins at raw intake or only upon formal qualification, and whether dormant reactivations are governed by de novo onboarding rules.* The process owner and model owner maintain irreconcilable positions; CS-9 and the scope documentation are explicitly ambiguous.
- **Disputed Proposition 4 (Formal vs. Substantive Conformance of Rebranded Labels)**: *Whether executing `QA_Clearance` prior to formal memo approval constitutes actionable noncompliance.* Compliance argues that executing unapproved activities breaches strict control; Operations argues that semantic equivalence verified by audit cures the administrative gap.

### Unproved / Indeterminate Propositions
- **Unproved Proposition 5 (Authenticity of Notification Linkage in SUP-706)**: *Whether the notification recorded in CRM was genuinely delivered to the requester as part of the case workflow.* Because the case link is a mutable text field entered two days post-hoc, backfilling or human error cannot be ruled out.
- **Unproved Proposition 6 (Fraudulent Intent in Alias Obfuscation in SUP-712)**: *Whether the dual aliases used by the requester/approver in SUP-712 were created to deliberately deceive segregation-of-duties controls or resulted from an automated IAM provisioning defect.* The objective control breach is proved; subjective mens rea is unproved.
- **Unproved Proposition 7 (Substantive Collusion in SUP-715)**: *Whether the spousal relationship between the quality reviewer and approver resulted in biased evaluation or improper procurement preference.* The failure to file a mandatory COI disclosure is proved; actual bad-faith collusion is unproved.
- **Unproved Proposition 8 (Substantive Eligibility of the 9 Ambiguous Pilot Cases)**: *Whether the 9 auto-approved cases lacking recorded attributes were factually low-risk and eligible.* Missing data leaves their compliance status indeterminate.

### Burden of Proof Framework
To resolve the conflict between formal auditability and operational reality, a dual burden-of-proof model is adopted:
1. **Burden for Declaring Noncompliance (Prosecution Burden)**:
   - Compliance/Audit carries the initial burden to establish a prima facie violation by demonstrating: (a) an applicable, binding normative standard; (b) an act or omission violating the standard's control flow, ordering, or governance rule; and (c) the absence of a contemporaneous, legally valid exception.
   - For standard operational noncompliance, the standard of proof is a *preponderance of process evidence*.
   - For allegations of willful bypass, fraud (e.g., SUP-712), or severe regulatory breaches (e.g., SUP-708), the standard of proof is *clear and convincing evidence*.
2. **Burden for Accepting an Exception (Defense Burden)**:
   - When a prima facie breach is established, the burden shifts to Operations to prove an affirmative defense of *Justified Exception* by satisfying a four-part test:
     1. *Competent Authority*: The exception emanated from a governing body or executive empowered to issue variances or emergency directives.
     2. *Strict Scope Adherence*: The case fell strictly within the temporal window, risk classification, geographic boundaries, and financial limits defined by the exception.
     3. *Execution of Compensating Controls*: All required compensating safeguards (e.g., retrospective reviews within defined SLA windows, daily transaction monitoring) were executed and documented.
     4. *Auditable Contemporaneous Trail*: Contemporaneous, verifiable evidence exists to corroborate eligibility and execution.
   - Failure to satisfy any of these four elements forfeits the exception defense.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

### The Operation is Materially Noncompliant and Facing Normalization of Deviance

Internal Audit and Compliance submit that the onboarding operation has systematically decoupled from controlled corporate policy, descending into informal practice, self-authorized authority, and dangerous control circumventions. Operational convenience has repeatedly taken precedence over internal accounting controls and statutory mandates.

#### 1. Primacy of Formal Authority and Illegality of Informal Waivers
Under corporate governance principles, Compliance Standard CS-9 is the sole controlled, formally approved standard. Operational deviations cannot be legitimized by steering-committee meeting minutes or follow-up emails. Source N2 (PD-3) was never incorporated into CS-9 or M0. The Compliance observer specifically recorded that the pilot could only proceed as a "monitored exception" and subsequently confirmed that she had never approved weakening CS-9. A steering committee lacks the unilateral legal authority to abrogate a board-approved compliance standard. Consequently, the 312 cases routed through `Auto_Approve` in June and July constitute unauthorized process departures from CS-9.

#### 2. Expiration of Authority and Unlawful Self-Extension
An exception without a valid temporal mandate is an illegal act. 
- **Flood Emergency Directive FD-7**: FD-7 was strictly limited to a 72-hour window (2026-07-10 through 2026-07-12 23:59) and contained an explicit automatic expiration clause. In **SUP-705**, Operations executed an emergency provisional activation on 2026-07-13 without a written extension, asserting that the flood emergency "still factually existed." In a governed organization, operations cannot declare states of emergency or grant itself emergency powers. Permitting operational personnel to self-certify ongoing necessity destroys corporate oversight.
- **Pilot Directive PD-3 Expiration**: PD-3 expired on 2026-07-31. Operations normalized the pilot flow and unilaterally continued auto-approving suppliers in August (**SUP-714** and 5 other cases) based on the belief that "permanent approval was imminent." This is a classic manifestation of *normalization of deviance*: informal operational habits hardening into unauthorized pseudo-policy.

#### 3. Normalization of Deviance and Control Breakdown (SUP-703)
The aggregate data proves that the pilot became a vehicle for active control circumvention. Out of 312 auto-approved cases, 14 were clearly ineligible, and 9 lacked eligibility attributes altogether. **SUP-703** exposes a critical breakdown: an operator manually selected the "pilot" flag for a **HIGH-risk new foreign supplier**, bypassing mandatory manual procurement approval and pre-activation quality review, immediately resulting in a **EUR 180,000** financial commitment. This was not a minor timing discrepancy; it was an egregious circumvention of controls on a high-exposure supplier, enabled by the complete absence of preventive system guardrails.

#### 4. Strict Liability and Severe Regulatory Exposure in Sanctions (SUP-708)
In **SUP-708**, a HIGH-risk supplier was formally activated in the procurement systems **40 minutes prior to executing the sanctions check**. 
- International sanctions regimes (OFAC, EU Sanctions Regulations) enforce strict liability. Activating a vendor creates legal privity, binds the corporation to contractual obligations, and opens system access.
- Operations defends this by arguing that "no transactions occurred before sanctions clearance" and that the check cleared 40 minutes later. This defense is legally vacuous. Relying on the commercial latency of purchasing agents to prevent a sanctions violation is reckless negligence. Had the supplier matched a designated entity list, the corporation would have faced immediate regulatory enforcement, multi-million euro statutory fines, and criminal exposure.

#### 5. Material Evasions Hidden by Model M0
Conformance to M0 is an inadequate metric for compliance. M0 models only control-flow syntax, creating a dangerous veil behind which profound governance failures hide:
- **Segregation-of-Duties Breach (SUP-712)**: The trace fits M0 perfectly. Yet forensic analysis reveals that the requester and the manual approver were the exact same human operating under two distinct system aliases. This is a direct, flagrant violation of CS-9's core four-eyes mandate. Conformance to M0 masked what appears to be identity fraud and internal control bypass.
- **Undisclosed Conflict of Interest (SUP-715)**: The quality reviewer and manual approver are spouses in the same managerial reporting line. While M0 records a compliant sequence of activities, the independence of the quality gate was fatally compromised. The mandatory corporate COI disclosure was never filed.

#### 6. Desirable Outcomes Do Not Retroactively Amend Policy
Operations points to **SUP-707** (47 cases executing `Request_More_Info` to obtain missing certificates) as proof that M0 is flawed. Compliance agrees that requesting information is commercially and substantively desirable. However, subjective desirability does not retroactively amend operational policies. Permitting ad-hoc loops without formal authorization, standard operating procedures, or defined SLA limits creates unmonitored operational latency and process drift.

#### Conclusion
The operation exhibits systemic control evasion, unauthorized continuation of expired mandates, failure of preventive controls, and exposure to catastrophic regulatory liability. The prosecution demands strict enforcement of CS-9, immediate shutdown of unauthorized auto-approvals, and disciplinary investigation of cases SUP-703, SUP-708, and SUP-712.

## 3. Defense Brief

### Model M0 is Obsolete, Incomplete, and Misapplied to Defective Data

The defense submits that Operations is being subjected to an untenable compliance audit governed by an obsolete, under-specified process model (M0), rigid bureaucratic literalism, and broken IT logging pipelines. The high nominal "nonconformance" rate does not reflect widespread operational delinquency, but rather the failure of Compliance to maintain models that reflect lawful executive directives, basic business realities, and modern system architectures.

#### 1. Architectural Obsolescence and Flaws in M0 Control Flow
Model M0 is mathematically and architecturally defective as a representation of compliant business logic:
- **The Early Rejection Absurdity (SUP-709)**: In M0, the `checks` construct forces both `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` to complete before the `decision` operator can fire. When a prospective supplier fails a sanctions pre-screen, M0 dictates that the organization must still conduct an expensive, time-consuming `Quality_Review` before rejecting the supplier! In **SUP-709**, Operations sensibly rejected the lead immediately upon sanctions failure. M0 flags this exemplary gatekeeping as noncompliant. A model that mandates wasting engineering and auditing resources on a legally dead lead is structurally defective.
- **Punishing Due Diligence (SUP-707)**: M0 contains no transition or loop for `Request_More_Info`. In **SUP-707** and 47 aggregate cases, operations encountered incomplete documentation and initiated clarification cycles to obtain missing quality certificates. In every single case, activation was halted until full evidence was secured. This is textbook operational excellence that protected the enterprise. Yet under M0, this rigorous due diligence is scored as a process deviation. M0 treats thoroughness as an offense.

#### 2. Lawful Authority of Executive and Emergency Overlays
Compliance dogmatically treats CS-9 as an immutable monolith, ignoring corporate reality:
- **Executive Pilot Authority (PD-3 / SUP-702)**: The Steering Committee—comprising senior executive leadership—formally authorized PD-3 to evaluate automation for low-risk domestic suppliers. The Compliance observer participated, recorded no formal veto, and confirmed the pilot could proceed as a monitored exception. In **SUP-702**, the supplier was verified low-risk, domestic, and part of an approved group. `Quality_Review` was executed on the next business day, and notification was sent. To brand operational staff noncompliant for executing a formal executive steering-committee mandate is an abdication of organizational governance.
- **Emergency Necessity and Disaster Mitigation (FD-7 / SUP-704 & SUP-705)**: Severe flooding threatened complete manufacturing shutdown. Executive Directive FD-7 authorized provisional fast-tracking for critical relief suppliers. In **SUP-704**, Operations onboarded a pump supplier under the EUR 20,000 cap (EUR 18,000), performed daily monitoring, and finalized retrospective approvals the next day, averting catastrophic plant flooding. In **SUP-705**, the flood emergency factually persisted into the following morning. Operations procured critical items under cap (EUR 12,000) and completed retrospective checks. Condemning teams who saved company infrastructure under force majeure conditions elevates bureaucratic form over the fiduciary survival of the enterprise.

#### 3. Data Infrastructure, Migration Artifacts, and Synthetic Deviations
A vast portion of the alleged noncompliances are synthetic illusions generated by IT data-pipeline failures:
- **CRM Webhook Synchronization Failures (SUP-702, SUP-706)**: On 2026-07-01, IT migrated notifications to an external CRM service without building an automated, robust event bridge. The 29% missingness rate in the onboarding log is an IT logging omission, not an operational failure. In **SUP-702**, CRM logs definitively prove the notification was transmitted at +2 hours with a stable ID. In **SUP-706**, the communication was dispatched at +3 hours. The fact that the CRM correlation link was entered via a mutable text field reflects technical debt in IT systems, not operational bad faith.
- **ETL Database Ingestion Duplication (SUP-711)**: SUP-711 contains two `Register_Supplier` entries. Forensic examination proves that both rows share the exact same source transaction ID. An ETL ingestion batch script duplicated the record during data warehouse migration. No human registered the supplier twice. To cite SUP-711 as an operational process failure is unscientific and absurd.
- **Semantic Rebranding vs. Substantive Compliance (SUP-713)**: The user interface was updated to label `Quality_Review` as `QA_Clearance`. The operational review was completed in full, verified by internal audit, and the formal dictionary mapping was signed off one week later. The trace deviation in SUP-713 is pure semantic lag.

#### 4. Scope Deficiencies: Lifecycle Confusion and Reactivations (SUP-710)
CS-9 and M0 are scoped exclusively to *de novo* supplier onboarding. In **SUP-710**, Operations reactivated a dormant supplier whose full quality and technical certifications had been audited six months prior and remained fully valid. Re-running registration and technical audits would have introduced pointless operational delay. Operations conducted a fresh `Sanctions_Check` and obtained `Manual_Procurement_Approval`. The lack of a defined reactivation lifecycle in M0 cannot be converted into a fault of the operational team.

#### 5. Separating Administrative Variance from Genuine Misconduct
The defense explicitly acknowledges that **SUP-703** (ineligible foreign auto-approval), **SUP-708** (sanctions check lagging activation), and **SUP-712** (alias circumvention of segregation of duties) represent genuine, serious control failures requiring corrective action. 
However, by lumping legitimate pilot executions (SUP-702), disaster mitigation (SUP-704), thorough due diligence (SUP-707), IT ETL bugs (SUP-711), and UI label changes (SUP-713) into the same "noncompliant" bucket, Compliance destroys audit credibility and paralyzes the business.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

A modern, robust normative architecture cannot rely on a single flat control-flow model. It requires a **multi-tiered, defense-in-depth architecture** that partitions constraints across appropriate technical layers.

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LAYER 4: ORGANIZATIONAL & GOVERNANCE CONTROLS                                    |
| - IAM Master Identity Resolution (maps aliases to single verified person_id)      |
| - Conflict-of-Interest Attestations & Disclosures (blocking related approvers)   |
| - Policy Overlay Registry (lifecycle states, formal authorizers, sunset clauses)  |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LAYER 3: DATA-AWARE INVARIANTS & INTEGRATION (Pandas / SQL / Event Pipeline)     |
| - Hard Pre-condition: Sanctions_Check completed BEFORE Activate_Supplier (Strict)|
| - Eligibility Guards: Auto_Approve requires risk=='LOW' & country=='DE' & group  |
| - Spend Caps: Emergency activation requires initial_spend <= EUR 20,000          |
| - Temporal SLAs: Retrospective Quality_Review within 2 business days of activation|
| - ETL Deduplication & UUID-based CRM Webhook Event Mirroring                     |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LAYER 2: DECLARE / LOG SKELETON CONSTRAINTS                                       |
| - Non-Coexistence: Reject_Supplier and Activate_Supplier                         |
| - Precedence: Sanctions_Check strictly precedes Activate_Supplier                |
| - Segregation of Duties: actor(Register_Supplier) != actor(Manual_Approval)      |
| - Succession: Decision strictly leads to Notify_Requester and Close_Case          |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LAYER 1: REVISED CONTROL-FLOW BACKBONE (POWL)                                    |
| - Multi-path routing: Standard, Auto-Approve, Emergency, Early-Reject, Reactivate|
| - Structured, repeatable Request_More_Info loop                                   |
| - Concurrency of standard checks; post-activation sequencing for fast-tracks       |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Detailed Layer Specifications

#### 1. Layer 1: Revised Control-Flow Model (POWL)
- Encodes admissible execution topologies.
- Replaces rigid sequentiality with explicit multi-track routing:
  - *Track A (Standard Onboarding)*: Concurrent `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` (with embedded `Request_More_Info` loop), followed by XOR choice between (`Manual_Procurement_Approval` $\rightarrow$ `Activate_Supplier`) and `Reject_Supplier`.
  - *Track B (Low-Risk Fast-Track / Auto-Approve)*: `Sanctions_Check` $\rightarrow$ `Auto_Approve` $\rightarrow$ `Activate_Supplier` $\rightarrow$ `Quality_Review`.
  - *Track C (Emergency Relief Fast-Track)*: `Sanctions_Check` $\rightarrow$ `Activate_Supplier` $\rightarrow$ concurrent retrospective `Quality_Review` and `Manual_Procurement_Approval`.
  - *Track D (Early Intake Rejection)*: `Sanctions_Check` $\rightarrow$ `Reject_Supplier` (bypassing `Quality_Review`).
  - *Track E (Reactivation Lifecycle)*: Bypasses registration and quality review; executes `Sanctions_Check` $\rightarrow$ `Manual_Procurement_Approval` $\rightarrow$ `Activate_Supplier`.
- All tracks converge into mandatory `Notify_Requester` $\rightarrow$ `Close_Case`.

#### 2. Layer 2: Declarative / Log-Skeleton Temporal Constraints
Certain invariants must hold across all tracks and are best verified via DECLARE/linear temporal logic:
- `Precedence(Sanctions_Check, Activate_Supplier)`: Cardinal statutory rule across all possible paths.
- `NotCoExistence(Activate_Supplier, Reject_Supplier)`: Mutual exclusivity of final lifecycle states.
- `Response(Activate_Supplier, Close_Case)`: Every activated supplier must reach closure.

#### 3. Layer 3: Data-Aware Constraints & Business Rules (Pandas / SQL Engine)
Enforces runtime data guards that pure control flow cannot express:
- **Pilot Eligibility Guard**: If transition is `Auto_Approve`, assert `risk_level == 'LOW' AND country == 'DE' AND corporate_group_approved == True`. If false, trigger immediate hard block.
- **Spend Cap Guard**: If route is `Emergency`, assert `initial_commitment <= 20000.00`.
- **Temporal Retrospective SLA**: If route is `Auto_Approve` or `Emergency`, assert `timestamp(Quality_Review) - timestamp(Activate_Supplier) <= 48h` (business days).
- **Temporal Directive Validity**: If case uses an overlay route, assert `case_start_time >= overlay.valid_from AND case_start_time <= overlay.valid_to`.

#### 4. Layer 4: Organizational, Resource, and IAM Controls
- **Enterprise IAM Identity Resolution**: All event actors are resolved from application-specific usernames/aliases (`samAccountName`, email aliases) to a single canonical `person_id`.
- **Automated Segregation of Duties (4-Eyes)**: Hard assertion: `actor_person_id(Register_Supplier) != actor_person_id(Manual_Procurement_Approval)`. System prevents the same `person_id` from claiming the approval task.
- **Conflict of Interest Attestation Matrix**: Approvers and reviewers must maintain an active annual COI declaration. If `managerial_line(reviewer) == managerial_line(approver)` or familial relation is recorded, the task is routed to an independent ombudsman.

#### 5. Layer 5: Policy-Overlay Governance Engine
Governs the lifecycle of exceptions:
- **Registry Attributes**: Every overlay must be registered in a central governance table with: `overlay_id`, `version`, `authorizing_body`, `valid_from`, `valid_to`, `scope_predicate`, `compensating_controls`.
- **Hard Runtime Sunset**: At `valid_to` (e.g., 2026-07-12 23:59), the workflow engine automatically locks the execution branch. Operational staff cannot select the route without an updated cryptographic signature from the governance authority.
- **Audit Traceability**: Every event generated under an overlay must record `applied_overlay_id` and `applied_overlay_version`.

#### 6. Layer 6: Explicit Lifecycle Scope Rules
- **Intake / Lead Pre-Screen**: Defined as a separate pre-onboarding lifecycle. Disqualifications at intake produce an `Intake_Lead_Rejected` event and do not instantiate an M0 onboarding case.
- **Reactivation**: Suppliers dormant $< 12$ months with unchanged manufacturing/tax details execute Track E. Suppliers dormant $> 12$ months or with altered product categories must undergo de novo onboarding.
- **Withdrawals**: Requester may execute `Withdraw_Application` at any point prior to activation, immediately routing the case to `Notify_Requester` $\rightarrow$ `Close_Case`.

### Responsibility and Enforcement Allocation Matrix

| Rule / Requirement | Primary Mechanism | Enforcement Point | Failure Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

### Executable Python POWL Construction

The following Python script defines and validates the revised normative POWL model using PM4Py classes. It encapsulates:
1. Standard onboarding with concurrent sanctions and quality checks;
2. Repeatable `Request_More_Info` loop;
3. Standard manual approval;
4. Low-risk auto-approval;
5. Emergency provisional activation with retrospective checks;
6. Early intake sanctions rejection;
7. Dormant supplier reactivation; and
8. Mandatory notification before case closure.

```python
"""
Revised Normative Onboarding POWL Model
Constructed using PM4Py POWL objects.
Represents multi-route onboarding, structured loops, and lifecycle variants.
"""

import pm4py
import pandas as pd
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def build_revised_normative_powl() -> StrictPartialOrder:
    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    # 1. Terminal / Common Transitions
    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
    close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

    # Helper function to generate a structured Request_More_Info loop:
    # Operator.LOOP(*(tau, Request_More_Info)) represents 0, 1, or more iterations.
    def create_rmi_loop():
        return OperatorPOWL(
            operator=Operator.LOOP,
            children=[SilentTransition(), Transition(label="Request_More_Info")]
        )

    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    # Route 1: Standard Onboarding (CS-9 Baseline)
    # Concurrent Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review with optional RMI loop,
    # followed by an XOR decision between Manual Approval and Rejection.
    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    sanc_std = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    qual_std = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
    rmi_std = create_rmi_loop()
    checks_std = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanc_std, qual_std, rmi_std])
    # sanc_std, qual_std, and rmi_std are concurrent (no edges between them)

    manual_app_std = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
    activate_std = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
    approved_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[manual_app_std, activate_std])
    approved_branch.order.add_edge(manual_app_std, activate_std)

    reject_std = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
    decision_std = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[approved_branch, reject_std]
    )

    standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[checks_std, decision_std])
    standard_route.order.add_edge(checks_std, decision_std)

    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    # Route 2: Low-Risk Fast-Track / Auto-Approval (PD-3 Pilot / Regularized)
    # Sanctions_Check (with optional RMI) -> Auto_Approve -> Activate_Supplier -> Quality_Review
    # Quality_Review occurs post-activation (provisional activation within 2-day SLA)
    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    sanc_auto = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    rmi_auto = create_rmi_loop()
    auto_app = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
    activate_auto = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
    qual_auto = Transition(label="Quality_Review")

    auto_route = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[sanc_auto, rmi_auto, auto_app, activate_auto, qual_auto]
    )
    auto_route.order.add_edge(sanc_auto, auto_app)
    auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_app, activate_auto)
    auto_route.order.add_edge(activate_auto, qual_auto)

    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    # Route 3: Emergency Relief Fast-Track (FD-7 Directive)
    # Sanctions_Check -> Activate_Supplier -> Retrospective Checks
    # Retrospective checks comprise Quality_Review and Manual_Procurement_Approval
    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    sanc_emg = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    activate_emg = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
    qual_emg = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
    manual_app_emg = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")

    retrospective_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[qual_emg, manual_app_emg])
    # Retrospective checks can execute concurrently or in any order

    emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[sanc_emg, activate_emg, retrospective_checks]
    )
    emergency_route.order.add_edge(sanc_emg, activate_emg)
    emergency_route.order.add_edge(activate_emg, retrospective_checks)

    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    # Route 4: Early Intake Sanctions Rejection (Pre-Screen Exit)
    # Sanctions_Check -> Reject_Supplier (bypassing Quality_Review)
    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    sanc_early = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    reject_early = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
    early_reject_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanc_early, reject_early])
    early_reject_route.order.add_edge(sanc_early, reject_early)

    # Combine the four core processing tracks for de novo onboardings
    de_novo_core = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[
            standard_route,
            auto_route,
            emergency_route,
            early_reject_route
        ]
    )

    # De novo onboardings must begin with Register_Supplier
    register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
    de_novo_workflow = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, de_novo_core])
    de_novo_workflow.order.add_edge(register, de_novo_core)

    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    # Route 5: Dormant Supplier Reactivation Lifecycle
    # Bypasses Register_Supplier and Quality_Review (prior audit still valid);
    # Requires fresh Sanctions_Check -> Manual_Procurement_Approval -> Activate_Supplier
    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    sanc_react = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    manual_react = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
    activate_react = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")

    reactivation_route = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[sanc_react, manual_react, activate_react]
    )
    reactivation_route.order.add_edge(sanc_react, manual_react)
    reactivation_route.order.add_edge(manual_react, activate_react)

    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    # Top-Level Composition: Lifecycle Choice -> Notify_Requester -> Close_Case
    # -------------------------------------------------------------
    lifecycle_choice = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[de_novo_workflow, reactivation_route]
    )

    revised_powl = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[lifecycle_choice, notify, close])
    revised_powl.order.add_edge(lifecycle_choice, notify)
    revised_powl.order.add_edge(notify, close)

    return revised_powl


# -----------------------------------------------------------------
# Self-Verification Script
# Demonstrates conversion and conformance across operational traces.
# -----------------------------------------------------------------
if __name__ == "__main__":
    revised_model = build_revised_normative_powl()
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(revised_model)
    print(f"POWL successfully compiled to Petri Net.")
    print(f"Places: {len(net.places)}, Transitions: {len(net.transitions)}")

    # Validation suite across key benchmark scenarios
    test_traces = {
        "SUP-701 (Standard Manual Approval)": [
            "Register_Supplier", "Sanctions_Check", "Quality_Review",

        ],
        "SUP-702 (Low-Risk Auto-Approve + CRM Notify)": [
            "Register_Supplier", "Sanctions_Check", "Auto_Approve",
            "Activate_Supplier", "Quality_Review",
            "Notify_Requester", "Close_Case"
        ],
        "SUP-704 (Emergency Relief Fast-Track)": [
            "Register_Supplier", "Sanctions_Check", "Activate_Supplier",
            "Quality_Review", "Manual_Procurement_Approval",
            "Notify_Requester", "Close_Case"
        ],
        "SUP-707 (Diligent RMI Loop x2)": [
            "Register_Supplier", "Sanctions_Check", "Request_More_Info",
            "Request_More_Info", "Quality_Review",
            "Manual_Procurement_Approval", "Activate_Supplier",

        ],
        "SUP-709 (Early Sanctions Pre-Screen Rejection)": [
```

### Expressiveness Limits of Pure POWL

While POWL provides expressive control-flow modeling (supporting strict partial orders and structured loops), it possesses strict theoretical limitations:
1. **Attribute and Data Blindness**: POWL cannot verify data guards. In Route 2, POWL verifies that `Auto_Approve` occurred after `Sanctions_Check`, but it cannot verify whether the vendor was `risk_level == 'LOW'` or `country == 'DE'` (SUP-703 vs. SUP-702).
2. **Financial Cap Enforcement**: POWL cannot inspect payload values such as `initial_spend <= 20000` (SUP-704 vs. SUP-703).
3. **Temporal Validity and Expiry**: POWL cannot verify whether a case occurred before or after a calendar sunset date (e.g., verifying that FD-7 was executed prior to 2026-07-12 23:59 or PD-3 before 2026-07-31).
4. **Resource and Identity Binding**: POWL cannot enforce that `actor(Register_Supplier) != actor(Manual_Procurement_Approval)` (SUP-712).
5. **Organizational Conflict of Interest**: POWL cannot model marital or reporting relationships between actors (SUP-715).

To bridge these limitations, the POWL control-flow model must be coupled with the Normalized Policy-Overlay Table and Data-Collection Specification detailed below.

   - When a notification is dispatched, the onboarding engine generates an immutable `notification_uuid` (UUIDv4) and injects it into the CRM API payload.
   - The CRM webhook callback must include `notification_uuid` and `case_id` in its payload.
   - The event pipeline joins on `notification_uuid` rather than mutable text strings, eliminating the 29% missingness and data-quality indeterminacy seen in SUP-702 and SUP-706.
2. **IAM Canonical Person ID Resolution (Eliminating Alias Evasion / SUP-712)**:
   - An enterprise directory service (LDAP/SCIM) syncs an authoritative mapping between corporate usernames, legacy aliases, and a unique `canonical_person_id`.
   - Before an approver can claim a `Manual_Procurement_Approval` task, the workflow engine asserts:
     $$\text{canonical\_person\_id}(\text{Manual\_Procurement\_Approval}) \ne \text{canonical\_person\_id}(\text{Register\_Supplier})$$
   - Any attempt to approve a case registered by the same individual, regardless of alias used, is rejected with a security alert.
3. **Automated Sanctions Pre-Activation Lock (Eliminating SUP-708)**:
   - The ERP database schema enforces a foreign-key and check constraint: `vendor_activation_status` cannot be toggled to `ACTIVE` unless `sanctions_clearance_timestamp` is populated and strictly precedes current database time.
   - The user interface omits the "Activate" button until the asynchronous sanctions screening service returns an authenticated clearance token.
4. **Automated Eligibility Enforcement (Eliminating SUP-703)**:
   - The "Auto-Approve" workflow option is rendered programmatically by the rule engine. If `vendor_risk_tier != 'LOW'` or `vendor_country != 'DE'`, the UI element is disabled. Manual override capability is removed from operational roles and restricted to C-level crisis personnel.
5. **Deduplication and Idempotency in Data Pipelines (Eliminating SUP-711)**:
   - Event streaming pipelines (e.g., Kafka / Flink / dbt) enforce an idempotency key defined as:
     $$\text{hash}(\text{case\_id}, \text{activity\_name}, \text{source\_transaction\_id})$$
   - Duplicate database rows emitted during system migrations or network retries are deduplicated at ingestion, preventing synthetic process deviations from reaching audit event logs.
6. **Bitemporal Event Tracking (Eliminating Retrospective Backfilling)**:
   - Every log row captures both `event_timestamp` (valid business time) and `record_timestamp` (transaction system insertion time).
   - Any log entry where $\text{record\_timestamp} - \text{event\_timestamp} > 24\text{ hours}$ is automatically flagged for forensic backfill review.
