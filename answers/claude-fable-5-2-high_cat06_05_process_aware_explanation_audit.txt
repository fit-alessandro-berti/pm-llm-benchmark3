## 1. Leakage and temporal-consistency findings

Framing facts used throughout the audit:

- At `t0` every case shown has the prefix `Claim Registered -> Coverage Check Completed`. Under the normative model, the number of document requests, appeals, settlement decisions and closures in that prefix is zero. Any feature or pattern that refers to those activities and varies across cases is computed from the future.
- The reported local attributions add up exactly to the predicted probabilities (X: 0.82, Y: 0.71, Z: 0.64). Inadmissible items account for +0.32 of X, +0.31 of Y (plus +0.05 from a defective feature), and essentially every named driver of Z except `calendar_week`. A corrected score cannot be obtained by subtracting these amounts: the base value and the weights of the remaining features were learned in the presence of the leaked features. The model must be retrained and the explanations recomputed.
- The label needs a precise definition: is the 10-day target measured to the first `Settlement Decision Recorded` or to the final one, and from which start event? If it is the first decision, appeal-related information is post-outcome. If it is the final decision, appeal is mechanically part of the label. It is inadmissible at `t0` either way.

## 2. Process-model consistency checks

The suite has six groups of tests. Each runs in CI on the feature pipeline and on a sample of scored cases, and fails closed: a feature or explanation item that cannot be proven admissible is treated as inadmissible.

**A. Prefix-time availability**

- A1, timestamp contract: every feature value carries an event time and a knowledge time (when the system first held it). Assert both are at or before `t0` of the case. Features without timestamps (`final_handler_team`, `risk_score_current`) fail automatically.
- A2, truncation invariance: rebuild all features from a log truncated at `t0`, keeping only events with a sequence position at or before the first `Coverage Check Completed`. Any difference from the stored feature vector is a leak. This catches `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `last_resource_role` and all five patterns.
- A3, suffix perturbation: randomly replace or shuffle the post-`t0` suffix of each trace. The features, the prediction and the explanation must be unchanged.
- A4, as-of joins: require a backward-only join for queue snapshots (snapshot time at or before `t0`, age within 5 minutes) and for nightly snapshots (use the last refresh before `t0`). For cross-case features, require `claim_created_at < t0`, and resource attributes as of the event date.
- A5, backtest realism: use out-of-time splits. Labels used to train the model applied at `t0` must come from cases whose outcome was known before `t0`.
- A6, label-proximity screen: flag any single feature with near-perfect univariate discrimination, or any feature that is a deterministic function of the end timestamp.

**B. Activity-order feasibility**

- Encode the normative process as a reference model (Petri net or process tree) plus declarative constraints:
  - `init(Claim Registered)`.
  - `precedence(Coverage Check Completed, Damage Assessment Completed)`.
  - `Documents Requested -> Documents Received -> Documents Validated` as alternating cycles.
  - `precedence(Settlement Decision Recorded, Appeal)` with the data condition that the outcome is Partial or Deny.
  - `precedence(final decision or withdrawal, Claim Closed)`.
  - Nothing except administrative events after `Claim Closed`.
  - Fraud investigation concurrent with evidence collection and damage assessment.
- Footprint check: for every pattern `a -> b` used by the explainer, look up the relation in the normative footprint (directly-follows allowed, only eventually-follows, concurrent, forbidden). `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` is forbidden. The other four patterns are allowed.
- Alignments on complete traces measure where and how often the log deviates and quantify data-quality problems. They are not a test of feature availability: a perfectly fitting trace can still be leaked.
- Limitations:
  - Optimal alignments are not unique and depend on the cost function.
  - Control-flow alignments ignore data conditions such as "Partial or Deny" unless a data-aware variant is used.
  - The normative model is simplified, so a deviation may be real behaviour and should not be deleted silently.
  - A DFG is a profiling tool only. A frequent edge is not a normative rule (systematic batch logging can make an impossible edge frequent), and a rare edge is not automatically invalid.
  - DFGs cannot separate concurrency from sequence, and the parallel fraud investigation creates many interleaving edges. They also cannot express long-distance rules such as appeal only after Partial or Deny.
  - Declarative constraints handle concurrency well but can be vacuously satisfied, and they under-specify behaviour that is not explicitly constrained.

**C. Same-timestamp ambiguity**

- C1: detect groups of events in a case with identical timestamps. Report the timestamp granularity (date-only or midnight values) and cross-case batch signatures. Flag a missing sequence index.
- C2: represent tied events as a partially ordered trace. A directly-follows feature is certain only if it holds in every linearization. If at least one linearization conforms to the model, classify the case as order-ambiguous with a conformant ordering, not as a deviation. If none conforms, classify it as a true deviation.
- C3: do not generate ordered pattern features from tied pairs. Use an unordered co-occurrence token or drop the feature. Record any tie-break rule explicitly. Silently imposing the normative order would hide real deviations.
- C4, ties with `t0`: an event with the same timestamp as the first `Coverage Check Completed` is included in the prefix only if a reliable sequence index places it at or before the `t0` event. Otherwise it is excluded.
- C5, sensitivity: recompute features and attributions under all permutations of tied events. An attribution that changes sign or rank is marked unstable and is not reported.

**D. Complete-trace information**

- D1, static lineage lint: scan SQL and feature code for case-level aggregations without an `event_time <= t0` predicate, joins on `extraction_date`, fields populated at closure, and suspicious names (`total`, `final`, `last`, `anywhere`, `full`, `current`).
- D2, dynamic check: A2 and A3 above.
- D3, process-implied constants: the model implies that counts of document requests, decisions, appeals and closures are zero at `t0`. Non-zero variance in such a feature means either leakage or a non-conformant prefix, and both must be explained.
- D4, multi-prefix comparison: compute each feature at `t0` and at case end. A feature that should evolve along the trace but always equals the end-of-case value is a full-trace feature.

**E. Enabled, impossible, or merely future-possible at `t0`**

Replay the prefix on the reference model (a prefix alignment if the prefix deviates) to obtain the state at `t0`. In parallel, track the monitoring state of each declarative constraint (temporarily or permanently satisfied or violated). Classify every activity or pattern mentioned in an explanation:

| Explanation item | Status at `t0` | How it may be used |
|---|---|---|
| `Claim Registered`, `Coverage Check Completed` | Occurred | As observed facts |
| `Documents Requested`, `Damage Assessment Completed` (fast track) | Enabled, not yet occurred | Only as a forecast from a prefix-only model, never as fact |
| `Fraud Investigation Opened` | Enabled only if a trigger exists at `t0`; otherwise future-possible | Report the trigger if it is time-stamped at or before `t0`; never the opening itself |
| `Documents Received`, `Documents Validated`, `Settlement Decision Recorded`, `Claim Closed` | Future-possible, not enabled | Not admissible as features; at most as predicted intermediate outcomes |
| `Appeal`, `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` | Future-possible, conditional on a later Partial or Deny decision | Not admissible at `t0`; legitimate behaviour in retrospective analysis |
| `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` | Future and forbidden by the model | Not admissible; route to data-quality investigation |

Limitation: prefix replay shows what the model permits, not what is likely. Classifying an activity as enabled does not justify presenting it as a driver.

**F. Repeated `Settlement Decision Recorded` events**

- A second or later decision is legitimate only if all of the following hold:
  - An `Appeal` lies strictly between it and the previous decision.
  - The previous decision was Partial or Deny.
  - The number of decisions is at most the number of appeals plus one.
  - No appeal follows an Approve or Withdrawn outcome.
  - `Claim Closed` follows the last decision.
- Otherwise classify the repeat as duplicate logging, a correction, or a tie artifact (apply group C).
- Refine the coarse label into `Settlement Decision (initial)` and `Settlement Decision (post-appeal)` with the outcome as an attribute. A naive "activity occurs at most once" rule would then not flag legitimate reconsideration, and explainers would not merge the two.
- Bind the label to one specific decision event and add a test that the label computation uses that event.
- Apply the same occurrence logic to `t0`: it is anchored on the first `Coverage Check Completed`, and repeated coverage checks must not shift it.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

Four standards apply, and passing one does not imply passing the others:

- **Predictive validity:** the value is available at `t0` in both event time and knowledge time, is computed identically in training and serving, and improves out-of-time discrimination and calibration.
- **Causal interpretation:** requires an identification argument (temporal precedence, confounder control, an experiment or credible quasi-experiment). Attribution scores describe the model's output, not the process.
- **Operational actionability:** the factor can be changed by the operation after `t0`, the effect of changing it has been estimated, and acting on it does not invalidate the model through feedback.
- **Fairness review:** asks whether using the feature, or its proxies, produces unjustified differences in treatment or error rates. It applies even to fully valid predictors and depends on what decision the prediction triggers.

**Defensible predictive signal**

- Features: `initial_damage_complexity`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, `initial_reserve` (once its as-of-`t0` lineage is confirmed), `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (after the SQL repair).
- May be claimed:
  - Claims with these characteristics at `t0` have historically breached more or less often, and the model relies on that regularity.
  - For elapsed time, that less of the time budget remains, if the clock starts at registration.
- May not be claimed:
  - That complexity, reserve size or claim history cause delay.
  - That changing the recorded value would change the outcome.
  - That "prior history indicates complexity", which is an untested story.

**Process-state or congestion signal**

- Features: `queue_length_at_t0`; after repair, `assigned_team_at_t0`, the role and as-of-date experience of the resource at `t0`, the prefix variant, and time-in-state.
- May be claimed:
  - The claim entered a specific operational state at `t0`, for example a queue of 18 items.
  - That state has historically been associated with higher or lower breach rates.
  - Congestion is a plausible mechanism.
- May not be claimed:
  - That reducing the queue by a given amount lowers this claim's risk by the attributed amount.
  - That a team "determines" delay.
- Queue length co-varies with season, case mix and staffing. It is the best candidate for action, but only after a queueing simulation, natural experiment or controlled prioritization trial.

**Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review**

- Features: `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, and to a lesser degree `initial_reserve` and prior claim history.
- May be claimed: the model uses a segment-level association that is predictive at `t0`.
- May not be claimed:
  - That region or channel drives delay.
  - That claimants in a segment are inherently slower.
  - That the feature justifies differential treatment.
- Review steps:
  - Test whether mechanism variables (repair-network capacity, language support, document completeness by channel) absorb the effect.
  - Compare calibration and error rates across segments.
  - Assess whether the downstream action is assistive (extra attention) or adverse.
- Deleting the feature is not a remedy by itself. Correlated features can reconstruct it, and the attribute is still needed for auditing.

**Drift/calendar proxy**

- Features: `calendar_week`.
- May be claimed: claims reaching `t0` in certain periods had higher breach rates, and these periods coincide with a holiday period and an operating-policy change.
- May not be claimed:
  - That the week itself is "operationally worse".
  - That the pattern will recur. The holiday effect is recurring seasonality; the policy change is a one-off regime shift.
  - That the effect is separable from congestion without further analysis.
- Prefer explicit calendar and regime features, and validate out of time across the policy change.

**Unusable until lineage is repaired**

- Features:
  - `final_handler_team`, to become `assigned_team_at_t0`.
  - `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, with the corrected filter.
  - `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, to become the count as of `t0`.
  - `risk_score_current`, to become the score as of `t0`.
  - `last_resource_role`, to become the role of the last prefix event.
  - Employee experience, joined as of event date.
- May be claimed now: nothing, including the direction of effect. Current attributions mix legitimate and future information.
- After repair they move into one of the classes above and inherit its limits.

**Not repairable at `t0`**

- Features: `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`, and the five sequence patterns.
- Legitimate uses:
  - Label construction.
  - Auxiliary prediction targets.
  - Prefix-count features at later prediction points.
  - Retrospective root-cause analysis, clearly marked as hindsight.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

Ordered by priority; earlier items are preconditions for later ones.

1. **Immediate containment.**
   - Withdraw the current global ranking, sequence-pattern report and local explanations as early-warning explanations.
   - Label them as hindsight analysis of a full-trace model.
   - Stop operational use of the scores.
   - Fix the label definition (which decision event, which clock start, calendar or business days).

2. **Feature-store design.**
   - Make point-in-time correctness a structural property. Every feature is keyed by case and as-of time, and served only through an as-of join at `t0`.
   - Registration requires a declared source, event time, knowledge time, mutability flag and owner.
   - Fields populated at closure, joined at extraction date, or named `total`, `final`, `last`, `current`, `anywhere` or `full` are rejected by default.
   - Training and serving use the same feature code.

3. **Historical feature timestamps.**
   - Introduce versioned history for mutable attributes: handler and team assignment, risk score, reserve, document counts, employee experience.
   - Store a reliable event sequence index.
   - Backfill where source systems allow. Where they do not (for example `final_handler_team`), exclude the feature for historical periods and start capturing it now.
   - Replace the nightly document snapshot with event-level upload timestamps.

4. **Prefix-only recomputation and retraining.**
   - Rebuild the training set from logs truncated at the first `Coverage Check Completed`.
   - Repair the cross-case SQL to `claim_created_at < t0`.
   - Retrain with out-of-time splits that straddle the policy change.
   - Expect a clear drop in apparent performance and report it. With a two-event prefix, the signal is limited to intake attributes, elapsed time and congestion.
   - Consider additional prediction points later in the process (after the first document request, after damage assessment), where prefix counts and patterns become legitimate. Each needs its own prefix-only model or bucket.

5. **Process-conformance tests in CI.**
   - Automate the Section 2 suite: truncation invariance, suffix perturbation, footprint and declarative checks on every pattern feature, tie detection with partial-order handling, enabled/future-possible/impossible classification of every explanation item, and the appeal-conditioned rule for repeated decisions.
   - A failing explanation item blocks release.
   - Deviating traces are logged for data-quality triage, not dropped silently.
   - The normative model is versioned and reviewed with process owners. Discovered DFGs are used for profiling only.

6. **Calibration after feature removal.**
   - Re-evaluate discrimination and calibration (reliability curves, Brier score, calibration error) on an out-of-time holdout, overall and by region, channel, complexity and period.
   - Recalibrate if needed and re-derive alert thresholds and expected workload.
   - Communicate predictive uncertainty, especially for cases like Y where the admissible evidence is thin.

7. **Explanation stability.**
   - Use a background set of `t0` prefixes from earlier periods.
   - Group correlated features (region with channel; queue with calendar) or use conditional attributions.
   - Report bootstrap intervals and rank stability across seeds, retrains and time folds.
   - Verify attributions against ablation and permutation tests, and run tie-permutation sensitivity for sequence features.
   - Suppress or flag attributions whose sign is unstable.
   - Use a standard template that separates observed prefix facts, model associations and unverified hypotheses.

8. **Proxy and fairness analysis.**
   - For region, channel and other proxies, test replacement with mechanism variables.
   - Measure residual dependence, and compare error rates and calibration across groups.
   - Assess the downstream decision that the prediction triggers.
   - Record the outcome explicitly: retain, constrain, replace, or use for audit only.
   - Keep sensitive attributes available for monitoring even if they are excluded from the model.
   - Causal or actionable statements about congestion, team or routing need separate evidence from experiments, quasi-experiments or simulation.

9. **Monitoring for schema and process drift.**
   - Schema: monitor field semantics, refresh schedules, null rates and snapshot-join ages.
   - Process: monitor new activities or variants, conformance rates, tie rates, sequence-index completeness and the share of repeated decisions without appeal.
   - Policy and calendar: monitor regime changes and seasonality.
   - Model: monitor feature distributions, calibration, and attribution distributions per feature.
   - Define triggers for re-audit and retraining.
   - Re-run the leakage suite whenever a source system, a join or the normative model changes.
   - Record interventions triggered by predictions, so that feedback effects on labels and on the queue can be separated from drift.
