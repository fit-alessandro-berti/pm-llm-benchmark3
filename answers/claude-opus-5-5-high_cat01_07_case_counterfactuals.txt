## 1. Feasibility model

```text
# Partial-order relation set
# Nodes: Reg, Tri, IC (Identity Check), OR (Originals Receipt), DC_k (k-th Document Check),
#        A_k (k-th Assessment), RMI_k, EA (Escalation Advice), PCS (preliminary compliance screen),
#        CR (final Compliance Review), Dec, Not
# "x < y" means complete(x) <= start(y). Pairs not listed are unordered and may overlap
# provided they use different resources.

Generic relations
  Reg < Tri
  Tri < IC
  Tri < DC_1
  Reg < OR                     [originals_required; external arrival, independent of Tri/IC]
  OR  < DC_1                   [originals_required]
  IC  < A_1
  DC_k < A_k
  A_k < RMI_k                  [if A_k issues the request]
  CR  < RMI_k                  [if CR issues the request]
  RMI_k < DC_(k+1) < A_(k+1)
  A_last < CR                  [high_risk OR high_value]
  CR < Dec                     [high_risk OR high_value]
  A_last < Dec
  Dec < Not
  Tri < EA ; Tri < PCS ; PCS < CR     [optional preliminary work]

Explicitly unordered (concurrency allowed)
  IC || DC_1                   [no originals required]
  IC || OR                     [originals required]
  EA || IC, EA || DC_1, PCS || IC, PCS || DC_1
  EA ? A_last                  [EA may occur before or after the last Assessment]

Case instantiations (counterfactual)
  K-104: Reg < Tri < {IC, DC_1, EA, PCS};  IC || DC_1 || EA || PCS;
         {IC, DC_1} < A_1;  {A_1, PCS} < CR < Dec < Not;  EA < CR (advice informs final review)
  K-219: Reg < Tri < IC;  Reg < OR < DC_1[D2];  IC || OR;  {IC, DC_1} < A_1 < Dec < Not
         rework branch: DC_1 < A_1 < RMI_1 < DC_2 < A_2 < Dec < Not
  K-337: Reg < Tri < IC;  Reg < OR < DC_1;  {IC, DC_1} < A_1 < RMI_1 < DC_2 < A_2 < CR < Dec < Not
         (RMI_2, DC_3, A_3 removed; A_2 becomes A_last)
  K-411: Reg < Tri < {IC, DC_1};  IC || DC_1;  {IC, DC_1} < A_1 < Dec < Not
```

```text
# Conditional and declarative constraints
C01 init(Reg); chain_succession(Reg, Tri)                -- Register first, then Triage
C02 if originals_required: precedence(OR, DC_1); IC is not constrained by OR
C03 if high_risk OR high_value: exists CR_final with complete(A_last) <= start(CR_final)
    and complete(CR_final) <= start(Dec)
C04 response(RMI_k, DC_(k+1)) and response(RMI_k, A_(k+1)) before Dec;
    if high_risk OR high_value: a new or explicitly reaffirmed CR after A_(k+1)
C05 four-eyes: resource(A_last) != resource(CR_final)
C06 PCS never satisfies C03 (preliminary != final)
C07 issuer(RMI) in {Assessment, Compliance Review}; PCS and EA may only flag issues
C08 no resource overlap: for each resource r, service intervals of r are disjoint
C09 temporal monotonicity: start <= complete; complete(pred) <= start(succ)
C10 inferred availability windows (behavioral, not normative): M1 signs roughly
    08:30-18:40 on weekdays, no weekend decisions; C1 observed until ~19:30; A2 observed
    until ~22:30; D1 observed working overnight; D2 evening coverage unknown
C11 queue snapshots = forecast hours of committed work ahead, roughly FIFO, error +/-50%
C12 K-337 policy: not_exists(RMI after A_2); Dec in {approve_with_conditions, reject};
    unresolved discrepancy must be recorded in A_2, CR and Dec rationale
C13 K-411 logging: logged start/complete = actual start/complete (real-time capture);
    start(DC_1) >= task_created(DC_1) >= complete(Tri)
C14 K-104 reservation: soft reservation of C1 may not start CR before complete(A_last)
```

Intervention types. K-411 changes timing only: the task for the document check is created earlier, which makes possible the concurrency that rule 3 already allows. The activity set, resources and decision rights stay the same; the only extra requirement is how timestamps are logged. K-104 also changes timing for the target case (escalation advice moves earlier, and a preliminary screen is added as rule 10 permits), but its reservation changes C1's queue discipline, so for other cases it is a priority change. Neither alters routing or decision policy. K-219 changes routing (D1 to D2). That shifts the service-time distribution and the probability of rework, so it can indirectly change the control-flow path by adding a loop, and it can change quality. K-337 changes the allowed decision policy: it removes a permitted branch (the request after Assessment 2) and restricts the outcomes to approve with conditions or reject. It changes what is decided and on what evidence, not merely when.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

Factual mechanism: Two delay mechanisms were separate. The first was verification: D1's backlog (13 h at Mon 08:00) set the document check at Mon 21:40-Tue 05:40, and A1's queue (9 h at Tue 08:00) set the Assessment at Tue 15:10-19:10. The second was the post-assessment segment, Tue 19:10 to Wed 18:40 (23.5 h). It consisted of escalation advice (2 h, which could only start once A1 rediscovered the ambiguity), a C1 queue of roughly 16 h (C1 backlog 16 h Tue 08:00, still 8 h Wed 08:00), a 4.5 h review in which most evidence was read from scratch, and M1. The ambiguity was known at Mon 08:50, about 34 hours before it reached S1.

Minimal intervention: Only three things change. Triage is extended by about 10-15 minutes to assemble a packet. S1's advice runs on Monday concurrently with the checks, and a preliminary compliance screen, logged under a distinct label, pre-reads the entity and sanctions material. C1 holds a soft reservation for its first working slot after A1 completes. Resources (I1, D1, A1, C1, M1), the position of the final CR after the final Assessment, and decision rights are unchanged.

Temporal propagation: Upstream almost nothing moves. The identity check still waits behind I1's two cases, and the document check still waits for D1's backlog (the triage extension shifts its start by at most 0-15 minutes). The Assessment still starts at Tue 15:10 because A1's queue is what binds. S1's memo may shorten the Assessment by 0-1 h because A1 no longer has to rediscover and research the ambiguity, so it completes Tue 18:10-19:10. Early preparation therefore does not produce early readiness for the final review. Downstream, three effects combine. First, escalation advice no longer sits between Assessment and CR, removing 2h10m, but this only matters if C1 can start right away. Second, the reservation replaces the ~16 h queue with a wait for C1's next working window: Tuesday evening if C1 works until about 19:30-21:30, otherwise Wed 08:00. Third, CR shrinks from 4.5 h to 2-3.5 h because the evidence has been pre-read (guidance: saves 1-3 h). Finally, M1's day window caps the gain: a CR that finishes Tuesday night still leads to a decision around Wed 08:30.

Process-feasibility check: Register precedes Triage, and both checks precede Assessment. Without originals the checks may overlap, and I1 and D1 are different resources. The preliminary screen is explicitly preliminary (rule 10), and the final CR comes after the latest Assessment (rule 5). A1 ≠ C1, so four-eyes holds (rule 9). S1's advisory work may overlap with other checks. The preliminary screen must fit a gap in C1's schedule or displace other work; C1 is never double-booked, and the displacement is counted as a spillover. The preliminary screen cannot issue a request (C07); if it finds a gap, it informs A1, who may issue one. Decision follows CR, and Notify follows Decision.

Best/base/worst case: In the best case, C1 does the final CR Tue ~19:15-21:30, M1 signs Wed 08:00-08:30 and Notify is at ~08:40, a cycle of ~48.5 h (-10 h). In the base case, CR runs Wed 08:00-10:30, Decision ~10:45-11:15 and Notify ~11:25, a cycle of ~51 h (-7 h). In the worst case on the same path, urgent cases override the reservation, CR runs Wed 13:10-16:30 (still shorter than factual), Decision ~17:00 and Notify ~17:30 (about -1 h). In a tail scenario, the early packet reveals missing ownership evidence and A1 issues a request, adding 16-40 h relative to factual. That is slower, but arguably a quality improvement. The decision is most likely unchanged (approve with conditions).

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: C1 time logs showing that review time does not fall after preliminary work, for example because "from scratch" referred to format rather than content. C1 calendars showing no free slot before Wed 13:10 whatever the reservation. Evidence that the two urgent compliance cases added Monday would breach their SLA if displaced. S1 being unavailable on Monday. Preliminary packets typically being so incomplete that C1 re-reads everything anyway.

### K-219

Factual mechanism: Routing to D1 on handwriting grounds put the case behind D1's backlog (11 h at Mon 16:00, 8 h at Tue 08:00). The originals were ready at Mon 17:30, but the document check started at Tue 09:30 (a 16 h wait). The check itself was fast (4 h) and accurate. A2's queue added 6.5 h, and A2 finished at 22:30, after M1's day, so the case waited another 10 h overnight. Two waits dominated: D1's queue and M1's overnight gap.

Minimal intervention: Only the document-check resource changes, to D2, with the task released at Originals Receipt. The identity check, A2, M1 and the activity order are unchanged. No compliance review is needed (low risk, low value).

Temporal propagation: D2's queue was about 5 h at 16:00, so the check starts around Mon 21:00-23:00, provided D2 covers evenings as D1 is observed to do. Service takes 5-8 h (standard documents, with handwriting pushing toward the upper half of D2's 4-8 h range), so it completes around Tue 02:00-07:00, some 6.5-11.5 h earlier than factual. Reaching A2's queue earlier puts the case ahead of cases that arrive Tuesday morning. With about 6 h of A2 work at Tue 08:00, the Assessment starts around 11:00-14:00 and completes around 13:00-17:00. The critical nonlinearity is whether the Assessment completes inside M1's window. If it does, the decision comes Tue ~15:00-18:30 and roughly 14-21 h are saved. If not, the decision is Wed 08:30, the same as factual. A readiness gain of about 6-11 h therefore turns into either about 15-20 h or about 0 h. If D2 works days only, it clears its backlog Tuesday morning, starts around 11:00 and finishes around 16:00-19:00, later than D1 did, so the intervention is harmful. If D2 asks for clarification, A2 must issue a formal request (rule 6), followed by an external response (8-36 h), a new document check and a new Assessment (rule 7).

Process-feasibility check: Originals Receipt precedes the document check (the D2 start is at or after 17:30). The identity check precedes the Assessment. D2 must first finish its existing queue, so it has no overlapping intervals. No compliance or four-eyes constraint applies. Decision follows Assessment, and Notify follows Decision. The rework branch satisfies rule 7.

Best/base/worst case: In the best case, D2's queue is shorter than forecast (start ~19:00, done ~01:00), A2 runs Tue 08:00-10:30, the decision is at ~11:00 and Notify ~11:30, a cycle of ~26.5 h (-21.5 h). In the base case (no rework, D2 evening coverage), Notify comes Tue ~16:00-18:40 (-14 to -17 h) with about 50-60% probability, otherwise Wed 09:00 (0 h). Before accounting for rework, the expected change is about -7 to -10 h. In the worst case, the clarification request is issued around Tue 13:00, the response arrives Wednesday or Thursday, the second check and Assessment happen Thursday, and the decision comes Thursday afternoon or Friday morning (+30 to +50 h). A separate worst case is a silent misreading that leads to a wrong decision, which the cycle time would not show.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: A D2 clarification rate above roughly 30% on handwritten files. D2 having no evening coverage. M1 having no Tuesday afternoon capacity. D2's real queue at 17:30 exceeding about 8 h. Audits showing D2's error rate on handwriting is materially higher than D1's.

### K-337

Factual mechanism: The loops were evidence-driven. The first check found inconsistent supplier totals, and A1 requested a corrected schedule (supplier took 20.5 h). The second check left one inconsistency, and A2 requested one more confirmation. That wait crossed the weekend (Fri 10:30 to Mon 09:00, 70.5 h) and was followed by the third check, the third Assessment (accepted), CR after a 5 h queue, and an overnight wait before the decision. The second loop and its knock-on steps span Fri 10:30 to Wed 09:10. The approval was grounded in resolved evidence.

Minimal intervention: The request after Assessment 2 is replaced by a conclusion. A1 records the unresolved discrepancy (amount, candidate causes, exposure) and recommends approve with conditions or reject. The final CR remains mandatory (high risk, high value) and follows Assessment 2, which is now the latest. Under the policy no further request may be issued, including by C1; otherwise the prohibition would simply move to compliance. Everything before Assessment 2 is unchanged.

Temporal propagation: Assessment 2 lengthens by 0.5-1 h for documentation (Fri 08:00 to about 11:00). The case enters C1's queue late Friday morning. Load was normal on Thursday (5 h queue), so CR starts around 13:00-15:30. It takes 4-6 h, because C1 must judge residual risk and proportionate conditions rather than confirm resolved evidence, so it ends around 17:00-21:30. M1's window closes around 18:30-18:40, so a Friday decision is possible only if CR finishes before about 18:00; otherwise the decision falls to Monday 08:30, possibly later if there is a Monday backlog. The weekend that factually hit the supplier now hits the internal decision in most scenarios. That is why the base saving is about 48 h rather than over 100 h. Removing the second request, the third check, the third Assessment and the factual CR also frees 3.5 h of D1 and 2.5 h of A1 time.

Process-feasibility check: After the first request, rule 7 is satisfied because a new check and a new Assessment precede the decision. CR follows the latest Assessment, and A1 ≠ C1. The originals (Mon 18:00) preceded the first check. The decision-policy change is explicit and permitted by the intervention. Nothing is silently ignored: the discrepancy appears in the Assessment and CR records and becomes either a condition (such as a holdback of the disputed amount, confirmation as a condition precedent to disbursement, or audit rights) or a reason for rejection.

Best/base/worst case: In the best case, C1 starts at 12:30 and needs 4 h, M1 signs at 17:00-17:30 and Notify is at ~17:40, a cycle of ~103 h (-111 h), approved with conditions. In the base case, CR ends Friday evening, the decision is Mon 08:30-09:00 and Notify ~09:10, a cycle of ~166 h (-48 h), approved with conditions. In the worst case, C1 will not sign off without the confirmation and escalation to S1 and M1 pushes the decision to Monday or Tuesday (-20 to -45 h). Alternatively, the case is rejected and appealed, and the appeal recreates the confirmation step after notification, so the real resolution comes later than factual. A third possibility is that a conditional approval is granted and the discrepancy proves material, producing a loss. In hindsight, the factual confirmation was accepted, which suggests the discrepancy was benign; a rejection would then be a false negative. That information was not available on Friday, so decision quality must be judged ex ante.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: The discrepancy exceeding the materiality threshold relative to exposure, in which case policy effectively forces rejection. Regulatory or internal rules requiring complete evidence for high-risk approvals. Historical data showing that second confirmations often change outcomes. High appeal rates for rejections. Conditional approvals routinely requiring the same confirmation before disbursement, in which case the loop merely moves after the decision and the real benefit is close to zero.

### K-411

Factual mechanism: A legacy task-creation defect and a case lock serialized the checks. The document task was created only when the identity check completed (13:20); D2 then queued for 1h40 and worked 15:00-19:30. The case reached A2 in the evening, waited overnight behind a grown queue until Wed 09:30, and then waited 2 h for M1.

Minimal intervention: Both tasks are created at Triage (about 09:35-09:40). A controlled workaround narrows the lock, for example with a separate document sub-record or ownership token for D2, so that I2 and D2 never write to the same fields. Starts and completions are captured live. Resources (I2, D2, A2, M1) are unchanged.

Temporal propagation: The identity check is unchanged: I2 had a 2 h queue and starts at 11:30. The document check starts when D2 reaches it. With about 4 h of D2 work at 08:00, that is roughly 12:00-13:30, and a 4-5 h check completes around 16:00-18:30. The critical path through verification becomes the document check alone instead of identity plus document, a readiness gain of 1-3.5 h. Downstream, A2's evening slot (20:00-22:30) is held by K-219, which was ready earlier and comes first under FIFO. Arriving earlier moves K-411 ahead of cases that became ready between about 17:00 and 19:30, so on Wednesday it starts around 08:00-09:00 instead of 09:30 (0-1.5 h earlier). The Assessment (2-2.5 h) finishes around 10:00-11:30, M1 adds a 0.5-2 h wait, and the decision comes around 10:30-13:30. The gain shrinks from the readiness gain to about 0-3 h in cycle time.

Process-feasibility check: Rule 3 permits the concurrency because no originals are required. Both checks precede the Assessment, and I2 and D2 are different resources. No compliance review is needed. D2 is not double-booked: its noon slot displaces another case D2 would otherwise have processed then. Timestamps must reflect true starts (C13). The workaround must not let two actors write the same case fields; otherwise the concurrency is only apparent or causes data corruption.

Best/base/worst case: In the best case, A2 has a gap Tue ~18:00-20:00 before K-219, the Assessment completes around 20:00, M1 signs Wed 08:30-08:50 and Notify is at ~09:00, a cycle of ~24 h (-5.5 h). In the base case, Notify is Wed ~11:30-13:00 (-1.5 to -3 h). In the worst case, A2 uses priority rules rather than FIFO and M1 still signs at 14:00, so there is no gain (0 h). If identity fails (rare), the document work is wasted.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: A2 not using FIFO, which removes the benefit. Spare A2 capacity in the evening, which increases it. Screen or task-claim logs showing the factual checks were actually concurrent but batch-entered, which would make the factual mechanism wrong and the benefit zero. Data-integrity incidents caused by the lock workaround.

Scaling each policy to 100 similar cases in one week changes the question from "does this case finish earlier?" to "who waits instead?".

Reservation of scarce compliance capacity: C1 is the only certified reviewer, and its queue clears most slowly (a 20 h backlog on Monday). One hundred high-risk cases would need roughly 300-600 h of final review, many times one person's weekly capacity, so reservations cannot create capacity; they can only reorder it. Hard time blocks cause no-shows when Assessments run late, leaving C1 idle while urgent cases wait. The policy helps the system only if preliminary work shifts 1-3 h per case from C1 to S1 and reservations are soft tokens that release automatically. Otherwise the bottleneck migrates to S1, whose queue is not even measured.

Substitution between D1 and D2: Routing handwritten cases to D2 relieves D1, which helps complex corporate files, but each case costs D2 about 1.3-2 times D1's time. At scale, D2's queue, currently the short one, becomes the long one and erases the early-start advantage that motivated the reroute. Routing should depend on system state: use D2 only when the expected D1 wait exceeds D2's extra service time plus the expected cost of rework.

Additional or avoided rework: Suppressing second requests avoids 100 external waits, D1 checks and A1 assessments, but turns discrepancies into conditions, rejections and appeals that reappear outside the logged case. D2 routing adds rework; if clarification rates rise from about 5% to 25%, the extra loops consume the capacity that was saved.

Assessment-queue bottlenecks: Both K-411 and K-219 deliver cases to A2 earlier. With one assessor in training and queues of 6-12 h, faster verification mostly lengthens the assessment queue. In the factual week, K-219 and K-411 already compete for the same A2 evening slot.

Weekend and external-response effects: Gains come in lumps. A case that misses M1's last Friday slot loses about 62 h. Supplier response times and weekend closures dominate rework durations.

Timestamp behavior versus true concurrency: Concurrency is real only if starts and completions are captured live. Batch entry can fabricate overlap, overstate the benefit and hide double-booking that violates rule 11.

## 7. Final synthesis

Parallel verification for K-411-type cases is the most robust intervention. It keeps the activity set, resources and decision rights unchanged and simply makes possible the concurrency that rule 3 already allows. The benefit is modest, typically 1.5-3 hours of cycle time, because the overnight assessment queue absorbs most of the earlier readiness. It is rarely negative, easy to reverse, and yields clean evidence about true concurrency if timestamps are captured live. K-104's early escalation ranks second. Moving S1's advice to Monday and pre-reading evidence can shorten the final compliance review, and a soft reservation removes most of the 16-hour compliance wait. The net effect depends on who does the preparation and whether reservations displace urgent work.

Suppressing the second rework loop in K-337 is the most dangerous despite its speed. It shortens that case by about 48 hours, possibly over 100, but it changes decision policy, not timing. An evidence gap becomes conditions or a rejection, risk moves into post-decision monitoring and appeals that the event log does not capture, and the defensibility of a high-risk, high-value approval weakens.

K-104's compliance reservation is where a local counterfactual transports least well. For one case, jumping C1's queue looks almost free. Across 100 cases, the single certified reviewer cannot absorb the demand, so reservations only reorder the queue, create idle no-show slots and push delay onto urgent cases. K-219's reroute is similar: D2's queue is short precisely because few cases are sent there.

The most valuable additional data are:
- resource working calendars, especially M1's and C1's windows and D2's evening coverage;
- event-level queue entries and task-claim timestamps instead of snapshots;
- the actual queue disciplines of A1, A2 and C1;
- C1's hands-on time with and without preliminary work;
- D2's clarification and error rates on handwriting;
- how often past second information requests changed the decision.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. `structural`: The preliminary compliance screen and escalation advice cannot issue a formal request for more information and never satisfy the final-review requirement. Under the K-337 policy, the prohibition on further requests also binds C1, since otherwise the intervention would be empty.
2. `temporal`: M1 signs decisions only on weekdays, roughly 08:30-18:40, as inferred from observed decision times. This creates the overnight and weekend cliffs behind the bimodal outcomes in K-219 and K-337 and the cap on K-104's best case.
3. `temporal`: Queue snapshots represent forecast hours of committed work, processed roughly FIFO. Interpolation between snapshots carries about ±50% error, so every derived start time is a range, not a point.
4. `resource`: D2 has evening coverage comparable to D1's observed overnight work. If D2 works days only, the K-219 intervention is neutral to harmful.
5. `behavioral`: S1 can take the K-104 packet within about four hours of Triage on Monday, and C1 honours a soft reservation without breaching urgent-case SLAs. Reservations are released when the Assessment slips.
6. `quality`: D2's probability of requesting clarification on difficult handwriting is about 15-35%, compared with 5-10% for D1, and D2 has a somewhat higher rate of silent misreading.
7. `quality`: In K-337, a proportionate conditional instrument (holdback, or confirmation as a condition precedent) exists and the discrepancy is below the automatic-rejection threshold. The factual acceptance of the confirmation suggests a benign discrepancy but was unknown when the counterfactual decision was made.
8. `interference`: In the single-case analyses, all other cases' arrivals, priorities and service times are held fixed. In reality, interventions interact: an earlier K-219 assessment would free A2's Tuesday evening slot that K-411 could then use, while reservations for K-104 delay other compliance cases.
