## 1. System boundary and assumptions

**Scope used: order-to-delivery operations, from order receipt to delivery confirmation, for the 12,000 spare-part orders.** The boundary is a gate-to-customer operational footprint, with upstream material extraction excluded.

**Inside the boundary**
- **Production energy** (energy_kwh) at CNC_A, CNC_B, heat treatment, and inspection cells, including energy consumed in rework loops.
- **Material scrap** (material_scrap_kg) from machining and rework.
- **Package material** (package_material_kg), including reinforced packaging and packaging rework.
- **Outbound transport emissions** (transport_kgCO2e) for ground parcel, air parcel, consolidated truck, and dedicated van.
- **Operational waste generated by process failure**, such as rework packaging, partial-shipment second parcels, and obsolete batch output, counted through the materials and energy already attributed to those cases.

**Outside the boundary**
- Upstream raw-material extraction and supplier manufacturing, beyond the scrap already recorded.
- Machine tooling, facility construction, and overhead energy not attributed to specific cases.
- Customer use phase and end-of-life of installed parts.
- Return-shipment transport, which is only partially visible through rework and packaging-rework flags. This is a known gap.

**Key assumptions and uncertainties**
- Emission factors are taken as recorded in the log. Energy (kWh) and transport (kgCO2e) are kept as separate indicators. No grid factor is assumed, so the energy-to-carbon conversion is not invented.
- Segment averages are used to compute mix-weighted totals. These are descriptive, not lifecycle totals. Derived from the segment table, the mix-weighted averages are roughly 7.4 kWh, 10.3 kgCO2e transport, and 0.35 kg scrap per order.
- Using the segment table, Made-to-order (34% of orders) accounts for roughly 87% of energy and roughly 85% of scrap. Critical-downtime Service-contract (11% of orders) accounts for roughly 41% of transport emissions. These are the two main leverage points.
- **Data quality caveats:** the flattened DFG does not balance perfectly. For example, inbound "Ship ground" flows total 6,660 while outbound "Confirm delivery" from ground is 6,550, and dedicated-van and consolidated flows lose about 20 cases each. This likely reflects unfinished, cancelled, or filtered cases. Named path variants cover about 89% of cases, so roughly 11% of orders sit in unlisted variants. The Critical emergency and partial-shipment combinations also reduce model precision during disruptions.
- Batch-level energy is assumed to be allocated evenly across items in a batch. Obsolescence is attributed only where the 14-day unshipped flag is set.
- Carbon-accounting of rework is only as good as rework-loop attribution. Energy and material for rework loops are assumed to be captured in the case's totals.

## 2. Sustainability-related process indicators

Indicators are grouped by theme and computed from event attributes and path membership. Each is reported by segment, path variant, and machine/batch band.

**Energy**
- **E1. Energy per shipped order (kWh/order)** by segment and path. Baseline: mix-weighted ~7.4; MTO ~18.5–20; Standard ~0.8–0.9.
- **E2. Energy per accepted unit (kWh per good unit shipped, including rework loops).** This is the more honest efficiency measure, because rework energy is hidden in simple per-order averages.
- **E3. Rework energy share** = energy in rework-production and rework-packaging loops ÷ total energy.
- **E4. Energy-vs-batch-size curve for CNC_A and heat treatment.** Expected: 2.8 kWh/item at batch 1–3, falling to 1.3 at 12–20.

**Transport**
- **T1. Transport kgCO2e per order** by segment and route_mode (ground, air, consolidated, dedicated van).
- **T2. Air share of transport emissions** and air-to-ground emission ratio (the prompt cites 4–6x).
- **T3. Consolidation eligibility rate.** Share of ground-eligible non-urgent stock orders that could have waited for a consolidated departure within the promised lead time.
- **T4. Dedicated-van utilization and justification completeness.** Share of van moves with an SP2 justification event (31% currently missing) and van return-empty rate.

**Materials and packaging**
- **M1. Scrap kg per order**, split into first-pass scrap and rework-generated scrap.
- **M2. Package material kg per order**, with reinforced-packaging share for MTO parts over 8 kg (Q1).
- **M3. Packaging rework rate** (rework packaging events ÷ packaged orders). The current figure is about 620 rework-packaging events across roughly 6,830 standard and 3,470 reinforced entries.
- **M4. Obsolescence rate**: batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days (currently ~6%, and dependent on forecast error).

**Batching**
- **B1. Median and p90 batch wait** by machine group and segment (CNC_A: 4h at small batch, 38h at large batch).
- **B2. Batch fill ratio** versus target (for energy efficiency).
- **B3. Heat-treatment batch wait share** and the associated late-delivery multiplier (cited at 1.7x).

**Service**
- **S-a. Late-delivery rate and p90 cycle time** by segment (current: Standard 5–7%, MTO 13–18%, Critical 4%).
- **S-b. Partial-shipment rate** (1,010 of 7,050 stock picks, about 14%) and backorder-wait rate.
- **S-c. Cancellation-due-to-delay rate** (410 at availability check, 60 at backorder).
- **S-d. Promised-lead-time attainment probability** by route and segment. This is needed to test the SP1 85% threshold.

**Quality and returns**
- **Q-a. Rework rate** by machine group and part family (CNC_B MTO 18% vs CNC_A 9%).
- **Q-b. Inspection-to-rework loop rate** (about 470 rework-from-inspection events against roughly 3,940 inspection exits, about 12%).
- **Q-c. Damage-related return rate**, especially for reinforced versus standard packaging.

**Resilience**
- **R-a. Alternative-route availability for Service-contract customers (R1)**: share of SC orders with a viable second route in the event of carrier disruption.
- **R-b. Emergency-production and express-courier share** under disruption, and the share of disrupted cases that also involved partial shipment.
- **R-c. Single-route concentration**: share of volume on one carrier, one CNC cell, or one heat-treatment batch queue.

**Governance**
- **G-a. SP1 violation rate** (currently 22% of Standard-stock Service-contract cases).
- **G-b. SP2 justification completeness** (currently 31% missing for dedicated vans).
- **G-c. Q1 violation rate** (MTO over 8 kg without reinforced packaging or inspection certification), and its correlation with return/rework loops.

## 3. Intervention options

Each option is assessed by target, mechanism, service/quality/resilience effect, and monitoring evidence. Options are grouped by the path they change.

### S4. Priority pick + ground for Standard-stock Service-contract orders (replaces default air)
- **Target path:** Stock  standard pack  air (5% of cases, with 18.5 kgCO2e per order against 3.6 for ground). Standard-stock Service-contract currently averages 7.8 kgCO2e per order, and SP1 is violated in 22% of its cases.
- **Mechanism:** Use priority picking to recover lead time and route to ground. Air is reserved for cases where slack is insufficient, computed from promised_lead_time_hours minus the historical ground attainment time.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Median cycle is 17h on air and 34h on ground for stock orders, so the slack check is essential. Expected effect is small on late delivery if the slack rule holds. Resilience is preserved because R1 requires a retained alternative route for these customers.
- **Monitoring evidence:** SP1 violation rate, promised-lead-time attainment by route, late rate on ground-after-priority-pick cases, and the air-override share with reason codes.

### S5. Twice-daily consolidated truck departures for non-urgent stock orders
- **Target path:** Package standard  Consolidated truck (850 cases) and Package standard  Ship ground (5,100 cases). Consolidated trucks cut transport emissions by about 35–55% versus individual ground parcels.
- **Mechanism:** Replace individual ground parcels with pooled departures for orders with slack exceeding the consolidation wait (12–24h).
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Adds 12–24h of waiting. This is acceptable for Standard-stock Regular and non-urgent orders but not for Service-contract orders with tight slack. Resilience improves if consolidated trucks are a second route, but a single truck departure is a single point of failure, so R1 must still hold.
- **Monitoring evidence:** Consolidation eligibility rate (T3), consolidation wait distribution, late rate for consolidated vs parcel ground, and the share of consolidated orders later reclassified as expedite.

### S3. Shift MTO production from CNC_B to CNC_A when queue and due-date slack allow
- **Target path:** MTO  batch wait  CNC_B  inspection  rework  ground (5% of cases; median 160h, late 32%, energy 32 kWh, scrap 1.8 kg). CNC_B has 18% rework on MTO against 9% on CNC_A.
- **Mechanism:** Route MTO work to CNC_A, which has roughly half the rework rate, and remove the rework loop energy and scrap. This is a dispatch rule, not a new machine investment.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** CNC_A has longer batch waits. Shifting load risks crowding CNC_A and raising its queue, so use the rule only when due-date slack exceeds the expected CNC_A batch wait. Improves quality and resilience if CNC_B remains as an alternative cell.
- **Monitoring evidence:** Rework rate by cell and part family, CNC_A queue length and batch wait, energy per good unit (E2), and late rate for shifted orders.

### S2. Dynamic batching with segment-specific maximum waiting caps (CNC_A and heat treatment)
- **Target path:** MTO  batch wait (3,020 cases, the largest waiting step in production). Heat treatment energy per item is lowest above batch size 10, but batch-waiting cases carry 1.7x late risk.
- **Mechanism:** Release batches when either the batch-fill target or a segment-specific maximum wait cap is reached. Use caps tighter for Service-contract and Critical customers and looser for Regular MTO with flexible promises. Group compatible heat-treatment parts to raise fill without waiting.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Captures most of the energy benefit (1.3 vs 2.8 kWh at larger batch) while limiting the 38h wait tail. Caps protect late rate for Service-contract. Batching increases obsolescence if forecasts are poor, so pair with M4 monitoring.
- **Monitoring evidence:** Batch fill ratio, p90 batch wait by segment, energy per item vs batch size (B2, E4), late rate of batch-waiting cases, and obsolescence (M4).

### S6. Packaging-route compatibility check before shipment
- **Target path:** Package standard  Rework packaging (260) and Package reinforced  Rework packaging (360, with 420 returns to reinforced and 130 to standard). Packaging rework occurs when packaging choice is incompatible with route_mode or part geometry.
- **Mechanism:** Add a validation step at packaging that checks route_mode, part geometry, and weight against the packaging specification and the Q1 rule. Route the order to a compatible packaging-route combination on the first pass.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Reduces packaging rework loops and the time they add. Does not reduce the reinforced packaging itself, so it preserves damage protection. Low service risk.
- **Monitoring evidence:** Packaging rework rate (M3), rework-packaging loop count per order, package material per order (M2), and damage-return rate by packaging type (Q-c).

### S7. Inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations
- **Target path:** CNC production  Inspection  Rework production (470 cases)  CNC production (390 cases). Inspection queues cause rework loops through rushed inspection that misses defects.
- **Mechanism:** Apply a targeted gate: full inspection for CNC_B MTO and any part family with rework above a threshold. Use sampling or fast-track inspection for stable CNC_A families. The gate must not be a blanket queue increase, or it will add inspection waiting, which in turn increases batch and cycle time.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Reduces rework energy, scrap, and damage-related returns. Adds some inspection time for flagged combinations. Net service effect is positive if rework loops are removed.
- **Monitoring evidence:** Rework rate by machine/part family, inspection-to-rework loop rate (Q-b), damage returns, inspection queue time, and the defect-escape rate after shipment.

### S8. Replace some dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes (Critical-downtime)
- **Target path:** Critical  emergency production  reinforced pack  dedicated van (8% of cases, median 14h, late 3%, 52 kgCO2e per order, with 31% missing SP2 justification). Dedicated vans often return empty.
- **Mechanism:** For precautionary or non-true-downtime Critical orders (see S9), use shared emergency courier lanes or consolidated pickup. Retain dedicated vans for true downtime, with SP2 justification captured as an event before dispatch.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Protects downtime service for true critical cases. Shared lanes may add some time and variability for borderline cases. Resilience improves if shared lanes are a second route, but the dedicated-van capacity for true downtime must remain.
- **Monitoring evidence:** Dedicated-van share and empty-return rate, SP2 completeness (G-b), Critical p90 cycle time, late rate for shared-lane cases, and the reclassification rate from precautionary to true downtime.

### S9. Split Critical-downtime into true downtime and precautionary urgent categories
- **Target path:** Critical-downtime Service-contract, 11% of orders and roughly 41% of transport emissions.
- **Mechanism:** Classify each Critical order at intake as either true downtime (production stopped) or precautionary urgent (spare held as safety stock or planned maintenance). Assign true downtime to dedicated van or emergency production, and precautionary urgent to stock/ground or shared lanes where slack allows. The "Critical  stock/ground exception" path already shows 4.8 kgCO2e and 23h median with 9% late, which indicates that slack exists for some cases.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Keeps the full service level for true downtime. Precautionary cases accept a slightly longer lead time, which must be agreed with the customer. Risk: misclassification, so the classification must be auditable.
- **Monitoring evidence:** Classification rate and audit sample, Critical late rate by category, transport emissions by category, and the number of precautionary orders later reclassified as true downtime.

### S10. Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular MTO customers
- **Target path:** MTO Regular (22% of orders; 18.5 kWh, 8.0 kgCO2e, 0.90 kg scrap per order; 18% late). Batch fill and consolidation require waiting time that the current promises may not accommodate.
- **Mechanism:** Offer Regular MTO customers an option to accept a slightly longer promise in exchange for a lower-impact fulfilment route (fuller batch, consolidated truck). The promise is set using each order's own attainment distribution, not a fixed extension.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Can reduce energy and transport emissions and make batching more reliable. Risk of churn and reputational damage if longer promises are not met. Only for customers who opt in, and only where the promise stays within each order's attainment distribution. Not applicable to Service-contract customers.
- **Monitoring evidence:** Opt-in rate, promised vs actual lead time, late rate after negotiation, customer churn and complaint rate for Regular MTO, and energy/transport per opt-in order vs control.

### S11. Reduce partial shipments by changing availability-check rules
- **Target path:** Availability check  Pick from stock  Partial shipment (1,010 cases)  Package standard, with backorder wait (220) and cancellation (60). Partial-shipment path: median 79h, late 24%, 1.4 kWh, 7.2 kgCO2e, 0.08 kg scrap.
- **Mechanism:** At availability check, hold the order until full stock is confirmed for high-demand SKUs, or pre-allocate safety stock to Service-contract and frequently partially shipped SKUs. Stop splitting orders where a single consolidated shipment can meet the promise.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Removes second parcels (both transport and packaging) and reduces late rate, but holds some orders longer while waiting for full availability. Safety stock adds inventory and obsolescence exposure, so limit it to specific SKUs.
- **Monitoring evidence:** Partial-shipment rate (S-b), second-parcel count, backorder wait, late rate of partial vs full orders, and inventory days for safety-stocked SKUs.

### S12. Sustainability exception log for SP1, SP2, and Q1 overrides
- **Target path:** All exception paths (air overrides, dedicated vans, reinforced-packaging waivers, emergency production).
- **Mechanism:** Require a justification event with reason codes for each override: insufficient slack for ground, critical downtime, inspection-certified low damage risk. Make the log a required data source and review it periodically.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** No direct service effect. Makes overrides visible and governable. Prevents silent drift back to high-impact routing.
- **Monitoring evidence:** Override rate by reason code, justification completeness (G-a, G-b, G-c), and whether overrides correlate with late or rework outcomes.

## 5. Rebound and displacement risks

Each risk describes how a gain in one place can produce a loss elsewhere, and what mitigates it.

1. **Batching  waiting  expediting (displacement into transport).** Larger CNC_A and heat-treatment batches reduce energy per item, but waiting increases late-delivery risk (1.7x for heat-treatment batch waits). Late orders are then expedited by air or dedicated van. Air is 4–6x ground per order, so the energy gain can be transferred into transport emissions. *Mitigation:* segment-specific wait caps (S2) and an explicit rule that expedite decisions are made against a slack threshold, not on lateness after the fact.

2. **Consolidation  inventory and buffer stock (displacement into materials and obsolescence).** Consolidated trucks reduce transport emissions but add 12–24h of waiting. To protect service, planners may add safety stock or pre-produce items in larger batches, increasing obsolescence (currently ~6% of batch items not shipped within 14 days). *Mitigation:* consolidation only for slack-rich orders (S5), with safety stock limited to specific SKUs (S11) and tied to forecast accuracy.

3. **Reinforced packaging  package material and transport weight (rebound within packaging).** Reinforced packaging reduces damage-related returns by about 40% for heavy MTO parts but roughly doubles package material. If Q1 is enforced broadly, including for parts under 8 kg or in low-risk routes, the material increase may outweigh the return reduction. *Mitigation:* apply the Q1 threshold strictly, use route/geometry compatibility checks (S6), and allow inspection-certified exceptions (S12).

4. **Rework reduction via stricter inspection  inspection queues  more batch waits (rebound across machine groups).** Tighter inspection (S7) can lengthen inspection queues, which holds batches and cases upstream, increasing batch waits and cycle time. *Mitigation:* target the gate to high-rework combinations only, and monitor inspection queue time alongside rework loops.

5. **Shifting MTO load from CNC_B to CNC_A  capacity concentration and queue (displacement across cells).** Reducing rework on CNC_B by routing to CNC_A (S3) concentrates load on the cell with longer batch waits and reduces redundancy. The energy and scrap gain can turn into a waiting and late-delivery loss if the cell saturates. *Mitigation:* the shift is conditional on queue length and due-date slack, and CNC_B remains as an alternative route.

6. **Air and van restrictions  shift of risk to emergency courier and partial shipment (displacement across exception paths).** Restricting air for Standard-stock SC orders (S4) or dedicated vans (S8) may push orders into partial shipment, backorder wait, or cancellation. The partial path currently has 24% late and 7.2 kgCO2e per order, which is not clean. *Mitigation:* S11 must accompany S4 and S8, and cancellation-due-to-delay must be monitored as a leading indicator.

7. **Carbon-aware promises  customer behavior (rebound through demand and churn).** If Regular MTO customers accept longer promises (S10), they may stop ordering through the distributor, switch to fast alternative suppliers, or place larger, less frequent orders that increase batch size and obsolescence. Alternatively, frequent smaller orders can increase the number of transport moves. *Mitigation:* opt-in only, with promises derived from each order's attainment distribution, and monitor order frequency and basket size.

8. **Stock-channel pre-allocation  obsolescence and reduced stock turnover.** Holding orders until full availability (S11) or pre-allocating safety stock for high-demand SKUs can lead to stock that is not shipped, creating waste and cash risk. *Mitigation:* SKU-level selection based on demand stability and a retirement rule.

9. **Governance without data  hidden rebound (displacement not visible in KPIs).** Without S12, overrides can silently return to air and dedicated vans, and Q1 exceptions can grow, which erodes gains without visible alerts. *Mitigation:* the exception log and conformance dashboards in Section 7.

## 6. Balanced recommendation

The recommendation is a single redesign package with four linked components. Each addresses one dominant source of impact while protecting the segments most sensitive to service, quality, or resilience. The package is differentiated by segment and slack, not applied uniformly.

### Component A. Slack-based dispatch for stock orders (S4, S5, S11, S6)
- Standard-stock Service-contract: priority pick and ground by default. Air is used only when the ground attainment probability is below the SP1 threshold (85%) given the promised lead time. Every air override is logged (S12).
- Standard-stock Regular and non-urgent orders: pool into twice-daily consolidated departures when consolidation wait fits within slack.
- Availability check: hold for full stock on high-demand SKUs and pre-allocate limited safety stock where partial-shipment frequency is high.
- Packaging compatibility check before shipment to remove rework loops.

**Why it balances:** Stock orders have the lowest energy and the largest volume (about 55% of orders), so small gains here add up cheaply. Service-contract protection is preserved by the slack rule, and partial-shipment reduction removes a path that is both high-carbon and high-late.

### Component B. Segment-aware MTO production and quality (S2, S3, S7, S10)
- Batch release governed by fill target plus segment-specific wait caps: tighter for Service-contract and Critical-linked parts, looser for Regular MTO.
- Dispatch MTO work to CNC_A when queue and due-date slack allow, reserving CNC_B for overflow with a targeted inspection gate.
- Inspection gate applied to high-rework machine/part combinations, not as a blanket queue increase.
- Carbon-aware promise options (S10) offered only to opted-in Regular MTO customers, with promises set from each order's attainment distribution.

**Why it balances:** MTO accounts for roughly 87% of energy and 85% of scrap in the mix-weighted view. Reducing rework (which currently drives the 160h, 32%-late, 1.8 kg scrap path) is a service and quality improvement as well as an environmental one. Caps prevent the batching benefit from turning into the 38h waiting tail.

### Component C. Critical-downtime triage and transport discipline (S8, S9, S12)
- Split Critical intake into true downtime and precautionary urgent.
- True downtime keeps dedicated vans and emergency production, with SP2 justification captured as a required event before dispatch.
- Precautionary urgent orders are routed to stock/ground or shared emergency lanes where slack allows, using the existing Critical stock/ground path (23h median, 9% late, 4.8 kgCO2e) as evidence that the slack exists.

**Why it balances:** Critical customers carry the highest service consequence. The package protects true downtime fully and reduces emissions only where the process evidence shows slack. This addresses the 41% share of transport emissions without touching downtime-critical service.

### Component D. Resilience and governance backbone (R1 protection, S12)
- Maintain at least one alternative route for every Service-contract customer, including a retained dedicated-van or emergency capacity for true downtime, and a second carrier or consolidated option for stock orders.
- Sustainability exception log for all SP1, SP2, Q1, and air/van overrides.
- Conformance and segment dashboards, described in Section 7.

**Why it balances:** The package reduces impact through routing and batching choices, but the resilience backbone prevents the consolidation and shared-lane changes from becoming single points of failure.

### Why this package and not the single lowest-emission option
- Routing everything to ground or consolidated would lower transport emissions but would violate service needs for Critical and Service-contract customers and remove the alternative routes R1 requires.
- Maximizing batch size everywhere would lower energy per item but would reintroduce the 38h waiting tail, raise late risk for heat-treatment waits, and increase obsolescence.
- Tightening inspection everywhere would reduce rework but would lengthen queues and push batch waits upstream.

The recommended package is the combination in which each green change is bounded by a segment-specific service constraint, and in which each rebound risk in Section 5 has a named mitigation and a monitored indicator.

## 7. Validation and governance plan

### 7.1 Staged rollout and evaluation design
- **Stage 1 (pilot, about one to two cycles):** Apply Component A to Standard-stock Service-contract and Component C to Critical-downtime. These have the clearest slack evidence and the most direct SP1/SP2 gaps.
- **Stage 2:** Extend Component B (batching caps, CNC_A dispatch, inspection gate) to MTO once queue and rework signals are stable.
- **Stage 3:** Introduce the Regular MTO promise option (S10) for opt-in customers only, after two stable cycles of Stage 2.
- Evaluate each stage with a **difference-in-differences** design: compare changed segments and paths against matched unchanged segments over the same period. Use the same segment definitions (Section 1) so that results are comparable.

### 7.2 Outcome measures from event logs
For each path variant and segment, compare pre and post on:
- **Environmental:** E1, E2, T1, T2, M1, M2, M4.
- **Service:** late rate, p90 cycle time, partial-shipment rate, backorder rate, cancellation-due-to-delay rate.
- **Quality:** rework rate, inspection-to-rework loop rate, packaging rework rate, damage-return rate.
- **Resilience:** R-a (alternative route availability), R-b (emergency and express share under disruption), R-c (route concentration).

Report results as ranges and shifts, not point estimates, and show the path-level changes alongside segment means. Because the log is flattened and the model precision is low during disruptions, check whether gains persist in disruption windows separately.

### 7.3 Conformance monitoring for SP1, SP2, and Q1
- **SP1 (ground/consolidated when promised lead time can still be met with probability above 85%):**
  - Build a per-route, per-segment attainment model from historical cycle times and promised lead times to estimate the probability that ground or consolidated will meet the promise.
  - Flag every air or van move for Standard-stock Service-contract where the estimated probability exceeds 85%.
  - Target: SP1 violations in Standard-stock Service-contract fall from 22% toward a low, justified residual, with overrides logged under S12.
- **SP2 (dedicated vans require explicit Critical-downtime justification):**
  - Add a required justification event before the dedicated-van dispatch activity.
  - Target: completeness moves from 69% toward 100%. Track the share of van moves classified as true downtime and their empty-return rate.
  - Add a check that precautionary cases are not dispatched by van without a justification.
- **Q1 (MTO parts over 8 kg use reinforced packaging unless inspection certifies low damage risk):**
  - Check weight at packaging against the packaging type used.
  - Require an inspection certification event for any waiver.
  - Correlate Q1 breaches with rework and return loops to confirm whether the rule is effective, and check whether reinforced packaging is over-applied to parts under 8 kg (rebound risk, Section 5.3).

### 7.4 Segment-level service monitoring and guardrails
Set explicit guardrails for each segment; if a guardrail is breached, the relevant intervention is paused or reverted:
- **Service-contract (Standard and Critical):** late rate and p90 cycle time must not exceed the pre-intervention baseline beyond an agreed tolerance. Any increase triggers review of the slack rule for air/van and of consolidation eligibility.
- **Critical true downtime:** no increase in cycle time or late rate for true-downtime cases. Any breach reverts S8 and S9 for that category.
- **Regular MTO:** late rate and promise attainment monitored after each batching-cap change. Obsolescence (M4) monitored against forecast error to catch batching rebound.
- **Resilience:** R1 must hold for all Service-contract customers at all times. A missing alternative route is a stop condition for any route change.

### 7.5 Rebound and displacement monitoring
Monitor the following indicators as early warnings for the risks in Section 5:
- Expedite and air-override rate after batching or consolidation changes (rebound into transport).
- Obsolescence rate and safety-stock days (rebound into materials).
- Reinforced-packaging share and package material per order, split by part weight (rebound within packaging).
- Inspection queue time and batch wait time after the inspection gate (rebound across production).
- CNC_A queue length and late rate for shifted MTO orders (capacity concentration).
- Cancellation and partial-shipment rates after air and van restrictions (displacement into exception paths).
- Order frequency and basket size for opt-in Regular MTO customers (customer behavior rebound).

### 7.6 Exception governance
- **Sustainability exception log (S12):** every override of ground, consolidated, reinforced packaging, or batch cap is recorded with a reason code, the actor or rule, the segment, and the outcome (late, rework, return).
- **Review cadence:** weekly review of override volumes and reason codes by the operations and sustainability leads; monthly review of whether overrides are concentrated in specific segments or cells.
- **Escalation:** overrides with late or rework outcomes above baseline trigger a root-cause review. Repeated overrides without justification trigger a rule change or a training action.
- **Audit:** sample a fixed share of Critical true-downtime and precautionary classifications each month to verify the S9 split.

### 7.7 Data and logging improvements needed for validation
- Add explicit events for SP2 justification, consolidation wait start and end, batch release decision (fill vs cap), packaging compatibility check, and inspection certification.
- Add a return-shipment event so that return transport and reverse-logistics emissions are visible within the boundary.
- Reconcile the DFG flow imbalances (for example, ground inbound vs outbound) and attribute the roughly 11% of cases in unlisted variants, so that the baseline is complete before any change is evaluated.
- Re-run process discovery after each stage to check whether the precision of the discovered model improves, especially in disruption windows where emergency and partial-shipment paths combine.

### 7.8 Success criteria
The redesign is considered successful if, after stabilization:
- Environmental indicators (energy per good unit, transport per order, and scrap per order) improve materially for MTO and Critical-downtime paths, with the largest gains in rework-related energy and scrap.
- Service-contract late rate and p90 cycle time are not worse than baseline beyond the agreed tolerance, and cancellation-due-to-delay does not rise.
- SP1 and SP2 violation rates fall to low, justified residuals, and Q1 breaches are explained by inspection-certified exceptions.
- Rework rate on MTO falls toward CNC_A levels, and inspection-to-rework loops decrease without a sustained increase in inspection queue time.
- R1 holds for all Service-contract customers, and no route change reduces the number of viable alternative routes in a disruption scenario.
- Obsolescence and safety-stock days remain within a tolerance linked to forecast accuracy, showing that batching and pre-allocation gains are not being offset by waste.
