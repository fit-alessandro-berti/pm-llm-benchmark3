# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

To separate **core role mandates** from **peripheral or situational activities**, activity frequency must be jointly evaluated with **case complexity**, **temporary substitution episodes**, and **formal organizational authority (certifications and sign-off rights)**.

### 1. Automated Intake & Pre-Validation Tier (`R14_Nora`)
- **Core (`A1`, `A2`):** `R14_Nora` executes 760 `A1 Register` and 610 `A2 Validate` events—the highest intake volume in the process.
- **Peripheral (None):** Zero events in `A3`–`A14`.
- **Complexity, Substitution & Authority:** `R14_Nora` is restricted to low-complexity cases (median `0.11`; zero high/critical cases) and holds no formal decision authority. In Sequence Fragment 2 (`R14_Nora:Register -> R01/R02:Validate`), when a case is non-trivial (medium/high complexity), human agents must perform `A2 Validate Entitlement` after `R14_Nora`'s registration. Thus, `R14_Nora`'s `A2` is an automated entitlement pre-check rather than authoritative validation.

### 2. Seasonal / Tier-0 Front-Office Support (`R11_Kai`, `R12_Lina`)
- **Core (`A1`, `A2`, `A4`, `A12`):** High-frequency execution of routine intake (`A1`: 180/170), entitlement checks (`A2`: 270/250), standard responses (`A4`: 220/205), and closures (`A12`: 190/180) on low-complexity cases (median `0.18`–`0.19`).
- **Peripheral (`A3`, `A8`):** Very rare diagnostic (8, 12) and customer negotiation (3, 4) touches when simple tickets unexpectedly surface minor questions before handoff.
- **Complexity, Substitution & Authority:** Although `A2 Validate Entitlement` is their single highest-count activity (520 combined events), **neither temp agent holds the `entitlement` certification**. Their high `A2` frequency reflects seasonal load offloading rather than certified authority, directly explaining the Weeks 8–10 quality failure where 17 closed cases reopened (`A13`) due to incomplete entitlement notes (Sequence Fragment 6).

### 3. Core Tier-1 Front-Office Service Generalists (`R01_Amira`, `R02_Ben`)
- **Core (`A1`, `A2`, `A4`, `A12`):** High-volume intake, certified entitlement validation, standard resolution, and closure (accounting for ~86% of `R01`'s and ~91% of `R02`'s total events).
- **Peripheral (`A3`, `A5`, `A7`, `A8`, `A10`, `A11`, `A13`, `A14`):** First-line diagnostic triage (`A3`: 75, 60), minor configuration/implementation (`A5`: 30, 18; `A10`: 12, 9), commercial pre-negotiation (`A7`: 18, 5; `A8`: 42, 25), and reopen/rework handling (`A13`: 8, 4).
- **Complexity, Substitution & Authority:** Both hold permanent contracts and `entitlement` certifications. Their peripheral activities arise from two mechanisms: (a) **boundary stretching on moderate cases** (especially `R01_Amira`, whose median complexity is `0.34` with 67 high/critical cases vs. `R02_Ben`'s `0.29` and 34 high/critical cases), where an experienced agent resolves minor technical or commercial issues without full escalation; and (b) **rework absorption** (Sequence Fragment 6), where `R01`/`R02` repair reopened temp-agent cases. Neither holds `approval`, `security`, or `pricing` certifications, so they never execute `A6` or `A9`.

### 4. Hybrid Senior Front-Office & Shadow Commercial Specialist (`R07_Giorgio`)
- **Core Dual Mandate (`A1`, `A2`, `A4`, `A12` + `A7`, `A8`):** `R07_Giorgio` has a genuine two-pillar core: senior front-office handling (`A1`=90, `A2`=190, `A4`=150, `A12`=130) and commercial exception/negotiation (`A7`=72, `A8`=88).
- **Peripheral (`A3`=40, `A9`=20, `A5`=12, `A10`=10, `A13`=10, `A11`=5, `A14`=3):** Diagnostic assessment on complex front-office cases (`0.58` median complexity) and occasional `A9 Supervisor Approval` (20 events) on mid-tier commercial concessions.
- **Complexity, Substitution & Authority:** Uniquely in the Front Office, `R07` holds **both `entitlement` and `pricing` certifications** (7.4y tenure). During `R06_Fatima`'s 6-day absence in Weeks 3–4, `R07` performed 31 `A7` and 18 `A8` events as a substitute; however, subtracting those leaves **41 `A7` and 70 `A8` events across the remaining 9 weeks**. Thus, `A8 Customer Negotiation` (explicitly shown in Fragment 4: `R07_Giorgio or R06_Fatima:Customer Negotiation`) and complex front-office `A7` reviews are structural parts of his hybrid role, whereas full-load `A7` ownership during `R06`'s absence is a temporary substitution where his lower specialization showed up as a 95% increase in median review duration (38 to 74 minutes).

### 5. Tiered Technical Engineering (`R04_Dina` [Tier-2] and `R03_Chen` [Tier-3])
- **Core (`A3`, `A5`, `A10`):** `A3 Diagnose` (260, 255), `A5 Configure` (240, 235), and `A10 Implement` (215, 220) constitute ~81–84% of both analysts' workload (Sequence Fragment 2).
- **Peripheral (`A1`, `A2`, `A4`, `A6`, `A8`, `A11`, `A12`, `A13`, `A14`):** Technical customer clarification (`A4`, `A8`), direct ticket closure (`A12`), KB documentation (`A14`), reopen diagnostics (`A13`), and—critically for `R03_Chen`—`A6 Security Review` (24 events vs. `R04`'s 6).
- **Complexity, Substitution & Authority:** Both hold `configuration` certifications, but `R03_Chen` handles high/critical complexity (`0.71` median, 222 high/critical cases) on the **late shift**, whereas `R04_Dina` handles standard technical work (`0.48` median, 91 high/critical cases) on the **day shift**. Because `R05_Erik` (Security Specialist) only works the day shift, `R03_Chen`'s 24 `A6` events represent late-shift security-adjacent technical reviews and co-execution on complex configurations rather than formal Risk-team sign-off authority.

- **Complexity, Substitution & Authority:** Certified in `pricing` with median case complexity `0.67` (130 high/critical cases). She is the authoritative owner of `A7`, achieving a 38-minute median service time that substitutes cannot match. However, for high-value/escalated exceptions, Fragment 4 shows she must still hand off to `R08_Hana` for final `A9 Supervisor Approval`.

### 7. Critical Risk & Security Intervention Specialist (`R05_Erik`)
- **Core (`A6`):** `A6 Security Review` (96 events, 56.5% of his total activity and 65.8% of all `A6` events company-wide).
- **Peripheral (`A10`=18, `A3`=16, `A5`=10, `A14`=9, `A11`=8, `A13`=6, `A8`=4, `A12`=3):** Deep security diagnostics (`A3`), secure configuration hardening (`A5`, `A10`), post-implementation security verification (`A11`), and security advisory updates (`A14`).
- **Complexity, Substitution & Authority:** Sole holder of the `security` certification, operating at a `0.86` median complexity (137 of 166 cases are high or critical). His peripheral `A3`/`A5`/`A10` events are not routine technical support; they are hands-on security remediations on critical incidents where standard technical analysts (`R03`/`R04`) lack risk clearance.

### 8. Quality Assurance & Knowledge Gatekeepers (`R09_Ivan`, `R10_Julia`)
- **Core (`A11`, `A14`):** `A11 Quality Check` (310, 298) and `A14 KB Update` (96, 104) account for ~75–77% of their events.
- **Peripheral (`A10`=38/35, `A12`=32/30, `A5`=26/22, `A3`=20/16, `A13`=12/8, `A4`=5/6, `A6`=4/0):** Post-QA ticket closure (`A12`), reopening failed implementations (`A13`), and direct corrective configuration/implementation (`A5`, `A10`).

### 9. Governance Authority (`R08_Hana`) vs. Escalation Coordination (`R13_Mateo`)
- **`R08_Hana` Core (`A9`=138) vs. Peripheral (`A13`=24, `A8`=22, `A2`=12, `A11`=12, `A3`=10, `A7`=8, `A12`=8, `A10`=6, `A4`=5):** `R08_Hana` holds the formal `approval` certification (Management team, `0.79` median complexity). Her peripheral activities represent executive intervention in disputed negotiations (`A8`), reopening defective closures (`A13`), and audit-level quality/pricing checks (`A11`, `A7`).
- **`R13_Mateo` Core Brokerage (`A13`=42, `A12`=52, `A2`=50, `A4`=45, `A9`=44, `A3`=35, `A8`=28, `A1`=25) vs. Peripheral Specialist Touches (`A11`=20, `A5`=18, `A14`=18, `A6`=16, `A10`=12, `A7`=10):** Certified in `escalation` (`0.77` median complexity, 76 critical cases). `R13_Mateo`'s core work is **cross-functional routing, reopen triage (`A13`), and case stabilization** (Fragments 3, 5, 6). # 3. Shadow and bridging roles

Shadow and bridging roles emerge organically to absorb bottlenecks, shift gaps, and handoff friction between siloed departments. They are **neither inherently improper nor purely formal**: they provide vital operational elasticity, but introduce hidden latency, compliance, and queuing risks when stretched beyond their design limits.

### 1. `R07_Giorgio` — Commercial Shadow Specialist & Front-Office Escalation Bridge
- **Structural Mechanism:** Formally titled a *Senior Service Agent* in the Front Office, `R07_Giorgio` holds both `entitlement` and `pricing` certifications. In steady state, he bridges Front Office and Commercial by absorbing `A8 Customer Negotiation` (88 events—nearly matching `R06_Fatima`'s 90) and routine/mid-complexity `A7 Pricing Exception Review` (41 non-substitution events), while during Weeks 3–4 he stepped in as full backup for `R06_Fatima` (31 `A7` and 18 `A8` events).
- **Operational Benefits:**
  - *Single-point-of-failure mitigation:* Prevents total stall of the commercial pipeline when `R06_Fatima` (the sole formal Commercial Specialist) is absent.
  - *Handoff compression:* Resolves bundled front-office + commercial requests in a single touch without cross-departmental queue delays, and offloads customer negotiation (`A8`) so `R06_Fatima` can focus on complex pricing structure analysis (`A7`).
- **Operational Risks:**
  - *Throughput and latency degradation under full substitution:* During Weeks 3–4, median `A7` duration nearly doubled from **38 minutes to 74 minutes** (+95%), proving that shadow familiarity does not equal primary specialist speed on complex commercial structures.
  - *Unclear approval boundaries:* `R07` logged 20 `A9 Supervisor Approval` events without holding an `approval` certification, creating potential audit ambiguity around delegated discount thresholds.

### 2. `R13_Mateo` — Cross-Functional Escalation Broker & Triage Gatekeeper
- **Structural Mechanism:** As *Incident Coordinator* (`escalation` cert), `R13_Mateo` spans every activity (`A1`–`A14`) and acts as the central hub in Sequence Fragments 3, 5, and 6—routing security reviews into implementation, triaging reopened cases (`A13`=42) to technical/commercial/security specialists, and acting as an emergency valve for `R08_Hana` (Week 5) and `R05_Erik` (Weeks 7–8).
- **Operational Benefits:**
  - *Cross-silo orchestration:* Prevents high/critical cases (76 critical cases—highest in the organization) from deadlocking when they require sequential technical, security, commercial, and management touches.
  - *Noise reduction for scarce specialists:* In Weeks 7–8, his pre-filtering of security referrals reduced false escalation complaints and protected `R05_Erik` from low-risk volume spikes.
  - *Continuity during management absence:* Kept cases moving in Week 5 via 21 preliminary `A9` approvals while `R08_Hana` attended an audit workshop.
- **Operational Risks:**
  - *Triage false negatives and critical queuing delays:* During the Weeks 7–8 `Enterprise Security` surge, `R13_Mateo`'s gatekeeping caused **two critical cases to wait >2 days** before reaching `R05_Erik`—showing the danger of a non-security-certified coordinator triaging high-risk technical referrals.
  - *Compliance rework:* In Week 5, **13 of his 21 preliminary `A9` approvals (62%) had to be rechecked by `R08_Hana`**, generating duplicate governance effort and potential policy exposure if preliminary approvals are acted upon before re-audit.

### 3. `R05_Erik` (with `R03_Chen`'s Late-Shift Bridge) — Rare Critical Specialist & Technical-Risk Boundary
- **Structural Mechanism:** `R05_Erik` is an ultra-low-volume, high-impact specialist (170 events, `0.86` median complexity) who intervenes on high/critical risk cases (`A6`=96) and directly executes security-critical diagnostics and implementation (`A3`=16, `A5`=10, `A10`=18). At his boundary, `R03_Chen` (24 `A6` events on the late shift) and `R13_Mateo` (16 `A6` events during triage) act as informal security-adjacent bridges.
- **Operational Benefits:**
  - *Deep risk containment:* Concentrates high-stakes security architecture and vulnerability review in a highly tenured (6.8y), certified specialist.
  - *24-hour operational continuity via `R03_Chen`:* Because `R05_Erik` works only the day shift, `R03_Chen`'s late-shift security-adjacent reviews prevent late-shift technical deployments from stalling overnight on routine security checks.
- **Operational Risks:**
  - *Extreme bus-factor bottleneck:* `R05_Erik` has no certified peer in the Risk team. Any surge (such as Weeks 7–8) or absence immediately forces uncertified filtering (`R13_Mateo`) or uncertified technical sign-off (`R03_Chen`, `R04_Dina`, `R09_Ivan`—who collectively logged 46 `A6` events without `security` certification).

### 4. Additional Boundary & Shadow Roles
- **Automation-to-Human Precheck Boundary (`R14_Nora` $\to$ `R01`/`R02` vs. `R11`/`R12`):** `R14_Nora` automates `A1` and pre-check `A2` for low-complexity requests (`0.11` median). When `R14_Nora` hands off to certified agents (`R01`/`R02` in Fragment 2), they re-validate `A2` for complex entitlements. However, when `R14_Nora` or direct intake flows to uncertified temps (`R11_Kai`, `R12_Lina`), the temps act as **shadow entitlement validators** (520 `A2` events without certification). *Benefit:* Offloads 1,687 routine events during seasonal peaks. *Risk:* High defect escape—17 reopened cases in Weeks 8–10 traced to incomplete `A2` notes, creating downstream rework for `R01`, `R02`, and `R13` (Fragment 6).
- **Quality Gatekeeping & Shadow Remediation (`R09_Ivan`, `R10_Julia`):** Beyond formal inspection (`A11`) and documentation (`A14`), `R09` and `R10` perform 48 `A5 Configure` and 73 `A10 Implement` events without `configuration` certification. *Benefit:* Fast inline remediation ("fix-on-check") avoids round-trip handoff delays back to Technical Analysts for minor configuration tweaks. *Risk:* Blurs segregation of duties between implementer and quality auditor if QA analysts modify production configurations they then self-approve.

# 4. Ambiguous assignments and alternative interpretations

Because event counts aggregate normal operations, shift handoffs, and temporary exceptions, several resources admit multiple plausible structural interpretations:

### 1. `R07_Giorgio` (Senior Service Agent, Front Office)
- **Interpretation A — Formal Hybrid / Dual-Hat Role (Front Office + Junior Commercial Specialist):** `R07` is intentionally staffed as a permanent cross-functional resource who handles Tier-2 front-office cases and owns standard customer price negotiations (`A8`) and Tier-1 pricing exceptions (`A7`), leaving only complex commercial structuring to `R06_Fatima`.
- **Interpretation B — Front-Office Lead Performing Ad-Hoc Emergency Commercial Coverage:** `R07`'s true formal role is strictly Senior Front-Office Agent; his `A7`/`A8` events are spillover artifacts from `R06_Fatima`'s queue saturation and absence (Weeks 3–4).
- **Deciding Evidence:**

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

The following Python pipeline demonstrates how to run PM4Py's organizational mining discovery functions while enriching the flat activity-frequency output with **complexity stratification**, **substitution regime separation**, **service-time inflation checks**, and **handover/subcontracting verification** so that activity similarity is never conflated with formal authority.

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py


def analyze_organizational_roles_and_shadow_structures(raw_df: pd.DataFrame) -> dict:
    """
    End-to-end PM4Py pipeline that discovers organizational roles, resource similarity,
    and social networks while controlling for case_complexity, temporary_substitution,
    escalation_level, and service-time distortion.
    """
    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # 1. Format DataFrame for PM4Py and compute service / waiting times
    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        raw_df.copy(),
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        timest_format=None,
    )
    # Ensure resource and contextual columns are preserved
    df = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df)
    service_times_by_activity = pm4py.get_service_time(df)

    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # 2. Naive Baseline: Discover Organizational Roles & Activity Similarity
    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # pm4py.discover_organizational_roles groups activities with similar resource
    # profiles; pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity computes
    # pairwise similarity over activity-resource frequency vectors.
    naive_roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(
        df,
        resource_key="org:resource",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    )
    resource_similarity = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(
        df,
        resource_key="org:resource",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    )

    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # 3. Separate Normal Operations from Temporary Substitution Episodes
    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    df_normal = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        df,
        attribute_key="temporary_substitution",
        values=[False, 0, "False", "no"],
        level="event",
        retain=True,
    )
    df_subst = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        df,
        attribute_key="temporary_substitution",
        values=[True, 1, "True", "yes"],
        level="event",
        retain=True,
    )

    # Re-run similarity on normal-regime events only to see if shadow overlap
    # (e.g., R07_Giorgio vs R06_Fatima) persists outside substitution windows
    normal_similarity = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(
        df_normal,
        resource_key="org:resource",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    )

    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # 4. Compute Social Networks: Handover, Working-Together, Subcontracting
    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # Handover of work reveals sequential routing hubs (e.g., R14_Nora -> Front Office,
    # R13_Mateo -> Specialists, Specialists -> R08_Hana)
    hw_net = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
        df,
        resource_key="org:resource",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    )
    # Working together captures case co-participation (e.g., R03_Chen + R09_Ivan on late shift)
    wt_net = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(
        df,
        resource_key="org:resource",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    )
    # Subcontracting (in-between work A -> B -> A) detects preliminary approval
    # rechecks (e.g., R08_Hana / R13_Mateo / R08_Hana) and QA rework loops
    sub_net = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(
        df,
        resource_key="org:resource",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    )

    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # 5. Join Role Discovery Output with Case Complexity, Substitution & Authority
    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # Map each activity to its mined PM4Py organizational role ID
    activity_to_role_id = {}
    for role_idx, role_entry in enumerate(naive_roles):
        # In PM4Py, each role entry contains the list of activities and resource counts
        activities = role_entry[0] if isinstance(role_entry, (list, tuple)) else getattr(role_entry, "activities", [])
        for act in activities:
            activity_to_role_id[act] = f"MinedRole_{role_idx:02d}"

    df["mined_role_id"] = df["concept:name"].map(activity_to_role_id)

    # Compute resource-level enriched profile combining activity counts,
    # complexity distribution, substitution share, escalation level, and service duration
    df["event_duration_min"] = (
        df.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"]
        .diff()
        .dt.total_seconds()
        .div(60.0)
    )

    resource_enriched = (
        df.groupby("org:resource")
        .agg(
            total_events=("concept:name", "count"),
            unique_activities=("concept:name", "nunique"),
            median_complexity=("case_complexity", "median"),
            p90_complexity=("case_complexity", lambda s: np.percentile(s.dropna(), 90)),
            high_crit_event_share=("case_complexity", lambda s: (s >= 0.65).mean()),
            substitution_event_share=(
                "temporary_substitution",
                lambda s: s.isin([True, 1, "True", "yes"]).mean(),
            ),
            mean_escalation_level=("escalation_level", "mean"),
        )
        .reset_index()
    )

    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # 6. Guardrails Against Conflating Activity Frequency with Formal Authority
    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # Guardrail A: Detect competence/speed gap during substitution (e.g. R07 vs R06 on A7)
    a7_comparison = (
        df[df["concept:name"] == "A7. Pricing Exception Review"]
        .groupby(["org:resource", "temporary_substitution"])["event_duration_min"]
        .agg(event_count="count", median_duration_min="median")
        .reset_index()
    )

    # Guardrail B: Detect preliminary vs. binding authority on A9 Supervisor Approval
    # Check how often an A9 event by resource X is followed by a second A9 or A13 by R08_Hana
    a9_events = df[df["concept:name"] == "A9. Supervisor Approval"].sort_values(
        ["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"]
    )
    a9_events["next_a9_resource"] = a9_events.groupby("case:concept:name")[
        "org:resource"
    ].shift(-1)
    a9_recheck_audit = (
        a9_events.groupby("org:resource")
        .agg(
            total_a9=("case:concept:name", "count"),
            rechecked_by_hana=(
                "next_a9_resource",
                lambda s: (s == "R08_Hana").sum(),
            ),
        )
        .assign(recheck_rate=lambda x: x["rechecked_by_hana"] / x["total_a9"])
        .reset_index()
    )

    # Guardrail C: Cluster log by case complexity & escalation variants to verify
    # whether similar activity profiles (e.g. R03_Chen vs R04_Dina, R01 vs R11)
    # operate on structurally different trace variants
    high_complexity_df = df[df["case_complexity"] >= 0.70]
    high_comp_variants = pm4py.get_variants(
        high_complexity_df,
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    )
    clustered_sublogs = list(
        pm4py.cluster_log(
            df,
            case_id_key="case:concept:name",
            activity_key="concept:name",
            timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        )
    )

    return {
        "naive_roles": naive_roles,
        "resource_similarity_all": resource_similarity,
        "resource_similarity_normal": normal_similarity,
        "handover_network": hw_net,
        "working_together_network": wt_net,
        "subcontracting_network": sub_net,
        "service_times_by_activity": service_times_by_activity,
        "resource_enriched_profile": resource_enriched,
        "pricing_substitution_audit": a7_comparison,
        "supervisor_approval_recheck_audit": a9_recheck_audit,
        "high_complexity_variants": high_comp_variants,
        "clustered_sublogs_count": len(clustered_sublogs),
    }
```

# 6. Operational interpretation

Management should translate this multi-layered role structure into targeted interventions across four operational levers:

### 1. Staffing & Shift Architecture
- **Eliminate Single-Resource Bottlenecks in Risk (`R05_Erik`) and Commercial (`R06_Fatima`):** Both `R05_Erik` (170 events, `0.86` complexity) and `R06_Fatima` (291 events, `0.67` complexity) are single points of failure on the day shift. Management should formalize a fractional deputy structure—formally allocating ~25–30% of `R07_Giorgio`'s capacity as Commercial Deputy and certifying `R03_Chen` (plus a day-shift technical analyst) as Tier-1 Security Reviewers for standard security checks.
- **Align Shift Staffing with Complexity:** Currently, the late shift relies on `R03_Chen` (Senior Technical), `R09_Ivan` (Quality), and `R12_Lina` (uncertified Temp), with **no certified Front-Office entitlement specialist (`R01`/`R02`/`R07`), no Security Specialist (`R05`), and no Supervisor (`R08`)**. Late-shift complex intake should either be routed to a certified permanent front-office agent or held after `R14_Nora`'s pre-check until day-shift validation.

### 2. Targeted Training & Certification Up-Skilling
- **Close `R07_Giorgio`'s Commercial Speed Gap:** Although `R07_Giorgio` holds a `pricing` certification, his median `A7` review time nearly doubled (38m $\to$ 74m) during `R06_Fatima`'s absence. Targeted training on complex pricing calculators, playbook standardization, and paired reviews with `R06_Fatima` will reduce his substitution latency penalty.
- **Formalize Security-Adjacent Triage Training for `R03_Chen` and `R13_Mateo`:** Since `R03_Chen` already performs 24 `A6` events on the late shift and `R13_Mateo` screens security referrals during incident surges, both should receive structured security-triage certification and explicit checklists so that low-risk referrals can be filtered **without** delaying critical cases (preventing the >2-day wait seen in Weeks 7–8).
- **Develop `R04_Dina` and `R01_Amira` Along Explicit Tier Progression Paths:** Pair `R04_Dina` (`0.48` complexity) with `R03_Chen` (`0.71` complexity) on high-complexity configurations, and cross-train `R01_Amira` (`0.34` complexity, 18 `A7` and 42 `A8` events) toward `pricing` certification so she can back up `R07_Giorgio`.

### 3. Risk Control & Governance Guardrails
- **Hard-Gate `A2 Validate Entitlement` on Non-Routine Cases for Temp Agents (`R11_Kai`, `R12_Lina`):** Because `R11` and `R12` lack `entitlement` certification and caused 17 reopenings in Weeks 8–10 due to incomplete validation notes, workflow rules should restrict them to closing cases where `R14_Nora`'s automated entitlement check passes with high confidence, or require mandatory structured form fields / `R01`/`R02` sign-off before closure.

  - Separate QA inline fixes (`R09`/`R10` performing `A5`/`A10`) into a distinct activity (`A11b Minor QA Remediation`) with a rule that substantive configuration changes require independent technical or peer QA verification.

### 4. Organizational Resilience
- **Preserve and Support `R13_Mateo`'s Brokerage Role:** `R13_Mateo` is the primary shock absorber for reopened (`A13`=42) and critical cases (76 critical cases). Rather than forcing him into a specialized silo or overloading him with manual approval/security execution during absences, give him explicit routing playbooks and priority SLA escalation triggers that bypass standard queues when `case_complexity > 0.75`.

### 5. Two Critical Cautions About Changing Staffing Based Only on Mined Roles
1. **Caution 1 — Do Not Treat High Activity Similarity or Substitution Volume as Proof of Interchangeable Competence or Authority:**
   A process owner looking only at cosine similarity (`R03_Chen`–`R04_Dina` = `0.94`; `R01_Amira`–`R11_Kai` $\approx$ high front-office overlap) or substitution event counts (`R07_Giorgio` performing 31 `A7` events; `R13_Mateo` performing 21 `A9` events) might conclude that `R04_Dina` can replace `R03_Chen` on critical late-shift incidents, that cheap temporary staff (`R11`/`R12`) can replace `R01`/`R02` on entitlement validation, or that `R13_Mateo` makes `R08_Hana` redundant. In reality, doing so would spike reopen rates (as seen in Weeks 8–10), double pricing review cycle times (38m $\to$ 74m in Weeks 3–4), and break governance compliance (where 62% of `R13_Mateo`'s preliminary approvals required `R08_Hana`'s recheck).
2. **Caution 2 — Do Not Eliminate Low-Volume Specialists or "Noisy" Generalist Brokers to Optimize Utilization Metrics:**
   In an activity-frequency matrix, `R05_Erik` appears underutilized (only 170 events across 10 weeks, ~17 events/week) and `R13_Mateo` appears unfocused (small event counts scattered across all 14 activities). Cutting `R05_Erik`'s role or merging it into general technical support would strip the organization of its sole certified risk specialist on `0.86`-complexity cases, while forcing `R13_Mateo` into a standard front-office or supervisor box would sever the cross-functional coordination tissue that resolves reopens and multi-department escalations.
