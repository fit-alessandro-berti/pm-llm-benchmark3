## 2. Process-model consistency checks

A concrete validation suite should combine event-log replay, process conformance, feature-lineage tests, and explanation-feasibility checks.

- **Prefix-time availability gate.** Define `t0` as the first `Coverage Check Completed` event using both timestamp and a reliable sequence index where available. Build a prefix snapshot containing only events with `(event_time < t0)` or `(event_time = t0 and sequence_index <= coverage_check_sequence)`. Every feature must have a provenance test proving that its source events/snapshots satisfy this predicate at prediction time. Test known failures: `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_handler_team`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`, `risk_score_current`, and `last_resource_role` should fail unless properly versioned.
- **Prefix-only recomputation.** Recompute features from the prefix event log and compare with the feature store. Any aggregate over the complete trace must be blocked. Sequence explainer patterns should be allowed only when every event in the pattern is contained in the prefix. This detects full-trace SHAP and sequence-pattern leakage.
- **Process feasibility and alignment checks.** Use alignment against the simplified process model and declarative/constraint checks: coverage before damage assessment; damage assessment before settlement; settlement before closure; appeal only after Partial/Deny; second settlement decision only after an appeal; fraud investigation only after a trigger and possibly in parallel. Alignment helps locate deviations, but it is not infallible: real deviations occur, and alignment can fit noisy behavior. A frequent DFG edge is exploratory and is **not** automatically a normative rule.
- **Explanation feasibility check.** For every explanation item, classify any referenced activity as: already observed in prefix, future-possible, not enabled at `t0`, or process-impossible. At immediate post-coverage `t0`, `Appeal`, `Settlement Decision`, `Claim Closed`, `final_decision`, and complete-trace variants are not admissible drivers. `Fraud Investigation Opened` and `Documents Requested` are future-possible only if their triggers/events occur later; they are not observed drivers at `t0`.
- **Same-timestamp ambiguity handling.** If two events share a timestamp and the ingestion sequence index is missing or unreliable, do not infer order. Flag the pattern as ambiguous, exclude it from explanations, or compute contributions under all plausible orderings and report a range. The `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` case in `Case Z` is a critical example: the edge is both future and process-inconsistent/ambiguous.
- **Repeated settlement semantics.** Validate that multiple `Settlement Decision Recorded` events are allowed only when a prior decision was Partial or Deny and an Appeal occurs between the first and reconsidered decision. Same-timestamp decisions or repeated decisions without appeal should be flagged as data-quality or process-consistency issues rather than interpreted causally.
- **Regime and drift checks.** For `calendar_week`, holiday/policy-period flags, queue snapshots, and document snapshots, test stability over time. Calendar features may be useful for drift monitoring but should not be hard-coded as causal operational rules.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

- **Defensible predictive signal.** Examples: `initial_damage_complexity`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, and prefix-observed process state such as outstanding documents if actually observed by `t0`. These may be claimed as “available at `t0` and associated with breach risk under the validated model.” They may not be claimed as causal effects or as proof that changing the feature would change breach risk. Operational actionability must be assessed separately.
- **Process-state or congestion signal.** Examples: `queue_length_at_t0`, prefix backlog, current resource assignment if versioned, and already-observed evidence-cycle status. These may be claimed as current workload/process-state associations and potential operational levers only after causal or experimental validation. They may not be claimed as individual culprits or stable causal laws; queue snapshots also require latency discipline.
- **Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review.** Examples: `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0` after repair, `initial_reserve` after lineage repair, and to some extent `initial_damage_complexity`. These may be used only if predictive validity, calibration, robustness, and fairness impact are established. They may not be used as causal explanations, nor to justify adverse or differential treatment without explicit policy and fairness review.
- **Drift/calendar proxy.** Examples: `calendar_week`, holiday periods, policy-change periods, and possibly same-day document-volume snapshots after repair. These may be claimed as temporal/regime associations useful for monitoring or capacity planning. They may not be claimed as stable causal drivers; week 52 being important may reflect holiday staffing, batching, or policy changes.
- **Unusable until lineage is repaired.** Examples: `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_handler_team`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, current `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, current `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, `final_decision`, current `risk_score_current`, `last_resource_role`, and employee experience joined by extraction date. These currently support no admissible early claim. Some can be repaired into prefix-only or as-of features; others, such as final outcomes and complete-trace appeal status, are not usable at the original `t0` by definition.
