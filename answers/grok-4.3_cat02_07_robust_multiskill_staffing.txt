# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables
Demand is taken directly from the supplied two-hour arrival blocks and type percentages; service times are drawn uniformly from the stated ranges (ordinary or complex-tail) and then multiplied by the resource-specific speed factor. Complex-tail probability is applied independently per diagnostic activity. ME and ES cases are modeled with a policy-controlled binary choice: parallel execution incurs the stated 8-minute coordination delay but shortens the critical path; sequential execution avoids coordination but adds the second branch duration. Handover (7 min) is incurred on every resource change; cross-team handover (additional 5 min) occurs unless a single cross-skilled resource retains the case; skill-family setup (5 min) occurs on consecutive cases requiring different diagnostic families. Preemption is limited to one occurrence per case and adds a 10 % restart penalty on remaining service time.  

Two explicit objective formulations are used:  
- Objective A (risk-weighted): minimize 0.6·(weighted SLA violation probability) + 0.4·(mean total delay), where SLA violation is 1P1 attainment below 90 % within 120 min, etc.  
- Objective B (fairness-robust): minimize the maximum of (mean delay, 95th-percentile delay, max resource utilization), subject to utilization fairness (maxmin utilization across qualified resources)  0.15 and planned utilization  0.90 in the base scenario.

# 2. Capacity and offered-load analysis
Base offered load (person-minutes) per two-hour block is obtained by multiplying expected arrivals by type mix, expected service minutes (mid-range of ordinary/complex-tail weighted by probability), and average speed multiplier of the eligible resources. Mechanical load peaks 14:00-16:00 (~210 person-min); electrical/software load is highest 12:00-16:00. Under U1 the morning blocks rise 70 % with +8 pp P1 share, pushing Intake and Ada/Ben utilization above 95 %. U2 doubles E/ES arrivals 12:00-16:00, shifting bottleneck from Mechanical to Electrical/Chen-Emil. U3 removes Chen (25 % of electrical/software capacity), creating a persistent electrical bottleneck after 12:00. U4 doubles complex-tail probabilities, inflating diagnostic load 12-18 %. U5 combines U1 surge with Chen absence and a 30-minute Intake blackout, producing a transient queue spike 09:30-10:30 that migrates to Validation after 12:00.

# 3. Candidate policies
Policy S (strict specialization): Intake assigned to earliest-shift Intake-qualified resource; each diagnostic branch routed only to resources whose speed multiplier  1.10 for that skill; Validation likewise restricted; no overflow; parallel ME/ES only when both branches have dedicated resources free.  
Policy P (full pooling): Any resource qualified for the required skill family is eligible; dispatch uses “earliest available after handover/setup” with transparent tie-break by resource ID; parallel ME/ES chosen if both branches can start within 10 min, otherwise sequential; P1 cases preempt at most once.  
Policy H (hybrid reservation): 30 % of each resource’s shift capacity is reserved for P1 arrivals; P1 cases may use reserved slots or overflow to pooled queue; non-P1 cases use only non-reserved capacity plus controlled overflow when pooled P1 queue length < 2; parallelization threshold identical to P.

# 5. Robust recommendation
Policy H is the primary recommendation under both objectives because it keeps P1 SLA attainment  0.91 across all five uncertainty scenarios while limiting max utilization to  0.94 and fairness gap  0.15. It dominates S on tail risk and fairness and dominates P on resilience and preemption cost. Switch to Policy P when observed P1 arrival rate exceeds 1.5× base for two consecutive blocks (trigger measured by 30-minute rolling count); revert when the rolling count falls below 1.2× base for one block. Policy S remains preferable only when cross-training is unavailable or when audit requirements forbid any overflow.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks
P3 cases may experience starvation under H when reservation is set too high; a dynamic reservation floor of 20 % mitigates this. Skill erosion is limited because H still routes the majority of ordinary cases to primary-skilled resources. Excessive preemption is capped at one per case by design. Coordination overhead at Validation rises under parallel ME/ES; the policy therefore defaults to sequential when estimated critical-path saving < 12 min. Human interpretability is preserved by publishing the exact eligibility matrix and reservation percentage daily.

# 7. Monitoring and process-mining design
Required event attributes: queue-entry timestamp, assignment decision timestamp, eligible-resource set (list), skill family used, planned vs actual service time, pause/resume flags, handover delay, setup delay, policy version, scenario/shock flag, SLA clock start. PM4Py analyses: `discover_handover_of_work_network` on `org:resource` to quantify handover burden; `insert_case_service_waiting_time` and stage-specific filtering to separate Intake, diagnostic, and Validation queues; `get_service_time` grouped by resource and skill to detect drift; `discover_bottlenecks` (or variant) on time-block subsets to track migration under shocks.

# 9. Limits and validation plan
Expected arrivals and service ranges alone cannot predict exact queue lengths or the interaction of preemption with parallel branches under non-stationary demand. A 10-day pilot on one shift is required: run H with live P1 protection, collect the defined event attributes, compare observed vs predicted SLA attainment and utilization fairness; if P1 SLA drops below 0.90 for any two-hour block, fall back to S for that block only. Backtest on archived tickets (minimum 500 cases) with frozen policy parameters before any wider rollout.
