# 1. Current-process diagnosis

## Dominant path and the speed-versus-quality contradiction

The **frequency DFG** shows a dominant fast corridor:

- `Intake claim  Auto eligibility screen`: 3,600 cases
- `Auto eligibility screen  Fast approval`: 2,550
- `Fast approval  Dispatch replacement`: 2,300
- `Dispatch replacement  Quality/compliance close`: 2,320

These are directly-follows counts rather than a single exact variant—loops and entries from escalation mean the edge counts should not be multiplied into a path probability—but they clearly establish that auto-screening, fast approval, and replacement dispatch drive the process.

The **performance DFG** explains its appeal. Median edge times are only 0.5 hours for intake-to-auto screen, 0.4 hours for auto-to-fast approval, 1.8 hours for approval-to-dispatch, and 3.2 hours for dispatch-to-close. Auto-screening also costs only EUR 0.20 per case.

However, that apparent advantage partly reflects where measurement stops:

- Complaints occur much later: median 50 hours after Fast approval and 70 hours after dispatch, with p90 values of 150 and 180 hours.
- Once reopened, the median wait to Manual eligibility review is another 31 hours; Escalation board takes 42 hours, with p90 values of 100 and 130 hours.
- Fast-approval cases have a 14.1% complaint/reopen rate, versus 3.3% for the quote-approved repair path.
- Fast approval is 31% cheaper before rework but only 9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments, and service credits.

Thus, the dominant path is genuinely fast and cheap for many first-pass claims, but it also **externalizes failure beyond nominal closure**. Optimizing only time-to-first-close would reward the path most associated with delayed complaints and compliance exceptions.

## Performance and queueing problems

The reviewed and evidence-heavy paths have severe tail latency:

- `Request missing evidence  Manual eligibility review`: mean 34 hours, median 26, p90 88.
- `Manual eligibility review  Repair quote`: mean 22 hours, median 15, p90 60.
- `Repair quote  Customer approval`: mean 30 hours, median 21, p90 80.
- `Manual eligibility review  Fraud/risk review`: p90 44 hours.

The **temporal-profile findings** specifically identify Manual review-to-Repair quote as too slow for Premium cases. The delay is not simply “careful work”: repair-quote delay is largely waiting for part-price confirmation, while manual and fraud queues have known utilization and calendar effects.

The evidence loop is especially damaging. The DFG contains `Manual eligibility review  Request missing evidence` 780 times and `Request missing evidence  Manual eligibility review` 890 times. Among cases entering evidence completion, 26% receive at least two requests. The unclear/missing-evidence segment consequently has a 140-hour median cycle and the highest abandonment.

## Quality and rework problems

Quality failures differ by resolution mode and product risk:

- Overall complaint/reopen is 9.2%, but 14.1% for Fast approval and 3.3% for the completed repair path.
- The **temporal-profile deviation** from dispatch to complaint is linked to incorrect replacement-part selection.
- A wrong replacement has expected cost of EUR 410 for a high-value device, versus EUR 95 for a low-risk accessory.
- Repair is not universally superior: high-value repaired devices have 12.5% rework, versus 4.0% for low-risk repaired devices. The DFG’s `Quality check  Rework repair  Repair device` loop confirms that the reviewed path also contains quality leakage.

The implication is not “replace fast approval with repair.” It is to distinguish claims where fast fulfillment is reversible and inexpensive from those where a wrong technical decision is costly.

## Compliance and model-control problems

Several process-mining artifacts show that aggregate fitness masks important policy breaches:

- **Token-based replay fitness is 0.91**, but only **68% of traces fit** the normative Petri net.
- **Alignment diagnostics** identify Fast approval before evidence completeness, complaint/reopen after close, and replacement dispatch without required review for high-value devices.
- These directly threaten R1 and R2. The reopen lifecycle also fails to reliably enforce R3 before re-close or denial.
- The discovered model has **low precision**, because low-frequency combinations of auto approval, risk review, and repair permit behavior not allowed by policy.

A high token fitness score should therefore not be interpreted as acceptable compliance. Nor should the low-precision discovered model simply be adopted as the new normative model; that would legitimize observed exceptions rather than remove them.

## Segment-specific customer impact

A blanket shift to manual review would be harmful:

- **Premium low-risk accessory:** fast median 8 hours versus 46 manual; exceeding 24 hours creates high service-credit exposure. The complaint difference is only 6% versus 3%, and R2 explicitly permits an evidence exception for this group.
- **Standard low-risk accessory:** fast median 13 hours versus 52 manual, with complaints of 8% versus 4%. These customers tolerate up to 72 hours if status is visible, creating some room for a more controlled process.
- **Premium high-value device:** fast median 20 hours but 22% reopen, versus 96 hours and 5% on the reviewed/repair path.
- **Standard high-value device:** fast median 28 hours and 18% reopen, versus 120 hours and 6% reviewed.
- **Unclear defect/missing evidence:** 22% of cases, 140-hour median, repeated requests, and high abandonment.

The quality benefit of review is strongest for high-value devices, while the speed penalty is least acceptable for Premium low-risk accessories.

## Resource constraints

- Seven manual reviewers provide 252 productive hours per week in total, and queues rise sharply above 82% utilization.
- Updating auto-screen rules may increase manual inflow by 15–35%, so indiscriminate tightening could turn compliance improvement into a large queue.
- Two fraud specialists are already a Monday and post-campaign bottleneck.
- Three repair-quote technicians are constrained mainly by part-price confirmation.
- Two quality inspectors are underused outside repair campaigns, creating a possible targeted control resource.
- The daily 45-minute Escalation board improves satisfaction but cannot absorb systematic routing failures.

The redesigns below therefore share only the non-negotiable enforcement of R1–R3. Their main operating mechanisms are materially different.

# 2. Redesign A: Risk-tiered compliant straight-through lanes

## Redesigned process flow

1. **Intake captures and validates routing attributes:** customer segment, claim value, device class, defect type, serial/SKU, evidence status, and risk indicators.
2. An updated, segment-aware auto screen assigns one of three lanes:
   - **Premium low-risk accessory lane:** if the claim is at or below EUR 400 and has no risk trigger, auto eligibility and Fast approval remain available. Dispatch may use the R2 evidence exception.
   - **Standard low-risk lane:** incomplete claims go to evidence completion; complete claims can proceed from auto eligibility to Fast approval and dispatch.
   - **High-value, above-EUR-400, or elevated-risk lane:** evidence is completed, followed by either Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review before approval. Both reviews are used only where separate triggers justify both.
3. The workflow engine creates explicit control states:
   - No above-EUR-400 Fast approval without a recorded manual or risk-review completion.
   - No replacement dispatch without evidence completeness, except for the authorized Premium low-risk exception.
   - Any complaint reactivates the claim or a linked continuation and forces Manual eligibility review before subsequent close or denial.
4. Technical decision leads to replacement or repair, followed by the applicable quality/compliance close.

## Target

- Complete, classifiable low-risk claims where speed has customer value.
- High-value and above-EUR-400 claims currently reaching Fast approval or dispatch without required review.
- Unauthorized combinations identified by the low-precision discovered model.
- Premium low-risk accessories that should not be forced into the much slower manual path.

## Mechanism of improvement

- It retains the EUR 0.20 auto screen for claims where human review adds little expected value.
- Segment-, value-, and defect-aware rules remove the current one-size-fits-all auto decision.
- Hard workflow states address the specific alignment deviations rather than merely reporting them after the fact.
- Selective review concentrates scarce human capacity where complaint rates and wrong-replacement costs are highest.
- It should preserve the 8-hour Premium low-risk experience while trading some high-value first-pass speed for fewer reopens and stronger compliance.
- Constraining allowed paths should improve both fitting-trace percentage and model precision, provided the normative model is not broadened to hide exceptions.

## Deliberate sacrifices and unresolved issues

- High-value claims will be slower than the current unsafe 20–28-hour fast paths.
- Manual and fraud inflow will rise; the estimated 15–35% increase could push manual utilization beyond 82%.
- The redesign does not materially improve the evidence-submission experience or eliminate repeated evidence requests.
- Review before approval does not by itself solve incorrect SKU/part selection or the 12.5% high-value repair-rework rate.
- Attribute errors could cause systematic misrouting, so the design depends on accurate value, segment, and defect data.

# 3. Redesign B: Evidence-first completion with assisted channels

## Redesigned process flow

1. Intake and a lightweight eligibility pre-screen generate a **claim-specific evidence manifest** based on device, defect, value, purchase record, and channel.
2. Evidence already available from warranty registration, serial records, or prior uploads is reused rather than requested again.
3. If evidence is incomplete, the customer receives one consolidated request containing:
   - Missing items and why they are needed.
   - Examples of acceptable evidence.
   - Real-time format and completeness validation.
   - Save/resume, reminders, and visible claim status.
4. Customers can use a digital portal or an assisted phone, retail, or agent-upload channel. The Premium low-risk R2 exception is not unnecessarily held for documentation.
5. The process does not repeatedly send the claim through Manual eligibility review after each upload. Once the manifest is complete, it proceeds to one eligibility decision and then technical decision.
6. A second evidence request requires a reason code and supervisory validation, making avoidable loops visible.
7. Evidence-independent risk triage may run in parallel, but approval and dispatch remain blocked until R1 and R2 conditions are met. Reopens remain subject to R3.

## Target

- The 22% unclear-defect/missing-evidence cohort.
- Claims following `Manual review  Request evidence  Manual review`.
- Cases with two or more evidence requests, currently 26% of evidence-entering cases.
- Standard customers who can tolerate up to 72 hours if they receive credible status visibility.
- Channels with high evidence-related abandonment, using assisted rather than mandatory digital service where needed.

## Mechanism of improvement

- It attacks the 34-hour mean and 88-hour p90 delay between evidence request and manual review.
- A single, validated manifest should reduce repeated requests by the portal’s estimated 30–45% range, subject to pilot confirmation.
- Moving document checking away from scarce eligibility reviewers reduces avoidable manual touches and may release capacity for Redesign A’s mandatory high-risk reviews.
- Status visibility improves the Standard-customer experience even when the underlying response time cannot be instantaneous.
- A recorded evidence-complete state directly supports R2 and gives auditors traceable evidence lineage.

## Deliberate sacrifices and unresolved issues

- Intake may take longer, and a more demanding portal could increase abandonment among customers with low digital literacy.
- Assisted channels reduce that risk but add staffing and operating cost.
- “Complete” evidence can still be incorrect or misleading; automated validation can create false confidence.
- This design does not modernize the substantive auto-approval logic beyond mandatory policy controls.
- It does not directly address wrong replacement selection, repair rework, repair-quote pricing delays, or the poor scalability of the Escalation board.

# 4. Redesign C: High-value first-time-right resolution cell

## Redesigned process flow

1. High-value devices enter a dedicated resolution cell after evidence completeness.
2. A single case owner coordinates:
   - Mandatory manual or fraud/risk review.
   - Technical diagnosis using defect, serial, configuration, and remote or bench diagnostics.
   - Customer communication and service commitment.
3. The cell uses current part-price catalogs or a pricing service rather than waiting for ad hoc confirmation. At intake, the customer may give consent for repair within a stated limit; quotes outside that limit still require explicit approval.
4. The technical decision can be repair or replacement:
   - Repair is selected where diagnosis and economics support it—not as a universal default.
   - Replacement is selected only after a serial/model/BOM compatibility check.
5. A lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate is applied specifically to high-value replacements, using quality inspectors where possible.
6. High-value repairs retain independent quality checking and use defect-specific test protocols. Rework is fed back by technician, part, and defect type.
7. Premium cases receive priority decision slots and proactive milestones. Only genuine policy exceptions go to the daily Escalation board.
8. Quality/compliance close occurs after fulfillment verification; any reopen returns to mandatory Manual eligibility review.

## Target

- Premium and Standard high-value devices, together representing 35% of cases.
- High-value claims on the fast path, with 22% and 18% complaint rates.
- Long-tail complaints linked to incorrect replacement-part selection.
- The 12.5% high-value repair-rework rate.
- The delays on Manual review-to-Repair quote and Repair quote-to-Customer approval.

## Mechanism of improvement

- It gains speed by removing handoffs and part-price waiting from the compliant reviewed path, rather than by skipping review.
- It postpones an expensive, hard-to-reverse dispatch until technical compatibility is checked.
- The 6–18-hour quality gate is targeted where a wrong replacement has an expected cost of EUR 410; applying it universally to EUR 95 accessory risks would be harder to justify.
- One case owner reduces repeated queue entries and prevents routine cases from reaching the one-slot-per-day Escalation board.
- Better diagnostics and feedback should reduce both incorrect replacement and repeated repair, although the size of the effect cannot be established from the current data.

## Deliberate sacrifices and unresolved issues

- It is unlikely to match the unsafe 20–28-hour high-value fast-path median.
- The cell has higher direct labor and coordination costs.
- A poorly designed pre-dispatch gate could add the full 18 hours without catching enough errors.
- Dedicated capacity may be difficult with only two fraud specialists, three quote technicians, and two quality inspectors.
- It does little for low-risk accessories or the cross-segment evidence problem.
- Customers who strongly prefer replacement may perceive diagnosis or repair-first recommendations as resistance unless communication is handled well.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

## Redesign A

1. **Rule or master-data error:** a high-value device may be coded as a low-risk accessory, or a claim value may be understated, turning automation into a systematic compliance bypass.
2. **Queue overload:** a 15–35% manual-inflow increase could push reviewers above 82% utilization, causing p90 delays across Manual review, Fraud review, and Repair quote.
3. **Risk-specialist starvation:** routing more claims to the two specialists could worsen Monday and post-campaign bottlenecks.
4. **Operational gaming:** agents may recode customers or defect classes to preserve service times.

**Likely bottleneck migration:** from Auto approval and downstream complaints to Manual eligibility and Fraud/risk queues. If those queues are protected by strict controls, the next migration is likely to Repair quote and part-price confirmation.

## Redesign B

1. **Digital exclusion:** customers with low digital literacy may abandon rather than complete a structured portal, making cycle time appear better by removing difficult cases.
2. **False completeness:** technically valid files may still fail to prove the relevant defect or entitlement, producing fewer requests but more downstream denials or complaints.
3. **Checklist obsolescence:** new product or defect types may trigger the wrong evidence manifest and recreate repeated requests.
4. **Release-wave congestion:** faster evidence completion may release many claims to Manual or Fraud review at once.

**Likely bottleneck migration:** from the evidence-request loop to assisted-channel support and evidence validation, then to eligibility queues in bursts. The metric must distinguish true end-to-end improvement from simply moving waiting time past “evidence complete.”

## Redesign C

1. **Cell overload:** routing all high-value cases to a small specialist group could recreate the same queue inside a new organizational label.
2. **Unproductive quality gate:** the pre-dispatch check may consume 12–18 hours while catching too few wrong parts, particularly if source product data are unreliable.
3. **Persistent pricing delay:** co-location will not solve Repair quote latency if part-price data remain unavailable.
4. **Repair-choice backlash:** customers may interpret diagnosis or repair recommendations as an attempt to avoid replacement.
5. **Campaign collision:** the quality inspectors are underused ordinarily but not after repair campaigns; gate capacity could disappear when most needed.

**Likely bottleneck migration:** from downstream reshipment and escalation to technical diagnosis, part-price confirmation, and pre-dispatch quality checking. If the cell cannot resolve policy exceptions itself, the daily Escalation board becomes the terminal bottleneck.

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

## Stage 0: Contain compliance risk and establish a truthful baseline

R1–R3 should not be randomized. Implement minimum hard stops immediately:

- No above-EUR-400 Fast approval without recorded Manual or Fraud/risk review.
- No dispatch without an evidence-complete event, except the explicit Premium low-risk accessory exception.
- No reopened claim can be re-closed or denied without Manual eligibility review.

At the same time:

- Define a claim episode from intake through **stable resolution**, not merely first close.
- Link complaints and reopens to the original claim.
- Observe outcomes for at least 30 days after fulfillment; the complaint p90 already reaches 180 hours, and the subsequent reopen handling adds more delay.
- Validate segment, value, defect, serial/SKU, and channel attributes.
- Establish a post-containment baseline so the redesign effects are not confused with the mandatory compliance change.

## First controlled pilot: Redesign B

Pilot the evidence-first design first because it can release manual capacity before Redesign A increases mandatory review inflow.

Use one or two Standard-customer product families from the unclear/missing-evidence cohort. Test:

- Structured evidence manifest and upload validation.
- Save/resume and proactive status.
- An assisted alternative for customers unwilling or unable to use the portal.
- One reason-coded second-request rule.

Where feasible, randomize or use a stepped-wedge introduction by channel. A low-code form plus evidence coordinator can test the mechanism before committing to a full portal.

### Go criteria

- Repeated evidence requests fall by at least roughly 20–30% relative to baseline, with results directionally consistent with the stated 30–45% potential.
- Median and p90 time from first request to evidence completion improve materially, for example by 20% or more.
- Abandonment does not increase by more than approximately 1–2 percentage points overall or within assisted-channel customers.
- Manual touches per completed claim fall.
- There is no increase in false-complete claims, downstream complaint/reopen, denial after fulfillment, or R2 violations.

### Stop or redesign criteria

- Abandonment rises by roughly 3–5 percentage points, especially in low-digital-literacy channels.
- Evidence requests decline but manual clarification calls, denials, or complaints rise.
- Faster evidence release pushes Manual eligibility above 82% utilization for sustained periods.
- The benefit exists only because more customers abandon before entering the measured denominator.

## Second pilot: Redesign A

Prepare the new auto rules in shadow mode while the evidence pilot runs. Compare proposed routing against audited reviewer decisions and alignment findings before activation.

Then activate the rules for a capped share of claims:

- Preserve the Premium low-risk exception.
- Include both Standard low-risk and Standard high-value cases so the differential-routing hypothesis is tested.
- Use a queue-aware admission cap rather than releasing the full projected 15–35% manual increase at once.
- Compare against concurrent compliant cases or a stepped rollout; do not use noncompliant high-value dispatches as an experimental control.

### Go criteria

- R1 and R2 prohibited alignment moves are near zero, with only documented Premium R2 exceptions.
- R3 is enforced for all sampled reopens.
- Percentage of fitting traces rises materially above 68% without broadening the normative model to allow prohibited paths.
- High-value 30-day complaint/reopen and wrong-replacement rates decline materially, for example by at least 20% relative.
- Premium low-risk completion beyond 24 hours and service-credit exposure do not increase materially.
- Manual-review utilization remains below 82% on a sustained basis, including Monday and post-campaign periods.
- Total 30-day episode cost—not just first-close cost—is stable or improving.

### Stop or narrow criteria

- Manual utilization remains above 82%, or manual and fraud p90 times deteriorate sharply.
- High-value claims become slower without a corresponding improvement in complaint, wrong-replacement, or compliance outcomes.
- Low-risk complaints do not improve while Premium service-credit exposure rises.
- Audit samples reveal systematic misclassification by value, segment, or defect.

## Third pilot: Redesign C

Start with one Standard high-value device family having a known wrong-part or repeat-repair pattern. Do not begin with Premium customers until the gate time and cell capacity are understood.

Pilot:

- One case owner.
- Integrated manual/technical decision.
- Part-price catalog or response SLA.
- Pre-dispatch serial/BOM compatibility check.
- High-value repair test protocol and independent quality check.

Compare it with the existing **compliant reviewed path**, not with unreviewed high-value dispatch.

### Go criteria

- Wrong replacement and 30-day complaint/reopen rates improve by a pre-agreed material margin, such as 20–30%.
- High-value repair rework moves meaningfully below the 12.5% baseline, ideally toward a single-digit rate.
- Pre-dispatch delay remains toward the low end of the 6–18-hour range; p90 should not exceed the 18-hour design bound.
- Repair-quote and customer-approval waits improve materially from the current p90 values of 60 and 80 hours.
- Stable-resolution cycle time is better than, or at least not materially worse than, the compliant reviewed path.
- The escalation board receives fewer routine cases rather than more.

### Stop or redesign criteria

- The quality gate adds 12–18 hours with no observable reduction in wrong replacements or reopens.
- The cell merely creates a new queue at diagnostics, pricing, or quality inspection.
- Repair recommendations increase customer complaints or repeat contacts.
- Benefits disappear during campaigns because inspector or specialist capacity is unavailable.

## Common metrics and checks against downstream displacement

All pilots should report results by Premium/Standard, value class, defect type, channel, and treatment assignment:

- **Cycle time:** median and p90 to eligibility decision, fulfillment, first close, and 30-day stable close.
- **Quality:** first-time-right resolution, wrong replacement, repeated evidence request, repair rework, complaint/reopen at 7 and 30 days.
- **Compliance:** per-rule R1–R3 violations, fitting-trace percentage, alignment moves, and model precision.
- **Customer experience:** abandonment, repeat contacts, status-view usage, service credits, and satisfaction.
- **Cost:** labor by activity, replacement/reshipment cost, repair rework, service credits, and escalation effort.
- **Resources:** utilization and queue age for Manual eligibility, Fraud/risk, Repair quote, Quality check, support channels, and the Escalation board.

To determine whether improvement is real rather than shifted:

1. Use intent-to-treat denominators so abandoned or rerouted cases do not disappear.
2. Keep claims open analytically through the downstream complaint window.
3. Measure total episode cost and stable-resolution time rather than first-close time alone.
4. Mine before-and-after DFGs and alignments for new loops or low-frequency unauthorized variants.
5. Use concurrent or staggered controls to separate redesign effects from Mondays, campaigns, product launches, and seasonality.
6. Check whether waiting, work, or cost moved to customer support, fulfillment, inspection, or escalation.

If all three pilots succeed, they can be composed without making every claim follow every intervention: Redesign B handles incomplete intake, Redesign A routes complete claims by value and risk, and Redesign C becomes the specialized high-value lane.
