## 7. Provisional conclusion

The measured conformance collapse from 93% to 32% is primarily an artifact of stacked data and model changes, not evidence of a behavioral meltdown. The May 31 UI release changed `Classify` to `Risk Screen`; remapping alone recovers roughly half the lost fitness. The June 3 gateway added `Queue Routed` as a technical event, and the June 15 ETL pipeline switched `Decision` and `Notify` to warehouse-ingestion time, producing artificial order inversions that source-sequence ordering largely corrects. Together, label mapping, technical-event removal, and sequence-based ordering raise Jun–Jul fitness to approximately 89%. This means the underlying control-flow process changed far less than the raw log suggests.

However, a separate, real process change also occurred: the August 1 policy mandated `Supervisor Sign-off` for claims above EUR 50,000, and the normative process tree was never updated. The appearance of sign-off in 150 enhanced and 60 standard traces, plus the jump to 86% fitness when an optional sign-off is permitted, confirms model obsolescence. Yet this improvement is potentially misleading: an unconditional optional model overgeneralizes because it allows sign-off for all cases, including those below the threshold. The true policy requires a data-aware guard that the control-flow tree cannot express.

Whether a real compliance problem remains is genuinely uncertain. The 25 June–July and 30 August–September enhanced-review traces lacking a recorded `Compliance Check` could reflect missing events, extraction gaps, or true bypasses—there is no reliable audit to decide. The July 10 supervisor memo did not cite a known increase in violations, and aggregate business outcomes (approval rate 72%71.5%, 30-day appeal 3.8%3.6%, median cycle 2627.4 h) are essentially unchanged, which argues against widespread noncompliance or process failure. But compliance explicitly warned that incident detection is incomplete, so stable aggregates do not prove absence of targeted bypasses, particularly among high-value cases where `high_value_case` was omitted from alignment reports.

Confidence levels: data-artifact explanation is high (~90%); model obsolescence is moderate-high (~75%); real behavioral change is moderate (~45%); employee noncompliance is low-to-moderate (~25%) because evidence is unverified. Immediate actions: IT must stabilize event semantics with a label registry and a technical-event policy; pipeline provenance must declare source sequence as ordering authority. Process owners must update the normative model to a conditional, data-aware specification for sign-off, not merely an optional activity. Compliance must conduct a targeted manual audit of all enhanced-review cases missing checks, stratified by high-value status, and improve detection before asserting clean compliance.

What must not be claimed yet: do not claim employees are noncompliant based solely on alignment moves, because timestamp errors and missing events explain much of the pattern; do not claim the process is fully compliant because aggregate metrics are stable; do not claim the optional sign-off model is correct, because it ignores the EUR 50,000 condition and risks overfitting; and do not assert that `Queue Routed` is meaningless without source-system provenance verification.

## 8. Model-governance recommendation

1. **Model versioning and change log:** Every normative process tree must carry a version tag and an impact-assessment checklist; any policy change (e.g., Aug 1 sign-off rule) triggers a model-update ticket before conformance jobs resume.
2. **Event-semantic contracts:** All activity labels must be governed by a registry mapping UI screens, gateway outputs, and warehouse columns to canonical process activities; UI or interface patches require validation against the registry before extraction.
3. **Technical-event policy:** Events like `Queue Routed` or gateway routing signals must be explicitly classified as technical or business in the event dictionary; conformance pipelines must expose configurable filters rather than silently including them.
4. **Timestamp provenance:** The authoritative ordering source (e.g., source-system sequence number) must be declared; ingestion time must never be used as the primary ordering key without provenance checks.
5. **Data-aware constraints:** When policy conditions depend on case attributes (e.g., EUR 50,000), the normative model or its evaluation must include attribute-aware guards; control-flow-only models must be flagged when such rules exist.
6. **Post-release validation:** After any IT or policy release, a 48-hour alignment-baseline check must compare fitness/precision to the previous version; any >10% drop requires manual audit sampling before declaring process failure or compliance breach.
