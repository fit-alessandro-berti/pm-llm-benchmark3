## 1. Decision rule

**Hard eligibility gates — all must pass before scoring**
1. **Stage:** Case is in `Triage` or `Diagnosis Started`. The swarm is hard-ineligible after `Diagnosis Complete`, irrespective of `p_now`. I-03-type cases may receive ordinary resolution escalation, customer communication, or queue prioritisation, but not a swarm slot.
2. **Feasible window:** Enough time remains to deliver a 45-minute swarm and for it to affect diagnosis, normally at least 60 minutes to the SLA deadline and no indication Diagnosis Complete is imminent within the dispatch window. Re-check stage immediately before dispatch; stage may change between scoring and dispatch.
3. **Repeat-swarm rule:** No prior swarm in the episode unless the re-entry test in Section 4 is passed. A second swarm requires duty-manager approval and a donor slot from reserve.
4. **Data-quality gate:** If a material live feature is missing/imputed and explains a large part of the risk jump, the case is `conditional`, not automatically eligible. It needs source-system verification within 30 minutes before a slot is spent.

**Calibration robustification**
Do not use raw `p_now` as a common threshold. Form a segment-adjusted planning estimate:
- SME: `p_adj = p_now + 0.04 to 0.09`, planning point `+0.065`.
- Strategic Enterprise: `p_adj = p_now - 0.05 to 0.12`, planning point `-0.085`.
- Public Sector: `p_adj = p_now`, planning interval about `±0.03` because calibration is broadly acceptable but sample size is moderate.
- Non-Native-Language Support: no point shift; planning interval about `±0.15` until validated. Do not exclude these cases merely because uncertainty is high, but also do not treat the point estimate or effect range as precise.

Clip `p_adj` to `[0.01, 0.99]`. Use `p_adj = p_adj_now - p_adj_prev` for evidence change. `p_adj` is used for triage and monitoring; it is **not** the post-intervention breach probability. Allocation is driven by the per-case effect range, value, burdens, and spillover, not by risk rank alone.

**Minimum evidence-change / alert-persistence gate**
A new allocation decision requires at least one of:
- `p_adj  0.05` since the last decision point for that case;
- a materially new event: new diagnostic/customer log, stage progression, corrected missing feature, SLA change, or documented specialist finding that plausibly changes risk or effect;
- an escalation trigger in Section 4.

A 30-minute refresh with `p_adj < 0.05` and no new event is suppressed, not a new independent alert.

**Prioritisation score**
For each eligible case calculate, using the supplied effect range as an uncertain prior, not as a known causal effect:
- `DirectLow = e_low × Value  €450  FalseAlarmBurden`
- `DirectMid = e_mid × Value  €450  FalseAlarmBurden`
- `SpillCharge = 6 × spill_per_case × average_value_of_the_six_untreated_diagnosis_cases`

For the 12:00 planning calculation, `SpillCharge_base = 6 × 0.02 × €15,000 = €1,800` per slot. In live operation this must be replaced with the actual values and congestion of the six untreated diagnosis-stage cases. If forecast delay is 15–20+ minutes per slot, multiply the charge by 1.5 and require duty-lead approval for marginal cases.
- `Score = DirectMid  SpillCharge_base`.

**Tiers**
- **Tier 1 — intervene, subject to capacity:** data verified, evidence gate passed, `DirectLow > 0`, `Score > 0`, and delay is not justified by the option value of waiting. Rank Tier 1 by `Score`, then by window closure.
- **Tier 2 — waitlist/watch:** upper-bound benefit is positive but `DirectLow  0`, data are unverified, `Score  0` under base spillover, or the case has enough time buffer that waiting may resolve uncertainty without losing the intervention window. Re-score at each refresh, new event, or trigger.
- **Tier 3 — do not intervene now:** stage-ineligible, suppressed, or upper-bound direct benefit cannot cover direct costs.

Capacity is allocated to Tier 1 only until slots minus the reserve are filled. Monetary value never overrides a safety/regulatory duty or minimum service obligation; if such a duty requires action despite negative monetary Score, record it as a compliance decision, not as a positive expected-value decision. Human overrides must be logged with reason, but cannot override the Diagnosis Complete hard gate for a swarm.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**Cool-down and suppression**
- Deduplicate alerts by case episode. A refresh is not a new alert.
- After a non-action decision, suppress new allocation eligibility for 120 minutes or two refresh cycles, whichever is later, unless an escalation trigger occurs.
- After a swarm, the case is closed to further swarms for the remainder of the diagnosis window. A repeat requires Diagnosis Started status, a materially new event, a fresh effect estimate, and duty-manager approval.
- Notify operators only on first eligibility, re-entry, escalation, or dispatch — not on every refresh.

**Escalation triggers requiring human re-scoring**
- Cumulative `p_adj  0.10` since suppression;
- progression from Triage to Diagnosis Started;
- time to deadline below 2 hours;
- corrected missing data;
- new customer/diagnostic log or specialist finding;
- actual queue delay exceeding the level assumed when the slot was granted.

**Re-entry rule**
Re-entry requires both a material event and a fresh Tier calculation. The event must be attached to the case record so the decision is auditable.

**I-05 versus I-07**
- **I-05:** Third alert, p +0.02, no new event. This is alert repetition, not new evidence. It is suppressed; its p_adj being high means it stays on a watchlist, but it does not regain priority merely because the model refreshed.
- **I-07:** Third alert, p 0.03, but a new customer diagnostic log has arrived. That is independent evidence and may not yet be fully represented in the model features. It therefore passes re-entry and is re-scored. It still does not receive a slot at 12:00 because its low value and spillover-adjusted Score are unfavourable. The difference is eligibility for reconsideration, not a guarantee of intervention.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Value-based prioritisation starving lower-value cases**
Value is likely correlated with Strategic Enterprise status. If value dominates, SME, Public Sector, and smaller customers may systematically breach because they are deprioritised. Safeguards: reserve at least one slot or 25% of daily swarm capacity for high-`p_adj`, window-closing cases below median value when the upper-bound direct benefit is positive; require human review for any case with `p_adj  0.70` and deadline under 2 hours regardless of value; report allocation, waiting time, and breach rates by value band and segment; and audit cases that expire unserved.

**Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support**
The validation sample is small and language-related features may perform unevenly. Safeguards: use wide intervals rather than a false-precision penalty; route such cases to a specialist able to review the underlying log; earmark a small exploration share of Tier 2 slots for positive-upper-bound cases in this segment so their effects can be learned; monitor calibration and outcomes with sample sizes and confidence intervals; and never assume a lower intervention effect solely because the segment is poorly measured.

**Differential calibration across segments**
Raw thresholds would under-alert SME cases by about 0.04–0.09 and over-alert Strategic Enterprise cases by about 0.05–0.12. Safeguards: use segment-adjusted `p_adj`, segment-specific calibration monitoring, and pause automated ranking for a segment if its calibration error exceeds 0.05 persistently. Strategic over-alerting wastes slots and creates false-alarm burden; SME under-alerting creates missed breaches.

**Selective intervention changing future training labels**
Cases selected for swarms are high-risk cases whose breaches may be prevented; untreated high-risk cases may breach. A naive retrain can learn that “high-risk-looking” cases do not breach, or learn the effect of the old workflow as if it were natural risk. Safeguards: log `p_prev`, `p_now`, `p_adj`, score components, tier, capacity state, congestion, decision, override, and dispatch time for every case; keep a shadow score for suppressed and untreated cases; train the risk model only on pre-intervention information; estimate effects with propensity/doubly robust methods, threshold discontinuities, and, for marginal Tier 2 cases where equipoise exists, transparent randomisation or lottery allocation; and version the policy so outcome drift can be attributed.

**Spillover harms falling systematically on untreated groups**
Because high-value cases are selected first, untreated diagnosis queues may contain disproportionately SME/Public Sector or lower-value cases. The spillover then transfers harm to those groups. Safeguards: include the actual untreated queue’s values and segment mix in `SpillCharge`; cap swarms when forecast delay exceeds 20–30 minutes per slot; stagger dispatches; track delay minutes and breach excess by segment for untreated cases; and give untreated cases that were delayed clear priority in the next allocation cycle.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational monitoring**
Track alert-to-decision time, alert-to-dispatch time, stage at dispatch, slot utilisation, reserve-slot use, repeat alerts per case, suppressed alerts, re-entries, specialist interruption/backlog, and dispatch sequence. Alert if a case reaches Diagnosis Complete after being scored Tier 1, if dispatch exceeds the feasible window, or if re-entry logic is bypassed.

**Predictive monitoring**
By segment, stage, and data-quality flag, monitor observed versus predicted breach rates, calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope, Brier score, and reliability bins. For untreated/shadow cases this measures natural risk; for treated cases it must not be interpreted as untreated risk. Monitor missing-feature rates, imputation frequency, unexplained `p_prev  p_now` jumps such as I-06, and alert volume per case. A missingness spike triggers conservative handling and human verification.

**Causal-effect monitoring**
Pre-register how effects will be estimated before outcomes accumulate. Compare treated cases with matched or propensity-weighted untreated cases, exploit the Tier/Score threshold as a discontinuity, and use marginal-case randomisation where ethically acceptable. Estimate effects by segment and stage, compare realised effects with the supplied ranges, and track whether Strategic Enterprise effects fall toward the lower bound after redesign. Do not update effect ranges from a handful of outcomes without uncertainty intervals.

**Capacity and spillover monitoring**
For each swarm, measure aggregate delay added to other diagnosis cases against the 8–20 minute planning range, with a warning above 20 minutes and a stop/escalation review above 30 minutes. Track untreated diagnosis-queue length, delay distribution, and excess breaches relative to the 0.01–0.03 per-slot planning range. If spillover is at the upper end, raise `SpillCharge`, reduce concurrent dispatches, or pause marginal Tier 1 cases.

**Fairness monitoring**
Report, by segment, value band, and Non-Native-Language Support status: eligibility rate, allocation rate, time to intervention, expired-unserved rate, delay minutes imposed on untreated cases, false-alarm burden, breach rate, and sample size/confidence interval. Investigate persistent allocation or delay disparities rather than relying only on aggregate portfolio benefit.

**Policy-induced drift and recalibration decisions**
Monitor treated share, score and tier distributions, threshold crossings, and outcome residuals over time. A fall in observed risk among treated-like cases after policy deployment is a drift warning, not evidence that the risk model suddenly improved. Review operations daily, calibration and causal estimates weekly, and the full allocation rule monthly. Change thresholds or allocation weights only when a trigger is sustained — for example segment calibration error >0.05 for two consecutive weeks, realised effects below the lower bound across enough cases, spillover breaches above forecast, or a material fairness disparity. Roll changes out first in shadow/champion–challenger mode, version them, and retain rollback criteria.
