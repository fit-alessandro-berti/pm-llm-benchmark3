Average cycle time may not lengthen if rework happens in parallel with other work, but quality and satisfaction degrade invisibly.

3. **Fairness across customer segments and locations.** Cases with regional accommodation needs (`customer_segment`) or from East may have different paths when centralized. Average metrics blend them; filtering by `location` and `customer_segment` reveals that some groups experience 2× wait after handover to shared nodes, even as the global median improves.

4. **Learning and skill atrophy.** If all hard cases are routed to Technical Shared Service, local caseworkers never develop technical judgment. `discover_organizational_roles` will show local roles narrowing over time; `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` will show reduced specialization diversity locally. This degrades resilience and is invisible in 8-week cycle averages but appears in turnover and error rates over quarters.

5. **Accountability and ownership blur in shared approval.** When approval moves to a shared queue (`local caseworker  shared approval queue  implementation`), failure attribution becomes diffuse. `discover_subcontracting_network` will show dense subs from caseworkers to the shared approval resource; `rework_flag` after approval may increase because no single owner tracks the case.

6. **Exception handling and absence resilience.** Cross-team cover collapses to ~3/100. If shared-service staff are absent, the network becomes a fragile star. Average cycle time stays fine when staff are present, but absence events cause extreme delays and case stagnation—detectable only by filtering to absence periods or measuring worst-case wait.

7. **Bottleneck spillover from technical to legal/risk.** The proposal notes central tech staff may absorb legal/risk triage. Average cycle time for “standard” cases ignores the spillover: legal/risk wait grows, and cases requiring `required_specialty` in {legal, risk, security} suffer. Use `filter_event_attribute_values` by `required_specialty` to isolate this subgroup.

8. **Standardization masking variation in decision quality.** A narrow variant profile (from `get_variants`) suggests standardization, but if standard rules misfit complex regional cases, rework and complaints rise. Average cycle time does not measure decision fit—only speed.

# 5. Pilot design

**Pilot scope:** Phase 1: Centralize **technical specialist review only** for cases with `case_complexity` = “high” in **Team East** (current highest utilization, 82%, expected to benefit). Do **not** yet merge legal/risk into the same queue, do not remove local supervisors, and do not create shared approval. Build the Technical Shared Service pool from E_Tech plus one rotating specialist from North/South (part-time) to test load-sharing without full consolidation.

**Comparison group:** Team North and Team South run as controls using current embedded model; plus Team East pre-pilot baseline (8 weeks).

**Pre/post observation window:** 8 weeks pre-pilot (baseline), 10 weeks pilot, 8 weeks post-pilot (sustainability check).

**Segmentation for analysis:**
- By `team`: East (pilot) vs. North/South (control).
- By `case_complexity`: low / medium / high (routing rules apply only to high).
- By `required_specialty`: tech vs. legal/risk/security vs. none.
- By `customer_segment`: standard vs. regional accommodation / language-sensitive.
- By `priority`: standard vs. expedited.

**Process-mining guardrail metrics (weekly):**
- Median wait to Technical Shared Service  3.5h.
- 90th percentile cycle time for high-complexity cases  20d.
- Rework rate (all cases)  9%; high-complexity  12%.
- `local_expertise_flag` rate  60% of pre-pilot baseline (prevent total atrophy).
- Cross-team handover per 100 cases  5.
- Variants of “high-complexity + shared”  35% of total (prevent over-concentration).

**Staff feedback collection:**
- Weekly 3-question pulse (role clarity, escalation ease, quality of shared advice).
- Biweekly 30-minute focus groups with local caseworkers and shared specialists.
- Monthly anonymous survey on accountability for approvals (even though approval not changed in pilot, baseline needed).

**Rollback / adjustment triggers (2 consecutive weeks breached):**
- Median wait > 6h.
- Rework rate > 11%.
- SLA miss rate > 9%.
- Staff role-clarity score drop > 20% from baseline.
- `local_expertise_flag` drops below 40% of baseline.
- Customer satisfaction in East drops > 0.3 points.
- Any high-complexity case stalls > 5 days in shared queue.

If triggers hit: revert East to embedded tech for high-complexity; keep shared pool for overflow only; do not expand pilot.

# 6. Recommendation

**Proceed with a conditional, phased pilot—not a full restructuring.**

The restructuring is not inherently good or bad; its impact depends on whether centralization creates true capacity pooling or merely shifts bottlenecks from local nodes to a hidden queue. Management should **not infer success from early average cycle time** for four reasons:
- Average improvements may be driven by faster processing of simple, low-complexity cases that bypass the central queue, masking severe delays for high-complexity cases.
- Specialist utilization gains can be a Jevons-paradox effect: easier access to specialists may increase demand (more referrals), raising total queue time without improving service.
- East’s likely improvement (from overloaded 82% utilization) is not predictive for North/South, which have lower baseline utilization and stronger local expertise; their loss of embedded capacity may hurt more than shared pooling helps.
- Short-term cycle-time averages miss learning effects (skill atrophy) and fairness effects (regional/customer-segment disparities) that accumulate over 3–6 months.

**Phased logic:**
1. **Pilot (10 weeks):** Technical Shared Service for high-complexity only in East, with guardrails above.
2. **Evaluate:** Use PM4Py before/after comparison (handover networks, performance DFG, wait-time distributions by segment, organizational roles, subcontracting density) plus staff surveys.
3. **Conditional expansion:** If all guardrails hold for 8+ weeks and fairness metrics (wait by `customer_segment`, `location`, `priority`) are stable or improved, expand to all high-complexity cases across North/South (but keep approval and legal/risk local for now).
4. **Second phase:** Only if first phase succeeds, introduce shared approval queue with its own 8-week guardrail.
5. **Reject / redesign:** If wait times exceed thresholds, if `local_expertise_flag` collapses, or if fairness disparities emerge, reject full centralization. Instead, invest in cross-training local specialists and improving cross-team note standardization (addressing the current 18% rework in cross-team cover).

**What management must not do:** Do not declare victory if average cycle time drops in week 2–4; do not use specialist utilization alone as a success metric; do not ignore the 90th percentile, the high-complexity subgroup, or staff role-clarity surveys; do not roll out legal/risk centralization until technical queue dynamics are proven stable.
