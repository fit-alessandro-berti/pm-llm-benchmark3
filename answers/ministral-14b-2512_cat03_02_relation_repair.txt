# Repairing an Incomplete OCEL 2.0 Dataset: A Plausible Reasoning Approach

## 1. Consistency Rules and Evidence Model

### Rule Classification and Evidence Combination

**Hard Invariants (must hold in all repairs):**
1. **E2O cardinality rules** (from domain semantics):
   - `Create Order`  exactly 1 Order (ev01ORD-A, ev02ORD-B)
   - `Add Item`  exactly 1 Order and 1 Item (ev03ORD-A/IT-A1, etc.)
   - `Record Payment`  exactly 1 Payment and 1 Invoice (ev13PAY-1/INV-44, ev14PAY-2/INV-44)
   - O2O qualifiers are directional (e.g., `carries` is not symmetric)

2. **Object identity constraints**:
   - Object IDs are unique and immutable (e.g., ORD-A  ORD-B)
   - Event timestamps are strictly ordered (no time travel)

**Strong Defaults (presumed true unless contradicted):**
1. **Lifecycle order**:
   - Create Order  Add Item  Pack Item  Dispatch Package  Confirm Delivery  Issue Invoice  Record Payment
   - Items must be added before being packed (ev03/04 before ev06/07/08)
   - Packages must be packed before dispatch (ev06/07/08 before ev09/10)

2. **Customer consistency**:
   - All events for customer C17 should align (ORD-A/B, INV-44, PAY-1/2)
   - Route consistency for NORTH deliveries (PK-7/8, DEL-X/Y)

3. **Amount consistency**:
   - ORD-A declared_value=130 + ORD-B declared_value=100 = 230  INV-44 total
   - PAY-1=100 + PAY-2=130 = 230 = INV-44 total (strong evidence but defeasible)

4. **Identifier similarity**:
   - Scanner_ref=PK7-1  PK-7 (ev06)
   - Scanner_ref=PK8-1/2  PK-8 (ev07/08)
   - External_ref=N-420  DEL-X (ev09)
   - External_ref=N-421?  DEL-Y (ev10)

**Weak Heuristics (supportive but not definitive):**
1. **Timestamp proximity**:
   - Events within minutes likely belong to same transaction
   - ev09/10 at 10:00  likely same dispatch batch
   - ev11 at 14:00  likely after dispatch but before invoice

2. **Route consistency**:
   - NORTH route appears in PK-7/8 and DEL-X/Y
   - Suggests PK-7DEL-X and PK-8DEL-Y

3. **Cardinality hints**:
   - One invoice for two orders is plausible (combined billing)
   - Two payments for one invoice is plausible (partial payments)

**Evidence Against a Repair:**
1. **Orphaned objects/events**:
   - Any object/event without plausible relations is suspect
   - Example: PK-8 without dispatch relation (ev10) or DEL-Y without confirmation

2. **Lifecycle violations**:
   - Item packed before added (ev08 before ev05)
   - Package dispatched before packed (ev10 before ev07/08)

3. **Customer/amount mismatches**:
   - Payment not linked to correct invoice
   - Invoice total not matching order sums

4. **Identifier mismatches**:
   - Scanner_ref doesn't match package ID
   - External_ref doesn't match delivery ID

**Evidence Combination Strategy:**
I combine evidence using a weighted approach:
1. **Hard invariants** (e.g., E2O cardinality) are non-negotiable
2. **Strong defaults** (e.g., lifecycle order) carry high weight unless contradicted
3. **Weak heuristics** (e.g., timestamp proximity) provide suggestive but not definitive support
4. **Identifier matching** is strong when consistent, weak when ambiguous
5. **Amount matching** is suggestive but not conclusive (due to fees/taxes)

The repair process involves:
- Starting with hard invariants as constraints
- Using strong defaults to guide provisional relations
- Testing weak heuristics for consistency
- Flagging unresolved cases where evidence is conflicting or insufficient

## 3. Alternative Repair Bundles

### Bundle A: Conservative/Minimum-Change Repair
**Philosophy**: Only add relations where evidence is strong and unambiguous. Preserve all uncertainty where evidence is weak or conflicting.

**Handling Ambiguous Cases**:
1. **ev08 and PK-8**:
   - Accept that PK-8 packs IT-B1 (R6, R11) based on scanner_ref=PK8-2
   - Do not assume IT-A2 is in PK-8 (reject R12)
   - Leave PK-8's dispatch relation unresolved (no DEL-Y relation added)

2. **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y**:
   - Do not add DEL-Y as target of ev10 (no confidence in external_ref=N-421?)
   - Leave ev10 unresolved (no relation added)
   - DEL-Y remains orphaned (no carries relation)

3. **ev11 and delivery objects**:
   - Only relate ev11 to DEL-X (R3) based on external_ref=N-420 match
   - Do not relate to DEL-Y (R4) due to no carrier_ref match
   - Do not relate to ORD-B (R5) due to no carrier_ref and ORD-A already confirmed

4. **INV-44 and ORD-B**:
   - Do not add bills relation between INV-44 and ORD-B (R8)
   - Leave ORD-B unbilled in this repair

5. **PAY-2 and INV-44**:
   - Add settles relation (R9) based on amount matching
   - Note this creates partial payment scenario (PAY-1=100, PAY-2=130 for INV-44=230)

### Bundle B: Plausible Alternative Interpretation
**Philosophy**: Make reasonable assumptions to complete lifecycles where evidence is suggestive but not definitive.

**Handling Ambiguous Cases**:

   - Add ev11 to ORD-B (R5) based on:
     - portal_customer=C17 matches ORD-B
     - ORD-A already confirmed, but ORD-B also belongs to C17

4. **INV-44 and ORD-B**:
   - Add bills relation between INV-44 and ORD-B (R8)
   - Justification: INV-44 customer=C17 matches ORD-B, and amount=230ORD-A+ORD-B

   - Add settles relation (R9) as in Bundle A
   - Note this creates partial payment scenario

**Coherence Check**:
- ORD-A lifecycle: complete
- ORD-B lifecycle: complete (confirmation added)
- PK-7 lifecycle: complete
- PK-8 lifecycle: complete (now packed with both items, dispatched)
- DEL-X lifecycle: complete
- DEL-Y lifecycle: complete
- INV-44 lifecycle: complete with both orders billed

### Bundle C: Bulk Dispatch Interpretation
**Philosophy**: Treat ev10 as a bulk dispatch covering both PK-7 and PK-8.

**Handling Ambiguous Cases**:
1. **ev08 and PK-8**:
   - Same as Bundle A (PK-8 packs IT-B1 only)

4. **INV-44 and ORD-B**:

5. **PAY-2 and INV-44**:
   - Same as Bundle A/B

**Coherence Check**:

## 7. PM4Py-Oriented Validation Outline

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.ocel.objects import OCEL
from pm4py.objects.ocel.objects import OCELObject, OCELObjectType
from pm4py.objects.ocel.objects import OCELEvent, OCELEventAttribute
from pm4py.objects.ocel.objects import OCELRelation, OCELRelationType
from pm4py.objects.ocel.objects import OCELObjectAttribute

def validate_and_repair_ocel(events, objects, relations, o2o):
    """
    Validate original OCEL and apply Bundle A repairs.
    Returns repaired OCEL and process mining artifacts.
    """

    # Make copies to preserve original
    events_repaired = events.copy()
    objects_repaired = objects.copy()
    relations_repaired = relations.copy()
    o2o_repaired = o2o.copy()

    # --- Bundle A Repairs ---

    # Add E2O relations
    bundle_a_e2o = [
        # ev08  PK-8 (already present in observed_related_objects as IT-B1, but we're adding the package relation)
        # Actually, ev08's observed_related_objects only has IT-B1, so we need to add PK-8
        {
            'ocel:eid': 'ev08',
            'ocel:oid': 'PK-8',
            'ocel:qualifier': None,
            'confidence': 0.8
        },
        # ev11  DEL-X
        {
            'ocel:eid': 'ev11',
            'ocel:oid': 'DEL-X',
            'ocel:qualifier': None,
            'confidence': 0.7
        }
    ]

    # Add O2O relations
    bundle_a_o2o = [
        # PK-8 packs IT-B1
        {
            'ocel:oid': 'PK-8',
            'ocel:oid_2': 'IT-B1',
            'ocel:qualifier': 'packs',
            'confidence': 0.8
        },
        # PAY-2 settles INV-44
        {
            'ocel:oid': 'PAY-2',
            'ocel:oid_2': 'INV-44',
            'ocel:qualifier': 'settles',
            'confidence': 0.9
        }
    ]

    # Apply E2O additions
    for repair in bundle_a_e2o:
        # Check if relation already exists
        existing = relations_repaired[
            (relations_repaired['ocel:eid'] == repair['ocel:eid']) &
            (relations_repaired['ocel:oid'] == repair['ocel:oid'])
        ]
        if len(existing) == 0:
            new_row = {
                'ocel:eid': repair['ocel:eid'],
                'ocel:oid': repair['ocel:oid'],
                'ocel:qualifier': repair['ocel:qualifier'],
                'confidence': repair['confidence']
            }
            relations_repaired = pd.concat([
                relations_repaired,
                pd.DataFrame([new_row])
            ], ignore_index=True)

    # Apply O2O additions
    for repair in bundle_a_o2o:
        # Check if relation already exists (considering directionality)
        existing = o2o_repaired[
            ((o2o_repaired['ocel:oid'] == repair['ocel:oid']) &
             (o2o_repaired['ocel:oid_2'] == repair['ocel:oid_2']) &
             (o2o_repaired['ocel:qualifier'] == repair['ocel:qualifier'])) |
            ((o2o_repaired['ocel:oid'] == repair['ocel:oid_2']) &
             (o2o_repaired['ocel:oid_2'] == repair['ocel:oid']) &
             # Check if reverse qualifier exists (but our semantics say qualifiers are directional)
             False)
        ]
        if len(existing) == 0:
            new_row = {
                'ocel:oid': repair['ocel:oid'],
                'ocel:oid_2': repair['ocel:oid_2'],
                'ocel:qualifier': repair['ocel:qualifier'],
                'confidence': repair['confidence']
            }
            o2o_repaired = pd.concat([
                o2o_repaired,
                pd.DataFrame([new_row])
            ], ignore_index=True)

    # --- Validation Checks ---
    # 1. Referential integrity
    all_eids = events_repaired['ocel:eid'].unique()
    all_oids = objects_repaired['ocel:oid'].unique()

    # Check E2O relations
    e2o_oids = relations_repaired['ocel:oid'].unique()
    missing_oids = set(e2o_oids) - set(all_oids)
    if missing_oids:
        raise ValueError(f"E2O relations reference non-existent objects: {missing_oids}")

    # Check O2O relations
    o2o_oids = set(o2o_repaired['ocel:oid'].unique()).union(
        set(o2o_repaired['ocel:oid_2'].unique())
    )
    missing_oids = o2o_oids - set(all_oids)
    if missing_oids:
        raise ValueError(f"O2O relations reference non-existent objects: {missing_oids}")

    # 2. Duplicate checks
    if relations_repaired.duplicated().any():
        print("Warning: Duplicate E2O relations found")

    if o2o_repaired.duplicated().any():
        print("Warning: Duplicate O2O relations found")

    # 3. Construct OCEL object (version-dependent)
    ocel = OCEL()

    # Add objects
    for _, obj in objects_repaired.iterrows():
        obj_type = OCELObjectType(obj['Object type'])
        ocel_obj = OCELObject(obj['Object ID'], obj_type)

        # Add attributes
        for attr_name, attr_value in obj['Selected attributes'].items():
            ocel_obj.add_attribute(OCELObjectAttribute(attr_name, attr_value))

        ocel.add_object(ocel_obj)

    # Add events
    for _, event in events_repaired.iterrows():
        ocel_event = OCELEvent(event['Event'], event['Timestamp'])

        # Add attributes
        for attr_name, attr_value in event['Event attributes'].items():
```

## 8. Over-Repair Warnings

An aggressive repair could create several false process behaviors in this dataset:

1. **False Bulk Dispatch Patterns**:
   - *Risk*: Assuming ev10 dispatches both PK-7 and PK-8 (like Bundle C) when it might only dispatch PK-8
   - *Effect*: Creates artificial bulk dispatch behavior that doesn't exist in reality
   - *Example*: The OC-DFG would show Dispatch activity with two outgoing edges to different packages, suggesting all dispatches are bulk when they might not be

2. **Incorrect Confirmation Scope**:
   - *Risk*: Relating ev11 to both DEL-X and DEL-Y (like Bundle B) when it might only confirm DEL-X
   - *Effect*: Overestimates confirmation batch sizes and creates false parallel confirmation paths
   - *Example*: The OTG would show Confirm Delivery transitioning to both orders simultaneously, suggesting parallel confirmations when they might be sequential

3. **Artificial Many-to-Many Relations**:
   - *Risk*: Creating PK-8 packs IT-A2 when IT-A2 was already packed in PK-7
   - *Effect*: Distorts item packaging patterns and creates impossible lifecycle paths
   - *Example*: The ET-OT graph would show Pack Item event connected to two different packages for the same item, violating business rules

4. **Over-Billing Assumptions**:
   - *Risk*: Automatically relating INV-44 to ORD-B based solely on customer match
   - *Effect*: Creates false billing relationships and distorts invoice-to-order patterns
   - *Example*: The OC-DFG would show Issue Invoice with two outgoing edges to different orders, suggesting all invoices cover multiple orders when they might not

5. **Premature Payment Application**:
   - *Risk*: Assuming PAY-2 settles INV-44 without considering partial payments or other invoices
   - *Effect*: Creates false payment-to-invoice mappings and distorts payment patterns
   - *Example*: The OTG would show Record Payment transitioning directly to Invoice without considering unapplied cash scenarios

6. **False Lifecycle Completion**:
   - *Risk*: Forcing all objects to complete lifecycles even when evidence is weak
   - *Effect*: Creates artificial process variants that don't reflect reality
   - *Example*: The ET-OT graph would show complete paths for ORD-B when it might actually be canceled or pending

7. **Identifier Matching Overfitting**:
   - *Risk*: Relying too heavily on prefix matching (e.g., N-42*  DEL-Y) without considering business context
   - *Effect*: Creates false relations based on superficial pattern matching
   - *Example*: The O2O relations would include DEL-Y carries PK-8 based solely on route matching, ignoring that PK-7 might also go to NORTH

8. **Temporal Proximity Misinterpretation**:
   - *Risk*: Assuming events close in time belong together without considering business processes
   - *Effect*: Creates artificial temporal dependencies that don't exist
   - *Example*: The OC-DFG would show Pack Item and Dispatch as always occurring together, masking legitimate delays

9. **Amount Matching Overconfidence**:
   - *Risk*: Treating amount matching as definitive proof of relations (e.g., PAY-2  INV-44)
   - *Effect*: Ignores legitimate partial payments, discounts, or fees
   - *Example*: The payment patterns would show all payments exactly matching invoice totals, hiding real-world complexity

10. **Circular Confirmation Logic**:
    - *Risk*: Using confirmation events to infer dispatch relations and vice versa
    - *Effect*: Creates self-reinforcing false patterns where confirmation implies dispatch and dispatch implies confirmation
    - *Example*: The ET-OT graph would show Confirm Delivery only after Dispatch Package, even when confirmations might happen out of order

Each of these over-repair scenarios would lead to:
- Incorrect process models that don't reflect actual business behavior
- False positive discoveries in process mining (e.g., finding "bulk dispatch" patterns that don't exist)
- Misleading performance metrics based on artificial process variants
- Incorrect recommendations for process improvement

The key principle is that repairs should only add relations where evidence is strong and unambiguous, and should always preserve the original uncertainty where evidence is weak or conflicting.
