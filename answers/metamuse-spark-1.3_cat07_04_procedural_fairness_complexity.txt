# 1. Fairness dimensions
## Outcome parity
Raw approval rates differ substantially: Group A 78%, B 63% (-15pp), C 69% (-9pp), D 58% (-20pp). Raw gaps alone cannot be equated with discrimination. Groups differ markedly in case mix: high risk_score_band 18% A vs 51% D, 29% B; incomplete documentation 21% A vs 46% B, 36% C; in-person 9% A vs 38% B; language need 57% B vs 2% A; accommodation requested 62% C vs 4% A; high prior-case-count 49% D vs 12% A. Raw parity is therefore an inadequate and potentially misleading fairness criterion.

## Adjusted outcome disparity
After controlling for risk_score_band, documentation_completeness, channel, household_size_band, prior_case_count, region, month, gaps shrink but do not disappear: B -5.5pp, C -2.2pp, D -3.1pp approval probability vs A; appeal probability +4.4pp B, +1.1pp C, +5.7pp D. Adjusted comparisons are more informative than raw parity, but still depend on the neutrality and completeness of covariates - see Sections 2 and 5. Residuals should be treated as disparity requiring explanation, not proof of discrimination.

## Procedural burden
Even if decisions were equal, procedures differ: probability of Request Missing Documents +9.5pp B, +4.2pp C vs A after adjustment; Supervisor Review +8.0pp B, +5.5pp C vs A. Variant shares show B concentrated in V2 (34%) and V4 language path (24% vs 1% A), C in V5 accommodation path (31% vs 2% A), D in V3 fraud path (28% vs 9% A). More steps, loops, and reviews mean more applicant effort, evidence demands, and opportunities for drop-out, independent of final outcome.

## Timeliness
Median cycle time: A 8.1d, B 13.7d (+5.6d raw), C 15.9d (+7.8d raw), D 12.1d (+4.0d raw); p90 gaps even larger (A 21.4d vs C 38.8d, B 34.2d). Adjusted: B +2.6d, C +4.1d, D +0.9d. C has the largest adjusted delay despite the smallest adjusted approval gap, indicating burden is not captured by outcome parity. Specific waits: Completeness Check  Request Missing Documents 0.4d A vs 1.6d B; Language Support  Eligibility 3.8d (B only); Accommodation Review  Eligibility 4.7d (C only); Supervisor Review  Decision Draft 1.0d A vs 1.9d B, 1.6d C. Timeliness matters for benefits access even when approval is eventually granted.

## Consistency of routing
Policy states higher risk  Fraud Review, accommodation request  Accommodation Review, minority-language request  Language Support Contact. Part of routing variation is therefore legitimate. Concern is *conditional* consistency: do similar cases receive similar routing regardless of group? Adjusted +8.0pp Supervisor Review for B and +9.5pp Request Missing Documents for B after controlling for documented complexity suggests inconsistent application of thresholds or communication barriers leading to differential routing.

## Quality / accessibility of support
Language Support Contact and Accommodation Review are rights-preserving steps. They must not be treated as neutral delays. They add 19.2d median (V4) and 21.6d median (V5) paths, with long waits for scarce specialists, and policy has no separate timeliness thresholds for those steps. A process can be formally accessible while substantively burdensome if support is under-capacity or poorly integrated.

## Appeal / rework burden
Appeal rate: A 6% vs B 13%, C 10%, D 16%; rework: A 11% vs B 22%, C 24%, D 20%. V6 appeal path: A 4% vs B 8%, D 13%. Adjusted appeal probability remains elevated for B (+4.4pp) and D (+5.7pp). High appeal/rework can indicate initial decision quality, explanation quality, or barriers to getting it right first time, imposing secondary burden on applicants.

## Resource-allocation fairness
Group A spread across 52 caseworkers; B disproportionately to 7 bilingual caseworkers + 3 overloaded regional officers; C to 5 accommodation specialists (two also do supervisor review); D to fraud-review queue. Concentration creates queueing, handovers (general  bilingual  general; general  specialist  supervisor), and backlog vulnerability in two regions. Fairness requires assessing whether staffing matches demand and whether some groups systematically face higher workload handlers or more handovers.

# 2. Adjusted comparison strategy
Distinguish four concepts: **disparity** (observed group difference), **legitimate routing** (difference justified by relevant need/risk and delivered without extra burden), **discrimination** (differential treatment on protected/irrelevant grounds, direct or via unjustified practice), and **uncertainty** (what cannot be identified from log + attributes).

## Matching or stratification variables
- Base stratum: risk_score_band, documentation_completeness, channel, household_size_band, prior_case_count, region, month as in preliminary model, but do not treat them as unquestionably neutral.
- Exact / coarsened matching: compare cases with same risk band + documentation status + channel + region + household band + prior-count band. Estimate within-stratum group differences in approval, cycle time, P(Supervisor Review), P(Request Docs), P(Appeal).
- Sequential adjustment: Model 1 with clearly legitimate exogenous variables (household_size_band, region, month); Model 2 adds channel; Model 3 adds documentation_completeness and risk_score_band. Observe how B/C/D coefficients shrink. Large shrinkage when adding potentially biased covariates signals possible mediation via biased measurement, not proof of legitimacy.

## Path / variant comparison
- Within-variant (conditional) comparison: restrict to V1 straight-through, to V2 request-docs, to V3 fraud path. If B vs A differences in cycle time, approval, appeal persist *within the same path*, this points to within-path treatment/quality differences rather than just different path mix.
- Decomposition: Oaxaca / shift-share: how much of raw approval and cycle-time gap is due to variant mix vs within-variant differences. E.g., is B slower because more B in V4, or because B in V2 is slower than A in V2?

## Resource-assignment comparison
- Compare same activity, same region, different resources: e.g., Eligibility Assessment duration/outcome for B cases handled by bilingual specialists vs generalists; Accommodation Review by the 5 specialists vs others.
- Measure handler concentration (Herfindahl, cases per resource), handover count per case by group conditional on path, and rework after handover.
- Test regional interaction: Group x Region effects to isolate the two backlog regions for Supervisor Review  Decision Draft.

## Waiting-time decomposition
- Split total cycle time into service time vs waiting time per transition using event timestamps. Focus on Submit  Verify, Completeness  Request Docs, Language Support  Eligibility, Accommodation Review  Eligibility, Risk Screening  Fraud Review, Supervisor Review  Decision Draft, Decision Draft  Notification.
- Conditional waiting-time: compare waits for same transition, same variant, same channel. E.g., Completeness  Request Docs wait A 0.4d vs B 1.6d conditional on in-person vs online separates channel effect from language-staffing effect.
- Queue vs processing: is Language 3.8d and Accommodation 4.7d wait due to service duration or queue for 7 / 5 staff? Daily workload granularity is coarse, so bound uncertainty.

## Sensitivity analysis for possibly biased covariates
- Do not treat risk_score_band and documentation_completeness as neutral. documentation_completeness may be affected by language barriers (forms, instructions, what counts as complete); risk_score_band may encode historical disparities.
- Sensitivity 1: exclude risk_score_band and documentation_completeness from controls to get upper bound of disparity; include them to get lower bound.
- Sensitivity 2: proxy audit: predict risk_score_band and incomplete-documentation flag from group + region + channel + prior-count. If group predicts risk/incompleteness conditional on other observables, flag potential measurement bias.
- Sensitivity 3: negative control: test disparity in steps that should not depend on risk/language (e.g., Submit  Verify for online-only subgroup). Persistent gap suggests systemic resource effect.
- E-value / robustness: how strong would unmeasured complexity have to be to explain residual +5.5pp, +2.6d, +4.1d?

## Subgroup checks by region and channel
- Region-stratified: repeat adjusted comparisons inside vs outside the two backlog regions; inside bilingual-staff regions vs others.
- Channel-stratified: online-only B vs A (removes in-person wait confound); in-person-only. Language-need vs no-need within B; accommodation-requested vs not within C; high-risk vs low-risk within D.
- Intersectional: e.g., B + in-person + high incomplete docs; C + language need. Small-n caution but important for accessibility.

# 3. Path and resource disparities
## Most important procedural disparities
1. **Language path concentration and bottleneck (B, V4):** V4 is 24% B vs 1% A, median 19.2d vs 5.8d V1. Legitimate in that 57% B needs language support, but Language Support  Eligibility median wait 3.8d with only 7 bilingual caseworkers, plus Completeness  Request Docs wait 1.6d B vs 0.4d A, and extra general  bilingual  general handovers. Adjusted +2.6d and +9.5pp Request Docs for B remain after controls, so delay is not just in-person share or measured incompleteness. Concerning: capacity-driven wait attached to a rights-preserving step.
2. **Accommodation path concentration and specialist queue (C, V5):** V5 is 31% C vs 2% A, median 21.6d (longest non-appeal). Accommodation Review  Eligibility 4.7d wait with 5 specialists, plus handovers to supervisor (two specialists also do supervisor review). Adjusted +4.1d cycle time for C is largest of all groups, while adjusted approval gap is smallest (-2.2pp) and appeal gap small (+1.1pp). This pattern - near-parity on decision, large penalty on time and Supervisor Review (+5.5pp) - is classic procedural burden from support design, not decision bias alone. Legitimate to route, concerning in timeliness/handovers.
3. **Extra scrutiny loop for B beyond documented complexity:** Adjusted +8.0pp Supervisor Review and +9.5pp Request Docs for B, plus Supervisor  Decision wait 1.9d B vs 1.0d A. V2 share 34% B vs 24% A with median 14.9d. Since model already controls for documentation_completeness, channel, risk, region, residual suggests either (a) completeness measure misses language-driven incompleteness, (b) differential standards/communication causing more requests, or (c) extra caution applied to B. 4. **Regional backlog and resource concentration:** B disproportionately assigned to 3 overloaded regional officers; Supervisor  Decision backlog in two regions (B 1.9d, C 1.6d vs A 1.0d). This is resource-allocation unfairness: even facially neutral supervisor step burdens B/C more because of where they live / which queue they join. Similarly Decision Draft  Notification small gap (0.8d B vs 0.5d A) suggests less discretion there.
5. **Fraud path for D (V3):** V3 28% D vs 9% A, median 18.4d; Risk Screening  Fraud Review wait 2.8d D vs 2.2d A (modest). Given 51% high-risk and 49% high prior-count in D vs 18%/12% A, heavy V3 use is largely expected under current policy, and adjusted differences are small (+0.9d cycle, +2.0pp supervisor, +1.5pp request docs). Least concerning for *conditional* procedural burden, but most dependent on assuming risk_score_band neutrality - which must not be assumed. 6. **Appeal/rework as second-order burden:** Rework 22% B, 24% C, 20% D vs 11% A; appeals elevated even after adjustment for B and D. Indicates initial process fails to get it right or explain it accessibly first time, shifting cost to applicants most burdened already.

## Justified vs remaining concern
- Potentially justified *routing* (but not justified *waiting*): BLanguage Support, CAccommodation Review, DFraud Review given stated policy and observed need/risk prevalence.
- Remaining concerning even after case-mix: residual Supervisor Review and Request Docs for B/C; 3.8d/4.7d support waits with no SLAs; handover-intensive design; regional backlog incidence; persistent appeal gaps for B/D. D's cycle/approval residuals are modest, suggesting raw D gaps are mostly case-mix-driven *under current risk definition* - but that conclusion collapses if risk score is biased.

# 4. Plausible mechanisms
1. **Legitimate complexity-driven routing:** Higher measured risk, incomplete docs, accommodation/language need trigger additional steps (Fraud Review, Request Docs, Accommodation Review, Language Support, Supervisor Review). This mechanically explains much of raw V1 vs V2/V3/V4/V5 mix and raw cycle-time gaps. Would produce variant concentration without within-variant waits/gaps if well-resourced.
2. **Capacity constraint on rights-preserving services:** Only 7 bilingual caseworkers and 5 accommodation specialists (with dual roles) serve concentrated demand. Queues (3.8d, 4.7d) and Completeness  Request Docs 1.6d for language-support cases reflect staffing mismatch, not applicant complexity. Generates adjusted timeliness penalties (+2.6d B, +4.1d C) and incentivizes workarounds or rushed support. Legitimate need + under-capacity = structural procedural burden.
3. **Handover-intensive workflow and regional overload:** Design requires bouncing between generalist  specialist  generalist/supervisor, multiplying waits, information loss, and rework (rework 22-24% B/C). Assignment of B to 3 overloaded officers and backlog in two regions amplifies Supervisor  Decision waits. Even with equal decision criteria, fragmentation produces slower, less consistent experience for support users.
4. **Communication / documentation-standard mismatch:** Forms, instructions, completeness criteria, and evidence requirements designed around majority-language, online, non-accommodation applicants. B's 46% incomplete share and residual +9.5pp Request Docs after controlling for completeness suggests language barriers affect ability to satisfy completeness, and/or staff apply completeness more strictly when communication is harder. This is concerning because documentation_completeness then mediates disparity rather than justifying it.
5. **Risk-score feedback and heightened scrutiny:** If risk_score_band encodes historical enforcement, residence, or prior-case patterns correlated with group, then D's 51% high-risk share and routing to Fraud Review + Supervisor Review reproduces past disparity. Even modest extra scrutiny (Risk  Fraud wait +0.6d, V3 18.4d) plus higher appeal (+5.7pp adjusted) can entrench higher prior-count  higher future risk. Concerning and requires audit, not assumption of neutrality.
6. **Discretionary caution / supervisor escalation:** Residual +8.0pp (B) and +5.5pp (C) Supervisor Review after adjustment may reflect risk-averse escalation for non-standard cases (language/accommodation) or unfamiliarity among generalists. Supervisors in two regions with backlog and dual-role specialists create inconsistent thresholds. Could be well-intentioned quality control that nonetheless imposes unequal burden and delay.

# 5. Limitations of available attributes
- **Measurement bias in key covariates:** documentation_completeness is recorded by the process itself. If bilingual applicants receive less understandable requests or stricter completeness judgments, controlling for it absorbs part of the discrimination. Similarly, channel (in-person 38% B vs 9% A) may reflect lack of accessible online options rather than preference.
- **Historical bias in risk_score_band:** Risk scores trained or calibrated on past fraud findings, prior-case-count (49% high for D), region, or enforcement intensity may reflect where scrutiny was historically applied, not underlying risk. Treating 51% high-risk for D as exogenous justifies V3 routing circularly. Log does not contain score features, model, calibration by group, or false-positive rates.
- **Missing applicant needs:** language_preference is coarse; proficiency, literacy, digital access, disability type/severity, need for in-person assistance, caregiving constraints, transport, and prior trauma are unobserved. household_size_band and region are crude proxies for complexity and vulnerability. Without them, residual gaps cannot be attributed, and apparent similarity may hide relevant difference or vice versa.
- **Unobserved resource workload:** Workload observed only at daily granularity; no real-time queue length, shift coverage for 7 bilingual / 5 specialist staff, sick leave, skill match, or time-on-task. Cannot separate whether 3.8d/4.7d waits are queuing, batching, or service effort, nor whether B/C cases are assigned to more junior/overloaded staff within same role. Handover networks show structure but not quality of handover.
- **Appeal quality and outcome meaning:** appeal_flag and case_outcome capture occurrence, not quality of explanation, perceived dignity, comprehension, or whether appeal was accessible (language/accommodation in appeal). High D/B appeal rates could reflect meritorious correction, low barrier to appeal, or poor initial explanations - log cannot distinguish. case_outcome does not capture partial benefits, delay harms, or abandonment after Request Docs.
- **Selection and censoring:** Log likely conditions on submitted applications; deterred non-applicants, abandoned incompletes, and reopened cases may be missing or coded as rework. 90th percentile gaps (34-39d vs 21d) hint at tail abandonment. Without denominator of eligible population and without text of notifications, fairness of access cannot be fully assessed.

# 6. Mitigation options and side effects
- **Adding capacity for language support and accommodation review:** Hire/train more bilingual caseworkers and accommodation specialists; decouple specialist and supervisor roles; pool coverage across regions. *Trade-offs:* cost, hiring time, training quality; pooling may reduce local knowledge; adding staff without redesigning handovers may still leave fragmentation. Must monitor utilization to avoid idle capacity vs persistent queues.
- **Service-level targets for support steps:** Introduce separate timeliness thresholds for Language Support Contact  Eligibility and Accommodation Review  Eligibility, with queue alerts and escalation, even though they are rights-preserving. *Trade-offs:* pressure to rush support could harm quality; gaming by reclassifying waits; target crowding out non-target steps. Pair with quality checks and applicant comprehension measures, not time alone.
- **Routing redesign to reduce handovers:** Keep specialist input but reduce transfers: e.g., bilingual caseworker owns case end-to-end; accommodation specialist advises rather than takes over; joint review instead of sequential general  specialist  supervisor; co-location or warm handoffs. *Trade-offs:* reduced generalist learning; specialist caseload increase; risk of siloing expertise. Needs clear ownership and handover quality standards.
- **Decision-explanation improvements:** Plain-language, translated, accessible decision letters; checklists for missing documents with examples in relevant languages/formats; pre-decision confirmation call for language/accommodation cases. *Trade-offs:* upfront time cost; translation maintenance; may increase short-term Request Docs clarity but reduce rework/appeals later. Must not add extra applicant steps as condition for benefits.
- **Monitoring adjusted disparities:** Regular dashboard of adjusted (not just raw) approval, cycle time, waits, P(Supervisor), P(Request Docs), P(Appeal/rework) by group, region, channel, with within-variant views and resource-load overlay. *Trade-offs:* metric fixation; privacy/small-cell disclosure for C/D subgroups; need to avoid incentivizing suppression of support steps to improve numbers. Include guardrail that support-step use should not be minimized.
- **Auditing risk_score_band and documentation_completeness:** Validate score calibration, error rates, and feature dependence by group; review completeness criteria for language/accommodation accessibility; test blinded re-review of Request Docs and Supervisor escalation decisions. *Trade-offs:* audit resource cost; may reveal need to rebuild score, creating transition inconsistency; documentation simplification could increase downstream eligibility work. Essential because these covariates gate legitimacy claims.
- *What not to do:* Do not remove Language Support Contact or Accommodation Review merely to improve cycle time. That would improve measured timeliness while harming substantive access and legal compliance.

# 8. Bottom-line assessment
The process does not appear demonstrably procedurally fair, but the evidence also does not support a finding of intentional discrimination.

Raw gaps overstate what can be attributed to group membership because case mix, legitimate need for language/accommodation support, and measured risk/prior-count differ substantially. After adjustment, Group D's timeliness and extra-review gaps largely attenuate, consistent with risk-driven routing - conditional on the contested assumption that risk_score_band is neutral, which remains unaudited.

More concerning are persistent adjusted burdens tied to rights-preserving steps: Group B retains -5.5pp approval, +2.6d cycle, +8.0pp Supervisor Review, +9.5pp Request Docs, +4.4pp appeal; Group C retains +4.1d cycle (largest) and +5.5pp Supervisor Review with 4.7d specialist wait and handover-intensive design. These indicate applicants using language and accommodation support face slower, more fragmented, more scrutinized procedures even after controlling for documented complexity, amplified by concentration on few bilingual/specialist staff and regional backlogs without SLA protection.

Overall assessment: **indeterminate on discriminatory intent, but substantively concerning on procedural fairness.** The pattern points to structural burden rather than parity alone. Fairness cannot be established without auditing risk scores and completeness measures, measuring support quality and real-time workload, and monitoring adjusted within-variant and wait-time disparities. Remedies should add capacity, set support-step timeliness guardrails, reduce handovers, and improve explanations - not remove support steps.
