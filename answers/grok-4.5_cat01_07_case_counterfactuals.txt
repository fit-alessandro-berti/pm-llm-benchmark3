## 1. Feasibility model

```text
Partial orders ( = must precede):

Common core:
  Register  Triage
  Triage  Identity Check
  Triage  (Document Check | Originals Receipt path)
  Identity Check  Assessment
  Document Check  Assessment
  Assessment  Decision  Notify

Originals path (when required):
  Triage  Originals Receipt  Document Check
  Identity Check may run  with waiting for Originals Receipt

High-risk / high-value:
  latest Assessment  final Compliance Review  Decision
  (preliminary compliance screen may run earlier but does not replace final CR)

Rework:
  Request More Info  Document Check_new  Assessment_new
  Assessment_new  (Compliance Review_new | reaffirmation)  Decision   [if high-risk/value]

Escalation (advisory):
  Escalation Advice may overlap other checks; does not replace final CR

Four-eyes:
  resource(final Assessment)  resource(final Compliance Review)

Case-specific admissible concurrency:
  K-104: Identity  Document (no originals); prelim S1/C1 work  Identity/Document; final CR after final Assessment
  K-219: Identity  wait(Originals); Document after Originals; Identity  Assessment  Document  Assessment
  K-337: same originals chain; multiple DC/Assess loops until decision policy stops rework
  K-411: Identity  Document if both tasks created and lock workaround applied
```

```text
Conditional / declarative constraints:
1. Register first; then Triage.
2. If originals_required: Originals Receipt  Document Check; else Document Check may start after Triage when tasked.
3. Identity Check and Document Check both complete before Assessment.
4. If high_risk  high_value: final Compliance Review after latest relevant Assessment and before Decision.
5. Assessment or Compliance Review may issue Request More Info; then new DC + new Assessment required; high-risk/value needs new or explicitly reaffirmed CR.
6. Decision  Notify.
7. Four-eyes: same person may not perform final Assessment and final Compliance Review.
8. Preliminary compliance  final compliance.
9. No resource performs two service intervals at once.
10. Queue times are forecasts, not guarantees.
11. C1 is sole certified compliance reviewer in window; S1 advisory may overlap.
12. K-337 intervention alters allowed decision policy: after Assessment 2, no second RMI; must approve-with-conditions or reject on available evidence (may not ignore discrepancy).
13. K-104: reserve provisional C1 slot + early S1 packet; final CR still after Assessment.
14. K-219: D2 substitution allowed at originals arrival; rework risk rises with handwriting.
15. K-411: controlled dual-task creation + real-time timestamps required; false concurrency from batch logging forbidden.
```

**Intervention character.** K-104 alters timing and early routing of advisory/compliance preparation (not final decision policy). K-219 alters resource routing (D1D2) and thereby timing and rework probability. K-337 alters allowed decision policy (forbids a second rework loop) and only secondarily timing. K-411 alters control-flow concurrency (task creation / lock workaround) and logging discipline; timing follows from earlier joint readiness for Assessment.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

**Factual mechanism:** Triage noticed sanctions ambiguity but did not escalate. Identity and complex Document Check (D1, long Monday queue) completed without compliance prep. A1 rediscovered ambiguity Tue afternoon; S1 advised Tue evening; C1 only then consumed a deep read Wed after a still-heavy compliance backlog—so most evidence was cold at final CR.

**Minimal intervention:** At Triage (Mon ~08:50), send preliminary escalation packet to S1 and place a provisional C1 reservation. Allow S1 (and limited C1 pre-read of known sanctions context) to run in parallel with Identity/Document. Do not waive final CR after final Assessment; do not change approve/reject policy.

**Temporal propagation:** Early S1 work can occur Mon–Tue while I1 and D1 run. Document Check remains on the long D1 complex path (still likely dominates readiness into Tue). Assessment still cannot start until both checks finish—so Assessment start may move only modestly (slightly earlier if any factual idle/coordination delay is removed, but not before document completion). The main saving is on the AssessmentCR handoff: C1 enters with partial context, cutting final CR service toward the lower end of 3–6 h (roughly 1–3 h prep credit) and possibly using an earlier reserved window Wed morning rather than Wed 13:10—if the reservation is real and not displaced by the two urgent compliance cases noted Mon 16:00. Decision/Notify then track the earlier CR end.

**Process-feasibility check:** High-risk/high-value still requires final CR after latest Assessment before Decision. Four-eyes: A1 assesses, C1 reviews—OK. Preliminary screen does not replace final CR. Resources: S1 advisory may overlap checks; C1 must not double-book service intervals—reservation must be an actual calendar hold. IdentityDocument allowed (no originals).

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: usable packet + honored early C1 slot  final CR Tue late or Wed morning, cycle ends ~Tue night–Wed noon (~0.75–1.5 d faster), same conditional approve. Base: prep saves ~1–2 h and trims queue wait modestly  finish Wed midday–afternoon (~0.5 d faster). Worst: incomplete packet forces full re-read; reservation canceled by urgent cases  little CR saving or other cases delayed; ambiguity unresolved earlier  similar end time, possible spillover harm.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Show that early packets systematically omit the documents D1 later flags, so C1 always re-reads fully; or Mon–Tue C1 utilization is already 100% with higher-priority work so reservations only reorder harm; or sanctions ambiguity is undetectable until Assessment interprets full document set.

### K-219

**Factual mechanism:** Triage routed handwriting to D1. Originals arrived Mon 17:30, but D1 was deep in backlog (snapshot D1 ~11–13 h Mon), so Document Check waited until Tue 09:30–13:30. Clean check enabled A2 Tue evening and Decision Wed morning. Delay was mostly D1 queue, not service difficulty.

**Minimal intervention:** At originals arrival (Mon 17:30), assign Document Check to D2 instead of waiting for D1. Keep Identity as factual (done Mon 16:20). No change to decision policy; still no compliance review required (not high-risk/value).

**Temporal propagation:** D2 Mon 16:00 snapshot queue ~5 h and falling overnight—D2 could start late Mon evening or early Tue, earlier than factual Tue 09:30. D2 standard service is slower (roughly 4–8 h vs D1’s accurate 4 h factual). If start ~Mon 20:00–Tue 07:00, clean completion could be ~Tue 04:00–Tue 15:00. Assessment then competes for A2 with Tue queues (A2 ~6–7 h forecast)—so Assessment may land Tue afternoon–evening rather than Tue 20:00, or similar if queue absorbs earliness. Decision could move into Tue evening–Wed early. If D2 requests clarification on handwriting, path inserts RMI (external 8–36 h) + new DC + new Assessment, erasing gains and possibly overshooting factual Wed 09:00 finish.

**Process-feasibility check:** Originals Receipt  Document Check preserved. Identity and Document both before Assessment. No CR required. M1 Decision before Notify. D2 single-threaded—assignment must respect D2’s other work. Rework rules apply if clarification issued.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: D2 starts soon after originals, no clarification, ~5–7 h service  Assessment Tue mid/late day  Decision Tue evening (~0.5–1.0 d faster), approve. Base: earlier start but slower service and partial queue absorption  ~0–0.5 d faster, still approve. Worst: handwriting clarification  +1–2 d beyond factual, extra DC/Assess load, possible customer friction.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** D2 historically triggers RMI on >40% of handwritten packs; or D2 night/early capacity is illusory (queue forecasts understate); or Assessment is the binding constraint so earlier DC completion does not move Decision.

### K-337

**Factual mechanism:** Supplier totals inconsistent across three document/assess cycles. After Assess2 Fri morning, a second RMI spanned the weekend to Mon; DC3/Assess3 then CR Tue and Decision Wed. Time cost is dominated by external response and weekend calendar, not internal service minutes alone. Factual terminal decision was approve after full clearance.

**Minimal intervention:** After Assessment 2 (Fri ~10:30), ban RMI2. Require Decision path on evidence then held: either reject or approve-with-conditions that explicitly address the remaining inconsistency—silent ignore forbidden. Still run Compliance Review after this (now final) Assessment because high-risk/high-value.

**Temporal propagation:** Removing RMI2+DC3+Assess3 eliminates external wait Fri–Mon and internal Mon–Tue work. From Assess2 end Fri 10:30, next is CR with C1. Thu–Fri compliance backlog is improving into next week snapshots, but Fri C1 load is uncertain—CR might start Fri afternoon, or more likely Mon when C1/M1 capacity and four-eyes sequencing are realistic, or late Fri if reserved. Decision then Fri evening–Mon/Tue rather than factual Wed. Cycle-time win is large because a weekend external delay is cut; the decision object changes (conditional/reject vs clean approve).

**Process-feasibility check:** Norm 7 is intentionally overridden by the intervention’s decision-policy change: no new DC/Assess after a forbidden second RMI. Final CR still required after latest Assessment (Assess2) before Decision. Four-eyes: A1 vs C1 OK. Discrepancy must appear in conditions or rejection rationale for compliance defensibility. M1 may need extra decision time under residual risk.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: C1 same-day Fri, conditional approve well-documented  finish Fri afternoon/evening (~4–5 d faster) with acceptable residual risk. Base: CR Mon, Decision Mon–Tue (~3–4 d faster), approve-with-conditions. Worst: C1/M1 refuse to clear residual discrepancy  reject or forced late external clarification anyway; appeal/loss exposure; little net benefit after disputes.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Policy/legal rules require resolved supplier totals before any approve-with-conditions; or residual discrepancy is material fraud risk making speed worthless; or C1 will always re-issue RMI effectively restoring the loop.

### K-411

**Factual mechanism:** Triage/legacy system created only Identity. Case lock blocked Document ownership until Identity completed Tue 13:20; Document then D2 15:00–19:30; Assessment waited into Wed morning as queues grew. Serialization was artifactual, not a norm requirement (no originals; digital standard docs allow IdentityDocument).

**Minimal intervention:** At Triage, create both verification tasks and apply a controlled lock workaround so I2 and D2 can work concurrently, with real-time timestamps (no later batch entry that fabricates overlap).

**Temporal propagation:** Identity factual window ~Tue 11:30–13:20. Document can start mid-morning Tue once tasked (D2 queue Tue AM moderate). Concurrent completion likely by ~Tue 13:20–17:00 (Document may finish near Identity or slightly after). Joint readiness for Assessment shifts from factual Tue 19:30 to roughly Tue mid-afternoon. Assessment queue Tue–Wed is non-trivial (A2 forecasts ~5–7 h)—so Assessment start may be Tue evening or still Wed morning; earliness partially queues away. Decision follows Assessment by manager availability (could remain Wed midday with a smaller pull-in, or late Tue if M1 available). Net cycle-time gain often smaller than raw parallelization arithmetic suggests.

**Process-feasibility check:** RegisterTriage both checks AssessmentDecisionNotify. No CR required. True concurrency must not violate resource single-tasking (I2 and D2 are different pools—OK). Workaround must leave auditable real-time stamps; false concurrency is a compliance defect. No four-eyes issue without CR.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best: both checks done by early Tue afternoon, A2 same-day slot, Decision Tue evening (~0.6–0.8 d faster). Base: checks parallelized but Assessment queue holds case overnight  Decision Wed late morning (~0.2–0.4 d faster). Worst: workaround logging failure / rework of audit trail; or Assessment congestion fully absorbs gain; simultaneous dual-tasking increases local WIP without throughput.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Legacy lock is a hard regulatory separation (not mere IT debt); or timestamp workaround fails audit; or Assessment is so congested that earlier readiness never changes completions for this class of case.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 early S1 + C1 reserve | Shifts C1/S1 demand earlier; slight  early C1 hold,  late cold CR minutes | Late CR wait for prepared cases; possibly Assessment idle gaps | C1 short-term reservation queue for non-reserved urgent cases | Other high-risk cases needing C1 Mon–Tue; S1 advisory clients | Ambiguous / often negative spillover if reserves > spare C1 | Reservation hoarding: small holds cascade when C1 is singleton |
| K-219 D2 at originals | Substitute D2 service for D1; expected service minutes  per case; RMI rate  | D1 queue for complex/handwriting backlog | D2 queue; rework DC/Assess if clarifications | Cases that need D2 generalist capacity; Assess pool if RMI | Mixed; fragile benefit under handwriting mix | Quality–speed tradeoff: mean faster, tail much slower with RMI |
| K-337 suppress 2nd RMI |  DC/Assess/external loops; earlier CR/Decision burst on residual-risk cases | DC and Assess rework queues; external waiting stock | C1/M1 decision load earlier; appeals/secondary reviews | Cases sharing C1/M1; future audit/appeals work | Faster locally; system risk migrates to quality/appeals | Calendar/weekend external delays dominate—cutting one loop removes whole days nonlinearly |
| K-411 parallelize ID | Same check work earlier and overlapped; brief  concurrent I+D WIP | Artificial serial wait on Document | Assessment queue (earlier arrivals); peak I/D concurrency | Cases behind in Assessment; identity/document peers at peak | Mild positive if Assess has slack; else bottleneck migration | Throughput limited by downstream Assess: parallelization moves delay, doesn’t destroy it |

**100-case weekly rollout.** Scaling any single-case counterfactual to ~100 similar cases per week changes the problem from path rewriting to queue physics. C1 is a singleton: a K-104-style policy that reserves provisional compliance slots for every high-risk/high-value triage would exhaust Mon–Tue capacity, displace the “two urgent compliance cases” pattern into chronic priority fights, and can increase average CR waiting even if prepared cases consume 1–3 h less service. Preliminary S1 packets help only if packet quality is high; at volume, incomplete packets create double handling (read early, read again), raising total C1 effort.

D1D2 substitution (K-219 class) at scale rebalances document queues. Routing all “awkward but standard” work to D2 shrinks D1’s complex corporate backlog—good for K-104-like cases—but lengthens D2 waits and amplifies clarification tails on handwriting. Interference appears as a heavier Assessment diet of rework cases; average cycle time can worsen while a median clean path looks better. Avoided rework from K-337-type stop-rules reduces DC/Assess throughput demand and removes weekend-external pileups on Mondays, which is a real system gift; however, the same wave delivers earlier, harder CR/Decision tasks and may increase appeals, secondary reviews, or loss-given-decision—work that may not sit in the same queues the pilot measures.

Assessment is the likely migration point for K-411-style parallelization: if 100 cases a week stop serializing Identity before Document, joint readiness advances by hours into already busy Assess pools (snapshots show multi-hour A1/A2 waits midweek). Without added Assess capacity, completion-time CDFs move little while WIP and perceived “stuck in assess” complaints rise. Weekend and external-response effects dominate K-337 economics: suppressing loops that bridge FriMon removes nonlinear calendar delay unmatched by internal service tweaks. Finally, timestamp behavior vs true concurrency matters at audit scale—batch entry can invent parallel paths in logs, corrupting process-mining feedback and masking whether the workaround is safe. System-wide sign is therefore not the sum of local deltas: scarce C1 reservations and Assess bottleneck migration can flip local wins into global slowdowns or silent risk transfer.

## 7. Final synthesis

K-411’s controlled parallelization is the most robust intervention among the four: it repairs an artificial serial constraint already allowed by norms for non-originals digital work, preserves decision policy, and fails soft when Assessment capacity absorbs earliness. Its benefits are modest but directionally stable, and a pilot can separate true concurrency from queue displacement with logging checks.

K-337 is the most dangerous despite the largest potential speed-up. Forbidding the second `Request More Info` is not a timing tweak; it rewrites decision policy on high-risk/high-value evidence. Days disappear because weekend external waits disappear, yet residual discrepancy, appeal exposure, and compliance defensibility can worsen. Local counterfactuals that keep a clean “approve” while deleting unresolved issues would be process-illegal under the intervention’s own rule and understate risk.

Local case counterfactuals are least transportable for K-104-style C1 reservation and, secondarily, for K-219-style D2 substitution. A single provisional C1 hold barely moves a singleton bottleneck; one hundred holds reorder the entire compliance market and can delay urgent non-target cases. Likewise, one handwriting case on D2 is a small routing experiment; at volume it reshapes D1/D2 specialization and rework tails. K-411 transportability fails when Assessment is saturated: case-level parallel gains vanish system-wide without migrating the bottleneck.

Most valuable additional data: C1 interval calendar with priority and cancel reasons; Assessment arrival vs start distributions by hour; D1/D2 clarification rates by document class (handwritten vs complex corporate); timestamp provenance (real-time vs batch); external RMI response-time distributions including weekends; and post-decision appeal/loss markers linked to conditional approvals. Queue forecasts should be stored as forecasts alongside realizations to calibrate interference models before policy rollout.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **structural** — Normative prerequisites (checks before Assessment; final CR after latest Assessment for high-risk/value; Decision before Notify; four-eyes AC) bind all counterfactual paths unless an intervention explicitly changes decision policy (K-337 only).  
2. **temporal** — Queue snapshot figures are uncertain forecasts; counterfactual starts use ranges, not deterministic subtraction of listed queue hours.  
3. **resource** — C1 remains the only fully certified compliance reviewer; reservations compete with non-pilot urgent work rather than creating new capacity.  
4. **resource** — D1 retains advantage on complex corporate documents; D2 is slower on average and more clarification-prone on handwriting.  
5. **behavioral** — Early escalation packets for K-104 are partially informative but incomplete until Document Check finishes; they never waive final CR.  
6. **quality** — Suppressing K-337’s second RMI forces explicit conditional approval or rejection; actors do not silently ignore the residual inconsistency.  
7. **temporal** — External RMI durations (especially weekend-spanning) dominate internal service variance for rework-heavy paths.  
8. **interference** — Advancing readiness (parallel ID or earlier DC) increases early arrivals to Assessment/Compliance without automatically increasing their service capacity, so bottlenecks can migrate downstream.
