## 2. Process-model consistency checks

The validation suite below runs per feature and per explanation artifact (global importance, sequence pattern, local attribution). Each check produces machine-readable verdicts feeding a leakage gate in CI.

**(a) Prefix-time availability check.** For every case, truncate the trace at t0 (immediately after the first `Coverage Check Completed`) and recompute every feature from the truncated prefix plus point-in-time reference data. Compare against the value stored in the training matrix. Any systematic mismatch means the stored feature used post-t0 information. This is the single most powerful test: it catches `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`, `last_resource_role`, and the SQL and snapshot defects (`prior_claim_count_before_t0`, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`), without needing to know how each feature is built. Requirement: every feature row must carry an `available_at` timestamp, and the test asserts `available_at <= t0`.

**(b) Activity-order feasibility (alignment/footprint checks).** Encode the normative process as a Petri net or BPMN model and compute alignments between each trace and the model. Deviations flag orderings the model forbids. A footprint (directly-follows relation) matrix of the normative model gives a fast static filter for pattern explanations: `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` violates the footprint (Closed is a sink after final decision) and is marked impossible; `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` conforms to the appeal-loop and is marked legitimate. Limitation: alignments assume the normative model is correct and complete; genuine but unmodeled behavior (e.g., administrative reopenings) would be falsely flagged, so "impossible" verdicts should route to a data-quality queue plus a domain review, not silent deletion.

**(c) Same-timestamp ambiguity check.** Scan all traces for equal-timestamp event pairs. Where a reliable ingestion sequence index exists, use it to fix the order. Where it is missing (Case Z), treat the pair as an unordered set: any directly-follows pattern spanning the pair is marked "order-unverifiable" and excluded from pattern-explanation inputs. Report the fraction of traces affected; if large, the sequence explainer's outputs are globally suspect. Limitation: even a sequence index only reflects ingestion order, which may differ from real-world order across source systems; cross-system pairs need clock-skew analysis.

**(d) Complete-trace-information detection.** Two complementary tests. Lineage-static: parse feature definitions/SQL for aggregations without a `<= t0` predicate (catches the `extraction_date` bug directly). Dynamic sensitivity: recompute the feature on progressively longer prefixes of the same case; a feature whose value at "t0-labelled" rows changes as the suffix grows is trace-complete by construction. Limitation: the dynamic test needs replayable raw events; features from non-replayable external systems (the mutable `risk_score_current`) can only be certified by the source system storing versions.

**(e) Enabled / impossible / future-possible classification at t0.** Replay the t0 prefix on the normative model and compute the marking. Every activity referenced by an explanation is classified: *enabled* (can occur next: `Documents Requested`, `Damage Assessment Completed`, `Fraud Investigation Opened` if triggered), *future-possible* (reachable but not yet enabled: `Settlement Decision Recorded`, `Appeal`, `Claim Closed`), or *impossible in this position* (e.g., assessment after closure). Explanations may only cite enabled activities or already-observed prefix events; future-possible references indicate leakage even when process-legal — this is exactly the distinction between the legitimate-but-leaked `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` and the impossible `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`.

**(f) Repeated Settlement Decision legitimacy (declarative checks).** DECLARE-style constraints: `Precedence(Settlement Decision Recorded[2nd], Appeal)` — a second decision must be preceded by an appeal; `Precedence(Appeal, Settlement Decision Recorded in {Partial, Deny})` — appeals only after Partial/Deny; `Response(Appeal, Settlement Decision Recorded)` — every appeal is eventually answered. Traces with two decisions and no interleaved appeal are data-quality defects (duplicate recording), not real reconsiderations, and must not feed "decision count" features. Limitation: declarative constraints check the coarse label; they cannot distinguish an appeal-decision from a duplicated original decision when timestamps collide — combine with check (c).

**(g) DFG caveat.** A directly-follows graph mined from the log is descriptive: a frequent edge may reflect noise, batching, or timestamp artifacts (the Case Z edge could easily be "frequent" if a nightly closure job writes both events at once). Frequency is never promoted to a normative rule; the normative model in (b) and the constraints in (f) are curated with domain owners, and the DFG is used only to discover candidate deviations for human review.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

**Defensible predictive signals** — `initial_damage_complexity`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, `claimant_channel` (also listed under proxy review), `initial_reserve` (after write-once verification). May be claimed: measured at or before t0, statistically associated with breach, usable for ranking/triage. May not be claimed: that they cause breaches, or that changing them would change outcomes (you cannot "lower" a claim's complexity; you can only resource it differently). Actionability is indirect: route high-complexity/high-reserve claims to senior assessors early.

**Process-state / congestion signals** — `queue_length_at_t0`; after repair, `team_assigned_at_t0`; a t0-available fraud-trigger flag if one exists. May be claimed: they describe the operational state the case enters, and they are the most operationally actionable class — congestion is something management can address (staffing, load balancing). May not be claimed: a per-case causal effect estimate from SHAP values; the attribution says the model uses queue length, not that clearing 14 queue positions removes 0.14 of breach probability. Interventional claims require an experiment or quasi-experimental design.

**Proxies requiring fairness/robustness review** — `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (after SQL repair; claimant-behavior history can encode segment effects). May be claimed: predictive contribution under the current data distribution. May not be claimed: any causal or dispositional story ("region drives delay", "repeat claimants are complex"). Required before production use: disparate-impact and error-rate analysis across region/segment; a mediation attempt replacing the proxy with the mechanism it stands for (repair-network coverage, document-quality metrics); robustness check that predictions do not degrade sharply for underrepresented regions. Do not reflexively delete: removing region while keeping correlated features can hide, not remove, the disparity — review first, then decide.

**Drift/calendar proxies** — `calendar_week` (and holiday period). May be claimed: seasonal association in the training window. May not be claimed: stability ("week 52 is risky" is really "the holiday staffing regime is risky", and the aligned policy change means part of the signal is a one-off regime shift). Remediation: encode the mechanism (holiday flag, planned staffing, backlog), use time-based validation splits, and monitor for concept drift; expect this feature's importance to be unstable across years.

**Unusable until lineage is repaired** — `final_handler_team` (needs assignment-at-t0 reconstruction), `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (SQL cutoff fix), `same_day_document_count_snapshot` (event-timestamped recount), `risk_score_current` (versioned scores), employee experience (event-date join), `initial_reserve` only if it turns out to be mutable. May be claimed: nothing, until each is recomputed point-in-time and the model retrained; the current attributions for these features are not interpretable because the values themselves are of unknown vintage.

**Not repairable at this prediction point** — `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`, `last_resource_role`, and all five sequence patterns as t0 features (four are merely future; one is also impossible/artifactual). Several become legitimate prefix features at later prediction points.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

Priority 1 — Stop the active leakage (immediate). Remove `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`, `last_resource_role`, and all sequence-pattern features from the t0 model; fix the `prior_claim_count_before_t0` SQL cutoff to `< t0`; retrain and re-baseline. Communicate that all previously issued explanations for this model are withdrawn.

Priority 2 — Point-in-time feature store. Every feature row must carry (feature_value, event_time, available_at) and every training join must be an as-of join on `available_at <= t0`. Mutable sources (risk scores, reserves, team assignments, employee experience) must be versioned in the source system or snapshotted with timestamps; forbid "current_*" fields in training schemas by naming convention and CI lint.

Priority 3 — Prefix-only recomputation harness in CI. Automate check 2(a): truncate each case at each prediction point, recompute features, and diff against the training matrix. Run on every feature-pipeline change and on a sampled basis in production. This converts leakage from an audit finding into a failing test.

Priority 4 — Process-conformance test suite. Maintain the curated normative model and declarative constraints (Section 2b, 2e, 2f); classify every activity referenced by any explanation as enabled/future-possible/impossible at the prediction point; enforce a sequence index for equal-timestamp events and quarantine order-unverifiable pairs; keep the DFG strictly descriptive.

Priority 5 — Recalibrate and re-evaluate after feature removal. Expect discrimination (AUC) to drop once leaked features are gone — that drop is the honest performance. Recalibrate probabilities (e.g., isotonic/Platt on a temporal holdout), re-derive alerting thresholds against the 10-day SLA, and report the before/after gap to stakeholders as the measured cost of the previous leakage.

Priority 6 — Explanation stability testing. Before publishing explanations: bootstrap/retrain-seed stability of global importances; perturbation tests (±5 min queue snapshot jitter, timestamp noise) on local attributions; agreement checks across explainers. Publish only drivers that are stable; unstable attributions are reported as "model-internal, not interpretable."

Priority 7 — Proxy and fairness program. For `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, and repaired history features: disparate error-rate analysis, mechanism-substitution experiments (replace region with repair-network coverage and re-measure), and a documented decision on retention with monitoring — explicitly not blanket deletion, which can mask rather than remove disparity.

Priority 8 — Drift and schema monitoring. Monitor feature distributions, prefix-variant frequencies, and conformance-violation rates for drift (the policy change aligned with `calendar_week` shows regime shifts are real); alert on schema changes in upstream systems (new mutable fields, changed refresh times like the 23:00 snapshot); re-run the full leakage and conformance suite on every upstream change and at a fixed cadence, and version explanations alongside model and feature-store versions so any published explanation can be traced to the exact lineage that produced it.
