## 1. Materiality Framework

### Severity Dimensions

| Dimension | Levels (1–5) | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Control Objective Violated** | 1 = cosmetic; 5 = core fiduciary/regulatory control bypassed | Not all process steps carry equal weight. Payment-before-approval (P3) is existential; a duplicate notification is not. |
| **Amount / Financial Exposure** | 1 = < EUR 2,000; 5 = > EUR 100,000 | Larger claims amplify every control failure's consequence. |
| **Reversibility** | 1 = fully reversible pre-payment; 5 = funds dispersed, beneficiary untraceable | A payment that has not yet cleared is less severe than one accepted by the bank. |
| **Timing / Sequencing** | 1 = order preserved; 5 = reversed (e.g., pay before approve) | Sequencing violations can void insurance coverage or trigger regulatory penalties. |
| **Customer / Regulatory Harm** | 1 = no harm possible; 5 = regulatory-reportable, claimant-rights violated | Delayed notification beyond the 24-hour window, missing fraud evidence for high-value claims. |
| **Recurrence** | 1 = isolated; 5 = systematic across portfolio | A pattern implies a systemic control design gap, not an individual error. |
| **Evidence Reliability** | 1 = immutable, linked audit trail; 5 = no evidence or mutable-only evidence | The absence of a log event is not proof an activity did not occur—but mutable-only evidence is weak. |
| **Model Scope** | 1 = fully in scope; 5 = out of scope entirely | When the process tree was not designed for the case type, raw alignment cost is a scope artifact, not a compliance signal. |

### Composite Severity (S)

S is not a simple sum. A case scores 5 on the composite if **any single dimension** reaches 5 **and** at least one other dimension reaches  3. This "high-watermark with corroboration" rule prevents both false negatives (ignoring a severe single failure) and false positives (inflating a trivial event with one anomalous dimension).

### Confidence vs. Severity

| | Definition | Influenced by |
|---|---|---|
| **Severity** | How bad is it *if the deviation occurred as inferred*? | Control objective, amount, timing, reversibility |
| **Confidence** | How certain are we that the deviation *actually occurred*? | Evidence availability, label consistency, alternative explanations, recurrence pattern |

A case can be **high severity / low confidence** (e.g., CL-608: missing notification but CRM evidence may exist) or **low severity / high confidence** (e.g., CL-603: duplicate notification definitely occurred, but it's harmless). Both dimensions must be reported; neither substitutes for the other.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

### The Core Argument

Process alignment engines measure **edit distance** between a log trace and a model trace under a cost function. This distance answers: "How many insertions, deletions, or substitutions are needed to make the log match the model?" It does **not** answer: "How much harm did this deviation cause?" The two questions are related but not equivalent, and they can diverge dramatically.

### Demonstrating the Divergence with Supplied Cases

**1. Zero Cost, High Risk: CL-609 and CL-610 (Data-Aware Policy Failures)**

Both cases have raw alignment cost = 0 because the log perfectly matches the model. The model permits Auto Approve, and Auto Approve occurred. However, CL-609 (EUR 18,000, amount > EUR 5,000) and CL-610 (EUR 4,500, but bank changed 1 hour prior) both violate P1. The cost function cannot detect these violations because the process tree does not encode P1's data conditions. The alignment engine optimizes over **control-flow structure**, not over **data predicates**. When the model is too permissive, perfect alignment means perfect compliance with the wrong standard.

**Why this matters:** The cost function was configured uniformly—cost 1 for every visible move. It has no mechanism to weight a missing Fraud Screen more heavily than a missing notification, or to check whether the Auto Approve branch was actually permitted given the case data. A cost function designed for business risk would need to be **data-aware** and **policy-encoded**, not just structural.

**2. High Cost, Zero Risk: CL-607 and CL-612 (Out-of-Scope Cases)**

CL-607 (denied claim) has raw cost = 4. CL-612 (withdrawn claim) has raw cost = 4. In both cases, the model expects Assess Loss  Approve  Pay  Notify, but the claim was denied or withdrawn before reaching approval. No money moved. No control was violated. The high cost is entirely an artifact of the model's scope: it was designed for payable claims and does not represent denial or withdrawal paths.

**Why this matters:** The cost function treats every model move as equally important. Missing an approval step costs the same whether the claim was supposed to be approved (control failure) or was never supposed to be approved (out of scope). The cost function cannot distinguish "the model expected X and X didn't happen because something went wrong" from "the model expected X and X didn't happen because the case took a different legitimate path."

**3. Low Cost, High Risk: CL-602 (Missing Fraud Screen on EUR 480K)**

Raw cost = 1. The only gap is one model move (Fraud Screen) that has no matching log event. But this single missing step involves a EUR 480,000 HIGH-risk claim with a recent beneficiary-bank change—the exact scenario P2 was designed to catch. The cost function assigns cost 1 to the missing Fraud Screen, the same cost it assigns to a duplicate notification event (CL-603) or a label mismatch (CL-611). The cost function has no mechanism to scale cost by amount, risk level, or policy criticality.

**Why this matters:** In a uniform cost function, a missing Fraud Screen on a EUR 480K claim costs the same as a missing Fraud Screen on a EUR 50 claim. Business risk scales with amount and context; alignment cost does not (unless explicitly designed to).

**4. Same Cost, Different Risk: CL-603 vs. CL-602 vs. CL-606 vs. CL-608**

All four cases have raw cost = 1. But:
- CL-603: A duplicate notification event (harmless noise, severity 1).
- CL-602: A missing Fraud Screen on EUR 480K (severe control gap, severity 5).
- CL-606: A missing core-log event for an externally performed assessment (moderate gap, severity 2).
- CL-608: A missing notification in the core log, with mutable CRM evidence (moderate gap, severity 2).

The cost function assigns identical scores to fundamentally different situations because it counts **moves**, not **consequences**.

**5. Silent Moves as Fitting: CL-605**

Raw cost = 0. The alignment includes a `T(tau,c=0)` hidden model move. This is technically fitting—the Petri net has a silent transition that the alignment engine used to route the trace. The cost is 0 because the hidden move has cost 0 by design. This is correct behavior: silent transitions are model-internal routing mechanisms, not business activities. But it illustrates that the cost function is optimized for **model fidelity**, not **business significance**. A hidden transition that routes a EUR 800 claim to Auto Approve has the same cost (0) as a hidden transition that would route a EUR 500,000 claim to Auto Approve.

### Summary: Structural Causes of the Divergence

| Factor | How It Causes Divergence |
|---|---|
| **Cost-function design** | Uniform cost (1 per move) treats all moves equally. It cannot weight by amount, risk, or policy criticality. |
| **Model scope** | Out-of-scope cases produce high costs that are scope artifacts, not risk signals. |
| **Hidden moves** | Silent transitions cost 0 regardless of their routing implications. |
| **Data conditions** | The cost function operates on control-flow structure, not on data predicates. P1/P2 violations are invisible. |
| **Activity granularity** | A missing Fraud Screen and a duplicate notification each cost 1, but their business significance differs by orders of magnitude. |
| **Duplicated telemetry** | UI events and transport retries inflate cost without indicating any business deviation. |
| **Missing-but-external evidence** | A missing log event costs the same whether the activity was genuinely skipped or performed externally. The cost function cannot access external evidence. |
| **Irreversibility** | The cost function does not distinguish between a deviation that can be corrected (e.g., a missing notification that can be re-sent) and one that cannot (e.g., a payment that has already cleared). |

### The Right Use of Alignment Cost

Raw alignment cost is a **screening signal**, not a **risk assessment**. It tells you where to look, not how bad it is. A high-cost case warrants investigation because it has more deviations; a zero-cost case warrants investigation if data-aware policies might be violated. The risk assessment must be performed separately, using the materiality framework defined in Section 1, with business context, policy overlays, and evidence reliability layered on top of the structural alignment.
