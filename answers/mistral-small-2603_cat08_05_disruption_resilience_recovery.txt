# Cyber Incident Response Analysis and Recovery Redesign

## 1. Disruption Timeline

**T0 (Day -30 to Day -1): Baseline Operations**
- **Operational State**: Fully automated warehouse management system with end-to-end traceability
- **Dominant Process Variants**: Standard order-to-delivery workflow with electronic authorization, scanning, and real-time inventory synchronization
- **Risk Posture**: Low (0.8% traceability exceptions, 0.4% cold-chain docs missing, 0.2% controlled-substance gaps)
- **Performance**: Hospital-critical median cycle time 8h, Pharmacy-standard 23h, Wholesale-bulk 36h

**T1 (Day 0 morning): Cyber Incident**
- **Operational State**: System failure disables WMS and parts of order-to-delivery workflow
- **Risk Posture**: Immediate threat to critical supply chains (hospitals)

**T2 (Day 0 afternoon to Day 2): Manual Workaround Phase**
- **Operational State**: Complete manual operation with printed snapshots, phone confirmations, and spreadsheets
- **Dominant Process Variants**:
  - Hospital-critical fast lane (A)
  - Manual authorization checks (B)
  - Skipped system authorization (C)
  - Manual inventory snapshots (D)
  - Manual pick lists (E)
  - Temporary release controls (F)
  - Dispatch without scans (G)
  - Phone proof of delivery (H)
- **Risk Posture**: High (9.5% traceability exceptions, 4.8% cold-chain gaps, 2.9% controlled-substance gaps)
- **Performance Degradation**: Hospital-critical fill rate 91% (protected), Pharmacy-standard 61% (severely impacted)

**T3 (Day 3 to Day 6): Partial Recovery**
- **Operational State**: Order intake system returns but inventory synchronization unreliable
- **Dominant Process Variants**:
  - Backlog prioritization (J)
  - Reconciliation of manual reservations (K)
  - Continued manual controls
- **Risk Posture**: Moderate (5.4% traceability exceptions, 2.3% cold-chain gaps)
- **Performance Improvement**: Hospital-critical median cycle time 9h, Pharmacy-standard 38h

**T4 (Day 7 to Day 21): Stabilization**
- **Operational State**: Systems mostly restored but backlog and audit remediation continue
- **Dominant Process Variants**:
  - Formal backlog prioritization (J)
  - Reconciliation flows (K)
  - Audit remediation (L)
- **Risk Posture**: Moderate (2.2% traceability exceptions, 0.9% cold-chain gaps)
- **Performance Recovery**: Approaching baseline metrics
- **Hidden Workload**: 2,300 cases in audit remediation queue after operational recovery

## 3. Resilience versus Concealed Noncompliance

**True Resilience** (Adaptations that preserved critical functions while maintaining essential controls):
- **A (Hospital-critical fast lane)**: Protected life-saving supply chains while maintaining 91% fill rate
- **B (Manual authorization check)**: Preserved compliance quality despite system failure
- **F (Temporary release control)**: Reduced controlled-substance errors through manual sampling
- **J (Backlog prioritization)**: Systematic approach to restoring order after systems returned
- **K (Reconcile manual reservation)**: Corrected inventory inaccuracies introduced during manual operations

**Risky Shortcuts** (Adaptations that preserved throughput but compromised essential controls):
- **C (Skip system authorization check)**: Created potential authorization gaps for ambient products
- **G (Dispatch without scan)**: Created the largest traceability gap (2,600 cases)
- **H (Phone proof of delivery)**: Weak evidence for controlled products and cold-chain items

**Ambiguous Adaptations** (Dependent on implementation and risk appetite):
- **D (Manual inventory snapshot)**: Reasonable early but became risky as inventory became stale
- **E (Manual pick list)**: Necessary for physical flow but caused downstream traceability issues
- **I (Defer noncritical order)**: Protected critical capacity but increased customer dissatisfaction
- **L (Audit remediation)**: Restored compliance but created hidden workload after operational recovery

**Key Distinction**:
- **Resilience** = Preserving critical functions while maintaining essential controls (A, B, F, J, K)
- **Concealed Noncompliance** = Preserving throughput at the expense of essential controls (C, G, H)
- **Ambiguous** = Depends on implementation quality and risk tolerance (D, E, I, L)

## 4. Recovery Process Model

**Future Disruption Recovery Process (POWL-style notation)**

```
[Start]  {Incident Detected}
     {Trigger Conditions: System failure >30min OR >20% orders impacted}
     [Triage Center Activation]
         [Customer Criticality Assessment]
             Hospital-critical  [Fast Lane Activation]
             Pharmacy-standard  [Standard Lane]
             Wholesale-bulk  [Deferral Queue]
         [Product Risk Assessment]
             Controlled-substance  [Enhanced Controls]
             Cold-chain  [Temperature Monitoring]
             Recall-sensitive  [Quarantine Check]
     [Minimum Controls by Risk Tier]
        Tier 1 (Hospital-critical):
            - Customer authorization verification (manual if needed)
            - Inventory reservation with time-stamped snapshot
            - Pick with scan-at-pickup
            - Temperature/security check for controlled/cold-chain
            - System scan before dispatch
            - Tiered proof of delivery (scan for high-risk, phone for standard)
        Tier 2 (Pharmacy-standard):
            - Customer authorization verification
            - Inventory reservation
            - Pick with scan-at-pickup
            - System scan before dispatch
            - Standard proof of delivery
        Tier 3 (Wholesale-bulk):
            - Customer authorization verification
            - Deferral if system unavailable >4 hours
     [Manual Evidence Capture]
        - Time-stamped photos of pick lists
        - Voice recordings of critical authorization checks
        - Temperature logs for cold-chain items
        - Controlled-substance dual-control signatures
     [Backlog Prioritization Rules]
        1. Hospital-critical orders
        2. Pharmacy-standard orders by due date
        3. Wholesale-bulk orders by margin
        - Formal escalation for >24h delays
     [Reconciliation Flow]
        - Real-time inventory updates for critical items
        - Daily reconciliation for all manual reservations
        - Automated flagging of >4h stale inventory snapshots
     [Audit-Remediation Flow]
        - Real-time alerts for missing controls
        - 24-hour remediation SLA for critical items
        - Weekly compliance reports during incident
     [Return to Normal Operations]
        - All manual controls validated against system records
        - 100% scan reconciliation
        - Customer notifications for any delays
```

## 5. Resilience Indicators

**Leading Indicators** (Early warning signs):
- System health metrics (response time, error rates)
- Order intake volume anomalies (>15% deviation from baseline)
- Inventory synchronization lag (>1 hour behind)
- Manual intervention rate (>5% of orders)
- Compliance staff utilization (>80%)

**Lagging Indicators** (Post-incident metrics):
- **Throughput Protection**:
  - Hospital-critical fill rate (>95% target)
  - Pharmacy-standard fill rate (>85% target)
  - Wholesale-bulk deferral rate (<10%)
- **Service Protection**:
  - Median cycle time recovery within 24h of system restoration
  - p90 cycle time within 120% of baseline
- **Traceability**:
  - Traceability exceptions (<2%)
  - Cold-chain documentation missing (<1%)
  - Controlled-substance dual-control missing (<0.5%)
- **Control Completeness**:
  - Authorization verification rate (>99%)
  - Scan completion rate (>98%)
  - Proof of delivery rate (>97%)
- **Backlog Management**:
  - Backlog age (<48h for critical orders)
  - Deferral communication SLA met (within 2h)
- **Rework**:
  - Rework/correction rate (<5%)
  - Cancellation rate (<3%)
- **Fairness**:
  - Customer satisfaction delta (<10% for any segment)
  - Escalation rate for non-critical orders (<5%)

## 7. Management Trade-off Statement

Management must accept that during severe disruptions:
- **Acceptable**: Temporary relaxation of non-critical controls (e.g., ambient product authorization) when life-saving supply chains are protected
- **Unacceptable**: Any relaxation of controls for controlled substances, cold-chain items, or recall-sensitive products
- **Evidence to Change Recommendation**: If any adaptation shows >1% compliance gap in critical controls during incidents, the relaxation must be immediately discontinued in favor of full compliance, even if it reduces throughput.

The key is balancing mission-critical protection with essential compliance. The hospital-critical fast lane (A) demonstrates this balance can be achieved, while the skipped authorization checks (C) and dispatch without scans (G) show where lines must not be crossed. Future resilience depends on pre-defining these boundaries and investing in the systems and processes to maintain them during disruptions.
