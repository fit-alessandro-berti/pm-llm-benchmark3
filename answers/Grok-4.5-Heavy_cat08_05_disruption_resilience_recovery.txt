**1. Disruption timeline**

**T0 (Day -30 to Day -1) – Baseline**  
Operational state: Fully functional electronic WMS and order-to-delivery systems. Dominant process (DFG): START → Receive order (42 000) → Validate authorization → Inventory reservation → Pick → Pack → Quality/release check → Dispatch → Proof of delivery → Invoice close, with limited side paths (compliance holds ~700, backorders ~900, repacks ~450).  
Risk posture: Low and controlled. Traceability exceptions 0.8 %, cold-chain temperature documentation missing 0.4 %, controlled-substance dual-control missing 0.2 %, rework 3.5 %, cancellation 2.1 %. Conformance fitness 0.96 (91 % fitting traces). Median cycle times: Hospital-critical 8 h, Pharmacy-standard 23 h, Wholesale-bulk 36 h (p90 15 h / 49 h / 72 h). Additional electronic controls enforced for Cold-chain and Controlled-substance products.

**T1 (Day 0 morning) – Incident onset**  
Operational state: Warehouse management system becomes unavailable after cyber incident. Electronic authorization, inventory reservation, scanning and quality-release paths are disrupted. Transition to human-mediated coordination begins.

**T2 (Day 0 afternoon to Day 2) – Manual workaround phase**  
Operational state: Complete reliance on offline methods. Dominant process variants (DFG): Receive → Emergency triage (5 100) or Defer noncritical (500) → Manual authorization check (2 500) / Skip system authorization (1 600) / Hospital-critical fast lane (1 000) → Manual inventory snapshot check → Manual pick list (3 920) → Manual pack → Temporary release control (1 650) or Dispatch without scan (~3 250 combined paths) → Phone proof of delivery (2 600) → Spreadsheet invoice hold. Backlog queue and cancellations also active.  
Risk posture: High and partially concealed. Traceability exceptions rose to 9.5 %, cold-chain missing 4.8 %, controlled dual-control missing 2.9 %, rework 12.5 %, cancellation 7.8 %. Hospital-critical fill rate held at 91 % (median cycle 10 h / p90 21 h); Pharmacy-standard fill fell to 61 % (median 54 h / p90 118 h); Wholesale-bulk median 96 h. Conformance fitness collapsed to 0.71 (44 % fitting traces). Move-on-log deviations concentrated on skip-authorization, dispatch-without-scan and phone PoD. 5 600 cases.

**T3 (Day 3 to Day 6) – Partial recovery**  
Operational state: Order intake restored; inventory synchronization and scanning remain unreliable. Mix of returning electronic paths and residual manual / backlog-prioritization / reconcile paths.  
Risk posture: Moderating but still elevated. Traceability 5.4 %, cold-chain 2.3 %, controlled 1.4 %, rework 9.0 %, cancellation 5.2 %. Cycle times improving (Hospital 9 h, Pharmacy 38 h, Wholesale 84 h) yet remain above baseline for non-critical segments. Conformance fitness 0.82 (59 % fitting). 11 400 cases.

**T4 (Day 7 to Day 21) – Stabilization**  
Operational state: Normal systems mostly restored. Dominant residual paths include Backlog prioritization (1 500), Reconcile manual reservation (1 400), Proof-of-delivery remediation (1 200) and especially Invoice close → Audit remediation (2 300).  
Risk posture: Operational metrics approaching baseline while a compliance tail persists. Traceability still 2.2 %, cold-chain 0.9 %, controlled 0.5 %, rework 5.8 %, cancellation 3.0 %. Hospital median returned to 8 h; Pharmacy 28 h and Wholesale 58 h remain elevated (p90 still stretched). Conformance fitness recovered to 0.90 (76 % fitting), yet the audit-remediation queue continued after cycle-time metrics appeared recovered. 35 000 cases.

Overall temporal pattern: Sharp collapse of control completeness and conformance in T2 while Hospital-critical throughput was deliberately protected; gradual operational recovery in T3–T4 that outpaced full restoration of real-time evidence, leaving measurable residual risk and hidden workload.

**3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance**

Resilience is the capacity to maintain essential outcomes—critical medicine supply to hospitals and product integrity—under disruption by purposeful adaptation that preserves or compensates for core controls. Concealed noncompliance is the creation of process gaps that temporarily sustain throughput or apparent recovery metrics but leave undetected or deferred violations of authorization, traceability, temperature or dual-control requirements. These gaps surface later as elevated exception rates, rework, audit queues and regulatory exposure.

True resilience adaptations in this case:
- A (Hospital-critical fast lane) and I (Defer noncritical) explicitly prioritized mission-critical demand, holding Hospital fill rate at 91 % and median cycle near baseline while accepting degradation elsewhere.
- B (Manual authorization) substituted trained human control for the unavailable system.
- F (Temporary release control) applied targeted pharmacist oversight, especially full coverage of controlled-substance orders.
- J (Backlog prioritization) restored order according to criticality and risk once systems partially returned.
- K (Reconcile manual reservation) restored inventory accuracy.

Risky shortcuts / concealed noncompliance:
- C (Skip system authorization) created an unverifiable eligibility gap for ~1 600 cases.
- G (Dispatch without scan) eliminated electronic chain-of-custody for the largest volume of cases and was the primary driver of the jump in traceability exceptions from 0.8 % to 9.5 %.
- E (Manual pick list) combined with G forced systematic later reconstruction of evidence.
- H (Phone proof of delivery) produced weak, non-contemporaneous evidence unsuitable for controlled products.
- D (Manual inventory snapshot) became hazardous once the morning printout aged.

Ambiguous (context- and risk-appetite-dependent):
- A when full authorization was bypassed without a compensating B-style check.
- F was strong for controlled substances but incomplete for cold-chain temperature documentation.
- J if prioritization rules were informal.
- L (Audit remediation) is necessary cleanup, yet after-the-fact remediation of 2 300 cases is not equivalent to real-time compliance; it creates a long hidden workload after operational metrics appear recovered.

Evidence: Conformance fitness fell from 0.96 to 0.71 (fitting traces 91 % → 44 %) precisely when the risky shortcuts dominated the DFG; residual elevation of exceptions and the persistent audit queue in T4 show that operational recovery masked incomplete control restoration. Hospital protection succeeded; Pharmacy and Wholesale bore the cost, and the organization carried compliance debt beyond the point at which cycle-time metrics looked recovered.

**4. Recovery process model**

Redesigned recovery process for future WMS / cyber disruptions. Described in staged BPMN-style sequential and parallel flows with explicit gates.

**Trigger conditions (explicit)**  
- Primary: WMS, inventory or scanning subsystem unavailable > 30 min, or confirmed cyber impact on order-to-delivery path.  
- Secondary: Concurrent outage of authorization or temperature-logging systems.  
- Activation: Joint decision by Operations + Compliance leads; automatic notification of QA and Regulatory.

**Stage 1 – Immediate Triage (0–2 h)**  
1. Activate Emergency Operations Mode.  
2. Classify every open and incoming case on the two-dimensional matrix:  
   - Customer: Hospital-critical > Pharmacy-standard > Wholesale-bulk  
   - Product: Controlled-substance > Cold-chain > Recall-sensitive > Ambient  
3. Route:  
   - Hospital-critical (any product) → Fast-lane queue  
   - Controlled-substance (any customer) → Mandatory dual-control path  
   - Cold-chain → Mandatory temperature-evidence path  
   - Non-critical Ambient Wholesale → Defer or cancel if capacity < 70 %

**Stage 2 – Minimum controls by risk (never skippable)**  
- Controlled-substance: Dual physical check + contemporaneous paper dual-sign-off + photo evidence; no single-person dispatch.  
- Cold-chain: Checkpoint temperature logging with timestamped record attached before dispatch (100 % for Hospital and Controlled; ≥ 50 % for others).  
- Authorization: Offline master-list or logged phone confirmation for all Hospital and Controlled; verified standing-authorization whitelist (with post-event verification) permitted only for Ambient Pharmacy.  
- Traceability: Unique case ID on paper label + photo of packed contents + courier handoff signature / photo. No “dispatch without any evidence.”  
- Release: Qualified-person sign-off for Controlled and Cold-chain; risk-based sampling for Ambient.

**Stage 3 – Manual evidence capture protocol**  
Pre-positioned crisis packs (paper forms or offline app) containing: authorization log, inventory-snapshot timestamp + checker ID, pick confirmation (picker + verifier), pack photo + seal number, temperature log sheet, dual-control signatures, courier handoff record, structured phone-PoD template.  
All forms photographed or scanned into a temporary repository within 4 h of dispatch and linked by case ID.  
No case may be marked operationally complete without the minimum evidence set required by its risk class.

**Stage 4 – Backlog prioritization**  
Priority score = f(Customer criticality, Product risk, Order age, Remaining shelf-life / cold-chain window).  
Rules published and reviewed daily by a triage board.  
Capacity reservation: ≥ 60 % of restored capacity allocated to Hospital-critical + Controlled until backlog age falls below baseline p90.

**Stage 5 – Reconciliation & audit-remediation flow (parallel, risk-gated)**  
Upon system restoration:  
1. Automated matching of manual evidence to electronic records; flag mismatches.  
2. Risk-tiered remediation:  
   - Controlled / Cold-chain / Hospital: Case remains open for compliance until dual-control, temperature, authorization and PoD evidence are complete or formally waived by QA. Cannot invoice-close high-risk without it.  
   - Ambient / low-criticality: Operational close permitted; compliance close required within 5 business days.  
3. Daily metrics on remediation-queue age, % high-risk still open, and exception rate by type.  
4. Feed root causes into permanent improvements (offline-capable mobile scanning, pre-positioned authorization lists, redundant temperature loggers).

Simplified process-tree notation:  
Trigger → Triage(Customer × Product) → (FastLane | StandardManual | Defer) → MinimumControls(risk-class) + EvidenceCapture → DispatchWithEvidence → (SystemRestore? → Reconcile → RiskGatedRemediation → Close)

This model prevents the observed pattern of “throughput first, compliance later” for high-risk items while still permitting rapid adaptation for low-risk ambient bulk.

**5. Resilience indicators**

**Leading indicators** (real-time during disruption)  
- Critical fill rate by segment (Hospital-critical ≥ 90 %; Pharmacy-standard ≥ 75 %; alert thresholds).  
- Control completeness in manual mode: % of controlled-substance orders with contemporaneous dual-control signature; % of cold-chain orders with temperature log captured at pack/dispatch.  
- Manual evidence capture rate: % of high-risk orders with photo/scan or dual-signed custody form before dispatch.  
- Authorization verification rate: % of orders with documented offline/phone evidence (not assumed history).  
- Snapshot age / inventory confidence (hours since last verified count).  
- Backlog age distribution by priority tier (% Hospital-critical backlog > 12 h; % Pharmacy > 48 h).  
- Triage-rule adherence (% of cases following the published priority matrix).

**Lagging indicators** (evaluate overall resilience and recovery quality)  
- Throughput and cycle time: median and p90 by segment versus baseline (Hospital within +25 %; others may degrade more).  
- Service protection: fill-rate differential Hospital vs Pharmacy/Wholesale; cancellation rate by segment.  
- Traceability exception rate (target < 2 % even in disruption).  
- Residual control completeness: cold-chain temperature missing %, controlled dual-control missing % at end of each phase.  
- Rework / correction rate and audit-remediation queue size + age (cases and days outstanding after operational recovery—key measure of hidden workload).  
- Conformance fitness and % fitting traces versus normative model (target recover > 0.85 within 7 days).  
- Fairness: ratio of cycle-time or fill-rate degradation Pharmacy/Wholesale versus Hospital; complaint rate by segment.  
- Post-incident residual risk: % of T2–T3 cases still open in audit remediation after Day 21.

These indicators distinguish true resilience (protected Hospital supply + maintained minimum controls) from mere throughput preservation or deferred compliance.

**6. Recommendations for future disruptions**

1. **Pre-position crisis packs and offline authorization matrices** (High priority, Medium effort). Benefit: Enables B-style and F-style controls immediately; reduces reliance on C and G. Residual risk: Matrices must be kept current.  
2. **Codify and publish the customer × product priority matrix and capacity-reservation rules** (High priority, Low effort). Benefit: Makes A, I and J transparent and auditable; protects fairness. Residual risk: Low if reviewed periodically.  
3. **Mandate never-skippable minimum evidence set for Controlled and Cold-chain** (High priority, Medium effort). Benefit: Eliminates the largest sources of concealed noncompliance (G, incomplete F/H). Residual risk: Slightly slower high-risk throughput under extreme capacity constraint.  
4. **Implement risk-gated close for high-risk cases** (High priority, Medium–High effort). Benefit: Prevents L-style hidden workload; forces real-time completeness. Residual risk: Temporary inventory/invoice friction.  
5. **Deploy offline-capable mobile capture (photo + barcode) and redundant temperature loggers** (Medium priority, High effort). Benefit: Strengthens E, G and cold-chain controls. Residual risk: Device management and cyber-hardening of the offline tools themselves.  
6. **Resource a standing surge compliance team and pre-train warehouse staff on dual-control paper protocols** (Medium priority, Medium effort). Benefit: Scales B and F without capacity collapse. Residual risk: Training decay.  
7. **Establish a disruption dashboard with the leading/lagging indicators defined above and phase-specific thresholds** (Medium priority, Low–Medium effort). Benefit: Makes hidden workload and control gaps visible in real time. Residual risk: Alert fatigue if thresholds poorly tuned.  
8. **Conduct tabletop exercises that force the choice between throughput and control completeness** (Ongoing, Low effort). Benefit: Calibrates risk appetite and surfaces process gaps before the next real incident.

**7. Management trade-off statement**

Management should explicitly accept temporary degradation of Pharmacy-standard and Wholesale-bulk service levels, elevated cycle times for non-critical orders, and the resource cost of manual dual-control and evidence capture in order to protect Hospital-critical supply and never-skippable product-integrity controls. Management should not accept any adaptation that systematically eliminates contemporaneous authorization evidence, chain-of-custody scanning or temperature documentation for Controlled-substance or Cold-chain products, nor should it treat after-the-fact audit remediation as equivalent to real-time compliance. The residual audit queue and elevated exception rates observed in T4 demonstrate that operational recovery metrics alone are insufficient. Evidence that would change this recommendation includes: (a) regulatory guidance explicitly permitting standing-authorization assumptions under cyber conditions, (b) validated technology that restores electronic controls within minutes rather than days, or (c) empirical data showing that the residual risk of the current workarounds is lower than the patient-harm risk of delayed Hospital-critical deliveries—none of which is present in the observed incident.
