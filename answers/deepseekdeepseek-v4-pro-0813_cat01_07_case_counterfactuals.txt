- Worst: early packet incomplete, assessment slips, provisional C1 slot is wasted, and other urgent compliance cases are delayed; case-level benefit near zero and system-level effect negative.

`Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:`  
If C1 is not actually able to reserve capacity without harming urgent cases; if the early S1 packet lacks enough detail for C1 preread; or if A1’s later work slips so the provisional slot cannot be used.

### K-219
`Factual mechanism:`  
Triage chose D1 for handwriting difficulty, but D1’s queue was long. The document check did not start until Tuesday morning, though originals arrived Monday afternoon. The case therefore could not enter A2’s queue until Tuesday midday.

`Minimal intervention:`  
Assign document checking to D2 at originals receipt rather than waiting for D1.

`Temporal propagation:`  
D2 starts earlier than D1 would have because D2’s queue is shorter. However D2 is slower: the service time likely expands from D1’s 4 h to 6-8 h. Net, the document check still finishes much earlier because the start-time gain is larger than the slower service. That earlier document completion lets the case enter A2’s queue sooner. Base case assessment starts Tuesday afternoon rather than Tuesday evening, enabling a same-day decision. If D2 requests clarification, the rework loop adds an external wait and a second document check/assessment, quickly erasing the gain.

`Process-feasibility check:`  
Originals receipt precedes document check; identity may complete before originals. D2 is allowed to perform the check. Both checks complete before assessment. No high-risk/value compliance step is required. Four-eyes is not relevant. Resource conflict is avoided because D2 is assigned only after its existing queue.

`Best/base/worst case:`  
- Best: D2 starts Mon 21:00, finishes Tue 02:00, A2 starts Tue 10:00, decision Tue 13:00; cycle time improves ~20 h.  
- Base: D2 starts Mon 21:30, finishes Tue 05:30, A2 starts Tue 14:00, decision Tue 17:30; cycle time improves ~15 h.  
- Worst: D2 finds handwriting ambiguous and requests clarification; external response takes 8-36 h; new document check and new assessment push completion later than factual.

`Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:`  
If D2’s queue is longer than forecast; if handwriting is too poor for D2 to process without clarification; or if A2 cannot take the early slot despite earlier document readiness.

### K-337
`Factual mechanism:`  
The first supplier correction fixed most issues, but a second assessment found one remaining inconsistency. A second external request crossed a weekend and forced a third document check and third assessment. The case then needed late compliance and next-week decision.

`Minimal intervention:`  
After Assessment 2, prohibit the second Request More Info loop. The process must either approve with conditions or reject based on the evidence already available. It may not ignore the unresolved discrepancy.

`Temporal propagation:`  
The intervention removes the second external wait, the third document check, the third assessment, and the later compliance/decision sequence that occurred after the weekend. Instead, Assessment 2 becomes the final assessment. The case proceeds Friday afternoon to compliance and then decision. The time savings are large because an entire loop is eliminated, not because individual service times shrink.

`Process-feasibility check:`  
The final assessment remains before compliance review; final compliance remains before decision; decision before notify. Four-eyes is satisfied by A1 and C1. However, the intervention changes decision policy: the case proceeds with an unresolved discrepancy. That is feasible only if the process explicitly allows conditional approval or rejection and documents the risk.

`Best/base/worst case:`  
- Best: C1 and M1 can decide Friday afternoon, approve with conditions, and save about 4.5 days.  
- Base: C1 reviews the unresolved discrepancy Friday afternoon; M1 issues a conditional approval or rejection Friday evening; large time savings but increased risk.  
- Worst: C1 or M1 refuses to decide without the missing confirmation, or a conditional approval produces appeal/loss/compliance consequences; time savings may be partly or fully offset by downstream costs.

`Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:`  
If the remaining inconsistency is material to entitlement, value, or fraud risk; if C1 requires explicit confirmation before sign-off; or if a conditional decision creates significant post-decision monitoring, appeal, or legal workload.

### K-411
`Factual mechanism:`  
A legacy system case lock prevented document task ownership until identity work completed. Identity and document checks were serial. The document check finished late Tuesday, so assessment occurred Wednesday.

`Minimal intervention:`  
At triage, create both verification tasks and use a controlled workaround so I2 and D2 can work concurrently. Require real-time timestamps rather than later batch entry.

`Temporal propagation:`  
The document task is created at triage, not after identity completion. D2 can enter its queue about three hours earlier. Base case document check starts around 12:30 instead of 15:00 and finishes around 16:30. Once both checks are complete, A2 can start earlier, but the benefit is partly absorbed by the assessment queue and M1 availability. In the base path, assessment moves to Tuesday late afternoon, but decision still waits until Wednesday morning because M1 is already occupied with other fixed decisions.

`Process-feasibility check:`  
Originals are not required, so identity and document checks may be concurrent. Both complete before assessment. No high-risk/value compliance step applies. I2 and D2 are different resource pools, so overlapping work is allowed. The workaround is feasible only if timestamps are real and not fabricated.

`Best/base/worst case:`  
- Best: D2 starts 11:30, finishes 15:30; A2 starts 16:00; M1 decides Wednesday mid-morning after other fixed decisions; saving ~5-6 h.  
- Base: D2 starts 12:30, finishes 16:30; A2 starts 17:00; M1 decides Wednesday 09:00; saving ~5 h.  
- Worst: early completion is absorbed by A2 queue; assessment still starts Wednesday 09:30; cycle time barely changes, while D2/A2 queues for other cases worsen. False concurrency from bad logging may also create audit defects.

`Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:`  
If D2 remains busy until after 15:00 despite the early task; if A2 cannot start earlier because of a larger queue; if M1’s morning decision slots are fixed; or if the workaround is not accepted as credible by audit.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 early escalation/C1 slot | Adds S1 prelim ~1 h; adds C1 preread ~1.5 h; moves final C1 earlier; final C1 may be shorter than factual | Case-level final compliance wait | C1 queue for other urgent cases; possible idle reserved C1 slot | Other high-risk/value cases needing C1 | Ambiguous to negative | Scarce C1 capacity: provisional slots can idle if assessments slip while blocking other cases |
| K-219 D2 substitution | Removes ~4 h D1 demand; adds ~6-8 h D2 demand | Case document wait; D1 queue slightly | D2 queue; A2 queue sees earlier arrival | D2-bound cases; possibly later D1 cases get relief | Ambiguous; negative if rework is common | Substitution increases total document service time; rework loops can overwhelm the earlier start |
| K-337 suppress second RMI | Removes third document check, third assessment, and second external wait; moves compliance/decision to Friday | Document and Assessment queues for this case; Tuesday C1 slot released | Friday C1/M1 queue; post-decision risk/appeal workload | Friday C1/M1 cases; legal/compliance follow-up | Ambiguous: positive for upstream queues, negative downstream if risk materializes | Avoided rework may reappear as appeals, conditions, or later legal/compliance work |
| K-411 parallel verification | Earlier D2 demand; same I2 demand; earlier A2 demand | Case-level document/assessment wait | D2/A2 queues for non-target cases | D2-queued cases; A2-queued cases; M1 morning slots | Near zero to negative at scale | Early readiness can be absorbed by assessment bottleneck; false concurrency can hide real resource collisions |

For a 100-case weekly rollout, the system-wide effects are stronger than the case-level effects. K-104 scaled to 100 cases would create roughly 100 early S1 reviews and 100 C1 preread/final slots. Because C1 is the only fully certified compliance reviewer, this is a severe bottleneck: provisional slots would either hold scarce capacity idle when assessments slip or displace other urgent compliance cases. The local benefit depends on lucky slot availability and complete early packets.

K-219 scaled to 100 cases shifts 100 document checks from D1 to D2. D1 demand falls by about 400 h, but D2 demand rises by 600-800 h if D2 is slower. The D2 queue would grow, possibly eliminating the earlier-start advantage. If D2’s handwriting rework rate is even modestly higher, the extra loops would create additional document and assessment demand, not just shift it.

K-337 scaled to 100 cases removes many third-cycle document checks and assessments, relieving upstream queues substantially. But it creates a second-order bottleneck at C1 and M1 because unresolved-risk cases must be reviewed and decided without full information. Conditional approvals and rejections may generate appeals, post-decision monitoring, or legal work. Weekend effects disappear, but the unresolved discrepancies do not.

K-411 scaled to 100 cases does not create much new resource demand, but it makes demand arrive earlier and more simultaneously. The main bottleneck moves to assessment: A2 can only absorb so many early-ready cases. If timestamps are not real-time, false concurrency makes capacity appear better than it is and may conceal resource conflicts. The policy may improve a few individual cases but produce little system-wide gain and possibly audit risk.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention appears to be K-219: assigning selected handwritten cases to D2 when D2 has a clear queue advantage and the handwriting is likely manageable. It is reversible, has no compliance-policy change, and its main uncertainty can be measured directly through rework and clarification rates. The cycle-time benefit is meaningful but not dependent on a single scarce resource like C1.

The most dangerous intervention despite its potential speed is K-337: suppressing the second Request More Info loop saves about 4.5 days but forces a conditional approval or rejection with an unresolved supplier discrepancy. This changes the decision outcome and creates appeal, loss, and compliance-defensibility risk. The time saved may reappear as legal or post-decision workload.

The local case counterfactual least transportable to system-wide policy is K-104. Individually, reserving a provisional C1 slot and doing early compliance preparation looks sensible. At 100-case scale, however, it consumes scarce single-resource compliance capacity and can idle C1 when assessments slip. The intervention depends on capacity slack that the system does not actually have.

The most valuable additional data would be: fine-grained event logs with true start/end timestamps, queue-entry timestamps, resource assignments, rework reasons, external-response times, and C1 reservation outcomes. The highest-value event data are probably C1 queue/availability histories and D1-versus-D2 rework rates.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **Structural** — The partial-order process rules are complete enough to model the relevant mandatory sequencing and four-eyes constraints.  
2. **Temporal** — Queue snapshots are forecasts, not deterministic schedules; actual starts can deviate materially.  
3. **Resource** — C1 is truly the only fully certified compliance reviewer available, with no hidden backup or partial delegate.  
4. **Behavioral** — Staff will keep provisional C1 slots reserved rather than filling them with other urgent cases when the K-104 assessment slips.  
5. **Quality** — D2’s higher risk of clarification on difficult handwriting is real, but selected K-219 cases can be chosen with low enough rework risk.  
6. **Interference** — In single-case counterfactuals, other system behavior is held approximately fixed; cross-case queue displacement is analyzed separately.  
7. **Structural** — In K-337, the unresolved discrepancy after Assessment 2 is immaterial enough to allow conditional approval or rejection without a second confirmation.  
8. **Temporal** — Real-time timestamps in K-411 are accurate and accepted by auditors; there is no later batch entry or false concurrency.
