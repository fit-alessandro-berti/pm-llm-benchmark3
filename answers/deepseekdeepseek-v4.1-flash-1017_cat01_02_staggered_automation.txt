The reproducible code estimates a continuous intensity model with branch and month fixed effects, clearly labeled descriptive.

**Pre-trends and regression to the mean.** Alpha/Beta were selected for poor historical performance, so mean reversion could create apparent improvement even without automation. Their raw median changes are mixed, not clearly improving. Gamma’s pre-adoption median and p90 worsened from May–Jun before automation, likely due product/case-mix changes, so simple pre/post improvement may overstate benefit. Delta/Epsilon also worsened in April–June, which supports using concurrent controls; however, their worsening may have been driven by the same product change, and they experienced different staffing changes. With only three pre months for Alpha/Beta and six for Gamma, formal pre-trend tests have little power.

**Bounded sensitivity.** For median cycle time, the DiD estimates are about -4 to -4.5 h, but possible selection/mean reversion and co-events allow a bounded plausible relative range of roughly -5 h to +1 h (about -9% to +2%). For p90, DiD suggests -6 to -7 h relative to controls, but raw adopter tails rose during rollout and shared queues are unmeasured; a plausible range is roughly -8 h to +5 h relative, with a real possibility of no tail reduction. Rework may have improved by about 1–2 pp relative to controls, but case-mix and loop definitions make -2 to +1 pp plausible. Escalation likely rose by about +0.5 to +3 pp after adoption, though the March fraud desk is a major confounder. CSAT changes are within survey noise and should not be interpreted as causal.

## 6. Threats, tests, and decision consequences
| threat | why it matters here | direction of likely bias | test or additional evidence | consequence if threat is severe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Selection into adoption | Alpha/Beta chosen for high volume/poor cycle; Gamma after readiness pilot | Likely overstates cycle-time benefit through mean reversion | Pre-trend leads; placebo adoption dates; compare with never-treated trends | A single “X% improvement” claim is invalid |
| Nonparallel trends | Product change in May and fraud desk in March affect branches differently | Ambiguous; can over- or understate automation effect | Event-study leads; sensitivity excluding May–Jun; branch-specific trends | DiD may reflect time shocks, not automation |
| Time-varying confounding | Routing, staffing, case mix, and local practices change during rollout | Ambiguous; if adopters got worse shocks, understate benefit; if better, overstate | Adjust for arrivals/FTE, FTE, complexity; collect weekly queue data | Effects remain descriptive only |
| Treatment intensity/endogenous use | Automation availability  use; teams may use it more when queues worsen or easier cases arrive | Likely overstates benefit if high use occurs during favorable periods | Instrument or process data on use; lagged intensity; case-level path analysis | Binary adoption effect is diluted and intensity effect is not causal |
| Spillovers through fraud desk | Automated triage sends borderline cases to shared desk; control branches also affected | Ambiguous; can contaminate controls and worsen adopter tails | Record fraud-desk waits, referrals, and cross-branch queue state | SUTVA fails; never-treated are not clean controls |
| Staffing changes | Alpha loses 2 FTEs in May; Gamma loses 1 in June; Beta and Delta gain staff | Likely understates benefit where adopters lost staff; can distort comparisons | Adjust for FTE and arrivals/FTE; collect skill mix and overtime | Capacity creation vs work reallocation cannot be separated |
| Case mix | May product change raises high-complexity cases in several branches | Likely understates benefit if unadjusted and adopters have more complex cases | Case-level risk adjustment; stratify by complexity | Aggregate outcomes may worsen despite automation benefit |
| Anticipation/readiness | Gamma had a readiness pilot before July | Likely overstates benefit if pre-adoption preparation improves outcomes | Test pre-pilot changes; exclude pilot period; inspect local practices | Causal effect of availability is contaminated by preparation |
| Survey nonresponse | CSAT response 20–45% and counts missing; response may correlate with satisfaction | Ambiguous; can overstate or understate satisfaction effect | Obtain response counts; mode; complaint mix; sensitivity bounds | CSAT claim is not defensible |
| Measurement aggregation | Branch-month medians/p90 and percentages; no case-level durations or response counts | Usually imprecise and may mask heterogeneous effects | Case-level event logs; path-specific cycle time; queue age distribution | Mean-of-medians is not a case-level median effect |
| Small number of branches | Only 5 clusters; staggered timing gives few independent comparisons | Usually understates uncertainty and invites overconfidence | Leave-one-branch-out; placebo/randomization inference; Bayesian hierarchical model | Confidence intervals and p-values are unreliable |
| Dynamic treatment effects/learning | Use ramps and teams learn; early effects may differ from mature effects | Could understate mature benefit if early period is harmed by learning | Event-time lags; separate first 2 months vs later; pre-register learning window | A single average effect can be misleading |
| Treatment-version heterogeneity | Availability, fast-path use, escalation, and manual fallback are different interventions | Binary `automation` dilutes actual use and mixes versions | Analyze path-specific outcomes and process mining; estimate LATE-like effects only with valid design | “Automation” effect may not generalize to future branches |
| Routing campaign | Alpha/Beta got ~10% more cases; Gamma ~5% after July | Likely worsens adopter outcomes, potentially understating benefit | Adjust for arrivals/FTE; model demand as time-varying confounder | Operational benefit may be hidden by demand pressure |

## 8. Rollout recommendation
**Decision: expand with an embedded evaluation.** Delay a universal Delta/Epsilon launch. Randomize which of Delta or Epsilon receives automation first, with the other held as a concurrent delayed control for at least six months. If operations must name a first branch, choose Epsilon first because its p90 and complexity are rising and CSAT is softer; retain Delta as the comparison. Use a phase-in/stepped-wedge assignment, not manager-selected adoption: coin-flip the immediate branch, pre-register the primary outcomes, and do not change local routing or staffing specifically for one arm except as documented operational necessities.

Minimum measurement: use the existing January–August pre-period, require at least three pre months after any new operational shock, and require six post months before a scale decision. Report monthly, not quarterly, and retain the delayed branch until the six-month comparison is complete. Collect process-mining outcomes at case/path level: automated fast path, manual triage, specialist review, optional rework loop, escalation, resolution, and close; cycle time by path; rework-loop counts; escalation destination; and manual fallback. Collect queue-state data the current data lack: specialist and fraud-desk queue length, waits, WIP, utilization, backlog age, arrivals per FTE, and referral rates.

Monitor spillovers through the shared fraud desk and routing. Record fraud-desk referrals, waiting times, cross-branch escalations, and whether the control branch’s workload changes after the other branch adopts. Guardrails: pause if p90 exceeds baseline or the control by more than 10%, if escalation exceeds specialist/fraud capacity, if rework rises by more than 2 pp, if CSAT falls by more than 0.15 points, or if high-complexity cases worsen materially. Scale-up rule: after six months, expand to the delayed branch only if fast-path use is at least 30%, adjusted median cycle time is no worse and p90 is improved or not worse relative to the delayed control, specialist/fraud queues remain within capacity, and there is no guardrail breach. Otherwise pause and redesign.

## 10. Final claim ladder
- `Observed:` Automation availability and fast-path use rose in Alpha/Beta from April and Gamma from July, while escalation increased in all branches after the March fraud-desk change and routing, staffing, and case-mix also changed.
- `Suggestive:` Relative to never-treated Delta/Epsilon, adopters show smaller worsening in median/p90 cycle time and rework, but raw adopter tails often rose and estimates are confounded by selection and shared queues.
- `Conditionally causal:` Under untestable parallel-trends, no-spillover, no-anticipation, and correct case-mix/staffing/routing-adjustment assumptions, automation may modestly reduce median cycle time and possibly p90 relative to controls, but the supplied data cannot establish a precise effect size.
- `Not supported:` A single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim, or a claim that automation uniformly lowers p90, rework, escalation, or improves CSAT, is not supported.
