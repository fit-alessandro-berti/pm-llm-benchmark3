# 2. Integrated explanation

Service time and error rates do not share a single clock. Over a shift they are produced by a *changing case mix*, a *real but limited warm-up*, and then a *queue-driven speed–quality tradeoff* that is amplified by experience, shift inheritance, batching, and a brittle bot.

The opening of the shift is not a clean “unwarmed worker” observation. Bins 1–5 combine leftover night/early backlog, higher complexity (0.72), higher priority (41%), and senior self-selection onto hard carryover. That is why service time starts high (18.8 min) while error is only moderately elevated (3.4%). Juniors look worse in the first 20 events (5.1% error, 19.6 min) in part because they are slower *and* because they still see difficult cases after seniors have skimmed the worst of the pile. Night and Early shifts institutionalize this: Night’s first hour is 56% high-complexity urgent exceptions; Early inherits a queue of 61 and spends the shift drawing it down to 42.

From roughly positions 6–30, three things move together: mix eases (complexity 0.61 → 0.54, high-priority 34% → 28%), queue actually *falls* (39 → 33), and both time and error improve. That joint movement is the only window that looks like warm-up or task re-familiarization. It is not “learning” in the sense of a durable skill gain — the same resources re-warm every shift — and it is not separable from mix without a within-resource residual analysis. Seniors’ drop from 15.9 to 11.8 min with flat quality under low WIP (2.1% → 2.2%) is the cleanest warm-up-plus-mix signature. Night staff barely show it (17.5 → 14.9), which already argues against a universal learning curve.

After position ~50 the story changes mechanism. Queue and WIP rebound (31 → 62 → 88) while leftover work gets *easier* (complexity 0.50 → 0.43, high-priority 25% → 19%). Service time keeps falling (12.9 → 11.4 → 10.8), but error and 7-day rework reverse and then spike (2.7% / 5.0% → 4.6% / 8.2% → 7.9% / 13.7%). That dissociation — faster *and* worse, on easier cases — is the opposite of fatigue-as-slowing and the opposite of learning. The WIP × complexity table identifies the engine: at high WIP, low-complexity work is rushed from 12.7 to 9.4 min and error nearly triples (2.1% → 5.8%); high-complexity work still takes 16.8 min but error reaches 10.9%. Pressure does not just add a main effect; it multiplies complexity.

Experience determines who absorbs that product. Seniors stay relatively safe (later high-WIP error 4.9%). Mid-level quality is flat under low WIP (2.9%) and breaks under high WIP (6.8%). Juniors break hard (12.4%). This is why a shift-position average is misleading: the late-shift mean is a mixture of seniors still coping, mid-level degrading, and juniors failing, on a caseload that looks easy in the aggregate.

Circadian and shift structure modulate the same pressure, they do not replace it. Day shift starts light (queue 38) and ends overloaded (75), with the fastest last-two-hour service (10.9 min) and the worst last-two-hour error (7.5%) — a demand-peak, not a biological dusk effect by itself. Late inherits the day pile (start queue 70) and never clears it (end 66). Night is a different operating mode: fewer events, more exceptions, slower service (15.2 min late), but *lower* late error (5.1%) than Day/Late. If circadian impairment were the dominant late-shift story, Night should be the worst; it is not.

Workarounds convert pressure into measured throughput. Junior_H and Junior_I batch Document Review late in Day: long consecutive runs, very short service times, same queue batch. Quality Check is parked until the last 45 minutes, so defects leave the station as “done” and reappear as 7-day rework. Bot_Risk returns instant classifications that humans rubber-stamp more often when queue > 70, converting an automation shortcut into a human-looking error on Eligibility, Referral, or later correction. These practices make the late-shift service-time decline look like competence or learning when it is partly accounting: work is finished in the log before it is finished in the world.

Fatigue is not absent, but it is not the residual after everything else is named, and it is not the only late-shift mechanism. Hours-on-task may reduce monitoring and make workers more willing to accept the bot, skip a check, or join a batch. That is compatible with error rising while service time falls. It is not compatible with treating the entire 18.8 → 10.8 minute decline as tired people, nor with treating the first-hour slowness as incompetence. Early slowness is mostly mix and carryover. Mid-shift improvement is mix plus warm-up under easing queues. Late speed with late defects is queue pressure, selection of leftovers, batching, deferred QC, and bot over-trust — with fatigue as a plausible amplifier, especially for juniors, and circadian effects as a Night/Early case-mix and exception-load story rather than a global quality collapse.

# 3. Proposed analyses

Build one analysis table from the event log so every later model uses the same clocks, queues, and outcomes.

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    raw,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
)
df = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
    df, timestamp_column="time:timestamp",
    start_timestamp="start_timestamp",
)
df = pm4py.insert_case_arrival_finish_rate(df)
df = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(df)

# event-level service seconds
df["service_sec"] = (
    pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"]) - pd.to_datetime(df["start_timestamp"])
).dt.total_seconds()
df["human"] = ~df["org:resource"].eq("Bot_Risk")
df["pos_bin"] = pd.cut(
    df["sequence_position_within_shift"],
    bins=[0, 5, 15, 30, 50, 75, 10_000],
    labels=["1-5", "6-15", "16-30", "31-50", "51-75", "76+"],
)
df["wip_band"] = pd.cut(
    df["wip_at_start"], bins=[-1, 30, 70, 10_000],
    labels=["low", "med", "high"],
)
df["clock_hour"] = pd.to_datetime(df["start_timestamp"]).dt.hour
```

**Within-resource comparisons.** Restrict to human resources. For each `org:resource`, compute position-bin and WIP-band means of service time, `error_flag`, and `rework_flag`. Then fit resource fixed effects:

`service_sec ~ f(sequence_position) + f(minutes_since_shift_start) + wip_at_start + queue_length_at_start + case_complexity_score + priority + C(concept:name) + C(shift_id) + C(org:resource)`

and a parallel GLM/logit for `error_flag`. The resource FE removes stable skill differences. The object of interest is the *within-person* position and WIP slopes. Split the sample at the first 20 events vs the rest to match the experience table.

**Within-shift position effects.** Use restricted cubics or dummies on `sequence_position_within_shift` and, separately, `minutes_since_shift_start`. If both are in the model they will collinear; prefer position as the operational “work period” clock and use minutes in a robustness spec. Plot partial residuals after mix and WIP adjustment. Expect: residual service-time decline through ~30 events that then flattens; residual error U-shape that only reappears after position 50 *if* WIP is *not* controlled, and a much smaller U-shape if WIP *is* controlled. That decomposition is the test of warm-up vs pressure.

**WIP / queue stratification.** Cross `wip_band` × complexity band (e.g. complexity ≥ 0.6) × experience group. Reproduce the six-cell table inside each resource group and each activity family. Estimate a continuous interaction `wip_at_start * case_complexity_score`. Scan thresholds at 30 and 70 to validate the proposed alert. Use arrival rate, not own service time, as the pressure instrument so speed does not define the regressor.

**Complexity adjustment.** Never interpret raw position means. Report (a) unadjusted, (b) regression-adjusted, (c) matching/stratified on complexity decile × priority × `activity_family`. Compare senior vs junior first-hour `case_complexity_score` to quantify selection. Re-run position curves on a *complexity-balanced* subsample (e.g. only mid-decile cases).

**Shift and time-of-day controls.** Add `shift_id`, clock hour, day-of-week, and weekend/Monday flags. In the 09:00–14:00 overlap, compare Early resources (hours 3–8 on task) with Day resources (hours 0–5) at matched WIP and complexity: remaining difference is hours-on-task; difference that tracks clock hour across Early/Day/Late is circadian/time-of-day. Contrast Day late-afternoon (queue rising) with Late early-afternoon (queue inherited) at the same clock hour.

# 4. Intervention options

**1. Strictly cap shift length for all workers.**  
*Benefit:* Trims the 76+ tail where error is 7.9% and rework 13.7%; may reduce hours-on-task fatigue and end-of-shift QC bursting.  
*Risk:* If demand is unchanged, a hard cap dumps WIP onto the next shift (Early already starts at queue 61; Late inherits 70). That recreates the high-complexity first-hour problem and can *raise* system rework. Night and Early are not the worst late-error shifts; a uniform cap mis-targets Day/Late demand peaks. Seniors still performing safely would lose capacity.  
*Monitor:* `minutes_since_shift_start` distribution; end-of-shift `queue_length`; next-shift first-hour complexity and error; handoff volume; service time and error in the new last two hours, not just the removed tail.

**2. Move complex cases away from the first hour of each shift.**  
*Benefit:* Reduces early mix that inflates first-hour time and junior early error; might let warm-up happen on moderate cases.  
*Risk:* Complex carryover from Night/weekend is often *urgent* (Night 56% high-complexity first hour, Early 48%). Parking it increases waiting time and SLA risk for the cases that most need seniors. Seniors currently taking those cases early is a *protective* selection pattern, not a bug.  
*Monitor:* First-hour `case_complexity_score` and `priority`; waiting time for high-complexity cases (`insert_case_service_waiting_time`); specialist-referral and eligibility cycle time; first-20-event junior error vs senior error; abandon/SLA-breach rate on carryover cases.

**3. Add a queue-pressure alert when WIP > 70.**  
*Benefit:* Directly targets the cell with the strongest causal story: high WIP shortens time and multiplies error, especially with high complexity (10.9%) and juniors (12.4%). Enables staffing, freeze-on-new-intake, or senior overflow before bin 76+.  
*Risk:* An alert without spare capacity becomes noise, or triggers even more rushing. A single WIP number ignores complexity and activity mix. Gaming: workers or bots may close events early to drop displayed WIP.  
*Monitor:* Share of events with `wip_at_start` > 70; error and rework *conditional on* high WIP (should fall if response is real); Bot_Risk correction rate above vs below 70; arrival vs finish rates; false-alert rate by shift.

**4. Force random assignment of cases to reduce selection bias.**  
*Benefit:* Improves fairness of resource comparisons and identifies true skill vs skim effects; stops juniors from inheriting a distorted leftover mix.  
*Risk:* Destroys useful matching: seniors on post-weekend complex carryover is probably value-creating. Random assignment of high-complexity work to juniors or Night exception load to inexperienced staff will raise error. May increase handovers and waiting.  
*Monitor:* Variance of complexity/priority across resources in the first hour; senior vs junior first-hour mix gap; overall and junior error; waiting time; handover count; do not treat equalization of mix as success if system error rises.

**5. Introduce mandatory micro-breaks in the final two hours.**  
*Benefit:* If fatigue or depletion amplifies late rushing, short breaks may cut late error without a full shift cap. May interrupt Junior_H/I sequential batches.  
*Risk:* On Day/Late, the last two hours are a demand peak (Day end queue 75). Removing labor then increases WIP, which is the better-identified error mechanism. Breaks can shift QC bursting later or onto the next shift.  
*Monitor:* Last-two-hour error, rework, and service time; WIP path during break windows; batch-run length; QC waiting time; next-shift start queue. Prefer a stepped-wedge trial by team.

**6. Defer low-priority quality checks to the next shift.**  
*Benefit:* Removes last-45-minute QC bursts that collide with peak Day WIP and may produce careless checks.  
*Risk:* This is not “remove QC to buy throughput.” Deferral increases the window in which defects travel, can inflate 7-day rework, and dumps QC onto Early — already clearing a night backlog. Low-priority labels are themselves error-prone under high WIP.  
*Monitor:* QC waiting time and same-shift vs next-shift QC share; defect escape rate (error discovered after Close Case); rework within 7 days for deferred vs same-shift QC; Early-shift QC load and Early error; never treat shorter same-shift cycle time as success if escaped defects rise.

**7. Add an extra senior reviewer during day-shift demand peaks.**  
*Benefit:* Best mechanism match. Day is the only shift that *creates* a large end-of-shift pile (38 → 75), has the worst late error (7.5%) and fastest late service (10.9). Seniors have the flattest quality under high WIP (4.9%). A senior on Risk Classification overrides, high-complexity work, and real-time QC during the peak attacks pressure × complexity × junior exposure.  
*Risk:* Cost; pulling seniors off early-shift carryover if scheduled poorly; bottleneck at the senior if all exceptions route there; Night/Late left unchanged.  
*Monitor:* Day last-two-hour WIP, error, rework; junior late high-WIP error; Bot_Risk override quality; senior utilization and their own error; waiting time for high-complexity and high-priority cases; whether peak WIP still exceeds 70.

**8. Penalize workers with higher late-shift error rates.**  
*Benefit:* None that survives the identification strategy. Raw late-shift error confounds WIP assigned by the system, leftover mix, bot quality, deferred QC, and batching incentives.  
*Risk:* Punishes juniors and whoever is scheduled on Day/Late peaks. Rewards seniors who skim easy late leftovers or who refuse high-WIP work. Encourages hiding errors, skipping flags, premature Close Case, and even more batching. Directly forbidden as a management response to unadjusted rates.  
*Monitor:* If any accountability remains, use *adjusted* error (WIP, complexity, priority, activity, bot-assist, shift) and system-level escaped-defect rates — never individual raw late-shift percentages. Watch for suppression of `error_flag` without a matching drop in 7-day rework.

Priority order if only a few moves are possible: (7) senior peak coverage, (3) WIP>70 alert *with* a capacity response, then careful experiments on (5) and limited (6) deferral of *true* low-risk QC. Do not lead with (1), (2), (4), or (8).

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

Worker-level league tables from this log are not skill tables. Sequence position, shift assignment, and `org:resource` determine which *environment* a person is scored in.

**Selection bias.** Seniors systematically take complex post-weekend and post-night cases. Their early service times are longer *because* the work is harder; their later error rates look good *because* leftovers are easier and they are more resistant to WIP. Juniors can appear both slow and inaccurate in the first 20 events and reckless after position 75 without any change in competence. Night staff handle a higher exception share. Comparing raw error or raw minutes across Senior_A, Junior_H, and Night_L is comparing jobs, not people.

**Measurement error.** `error_flag` and `rework_flag` are not interchangeable with worker fault. Rework within 7 days includes deferred Quality Check, Bot_Risk mislabels that a rushed human accepted, incomplete Document Review batches, and cases that were already high-risk at arrival. Service time from `start_timestamp` to complete can record batch scanning, not cognitive work. Missing or delayed QC makes late work look clean until rework arrives. Instant Bot_Risk times will dominate activity-level averages if not separated.

**Queue pressure created by management.** WIP > 70 is not a personality trait. Day-shift staffing, intake pacing, and refusal to add a senior reviewer *create* the 38 → 75 queue path that then produces 7.5% late error. Penalizing the people who happen to be on the station when that wave arrives attributes a rostering failure to individuals. Arrival-rate features belong in the model precisely to keep that distinction.

**Automation quality.** Bot_Risk compresses Risk Classification to zero seconds and shifts failure into human correction, more so when queue > 70. If the bot is worse on high-complexity or night-exception cases, humans who “own” the next event inherit a tainted starting point. Scoring those humans for downstream error without a bot-quality split launders model failure into employee failure.

**Punitive use of error rates.** Raw late-shift error is highest where the system is most loaded, the mix looks deceptively easy, juniors are over-exposed, QC is batched, and the bot is over-trusted. Punishment on that metric will teach people to stop recording errors, close cases before QC, avoid hard work, and game WIP. It will not teach them to be less tired. Any individual feedback must be mix- and WIP-adjusted, compared within shift type, and paired with system indicators (escaped defects, waiting time, handover quality). Do not rank, bonus, or discipline on unadjusted late-shift error.

Operationally, also avoid “improving” the dashboard by deleting Quality Check or Close Case controls. Throughput gains that show up as shorter service time in bins 51–76+ are already entangled with a doubling-plus of 7-day rework. The ethical reading of that pattern is that the process is already under-measuring unfinished work.

# 6. Monitoring dashboard specification

Audience: operations + quality, refreshed at least hourly within the shift and daily for risk. Unit of analysis: event, with case-level and resource-level rollups. Filters: `shift_id`, clock hour, `org:resource` / experience group, `activity_family`, `priority`, complexity band. Never show an unadjusted worker ranking as a default tile.

| 6 | Complexity mix | Mean `case_complexity_score`, high-complexity share, high-priority share, by position bin and by resource in the first hour | Makes selection and carryover visible so early slowness is not misread as incompetence | Watch first-hour mix after weekends/nights; watch late-shift mix becoming easier while errors rise |
| 7 | Batching indicators | `discover_batches` volume; max consecutive same-resource same-activity run length; share of events below resource–activity 20th percentile service time; last-45-minute QC burst size | Explains artificial late throughput (Junior_H/I, end-of-shift QC) | Alert on Document Review runs ≥9 in Day last two hours; alert if >X% of QC starts in last 45 minutes |
| 8 | Handover / queue ownership | Resource changes inside a case; shift-boundary handovers; ownership of leftover queue (who works carryover vs who created it) | Shift inheritance (Night→Early, Day→Late) is a core mix mechanism | Alert when end-of-shift queue and next-shift first-hour high-complexity share both rise |
| 9 | Worker-load fairness | Per resource, per shift: event count, minutes worked, mean complexity, mean WIP faced, share of high-priority, overtime past minute 420 | Prevents interpreting junior late error as individual failure when load is unequal | Show load *and* adjusted error together; investigate if juniors’ mean WIP or complexity exceeds seniors’ in the same hour |
| 10 | Automation interaction | Bot_Risk volume; human correction rate split by `queue_length_at_start` > 70; downstream rework after bot-accept vs bot-correct | Stops bot failures being scored as human late-shift error | Alert if correction rate diverges above vs below WIP 70, or if bot-accept rework rises |
| 11 | Experience × pressure cell | Error and service time for Senior / Mid / Junior / Night × low vs high WIP, later-in-shift only | The table already shows juniors at 12.4% vs seniors at 4.9% under high WIP — this is the operational risk cell | Staffing alert when junior share of high-WIP events exceeds a set cap without a senior reviewer on duty |
| 12 | Flow / performance DFG | `discover_performance_dfg` plus dotted chart / events distribution by hour | Shows where waiting pools (QC, Callback, Referral) and when the Day peak actually arrives | Use for weekly review, not vanity throughput |
