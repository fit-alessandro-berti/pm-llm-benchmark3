```python
# =============================================================================
#  M1 - revised normative POWL family for supplier onboarding
#  Executable, self-contained: constructs POWL objects only (no file I/O).
#  Requires: pm4py (POWL objects).  pandas is used only for the data-rule layer.
# =============================================================================
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import OperatorPOWL, SilentTransition, StrictPartialOrder, Transition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# ---------- tiny builders ----------------------------------------------------
def T(label):
    """A visible activity.  Labels are the conformance vocabulary; behaviour
    modes (provisional activation, route id, risk class) are EVENT ATTRIBUTES,
    never new labels."""
    return Transition(label=label)

def EPS():
    """Tau / silent step."""
    return SilentTransition()

def XOR(*kids):
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=list(kids))

def LOOP(do, redo):
    """Process-tree loop  do . (redo . do)*  (POWL uses the same semantics)."""
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[do, redo])

def STAR(label):
    """'label*' = zero or more repetitions.
 Approximation: LOOP(tau, label) = tau . (label . tau)* = label*.
       POWL cannot express "interleave one or more X with an arbitrary subset of
       the checks and re-enter the checks"; we therefore keep X optional and
       repeatable and forbid re-execution of the checks (see DECLARE D-5)."""
    return LOOP(EPS(), T(label))

def PO(nodes, edges=()):
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    for a, b in edges:
        po.order.add_edge(a, b)
    return po
```

| # | M0 entailment | Consequence for the case sample |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | `checks` is a `StrictPartialOrder` with **no** edge between the two checks  they are **concurrent and both mandatory**, and the whole block completes before the decision node. | Order *within* the checks is unconstrained (SUP-701 and the QR-before-SC part of SUP-708 are **not** ordering deviations). But both checks must complete **before any decision child** — including `Activate_Supplier` and including `Reject_Supplier`. |
| E2 | `approved_branch` is a `StrictPartialOrder` with `manual_approval  activate`  approval strictly precedes activation. | SUP-708's `Activate_Supplier  Manual_Procurement_Approval` is a hard deviation. |
| E3 | `OperatorPOWL(XOR, [approved_branch, reject])` is a **free** exclusive choice  exactly one branch, no data guard. | M0 cannot restrict Auto/Emergency behaviour because it has no attributes at all. |
| E4 | `Notify_Requester` is mandatory on **every** path, before `Close_Case`. | Missing notification (SUP-706) is a deviation **against M0**, but it is not a CS-9 duty (CS-9 never mentions notification). |

Two non-entailments are equally important: M0 says **nothing** about identity/SoD, amounts, risk class, dates, or authority (so SUP-712 and SUP-715 are M0-conformant and still CS-9/CO-breaching); and M0 has **no** activity alphabet beyond its seven labels (so `Request_More_Info`, `Auto_Approve`, `Master_Data_Validation`, reactivation and withdrawal are structurally inexpressible).

Finally, a fact about the model's own authority that the record does **not** supply: M0's approval status, owner, version and effective date are not given. N1 says CS-9 is "controlled and formally approved"; nothing says the same of M0. Where M0 and CS-9 conflict (e.g. M0 forces `Quality_Review` before `Reject_Supplier`, while CS-9 conditions the checks on *activation*), CS-9 — the formally approved standard — must prevail unless M0's provenance is produced.

## 1.5 Burden of proof and authority test

**Deviation taxonomy (applied to every case).** A departure from M0 is exactly one of: (i) genuine policy violation; (ii) justified exception under a valid overlay; (iii) behaviour outside the model's scope; (iv) model or logging artifact. Only (i) is sanctionable conduct.

**Burden to declare NONCOMPLIANT.** The reviewer must establish on the balance of probabilities: (a) an applicable requirement in force at case-execution time and within its material and personal scope; (b) an observable departure from it; (c) that the departure is not merely (iii) or (iv). The burden then **shifts to the operator** to produce the exception evidence, because authority instruments, eligibility snapshots and invocation records are exclusively in the operator's hands.

**Burden to accept an exception (operator's burden).** All five must be shown: **A** — a written instrument with an identifiable issuer and effective dates; **B** — the issuer's **competence** to derogate the base standard; **C** — per-case **invocation** (authority actor + timestamp); **D** — case-level **eligibility predicates** satisfied (frozen at decision time); **E** — the overlay's **compensating controls** completed within their deadlines. Failure of A/B/C/D is fatal to the exception; failure of E is fatal unless the unperformed control is a system-internal step evidenced by an immutable timestamp (D9).

**Effect of model/data defects.** A defect may reclassify a case as MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT or DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE, but **never cures** a proven breach of a rule that exists independently of M0 (CS-9, the COI policy, FD-7's cap). Conversely, where a required act is unobservable because of a known collection defect (N4), the finding is evidential (DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE), not substantive — unless the norm itself requires evidencing, in which case the auditability failure is the breach.

**Standard for severity.** "Severe/High" is reserved for cases with high inherent risk or a breached preventive control. Intent is only asserted where the record establishes it (SUP-703: "operator selected 'pilot' despite ineligibility" is stipulated).

# 2. Prosecution Brief

## 2.1 Formal authority: PD-3 does not repeal CS-9

CS-9 is "controlled and formally approved" and effective 2025-01-01. PD-3 is a steering-committee decision recorded in minutes and a follow-up email, explicitly **not incorporated into CS-9 or M0**. Under D1 the steering committee's competence to suspend a compliance control — the procurement approval of supplier activation — is unestablished. The only compliance-side evidence is the observer's note "pilot may proceed as a monitored exception," and she later stated she had not approved weakening CS-9. Even read favourably, "monitored exception" authorises a *watched* departure, not a permanent substitution of Auto_Approve for Manual_Procurement_Approval. An unratified operational convenience cannot silently create a derogation regime for a formally approved standard. At best PD-3 is a tolerated, monitored, time-boxed departure that required compliance ratification to become a *justified* exception — and that ratification is still listed as pending.

## 2.2 Expiry: two different instruments, the same failure

FD-7 expired **2026-07-12 23:59 by its own terms**. SUP-705 executed the emergency pattern on **2026-07-13**. That is not a grey area of interpretation: the instrument had lapsed, no written extension exists, and the operation proceeded anyway. The defence that "the flood emergency still factually existed" is precisely the reasoning that dissolves emergency governance: every emergency is factually arguable after the fact, which is why authority is time-stamped and self-expiring. A one-day overrun matters because it shows the expiry mechanism was not operative in the field.

PD-3 expired **2026-07-31**. SUP-714 used Auto_Approve on **2026-08-02**, and the aggregate shows **six** such cases in the first week of August. Operations' stated belief that "permanent approval was imminent" is not authority — it is a forecast. Anticipated legislation has never authorised conduct. And this is not a single lapse: six cases in five days is a pattern.

## 2.3 Normalisation of exceptions is the actual systemic finding

The population evidence shows the exception regime escaping its container:

- **312** Auto_Approve cases in two months. Even taking "289 appear eligible" at face value, **14 are clearly ineligible** and **9 cannot be assessed at all**. Twenty-three cases — 7.4% of all pilot usage — are nonconforming or unprovable, and the 289 are *unverified* ("appear"), not verified.
- **6** further unauthorised cases after PD-3's sunset.
- SUP-705 shows the same behaviour for a *second* instrument.

The shape of this is textbook exception drift: an authorised, monitored, time-boxed pilot becomes the default mental model, and the organisation must then argue case by case that it meant no harm. Aggregate counts cannot be dismissed as "model obsolescence": eligibility predicates (risk class, country, group membership) and effective dates are **data facts**, not model constructs, and 14 cases failed them outright.

## 2.4 Deviations that M0 hides — conformance is not compliance

The prosecution's strongest structural point is that **M0-conformant traces include real breaches**:

- **SUP-712** is a letter-perfect M0 trace and a plain violation of CS-9's third bullet: the requester and the manual approver are the **same human** operating through two aliases. Nothing in M0 could ever have caught this. If one alias collision surfaced in a 15-case review, the population exposure is unknown and unmonitored — segregation of duties is the whole point of the manual-approval control, and it was defeated on a case that "looks clean" in every tool the organisation runs.
- **SUP-715** is also M0-conformant: the quality reviewer and the manual approver are spouses in the same reporting line and **the required disclosure was never recorded**. The undisclosed related-party relationship sits on top of the one control (independent quality review) that is supposed to corroborate activation.

These two cases invert the defence's narrative. If M0's high deviation rate were the problem, the operation would look clean on SUP-712/715. It does not. The deviation rate is not the compliance signal; the rules M0 cannot see are where the risk is.

## 2.5 High-risk cases: SUP-703 and SUP-708

**SUP-708** is the most serious single case: a HIGH-risk supplier was **activated 40 minutes before its sanctions check completed and before any procurement approval**. This violates CS-9's first two bullets *and* M0's E1/E2 simultaneously. No pilot, no emergency, no scope ambiguity, no logging artifact. The fact that no transactions occurred before clearance is mitigation of *impact*, not of *control failure*: the preventive control did not function at the moment it was designed to function. Internal Audit is right to flag it.

**SUP-703** is a misuse of the exception machinery: a HIGH-risk **new foreign** supplier fails *every* PD-3 predicate (LOW-risk, DE, approved corporate group), and the operator selected "pilot" anyway — using it also to defer Quality_Review past activation, which CS-9 requires beforehand and which PD-3 permits only for pilot-eligible cases. First purchase: **EUR 180,000**. This is not a modelling dispute; it is an ineligible party bypassing the manual approval control entirely, and it proves the "monitored exception" was not monitored.

## 2.6 Desirable outcomes do not retroactively amend policy

Compliance's present view that Request_More_Info is desirable is a **policy argument for the future**, not a defence for the past. The same applies to Operations' expectation of a permanent auto-approval amendment. Policy is changed by instruments with effective dates; otherwise every practice becomes lawful by being common, and the 312-case aggregate would become its own justification. Forty-seven uneventful RMI cycles show the practice is *safe* — which is exactly why it should be ratified **now** and modelled going forward (§6–7), while remaining, at the time, unmodelled behaviour executed outside the controlled process.

## 2.7 Prosecution's acknowledgements (genuine model and data defects)

Candour requires conceding four points, none of which rescues the four core violations:

1. **M0 is genuinely incomplete.** It has no Request_More_Info loop (SUP-707), no Auto/Emergency routes, no reactivation or withdrawal variant, and no activity-label versioning (SUP-713). Traces SUP-707, SUP-713 are only "deviations" because of these omissions.
2. **M0 is genuinely over-specified in one place**: by putting `Quality_Review` in a mandatory `checks` block before the decision, it requires a quality review even for a lead rejected at sanctions pre-screen (SUP-709), which CS-9 — which conditions the checks on *activation* — does not.
3. **N4 is a real data-collection defect.** 29% notification missingness  the 30% implied by 70% mirroring. SUP-706's missing `Notify_Requester` is, on the balance of probabilities, a logging failure rather than a control failure.
4. **SUP-711 is a migration artifact** (identical `source_transaction_id`), and SUP-710 sits in a genuine policy vacuum (CS-9 is silent on reactivation).

But note the boundary of these concessions: **no** model or data defect explains SUP-708 (CS-9's two ordering rules), SUP-703 (ineligible use of an exception), SUP-705/SUP-714 (expired authority), or SUP-712/SUP-715 (CS-9's SoD rule and the COI disclosure duty). Those rules exist in instruments that do not depend on M0 at all.

# 3. Defense Brief

## 3.1 Read M0 for what it is: an over-specified, under-scoped skeleton

M0 forces `Quality_Review` before a **rejection** (E1), demands `Notify_Requester` in the **onboarding log** on every path (E4), and assumes every case is a first-time full onboarding. Each of these is contradicted by the governing standard or by the facts:

- CS-9's first bullet is a **conditional** requirement — the checks are required **before activation**. A lead that is rejected at pre-screen never activates, so the condition never triggers; CS-9 expressly contemplates "an abbreviated pre-screen process" for rejected leads. **SUP-709 is therefore not a violation of anything**; M0's requirement of a quality review for a dead lead is an artefact of putting the checks inside a mandatory block. The defect is in the model and in an ambiguous scope document (D2).
- CS-9 says nothing about notification. The notification "requirement" exists only in M0, and after N4 the notification is created in a **CRM system of record** with a stable `notification_id`. Demanding the event in the onboarding log after the organisation deliberately moved it is enforcing a data layout, not a control. **SUP-706 is a collection defect (N4)**, corroborated by the near-exact match between the 29% observed missingness and the 30% implied by 70% mirroring.
- **SUP-710 (reactivation)**: CS-9 has no reactivation procedure (D3). Requiring `Register_Supplier` and a fresh `Quality_Review` for a supplier whose prior certification is stated to remain valid imports a duty that no instrument contains. Sanctions **was** re-checked and manual approval **was** obtained — the two controls whose currency genuinely matters on reactivation.

## 3.2 Concurrency semantics: where the model already permits what looks deviant

M0's `checks` block is a `StrictPartialOrder` with **no** edge between Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review. So:

- In **SUP-708**, `Quality_Review` before `Sanctions_Check` is **not** an ordering deviation — the concurrent block permits it. The only deviation is that the block did not complete before activation and before approval. Framing SUP-708 as "the checks were out of order" misstates the model.
- In **SUP-707**, `Request_More_Info` appears *between* the two concurrent checks. Projecting out an activity the model has no opinion about leaves a trace that conforms to M0 exactly. Under an open-world reading (D7) it conforms even without projection.

## 3.3 The missing loop is a model defect, and the practice is demonstrably safe

N5 is explicit: Compliance agrees that requesting more information is desirable. The aggregate closes the risk question — **47 cases, none activated before the requested evidence arrived**. The activity is purely additive verification; it cannot cause a control failure it is designed to prevent. M0's inability to express `rmi*` means the conformance engine reports a deviation for a behaviour that every stakeholder wants. That is a defect of the model, not of the operation. The correct remedy is the loop in §7 — not a finding of noncompliance.

## 3.4 Pilot and emergency overlays: the deviations are mostly authorised

**SUP-702** (2026-06-15) sits inside PD-3's window and satisfies every scope predicate: LOW-risk, DE, approved corporate group, eligibility fields complete. Auto_Approve replaced Manual_Procurement_Approval as PD-3 expressly allows; Sanctions_Check preceded activation; Quality_Review occurred the next business day, inside PD-3's two-business-day allowance; CRM proves notification at +2 hours with a **stable `notification_id`**. This case is the poster child for what a monitored exception is supposed to look like — and the prosecution would condemn it because the instrument was recorded in minutes rather than re-issued as a standard amendment. Where an instrument is in force, within scope and complied with, the trace deviation is a **model** deviation: M0 simply has no Auto route.

**SUP-704** (2026-07-11) sits inside FD-7's window on its face: critical pump supplier, sanctions before provisional activation, initial spend EUR 18,000 (under the cap), daily monitoring present, Quality_Review and retrospective approval the next business day. FD-7 expressly permits activation to precede both. The observed trace is FD-7's own choreography.

**SUP-705** (2026-07-13): the one-day overrun is a governance failure of the *escalation mechanism*, not an attempt to circumvent a control — the operator applied every substantive FD-7 safeguard (spend EUR 12,000, next-day review and approval). The absence of any extension instrument is a real problem, but the remedy is a standing continuation/extension procedure, not the conclusion that the operation is uncontrolled.

## 3.5 Label equivalence and event identity are logging problems

**SUP-713**: `QA_Clearance` is `Quality_Review` — same procedure, same UI step, audit documents show equivalent content. Conformance tooling that penalises an IT rename is measuring vocabulary drift. What is missing is a **versioned activity-label registry approved before use** (the post-hoc approval is a process defect in change management, not a compliance failure of the case).

**SUP-711**: two `Register_Supplier` rows sharing **one `source_transaction_id`** is the fingerprint of a migration duplicate, not a double registration. After canonical deduplication the trace is M0-conformant in full. What is missing is a normative event-identity/deduplication rule.

## 3.6 Trace deviation  business misconduct

Decomposing the 15-case sample against M0 **literally**, only 3 traces fit (SUP-701, 712, 715) — a 20% "conformance rate" that looks catastrophic. But stepwise:

| Step | Additional cases explained | Running total |
|---|---|---|
| M0 literal | — | 3/15 |
| Project out non-modeled activity (RMI) and apply versioned label mapping + dedup | SUP-707, SUP-711, SUP-713 | 6/15 |
| Apply scope rules (rejected leads, reactivations) | SUP-709, SUP-710 | 8/15 |
| Apply in-force overlays (PD-3, FD-7) | SUP-702, SUP-704 | 10/15 |
| Genuinely unauthorised control-flow deviations | SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-714 | 4 |
| Indeterminate on evidence | SUP-706 | 1 |

Operations' claim is therefore substantially right: **most of M0's "deviation rate" is obsolescence, scope incompleteness and logging**. Two-thirds of the sample is explained without any misconduct finding.

## 3.7 Defense's acknowledgements (genuine risk and the limits of informal practice)

1. **SUP-708 is indefensible.** Activation before sanctions clearance and before approval on a HIGH-risk supplier is a failure of CS-9's two core preventive rules. Concurrency semantics, model obsolescence and logging defects explain nothing here. Severe.
2. **SUP-703 is indefensible.** "The operator selected pilot" is not a justification; it is the description of the violation. An ineligible HIGH-risk new foreign supplier obtained an unapproved activation route and a deferred quality review. The EUR 180,000 first purchase shows the exposure the manual-approval control exists to mediate. Severe.
3. **SUP-705 and SUP-714 fail on authority.** Factual necessity (SUP-705) and an anticipated amendment (SUP-714) are arguments to the *rule-maker*, not permissions from the rule. The 6 post-sunset August cases show the informal practice outliving its legal basis — exactly the drift that cannot be self-corrected. Operations' culpability is limited (the pilot should have been technically disabled at sunset), but the cases are unauthorised.
4. **SUP-712 is a real CS-9 breach.** Alias-based SoD defeat is invisible to control-flow conformance, and "M0 fits" proves only that M0 is the wrong instrument for this rule.
5. **The disclosure duty in SUP-715 was not performed.** The relationship itself is not prohibited (so no independence rule is broken on this record), but the COI policy required disclosure and none exists. A narrow breach, but a breach.
6. **Structural limit of informal practice:** pilots and emergency customs can authorise *behaviour* only through instruments with issuers, windows and sunsets. Everything the operation wants — the RMI loop, the low-risk auto route, a reactivation variant — is defensible *as proposed policy* (§6–7). None of it is defensible as custom.

Notes on the two uses that carry analysis rather than dodge it: **SUP-702 is MIXED** because it contains two independent findings of different types (a justified deviation plus an evidence defect) and neither is a way of avoiding a verdict — the deviation is *accepted*, and the evidence gap is separately remediable. **No other case uses MIXED**: SUP-705's mitigation is a severity input, not a second verdict; SUP-715's unproved allegation is an evidential gap, not a competing finding.

**Cross-cutting aggregate judgments.** Of the 312 Jun–Jul Auto_Approve cases: 289 are *presumptively* justified (unverified — "appear eligible" is not a frozen eligibility snapshot), **14 are NONCOMPLIANT** (ineligible), **9 are DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE**. The 6 first-week-of-August cases (incl. SUP-714) are **NONCOMPLIANT** — expired authority. The 47 RMI cases support a **model revision**, not a misconduct finding (no premature activation in any of them). The notification- missingness jump (2%29%) is a **data-collection defect** attributable to N4.

## 5.2 System-level judgment

**Operational compliance: materially noncompliant, but in bounded and identifiable areas — not globally.** Four distinct failures are proven at system level:

1. **Exception governance (High).** Two instruments, two expiries, two silent continuations (SUP-705; 6 cases after PD-3's sunset). Sunsets are not enforced in the field or in the tooling.
2. **Scope discipline on exceptions (High).** 14 ineligible Auto_Approve cases inside the pilot window plus SUP-703's deliberate selection — the eligibility predicates were not enforced at the point of use.
3. **Severe isolated preventive-control failure (Severe, low frequency).** SUP-708 proves the checks-then-approval-then-activation sequence can be executed out of order without any forcing function.
4. **Non-control-flow rules are unmonitored (High).** SUP-712 and SUP-715 are invisible to every tool the organisation runs; the population frequency of SoD and COI breaches is entirely unknown.

What is **not** established: that the onboarding process is generally out of control. After overlays, scope rules, projection and data corrections, 10 of the 15 sampled traces require no misconduct finding; the dominant pilot population operated inside a time-boxed instrument; the informal RMI practice is substantively safe.

**Model validity: M0 is materially obsolete and incomplete and must be withdrawn as the sole normative control model, while its CS-9-derived core is retained.** Specifically: M0 omits two in-force overlay routes, the RMI loop, three scope variants (rejected leads, reactivations, withdrawals), activity-label versioning, and **every** non-control-flow rule including CS-9's own SoD rule; it over-specifies the rejection path (QR before reject) contrary to CS-9's activation-conditional wording; and it locates notification in a log that no longer produces it (N4). M0 remains correct and worth keeping on exactly three points — checks before activation, approval before activation, notification before closure.

## 5.3 Where each side's argument is rejected

**Prosecution rejected on:**
- *"All Auto_Approve usage is a violation."* PD-3 was an instrument in force from 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31 with defined predicates, and 289 cases meet them on the record. The competence question (D1) is **disputed, not resolved in the prosecution's favour**; a steering committee's decision recorded in minutes is at minimum an operational authorisation, and the observer's later denial does not retroactively void it. The correct finding is "justified exception, authority ratification outstanding" — not violation.
- *"Quality_Review after activation is per se noncompliant."* CS-9 conditions quality review on **activation**; PD-3 and FD-7 both expressly permit deferral, with deadlines. SUP-702/704's QR placement is lawful within their overlays.
- *"Missing Notify_Requester is a control failure."* Notification is an M0 element, not a CS-9 duty, and N4 relocated it to a CRM system of record with 70% mirroring — matching the 29% observed missingness almost exactly. This is a collection defect (with a residual auditability concern), not a substantive breach.
- *"The 20% literal conformance rate shows material noncompliance."* It shows model obsolescence: two-thirds of the sample is explained by scope, vocabulary, identity and logging. Trace-deviation rate is not a compliance metric — as SUP-712 and SUP-715 prove in the opposite direction.

**Defense rejected on:**
- *"The flood factually continued, so SUP-705 stands."* No. Necessity arguments are addressed to the rule-maker; FD-7 expired at 2026-07-12 23:59 and no extension instrument exists. Test A and Test F of the authority test both fail.
- *"Approval was imminent, so SUP-714 stands."* No. A forecast has no authorising effect. The six August cases are unauthorised regardless of merit.
- *"Steering-committee minutes can derogate CS-9."* Unproved and, on the better view, wrong as to competence: a formally approved compliance standard can only be amended or derogated by the body that controls it, or by an explicitly empowered exception instrument with compliance co-signature. PD-3 was legitimate as a *monitored* operational departure and illegitimate as a silent rule change. This is why the ratification item stays open rather than being resolved against Operations.
- *"M0's defects excuse SUP-708, SUP-703, SUP-712."* No. The rules breached there live in CS-9 and the COI policy, which do not depend on M0's adequacy. A defective model lowers the *explanation* of a deviation, never its *legality*.
- *"Desirable practice is lawful practice."* Agreed only prospectively. But note the prosecution overreaches here as well: CS-9 never **prohibited** RMI, so SUP-707 is a model gap and not misconduct.

# 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The architecture is layered so that each rule lives in the weakest formalism that can still **enforce** it. Putting data rules into POWL would make the model unsound; putting ordering rules into pandas would make them unverifiable.

## Layer 0 — Scope and case typing (organisational control + data attribute)
A case is typed at registration: `case_type  {new_intake, pre_screen_lead, reactivation, withdrawal}` and routed to a model variant or filtered out **before** conformance checking.

| Rule | Content | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **S1 (rejected leads)** | A lead rejected at sanctions pre-screen is `pre_screen_lead`: in scope of the *intake* variant (Register  Sanctions_Check  Reject  Notify  Close) and **out of scope** of the full checks requirement, because CS-9 conditions Quality_Review on activation. It remains in scope of the COI/SoD and notification rules. | Must be ratified; resolves SUP-709/D2 |
| **S2 (reactivations)** | `reactivation` = activation of a supplier with a prior closed case within the certification validity window. Sanctions_Check must be re-performed; Quality_Review may be satisfied by a **carried certificate** whose `valid_to`  activation date and whose issuer is recognised; Manual_Procurement_Approval always required; `Register_Supplier` is not required (a `Record_Reactivation` event replaces it). If the certificate has expired or the supplier profile changed, the case converts to `new_intake`. | Must be ratified; resolves SUP-710/D3 |
| **S3 (withdrawals)** | A requester withdrawal before any decision ends the case: Register  (RMI*)  Withdraw_Request  Notify  Close. No checks obligation arises (CS-9's duties are activation-conditional); if the request is later revived it becomes a `reactivation`. | Must be created |
| **S4** | Cases outside all types are out of scope and must be excluded explicitly with a recorded reason — never silently. | Must be created |

## Layer 1 — Core control-flow (POWL, model family M1)
Retains M0's valid core and adds routes: standard (concurrent checks, manual approval), low-risk auto-approval, emergency/provisional, full rejection, pre-screen rejection, reactivation, withdrawal; `Request_More_Info*` and `Transaction_Monitoring*`; notification strictly before closure on **every** route. **POWL owns only ordering, concurrency, repetition and alternatives.**

## Layer 2 — Behavioural constraints not expressible as one partial order (DECLARE / log-skeleton)

| ID | Constraint | Why not POWL |
|---|---|---|
| D-1 | `Precedence(Sanctions_Check, Activate_Supplier)` — mandatory in **all** routes. | Cross-cutting invariant spanning route alternatives. |
| D-2 | `Precedence(Quality_Review, Activate_Supplier)` in standard/reactivation routes; **bounded response** `Quality_Review  activation + 2 business days` in auto/emergency routes. | Time bounds are not ordering relations. |
| D-3 | `Absence(Auto_Approve)` unless `overlay_id` is populated and in force (see L3/L4). | Data-dependent. |
| D-4 | `Exclusive(Auto_Approve, Manual_Procurement_Approval)` — exactly one approval per activating case. | Cardinality, not order. |
| D-5 | **No re-execution** of `Register_Supplier`, `Sanctions_Check`, `Quality_Review`, `Activate_Supplier` per case (`Request_More_Info`, `Transaction_Monitoring` excepted) — log-skeleton `equal-activities` frequency = 1. | Duplicate detection needs multiset reasoning (fixes SUP-711 class). |
| D-6 | `Response(Request_More_Info, Evidence_Arrival)` and every cycle must close before the decision; i.e. no activation between a request and its evidence. | Relates two event classes across a loop. |
| D-7 | `Chain-Precedence(Activate_Supplier, Notify_Requester, Close_Case)` and `Close_Case` last, for every route including rejection and withdrawal. | Already in POWL; retained as an audit invariant. |
| D-8 | Log-skeleton `eventually-follows` profiles per route monitored monthly to detect drift (e.g. growing Auto_Approve outside its route). | Statistical, not logical. |

## Layer 3 — Data-aware eligibility, amount, identity and time rules (pandas / engine guards)

| ID | Rule | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| R-C1 | Sanctions before activation | DECLARE D-1 + timestamp check |
| R-C2/R-C3 | Quality review before activation, or  2 business days (deferred routes) | pandas business-day calendar |
| R-C4 | Exactly one approval, before activation | pandas cardinality + timestamps |
| R-A1 | `Auto_Approve` permitted only if: overlay in force at `case_ts`  `risk_class=='LOW'`  `country=='DE'`  `group_status=='APPROVED'` (or a ratified permanent amendment)  `overlay_invocation_ts` present | pandas guard, applied **at run time** in the workflow engine and re-checked post hoc |
| R-A2 | `Master_Data_Validation` timestamp present and  activation for auto routes | pandas (evidence for a system-internal step) |
| R-E1 | Emergency: `spend_cumulative_before_retro_approval_eur  20000` | pandas ledger aggregation |
| R-E2 | Emergency:  1 `Transaction_Monitoring` event per calendar day until retrospective approval | pandas date bucketing |
| R-R1 | `requester_actor_id  approver_actor_id` after alias resolution; delegation permitted only with a `delegation_id` valid at `approver_ts` | pandas join on canonical actor IDs |
| R-R2 | If `relation_between_reviewer_and_approver  none` then `coi_disclosure_id` must be non-null and dated  the review | pandas |
| R-N1 | `Notify_Requester` present before `Close_Case` **with an immutable `notification_id`**; CRM-only evidence admissible only via an immutable FK | pandas; mutability flag makes the link inadmissible (SUP-706) |
| R-D1 | Event identity: one logical event per `source_transaction_id`; duplicates collapsed via `is_duplicate_of_event_id` | pandas dedup before conformance checking |
| R-D2 | Label equivalence applied only through a **versioned** mapping whose `mapping_approval_ts  event_ts` (post-hoc mappings are evidence, not authority — SUP-713) | pandas mapping table |
| R-T1 | All windows computed on a holiday-aware business-day calendar supplied as data | pandas |

## Layer 4 — Policy-overlay governance (organisational controls + a registry)

1. **Instrument register.** Every overlay (pilot, emergency, amendment) is recorded with issuer, competence basis, effective window, scope predicates, permitted derogations, compensating controls, required evidence, and ratification status. Nothing may derogate CS-9 without an explicit **competence statement** and compliance co-signature.
2. **Invocation is mandatory and recorded**: `overlay_id`, `overlay_authority_actor_id`, `overlay_invocation_ts`, `eligibility_snapshot_id` — per case, at decision time.
3. **Sunset is technical, not cultural.** On `effective_to`, the Auto_Approve and provisional-activation affordances are **disabled in the workflow engine**, and any attempt raises an alert. This single control would have prevented SUP-714 and the six August cases and, with an escalation path, SUP-705.
4. **Continuation/extension procedure.** Where an emergency's factual basis persists, a named authority issues a *written, dated extension* the same day. Silence is expiry (resolves SUP-705's systemic cause).
5. **Exception ledger.** Every invoked overlay produces a ledger entry reviewed weekly during the window and closed within 5 business days of expiry; "monitored exception" must mean monitoring with a named monitor and a report (which is where PD-3 failed).
6. **Prospective ratification of desirable practice.** Adopt the RMI loop and, if approved, a permanent low-risk auto route as CS-9 amendments with effective dates. No retroactive validation of SUP-703/705/708/712/714.

## Layer 5 — Evidence and auditability (organisational + data)
Immutable `recorded_ts` and `effective_ts`; immutable FKs for notifications; frozen eligibility snapshots; alias-resolution versioning with periodic population scans; pre-approved label mappings; retention and access rules; monthly KPIs: notification missingness (target < 2%, the pre-migration level), exception counts by overlay, post-sunset affordance attempts (target 0), SoD/COI rule hits.

**Rule  mechanism assignment summary:** ordering, concurrency, repetition, alternative  **POWL**; cross-route precedence/response/absence/cardinality/duplicate rules  **DECLARE / log-skeleton**; predicates over risk, country, group, amount, dates, identities and evidence presence  **pandas (with run-time guards)**; authority, competence, invocation, sunset, extension, disclosure  **organisational controls with a registry**.

# 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

```python
# =============================================================================
#  M1 -- revised normative POWL family for supplier onboarding
#  Self-contained: builds POWL objects in memory and runs in-memory data checks.
#  Reads/writes NO external files.  Requires: pm4py (POWL objects), pandas.
# =============================================================================
from datetime import datetime, timedelta

import pandas as pd
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    OperatorPOWL,
    SilentTransition,
    StrictPartialOrder,
    Transition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# --------------------------------------------------------------- builders ---
EDGES = {}          # id(SPO) -> [(src, trg)]  (kept outside the pm4py object)


def T(label):
    """Visible activity.  Labels are the conformance vocabulary; behaviour MODES
    (provisional activation, route id, risk class) are EVENT ATTRIBUTES, never
    new labels (see SUP-713)."""
    return Transition(label=label)


def EPS():
    """tau / silent step."""
    return SilentTransition()


def XOR(*kids):
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=list(kids))


def LOOP(do, redo):
    """Process-tree loop  do . (redo . do)*  -- POWL uses the same semantics."""
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[do, redo])


def STAR(label):
    """'label*' = ZERO OR MORE repetitions.
       Approximation: LOOP(tau, label) = tau . (label . tau)* == label*.
       LIMITATION: POWL cannot express 'interleave n>=1 X with an arbitrary
       subset of the checks and then re-enter the checks'.  We therefore make X
       optional-repeatable and FORBID re-execution of the checks (DECLARE D-5),
       which is the safer approximation."""
    return LOOP(EPS(), T(label))


def PO(nodes, edges=()):
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    for src, trg in edges:
        po.order.add_edge(src, trg)
    EDGES[id(po)] = list(edges)
    return po


# ------------------------------------------- Layer 0/1: scope + route family --
def r_standard():
    """CS-9 route: concurrent sanctions+quality, both complete, informed manual
    approval, then activation."""
    rmi = STAR("Request_More_Info")
    sanc = T("Sanctions_Check")
    qual = T("Quality_Review")            # CONCURRENT with sanc (no edge between)
    appr = T("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
    act = T("Activate_Supplier")
    return PO([rmi, sanc, qual, appr, act],
              [(rmi, appr), (sanc, appr), (qual, appr), (appr, act)])


def r_auto():
    """PD-3 style / future permanent low-risk route (guarded by R-A1).
       Quality_Review is ORDER-FREE here on purpose: PD-3 allows it before OR up
       to 2 business days after activation.  The 2-BD bound is enforced by R-C3
       (pandas), not by an edge, so that early review stays permitted."""
    rmi = STAR("Request_More_Info")
    sanc = T("Sanctions_Check")
    mdv = XOR(T("Master_Data_Validation"), EPS())   # system step: allowed silent
                                                    # ONLY with R-A2 timestamp
    appr = T("Auto_Approve")
    act = T("Activate_Supplier")
    qual = T("Quality_Review")
    return PO([rmi, sanc, mdv, appr, act, qual],
              [(rmi, appr), (sanc, appr), (mdv, appr), (appr, act)])


def r_emergency():
    """FD-7 style route: sanctions before PROVISIONAL activation (mode is an
    attribute, not a label); quality review and retrospective approval after."""
    rmi = STAR("Request_More_Info")
    sanc = T("Sanctions_Check")
    act = T("Activate_Supplier")                    # attr activation_mode=provisional
    qual = T("Quality_Review")
    retro = T("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
    mon = STAR("Transaction_Monitoring")
    return PO([rmi, sanc, act, qual, retro, mon],
              [(rmi, act), (sanc, act), (act, qual), (act, retro), (act, mon)])


def r_reject_full():
    """Rejection after the full checks block (both checks complete)."""
    rmi = STAR("Request_More_Info")
    sanc = T("Sanctions_Check")
    qual = T("Quality_Review")
    rej = T("Reject_Supplier")
    return PO([rmi, sanc, qual, rej], [(rmi, rej), (sanc, rej), (qual, rej)])


def r_reject_prescreen():
    """S1: abbreviated pre-screen for leads failing sanctions.  Conditionally in
    scope (SCOPE-INTAKE).  CS-9 conditions Quality_Review on ACTIVATION, so no
    quality review is required here."""
    sanc = T("Sanctions_Check")
    rej = T("Reject_Supplier")
    return PO([sanc, rej], [(sanc, rej)])


def r_reactivation():
    """S2: reactivation.  Fresh Quality_Review OR silent carry-forward of a
    still-valid certificate -- validity is R-T1/pandas, not expressible here."""
    rmi = STAR("Request_More_Info")
    sanc = T("Sanctions_Check")
    qual = XOR(T("Quality_Review"), EPS())          # fresh review OR carry-forward
    appr = T("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
    act = T("Activate_Supplier")
    return PO([rmi, sanc, qual, appr, act],
              [(rmi, appr), (sanc, qual), (qual, appr), (appr, act)])


def r_withdrawal():
    """S3: requester withdrawal -> closure only (notification still required)."""
    rmi = STAR("Request_More_Info")
    wdr = T("Withdraw_Request")
    return PO([rmi, wdr], [(rmi, wdr)])


stage = XOR(
    r_standard(),
    r_auto(),
    r_emergency(),
    r_reject_full(),
    r_reject_prescreen(),
    r_reactivation(),
    r_withdrawal(),
)

register = T("Register_Supplier")
notify = T("Notify_Requester")
close = T("Close_Case")

# Notify_Requester strictly before Close_Case on EVERY route (D-7).
M1 = PO([register, stage, notify, close],
        [(register, stage), (stage, notify), (notify, close)])


# --------------------------------------------------------------- pretty print
def _name(node):
    if isinstance(node, Transition):
        return node.label if node.label else "tau"
    return type(node).__name__


def describe(node, depth=0):
    pad = "  " * depth
    if isinstance(node, StrictPartialOrder):
        print(pad + "StrictPartialOrder")
        for n in node.nodes:
            describe(n, depth + 1)
        for src, trg in EDGES.get(id(node), []):
            print(pad + "    order: " + _name(src) + "  ->  " + _name(trg))
    elif isinstance(node, OperatorPOWL):
        op = getattr(node.operator, "value", node.operator)
        print(pad + "OperatorPOWL " + str(op))
        for n in node.children:
            describe(n, depth + 1)
    else:
        print(pad + _name(node))


# ===================== Layer 4: normalized policy-overlay registry ===========
POLICY_OVERLAYS = [
    dict(overlay_id="BASE-CS9", instrument="CS-9", version="2025.1",
         authority_body="Compliance (controlled, formally approved)",
         competence="full: creates/removes duties for all onboarding",

def case(case_id, route, case_date, ts, actors, attrs=None, notification=None, coi=None):
    return dict(
        case_id=case_id, route=route, case_date=case_date, ts=ts, actors=actors,
        attrs={**DEFAULT_ATTRS, **(attrs or {})},
        notification=notification or dict(present=False, notification_id=None,
                                          ts=None, link_mutable=True),
        coi=coi or dict(relation="none", disclosure_id=None),
    )


def add_business_days(ts, n):
    d, k = ts, 0
    while k < n:
        d += timedelta(days=1)
        if d.weekday() < 5:
            k += 1
    return d


def overlay_applicable(c):
    """Layer 4 -> Layer 3 bridge: which overlay (if any) covers this case?"""
    for ov in POLICY_OVERLAYS:
        if not ov["overlay_id"].startswith("OVL-") or ov["ratification"].startswith("NOT"):
            continue
        frm = datetime.fromisoformat(ov["effective_from"])
        to = datetime.fromisoformat(ov["effective_to"]) if ov["effective_to"] else datetime.max
        if not (frm <= c["case_date"] <= to):
            continue
        at = c["attrs"]
        if ov["overlay_id"].endswith("PD3") and not (
                at["risk_class"] == "LOW" and at["country"] == "DE"
                and at["group_status"] == "APPROVED"):
            continue
        if ov["overlay_id"].endswith("FD7") and not at["critical_relief"]:
            continue
        return ov
    return None


def evaluate_case(c):
    """Run the data-aware rule layer on one normalised case record."""
    out, ts, at, ac = [], c["ts"], c["attrs"], c["actors"]
    act = ts.get("Activate_Supplier")
    sanc = ts.get("Sanctions_Check")
    qual = ts.get("Quality_Review")
    given = [x for x in (ts.get("Manual_Procurement_Approval"), ts.get("Auto_Approve")) if x]

    # R-C1  sanctions before activation (all routes)
    out.append(("R-C1", "PASS" if (act and sanc and sanc <= act) else "FAIL",
                "sanctions before activation" if (act and sanc and sanc <= act)
                else "sanctions missing or not before activation"))

    # R-C2 / R-C3  quality review position
    if c["route"] in ("standard", "reactivation"):
        ok = bool(qual and act and qual <= act)
        out.append(("R-C2", "PASS" if ok else "FAIL", "quality review before activation"))
    elif c["route"] in ("auto", "emergency"):
        ok = bool(qual and act and qual <= add_business_days(act, 2))
        out.append(("R-C3", "PASS" if ok else "FAIL", "quality review within 2 business days"))
    elif c["route"] == "reject_full":
        out.append(("R-C2", "PASS" if qual else "FAIL", "quality review before rejection"))
    else:
        out.append(("R-C2", "NA", "quality review not required for this case_type (S1/S3)"))

    # R-C4  exactly one approval, before activation
    if act:
        out.append(("R-C4", "PASS" if (len(given) == 1 and given[0] <= act) else "FAIL",
                    "exactly one approval before activation"))
    else:
        out.append(("R-C4", "NA", "no activation in this case"))

    # R-A1 / R-A2  auto-approval authority and compensating control
    if ts.get("Auto_Approve"):
        ov = overlay_applicable(c)
        out.append(("R-A1", "PASS" if ov else "FAIL",
                    "Auto_Approve under " + (ov["overlay_id"] if ov else "NO VALID OVERLAY")))
        mdv = at["master_data_validation_ts"]
        out.append(("R-A2", "PASS" if (mdv and act and mdv <= act) else "FAIL",
                    "automated master-data validation evidenced before activation"))
    else:
        out.append(("R-A1", "NA", "no Auto_Approve in this case"))

    # R-E1 / R-E2  emergency conditions
    if c["route"] == "emergency":
        out.append(("R-E1", "PASS" if at["spend_before_retro_eur"] <= 20000 else "FAIL",
                    "EUR 20,000 cap until retrospective approval"))
        out.append(("R-E2", "PASS" if at["monitoring_days"] >= 1 else "FAIL",
                    "daily transaction monitoring"))

    # R-R1 / R-R2  identity and conflict of interest
    out.append(("R-R1", "PASS" if ac["requester"] != ac["approver"] else "FAIL",
                "separation of requester and approver (alias-resolved)"))
    coi = c["coi"]
    need = coi["relation"] not in (None, "none")
    out.append(("R-R2", "PASS" if (not need or coi["disclosure_id"]) else "FAIL",
                "COI disclosure" if need else "no disclosable relation"))

    # R-N1  notification evidenced by an immutable link, before closure
    nf = c["notification"]
    ok = bool(nf["present"] and nf["notification_id"] and not nf["link_mutable"]
              and ts.get("Close_Case") and nf["ts"] and nf["ts"] <= ts["Close_Case"])
    out.append(("R-N1", "PASS" if ok else "FAIL",
                "immutable notification link present" if ok else
                "notification absent / link mutable / after closure"))

    # R-D1 / R-D3  event identity and no re-execution
    out.append(("R-D1", "PASS" if at["duplicate_count"] == 0 else "FAIL",
                "duplicate source_transaction_id rows"))
    return out
```

**Documented limitations of the encoding (stated, not hidden).**

1. **Route selection is not enforced by POWL.** `OperatorPOWL(XOR, …)` is a *free* choice, so `M1` over-approximates any single case: it would "permit" an emergency route for a non-critical supplier. The binding of `route_id`/`overlay_id` to case attributes is a **data guard** (R-A1 and the L4 registry) that must be enforced at run time by the workflow engine and re-verified post hoc. Encoding the guards *inside* POWL is not expressible (POWL has no data).
2. **`STAR(label)` = `LOOP(tau, label)`** is the standard idiom for `label*`. It approximates "n  0 requests interleaved with the checks": the loop node is placed with no edge to the checks, so interleaving is allowed. What is deliberately **not** encoded is "re-enter the checks after a request" — that would permit duplicate Sanctions/Quality events (the SUP-711 pathology), so re-execution is forbidden by D-5 and the evidence-arrival obligation is R-I1/`Response`.
3. **`Quality_Review` in `r_auto` has no edges** on purpose (PD-3 allows review before *or* up to 2 business days after activation); the bound is R-C3. Adding `act  qual` would forbid legitimate early review.
4. **`Master_Data_Validation` is `XOR(label, tau)`** because it is a system-internal step that is not always logged. The silent branch is lawful **only** with an immutable `master_data_validation_ts` (R-A2) — this is the approximation for SUP-702/D9.
5. **Time windows, amount caps, identity, SoD, COI and authority/sunset are entirely outside POWL's expressive remit** and live in L3/L4. Amount caps are aggregate ledger properties; SoD is a relational property of actors; competence is a property of instruments.
6. **Carried quality certification in `r_reactivation` is `XOR(Quality_Review, tau)`**, which cannot express "only if the certificate is still valid"; that is `certificate_valid_to >= activation_ts` in pandas.
7. **The model assumes canonical event identity**: SUP-711-style duplicates must be collapsed (R-D1) before conformance checking, otherwise every partial-order node "fires twice".

## Event and data-collection proposal

**Two timestamps on every event:** `effective_ts` (business time) and `recorded_ts` (immutable write time). **Both immutable after write.** Corrections are new rows with `is_correction_of_event_id`, never updates.

| Field | Type | Immutability | Purpose / rule served |
|---|---|---|---|
| `event_id` | UUID | immutable | canonical event identity (R-D1) |
| `case_id`, `case_type` | str | immutable | Layer 0 scope typing (S1–S4) |
| `activity_label`, `label_version`, `mapping_approval_id` | str/str/UUID | immutable; mapping must be approved **before** use | R-D2 (SUP-713) |
| `source_transaction_id`, `is_duplicate_of_event_id`, `is_correction_of_event_id` | str/UUID/UUID | immutable | R-D1 deduplication (SUP-711) |
| `effective_ts`, `recorded_ts` | timestamp | immutable | ordering + auditability |
| `notification_id` (**FK to CRM**, never free text), `notification_ts`, `link_mutable_flag` | UUID/timestamp/bool | FK + ts immutable; `link_mutable` must be **false** to be admissible | R-N1 (SUP-706) |
| `rule_set_id`, `rule_version_id` (e.g. `CS9-2025.1`, `PD3-2026.1`, `FD7-2026.1`) | str | immutable, stamped **at event time** | which ruleset governed the decision |
| `overlay_id`, `overlay_authority_actor_id`, `overlay_invocation_ts` | str/UUID/timestamp | immutable | L4 invocation (Tests A–C) |
| `eligibility_snapshot_id` + frozen snapshot: `risk_class`, `country`, `group_status`, `critical_relief_flag`, `spend_initial_eur`, `spend_before_retro_eur`, `currency` | JSON | frozen at decision time | R-A1, R-E1 (SUP-703, SUP-714) |
| `master_data_validation_ts` | timestamp | immutable | R-A2 (SUP-702) |
| `requester_actor_id`, `approver_actor_id`, `quality_reviewer_actor_id`, `actor_alias_as_typed`, `alias_resolution_version` | UUID/str/str/str/str | immutable; alias table versioned | R-R1 (SUP-712) |

**Collection fixes, in priority order:** (1) make `notification_id` an immutable foreign key and backfill all post-2026-07-01 notifications; (2) freeze eligibility snapshots at decision time and stop editing them afterwards; (3) version and freeze the actor-alias table and run a population scan for requester/approver identity collisions; (4) approve label mappings **before** the UI change takes effect; (5) restore notification log completeness to < 2% missingness or designate the CRM as the system of record and validate by `notification_id`; (6) technical sunset enforcement so that Auto_Approve and provisional-activation affordances disappear with their overlay.
