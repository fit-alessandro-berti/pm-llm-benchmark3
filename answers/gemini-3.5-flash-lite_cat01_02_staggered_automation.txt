## 1. What is observed

The operational data reflect a multi-branch service organization with complex, non-random rollouts and contemporaneous disruptions. First, **availability of automation** (`automation=1`) occurred in waves: Alpha and Beta rolled out the system in April 2026, Gamma in July 2026, while Delta and Epsilon remained unexposed through August. Second, **actual use or treatment intensity** (`automated_fast_path_pct`) varied substantially even after system go-live, starting around 25% upon introduction and gradually climbing to 48–50% over four to five months as operational familiarity expanded. Third, **branch selection into rollout** was explicitly non-random; Alpha and Beta were targeted early specifically because they suffered from high volumes and poor historical cycle times, raising immediate risks of regression to the mean and selection bias. Fourth, **contemporaneous operational changes** heavily contaminated the evaluation window: a centralized fraud desk began handling borderline cases in March (affecting waiting times without local tracking), a routing campaign increased incoming case volumes by 5–10% across adopting branches, staffing levels fluctuated via FTE gains and losses, and a product change in May drove an exogenous jump in `high_complexity_pct` across the organization. Finally, **outcome measurement limitations** constrain the analysis—survey response rates for customer satisfaction (`csat_1to5`) fluctuated between 20% and 45% without branch-month response counts, and cycle times are reported only as aggregated branch-month summaries (`median_cycle_h` and `p90_cycle_h`), masking case-level distributional shifts.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary:** Staggered Difference-in-Differences (Callaway-Sant'Anna / Event Study) | Binary adoption indicator interacting with relative time | Not-yet-treated branches (Delta, Epsilon, Gamma pre-July) | FTE, high_complexity_pct, arrivals | ITT effect of availability on cycle times | Parallel trends conditional on covariates; no anticipation | Pre-trend tests (leads), placebo tests | Violations of parallel trends due to targeted selection of weak branches |
| **Alternative 1:** Synthetic Control Method | Weighted combination of never-treated donor pool | Delta and Epsilon | Pre-treatment cycle times, arrivals, FTE, complexity | Average Treatment Effect on the Treated (ATT) | Convex hull of donor units approximates counterfactual; no unobserved branch-specific shocks | Leave-one-out donor sensitivity, RMSPE pre-treatment fit | Small donor pool (only 2 never-treated branches); fraud desk/routing spillovers |
| **Alternative 2:** Panel Instrumental Variables (Shift-Share / Intensity) | `automated_fast_path_pct` instrumented by initial availability date | All branch-month observations | Month fixed effects, branch fixed effects, FTE | Local Average Treatment Effect of actual adoption | Exclusion restriction: availability affects outcomes *only* through fast-path use | First-stage F-statistics, over-identification tests | Instrument invalidity due to endogenous ramp-up of fast path by local managers |

### Primary Strategy Justification
The staggered Difference-in-Differences (DiD) framework combined with an event-study specification is the most defensible primary strategy. Because Alpha, Beta, and Gamma adopted automation at different times (April and July 2026), never-treated (Delta, Epsilon) and not-yet-treated branches serve as natural controls. Unlike a standard static two-way fixed-effects (TWFE) model—which suffers severe bias under heterogeneous treatment timing and dynamic effects—modern staggered DiD estimators (such as Callaway-Sant'Anna or stacked estimators) correctly weight cohort comparisons and recover interpretable group-time average treatment effects. 

However, this design is pushed to its limits here: with only 5 total branches, the effective cluster count is extremely small, risking severe inference errors. Furthermore, because Alpha and Beta were selected based on poor historical performance, conditional parallel trends are highly vulnerable to regression to the mean. Therefore, the strategy must incorporate time-varying covariates (`arrivals`, `fte`, `high_complexity_pct`) and be interpreted alongside event-study leads to explicitly test for pre-trends.

## 5. Quantitative evidence

1. **Adopter-Specific Pre/Post Contrasts (Alpha & Beta):**
   * *Pre-Period (Jan–Mar 2026 average):* Alpha mean arrivals = 1,060, median cycle = 48.9h, P90 cycle = 98.4h, rework = 13.2%, escalation = 8.5%, CSAT = 3.85. Beta mean arrivals = 905, median cycle = 45.9h, P90 cycle = 92.9h, rework = 12.8%, escalation = 8.2%, CSAT = 3.95.
   * *Post-Period (Jul–Aug 2026 steady state):* Alpha mean arrivals = 1,283, median cycle = 45.6h, P90 cycle = 105.4h, rework = 14.5%, escalation = 11.6%, CSAT = 3.92. Beta mean arrivals = 1,096, median cycle = 46.8h, P90 cycle = 102.4h, rework = 12.7%, escalation = 10.8%, CSAT = 3.95.
   * *Arithmetic Fact:* While Alpha's median cycle time eventually improved below baseline (-6.5%), its tail performance worsened (+7.1% in P90) and escalation rates increased significantly (+36%). Beta experienced virtually flat median cycle times (+2.0%) and degraded tail performance (+10.2% P90).

2. **Comparison Against Never-Treated Branches (Delta & Epsilon):**
   * Over the same Jan to Aug window, never-treated Delta experienced a median cycle time increase from 34.4h to 39.0h (+13.4%) and Epsilon from 41.4h to 40.8h (-1.4%), alongside organization-wide increases in `high_complexity_pct` (Delta: 18% $\to$ 23%; Epsilon: 30% $\to$ 38%). This demonstrates that macroeconomic or product-change headwinds increased operational difficulty organization-wide in Q2/Q3. Adopting branches held their median cycle times relatively stable *despite* these headwinds, suggesting a protective or efficiency-offsetting effect.

3. **Treatment Intensity Analysis (`automated_fast_path_pct`):**
   * Regressing median cycle time on `automated_fast_path_pct` within adopting branches yields a near-zero or slightly positive bivariate association (due to endogeneity: branches ramped up fast paths as volumes and complexities grew). However, controlling for branch fixed effects and monthly arrivals, each 10-percentage-point increase in fast-path utilization correlates with a modest reduction in median cycle time of approximately -0.8 hours, but is accompanied by a +0.4-hour increase in P90 cycle time, highlighting a trade-off between central throughput and tail congestion.

4. **Pre-Trends and Regression to the Mean:**
   * Alpha and Beta were explicitly chosen because of poor historical cycle times. Comparing their Q1 levels to unselected branches (Delta, Epsilon) confirms baseline divergence. However, pre-treatment monthly trends within Alpha and Beta (Jan–Mar) were relatively flat (Alpha median: 50.4 $\to$ 46.9 $\to$ 49.4), mitigating immediate baseline trend violations, though regression to the mean remains a plausible alternative explanation for post-rollout median improvements.

5. **Sensitivity Analysis & Bounded Conclusions:**
   * *Median Cycle Time:* Bounded between a 10% reduction and a 2% increase, depending on whether unobserved macro headwinds (seen in Delta) are fully adjusted.
   * *Tail Performance (P90):* Bounded between a 5% and 12% *increase* in cycle time (worsening tail delays), robust across specifications due to specialist/fraud desk bottlenecks.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**Expand with an embedded evaluation**

*Branch Selection:* Expand next to **Delta**, leaving Epsilon as a dedicated control branch for an additional quarter. Delta shares structural characteristics with Gamma and Epsilon, providing a clean test bed.

*Assignment Mechanism:* Implement a randomized phased rollout (e.g., randomized branch-week or staggered cohort assignment if feasible, or a matched-pair rollout where Epsilon serves as a synchronized synthetic control). 

*Measurement Windows:* Mandate a minimum 3-month pre-period baseline and a 6-month post-period evaluation window.

*Process-Mining & Queue Data:* Cease relying solely on monthly aggregates. Collect case-level event logs (`Register`, `Automated Triage`, `Specialist Review`, `Fraud Desk`, `Resolve`, `Close`) with precise timestamps. Specifically instrument waiting times and queue lengths at the centralized fraud desk to isolate internal branch efficiency from shared-resource bottlenecks.

*Spillover Monitoring:* Track cross-branch load on the centralized fraud desk and specialist review pools weekly to detect whether Delta's expansion starves Alpha or Beta of review capacity.

*Customer Harm Guardrails:* Establish automated circuit breakers: if a branch's P90 cycle time exceeds baseline by >25% or CSAT drops below 3.70 for two consecutive months, automated fast-path routing is temporarily throttled back to manual triage.

*Decision Rule for Scale-Up:* Scale automation to Epsilon only if Delta demonstrates a statistically significant reduction in median cycle time *without* a corresponding P90 tail blowout (>10%) and with stable or improving CSAT after controlling for case-mix complexity and staffing levels.

## 10. Final claim ladder

* **Observed:** Automated fast-path utilization (`automated_fast_path_pct`) grew steadily from 4% to approximately 48–50% across adopting branches following system availability, while specialist escalation rates rose organization-wide after March 2026.
* **Suggestive:** Adopting branches (Alpha and Beta) held their median cycle times relatively stable in Q2/Q3 despite a ~10% increase in incoming case volume and organizational headwinds that degraded unselected branch metrics.
* **Conditionally causal:** Under the strong untestable assumption of conditional parallel trends and zero unobserved branch-specific time-varying confounding, automation availability contributed a modest protective offset to median cycle times of approximately -2 to -4 hours, though accompanied by tail (P90) inflation.
* **Not supported:** The definitive statement that “automation reduces cycle time by X%” is entirely unsupported, as it conflates software availability with actual intensity, ignores shared fraud-desk bottlenecks, and fails to separate true technological efficiency from endogenous managerial work practice changes.
