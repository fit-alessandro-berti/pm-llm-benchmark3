## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

### Upstream data-quality and flow dependencies

The automation sequence should follow the information dependencies of the case, rather than the apparent order in the event log.

1. **Intake, registration, and classification**
   - A1 and A2 improve early data capture and routing.
   - A2 affects the required-document checklist in A5, the KYC risk path in A3, the potential collateral route in A7, and the approval path for High-exposure SME cases.
   - Because classification errors currently misroute cases, A2 should not autonomously alter a control path. It should propose a route with an explicit human confirmation or a rules-based confidence threshold.

2. **KYC and entity resolution**
   - A3 can triage potential KYC hits; A4 can assist with the difficult entity-mismatch cases.
   - A4 must be an evidence-gathering and recommendation tool, not an autonomous KYC-clearance tool. The `Resolve entity mismatch -> Contract preparation` relation for 380 cases is a control concern, not evidence that direct progression to contract preparation is acceptable.
   - The principal path is `KYC check -> Resolve entity mismatch` for 1,750 cases, with 1,160 returning to KYC. That loop must retain the KYC re-check and evidence record.

3. **Documents, normalization, and analysis**
   - A5 is a practical prerequisite for A6. Extracting and normalizing incomplete statements simply moves bad inputs through the process faster.
   - A6 should feed a human-approved normalized-statement artifact into credit analysis, not a direct autonomous credit outcome.
   - The `Financial document review -> Request missing documents` relation occurs 3,300 times and returns to review 2,800 times. Reducing that loop is likely more valuable than accelerating routine registration.
   - KYC and financial review are often concurrent in reality, even though the system records them sequentially. The target workflow should maintain independent KYC and financial-document states. It should not impose a new artificial rule that financial work must wait for KYC unless policy actually requires it.

4. **Collateral, committee, and synchronization**
   - A7 is dependent on valid collateral eligibility, valuer capacity, accepted valuation standards, and usable provider-performance data.
   - Committee packaging depends on reconciled KYC status, normalized financial statements, collateral valuation when required, and credit-analysis notes. Therefore A8 is downstream of A4, A6, A7, and the manual credit-analysis process.
   - The 58-hour external valuation wait cannot be assumed to disappear through routing alone. A7 can reduce avoidable routing delay and improve vendor allocation, but it cannot create external capacity.
   - The weekly committee cadence for High-exposure cases remains a structural delay even if A8 reduces pack assembly work.

5. **Contract, signing, and activation**
   - A9 requires authoritative KYC status and authoritative approval terms. It must account for both committee decisions and other legitimate decision authorities, because `Committee decision -> Contract preparation` occurs 3,650 times while `Contract preparation -> Customer signing` occurs 7,300 times.
   - A10 must consume a hard “KYC cleared and contract releasable” status. It must not merely rely on a case appearing to be near contract completion.
   - A11 depends on all preceding controls: valid contract, verified signing, KYC clearance, decision consistency, and all required activation controls. It should not be implemented before these controls are stable.

6. **Feedback and remediation**
   - A12 should use the same issue taxonomy as A4, A5, A6, A9, and manual control processes.
   - Remediation findings should feed back into document checklists, classification rules, KYC evidence requirements, and the legal-rule library rather than remain isolated case notes.

### Legal and compliance controls

- **KYC must be a hard signing gate.** Provisional signing occurred in 6.5% of cases and 1.1% required remediation. Automation should prevent new instances, not merely make them easier to remediate.
- **Four-eyes separation must be enforced before committee packaging.** For High-exposure SME cases, the person completing Credit analysis must not complete Committee packaging. This cannot be checked only after contract generation; it needs identity-aware workflow allocation and a preventive block or independent reassignment.
- **Decision-to-contract consistency must use versioned data.** A9 needs a versioned decision record, terms snapshot, legal rule version, and evidence that the final contract was generated from the approved version.
- **Every model-assisted action needs an audit trail.** This includes input data, model/rule version, confidence or recommendation, human override, final decision, user identity, timestamp, and linked source evidence.

### Human escalation capacity and changed work

- A3 and A4 cannot be assessed only by automation rate. The compliance-specialist pool has only three FTE, and the handover network is concentrated on two senior specialists.
- A4 should reduce time spent locating evidence, but ambiguous cases must still be routed to qualified reviewers. A backup roster and skill transfer are prerequisites; otherwise the tool will concentrate work even more heavily on the same two people.
- A5 reduces repetitive RM and back-office chasing, but it also changes RM work from ad hoc follow-up to clearer up-front guidance and exception explanation.
- A6 releases analysts from routine normalization, but increases the number of decision-ready cases reaching the 110-minute credit-analysis task. WIP limits and analyst queue monitoring are necessary.
- A9 shifts some work from drafting toward legal-rule ownership, exception adjudication, and month-end release management. Legal Ops may become more productive overall while still being overloaded at month end.

### External valuation dependency

A7 should be treated as a combination of technology and supplier-management intervention:

- measure turnaround, late rate, rework, quality disputes, geography, and case type by valuer;
- use routing only where the bank has genuine choice of qualified provider;
- create clear escalation and substitution rules;
- avoid automated routing that favors speed at the cost of valuation quality or policy compliance;
- consider whether valuation-readiness checks can be started earlier without violating credit policy.

The bank should not claim that a routing tool controls the 58-hour external wait until it proves that routing choices, rather than supplier capacity, are the primary cause.

### Core-banking integration constraint

Only two modules should receive deep core-banking integration in the first six months:

1. **A9** — access to authoritative KYC, decision, contract, and release-status data is necessary for meaningful contract controls.
2. **A4** — controlled access to party-master and KYC-case data is needed to support entity-mismatch evidence and reliable status updates.

A5 and A6 can begin with portal/document-management and analyst-workbench integration, with human approval before any core update. A1 and A11 should not compete for the scarce first-half core integrations: A1 is operationally attractive but not process-critical, and A11 is unsafe until upstream controls are mature.

## 3. Portfolio alternatives

### Quick-win portfolio: A1, A2, A9, A10

**What it optimizes**
- Visible automation and direct labor reduction in registration, classification, contract generation, and customer follow-up.
- A1 benefits from Register appearing in position 2 in 98% of cases.
- A10 can improve the customer-signing portion of the journey after contract preparation.

**What it sacrifices**
- It does little for document loops, analyst capacity, entity-mismatch delays, or external valuation waits.
- It risks accelerating incomplete or wrongly classified cases into KYC and financial review.
- It needs A9’s KYC-release control before A10 can be safely expanded.
- It may create a politically visible improvement in early-stage throughput without materially improving difficult cases or end-to-end tail cycle time.

**Conditions**
- A2 is recommendation-only until classification accuracy and control-path routing are validated.
- A10 must be disabled for cases without recorded KYC clearance and valid consent.
- A1 should not consume a core integration slot ahead of contract and KYC controls.

### Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A4, A5, A6, A9

**What it optimizes**
- The major internal exception loops and synchronization failures:
  - entity mismatch after KYC;
  - missing-document loops;
  - financial normalization workload;
  - contract-control and remediation loops.
- It improves the quality and availability of artifacts needed by credit analysis, committee packaging, contract preparation, and signing.
- It includes A9 as the first-wave control-strengthening module.

**What it sacrifices**
- It does not directly automate the external valuation wait or customer-signing reminders.
- Equipment-finance cases with required collateral valuation may still have long tails because of the 58-hour external wait.
- It does not automate the committee decision itself, including the weekly cadence for High-exposure cases.
- It requires active workload management so that freed-up upstream capacity does not overload credit analysts or Legal Ops.

**Treatment of A7**
- A7 is not ignored; it is managed as a readiness-gated supplier and process intervention during the first year.
- If valuer-performance data demonstrates that routing materially changes wait time without degrading quality, A7 becomes a strong second-year or substitution candidate.

### Compliance-first portfolio: A3, A4, A9, A12

**What it optimizes**
- KYC triage and entity-resolution evidence.
- Reduction of provisional signing and contract-decision mismatch risk.
- Better visibility into the causes of control remediation.
- Stronger auditability, exception ownership, and policy feedback.

**What it sacrifices**
- It does not materially reduce the missing-document loop or normalization burden.
- Credit analysts may remain constrained by document quality and statement normalization.
- Cycle time may initially increase because more suspicious cases are surfaced, reviewed, and correctly routed.
- A3 can increase load on the three-person specialist team if thresholds are not carefully tuned.

**Conditions**
- A3 has no autonomous KYC-clearance authority.
- A12 must have a named policy owner able to resolve recurring ambiguity; otherwise it becomes a better queue for unresolved issues rather than a control improvement.

## 4. Expected process-level effects

The effects below are directional. They should be measured by customer segment, product, exposure class, collateral requirement, and exception path; median and tail elapsed times should not be summed across activities because KYC and financial review may run concurrently.

### Quick-win portfolio

- **Cycle time:**  
  Likely improves the fast, clean path modestly. A1 affects the high-volume `Receive application -> Register application` flow, but the activity itself has short service time and a 1.5-hour median pre-wait. A10 can improve the post-contract portion of the path, particularly the 44-hour Customer-signing wait. Long-cycle cases driven by document loops, entity mismatch, analyst queues, or external valuation will remain largely unchanged.

- **Waiting time:**  
  Back-office waits may fall, but the process can simply move waiting into KYC, financial review, and credit analysis. A10 may reduce some reminder-related signing delay, but only after a valid contract has been released.

- **Rework:**  
  A9 can reduce the `Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue -> Contract preparation` loop, especially important for Credit-line renewals where contract remediation is higher. A1 can reduce duplicate creation. Conversely, weak A2 classification can generate new rework by sending cases to the wrong document, collateral, or control path.

- **Handovers:**  
  Registration and routine customer follow-up handovers decline. New handovers may appear where A2 recommendations require review or where A10 identity/consent exceptions are routed to RMs and operations.

- **Resource demand:**  
  Back-office workload declines, but the observed below-65% utilization after 15:00 suggests that better scheduling and queue allocation may matter as much as headcount reduction. Faster entry can raise KYC, document-review, analyst, and legal demand. Legal Ops may receive more contracts sooner while still remaining exposed to month-end peaks.

- **Control violations:**  
  A9 can prevent certain contract and KYC-release failures if it reads authoritative statuses. Without hard KYC and consent gates, A10 could worsen the existing provisional-signing problem. The four-eyes issue remains unresolved unless the workflow independently prevents the same person from completing Credit analysis and Committee packaging.

### Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio

- **Cycle time:**  
  This portfolio should improve exception-heavy internal paths rather than merely the clean front end.
  - A4 targets the 1,750 entity-mismatch cases, where median waiting is 31 hours and the activity is on the critical path in 82% of affected cases.
  - A5 targets the high-volume document loop: 3,300 `Financial document review -> Request missing documents` transitions and 2,800 returns to review.
  - A6 improves the `Financial document review -> Normalize financial statements -> Credit analysis` path, represented by 5,050 and 5,000 directly-follows relations.
  - A9 reduces avoidable contract loops and blocks release of contracts that do not satisfy KYC and decision-data controls.
  
  The portfolio will not remove the 58-hour external valuation wait, so Equipment-finance cases and collateral-required Working-capital cases may continue to dominate the longest tail.

- **Waiting time:**  
  A4 can reduce time spent searching for identity evidence and routing mismatches to the right specialist. A5 reduces avoidable internal wait and repeated customer requests, although customer response time remains partly outside bank control. A6 reduces routine analyst preparation effort and should reduce the wait before usable credit analysis. A9 reduces wait caused by preventable contract corrections, but it may deliberately hold unsafe cases.

- **Rework:**  
  The largest expected improvement is in repeated document review and avoidable normalization correction. A9 should reduce contract-remediation loops, provided it uses a current legal-rule library and approved decision terms. It should also expose, rather than hide, unresolved KYC or decision-data defects.

- **Handovers:**  
  RMs, back office, analysts, compliance specialists, Legal Ops, and customers should have fewer unstructured handoffs. The intended handoff becomes a structured exception with ownership, evidence, and due date. The concentration of KYC escalation work on two senior specialists must be reduced through backup assignment and knowledge transfer; otherwise A4 could simply make them the faster but still dominant bottleneck.

- **Resource demand:**  
  This portfolio shifts work rather than eliminating it:
  - RMs spend less time sending incomplete instructions but more time handling genuinely non-standard customer situations.
  - Back-office workload falls in repeated requests and can be redeployed to exception-quality checks.
  - Analysts spend less time on routine normalization, but more complete files may increase credit-analysis demand. Since analysts are central in the working-together network, WIP controls are necessary.
  - Compliance specialists receive better-prepared mismatch cases, but still retain final responsibility.
  - Legal Ops receives cleaner contracts, but contract exceptions and rule-library maintenance remain human work.

- **Control violations:**  
  A4 improves KYC evidence quality but does not grant autonomous clearance. A9 should create a hard release hold for missing KYC clearance and inconsistent decision terms. For High-exposure SME cases, the bank must add an identity-aware workflow control before Committee packaging so that the 5.8% four-eyes violation rate is prevented rather than detected after the fact.

### Compliance-first portfolio

- **Cycle time:**  
  Clean cases may see limited improvement. Some cases may initially take longer because A3, A4, and A12 expose ambiguous entities, weak evidence, and policy uncertainty that previously passed through informally. The trade-off is appropriate where the bank prioritizes legal and regulatory risk over throughput.

- **Waiting time:**  
  KYC wait can improve for clearly resolvable cases, but the specialist queue may worsen if the pre-classifier has a high false-positive rate. The three-person specialist pool and dependency on two senior employees make capacity monitoring essential. Financial-document, analyst, collateral, and signing waits remain mostly unchanged.

- **Rework:**  
  A4 and A12 can reduce repeated KYC and remediation work by preserving evidence and identifying root causes. A9 should reduce contract mismatches. The document loop remains unaddressed, so `Request missing documents -> Financial document review` rework will persist.

- **Handovers:**  
  Compliance handovers become more explicit and auditable. However, the portfolio can create additional escalations to specialists, Legal Ops, and policy owners. It is therefore a control-strengthening portfolio, not necessarily a handover-reduction portfolio in the short term.

- **Resource demand:**  
  Compliance, legal, and policy teams receive more structured work. Credit analysts gain little capacity relief. The risk is that scarce senior compliance capacity becomes an even more visible queue unless case triage, delegation rules, and backup coverage are established.

- **Control violations:**  
  This is the strongest portfolio for preventing new provisional-signing events and reducing contract-decision mismatches. It can also use A12 to analyze the root causes of the month-end four-eyes violations. It still requires preventive access and assignment controls at Credit analysis and Committee packaging; a remediation workbench alone is not sufficient.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

| Automation risk | Where it arises | Compensating control |
|---|---|---|
| Incomplete audit trail or inability to explain an automated recommendation | A2, A3, A4, A6, A9 and any future A7/A11 implementation | Store source documents/data, model or rule version, confidence, suggestion, human override, final action, user identity, timestamps, and linked case evidence. Retain immutable audit logs and provide an investigator view. |
| False negative KYC outcome | A3 or A4 | No model-generated KYC clearance. Low-risk triage may prioritize work, but final clearance requires an authorized human decision and evidence. Use retrospective sampling, challenger reviews, and immediate suspension rules for suspected misses. |
| Excessive false positives and specialist overload | A3, A4 | Capacity-based escalation thresholds, aged-queue alerts, named backup specialists, skill transfer from the two senior specialists, and a separate “information missing” queue rather than repeatedly sending cases to the same expert. |
| Wrong entity match | A4 | Require evidence from approved sources, display conflicting attributes, require dual confirmation or specialist approval for material mismatches, and preserve the reason for match acceptance. |
| Misclassification sends a case to the wrong control path | A2 | Recommendation-only deployment; deterministic mandatory rules for High-exposure, collateral, and restricted products; confidence-based review; periodic review of route overrides and downstream rework. |
| Rigid document prompts harm customers or create abandonment | A5 | Co-design instructions with RMs; allow customer explanation and document substitution; do not auto-reject because a document is absent; measure complaints, abandonment, repeat requests, and RM overrides by customer segment. |
| Extraction or normalization errors affect credit judgment | A6 | Use confidence thresholds, source-to-output reconciliation, analyst approval for every material statement, separate treatment for low-quality PDFs, and testing by product, applicant type, source format, and language. |
| Model drift or changes in customer/document populations | A2, A3, A4, A6 | Monitor override rates, error types, confidence distributions, exception rates, and performance by Micro, SME, High-exposure SME, product, and source channel. Require model approval and rollback before material change. |
| Stale legal rule library or mismatched contract terms | A9 | Legal ownership of rule versions; effective dates; approved decision-term snapshot; automated comparison against the correct approval authority; exception cannot silently overwrite approved terms. |
| Signing before KYC clearance or without valid consent | A9, A10 | Hard release gate in the contract/e-sign workflow; no signing link before a recorded KYC-cleared status; identity and consent verification; exception process that cannot bypass compliance approval. |
| Four-eyes violation in High-exposure SME cases | Credit analysis and Committee packaging, including any future A8 | Preventive segregation-of-duties check using authenticated user identities; block same-person completion; emergency exception requires independent approver and audit trail. Monitor the month-end period separately. |
| Automation creates a surge into an already constrained queue | A5, A6, A7, A1 | WIP limits, queue-age monitoring, controlled rollout by variant, analyst capacity planning, and throttling of automated release if credit-analysis or committee wait rises. |
| External valuation routing reduces quality or merely shifts delay | A7 | Approved valuer panel, quality and turnaround scorecards, routing explainability, policy constraints, vendor SLA escalation, and manual override by collateral specialists. |
| Unsafe downstream booking or activation | A10, A11 | Do not automate activation until KYC, signing, contract, and operational controls are proven complete. Reconcile activation status daily and retain a kill switch with manual fallback. |

Automation does not transfer compliance accountability to a model, portal, or bot. Compliance, Legal Ops, credit approvers, and operations remain accountable for the final controlled state of the application.

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

The rollout below implements the **bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A4, A5, A6, and A9**.

### Phase plan

| Phase | Timing | Scope and module order | Deep core-banking integration use | Key gate criteria |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Foundation and baselining** | Months 0–1 | Validate event data; distinguish actual KYC/document concurrency from sequential system logging; define case-state model; assign control owners; establish variant baselines | None | Reliable timestamps and identities for KYC clearance, document loops, normalized statements, credit analysis, committee packaging, decision terms, contract release, signing, and activation |
| **1. Document-quality pilot and shadow normalization** | Months 2–3 | Launch A5 for a limited product/customer cohort with RM co-design; run A6 in shadow mode on common, high-quality statement formats; begin A9 legal-rule library and decision-data mapping | No deep core writes; use portal, CRM, document-management, and analyst workbench | No material increase in customer complaints, abandonment, or repeat contacts; human reconciliation confirms A6 outputs; analyst capacity plan accepted |
| **2. KYC/contract control integration** | Months 4–6 | Implement A4 as an evidence and routing assistant with mandatory human approval; put A9 into monitor-and-hold mode for missing KYC clearance and term mismatches | **Integration 1: A9** to authoritative decision, KYC, contract-release data. **Integration 2: A4** to party-master/KYC case data | No autonomous KYC clearance; complete evidence logging; A9 holds unsafe releases rather than generating workaround paths; four-eyes identity data available for High-exposure cases |
| **3. Controlled production expansion** | Months 7–9 | Expand A5 by product and document type; permit A6 human-approved normalized outputs into credit workflow; expand A4 only for tested mismatch classes; activate A9 contract checks and controlled generation for clean cases | No additional deep core modules | No increase in analyst queue age or credit-analysis wait caused by cleaner inflow; specialist aged exceptions remain within approved service levels; contract exceptions are traceable and owned |
| **4. Stabilize, scale, and decide next option** | Months 10–12 | Scale successful cohorts; refine rules and exception routes; assess A7 and A10 readiness for the next investment cycle | No additional first-year deep integration commitment | Sustained audit completeness; no new automated signing-before-KYC event; no automated four-eyes breach; decision-to-contract mismatch trends improve; external valuer data supports or rejects A7 business case |

### Monitoring metrics

Metrics should be reported by customer segment, product, exposure class, collateral requirement, and exception path. Month-end should be reported separately because the control and resource profile differs.

| Area | Monitoring metrics |
|---|---|
| **End-to-end performance** | Median and tail case elapsed time; time to KYC clearance; time from complete documents to credit analysis; time from decision to contract; time from contract release to signing; time to activation |
| **Waiting and WIP** | Queue age and work-in-progress for compliance specialists, analysts, Legal Ops, committee packaging, collateral team, and activation; wait after entity mismatch; wait after normalization; valuer wait by provider |
| **Rework and quality** | Repeated missing-document requests; review-return loops; extraction reconciliation errors; entity-mismatch reopen rate; contract-remediation rate, especially for Credit-line renewal |
| **Controls** | New signing-before-KYC events; KYC remediation; four-eyes violations for High-exposure SME cases; decision-to-contract mismatches; rule overrides; evidence completeness; audit-log completeness |
| **Human resource effects** | Specialist escalation volume and aging; dependence on the two senior specialists; analyst utilization and credit-analysis queue; RM override rate and incomplete-instruction rate; Legal Ops monthly workload pattern |
| **Customer effects** | Portal completion, reminder response, customer complaints, abandonment, document substitution requests, and RM-assisted completion rate |
| **Model and rule health** | False-positive/false-negative investigations, confidence distributions, overrides, drift by segment, rule-version usage, and rollback events |

### Common release gates

A module should not be expanded merely because direct labor time falls. Expansion requires:

- complete and retrievable audit evidence for every automated recommendation and exception;
- no autonomous KYC-clearance decision from A3 or A4;
- no new digitally released signing event without a recorded cleared KYC status;
- preventive four-eyes enforcement for High-exposure SME cases, with no same-person Credit analysis and Committee packaging completion;
- stable or improved analyst and specialist queue age after upstream automation;
- no material deterioration in customer experience or RM workarounds;
- legal approval of the A9 rule library and demonstrated traceability from decision terms to contract output;
- a manual fallback and kill switch for each production module.

A7 should be funded next only if the vendor data shows that routing changes can reduce external wait or late valuations without increasing quality disputes. A10 should follow only after the KYC-release and consent gates have been demonstrated in production.

## 7. Final recommendation

Recommend the **bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A4, A5, A6, and A9**, with A7 managed through a first-year supplier-readiness track rather than treated as an assumed automation win.

This portfolio is the best trade-off because it addresses the internal conditions that make cases slow, reworked, and unsafe:

- **A4** targets a relatively infrequent but consequential exception: entity mismatch has a 31-hour median wait and is on the critical path for most affected cases. It also addresses the concentrated dependence on two senior compliance specialists.
- **A5** reduces the high-volume document loop that repeatedly returns cases from customer follow-up to financial review and is partly caused by incomplete RM instructions.
- **A6** releases analyst time from normalization, which is important because analysts are central in the working-together network and credit analysis is both long and highly waited.
- **A9** provides the required first-wave control benefit: it reduces contract remediation, supports decision-term consistency, and provides a hard release mechanism for KYC and contract controls.

The highest-frequency activity, Register application, should **not** be the first automation priority. A1 is feasible and politically attractive, but it saves effort around an 8-minute activity with a 1.5-hour median pre-wait, while 72% of intake is already digital and back-office utilization is below 65% after 15:00. Automating it first would likely accelerate applications into KYC, document review, normalization, and credit analysis without reducing the exception loops or synchronization waits that dominate difficult cases.

A1 remains a valid later quick win once the bank has protected KYC, document quality, analyst capacity, and contract controls. A11 should remain deferred. A7 and A10 should be the next candidates only after the bank proves that external routing choices and KYC-controlled signing can safely improve the relevant variants.
