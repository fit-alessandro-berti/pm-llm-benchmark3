## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The March raw fitness collapse from 0.93 to 0.55 is largely a semantic and technical recording signal: mapped fitness is 0.90, recovering 0.35 fitness points in March. In later months the gap remains large. This supports the migration explanation, but does not quantify how much actual work changed, nor establish that every fitness difference is caused by the two corrections. Fitness depends on the model and alignment or replay method; a high score also does not guarantee appropriate behavior.

Mapping `Eligibility Screen` to `Validate Eligibility` can restore a match to the old model if the two events represent the same business step. It cannot establish that their execution, decision criteria, ordering or surrounding work are equivalent. Validate the mapping against system specifications, timestamps and case samples, including the minority still using the old label. Removing only confirmed duplicated `Open Case` events reduces artificial event repetitions and distorted variants and direct-follows relations; removing legitimate opens would hide real behavior. Missing resource identifiers principally weaken workload, handoff, team and individual analyses and may bias them because missingness is concentrated in migrated activities. They do not automatically invalidate activity-level conformance.

After normalization, `Assess -> Request Info` rises from 12 to 24 and then 29 occurrences per 100 cases in May and July, and its return-to-document relation rises similarly. Operational-table rework and cycle-time measures also worsen, while reconstructed assessment waiting increases much more than service time. These independent patterns are consistent with substantive rework and congestion, though changes in logging, case mix and cohort construction still require checking. Other monthly metrics were not normalized in the supplied series, so their definitions and provenance must be verified before treating them as fully independent evidence.

**DFG fragment distance.** For each supplied month, use the sum of absolute differences in the seven relation rates versus January, in occurrences per 100 cases. The distances are March 3, May 40, July 59, September 28, October 64 and December 36; divided by seven, the mean absolute differences are 0.43, 5.71, 8.43, 4.00, 9.14 and 5.14 respectively. This is a descriptive L1 distance over a selected fragment, not a probability-distribution distance: edges can occur repeatedly and their rates need not sum to 100. Omitted edges are unobserved, not zero; the score neither bounds nor represents the full-graph distance. The combined decision edge and changing senior-assessment recording further limit interpretation. October's larger distance includes an 18-per-100 increase in the senior edge, which is not necessarily a new routing practice.

**Cycle-time decomposition.** The aggregate median increase of 13.05 h by July, 16.65 h by October and 11.15 h by December cannot be arithmetically assigned to mechanisms: medians of components do not add, rework creates repeated service and waiting, and the same case can have multiple drivers. Qualitatively:

- **Case mix:** the complexity and supplier shares rise, especially in October. Standardize cycle-time distributions to baseline supplier, complexity, region and priority weights to estimate the composition contribution; complexity is also correlated with supplier.
- **Control flow/rework:** more information-request loops plausibly add document turnaround, repeat assessment and repeat queue visits. Estimate total elapsed time and visit counts for comparable cases, recognizing that rework is a mediator of supplier quality and may itself be affected by complexity.
- **Service:** recorded median assessment service time increases only about 0.6 h from baseline to July–August. It is a relatively small signal per recorded assessment, not a bound on total service contribution: visit counts, service distributions, other activities and missing timestamps matter.
- **Queueing:** reconstructed pre-assessment waits increase substantially and the p90 grows faster in absolute hours than the median. Queueing is a strong candidate for much of the deterioration, but no numerical share is identifiable from the summaries. Rework, capacity, mix and prioritization all affect the queue.

Candidate change points are hypotheses at the available monthly resolution, not formal detections:

- **Logging:** March, with label replacement, duplicate opens and missingness; April, as a candidate continuation or further change because raw fitness falls again and missingness rises. April may simply be noise or maturation of the March migration, so it needs event-level testing.
- **Behavior:** May, when normalized rework edges rise sharply; October, when senior-assessment rates jump and rework rises again. The latter may reflect mix or recording. September is a candidate partial reversal.
- **Performance:** May, when median and p90 jump; June or July, when waiting and cycle time continue to worsen around the staffing loss and priority change. October is a further candidate deterioration; December a candidate improvement.

These asynchronous signals and known interventions favor multiple mechanism-specific changes, possibly with gradual transitions and reversals. Monthly aggregation cannot date mid-month events precisely or rule out coincident changes; a single global breakpoint would obscure the different mechanisms.

## 6. Segmentation plan

Use case-start cohorts for full journeys, and separately monitor event-time queues. Define support on independent cases, not event counts; retain unknown values explicitly. The rules below are starting guardrails, not guarantees of statistical power. Pool adjacent periods or related categories when counts are low, and suppress unstable cells rather than treating them as zero.

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary population | All eligible cases by case-start month; retain open cases and unknown categories | Detect total business impact and shifting composition. | All cases; report denominator and completion/censoring. | Baseline, adjacent periods and same season across years. | Completed-only reporting selects faster cases; open cases require survival methods. |
| Standardized broad strata | Pre-existing complexity, supplier Z/other/unknown, and period | Separate composition from within-stratum changes. | At least 50 cases per compared stratum per window; pool months if needed. | Fixed baseline-weight mix and within-stratum trends. | Complexity coding may change; supplier selection is non-random. |
| Priority and capacity | Priority class, team at intake or queue entry, pre/post policy and staffing | Reveal delay redistribution and capacity effects. | At least 50 cases and 20 observed assessment starts per class/team/window; aggregate teams or periods otherwise. | Class-specific wait quantiles and affected versus comparison teams. | Priority and team assignment may be responses to difficulty or backlog; missing teams need a separate category. |
| Targeted diagnostic drill-down | Region, supplier, complexity and major variant, only for flagged strata | Localize supplier or route changes. | At least 30 cases per cell and 10 relevant events; pool or report descriptively if lower. | Matched regional and variant comparisons. | Selecting on a post-outcome variant or rework can induce bias; use for diagnosis, not total-effect estimation. |
| Logging quality | Source system, activity label, migration cohort, resource-known status | Verify schema, timestamp and resource changes. | At least 100 relevant events per window, plus case counts; pool rare labels. | Raw/mapped and known/unknown comparisons. | Resource-known status is an observation mechanism, not an independent causal group. |

The primary monitoring population should be **all eligible cases grouped by case-start month**, with completed and still-open cases represented. It captures the effect experienced by the actual incoming population without silently excluding complex or delayed cases. Accompany it with fixed-weight strata and event-time queue monitors: a case cohort alone detects delays late, and comparisons of completed cases alone suffer right-censoring bias.

# Part B: reusable event-level plan (requires pm4py). Not executed without a log.
import pm4py

CASE = "case:concept:name"
ACTIVITY = "concept:name"
TIME = "time:timestamp"


def format_events(events, case_col, activity_col, time_col):
    """Return a new PM4Py-formatted DataFrame; timestamps are interpreted as UTC."""
    frame = events.copy(deep=True)
    if frame[[case_col, activity_col, time_col]].isna().any().any():
        raise ValueError("Case ID, activity and timestamp must be present for every event")
    frame[time_col] = pd.to_datetime(frame[time_col], utc=True, errors="raise")
    # Preserve an input-order tie breaker for equal timestamps.
    frame["_input_order"] = np.arange(len(frame))
    frame = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        frame, case_id=case_col, activity_key=activity_col, timestamp_key=time_col
    )
    return frame.sort_values([CASE, TIME, "_input_order"], kind="stable").reset_index(drop=True)


def normalize_events(formatted, label_map, duplicate_mask=None):
    """Copy and map labels; remove only independently confirmed technical duplicates.

    duplicate_mask must be a boolean Series indexed exactly like formatted. If absent,
    no events are removed; do not infer duplicates merely from the Open Case label.
    """
    result = formatted.copy(deep=True)
    result[ACTIVITY] = result[ACTIVITY].replace(label_map)
    if duplicate_mask is not None:
        if not duplicate_mask.index.equals(result.index) or duplicate_mask.isna().any():
            raise ValueError("Duplicate mask must align exactly and contain no missing values")
        result = result.loc[~duplicate_mask.astype(bool)].copy()
    return result.reset_index(drop=True)


def event_window(formatted, start, end):
    """PM4Py event-time slice; may contain partial traces at window boundaries.

    start/end use PM4Py's accepted timestamp strings, e.g. '2026-03-01 00:00:00'.
    For a half-open window, filter out events at the end boundary explicitly.
    """
    start_ts, end_ts = pd.to_datetime(start, utc=True), pd.to_datetime(end, utc=True)
    if start_ts >= end_ts:
        raise ValueError("start must precede end")
    sliced = pm4py.filter_time_range(
        formatted.copy(deep=True), start, end, mode="events", timestamp_key=TIME
    )
    return sliced.loc[(sliced[TIME] >= start_ts) & (sliced[TIME] < end_ts)].copy()


def case_start_cohort(formatted, start, end):
    """Full observed traces for cases starting in [start, end); may be right-censored."""
    start_ts, end_ts = pd.to_datetime(start, utc=True), pd.to_datetime(end, utc=True)
    if start_ts >= end_ts:
        raise ValueError("start must precede end")
    starts = formatted.groupby(CASE)[TIME].min()
    case_ids = starts.index[(starts >= start_ts) & (starts < end_ts)]
    return formatted.loc[formatted[CASE].isin(case_ids)].copy()


def monthly_artifacts(formatted, months, cohort=True, optional_models=False):
    """months is an iterable of YYYY-MM strings. Use full case cohorts by default."""
    output = {}
    for month in months:
        start = pd.Timestamp(month, tz="UTC")
        end = start + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1)
        # PM4Py event filter uses timestamp strings; cohort mode retains full traces.
        start_s = start.strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S")
        end_s = end.strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S")
        data = (case_start_cohort(formatted, start_s, end_s) if cohort
                else event_window(formatted, start_s, end_s))
        if data.empty:
            output[month] = None
            continue
        dfg, start_activities, end_activities = pm4py.discover_dfg(data)
        artifacts = {
            "cases": data[CASE].nunique(),
            "dfg": dfg,
            "start_activities": start_activities,
            "end_activities": end_activities,
            "variants": pm4py.get_variants(data),
            "temporal_profile": pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(data),
        }
        if optional_models:
            artifacts["log_skeleton"] = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(data)
            artifacts["declare"] = pm4py.discover_declare(data)
        output[month] = artifacts
    return output


def compare_raw_normalized(events, case_col, activity_col, time_col,
                           months, duplicate_mask=None):
    """Return both artifacts and both logs; original input is never overwritten.

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest conclusion is that the abrupt March raw-fitness collapse is predominantly a logging and semantic change associated with migration: mapping and confirmed technical-event removal recover 0.35 fitness points in March, while normalized request-loop rates remain at January levels. Confidence is high in the recording discontinuity and moderate that it accounts for most of the raw-fitness drop. The mapping does not prove the two eligibility activities have identical semantics, and the modest mapped-fitness decline leaves room for real change or model mismatch. Missing resource identifiers are a separate, nonrandom measurement problem and weaken organizational analyses.

A real increase in rework from May is strongly supported by both normalized DFG relations and the operational rework series. Supplier Z's incomplete bundles are a plausible major contributor, with moderate confidence: their disruption coincides with a sharp increase in Z's share and rework, and both subsequently ease. Yet Z serves a non-random population, complexity also rises, and monthly aggregates do not show whether comparable Z cases changed more than comparable non-Z cases. Rework can mediate the supplier effect on waiting and cycle time, so assigning those effects separately would double-count.

Capacity loss is a plausible contributor to deteriorating assessment queues and the growing tail after mid-June, with moderate confidence. The relative stability of per-assessment service time supports queue pressure but does not establish its cause or size. Increased arrivals and repeated assessments also load the same capacity. The July priority rule plausibly redistributed delay between classes; its direction and magnitude cannot be inferred from the aggregate, so confidence in an actual redistribution here is low.

October–November's high volume and complexity fit the known renewal pattern and plausibly amplified queueing and senior routing. Confidence in a seasonal contribution is moderate, but a single year's observations cannot establish recurring drift in each metric, and historically inconsistent senior logging prevents a clean routing inference. December's improvement is consistent with lower seasonal load, overtime and the quality campaign, with low confidence in their separate contributions. Persistent elevated rework may reflect backlog or adaptation.

I would revise this account if validated old/new event semantics, case-level supplier comparisons with credible pre-trends, class-specific queue histories and capacity records, or several prior renewal seasons contradicted these signatures. Completion and censoring checks, along with audits of senior and resource recording, are essential before interpreting those comparisons.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF a new label or technical duplicate coincides with a sharp raw-fitness decline while validated mapped fitness remains stable, THEN audit the schema, mapping and source events, BECAUSE a semantic or ingestion change can create apparent conformance drift.
2. IF normalized request–collect loops rise within comparable Supplier Z cases more than in comparable other-supplier cases, THEN investigate bundle quality and supplier-specific intake, BECAUSE incomplete documents can generate rework.
3. IF pre-assessment queue age and p90 waiting rise while comparable per-visit service times remain stable and capacity falls, THEN review staffing, arrivals and backlog, BECAUSE constrained capacity can accumulate waiting without a large service-time change.
4. IF high-priority waiting improves relative to standard-case waiting after the policy change, THEN inspect queue order and standard-case service levels, BECAUSE priority can redistribute delay across classes.
5. IF aggregate delays rise during renewal season but fixed-mix within-stratum distributions remain stable, THEN update capacity forecasts and compare with prior renewal seasons, BECAUSE recurring population shifts can move aggregate metrics.
6. IF normalized rework stays elevated within comparable strata after supplier recovery and backlog clearance, THEN audit current practices and policies, BECAUSE sustained within-stratum changes may indicate behavioral adaptation.
7. IF resource missingness increases selectively by source or activity, THEN repair provenance and report unknown-resource cases alongside sensitivity analyses, BECAUSE nonrandom missingness can distort team and workload conclusions.
