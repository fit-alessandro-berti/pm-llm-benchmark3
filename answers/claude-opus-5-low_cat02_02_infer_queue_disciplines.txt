# 1. Queue-by-queue diagnosis

Notation used throughout: a *decision opportunity* is a time `t` at which a resource becomes free (or a running job is interrupted) and at least one job is waiting and eligible. The *eligibility set* at `t` = jobs with `arrival <= t` (scan-delay caveat below), not yet completed, not in service elsewhere, whose `skill_required` is covered by the deciding resource, and whose appointment (if any) does not forbid an early start. Waiting time is measured from the recorded arrival scan, which may lag true arrival by up to 2 minutes; this only ever makes a job look *younger* than it was, so FIFO violations near a 2-minute margin are not trustworthy.

| queue | primary_discipline | secondary_or_hybrid_component | confidence_0_to_100 | strongest_supporting_episode | strongest_contradicting_episode | main_identifiability_problem |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| QA | FIFO on arrival order | Hard appointment overlay (appointment time acts as a release date and as a jump-the-queue token); the `priority` field appears inert | 78 | 09:32 → A07 (est 9, p3) selected ahead of A08 (p1, est 25) that had waited since 08:40: pure arrival order, priority ignored | 08:20 → A03 selected although A02 (older) and A04 (p1) were waiting; only the appointment explains it | Every non-appointment start also happens to be the oldest job, so FIFO, "aging", and "priority never triggered because dispatch ignores it" are observationally equivalent; a single 08:58 start (A04, p1 and est-shortest) is simultaneously predicted by FIFO, priority and SPT |
| QB | FIFO backbone with **preemptive-resume** service | Escalation channel for flagged-urgent jobs (B02) implemented with ~one-decision latency, plus a "resume the interrupted job first" rule; appointments explicitly *not* honoured | 55 | 09:16 → running B01 (p3) is preempted mid-service the moment B02 (p1) arrives, and B01 later resumes rather than restarts (14+17+14 = 45 = est) | 10:00 → B05 (p1, arrived 09:46) is selected ahead of B04 (p3, arrived 09:35), which FIFO forbids; yet 12 minutes earlier at 09:48 the same B05 was passed over for B03 | The queue shows one clear priority-1 preemption and one clear priority-1 non-preemption; with n=2 urgent jobs we cannot separate "strict preemptive priority with a data gap" from "FIFO plus discretionary dispatcher escalation" |

| QD | Skill-based routing (hard feasibility filter) | FIFO within each resource's eligible set + a hard appointment (D-X idles 10 minutes to honour D06 at 11:30); `priority` again inert | 80 | 11:48 → D-X takes D07 (p3, est 28, arrived 11:14) ahead of D10 (p1, est 12, arrived 11:25): only FIFO-within-eligible-set survives | 11:25 → D-M takes D04 (p3, est 10) while D08 (p1, est 8, arrived 11:16) waits; consistent with FIFO but a flat contradiction of any priority claim | Skill constraints alone explain most of the sequence (D05 waits for D-F's meeting to end, D07/D10 need the mech+elec resource), so the *within-queue* rule is only tested at the four instants where two jobs were simultaneously feasible for the same resource |

Confidence rationale: QD and QA are high because several decisions have two or more jobs feasible for the same resource and the competing rules disagree. QB is low because its two informative episodes point in opposite directions. QC is intermediate: the SPT signal is strong at three instants but two starts openly violate it and the alternative (EDD/priority + escalation) is not refuted.

Three of six starts are uniquely predicted by SPT and by nothing else (10:10, 10:18, 11:34). The 10:54 start is where all urgency rules converge — it is *uninformative* for separating priority, EDD and slack, but it is informative against pure SPT. The 11:40 start is the aging signal: C01 had waited 100 minutes, and serving it first knowingly costs C07 its 12:00 deadline (C07 completes 12:34), which no deadline-driven rule would choose. 10:32 (C04 over C06) is the one start no clean rule predicts.

Minimal reconstruction script (self-contained; parses the tables above and replays the eligibility sets):

```python
import pandas as pd

QC = pd.DataFrame([
    ("C01","10:00","13:00",3,36,"11:40","12:16"), ("C02","10:03","14:00",3, 8,"10:10","10:18"),
    ("C03","10:05","12:30",2,14,"10:18","10:32"), ("C04","10:07","14:30",3,22,"10:32","10:54"),
    ("C05","10:11","10:50",1,40,"10:54","11:34"), ("C06","10:25","15:00",3, 6,"11:34","11:40"),
    ("C07","10:40","12:00",2,18,"12:16","12:34"),
], columns=["job","arrival","deadline","prio","est","start","end"])
for c in ["arrival","deadline","start","end"]:
    QC[c] = pd.to_datetime("2024-01-01 " + QC[c])

def predict(t):
    e = QC[(QC.arrival <= t) & (QC.start >= t)]          # waiting & eligible at t
    return dict(observed=QC.loc[QC.start == t, "job"].tolist(),
                fifo=e.loc[e.arrival.idxmin(), "job"], spt=e.loc[e.est.idxmin(), "job"],
                prio=e.loc[e.prio.idxmin(), "job"], edd=e.loc[e.deadline.idxmin(), "job"])

for t in sorted(QC.start.unique()):
    print(pd.Timestamp(t).strftime("%H:%M"), predict(pd.Timestamp(t)))
```
The same replay applied to QA and QB (identical structure, `est` in place of `deadline`) reproduces the two tables above; for QD the eligibility filter must additionally intersect `skill_required` with the resource's skill set and its availability window.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

**QA — diagnosis: FIFO + appointment release/override.**
Supporting: A01 (immediate, arrival order); A02 at 08:32 (oldest, chosen over a p1 and over shorter jobs); A05 at 09:05 (oldest, chosen over a p2 with a pending appointment and over a shorter job); A07 at 09:32 (oldest, chosen over a p1); A03 and A06 as appointment starts. A04 at 08:58 supports FIFO but is *not* discriminating.
Contradicting: A04 and A08 are priority-1 jobs with 45- and 86-minute waits. These do not require an undocumented exception under the FIFO diagnosis — they only contradict management's claim that priority is used. The genuinely awkward point is A03: served at exactly its appointment time although it arrived early, i.e. it was *held back* until 08:20 and then jumped ahead of A02. That is not FIFO and needs the appointment overlay. Secondary anomaly: the 08:30–08:32 idle gap while four jobs waited — 2 minutes, best explained as handover/administration, not policy.

**QB — diagnosis: FIFO + preemptive-resume + discretionary urgency escalation.**
Supporting: the B01/B02 preemption and the exact resumption arithmetic (14+17+14 = 45 min = est, so no restart penalty and no lost work); B01 resumed at 09:31 and again at 10:14 ahead of younger jobs; B04 at 10:28 (oldest) over B06 (higher priority) and B07 (shorter, appointment due).
Contradicting: B05 at 10:00 ahead of the older B04 — this cannot be explained by any known constraint (both need only the general skill, B1 was free, no availability exception) and requires either a real priority rule or an undocumented manual escalation. B03 at 09:48 ahead of B05 (p1) is the mirror image. B07's missed 10:30 appointment contradicts any appointment discipline; it is not a data artefact, since the note explicitly says the appointment was not honoured. B01's second interruption is annotated as a dispatcher action, so it is evidence about *humans*, not about a discipline.

**QC — diagnosis: SPT + SLA/aging escalation.**
Supporting: C02, C03, C06 are each the shortest eligible job at their start; C01 (longest, oldest) executed last among the initial backlog — the classic SPT starvation signature, explicitly noted in the data.
Contradicting: (i) C04 at 10:32 over C06 (22 vs 6). C06's scan is 10:25, and the ≤2-minute scan delay cannot push visibility past 10:32, so this is a real violation unless the dispatcher had committed to C04 earlier (batched review) or the *live* estimate for C06 differed from the recorded `est_min`. (ii) C05 at 10:54 — explainable by the escalation rule (its 10:50 deadline was already breached, and the note flags exactly this). (iii) C01 at 11:40 over C07 — explainable by aging, but it *creates* a deadline violation for C07 (12:34 vs 12:00), which means the escalation trigger is elapsed waiting time, not slack. If instead the trigger were slack, C07 should have gone first. So QC needs two distinct override rules, or one override plus a manual decision.

**QD — diagnosis: skill routing + FIFO-within-eligible + hard appointment.**
Supporting: D05 waits 73 minutes purely because D-F is the only fully qualified resource and is in a meeting until 12:20 — a pure skill/availability explanation with no policy content; D07 and D10 wait for D-X, the only mech+elec / free-electrical resource; D-X's deliberate 11:20–11:30 idle for D06; D04-before-D08 and D07-before-D10 as FIFO confirmations.
Contradicting: D08 (p1) waits 19 minutes while a shorter-than-nothing p3 job runs; D10 (p1) waits 51 minutes. Both are real contradictions of a priority claim, and neither is rescued by skill or availability: at 11:25 D-M could have taken D08, and at 11:48 D-X could have taken D10. These are *not* undocumented exceptions to my diagnosis — they are the evidence for it.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

**Test 1 — QA at 08:58 (FIFO vs. priority vs. SPT; all three predict A04).**
Competing explanations: (a) FIFO with an appointment overlay; (b) non-preemptive priority. Both fit 08:58. They diverge at 09:20: under (b) the free server should take A08 (p1, waiting since 08:40) and treat A06's overdue appointment as a lower claim; under (a) A06's appointment fires and A08 waits. Observed: A06 — so the historical data already discriminates, and (b) survives only if appointments outrank priority. The residual ambiguity is at 09:05 (A05 vs A06 vs A07). *Additional observable that discriminates:* the dispatch-decision timestamp and the eligibility set actually shown to A1 at 08:58 and 09:05. If A04's priority-1 flag was set at 08:13 and visible in the 08:32 decision set, priority is refuted outright; if the flag was assigned late (say, at 08:55 after a re-triage), then 08:58 becomes a genuine priority start and the queue is a FIFO/priority hybrid with late escalation. A priority-change audit trail settles it.

**Test 2 — QC at 10:54 (SPT-with-escalation vs. EDD/priority throughout).**
Competing explanations: (a) SPT with a deadline-breach override; (b) EDD (or priority, which is collinear with the deadlines here) all along, with 10:10/10:18/11:34 being coincidences. They already disagree at 10:10 (a→C02, b→C03) and at 11:34 (a→C06, b→C07), and observation favours (a). The sharper discrimination is *forward*: give the queue one more job arriving at 12:00 with est 5 and deadline 16:00 while C07 is running. (a) predicts it is served immediately after C07 ahead of any longer waiting job; (b) predicts it is served last. *Additional observable:* the estimate actually used at decision time. If a `current_estimated_remaining_time` field exists and C06's live estimate at 10:32 was, say, 25 rather than 6, the single SPT violation disappears and confidence in (a) jumps from ~68 to ~85. Conversely, if the estimate was stable at 6, (a) needs a second override (batched review or manual choice) and confidence falls.

**Test 3 — QB at 10:00 (FIFO+escalation-with-latency vs. strict preemptive priority).**
Both explain B05 at 10:00. They disagree about 09:46: under strict preemptive priority, B05's arrival should have preempted the running B01 immediately (as B02's did at 09:16); under FIFO+escalation, B01 keeps the server and B05 merely gets promoted in the *next* selection. Observed: no preemption at 09:46, and B01 ran until an annotated manual reassignment at 09:48 — that favours the FIFO+escalation reading, but the 2-minute margin is exactly the size of the timestamp/scan tolerance, so it is not decisive. *Additional observable:* the preemption trigger record — whether a preemption is raised by an arrival event (priority rule) or by a dispatcher action (discretion). The existing annotations ("urgent arrival" vs. "dispatcher reassignment") already hint that QB has two different mechanisms; a formal `preemption_reason` code with an actor field would confirm it.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked most to least plausible.

1. **`priority` is a stored attribute that dispatch does not read (stale or decorative).** Very plausible: in QA and QD, priority-1 jobs are passed over at instants where they were fully eligible, repeatedly, on different resources. Effect on diagnosis: this is the *assumed* explanation behind the QA/QD diagnoses; if instead the field were live, both queues would have to be reclassified as "policy violated in practice", which is a management finding rather than a discipline.
2. **Appointments are windows with tolerance, not exact times, and the tolerance differs per queue.** Plausible: A03 starts exactly on time after arriving early (early start suppressed), A06 starts 5 minutes late (running job not preempted), D06 starts exactly on time with the server idling, B07 starts 26 minutes late. Effect: QA and QD become "FIFO/skill + hard appointment"; QB becomes "appointment recorded but not enforced", which is itself a compliance finding. If the tolerance were formally ±30 minutes, B07 would no longer be a violation and QB's appointment component would vanish rather than fail.
3. **Estimates are noisy or strategically inflated/deflated.** Plausible and directly consequential for QC, the only queue diagnosed from `est_min`. Every QC job's realized duration equals its estimate exactly, which is *suspiciously* clean and suggests the "realized" durations were back-filled from estimates; if so, an SPT diagnosis built on those numbers is partly circular. Effect: lowers QC confidence toward 55 and raises the standing of EDD/priority.
4. **Manual dispatcher override (documented once, likely present elsewhere).** The 09:48 "dispatcher reassignment" in QB proves overrides happen and are only sometimes annotated; the prompt warns that a missing interruption reason does not prove absence of a reason. Effect: QB's two contradictory episodes may both be human decisions on top of a plain FIFO queue — which would raise QB's FIFO confidence but make its "priority" component unidentifiable from this data.
5. **Batched/periodic dispatch review rather than event-driven dispatch.** If the queue is re-sorted only every N minutes, a job scanned at 10:25 may not enter the sorted list used for the 10:32 decision. This single assumption removes QC's only unexplained violation (C04 over C06). Effect: QC becomes near-clean SPT + escalation, confidence ~80. It also explains the small idle gaps (QA 08:30–08:32).
6. **Data-quality: arrival scan lag (≤2 min) and 1-minute rounding.** Affects only near-ties. Concretely it could flip D08 vs D09 (11:16 vs 11:20 — safe margin) and B04 vs B05 (09:35 vs 09:46 — safe), so it rescues none of the substantive violations. Effect: negligible on diagnoses, but it means no rule should be rejected on evidence with margins under ~3 minutes.
7. **Setup/handover or travel time between jobs, and preemption/resumption cost.** QA's 08:30–08:32 gap and QB's 09:00→09:02 start suggest ~2 minutes of fixed overhead. QB shows *zero* resumption penalty (segments sum exactly to est), which is either genuine (fully interruptible work) or an artefact of estimate-derived durations. Effect: if resumption were costly, the twice-preempted B01 would be evidence of a badly tuned policy rather than of a preemptive discipline.
8. **Policy switching under SLA stress.** QC's 10:54 and 11:40 starts are consistent with a documented "escalate when deadline breached / waiting > 90 min" clause. Effect: converts QC from "SPT with anomalies" to "documented hybrid", which is the benign reading; the dangerous alternative is that a human noticed C05 and intervened once, in which case the escalation is not a policy at all.

# 6. Additional event data needed

Prioritized by how much each field would move the diagnoses above.

1. **Dispatch-decision timestamp + the eligibility set as the dispatcher saw it** (highest value, all four queues). This is the single field that converts "start order" into "decision evidence" and would immediately settle QC's 10:32 anomaly (was C06 in the list?) and QA's 08:58 ambiguity.
2. **Live `estimated_remaining_time` at each decision, with a revision history** (QC critical, QB useful for the preemption arithmetic). Without it, SPT for QC is inferred from a number that may have been written after the fact.
3. **Priority assignment/change log with actor and timestamp** (QA, QB, QD). Determines whether A04/A08/D08/D10 were priority-1 *at decision time*. This is the make-or-break test for the "priority field is inert" hypothesis, which currently carries QA and QD.
4. **Preemption reason coded as a controlled value, with actor (system vs. dispatcher) and the triggering job** (QB). Separates automatic priority preemption from manual reassignment — precisely the QB 09:16 vs 09:48 distinction.
5. **Appointment tolerance / window definition per queue, plus whether early start is permitted** (QA, QB, QD). A03 (no early start), A06 (+5), D06 (server idles), B07 (+26) currently force me to infer four different tolerances from four observations.
6. **Resource state timeline: idle / blocked / on-break / calibrating, with reason** (QD, QA). QD's diagnosis leans on availability windows given in prose; as event data these become verifiable, and D-X's 11:20–11:30 idle becomes an *observed* idle-to-honour-appointment rather than an inference.
7. **Queue-entry time distinct from arrival scan** (all queues). Makes the ≤2-minute lag explicit instead of a blanket caveat, and lets near-tie FIFO tests (D08/D09) be decided rather than waived.
8. **Skill/qualification matrix as versioned data, including partial qualification** (QD). D05's "only fully qualified resource" is currently prose; if D-X could have done part of D05, the skill explanation for its 73-minute wait weakens.
9. **Policy version / configuration identifier per decision, and any SLA-escalation flag raised** (QC above all). Would distinguish a genuine documented escalation clause from a one-off human rescue of C05 — the difference between "hybrid policy" and "no policy".
10. **Deadline/SLA definitions and whether breaches are recorded as events** (QC). Needed to test the aging-vs-slack question raised by C01 over C07.
11. **Batch/review-cycle configuration** (QC, QA). Confirms or kills exception #5 above at near-zero cost.

# 7. Final synthesis

**Most identifiable: QD.** Its skill matrix and availability windows are hard constraints that eliminate most of the hypothesis space, and the residual choices are decided at four clean instants where FIFO, priority and SPT disagree — FIFO wins twice outright (D04 over D08, D07 over D10) and the D-X idle at 11:20–11:30 is costly, deliberate, and interpretable only as a hard appointment. QA is a close second for the same structural reason: a single resource, dense contention, and repeated instants where the oldest job wins against both shorter and higher-priority rivals.

**Least identifiable: QB.** It has the smallest number of discriminating instants, its two priority-1 jobs are treated in opposite ways, and its one clearly annotated switch is a manual reassignment. Any label above ~55 confidence would be rhetoric: "FIFO with preemptive-resume and occasional escalation" and "preemptive priority with one human exception" both fit, and only a preemption-trigger log separates them.

**Most dangerous claim to infer from these observations alone: priority scheduling (in any queue), and specifically the temptation to read QC's C05 start as evidence of it.** Priority, earliest-deadline and "urgent" are collinear in QC — C05 is simultaneously priority-1, earliest-deadline and deadline-breached — so a single observation is being asked to certify a rule, while the queues where priority *is* testable (QA, QD) refute it four times. The corresponding operational risk is real rather than academic: if management believes priority is enforced, they will keep flagging jobs as priority-1 (A04, A08, D08, D10) and keep receiving FIFO service, with the flag providing false assurance. The second-most dangerous inference is "appointment scheduling" as a queue-level label: appointments are enforced in QA and QD, and recorded but ignored in QB, so the same field means different things per queue. The safest defensible cross-queue statement is: **QA = FIFO + appointments; QB = FIFO + preemptive-resume + discretionary escalation; QC = SPT with SLA/aging overrides; QD = skill routing + FIFO within eligible set + hard appointments; and the `priority` attribute is, on this evidence, non-operational everywhere except possibly QB.**
