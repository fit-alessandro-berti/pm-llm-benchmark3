# 1. Assumptions and notation

- **A1. Rounding.** All timestamps were rounded to the nearest five minutes before extraction. I use the reported rounded timestamp as the nominal event time. For any reported time \(t\), the true time is treated as approximately \([t-2.5, t+2.5]\) minutes. A difference between two rounded timestamps therefore has an uncertainty band of about \(\pm 5\) minutes.
- **A2. Simultaneous-event convention.** When multiple events share the same rounded timestamp, I process service completions first, then queue arrivals, then service starts. This means a service that ends at 12:00 is available before a new case that is reported to arrive at 12:00, and a reported 12:00 arrival is not assumed to start service instantly at 12:00. This convention affects only the instantaneous tie point, not the integrated queue lengths over time.
- **A3. Capacity and availability.** S1 has two single-task resources, I1 and I2. S2 has C1 and C2. C2 was unavailable from 10:55 through 11:10, so S2 available capacity over the 360-minute window is \(2\times 360 - 15 = 705\) service-minutes rather than 720. I use nominal capacity 720 for S1.
- **A4. Semantics of MISSING, OPEN, and blank.**  
  - `MISSING` start means the activity occurred and the completion/resource are known, but the start was not recorded.  
  - `OPEN` means the activity started but had not completed by 14:00.  
  - Blank S1 start/end means the case was waiting for S1 at 14:00.  
  - Blank S2 start/end with completed S1 means the case was waiting for S2 at 14:00.  
  These three cases are never treated as the same state.
- **A5. Censoring.** For open activities, I use 14:00 as a provisional censoring time for elapsed-service and WIP calculations, but I do not treat 14:00 as the true completion time. For waiting cases at 14:00, the reported wait at 14:00 is a lower bound on the eventual wait.
- **A6. Missing-start imputation scenarios.** I use four explicit scenarios rather than a single arbitrary replacement.  
  - **Early:** missing starts are placed at the earliest feasible times consistent with resource non-overlap and nonnegative service; P11 is still constrained to start after P08 on C2.  
  - **Central:** uses a typical observed service-pattern choice: P07 S1 start = 09:30, P15 S1 start = 11:45, P05 S2 start = 09:45, P11 S2 start = 12:00.  
  - **Late:** missing starts are placed as late as possible while retaining at least 5 minutes of service: P07 = 09:55, P15 = 12:10, P05 = 10:10, P11 = 12:20.  
  - **Conflict:** same as central except P11 S2 start = 11:10, which creates an apparent overlap with P08 and is examined as a deliberate data-integrity scenario.
- **A7. No bypass and no fabricated scheduling.** No case intentionally bypasses either station. I do not invent starts for blank/waiting cases. When a resource appears idle while a queue is nonempty, I leave that as an observed idle/unobserved-unavailability fact; I do not force a greedy service start.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

Let the following be defined for a case at a station:

- \(A\) = time the case arrives/enters the station queue.  
  For S1, \(A=\text{arrival\_s1}\). For S2, \(A=\text{s1\_end}\).
- \(B\) = service start time.
- \(E\) = service completion time.
- For open service, I use the censoring time \(T_c=14:00\).

Then:

- **Waiting time:**  
  \[
  Q = B - A
  \]
  if the service has started. If the case is still waiting at 14:00, the quantity \(T_c - A\) is a lower bound.
- **Active service time:**  
  \[
  S = E - B
  \]
  for completed service, and \(T_c - B\) is the elapsed service for an open service at 14:00.
- **Station sojourn time:**  
  \[
  J = E - A
  \]
  for completed service, and \(T_c - A\) is the lower-bound sojourn for open service.
- **Case-level total sojourn for a completed case:**  
  \[
  \text{Total} = \text{s2\_end} - \text{arrival\_s1}
  \]
  because S1 must finish before S2 begins.
- **Queue length and WIP over time.** I build an event stream for each station:
  - queue arrival events,
  - service start events,
  - service end events, with 14:00 used only as a provisional end for open activities.
  
  Let \(Q(t)\) be the queue length, \(N(t)\) the number in active service, and \(L(t)=Q(t)+N(t)\) the total WIP at the station. I report:
  \[
  \bar Q = \frac{1}{360}\int_0^{360} Q(t)\,dt,\quad
  \bar L = \frac{1}{360}\int_0^{360} L(t)\,dt,
  \]
  plus peak values.
- **Rates.** For a time period \(P\), arrival rate is the number of arrivals into that station during \(P\) divided by the period length; completion rate is the number of completed services during \(P\) divided by the period length.
- **Busy time and utilization.** Busy time is the total service time performed by resources within the 08:00–14:00 window, including elapsed open-service time up to 14:00. Utilization is busy time divided by available capacity: 720 minutes for S1 and 705 minutes for S2.
- **Little’s-law check.** For a stable subsystem, \(\bar L \approx \lambda \bar W\), where \(\lambda\) is the completion rate and \(\bar W\) is the mean sojourn time of completed cases. Because this window is finite and non-stationary, I use this only as a diagnostic. A positive gap \(\bar L - \lambda\bar W\) is expected when WIP is building over the window.
