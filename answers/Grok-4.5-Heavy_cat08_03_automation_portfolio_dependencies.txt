**2. Dependency and prerequisite map**

Upstream data quality dependencies dominate. A1 and A2 improve the starting data for classification and routing; classification errors already produce 8% rework and misroute cases onto incorrect control paths. A5 and A6 are the primary cleaners of financial artifacts: without them, incomplete documents and un-normalized statements flow into credit analysis and committee packaging, producing the observed 24% rework on financial review, 16% on normalization, and GIGO risk for A8.

Legal and compliance controls form hard prerequisites. KYC clearance (improved by A3 and especially A4) must precede customer signing; provisional signing already occurs in 6.5% of cases. Contract preparation and activation cannot safely proceed without decision-matched terms and complete controls. A9 and A12 directly encode and remediate these rules. A11 is explicitly downstream of every control gate and must not be activated until the gates are reliable.

Human escalation capacity is constrained. The three compliance specialists (with heavy dependency on only two seniors in the handover network) are the bottleneck for entity-mismatch resolution. Any A3/A4 design must preserve human-in-the-loop escalation with full audit trails; flooding them with low-quality escalations would lengthen the 31 h median wait. Credit analysts (9 FTE, central in the working-together network) require A6 protection: cleaner volume from A1/A5 without normalization capacity will overload judgment-heavy credit analysis and packaging.

External valuation is a pure synchronization dependency outside direct control. A7 can triage and route but cannot eliminate the 58 h median wait or partner variability; committee packaging already waits on valuation results when required (71% of equipment-finance cases).

Core-banking integration constraints limit the first six months to two deep integrations. A1, A9 and A11 are the most integration-heavy; A6 may require model-to-core hooks. A4, A5, A7, A3, A10 and A12 can be delivered as specialist assistants, portals, workbenches or lighter orchestration and therefore conserve deep-integration slots.

**3. Portfolio alternatives**

**Quick-win portfolio (A1 + A5 + A10 + A9)**  
Optimizes political visibility (highest-frequency registration), missing-document loops, customer-signing wait, and an explicit control strengthener. High feasibility and two deep integrations fit the first-six-month limit.  
Sacrifices the true critical-path waits (entity-mismatch 31 h, external valuation 58 h), specialist capacity protection, and analyst capacity protection. Accelerates volume into already constrained mid-process bottlenecks.

**Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio (A4 + A5 + A6 + A7)**  
Optimizes the longest waits, critical-path blockage (entity mismatch on critical path in 82% of cases when present), rework loops, scarce specialist and central-analyst capacity, and the external synchronization point that feeds committee packaging. Mostly lighter integrations.  
Sacrifices an explicit high-visibility control-rule module (although A4 improves KYC exception quality) and the pure high-frequency political win. External-partner dependency remains.

**Compliance-first portfolio (A3 + A4 + A9 + A12)**  
Optimizes KYC reliability, entity-resolution quality, decision-to-contract matching, remediation workbench, audit trails, and reduction of provisional-signing and four-eyes exposure. Strongly satisfies the “at least one control-strengthening module” mandate.  
Sacrifices the highest-volume rework loops (missing documents), financial-normalization capacity, and external-valuation synchronization. Throughput and cycle-time gains are slower.

**4. Expected process-level effects**

**Quick-win (A1+A5+A10+A9)**  
Cycle time and waiting time improve modestly on registration and signing (44 h) and on the financial-review ↔ request-missing loop (DFG 3300/2800). Rework falls on missing documents and contract remediation (already 8.5% overall, 14% for credit-line renewals). Handovers decline for back-office and relationship-manager reminder work. Resource demand shifts: back-office capacity (already <65% after 15:00) is further freed, but volume accelerates into KYC, entity-mismatch and credit-analyst queues; legal-ops month-end load is smoothed by A9 only if upstream data are clean. Control violations: contract-match improves, but provisional-signing risk and four-eyes pressure may rise if entity and KYC queues lengthen.

**Bottleneck/synchronization (A4+A5+A6+A7)**  
Cycle time and waiting time drop materially on the critical path: entity-mismatch (31 h, 82% critical-path presence), missing-document loops, normalization (20 h), and external valuation (58 h). Concurrent KYC/financial-review practice is better supported once entity and document quality improve. Rework falls on Resolve↔KYC loops (DFG 1750/1160), financial-review loops, and late valuations. Handovers decline for compliance specialists, back-office and collateral team. Resource demand: the three specialists and nine central analysts are protected and partially liberated; committee packaging receives cleaner, more timely inputs. Control violations: KYC clearance quality rises, reducing provisional-signing incidence; four-eyes pressure is indirectly eased by shorter queues, but no new automated four-eyes enforcement is added.

**Compliance-first (A3+A4+A9+A12)**  
Cycle time and waiting time improve mainly on entity-mismatch and the path from decision to clean contract. Rework falls on contract-remediation loops (DFG 650/510) and entity-mismatch loops. Handovers for remediation become structured. Resource demand: specialists and legal-ops are supported; analysts and missing-document volume are largely untouched, so overall throughput gains remain limited. Control violations: strongest reduction—provisional signing, decision-contract mismatch, and visibility of four-eyes breaches all improve; full audit trails of suggestion/override/decision are enforced.

**5. Risks and compensating controls**

- False negatives in KYC/entity handling (A3/A4): severe regulatory exposure. Compensate with mandatory human escalation thresholds, dual-review sampling, and immutable audit logs of model suggestion + override reason + final decision.  
- Model drift and PDF extraction failures (A6, A9 rule library): compensate with continuous monitoring of extraction confidence scores, human review queues for low-confidence cases, scheduled rule-library reviews, and parallel shadow-mode running before cut-over.  
- GIGO for any downstream assembly or generation (A8/A9/A11): enforce upstream completeness gates; do not enable auto-assembly or activation until KYC status, normalized statements and decision data pass automated checks.  
- Customer friction and RM resistance (A5/A10): design flexible checklists with RM override, measure friction scores, and keep e-sign optional with paper fallback.  
- Four-eyes violations (already 5.8% in high-exposure month-end cases): automation of analysis or packaging must embed segregation-of-duties checks; monitor same-person completions and route high-exposure cases to separate pools.  
- Specialist or analyst overload from shifted volume: pair any inflow automation with capacity-protecting modules (A4, A6); track utilization and queue ages daily.  
- External quality disputes (A7): retain human triage of valuer reports and clear escalation paths back to relationship managers.  
- Auditability: every candidate must emit an immutable trail of input data, model output, human action and final disposition; this is non-negotiable for compliance acceptance.

**6. Recommended phased rollout**

**Phase 0 (months 0–1): Foundation**  
Instrument additional process-mining metrics (entity wait, missing-loop rate, specialist utilization, four-eyes same-person rate, provisional-signing rate). Confirm core-banking integration capacity for at most two deep modules.

**Phase 1 (months 1–6, ≤2 deep integrations)**  
Deploy A5 (portal/checklist/nudging – lighter) and A4 (specialist assistant – medium). Optionally begin A6 extraction models if one deep slot remains.  
Monitoring metrics: median entity-mismatch waiting time, Resolve↔KYC loop frequency, missing-document repeat rate, specialist utilization and escalation volume, audit-trail completeness.  
Gate criteria to Phase 2: entity wait reduced ≥40%, missing-loop rate down ≥30%, no increase in control violations or provisional signing, specialist capacity stable or improved, RM friction scores acceptable.

**Phase 2 (months 6–12)**  
Complete A6 (normalization) and deploy A9 (contract generation + control-rule checks – likely the second deep integration).  
Monitoring metrics: end-to-end cycle time, normalization wait and service time, analyst utilization, contract-remediation rate, decision-contract match rate, four-eyes compliance, customer signing wait.  
Gate criteria for year-2 expansion: cycle-time reduction visible on critical path, analyst utilization not exceeding sustainable levels, contract remediation <5%, full auditability demonstrated, Compliance sign-off on control posture.

A7, A10, A3 and A12 become year-2 candidates once foundations are stable. A1 and A11 remain deferred until mid-process reliability is proven.

**7. Final recommendation**

Recommend the hybrid portfolio **A4 + A5 + A6 + A9**.

It combines the highest process-level leverage (critical-path entity mismatch, missing-document rework loops, financial-normalization capacity that protects the central analyst network) with an explicit control-strengthening module that satisfies Compliance, reduces contract-decision mismatch, and embeds auditable rule checks. The combination attacks the waits and rework that dominate end-to-end performance (31 h entity, 18–20 h document/normalize, 58 h external valuation indirectly unblocked by cleaner upstream) while protecting the scarcest internal resources (3 compliance specialists, 9 credit analysts). Deep-integration demand is manageable (primarily A9, possibly A6), leaving capacity inside the first-six-month limit.

The highest-frequency activity (Register / A1) should **not** be automated first. Process-mining evidence shows it is not rate-limiting: median wait is only 1.5 h, back-office utilization already falls below 65% after 15:00, and accelerating it merely exposes the true bottlenecks—entity-mismatch resolution on the critical path, document loops, normalization, and the thin specialist layer. Automating A1 in isolation would shift demand onto constrained mid-process resources without reducing cycle time or control risk. Frequency alone is therefore misleading; the recommended portfolio prioritizes dependency resolution, exception handling capacity, synchronization points and control integrity over raw volume.
