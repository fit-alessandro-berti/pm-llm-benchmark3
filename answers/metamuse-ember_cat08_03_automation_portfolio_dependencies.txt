## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

**Upstream data quality chain.** Receive  Register  Classify splits 7,250 to KYC check and 3,940 to Financial document review, but in practice KYC and Financial document review are often concurrent despite sequential system recording. Do not impose a false sequence between A3 and A5/A6. Classify errors (8%) misroute cases into the wrong KYC, collateral and committee path, so A2 cannot be trusted until application data quality is proven. A5 is a quality gate for A6: extraction/normalization performs poorly on low-quality PDFs, and RM-triggered incomplete instructions are a root cause of the 3,300/2,800 missing-document loop. A6 output is prerequisite for Credit analysis and for Committee packaging, which waits for KYC status, normalized statements, valuation if required, and credit notes - a synchronization point fed by DFG relations KYCCredit analysis (4,200), NormalizeCredit analysis (5,000), Credit analysisCommittee packaging (2,700) and Wait for external valuationCommittee packaging (1,980).

**KYC / mismatch loop.** KYC check  Resolve entity mismatch (1,750)  KYC check (1,160) is a rework loop; Resolve  Contract preparation (380) shows the shortcut that creates control risk when KYC is not cleared. A3 and A4 are complements: A3 triages routine hits, A4 resolves the residual mismatches that block KYC and signing. Neither works alone - A3 without A4 floods specialists with escalations; A4 without A3 leaves specialists handling volume that could have been pre-classified.

**Legal and compliance controls.** KYC clearance must precede Customer signing; provisional signing occurred in 6.5% of cases. Contract terms must match Committee decision (CommitteeContract 3,650; ContractRemediate 650/510). Four-eyes: Credit analysis and Committee packaging must not be by the same person for High-exposure SME, violated in 5.8% at month-end. Therefore A8, A9, A10 and especially A11 are downstream of A3/A4/A12: automating them before KYC, committee and four-eyes gates are hard-enforced would automate violations. All model-assisted steps (A2, A3, A6, A9) need an auditable trail of suggestion, human override and final decision.

**Human escalation capacity.** Compliance specialists are 3 FTE with high dependency on two seniors; Credit analysts are 9 FTE and central in the working-together network; Legal ops is 4 FTE, underutilized when KYC is slow but overloaded at month-end. A5+A6 shift analyst work from 75-min normalization to exception review and 110-min Credit analysis - clean inflow without rebalancing will overload analysts. A3 shifts work from routine KYC to judged escalations - without cross-training beyond the two seniors, the bottleneck simply moves. Conversely, Back office (12 FTE, <65% after 15:00) has releasable capacity for document-quality assistance if A1 is not the priority.

**External valuation dependency.** Credit analysis  Collateral valuation request (2,400)  Wait for external valuation (2,350, 58.0h wait, 19% late)  Committee packaging. Valuation is required in 71% of Equipment finance but only 18% of Working-capital, so segment mix matters. A7 triage/routing can prioritize and parallelize, but cannot control valuer turnaround or quality. The internal lever is earlier trigger - initiating valuation in parallel with credit work rather than after it - which requires no deep integration and should precede a formal A7 module.

**Core-banking integration constraints.** IT can deeply integrate only two modules in six months. A11 and A9 and A1 consume scarce deep-integration slots for low process-level gain. The recommended pair is: (1) A6 document/credit platform, and (2) one KYC platform integration shared by A3 and A4. A5 uses the existing customer portal with light integration. A12 workbench, A8, A9, A10, A11 should wait for post-month-6 integration capacity and for upstream quality gates.

## 3. Portfolio alternatives

**Quick-win portfolio: A1, A2, A5, A10**
*Optimizes:* visible touch-time reduction, feasibility, political momentum. High-frequency Register (11,880) and Classify (11,820) are ~98% variant-stable, and A5/A10 are portal-side.
*Sacrifices:* almost no reduction in dominant waits (58.0h valuation, 44.0h signing, 31.0h mismatch, 28.0h credit wait), no relief for analysts/specialists, and A10 without a KYC gate amplifies provisional signing. A1/A2 accelerate arrival into the constrained downstream - the classic frequency trap.

**Bottleneck / synchronization portfolio: A6, A5, A4, A7**
*Optimizes:* end-to-end cycle time, rework loops and committee synchronization - normalization, missing-document loop, mismatch critical path and valuation wait.
*Sacrifices:* lower headline labor saving, dependence on external valuers, customer-facing friction from A5, and no explicit false-negative control module. It delivers cycle time but would face a compliance objection as a first wave on its own.

**Compliance-first portfolio: A3, A12, A9, A4**
*Optimizes:* control violations, auditability and legal risk - KYC false negatives, provisional signing, contract-committee mismatch, remediation visibility.
*Sacrifices:* speed. Normalization and missing-document loops still feed poor artifacts to analysts and contracts; A9 increases contract throughput into month-end legal overload before upstream quality improves. Controls improve, but cycle time gains are slower and smaller.

## 4. Expected process-level effects

**Quick-win (A1, A2, A5, A10):** *Cycle/waiting* - marginal: Register wait is only 1.5h and Classify 2.0h, so savings are minutes against total waits dominated by valuation, signing and mismatch; A5 helps the document loop, A10 helps reminders but not the 44.0h signing wait which is largely customer/KYC-gated. *Rework* - 8% misclassification may rise if A2 is untested; duplicate false merges add rework. *Handovers* - fewer back-office touches, more downstream handovers as cases arrive faster to KYC, analysts and committee. *Resource demand* - back office freed where utilization is already <65% after 15:00; demand rises for 3 compliance specialists and 9 analysts. *Controls* - misclassification can place High-exposure cases on the wrong four-eyes/committee path; e-sign prompts before KYC clearance increase the 6.5% provisional-signing problem.

**Bottleneck/synchronization (A6, A5, A4, A7):** *Cycle/waiting* - largest gains: A6 cuts the 75-min normalization service and 20.0h wait before Credit analysis; A4 cuts the 31.0h mismatch wait on the critical path in 82% of affected cases; A7 triage targets the 58.0h valuation wait for the Equipment-finance subset feeding Committee packaging. *Rework* - A5 directly attacks the 3,3002,800 loop and 52% repeat rate and the 24% Financial-review rework; A6 reduces the 16% normalization rework feeding credit errors. *Handovers* - fewer document/specalist loops, but more explicit analyst-validation and valuer-routing handovers. *Resource demand* - shifts work from normalization volume to credit judgment and exception review (analyst overload risk); frees senior specialists from routine matches, concentrating their work on complex cases. *Controls* - cleaner KYC/statements reduce downstream contract and committee errors, but A7 routing can create valuer quality disputes and A5 friction needs RM co-design.

**Compliance-first (A3, A12, A9, A4):** *Cycle/waiting* - signing and contract waits may initially *rise* as provisional cases are correctly blocked - a desirable increase; mismatch wait falls if A4 works. *Rework* - ContractRemediate (650/510) and contract-rework 12% fall if A9 rules are current; detected remediation may rise initially as A12 surfaces hidden breaches. *Handovers* - more formal escalations to specialists and a remediation workbench handover. *Resource demand* - specialist and legal demand rises before it falls; month-end legal overload is a real risk. *Controls* - strongest improvement: KYC-before-signing enforced, contract-committee matching, four-eyes monitoring, audit trail - provided A9's rule library is versioned and owned by Legal, not IT.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

| Risk | Candidates | Compensating control |
|---|---|---|
| False negatives in KYC enabling signing/activation | A3, A10, A11 | Conservative thresholds, human escalation by design, hard system block: no signing/activation without KYC-cleared flag; manual sampling of auto-cleared hits |
| False positives / escalation flood | A3, A2, A1 | Threshold tuning in shadow mode; cross-train beyond 2 senior specialists; measure specialist queue and override rates by segment |
| Extraction/model errors propagate | A6, A8, A9 | Analyst validation with confidence flags; low-quality PDF routing to manual path; no auto-assembly of committee pack or contract from unvalidated artifacts |
| Model drift across products/segments | A2, A3, A6 | Drift monitoring by Micro/SME/High-exposure and Working-capital/Equipment/Renewal; versioned models and rule libraries; periodic revalidation |
| Automation bias / rubber-stamping | All | Mandatory override reason capture; QA sampling of accepted suggestions; audit trail of suggestion  override  final decision for every case |
| Four-eyes violation | A8, A6/A8 combined | Hard segregation in workflow: system prevents same user completing Credit analysis and Committee packaging on High-exposure cases; month-end surge staffing, not rule waiver |
| Outdated legal rules | A9 | Legal-owned, versioned rule library with change control; contract-committee three-way match check before signing |
| Customer friction / exclusion | A5, A10 | RM co-design of checklists (RM instructions cause many loops); assisted/non-digital route; identity/consent verification for e-sign; friction/complaint monitoring |
| Duplicate false merge | A1 | Human confirmation for uncertain duplicates; reversible merge |
| External valuer quality/lateness | A7 | Valuer scorecards, dual routing for late cases, early trigger in parallel with credit analysis; contracts with late-rate SLAs |

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

**Phase 1 - Foundations and input quality (Months 0-2, light integration only).** Baseline all metrics by segment/product from process-mining artifacts. Co-design A5 checklist with RMs and customers. Run A6 extraction and A3 scoring in *shadow mode* on historical and live cases - no decisions affected. Define hard gates: KYC-cleared before signing, four-eyes segregation, contract-committee match. *Integration used: 0 deep.* Gate: shadow accuracy by PDF quality and hit type is measured; checklist accepted by RMs.

**Phase 2 - Deep-integration wave (Months 2-6, exactly 2 deep integrations).** Deep 1: A6 into document/credit platform - go live first for Micro/SME Working-capital, analyst-validated. Deep 2: KYC platform - A3 go-live with conservative thresholds and human escalation, with A4 deployed as a specialist workbench on the same integration (one programme). A5 goes live on the existing portal before/with A6 so cleaner documents feed extraction. *Order: A5  A6  A3  A4.* Gate at Month 6: no increase in KYC false negatives or four-eyes violations; specialist and analyst queues within capacity; audit-trail completeness 100% for model-assisted cases.

**Phase 3 - Scale and prepare next wave (Months 6-12, no new deep integrations).** Extend A6 to Equipment finance and High-exposure with enhanced validation; tune A3 thresholds; expand A4; redeploy Back-office capacity to document-quality support; train additional compliance staff. Introduce the collateral *policy* (early valuation trigger parallel to credit analysis, triage routing rules) and measure valuer late-rates to build the A7 case without consuming integration slots. Monitor: median/percentile wait before Resolve mismatch, Normalize, Credit analysis, Committee packaging, Signing; loop counts (FinancialRequest, KYCResolve, ContractRemediate); rework rates; analyst/specialist/legal utilization; override and exception rates; provisional signing and four-eyes violation rates; customer friction. Gate for Year 2: decide between A12 (control workbench) and A9 (contract generation) as the next deep integration; A8 only when artifact-quality gates are met; A1/A2/A10/A11 thereafter.

## 7. Final recommendation

**I recommend a compliance-hardened bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A6, A5, A4 and A3** - i.e., the bottleneck portfolio rebalanced by replacing A7 with A3. A7 is deferred as a formal module (mitigated meanwhile by the early-trigger policy) because its benefit is capped by external valuer turnaround and it does not satisfy Compliance's requirement that the first wave strengthen controls; A3 does, while A4 ensures its escalations have somewhere to go.

This portfolio attacks the process where time and risk actually accumulate: the missing-document loop (3,300/2,800), normalization before credit analysis (5,000 DFG, 75 min, 20.0h wait) that starves and overloads the 9 analysts, and the mismatch loop (31.0h, 82% critical path) that blocks the 3-person specialist pool and legally blocks signing. It creates the clean artifacts that A8, A9, A10 and A11 need as prerequisites - so deferring those downstream bots is not a delay, it is sequencing.

**Why the highest-frequency activity should not go first:** Register application (11,880 cases) is the obvious political win, but its wait is 1.5h and service 8 min, and Back office (12 FTE) is already below 65% utilized after 15:00 - saved minutes there do not relieve any constraint. Automating A1 (and classifying faster via A2) would simply deliver cases sooner to the real constraints - KYC specialists, credit analysts and committee synchronization - increasing their queues without reducing the 58.0h, 44.0h or 31.0h waits that dominate end-to-end performance. Frequency measures volume, not leverage. A1/A2 remain worthwhile *later* quick wins, once A5/A6/A3/A4 have raised downstream throughput and data quality - at which point their speed becomes an asset rather than a flood.
