## 7. Provisional conclusion

The measured collapse in conformance from ~93% in Apr-May to ~32% in Jun-Jul is primarily a data-provenance and logging artifact, not a sudden operational breakdown. Staged normalisation recovers fitness to 89% by mapping `Risk Screen` to `Classify`, removing the technical `Queue Routed` events introduced on 2026-06-03, and reordering by source sequence number after the 2026-06-15 ETL switch to warehouse ingestion time. The manual screenshot check for the 70 Notify-before-Decision traces supports timestamp/order unreliability rather than real reversal. 

Separately, a genuine normative obsolescence exists. The 2026-08-01 policy requiring `Supervisor Sign-off` for claims above EUR 50,000 is absent from the control-flow model, and Aug-Sep variants show sign-off after both Standard and Enhanced Review. Adding an optional sign-off raises fitness from 66% to 86% after preprocessing, confirming model lag. Confidence in the data-artifact explanation is high ~85-90% for the Jun-Jul drop. Confidence that a real compliance problem remains is moderate ~30-40%: Enhanced Review traces without a recorded Compliance Check persist in both periods, and `Manual Override` usage increased, but no change in appeal rate, approval rate or cycle time is observed and events missing resource rose from 1.2% to 6.4%, making missing extraction a plausible alternative.

Immediate actions: IT should restore source-system timestamps and sequence ordering for conformance jobs and publish an event-semantic contract distinguishing business vs technical events. Process owners must version the normative model and update it to reflect the sign-off policy with a data-aware guard on claim value. Compliance should conduct a targeted attribute-stratified manual audit of Enhanced Review without Compliance Check and of high-value cases with and without sign-off, rather than broad noncompliance actions. No claim of widespread employee bypass should be made yet; fitness improvements obtained by an over-permissive model must not be interpreted as proof of compliance.

## 8. Model-governance recommendation

1. Version normative models with policy effective dates and store them immutably alongside conformance reports.
2. Maintain an event-semantic contract that defines business activities, technical events, and label mappings, with change control for UI releases.
3. Prohibit technical events from entering conformance logs without explicit tagging and a removal policy.
4. Preserve and log source-system sequence numbers and timestamps; conformance ordering must default to source sequence with documented exceptions.
5. Encode data-aware constraints such as EUR 50k sign-off as decision rules in the model or as separate compliance checks, not as control-flow only.
6. Require post-release validation: after any UI, ETL or policy change, run label mapping, technical-event filtering and source-ordered conformance before reporting fitness to operations.
