## 1. Decision rule

**Scope and eligibility**
- **Stage eligibility**: A case is swarm-eligible only while in `Triage` or `Diagnosis Started`. Any case at or beyond `Diagnosis Complete` (e.g. I-03) is **ineligible** for swarms, regardless of predicted risk.
- **Time window**: Evaluate all eligible cases with **≤ 12 hours** to SLA; below ~2 hours remaining, we raise urgency but still require positive expected value (EV).
- **One-swarm norm**: At most **one swarm per case** unless a materially new event occurs (e.g. stage change, major new diagnostic information, or large risk jump linked to a clear process change).

**Calibration-adjusted risk and effect inputs**
- For scoring, replace `p_now` with a **segment-adjusted interval** \(p_{\text{adj,low}}, p_{\text{adj,high}}\) and a midpoint \(p_{\text{adj,mid}}\):
  - **SME**: model underpredicts by 0.04–0.09 ⇒ treat \(p_{\text{adj,mid}} = p_{\text{now}} + 0.06\) with band \([p_{\text{now}} + 0.04,\ p_{\text{now}} + 0.09]\).
  - **Strategic Enterprise**: model overpredicts by 0.05–0.12 ⇒ use \(p_{\text{adj,mid}} = p_{\text{now}} - 0.08\) with band \([p_{\text{now}} - 0.12,\ p_{\text{now}} - 0.05]\), truncated to \([0,1]\).
  - **Public Sector**: calibration acceptable ⇒ \(p_{\text{adj,mid}} \approx p_{\text{now}}\) with a moderate band (±0.05).
  - **Non-Native-Language Support**: high uncertainty ⇒ treat \(p_{\text{adj}}\) as a **wide interval**, e.g. \([p_{\text{now}} - 0.1,\ p_{\text{now}} + 0.1]\), and discount effect estimates (see below).
- The **estimated absolute risk reduction** ranges are treated as **uncertain scenario inputs**, not as known causal effects. For scoring:
  - Use the **midpoint** of the case’s range as a working estimate \(\Delta p_{\text{mid}}\).
  - Construct an **effect interval**: optimistic = upper half of the range; adverse = lower bound, with the possibility that for some segments (e.g. Non-Native-Language Support, imputed features) the true effect is **near zero**.

**Per-case net-benefit score (ignoring fairness caps)**
- For each eligible case \(i\), compute an approximate **expected direct net benefit**:
  - \(EV^{\text{direct}}_i \approx \Delta p_{\text{mid},i} \times V_i - C_{\text{swarm}} - C_{\text{false},i}\),
  - where \(V_i\) is the value of avoiding breach, \(C_{\text{swarm}} = €450\), and \(C_{\text{false},i}\) is the case’s false-alarm burden.
- Approximate **queue externalities per slot** as:
  - Per slot, base-case extra expected breaches on other diagnosis-stage cases \( \approx 6 \times 0.02 = 0.12\),
  - With typical value per impacted case ≈ €20k ⇒ spillover cost ≈ **€2,400 per slot** (range ~€1,500–€3,500).
- Define a **conservative net score**:
  - \(EV^{\text{net}}_i \approx EV^{\text{direct}}_i - EV^{\text{spillover-per-slot}}\),
  - Using the mid spillover estimate (~€2,400) for ranking, but testing robustness under low/high spillover.
- **Minimum economic threshold**: consider a case **swarm-candidate** only if:
  - \(EV^{\text{net}}_i > 0\) under base assumptions, **and**
  - EV remains **non-negative under a modestly adverse scenario** (lower-half effect, higher spillover).

**Non-monetary and operational adjustments**
- **Stage urgency weight**: Among candidates with similar \(EV^{\text{net}}\), prioritize:
  - (1) Cases nearest the SLA deadline, especially if still in `Triage` (e.g. I-07),
  - (2) Cases in `Diagnosis Started` with high risk that may unlock downstream work (e.g. I-02, I-06, I-09).
- **Evidence-change requirement for repeated alerts**:
  - Treat an alert as “new” only if **either**:
    - A material **process/event change** (stage change, new customer diagnostic log, escalation, major queue change), or
    - An absolute risk change \(|p_{\text{now}} - p_{\text{prev}}| ≥ 0.05\) **and** attributable to new information rather than imputation/noise.
  - Repeated alerts without new evidence (e.g. I-05) generally **do not upgrade priority** and may enter a cool-down.

**Fairness-aware prioritization**
- Compute a **raw net score** \(EV^{\text{net}}_i\) and then apply:
  - A **value cap**: beyond €50k, use a concave transform (e.g. log or square root) so that very high-value cases (I-08) do not completely dominate lower-value but high-risk cases.
  - **Segment floors**: ensure that, over time, each major segment (SME, Public Sector, Strategic Enterprise, Non-Native-Language Support) receives at least a **minimal intervention share conditional on risk**, so low-value or uncertain segments are not starved.
- Final **priority tiers**:
  - **Tier 1**: Positive base EV after spillover, robust under mild adverse assumptions, and either near-deadline or very high breach impact.
  - **Tier 2**: Positive EV but weaker robustness or lower urgency.
  - **Tier 3**: Eligible by stage but EV close to zero or negative once spillovers and uncertainty are accounted for.

**Capacity and reserve rule**
- **Daily capacity**: four slots, but **target use is three**, keeping **one slot in reserve** for:
  - An unlisted regulatory incident, or
  - A late-arriving, clearly superior candidate (e.g. a very high-risk, high-value case discovered in the next cycle).
- Exception: using all four slots is allowed if:
  - Aggregate EV remains clearly positive **even under adverse assumptions**, and
  - No strong regulatory or fairness reason exists to keep a slot in reserve.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**Alert life cycle**
- **First alert** for an eligible case:
  - Score using the decision rule; if Tier 1 with positive robust EV, consider immediately for a swarm.
  - If Tier 2–3, log the decision, communicate any manual checks, and continue routine processing.
- **Repeated alerts** are handled according to evidence change:
  - If **no new event** (no stage change, no new logs, no substantive process update) and \(|p_{\text{now}} - p_{\text{prev}}| < 0.05\), classify the alert as **refresh-only**:
    - Do **not** upgrade priority.
    - Optionally **suppress swarm recommendations** for a configurable cool-down (e.g. 2–3 refresh cycles) unless human reviewers override.
  - If there **is** a material new event or a large attributable risk jump (≥0.05–0.10) with credible data quality, treat the alert as **new evidence** and recompute the score.

**Cool-down, suppression, escalation, and re-entry**
- **Cool-down after swarm**:
  - Once a swarm has been run, block further swarms on that case unless:
    - A new, clearly distinct issue emerges, or
    - Risk increases sharply again with new evidence and the case is still pre–Diagnosis Complete.
- **Suppression for noisy repeated alerts**:
  - Cases like **I-05**, where alerts repeat solely due to scheduled refresh with no new events, enter a **suppression state**:
    - Further alerts are logged but do not generate swarm recommendations unless the risk band moves substantially (e.g. new upper-bound above a higher threshold) or a stage/event change occurs.
- **Escalation and human override**:
  - For borderline or contentious cases (e.g. conflicting data-quality notes, high-value but overpredicted segments), a human incident manager can:
    - Override suppression and request a swarm, or
    - Explicitly label the case as “no-swarm unless X occurs,” which feeds back into training and policy evaluation.
- **Re-entry**:
  - A suppressed or cooled-down case may re-enter the candidate pool when:
    - It moves to a new stage (e.g. Triage → Diagnosis Started) with new missing information risks, or
    - New customer logs or monitoring signals materially change the diagnostic picture, or
    - A significant simulated EV gain is observed under updated calibration.

**Explicit comparison: I-05 vs. I-07**
- **I-05** (SME, Triage, repeated alerts, no new event):
  - The repeated alerts result from model refresh, not new information.
  - Under the policy, I-05 stays in **cool-down/suppression**: we do not use a swarm slot now despite a high effect range, because:
    - The evidence base has not changed.
    - We risk wasting capacity and increasing spillovers with little incremental learning.
- **I-07** (SME, Triage, 1.5h to SLA, new customer log):
  - The repeated alert is linked to **new diagnostic evidence** and imminent SLA risk.
  - It is re-scored and promoted to **Tier 1**, and we **allocate a swarm slot now**.
  - This distinction makes the policy **evidence-aware over time**, rather than simply reacting to every refresh.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Value-based prioritization and low-value cases**
- Relying purely on monetary **value of avoiding breach** risks starving lower-value but often more numerous customers (e.g. some SME or Public Sector cases).
- Safeguards:
  - Apply a **concave transform or cap** to value in the priority score so that cases like I-08 (very high value) do not always outrank moderate-value, high-risk SME or Public Sector cases.
  - Introduce **segment-conditional floors**: e.g. within each segment, maintain a minimum intervention rate for cases exceeding a risk threshold, independent of absolute monetary value.

**Non-Native-Language Support uncertainty (e.g. I-09)**
- Calibration and effect estimates for Non-Native-Language Support are explicitly uncertain.
- Risks:
  - Overestimation may cause **over-intervention** in this group, with unnecessary disruptions.
  - Underestimation may cause **systematic neglect** of language-minority customers.
- Safeguards:
  - Treat this segment as an **explicit analytic stratum**: monitor predictions, swarms, and outcomes separately.
  - Use **structured experimentation** (see Section 6) to learn true effects in this group, including deliberate oversampling in some periods under close monitoring.
  - Require human review before **denying** swarms to high-risk cases from this segment solely on model grounds.

**Differential calibration across segments**
- SME underprediction and Strategic Enterprise overprediction create asymmetric errors:
  - SMEs may be **under-served**, missing beneficial swarms.
  - Strategic Enterprise may receive **excessive attention**, amplifying existing power imbalances.
- Safeguards:
  - Use **segment-specific calibration curves** and maintain ongoing reliability plots (predicted vs. actual breach) by segment and stage.
  - Recompute **segment-level offsets** regularly and feed them into the decision rule, not just the offline model.

**Selective intervention and label feedback**
- When high-risk or high-value cases systematically receive swarms, their ultimate outcomes (often “no breach”) reflect the **intervention**, not the underlying untreated risk.
- If training data later ignore swarm usage, the model will:
  - Learn that some risky patterns are “safe,”
  - Underestimate risk for patterns that historically benefited from swarms, and
  - Reinforce the same intervention choices in a **positive feedback loop**.
- Safeguards:
  - Log swarm interventions as an explicit **treatment variable**.
  - When retraining, use **causal methods** (e.g. inverse propensity weighting, doubly robust estimators) or, at minimum, exclude highly treated slices from naive calibration.
  - Keep a **hold-out cohort** where the policy is partly randomized near the decision boundary to keep counterfactual data available.

**Spillover harms and untreated groups**
- Queue delays from swarms disproportionately harm **non-intervened diagnosis-stage cases**, which may be:
  - Lower-value, lower-priority segments,
  - Segments already less likely to receive attention (e.g. SMEs, some Public Sector cases).
- Safeguards:
  - Monitor **waiting-time distributions and breach increases** by segment and by “never-swarmed vs. often-swarmed” status.
  - Set **capacity-aware limits**: e.g. cap swarms per hour when diagnosis queues are already long, to avoid concentrating harm on untreated groups.
  - Consider **rotating priority** among borderline candidates to avoid one subgroup absorbing most spillovers.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational monitoring**
- Track, by time of day and segment:
  - Swarm slot utilization, including how often the reserve slot is used or left idle.
  - Queue metrics: average and tail waiting times in Triage and Diagnosis, and the distribution of time to SLA at each stage.
  - Frequency of repeated alerts, cool-down activations, and human overrides.
- Use these metrics to adjust **hourly caps** on swarms and to refine when to honor vs. override the reserve-slot preference.

**Predictive calibration monitoring**
- Maintain **calibration plots and Brier scores** by:
  - Segment (SME, Strategic Enterprise, Public Sector, Non-Native-Language Support),
  - Stage (Triage vs. Diagnosis Started),
  - Time-to-SLA buckets.
- Re-estimate **segment-specific offsets** (e.g. SME underprediction magnitude, Strategic overprediction) quarterly or when:
  - The observed breach rate in a decile differs from predicted by more than ~5–7 percentage points.
  - A process change (like the redesign for Strategic Enterprise) is introduced.
- Feed updated offsets back into the **decision rule** even before fully retraining the model.

**Causal-effect and policy-impact monitoring**
- Implement **randomization near the decision boundary**:
  - For borderline Tier 1/Tier 2 cases, randomly assign a fraction to receive or not receive a swarm.
  - Use this to estimate **causal risk-reduction effects** overall and by segment (including Non-Native-Language Support).
- Build simple **uplift or treatment-effect models** that:
  - Predict the **incremental impact** of a swarm, not just breach risk,
  - Inform updates to the effect ranges used in EV calculations.
- Periodically re-estimate **spillover effects**:
  - Compare breach rates and delays for diagnosis-stage cases exposed to different levels of swarm activity in their queues.

**Capacity and queue externality monitoring**
- Track:
  - Number of diagnosis-stage cases and their average remaining time to SLA at the time of each swarm.
  - Observed **increment in breach probability and delay** on non-intervened diagnosis-stage cases after periods of high swarm usage.
- If observed spillovers are **consistently higher** than the assumed 0.01–0.03 per slot/case, tighten:
  - The per-hour swarm cap,
  - The EV threshold (require higher \(EV^{\text{net}}\)),
  - Or the requirement to keep one or more slots in reserve.

**Fairness monitoring**
- For each segment and key subgroup (including Non-Native-Language Support, SMEs, Public Sector, Strategic Enterprise):
  - Monitor **intervention rates**, average predicted and realized breach probabilities, and average queue delays.
  - Track **who absorbs spillover harms** (extra delay and breach rate increases) when swarm usage is high.
- If any group shows:
  - Systematically lower intervention rates despite high risk, or
  - Disproportionately high spillover harm,
  - Adjust the **segment floors, value caps, or prioritization penalties** to correct the imbalance.

**Policy revision triggers**
- Revisit and potentially change the **thresholds or allocation rules** when:
  - Segment-level calibration error exceeds a predefined bound for two consecutive review periods.
  - Estimated swarm effect sizes shift materially (e.g. by >30%) in updated causal analyses.
  - Queue externalities increase beyond acceptable limits (e.g. average spillover breach cost approaching or exceeding direct benefits).
- Conduct a **formal policy review** (including stakeholders from operations, risk, and fairness/governance) at least **annually**, or earlier after major workflow changes, to revise:
  - Eligibility and cool-down rules,
  - Segment-specific adjustments,
  - Capacity caps and reserve-slot strategy.
