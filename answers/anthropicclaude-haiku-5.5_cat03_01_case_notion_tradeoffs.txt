## 1. Analytical assumptions

**Case notion.** A flattened case is the set of events selected by one chosen object type, with each event included whenever it is directly related (E2O) to an object of that type. Flattening is a projection, not a recovery of a true process. I do not treat the longest trace as evidence of correctness. For example, O3 has the longest order trace (13 events), but that reflects its position as a hub, not a better case notion.

**Convergence** means that one flattened case absorbs events or objects belonging to several real-world instances. Examples: V2 in the invoice view bills O2 and O3, and D2 in the delivery view carries P2 (O1), P3 (O2, O3).

**Divergence** means that one real-world instance is split across several cases. Examples: O1 is split across P1/P2 and D1/D2, and O3 across P3/P4, D2/D3, and V2 with O2.

**Shared events** are both evidence and duplication. As evidence, e16 really did dispatch P2 and P3 together, so the co-participation is factual and should be kept in object-centric (OC) analysis. As duplication, each flattened case counts that event again, so flattened frequencies and durations are inflated. I will never delete shared events silently. Any reduction is a named sensitivity variant.

**Permitted transformations:**
- (a) One-hop O2O propagation, labelled `propagated` and never mixed with direct E2O without a flag. The main candidate is the `contains` link from order to item.
- (b) Weighting each copy by 1/k, where k is the number of same-type objects attached to the event.
- (c) Augmenting events with qualifiers from the relation table, such as `partially_settles` and `completes_settlement` for M2 and M3. This adds attributes and does not add events.
- (d) No event invention and no multi-hop propagation.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

**D1. Cross-order package P3 (Order and Package flattenings).** e13 packs I21 (O2) and I31 (O3) together. In the order view, O2 receives O3's packing event, and O3 receives O2's. The package case for P3 also picks up e16 and e24, which concern P2 (O1). The effect is attributional: O2's pack-to-dispatch time is partly determined by O3's item. Here both items were packed at 10:12 and dispatched at 11:05, so the error is zero in this instance. The distortion would matter if the timestamps differed. **Harmful** for per-order attribution. **Informative** for OC analysis of shared packing.

**D2. Cross-order package P4 (O3, O4).** e14 packs I32 (O3) and I41 (O4) together at 10:20. Both are dispatched at 11:15 (e17) and confirmed at 10:00 on 4 Feb (e27). O4's delivery-confirmation time is therefore driven by O3's item. In the O4 flattening, the 22 h 45 min confirmation lag belongs to a delivery that also carries O3's item. This is a **harmful** entanglement for per-order SLAs but **informative** for OC-DFG, where the shared package object reveals the coupling.

**D3. Consolidated delivery D2 (Delivery flattening, plus O1 order view).** D2 carries P2 (O1) and P3 (O2, O3). In the delivery view, the 4 h 55 min dispatch-to-confirm time mixes three commercial instances. In the O1 view, the trace shows Dispatch (e15, 11:00, D1) then Dispatch (e16, 11:05, D2), then Confirm (e23 at 14:00, then e24 at 16:00). This looks like repeated dispatch and confirmation, but it is a split shipment. **Harmful** if read as rework. **Context-dependent**, because the OC-DFG can show D2 as a shared object.

**D4. Consolidated invoice V2 (Order and Invoice flattenings).** One invoice (V2) bills two orders. Order flattening gives four Issue Invoice events (e18, e19 twice, e20) from three real ones, so invoice-count KPIs are inflated by one. The invoice view shows one case with two orders, while the order view shows an invoice for O2 and an invoice for O3. **Harmful** for count-based KPIs. **Informative** if the ET–OT graph shows V2 as a convergence object.

**D5. Partial payment (Invoice and Order flattenings).** M2 (partially_settles, 12:40 on 3 Feb) and M3 (completes_settlement, 09:00 on 4 Feb) are both labelled Record Payment. The invoice view therefore shows V2 as IssuePaymentPayment, a self-loop. The order views of O2 and O3 show the same pattern. The self-loop looks like double recording or rework. The qualifier that explains it exists only in the relation table. Invoice-issue to final settlement for V2 is 20 h 55 min (e19 to e25), and the gap between instalments is 20 h 20 min. **Harmful** unless the qualifiers are augmented onto events (transformation c). **Informative** after augmentation.

**D6. Near-singleton payment lifecycle (Payment flattening).** Each of M1–M4 has one event. The Payment DFG has no edges, and no lead time can be computed. Event-count inflation also appears in the order views (six Record Payment copies from four events). **Harmful** for DFG and performance. Only its count is informative.

**D7. Near-singleton delivery lifecycle (Delivery flattening).** D1, D2, and D3 each have two events. The delivery view cannot show that payment for O1 (M1, 12:30) and for O4 (M4, 09:30 on 4 Feb) precedes the confirmation of the delivery. **Harmful** for end-to-end timing. **Informative** only for dispatch-to-confirm duration.

**D8. Item flattening drops Create and Close.** The item view starts every trace with Add Item. e01–e04 and e28–e31 have no item and are absent. Dispatch appears as 6 item-level instances from 3 real events (e16 contributes three). **Harmful** for start-activity and frequency analysis. **Informative** for physical item timing: PackDispatch is 1 h for I11 and about 53–55 min for the others, and DispatchConfirm is 3 h for I11, 4 h 55 min for I12, I21, and I31, and 22 h 45 min for I32 and I41.

**D9. False cycle in O3.** The O3 trace is Create, Add, Add, Pack (e13), Pack (e14), Dispatch (e16), Dispatch (e17), Issue Invoice (e19), Pay (e22), Confirm (e24), Pay (e25), Confirm (e27), Close (e30). The per-order DFG therefore contains Record PaymentConfirm Delivery and Confirm DeliveryRecord Payment, which forms a cycle. The cycle comes from two deliveries (D2, D3) and two instalments. It is not rework. **Harmful** if read as a loop. **Informative** in the OC-DFG, where it shows O3 as the coupling point between V2 and D2/D3.

**D10. Apparent payment-before-delivery.** In every order, Record Payment precedes Confirm Delivery:
- O1: M1 (12:30) before e23 (14:00).
- O2 and O3: M2 (12:40) before e24 (16:00 on 3 Feb).
- O3: M3 (09:00 on 4 Feb) before e27 (10:00 on 4 Feb).
- O4: M4 (09:30 on 4 Feb) before e27 (10:00 on 4 Feb).

The invoice and payment views cannot see this ordering because they contain no delivery events. The order views show it, but only by pooling events. This could be a prepayment policy or an artefact of how payment-to-invoice is recorded. **Context-dependent.** The OC-DFG and ET–OT graph must test it before it is reported.

**D11. Batch close artefact.** Close Order events are concentrated at 11:00–11:10 on 4 Feb (e28–e31). Create-to-close is therefore about 26 h for every order (O1 26 h 00 min, O2 26 h 03 min, O3 26 h 01 min, O4 26 h 04 min). Confirm-to-close differs sharply: 19 h for O1 and O2 (last confirm at 16:00 on 3 Feb), and about 1 h for O3 and O4 (last confirm at 10:00 on 4 Feb). Lead-time comparisons based on Close are dominated by batching. **Harmful.**

**D12. Longest-trace selection.** O3, with 13 events, is the longest order case. Choosing it as a representative case would reward flattening that maximises copies. This is not a valid selection criterion.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

**Flattened logs to create:**
- **Order log.** Keep it as the primary flattened log, with a `is_shared` flag and a `weight` attribute on every event.
- **Item log.** Keep it for pick-pack-dispatch timing, with Create and Close events removed explicitly.
- **Delivery log.** Keep it for dispatch-to-confirm SLA only, and state that it hides three-order consolidation in D2.
- **Invoice log.** Keep it for billing and settlement, with qualifiers augmented onto payment events.
- **Package and Payment logs.** Do not create them as standalone logs. Package adds no variant information beyond the OC-DFG, and Payment is a singleton. Use Payment as an augmented attribute of the invoice log.

**Questions each flattened log answers safely:**
- Order: order-level stage presence and absence, and the proportion of orders that close after all deliveries are confirmed. Avoid absolute durations and any count of invoices or payments.
- Item: PackDispatch and DispatchConfirm distributions by item. The result is safe as long as Add and Pack are not treated as order-level starts.
- Delivery: dispatch-to-confirm for D1 (3 h), D2 (4 h 55 min), and D3 (22 h 45 min), reported separately with the number of orders per shipment.
- Invoice: issue-to-final-settlement durations: V1 (about 30 min), V2 (20 h 55 min), V3 (21 h 20 min). Report V2 as one invoice with two orders.

**Questions requiring OC analysis:**
- Whether payment precedes delivery across orders (D10). Requires OC-DFG plus ET–OT graph.
- Which orders are coupled through D2, P3, P4, and V2. Requires object interaction summary and ET–OT graph.
- Which objects dominate shared events (e16, e24, e13, e14). Requires OC-DFG with event, unique-object, and total-reference frequencies.
- Whether O1 and O3 follow structurally equivalent executions. Requires equivalence clustering, as O1 and O3 have different shapes (O1 has a 12-event lifecycle with split shipments, O3 has two deliveries and two payments).
- Whether the O3 loop is real. Requires OTG-based dependency check.

**Reconciling contradictions:**
1. Identify the flattened claim (for example, "O4 delivery is slow").
2. Trace each supporting event in the OCEL. Check whether the event is shared.
3. Re-run the claim with shared events removed (S1) and with 1/k weighting (S2). If the claim disappears, it was a duplication artefact.
4. If the claim survives, check whether it depends on propagation (S3). If so, it is a propagation artefact.
5. If the claim survives all three, report it in OC terms (for example, "O4's delivery confirmation is coupled to P4's shared package, which also carries O3's item"). Do not report it as an order-level SLA.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Position: B.** Use Order as the primary lead, with explicitly secondary lifecycles for Item (physical), Delivery (logistics), and Invoice (financial). Package and Payment are attributes, not lifecycles.

**Decision criteria:**
1. Activity coverage: Order covers 8 of 8 activities, the others 4 or fewer.
2. Business meaning: the customer order is the commercial commitment that all parties reference.
3. Lifecycle completeness: only Order has Create and Close.
4. Duplication and convergence cost: Order has 48% shared copies. This is high, but Item has 58%, Package has about 33%, and Delivery and Invoice have 0% duplication but high convergence.
5. Sensitivity to lead choice: Order is the only type whose results remain interpretable after the shared-event filter, because the non-shared core still gives 3 to 10 events per order.

**Strongest supporting evidence:**
- Only Order covers all 8 activities. O1's 12-event lifecycle, from e01 to e28, is the only fully traceable instance that includes Create, Pack, Dispatch, Invoice, Payment, Confirm, and Close.
- After removing shared events, O1, O4 retain 10 and 5 events, showing that the order core is not destroyed by the convergence.

**Strongest counterargument:**
Order is an administrative aggregator. Fulfilment is physically organised by package and delivery, and the operationally important bottlenecks (D2 and D3) are shared objects. O3's loop (D9) and the O1 split-shipment (D3) show that an order is not a clean unit of work. The O-case is also the most duplicated view (48%). A serious analyst could argue that Delivery or Package should lead the physical process, with orders derived from them.

**Response:** That counterargument is correct for logistics questions. It does not hold for the end-to-end lifecycle, because Delivery covers 2 activities and Package covers 3. Choosing Delivery as lead would truncate Create, Close, Invoice, and Payment. The secondary lifecycles exist to cover that gap.

**Residual risk:**
- O3 remains a coupling hub across O2, O4, V2, D2, and D3, so per-order metrics for O2, O3, and O4 are entangled.
- The batch close (D11) makes order-level total lead times uninformative.
- The payment-before-delivery pattern (D10) is visible only in the order view, and only through pooled events.

**Conditions under which I would change the decision:**
- If the question is logistics SLA performance, change to Delivery as lead, with Order as a secondary lifecycle.
- If the question is cash collection or days-sales-outstanding, change to Invoice as lead, but keep V2 explicitly multi-order.
- If shared events exceed roughly half of all event copies in a larger dataset, or if the S1 variant changes the main conclusions, reject a single lead (position C) and present the process through an OC-DFG as the primary artefact.
- If qualifiers or timestamps show that payment-before-delivery is a systematic policy, the payment lifecycle should become a first-class secondary lifecycle, not an attribute.

def build_case_table(ev_obj, object_type):
    """Direct E2O case table for one object type.
    weight = 1/k where k = number of same-type objects attached to the event.
    """
    sub = ev_obj[ev_obj["ocel:type"] == object_type].copy()
    n_per_event = (
        sub.groupby("ocel:eid")["ocel:oid"].nunique()
        .rename("n_cases_for_event")
    )
    sub = sub.join(n_per_event, on="ocel:eid")
    sub["weight"] = 1.0 / sub["n_cases_for_event"]
    sub["is_shared"] = sub["n_cases_for_event"] > 1
    return sub.rename(columns={
        "ocel:oid": "case_id",
        "ocel:activity": "activity",
        "ocel:timestamp": "timestamp",
    })


def propagate_contains(ocel, ev_obj):
    """Explicit one-hop propagation: Order events copied to child Items via
    O2O 'contains'. Labelled so it is never silently mixed with direct E2O.
    """
    o2o = ocel.o2o
    contains = (
        o2o[o2o["ocel:qualifier"] == "contains"][["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2"]]
        .rename(columns={"ocel:oid": "parent", "ocel:oid_2": "child"})
    )
    parent_events = (
        ev_obj[ev_obj["ocel:type"] == "Order"]
        [["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp"]]
        .rename(columns={"ocel:oid": "parent"})
    )
    prop = contains.merge(parent_events, on="parent")
    prop = prop.rename(columns={
        "child": "case_id",
        "ocel:activity": "activity",
        "ocel:timestamp": "timestamp",
    })
    prop["provenance"] = "propagated_via_contains"
    return prop[["ocel:eid", "case_id", "activity", "timestamp", "provenance"]]


def weighted_dfg(case_tbl, weight_col="weight"):
    """Directly-follows counts within cases, weighted by per-event weight."""
    t = case_tbl.sort_values(["case_id", "timestamp"]).copy()
    if weight_col not in t.columns:
        t[weight_col] = 1.0
    t["next_activity"] = t.groupby("case_id")["activity"].shift(-1)
    pairs = t.dropna(subset=["next_activity"])
    return (
        pairs.groupby(["activity", "next_activity"])[weight_col]
        .sum()
        .sort_values(ascending=False)
    )

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

**S1. Remove or down-weight shared events.** Compare the order view with and without the nine multi-order events (`order_no_shared` against `order_direct`). Expected results from the supplied data:
- O1 retains 10 events (losing e16 and e24).
- O2 falls from 9 events to 3 (e02, e07, e29).
- O3 falls from 13 to 4 (e03, e08, e09, e30).
- O4 falls from 8 to 5 (e04, e10, e20, e26, e31).

If the O3 cycle (D9) disappears in the shared-free view, it is shown to be a convergence artefact. If O2's and O3's durations change sharply, the per-order SLA claims are not robust. The 1/k weighted variant sums to the true event counts (validated by the assertion in Section 6), so flattened frequencies can be compared with true event counts.

**S2. Direct E2O versus explicitly propagated relations.** Compare the direct item view with the `contains`-propagated view. Propagation over-generates. For I11, the propagated view adds all O1 events: e01, e06, e12, e16, e18, e21, e24, e28. Of these, e06 and e12 concern I12, and e16 and e24 concern D2, which I11 is not in. The check is that any claim surviving propagation must also survive direct E2O. A claim present only in the propagated view is treated as an artefact.

**S3. Changing the lead object.** Run the same metrics (case count, mean length, variant count, median lead time) for Item, Package, Delivery, Invoice, and Payment flattenings, and compare with the Order view.
