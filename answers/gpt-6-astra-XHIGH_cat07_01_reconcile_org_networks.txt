# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

Mira’s pattern is consistent with an exception-handling coordination function that crosses team boundaries. It also suggests a possible dependency on her availability, knowledge, or permissions. These explanations can coexist: useful coordination can become a point of organizational fragility. Neither centrality nor the process owner’s description establishes her formal authority, effectiveness, or replaceability.

## Handover-of-work network

Mira’s high reported betweenness (0.77), broad outgoing connections, and exchanges with specialists and adjusters suggest that many recorded transitions between organizational actors pass through her. Bot_Rules → Mira is consistent with automated exception detection feeding a human routing function. Specialist → Mira transitions suggest that some review results return through that function.

This supports a role in connecting stages and allocating work. It does **not** establish that Mira manages the recipients, makes substantive claims decisions, or is an overloaded bottleneck. A temporal succession between resources is not necessarily an explicit assignment or communication. Parallel tasks, completion ordering, and automated status updates can also produce apparent handovers.

Betweenness describes shortest paths in the constructed resource graph, not necessarily actual case trajectories. Its interpretation depends on directionality, edge selection, normalization, and how frequency weights are converted into path lengths. The supplied list contains only top edges, so it cannot independently substantiate the reported centralities.

Bot_Rules is itself highly central, with betweenness 0.41. That reflects the routing architecture rather than human leadership. Mira’s position should therefore be understood partly as an interface between automation and human exception handling.

## Working-together network

This network measures shared case participation. Its stronger North and South connections are consistent with recurring operational teams: Alice–Carla–Elise and Bruno–Diego–Farid. Mira’s lower weighted degree suggests weaker membership in those recurring co-case groupings under the supplied normalization.

A resource can have many handovers concentrated in a subset of cases, distributed across different partners, without sharing a large fraction of the overall caseload with any one partner. Mira touches 22% of claims, often briefly, and then other people perform subsequent work. Event-level transition counts and case-level co-occurrence therefore measure different relationships.

However, the supplied explanation that co-occurrence is low *because her events are short* requires correction. In a binary co-case measure, one short event counts as participation just as a long event does. Disappearing for several events also does not erase her participation in that case. The relevant mechanisms are selective case exposure, dispersion across partners, repeated transitions within cases, and the denominator used—not event duration itself.

Low working-together strength does not demonstrate poor collaboration, social isolation, weak influence, or dispensability. Conversely, co-occurrence does not establish direct interaction or simultaneous work.

## Subcontracting network

The n=2 patterns identify short resource returns of the form A → B → A under the stated interpretation. Mira → Omar → Mira and Mira → Priya → Mira are consistent with obtaining specialist input and then resuming coordination. Her high subcontracting betweenness suggests a prominent position in the graph derived from such returns.

“Subcontracting” here is a process-mining pattern, not proof of a contractual relationship, managerial delegation, or ownership of the intervening work. An A–B–A fragment may represent consultation, review, rework, clarification, or a recorded status update. The activity labels are necessary to distinguish these mechanisms.

The n=2 setting also creates a narrow observation window. If specialist responses occur after several intervening events, the return may be missed. Lifecycle duplication and concurrent event ordering can create or suppress short patterns. Thus the network supports a consultation-and-return hypothesis but cannot alone establish why the return happens or whether it is necessary.

## Performance/time statistics

Mira’s 4.2-minute median service time and 905 distinct cases suggest many brief recorded interventions. Her concentration in reopened claims and complaints supports an association with difficult work. Neither proves low effort: medians conceal long tails, and coordination may include work outside the recorded activity intervals.

During her six-day absence, the exception-case 90th percentile cycle time increased from 8.4 to 13.9 days—5.5 days, approximately 65%—while the ordinary low-value median was unchanged. Together with exception queue accumulation, this is meaningful evidence of a possible coverage or dependency problem. It is not a controlled estimate of Mira’s causal effect. Demand, staffing, specialist availability, case severity, outages, and unfinished cases could affect the comparison. The two reported statistics also concern different populations and different quantiles.

The 42- and 51-minute elapsed transitions to specialists are not automatically waiting for Mira or waiting for the specialist. Depending on lifecycle semantics, an event-to-event gap may include recipient service, off-hours, parallel work, or delayed recording. “Average case cycle-time contribution” similarly needs a defined attribution method before it can support individual accountability.

Vendor_Verify and Vendor_Pay have long recorded durations and substantial volumes. Their times may describe external turnaround rather than active processing. Specialist service times are also much longer than Mira’s. End-to-end delay must therefore be decomposed across routing, specialist queues, substantive processing, vendors, and customer responses before attributing it to one person.

## Role discovery or activity-profile similarity

Mira’s profile—missing-information requests, reopening, complaints, and assignment—is compatible with exception coordination. It overlaps partly with team leads and junior adjusters but differs from specialist review and routine damage assessment. Activity-based similarity could reveal those overlaps; organizational role discovery could identify activity groups associated with similar resource participation.

Neither method recovers a ground-truth job title, competence, reporting relationship, authorization, or interchangeable staffing pool. A person with similar activity frequencies may lack Mira’s specialist knowledge or access rights. A distinct profile might represent a necessary function, unusual logging practice, or both. Bots and vendors must be labeled separately when translating discovered patterns into human staffing recommendations.

There is also an activity-dictionary issue: “Assign Specialist” appears in Mira’s profile but is absent from the initial activity list. “Approve?” is ambiguous, and a subcontracting fragment uses “Vendor_Verify,” a resource name, as though it were an activity. These distinctions must be resolved before interpreting discovered roles or motifs.

## Reconciliation and numerical qualifications

The qualitative reconciliation is that **transition brokerage, short return patterns, and recurring shared-case participation are different dimensions**. Mira could bridge multiple stable teams through selected difficult cases without belonging strongly to any one team. Her short events help explain why recorded service demand may look modest; they do not directly explain binary co-case strength.

Some numerical summaries nevertheless cannot all be literal measurements from the same conventional event-log construction:

- The six listed outgoing Mira edges total 1,640, exceeding her 960 events. Under immediate-successor handover counting, each event contributes at most one outgoing transition. Wider-distance counting, scaling, differing lifecycle treatment, or different cohorts would need to explain this.
- Mira has only 960 − 905 = 55 events beyond one per distinct case. Under ordinary n=2 A–B–A counting, she can therefore close at most 55 such return windows. The two listed specialist motifs alone total 224. These cannot be raw windows on that same set of records without a different counting convention or inconsistent inputs.
- If 4,100 is the common case universe, an Alice–Carla co-case rate of 27% implies about 1,107 shared cases, more than Alice’s 665 cases. The rate must use another denominator or cohort, or be incorrect. Conditional, pair-union, and global rates have different meanings.
- Under standard directed betweenness on the same graph, a node with zero out-degree, such as the reported Vendor_Pay, cannot lie internally on a directed path. Its positive betweenness needs an explanation such as an undirected calculation or different graph filtering.

These are reasons to audit definitions, not to discard the operational observations. They limit confidence in exact rankings and comparisons until the summaries are reconciled.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

These hypotheses are partly complementary: one describes an assigned function, another its vulnerability, and another the knowledge needed to perform it.

## Hypothesis A: Formal coordinator / exception desk

- **Mechanism:** Policy sends defined exceptions to Mira for classification, specialist assignment, clarification, and subsequent routing, while team leads retain ordinary team authority.
- **Supporting evidence:** Frequent Bot_Rules → Mira routing, specialist return patterns, the coordinator label, and disproportionate involvement in reopening and complaints all fit this mechanism.
- **Contradicting or limiting evidence:** A resource label and sequence do not establish a formally specified mandate. The process owner’s dismissive description suggests possible ambiguity over ownership or value. The supplied record contains no assignment policy or authorization evidence.
- **Observable predictions:** After conditioning on routing-time case attributes and exception category, assignment to the desk should be relatively consistent. Routing reasons should correspond to defined categories. An authorized substitute should reproduce similar routing choices and outcomes when covering the function.

## Hypothesis B: Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency

- **Mechanism:** Exceptions require Mira’s availability, tacit knowledge, or permissions. Queues can grow when she is unavailable, even if her recorded actions are short. A mandatory coordination step might also constrain throughput during demand peaks.
- **Supporting evidence:** Exception queues accumulated during her absence, exception tail cycle time increased, and difficult-case flows concentrate around her. High reported handover and return-pattern centrality is compatible with dependency.
- **Contradicting or limiting evidence:** There is no utilization measure, arrival-versus-capacity comparison, or direct measurement of waiting specifically for Mira. Ordinary claims were unaffected. Specialist or vendor constraints and temporary demand changes could explain some delay. Dependency during absence is not proof of chronic overload while present.
- **Observable predictions:** Comparable exception cases should wait longer before routing during uncovered shifts or absence periods. Queue age should track desk coverage after adjusting for arrivals and downstream capacity. Qualified backup should reduce that effect. If delay instead occurs mainly after routing, specialist capacity becomes a stronger explanation.

## Hypothesis C: Organizational boundary spanner / triage broker

- **Mechanism:** Mira knows which team or specialist should handle ambiguous cases and transfers context across organizational boundaries. Her value lies in accurate matching and maintaining continuity through exceptions.
- **Supporting evidence:** Her contacts span teams and specialties, adjusters describe her as knowing where hard cases belong, and the working-together network suggests that stable teams exist elsewhere. The manager’s concern about escalation and specialist overload fits a filtering function.
- **Contradicting or limiting evidence:** Broad contacts could also reflect routine administrative forwarding or logging conventions. No supplied measure shows that her decisions improve assignment accuracy, outcomes, or downstream workload. Staff statements are informative but not independent validation.
- **Observable predictions:** In comparable ambiguous cases, effective brokerage should be associated with fewer rejected referrals, fewer subsequent transfers, and less duplicated specialist review. Uncovered periods or poorly supported substitutes should increase misrouting or formal escalation. These comparisons must account for Mira receiving harder cases.

## Hypothesis D: Automation exception monitor / quality gatekeeper

- **Mechanism:** Mira reviews uncertain automated decisions, supplies missing context, and returns cases to automated processing or obtains targeted expert input. Her discretion may compensate for gaps in routing rules.
- **Supporting evidence:** Bot_Rules → Mira is prominent; Bot_Rules → Mira → Bot_Rules occurs in the subcontracting patterns; and her work includes complaints, reopening, and clarification.
- **Contradicting or limiting evidence:** Rule decisions, confidence scores, override reasons, and review outcomes are absent. A second bot event could merely acknowledge a status change. Detecting a problem and selecting a specialist does not establish that Mira overrode any automated decision.
- **Observable predictions:** Her workload should concentrate in identifiable rule-failure or ambiguity categories. Rule-version changes should alter that case mix. Valid improvements to particular routing rules should reduce related interventions without increasing misrouting, reopening, or complaints.

## Hypothesis E: Logging or attribution artifact exaggerates brokerage

- **Mechanism:** A shared coordinator account, automatically attributed assignment events, duplicate lifecycle records, or an unusual network estimator makes the Mira node appear more individually central than the underlying work warrants.
- **Supporting evidence:** The numerical inconsistencies and ambiguous activity vocabulary make this a serious measurement hypothesis. Routing events are particularly sensitive to whether the recorded resource is the decision-maker, executor, or system account.
- **Contradicting or limiting evidence:** The absence episode and staff descriptions point toward a real coordination dependency, even if its measured size is distorted. A data artifact need not explain all the organizational evidence.
- **Observable predictions:** Separating actual actor from assigned owner, resolving shared accounts, and reconstructing activity instances should materially change centrality or motif counts if the artifact is important. If validated person-level patterns persist, the artifact explanation weakens.

# 3. Risk assessment

- **Bottleneck risk:** There is credible concern about exception throughput when coordination coverage is missing. Chronic personal overload remains unproven. Routing delays, specialist queues, vendor turnaround, and the difficulty of cases handled must be distinguished before choosing between staffing, routing-rule changes, and downstream capacity improvements.

- **Coordination risk:** Removing or bypassing the desk could increase duplicate referrals, misrouting, fragmented accountability, or unnecessary specialist demand. Conversely, an unnecessary mandatory return through the desk could add delay. Which effect dominates should be tested by case category rather than inferred from centrality.

- **Key-person risk:** The absence evidence and reported tacit knowledge suggest insufficient redundancy. The dependency could reside in Mira’s expertise, permissions, relationships, or an under-resourced function. Merely naming a backup will not solve it unless that person has the knowledge, access, and capacity to act.

- **Compliance/control risk:** Detecting problems and choosing the reviewer creates potential for inconsistent specialist selection, undocumented exceptions, or conflicts with required independence. It is not, by itself, evidence of misconduct or a control violation. Assess actual policy, access rights, selection criteria, conflicts of interest, override records, and who makes final approval decisions. Controls should be proportional to the decision’s consequences.

- **Workload fairness risk:** Raw case counts and median event times can understate cognitive and emotional demands from complaints and complex exceptions. Raw cycle time can also unfairly penalize specialists or Mira for difficult case mix and external delays. Temporary staff may appear peripheral while still triggering substantial exception work. Compare workload using complexity, available hours, hidden coordination effort, and outcomes; avoid interpreting low centrality as low contribution.

- **Data-quality risk:** Inconsistent scopes, denominators, resource identities, lifecycle handling, and activity labels could generate a false organizational diagnosis. Bot throughput and vendor durations also make undifferentiated employee rankings misleading. Until definitions are validated, use the networks to frame hypotheses rather than set individual performance targets or staffing cuts.

# 4. Further process-mining analyses

The raw event log is not supplied, so the following are proposed analyses, not results already established.

## A. Reconstruct a consistent analytical log

Use `pm4py.format_dataframe` with explicit case, activity, resource, and timestamp mappings. Standardization does not itself resolve duplicated events, ambiguous lifecycle records, or resource attribution.

Check resource aliases and shared accounts, missing actors, timestamp precision and time zones, activity vocabulary, and case continuity through reopening. Establish whether start and complete records represent one activity instance; retain the information needed to measure intervals without counting both as separate completed tasks. Flag uncertain ordering for tied or concurrent events.

Reconcile the counts and denominators identified above. Record the observation window, case universe, actor set, normalization, graph direction, and edge-counting convention for every output. Verify whether `exception_flag` and other attributes are stable per case or change over time.

## B. Define cohorts and preserve their sequences

Compare ordinary claims, exception claims, reopened claims, and complaints; stratify further by case type, priority, amount band, region, channel, and calendar period. Distinguish an exception known at routing time from a flag assigned later to avoid using future information as though it were an intake characteristic.

Use `pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values` with explicit event-versus-case retention semantics, `pm4py.filter_variants` for comparable paths, and timestamp-based filtering for before, during, and after the absence. Where the objective is to select cases, retain their full relevant traces.

Filtering events before discovery can create artificial adjacency: deleting Bot_Rules or an intervening activity may manufacture a direct human handover or A–B–A motif. Label deliberately projected sequences and compare them with networks derived from the original event order. Variant restriction is useful descriptively but can remove the very rerouting consequences an intervention causes, so it should not automatically be used for causal adjustment.

## C. Handover-of-work analysis

Use `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` on the validated full log and the defined cohorts. For Mira’s edges, report transition counts, distinct contributing cases, transitions per case, and outgoing destination shares. This separates broad exposure from repeated transfers within a small subset of cases.

Compare the full actor network with a human-only induced graph that removes bot/vendor nodes without inventing replacement edges. If an analysis instead traces the next human actor across automation, state that different meaning explicitly. Keep comparable graph-construction choices across periods, and distinguish edge strength from distance when calculating weighted betweenness.

Use `pm4py.discover_network_analysis` or direct Python aggregation to attach activity-pair and elapsed-time distributions under an explicitly chosen relationship definition. Examine whether Mira bridges organizational units and whether successor destinations change during coverage gaps. Use `pm4py.view_sna` or `pm4py.save_vis_sna` to present comparable graphs with consistent scales and resource-type labels.

## D. Working-together analysis

Use `pm4py.discover_working_together_network` and independently construct a binary case-by-resource participation matrix. Define and compare:

- Global co-case rate: shared cases divided by all eligible cases.
- Conditional participation: shared cases divided by Mira’s cases, or separately by the partner’s cases.
- Jaccard similarity: shared cases divided by cases involving either resource.

Check observed co-occurrence against what resource prevalence and case size would predict, preferably within comparable case types. Bootstrap whole cases to assess uncertainty. Determine whether Mira remains relatively peripheral within exception cases, or whether that appearance results mainly from an all-claims denominator.

This also tests whether North and South communities persist after accounting for region and allocation rules. Co-case structure should not be labeled interpersonal collaboration without corroboration.

## E. Subcontracting analysis

Use `pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network` with the stated n=2 configuration, confirming whether the installed estimator uses exact separation or a wider return convention. Extract the underlying A–B–A fragments to reconcile aggregate frequencies.

Report motif counts, distinct cases, and rates per eligible window or relevant referral, with denominators stated. Inspect activity sequences to distinguish consultation, correction, duplicate recording, and administrative closure. Test several larger event separations and separately examine elapsed-time-bounded returns; event distance is not a measure of duration.

Compare Mira-mediated returns with team-lead and bot-mediated returns. Link patterns to subsequent reassignment, escalation, reopening, and elapsed time. A return associated with better resolution may be a useful consultation; repeated returns without progress may indicate avoidable rework.

## F. Activity similarity and role discovery

Use `pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` and `pm4py.discover_organizational_roles` on validated activity labels. Compare profiles across periods and case categories; supplement frequency-based profiles with distinct-case participation and reliable effort measures.

Inspect whether proposed substitutes cover Mira’s exception activities and specialist interfaces. Check discovered role candidates against documented competencies and permissions. Similarity can identify candidates for training; it cannot certify that a team lead, junior adjuster, or temporary worker can safely perform the same decisions.

## G. Service, waiting, and workload analysis

Determine whether lifecycle transitions allow reliable pairing of activity starts and completions. With valid intervals, use `pm4py.get_service_time` and `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time`, checking the installed functions’ interval and aggregation semantics. If only completion timestamps are reliable, label event gaps as elapsed time and do not claim to have measured active service.

Use assignment, acceptance, start, and completion milestones where available to separate waiting for coordination, specialist queues, active review, external vendor turnaround, and customer response time. Account for calendars and overlapping activities. Summed activity durations can exceed case elapsed time under parallel work; waiting time cannot safely be defined by subtracting that uncorrected sum.

Examine distributions, arrival and completion rates, queue age, and work in progress, especially for exceptions. Attribute delay to measured stages rather than to every person who touched a long case. Do not estimate total workload by multiplying event counts by median service times.

## H. Test absence and control hypotheses

Construct arrival cohorts before, during, and after the absence using eligibility for exception handling, not whether Mira actually touched the case. Otherwise the absence changes the selection rule itself. Include unfinished cases through adequate follow-up or time-to-event methods; completed-case-only analysis may hide the worst backlog.

Adjust comparisons for observable case mix, demand, staffing, weekday effects, specialist availability, and vendor incidents. Ordinary claims can provide context, but a difference-in-differences interpretation requires plausible comparable trends, which have not been shown. Repeated coverage episodes and a planned, qualified-backup pilot would provide stronger evidence than one six-day absence.

Finally, enrich the log with assignment reasons, applicable routing rules, overrides, actual decision-makers, and approval authority. Test conformance with the organization’s documented requirements. Review unusual paths as cases needing explanation, not automatic evidence of wrongdoing.

# 5. Recommendation

## Change immediately

Preserve exception coordination and make its operational ownership explicit while the formal role is clarified. Document the routing criteria, escalation boundaries, specialist contacts, and permissions needed to perform it. Establish trained backup coverage with sufficient capacity and equivalent authorized access, and give the desk a shared, visible exception queue with aging information.

Record who selected a specialist, why, and any rule override. Apply review or independent approval where existing policy and decision risk require it; avoid adding blanket approvals that would simply create another queue. Reconcile the metric definitions and resource attribution before using the supplied rankings for personnel decisions.

## Monitor

Track exception queue age, arrivals and completions, median and tail cycle times, and time spent before and after routing. Break these down by complexity, staffing coverage, team, specialist, and vendor. Track repeated transfers, rejected referrals, specialist demand, reopening, complaints, and routing-rule exceptions alongside service quality.

Evaluate backup coverage through comparable case cohorts and sufficient follow-up. Combine event-log measures with staff evidence about unrecorded coordination effort and capacity. A successful change should improve resilience without increasing errors, unnecessary specialist referrals, or unfair workload concentration.

## Defer until more evidence is available

Do not remove Mira, infer a management title from centrality, reduce staffing from short recorded durations, or automate discretionary routing solely from these summaries. Do not assume direct access to specialists will improve flow: it may increase their workload and fragment coordination.

Consider redistributing or automating well-defined categories only through a monitored pilot. Any redesign that reduces Mira’s involvement must explicitly replace her routing knowledge, exception ownership, specialist coordination, and coverage responsibilities with trained people or a validated process. Expand it only when evidence shows acceptable quality, controls, capacity, and cycle time.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira appears to provide an exception-handling coordination function, although the evidence does not establish her formal role. She routes difficult claims across teams and specialists and often receives their responses. That can make her prominent in handover and return patterns while leaving her outside stable teams that repeatedly handle claims together.

Her absence coincided with a substantial increase in delays for exception cases, suggesting a coverage or knowledge dependency. It does not prove that she personally causes delays or lacks capacity. Her discretion over specialist selection warrants documented rules and review, but the log provides no evidence of misconduct.

Management should preserve the coordination function, establish trained backup coverage, document routing decisions, and monitor exception queues and outcomes. Before changing staffing or automating the role, validate the inconsistent network counts and compare similar claims over time. Any redesign should demonstrate that another person or process can reliably replace her coordination work.
