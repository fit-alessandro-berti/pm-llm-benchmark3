# 1. Objectives and constraints

The policy is a rolling decision aid, not a claim of a single mathematical optimum. It should be recalculated when availability, queue age, absence, or SLA forecasts change. Its purpose is to balance timely completion with quality, sustainable work, learning, and continuity.

## Objectives

The policy jointly seeks to:

1. **Protect SLA performance**, especially urgent and high-priority cases.
2. **Maintain service quality**, reducing skill mismatch, rework, and appeals.
3. **Preserve case continuity** where a handover would be costly or disruptive.
4. **Distribute work sustainably**, considering current workload, remaining hours, and overtime risk.
5. **Develop capability**, using supervised, low-risk work to support documented training priorities.
6. **Avoid brittle specialist bottlenecks**, especially in Security Review and Final Approval.
7. **Make decisions explainable**, with a recorded reason, alternatives considered, supervisor where applicable, and policy version.

Historical evidence should be used as a calibrated warning, not as an automatic rule:

- A third or later reassignment is especially undesirable because cases with more than two reassignments historically have higher rework and appeal rates.
- A high-complexity owner change should include an expected waiting-time penalty, informed by the historical median of 2.7 hours.
- Retaining a weakly skilled owner can be worse than a handover if it adds the observed 3.3-hour service-time penalty and rework risk.
- Routing every case to a specialist may reduce immediate errors but can create Security and Approval queues.

## Safety and compliance constraints

These are non-negotiable. The policy must not relax them automatically.

- A resource with skill level **0** for an activity is ineligible.
- **Security Review**
  - Skill level 3 may perform independently.
  - Skill level 1 or 2 may perform only with explicit, recorded supervision by `R7_Gita` or `R8_Hugo`.
  - A supervisor must be available, capable of reviewing the work, and named in the assignment record. Nominal supervision is not sufficient.
- **Final Approval**
  - The final decision must be performed by skill level 2 or 3.
  - Skill level 1 may prepare evidence or documentation but may not finalize.
- **Urgent and high-priority work**
  - Must be assigned to skill level 2 or above, unless an explicit, competent supervisor is assigned.
- The assigned resource and supervisor must have enough forecast capacity to begin or complete the relevant work within the SLA plan.
- An exception may never be used to bypass Security or Final Approval qualification rules.

## Binding operating guardrails

- No resource receives more than **four assignments in the decision batch**, including substantive supervised trainee work.
- Quality Check batching is allowed only where:
  - priority is normal or low, and
  - SLA time remaining is greater than 24 hours.
- At least **20% of normal/low-priority assignments** should support a documented training priority, provided SLA and quality forecasts remain acceptable.
  - If this is infeasible, the policy records a training-quota waiver rather than silently assigning unsafe trainee work.
- Resources with `overtime risk = high` should not receive new work unless:
  - the case is urgent, and
  - no safer eligible option is available.
  - Continuing an already-owned case is different from giving that person additional new work, but must still be monitored for overload.
- A high-complexity, high-handover-cost case should normally stay with its owner. An override requires a documented reason such as mandatory qualification, SLA infeasibility, or an unavailable supervisor.

## Preference trade-offs

After the hard constraints are satisfied, the policy weighs the following preferences:

- Meet SLA with the least likely waiting time.
- Prefer competent skill level 2 where it is sufficient; do not send every routine task to a level-3 specialist.
- Give additional value to level 3 for high complexity, urgent work, rare skills, and cases with substantial quality risk.
- Preserve ownership when case complexity, handover cost, or reassignment count is high.
- Avoid giving extra work to people with high open-case counts or high overtime risk.
- Create protected, supervised training assignments for documented development goals.
- Preserve scarce Security and Approval capacity for work that genuinely needs it.
- Prefer stable teams and known handover paths where the performance data supports this, without treating social-network proximity as a substitute for competence.

# 2. Assignment policy

## Decision logic

For each queued case \(c\), estimate the completion risk for each feasible resource \(r\):

\[
\widehat{T}_{c,r} =
\widehat{Q}_{r} +
\widehat{S}_{activity, skill, complexity} +
\widehat{H}_{c,r}
\]

Where:

- \(\widehat{Q}_{r}\): forecast wait caused by already allocated urgent work, open cases, and available hours.
- \(\widehat{S}\): expected service time, learned from historical cases by activity, complexity band, and skill level.
- \(\widehat{H}\): expected handover delay. It is near zero for continuity, but increases for owner changes, especially when complexity and handover cost are high.

A practical SLA urgency measure is:

\[
U_c =
P_c \times
\left(1 + \max\left(0,
\frac{\widehat{T}_{c,\min} - SLA_c}{\max(\widehat{T}_{c,\min},1)}
\right)\right)
\]

where \(P_c\) is a priority multiplier, for example urgent = 4, high = 3, normal = 2, low = 1.

Cases are ordered primarily by predicted lateness and priority, not merely by queue order.

## Candidate score

For eligible candidates, use a published scorecard rather than an opaque model:

\[
Score(c,r) =
-w_L \cdot LatenessRisk
+w_M \cdot SkillMatch
+w_C \cdot Continuity
+w_F \cdot Fairness
+w_D \cdot Development
+w_R \cdot Resilience
\]

The weights should be reviewed by operations, workers, quality, and compliance representatives. SLA and safety terms receive much more weight for urgent cases.

### Score components

| Component | Meaning |
|---|---|
| SLA urgency and lateness risk | Strongly favors an assignment whose predicted start and completion time preserve SLA slack. |
| Skill match | Skill 2 is normally competent; skill 3 receives extra value for high complexity, urgent cases, scarce skills, and quality-sensitive work. Skill 1 requires supervision. |
| Continuity and handover cost | Rewards retaining the owner when complexity, handover cost, or reassignment count is high. Penalizes a change likely to create waiting or rework. |
| Workload and overtime fairness | Rewards lower normalized workload and sufficient hours; strongly penalizes assigning new work to high-overtime-risk resources. |
| Training and development | Rewards documented trainee assignments only when a qualified supervisor is available and projected SLA slack remains acceptable. |
| Resilience and bottleneck avoidance | Penalizes using scarce Security or Approval specialists for work that a competent alternative can perform; rewards development of safe backup capacity. |

The system should store the component values used for the decision, not only the final score.

## Pseudocode

```text
INPUT:
    queue cases C
    resources R
    skill matrix
    live workload and availability
    historical service, handover, rework, and SLA estimates

1. Refresh state
    - Remove unavailable resources.
    - Estimate queue delay and service time by activity, complexity, and skill.
    - Identify current owners, reassignment counts, handover-cost bands.
    - Reserve capacity for already committed urgent work.
    - Identify eligible Quality Check batch candidates.

2. Build valid assignment options for each case
    FOR each case c:
        FOR each resource r:
            skill = skill_level(r, c.required_skill)

            Reject if skill == 0.

            IF c.activity == "Security Review":
                Allow independent execution only if skill == 3.
                Allow skill 1 or 2 only with available supervisor R7_Gita or R8_Hugo.

            IF c.activity == "Final Approval":
                Allow finalization only if skill >= 2.
                Allow skill 1 only as a preparation role paired with a level-2-or-3 finalizer.

            IF c.priority in {urgent, high} AND skill < 2:
                Require explicit competent supervision.

            Reject if resource would exceed four assignments in this batch.

            Reject or defer if predicted capacity is infeasible.

            If overtime risk is high and r is not the current owner:
                Reject unless case is urgent and no safer eligible option exists.

            If c is high complexity and high handover cost and r is not current owner:
                Require an override reason:
                    - current owner has skill 0 or 1 and no SLA-feasible supervision exists, or
                    - mandatory qualification/SLA risk makes continuity unsafe.

            Add valid independent or supervised option.

3. Score options
    - Calculate urgency and predicted lateness.
    - Calculate skill, continuity, fairness, development, and resilience scores.
    - Apply large penalties for predicted SLA breach, unsafe overload, or avoidable handover.

4. Allocate in risk order
    - Allocate urgent predicted-late cases first.
    - Reserve Security and Approval specialists for work that requires them.
    - Allocate high-priority cases next.
    - Jointly allocate eligible Quality Check batches.
    - Allocate normal/low work while satisfying the supervised-training target.

5. Improve the draft
    - Run local swaps if they improve fairness or resilience without reducing SLA slack,
      violating continuity protections, or breaking a hard rule.
    - Verify no resource exceeds four assignments.

6. Produce an explanation record
    For each decision, store:
        assignment_reason
        policy_version
        primary reasons
        rejected alternatives
        skill level
        supervisor, if any
        predicted SLA slack
        handover decision
        workload and overtime indicators
        exception approval, if any

7. Dispatch and monitor
    - Recalculate when a worker becomes unavailable, an SLA forecast worsens,
      a supervisor cannot support the trainee, or the batch is delayed.
```

## Tie-breaking

If two candidates are similarly safe and SLA-feasible:

1. Keep the current owner if continuity benefit is material.
2. Prefer lower normalized workload, not simply fewer open cases.
3. Prefer lower overtime risk.
4. Prefer the resource with fewer assignments in the current batch.
5. Prefer the option that preserves a scarce specialist for scarce work.
6. Use a rotating, deterministic tie-break rather than alphabetical order, so the same people are not repeatedly favored or burdened.

## Exception handling

- If there is no eligible Security or Approval resource, escalate to a human duty manager; do not assign an unqualified substitute.
- If an urgent case has no safe option, record an `SLA_EXCEPTION_ESCALATION` reason and seek temporary qualified support.
- If supervision is unavailable, the trainee assignment is removed rather than left nominally supervised.
- Workers may correct stale availability, workload, skill, or ownership information before the assignment is finalized.
- Exceptions are reviewed as system-design signals, not presumed worker failures.

# 4. Comparison with baseline policies

The comparisons below are expected tendencies, not guaranteed outcomes. They depend on accurate workload and service-time estimates.

| Dimension | First-available routing | Specialist-only routing | Proposed dynamic policy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expected throughput | Often high immediate dispatch because low-load workers are selected quickly. End-to-end throughput can fall if mismatches create rework and handovers. | Can be slow when Security, Approval, Diagnosis, or Quality specialists accumulate queues. | Aims for good end-to-end throughput by using competent level-2 staff where safe, batching eligible Quality work, and avoiding avoidable rework. |
| SLA risk | Can reduce average waiting time, but urgent work may go to a weakly matched person and require later correction. | Strong on task quality but can create SLA risk through specialist queues. | Prioritizes predicted lateness, preserves urgent specialist capacity, and uses continuity unless it makes the SLA unsafe. |
| Error and rework risk | Higher risk because it ignores specialization except skill 0. Literal first-available routing can violate Security or high-priority supervision rules. | Usually lower immediate mismatch risk, but long waits and unnecessary handovers can still cause errors or customer dissatisfaction. | Uses competency thresholds, supervision, complexity, and handover cost. It explicitly avoids a third handover on C003 and further handover on C007. |
| Fairness of workload | May equalize simple open-case counts but ignores overtime risk, complexity, and invisible supervisory work. | Can overload a small number of specialists while underusing competent resources. | Uses normalized workload, remaining hours, overtime risk, and a four-assignment cap. It moves work from Bruno, Iris, and David where safe. |
| Skill development | Accidental exposure, often without adequate supervision or a documented learning objective. | Limited development because specialists keep receiving the work. | Uses planned, supervised training for Amal and Farid on Diagnosis work, with stop rules if SLA risk rises. |
| Continuity of ownership | Often poor because the next available worker may not be the owner. | Often poor when the owner is not the highest-scoring specialist. | Continuity is a major factor, particularly for C003, C007, and C016. Exceptions are documented, as in C001. |
| Resilience to absence or bottlenecks | Broad but unmanaged; may appear resilient while actually creating unsafe skill use. | Fragile because queues and knowledge concentrate in a few specialists. | Preserves scarce Security and Approval capacity, uses competent non-specialists where appropriate, and grows capability through supervised work. |

A literal first-available baseline is not deployable without a compliance wrapper because it can assign Security work to a level-1 or level-2 resource without explicit supervision. A literal specialist-only baseline is compliant more often, but it creates a predictable concentration risk in Security and Approval.

The proposed policy accepts some short-term efficiency losses: structured handovers, supervision time, and avoiding overloading high-overtime-risk resources. The expected return is lower rework, fewer disruptive handovers, more sustainable workloads, and improved future capability.

# 5. Explanation to workers

Assignments are made to help cases move safely and fairly, not to rank people or punish anyone for workload, absence, or past outcomes.

For each case, we look at:

- what skill the work requires;
- how close the case is to its deadline;
- whether someone already knows the case;
- whether changing owner would make the customer wait or increase rework;
- who has capacity today and who is already at overtime risk;
- whether the work is a safe, supervised opportunity for a documented development goal; and
- whether assigning the work would create a bottleneck for Security, Approval, or another scarce skill.

Keeping a case with its owner usually means we are avoiding the customer having to repeat information and avoiding unnecessary rework. It does not mean that person must carry the case alone: peer review, structured handover, or support can be requested.

Moving a case does not imply that the owner did something wrong. A move may happen because another person has a mandatory qualification, because the SLA is at risk, or because the current owner is already overloaded.

Training assignments are protected assignments. A trainee should know who is supervising, what part of the task they are responsible for, and when they should escalate. Training is not used as a reason to give someone risky work without support.

Workers can challenge or correct an assignment if the system has outdated information about availability, workload, skills, customer context, or case ownership. Managers must record exceptions and explain them. The policy should use workload information to protect people from unsustainable work, not as a productivity scorecard.

# 6. Process-mining audit indicators

The audit should distinguish between policy compliance, operational outcomes, and possible unintended consequences. Outcomes should be reviewed by case complexity, priority, activity, team, and handover-cost band; raw averages alone can be misleading.

| Indicator | Definition | Audit use |
|---|---|---|
| Handover frequency | Number of owner changes divided by cases, and handovers per 100 activities. Break down by activity, complexity band, and priority. | Detect whether the policy actually reduces unnecessary transfers, especially for high-complexity cases. |
| Reassignment count | Distribution of `reassignment_count`; share of cases with more than 2 reassigments; median reassignment count by activity. | Verify whether repeated handovers decline and whether C003/C007-like patterns are being controlled. |
| Skill mismatch | Compare assigned resource skill with `required_skill`. Report skill-0 assignments, level-1 assignments, supervisor presence, and level-2-or-3 assignments. | Detect non-compliant Security, Approval, and high-priority assignments. Separate supervised trainee work from unsafe mismatch. |
| Workload distribution | New assignments, active open cases, estimated work hours, and assignment-cap breaches by resource and team. Use normalized workload and dispersion measures such as percentile spread or Gini coefficient. | Check that workload is sustainable rather than merely evenly counted. |
| Overtime-risk exposure | Share of assignments sent to high-overtime-risk staff, stratified by priority and whether a safer eligible alternative existed. | Detect whether exceptions are becoming routine or whether staffing capacity is inadequate. |
| SLA misses | Case and activity completion after `sla_deadline`; predicted-risk calibration; misses by activity, priority, skill level, and handover status. | Test whether urgency scoring is useful and identify systematic bottlenecks. |
| Rework and appeal after assignment | Probability of `Rework Correction` or appeal-related activity after an assignment. Compare cohorts by number of handovers, skill match, complexity, and ownership change. | Test the historical claim that excessive reassignment is associated with higher rework and appeals. This is association analysis, not proof of causation. |
| Training assignment share | Number of normal/low assignments where a documented trainee performs substantive work under recorded supervision, divided by all normal/low assignments. | Verify the 20% target and prevent superficial “training” labels without real trainee participation. |
| Training outcomes | Subsequent independent skill progression, rework rate, supervisor review completion, and SLA impact for trainee assignments. | Ensure development is safe and useful rather than only quota-driven. |
| Specialist bottleneck indicators | Security and Approval queue length, waiting time, utilization, specialist share of work, eligible-resource count, and resource-concentration index such as HHI. | Identify queue concentration and resilience risk. |
| Continuity benefit | Waiting and rework for retained-owner versus changed-owner cases, stratified by complexity and handover-cost band. | Check whether continuity rules create the expected benefit and where they should be adjusted. |
| Batch effectiveness | Quality Check batch size, batch waiting time, service time, SLA compliance, and error rate compared with non-batched Quality work. | Ensure batching improves efficiency without hiding delay. |
| Deviations from assignment policy | Percentage of assignments that differ from the highest-ranked eligible option or violate a guardrail; reasons, approver, and policy version. | Distinguish legitimate human overrides from recurring policy or data problems. |
| Network concentration | Handover-network centrality, working-together patterns, and activity-based resource similarity. | Identify dependence on a small number of people and opportunities for safe cross-training. |

For reliable auditing, the event log should retain or add:

- `policy_version`
- `decision_id`
- `assigned_resource`
- `supervisor_resource`
- `assignment_reason`
- `candidate_count`
- `predicted_sla_slack`
- `skill_at_assignment`
- `exception_code`
- `exception_approver`
- an immutable workload snapshot at assignment time

Audit findings should be used for process improvement and staffing design, not as automatic individual performance judgments. A high rework rate may reflect case mix, poor upstream data, tools, customer delays, or policy design rather than worker behavior.

# 7. PM4Py implementation sketch

The following is an illustrative sketch. Exact PM4Py signatures vary by release, so the installed PM4Py version should be checked. The event log needs complete timestamps and ideally lifecycle start/complete events to distinguish service time from waiting time reliably.

```python
import json
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

CASE = "case:concept:name"
ACTIVITY = "concept:name"
TIME = "time:timestamp"
RESOURCE = "org:resource"

events = pd.read_parquet("service_event_log.parquet")
events[TIME] = pd.to_datetime(events[TIME], utc=True)

required = [
    CASE, ACTIVITY, TIME, RESOURCE,
    "required_skill", "case_priority", "sla_deadline",
    "case_complexity", "current_owner", "assignment_reason",
    "reassignment_count", "workload_snapshot", "handover_cost_band"
]
missing = set(required) - set(events.columns)
if missing:
    raise ValueError(f"Missing log columns: {missing}")

elog = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    events.copy(),
    case_id=CASE,
    activity_key=ACTIVITY,
    timestamp_key=TIME
).sort_values([CASE, TIME])

handover_sna = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(elog)
working_together_sna = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(elog)
resource_similarity_sna = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(elog)
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(elog)

pm4py.save_vis_sna(handover_sna, "handover_network.html")
pm4py.save_vis_sna(working_together_sna, "working_together_network.html")

performance_dfg = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(elog)
variants = pm4py.get_variants(elog)

service_time = pm4py.get_service_time(elog)
case_times = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(elog)

urgent_or_high = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    elog,
    "case_priority",
    ["urgent", "high"],
    level="case",
    retain=True
)

short_cases = pm4py.filter_case_performance(
    elog,
    min_performance=0,
    max_performance=48 * 60 * 60
)

quality_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    elog,
    ACTIVITY,
    ["Quality Check"],
    level="event",
    retain=True
)

quality_batches = pm4py.discover_batches(
    quality_log,
    merge_distance=15 * 60,
    min_batch_size=2
)
```

The resource-network outputs should be treated as diagnostic evidence:

- `discover_handover_of_work_network` identifies frequent transfer paths and possible handover dependencies.
- `discover_working_together_network` identifies collaboration patterns, but should not be used to infer competence.
- `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` helps identify resources with similar historical activity profiles.
- `discover_organizational_roles` helps validate or update role definitions.
- `discover_performance_dfg` identifies slow transitions and loops such as repeated Diagnosis-to-Rework patterns.
- `get_variants` identifies common and exceptional case paths.
- `discover_batches` supports monitoring whether Quality Check batching is beneficial.

A policy engine can use a separate skill matrix and live resource state:

```python
skills = {
    ("R1_Amal", "Diagnosis"): 1,
    ("R3_Celine", "Diagnosis"): 3,
    ("R4_David", "Diagnosis"): 3,
    ("R5_Eva", "Diagnosis"): 2,
    ("R6_Farid", "Approval"): 3,
    ("R7_Gita", "Security"): 3,
    ("R8_Hugo", "Security"): 3,
    ("R10_Jonas", "Quality"): 3,
}

priority_weight = {"urgent": 4, "high": 3, "normal": 2, "low": 1}

def handover_hours(case_row, resource):
    owner = case_row["current_owner"]
    if pd.isna(owner) or owner in ("none", None) or resource == owner:
        return 0.0

    if (
        case_row["handover_cost_band"] == "high"
        and case_row["case_complexity"] >= 0.75
    ):
        return 2.7

    if case_row["handover_cost_band"] == "medium":
        return 1.0

    return 0.3

def security_supervisor_available(state):
    for supervisor in ["R7_Gita", "R8_Hugo"]:
        if state["available"].get(supervisor, False):
            if state["batch_assignments"].get(supervisor, 0) < 4:
                return supervisor
    return None

def legal_option(case_row, resource, state):
    activity = case_row[ACTIVITY]
    required_skill = case_row["required_skill"]
    level = skills.get((resource, required_skill), 0)
    priority = case_row["case_priority"]

    if level == 0:
        return None

    supervisor = None

    if activity == "Security Review":
        if level == 3:
            pass
        elif level in (1, 2):
            supervisor = security_supervisor_available(state)
            if supervisor is None:
                return None
        else:
            return None

    if activity == "Final Approval" and level < 2:
        return None

    if priority in {"urgent", "high"} and level < 2:
        supervisor = supervisor or state["competent_supervisor"].get(
            (resource, required_skill)
        )
        if supervisor is None:
            return None

    if state["batch_assignments"].get(resource, 0) >= 4:
        return None

    is_new_owner = resource != case_row["current_owner"]
    high_overtime = state["overtime_risk"].get(resource) == "high"

    if is_new_owner and high_overtime and priority != "urgent":
        return None

    return {
        "resource": resource,
        "supervisor": supervisor,
        "skill_level": level
    }

def score_option(case_row, option, state, service_forecast):
    resource = option["resource"]
    level = option["skill_level"]

    wait = state["forecast_queue_hours"].get(resource, 0.0)
    service = service_forecast(case_row, resource, level)
    handover = handover_hours(case_row, resource)

    deadline = pd.to_datetime(case_row["sla_deadline"], utc=True)
    hours_to_sla = max(
        0.0,
        (deadline - state["now"]).total_seconds() / 3600
    )

    predicted_hours = wait + service + handover
    lateness = max(0.0, predicted_hours - hours_to_sla)

    skill_score = min(level, 2) / 2
    if level == 3 and (
        case_row["case_complexity"] >= 0.65
        or case_row["case_priority"] in {"urgent", "high"}
    ):
        skill_score += 0.3

    continuity = 1.0 if resource == case_row["current_owner"] else -handover
    workload = -state["normalized_load"].get(resource, 0.0)
    overtime_penalty = -2.0 if state["overtime_risk"].get(resource) == "high" else 0.0

    training_score = 0.0
    if (
        case_row["case_priority"] in {"normal", "low"}
        and state["training_priority"].get(resource) == case_row["required_skill"]
        and option["supervisor"] is not None
        and lateness == 0
    ):
        training_score = 0.8

    resilience_score = -state["scarce_skill_pressure"].get(
        (resource, case_row["required_skill"]), 0.0
    )

    urgency = priority_weight[case_row["case_priority"]]

    return (
        -10.0 * urgency * lateness
        + 2.0 * skill_score
        + 2.0 * continuity
        + 1.5 * workload
        + overtime_penalty
        + training_score
        + resilience_score
    )
```

Assignment auditing can be performed from recorded assignment decisions. The current schema has `assignment_reason`, but supervised work should also record a `supervisor_resource` field.

```python
assignment_events = elog[
    elog["assignment_reason"].notna()
].copy()

assignment_events["owner_change"] = (
    assignment_events["current_owner"].notna()
    & assignment_events["current_owner"].ne("none")
    & assignment_events[RESOURCE].ne(assignment_events["current_owner"])
)

handover_rate = assignment_events["owner_change"].mean()

def skill_for_row(row):
    return skills.get((row[RESOURCE], row["required_skill"]), 0)

assignment_events["assigned_skill_level"] = assignment_events.apply(

unsafe_skill_zero = (
    assignment_events["assigned_skill_level"] == 0

high_overtime_assignments = assignment_events[
    assignment_events[RESOURCE].map(

training_assignments = assignment_events[
    assignment_events["assignment_reason"].str.contains(

    )
]

normal_low = assignment_events[
    assignment_events["case_priority"].isin(["normal", "low"])
]

training_share = (
    training_assignments.index.intersection(normal_low.index).size
    / max(len(normal_low), 1)
)

case_end = elog.groupby(CASE)[TIME].max().rename("case_end")
case_deadline = (
    elog.sort_values([CASE, TIME])
    .groupby(CASE)["sla_deadline"]

sla_audit = pd.concat([case_end, case_deadline], axis=1)
```

Finally, the policy should write a decision event or decision table for every assignment. That record makes it possible to compare:

- what the policy recommended;
- what was actually assigned;
- whether an override occurred;
- whether the supervisor was present;
- whether SLA, handover, fairness, and training outcomes matched the expected trade-off.
