## 1. Analytical assumptions

A case notion defines whose history a trace represents. For a flattening on type T, each object of T is one case. Its trace contains each event directly related to that object, once, in timestamp order. The simultaneous closes e29 and e30 belong to different orders and impose no order on each other. A directly-follows graph (DFG) edge means that two activities are consecutive in at least one projected trace. An intervening event on another object can be invisible in that trace.

**Convergence** occurs when one event participates in several cases and flattening copies it across them. **Divergence** occurs when a case combines distinguishable threads, such as two packages of one order, so their interleaving can appear to be a single workflow. Repetition alone establishes neither rework nor error. Copies are evidence when counting the orders served by e16, and duplication when counting dispatch operations. I retain the source event ID and distinguish participating cases, case-event rows, and distinct events throughout.

The baseline uses direct E2O links. I use qualified O2O paths to interpret events and define milestones, such as the last confirmation covering an order’s items. I would add derived E2O links only in a separately identified sensitivity run, with a specified path, eligible activities, and provenance. All durations below are elapsed clock time between recorded milestones.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

1. **P3 as two packing jobs (Order and Item).** The single e13 packs I21 for O2 together with I31 for O3. Both item traces have Add Item → Pack Package, from e07 and e08 respectively into the *same* e13. A supervisor can count two items handled or two orders touched. Charging two parcel-packing operations would inflate workload. The Package case P3 contributes one operation.

2. **P4 apparently dispatched on D2 (Order).** In O3, e14→e16 gives a 45-minute Pack Package → Dispatch edge. The pack concerns P4, but e16 carries P2 and P3. P4’s dispatch is e17, 55 minutes after e14. O4 shares e14 and sees e17 next. Using the O3 edge for parcel waiting time gives a wrong answer even though its endpoints and timestamps are genuine.

3. **D2 counted at different weights (Delivery, Package, Item).** The pair e16→e24 contributes one transit observation for D2, two for P2/P3, and three for I12/I21/I31. With D1 at 3 h, D2 at 4 h 55 min, and D3 at 22 h 45 min, the delivery mean is 10 h 13 min 20 s. The package mean is 8 h 53 min 45 s because D2 carries two packages. Both describe service to their respective units. Substituting the latter for the mean over deliveries understates that KPI.

4. **Consolidated billing looks like independent invoices (Order).** Both O2 and O3 have Issue Invoice → Record Payment from e19 to e22. Counting the two edges as two invoices receiving a first payment doubles one V2 observation. Counting two orders covered by the first receipt is informative. Neither that edge nor the bills links supply an amount allocable to either order.

5. **Partial payment mistaken for completion or rework (Invoice).** On V2, first receipt is 35 minutes after issue. Full settlement takes 20 h 55 min. Treating e22 as terminal drops 20 h 20 min. Conversely, flagging e22→e25 as a duplicate collection solely because the labels repeat would misclassify two receipts. The partially_settles link from M2 and completes_settlement link from M3 identify their roles.

6. **A singleton looks instantaneous (Payment).** M3 appears only at e25. A duration calculation gives zero and an activity-variant count groups it with M1, M2, and M4. This is informative about recording coverage. It gives no observed start for authorizing, initiating, or processing M3. Subtracting e22 from e25 measures the gap between V2 instalments, a different quantity.

7. **A split order looks redelivered (Order).** O1 has e23→e24, a Confirm Delivery → Confirm Delivery self-loop two hours apart. D1 confirms P1/I11, then D2 confirms P2/I12. The loop is informative for partial fulfilment. Interpreting it as a retry manufactures a failed delivery. Ending O1’s physical-fulfilment clock at e23 understates time to receipt of all its items by two hours.

8. **Payment appears to trigger transport (Order).** In O3, e24→e25→e27 yields Confirm Delivery → Record Payment → Confirm Delivery across D2, V2, and D3. An order-status timeline benefits from this sequence. Treating it as a release rule for D3 is harmful. D3 was already dispatched at e17 on the preceding day, and the snapshot records no dependency of confirmation on settlement.

9. **A uniform item variant hides an order-level wait (Item).** I31 finishes at e24, while its sibling I32 finishes at e27 the next day. Both have exactly the same four activity labels. A variant-based comparison can legitimately report identical step sequences. Treating either item as representative of O3 completion misses an 18-hour gap between its first and last physical receipts, followed by a separate administrative close at e30.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

I would keep Order for create-to-close and first/last receipt per order, Item for add-to-confirm service, Package for pack-to-dispatch wait, Delivery for dispatch-to-confirm transit, and Invoice for issue-to-first-receipt and issue-to-settlement. The invoice calculation joins the terminal event to its qualified payment relation. Payment needs only a case-indexed extract for receipt counts and times. Mining a log skeleton on the richer projections can describe regularities in these cases, such as the sequence shared by all six items. Four orders give little basis for declaring a business rule.

Use an **OC-DFG** to ask which object lifecycles support a transition. For example, dispatch directly precedes confirmation on D2 and P2/P3, while invoicing and payment intervene for the orders involved. Use the **ET–OT graph** to inspect activity coverage by object type. Record Payment involves Order, Invoice, and Payment here, which explains why searching the Package log for a payment delay fails. Use the **OTG** to examine the structure of associations among types, then an **object interaction summary** and event IDs to identify the actual participants. To establish what D2 carried for O3, follow D2→P3→I31 and O3→I31. Mere co-participation of O3 and P2 in e16 would assign the wrong parcel. Qualified O2O links also distinguish V2 billing two orders from M2 partially settling one invoice.

Equivalent-execution clustering would address whether the same split and consolidation structures recur across executions. In this snapshot, D2 connects O1, O2, and O3, and P4 connects O3 to O4. Taking connected components as executions would yield only one component. A claim about recurrent structures would need a defensible execution boundary and multiple comparable executions.

For a disagreement, restate both metrics with their unit, endpoints, and denominator, then trace contributing rows back to event IDs and qualified paths. Six order-case dispatch rows reconcile to e15 once, e16 three times, and e17 twice. Deduplicating gives three dispatch operations. Counting distinct orders gives four orders served. If a claimed discrepancy survives that reconciliation, check whether the two projections made different events adjacent, as with P4 and e16.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**C. Reject a single lead object for this process.**

- **Decision criteria.** An end-to-end analysis must identify customer completion, physical movement, and financial settlement with defensible counts and endpoints in each. It must also explain which orders share a movement or liability.
- **Strongest evidence.** D2 serves O1/O2/O3, V2 bills O2/O3, and D3 serves O3/O4. These groupings cross. O3 participates in both deliveries and in V2, yet a single O3 trace makes adjacent events out of different parcel histories. A delivery or invoice lead would lose the other milestones altogether.
- **Strongest counterargument.** Order reaches all 31 original events across its four cases and captures the customer-facing span. For example, e01–e28 gives 26 hours for O1. That supports an order-level completion KPI. The same projection contributes three copies of e16 to a dispatch count, which makes it a poor authority for shared operational work.
- **Residual risk.** Multiple views can leave stakeholders using “complete” inconsistently. Report O3’s physical completion at e27, its billed invoice’s settlement at e25, and its administrative close at e30 as separately named milestones, preserving their source IDs.
- **Change condition.** If the mandate narrowed to elapsed time per customer order and specified its completion milestone, I would use Order as lead for that mandate. Consolidated shipments and invoices would still need their own IDs for attribution.

## 6. PM4Py analysis outline

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

print(ocel)
eid, oid = ocel.event_id_column, ocel.object_id_column
activity, typ = ocel.event_activity, ocel.object_type_column
types = ("Order", "Item", "Package", "Delivery", "Invoice", "Payment")

events = ocel.events.drop_duplicates(eid)
objects = ocel.objects.drop_duplicates(oid)
activity_frequencies = events[activity].value_counts()
object_type_frequencies = objects[typ].value_counts()
print(activity_frequencies, object_type_frequencies, sep="\n")

# Count baseline participation once per event and object, regardless of qualifier.
links = ocel.relations[[eid, oid, typ]].drop_duplicates()
participation = links.groupby(typ).agg(
    case_event_rows=(eid, "size"), distinct_events=(eid, "nunique"),
    participating_cases=(oid, "nunique")
)
participation["extra_rows"] = participation.case_event_rows - participation.distinct_events
print(participation)

flat_logs, activity_dfgs, start_counts, end_counts, log_skeletons = {}, {}, {}, {}, {}
for object_type in types:
    flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type)
    flat_logs[object_type] = flat
    assert len(flat) == participation.loc[object_type, "case_event_rows"]
    dfg, starts, ends = pm4py.discover_dfg(flat)
    activity_dfgs[object_type] = dfg
    start_counts[object_type], end_counts[object_type] = starts, ends
    if object_type != "Payment":
        log_skeletons[object_type] = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(flat)

oc_dfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
object_type_graph = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
event_type_object_type_graph = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)
object_interactions = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)

# Audit transit with original event pairs as well as the number of cases served.
# Within each case the timestamps in this snapshot are distinct.
time = ocel.event_timestamp
histories = links.merge(events[[eid, activity, time]], on=eid, validate="many_to_one")
histories = histories.sort_values([typ, oid, time, eid]).copy()
following = histories.groupby([typ, oid])[[eid, activity, time]].shift(-1)
transit = histories.loc[
    histories[activity].eq("Dispatch") & following[activity].eq("Confirm Delivery")
].copy()
transit["confirm_event"] = following.loc[transit.index, eid]
transit["elapsed"] = pd.to_datetime(following.loc[transit.index, time]) - pd.to_datetime(transit[time])
pair_audit = transit.groupby([typ, eid, "confirm_event"]).agg(
    cases_served=(oid, "nunique"), elapsed=("elapsed", "first")
)
print(pair_audit)
d2_case_counts = pair_audit.xs(("e16", "e24"), level=[eid, "confirm_event"])
assert d2_case_counts["cases_served"].to_dict() == {"Delivery": 1, "Package": 2, "Item": 3}
```

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

1. **Change the weight of shared events.** For activity workload, give each Order row for event e weight 1 divided by its number of directly related orders. The three e16 rows then sum to one dispatch and the six dispatch rows sum to three operations. For a DFG, also count distinct source *pairs* supporting an edge. Deleting e16 from two arbitrary traces would create new adjacencies and erase evidence of service to those orders.

2. **Vary relation propagation.** In a marked copy, link Create Order to items via Order–contains–Item, and Issue Invoice/Record Payment to items via Invoice–bills–Order–contains–Item. Recompute coverage, variants, and start/end times from the original events. I31 would start at e03 instead of e08 and gain e25 after its physical receipt at e24. That extension quantifies how much the item history depends on inherited financial context. It cannot establish the amount paid for I31.

3. **Change the analysis anchor.** Recompute O3’s first and last physical receipt from item and delivery memberships and compare them with the Order trace. I31/D2 gives e24 and I32/D3 gives e27. Switch to Invoice and V2 ends at e25. Matching those IDs validates the joins. The three endpoint times answer different completion questions.

4. **Compare OC-DFG frequency definitions on the same typed edge.** For Item Dispatch → Confirm Delivery there are three distinct event pairs, six unique items, and six total item occurrences. For Order Record Payment → Confirm Delivery there are four pairs (e21→e23, e22→e24, e25→e27, e26→e27), four unique orders, and five occurrences. O2/O3 share one pair and O3 contributes twice. These checks distinguish the event-based edge metric from unique-object reach and total object participations, including repeated participation by one object.

5. **Vary the completion rule while holding the denominator fixed.** For each invoice compare time to first receipt with time to the payment qualified as settling or completing settlement. V1 and V3 have one receipt each, so their times should be invariant. Only V2 should change, by 20 h 20 min. Any change for V1 or V3 would point to a join or endpoint-selection error.
