# 1. Data audit and estimands

**Opening-hours convention:** 07:00–19:00 each day, i.e., \(H=12\) open hours and \(720\) open minutes. All rates below are **per open hour** unless stated otherwise.

## Observed daily aggregates (given)

For each day \(d\):

- \(A_d\): `arrivals` (count of cases arriving during opening hours)
- \(C_d\): `completed` (count of cases completed during opening hours; may include cases that arrived earlier)
- `median_service_min`, `p90_service_min`: quantiles of **active handling time** (exclude waiting)
- \(L_d\): `mean_wip` (mean number of cases “in the system” from 15-minute snapshots; waiting + in-service are not separated)
- \(c_d\): `staff_fte` (mean concurrently available capacity, in FTE)
- `complex_pct`: % of arrivals in a multi-step complex variant
- `peak_08_10_pct`: % of daily arrivals in 08:00–09:59
- `outage_min`: minutes of service outage (nonzero on 2026-04-15)

## Derived quantities (estimands)

- **Arrival intensity (open-hour average):** \(\hat\lambda_d = A_d/H\) [cases/hour]
- **Completion intensity (open-hour average):** \(\hat\theta_d = C_d/H\) [cases/hour]
- **Backlog change proxy:** \(\Delta B_d \propto A_d - C_d\) (positive suggests backlog growth; negative suggests burn-down)
- **Capacity minutes per day:** \(K_d = c_d \cdot 720\) [FTE·min]

### Assumption A1 (needed for capacity-pressure comparisons)

We are not given \(E[S]\) (mean active service time). To compare “work demanded” to capacity, I assume daily active service time \(S\) is approximately **lognormal**, using the provided median \(m\) and \(p90\):

\[
\sigma \approx \frac{\ln(p90)-\ln(m)}{z_{0.9}},\quad z_{0.9}\approx 1.2816,\qquad
E[S]\approx \exp(\ln(m)+0.5\sigma^2).
\]

This is an **approximation** used only to build a defensible “pressure index,” not a ground-truth distributional claim.

- **Arrival-based pressure:** \(\rho^{(A)}_d = (A_d \cdot E[S_d]) / K_d\)
- **Completion-based pressure:** \(\rho^{(C)}_d = (C_d \cdot E[S_d]) / K_d\)

Values \(\rho>1\) indicate “more work than capacity” under A1, consistent with backlog growth or spillover.

## Threats to naive inference (non-exhaustive)

1. **Carry-over + closure:** backlog can carry overnight; “system time” spans closed hours but no service occurs then.
2. **Cohort mismatch:** \(C_d\) includes cases that may have arrived on earlier days; daily \(A_d\) and \(C_d\) are not paired cohorts.
3. **Outage nonstationarity (2026-04-15):** service capacity drops abruptly for ~3 hours; a steady pooled model is invalid for that day.
4. **Intraday nonstationarity:** `peak_08_10_pct` and representative hourly vectors show strong within-day rate variation and burstiness.
5. **Endogenous staffing:** staffing may respond to backlog/outage; pooled correlations can reverse (Simpson-like).
6. **Service summaries are quantiles, not means:** cannot use median service as mean service or mean flow time; p90 suggests heavy tails.
7. **Flow time is not observed:** service excludes waiting; Little’s Law needs mean time-in-system, which is not directly provided.
8. **`mean_wip` sampling ambiguity:** 15-minute snapshots may be during opening hours only; it mixes waiting + in-service; boundary definition matters.
9. **Serial dependence:** backlog persistence makes daily rows non-independent.
10. **Case-mix metric ambiguity:** `complex_pct` may reflect real mix shift, a coding change, or both; either way it changes observed statistics.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

## Alternative A: “Mix-state” first, calendar second

**Story:** One structural driver dominates: a **low-complex state** (pre-2026-04-20) and a **high-complex state** (from 2026-04-20 onward). Within each, treat weekday/weekend separately; treat the outage as a transient overlay.

- **Low-complex state (complex_pct \(\lesssim 30\%\))**: roughly 2026-04-06 to 2026-04-19 excluding the outage/recovery transient.
- **High-complex state (complex_pct \(\gtrsim 35\%\))**: 2026-04-20 onward, then split into pre-intervention vs intervention (staff up on/after 2026-04-29).

**Strengths:** directly matches the largest, sharpest shifts (complex_pct, peak_08_10_pct, service quantiles, WIP), and reduces the risk of overfitting many small regimes to short samples.

**Weaknesses:** can under-emphasize that weekends have different capacity (2.5→3.0 FTE) and that “high-complex + weekend” behaves differently than “high-complex + weekday” even within the same mix-state.

## Alternative B: “Queue-state” regimes based on backlog dynamics

**Story:** operations oscillate among **state-of-the-system** modes regardless of calendar:

- **Stable/low-backlog:** low WIP (e.g., \(L\lesssim 25\)) and \(|A-C|\) small
- **Overloaded:** high WIP and persistent \(A>C\)
- **Recovery/burn-down:** high WIP but \(C>A\)
- **Shock:** outage day (capacity discontinuity)

**Strengths:** aligns segmentation with what customers feel (congestion/backlog), and makes serial dependence explicit.

**Weaknesses:** backlog state is an **outcome**, not an exogenous driver; it can mix fundamentally different causes (outage vs mix shift) and may not map cleanly to actionable levers without additional instrumentation (e.g., intraday staffing, arrival routing).

Compared to the primary segmentation, Alternative A is simpler and driver-focused; Alternative B is state-focused and better for backlog-control policy design, but risks conflating causes.

# 4. Pooled versus segmented statistics

Below are concrete ways a single pooled model (e.g., one stationary \(M/M/c\) or even one \(G/G/c\) with fixed parameters) would be misleading.

## 4.1 Weekday/weekend composition creates “fake overdispersion”

- **All days pooled (n=28):** arrivals median **103.5/day** (mean **90.0/day**); staff mean **5.14 FTE**; complex median **33%**; median service **32.5 min**.
- **Weekdays only (n=20):** arrivals median **107.5/day**; WIP median **36.5**; staff median **6.0**.
- **Weekends only (n=8):** arrivals median **44.5/day**; WIP median **17.5**; staff median **2.5**.

If you treat pooled arrivals as a single stationary count process, the **index of dispersion** (variance/mean) for daily arrivals is about **10.0** pooled, but only **0.66** within weekdays—i.e., the “overdispersion” is largely a **mixture-of-regimes artifact**, not necessarily burstiness within a regime.

## 4.2 Case-mix shift drives a structural parameter jump (Simpson-like pooling of service and demand)

Splitting at 2026-04-20 (14 days pre / 14 post):

- `complex_pct` median: **22.5%** → **43.5%**
- `median_service_min` median: **27** → **35**
- `mean_wip` median: **20** → **39**
- `peak_08_10_pct` median: **29%** → **42.5%**

Pooling yields intermediate “averages” (e.g., complex median **33%**) that describe **no actual operating mode** and can misattribute congestion to noise rather than a structural shift.

## 4.3 Outage + recovery days distort throughput and WIP if treated as ordinary noise

- Outage day 2026-04-15: **C=58** vs baseline weekday median **C≈100**, and **WIP=47** vs baseline weekday **WIP≈20**.
- Recovery days 2026-04-16–17: \(C>A\) (catch-up), which is the opposite drift direction of the overloaded high-complex period \(A>C\).

In a pooled model, these transients inflate variability and can bias inferred “typical” throughput downward while biasing inferred WIP upward—yet they are operationally **qualitatively different** (capacity interruption and burn-down).

## 4.4 Staffing intervention confounding produces a sign reversal (classic Simpson pattern)

Using the daily aggregates:

- **Pooled across all days:** correlation \(r(\text{staff\_fte}, \text{mean\_wip}) \approx +0.65\) (looks like “more staff → more WIP”).
- **Within high-complex weekdays (2026-04-20 to 2026-05-01, weekdays):** \(r(\text{staff\_fte}, \text{mean\_wip}) \approx -0.43\) (consistent with “more staff helps reduce WIP”).

This happens because staffing is partly **reactive** to high-backlog conditions; a pooled regression would be causally misleading.

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

## 5.1 Representative hourly vectors (within-day burstiness)

Using the 12 hourly bins (07–08 … 18–19), define a simple burstiness proxy across hours: **Fano factor** \(=\mathrm{Var}(X)/\mathrm{Mean}(X)\) and **CV** \(=\mathrm{SD}/\mathrm{Mean}\).

Arrivals by hour (across the 12 bins):

- **2026-04-07 (ordinary weekday):** mean **8.42/h**, Fano **3.23**, CV **0.62**, max **20**, min **3**
- **2026-04-15 (outage day):** mean **8.75/h**, Fano **4.14**, CV **0.69**, max **22**, min **2**
- **2026-04-21 (bursty post-mix weekday):** mean **9.58/h**, Fano **10.12**, CV **1.03**, max **34**, min **2**
- **2026-04-25 (weekend):** mean **4.25/h**, Fano **1.29**, CV **0.55**, max **8**, min **1**

Completions by hour show a different pattern:

- Outage day has **three consecutive zero** completion hours (11:00–14:00), confirming capacity discontinuity.
- Bursty high-complex weekday completions are comparatively **smoothed** (Fano \(\approx 0.98\), CV \(\approx 0.34\)), consistent with **capacity-limited output** and backlog acting as a buffer: arrivals spike, completions cannot spike proportionally.

**Interpretation:** even within a “weekday regime,” treating arrivals as a stationary Poisson process is implausible; intensity is time-varying and occasionally highly bursty.

## 5.2 Daily sequence (serial dependence and change points)

Simple lag-1 autocorrelation indicators (computed on the 28-day sequence):

- arrivals lag-1 autocorr \(\approx 0.41\): moderate persistence (day-of-week + stable weekday levels)
- mean_wip lag-1 autocorr \(\approx 0.61\): strong persistence (backlog carries over)
- net inflow lag-1 autocorr \(\approx 0.02\): near-zero, consistent with “build-up then burn-down” patterns rather than independent noise

A transparent “best split” heuristic (maximize standardized mean differences on `complex_pct`, `mean_wip`, `peak_08_10_pct`, `median_service_min`) selects **2026-04-20** as the strongest change point, matching the visible step changes.

## 5.3 Why four representative days are insufficient for a full stochastic model

The four days illustrate plausible patterns (ordinary, outage, bursty, weekend), but they cannot identify:

- the full distribution of intraday intensity functions \(\lambda(t)\),
- the true service-time mean and tail behavior (only median and p90 are provided),
- the queue discipline, routing, and any correlation between service times and arrival bursts,
- the backlog at 07:00 and 19:00 each day (critical for nonstationary closed/open boundary effects).

Thus, the correct stance is **regime-aware descriptive modeling** and cautious approximation, not a single fully specified stationary queue.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Little’s Law \(L=\lambda W\) can be used to infer an average time-in-system \(W\) only when averages are taken over a window with (approximately) **stable boundaries** and meaningful throughput \(\lambda\). Here \(L\) is `mean_wip` (open-hour mean) and \(\lambda\) is proxied by mean **completions per open hour** when stability is plausible.

**Important:** \(W\) inferred from Little’s Law is **mean time in system (waiting + service)** in the measurement window; it is not the median service time, and closure can make calendar-time interpretation tricky.

# 7. Operational implications

## 7.1 Staffing and capacity management (regime-aware)

- **Baseline weekday appears comfortably loaded:** \(\rho^{(A)}\) med \(\approx 0.68\), WIP med \(\approx 20\).
- **High-complex weekday looks overloaded:** \(\rho^{(A)}\) med \(\approx 1.18\) with net inflow med **+14/day**, consistent with backlog growth and rising WIP (med \(\approx 44\)).
- **Intervention weekday is closer to sustainable:** \(\rho^{(A)}\) med \(\approx 0.94\) and \(C>A\) suggests backlog reduction.

**Action:** treat staffing as a **regime-switching control**:

- Plan weekday staffing for the **high-complex** regime, not the pooled average.
- Use **surge capacity** (extra FTE or overtime) immediately after outages and during identified high-burst days; completions are smoother than arrivals, implying backlog buffering is already happening—make it intentional.

## 7.2 Backlog recovery policy (don’t normalize outage days)

- The outage day is a **shock**, not a productivity regime. Recovery days show deliberate catch-up (\(C>A\)).
- **Action:** define explicit burn-down targets after shocks (e.g., “next-day completions exceed arrivals by X”) and track WIP at opening/closing to verify burn-down.

## 7.3 Peak smoothing and burst mitigation

- Post-2026-04-20, `peak_08_10_pct` jumps (median ~47% in high-complex weekdays), and the representative 2026-04-21 shows extreme bursts (hourly max 34 vs mean 9.6).
- **Action options:** appointment/slotting, controlled release, triage queues, or routing “complex” work away from the peak window; goal is to reduce instantaneous load that drives nonlinear waiting-time growth.

## 7.4 A pooled-average policy that looks good but is risky when segmented

**Tempting pooled view:** mean arrivals \(\approx 90/day\) and mean staff \(\approx 5.14\) might suggest reducing weekday staffing from ~6 to ~5 “on average.”

**Segmented reality:** high-complex weekdays have arrivals ~115/day and longer service times; pressure exceeds 1.0 even at ~6 FTE. Cutting staffing based on pooled averages would push the system deeper into the overloaded regime, increasing WIP and delay nonlinearly.

## 7.5 Data-collection improvements to support nonstationary queue models

- Log **intervention start/end timestamps** (intra-day), and **outage start/stop** precisely.
- Capture backlog at **07:00 and 19:00** (opening/closing WIP), not only open-hour mean WIP.
- Record **hourly** staffing (or shift-level), not only daily mean FTE.
- Move from daily aggregates to an event log (case_id, timestamps for arrival/start/end), enabling direct estimation of:
  - arrival intensity \(\lambda(t)\),
  - waiting vs service decomposition,
  - per-variant service distributions and routing effects.

# --- 3) Burstiness/dispersion indicators (at least two) ---
def burst_metrics(vec):
    x = np.asarray(vec, dtype=float)
    m = x.mean()
    v = x.var(ddof=1)
    fano = (v / m) if m > 0 else np.nan
    cv = (x.std(ddof=1) / m) if m > 0 else np.nan
    return pd.Series({"mean": m, "fano": fano, "cv": cv, "max": x.max(), "min": x.min()})

arr_burst = rep["arrivals_by_hour"].apply(burst_metrics)
arr_burst["representative_day"] = rep["representative_day"]
comp_burst = rep["completions_by_hour"].apply(burst_metrics)
comp_burst["representative_day"] = rep["representative_day"]

def index_of_dispersion(series):
    # daily var/mean (Poisson would be ~1 under idealized assumptions)
    s = series.astype(float)
    return float(s.var(ddof=1) / s.mean())

iod_all = index_of_dispersion(df["arrivals"])
iod_weekdays = index_of_dispersion(df.loc[~df["is_weekend"], "arrivals"])

# --- 4) Simple serial-dependence indicator ---
def lag1_autocorr(series):
    x = series.astype(float).to_numpy()
    if len(x) < 3:
        return np.nan
    x0 = x[:-1] - x[:-1].mean()
    x1 = x[1:] - x[1:].mean()
    denom = np.sqrt((x0**2).sum() * (x1**2).sum())
    return float((x0 * x1).sum() / denom) if denom > 0 else np.nan

serial = pd.Series({
    "arrivals_lag1": lag1_autocorr(df.sort_values("date")["arrivals"]),
    "mean_wip_lag1": lag1_autocorr(df.sort_values("date")["mean_wip"]),
    "net_inflow_lag1": lag1_autocorr(df.sort_values("date")["net_inflow"]),
})

# --- 5) Transparent change-point heuristic (not definitive ground truth) ---
features = ["complex_pct", "mean_wip", "peak_08_10_pct", "median_service_min"]
df_s = df.sort_values("date").reset_index(drop=True)
scores = []
for i in range(5, len(df_s) - 5):
    left = df_s.loc[: i - 1, features]
    right = df_s.loc[i:, features]
    score = 0.0
    for f in features:
        m1, m2 = left[f].mean(), right[f].mean()
        # scale by pooled variability (simple and transparent)
        s = np.sqrt(left[f].var(ddof=1) + right[f].var(ddof=1))
        if s > 0:
            score += abs(m2 - m1) / s
    scores.append((i, df_s.loc[i, "date"], score))
best_split = max(scores, key=lambda t: t[2])

def little(window, throughput_col="completed"):
    # L is mean open-hour WIP; throughput proxy is mean completed per open hour.
    L = window["mean_wip"].mean()
    thr_per_hr = window[throughput_col].mean() / OPEN_HOURS
    W_hours = (L / thr_per_hr) if thr_per_hr > 0 else np.nan
    return pd.Series({"L_mean": L, "thr_per_hr": thr_per_hr, "W_hours": W_hours, "W_min": W_hours * 60})
