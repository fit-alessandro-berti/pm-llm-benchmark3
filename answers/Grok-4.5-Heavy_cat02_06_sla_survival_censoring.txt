# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

**Time origin.** For every case the origin is the activity `Submit` (t = 0). The supplied field `duration_or_age_h` measures elapsed time from this origin to the observed terminal activity or to the single extract freeze, whichever occurs first. No left-truncation is indicated.

**Horizon.** The contractual SLA threshold is τ = 72 h.

**Events.** Operational success is the absorbing state `Resolve`. Competing terminal events that permanently preclude further internal observation of resolution are `Withdraw`, `Reject` and `Transfer`. Cases still marked `open` at the freeze are right-censored at their observed age. Any case whose recorded time already exceeds 72 h supplies direct evidence of an already-observed breach (or of residual non-terminal status past the horizon).

**Estimand A – Probability of not achieving internal resolution by 72 h**  
\[
\theta_A = 1 - F_R(72),\qquad F_R(t)=P(T_R\le t,\ \text{event=Resolve}),
\]  
where \(F_R\) is the cumulative incidence function of `Resolve` in the presence of the three competing terminals. \(\theta_A\) aggregates late resolves, competing exits and cases still open after 72 h. It is the quantity required for SLA-compliance reporting and governance.

**Estimand B – Probability of still having no terminal disposition at 72 h**  
\[
\theta_B = S_{\text{any}}(72)=P(T_{\text{any}}>72),
\]  
where \(T_{\text{any}}\) is time to the first absorbing state. \(\theta_B\) quantifies residual active backlog past the SLA and is the natural input to staffing and alert thresholds.

A supporting quantity is the cause-specific mass of resolves that occur after 72 h (tail recovery after an already-observed breach).

**Contribution of the four illustrative cases**

| Case | Observed | Under \(\theta_A\) (1 − CIF_Resolve(72)) | Under \(\theta_B\) (S_any(72)) |
|------|----------|------------------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| S03 resolved @ 76 h | late Resolve | Counts as non-resolution by 72 (event after horizon) | Survives past 72 without terminal, then resolves |
| S09 open @ 92 h | censored @ 92 | At risk past 72; still unresolved → contributes to non-resolution | Still non-terminal at 92 → contributes to survival past 72 |
| S06 withdrawn @ 27 h | competing @ 27 | Competing event before 72; never resolved → counts in \(\theta_A\) | Terminal before 72 → does not survive to 72 |
| S08 transferred @ 68 h | depends on encoding | Competing-non-resolve or worst-case: counts in \(\theta_A\); pure censor: removed at 68 | Terminal (or censored) before 72 → does not contribute to survival past 72 |

Right-censoring of open cases is distinct from competing events and from already-observed breaches. Treating `Transfer` or `Withdraw` as ordinary non-informative censoring for the resolution process would bias both estimands.

# 2. Event and censoring encoding

All 32 rows are retained. Let \(T_i=\) `duration_or_age_h`.

**Base mapping (statuses other than transferred)**

| observed_status | time | event_Resolve | competing_code | default censor rule |
|-----------------|------|---------------|----------------|---------------------|
| resolved | \(T\) | 1 (event time \(T\); may be >72) | 0 | none |
| withdrawn | \(T\) | 0 | 1 (Withdraw) | none (competing) |
| rejected | \(T\) | 0 | 2 (Reject) | none (competing) |
| open | \(T\) | 0 | 0 | right-censor at \(T\) |

**Three plausible treatments of `transferred`**

1. **Non-informative right-censoring** (optimistic for internal resolution): code exactly as an open case; censor at transfer time. Assumes the external hazard equals the internal hazard.
2. **Competing event “lost-to-internal-resolution”** (matches the observation scheme): competing cause code 3; the case can never later contribute a `Resolve`.
3. **Worst-case / breach-equivalent**: if \(T\le 72\) treat as a non-resolution event at \(T\) (or at 72); if \(T>72\) treat as already breached. Supplies an upper bound when external outcomes are unknown and transfers concentrate among escalated P1 cases.

**Additional sensitivity toggles.** (a) Recode Withdraw/Reject as ordinary censoring (interest becomes time-to-any-disposition). (b) For open cases with `last_event_gap_h` > 24 h re-code as “stalled” competing events or apply worst-case bounds. (c) Landmark at 24 h or 48 h: retain only cases still non-terminal at the landmark and re-analyse remaining time to 72.

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

Restricting analysis to the 13 resolved cases (or to any terminal subset) produces interlocking biases.

**Survivor bias.** Rapid resolvers (S01 18 h, S16 16 h, S18 12 h) dominate the sample while long-open cases (S09 92 h, S11 81 h, S26 104 h) and late resolvers (S03 76 h, S13 110 h) are either dropped or appear only conditionally on eventual resolution. The empirical distribution of resolution times is shifted left, under-estimating \(\theta_A\).

**Informative censoring.** Open cases are not a random subsample of the risk set. S11 (P1, escalated, gap 55 h) and S25 (P1, escalated, gap 48 h) exhibit prolonged inactivity; their continued openness may signal difficulty or overload. The three transfers (all P1 and escalated) leave precisely after escalation. Treating them as non-informative censoring assumes an unverifiable equality of external and internal hazards.

**Competing risks.** Withdrawals (S06, S12, S22, S30) and rejections (S05, S14, S23) permanently remove cases from the resolution risk set. A completed-case fraction “resolved before 72 among the 13 resolved rows” ignores these exits and therefore does not estimate \(F_R(72)\).

**Cohort imbalance.** Cohort B reflects a non-randomized, incompletely complied-with policy change. Differential path prefixes (AutoCheck placement) and differential transfer rates (2 of 3 transfers in B) confound any naïve A-versus-B comparison of completed cases.

**Open cases below versus above 72 h.** Opens still below the horizon (S10 47 h, S15 35 h, S24 67 h, S29 51 h, S31 23 h) remain in the risk set and can still experience any terminal event before 72 h. Opens already above 72 h (S09, S11, S25, S26) are observed failures for both \(\theta_A\) and \(\theta_B\) and must not be discarded. A completed-case analysis that waits for their eventual resolution either never terminates or introduces further length bias.

Concrete numerical illustration: among the 13 resolved cases four breached (S03, S13, S20, S28), giving a naïve 4/13 ≈ 31 % breach rate. Adding the four already-open-past-72 cases and the competing terminals that never resolved changes both numerator and denominator; only a competing-risk analysis that retains all 32 rows yields a coherent estimate of the defined estimands.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

With n = 32, no intermediate timestamps, competing terminals, and plausible informative mechanisms (transfers concentrated in escalated P1; several large inactivity gaps), the plan privileges absolute-risk estimators that use every observation and remain feasible under the limited temporal granularity.

Kaplan–Meier of time-to-Resolve treating all other statuses as ordinary right-censoring is simple but rests on independent censoring—an assumption doubtful for transfers and long-gap opens. It is retained only as an optimistic sensitivity bound, never as primary.

Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence of `Resolve`, with Withdraw/Reject/Transfer as competing events and opens censored at observed age, directly estimates \(F_R(t)\) and therefore \(\theta_A=1-F_R(72)\). Because each case supplies only one time, AJ reduces to a stratified nonparametric calculation; it utilises the entire sample and answers the operational question of internal-resolution success probability under the observed competing-risk regime. Stratification by cohort and priority is immediate.

Cause-specific hazards isolate associations with the instantaneous rate of Resolve but do not translate into absolute incidence. Fine–Gray subdistribution hazards model the CIF directly yet are under-powered with only 13 Resolve events. Restricted mean unresolved time up to τ = 72 h (area under the AJ curve) supplies a robust hour-scale summary attractive for small samples. Landmark analysis at fixed clock times or at the first appearance of Review/Escalate conditions on still being open and thereby safely incorporates time-varying stage features. A multi-state model whose transient states mirror observed prefixes can estimate occupancy probabilities at 72 h; lacking intermediate times it must rely on discrete-stage counts or assumed sojourns and is therefore exploratory.

**Primary recommendation for these data:** stratified Aalen–Johansen CIF of Resolve by 72 h with transfers coded as competing events. Absolute incidence matches SLA-governance needs, respects competing risks, and uses all cases.  
**Secondary:** landmark analyses embedding current-stage and gap features; RMST contrasts; exploratory multi-state occupancy; cause-specific Cox solely for factor screening. Every analysis is repeated under the three transfer-encoding scenarios of Section 2.

# 5. Process-aware features and stage hazards

Prefixes, the supplied flags and the inactivity gap are the only process detail.

- **Baseline-safe:** cohort and priority (fixed at or before Submit).
- **Time-varying / current-stage:** last activity token of the observed prefix; prefix length; count of Review tokens. For open cases this is the state occupied at the freeze.
- **Path-pattern / variant:** relative order of AutoCheck versus Screen (systematically different by cohort), presence of Escalate or Rework, full variant string. Treat as baseline only when the relevant activity is known to occur early; otherwise time-dependent.
- **Post-baseline / leakage-prone if used statically:** the binary `escalated` flag and integer `rework_count`. Both become known only after the corresponding activity; naïve baseline inclusion induces immortal-time bias and leakage.
- **Inactivity marker:** `last_event_gap_h`. Large values among opens (S11 55 h, S25 48 h, S26 72 h) may signal informative censoring or elevated residual risk.

Immortal-time and leakage are controlled by landmarking, time-dependent covariates, or by embedding the features in a multi-state state space so that hazards are estimated conditional on current state.

# 6. Sensitivity analysis

Three core analyses (plus variants) are compared.

1. **Primary – Transfer (and Withdraw/Reject) as competing events.** Aalen–Johansen CIF_Resolve; open censored at age; stratified by cohort and priority. Yields \(\theta_A\) under the observed competing regime. Assumption: open censoring non-informative conditional on strata and stage.

2. **Transfer as independent right-censoring.** Identical except Transfer is coded as censor. Inflates apparent CIF_Resolve because the three P1 transfers are removed from the competing-risk denominator rather than counted as non-resolves. Optimistic if transfers preferentially remove hard cases.

3. **Worst-case / bounding.** Code Transfer as a non-resolution event; for opens with `last_event_gap_h` > 24 h apply worst-case (remain unresolved at 72) and best-case (resolve immediately after last event) bounds. Separate A-versus-B analyses without pooling (policy change non-randomized). Additional toggles: Withdraw/Reject as censor versus competing; landmark-only re-estimation; simple IPW or frailty for possible informative open censoring driven by gap and escalation.

**Qualitative conclusions that can reverse.**  
- Apparent cohort-B superiority on SLA success: B contains more transfers; independent-censoring favours B, while competing or worst-case treatment can erase or reverse the difference.  
- P1 performance: all transfers are P1; independent-censoring makes P1 look better on internal resolution than competing-risk or worst-case views.  
- Magnitude of tail risk: primary may produce a moderate \(1-\text{CIF}\); adding transfers plus long-gap opens as breaches materially raises estimated \(\theta_A\).  
- Naïve protective associations of escalation or rework (if entered as baseline) can reverse under landmark or time-dependent encoding because of immortal time.  
- Claim that “the policy change improved SLA”: observational only; under selection and transfer-handling sensitivity the claim can weaken or reverse.

# 7. Operational interpretation

Survival and CIF outputs are converted into actions without causal language.

- **Real-time alerts.** For each open case report the stratified conditional probability of failing to Resolve by 72 given current age, last stage (from prefix), priority and gap. Alert when the conditional risk exceeds a calibrated threshold or when the gap indicates stall (S11, S25, S26). Landmark or multi-state occupancy tables supply the numbers.
- **Workload / capacity actions.** From the current risk set of opens, sum expected unresolved indicators at 72 h under each sensitivity scenario. Prioritise staffing toward high-risk stages (Review with large gap, Escalated P1). RMST supplies average remaining exposure hours.
- **SLA governance metrics.** Publish stratified AJ CIF_Resolve(72) and \(1-\text{CIF}\) as the headline “probability of operational success inside SLA” / “tail non-resolution risk”. Track by cohort for policy monitoring but annotate “observational, non-randomized rollout”. Cause-specific hazards are used only diagnostically.

**Calibration.** In each priority × cohort cell compare predicted versus observed Resolve-by-72 counts (or bootstrap). With n = 32 cells are sparse—report raw proportions alongside any model-based figures.  
**Fairness.** Always present metrics broken by priority (P1 should not exhibit elevated internal non-resolution or transfer rate if policy is equitable) and cohort. Transfers concentrate in P1; any single score that ignores this masks disparity. Process features such as Escalate are markers of complexity, not proven levers.

# 3. Risk-set / cumulative-incidence ready tables (no rows dropped)
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
def empirical_cif_at_horizon(enc, tau=TAU, by=None):
    """
    Simple nonparametric CIF-style counts at horizon tau.
    For illustration; full AJ would sort unique times and accumulate.
    Returns dict of stratum -> summary.
    """
    if by is None:
        groups = [("ALL", enc)]
    else:
        groups = list(enc.groupby(by))
    summaries = {}
    for key, g in groups:
        n = len(g)
        # Resolve by tau
        res_by = ((g["event_resolve"] == 1) & (g["time"] <= tau)).sum()
        # Competing by tau
        comp_by = ((g["competing"] == 1) & (g["time"] <= tau)).sum()
        # Still open / censored and time > tau (already past horizon)
        open_past = ((g["status_code"] == "censor") & (g["time"] > tau)).sum()
        # Still at risk and time <= tau (censored before horizon)
        cens_before = ((g["status_code"] == "censor") & (g["time"] <= tau)).sum()
        # Late resolve (after tau)
        late_res = ((g["event_resolve"] == 1) & (g["time"] > tau)).sum()
        # Crude 1 - CIF approx = 1 - res_by/n  (ignores timing of competitors)
        # Better: proportion never resolved by tau among those who could be observed
        theta_A_approx = 1.0 - (res_by / n)
        # Estimand B approx: proportion with time > tau and no terminal
        still_open_past = open_past / n
        summaries[key] = {
            "n": n,
            "resolve_by_72": int(res_by),
            "competing_by_72": int(comp_by),
            "open_past_72": int(open_past),
            "censored_before_72": int(cens_before),
            "late_resolve": int(late_res),
            "theta_A_approx_1_minus_resfrac": round(theta_A_approx, 3),
            "theta_B_approx_still_open_past": round(still_open_past, 3),
        }
    return summaries

# 9. Limitations and additional data

Stronger inference requires, in priority order:

1. **Exact timestamps of every activity** inside each prefix. Without them multi-state intensities, precise time-to-Escalate, immortal-time correction and fine-grained landmarking remain approximate.
2. **External outcomes after Transfer** (whether and when the receiving organisation resolved or closed the case). Critical for any end-to-end or “ultimate resolution” estimand.
3. **Absolute calendar start dates and the precise observation-cutoff date(s).** Needed to verify the administrative-censoring mechanism, detect calendar-time effects and locate the cohort-B rollout relative to case starts.
4. **Time-varying workload, queue lengths and staffing levels.** Essential to decide whether long gaps are capacity-driven (informative) or case-specific.
5. **Assignment mechanism and policy-rollout compliance** for cohort B. Without knowledge of who received the new automated-screening / escalation rules and why some cases were transferred, causal claims about the policy change are impossible.
6. Supplementary items: reason codes for Withdraw/Reject/Transfer/Escalate, baseline complexity covariates beyond priority, and repeated extracts for temporal validation.

Labelled assumptions that remain: conditional non-informative censoring of opens given observed strata and stage; Markov property if a multi-state model is fitted; that the recorded prefix captures all relevant history. The modest sample size (32 cases, 13 Resolve events) limits precision of subgroup CIFs and precludes rich regression. All reported associations are observational.
