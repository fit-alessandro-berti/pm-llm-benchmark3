# 1. Data audit and estimands

### Queueing quantities and units

For date \(d\):

- \(A_d\): arrivals recorded during the 12 scheduled opening hours.
- \(C_d\): completions during those hours. These may include cases arriving on earlier dates.
- Arrival intensity estimate: \(\hat\lambda_{A,d}=A_d/12\), in cases per scheduled open hour.
- Completion intensity estimate: \(\hat\lambda_{C,d}=C_d/12\), also per scheduled open hour.
- \(L_d\): `mean_wip`, the mean number in the system across 15-minute snapshots during opening hours. It is not the opening or closing backlog.
- \(S_{50,d}\) and \(S_{90,d}\): reported median and p90 active handling times. They exclude waiting and are not flow-time statistics.
- Observed daily imbalance: \(N_d=A_d-C_d\). Positive values are consistent with accumulation; negative values are consistent with drainage from earlier backlog.
- `staff_fte`: mean concurrent available capacity, not necessarily productive service time or skill-adjusted capacity.

All intensities below use the **scheduled 12-hour day**. On 2026-04-15, 58 completions therefore equal 4.83 completions per scheduled open hour. Dividing by the approximately nine non-outage hours would give 6.44, but that is still not a stationary service rate because inventory and timing changed sharply.

### Assumptions used

1. **Equal-snapshot assumption:** Daily WIP means are treated as comparable because every day has the same 12-hour opening window and nominal 15-minute snapshot frequency. Missing snapshots are not reported.
2. **Diagnostic mass-balance assumption:** When discussing cumulative \(A-C\), assume temporarily that there were no unreported out-of-hours arrivals, abandonments, transfers, or other entries/exits. This assumption is not established by the data, so cumulative imbalance is evidence about pressure, not a proven backlog count.
3. **Intervention boundary assumption:** April 29 is treated as a mixed transition day because the intervention began sometime during that date.
4. **Rounded-mix assumption:** Arrival-weighted complex percentages treat the reported integer percentages as exact, although they were likely rounded.

Under the diagnostic mass-balance assumption, observed cumulative imbalance is:

- April 6–14: \(+5\)
- April 15 outage: another \(+47\)
- April 16–19 recovery: \(-30\)
- April 20–28 high-mix period: \(+106\)
- April 29–May 3: \(-18\)

The full observed imbalance is therefore \(+110\). The actual backlog level remains unknown because the initial backlog and other possible flows are missing.

### Important audit finding

The representative hourly vectors sum exactly to their corresponding daily arrival and completion totals, but they do **not** reconcile with `peak_08_10_pct`. Using the stated bins, vector-based 08:00–09:59 shares are:

| Date | Supplied `peak_08_10_pct` | Share from vector bins 08–09 and 09–10 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2026-04-07 | 31% | 16.8% |
| 2026-04-15 | 32% | 18.1% |
| 2026-04-21 | 47% | 9.6% |
| 2026-04-25 | 22% | 11.8% |

This cannot be explained by rounding. Possible explanations include a one-hour bin offset, different source extracts, or a definition error. The hourly vectors and daily peak field should therefore be analyzed separately rather than silently reconciled.

### Threats to naive inference

1. **Weekday/weekend mixture:** Overall averages combine about 108 weekday arrivals with only 44.9 weekend arrivals.
2. **Nonstationary case mix:** `complex_pct` has no overlap across the main change: 18–27% through April 19 versus 39–47% from April 20 onward. This may be operational, a coding change, or both.
3. **Outage and endogenous recovery:** April 15 is a capacity shock, while April 16–17 completions draw on accumulated inventory. Recovery output is not a normal service-rate observation.
4. **Unknown opening and closing inventories:** Near-equality of arrivals and completions does not prove stable boundaries.
5. **Overnight carry-over:** Cases can remain in the system while service is closed. Opening-hours WIP cannot directly identify calendar elapsed flow time.
6. **Cohort mismatch:** Daily completions need not belong to the same-day arrival cohort.
7. **Quantile/mean mismatch:** Median and p90 service times do not identify mean service time, so utilization and service capacity cannot be calculated reliably.
8. **Active service versus flow time:** Handling times exclude queueing; they must not be substituted for mean time in system.
9. **Serial dependence:** A high-backlog day changes the starting condition and completion opportunity of following days.
10. **Time-varying staffing:** Mean daily FTE hides intraday schedules, skills, breaks, and the exact April 29 intervention time.
11. **Selected hourly examples:** Four representative days do not form a random or repeated sample sufficient to fit a stochastic arrival model.
12. **Aggregation and ecological bias:** Daily medians cannot be pooled into a case-level median, and associations between staff, WIP, mix, and completions can reverse after stratification.

Consequently, an \(M/M/c\)-style pooled model would be especially misleading: arrivals are time-varying and bursty, service behavior changes, capacity changes, the queue closes overnight, and inventory is not stationary.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

A materially different approach would use a **factorial state model** rather than mutually exclusive chronological phases. Each day would receive several labels:

| Dimension | Suggested states | Operational story |
|---|---|---|
| Day type | Weekday / weekend | Captures the large recurring demand and staffing cycle |
| Mix state | Low mix, 18–27% / high mix, 39–47% | Represents either a real process-variant shift or a coding-era change |
| Capacity state | Normal day-type staffing / augmented staffing / outage-impaired | Separates nominal staffing from effective capacity |
| Inventory direction | Accumulating \(A>C\) / balanced / draining \(C>A\) | Describes queue state rather than assuming output is exogenous |
| Arrival morphology | Ordinary / bursty / unknown | Uses hourly vectors where available and treats the inconsistent peak field cautiously |

Under this story, April 16 is not a new “high-throughput regime”; it is a low-mix, augmented-capacity, backlog-draining weekday. April 21 is a high-mix, normal-capacity, bursty, accumulating weekday. April 30 is a high-mix, augmented-capacity, draining weekday.

**Strengths relative to the primary segmentation**

- Better separates demand, case mix, capacity, and backlog.
- Avoids interpreting high completions during recovery as a permanent service-rate change.
- Can support a regression or hierarchical model with weekday, outage, complex share, staffing, and lagged backlog proxies.
- Allows operational states to recur rather than forcing a single date boundary.

**Weaknesses**

- Daily \(A-C\) is an imperfect inventory-state classifier because opening and closing backlog are absent.
- Many factorial cells contain only one or two days.
- Staffing is endogenous: more staff may be assigned because WIP is already high.
- The complex threshold is data-supported by a clear gap but cannot distinguish a coding change from a real shift.
- Burst classification is available for only four days.

A second serious alternative is an **interrupted-time-series segmentation**: pre-intervention through April 28, mixed transition on April 29, and post-intervention from April 30 onward, with weekday/weekend, outage, complex share, and peak as covariates and autocorrelated errors. This directly targets intervention evaluation, but it has only four clean post days, an unknown intervention time, strong backlog confounding, and no contemporaneous control queue. It is therefore weaker for causal inference than the primary segmentation is for descriptive operations.

- Across all days, higher staffing can appear associated with higher WIP because staff are added on high-demand or recovery days.

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

### Representative hourly vectors

Using population variance across each day’s 12 hourly bins:

| Representative day | Arrival Fano factor | Arrival hourly CV | Completion Fano factor | Completion hourly CV | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Apr 7 ordinary weekday | 2.96 | 0.59 | 1.56 | 0.43 | Pronounced time-of-day profile, with somewhat smoother completions |
| Apr 15 outage | 3.79 | 0.66 | 2.06 | 0.65 | Completion dispersion is driven partly by three forced zero hours |
| Apr 21 bursty high-mix weekday | 9.28 | 0.98 | 0.90 | 0.33 | Extreme arrival concentration; completions are strongly smoothed relative to arrivals |
| Apr 25 weekend | 1.18 | 0.53 | 0.72 | 0.45 | Lower-volume, comparatively smoother profile |

For April 21, one hour contains 34 of 115 arrivals and another contains 24. The completion vector has no corresponding spikes, consistent with buffering by the queue. This supports time-varying or burst-arrival modeling rather than a homogeneous daily Poisson rate.

These Fano factors are **descriptive across different clock hours**, not formal Poisson dispersion tests. A deterministic nonhomogeneous arrival rate can produce a Fano factor above one across hour bins even if conditional arrivals are Poisson. Repeated observations for each day-of-week/hour cell would be needed to separate predictable seasonality from stochastic overdispersion. The hourly CVs are count-profile CVs, not interarrival-time CVs.

### Daily dispersion and dependence

Across all 28 arrival counts:

- Population Fano factor: approximately **9.67**
- Daily count CV: approximately **0.33**

Much of that apparent overdispersion is compositional. Conditional on day type:

- Weekday arrival Fano factor: approximately **0.63**, CV **0.08**
- Weekend arrival Fano factor: approximately **0.64**, CV **0.12**

This does not establish underdispersed arrival processes. The samples still combine several regimes, and daily totals suppress intraday bursts: high-mix weekday totals are relatively stable even though April 21 is highly bursty within the day.

Simple raw lag-1 Pearson correlations are approximately:

- Arrivals: **0.41**
- `complex_pct`: **0.93**
- Mean WIP: **0.61**

The very high complex-share correlation reflects the persistent level shift rather than necessarily an autoregressive mechanism. WIP dependence is operationally plausible because cases carry over and recovery spans days. Arrival dependence is confounded by the weekly pattern and the rising demand era. Residual autocorrelation after day-type and regime adjustment cannot be estimated reliably with only 28 observations.

### Change-point evidence

A transparent one-split heuristic minimizing within-segment squared error on standardized `complex_pct`, median service, and p90 service places the main persistent boundary between April 19 and April 20. This is consistent with:

- Complex share jumping from 23% to 41%.
- No overlap between pre-boundary and post-boundary complex ranges.
- Sustained higher service medians and p90s afterward.
- Higher reported weekday peak shares.

That heuristic is not ground truth. It does not identify whether the change was operational or a coding revision, and it does not replace separately known intervention and outage information. The April 15 completion deficit and the April 29 staffing change are distinct change mechanisms.

Four representative days are insufficient to estimate a full arrival distribution, interarrival-time SCV, day-of-week seasonal model, or autocorrelated count process. They can identify plausible morphology, not its frequency.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

For an opening-hours version of Little’s Law, \(L=\lambda W\), \(L\) and \(\lambda\) must cover the same queue, population, and operational-time window. Completion rate is preferred below because it is the observed departure throughput. Arrival rate is nearly equivalent only in approximately balanced windows.

Any resulting \(W\) is in **open operational hours**, not calendar elapsed hours. It includes queueing plus service in principle, but overnight closed time is removed. It must not be compared directly with the median active service time or used to derive mean waiting by subtraction.

# 7. Operational implications

### Staffing and capacity

- **Recommendation:** Do not staff to the pooled 90 arrivals/day or pooled 5.14 FTE. Such a policy looks efficient in aggregate but would under-resource weekdays—especially the 114-arrival high-mix regime—and over-resource low-volume weekends.
- The ordinary weekday pattern was approximately balanced near 6 FTE only under low mix. The same mean 5.93 FTE during high mix accumulated about 13.4 cases/day.
- The observed 6.5–7 weekday FTE after the intervention coincided with drainage, but an exact staffing requirement cannot be inferred without mean service requirements, skill mix, productive-time fractions, and more post-period data.
- On high-mix weekends, 2.5 FTE coincided with +6 cases/day, while 3.0 FTE coincided with balance over two later days. This is a useful pilot signal, not a stable capacity estimate.
- Complex work may require skill-based routing rather than merely more interchangeable FTE.

### Backlog recovery

- Treat outage recovery as a separate operating mode with explicit recovery capacity and a backlog target.
- Under the diagnostic mass-balance assumption, only 18 of the 128 accumulated observed cases through April 28 were offset after April 29. Returning to daily flow balance would therefore not prove that backlog had returned to baseline.
- Track opening backlog, closing backlog, oldest-case age, and cohort completion curves. A declining daily mean WIP alone can conceal substantial residual carry-over.

### Peak smoothing

- Use intraday flex staffing, cross-trained reserves, appointment spreading, or early triage around observed burst hours.
- April 21 shows that a stable daily total can coexist with extreme hourly concentration. Daily capacity sizing alone is insufficient.
- Reconcile the `peak_08_10_pct` definition before designing an 08:00–10:00 staffing policy. The representative vectors place their principal peaks elsewhere.
- If complex cases can be identified at arrival, separate triage or protected complex-case capacity could reduce tail growth and prevent simple cases from being blocked behind multi-step variants.

### Data collection and process mining

Collect at minimum:

- Case identifier and true arrival timestamp.
- Queue-entry and queue-exit timestamps.
- Activity start, suspend, resume, and complete timestamps.
- Resource, skill, and schedule at 15-minute resolution.
- Opening and closing backlog, including case age.
- Abandonments, transfers, rework, and out-of-hours arrivals.
- Complex-variant definition and coding-version history.
- Exact intervention and outage start/end times.
- Full 24-hour inventory snapshots if calendar flow time is an objective.

With event-level data, process mining can distinguish true variant change from recoding, compare waiting and service by variant, identify rework, assess resource handoffs, and evaluate intervention effects on arrival cohorts rather than same-day totals.

# 2. Primary regime summaries.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
def assign_regime(row):
    d = row["date"]
    weekend = row["day_type"] == "weekend"
    if d <= pd.Timestamp("2026-04-14"):
        return "ordinary_weekend" if weekend else "ordinary_weekday"
    if d == pd.Timestamp("2026-04-15"):
        return "outage"
    if d <= pd.Timestamp("2026-04-19"):
        return "recovery_weekend" if weekend else "recovery_weekday"
    if d <= pd.Timestamp("2026-04-28"):
        return "highmix_weekend" if weekend else "highmix_weekday"
    if d == pd.Timestamp("2026-04-29"):
        return "intervention_transition"
    return "post_weekend" if weekend else "post_weekday"

daily["regime"] = daily.apply(assign_regime, axis=1)

regime = daily.groupby("regime", sort=False).agg(
    days=("date", "size"),
    arrivals_total=("arrivals", "sum"),
    completed_total=("completed", "sum"),
    arrivals_day=("arrivals", "mean"),
    completed_day=("completed", "mean"),
    daily_service_median=("median_service_min", "median"),
    daily_p90_median=("p90_service_min", "median"),
    mean_wip=("mean_wip", "mean"),
    mean_staff=("staff_fte", "mean"),
    staff_days=("staff_fte", "sum"),
)
regime["arrivals_open_hr"] = (
    regime["arrivals_total"] / (regime["days"] * OPEN_HOURS)
)
regime["completed_open_hr"] = (
    regime["completed_total"] / (regime["days"] * OPEN_HOURS)
)
regime["net_day"] = (
    regime["arrivals_total"] - regime["completed_total"]
) / regime["days"]
regime["wip_per_mean_fte"] = regime["mean_wip"] / regime["mean_staff"]
regime["completed_per_fte_hr"] = (
    regime["completed_total"] / (regime["staff_days"] * OPEN_HOURS)
)

def summarize_window(label, mask):
    x = daily.loc[mask]
    return {
        "window": label,
        "days": len(x),
        "arrivals_day": x["arrivals"].mean(),
        "completed_day": x["completed"].mean(),
        "net_day": (x["arrivals"].sum() - x["completed"].sum()) / len(x),
        "mean_wip": x["mean_wip"].mean(),
        "mean_staff": x["staff_fte"].mean(),
        # Approximate because reported daily percentages may be rounded.
        "weighted_complex_pct": (
            (x["arrivals"] * x["complex_pct"]).sum() / x["arrivals"].sum()
        ),
        "daily_service_median": x["median_service_min"].median(),
        "daily_p90_median": x["p90_service_min"].median(),
    }

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Burstiness: population Fano factor, count CV, and max-hour share.
# These are dispersion across clock-hour bins, not interarrival-time CVs.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
def dispersion_stats(values):
    values = pd.Series(values, dtype=float)
    mean = values.mean()
    variance = values.var(ddof=0)
    return {
        "mean": mean,
        "fano_var_over_mean": variance / mean if mean else float("nan"),
        "hourly_count_cv": (variance ** 0.5) / mean if mean else float("nan"),
        "max_hour_share_pct": 100 * values.max() / values.sum(),
    }

burst_rows = []
for date, group in hourly.groupby("date"):
    for stream in ["arrivals", "completed"]:
        stats = dispersion_stats(group[stream])
        burst_rows.append({"date": date, "stream": stream, **stats})
burst = pd.DataFrame(burst_rows)

daily_dispersion_rows = []
for label, x in [
    ("all_days", daily),
    ("weekdays", daily.loc[daily["day_type"].eq("weekday")]),
    ("weekends", daily.loc[daily["day_type"].eq("weekend")]),
]:
    stats = dispersion_stats(x["arrivals"])
    daily_dispersion_rows.append({
        "population": label,
        "arrival_fano": stats["fano_var_over_mean"],
        "arrival_cv": stats["hourly_count_cv"],
    })
daily_dispersion = pd.DataFrame(daily_dispersion_rows)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Transparent, non-definitive one-change-point heuristic.
# Minimize within-segment SSE on standardized mix and service features.
# A five-day minimum segment prevents one-day edge splits.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
cp_features = ["complex_pct", "median_service_min", "p90_service_min"]
z = (daily[cp_features] - daily[cp_features].mean()) / \
    daily[cp_features].std(ddof=0)

base_sse = ((z - z.mean()) ** 2).sum().sum()
cp_candidates = []
minimum_segment = 5
for k in range(minimum_segment, len(z) - minimum_segment + 1):
    left = z.iloc[:k]
    right = z.iloc[k:]
    sse = (
        ((left - left.mean()) ** 2).sum().sum()
        + ((right - right.mean()) ** 2).sum().sum()
    )
    cp_candidates.append({
        "split_index": k,
        "left_end": daily.iloc[k - 1]["date"],
        "right_start": daily.iloc[k]["date"],
        "within_sse": sse,
        "sse_reduction_pct": 100 * (base_sse - sse) / base_sse,
    })
cp_results = pd.DataFrame(cp_candidates).sort_values("within_sse")
best_cp = cp_results.head(1)

# Independently flag the largest observed one-day flow imbalance.

].to_frame().T

    "ordinary_cycle_04-06_to_04-14":
        daily["date"].between("2026-04-06", "2026-04-14"),
    "ordinary_weekdays":
        daily["regime"].eq("ordinary_weekday"),
    "ordinary_weekends":
        daily["regime"].eq("ordinary_weekend"),
    "post_weekends_05-02_to_05-03":
        daily["regime"].eq("post_weekend"),
}

ll_rows = []
for label, mask in ll_masks.items():
    x = daily.loc[mask]
    departure_rate = x["completed"].sum() / (len(x) * OPEN_HOURS)
    mean_l = x["mean_wip"].mean()
    implied_open_hours = mean_l / departure_rate
    ll_rows.append({
        "window": label,
        "days": len(x),
        "observed_net": x["arrivals"].sum() - x["completed"].sum(),
        "mean_wip_L": mean_l,
        "completion_rate_open_hr": departure_rate,
        "implied_open_hours": implied_open_hours,
        "implied_open_minutes": 60 * implied_open_hours,
        "verdict": "approximate only",
    })
little = pd.DataFrame(ll_rows)
