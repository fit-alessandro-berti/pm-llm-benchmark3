## 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

### 1.1 Material assumptions

- **Case notion and attributes**: Each trace represents one vendor onboarding case. Activities are stored in the event attribute `concept:name`, the case id in `case:concept:name`, the resource in `org:resource`, and timestamps in `time:timestamp`.
- **Backbone activities**: Every case starts with `Receive Onboarding Request` and ends with exactly one `Close Onboarding`. Accepted cases are those with `Activate Vendor`; rejected cases have `Reject Vendor` and no activation.
- **Checks and classification**: `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, and `Verify Bank Account` are mandatory once per case before `Classify Risk`, and may execute in any order (modeled as parallelism). Classification occurs after all three checks and after any clarification loop.
- **Clarification cycles**: `Request Clarification` / `Receive Clarification` may occur multiple times before `Classify Risk`. The procedural model approximates this with at most one explicit loop; the exact “many cycles” semantics is delegated to DECLARE and the resource/data-aware layer.
- **Risk routes and approvals**: After `Classify Risk` the case takes exactly one of three routes: rejection; low‑risk (single `Manager Approval`); or high‑risk (`Enhanced Due Diligence` then `Compliance Approval`). Approvals never occur in rejected cases in the intended model.
- **Post-activation behaviour**: Accepted cases must perform `Release First Payment` once after `Activate Vendor` and then `Close Onboarding`. `Change Bank Details`, `Suspend Vendor`, and a second `Verify Bank Account` are optional post‑activation controls that may appear zero or more times in reality; the process tree explicitly models at most one such cycle and pushes the general rule (R11) to DECLARE and the resource/data layer.
- **Sanctions-related activities**: `Sanctions Escalation` and `Obtain Legal Opinion` are rare escalation activities. They are treated as cross‑cutting controls layered on top of the backbone rather than part of the main control‑flow skeleton.
- **Multiplicity and uniqueness**: `Activate Vendor`, `Reject Vendor`, and `Release First Payment` occur at most once per case; `Close Onboarding` occurs exactly once.

### 1.2 Interpreting R5 and R6

- **R4**: If `Sanctions Escalation` occurs, `Obtain Legal Opinion` must eventually occur (existential response).
- **R5 (legal department)**: After `Sanctions Escalation`, `Obtain Legal Opinion` must precede `Activate Vendor` under all circumstances. This is a strict order requirement: in any case with escalation and activation, there must be an opinion, and it must occur before the first activation.
- **R6 (operations department)**: In emergencies, activation may occur before `Obtain Legal Opinion`, but only with joint authorization outside the event log, and the opinion must then arrive within 24 hours of escalation. The event log contains neither an “emergency” flag nor the authorization details.
- **Conflict**: R5 forbids activation before opinion after escalation; R6 permits it in a regulated subset of cases that cannot be identified from the log alone. Any automated model based purely on activities and timestamps must therefore either (a) reject some legitimately authorized emergencies (legal‑first stance) or (b) accept some unauthorized early activations as if they were emergencies (operations‑first stance).

### 1.3 Resolution A – legal‑first (chosen for the implemented model)

- **Definition**: Automated conformance treats R5 as authoritative. For any case where `Sanctions Escalation` occurs, the resource/data layer enforces:
  - `Obtain Legal Opinion` must occur at least once; and
  - any `Activate Vendor` must occur strictly after the first `Obtain Legal Opinion`.
- **Encoding**: This is implemented as a dedicated Python check (Section 7) that scans each trace and fails any case with escalation and activation where opinion is missing or ordered after activation. DECLARE and the log skeleton keep only the weaker R4 response (`Sanctions Escalation` → `Obtain Legal Opinion`) so that normal non‑escalated accepted cases remain conformant.
- **Treatment of R6**: Emergencies are allowed only as documented manual overrides: if a case fails the strict R5 check but operations can demonstrate out‑of‑band emergency authorization, the case may be accepted operationally while remaining non‑conformant in automated reports.
- **Compliance risk**:
  - **Pros**: No unauthorized early activation after escalation can slip through undetected; all such traces are flagged as violations that must be justified.
  - **Cons**: Legitimate emergencies (like T6) become automatic violations in automated conformance, generating false positives and extra review workload. The model errs on the side of legal robustness over operational flexibility.

### 1.4 Resolution B – emergency‑aware (alternative, not used as authoritative)

- **Definition**: R6 becomes authoritative. Activation before opinion is allowed in cases explicitly marked as emergencies, provided `Obtain Legal Opinion` occurs within 24 hours of `Sanctions Escalation`. For non‑emergency cases, R5 still holds.
- **Encoding sketch**:
  - Extend the case attributes with an `emergency_activation` boolean and, optionally, a list of authorizing users.
  - A second Python check (Section 7) allows activation before opinion only when `emergency_activation` is true and the escalation→opinion delay is ≤ 24 hours.
  - DECLARE and log skeleton remain as in Resolution A (R4 only); the emergency semantics are entirely in the resource/data‑aware layer and temporal profile.
- **Compliance risk**:
  - **Pros**: Appropriately labeled emergencies (e.g., T6) pass automated checks if they meet the 24‑hour requirement, reducing false positives and better matching operational practice.
  - **Cons**: The model now trusts external flags; mis‑labelled or missing `emergency_activation` attributes can hide legal violations. The combination of activity data and temporal profile alone can no longer guarantee detection of early, unauthorized activations.
- **Use in this answer**: Resolution B is presented as an optional variant and is used in the T6 discussion to show how verdicts would change, but all concrete “pass/fail” evaluations in the core model assume Resolution A (legal‑first) as the authoritative automation policy.

## 3. Procedural Backbone

### 3.1 Compact formal description

We use a block-structured process tree (sequence `→`, exclusive choice `×`, parallelism `∧`, and silent τ) as the canonical procedural model.

Let:

- `post_activation = →( ×( τ, →("Change Bank Details", "Suspend Vendor", "Verify Bank Account") ), "Release First Payment", "Close Onboarding" )`

Then the full tree is:

- `procedural_model = →(`
  - `"Receive Onboarding Request",`
  - `∧("Capture Master Data", "Run Sanctions Screening", "Verify Bank Account")`,  (parallel, order not constrained)
  - `×( τ, →("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification") )`,  (zero or one clarification cycle; additional cycles are handled declaratively)
  - `"Classify Risk",`
  - `×(`
    - `→("Reject Vendor", "Close Onboarding"),`  (rejected)
    - `→("Manager Approval", "Activate Vendor", post_activation),`  (low‑risk accepted)
    - `→("Enhanced Due Diligence", "Compliance Approval", "Activate Vendor", post_activation)`  (high‑risk accepted)
  - `)`
- `)`

`Sanctions Escalation` and `Obtain Legal Opinion` are intentionally omitted from the backbone tree and handled as cross‑cutting constraints in the DECLARE, log‑skeleton, temporal, and resource/data layers. This keeps the backbone focused on the stable “happy path” and terminal routing.

### 3.2 PM4Py Python code for `procedural_model`

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator

# Leaf nodes for backbone activities (one node per structural position)
receive_request = ProcessTree(label="Receive Onboarding Request")

capture_master_data = ProcessTree(label="Capture Master Data")
run_sanctions_screening = ProcessTree(label="Run Sanctions Screening")
verify_bank_initial = ProcessTree(label="Verify Bank Account")

request_clarification = ProcessTree(label="Request Clarification")
receive_clarification = ProcessTree(label="Receive Clarification")

classify_risk = ProcessTree(label="Classify Risk")

manager_approval = ProcessTree(label="Manager Approval")
enhanced_due_diligence = ProcessTree(label="Enhanced Due Diligence")
compliance_approval = ProcessTree(label="Compliance Approval")

activate_vendor = ProcessTree(label="Activate Vendor")

change_bank_details = ProcessTree(label="Change Bank Details")
suspend_vendor = ProcessTree(label="Suspend Vendor")
verify_bank_recheck = ProcessTree(label="Verify Bank Account")

release_first_payment = ProcessTree(label="Release First Payment")
close_onboarding_after_reject = ProcessTree(label="Close Onboarding")
close_onboarding_after_payment = ProcessTree(label="Close Onboarding")

# Parallel backbone checks after receiving the request
checks_parallel = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.PARALLEL,
    children=[capture_master_data, run_sanctions_screening, verify_bank_initial],
)

# Approximate clarification loop: either no clarification or exactly one request/receive pair
clarification_cycle = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
    children=[request_clarification, receive_clarification],
)
clarification_block = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[ProcessTree(), clarification_cycle],  # tau vs one cycle
)

# Post-activation behaviour: optional bank-change cycle before payment and closing
bank_change_block = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[
        ProcessTree(),  # no change
        ProcessTree(
            operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
            children=[change_bank_details, suspend_vendor, verify_bank_recheck],
        ),
    ],
)
post_activation = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
    children=[bank_change_block, release_first_payment, close_onboarding_after_payment],
)

# Rejection route
reject_route = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
    children=[ProcessTree(label="Reject Vendor"), close_onboarding_after_reject],
)

# Low-risk accepted route
low_risk_route = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
    children=[manager_approval, activate_vendor, post_activation],
)

# High-risk accepted route
high_risk_route = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
    children=[enhanced_due_diligence, compliance_approval, activate_vendor, post_activation],
)

# Choice between rejection, low-risk, and high-risk acceptance
post_classification_choice = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[reject_route, low_risk_route, high_risk_route],
)

# Root process tree
procedural_model = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
    children=[receive_request, checks_parallel, clarification_block, classify_risk, post_classification_choice],
)
```

### 3.3 Behavioral explanation

- **Concurrency**: The three checks (`Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, `Verify Bank Account`) are placed under a parallel (`Operator.PARALLEL`) node, allowing any ordering and even true overlap at runtime, as long as all three complete before `Classify Risk`.
- **Clarification loops**: The process tree explicitly supports zero or one clarification cycle via an XOR between τ and one `Request Clarification` → `Receive Clarification` sequence. The intended “0..n cycles” semantics is enforced in the DECLARE model (`response`/`altresponse`) and log skeleton (`always_after`), plus an external check that forbids final outcomes while a request is still open.
- **Choices**: After `Classify Risk`, an XOR node routes the case into one of three structurally disjoint branches: rejection (no activation or payment), low‑risk acceptance (manager approval), or high‑risk acceptance (enhanced due diligence plus compliance approval).
- **Terminal outcomes**: Rejection ends with `Close Onboarding` directly; both acceptance branches pass through `Activate Vendor`, then the post‑activation block, then `Release First Payment`, and finally `Close Onboarding`. The tree allows no traces with both `Reject Vendor` and `Activate Vendor`, and no traces without exactly one `Close Onboarding`.
- **Post-activation behaviour**: The post‑activation XOR allows either no bank change or a single `Change Bank Details` → `Suspend Vendor` → second `Verify Bank Account` cycle before `Release First Payment`. R11’s possibility of multiple such cycles is approximated; repeated changes are handled conservatively in the declarative and resource/data checks.
- **Excluded or approximated behavior**: `Sanctions Escalation` and `Obtain Legal Opinion` are not placed in the tree to avoid over‑constraining their order relative to enhanced due diligence and approvals; their obligations (R4–R6) are enforced declaratively, temporally, and in the resource/data layer. Similarly, the exact position of clarifications relative to the three checks is left unconstrained beyond “before classification”.

### 4.2 Rule directions and semantics

| Template | Key | Direction and semantics | Linked requirements |
|---|---|---|---|
| `init` | `"Receive Onboarding Request"` | This activity must be the first event of every trace. | Stable backbone start condition. |
| `existence` | `"Classify Risk"` | Every case must contain at least one `Classify Risk` event. | Backbone step 3. |
| `exactly_one` | `"Close Onboarding"` | Each case must contain exactly one `Close Onboarding`. | Ensures a single, explicit termination. |
| `response` | `("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification")` | Every `Request Clarification` must eventually be followed by a later `Receive Clarification`. | R3 (eventual answer). |
| `altresponse` | `("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification")` | Between any two requests (or between a request and case end), there must be a receive; no second request is allowed before the first is answered. | R3 (no overlapping requests). |
| `response` | `("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion")` | If escalation occurs, a legal opinion must eventually follow later in the trace. | R4 (existential link). |
| `response` | `("Manager Approval", "Activate Vendor")` | Any manager approval must eventually be followed by activation. | R2 (low‑risk path). |
| `response` | `("Compliance Approval", "Activate Vendor")` | Any compliance approval must eventually be followed by activation. | R2 (high‑risk path). |
| `response` | `("Change Bank Details", "Suspend Vendor")` | A suspension must eventually follow any post‑activation bank detail change. | R11 (first part). |
| `response` | `("Suspend Vendor", "Verify Bank Account")` | After suspension, a (re)verification must occur. | R11 (second part). |
| `precedence` | `("Run Sanctions Screening", "Sanctions Escalation")` | Any sanctions escalation must be preceded by at least one `Run Sanctions Screening`. | R7. |
| `precedence` | `("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment")` | Any first payment must be preceded by activation. | Backbone step 5. |
| `noncoexistence` | `("Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor")` | The two terminal decisions must never occur together in a single case. | R1. |
| `noncoexistence` | `("Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval")` | A case may not contain both kinds of approval. | R2, R8. |
| `noncoexistence` | `("Enhanced Due Diligence", "Manager Approval")` | Enhanced due diligence and manager approval must not co‑occur. | R8. |
| `nonsuccession` | `("Change Bank Details", "Release First Payment")` | No `Release First Payment` may occur after a bank change without at least one intervening event (combined with the response rules, this approximates “suspend and re‑verify before paying again”). | R11 (approximation). |

The DECLARE layer focuses on local order, existence, and noncoexistence constraints and deliberately omits the R5 precedence condition between `Obtain Legal Opinion` and `Activate Vendor`, which is instead handled in the explicit resource/data‑aware checks to avoid breaking non‑escalated cases.

## 5. Log-Skeleton Model

### 5.1 PM4Py `log_skeleton` dictionary

```python
log_skeleton = {
    "equivalence": set(),
    "always_before": {
        ("Receive Onboarding Request", "Classify Risk"),
        ("Run Sanctions Screening", "Sanctions Escalation"),
    },
    "always_after": {
        ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"),
        ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"),
    },
    "never_together": {
        ("Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor"),
        ("Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval"),
        ("Enhanced Due Diligence", "Manager Approval"),
    },
    "activ_freq": {
        "Activate Vendor": {0, 1},
        "Reject Vendor": {0, 1},
        "Release First Payment": {0, 1},
        "Close Onboarding": {1},
    },
    "directly_follows": set(),
}
```

### 5.2 Relation to other layers

- **Orientation**:
  - `always_before("Receive Onboarding Request", "Classify Risk")` expresses that any classification must be preceded by a request, harmonizing with the `init` and `existence` DECLARE rules and the initial segment of the process tree.
  - `always_before("Run Sanctions Screening", "Sanctions Escalation")` captures R7 in the same direction as the DECLARE precedence rule.
  - `always_after("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification")` acts as a log‑skeleton version of the DECLARE response rule for R3.
  - `always_after("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion")` mirrors the DECLARE response for R4 and is compatible with both R5 and R6 resolutions.
- **Noncoexistence and frequencies**:
  - `never_together` duplicates key noncoexistence constraints from DECLARE (R1, R2, R8), giving a second perspective based purely on relation mining.
  - `activ_freq` encodes R10 and the “exactly once” constraint for `Close Onboarding`. This overlaps with the process tree (no loops on those activities) and DECLARE’s `exactly_one`.
- **Overlap and disagreement**:
  - Where the process tree omits `Sanctions Escalation` and `Obtain Legal Opinion`, the log skeleton still constrains their occurrences using `always_before`/`always_after`. This means a trace can be perfectly conformant to the procedural backbone yet still fail the log skeleton due to sanctions‑related behavior.
  - Conversely, the skeleton does not encode the XOR branching logic of approvals vs rejection; violations like T8’s contradictory terminal outcomes only appear via `never_together` and `activ_freq`, not via structural routing.

## 6. Temporal Profile

### 6.1 PM4Py `temporal_profile` dictionary

All durations are in seconds.

```python
temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (
        48 * 3600,  # mean 48 hours
        24 * 3600,  # standard deviation 24 hours
    ),
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"): (
        12 * 3600,  # mean 12 hours
        6 * 3600,   # standard deviation 6 hours
    ),
    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"): (
        72 * 3600,  # mean 72 hours
        36 * 3600,  # standard deviation 36 hours
    ),
}

zeta = 2.0
```

### 6.2 Interpretation

- For each configured pair `(A, B)`, `pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile(log, temporal_profile, zeta=2.0)` compares observed A→B durations with the configured mean and standard deviation.
- Deviations with absolute z-score \(|t - \mu| / \sigma > zeta\) are reported as **warnings**, not hard violations:
  - **Rationale**: These expectations reflect normative service levels (SLA‑style performance constraints), not strict control‑flow rules. A slow clarification or late legal opinion is often undesirable but may be justifiable (e.g., waiting for external information).
  - The control‑flow consistency (whether the right activities occur in the right order) is already handled by the process tree, DECLARE, log skeleton, and resource/data checks. Temporal deviations refine the diagnosis but do not change a structurally conformant case into an automatic rejection.
- Missing B events (e.g., unanswered clarifications) are interpreted primarily as structural violations of R3/R4; the temporal profile layer can still flag them as extreme deviations, but the authoritative fail/pass decision comes from the other layers.

## 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

The following checks assume an event log in PM4Py format with at least these attributes:

- `trace.attributes["case:concept:name"]`: case identifier.
- `event["concept:name"]`: activity label.
- `event["org:resource"]`: resource id (may be missing).
- `event["time:timestamp"]`: timestamp (Python `datetime`).

Missing required attributes cause the corresponding check to return a `policy-dependent` result for the affected case, except where explicitly stated otherwise.

### 7.1 Segregation of duties (R9)

```python
from collections import defaultdict

def check_four_eyes_verify_vs_approval(log):
    """
    R9, first part: the resource performing 'Verify Bank Account'
    must differ from the resource performing the applicable approval
    ('Manager Approval' or 'Compliance Approval').
    """
    results = {}
    for trace in log:
        case_id = trace.attributes.get("case:concept:name", "<unknown>")
        verifiers = []
        approvers = []
        missing_resource = False

        for e in trace:
            if e["concept:name"] in {"Verify Bank Account", "Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval"}:
                if "org:resource" not in e:
                    missing_resource = True
                else:
                    if e["concept:name"] == "Verify Bank Account":
                        verifiers.append(e["org:resource"])
                    else:
                        approvers.append(e["org:resource"])

        if missing_resource:
            results[case_id] = ("policy-dependent", "Missing org:resource for verify/approval.")
            continue

        if not approvers:
            # No approval in this case (e.g., rejected) – R9 not applicable
            results[case_id] = ("pass", "No approval present.")
            continue

        if set(verifiers) & set(approvers):
            results[case_id] = ("fail", "Same resource performed verification and approval (R9 violated).")
        else:
            results[case_id] = ("pass", "Four-eyes between verification and approval satisfied.")

    return results


def check_four_eyes_masterdata_vs_activation(log):
    """
    R9, second part: the resource performing 'Capture Master Data'
    must differ from the resource performing 'Activate Vendor'.
    """
    results = {}
    for trace in log:
        case_id = trace.attributes.get("case:concept:name", "<unknown>")
        capturers = []
        activators = []
        missing_resource = False

        for e in trace:
            if e["concept:name"] in {"Capture Master Data", "Activate Vendor"}:
                if "org:resource" not in e:
                    missing_resource = True
                else:
                    if e["concept:name"] == "Capture Master Data":
                        capturers.append(e["org:resource"])
                    else:
                        activators.append(e["org:resource"])

        if missing_resource:
            results[case_id] = ("policy-dependent", "Missing org:resource for capture/activation.")
            continue

        if not activators:
            # No activation (rejected vendor)
            results[case_id] = ("pass", "No activation present.")
            continue

        if set(capturers) & set(activators):
            results[case_id] = ("fail", "Same resource performed capture and activation (R9 violated).")
        else:
            results[case_id] = ("pass", "Four-eyes between capture and activation satisfied.")

    return results
```

These checks could also be approximated using `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle`, but the explicit code makes the required attributes and behavior on missing data clear.

### 7.2 Strict legal escalation policy (R5 – implemented)

```python
from datetime import timedelta

def check_strict_legal_escalation_policy(log):
    """
    Implements R5 under the legal-first resolution:
    - If 'Sanctions Escalation' occurs in a case, then
      'Obtain Legal Opinion' must occur, and
      any 'Activate Vendor' must occur strictly after the first legal opinion.

    Returns a dict case_id -> (status, reason), where status is 'pass' or 'fail'.
    """
    results = {}
    for trace in log:
        case_id = trace.attributes.get("case:concept:name", "<unknown>")
        positions = {"Sanctions Escalation": [], "Obtain Legal Opinion": [], "Activate Vendor": []}

        for idx, e in enumerate(trace):
            act = e["concept:name"]
            if act in positions:
                positions[act].append(idx)

        if not positions["Sanctions Escalation"]:
            # No escalation: R5 not applicable, rely on R4 only.
            results[case_id] = ("pass", "No sanctions escalation.")
            continue

        if not positions["Obtain Legal Opinion"]:
            results[case_id] = ("fail", "Escalation without legal opinion (R4/R5 violated).")
            continue

        if positions["Activate Vendor"]:
            first_opinion_idx = positions["Obtain Legal Opinion"][0]
            first_activation_idx = positions["Activate Vendor"][0]
            if first_activation_idx <= first_opinion_idx:
                results[case_id] = (
                    "fail",
                    "Activation occurs before legal opinion after escalation (R5 violated).",
                )
            else:
                results[case_id] = ("pass", "Opinion precedes activation after escalation (R5 satisfied).")
        else:
            results[case_id] = ("pass", "Escalation with opinion but no activation in case.")

    return results
```

This check ignores the emergency allowance of R6 and treats every escalation case with activation before opinion as a legal violation, matching Resolution A.

### 7.3 Emergency-aware alternative (R6 – optional)

```python
def check_emergency_policy_variant(log, emergency_cases):
    """
    Implements Resolution B (operations-first) as an alternative to R5:
    - For cases without escalation, defer to R4 (handled elsewhere) and return 'pass'.
    - For cases with escalation and activation AFTER opinion: 'pass'.
    - For cases with escalation and activation BEFORE opinion:
        * If case_id not in emergency_cases: 'fail' (unauthorized early activation).
        * If case_id in emergency_cases:
            - 'Obtain Legal Opinion' must still occur.
            - Escalation -> opinion delay must be <= 24 hours.

    emergency_cases is a set of case ids authorized as emergencies.
    """
    results = {}
    max_delay = timedelta(hours=24)

    for trace in log:
        case_id = trace.attributes.get("case:concept:name", "<unknown>")
        esc_time = None
        act_time = None
        opinion_time = None

        for e in trace:
            act = e["concept:name"]
            if act == "Sanctions Escalation" and esc_time is None:
                esc_time = e.get("time:timestamp")
            elif act == "Obtain Legal Opinion" and opinion_time is None:
                opinion_time = e.get("time:timestamp")
            elif act == "Activate Vendor" and act_time is None:
                act_time = e.get("time:timestamp")

        if esc_time is None:
            results[case_id] = ("pass", "No sanctions escalation.")
            continue

        if opinion_time is None:
            results[case_id] = ("fail", "Escalation without legal opinion (R4 violated).")
            continue

        if act_time is None or act_time > opinion_time:
            # Either no activation or activation after opinion – compliant under both R5 and R6.
            results[case_id] = ("pass", "Opinion precedes activation or no activation.")
            continue

        # Activation before opinion: potential emergency.
        if case_id not in emergency_cases:
            results[case_id] = ("fail", "Early activation without emergency authorization (R6 violated).")
            continue

        if esc_time is None or opinion_time is None:
            results[case_id] = ("policy-dependent", "Missing timestamps for escalation/opinion.")
            continue

        if opinion_time - esc_time <= max_delay:
            results[case_id] = ("pass", "Authorized emergency activation with timely opinion (R6 satisfied).")
        else:
            results[case_id] = ("fail", "Authorized emergency, but opinion arrived too late (>24h).")

    return results
```

This alternative check relies on an external `emergency_cases` set; it is not used in the main verdicts but shows how T6 could be accepted when flagged as an emergency with a 17‑hour escalation→opinion delay.

### 7.4 Bank detail change obligations (R11)

```python
def check_bank_change_after_activation(log):
    """
    Implements R11:
    - If 'Change Bank Details' occurs after 'Activate Vendor', then
        * 'Suspend Vendor' must follow each change, and
        * a new 'Verify Bank Account' must occur after each suspension and
          before any subsequent 'Release First Payment'.
    """
    results = {}
    for trace in log:
        case_id = trace.attributes.get("case:concept:name", "<unknown>")
        # Collect indices of key activities
        idxs = {"Activate Vendor": [], "Change Bank Details": [], "Suspend Vendor": [], "Verify Bank Account": [], "Release First Payment": []}
        for i, e in enumerate(trace):
            act = e["concept:name"]
            if act in idxs:
                idxs[act].append(i)

        if not idxs["Change Bank Details"]:
            results[case_id] = ("pass", "No bank-detail changes after activation.")
            continue

        if not idxs["Activate Vendor"]:
            # Bank changes without activation are out of scope; flag conservatively.
            results[case_id] = ("policy-dependent", "Bank change without activation.")
            continue

        first_activation = idxs["Activate Vendor"][0]
        release_indices = idxs["Release First Payment"]

        ok = True
        reason = "All bank changes correctly suspended and re-verified."

        for change_idx in idxs["Change Bank Details"]:
            if change_idx <= first_activation:
                # Pre-activation change: not governed by R11.
                continue

            # Find the first suspend after this change.
            suspend_idx = next((i for i in idxs["Suspend Vendor"] if i > change_idx), None)
            if suspend_idx is None:
                ok = False
                reason = "Change Bank Details after activation without subsequent suspension."
                break

            # Find the first verify after this suspend.
            verify_idx = next((i for i in idxs["Verify Bank Account"] if i > suspend_idx), None)
            if verify_idx is None:
                ok = False
                reason = "Suspension not followed by re-verification."
                break

            # Ensure no Release First Payment occurs between change and verify.
            for rel_idx in release_indices:
                if change_idx < rel_idx < verify_idx:
                    ok = False
                    reason = "Payment released after change before re-verification."
                    break
            if not ok:
                break

        results[case_id] = ("pass", reason) if ok else ("fail", reason)

    return results
```

This check assumes that any additional `Verify Bank Account` after activation is a re‑verification of the current bank details. If timestamps or bank‑account identifiers are missing, the check becomes conservative and may need to be downgraded to `policy-dependent`.

## 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

- **Duplicated enforcement**: Several requirements (R1, R2, R8, R10, and parts of R11) are enforced simultaneously in the process tree, DECLARE, and log skeleton. This redundancy increases robustness but can generate multiple “duplicate” violations for a single defect; tooling should collapse such findings into a single root‑cause explanation where possible.
- **Loops and post-activation changes**: `Request Clarification` loops and multiple post‑activation bank‑change cycles cannot be perfectly represented in the block‑structured process tree. The tree supports at most one clarification cycle and one change/suspend/re‑verify cycle, while DECLARE and the resource/data layer handle the more general “0..n” behavior; inconsistencies may arise if the tree is treated as the sole source of truth.
- **Vacuous declarative rules**: Many DECLARE and log‑skeleton constraints (e.g., R3–R8) are vacuously satisfied when their antecedent activities do not occur (no escalation, no clarifications, no bank changes). This can hide under‑use of important controls; additional monitoring should track control activation rates rather than only violations.
- **Missing attributes and timestamp-only limitations**: The emergency authorization for R6 is outside the event log, and case‑level flags like `emergency_activation` may be absent or unreliable; resource attributes may also be missing. In such cases, checks must return `policy-dependent` rather than silently accepting the behavior. Even where timestamps are present, temporal profiles cannot distinguish intentional waiting (e.g., vendor delay) from internal inefficiencies, nor can they determine true concurrency when tasks overlap in time.
- **Support/confidence as normative truth**: Although all rules in this design are assigned `support = 1.0`, `confidence` should still be treated as descriptive metadata, not as a normative guarantee. Mining‑derived support/confidence values can be biased by sampling, incomplete logs, or historical non‑compliance; over‑trusting them risks encoding past bad practice as policy.

## 10. Deployment Recommendation

- **Canonical procedural representation**: Use the process tree defined in Section 3 as the canonical procedural model of the onboarding process, focusing on the stable backbone (from request through checks, classification, routing, activation/rejection, and closing) and only approximating loops and post‑activation variants.
- **Authoritative policy layer and check order**: Treat the resource/data-aware layer (segregation of duties, strict legal escalation policy, and bank‑change checks) plus the DECLARE rules as the primary sources of normative obligations; run conformance in the following order: (1) procedural alignment; (2) DECLARE conformance; (3) log‑skeleton sanity checks; (4) resource/data checks (including R5); and (5) temporal-profile analysis for performance warnings.
- **Handling contradictory findings**: When different layers disagree, report the earliest decisive structural violation (typically procedural, DECLARE, or resource/data) as the main failure, and attach log‑skeleton and temporal deviations as supporting evidence. For escalation cases like T6, present both R5‑based and R6‑based assessments explicitly, marking them as “legal‑first” versus “emergency‑aware” views so that compliance officers can make a documented policy decision.
