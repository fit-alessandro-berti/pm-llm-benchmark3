# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

Each network measures a different relation. Mira_Coordinator's position is only interpretable once the networks are read together.

**Handover-of-work network**
- **What it measures:** Directed transfers of work between consecutive events of the same case, weighted by frequency and elapsed time.
- **What it implies:** Mira_Coordinator is a major dispatch point. She receives from Bot_Rules (920 edges) and from the returning specialists and temporaries (Omar, Priya, Temp_1, Temp_2), and she sends to Omar, Priya, and four adjusters (about 1,250 edges in total). Out-degree centrality of 0.82 and betweenness of 0.77 mean she lies on many routing paths between automated triage and downstream specialists.
- **What it does not imply:** It does not show that she performs much substantive work on those cases. Handover edges count transitions, not effort. Her high centrality could come from many short routing events, from her being the only path for exceptions, or from rework loops that send cases back through her. High handover centrality is a structural fact, not evidence of quality or of harm.

**Working-together network**
- **What it measures:** Co-occurrence of two resources in the same cases, expressed as a percentage of cases.
- **What it implies:** Her co-case rates with adjusters (4–8%) are low, and her rates with the team leads (4–5%) are the lowest in the network. Her weighted degree is only 0.16, while Alice and Carla score 0.46 and 0.49. This means she is not embedded in the team-level sharing patterns that drive most adjuster work.
- **What it does not imply:** It does not show that she is peripheral to the process. The denominator matters. If the percentages are of all cases, then 8% with Carla is roughly 330 cases, or about 36% of Mira's roughly 905 cases. That is substantial. The metric is also sensitive to event length: a 30-second reassignment and a 2-hour negotiation count equally toward co-occurrence.

**Subcontracting network**
- **What it measures:** Patterns in which resource A hands to B and A reappears within two later events, a signature of delegation with return.
- **What it implies:** Mira's subcontracting betweenness is 0.69, and she appears in the top patterns with Omar (126), Priya (98), and Bot_Rules (62). The pattern "Request Missing Information  Medical Review  Assign/Reopen" suggests she delegates specialist work and then re-enters the case to decide the next step.
- **What it does not imply:** It does not show that she is delegating to be a supervisor. The same pattern appears for the team leads (Alice–Carla, Bruno–Diego), and for Temp_1 with Vendor_Verify. Re-entry after delegation is common to many coordination structures and can reflect either control or rework.

**Performance and time statistics**
- **What it measures:** Median service time per event, elapsed time between events, and cycle-time contribution.
- **What it implies:** Mira's median event is short (4.2 min), which is consistent with routing and assignment work. Her "high variance" cycle-time contribution suggests that her events are either quick or sit at the front of long exception paths. The absence observation (90th percentile cycle time for exception cases rising from 8.4 to 13.9 days, with ordinary cases unchanged) suggests her function is concentrated on the exception path.
- **What it does not imply:** Service time does not measure waiting time. Her cases may wait in her queue, and the metric does not show that. The absence finding comes from one 6-day window, which may be confounded by volume, seasonality, or other staffing changes. It also cannot separate "she was the only one who could route exceptions" from "nobody had a clear procedure during her absence."

**Role-discovery or activity-profile similarity**
- **What it measures:** Whether resources perform similar activities, which can be used to infer roles.
- **What it implies:** Her activity profile (Request Missing Information, Reopen Claim, Escalate Complaint, Assign Specialist) differs from both the adjusters and the team leads. The profile resembles an exception-desk or triage role rather than a team-lead or adjuster role. It is also distinct from the automated resources, which handle the routine path.
- **What it does not imply:** Activity similarity does not reveal authority. Two resources can perform the same activities with very different decision rights. Discovered roles are labels for behavior, not a description of formal authority or responsibility.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

**H1. Formal coordinator / exception desk**
- **Mechanism:** The organization designed a central exception queue. Bot_Rules routes non-standard cases to Mira, who requests missing information, reopens cases, handles complaints, and assigns specialists or adjusters. The exception desk is intended to be the single point for hard cases.
- **Evidence supporting it:** Bot_Rules  Mira is the largest routing edge after the ingest handover (920). Her profile is consistent with routing and assignment. She touches 64% of reopened claims and 71% of complaints, which matches an intake role for hard cases. The operations manager's view that removing her would increase formal escalations is consistent with a designed desk.
- **Evidence contradicting or limiting it:** No formal documentation of the desk is included here. The "queue cleaner" comment suggests the process owner may see the function as administrative rather than designed. Her 48% exception-flag share may reflect the routing rules rather than the design.
- **Observable predictions:** Routing rules (region, amount_band, priority, exception_flag) should explain most of Mira's assignments. Her queue should appear in documented SOPs. Her share of complaints and reopens should stay stable across time if the desk is designed.

**H2. Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency**
- **Mechanism:** Mira is the only effective path for exception cases. Specialists and adjusters depend on her to decide next steps, so exception cases queue behind her, and her absence accumulates work.
- **Evidence supporting it:** The absence result: exception-flagged cases' 90th percentile cycle time rose by about 66% (8.4 to 13.9 days) while ordinary cases did not change. Her handover betweenness (0.77) and subcontracting betweenness (0.69) are the highest in the network. Her handovers to specialists take 16–51 min, and she has no comparable backup in the network.
- **Evidence contradicting or limiting it:** Her median service time is short (4.2 min), so the bottleneck may lie in waiting, not in her own processing. One absence is weak evidence. The adjusters and the operations manager may have compensated informally during her absence, which the log may not record. Exception cases may also have been delayed by specialist capacity (Omar, Priya) rather than by her.
- **Observable predictions:** Queue waiting time before Mira's events should increase during absence and recover afterward. Exception cases that skip her (if any) should show lower cycle time but more reopen rates. Specialist arrival times should not change during her absence if the bottleneck is her.

**H3. Organizational boundary spanner / triage broker**
- **Mechanism:** Mira connects structures that otherwise do not share work: automated triage, two team silos (North and South), specialist groups (medical and legal), and the temporaries. She transfers information and cases across these boundaries without being embedded in any single team's sharing pattern.
- **Evidence supporting it:** Her structural position is consistent with brokerage: high handover betweenness, low working-together weighted degree, and absence of persistent team pairs. The team pairs (Alice–Carla, Bruno–Diego) are dense internally, and she sits between them. Adjusters' view that she is "the person who knows where hard cases should go" is consistent with brokerage knowledge.
- **Evidence contradicting or limiting it:** Brokerage would typically produce bridging co-work, and her co-case rates with both teams are low. A pure broker may not need deep co-work, but the observed pattern does not yet confirm the brokerage mechanism.
- **Observable predictions:** Her handovers should cross team boundaries (North  South, adjuster  specialist) more often than within-team handovers. Cases she touches should show more cross-team variants. Removing her should increase the number of cases with no cross-team route, not just increase queue time.

**H4. Quality gatekeeper or rework generator (ambiguous direction)**
- **Mechanism:** Mira detects defects (missing information, incorrect validation, complaints) and sends cases back for rework. This could be a quality control function (she catches problems) or a rework-generating function (her reopen decisions create the rework loops).
- **Evidence supporting it:** She touches 64% of reopened claims. The "Request Missing Information  Medical Review  Reopen" fragments suggest repeated returns. Several handovers from her to adjusters are labeled "rework/missing info" or "reassignment after missing info."
- **Evidence contradicting or limiting it:** Reopen may be an effect of the case's complexity rather than of her decisions. Without outcome data, the log cannot tell whether her interventions improve or degrade final quality.
- **Observable predictions:** Reopen rates should be compared for cases she handles versus similar cases she does not handle, controlling for case_type, amount_band, and region. If she is a quality gatekeeper, cases she releases should show fewer subsequent reopens, not more.

**H5. Data artifact or logging structure**
- **Mechanism:** Some of Mira's events are not her own work. They may be system-generated routing events, supervisory overrides, or events logged under a shared or generic account. Her apparent centrality could reflect how events are attributed, not how work is performed.
- **Evidence supporting it:** "Assign Specialist" appears in the resource table but not in the activity list for the 14 claims-process activities. Her short "routing," "assignment," "reopen," and "clarification" events may be system-generated. Her 48% exception share could partly reflect how the exception flag is set when she logs an event.
- **Evidence contradicting or limiting it:** The handover times to specialists (42–51 min) look like human elapsed times, not automated ones. The absence finding suggests a real operational dependency, and that is hard to explain by logging alone.
- **Observable predictions:** Checking event sources (system IDs, shared logins, timestamps with exact second-level clustering) should reveal whether some of her events are automated. A logging change or activity-catalogue fix should shift her metrics without changing outcomes.

**H6. Automation monitor / override supervisor**
- **Mechanism:** Mira supervises Bot_Rules, reviewing or overriding automated fraud screens and routing. Her interventions are the human half of an automated decision loop.
- **Evidence supporting it:** The subcontracting pattern "Bot_Rules  Mira  Fraud Screen update" (62 instances) indicates re-entry into automated screening. Bot_Rules' 31% exception share is high, and Mira handles the exceptions it generates.
- **Evidence contradicting or limiting it:** The number of such override loops (62) is small relative to Bot_Rules' 4,750 events. Most automated routing does not involve her.
- **Observable predictions:** Overrides of Bot_Rules decisions should be logged, and their rate should correlate with rule changes. If she is an override supervisor, the override rate should fall when rules are better calibrated.

**H7. Informal team lead / shadow leader (weaker hypothesis)**
- **Mechanism:** Adjusters rely on her for decisions beyond her formal role, so she shapes team outcomes without formal authority.
- **Evidence supporting it:** Adjuster comments about her knowledge, and her position in the handover network.
- **Evidence contradicting or limiting it:** Her working-together ties to adjusters and team leads are weak. Team leads (Alice, Bruno) have the strong team-level ties, which is the signature of formal team leadership in this data. The process owner's view argues against a formal team-lead role.
- **Observable predictions:** Informal leadership would show up as decision requests from adjusters to her that are not mirrored by formal assignments, but such requests are not visible in the current log.

These hypotheses are not mutually exclusive. The most likely reading is a combination: H1 as the designed function, H2 and H3 as the emergent properties, and H4 and H5 as open questions that the log cannot yet resolve.

# 3. Risk assessment

**Bottleneck risk (moderate to high, uncertain).** The absence evidence suggests that exception cases depend on her. The short median service time suggests the constraint may be queueing, not her processing. Measure queue waiting before her events before attributing delay to her.

**Coordination risk (moderate).** Her handovers to specialists take 16–51 minutes, and her working-together ties are weak. Decisions may be made without shared context: a routing decision may not reflect what the adjuster has already learned about the case. This risk is structural, not evidence of error.

**Key-person risk (high).** The exception path has no evidenced substitute in the log. Absence increased exception-case cycle time by about 5.5 days at the 90th percentile. A departure or extended leave without a trained deputy would produce the same effect at larger scale.

**Compliance and control risk (moderate, requires care).** She appears both to detect problems (missing information, complaints, reopens, exception flags) and to select the next specialist or adjuster. This concentrates detection and routing in one role, which weakens segregation of duties. This is a control design concern, not evidence of wrongdoing. The log shows no misuse of discretion, and nothing here should be read as an allegation. The concern is that, without a second check, errors or biases in her routing decisions would be hard to detect. Her exception-flag share (48%) also raises the question of whether she sets the flag that routes cases to her.

**Workload fairness risk (moderate).** She touches 22% of claims but 64% of reopens and 71% of complaints, which are the most demanding categories. Her work is short per event, so workload measures based on event count may understate its difficulty. Adjusters may also benefit from her routing while she carries the cost.

**Data-quality risk (high).** Several issues need resolution before strong conclusions are drawn:
- "Assign Specialist" appears in the resource table but not in the activity catalogue.
- The working-together denominator is not specified.
- The exception flag may be set by the same role that routes the case, creating circularity.
- Some routing events may be system-generated under her attribution.
- Absence evidence comes from one window with possible confounders.

**Caution about centrality.** High centrality is not automatically good or bad. A high-betweenness exception desk can be efficient and well-controlled, or it can be a single point of failure. The network statistics show where she sits, not whether her position is healthy.

# 4. Further process-mining analyses

**Handover-of-work network**
- Build the handover network separately for exception_flag=True and exception_flag=False cases, and compare Mira's in- and out-edges. Test whether her dispatch targets (Omar, Priya, adjusters) depend on region, priority, or amount_band, using a decision-tree or logistic model over the routing decisions.
- With PM4Py, `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` can be applied to a filtered log, with `pm4py.format_dataframe` ensuring the correct case, activity, timestamp, and resource columns. Parameter names may differ across PM4Py versions, so confirm them against the installed release.

**Working-together network**
- Recompute co-case rates with explicit denominators: (a) share of all cases, (b) share of cases touched by either resource, and (c) share of cases touched by the first resource. This resolves whether the low rates are real or a denominator artifact.
- Compute co-work weighted by event duration rather than by case presence, to test whether her short events undercount her contribution.

**Subcontracting network**
- Use `pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network` on the exception-flagged subset with n=2 and n=3. Compare the Mira–Omar and Alice–Carla patterns: if Mira's re-entry is a control pattern, the re-entry should follow specialist returns; if it is rework, re-entry should follow reopens.
- Test whether re-entry predicts subsequent reopens, using a survival analysis on reopen timing.

**Activity-based resource similarity and organizational roles**
- Run `pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` and `pm4py.discover_organizational_roles` on the full log, then on the pre-absence and absence windows. Check whether Mira's role cluster is stable and whether any other resource moves toward her profile during her absence (evidence of informal substitution).
- Compare her activity profile with the same profile for an alternative set of resources, to identify candidate deputies.

**Filtering by exception_flag, activity, variants, or time ranges**
- Filter with `pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values` on exception_flag and on the activity "Assign Specialist" to check whether that activity is system-generated or human.
- Use `pm4py.filter_variants` to compare the top variants that include Mira with those that do not. Check whether the variants with Mira have higher reopen rates, higher cycle times, or both.
- Define the absence window and compare the pre-absence, absence, and post-absence periods, controlling for weekly volume and case_type mix. This tests whether the absence effect persists or is a one-off.

**Service and waiting-time analysis**
- Use `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` and `pm4py.get_service_time` to split Mira's cases into service time and queue waiting time. Test H2 directly: if waiting before her events dominates, the bottleneck is queueing, not her processing.
- Compare waiting time for exception cases routed to Mira with those routed directly to specialists (if any such cases exist), to estimate whether her routing adds delay.

**Conformance and decision analysis (supplementary)**
- Define routing rules from documented policy (for example, complaints go to Mira; medical flags go to Omar) and check conformance of Mira's assignments. Deviations are not necessarily errors, but they indicate discretion that is not captured by rules, which is directly relevant to the compliance concern.

**Data-quality checks**
- Check event source fields, timestamp clustering, and lifecycle transitions for Mira's events to identify system-generated or batch-logged events (H5).

# 5. Recommendation

**Do not remove or restructure Mira's role based on these network statistics.** Removing her would shift exception routing, complaints, and reopens to other resources without a defined replacement. The operations manager's concern about specialist overload and escalations is plausible, and the absence evidence suggests the function has no tested substitute.

**Change immediately:**
- Correct the data gaps: add "Assign Specialist" to the activity catalogue with a clear definition, and confirm which of Mira's events are human-entered versus system-generated.
- Document the exception-routing rules (which cases go to which specialist, and under what conditions) so that her discretion is visible. Record a short rationale field for each routing decision on complaints, medical, and legal exceptions.
- Designate and train a backup exception coordinator now, so that an absence does not again leave exception cases unrouted. The backup should be someone who has worked alongside her, and should be drawn from the team-lead or senior-adjuster group if possible. This is the replacement function the desk needs.
- Introduce a second check on routing decisions for high-impact categories (complaints and legal/medical exceptions), so that one person is not both detecting and selecting. This addresses the control concern without assuming misconduct.

**Monitor:**
- Exception-case cycle time (median and 90th percentile), split into queue waiting and service time, by week.
- Reopen rates for cases she releases versus cases released by others, controlled for case type and amount band.
- Her workload distribution (events, cases, and hours), and whether her share of complex categories is changing.
- Specialist (Omar, Priya) utilization and waiting time before specialist events, to test whether she is creating overload or simply exposing existing capacity limits.
- Cross-team handover patterns (North  South, adjuster  specialist) to test the brokerage hypothesis.
- Recurrence of absence effects if any future leave occurs.

**Do not change until more evidence is available:**
- Do not reclassify her as a team lead or as an adjuster based on her network position or the adjusters' comments.
- Do not redesign reporting lines or job descriptions based on working-together statistics, which are sensitive to denominators and event length.
- Do not reassign specialists or change routing rules based on a single absence window.
- Do not draw conclusions about the quality of her decisions until outcome data (final settlement accuracy, complaint resolution, reopen causes) is linked to her decisions.
- Do not treat the compliance concern as a finding of misconduct. It is a control design question to be resolved through documentation and a second check, not through an investigation of the individual.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira Coordinator runs the exception desk for claims that the automated system cannot handle. Her work is short and frequent, so the data shows her as a central point for routing and reopening cases, while her direct collaboration with the adjusters is limited. The main concern is dependency. When she was absent for six days, complex cases waited much longer, and no other person clearly took over that role. The process also depends on one person both spotting problems and choosing who handles them, which needs a second check to stay controlled. Her role should not be removed or reclassified based on these statistics. The practical next steps are to document her routing rules, train a backup coordinator, add a review step for the most sensitive decisions, and track waiting times for complex cases. Some of the data also needs correcting before firm conclusions are drawn.
