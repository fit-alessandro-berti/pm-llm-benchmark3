# 2. Benign alternative explanations

A calibrated assessment requires evaluating plausible non-fraudulent explanations for observed anomalies. Below are benign alternative mechanisms for each major suspicious pattern, along with the evidence required to support or refute them.

### Pattern A: VendorMaster_Lena & AP_Kim Bank Changes, Fast Approvals, and Vendor V-77x Concentration
1. **Benign Explanation 1: Enterprise Banking Platform Migration for Vendor Family V-77x**
   * *Mechanism:* Vendor family V-77x underwent a legitimate corporate banking transition. Master data updates were executed via mass maintenance/manual upload files, and AP_Kim (assigned to high-risk/complex suppliers) prioritized payments to prevent supply chain disruption.
   * *Supporting Evidence:* IT/Treasury change management tickets documenting the V-77x banking migration; verified bank confirmation letters and independent call-back logs dated prior to changes; official vendor tax ID/IBAN matching the master record; lack of linkage tickets explained by known ERP logging gaps.
   * *Refuting Evidence:* Absence of external banking confirmation documentation; discrepancies between invoice payment instructions and master file changes; payments routed to accounts registered under unrelated corporate entities or individual names.
2. **Benign Explanation 2: Process Specialization and Pre-Coordinated Payment Run Deadlines**
   * *Mechanism:* AP_Kim is formally assigned to higher-risk vendor accounts. When critical V-77x invoices were pending payment run cut-offs, Kim coordinated with Lena via telephone/email to ensure bank master updates were committed just before payment release, resulting in a short elapsed duration (17 min).
   * *Supporting Evidence:* Formal Accounts Payable desk allocation schedules assigning Kim to V-77x; email/ticketing records requesting urgent master updates prior to scheduled payment runs; standard invoices with valid purchase orders and verified goods receipts.
   * *Refuting Evidence:* Off-cycle manual payment releases outside scheduled payment batches; transactions lacking valid underlying three-way matches; lack of any contemporaneous communication records.

### Pattern B: Buyer_Carla & Approver_Gita Rapid PO Approvals and Subcontracting Loops
1. **Benign Explanation 1: Clinical Urgency and Pre-Negotiated Framework Agreements**
   * *Mechanism:* Carla specializes in emergency medical supplies where stock-outs threaten patient care. Orders are placed against pre-negotiated catalog/framework contracts with pre-approved pricing, enabling Gita to perform rapid verification (8 min median) of quantity and delivery urgency.
   * *Supporting Evidence:* Hospital clinical requisition timestamps indicating urgent patient need; references to valid master framework contracts (`contract_type="framework"`); matched goods receipts at hospital receiving docks; tight correlation with `emergency_flag=True` (88%).
   * *Refuting Evidence:* Purchases of non-emergency or off-catalog goods labeled with emergency flags; pricing exceeding standard master rates; split purchase orders avoiding threshold limits; missing physical inventory receipts.
2. **Benign Explanation 2: Shared Operations Desk and Assistant Delegation**
   * *Mechanism:* During hospital emergency surges, procurement assistants enter orders under the supervisor's physical terminal or shared account (`Approver_Gita`), and direct phone/in-person communication between Carla and the desk enables immediate sign-off.
   * *Supporting Evidence:* Granular audit trail logs showing assistant user IDs in `entered_by` despite `org:resource="Approver_Gita"`; physical shift logs in the emergency procurement room; workstation IP logs.
   * *Refuting Evidence:* Approvals originating from external IP addresses or off-hours without assistant shift records; total absence of assistant identifiers across the audit trail.

### Pattern C: Buyer_Dan & Manager_Hugo Rapid Weekend Approvals and Subcontracting Loops
1. **Benign Explanation 1: Urgent Facilities Maintenance Breakdowns**
   * *Mechanism:* Facilities repairs frequently involve urgent infrastructure failures (e.g., plumbing leaks, HVAC failure, electrical outages) occurring outside standard business hours. Hugo authorizes repairs rapidly (10 min median) to prevent property damage or regulatory non-compliance.
   * *Supporting Evidence:* Facilities incident management tickets matching case dates; security badge access logs for third-party repair technicians on weekends; completed maintenance inspection reports.
   * *Refuting Evidence:* Routine, non-critical maintenance scheduled as emergency repairs; repetitive single-source awards to specific contractors without standard rate sheets; absence of physical facility access logs.
2. **Benign Explanation 2: Synchronous Verbal Escalation Prior to System Entry**
   * *Mechanism:* Dan contacts Hugo verbally (phone/radio) to describe the breakdown and agree on scope and contractor selection before entering the PO. Once Dan creates the PO, Hugo is already awaiting it and approves immediately.
   * *Supporting Evidence:* Mobile call logs or radio dispatch logs preceding PO creation; documented operational procedures authorizing verbal pre-approvals for urgent repairs.
   * *Refuting Evidence:* PO creation occurring days after invoice submission with no contemporaneous records; split invoices clustering below Hugo's authorization threshold.

### Pattern D: Same-Person Emergency Override and Retrospective Justification Batching
1. **Benign Explanation 1: Quarter-End Financial Close Administrative Data Migration**
   * *Mechanism:* Emergency overrides were legitimately authorized operationally during the quarter, but documentation was maintained in offline spreadsheets. During quarter-end reconciliation, a single administrator uploaded these records in bulk via `quarter_close_upload`, creating identical timestamps (12 cases) and apparent self-justifications.
   * *Supporting Evidence:* Physical/electronic justification memos signed by department heads dating back to the actual emergency events; batch upload job logs from `quarter_close_upload`; financial close reconciliation packs.
   * *Refuting Evidence:* Generic, boilerplate justification texts lacking specific event details; retroactive justifications created for unauthorized spending without underlying departmental records.
2. **Benign Explanation 2: Workflow Routing Limitation in Exception Management System**
   * *Mechanism:* The ERP workflow engine lacked a distinct task queue for independent retrospective justifications, forcing the manager who initiated the override to administratively complete the task to close the workflow ticket.
   * *Supporting Evidence:* System configuration scripts showing no routing rule for secondary justification; helpdesk tickets requesting workflow clearance assistance.
   * *Refuting Evidence:* Evidence that secondary routing was technically enforced and successfully utilized by other departments.

### Pattern E: Legacy ERP Bot_PO Self-Approval / Four-Eyes Collisions
1. **Benign Explanation 1: Middleware User-Context Mapping Error**
   * *Mechanism:* In `source_system="legacy_erp"`, the interface middleware populates `org:resource` for `Create Purchase Order` using the session ID of the subsequent human approver rather than the system account `Bot_PO`, creating an artifactual four-eyes violation.
   * *Supporting Evidence:* Middleware integration code showing user ID inheritance bugs; IT change tickets documenting legacy mapping defects; event attribute `entered_by` populated as `Bot_PO` while `org:resource` shows the human approver.
   * *Refuting Evidence:* User activity audit logs confirming interactive human creation of the PO via GUI at the exact timestamp.

# 4. Network and conformance analyses

The following Python script utilizing `pm4py` and `pandas` implements the complete analytical pipeline: filtering four-eyes violations, generating organizational interaction networks, comparing emergency vs. standard cohorts, evaluating path performance, evaluating declarative and log-skeleton conformance, and isolating source-system effects.

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Load and Prepare Event Log
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
def load_and_format_log(file_path: str):
    df = pd.read_csv(file_path)
    df['time:timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(df['time:timestamp'])
    
    # Format dataframe to PM4Py standards
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id='case:concept:name',
        activity_key='concept:name',
        timestamp_key='time:timestamp'
    )
    event_log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df)
    return df, event_log

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Four-Eyes Principle Conformance Filtering
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
def detect_four_eyes_violations(df: pd.DataFrame):
    """
    Identifies cases where the same resource executed segregated activity pairs.
    """
    # Filter using PM4Py built-in four-eyes principle filter
    po_violations = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
        df, 
        "Create Purchase Order", 
        "Approve Purchase Order"
    )
    
    bank_payment_violations = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
        df, 
        "Change Vendor Bank Details", 
        "Payment Approval"
    )
    
    override_just_violations = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
        df, 
        "Emergency Override", 
        "Retrospective Justification"
    )
    
    print(f"PO Creation/Approval Violations: {po_violations['case:concept:name'].nunique()} cases")
    print(f"Bank Change/Payment Approval Violations: {bank_payment_violations['case:concept:name'].nunique()} cases")
    print(f"Override/Justification Violations: {override_just_violations['case:concept:name'].nunique()} cases")
    
    return {
        "po_violations": po_violations,
        "bank_pay_violations": bank_payment_violations,
        "override_violations": override_just_violations
    }

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Organizational Network Analysis (SNA)
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
def discover_organizational_networks(event_log):
    """
    Discovers Handover of Work, Working Together, and Subcontracting Networks.
    """
    # Handover of Work Network
    handover_net = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(event_log)
    
    # Working Together Network
    working_together_net = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(event_log)
    
    # Subcontracting Network (n=2: A -> B -> A)
    subcontracting_net = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(event_log)
    
    return handover_net, working_together_net, subcontracting_net

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Cohort Comparison: Emergency vs. Non-Emergency Cases
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
def compare_emergency_cohorts(df: pd.DataFrame):
    """
    Compares process variants, durations, and networks between cohorts.
    """
    df_emergency = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        df, "emergency_flag", [True], level="case", retain=True
    )
    df_standard = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        df, "emergency_flag", [False], level="case", retain=True
    )
    
    log_emergency = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df_emergency)
    log_standard = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df_standard)
    
    variants_em = pm4py.get_variants(log_emergency)
    variants_std = pm4py.get_variants(log_standard)
    
    print(f"Emergency Cohort: {len(log_emergency)} cases, {len(variants_em)} variants")
    print(f"Standard Cohort: {len(log_standard)} cases, {len(variants_std)} variants")
    
    return df_emergency, df_standard

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Timing and Path Performance Analysis
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
def analyze_path_performance(df: pd.DataFrame):
    """
    Filters paths with anomalous throughput times (e.g. ultra-fast handoffs).
    """
    # Insert service and waiting times
    df_performance = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df)
    
    # Filter fast Bank Change -> Payment Approval (< 30 minutes / 1800 sec)
    fast_bank_payments = pm4py.filter_paths_performance(
        df,
        [("Change Vendor Bank Details", "Payment Approval")],
        min_duration=0,
        max_duration=1800
    )
    
    # Filter fast PO approvals (< 15 minutes / 900 sec)
    fast_po_approvals = pm4py.filter_paths_performance(
        df,
        [("Create Purchase Order", "Approve Purchase Order")],
        min_duration=0,
        max_duration=900
    )
    
    return fast_bank_payments, fast_po_approvals, df_performance

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Declarative Rule & Log-Skeleton Conformance
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
def evaluate_declarative_conformance(event_log, df: pd.DataFrame):
    """
    Checks temporal and separation rules for Retrospective Justifications:
    1. Emergency Override must eventually be followed by Retrospective Justification.
    2. Retrospective Justification must occur within 3 working days (72 hours).
    3. Retrospective Justification must be executed by a distinct human resource.
    """
    # 6a. Discover and check Log Skeleton
    skeleton = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(event_log)
    skeleton_conformance = pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton(event_log, skeleton)
    
    # 6b. Check 3-day SLA and distinct resource rule programmatically
    overrides = df[df['concept:name'] == 'Emergency Override'][
        ['case:concept:name', 'time:timestamp', 'org:resource']
    ].rename(columns={'time:timestamp': 'override_time', 'org:resource': 'override_user'})
    
    justifications = df[df['concept:name'] == 'Retrospective Justification'][
        ['case:concept:name', 'time:timestamp', 'org:resource']
    ].rename(columns={'time:timestamp': 'just_time', 'org:resource': 'just_user'})
    
    merged = pd.merge(overrides, justifications, on='case:concept:name', how='left')
    merged['delay_hours'] = (merged['just_time'] - merged['override_time']).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0
    merged['same_resource'] = merged['override_user'] == merged['just_user']
    merged['sla_breached'] = (merged['delay_hours'] > 72) | (merged['just_time'].isna())
    
    sla_violations = merged[merged['sla_breached']]
    self_justifications = merged[merged['same_resource']]
    
    print(f"SLA Breaches (>72h or missing): {len(sla_violations)} cases")
    print(f"Self-Justifications: {len(self_justifications)} cases")
    
    return skeleton_conformance, merged

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 7. Source-System Segmentation Analysis
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
def isolate_source_system_effects(df: pd.DataFrame):
    """
    Segments metrics by source_system to detect system-specific biases.
    """
    systems = df['source_system'].unique()
    system_metrics = {}
    
    for sys in systems:
        sys_df = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            df, "source_system", [sys], level="event", retain=True
        )
        sys_cases = sys_df['case:concept:name'].nunique()
        manual_events = len(sys_df)
        
        system_metrics[sys] = {
            "cases": sys_cases,
            "events": manual_events,
            "unique_resources": sys_df['org:resource'].nunique()
        }
        print(f"System: {sys:<25} | Cases: {sys_cases:<6} | Events: {manual_events:<6}")
        
    return system_metrics
```

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

During our process mining review of the procure-to-pay event log, internal audit identified several control exceptions, workflow bottlenecks, and data-quality anomalies. We emphasize that process deviations and repeated resource interactions do not, in themselves, substantiate intentional misconduct or collusion. Many observed patterns—including rapid approvals and tight handover loops—are consistent with operational necessity, such as urgent medical procurement and emergency weekend facility repairs, or administrative practices during quarter-end closing.

However, the analysis identified a critical internal control vulnerability within the Accounts Payable and Vendor Master workflows. Specifically, the resource pair VendorMaster_Lena and AP_Kim processed 44 transactions characterized by high financial materiality (68% high-value band), extreme vendor concentration (72% within vendor family V-77x), rapid turnaround times (17-minute median between bank detail changes and payment release), and a heavy reliance on manual file uploads (83%). This subgroup also accounted for 9 instances where bank modifications and payment releases lacked independent four-eyes segregation. This combination represents an elevated disbursement control risk requiring immediate substantive review.

Furthermore, audit conclusions are subject to data-quality limitations, including legacy system user-mapping errors that falsely attributed automated bot actions to human approvers, shared credential usage for emergency approvals, and unlinked batch spreadsheet migrations.

We recommend the following targeted actions:
1. Conduct targeted substantive testing of all bank detail changes and disbursements for vendor family V-77x, verifying independent callback documentation and beneficiary accounts.
2. Eliminate shared accounts and remediate legacy ERP user-attribution mapping errors.
3. Enforce automated four-eyes controls within the ERP to prevent self-approvals and unsegregated master data adjustments.
