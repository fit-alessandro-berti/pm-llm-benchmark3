## 1. Current-process diagnosis

The warranty-claims process exhibits a deep tension between two dominant paths, visible across the process-mining artifacts. The **fast path** (Intake claim  Auto eligibility screen  Fast approval  Dispatch replacement  Quality/compliance close) is numerically prevalent and superficially attractive: the directly-follows graph shows 2550 cases flowing AutoFast approval, and the performance DFG reports small median cycle times (0.5h to auto-screen, 0.4h to fast approval, 1.8h to dispatch). Yet the same data reveal serious hidden costs.

**Key problems**

1. **Rework and complaint amplification** – The fast path generates a downstream bulge of complaints and rework. *Customer complaint/reopen* occurs in 14.1% of Fastapproval cases but only 3.3% of cases following Repair quote  Customer approval  Repair device. Fastapproval cases are 31% cheaper before rework, but only 9% cheaper after accounting for complaints, reshipments, and service credits. This is a direct contradiction: the more attractive the path on speed and cost, the worse its true performance once quality is considered.

2. **Compliance deviation hotspots** – Tokenbased replay shows only 68% fitting traces (fitness 0.91). Alignment diagnostics flag “Fast approval before Evidence completeness,” “Dispatch replacement without risk review for highvalue devices,” and “Customer complaint/reopen after close” as frequent moveonlog deviations. Normative rule R2 is routinely broken, and R1 is bypassed for claims above EUR400.

3. **Selfreinforcing evidence loops** – The *Request missing evidence* activity is entered by 890+650 cases, and *26% of cases that enter it do so at least twice*. The cycle Request missing evidence  Manual eligibility review takes a median of 26h (p9088h), turning “evidence completion” into a major delay driver and leading to 320 abandonments.

4. **Segment mismatch** – Customersegment outcomes highlight the contradiction. For *Premium highvalue device* claims, the fast path median is only 20h but the complaint/reopen rate is 22%; the reviewed/repair path median is 96h with only 5% complaints. ## 2. Redesign A: SegmentAware Triage with PreDispatch Quality Gate

### Redesigned process flow
- **New autoscreen 2.0** – The rules engine is updated with segment attributes (Premium/Standard), devicerisk category (lowrisk accessory / midrisk / highvalue), and defecttype clarity. It routes claims to four channels:
  1. *Fastlane eligible* – Premium lowrisk accessory EUR400 and Standard lowrisk accessory where evidence completeness is already confirmed by the intake portal.
  2. *Standardreview* – All other lowrisk accessories and midrisk devices, sent to manual eligibility review with the output of the new evidenceportal check.
  3. *Highvalue mandatory review* – All highvalue devices, directed to combined manualeligibility + fraud/risk review before any approval.
  4. *Uncleardefect triage* – Claims with incomplete evidence or ambiguous fault, redirected to the dedicated evidenceportal first, then manual review.
- **Predispatch quality gate** – Any dispatch (replacement or repair) for devices above “Premium lowrisk accessory EUR400” must pass a lightweight quality gate. The gate verifies: evidence completeness, compliance with R1/R2, correct partnumber match against the defecttype database, and customerhistory risk score. Gate duration is targeted at 6h median, using a pooled team of senior reviewers.
- **Evidence portal with nudges** – The portal is offered, but for customers with low digital literacy a phoneassisted evidencecapture workflow runs in parallel to avoid abandonment.

### Segments/paths targeted
Primarily highvalue devices, uncleardefect claims, and the fastpath volume that currently generates 14% complaint/reopen. Premium lowrisk accessory fast lane is preserved to meet servicelevel agreements.

### Improvement mechanism
- **Compliance by design**: The gate enforces R1 and R2 before dispatch, directly eliminating the “Dispatch without risk review” and “Fastapproval before evidence completeness” deviations.
- **Rework prevention**: Wrongreplacement cost (EUR410 / EUR95) is caught at the gate, drastically reducing the *Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen* edge (p90180h) and the 14.1% complaint rate on fastapproval cases.
- **Evidenceloop reduction**: Portal reduces repeated *Request missing evidence* cycles (currently 26% looping) by 30–45%, shortening median cycle time for uncleardefect cases and cutting abandonment.
- **Segmentappropriate speed**: Premium lowrisk accessories stay on the 8hour fast path; highvalue devices are protected from the false economy of bypassing review.

### What it deliberately sacrifices
- Adds 6–18h to the dispatch stage for many nonpremium cases, potentially pushing Standard lowrisk accessory fastpath cycle times beyond the 72hour tolerance if gate capacity is insufficient.
- Leaves the manualreview and fraudreview capacities unchanged, meaning the increased inflow to Standardreview and Highvalue review may deepen the existing queue problem unless resources are later rebalanced.

## 3. Redesign B: Upstream Quality Investment and Capacity Rebalancing

### Redesigned process flow
- **Expand manualeligibility capacity** – Add two reviewers (from 7 to 9) to keep utilisation below 80%, absorbing any influx from better triage or evidencecompletion rules.
- **Eliminate the evidenceloop spiral** – When missing evidence is requested, the customer gets one 48hour window; if incomplete, the claim is automatically escalated to a “reviewwithincompleteevidence” track where a senior eligibility officer makes a binding decision based on available data, avoiding the 34h median loop and repeated requests.
- **Realtime partprice lookup** – Deploy a database integrated with supplier feeds so that the *Repair quote  Customer approval* step is reduced from median 21h to a target of 4h.
- **Continuous escalation capacity** – Replace the daily 45minute slot with an ondemand escalation pod (two senior staff) available during business hours, preventing the 54h median complainttoescalation delay.
- **Hard R2 enforcement** – Systemlevel block: the “Dispatch replacement” activity is disabled for any highvalue device until manual eligibility review and fraud/risk review are both completed and recorded, with an exception only for Premium lowrisk accessory claims EUR400.
- **Postrepair qualitycheck redesign** – Quality check is moved directly before dispatch (for repairs) and its inspection scope widened to include replacementpart verification, sharing the gate function with Redesign A but embedded in the repair flow.

### Segments/paths targeted
The entire reviewed/repair path, the missingevidence spiral, and the complaintreopen feedback loop. By strengthening the slow path’s efficiency and capacity, the redesign removes the excuse for bypassing it with the risky fast path.

### Improvement mechanism
- **Cycle time on reviewed path collapses**: Cutting repairquote delay and eliminating the evidenceloop spiral reduces the reviewedpath median for Premium highvalue from 96h to an estimated 50–60h, making it competitive with the fast path once rework is accounted for.
- **Rework elimination**: The complaint/reopen rate on repaired devices (already low at 3.3%) stays low, while the fastpath complaint load falls because more cases that require review are actually routed there, removing the source of the 14.1% complaint rate.
- **Cost shift but net save**: Higher labour cost (EUR34/h reviewers, EUR52/h escalation) is offset by avoiding EUR410 wrongreplacement errors, reshipments, and service credits. - The “reviewwithincompleteevidence” track may occasionally approve a claim with insufficient evidence, shifting the risk from abandonment and delay to a small increase in improper approvals (a controlled risk).

## 4. Redesign C: CustomerCentric Transparency and Proactive Case Orchestration

- **Failure modes**:
  1. The concierge pod becomes overwhelmed because the uncleardefect segment grows (22%  30% as more customers use the portal to report ambiguous faults), eroding the speed advantage and causing conciergedelay complaints.
  2. The customerconfirmation step is ignored by a large fraction of customers, allowing wrongreplacement issues to go undetected until later complaints, which still occur but now outside the rapidrecovery window, replicating the old complaint/reopen loop with added portal cost.
- **Bottleneck migration**: The AIscoring model requires continuous tuning and specialised datascience resources; without maintenance, its accuracy degrades and misrouted cases create a new type of bottleneck in the fraud/risk review or, conversely, bypass it inappropriately, recreating the compliance risk.

## 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**First pilot**  
Implement the **predispatch quality gate (Redesign A)** for **highvalue devices only** – the segment where wrongreplacement cost (EUR410) and 22% complaint rate on the fast path create the strongest improvement case. Run for six weeks on a representative 30% random sample of highvalue claims, with a control group flowing through the current process.

**Metrics to monitor**  
- *Primary*: Complaint/reopen rate within 30 days of dispatch (target: reduction from ~22% to <10%).  
- *Secondary*: Median and p90 cycle time from *Dispatch replacement* to *Quality/compliance close*; number of wrongreplacement incidents captured by gate; servicecredit payouts for highvalue claims.  
- *Balancing*: Gateinduced delay in *Fast approval  Dispatch replacement* and any rise in Premium highvalue servicecredit exposure; customer satisfaction (CSAT) survey for the pilot segment.

**Stop/go thresholds**  
- **Go**: Complaint/reopen rate drops >30% without increasing median dispatchtoclose cycle time beyond 50% of the current fastpath median for highvalue Premium (current ~20h, so not exceeding 30h). Gatedetected wrongreplacement savings exceed gate operating cost by a factor 1.5.  
- **Stop and reassess**: Gate adds so much delay that the pilot segment shows a statistically significant *increase* in servicecredit payouts or a 5point drop in CSAT. If the gate becomes the dominant bottleneck, pause and redesign its resourcing before expanding.

**Sequencing logic**  
1. **Gate for highvalue devices** – proves that stopping wrong replacement early brings real savings and quality improvement without destroying the customer experience.  
2. **Add evidenceportal and autoscreen 2.0 (elements from Redesign A and C) for the uncleardefect segment** – measure whether the portal reduces repeated *Request missing evidence* loops and abandonment. Implement the “oneevidencerequest” rule from Redesign B only after portal adoption is stable, to avoid forcing abandonment.  
3. **Rebalance capacity (Redesign B)** – once the new routing rules and gate shift the inflow pattern, use the utilisation data to decide how many manualreview FTEs are needed and whether fraud/risk capacity should be expanded. Introduce partprice lookup and continuous escalation in parallel.  
4. **Customerpromise engine and concierge (Redesign C)** – deploy after the backoffice is stable; the promise engine requires predictable cycle times to give reliable dates, and the concierge pod can be sized from the demonstrable demand of uncleardefect and Premium highvalue flows.

**Learning about shifted vs. real improvements**  
- Track **total cost of claim** (labour, replacement parts, service credits, rework) per segment before and after each change, not just local cycle time. If cycle time falls at one stage but escalates at another (e.g., gate reduces dispatch errors but manual review queue grows), the improvement is displaced, not real.  
- Use **endtoend complaint/reopen rate and net promoter score** as the ultimate quality outcome; a redesign that reduces complaints only by making the process inescapably slow (e.g., forcing every case through manual review) would show a drop in complaints but a rise in dissatisfaction and likely abandonment – that tradeoff must be visible in the segmentlevel data.  
- During the highvalue gate pilot, simultaneously measure the volume and cycle time of *Customer complaint/reopen  Manual eligibility review* and *Escalation board* for the pilot group; if those edges decline while dispatchtoclose stays acceptable, the gain is genuine and not merely displaced downstream.
