A single statement of the form "automation reduces cycle time by X%" is not defensible from these data. The raw median changed by −0.8% at Alpha, +4.1% at Beta and −8% to −18% at Gamma depending on the baseline window, the 90th percentile rose about 10% at Alpha and Beta, and every favourable number depends on comparison branches that share a fraud desk with the adopters. The recommendation is to expand with an embedded evaluation, starting at Epsilon.

## 1. What is observed

**Availability.** `automation=1` marks branch-months in which the system was switched on: Alpha and Beta from April, Gamma from July, Delta and Epsilon never. That gives 12 available and 28 unavailable branch-months across five branches.

**Actual use.** The automated fast-path share was 4–6% everywhere before go-live, including in branches without the system, so the measure has a non-zero floor. After go-live it rose from 25–27% to 48–50% over five months at Alpha and Beta, and from 25% to 32% over two months at Gamma. It excludes automated cases that were escalated, so it understates total automated triage.

**Selection.** Alpha and Beta were chosen for high volume and poor cycle times; their January–March medians (45–50 h) were the highest of the five. Gamma followed a readiness pilot. No branch was randomized.

**Contemporaneous changes.** The fraud desk opened in March, and escalation rose 2.0 points in every branch that month. Routing added about 10% volume at Alpha and Beta from April and 5% at Gamma from July. Four branches had FTE changes. The high-complexity share rose 5–8 points from May (3 net at Beta).

**Measurement limits.** Outcomes are branch-month aggregates: medians and 90th percentiles cannot be pooled or split by variant. CSAT is a mean over an unknown number of respondents with a 20–45% response rate. Fraud-desk waiting times were not recorded.

## 2. Causal estimands

"ITT" means intention-to-treat: the effect of making the system available, whatever use followed. All branch-level estimands are effects on the adopting branches, not on branches in general.

**Interference.** The no-interference assumption fails in two known ways. All branches share the fraud desk, and automated triage sends more borderline cases to it, so automation in one branch can lengthen specialist waits in untreated branches. The routing campaign moved cases toward adopters, which changes load at the adopters and possibly at the branches the cases came from. E1 and E2 are therefore effects of "this branch on, given which other branches were on", and the comparison branches are not clean.

**Treatment versions.** "Available" covers several different treatments: 25% use in the first month versus 50% in the fifth; Alpha with two fewer FTEs versus Beta with one more; Gamma after a pilot; and whatever local practice changes each manager made. A pooled effect averages over versions that expansion to Delta and Epsilon would not reproduce exactly.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary: cohort-by-month difference-in-differences** (group-time contrasts in the style of Callaway and Sant'Anna, computed by hand and reported unpooled) | Cohort g = April (Alpha, Beta) and g = July (Gamma); event time e = month − g; each contrast is a long difference from the cohort's pre-window | Never-treated Delta and Epsilon; Gamma as a not-yet-treated control for April–June; never an already-treated branch | None in the main contrast, because there are too few units; complexity share and arrivals per FTE as sensitivity; hours and logs | E1, E2, E5, E6, E7, E8 | Parallel trends on the stated scale; no anticipation; no spillover to comparison branches; the bundle is the treatment | Placebo leads; leave-one-control-out; Gamma's untreated April–June path against the controls; exact permutation over the 10 possible treated pairs; hours versus logs | Controls contaminated through the fraud desk, or reacting differently to the May product change; only two never-treated branches |
| Alternative 1: matched or synthetic control per adopter | Adopter-specific go-live; weights chosen to fit the pre-period | Weighted Delta, Epsilon and pre-July Gamma; in practice Alpha with Epsilon (complexity 28→36 versus 30→38) and Gamma with Delta (both +5 points) | Pre-period outcome path, complexity, arrivals per FTE, used for matching | Branch-specific E1 and E2 | The adopter's untreated path can be reproduced from donors; donors unaffected by treatment | Pre-period fit; placebo adopters among donors; leave-one-donor-out | Alpha and Beta levels (45–50 h) lie outside the donor range (33–41 h), so no convex combination fits; three pre-months; with an intercept shift it collapses into the primary design |
| Alternative 2: comparative interrupted time series | Level break and ramp term at each branch's go-live | The branch's own pre-trend, differenced against the control series | Calendar-month effects taken from controls; complexity; arrivals per FTE | E1 and E8, branch by branch | The pre-trend would have continued; no other break at go-live | Fit on pre-period; placebo break dates; residual autocorrelation | Three pre-months for Alpha and Beta; the March fraud desk and May product change are breaks themselves. Usable only for Gamma, which has six pre-months |
| Alternative 3: dose-response panel (continuous-treatment difference-in-differences) | Excess automated fast-path share by month, zero before go-live; optionally a separate availability step | All untreated branch-months plus variation in use within adopters | Branch and month effects, complexity, arrivals per FTE | E3 | Use is as good as random given the effects and covariates; linear response; parallel trends across dose levels | Separate step from slope; monotonicity within cohort; same dose in different calendar months (Gamma in July versus Alpha and Beta in April) | Use rises with time since go-live, so learning, staffing and seasonal relief are collinear with dose; busy teams may use the system less |
| Not feasible with supplied data: case-level design | Triage route recorded per case | Randomized or threshold-adjacent cases in the same branch and week | Risk class, arrival week, queue state at arrival | E4, E9 | Randomization, or continuity at the triage-score threshold | Balance tables; manipulation checks at the threshold | Within-branch queue sharing between arms, which needs a switchback component |

The cohort-by-month contrasts fit these data for four reasons. First, they never use an already-treated branch as a control. A pooled two-way fixed-effects coefficient would compare Gamma's July–August change with Alpha and Beta, whose use was still climbing from about 40% to 50%; when effects vary by cohort and by time since go-live, that coefficient is a weighted mix with possibly negative weights, not an average effect. Here it happens to land near the cohort averages (−4.2 h on the median), but that agreement is a property of this sample, not a guarantee. Second, the method keeps the two cohorts and each post-month separate, which matters because the April cohort's event time coincides with the May product change and Alpha's staffing loss. Third, it needs no model fitted to three pre-period points, which rules out interrupted time series and synthetic control for Alpha and Beta; their baseline levels also lie outside the range of every possible donor. Fourth, each number is a difference of four averages that leadership can check by hand, and the uncertainty can be shown honestly as a range across control choices, scales and placebo months instead of a standard error from five clusters. Its weakness is shared by every alternative: two never-treated branches that are connected to the adopters through the fraud desk.

### 5.3 Treatment intensity

Dose is the automated fast-path share minus the branch's own pre-go-live level (4%, or 6% at Beta). It was 21 points in every adopter's first month and reached 44 points at Alpha and Beta by August and 28 at Gamma. Coefficients are hours per 10 points of dose, with branch-clustered 95% intervals from five clusters, which are too narrow to be trusted.

| model | median_cycle_h | p90_cycle_h |
|---|---|---|
| Dose with branch and month effects | −1.30 (−2.19 to −0.41) | −1.84 (−2.97 to −0.71) |
| Adding complexity share and arrivals per FTE | −0.62 (−1.67 to +0.44) | −1.01 (−3.17 to +1.14) |
| Availability step and dose slope together, with the same covariates | step −1.5 (−6.4 to +3.3); slope −0.43 (−1.20 to +0.35) | step −6.0 (−10.8 to −1.2); slope −0.29 (−2.15 to +1.59) |

**Arithmetic.** For rework and escalation the third model puts everything in the step and nothing in the slope: rework step −2.4 points with slope +0.06; escalation step +1.0 with slope 0.0. Within the April cohort, the correlation between dose and the monthly median contrast is −0.59 over five months. At the same 21-point dose, the contrast was −2.6 h for Alpha and Beta in April and −4.1 h for Gamma in July.

**Interpretation.** The data cannot tell "a one-off shift when the system is switched on" from "a benefit that grows with use". Escalation and rework behave like switch-on steps. For cycle time, the dose slope is indistinguishable from zero once load and complexity are in the model, and dose is nearly collinear with time since go-live. A branch median is also not linear in case shares, so "hours per 10 points" cannot be converted into hours saved per automated case.

### 5.4 Pre-trends and regression to the mean

**Arithmetic.**
- April cohort placebo (March against January–February, adopters minus never-treated): median −0.5 h, p90 −0.9 h, rework 0.0, escalation 0.0, CSAT 0.00. This rests on three pre-months.
- Gamma's four placebo months (January–April, against its May–June base): median between −1.9 and +1.1 h; p90 between −3.5 and +2.0 h. This is the best available gauge of month-to-month noise in a contrast: roughly ±2 h on the median and ±3.5 h on p90.
- Gamma while untreated moved like the controls: +6.2 h from January–March to April–June against +6.3 h at Delta and Epsilon.
- Alpha's pre-period medians were 50.4, 46.9, 49.4 h; Beta's were 45.4, 45.9, 46.4 h.

**Interpretation.** There is no visible pre-trend, but three pre-months cannot rule one out. Regression to the mean is not the main risk for Alpha and Beta: they were selected on persistently poor levels, their pre-period was flat, and their raw medians did not fall afterwards, so there is no recovery to explain away. The real question is whether Delta's and Epsilon's deterioration is the right counterfactual for branches that started 6–14 h slower. For Gamma the risk is more concrete, because it went live straight after its two worst months. Using the longer January–June baseline changes its median contrast only from −4.2 to −3.9 h, so a transient dip accounts for well under an hour of it.

|---|---|---|
| No control adjustment (Alpha, Beta, Gamma long baseline) | −0.4, +1.9, −3.4 h | +9.7, +9.8, −0.9 h |
| Difference-in-differences in hours, across adopters and single controls | −2.6 to −6.4 h | −4.8 to −9.4 h |
| Same on the log scale | −6% to −16% | −6% to −12% |
| Gamma as not-yet-treated control | −3.6 to −4.6 h | −5.3 to −6.7 h |
| Pooled model with complexity and load | −2.2 h (−7.3 to +3.0) | −6.4 h (−13.4 to +0.6) |
| Contamination: one third to one half of the controls' deterioration is spillover from adopters (April cohort) | −0.7 to −3.9 h | −1.0 to +1.8 h |
| Load-equivalent: controls' load slope (1.3 h and 2.6 h per extra arrival per FTE) applied to the adopters' extra 2.8–6.0 arrivals per FTE | −7 to −13 h | −13 to −22 h |

**Arithmetic.** The share of control deterioration that would have to be spillover for the April-cohort contrast to reach zero is about 40% for p90 (both branches) and 64% for Beta's median; for Alpha's median it exceeds 100%. In the pooled model with covariates, the load coefficient for the median is −0.5 h per arrival per FTE, the wrong sign, which shows that load cannot be separated from adoption in this panel.

**Interpretation, median.** The effect of the package on adopters' median over the first two to five months is probably a reduction. A plausible range is −1 to −8 h (about −2% to −16%), with −3 to −6 h the centre of the control-based estimates. Zero is not excluded if spillover and non-parallel trends are both material. The load-equivalent scenario is an optimistic extrapolation and is not a basis for a claim.

**Interpretation, p90.** The sign is not determined. The range runs from about −9 h to +2 h relative to a counterfactual, and in absolute terms the tail at Alpha and Beta lengthened by about 10 h. The favourable contrast disappears under a moderate amount of fraud-desk spillover.

**Interpretation, rework and escalation.** Rework: −1.2 to −2.6 points across adopters, controls and windows; −2.0 to −2.4 in the go-live month. Escalation: +0.6 to +1.2 points, and exactly +1.0 once complexity is held fixed. That exactness reflects how regular the supplied aggregates are and should not be read as statistical certainty.

## 6. Threats, tests, and decision consequences

| threat | why it matters here | direction of likely bias | test or additional evidence | consequence if threat is severe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Selection into adoption | Alpha and Beta were picked for poor cycle times and high volume, Gamma after a pilot; adopters started at 45–50 h against 33–41 h elsewhere | Toward benefit if weak branches were under management pressure to recover anyway; against benefit if weak branches are more fragile under load | Twelve or more months of pre-history for all branches; response of each branch to earlier demand shocks | The effect on adopters does not transfer to Delta and Epsilon, which start from better baselines |
| Non-parallel trends | Three pre-months for the April cohort; the product change and seasonal demand may hit slow and fast branches differently; hours and logs disagree on size | Unknown in sign; the log scale gives the larger effect | Placebo leads; longer history; both scales; Gamma's untreated April–June path (+6.2 h against +6.3 h at controls) is mildly reassuring | The control-based contrasts lose meaning and the fallback is the raw series: no median improvement at Alpha and Beta |
| Time-varying confounding | Routing campaign, product change and local practice changes all began at or near go-live | Extra load hides a benefit; easier routed cases or managerial attention create one | Case-level mix of routed cases; dated log of local practice changes | Any effect belongs to the package, not the software |

## 8. Rollout recommendation

`expand with an embedded evaluation`

**Next branch.** Epsilon goes first. It has the highest complexity share (38%) and rework (16.2%), so it is where a benefit matters most and where tail risk is highest; its case mix also mirrors Alpha's. Delta stays untreated for at least three more months as the concurrent comparison, then follows the same protocol.

**Assignment.** Within Epsilon, randomize at registration: eligible cases go to automated or manual triage, stratified by risk class, starting at 30% automated and moving to 50% and 70% in pre-announced four-week steps. Because the two arms share staff and queues, add a randomized weekly switchback between high-share and low-share weeks to measure congestion. In Alpha, Beta and Gamma, hold back a random 10% of eligible cases for manual triage to measure the effect at mature use. Freeze routing and planned staffing changes during the test, or log them by date.

**Measurement periods.** At least eight weeks of case-level event logs before go-live, which should already exist, and at least twelve weeks after, with a review at six months, because use took five months to reach 50% at Alpha and Beta.

**Data to collect.** Activity timestamps for every case, so that waiting and service time are separated at each step; variant; triage route, including automated cases that escalate; risk class; resource; queue length on arrival at triage, specialist review and the fraud desk; reopenings after close; and survey responses linked to cases, with response counts.

**Spillover monitoring.** Weekly fraud-desk and specialist queue length and waiting time by originating branch, including Delta, plus network-wide referral counts.

**Guardrails.** Pause the ramp if, for two consecutive weeks, the automated arm's p90 exceeds the manual arm's by more than 10%, the fraud-desk p90 wait rises more than 20% above baseline, rework plus reopenings exceed the manual arm by more than one point, or CSAT among high-complexity cases falls by 0.1.

**Scale-up rule.** Move to full availability at Epsilon and then Delta if, after twelve weeks, the randomized median cycle time is at least 5% lower with a 90% interval excluding zero, p90 is no worse than +5%, rework plus reopenings are no worse, and fraud-desk waits for other branches have not risen. If the median improves but the tail or the fraud desk fails, retune the borderline-case threshold or add desk capacity before expanding.

def fit(formula, data=df):
    """OLS with branch-clustered covariance and t(G-1) reference distribution. Descriptive only."""
    with warnings.catch_warnings():
        warnings.simplefilter("ignore")
        return smf.ols(formula, data=data).fit(cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": data["branch"]},
                                              use_t=True)


def show(res, terms, label):
    with np.errstate(invalid="ignore"):
        se, ci = res.bse, res.conf_int()
    for term in terms:
        note = ""
        if not np.isfinite(se[term]) or se[term] < 1e-6:
            note = "  <- exact fit of very regular aggregates: degenerate SE, not certainty"
        print(f"   {label:34s} {term:12s} coef={res.params[term]:+8.3f}  se={se[term]:6.3f}  "
              f"95% CI [{ci.loc[term, 0]:+7.3f}, {ci.loc[term, 1]:+7.3f}]{note}")

## 10. Final claim ladder

- Observed: In each go-live month escalation rose by 0.8–1.0 points and recorded rework fell by 2.0–2.4 points while untreated branches showed no change, and over April–August Alpha and Beta took 19–29% more arrivals per FTE with medians at −0.8% and +4.1% and p90 up about 10%.
- Suggestive: Against Delta, Epsilon and not-yet-treated Gamma, adopters' median cycle time ran 2.6–6.4 h (about 6–16%) below a parallel-trend projection in both cohorts, with rework about 1–2.5 points lower; this rests on two comparison branches that slowed down and on three pre-months.
- Conditionally causal: If trends were parallel, the fraud desk passed no delay on to comparison branches, and routed cases resembled existing ones, then switching on the rollout package lowered adopters' median by roughly 1–8 h and raised escalation by about 1 point over the first two to five months; the 90th percentile and CSAT remain unidentified even under these conditions.
- Not supported: "Automation reduces cycle time by X%" as a single number, any claim that automation shortens the slowest cases or raises satisfaction, any per-case or steady-state effect, and any assurance that Delta and Epsilon would see the same result.
