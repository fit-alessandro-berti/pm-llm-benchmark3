# 1. Objectives and constraints

Designing a dynamic resource-assignment policy for a high-consequence service process requires balancing multiple competing operational, human, and organizational dimensions. The policy rejects single-dimensional optimization (such as pure throughput maximization) in favor of a resilient socio-technical framework that aligns short-term execution with long-term workforce sustainability and auditability.

### 1.1 Optimization Objectives

1. **SLA Compliance & Throughput Acceleration**:
   - Minimize the probability and severity of SLA breaches across all priority tiers.
   - Maximize case velocity through the process lifecycle by matching urgent work with immediate resource availability, avoiding queue starvation.

2. **Quality, Accuracy, and First-Time-Right Execution**:
   - Minimize rework loops, error rates, and customer appeal rates.
   - Ensure that high-complexity and regulated activities (Security Review, Final Approval) receive sufficient competence or specialist oversight to eliminate post-assignment failure.

3. **Workload Fairness & Burnout Prevention**:
   - Balance operational loads across teams and individuals to avoid overburdening high-performing or specialist resources.
   - Proactively mitigate overtime risk by dampening assignment velocity to workers already flagged with `overtime_risk = "high"`.

4. **Case Continuity & Friction Reduction**:
   - Preserve case ownership across activity handovers, particularly for high-complexity cases, to eliminate context-switching delays, cognitive reload overhead, and duplicate investigations.
   - Prevent cases from exceeding critical reassignment thresholds where historical evidence demonstrates severe compounding quality degradation.

5. **Workforce Resilience & Skill Development**:
   - Continuously expand organizational capability by scheduling controlled learning opportunities for trainees and competent workers in their documented training priorities.
   - Eliminate single points of failure (specialist silos) without creating unacceptable SLA risks or quality lapses.

---

### 1.2 Hard Constraints (Compliance, Regulatory, and Safety Rules)

Hard constraints must never be violated by the assignment policy under any automated scoring condition:

1. **Batch Allocation Ceiling**:
   - No individual resource may be assigned more than 4 new cases within a single decision batch ($N_{assigned}(r) \le 4$).
2. **High-Priority Competence Threshold**:
   - Cases with priority `urgent` or `high` must never be assigned to a resource with skill level $< 2$ unless explicitly assigned to a formally supervised mentor-trainee pair ($Skill(r, a) \ge 2 \lor SupervisedPair(r_{trainee}, r_{supervisor})$).
3. **Security Review Gate**:
   - The activity `Security Review` must be executed exclusively by a skill level 3 specialist, OR by a skill level 1–2 worker under explicit, direct supervision by either `R7_Gita` or `R8_Hugo`.
4. **Final Approval Authority Gate**:
   - The activity `Final Approval` must be finalized exclusively by a resource with skill level 2 or 3. Skill level 1 resources may prepare the documentation and recommendation but are strictly barred from final sign-off. Skill level 0 is completely excluded.
5. **Quality Check Batching Window**:
   - The activity `Quality Check` may be grouped into multi-case execution batches if and only if the case priority is `normal` or `low` AND the remaining SLA time is strictly greater than 24 hours ($Priority \in \{normal, low\} \land SLA_{rem} > 24\text{h}$).

---

### 1.3 Soft Constraints (Preference Trade-offs & Operational Targets)

Soft constraints represent operational preferences negotiated through multi-objective scoring functions:

1. **Overtime Shielding**:
   - Avoid assigning work to resources with `overtime_risk = "high"` unless the incoming case is `urgent` ($SLA \le 4\text{h}$) and no qualified alternative resource possesses capacity.
2. **Ownership Continuity vs. Skill Mismatch**:
   - For cases with high complexity ($Complexity \ge 0.70$) and `handover_cost_band = "high"`, the current owner should be retained unless the current owner has skill 0 or 1 and supervision is infeasible.
   - Historical evidence dictates that changing ownership on high-complexity cases introduces a median delay of 2.7 hours, whereas retaining an unqualified owner (skill 0–1) adds a median 3.3 hours of service time plus elevated rework. The policy prioritizes continuity when skill is competent ($\ge 2$), but enforces reassignment if the owner is trainee/unskilled and the deadline is urgent.
3. **Reassignment Throttling (The Threshold Penalty)**:
   - Historical evidence demonstrates that cases with $> 2$ reassignments experience a $1.8\times$ higher rework rate and a $1.4\times$ higher appeal rate. The policy introduces an aggressive penalty against reassigning any case that already has $\ge 2$ reassignments.
4. **Training Quota Target**:
   - At least 20% of `normal` and `low` priority cases must be routed to support documented training priorities, provided the SLA buffer is sufficient to absorb expected trainee service-time dilation.
5. **Specialist Bottleneck Preservation**:
   - Avoid routing routine, low-complexity cases to scarce specialists (e.g., Risk team specialists `R7_Gita` and `R8_Hugo`) when competent alternatives exist, preserving specialist availability for incoming critical escalations.

# 2. Assignment policy

The proposed Explainable Dynamic Resource-Assignment Policy operates as a multi-stage decision pipeline executed at each queue dispatch cycle. It combines deterministic constraint pruning with a transparent, weighted multi-criteria utility function, followed by pairing and batching heuristics.

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                         INCOMING QUEUE SNAPSHOT                         |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                     |
                                     v
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| STAGE 1: CASE TRIAGE & SLA URGENCY INDEXING                             |
| - Calculate Urgency Index: UI(c) = (EstServiceTime / SLA_rem) * (1 + Comp)|
| - Sort queue into Tiers: Tier 1 (Urgent/Critical), Tier 2, Tier 3       |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                     |
                                     v
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| STAGE 2: ELIGIBILITY & HARD CONSTRAINT FILTERING                        |
| - Filter candidate resources per activity by Hard Constraints 1-5       |
| - Enforce skill minimums, security/approval gates, and batch caps (<= 4)|
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                     |
                                     v
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| STAGE 3: MULTI-CRITERIA UTILITY SCORING                                 |
| - For each eligible (case, resource) pair, compute Score S(c, r):       |
|   S(c, r) = W_skill*M_skill + W_cont*M_cont + W_fair*M_fair             |
|             + W_train*M_train - P_overtime - P_reassign - P_spec        |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                     |
                                     v
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| STAGE 4: SUPERVISION PAIRING & BATCHING LOGIC                           |
| - Match trainees (skill 1) with available mentors (skill 3 / R7 / R8)   |
| - Group eligible Quality Checks (SLA > 24h, normal/low) into batches   |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                     |
                                     v
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| STAGE 5: FINAL RESOLUTION, TIE-BREAKING & EXPLANATION AUDIT LOGGING     |
| - Greedy / Hungarian matching by Score S(c, r); log decision rationale  |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 2.1 Mathematical Formulation of Scoring Components

For each eligible pair of case $c$ and resource $r$, an explainable total utility score $S(c, r)$ is calculated:

$$S(c, r) = S_{\text{match}}(c, r) + S_{\text{continuity}}(c, r) + S_{\text{workload}}(r) + S_{\text{training}}(c, r) - P_{\text{overtime}}(r) - P_{\text{reassign}}(c) - P_{\text{bottleneck}}(c, r)$$

#### 1. Skill Match Utility ($S_{\text{match}}$)
Reflects the appropriateness of resource $r$'s skill level $k \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ for activity $a$:
- **Urgent / High Priority ($P \in \{\text{urgent}, \text{high}\}$)**:
  - Skill 3 (Specialist): $+40$
  - Skill 2 (Competent): $+25$
  - Skill 1 (Trainee under explicit supervision): $+10$
  - Skill 0 / Unsupervised Trainee: $-\infty$ (Filtered out in Stage 2)
- **Normal / Low Priority ($P \in \{\text{normal}, \text{low}\}$)**:
  - Skill 2 (Competent): $+30$ (Ideal fit; preserves specialists)
  - Skill 3 (Specialist): $+20$ (Acceptable, but slight penalty for overqualification)
  - Skill 1 (Trainee): $+25$ if matched to documented training priority; $+5$ otherwise

#### 2. Continuity & Handover Utility ($S_{\text{continuity}}$)
Accounts for current case ownership, cognitive load, and handover friction:
- If $r == \text{current\_owner}(c)$:
  - Base ownership retention bonus: $+20$
  - Added complexity amplification: $+25 \times \text{complexity}(c)$
  - Added handover cost avoidance: $+15$ if $\text{handover\_cost} == \text{"high"}$; $+5$ if $\text{"medium"}$
- If $r \ne \text{current\_owner}(c)$:
  - If $\text{current\_owner}(c) \ne \text{None}$: Penalty equal to $-(10 + 15 \times \text{complexity}(c) + \text{CostBand}(c))$
  - If $\text{current\_owner}$ is skill $0$ or $1$ and case is urgent: Continuity bonus is zeroed out to prevent the $+3.3\text{h}$ service-time trap.

#### 3. Workload & Capacity Utility ($S_{\text{workload}}$)
Measures resource availability and workload equity:
$$S_{\text{workload}}(r) = 20 \times \left( \frac{\text{hours\_remaining}(r)}{\text{max\_shift\_hours}} \right) - 15 \times \left( \frac{\text{current\_open\_cases}(r)}{\text{max\_open\_cases}} \right)$$
Resources with low open cases and high remaining hours are strongly favored.

#### 4. Training & Development Utility ($S_{\text{training}}$)
Encourages capability growth on safe cases:
- If $P \in \{\text{normal}, \text{low}\}$ AND $\text{SLA\_rem}(c) \ge 18\text{h}$ AND $\text{activity}(c) == \text{training\_priority}(r)$:
  - Bonus $+35$ (Elevates trainee assignment above routine competent routing).
- Mentorship capacity bonus: If $r$ serves as an active mentor to an eligible trainee on case $c$: $+15$.

#### 5. Overtime Penalty ($P_{\text{overtime}}$)
Protects at-risk employees from burnout:
- If $\text{overtime\_risk}(r) == \text{"high"}$:
  - If $P(c) == \text{"urgent"}$ and no other resource is eligible: Penalty $-15$ (Permitted as last resort).
  - Otherwise: Penalty $-60$ (Effectively blocks non-critical assignments).
- If $\text{overtime\_risk}(r) == \text{"medium"}$: Penalty $-10$.
- If $\text{overtime\_risk}(r) == \text{"low"}$: Penalty $0$.

#### 6. Reassignment Compounding Penalty ($P_{\text{reassign}}$)
Penalizes pushing cases into high-risk rework territory:
- If $r \ne \text{current\_owner}(c)$ and $\text{reassignment\_count}(c) == 2$: Penalty $-40$ (Prevents breaching the $>2$ rework threshold).
- If $r \ne \text{current\_owner}(c)$ and $\text{reassignment\_count}(c) \ge 3$: Penalty $-80$ (Extreme resistance to further handovers).

#### 7. Specialist Preservation Penalty ($P_{\text{bottleneck}}$)
Prevents wasting scarce specialists on routine tasks:
- If $\text{skill}(r, a) == 3$ on a `normal`/`low` priority case with complexity $< 0.40$ while scarce queues in Security/Approval exist: Penalty $-25$.

### 2.2 Algorithmic Pseudocode

```python
def dynamic_resource_assignment_policy(queue, resources, hard_constraints):
    """
    Executes explainable multi-criteria resource assignment.
    """
    assignments = []
    batch_counts = {r.id: 0 for r in resources}
    
    # Stage 1: Triage and sort queue by SLA Urgency Index
    def urgency_index(case):
        weight = {"urgent": 4.0, "high": 2.5, "normal": 1.0, "low": 0.5}[case.priority]
        return (weight * (1.0 + case.complexity)) / max(case.sla_hours_remaining, 0.5)

    sorted_queue = sorted(queue, key=urgency_index, reverse=True)
    
    # Track training quota fulfillment
    normal_low_cases = [c for c in sorted_queue if c.priority in ['normal', 'low']]
    target_training_count = math.ceil(0.20 * len(normal_low_cases))
    current_training_count = 0

    for case in sorted_queue:
        eligible_candidates = []
        
        # Stage 2: Eligibility Filtering
        for r in resources:
            if batch_counts[r.id] >= hard_constraints.MAX_BATCH_ASSIGNMENTS: # 4
                continue
            
            # Constraint 4 check: Overtime shielding
            if r.overtime_risk == "high" and case.priority != "urgent":
                continue

            # Activity-specific hard constraints
            if case.activity == "Security Review":
                if r.skill[case.activity] == 3:
                    eligible_candidates.append({'resource': r, 'type': 'solo'})
                elif r.skill[case.activity] in [1, 2]:
                    # Find eligible supervisor (R7_Gita or R8_Hugo)
                    supervisor = find_available_supervisor(["R7_Gita", "R8_Hugo"], resources, batch_counts)
                    if supervisor:
                        eligible_candidates.append({'resource': r, 'type': 'supervised', 'supervisor': supervisor})
            
            elif case.activity == "Final Approval":
                if r.skill[case.activity] in [2, 3]:
                    eligible_candidates.append({'resource': r, 'type': 'solo'})
                elif r.skill[case.activity] == 1:
                    # Trainee may prepare; must be finalized by skill 2-3
                    finalizer = find_available_finalizer(case.activity, resources, batch_counts)
                    if finalizer:
                        eligible_candidates.append({'resource': r, 'type': 'prep_only', 'finalizer': finalizer})
            
            else:
                # General activities
                skill_level = r.skill[case.activity]
                if case.priority in ["urgent", "high"]:
                    if skill_level >= 2:
                        eligible_candidates.append({'resource': r, 'type': 'solo'})
                    elif skill_level == 1:
                        # Allowed only under explicit supervision
                        mentor = find_available_mentor(case.activity, r.team, resources, batch_counts)
                        if mentor:
                            eligible_candidates.append({'resource': r, 'type': 'supervised', 'supervisor': mentor})
                else:
                    if skill_level >= 1:
                        eligible_candidates.append({'resource': r, 'type': 'solo'})

        # Stage 3 & 4: Multi-Criteria Utility Scoring
        scored_candidates = []
        for cand in eligible_candidates:
            r = cand['resource']
            score, audit_breakdown = compute_utility_score(
                case, r, cand, current_training_count, target_training_count
            )
            scored_candidates.append((score, cand, audit_breakdown))
        
        # Select best candidate
        if scored_candidates:
            scored_candidates.sort(key=lambda x: x[0], reverse=True)
            best_score, best_cand, breakdown = scored_candidates[0]
            
            # Check for Quality Check batching opportunity
            if case.activity == "Quality Check" and case.priority in ['normal', 'low'] and case.sla_hours_remaining > 24:
                # Group with pending batch if active
                best_cand['is_batched'] = True

            assignments.append({
                'case_id': case.id,
                'assignment': format_assignment(best_cand),
                'score': best_score,
                'audit': breakdown
            })
            
            batch_counts[best_cand['resource'].id] += 1
            if 'supervisor' in best_cand:
                batch_counts[best_cand['supervisor'].id] += 0.5 # Fractional capacity load
            if breakdown.get('supports_training'):
                current_training_count += 1
        else:
            # Stage 6: Exception Handling / Escalation
            assignments.append(handle_assignment_exception(case, resources))
            
    return assignments
```

| **Error & Rework Risk** | **Critical**: High error rates; frequent assignment of skill 1 to complex tasks; reassignments frequently exceed 2 ($1.8\times$ rework). | **Lowest per task**: Tasks executed by skill 3 have few immediate mistakes, but secondary rework rises from rushed reviews. | **Near-Specialist Level**: Hard constraints protect high-risk tasks; pairing ensures safety; capping reassignments avoids the $>2$ rework spike. |
| **Workload Fairness** | **Superficial**: Balances raw case numbers evenly, but ignores complexity, shift hours remaining, and overtime risk. | **Extremely Unfair**: Specialists (Hugo, Gita, David, Farid) face crushing workloads while junior workers are underutilized. | **True Socio-Technical Equity**: Balances open case counts, shift hours remaining, and explicitly shields workers flagged with high overtime risk. |
| **Skill Development** | **Uncontrolled**: Trainees randomly receive tasks without supervision, causing frustration, failure, and cognitive overload. | **Non-Existent**: Entrenches skill silos; trainees never receive opportunities; deepens organizational vulnerability. | **Structured & Auditable**: Meets $\ge 20\%$ training quota on low-risk cases; mandates mentor-mentee pairing with dual sign-off. |
| **Continuity of Ownership** | **Very Poor**: Passes cases to whoever becomes free first, maximizing context switching and accumulating 2.7h handover delays. | **Moderate**: Preserves ownership only when consecutive activities share the exact same specialist requirement. | **High**: Actively preserves owner on complex cases ($Complexity \ge 0.70$) and penalizes reassignments exceeding count 2. |
| **Resilience to Shocks** | **Brittle**: Any unexpected surge in complex cases overwhelms the pool, causing uncontained error propagation. | **Fragile**: Single point of failure; absence or sickness of Hugo, Gita, or Farid completely halts process flows. | **Maximum Resilience**: Continuously builds cross-functional skill reserves; dynamic fallback paths absorb resource absences. |

---

### 4.1 Detailed Trade-Off Reasoning

1. **Throughput vs. Queue Starvation**:
   - *Specialist-only* routing operates under the fallacy that assigning only the best person yields the fastest process. In our scenario, with only two Security specialists (`R7_Gita`, `R8_Hugo`) and two Approval specialists (`R2_Bruno`, `R6_Farid`—with Bruno at high overtime risk), all cases channel into these narrow funnels. Security and Approval become structural bottlenecks. 
   - *First-available* routing swings to the opposite extreme, assigning `C001` (urgent enterprise Security Review) to whoever is free, risking immediate catastrophic rework.
   - The *proposed policy* recognizes that skill 2 (competent) workers achieve acceptable service times without errors on normal tasks, reserving skill 3 specialists for urgent, high-complexity, or regulatory-gated cases.

2. **Continuity vs. Capacity**:
   - For `C016` (Technical Diagnosis, complexity 0.79, SLA 7h), `R5_Eva` is skill 2 (competent). A specialist-only policy would reassign this case to `R3_Celine` or `R4_David` (skill 3). However, process data proves that changing owners on high-complexity cases adds a median 2.7 hours of waiting time. In a 7-hour SLA window, a 2.7-hour delay consumes 38% of the total available time before diagnosis even begins. The proposed policy keeps Eva as owner, accepting competent execution over handover paralysis.
   - Conversely, for `C003` (Technical Diagnosis, normal 18h, complexity 0.66, reassignments 2), current owner `R4_David` has high overtime risk. Offloading David would relieve his overtime, but would push C003 to 3 reassignments, crossing the historical threshold that multiplies rework by $1.8\times$. The policy resolves this by retaining C003 with David while simultaneously stripping C014 off his desk, achieving net workload reduction without triggering the handover penalty.

# 6. Process-mining audit indicators

To ensure the dynamic assignment policy operates as intended, prevents gaming, and continuously surfaces operational bottlenecks, the following 10 process-mining indicators must be monitored continuously using event logs.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    AUDIT DASHBOARD ARCHITECTURE                                    |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| [1. Handover Frequency]           [2. Reassignment Threshold]        [3. Skill Compliance]        |
| - Target: Median <= 1/case        - Target: Cases > 2 reassign = 0%  - Target: 0 unauthorized      |
|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| [4. Workload Equity (Gini)]       [5. Overtime Shielding Violations] [6. SLA Breaches by Priority] |
| - Target: Gini <= 0.25            - Target: 0 high-risk assignments  - Target: Urgent = 0%         |
|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| [7. Post-Handoff Rework Rate]     [8. Training Quota Attainment]     [9. Specialist Queue Dwell]   |
| - Target: Rework < 5%             - Target: >= 20% normal/low        - Target: Wait < 2.0h         |
|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| [10. Algorithmic Policy Drift / Manual Override Rate] - Target: < 5% overrides                     |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 6.1 Indicator Definitions & Mathematical Formulations

1. **Handover Frequency & Network Centrality**:
   - *Definition*: The number of ownership transfers occurring across the lifecycle of a case, and the degree of handover concentration between specific resource pairs.
   - *Formula*: $HF(c) = \sum_{i=1}^{n_c - 1} \mathbb{I}(r_i \ne r_{i+1})$, derived from the handover-of-work social network graph $G_{HW} = (V, E)$.
   - *Target / Threshold*: Median handovers per case $\le 1.0$; 90th percentile $\le 2.0$. Flag any process variant where mean handovers exceed $1.8$.

2. **Reassignment Threshold Exceedance Rate**:
   - *Definition*: The percentage of active cases that have accumulated strictly more than 2 reassignments.
   - *Formula*: $R_{>2} = \frac{1}{|C|} \sum_{c \in C} \mathbb{I}(\text{reassignment\_count}(c) > 2) \times 100\%$.
   - *Target / Threshold*: $0.0\%$. Any case reaching reassignment count 3 triggers an automated audit alert due to the historical $1.8\times$ rework penalty.

3. **Skill Mismatch & Compliance Violation Rate**:
   - *Definition*: The proportion of events executed by resources who do not meet mandatory skill gates (e.g., skill level 0 performing solo work, skill level 1 executing urgent cases without supervision, or uncertified resources signing Final Approvals).
   - *Formula*: $SM = \frac{\sum_{e \in E} \mathbb{I}(\text{skill}(r_e, a_e) < \text{Threshold}(a_e, priority_e) \land \neg \text{Supervised}(e))}{|E|} \times 100\%$.
   - *Target / Threshold*: Absolute $0.0\%$ tolerance for Security Review and Final Approval breaches.

4. **Workload Equity (Gini Coefficient of Active Cases)**:
   - *Definition*: The statistical dispersion of active open cases across the workforce, measuring whether case allocations are equitable.
   - *Formula*: $G = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} |w_i - w_j|}{2n^2 \bar{w}}$, where $w_i$ is the active open case count of resource $i$.
   - *Target / Threshold*: Gini coefficient $G \le 0.25$ within teams. A Gini coefficient $> 0.40$ indicates severe load-leveling failure.

5. **Overtime Risk Exposure Violation Rate**:
   - *Definition*: The frequency with which new cases are assigned to resources currently flagged with `overtime_risk = "high"`, segmented by case priority.
   - *Formula*: $ORE = \sum_{e_{\text{assign}}} \mathbb{I}(\text{overtime\_risk}(r) == \text{"high"} \land priority(c) \ne \text{"urgent"})$.
   - *Target / Threshold*: 0 occurrences. Any non-urgent assignment to a high-overtime worker represents a policy failure.

6. **SLA Miss Attributability Index**:
   - *Definition*: The proportion of cases breaching SLA, decomposed by the primary root cause identified via performance DFG analysis (Waiting Time in Queue vs. Trainee Execution Dilatation vs. Reassignment Latency).
   - *Formula*: $SLA_{\text{breach}} = \frac{|\{c \in C \mid t_{\text{end}}(c) > \text{sla\_deadline}(c)\}|}{|C|} \times 100\%$.
   - *Target / Threshold*: $0\%$ on `urgent` and `high` priority; $< 2.5\%$ on `normal` and `low` priority.

7. **Rework and Appeal Correlation after Handover**:
   - *Definition*: The probability that a case requires a rework activity (`Rework Correction`, loopback diagnosis) or generates a customer appeal within 30 days of an ownership change.
   - *Formula*: $P(\text{Rework} \mid \Delta \text{Owner}) = \frac{|\{c \in C_{\text{reassigned}} \mid \text{has\_rework}(c)\}|}{|C_{\text{reassigned}}|}$.
   - *Target / Threshold*: Rework rate following a handover must not exceed $5.0\%$. A statistically significant increase above the baseline of non-reassigned cases triggers policy re-tuning.

8. **Training Assignment Share (Quota Conformance)**:
   - *Definition*: The proportion of `normal` and `low` priority cases assigned to resources with documented training priorities under verified mentor supervision.
   - *Formula*: $TAS = \frac{|\{c \in C_{\text{norm,low}} \mid \text{activity}(c) == \text{training\_priority}(r) \land \text{Supervised}(c)\}|}{|C_{\text{norm,low}}|} \times 100\%$.
   - *Target / Threshold*: Minimum $\ge 20.0\%$ per reporting cycle.

9. **Specialist Bottleneck Dwell Index**:
   - *Definition*: The ratio of waiting time in queue to active processing time for activities requiring skill 3 specialists (Security Review, Final Approval).
   - *Formula*: $BDI(a) = \frac{\text{Median}(T_{\text{waiting}}(a))}{\text{Median}(T_{\text{service}}(a))}$.
   - *Target / Threshold*: $BDI \le 1.5$. A ratio $> 3.0$ indicates that specialist capacity is bottlenecked, requiring immediate pairing and offloading of competent (skill 2) workers.

10. **Policy Deviation & Override Rate**:
    - *Definition*: The percentage of dispatch assignments where the human team lead manually overrode the algorithmic recommendation.
    - *Formula*: $PDR = \frac{|E_{\text{override}}|}{|E_{\text{total\_dispatched}}|} \times 100\%$.
    - *Target / Threshold*: $< 5.0\%$. High override clusters identify unmodeled real-world constraints or shifting operational realities.

# 7. PM4Py implementation sketch

The following production-ready Python script demonstrates how historical event log data is formatted, analyzed, and audited using **PM4Py**. It computes social networks, organizational roles, service/waiting times, performance DFGs, and audits compliance against the dynamic resource assignment policy.

```python
"""
PM4Py Audit and Learning Pipeline for Dynamic Resource Assignment Policy
========================================================================
This script processes service event logs to:
1. Discover Handover and Collaboration Networks (SNA).
2. Discover Organizational Roles and Activity-Based Resource Similarities.
3. Quantify Case Waiting vs. Service Times.
4. Discover Performance Directly-Follows Graphs (DFG).
5. Audit Policy Indicators (Handover Thresholds, Skill Gates, Training Quotas).
"""

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.log.util import dataframe_utils
from pm4py.algo.filtering.log.attributes import attributes_filter


def run_pm4py_policy_audit(event_log_path: str):
    print("=" * 80)
    print("STEP 1: INGESTION AND PM4PY DATAFRAME FORMATTING")
    print("=" * 80)
    
    # Load raw event log CSV
    raw_df = pd.read_csv(event_log_path)
    
    # Ensure correct datetime parsing
    raw_df['time:timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(raw_df['time:timestamp'], utc=True)
    
    # Format dataframe using standard PM4Py conventions
    formatted_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        raw_df,
        case_id='case:concept:name',
        activity_key='concept:name',
        timestamp_key='time:timestamp'
    )
    
    # Convert to EventLog object for advanced SNA and role discovery
    event_log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(formatted_df)
    print(f"Ingested {len(formatted_df)} events across {formatted_df['case:concept:name'].nunique()} cases.")

    print("\n" + "=" * 80)
    print("STEP 2: ORGANIZATIONAL PROCESS MINING (SNA & ROLE DISCOVERY)")
    print("=" * 80)

    # 1. Handover of Work Network
    # Identifies friction, handoff bottlenecks, and ping-pong patterns between workers
    handover_network = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(event_log)
    print("Handover of Work Network discovered successfully.")
    
    # Optionally export SNA visualization to file
    try:
        pm4py.save_vis_sna(handover_network, "audit_handover_network.html")
        print(" -> Handover network visualization saved to audit_handover_network.html")
    except Exception as e:
        print(f" -> Visualization export skipped: {e}")

    # 2. Working Together Network (Collaborative / Mentorship Clusters)
    working_together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(event_log)
    print("Working Together Network discovered successfully.")

    # 3. Activity-Based Resource Similarity
    # Discovers latent competence profiles and substitution possibilities
    resource_similarity = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(event_log)
    print("Activity-Based Resource Similarity Matrix generated.")

    # 4. Organizational Roles Discovery
    # Clusters resources into formal roles based on execution frequencies
    discovered_roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(event_log)
    print(f"Discovered {len(discovered_roles)} distinct operational roles:")
    for idx, role in enumerate(discovered_roles):
        members = list(role.resources)
        activities = [a for a in role.activities]
        print(f"   Role {idx+1}: Members={members} | Activities={activities[:3]}...")

    print("\n" + "=" * 80)
    print("STEP 3: PERFORMANCE MINING (SERVICE & WAITING TIMES, BATCHES)")
    print("=" * 80)

    # 5. Case Service and Waiting Times
    # Augments dataframe with event-level service duration and case-level queue waiting times
    log_with_perf = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(event_log)
    perf_df = pm4py.convert_to_dataframe(log_with_perf)
    
    mean_wait = perf_df['case_waiting_time'].mean() / 3600.0 if 'case_waiting_time' in perf_df else 0.0
    mean_service = perf_df['case_service_time'].mean() / 3600.0 if 'case_service_time' in perf_df else 0.0
    print(f"Performance Metrics: Mean Waiting Time = {mean_wait:.2f}h | Mean Service Time = {mean_service:.2f}h")

    # 6. Batching Detection
    # Audits whether Quality Check batching conformed to policy constraints
    discovered_batches = pm4py.discover_batches(event_log)
    print(f"Discovered {len(discovered_batches)} batch execution clusters in the log.")

    # 7. Performance DFG Discovery
    # Highlights handover delay bottlenecks across activity transitions
    perf_dfg, start_activities, end_activities = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(event_log)
    print("Performance DFG computed. Top handover latencies identified across edges.")

    print("\n" + "=" * 80)
    print("STEP 4: POLICY AUDIT INDICATORS & COMPLIANCE EVALUATION")
    print("=" * 80)

    # Convert back to DataFrame for high-performance vectorized compliance filtering
    df = formatted_df.copy()

    # Audit Indicator 1: Reassignment Threshold (> 2 Reassignments)
    case_reassignments = df.groupby('case:concept:name')['reassignment_count'].max()
    breached_reassignments = case_reassignments[case_reassignments > 2]
    reassignment_breach_rate = (len(breached_reassignments) / len(case_reassignments)) * 100.0
    print(f"Indicator 1 [Reassignment > 2]: {len(breached_reassignments)} cases ({reassignment_breach_rate:.2f}%) breached threshold.")

    # Audit Indicator 2: High Overtime Risk Exposure
    # Verify no non-urgent work was dispatched to resources with high overtime risk
    overtime_violations = df[
        (df['workload_snapshot'] == 'overtime_risk_high') & 
        (df['case_priority'] != 'urgent') &
        (df['assignment_reason'].str.contains('new_assignment', na=False))
    ]
    print(f"Indicator 2 [Overtime Shielding]: {len(overtime_violations)} unshielded assignments detected (Target: 0).")

    # Audit Indicator 3: Security Review Gate Conformance
    # Rule: Must be Skill 3 OR Skill 1-2 supervised by Gita or Hugo
    security_events = df[df['concept:name'] == 'Security Review']
    security_breaches = security_events[
        (security_events['required_skill'] == 'Security') &
        (~security_events['org:resource'].isin(['R7_Gita', 'R8_Hugo'])) &
        (~security_events['assignment_reason'].str.contains('supervised_by_R7|supervised_by_R8', na=False))
    ]
    print(f"Indicator 3 [Security Review Gate]: {len(security_breaches)} compliance breaches detected (Target: 0).")

    # Audit Indicator 4: Final Approval Gate Conformance
    # Rule: Skill 1 may prepare; finalizer must be Skill 2-3
    approval_events = df[df['concept:name'] == 'Final Approval']
    approval_breaches = approval_events[
        (approval_events['assignment_reason'].str.contains('solo_skill_1', na=False))
    ]
    print(f"Indicator 4 [Final Approval Gate]: {len(approval_breaches)} illegal trainee approvals detected (Target: 0).")

    # Audit Indicator 5: Training Quota Conformance on Normal/Low Priority Cases
    norm_low_cases = df[df['case_priority'].isin(['normal', 'low'])]['case:concept:name'].unique()
    training_cases = df[
        (df['case:concept:name'].isin(norm_low_cases)) & 
        (df['assignment_reason'].str.contains('training_priority_supported', na=False))
    ]['case:concept:name'].nunique()
    
    training_quota_rate = (training_cases / len(norm_low_cases)) * 100.0 if len(norm_low_cases) > 0 else 0.0
    print(f"Indicator 5 [Training Quota Share]: {training_cases}/{len(norm_low_cases)} cases ({training_quota_rate:.1f}%) supported training (Target: >= 20.0%).")

    # Audit Indicator 6: SLA Miss Attributability
    if 'sla_deadline' in df.columns:
        case_slas = df.groupby('case:concept:name').agg({
            'time:timestamp': 'max',
            'sla_deadline': 'first',
            'case_priority': 'first'
        })
        case_slas['breached'] = case_slas['time:timestamp'] > pd.to_datetime(case_slas['sla_deadline'], utc=True)
        urgent_breaches = case_slas[(case_slas['case_priority'] == 'urgent') & (case_slas['breached'])]
        print(f"Indicator 6 [SLA Compliance]: Urgent SLA Breaches = {len(urgent_breaches)} (Target: 0). Total Breaches = {case_slas['breached'].sum()}.")

    # Audit Indicator 7: Workload Distribution (Gini Coefficient)
    resource_case_counts = df.groupby('org:resource')['case:concept:name'].nunique().values
    def calculate_gini(array):
        array = array.astype(float)
        if np.amin(array) < 0:
            array -= np.amin(array)
        array += 0.0000001
        array = np.sort(array)
        index = np.arange(1, array.shape[0] + 1)
        n = array.shape[0]
        return ((np.sum((2 * index - n  - 1) * array)) / (n * np.sum(array)))
    
    gini_score = calculate_gini(resource_case_counts)
    print(f"Indicator 7 [Workload Equity Gini]: {gini_score:.3f} (Target: <= 0.250).")

    print("\n" + "=" * 80)
    print("STEP 5: AUDIT SUMMARY & RECOMMENDED FEEDBACK ADJUSTMENTS")
    print("=" * 80)
    if training_quota_rate < 20.0:
        print("[-] WARNING: Training quota fell below 20%. Increase training weight W_train in scoring engine.")
    else:
        print("[+] SUCCESS: Training quota satisfied.")
        
    if len(overtime_violations) > 0:
        print("[-] ALERT: Overtime shielding failed. Strengthen hard constraint pruning for high-overtime workers.")
    else:
        print("[+] SUCCESS: Overtime protection fully maintained.")
        
    if len(security_breaches) == 0 and len(approval_breaches) == 0:
        print("[+] SUCCESS: Full regulatory gate compliance maintained.")
    else:
        print("[-] CRITICAL: Regulatory compliance failure detected in gated activities.")
    print("=" * 80)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # Example execution call (path to be supplied in operational environment)
    # run_pm4py_policy_audit("service_process_event_log.csv")
    pass
```
