## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

**Derived diagnostics [D]**

| Month | Mix-implied breach (month variant shares × that month's matured variant rates) | Observed | Brier of a constant predictor at the observed rate | Model Brier |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan | 0.171 | 0.18 | 0.148 | 0.132 |
| Jun | 0.158 | 0.15 | 0.128 | 0.139 |
| Jul | 0.199 | 0.19 | 0.154 | 0.151 |
| Aug | 0.313 | 0.28 | 0.202 | 0.197 |
| Sep | 0.341 | 0.31 | 0.214 | 0.214 |
| Oct | about 0.33 (projection using Sep variant rates, unverified) | 0.16 (55% mature) | 0.134 | 0.205 |

- In Jan–Mar the model beats the constant predictor by about 11–15%. From Jun it is roughly at or below it.
- In Sep it only matches it. This is an in-sample benchmark and is prevalence-dependent, so use it as a triangulation cue, not a formal score.
- Observed rates 0.03 below the mix-implied values in Aug–Sep are the size expected from maturity selection.

**Month by month**

- **Jan–Mar:** Stable baseline. AUROC 0.81–0.82, ECE 0.02, unknown activity 0.2–0.3%, variants 45/35/20, V3 edge 28–35 h. This is the reference window.
- **April:** A schema and pipeline incident. The rename produced 17% unknown activities, 18% unmapped variants and 14% missing queue features. Prefixes were built at the wrong event or not at all, and duplicates inflated event counts before 20 Apr. Predicted risk rose to 0.28 against an observed 0.19 that did not move. Labels were 100% mature and variant-specific breach rates were about flat, so the world did not change; the model's inputs did. Its Brier (0.184) is worse than a constant predictor. The 18% unmapped cases are also missing from the variant-specific rates.
- **May:** Partial recovery. The 6 May patch fixed labels, but the cached "events before diagnosis" feature was not recomputed for April. Unknown activity is 2% and queue missingness 3%, both above baseline. AUROC is 0.78 and ECE 0.05. This is mostly residual pipeline debt, and should be confirmed by recomputation.
- **June:** A real process mix change with little evidence of a worse model. V2 rose to 52% under the routing policy, V2 execution time stayed flat, and the variant-mix-implied breach rate (0.158) matches the observed 0.15. The lower breach rate is not better service; it is mix. The model over-predicts (0.20 vs 0.15, ECE 0.06) because it did not absorb the routing change, while AUROC holds at 0.79. This is a calibration and prior problem, not a ranking failure.
- **July:** The supplier problem emerges. The parts edge is 65 h, V3 breach is 0.46, and V1 and V2 are unchanged. Aggregate calibration looks acceptable (ECE 0.042, predicted 0.18 vs observed 0.19) only because V2 at 50% dilutes V3 errors. This is a masking effect. AUROC is 0.76.
- **August:** Supplier-driven process and concept drift. The edge is 130 h, V3 breach is 0.64, and V3 share is 35% with no Partner Z. Predicted 0.19 vs observed 0.28 and AUROC 0.68. This is genuine degradation with 93% mature labels, so it is not a label artifact. The pre-Z V3 share rise is unexplained.
- **September:** Three overlapping effects.
  1. Supplier concept drift continues (V3 breach 0.61).
  2. Partner Z adds composition: 24% of cases, 58% V3, plus features the model lacks.
  3. Labels are 82% mature and Z's are slower, so observed rates are biased low.

  AUROC is 0.66 and ECE 0.14. Volume jumped 22%, but non-Z volume (about 3,900) is slightly below August's, so congestion is not clearly implicated. Z and non-Z breach gaps are not yet attributable to the model.
- **October:** The data are heavily confounded by label maturity. Only 55% of labels are mature and the API backlog removes long paths disproportionately. The 0.16 observed rate is not a recovery, and Oct AUROC, Brier and ECE are computed on a biased short-path subset. The true rate is probably around 0.30–0.35 [H/D], which would mean about 0.10 underprediction. The part-edge slowdown (155 h, a process observation) continues. Treat Oct as provisional.

**Why these are not one failure**

| Period | Cause | Mechanism | Right fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apr–May | Pipeline | Bad inputs | Repair and re-backfill, not retrain |
| Jun | Routing policy | Prior/mix shift | Intercept or policy feature |
| Jul–Sep | Supplier | V3 concept drift | Segment recalibration plus supplier features |
| Sep | Partner Z | Population shift | Source-aware features and monitors |
| Oct | Censoring | Measurement bias | Wait or censor-aware evaluation |

A single "drift" label would recommend the wrong action in at least three of these periods.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

1. **Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting**
   - *Procedure:*
     - For each start-month cohort, plot the labeled fraction and the observed breach rate by label age (30, 45, 60, 90 d).
     - Re-score each cohort at the same age. Compare Aug and Sep at Oct's equivalent age.
     - Use IPCW or survival-style adjustment for open cases.
   - *If censoring (label delay):* Oct's rate climbs toward about 0.30+ as labels mature, and Oct metrics move toward Sep's at equal age.
   - *If real improvement:* Oct stays about 0.16 at matched age.
   - *Also:* Aug and Jul stay degraded at full maturity under concept or process drift. Under censoring alone they would recover.

2. **Recompute after label normalization and duplicate cleaning**
   - *Procedure:*
     - Rebuild Apr–May prefixes and features from raw events with uniform normalization and uniform dedupe.
     - Re-score with the frozen model and compare to the served predictions.
   - *If pipeline failure:* April AUROC returns to about 0.80, ECE drops below 0.04, and mean prediction goes back to about 0.19.
   - *If real April change:* metrics stay degraded after recompute. A partial recovery means a mixed cause.
   - Run a sensitivity check with and without near-duplicate removal.

3. **Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source and month**
   - *Procedure:*
     - Compute intercept, slope, ECE and within-stratum AUROC with bootstrap CIs, on mature labels only.
     - Stratify by realized variant, by source, and by at-diagnosis segments such as a V3-likely flag. Be cautious: realized variant is post-prediction, so conditioning on it is selection.
   - *If V3 concept drift:* the V3 intercept shifts with a large negative residual, V1 and V2 stay calibrated, and Z and non-Z residuals are similar after controlling for V3.
   - *If pipeline failure:* errors spread across all strata and concentrate in the April–May 6 and new-source rows.
   - *If Z-specific:* Z residuals persist after controlling for variant.

4. **Variant and DFG/performance-DFG comparison**
   - *Procedure:*
     - Compare monthly variant shares and edge frequencies with JS divergence or a chi-square test.
     - Compare edge median and p90 times, by case-start cohort, matured cases only, and by source.
     - Check for activity self-loops and unknown-activity edges.
   - *If process drift:* the Order PartPart Received slowdown appears in every source and supplier, and control flow for V1 is unchanged.
   - *If pipeline:* April shows spurious `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` edges and duplicate self-loops that disappear after cleaning.
   - Compare event-calendar and case-start cohorts. If slowdowns appear only in the event-calendar view, they may be survivorship.

5. **Outcome relationship conditional on the same path**
   - *Procedure:*
     - Fit breach ~ variant + realized edge durations + prefix X + month interactions, or a discrete-time hazard model, using mature data.
     - Test month × part-wait interactions with a likelihood ratio.
     - Audit promised-date rules: promised-minus-created by variant, month and source.
   - *If only process drift:* P(breach | V3, part-wait bin) is stable across months. Only the wait distribution moved.
   - *If outcome-mechanism change:* the curve shifts, for example because promised dates were not adjusted, technician capacity changed, or SLAs differ.
   - *If virtual drift:* conditional on prefix X only, the relationship shifts. Conditional on prefix X plus supplier lead time, it is stable.

6. **Population reweighting / transport check for Partner Z**
   - *Procedure:*
     - Fit a source classifier on model inputs plus distance and device age.
     - Reweight non-Z cases to look like Z and check overlap.
     - Decompose the Z vs non-Z breach gap into composition and residual (Oaxaca/Kitagawa style), age-matched.
   - *If composition only:* reweighted non-Z reproduces Z breach and calibration. The residual is about 0, consistent with the [D] estimate of 0.41 vs 0.45.
   - *If Z-specific mechanism:* the residual persists and shrinks after adding distance and device age.
   - *If positivity is poor:* the transport estimate is unreliable, so report that rather than a point estimate.

7. **Feature missingness and lineage audit**
   - *Procedure:*
     - Per feature, source and month, report null rate, cached vs recomputed disagreement, feature version, and compute time vs ingestion time.
     - Report prefix-builder success rate and the share of prefixes built at the correct event.
   - *If pipeline:* disagreement concentrates in 1 Apr–6 May and the new source, and stale cached features persist.
   - *If true drift:* cached and recomputed values agree, and only distributions differ.

8. **Mix vs rate decomposition**
   - *Procedure:* Kitagawa-decompose each month's breach change vs Q1 into variant-mix and within-variant-rate effects, on mature data.
   - *Expected:* June is almost all mix. Aug is mostly V3 rate. Sep adds mix from Z.
   - *If June shows a large rate effect:* real service change, not just routing.

9. **Hidden-context test**
   - *Procedure:* Train a challenger on forward-chaining, mature, clean folds with trailing supplier lead time (for example a 7-day median OrderReceived over completed orders), with no realized durations.
   - *If hidden context:* calibration largely recovers.
   - *If the mechanism truly changed:* only slight improvement.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Stage 0: Immediate containment (days)**
- Do not retrain and do not auto-trigger retraining.
- Label Oct metrics provisional. Mark Apr–6 May predictions as invalid in the audit record and mark their features as quarantined.
- Abstain or fall back to a simple baseline for cases with unmapped or unknown prefixes.
- Tell consumers that absolute probabilities understate risk for V3-likely cases and Partner Z, and use score ranking and relative thresholds cautiously (AUROC is about 0.64–0.68).
- If a threshold drives action, add an interim business rule, for example escalating cases that need parts.
- Escalate the outcome API backlog.

**Stage 1: Short-term recalibration (1–3 weeks)**
- Use logistic recalibration (intercept and slope) on mature, pipeline-clean data only.
- Jul–Aug are usable with censoring adjustment. Sep is usable only age-matched or IPCW-weighted and for non-Z at first.
- Segment by at-diagnosis features (V3-propensity band, source), not by realized variant.
- Use a short, time-decayed window and refit frequently, because supplier conditions move (42  155 h in four months).
- Do not apply a static June correction. V2 share fell from 52% to 35%, so a fixed adjustment would now be wrong.
- Never recalibrate on April, because that would bake the pipeline error into the correction.

**Stage 2: Feature repair (parallel, 2–6 weeks)**
- Re-backfill all derived features from raw events: normalized labels, uniform dedupe, recomputed "events before diagnosis". Version features and enforce schema-contract tests.
- Verify Partner Z lineage, since it is a new source after the migration.
- Add features that are available at diagnosis time (no leakage):
  - trailing supplier lead time and part availability
  - parts-likely flag
  - routing-policy version
  - source
  - rural distance and device age
  - queue load

**Stage 3: Full retraining (only when gates pass)**
- Retrain only if a challenger beats the recalibrated incumbent on a forward-chaining, mature, clean holdout, with a Brier interval that excludes zero and adequate V3 and Z sample sizes.
- Wait until Sep and Z cohorts are at least about 95% mature, or use censoring-aware training.

| Month | Training status |
|---|---|
| Jan–Mar | Safe (baseline) |
| Apr | Unsafe. Use as a pipeline test, not training, unless fully recomputed and validated. |
| May | Conditional: safe only after recompute and validation |
| Jun | Safe after recompute, with a routing-policy flag |
| Jul | Safe (98% mature) |
| Aug | Conditional: 93% mature, use censoring-aware weights or wait |
| Sep | Unsafe until mature, especially Z |
| Oct | Unsafe (55% mature, long-path biased) |

**Segment/variant-specific models**
- A model split by realized variant is not deployable, because variant is determined after the prediction point and would leak. It is also not needed directly.
- A two-stage or mixture structure is justified by the evidence: P(variant | X, routing policy) plus variant-specific breach models. V3 is volatile, V1 and V2 are stable, and the routing policy changes the first stage only. Test it as a challenger.
- A Partner-Z-specific model is not yet justified. There is no stratified evidence of Z-specific error and no matured Z sample. Start with Z features and a source-aware calibration layer, and revisit after enough mature Z cases accumulate. Consider fairness and business implications before using source as a feature.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

| Monitor | Metrics | Cadence | Illustrative alert | Depends on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Data quality (gate) | Unknown activity share, schema/label coverage, duplicate rate by source, feature null rate, cached vs recomputed disagreement, prefix-builder success | Daily | Unknown > 2%, queue missing > 5%, new activity names | None (upstream of everything) |
| Label maturity (gate) | Label fraction by age, maturity of long paths, median delay (on matured only), API lag | Daily | Maturity < 90% or long-path coverage gap | Data quality |
| Predictive | AUROC, Brier, Brier vs constant, within-variant AUROC on age-matched cohorts | Weekly | Sustained drop vs baseline CI | DQ green and maturity green, else "provisional" |
| Calibration | Intercept, slope, ECE by segment, source, V3-propensity band | Weekly | Intercept outside CI for 2+ windows | Same gates |
| Process variant | Variant shares (completed traces, plus open-case share reported separately), unmapped share, JS divergence | Weekly | Shift > threshold (informational) | DQ green |
| Performance DFG | Edge median and p90 (OrderReceived, DiagRemote Fix), by supplier and source, case-start cohort | Daily/weekly | Edge median above trailing baseline + k×MAD | DQ green |
| Population | Source share, feature PSI, domain-classifier AUC, Z breach and calibration residual | Weekly | Share change > 5 pp, PSI > 0.2 | DQ green. Z residual needs minimum mature n. |
| Intervention / deployment | Model and feature version, threshold, intervention rate, policy changes (routing, SLA, supplier events), shadow scores | Continuous | Unannotated change in any of these | None |

**Alert dependencies**
1. If data quality is red, suppress performance and drift alerts (mark "unreliable"), page data engineering, and block retraining.
2. If label maturity is red, mark metrics "provisional" and hold retraining. The label-delay alarm escalates to the API owner and cannot trigger retraining. Use leading indicators meanwhile, such as elapsed-time-vs-promised-date hazard among open cases, and compare age-matched cohorts.
3. Process alerts are explanatory: a variant-mix change with calibration intact is informational. A performance-DFG slowdown plus calibration drift in the matching segment escalates to recalibration and a feature review.
4. Retraining requires all of: DQ green, maturity green, conditional drift persisting for at least 2–3 cohorts, and a challenger beating the recalibrated incumbent.
5. Annotate routing, supplier and onboarding events in the timeline so monitors can attribute changes.

```python
import pandas as pd, pm4py
from collections import Counter

CASE, ACT, TS = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
RENAME = {"Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"}
DX = "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
V = {  # baseline variants
 ("Case Created","Triage",DX,"Dispatch Technician","Repair Completed","Case Closed"): "V1",
 ("Case Created","Triage",DX,"Remote Fix","Case Closed"): "V2",
 ("Case Created","Triage",DX,"Order Part","Part Received","Dispatch Technician",
  "Repair Completed","Case Closed"): "V3"}
EDGES = {"part_wait_h": ("Order Part","Part Received"),
         "remote_fix_h": (DX,"Remote Fix")}

def load_clean(path):
    raw = pd.read_csv(path)  # assumes columns: case_id, activity, timestamp, source
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(raw, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity",
                                timestamp_key="timestamp")
    df["is_unknown"] = df[ACT].eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY")      # flag; never silently impute
    df[ACT] = df[ACT].replace(RENAME)                      # normalize BEFORE features/dedupe
    df = df.sort_values([CASE, TS])
    df["is_exact_dup"] = df.duplicated([CASE, ACT, TS], keep="first")
    gap = df.groupby([CASE, ACT])[TS].diff().dt.total_seconds()
    df["is_near_dup"] = gap.lt(60) & ~df["is_exact_dup"]   # flag only: may be genuine rework
    clean = df[~df.is_exact_dup].copy()                    # same rule for ALL sources and dates
    return df, clean   # keep df as the raw audit trail; report dup rate by source/month

def summarize(d, tag, month):
    out = {"month": month, "cohort": tag, "cases": d[CASE].nunique(), "events": len(d),
           "unknown_share": d["is_unknown"].mean(),
           "closed_share": d.groupby(CASE)[ACT].apply(lambda s: "Case Closed" in set(s)).mean()}
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(d)                       # {activity tuple: count}
    tot, c = sum(variants.values()), Counter()
    for k, n in variants.items():
        c[V.get(tuple(k), "other_or_open")] += n           # open cases are NOT variant drift
    out.update({f"share_{k}": n / tot for k, n in c.items()})
    dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(d)
    out["n_dfg_edges"] = len(dfg)
    out["unknown_edges"] = sum(v for (a, b), v in dfg.items()
                               if "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY" in (a, b))
    try:   # aggregation option is version dependent; fallback is mean, label accordingly
        perf, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(d, perf_aggregation_key="median")
    except TypeError:
        perf, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(d)
    for name, edge in EDGES.items():
        out[name] = perf.get(edge, float("nan")) / 3600
    try:   # descriptive temporal features, not a drift detector
        tf = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(d, grouper_freq="W")
        out.update({f"tf_{k}": v for k, v in tf.mean(numeric_only=True).items()})
    except Exception as e:
        out["tf_error"] = str(e)
    dx = d[d[ACT] == DX].groupby(CASE)[TS].min().rename("dx_ts")
    pre = d.join(dx, on=CASE)
    pre = pre[pre[TS] < pre["dx_ts"]].groupby(CASE).size()  # fresh, uncached feature
    out["no_dx_share"] = 1 - len(dx) / out["cases"]
    out["events_before_dx_mean"] = pre.mean()
    return out

def monthly_audit(clean, months):
    start = clean.groupby(CASE)[TS].min()
    rows = []
    for m0, m1 in months:  # e.g. ("2026-04-01 00:00:00", "2026-05-01 00:00:00")
        # A) event-calendar: events occurring in the month, from cases of mixed age.
        #    Good for workload and throughput. Biased for outcomes and durations.
        ev = pm4py.filter_time_range(clean, m0, m1, mode="events")
        rows.append(summarize(ev, "event_calendar", m0[:7]))
        # B) case-start cohort: all events of cases that began in the month.
        #    Right unit for model evaluation. Needs a maturity cutoff for interpretation.
        ids = start[(start >= pd.Timestamp(m0, tz=start.dt.tz)) &
                    (start < pd.Timestamp(m1, tz=start.dt.tz))].index
        rows.append(summarize(clean[clean[CASE].isin(ids)], "case_start", m0[:7]))
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)   # auditable table; no model fitted, no drift detector invented
```
