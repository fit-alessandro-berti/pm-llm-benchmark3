# 2. Integrated explanation

The observed pattern—service time falling while error rates rise only under specific conditions—cannot be explained by any single mechanism. Instead, multiple mechanisms interact, sometimes reinforcing and sometimes counteracting each other.

**Early shift (positions 1–30):** Service times are high (18.813.6 min) primarily due to **case-mix selection**. Complex cases accumulate overnight or between shifts, and senior resources intentionally take these first (48–56% high-complexity in first hour). This creates an apparent "warm-up" effect, but it's largely compositional. Error rates are moderate (2.5–3.4%) because seniors handle complex cases and WIP is manageable (33–46 cases).

**Mid shift (positions 31–50):** This is the optimal zone. Service time reaches minimum (12.9 min), error rates are lowest (2.5–2.7%), WIP is moderate (31 cases), and complexity is lower (0.50). Learning has occurred, fatigue hasn't set in, and queue pressure hasn't built. This represents the system's designed capacity.

**Late shift (positions 51–75+):** Here mechanisms diverge. Service time continues falling (11.410.8 min), but this reflects **queue pressure** not learning. Workers rush to clear backlog before shift end. Error rates spike (4.67.9%) and rework doubles (8.213.7%). However, this spike is **conditional**: it's concentrated in high-WIP periods (>70 cases) and affects juniors disproportionately (12.4% error rate under high WIP vs 4.4% under low WIP).

**Critical interaction:** Queue pressure amplifies other vulnerabilities. High WIP doesn't cause errors uniformly—it interacts with:
- **Experience level:** Juniors show 3× error increase under high WIP; seniors show 2×
- **Case complexity:** High complexity + high WIP = 10.9% errors vs 2.1% baseline
- **Shift timing:** Day shift end (demand peak) shows 7.5% errors vs Night shift 5.1%
- **Batching behavior:** Quality Check delays create correction bursts that inflate late-shift error metrics

**Why speed increases while errors rise only sometimes:** Workers adapt to queue pressure by speeding up. Under moderate pressure with simple cases, this works (medium WIP, low complexity: 3.0% errors). Under high pressure with complex cases or inexperienced workers, quality degrades. The system incentivizes throughput (cases closed per shift) more than quality (errors caught later), creating rational but problematic adaptation.

**The fatigue question:** True physiological fatigue may contribute to late-shift errors, but the data suggests **motivational fatigue** (reduced care under pressure) and **cognitive overload** (too many cases in working memory) are larger factors. Night staff maintain quality better despite circadian disadvantage, suggesting queue pressure matters more than time-of-day.

## 3.1 Within-resource comparisons

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd

# Load and format event log
event_log = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id='case:concept:name', 
                                    activity='concept:name', 
                                    timestamp='time:timestamp')

# Compare first-20 vs later events per resource
def analyze_resource_learning(event_log, resource_col='org:resource', 
                               position_col='sequence_position_within_shift'):
    results = []
    for resource in event_log[resource_col].unique():
        resource_events = event_log[event_log[resource_col] == resource]
        early = resource_events[resource_events[position_col] <= 20]
        late = resource_events[resource_events[position_col] > 20]
        
        results.append({
            'resource': resource,
            'early_mean_time': early['service_time'].mean(),
            'late_mean_time': late['service_time'].mean(),
            'early_error_rate': early['error_flag'].mean(),
            'late_error_rate': late['error_flag'].mean(),
            'early_wip': early['wip_at_start'].mean(),
            'late_wip': late['wip_at_start'].mean()
        })
    return pd.DataFrame(results)
```

```python
# Stratify by position bins, controlling for WIP and complexity
def position_effect_analysis(event_log):
    event_log['position_bin'] = pd.cut(event_log['sequence_position_within_shift'],
                                        bins=[0, 5, 15, 30, 50, 75, 100],
                                        labels=['1-5', '6-15', '16-30', '31-50', '51-75', '76+'])
    
    # Control for WIP
    event_log['wip_bin'] = pd.cut(event_log['wip_at_start'],
                                   bins=[0, 30, 70, 100],
                                   labels=['low', 'medium', 'high'])
    
    # Control for complexity
    event_log['complexity_bin'] = pd.cut(event_log['case_complexity_score'],
                                          bins=[0, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0],
                                          labels=['low', 'medium', 'high'])
    
    # Grouped analysis
    grouped = event_log.groupby(['position_bin', 'wip_bin', 'complexity_bin']).agg({
        'service_time': 'mean',
        'error_flag': 'mean',
        'rework_flag': 'mean',
        'case:concept:name': 'count'
    }).reset_index()
    
    return grouped
```

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

## 5.1 Worker-level conclusions can be unfair

**Selection bias:** Junior resources show 12.4% error rates under high WIP, but this reflects **system design** not individual capability. Juniors are disproportionately assigned to high-pressure periods (Day shift peaks) and receive more complex cases late in shifts when seniors have selected easier cases. Punishing based on raw error rates penalizes workers for conditions they cannot control.

**Measurement error:** Error flags may be inconsistently applied. Quality Check batching in final 45 minutes means late-shift workers face more scrutiny. Early-shift errors may be caught by later Quality Checks but attributed to original worker without context. The `error_flag` variable may reflect detection probability more than actual error incidence.

**Queue pressure created by management:** High WIP (>70) is not a worker failure—it's a staffing and demand-matching failure. Workers adapt rationally to queue pressure by speeding up. When errors result, the system has incentivized throughput over quality. Holding workers accountable for system-created pressure is fundamentally unjust.

## 5.2 Automation quality concerns

**Bot_Risk interactions:** Bot_Risk performs Risk Classification instantly, but human corrections increase when queue_length_at_start > 70. This suggests:
- Bot accuracy may degrade on edge cases that accumulate in queues
- Workers may override bot decisions under pressure without proper review
- Automation errors may be attributed to human workers who "corrected" them

**Recommendation:** Audit Bot_Risk accuracy independently. Don't count bot-originated errors against human workers who inherit them.

## 5.3 Punitive use of error rates

**Why intervention 8 is harmful:**
- Late-shift error rates reflect queue pressure (positions 76+: 88 mean queue length), not worker quality
- Night staff show 8.2% error rates under high WIP despite circadian disadvantage—this is system failure, not worker failure
- Punishment creates underreporting incentives, reducing data quality for improvement
- High performers may avoid late shifts, worsening the problem

**Alternative approach:** Use error rates for **system improvement** (where does the process fail?) not **individual accountability** (who failed?). Aggregate by shift, WIP band, and complexity—not by individual worker.

## 5.4 Case-mix fairness

**Complex case assignment:** Senior resources take complex cases early (48–56% high-complexity in first hour). This is rational but creates apparent performance differences. If seniors are measured on overall error rates without complexity adjustment, they appear worse despite better skill.

**Recommendation:** Always adjust performance metrics for case_complexity_score. Use risk-adjusted control charts, not raw rates.

## 5.5 Handover and queue ownership

**Shift boundary effects:** Early shift inherits Night backlog (61 mean queue at start). Late shift inherits Day peak (70 mean queue at start). Workers are measured on performance but not on queue conditions they inherit.

**Recommendation:** Track queue ownership changes at shift boundaries. Attribute errors to the shift where the case was primarily processed, not where it was closed.
