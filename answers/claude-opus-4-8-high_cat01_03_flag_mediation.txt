## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

I treat the flag as an **exposure that is one component of a policy bundle**, not a freely manipulable atomic cause. Because the mediators (manual-review occurrence, queue waits, escalation) are downstream of the flag *and* are themselves affected by exposure-induced confounders (system congestion, other cases' treatment, senior-team scarcity), classical natural direct/indirect effects (NDE/NIE) require cross-world independence assumptions that are almost certainly violated here (mediator-outcome confounding by `arrival_load`, `U_congestion`, and shared queues). I therefore prefer **separable effects** (Robins–Richardson) and **interventional (in)direct effects** where the mediator distribution is set by a *policy* rather than a cross-world counterfactual, and **controlled direct effects (CDE)** where I want the effect of the flag holding a mediator at a fixed, well-defined value (e.g., "no mandatory review"). Separable effects are attractive because the flag naturally decomposes into distinct operational channels (a *display/priority* component and a *routing/review-trigger* component) that map to real interventions P1–P4. Under interference, all estimands are defined as **policy contrasts on the full case stream**, not per-unit-in-isolation effects.

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 (total policy) | P0 vs P1 (suppress flag display; keep score/humans) | all cases in a period (stream-level) | mean/median cycle time | total policy effect (separable: display+routing bundle) | Yes — a real system-level switch | interference via shared queues; confounding of who gets flagged by `complexity`/`U_risk` |
| E2 (triage completion) | flagged vs comparable unflagged at same risk region | cases near a risk threshold | arrival→triage_done duration | controlled/component direct effect on triage channel | Mostly — priority-lane is a concrete manipulation | positivity: few unflagged near high risk; `triage_resource` assignment differs |
| E3 (review-occurrence mediation) | P0 vs P2 (priority triage, no mandatory review) | flagged cases | cycle time attributable to *whether* review happens | interventional indirect effect via `manual_review` occurrence | Partly — "occurrence" is a binary policy lever | occurrence and its duration are entangled; review may still be chosen by judgment |
| E4 (review-wait mediation) | P0 vs P3 (add protected review capacity) | flagged cases | cycle time attributable to `manual_queue_wait` | interventional indirect effect via waiting-time mediator | Partly | wait is a queue equilibrium object → treatment-confounder feedback, interference |
| E5 (escalation mediation) | P0 vs P4 (change escalation criteria/capacity) | reviewed cases (esp. flagged) | cycle time via `escalation`+`senior_service` | interventional indirect effect via escalation channel | Partly | scarce senior team → strong interference; escalation is risk-selected |
| E6 (total cycle time) | any Pk vs P0 | all cases (stream) | total cycle time distribution | total interventional effect | Yes as a policy contrast | same as E1; distributional not just mean |
| E7 (decision quality) | any Pk vs P0 | all cases with adjudicable ground truth | outcome accuracy / reject-error rate / rework | total effect on quality (co-primary) | Weakly — no ground-truth label here | outcome is not verified correctness; `U_risk` unobserved |
| E8 (triage-only CDE) | flag on vs off, review forced to "none" | flagged-eligible cases | cycle time | controlled direct effect (mediator fixed) | Yes conceptually | may be off-support / unsafe; not the same as P2 in practice |

Why not pure NDE/NIE: setting `manual_review` to its "natural unflagged value" is a cross-world quantity requiring no mediator-outcome confounder affected by the flag — violated by `arrival_load` and shared-queue state. Separable/interventional effects keep every contrast tied to a runnable policy.

Interference / exposure-induced confounding / feedback (≤180 words): The manual-review and escalation queues are **shared**, so treating one case (routing it to review/escalation) consumes reviewer- and senior-team capacity and lengthens `manual_queue_wait`/`escalation_queue_wait` for *other* concurrent cases. This is classic **interference**: a unit's outcome depends on others' exposures, so per-case "flag effects" are ill-defined; only stream-level policy contrasts are. **Exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounding** arises because `flag` (via priority routing and mandatory review) changes queue state, and that same queue state (`arrival_load`, `U_congestion`) confounds the mediator→`cycle_time` and mediator→`decision_outcome` relations — the precise structure that breaks NDE/NIE identification. **Treatment-confounder feedback** appears across time: the flag's routing raises congestion, congestion feeds back into later cases' waits and possibly into human review decisions and even future scoring thresholds, creating a dynamic loop that only g-methods / separable-effect reasoning under explicit congestion models can handle.

## 3. Stage decomposition

Intervals are computed strictly from timestamps as differences (minutes). Waiting vs service are separated: e.g. `triage-done-to-manual-start` is *manual queue wait*, `manual service` is `manual_done−manual_start`. **Absent stages are `NA`, never 0**: a case that never enters review contributes to no review/escalation interval and is excluded from those medians (I report per-stage support n). `residual-to-close` = (last observed stage completion, i.e. escalation_done else manual_done else triage_done) → close. Values are medians in minutes.

Handling of non-occurring stages: cases with a `-` are omitted from that stage's median (support n shown) and never coerced to zero, so subgroup denominators differ across columns. Low-complexity cases are all unflagged and never reviewed/escalated, so mediator columns are structurally `NA`.

Interpretation (100–180 words): The contrast most consistent with **acceleration at triage, delay downstream** is: flagged cases start triage far sooner (median 12 vs 150 min) and finish triage faster (36 vs 57 min), yet their **manual queue wait explodes** (405 vs 105 min) and review is essentially universal (n=10/10 vs 4/10), and 6/10 also incur escalation queueing (~420 min) plus senior service. Total cycle is ~3× longer (987 vs 345). Within flagged cases, escalated ones dominate the tail (1314 vs 528). This pattern is **not automatically causal**: flagged cases are also higher `complexity`/`risk_score`, high-`arrival_load`, and are routed to a *scarce* senior team, so complexity-driven service time, congestion-driven waiting, and selection all co-move with the flag. The unflagged reviewed cases are few (n=4) and human-selected, giving poor overlap for any clean flag-vs-no-flag comparison at matched risk.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Does the flag itself (display/priority) cause faster triage, net of risk? | **Local threshold / regression-discontinuity** around the score→flag cutoff | condition on `risk_score` in a narrow window; also `complexity` | flag assignment locally as-if-random at threshold; no manipulation of score | McCrary density test at cutoff; balance of `complexity` just above/below | smooth outcome through the cutoff (no jump) → triage speedup is not flag-caused |
| Are downstream delays queue-driven or case-driven? | **Queue-state-aware regression**: model each wait on concurrent WIP, server-busy, `arrival_load` | queue occupancy at each case's stage-entry; server identity | wait = f(queue state) + noise; congestion measurable/reconstructable from timestamps | do reconstructed waits predict actual `td2ms`? residual flag effect after conditioning on WIP | if flag coefficient vanishes once WIP is controlled → delay is congestion, not flag-intrinsic |
| What is the true policy effect free of selection? | **Policy-change / stepped-wedge or randomized encouragement** (randomly suppress flag display for a share of cases) | randomization block (time, unit) | compliance; SUTVA relaxed to cluster level to allow interference | pre/post and treated/control cycle-time distributions; placebo periods | cycle time unchanged under display suppression → flag display is not the driver |
| How much delay is *mediated* by review occurrence vs wait? | **Interventional mediation** (fix occurrence via P2-like rule; separately add capacity via P3) | mediator model conditioning on `arrival_load`, `U_congestion` proxy, complexity | no *unmeasured* mediator-outcome confounder given proxies; correct mediator model | compare interventional indirect effect estimates under alternative congestion proxies | indirect effect concentrated in *wait* not *occurrence* → prioritize P3 over P2 |
| Is the flag a mere risk proxy? | **Negative-control outcome/exposure**: use a pre-flag-era outcome or an unrelated stage (e.g., registration time) as neg-control exposure | same adjustment set | neg-control shares confounding but no causal path | flag "effect" on a stage it cannot plausibly affect (e.g., arrival→register) | nonzero effect on neg-control outcome → residual confounding, discount flag effect |
| How robust to hidden risk `U_risk`? | **Sensitivity analysis** (E-value / Rosenbaum bounds) on residual flag→cycle and flag→outcome | current observed set | bounded strength of `U_risk`→flag and `U_risk`→outcome | how large must `U_risk` association be to nullify effect? | small E-value → conclusion fragile to unobserved risk |
| Do treated cases delay others? | **Interference-aware design**: cluster/switchback randomization by time-window; estimate spillover on non-flagged concurrent cases | concurrency clusters; exposure density of neighbors | partial interference within window; no cross-window carryover | does non-flagged wait rise with concurrent flagged volume? | strong positive spillover → per-case effects invalid; only stream effects meaningful |

Each design identifies a *different* object: RD → local flag-display/priority effect at threshold; queue model → decomposition of wait into structural vs congestion; encouragement/stepped-wedge → the actual deployable policy effect (E1/E6); interventional mediation → E3 vs E4 split; negative controls + sensitivity → whether apparent effects survive `U_risk`; interference design → validity of the unit of analysis itself.

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 keep current | fast triage (baseline) | large review+escalation waits (baseline) | long (median ~987 flagged) | baseline; audit trail intact | baseline congestion | observational only | reference |
| P1 suppress display, keep score/humans | likely loses priority speedup (a2ts rises toward 150) | review demand may fall to judgment-based → shorter waits | ambiguous: slower triage but lighter queues; net possibly ↓ | may re-introduce human bias/omission; risk of under-review | frees review/senior capacity | weak — needs encouragement trial | test, don't deploy blind |
| P2 priority triage, drop mandatory review | keeps fast triage | fewer forced reviews → **large** wait reduction | likely ↓↓ cycle time | **highest quality risk**: high-`U_risk` cases skip review | large positive (frees capacity) | mechanistic, untested on quality | pilot with quality co-primary |
| P3 priority triage + protected review capacity | keeps fast triage | flagged `manual_queue_wait` ↓ (targets the 405-min gap) | ↓ moderate–large, preserves review | low; review still happens | **may starve unflagged** unless capacity is *added*, not moved | strong mechanistic rationale | strong candidate |
| P4 change escalation criteria/capacity | no triage change | escalation wait ↓ (targets ~420-min senior queue; helps tail) | ↓ for escalated tail (esc cases 1314→lower) | risk if criteria loosened → miss serious cases | senior team is scarcest resource → biggest spillover leverage | plausible; tail-focused | complementary to P3 |

The shortest expected cycle time (P2) is **not** automatically best: it buys speed by deleting the review that may be catching genuine high-risk cases (quality/compliance externality that this log cannot verify).

**Selected pilot — P3 (protected review capacity for flagged cases), ≤250 words.**
*Eligibility:* cases whose `risk_score` falls in a band around the flag threshold (e.g., 0.55–0.85) so both flagged and comparable near-threshold cases are represented; exclude Low-complexity cases that never review.
*Assignment:* **switchback randomization by day/shift** (not per-case, to respect shared-queue interference): on "treatment" shifts, a *reserved* review slot pool (added capacity, funded, not reallocated from unflagged) serves flagged cases; on "control" shifts, current pooling applies. Randomize shift blocks; stratify by `arrival_load`.
*Queue-capacity guardrail:* cap reserved slots so unflagged median `manual_queue_wait` may not rise >15% vs control; auto-release reserved capacity to the shared pool if unflagged wait breaches the cap; monitor senior-team utilization to avoid pushing the bottleneck to escalation.
*Primary outcome:* flagged-case total cycle time (and its `manual_queue_wait` component). *Secondary:* unflagged cycle time (spillover), escalation wait, decision-quality proxies (reject/rework/appeal rate), reviewer overtime.
*Minimum process data:* per-stage start+done timestamps, queue occupancy / concurrent WIP at each stage-entry, reviewer and senior-team IDs, and any post-hoc correctness/appeal label.
*Stopping conditions:* stop for harm if unflagged waits breach the guardrail on ≥2 consecutive blocks, if quality proxies worsen beyond a pre-set margin, or if senior-queue congestion rises materially; stop for success if flagged cycle time drops with no unflagged/quality degradation; pre-register the analysis and an interference-aware (cluster) effect estimator.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk (`U_risk`).** If flagged cases carry substantial unobserved risk that genuinely warrants review and escalation, then the "extra" cycle time is *appropriate processing*, not policy-induced waste; P2 (drop mandatory review) would then harm outcomes and my preference shifts entirely to P3/P4, and E1's "effect" is largely confounded.
2. **Queue interference dominates.** If a queue-state-aware analysis shows the long `manual_queue_wait` is driven by concurrent WIP rather than the flag per se, then adding protected capacity (P3) may simply relocate the bottleneck (e.g., to escalation), reversing P3's benefit; only system-wide capacity or WIP control would help.
3. **Decision quality degrades under speed.** If pilot data show that removing mandatory review (P2) or loosening escalation (P4) raises reject-errors, appeals, or rework, the shortest-cycle policy becomes the worst policy and the recommendation flips toward preserving P0/P3.
4. **Timestamp semantics.** If "start" timestamps are actually assignment/scheduling times (not work-start), then my split of waiting vs service is wrong—apparent "service" may embed idle time—so the claim "faster triage service, slower queue wait" could invert once true work intervals are recovered.
5. **Positivity / lack of overlap.** If, at matched `risk_score`/`complexity`, there are essentially no unflagged analogues to flagged cases (the flag is near-deterministic in the high-risk region), then no flag-vs-no-flag contrast is identified there; the RD/matching estimands collapse and conclusions about a "flag effect" must be withdrawn.
6. **Reviewer behavior change.** If suppressing the flag (P1) changes how reviewers judge cases (e.g., they compensate by reviewing more, or anchor differently), the mediator distribution is not stable under the intervention, violating the separable/interventional assumptions and potentially reversing the predicted direction of P1's effect.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed:** Flagged cases begin triage much sooner (median 12 vs 150 min) and complete triage faster (36 vs 57 min), yet almost always enter manual review (10/10 vs 4/10), wait far longer for it (405 vs 105 min), frequently escalate to a scarce senior team (6/10), and finish with ~3× longer total cycle time (987 vs 345 min). The tail is driven by escalated, high-complexity, high-load cases.

**Plausible mechanism:** The flag is a *bundle*—a display/priority component that genuinely accelerates triage, plus a routing component that forces near-universal review and feeds shared, capacity-limited review/escalation queues. The net "flag makes cases slower" is thus mostly *mediated by occurrence and waiting downstream*, not by triage.

**Unidentified:** Whether the downstream time is warranted (hidden risk), how much wait is flag-caused vs congestion-caused, the true direct effect at matched risk (positivity is poor), interference magnitude across the shared queues, and decision-quality consequences (no verified ground truth). Natural direct/indirect effects are not identified; only policy contrasts are.

**Most useful next evidence:** A **switchback-randomized P3 pilot** (added, guardrailed protected review capacity for near-threshold flagged cases) with logged queue-state and quality proxies. It directly tests the dominant hypothesized channel (`manual_queue_wait`), respects interference by randomizing at the shift/cluster level, and yields a deployable effect estimate plus the queue and quality data needed to arbitrate between P2, P3, and P4.
