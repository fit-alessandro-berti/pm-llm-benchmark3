## 1. Executive causal assessment
Based on the available evidence, the proposal to reassign 50% of Orion cases to Nova is **partly causal through mechanisms** with a confidence level of 55%. The data show Orion cases generally have longer cycle times (21.96 hours) compared to Nova (15.24 hours), suggesting a potential efficiency gain. However, this difference is partly confounded by unobserved factors such as case complexity, shift, arrival load, compliance requirements, rework instances, and quality outcomes. Orion handles a higher proportion of high-complexity and night shift cases, which tend to be more time-consuming. Additionally, Orion cases are more likely to require compliance checks and rework, both of which can extend cycle times. The reassignment may succeed in reducing overall cycle times if Orion's workload is predominantly high-complexity cases that benefit from Nova's reviewer pool. However, it could backfire if high-complexity cases, often handled by Orion, require longer review times. Therefore, a cautious approach with targeted pilots is recommended rather than a blanket reassignment.

## 8. Residual uncertainty
1. **Unobserved case risk**: The true risk profile of cases handled by Orion is unknown.
2. **Queue dynamics**: The exact queue state and its evolution are not captured.
3. **Reviewer skill variation**: Unobserved skill levels of reviewers could influence review times.
4. **Compliance process variability**: The compliance check implementation details are not recorded.
5. **Selection bias**: Orion cases may inherently be more complex or risky, affecting generalizability.
