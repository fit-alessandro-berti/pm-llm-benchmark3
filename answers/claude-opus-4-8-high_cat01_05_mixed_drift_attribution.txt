## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The raw/mapped fitness gap in March (0.55 vs 0.90) implies that most of the conformance collapse is a measurement/provenance artifact, not a change in what the process actually does. The old normative model never saw the label `Eligibility Screen` or the duplicated technical `Open Case`, so raw alignments break on tokens, not on behavior. Mapping the eligibility label and stripping duplicated `Open Case` restores fitness to near-baseline, showing the underlying trace grammar (Register -> Validate/Screen -> Collect -> Assess -> Decision -> Close) is essentially intact through March-April.

However, recovering conformance via mapping does not prove the business process was unchanged. A mapping repairs vocabulary; it is silent about frequencies, timing, and rework structure. Mapped fitness is a coarse fit-to-model score that tolerates allowed-but-more-frequent behavior (extra Request Info loops are legal in the model), so it can stay high while rework and cycle time deteriorate.

Duplicated technical events and missing resources bite different analyses unevenly: they wreck token-based conformance and any resource/handover or social-network analysis, but barely perturb control-flow variant discovery once normalized. Missing resources being concentrated in migrated activities also biases workload attribution.

Crucially, the post-May patterns resist a logging-only explanation: mapped fitness stays high yet Assess->RequestInfo rates double (12->24->29 per 100), rework_variant_pct doubles, supplier_z_pct triples, and p90 cycle time climbs 71->121h. These are frequency and timing shifts within legal behavior, correlated with dated external events, not vocabulary artifacts. Logging drift is real but confined mainly to March provenance.

### 3.2 Normalized DFG-distance analysis

Distance measure: L1 (Manhattan) distance over the seven supplied normalized relation rates (occurrences per 100 cases), versus the January reference vector, i.e. d(m) = sum_e |rate_e(m) - rate_e(Jan)|. This is a transparent, interpretable "total edge-rate displacement per 100 cases." Assumptions and caveats: (a) `Register->Eligibility` is pinned at 100 and contributes 0, so it carries no signal; (b) the fragment omits edges (e.g. Collect->Assess reruns after Request Info, Decision->Close), so the true DFG distance is understated and only relative comparisons are meaningful; (c) rates are post-mapping, so pure March logging drift is deliberately removed from this metric; (d) I do not weight edges, so a 1-unit change on a rare edge counts like a 1-unit change on a common one.

Reference (Jan): [100, 98, 95, 12, 11, 4, 83].

|---|---|---:|

### 3.3 Cycle-time deterioration decomposition (Jan-Feb ~31h to Jul-Aug ~44.6h median; p90 68 -> ~107h)

Total median deterioration ~ +13.5h (+43%). Qualitative/ranged shares, justified from the tables:

- Service component: SMALL. Recorded `Assess` service time rose only ~4.8h -> ~5.4h (+0.6h). Even allowing for other steps, direct service growth explains at most ~2-3h, i.e. roughly 10-20% of the median increase.
- Control-flow/rework component: MODERATE. Rework_variant_pct roughly doubled (11.5% -> 27-29%). Each rework loop adds a Collect+Assess round-trip plus its own waiting. If ~15-17 extra cases per 100 each add on the order of one extra cycle segment, this plausibly accounts for ~25-40% of the median rise and a larger share of the p90 tail.
- Case-mix component: SMALL-to-MODERATE in summer, LARGE in autumn. Complexity only moved +7-8pp by July (small contributor to the summer median), but jumped +20-25pp in Oct-Nov with Senior Assessment routing, making case mix the dominant driver of the Oct-Nov peak rather than the summer peak.
- Queueing component: LARGE and dominant. The additional observation states reconstructed waiting before `Assess` grew much more than service time, and p90 grew faster than median (61.8% vs 44.5% by August). With near-constant service and rising arrivals-of-work (rework + retained cases) against reduced assessor capacity (four leavers), classic M/M/c-style queue amplification is expected. Queueing plausibly explains ~40-55% of the median deterioration and the majority of the p90 blow-out.

Because these ranges overlap and the aggregates cannot pin exact hours, I present shares as ranges, not point estimates.

### 3.4 Candidate change points (>=2 per drift class)

- Logging drift: 2026-03 (primary: relabeling, duplicate Open Case, resource missingness step) and a secondary latent point if resource-capture rules changed again (watch 2026-06/07 where missing_resource peaks 6.2-6.5%).
- Behavioral drift (control-flow/rework): 2026-05 (supplier onset -> rework step) and 2026-07 (rework peak / priority rule) and 2026-10 (senior-assessment routing surge) - at least two clearly, arguably three.
- Performance drift: 2026-06 (staffing loss -> queue ramp begins) and 2026-10 (seasonal load pushes p90 to record) with a recovery change point at 2026-09 and 2026-12.

### 3.5 Global versus mechanism-specific change points

The data do not support one global change point. Different artifacts break at different times: fitness/labels/resources at March; supplier/rework at May; queue/performance at June; scheduling policy at July; population/senior-assessment at October; partial reversals at September and December. A single change-point model fit to, say, median cycle time would misdate the "start" and conflate mechanisms. The correct model is multiple, artifact-specific change points, some sudden (March, May, July policy, October season) and some gradual (June-August queue ramp), with recurring/seasonal structure that must be compared year-over-year rather than treated as novel.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 global monthly | month | Overall trend / sanity baseline | >=100 cases/month (met: 605-790) | Trend, seasonality year-over-year | Aggregation hides mechanism-specific and within-class effects |
| L1 supplier | month x supplier(Z / non-Z) | Isolate supplier-driven rework | >=50 cases/cell/month | Z vs non-Z rework and cycle time | Z tied to regions/customers -> supplier confounds population |
| L2 complexity | month x complexity(high/standard) | Separate case-mix from behavior | >=50 cases/cell/month | Per-class routing, senior assessment, cycle time | Complexity partly correlates with supplier |
| L3 priority class | month x complexity x priority-served(before/after 07-01) | Detect delay redistribution | >=40 cases/cell; pool months if thin | Standard vs high waiting pre/post rule | Thin cells if further split; conditioning on served-order can induce collider bias |
| L4 provenance | month x label-source(migrated / native) x resource-present(y/n) | Quarantine logging/missingness artifacts | >=30 events/cell | Clean vs artifact-affected conformance | Over-splitting fragments support; missingness not random |
| L5 region/team (optional) | month x region x team | Localize supplier/staffing effects | >=40 cases/cell, else roll up | Regional supplier spread, team workload | Small cells; team labels degraded post-migration |

Primary monitoring population: L2 x supplier-aware, i.e. monthly cases segmented by complexity (high/standard) and supplier (Z / non-Z), on the mapped/normalized log with migrated-provenance cases flagged. This level is coarse enough to keep >=50 cases per cell (avoiding tiny-cell noise) yet fine enough to separate the three dominant mechanisms (supplier rework, seasonal complexity, staffing queueing) that the global aggregate confounds. Standard-complexity, non-Z cases are the cleanest stable reference sub-population for detecting genuinely new drift, because they are least affected by supplier and seasonal case-mix swings.

Thresholds are per-monitor and per-segment, not universal: M9/M4/M12 use year-over-year seasonal references (compare October to prior October, not to September) so recurrence is not flagged as new drift; performance monitors (M6-M8) use rolling clean-baseline control bands. Seasonality handling: maintain a seasonal reference calendar and compare like-with-like periods; treat a deviation as drift only when it exceeds the seasonal expectation. Multiple testing: with ~12 monitors x several segments, control the false-discovery rate (e.g. Benjamini-Hochberg) across simultaneous tests each period, require persistence (>=2 consecutive periods) before escalation, and use wider bands for low-support cells. An alert is escalated only when a monitor AND its diagnostic follow-up agree, reducing single-metric false positives.

def load_data():
    m = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(MONTHLY_CSV))
    d = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(DFG_CSV))
    return m, d

def validate_data(m, d):
    """1. Validate the aggregate tables before drawing inferences."""
    problems = []
    if m['cases'].min() <= 0:
        problems.append("Non-positive case counts.")
    for col in ['high_complexity_pct', 'supplier_z_pct', 'fast_variant_pct',
                'rework_variant_pct', 'missing_resource_pct']:
        if not m[col].between(0, 100).all():
            problems.append(f"{col} out of [0,100].")
    for col in ['raw_model_fitness', 'mapped_model_fitness']:
        if not m[col].between(0, 1).all():
            problems.append(f"{col} out of [0,1].")
    if not (m['p90_cycle_h'] >= m['median_cycle_h']).all():
        problems.append("p90 below median in some month.")
    # DFG rates should be non-negative; Reg_Elig pinned at 100 by construction.
    if not (d.drop(columns=['month']) >= 0).all().all():
        problems.append("Negative DFG rate.")
    return problems

def baseline_relative_changes(m, baseline_months=('2026-01', '2026-02')):
    """2. Baseline-relative changes vs mean of baseline months."""
    base = m[m['month'].isin(baseline_months)].mean(numeric_only=True)
    metrics = ['high_complexity_pct', 'supplier_z_pct', 'rework_variant_pct',
               'median_cycle_h', 'p90_cycle_h', 'mapped_model_fitness']
    out = m[['month']].copy()
    for col in metrics:
        out[col + '_abs_delta'] = (m[col] - base[col]).round(3)
        out[col + '_pct_vs_base'] = ((m[col] / base[col] - 1) * 100).round(1)
    return out

def dfg_l1_distance(d, ref_month='2026-01'):
    """3. Transparent L1 distance between monthly DFG fragments vs reference.
    Assumptions: fragment is incomplete (omitted edges ignored); rates are
    post-mapping so March logging drift is already removed; edges unweighted;
    Reg_Elig pinned at 100 contributes ~0 signal. Only RELATIVE distances
    are meaningful."""
    edges = [c for c in d.columns if c != 'month']
    ref = d.loc[d['month'] == ref_month, edges].iloc[0].values.astype(float)
    rows = []
    for _, r in d.iterrows():
        vec = r[edges].values.astype(float)
        rows.append({'month': r['month'],
                     'L1_distance': float(np.abs(vec - ref).sum()),
                     'top_moving_edge': edges[int(np.argmax(np.abs(vec - ref)))]})
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

def fitness_recovery(m):
    """4. Raw-to-mapped fitness recovery per month."""
    out = m[['month', 'raw_model_fitness', 'mapped_model_fitness']].copy()
    out['recovery_gap'] = (out['mapped_model_fitness'] - out['raw_model_fitness']).round(3)
    # High gap => logging/semantic artifact dominates the raw drop.
    out['logging_artifact_flag'] = out['recovery_gap'] > 0.15
    return out

def flag_change_points(m):
    """5. Simple, explainable change-point flags: month-over-month jumps
    exceeding a robust threshold (median + k*MAD of |diff|) per metric."""
    flags = {}
    for col in ['supplier_z_pct', 'rework_variant_pct', 'median_cycle_h',
                'p90_cycle_h', 'high_complexity_pct', 'raw_model_fitness',
                'missing_resource_pct']:
        diff = m[col].diff().abs()
        med = diff.median()
        mad = (diff - med).abs().median()
        thr = med + 3 * (mad if mad > 0 else 1e-9)
        cps = m.loc[diff > thr, 'month'].tolist()
        flags[col] = cps
    return flags

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest conclusion is that the March fitness collapse is a logging/semantic and provenance artifact, not behavioral drift (confidence ~90%). The evidence is close to decisive: raw fitness falls to 0.55 while mapped fitness holds at ~0.90, the label count rises 9->11 exactly at migration, duplicated `Open Case` and resource missingness step up simultaneously, and the normalized DFG barely moves (L1 ~3). I would treat March as "instrument change," not process change, and analyze everything post-March on the mapped log.

Also strong is that the May-August deterioration is real behavioral-plus-performance drift, driven by Supplier Z rework feeding a staffing-constrained queue (confidence ~75-80%). Supplier_z_pct triples and rework doubles precisely at May; the DFG distance in that period is dominated by the Request Info loop, not by labels; and p90 grows faster than the median while `Assess` service time is nearly flat, which is the signature of queueing rather than slower work. The June departures plausibly convert rising rework load into disproportionate waiting.

Plausible but not proven: the July priority rule redistributed delay from high- to standard-complexity cases. The mechanism is credible and dated, but the monthly aggregate cannot show it; I hold this at ~40-45% pending case-level per-class waiting.

Also plausible: the October-November worsening is primarily seasonal population drift. Complexity jumps to 41-45% and Senior Assessment routing surges (7->22 per 100), which is a case-mix signature distinct from the summer rework signature. Confidence ~70%, but I cannot cleanly separate season from residual supplier backlog and continued understaffing.

Mediation/interaction prevents clean attribution in two places: (a) summer, where reduced capacity multiplies the effect of supplier rework rather than adding to it; and (b) autumn/December, where seasonal reversion and the December interventions push metrics the same direction, so the intervention's independent effect is unidentifiable from these data.

I would revise the account if: case-level data showed rework rising equally in non-Z cases (weakening the supplier link); if standard-case waiting did not rise after July (killing the redistribution story); if a clean prior renewal season showed the same October complexity spike is anomalous rather than seasonal; or if the mapping table proved incomplete, meaning some "recovered" fitness masks a genuine control-flow change.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF raw fitness drops sharply but mapped fitness stays near baseline and the distinct-label count changes, THEN classify as logging/semantic drift, freeze behavioral alerts, and update the label-mapping table, BECAUSE a vocabulary/provenance change breaks token-based conformance without altering the underlying process behavior.

2. IF both raw AND mapped fitness fall together with no new labels, THEN escalate as candidate real control-flow drift and run alignments on deviating traces, BECAUSE a drop that survives normalization cannot be explained by relabeling and points to genuine behavioral change.

3. IF the Assess->RequestInfo rework rate rises and the increase is concentrated in Supplier Z cases beyond what complexity predicts, THEN trigger a supplier bundle-quality audit rather than an internal-process review, BECAUSE incomplete external document bundles, not internal behavior, are generating the rework loops.

4. IF p90 cycle time rises substantially faster than the median while recorded Assess service time is roughly flat, THEN treat it as a queueing/capacity problem and check active-assessor headcount versus WIP, BECAUSE stable service with a growing right tail is the signature of understaffed queue amplification, not slower work.

5. IF aggregate cycle time worsens after a priority-rule change, THEN split waiting-time distributions by complexity/priority class before alerting, BECAUSE a scheduling rule can redistribute delay between classes while leaving the aggregate ambiguous, and only per-class views reveal it.

6. IF high_complexity_pct and Senior Assessment routing rise during the known renewal window and match the prior-year seasonal profile, THEN suppress a "new drift" alert and plan capacity instead, BECAUSE recurring seasonal population shifts should be compared year-over-year, not against the immediately preceding months.

7. IF metrics improve during a period when both an intervention and a seasonal load decline coincide, THEN withhold attribution of the improvement to the intervention and require an interrupted-time-series comparison against a comparable prior post-peak period, BECAUSE two same-direction confounded causes cannot be separated from aggregate data without a seasonal counterfactual.
