## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 is not merely “Prepare_Payment resource  Release_Payment resource.” Its objective is to ensure that no single human can initiate/execute and authorize/release a payment without an independent, competent, timely challenge sufficient for the risk. The objectives/threat scenarios include:

| # | Objective / threat scenario | What FEP-1 is trying to prevent | AV-2 role | MG-4 role | RA-7 role | ED-1 role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Same-person prepare and release | One person initiates and authorizes an erroneous or fraudulent transfer | Cannot verify actor independence; may block specific data anomalies | Can prevent if truly independent and performed before release/settlement; otherwise detective/hybrid | Detects after release; corrective only if recall/stop still possible | Conditionally permits same-operator processing only if strict preconditions are met; does not eliminate the objective |
| 2 | Lack of independent challenge of payment substance | Releaser rubber-stamps invoice, bank details, receipt, and business reason | Checks duplicate, sanctions, bank ownership/change, limits, PO match; does not prove goods/services received or commercial reasonableness | Strong preventive/detective challenge if substantive and independent; weak if rubber-stamped or conflicted | May detect after payment; too late for settlement loss unless recall possible | Requires independent MG-4 before settlement; otherwise single-operator permission is invalid |
| 3 | Bank-account redirection or compromised vendor account | Payment to wrong or newly changed account | Strong only when operational, covered, and not overridden; checks recent bank change/ownership | Can review bank details if competent and independent | Can detect and recall if within practical window | Fails if bank change occurred in previous 7 days; requires no such change |
| 4 | Duplicate, limit, sanctions, or PO-match anomaly | Invalid payment passes because no second check | Preventive/detective for covered items; can warn or block | Secondary review if substantive | Detective after the fact | Requires full AV-2 pass without override |
| 5 | Collusion, shared accounts, conflicts of interest, repeated pairs | Nominal two actors are not genuinely independent | Does not detect collusion or relationship conflicts | Only mitigates if manager is independent and not conflicted | May detect patterns later; weak if late or sampled | Still relies on independent manager and audit; not collusion-resistant by itself |
| 6 | Emergency single-operator misuse | “Emergency” becomes convenience bypass | Must be fully operational and passed | Independent manager approval before settlement | 100% audit within one business day for emergency same-user payments | Permission with seven conditions; not a waiver of the control objective |
| 7 | Late detection / irrecoverable loss | Error or fraud found after funds are gone | No after-the-fact recovery role | Post-settlement approval is mostly detective | Corrective only if within recall window; otherwise detective/mitigating | Requires RA-7 within one business day; late audit weakens the permission |
| 8 | Evidence quality and override accountability | Control “exists” but cannot prove review, token validity, or independence | Logs warnings/overrides; unavailable or unsigned checklist is a control gap | Evidence of review, conflict clearance, and timing matters | Independent audit evidence and recall attempts matter | Token timing, AV status, manager timing, and audit timing must be evidenced |

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

Use a structured sufficiency test rather than a single “control occurred” check.

### Classification standard

| Classification | Sufficiency standard |
|---|---|
| **Fully equivalent** | Covers the material FEP threats with a genuinely independent, competent human authorization before irrevocable settlement, or a fully satisfied ED-1 permission; AV-2 covers applicable anomalies without override; RA-7 is timely enough for recall/containment where same-user/emergency processing occurred; evidence is complete; conflicts, shared accounts/workstations, and repeated pairs are resolved; residual risk is low and within tolerance. |
| **Partially equivalent / technically nonconforming but tolerable** | A nominal FEP exception or independence weakness exists, but compensating controls provide substantive independent review before irrevocable settlement or reliable containment within the recall window; amount/vendor/bank risk is limited; evidence gaps are identified and monitored; residual risk is low-to-medium. This is not the same as saying the nominal rule was met. |
| **Merely mitigating** | Controls may detect or reduce loss after release, but there is no effective independent authorization before settlement, or recoverability is uncertain; warnings are overridden, manager is conflicted/late, AV is unavailable, or audit is too late; residual risk remains medium-to-high. |
| **Ineffective** | The control package fails on timing, independence, coverage, overrideability, evidence, or recoverability in a way that leaves high/severe residual risk. The control may exist in name only. |

### Dimensions to test

1. **Timing**  
   - Before prepare, before release, after release but before settlement, after settlement, before/after recall window.  
   - Manager approval after release but before settlement is a hybrid: detective as to the release event, potentially preventive as to final settlement loss if a real hold/stop exists. It is not equivalent to a truly independent second releaser before release unless the system enforces hold and the manager substantively reviews.

2. **Independence**  
   - Distinct human actor, no shared service account, no reporting conflict, no shared workstation that undermines segregation, no repeated-pair concentration, no conflict of interest.  
   - Nominal distinct users are not sufficient if evidence suggests collusion, shared credentials, or repeated uncontrolled pairing.

3. **Coverage**  
   - AV-2 covers duplicate, sanctions, bank ownership/change, limits, basic PO match. It does not prove receipt, commercial reasonableness, collusion, or who controlled a compromised account.  
   - MG-4 must review invoice, bank details, and supporting receipt; otherwise it is weak.  
   - RA-7 is detective; it only becomes partly corrective when a defect can still be stopped, recalled, or recovered.

4. **Overrideability**  
   - Warnings overridden by the preparer/releaser, use of another manager’s credential, or unsigned manual checklists reduce assurance sharply.  
   - A manager override is acceptable only with documented, independent, case-specific justification.

5. **Recoverability**  
   - Internal control generally treats settlement as irrevocable. Bank recall within 24 hours is useful but uncertain; audit after the window is largely detective only.

6. **Evidence quality**  
   - Token activation before Prepare, AV pass/override logs, manager review evidence, bank-verification evidence, receipt confirmation, audit timing, and recall attempts must be reliable.  
   - “Control occurred” without evidence of timing and substance is weak.

7. **Collusion resistance**  
   - Shared workstations, repeated resource pairs, direct reporting conflicts, and common credentials are red flags. They require stronger evidence, rotation, independent audit, and sometimes forensic review.

“Same expected loss” is not the only standard because controls also provide authorization integrity, legal/regulatory conformance, deterrence, detection speed, evidence quality, recoverability, and protection against tail/collusion risk. A package can have low expected monetary loss yet still fail the control objective because independence, timing, or evidence is absent. Conversely, a technically nonconforming case can be tolerable if the risk is low and compensating controls are strong and evidenced.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

Collect and monitor the following as **alerts/evidence**, not automatic proof of misconduct:

1. **Same-user rate**
   - Metric: completed payments where Prepare_Payment resource equals Release_Payment resource, split by valid ED-1 vs non-ED-1.
   - Rule: any non-ED-1 same-user completed payment is an alert. Trend by user, manager, amount, vendor, bank change.

2. **Override rate**
   - Metric: AV-2 warning overrides per user, per credential, per manager, and per vendor.
   - Rule: preparer/releaser overriding own warning, use of another manager’s credential, repeated overrides, or overrides near limits are high-priority alerts.

3. **Late manager approval**
   - Metric: manager approval before release, after release but before settlement, after settlement.
   - Rule: after-settlement approval is a control failure alert. Before-settlement post-release approval is a hybrid and needs evidence of real hold/stop authority.

4. **Audit before recall window**
   - Metric: time from settlement to audit for all same-user/emergency payments; 100% required within one business day.
   - Rule: late audit is an alert; missed audit for emergency same-user is a control failure. Track recall attempts if defect found.

5. **Repeated resource pairs**
   - Metric: count and value of payments by each Prepare/Release pair; concentration over 30/90 days.
   - Rule: repeated pairs above threshold trigger independence review. Not proof of collusion; verify dedicated payment-cell design, rotation, and workstation separation.

6. **Shared workstations / shared credentials**
   - Metric: terminal/workstation mapping to users; shared accounts; credential-use logs.
   - Rule: same workstation for both steps or shared credentials is an independence alert. Requires forensic review if combined with fabricated receipts or high-risk payments.

7. **Bank-change proximity**
   - Metric: payments to vendors with bank changes within 7/30 days; AV warning and override status.
   - Rule: any bank change within 7 days plus same-user, override, or AV outage is high risk. Require independent bank-ownership verification.

8. **Control outages**
   - Metric: AV unavailable, manual checklist unsigned, validation service down, emergency token late/missing.
   - Rule: high-risk payments should be blocked or require independent pre-settlement manager and second verifier. Track manual checklists for signatures and evidence.

9. **ED-1 token timing**
   - Metric: token activation timestamp vs Prepare_Payment timestamp.
   - Rule: token must be active before Prepare. Late token is an alert; a claimed logging delay requires independent log/outage evidence.

10. **Evidence collection**
   - Preserve immutable logs for token activation, AV results, overrides, credential use, manager review, bank verification, receipts, recall attempts, workstation mapping, and org design/rotation approvals.  
   - Distinguish alert from proof: repeated patterns, shared workstations, and same-user cases justify investigation and monitoring; they do not by themselves prove fraud or collusion.

    return set()

**PM4Py version note:** The function `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, "Prepare_Payment", "Release_Payment")` is not guaranteed to return the same object type or the same polarity across PM4Py versions—some versions may return a filtered log of conforming cases, others may return violating cases or a list. The code therefore calls the required function, extracts case IDs defensively, and reconciles against a manual same-user completed-payment check. The final compliance conclusion must still be based on timing, independence, coverage, overrideability, recoverability, evidence quality, and case risk—not on the count of activities alone.
