1. System boundary and assumptions

**Boundary used:** an operational order-fulfillment boundary from `Receive order` through `Confirm delivery` / `Close`, plus `Cancel due to delay` as a loss path. It includes stock picking, Made-to-order production on `CNC_A`, `CNC_B`, `heat_treat`, and `inspection_cell`, packaging, outbound transport, rework production, rework packaging, partial shipment/backorder, emergency production, and cancellation.

**Inside the boundary:** process energy (`energy_kwh`), production scrap (`material_scrap_kg`), packaging mass (`package_material_kg`), outbound transport emissions (`transport_kgco2e`), batching/waiting behavior, rework loops, route/mode choice, and service outcomes such as cycle time, late delivery, and cancellation.

**Outside the boundary:** upstream supplier manufacturing and raw-material extraction except where represented by `material_scrap_kg`; inbound logistics not present in the log; product use phase; end-of-life; customer-side installation; facility HVAC/lighting/IT; employee commuting; capital goods; and returns not captured as rework loops. Packaging is included as material mass, not converted to a full lifecycle impact because no characterization factors are provided.

**Key assumptions:** the log is flattened by order, so line-item heterogeneity within an order is hidden; `transport_kgco2e` is an allocated per-order value, but shared modes such as `consolidated_truck` and empty-return legs of `dedicated_van` may be imperfectly allocated; `energy_kwh` is treated as machine/process energy rather than whole-site energy; `promised_lead_time_hours` is contractual; no cost, churn, or customer-satisfaction data are available; service is proxied by cycle time, late delivery, and cancellation; quality is proxied by rework, packaging rework, Q1 conformance, and damage/return loops; resilience is proxied by route alternatives, expedite flags, emergency production, and policy conformance rather than a full disruption dataset.

**Remaining uncertainty:** the discovered model has low precision because emergency paths and partial shipments combine in many ways; obsolescence is only proxied by “batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days”; rebound effects such as express recovery after a ground shift are not directly observed and must be monitored after implementation.

3. Intervention options

**S1. Green shipping recommender using promised lead time, route reliability, and customer segment.**  
- **Target path/segment:** `Package standard/reinforced -> Ship air` and any case where `route_mode` is higher-impact than necessary; especially Standard-stock Service-contract, where SP1 is violated in 22% of cases.  
- **Mechanism:** recommend the lowest-emission `route_mode` that still meets `promised_lead_time_hours` with probability above 85%, using route reliability, consolidation schedules, and customer segment. Air and dedicated van remain available when slack is insufficient.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** transport emissions fall where air is avoided; cycle time may rise toward ground/consolidated times but should remain within promise; late-delivery risk is controlled by the 85% feasibility rule; resilience improves because express capacity is preserved for true exceptions.  
- **Monitoring evidence:** SP1 conformance, predicted vs actual on-time probability, `route_mode` mix, `expedite_flag`, `transport_kgco2e/order`, late delivery by segment, override reasons.

**S2. Dynamic batching for CNC and heat treatment with maximum waiting-time caps by segment.**  
- **Target path/segment:** `Plan production -> Batch wait -> CNC production` and heat-treatment batching, mainly Made-to-order Regular and Service-contract.  
- **Mechanism:** form larger batches to capture CNC_A energy reduction from 2.8 kWh/item at batch 1–3 to 1.3 kWh/item at batch 12–20, and heat-treatment efficiency above batch size 10, but cap waiting by promised lead time and customer segment.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** energy per item falls; scrap may fall with more stable batches; however batch wait rises from 4h to 38h across the observed batch-size range, heat-treatment waiting carries 1.7× higher late-delivery risk, and large batches increase obsolescence when forecasts are wrong. Resilience can fall if capacity is locked into large batches.  
- **Monitoring evidence:** `batch_size` distribution, batch-wait median/p90, cap-breach rate, `energy_kwh/item`, late delivery, heat-treatment wait risk, obsolescence proxy, forecast error.

**S3. Shift Made-to-order production from CNC_B to CNC_A when queue and due-date slack allow.**  
- **Target path/segment:** CNC_B MTO cases, especially `MTO -> batch wait -> CNC_B -> inspection -> rework -> ground`, which has 32.0 kWh energy, 1.8 kg scrap, and 32% late delivery.  
- **Mechanism:** prefer CNC_A where rework is lower, 9% vs 18% for CNC_B, and energy per item is lower at batch size 12; keep CNC_B as overflow for low-slack cases.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** energy, scrap, and rework fall; quality improves; cycle time may increase if CNC_A queue is long; late risk rises if slack is misjudged; resilience is maintained if CNC_B remains available as overflow.  
- **Monitoring evidence:** `machine_group`, queue length, due-date slack, `rework_flag`, rework loops, `energy_kwh`, `material_scrap_kg`, late delivery, CNC_B utilization.

**S4. Replace air for Standard-stock Service-contract orders with priority pick + ground unless slack is insufficient.**  
- **Target path/segment:** Standard-stock Service-contract, 16% of orders, often shipped by air even when promised lead time still allows ground plus priority picking; `Stock -> standard pack -> air` averages 18.5 kgCO2e vs 3.6 for ground.  
- **Mechanism:** add priority picking/packing so ground or consolidated truck can meet the promise; use air only when the SP1 feasibility test fails or a logged exception applies.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** transport emissions fall substantially; median cycle time may move from the air-path 17h toward the ground-path 34h but should remain within promise; late risk is controlled by priority picking and slack checks; resilience improves by reducing dependence on air and preserving it for low-slack cases.  
- **Monitoring evidence:** SP1 conformance, `promised_lead_time_hours` vs actual, priority-pick cycle time, air share, late delivery, `expedite_flag`, customer complaints/returns proxy.

**S5. Consolidate truck departures twice daily for non-urgent stock orders.**  
- **Target path/segment:** `Package standard -> Ship ground` for non-urgent Standard-stock Regular and Service-contract orders with sufficient slack; current `consolidated_truck` volume is only 850.  
- **Mechanism:** shift eligible individual ground parcels to consolidated truck departures, accepting a 12–24h consolidation wait only where promised lead time remains achievable.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** transport emissions fall by about 35–55% versus individual ground parcels; cycle time increases by the consolidation wait; late risk rises if applied to tight promises; resilience can be weakened if consolidation becomes a single dependency, so parcel ground or another alternative must remain available under R1.  
- **Monitoring evidence:** consolidation wait, missed departures, `route_mode` mix, `transport_kgco2e/order`, late delivery by promise slack, R1 alternative availability.

**S6. Improve packaging-route compatibility checks before shipment.**  
- **Target path/segment:** `Package standard/reinforced -> Rework packaging` and `Rework packaging -> Package reinforced/standard` loops; also Q1 violations for MTO parts over 8 kg.  
- **Mechanism:** add a pre-shipment compatibility check using part geometry, weight, `route_mode`, and customer handling requirements; enforce reinforced packaging for MTO parts >8 kg unless inspection certifies low damage risk.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** package material may increase where reinforced packaging is correctly applied, because it doubles package material, but rework packaging, damage-related returns, scrap, and reshipment emissions fall; reinforced packaging reduces damage-related returns by 40% for heavy MTO parts. Service and quality improve through fewer packaging loops.  
- **Monitoring evidence:** rework-packaging count, Q1 conformance, `package_material_kg`, damage/return proxy, `rework_flag`, compatibility-error rate, route-specific return loops.

**S7. Add inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations.**  
- **Target path/segment:** `Inspection -> Rework production` (470 cases), `Rework production -> CNC production` (390 cases), `inspection_cell` queues, and CNC_B-heavy MTO paths.  
- **Mechanism:** add in-process or pre-packaging inspection gates for high-risk combinations, especially CNC_B output and heavy MTO parts, to catch defects before heat treatment, packaging, or customer use.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** rework, scrap, and rework-related energy fall; the CNC_B rework path shows why this matters: 32.0 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap, 32% late delivery. Inspection queue time may rise, but total cycle time can fall by avoiding downstream loops. Quality improves; resilience is broadly neutral if inspection capacity is managed.  
- **Monitoring evidence:** inspection queue, first-pass yield, `rework_flag`, rework-loop count, late delivery, `energy_kwh` and `material_scrap_kg` per good item, Q1 conformance.

**S8. Replace some dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes.**  
- **Target path/segment:** `Package reinforced -> Dedicated van` (620 cases) and `Critical -> emergency production -> reinforced pack -> dedicated van` (8% of orders, 52.0 kgCO2e/order); SP2 justification is missing in 31% of dedicated-van cases.  
- **Mechanism:** reserve dedicated vans for true Critical-downtime cases with explicit SP2 justification; use shared emergency courier lanes for precautionary urgent cases; reduce empty-return legs.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** transport emissions fall for cases that do not require a dedicated van; however service risk rises if shared-lane capacity is constrained or if true downtime cases are misrouted. Resilience improves only if true Critical-downtime service is protected and alternatives remain available.  
- **Monitoring evidence:** SP2 justification completeness, triage accuracy, dedicated-van utilization and empty-return proxy, `transport_kgco2e`, Critical-downtime late delivery, R1 alternative availability, `expedite_flag`.

**S9. Split Critical-downtime into true downtime and precautionary urgent categories.**  
- **Target path/segment:** Critical-downtime Service-contract, 11% of orders, averaging 38.0 kgCO2e/order; `Availability check -> Emergency production` (760 cases).  
- **Mechanism:** triage at availability check/emergency production into true downtime versus precautionary urgent. True downtime receives emergency production and protected express transport; precautionary urgent receives expedited ground, consolidated truck, stock substitution, or monitored waiting.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** transport and energy fall for precautionary cases; true downtime service is protected; the main risk is misclassification, which could create unacceptable downtime. Resilience improves by preventing overuse of emergency capacity.  
- **Monitoring evidence:** triage category, reclassification rate, late delivery and cancellation by subcategory, `transport_kgco2e`, `energy_kwh`, emergency-production share, SP2 conformance.

**S10. Introduce carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular made-to-order customers.**  
- **Target path/segment:** Made-to-order Regular, 22% of orders, with 94h median, 180h p90, 18% late delivery, 18.5 kWh, and 0.90 kg scrap per order.  
- **Mechanism:** offer longer or flexible promised lead times in exchange for batching, CNC_A allocation, ground/consolidated transport, and lower rework. This creates the slack needed by S2, S3, and S5.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** energy, transport, and scrap can fall through better batching and mode choice; promised cycle time may lengthen, but only with customer consent; late-delivery risk may fall if promises become more realistic. The main risk is customer behavior: if Regular customers perceive worse service, they may shift to expedite or service-contract behavior.  
- **Monitoring evidence:** offer acceptance rate, `promised_lead_time_hours` vs actual, batch size/wait, `energy_kwh/item`, late delivery, cancellation, segment mix, `expedite_flag`, churn proxy.

**S11. Reduce partial shipments by changing availability-check rules.**  
- **Target path/segment:** `Pick from stock -> Partial shipment` (1010 cases), `Partial shipment -> Backorder wait` (220), and the stock partial-shipment path with 79h median, 24% late delivery, 7.2 kgCO2e, and a second shipment.  
- **Mechanism:** revise availability-check ATP/CTP rules to avoid partial shipment when the full order can be promised within an acceptable window; require customer consent for splits; consolidate backorders where possible.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** transport falls by avoiding second shipments and reducing fragmented customer contact; however, some customers may wait longer for the full order, and overly strict full-order rules can increase `Backorder wait -> Cancel due to delay` and total cancellations. Quality is broadly neutral.  
- **Monitoring evidence:** partial-shipment rate, backorder wait, cancellation due to delay, late delivery, `transport_kgco2e/order`, customer consent/return proxy, stock availability.

**S12. Create a sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides.**  
- **Target path/segment:** all overrides of SP1, SP2, Q1, and R1, including air shipments, dedicated vans, packaging deviations, and emergency paths.  
- **Mechanism:** require reason codes, evidence, approver, and expiry for every override; feed the log into recommenders, audits, and policy updates.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** indirect but essential; prevents unjustified air, dedicated-van, and packaging choices while preserving legitimate service exceptions; improves traceability and reduces greenwashing.  
- **Monitoring evidence:** conformance rates for SP1/SP2/Q1, override reasons, outcomes of overridden cases, audit closure time, R1 exceptions during disruptions.

5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching rebound into waiting, lateness, and obsolescence.** Larger CNC and heat-treatment batches reduce energy per item, but CNC_A batch wait rises from 4h to 38h across the observed batch-size range, heat-treatment waiting carries 1.7× higher late-delivery risk, and 6% of batch-produced items are not shipped within 14 days when forecasts are wrong. The energy saving can be offset by scrap, obsolescence, and later expediting. Mitigation: segment-specific maximum wait caps, forecast-error monitoring, and an obsolescence KPI.

2. **Transport mode-shift displacement into recovery shipments.** Moving air to ground or consolidated truck cuts emissions, but ground can add about 24h and consolidation adds 12–24h. If promised-lead-time slack is misestimated, late delivery can trigger express recovery, emergency production, or dedicated-van use, displacing emissions back into the system and harming service. Mitigation: enforce SP1’s >85% feasibility rule, use priority picking, and monitor `expedite_flag` after mode shifts.

3. **Consolidation dependency and resilience loss.** Consolidated trucks reduce transport kgCO2e by about 35–55%, but fixed departures create a potential single point of failure. A carrier disruption could strand Service-contract orders and violate R1 if no parcel ground or van alternative remains available. Mitigation: keep at least one alternative route for Service-contract customers, monitor missed departures, and define fallback routes before shifting volume.

4. **Packaging material versus damage-return trade-off.** Reinforced packaging doubles package material but reduces damage-related returns by 40% for heavy MTO parts. Applying it indiscriminately increases packaging waste; omitting it where Q1 requires it increases returns, rework packaging, and reshipment emissions. Mitigation: packaging-route compatibility checks, Q1 enforcement, inspection certification for low-risk parts, and joint monitoring of `package_material_kg` and return/rework loops.

5. **Inspection and rework displacement.** Adding inspection gates reduces rework loops, but `inspection_cell` queues already contribute to rushed inspections and escaped defects. If inspection becomes a bottleneck, work may be expedited later onto CNC_B or air routes, reintroducing high rework, scrap, and transport emissions. Mitigation: target gates to high-risk machine/part combinations, monitor inspection queue and first-pass yield, and protect CNC_A/CNC_B overflow rules.

6. **Obsolescence from forecast-driven batching.** Producing large energy-efficient batches can create finished items that are not shipped within 14 days when demand forecasts are wrong. The environmental burden shifts from process energy to inventory obsolescence, scrap, or write-offs. Mitigation: cap batch size by demand confidence, use S10 promise negotiation to align production with accepted lead times, and track the 14-day shipment proxy.

7. **Customer-behavior rebound from promise-date negotiation.** Carbon-aware longer promises for Regular MTO customers may be perceived as service deterioration. Customers may shift to Service-contract terms, request expedites, or move to competitors, increasing `expedite_flag`, air, and dedicated-van use. Mitigation: consent-based negotiation, segment guardrails, acceptance-rate monitoring, and protection of Service-contract service levels.

8. **Partial-shipment reduction increasing cancellations.** Reducing partial shipments lowers second-shipment emissions, but stricter full-order availability rules can increase backorder wait and `Cancel due to delay`. The log already shows `Backorder wait -> Cancel due to delay` and 410 cancellations due to delay. Lost orders may later require emergency replenishment. Mitigation: ATP/CTP feasibility checks, customer consent for splits, and cancellation monitoring.

9. **Critical-downtime triage and shared-lane risk.** Splitting Critical-downtime and using shared emergency lanes reduces emissions, but misclassification or shared-capacity constraints can cause unacceptable downtime. Dedicated vans often return empty, but they protect true Critical-downtime service. Mitigation: strict triage, SP2 justification, dedicated van for true downtime, R1 alternatives, and reclassification monitoring.

6. Balanced recommendation

**Recommended package: a guardrailed, segment-and-slack redesign rather than a blanket modal ban.**

The package has four linked parts:

1. **Route policy with explicit slack rules — S1, S4, S5, S12.** Deploy the green shipping recommender and exception log. Shift Standard-stock Service-contract air shipments to priority pick plus ground or consolidated truck only when SP1 feasibility is above 85%. Use twice-daily consolidated truck for non-urgent stock orders with sufficient slack. Do not ban air or dedicated vans; reserve them for insufficient slack, true Critical-downtime, and logged exceptions. This targets the largest transport emitters — air at 4–6× ground and the dedicated-van path at 52.0 kgCO2e/order — while protecting Service-contract resilience through R1.

2. **Critical-downtime triage and protected emergency capacity — S9, S8, S12.** Split Critical-downtime into true downtime and precautionary urgent. True downtime keeps emergency production and dedicated van/air with SP2 justification. Precautionary urgent cases use shared emergency lanes, expedited ground, or consolidated truck. This reduces the 38.0 kgCO2e/order Critical-downtime average without creating unacceptable downtime risk.

3. **Slack-creating production and packaging redesign — S2, S3, S6, S7, S10, S11.** Use carbon-aware promise negotiation for Regular MTO customers to create slack. Apply dynamic CNC and heat-treatment batching with maximum wait caps by segment. Prefer CNC_A when queue and due-date slack allow, while keeping CNC_B as overflow. Add packaging-route compatibility checks and Q1 enforcement. Add inspection gates for high-rework machine/part combinations. Reduce partial shipments only where full-order promise feasibility and customer consent are satisfied. This reduces energy, scrap, rework, and second shipments while explicitly managing waiting, obsolescence, cancellation, and return risks.

4. **Governance and validation — S12 plus the monitoring plan below.** Every override of SP1, SP2, Q1, or R1 requires a reason code, evidence, approver, and expiry. The exception log feeds the recommender, batching caps, and audit process.

**Sequencing:** Phase 0 instruments the log and establishes baselines. Phase 1 implements low-risk route and packaging changes for stock segments: S1, S4, S5, S6, S12. Phase 2 implements MTO Regular promise negotiation and capped batching: S10, S2, S3. Phase 3 adds quality and partial-shipment rules: S7, S11. Phase 4 implements Critical-downtime triage and shared emergency lanes with strict service guardrails: S9, S8.

**Why this balances the criteria:** it reduces transport where emissions are highest and slack exists, but does not remove express capability needed for true Critical-downtime or low-slack Service-contract cases. It reduces production energy and scrap through batching and CNC_A allocation, but caps waiting to avoid the observed 4h38h batch-wait deterioration, heat-treatment late risk, and 6% obsolescence proxy. It improves quality and packaging, accepting higher package material where reinforced packaging prevents larger return/rework burdens. It reduces partial shipments without ignoring the cancellation risk in the DFG. Finally, exception governance makes the trade-offs explicit and auditable, which is essential because the discovered model has low precision around emergency and partial-shipment combinations.

7. Validation and governance plan

**Extend the event log after implementation.** Add intervention tags and outcomes: `route_recommendation_id`, `predicted_on_time_probability`, `slack_hours`, chosen `route_mode`, `override_reason_code`, approver, expiry; `batch_id`, `batch_size`, `cap_breach_flag`, `machine_group`, `inspection_gate_result`, first-pass yield; `package_check_result`, Q1 certificate ID; `triage_category` for true downtime versus precautionary urgent; `dedicated_van_justification_event`; `promised_lead_time_hours`, actual delivery timestamp, on-time flag, cancellation reason; `expedite_flag`, `emergency_production_flag`, `partial_shipment_flag`, backorder wait; and disruption events with `alternative_route_available`.

**Rebuild the process evidence.** Reconstruct the DFG and variant tables before and after implementation. Stratify by `case_type` × `customer_type` × `route_mode` × `machine_group` × `batch_size`. Compare median/p90 cycle time, late delivery, cancellation, energy, transport, scrap, packaging mass, and rework. Use matched control segments or a phased pilot with interrupted time-series analysis. Because model precision is low around emergency and partial-shipment combinations, validate with conformance checks and human review of exception paths rather than relying only on automated discovery.

**Conformance monitoring.** Track SP1: share of cases where ground or consolidated truck was feasible with predicted on-time probability above 85% but air or another higher-impact mode was used; baseline finding is 22% violation in Standard-stock Service-contract. Track SP2: share of dedicated-van cases with a justification event; baseline finding is 31% missing. Track Q1: share of MTO parts over 8 kg using reinforced packaging or holding an inspection low-risk certificate. Track R1: for Service-contract customers, whether at least one alternative route remains available during carrier disruption. Alert on any deterioration in these conformance rates.

**Segment-level service guardrails.** Monitor median and p90 cycle time, late delivery, cancellation due to delay, backorder wait, and partial-shipment rate by segment. Set guardrail thresholds with operations before rollout. Critical-downtime late delivery and p90 must be protected. Service-contract p90 and late delivery must not deteriorate beyond agreed tolerance. Regular MTO promised lead time may change only with recorded customer consent. Roll back or adjust caps if any segment breaches its guardrail.

**Environmental KPIs.** Monitor `energy_kwh/order` and per produced item by machine and batch size; `transport_kgco2e/order` by route mode and per on-time order; `material_scrap_kg/order`; `package_material_kg/order`; rework loops; obsolescence proxy; and a composite impact per conforming on-time order. This composite prevents improvements that simply shift burden into late, cancelled, returned, or reworked orders.

**Rebound detection.** After air-to-ground or consolidation shifts, monitor `expedite_flag`, recovery air shipments, and late-delivery spikes. After batching changes, monitor cap breaches, heat-treatment wait, late delivery, and items not shipped within 14 days. After inspection gates, monitor inspection queue, first-pass yield, and downstream rework loops. After partial-shipment reduction, monitor backorder wait and cancellation due to delay. After promise negotiation, monitor acceptance rate, segment mix, expedite behavior, and customer complaints proxy. After Critical-downtime triage, monitor reclassification, shared-lane capacity, dedicated-van empty returns, and true-downtime service.

**Governance.** Maintain the sustainability exception log with reason codes, evidence, approver, and expiry. Review overrides monthly in a cross-functional board including operations, quality, logistics, and sustainability.
