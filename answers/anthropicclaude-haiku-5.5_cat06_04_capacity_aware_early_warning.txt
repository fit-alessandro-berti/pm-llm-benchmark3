## 1. Decision rule

**Eligibility gates.** A case may receive a slot only if all of the following hold:

- **G1 Stage:** the case is in Triage or Diagnosis Started. Diagnosis Complete or later is ineligible.
- **G2 Time:** at least 1.0 h remains to the SLA deadline. This covers the 45-minute swarm plus handoff.
- **G3 Repeat use:** no swarm has been used on this case, or a material new event has occurred since the last swarm.
- **G4 Evidence change:** either the smoothed probability has moved, |p_s|  0.10 over the last two refreshes (one hour), or a verified material event has occurred. A material event is a new customer artefact, a stage transition, a validated correction to an input feature, or a severity change. A model refresh alone is not an event.
- **G5 Data validity:** no key feature is imputed or flagged as failed at decision time.
- **G6 Reserve:** slot 4 is held for the possible 15:00 regulatory incident unless that incident is cleared or the candidate's robust score exceeds €3,000.

**Probability adjustment.** Treat the model output as observational and correct it before use:

- SME: p_adj = p_now + 0.065 (band +0.04 to +0.09). The model underpredicts here.
- Strategic Enterprise: p_adj = p_now  0.085 (band 0.05 to 0.12). The model overpredicts after the workflow redesign.
- Public Sector: p_adj = p_now ± 0.05, a moderate-sample band.
- Non-Native-Language Support: no point correction, but a wide band of ±0.15. Use the lower bound for gating.

Gating requires p_adj_central  0.50 and p_adj_lower  0.35.

**Intervention effect used for valuation.** Do not use the supplied range as a causal effect.

- Use the 25th percentile of the stated range for complete-data, calibrated segments.
- Use the 10th percentile for NNLS cases and for cases with imputed inputs.
- Cap any absolute risk reduction at 0.5 × p_adj_central. A swarm halving the breach risk is implausible without evidence.

**Robust priority score (per slot, €).**

S = ARR_use × V  €450  B  S_spill

- V is the value of avoiding breach.
- B is the full false-alarm burden. I charge it in full as a conservative upper bound.
- S_spill = 6 × 0.02 × €15,000  €1,800 per slot at base congestion. The €15,000 average value of untreated diagnosis-stage cases is my assumption.

A candidate is eligible for a slot only if S > 0 at the robust bound.

**Persistence and prioritisation.** Smooth probabilities over two refreshes. Suppress repeat alerts that carry no new evidence (Section 4). Rank eligible cases by S, with time-criticality as a tie-breaker. Within a tie, prefer the case with the earlier deadline.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**Cool-down.** After a swarm, no new swarm on that case for a minimum of 2 hours. Re-entry requires a material event (Section 1, G4), not a refresh.

**Suppression.** After any alert that does not lead to a swarm, suppress further alerts on that case for 60 minutes, unless |p_s|  0.10 or a material event occurs. A third alert with no event is closed to the swarm queue and routed to owner review.

**Escalation.** If a case stays eligible for two or more consecutive refreshes with p_adj rising, escalate to the queue lead for a non-swarm diagnostic review (30 minutes or less). Escalation does not consume a senior slot. A case reaching Diagnosis Complete exits the swarm queue and goes to expedited resolution.

**Re-entry.** A suppressed or cooled-down case returns to eligibility only when:

1. the stage is still pre-Diagnosis Complete,
2. at least one material event has occurred, and
3. its robust score is positive at current congestion.

**I-05 versus I-07.**

- **I-05** is a third alert with no new event and p of +0.02. The refresh has added no information, so suppression applies. Its underlying concern may be real, because the SME correction raises true risk toward 0.60–0.66. That justifies an owner review of the known blocker, not a swarm. The case also fails the economic test even if eligible, because a €7k value does not cover the spillover. Confirm that no swarm has already been used, since that would make it ineligible outright.
- **I-07** is a third alert, but it carries a new customer log, which is a material event. The risk estimate fell slightly, which is noise-sized. The log can change the picture in either direction, so the case is reopened for decision. The right response is a 15-minute targeted log review by a non-senior reviewer. A 45-minute senior swarm is justified only if the log reveals a specific blocker that a swarm uniquely resolves. At €5k value, it does not clear the spillover hurdle even then.

The contrast is deliberate. Repeated alerts with no new evidence are suppressed. An alert with a changed evidence base is re-decided. Neither automatically earns a swarm.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Value-based starvation.** Ranking by value systematically under-serves SME and low-value Public Sector cases. Currently I-01, I-05, I-07 and I-10 are all low-value and all deferred or suppressed.

Safeguards:
- Reserve a floor of one slot in every four for cases with V < €10k and p_adj_central  0.5. This is a fairness quota, not a value judgement.
- Add an ageing bonus: a case with p_adj  0.5 for two consecutive hours without a swarm gains priority.
- Protect contractual obligations by setting a separate minimum service rule for regulatory or public-sector contracts whose penalties are not captured by V.

**NNLS uncertainty.** The validation sample is small, and both calibration and effects are unknown. Treating the midpoint as truth would over-allocate or under-allocate systematically.

Safeguards:
- Use the 10th-percentile effect and ±0.15 calibration band for NNLS.
- Require a non-swarm language-support check before any NNLS swarm where it is feasible.
- Cap NNLS slot share at one slot per cycle until at least 30 treated and untreated NNLS cases are observed.
- Test a randomised deferral among NNLS cases that are otherwise eligible, subject to ethics and contract review, to estimate the effect without relying on the observational model.

**Differential calibration.** Uncorrected, SME cases are under-ranked (true risk is higher), and Strategic cases are over-ranked. Allocation then drifts toward Strategic cases and away from SME cases, even though the model's apparent ranking looks fair.

Safeguard: apply the segment offsets in Section 1, and audit ranking quality within each segment, not just across the pooled portfolio. Require the offsets to be re-estimated monthly.

**Selective intervention changing training labels.** Treated cases have lower observed breach rates. A model retrained on these labels learns that swarm-tagged features are protective, and it may learn to prefer proxies for the swarm. Predictions then drift away from the causal risk the policy is meant to track.

Safeguards:
- Log every intervention and its time as a feature, and never train on post-intervention outcomes without it.
- Maintain an untreated but eligible holdout, so that counterfactual outcomes remain observable.
- Use inverse-propensity-weighted or doubly robust estimators for outcome models.
- Keep the observational risk model separate from the effect estimator.

**Spillover falling on untreated groups.** Congestion harm is borne by untreated diagnosis-stage cases. These are likely to be lower-priority cases, and they may disproportionately come from SME or public segments. A swarm for a Strategic case can therefore raise breach risk for SME cases in the queue.

Safeguards:
- Maintain a spillover ledger. Each swarm charges its expected spillover to untreated diagnosis-stage cases, by segment.
- Cap daily expected spillover per segment at a fixed budget, for example €1,800 per segment per day.
- If an untreated case crosses p_adj  0.5 because of congestion, grant it an automatic priority restoration.
- Review the spillover distribution by segment weekly. If any segment bears more than 1.5× its share of queue exposure, revise the allocation.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational monitoring.**
- Slot utilisation, reserve preservation, and time from alert to swarm start.
- Queue delay minutes per slot, compared with the 8–20 minute assumption.
- Time from alert to Diagnosis Complete for treated and untreated cases.
- Count of suppressed alerts, escalations and re-entries.

**Predictive monitoring.**
- Reliability curves and calibration error by segment, updated daily.
- Brier score and calibration-in-the-large by segment, with a two-week rolling window.
- Noise level of p on refreshes, to check whether the 0.10 evidence-change threshold is set correctly.
- Feature-missingness rates, especially the live queue feature, with an alert when imputation exceeds 2%.

**Causal-effect monitoring.** The observational prediction is not the effect under intervention, so estimate the effect separately.

- Run a randomised tie-breaker among eligible cases with near-equal robust scores. This gives unbiased local effects.
- Use a regression-discontinuity design around the priority cutoff as a complementary estimate.
- Use matched controls as a sensitivity check, not as the primary estimate.
- Estimate effects by segment and stage, and shrink segment estimates toward the pooled estimate using a Bayesian hierarchical model. Shrinkage is strongest for NNLS.
- Pre-register the effect metric: breach indicator within the SLA, with a 14-day outcome window.

**Capacity monitoring.**
- Realised spillover per slot, compared with the 0.01–0.03 band. Trigger review if the mean exceeds 0.025 for a week.
- Delay minutes per slot, compared with the 8–20 minute band. Trigger review above 25 minutes.
- Senior specialist time lost to swarms, as a share of diagnosis capacity.

**Fairness monitoring.**
- Slot share by segment, compared with the share of eligible cases with p_adj  0.5.
- Spillover harm by segment.
- False-alarm burden by segment.
- Ageing: maximum time a case has spent above threshold without a swarm, by segment.
- A disparate-impact trigger: if any segment's slot share falls below half its share of eligible high-risk cases for two consecutive weeks, revise the allocation rule.

**Policy-induced drift.** Compare the covariate distribution of treated and untreated cases over time. Track label mix changes, because selective intervention shifts it. Refit the calibration offsets when the treated-untreated outcome gap changes by more than 0.05 for two weeks.

**When to change the threshold or allocation rule.**

- **Recalibrate the segment offsets** when calibration error in any segment exceeds 0.05 for two consecutive weeks.
- **Revise the ARR assumption** when the posterior mean effect for a segment moves by more than 0.05, or when the 25th-to-75th-percentile range no longer covers the realised effect.
- **Revise the spillover assumption** when realised spillover per slot is outside 0.01–0.03 for a week, or the average value of untreated cases changes by more than 20%.
- **Tighten or relax the reserve** when regulatory incident frequency changes materially.
- **Move NNLS out of the calibration gap** only when at least 30 treated and 30 untreated outcomes are available, and the effect interval is narrower than ±0.10.
- **Retire the policy** if three consecutive weekly reviews show adverse-scenario net impact (Section 3) with no compensating improvement.
