# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

## Demand and service assumptions

**Observation:** only expected arrivals, ranges, tail probabilities, and shifts are supplied. Arrival variability, correlation between priority/type/tail, overnight staffing, and actual handover effort are not supplied.

The following assumptions are therefore used for planning calculations and the reproducible simulation:

1. **Service-time distribution assumption.** Within each ordinary or complex-tail range, service time is uniformly distributed. The complex-tail mixture is selected independently for each activity.
2. **Multiplier assumption.** A resource multiplier applies to the drawn active service time, not to queueing, handover, coordination, or break time.
3. **Arrival assumption.** Arrivals are Poisson around the stated expected count and uniformly distributed within each two-hour block. This is an assumption for uncertainty estimation, not an observation.
4. **Priority/type independence assumption.** Priority, case type, and complex-tail selection are independent unless altered by a supplied scenario.
5. **U1 priority adjustment assumption.** The extra eight percentage points of P1 share are removed proportionally from P2 and P3 so that priority shares still sum to 100%.
6. **Shift assumption.** A diagnostic or front/back-office activity is not deliberately started if it cannot fit into the current uninterrupted working interval. Breaks and unavailable periods therefore create waiting, not unrecorded service.
7. **Overnight assumption for SLA estimates.** The simulation repeats the stated daily shifts on the following day so that cases can eventually complete. This is necessary to evaluate P3. If no next-day work exists, P2 performance for late-day arrivals cannot be inferred and will be materially worse.
8. **Handover interpretation.** The seven-minute handover and five-minute cross-team transfer are case delays. The simulation conservatively treats them as reserving the receiving resource; this may overstate resource consumption if handovers are asynchronous.
9. **Preemption interpretation.** Only lower-priority diagnostic work is preempted. A preempted case receives one restart penalty at most, equal to 10% of the remaining active service work. Breaks are not counted as priority preemptions.

Using the uniform-mixture assumption, expected unmultiplied active service times are:

| Activity | Calculation | Expected minutes |
|---|---:|---:|
| Intake | \(0.92(6.5)+0.08(12)\) | 6.94 |
| Mechanical | \(0.85(23)+0.15(45)\) | 26.30 |
| Electrical | \(0.82(28.5)+0.18(52.5)\) | 32.82 |
| Software | \(0.80(32.5)+0.20(62.5)\) | 38.50 |
| Validation | \(0.88(14)+0.12(28.5)\) | 15.74 |

For U4, diagnostic tail probabilities double, producing expected diagnostic active times of approximately 29.60 minutes for Mechanical, 37.14 for Electrical, and 44.50 for Software. Intake and Validation tail probabilities do not change in U4.

## Parallel versus sequential branch rule

For a multi-skill case, parallelism is useful only when its critical-path saving exceeds congestion, handover, and coordination costs.

Ignoring queues and transfers:

- ME sequential diagnostic mean: \(26.3 + 32.82 = 59.12\) minutes.
- ME parallel rough critical path: \(\max(26.3,32.82)+8 \approx 40.82\) minutes.
- ES sequential diagnostic mean: \(32.82 + 38.5 = 71.32\) minutes.
- ES parallel rough critical path: \(\max(32.82,38.5)+8 \approx 46.5\) minutes.

Thus, parallelism is attractive for P1 cases if two suitable resources are available. It is less attractive when it fragments scarce Electrical/Software capacity, creates Validation bursts, or would require slow overflow staff. Sequential execution is preferred for lower-priority ME/ES work when one cross-skilled resource can retain the case and queue pressure is high.

## Decision variables

The policy controls:

- resource assignment by activity;
- queue ordering and priority score;
- overflow eligibility;
- whether an ME/ES case is parallel or sequential;
- whether P1 can preempt lower-priority diagnostic work;
- which lower-priority task is preempted;
- whether a resource is protected from lower-priority Validation work;
- when controlled pooling is activated;
- how P3 aging is incorporated to prevent indefinite starvation.

## Objective formulations

Two defensible objectives lead to different recommendations.

**Objective A: SLA-and-tail-first policy.**

A lexicographic objective is appropriate when P1 safety dominates:

1. maximize P1 SLA attainment and minimize \(P(T_{P1}>120)\);
2. minimize P1 and all-case 90th/95th percentile elapsed time;
3. maximize P2 and P3 SLA attainment;
4. minimize mean elapsed time and operational overhead.

One scalar approximation is:

\[
J_A =
12P(T_{P1}>120)
+4P(T_{P2}>480)
+P(T_{P3}>1440)
+0.01 \operatorname{CVaR}_{0.90}(T)
+0.002E[T].
\]

The weights are policy choices, not observed costs.

**Objective B: robust and fair minimax policy.**

Let \(s\) range over Base and U1-U5, \(u_{r,s}\) be normalized utilization of resource \(r\), and \(CV_s(u)\) be the coefficient of variation of individual utilization.

\[
J_B =
\min_{\pi}
\max_s \left[
P(T_{P1,s}>120)
+0.35P(T_{P2,s}>480)
+0.2P(T_{P3,s}>1440)
+\lambda \max_r(u_{r,s}-0.90)_+
+\gamma CV_s(u)
\right].
\]

This objective explicitly penalizes policies that achieve an attractive base-case mean by overloading Chen, Deepa, or Emil, or by creating poor outcomes under U1/U3/U5.

# 3. Candidate policies

## Policy 1: strict specialization with fixed queues

This policy deliberately minimizes flexibility.

**Fixed pools**

| Activity | Permitted primary resources |
|---|---|
| Intake | Ada, Farah |
| Mechanical | Ada, Ben |
| Electrical | Chen, Deepa |
| Software | Chen, Emil |
| Validation | Ben, Deepa |

**Dispatch rule**

1. Each pool has FIFO queues separated by P1, P2, and P3.
2. P1 is selected before P2, and P2 before P3, but there is no P1 preemption once a lower-priority diagnostic task has started.
3. No overflow occurs outside the listed pool.
4. ME and ES cases are always parallelized into their specialist queues.
5. Resources do not switch into other skill pools even if idle.
6. Handover is minimized only incidentally through fixed routing; no dynamic case-retention mechanism is used.

**Strength:** simple, explainable, stable skill roles, comparatively low cross-skill setup burden.

**Weakness:** it wastes Farah’s diagnostic capability, Emil’s Validation capability, and Ada/Deepa’s cross-skill options. It is especially fragile under U2, U3, and U5.

## Policy 2: full pooling with earliest-risk dispatch

All qualified resources are eligible according to the skill matrix.

**Dispatch rule**

1. Rank ready work by priority, then by estimated remaining SLA slack:
   \[
   \text{slack}=\text{deadline}-\text{elapsed time}-\text{estimated remaining work}.
   \]
2. For the highest-ranked task, choose the eligible idle resource with the lowest predicted completion time:
   active service after multiplier + handover + team-transfer + setup.
3. Break ties using lower normalized planned utilization.
4. P1 may preempt a P2/P3 diagnostic task if no qualified idle resource can start the P1 task.
5. The preemption victim is the eligible lower-priority diagnostic activity with the shortest remaining active service time, minimizing restart loss.
6. ME and ES work is normally parallelized whenever different qualified resources are available.
7. No resource is reserved; all capacity is dynamically available to all qualifying work.

**Strength:** best expected response to U2 and some U3 congestion because Ada, Emil, Farah, and Deepa can assist beyond their strict roles.

**Weakness:** can create excessive handovers, setup churn, Chen/Deepa overload, and Validation bursts. It can also improve mean delay while worsening tail risk for cases repeatedly displaced by P1 work.

## Policy 3: hybrid protected-P1 lanes with controlled overflow

This is the recommended candidate.

**Primary lanes**

| Activity | Primary lane |
|---|---|
| Intake | Ada, Deepa, Farah |
| Mechanical | Ada, Ben |
| Electrical | Chen, Deepa |
| Software | Emil, Chen |
| Validation | Ben, Deepa, Emil |

**Dispatch and reservation rule**

1. P1 work is eligible for every qualified resource immediately.
2. Ada and Deepa form a recoverable P1 protection pair:
   - do not place both on lower-priority Validation simultaneously;
   - lower-priority diagnostic work on them is explicitly preemptible;
   - a P1 task receives first claim on the pair.
3. P2 work may overflow after 35 minutes of stage waiting or when projected slack is negative.
4. P3 work may overflow after 75 minutes of stage waiting or when aging makes its 24-hour target unsafe.
5. Overflow is limited to qualified resources; it is not unrestricted “anyone does anything.”
6. P1 preempts after five minutes of waiting if no qualified idle resource can start it. A case may still be preempted only once.
7. The preemption victim is the shortest remaining eligible P2/P3 diagnostic activity.

**Parallelization rule**

- P1 ME/ES: parallelize if two distinct qualified resources can be made available.
- P2/P3 ME/ES: parallelize only if two resources are idle or forecast to start soon and there is no P1 backlog.
- Otherwise execute sequentially on a cross-skilled resource:
  - ME: Ada, Deepa, or Farah where available;
  - ES: Chen, Emil, or Farah where available.
- Sequential execution locks the case to that cross-skilled resource for its next branch, avoiding the extra five-minute team transfer and most handover risk.

**Strength:** protects P1 without permanently idling a person, reduces unnecessary branch fragmentation, and uses pooling only when queues justify it.

**Weakness:** it cannot create missing capacity. Under U5, even a good hybrid policy must prioritize which non-P1 work will miss targets.

# 5. Robust recommendation

## Primary recommendation: hybrid protected-P1 lanes with controlled overflow

Under Objective A and Objective B, the hybrid policy is preferred.

It does not dominate full pooling on base-case mean delay; full pooling may be slightly faster when service estimates are accurate and handovers are cheap. The hybrid policy is preferred because it:

- keeps P1 capacity recoverable rather than dedicating a person permanently;
- limits unnecessary handovers and skill-family setup;
- uses sequential multi-skill execution when parallelism would worsen congestion;
- can temporarily behave like full pooling during U2 Electrical pressure;
- reduces dependence on Chen relative to strict specialization;
- prevents both Ada and Deepa from becoming simultaneously unavailable to P1 behind lower-priority Validation.

## Operational trigger thresholds

These thresholds are policy parameters to calibrate in a pilot.

| Trigger | Threshold | Action |
|---|---|---|
| P1 urgency | Oldest P1 stage wait exceeds 15 minutes or projected P1 slack below 45 minutes | Activate P1 protection; permit immediate P1 overflow and preemption |
| Electrical pressure | Electrical queued work exceeds 80% of next-hour qualified Electrical capacity, or Electrical wait exceeds 25 minutes | Temporarily widen Electrical overflow to Ada, Emil, and Farah if qualified |
| System surge | Rolling two-hour workload exceeds 90% of usable two-hour capacity, or arrivals exceed 1.4 times expected | Use sequential ME/ES for P2/P3, reserve parallelism for P1, escalate workload risk |
| Chen absence | Chen unavailable for more than 15 minutes | Treat Software as scarce; protect Emil from nonessential Validation during 12:00-18:00 |
| Intake outage | Intake unavailable or Intake backlog exceeds 20 minutes | Preserve original queue-entry timestamps, process P1 Intake first after restoration, avoid false SLA-clock resets |
| P3 starvation | P3 age exceeds 240 minutes or residual 24-hour slack becomes unsafe | Allow controlled P3 overflow despite P2 pressure |

## Fallback: P1 emergency mode

P1 emergency mode is appropriate for U1 and U5-like conditions:

- all qualified resources become eligible for P1;
- lower-priority diagnostic tasks remain preemptible;
- P1 ME/ES work is parallelized where feasible;
- no new lower-priority Validation starts are assigned to both Ada and Deepa simultaneously;
- P2/P3 multi-skill cases default to sequential execution;
- P3 work is delayed transparently, with a recorded reason code.

This is a damage-control mode, not a way to meet every SLA.

## Capacity caveat

Even the base case requires additional capacity or demand reduction to satisfy the stated 90% fairness standard. Under ideal multiplier-1.00 work, the base case needs approximately:

\[
\frac{2,721}{0.90}-2,490 \approx 533
\]

additional generic available minutes per day to operate below 90% utilization, before handovers and skill restrictions. This is not an FTE recommendation because skill composition and shift timing matter. It is a lower bound.

Under U5, the equivalent lower-bound gap is much larger. Therefore, the robust recommendation is a **dispatch policy plus explicit overload escalation**, not a claim that routing alone makes U5 safe.

A different policy would be preferable when:

- handover cost is demonstrated to be near zero and service estimates are accurate: full pooling may be preferred;
- demand is stable, low, and single-skill dominated: strict specialization may be acceptable;
- management explicitly prioritizes equal treatment and skill preservation over P1 protection: a less aggressive preemptive policy may be chosen.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

- **Gaming of estimates.** A shortest-remaining-work or slack policy can encourage under-reporting. Use historical distributions by type and activity rather than only operator-entered estimates. Log estimate revisions.
- **P3 starvation.** P1 protection and P2 urgency can silently starve P3. The hybrid policy needs aging thresholds, a visible P3 backlog board, and an escalation rule when P3 residual slack is unsafe.
- **Skill erosion.** Strict specialization may preserve role clarity but can prevent cross-skill practice. Full pooling can create the opposite problem: people repeatedly perform low-complexity overflow work and lose specialist depth.
- **Excessive preemption.** Although each case is preempted at most once, repeated P1 arrivals can still create broad disruption. Preempt only diagnostic work, prefer the shortest remaining eligible task, and log preemption reason and restart penalty.
- **Coordination overhead.** Parallel ME/ES work can reduce diagnostic critical path but create branch synchronization delays, duplicate handovers, and bursts at Validation.
- **Validation bottleneck.** Pooling can make diagnostics appear faster while moving the queue to Validation. Validation waiting must be measured separately rather than inferred from total cycle time.
- **Fairness.** “Full pooling” is not automatically fair. It may repeatedly select Chen, Deepa, and Emil because they are fast and cross-skilled. Monitor normalized utilization, overtime, setup burden, and undesirable task mix by person.
- **Interpretability.** Operators need simple explanations such as “P1 has less than 45 minutes of slack” or “Electrical workload exceeds the next-hour capacity,” not opaque optimization scores.
- **Override risk.** Manual overrides should be allowed for safety but recorded with a structured reason. Otherwise policy evaluation becomes confounded.
- **Tail-day psychology.** On U4-like days, workers may perceive routing as unfair because long jobs are random. Explain that fairness is assessed over workload, overtime, and setup burden, not just case count.

# 7. Monitoring and process-mining design

## Required operational indicators

Monitor at least:

- arrivals by block, priority, and type;
- queue length and queued work minutes by stage and skill;
- P1/P2/P3 SLA attainment and residual SLA slack;
- p50, p90, p95, and CVaR elapsed time;
- activity service distributions by resource and skill;
- complex-tail frequency by activity;
- resource utilization, overtime, break conflicts, and utilization CV;
- handover minutes, setup minutes, coordination minutes, and preemption penalties;
- ME/ES parallel versus sequential outcome;
- Validation queue length and delay;
- resource-specific assignment mix and skill-family switching;
- percentage of tasks using overflow;
- P3 aging and starvation incidents;
- policy overrides and their reasons.

## Event and decision attributes

The interval event log should include at least:

- `case:concept:name`;
- `concept:name`;
- `start_timestamp`;
- `time:timestamp`;
- `org:resource`;
- case type and priority;
- queue-entry timestamp;
- assignment-decision timestamp;
- eligible-resource set at decision time;
- chosen resource and skill used;
- planned base service, multiplier-adjusted planned service, and actual active service;
- pause/resume flag;
- preemption count and restart penalty;
- previous resource, new resource, handover minutes, and team-transfer minutes;
- setup minutes and prior skill family;
- branch mode: parallel or sequential;
- coordination delay;
- policy version and dispatch reason;
- scenario/shock flag, including outage and absence;
- SLA clock start, target, residual slack, and breach flag.

The decision audit should be stored separately from the business event trace if queue-entry and assignment-decision events would otherwise distort the process model.

## PM4Py analyses

PM4Py can support several useful analyses:

- `pm4py.format_dataframe` standardizes the interval event log.
- `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` provides **case-level** service, waiting, and sojourn fields. These are repeated across events in a case and must not be mislabelled as stage queue time.
- `pm4py.get_service_time` compares observed activity service duration by activity and can reveal multiplier drift or a U4-like tail shift.
- `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` reveals resource-to-resource handover patterns and whether pooling creates excessive dependence on Chen, Deepa, or Emil.
- Performance DFGs and time-sliced DFGs can reveal route changes and bottleneck migration.
- Variant analysis can compare parallel versus sequential ME/ES paths.
- Resource-role discovery can identify unintended role drift.
- Conformance checking against the intended Intake  diagnostic  Validation model can detect skipped Validation, undocumented rework, or unusual branch patterns.

Stage-specific queue delay should be calculated outside the case-level PM4Py service/waiting fields as:

\[
\text{stage queue delay} =
\text{activity actual start} -
\text{stage queue-entry timestamp}.
\]

This is particularly important for distinguishing Intake delay, Electrical delay, Software delay, and Validation delay.

# It implements three policies: strict specialization, full pooling, and hybrid.

#
# Known simplifications:
# - No correlation is modeled between priority, type, and complex-tail status.
# - Handover delay is conservatively treated as receiving-resource reservation.
# - Parallel branches use one final predecessor resource for Validation handover;
#   the 8-minute coordination delay represents remaining synchronization burden.
# - No new arrivals are generated after the supplied day.

# ---------------------- Explicit policy/mechanism constants ----------------------

HANDOVER_MINUTES = 7
TEAM_TRANSFER_MINUTES = 5
SETUP_MINUTES = 5
PARALLEL_COORDINATION_MINUTES = 8
PREEMPT_RESTART_RATE = 0.10
OVERTIME_LIMIT_MINUTES = 30

P1_DEADLINE = 120
P2_DEADLINE = 8 * 60
P3_DEADLINE = 24 * 60
DEADLINES = {"P1": P1_DEADLINE, "P2": P2_DEADLINE, "P3": P3_DEADLINE}

# Repeated calendars permit completion of day-0 cases on the following day.
HORIZON = 3 * 24 * 60
ORIGIN = pd.Timestamp("2024-01-01 00:00:00")

TYPE_TO_DIAGNOSTICS = {
    "M": ["Mechanical"],
    "E": ["Electrical"],
    "S": ["Software"],
    "ME": ["Mechanical", "Electrical"],
    "ES": ["Electrical", "Software"],
}

STRICT_POOLS = {
    "Intake": ["Ada", "Farah"],
    "Mechanical": ["Ada", "Ben"],
    "Electrical": ["Chen", "Deepa"],
    "Software": ["Chen", "Emil"],
    "Validation": ["Ben", "Deepa"],
}

HYBRID_PRIMARY = {
    "Intake": ["Ada", "Deepa", "Farah"],
    "Mechanical": ["Ada", "Ben"],
    "Electrical": ["Chen", "Deepa"],
    "Software": ["Emil", "Chen"],
    "Validation": ["Ben", "Deepa", "Emil"],
}

HYBRID_OVERFLOW_WAIT = {"P1": 0, "P2": 35, "P3": 75}
POLICIES = ["strict", "pool", "hybrid"]
SCENARIOS = ["Base", "U1", "U2", "U3", "U4", "U5"]


# ----------------------------- Helper functions -----------------------------

def expected_base_service(activity):
    r = service.loc[activity]
    ordinary = (r["ordinary_lo"] + r["ordinary_hi"]) / 2.0
    tail = (r["tail_lo"] + r["tail_hi"]) / 2.0
    return (1 - r["tail_p"]) * ordinary + r["tail_p"] * tail


EXPECTED_BASE = {a: expected_base_service(a) for a in ACTIVITIES}


def family(activity):
    if activity == "Mechanical":
        return "M"
    if activity in {"Electrical", "Software"}:
        return "ES"
    return "GEN"


def qualified(resource, activity):
    return pd.notna(skills.loc[resource, activity])


def resource_available(resource, minute):
    """Calendar repeats daily. Breaks and overtime are unavailable/available explicitly."""
    row = shift_table.loc[resource]
    clock = minute % (24 * 60)
    within_shift_or_ot = row["shift_start"] <= clock < row["shift_end"] + OVERTIME_LIMIT_MINUTES
    if not within_shift_or_ot:
        return False
    if pd.notna(row["break_start"]) and row["break_start"] <= clock < row["break_end"]:
        return False
    return True


def intake_outage(minute, scenario):
    # The stated outage occurs on day 0 only.
    return scenario == "U5" and 570 <= minute < 600


def draw_base_service(activity, scenario, rng):
    """Draw unmultiplied active service work."""
    r = service.loc[activity]
    tail_p = r["tail_p"]
    if scenario == "U4" and activity in DIAGNOSTICS:
        tail_p = min(1.0, 2.0 * tail_p)

    if rng.random() < tail_p:
        return rng.uniform(r["tail_lo"], r["tail_hi"])
    return rng.uniform(r["ordinary_lo"], r["ordinary_hi"])


def scenario_demand(scenario):
    """Apply U1/U2 changes to a demand copy."""
    d = demand.copy()
    type_cols = ["M_pct", "E_pct", "S_pct", "ME_pct", "ES_pct"]

    if scenario in {"U1", "U5"}:
        morning = d["start"] < 720
        d.loc[morning, "expected_arrivals"] *= 1.7

        # Assumption: P2/P3 shrink proportionally after P1 gains eight percentage points.
        for idx in d.index[morning]:
            old_p1, old_p2, old_p3 = d.loc[idx, ["P1_pct", "P2_pct", "P3_pct"]]
            new_p1 = min(100.0, old_p1 + 8.0)
            residual = 100.0 - new_p1
            d.loc[idx, "P1_pct"] = new_p1
            d.loc[idx, "P2_pct"] = residual * old_p2 / (old_p2 + old_p3)
            d.loc[idx, "P3_pct"] = residual * old_p3 / (old_p2 + old_p3)

    if scenario == "U2":
        affected = (d["start"] >= 720) & (d["start"] < 960)
        for idx in d.index[affected]:
            mix = d.loc[idx, type_cols].astype(float).to_numpy()
            mix[type_cols.index("E_pct")] *= 2.0
            mix[type_cols.index("ES_pct")] *= 2.0
            d.loc[idx, type_cols] = 100.0 * mix / mix.sum()

    return d


def generate_arrivals(scenario, rng):
    """Generate a day-0 arrival stream from supplied expected arrivals."""
    d = scenario_demand(scenario)
    arrivals = defaultdict(list)
    counter = 0

    for _, row in d.iterrows():
        n = int(rng.poisson(row["expected_arrivals"]))
        priorities = ["P1", "P2", "P3"]
        priority_prob = np.array([row["P1_pct"], row["P2_pct"], row["P3_pct"]], dtype=float) / 100.0
        types = ["M", "E", "S", "ME", "ES"]
        type_prob = np.array(
            [row["M_pct"], row["E_pct"], row["S_pct"], row["ME_pct"], row["ES_pct"]],
            dtype=float,
        ) / 100.0

        for _ in range(n):
            minute = int(rng.integers(int(row["start"]), int(row["end"])))
            counter += 1
            arrivals[minute].append(
                {
                    "case_id": f"C{counter:04d}",
                    "priority": str(rng.choice(priorities, p=priority_prob)),
                    "case_type": str(rng.choice(types, p=type_prob)),
                    "arrival": minute,
                }
            )
    return arrivals


# ----------------------------- Simulation -----------------------------

def run_one(policy, scenario, seed=1):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    workers = [r for r in RESOURCES if not (scenario in {"U3", "U5"} and r == "Chen")]
    arrivals_by_time = generate_arrivals(scenario, rng)

    cases = {}
    pending = []
    state = {r: None for r in workers}
    last_diag = {r: (None, None) for r in workers}  # (skill family, case id)
    assigned_work = {r: 0.0 for r in workers}
    occupancy = {r: [] for r in workers}  # [reserve_start, release_time]
    event_rows = []

    def can_fit(resource, start, total_reserved_minutes, activity):
        """Require activity reservation to fit uninterrupted work availability."""
        total_reserved_minutes = int(math.ceil(total_reserved_minutes))
        if start + total_reserved_minutes > HORIZON:
            return False
        for minute in range(start, start + total_reserved_minutes):
            if not resource_available(resource, minute):
                return False
            # Conservative choice: Intake preparation/service cannot cross the IT outage.
            if activity == "Intake" and intake_outage(minute, scenario):
                return False
        return True

    def add_task(case, activity, ready, previous_resource=None, previous_family="GEN",
                 lock_resource=None, cross_skill_requirements=None):
        case["task_counter"] += 1
        task = {
            "id": f"{case['id']}:T{case['task_counter']}",
            "case_id": case["id"],
            "activity": activity,
            "ready": int(ready),
            "previous_resource": previous_resource,
            "previous_family": previous_family,
            "lock_resource": lock_resource,
            "cross_skill_requirements": cross_skill_requirements,
            "base_remaining": float(draw_base_service(activity, scenario, rng)),
            "pending_restart_penalty": 0,
        }
        pending.append(task)
        return task

    def hybrid_protected_validation(task, resource):
        """Preserve one Ada/Deepa slot from simultaneous lower-priority Validation."""
        if policy != "hybrid":
            return False
        case = cases[task["case_id"]]
        if task["activity"] != "Validation" or case["priority"] == "P1":
            return False
        if resource not in {"Ada", "Deepa"}:
            return False
        other = "Deepa" if resource == "Ada" else "Ada"
        if other not in state:
            return False
        other_task = state[other]["task"] if state[other] is not None else None
        return (
            other_task is not None
            and other_task["activity"] == "Validation"
            and cases[other_task["case_id"]]["priority"] != "P1"
        )

    def eligible(task, resource, now):
        case = cases[task["case_id"]]
        activity = task["activity"]

        if not qualified(resource, activity):
            return False

        if task["lock_resource"] is not None and resource != task["lock_resource"]:
            return False

        # Sequential ME/ES first branch must be assigned to a resource capable of all branches.
        requirements = task["cross_skill_requirements"]
        if requirements is not None and not all(qualified(resource, a) for a in requirements):
            return False

        if policy == "strict":
            if resource not in STRICT_POOLS[activity]:
                return False

        if policy == "hybrid":
            # P1 may use any qualified resource. P2/P3 overflow is delayed.
            if resource not in HYBRID_PRIMARY[activity]:
                if now - task["ready"] < HYBRID_OVERFLOW_WAIT[case["priority"]]:
                    return False
            if hybrid_protected_validation(task, resource):
                return False

        return True

    def handover_and_setup(task, resource):
        """Return explicit transfer and setup components for an assignment."""
        case = cases[task["case_id"]]
        activity = task["activity"]
        current_family = family(activity)

        handover = 0
        if task["previous_resource"] is not None and task["previous_resource"] != resource:
            handover += HANDOVER_MINUTES
            if (
                task["previous_family"] in {"M", "ES"}
                and current_family in {"M", "ES"}
                and task["previous_family"] != current_family
            ):
                handover += TEAM_TRANSFER_MINUTES

        setup = 0
        prior_family, prior_case = last_diag[resource]
        if (
            activity in DIAGNOSTICS
            and prior_family is not None
            and prior_family != current_family
            and prior_case != case["id"]
        ):
            setup = SETUP_MINUTES

        return handover, setup

    def task_options(task, now):
        """Find currently idle resources that can start and finish the task."""
        options = []
        for resource in workers:
            if state[resource] is not None:
                continue
            if not eligible(task, resource, now):
                continue

            handover, setup = handover_and_setup(task, resource)
            multiplier = float(skills.loc[resource, task["activity"]])
            active_duration = int(math.ceil(task["base_remaining"] * multiplier))
            active_duration += int(task["pending_restart_penalty"])
            total_reserved = handover + setup + active_duration

            if can_fit(resource, now, total_reserved, task["activity"]):
                options.append(
                    {
                        "resource": resource,
                        "handover": handover,
                        "setup": setup,
                        "multiplier": multiplier,
                        "active_duration": active_duration,
                        "total_reserved": total_reserved,
                    }
                )
        return options

    def remaining_proxy(task):
        """Transparent slack proxy; it is not a calibrated duration predictor."""
        case = cases[task["case_id"]]
        activity = task["activity"]

        if activity == "Intake":
            diagnostics = TYPE_TO_DIAGNOSTICS[case["case_type"]]
            if len(diagnostics) == 1:
                diagnostic_proxy = EXPECTED_BASE[diagnostics[0]]
            else:
                diagnostic_proxy = max(EXPECTED_BASE[a] for a in diagnostics) + PARALLEL_COORDINATION_MINUTES
            return task["base_remaining"] + diagnostic_proxy + EXPECTED_BASE["Validation"]

        if activity in DIAGNOSTICS:
            remaining = task["base_remaining"] + EXPECTED_BASE["Validation"]
            if case.get("mode") == "sequential":
                diagnostics = case["diagnostics"]
                idx = diagnostics.index(activity)
                remaining += sum(EXPECTED_BASE[a] for a in diagnostics[idx + 1 :])
            return remaining

        return task["base_remaining"]

    def priority_key(task, now):
        case = cases[task["case_id"]]
        rank = {"P1": 0, "P2": 1, "P3": 2}[case["priority"]]

        # Controlled P3 aging in the hybrid policy.
        if policy == "hybrid" and case["priority"] == "P3" and now - task["ready"] > 240:
            rank = 1.5

        if policy == "strict":
            return (rank, task["ready"], task["id"])

        slack = DEADLINES[case["priority"]] - (now - case["arrival"]) - remaining_proxy(task)
        return (rank, slack, task["ready"], task["id"])

    def choose_resource(task, options):
        activity = task["activity"]

        if policy == "strict":
            return min(
                options,
                key=lambda x: (
                    STRICT_POOLS[activity].index(x["resource"]),
                    RESOURCES.index(x["resource"]),
                ),
            )

        if policy == "hybrid":
            return min(
                options,
                key=lambda x: (
                    0 if x["resource"] in HYBRID_PRIMARY[activity] else 1,
                    x["total_reserved"],
                    assigned_work[x["resource"]],
                ),
            )

        # Full pooling: predicted completion first, then workload balance.
        return min(
            options,
            key=lambda x: (x["total_reserved"], assigned_work[x["resource"]]),
        )

    def parallel_possible(case, now):
        diagnostics = TYPE_TO_DIAGNOSTICS[case["case_type"]]
        idle = [r for r in workers if state[r] is None and resource_available(r, now)]
        for r1 in idle:
            for r2 in idle:
                if r1 != r2 and qualified(r1, diagnostics[0]) and qualified(r2, diagnostics[1]):
                    return True
        return False

    def choose_branch_mode(case, now):
        if len(TYPE_TO_DIAGNOSTICS[case["case_type"]]) == 1:
            return "single"
        if policy in {"strict", "pool"}:
            return "parallel"
        if case["priority"] == "P1":
            return "parallel"
        p1_waiting = any(
            cases[t["case_id"]]["priority"] == "P1" and t["ready"] <= now
            for t in pending
        )
        return "parallel" if parallel_possible(case, now) and not p1_waiting else "sequential"

    def write_event(rec, end_minute, status):
        """Write one interval fragment; setup/handover are attributes, not activity duration."""
        task = rec["task"]
        case = cases[task["case_id"]]
        active_start = rec["active_start"]
        if end_minute <= active_start:
            return

        event_rows.append(
            {
                "case:concept:name": case["id"],
                "concept:name": task["activity"],
                "org:resource": rec["resource"],
                "start_minute": active_start,
                "end_minute": end_minute,
                "priority": case["priority"],
                "case_type": case["case_type"],
                "branch_mode": case.get("mode", "not_yet_decided"),
                "queue_minutes": active_start - task["ready"],
                "assignment_wait_minutes": rec["reserve_start"] - task["ready"],
                "handover_minutes": rec["handover"],
                "setup_minutes": rec["setup"],
                "preemption_penalty_minutes": rec["restart_penalty"],
                "active_minutes": end_minute - active_start,
                "fragment_status": status,
                "policy_version": policy,
                "scenario": scenario,
            }
        )

    def on_complete(rec, now):
        """Advance the case control flow after a normal activity completion."""
        task = rec["task"]
        case = cases[task["case_id"]]
        activity = task["activity"]
        resource = rec["resource"]
        task["base_remaining"] = 0.0
        case["last_resource"] = resource
        case["last_family"] = family(activity)

        if activity == "Intake":
            case["diagnostics"] = TYPE_TO_DIAGNOSTICS[case["case_type"]]
            case["mode"] = choose_branch_mode(case, now)

            if case["mode"] == "single":
                add_task(case, case["diagnostics"][0], now, resource, family(activity))

            elif case["mode"] == "parallel":
                for diagnostic in case["diagnostics"]:
                    add_task(case, diagnostic, now, resource, family(activity))

            else:  # sequential
                add_task(
                    case,
                    case["diagnostics"][0],
                    now,
                    resource,
                    family(activity),
                    cross_skill_requirements=case["diagnostics"],
                )
            return

        if activity in DIAGNOSTICS:
            case["diagnostics_completed"] += 1

            if (
                case.get("mode") == "sequential"
                and case["diagnostics_completed"] < len(case["diagnostics"])
            ):
                next_activity = case["diagnostics"][case["diagnostics_completed"]]
                add_task(
                    case,
                    next_activity,
                    now,
                    resource,
                    family(activity),
                    lock_resource=case["lock_resource"],
                )
                return

            if case["diagnostics_completed"] == len(case["diagnostics"]):
                coordination = (
                    PARALLEL_COORDINATION_MINUTES
                    if case.get("mode") == "parallel" and len(case["diagnostics"]) > 1
                    else 0
                )
                case["coordination_minutes"] += coordination
                add_task(
                    case,
                    "Validation",
                    now + coordination,
                    resource,
                    family(activity),
                )
            return

        if activity == "Validation":
            case["completion"] = now

    def start_task(task, option, now):
        resource = option["resource"]
        case = cases[task["case_id"]]

        if task["cross_skill_requirements"] is not None and case["lock_resource"] is None:
            case["lock_resource"] = resource
            task["lock_resource"] = resource

        restart_penalty = task["pending_restart_penalty"]
        task["pending_restart_penalty"] = 0

        reserve_start = now
        active_start = now + option["handover"] + option["setup"]
        finish = active_start + option["active_duration"]
        occ = [reserve_start, finish]
        occupancy[resource].append(occ)

        rec = {
            "task": task,
            "resource": resource,
            "reserve_start": reserve_start,
            "active_start": active_start,
            "finish": finish,
            "handover": option["handover"],
            "setup": option["setup"],
            "multiplier": option["multiplier"],
            "restart_penalty": restart_penalty,
            "occupancy": occ,
        }
        state[resource] = rec
        assigned_work[resource] += option["total_reserved"]

        if task["activity"] in DIAGNOSTICS:
            last_diag[resource] = (family(task["activity"]), case["id"])

    def preempt(rec, now):
        """Preempt a lower-priority diagnostic activity and add stated restart penalty."""
        resource = rec["resource"]
        task = rec["task"]
        case = cases[task["case_id"]]

        write_event(rec, now, "preempted")
        rec["occupancy"][1] = now

        active_elapsed = max(0, now - rec["active_start"])
        penalty_spent = min(active_elapsed, rec["restart_penalty"])
        base_elapsed = max(0, active_elapsed - penalty_spent)
        task["base_remaining"] = max(
            0.0,
            task["base_remaining"] - base_elapsed / rec["multiplier"],
        )

        remaining_active = max(0, rec["finish"] - now)
        old_penalty_remaining = max(0, rec["restart_penalty"] - active_elapsed)
        task["pending_restart_penalty"] = old_penalty_remaining + int(
            math.ceil(PREEMPT_RESTART_RATE * remaining_active)
        )
        task["ready"] = now
        task["previous_resource"] = resource
        task["previous_family"] = family(task["activity"])
        case["preemptions"] += 1

        state[resource] = None
        pending.append(task)

    def maybe_preempt(now):
        """P1 may preempt only a lower-priority diagnostic task, never Intake/Validation."""
        if policy == "strict":
            return

        while True:
            p1_tasks = sorted(
                [
                    task
                    for task in pending
                    if task["ready"] <= now and cases[task["case_id"]]["priority"] == "P1"
                ],
                key=lambda task: priority_key(task, now),
            )
            made_preemption = False

            for p1_task in p1_tasks:
                if policy == "hybrid" and now - p1_task["ready"] < 5:
                    continue

                # If an idle qualified resource can start P1, no preemption is needed.
                if task_options(p1_task, now):
                    continue

                victims = []
                for resource, rec in state.items():
                    if rec is None:
                        continue
                    victim_task = rec["task"]
                    victim_case = cases[victim_task["case_id"]]

                    if victim_task["activity"] not in DIAGNOSTICS:
                        continue
                    if victim_case["priority"] == "P1" or victim_case["preemptions"] >= 1:
                        continue
                    if rec["active_start"] > now:
                        continue
                    if not eligible(p1_task, resource, now):
                        continue

                    handover, setup = handover_and_setup(p1_task, resource)
                    multiplier = float(skills.loc[resource, p1_task["activity"]])
                    p1_duration = int(math.ceil(p1_task["base_remaining"] * multiplier))
                    p1_duration += int(p1_task["pending_restart_penalty"])
                    if can_fit(
                        resource,
                        now,
                        handover + setup + p1_duration,
                        p1_task["activity"],
                    ):
                        victims.append((rec["finish"] - now, resource, rec))

                if victims:
                    # Shortest remaining victim minimizes expected restart loss.
                    _, _, selected = min(victims, key=lambda x: x[0])
                    preempt(selected, now)
                    made_preemption = True
                    break

            if not made_preemption:
                break

    def schedule_ready_work(now):
        """Greedy dispatch: highest-risk task first, then policy-specific resource selection."""
        while True:
            ready = [task for task in pending if task["ready"] <= now]
            if not ready:
                return

            selected_task = None
            selected_option = None

            for task in sorted(ready, key=lambda t: priority_key(t, now)):
                options = task_options(task, now)
                if options:
                    selected_task = task
                    selected_option = choose_resource(task, options)
                    break

            if selected_task is None:
                return

            pending.remove(selected_task)
            start_task(selected_task, selected_option, now)

    # ----------------------------- Main event loop -----------------------------

    for now in range(HORIZON + 1):
        # Finish normal work first, making resources available at this minute.
        for resource in list(workers):
            rec = state[resource]
            if rec is not None and rec["finish"] == now:
                write_event(rec, now, "completed")
                state[resource] = None
                on_complete(rec, now)

        # Create arrivals and their Intake tasks.
        for arrival in arrivals_by_time.get(now, []):
            case = {
                "id": arrival["case_id"],
                "arrival": arrival["arrival"],
                "priority": arrival["priority"],
                "case_type": arrival["case_type"],
                "task_counter": 0,
                "diagnostics": [],
                "diagnostics_completed": 0,
                "mode": None,
                "lock_resource": None,
                "last_resource": None,
                "last_family": "GEN",
                "preemptions": 0,
                "coordination_minutes": 0,
                "completion": None,
            }
            cases[case["id"]] = case
            add_task(case, "Intake", now)

        if now < HORIZON:
            maybe_preempt(now)
            schedule_ready_work(now)

    # ----------------------------- Metrics and interval event log -----------------------------

    log = pd.DataFrame(event_rows)
    if not log.empty:
        log["start_timestamp"] = ORIGIN + pd.to_timedelta(log["start_minute"], unit="m")
        log["time:timestamp"] = ORIGIN + pd.to_timedelta(log["end_minute"], unit="m")
        # Required interval-event columns are present:
        # case:concept:name, concept:name, start_timestamp, time:timestamp, org:resource.

    case_rows = []
    for case in cases.values():
        completed = case["completion"] is not None
        elapsed = (
            case["completion"] - case["arrival"]
            if completed
            else HORIZON - case["arrival"]
        )
        case_rows.append(
            {
                "case_id": case["id"],
                "priority": case["priority"],
                "completed": completed,
                "elapsed": elapsed,
                "preemptions": case["preemptions"],
                "coordination_minutes": case["coordination_minutes"],
            }
        )
    case_df = pd.DataFrame(case_rows)

    def sla(priority, target):
        subset = case_df[case_df["priority"] == priority]
        if subset.empty:
            return np.nan
        return np.mean(subset["completed"] & (subset["elapsed"] <= target))

    completed_elapsed = case_df.loc[case_df["completed"], "elapsed"]
    if completed_elapsed.empty:
        mean_delay = np.nan
        p90_delay = np.nan
    else:
        mean_delay = completed_elapsed.mean()
        p90_delay = completed_elapsed.quantile(0.90)

    # Day-0 utilization includes reservation time: handovers + setup + active work.
    utilization = {}
    for resource in workers:
        available = sum(resource_available(resource, minute) for minute in range(24 * 60))
        busy = sum(
            max(0, min(end, 24 * 60) - max(start, 0))
            for start, end in occupancy[resource]
        )
        utilization[resource] = busy / available if available else np.nan

    util_values = np.array([v for v in utilization.values() if pd.notna(v)])
    fairness_cv = np.std(util_values) / np.mean(util_values) if np.mean(util_values) > 0 else np.nan

    if log.empty:
        mechanism_minutes = 0.0
    else:
        mechanism_minutes = log["handover_minutes"].sum() + log["setup_minutes"].sum()
    mechanism_minutes += case_df["coordination_minutes"].sum()

    metrics = {
        "mean_delay_min": mean_delay,  # completed cases only; completion rate is reported separately
        "p90_delay_min": p90_delay,
        "P1_SLA": sla("P1", P1_DEADLINE),
        "P2_SLA": sla("P2", P2_DEADLINE),
        "P3_SLA": sla("P3", P3_DEADLINE),
        "completion_rate": case_df["completed"].mean() if not case_df.empty else np.nan,
        "max_resource_utilization": np.nanmax(util_values) if len(util_values) else np.nan,
        "utilization_fairness_cv": fairness_cv,
        "handover_setup_coordination_per_case": (
            mechanism_minutes / len(case_df) if len(case_df) else np.nan
        ),
        "mean_preemptions_per_case": case_df["preemptions"].mean() if not case_df.empty else np.nan,
        "n_cases": len(case_df),
    }
    return metrics, log, case_df, pd.Series(utilization, name="utilization")

# 9. Limits and validation plan

## What cannot be concluded from the supplied data

The supplied information does not identify:

- actual arrival variability or burstiness within blocks;
- whether priority correlates with case type or service tails;
- actual service-time distributions beyond ranges and tail probabilities;
- whether handover delays consume worker time, case time, or both;
- whether work may safely span breaks;
- actual overnight/weekend staffing;
- whether overtime is voluntary, equally feasible, or already committed;
- real preemption acceptance and restart behavior;
- resource learning, fatigue, or skill decay;
- the business impact of missing P2/P3 targets;
- current queue lengths at the beginning of the day;
- whether Validation is truly independent of diagnostic resources;
- whether a P1 may interrupt an activity already in setup or transfer.

Therefore, the numerical bands should be used for policy screening, not as a staffing guarantee.

## Pilot and backtest design

A safe evaluation should be phased.

1. **Instrument before changing routing.**
   - Log queue-entry, assignment decisions, candidate resources, handovers, service durations, pauses, and SLA clocks.
   - Estimate empirical service distributions by activity, type, resource, and priority.
   - Validate whether the assumed 7/5/5/8-minute overheads match reality.

2. **Offline replay and calibration.**
   - Reconstruct historical cases from interval logs.
   - Replay strict, full-pooling, and hybrid assignment decisions against the same arrivals.
   - Use common random numbers and bootstrap confidence intervals.
   - Stress test observed days with U1-U5-style perturbations.

3. **Shadow mode.**
   - Display hybrid recommendations to coordinators without automatically changing assignments.
   - Record accepted, overridden, and rejected recommendations with reasons.
   - Check whether predicted bottlenecks match observed queues.

4. **Limited live pilot.**
   - Start with P2/P3 controlled overflow and Validation protection.
   - Keep P1 handling under incumbent rules initially, except for advisory alerts.
   - Introduce P1 preemption only after confirming restart penalties and operator acceptance.

5. **P1 safety guardrails.**
   - Never deny P1 an eligible resource because of experiment assignment.
   - Allow immediate human override.
   - Stop the pilot if the lower confidence bound of P1 SLA deteriorates materially versus baseline, if P1 preemptions become frequent, or if P1 restart penalties exceed assumptions.

6. **Stepped rollout.**
   - Compare time blocks or teams rather than randomly exposing individual P1 cases.
   - Use a stepped-wedge rollout with matched demand/type mix where possible.
   - Evaluate mean, p90/p95, SLA attainment, utilization CV, handover burden, and P3 aging jointly.

7. **Decision criterion.**
   - Adopt the hybrid policy only if it improves or preserves P1 SLA while reducing tail delay or overload concentration relative to strict specialization.
   - If U5-like conditions occur, measure whether the policy correctly identifies infeasibility and escalates early rather than silently accumulating SLA breaches.
