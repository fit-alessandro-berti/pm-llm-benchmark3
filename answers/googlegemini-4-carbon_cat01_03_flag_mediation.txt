## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

### Choice of Causal Mediation Framework
The high-risk `flag` is not an autonomous biological or physical exposure; it is a deterministic threshold indicator ($F = \mathbb{I}(\text{risk\_score} \ge \tau)$, empirically firing between `0.61` and `0.64`) bundled with multiple organizational rules: (i) badge display to staff ($A_{\text{disp}}$), (ii) routing to dedicated priority triage resources `T1`/`T2` ($A_{\text{tri}}$), (iii) automatic mandatory manual review ($A_{\text{man}}$), and (iv) downstream escalation criteria/capacity ($A_{\text{esc}}$). Saying “set `flag = 0`” is ill-defined unless we specify whether we alter underlying case risk, recalibrate `risk_score`, hide the badge (`P1`), or unbundle routing/capacity rules (`P2`–`P4`).

Accordingly, we reject pure cross-world **Natural Direct and Indirect Effects (NDE/NIE)** as primary targets and instead combine **Separable Effects** (decomposing the bundled flag policy into distinct operational levers $A_{\text{disp}}, A_{\text{tri}}, A_{\text{man}}, A_{\text{esc}}$), **Interventional Direct and Indirect Effects (IDEs/IIEs)** (replacing cross-world individual mediator values with draws from counterfactual regime-specific mediator distributions given covariates), and **Regime Contrasts under Partial Interference** (indexing potential outcomes by the system-wide policy regime $P_k$ rather than case $i$'s flag alone). Cross-world NDE/NIE fail here because priority triage changes `triage_done` clock time, reviewer assignment, and queue congestion—exposure-induced intermediate states ("recanting witnesses") that confound downstream mediators (`manual_queue_wait`, `escalation`) and `cycle_time`.

Three structural complications prevent naive causal mediation decomposition. First, **shared-queue interference** violates SUTVA: because flagged and unflagged cases compete for reviewers (`M1`–`M3`) and senior escalation staff, routing `flag=1` cases into mandatory manual review inflates `manual_queue_wait` and `escalation_queue_wait` for concurrent cases (including unflagged high-complexity arrivals like `R09` and `R20`), biasing cross-group contrasts as estimates of system-wide policy effects. Second, **exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounding** arises sequentially: entering `priority_lane` reduces `triage_wait` and `triage_service`, altering the clock time a case enters downstream queues and its assigned `review_resource`. These intermediate states are caused by the flag policy yet simultaneously confound `manual_queue_wait`, `escalation`, and `cycle_time`, invalidating cross-world natural direct and indirect effects. Third, **treatment-confounder feedback** emerges dynamically across arrivals: bursts of `flag=1` cases deplete shared review capacity, elevating system congestion (`arrival_load` and `U_queue_state`) faced by subsequent arrivals, which in turn alters reviewer availability, discretionary referral thresholds, and downstream waiting times.

Observed contrasts strongly exhibit **upstream acceleration paired with severe downstream delay**. Flagged cases complete triage in `0.84h` on average (`0.24h` wait + `0.60h` service) versus `3.43h` for unflagged cases (`2.48h` wait + `0.95h` service), saving `2.59h` initially. However, total cycle time doubles (`16.51h` vs. `8.17h`) because 100% of flagged cases enter manual review (vs. 40% unflagged), wait `6.50h` before review starts (vs. `1.88h` for reviewed unflagged cases), and escalate more frequently (60% vs. 20%, adding `8.92h` when escalated). This reversal persists within Medium (`10.62h` vs. `6.03h`) and High complexity (`22.40h` vs. `15.40h`). Yet these contrasts are **not automatically causal**: (1) `flag=1` fires deterministically at `risk_score >= 0.64` with zero Low-complexity overlap, confounding policy routing with `U_underlying_risk`; (2) early triage completion discharges morning flagged cases (`R01`–`R03`) hours before afternoon review starts (`14:06`–`18:18`), converting triage savings into pre-shift review waiting; (3) High-complexity cases cluster on reviewer `M2` (100% escalated); and (4) shared-queue congestion contaminates unflagged comparison baselines.
