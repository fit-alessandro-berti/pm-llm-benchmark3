## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

**C1 — J1 batch-synchronization wait.** 08:10  12:05 = **3 h 55 min**. Counterfactual: batch starts when the earliest *complete feasible subset* exists (J1+J2 ready at 08:25)  15 min. Avoidable under that policy  **3 h 40 min**. Arithmetic on timestamps, not causal proof: the 12:05 start may itself have been gated by something unlogged.

**C2 — J2 batch-synchronization wait.** 08:25  12:05 = **3 h 40 min**, of which J3's readiness (11:55) plus 10 min setup accounts for the last 3 h 50 min of the gate. Under a "start when two of three ready" counterfactual  ~0. J3's own wait is only 10 min, so the cost falls entirely on the early members — classic tail-wait distribution.

**C3 — FIX-A delay for J4.** J3 holds FIX-A 09:00–11:40 (160 min). J4 arrives CNC-2 at 09:50, `Machine Process` starts 11:45  **115 min** elapsed. Counterfactual: tool free at 09:50 with a 5-min setup  ~5 min, so  **110 min (1 h 50 min)** attributable to the fixture. Sub-evidence: CNC-2 was demonstrably idle 10:05–10:47 (**42 min**) while J4 could not start; the 11:4011:45 lag is tool handover. Upper-bound attribution only — no `Wait for Tool` event exists.

**C4 — S1 consolidation delay to J1.** Packed 13:30, dispatch 19:25 = **5 h 55 min** of post-pack wait. Benchmarks: S2 = 25 min, S3 = 35 min  excess  **5 h 20–5 h 30 min**; against the shipment-flattened median (0.8 + 0.3 = 1.1 h)   **4 h 45 min**. J4, packed 19:10, waits only 15 min, so J1 absorbs essentially all of J4's lateness (19:10  13:30 = 5 h 40 min).

**C5 — CAL-3 delay for J6.** 16:05  17:25 = **80 min**, decomposed: 5 min TEST-1 queue + 25 min J5's test + **45 min CAL-3 recalibration** + 5 min handover. Tool-attributable  **50 min (62.5%)**; machine-attributable 5 min (6.3%); peer sequencing 25 min (31.3%). Counterfactual: off-line or pre-shift recalibration  J6 tests  16:40, saving ~45 min.

**C6 — B2 membership vs. rack.** J4 ready 13:10  B2 starts 14:50 = **100 min** = 90 min waiting for J5 (14:40) + 10 min post-readiness setup. RACK-7 free 14:35, so the rack constraint is **slack** (J5 binds first). OVEN-1 idle 12:55–14:50 = 115 min, yet the machine summary reports a longest recorded gap of only 73 min — a 42 min discrepancy that is itself evidence of logging incompleteness.

**C7 — L7 quarantine asymmetry.** 10:20  13:50 = **3 h 30 min**. J2 entered B1 anyway (override), J5 waited: its readiness (14:40) is 50 min after release. The same lot event therefore produced two different couplings — one invisible (override), one visible.

## 4. What flattening misses

**Job.** *Visible:* per-stage DFG, the `InspectReworkInspect` loop (2 of 6 jobs, 33% rework rate). *Hidden/duplicated:* lot events, tool waits, batch and shipment partner waits — a single 12:05 cure is triplicated across J1–J3. *Misleading:* "no job exceeds 1.8 SD" while J1 sits 5 h 55 min between pack and dispatch; the median `PackDispatch` of 1.1 h conceals a 5h55 tail.

**Machine.** *Visible:* utilization (54/44/58%), sequences, event gaps. *Hidden:* why CNC-2 idled 10:05–10:47 (FIX-A held elsewhere) and why OVEN-1 idled 12:55–14:50 (J5 not ready, RACK-7 quarantined). *Duplicated:* one cure counted as one machine event though it serves three jobs. *Misleading:* "no severe bottleneck" — and the 73-min reported gap contradicts the 115-min observed idle.

**Batch.** *Visible:* membership, cycle times (B1 starts 12:05, B2 14:50). *Hidden:* the readiness spread within a batch (08:10–11:55 for B1, a 3 h 45 min spread) and that both batches share RACK-7 and OVEN-1. *Misleading:* an acceptable batch cycle time alongside ~9 h of member waiting.

**Tool.** *Visible:* hold durations (FIX-A 160 min) and acquirer queues. *Hidden:* the co-scheduled machine (CNC-2 free at 10:05) and downstream shipment consequences. *Misleading:* FIX-A looks only moderately busy while costing J4 ~1 h 50 min.

**ComponentLot.** *Visible:* quarantine 10:20–13:50, membership. *Hidden:* job consequences — L8 has no quarantine event shown, making L7 look like an isolated outlier. *Misleading:* "lot released within a normal shift," while J5's entire B2 chain pivots on it.

**Shipment.** *Visible:* CreateLoad 0.8 h, LoadDispatch 0.3 h. *Hidden:* J1's packed-to-dispatch wait of 5 h 55 min, because partner readiness is not an event. *Misleading:* a fast, healthy shipment process.

**Why low utilization coexists with long waiting.** Utilization measures machine busy-time over calendar; the dominant waits occur *upstream of machine entry* (batch gate, lot gate, tool gate) or *downstream* (shipment partner). Machines idle precisely because complements are missing — the constraint is availability of the *set* {component, tool, rack, partner job, partner shipment}, not of any single machine. Worse, waiting that is never logged does not appear as queue time at all, inflating apparent idle time and deflating apparent flow time.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**Narrative A — FIX-A  CNC-2  B1  OVEN-1/RACK-7  S1.**
*Temporal precedence:* J3 holds FIX-A 09:00–11:40; J3 ready for B1 at 11:55; B1 cures 12:05; J1 packed 13:30; J4, blocked by the same fixture, starts CNC-2 at 11:45 and packs at 19:10; S1 dispatches 19:25. *Resource feasibility:* OVEN-1 was idle 12:55–14:50 and CNC-2 idle 10:05–10:47, so neither machine lacked capacity at the relevant moments. *Policy constraints:* batch completeness (all three members) and shipment consolidation (J1+J4) both act as hard gates. *Causal speculation:* fixture contention may have made J3 the last member of B1, which delayed J1's cure, which — together with J4's independent fixture delay — converged on S1. Two distinct couplings, one shipment.

**Narrative B — L7  C2/C5  J2/J5  B1/B2  OVEN-1  S2/S3.**
*Precedence:* quarantine 10:20, release 13:50, J5 ready 14:40, B2 14:50. *Feasibility:* components existed throughout; only permission was withdrawn. *Policy:* supervisor override admitted J2 into B1 despite the quarantine, so the same lot event delayed one job and not the other — genealogically inconsistent product. *Speculation:* the quarantine may share a root cause with the 2 observed rework loops; unproven.

**Narrative C — CAL-3  TEST-1  J5/J6  S3.**
*Precedence:* J5 tests 16:10–16:35; recalibration 16:35–17:20; J6 tests 17:25–17:50; TEST-1 idle throughout the recalibration; S3 dispatches 35 min after its last-packed job. *Feasibility:* TEST-1 was free for the entire 45 min window. *Policy:* `Final Test` requires a valid CAL-3 state — a metrology rule, not a capacity limit. *Speculation:* if calibration were scheduled off-shift, J6 would pack ~45 min earlier and S3 would dispatch correspondingly earlier — only if S3's clock is set by J6.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

**Observations that would weaken the top-ranked (batch-synchronization) explanation.**

1. Evidence that OVEN-1 was occupied by another batch before 12:05 — then machine availability, not membership, gated B1.
2. Proof that the cure is metallurgically indivisible (all three components must cure together) — the 3 h 40 min is then a requirement, not avoidable waste.
3. Clock or provenance problems with the 08:10/08:25 readiness timestamps (backfilled, different time zone).
4. Demonstration that RACK-7, not membership, was unavailable until 12:00 for B1 (as its cleaning window appears to be for B2).
5. A baseline where other batches show comparable `readystart` waits, making 3 h 55 min unremarkable.
6. Evidence that J3's 11:55 readiness was caused by an upstream rework loop, relocating the root cause one step earlier.
7. Jobs dispatched *without* consolidation showing the same 5 h+ pack-to-dispatch times, which would falsify the shipment-policy ranking too.
8. A direct `Wait for Batch` event showing J1's wait was actually spent waiting for the lot L8, not for J3.

**Additional fields / event types that discriminate among hypotheses.**

1. `Wait_Start` / `Wait_End` with a `reason` code (TOOL, BATCH_MEMBER, LOT, RACK, SHIPMENT_PARTNER, MACHINE).
2. `Tool_Available` and `Tool_Acquire/Release` with holder Job ID (currently only inferable).
3. `Batch_Cutoff_Decision` with policy ID, deciding user, and any partial-batch approval.
4. `Lot_Quarantine` reason code, scope, and `Override_Approval` records in the same system.
5. Shipment `Scheduled_Departure` / freight appointment time, distinguishing partner lateness from carrier lateness.
6. Machine `Unavailable` events (cleaning, planned maintenance) separated from genuine idle.
7. Shift rosters and operator calendars for OVEN-1, TEST-1 and CNC-2.
8. `Component_Kitting/Staging` events, to separate lot permission from physical availability.
9. Calibration policy ID and `Calibration_Due` timestamp for CAL-3.
10. Event-level object ID sets as stored (to audit extraction losses such as the job-source-only rule that dropped the 10:20 and 13:50 lot events).
