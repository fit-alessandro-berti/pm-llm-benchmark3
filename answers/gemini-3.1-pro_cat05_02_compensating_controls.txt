## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 (the four-eyes principle for payments) can be decomposed into the following control objectives and threat scenarios:

1. **Unauthorized/Fraudulent Payee (e.g., Sanctions, Fake Vendor):** An operator attempts to send funds to a disallowed or fabricated vendor.
   - **AV-2:** Prevents (checks sanctions, duplicate invoice, basic PO match).
   - **MG-4:** Prevents/Detects (if performed before settlement, can stop it by reviewing invoice/receipt).
   - **RA-7:** Detects/Partially Corrects (if within the 24-hour recall window).
   - **ED-1:** Fails to address directly (relies on AV-2 and MG-4).
2. **Bank Account Tampering:** An operator modifies the vendor's bank account details to divert funds to a personal account.
   - **AV-2:** Prevents (checks bank-account ownership and recent changes).
   - **MG-4:** Prevents/Detects (if reviewing bank details against master data).
   - **RA-7:** Detects/Partially Corrects.
   - **ED-1:** Prevents (condition 4 explicitly requires no bank-account change in the previous 7 days).
3. **Fictitious/Unreceived Goods or Services:** An operator creates a fake invoice and receipt for goods never delivered.
   - **AV-2:** Fails to address (does not establish goods/services were received).
   - **MG-4:** Prevents/Detects (if reviewing supporting receipt thoroughly).
   - **RA-7:** Detects (audit review of documentation).
   - **ED-1:** Fails to address directly.
4. **Collusion between Preparer and Releaser:** Two operators work together to bypass four-eyes.
   - **AV-2:** Fails to address (does not detect collusion).
   - **MG-4:** Prevents/Detects (independent manager review introduces a non-colluding third party).
   - **RA-7:** Detects (retrospective audit).
   - **ED-1:** Fails to address directly.
5. **Compromised Account/Credential Sharing:** An attacker or another employee uses an operator's account to prepare and release.
   - **AV-2:** Fails to address (does not establish who actually controlled the account).
   - **MG-4:** Prevents/Detects (independent review adds a hurdle).
   - **RA-7:** Detects.
   - **ED-1:** Fails to address directly.
6. **Limit Exceedance/Unauthorized Large Transfer:** An operator attempts to transfer an amount above their authorized limit.
   - **AV-2:** Prevents (checks amount against operator limit).
   - **MG-4:** Prevents/Detects.
   - **RA-7:** Detects.
   - **ED-1:** Prevents (condition 2 requires amount < EUR 100,000).

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

To determine if a compensating control package is sufficient, we apply the following structured test:

- **Fully Equivalent:** The compensating package provides equal or greater risk reduction across all threat scenarios compared to strict FEP-1. This requires strong preventive controls (AV-2 fully operational, no overrides), strict independence (MG-4 by an independent manager before settlement), high evidence quality, full coverage of the payment amount, and resistance to collusion.
- **Partially Equivalent:** Controls address the primary risks but leave minor gaps. For example, MG-4 occurs after release but before settlement (allowing a brief window of internal exposure but preventing external loss), or RA-7 is timely enough to ensure a high probability of recall.
- **Merely Mitigating:** Controls reduce some risk but fail to provide the core protection of FEP-1. For example, MG-4 occurs after settlement, AV-2 is bypassed/overridden, or RA-7 occurs after the recall window.
- **Ineffective:** Controls are bypassed, overridden by conflicted parties, or fail to operate entirely (e.g., AV-2 unavailable, MG-4 by a conflicted supervisor, RA-7 severely delayed).

**Why “same expected loss” is not the only possible standard:**
Expected loss focuses purely on financial outcomes (Probability × Financial Impact). However, control objectives also encompass regulatory compliance, deterrence, auditability, and operational integrity. A process might have a low expected loss due to sheer luck or low transaction volumes, but still represent a severe control failure that violates internal policies. Furthermore, "same expected loss" ignores the qualitative difference between preventing fraud (maintaining integrity) and recovering from it (which incurs operational costs, legal friction, and reputational damage).

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control | Type | Timing | Action/Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| AV-2 | Preventive | Before Release | Blocks or warns based on automated rules (sanctions, duplicates, limits). |
| MG-4 (Pre-release) | Preventive | Before Release | Independent manager reviews evidence and approves, preventing unauthorized release. |
| MG-4 (Post-release, Pre-settlement) | Hybrid | Between Release and Settlement | Detects unauthorized release and prevents loss by stopping bank settlement. |
| MG-4 (Post-settlement) | Detective | After Settlement | Identifies unauthorized payments after funds have left the organization. |
| RA-7 (Within 24h) | Hybrid | Post-settlement (<24h) | Detects anomalies and triggers corrective action (bank recall) before funds are unrecoverable. |
| RA-7 (After 24h) | Detective | Post-settlement (>24h) | Identifies anomalies after funds are likely unrecoverable. |

**Semantics of Post-Release, Pre-Settlement Manager Action:**
A manager action occurring after `Release_Payment` but before `Bank_Settlement` (e.g., AP-201, AP-207) is a **hybrid (detective-preventive)** control. 
- It is *detective* with respect to the internal release process, as it identifies that an operator has already executed the release step.
- It is *preventive* with respect to the actual loss event (funds leaving the company), because the manager can intervene and cancel the transfer before the irrevocable bank settlement occurs.
In financial controls, the ultimate objective is preventing unauthorized loss; therefore, pre-settlement interventions effectively serve a preventive function against the primary risk, even if they occur late in the internal workflow.

## 5. Counterfactual and Residual-Risk Analysis

1. **AP-202 (EUR 480k, New vendor, Bank changed):**
   - **Plausible loss mechanism:** Fraudulent diversion of funds to an employee's relative via a recent bank account change.
   - **Control breaking the chain:** None successfully broke the chain (AV-2 warned but was overridden).
   - **Remains exposed:** The entire EUR 480,000 was lost.
   - **Counterfactual:** A truly independent second releaser would have seen the AV-2 warning regarding the bank change and operator limit, investigated the new vendor, and refused to release, preventing the loss.
2. **AP-204 (EUR 220k, Fabricated receipt):**
   - **Plausible loss mechanism:** Payment for fictitious services via a fabricated receipt, likely involving collusion.
   - **Control breaking the chain:** RA-7 (Audit) detected the fabricated receipt, but too late to prevent loss.
   - **Remains exposed:** EUR 220,000 (recovery uncertain after 4 days).
   - **Counterfactual:** A *truly* independent second releaser (not sharing a workstation or colluding) would have scrutinized the receipt, identified it as fabricated, and rejected the payment.
3. **AP-205 (EUR 12k, Duplicate invoice):**
   - **Plausible loss mechanism:** Accidental double-payment for the same goods/services.
   - **Control breaking the chain:** AV-2 blocked the duplicate before release.
   - **Remains exposed:** None.
   - **Counterfactual:** An independent second releaser might have also noticed the duplicate, but AV-2 handled it faster and more reliably. The outcome would be the same (payment prevented).
4. **AP-207 (EUR 35k, Safety repair, Emergency):**
   - **Plausible loss mechanism:** Unauthorized payment disguised as an emergency.
   - **Control breaking the chain:** MG-4 (pre-settlement ratification) and AV-2 (full pass) verified the legitimacy.
   - **Remains exposed:** Negligible (valid vendor, valid emergency).
   - **Counterfactual:** An independent second releaser would have added a slight delay to a genuine safety repair. The risk outcome would be identical, but operational speed would have decreased.
5. **AP-208 (EUR 900k, Bank changed 3 days prior, AV-2 down):**
   - **Plausible loss mechanism:** Fraudulent diversion of a massive payment to a recently changed bank account while automated checks were offline.
   - **Control breaking the chain:** None. Manual checklist was ignored, and manager ratified post-settlement.
   - **Remains exposed:** EUR 900,000 (high unrecoverable exposure).
   - **Counterfactual:** A truly independent second releaser would have enforced the manual checklist since AV-2 was down, noticed the recent bank change, and demanded proof of bank-account ownership before releasing.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

**Monitoring Rules & Metrics:**
1. **Same-User Rate:** Track the percentage of payments where Preparer == Releaser. Alert if this exceeds a low threshold (e.g., 5%) for any given department, indicating abuse of ED-1.
2. **Override Rate:** Monitor the frequency of AV-2 overrides. Alert immediately on any override involving a recent bank change or new vendor.
3. **Late Manager Approval:** Flag cases where MG-4 occurs after `Bank_Settlement`. This is a severe control failure.
4. **Audit-Before-Recall-Window:** Measure the percentage of RA-7 audits completed within 24 hours of release. Target should be 100% for ED-1 cases.
5. **Repeated Resource Pairs:** Track the concentration of preparer-releaser pairs. Alert on highly concentrated pairs (e.g., u12/u13) to investigate potential collusion.
6. **Shared Workstations:** Cross-reference IP addresses or terminal IDs for preparers and releasers. Alert if distinct users approve from the same terminal within a short timeframe.
7. **Bank-Change Proximity:** Alert on any payment released within 7 days of a bank account change, regardless of FEP-1 conformance.
8. **Control Outages:** Alert immediately if AV-2 is offline. Enforce a hard stop on payments > EUR 100,000 during outages unless a manual checklist is signed by two independent directors.

**Alert vs. Proof of Misconduct:**
An alert (e.g., a repeated resource pair or a same-user release) is merely a signal that a transaction deviates from the standard low-risk path. It warrants investigation but is not proof of misconduct. Proof of misconduct requires substantive evidence of intent to defraud or bypass controls, such as fabricated receipts (AP-204), unauthorized use of another's credentials (AP-202), or deliberate circumvention of manual checklists during outages (AP-208).
