## 2. Process-model consistency checks

A credible validation suite should be run before any global or local explanation is released. The goal is not only to detect obvious future leakage, but also to classify each explanation term as available, ambiguous, future-possible, process-inconsistent, or inadmissible.

### 2.1 Establish a reliable `t0` and prefix

1. Identify the first `Coverage Check Completed` event for each case.
2. Do not rely only on timestamps. Use a reliable sequence index, transaction identifier, or causal ordering field.
3. Define `t0` as the moment immediately after that event.
4. Build a prefix log containing only events with sequence index at or before the `t0` event and with timestamps not after `t0`, subject to a documented and conservative tolerance.
5. If multiple events have identical timestamps and no sequence index, mark the ordering as ambiguous and quarantine order-dependent features.

**Limitation:** If the source system does not store sequence indices, some same-timestamp cases cannot be safely ordered. In those cases, excluding directed patterns is safer than guessing.

### 2.2 Prefix-time feature availability checks

For every feature used in training or explanation, enforce point-in-time correctness:

- Static registration attributes: registration timestamp must be before `t0`.
- Snapshot features: snapshot timestamp must be at or before `t0`. For `queue_length_at_t0`, use the last valid snapshot before `t0`, not a nightly or post-`t0` refresh.
- Mutable scores: require a versioned `score_valid_at` timestamp. `risk_score_current` is invalid unless it can be reconstructed as `risk_score_at_t0`.
- Resource/team features: require assignment intervals, such as `team_assigned_from <= t0 < team_assigned_to`.
- Document counts: count only documents received or validated before `t0`.
- Prior claim counts: count only other claims with `claim_created_at < t0`, excluding the current claim.
- Reserve values: require an effective timestamp showing the reserve was set before or at `t0`.

A feature without a trustworthy temporal anchor should be treated as unusable for early prediction.

### 2.3 Activity-order feasibility and enabled-state classification

At the normative prediction point, the observed prefix is:

`Claim Registered -> Coverage Check Completed`

From that state:

- Enabled or immediately possible next activities:
  - `Damage Assessment Completed` for low-complexity fast track.
  - `Documents Requested` for evidence cycles.
  - `Fraud Investigation Opened` only if a fraud trigger is already known at or before `t0`.
- Future-possible but not yet observable:
  - `Documents Received`.
  - `Documents Validated`.
  - `Damage Assessment Completed` in the normal sequence.
  - `Settlement Decision Recorded`.
  - `Claim Closed`.
- Process-impossible at `t0` under the normative model:
  - `Appeal`, because it requires a prior Partial or Deny decision.
  - A second `Settlement Decision Recorded` caused by appeal.
  - `Claim Closed` before any decision or withdrawal.

Every explanation term should be mapped to this enabled/impossible/future-possible classification. An explanation for a `t0` prediction should not refer to future-only or process-impossible activities.

### 2.4 Same-timestamp ambiguity checks

For events with identical timestamps:

1. Require a sequence index, ingestion order, transaction ID, or causal dependency.
2. If no reliable ordering exists, do not infer a directed relationship.
3. Treat patterns such as `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` as either:
   - a data-quality violation,
   - a timestamp-granularity artifact,
   - or a same-timestamp tie that cannot be directionally interpreted.

Footprint matrices can show that two activities occur close in time, but they cannot safely establish direction when timestamps tie.

### 2.5 Complete-trace detection

Use both static and dynamic checks:

- Static naming/code scan: flag features named `total`, `final`, `last`, `anywhere`, `current`, or `full_trace` unless they have explicit as-of semantics.
- Dynamic prefix comparison: compute both the full-trace value and the prefix-only value. If they differ, the feature is using future information.
- Variant checks: `full_trace_variant_id` must be replaced by `prefix_variant_at_t0` for early prediction.

### 2.6 Repeated `Settlement Decision Recorded` validation

Because the activity label is coarse, repeated settlement decision events can be legitimate. The validation suite should:

1. Distinguish decision instances by sequence index, not only by activity name.
2. Check that an appeal is preceded by a Partial or Deny decision.
3. Check that a second decision after appeal occurs after the appeal event.
4. Flag cases where:
   - a second decision appears without an appeal,
   - an appeal follows Approve or Withdrawn,
   - decision order cannot be resolved because of identical timestamps.

These checks are semantic and temporal. Even when a repeated decision pattern is semantically legitimate, it remains inadmissible for a prediction made at `t0`.

### 2.7 Role and limitations of DFG, alignment, footprint, prefix, and declarative checks

- **Directly-follows graph (DFG):** Descriptive, not normative. A frequent edge is not automatically a valid process rule. The impossible-looking `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` edge may be frequent because of timestamp ties or logging artifacts.
- **Alignment checks:** Compare observed traces against the normative model. They can detect deviations such as closure before damage assessment, but they do not by themselves prove leakage or causality.
- **Footprint checks:** Compare prefix behavior with full-trace behavior. Useful for detecting whether an explanation depends on future events.
- **Prefix checks:** Ensure that only events up to `t0` are used. These are essential for temporal validity.
- **Declarative constraints:** Encode rules such as precedence, response, non-coexistence, and appeal conditions. They help classify feasibility but depend on the correctness and completeness of the normative model.

A frequent DFG edge, even a statistically strong one, should never be treated as an admissible early predictor if it violates `t0` availability or normative ordering.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

Different standards apply:

- **Predictive validity:** the feature is available at `t0` and statistically associated with the target.
- **Causal interpretation:** requires evidence that changing the feature would change the outcome, typically through experimentation, intervention analysis, or strong causal identification.
- **Operational actionability:** requires a controllable lever, a policy, and an understanding of side effects.
- **Fairness review:** a feature can be temporally valid and predictive but still be a proxy for protected or sensitive attributes and therefore require governance before use.

| Class | Surviving or repairable examples | What may be claimed | What may not be claimed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defensible predictive signal | `initial_damage_complexity`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, repaired `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, verified `initial_reserve_at_t0`, verified `fraud_trigger_at_t0`, prefix document counts if any existed before `t0` | The feature is associated with higher or lower breach risk and can support risk triage or prioritization. | The feature caused the breach, guarantees breach or non-breach, or alone justifies an adverse decision. |
| Process-state or congestion signal | `queue_length_at_t0`, repaired staffing/load snapshots at `t0` | The claim entered a congested or uncongested operational state; this may help explain predicted risk and support operational monitoring. | Queue length alone caused this claim’s breach, or the claim should be denied/deprioritized solely because of queue conditions. Causal effects require intervention analysis. |
| Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review | `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, derived repair-network/language/socioeconomic proxies | The feature may be statistically predictive and can be retained temporarily only with disclosure, fairness testing, robustness checks, and human oversight. | The feature is an inherent delay driver, a causal reason, or an acceptable standalone operational reason for adverse treatment. |
| Drift/calendar proxy | `calendar_week`, holiday indicators, operating-policy-change indicators | Certain periods may have higher operational risk due to holidays, staffing, or policy changes. The feature can support monitoring and calibration. | The week itself intrinsically causes breach, or the effect is stable over time without drift monitoring. |
| Unusable until lineage is repaired | `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_handler_team`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, future sequence patterns, `final_decision`, `risk_score_current`, `last_resource_role`, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, defective `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, `employee_experience` joined by extraction date | These may be used for post-hoc closed-case analysis if clearly separated from early prediction. | They may not be used in `t0` predictions or explanations until point-in-time lineage is repaired and the model is recomputed. |

Repairable features can move into the defensible or process-state classes only after temporal reconstruction. For example, `prior_claim_count_before_t0` becomes defensible if computed strictly before `t0`; `risk_score_current` becomes defensible only if a versioned `risk_score_at_t0` exists.
