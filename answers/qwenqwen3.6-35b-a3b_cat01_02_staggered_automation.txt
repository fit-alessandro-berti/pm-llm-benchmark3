The primary staggered DiD with cohort-specific trends is preferable because it explicitly addresses the non-random, phased rollout and allows each cohort to have its own pre-adoption trajectory, which is critical given that Alpha/Beta and Gamma started from different baseline performances. By weighting cohort-specific parallel trends rather than pooling them into a single two-way fixed effects model, it mitigates the negative-weight bias that arises when treatment effects vary over time or across groups. This design directly targets the ITT effect of availability while accommodating the staggered timing. The synthetic control alternative is fragile here due to only two donor branches and a major May product change that disrupts linear trends. The weighted ITS alternative relies heavily on correct functional form specification and cannot easily disentangle automation from concurrent routing and staffing shifts. All three strategies require strong assumptions about unmeasured confounding, but the staggered DiD offers the most transparent framework for diagnostic testing and sensitivity analysis given the panel structure. Its main limitation remains the small number of clusters, which necessitates cluster-robust standard errors and cautious inference.

## 10. Final claim ladder
- Observed: Automation availability coincides with increased fast-path usage (25–50%), rising case complexity, and system-wide upward trends in cycle times, rework, and escalation across all branches.
- Suggestive: Within adopter branches, higher automated fast-path intensity descriptively correlates with slightly lower median cycle times but higher p90 tails, indicating fast-path substitution that may shift queue pressure to specialist review.
- Conditionally causal: If non-adopters’ pre-adoption trends approximate the counterfactual for adopters, automation likely mitigated median cycle time growth by 2–4 hours relative to what would have occurred without it, though this depends on stable parallel trends and no unmeasured routing confounders.
- Not supported: A blanket statement that “automation reduces cycle time by X%” is not defensible given the overlapping time trends, treatment intensity variation, staffing and complexity shifts, and unmeasured spillovers through the fraud desk and specialist queues.
