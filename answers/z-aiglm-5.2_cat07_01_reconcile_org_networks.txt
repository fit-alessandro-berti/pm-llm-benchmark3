# 3. Risk assessment

## Bottleneck risk — **Moderate to High**

Mira is not a processing-time bottleneck (4.2 min median) but is a *structural throughput bottleneck* for exception cases. The absence data provides direct evidence: exception cases accumulated and tail cycle time rose 65% (8.4  13.9 days). However, this risk is specific to the exception stream; ordinary claims are unaffected. The risk is that exception volume grows (e.g., due to regulatory change or new claim types) and her single-person capacity becomes insufficient even when she is present.

## Coordination risk — **High**

Mira's handover betweenness (0.77) means a large fraction of exception-case paths pass through her. If her routing decisions are inconsistent — or if she routes to specialists unevenly — coordination quality degrades. The subcontracting patterns show she delegates and reintegrates, but we have no evidence of a formal routing protocol. The compliance reviewer's concern about uncontrolled discretion is a coordination-governance risk, not necessarily a performance risk.

## Key-person risk — **High**

This is the most clearly evidenced risk. No other resource has a comparable activity profile or network position. The 6-day absence already demonstrated measurable degradation. Key-person risk is not about whether Mira is good at her job — it is about whether the process can tolerate her unavailability. Currently, it cannot, for exception cases.

## Compliance/control risk — **Moderate, unverified**

The compliance reviewer worries that Mira both detects problems and selects the next specialist, creating uncontrolled discretion. This is a *latent* compliance risk: it does not imply wrongdoing, but it means routing decisions are not governed by auditable rules. In a regulated claims process, this could attract scrutiny. The risk is compounded by the fact that her routing decisions are not visible as formal "assignment rules" in the event log — we see the *outcome* (she hands to Omar or Priya) but not the *rationale*.

## Workload fairness risk — **Moderate**

Mira touches 22% of cases but 64% of reopened claims and 71% of complaints. Her exception_flag share is 48%. This concentration is not necessarily unfair, but it means her workload is disproportionately complex and emotionally taxing (complaints, reopens). If exception volume spikes, she has little buffer. Additionally, the specialists she routes to (Omar, Priya) also have high exception_flag shares (62%, 71%), suggesting the exception sub-process is concentrated across a small set of resources.

## Data-quality risk — **Low to Moderate**

Mira's events are short (4.2 min) and include "Assign Specialist," which may or may not be a distinct logged activity vs. an implicit routing action. If some of her routing decisions are not logged (e.g., informal messages, phone calls), the handover network underestimates her true centrality. Conversely, the "Assign Specialist" activity may create duplicate or ambiguous handover edges if it is logged as both an activity and a resource transition. This is a data-completeness and data-semantics risk, not a performance risk.

# 5. Recommendation

## What should be changed immediately

1. **Assign a named backup for the exception desk.** The absence data proves the process cannot tolerate Mira's unavailability for exception cases. A backup does not need to replicate her full skill set immediately but must be able to receive Bot_Rules exception routing and perform basic triage (Request Missing Information, Assign Specialist). This is an operational resilience fix, not a structural redesign.

2. **Log Mira's routing decisions with case attributes.** Since the compliance reviewer has flagged uncontrolled discretion, management should ensure that when Mira performs "Assign Specialist," the case attributes driving the assignment (case_type, region, amount_band, exception type) are captured. This does not change her behavior but creates an auditable trail.

## What should be monitored

1. **Mira's handover betweenness over time.** If it is rising, exception volume is growing faster than capacity, and the key-person risk is worsening. Track this monthly.

2. **Exception-case tail cycle time (90th percentile).** The 8.4-day baseline should be tracked. Any sustained increase above ~10 days without a corresponding increase in exception volume suggests emerging coordination strain.

3. **Routing distribution to specialists and adjusters.** Track whether Mira's assignments to Omar, Priya, Carla, Diego, Elise, and Farid are proportional to their capacity or show skew. If one specialist is consistently over-assigned, coordination quality may degrade.

4. **Working-together co-occurrence between the backup and Mira.** Once a backup is in place, monitor whether cases increasingly co-occur with both, indicating knowledge transfer.

## What should not be changed until more evidence is available

1. **Do not remove or replace Mira_Coordinator.** The operations manager's argument is supported by the absence data: removing her would increase formal escalations and specialist overload, and the 6-day absence already demonstrated measurable harm. The coordination function she provides is real and structurally embedded.

2. **Do not restructure the North/South team boundaries based on Mira's cross-team routing.** Her cross-team handovers could reflect either a design flaw (she should not cross team boundaries) or a deliberate exception-handling feature (exceptions are inherently cross-team). The current data cannot distinguish these without qualitative investigation.

3. **Do not automate her routing decisions yet.** The Bot_RulesMiraBot_Rules subcontracting pattern suggests she feeds back into the rules engine, but we do not know the content of that feedback. Automating her decisions without understanding them could degrade exception handling and increase compliance risk.
