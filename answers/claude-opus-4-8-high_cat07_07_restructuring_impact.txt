# 1. Mechanism map

The restructuring is a set of structural interventions. Each one propagates through the organization into measurable process-mining signals. The map below links each action to intermediate mechanisms and then to observable event-log measures.

**Restructuring action → mechanism → observable PM measure**

- **Merge N_Tech/S_Tech/E_Tech into a central Technical Shared Service (TSS)**
  - *Nominal handover reduction*: The old "local caseworker → embedded tech specialist → same local caseworker" loop (36+31 per 100 cases, short 1.6–3.2h waits) collapses into a single node in the resource network. In the **handover-of-work network** the many local caseworker→local-tech edges are replaced by a few high-frequency caseworker→TSS edges. Edge *count* drops; edge *weight concentration* rises. This is the "fewer handovers" management sees, but it is partly an artifact of relabeling three local resources as one shared resource.
  - *Central queue formation*: Because all high-complexity cases plus legal/risk triage plus difficult Quality Check funnel into one queue, TSS becomes a convergence point. Observable via **discover_performance_dfg** (long sojourn on edges into "Specialist Review"/TSS activities), **insert_case_service_waiting_time** (rising waiting-time component), and a **working-together network** where TSS resources co-occur with nearly every case.
  - *Specialist utilization*: Pooling raises utilization (management expects +15%). Measurable as fraction of active time per specialist resource. But utilization near 85–95% is exactly where queueing theory predicts non-linear wait blow-ups (see risk section).

- **Centralize Specialist/Legal/Risk Review and difficult Quality Check into the same queue**
  - *Central queue formation (compounded)*: One queue now serves several distinct workloads. In the DFG this appears as multiple activity types feeding one resource cluster. **Activity-based resource similarity** will show TSS resources becoming similar to each other and dissimilar from local caseworkers — i.e., a sharpening org-role boundary.
  - *Legal/Risk already at 91–93% utilization*: Adding broader detection (12 vs 9 per 100 cases) to an already saturated resource is the single most dangerous input. Expect the highest tail-latency growth here (**90th/99th percentile cycle time**), even if the median barely moves.

- **Keep Intake and simple Eligibility local**
  - *Local expertise loss (partial protection)*: Intake stays local so early customer/regional context is preserved at the front end, but the *technical* early informal advice (previously given by embedded tech) disappears. Measurable as change in **subcontracting network** (previously local tech "helped" mid-case without a formal handover; that informal help vanishes) and as a rise in specialist *referral* frequency because local caseworkers can no longer self-resolve borderline cases.

- **Shared-service routing rules (all high-complexity → TSS; specialty∈{legal,risk,security} → shared queue; pull advice from shared queue)**
  - *Rework*: Standardized routing can reduce mis-routing rework, but "advice returned through a queue" introduces asynchronous, context-lossy handoffs. Rework after a shared-queue handover is likely to rise for complex cases (loss of the tight local loop that had 8% rework). Measurable via **get_rework_cases_per_activity** segmented by case_complexity and by whether the case passed through TSS.
  - *Fairness across teams/segments*: A single routing rule applies uniform priority, so customers needing language/regional accommodation lose the local advocate. Measurable as divergence in cycle time and rework **by customer_segment** and **by location** after the change.

- **Remove one local supervisor; create shared approval queue for medium/high-value cases**
  - *Role clarity / accountability*: Approval moves from a named local supervisor to a pooled queue. In **organizational role discovery** the "Supervisor Approval" role that used to map cleanly to one resource per team now maps to a diffuse pool. Ownership per case becomes ambiguous. Measurable as a rise in **reassignment_count**, more distinct approvers per case, and lower resource-role purity in role discovery.
  - *Central queue formation (second queue)*: Approval is now also a shared queue (49 handovers in, 3.0h wait), a second potential bottleneck stacked after the first.

- **Cross-cutting mechanism: resilience to absence**
  - Pooling *improves* resilience for routine load (any TSS member can take a case; the old poor cross-team cover at 8.9h/18% rework should improve, and cross-team handovers drop 6→3). But it *reduces* resilience to correlated shocks: if the single TSS queue is understaffed or one specialty owner is absent, the whole organization stalls rather than one team. Measurable as sensitivity of queue length to resource availability (waiting time vs. number of active TSS resources per day).

**Summary of directional expectations**

| Dimension | Likely direction | Primary observable |
|---|---|---|
| Nominal handovers | ↓ (partly artifact) | handover network edge count |
| Central queue / waiting | ↑ at TSS & approval | performance DFG, service/waiting time |
| Specialist utilization | ↑ (+~15%) | active-time fraction per resource |
| Rework (complex cases) | ↑ risk | rework per activity × complexity |
| Local expertise | ↓ over time | fewer self-resolved cases, ↑ referral rate |
| Role clarity | ↓ | role-discovery purity, reassignment_count |
| Resilience (routine) | ↑ | cross-team handover ↓, absence-cover wait ↓ |
| Resilience (shock) | ↓ | queue-length sensitivity to staffing |
| Fairness | ↓ risk | cycle/rework variance by segment/location |

# 2. Scenario forecasts

### Optimistic scenario ("pooling dominates")

- **Handover pattern**: Handover network simplifies as intended. Local loop edges disappear; caseworker→TSS and caseworker→approval-queue edges are high-weight but stable. Cross-team handovers fall 6→3 per 100 and their rework rate improves because notes are now standardized in a shared queue rather than ad-hoc.
- **Queue/waiting**: TSS and approval queues stay below ~80% utilization. Waiting-time component (from insert_case_service_waiting_time) rises modestly, matching management's estimate (tech wait 3.2h→~4.0h round-trip within tolerance).
- **Cycle-time distribution**: Median improves slightly, especially for East (E_Tech was overloaded at 82% and is relieved). 90th percentile roughly flat. Distribution shape stays uni-modal.
- **Rework/quality**: Overall rework flat or slightly down because routing corrects prior mis-referrals; standardized Quality Check catches defects earlier. No complexity-segment blow-up.
- **Role clarity/accountability**: Shared approval documented with clear ownership fields; reassignment_count stable. Role discovery still shows coherent (if larger) TSS and approval roles.
- **Fairness**: Segment/location variance stable; regional accommodation handled via metadata routing so no systematic penalty.
- **Early warning signs (that would still bear watching)**: Even here, watch TSS utilization creeping past 85%, and any early rise in complex-case tail latency.

### Central scenario ("mixed, most likely")

- **Handover pattern**: Nominal handovers drop as reported, but a portion is relabeling. New caseworker→TSS→caseworker round-trips appear with longer, more variable waits. A subtle rise in *repeat* handovers to TSS for the same case (context re-explanation) shows up as self-loops/back-edges in the DFG.
- **Queue/waiting**: TSS utilization rises toward 88–92% because it absorbs high-complexity + legal/risk triage + difficult QC simultaneously. Waiting time grows more than management's estimate for the *upper* portion of the distribution while the median stays close to plan. Legal/Risk (already 91–93%) becomes the hardest constraint; their queue waits (10.5h estimate) realize higher in practice.
- **Cycle-time distribution**: Median roughly unchanged or slightly better (East improves, North/South slightly worse). The distribution becomes **right-skewed / bimodal**: a fast lane for simple local cases and a slow lane for anything touching the shared queue. 90th/99th percentile worsen even as the mean looks fine.
- **Rework/quality**: Simple-case rework flat; **complex-case rework rises** because the tight local expert loop (8% rework) is replaced by asynchronous queue advice. get_rework_cases_per_activity segmented by complexity shows the divergence.
- **Role clarity/accountability**: Diffuse. reassignment_count rises for approval-queue cases; more distinct approvers per case; ownership disputes on failed cases. Role-discovery purity for the approval role drops.
- **Fairness**: Emerging gap by customer_segment (language/regional cases accrue extra handovers) and by location (North/South lose local expertise advantage; East gains). Variance widens even if aggregate mean is stable.
- **Early warning signs**: TSS/approval waiting time p90 climbing while median flat; rising reassignment_count; growth in caseworker→TSS back-edges; specialist utilization pinned >90%; complex-case rework ticking up; segment cycle-time variance widening.

### Adverse scenario ("central bottleneck dominates")

- **Handover pattern**: Handover *count* looks great to management, masking a severe funnel. The working-together network collapses onto a small TSS+approval core that touches almost every case — a structural single point of failure.
- **Queue/waiting**: TSS and Legal/Risk queues saturate (>95%). Small load increases produce large wait spikes (queueing non-linearity). Waiting-time is now the dominant component of cycle time. Backlogs form and persist across days.
- **Cycle-time distribution**: Median may *still* look acceptable because simple local cases are fast and numerous, but the tail explodes: 90th percentile and especially 99th percentile balloon. Strongly bimodal.
- **Rework/quality**: Complex-case rework rises sharply; difficult Quality Check in the shared queue is rushed under backlog pressure, so escaped defects (later rework, reopened cases) increase. rework_flag rate for TSS-touched cases climbs.
- **Role clarity/accountability**: Ownership breaks down. Cases bounce between pool members; reassignment_count spikes; no single accountable approver; blame diffuses. Role discovery no longer finds clean roles.
- **Fairness**: Clear inequities. High-priority or well-documented segments get pulled from the shared queue faster; language/regional and low-priority segments wait longest. North/South customer satisfaction drops as local expertise erodes; East improves — producing a *cross-team fairness inversion* that management may misread as "East got better."
- **Early warning signs**: Sustained TSS utilization >92%; monotonically growing backlog; p99 cycle time diverging from median; complex-case rework up >2–3 points; reassignment_count sharply up; satisfaction dropping in North/South; a widening segment/location gap; increasing "advice returned but re-queued" loops.

# 3. Leading and lagging indicators

Leading indicators are computable from the event log within days–weeks (they concern *flow*, not final outcomes). Lagging indicators require outcomes to accumulate (weeks–months).

| Indicator | Leading / Lagging | PM measure / source | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| TSS & approval-queue waiting time (median and p90) | Leading | insert_case_service_waiting_time, performance DFG edges into TSS | Early bottleneck detection |
| Specialist/queue utilization (active-time fraction) | Leading | resource active time from org:resource + timestamps | >85–90% signals impending non-linear waits |
| Handover network edge count & weight concentration | Leading | discover_handover_of_work_network | Confirms funneling onto TSS core |
| Back-edges / self-loops caseworker↔TSS per case | Leading | discover_dfg, variants | Context-loss re-queries rising |
| reassignment_count per case; distinct approvers per case | Leading | attribute + resource distinct-count | Role-clarity / accountability erosion |
| Working-together network centralization | Leading | discover_working_together_network | Single-point-of-failure formation |
| Variant count and drift (new dominant variants) | Leading | get_variants, filter_variants | Process reshaping into fast/slow lanes |
| Queue-length sensitivity to daily staffing | Leading | daily active-resource vs. wait | Resilience-to-shock proxy |
| Cycle-time **distribution** (median, p90, p99), per complexity | Lagging | extract_temporal_features_dataframe, performance DFG | Tail growth vs. mean stability |
| Rework rate per activity, per complexity, per TSS-touch | Lagging | get_rework_cases_per_activity, rework_flag | Quality degradation in complex cases |
| SLA miss rate, per team/segment/location | Lagging | outcome + timestamps vs. SLA | Fairness and service degradation |
| Local-expertise proxy: self-resolved rate / referral rate | Lagging | share of cases without specialist handover, local_expertise_flag | Learning/expertise erosion |
| Escaped defects / reopened-close rate | Lagging | Close→Rework loops in DFG | Rushed Quality Check under backlog |
| Customer satisfaction by team/segment | Lagging | survey joined to cases | Fairness / expertise-loss impact |
| Cross-team fairness gap (cycle/rework variance by location & segment) | Lagging | segmented temporal features | Distributional fairness |
| Role-discovery purity | Lagging | discover_organizational_roles | Stability of role structure post-change |

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

A stable or improved *mean* can coexist with serious harm. At least six such risks:

1. **Tail latency (p90/p99).** A large mass of fast simple local cases can hold the mean flat while a minority of shared-queue cases wait far longer. The mean is dominated by volume; the tail is where SLA breaches, escalations, and customer anger live. Always report the full distribution and percentiles, not the average.
2. **High-complexity cases.** These are exactly the ones routed to the saturated TSS/Legal/Risk queues. Their cycle time and rework can worsen materially while their small count keeps the aggregate average unchanged. Segment by case_complexity and required_specialty.
3. **Local knowledge loss (learning).** Removing embedded tech ends informal early advice and self-resolution. This shows first as *fewer self-resolved cases* and *higher referral rates*, not as slower averages. Over months it degrades local caseworker capability — a latent risk the mean cannot see.
4. **Fairness across segments/teams.** Aggregate mean hides distributional inequity: language/regional and low-priority segments may absorb the extra handovers and tail waits, and North/South may lose while East gains. Variance and per-segment/per-location breakdowns are required.
5. **Accountability / role clarity.** Pooled approval and pooled specialists blur ownership. reassignment_count and distinct-approver counts can rise with no change in mean cycle time, yet decision quality, appeal handling, and error correction all suffer.
6. **Exception handling / resilience to shock.** The mean under normal load says nothing about behavior when the central queue is understaffed or a specialty owner is absent. A single-point funnel can be fast on average yet catastrophic under a correlated shock — visible only in queue-length sensitivity and backlog dynamics, not the mean.

(Additional, related) **Escaped-defect quality risk**: difficult Quality Check done under backlog pressure produces defects that surface *later* as reopened/rework cases — a delayed signal the current-period average entirely misses.

# 5. Pilot design

**Pilot scope.** Do not roll out fully. Run a phased pilot that isolates the highest-leverage, lowest-regret change first. Suggested Phase 1: centralize only the **Technical Shared Service** (merge N_/S_/E_Tech) and the **shared approval queue** for a single region, while *keeping* Legal/Risk local-plus-referral unchanged (they are already saturated at 91–93% and are the most fragile input). Begin with **East**, where E_Tech is overloaded (82%) and expected to benefit, plus one strong-expertise team (e.g., **North**) to expose expertise-loss risk. Keep **South** on the current model as long as possible.

**Comparison group.** Use South (unchanged) as a concurrent control. Additionally use each pilot team as its own historical control (difference-in-differences). This separates restructuring effects from seasonality/volume trends. Match on volume, complexity mix, and segment mix.

**Pre/post observation window.** Minimum **8–12 weeks pre** (to establish baseline distributions, not just means) and **12–16 weeks post** (long enough for lagging outcomes — rework, reopens, satisfaction — to accumulate). Include a 1–2 week stabilization buffer after cutover that is excluded from analysis. Ensure the window spans at least one full workload cycle to capture peak load, since bottlenecks appear under load.

**Segmentation.** Analyze every metric split by:
- **team/location** (East vs North vs control South),
- **case_complexity** (low/medium/high — watch high),
- **required_specialty** (legal/risk/security vs technical vs none),
- **customer_segment** (esp. language/regional-accommodation segments),
- and by **priority** and **TSS-touch (yes/no)**.
Never report a pilot result only in aggregate.

**Guardrail metrics (hard limits that pause/adjust rollout).**
- TSS or approval-queue utilization sustained > **90%** for 5+ business days.
- p90 cycle time (any segment) up > **20%** vs baseline, or p99 up > **35%**.
- High-complexity rework rate up > **2 percentage points**.
- reassignment_count median up > **50%**.
- Any customer_segment SLA-miss rate up > **3 points** relative to control.
- Backlog (open shared-queue cases) trending monotonically upward for 2+ weeks.
- North/South customer satisfaction down > **0.3/5**.

**Staff feedback collection.** Structured, recurring (biweekly) input from local caseworkers and shared-service specialists on: perceived ownership/accountability, quality of asynchronous advice vs. old local loop, skill-development concerns, and handling of language/regional cases. Combine qualitative feedback with the quantitative reassignment_count/rework signals to interpret role-clarity effects. Include a channel for flagging fairness concerns.

**Rollback or adjustment triggers.**
- *Adjust* (don't roll back) if bottlenecks are staffing-limited: add TSS/approval capacity, split the single shared queue by workload type (separate technical vs legal/risk vs difficult-QC queues), or introduce priority/segment-aware routing to protect fairness.
- *Rollback* (return that team to local model) if two or more guardrails breach simultaneously, if backlog is uncontrollable after capacity added, or if complex-case quality degrades with rising escaped defects.
- Do not expand to the next region until the pilot region holds all guardrails for a sustained period (e.g., 4+ weeks) on **lagging**, not just leading, metrics.

# 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation: Pilot first (phased), with modification — do not proceed to full rollout, and do not reject outright.**

The proposal has a genuine upside (relieving East's overloaded E_Tech, reducing poor ad-hoc cross-team cover, standardizing decisions, raising utilization). It also has structurally predictable downsides (a single central queue absorbing technical + legal/risk + difficult QC, on top of specialists already at 91–93% utilization; diffused approval accountability; erosion of local expertise and regional fairness). Because the risks are distributional and delayed rather than average-level, the responsible path is a controlled pilot with the modifications in Section 5 — most importantly, **not** pooling the already-saturated Legal/Risk work in the first phase, and **separating** the shared queues by workload type rather than one combined queue.

**Conditions the recommendation depends on (monitoring-gated):**
- Proceed to expand *only while* leading indicators (TSS/approval waiting-time p90, utilization, reassignment_count, back-edge loops, queue-length sensitivity) stay within guardrails, **and** lagging indicators (tail cycle time, complex-case rework, segment/location fairness gaps, satisfaction, escaped defects) confirm no harm after outcomes accumulate.
- Halt/adjust on any guardrail breach per Section 5.

**What management must NOT infer from early averages:**
- A flat or improved **mean/median cycle time** in the first weeks does **not** validate the change. Early data is dominated by fast simple local cases and by leading flow metrics; the damaging effects (tail latency, complex-case rework, escaped defects, expertise erosion, fairness gaps) are lagging and load-dependent.
- A **drop in handover count** is partly a relabeling artifact (three local specialists become one shared node) and does not by itself mean less coordination cost or shorter waits.
- A **rise in specialist utilization** toward 90%+ is not unambiguously good — it is the regime where queueing waits blow up non-linearly and resilience to absence falls.
- **East improving** must not be generalized to North/South, which carry the expertise-loss and fairness risks; aggregate gains can mask a cross-team inversion.

In short: **pilot, modified, and gate every expansion on distributional and lagging evidence — not on early averages.**

# 7. PM4Py analysis sketch

The pattern below computes each network/metric on a **before** log and an **after** log (or on filtered slices), then compares. Assume two dataframes `df_before` and `df_after` with the columns from the log.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

RENAME = {
    "case:concept:name": "case:concept:name",
    "concept:name": "concept:name",
    "time:timestamp": "time:timestamp",
    "org:resource": "org:resource",
}

def prep(df):
    # Ensure standard columns and datetime
    df = df.copy()
    df["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"])
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    )
    # org:resource is used automatically by org-mining functions
    return log

log_before = prep(df_before)
log_after  = prep(df_after)

def org_networks(log):
    return {
        "handover":  pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log),
        "together":  pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log),
        "subcontract": pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(log),
        "similarity": pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log),
        "roles":     pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log),
    }

nets_before = org_networks(log_before)
nets_after  = org_networks(log_after)

# ---- Compare handover-of-work networks (edge count, concentration) ----
def edge_summary(network):
    # network.value: dict {(res_a, res_b): weight}
    edges = network.value if hasattr(network, "value") else network
    weights = list(edges.values())
    total = sum(weights) or 1
    n_edges = len(weights)
    # concentration: share carried by top-5 edges (funneling signal)
    top5 = sum(sorted(weights, reverse=True)[:5]) / total
    return {"n_edges": n_edges, "top5_share": top5}

print("Handover before:", edge_summary(nets_before["handover"]))
print("Handover after :", edge_summary(nets_after["handover"]))
# Expect: n_edges down, top5_share up  => funneling onto TSS core

# Working-together centralization (single-point-of-failure proxy):
print("Together before:", edge_summary(nets_before["together"]))
print("Together after :", edge_summary(nets_after["together"]))

# Subcontracting: informal "specialist helps mid-case" edges.
# A drop in subcontracting after change => loss of informal local advice.
print("Subcontract before:", edge_summary(nets_before["subcontract"]))
print("Subcontract after :", edge_summary(nets_after["subcontract"]))

# ---- Variants and performance DFG ----
variants_before = pm4py.get_variants(log_before)
variants_after  = pm4py.get_variants(log_after)
print("Variant count:", len(variants_before), "->", len(variants_after))

dfg_b, sa_b, ea_b = pm4py.discover_dfg(log_before)
perf_dfg_b, _, _  = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log_before)
perf_dfg_a, _, _  = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log_after)
# Compare sojourn/performance on edges into the shared-service activities
def edge_perf(perf_dfg, activity_substr):
    return {e: v for e, v in perf_dfg.items()
            if activity_substr.lower() in (e[1] or "").lower()}
print("Waits into Specialist/TSS (before):",
      edge_perf(perf_dfg_b, "Specialist Review"))
print("Waits into Specialist/TSS (after) :",
      edge_perf(perf_dfg_a, "Specialist Review"))

# ---- Service and waiting times (queue formation) ----
log_before = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
    log_before, service_time="mean")
log_after  = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
    log_after,  service_time="mean")
st_before = pm4py.get_service_time(log_before)  # activity -> service time
st_after  = pm4py.get_service_time(log_after)
# waiting-time column now available per case: aggregate median AND p90/p99
for name, log in [("before", log_before), ("after", log_after)]:
    w = log.groupby("case:concept:name")["@@waiting_time"].max()
    print(name, "wait p50/p90/p99:",
          w.quantile([0.5, 0.9, 0.99]).to_dict())

# ---- Resource similarity & role discovery (role clarity) ----
roles_before = nets_before["roles"]
roles_after  = nets_after["roles"]
print("Discovered roles:", len(roles_before), "->", len(roles_after))
# Also: distinct approvers per case & reassignment_count as role-clarity proxies
def role_clarity(df):
    appr = df[df["concept:name"] == "Supervisor Approval"]
    distinct_appr = appr.groupby("case:concept:name")["org:resource"].nunique()
    return {
        "median_distinct_approvers": distinct_appr.median(),
        "median_reassignment": df.groupby("case:concept:name")
                                 ["reassignment_count"].max().median(),
    }
print("Role clarity before:", role_clarity(df_before))
print("Role clarity after :", role_clarity(df_after))

# ---- Segmented comparison: team, complexity, specialty, segment ----
def slice_and_compare(df, **filters):
    log = prep(df)
    for col, vals in filters.items():
        log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            log, col, vals, level="case", retain=True)
    # cycle time distribution on the slice
    feats = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(log)
    end = log.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].agg(["min","max"])
    ct = (end["max"] - end["min"]).dt.total_seconds() / 86400.0  # days
    return ct.quantile([0.5, 0.9, 0.99]).to_dict()

for team in ["North", "South", "East"]:
    print(team, "before high-complexity cycle days:",
          slice_and_compare(df_before, team=[team],
                            case_complexity=["high"]))
    print(team, "after  high-complexity cycle days:",
          slice_and_compare(df_after,  team=[team],
                            case_complexity=["high"]))

# Specialty and customer-segment fairness slices
for spec in ["legal", "risk", "security"]:
    print("Specialty", spec, "after:",
          slice_and_compare(df_after, required_specialty=[spec]))
for seg in df_after["customer_segment"].unique():
    print("Segment", seg, "after:",
          slice_and_compare(df_after, customer_segment=[seg]))

# Variant-level focus on TSS-touched cases:
tss_variants = pm4py.filter_variants(  # keep only variants that hit TSS/Specialist
    log_after, [v for v in pm4py.get_variants(log_after)
                if any("Specialist Review" in a for a in v)])

# ---- Rework and SLA misses ----
rw_before = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(log_before)
rw_after  = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(log_after)
print("Rework per activity before:", rw_before)
print("Rework per activity after :", rw_after)

def rework_by_complexity(df):
    return (df.groupby("case_complexity")["rework_flag"]
              .apply(lambda s: (s == True).mean()).to_dict())
print("Rework by complexity before:", rework_by_complexity(df_before))
print("Rework by complexity after :", rework_by_complexity(df_after))

def sla_miss_by(df, by):
    # assumes an SLA breach is encoded in outcome or derived from cycle time
    return (df.drop_duplicates("case:concept:name")
              .assign(miss=lambda d: d["outcome"].eq("SLA_miss"))
              .groupby(by)["miss"].mean().to_dict())
print("SLA miss by team before:", sla_miss_by(df_before, "team"))
print("SLA miss by team after :", sla_miss_by(df_after, "team"))
print("SLA miss by segment after:", sla_miss_by(df_after, "customer_segment"))
```

**How to read the comparison (tie back to the forecast):**
- *Handover network*: fewer edges but higher top-5 concentration = intended reduction is partly funneling onto a TSS core (Section 1). Watch working-together centralization for single-point-of-failure risk.
- *Subcontracting drop* = loss of informal local advice (learning/expertise risk).
- *Performance DFG + waiting time*: rising p90/p99 waits into TSS/Specialist while median flat = the hidden bottleneck and tail-latency risk (Sections 2, 4).
- *Role discovery + distinct approvers + reassignment_count*: diffusion = role-clarity/accountability erosion (Sections 1, 4).
- *Segmented cycle/rework by team, complexity, specialty, segment*: reveals East-benefit vs North/South-loss and customer-segment fairness gaps that the aggregate mean hides (Sections 2, 4, 6).
- *Rework by complexity and SLA-miss by segment*: the lagging quality/fairness signals that must confirm safety before any rollout expansion (Sections 3, 5, 6).
