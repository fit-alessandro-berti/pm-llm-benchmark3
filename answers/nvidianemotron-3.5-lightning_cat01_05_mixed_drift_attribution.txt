**Seasonality & multiple testing**: Each monitor uses a 3month moving average as reference, with alerts triggered only when the current month exceeds the moving average by the stated margin. BenjaminiHochberg FDR control (q=0.10) across the ten monitors per month limits false discoveries. Seasonal windows (renewal: Sep–Nov; offpeak: May–Aug) are preflagged; comparisons are performed within window, and crosswindow trends are tracked separately. If a monitor fires repeatedly in the same seasonal window, the alert logic shifts from “deviation from baseline” to “deviation from seasonal norm,” reducing typeI error during predictable peaks.

## 9. Provisional attribution

The most durable pattern across the observation window is a threephase drift trajectory. March 2026 marks a sudden technical collapse: the system migration introduced `Eligibility Screen` as a label alias for `Validate Eligibility`, duplicated `Open Case` events, and missing resource identifiers. Raw-model fitness plummeted from 0.92 to 0.55, while the mapped model—after correcting the label alias and removing duplicated technical events—recovered to ~0.90. This gap demonstrates that the bulk of the March decline is semantic/provenance drift, not a genuine businessprocess deterioration. The mapped fitness stability through April confirms that, once the schema is normalized, the underlying conformance skeleton remains largely intact.

From May onward, real organizational drift takes hold. Supplier Z’s API and staffing disruption (effective 20260505) lifted supplier Z’s share from 16% to 43% of cases, while the rework variant more than doubled (12%  27%) and the `AssessRequestInfo` rate in the normalized DFG jumped from 12/100 to 24/100. The data show that supplierdriven rework and casemix elevation are the primary engines: supplier Z cases were already somewhat more complex before May, and the disruption amplified both the proportion and the intrusive loop frequency. Four assessors left around 20260615, and the organization’s redistribution—without full replacement—coincides with a sustained rise in p90 cycle time (69h  116h by October) and a marked increase in reconstructed queue waiting, even though median `Assess` service time crept up only from ~4.8h to ~5.4h. The July1 priority rule then reorganized queue ordering: highcomplexity cases were advanced, which the aggregate mask may hide, but the `AssessSeniorAssessment` rate climbed from singledigits to 22% by October, and the fast variant share shifted accordingly. 

October’s annual renewal season (20261001) superimposed a seasonal surge on top of these persistent mechanisms. Volume jumped from 620 to 760 cases, highcomplexity share rose to 41% (from 21% in January), and the seniorassessment pathway became the dominant decision route (22% of assessed cases). The priority rule, supplier recovery (reported 20260910), and overtime + quality campaign (20261205) all contributed to a modest mappedfitness rebound to 0.90 in December, while raw fitness also recovered slightly (0.58). The December improvement aligns with both lower seasonal load and the active interventions, but the data cannot cleanly separate how much is seasonal taper versus intervention effect.

**Strongest conclusions**: The March fitness collapse is primarily a migrationinduced semantic artifact; after mapping and deduplication, conformance stabilizes. From May, supplier disruption and assessor turnover are the dominant drivers of rework, queueing, and cycletime lengthening. The July priority rule redistributes delay between complexity classes but does not create new work. October’s surge is largely seasonal volume and complexity increase, amplified by the prior two months’ drift. 

**Plausible but not unambiguous**: The exact quantitative split between supplierdriven rework, staffingdriven queueing, and priorityinduced redistribution cannot be resolved from monthly aggregates alone; the observed correlations are consistent with each, and mediation (e.g., priority rule effect amplified by low staffing) is likely. 

**Mediation/interaction preventing clean attribution**: Supplier Z’s nonrandom regional distribution confounds its effect with complexity; assessor loss magnitude is uncertain and overlaps with seasonal volume rise; the priority rule’s introduction coincides with the senior assessment pathway’s emergence, making it hard to tell whether seniorassessment rise is a rule artifact or a complexity response. 

**What would revise the account**: Arrival of eventlevel logs would allow (a) exact attribution of `RequestInfo` loops to supplier Z versus staffing scarcity, (b) assessment of whether priorityrule fasttracking genuinely reduces highcomplexity cycle time without inflating standardcase waits, and (c) changepoint detection at the case level rather than monthly bins, revealing whether any drift began earlier or later than the stated dates.
