# 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

## Upstream data-quality chain

The primary dependency chain is:

**A1 registration  A2 classification  parallel KYC/document branches  A6 normalization  credit analysis  A7 valuation where required  A8 committee pack  decision  A9 contract  A10 signing  A11 activation.**

This is not purely sequential. KYC and financial document review are often concurrent even though the event log records them sequentially. Designs should therefore launch both branches from classification and synchronize only where legally or operationally required.

Key dependencies are:

- **A1** improves intake data but does not itself correct product/exposure classification, missing documents, or KYC exceptions.
- **A2** governs downstream routing. Wrong exposure classification could omit committee or segregation requirements; wrong product classification could omit collateral valuation.
- **A5** should precede or accompany **A6**. Extracting incomplete statements efficiently does not remove the `Financial document review  Request missing documents  Financial document review` loop.
- **A6** provides structured statements needed by credit analysis and reliable **A8** assembly.
- **A8** depends on reconciled KYC status, normalized statements, valuation where required, and credit notes. It should not treat “artifact present” as equivalent to “artifact approved.”
- **A9** depends on an authoritative committee decision and cleared KYC; it must compare generated terms with approved terms.
- **A10** requires a system-enforced KYC-clearance gate.
- **A11** depends on all prerequisite controls being complete and current. It is unsuitable as an isolated downstream bot.

## Legal and compliance controls

- **A3 and A4** may recommend, rank, or pre-populate, but ambiguous KYC hits and entity mismatches must remain with accountable compliance personnel.
- No automation should convert “likely clear” into legal clearance without an authorized human or approved deterministic rule.
- **A10** must block signing unless KYC clearance is recorded in an authoritative source. The 6.5% provisional-signing rate shows that reminders alone would automate an unsafe pattern.
- **A9** must perform a field-level contract-versus-decision reconciliation, retain the source decision version, and block unresolved discrepancies.
- High-exposure cases require a role-based four-eyes check between credit analysis and committee packaging. **A8** must verify user identity and role separation, not merely assemble files.
- Every model-assisted module must retain the suggestion, confidence, source evidence, human override, override reason, final outcome, timestamp, and model/rule version.

## Human escalation capacity

Automation changes the shape of work rather than eliminating it:

- **A3/A4** may reduce simple handling but concentrate difficult cases on three compliance specialists, with strong dependence on two senior staff. Escalation arrival rates, age, and senior concentration must be monitored.
- **A1/A2** accelerate clean inflow into KYC, document review, and credit analysis. Because analysts are central in the working-together network, these modules should not be scaled before **A5/A6** relieve analyst rework and normalization effort.
- **A6** requires a review lane for low-quality PDFs and unusual statements. Analysts should review exceptions, not reperform all extraction.
- **A9** may smooth legal work generally but create month-end exception spikes. Legal approval capacity and rule-maintenance ownership remain necessary.
- **A12** requires named policy owners; otherwise cases will be organized more clearly but not resolved.

## External valuation dependency

**A7** cannot automate external valuers, but it can:

- Identify early which cases require valuation.
- Route by asset type, region, performance, and capacity.
- Request valuation in parallel with permissible credit work.
- Monitor SLA breaches and reassign where contractually allowed.
- Validate returned reports and route quality disputes.

The largest benefit is synchronization: Equipment finance requires valuation in 71% of cases, and `Wait for external valuation  Committee packaging` occurs 1,980 times. Committee packaging should not wait to begin all preparation, but final readiness must remain gated on an acceptable valuation.

## Core-banking integration constraints

Only two modules should receive deep integration in the first six months. The recommended allocation is:

1. **A6**, for controlled ingestion/output of normalized financial data into the credit workflow.
2. **A9**, for authoritative committee-decision retrieval, contract-rule checking, and status write-back.

**A5** can begin through the portal/workflow layer without deep core integration. **A4** can initially operate as a specialist workbench using KYC/entity APIs and read-only case data. Deep automation for A10 or A11 should wait; both require trusted control states across multiple systems.

# 3. Portfolio alternatives

## Quick-win portfolio: A1, A5, A9, A12

**Optimizes:** visible labor savings, delivery feasibility, management confidence, document-loop reduction, and one explicit control-strengthening capability.

- A1 provides the politically attractive high-frequency win.
- A5 attacks repeated missing-document loops.
- A9 saves legal preparation effort while checking decision-term consistency.
- A12 structures remediation and satisfies the control-strengthening requirement.

**Sacrifices:** it does not directly address the 31-hour entity-mismatch wait, 58-hour valuation wait, or 75-minute statement normalization burden. A1 may expose KYC and analyst bottlenecks, and the portfolio may look better on task productivity than on end-to-end time.

## Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A4, A5, A6, A7

**Optimizes:** critical-path waiting, repeated loops, analyst capacity, and multi-branch synchronization.

- A4 targets the low-frequency but high-impact KYC/entity exception.
- A5 reduces customer-document cycling.
- A6 relieves analyst effort and enables cleaner downstream artifacts.
- A7 targets the long external valuation wait through early triage and parallelization.

**Sacrifices:** lower headline transaction counts and less immediate back-office labor saving. Contract mismatches and signing-control issues are not directly automated, so manual gates and monitoring must remain strong. A4 counts as control-strengthening only if implemented conservatively with mandatory specialist escalation and audit evidence.

## Compliance-first portfolio: A3, A4, A9, A12

**Optimizes:** KYC decision quality, entity-resolution traceability, contract-decision consistency, and remediation governance.

- A3 prioritizes KYC hits for accountable human resolution.
- A4 reduces specialist search and handoff time.
- A9 enforces contract-rule and decision-term checks.
- A12 manages control failures and policy ambiguity.

**Sacrifices:** missing-document and normalization loops remain, so analyst congestion may continue. Customer and external-valuation waits are largely unaffected. Specialist capacity may worsen unless rollout thresholds are conservative.

# 4. Expected process-level effects

## Quick-win portfolio

### Cycle time and waiting time

- **A5** should reduce time lost in the 3,300 `Financial document review  Request missing documents` transitions and 2,800 returns to review. Customer response will still dominate some cases, so benefit depends on sending complete, comprehensible requests at the first contact.
- **A9** can reduce the 22-hour wait before contract preparation, particularly where the decision data is complete.
- **A1** reduces eight-minute registration work and perhaps part of the 1.5-hour queue, but registration is already in position two for 98% of cases and is not a major critical-path wait.
- Entity mismatch and external valuation remain major sources of cycle time.

### Rework and handovers

- A5 should lower repeated document requests and the RM/back-office/analyst handoff loop, especially if RMs see the same product-specific checklist before contacting customers.
- A9 should reduce `Contract preparation  Remediate control issue  Contract preparation` loops and the 14% renewal mismatch problem.
- A12 creates clearer ownership and evidence for remaining remediation cases.
- A1 may reduce duplicate-case handling but could create difficult merge/split exceptions.

### Resource demand

- Back-office demand falls, but the pool is already below 65% utilization after 15:00; saved capacity should be redeployed to document-quality support and exception queues rather than treated as the main economic gain.
- Faster registration increases arrivals to KYC and financial review. This requires workload caps because analysts and compliance specialists are more constrained.
- Legal ops may see smoother preparation but more concentrated review of exceptions near month end.

### Control violations

- A9 and A12 should reduce contract-decision mismatches and improve remediation traceability.
- The portfolio does not directly prevent provisional signing unless A9/A12 workflows include a hard KYC-status gate. A10 and A11 should remain disabled.

## Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio

### Cycle time and waiting time

- **A4** targets the 31-hour median wait for entity mismatch; because it is critical path in 82% of affected cases, even limited coverage can materially improve affected-case cycle time.
- **A5/A6** shorten the financial branch by reducing document loops, the 20-hour wait before normalization, and 75 minutes of analyst handling.
- **A7** addresses the largest listed wait: 58 hours for external valuation. Early initiation and routing are particularly relevant for the 71% of Equipment-finance cases requiring valuation.
- The overall benefit comes from shortening both parallel branches before they synchronize at committee packaging, rather than simply accelerating one task.

### Rework and handovers

- A5 reduces the repeated customer/RM/back-office/analyst loop.
- A6 replaces manual transcription with exception review, reducing repeated statement correction.
- A4 gives KYC ops and specialists a shared evidence package, reducing dependence on informal handovers to two senior specialists.
- A7 reduces collateral-team/valuer status chasing, although quality disputes remain human work.

### Resource demand

- Analysts regain capacity through A5/A6, limiting the overload that would occur if clean cases were merely accelerated by A1/A2.
- Compliance specialists receive fewer routine searches but a more concentrated ambiguous-case queue. Daily escalation caps and cross-training are necessary.
- Back office demand shifts from registration-like handling toward customer exception support.
- External valuer demand may be initiated earlier; contracts and routing rules must prevent overloading one partner.

### Control violations

- A4 can improve KYC evidence and auditability, but must never silently clear mismatches.
- A5/A6 improve data completeness and lineage, indirectly reducing downstream pack and contract errors.
- This portfolio does not itself eliminate provisional signing or contract-term mismatches. Existing hard gates, four-eyes controls, and manual legal checks must remain.

## Compliance-first portfolio

### Cycle time and waiting time

- A3/A4 may reduce KYC’s four-hour queue and the 31-hour entity-mismatch delay.
- A9/A12 may reduce the 22-hour contract queue and remediation cycling.
- Financial normalization, missing-document, valuation, committee cadence, and customer-signing waits remain; therefore end-to-end improvement is likely concentrated in risk-heavy variants rather than universal.

### Rework and handovers

- `KYC check  Resolve entity mismatch  KYC check` should become more structured, with evidence pre-collected and specialist decisions reused.
- A9 should reduce contract mismatch loops, especially Credit-line renewals.
- A12 provides a common handoff record among compliance, legal, RMs, and operations.
- Missing-document loops remain largely untouched.

### Resource demand

- Compliance work becomes more exception-heavy and may initially increase as A3 identifies previously missed ambiguities.
- The two senior specialists remain a concentration risk unless other staff are trained and authority limits are clarified.
- Legal ops should spend less time drafting and more time resolving non-standard clauses and governing the rule library.
- Analyst demand changes little.

### Control violations

- This portfolio gives the strongest direct reduction in KYC, contract-consistency, and remediation risks.
- It must include an enforced KYC-before-signing state transition.
- A9/A12 should provide evidence that no contract or activation proceeds with unresolved controls.
- Four-eyes violations still require an explicit identity/role check in the committee workflow.

# 5. Risks and compensating controls

| Risk introduced or amplified | Compensating controls |
|---|---|
| **KYC false negatives** from A3/A4 | Conservative thresholds; no autonomous clearance for ambiguous hits; mandatory specialist review; sampled review of “clear” recommendations; adverse-outcome testing; model-version audit trail |
| **False positives** increase specialist workload or customer friction | Confidence bands, reason codes, deduplication of alerts, service-level queues, periodic threshold tuning, and capacity-based rollout limits |
| **Extraction errors** in A6, especially low-quality PDFs | Document-quality scoring, field-level confidence, reconciliations and balance checks, source-image links, human review for low-confidence fields, prohibition on silent imputation |
| **Misclassification** by A2 | Recommender-only operation initially, authoritative exposure rules, mandatory confirmation for high-exposure cases, testing by product and applicant segment |
| **Rule/model drift** | Versioned models and rules, monthly performance monitoring, segment-level error analysis, change approval, champion/challenger tests, rollback capability |
| **Stale legal rule library** in A9 | Named legal owner, effective dates, dual approval for rule changes, regression suite against historical contracts, manual review of non-standard clauses |
| **Garbage-in/garbage-out** in A8/A9 | Artifact completeness and status checks, source-system reconciliation, blocking rather than auto-filling contradictory data, visible provenance |
| **Automating an illegal sequence** | Hard state-machine gate: KYC clearance must precede signing; no reminder or signature link before clearance; no activation while controls remain open |
| **Four-eyes violations** | Identity- and role-based segregation: credit analyst cannot finalize the pack for the same High-exposure case; substitute assignment workflow; immutable override approval; month-end monitoring |
| **Exception queue overload** | Forecast exception volumes, daily queue limits, skill-based routing, escalation SLAs, cross-training beyond the two senior compliance specialists, pause triggers |
| **Shifted downstream bottlenecks** | Measure arrival rates and queues at KYC, normalization, analysts, committee, legal, signing, and activation; throttle upstream automation when downstream WIP exceeds limits |
| **External valuation quality disputes** | Approved valuer panels, routing transparency, SLA/quality scorecards, second-valuation criteria, human review of outliers |
| **Customer friction from A5/A10** | Product- and segment-specific language, RM preview, multilingual support, channel choice, consolidated rather than repeated nudges, accessibility testing |
| **Duplicate detection mistakes** in A1 | Non-destructive case linking, human approval before merge, reversible actions, audit of source records |
| **Automation bias and weak accountability** | Show supporting evidence rather than only a recommendation; require override reasons; train users that responsibility remains with authorized staff |
| **Poor auditability** | Retain inputs, source documents, suggestion, confidence, model/rule version, human action, override reason, final decision, timestamps, and user identity |
| **Policy ambiguity exposed by A12** | Named policy owners, decision deadlines, temporary conservative rules, issue taxonomy, governance forum, and prohibition on treating unresolved ambiguity as cleared |

# 6. Recommended phased rollout

The first-year rollout should implement the **bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A4, A5, A6, and A7**. A4 is the first-wave control-strengthening module because it improves KYC evidence and escalation without transferring compliance accountability to the tool.

## Phase 0 — Baseline and control design: Months 0–2

Actions:

- Reconstruct concurrency explicitly: treat KYC and financial review as parallel branches rather than trusting recorded sequence.
- Segment baselines by Micro/SME/High-exposure, product, month end, and exception status.
- Define authoritative KYC, document, valuation, and financial-data states.
- Establish human escalation ownership, especially for the two senior compliance specialists.
- Implement interim manual hard gates for KYC-before-signing and high-exposure four-eyes separation.
- Define audit-event standards shared by all four modules.

Monitoring metrics:

- End-to-end cycle time and branch completion time.
- Entity-mismatch wait, repeat rate, and specialist queue age.
- Missing-document request and repeat-request rates.
- Normalization handling time, error rate, and low-confidence rate.
- Valuation request lead time, external wait, late rate, and dispute rate.
- Provisional-signing rate and four-eyes violation rate.

Gate criteria:

- Authoritative status definitions approved by compliance.
- Exception owners and SLAs assigned.
- Audit logging tested end to end.
- No module permitted to bypass manual legal or compliance gates.

## Phase 1 — First two modules and two deep integrations: Months 2–6

### Deploy A5 first through the portal/workflow layer

- Introduce product/exposure-sensitive document checklists.
- Show RMs the same instructions customers receive.
- Consolidate nudges and provide a human-help route.
- Pilot on Working-capital and Equipment-finance SME cases before High-exposure cases.

### Deploy A6 as deep integration 1

- Start with clean, machine-readable statements.
- Write normalized outputs to the credit workflow with full source lineage.
- Route low-quality PDFs and low-confidence fields to analysts.
- Do not auto-populate final credit judgments.

### Reserve and establish deep integration 2 for A4

- Integrate authoritative KYC/entity sources and case status.
- Operate initially as a specialist assistant: candidate matches, discrepancy summary, and evidence retrieval.
- Keep final clearance with authorized compliance staff.

Gate criteria for expansion:

- A5 reduces repeat document loops without material increases in complaints or abandonment.
- A6 achieves an approved field-accuracy threshold and passes financial reconciliations; low-confidence cases are reliably diverted.
- Analyst WIP and waiting do not rise as cleaner inflow increases.
- A4 demonstrates no unauthorized clearance, complete audit trails, and stable specialist queue age.
- No deterioration in provisional-signing or four-eyes rates.

## Phase 2 — Scale A4 and add A7: Months 6–9

- Expand A4 by entity type and segment only after specialist validation.
- Cross-train compliance staff to reduce reliance on two senior specialists.
- Introduce A7 without necessarily requiring deep core integration:
  - detect valuation need at classification/early analysis,
  - route to approved valuers,
  - initiate in parallel where policy allows,
  - monitor SLA and quality,
  - alert committee packaging when the synchronization prerequisite is complete.

Gate criteria:

- Material reduction in entity-mismatch waiting for assisted cases.
- Escalation false-positive volume remains within specialist capacity.
- External valuation waiting and late rate fall without increased dispute or revaluation rates.
- No increase in collateral exceptions at committee or contract stages.

## Phase 3 — Stabilize, optimize, and prepare the next wave: Months 9–12

- Tune A5 instructions based on loop causes and customer feedback.
- Expand A6 cautiously to lower-quality document formats.
- Analyze whether A4 has reduced senior-specialist concentration or merely changed the queue.
- Negotiate valuer SLA and routing changes using A7 performance data.
- Shadow-test **A9** for year two using committee decisions and contracts, but do not count it as a fifth production module.
- Keep **A10/A11** out of production until KYC-before-signing and control-completeness gates are demonstrably reliable.

Portfolio-level success metrics:

- Median and tail end-to-end cycle time, not only task time saved.
- Time until both KYC and financial branches are ready.
- DFG loop rates:
  - `Resolve entity mismatch  KYC check`,
  - `Request missing documents  Financial document review`,
  - contract-remediation returns.
- Committee packaging wait attributable to each missing prerequisite.
- Analyst, specialist, back-office, collateral, and legal utilization/WIP.
- Customer complaint and abandonment rates.
- KYC false-negative review results.
- Provisional-signing and four-eyes violations.
- Override rate, override reasons, and model/rule drift by segment.

Stop or rollback criteria include any unauthorized KYC clearance, material increase in false-negative findings, uncontrolled specialist queue growth, extraction errors reaching credit decisions, increased valuation disputes, or worsening control violations.

# 7. Final recommendation

Recommend the **bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A4, A5, A6, and A7**.

It provides the strongest process-level balance within four modules:

- **A4** addresses a 31-hour wait that is critical path in 82% of affected cases and strengthens KYC exception control.
- **A5** attacks the most visible rework loop rather than merely accelerating its entry point.
- **A6** removes substantial analyst effort and prevents faster upstream processing from overloading a central nine-FTE resource pool.
- **A7** targets the 58-hour external synchronization delay, especially in Equipment finance.

The highest-frequency candidate, **A1**, should not be the first major investment. Registration is stable in position two, has only a 1.5-hour median wait and 4% rework, and is performed by a back-office pool that is not continuously saturated. Automating it first would produce an attractive labor metric but accelerate cases into constrained KYC, document, and analyst queues. It is suitable later as a limited quick win once downstream WIP controls and capacity are in place.

This recommendation deliberately sacrifices some immediate headline labor savings and does not yet automate contracting, signing, or activation. That is defensible: downstream automation is unsafe while KYC sequencing, contract consistency, and control completeness remain imperfect. Existing hard gates and manual legal checks must continue, with A9 as the logical next-wave control module after upstream artifacts become reliable. The objective in year one should be to reduce critical-path waits and rework while preserving accountable human compliance decisions—not simply to automate the task with the largest event count.
