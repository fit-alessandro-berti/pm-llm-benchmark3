# 2. Adjusted comparison strategy

The analysis should separate four questions: (1) raw disparity, (2) disparity after documented need/complexity adjustment, (3) disparity within the same process path or same support need, and (4) whether remaining differences are likely due to legitimate routing, resource constraints, measurement bias, or unjustified treatment. The goal is not to “control away” support needs; it is to distinguish legitimate needs-based routing from avoidable procedural disadvantage.

## 2.1 Define exposure, needs, and outcomes

- Applicant group is the comparison label, but need variables such as `language_preference`, `disability_accommodation_requested`, `risk_score_band`, and prior case count must be modeled explicitly.
- Estimate both “total adjusted disparity” and “need-specific disparity.” For example, compare Group B applicants needing language support against non-B applicants needing language support where possible, rather than treating all Group B cases as equivalent.
- Outcome variables: approval, case duration, supervisor review, missing-document request, appeal, reopen/rework.
- Process outcomes are not mere controls: waiting for Language Support Contact or Accommodation Review is part of procedural fairness.

## 2.2 Matching or stratification variables

Use exact matching, coarsened exact matching, propensity weighting, or regression adjustment on:

- `risk_score_band`
- `documentation_completeness`
- `channel`
- `language_preference` and documented need for language support
- `disability_accommodation_requested`
- `household_size_band`
- `prior_case_count`
- `region`
- `month`
- possibly `channel × region` and `region × month`

Run at least three adjustment sets:

1. Core case-mix: risk, documentation, channel, household, prior case count, region, month.
2. Core plus explicit support needs: add language support need and accommodation request.
3. Sensitivity set excluding or reweighting suspect covariates such as `risk_score_band` and `documentation_completeness`.

Do not condition on support-step receipt as if it were exogenous when estimating total procedural disparity; support-step receipt is itself part of the process and may mediate unequal treatment.

## 2.3 Path/variant comparison

- Compute variant shares by applicant group.
- Compare cycle time, waiting time, appeal, rework, and supervisor-review rates within the same variant.
- Compare variants that add a support step against matched cases with the same documented need but different routing.
- Decompose cycle time by path: e.g., V2 missing-document path, V4 language-support path, V5 accommodation path, V3 fraud-risk path, V6 appeal path.
- Ask whether within-variant differences remain after matching on need, region, channel, and resource queue.

## 2.4 Resource-assignment comparison

- Compare assignment to bilingual staff, accommodation specialists, fraud reviewers, supervisors, and general caseworkers after conditioning on documented need and region.
- Measure caseload, queue length, handover count, and supervisor dual-role burden.
- Use handover and working-together networks to test whether B and C cases experience more transitions or transitions through constrained staff.
- Compare same-need cases: e.g., language-support cases handled by bilingual staff vs language-support cases waiting for bilingual staff; accommodation cases routed directly to specialists vs through general caseworkers.

## 2.5 Waiting-time decomposition

Split case time into:

- submission to identity verification;
- completeness check to missing-document request;
- missing-document response to eligibility;
- language-support queue vs language-support contact;
- accommodation-review queue vs review;
- fraud-review queue vs review;
- supervisor-review queue vs decision;
- decision-draft to notification;
- appeal intake/review/closure.

Attribute delays to queueing, handovers, batching, resource availability, or rework. Report medians, 90th percentiles, and distributions, not only means. Use region and channel strata because in-person and two-region supervisor backlogs may explain part of the pattern.

## 2.6 Sensitivity analysis for biased covariates

`risk_score_band` may encode historical disparities. `documentation_completeness` may be endogenous to language barriers and administrative practice. Therefore:

- Re-run models with risk score excluded or replaced by audited, bias-corrected risk measures.
- Re-run with documentation completeness redefined using independent records where possible.
- Use negative-control outcomes and negative-control groups to detect residual confounding.
- Quantify how strong unmeasured confounding would need to be to explain away the disparity.
- Report results as ranges, not a single definitive adjusted estimate.

## 2.7 Subgroup checks by region and channel

- Stratify by region, online vs in-person, and channel × language need.
- Inspect small-cell instability using confidence intervals and Bayesian/shrinkage estimates.
- Pre-specify primary subgroup checks to avoid cherry-picking.
- Report both statistical and practical significance.

# 4. Plausible mechanisms

1. **Legitimate needs-based routing.** Higher `risk_score_band` triggers Fraud Review; accommodation requests trigger Accommodation Review; language needs trigger Language Support Contact. This can legitimately increase steps and cycle time.

2. **Specialist capacity bottlenecks.** Only 7 bilingual caseworkers and 5 accommodation specialists handle a large share of B/C support needs. Queues form, and support steps become de facto delay points.

3. **Handover and coordination failures.** B cases may go generalbilingualgeneral; C cases generalspecialistsupervisor. Each handover can add waiting, information loss, and inconsistency, especially when small specialist teams also hold other duties.

4. **Documentation barriers and administrative loop effects.** Language barriers and in-person/online channel differences may increase missing-document requests and rework. `documentation_completeness` may measure administrative friction as much as applicant behavior.

5. **Supervisor-review inconsistency.** B and C have higher adjusted supervisor review. Some may be complexity-related, but two-region supervisor backlog and dual-role accommodation staff suggest workload and routing may create avoidable supervision.

6. **Historical bias in risk scoring.** `risk_score_band` may encode prior case counts, region, or group-correlated history, causing D to enter fraud review and appeal paths disproportionately even if current risk is similar.

7. **Explanation and communication quality.** Higher appeal for B and D may reflect unclear decision explanations, translation quality, or mistrust rather than only decision correctness. The log does not capture dignity or explanation quality.

8. **Resource allocation by dispatch rules.** B being assigned to overloaded regional officers and C to specialists who also supervise may reflect a reasonable matching rule but still produce systematically slower service.

# 5. Limitations of available attributes

- **Measurement bias in `risk_score_band`.** Risk scores may reflect historical enforcement, prior applications, region, or group membership rather than neutral current need. Treating them as clean controls can hide discrimination.
- **Endogenous `documentation_completeness`.** Incomplete documentation is not a pure applicant attribute. It may be caused by language barriers, unclear instructions, inaccessible channels, or inconsistent staff requests.
- **Missing applicant needs and severity.** The log does not measure severity of disability, interpreter need, literacy, digital access, urgency, caregiving constraints, or quality of language/accommodation support.
- **Unobserved resource workload.** Workload is observed only at daily granularity. Queue length, staff leave, experience, task batching, and real-time staffing are missing.
- **Appeal quality and meaning.** Appeal rate alone does not show whether appeals are successful, well-explained, accessible, or burdensome. A higher appeal rate could reflect better voice mechanisms or worse decisions.
- **Outcome does not capture procedural dignity.** `case_outcome` does not measure explanation quality, respect, wait communication, translation accuracy, or disability accommodation quality.
- **Coarse case-mix bands.** `household_size_band`, `prior_case_count`, and `risk_score_band` may hide nonlinear complexity.
- **Region/month confounding.** Two regions have supervisor backlog; channel and region interact. Small cells may make subgroup estimates unstable.
- **Causal identification is limited.** Even strong adjustment cannot prove discrimination or intent. It can identify unexplained procedural disparities that require audit and remediation.

# 6. Mitigation options and side effects

| Mitigation option | Intended effect | Possible side effects / trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| Add capacity for language support or accommodation review | Reduce 3.8d/4.7d support waits, reduce handovers, improve timeliness. | Cost, recruitment/training time, risk of over-specialization, need for coverage across regions. Do not remove support steps. |
| Set service-level targets for support steps | Make rights-preserving steps timely and visible; prevent support from becoming a hidden delay. | Targets may incentivize rushing, gaming, or reducing support quality. Pair with quality/accessibility measures. |
| Redesign routing to reduce handovers | E.g., end-to-end bilingual caseworker, co-located specialists, parallel review. | Specialization trade-offs, caseload imbalance, staff coverage issues, possible loss of specialist oversight. |
| Improve decision explanations and notifications | Plain-language, translated, reason-giving notices; clear appeal instructions. | Translation/legal-review cost; risk of boilerplate; cannot substitute for correct decisions or timely support. |
| Monitor adjusted disparities continuously | Detect changes by group, path, region, channel, support queue; use confidence intervals. | Metric gaming, small-cell noise, multiple testing, need for transparent definitions and oversight. |
| Audit `risk_score_band` and `documentation_completeness` | Identify historical bias, measurement error, and endogenous documentation loops. | Audited/reweighted models may reduce predictive accuracy; risk of unintended substitution effects; need human review. |
| Balance workloads and specialist queues | Reduce concentration in 7 bilingual workers, 3 overloaded officers, and dual-role specialists. | May reduce specialization efficiency; requires cross-training and clear escalation rules. |
| Add support-specific appeal/rework review | Distinguish legitimate appeals from communication failures and rework loops. | Appeals data may be incomplete; additional review capacity needed. |

A key principle: rights-preserving support steps should be made faster and less burdensome, not removed to improve average cycle time. Fairness improvements should target avoidable queueing, handovers, documentation friction, and explanation quality.

# 8. Bottom-line assessment

The process is not demonstrably procedurally fair, and the evidence is more concerning than raw approval-rate differences alone would suggest. After adjustment there remain gaps in cycle time, supervisor review, missing-document requests, and appeals. More importantly, the rights-preserving support steps—Language Support Contact and Accommodation Review—are associated with long waits and extra handovers because they rely on small specialist teams, lack separate timeliness targets, and are embedded in routing designs that can create bottlenecks.

At the same time, the evidence does not prove intentional discrimination. The preliminary adjusted model omits explicit language-support and accommodation needs, and `risk_score_band` and `documentation_completeness` may be biased or endogenous. Some delay is consistent with legitimate needs-based routing and constrained specialist capacity. The calibrated conclusion is therefore: there are substantial, partly unexplained procedural disparities and clear signs of avoidable support-step burden, but a stronger causal claim of discrimination requires better need measurement, audit of risk/documentation variables, path-specific resource analysis, and subgroup checks by region and channel. The appropriate response is to strengthen and adequately resource support steps, monitor adjusted procedural disparities, and audit the administrative categories that drive routing—not to remove support steps in order to improve cycle time.
