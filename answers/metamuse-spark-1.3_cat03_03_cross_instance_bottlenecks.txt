## 1. Interaction-graph description
Typed nodes: `Job {J1-J6}`, `Component {C1-C6}`, `ComponentLot {L7,L8}`, `Machine {CNC-2, OVEN-1, TEST-1}`, `Tool {FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3}`, `Batch {B1,B2}`, `Shipment {S1-S3}`.

Important edge types with qualifiers from O2O plus event-level relations:
- `Job -requires-> Component`: J1-C1, J2-C2, J3-C3, J4-C4, J5-C5, J6-C6 (static BOM, direct observation).
- `ComponentLot -contains-> Component`: L7-{C2,C5}, L8-{C1,C3} (direct).
- `Batch -batches-> Job`: B1-{J1,J2,J3}, B2-{J4,J5} (direct, plus joint `Batch Cure` event co-relating B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J1-3, C1-3 at 12:05).
- `Shipment -consolidates-> Job`: S1-{J1,J4}, S2-{J2,J3}, S3-{J5,J6} (direct).
- `Job -uses-> Machine/Tool` per event: e.g. J4,C4,CNC-2,FIX-A in `Machine Process` 11:45; J1-3,OVEN-1,RACK-7 in `Batch Cure` 12:05; J5/J6,TEST-1,CAL-3 in `Final Test` 16:10-16:35 and 17:25-17:50 (direct event-to-object relations).
- `Job -blocked_by-> Tool` and `Job -waits_for-> Batch/Shipment/Lot`: inferred, no explicit event. E.g. J4 waiting for FIX-A 09:50-11:45; J1/J2 waiting for J3 to complete B1; J1 waiting for J4 to complete S1; J5 waiting for L7 release 10:20-13:50.

Higher-order motifs (>2 types):
1. **Batch synchronization barrier:** `Job(3x) -batches- Batch(B1) -processed_on- Machine(OVEN-1) -handled_with- Tool(RACK-7) -requires- Component -in- ComponentLot`. Single event fans out to 9 objects, creating AND-join: earliest jobs cannot proceed until latest (J3 ready 11:55) arrives.
2. **Fixture-machine starvation:** `Job(J3)-holds-Tool(FIX-A)-blocks-Job(J4)-needs-Machine(CNC-2)`. Machine idle 10:05-10:47 while holder J3 is elsewhere and waiter J4 is present. Requires Job-Tool-Machine triangle.
3. **Lot-induced cross-batch coupling:** `ComponentLot(L7)-contains-Component(C2 for J2 in B1/S2, C5 for J5 in B2/S3)`. `Quarantine Lot` 10:20 to `Release Lot` 13:50 co-relates L7,C2,C5,J2,J5 but inconsistently propagated to Jobs. Couples otherwise disjoint B1 vs B2 and S2 vs S3.
4. **Calibration serialization + shipment consolidation chain:** `Job(J5,J6)-tested_on-Machine(TEST-1)-requires-Tool(CAL-3)-then-Job-packed-into-Shipment(S3)`. TEST-1 idle 16:35-17:25 due to CAL-3 recalibration 16:35-17:20, then S3 adds 35 min post-pack wait.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition
All results are descriptive gaps under stated counterfactuals, not causal proof. Temporal succession alone does not prove resource causation.

**1-2. B1 synchronization waiting for J1 and J2.** J1 ready 08:10, J2 ready 08:25, J3 ready 11:55, B1 starts 12:05. Raw waits: J1 3h55 (235 min), J2 3h40 (220 min), J3 10 min. Counterfactual A: trust readiness timestamps and assume OVEN-1+RACK-7 were feasible earlier, with batch policy requiring full B1 (J1+J2+J3). Then unavoidable wait = wait for last arrival: J1 excess = 235-10=225 min (3h45), J2 excess = 220-10=210 min (3h30). Counterfactual B: allow size-2 early batch of J1+J2 at ~08:25. Then avoidable wait ~210-225 min minus cure re-scheduling effects. Caveat: if J2's readiness at 08:25 is invalid because C2/L7 was already quarantined at 10:20 (and arguably before), or if OVEN-1/RACK-7 were down, the excess is overestimated.

**3. FIX-A blocking for J4.** J4 arrives CNC-2 09:50, FIX-A held by J3 09:00-11:40, CNC-2 idle 10:05-10:47, J4 starts 11:45. Total presence-to-start = 115 min. Counterfactual: with second FIX-A instance, J4 could start at earliest machine-free time 10:05. Plausibly associated delay = 11:45-10:05 = 100 min (1h40). Stricter counterfactual (start at arrival 09:50 if no setup contention) gives 115 min. This assumes CNC-2 idle truly means capable and staffed, and that no other hidden prerequisite (program, operator, C4 availability) blocked J4. The 5-min gap 11:40 release to 11:45 start is consistent with handling, not additional contention.

**4. S1 consolidation delay on J1.** J1 packed 13:30, J4 packed 19:10, S1 dispatch 19:25. Pack-to-dispatch for J1 = 325 min (5h25? 13:30 to 19:25 = 5h55 = 355 min). Let handling = 15 min (19:10 to 19:25). Consolidation wait = 19:10-13:30 = 340 min (5h40). Counterfactual: if S1 followed S2 norm (dispatch 25 min after last pack) but still consolidated, J1 wait is unchanged; if J1 allowed solo dispatch at S2 norm (13:30+25 min=13:55), saving = 330 min (5h30). If S3 norm (35 min), saving = 320 min. Assumes J1 was shippable at pack and transport was available; ignores cost of split shipment.

**5. CAL-3 delay for J6.** J6 ready 16:05, J5 tests 16:10-16:35, CAL-3 recal 16:35-17:20, J6 tests 17:25-17:50. Ready-to-start = 80 min. TEST-1 idle 16:35-17:25 while J6 waits. Counterfactual: with pre-calibrated spare CAL-3 or off-critical-path calibration, J6 could start at ~16:10-16:35 window (if TEST-1 serialized) or at 16:35. Conservative attribution to calibration policy = idle-machine-while-waiting overlap = 50 min (16:35-17:25). Full ready-to-start = 80 min, of which ~5-10 min is queuing behind J5. Assumes TEST-1 idle means ready, and recal duration is policy-fixed, not condition-triggered by J5's test.

**6. B2/RACK-7 coupling.** J4 ready 13:10, J5 ready 14:40, B2 start 14:50. Waits: J4 100 min, J5 10 min. RACK-7 quarantine until 14:35, OVEN-1 idle 12:55-14:50. Counterfactual: with independent rack, B2 could start at max(J4,J5 ready, OVEN free, L7 release 13:50) = 14:40, saving J4 only 10 min beyond observed? Actually J4 still waits for J5 until 14:40. Deeper counterfactual allowing J4 alone at 13:10 (OVEN free, RACK free) saves 100 min but violates batch-size policy and ignores L7 effect on J5 (J5 ready 14:40 is after 13:50 release, so L7 added 50 min upstream to J5 if earliest otherwise 13:10). Shows interaction: synchronization + tool + lot jointly determine B2.

## 4. What flattening misses
- **Job viewpoint:** Visible: per-job sequence Release->Stage->Process->Inspect->[Rework]->Pack->Dispatch and medians (e.g. Pack->Dispatch 1.1h). Hidden/duplicated: waits for other jobs (B1 barrier, S1 consolidation), tool holds, lot quarantine (especially L7 events not linked). Joint `Batch Cure` appears as 3 separate Job events, inflating frequency and averaging away the 225-min skew between J1 and J3. Misleading: Inspect->Pack 1.0h (n=4) vs Inspect->Rework 0.2h suggests rework is fast, while true delay is J1 waiting 5h40 in Pack for S1.
- **Machine viewpoint:** Visible: per-machine util 44-58% and typical sequences. Hidden: why idle gaps occur. CNC-2 idle 10:05-10:47 looks like lack of demand, but J4 was present 09:50 waiting for FIX-A. OVEN-1 idle 12:55-14:50 looks like starvation, but cause is RACK-7 cleaning to 14:35 + B2 sync to J5 14:40, not OVEN capacity. Misleading: low util => no bottleneck, yet jobs wait long for non-machine resources.
- **Batch viewpoint (not provided, inferred):** Visible: Load->Cure->Unload per batch (2 events). Hidden: within-batch unfairness (J1 vs J3) and cross-batch tool linkage via RACK-7. Frequency 2 suggests low impact, but barrier affects 5 jobs. Misleading: average batch lead time hides that early arrivals subsidize late arrivals.
- **Tool viewpoint:** Visible if flattened: FIX-A acquire 09:00 release 11:40, RACK-7 holds for B1/B2, CAL-3 use + recal 16:35-17:20. Hidden in given logs: no `Wait for Tool` activity, so contention must be inferred from acquire/release overlap. Duplication: CAL-3 recal relates to no Job but blocks J6. Misleading without it: TEST-1 idle 16:35-17:25 appears as operator idle.
- **ComponentLot viewpoint:** Visible: Quarantine 10:20 -> Release 13:50 for L7 (3h30). Hidden in Job flattening due to inconsistent ETL (events lack Job link in source). Thus Job DFG shows J5 delay as unexplained Stage time, while Lot view shows policy block. Misleading: Job-flattened frequencies suggest J2 and J5 are independent; Lot view shows they share fate via L7, with J2's override as exception.
- **Shipment viewpoint:** Visible: Create->Load 0.8h, Load->Dispatch 0.3h (n=3). Hidden: packed-but-not-loaded wait (J1 13:30 to 19:10). The log measures shipment lifecycle after creation, not job waiting for partners. Misleading: shipment performance looks excellent while J1's total Pack->Dispatch is 5h55.

Why low util coexists with long waiting: utilization = busy_time / wall_time for that machine only. Jobs wait for synchronization (need all B1 jobs), complementary resources (FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3), policy holds (L7 quarantine, S1 consolidation) while machines starve. Object-centric systems exhibit blocking/starvation, not just saturation: one object's hold propagates as another machine's idle.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence
**Narrative A – J1: early batch arrival to late shipment (J1,C1,L8,B1,OVEN-1,RACK-7,S1,J4).** Temporal precedence: C1 in L8 (no quarantine observed) -> J1 ready 08:10 -> B1 start 12:05 with J2,J3 -> J1 packed 13:30 -> S1 dispatch 19:25 after J4 packed 19:10. Resource feasibility: OVEN-1+RACK-7 could physically cure J1 earlier, but policy requires full B1; S1 requires both J1+J4. Policy constraints: batch-size rule, shipment-consolidation rule. Causal speculation (not proven): J3 lateness (11:55) caused J1's 225-min excess; J4's downstream lateness (FIX-A + B2) caused J1's 340-min shipment wait. Alternative: J1 readiness may be premature (early staging) or OVEN unavailable earlier.

**Narrative B – J4: fixture block to batch to shipment anchor (J4,C4,FIX-A,CNC-2,B2,RACK-7,OVEN-1,S1,J1).** Temporal: J3 holds FIX-A 09:00-11:40 -> J4 arrives 09:50 -> CNC idle 10:05-10:47 -> J4 processes 11:45 -> J4 ready for B2 13:10 waits to 14:50 (RACK to 14:35, J5 to 14:40) -> J4 packed 19:10 anchors S1. Feasibility: CNC-2 free but infeasible without FIX-A; B2 infeasible without RACK-7 and J5. Policy: single FIX-A, single RACK-7, B2 completion rule. Speculation: FIX-A contention plausibly added ~100 min to J4, which then contributed to J1's S1 wait, creating cascade J3->J4->J1 across Tool->Machine->Batch->Shipment. Alternative: J4 may have lacked operator/program until 11:45 regardless.

**Narrative C – L7 lot split with override (L7,C2,C5,J2,J5,B1,B2,S2,S3 + CAL-3 chain for J5/J6).** Temporal: Quarantine L7 10:20 -> J2 already in B1 (override) cures 12:05 -> Release 13:50 -> J5 ready 14:40 -> B2 14:50 -> J5 tests 16:10-16:35 -> CAL recal to 17:20 -> J6 tests 17:25. Feasibility: J5 could not (per policy) proceed pre-release; J2 physically could but procedurally should not have. Policy: lot-hold + supervisor-override + calibration-after-test. Speculation: L7 hold coupled J5 to J2's lot, delaying B2 and thus S1/S3; CAL-3 policy then coupled J6 to J5, extending to S3. Without override, B1 would have missed J2 entirely (worse sync). Alternative: quarantine may have been scoped to C5 only, or J5's 14:40 readiness reflects unrelated work, weakening lot attribution.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers
1. **Reduce synchronization: cap batch wait / allow incomplete oven loads.** Target: B1/B2 barrier. Benefit: cut 200+ min for early jobs like J1/J2. Helped: early-ready jobs, OVEN utilization smoothing. Harmed: late jobs (J3) may wait for next cycle; energy per unit rises. Cost/risk: more cycles, RACK cleaning load rises. Perverse metric: `oven fill %` incentivizes holding for full loads. Evaluation: compare OCEL `Batch Cure` fan-out size vs Job waiting (extract features per Batch, A/B time-window rule, track OTG Batch->Job waiting, not just Machine DFG).
2. **Tools: pool FIX-A (second fixture) + spare RACK, stagger CAL-3 calibration.** Target: FIX-A/CAL-3/RACK contention. Benefit: ~100 min for J4-type waits, ~50 min for J6-type waits, decouples B1/B2. Helped: J4,J6,J1 (via cascade). Harmed: none directly, but capital/maintenance tied up. Cost/risk: calibration drift if spare not tracked; extra fixtures need storage/qualification. Perverse metric: `tool utilization` would punish spares. Evaluation: Tool-centric ODFG + ETOT: measure acquire-to-release overlap and `TEST-1 idle while CAL-3 busy` time; flatten by Tool, not Machine.
3. **Shipment policy: time-window or split shipment for S1-type pairs.** Target: S1 consolidation. Benefit: save ~5h30 for J1 if dispatched at 13:55. Helped: J1, customer of early job. Harmed: J4 shipment cost rises if truck half-empty; S1 carbon/cost per unit. Cost/risk: freight + handling. Perverse metric: `cost per shipment` incentivizes over-consolidation. Evaluation: track Job Pack->Dispatch by Shipment (Shipment flattening extended to include pre-Load wait) and total landed cost; randomized dispatch-window pilot on S1 lane.
4. **Improve logging, not operations: fix ETL to propagate Lot events to Jobs + log explicit waits.** Target: data artifact. Benefit: makes L7 coupling and tool queues visible, prevents false independence conclusions. Helped: analysts, future prioritization (J2 override audit). Harmed: none operationally; short-term dashboard noise as hidden waits surface. Cost/risk: pipeline rework, historic recomputation. Perverse metric: `mean Job duration` will appear to worsen when waits become visible. Evaluation: before/after interaction summary (`ocel_objects_interactions_summary`) Job–ComponentLot count 4->expected 6+; check `discover_objects_graph` gains L7-J5 edge.
5. **Kitting / lot segregation: avoid placing coupled jobs in same lot across batches/shipments (L7 split).** Target: lot coupling. Benefit: J2/B1/S2 decoupled from J5/B2/S3; quarantine blast radius halved. Helped: J5-type jobs. Harmed: incoming QC workload, smaller lots = more holds. Cost/risk: inventory fragmentation. Perverse metric: `lots quarantined` may rise with more lots. Evaluation: ComponentLot OTG analysis + `extract_ocel_features` per Job (lot-size, quarantine exposure) vs waiting; pilot segregated lots for critical components.

## 8. Falsification and additional data
Weakening observations for top rank (batch+RACK barrier):
1. OVEN-1 or RACK-7 logged downtime/operator absence 08:10-12:05 making early start infeasible.
2. J1/J2 readiness timestamps are system creation, not physical arrival; goods were not actually ready until 11:55.
3. J3 11:55 arrival is itself caused by FIX-A or L8 hold, making batch wait a symptom, not root cause.
4. B1 cure recipe requires full thermal mass (3 jobs) for quality; partial load would have required rework, negating saving.
5. RACK-7 cleaning 12:05-14:35 is fixed cure-dependent duration, not avoidable by spare rack due to  constraints.
6. Re-running with Batch-flattened performance shows B1 total cycle efficient (energy/unit) and J1 shipment wait dominates total lateness, demoting batch rank.

Additional fields/event types to discriminate:
1. Explicit `Wait for Tool / Wait for Batch / Wait for Lot` start-end events with reason code.
2. Tool acquire-queue with request timestamp per Job (distinguishes arrival vs request).
3. Operator/shift calendar + OVEN/RACK capability matrix to validate idle-means-available assumption.
4. Component physical availability timestamp vs system readiness, plus override authorization + reason for J2.
5. Lot quarantine scope (lot vs component), inspection result, and per-component release timestamps.
6. Shipment truck schedule, earliest feasible dispatch, cost, and pack-to-shippable QA flag for J1 13:30.
7. CAL-3 calibration trigger (time-based vs result-based) and spare availability log.
8. RACK-7 cleaning standard time vs quarantine hold split, and second-rack feasibility.
