## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional verdict: mainly confounded. Confidence: 78/100.** The supplied completed-case sample does not support management’s claim that Orion itself causes longer completion. Orion’s crude mean is 21.96 hours versus Nova’s 15.24, a 6.71-hour disadvantage. Yet Orion handles far more high-complexity, night, high-load, and compliance-required work. Within every observed complexity stratum, the sign reverses: Orion is faster by 2.14 hours for Low, 2.21 for Medium, and 3.43 for High. Standardizing both groups to the full sample’s complexity distribution gives 17.14 hours for Orion and 19.65 for Nova, a 2.51-hour Orion advantage. That reversal is the strongest evidence for confounding and possibly a beneficial Orion mechanism. 

The strongest evidence against a confident causal verdict is nonrandom routing, coarse complexity, extreme shift/load imbalance, sparse overlap, and conditioning on completion. A harmful Orion queue effect, a beneficial certified-reviewer effect, or both could remain after adjustment.

The **total effect** of routing to Orion under the current operating regime includes downstream queue waiting, reviewer-pool service, compliance coordination, and Request Info rework. A **direct effect** would hold those mediators fixed, but that estimand is weakly defined because “Orion” is an operational bundle. The proposal could succeed if Nova has spare equivalent capacity and Orion’s queue is intrinsically slower; fail if the crude gap is merely case mix; or backfire by overloading Nova, removing difficult cases from more-certified reviewers, and changing both queues’ waiting times. A 50% policy effect therefore cannot be read from the observed case-level association.

The graph also omits unspecified post-process causes of the 30-day outcome. A static DAG suppresses feedback: earlier routing changes each group’s queue, Request Info creates later workload, and the routing engine may react to those evolving queues. Thus a 50% reallocation creates interference and a different estimand from one-case reassignment.

**Final recommendation.** Do not reassign 50% of Orion’s future workload on the basis of the crude mean. The observed disadvantage reverses after even coarse complexity control, while the remaining comparisons lack overlap and exclude unfinished cases. Preserve mandatory compliance and reserve Orion’s certified capacity for cases that are not demonstrably dual-eligible. First, instrument the routing decision and reconstruct both queues. In parallel, test a bounded night/compliance-capacity intervention because the descriptive advantage within complexity is concentrated in waiting rather than review service. Then run a small blocked randomized—or genuinely quasi-random tie-break—routing pilot among cases that both groups can safely handle, with capacity caps and intent-to-treat analysis.

The decision criterion should be a joint policy outcome: completed and censored cycle time, queue spillovers to non-pilot cases, rework, and independently assessed quality. Expand reassignment only if the pilot shows a credible cycle-time benefit at the intended allocation share without worsening Nova backlog or quality. Otherwise retain case-mix-aware routing and address the identified queue or compliance mechanism rather than relabeling work.

df = pd.read_csv(StringIO(CSV))
df["rework_case"] = df["rework_count"].gt(0)

GROUPS = ["Orion", "Nova"]
COMPLEXITIES = ["Low", "Medium", "High"]
SHIFTS = ["Day", "Night"]


def heading(text: str) -> None:
    print("\n" + "=" * 88)
    print(text)
    print("=" * 88)


def warn_if_small(n: int, label: str) -> None:
    if n < 3:
        print(
            f"WARNING: {label} has only {n} case(s); "
            "estimates are poorly supported."
        )


# 1) Crude group comparison
heading("CRUDE CYCLE-TIME COMPARISON")

crude = (
    df.groupby("group")["cycle_time_h"]
    .agg(cases="size", mean_cycle_h="mean", median_cycle_h="median")
    .reindex(GROUPS)
)

print(crude.round(3).to_string())

crude_contrast = (
    crude.loc["Orion", "mean_cycle_h"]
    - crude.loc["Nova", "mean_cycle_h"]
)
print(f"\nCrude Orion - Nova mean contrast: {crude_contrast:+.3f} h")


# 2) Complexity-stratified comparisons
heading("COMPLEXITY-STRATIFIED CYCLE-TIME COMPARISONS")

stratified = (
    df.groupby(["complexity", "group"])["cycle_time_h"]
    .agg(cases="size", mean_cycle_h="mean", median_cycle_h="median")
    .reindex(
        pd.MultiIndex.from_product(
            [COMPLEXITIES, GROUPS],
            names=["complexity", "group"],
        )
    )
)

print(stratified.round(3).to_string())

for complexity in COMPLEXITIES:
    cell = stratified.loc[complexity]

    if cell["mean_cycle_h"].isna().any():
        print(
            f"WARNING: {complexity} lacks one group; "
            "no within-stratum contrast."
        )
        continue

    contrast = (
        cell.loc["Orion", "mean_cycle_h"]
        - cell.loc["Nova", "mean_cycle_h"]
    )
    print(f"{complexity}: Orion - Nova = {contrast:+.3f} h")

    for group in GROUPS:
        warn_if_small(
            int(cell.loc[group, "cases"]),
            f"{complexity}/{group}",
        )


# 3) Complexity-standardized contrast
heading("COMPLEXITY-STANDARDIZED CONTRAST")

# Common weighting distribution:
# empirical complexity proportions among all 30 completed cases.
weights = (
    df["complexity"]
    .value_counts(normalize=True)
    .reindex(COMPLEXITIES)
)

stratum_means = (
    df.groupby(["complexity", "group"])["cycle_time_h"]
    .mean()
    .unstack("group")
    .reindex(COMPLEXITIES)[GROUPS]
)

if stratum_means.isna().any().any():
    raise ValueError(
        "Standardization requires both groups in every complexity stratum."
    )

standardized_means = stratum_means.mul(weights, axis=0).sum(axis=0)

print("Common weights (full-sample empirical distribution):")
print(weights.rename("weight").round(4).to_string())

print("\nStratum means:")
print(stratum_means.round(3).to_string())

for group in GROUPS:
    terms = " + ".join(
        f"{weights.loc[c]:.4f}*{stratum_means.loc[c, group]:.3f}"
        for c in COMPLEXITIES
    )
    print(
        f"{group}: {terms} = "
        f"{standardized_means.loc[group]:.3f} h"
    )

standardized_contrast = (
    standardized_means["Orion"]
    - standardized_means["Nova"]
)
print(
    "Standardized Orion - Nova contrast: "
    f"{standardized_contrast:+.3f} h"
)


# 4) Overlap counts for complexity x shift x resource group
heading("OVERLAP: COMPLEXITY x SHIFT x RESOURCE GROUP")

# Reindex to the full grid so that zero-support combinations are visible.
full_index = pd.MultiIndex.from_product(
    [COMPLEXITIES, SHIFTS, GROUPS],
    names=["complexity", "shift", "group"],
)

overlap = (
    df.groupby(["complexity", "shift", "group"])
    .size()
    .reindex(full_index, fill_value=0)
    .rename("cases")
    .reset_index()
)

print(overlap.to_string(index=False))

for complexity, shift in product(COMPLEXITIES, SHIFTS):
    cell = overlap[
        (overlap["complexity"] == complexity)
        & (overlap["shift"] == shift)
    ].set_index("group")["cases"]

    n_orion = int(cell.loc["Orion"])
    n_nova = int(cell.loc["Nova"])
    label = f"{complexity}/{shift}"

    if n_orion == 0 or n_nova == 0:
        print(
            f"WARNING: {label} has no two-group overlap "
            f"(Orion={n_orion}, Nova={n_nova}); "
            "positivity fails in this cell."
        )
    elif min(n_orion, n_nova) < 3:
        print(
            f"WARNING: {label} has weak overlap "
            f"(Orion={n_orion}, Nova={n_nova}); "
            "at least one arm has <3 cases."
        )


# 5) Trace variants
heading("TRACE-VARIANT COMPARISON")

variant_counts = (
    df.groupby(["trace", "group"])
    .size()
    .unstack("group", fill_value=0)
    .reindex(columns=GROUPS, fill_value=0)
    .sort_index()
)

print(variant_counts.to_string())

for trace, row in variant_counts.iterrows():
    if (row == 0).any():
        print(
            "WARNING: Variant lacks support in one group: "
            f"{trace}"
        )
    elif row.min() < 3:
        print(
            "WARNING: Variant has <3 cases in at least "
            f"one group: {trace}"
        )


# 6) Group-specific frequency DFGs
heading("DIRECTLY-FOLLOWS COMPARISON USING PM4PY")

# parse_event_log_string is used only to recover activity order.
# Its synthetic ordering timestamps are not used for performance analysis.
# discover_dfg returns frequency relations, not timing performance.
dfgs = {}
starts = {}
ends = {}

for group in GROUPS:
    trace_strings = (
        df.loc[df["group"].eq(group), "trace"]
        .str.replace(">", ",", regex=False)
        .tolist()
    )

    log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(
        trace_strings,
        sep=",",
    )

    dfgs[group], starts[group], ends[group] = (
        pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
    )

all_edges = sorted(
    set(dfgs["Orion"])
    | set(dfgs["Nova"])
)

group_sizes = df["group"].value_counts()
edge_rows = []

for edge in all_edges:
    orion_count = int(dfgs["Orion"].get(edge, 0))
    nova_count = int(dfgs["Nova"].get(edge, 0))

    edge_rows.append(
        {
            "directly_follows": f"{edge[0]} -> {edge[1]}",
            "Orion_count": orion_count,
            "Nova_count": nova_count,
            "Orion_per_case": (
                orion_count / group_sizes["Orion"]
            ),
            "Nova_per_case": (
                nova_count / group_sizes["Nova"]
            ),
        }
    )

edge_comparison = pd.DataFrame(edge_rows)

print(
    edge_comparison
    .round(3)
    .to_string(index=False)
)

print("\nStart activities:", starts)
print("End activities:", ends)

for _, row in edge_comparison.iterrows():
    if (
        row["Orion_count"] == 0
        or row["Nova_count"] == 0
    ):
        print(
            "WARNING: DFG relation appears in only one group: "
            f"{row['directly_follows']}"
        )


heading("INTERPRETATION WARNING")

print(
    "These are descriptive diagnostics. Standardization, variant counts, "
    "and frequency DFGs do not identify a causal effect under nonrandom "
    "routing, poor overlap, interference between queues, or "
    "completed-case selection."
)

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Whether Orion versus Nova has any total causal effect among safely exchangeable cases.** The single most valuable design change is a blocked randomized routing pilot restricted to cases certified as eligible for both groups.

2. **How much residual case difficulty remains within Low, Medium, and High.** The single most valuable additional field is the pre-assignment `routing_score_and_reason`, captured before the group decision and including manual-override justification.

3. **Whether waiting differences are caused by queue congestion, compliance synchronization, or staffing composition.** The single most valuable additional field is a timestamped `queue_state_snapshot` at Classify containing WIP and available certified capacity for both groups.

4. **How completed-case conditioning changes the comparison.** The single most valuable design change is an inception cohort retaining every routed case, including open and abandoned cases, with status and censoring timestamps.

5. **Whether faster processing trades off against decision quality.** The single most valuable additional field is a blinded, independently adjudicated `quality_audit_score` measured consistently for both groups.
