# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

To decompose the case flow time without double counting, we define the timeline for a specific case $c$. The process follows a parallel fork-join pattern. The total flow time is the sum of sequential stages, where the parallel stage duration is determined by the *critical branch* (the slowest path).

**Definitions:**
Let $t_{arrival}$, $t_{fork}$, $t_{start}^{i}$, $t_{end}^{i}$, $t_{final\_start}$, $t_{final\_end}$ be the timestamps for case $c$, where $i \in \{A, B, C\}$.

1.  **Upstream Elapsed:** $T_{upstream} = t_{fork} - t_{arrival}$.
    *   *Observation:* Time spent before parallel work is permitted.
2.  **Branch Queue:** $T_{queue}^{i} = t_{start}^{i} - t_{fork}$.
    *   *Observation:* Wait time specific to branch $i$ after the fork.
3.  **Branch Service:** $T_{service}^{i} = t_{end}^{i} - t_{start}^{i}$.
    *   *Observation:* Active processing time for branch $i$.
4.  **Branch Total Duration:** $T_{branch}^{i} = T_{queue}^{i} + T_{service}^{i}$.
5.  **Join Ready:** $t_{join} = \max(t_{end}^{A}, t_{end}^{B}, t_{end}^{C})$.
    *   *Definition:* The moment the case is eligible for the final activity.
6.  **Critical Branch:** $i^* = \operatorname{argmax}_{i} (t_{end}^{i})$.
    *   *Assumption:* In case of ties, we select deterministically (Priority: A > B > C) for labeling, though physically multiple branches may finish simultaneously at the critical time.
7.  **Synchronization Wait:** $T_{sync}^{i} = t_{join} - t_{end}^{i}$.
    *   *Note:* $T_{sync}^{i^*} = 0$. For non-critical branches, this is the time the completed branch waits for the critical branch.
8.  **Elapsed Fork-Join Stage:** $T_{stage} = t_{join} - t_{fork} = T_{branch}^{i^*}$.
    *   *Crucial Logic:* The stage duration is defined by the maximum branch duration, not the sum.
9.  **Downstream Queue:** $T_{down\_queue} = t_{final\_start} - t_{join}$.
    *   *Observation:* Wait time for the final resource after synchronization.
10. **Final Service:** $T_{final} = t_{final\_end} - t_{final\_start}$.
11. **Total Flow Time:** $T_{total} = t_{final\_end} - t_{arrival}$.

**Why summing synchronization waits double counts elapsed time:**
The total flow time is a linear timeline: $T_{total} = T_{upstream} + T_{stage} + T_{down\_queue} + T_{final}$.
The stage time $T_{stage}$ is already fully accounted for by the critical branch's duration ($T_{queue}^{i^*} + T_{service}^{i^*}$).
The synchronization wait for a non-critical branch $j$ is $T_{sync}^{j} = T_{stage} - T_{branch}^{j}$.
If one were to sum all synchronization waits ($\sum T_{sync}^{i}$) and add them to the flow time components, one would be adding time that has *already elapsed* during the $T_{stage}$ period. Specifically, the clock ticks once during the stage. The non-critical branches are idle during their $T_{sync}$, but that idle time is *inside* the $T_{stage}$ window. Adding $\sum T_{sync}^{i}$ effectively adds "shadow time" to the case duration, violating conservation of time. Furthermore, summing branch services ($\sum T_{service}^{i}$) would triple-count the parallel elapsed time.

**Assumptions:**
1.  **Timestamp Precision:** All times are accurate to one minute; calculations are in minutes.
2.  **Non-Preemption:** Once a branch starts, it runs to completion without interruption.
3.  **Fork Logic:** `fork_release` is the hard constraint for branch starts; no branch starts before this time.
4.  **Join Logic:** `final_start` cannot occur before `join_ready` ($t_{join}$).
5.  **Tie-Breaking:** For the `critical_branch` label, if multiple branches share the maximum end time, 'A' takes precedence over 'B', and 'B' over 'C'. This is for labeling consistency only; physically, they are co-critical.

| **Critical Frequency** | 2 / 12 (16.7%) | 8 / 12 (66.7%) | 2 / 12 (16.7%) |
| **Mean Sync Wait** | 7.1 min | 0.0 min | 26.5 min |

*Note: Mean Sync Wait is calculated only over cases where the branch was **not** critical. For critical branches, sync wait is 0.*

**Influence Ranking:**
1.  **Branch B (Highest Influence):** Despite having a lower mean service time (37.5) than A (50.0), B is the critical branch in 67% of cases. This is driven by high queueing variability (5 to 30 mins) and service variability (30 to 45 mins). B determines the `join_ready` time in the majority of the flow time tail (e.g., F07, F12).
2.  **Branch A (Moderate Influence):** A has the highest constant service time (50 mins) but zero queueing. It becomes critical only when B and C are unusually fast (e.g., F11). It acts as a "floor" for the stage duration; the stage can never be shorter than 50 minutes (plus upstream).
3.  **Branch C (Low Influence):** C is rarely critical (only when B is fast and C has a service spike, e.g., F08). Its low service time generally keeps it well behind A and B.

**Reasoning:**
Equating largest mean service time (A) with most influential branch is incorrect here. Influence is determined by $P(\text{Branch}_i = \text{Critical}) \times \text{Impact on Join}$. Branch B's queueing instability makes it the primary driver of total flow time variance. Branch A provides a deterministic baseline.

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

**Counterfactual 1: Reduce A Service Time by 20% (50m $\to$ 40m)**
-   **Direct Benefit:** Only applies when A is critical.
-   **Cases Affected:** F01, F11.
-   **Impact:**
    -   F01: A_end moves 09:00 $\to$ 08:50. B_end is 08:55. New Critical = B (08:55). Saving = 5 mins.
    -   F11: A_end moves 11:30 $\to$ 11:20. B_end is 11:20. New Critical = Tie (11:20). Saving = 10 mins.
    -   Other 10 cases: **Zero benefit**. B or C remains critical.
-   **Secondary Effects:** A becomes even less likely to be critical. Synchronization wait for A increases in non-critical cases (more idle time for A resources).
-   **Total Estimated Savings:** 15 minutes across 12 cases (Avg 1.25 min/case). Low ROI.

**Counterfactual 2: Reduce B Queue Delay by 50%**
-   **Direct Benefit:** Applies to all cases where B is critical or co-critical, and reduces B's end time in others.
-   **Cases Affected:** All 12 cases (B queue exists in all).
-   **Impact:**
    -   Average B Queue reduces from ~19.6m to ~9.8m.
    -   In critical cases (e.g., F07: Queue 30 $\to$ 15), `join_ready` drops significantly.
    -   F07 Example: B_end moves 10:55 $\to$ 10:40. A_end is 10:30. New Critical = B (10:40). Saving = 15 mins.
    -   In cases where A is critical (F11), B reduction might not change `join_ready` if B_end remains < A_end.
-   **Secondary Effects:** Reduces variance in `join_ready`. May shift criticality to A more often (A becomes the bottleneck more frequently).
-   **Total Estimated Savings:** High. B is critical in 67% of cases.

**Counterfactual 3: Add Final-Station Capacity (Reduce Downstream Queue)**
-   **Direct Benefit:** Reduces `final_start - join_ready`.
-   **Cases Affected:** All 12 cases.
-   **Impact:**
    -   Current Mean Downstream Queue = ~15 mins.
    -   If capacity is doubled, assume queue halves or eliminates (depending on arrival rate).
    -   Saving is additive to total flow time regardless of branch criticality.
    -   Unlike branch improvements, this does not shift the bottleneck upstream; it simply clears the exit.
-   **Secondary Effects:** Resources F1/F2 utilization might drop if work isn't available fast enough, but flow time improves uniformly.
-   **Total Estimated Savings:** Moderate to High (applies to 100% of cases).

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

**Recommendation 1: Stabilize Branch B Queue (Priority: High)**
-   **Action:** Investigate the resource or logic causing B's queue (5-30 min variance). Implement a dedicated resource or priority queue for B to ensure start time $\approx$ `fork_release`.
-   **Throughput:** Likely increases as B is the primary constraint on `join_ready`.
-   **Flow-Time Tail:** Significantly reduces the tail (cases like F07, F12 where B queue is high).
-   **Resource Utilization:** May require adding a B-resource or optimizing the existing one.
-   **New Bottleneck:** If B queue is eliminated, **Branch A** (fixed 50m) will become the critical path for almost all cases. The process floor becomes 50m stage time.
-   **Pilot:** Measure B queue causes for 1 week. Simulate impact of B_queue = 0 on total flow time.

**Recommendation 2: Optimize Final Station Handshake (Priority: Medium)**
-   **Action:** Reduce `downstream_queue` by allowing `final_start` to trigger immediately upon `join_ready` (e.g., if F1/F2 are idle). Currently, there is a consistent 10-20 min lag after join.
-   **Throughput:** Increases final station throughput.
-   **Flow-Time Tail:** Reduces tail uniformly across all cases.
-   **Synchronization Effects:** Does not affect branch synchronization, but ensures the benefit of branch improvements is realized immediately.
-   **Risks:** If the lag is due to a batch release policy (e.g., "release decisions at :00 and :30"), changing this might increase administrative overhead.
-   **Pilot:** Check if `final_start` aligns with specific clock intervals. If so, move to continuous flow.

# 7. Limits of inference

Based strictly on the supplied timestamps, several critical factors cannot be identified:

1.  **Causality of Queues:** We observe `B_queue` varies from 5 to 30 minutes. We cannot determine if this is due to resource contention (busy server), batched releases, or external dependencies. The data shows correlation, not causation.
2.  **Branch Resource Capacity:** We know Final has resources F1/F2. We do not know how many resources perform A, B, or C. If B has only one resource and high arrival rate, the queue is capacity-driven. If B has 10 resources, the queue might be policy-driven.
3.  **Work Preemption:** We assume non-preemptive service (start to end is continuous). If technicians switch tasks, `A_service` might include hidden wait time, inflating the service estimate.
4.  **Queue vs. Release Policy:** The `downstream_queue` (`final_start - join_ready`) is consistently 10-20 minutes. This could be a physical queue (F1/F2 busy) or a policy (e.g., "Decisions only reviewed at :10 and :40"). Timestamps alone cannot distinguish congestion from administrative batching.
5.  **Synchronization Causality:** We calculate `sync_wait` based on the assumption that `final_start` *must* wait for `join_ready`. If the process allows `F` to start with partial data (speculative execution), our critical path analysis overestimates the necessity of the wait.
6.  **Upstream Black Box:** `arrival` to `fork_release` is a black box (15-20 mins). We cannot optimize what we cannot see.

**Conclusion:** The analysis identifies *where* time is spent (Branch B queue + Final downstream queue), but not *why*. Process redesign should begin with qualitative observation of Branch B resources and Final Station release policies.
