## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 asks who prepared and released a completed payment. Its purpose is to give someone independent of the initiator a meaningful opportunity to challenge the transfer while it can still be stopped. Those are separate findings. A system check can catch a duplicate, and an independent manager can catch a false service claim. Neither fact changes the identities on the release record.

| Objective or threat | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stop a duplicate, wrong amount or payment outside the operator’s authority | Can block a duplicate, limit breach or basic PO mismatch before release. A plausible but wrong in-limit amount can pass. | Can compare invoice, quantity, price and authorization with source records and withhold approval. | Can detect overpayment by reconciliation and initiate recall or recovery if still feasible. | Caps the exception below EUR 100,000 and requires a clean pass. An in-limit error still needs review. |
| Prevent diversion to a substituted bank account or sanctioned payee | Checks ownership, recent bank changes and sanctions. An override can defeat the warning. A pass cannot identify who actually controls compromised credentials. | Can challenge a changed destination against trusted vendor details and stop settlement pending confirmation. | Can detect a diverted transfer through bank and vendor reconciliation. Recovery depends on timely bank action. | Excludes bank changes in the preceding seven days and vendors active less than 180 days. An established vendor can still be compromised. |
| Establish that goods or services were received and the charge makes commercial sense | Basic PO matching cannot authenticate a service receipt or establish value. | A reviewer can corroborate delivery with the receiving unit and challenge implausible terms. Simply inspecting a fabricated receipt can miss the fraud. | Independent confirmation can uncover fabrication after release. A finding alone does not return funds. | Requires independent approval and audit but supplies no evidence of delivery itself. |
| Prevent unilateral authorization, including misuse of a manager’s override credential | Can enforce limits and log warnings. Accepting a borrowed credential defeats attribution to the manager. | A personally authenticated, conflict-free L3+ manager who reviews the specific transaction and can veto it supplies an independent decision. | Can detect credential reuse or a self-override by reconciling identity, session and approval records. Attribution may require investigation. | Requires a token before preparation and excludes overrides. The token alone supplies neither a second decision nor proof of identity. |
| Resist collusion or systematic rubber-stamping by formally different people | Cannot assess collusion. Syntactically valid invoices can pass every check. | A reviewer outside the suspect relationship can obtain independent evidence. A conflicted or complicit reviewer adds little protection. | Pair analysis and independent re-performance can detect patterns and specific defects. A frequent pairing alone has innocent explanations. | Keeps independent review and audit mandatory during emergencies. All reviewers relying on the same false evidence remains a common weakness. |
| Contain an error before finality and preserve a realistic route to recovery | An enforced block keeps money in the company. A warning has no such effect when the operator can clear it. | A veto before release prevents dispatch. An actionable veto after release can still stop an unsettled transfer. | Detection can lead to a stop, timely recall or later recovery. Recording an audit is distinct from executing any of those actions. | Requires approval before settlement and 100% audit within one business day. The business-day deadline can outlast the bank’s usual 24-hour recall window. |

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

For each case, record the nominal identity result, then test the package against the decisions a competent independent second releaser could have made for that payment.

1. **Identify the credible loss paths.** Consider who could introduce a bad instruction, who benefits, whether delivery and destination can be verified, and the size and recoverability of the exposure. Give a clean AV-2 pass credit only for its six specified checks.
2. **Find the last effective intervention point.** Order preparation, validation, override, human review, release, settlement, audit completion and bank action. Establish whether a reviewer could actually hold or cancel the exact payment and whether edits after approval would require reapproval. A timestamp before settlement shows an opportunity. The bank’s hold status and cutoff establish whether it was actionable.
3. **Test independence and bypass paths.** Identify the humans behind the accounts, their authority, conflicts, access to override credentials, and the origin of the documents they relied on. Consider whether one person or a regular pair could fabricate the instruction and its apparent corroboration. Several checks sharing that weakness provide little additional assurance against it.
4. **Establish detection and correction separately.** Determine coverage of the emergency population, what the auditor tested, when a defect could first be acted upon, and who could instruct the bank. A recall request within 24 hours is a chance of recovery. Bank confirmation or a credit back establishes the outcome. Use a bank-specific cutoff when known.
5. **Grade the evidence and the decision.** Prefer transaction-bound approvals, immutable identity and token records, independent receipt confirmations and bank acknowledgments over activity labels. Record missing evidence as uncertainty. Check all seven ED-1 gates conjunctively. Failure of one denies the permission on the available record even if another control materially reduced loss risk.

| Package assessment | Decision criterion |
|---|---|
| Fully equivalent for this objective and case | Every material loss path has a comparably reliable independent challenge or enforced block while loss can still be prevented. Authority, coverage, resistance to override or collusion, and the operating evidence support that conclusion. For an ED-1 permission finding, all seven conditions must also be established. |
| Partially equivalent | A substitute performs a material part of the second reviewer’s function, but its scope, timing or independence leaves a gap. A low, bounded residual exposure can support tolerating a particular deviation, with its policy status recorded separately. |
| Merely mitigating | A control reduces exposure or helps discover a problem, but the operator can still complete a material bad payment without effective challenge. A late audit or a narrow technical check on a questionable service invoice can fall here. |
| Ineffective for the material threat | The relevant control was unavailable, bypassed, compromised or too late to affect the transfer or give a credible route to recovery. A legitimate outcome does not supply the missing protection. |

Where decisive facts about actual independence, authority or timing are unresolved, give a conditional finding or classify the objective as indeterminate. Equal expected monetary loss can conceal a rare unrecoverable loss, a systematic insider opportunity, unequal detection delays or lack of attributable authorization. It also cannot establish entitlement to an exception with explicit conditions.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control | Prevention | Detection | Correction and limiting condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| AV-2 | A hard block prevents release of an instruction matching a covered rule. | A pass or warning reports results of specific tests at a particular time. | Correction requires someone to cancel or repair the instruction and revalidate. AP-205 shows an effective block. AP-202 shows the effect of a bypassable warning. |
| MG-4 | Informed, independent approval can stop release or, with genuine veto power, stop settlement. | The reviewer may discover false documentation or a changed destination that AV-2 missed. A post-release reviewer examines an action already taken. | After release the manager must be able to reach the bank or hold queue in time. After settlement, ratification alone changes nothing about the transfer. |
| RA-7 | Ordinarily no direct barrier to release. | Audit checks evidence retrospectively. For emergency same-user payments it should cover every case within one business day. | An identified defect plus an executed stop, recall or recovery action gives corrective value. After normal finality the bank may cooperate, but the company cannot reverse the transfer unilaterally. |
| ED-1 | Enforced entry gates restrict who may use the exception and under what risk conditions. | Token chronology, clean validation, independent approval and audit completion can be tested against each use. | ED-1 has no recovery mechanism of its own. It depends on MG-4 and RA-7 while the bank can still act. |

I use release as the boundary for internal authorization and settlement as the boundary for loss prevention. A substantive review between them is **hybrid** if the reviewer can still stop the money. It detects defects in the released instruction and can prevent settlement. If the transfer was already beyond the bank’s stop cutoff, the review is detective only. In AP-207 the 80-minute interval is relevant evidence. In AP-208 the manager had no pre-settlement interval at all.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

Link the payment version to human identity and session, token issuance and effective time, vendor-master history, validation rules and results, manager delegation and signed review, bank acceptance, settlement and stop cutoff, audit start and completion, and recall request and disposition. Preserve event time and ingestion time with synchronized clocks. Record the source of receipt and bank confirmation so an investigator can tell whether two reviewers relied on the same assertion.

| Monitor | Rule and follow-up |
|---|---|
| Same-user rate and emergency use | Divide completed same-human prepare/release payments by all completed payments. Report unknown identity separately, resolve service accounts to people where possible, and split the numerator by verified ED-1, failed gates and unknown gates. Track blocked attempts separately. Investigate convenience use and tokens effective after preparation. |
| Override rate | Divide overridden warnings by warnings, stratified by rule, operator, credential owner and manager. Flag self-overrides, operator/manager session crossover and bank-change-plus-limit overrides for immediate review. Reconcile the identity that acted with the credential’s assignee. |
| Late manager approval | For payments requiring MG-4, report missing approvals and counts before release, between release and settlement, and at or after settlement. Record the remaining stop time, reviewer’s L3+ authority and evidence examined. Escalate a missing approval before the bank cutoff. |
| Audit before recall window | For all emergency same-user releases, measure both audit completion within one business day and completion before the bank-specific recall deadline. Keep missing audits in the denominator. Use release plus 24 hours as an early operational alert when the bank clock is unknown. Track time left to act, recall requests, acknowledgments and amounts recovered. |
| Repeated resource pairs and shared workstations | Count ordered preparer/releaser pairs over a rolling 30 days, their share of each person’s work and the number of available partners on that shift. Compare with cell assignments. Examine device IDs, overlapping sessions, independent authentication and evidence of challenge. Re-perform a sample from u12/u13, including AP-204 and AP-209, using source documents from the receiving unit. |
| Bank-change proximity | Calculate time from the last verified vendor-master change to release. Flag changes within seven days, particularly with a new vendor, override or outage. For ED-1 this is a disqualifying fact. Verify ownership using a previously trusted contact or bank channel. |
| Control outages | Join releases to validation-service health and per-payment results. Flag every ED-1 release without a full clean pre-release run. Reconcile all outage-period releases, unsigned checklists, manual bank confirmations and later revalidations. Escalate pending high-exposure items while a hold is possible. |

Pair concentration, use of one workstation and a reporting relationship are investigation signals. They do not prove collusion or deliberate credential sharing. The fabricated AP-204 receipt and u23’s use of a credential assigned to m09 are specific control failures. Intent, responsibility for the fabrication and how the credential was obtained require separate evidence.

## 7. PM4Py and pandas Checking Code

On this extract, six of eight completed payments violate nominal FEP-1 (75%). AP-205 is a ninth case with a blocked attempt. The u12/u13 pair appears twice among the eight completions. The reported 37 joint payments in 30 days come from the case facts and cannot be reconstructed from these nine traces.

In PM4Py 2.7.23.4, the specified filter call on the completed-payment DataFrame returned the two different-user cases. Taking the complement within completed cases yields AP-201, AP-202, AP-203, AP-206, AP-207 and AP-208. The code checks filter polarity against the actor pairs and handles a DataFrame or convertible event log. It requires a PM4Py version exposing the named public APIs and stops if the returned case set cannot be reconciled. The identity check works on this extract because it contains one prepare/release pair per completed payment and the case facts identify the operators. Rework loops would require matching the preparation and release of the same payment version.

The business-day flag uses the next weekday in UTC, without a holiday calendar. The two 24-hour flags show how the assumed start of the bank clock affects the assessment. For AP-201 an audit start falls within one business day and within 24 hours of settlement, but outside 24 hours of release. None of those observations establishes when the audit finished or whether a recall could have succeeded.

settle = x["Bank_Settlement"].combine_first(x["fact_settlement"])
manager = x["Manager_Approve"].combine_first(x["Manager_Ratify"])
audit = x["Audit_Review"]

x["same_actor_violation"] = x.index.isin(violating)
x["blocked_attempt"] = x.index.isin(blocked)
x["repeated_pair_in_extract"] = x["pair_count_in_extract"].astype("Int64").gt(1)
x["av_full_clean_before_release"] = (
    x["av_result"].eq("full_pass") & x["Automated_Validate"].lt(release)
    & x["Override_Warning"].isna()
).astype("boolean").where(release.notna())

x["manager_timing"] = "no manager event"
x.loc[manager.notna(), "manager_timing"] = "post-release, settlement time unknown"
x.loc[manager.lt(release), "manager_timing"] = "pre-release"
x.loc[manager.ge(release) & manager.lt(settle), "manager_timing"] = "released, pre-settlement"
x.loc[manager.ge(settle), "manager_timing"] = "at/after settlement"

# Weekday UTC calendar, through end of next business day. Audit_Review
# is an event-time proxy unless completion is independently substantiated.
business_date_limit = release.dt.normalize() + pd.offsets.BDay(1)
x["audit_event_by_1bd"] = (
    audit.ge(release) & audit.dt.normalize().le(business_date_limit)
).astype("boolean").where(audit.notna() & release.notna())
audit_completion_proxy = x["audit_event_by_1bd"].mask(x["audit_start_only"])
for origin_name, origin in [("release", release), ("settlement", settle)]:
    elapsed = audit - origin
    x["audit_hours_from_" + origin_name] = elapsed.dt.total_seconds().div(3600).round(1)
    # Compare the unrounded durations. The bank's actual recall cutoff is unknown.
    x["audit_event_inside_24h_from_" + origin_name] = (
        elapsed.ge(pd.Timedelta(0)) & elapsed.lt(pd.Timedelta(hours=24))
    ).astype("boolean").where(elapsed.notna())

manager_before_settle = manager.lt(settle).astype("boolean").where(settle.notna())
manager_before_settle = manager_before_settle.mask(manager.isna(), False)
gates = pd.DataFrame({
    "prior_token": x["token_pre_prepare"],
    "amount_below_limit": x["eur"].lt(100000),
    "established_vendor": x["vendor_180d"],
    "stable_bank": ~x["bank_change_7d"].astype("boolean"),
    "clean_validation": x["av_full_clean_before_release"],
    "manager_independent_and_in_time": manager_before_settle & x["manager_independent"].astype("boolean"),
    "audit_by_one_business_day": audit_completion_proxy,
}, index=x.index).astype("boolean")
x["ED1_screen"] = "recorded gates supported, verify grade, review and audit completion"
x.loc[gates.isna().any(axis=1), "ED1_screen"] = "unverified gate(s)"
x.loc[gates.eq(False).fillna(False).any(axis=1), "ED1_screen"] = "failed gate(s) on record"
x.loc[~x["same_actor_violation"], "ED1_screen"] = "not applicable"

print("PRELIMINARY EVIDENCE FOR JUDGMENT ONLY")
print("Nominal violating completed cases:", sorted(violating))
print("Blocked, attempted and canceled without release:", sorted(blocked))
print(x[["same_actor_violation", "blocked_attempt", "ED1_screen", "av_result",
         "av_full_clean_before_release", "manager_timing", "bank_change_7d",
         "audit_event_by_1bd", "audit_start_only", "audit_hours_from_release",
         "audit_event_inside_24h_from_release", "audit_hours_from_settlement",
         "audit_event_inside_24h_from_settlement", "repeated_pair_in_extract",
         "pair_count_in_extract"]].to_string())
