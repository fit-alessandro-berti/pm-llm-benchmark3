1. Current-process diagnosis
The event and performance data reveal a process that is fast and inexpensive on its most common path, yet that same path is responsible for a disproportionate share of rework, customer pain, and policy breaches.

Main problems
• Rework & complaints: Fast approval  Dispatch replacement has a mean of 3 h, but 14.1 % of those cases bounce back through Customer complaint/reopen (Rework statistics, DFG edge Fast approval  Customer complaint/reopen mean 72 h).  
• Compliance violations: 32 % of high-value devices skip Manual review or Fraud/risk before Fast approval, breaking rule R1 (Alignment diagnostics, move-on-log deviations).  
• Cost erosion: Fast path is 31 % cheaper up-front but only 9 % cheaper after reshipments and service credits (Rework statistics).  
• Quality gaps: Wrong replacement cost on high-value devices averages €410 (Resource info).  
• Long tails on slow path: Request missing evidence loops (26 % repeat) drive a p90 of 88 h Intake  Manual review round-trip (Performance DFG).  
• Segment mismatch: Premium high-value devices have 22 % complaint rate on the fast path versus 5 % on the reviewed path (Segment outcomes), creating both satisfaction and compliance risk.  
Contradiction explained
The Auto-screen  Fast approval chain (DFG frequency 2 550) delivers the headline “8-13 h median cycle” that management likes, but the same chain is linked to:  
– the highest complaint edge frequency (260 Dispatch replacement  Complaint)  
– the largest move-on-log conformance errors (Fast approval before Evidence completeness)  
Hence speed and direct cost are achieved by externalising defect costs downstream; once rework, credits, and fines are included, the advantage shrinks or reverses.

2. Redesign A: Smart Segmented Gatekeeping
Redesigned flow
Intake  Updated Auto eligibility screen (segment- & value-aware)  
If Low-risk accessory (any customer) OR Premium accessory Fast approval  Dispatch replacement  
Else Manual eligibility review  
Manual review may still call Fraud/risk or Repair quote as today.  
Add lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate only for high-value devices (checks part number & R1/R2 conformance; SLA 6–10 h, staffed by inspectors currently under-used).

Targets
• High-value devices (Premium & Standard) suffer from wrong replacements and compliance breaches.  
• Preserves current speed for 43 % low-risk accessories where complaints are already  8 %.

Mechanism of improvement
• Rule update eliminates > 90 % of R1/R2 violations by forcing reviewed path for high-value devices.  
• Pre-dispatch gate catches wrong SKUs, reducing €410 average error.  
• Uses idle capacity at Quality check station (under-used in data).  

Deliberate sacrifices / open issues
• 15–35 % higher inflow to Manual review raises reviewer load; cycle times for mid-risk devices may grow unless resources are added or rules tuned.  
• Evidence loops remain unchanged; unclear-defect segment still slow.

3. Redesign B: Evidence-First Digital Intake
Redesigned flow
Intake claim  Evidence portal (mobile upload, guided)  
If evidence complete (auto rules)  Auto eligibility screen  as-is routing  
Else  Assisted evidence collection team (chat/phone) before any eligibility routing  
Manual/Fraud/Repair steps remain but now start with full evidence package; loop “Request missing evidence” is removed.

Targets
• “Unclear defect / missing evidence” (22 % of volume) and repeat evidence loops (26 % of cases entering that step).  
• Premium customers who incur service credits when loops push them past 24 h.

Mechanism of improvement
• Portal expected to cut repeat evidence requests by 30–45 %  removes 34 h mean re-entrance delay.  
• Parallel assisted channel prevents abandonment from low digital literacy.  
• Earlier evidence completeness also raises conformance (R2) before any Fast approval.

Deliberate sacrifices / open issues
• Initial development cost; some customers may still abandon earlier because “friction” is front-loaded.  
• Does not address wrong part selection after Fast approval; compliance on R1 remains unchanged.

4. Redesign C: Repair-First with Conditional Fast Replacement
Redesigned flow
Intake  Triage (simple rule: high-value device OR defect type unknown  Repair pathway; else Fast pathway)  
Repair pathway:  
Manual eligibility review  Repair quote  Customer approval  Repair device  Quality check  Close.  
Fast pathway (low-value, known defect):  
Auto eligibility  Provisional approval  Provisional dispatch hold (12 h)  Fraud/risk sampling 10 %  Dispatch replacement  Close.  
Escalation board capacity doubled (two daily slots) to cushion customer escalations.

Targets
• Aims to eliminate high cost of wrong replacement by making repair default for high-value devices and unknown defects.  
• Provides still-fast experience for simple low-risk accessories.  

Mechanism of improvement
• Shifts high-value volume (35 % of cases) off Fast approval entirely, hence removes 22 % complaint rate there.  
• 12-h hold window allows cancellation if risk or evidence mismatch is detected, yet is short enough for Premium accessory SLA.  
• Sampling reduces Fraud team overload (only 10 % of fast cases checked).

Deliberate sacrifices / open issues
• Repair path median is 96–120 h, so Premium high-value customers may breach 24 h SLA unless compensated.  
• Requires more technician hours; could clash with part-price confirmation delays.

6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks
Redesign A  
• Failure 1: Manual queue blows past 82 % utilisation, causing premium-case delays.  
• Failure 2: Pre-dispatch gate becomes rubber-stamping if volume surges.  
• Bottleneck shift: From Fraud specialists  Manual reviewers.

Redesign B  
• Failure 1: Portal increases abandonment among low digital-literacy customers, reducing revenue.  
• Failure 2: Up-front evidence burden discourages Premium customers, triggering credits.  
• Bottleneck shift: From evidence loops  Assisted evidence team capacity.

Redesign C  
• Failure 1: Repair technicians backlog; parts unavailable, cycle explodes > 150 h.  
• Failure 2: Customers perceive “repair first” as downgrade, NPS drops despite fewer complaints.  
• Bottleneck shift: From Fast approval errors  Repair quote wait / parts pricing confirmation.

7. Recommended experiment sequence
Stage 1 – Limited rule update pilot (Redesign A core)  
• Scope: High-value devices in one region for four weeks.  
• Metrics: % R1/R2 violations (target < 5 %), complaint rate (target –40 %), reviewer utilisation (< 90 %).  
• Stop/go: If complaints not down by at least 25 % or reviewer queue > 48 h median, stop.

Stage 2 – Evidence portal A/B (Redesign B core)  
• Scope: Unclear-defect claims; random 50 % to portal.  
• Metrics: repeat evidence requests (target –30 %), abandonment (< +5 %), median cycle time (target –24 h).  
• Sequencing logic: Run after Stage 1 so reviewer load is known; portal success may further change volumes.

Stage 3 – Repair-first prototype (Redesign C core)  
• Scope: Premium high-value devices only, opt-in; hold window 12 h.  
• Metrics: wrong replacement cost (target –70 %), cycle time (must stay < 120 h), NPS (no drop > 5 pts).  
• Stop/go: Abort if NPS falls > 8 pts or technician backlog exceeds 2-day median.

Learning approach  
• Track downstream complaint, credit, and re-dispatch cost for 30 days post-close to ensure savings are not merely shifted.  
• Compare pilot cohorts to matched historical controls for seasonality.  
• Use token-based replay on pilot logs to verify conformance gain persists.
