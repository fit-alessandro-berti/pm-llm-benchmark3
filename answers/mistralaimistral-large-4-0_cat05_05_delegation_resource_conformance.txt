## 2. Rule Interpretation

**RR1 — Drafter/approver separation.** For each case, after alias normalization and service-account attribution, the set of humans performing `Draft_Change_Request` must be disjoint from the set performing `Approve_Change`.

**RR2 — Implementer/verifier separation.** For each case, the set of humans performing `Implement_Change` must be disjoint from the set performing `Verify_Implementation`.

**RR3 — High-risk approval authority.** For HIGH-risk cases, `Approve_Change` must be performed by the Quality Manager (QM) or a valid QM delegate. A delegation is valid only if:
- its effective interval covers the event timestamp;
- its scope covers the change;
- the principal is absent or formally unavailable when required by the delegation; and
- no prohibited subdelegation occurred.

**RR4 — Emergency substitution.** A HIGH-risk emergency change may be implemented after approval by the Incident Commander (IC) or a valid IC delegate, but a QM or valid QM delegate must perform `Retrospective_QM_Review` within two business days. This is a justified exception only when the emergency flag and safety rationale are credible.

**Business-day counting for RR4.** I exclude the event day and count the next two business days (Monday–Friday, excluding site holidays; the site calendar had no holidays in the period). For an event on Tuesday 2026-08-11, the two-business-day deadline is end of Thursday 2026-08-13 23:59 UTC. A review on Friday 2026-08-14 is therefore late.

**RR5 — Identity evidence.** A service account is not a human identity. It can support conformance only if reliable session evidence maps the action to an authorized human. Aliases belonging to the same human must be normalized before segregation-of-duties checks.

**RR6 — Suspicion versus proof.** Repeated resource pairs, unusual reciprocal approvals, shared workstations, and concentrated handovers are indicators. They are not proof of collusion without supporting evidence.

**Action by a principal while on leave.** Leave does not automatically revoke standing QM authority. If the principal performs an action within her standing authority, the action is technically authorized under RR3. However, the leave/absence evidence may undermine the factual predicate for a delegation and may indicate credential sharing. If the action is performed by the principal’s account during leave, identity must be corroborated; otherwise the case is indeterminate or suspicious.

**Later database entry.** A later database entry cannot by itself prove an earlier delegation. It is stale or backfilled master data. Contemporaneous evidence — a signed PDF timestamped before the event, or a delegation registered before use — can prove event-time validity. A later record may corroborate but does not establish event-time existence.

## 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

**Repeated Clara–Ben pair.** In the preceding 60 days, Clara drafted and Ben approved 18 of 23 HIGH-risk production changes.  
- *Benign explanation:* Clara and Ben are assigned to the same production cell; Ben is the Quality Engineer; there is no formal pair-rotation policy, so the concentration may reflect normal workflow.  
- *Adverse explanation:* The concentration may indicate rubber-stamping, inadequate review, or collusion.  
- *Discriminating evidence:* approval rationale quality, time spent per approval, independent review of a sample, delegation records, system logs showing actual review actions, and interviews.

**Reciprocal pattern.** Ben drafted four LOW-risk training changes that Clara approved.  
- *Benign explanation:* Small team, cross-training, and normal reciprocal work; Clara may have authority for LOW-risk approvals.  
- *Adverse explanation:* Reciprocal approvals may be used to circumvent segregation of duties or create mutual obligations.  
- *Discriminating evidence:* whether Clara had authority for those approvals, whether Ben reviewed Clara’s drafts, timing, and whether any HIGH-risk cases were involved.

**Shared workstation / service account — CHG-503.** The `svc_qms` verification came from WS-DM-04, normally assigned to Diego; badge log shows Diego entered the room; the terminal is shared; no individual re-authentication occurred; CCTV is unavailable.  
- *Benign explanation:* Diego was present, and another authorized verifier used the shared terminal under the service account.  
- *Adverse explanation:* Diego used `svc_qms` to verify his own implementation, violating RR2.  
- *Discriminating evidence:* individual authentication logs, FIDO/badge logs, session logs, CCTV, verifier identity, and service-account mapping.

**Asha VPN during leave — CHG-504.** Asha’s account established a VPN session on 2026-08-13 09:15–09:40; the approval occurred at 09:22. Asha says she personally handled one urgent case despite leave; security cannot exclude credential sharing.  
- *Benign explanation:* Asha genuinely handled an urgent case remotely despite being on leave; the leave record was not updated.  
- *Adverse explanation:* Someone else used Asha’s credentials to approve the change.  
- *Discriminating evidence:* MFA logs, VPN logs, endpoint evidence, FIDO tokens, session actions, corroboration from Asha, and leave-policy review.

**Ben account during sick leave — CHG-506 and CHG-505 retrospective.** Ben was on certified sick leave from 2026-08-13 12:00 through 2026-08-14 23:59, but his account was used during part of that interval. CHG-506 approval occurred at 13:00 on 2026-08-13, one hour after sick leave began; the IP was an office workstation, not his home. CHG-505 retrospective review occurred on 2026-08-14 16:00, also during sick leave.  
- *Benign explanation:* Ben logged in from the office despite sick leave; the attendance record may be wrong; or he was briefly available for urgent work.  
- *Adverse explanation:* Someone else used Ben’s account to approve or review changes.  
- *Discriminating evidence:* MFA logs, endpoint evidence, badge logs, VPN logs, witness statements, session actions, and corroboration from Ben.

**Stale master data — D-QM-01 QMS record.** The QMS database record for D-QM-01 was created 2026-08-12 09:15 during migration, after the 2026-08-11 approval.  
- *Benign explanation:* The delegation existed on paper (signed PDF 2026-08-09 18:00) and was backfilled during migration.  
- *Adverse explanation:* The delegation was not in the system at event time, so Ben’s approval was unauthorized.  
- *Discriminating evidence:* migration audit trail, signed PDF verification, system access logs, and QMS configuration history.

**Alias issue — CHG-508.** `b.koch` and `u_bk` are the same human.  
- *Benign explanation:* Alias mapping error or system allowed self-approval without malicious intent.  
- *Adverse explanation:* The alias was used to hide a self-approval.  
- *Discriminating evidence:* alias mapping verification, system logs, intent, and whether the system enforced SoD on normalized identities.

**Emergency subdelegation — D-IC-04 / CHG-510.** Farid purported to pass IC authority to Clara by email; D-IC-03 explicitly prohibited subdelegation.  
- *Benign explanation:* Farid believed he could delegate in an urgent situation and did not understand the prohibition.  
- *Adverse explanation:* Deliberate circumvention of the no-subdelegation rule to allow Clara to self-approve.  
- *Discriminating evidence:* registration of D-IC-04, approval workflow, safety rationale, incident logs, and interviews.

**Network indicators.** Handover and working-together networks may show concentrated collaboration among Clara, Ben, Diego, and Eva. These are indicators only. They do not prove collusion. Discriminating evidence requires session-level attribution, approval rationale, and independent review.

def delegation_valid_for_event(deleg, ts, case_scope, activity):
    if deleg['valid'] is False:
        return False, 'delegation invalid (prohibited subdelegation or email only)'
    if not (deleg['start'] <= ts <= deleg['end']):
        return False, 'outside effective interval'
    if deleg['scope'] != case_scope:
        return False, 'scope mismatch'
    if activity not in deleg['covers_activities']:
        return False, 'activity not covered by delegation'
    if deleg['principal_absence_required']:
        absent = any(start <= ts <= end for start, end in deleg['principal_absence_intervals'])
        if not absent:
            if deleg['valid'] is None:
                return None, 'principal absence not evidenced'
            return False, 'principal absence condition not met'
    return True, 'valid'

## 6. Control Improvements

- **Delegation creation.** Require contemporaneous registration before the effective time; prohibit email-only delegations; enforce system-level scope and activity restrictions; explicitly state whether subdelegation is allowed; require principal-absence attestation.
- **Effective-time recording.** Record the effective time at creation, not during later migration; maintain an immutable audit trail for delegation creation, modification, and revocation.
- **Principal availability.** Integrate HR leave/availability data with the QMS; block delegate approvals when the principal is available; require documented principal absence for absence-conditioned delegations.
- **Alias management.** Maintain a central identity mapping; normalize aliases before segregation-of-duties checks; prevent duplicate accounts for the same human.
- **Individual authentication.** Eliminate shared service accounts for human activities; require individual authentication (badge, FIDO, MFA) for verification and approval; prohibit shared workstations for critical activities.
- **Emergency review.** Enforce the two-business-day RR4 deadline automatically; require a QM or valid QM delegate with correct scope; alert on late or out-of-scope retrospective reviews.
- **Pair rotation.** Monitor repeated drafter–approver pairs; rotate approvers; limit concentration of approvals; investigate outliers.
- **Network monitoring.** Use handover and working-together networks as indicators, not proof; investigate unusual concentrations with session-level evidence.
- **Evidential retention.** Retain session logs, VPN logs, MFA logs, badge logs, CCTV, FIDO logs, signed PDFs, migration audit trails, and approval rationales for a defined period.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

The apparent nonconformance is a mixture of genuine authority failure, identity/data-quality problems, and organizational-pattern risk.

**Genuine authority failures** are clear in CHG-502 (Clara self-approval), CHG-507 (Eva’s unauthorized HIGH-risk approval), CHG-508 (Ben self-approval via alias), and CHG-510 (Clara self-approval of a HIGH-risk emergency change using an invalid subdelegation). CHG-505 also fails RR4 because the retrospective QM review was late, out of scope, and performed during Ben’s sick leave.

**Identity/data-quality problems** dominate CHG-503 (unattributed service-account verification), CHG-504 (Asha’s leave/VPN conflict), CHG-506 (Ben’s sick-leave account use), and CHG-509 (unproven principal absence for D-QM-02). CHG-501 is likely a valid substitution, but the QMS record was backfilled during migration; the signed PDF resolves the event-time validity.

**Organizational-pattern risk** is present in the repeated Clara–Ben approvals, reciprocal training-document approvals, shared workstation use, and concentrated handovers. These are indicators, not proof of collusion.

**Uncertainty** remains around service-account attribution, credential sharing, CI-44 linkage, and principal absence on 2026-08-15.

**Three highest-priority investigations:**
1. **CHG-510** — invalid subdelegation and self-approval of a HIGH-risk emergency cooling change; potential deliberate circumvention of RR1 and RR4.
2. **CHG-503 / CHG-509 service-account usage** — determine who actually performed the verifications; CHG-503’s weak attribution from Diego’s workstation is especially concerning.
3. **Ben’s account activity during sick leave (CHG-506 and CHG-505 retrospective) and Asha’s VPN during leave (CHG-504)** — possible credential compromise or unauthorized use of privileged accounts.
