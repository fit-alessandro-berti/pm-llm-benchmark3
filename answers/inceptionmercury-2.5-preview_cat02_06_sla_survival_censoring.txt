

# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

**Time Origin:** Case submission (`Submit` event). All durations (`duration_or_age_h`) are measured from this point.
**Horizon:** 72 hours (SLA threshold).
**Event of Interest:** Internal resolution (`Resolve`).

We define two estimands:

1.  **Breach Probability ($P_{breach}$):** The probability that a case has not reached `Resolve` by time 72h.
    *   *Definition:* $P(T_{resolve} > 72)$, treating all non-Resolve terminal events (`Reject`, `Withdraw`, `Transfer`) as "not resolved" by 72h.
    *   *Operational Decision:* High-level compliance monitoring. Are we missing the 72h target?
2.  **Cumulative Incidence of Resolution ($CIF_{resolve}$):** The probability that a case *does* reach `Resolve` by time 72h, while treating `Reject`, `Withdraw`, and `Transfer` as competing risks (i.e., they preclude `Resolve`).
    *   *Definition:* $P(T_{resolve} \le 72, \text{type}=\text{Resolve})$.
    *   *Operational Decision:* Resource planning for completed resolutions. How many cases do we expect to close internally by the deadline?

**Case Contributions (Illustrative):**

| Case | Status | Time | Contribution to $P_{breach}$ (Est 1) | Contribution to $CIF_{resolve}$ (Est 2) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| S03 | Resolved | 76h | **Breach** (Not resolved by 72h) | **Survived past 72h** (Censored at 76h or after, depending on method) |
| S09 | Open | 92h | **Breach** (Not resolved by 72h) | **Censored at 72h** (Active at horizon) |
| S06 | Withdrawn | 27h | **Breach** (Not resolved) | **Competing Risk** (Precluded resolution) |
| S08 | Transferred | 68h | **Breach** (Not resolved) | **Competing Risk** (or Censored, see Sec 2) |

*Assumption 1.1:* Cases remaining `open` beyond 72h are counted as breaches for Est 1, but censored for Est 2 until the actual cutoff.

# 2. Event and censoring encoding

We distinguish between terminal outcomes (event type), censoring, and competing risks.

| Observed Status | Event Type | Risk Event Indicator | Censoring | Sensitivity Alternative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `resolved` | Success | 1 | 0 | N/A |
| `rejected` | Failure (Comp.) | 2 | 0 | Treat as general "failure" |
| `withdrawn` | Failure (Comp.) | 3 | 0 | Treat as general "failure" |
| `transferred` | Comp. / Cens. | 4 | 0 | **A:** Competing Risk (Internal closure)<br>**B:** Censored (Outcome unknown)<br>**C:** Failure (Escalation failure) |
| `open` | — | 0 | 1 (at `duration_or_age_h`) | **A:** Right-censored<br>**B:** Imputed failure at 72h if >72h |

**Handling `transferred`:**
*   **Assumption A (Competing Risk):** The case is removed from the internal process; it cannot be `Resolve` inside the system. This underestimates internal throughput but accurately models SLA compliance.
*   **Assumption B (Right-Censored):** We do not know the external outcome. For Est 1 (Breach Probability), we treat it as "not resolved by 72h".
*   **Assumption C (Failure):** The internal process failed to retain the case (SLA breach).

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

Analyzing only `resolved` cases excludes S06 (withdrawn), S08 (transferred), S09-S15 (open), and S22, S30 (withdrawn).

**Survivor Bias:**
*   S01, S02, S04, S16, S20 (all `resolved`) suggest average time ~30h.
*   However, S09 (`open` at 92h) and S03 (`resolved` at 76h) indicate many cases exceed the 72h SLA. By dropping `open` and `open` cases, we ignore 30%+ of the cohort that is past the SLA threshold.

**Informative Censoring:**
*   S09, S10, S11, S15, S24-S26, S29, S31 are `open`. Their durations (`duration_or_age_h`) often exceed 72h (e.g., S09: 92h, S13: 110h, S26: 104h). They are not just censored; they are **active breaches**. Naive analysis treats them as if they might finish in 1 hour, leading to optimistic SLA forecasts.

**Competing Risks:**
*   S05 (Rejected 9h), S12 (Withdrawn 15h) finish quickly but do not contribute to "Resolution" volume. Naive analysis that ignores them underestimates the risk that a case will fail to close internally.

**Cohort Imbalance:**
*   Cohort B (post-policy) has faster early outcomes (e.g., S18: 12h resolved). Cohort A has more complex paths (S03, S13 involve Escalate/Rework). Simple averaging ignores structural changes.

**Open Cases: Below vs. Above 72h**
*   S15 (Open at 35h): Uncertain future.
*   S09 (Open at 92h): Confirmed breach. Mixing these two without survival analysis treats them identically.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

| Method | Handles Competing Risks? | Handles Censoring? | Suitability for SLA |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Kaplan-Meier** | No (Overestimates Success) | Yes | Only for $P_{breach}$ if `Reject`/`Transfer` are ignored failures. |
| **Aalen-Johansen** | Yes | Yes | Primary for $CIF_{resolve}$. Correctly weights `Withdraw`/`Reject` as competitors. |
| **Fine-Gray (Subdistribution)** | Yes | Yes | Secondary for modeling covariates (Priority/Path) affecting SLA compliance. |

**Recommendation:**
*   **Primary:** **Aalen-Johansen**. It computes the Cumulative Incidence Function (CIF) for `Resolve` while accounting for `Withdraw`/`Reject`/`Transfer` as competing events. This aligns with operational goals: "What fraction of cases will be closed internally by 72h?"
*   **Secondary:** **Cause-Specific Hazards** via Cox model. Models the risk of resolution conditional on remaining at risk, allowing us to identify process bottlenecks (e.g., does `Escalate` increase resolution rate?).

# 5. Process-aware features and stage hazards

Prefix features (e.g., `Submit>Screen>Review`) must be transformed carefully to avoid leakage (using post-start activity to predict future outcomes).

**Derived Features:**
1.  **Current Stage (Baseline/Time-varying):** Last observed activity (e.g., `Review`, `Escalate`).
2.  **Escalation Flag (Post-treatment):** Presence of `Escalate` in prefix. *Leakage Risk:* `Escalate` occurs *after* start. It cannot be a baseline covariate in a standard Cox model. Must use **landmarking** (e.g., state at 24h) or **time-varying** covariates.
3.  **Rework Count:** `rework_count` column (0/1).
4.  **Process Path:** Unique variant (sequence string).
5.  **Idle Time:** `last_event_gap_h` indicates inactivity. High gaps often precede breaches.

# 6. Sensitivity analysis

We propose three analyses to test robustness of $P_{breach}$:

1.  **Transfer as Competing Risk:** Assume `Transfer` = `Resolve` is impossible (Internal process failed).
2.  **Transfer as Censoring:** Assume `Transfer` may resolve externally at any time.
3.  **Open > 72h as Breach:** Treat any `open` case at $t \ge 72$ as immediate failure.

**Potential Conclusion Reversal:**
*   **Cohort Effect:** Cohort B appears "faster" (more quick resolutions). If `Transfer` rates are higher in B, but treated as censoring, B looks better for $CIF_{resolve}$ but worse for SLA compliance ($P_{breach}$). If `Transfer` is failure, B's SLA performance might look comparable to A.

# 7. Operational interpretation

**Alerts:**
*   Trigger a "High Risk" alert if $P_{breach}$ for a specific case (based on its current state path) exceeds 60%.

**Workload Actions:**
*   Increase capacity if $CIF_{resolve}(72)$ drops below 70%.
*   Investigate paths with high `Escalate` counts (S13, S10) as they often have longer durations.

**Governance:**
*   SLA Compliance Rate = $CIF_{resolve}(72)$.
*   Use **Fairness Checks:** Compare $CIF_{resolve}(72)$ by `priority`. If P1 has significantly lower probability than P3, investigate if prioritization is working.

# 9. Limitations and additional data

1.  **Exact Timestamps:** We only have duration at cutoff (`duration_or_age_h`). We cannot know the precise time an activity started or finished inside a prefix.
2.  **Transfer Outcomes:** If `Transfer` results in `Resolve` externally, Est 1 is biased upward. External resolution tracking is needed.
3.  **Policy Rollout:** Assignment to Cohort B was non-randomized. High-priority cases may have drifted into B. Exact start/end dates for the policy change are needed to isolate the causal effect.
4.  **Workload Data:** No information on case arrival rate or queue length, which are strong predictors of SLA breaches.
