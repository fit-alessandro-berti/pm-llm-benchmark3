I use an **interventional (controlled) mediation framework** rather than natural direct/indirect effects. The flag is not a standalone manipulable cause; it is part of a policy bundle (priority lane + routing rules). ## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk:** If `U_underlying_risk` strongly drives both flagging and long manual/escalation service independent of policy, the longer cycle may reflect true risk rather than flag-mediated queueing, reversing the recommendation to weaken the flag bundle.
2. **Queue interference:** If P3’s protected capacity noticeably delays unflagged cases and total system cycle worsens, shared-queue interference dominates and P3 is no longer advisable.
3. **Decision quality:** If removing or suppressing mandatory review (P1/P2) increases missed high-risk rejections beyond a prespecified threshold, the current routing is justified despite longer cycle.
4. **Timestamp semantics:** If `triage_start` records administrative registration rather than actual work start, or manual start is batched, the observed triage acceleration is an artifact and the stage decomposition is invalid.
5. **Positivity / overlap:** If unflagged high-complexity cases are absent in key `risk_score` bands, within-band comparison lacks overlap and the total effect cannot be identified.
6. **Reviewer behavior change:** If reviewers deliberately slow flagged cases because they expect extra scrutiny, waiting mediation is behavior-induced; if that behavior changes under P1/P2, expected effects reverse.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed:** Flagged cases wait a median ~0.2 h to triage start versus ~2.5 h for unflagged, and their triage service is slightly shorter. Yet flagged cases show a much longer triage-done-to-manual-start gap (mean ~6.5 h) and higher escalation rates, yielding total cycle times roughly three times larger. Residual-to-close is similar across groups.

**Plausible mechanism:** The priority lane accelerates triage, but the flag triggers mandatory manual review and frequent escalation. These extra steps, combined with shared reviewer/senior queues and high arrival load, create downstream waiting that dominates the saved triage time. Complexity and risk score partially explain case mix.

**Unidentified:** The causal direct effect of the flag separate from `U_underlying_risk`; the magnitude of interference on unflagged cases; whether longer review improves decision quality; and how reviewer behavior would change under alternative policies.

**Most useful next evidence:** A randomized pilot of P3 (protected review capacity) with guardrails, because it cleanly separates review-occurrence from queue-waiting mediation and measures spillover, generating actionable total and interventional indirect effects.
