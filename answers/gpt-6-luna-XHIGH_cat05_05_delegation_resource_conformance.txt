## 2. Rule Interpretation

- **RR1:** For each case, resolve every drafter and approver to a human, normalize aliases, and fail the rule if the sets overlap. Different account strings do not establish different people. If either actor cannot be resolved, the result is unknown rather than a pass.
- **RR2:** Apply the same human-set comparison to implementers and verifiers. An unknown service-account actor leaves the check unresolved unless reliable session evidence attributes that event to a human. RR2 itself states separation, not a verifier job title; a separate qualification rule would be needed to conclude that only someone titled “Independent Verifier” may verify.
- **RR3:** For a HIGH-risk approval, accept Asha as the standing QM, or accept a delegate only if the event timestamp is inside the effective interval, the specific change and action are within scope, the principal is absent or formally unavailable as required, and the delegation chain has no prohibited subdelegation. A database row is not enough if its creation is after the action; a reliable earlier signed instrument can establish authority even if a migration later populates the database. Here D-QM-01’s signed PDF predates its effective interval. Its 2026-08-12 database entry is before CHG-503’s 11:30 approval but after CHG-501’s 2026-08-11 approval; the PDF, not the later entry, is the evidence for CHG-501. Confirm the PDF’s signature, version, and integrity. D-QM-02 was recorded before it became effective.
- **RR4:** A HIGH-risk emergency may use a standing IC or valid IC delegate for approval before implementation, in place of the ordinary QM approval route. The exception also requires a QM or valid QM delegate’s retrospective review within two business days and a credible emergency flag and safety rationale. Count business days after the emergency approval/implementation date, excluding the event date: for a Tuesday 2026-08-11 event, Wednesday 2026-08-12 is day 1 and Thursday 2026-08-13 is day 2; the review is due by Thursday’s end. Friday 2026-08-14 is late. A review by Ben under D-QM-01 is not established as valid: that instrument grants QM approval authority for production-equipment changes, while these are cooling-system cases and the action is a retrospective review. The supplied log has an emergency flag but no supporting safety rationale or incident record, so exception credibility and CI-44 scope remain evidential questions.
- **RR5:** A service account is not a human. Treat its event as attributable only where the session evidence reliably maps it to a human who is authorized for that action. Badge presence at a shared terminal without individual re-authentication does not identify the operator. A FIDO and badge match is stronger, but role authorization remains a separate check.
- **RR6:** Repeated pairs, reciprocal approvals, shared workstations, and handovers are risk indicators, not proof of collusion. Test them against authorization, assignment, access, and decision evidence; do not infer guilt or innocence from collaboration alone.

A leave record is evidence of absence, not a revocation of a person’s standing role. It can satisfy a delegation’s absence condition, subject to stronger contrary availability evidence. A recorded VPN session is evidence that an account connected; it does not by itself prove the account owner was the user or establish that the principal was available to perform the work. Thus Asha’s 09:22 CHG-504 approval is authorized if she performed it; it is not automatically unauthorized because it occurred during leave. For a delegated approval during a conflicting session, investigate whether Asha was actually present/available at that event. Ben’s sick leave is not a stated D-QM-01 condition, but Ben’s denial and the office workstation make the identity of the CHG-506 actor uncertain.

## 6. Control Improvements

- Create and approve delegations in the controlled system before use. Store immutable signed versions, signer identity, effective start/end timestamps, scope, permitted activities, absence trigger, revocation, and whether subdelegation is permitted. Reject attempts to delegate authority further when prohibited.
- Record both the instrument’s signed/effective time and its database-ingestion time. Preserve migration history so a later database row cannot be mistaken for contemporaneous authorization.
- Define “absent or formally unavailable” in operational terms and capture the principal’s duty roster/availability at the event time. Record approved exceptions when someone works while on leave. Do not use a generic leave flag or VPN session as a complete proxy for event-time availability or actor identity.
- Maintain one stable human identity across aliases and historical usernames. Run SoD checks on the normalized person identifier while retaining raw IDs and identity-map versions for audit replay.
- Prohibit shared interactive service-account sessions for approvals and verifications. Require individual SSO/FIDO re-authentication per critical action; log the human, device, session, timestamp, and originating process. If a shared service account must remain, block it from critical controls unless a reliable human attribution is captured.
- Separate approval authority from retrospective review authority in delegation language. Name whether a delegate may perform approval, emergency review, or both, and require a valid QM review with a timestamped due-date alert and escalation. For emergency cases, capture incident ID, contemporaneous safety rationale, IC identity/authority, implementation time, reviewer identity, and a two-business-day deadline calculation.
- Keep event-time qualification history for verifiers and other controlled roles. Make the system reject or route an event when the user’s qualification expired or changed, once the site confirms the required role for verification.
- Review the concentrated Clara–Ben pair and reciprocal approvals against an assignment baseline. Consider periodic independent sampling or a proportionate rotation of reviewer coverage; avoid assuming that rotation alone proves independence or that repeated pairs imply misconduct.
- Use handover and working-together networks as triage dashboards with contextual baselines and explainable thresholds. Require transaction-level evidence before escalating an inference of collusion.
- Retain source event logs, delegation artifacts, identity-provider and endpoint records, FIDO events, badge data, email headers, and incident/change rationale under a common case identifier with synchronized UTC timestamps and controlled access.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

The evidence shows several genuine control failures: the same human drafted and approved CHG-502, CHG-508, and CHG-510; CHG-507 lacks a QM-authorized approver; CHG-509 lacks proof that Asha was absent for Eva’s QM delegation; and the emergency reviews in CHG-505 and CHG-510 do not meet the stated QM-authority requirement, with CHG-505 also late. CHG-501 and CHG-503 have supportable D-QM-01 substitutions, subject to document integrity, while CHG-504 is an authorized direct-QM action if Asha was the actor.

Identity and record quality materially limit conclusions for CHG-503, CHG-504, CHG-505’s review, and especially CHG-506. The 18-of-23 Clara–Ben pairing and reciprocal approvals are organizational-pattern risks that warrant a focused audit, but they do not prove collusion. The relative share of apparent nonconformance attributable to deliberate misconduct cannot be quantified from this log; at least three SoD failures are directly evidenced, while several high-risk outcomes turn on identity, emergency scope, or missing authority records.

The three highest-priority investigations are: (1) determine the actual human behind CHG-503/506 and Ben’s CHG-505 review, using session, device, FIDO, and badge evidence; (2) verify the approval history and event-time availability/authority for the Clara–Ben high-risk approvals, plus CHG-509’s absence condition; and (3) establish CI-44 linkage and safety rationale for CHG-505/510, then audit the unauthorized Clara approval and missing/late valid QM reviews.
