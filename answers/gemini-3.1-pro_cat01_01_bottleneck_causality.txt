## 1. Executive causal assessment
My provisional verdict is `mainly confounded`. Confidence level: 85.

The strongest evidence for this verdict is the classic Simpson's Paradox present in the descriptive data. While Orion's overall mean cycle time is significantly higher than Nova's (21.96h vs 15.24h), this crude comparison is heavily confounded by case mix. Within every single complexity stratum (Low, Medium, High), Orion actually completes cases faster than Nova. Orion is disproportionately assigned the most difficult work (42.9% High complexity vs 12.5% for Nova) and the vast majority of the night shift (92.9% vs 25%). These factors structurally require more wait and service time, masking Orion's true efficiency.

The strongest evidence against this verdict is that stratification by a coarse "complexity" label might not capture all unobserved confounding. It is possible that within the "High" complexity bucket, Orion receives the easiest cases while Nova receives the hardest, or vice versa. Additionally, wait times are a massive component of cycle time, and queueing dynamics driven by shift and arrival load act as mechanisms rather than just confounders.

The proposed 50% reassignment of Orion's cases to Nova will likely backfire. Because Nova is slower within every complexity level, moving difficult cases to a group with fewer certified reviewers will likely increase their service times, increase rework rates, and exacerbate queue wait times. This would lead to a degradation in the overall average cycle time across the system, rather than the improvement management expects.

Supported by facts: `complexity -> routing_rule -> resource_group` (routing engine assigns group after classification). `shift -> resource_group` (Orion covers night). `compliance_required -> compliance_performed`. `queue_wait`, `review_service`, `rework` -> `cycle_time` (components of cycle time).
Assumptions: `unobserved_case_risk` affects both complexity and service time, acting as unmeasured confounding. `resource_group` affects `unobserved_reviewer_skill` (Orion has more certified reviewers). `cycle_time -> case_completed` assumes longer cases might be abandoned, creating selection bias.
Feedback/time-varying relation: A static DAG suppresses queueing feedback loops. In reality, high `arrival_load` or long `review_service` in one period dynamically increases `queue_wait` for subsequent cases, altering the bottleneck over time.

Final recommendation:
Do not proceed with the proposed 50% reassignment of Orion's cases to Nova. The descriptive data strongly suggests a Simpson's Paradox: while Orion's overall average cycle time is higher, they are actually faster than Nova within every complexity stratum. The crude difference is heavily confounded by case mix, as Orion handles the vast majority of High-complexity cases and night-shift work, both of which structurally require more time due to compliance checks and queueing. Blindly moving difficult cases to Nova, who has fewer certified reviewers and currently processes them slower, will likely increase overall cycle times and degrade quality. Instead, execute a small randomized routing pilot for Low/Medium cases on the day shift to cleanly identify the true causal effect of the resource group. Simultaneously, investigate the massive wait times associated with compliance checks on the night shift, as this is the primary bottleneck mechanism driving long cycle times for High-complexity cases.

## 8. Residual uncertainty
1. **Unobserved case complexity within strata**: The "High/Medium/Low" categories might be too broad, masking true difficulty.
   *Most valuable addition*: A continuous case-complexity score recorded at the `Classify` step.
2. **Queueing dynamics and arrival times**: `wait_before_review_h` aggregates queueing and compliance wait, masking the true bottleneck.
   *Most valuable addition*: Exact timestamps for every activity start and complete event.
3. **Compliance check duration**: We don't know how long the compliance check itself takes versus waiting for it to start.
   *Most valuable addition*: Separate timestamp logs for `Compliance Check Start` and `Compliance Check Complete`.
4. **Missing data on abandoned/open cases**: The dataset only includes completed cases, introducing potential selection bias.
   *Most valuable addition*: A full event log including all cases (completed, abandoned, and currently open) with their current status.
5. **Reviewer-level heterogeneity**: The "resource group" aggregates many reviewers, obscuring individual performance.
   *Most valuable addition*: An anonymized `reviewer_id` for each activity to enable within-reviewer comparisons.
