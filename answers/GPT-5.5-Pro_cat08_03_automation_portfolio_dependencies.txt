# 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

**Upstream data quality dependencies.**
A1 and A2 sit near the start of the process. They improve speed and routing, but they also determine the quality of everything downstream. A2 is especially sensitive because product/exposure classification affects whether cases require collateral valuation, committee treatment, High-exposure controls, and possibly stricter four-eyes handling. Automating classification without reliable human confirmation could make the process faster but less compliant.

A5 and A6 are tightly linked. A5 reduces missing-document loops by improving the completeness of customer submissions. A6 then extracts and normalizes financial statements. If A6 is implemented without A5, analysts may still receive incomplete, inconsistent, or low-quality PDFs, so automation exceptions remain high.

A8, A9, and A11 are downstream-dependent. Committee pack auto-assembly requires clean KYC status, normalized statements, collateral valuation if required, and credit-analysis notes. Contract generation requires a final Committee decision and clean control status. Loan activation should not be automated broadly until KYC clearance, signing validity, contract terms, and remediation completion are reliable.

**Legal and compliance controls.**
The most important gating rules are: KYC clearance must precede Customer signing; contract terms must match the Committee decision; High-exposure SME cases must respect the four-eyes principle between Credit analysis and Committee packaging; and every model suggestion, human override, and final decision must be auditable. This makes A3, A4, A9, and A12 control-relevant modules, not merely labor-saving tools.

**Human escalation capacity.**
Compliance specialists are only 3 FTE, and the handover-of-work network shows dependency from KYC ops to two senior specialists. A3 and A4 therefore need careful escalation design. A model that escalates too many ambiguous KYC hits can make the specialist bottleneck worse. A4 is attractive because it targets a low-frequency but high-wait, high-criticality activity, but it should assist specialists rather than replace them.

Credit analysts are 9 FTE and central in the working-together network. A1 or A2 may increase clean inflow into Financial document review, normalization, and Credit analysis. Unless A5 and A6 improve document completeness and normalization, upstream automation could overload analysts.

**External valuation dependency.**
A7 cannot remove the external wait entirely because Wait for external valuation is outside direct bank control. However, it can still improve process performance through triage, earlier routing, better valuer selection, parallelization, and SLA monitoring. This matters because collateral valuation is required in 71% of Equipment finance and 18% of Working-capital cases, and the external wait is a major synchronization delay before Committee packaging.

**Core-banking integration constraints.**
Only two modules can be deeply integrated with core banking in the first six months. That argues against starting with too many downstream automation modules. A sensible design is to deeply integrate the modules that improve data quality and upstream readiness first, while deploying other tools as sidecar or shadow-mode assistants until the control gates are proven.

# 3. Portfolio alternatives

### Quick-win portfolio: A1, A2, A5, A10

This portfolio optimizes visible progress, management confidence, and early labor savings. A1 addresses the high-frequency Register application step. A2 improves initial routing. A5 attacks missing-document loops. A10 reduces Customer signing delays and reminder loops.

It sacrifices deeper synchronization work. It does not directly address entity mismatches, financial-statement normalization, collateral valuation waits, contract-rule consistency, or four-eyes violations. It risks accelerating applications into KYC, document review, and Credit analysis before those areas are ready.

### Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A4, A5, A6, A7

This portfolio optimizes end-to-end flow rather than frequency. A4 targets entity mismatches, which are low frequency but often critical-path. A5 and A6 reduce document and normalization delays before Credit analysis and Committee packaging. A7 targets the long external valuation wait.

It sacrifices downstream legal/control automation. Contract-remediation loops, signing controls, and activation readiness may remain problematic. This portfolio is strongest for cycle-time reduction, especially for Equipment finance and cases with collateral requirements, but it needs added governance to satisfy compliance expectations.

### Compliance-first portfolio: A3, A4, A9, A12

This portfolio optimizes risk reduction and auditable control. A3 and A4 strengthen KYC triage and entity-resolution handling. A9 checks contract generation against Committee decisions and control rules. A12 creates a structured way to manage control remediation and policy ambiguity.

It sacrifices some operational speed. Missing-document loops, statement normalization effort, and external valuation delays remain mostly untouched. Analysts may still spend substantial time on normalization, and Committee packaging may still wait for inconsistent upstream artifacts.

### Recommended balanced first-year portfolio: A4, A5, A6, A9

This portfolio combines bottleneck reduction with control strengthening. A4 reduces KYC/entity delays. A5 reduces missing-document loops. A6 reduces analyst normalization burden and prepares cleaner credit artifacts. A9 strengthens contract-rule consistency and gives compliance a visible first-year control module.

It sacrifices A1’s politically simple high-frequency win and defers A7’s external valuation optimization. That is defensible because A1 mainly speeds an early, short activity, while A4/A5/A6/A9 address longer waits, loops, synchronization points, and control defects.

# 4. Expected process-level effects

### Quick-win portfolio: A1, A2, A5, A10

**Cycle time.**
A1 reduces early processing time at Register application, but Register has only 8 minutes mean service time and 1.5 hours median waiting time. The larger cycle-time effect would come from A5 reducing Financial document review loops and A10 reducing the 44-hour Customer signing wait. However, the portfolio does not address the 31-hour wait before Resolve entity mismatch, the 20-hour wait before Normalize financial statements, the 28-hour wait before Credit analysis, or the 58-hour external valuation wait.

**Waiting time.**
The DFG relation Receive application → Register application is very frequent, so A1 affects many cases. But the larger waits are later. A5 targets Financial document review → Request missing documents and Request missing documents → Financial document review, where repeated loops are prominent. A10 targets Contract preparation → Customer signing → Loan activation.

**Rework.**
A5 should reduce the 52% repeated missing-document loop if document instructions become clearer. A2 may reduce misrouting caused by product/exposure classification errors. A10 may reduce reminder loops, but only if signing is legally gated after KYC clearance.

**Handovers.**
A1 may reduce back-office touches. A5 may reduce RM-triggered customer confusion. A10 may reduce RM chasing. But handovers to compliance specialists and credit analysts remain.

**Resource demand.**
Back-office demand falls, but back office is already under 65% utilized after 15:00. Clean inflow may increase pressure on compliance ops and credit analysts. Without A6, analysts may receive more files but still spend heavy time normalizing statements.

**Control violations.**
A2 could reduce wrong control-path routing if well governed, but it could also create control risk if misclassification is accepted automatically. A10 could worsen KYC-before-signing violations unless e-signature is blocked until KYC clearance is confirmed.

### Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A4, A5, A6, A7

**Cycle time.**
This portfolio should have the strongest cycle-time effect. A4 targets Resolve entity mismatch, which is present in fewer cases but creates 31 hours of median waiting and is on the critical path in 82% of cases when present. A5 and A6 improve the document-review and normalization path before Credit analysis. A7 addresses the longest external synchronization delay.

**Waiting time.**
A4 reduces delay around KYC check → Resolve entity mismatch and Resolve entity mismatch → KYC check. A5 targets Financial document review → Request missing documents → Financial document review. A6 targets Financial document review → Normalize financial statements → Credit analysis. A7 targets Collateral valuation request → Wait for external valuation → Committee packaging.

**Rework.**
A5 should reduce repeated missing-document requests. A6 should reduce normalization errors and analyst rework. A4 may reduce repeated KYC/entity cycles. A7 may reduce valuation lateness, but quality disputes must be monitored.

**Handovers.**
A4 reduces dependency from KYC ops to senior compliance specialists by preparing evidence and standardizing resolution. A5 reduces RM/customer/back-office loops. A6 reduces analyst back-and-forth over financial data. A7 improves handoff to external valuers but does not eliminate partner dependency.

**Resource demand.**
Compliance specialists should spend less time assembling entity evidence, but unresolved or ambiguous cases still need them. Credit analysts may benefit substantially because A6 reduces normalization workload. Faster valuations may create more synchronized arrivals at Committee packaging.

**Control violations.**
A4 supports KYC quality, but the portfolio does not directly address contract-term mismatch or four-eyes violations. Additional governance would still be needed around Committee packaging and Contract preparation.

### Compliance-first portfolio: A3, A4, A9, A12

**Cycle time.**
Cycle time may improve for KYC exceptions and contract remediation, but the portfolio does not directly reduce Financial document review loops, statement normalization effort, or external valuation waits. Cycle-time benefits are therefore narrower but safer.

**Waiting time.**
A3 and A4 may reduce waiting around KYC check and Resolve entity mismatch. A9 and A12 may reduce Contract preparation → Remediate control issue → Contract preparation loops. Customer signing wait may improve indirectly if fewer cases reach signing with unresolved KYC or contract issues.

**Rework.**
This portfolio should reduce KYC remediation, contract remediation, and control rework. A12 is especially useful where remediation exposes policy ambiguity rather than simple data defects.

**Handovers.**
A3/A4 reshape the handover from KYC ops to compliance specialists. A9/A12 reshape handovers between legal ops, compliance, relationship managers, and operations. The portfolio should make handoffs more auditable but may initially increase visible exception volumes.

**Resource demand.**
Compliance and legal workload may rise in the short term because exceptions become more visible. Over time, better triage and remediation routing should reduce avoidable manual effort.

**Control violations.**
This is the strongest portfolio for reducing false KYC clearance, provisional signing, contract-term mismatch, and unresolved remediation. It can also enforce or monitor four-eyes compliance in High-exposure SME cases, especially during month-end rush.

### Recommended balanced first-year portfolio: A4, A5, A6, A9

**Cycle time.**
This portfolio should reduce cycle time in both exception-heavy and mainstream cases. A4 improves KYC exception flow. A5 reduces missing-document loops. A6 reduces the heavy normalization step before Credit analysis. A9 reduces contract-preparation rework and prevents downstream remediation.

**Waiting time.**
It targets several major waits: Resolve entity mismatch at 31 hours, Financial document review at 18 hours, Normalize financial statements at 20 hours, Credit analysis preparation, and Contract preparation at 22 hours. It does not directly reduce the 58-hour external valuation wait, so Equipment finance cases may still have a major external bottleneck.

**Rework.**
A5 targets missing-document rework. A6 targets financial-data rework. A9 targets Contract preparation → Remediate control issue → Contract preparation and contract-term mismatch. A4 reduces repeated KYC/entity handling.

**Handovers.**
A4 improves KYC-to-specialist handovers. A5 reduces RM/customer/back-office loops. A6 improves analyst handoffs into Credit analysis and Committee packaging. A9 improves legal-ops and operations handoffs by making control-rule checks explicit.

**Resource demand.**
Back-office and RM follow-up demand should decrease. Compliance specialists should receive better-prepared entity-mismatch cases. Credit analysts should spend less time on normalization and more time on judgment. Legal ops may initially see more flagged exceptions but fewer late remediation loops.

**Control violations.**
A4 supports KYC clearance quality. A9 directly addresses contract-term consistency and control-rule checking. The portfolio should also reduce the chance that Customer signing proceeds with unresolved KYC, provided A9 includes a hard KYC-before-signing gate.

# 5. Risks and compensating controls

| risk introduced by automation                              | compensating control                                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **False negatives in KYC or entity matching**              | Conservative thresholds; mandatory human review for High-exposure SME, fuzzy sanctions matches, and low-confidence entity matches; sampled quality review of cleared cases |
| **False positives causing specialist overload**            | Escalation quotas, priority rules, separate queues for severe vs low-risk alerts, monitoring of compliance-specialist WIP                                                  |
| **Misclassification of product or exposure**               | Human confirmation for exposure class; policy-based validation; automatic flag if classification changes required controls                                                 |
| **Garbage-in/garbage-out in Committee packs or contracts** | Do not auto-assemble or auto-generate unless KYC, financial normalization, collateral, and decision data are complete and reconciled                                       |
| **Outdated contract-rule library**                         | Legal owner for rule library, version control, rule-testing suite, release approval, rollback plan                                                                         |
| **Model drift**                                            | Monthly monitoring by product, applicant segment, exposure band, document type, region, and exception type; recalibration only after governance approval                   |
| **Loss of auditability**                                   | Immutable log of model suggestion, confidence, input version, human override, final decision, timestamp, and responsible user                                              |
| **Four-eyes violations in High-exposure SME cases**        | System-enforced segregation: the same person cannot complete Credit analysis and Committee packaging; month-end exception dashboard                                        |
| **KYC-before-signing violations**                          | Hard system gate preventing Customer signing or e-signature invitation before KYC clearance; separate audit of provisional signing attempts                                |
| **Customer friction from rigid document checklists**       | Segment-specific and language-specific instructions; RM preview; flexible upload paths; monitor abandonment and complaint rates                                            |
| **Shifted workload to analysts or legal ops**              | Capacity gates before expanding automation; monitor WIP, queue length, aging, and utilization in Credit analysis and Legal ops                                             |
| **External valuer quality disputes**                       | SLA scorecards, valuer performance monitoring, escalation rules, second review for high-value or disputed collateral                                                       |

# 6. Recommended phased rollout

### Phase 0 — Process and control instrumentation

Before production automation, establish measurement and control baselines. The event log should better represent concurrency because KYC check and Financial document review are often concurrent in practice but recorded sequentially. Add or validate start/complete timestamps, control-state fields, KYC-clearance timestamps, signing timestamps, rule-version IDs, and user IDs for four-eyes monitoring.

**Baseline metrics**

| area                   | metrics                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Cycle time and waiting | end-to-end cycle time by applicant/product/exposure; median waiting before KYC, Financial document review, Normalize financial statements, Credit analysis, Contract preparation, Customer signing |
| Rework                 | Financial document review → Request missing documents → Financial document review loop rate; Contract preparation → Remediate control issue → Contract preparation loop rate                       |
| KYC                    | Resolve entity mismatch waiting time, service time, repeat rate, specialist WIP                                                                                                                    |
| Analyst capacity       | Normalize financial statements service time, Credit analysis queue, analyst utilization, aged WIP                                                                                                  |
| Controls               | KYC-before-signing violations, provisional signing rate, four-eyes violations, contract-term mismatch                                                                                              |
| Auditability           | percentage of automated suggestions with logged input, confidence, user override, and final decision                                                                                               |

**Gate to proceed:** baseline is stable enough to distinguish real improvement from mix effects, and every automated module has an audit-log design.

### Phase 1 — Months 1–3: launch A5 and sidecar A4

Start with **A5 Missing-document checklist and customer portal nudging** and **A4 Entity-mismatch resolution assistant**.

A5 can be integrated into the customer/document workflow as one of the first two deep integrations. A4 should initially be a sidecar assistant for compliance specialists, not an autonomous KYC decision engine.

**Monitoring metrics**

| module | metrics                                                                                                                              |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| A5     | missing-document request rate, repeat-request rate, customer response time, complaint/abandonment rate, RM override rate             |
| A4     | entity-mismatch waiting time, repeat KYC cycle rate, specialist WIP, percentage of suggestions accepted, false-match review findings |

**Gate to proceed:** missing-document loops decrease without unacceptable customer friction; A4 reduces specialist preparation time without increasing KYC-quality issues.

### Phase 2 — Months 4–6: deep integration of A6; shadow A9

Deploy **A6 Financial-statement extraction and normalization** as the second deep integration in the first six months. Run **A9 Contract generation with control-rule checks** in shadow mode only: it flags mismatches but does not yet generate or write final contracts automatically.

This respects the constraint that only two modules can be deeply integrated with core banking in the first six months.

**Monitoring metrics**

| module    | metrics                                                                                                                          |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A6        | extraction confidence, normalization accuracy, analyst correction rate, normalization service time, Credit analysis queue length |
| A9 shadow | detected term mismatches, KYC-before-signing blocks that would have fired, rule false positives, legal-ops feedback              |

**Gate to proceed:** A6 reduces analyst normalization effort without increasing credit-data errors; A9 shadow checks show acceptable precision and a stable rule library.

### Phase 3 — Months 7–9: controlled production rollout of A9

Move **A9** from shadow mode to controlled production for lower-risk products first, then expand to Credit-line renewal and High-exposure SME only after rule checks perform reliably.

A9 should include hard controls: no contract generation unless KYC is clear, Committee decision data is final, required collateral status is complete, and the rule-library version is approved.

**Monitoring metrics**

| area             | metrics                                                                                                 |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Contract quality | contract-remediation loop rate, contract-term mismatch rate, legal override rate                        |
| Controls         | KYC-before-signing violations, provisional signing attempts blocked, High-exposure four-eyes exceptions |
| Resource impact  | legal-ops WIP, month-end backlog, operations activation defects                                         |

**Gate to proceed:** contract-remediation loops fall, no increase in legal defects, and control violations decline.

### Phase 4 — Months 10–12: stabilize and prepare next wave

Do not add a fifth production module if the first-year budget is capped at four. Use the last quarter to stabilize A4/A5/A6/A9, quantify realized benefits, and prepare the next-wave choice.

The likely next-wave candidates are:

| next-wave candidate                    | when to choose it                                                                            |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A7 Collateral valuation triage**     | if Equipment finance and collateral-required Working-capital cases still dominate cycle time |
| **A10 E-signature adoption/reminders** | if KYC-before-signing control is stable but Customer signing remains the major wait          |
| **A1 Auto-registration**               | if upstream data quality is stable and downstream capacity can absorb faster inflow          |
| **A8 Committee pack auto-assembly**    | if A4/A6/A7 produce clean enough artifacts for reliable pack assembly                        |

# 7. Final recommendation

I recommend the **balanced first-year portfolio: A4, A5, A6, and A9**.

This portfolio is stronger than a frequency-led plan because it attacks the activities that shape end-to-end performance: KYC/entity exceptions, missing-document loops, financial normalization before Credit analysis, and contract/control rework. It also satisfies the compliance requirement because A4 and A9 strengthen controls rather than merely saving labor.

The highest-frequency candidate, **A1 Auto-registration**, should not be the first priority as a standalone automation win. Register application is frequent and easy to automate, but its mean service time and waiting time are modest compared with later bottlenecks. Automating it first would mainly push more work into KYC, Financial document review, Normalize financial statements, and Credit analysis. Given that compliance specialists are scarce and credit analysts are already central in the working-together network, A1 could improve local productivity while worsening process-level congestion.

A1 can still be a useful later module or a small political pilot, but it should follow—not precede—the modules that improve upstream quality, exception handling, and control reliability. The bank should automate for flow and control first, then for volume.
