# 1. Risk indicators

**Overall assessment.** The evidence is most consistent with a mixture of mechanisms that differ by cluster. It does not establish collusion anywhere.

- **Buyer_Carla / Approver_Gita and Buyer_Dan / Manager_Hugo:** mainly emergency handling plus informal specialization (79–88% emergency-flagged, low vendor concentration, low manual share). There are real control exceptions in the retrospective-justification step, and data-quality distortion from the shared account and batch upload.
- **VendorMaster_Lena / AP_Kim on vendor family V-77x:** the least explained cluster. Emergency handling accounts for little of it (16% emergency). A legitimate mass bank-maintenance exercise is a plausible explanation, but the log cannot verify it. It combines the attributes that matter most for payment-diversion risk, so it is the first review priority. That is a statement about exposure and verifiability, not about anyone's conduct.
- **Buyer_Aline / Approver_Elena and Buyer_Boris / Approver_Felix:** team structure. These are the most frequent pairs and the least concerning.
- **Bot and mapping patterns:** data-quality and automation effects.

Co-working frequency is not treated as an indicator. The ranking by frequency is roughly the inverse of the ranking by concern: Aline–Elena share about 77% of Aline's cases and Lena–Kim only about 18% of Lena's.

# 4. Network and conformance analyses

All checks run on two views: raw `org:resource` and an adjusted identity. Cases flagged only in the raw view are probable artefacts. Cases flagged only in the adjusted view were masked by data quality.

**Preparation and identity adjustment**

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

**Evidence that would increase concern**

- Bank accounts entered for V-77x that do not match vendor-confirmed details, belong to a different beneficiary name or country, or were reverted after Release Payment.
- No maintenance ticket, vendor notification or callback record for the 9 same-resource cases or the 16 fast cases, or tickets created after the audit started.
- ERP change logs and access logs (user ID, terminal, session) confirming that one person performed both Change Vendor Bank Details and Payment Approval interactively, not through a batch upload.
- Speed specific to the pair: V-77x manual-upload cases approved by AP_Iris or AP_Jonas take hours, while only Lena → Kim cases take minutes.
- V-77x `amount_band` values clustering just below approval thresholds, with several cases for the same vendor and week split across `region`.
- The Lena → Kim → Lena loop containing a second bank-detail change between Payment Approval and Release Payment.
- Four-eyes violations for Create PO + Approve PO that persist after correcting the `legacy_erp` mapping and using `entered_by` / `approved_by`, especially in non-emergency, high-amount cases.
- `emergency_flag` set or changed after approval, or rush-channel cases with no clinical request, stock-out record, incident report or work order.
- Prices above contract or market for Carla/Gita or Dan/Hugo vendors, no quotes on spot purchases, or re-approval within minutes after an increase in amount or change of vendor.
- Retrospective justifications that are generic copies, created after quarter close for old overrides, or where the original spreadsheet names the same person as the override.
- Undisclosed relationships in conflict-of-interest declarations or vendor ownership data. This is outside the log and needs proper authorisation.

**Evidence that would reduce concern**

- A dated mass-maintenance ticket for the V-77x banking-platform change listing the affected `vendor_id` values, with vendor letters and callback verification by someone other than the person who made the change.
- Payment destinations matching confirmed accounts, with no vendor complaints of non-receipt.
- `entered_by` / `approved_by` showing different individuals in the 9 and 42 violating cases, or upload metadata showing the uploader was stamped as `org:resource`.
- Corrected bot mapping removing most Create PO + Approve PO violations, with the remainder covered by an override and a timely justification by another person.
- V-77x cases processed by other staff with the same speed and manual share, pointing to a process effect.
- Formal delegation of authority for Approver_Gita and Manager_Hugo, on-call rosters matching the weekend cases, and hospital or facilities records matching the emergency cases.
- Original spreadsheet for the 12 batch-uploaded justifications showing different justifiers and dates within 3 working days.
- Loop content showing clarification or correction (unchanged or lower amount, typo fixes), and loop rates that are similar for any buyer–approver pair handling emergencies.
- Late justifications that fall within 3 working days once holidays and weekends are applied correctly.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

Our analysis of the procurement-to-payment event log for the last quarter identified several control exceptions. These are departures from the designed segregation of duties and from the emergency procedure. On the evidence available they are not findings of misconduct, and the log contains no direct evidence of wrongdoing by any individual.

Most rapid approvals and recurring working pairs relate to emergency medical-supply and weekend facilities-repair purchases, where an urgent channel is expected. In these areas the concerns are procedural: retrospective justifications were late in roughly one fifth of cases, and in 17 cases the justification was recorded under the same user as the override.

The subgroup warranting the closest attention is the 44 cases in which vendor bank details were changed and payment was approved shortly afterwards, mostly for vendor family V-77x. These cases combine high amounts, manual uploads, vendor concentration, a low emergency share and nine apparent four-eyes exceptions. A recent banking-platform change at the vendor may explain them, but the related maintenance tickets are not linked in the log, so this could not be confirmed.

Our conclusions are limited by data quality: a shared approver account, a bot-to-user mapping error in the legacy ERP, batch-uploaded justifications with identical timestamps, and missing ticket references. Some reported exceptions may be recording artefacts, and others may be hidden.

We recommend: (1) full review of the V-77x bank-change cases against tickets, vendor confirmations and payment destinations; (2) re-performance of the four-eyes tests using corrected user mappings and the detailed audit trail; (3) review of late and same-user justifications; and (4) remediation of the shared account and mapping table. Conclusions about individual conduct should be deferred until this work is complete.
