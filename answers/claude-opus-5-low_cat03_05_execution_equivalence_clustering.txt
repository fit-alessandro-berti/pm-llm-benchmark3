## 1. Equivalence design space

Throughout, an execution is a tuple E = (V, <, O, type, inc, R, q, ext), where V is the set of events, < their timestamp order, O the set of objects touched, type: O -> {Order, Item, Package, Delivery, Invoice, Payment}, inc ⊆ V × O the E2O incidence, R ⊆ O × O the O2O relation with qualifier map q, and ext: O -> {true,false} the flag that is true exactly for identifiers prefixed `EXT`. Every execution has a distinguished lead object of type Order.

### R1 — Activity-sequence equivalence (≡_A)

**Definition.** Let seq(E) = ⟨act(v1), ..., act(vn)⟩ be the activity labels of the events in timestamp order. E ≡_A E' iff seq(E) = seq(E') as strings.

**Preserved.** Number of events, activity labels, their multiplicity, and their total order. Repetition is preserved (two `Pack Package` events remain two symbols).

**Discarded.** All object information: types, identifiers, multiplicities, E2O incidence, O2O structure, qualifiers, externality. This is exactly the classical flattened-trace view of the lead object.

**Algebraic properties.** Equality of strings: reflexive, symmetric, transitive. A genuine equivalence relation, hence it induces a partition.

**Concurrency / ties.** With identical timestamps the total order is not well defined, so ≡_A as stated is not well defined either. The repair used here: when a set of events shares a timestamp, replace the block by its lexicographically sorted label multiset rendered as a sorted sub-string. This keeps the relation an equivalence and makes it invariant to arbitrary tie-breaking. Under a genuinely partial-order semantics one would instead compare Hasse diagrams up to label-preserving isomorphism, which is a strictly coarser relation (see R4).

### R2 — Typed structural equivalence under identifier renaming (≡_T)

**Definition.** E ≡_T E' iff there is a bijection φ: V -> V' and a bijection ψ: O -> O' such that (i) act(v) = act(φ(v)); (ii) φ is order-preserving on the (possibly partial) order; (iii) type(o) = type(ψ(o)); (iv) (v,o) ∈ inc ⟺ (φ(v), ψ(o)) ∈ inc'; (v) ψ maps the lead Order to the lead Order. In words: typed labelled isomorphism of the event–object bipartite graph, respecting event order.

**Two variants are used below**, and the distinction matters:
- ≡_T (plain): ext is *not* required to be preserved; an EXT object is just another object of its type.
- ≡_T+ (externality-aware): additionally ext(o) = ext'(ψ(o)).

**Preserved.** Object types, per-event object multiplicities (the "typed arity vector" of every event, e.g. `Pack Package{Order:1, Item:2, Package:1}`), sharing patterns (which events touch the same object), event order, and repeated activities.

**Discarded.** Local identifiers, O2O relations and their qualifiers (they are not part of inc), attribute values, and — in the plain variant — externality.

**Algebraic properties.** Isomorphism: reflexive, symmetric, transitive. Deciding it is graph-isomorphism-hard in general; for executions of this size the canonical form of §2 decides it exactly.

**Concurrency / ties.** Condition (ii) is imposed on the partial order induced by timestamps: φ must be an isomorphism of the order relation, so concurrent events may be permuted freely. With strict total orders this degenerates to sequence-preserving isomorphism.

### R3 — Business-semantic equivalence (≡_B) — *preferred relation*

**Definition.** E ≡_B E' iff sem(E) = sem(E'), where sem is the following feature vector, computed relative to the lead Order L:

1. **Fulfilment shape**: (#Item objects incident to L, #Package objects, #Delivery objects, #`Pack Package` events, #`Dispatch` events).
2. **Financial shape**: (#Invoice objects, #`Record Payment` events, payment-qualifier pattern — the sorted multiset of O2O qualifiers on Payment→Invoice edges, e.g. {settles} vs {partially_settles, completes_settlement}).
3. **Isolation profile**: a triple (logistics_shared, financial_shared, item_shared) of booleans, where logistics_shared is true iff some Package or Delivery event of L also carries an external Order or external Item; financial_shared is true iff some Invoice or Payment event carries an external Order; item_shared analogously for Items.
4. **Exception motifs**: the set of motifs detected from O2O qualifiers and the reverse-flow alphabet — `substitution` (a `replaces`/`removed_before_dispatch` qualifier between two Items or an Item and a Package), `return_repack` (presence of `Return Item`/`Inspect Return` plus a second Pack of the returned item), `installment_payment` (≥2 payments against one invoice), `split_shipment` (#Delivery ≥ 2), `split_packaging_consolidated_shipment` (#Package ≥ 2 and #Delivery = 1).

**Preserved.** Everything a manager acts on: how fragmented fulfilment and settlement are, whether the execution is self-contained, and which named exception occurred.

**Discarded.** Identifiers, exact interleaving of Pack/Dispatch, which particular item was substituted or returned, and cardinalities beyond the counted ones (e.g. two vs three items only matters through the count, not through identity).

**Algebraic properties.** Equality of a computed feature vector: reflexive, symmetric, transitive. This is deliberate — feature-vector equality is an equivalence, whereas a threshold/similarity criterion ("differ in at most one feature") would be reflexive and symmetric but *not* transitive, and would therefore only yield a tolerance relation, not a partition. I avoid that.

**Concurrency / ties.** ≡_B is almost order-free: only the motif detectors use order, and each is defined on a precedence *pattern* (e.g. `Return Item` after the `Dispatch` that carries the returning package), not on positions. Ties therefore do not affect it, which is one of its practical advantages.

### R4 — (optional) Partial-order behavioural equivalence (≡_P)

E ≡_P E' iff the labelled partial orders obtained by keeping only *causally justified* precedences are isomorphic. Causal justification: v → w is kept only if v and w share at least one object. `Issue Invoice` and `Pack Package` share only the Order, so under a rule "shared non-lead object" they become concurrent. This deliberately erases the accidental fact that invoicing came last in all eight cases, and is used in §7.

## 2. Canonical signatures

Canonical scheme for the preferred relation ≡_B, with the typed-structural canonical form given too since ≡_T is reported in §3.

```python
from collections import Counter
import json

LEAD = "Order"

def typed_arity(ev):
    """{'Order':1,'Item':2,'Package':1} — identifier-free."""
    return {t: len(ids) for t, ids in sorted(ev["objects"].items())}

def structural_signature(ex):
    """Canonical form for =_T+ : order-preserving, id-free, externality-aware."""
    # 1. canonical object names: type + externality + sorted list of event
    #    positions in which the object occurs  -> refined until stable
    pos = {o: [] for o in ex["objects"]}
    for i, ev in enumerate(ex["events"]):
        for t, ids in ev["objects"].items():
            for o in ids:
                pos[o].append(i)
    canon = {o: (ex["type"][o], "EXT" if o.startswith("EXT") else "LOC",
                 tuple(pos[o])) for o in pos}
    # ties broken by sorting equal keys (safe: equal keys are interchangeable)
    order = sorted(canon, key=lambda o: (canon[o], o))
    rank = {o: i for i, o in enumerate(order)}
    body = []
    for ev in ex["events"]:
        body.append((ev["activity"],
                     tuple(sorted((ex["type"][o], canon[o][1], rank[o])
                                  for t, ids in ev["objects"].items()
                                  for o in ids))))
    return json.dumps(body, sort_keys=True)

def semantic_signature(ex):
    """Canonical form for =_B (preferred)."""
    typ, o2o = ex["type"], ex["o2o"]           # o2o: list of (src, qual, tgt)
    objs = set(ex["objects"])
    def n(t, external=False):
        return sum(1 for o in objs
                   if typ[o] == t and o.startswith("EXT") == external)
    acts = Counter(ev["activity"] for ev in ex["events"])

    fulfil = (n("Item"), n("Package"), n("Delivery"),
              acts["Pack Package"], acts["Dispatch"])
    pay_q = tuple(sorted(q for s, q, t in o2o if typ[s] == "Payment"))
    finance = (n("Invoice"), acts["Record Payment"], pay_q)

    ext_in = lambda ev: any(o.startswith("EXT")
                            for ids in ev["objects"].values() for o in ids)
    logistics_shared = any(ext_in(ev) for ev in ex["events"]
                           if ev["activity"] in ("Pack Package", "Dispatch"))
    financial_shared = any(ext_in(ev) for ev in ex["events"]
                           if ev["activity"] in ("Issue Invoice",
                                                 "Record Payment"))
    isolation = (logistics_shared, financial_shared,
                 logistics_shared or financial_shared)

    motifs = set()
    if any(q in ("replaces", "removed_before_dispatch") for _, q, _ in o2o):
        motifs.add("substitution")
    if acts["Return Item"] and acts["Pack Package"] > 1:
        motifs.add("return_repack")
    if acts["Record Payment"] > 1:
        motifs.add("installment_payment")
    if n("Delivery") > 1:
        motifs.add("split_shipment")
    elif n("Package") > 1:
        motifs.add("split_packaging_consolidated_shipment")

    return json.dumps({"fulfil": fulfil, "finance": finance,
                       "isolation": isolation, "motifs": sorted(motifs)},
                      sort_keys=True)
```

The scheme is invariant to local renaming (identifiers never enter the output; in `structural_signature` they appear only as a deterministic tie-break among already-interchangeable objects) and preserves types, multiplicities, EXT flags, event order (structural signature), E2O incidence, O2O qualifiers (semantic signature) and repeated activities.

## 4. Cluster interpretations

**S1 — Clean isolated order (X1, X2).** Signature: everything singular, no EXT, no motif. Behaviour: happy path — items added, one package, one dispatch, one invoice, one payment. Opportunity: the benchmark class against which all others are cost-compared; safe for full automation and straight-through processing. Risk: none intrinsic; the risk is analytical, namely that other clusters get silently mixed into it. Flattening on Order represents it *well* — this is the only cluster for which flattening is faithful. Stable under repairs: yes; it survives every perturbation in §7 except that under "ignore externality" it absorbs X5 and X6.

**S2 — Consolidated multi-pack (X3).** Signature: Packages > Deliveries = 1. Behaviour: warehouse splits an order across cartons for volumetric/handling reasons but the carrier consolidates. Opportunity: packing cost per order is a controllable KPI; carton count drives material and handling spend. Risk: modest — a second carton is a chance for a missing-carton claim. Flattening on Order collapses the two `Pack Package` events into an undifferentiated repetition and loses the crucial fact that they feed *one* delivery; flattening on Package splits the order into two traces and destroys the consolidation. Stable: yes, unless one treats `Pack Package` repetitions as collapsible (§7), which merges S2 into S1.

**S3 — Split shipment with installments (X4).** Signature: Deliveries = 2 and Payments = 2 with `partially_settles` then `completes_settlement`. Behaviour: the order is fulfilled incrementally and paid incrementally against a single invoice. Risk: partial-delivery SLA disputes, revenue recognised before full settlement, dunning exposure on the open balance; also double the transport cost. Opportunity: detect whether split shipments *cause* installment behaviour — the correlation is visible only object-centrically. Flattening on Order shows `Pack, Dispatch, Pack, Dispatch` as a loop and hides that each dispatch has its own delivery object; flattening on Invoice hides the shipment split entirely. Stable: the split-shipment part is robust; the installment part disappears if payment multiplicity is remodelled as an amount attribute (§7), collapsing S3 towards a plain split-shipment class.

**S4 — Logistics-shared execution (X5).** Signature: iso = (True, False, True) with an external Order and Item on `Pack Package`/`Dispatch`. Behaviour: co-loading — one carton carries items from two orders, dispatched once. Opportunity: real transport-cost savings; this is often a deliberate consolidation policy. Risk: a delivery failure or carrier delay propagates to an unrelated order, so lead-time variance is coupled across cases; and per-order cost allocation is ambiguous. Flattening on Order duplicates the shared Pack/Dispatch event into both orders' traces (convergence), inflating event counts and making the shared package look like two independent packages. Stable: yes under qualifier removal, but it *vanishes* if external objects are dropped — then X5 becomes indistinguishable from S1, which is the single most dangerous data-repair choice here.

**S5 — Finance-shared execution (X6).** Signature: iso = (False, True, True). Behaviour: one invoice bills two orders and one payment settles it, with no order-level allocation recorded. Risk: revenue and DSO cannot be attributed per order without an allocation rule; any per-order payment KPI is fabricated. Opportunity: consolidated billing reduces transaction cost for repeat customers — worth measuring deliberately rather than discovering by accident. Flattening on Order double-counts the payment; flattening on Invoice merges two orders into one case. Stable: same fragility as S4 — it collapses into S1 when externality is ignored.

**S6 — In-flight substitution (X7).** Signature: motif `substitution`, all multiplicities identical to S1. Behaviour: an item is replaced or pulled from the carton before dispatch; only the qualified O2O edge and the item-level E2O on `Dispatch` reveal it. Risk: the customer may be invoiced for the removed item — a silent billing-accuracy defect; also a stock-accuracy defect. Opportunity: substitution rate is a supply-availability indicator. Flattening on Order is maximally misleading: X7's trace is byte-identical to X1's, so a flat model would report a perfect happy-path case. Stable: **no** — this cluster exists only because O2O qualifiers are kept; ignoring qualifiers merges it into S1 (a weaker residual signal, the extra `Item:I72` on `Dispatch`, keeps it separate at the ≡_T+ level but not at ≡_B).

**S7 — Reverse flow / return-repack (X8).** Signature: motif `return_repack`, explicit `Return Item` / `Inspect Return`, second Delivery. Behaviour: goods go out, one item comes back, is inspected, repacked and re-dispatched, then the order is invoiced once. Risk: highest cost-to-serve of all clusters (double transport, inspection labour, delayed cash); also a quality signal about the returned SKU. Opportunity: separating corrective from incremental second shipments is exactly what makes return cost measurable. Flattening on Order yields the same "two Pack, two Dispatch" skeleton as X4 plus two extra labels, inviting a spurious merge. Stable: yes — the motif rests on distinct activity labels, not only on qualifiers, so it survives qualifier removal.

## 5. Borderline cases

**X1 vs X2.** Identical under every relation defined here (≡_A, ≡_T, ≡_T+, ≡_B). Disagreement arises only against a relation that keeps raw identifiers or the *item-identifier* ordering — e.g. PM4Py's exact key with unsorted object lists, or a definition that reads `I22` before `I21` as "reverse picking order". Such a relation splits X1 from X2. That is defensible only if identifiers encode something (sequence numbers, SKU rank); here nothing says they do, and the task explicitly forbids treating local identifiers as meaningful. Verdict: merge.

**X1 vs X5.** ≡_A merges them: identical label sequences. ≡_T and ≡_T+ separate them: the `Pack Package` event has typed arity (Order:2, Item:3, Package:1) instead of (Order:1, Item:2, Package:1). ≡_B separates them via the isolation profile. Two defensible positions: (a) "the Order's *own* behaviour is unchanged — the customer experience is a single package, a single delivery, one payment; the co-load is a warehouse implementation detail" → merge; (b) "the execution is not causally isolated; its lead time is now a function of another order" → split. I take (b), because the whole point of an object-centric view is that shared objects are real dependencies, not noise.

**X1 vs X6.** Same disagreement, mirrored to the financial side. ≡_A merges; ≡_T+/≡_B split. Additional subtlety: X6's *local* object multiplicities around O6 are exactly X1's (one invoice, one payment); only the extra `EXT-OB` on the invoice events differs. A relation that counts objects per type but ignores externality merges them; the note "M61 settles V61 without an order-level allocation" is the decisive semantic evidence for splitting, because it means no per-order payment fact exists.

**X1 vs X7.** ≡_A merges — this is the strongest argument in the whole exercise against label-only clustering. ≡_T+ splits, but only by the thin margin of `Item:I72` appearing on `Dispatch` (arity (Order:1, Item:1, Package:1, Delivery:1) vs (Order:1, Package:1, Delivery:1)). If the log had omitted that E2O edge, ≡_T+ would also merge them and *only* the qualifier-aware ≡_B would separate. Defensible counter-position: `removed_before_dispatch` is a data-quality annotation rather than a business event, so the two executions delivered the same commercial outcome. That is tenable for a revenue report and untenable for a billing-accuracy audit.

**X3 vs X4.** ≡_A splits (different label sequences and lengths). ≡_T splits. ≡_B splits: (2,2,1,2,1) vs (2,2,2,2,2) plus installment payments. But a coarser defensible relation — "fragmentation of *packaging*", counting Packages only — merges them, since both use two cartons. A different coarse relation — "customer-visible shipments" — puts X3 with X1 (one delivery each) and leaves X4 alone. Both are reasonable; they answer different questions (warehouse cost vs customer experience). Under the partial-order reading (§7) X4's interleaving Pack/Dispatch/Pack/Dispatch also becomes order-equivalent to X3's Pack/Pack/Dispatch-style grouping in everything except delivery count, tightening the case for merging.

**X4 vs X8.** Both have 2 Packages, 2 Deliveries, 2 Pack and 2 Dispatch events — their fulfilment shapes are identical. ≡_A splits them (X8 has `Return Item`, `Inspect Return`); ≡_T+ splits them; ≡_B splits them by motif *and* by financial shape (2 payments vs 1). The genuinely hard question is whether the *second delivery means the same thing*: in X4 it is incremental (new value flowing to the customer), in X8 it is corrective (value already sent, being re-sent). A relation built purely on multiplicities — a very common practical choice — merges them and will report identical "split shipment rate" for a growth pattern and a failure pattern. Any defensible clustering here must use either the qualifier `P82 replaces P81 for item I82` or the return activity labels.

## 6. Relation to `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel`

**What the PM4Py key preserves.** `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel, object_type, max_objs)` extracts, for each object of the given lead type, its connected object-centric execution, and builds a hashable key from that execution's structure — in essence the ordered sequence of events with, per event, the activity and the participating objects (with their types), plus the objects' own types. Executions producing the identical key land in the same cluster; the result is a dict from key to the list of member executions. It is an *exact* equality on a serialised structure, not a similarity measure. In particular it captures E2O incidence and repeated activities, and it does **not** interpret O2O qualifiers, does not distinguish "external" objects (it has no such notion — it simply pulls the connected component in), and does not normalise identifiers unless the serialisation does.

**Why raw identifiers make clusters too fine.** If identifiers survive into the key, X1 and X2 get different keys and every execution becomes its own singleton — the finest partition, which the task rightly warns against. Even with types substituted for identifiers, unsorted object lists make `Item:I21,I22` differ from `Item:I22,I21`, reintroducing spurious splits. The fix is canonicalisation: replace each identifier by a rank derived from (type, externality, occurrence positions) and sort every object list, exactly as in `structural_signature` above.

**Canonicalisation / post-processing.** Three layers: (1) rewrite the key into the id-free canonical form; (2) enrich it with the externality flag, which PM4Py will not give you — derivable from whether the connected component contains a second object of the lead type; (3) aggregate the exact clusters into semantic clusters by mapping each exact key to the `semantic_signature` above, so that "clean orders with 2 items" and "clean orders with 5 items" can be merged if the item count is not what you are studying.

**When exact equivalence is preferable.** When you need *provable* identity: regression testing of a process implementation, deduplicating executions before expensive analysis, verifying that a migration preserved structure, or building a lossless index. Exact clusters are also the right *input* to a coarser clustering — cluster exactly first (cheap, deterministic), then merge keys, rather than computing an expensive semantic function per execution.

**`max_objs` and large components.** `max_objs` caps the size of the connected execution considered. Shared objects make components grow transitively: a co-loaded package (X5) links two orders, whose invoices may link further orders, and in a real log a single component can swallow thousands of objects. Consequences: (i) with a small `max_objs` large executions are truncated or dropped, which systematically *removes exactly the shared cases* (S4, S5) — the ones most worth studying — and biases the analysis towards clean orders; (ii) with a large `max_objs` keys become huge and near-unique, so cluster count approaches execution count and the partition is useless. Practical rule: set `max_objs` generously, but report how many executions hit the cap, and treat capped executions as their own class rather than silently discarding them.

```python
import pm4py
from collections import defaultdict

LEAD = "Order"
MAX_OBJS = 200

clusters = pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel, LEAD, max_objs=MAX_OBJS)
# clusters: {structural_key: [sub-OCELs / executions]}

def to_execution(sub_ocel, lead_type=LEAD):
    """Project a returned sub-OCEL into the dict shape used in section 2."""
    ev = sub_ocel.relations.groupby(
        [sub_ocel.event_id_column, sub_ocel.event_activity,
         sub_ocel.event_timestamp])
    events = []
    for (eid, act, ts), grp in sorted(ev, key=lambda kv: kv[0][2]):
        objs = defaultdict(list)
        for _, r in grp.iterrows():
            objs[r[sub_ocel.object_type_column]].append(
                r[sub_ocel.object_id_column])
        events.append({"activity": act,
                       "objects": {t: sorted(v) for t, v in objs.items()}})
    types = dict(zip(sub_ocel.objects[sub_ocel.object_id_column],
                     sub_ocel.objects[sub_ocel.object_type_column]))
    o2o = [(r["ocel:oid"], r["ocel:qualifier"], r["ocel:oid_2"])
           for _, r in getattr(sub_ocel, "o2o",
                               __import__("pandas").DataFrame()).iterrows()] \
          if getattr(sub_ocel, "o2o", None) is not None else []
    return {"events": events, "objects": types, "type": types, "o2o": o2o}

# --- custom canonicalisation + semantic aggregation -----------------------
semantic = defaultdict(list)
capped = []
for key, executions in clusters.items():
    for ex_ocel in executions:
        ex = to_execution(ex_ocel)
        if len(ex["objects"]) >= MAX_OBJS:
            capped.append(ex); continue
        struct = structural_signature(ex)      # id-free, externality-aware
        sem = semantic_signature(ex)           # preferred =_B key
        semantic[sem].append((struct, ex))

for sem_key, members in sorted(semantic.items(),
                               key=lambda kv: -len(kv[1])):
    n_struct = len({s for s, _ in members})
    print(f"{len(members):5d} orders | {n_struct:3d} structural variants "
          f"| {sem_key}")
print(f"{len(capped)} executions hit max_objs={MAX_OBJS} "
      f"(reported separately, not discarded)")
```

## 7. Robustness analysis

**(a) Ignore all O2O qualifiers.** S6 (X7) loses its defining evidence and merges into S1 under ≡_B — X7 would be reported as a clean order. S3 loses the `partially_settles`/`completes_settlement` distinction, but survives because payment *count* is still 2. S7 survives because `Return Item`/`Inspect Return` are activities, not qualifiers. Conclusion: exactly one cluster is qualifier-fragile, and it is the one whose failure mode (billing for a removed item) is the most costly. Recommendation: never drop qualifiers in a billing-accuracy context.

**(b) Ignore external objects.** S4 (X5) and S5 (X6) both collapse into S1: X5's Pack loses `EXT-OA`/`EXT-IA` and becomes (Order:1, Item:2, Package:1); X6's invoice events lose `EXT-OB`. The partition drops from seven semantic clusters to five, and two dependency structures become invisible. This is precisely what naive flattening on Order does. It is the least defensible perturbation of the four.

**(c) Collapse repeated same-label events.** S2 (X3) collapses into S1 — two `Pack Package` become one label, and if object counts are also collapsed the packaging split disappears entirely. X4 collapses toward S1 as well at the ≡_A level (its label sequence becomes `Create, Add, Pack, Dispatch, Invoice, Payment`), though ≡_B keeps it separate because Delivery *object* count is still 2. X8 keeps `Return Item`/`Inspect Return` and stays separate. Lesson: collapsing repetitions is safe only if object multiplicities are retained as separate features — which is the main reason ≡_B counts objects rather than events wherever possible.

**(d) Pack/Dispatch as a partial order.** X4's `Pack(P41), Dispatch(D41), Pack(P42), Dispatch(D42)` and a hypothetical `Pack, Pack, Dispatch, Dispatch` become order-equivalent, because the two package chains share no object other than the lead Order and are therefore concurrent. Under ≡_A this alone would merge two currently distinct classes; under ≡_B nothing changes, since ≡_B never used the interleaving. X8 is *not* affected: `Return Item` is causally after `Dispatch(D81)` through the shared Delivery D81, and the repack is after the return through I82, so the corrective chain remains ordered. This is a good property — the partial-order reading removes exactly the accidental ordering and keeps the meaningful one.

**(e) Payment multiplicity as an amount attribute.** If X4's two payments are remodelled as one settlement with an amount schedule, the `installment_payment` motif disappears and S3 degrades to "split shipment, single settlement". X4 then differs from X8 only by motif (`split_shipment` vs `return_repack`) — and if qualifiers are *also* ignored (perturbation a), X4 and X8 differ only by the two return activities. Stacking perturbations, not any single one, is what destroys the partition; a robustness report should therefore evaluate combinations, not just one-at-a-time changes.

## 8. Limitations and recommendation

**Compliance monitoring → typed structural equivalence with qualifiers (≡_T+ enriched with O2O qualifiers).** Compliance asks whether the recorded structure is legitimate: was every dispatched item invoiced, was every invoice allocated to an order, was a removed item billed? These questions fail exactly on the distinctions that only the fine relation keeps (X7 vs X1, X6's missing allocation). A coarse relation would certify X7 as clean.

**Operational segmentation → business-semantic equivalence ≡_B.** Planners need a handful of actionable classes with meaningful volumes: clean, multi-pack, split-shipment, co-loaded, consolidated-billing, substituted, returned. Seven classes over eight executions is an artefact of the toy size; on a real log ≡_B produces tens, not thousands, of classes, and each maps to a lever (packing policy, carrier consolidation, billing policy, returns handling).

**Root-cause analysis → ≡_B as the stratification, but drilling down into ≡_T+ / ≡_P inside a cluster.** Root cause needs the coarse class to define the population ("late split-shipment orders") and the fine structure to compare members within it ("the late ones dispatched the second package before repacking"). The partial-order variant ≡_P matters here specifically, because it prevents attributing causality to an ordering that is merely a logging artefact.

**Descriptive reporting → activity-sequence equivalence ≡_A, with an explicit caveat.** For a management dashboard, the label-level variant count is legible and comparable to conventional process-mining outputs. It must be published alongside an object-centric breakdown, because ≡_A alone maps X1, X2, X5, X6 and X7 to a single "happy path" figure — a materially false statement about four of those five.

**Why no single notion suffices.** The four purposes disagree about what counts as an error. Compliance treats any structural deviation as signal, so it needs the finest partition and tolerates thousands of classes. Segmentation needs classes with enough mass to justify a policy change, so it needs the coarsest partition that still separates cost drivers. Root cause needs both at once, at different stages. Reporting needs stability over time, which argues for a definition that does not shift when the log's object model is extended. These are conflicting optimisation targets on the same lattice of partitions — finer is more faithful and less actionable, coarser is more actionable and less faithful — and there is no point on that lattice that is simultaneously the most faithful and the most actionable. The practical recommendation is therefore not to choose one relation but to compute the exact structural partition once and expose the coarsening function (`semantic_signature`) as a configurable, versioned artefact, so that each consumer chooses its own level from a common, reproducible base.

Two limitations of what is proposed here should be stated plainly. First, ≡_B's feature vector is hand-designed; its motif detectors encode domain assumptions (e.g. that `Record Payment` twice means installments rather than a correction and a re-post) that must be validated against the source system. Second, all eight executions are tiny and complete; on real logs, truncated executions and missing O2O edges are the norm, and every cluster boundary that depends on the *absence* of a qualifier — notably the S1/S6 boundary — becomes a data-quality claim rather than a business claim, and should be reported as such.
