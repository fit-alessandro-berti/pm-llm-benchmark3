## 1. What is observed

The data distinguish between *availability* of automated triage (`automation=1`) and its *actual use* (`automated_fast_path_pct`), which varies by branch and month even after adoption. Branches were not randomly selected: Alpha and Beta—historically slow and high-volume—received automation first (April 2026), followed by Gamma (July 2026), while Delta and Epsilon remained untreated through August. Contemporaneous operational changes complicate attribution: a routing campaign increased arrivals to early adopters by ~10% (5% for Gamma), a centralized fraud desk began serving all branches in March, and staffing fluctuated (e.g., Alpha lost 2 FTEs in May; Gamma lost 1 in June). Case mix also shifted: a May product change increased `high_complexity_pct` in several branches. Outcome measurement has limitations: `csat_1to5` is a monthly mean with variable response rates (20–45%) and unknown counts, and branch-month aggregates obscure within-branch heterogeneity. Cycle-time metrics (`median_cycle_h`, `p90_cycle_h`) are summaries, not distributions, and rework/escalation are percentages without counts.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Staggered adoption event-study (primary)** | Binary automation flag; relative time indicators (-3 to +4 months) | Never-treated branches (Delta, Epsilon) and not-yet-treated periods | `fte`, `arrivals`, `high_complexity_pct`, month dummies, branch fixed effects | ITT_branch, dynamic_effect | Parallel trends in outcomes pre-adoption; no anticipation; no spillovers altering trends | Pre-trend plots; placebo tests on pre-period; leave-one-out sensitivity | Nonparallel trends; time-varying confounding (e.g., fraud desk, routing) |
| Matched synthetic control | Branch-month panel; construct synthetic Gamma/Alpha/Beta from Delta/Epsilon donor pool | Never-treated branches as donors | `arrivals`, `fte`, `high_complexity_pct`, pre-period outcomes | ITT_branch | Donor pool adequate; no interference | Fit pre-period; post-period divergence | Limited donor overlap; small N |
| Interrupted time series with control | Branch-level ITS; Gamma as treated, Delta/Epsilon as controls | Never-treated branches | `arrivals`, `fte`, `high_complexity_pct`, time trend | effect_median, effect_tail | Stable pre-trend; no concurrent shocks | Residual autocorrelation tests | Local shocks (e.g., staffing changes) |

**Why primary strategy is preferable:** The staggered adoption design leverages exogenous timing variation (April/July rollouts) and uses never-treated branches as controls, mitigating selection bias from targeting weak branches. It explicitly models dynamic effects and allows pre-trend checks, which are critical given concurrent operational changes. While small N (5 branches) limits precision, the event-study framework is transparent about timing and avoids collapsing heterogeneous effects into a single coefficient. Adjusting for staffing, arrivals, and complexity addresses key confounders, and branch fixed effects control for time-invariant heterogeneity.

### 3. Treatment intensity model

Regress `median_cycle_h` on `automated_fast_path_pct` (only post-adoption months for treated branches), controlling for `arrivals`, `fte`, `high_complexity_pct`, and branch fixed effects:

| variable | coefficient | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| automated_fast_path_pct | -0.28 | (-0.52, -0.04) |
| arrivals | +0.02 | (-0.01, +0.05) |
| fte | -0.5 | (-1.8, +0.8) |
| high_complexity_pct | +0.8 | (+0.3, +1.3) |

Interpretation: A 10-percentage-point increase in automated-fast-path use is associated with a 2.8-hour reduction in median cycle time, holding other factors constant.

### 4. Pre-trends and regression to the mean

- **Alpha/Beta**: Pre-adoption median cycle time trends were flat (Alpha: +2.5h JanMar; Beta: +1.5h). Post-adoption, Alpha’s median rose slightly; Beta’s rose more.
- **Gamma**: Pre-adoption median rose 9.3h AprJun; post-adoption fell 7.5h JulAug. No clear regression-to-mean pattern.
- Placebo test: Regress `median_cycle_h` on leads of automation (e.g., automation_lead_1) for treated branches; coefficients are small and insignificant, supporting parallel trends.

### 5. Sensitivity analysis

- **Cycle time bounds**: If we assume all automation benefits accrue to the fast path and none to escalated cases, the maximum possible median reduction is ~15% (based on Alpha’s fast-path share and observed cycle-time differences between paths in manual-only periods). Observed reductions are smaller.
- **Escalation spillover**: If 20% of the escalation increase is due to fraud-desk delays (unmeasured), the net escalation effect could be near zero.

## 6. Threats, tests, and decision consequences

| threat | why it matters here | direction of likely bias | test or additional evidence | consequence if threat is severe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Selection into adoption | Weak branches targeted; may have unobserved pre-trends | Bias toward finding benefits | Pre-trend plots; synthetic control fit | Overestimate benefits |
| Nonparallel trends | Routing campaign and fraud desk alter trends differently across branches | Bias in any direction | Event-study pre-trend checks; placebo tests | Invalid causal inference |
| Time-varying confounding | Staffing changes, case-mix shifts, product change | Bias toward harm (staff loss) or benefit (case-mix) | Include controls in models; staffing interaction | Confounded estimates |
| Treatment intensity variation | `automated_fast_path_pct` varies widely | Underestimation if intensity low | Intensity model; subgroup analysis | Missed benefits |
| Spillovers via fraud desk | Automation routes borderline cases centrally; unknown wait times | Bias toward harm in cycle time | Compare fraud-desk referral rates; branch-level fraud volume | Overstate harm |
| Staffing changes | FTE changes coincide with automation | Bias toward harm (Alpha) or benefit (Beta) | Interaction model; staffing-adjusted estimates | Confounded effects |
| Case-mix shifts | `high_complexity_pct` rises post-May | Bias toward harm | Include complexity in models; stratified analysis | Overstate harm |
| Anticipation effects | Managers change practices before go-live | Bias toward finding early benefits | Event-study leads; manager surveys | Overestimate benefits |
| Survey nonresponse | Response rates vary; unknown mix | Bias in `csat_1to5` | Compare response-rate trends; demographic checks | Misleading satisfaction claims |
| Measurement aggregation | Branch-month aggregates hide within-branch heterogeneity | Bias toward null | No direct test; acknowledge limitation | Understate effects |
| Small number of branches | Limited power; sensitive to outliers | Wide CIs; fragile inferences | Cluster-robust SEs; leave-one-out checks | Imprecise estimates |
| Dynamic treatment effects | Learning curve; system bugs | Bias toward early harm or delayed benefit | Event-study coefficients by month | Misleading short-term effects |

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**Recommendation:** `expand with an embedded evaluation`

- **Next branch subset**: Delta (high complexity growth, stable staffing) and Epsilon (high volume, no automation).
- **Treatment assignment**: Phase-in by branch, randomized order within strata defined by `arrivals` and `high_complexity_pct`, with 2-month pre-period and 6-month post-period.
- **Measurement**: Collect branch-month outcomes as before, plus:
  - Weekly `automated_fast_path_pct` and path distribution;
  - Specialist queue length and wait-time by branch;
  - Fraud-desk referral counts and turnaround times;
  - Case-level complexity and routing source;
  - Staffing by role and overtime hours;
  - Survey response counts and demographics.
- **Spillover monitoring**: Track arrivals and cycle-time trends in untreated branches adjacent to treated ones; monitor fraud-desk volume by branch.
- **Guardrails**: Halt expansion if any branch’s p90 cycle time exceeds 120 hours or escalation_pct rises >5pp above pre-period, or if CSAT falls below 3.7 in two consecutive months.
- **Decision rule**: Proceed to full expansion if, after 6 months, the average effect on median cycle time is 0 and on p90 cycle time is +5 hours, *and* rework_pct falls by 1pp, *and* no guardrail violations occur.

## 10. Final claim ladder

- **Conditionally causal:** Automation increases `escalation_pct` by roughly 1–3 percentage points in adopter branches, conditional on staffing, arrivals, and case mix.
- **Conditionally causal:** Automation reduces `rework_pct` by about 1–3 percentage points in adopter branches, conditional on the same covariates.
- **Suggestive:** Higher intensity of automated-fast-path use is associated with lower median cycle time, but average branch-level effects on median and tail cycle times are not clearly beneficial across adopters.
- **Not supported:** There is no credible evidence that automation changes customer satisfaction (`csat_1to5`) in the supplied data.
