## 2. Rawversusnormalized interpretation

The gap between raw and mapped model fitness – from 0.920.55 raw vs. 0.920.90 mapped in March – is produced almost entirely by a label mismatch (`Eligibility Screen` instead of `Validate Eligibility`) and the insertion of duplicated `Open Case` events. The mapping recovers conformance because it restores the activity names the model expects and removes spurious events. This shows that the *business process* did not suddenly break in March; the alleged collapse is a measurement artefact.

However, a mapping that lifts conformance does **not** prove the underlying process is unchanged. The mapped fitness still declines from 0.92 to 0.86–0.88 after May, revealing real behavioural drift (increased rework, longer queues) that no label surgery can remove. Duplicated technical events inflate trace lengths and can distort variant discovery and frequencybased metrics; missing resources cripple resourcebased analyses (social networks, handover) but do not affect controlflow conformance if the model ignores resources. The postMay DFG and performance patterns – a doubling of the rework loop, a ~40h increase in p90 cycle time, and a drop in fast variants – cannot be explained by logging drift alone because the affected activities (`Assess`, `Request Info`, `Collect Documents`) kept their names, and the mapped fitness fell while the raw fitness remained stable. Therefore, the data contain a genuine behavioural shift superimposed on the March semantic drift.

| 4. **Rework** | Fraction of cases with `Request Info` loop, and average number of loops per case | JanFeb 2026 | Rework rate > 15 % and increase > 5 pp monthovermonth | Segment by supplier, complexity, and document source; check supplier quality | Process improvement initiatives that intentionally increase reviews | Quality manager |

| 7. **Service time** | Median service time for `Assess` and `Collect Documents` | JanFeb 2026 | Median > 6 h (from 4.8 h) or increase > 20 % | Review assessor workload, case complexity, tool performance | Change in case complexity, not pure service degradation | Process owner |
| 8. **Case mix** | Proportion of highcomplexity cases, supplierZ share, document source distribution | JanFeb 2026, with seasonal model | Highcomplexity share deviates > 1.5 from seasonal forecast | Review intake channels, marketing campaigns, external events | Seasonal model misspecification, classification drift | Business intelligence |
| 9. **Resource completeness** | Fraction of events with missing resource, by activity | JanFeb 2026 | Missingresource pct > 5 % for any migrated activity | Audit system logging, migration completeness; if intentional, update baseline | Temporary system outages, batch processing | Data engineering |
| 10. **Conformance** | Mapped model fitness (using current mapping) | JanFeb 2026 | Drop > 0.05 from baseline, or continuous decline over 3 months | Full alignment analysis, variant breakdown, rework loop quantification | Incorrect mapping update, model change | Process analyst |
| 11. **Outcomes** | Approval/rejection rate, reworktoapproval conversion | JanFeb 2026 | Rejection rate shifts > 5 pp, or reworktoapproval ratio changes | Correlate with case mix, supplier, assessor; may indicate quality drift | Seasonal changes in case eligibility, policy changes | Business owner |

**Handling seasonality and multiple testing:** Seasonality is addressed by modelling the historical pattern (e.g., October renewal surge) and comparing each month to its seasonal forecast rather than a flat baseline. For multiple testing (11 monitors), we avoid a single universal threshold; each monitor uses its own alert logic based on historical variability. To control falsepositive rate, we use a combination of adjustments: (a) require sustained deviation (e.g., 3 consecutive months) for slowchanging metrics, (b) use 2 or 3 limits on residuals from a seasonaltrend model, and (c) treat alerts as confirmatory signals that must be corroborated by at least two related monitors before triggering a diagnostic.

def prepare_event_log(df: pd.DataFrame, case_id_col='case_id', activity_col='activity',

def compute_monthly_dfg(log, year, month):
    """
    Filter log for a given month and discover DFG.
    Assumes log has a 'time:timestamp' column after formatting.
    """
    start = f"{year}-{month:02d}-01"
    # compute end date of month
    if month == 12:
        end = f"{year+1}-01-01"
    else:
        end = f"{year}-{month+1:02d}-01"
    monthly_log = slice_time_window(log, start, end)
    if len(monthly_log) == 0:

    dfg, start_act, end_act = pm4py.discover_dfg(monthly_log)

## 9. Provisional attribution

The **strongest conclusions** are the following:

- The March fitness collapse is a **logging/semantic artefact** caused by the system migration. The raw fitness drop is completely explained by label replacement (`Eligibility Screen`) and duplicate `Open Case` events; the mapped fitness remains near baseline. This is the cleanest separation in the data.
- The **May jump in rework and cycle time** is primarily driven by **Supplier Z’s disruption**. The simultaneous quadrupling of the supplierZ share, the doubling of the rework variant, and the DFG distance surge leave little room for alternative explanations. The subsequent partial recovery in September mirrors the supplier’s reported recovery.
- The **October–November surge** is a **seasonal population shift** (annual renewal season). The sharp rise in highcomplexity share and Senior Assessment usage, combined with the historical pattern, make seasonality the dominant cause, although residual rework and staffing constraints amplify the effect.

Conclusions that are **plausible but less certain**:

- The **staffing loss** (June 15) contributed to the growing queue waiting and cycle time from June onward, but it is confounded with the supplier disruption and the priority rule. The lack of a separate control group makes it impossible to isolate the staffing effect precisely.
- The **priority rule** (July 1) likely redistributed waiting time (benefitting highcomplexity cases, penalising standard cases), but the aggregate data cannot confirm this without caselevel priority flags. The rule’s effect is plausible given the rise in p90 while the median also rose, but it is not directly observable.

**Mediation and interaction** prevent clean attribution for the June–August deterioration. The supplier disruption, staffing cut, and priority rule all act simultaneously and interact: rework increases workload, staffing cuts reduce capacity, and the priority rule changes the queuing discipline. A significant portion of the cycletime increase is a joint product of these three mechanisms.

I would **revise the account** if:
- Eventlevel data showed that queue waiting increased disproportionately for standard cases after July, confirming the priority rule effect.
- Rework rates remained high in nonZ cases after September, indicating persistent behavioural adaptation rather than supplier recovery.
- Staffing levels were restored earlier than reported, which would shift the attribution toward the supplier and priority mechanisms.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. **IF** raw model fitness drops by more than 0.10 while mapped fitness remains stable, **THEN** immediately inspect the activity label set and event counts for new labels or duplicate technical events, **BECAUSE** a sudden divergence indicates a logging or semantic change rather than a real process deviation.

2. **IF** the rework variant percentage (cases with the `Assess`  `Request Info`  `Collect Documents` loop) increases by more than 5 percentage points monthovermonth, **THEN** segment by supplier and document source and initiate a supplier quality review, **BECAUSE** past disruptions (e.g., Supplier Z) caused precisely this pattern.

3. **IF** the median queue waiting time before `Assess` rises for three consecutive months and exceeds the 95th percentile of the seasonal baseline, **THEN** trigger a capacity review (staffing, overtime, prioritisation rules), **BECAUSE** persistent queue growth signals a structural imbalance between demand and capacity.

4. **IF** the highcomplexity case share in October exceeds the seasonal forecast by more than 1.5 standard deviations, **THEN** activate additional seniorassessor capacity and preemptive documentquality checks, **BECAUSE** the renewal season historically brings a surge of complex cases that strain the standard process.

5. **IF** missingresource percentage for migrated activities remains above 5 % for more than two months, **THEN** audit the system logging and migration completeness, and temporarily exclude those cases from resourcebased metrics, **BECAUSE** persistent missingness biases social network and handover analyses and may mask real resource shifts.

6. **IF** mapped model fitness declines by more than 0.05 from the baseline while rework and DFG distance increase, **THEN** conduct a full alignment analysis and variant drilldown to identify the deviating fragments, **BECAUSE** a drop in mapped fitness is a reliable indicator of behavioural drift that cannot be explained by logging.

7. **IF** the p90 cycle time increases by more than 30 % while the median service time for `Assess` changes by less than 10 %, **THEN** investigate queue management and resource allocation rather than processing efficiency, **BECAUSE** such a pattern indicates that delays are occurring in waiting states, not in the execution of tasks.
