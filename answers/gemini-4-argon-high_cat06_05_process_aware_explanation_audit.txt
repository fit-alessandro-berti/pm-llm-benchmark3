## 2. Process-model consistency checks

To prevent future leakage, ordering artifacts, and semantically invalid explanations from reaching production, the audit pipeline must execute a six-stage formal validation suite before training and before serving explanations.

### 2.1 Prefix-Time Availability Gate (Temporal Cut & Point-in-Time Metadata Check)
- **Rule**: For each case $c$, define the prediction anchor $(t_0(c), k_0(c))$ as the timestamp and monotonic ingestion sequence index of the first `Coverage Check Completed` event. Every event $e$ consumed by a feature or sequence extractor must satisfy $(t(e), \text{seq}(e)) \preceq (t_0(c), k_0(c))$. Every relational or feature-store attribute $x$ must carry an immutable `valid_from` timestamp satisfying $\text{valid\_from}(x) \le t_0(c)$ (and for periodic snapshots such as `queue_length_at_t0`, a strictly backward-looking window $t_0(c) - \Delta \le t_{\text{snap}} \le t_0(c)$).
- **Enforcement**: Any feature lacking a historical availability timestamp (`final_handler_team`, `risk_score_current`), using `extraction_date` (`prior_claim_count_before_t0`, `employee_experience`), or using end-of-day batch cuts (`same_day_document_count_snapshot` at 23:00) automatically fails schema validation.

### 2.2 Complete-Trace vs. Prefix Invariance Test (Counterfactual Future Perturbation)
- **Rule**: Column names often mask full-trace queries (e.g., `last_resource_role` or `prior_claim_count_before_t0`). For every trace $\sigma_c = \langle e_1, \dots, e_{k_0}, e_{k_0+1}, \dots, e_n \rangle$, construct the truncated prefix $\sigma_c^{\le k_0} = \langle e_1, \dots, e_{k_0} \rangle$ and synthetic future perturbations $\sigma_c' = \langle e_1, \dots, e_{k_0}, e'_{k_0+1}, \dots, e'_m \rangle$ where all post-$t_0$ events, timestamps, decisions, appeals, and closing teams are randomized or scrubbed.
- **Enforcement**: Require exact deterministic equality across all extractors:
  $$\Phi(\sigma_c, t_0) \equiv \Phi(\sigma_c^{\le k_0}, t_0) \equiv \Phi(\sigma_c', t_0)$$
  Any feature whose value changes when post-$t_0$ events are altered (`total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`, `last_resource_role`, and all five reported sequence patterns) is flagged as complete-trace leakage.

### 2.3 Same-Timestamp Ambiguity Detection and Partial-Order Conversion
- **Rule**: Inspect all event pairs $(e_i, e_j)$ within a case where $t(e_i) = t(e_j)$.
  1. If a reliable, source-generated monotonic sequence index $\text{seq}(e)$ is present, order tied events by $\text{seq}(e)$ and validate against normative transition constraints.
  2. If $\text{seq}(e)$ is missing or null (as in Case Z for `Claim Closed` and `Damage Assessment Completed`), forbid arbitrary tie-breaking (such as alphabetical sorting where `'Claim Closed' < 'Damage Assessment Completed'`). Instead, collapse same-timestamp events into a concurrent partial-order equivalence class $E_{t} = \{e_i, e_j\}$ and emit a `same_timestamp_ambiguity = True` data-quality flag.
- **Why this matters for DFG extractors**: Standard Directly-Follows Graph (DFG) builders coerce logs into a strict total order before counting edges $a \rightarrow b$. When timestamps tie at coarse granularity or during batch ingestion, deterministic sorting creates illusory edges like `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`. Representing tied events as partial orders blocks sequence explainers from firing on arbitrary sort order.

### 2.4 Activity-Order Feasibility (Alignments, Footprints, DFG, and Declarative Checks)
We encode the normative process as a sound workflow net $\mathcal{N}$ (and equivalent $\text{LTL}_f$/Declare constraints) and apply complementary conformance checks:
- **Why a frequent DFG edge is NOT a normative rule**: A DFG edge $a \rightarrow b$ merely counts how often $b$ immediately follows $a$ in total-ordered logs. High frequency does not imply normative validity:
  1. **Concurrency interleavings**: Because `Fraud Investigation` runs in parallel with evidence collection (`Documents Requested -> Documents Received -> Documents Validated`) and `Damage Assessment Completed`, a DFG will exhibit dense bidirectional edges between fraud activities and evidence/damage activities. Treating those frequent edges as sequential rules confuses parallel interleaving with causal precedence.
  2. **Systematic logging artifacts**: Batch updates or midnight timestamp truncation can make an illegal edge like `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` appear frequently enough to pass support thresholds and gain high SHAP weight (+0.21) if batch closures correlate with complex claims.
- **Role and limitations of formal checks**:
  - **Footprint Matrix Checks ($\rightarrow, \leftarrow, \|, \#$)**: Derived from the normative model $\mathcal{N}$, footprints distinguish strict causality ($\rightarrow$), concurrency ($\|$ between `Fraud Investigation` and evidence/assessment), and conflict/impossibility. A footprint check immediately flags `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` because `Claim Closed` is strictly preceded by `Settlement Decision Recorded` and cannot precede any activity. *Limitation*: Footprints are pairwise relations and cannot verify multi-step state history or data guards (such as whether a repeated `Settlement Decision Recorded` followed an `Appeal` on a `Partial`/`Deny` outcome).
  - **Declarative ($\text{LTL}_f$ / Declare) Checks**: Express exact temporal invariants, e.g., `Init(Claim Registered)`, `Precedence(Coverage Check Completed, Damage Assessment Completed)`, and terminal finality $\square(\text{Claim Closed} \rightarrow \bigcirc \neg \top)$ (no event may follow `Claim Closed`). *Limitation*: Evaluating declarative constraints independently does not directly yield a single global state marking at $t_0$ unless compiled into a deterministic finite automaton (DFA).
  - **Alignment-Based Conformance**: Aligning a trace against the normative Petri net computes the minimum-cost sequence of synchronous moves, model-only moves (skipped required steps), and log-only moves (unexpected/out-of-order steps). On Case Z, alignment proves that `Claim Closed` occurring before `Damage Assessment Completed` requires an illegal log move (or timestamp swap repair), confirming it is an ingestion defect rather than a valid behavioral pattern. *Limitation*: Alignments are computationally heavier and, if run on full traces, will leak future optimal alignments unless restricted to prefix markings at $t_0$.

### 2.5 Prefix-State Reachability Taxonomy at `t0` (Observed vs. Enabled vs. Future-Possible vs. Impossible)
By replaying the prefix $\sigma_c^{\le k_0}$ up to `Coverage Check Completed` (`t0`) on the normative Petri net/automaton to obtain the marking $M(t_0)$, every activity or transition referenced by an explanation is classified into one of four states at `t0`:
1. **Observed in Prefix ($\le t_0$)**: `Claim Registered`, `Coverage Check Completed`, and their direct transitions. *Status*: Admissible as observed facts in `t0` explanations.
2. **Enabled at `t0` (Immediately Reachable in One Step from $M(t_0)$)**: Activities whose input places hold tokens at $M(t_0)$ immediately after `Coverage Check Completed`:
   - `Documents Requested` (entering an evidence cycle),
   - `Damage Assessment Completed` (low-complexity fast track),
   - `Fraud Investigation Opened` (parallel branch after trigger).
   *Status*: At `t0`, these transitions are *enabled* to fire next, but **have not yet occurred**. An explanation at `t0` may state that prefix features (e.g., `initial_damage_complexity = High`) increase the likelihood of entering the enabled evidence-cycle branch, but it **cannot** use the actual firing of `Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened` or `Documents Requested` as an observed feature at `t0`.
3. **Merely Future-Possible at `t0` (Reachable Only After Intermediate Post-`t0` Steps)**: Activities not yet enabled at $M(t_0)$ because prerequisite post-`t0` activities must complete first:
   - `Documents Received`, `Documents Validated` (require prior `Documents Requested`),
   - `Settlement Decision Recorded` (requires `Damage Assessment Completed`),
   - `Appeal` (requires `Settlement Decision Recorded` with `Partial` or `Deny`),
   - `Claim Closed` (requires final decision or withdrawal),
   - Transitions `Documents Requested -> Documents Received`, `Damage Assessment Completed -> Settlement Decision Recorded`, and `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded`.
   *Status*: Strictly inadmissible as observed features at `t0`.
4. **Process-Impossible (Unreachable from Any Valid Marking)**:
   - `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` (violates terminal finality of `Claim Closed` and backward ordering),
   - `Appeal` following `Settlement Decision Recorded = Approve` or `Withdrawn`.
   *Status*: Inadmissible at any prediction time $t$; must trigger a conformance error and data quarantine.

### 2.6 Guarded State Validation for Repeated `Settlement Decision Recorded` Events
Because `Settlement Decision Recorded` is a coarse activity label that can legitimately occur twice when an appeal triggers reconsideration, the validation suite uses a **data-aware guarded automaton check** rather than a naive duplicate-activity filter:
- Let $d_1, d_2, \dots, d_m$ denote the occurrences of `Settlement Decision Recorded` in a trace.
- **First occurrence ($d_1$)**: Valid only after `Damage Assessment Completed`.
- **Repeated occurrence ($d_k, k \ge 2$)**: Semantically legitimate **if and only if**:
  1. The immediately preceding decision carried payload $\text{outcome}(d_{k-1}) \in \{\texttt{Partial}, \texttt{Deny}\}$ (if $\text{outcome}(d_{k-1}) \in \{\texttt{Approve}, \texttt{Withdrawn}\}$, any subsequent `Appeal` or second decision fails validation);
  2. An `Appeal` event $a_{k-1}$ occurred strictly between $d_{k-1}$ and $d_k$: $(t(d_{k-1}), \text{seq}(d_{k-1})) \prec (t(a_{k-1}), \text{seq}(a_{k-1})) \prec (t(d_k), \text{seq}(d_k))$.
- **Audit implication**: Unlike `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` (which is process-impossible), the sequence `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` passes normative conformance as a legitimate post-appeal reconsideration loop. However, it still fails the **Prefix-Time Availability Gate** at `t0` because both $d_1$, $a_1$, and $d_2$ occur strictly after `Coverage Check Completed`.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

### 3.1 Four Distinct Standards for Auditing Features and Explanations
Rather than indiscriminately deleting every correlated or sensitive variable, the audit separates four evaluation standards:
1. **Predictive Validity (at `t0`)**: Requires strict point-in-time availability ($\le t_0$), immutable feature lineage, and out-of-time statistical generalization. A feature can be predictively valid without being causal.
2. **Causal Interpretation**: Requires a credible structural mechanism (DAG / counterfactual invariance under intervention $do(X=x)$) unconfounded by unobserved common causes. SHAP values measure *marginal or conditional model attribution*, never causal effects.
3. **Operational Actionability**: Requires that an organization can intervene on the feature or on a downstream process mechanism at `t0` (e.g., dynamically adding adjuster capacity when `queue_length_at_t0` is high, or pre-requesting structured documentation for complex claims) in a way that causally alters remaining cycle time.
4. **Fairness and Robustness Review**: Requires evaluating whether a temporally valid feature acts as a proxy for protected or socioeconomic attributes (`claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`) or non-stationary regime shifts (`calendar_week`), ensuring predictions do not induce disparate impact or collapse under policy/network drift.

### 3.2 Classification of Surviving and Repairable Signals

*(Note: Pure post-`t0` outcome variables with no non-trivial `t0` state—`total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_decision`, and post-`t0` sequence patterns—are permanently discarded from the `t0` model.)*

#### 1. Defensible predictive signal
- **Features**:
  - `initial_damage_complexity` (frozen before `t0`)
  - `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` (derived strictly from $t_0 - t_{\text{reg}}$)
  - `initial_reserve` (verified frozen at intake/coverage check $\le t_0$)
  - Repaired prefix control-flow/resource features: `prefix_variant_id_at_t0` (repaired from `full_trace_variant_id`) and `resource_role_at_t0` / `assigned_handler_team_at_t0` (extracted directly from the `Coverage Check Completed` event).
- **What MAY be claimed**:
  - Claims entering `t0` with high initial damage complexity or high initial financial reserves have a substantially higher baseline probability of taking the multi-cycle evidence and specialist damage-assessment path rather than the fast track.
  - Claims with longer `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` have already consumed more of their fixed 10-day SLA budget prior to coverage completion, leaving less slack for downstream steps.
- **What MAY NOT be claimed**:
  - That artificially lowering the recorded complexity label or initial reserve estimate in the IT system will causally speed up settlement (intervening on the score/reserve without changing physical claim complexity does not remove required assessment work).
  - That these features deterministically guarantee a breach regardless of downstream staffing and execution.

#### 2. Process-state or congestion signal
- **Features**:
  - `queue_length_at_t0` (with timestamp join restricted to $t_0 - 5\text{ min} \le t_{\text{snap}} \le t_0$).
- **What MAY be claimed**:
  - Elevated queue length at `t0` is a valid operational state indicator showing that workloads waiting for post-coverage processing are congested, increasing expected queuing delay before `Documents Requested` or `Damage Assessment Completed` can begin.
  - It supports operational capacity interventions (e.g., triggering overflow staffing, cross-team load balancing, or fast-track triage when `queue_length_at_t0` exceeds a threshold).
- **What MAY NOT be claimed**:
  - That the raw SHAP coefficient of `queue_length_at_t0` equals the exact causal effect of queue size on duration, because queue surges may co-occur with external demand shocks (e.g., regional storms) that simultaneously alter claim complexity and repair-shop bottlenecks.
  - That prioritizing a single claim out of a congested queue reduces aggregate breaches without delaying other queued claims (zero-sum queueing spillover).

#### 3. Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review
- **Features**:
  - `claimant_region` (known at registration; correlated with repair-network coverage, language, channel, and socioeconomic characteristics)
  - `claimant_channel` (e.g., `Broker`; known at registration; correlated with commercial/intermediated handling and regional segments).
- **What MAY be claimed**:
  - `claimant_region` and `claimant_channel` are temporally valid at `t0` and statistically predictive of historical settlement duration because they correlate with structural operational factors (local repair-shop capacity, translation/document validation needs, and broker communication turnaround).
- **What MAY NOT be claimed**:
  - That a claimant's region or channel inherently "causes" delay, or that claims from Region R4 should be deprioritized or penalized.
  - Because `claimant_region` correlates with language and socioeconomic characteristics, using it to gate service levels without auditing disparate impact violates fairness standards. Operationally, teams should decompose `claimant_region` into actionable infrastructure covariates known at `t0` (e.g., `local_repair_network_backlog_at_t0`) so interventions target repair capacity rather than claimant demographics.

#### 4. Drift/calendar proxy
- **Features**:
  - `calendar_week` (known at `t0`, e.g., `calendar_week = 52`).
- **What MAY be claimed**:
  - In the historical training window, claims reaching `t0` during specific calendar weeks experienced higher breach rates due to the combined effect of holiday staffing reductions and a contemporaneous operating-policy change.
- **What MAY NOT be claimed**:
  - That integer `calendar_week` is a stable, recurring causal driver ("Week $k$ is operationally worse"). Because a one-off operating-policy change is confounded with the calendar index, a model relying on raw `calendar_week` will mispredict in subsequent years when the new operating policy applies across all weeks. It must be replaced by explicit regime and capacity variables (`policy_version_at_t0`, `holiday_capacity_ratio_at_t0`).

#### 5. Unusable until lineage is repaired
- **Features**:
  - `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (SQL `<= extraction_date` bug)
  - `same_day_document_count_snapshot` (23:00 nightly batch includes post-`t0` uploads)
  - `risk_score_current` (mutable without historical feature timestamp)
  - `final_handler_team` (warehouse closure field without historical availability timestamp)
  - `employee_experience` (joined as of `extraction_date` rather than `t0`)
  - `last_resource_role` and `num_document_requests_total` (unusable in their complete-trace form; usable only when recomputed strictly over prefix events $\le t_0$).
- **What MAY be claimed**:
  - **Before lineage repair**: Nothing. Their current values and SHAP contributions cannot be cited in any `t0` explanation because they blend pre-`t0` state with post-`t0` information.
  - **After lineage repair and model retraining**: Once reconstructed with strict point-in-time joins (`claim_created_at < t0 AND claim_id != current_claim_id`, `doc_uploaded_at <= t0`, immutable `risk_score_at_t0`, `assigned_handler_team_at_t0`, and `t0 - hire_date`), they may be interpreted as valid prefix predictors of baseline risk, documentation completeness at intake, and initial handler experience.
- **What MAY NOT be claimed**:
  - That current SHAP values (such as `-0.06` for `same_day_document_count_snapshot = 6` or `+0.15` for `risk_score_current = 74` in Case Z) reflect early warning signals at `t0`.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

The following prioritized engineering and governance plan remediates the audit findings end-to-end.

### Priority 1 (P0 — Immediate): Point-in-Time Feature Store & Historical Timestamp Lineage
1. **Bitemporal / Append-Only Feature-Store Architecture**:
   - Prohibit in-place `UPDATE` overwrites on analytical warehouse tables used for ML (`final_handler_team`, `risk_score_current`, `initial_reserve`).
   - Redesign the feature store around append-only state-transition logs keyed by `(entity_id, valid_from_ts, ingestion_ts)`. Every mutable attribute (assigned team, risk score, reserve amount, employee role/tenure) must be written as a new immutable versioned record when changed.
2. **Strict `AS OF` Point-in-Time Joins and Clock Hygiene**:
   - Enforce automated `AS OF` joins where a feature record $r$ is eligible for case $c$ at $t_0(c)$ if and only if $\text{valid\_from\_ts}(r) \le t_0(c)$ and $\text{ingestion\_ts}(r) \le t_0(c)$.
   - Fix the SQL defect in `prior_claim_count_before_t0` to `claim_created_at < t0 AND claim_id != current_claim_id`.
   - Replace the 23:00 nightly `same_day_document_count_snapshot` with event-level aggregation `COUNT(*) WHERE doc_uploaded_at <= t0`.
   - Enforce asymmetric backward tolerance on `queue_length_at_t0` ($t_0 - 5\text{ min} \le t_{\text{snap}} \le t_0$, never $> t_0$).
   - Require a mandatory, source-generated monotonic sequence index (`event_seq_id`) alongside millisecond timestamps in the ingestion pipeline to eliminate same-timestamp ordering ambiguity.

### Priority 2 (P1 — Pre-Retraining Gate): Prefix-Only Recomputation & Process-Conformance CI Suite
3. **Prefix-Projection Engine & Counterfactual Perturbation Tests**:
   - Architect the feature pipeline so that raw event logs are physically sliced to the prefix $\sigma_c^{\le k_0}$ (events up to and including the first `Coverage Check Completed`) *before* any feature, sequence, variant, or resource extractor executes.
   - Add the **Counterfactual Future-Truncation Invariance Test** (Section 2.2) to CI/CD: any candidate feature whose value changes when post-$t_0$ events or warehouse closure fields are mutated blocks deployment.
4. **Automated Process-Conformance & Explainability Gate**:
   - Integrate the normative workflow automaton and guarded transition rules into the data pipeline:
     - Flag and quarantine same-timestamp ties lacking `event_seq_id` (preventing artifacts like `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`).
     - Validate that every sequence or activity feature referenced by the model at checkpoint $t$ belongs to the **Observed in Prefix ($\le t$)** state set—automatically rejecting **Enabled** (next-step), **Future-Possible**, and **Process-Impossible** patterns.
     - For models built for later checkpoints (e.g., post-decision monitoring), enforce the guarded rule that repeated `Settlement Decision Recorded` events are valid only after `Settlement Decision Recorded in {Partial, Deny} -> Appeal`.

### Priority 3 (P2 — Model Rebuild): Retraining, Calibration After Feature Removal, & Explanation Stability
5. **Model Retraining and Post-Removal Probability Calibration**:
   - Do **not** simply zero out leaked SHAP values from the existing model; because `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_handler_team`, `num_document_requests_total`, and `full_trace_variant_id` dominated the model's splits and base values, the model must be retrained from scratch on the clean prefix feature space.
   - Evaluate the retrained model on strictly out-of-time validation cohorts (splitting by registration/coverage timestamp so no training trace closes after the validation split point).
   - Assess probability calibration via **Expected Calibration Error (ECE)**, **Brier Score**, and reliability diagrams across key operational strata (complexity tiers, queue congestion deciles, regions, and policy regimes). Apply isotonic or spline recalibration if removing leaked features compresses predicted probability ranges.
6. **Explanation Stability and Attribution Rigor**:
   - Evaluate local and global explanation stability across bootstrap model ensembles and small prefix perturbations (e.g., $\pm 1$ queue length, slight elapsed-time shifts) using **rank-biased overlap (RBO)** and **Kendall's $\tau$** on top-k feature rankings.
   - Because prefix features exhibit structural collinearity (e.g., `initial_damage_complexity`, `initial_reserve`, `claimant_channel`, and `claimant_region`), use conditional/grouped SHAP or hierarchical coalition attributions so correlated proxies do not split or unstable-flip credit arbitrarily across trees.

### Priority 4 (P3 — Governance & Production Operations): Proxy/Fairness Auditing & Drift Monitoring
7. **Proxy Decomposition and Fairness / Disparate-Impact Review**:
   - Conduct formal fairness audits across `claimant_region` and `claimant_channel`: test for **calibration within groups** (whether a predicted 0.70 breach risk corresponds to a 70% empirical breach rate in every region) and audit false-positive/false-negative disparities.
   - Replace coarse regional/demographic proxies wherever possible with direct operational mechanism covariates known at `t0` (e.g., regional repair-shop queue backlog, language-specialist availability at `t0`).
   - Establish an operational governance boundary: high predicted breach risk may only trigger **supportive interventions** (e.g., assigning additional adjuster capacity, early document checklist guidance, or expedited assessment routing) and must never be used to penalize, deprioritize, or auto-deny claimants.
8. **Continuous Monitoring for Schema, Concept, and Process Drift**:
   - **Schema & Lineage Drift**: Monitor null rates, missing `event_seq_id` rates, and same-timestamp collision rates in real time; alert immediately if `Claim Closed` or other post-`t0` events appear with timestamps $\le t_0$.
   - **Process & Control-Flow Drift**: Track prefix footprint frequencies, intake-to-coverage elapsed time distributions, and downstream conformance fitness over rolling time windows to detect unannounced workflow changes.
   - **Calendar & Regime Drift**: Replace integer `calendar_week` with explicit `policy_regime_id` and `holiday_capacity_ratio_at_t0`, and monitor Population Stability Index (PSI) and SHAP attribution drift across weeks to ensure policy shifts or seasonal surges trigger model recalibration rather than spurious calendar explanations.
