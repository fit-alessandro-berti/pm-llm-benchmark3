# 1. Executive Verdict

The given Petri net **is a valid workflow net (WF-net)**: it has a unique source (`source`), a unique sink (`sink`), and every place and transition lies on at least one directed path from `source` to `sink`.

However, it is **unsound** and fails the business intent. The principal defect is an incomplete synchronization on the waiver branch: `t_join_waived` consumes only `p_waived` and lacks an input arc from `p_analysis_done`, even though `t_split` always spawns a parallel token in `p_analysis`.

The business consequence is twofold: (1) a waived case can fire `t_authorize` **before** mandatory analysis (`t_analyze`) occurs, violating the compliance rule; and (2) even if `t_analyze` eventually completes, its token in `p_analysis_done` can never be consumed on the waiver path, causing improper completion and deadlock outside `fm`.

Consider the legal firing sequence:

$$\sigma_1 = \langle \texttt{t\_register},\; \texttt{t\_split},\; \texttt{t\_waive\_review},\; \texttt{t\_join\_waived},\; \texttt{t\_authorize},\; \texttt{t\_analyze} \rangle$$

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | — (initial `im`) | `{source: 1}` | `{t_register}` | Case starts at initial marking `im`. |
| 1 | `t_register` | `{p_ready: 1}` | `{t_split}` | Case registered; ready to fork parallel branches. |
| 2 | `t_split` | `{p_analysis: 1, p_review: 1}` | `{t_analyze, t_standard_review, t_waive_review}` | AND-split creates one token in each parallel branch (`p_analysis` and `p_review`). |
| 3 | `t_waive_review` | `{p_analysis: 1, p_waived: 1}` | `{t_analyze, t_join_waived}` | Waiver route selected; `p_review` is consumed, so `t_standard_review` can never fire. Crucially, `t_join_waived` is already enabled even though `t_analyze` has not run. |
| 4 | `t_join_waived` | `{p_analysis: 1, p_decision: 1}` | `{t_analyze, t_authorize}` | `t_join_waived` moves the waiver token into `p_decision` without synchronizing `p_analysis`. `t_authorize` is now enabled before analysis. |

| 6 | `t_analyze` | `{p_analysis_done: 1, sink: 1}` | `` | `t_analyze` fires **after** `t_authorize`, yielding a dead marking (`M  fm`) with a token in `sink` and a leftover token stranded in `p_analysis_done`. |

**Why the leftover token in `p_analysis_done` cannot be consumed:**
The only outgoing arc from `p_analysis_done` enters `t_join_normal`, whose preset is `•t_join_normal = {p_analysis_done, p_review_done}`. Enabling `t_join_normal` therefore requires a token in `p_review_done`. The sole producer of `p_review_done` is `t_standard_review`, which requires `p_review`. Because the net is acyclic and the single token in `p_review` was already consumed by `t_waive_review` at Step 3 (with `source` and `p_ready` permanently empty), `p_review_done` can never be marked. Consequently, `t_join_normal` is permanently disabled, `p_analysis_done: 1` is permanently stranded, and `fm = {sink: 1}` is unreachable from Step 3 onward.

| 4 | `t_waive_review` | `{p_analysis_done: 1, p_waived: 1}` | `{t_join_waived}` | Waiver recorded; `p_review_done` is now permanently unreachable. |
| 5 | `t_join_waived` | `{p_analysis_done: 1, p_decision: 1}` | `{t_authorize}` | `t_join_waived` consumes `p_waived` but leaves `p_analysis_done: 1` behind. |
| 6 | `t_authorize` | `{p_analysis_done: 1, sink: 1}` | `` | **Improper completion & deadlock:** even though `t_analyze` preceded `t_authorize`, a token exists in `sink` alongside `p_analysis_done: 1`, so `fm = {sink: 1}` is never reached. |

Together, $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$ establish that the defect is not merely an ordering race: once `t_waive_review` fires, the net violates both **proper completion** and the **option to complete** on every waiver execution.

# 4. Minimal-Repair Alternatives

We define an explicit unit **graph edit-cost model**:
$$\text{Cost} = |\Delta P| + |\Delta T| + |\Delta F|$$
where each added or removed place ($P$), transition ($T$), or directed arc ($F$) has cost $1$. Renaming existing elements is excluded from structural edits.

| Repair | Exact edits | Edit cost | Preserved intent | Changed behavior | New risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **R1: Two-input waiver synchronization** | Add 1 arc: `(p_analysis_done, t_join_waived)`. | **1** | Fully preserves business intent: parallel mandatory analysis with either standard review or waiver, synchronization before authorization, clean termination at `fm`. | `t_join_waived` becomes a true 2-input AND-join (`•t_join_waived = {p_analysis_done, p_waived}`). Blocks premature `t_authorize` and drains `p_analysis_done` on the waiver path. Preserves separate `p_waived` and `t_join_waived`. | Introduces a **non-free-choice** (asymmetric) choice at `p_analysis_done` (shared between `t_join_normal` and `t_join_waived`), which can hinder BPMN/process-tree translation and leaves two silent join transitions in replay diagnostics. |
| **R2: Unified free-choice synchronization** | Remove place `p_waived` (1), transition `t_join_waived` (1), arcs `(t_waive_review, p_waived)`, `(p_waived, t_join_waived)`, `(t_join_waived, p_decision)` (3); add arc `(t_waive_review, p_review_done)` (1). | **6** | Fully preserves business intent and the exact same visible trace language as R1. | Collapses the review XOR-split into a clean XOR-join at `p_review_done` inside a single AND-join (`t_join_normal`). Produces a **strictly free-choice**, block-structured net with fewer markings and one fewer silent transition. | Eliminates `p_waived`, so the marking at `p_review_done` no longer distinguishes whether a waiver or standard review occurred (distinction survives only in the transition/event history). Breaking change if external tooling references `p_waived` or `t_join_waived`. |
| **R3: Policy-changing fast-track waiver** | Remove arc `(p_review, t_waive_review)` (1); add arc `(p_ready, t_waive_review)` (1). | **2** | **Explicit policy change:** preserves sound completion and mandatory parallel analysis + standard review on non-waived cases, but makes analysis **optional/bypassed** under waiver. | Moves waiver decision before `t_split`. Waived cases follow `t_register -> t_waive_review -> t_join_waived -> t_authorize`, bypassing both `t_analyze` and `t_standard_review`. Strictly free-choice and sound, but changes the observable language (forbids running analysis concurrently with waiver). | Violates the stated mandatory-analysis rule unless stakeholders formally approve a fast-track policy; creates a compliance bypass if waiver recording is not tightly governed. |

### Minimality Justification

1. **Global minimality of R1 (cost 1) among intent-preserving repairs:**
   Cost $0$ is unsound. Removing any single place or transition leaves dangling arcs (invalid without removing incident arcs, cost $\ge 2$). Removing any single arc breaks WF-net connectedness or leaves an empty preset (`t_waive_review` would become unbounded). Adding a single place or transition alone disconnects it. Among single-arc additions:
   - Adding `(p_analysis, t_join_waived)` fails soundness because if `t_analyze` fires before `t_join_waived`, `{p_analysis_done: 1, p_waived: 1}` deadlocks.
   - Adding `(p_analysis_done, t_authorize)` breaks the normal path because `t_join_normal` already consumes `p_analysis_done`.
   - Adding `(p_analysis_done, t_waive_review)` **ties R1 at cost 1** for achieving a sound WF-net, because `t_waive_review` would consume both `p_analysis_done` and `p_review`. However, it **violates the business intent** by serializing `t_waive_review` after `t_analyze` (destroying parallelism on the waiver route).
   - Therefore, **R1 is the unique cost-1 repair** that achieves soundness while preserving full business intent.

2. **Minimality of R3 (cost 2) among policy-changing waiver-bypasses-analysis repairs:**
   Allowing a waived case to reach `fm` without firing `t_analyze` requires either (a) deciding the waiver before `t_split` spawns `p_analysis`—requiring relocating `t_waive_review`'s input arc from `p_review` to `p_ready` (1 deletion + 1 addition = cost **2**)—or (b) keeping concurrency and draining *both* `p_analysis` (unstarted) and `p_analysis_done` (already completed), which requires an extra cancellation join transition and 4 arcs (cost **5**). Thus R3 is minimal for its policy.

3. **Note on the cost-2 free-choice tie vs. R2 (cost 6):**
   One can obtain a strictly free-choice, intent-preserving net at cost **2** (tying R3) by removing `(t_join_waived, p_decision)` and adding `(t_join_waived, p_review_done)`. However, that leaves `t_join_waived` as a misleadingly named, redundant silent pass-through transition. **R2 (cost 6)** is the minimal edit that yields a strictly free-choice, block-structured, redundancy-free net preserving the full business intent.

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import Marking


def format_marking(marking):
    items = sorted((p.name, count) for p, count in marking.items() if count > 0)
    return "{" + ", ".join(f"{name}: {count}" for name, count in items) + "}"


def fire_named_transition(pnet, marking, transition_name):
    enabled = pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(pnet, marking)
    matches = [t for t in enabled if t.name == transition_name]
    if not matches:
        all_names = {t.name for t in pnet.transitions}
        enabled_names = sorted(t.name for t in enabled)
        if transition_name not in all_names:
            raise ValueError(f"Transition '{transition_name}' does not exist in net '{pnet.name}'.")
        raise ValueError(
            f"Transition '{transition_name}' is NOT enabled at {format_marking(marking)}. "
            f"Enabled: {enabled_names}"
        )
    t = matches[0]
    next_marking = Marking(marking)
    for arc in t.in_arcs:
        next_marking[arc.source] -= arc.weight
        if next_marking[arc.source] == 0:
            del next_marking[arc.source]
    for arc in t.out_arcs:
        next_marking[arc.target] = next_marking.get(arc.target, 0) + arc.weight
    return next_marking


def replay_sequence(title, pnet, start_marking, target_fm, sequence):
    print(f"\n--- {title} ({pnet.name}) ---")
    current = Marking(start_marking)
    enabled_names = sorted(t.name for t in pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(pnet, current))
    print(f"Step 0 | start -> {format_marking(current)} | enabled={enabled_names}")
    for idx, t_name in enumerate(sequence, start=1):
        try:
            current = fire_named_transition(pnet, current, t_name)
        except ValueError as err:
            print(f"Step {idx} | BLOCKED firing '{t_name}': {err}")
            return current
        enabled_names = sorted(t.name for t in pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(pnet, current))
        has_sink = any(p.name == "sink" and c > 0 for p, c in current.items())
        exact_fm = (current == target_fm)
        print(
            f"Step {idx} | fired '{t_name}' -> {format_marking(current)} | "
            f"enabled={enabled_names} | sink_marked={has_sink} | exact_fm={exact_fm}"
        )
    return current


def inspect_net(label, pnet, init_m, final_m):
    print(f"\n================ {label}: {pnet.name} ================")
    is_wf = pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(pnet)
    print("Is workflow net:", is_wf)

    soundness_result = pm4py.check_soundness(pnet, init_m, final_m, print_diagnostics=True)
    is_sound = soundness_result[0] if isinstance(soundness_result, tuple) else soundness_result
    print("Is sound:", is_sound)

    init_enabled = sorted(t.name for t in pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(pnet, init_m))
    print("Enabled at initial marking:", init_enabled)

    rg = pm4py.convert_to_reachability_graph(pnet, init_m, final_m)
    dead_states = [s for s in rg.states if len(s.outgoing) == 0]
    print(f"Reachability graph built: {len(rg.states)} states, {len(rg.transitions)} edges")
    print(f"Dead states ({len(dead_states)}):", sorted(s.name for s in dead_states))


# 1. Structural, soundness, and reachability diagnostics on both nets
inspect_net("ORIGINAL", net, im, fm)
inspect_net("REPAIRED (R1)", repaired_net, repaired_im, repaired_fm)
```

### Expected Qualitative Differences

- **Workflow-net check (`check_is_workflow_net`):** Returns `True` for **both** the original and repaired nets, confirming the defect is behavioral rather than a violation of WF-net graph connectivity.
- **Soundness check (`check_soundness`):** Reports **unsound** (`False`) on the original net—flagging deadlocks and/or improper completion (leftover tokens when `sink` is marked, inability to reach `fm` after `t_waive_review`)—and **sound** (`True`) on `repaired_net`.
- **Counterexample replay (`get_enabled_transitions` + `fire_named_transition`):** On `net`, `t_join_waived` and `t_authorize` fire prematurely before `t_analyze`, reaching `{p_analysis: 1, sink: 1}` (`sink_marked=True`, `exact_fm=False`) and then deadlocking in `{p_analysis_done: 1, sink: 1}`. On `repaired_net`, Step 4 raises a blocked-transition error because `t_join_waived` is **not enabled** at `{p_analysis: 1, p_waived: 1}` (only `t_analyze` is enabled). Firing `t_analyze` first enables `t_join_waived` and finishes cleanly with `exact_fm=True`.
- **Reachability graph (`convert_to_reachability_graph`):** The original net's reachability graph contains multiple dead states (both `fm` and the stranded `{p_analysis_done: 1, sink: 1}` deadlock) as well as premature-completion states with `sink` marked alongside `p_analysis`. The repaired net's reachability graph is strictly smaller (eliminating the unsafe `{p_analysis, p_decision}`, `{p_analysis, sink}`, `{p_analysis_done, p_decision}`, and `{p_analysis_done, sink}` states) and has a **single** dead state corresponding exactly to `repaired_fm = {sink: 1}`.

# 7. Repair Trade-Offs and Residual Risks

1. **Distinguishability of waiver vs. standard review:**
   Under **R1**, waiver and standard review remain distinguishable at **both** the trace/log level (`Record Review Waiver` vs. `Perform Standard Review`) and the intermediate marking level (`p_waived` vs. `p_review_done`) up until synchronization into `p_decision`. By contrast, **R2** preserves trace-level distinguishability but collapses intermediate state markings into a single `p_review_done` place.

2. **Silent transitions and duplicate behavior:**
   R1 retains three silent (`None`) transitions: `t_split`, `t_join_normal`, and `t_join_waived`. Crucially, keeping both `t_join_normal` and `t_join_waived` does **not** introduce nondeterministic choice or duplicate trace behavior: because `p_review_done` and `p_waived` are mutually exclusive in every reachable marking, `t_join_normal` and `t_join_waived` are never simultaneously enabled. However, R1 introduces two minor residual trade-offs: (a) an extra silent transition can add visual/diagnostic noise in alignment or token-replay visualizations, and (b) sharing `p_analysis_done` between two joins makes the net **non-free-choice**, which can complicate automated translation to block-structured BPMN or process trees.

3. **Why soundness alone does not prove business-rule alignment:**
   Soundness guarantees only generic control-flow hygiene (boundedness, option to complete, proper completion, no dead transitions)—**not** domain semantics. Two concrete proofs appear in Section 4:
   - **R3** (cost 2) is a strictly free-choice, **sound** WF-net, yet it violates the mandatory-analysis policy by letting waivers bypass `t_analyze`.
   - Adding `(p_analysis_done, t_waive_review)` (cost 1) is also a **sound** WF-net, yet it violates the parallelism requirement by blocking `Record Review Waiver` until after `Perform Analysis`.
   Therefore, passing `pm4py.check_soundness` must always be paired with trace-level or temporal-logic verification of business rules.

4. **Regression-test traces:**
   - **Positive traces (must be accepted and reach `repaired_fm`):**
     1. `["Register Case", "Perform Analysis", "Perform Standard Review", "Authorize Case"]`
     2. `["Register Case", "Perform Standard Review", "Perform Analysis", "Authorize Case"]`
     3. `["Register Case", "Perform Analysis", "Record Review Waiver", "Authorize Case"]`
     4. `["Register Case", "Record Review Waiver", "Perform Analysis", "Authorize Case"]`
   - **Negative traces (must be rejected / unable to reach `repaired_fm`):**
     1. `["Register Case", "Record Review Waiver", "Authorize Case"]` (omitted analysis)
     2. `["Register Case", "Record Review Waiver", "Authorize Case", "Perform Analysis"]` (premature authorization before analysis)
     3. `["Register Case", "Perform Standard Review", "Authorize Case"]` (omitted analysis on standard path)
     4. `["Register Case", "Perform Analysis", "Authorize Case"]` (omitted review/waiver)
     5. `["Register Case", "Perform Analysis", "Perform Standard Review", "Record Review Waiver", "Authorize Case"]` (both review outcomes executed)

5. **Detecting accidental bypass of mandatory analysis:**
   - **Static model verification:** Traverse the reachability graph (or verify LTL/DECLARE precedence) to assert that *no* path from `im` reaches a marking where `t_authorize` is enabled (or `sink` is marked) without firing `t_analyze`.
   - **Conformance checking in CI/production:** Run PM4Py alignment-based conformance checking of event logs against the normative repaired net; any model-move on `Perform Analysis` paired with a synchronous move on `Authorize Case` flags a bypass.
   - **Runtime audit guardrail:** Enforce an application/database precondition (and automated audit query) verifying that every `Authorize Case` event shares a case ID with a completed `Perform Analysis` event having an earlier timestamp.

# 8. Final Recommendation

**Deploy R1 (two-input waiver synchronization)** and retain **R2 (unified free-choice synchronization)** as the fallback.

**R1** is the strongest immediate deployment choice because it is the unique cost-1 edit that restores classical soundness while preserving 100% of the business intent—mandatory parallel analysis, concurrent waiver recording, and proper synchronization before authorization. Because R1 adds only a single arc `(p_analysis_done, t_join_waived)` without deleting or renaming any existing places or transitions, it minimizes regression risk and preserves full backward compatibility with any monitoring dashboards, token-state queries, or audit hooks that inspect `p_waived` or `t_join_waived`. Moreover, keeping `p_waived` distinct from `p_review_done` retains marking-level observability of the waiver path right up to authorization readiness without introducing nondeterminism.

**R2** is the right fallback if the process must be exported to BPMN, converted to a process tree, or analyzed by tooling that requires **strictly free-choice, block-structured** nets. R2 yields the exact same observable trace language as R1 while eliminating the non-free-choice sharing at `p_analysis_done` and removing one redundant silent transition (`t_join_waived`), at the acceptable cost of a larger structural diff (cost 6) and relying on event history rather than marking places to distinguish waiver from standard review. **R3** should be rejected unless business leadership formally amends the compliance policy to make analysis optional under waiver.
