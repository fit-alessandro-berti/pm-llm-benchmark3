## 6. Control Improvements

- **Delegation creation:** Require an approved, signed delegation record before the delegation interval begins. Do not allow email-only delegation, especially for IC or QM authority. Store the delegation in the controlled QMS before first use.
- **Effective-time recording:** Record delegation effective times in UTC with explicit start and end timestamps. Do not rely on database migration times; use the pre-event signed instrument as the authoritative timestamp.
- **Principal availability:** Integrate absence/leave calendars with the QMS so that a delegation cannot be used while the principal is actually available, unless a specific exception is documented. If the principal works during leave, require an override reason and individual authentication.
- **Alias management:** Maintain a controlled alias directory so `b.koch` and `u_bk` resolve to the same human before SoD checks. Prohibit role accounts that hide individual identity.
- **Individual authentication:** Replace shared workstation sessions and service-account logins with individual re-authentication using FIDO tokens or equivalent. Require session-level attribution for every QMS action, especially `svc_qms`.
- **Emergency review:** Enforce RR4 with automated business-day calculations and QM-delegation scope checks. Do not allow a QM delegate whose delegation is for production equipment to perform a cooling-system retrospective review.
- **Pair rotation:** Add a documented rotation policy for draft/approve and implement/verify pairs in the same production cell. Even without collusion, high repeated-pair rates create organizational risk.
- **Network monitoring:** Monitor handover and working-together networks for unusually concentrated pairs. Use the network metrics as triggers for sampling, not as proof of misconduct.
- **Evidential retention:** Retain CCTV, badge logs, FIDO logs, VPN metadata, workstation user-switch logs, and signed delegation PDFs for at least the required QA record period. Missing evidence should itself be treated as a data-quality finding in investigations.
