## 1. Feasibility model

### Partial-order relation set
```
Register -> Triage 
Triage -> Identity Check && Document Check
Identity Check && Document Check -> Assessment && (Originals Receipt | Compliance Review)
Assessment -> Request More Info && Decision
Compliance Review -> Decision
```

### Conditional and declarative constraints
- `Register` must be first, followed by `Triage`.
- `Identity Check` and `Document Check` both must complete before `Assessment`.
- When originals are required, `Originals Receipt` must precede `Document Check`; `Identity Check` may still proceed earlier.
- A high-risk or high-value case requires `Compliance Review` after the latest relevant `Assessment` and before `Decision`.
- `Assessment` or `Compliance Review` may issue `Request More Info`.
- After `Request More Info`, a new `Document Check` and a new `Assessment` are required before a decision; high-risk/high-value cases then need a new or explicitly reaffirmed compliance review.
- `Decision` must precede `Notify`.
- Four-eyes rule: the same person may not perform both the final `Assessment` and final `Compliance Review`.
- A preliminary compliance screen may occur earlier, but it does not replace the final compliance review.
- Resources cannot perform two service intervals at the same time.
- Queue estimates are forecasts, not guarantees.

### Intervention categorization
- **Timing alteration**: Interventions that adjust the start or duration of activities (e.g., early escalation, alternative document resource).
- **Routing alteration**: Interventions that change the sequence of activities (e.g., parallelizing verification, suppressing rework loops).
- **Policy alteration**: Interventions that change the decision-making process (e.g., suppressing a second rework loop, prohibiting further requests for more information).

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### ### K-104
**Factual mechanism**: The process follows the original sequence with high-risk/high-value case requiring a compliance review after assessment, which led to a long compliance review.

**Minimal intervention**: Reserve a provisional slot for compliance review at the triage stage and assign the document check to a faster resource (D2).

**Temporal propagation**: The reservation of the compliance slot at triage allows earlier completion of the document check, reducing the overall cycle time. The provisional compliance review may delay other cases if it is not completed quickly.

**Process-feasibility check**: The provisional compliance review is feasible as it is a preparation step before the final compliance review. The use of D2 for document checks is feasible but may lead to rework if the documents are complex.

**Best/base/worst case**:
- **Best case**: Document check completed quickly, provisional compliance review completed quickly, total cycle time reduced to ~16 hours.
- **Base case**: As specified in factual execution.
- **Worst case**: Document check takes longer due to complex documents, provisional compliance review delayed, total cycle time increased to ~20 hours.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion**: If the provisional compliance review is completed quickly and does not delay other cases, the adverse side effects are minimized.

### ### K-219
**Factual mechanism**: The process follows the original sequence with originals required, which led to a long document check and rework due to inconsistencies in supplier responses.

**Minimal intervention**: Assign the document check to a slower resource (D2) instead of waiting for the faster resource (D1).

**Temporal propagation**: Assigning D2 for document check allows it to start immediately after originals receipt, reducing the overall cycle time. However, D2's slower pace may require clarification, leading to rework.

**Process-feasibility check**: Assigning D2 is feasible but may lead to rework if there are inconsistencies in supplier responses. The intervention does not alter the compliance review process.

**Best/base/worst case**:
- **Best case**: D2 completes document check quickly, minimal rework, total cycle time reduced to ~16 hours.
- **Base case**: As specified in factual execution.
- **Worst case**: D2 takes longer due to inconsistencies, rework required, total cycle time increased to ~20 hours.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion**: If D2 completes document check quickly and with minimal rework, the adverse side effects are minimized.

### ### K-337
**Factual mechanism**: The process follows the original sequence with a high-risk/high-value case, requiring a compliance review after assessment, leading to a second rework loop after external supplier response.

**Minimal intervention**: After Assessment 2, prohibit the second `Request More Info` loop and require a decision using the evidence already available.

**Temporal propagation**: Prohibiting the second `Request More Info` loop reduces the overall cycle time by eliminating the need for a second assessment and compliance review. The decision may be made with conditions or rejection based on the existing evidence.

**Process-feasibility check**: The intervention is feasible as it adheres to the four-eyes rule and does not alter the final compliance review process. However, it changes the decision policy by not allowing a second rework loop.

**Best/base/worst case**:
- **Best case**: Decision made quickly with existing evidence, total cycle time reduced to ~18 hours.
- **Base case**: As specified in factual execution.
- **Worst case**: Decision may be rejected or with conditions, leading to potential appeal risk and loss exposure, total cycle time increased to ~22 hours.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion**: If the evidence available after Assessment 2 is sufficient to make a decision, the adverse side effects are minimized.

### ### K-411
**Factual mechanism**: The process follows the original sequence with a case lock preventing the document task from being owned by the same resource, leading to a longer document check.

**Minimal intervention**: Create both verification tasks and use a controlled workaround to allow I2 and D2 to work concurrently, requiring real-time timestamps rather than later batch entry.

**Temporal propagation**: Creating both verification tasks and using a controlled workaround allows both tasks to be completed concurrently, reducing the overall cycle time. However, earlier completion of both checks may push the case into an already congested assessment queue.

**Process-feasibility check**: The intervention is feasible as it adheres to the four-eyes rule and allows concurrent verification tasks. However, it may increase the load on the assessment queue if not managed properly.

**Best/base/worst case**:
- **Best case**: Both verification tasks completed quickly, assessment completed quickly, total cycle time reduced to ~14 hours.
- **Base case**: As specified in factual execution.
- **Worst case**: Assessment queue congested, total cycle time increased to ~20 hours.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion**: If the assessment queue is not congested and the intervention does not increase the load on the queue, the adverse side effects are minimized.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 | Early escalation packet and provisional C1 slot | None | Originals receipt queue may grow due to provisional compliance review | High-risk/high-value cases | Indirect effect on overall system load | Resource reservation may affect other high-risk/high-value cases |
| K-219 | Assign D2 for document check | None | None | High-value cases requiring originals | Indirect effect on assessment queue | D2's slower pace may affect other high-value cases requiring originals |
| K-337 | Prohibit second `Request More Info` loop | None | None | High-risk/high-value cases | Direct effect on decision policy | Changing decision policy may affect other high-risk/high-value cases |
| K-411 | Parallelize verification tasks for I2 and D2 | None | Assessment queue may grow due to earlier completion | All cases | Indirect effect on overall system load | Nonlinearity due to increased concurrency |

**100-case weekly rollout analysis**:
Reserving scarce compliance capacity (like in K-104) can prevent bottlenecks in the compliance review process, ensuring timely decisions for high-risk/high-value cases. Substituting a slower resource (like using D2 for document checks in K-219) may lead to rework but can improve overall efficiency if the substitution is feasible. Suppressing a second rework loop (as in K-337) can reduce cycle time but may compromise decision quality if the evidence is insufficient. Parallelizing verification tasks (as in K-411) can reduce cycle time but may increase congestion in the assessment queue, especially during peak periods or with external response delays.

## 5. Robustness comparison

| rank | intervention | robustness across plausible assumptions | expected benefit | principal risk | reversibility | information value of a pilot |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | K-411 | High robustness across assumption variations (parallel verification is generally feasible) | High (significant cycle time reduction) | Risk of assessment queue congestion | Low (requires real-time timestamps) | High (provides clear insights into concurrency impacts) |
| 2 | K-104 | Moderate robustness (depends on resource availability for C1) | Moderate (reduces compliance review delay) | Risk of delaying other high-risk cases | Moderate (depends on provisional compliance review) | Moderate (provides insights into compliance review efficiency) |
| 3 | K-219 | Low robustness (depends on document clarity and supplier response) | Moderate (reduces rework) | High risk of rework and decision quality issues | High (can be reversed by allowing rework loop) | Low (limited insights into document handling) |

**Ranking rationale**: Intervention K-411 is the most robust due to its general feasibility and significant cycle time reduction. K-104 is moderately robust with a moderate benefit and risk. K-219 is less robust due to its dependence on document clarity and supplier response. K-337 is the least robust due to its dependence on evidence sufficiency and high risk of decision quality issues.

## 6. Pilot designs

### Pilot designs for the two highest-ranked interventions

| pilot element | intervention 1 (K-411) | intervention 2 (K-104) |
|---|---|---|
| **Eligibility** | Cases requiring digital documents and where verification tasks can be parallelized | Cases requiring high-risk/high-value handling and compliance review |
| **Assignment rule** | Assign verification tasks (I2 and D2) to start concurrently with real-time timestamps | Reserve provisional compliance review slot and assign D2 for document check |
| **Sample/support logic** | Use historical data on document verification times and concurrency impacts | Use historical data on compliance review times and resource availability |
| **Primary outcome** | Reduction in overall cycle time | Reduction in compliance review delay |
| **Process-path outcome** | Both verification tasks completed quickly, assessment completed quickly | Provisional compliance review completed quickly, document check completed quickly |
| **Quality outcome** | Potential congestion in assessment queue | Potential delay in other high-risk cases |
| **Queue-spillover outcome** | Potential increase in assessment queue | Potential increase in originals receipt queue |
| **Logging validation** | Real-time timestamp logging | Provisional compliance review logging |
| **Stopping rule** | If assessment queue congestion observed, adjust resource allocation | If originals receipt queue grows significantly, reassess resource allocation |

## 7. Final synthesis

Among the four interventions, K-411 appears the most robust due to its general feasibility and significant potential for cycle time reduction. However, it comes with the risk of increasing congestion in the assessment queue if not managed properly. K-104 offers a moderate robustness with a moderate benefit and risk, primarily affecting compliance review efficiency. K-337 is less robust due to its dependence on evidence sufficiency and high risk of decision quality issues. K-219 is the least robust due to its dependence on document clarity and supplier response.

Despite potential speed gains, K-337 is the most dangerous intervention due to its high risk of compromising decision quality if insufficient evidence is available. The counterfactual approach of K-411 is least transportable to a system-wide policy due to its reliance on real-time timestamps and concurrency management, which may not be universally applicable.

Additional event or queue data that would be most valuable include real-time queue metrics, historical data on document verification times, and supplier response times to better assess the impact of interventions on overall system performance.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. Structural: Processes must follow a defined order of activities.
2. Temporal: Activities have specific start and completion times.
3. Resource: Each resource has specific capabilities and availability.
4. Behavioral: Resources and participants behave according to defined rules.
5. Quality: Decisions and outcomes must meet quality standards.
6. Interference: Interventions may affect other cases or processes.
7. Queue estimates are forecasts, not guarantees.
8. Four-eyes rule: Final decisions and compliance reviews must be performed by different individuals.
