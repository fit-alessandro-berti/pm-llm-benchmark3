## 1. Analytical assumptions

A case notion selects one object type and constructs a timestamp-ordered trace for each object of that type. The baseline uses only direct E2O relations: an O2O connection does not automatically put an event into a case. DFG edges represent temporal succession within these traces, not proven causation.

I use convergence for one event involving multiple objects of the chosen type, producing copies across cases. Divergence means several other-object lifecycles appear inside one case, potentially creating repeated activities or misleading succession. Repetition can nevertheless represent legitimate business behavior, such as V2's instalments.

Copied events are both evidence of each object's participation and duplication of a physical occurrence. I retain original event IDs and distinguish occurrence counts from object participation counts. Cases sharing e16 are not independent observations of dispatch.

I permit separately labelled, qualifier-restricted O2O propagation, with explicit paths and provenance. I do not permit silent transitive closure, invented events, inferred payment amounts, or causal conclusions based solely on adjacency. “Lost” below means unavailable from the chosen traces or their case-only models without consulting retained cross-object information.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

1. **Cross-order P3, Item flattening.** e13 packs I21 from O2 and I31 from O3 once. Both item traces contain e13→e16, producing two Pack Package→Dispatch observations of the same 53-minute interval. Item flattening yields six Pack Package rows from four physical packing events. This is informative for item exposure, but harmful when interpreted as packing workload or independent timing observations.

2. **Cross-order P4, Order flattening.** O3 contains e14→e16: packing P4 at 10:20 apparently precedes dispatch at 11:05. However, e16 dispatches D2 carrying P2/P3, whereas P4 leaves in D3 at e17. The apparent Pack Package→Dispatch lag is 45 minutes; P4's actual packing-to-dispatch lag is 55 minutes. O4's trace contains e14→e17. The order projection therefore assigns different successors to one packing event and can misattribute parcel performance.

3. **Split fulfilment, Order flattening.** O1 has Pack Package→Pack Package, e11→e12, and Dispatch→Dispatch, e15→e16. O3 similarly has e13→e14 for P3/P4. These apparent repeat-work variants describe different parcels. They are informative about order fan-out, but harmful if a one-package model labels them rework. O1's e23→e24 Confirm Delivery self-loop likewise represents two deliveries.

4. **Consolidated D2, Package and Order flattening.** Package traces P2/P3 both contain e16→e24, duplicating D2's 4h55 interval. Order flattening copies each endpoint into O1/O2/O3. Three affected orders do not mean three movements. Across the snapshot, the package-weighted dispatch-to-confirmation mean is 8h53m45s; the delivery-weighted mean is 10h13m20s. Both are defensible estimands, but interchanging them biases reporting.

5. **Consolidated V2, Order flattening.** e19 appears in O2 and O3, so three issued invoices become four Issue Invoice rows. Each of e22/e25 also appears twice; four payment occurrences become six Record Payment rows across orders. These copies document shared financial involvement. They cannot establish two separately issued V2 invoices or payment amounts attributable to each order.

6. **Partial settlement, Invoice versus Order flattening.** V2 has Record Payment→Record Payment, e22→e25, separated by 20h20. Issue-to-first-payment is 35 minutes, but issue-to-complete-settlement is 20h55, using M3's qualifier. Calling the first payment completion is harmful. In O2/O3, e24 intervenes, replacing this adjacency with Confirm Delivery→Record Payment. That edge is legitimate chronological interleaving, but does not establish that delivery triggered settlement.

7. **Mixed branches, Order flattening.** O1's e21→e23 gives Record Payment→Confirm Delivery with a 90-minute lag. Its events concern V1/M1 and P1/D1 respectively. Treating that lag as the service time of a payment-to-delivery task invents a dependency. It remains useful as a chronology of order milestones. An OC-DFG also contains order-level succession; object-centric representation alone does not prove causality.

8. **Artificial item completion, Item flattening.** All six item traces have the same four activities. I31 ends at e24 on February 3 at 16:00, while O3's I32 is confirmed only at e27 the following morning and O3 closes at e30. Interpreting I31's terminal event as completion of O3 hides its remaining obligation. The shorter lifecycle is useful for item handling, and misleading for end-to-end customer commitments.

9. **Truncated delivery clock, Delivery flattening.** D3 starts at e17, excluding the 55 minutes between P4's packing e14 and dispatch. Its 22h45 observed span measures dispatch-to-confirmation, not order fulfilment. Similarly, Invoice V1's 30-minute span omits almost all of O1's 26-hour creation-to-closure lifecycle. These are useful scoped durations, not competing measurements of one common process duration.

10. **Singleton payment lifecycle, Payment flattening.** M2 contains only e22 and M3 only e25. Each has zero observed duration and no DFG edge; their relationship as successive settlements of V2 disappears. Uniform singleton traces do not show instantaneous processing or an absence of exceptions. Their settlement qualifiers remain informative relational evidence, even though payment-case sequence discovery is degenerate.

11. **Unbounded propagation, every flattening.** If O2O links are treated as undirected, all 24 objects form one connected component: O1 connects through P2–D2–P3 to O2/O3, and O3 through P4 to O4. Propagating every event to every reachable object gives each order all 31 events: 124 order rows instead of 42. O4 would acquire O1's e01, and multiple Create Order events would induce artificial self-loops. This transformation destroys lifecycle boundaries.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

Create Order, Item, Package, Delivery and Invoice logs for the scoped questions in the matrix. Keep Payment primarily as a register, while computing its empty DFG to document the limitation. Preserve event IDs alongside every flattened row.

Order analysis safely measures e01–e28 and the other explicit creation-to-closure spans. Item analysis distinguishes I31's completion from I32's. Package and Delivery views separately measure parcel handling and movement intervals. Invoice analysis distinguishes V2's first and complete settlement, with qualified O2O evidence. None supports an allocation of payment amounts absent from the snapshot.

Use an OC-DFG to compare these typed lifecycles, including V2's payment self-loop. Use an OTG together with qualified O2O relations to examine the logistics and finance connections. An ET–OT graph explains coverage: Create Order/Close Order involve only Order, while Record Payment involves Order, Invoice and Payment. Object interaction summaries expose P3's O2/O3 and P4's O3/O4 coupling. Preserve original E2O sets because pairwise summaries can hide D2's simultaneous three-order consolidation.

Do not impose lead-anchored equivalent-execution clustering here. Unrestricted component-based execution grouping yields one execution. A separately scoped cohort analysis would first need an explicit anchor and boundary policy.

Reconcile disagreements by checking event identity, object membership, endpoints and denominator. “Three affected orders” and “one D2 movement” are compatible. Retain both measurements with their meanings instead of choosing whichever model looks simpler.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**C. Reject a single lead object for this process.**

- **Decision criteria:** Represent shared work faithfully, distinguish customer completion from parcel, delivery and settlement completion, and use denominators appropriate to each question. Trace length alone is not a criterion.
- **Strongest supporting evidence:** P3/P4 cross order boundaries, O1 splits across deliveries, D2 consolidates three orders, and V2 joins two orders with two payments. No single partition preserves all these cardinalities.
- **Strongest counterargument:** Order covers every event and supplies explicit creation/closure boundaries. It is a compelling anchor for order accountability. However, that purpose does not justify treating its 42 rows as 42 operational occurrences.
- **Residual risk:** A portfolio can produce inconsistent headline metrics unless endpoint, unit and duplication policies are recorded. It also leaves causal dependencies and payment allocation unresolved.
- **Conditions for change:** If the objective were explicitly order SLA accountability, I would adopt Order as the primary anchor with mandatory package, delivery and invoice lifecycles. A narrower logistics or settlement objective would justify its corresponding scoped anchor.

## 6. PM4Py analysis outline

This outline assumes compatible PM4Py APIs and the supplied OCEL. The source ledger preserves identities independently of flattening output. Accounting assertions check coverage; they do not validate business semantics. Discovered skeleton constraints describe this small snapshot, not established process rules. Event weights and event-pair counts are separate sensitivity outputs; the ordinary PM4Py DFG remains unweighted.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

print(ocel)

event_id_col = ocel.event_id_column
activity_col = ocel.event_activity
time_col = ocel.event_timestamp
object_id_col = ocel.object_id_column
object_type_col = ocel.object_type_column

event_table = ocel.events[
    [event_id_col, activity_col, time_col]
].drop_duplicates(event_id_col).copy()
event_table[time_col] = pd.to_datetime(event_table[time_col], utc=True)
object_table = ocel.objects[
    [object_id_col, object_type_col]
].drop_duplicates(object_id_col)

activity_frequencies = event_table[activity_col].value_counts()
object_type_frequencies = object_table[object_type_col].value_counts()
print(activity_frequencies)
print(object_type_frequencies)

# Direct E2O membership only: no O2O propagation.
direct_memberships = (
    ocel.relations[[event_id_col, object_id_col]]
    .drop_duplicates()
    .merge(object_table, on=object_id_col, validate="many_to_one")
    .merge(event_table, on=event_id_col, validate="many_to_one")
)

expected_direct_counts = {
    # cases, covered event IDs, flattened rows
    "Order": (4, 31, 42),
    "Item": (6, 16, 24),
    "Package": (4, 10, 12),
    "Delivery": (3, 6, 6),
    "Invoice": (3, 7, 7),
    "Payment": (4, 4, 4),
}

flattened_logs_by_type = {}
dfgs_by_type = {}
log_skeletons_by_type = {}
case_event_ledgers_by_type = {}
observed_spans_by_type = {}
edge_ledgers_by_type = {}
edge_frequency_audits_by_type = {}
bookkeeping_by_type = {}

for object_type, expected_counts in expected_direct_counts.items():
    rows = direct_memberships[
        direct_memberships[object_type_col] == object_type
    ].copy()
    rows = rows.sort_values(
        [object_id_col, time_col, event_id_col], kind="stable"
    )
    # Tie ordering is bookkeeping; it does not establish causality.
    copies_per_event = rows.groupby(event_id_col)[object_id_col].nunique()
    rows["copies_in_type"] = rows[event_id_col].map(copies_per_event)
    rows["event_weight"] = 1.0 / rows["copies_in_type"]
    case_event_ledgers_by_type[object_type] = rows

    flattened_log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type)
    flattened_logs_by_type[object_type] = flattened_log

    observed_counts = (
        flattened_log["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
        rows[event_id_col].nunique(),
        len(flattened_log),
    )
    assert observed_counts == expected_counts
    assert len(flattened_log) == len(rows)
    assert abs(
        rows["event_weight"].sum() - rows[event_id_col].nunique()
    ) < 1e-9
    bookkeeping_by_type[object_type] = observed_counts

    dfg_edges, start_counts, end_counts = pm4py.discover_dfg(
        flattened_log,
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    )
    dfgs_by_type[object_type] = {
        "edges": dfg_edges,
        "start_activities": start_counts,
        "end_activities": end_counts,
    }
    log_skeletons_by_type[object_type] = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(
        flattened_log,
        noise_threshold=0.0,
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    )

    bounds = rows.groupby(object_id_col)[time_col].agg(
        first_event="min", last_event="max"
    )
    bounds["observed_span"] = bounds["last_event"] - bounds["first_event"]
    observed_spans_by_type[object_type] = bounds

    edge_rows = rows[
        [object_id_col, event_id_col, activity_col, time_col]
    ].copy()
    grouped_cases = edge_rows.groupby(object_id_col, sort=False)
    edge_rows["target_event_id"] = grouped_cases[event_id_col].shift(-1)
    edge_rows["target_activity"] = grouped_cases[activity_col].shift(-1)
    edge_rows["target_time"] = grouped_cases[time_col].shift(-1)
    edge_rows = edge_rows.dropna(subset=["target_event_id"]).copy()
    edge_rows["elapsed_seconds"] = (
        edge_rows["target_time"] - edge_rows[time_col]
    ).dt.total_seconds()

    pair_keys = [event_id_col, "target_event_id"]
    edge_keys = [activity_col, "target_activity"]
    edge_rows["pair_copies"] = edge_rows.groupby(pair_keys)[
        object_id_col
    ].transform("size")
    edge_rows["pair_weight"] = 1.0 / edge_rows["pair_copies"]
    edge_ledgers_by_type[object_type] = edge_rows

    # Audit the three OC-DFG edge-frequency interpretations.
    edge_frequency_audits_by_type[object_type] = pd.concat(
        {
            "event_couples": edge_rows.drop_duplicates(pair_keys)
                .groupby(edge_keys).size(),
            "unique_objects": edge_rows.groupby(edge_keys)[
                object_id_col
            ].nunique(),
            "total_objects": edge_rows.groupby(edge_keys).size(),
        },
        axis=1,
    ).fillna(0).astype(int)

object_centric_dfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
object_type_graph = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
event_type_object_type_graph = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)
object_interactions = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)

print(bookkeeping_by_type)
print(edge_frequency_audits_by_type["Package"])
print(edge_frequency_audits_by_type["Order"])

# No global lead-anchored clustering under decision C.
# Inspect identities, qualified O2O links, denominators and endpoints
# alongside these results before interpreting constraints or durations.
```

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

1. **Copies and independence.** Compare raw participation counts with deduplicated occurrence counts and inverse-copy weights: Order e16/e24 receive weight 1/3 per row; e13/e14 receive 1/2. Audit edges using event-pair multiplicity separately. Arbitrarily removing e16 from O2 would remove its real dispatch participation and induce Pack Package→Issue Invoice, so deletion requires an explicit ownership policy. Report shared-event dependence rather than treating copied cases as independent samples.

2. **Direct versus propagated membership.** Compare the baseline with a specified Order–contains–Item transformation attaching each order's creation/closure to its own items. I31 would then extend through O3's e30, changing the meaning of item duration. Separately test attaching Add Item events through Package–packs–Item: P3 would gain e07/e08. Record source paths, deduplicate event–case pairs, and prohibit propagation into unrelated sibling orders. Contrast with the connected-component failure in section 3.

3. **Change the analytical anchor.** Compare Order, Item, Package, Delivery and Invoice outputs for O3/P3/P4/D2/D3/V2. Check whether alleged rework survives the change: Order's packing self-loop disappears in Package, while V2's instalment self-loop appears only in Invoice. Compare durations only after defining the measured obligation.

4. **Frequency semantics.** Compare distinct-event frequencies at activities and distinct-event-couple, unique-object and total-object frequencies at edges. Package Dispatch→Confirm Delivery has values 3/4/4: e16→e24 is shared. Order Record Payment→Confirm Delivery has 4/4/5: e22→e24 is shared and O3 contributes two occurrences. These metrics answer different questions; disagreement is expected.

5. **Endpoints and qualifiers.** Compare O1's first confirmation e23 with its last e24: choosing the first advances apparent physical completion by two hours. Compare V2's 35-minute first-payment lag with 20h55 to complete settlement. Validate completion using M2/M3 qualifiers, not merely the activity label. Keep the absence of payment amounts explicit.

6. **Discovery robustness.** Treat skeletons from four orders, six items and three invoices as descriptive. Verify repetitions against P1/P2, P3/P4 and M2/M3 before calling them exceptions. Equal-time e29/e30 belong to different orders; any propagated trace combining them needs a tie policy and cannot infer causal order from an event-ID sort.
