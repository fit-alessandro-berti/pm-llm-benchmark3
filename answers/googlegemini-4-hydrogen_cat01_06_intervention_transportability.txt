The shared core causal mechanism is critical-path compression: when `Identity Check` and `Document Check` use independent data and have no statutory ordering constraint, executing them concurrently replaces the sum of their service and inter-task handoff times with the maximum of their completion times, reducing `waiting_before_assessment` and `cycle_time`. A second shared mechanism is coordination risk: premature parallel work without joint visibility can increase `rework` and downstream `appeal`.

Several mechanisms are site-specific. At Site A, `S_co_interventions` (an API upgrade lowering `API_latency`, two added document reviewers raising `document_capacity`, `queue_huddles`, and a simpler `case_complexity` mix) directly reduced cycle time alongside parallelization. At Site B, `S_originals_required` imposes a statutory barrier on 62% of cases, while `S_legal_requirement` routes 40% of cases through constrained `legal_capacity` and supplement loops. Furthermore, `S_IT_case_lock` forces serialization or off-system back-entry, and `S_role_specialization` prevents cross-trained load balancing.

Key effect modifiers are `originals_required`, `digital_documents`, `IT_case_lock`, `role_specialization`, `assessment_capacity`, `legal_capacity`, and `case_complexity`. Each alters the slope from `treatment_parallelization` to `true_overlap` or from `true_overlap` to `cycle_time`.

Consequently, a similar control-flow diagram (`+(Identity, Document)`) does not guarantee transportability. Control-flow syntax abstracts away data preconditions (originals), resource coupling, IT locking, queue saturation, and confounded co-interventions. Two sites can share an identical Petri net or process tree yet experience opposite tail-latency effects.

On the remaining 38% digital cases, critical-path service compression is at most `min(3.4, 8.1) = 3.4h` plus ~1–1.5h handoff reduction (3.0–5.0h subgroup gain, diluted by 0.38 to ~1.1–1.9h site-wide). Without Assessment/Legal throttling (Scenario 3), faster upstream completion shifts wait variance into the 3-assessor and 2-legal queues, pulling the median down slightly for simple digital cases while pushing p90 upward via nonlinear M/M/c queueing congestion and supplement feedback loops.

## 6. Candidate Site B intervention

**Modified Intervention Process Specification:**
1. **Eligibility rules:** At `Register`, evaluate statutory document channel and case tier.
   - *Tier 1 (Eligible immediately in Pilot):* Cases where `originals_required == False` (digital-only, 38% of volume), initially starting with non-legal-review cases (23%) before expanding to digital legal-review cases (15%).
   - *Tier 2 (Ineligible for concurrent `Document Check` start):* Cases where `originals_required == True` (62% of volume).
2. **Concurrent task creation & execution:**
   - For Tier 1 cases, create and unlock both `Identity Check` and `Document Check` immediately after `Register` so Identity and Document reviewers may execute them concurrently.
   - For Tier 2 cases, create `Identity Check` and `Originals Receipt` concurrently after `Register` (preserving existing parallel behavior); create/enable `Document Check` only upon `Originals Receipt` completion.
3. **Mandatory statutory prerequisites:** `Document Check` remains strictly blocked until `Originals Receipt` completes whenever `originals_required == True`. `Assessment` remains strictly blocked until both `Identity Check` and `Document Check` complete. `Legal Review` remains mandatory for all public-sector or high-value cases (40%).
4. **Queue and WIP controls:** Enforce a hard cap of <=6 open verification tasks per reviewer (matching Site A). Add an upstream admission gate: if the `Assessment` waiting queue exceeds a pre-set WIP threshold (e.g., >30 waiting cases) or `Legal Review` >20 cases, pause automatic concurrent release on complex cases and hold daily 15-minute cross-pool queue huddles.
5. **Resource responsibilities:** Preserve specialized pools (4 Identity, 7 Document, 3 Assessment, 2 Legal) with dual-task sub-locking so an Identity reviewer and Document reviewer can hold distinct sub-tasks on the same case simultaneously without lock overrides. Document reviewers must check a mandatory "Identity status reviewed/pending" reconciliation flag prior to final submission.
6. **Legal-review and supplement loops:** `Assessment` routes high-value/public-sector cases to `Legal Review`. If `Legal Review` triggers `Request Supplement`, `Document Check` (re-check) and `Assessment` (re-assessment) execute sequentially on the supplement delta, prioritized ahead of new standard arrivals to prevent tail aging.
7. **Logging requirements:** Modify the case system to record real-time `task_created`, `task_claimed` (start), `task_suspended`, and `task_completed` timestamps per sub-task with owner ID and lock-bypass flag (must equal 0).

**Compact Textual POWL-Style Specification:**
Let `B_candidate` be defined hierarchically over nodes and partial orders:
- `Register` -> `Branch_Verification`:
  - `XOR( Tier1_Digital, Tier2_Originals )`:
    - `Tier1_Digital` = `StrictPartialOrder({Identity Check, Document Check}, order = )` *(concurrent)*
    - `Tier2_Originals` = `StrictPartialOrder({Identity Check, Originals Receipt, Document Check}, order = {(Originals Receipt, Document Check)})` *(`Identity Check` concurrent with `Originals Receipt -> Document Check`)*
- `Branch_Verification` -> `Assessment` *(synchronization barrier: waits for both `Identity Check` and `Document Check`)*
- `Assessment` -> `XOR( tau_no_legal, Loop(do = Legal Review, redo = StrictPartialOrder({Request Supplement -> Document Check -> Assessment})) )`
- `Legal/Post-Assessment` -> `XOR( Approve, Reject )` -> `Notify`

The pilot separates mechanism failure from implementation failure using a three-arm design combined with process-mining fidelity diagnostics. First, Arm 0 (status quo) vs. Arm 1 (IT multi-owner unlock + WIP caps + huddles, without parallel task creation) isolates implementation and co-intervention effects—such as timestamp accuracy, lock removal, and queue discipline—from structural parallelization. Second, comparing Arm 1 vs. Arm 2 (unlock + WIP caps + concurrent task creation on eligible digital claims) identifies the pure causal effect of parallelization net of IT and management changes.

Process-mining conformance metrics adjudicate where breakdowns occur. Implementation failure manifests as low `true_overlap` in Arm 2 (e.g., <45% overlapping active work intervals despite dual task creation), high lock-override rates, batch back-entry timestamps (zero-second intervals or end-of-day clusters), or WIP-cap breaches. In that case, the mechanism was never actually activated. Conversely, mechanism failure occurs when conformance logs confirm high `true_overlap` (>60%) with clean, real-time timestamps and respected WIP caps, yet median cycle time fails to drop or Assessment/Legal queues, rework, and p90 latency degrade. Separating compliance-adjusted (complier average causal effect) estimates from intention-to-treat estimates prevents prematurely abandoning a sound design or scaling a flawed mechanism.

## 8. PM4Py model-construction code

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def build_site_a_models():
    # 1. Construct Site A before and after process trees
    tree_a_before = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
        "-> ( 'Register', 'Identity Check', 'Document Check', 'Assessment', X ( 'Approve', 'Reject' ), 'Notify' )"
    )
    tree_a_after = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
        "-> ( 'Register', + ( 'Identity Check', 'Document Check' ), 'Assessment', X ( 'Approve', 'Reject' ), 'Notify' )"
    )

    # 2. Convert both to accepting Petri nets
    net_a_before, im_a_before, fm_a_before = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_a_before)
    net_a_after, im_a_after, fm_a_after = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_a_after)

    return (
        (tree_a_before, net_a_before, im_a_before, fm_a_before),
        (tree_a_after, net_a_after, im_a_after, fm_a_after),
    )


def build_site_b_candidate_powl():
    # 3. Construct Site B candidate using POWL classes
    t_register = Transition(label="Register")
    t_identity = Transition(label="Identity Check")
    t_originals = Transition(label="Originals Receipt")
    t_document = Transition(label="Document Check")
    t_assessment = Transition(label="Assessment")

    # Optional Originals Receipt (skipped for 38% digital-only cases, required for 62%)
    xor_originals = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR, [t_originals, SilentTransition()]
    )

    # Optional Legal Review with Request Supplement -> Document Check -> Assessment loop
    t_legal = Transition(label="Legal Review")
    t_req_supp = Transition(label="Request Supplement")
    t_doc_repeat = Transition(label="Document Check")
    t_assess_repeat = Transition(label="Assessment")

    redo_supplement = StrictPartialOrder(
        [t_req_supp, t_doc_repeat, t_assess_repeat]
    )
    redo_supplement.order.add_edge(t_req_supp, t_doc_repeat)
    redo_supplement.order.add_edge(t_doc_repeat, t_assess_repeat)

    legal_loop = OperatorPOWL(Operator.LOOP, [t_legal, redo_supplement])
    xor_legal_branch = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR, [SilentTransition(), legal_loop]
    )

    # Final decision and notification
    t_approve = Transition(label="Approve")
    t_reject = Transition(label="Reject")
    xor_decision = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [t_approve, t_reject])
    t_notify = Transition(label="Notify")

    # Top-level strict partial order
    site_b_powl = StrictPartialOrder(
        [
            t_register,
            t_identity,
            xor_originals,
            t_document,
            t_assessment,
            xor_legal_branch,
            xor_decision,
            t_notify,
        ]
    )

    # 4. Explicitly set POWL order edges:
    # - Register precedes Identity Check and optional Originals Receipt
    # - Document Check waits for optional Originals Receipt (thus concurrent with Identity when skipped)
    # - Assessment explicitly waits for BOTH Identity Check and Document Check
    site_b_powl.order.add_edge(t_register, t_identity)
    site_b_powl.order.add_edge(t_register, xor_originals)
    site_b_powl.order.add_edge(xor_originals, t_document)
    site_b_powl.order.add_edge(t_identity, t_assessment)
    site_b_powl.order.add_edge(t_document, t_assessment)
    site_b_powl.order.add_edge(t_assessment, xor_legal_branch)
    site_b_powl.order.add_edge(xor_legal_branch, xor_decision)
    site_b_powl.order.add_edge(xor_decision, t_notify)

    return site_b_powl


def verify_and_simulate():
    (_, net_a_bef, im_a_bef, fm_a_bef), (_, net_a_aft, im_a_aft, fm_a_aft) = (
        build_site_a_models()
    )
    site_b_powl = build_site_b_candidate_powl()

    # 5. Convert POWL model to accepting Petri net
    net_b, im_b, fm_b = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(site_b_powl)

    # 6. Check workflow-net and soundness properties
    for name, (net, im, fm) in [
        ("Site A Before", (net_a_bef, im_a_bef, fm_a_bef)),
        ("Site A After", (net_a_aft, im_a_aft, fm_a_aft)),
        ("Site B Candidate", (net_b, im_b, fm_b)),
    ]:
        is_wf = pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net)
        sound_res = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm)
        print(f"{name}: workflow_net={is_wf}, soundness={sound_res}")

    # 7. Playout sample traces from Site B candidate Petri net
    simulated_log = pm4py.play_out(
        net_b, im_b, fm_b, parameters={"noTraces": 5}
    )
    print("\nSample simulated Site B traces:")
    for idx, trace in enumerate(simulated_log, start=1):
        activities = [
            ev["concept:name"] for ev in trace if ev.get("concept:name") is not None
        ]
        print(f"  Trace {idx}: {activities}")


# 8. Modeling limitations (what control-flow Petri nets / POWL cannot encode):
# - Data-dependent branching: POWL XORs cannot enforce that Originals Receipt is
#   selected iff `originals_required == True` (62%) or Legal Review iff
#   `high_value_or_public == True` (40%); playout samples XOR branches blindly.
# - Resource capacity & specialization: The model omits finite pool sizes
#   (4 Identity, 7 Document, 3 Assessment, 2 Legal), WIP caps (<=6), and queueing delays.
# - IT case locking: Single-owner system locks and manual timestamp back-entry
#   cannot be represented in pure activity partial orders.
# - Durations & stochastic tails: Variable delays (e.g., 18h Originals Receipt,
#   service times, and p90 tail behavior) require timed/stochastic simulation.

if __name__ == "__main__":
    verify_and_simulate()
```

## 9. Recommendation

`conditional go`

Site B should reject management's blanket proposal to create `Identity Check` and `Document Check` concurrently for all cases, and instead proceed with a conditional go restricted to a segmented, IT-enabled pilot.

What should be transferred is the selective partial-order principle: allow `Identity Check` and `Document Check` to execute concurrently only when no statutory `Originals Receipt` prerequisite exists, paired with explicit per-reviewer WIP caps and structured daily queue visibility. What should not be copied is unconditional parallelization after `Register`, nor Site A's expectation of a nine-hour median reduction. At Site A, roughly half to two-thirds of the observed improvement likely stemmed from confounded co-interventions—added FTEs, simpler digital mix, API latency improvements, and queue huddles—rather than parallelization alone.

The subgroup that should enter first is non-originals, non-legal-review digital claims (roughly 23% of total volume, or the broader 38% not requiring physical originals, starting with standard-complexity cases). In this segment, identity and document checks are genuinely data-independent, avoiding the 18-hour `Originals Receipt` block and shielding the two-person `Legal Review` pool and `Request Supplement` loop from premature congestion.

Expected benefits for the eligible digital subgroup are a modest 2.5–4.5 hour median cycle-time reduction and faster readiness for `Assessment`. Primary risks are lock-workaround timestamp corruption if the legacy IT lock is not lifted for dual task ownership, and bottleneck shifting to the three-person `Assessment` pool or two-person `Legal Review` pool, which could worsen p90 tail latency and supplement rework.

Expansion to complex or legal-review digital cases requires: (1) verified dual-ownership IT logging with >60% real-time interval overlap in the pilot arm, (2) 2.0 hours median reduction in `waiting_before_assessment` without p90 degradation, and (3) no statistically meaningful rise in `Request Supplement` or 90-day appeal rates. If WIP caps fail to prevent Assessment/Legal queue growth or rework rises >1.5 percentage points, modify by tightening admission throttling; if IT locks cannot support true concurrent work or mechanism failure occurs under clean execution, abandon parallelization and invest instead in Assessment/Legal capacity and originals tracking.

## 10. Assumption register

1. `Identity Check` and `Document Check` use genuinely independent inputs on Site B's 38% non-originals cases — why needed: parallel execution only saves critical-path time and avoids rework if Document reviewers do not depend on Identity outputs — how to test: audit 100 digital case files and DFG handoff reasons to verify zero data dependency — consequence if false: parallel `Document Check` will stall mid-task or require repeat checks, increasing rework and p90 cycle time.

2. The legacy IT case lock can be reconfigured within a 2-week dry run to support dual task ownership and real-time timestamps — why needed: without technical multi-ownership, true concurrency is impossible and pilot overlap measurements will be corrupted — how to test: run integration tests in staging and verify 0% lock-override events during the dry-run weeks — consequence if false: pilot must be delayed or cancelled, as lock workarounds cause data loss and invalid inference.

3. `Assessment` (3 FTE) and `Legal Review` (2 FTE) have sufficient slack to absorb modest upstream arrival variance on the 23%–38% digital cohort — why needed: if downstream pools are already at >90% utilization, faster upstream verification shifts wait time downstream rather than reducing cycle time — how to test: measure current arrival-to-capacity utilization, queue growth rate, and idle time across Assessment and Legal pools over 4 weeks pre-pilot — consequence if false: bottleneck shift worsens p90 tail latency even if median verification finishes faster.

4. Site B's `Originals Receipt` requirement (62%) is strictly binary and identifiable at `Register` — why needed: eligibility routing depends on knowing at intake whether statutory originals are required — how to test: check registration metadata accuracy against actual `Originals Receipt` events in historical logs — consequence if false: misclassified cases will enter parallel verification prematurely and hit statutory blocks or rework.

5. Reviewers will adhere to the 6-task WIP cap and will not cherry-pick Arm 2 cases over Arm 0/1 cases within shared pools — why needed: cross-arm interference or preferential scheduling would bias pilot treatment estimates — how to test: compare Arm 0 service/wait times during pilot against pre-pilot baseline and monitor reviewer WIP distributions daily — consequence if false: pilot overstates parallelization gains due to capacity stealing from control cases.

6. Baseline active-work vs. queue-wait splits in Site B's 3.4h `Identity` and 8.1h `Document` medians reflect genuine service duration — why needed: quantitative scenario gains depend on `min(service_1, service_2)` compression — how to test: run time-motion or active-session timestamp logging on 50 cases per pool before pilot launch — consequence if false: if recorded durations are mostly idle open-time rather than active service, parallelizing tasks will compress less actual work than projected.

7. The ~38% non-originals share and 40% legal-review share remain roughly stable during the 10-week pilot — why needed: statistical power and queue-throttling thresholds rely on ~130 eligible cases/month — how to test: monitor weekly intake mix and run rolling seasonal intake checks — consequence if false: low intake underpowers the pilot, requiring an extended duration beyond 10 weeks.

8. Short-term rework (`Request Supplement`, repeated `Document Check`) is a reliable leading indicator for 90-day `appeal` risk — why needed: waiting 90 days for every appeal delays go/no-go governance decisions — how to test: correlate historical `Request Supplement` and rework flags against 90-day appeals in Site B's 3,400-case baseline — consequence if false: quality erosion on fast parallelized cases could go undetected until 90 days post-launch.
