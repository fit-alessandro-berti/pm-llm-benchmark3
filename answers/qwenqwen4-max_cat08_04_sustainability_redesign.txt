# 1. System boundary and assumptions

**Functional unit.** Impact is measured *per conforming line item delivered within the promised lead time* — not per order and not per shipped parcel. The log is flattened by order, so per-order averages hide multi-item orders, split shipments, and rework loops. Any per-order metric will look "greener" if we simply cancel more orders (530 cancellations today) or let batch items age out; the functional unit must therefore carry cancelled, obsolesced, and returned items in the denominator's cost base.

**Inside the boundary (gate-to-customer, plus loops):**
- Direct machine energy (`energy_kwh` on CNC_A, CNC_B, heat_treat, inspection_cell), including **re-processing energy** from the `Inspection -> Rework production -> CNC production` loop (470 / 390 events).
- Process scrap (`material_scrap_kg`) and packaging material (`package_material_kg`), including the second kit consumed by `Rework packaging` (620 inbound events).
- Outbound transport (`transport_kgco2e`) for all four `route_mode`s, **plus the second leg of partial shipments** and the reverse leg of damage returns.
- Finished-goods obsolescence: the 6% of batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days — their full CNC + heat-treat + packaging burden is wasted.
- Wasted work on `Cancel due to delay` (530 cases) where production or picking already occurred.

**Outside the boundary (declared, not ignored):** upstream raw-material and packaging embodied carbon (no attribute exists), inbound supplier transport, warehouse lighting/HVAC, capital goods, customer use phase, and end-of-life. This matters directionally: reinforced packaging's *material* penalty is understated by mass alone, while its *avoided-return* benefit (40% fewer damage returns on heavy MTO) is also understated because a return triggers re-transport and often re-production.

**Key uncertainties to state openly:**
1. `energy_kwh` allocation across a `batch_id` is unknown (per item vs per batch vs per order); the 2.8  1.3 kWh/item curve is per item, the segment table is per order. These are not directly additive.
2. `transport_kgco2e` appears modelled (air  4–6× ground is a factor range, not a measurement); van figures ignore load factor / empty returns.
3. **Returns and obsolescence are not events in the DFG.** `Confirm delivery -> Close` is 11,200 with no return loop, yet the evidence text cites customer returns. These are blind spots, not absences.
4. Count inconsistencies signal an incomplete or filtered log: cancels 410 + 60 = 470 but `Cancel -> END` = 530; `Inspection -> Rework production` 470 vs `Rework production -> CNC` 390; CNC inbound 4,780 vs outbound 3,530. Reconciliation is a prerequisite for any claim of savings.
5. Model **precision is low** (emergency + partial-shipment paths interleave during disruptions), so a discovered imperative model is a weak basis for an 85%-reliability rule. Use declarative constraints and empirical per-lane p90s instead.

- *Target:* `Critical -> emergency production -> reinforced pack -> dedicated van` (8%, 52 kgCO2e/order).
- *Mechanism:* Pairing outbound runs and backhauls raises load factor on the highest-intensity mode.
- *Service/quality/resilience:* Only viable where lane density supports pairing inside the downtime window; retain dedicated vans as the R1 fallback and for true downtime. Do not pool where waiting-for-a-partner risks a line stoppage.
- *Evidence:* Van load factor, empty-return km, pairing wait, Critical late % (3%) and p90 (30h), SP2 justification completeness.

**S9 — Split Critical into true downtime vs precautionary urgent**
- *Target:* The 11% Critical segment that generates ~41% of transport emissions.
- *Mechanism:* Precautionary orders with real slack move to priority pick + ground/consolidated; the existing `Critical -> stock/ground exception` path (3%) shows 4.8 vs 52 kgCO2e.
- *Service/quality/resilience:* **Highest-value and highest-risk lever.** That same path shows 9% late vs 3% — downgrading triples lateness. Therefore reclassify only with contract-validated downtime confirmation, never by heuristic, and never for confirmed line-down cases.
- *Evidence:* Reclassification audit, late % and p90 for *both* sub-categories, customer downtime incidents, escalation rate back to true-downtime.

**S10 — Carbon-aware promise negotiation, Regular MTO only**
- *Target:* MTO Regular (22%, 94h median, 18% late) — the only segment with structural slack to sell.
- *Mechanism:* Opt-in longer promise in exchange for ground shipping and slack-safe batching; converts a service constraint into a batching/consolidation opportunity.
- *Service/quality/resilience:* Late % improves partly by *definition* — must be reported alongside absolute cycle time. Risk of order loss to faster competitors (impact displacement).
- *Evidence:* Promised-hours drift, absolute cycle time, opt-in rate, quote-to-order conversion, expedite requests after opting in.

**S11 — Availability-check rules to reduce partial shipments**
- *Target:* `Pick from stock -> Partial shipment` (1,010), `Backorder wait` (220), 60 backorder cancellations; partial path 79h, 24% late, 7.2 kg.
- *Mechanism:* ATP/reservation logic and ship-complete-when-slack-allows remove second shipments and second packaging kits.
- *Service/quality/resilience:* Holding an order complete can *worsen* service for downtime-critical lines — keep split mandatory when any line is Critical or the promise cannot otherwise be met.
- *Evidence:* Partial-shipment rate, second-shipment transport kg, backorder wait, cancellation rate, line-level fill rate.

**S12 — Sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides**
- *Target:* SP2 justification missing in 31% of van cases; SP1 violations; Q1 violations.
- *Mechanism:* Makes overrides first-class events with reason codes, approver, and expiry — turning unmeasured emissions into governed, priced decisions.
- *Service/quality/resilience:* Preserves operational flexibility (essential during disruption) while preventing silent policy erosion.
- *Evidence:* Exception rate by segment/shift, reason-code mix, expiry compliance, and *quality* audit of justifications (presence  validity).

# 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching  expedite rebound.** Larger CNC/heat-treat batches cut kWh/item but push wait from 4h to 38h; heat-treat batching alone carries 1.7× late risk. Lateness triggers `expedite_flag`, air (18.5 kg) or vans (52 kg), erasing the energy saving several times over. *Detection:* batch wait  paired with expedite/air share  on the same `batch_id` cohort. *Mitigation:* segment caps; forbid batching for Critical and tight-slack SC.
2. **Batching  obsolescence.** 6% of batch items unshipped in 14 days carry the *full* CNC + heat-treat + packaging burden with zero delivered value — worse per delivered item than small-batch production. *Detection:* obsolescence event rate by batch size and forecast error. *Mitigation:* batch only against firm demand; cap speculative fill.
3. **Consolidation  partial-shipment rebound.** A 12–24h consolidation wait can make an order miss its date, prompting a split shipment; two ground legs (7.2 kg on the partial path) can exceed the 35–55% saving on one consolidated leg. *Detection:* consolidation share  with partial-shipment rate . *Mitigation:* ATP + promise check before assigning to a consolidated departure.
4. **Packaging light-weighting  return rebound.** Right-sizing below Q1 saves package mass but heavy MTO damage returns rise (reinforced cuts them 40%); each return adds reverse transport, re-inspection, and often re-production — embodied energy far exceeds the saved cardboard. *Detection:* package kg/item  paired with damage-return rate . *Mitigation:* Q1 as a hard constraint, not a scored trade-off.
5. **Air downgrade  picking/inspection pressure  rework.** Priority picking compresses pick and inspection time; rushed inspection is already identified as the root cause of rework loops. The greenest mode choice can thus create a 32 kWh / 1.8 kg rework case. *Detection:* SP1-driven downgrades correlated with `rework_flag` and packaging rework. *Mitigation:* S7 capacity funded *before* S4 scale-up.
6. **CNC_BA  congestion displacement.** Shifting load to the better-quality machine lengthens A's queue, pushing Service-contract MTO (already 13% late, p90 156h) into emergency production (24 kWh + air). *Detection:* A queue length, SC MTO p90, emergency-production share. *Mitigation:* slack-aware routing with an SC reservation quota; keep B qualified for low-risk geometries.
7. **Customer-behaviour gaming of urgency.** If precautionary Critical orders are downgraded (S9), customers learn to declare every order line-down; van volume and 52 kg/order intensity return, plus contract friction. Similarly, S10's longer promises may induce earlier/hoarding orders or drive Regular MTO customers to faster, higher-impact competitors (displacement outside the boundary). *Detection:* Critical share drift, reclassification appeals, opt-in and conversion rates. *Mitigation:* contract-validated downtime evidence, priced expedite options, no promise stretching for SC.
8. **Van pooling  downtime exposure.** Shared lanes reduce empty returns but add pairing wait; a single missed downtime window outweighs months of transport savings. *Detection:* pooling wait distribution vs Critical p90 (30h). *Mitigation:* density thresholds, dedicated-van fallback under R1.
9. **Metric displacement.** Improving kgCO2e/order by cancelling more delay cases, reclassifying scrap as rework, or lengthening `promised_lead_time_hours` improves the dashboard while total impact per *delivered conforming item* worsens. *Detection:* reconciliation of totals vs intensities; absolute cycle time; cancellation rate.

# 6. Balanced recommendation

Adopt a **quality-first, slack-aware redesign** in four waves, rather than a set of independent green levers. The evidence says the impact is concentrated: MTO carries ~87% of energy and ~85% of scrap, much of it via rework; Critical carries ~41% of transport from 11% of orders. So attack *defects and mis-classification*, not service levels.

**Wave 0 — Make waste visible and stop the bleeding (no service cost).**
S12 exception log; S6 packaging–route compatibility; Q1 as a hard system constraint; S7 inspection gate **with added inspection capacity**; log enrichment (un-flatten line items, add return, obsolescence, van-load-factor, and batch-allocation attributes; reconcile the 530/470 and 470/390 count gaps). Rationale: rework is the dominant hidden impact (32 kWh, 1.8 kg on the CNC_B loop) and the cheapest abatement is not producing it twice.

**Wave 1 — Justify premium transport, don't ban it.**
S1 in shadow mode, then advisory with hard Q1/R1 gates; S4 priority pick + ground for Standard-stock Service-contract where **p90-based** slack clears the 85% threshold; S9 triage of Critical into contract-validated true downtime vs precautionary urgent. Air and dedicated vans remain fully available — but as *logged, expiring, justified exceptions* (closing the 31% SP2 gap), not defaults. This respects the evidence that the Critical ground exception is 3× later (9% vs 3%): we downgrade only where slack is demonstrated, never by segment label alone.

**Wave 2 — Rebalance production and flow.**
S3 slack-aware CNC_BA routing with an SC capacity reservation; S2 due-date-aware batching with hard caps (Regular MTO  24h, SC MTO  8h, Critical none, heat-treat tighter than CNC given the 1.7× risk); S5 consolidation for Regular stock only, suppressed when it would cause a split; S11 ATP rules that keep splitting mandatory for urgent lines. Note the honest trade: caps may *raise* energy on slack-poor batches. We accept that, because the alternative — buying kWh/item with lateness, expedite, and obsolescence — is worse on the full functional unit.

**Wave 3 — Structural, opt-in changes.**
S8 shared emergency lanes only above a lane-density threshold, with dedicated vans retained as the R1 fallback; S10 opt-in promise negotiation for Regular MTO, reported against absolute cycle time.

**Why this balances.** Every emissions lever is paired with the constraint it could break: batching with wait caps and obsolescence tracking; mode downgrade with p90 slack and R1 fallback; packaging reduction with Q1 and damage-return monitoring; machine consolidation with queue and SC-reservation guards. Service-contract and true-downtime promises are treated as constraints, not objectives. And the package deliberately spends *some* energy and packaging mass (inspection capacity, reinforced packs, smaller batches) to buy larger reductions in rework, returns, and obsolescence.

**Explicitly rejected:** blanket air bans; eliminating dedicated vans; maximising batch size for kWh/item; consolidation for SC/Critical; downgauging below Q1; and any promise-lengthening for Service-contract customers.

# 7. Validation and governance plan

**Design.** Staged rollout by segment and lane with matched controls and a holdout (e.g., 20% of Regular-stock lanes retain current rules for one quarter). Pre-register the expected direction and the stop rules *before* deployment. Because model precision is low, evaluate with declarative constraint checks and cohort comparisons rather than replay against the discovered imperative model alone.

**Guardrails with automatic rollback:** Service-contract late % > baseline +1pp for two consecutive weeks; any confirmed downtime miss attributable to S8/S9; segment p90 cycle time regression >10%; damage-return or `rework_flag` rate above baseline; obsolescence rate above 6%. Rollback is per-lever and per-segment, not global.

**Conformance monitoring.**
- *SP1:* recompute the >85% on-time probability from empirical per-lane, per-season p90 transit (not the mined model's median); report violation rate by segment and shift; target the 22% Standard-stock SC violation rate toward <5% while late % stays 5%.
- *SP2:* require a justification event with reason code, approver, and downtime evidence before the van booking; target 100% presence (from 69%) plus a monthly **quality audit** of a sample — presence is not validity.
- *Q1:* declarative check on weight >8 kg  reinforced pack OR inspection certificate; correlate violations with return/rework loops to confirm the mechanism, not just the count.
- *R1:* nightly check that every Service-contract lane has 1 qualified alternative route; quarterly carrier-disruption drill measuring recovery time and emergency-production share.

**Impact accounting.** Report both absolute totals and intensity per conforming delivered item, always segmented (avoids mix-shift/Simpson effects). Include cancelled-order work, obsolesced batch items, second shipment legs, reverse legs, and rework energy as explicit line items. Re-estimate energy and transport factors quarterly and re-run the boundary reconciliation so claimed savings survive an audit.

**Rebound detection dashboard (paired signals).** Batch wait  expedite/air share; consolidation share  partial-shipment rate; package kg/item  damage returns; promised hours  absolute cycle time; A-queue length  SC MTO p90 and emergency production; van pooling wait  Critical p90; Critical share  reclassification appeals. Any pair moving in the rebound direction triggers a lever-level review, not a celebratory report.

**Governance.** Weekly operations review of guardrails and exception queues; monthly sustainability–service board with authority to tighten caps or revoke a lever; a per-segment **exception budget** with expiry and auto-escalation so overrides cannot become the silent default; root-cause coding of every SP1/SP2/Q1 override feeding back into recommender calibration; drift monitoring on the recommender's reliability estimates; and an annual re-baselining of the boundary as return, obsolescence, and load-factor instrumentation matures.
