## 1. Decision rule

**Eligibility:**
- **Stage eligibility:** Only cases in `Triage` or `Diagnosis Started` are eligible for a Senior Diagnostic Swarm.
- **Minimum evidence change:** For repeated alerts, a case is eligible only if:
  - It is the first alert, **or**
  - A material new event has occurred (e.g., new customer log, workflow change, or data correction), **or**
  - The predicted breach probability (`p_now`) has increased by 0.10 since the last alert **and** the case has not received a swarm in the last 4 hours.
- **Cooldown:** No case may receive more than one swarm in a 4-hour window unless a material new event occurs.

**Probability adjustment for calibration:**
- **Strategic Enterprise:** Reduce `p_now` by 0.08 (midpoint of 0.05–0.12 overprediction).
- **SME:** Increase `p_now` by 0.06 (midpoint of 0.04–0.09 underprediction).
- **Non-Native-Language Support:** Use `p_now` but widen the risk-reduction range by 50% to reflect uncertainty.
- **Public Sector:** No adjustment (calibration acceptable).

**Prioritization score:**
For eligible cases, compute a **priority score** as:
```
score = (adjusted_p_now × value_of_avoiding_breach × midpoint_risk_reduction) - (swarm_cost + false_alarm_burden) - (spillover_penalty × 6 × 0.02 × value_of_avoiding_breach_for_untreated_cases)
```
- `midpoint_risk_reduction` = midpoint of the estimated absolute risk reduction range.
- `spillover_penalty` = 0.02 (midpoint of 0.01–0.03 spillover per slot).
- The spillover penalty is applied to the average value of untreated diagnosis-stage cases (approximated as €25,000).

**Allocation:**
- Select up to 4 cases with the highest priority scores, reserving 1 slot if a regulatory incident is likely to arrive.
- If two cases have similar scores, prioritize:
  1. Higher value of avoiding breach.
  2. Earlier stage (Triage > Diagnosis Started).
  3. Lower false-alarm burden.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**Cool-down:**
- A case may not receive a swarm more than once in a 4-hour window unless a material new event occurs.
- After a swarm, the case is ineligible for another swarm for 4 hours, regardless of new alerts.

**Suppression:**
- If a case generates repeated alerts without a material new event (e.g., I-05), suppress further alerts for 2 hours.
- If `p_now` increases by 0.10 since the last alert, the case re-enters the queue.

**Escalation:**
- If a case remains in `Diagnosis Started` for >2 hours after a swarm without progressing to `Diagnosis Complete`, escalate to a manager for manual review.

**Re-entry logic:**
- I-05: Suppressed for 2 hours due to repeated alerts without new events. If `p_now` increases by 0.10, re-enter the queue.
- I-07: Eligible because the new customer log is a material new event. No suppression.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Risks:**
1. **Value-based prioritization:** High-value cases (e.g., I-08) may dominate slots, starving lower-value cases (e.g., I-07) even if their risk reduction is higher.
2. **Non-Native-Language Support:** I-09’s uncertainty may lead to systematic under-allocation, reinforcing disparities.
3. **Differential calibration:** Strategic Enterprise cases are overpredicted, leading to over-intervention; SME cases are underpredicted, leading to under-intervention.
4. **Feedback loops:** Selective intervention changes the distribution of training labels, potentially biasing future models (e.g., untreated cases may appear to have higher breach rates).
5. **Spillover harm:** Untreated cases (e.g., I-06, I-09) bear the cost of congestion, which may disproportionately affect segments with lower calibration quality.

**Safeguards:**
1. **Value cap:** Limit the number of slots allocated to cases with value >€50,000 to 1 per day.
2. **Segment quotas:** Reserve at least 1 slot per day for Non-Native-Language Support or SME cases.
3. **Calibration monitoring:** Track breach rates by segment and adjust thresholds quarterly.
4. **Spillover tracking:** Monitor breach rates for untreated diagnosis-stage cases and adjust spillover penalties if harm exceeds expectations.
5. **Feedback-loop mitigation:** Randomly allocate 10% of slots to cases below the priority threshold to maintain unbiased training data.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational monitoring:**
- **Daily:** Track slot utilization, intervention stage, and progression to `Diagnosis Complete`.
- **Weekly:** Review false-alarm burden (customer complaints, case rework) and spillover harm (breach rates for untreated cases).

**Predictive monitoring:**
- **Monthly:** Backtest model calibration by segment (e.g., compare `p_now` to actual breach rates).
- **Quarterly:** Re-estimate intervention effects using causal methods (e.g., instrumental variables for swarm allocation).

**Causal-effect monitoring:**
- **A/B testing:** Randomly assign 5% of eligible cases to swarm/no-swarm to estimate average treatment effect.
- **Heterogeneity analysis:** Stratify effect estimates by segment, stage, and time-to-deadline.

**Capacity monitoring:**
- **Real-time:** Track queue congestion and adjust spillover penalties dynamically.
- **Long-term:** Model capacity needs under different breach-rate scenarios.

**Fairness monitoring:**
- **Monthly:** Report breach rates and intervention rates by segment, value tier, and language support.
- **Quarterly:** Audit for disparate impact (e.g., higher spillover harm for Non-Native-Language Support).

**Recalibration triggers:**
- If calibration drift exceeds 0.05 for any segment, adjust thresholds or retrain the model.
- If spillover harm exceeds 0.04 per slot, reduce the number of slots or increase the priority threshold.
- If intervention effects decline by >20%, investigate workflow changes or model drift.
