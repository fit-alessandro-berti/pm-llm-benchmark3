## 1. What is observed

Between January and August 2026, automated triage availability (`automation=1`) was rolled out to three of five branches in two waves: Alpha and Beta gained access on April 1, Gamma gained access on July 1 following a short readiness pilot, and Delta and Epsilon remained unexposed through August. System availability did not equal uniform adoption. Even when `automation=0`, 4%–6% of cases carried an automated-fast-path classification; once `automation=1`, `automated_fast_path_pct` jumped to 25%–27% in month one and climbed to 48%–50% in Alpha and Beta by August and 32% in Gamma.

Rollout was non-random: Alpha and Beta were prioritized because they had the largest baseline volumes (905–1,060 arrivals/month) and slowest cycle times (45.9–48.9h medians; 92.9–98.5h p90s). Multiple operational shifts coincided with deployment. A centralized fraud desk opened in March, alongside a 2.0-percentage-point escalation rise across all branches. Routing campaigns added roughly 10% more arrivals at Alpha and Beta from April and 5% at Gamma from July, atop seasonal arrival peaks in June. Productive FTEs changed in April–June (Beta +1, Alpha -2, Gamma -1, Delta +1), while high-complexity case shares rose 5–8 percentage points across branches following a May product change.

Outcome measurement has key limitations: cycle times appear only as branch-month medians and p90s rather than case timestamps, fraud-desk wait times were unrecorded, and `csat_1to5` reflects monthly means from 20%–45% response rates without response counts.

**Interference and treatment-version clarification:**
- **Interference (SUTVA violation across branches):** All branches share a single centralized fraud desk opened in March. Because automation increased `escalation_pct` by ~1.0 percentage point at adopting branches, higher referrals from Alpha, Beta, and Gamma could lengthen fraud-desk queues for Delta and Epsilon, biasing control outcomes upward (and making adopters look slightly better than true no-interference counterfactuals). Likewise, the routing campaign shifted volume into Alpha, Beta, and Gamma, altering queue load across the network.
- **Treatment versions:** `automation=1` is not a uniform intervention. Alpha and Beta launched at 25%–27% fast-path use and ramped to 48%–50% alongside local managerial adaptations, whereas Gamma underwent a readiness pilot before July launch. Thus E1/E2 estimate an average across evolving implementation versions rather than a fixed software effect.

| **Alternative 1:** Demeaned / Augmented Synthetic Control (per treated branch) | Binary post-adoption indicator per treated branch (Apr+ for Alpha/Beta; Jul+ for Gamma) | Convex combination of Delta, Epsilon (and pre-Jul Gamma for Apr–Jun) | Pre-period outcome trajectory + pre-period `high_complexity_pct`, `arrivals/fte` | Branch-specific ATTs (E1, E2, E4) | Factor model / linear latent trend captured by weighted controls after branch-level intercept demeaning | Pre-treatment RMSPE fit; placebo adoption assigned to Delta and Epsilon; leave-one-donor-out | Alpha (and Beta) exceed donor volume/cycle-time range, forcing intercept extrapolation; only 2–3 donors limits factor identification |

*Note on naïve TWFE:* A naïve static two-way fixed-effects regression (`outcome ~ automation + branch_FE + month_FE`) is inappropriate here because treatment effects vary over time as `automated_fast_path_pct` doubles from 25% to 50% and because Gamma adopts later—causing static TWFE to use already-treated Alpha and Beta as implicit controls for Gamma in July–August ("forbidden comparisons").

**Why the primary strategy is preferable (198 words):**
Staggered cohort-relative-time DiD is preferable because it directly matches the two-wave rollout (April and July) without introducing the forbidden comparisons of naïve TWFE. By comparing April adopters (Alpha and Beta) against not-yet-treated Gamma (April–June) and never-treated Delta and Epsilon, and comparing July adopter Gamma against Delta and Epsilon, it prevents dynamic learning effects in Alpha and Beta from contaminating Gamma’s counterfactual. Explicit event-time indicators ($k=0,1,2,3,4$) also allow immediate effects—such as the sharp step-changes in `rework_pct` and `escalation_pct`—to be separated from slower cycle-time changes as `automated_fast_path_pct` scales.

Compared with Synthetic Control, demeaned DiD does not require Alpha’s extreme baseline arrival volume (1,060/month) and cycle time (48.9h) to lie inside the convex hull of smaller branches like Delta (655/month, 34.5h). Compared with pure interrupted time series, using contemporaneous control branches absorbs major system-wide shocks that hit all units simultaneously—specifically the March centralized fraud desk, May’s 5-percentage-point complexity surge, and July–August’s seasonal demand decline. While $G=5$ clusters prevents reliable asymptotic standard errors and requires conditioning on branch-specific FTE and routing-driven workload shifts, this design provides the cleanest counterfactual benchmark available in the aggregate panel.

### 5.2 Comparisons against not-yet-treated and never-treated branches

- **April–August vs Jan–March against never-treated Delta & Epsilon:**

### 5.3 Treatment-intensity analysis (`automated_fast_path_pct`)

Two distinct intensity tests reveal sharp differences between *initial launch* and *subsequent ramp-up*:
1. **Immediate step vs ramp-up:** At launch ($k=0$: Apr for Alpha/Beta, Jul for Gamma), `automated_fast_path_pct` jumped +21 pp everywhere. Holding `high_complexity_pct` fixed (Alpha Mar $\to$ Apr; Gamma Jun $\to$ Jul), `rework_pct` dropped immediately by -2.0 pp and `escalation_pct` rose +1.0 pp. Yet in months $k=1 \dots 4$ (when `automated_fast_path_pct` climbed another +21 to +23 pp), `rework_pct` and `escalation_pct` showed zero additional improvement once complexity changes are netted out.
2. **Within-post ramp-up (June $\to$ August) vs controls:** From June to August, `automation=1`, `high_complexity_pct`, and `fte` were completely constant in Alpha (36%, 32 FTE) and Beta (28%, 30 FTE), while `automated_fast_path_pct` rose +9 pp (39% $\to$ 48% in Alpha; 41% $\to$ 50% in Beta). Over those two months, Beta’s `median_cycle_h` fell -4.0h (-8.2%) and `p90_cycle_h` fell -7.5h (-7.0%), nearly identical to never-treated Delta (-3.6h / -8.5% median; -6.7h / -7.1% p90) as arrivals fell ~9.7% seasonally across all branches. Alpha fell -10.2h (-19.1%) median and -18.7h (-15.6%) p90, closely matching Epsilon (-8.6h / -17.4% median; -16.1h / -15.0% p90). **Crucial caution:** A naïve within-post regression on `automated_fast_path_pct` without month controls mistakes seasonal summer decongestion for a steep learning/dose-response curve; once month fixed effects and arrival declines are controlled, incremental cycle-time gains from 39% to 50% fast-path uptake are modest or statistically indistinguishable from seasonal dynamics.

### 5.4 Pre-trends and regression to the mean (RTM)

Pre-trends (January–March) are short (only 3 months for Alpha/Beta) and not strictly parallel:
- `median_cycle_h` drifted slightly downward in Alpha (50.4 $\to$ 46.9 $\to$ 49.4h; -1.0h net) and Epsilon (41.4 $\to$ 40.2h), flat-upward in Beta (45.4 $\to$ 46.4h; +1.0h) and Delta (34.4 $\to$ 36.2h; +1.8h), and upward in Gamma (36.4 $\to$ 39.6h; +3.2h).
- **Regression to the mean:** Alpha and Beta were explicitly selected for poor baseline cycle times (48.9h and 45.9h medians). However, Alpha’s February dip (46.9h) had already rebounded in March (49.4h), and Beta’s baseline trend was worsening, not peaking. More importantly, Alpha and Beta’s cycle times *rose* in May–June and stayed elevated relative to Jan–Mar until August. Thus, simple RTM from a transitory January–March spike cannot explain the persistent DiD gap relative to Delta, Epsilon, and pre-July Gamma. Still, with only 3 pre-months for Alpha/Beta, pre-trend tests lack power to rule out divergent secular trends.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

`expand with an embedded evaluation`

Leadership should **expand with an embedded evaluation** rather than scaling blindly across both remaining branches or halting a promising workflow improvement. Deploying immediately (`expand immediately`) ignores severe identification limits ($G=5$ branches, unrecorded fraud-desk queues, and unresolved p90 tail behavior), whereas halting (`do not expand` / `pause and redesign`) would forfeit credible fast-path buffering on medians and a ~2 pp rework reduction observed especially after Gamma’s readiness pilot.

**Next branch and assignment mechanism:** Expand next to **Epsilon** only, holding **Delta** as a pure unexposed branch control for at least 3–4 months. Epsilon closely resembles Alpha and Beta in high complexity (38%) and elevated cycle times (40.8–49.4h), delivering higher operational upside while preserving Delta as a clean comparison unit. Within Epsilon, run a **4-week readiness pilot** (replicating Gamma’s smoother launch), followed by **randomized case-level or day/shift-level assignment (50/50 split)** of eligible non-high-risk cases between Automated Triage and Manual Triage (or shadow-scoring all cases first, then randomizing routing on borderline tiers). This breaks ecological aggregation and directly identifies the case-level CACE (Estimand E7).

**Minimum pre/post horizons:** Require at least 8 weeks of case-level baseline event-log capture across Epsilon, Delta, and the centralized fraud desk before launch, and evaluate after a minimum 12-week post-launch window (including ramp-up and steady-state weeks).

**Process-mining and queue-state instrumentation:** Replace monthly branch aggregates with event-level logs (`case_id`, `activity`, `start_ts`, `complete_ts`, `resource_role`, `triage_mode`, `risk_class_at_reg`), capturing queue lengths and WIP at Specialist Review and the fraud desk at the moment of arrival/escalation.

**Spillover, guardrails, and scale-up decision rule:** Track fraud-desk wait times by originating branch weekly to detect cross-branch congestion on Delta. Pause automated routing at Epsilon if `escalation_pct` exceeds baseline by >2.0 pp, fraud-desk p90 wait time rises >25% across all branches, or randomized automated cases show statistically higher rework or lower weighted CSAT (requiring >40% response tracking with respondent path linkage). Scale to Delta only if Epsilon’s randomized evaluation demonstrates statistically and operationally meaningful median reduction without p90 tail degradation or fraud-desk spillover.

## 10. Final claim ladder

- **Observed:** Across all three adopting branches, `automated_fast_path_pct` rose immediately by ~21 percentage points at launch (reaching 48%–50% in Alpha/Beta by August and 32% in Gamma), coinciding with ~2.0-point immediate drops in `rework_pct` (holding complexity constant), ~1.0-point jumps in `escalation_pct`, and smaller raw cycle-time increases than occurred in non-adopting Delta and Epsilon.
- **Conditionally causal:** Conditional on parallel counterfactual trends across branches adjusted for workload (`arrivals/fte`) and `high_complexity_pct`—and assuming no cross-branch fraud-desk interference—automated triage reduced `rework_pct` by roughly 1.4–2.5 percentage points, increased `escalation_pct` by ~0.7–1.1 percentage points, and lowered `median_cycle_h` relative to non-adopting trajectories by roughly 2–6 hours.
- **Suggestive:** Readiness piloting (Gamma) may yield cleaner immediate median and tail improvements than cold-start deployment under heavy volume surges (Alpha/Beta), and `csat_1to5` appears non-degrading or modestly protected relative to non-adopters—though both patterns remain vulnerable to seasonal decongestion, small cluster count ($G=5$), and 20%–45% survey selection.
- **Not supported:** A blanket leadership statement that “automation reduces cycle time by X%” across branches and quantiles is not defensible, because raw `p90_cycle_h` remained elevated above pre-adoption baselines in Alpha and Beta, `p90/median` tail skew widened, fraud-desk queue spillovers are unmeasured, and case-level effects cannot be identified from branch-month aggregates.
