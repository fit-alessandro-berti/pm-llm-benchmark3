## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional verdict: `not identifiable from the supplied evidence` (confidence 88/100 in that verdict, not in a zero effect).** Resource group is a manipulable routing destination, but in these data it is also a proxy for the routing rule and a bundle of night coverage, reviewer pool, and queue conditions. The policy-total effect of routing a classified case to Orion versus Nova includes changed reviewer mix, queueing, compliance timing, review effort, and rework. A controlled direct “group-label” effect with those mechanisms fixed is neither operationally well specified nor identified; queue wait, service, and rework are potential mediators.

The strongest evidence for nonidentification is purposeful difficult-case routing, Orion’s 42.9% versus Nova’s 12.5% high complexity, 92.9% versus 25.0% nights, 64.3% versus 25.0% compliance requirements, unrecorded case risk/reviewer choice, and restriction to completed cases. The crude +6.72 h Orion mean therefore combines case mix and operating conditions. Counter-evidence to a simple harmful-Orion story is consistent reversal in all three complexity strata and the pooled-complexity standardized contrast: 17.14 h Orion versus 19.65 h Nova (2.51 h); reported waits are also lower within each stratum. Those comparisons remain thin and shift/load-imbalanced.

Moving half the work could help if it relieves a real Orion bottleneck, fail if the crude gap is selection, or backfire by putting night/high-risk/compliance work into Nova’s capacity or less suitable reviewer pool. It also changes queues, so current case-level contrasts need not transport to a 50% reallocation.

Supported arrows include routing after classification; difficult-case and night-work selection into Orion; complexity-driven compliance requirements; required compliance being performed; and waits, service, and rework contributing to cycle time. The remaining arrows, especially those involving unobserved risk, reviewer skill, routing responses to load, and completion, are working assumptions. A static DAG suppresses feedback: prior routing and completions alter later queue state and arrival load; a `Request Info` loop can create a later queue wait. A 50% reassignment therefore creates interference and capacity effects.

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests

| rank | hypothesis | process mechanism | evidence supporting it | evidence contradicting it | discriminating analysis or falsification test | predicted observation |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Orion has a beneficial routing-pool effect that is masked by case mix | Certified reviewers and/or better Orion operational handling reduce waits and service time for comparable work | Orion is faster in Low, Medium, and High strata; pooled-complexity standardized contrast is 2.51 h; reported waits are lower within each complexity stratum | Orion is overwhelmingly night/high-load; each cross-group stratum has weak overlap, especially Low Orion and High Nova | Restrict to cases eligible for either group; block or match on complexity, shift, arrival load, compliance requirement, and assignment-time queue state. Then run a constrained randomized routing pilot. A negative OrionNova intention-to-treat contrast favors this hypothesis; a null/positive contrast weakens it | Within common-support blocks, Orion-assigned cases have lower pre-review waits and no worse service, rework, or validated quality |
| 2 | Orion genuinely causes longer completion through congestion or slower route-specific work | Orion’s night queue, specialized review procedures, or overloaded capacity produces excess wait and possibly service delay | Crude Orion cycle time is +6.72 h; crude reported wait is +4.01 h higher; Orion handles most night/high-load work | Orion is faster in every complexity stratum, including lower observed waits within strata | Randomize eligible cases between groups within the same shift and contemporaneous queue-state block, while holding certification eligibility fixed. A persistent positive Orion effect on wait/cycle time favors harm; reversal or disappearance weakens it | Orion retains longer wait and cycle time after balanced assignment, especially during comparable high-load night periods |
| 3 | Shift and arrival-load composition, not group, drive the aggregate difference | Night staffing, demand peaks, and accumulated queues lengthen time before review; Orion is the night-work receiver | Night average is 21.98 h versus day 13.98 h; Orion is 92.9% night and has many high-load cases | Within-complexity Orion waits are lower despite its unfavorable night/high-load mix; however, support is thin | Reconstruct queue state at classification from intake, assignment, service-start, and completion events. Use shift-week switchbacks or stepped-wedge staffing changes. | 4 | Compliance handling or rework is a genuine group-mediated pathway | Group assignment changes compliance-queue handling, information requests, or repeat-review burden | Compliance-required share is 64.3% for Orion versus 25.0% for Nova; rework is 28.6% versus 18.8%; Orion has more compliance/rework variants | Compliance is policy-required largely by complexity; High cases all require compliance; review service already includes repeat-review time | Collect compliance start/end and Request Info timestamps in a randomized or strongly balanced routing pilot. | 5 | The true group effect is near null; residual unobserved case risk drives both routing and duration | Routing engine sends cases with hidden severity, evidence gaps, claimant risk, or urgency to Orion; coarse complexity does not capture them | Operational fact says Orion is usually selected for difficult cases; high complexity and compliance are strongly imbalanced | The within-complexity direction consistently favors Orion, so hidden risk would need to remain materially different after coarse stratification | Add pre-routing risk variables: claim type, injury/coverage features, document completeness, urgency, fraud flags, channel, and routing rationale. Attenuation of the adjusted contrast toward zero supports this hypothesis | Rich risk adjustment or plausible sensitivity parameters move the OrionNova contrast from negative toward approximately zero |
| 6 | Completed-case selection and measurement obscure the operational effect | Group capacity may affect whether cases remain open, are abandoned, or are observed by the extract date; the wait field mixes queueing and compliance delay | Only completed cases are available; no abandoned/still-open cases are known; 30-day quality is coarse and post-process affected | No direct evidence indicates differential abandonment or censoring, only that it is unmeasured | Build an all-intake cohort with assignment date, closure/censoring status, and reason for non-completion. Compare completion probability and use time-to-close analysis with appropriate censoring treatment. A changed contrast after inclusion favors selection bias | Orion and Nova differ in still-open/abandonment rates or the group cycle-time contrast changes materially when all assigned cases are followed |

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Instrument and define eligibility | Do not bulk reassign. Capture routing rationale, assignment-time queue state, actual reviewer, compliance start/end, review start/end, Request Info, and closure/censoring status | Measurement of case mix, reviewer mechanism, queue mechanism, and completed-case selection | All incoming cases and operational shifts | Preserve current routing for cases requiring specialized certification; validate timestamps against work systems | Variant frequencies; directly-follows relations; queue-entry-to-review-start time; compliance duration; repeat-review loops; completion rates | Establishes common-support population and separates reported waiting from actual process segments; does not itself identify a treatment effect | Pause data-driven routing changes if required-compliance conformance cannot be measured or data capture is materially incomplete |
| 1. Queue/compliance capacity experiment | Add or re-time night compliance capacity, such as a protected compliance lane or scheduled compliance reviewer coverage, using a stepped-wedge shift-week rollout | Compliance-related queueing and night backlog, without changing group label | Shift-week or compliance-queue block | Required checks remain mandatory; retain escalation path for high-risk cases; cap workload per reviewer | Compliance conformance; time from classification to compliance completion; pre-review queue p50/p90; variant changes; cycle time by complexity and shift | Tests whether a concrete bottleneck, rather than group label, is responsible for longer cycles | Roll back immediately for missed required checks, capacity safety breach, or a pre-agreed worsening of p90 wait or quality guardrails |
| 2. Small blocked randomized routing pilot | Route a small capped share of cases eligible for either group by random assignment at `Classify`, blocked by complexity, shift, arrival load, and compliance requirement | Total effect of routing to the Orion versus Nova operational pool | Case, within contemporaneous eligibility and queue-state blocks | Exclude cases requiring capabilities unavailable in one group; maintain staffing caps; retain override with documented reason; analyze intention-to-treat | Per-group DFGs and variants; queue wait; service time; Request Info/rework; time to compliance; all-intake completion; validated quality follow-up | Provides the cleanest estimate available of the short-run total routing effect in the eligible population | Suspend the affected block for policy violations, unstable queue growth, or crossing pre-specified noninferiority limits for cycle-time tail risk, quality, or completion |
| 3. Controlled scale decision | If Phase 2 is safe, use randomized shift-block switchbacks or a phased 10–25% expansion rather than an immediate 50% move | System-level interference, capacity displacement, and transportability of pilot findings | Shift-block or service-week | Maintain reserve capacity; pre-register analysis; monitor Nova capacity and high-complexity routing separately | Queue length trajectories; work-in-progress; completion hazard; cycle p50/p90; variant/conformance drift; rework and appeal outcomes | Tests equilibrium effects that a case-level pilot may miss because assignments change each group’s queue | Revert to prior routing if Nova queue growth, tail cycle time, compliance delay, or validated quality breaches agreed operational thresholds |

**Recommendation.** Do not approve a blanket 50% Orion-to-Nova reassignment based on the 6.72-hour crude difference. The observed complexity-standardized direction is the opposite: Orion is 2.51 hours faster under pooled complexity weighting, although that is still not causal evidence because shift, load, hidden case risk, and selection into completed cases remain unresolved. Treat the group label as a routing-policy bundle, not as an isolated employee-performance variable.

First address observable bottlenecks, especially night compliance and pre-review queue capacity, because this can improve service regardless of the eventual group verdict. In parallel, instrument the process and run a small constrained randomized routing pilot only among cases genuinely eligible for either group. Evaluate all intake cases, not merely completed cases, and report total cycle effects separately from mediator changes in queueing, service, and rework. Expand only if the pilot and subsequent shift-block test show no deterioration in tail waits, compliance conformance, completion, or validated quality.

print("\n=== Trace variants by group ===")
variant_compare = (
    df.groupby(["trace", "resource_group"])
    .size()
    .unstack(fill_value=0)
    .reindex(columns=groups, fill_value=0)
)
print(variant_compare.to_string())

for trace, row in variant_compare.iterrows():
    if row.min() < 3:
        warning(
            f"Trace variant has weak cross-group support: {trace} "
            f"(Orion={row['Orion']}, Nova={row['Nova']})."
        )

# 6. PM4Py directly-follows comparisons.
# No timestamps are fabricated: these are control-flow DFGs only, not performance DFGs.
def parse_group_event_log(frame):
    trace_string = "\n".join(
        frame["trace"].str.replace(">", ",", regex=False).tolist()
    )
    return pm4py.parse_event_log_string(trace_string)


dfg_by_group = {}
for group in groups:
    event_log = parse_group_event_log(df.loc[df["resource_group"] == group])
    dfg_result = pm4py.discover_dfg(event_log)

    # PM4Py commonly returns (dfg, start_activities, end_activities).
    # This also tolerates releases that return only the DFG mapping.
    dfg = dfg_result[0] if isinstance(dfg_result, tuple) else dfg_result
    dfg_by_group[group] = dict(dfg)

all_edges = sorted(set(dfg_by_group["Orion"]) | set(dfg_by_group["Nova"]))
edge_compare = pd.DataFrame(
    [
        {
            "directly_follows": f"{source} -> {target}",
            "Orion": dfg_by_group["Orion"].get((source, target), 0),
            "Nova": dfg_by_group["Nova"].get((source, target), 0),
        }
        for source, target in all_edges
    ]
)

print("\n=== PM4Py directly-follows graph comparison ===")
print(edge_compare.to_string(index=False))

for _, row in edge_compare.iterrows():
    if min(row["Orion"], row["Nova"]) < 3:
        warning(
            f"DFG edge comparison is weakly supported: {row['directly_follows']} "
            f"(Orion={row['Orion']}, Nova={row['Nova']})."
        )

print(
    "\nDiagnostic conclusion: observed reversals and DFG differences are useful "
    "for hypothesis generation, but poor overlap, unobserved case risk, "
    "interference through queues, and completed-case selection prevent a causal verdict."
)

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Residual case severity within Low/Medium/High categories.** Most valuable addition: a pre-routing granular case-risk and document-completeness score captured at `Classify`.

2. **Whether the apparent group effect is actually an individual reviewer effect.** Most valuable addition: actual reviewer identifier at assignment, linked to certification and experience level.

3. **Whether reported waiting is caused by queues, compliance handling, or another unmeasured stage.** Most valuable addition: an event-level timestamped process log covering queue entry, compliance start/end, review start/end, and rework.

4. **Whether restriction to completed cases changes the comparison.** Most valuable design change: follow a full intake cohort through closure, abandonment, or censoring rather than analyzing completed cases only.

5. **The counterfactual effect of changing routing while preserving safe capability coverage.** Most valuable design change: a blocked randomized routing pilot among cases eligible for either group, with documented override reasons.
