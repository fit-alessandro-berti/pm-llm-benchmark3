## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

**Rule Classification**
- **Hard invariants**: OCEL schema constraints (events/objects must exist before linking); O2O directionality (`carries`, `packs`, `bills`, `settles` follow business semantics); cardinality ceilings per activity (e.g., `Create Order`  exactly 1 Order).
- **Strong defaults**: Lifecycle ordering (Pack  Dispatch  Confirm); 1:1 or 1:N cardinality expectations per domain semantics; scanner-outage reconciliation rule for `Pack Item`; bulk-dispatch allowance for `Dispatch Package`.
- **Weak heuristics**: Identifier prefix matching (`N-42*`  `N-420`/`N-421`); route hint alignment (`NORTH`  `NORTH`); timestamp proximity; arithmetic summation (`130+100=230`).
- **Evidence against a repair**: Lifecycle inversion (e.g., confirming delivery before dispatch); cardinality violation without explicit bulk exception; cross-object-type mismatch (e.g., linking a Payment to an Order directly); arithmetic equality treated as proof rather than corroboration.

**Evidence Combination Strategy**
I prioritize evidence hierarchically: (1) Lifecycle order and cardinality constraints override attribute matches; (2) Explicit O2O relations anchor E2O inferences; (3) Identifier and route matching provide directional priors; (4) Arithmetic equality is treated as weak corroborative evidence, never decisive, because fees, taxes, discounts, or partial payments can break exact sums. Uncertainty is calibrated by assigning confidence scores that reflect the strength of the highest-tier evidence available. When E2O and O2O evidence are mutually reinforcing without circular dependency, confidence increases; when an O2O edge is inferred solely from a guessed E2O edge, confidence is capped and flagged as provisional.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Forcing ev08PK-8 creates false multi-item packaging patterns**: If PK-8 actually contains only IT-A2, adding IT-B1 inflates package consolidation metrics and misleads warehouse capacity planning.
2. **Linking INV-44ORD-B via arithmetic equality fabricates cross-order billing**: Exact sums often mask fees, taxes, or partial invoicing. Treating this as proof introduces a false billing variant that distorts financial conformance and cycle-time analysis.
3. **Linking ev11DEL-Y creates artificial cross-order confirmation**: DEL-Y carries an item from ORD-B. Confirming it under ORD-A suggests a portal bug or process deviation that may not exist, skewing delivery success rates and customer satisfaction metrics.
4. **Linking PAY-2INV-44 without explicit audit trail assumes automatic cash application**: Unapplied or pending payments are common. Forcing the link hides payment processing delays and creates false settlement completeness in the OTG.
5. **Deriving O2O edges from weak E2O guesses pollutes the object network**: If provisional E2O relations are later rejected, the dependent O2O edges remain in cached models, causing persistent spurious edges in OC-DFG/OTG and misleading variant clustering downstream.
