## 1. Executive causal assessment

Provisional verdict: **mainly confounded** — for the specific claim management is acting on ("Orion causes longer cycle times"). Confidence: **70**. The complementary claim that Orion is genuinely *faster* is a different estimand and is **not identifiable from the supplied evidence**.

Strongest evidence for the verdict: the crude gap (Orion 21.96 h vs Nova 15.24 h, +6.72 h) reverses in every observed complexity stratum (High −3.43 h, Medium −2.21 h, Low −2.14 h, all favouring Orion). Standardizing to the pooled complexity mix gives Orion 17.14 h vs Nova 19.65 h, a −2.5 h contrast. Orion carries 42.9% high-complexity, 64.3% compliance-required and 92.9% night/high-load work versus Nova's 12.5%/25.0%/25.0%. That is textbook Simpson reversal driven by the routing rule itself.

Strongest evidence against: stratification is not identification. Complexity is a coarse three-level proxy for unobserved case risk, and the routing engine plausibly selects on risk *within* level, so residual confounding runs against Orion. Overlap is also fatal: no complexity×shift cell has ≥3 cases in both groups, Orion has zero Medium-Day and High-Day cases, and shift, load and group are nearly collinear. The within-stratum advantage sits almost entirely in `wait_before_review_h`, not service time — a queue/capacity signal, not a skill signal.

The 50% reassignment may therefore backfire: it moves hard, night, compliance-heavy cases to the group with fewer certified reviewers and thinner night cover, lengthening waits and possibly raising rework and appeals, while the expected "improvement" is largely an artefact of remixing.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Instrument before acting (2–3 weeks)** | Do not change routing. Add step-level timestamps (start/complete per activity), reviewer ID, queue-entry time, WIP counters, compliance-desk queue, and an open/abandoned-case extract | Measurement of `queue_wait` vs `review_service`, and of `case_completed` selection | None (observational) | Zero operational risk; freeze the 50% proposal pending Phase 1 | Split `wait_before_review_h` into pure queue vs compliance wait; DFG and variant comparison by group; conformance against the reference model; censoring rate by group | Establishes whether the reversal in §4 survives correct measurement, and tests H6 and H4 before any exposure | Abort the whole programme if the negative-control step (Register→Classify) differs by group — the log is not comparable and no routing decision should be based on it |
| **1. Randomized routing pilot on the overlap region (6–8 weeks)** | For the *eligible-both* subset only (cases the routing engine scores as assignable to either group — realistically Medium complexity, non-urgent), randomize group 50/50 with stratification on complexity × shift × compliance_required | The direct effect of `resource_group` at fixed case mix | Case, randomized at the Classify→routing decision point | Cap at ~15–20% of eligible volume; automatic override back to Orion for any case flagged high-risk by the routing engine; clinical/legal escalation path unchanged | Per-arm DFG and variant frequencies; wait vs service decomposition; rework rate; 30-day appeal rate as a guardrail; positivity/overlap report per stratum | This is the only design here that identifies a group effect. It directly discriminates H1/H3 from H4 and removes reliance on complexity as a proxy for risk | Stop if the randomized arm's 30-day appeal rate exceeds baseline by a pre-registered margin, if wait in either arm exceeds SLA for two consecutive weeks, or if enrolment cannot fill the strata (report non-identifiability rather than force an answer) |
| **2. Capacity intervention on the queue, not the label (4–6 weeks)** | Add compliance-desk and night reviewer capacity (e.g. one extra certified reviewer + extended compliance-desk hours) without changing any group assignment | `arrival_load -> queue_wait` and `compliance_performed -> queue_wait` — the mediator that carries 80–90% of the observed contrast | Shift-week (stepped-wedge across night weeks), interrupted time series | Additive capacity only; no case is reassigned, so case-mix and skill matching are untouched | Wait decomposition before/after; WIP and queue-length curves; bottleneck localisation on the DFG (Classify→Compliance Check and Compliance Check→Review edges) | Tests H2 and H5 with an intervention that cannot backfire through skill mismatch; if wait falls sharply, the "group problem" was a capacity problem | Roll back if wait does not improve within two wedge steps, or if the added capacity displaces day-shift service levels |
| **3. Conditional, monitored rebalancing (only if warranted)** | If and only if Phase 1 shows no Orion advantage in the overlap region, shift load gradually — 10%, then 20% — of *eligible-both* cases to Nova, never High-complexity or compliance-required cases | The total effect of the routing policy under realistic capacity | Case, within eligible-both stratum | Hard exclusion of High complexity and compliance-required cases; weekly review; certified-reviewer coverage floor for Nova nights | Continuous cycle-time, rework and appeal monitoring by arm; variant drift detection; conformance checking for new deviant paths | Estimates the dose-response of the policy change rather than assuming linearity from a 50% jump | Revert one step whenever median cycle time, rework rate or appeal rate worsens for two consecutive weeks |

**Final recommendation.** Do not move 50% of Orion's work. The +6.72 h crude gap that motivates the proposal reverses to roughly −2.5 h once complexity is standardized, so the arithmetic supporting the decision is a Simpson artefact: Orion is slower in aggregate because it is *given* the hard, night, compliance-heavy cases, and it handles each stratum at least as fast as Nova. Reassigning half of that work would move difficult night cases to the group with fewer certified reviewers and thinner night cover — the most likely outcome is longer waits and more rework, with cycle time improving only because the *reported* Orion mean is remixed.

But do not invert the decision either. The stratified advantage is not identified: complexity is a three-level proxy for the risk the router actually sees, no complexity×shift cell has joint support, and only completed cases are observed. Instead: instrument step-level timing and censoring first (Phase 0), run a small stratified randomized routing pilot in the genuine overlap region (Phase 1), and attack the queue and compliance capacity that carry 80–90% of the measured difference (Phase 2). Rebalance only if the pilot says so.

              f"| wait explains {share:5.1f}% of the gap")
        for g in ["Orion", "Nova"]:

            if n < MIN_CELL:
                warn(f"{cx}/{g} has only {n} case(s) - not a stratum estimate.")
print("\nIf the crude sign and every stratified sign disagree, this is a "
      "Simpson reversal. It shows the crude gap is largely case mix; it does "
      "NOT identify a causal effect, because complexity is a coarse proxy for "
      "the unobserved case risk the routing engine conditions on.\n")

# --------------------------------- 3. complexity-standardized contrast
print("=" * 72)
print("3. COMPLEXITY-STANDARDIZED contrast")
print("=" * 72)
# Common weighting distribution, stated explicitly: the pooled (both-group)
# complexity distribution of the 30 completed cases. This is a 'total
# population' standardization; the Orion-only or Nova-only mix would give
# different (ATT / ATU-style) numbers and is reported alongside.
weight_sets = {
    "pooled (all 30 cases)": df["complexity"].value_counts(normalize=True),
    "Orion mix (ATT-like)": df.loc[df.group == "Orion", "complexity"].value_counts(normalize=True),
    "Nova mix (ATU-like)": df.loc[df.group == "Nova", "complexity"].value_counts(normalize=True),

cell_mean = df.groupby(["group", "complexity"])["cycle_time_h"].mean()
cell_n = df.groupby(["group", "complexity"])["cycle_time_h"].size()

for label, w in weight_sets.items():
    w = w / w.sum()
    std = {}
    for g in ["Orion", "Nova"]:
        tot, wsum, missing = 0.0, 0.0, []
        for cx, wt in w.items():
            if (g, cx) in cell_mean.index:
                tot += wt * cell_mean[(g, cx)]
                wsum += wt
            else:
                missing.append(cx)
        std[g] = tot / wsum if wsum else float("nan")
        if missing:
            warn(f"{g} has no cases in stratum(s) {missing} -> standardization "
                 f"extrapolates beyond support (positivity violation).")
    print(f"  weights = {label}")
    print(f"    Orion {std['Orion']:.2f} h | Nova {std['Nova']:.2f} h "
          f"| Orion - Nova {std['Orion'] - std['Nova']:+.2f} h")
print("\nStandardization removes only the OBSERVED complexity imbalance. It "
      "does not address shift, arrival load, unobserved case risk, or "
      "completion-based selection.\n")

print("=" * 72)
print("4. OVERLAP DIAGNOSTICS: complexity x shift x group")

full_index = pd.MultiIndex.from_product(

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Residual confounding by unobserved case risk within complexity level.** The routing engine sees a richer risk signal than the three-level `complexity` field, so Orion's within-stratum advantage may be understated *or* the reversal may be spurious. *Most valuable addition:* the routing engine's own case-level risk score and input features at the moment of assignment — this converts an unmeasured confounder into a measured one and makes propensity-score adjustment meaningful.

2. **Selection into the completed-case sample.** With only closed cases, we cannot tell whether Orion's compressed upper tail (max 33.5 h vs Nova's 36.0 h) reflects speed or truncation of its slowest work. *Most valuable addition:* an extract containing open and abandoned cases with their current age, enabling right-censored time-to-close analysis instead of a completed-only mean.

3. **Non-separability of queue time and compliance wait inside `wait_before_review_h`.** The single field carries 80–90% of every within-stratum contrast, yet we cannot say whether it is generic queueing (a capacity problem) or the compliance step (a policy problem) — and these imply opposite interventions. *Most valuable addition:* start/complete timestamps for every activity, especially Compliance Check enqueue and start.

4. **Collinearity of resource group with shift and arrival load.** With Orion 92.9% night and 13/14 high-load, no design in this dataset can separate group identity from operating conditions; the "Orion effect" and the "night effect" are the same column. *Most valuable design change:* the stratified randomized routing pilot in Phase 1, which breaks the group–shift dependence by construction for the eligible-both subset.

5. **Measurement integrity of `review_service_h` and `rework_count`.** Rework cases show essentially the same service time as non-rework cases (C06: 5.9 h with one loop vs C03: 5.9 h with none), which is mechanically implausible and casts doubt on how repeated reviews are attributed. *Most valuable addition:* per-touch review durations with reviewer ID, so each Review occurrence is timed separately rather than aggregated into one case-level field.
