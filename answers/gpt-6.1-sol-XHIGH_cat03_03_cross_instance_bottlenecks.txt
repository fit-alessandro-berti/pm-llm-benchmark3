## 1. Interaction-graph description

Use a typed graph containing Job, Component, ComponentLot, Machine, Tool, Batch, Shipment, and Event nodes. Preserve directed, qualified O2O relations separately from event–object incidence. An interaction edge derived from two objects sharing an event establishes participation, not causation, exclusivity, or a precedence constraint.

Compact adjacency list:

- `Ji --requires--> Ci`, for i = 1,…,6.
- `L7 --contains--> {C2,C5}`; `L8 --contains--> {C1,C3}`.
- `B1 --batches--> {J1,J2,J3}`; `B2 --batches--> {J4,J5}`.
- `S1 --consolidates--> {J1,J4}`; `S2 --> {J2,J3}`; `S3 --> {J5,J6}`, with the same qualifier.
- `Batch Cure@12:05 --participation--> {B1,OVEN-1,RACK-7,J1,J2,J3,C1,C2,C3}`.
- `Machine Process(J4)@11:45 --participation--> {J4,C4,CNC-2,FIX-A}`.
- `Final Test(J5/J6) --requires/uses--> {TEST-1,CAL-3}`.

The listed O2O relations and supplied event associations are observations. J3’s FIX-A ownership interval is reconstructed from acquire/release observations. Edges such as “J3 blocks J4,” “J5 gates B2,” and “J4 delays J1 through S1” are inferred dependencies requiring resource or policy assumptions. The stated lot-event associations with J2/J5 are semantically relevant but inconsistently materialized as direct source links; distinguish source incidence from integration-derived links.

Higher-order motifs include:

1. **Batch barrier:** Job–Component–Batch–Machine–Tool links join three B1 jobs at one cure event.
2. **Lot coupling:** ComponentLot–Component–Job–Batch links connect L7’s C2/J2 in B1 with C5/J5 in B2.
3. **Cross-batch consolidation:** Batch–Job–Shipment connects B1/J1 to B2/J4 through S1.
4. **Resource-mediated propagation:** Job–Tool–Machine–Batch connects J3’s FIX-A hold to J4’s processing and B2 eligibility.

L8 membership alone supplies no evidence of a release barrier. Reversed ComponentLot–Job and Job–ComponentLot counts should not be added as independent dependencies.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

These are observed intervals and conditional counterfactuals, not proven causal effects. Overlapping constraints cannot be added as independent delay contributions.

1. **B1 readiness-to-start:** J1 waits 12:05 − 08:10 = **235 minutes**; J2 waits 12:05 − 08:25 = **220 minutes**; J3 waits **10 minutes**. The total is **465 job-minutes**, not 465 minutes of oven occupation. Calling these waits avoidable assumes readiness is valid, independent or smaller-batch curing is feasible, and oven/rack capacity is available. J2’s later quarantine particularly complicates that assumption.

2. **B1 synchronization versus launch lag:** The last member is ready at 11:55. J1’s wait decomposes into **225 + 10 minutes**; J2’s into **210 + 10 minutes**. Under an unchanged all-members-ready policy and immediate launch at 11:55, only ten minutes per job disappear. Removing the earlier 225/210 minutes requires changing grouping or advancing J3’s readiness, with other gates held feasible.

3. **FIX-A exposure for J4:** J3 holds the fixture for 11:40 − 09:00 = **160 minutes**. J4 arrives at 09:50, **110 minutes** before release, and starts five minutes afterward: **115 minutes** from arrival to processing. CNC-2’s 10:05–10:47 idle interval contributes **42 minutes** of observed overlap with fixture occupation. Attributing the 110 minutes to FIX-A assumes J4 was otherwise ready and the fixture was exclusive. Starting at 10:05 instead would advance processing **100 minutes**, but requires machine availability that the supplied partial idle interval does not establish.

4. **B2 intersecting gates:** J4 waits 14:50 − 13:10 = **100 minutes**; J5 waits **10 minutes**. Rack quarantine lasts another **85 minutes** after J4 becomes ready, ending at 14:35. With all-member synchronization, the earliest supplied feasible gate is `max(13:10,14:40,14:35) = 14:40`, leaving ten minutes of launch lag. Assuming no other prerequisites, removing rack quarantine alone saves **zero minutes** because J5 remains later. Oven idleness totals **115 minutes**, of which **100** overlap rack quarantine; overlap does not establish sole attribution.

5. **L7 hold and override:** Quarantine lasts 13:50 − 10:20 = **210 minutes**. J5 becomes batch-ready **50 minutes** after release, so 210 minutes is not an identified J5 delay. If L7 release were mandatory for B1 entry and J2’s override were unavailable, B1 could not start before 13:50: at least **105 minutes** later than 12:05. This counterfactual assumes the rule applies at batch entry and that no further gate delays launch.

6. **CAL-3 and J6:** Readiness-to-test is 17:25 − 16:05 = **80 minutes**, decomposed as **30** until J5 finishes, **45** recalibration, and **5** afterward. With one test station, no recalibration, and immediate turnover, J6 could start at 16:35, saving **50 minutes**. Retaining a normal five-minute turnover gives a 16:40 start and **45 minutes** saved. Both assume CAL-3 availability is the remaining constraint.

7. **S1 and J1:** Pack-to-dispatch is 19:25 − 13:30 = **355 minutes**. Waiting until J4 is packed accounts for **340 minutes**; the remaining **15** follow last-job readiness. If J1 could ship separately with the same fifteen-minute preparation, departure at 13:45 saves **340 minutes**. That assumes transport availability and permission to split S1. S2/S3’s 25/35-minute final preparation intervals do not reveal their early members’ waits.

## 4. What flattening misses

- **Job:** Shows lifecycle order, rework, and pack-to-dispatch dwell when events are correctly linked. It hides shared ownership and omits source-unlinked lot events. B1’s single cure may appear in three traces. Summing projected processing durations can triple-count shared work. Medians and the absence of a 1.8-standard-deviation outlier cannot rule out common delays affecting several jobs.
- **Machine:** Shows processing sequences and recorded utilization. It hides jobs waiting without machine events, tool unavailability, and cohort readiness. The misleading conclusion is “44% oven utilization implies adequate effective capacity.” OVEN-1’s 115-minute idle interval also exceeds the reported 73-minute event gap: intervening events or differing windows/definitions require reconciliation.
- **Batch:** Shows load/cure/unload and cohort membership if retained. It hides individual readiness unless linked, component holds, and shipment consequences. Two apparently smooth batch traces can conceal 465 job-minutes of B1 readiness waiting. Projecting shared events across members can inflate counts elsewhere.
- **Tool:** Shows acquire/release, cleaning, calibration, and reconstructed holds. It hides unmet requests without request events and competing machine constraints. A normal tool DFG does not establish adequate availability; short recalibration edges can block an otherwise idle station.
- **ComponentLot:** Shows quarantine/release and affected components when linked. It hides job-specific readiness and override semantics, and expansion through components can duplicate a single hold. “L7 release precedes every affected process” is false for J2; applying 210 minutes to both jobs is unjustified.
- **Shipment:** Shows creation/loading/dispatch. It hides packed-job readiness and earlier shared-resource constraints. The 0.3-hour Load→Dispatch median measures final handling, not J1’s 355-minute dwell. Projecting one dispatch to multiple jobs is valid for job completion counts but not independent shipment throughput.

Low utilization coexists with long waiting because work needs simultaneous access to components, tools, machines, and permitted partners. Missing any prerequisite can idle a machine while jobs remain blocked. Aggregate utilization also conceals short busy periods.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**J3 → FIX-A → CNC-2/J4 → B2/OVEN-1/RACK-7 → S1/J1.** Temporal precedence: J3 holds FIX-A until 11:40; J4 starts machining at 11:45, becomes batch-ready at 13:10, joins B2 at 14:50, and is packed at 19:10. Resource feasibility: J4’s process explicitly requires FIX-A; some CNC idle time overlaps its occupation. Policy: B2 combines J4/J5 and S1 combines J1/J4. Causal speculation: releasing FIX-A earlier might advance J4, but J5’s 14:40 readiness could absorb the gain. An earlier machining start therefore need not advance S1.

**L7 → C5/J5 → B2/OVEN-1/RACK-7 → CAL-3/TEST-1 → J6/S3.** Temporal precedence: release at 13:50 precedes J5’s 14:40 batch readiness and 16:10–16:35 final test; CAL-3 recalibration then precedes J6’s 17:25 start. Resource feasibility: B2 needs the rack and oven; both tests need CAL-3. Policy: J5 observes lot release, whereas C2/J2 has an override; S3 consolidates J5/J6. Causal speculation: advancing J5 could move recalibration earlier and help J6, or move downtime into another demand window. S3 improvement cannot be quantified without packing times.

**L8 → C1/J1 and C3/J3 → B1/OVEN-1/RACK-7 → S1/J4.** Temporal precedence: J1 is ready at 08:10, J3 at 11:55, cure starts 12:05, and J1 is packed 13:30 before S1 dispatches 19:25. Resource feasibility: earlier curing requires oven/rack availability and valid components. Policy: cohort completion and shipment consolidation create successive barriers. Causal speculation: accelerating J3 could advance J1 packing while leaving dispatch unchanged; measured Pack→Dispatch waiting could consequently increase. Nothing establishes L8 as the reason J3 is late.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

Evaluate by matched or randomized day/resource blocks: these connected jobs are unsuitable as independent treatment/control units. Measure all affected jobs’ end-to-end times, quality, service, cost, and shared-resource workload, avoiding double-counted service and overlapping waits.

1. **Flexible batch grouping or smaller compatible batches.** Target synchronization; potentially release J1/J2 earlier. Additional oven/rack cycles can delay J3, B2, J4/J5 and their shipments. Costs include setups, cleaning, energy and thermal-process qualification. Maximizing batch fill or oven utilization incentivizes waiting. Compare readiness-to-cure and release-to-dispatch across linked batches and shipments, recording actual availability; distinguish B1 improvement from downstream absorption.

2. **Tool availability portfolio.** Add qualified FIX-A/CAL-3 alternatives, shorten validated rack cleaning, or schedule calibration around demand. J4/J6 and potentially B2 benefit; displaced cleaning/calibration can harm other jobs, and added throughput can overload OVEN-1 or shipments. Costs include capital, qualification and maintenance. Minimizing calibration downtime encourages unsafe postponement. Evaluate request-to-grant and ready-to-start intervals across Job–Tool–Machine links, plus quality and downstream queues. Test rack changes separately: J5 may still gate B2.

3. **Split S1 or set a maximum consolidation wait.** Target shipment policy; J1 could depart around 13:45 under the stated fifteen-minute preparation counterfactual. Extra loads can consume carrier capacity needed by J4/S2/S3. Costs include freight, handling and partial-delivery complexity. Maximizing truck fill encourages holding early jobs; minimizing dispatch delay can encourage excessive splitting. Compare job-level delivery/service outcomes and cost across shipment partners, retaining customer windows and carrier constraints.

4. **Targeted, traceable lot release and explicit overrides.** Target L7 coupling through faster disposition or evidence-supported component-level release. J5/B2 may improve; J2’s existing override means benefits differ. Premature release could harm product quality and trigger rework across both batches. Costs include testing and traceability. Short quarantine duration rewards weak release decisions. Evaluate matched lot cohorts using hold eligibility, component disposition, overrides, processing times and subsequent quality outcomes.

5. **Coordinate scheduling across shipment partners and resource gates.** Prioritize feasible work that advances the latest member of a batch or shipment. Advancing J4 could help S1, but advancing J5 may be necessary first. J2/J3/S2 or J6 can lose priority and starve. Scheduling complexity and fairness are risks. Optimizing only S1 lateness hides displaced delay. Compare full-cohort completion distributions with recorded priorities and alternative feasible schedules, including lateness tails and resource constraints.

6. **Repair event integration and lifecycle logging.** Preserve source IDs, direct versus derived links, qualifiers, tool requests, readiness, state transitions, and J2’s override. This helps diagnosis for L7/J2/J5 and all shared-resource users; it yields no automatic physical acceleration. Operators may bear logging overhead; incorrect enrichment can harm planning. Event-count targets encourage duplicates. Reprocess the same period under both pipelines, audit against source records, and compare unique-event, object-traversal and waiting measures before attributing any change to operations.

## 7. PM4Py analysis outline

The outline retains structural relations separately, compares event-couple frequency with object traversal and distinct-object frequency, and inspects all six projections. Deterministic timestamp tie-breaking is computational only. Readiness/hold evidence must support waiting attribution; machine gaps alone never supply it. Missing source links or readiness records remain missing information.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Existing OCEL: no external data retrieval.
interaction_summary = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)
object_graph = pm4py.discover_objects_graph(
    ocel, graph_type="object_interaction"
)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
features = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(ocel, "Job")
print(interaction_summary)
print("OTG:", otg)
print("ETOT:", etot)
print("Feature output:", type(features).__name__)

# Co-event graphs do not replace qualified, structural O2O relations.
o2o = getattr(ocel, "o2o", None)
if o2o is not None:
    print("Qualified O2O sample:", o2o.head())

# Standard OCEL column attributes; preserve each event's original identity.
eid = ocel.event_id_column
aid = getattr(ocel, "event_activity", "ocel:activity")
ts = getattr(ocel, "event_timestamp", "ocel:timestamp")
oid = ocel.object_id_column
otype = ocel.object_type_column

ev = ocel.events[[eid, aid, ts]].rename(
    columns={eid: "eid", aid: "activity", ts: "time"}
)
obj = ocel.objects[[oid, otype]].rename(
    columns={oid: "oid", otype: "object_type"}
)
rel = ocel.relations[[eid, oid]].rename(
    columns={eid: "eid", oid: "oid"}
).drop_duplicates()
seq = rel.merge(ev, on="eid", validate="many_to_one").merge(
    obj, on="oid", validate="many_to_one"
)
seq["time"] = pd.to_datetime(seq["time"], utc=True)
ties = seq.duplicated(["object_type", "oid", "time"], keep=False)
print("Rows with within-object timestamp ties:", int(ties.sum()))
seq = seq.sort_values(["object_type", "oid", "time", "eid"])
groups = seq.groupby(["object_type", "oid"], sort=False)
for col in ("eid", "activity", "time"):
    seq["next_" + col] = groups[col].shift(-1)

# Compare OCDFG-style frequency meanings explicitly, even when the
# discovered model's internal metric representation differs by version.
edges = seq.dropna(subset=["next_eid"]).copy()
edges["event_pair"] = list(zip(edges["eid"], edges["next_eid"]))
frequency_comparison = edges.groupby(
    ["object_type", "activity", "next_activity"]
).agg(
    event_couples=("event_pair", "nunique"),
    unique_objects=("oid", "nunique"),
    object_traversals=("oid", "size"),
).reset_index()
print(frequency_comparison)
# A shared event pair can produce several object traversals but only
# one event couple. Neither count is a waiting-duration measurement.

performance_views = {}
available_types = set(obj["object_type"])
for typ in ("Job", "Machine", "Batch", "Tool", "ComponentLot", "Shipment"):
    if typ not in available_types:
        continue
    flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, typ)
    if len(flat):
        perf, starts, ends = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(flat)
        performance_views[typ] = (perf, starts, ends)
        print(typ, "performance DFG:", perf)

# Inspect evidence by event ID so one lot hold/cure/dispatch remains
# one occurrence even when it participates in multiple object traces.
needed = {
    "Ready for Batch", "Ready for Final Test", "Acquire Tool",
    "Release Tool", "Quarantine Lot", "Release Lot", "Supervisor Override",
    "Batch Cure", "Final Test", "Calibrate", "Recalibrate",
    "Cleaning", "Pack", "Dispatch"
}
lifecycle_evidence = seq[seq["activity"].isin(needed)][
    ["eid", "activity", "time", "oid", "object_type"]
]
lot_ids = set(seq.loc[
    (seq["object_type"] == "ComponentLot") &
    seq["activity"].isin({"Quarantine Lot", "Release Lot"}), "eid"
])
lot_participation = seq[seq["eid"].isin(lot_ids)][
    ["eid", "activity", "oid", "object_type"]
]
print("Lot-event participation audit:", lot_participation)
print("Readiness/hold/policy evidence:", lifecycle_evidence)
# Next: validate required components and tools, exclusivity, overrides,
# batch membership and shipment policy; join readiness to feasible starts.
# Do not manufacture absent Job links or label machine gaps as waiting.
```

## 8. Falsification and additional data

Observations weakening batch synchronization as the leading explanation, its causal attribution, or its avoidability include:

1. J1’s 08:10 or J2’s 08:25 readiness is provisional; required work remains incomplete.
2. The 12:05 cure timestamp represents completion or scheduled time rather than actual start.
3. OVEN-1 or RACK-7 is unavailable until 12:05 independently of J3.
4. A fixed noon release rule governs B1 regardless of member readiness.
5. Comparable batches with earlier last-member readiness still start equally late after controlling for resources.
6. Feasible smaller-batch trials leave end-to-end waiting unchanged, while shipment/tool interventions consistently reduce it; this weakens the ranking even if batch exposure persists.

Discriminating additional data should include:

- Authoritative readiness timestamps, prerequisites, validity changes, and component status at each readiness declaration.
- Tool requests, grants, owners, capacities, exclusive/shared modes, releases, and qualified substitutes.
- Rack cleaning/quarantine and CAL-3 calibration reasons, planned versus actual intervals, and release criteria.
- Batch membership history, capacity/minimum-fill rules, start decisions, and actual load/cure/unload timestamps.
- Lot disposition, component-specific hold applicability, and supervisor override scope, authorization, and effective time.
- Machine processing start/end, availability calendars, downtime reasons, queue demand, and scheduling priorities.
- Per-job packing/loading times, shipment membership changes, cutoffs, split permissions, carrier capacity and delivery windows.
- Source-system IDs, clock alignment, extraction windows, event deduplication, and direct/derived relation provenance, including reconciliation of the 115-minute oven idle interval with the 73-minute event-gap summary.
