# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

**Resilience is not conformity to the ordinary path.** During a genuine disruption, new activities such as emergency triage, manual authorization, an invoice hold, or manual reconciliation may be appropriate even though they reduce fitness against the baseline model. The relevant test is whether the adaptation:

1. preserves a valuable service;
2. preserves the purpose of non-negotiable controls through an equivalent mechanism;
3. creates contemporaneous, attributable evidence; and
4. does not merely transfer an unmeasured burden to later periods or other customer groups.

On that basis, **B, J, and K are the clearest resilience mechanisms**. Manual authorization preserved the authorization decision through another channel. Backlog prioritization and reservation reconciliation are legitimate ways to manage constrained capacity and restore record integrity, provided they are formal and auditable.

**A and I represent service-allocation resilience, but not control exemptions.** The Hospital-critical fast lane and deferral of lower-priority work plausibly explain why hospital fill remained 91% and Hospital-critical delay increased much less than Pharmacy-standard or Wholesale-bulk delay. That is mission-oriented resilience. It becomes concealed noncompliance when priority is used to bypass authorization or applicable product controls. It also becomes operationally questionable when the resulting pharmacy impact—61% fill and a 30-percentage-point hospital–pharmacy fill gap—is not bounded by a service floor or reviewed for fairness.

**D, E, F, and H are conditional alternatives.** A printed snapshot, manual pick list, temporary pharmacist release, and phone POD are not inherently wrong. Their classification depends on timing, scope, and evidence quality:

* The snapshot was defensible immediately after T1 but became progressively less reliable as transactions accumulated.
* A manual pick list is resilient if it captures item, lot, expiry, quantity, actor, and time; it is a hidden traceability gap if these are reconstructed later.
* Temporary release was beneficial for Controlled-substance orders, but sampling cannot substitute for applicable cold-chain or release controls.
* Phone POD may be useful provisional evidence for a low-risk Ambient delivery but is inadequate as the sole evidence for controlled custody or cold-chain receipt.

**C and G are the strongest examples of risky shortcuts.** Skipping authorization converted an unavailable electronic control into no demonstrated control. Dispatching without either a scan or an equivalent offline custody record removed a key control at the moment it was needed. Their apparent throughput benefit should therefore be treated as unsafe throughput until evidence shows otherwise.

**L is necessary recovery work, but it is not proof that the original execution was compliant.** Obtaining a courier record later may repair missing documentation. It cannot retroactively perform a release check, temperature observation, or dual-control handoff that did not occur. Remediation should distinguish:

* evidence that existed contemporaneously but was not yet imported;
* evidence reconstructed from independent records;
* a control known to have occurred but weakly documented; and
* a control that cannot be shown to have occurred.

Only the first category is close to a technical evidence delay. The latter categories are substantive exceptions requiring investigation and possibly corrective action.

The conformance findings reinforce this distinction. A move on log such as `Spreadsheet invoice hold` may be an additional protective activity, while `Skip system authorization check` is a control-removing activity. Conversely, move-on-model deviations involving missing quality/release, temperature, scan, or POD evidence are more concerning because they concern expected controls or traceability. Raw fitness alone cannot make that judgment.

Finally, the aggregate DFG supports plausible explanations but not exact case-level causality. The simultaneous rise in scanless dispatch, phone POD, manual lists, and traceability exceptions strongly suggests a relationship, but the supplied aggregate edges do not prove which adaptation caused each exception. The classifications should therefore be used as risk-based abductive conclusions, followed by case-level linkage in the remediation analysis.

# 4. Recovery process model

The future recovery model should be an **approved incident-specific process**, not an improvised version of the baseline. It should explicitly separate full outage, partial recovery, stabilization, and final compliance recovery.

**Operating modes and trigger conditions**

| Mode                                      | Entry trigger                                                                                                                              | Operating rule                                                                                                                              | Exit condition                                                                                                                                                         |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **M0 — Normal**                           | Core services are available, synchronized, and integrity-validated.                                                                        | Standard electronic process.                                                                                                                | Enter M1 or M2 when availability or integrity triggers are breached.                                                                                                   |
| **M1 — Full manual recovery**             | WMS, authorization, inventory, scanning, or evidence services are unavailable; or the cyber-response team declares the data untrustworthy. | One approved manual system of record is used. Electronic displays are not treated as authoritative merely because they remain accessible.   | Move to M2 only after controlled technical tests show that selected modules can be trusted.                                                                            |
| **M2 — Restricted partial recovery**      | Some services have returned, but inventory synchronization, scanning, or record completeness has not passed end-to-end validation.         | Use explicit dual-record or controlled migration rules. Do not allow teams to choose informally between manual and electronic stock states. | Move to M3 after test orders reconcile across order, inventory, scan, release, dispatch, and evidence systems.                                                         |
| **M3 — Stabilization and reconciliation** | Normal processing is technically available, but fulfillment backlog, manual reservations, or evidence debt remains.                        | New orders may use the standard path; backlog and remediation use separate governed queues.                                                 | Return to M0 only when operational health and defined compliance-recovery thresholds are both met. An open high-risk remediation queue prevents a full “green” status. |

Activation should be triggered by **either availability failure or integrity uncertainty**. Additional restricted-mode triggers should include an inventory snapshot exceeding its approved lifetime, unexplained manual-versus-electronic stock variance, inability to capture an applicable high-risk control, or evidence-capture delays beyond the incident policy.

A BPMN-style representation is:

```text
Receive order
→ Determine operating mode
→ Triage customer criticality, clinical urgency, product risk, control feasibility, and backlog age
→ X {
     Reject invalid order,
     Hold or reroute order that cannot be processed safely,
     Defer eligible lower-priority order,
     Standard electronic fulfillment,
     Approved manual-recovery fulfillment
   }

Approved manual-recovery fulfillment:
→ Offline authorization or signed emergency-whitelist confirmation
→ Manual inventory reservation in the authoritative decrement ledger
→ Pick with item, quantity, lot, expiry, and actor capture
→ Pack with package identifier
→ Applicable controls in parallel:
     ∧ {
         Quality/release check,
         Cold-chain temperature and validated-carrier check, if applicable,
         Controlled-substance dual control and custody check, if applicable,
         Recall/lot-status check, if applicable
       }
→ X {
     Controls complete: release,
     Controls unavailable or failed: compliance hold or reroute
   }
→ Dispatch with offline custody token and courier acknowledgement
→ Signed or independently verifiable proof of delivery
→ Operational delivery complete
→ ∧ {
     Invoice processing or invoice hold,
     Import and reconcile manual records,
     Evidence validation and exception classification
   }
→ X {
     Evidence and reconciliation complete: final close,
     Repairable evidence gap: audit remediation,
     Irreparable or substantive control gap: incident/CAPA escalation
   }
```

Here, `X` means an exclusive choice and `∧` means that all applicable branches must be completed. Product categories can overlap; where they do, the strictest combined controls apply.

**Triage rules**

Triage should first determine **whether an order is safe and lawful to process**, and only then determine its queue priority.

1. **Hard feasibility gate:** An order enters fulfillment only if its applicable minimum controls can be executed. Otherwise it is held or rerouted to a validated alternate warehouse, courier, or control team.
2. **Clinical and customer priority:** Among feasible orders, documented clinical urgency and Hospital-critical status receive highest service priority.
3. **Product-risk routing:** Controlled-substance, Cold-chain, and Recall-sensitive orders receive scarce specialist and evidence-capture capacity, but their risk class does not automatically move them ahead if the required controls are unavailable.
4. **Pharmacy service floor:** Pharmacy-standard work receives a protected minimum capacity allocation so that hospital protection does not produce indefinite pharmacy deprivation.
5. **Wholesale deferral:** Wholesale-bulk work is the most deferrable, but deferral requires a reason code, customer notification, maximum deferral age, and reassessment.
6. **Anti-starvation rule:** Within each eligible tier, age and promised-delivery exposure increase priority. No segment should be perpetually displaced by newly arriving higher-priority work.
7. **Cancellation control:** Incident-related cancellations require a documented cause, approval level, and consideration of partial fulfillment or rerouting.

**Minimum controls by customer and product risk**

| Scope                         | Minimum incident control                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **All orders**                | Unique recovery order ID; authenticated customer and product authorization decision; item and quantity; authoritative inventory reservation; picker and packer identity; release decision; dispatch evidence; delivery evidence; actual timestamps; and exception reason where applicable. |
| **Hospital-critical overlay** | Priority and rapid decision-making, but no waiver of product-specific controls. An emergency approval must identify the clinical basis and approving role.                                                                                                                                 |
| **Ambient**                   | Offline authorization from a versioned source; manual reservation ledger; complete pick/pack/dispatch evidence. A documented risk-based release approach may be used only where the approved continuity policy permits it.                                                                 |
| **Cold-chain**                | Continuous or approved interval temperature evidence, device/logger identifier, pack-out confirmation, dispatch temperature, validated courier handling, and receipt evidence. If temperature capture is unavailable, hold or reroute rather than document later from assumption.          |
| **Controlled-substance**      | Two identified authorized persons for required dual control, quantity reconciliation, secure custody record, package seal or identifier, courier identity and acknowledgement, and strong recipient evidence. A pharmacist check alone does not replace dual control.                      |
| **Recall-sensitive**          | Lot/batch, expiry, recall/quarantine status, source location, destination, and package mapping captured before release. A stale inventory snapshot without a reliable lot-status check is insufficient.                                                                                    |

**Manual evidence capture**

The manual package should use pre-numbered or securely generated recovery IDs and controlled templates. It should record the actual performer and actual event time, not merely the later data-entry time. Required metadata should include:

* authorization source, version, confirmer, and scope;
* inventory snapshot version and age;
* each manual reservation and decrement;
* product, lot, expiry, quantity, and storage condition;
* picker, packer, releaser, and dual-control identities where applicable;
* temperature device and readings;
* package, courier, custody transfer, and recipient evidence;
* correction history and reason codes.

Records should be append-only. Later import must create explicit events such as `Manual event imported`, `Evidence reconstructed`, or `Reservation corrected`; it should not create synthetic backdated scan events that falsely imply that normal scanning occurred.

**Backlog prioritization**

Two queues should be visible and separately staffed:

* a **fulfillment backlog**, ranked by safe-processing feasibility, clinical urgency, customer class, product constraints, promised-delivery exposure, and age;
* a **compliance-remediation backlog**, ranked first by Controlled-substance, Cold-chain, Recall-sensitive, and authorization severity, then by evidence age and customer impact.

Capacity must be reserved for both. Otherwise, new fulfillment work will continuously displace remediation, reproducing the T4 audit tail. Dashboards should show deferred and cancelled orders by customer group, not merely the number of hospital orders protected.

**Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow**

1. Import manual orders, reservations, picks, releases, dispatches, PODs, and invoice holds without overwriting their provenance.
2. Compare the manual ledger with WMS orders, physical stock, lot balances, courier records, POD records, and invoices.
3. Classify discrepancies:

   * **Critical:** missing authorization, controlled-substance control/custody gap, cold-chain temperature gap, recall/lot uncertainty;
   * **Major:** stock mismatch, wrong quantity, missing dispatch linkage, uncertain recipient;
   * **Administrative:** format, duplicate record, or timing correction where independent evidence is complete.
4. Apply immediate containment for critical discrepancies, including inventory hold, customer contact, product assessment, or formal incident escalation where required.
5. Mark each evidence item as contemporaneous, imported, independently reconstructed, or unverified.
6. Allow operational and commercial milestones to be reported separately, but do not mark the recovery case **finally closed** until reconciliation and required evidence validation are complete.
7. Report irreparable missing controls as exceptions. Do not relabel them as successful remediation merely because a note was added later.

Leading indicators should expose unsafe adaptations while management can still intervene. Lagging indicators should measure the eventual operational, customer, and compliance outcome.

A management dashboard should therefore display at least three recovery states separately: **service availability**, **control-complete processing**, and **compliance-debt clearance**.

# 6. Recommendations for future disruptions

| Priority          | Recommendation                                                                                                                                                                                                            |                    Implementation effort | Expected benefit                                                                                                                                     | Residual risk                                                                                                           |

# 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should accept slower processing, explicit deferral of lower-priority bulk work, temporary invoice holds, and the additional cost of manual authorization, evidence capture, and reconciliation when those measures protect hospital supply without removing essential controls. It should not accept assumed authorization, uncontrolled scanless dispatch, missing Cold-chain or Controlled-substance controls, retrospective records presented as contemporaneous evidence, or a declaration of full recovery while material remediation remains open. The recommendation would change only with evidence that an alternative manual control provides demonstrably equivalent authorization, custody, temperature, release, and recall traceability; that the clinical harm from holding an order exceeds the clearly characterized product and compliance risk; and that case-level analysis shows the proposed shortcut did not produce the observed exception, rework, and audit tail.
