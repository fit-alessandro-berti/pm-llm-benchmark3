## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

The comparison should use a **conformance gate before utility optimization**. Actions that cannot produce a valid payment execution under the current control state—A and B—are inadmissible even when their narrow operational forecast looks favorable.

The immediate deadline benefit is bounded: timely execution is worth approximately €20,000–€35,000. By contrast, unlawful execution creates a plausibly €100,000–€500,000 exposure plus consequences that are not appropriately reduced to money, including sanctions-control failure, regulatory intervention, license or supervisory consequences, reputational damage, individual accountability, and invalidation of the control environment. The upper end of the operational benefit is therefore small relative to the stated compliance-loss range, and the process explicitly treats the independent review as mandatory rather than as a priced option.

Customer harm creates a different trade-off. A false rejection or prolonged hold may cost €15,000–€80,000, potentially exceeding the deadline benefit. This argues against both an unsupported immediate rejection and an unnecessarily long evidence cycle. It supports C or D because those paths preserve the option to approve, reject, or hold after an independent review, rather than committing prematurely to either execution or rejection.

D ranks narrowly above C because it adds information while the mandatory review is taking place. It is not strictly superior: its value depends on vendor quality, whether the result arrives before S2 decides, and the opportunity cost of one of the three remaining calls. If it introduces delay, C dominates it for this case.

E and F represent opposite errors. E accepts a near-certain delay to reduce decision error. F accepts greater decision uncertainty to conclude the case quickly. The observational 0.55–0.75 ultimate-approval estimate makes unsupported rejection unattractive, but it does not prove that this particular case should be approved or that additional evidence will be useful. Consequently, E ranks above F unless the rejection standard is affirmatively shown to be met.

The model estimates should not be multiplied mechanically into a point expected value. The 0.48–0.64 breach estimate applies to “continue normal processing,” while C, D, E, and G change timing, routing, information, or resource allocation. Likewise, the approval estimate is observational and may change after alternate screening, additional evidence, or a different reviewing officer. The appropriate treatment is interval-based scenario analysis, not a claim such as “C reduces breach probability by a known number.”

## 5. Resource spillovers

Using S2 immediately gives P-884 the only stated review path whose full duration can still fit inside the deadline across the entire supplied range:

[
35\text{–}75 \text{ min review} + 5\text{–}15 \text{ min recording/execution}
= 40\text{–}90 \text{ min}.
]

This calculation is already tight and excludes any additional time needed for R17’s required post-evidence review. Immediate S2 therefore preserves the best deadline option, but it creates a material portfolio externality: P-901’s breach probability rises by 0.12–0.28.

If (L_{901}) denotes the consequence of a P-901 breach, the first-order incremental spillover is approximately:

[
(0.12\text{–}0.28)\times L_{901},
]

before considering nonlinear customer, queue, continuity, or fairness effects. Because the prompt does not provide (L_{901}), it is impossible to prove that sacrificing P-901 is portfolio-optimal. The interruption, its reason, and the induced risk should therefore be logged as part of the decision, not treated as a free resource transfer.

For S4:

* **If S4 becomes available in 35 minutes**, the total time from now is approximately (35 + 40\text{–}90 = 75\text{–}125) minutes. Timely execution remains possible only in the faster portion of the review-duration range. A confirmed 35-minute availability can move a D+S4 plan above immediate C when the expected P-901 spillover is very costly and S4 is expected to complete review, recording, and execution within the remaining 55 minutes. Immediate S2 nevertheless remains safer for P-884’s deadline.
* **If S4 becomes available in 65 minutes**, the total becomes approximately (65 + 40\text{–}90 = 105\text{–}155) minutes. Even the fastest stated completion misses the deadline. In that scenario, immediate S2 clearly remains the highest-ranked deadline-preserving conformant path; S4 becomes a late but still conformant review path.

The alternate-screening quota should be treated as a portfolio resource. There are only three expedited calls left, but this is a €480,000 payment with both a high score and an unresolved sanctions match, so spending one call is proportionate if the result is expected within 15–30 minutes and is incorporated without delaying S2. If the alternate vendor has poor incremental accuracy, or other known cases have greater expected information value, C should move above D.

The override path also consumes scarce capacity and, more importantly, time. Waiting 60 minutes for a failed override attempt would leave only 30 minutes, less than the fastest stated 40-minute review-and-execution path. G should therefore never be serialized ahead of C or D. It can be explored in parallel only when genuine incident facts already exist and the exploration does not delay the ordinary conformant path.

## 6. Final recommendation and confidence

**Primary recommendation:** Execute the rank-1 composite plan. Immediately return the validated evidence to R17 for the required post-evidence Analyst Review, reserve and interrupt S2 for the independent Second Officer Review, and run one alternate screening call in parallel. The alternate result should be attached before S2’s decision but must not be treated as a substitute for S2. If S2 approves, record the approval before `Payment Executed`. If S2 rejects or holds the payment, do not execute. If a Suspicious Activity Report is filed, keep the payment held unless a separate valid approval path later exists, and disclose nothing about the report to the customer.

**Fallback:** If S2 cannot perform a genuinely independent review, use a bounded S4 plan only if S4’s 35-minute availability is firmly confirmed. If S4 slips toward 65 minutes or no qualified officer can decide, move to the conformant evidence/hold path in E rather than using A, B, or an unsupported rejection. F should replace the hold only when the formal rejection threshold is documented and demonstrably met.

**Confidence:** **Medium-high overall**—high on the conformance ordering and rejection of A/B, but medium on whether D should narrowly precede C because the API’s information value, R17’s remaining handling time, P-901’s breach consequence, and the intervention-specific timing distributions are unknown.

**Immediate reranking trigger:** the next verified second-officer availability and completion estimate, especially confirmation that S4 is truly available at 35 rather than 65 minutes. A fresh risk score below 70 together with a resolved fuzzy match would separately alter whether rule 6 still mandates the second review.
