## 1. Conformance semantics

| Class | Operational definition |
|---|---|
| **Forbidden behavior** | A relation marked forbidden (N3, T6, E10) is present, and the event- or object-level evidence supports a real business occurrence. Closed-world applies, so no matching "allowed" relation is needed. |
| **Missing mandatory behavior** | The relation is mandatory, the case is in scope, and no relation or plausible equivalent exists at the level where the norm is stated (N1, N4, T1/T2, E2–E8). |
| **Unexpected but unregulated** | The relation is absent from the norm and the norm says nothing about it. It is reported as a model-coverage observation, not a violation (ValidateIssue Invoice, codeath, ReopenClose, MCR–Customer). |
| **Typical-pattern absence** | A typical relation (N2, N5, T3) is missing for some objects. This is a deviation of interest and is benign if a legitimate exception is plausible. |
| **Likely data-quality error** | The observation is explained better by identity, join, or linkage faults than by behavior (Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice). |
| **Abstraction mismatch** | An "eventually" rule is tested with a directly-follows edge, or an event-level rule is tested on aggregated graph weights (N1, N4, T1/T2). |
| **Insufficient evidence** | The summary cannot separate behavior from artefact. Examples are the two unexplained CancelDispatch orders and the single Order–Payment cobirth. |

**Open versus closed world.** Closed-world reasoning applies only to forbidden relations and to mandatory relations within scope. A forbidden relation found in the data is a formal violation. Whether it is real behavior is a separate question, which is why E10 is formally violated but probably an artefact. Everything else is open-world. An edge missing from the norm is "unregulated", not "wrong". An observed gap in a mandatory relation is a *candidate* violation until scope and abstraction are checked. Typical relations never produce a hard violation from absence alone.

Two rules follow. Severity combines norm status, business impact, and evidence strength. Rarity alone never raises severity.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

- **What it establishes:** per-object-type directly-follows relations and their frequencies, such as CancelDispatch (2) and PackDispatch (198/200).
- **What it cannot establish:**
  - It cannot show eventual ordering beyond adjacency.
  - It cannot show the cause of an edge (hidden event, timestamp tie, or convergence).
  - It cannot show whether a missing edge means a missing step or a different path.
  - It cannot show scope, such as "standard" orders or "externally shipped" packages, unless the log is pre-filtered.
- **Counts and scopes:** "98 of 100" and "198 of 200" are scoped, so they are meaningful. "ValidateClose 83" has no stated denominator, and N2 tolerates intermediate events. Fractions are also not thresholds, because the norm sets no tolerance band. Forbidden N3 is count-independent, since two occurrences is still a violation.
- **Abstraction:** N1 says "eventually", but the summary approximates it with directly-follows. An intervening review event (as in the stated context) makes a compliant order look non-compliant (false positive). The reverse also happens. A direct edge CreateValidate can exist on an earlier loop while a later revalidation never occurs, so directly-follows can over-credit. Treating an eventual obligation as adjacency produces spurious violations. The mismatch should be resolved with trace-level eventually-follows checks. N3 is an eventual prohibition ("after"), and the directly-follows graph captures only *immediate* succession. Dispatch after an intervening event would be missed, so the 2 observed orders are a lower bound.

### OTG

- **What it establishes:** which object-type pairs interact or share birth or death, with weights.
- **What it cannot establish:** event-level obligations. T1 and T2 are mandatory *for Add Item and Dispatch Package interactions*, but OTG weights aggregate across all events.
- **Counts:**
  - Package–Delivery 200 exceeds the 198 Dispatch events that carry Delivery. This may mask the 2 missing links (other events contribute) or reflect different counting units.
  - Order–Item 1450 does not match the 1448 Add Item–Order events.
  - Presence of the edge therefore does not prove mandatory per-event compliance. Absence would prove a violation.
- **Abstraction:**
  - T6 cobirth weight 1 is forbidden at graph level, but it may reflect a single erroneous event, so it needs event-level confirmation.
  - Codeath (7), Order–Customer (100), and Order–RiskCase (3) are unregulated, so the open-world rule applies.
  - Invoice–Payment 92 is consistent with N5 and T3.

### ET–OT

- **What it establishes:** event-type/object-type participation, which is the right level for per-event mandatory rules E1–E8 and forbidden E10.
- **What it cannot establish:** order, causality, or whether a missing link is a modeling or extraction gap.
- **Counts:**
  - Weights are compared with event totals. Create Order 100 matches E1, Pack 200 matches E4, and Dispatch–Package 200 matches E5.
  - Shortfalls are Add Item–Order 1448/1450, Dispatch–Delivery 198/200, and Record Payment–Invoice 92/93.
  - The summary is evidently partial. There is no Validate Order row, no Order–Customer row even though the OTG has 100, and no MCR–Order row even though the OC-DFG shows Orders in MCR flows. Absence of a row therefore does not show absence of the relation.
- **Abstraction:** E6 is formally violated twice, but a carrier reference attribute exists, so the information is present and only the modeling is incomplete. E8 is formally violated but may be legitimate unapplied cash. E10 is forbidden at the ET–OT level and probably a join artefact.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

3. **N4 scope "external shipment" is not encoded.**
   - Risk: denominators cannot be verified.
   - Clarification: require a shipment-type attribute. **Mandatory** within scope, with directly-follows or eventually-follows stated.
4. **E8 unapplied cash is not labeled.**
   - Risk: legitimate cash is flagged, or illegitimate unlinked payments are excused.
   - Clarification: require an explicit event attribute. **Typical** (mandatory Invoice link unless typed).
5. **Cobirth/codeath semantics and T6 definition.**
   - Risk: artefacts count as violations, or the meaning of "same business event" is ambiguous.
   - Clarification: define the birth event and treat codeath as **explicitly unspecified**.
6. **Identifier governance (E10).**
   - Risk: join collisions reproduce forbidden links.
   - Clarification: require namespaced object IDs. **Mandatory** (data-integrity rule), with E10 staying **forbidden**.
7. **T1/T2 are event-scoped but the OTG is aggregate.**

8. **No tolerance bands or minimum frequencies, and no treatment of carrier reference vs Delivery.**
   - Risk: arbitrary thresholds.
