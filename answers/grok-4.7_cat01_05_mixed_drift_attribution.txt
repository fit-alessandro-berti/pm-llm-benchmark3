## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

In January–February raw and mapped fitness match at 0.92–0.93. In March raw fitness falls to 0.55 while mapped fitness holds at 0.90, so renaming `Eligibility Screen` to `Validate Eligibility` and dropping duplicated `Open Case` events closes about 93 percent of that month’s loss versus the January–February mean of 0.925. This identifies a label and technical-event mismatch with the old normative model. Routing has to be read from the mapped series: mapped fitness later eases from 0.91 in April to 0.86 in July, then returns to 0.90 in December even though rework variants are still 21 percent. Fitness on a model that allows the info-request loop is a weak gauge of loop frequency.

A label map restores token alignment and can do so while a genuine path change travels under the new name. Timestamps, quantities, and resources stay as recorded. March’s mapped directly-follows fragment is nearly January’s (partial L1 of 3 per 100 cases). Median cycle time stays near 31 hours and rework variants near 12 percent, so through April the repaired log still has the pre-migration shape. About 22 percent of March cases still carry the old eligibility label, so the migration is a mixture, not a clean synonym switch. Both labels proceed to document collection at the historical rate.

Duplicated `Open Case` events touch about 4–6 percent of cases after March. They insert a spurious prefix (`Open Case → Register`, 26 cases in March), split raw variants, and pull raw fitness down. That frequency cannot account for multi-hour cycle-time moves. Missing resources rise from about 1 percent to 5–6.5 percent and concentrate on migrated activities, which biases handover, workload, and social-network statistics. Variant percentages barely move in March (fast 58→57, rework 12→12), so those operational definitions are insensitive to the rename; the caveat remains that only the mapped-fitness series was formally relabeled.

The post-May shifts remain after the same cleaning. `Assess → Request Info` rises from 12 per 100 cases in January and March to 24 in May and 29 in July. Rework variants rise from 12–13 percent to 24–29 percent. Median cycle time rises from about 31 hours to 45 hours by August, while recorded `Assess` service time moves only from about 4.8 to 5.4 hours. Those movements track Supplier Z’s share and partly recede in September: operational drift on top of an unresolved logging defect.

Mapping recovers about 93% of the March raw-fitness loss, about 90% in May, and about 85% at the July mapped-fitness trough of 0.86. The shrinking recovered share means a growing slice of the raw conformance loss is residual routed deviation. December’s mapped fitness is back at the March mapped level while rework is still about 9 points above baseline, so conformance recovery and behavioral recovery are different events.

**Partial DFG distance.** Reference is January, the only pre-migration month in the fragment. The measure is L1 on occurrence rates per 100 cases: sum of absolute differences. It is not a probability distance; a case contributes to several edges, and the seven edges are not a partition. `Request Info → Collect Documents` tracks `Assess → Request Info` within 1 occurrence in every supplied month, so the headline distance drops that edge to avoid counting one loop twice. Omitted edges are unknown, not zero. This partial L1 equals the full-DFG L1 only if every omitted edge is unchanged; otherwise it is a lower bound on full L1. February is absent from the fragment.

| 2026-05 | 40 | 28 | 15 | Request Info ~80%; senior ~20% |

March is a near-zero behavioral distance. May’s distance is a rework-loop distance. October’s distance is a different composition, dominated by the senior-assessment edge. Relative to January’s mass on the six edges (392 per 100 cases), the de-duplicated L1 is about 1% in March, 7% in May, 11% in July, 5% in September, 13% in October, and 7% in December.

**Cycle-time decomposition.** These components overlap and are not an additive identity. Recorded `Assess` service growth is the only tight quantity: about +0.6 h by July–August, under a tenth of the +13.9 h August median gap and a still smaller share of the +42 h p90 gap. Extra assessment touches, if each added rework variant carried one more ~5.4 h assessment, would contribute on the order of 1 h. `Collect Documents → Assess` itself falls from 95 to the high 80s per 100, so repeat visits are already inside that edge and are not a large new service mass.

- **Wave 1, baseline → May (before the departures):** median about +5.4 h, p90 about +16 h. Fast share crosses 50% (55% in April to 45% in May) in the same month. If fast-variant cases are systematically shorter than other cases, the pooled median changes case type; if the duration distributions overlap, the composition effect is smaller. Within-variant times are not in the extract, so this channel is plausible and unquantified. Service change can explain well under 1 h. The remaining wave-1 hours align with the new Request Info loop, whose elapsed time is mostly waiting on documents and customers rather than assessor touch time.
- **Wave 2, May → August:** median about +8.5 h more, p90 about +26 h more, while rework rises only another 3–5 points and Z’s share stops rising and then falls. This is the portion most consistent with queueing under lost assessor capacity, a larger caseload, and extra loop visits. A plausible qualitative share for capacity-driven queueing is around half or more of wave 2, and a larger share of the p90 than of the median. Headcount is unknown, so utilization cannot be computed. The priority rule can fatten the tail inside this wave; the pooled series cannot assign it hours.
- **Case mix:** through August the high-complexity share is only +8.5 points, and Z’s complexity premium under the April–May balance is about 8 points. Demographic mix is a small direct contributor next to rework and queueing. In October–November it becomes a major contributor: high-complexity +19 points versus September with Z almost flat, median +7.5 h and p90 +25 h versus September.
- **Wave 3, September:** median −3.5 h and p90 −14 h as Z and rework fall. Partial, lagged unwind.
- **Wave 4, October–November, then December:** seasonal load and complexity, senior-path frequency up sharply, then a December giveback of about 6.7 median hours and 22 p90 hours that coincides with both a lighter mix and the interventions.

**Candidate change points**

- Logging drift: (1) 2026-03-01, sudden, raw-fitness gap, +2 labels, duplicate `Open Case`, missing resources 1%→5%. (2) March–July gradual completeness creep, missing resources 4.8%→6.5%, with no single month jumping by 2 points. December does not reverse either.
- Behavioral drift: (1) between the April and May snapshots, aligned with 2026-05-05, Request Info rate and rework variants. (2) October 2026, senior-assessment rate 7→22 per 100 and a fresh fast-share drop while Z’s share is flat. September’s partial reversal is a third, opposite, movement.
- Performance drift: (1) May 2026, median +4.5 h and p90 +13 h versus April, before the departures. (2) June–August accumulation, with June’s +4.3 h median and +11 h p90 as the first post-15-June monthly bin. October is a further seasonal performance point, not the same mechanism.

A simple rule that flags a month when the raw−mapped gap grows by ≥0.15, labels increase, rework or fast share moves by ≥8 points, median moves by ≥4 h, p90 by ≥10 h, high-complexity share by ≥8 points, or caseload by ≥10% produces logging at March only; behavioral flags at May, September, October, and December; performance flags at May, June, September, October, and December; population flags at October and December. July is not flagged. That absence is informative and still compatible with a priority rule whose effects cancel in the pool.

**One break or many.** The artifacts do not share a change point. Cycle time and rework are flat where raw fitness collapses. Labels, duplicates, and the fitness gap do not jump where rework and Z’s share jump. Z’s share does not jump where volume, complexity, the senior edge, and cycle time jump in October. Mapped fitness troughs in July (0.86); cycle time troughs in November (median 49 h, p90 121 h). A single change-point detector on raw fitness would date the story to March and miss the operational drift. The same detector on median cycle time would miss the migration. The supported description is mechanism-specific change points, plus lagged backlog that makes performance peak after the behavioral driver has already started to ease. No formal change-point procedure has been applied; mid-month events can be displaced by up to one monthly bin.

**Seasonality.** Keep two regimes, non-renewal and renewal (October–November, with December treated as a mixed exit month rather than as either baseline). This extract contains only one renewal, so October–November 2026 must not become the control for itself and must not be folded into the January reference. Until the next renewal, renewal alerts use wider bands and mix-adjusted residuals: predict rework, waits, and senior-edge rates from supplier share and high-complexity share fitted on non-renewal months, and alert on the residual. When a second season exists, switch volume and complexity to a seasonal naive comparison (same month, prior year) and keep the residual monitor for within-mix behavior. Do not let December rewrite the reference while the campaign and the seasonal decline are still entangled. Service-time references can stay on January–February if, and only if, the timestamp schema is unchanged.

**Multiple testing.** Thirteen monitors fired every week will produce noise. Group them into five families — schema and resources, control flow, time, case mix, outcomes — and open one investigation ticket per family per week. Require a two-period persistence for level breaches; keep single-period paging only for a new activity label or a duplicate-event spike. Flow monitors (DFG, variants, rework loop, mapped fitness) are one family because they move together; a fitness dip that is the same event as a rework spike is one finding. Bonferroni correction across correlated monitors would mostly hide real shifts. Pre-specify the thresholds rather than refitting them after a breach. With five roughly independent family tests at a 5% monthly false-alarm rate, expect on the order of one false family ticket every four months before the persistence rule, and materially fewer after it. Publish the family test so a quiet month is visible as quiet.

def load_table(text):
    rows = []
    for raw in csv.DictReader(io.StringIO(text)):
        row = {"month": raw["month"]}
        for key, value in raw.items():
            if key != "month":
                row[key] = float(value)

def validate(summary, dfg):
    issues = []
    months = [r["month"] for r in summary]
    expected = [f"2026-{m:02d}" for m in range(1, 13)]
    if months != expected:
        issues.append(f"month sequence {months} != {expected}")
    for row in summary:
        for key in ("raw_model_fitness", "mapped_model_fitness"):
            if not 0.0 <= row[key] <= 1.0:
                issues.append(f"{row['month']} {key} out of [0, 1]")
        if row["mapped_model_fitness"] + 1e-9 < row["raw_model_fitness"]:
            issues.append(f"{row['month']} mapped fitness below raw fitness")
        for key in PCT_FIELDS:
            if not 0.0 <= row[key] <= 100.0:
                issues.append(f"{row['month']} {key} out of [0, 100]")
        if row["cases"] <= 0 or row["distinct_activity_labels"] < 1:
            issues.append(f"{row['month']} non-positive volume or label count")
    dfg_months = {r["month"] for r in dfg}
    if not dfg_months <= set(months):
        issues.append("DFG month outside the monthly summary")
    for row in dfg:
        for edge in DFG_EDGES:
            if row[edge] < 0:
                issues.append(f"{row['month']} negative rate on {edge}")
    return issues


def mean_baseline(summary, keys, months=("2026-01", "2026-02")):
    chosen = [r for r in summary if r["month"] in months]
    return {key: sum(r[key] for r in chosen) / len(chosen) for key in keys}


def l1(left, right, edges):
    return sum(abs(left[edge] - right[edge]) for edge in edges)


def positive_mass(left, right, edges):
    return sum(max(0.0, left[edge] - right[edge]) for edge in edges)

    if (
        abs(cur["rework_variant_pct"] - prev["rework_variant_pct"]) >= 8

def format_event_log(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    start_timestamp_key=None,
):
    """Return a PM4Py-formatted copy. The caller's dataframe is left as passed."""
    import pm4py

    frame = df.copy()
    kwargs = {
        "case_id": case_id,
        "activity_key": activity_key,
        "timestamp_key": timestamp_key,
    }
    if start_timestamp_key and start_timestamp_key in frame.columns:
        kwargs["start_timestamp_key"] = start_timestamp_key
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(frame, **kwargs)


def slice_window(log, start, end, mode="traces_contained"):
    """Cohort slice. start/end are 'YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS'.

    mode='traces_contained' keeps cases fully inside the window.
    Use mode='traces_intersecting' when studying open cases and censoring.
    """
    import pm4py

    return pm4py.filter_time_range(log, start, end, mode=mode)


def monthly_dfg(log):

def monthly_variants(log):

def variant_shares(variants):
    """Normalize pm4py.get_variants output whether values are counts or case lists."""
    if not isinstance(variants, dict) or not variants:
        return {}
    sample = next(iter(variants.values()))
    if isinstance(sample, (int, float)):
        counts = {key: int(value) for key, value in variants.items()}
    elif isinstance(sample, (list, tuple, set)):
        counts = {key: len(value) for key, value in variants.items()}
    else:
        counts = {key: int(value) for key, value in variants.items()}
    total = float(sum(counts.values())) or 1.0
    return {key: value / total for key, value in counts.items()}


def monthly_temporal_profile(log):
    import pm4py

    return pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(log)

        return pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(log)

def normalize_labels(formatted_df, drop_open_case=True):
    """Copy the formatted log, map the eligibility synonym, optionally drop Open Case.

    In this process the observed Open Case -> Register count matches
    duplicate_open_case_pct (about one redundant prefix on 4-6% of cases),
    and the normative model has no Open Case activity. Dropping every Open Case
    event is the preparation that corresponds to the published mapped fitness.
    The input frame is not modified.
    """
    out = formatted_df.copy()
    out["concept:name"] = out["concept:name"].replace(
        {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"}
    )
    if drop_open_case:
        out = out.loc[out["concept:name"] != "Open Case"].copy()
    return out


def compare_raw_and_normalized(df, start, end, mode="traces_contained"):
    """Discover DFGs and variants on raw and normalized copies. Does not mutate df."""

        "raw_end_activities": raw_ea,

        "normalized_temporal_profile": monthly_temporal_profile(norm_window),
        "caller_object_unchanged": formatted_is_copy(df, original),

## 9. Provisional attribution

March is the firmest break, and it is logging and semantic. Raw fitness falls by about 0.37 from the January–February mean of 0.925, while mapped fitness falls by about 0.03. The vocabulary grows by `Eligibility Screen` and `Open Case`. About 78 percent of March cases carry the new label and about 22 percent keep the old one; both still flow on to `Collect Documents` at the historical rate. Rework, the fast path, and median cycle time stay flat. December still has 11 labels, duplicate `Open Case` near 4–6 percent, and missing resources near 5–6 percent. Divergent mapped paths under the two eligibility labels would weaken this claim.

May is a separate, supplier-linked behavioral break. Rework variants move from 13 to 24 percent and Request Info edges from 12 to 24 per 100 cases. The fast share falls from 55 to 45 percent, which can lift the pooled median by composition if fast cases are systematically shorter. High-complexity share rises only from 23 to 25 percent. With within-supplier complexity held fixed, April and May imply Supplier Z near 30 percent high-complexity and other suppliers near 22 percent. That premium predicts the 2-point complexity increase, so the rework doubling is a within-segment change. September partly reverses with Z, from 38 to 24 percent share and from 27 to 20 percent rework, cycle time lagging. Confidence is moderately high for the supplier link and moderate for sole causation, because Z is a selected region and customer subset.

Queueing carries the performance damage. Assess service rises by about 0.6 hours, against about +5 median hours from the baseline to May and about +8.5 more from May to August. The p90 rises about 16 hours, then about 26. Wave one matches the rework shock and predates the departures. Wave two keeps rising as Z’s share eases, as expected if unrepaired capacity, volume, and extra visits pile into a backlog. Those mechanisms multiply rather than compete. The 1 July priority rule would split waits by complexity. The pooled series shows no distinct 1 July step, so the rule is plausible and weakly supported.

October–November is a seasonal population wave at its own change point: volume and high-complexity share jump while Z’s share stays near the September level. Senior-assessment edges rise from 7 to 22 per 100, above January’s rate scaled by complexity, so practice and recording both stay open. December improves as load falls and as overtime and a document campaign start. Within-segment change points, waits by priority, and a mix-matched December comparison would revise those pieces.

The strongest results are the March logging identification, the separation of that break from everything after April, the May rework shift’s failure to fit complexity mix, and the service-versus-wait split. Plausible but not cleanly attributed are staffing’s share of wave 2, the priority rule, the October senior-edge excess, and any December campaign effect. Mediation runs through rework and backlog: Supplier Z and the calendar change cycle time largely by changing work content and arrivals, and the smaller assessor group converts that work content into waiting. Mapped fitness returning to 0.90 in December while rework is still 21 percent is a reminder that the conformance number and the loop rate are different claims.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF raw fitness drops by 0.10 or more while mapped fitness drops by 0.03 or less and rework and median cycle time stay inside the January–February band, THEN open a schema incident, stop publishing raw-label conformance, and repair the activity map before any process redesign, BECAUSE that pattern is a label and technical-event mismatch of the March type.

2.
