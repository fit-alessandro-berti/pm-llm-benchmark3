# 1. Mechanism map

The restructuring proposed by management alters organizational topologies, replacing locally embedded units with a centralized shared-service model. Below is the mechanism map linking structural interventions to observable process-mining measures.

| Structural Intervention | Behavioral & Operational Mechanism | Observable PM4Py / Event Log Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Centralization of Technical Specialists** | Eliminates local team-specific technical pools (`N_Tech`, `S_Tech`, `E_Tech`) into a unified queue. Standardizes assignments but severs proximity to local caseworkers. | Increased frequency in `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network()` between local caseworkers and the `Technical_Shared_Service` resource pool. Drop in local `working_together_network` edges between caseworkers and local specialists. |
| **Unified Legal, Risk, and Technical Shared Queues** | Removal of local triage discretion. Routing is centralized based on `required_specialty` and `case_complexity`. | Changes in resource similarity profiles (`pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity()`), as all technical resources execute from a pooled queue rather than segregated team rosters. |
| **Removal of One Local Supervisor Position & Shared Approvals** | Centralizes approvals for medium/high-value cases, standardizing decision criteria at the cost of direct local oversight. | Increase in wait times captured via `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time()` at the approval step; shift in performance DFG edge weights (`pm4py.discover_performance_dfg()`). |
| **Loss of Local Embedded Expertise** | Local caseworkers noonger have immediate informal consultations with adjacent specialists; complex cases require formal queuing. | Rise in `rework_flag` rates and `reassignment_count` on complex cases, visible in `pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity()`. |
| **Resource Pooling & Utilization Targets** | Balances workload across teams (especially rescuing the overloaded East team) by increasing nominal specialist utilization by 15%. | Increased throughput for East cases in `pm4py.filter_variants()`, offset by longer queue wait times for North and South cases due to shared-service saturation. |

# 2. Scenario forecasts

## Optimistic Scenario
- **Handover Pattern:** Nominal handover count drops slightly. Central queues are processed efficiently with high resource flexibility. Cross-team handovers drop to near zero.
- **Queue/Waiting-Time Pattern:** Central queue wait times remain low (under 3 hours). East team cycle times drop from 8.6 days down to match North/South (approx. 7.0 days).
- **Cycle-Time Distribution:** Tight distribution. 90th percentile cycle times compress across all teams as East bottlenecks are resolved.
- **Rework and Quality Pattern:** Rework rates stay flat or drop slightly due to standardized decision-making rules in the shared queue.
- **Role Clarity and Accountability:** Clear separation of duties between local intake/case handling and centralized technical/approval gates.
- **Fairness Implications:** East customers experience faster resolutions, bringing service levels in parity with North and South.
- **Early Warning Signs:** Minor initial spike in shared-queue wait times that normalizes within two weeks as specialists adapt to the pooling tool.

## Central (Management Baseline) Scenario
- **Handover Pattern:** Handover volumes shift from local loops (e.g., `local caseworker  embedded tech specialist`) to central queue routing. Total handovers per case remain stable.
- **Queue/Waiting-Time Pattern:** Central technical queue wait times rise moderately (from 3.2h to 4.0h), offset by faster intake and elimination of ad-hoc cross-team cover.
- **Cycle-Time Distribution:** Average cycle times improve slightly (driven heavily by East gains), but the 90th percentile for complex cases widens due to central queuing.
- **Rework and Quality Pattern:** Rework rates increase marginally on complex cases (from 9% to ~11%) due to the loss of informal local pre-checks.
- **Role Clarity and Accountability:** Local caseworkers feel a loss of autonomy; accountability for overall case speed becomes blurred between local and central management.
- **Fairness Implications:** North and South customers experience longer wait times for complex cases to subsidize East's capacity recovery.
- **Early Warning Signs:** Rising `reassignment_count` on complex cases; caseworkers logging duplicate inquiries to chase central specialists.

## Adverse Scenario
- **Handover Pattern:** Proliferation of ping-pong handovers (`Technical Shared Service  local caseworker  Technical Shared Service`) because remote specialists lack local context and return cases for clarification.
- **Queue/Waiting-Time Pattern:** Severe bottlenecks in the centralized technical and legal queues. Median wait times exceed 12 hours; 90th percentile wait times spike past 48 hours.
- **Cycle-Time Distribution:** Bimodal distribution. Simple cases clear quickly, while complex cases experience massive tail latency, blowing out SLA miss rates.
- **Rework and Quality Pattern:** Rework rates surge across all teams (exceeding 15%). Quality checks catch omissions caused by missing regional customer context.
- **Role Clarity and Accountability:** Complete loss of ownership. Local staff treat cases as "out of sight, out of mind," while central specialists complain of poor upstream data quality.
- **Fairness Implications:** Severe disparities emerge. Complex cases and non-standard customer segments suffer long delays; specific geographic regions notice marked degradation in responsiveness.
- **Early Warning Signs:** Immediate spikes in `handover_reason` indicating missing information; sharp rise in customer escalation flags within 7 days of go-live.

# 3. Leading and lagging indicators

| Indicator Type | Metric Name | PM4Py / Event Log Source | Target / Guardrail Threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Leading** | Central Queue Inflow vs. Outflow Velocity | `pm4py.discover_dfg()` on shared queues | Inflow rate must not exceed outflow rate by >10% over any 4-hour window. |
| **Leading** | Reassignment / Ping-Pong Frequency | `pm4py.format_dataframe()` filtered on `reassignment_count` | Average reassignments per complex case $< 0.5$. |
| **Leading** | Informal Advice Drop-off Rate | `pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values` on handover types | Drop in local specialist handovers must be offset by centralized technical intake without increasing total case duration. |
| **Lagging** | 90th Percentile Cycle Time | `pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe()` | Must not increase by more than 5% overall, and no more than 15% for complex cases. |
| **Lagging** | Rework Rate by Team & Complexity | `pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity()` | Overall rework rate $\le 10\%$; no team exceeds 14%. |
| **Lagging** | SLA Miss Rate | Computed from `case:concept:name` timestamps against due dates | Overall SLA miss rate must remain below 8%. |

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1. **Tail Latency Explosion:** While mean cycle times may look stable due to high volumes of fast, simple cases, complex cases can get trapped in centralized queues, causing 90th and 99th percentile cycle times to balloon unnoticed.
2. **Degradation of Complex Case Handling:** Central specialists working from abstract queues lack the contextual knowledge embedded in local teams, leading to repeated information requests and stalled workflows on non-standard cases.
3. **Loss of Local Knowledge and Resilience:** Local caseworkers stop developing technical diagnostic skills because hard cases are automatically offloaded. If shared specialists fall ill or leave, the organization loses its distributed fallback capability.
4. **Fairness and Segment Disparities:** Customers requiring regional accommodations, non-standard documentation, or specialized legal handling may face systemic prioritization bias in a faceless queue, degrading service equity.
5. **Erosion of Learning Loops:** Informal feedback loops between local technical specialists and caseworkers disappear. Mistakes made by caseworkers are corrected downstream rather than coached upstream, preventing root-cause learning.
6. **Diffusion of Accountability:** When a customer complains about a stalled case, neither the local caseworker nor the central shared-service manager takes ownership, as handovers obscure responsibility boundaries.

# 5. Pilot design

- **Pilot Scope:** Centralize only the `Legal` and `Risk` shared queues first; keep technical specialists embedded locally for 30 days before touching technical centralization. Alternatively, pilot the Technical Shared Service exclusively with the **East team** (which is currently overloaded) while keeping North and South on the local model as a control group.
- **Comparison Group:** East team (treatment group) vs. North and South teams (control group).
- **Pre/Post Observation Window:** 4 weeks pre-pilot baseline event log vs. 4 weeks active pilot monitoring.
- **Segmentation:** Stratify analysis by:
  - `team` (North, South, East)
  - `case_complexity` (Low, Medium, High)
  - `required_specialty` (Standard, Legal, Risk)
  - `customer_segment` (Standard, Premium, Special Accommodation)
- **Guardrail Metrics:** 
  - 90th percentile cycle time per segment cannot increase by >15%.
  - Rework rate cannot exceed 12% in any pilot cohort.
  - Shared queue wait time must remain under 6 hours median.
- **Staff Feedback Collection:** Bi-weekly pulse surveys of caseworkers and shared specialists tracking role clarity, friction points, and perceived information completeness.
- **Rollback or Adjustment Triggers:** Immediate rollback or pause if:
  1. Central queue wait times exceed 12 hours for 3 consecutive days.
  2. Rework rates on complex cases increase by more than 50 relative percentage points.
  3. Customer satisfaction scores drop by >0.5 points in any segment.

---

# 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation: Pilot first (phased rollout) with strict monitoring guards.**

Management should **not** infer from early average cycle-time improvements that the restructuring is fully successful. Average cycle times are easily skewed downward by faster processing of high-volume, low-complexity cases handled by automated intake and streamlined local routing. 

Management must explicitly evaluate tail latency, complex-case rework rates, and team-level variance. If the pilot in the East team proves that capacity bottlenecks are relieved without degrading complex case outcomes or spiking rework, proceed cautiously to North and South. If tail latency or ping-pong handovers spike, pause centralization and retain embedded technical triage for complex cases.

# 7. PM4Py analysis sketch

Below is Python pseudocode using PM4Py-style concepts to compare event logs before and after the organizational restructuring:

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# 1. Load and format event logs
df_before = pd.read_csv("event_log_before.csv")
df_after = pd.read_csv("event_log_after.csv")

df_before = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df_before,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)
df_after = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df_after,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)

# 2. Handover of work networks
net_before = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
    df_before,
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)
net_after = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
    df_after,
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)

# 3. Working together and subcontracting networks
wt_before = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(df_before)
wt_after = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(df_after)

sub_before = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(df_before)
sub_after = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(df_after)

# 4. Variants and performance DFGs
dfg_perf_before = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(df_before)
dfg_perf_after = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(df_after)

variants_before = pm4py.get_variants(df_before)
variants_after = pm4py.get_variants(df_after)

# 5. Service and waiting times
df_before_svc = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df_before)
df_after_svc = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df_after)

service_times_before = pm4py.get_service_time(df_before)
service_times_after = pm4py.get_service_time(df_after)

# 6. Resource similarity and organizational roles
resource_sim_before = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(
    df_before
)
resource_sim_after = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(df_after)

roles_before = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(df_before)
roles_after = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(df_after)

# 7. Filtering by team, complexity, specialty, and customer segment
# Example: Evaluate East team, high complexity, legal specialty
filtered_df_east_complex = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df_after, "team", ["East"], level="cases", retain=True
)
filtered_df_east_complex = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    filtered_df_east_complex,
    "case_complexity",
    ["High"],
    level="cases",
    retain=True,
)
filtered_df_east_complex = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    filtered_df_east_complex,
    "required_specialty",
    ["legal"],
    level="cases",
    retain=True,
)

# 8. Rework and SLA analysis
rework_before = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(df_before)
rework_after = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(df_after)

temporal_features_before = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
    df_before,
    case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    activity_key="concept:name",
)
temporal_features_after = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
    df_after,
    case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    activity_key="concept:name",
)

print("Pre- vs Post-Restructuring Analysis Complete.")
```
