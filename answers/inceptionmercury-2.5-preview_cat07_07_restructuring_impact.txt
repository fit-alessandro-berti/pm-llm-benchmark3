

# 1. Mechanism map

This map links the proposed restructuring actions to specific process-mining observables and outcomes.

*   **Action:** Merge embedded tech specialists into a centralized Technical Shared Service.
    *   **Effect:** **Central queue formation**; increased frequency of handovers to the shared queue (`local caseworker  Technical Shared Service`).
    *   **PM Effect:** Higher density in `discover_handover_of_work_network` for the shared resource; increased `service_waiting_time` at the shared queue.
    *   **Outcome:** **Specialist utilization** rises (more work assigned); potential **resilience** drop (single point of failure for tech advice).

*   **Action:** Centralize approvals and standardize routing rules.
    *   **Effect:** **Nominal handover reduction** between local teams; elimination of ad-hoc cross-team handovers.
    *   **PM Effect:** More standardized variants in `discover_variants`; reduced `cross-team handover` frequency.
    *   **Outcome:** **Role clarity** may decrease as ownership shifts from individual supervisors to queues; potential **fairness** issues if routing rules bias case allocation.

*   **Action:** Remove local supervisor positions; introduce shared approval queue.
    *   **Effect:** Local loop broken.
    *   **PM Effect:** `discover_organizational_roles` shows fewer unique roles; reduced local supervisor resource activity.
    *   **Outcome:** **Local expertise loss** (fewer opportunities for junior staff to observe senior decisions); increased reliance on written specs.

*   **Action:** Route high-complexity cases centrally.
    *   **Effect:** Cases routed by `case_complexity` or `required_specialty`.
    *   **PM Effect:** Distinct `performance_dfg` branches for high vs. low complexity.
    *   **Outcome:** **Fairness** risk if high-complexity cases (often specific segments) wait longer in central queue.

*   **Action:** Reduce local technical knowledge transfer.
    *   **Effect:** Embedded loops (`local caseworker  embedded tech specialist  same local caseworker`) disappear.
    *   **PM Effect:** Decrease in working-together network density for local pairs.
    *   **Outcome:** **Learning** degradation; **rework** may rise for complex cases due to context loss.

# 2. Scenario forecasts

### Optimistic Scenario
*   **Handover pattern:** Cross-team handovers drop to near-zero; total local-specialist handovers decrease.
*   **Queue/waiting-time:** Shared queue capacity matches demand; wait times stabilize below 3.0h.
*   **Cycle-time distribution:** Median improves; 90th percentile remains stable due to parallel processing benefits.
*   **Rework and quality:** Rework rate drops due to standardized approval decisions.
*   **Role clarity and accountability:** Shared approval queue has clear owner; SLA tracking simplifies.
*   **Fairness:** East team benefits from reduced bottleneck; North/South satisfaction remains stable due to process clarity.
*   **Early warning signs:** None. Queue depth remains under threshold.

### Central Scenario
*   **Handover pattern:** Total handovers rise slightly (due to new shared queue handovers), but cross-team drops.
*   **Queue/waiting-time:** Tech Shared Service queue wait increases to 4.5h (above expectation).
*   **Cycle-time distribution:** Median stable; 90th percentile degrades (tail latency increases).
*   **Rework and quality:** Rework rate stable for simple cases, increases for complex cases due to context loss.
*   **Role clarity and accountability:** Some confusion on who approves edge cases; supervisors less involved.
*   **Fairness:** East improves; North/South see slight satisfaction drop; customers needing regional nuance face delays.
*   **Early warning signs:** Rising wait time at Technical Shared Service; increasing reassignment_count for complex cases.

### Adverse Scenario
*   **Handover pattern:** Frequent reassignments as cases bounce between shared queue and local teams.
*   **Queue/waiting-time:** Technical Shared Service becomes bottleneck; wait time >8.0h.
*   **Cycle-time distribution:** Median increases; 90th percentile doubles.
*   **Rework and quality:** Rework rate spikes (12%+) due to lack of local context in shared advice.
*   **Role clarity and accountability:** Disputes arise; supervisors disengaged but still blamed for delays.
*   **Fairness:** East team overwhelmed (cannot scale); North/South feel loss of autonomy; high-value customers experience SLA misses.
*   **Early warning signs:** SLA miss rate increases; local_expertise_flag correlates with better outcomes but cases routed away; staff attrition signals.

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1.  **Tail Latency:** Average cycle time may remain stable while the 90th percentile inflates due to shared queue congestion. High-value customers face delays not reflected in the mean.
2.  **High-Complexity Cases:** Complex cases routed to the shared service may stall. If these are rare, they don't impact the average but drive SLA failures and reputational risk.
3.  **Local Knowledge Loss:** Embedment allowed informal advice. Post-restructuring, this is lost. It appears only later as increased rework on complex cases handled without prior local context.
4.  **Fairness Disparities:** East may benefit while North/South suffer, or specific customer segments may wait longer due to routing bias. Averages hide segment-specific performance degradation.
5.  **Learning & Skill Decay:** Junior caseworkers lose exposure to technical decision-making. This is invisible in logs but will surface years later as a lack of internal candidates for senior roles.
6.  **Accountability Gaps:** Shared queues blur ownership. Dispute resolution time or "reassignment_count" may increase even if execution speed remains steady, indicating friction in ownership.

# 5. Pilot design

*   **Pilot Scope:** Deploy shared service for only **Technical Shared Service** on **East** team initially. Keep North/South in current structure.
*   **Comparison Group:** North and South teams act as control groups for benchmarking utilization, handover, and satisfaction.
*   **Pre/Post Observation Window:** 4 weeks pre-restructuring data + 8 weeks post-implementation.
*   **Segmentation:** Analyze by:
    *   Team (East vs. North/South)
    *   Case Complexity (High vs. Low)
    *   Specialty (Technical vs. Non-technical)
    *   Customer Segment (Standard vs. Premium)
*   **Guardrail Metrics:**
    *   SLA miss rate  8%
    *   Queue wait time at Tech Service  4.0h
    *   Rework rate increase  1%
    *   Local customer satisfaction  4.0/5
*   **Staff Feedback:** Bi-weekly anonymous surveys on role clarity and decision authority.
*   **Rollback/Adjustment Triggers:** If SLA miss rate exceeds 10% or rework increases >5%, revert East team to embedded specialists immediately.

# 6. Recommendation

**Pilot first.** Do not proceed with full rollout.

The benefits of standardization and utilization must be weighed against the fragility of centralizing expertise. East team may absorb the change differently than North/South due to existing utilization rates.

**Do not infer success from early averages.** If average cycle time drops in the pilot, management must verify:
1.  That tail times (90th percentile) have not worsened.
2.  That rework rates for complex cases are stable.
3.  That customer satisfaction has not dropped in regions dependent on local nuance.

Only if guardrails are met for 8 weeks, and role clarity remains high should a full rollout be considered.
