## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

M0 requires registration, both checks in either order, a decision, notification and closure. Its approved branch requires manual approval before activation; its reject branch also follows both checks. It contains neither auto approval nor information-request loops. It does not express identities, conflicts of interest, amounts, deadlines, authority or case scope. An exact M0 trace match is therefore neither necessary nor sufficient to establish compliance with all applicable rules.

CS-9 is controlled and effective throughout the observations. It requires both checks and manual approval before activation and different human requester and approver. PD-3 describes an eligible, time-limited pilot with different requirements, but its authority to override CS-9 is disputed. The Compliance observer's words support permission to run a monitored exception, while her later statement contests approval of a weaker standard. FD-7 expressly permits specified provisional activations during its window, subject to its controls; the materials do not establish its position in a formal hierarchy relative to CS-9. PD-3 and FD-7 have expired. Subsequent operational need or expectations of amendment do not themselves extend either instrument.

The table gives observed sequences and selected contextual facts, not a complete audit of every control. A missing event is not necessarily a missing business act, and an event recorded after activation establishes a sequence only if timestamps and case linkage are reliable. Case membership, the identity behind aliases, equivalence of renamed activities, authority of exceptions, and the timing and completeness of external evidence require separate proof. The 289 apparently eligible pilot cases are not 289 proven authorized, fully controlled exceptions; the nine cases with missing eligibility fields cannot be treated as eligible or ineligible by default. The aggregate counts do not establish how many total cases were processed or how many are noncompliant.

For a noncompliance finding, identify the applicable rule and case scope, then establish the relevant act, omission, timing or identity with sufficiently reliable evidence. A documented sequence can establish a control-flow breach; missing data alone ordinarily establishes an evidence gap, not that an unlogged act did not occur. The proponent of an exception bears the burden of showing an authorized instrument, effective time, scope and eligibility, and fulfillment of its conditions. If authority or required facts remain unresolved, report the exception as unproved, not automatically as misconduct. Where the baseline rule and its breach are established, an unproved exception does not erase that baseline finding, but the contested authority and uncertainty must be recorded.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The controlled standard is still CS-9. M0's manual approval and preactivation checks reflect important parts of it, and PD-3 was never incorporated into either. A meeting decision and an observer's ambiguous comment are not, on the evidence, proof of a competent waiver of a controlled standard. SUP-702 therefore has a documented CS-9 sequencing and approval conflict despite apparent pilot eligibility. SUP-703 is stronger still: it is high risk and ineligible even under PD-3, yet was activated after auto approval and before quality review; EUR 180,000 of purchasing demonstrates exposure, although the timing and losses are not supplied. SUP-714 and six August auto-approval cases continued after pilot expiry, when anticipated permanent approval was no authorization. The 14 clearly ineligible pilot-period cases and nine with unknown eligibility show that pilot controls and evidence deserve investigation; their individual violations cannot be established from aggregate counts alone.

FD-7 is a reason to distinguish SUP-704 from ordinary bypasses, not a general exemption. SUP-705 occurred after the directive expired and lacks a written extension. Operations' account of continuing flooding may explain the decision but does not establish authority for late approval and quality review. SUP-708 is a serious, directly evidenced breach: activation preceded both sanctions and manual approval, with no stated exception. No transaction before clearance limits observed exposure but does not undo activation in breach of a preactivation control.

M0 also misses breaches that an alignment alone would call conforming. SUP-712 has the same human as requester and approver, contrary to CS-9. SUP-715 has no recorded disclosure of a relationship for which the conflict policy requires one; the policy requires disclosure, not necessarily a prohibition on the decision. Good outcomes or desirable information requests cannot retrospectively change a rule. Nor does continued use of a pilot create a permanent approval route.

This case is not an argument that every trace deviation is misconduct. The duplicate in SUP-711, renamed quality activity in SUP-713, potentially incomplete notification collection in SUP-706, and desirable request cycles in SUP-707 expose real data or model defects. SUP-709 and SUP-710 require scope and reuse decisions before their deviations can be called breaches.

## 3. Defense Brief

M0 is a useful baseline but an incomplete representation of the operating system. The two checks are concurrent: either observed order is allowed, provided both finish before the standard decision. A checker that demands sanctions before quality would create false violations. M0 cannot express a routine request for evidence; all 47 observed cases with information requests obtained the requested evidence before activation. That supports adding the behavior, though it does not prove all other controls were met or authorize any approval bypass.

PD-3 was a documented steering decision with a Compliance observer's contemporaneous permission to proceed as a monitored exception. SUP-702 fits the stated supplier, jurisdiction, group and date conditions and completed quality review the next business day. The strongest defense is that an authorized pilot temporarily changed the applicable requirements. Yet the observer later contested any weakening of CS-9, and automated master-data validation is not shown in the trace; eligibility is not proof of both authority and execution. The defense is weaker for SUP-703 and unavailable for SUP-714's expired pilot. FD-7 more directly permits SUP-704's provisional sequence for a critical relief supplier within its window. SUP-705's continuing factual emergency has practical weight, but its expired directive cannot be assumed to remain in force.

The CRM migration gives a concrete reason for notification events to be absent from the onboarding log: missingness rose from 2% to 29%, close to the stated 30% not mirrored. SUP-702 has a stable CRM identifier; SUP-706 has weaker, mutable linkage. Neither log omission alone proves no notification, and timing relative to closure must be established. SUP-711's shared source transaction ID supports deduplication, and SUP-713's audit evidence supports semantic mapping of QA_Clearance to Quality_Review, with the mapping's later approval retained as a provenance issue.

SUP-709 could be a pre-screen lead outside M0 or a rejected intake case needing a shortened route; CS-9 permits an abbreviated pre-screen but does not resolve M0's boundary. SUP-710 is a reactivation with a prior valid certification, which may make reuse sensible, but CS-9 does not expressly authorize reusing it or omitting registration. Both require a scope decision and evidence, not an invented exception. These points establish that trace deviation and business misconduct are different questions; they do not excuse SUP-708's sanctions timing or SUP-712's identity conflict.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

Use versioned layers, with a rule's effective interval, scope, precedence and approving authority explicitly recorded. The following design is a proposal, not evidence that the pilot became permanent or that any proposed scope change has already been approved.

- **Control flow in POWL:** registration; an optional repeatable information-request stage; standard concurrent sanctions and quality checks; manual approval then activation or rejection; distinct provisional pilot and emergency paths; notification before closure. Include a proposed abbreviated rejection route. POWL provides a readable structural envelope, while external guards determine which paths are legally available. The code below approximates information requests as a stage immediately after registration; a fuller model should allow requests at appropriate points without allowing an unbounded loop to bypass mandatory checks. Model evidence receipt separately if its order matters.
- **Data and temporal constraints:** use DECLARE/log-skeleton-style constraints for existence, precedence and response, including sanctions before every activation, standard quality and manual approval before activation, notification before closure, and required follow-up after provisional activation. Evaluate business-day deadlines using an approved calendar and timestamped event data; use pandas or equivalent for risk, jurisdiction, group membership, eligibility snapshots, dates, cumulative spend and exception-specific calculations. Ensure rules distinguish provisional from final activation and do not require standard preactivation timing when a valid overlay authorizes otherwise.
- **People and conflicts:** resolve aliases to immutable human identities and check requester differs from manual approver. Record disclosures, review, mitigation, decisions and applicable delegations under conflict policy. The spouse relationship is a disclosure trigger as stipulated, not automatically a barred approval. These are data checks supported by organizational review, not control-flow claims.
- **Overlay governance:** authorized policy owners must define who can approve a waiver, precedence over CS-9, eligible population, conditions, effective and expiry instants, monitoring, and retrospective obligations. Record the governing version on each case, prevent new use after expiry, alert on missing eligibility and follow-up, and review exceptions independently. An approval route is enabled only with verified authority and conditions; proposed routes in a model do not themselves grant permission.
- **Scope:** formally choose when a lead becomes an onboarding case. If intake leads are included, approve a sanctions pre-screen rejection path that does not demand quality review for a rejected supplier; otherwise preserve a link to the preceding intake process. Define reactivation as a separate case type or linked route and specify which prior checks can be reused, their validity, scope and evidence; absent such authority, do not assume reuse fulfills CS-9. Define withdrawals explicitly, including permissible withdrawal points, requester notification, closure, retained audit trail, and safeguards against activation without required controls. The code does not claim to settle reactivation or withdrawal policy.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

The executable structural envelope below is deliberately permissive until the overlay checks are applied. Each branch uses fresh transition objects. It permits zero or more consecutive information requests immediately after registration, with the loop's silent redo enabling repetition. It does not model requests interleaved with checks, evidence receipt, actual business-day timing, spend, authority, reactivation or withdrawal. Pilot validation is explicit. Provisional and ordinary activations share the existing activity label; the selected route and activation status must be captured as case/event data. The standard branch allows full-check rejection, and the separate abbreviated rejection branch is a proposed scope-dependent route. Notification is modeled before closure; collection from CRM must be reconciled before judging it absent.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def sequence(*nodes):
    """A strict partial order whose children execute in the given order."""
    result = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    for before, after in zip(nodes, nodes[1:]):
        result.order.add_edge(before, after)
    return result


def choice(*nodes):
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=list(nodes))


def concurrent(*nodes):
    return StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))


# Process-tree LOOP: execute the body once, then repeat it after each redo.
# XOR with a silent transition makes the complete request stage optional.
requests = choice(
    SilentTransition(),
    OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.LOOP,
        children=[Transition(label="Request_More_Info"), SilentTransition()],
    ),
)

standard_checks = concurrent(
    Transition(label="Sanctions_Check"),
    Transition(label="Quality_Review"),
)
standard_decision = choice(
    sequence(
        Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval"),
        Transition(label="Activate_Supplier"),
    ),
    Transition(label="Reject_Supplier"),
)
standard_route = sequence(standard_checks, standard_decision)

pilot_route = sequence(
    Transition(label="Sanctions_Check"),
    Transition(label="Automated_Master_Data_Validation"),
    Transition(label="Auto_Approve"),
    Transition(label="Activate_Supplier"),
    Transition(label="Quality_Review"),
)

# FD-7 requires retrospective approval and timely quality review but does not
# specify their order relative to each other; both follow provisional activation.
emergency_route = sequence(
    Transition(label="Sanctions_Check"),
    Transition(label="Activate_Supplier"),
    concurrent(
        Transition(label="Quality_Review"),
        Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval"),
    ),
)

# Proposed only if the governed case scope includes abbreviated pre-screen leads.
abbreviated_rejection = sequence(
    Transition(label="Sanctions_Check"),
    Transition(label="Reject_Supplier"),
)

revised_model = sequence(
    Transition(label="Register_Supplier"),
    requests,
    choice(standard_route, pilot_route, emergency_route, abbreviated_rejection),
    Transition(label="Notify_Requester"),
    Transition(label="Close_Case"),
)
```

The pilot path's ordering of sanctions, validation and auto approval is a conservative structural choice; PD-3 requires sanctions and validation before activation but does not specify their order relative to one another or auto approval. Similarly, the code places notification after retrospective emergency steps, although FD-7 does not expressly require that ordering. If earlier notification or a different pilot ordering is authorized, refine the POWL rather than treating this approximation as policy. The model also assumes completed quality review before closure for provisional paths; assess open cases separately so pending obligations are not mistaken for completed compliant cases.

A normalized overlay table can be represented as separate versioned rule records and case-to-rule applications:

| Rule/version | Scope and effective interval | Required conditions and evidence | Authority/status |
|---|---|---|---|
| CS-9 baseline, effective 2025-01-01 onward | Onboarding activations; reactivation boundary unresolved | Sanctions, quality and manual approval before activation; requester and approver different humans | Controlled and approved |
| PD-3 pilot, 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31 | LOW-risk DE supplier in approved corporate group | Auto approval may replace manual; sanctions and automated validation before activation; quality within two business days after provisional activation | Documented pilot; authority to override CS-9 disputed. Verify competent waiver before accepting exception; no permanent authorization established |
| FD-7 emergency, 2026-07-10 00:00 through 2026-07-12 23:59 | Critical relief supplier needed to prevent flood shutdown | Sanctions before activation; provisional activation may precede quality and manual approval; quality within two business days; daily transaction monitoring; spend capped at EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval | Express emergency directive; verify authority and all conditions. Expired automatically |
| Proposed abbreviated rejection | Rejected pre-screen leads, only if approved as in scope | Record sanctions pre-screen, rejection, notification and closure; no activation | Proposed scope/model revision, not an existing waiver |
| Conflict-of-interest policy | Applicable disclosed relationships and decisions | Record required disclosure and its handling; no blanket prohibition on spouses is stated | Existing disclosure requirement; verify applicability and evidence |

Store timestamps with timezone, immutable event occurrence time and immutable recording/ingestion time, plus correction records rather than overwrites. Record a stable case ID, source system and transaction ID, event ID, activity and versioned semantic mapping, lifecycle/status, and actor's source alias linked through an auditable identity-resolution table to a stable human ID. Preserve evidence of requester, approver, reviewer, delegation and its effective period. Deduplicate only when source evidence supports a single underlying transaction, retaining both raw rows and the transformation history.

For every exception, capture the policy and rule/version IDs, competent authority and approval or delegation evidence, effective interval, decision timestamp, route and provisional status, immutable eligibility snapshot with source and capture time, business calendar, validation and follow-up evidence, spend transactions and cumulative amount until retrospective approval, and daily monitoring records. Preserve immutable links between onboarding and CRM using stable `notification_id`, case ID, recipient, message type, event and delivery timestamps; reconcile CRM records with onboarding closure and flag ambiguous legacy mutable-text links for review. Record evidence provenance and missingness explicitly, and retain raw facts alongside derived classifications so policy or mapping changes can be replayed without rewriting history.
