# 1. Executive Verdict
* **Workflow net?** Yes - there is a unique source `source` without inputs, unique sink `sink` without outputs, and every place/transition lies on a sourcesink directed path.
* **Expected to be sound?** Yes, given the business intent of proper completion (option to complete, no leftover tokens, no dead mandatory activity). 
* **Principal defect:** `t_join_waived` has a single input `p_waived`, while `t_join_normal` is an AND-join on `p_analysis_done`+`p_review_done`. The waiver path can fire the decision/authorization without consuming `p_analysis`/`p_analysis_done`.
* **Business consequence:** A waived case can be authorized *before* or *without* the mandatory analysis, violating the segregation rule, and the accepting net reaches `sink=1` with an uncleared token in the other parallel branch, i.e. a token exists in the sink $\neq$ exact final marking `fm={sink:1}`.

| `source` / `sink` / `im={source:1}`, `fm={sink:1}` | unique entry/exit, clean termination | correct unique source/sink, `im`/`fm` well-formed | none structurally; symptom is behavioural improper completion |
| `t_register``p_ready``t_split` | sequence registration then AND-fork | `t_split` produces `p_analysis` **and** `p_review` simultaneously | tokens correctly created in both parallel branches |
| `p_analysis``t_analyze``p_analysis_done` | mandatory branch | linear chain, sole producer of `p_analysis_done` | correct if synchronized downstream |
| `p_review``t_standard_review``p_review_done` / `p_review``t_waive_review``p_waived` | XOR choice of review outcome | free choice on `p_review`; `p_review_done` and `p_waived` mutually exclusive, as intended | choice itself is correct |
| `t_join_normal`: `p_analysis_done`+`p_review_done``p_decision` | AND-join for standard-review case | requires both branches, implements intent for that variant | sound locally; deadlock if counterpart fired |
| `t_join_waived`: `p_waived``p_decision` **as implemented** | intended AND-join `p_analysis_done`+`p_waived``p_decision` | only consumes `p_waived`; ignores `p_analysis_done`; produces `p_decision` unconditionally | early-enable of `t_join_waived`/`t_authorize`, bypass of mandatory analysis, leftover token |
| `p_decision``t_authorize``sink` | authorization only after both prerequisites | enabled as soon as *either* join produced `p_decision` | becomes enabled on waiver without analysis |

Source-to-sink path condition holds, so diagnose must move to marking behaviour.

# 3. Marking-Based Counterexample

Legal firing sequence exposing the defect (waiver-before-analysis):

`t_register`, `t_split`, `t_waive_review`, `t_join_waived`, `t_authorize`

| Step | Fired transition | Resulting nonzero marking | Enabled transitions | Observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | — | `{source:1}` | `t_register` | `im` |
| 1 | `t_register` | `{p_ready:1}` | `t_split` | registration complete |
| 2 | `t_split` | `{p_analysis:1, p_review:1}` | `t_analyze`, `t_standard_review`, `t_waive_review` | token created in *each* parallel branch |
| 3 | `t_waive_review` | `{p_analysis:1, p_waived:1}` | `t_analyze`, `t_join_waived` | waiver chosen; `p_review` consumed so `t_standard_review` dead forever. `t_join_waived` becomes enabled **although `p_analysis` not yet processed** |
| 4 | `t_join_waived` | `{p_analysis:1, p_decision:1}` | `t_analyze`, `t_authorize` | `t_authorize` is now enabled *before* `t_analyze` — violates “only after analysis **and** review” |
| 5 | `t_authorize` | `{p_analysis:1, sink:1}` | `t_analyze` | a token *exists* in `sink` but marking $\neq$ `fm={sink:1}`. Leftover `p_analysis:1` cannot be consumed soundly (see below). Improper completion |

Why the leftover cannot be removed: after step 5, firing `t_analyze` yields `{p_analysis_done:1, sink:1}`. The only consumers of `p_analysis_done` are `t_join_normal` (needs `p_review_done` which is now unreachable) and -in this net- not `t_join_waived`/`t_authorize` (they already consumed `p_waived`/`p_decision`). Hence the token is trapped. The net can put a token in `sink` but never reach *exactly* `fm` without stray tokens - classic improper completion / no clean termination. Note `sink:1  extra>0`  `fm`.

Second variant (waiver-after-analysis) with same consequence:

`t_register`, `t_split`, `t_analyze`, `t_waive_review`, `t_join_waived`, `t_authorize`  `{p_analysis_done:1, sink:1}` enabled: none that clears `p_analysis_done`. Same deadlock of stray token, showing `t_authorize` can occur after analysis *but still leaves* `p_analysis_done`.

The normal path `…t_standard_review, t_join_normal, t_authorize` is sound locally; soundness fails globally due to the waiver branch.

# 4. Minimal-Repair Alternatives

**Edit-cost model (explicit):**
`cost = (#added_arcs + #removed_arcs)×1 + (#added_or_removed_places)×2 + (#added_or_removed_transitions)×2`
Arc rewiring is cheapest; adding/removing nodes is penalized. Semantic equivalence judged on mandatory analysis and trace distinguishability.

| Repair | Exact edits | Edit cost | Preserved intent | Changed behavior | New risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R1 – Unified Review-Done** | remove `p_waivedt_join_waived`, `t_join_waivedp_decision`, `t_waive_reviewp_waived`; delete place `p_waived`+transition `t_join_waived`; add `t_waive_reviewp_review_done` | 4 arc edits +2 nodes×2 = **8** (lean variant keeping dead `p_waived`/`t_join_waived` disconnected: **4**) | mandatory analysis always before `t_authorize`; restores classical AND-join on single `p_review_done` | both `t_standard_review` and `t_waive_review` now produce *same* `p_review_done``t_join_normal`; waiver vs standard indistinguishable downstream | loss of auditability; requires process mining to infer waiver from transition label only; invisible-merge if silent |
| **R2 – Proper Dual AND-Join (keeps audit trail)** | add `p_analysis_donet_join_waived`; `t_join_waived` becomes 2-input AND-join on `p_analysis_done`+`p_waived``p_decision` | **1** arc added = **1** | mandatory analysis on *both* paths; preserves distinct `p_review_done` vs `p_waived` places hence distinguishable outcomes | no trace lost; formerly allowed early-authorization trace `{…,t_join_waived,t_authorize}` without analysis is removed; otherwise same language except removal of unsound traces | duplicates silent synchronisation (`t_join_normal` vs `t_join_waived` compete for `p_analysis_done`); two silent transitions with identical postcondition may confuse log replay / cause duplicate enabled-choice representation |
| **R3 – Policy-change: Optional Analysis under Waiver (explicit)** | keep single-input `t_join_waived` as-is; add XOR-split alternative `t_split_waived: p_readyp_review` (no `p_analysis`), i.e. add transition `t_split_waived`+arcs `p_readyt_split_waived`,`t_split_waivedp_review`; `t_split` remains `p_ready{p_analysis,p_review}` | 1 trans×2 +2 arcs = **4** | **Changes** business policy stated explicitly: when waiver taken, analysis is intentionally not required | waiver case never creates `p_analysis` token, so no leftover; net becomes sound without forcing analysis. Formally relaxes mandatory rule | regulatory risk; if waiver used to circumvent analysis covertly, compliance gap; two competing splits increase non-determinism; silent-transition confusion if made silent |

*R1 and R2 retain mandatory analysis on waiver path as required; R3 is the admitted policy-change alternative.*

**Justification of minimality:**
Under the stated cost, no mandatory-preserving sound repair can cost `<1`: any sound net must at least prevent `t_authorize` without `p_analysis_done`. That needs either to route waiver into the existing AND-join (1 add+1 remove) or give `t_join_waived` the missing precondition (1 add). R2 achieves this lower bound with a single added arc, therefore globally minimal. R1 needs strictly more edits (reroute + deletions) but is minimal *among node-merging* solutions that eliminate duplicate joins. R1 and R2 therefore do not tie; R2 dominates cost, R1 dominates conceptual simplicity/readability if duplicative silent joins are unwanted. R3 tie-cost with leaning variants but trades semantic compliance for policy flexibility, hence ranked separately.

# 6. Diagnostic and Reachability Code

```python
import pm4py
# assumes original net, im, fm and repaired_net, repaired_im, repaired_fm exist

def marking_str(m):
    return "{" + ", ".join(f"{p.name}:{c}" for p,c in m.items() if c>0) + "}" if m else "{}"

def fire_and_print(net, marking, t_name):
    # check enabledness explicitly, do not silently move tokens
    trans = next((t for t in net.transitions if t.name == t_name), None)
    if trans is None:
        raise ValueError(f"transition {t_name} not found")
    enabled = pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, marking)
    if trans not in enabled:
        raise RuntimeError(f"{t_name} not enabled in {marking_str(marking)} ; enabled={[t.name for t in enabled]}")
    # manual token game via arcs
    new_m = Marking(marking)
    for a in trans.in_arcs:
        plc = a.source
        new_m[plc] -= 1
        if new_m[plc]==0:
            del new_m[plc]
    for a in trans.out_arcs:
        plc = a.target
        new_m[plc] = new_m.get(plc,0)+1
    return new_m

# --- 1) workflow-net and soundness diagnostics ---
print("=== Original ===")
print("is_workflow_net:", pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net))
print(pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True))
```

# 7. Repair Trade-Offs and Residual Risks
* **Waiver vs standard distinguishability:** R2 preserves `p_waived` vs `p_review_done` and thus downstream audit distinction; R1 collapses them – you can only tell waiver by transition label history, not by state. R3 preserves distinction but changes semantics.
* **Duplicate behaviour / silent-transition confusion:** R2 introduces two silent AND-joins (`t_join_normal`, `t_join_waived`) competing for `p_analysis_done`. Replay/importance measures may split frequencies arbitrarily between them; conformance alignments will show artificial choice. Adding labels or merging visibly reduces confusion, at cost of R1's information loss.
* **Soundness  business alignment:** A positive `check_soundness` guarantees absence of deadlock/livelock and clean termination, *not* that authorization policy equals specification. All three repairs can be made sound, yet R3 is sound while violating the original mandatory-analysis rule. Formal verification of traces against LTL property `¬authorize U (analysis_done  (review_done  waived))` is still required.
* **Positive/negative regression traces:** POS must include `[Register,Split,Analysis,StandardReview,Authorize]` and `[Register,Split,Analysis,Waive,Authorize]` in any order. NEG must include `[Register,Split,Waive,Authorize]` (no analysis) and partial `[Register,Split,Waive]` followed by authorize attempts; NEG should be rejected under R1/R2 and accepted under documented R3 only.
* **Detecting accidental bypass:** Monitor enabledness invariant: `t_authorize` must never be enabled when `p_analysis` or `p_analysis_done` predecessor not consumed. Implement replay-time assertion `if t_join_waived.enabled  p_analysis_done==1`, and alerting log query `Waive without preceding Analysis`. For R3, gate waiver usage by role/right rather than net structure.

# 8. Final Recommendation
**Deploy R2** - adding `p_analysis_donet_join_waived`. It costs one arc, keeps the mandatory-analysis policy intact, retains `p_waived` for compliance reporting, and restores soundness with minimal change to existing mining baselines. Document the duplicated silent joins to avoid misinterpretation in dashboards.

**Fallback R1** - unified `p_review_done`. Use only if stakeholders require a single join and accept losing state-level waiver distinction (acceptable when audit relies solely on event labels). Reject R3 unless governance formally amends the policy to “analysis optional under waiver,” with compensating detective controls and updated training, because it re-introduces the bypass as intended behaviour.
