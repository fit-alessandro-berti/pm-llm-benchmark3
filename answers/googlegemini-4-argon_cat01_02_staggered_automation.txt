## 1. What is observed

The supplied panel tracks five branches over eight months (January–August 2026), capturing a staggered, non-random rollout of automated triage. System availability (`automation=1`) is distinct from actual treatment intensity (`automated_fast_path_pct`). Prior to rollout, all branches exhibited baseline fast-path rates of 4% to 6%. When automation became available in Alpha and Beta in April, and Gamma in July, actual fast-path utilization jumped immediately to 25%–27% and climbed steadily across subsequent months, reaching 48%–50% in Alpha and Beta and 32% in Gamma by August. Delta and Epsilon remained unexposed at 4%.

Branch selection was explicitly endogenous: Alpha and Beta were prioritized in April due to high volumes and poor historical cycle times, while Gamma adopted in July following a readiness pilot. Rollout coincided with major operational shocks. A centralized fraud desk launched in March, coinciding with an immediate ~2 percentage point jump in `escalation_pct` across all branches. Routing campaigns increased arrivals by roughly 10% in Alpha and Beta starting in April, and by 5% in Gamma in July. A May product change increased `high_complexity_pct` by 5 to 8 percentage points across branches, with further increases in Alpha and Epsilon in June. Productive staffing (`fte`) also shifted: Beta gained 1 FTE in April, Alpha lost 2 in May, while Gamma lost 1 and Delta gained 1 in June. Finally, outcomes are branch-month aggregates: `median_cycle_h` and `p90_cycle_h` mask case-level distributions, fraud-desk wait times are unrecorded, and `csat_1to5` suffers from unquantified 20%–45% response rates.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |

| **Primary: Stacked Cohort Difference-in-Differences (Callaway-Sant'Anna / Imputation Event Study)** | Staggered cohort indicators ($G_{\text{Apr}} = \{\text{Alpha, Beta}\}$, $G_{\text{Jul}} = \{\text{Gamma}\}$); event-time $k = t - g$ | Not-yet-treated (Gamma in Apr–Jun) and Never-treated (Delta, Epsilon in Apr–Aug) | Workload ratio (`arrivals/fte`), `high_complexity_pct` (linear or matched baseline trends) | **E1, E3, E4, E6** (Cohort-time specific $\text{ATT}(g,t)$ and dynamic event-study averages) | Conditional parallel trends between adopter cohorts and clean controls; no anticipation prior to $k=0$; no cross-branch interference via shared fraud desk; homogenous covariate slopes. | Pre-trend falsification tests at $k = -3, -2, -1$; leave-one-out control sensitivity (dropping high-complexity Epsilon vs low-complexity Delta); covariate balance checks. | Routing shocks (+10% volume to Alpha/Beta in Apr) and May FTE/complexity shocks are collinear with treatment timing; $G=5$ branches prevents asymptotic inference. |
| **Alternative 1: Continuous Treatment-Intensity Panel Model (Dose-Response FE)** | Continuous dose $D_{it} = \text{automated\_fast\_path\_pct}_{it}$ | Within-branch over-time variation + cross-branch month fixed effects | Branch FEs ($\alpha_i$), Month FEs ($\tau_t$), `arrivals/fte`, `high_complexity_pct` | **E2** (Marginal effect of actual fast-path routing share) | Conditional exogeneity of utilization growth given branch, month, load, and complexity; linear/correctly specified dose-response; no reverse causality (e.g., backlogs forcing manual overrides). | Residual plots against event time; testing quadratic dose terms; Oster-style coefficient stability when adding/dropping `arrivals/fte` and `high_complexity_pct`. | Endogenous learning/usage: if branches push more borderline cases into the fast path when swamped, or only expand use when complexity allows, dose estimates are biased. |
| **Alternative 2: Interrupted Time Series (ITS) with Covariate-Adjusted Structural Breaks** | Exact branch-specific intervention step ($I_{it}$) and post-adoption slope ($\text{trend}_{it}$) | Within-branch historical baseline trend (Jan–Mar for Alpha/Beta; Jan–Jun for Gamma) | Branch-specific calendar trends, `arrivals/fte`, `high_complexity_pct`, March fraud-desk step dummy | **E1, E4** (Immediate on-impact level shift at $k=0$ and slope change) | Counterfactual branch trajectory is smooth and captured by pre-trend plus observed workload/complexity shifts; no concurrent unmeasured shocks at exact adoption month. | Placebo break tests in Feb/Mar/May; checking whether April step change in Alpha/Beta replicates in July for Gamma. | Routing campaigns hit Alpha/Beta in April (+10% arrivals) and Gamma in July (+5% arrivals) at the exact moment of adoption, confounding the $k=0$ step estimate. |

### Justification of Primary Strategy
A stacked cohort Difference-in-Differences (DiD) / imputation event-study design is preferable to a naïve two-way fixed-effects (TWFE) regression and to pure time-series or dose-response models for three reasons. First, in a staggered rollout where treatment intensity (`automated_fast_path_pct`) grows from 25% to 50% over five months, dynamic treatment effects are guaranteed. A standard binary TWFE regression compares late adopter Gamma (in July–August) against already-treated early adopters Alpha and Beta (whose treatment effects are actively evolving due to learning curves). This "forbidden comparison" introduces negative weighting bias that can distort or even reverse the sign of the estimated effect. By explicitly restricting comparisons for each cohort $g$ and horizon $k$ to clean never-treated (Delta, Epsilon) and not-yet-treated (Gamma prior to July) controls, the stacked cohort approach isolates transparent 2x2 building blocks. Second, calendar months May through August contain massive common shocks—specifically the May product complexity surge and an August volume moderation. Pure within-branch ITS cannot separate the August cycle-time drop from seasonal arrival declines, whereas control branches (Delta and Epsilon) directly benchmark those common macro shifts. Pairing the cohort event study with explicit adjustment for branch workload (`arrivals/fte`) and `high_complexity_pct`—and cross-checking with the continuous treatment-intensity model—maximizes transparency while preventing improper extrapolations across only five branches.

### 2. Comparisons Against Not-Yet-Treated and Never-Treated Branches
* **Early Cohort (Alpha, Beta) On-Impact DiD (Mar $\to$ Apr vs. Untreated Gamma, Delta, Epsilon):**
  Between March and April, untreated branches (Gamma, Delta, Epsilon) saw mean `median_cycle_h` rise by **+1.47h** (+0.1h, +2.4h, +1.9h), `p90_cycle_h` rise by **+2.70h**, while `rework_pct` and `escalation_pct` were **0.00 pp** unchanged. In contrast, Alpha and Beta averaged a `median_cycle_h` change of **-0.10h** (-1.1h and +0.9h), yielding a raw on-impact DiD of **-1.57h** despite absorbing a +10% routing shock. More strikingly, `rework_pct` fell by **-2.20 pp** (DiD = **-2.20 pp**), and `escalation_pct` rose by **+0.90 pp** (DiD = **+0.90 pp**).
* **Late Cohort (Gamma) On-Impact DiD (Jun $\to$ Jul vs. Never-Treated Delta, Epsilon):**
  Between June and July, Complexity and FTEs were completely constant across Gamma, Delta, and Epsilon. Never-treated Delta and Epsilon experienced a seasonal arrival dip (-2.6%), causing their `median_cycle_h` to fall by **-3.85h** (-3.1h and -4.6h) and `p90_cycle_h` to fall by **-7.20h** (-5.8h and -8.6h), with **0.00 pp** change in rework or escalation. Simultaneously, Gamma adopted automation (and absorbed a +5% routing increase, so its arrivals *rose* +2.2%). Gamma's `median_cycle_h` fell by **-7.50h** (DiD = **-3.65h**), `p90_cycle_h` fell by **-13.90h** (DiD = **-6.70h**), `rework_pct` fell by **-2.00 pp** (DiD = **-2.00 pp**), `escalation_pct` rose by **+1.00 pp** (DiD = **+1.00 pp**), and `csat_1to5` jumped **+0.160** vs **+0.065** in controls (DiD = **+0.095**).
* **Long-Window DiD (Jan–Mar vs. Jul–Aug for Alpha/Beta vs. Delta/Epsilon):**
  Comparing Jan–Mar to Jul–Aug, never-treated controls worsened by **+3.64h** on median cycle time and **+14.72h** on p90 cycle time due to higher complexity and volume. ### 3. Treatment-Intensity (Dose-Response) Analysis
Replacing the binary adoption indicator with actual utilization (`automated_fast_path_pct`) in a two-way fixed-effects model controlling for branch fixed effects, month fixed effects, workload (`arrivals/fte`), and `high_complexity_pct` across all $N=40$ observations yields strong conditional associations:
* **`median_cycle_h`:** Each **+10 percentage point** increase in `automated_fast_path_pct` is associated with a **-1.91h** reduction in median cycle time ($\text{coef} = -0.191$, clustered $p < 0.01$), while each additional arrival per FTE adds **+0.80h** and each +1 pp of complexity adds **+0.67h**. At a 40% fast-path share (+36 pp above the 4% baseline), this implies an adjusted median reduction of **-6.88h** ($\approx 14\%$ to $15\%$).
* **`p90_cycle_h`:** Each **+10 pp** increase in `automated_fast_path_pct` is associated with a **-2.99h** reduction in p90 cycle time ($\text{coef} = -0.299$), offset heavily by workload (+1.29h per arrival/FTE) and complexity (+2.33h per pp).
* **`rework_pct` and `escalation_pct`:** Notice that within each branch, `rework_pct` drops sharply at $k=0$ (-2.0 to -2.4 pp) and `escalation_pct` rises at $k=0$ (+0.8 to +1.0 pp), but both remain completely flat from June to August even as `automated_fast_path_pct` rises from 39% to 48% in Alpha and 41% to 50% in Beta. This arithmetic fact shows that **rework and escalation shifts are step-function consequences of turning on the automated triage rules at $k=0$**, whereas **cycle-time reductions scale continuously with fast-path utilization ($k \ge 1$)**.

### 4. Pre-Trends and Regression to the Mean (RTM)
Could the apparent gains in Alpha and Beta simply be regression to the mean (RTM) because they were selected for poor historical performance? Three arithmetic facts refute pure RTM:
1. **Stable Pre-Trends:** In Jan–Mar, Alpha (50.4h, 46.9h, 49.4h) and Beta (45.4h, 45.9h, 46.4h) were not experiencing temporary acute spikes; their cycle times were structurally high due to high scale/complexity and tracked parallel to Delta, Epsilon, and Gamma (which all rose ~2h–3h in March when the fraud desk launched).
2. **Adverse Post-Rollout Shocks:** RTM requires reverting toward a mean under stable conditions, yet Alpha and Beta were immediately hit with a +10% routing inflow in April and a +5 to +8 pp complexity hike and -2 FTE loss (Alpha) in May–June. Without a countervailing efficiency boost, their cycle times would have diverged upward, not converged downward.
3. **Replication in Gamma:** Gamma was not selected for poor performance (its Jan–Feb median was 36.4h, near Delta's 33.6h) and adopted after a readiness pilot. Yet when Gamma adopted in July, it exhibited the exact same mechanistic signature: a sharp drop in median cycle time (-3.65h DiD vs controls), a -2.0 pp drop in rework, and a +1.0 pp jump in escalation.

### 5. Sensitivity Analysis and Bounded Conclusions
Because simultaneous shocks (routing, FTE, complexity, fraud desk) prevent point identification, we bound the plausible causal effect of mature automation (~30%–45% fast-path share) on **E1 (`median_cycle_h`)** and **E3 (`p90_cycle_h`)**:
* **`median_cycle_h` Bounds:**
  * *Conservative Lower Bound (-2.5h to -3.7h, or ~6%–8% reduction):* Derived from Gamma's clean June-to-July DiD (-3.65h) and early cohort on-impact DiD (-1.57h at 25% usage), attributing none of the workload-adjusted benefit of absorbing the routing campaign to automation.
  * *Workload-Adjusted Upper Bound (-6.5h to -8.0h, or ~14%–16% reduction):* Derived from the covariate-adjusted panel model where +35–43 pp of fast-path usage offsets high arrival/FTE loads (-6.9h to -8.2h).
* **`p90_cycle_h` Bounds:**
  * *Adverse/Null Scenario (+2.0h to +7.0h increase in raw tail delay):* Under the observed operational bundle (where automation is paired with +10% routing, FTE reductions, and +1 pp escalation into a congested central fraud desk), raw p90 cycle time in Alpha and Beta remained +6.9h to +9.5h above baseline in Jul–Aug. Furthermore, if automation caused fraud-desk spillovers that inflated control-branch p90 times by $S = 3\text{h to }5\text{h}$, then DiD estimates overstate tail benefits by $2S$.
  * *Ceteris Paribus Adjusted Scenario (-6.7h to -11.0h reduction relative to counterfactual overload):* Holding workload and complexity constant (as seen in Gamma's June-to-July DiD of -6.70h and the dose-response slope of -2.99h per 10 pp fast-path), automation prevents the much worse +12.6h to +16.9h tail explosion suffered by untreated Delta and Epsilon.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**Decision:** `expand with an embedded evaluation`

Leadership should reject a blanket "automation reduces cycle time by X%" claim and instead execute a **phased, evaluation-embedded rollout to Epsilon first, keeping Delta as an untouched holdout control for 8 weeks**, before transitioning Delta.

### Target Branch and Phase-In Mechanism
**Epsilon** should receive automation next (e.g., October 1), while **Delta** remains on manual triage until December 1. Epsilon has the highest complexity (38.0%), highest rework (16.2%), and heavy volume (867–960 arrivals), making it the highest-value operational target and the closest structural match to Alpha. Because Alpha, Beta, and Gamma are now in steady-state usage (32%–50%), introducing Epsilon while holding Delta constant creates a clean stepped-wedge contrast during autumn without confounding routing or FTE changes. Furthermore, within Epsilon, rollout should use **case-level randomized or alternating routing** (e.g., routing 80% of eligible incoming registrations to automated triage and holding out a random 20% for manual triage for 60 days) to identify within-branch path effects free of macro shocks.

### Minimum Measurement Windows and Data Collection
Maintain a minimum **8-week pre-period** (already available from Jul–Aug plus September) and an **8-week post-period** (October–November) before converting Delta. Crucially, leadership must stop relying solely on branch-month summaries and extract **case-level event logs** (`case_id`, `branch`, `activity`, `start_timestamp`, `end_timestamp`, `resource_id`, `risk_class`) to measure:
1. **Stage-specific waiting vs. service times:** Separate queue wait time before triage, triage execution time, local specialist wait time, and **centralized fraud-desk wait time**.
2. **Conditional rework:** Measure rework rates *specifically among escalated cases* (`Register -> Automated Triage -> Specialist Review -> Rework`), not just branch-wide averages diluted by fast-path cases.
3. **Stratified CSAT:** Log survey invitations and responses by path (`fast_path` vs `escalated`) to correct for nonresponse bias via inverse-probability weighting.

### Spillover Monitoring, Guardrails, and Scale-Up Rule
* **Operational Freeze:** Freeze discretionary routing campaigns and FTE reductions in Epsilon and Delta during the 8-week evaluation window.
* **Spillover Monitoring:** Track weekly arrival volumes and queue lengths at the centralized fraud desk by originating branch.
* **Customer Harm Guardrails:** Trigger an automatic threshold review if (a) Epsilon's `escalation_pct` rises by $>1.5$ pp, (b) conditional rework on escalated cases rises by $>2.0$ pp, or (c) fraud-desk wait times increase by $>15\%$.
* **Scale-Up Decision Rule:** Proceed to full rollout in Delta on December 1 if Epsilon achieves $\ge 25\%$ `automated_fast_path_pct` within 6 weeks, demonstrates a statistically verifiable reduction in case-level median cycle time relative to holdout cases and Delta, and keeps branch `p90_cycle_h` within $\le 5\%$ of Delta's trend.

## 10. Final claim ladder

* `Observed:` Following the activation of automated triage in Alpha and Beta (April) and Gamma (July), actual utilization of the automated fast path rose from a 4%–6% baseline to 25%–27% in the first month and 32%–50% by August, coinciding with an immediate 2.0 to 2.4 percentage point drop in branch-wide `rework_pct` and a 0.8 to 1.0 percentage point increase in `escalation_pct` at the exact month of adoption across all three treated branches.
* `Conditionally causal:` Under the assumptions that workload per FTE and recorded case complexity adequately capture concurrent routing, staffing, and product shocks—and that fraud-desk spillovers onto control branches are modest—achieving a 30%–50% automated fast-path share reduces branch median cycle time by roughly 2.5 to 7.0 hours (6%–15%) relative to what those branches would have experienced under manual triage, primarily by diverting routine cases away from manual triage queues.
* `Suggestive:` Automated triage appears to buffer 90th-percentile tail cycle times (`p90_cycle_h`) and respondent customer satisfaction (`csat_1to5`) against severe degradation during high-complexity surges when compared against never-treated branches (Delta and Epsilon), where p90 delays grew by 12.6 to 16.9 hours between Q1 and late summer.
* `Not supported:` A single unqualified statement that "automation reduces cycle time by X%"—or that automation unconditionally reduces raw tail delays (`p90_cycle_h`) and improves true population customer satisfaction—is scientifically indefensible given non-random branch selection, simultaneous volume/staffing/complexity shocks, unrecorded wait times at the shared centralized fraud desk, and unquantified 20%–45% survey response rates.
