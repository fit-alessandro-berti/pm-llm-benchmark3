## 1. Leakage and temporal-consistency findings

At `t0`, the observed prefix ends immediately after the first `Coverage Check Completed`. A value is admissible only if both the underlying fact and the recorded version used by the model were available by `t0`. The reported importances, contributions, and probabilities come from a model containing future information; they cannot be carried over numerically after repair.

## 2. Process-model consistency checks

1. **Reconstruct the exact checkpoint.** Identify the first coverage-completion event per case. Build its prefix using event time, *availability/ingestion time*, and a reliable sequence index. For timestamp ties without that index, keep a partial order or an “unknown order” state; do not silently sort by activity name or ingestion position. Require every feature and every source record to have been available by `t0`. Test source snapshot joins separately, including the ±5-minute queue tolerance.
2. **Audit feature dependencies.** Trace each feature to raw events and warehouse fields. Flag dependencies on closure, final decision, full-trace aggregations or variants, later employee records, extraction-date joins, and same-day snapshots produced after `t0`. Recompute candidate features on the truncated prefix and compare them with production values. As a counterfactual leakage test, change or remove the suffix while holding the prefix fixed: a valid `t0` score and explanation must not change.
3. **Check activity feasibility against the normative process.** Validate registration before coverage; the fast-track damage-assessment path or one or more ordered request/receive/validate cycles; assessment before a settlement decision; a fraud investigation only after a supported trigger, while allowing parallel work; appeal only after Partial or Deny; and closure only after a final decision or withdrawal. Use trace alignment or declarative constraints to locate violations. Treat a frequent directly-follows-graph (DFG) edge as empirical evidence, not permission to override these rules. A DFG also cannot prove concurrency or the order of equal-timestamp events.
4. **Classify explanation references relative to `t0`.** Mark an activity as observed in the prefix, currently enabled by the known state, merely possible in a future branch, impossible under the process, or unknown because ordering/trigger information is missing. At this checkpoint, a document request or fast-track assessment is a possible next branch; receipt requires a request; an appeal is only future-possible after a qualifying decision. A following fraud opening needs a trigger. None of these future events may be described as already observed.
5. **Handle decisions and timestamp ties explicitly.** Match each appeal to an earlier Partial or Deny decision and allow a later reconsideration decision; two occurrences of the coarse `Settlement Decision Recorded` label can therefore be legitimate. For `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`, the equal timestamp without a sequence index supports neither that order nor a valid reopened path. Record an unresolved ordering/data-quality exception and seek source-system evidence before treating it as a process violation. Conformance checks diagnose chronology and semantics; they do not by themselves prove why a claim breaches.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

| Class | Signals | What may be claimed | What may not be claimed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defensible predictive signal | Frozen `initial_damage_complexity`; `prior_claim_count_before_t0` only after its historical cutoff is repaired; `initial_reserve` or `claimant_channel` only if their registration-time versions are verified. | These values may help forecast breach risk in out-of-time validation. Complexity or reserve can stand for anticipated workload or claim size. | That changing the value would change settlement time, or that prior claimants or brokers inherently cause delays. |
| Process-state or congestion signal | As-of `queue_length_at_t0`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, and an observed prefix state. At later checkpoints, observed evidence-cycle counts or waiting times may also qualify. | The process has a measured queue and has consumed a measured amount of time; these can be useful early-warning signals. | That congestion caused this particular breach, or that an unobserved future cycle has already begun. An operational intervention needs evidence that changing the queue or workflow improves outcomes. |
| Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review | `claimant_region` and, if verified, `claimant_channel`; potentially other correlated service-access variables. | They can be predictive associations if stable, legally and operationally appropriate, and acceptably calibrated across groups. Investigating repair-network coverage, language support, and service levels may yield actionable hypotheses. | That region, socioeconomic status, or channel is itself a causal delay driver, or that predictive accuracy alone settles fairness or use policy. |
| Drift/calendar proxy | `calendar_week` derived at `t0`. | A week can summarize seasonal load or an observed policy regime and improve forecasts if it generalizes. | That the calendar week itself causes delays, or that an effect aligned with a holiday and policy change will persist unchanged. |
| Unusable until lineage is repaired | `risk_score_current`, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, extraction-date employee experience, and any intended `t0` team or role reconstructed from `final_handler_team`/`last_resource_role`. | After historical versions and availability are established, repaired *as-of* values may be tested as new predictors. | That their current warehouse values were known at `t0`. Full-trace duration, appeal, variant, request total, final decision, and observed suffix patterns cannot be repaired into the same `t0` facts; only new prefix-only counterparts are eligible. |

Predictive validity requires a feature that was available at the checkpoint and that improves out-of-time forecasts. Causal interpretation requires a separate design that addresses confounding and process feedback. Operational actionability requires a feasible intervention with evidence of benefit and acceptable side effects. Fairness review asks whether use or error rates disadvantage groups, including through proxies; it is a separate gate, not an automatic reason to delete every correlated feature.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **Immediate containment and feature-store repair.** Stop presenting the current probabilities and SHAP-like values as valid `t0` explanations. Define a checkpoint-specific feature contract. Store event time, source-record time, ingestion/availability time, effective version interval, feature computation time, and lineage for every input. Make as-of joins require availability by `t0`; reject extraction-date joins and future-tolerant queue joins. Version mutable team, role, reserve, score, and employee attributes.
2. **Prefix-only recomputation and backtesting.** Rebuild training and validation rows from prefixes ending at the first coverage completion, with no suffix dependency. Repair prior-claim SQL and document snapshots, remove complete-trace outcomes and patterns, and audit all unnamed features. Use chronological held-out periods, including the policy change and holiday period. Retrain, recalibrate, and report discrimination and calibration after feature removal; do not preserve the old 0.82, 0.71, or 0.64 by assumption.
3. **Process-conformance gates.** Automate the chronology, branching, evidence-cycle, fraud-trigger, appeal, repeated-decision, and closure checks described above. Preserve uncertainty for equal timestamps without a reliable sequence index. Test that a future suffix edit cannot change a `t0` feature, score, or explanation. Investigate violations rather than turning every unusual DFG edge into a forbidden transition.
4. **Explanation quality and fairness.** Regenerate local and global explanations on the repaired model. Check stability across refits, time windows, correlated feature sets, and reasonable queue-snapshot choices; distinguish attribution direction from a causal mechanism. Examine region, channel, and related proxies for subgroup calibration, error rates, adverse impact, and performance under policy or network changes. Validate any proposed operational action with an intervention design rather than SHAP importance.
5. **Ongoing monitoring.** Alert on schema and join changes, missing or late timestamps, snapshot lag, unexpected event orders, new process variants, decision/appeal frequencies, queue distribution shifts, and performance or calibration drift by time and subgroup. Re-audit feature lineage and explanations whenever the workflow or operating policy changes.
