# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

**Demand.** Each two-hour block's `expected_arrivals` is treated as the mean of a Poisson count (labelled assumption: arrivals are stochastic, not deterministic, so I can report seed variance). Within a block, arrival instants are Uniform. Priority and case-type are drawn from the block's percentage vectors, renormalised after any scenario transform.

**Service ranges → distributions.** Each activity is a two-component mixture: with probability `complex-tail probability` draw Uniform(complex_lo, complex_hi), else Uniform(ordinary_lo, ordinary_hi) (assumption: uniform within each band because only endpoints are given; this is a bounded, defensible choice and I also carry the analytic means for the capacity bounds). Effective time = base draw × resource multiplier. Means before multipliers, used throughout §2: Intake 6.94, Mechanical 26.3, Electrical 32.8, Software 38.5, Validation 15.7 min.

**ME/ES parallelism decision.** A decision variable θ. My default rule `prio_or_big`: parallelise if the case is P1, or if the summed ordinary-band mean of its two branches exceeds 60 min; otherwise run sequentially on one cross-skilled resource. Parallel adds the 8-min pre-Validation coordination and needs two resources at once; sequential avoids the 8 min and the cross-team +5, but lengthens the critical path and incurs a 5-min setup if one resource switches skill family. This trade-off is exposed, not hard-coded to one branch.

**Penalties (all explicit constants in §8).** Resource change between stages +7; mech↔(elec/soft) family move +5 unless the same cross-skilled resource retains the case; consecutive-case skill-family switch on a resource +5 setup; parallel coordination +8; preemption restart = 10% of remaining active service, at most once per case; overtime ≤30 min/resource.

**Decision variables of a policy.** (i) eligibility set E(skill) — who may perform a skill; (ii) a priority score used to order the queue; (iii) a reservation set R protecting P1 capacity; (iv) a preemption rule π; (v) the parallelisation threshold θ; (vi) an overflow trigger that widens E under load.

**Two objective formulations (both used).**

- **O1 — SLA/tail-risk (lexicographic with tail penalty).** Maximise, in order, P1 SLA attainment, then P2 SLA, then overall completion, then minimise the p95 sojourn of completed work. Formally minimise `L = (1−P1_SLA, 1−P2_SLA, 1−completion, p95_delay)` compared lexicographically. This is the operator-facing "protect the urgent, then clear the board" objective.
- **O2 — robust-fairness (minimax / CVaR).** Maximise the worst-case P1 SLA across scenarios s∈{base,U1..U5}, subject to Jain utilisation-fairness ≥ 0.90 and planned max-utilisation ≤ 0.90: `max_π min_s P1_SLA(π,s)  s.t.  Jain(π,s)≥0.9, maxutil≤0.9`. Equivalently one can replace `min_s` with CVaR at the 20% worst tail of seeds×scenarios. This is the resilience/fairness objective the "unfair above 90%" rule points to.

The two objectives can disagree, which is why the recommendation in §5 is conditional on which one governs.

# 3. Candidate policies

All three share the process route and the penalty model; they differ in eligibility, scoring, reservation, and overflow.

**P-Strict — specialisation with fixed queues.** Eligibility is fixed ownership: Intake {Ada, Ben}; Mechanical {Ben, Deepa}; Electrical {Chen, Emil}; Software {Chen, Emil}; Validation {Ben, Deepa, Emil}. Priority score = (priority rank, then FIFO by ready time). Reservation: none beyond ownership. Preemption: P1 may preempt a lower-priority job **only within the same fixed queue**. Parallelisation: sequential unless P1. Overflow: none — this is the audit baseline. Handover is naturally low because ownership keeps cases within a small resource set. Failure mode: no relief valve when an owner is absent or a family surges.

**P-Pool — full pooling with a transparent dispatch rule.** Eligibility = every qualified resource (the skill matrix). Dispatch is **resource-centric, work-conserving**: whenever a resource frees, it takes the highest-priority, earliest-ready job it is qualified for, breaking ties by *its own* speed multiplier for that skill (fastest-qualified-first). Preemption: any idle-blocked P1 with no free eligible resource preempts the lowest-priority in-service job on an eligible resource (once per case; 10% restart penalty). Parallelisation: θ = `prio_or_big`. Overflow is implicit (everyone is always eligible). Maximises completion and fairness; cost is more handovers/setups and skill churn.

**P-Hybrid — reserved P1 capacity + controlled cross-skill overflow.** Eligibility = full pool, **but** one fast resource per family is *reserved* for P1: Ben (mechanical), Chen (electrical/software). Non-P1 work uses a reserved resource only if no other eligible resource can start now (soft reservation, implemented as a dispatch penalty so it degrades gracefully rather than hard-blocking). Priority score = (priority, ready, speed) as in P-Pool. Preemption and θ as in P-Pool. Overflow trigger: the reservation is *released* to non-P1 work automatically when no P1 is waiting — so reserved capacity is never idle if P1 demand is absent. This is the O1-optimising policy.

Optional extensions encoded as knobs (θ, reservation set R, the fastest-qualified-first score) so operators can tune without changing code paths: shortest-expected-remaining-processing ordering (swap the tie-break), dynamic reservation (grow R when the AM P1 share exceeds a threshold), and a fairness penalty in the score (down-weight an over-utilised resource).

# 5. Robust recommendation

**Primary: P-Hybrid.** Under **O1 (protect P1, then P2, then throughput)** it is the top or joint-top policy in every scenario, delivering the best or equal-best P1 SLA (base 0.71; and the highest floor under morning surge, 0.74 @U1) while matching pooling on completion, delay, and fairness. Under **O2 (worst-case P1 with fairness ≥0.9, util ≤0.9)** hybrid and pooling are statistically tied on the worst-case floor (min P1 SLA ≈0.57–0.59) and both satisfy the fairness/util constraints (Jain ≈0.99, max-util ≤0.83); hybrid is preferred as the tie-break because it protects P1 without a fairness or utilisation cost. It dominates strict decisively: strict's worst-case P1 SLA (0.26) violates any reasonable urgency objective.

**Fallback mode — "Strict-lite during forecastable P1 spikes only."** Pure strict is never recommended as steady state, but its *low-churn, auditable* behaviour is valuable when the pool would otherwise thrash. Concretely, hybrid should **narrow eligibility toward owners** (reduce cross-skill overflow) only when handover burden per case exceeds ~3 and P1 demand is low, to cut skill-family setups.

**Trigger thresholds for mode switching (operator-auditable):**
- **Grow the P1 reservation** (add Emil-electrical to R) when the rolling 1-hour P1 arrival share exceeds base+6pp *or* the incoming block's forecast P1 count ≥3 (fires in U1/U5 mornings).
- **Release all reservation** whenever no P1 has waited in the last 20 min (prevents reserved idle — the main hybrid risk).
- **Force parallelisation** (θ→always) for ME/ES when the electrical queue depth ≥3, to shorten critical paths during U2.
- **Declare afternoon overflow**: from 15:00, widen Validation and Mechanical eligibility to every on-shift resource and pre-authorise the 30-min overtime for Deepa/Emil, because the 16:00 cliff is the structural failure and only added evening capacity truly fixes it.

**When another policy is preferable.** If the governing objective were *pure fairness or minimum coordination cost* (e.g., a training day where skill-family switching is undesirable, or an audit where every routing decision must be trivially explainable), **P-Strict** is preferable despite worse SLA. If the objective were *raw completion with no P1 emphasis and forecasts are unreliable*, **P-Pool** is marginally simpler than hybrid with essentially identical completion and should be chosen to avoid reservation mis-tuning.

**Blunt caveat:** no policy meets the SLA targets in the afternoon/evening because the roster is under-resourced there (§2). Policy choice changes *who is protected* and *how gracefully the system degrades*; it cannot manufacture the missing afternoon capacity. The robust recommendation is P-Hybrid **plus** an evening-capacity fix (extend one mechanical-capable resource past 18:00, or shift Farah's 4-hour block later).

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

**Gaming of estimates.** "Expected remaining time" tie-breaks invite manipulation — a resource could log conservative service estimates to dodge hard jobs. Mitigation: score on *observed* historical service by (resource, skill), refreshed from the event log, not self-reported estimates; monitor for estimate-vs-actual drift (§7).

**P3 starvation.** Real and measured: base P3 completion ≈0.17 vs P1 ≈0.93. Strict priority plus an overloaded afternoon means P3 is the shock absorber. Mitigation: an ageing term in the score (promote P3 after it has waited > a fraction of its 24-h target), and an explicit floor (reserve ~1 late-shift slot for the oldest P3). Report P3 attainment as a first-class metric so the trade-off is visible, not hidden.

**Skill erosion.** Reservation and fastest-qualified-first concentrate software on Chen/Emil and electrical on the fast few; rarely-used competencies (e.g., Farah's software) atrophy and Ada's electrical stays slow. Mitigation: a small "training quota" — occasionally route non-urgent work to a slower qualified resource to preserve breadth, accepting a minor SLA cost.

**Excessive preemption.** The 10%-restart penalty plus the once-per-case cap limits churn, but frequent P1 spikes (U1/U5) can still thrash. Monitor preemption count/case; if it rises, prefer *reservation* over *preemption* (grow R) since reserved idle is cheaper than repeated restarts.

**Coordination overhead / new Validation bottleneck.** Pooling roughly doubles handovers (2.5 vs 1.7/case) and adds the +8 parallel coordination; because Validation is the last stage and everyone can do it, a burst of near-simultaneous diagnostic completions can pile onto Validation. Watch Validation queue-entry timestamps for a secondary bottleneck, especially when many parallel ME/ES finish together.

**Fairness and human factors.** Jain ≈0.99 for pooling/hybrid looks equitable in aggregate, but even utilisation can still mean unpleasant work (constant skill-switching, preemption interruptions) lands on the cross-skilled few (Farah, Emil, Ada). Fairness of *experience* ≠ fairness of *load*.

**Interpretability.** Strict is the most auditable ("why did case X go to Chen? because Chen owns electrical"). Hybrid's reservation-release and overflow triggers must be logged as explicit decisions (§7) or operators will distrust the routing.

    blocks,cx,absent,outage=scenario_tables(scn)
    cases=sample_cases(rng,blocks,cx); cases_by_id={c["cid"]:c for c in cases}
    res_free={r:SHIFT[r][0] for r in SKILL}; res_last_skill={r:None for r in SKILL}
    res_job={r:None for r in SKILL}; preempted_once=set()
    busy_minutes=defaultdict(float); counters=defaultdict(int)
    waiting=defaultdict(list); events=[]; seq=itertools.count(); heap=[]
    def push(t,kind,data): heapq.heappush(heap,(t,next(seq),kind,data))

    st={}
    for c in cases:
        diag=TYPE_ROUTE[c["ctype"]]
        parallel=(len(diag)==2) and (parallel_rule=="always" or
            (parallel_rule=="prio_or_big" and (c["prio"]=="P1" or
             sum(np.mean(SERVICE[a][:2]) for a in diag)>60)))   # theta rule
        st[c["cid"]]=dict(diag=diag,parallel=parallel,branch_left=0,diag_fin=0.0,
                          prev_res=None,done=None)
        push(c["arrival"],"ready",dict(cid=c["cid"],act="Intake",ready=c["arrival"]))
    for r in SKILL:                                             # wake at shift start & break end
        push(SHIFT[r][0],"wake",None)
        if BREAK[r]: push(BREAK[r][1],"wake",None)

    def enqueue(cid,act,ready):
        waiting[act].append(dict(cid=cid,prio=cases_by_id[cid]["prio"],ready=ready,
                                 prev_res=st[cid]["prev_res"]))

    def try_preempt(policy,skill,job,now):
        if job["prio"]!="P1": return None
        for r in eligible(policy,skill):
            if r in absent or r in preempted_once: continue
            cur=res_job[r]
            if cur is None: continue
            if PRIO_RANK[cur["prio"]]>PRIO_RANK[job["prio"]]:  # preempt lower-prio in service
                remaining=max(res_free[r]-now,0.0)
                cur["cancelled"]=True; preempted_once.add(r); counters['preempt']+=1
                busy_minutes[r]-=remaining; res_free[r]=now; res_job[r]=None
                enqueue(cur["cid"],cur["act"],now)             # requeue with restart penalty
                cases_by_id[cur["cid"]].setdefault("_pen",0.0)
                cases_by_id[cur["cid"]]["_pen"]+=PREEMPT_PENALTY*remaining
                return r
        return None

    def start_job(policy,skill,job,res,start,setup,now):
        c=cases_by_id[job["cid"]]; delay=setup
        had_h=0; had_x=0; had_s=1 if setup>0 else 0
        if setup>0: counters['setup']+=1
        if job["prev_res"] is not None and job["prev_res"]!=res:
            delay+=HANDOVER_MIN; had_h=1; counters['handover']+=1
            ls=st[job["cid"]].get("last_act")
            if ls and TEAM.get(ls,"none")!="none" and TEAM[skill]!="none" and TEAM.get(ls)!=TEAM[skill]:
                delay+=CROSS_TEAM_MIN; had_x=1; counters['cross_team']+=1
        s0=avail_from(res,start+delay,absent)
        if s0 is None: return False
        dur=c["base"][skill]*SKILL[res][skill]
        if skill!="Intake" and c.get("_pen",0)>0: dur+=c.pop("_pen")   # restart penalty lands here
        br=BREAK[res]
        if br and s0<br[0] and s0+dur>br[0]:                   # do not split across a break
            s0=avail_from(res,br[1],absent)
            if s0 is None: return False
        if s0+dur>SHIFT[res][1]+OVERTIME_MIN: return False     # overtime cap
        finish=s0+dur
        res_free[res]=finish; res_last_skill[res]=skill
        job2=dict(cid=job["cid"],act=skill,cancelled=False,prio=job["prio"])
        res_job[res]=job2; busy_minutes[res]+=dur
        st[job["cid"]]["prev_res"]=res; st[job["cid"]]["last_act"]=skill
        events.append(dict(case=job["cid"],act=skill,res=res,start=s0,finish=finish,
                           prio=c["prio"],ctype=c["ctype"],handover=had_h,cross_team=had_x,
                           setup=had_s,planned=c["base"][skill]*SKILL[res][skill]))
        push(finish,"finish",dict(res=res,job=job2,cid=job["cid"],act=skill,finish=finish))
        return True

    def dispatch(now):
        changed=True
        while changed:
            changed=False
            free_res=[r for r in SKILL if r not in absent and res_free[r]<=now+1e-9
                      and avail_from(r,now,absent)==now]
            for r in free_res:                                 # resource-centric, work-conserving
                if res_free[r]>now+1e-9: continue
                cands=[]
                for skill,q in list(waiting.items()):
                    if skill not in SKILL[r] or r not in eligible(policy,skill): continue
                    for job in q:
                        if job["ready"]<=now+1e-9: cands.append((skill,job))
                if not cands: continue
                cands.sort(key=lambda sj:(PRIO_RANK[sj[1]["prio"]],sj[1]["ready"],SKILL[r][sj[0]]))
                skill,job=cands[0]
                if policy=="hybrid" and job["prio"]!="P1" and RESERVED.get(skill)==r:
                    alt=[rr for rr in eligible(policy,skill) if rr!=r and rr not in absent
                         and res_free[rr]<=now+1e-9 and avail_from(rr,now,absent)==now]
                    if alt: continue                           # keep reserved resource for P1
                setup=SETUP_MIN if (res_last_skill[r] not in (None,skill) and
                                    TEAM.get(res_last_skill[r])!=TEAM.get(skill) and
                                    TEAM[skill]!="none") else 0.0
                if start_job(policy,skill,job,r,now,setup,now):
                    waiting[skill].remove(job); changed=True
            for skill,q in list(waiting.items()):              # P1 preemption pass
                for job in list(q):
                    if job["prio"]!="P1" or job["ready"]>now+1e-9: continue
                    free_now=[r for r in eligible(policy,skill) if r not in absent
                              and res_free[r]<=now+1e-9 and avail_from(r,now,absent)==now]
                    if free_now: continue
                    r=try_preempt(policy,skill,job,now)
                    if r is not None and start_job(policy,skill,job,r,now,0.0,now):
                        if job in waiting[skill]: waiting[skill].remove(job); changed=True

    def on_finish(data,now):
        cid=data["cid"]; act=data["act"]; job=data["job"]
        if job.get("cancelled"): return
        res_job[data["res"]]=None; s=st[cid]; diag=s["diag"]
        if act=="Intake":
            if len(diag)==1: enqueue(cid,diag[0],now)
            elif s["parallel"]:
                s["branch_left"]=2; s["diag_fin"]=now
                for a in diag: enqueue(cid,a,now)              # parallel: seize two resources
            else: s["seq_idx"]=0; enqueue(cid,diag[0],now)     # sequential
        elif act in ("Mechanical","Electrical","Software"):
            if len(diag)==1: enqueue(cid,"Validation",now)
            elif s["parallel"]:
                s["branch_left"]-=1; s["diag_fin"]=max(s["diag_fin"],now)
                if s["branch_left"]==0:
                    enqueue(cid,"Validation",s["diag_fin"]+PARALLEL_COORD_MIN)  # +8 coord
            else:
                s["seq_idx"]=s.get("seq_idx",0)+1
                enqueue(cid,diag[s["seq_idx"]],now) if s["seq_idx"]<len(diag) \
                    else enqueue(cid,"Validation",now)
        elif act=="Validation": s["done"]=now
        dispatch(now)

    while heap:
        t,_,kind,data=heapq.heappop(heap)
        if kind=="ready": enqueue(data["cid"],data["act"],data["ready"]); dispatch(t)
        elif kind=="wake": dispatch(t)
        elif kind=="finish": on_finish(data,t)

    rows=[dict(cid=c["cid"],prio=c["prio"],ctype=c["ctype"],arrival=c["arrival"],
               done=st[c["cid"]]["done"],
               delay=(st[c["cid"]]["done"]-c["arrival"] if st[c["cid"]]["done"] is not None else np.nan))
          for c in cases]
    mdf=pd.DataFrame(rows); edf=pd.DataFrame(events)
    util={r:busy_minutes[r]/max((SHIFT[r][1]-SHIFT[r][0]),1) for r in SKILL if r not in absent}
    return mdf,edf,util,dict(counters)

def jain(xs):
    xs=[x for x in xs if x is not None]
    if not xs: return float('nan')
    s=sum(xs); s2=sum(x*x for x in xs)
    return (s*s)/(len(xs)*s2) if s2>0 else float('nan')

def metrics_from_run(m,e,u,c):
    done=m["done"].notna(); dl=m.loc[done,"delay"]
    def sla(prio,target):
        sub=m[m["prio"]==prio]
        if len(sub)==0: return float('nan')
        return ((sub["done"].notna())&(sub["delay"]<=target)).sum()/len(sub)
    burden=(c.get("handover",0)+c.get("setup",0)+c.get("cross_team",0))/max(len(m),1)
    return dict(completion=done.mean(),
                mean_delay=dl.mean() if len(dl) else float('nan'),
                p90_delay=dl.quantile(0.90) if len(dl) else float('nan'),
                P1_SLA=sla("P1",SLA["P1"]),P2_SLA=sla("P2",SLA["P2"]),P3_SLA=sla("P3",SLA["P3"]),
                max_util=max(u.values()) if u else float('nan'),fairness=jain(list(u.values())),
                burden_per_case=burden,preempts=c.get("preempt",0))

SCENARIOS=["base","U1","U2","U3","U4","U5"]; POLICIES=["strict","pooling","hybrid"]
def sweep(seeds=range(10)):
    agg={}
    for pol in POLICIES:
        for scn in SCENARIOS:
            df=pd.DataFrame([metrics_from_run(*simulate(pol,scn,s)) for s in seeds])
            agg[(pol,scn)]=dict(mean=df.mean(numeric_only=True),sd=df.std(numeric_only=True))
    return agg
def fmt(m,s,d=2): return f"{m:.{d}f}\u00b1{s:.{d}f}"
def print_tables(agg):
    keys=["completion","mean_delay","p90_delay","P1_SLA","P2_SLA","max_util","fairness","burden_per_case"]
    for scn in SCENARIOS:
        print(f"\n=== scenario {scn} (mean\u00b1sd) ===")
        print(f"{'metric':16s}"+"".join(f"{p:>16s}" for p in POLICIES))
        for k in keys:
            line=f"{k:16s}"
            for p in POLICIES:
                mm=agg[(p,scn)]['mean'][k]; ss=agg[(p,scn)]['sd'][k]
                line+=f"{fmt(mm,ss,0 if k.endswith('delay') else 2):>16s}"
            print(line)
def resilience_table(agg):
    print("\n=== robustness: worst value across U1-U5 ===")
    print(f"{'policy':8s}{'min_compl':>11s}{'min_P1_SLA':>12s}{'min_P2_SLA':>12s}{'max_delay':>11s}{'base_compl':>12s}")

        print(f"{p:8s}{min(x['completion'] for x in shock):11.2f}"
              f"{min(x['P1_SLA'] for x in shock):12.2f}{min(x['P2_SLA'] for x in shock):12.2f}"
              f"{max(x['mean_delay'] for x in shock):11.0f}{base['completion']:12.2f}")

def build_event_log(policy="hybrid",scn="base",seed=0):
    m,e,u,c=simulate(policy,scn,seed); base=pd.Timestamp("2025-06-02 00:00:00"); df=e.copy()
    df["case:concept:name"]=df["case"].astype(str); df["concept:name"]=df["act"]

    df["time:timestamp"]=[base+pd.Timedelta(minutes=float(x)) for x in df["finish"]]
    df=df.sort_values("start_timestamp").reset_index(drop=True)
    return df[["case:concept:name","concept:name","start_timestamp","time:timestamp",
               "org:resource","handover","setup","cross_team","planned"]]

def pm4py_analysis(df):
    import pm4py
    log=pm4py.format_dataframe(df.copy(),case_id="case:concept:name",activity_key="concept:name",
                              timestamp_key="time:timestamp",start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")
    # CASE-LEVEL service/waiting/sojourn (labelled as case-level, NOT stage-level)
    cw=pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(log,service_time_column="@@service_time",
        waiting_time_column="@@waiting_time",sojourn_time_column="@@sojourn_time",
        start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",activity_key="concept:name")
    case_level=(cw.groupby("case:concept:name")
                  .agg(svc=("@@service_time","first"),wait=("@@waiting_time","first"),
                       soj=("@@sojourn_time","first")).reset_index())
    for col in ["svc","wait","soj"]: case_level[col]=case_level[col]/60.0   # -> minutes
    # STAGE-LEVEL (per-activity) service time, computed SEPARATELY
    svc=pm4py.get_service_time(log,aggregation_measure="mean",start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
                               timestamp_key="time:timestamp",case_id_key="case:concept:name",
                               activity_key="concept:name")
    svc_min={k:v/60.0 for k,v in svc.items()}
    han=pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log,resource_key="org:resource",
            timestamp_key="time:timestamp",case_id_key="case:concept:name")
    return case_level,svc_min,han

# 9. Limits and validation plan

**What cannot be concluded from expected arrivals and service ranges alone.** (1) Nothing about *variance-driven* congestion: expected counts hide the burstiness that actually breaks SLA — two P1s arriving in the same minute is invisible in a mean. (2) The uniform-within-band service assumption is unverified; real tails may be heavier or bimodal, which would change the Software bottleneck severity (U4 is a crude proxy). (3) Correlations are unknown: are complex-tail cases correlated with priority, or clustered in time? (4) The absolute SLA numbers are *model artefacts of an under-resourced afternoon*, not calibrated predictions — the reliable content is the **ranking and the failure modes**, not the decimals. (5) Human effects (fatigue, setup learning, handover errors) and the true cost of preemption interruptions are not identifiable from the given tables. (6) My scheduler is a transparent greedy heuristic, not an optimum, so it may understate the best achievable throughput of any policy.

**Pilot / backtest that protects P1 and avoids unsafe rollout.**
- **Backtest first.** Replay 4–8 weeks of historical event logs through each policy's dispatch rule in *shadow mode* (log the counterfactual assignment without acting). Compare shadow P1/P2/P3 SLA, handover counts, and per-resource load against what actually happened. This distinguishes the policies on real arrival/service correlations at zero patient risk.
- **Guarded A/B pilot.** Run hybrid vs the incumbent on alternating days (or randomised at the case level *only for P2/P3*), with **P1 never randomised** — every P1 always gets the most protective routing (hybrid reservation + preemption) regardless of arm. Pre-register the primary endpoint (P1 SLA non-inferiority, then P2 SLA superiority) and a fairness guardrail (Jain ≥ 0.9, no resource > 0.9 planned util).
- **Stopping rules.** Halt the pilot immediately if P1 SLA in any arm drops below the incumbent by a pre-set margin, if P3 completion falls below its floor (starvation guard), or if preemption/case exceeds a threshold. Ramp exposure only after each shock analogue (a real absence day ≈ U3, a real surge ≈ U1) has been observed without a P1 breach.
- **Instrumentation to validate the model.** Emit the §7 attributes from day one and reconcile *planned vs actual* service and the discovered handover network against the simulation; a divergence tells you which assumption (service distribution, handover cost, parallelisation) to recalibrate before any full rollout.
- **Capacity caveat as a gate.** Because §2 shows the afternoon/evening is the true constraint, the pilot must be paired with the evening-capacity fix; otherwise it will "prove" that all policies miss SLA after 16:00 and wrongly implicate the routing rather than the roster.

Two things worth flagging in one place, since they drive everything: the roster is **structurally short in the afternoon/evening** (offered load ρ≈1.6 after 16:00 when three of six resources leave, and *no one* can do Mechanical after 18:00), so completion collapses block-by-block (0.85→0.00) regardless of policy — the routing choice changes *who is protected and how gracefully it degrades*, not whether SLA is met late in the day. And because ~40–60% of cases don't finish, **mean/p90 delay are survivorship-biased**, so I lean on SLA-attainment and completion as the real discriminators. On those, pooling and hybrid dominate strict everywhere, hybrid edges pooling on P1 protection (its whole reason to exist), and U3 (Chen absent) is the clean stress test where strict's P1 SLA falls to 0.26 while hybrid holds 0.69.
