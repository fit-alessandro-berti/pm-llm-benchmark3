## 2. Competing Explanations and Timeline

```
Timeline of System Performance & Drift Drivers (2026)
==================================================================================================
Jan-Mar: Stable Baseline (AUROC ~0.81, Maturity 100%, Breach Rate ~0.18)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Apr:     [DATA-PIPELINE FAILURE] Schema renaming -> 17% UNKNOWN, Missing features, AUROC 0.67
May:     [PARTIAL PIPELINE REPAIR] Mapping patch deployed, derived features uncomputed, AUROC 0.78
Jun:     [COVARIATE DRIFT] Policy shift -> Remote V2 increases to 52%, Breach Rate drops to 0.15
Jul:     [PROCESS DRIFT SETS IN] Supplier delay begins (65h edge time), AUROC drops to 0.76
Aug:     [CONCEPT DRIFT] Supplier failure severe (130h edge time), V3 breaches 64%, AUROC 0.68
Sep:     [POPULATION SHIFT + DRIFT] Partner Z onboarded (24% share, rural/V3 heavy), AUROC 0.66
Oct:     [LABEL CENSORING] Outcome API backlog -> 55% maturity, 52d delay, false breach drop (0.16)
==================================================================================================
```

### January – March 2026: Baseline Operational Stability
The system operated in a stable regime. Volume averaged 3,300 cases/month with 100% label maturity (median delay 33 days). Observed breach rates (0.18–0.19) closely tracked mean predicted risk (0.18–0.19). Discriminative power was stable (AUROC 0.81–0.82), calibration error was minimal (ECE 0.022–0.025), and data quality was clean ($<0.3\%$ unknown activities, 1% missing features). Variant mix was steady: 45% V1, 35% V2, 20% V3.

### April 2026: Schema Migration and Pipeline Failure
On 1 April, a source system migration renamed `Initial Diagnosis Completed` to `Diagnostic Assessment Completed`. The production inference pipeline did not recognize the new string, causing prefix generation to fail or trigger at incorrect stages, while downstream logs recorded 17.0% `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` and 18% unmapped variants. Key features dependent on event history (e.g., queue duration) became 14% missing. Mean predicted risk spiked artificially to 0.28 while actual breaches remained constant at 0.19, degrading AUROC to 0.67 and ECE to 0.110. **This was a pure data-pipeline failure, not operational process degradation.**

### May 2026: Partial Feature Backfill Recovery
A patch mapped `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` on 6 May, reducing `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` to 2.0% and missing features to 3%. However, historical derived features (such as cached counts of events prior to diagnosis) were not recomputed for April cases. Consequently, performance partially recovered (AUROC 0.78, ECE 0.050), but subtle feature errors persisted in transformed data.

### June 2026: Routing Policy Shift (Covariate Drift)
On 1 June, management expanded remote resolution (V2), causing V2 share to rise from 34% to 52% and V1 share to fall to 30%. Because V2 cases inherently have low breach probability (0.09–0.10) compared to V1 (0.16) or V3 (0.35), the overall population breach rate dropped to 0.15. The model’s mean predicted risk (0.20) slightly overpredicted because feature inputs shifted toward remote execution paths without changing the fundamental mechanics ($P(Y \mid X)$ within variants remained stable). AUROC remained strong at 0.79. **This represents pure covariate drift ($P(X)$ shift).**

### July – August 2026: Parts Supplier Latency & Concept Drift
On 10 July, a major parts supplier missed SLAs. The DFG edge duration for `Order Part -> Part Received` rose from 38h (May) to 65h (Jul) and 130h (Aug). V3 cases (parts-dependent) expanded from 22% to 35% of overall volume, while the matured breach rate for V3 surged from 0.35 to 0.64 in August. Because features extracted at `Initial Diagnosis Completed` had no visibility into downstream external supplier delays, the model continued predicting a mean risk of 0.19 against an actual breach rate of 0.28. AUROC plummeted to 0.68 and ECE rose to 0.120. **This is structural concept drift ($P(Y \mid X)$ breakdown) driven by physical process drift on the parts procurement edge.**

### September 2026: Partner Z Onboarding (Population Shift)
Partner Z was onboarded on 1 September, accounting for 24% of volume. Partner Z operates in rural regions with older devices and routes 58% of its cases through V3 (versus 36% for non-Partner Z). Overall observed breach rate rose to 0.31 (Partner Z observed breach rate was 0.45 vs. 0.27 for legacy cases). Crucially, key predictive attributes (rural travel distance, device age) were absent from the inference payload. The model's mean predicted risk (0.21) severely under-predicted risk, dragging AUROC to 0.66 and ECE to 0.140. **This represents population shift compounded by missing domain features, overlaying the ongoing parts supplier failure.**

### October 2026: Outcome API Backlog and Label Censoring Bias
In October, an outcome API backlog delayed label collection, reducing label maturity to 55% at cutoff (median label delay spiked to 52 days). Because complex cases (V3, Partner Z, rural repairs) take longer to complete, unresolved cases in the system at reporting cutoff were disproportionately high-risk cases that had not yet breached. The observed breach rate among available labels appeared to fall artificially to 0.16 (compared to 0.31 in September), creating a false impression of process recovery. Mean predicted risk stood at 0.23, and apparent AUROC fell to 0.64. **This failure is caused by right-censoring bias and label delay, masking real underlying risk.**

## 3. Diagnostic Tests

### Test 1: Label-Maturity Curves & Fixed-Vintage Backtesting
* **Method**: For each cohort (Jan–Oct), compute observed breach rates at fixed maturation vintages (30, 60, 90 days post case creation) rather than at cutoff. Construct maturity curves $R(t) = \frac{\text{Breaches closed by day } t}{\text{Total cases started}}$.
* **Expected Result under Label Censoring (October Hypothesis)**: October’s observed breach rate at day 30 will be severely depressed (~0.12), but will match September’s high rate (~0.32) when evaluated at day 60/90 once backlogged labels mature.
* **Expected Result under Real Process Recovery**: October's breach rate remains low (~0.16) even at 60/90 days maturity.

### Test 2: Pipeline Schema Normalization & Feature Recomputation
* **Method**: Re-parse raw April–May event logs using normalized activity mappings (`Diagnostic Assessment Completed` $\rightarrow$ `Initial Diagnosis Completed`), apply uniform post-April 20 duplicate removal logic, and recompute all derived temporal and event-count features.
* **Expected Result under Pipeline Failure Hypothesis (April)**: ECE drops from 0.110 back to $<0.035$, and AUROC recovers from 0.67 to $>0.78$.
* **Expected Result under Genuine Operational Shift Hypothesis**: Performance metrics remain degraded (AUROC ~0.67) even after feature recomputation.

### Test 3: Stratified Discrimination and Calibration Analysis
* **Method**: Segment performance metrics (AUROC, Brier, ECE) by Process Variant (V1, V2, V3), Partner Source (Legacy vs. Partner Z), and Month.
* **Expected Result under Pure Covariate Drift (June Policy Shift)**: Stratified AUROC within V1, V2, and V3 individually remains stable ($\ge 0.79$), while overall unstratified ECE shows mild calibration bias due to variant weighting changes.
* **Expected Result under Concept Drift (August Supplier Failure)**: V1 and V2 maintain high AUROC ($\ge 0.78$) and low ECE, whereas V3 exhibits severe discrimination collapse (AUROC $<0.60$) and large under-prediction calibration error.

### Test 4: Performance-DFG Edge Latency Analysis
* **Method**: Extract median and 90th percentile transition durations on Directly-Follows Graph (DFG) edges (`Order Part -> Part Received`, `Dispatch Technician -> Repair Completed`) per month.
* **Expected Result under Process Drift (July–August Supplier Failure)**: DFG edge `Order Part -> Part Received` median time shows a sharp step-function increase (28h $\rightarrow$ 130h+), directly correlating with V3 breach rate elevation ($r > 0.85$).
* **Expected Result under Internal Technician Inefficiency Hypothesis**: Latency surges on `Dispatch Technician -> Repair Completed` edges while `Order Part -> Part Received` remains flat.

### Test 5: Conditional Outcome Stability Test $P(Y \mid X, \text{Variant})$
* **Method**: Fit logistic regression or GBDT models on historical data (Jan–May) and evaluate prediction residuals $Y - \hat{P}(Y \mid X)$ on June–September cohorts, controlling for variant path and explicit diagnosis features.
* **Expected Result under Concept Drift**: Residuals for V3 cases in Aug–Sep show systematic positive bias ($\text{Mean Residual} > +0.25$), confirming that historical diagnosis features no longer predict outcome probabilities accurately.
* **Expected Result under Stable Mechanics**: Mean residuals within each variant class remain close to zero ($\pm 0.02$).

### Test 6: Population Reweighting & Transportability Checks (Partner Z)
* **Method**: Apply Inverse Probability of Treatment Weighting (IPTW) or covariate propensity reweighting to balance Partner Z's population (rural distance, device age, variant mix) against Legacy cases in September.
* **Expected Result under Population Shift Hypothesis**: Weighting Partner Z cases to match Legacy covariate distributions reduces observed breach rate gap from 0.18 (0.45 vs 0.27) down to $<0.05$.
* **Expected Result under Subpopulation Operational Breakdown**: Partner Z breach rates remain elevated (0.45) even after propensity matching, indicating operational bottlenecks specific to Partner Z infrastructure.

### Test 7: Data Lineage & Feature Missingness Audit
* **Method**: Calculate missing value percentages, string encoding anomalies, and distribution statistics (KS-test / PSI) for raw and derived inference features across pipeline ingestion dates (April 1 – May 10).
* **Expected Result under Un-backfilled Derived Features (May Patch Audit)**: Features relying on prefix history (e.g., `events_prior_to_diagnosis`) display a step-change jump in zero-values or missingness for April cases processed before May 6.
* **Expected Result under Intact Pipeline**: Feature distributions match pre-April baseline distributions without point anomalies.

## 4. Recalibration-Versus-Retraining Decision

### Staged Decision Matrix

```
[Phase 1: Immediate Containment]
    Patch April schema in pipeline & recompute derived features
    Quarantine Oct (and late Sep) data from model retraining routines
    Apply static probability adjustment factors for V3 cases

[Phase 2: Short-Term Recalibration]
    Recalibrate prediction heads via Platt Scaling / Isotonic Regression
    Stratify calibration vectors by Process Variant (V1, V2, V3)

[Phase 3: Feature Engineering & Repair]
    Ingest supplier SLA / part-backorder queue latency into feature store
    Add Partner Z features: Rural Distance, Device Age, Partner ID

[Phase 4: Full Model Retraining]
    Retrain on clean, fully-matured cohorts (Jan-Jul + mature Aug/Sep)
    Deploy multi-head architecture (Variant-specific prediction branches)
```

1. **Phase 1: Immediate Containment (Days 1–5)**
   - Hardcode schema normalization mapping (`Diagnostic Assessment Completed` $\rightarrow$ `Initial Diagnosis Completed`) in the ingestion pipeline.
   - Freeze October data (and late September data with $<85\%$ maturity) from all training pipelines to prevent censoring contamination.
   - Inject an interim risk-multiplier (+0.25 risk score offset) for active V3 cases to compensate for parts delays until retrained models are live.

2. **Phase 2: Short-Term Recalibration (Weeks 2–3)**
   - Perform variant-stratified recalibration (Isotonic Regression or Platt Scaling) using mature June–July data.
   - Adjust baseline probability thresholds to account for the June policy shift (V2 mix expansion).

3. **Phase 3: Feature Architecture Repair (Weeks 3–4)**
   - Incorporate dynamic external features into the model input payload at `Initial Diagnosis Completed`:
     - Real-time supplier part lead-time expectations / stock status.
     - Partner Z domain features: `rural_travel_distance`, `device_age`, `partner_id`.
   - Complete historical backfill of derived prefix features for April–May cases.

4. **Phase 4: Full Retraining & Architecture Redesign (Weeks 5–6)**
   - Train a multi-head or variant-aware model (e.g., separate risk heads for V1/V2 vs. V3) using clean, fully matured data.

### Cohort Safety Classification for Training Data
* **Safe for Retraining**:
  * **Jan–Mar 2026**: Fully mature, high quality data (100% maturity, baseline process).
  * **Jun–Jul 2026**: Clean pipeline data; safe for capturing the V2 routing shift (provided July V3 cases include supplier latency context).
* **Conditionally Safe (Requires Pipeline Backfill/Correction)**:
  * **Apr–May 2026**: Safe **only after** re-parsing raw logs, normalizing activity labels, applying consistent duplicate handling, and recomputing derived event-count features.
* **Unsafe for Retraining (Exclude Immediately)**:
  * **Aug–Sep 2026**: Immature/censored labels; can only be used once labels reach $>95\%$ maturity and Partner Z features are populated.
  * **Oct 2026**: **Highly Unsafe**. 55% label maturity introduces extreme right-censoring bias that would severely underestimate breach probabilities.

### Segmentation Strategy: Segment-Specific vs. Variant-Specific Models
* **Decision**: Implement a **Variant-Aware Multi-Head Model Architecture** rather than completely separate models per variant.
* **Justification**:
  * **V2 (Remote Resolution)**: Predicts risk almost immediately with minimal variance (breach rate ~0.10). A dedicated low-complexity branch prevents V3 noise from distorting remote predictions.
  * **V3 (Parts-Dependent)**: Requires explicit integration of external procurement queue states. A dedicated prediction branch conditioned on part availability and travel distance ensures part supply degradation directly escalates risk scores.
  * **Partner Z Subpopulation**: Should be handled via dedicated feature inputs (`partner_id`, `rural_distance`) within the unified architecture rather than a separate model, enabling transfer learning from core operational data while capturing rural travel penalties.

### Python Process Evidence Extraction Script

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.conversion.log import converter as log_converter

def extract_monthly_process_evidence(csv_path: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Extracts auditable monthly process evidence, variant distributions,
    and DFG edge latencies from field service event logs.
    """
    # 1. Ingest Raw Event Data
    df = pd.read_csv(csv_path)
    df['timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(df['timestamp'])
    
    # 2. Schema Normalization (Fix April Pipeline Failure)
    label_mapping = {
        'Diagnostic Assessment Completed': 'Initial Diagnosis Completed'
    }
    df['activity'] = df['activity'].replace(label_mapping)
    
    # 3. Duplicate Handling Strategy (Deterministic Deduplication post-diagnosis)
    df = df.sort_values(['case_id', 'timestamp', 'event_id'])
    df = df.drop_duplicates(subset=['case_id', 'activity', 'timestamp'], keep='first')
    
    # Format for PM4Py compliance
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id_key='case_id',
        activity_key='activity',
        timestamp_key='timestamp'
    )
    
    # 4. Cohort Definitions: Event-Calendar vs Case-Start
    # Case-Start Cohort: Defines month based on case creation timestamp
    case_start_times = df.groupby('case_id')['timestamp'].min().reset_index()
    case_start_times.rename(columns={'timestamp': 'case_start_time'}, inplace=True)
    case_start_times['case_start_month'] = case_start_times['case_start_time'].dt.to_period('M')
    
    df = df.merge(case_start_times[['case_id', 'case_start_month']], on='case_id')
    
    monthly_summaries = []
    
    # Process monthly case-start cohorts
    for month, cohort_df in df.groupby('case_start_month'):
        # Convert cohort subset to PM4Py Event Log
        event_log = log_converter.apply(cohort_df)
        
        # Extract Process Variants
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(event_log)
        total_cases = len(cohort_df['case_id'].unique())
        
        # Count variants
        v1_count, v2_count, v3_count, unmapped_count = 0, 0, 0, 0
        for var_key, cases in variants.items():
            var_str = ",".join([act for act in var_key])
            n_cases = len(cases)
            if 'Remote Fix' in var_str:
                v2_count += n_cases
            elif 'Order Part' in var_str:
                v3_count += n_cases
            elif 'Dispatch Technician' in var_str:
                v1_count += n_cases
            else:
                unmapped_count += n_cases
                
        # Extract Performance-DFG (Edge Durations)
        # Specifically targeting 'Order Part' -> 'Part Received'
        perf_dfg, start_act, end_act = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(event_log)
        order_part_edge_median_hours = None
        
        for (src, target), stats in perf_dfg.items():
            if src == 'Order Part' and target == 'Part Received':
                # Median duration in hours
                order_part_edge_median_hours = stats['median'] / 3600.0
                break
                
        # Unknown Activity Share
        unknown_share = (cohort_df['activity'] == 'UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY').mean()
        
        monthly_summaries.append({
            'month': str(month),
            'total_cases': total_cases,
            'v1_share': round(v1_count / total_cases, 3) if total_cases else 0,
            'v2_share': round(v2_count / total_cases, 3) if total_cases else 0,
            'v3_share': round(v3_count / total_cases, 3) if total_cases else 0,
            'unmapped_share': round(unmapped_count / total_cases, 3) if total_cases else 0,
            'order_part_median_hours': round(order_part_edge_median_hours, 2) if order_part_edge_median_hours else None,
            'unknown_activity_share': round(unknown_share, 4)
        })
        
    return pd.DataFrame(monthly_summaries)
```

## 6. Risks of Acting on the Wrong Diagnosis

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                        MISDIAGNOSIS RISKS & MITIGATIONS                           |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  [1] Immature Retraining
      Risk: Training on censored Oct data introduces severe false-negative bias.
      Mitigation: Enforce hard label-maturation threshold (>90%) before inclusion.

  [2] Calibrating Over Pipeline Bug
      Risk: Fitting calibration layers to broken April data permanently warps model.
      Mitigation: Mandate pipeline verification tests prior to calibration updates.

  [3] Ignoring Supplier Concept Drift
      Risk: Retraining on feature space without supplier ETA context fails completely.
      Mitigation: Inject explicit supplier latency and inventory backorder features.

  [4] Misattributing Partner Z Shift
      Risk: Accusing model of bias or bad tuning when population drift is the cause.
      Mitigation: Perform stratified subgroup evaluations & incorporate domain features.
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Failure Mode 1: Retraining directly on immature October labels
* **Mechanism**: In October, only 55% of labels had matured, heavily biased toward fast-resolving non-breached V2 cases. Long-running V3 cases that will eventually breach remain open and unlabelled. Retraining a model on this cohort treats open/unlabelled cases as non-breaches (or excludes them), teaching the model that V3/rural cases are low-risk.
* **Consequence**: The retrained model will severely under-predict breach risks in production, causing field operations to miss SLA interventions.
* **Mitigation**: Implement a strict data-governance rule: **No cohort may enter the training dataset until label maturity reaches at least 90%** (or apply right-censored survival models like Cox/Random Survival Forests with Inverse Probability of Censoring Weighting).

### Failure Mode 2: Recalibrating around an uncorrected pipeline bug
* **Mechanism**: If engineers attempt to "fix" April's performance drop (AUROC 0.67, ECE 0.110) by applying Isotonic Regression calibration to prediction outputs without fixing the schema renaming bug (`Diagnostic Assessment Completed`), the recalibration layer will learn to adjust scores derived from corrupted event prefixes.
* **Consequence**: When the schema bug is eventually patched, the calibration layer will distort accurate predictions, causing secondary model failure.
* **Mitigation**: Establish a prerequisite audit pipeline: **Data quality and lineage checks (schema compliance, feature missingness $<2\%$) must pass before recalibration algorithms are executed.**

### Failure Mode 3: Treating supplier-driven concept drift as standard feature noise
* **Mechanism**: Retraining the model on August–September data using only existing features (without adding supplier lead-time features) assumes that historical features contain enough signal to capture the shift. However, `Initial Diagnosis Completed` events carry no internal signal regarding external supplier warehouse delays.
* **Consequence**: Retraining fails to improve performance; the model remains blind to parts delays because the root cause exists in an unobserved external variable.
* **Mitigation**: Explicitly augment the feature store with dynamic operational context: **Ingest real-time supplier part backorder queues and average part-fulfillment latency as explicit features** into the inference payload.

### Failure Mode 4: Misattributing Partner Z population shift to model degradation or unfair bias
* **Mechanism**: Observing high breach rates (0.45) and poor AUROC (0.66) for Partner Z in September, management might conclude that the model is inherently biased or broken for rural operations, triggering unnecessary regional model forks or algorithm overhauls.
* **Consequence**: Wasted engineering effort building custom models when the true driver is missing domain features (`rural_travel_distance`, `device_age`) combined with high V3 variant concentration (58%).
* **Mitigation**: Perform **Stratified Subgroup Drift Attribution**: evaluate model performance on Partner Z cases after controlling for variant mix and adding missing geographic/demographic features before deciding to split model pipelines.
