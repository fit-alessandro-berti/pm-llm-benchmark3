## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts relied on.**  
I treat the following as case facts: amounts, supplier status, stated purposes, recorded event times, the ECD-2 effective window, the ERP/document outage evidence note, and the explicit source texts A1–A7, B1–B7, C1–C6, and D1–D4.

**Interpretations.**  
I do not treat GPIP-4, ECD-2, the CFO email, or the Aachen SOP as automatically superior in every dimension. Instead, I use an issue-specific hierarchy:

1. **Core integrity controls**—segregation of duties, pre-commitment Compliance_Clearance for new/high-value suppliers, and formal exception authority—are presumptively controlled by GPIP-4 and can be displaced only by a clear, competent, written exception.
2. **Operational emergency workflow**—PO sequencing, approved-supplier-register use, and local plant/finance operational approvals—can be modified by ECD-2 and the CFO’s finance interpretation, but only within ECD-2’s time window and narrow contamination-containment scope.
3. **Local SOP controls** are valid and may be stricter than global policy, but their emergency relaxations are narrow and do not silently waive finance, compliance, or segregation controls.
4. **The CFO email** is treated as temporary interpretive guidance for finance/operational approvals during ECD-2, not as a controlled policy amendment and not as authority to waive Compliance_Clearance or segregation of duties.
5. **A later approval or later record** may explain, mitigate, or evidence a timely act, but does not automatically cure a missing pre-commitment control.

**Assumptions.**  
These are assumptions, not established facts:

- An “unapproved supplier” is treated as a new supplier for A3 unless evidence shows it was already an existing supplier merely missing from the register.
- When Incident Command authorization is used as the approval mechanism, it is treated as a final business approval for segregation-of-duties analysis.
- “Critical sterilization-line lots” is construed narrowly: items needed to contain the current contamination event, generally within 24 hours, or backup items only where a documented risk assessment connects them to immediate containment.
- The CFO’s email can satisfy local finance/plant operational approval evidence up to EUR 150,000 only where the Incident Command authorizers are independent and there is no segregation-of-duties breach.
- Signed PDFs, telephone recordings, and supplier acknowledgments may establish the real time of an act despite late ERP entry, provided their metadata or corroboration is credible.
- ECD-2 was not a Chief Risk Officer A7 exception because the CRO was unavailable and did not ratify it.

**Treatment of emergency necessity.**  
Emergency necessity is relevant to scope, proportionality, and severity, but it is not a free-standing waiver. Where a control is not expressly waived by a competent authority, the deviation remains a breach or an unproven exception, even if operationally understandable.

**Burden of proof.**  
The party relying on an exception bears the burden of showing:  
- the exception was validly issued;  
- it applied to the purchase scope;  
- it applied at the time of commitment; and  
- it did not remove a core integrity control without clear authority.  

Where a required pre-commitment control is absent and no valid exception is proven, I classify the case as NONCOMPLIANT. Where the decisive fact cannot be established from available evidence, I identify the missing evidence and explain whether it would reverse the conclusion.

**Decision method.**  
For each case I:  
1. identify the time of Commit_to_Supplier;  
2. determine which sources were active at that time;  
3. determine whether the purchase fell within ECD-2 scope;  
4. check pre-commitment controls;  
5. check segregation of duties;  
6. evaluate any claimed exception;  
7. weigh contemporaneous evidence over late ERP entries; and  
8. classify the case under the stated interpretation.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

**Distinguishing issue types**

1. **Policy breach**  
   A required control was missing at the relevant time and no valid exception is proven.  
   - P-501: missing pre-commitment Plant/Finance and invalid Shift Lead substitution.  
   - P-502: missing pre-commitment Compliance_Clearance for new supplier.  
   - P-503: self-authorization, missing Compliance_Clearance, missing finance/budget approval, doubtful scope.  
   - P-504: outside ECD-2 scope, unapproved supplier, no IC authorization.  
   - P-506: expired ECD-2, no written extension, missing Plant/budget approval before commitment.  
   - P-508: missing pre-commitment Finance/budget approval above EUR 150,000 under the strict interpretation.

2. **Logging defect**  
   The underlying act may have been timely, but the system record is late, incomplete, or unreliable.  
   - P-502: ERP entries during recovery may be delayed.  
   - P-505: ERP timestamp appears after expiry but may reflect clock drift.  
   - P-507: authorization appears late in ERP, but signed PDF was created within four hours.  
   - P-505: Create_PO record is not evidenced.  
   - Several ECD cases: local emergency form is not consistently evidenced.

3. **Late evidence of a timely act**  
   The evidence shows the act occurred timely, even if entered late.  
   - P-502: supplier recording/acknowledgment corroborates 10:15 commitment.  
   - P-505: supplier acknowledgment at 17:59:31 supports commitment before ECD-2 expiry.  
   - P-507: signed PDF at 12:40 supports recording within four hours of 08:55 authorization.

4. **Inability to prove compliance**  
   A required fact cannot be established from available evidence.  
   - P-501: no proof of Plant Manager delegation or pre-commitment approval.  
   - P-503: no proof of independent authorization or scope urgency.  
   - P-505/P-507: no proof of IC authorizer roles/independence or requester/committer identities.  
   - P-506: no written extension.  
   - P-508: no proof that > EUR 150,000 finance control was validly replaced.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Replacement Emergency Procurement Exception Clause**

1. **Authority and hierarchy**  
   GPIP-4 remains the controlling global policy. An Incident Command directive may modify only local operational workflow requirements, including PO sequencing and approved-supplier-register use, when the directive is written, scoped, time-limited, and signed by the COO or Incident Commander. Any modification of GPIP-4 segregation of duties, Compliance_Clearance, or finance threshold controls requires a written Chief Risk Officer exception or, if the CRO is unavailable, a temporary exception signed jointly by the CFO, COO, and Chief Compliance Officer, subject to CRO ratification within 24 hours.

2. **Scope of emergency purchases**  
   Emergency procurement scope is limited to goods or services required to contain an active incident within 24 hours, or to prevent imminent safety harm, regulatory breach, or production stoppage. Backup or contingency items are covered only if supported by a documented risk assessment signed by Plant Management, Quality/Compliance, and Incident Command.

3. **Threshold controls**  
   - Below EUR 25,000: Incident Command authorization and emergency form within 24 hours are sufficient if scope is met.  
   - EUR 25,000 to EUR 49,999: two distinct Incident Command authorizations plus Plant or Finance approval before commitment.  
   - EUR 50,000 to EUR 150,000: two distinct Incident Command authorizations, plus evidence of independent budget-owner and Finance Controller approval. Joint Incident Command authorization may substitute only if at least one authorizer has documented finance/budget delegation and no segregation conflict exists.  
   - Above EUR 150,000: Legal_Clearance, Finance Controller approval, and independent budget-owner approval are required before commitment unless the Chief Risk Officer issues a written exception.  
   - Above EUR 100,000 or any new/unapproved supplier: Compliance_Clearance is required before commitment and cannot be waived by Incident Command or CFO email.

4. **Segregation of duties**  
   A requester or person who commits to a supplier may not be an Incident Command authorizer, final business approver, Finance approver, or budget-owner approver for the same purchase. Two required authorizers must be distinct and independent from the requester/committer.

5. **New suppliers**  
   Use of a supplier outside the approved register is allowed only if:  
   - Compliance_Clearance is obtained before commitment, or a written CRO exception expressly permits deferred clearance;  
   - Incident Command documents the operational necessity; and  
   - Quality/Compliance performs post-receipt verification before use where clearance was expedited.

6. **Retrospective records**  
   Verbal emergency authorizations must be recorded in a controlled system within four hours. If systems are unavailable, signed PDFs, telephone recordings, access logs, or supplier acknowledgments with reliable metadata are acceptable temporary evidence. ERP timestamps are rebuttable where outage or clock-drift evidence exists. Emergency forms must be completed within 24 hours of commitment.

7. **Expiry and extension**  
   Every emergency directive must state an explicit expiry date and time. Extensions must be written and signed by the authorized exception issuer. Verbal extensions are invalid. Upon expiry, normal GPIP-4 and local SOP controls automatically resume.

8. **Safeguard against emergency normalization**  
   Emergency procurement authority is limited to 72 hours per incident unless renewed by the Chief Risk Officer. Total emergency spend under an incident directive must be reported daily to Internal Audit. Any incident using emergency procurement for more than 72 hours, or exceeding a predefined spend cap, triggers mandatory post-event review and corrective-action tracking before further emergency use.
