## 4. What flattening misses

### Jobflattening
- **Visible**: active steps (Release  Stage  Machine Process  Inspect  Pack  Dispatch) and their median durations.
- **Hidden**: waiting periods before batches, tool contention, lot quarantine, shipment consolidation waits. “Inspect  Pack” has median 1.0h but does not show that J1’s PacktoDispatch lag is 5h55 because the shipment edge is instantaneous.
- **Misleading DFG conclusion**: the DFG suggests a smooth 6step pipeline with no more than 2% rework; all jobs appear similar. It fails to expose that J1 and J2 spent 3+ hours blocking on a batch, and J1 then waited almost 6h for a shipment partner.

### Machineflattening
- **Visible**: utilisation (54%, 44%, 58%) and longest recorded gap between machine events.
- **Hidden**: whether an idle gap reflects lack of work or a job waiting for a **tool** (CNC2 idle while J4 waited for FIXA). It also hides rack cleaning because that is not a machine event.
- **Misleading conclusion**: CNC2 “can absorb more jobs,” but adding jobs would likely increase tool contention without helping flow.

### Batchflattening
- **Visible**: batch start, cure, unload.
- **Hidden**: prestart waiting caused by job readiness asymmetry, tool cleaning, and lot release; postbatch inspection/pack durations.
- **Misleading conclusion**: a batch DFG would show only straightforward cure cycle, missing the 4h staging delay.

### Toolflattening
- **Visible**: acquire/release timestamps, if extracted.
- **Hidden**: effect on machine idle time, multiresource blocking, and cleaning transitions.
- **Misleading**: a tool DFG might show normal holding times without revealing that a held tool causes idle machines and cascading shipment delays.

### ComponentLotflattening
- **Visible**: quarantine and release events.
- **Hidden**: impact on job progress because lots are not linked to jobs in the standard pipeline; J2’s override escapes visibility.
- **Misleading**: lot handling would appear independent of production flow, masking a potential quality risk.

### Shipmentflattening
- **Visible**: create  load  dispatch.
- **Hidden**: how long a packed job waits for its shipment partners; the consolidation logic.
- **Misleading**: the DFG suggests all shipments dispatch within 35min of loading, completely missing J1’s 355min postpack hold.

**Why low machine utilisation coexists with long job waiting:** progress is gated by nonmachine resources (tools, batch completeness, lot release, shipment consolidation). A job can be “in process” but blocked – the machine remains idle because the job cannot start its machine step without the required tool, or the job has finished but cannot ship. Traditional flattening only measures active machine events, so waiting is shoved into interevent gaps that are averaged away.

## 5. Objectlifecycle evidence

### Narrative 1: J1 – from fast producer to shipment hostage
- **Temporal precedence**: J1 completes staging (08:00), waits for B1 (12:05), cures, inspects, packs by 13:30. S1 dispatches only after J4 is packed at 19:10.
- **Resource feasibility**: J1’s part C1 is from L8 (no reported lot delay). OVEN1 is available but requires a full batch. Shipment S1 requires both J1 and J4.
- **Policy constraints**: Batch policy enforces grouping of J1,J2,J3; shipment policy consolidates orders S1(J1,J4).
- **Causal speculation**: J1’s total lead time is dominated first by waiting for the slowest batch member (J3) and then by waiting for J4, whose own delays (FIXA, RACK7) propagate horizontally.

### Narrative 2: J4, FIXA, and RACK7 – toolamplified delay
- **Temporal precedence**: J4 reaches CNC2 at 09:50; CNC2 becomes idle at 10:05 but J4 cannot start because FIXA is held by J3 until 11:40. After processing, J4 enters B2 but waits for RACK7 cleaning (until 14:35). It packs at 19:10.
- **Resource feasibility**: CNC2 is free; FIXA is the single constraint. OVEN1 is free but B2 cannot start due to RACK7.
- **Policy constraints**: No explicit “wait for tool” activity; the system relies on implicit queuing.
- **Causal speculation**: The FIXA bottleneck alone added ~1.7h to J4; without it J4 might have finished early enough to reduce J1’s shipment wait, illustrating how local tool contention amplifies globally.

### Narrative 3: J5, J6, and CAL3 – lot and calibration coupling
- **Temporal precedence**: J5 needs C5 from L7; L7 quarantined 10:2013:50. J5 becomes ready for batch only at 14:40 (after lot release). It cures in B2, then final test at 16:1016:35. CAL3 is recalibrated 16:3517:20, delaying J6 (ready 16:05) until 17:25.
- **Resource feasibility**: Lot release governed by quality; TEST1 is idle during recalibration; CAL3 is the limiting factor.
- **Policy constraints**: Lot quarantine overrides normal flow; supervisor override for J2 (not J5) creates inconsistency. CAL3 recalibration policy is mandatory.
- **Causal speculation**: The lot quarantine may have been unnecessary delay for J5 if the issue was confined to C2; the override on J2 suggests the rule was selectively applied. CAL3 policy is technical but could be relaxed with parallel calibrators.

In all narratives, temporal precedence is established by timestamps; resource feasibility by the evidence of idle machines; policy constraints by the existence of batches and shipments; causal links remain speculative because unobserved counterfactuals are missing.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

### 1. Reduce batch synchronisation (oven)
- **Target mechanism**: batch formation policy.
- **Expected benefit**: eliminate up to 3h55min of waiting for J1/J2; reduce average flow time.
- **Jobs helped**: J1, J2 (and similar earlyready jobs in future).
- **Jobs potentially harmed**: if smaller batches increase number of oven cycles, all jobs may face slightly longer wait for machine availability – but utilisation is only 44%, so risk is low.
- **New cost/risk**: more frequent oven heating, higher energy cost; possible tooling constraints.
- **Perverse incentive metric**: maximising **oven utilisation** would favour large batches, working against this intervention.
- **Objectcentric evaluation**: compare “readyforbatch” to “batchstart” intervals across batches using OCEL features; track joblevel cycle times with `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg` on a flattened log that includes a waiting state.

### 2. Add or schedule FIXA fixtures
- **Target mechanism**: sharedtool contention.
- **Expected benefit**: J4 could have started up to 1h40min earlier, reducing cascade to S1.
- **Jobs helped**: J4 directly; J1 indirectly via shipment.
- **Jobs potentially harmed**: none, unless fixture acquisition causes cost passed through.
- **New cost/risk**: capital for second fixture; scheduling complexity if more than two contend.
- **Perverse incentive**: focusing on CNC2 utilisation might ignore fixture bottleneck and even add more work.
- **Evaluation**: extract toolacquire/release events into an OCEL, then compute toolwaiting time using `pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary` for JobTool pairs before and after intervention.

### 3. Decouple shipment consolidation
- **Target mechanism**: shipment policy (consolidate J1+J4).
- **Expected benefit**: J1 would ship at 13:30, saving 5h55min of postpack wait.
- **Jobs helped**: any early finisher in a consolidated shipment.
- **Jobs potentially harmed**: later jobs may lose transportation efficiency; shipment cost per item could rise.
- **New cost/risk**: higher freight cost if partial loads; risk of missed consolidation savings.
- **Perverse incentive**: “ontime delivery” metric measured from requested date may encourage delaying dispatch until full, hiding the internal wait.
- **Evaluation**: model shipmentjob relationships in OCEL; calculate interval `pack  dispatch` per job, compare against business rules, simulate partial shipments.

### 4. Optimise CAL3 recalibration or add parallel calibrator
- **Target mechanism**: calibrationtool contention.
- **Expected benefit**: J6 would start up to 50min earlier.
- **Jobs helped**: J6 and subsequent test jobs.
- **Jobs potentially harmed**: if recalibration frequency is reduced, test quality may suffer.
- **New cost/risk**: additional calibrator cost; quality risk if calibration is skipped.
- **Perverse incentive**: “TEST1 utilisation” might drop if calibration time is removed, falsely suggesting underuse.
- **Evaluation**: extract test+calibration events in OCEL; measure waitingfortool time using `pm4py.extract_ocel_features` with objectlifetimes.

### 5. Improve logging of lot events and tool states
- **Target mechanism**: dataintegration artifacts.
- **Expected benefit**: full visibility of quarantine/supervisor overrides, explicit “waiting for tool” states; better rootcause analysis.
- **Jobs helped**: all, through improved management decisions.
- **Jobs potentially harmed**: none directly; may reveal delays that were previously hidden.
- **New cost/risk**: instrumentation effort, storage.
- **Perverse incentive**: managers might game the new metrics (e.g., shorten quarantine at expense of quality).
- **Evaluation**: compare objectcentric process models with and without the additional event types; use `pm4py.discover_ocdfg` to verify that lot–job edges appear.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

### Observations that would weaken the topranked explanations
1. If J1’s “readyforbatch” timestamp actually represents a preliminary material ready, not the true full readiness, then batch waiting is overstated.
2. If FIXA was deliberately booked for maintenance between J3 and J4, the 1h40min would be planned and not contention.
3. If S1’s late dispatch was forced by a carrier schedule (e.g., 19:30 daily truck), shipconsolidation would be a policy constraint, not a bottleneck removable by rescheduling.
4. If J6’s readyfortest time actually included an unrecorded hold for tooling or material, the CAL3 delay would be smaller.
5. If RACK7 cleaning had a fixed 2hour regulatory minimum, the B2 start could not have been advanced.
6. If supervisor override for J2 had been recorded as a legitimate exception and did not cause quality issues, the componentlot coupling would be inconsequential.

### Additional fields or event types to discriminate hypotheses
1. **`Wait for Tool` event type** – records when a job is blocked by tool unavailability, with tool ID and timestamp.
2. **`Batch Ready` event** – explicit event when the batch formation rule is satisfied, distinguishing readiness from start.
3. **ComponentLot–Job link** – a separate relationship table linking lot events directly to jobs (not just via component).
4. **`Supervisor Override` logged event** – with reason code and authorisation timestamp.
5. **Tool cleaning/maintenance log** – to distinguish planned from unplanned idle.
6. **Shipment consolidation policy parameter** – attribute on shipment object (e.g., `max_wait_allowed`) to test counterfactual dispatch rules.
7. **Detailed machinestate events** – “idleblockedbytool”, “idlenojob” to separate causes of idle intervals.
8. **Component readiness timestamps** – separate from job staging, to decouple lotrelease from job readiness.

These would allow direct measurement of waiting causes instead of relying on timing gaps and inference.
