## 1. Materiality Framework

Alignment cost answers a narrow question: "how many edits does it take to make this trace fit M0 under a uniform cost function?" Business severity answers a different one: "what control objective failed, how much money and customer harm is exposed, and can it still be undone?" The two are only loosely correlated, and in this dataset they are sometimes anti-correlated (CL-610 has cost 0 and is one of the worst cases; CL-612 has cost 4 and is nearly harmless).

**Severity dimensions (what is at stake).**

1. **Control objective at risk.** Which of the four core objectives failed: (a) segregation/authorization (approval before payment), (b) anti-fraud (screening of high-risk or bank-changed claims), (c) financial accuracy (right amount, right payee), (d) customer/regulatory duty (24h notification). A deviation that touches (a) or (b) on a payable claim is structurally more severe than one touching (d).
2. **Amount / exposure.** Use the actual euro amount at risk, not the number of moves. EUR 480,000 unscreened (CL-602) and EUR 4,500 paid to a freshly changed bank account (CL-610) are not comparable to EUR 1,200 of telemetry noise (CL-601). Exposure should also include *loss-given-event* — a bank-change fraud typically recovers a small fraction of funds.
3. **Reversibility.** Payment is the point of no return. Anything that removed a control *before* an irreversible cash movement (CL-602, CL-604, CL-610) ranks above anything discovered before payment or affecting only downstream records (CL-608, CL-611).
4. **Timing.** Ordering violations matter more than presence/absence violations when the order *is* the control. CL-604's 17-minute payment-before-approval gap is the whole control, not a logging quirk. Conversely CL-603's duplicate is a sub-second artifact.
5. **Customer and regulatory harm.** CL-608 (notification possibly only in a mutable CRM field) is mainly a *evidence/regulatory* harm — the claimant may well have been notified; the auditor cannot prove it. CL-603's duplicate notification is a minor customer-experience irritant.
6. **Recurrence and systematicity.** A one-off human slip is a coaching issue; a pattern of 74 or 9 or 4 cases is a system defect with an aggregate exposure. Frequency multiplies severity but also *lowers* per-case blame.
7. **Evidence reliability.** How much do we trust the diagnostic itself? Mutable text-field links (CL-608), documents without stable case identifiers (2 of the 9 adjuster cases), verbal-only assertions (CL-602) all degrade confidence.
8. **Model scope.** If the case is out of M0's intended scope (CL-607 denied lead, CL-612 withdrawal), the alignment cost measures *model incompleteness*, not misbehaviour. Severity for the case is near zero; severity for the *measurement system* is real, because these cases pollute every conformance KPI.

**Severity scale used below (1–5).**
- **5 — Critical:** irreversible payment with a mandated anti-fraud/authorization control absent or bypassed; material amount. Escalate to fraud/financial-crime and audit today.
- **4 — High:** mandated control demonstrably weakened on a payable claim, or ordering of authorization vs. payment broken; recovery may be possible.
- **3 — Medium:** substantive activity plausibly performed but evidence is outside the system of record, or a systemic measurement defect that hides real risk.
- **2 — Low:** regulatory/record-keeping or customer-experience defect with no financial exposure; label/mapping defects.
- **1 — Negligible:** noise, silent moves, scope artifacts. Fix the tooling, not the process.

**Confidence is orthogonal to severity.** Severity = "how bad if true." Confidence = "how sure am I that my reading of the evidence is correct." I state them separately and never let a low-confidence reading silently downgrade a high-severity finding — a high-severity/low-confidence item becomes an *investigation* action, not a *no action* item. Concretely: CL-602 is severity 5 with high confidence that the screening evidence is missing but only medium confidence that the screening never happened — so the action is a forensic review plus a hold, not an immediate accusation. CL-610 is severity 5 with *very high* confidence, because the policy breach is computable from the data attributes alone and needs no inference at all. And a critical epistemic rule throughout: **a missing event is missing evidence, not proof of a missing activity.** `M(A)` means "the model demanded A and nothing in this log matched it," which is consistent with (i) A never happened, (ii) A happened in another system, (iii) A happened under a different label, (iv) A happened but correlation to the case failed.

## 3. Repeated Deviation Patterns

**P-A. Retry duplicates on notification (harmless technical noise).**
*Cases:* CL-603 plus 74 cases in the month.
*Frequency evidence:* 74 cases with `L(Notify Claimant)` immediately following a synchronous notification carrying the **same message ID**, all attributable to a single retrying connector.
*Business risk:* Very low. At worst duplicate customer messages; no financial or control impact. The real cost is analytic: 74 spurious deviations dominate the deviation count and desensitize reviewers.
*Likely cause:* Logging/integration defect — the connector emits an event per delivery *attempt* rather than per logical message; the event sink has no idempotency key.
*Confidence:* Very high (shared message ID + transport retry logs + single-connector attribution).
*Recommended owner:* Integration/platform engineering, with the event-log data steward.

**P-B. External-adjuster assessment gap (missing evidence, not missing activity).**
*Cases:* CL-606 plus 9 high-value cases in the month.
*Frequency evidence:* 9 cases with `M(Assess Loss)` accompanied by an external-adjuster document; **2 lack a stable document-to-case identifier**.
*Business risk:* Medium for the 7 well-identified cases (assurance/record-keeping on high-value claims); higher for the 2 unidentifiable ones, where we genuinely cannot confirm the assessment happened for *that* claim — an unassessed high-value payment would be a real control failure.
*Likely cause:* Organizational/architectural — assessment outsourced to licensed adjusters working in a separate DMS with no event integration back into the core log.
*Confidence:* High that a systematic integration gap exists; medium-high per case that assessment occurred; low for the 2 unlinked cases.
*Recommended owner:* Claims operations (adjuster process) jointly with integration engineering.

**P-C. Activity-label / mapping drift (event-label mismatch).**
*Cases:* CL-611 (and, by construction, every case in the pre-update window with the same source system).
*Frequency evidence:* The mapping table was updated *after* this case, so the population is bounded by the pre-update period rather than by a single case — the affected count must be measured, not assumed.
*Business risk:* Low operationally, but it systematically fabricates `L`+`M` pairs on high-value cases, inflating cost 2 per case and hiding real signal.
*Likely cause:* Uncontrolled, manually maintained mapping between legacy system labels and procedure vocabulary; no effective-dating.
*Confidence:* High.
*Recommended owner:* Data governance / event-log data steward.

**P-D. Out-of-scope terminal outcomes: denials and withdrawals (process-model mismatch).**
*Cases:* CL-607, CL-612.
*Frequency evidence:* Denied and withdrawn cases account for **most alignments with raw cost ≥ 4** in the month.
*Business risk:* Near zero operationally; high *measurement* risk. Because these dominate the high-cost tail, any triage that starts from the top of a cost-sorted list burns its entire budget on non-issues and never reaches CL-602 (cost 1) or CL-610 (cost 0).
*Likely cause:* Model scope defect plus an unresolved governance dispute (P6): M0 covers payable claims only, while extraction includes all intake outcomes.
*Confidence:* High on the mechanism; the scope question itself is a decision to be made, not a fact to be discovered.
*Recommended owner:* Process governance board (to decide scope), then the model owner (to implement).

**P-E. Data-aware auto-approval failures (zero-cost policy breaches).**
*Cases:* CL-609, CL-610.
*Frequency evidence:* **Four cases** in the month with a fully synchronous Auto Approve path despite violating P1 or P2 — all invisible to control-flow conformance.
*Business risk:* **Highest in the portfolio.** Payments made without mandated screening/authorization, including at least one bank-change diversion signature. Systematic, exploitable, and by construction undetectable by the current dashboard.
*Likely cause:* The rule engine's eligibility guard omits (or mis-thresholds) amount, risk level, and especially the 7-day beneficiary-bank-change lookback; the process tree encodes the branch as a free choice with no guard.
*Confidence:* Very high — breaches are computable deterministically from case attributes.
*Recommended owner:* Claims risk/controls owner with the rule-engine product owner; fraud team for the individual cases.

**P-F. Payment-before-authorization (ordering control failure).**
*Cases:* CL-604 (and CL-602 in substance, where the authorization present was not the mandated screened one).
*Frequency evidence:* One case in this sample; the `L(Pay Claim)` … `M(Pay Claim)` bracket is a machine-detectable signature, so month-wide frequency should be measured immediately.
*Business risk:* Critical — the authorization control is the ordering.
*Likely cause:* A payment channel that can be initiated outside the workflow gate, with workflow approval recorded afterwards to close the task.
*Confidence:* High for CL-604 (independent cross-system timestamps).
*Recommended owner:* Finance/treasury controls with claims IT.

**P-G. UI telemetry contamination (harmless noise).**
*Cases:* CL-601.
*Business risk:* None operationally; pure log-hygiene defect that inflates cost on the cleanest cases.
*Likely cause:* Extraction filter includes UI/telemetry event classes.
*Confidence:* Very high. *Owner:* Event-log data steward.

**P-H. Representation artifacts (silent moves).**
*Cases:* CL-605. Not a deviation; must be excluded from deviation reporting by construction. *Owner:* Process-mining tooling team.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

Throughout: **absence of an event is not proof of absence of the activity.** Each hypothesis set below is designed so that the discriminating evidence lives *outside* the alignment.

**1. CL-602 — missing Fraud Screen on EUR 480,000 with a one-day-old bank change.**
- H1: Screening genuinely never occurred; the approver overrode the control.
- H2: Screening occurred in a separate fraud/case-management tool that does not emit events into the core log.
- H3: Screening occurred but was recorded against a different case or a parent/related case (correlation failure).
- H4: Deliberate circumvention connected to the bank change (internal or social-engineering fraud).
*Discriminating evidence:* query the fraud tool directly for any screening record referencing this claim, claimant, IBAN or the changed account in the relevant window; pull the approver's UI session/audit trail to see whether a screening screen was opened; check whether the fraud tool has *ever* emitted events for other cases (if it has, H2 weakens sharply); examine the bank-change request — who submitted it, through which channel, from which IP/device, and whether the claimant confirmed it out-of-band; check whether the same approver has other unscreened high-value approvals. H4 is supported if the bank change originated outside the claimant's known channels. Note that H2 and H1 have opposite remediations (integration vs. control enforcement), so this test must run before any conclusion.

**2. CL-604 — payment timestamp precedes approval timestamp by 17 minutes.**
- H1: The payment was genuinely initiated before approval (true ordering breach).
- H2: The approval happened first in reality but was *recorded* late — the workflow "Manual Approve" event captures form submission, not decision time.
- H3: Clock skew or timezone mismatch between the banking system and the workflow system.
- H4: The 10:03 bank event is an acknowledgment of a file submitted earlier under a legitimately approved batch, so the causal order is intact but the events are mislabelled.
*Discriminating evidence:* compare the two systems' clock sources and measure skew on a control population (17 minutes is far too large for ordinary NTP drift, so H3 is weak a priori but cheap to exclude); read the payment record's own audit trail for the initiating user, channel and instruction time; check whether the approval record carries a separate "decision time" attribute distinct from "recorded time"; inspect the bank file's composition timestamp and whether an approval token accompanied it. If the payment instruction carries no approval reference at all, H1 is confirmed.

**3. CL-606 / P-B — `M(Assess Loss)` with an external adjuster report.**
- H1: Assessment occurred externally and simply is not logged (integration gap).
- H2: The report exists but pertains to a different claim (correlation failure) — most plausible for the 2 cases lacking a stable identifier.
- H3: The report was produced *after* the fact to paper over a skipped assessment.
- H4: The activity was performed in-system under a different label (overlaps with P-C).
*Discriminating evidence:* verify the adjuster's report header against the claim number, policy number, loss date and loss location; check the DMS's immutable creation timestamp and signature time (10:05) against the fraud-screen and approval timestamps — H3 requires the report's *creation* to postdate the downstream steps, which the given ordering contradicts; confirm the adjuster's licence and engagement record for this claim; for H4, search the raw log for any unmapped label in the assessment time window. For the 2 unlinked cases, escalate: absent a verifiable link, treat as *unverified assessment on a high-value claim*, not as "assessed."

**4. CL-608 — missing notification with a mutable CRM link.**
- H1: The claimant was notified via the secure portal at +3 hours; only the evidence linkage is weak.
- H2: The CRM text field was edited after the fact and no notification was sent.
- H3: A message was generated but never delivered (bounce/failed delivery).
*Discriminating evidence:* the portal's own delivery and read receipts, which are independent of the CRM field; the CRM record's field-level change history (does the link's last-modified timestamp precede or follow the audit request?); the outbound message store keyed by claimant ID; whether the claimant subsequently acted on the notification (e.g. logged in, replied, acknowledged payment). H1 is strongly supported if a portal-side record exists at +3h independent of the CRM field.

**5. CL-610 / P-E — zero-cost auto-approval after a one-hour-old bank change.**
- H1: The rule engine never evaluates the bank-change lookback (design gap).
- H2: It evaluates it, but the bank-change data arrives asynchronously and was not yet visible at decision time (data-latency race).
- H3: A manual override/exemption flag was applied.
- H4: The bank change is legitimate (claimant genuinely switched banks) and the failure is "only" a control breach, not a loss.
*Discriminating evidence:* read the rule engine's decision log for this case — which predicates were evaluated and with what input values (this single artifact separates H1 from H2 and H3 decisively); compare the bank-change record's write timestamp to the auto-approval decision timestamp; test the rule in a sandbox with a synthetic recent bank change; for H4, contact the claimant out-of-band on a previously verified channel and check whether the new IBAN appears on other claims or is known to fraud consortium data. Note H4 changes the *loss* estimate but not the severity of the control failure. The four-case month-level pattern favours H1 over a one-off race.

**6. CL-609 — MEDIUM-risk EUR 18,000 auto-approved with zero cost.**
- H1: Threshold mis-configured (e.g. 50,000 used where 5,000 was intended, or the risk-level predicate omitted).
- H2: The published policy P1 and the implemented rule diverged after an undocumented change.
- H3: The risk score was LOW at decision time and was re-scored to MEDIUM afterwards (temporal attribute drift).
- H4: A documented exemption applies (e.g. a trusted-claimant scheme) not captured in the overlay.
*Discriminating evidence:* the rule-engine configuration and its change history versus the signed policy; the *as-of* risk score stored with the decision rather than the current score — H3 is a genuinely common and exculpatory explanation and must be tested before blaming the rule; the exemption register for H4. If the rule config shows no risk predicate at all, H1 is confirmed and the population of affected cases follows immediately from a re-run.

**7. CL-607 / CL-612 / P-D — high-cost denied and withdrawn cases.**
- H1: Model scope defect — these outcomes are legitimate and simply unmodelled.
- H2: Extraction scope defect — these cases should never have entered the payable-claim conformance population.
- H3: Genuine premature termination (e.g. a claim wrongly denied, or a withdrawal induced by poor service).
*Discriminating evidence:* the denial reason code and whether it matches the coverage pre-screen outcome; complaint or re-open records for the same claimant (a denied claim later re-opened and paid would suggest H3); for CL-612, whether withdrawal correlates with assessment outcome or with handling delays. H1 and H2 are both "true" in different senses and the fix differs: H1 means extend the model with denial/withdrawal branches; H2 means partition the log. The pattern that these dominate the cost ≥ 4 tail is itself evidence that the measurement, not the process, is at fault.

**8. CL-603 / P-A — 74 duplicate notifications.**
- H1: Connector logs one event per delivery attempt (logging defect, one message sent).
- H2: The connector genuinely re-sends the message (customer receives duplicates).
*Discriminating evidence:* the customer-facing message store or the claimant's mailbox/portal inbox — does one physical message exist or two? This distinguishes a pure logging artifact from a (still minor) customer-experience defect and determines whether the fix belongs at the sink or in the connector's retry logic.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Alignment cost is a well-defined quantity: the minimum total cost of edits transforming an observed trace into a model-permitted one, under a chosen cost function. Every clause in that sentence limits what the number can mean. Here it is a uniform cost of 1 per visible move — an explicitly stated configuration choice with no business weighting — so the metric literally counts edits. Business risk is a function of money, irreversibility, control objectives, customer harm and evidence quality, none of which appear in the objective. The supplied cases demonstrate the gap along eight distinct axes.

**Cost-function design.** With uniform weights, every move is worth the same edit: a stray `Autosave` (CL-601), a duplicated message (CL-603), a missing EUR 480,000 fraud screen (CL-602). CL-601 accumulates cost 3 and is risk-free; CL-602 scores 1 and is the largest exposure in the table. The ordering isn't merely uninformative, it is inverted. Worse, the cost function shapes the *diagnosis*, not just the score: CL-604's out-of-order payment is repaired as delete-then-reinsert (cost 3) because that is cheaper than any alternative under uniform weights, and CL-611's rename is charged twice (cost 2) because substitution isn't a primitive move. Both numbers are properties of the encoding. One could reweight — screening moves at 100, telemetry at 0 — and get a better-behaved number, but that only relocates the judgment into the weights and gives it false precision; it still cannot see CL-609 or CL-610, whose cost is zero regardless of weighting.

**Scope.** Cost measures distance to *this* model, so if the model is scoped wrongly the cost measures the scoping error. CL-607 (correctly denied lead) and CL-612 (voluntary withdrawal) score 4 apiece — the joint maximum — precisely because M0 was built for payable claims and demands `Assess Loss`, `Auto Approve`, `Pay Claim` and `Notify Claimant` that reality never needed to produce. The month-level evidence makes the consequence concrete: denied and withdrawn cases account for most alignments with cost ≥ 4. A reviewer working a cost-sorted queue top-down spends the entire budget on correct outcomes. Note also the perverse gradient: CL-612 would have scored *higher* had the claimant withdrawn earlier, since more unrealized steps would remain — cost rises as the company's exposure falls.

**Hidden moves.** CL-605's `T(tau, c=0)` is free and fitting: an invisible routing transition introduced by converting the process tree's XOR into a Petri net. It is a representation artifact with no business meaning, and reporting it as a deviation would be a category error. But the correct handling of tau also exposes the trap: zero cost sometimes means "conformant and fine" (CL-605) and sometimes means "conformant and catastrophic" (CL-610). The number cannot tell them apart; only context can.

**Data conditions.** This is the decisive failure. P1 and P2 are guards over *case attributes* — amount, risk level, beneficiary-bank-change recency — and the process tree encodes the Auto Approve branch as a free choice with no guard at all. CL-609 (EUR 18,000, MEDIUM risk) and CL-610 (bank changed one hour before payment) are therefore **perfectly synchronous, cost 0**, and both breach mandatory policy. CL-610 in particular carries the classic payment-diversion signature and would be certified "fully conformant" by any cost-based dashboard. Four such cases exist in the month. A metric whose blind spot is exactly where fraud is engineered to sit — under the amount thresholds — is not a risk metric. Note too that the failure is not that the model is *wrong*; the tree faithfully describes the permitted orderings. It is that control-flow conformance answers a question about sequence when the control lives in the data.

**Activity granularity and labelling.** CL-611's core system logs `Assess Damage` where the signed procedure says `Assess Loss`. Nothing operational deviated on this EUR 62,000 HIGH-risk claim — every substantive control fired in order — yet the vocabulary mismatch generates a delete/insert pair for cost 2, ranking it above CL-602 and CL-606. Cost here is measuring the maturity of a mapping table. That the table was updated after the case is corroboration, and it also means the metric is non-stationary: identical behaviour scores differently depending on when it was extracted.

**Duplicated telemetry.** CL-603's second `Notify Claimant` shares a message ID with the first and coincides with a documented transport retry: one logical notification, two log records. Multiplied by 74 cases from a single retrying connector, this one integration defect dominates the month's deviation volume. CL-601 adds the same distortion from the other direction, with UI telemetry (`Open Workbench`, `Autosave`, `Close Workbench`) inflating the cleanest case in the table to the joint-highest payable-case cost. In both, cost measures the log pipeline. The second-order harm is the serious one: 74+ spurious findings train reviewers to discount deviations, which is the mechanism by which a cost-1 finding like CL-602 gets closed as noise.

**Missing-but-external evidence.** `M(A)` means the model required A and nothing in *this log* matched — a statement about the log, never about the world. CL-606 has a licensed adjuster's signed report at 10:05 with fraud screening and approval coherently sequenced after it; the assessment near-certainly occurred, in another system. CL-608 has a secure-portal message at +3 hours, which P4 explicitly permits, though the linkage sits in a mutable text field. Both score 1, identical to CL-602 and CL-603, yet the three underlying realities are an evidence gap, a technical duplicate and a probable control failure. The discriminator is always external: the DMS record, the portal receipt, the fraud tool's query log. This is why the *confidence* column must be separate — and why the 2 adjuster cases lacking a stable case identifier are materially worse than the other 7 despite scoring identically.

**Irreversibility.** Alignment treats all repairs as symmetric edits: inserting a missing `Pay Claim` costs the same as inserting a missing `Notify Claimant`. Reality is asymmetric. CL-604's EUR 90,000 left the bank at 10:03 and cannot be un-paid; the approval at 10:20 was a formality over an accomplished fact. CL-602's EUR 480,000 went to a one-day-old bank account. CL-608's notification, by contrast, can be re-sent in minutes at no loss. The alignment repairs CL-604 by *deleting* the early payment and inserting a compliant one — an operation available to the algorithm and to nobody else. That fiction is the essence of why edit distance cannot represent exposure.

**What alignment cost is genuinely good for.** It is an excellent *detector and localizer*, and it earned its place here: the `L(Pay)`…`M(Pay)` bracket is a reliable machine-detectable signature of payment-before-authorization; `L(X)`+`M(Y)` adjacency reliably indicates label drift; `M(A)` reliably marks where evidence must be sought. Used as a triage index it points at the right cases. Used as a *ranking* it inverts the risk order at both ends of this dataset. The remedy is not a cleverer cost function but a two-column discipline: keep technical cost and business severity separate, let cost route attention, and let context, data conditions and evidence quality determine priority.

    )
    if p2_trigger:
        if not r.fraud_screen_logged:
            breaches.append("P2:no-fraud-screen-evidence")
        if not r.manual_approve_logged:
            breaches.append("P2:no-manual-approval")
    # P3: payment before the applicable approval
    if r.pay_before_approval:
        breaches.append("P3:pay-before-approval")
    # P4: notification evidence (multi-channel allowed IF linked evidence retained)
    if r.paid and not r.notify_in_core and not r.notify_external_evidence:
        breaches.append("P4:no-notification-evidence")
    elif r.paid and not r.notify_in_core and r.evidence_quality == "low":
        breaches.append("P4:weak-notification-evidence")
    return "; ".join(breaches) if breaches else "compliant"

    "CL-608": (2, "medium",    "missing evidence (weak linkage)", "P-B/P4 evidence integrity"),
    "CL-609": (4, "very high", "data-aware policy failure",       "P-E auto-approval guard"),
    "CL-610": (5, "very high", "data-aware policy failure",       "P-E auto-approval guard"),
    "CL-611": (2, "high",      "event-label mismatch",            "P-C label mapping"),
    "CL-612": (1, "n/a (governance decision)", "process-model mismatch", "P-D out-of-scope"),

evidence = (
    supplied_df
    .merge(context_df, on="case")
    .merge(computed_df, on="case", how="left")

evidence["policy_overlay_result"] = evidence.apply(evaluate_policy_overlay, axis=1)
evidence["policy_breach"] = evidence["policy_overlay_result"].isin(["compliant"]) == False
evidence["business_severity"] = evidence["case"].map(lambda c: JUDGMENT[c][0])
evidence["confidence"] = evidence["case"].map(lambda c: JUDGMENT[c][1])
evidence["deviation_type"] = evidence["case"].map(lambda c: JUDGMENT[c][2])

# The two flags that matter most and that raw_cost provably cannot express:
evidence["zero_cost_policy_failure"] = (
    (evidence["raw_cost"] == 0)
    & evidence["policy_breach"]

)
evidence["irreversible_exposure_eur"] = evidence.apply(
    lambda r: r.amount_eur if (r.paid and r.policy_breach and r.in_model_scope) else 0,
    axis=1,

EVIDENCE_COLS = [
    "case", "raw_cost", "pm4py_cost", "supplied_move_signature", "deviation_type",
