## 1. Executive causal assessment

Provisional verdict: `mainly confounded`. Confidence: 65/100. The crude Orion-Nova gap (+6.7h mean cycle) is not credible as a total causal effect because it reverses after conditioning on complexity: within Low, Medium, and High strata Orion's observed means are 2.1h, 2.2h, and 3.4h lower than Nova's, and direct standardization to the observed complexity mix gives Orion about 17.1h versus Nova 19.6h. The total effect would include queue, compliance, and rework pathways; a controlled direct effect would hold those mechanisms fixed; a mediated interpretation attributes differences to them. Strong evidence for confounding is the reversal and the fact that the crude gap is concentrated in queue wait tied to high-complexity, night, compliance-heavy work. Strong evidence against a firm causal benefit is poor overlap: Orion has almost no day cases, Nova has few high-complexity night cases, and several cells have fewer than three cases; unobserved risk, reviewer skill, completed-case selection, and wait measurement remain unresolved. The 50% reassignment may fail or backfire: if Orion's advantage reflects certification, moving cases to Nova may worsen cycle/rework; if the gap reflects night/compliance bottlenecks, relabeling cases without capacity will not help. It could succeed only if Orion genuinely harms low-risk cases, but the observed low/medium strata do not support that.

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | instrument routing and process: capture routing score, reviewer ID/certification, queue entry/exit, compliance start/end, request-info reason, open/censored status | all mechanisms; especially queue_wait, compliance, skill, selection | all cases, observational baseline | no routing change; privacy controls; data-quality validation before pilot | variant frequencies, DFGs, conformance to standard process, queue-wait distributions, compliance/rework rates, positivity map | identifies confounders, overlap gaps, bottlenecks, and feasible matching/pilot strata | if key fields are <95% complete, pause pilot until data quality is fixed |

| 3 | night/compliance capacity experiment: add cross-trained night compliance reviewers or priority compliance queue for both groups; do not change resource-group label | queue_wait, shift/load, compliance bottleneck | shift/queue/time block, preferably stepped-wedge | maintain compliance policy; monitor overtime/fatigue; rollout order randomized or pre-specified where possible | compliance step duration, queue_wait before/after, variant cycle times by group/shift, throughput and bottleneck rank | tests whether delay is shift/compliance capacity rather than group label; mechanism-specific learning without large reassignment | stop or modify if no queue_wait reduction after 4 weeks or quality/rework worsens |

Do not implement the blanket 50% reassignment now. The supplied data show a Simpson-type reversal: Orion looks slower overall because it receives more high-complexity, night, compliance-required work, but within observed complexity strata it is not slower and may be slightly faster. A large forced transfer could therefore move cases from a group with more certified reviewers to a group with less capacity for difficult night work, increasing cycle time, rework, or appeals. Instead, run a small randomized pilot on low-risk day-shift cases, instrument queues, reviewer skill, routing scores, compliance timestamps, and open/censored status. In parallel, relieve the night/compliance bottleneck directly. This preserves service while learning whether group, shift, compliance, or selection drives the observed gap. The pilot should be powered enough to detect a few hours of cycle-time change and pre-registered with stop rules.

```python
        acts = tr.split(">")
        for a, b in zip(acts[:-1], acts[1:]):
            edges[(a, b)] = edges.get((a, b), 0) + 1
    return pd.Series(edges, name="count").sort_values(ascending=False)

print("\n6) Directly-follows fallback/diagnostic counts by group")
for grp, sub in df.groupby("resource_group"):
    warn_if_small(len(sub), f"DFG for {grp}")
    print(f"Manual DFG counts for {grp}:")
    print(manual_dfg(sub).head(12))

print("\n7) PM4Py DFG diagnostic (no fabricated timestamps; frequency only)")
try:
    import pm4py

    def xes_for_group(grp):
        lines = ['<log xes.version="1.0">']
        for row in df[df["resource_group"] == grp].itertuples():
            lines.append("<trace>")
            lines.append(f'<string key="concept:name" value="{row.case_id}"/>')
            lines.append(f'<string key="resource_group" value="{row.resource_group}"/>')
            for act in row.trace.split(">"):
                lines.append("<event>")
                lines.append(f'<string key="concept:name" value="{act}"/>')
                lines.append("</event>")
            lines.append("</trace>")
        lines.append("</log>")
        return "\n".join(lines)

    for grp in group_levels:
        log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(xes_for_group(grp))
        dfg, start_activities, end_activities = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
        print(f"PM4Py DFG for {grp}; start={start_activities}; end={end_activities}")
        for (a, b), count in sorted(dfg.items(), key=lambda item: item[1], reverse=True)[:12]:
            print(f"  {a} -> {b}: {count}")

except Exception as exc:
    print(f"PM4Py DFG unavailable or failed ({exc}); use manual directly-follows counts above as fallback.")
```

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. Unobserved case severity or risk beyond the coarse complexity label. Most valuable addition: an underwriting or adjuster `risk_score` recorded at routing.
2. The routing engine’s actual assignment rule and propensities. Most valuable addition: `routing_score` or decision-reason fields, including any threshold or override indicator.
3. True queue dynamics and compliance-step timing. Most valuable addition: queue entry/exit timestamps and compliance start/end timestamps.
4. Reviewer skill, certification, and staffing exposure. Most valuable addition: `reviewer_id` with certification level and shift staffing counts.
5. Completed-case selection and open/abandoned case behavior. Most valuable addition: `case_status` with censoring/open-close timestamps, or a randomized routing pilot that tracks all assigned cases.
