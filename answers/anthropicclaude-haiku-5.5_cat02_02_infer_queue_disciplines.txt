# 1. Queue-by-queue diagnosis

### Working assumptions (labelled)

- **A1 (decision points):** Each queue is treated as dispatching at service-completion or preemption events. A decision time is the completion time of the previous job, or the event time when a resource became available.
- **A2 (visibility):** A job is visible to dispatch at its recorded arrival time. The two-minute scan delay makes the physical arrival earlier than or equal to the record, so it cannot make a recorded-visible job invisible. It can only explain differences of up to about two minutes.
- **A3 (estimates):** `est_min` is the dispatch-visible estimate and does not change. (Observation: for all 37 jobs, `est_min` equals the realized segment total exactly. This is treated as a data-quality flag, not as evidence of perfect forecasting. See Section 5.)
- **A4 (ties):** Equal priorities are broken by arrival order, which is the most common default.
- **A5 (appointments):** "Due" means the appointment time has been reached. Grace windows are unknown.
- **A6 (QD pools):** Electrical, mechanical, and firmware jobs are routed to skill pools. Pool membership is inferred from which resource actually served each job, because no routing log exists.
- **A7 (deadlines):** QC deadlines are treated as completion deadlines unless stated otherwise. Start-by semantics are a rival reading (Section 5).

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

### QA

- **Supporting:** A02 at 08:32 over A04 (p1), which contradicts priority. A05 at 09:05 over A08 (p1). A06 at 09:20, a due appointment that is also the FIFO head. A07 at 09:32. A03 at 08:20 is supported in a different way: its appointment rule explains a deviation from FIFO.
- **Contradicting:** A04 waited 45 minutes behind p3 jobs. A08 waited 61 minutes behind p3 jobs. A05 ran into A06's appointment slot, so A06 started 5 minutes late (09:20 instead of 09:15).
- **Explanation:** A03 is explained by a known rule (appointment-due). A06's lateness is explained by non-preemption and the absence of a pre-appointment hold. The priority-1 neglect at 08:30 and 09:05 is not explained by any documented constraint, so it counts against priority. A1's 2-minute idle at 08:30–08:32 is unexplained and could be a scan or setup delay. A01's 2-minute start delay (08:00 arrival, 08:02 start) is within the scan allowance.

### QB

- **Supporting:** B01 resumed at 09:31 and 10:14 over p2 jobs, which is FIFO and resume-first. B04 at 10:28 and B06 at 10:36 are FIFO choices. B02 preempting B01 at 09:16 is a p1 preemption, which is a clear priority signal.
- **Contradicting:** B05 (p1) was waiting at 09:48 but B03 (p2) was chosen. B07's appointment (10:30) was not honoured; it started 26 minutes late, while B06 started at 10:36.
- **Explanation:** The 09:48 choice is annotated "dispatcher reassignment." This is a labelled manual exception and is not explained by the documented rule. B07 is not explained by a known constraint: no grace period exists, and QA's appointment rule would have started it at 10:36. Its 26-minute lateness is undocumented. The 2-minute scan bound cannot rescue 09:48 because B05 was recorded at 09:46, two minutes before the reassignment.

### QC

- **Supporting:** C02 at 10:10 and C03 at 10:18 are SPT choices that contradict FIFO and priority. C06 at 11:34 is an SPT choice that contradicts priority and EDD (C07 had higher priority and an earlier deadline).
- **Contradicting:** C04 at 10:32 was chosen while C06 (est 6) was waiting. C01 at 11:40 was chosen over C07 (est 18), which causes C07 to miss its deadline by 34 minutes. C05 was run at 10:54 only after its deadline had passed.
- **Explanation:** C05's deadline (10:50) was already infeasible at arrival (10:11 plus 40 minutes = 10:51). The stated note, "deadline already at risk when selected," understates the situation: the deadline had already passed. C05 is therefore an overdue-trigger exception. C04 at 10:32 is unexplained by any documented or tested rule. C01 at 11:40 is the strongest undocumented exception. It is plausibly an aging rule (C01 had waited about 100 minutes) or a backlog-first rule, based on the note "executed last among initial backlog."

### QD

- **Supporting:** D03 at 11:21 (FIFO and SPT). D04 at 11:25 over the p1 job D08 (pool-FIFO). D06 at 11:30 (appointment and FIFO). D07 at 11:48 (FIFO, scarcity). D05 at 12:20 (meeting and only qualified resource).
- **Contradicting:** D10 (p1) waited 51 minutes, and was passed over at 11:30 and 11:48. D-F was idle for 24 minutes before the meeting (11:36–12:00) while D10 fitted in the gap. D-X was idle from 11:20 to 11:30 while D07 waited.
- **Explanation:** The D10 neglect and D-F idleness are explained by pool-based routing and head-of-line blocking, provided D10 was routed to the D-X pool at arrival. This is a hypothesis, since no routing log exists. The D-X idle period is explained by an appointment hold on the head of the D-X pool (D06), but this is also a hypothesis. Other explanations include unrecorded setup or a delayed release of D-X.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

### Test 1: QC at 11:40, C01 versus C07

- **Competing explanations:**
  - **Hybrid SPT with aging or backlog promotion:** predicts C01 (age about 100 minutes, waiting since 10:00).
  - **Pure SPT with a deadline-blind dispatcher:** predicts C07 (est 18 < 36).
- **Predicted next job:** C01 under aging or backlog; C07 under SPT.
- **Discriminating observable:** The queue-entry and wait-age record for each job at each decision time. A promotion flag or a documented age threshold would separate the two readings. Under aging, C01's age must exceed a threshold in (94, 100] minutes, since it was not promoted at 11:34 (94 minutes) and was promoted at 11:40 (100 minutes). If the age threshold is logged, it can be tested prospectively.

### Test 2: QD at 11:36, D-F idle versus D10 started on D-F
