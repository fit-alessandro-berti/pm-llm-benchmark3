# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

**Labels used.** Observation = directly in the data. Estimate = computed under stated assumptions. Bound = assumption-free or near assumption-free range. Hypothesis = untested explanation. Recommendation = design advice.

## Assumptions

- **A1.** `duration_or_age_h` is wall-clock elapsed hours from `Submit`, and the SLA clock is never paused. Without this the 72 h comparison is undefined.
- **A2.** The extract contains every case submitted in the window. There is no outcome-dependent selection and no left truncation.
- **A3.** An `open` case has no terminal event before the cutoff. Its `duration_or_age_h` is the censoring time.
- **A4.** For an open case, `last_event_gap_h` is the time spent so far in the current stage. The entry time to that stage is therefore `age  gap`. This is derived from supplied fields, not invented.
- **A5.** Prefix order is true event order. No intermediate event times are available, so none are used.

## Time definitions

- **Origin:** `Submit`, t = 0.
- **Horizon:** H = 72 h.
- **Terminal time and cause:** T = time to first terminal disposition, with cause J coded 1 = Resolve, 2 = Withdraw, 3 = Reject, 4 = Transfer.
- **Censoring time:** C = age at cutoff for open cases.
- **What we observe:** X = min(T, C) and  = 1{T  C}·J.
- **Functions:**
  - F_j(t) = P(T  t, J = j).
  - S(t) = P(T > t).
  - Identity: S(t) + F1(t) + F2(t) + F3(t) + F4(t) = 1.

## Three distinct data situations (not interchangeable)

1. **Right-censored before H.** The case is open with age < 72. Its status at 72 is unknown. These are S10, S15, S24, S29, S31.
2. **Already-observed breach.** Terminal after 72, or open with age  72. The case was provably not terminal at 72. This applies to S03, S13, S20, S28, S32, S09, S11, S25 and S26. Being "open at 92" is not censoring with respect to the horizon-72 question. It is censoring only for what happens after its age.
3. **Competing event before H.** Withdrawn, rejected or transferred at or before 72. The case can no longer be resolved internally in the ordinary way.

**Observation:** of 32 cases, 18 are terminal by 72 (9 resolved, 9 other), 5 are terminal after 72, 4 are open at 72, and 5 are open at <72.

**Competing risks (Estimate).** Treating withdrawals, rejections and transfers as ordinary censoring inflates resolution. **Informative censoring.** Two separate problems arise.

- Open cases with long gaps look abandoned or stalled:

  - S11 is in `Escalate` with a 55 h gap.
  - S25 is in `Escalate` with a 48 h gap.
  - S09 is in `Review` with a 31 h gap.

  Their breach status at 72 is known. What is uncertain is the tail: if they never resolve, treating them as independently censored overstates late resolution.

**Open cases below versus above 72.**

- Dropping all open cases removes four known breaches (S09, S11, S25, S26).
- Treating all open cases as "unknown" wastes that information.
- Open cases with age < 72 (S10, S15, S24, S29, S31) are unknown at the horizon and need a censoring model or bounds.

| AutoCheck-first | The activity right after `Submit` is `AutoCheck` | Nearly baseline (first routing decision) | Possibly assigned by priority (all B P1 and P2, no P3). Treat as treatment-received with confounding. |
| current stage (open cases) | Last activity | Time-varying, valid at cutoff | Safe for forecasting open cases. For terminal cases it is the outcome (leakage). |
| time in current stage | `last_event_gap_h` for open cases | Time-varying | **Observation:** gap = 0 for every terminal row and > 0 for every open row, so it perfectly separates status. Never use it as a baseline predictor. |
| stage entry time | age  gap (open only) | Time-varying | Derived from supplied fields. Terminal rows have no equivalent. |
| escalated / Escalate position | Prefix | Post-baseline exposure | Immortal-time and confounding-by-indication risk. |
| number of reviews / repeated Review | Prefix (S03, S07, S13 have 2) | Post-baseline | Leakage if taken from the whole prefix. A later review requires surviving long enough. |
| rework_count | Column | Post-baseline | **Observation:** all 3 rework cases are resolved (S07, S13, S27). Open cases show "0 so far", not "never". |
| review-free path | No `Review` in the prefix | Post-baseline | **Observation:** all four late resolutions involve `Review`. Review-free resolutions are  29 h. Selection into the fast track is not baseline. |
| path pattern / variant | PM4Py variants | Post-baseline | A completed variant describes the outcome. |

## Immortal-time and leakage

- **Escalation as a baseline flag.** A case escalated at t = e must survive to e. Naively comparing "ever escalated" with "never" gives the escalated group guaranteed survival up to e. Yet escalation is also triggered by difficulty and delay, which biases the other way.
- **Right-truncated prefixes.** Open or recently censored cases have had less opportunity to escalate, review or rework. "Escalated = no" for S31 (age 23) and S24 means "not yet".
- **Safe design.** Code escalation as a time-varying covariate in counting-process form, or use a landmark at a fixed time s using only information before s. Both need event timestamps.
- **Observation:** every Transfer (S08, S21, S32) immediately follows `Escalate`.
- **Hypothesis:** escalation acts as a gateway to transfer for P1 cases, not simply a rescue.

## Proposed state-transition structure (text, not a code block)

- **Transient states:** Submitted, AutoChecked, Screened, InReview, Escalated, Rework.
- **Absorbing states:** Resolved, Withdrawn, Rejected, Transferred.
- **Observed transitions:**
  - Submitted  Screened, and Submitted  AutoChecked (cohort B, 12 cases).
  - Screened  AutoChecked, Review, Escalated, Resolved, Rejected, Withdrawn.
  - AutoChecked  Screened, Review, Escalated, Resolved.
  - InReview  Resolved, Escalated, Rework, Withdrawn, Rejected.
  - Rework  InReview, Resolved.
  - Escalated  Resolved, InReview, Transferred.
- **Censoring:** at the cutoff from any transient state. For open cases, the entry time into the current state is age  gap.
- **Identifiability:**
  - Transition counts from the embedded chain are identifiable.
  - Sojourn-time hazards are not, except the current sojourn of open cases.
  - For terminal cases the time in the final stage is unknown.

**Stage observations (Observation).** The open cases are in these stages:

| Stage | Cases |
|---|---|
| Review | S09, S15, S24, S26, S29 |
| Escalate | S10, S11, S25 |
| Screen | S31 |

The three open Escalate cases have gaps of 19, 55 and 48 h. The three known long-stalled open cases, S26, S11 and S25, are each in a single stage with  48 h of inactivity.

# 7. Operational interpretation

**Estimand to decision.**

- Backlog and staffing at the 72 h mark  E3.
- Penalty and credit exposure  E2 and E1.
- Transfer management  F4 and the external outcome (needed).
- Customer communication  E5.

**Alert tiers (pooled, illustrative).**

- **Already breached (age  72):** S09, S11, S25, S26. All four have gaps  31 h and are stalled. The action is triage and reassignment, not prediction.
- **Approaching (age  48 and < 72, still active):**
  - S24 (67 h, `Review`, gap 4) has about 5 h left.
  - S29 (51 h, `Review` after `Escalate`, gap 6).
  - S10 (47 h, `Escalate`, gap 19) is next.
- **Watch:** S15 (35 h) and S31 (23 h).
- **Conditional risk (from pooled AJ):**
  - For a case still active at 24 h, P(still active at 72) = 0.467 and P(resolved in (24, 72]) = 0.259.
  - At 48 h the figures are 0.655 and 0.133.

**Workload actions.**

- Review the `Review` and `Escalate` queues for gaps above a threshold.
- Track externally transferred P1 cases, which are the current "black hole" (S08, S21, S32).

**Governance.**

- Publish the five-way partition with bounds and intervals.
- State which transfer assumption is used.
- Do not treat "escalated" as a causal lever. It reflects who was escalated and when.
- Risk scores from these data rank cases for attention. They are not treatment effects, and the cohort difference is not a policy effect.
- Do not use alert counts to rank teams.

**Calibration and fairness checks (recommended; data here are too small to validate).**

- Time-dependent calibration by IPCW or pseudo-observations at 24, 48 and 72 h, by priority and cohort.
- Discrimination (time-dependent AUC or C-index) by priority and cohort.
- Require a minimum of about 10 events per stratum, otherwise pool or report intervals only.
- Compare alert recall and false-alert burden across priorities. Watch P3 (50% exit through rejection or withdrawal, so a model focused on P1 and resolution could deprioritize it) and P1 (high transfer).
- Monitor drift after the policy change, including AutoCheck compliance.

# ---- 2. Encodings: competing-risk codes 0=cens 1=resolve 2=withdraw 3=reject 4=transfer 5=abandon --
def encode(d, transfer="competing", stalled="none", early_open="none", other_as_censor=False):
    t = d["time"].copy()
    c = d["code0"].copy()
    tr = d["observed_status"].eq("transferred")
    if transfer == "censor":
        c[tr] = 0                                   # T2: independent censoring at transfer
    elif transfer == "optimistic":
        c[tr] = 1                                   # T3: resolved at transfer time
    elif transfer != "competing":                   # T1: stays code 4
        raise ValueError(transfer)
    op = d["observed_status"].eq("open")
    if stalled == "never_resolves":                 # stalled open cases become a competing 'abandon' at age
        c[op & (d["last_event_gap_h"] >= STALL_H)] = 5
    lt = op & (d["time"] < H)
    if early_open == "best":
        c[lt] = 1                                   # resolves immediately after last observation
    elif early_open == "worst":
        t[lt] = H + 0.001                           # no event before H, stays at risk through H
    if other_as_censor:                             # naive KM: all non-resolve exits censored
        c[c.isin([2, 3, 4])] = 0
    return pd.DataFrame({"case": d["case"], "time": t, "code": c})

# ---- 3. Aalen-Johansen / risk-set table ----------------------------------------------------------
def aalen_johansen(e, causes=(1, 2, 3, 4, 5)):
    tm, cd = e["time"].to_numpy(), e["code"].to_numpy()
    S, F = 1.0, {k: 0.0 for k in causes}
    rows = [dict(time=0.0, n_risk=len(e), S=1.0, **{f"F{k}": 0.0 for k in causes})]
    for t in np.unique(tm[cd > 0]):
        n = int((tm >= t).sum())
        ev = cd[(tm == t) & (cd > 0)]
        for k in causes:
            F[k] += S * (ev == k).sum() / n
        S *= 1 - len(ev) / n
        rows.append(dict(time=float(t), n_risk=n, S=S, **{f"F{k}": F[k] for k in causes}))
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

def at(tab, h, col):
    return float(tab.loc[tab["time"] <= h, col].iloc[-1])

def estimands(e):
    tab = aalen_johansen(e)
    F1H, F1T = at(tab, H, "F1"), at(tab, TAU2, "F1")
    return {"E1 unresolved by 72": 1 - F1H,
            "E2 late-resolve CI (72,96]": F1T - F1H,
            "E3 no terminal at 72": at(tab, H, "S"),
            "E4a withdrawn by 72": at(tab, H, "F2"),
            "E4b rejected by 72": at(tab, H, "F3"),
            "E4c transferred by 72": at(tab, H, "F4"),
            "E5 owed at 72 (E3+F4)": at(tab, H, "S") + at(tab, H, "F4"),
            "n": len(e)}

# 9. Limitations and additional data

Ordered by how much each would improve inference.

1. **Exact event timestamps, with stage entry and exit times.** They would identify stage-specific hazards and sojourn distributions, and make escalation a proper time-varying exposure. Landmarking and multi-state modelling require them. Until then, prefix features are ordinal only.
2. **External outcomes after transfer.** Transfer is a competing event, a bound, or censoring depending on an unobserved outcome. All three transfers are escalated P1 cases, so this is the largest driver of P1 results.
3. **Cutoff date and case start dates.** These allow checking that censoring is administrative, allow calendar-time and cohort adjustment, and allow stratifying censoring by start date. The informative-censoring assumption for open cases cannot be tested without them.
4. **Policy rollout compliance and assignment mechanism.** AutoCheck-first is assigned by priority (all B P1 and P2, no B P3). Two A cases had AutoCheck. Without the assignment rule, per-protocol and intention-to-treat contrasts are confounded and cohort effects cannot be interpreted causally.
5. **Time-varying workload and queue data.** These explain stalling (S26, S11, S25, S09) and distinguish capacity from case-specific delay.
6. **Reasons for withdrawal, rejection and escalation.** They allow judging whether withdrawals follow slow service and whether early rejections are ineligibility.
7. **Larger sample and a reliable SLA clock definition.** With 13 resolves and 9 at risk at 72 h, all stratified estimates are descriptive, and the tail beyond about 96 h rests on 3 or fewer cases. Confirm that business-hour rules or pauses are not applied (A1).
