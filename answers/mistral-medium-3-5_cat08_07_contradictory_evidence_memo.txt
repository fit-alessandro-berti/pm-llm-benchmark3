# Decision Memo: Next Steps for Fiber-Installation Process Redesign

---

## 1. Executive memo

**Recommendation:** Adopt **Alternative E (Hybrid Deployment with Controls and Measurement)** as the immediate path forward. Scale Redesign R *only* for **simple low-risk houses** (34% of cases), where it delivers clear benefits (median cycle time 6 days, first-time-right 3pp, complaints 1pp). For all other segments—especially **apartments, old buildings, and business customers**—pause further rollout and implement targeted controls (reinstate mandatory field surveys for medium/high-risk addresses, prohibit billing before activation, and enforce permit checks). Concurrently, launch **M1, M3, M4, and M7** to resolve critical uncertainties around risk stratification, rework costs, and compliance gaps.

**Trade-off:** The redesign’s **coordinator ownership** and **merged validation/scheduling** reduce handovers and improve median performance, but **skipping surveys, post-billing activation, and high coordinator load** introduce tail risks, rework, and compliance issues. The **segment-specific benefits** (e.g., simple houses) justify partial scaling, but the **deterioration in apartments/old buildings** (p90 cycle time 25 days, first-time-right 11pp) and **low model precision** (0.51) signal systemic flaws in the current design. Rolling back entirely (B) would forfeit gains for simple cases; scaling nationally (A) would amplify tail risks. Controlled experiments (D) are valuable but delay action on the 34% of cases where R is already working.

**Rationale:** The process-mining evidence shows **high fitness (0.94)** but **low precision (0.51)**, meaning the pilot process *describes* observed cases well but *allows too many invalid paths* (e.g., billing before activation, skipped permits). This aligns with the **worse tail metrics** (p90/p95 cycle time, complaints, SLA breaches) and **higher rework** (second visits 17%, manual activation 15%). The **segment breakdown** reveals that R’s predictive rule for skipping surveys is **inaccurate for apartments/old buildings**, while coordinator ownership is **overloaded** (93% utilization). Addressing these with **targeted controls** and **better measurement** will clarify whether R can be refined or if a different approach is needed for complex cases.

## 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

### **High fitness but low precision**
- **Fitness (0.94)** is high because the pilot model *captures most observed behavior* (e.g., the new coordinator path, merged validation/scheduling).
- **Precision (0.51)** is low because the model *allows many unrealistic paths* not seen in the baseline, such as:
  - Billing start before activation test (frequent move-on-log).
  - Skipped permit checks for old apartments (frequent move-on-model).
  - Repeated installation visits without field surveys.
- **Cause:** The redesign introduced **flexibility** (e.g., optional surveys, post-billing activation) that the process model cannot constrain, leading to **non-conformant but observed** behavior.

### **Improved median but worse tail duration**
- **Median improvement (3.4 days):** Driven by **simple houses** (6-day reduction) and **merged validation/scheduling**, which accelerated early stages.
- **Tail deterioration (p90 7.5 days, p95 17 days):** Caused by:
  - **Complex cases (apartments/old buildings)** where skipped surveys led to **failed visits, second visits, and permit delays**.
  - **Post-billing activation** creating **rework loops** (complaints after billing start).
  - **Coordinator bottlenecks** (93% utilization) delaying resolutions for stalled cases.

### **Fewer handovers but lower resilience**
- **Handovers  (34%  12%):** Coordinator ownership reduced transitions between teams, improving accountability for simple cases.
- **Resilience :** Centralization around coordinators meant:
  - **Single points of failure** (absences stalled cases).
  - **Context not shared** (no handover documentation for edge cases).
  - **Activation specialists overloaded** with manual exceptions.

### **Lower visible cost but higher rework/downstream cost**
- **Visible cost  (EUR 184  161):** Fewer handovers and merged tasks reduced labor in early stages.
- **Rework/downstream cost  (EUR 198  207):** Incomplete data (costs exclude complaints after week 4), but:
  - **Second visits 17%**, **manual activation 15%**, and **complaints 9.8%** imply higher hidden costs.
  - **Billing before activation** likely triggered **customer service disputes** (not fully captured).

### **Improvement for simple houses but deterioration for apartments/old buildings**
- **Simple houses:** Benefited from **fewer handovers** and **predictive rule accuracy** (low risk  no survey needed).
- **Apartments/old buildings:**
  - **Survey skip rule was wrong** (medium/high risk misclassified as low), leading to **failed visits and permit issues**.
  - **Post-billing activation** caused **complaints** (service not working but billed).
  - **Coordinator overload** in regions with more apartments exacerbated delays.

## 4. Decision alternatives

| **Alternative** | **Advantages**                                                                 | **Risks**                                                                                     | **When Reasonable**                                                                 |
|-----------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **A. Scale R nationally** | Captures median improvements (10.8-day cycle time) and handover reductions. | Amplifies tail risks (p90/p95 worse), rework (complaints, second visits), and compliance gaps (billing before activation). | Only if **M6 validates the survey-skip rule** and **M7 confirms no billing-activation violations**. |
| **B. Roll back to baseline** | Reverts to known stability (higher precision, fewer compliance issues).       | Forfeits gains for **simple houses** (34% of cases) and **merged validation/scheduling**.    | If **M4 shows downstream costs outweigh benefits** or **M1 proves survey skipping is unsafe**. |
| **C. Scale coordinator ownership only** | Retains handover reductions (12% vs. 34%) and median improvements.            | Does not address **survey skipping** or **post-billing activation**, which drive rework.     | If **M3/M7 show these are the primary tail drivers** and coordinator load can be managed. |
| **D. Controlled regional experiment** | Tests R with **better segment/risk measurement** (e.g., M1, M6).              | Delays action on **simple houses** where R is already working.                              | If **uncertainty about segment effects is high** and management prioritizes learning over speed. |
| **E. Hybrid deployment** | Scales **proven benefits** (simple houses) while **controlling risks** (apartments/old buildings). | Requires **operational changes** (reinstate surveys, enforce activation before billing).     | **Default recommendation**: Balances **immediate gains** with **risk mitigation** and **measurement**. |

## 5. Value-of-information analysis

**Ranking (Highest to Lowest Value):**

1. **M6: Validate the building-risk predictive rule against field-survey outcomes**
   - **Uncertainty resolved:** Is the survey-skip rule accurate for medium/high-risk cases?
   - **Decision impact:** If **inaccurate**, survey skipping must be suspended (supports E or C). If **accurate**, could expand R to more segments (supports A).
   - **Urgency:** Directly addresses the **worst segment deterioration** (old buildings/apartments).

2. **M3: Add structured reason codes for Failed visit, Manual activation, Complaint escalation, Second visit**
   - **Uncertainty resolved:** What are the **root causes** of rework (e.g., skipped surveys, permit issues, coordinator errors)?
   - **Decision impact:** Informs **targeted fixes** (e.g., reinstate permits for old buildings) and **prioritization of controls** in E.

3. **M4: Measure full downstream cost for 60 days after Billing start**
   - **Uncertainty resolved:** Are the **hidden costs** (complaints, revisits) **materially worse** than visible savings?
   - **Decision impact:** If **downstream costs > EUR 161 savings**, scaling R is unjustified (supports B or E). If **marginal**, E remains viable.

4. **M7: Run conformance monitoring for Billing start before Activation test and Permit check absence**
   - **Uncertainty resolved:** How **frequent and costly** are compliance violations (e.g., billing before activation)?
   - **Decision impact:** If **violations are systemic**, post-billing activation must be **banned** (supports E or C).

5. **M1: Randomized pilot of survey skipping by building-risk stratum**
   - **Uncertainty resolved:** Does **risk-stratified skipping** (vs. current rule) improve outcomes?
   - **Decision impact:** Could **refine R** to expand to medium-risk cases (supports A or E).
   - **Lower priority than M6** because M6 is a prerequisite (current rule is likely flawed).

6. **M8: Collect customer experience separately for "speed to appointment" and "working service after installation"**
   - **Uncertainty resolved:** Are **NPS drops** driven by **speed** (improved) or **quality** (worse)?
   - **Decision impact:** Helps **prioritize fixes** (e.g., if quality is the issue, focus on activation checks).

7. **M2: Instrument start timestamps for Installation visit, Activation test, and Manual activation**
   - **Uncertainty resolved:** What are the **true durations** of critical steps (currently sparse)?
   - **Decision impact:** Useful for **process optimization** but **not decision-critical** for scaling.

8. **M5: Compare coordinator ownership with shared-team ownership under coordinator absence**
   - **Uncertainty resolved:** Does **centralization reduce resilience**?
   - **Decision impact:** Could inform **resource allocation** but **not urgent** for the scaling decision.

## 6. Final recommendation

**Immediate Action:**
- **Scale Redesign R for simple low-risk houses only** (34% of cases), where it delivers **clear benefits** (median cycle time 6 days, complaints 1pp).
- **Pause further rollout for all other segments** (apartments, old buildings, business customers).

**What to Pause:**
- **Survey skipping for medium/high-risk addresses** (reinstate mandatory field surveys for apartments and old buildings).
- **Post-billing activation** (enforce activation test completion before billing start).
- **National scaling of R** until critical uncertainties are resolved.

**What to Scale:**
- **Coordinator ownership** (retain for all cases, but **cap utilization at 80%** to avoid bottlenecks).
- **Merged validation/scheduling for simple cases** (proven to reduce handovers and median time).

**What to Measure Next (Priority Order):**
1. **M6** (Validate survey-skip rule) – *Threshold: If rule accuracy <80% for medium/high risk, suspend skipping permanently.*
2. **M3** (Structured reason codes) – *Threshold: If >50% of second visits are due to skipped surveys/permits, reinstate mandatory checks for all non-simple cases.*
3. **M4** (Full downstream cost) – *Threshold: If total cost > EUR 190, roll back R for all segments except simple houses.*
4. **M7** (Conformance monitoring) – *Threshold: If >5% of cases have billing before activation, ban post-billing activation.*

**Threshold for Broader Rollout:**
- **Expand R to apartments/old buildings** only if:
  - M6 shows **survey-skip rule accuracy 85%** for medium risk.
  - M4 shows **downstream costs  EUR 185**.
  - M7 shows **<2% compliance violations** (billing before activation).
- **National scaling (A)** requires **all above + M1 proving risk-stratified skipping works**.

**Rationale:** This approach **captures immediate wins** while **controlling the worst risks**, and the **measurement plan** directly targets the **most critical uncertainties** (segment accuracy, rework costs, compliance). It avoids the **false choice** between full rollback (B) and reckless scaling (A), instead **iterating toward a refined design**.
