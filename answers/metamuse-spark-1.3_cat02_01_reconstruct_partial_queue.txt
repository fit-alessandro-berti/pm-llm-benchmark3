# 1. Assumptions and notation
- **A1 Observation window:** $T=[08{:}00,14{:}00)$, 360 min. `arrival_s1` = S1 queue entry. S1 completion = S2 queue entry. Blank S2 for completed S1 = waiting for S2 at 14:00. Blank S1 = waiting for S1 at 14:00. OPEN = started but not completed by 14:00; elapsed time is a lower bound on final service. MISSING = occurred, resource and completion known, start unknown.
- **A2 Rounding:** Reported $t$ represents actual $\in [t-2.5,t+2.5]$ min approx. Differences of two rounded times have $\pm5$ min uncertainty. Ordering of events with same rounded stamp is not observed. No systematic bias assumed, but bounds are widened by $\pm5$ min for fully observed wait/service unless otherwise stated.
- **A3 Capacity:** S1: 2 single-task servers I1,I2. S2: 2 single-task servers C1,C2, except C2 unavailable 10:55-11:10 (15 min). In principle no overlap on same resource. Apparent overlaps are treated as evidence of rounding/logging/assistance, not as proof of extra capacity.
- **A4 Event-order convention for counting:** At equal rounded $t$, order as: arrivals $\le$ completions $<$ starts. I.e. a completion at $t$ leaves queue/service before a start at $t$ is counted; a case with `s1_end=s2_start=t` is counted in S2 from $t$; a case with service end = next service start on same resource does not double-count busy. WIP and queue series are right-continuous. This avoids negative queues and false double-busy from rounding. Alternative convention (starts before completions) would inflate peak WIP by at most 1-2 cases at tie points; bounds report this.
- **A5 Resource discipline:** FIFO within each station is assumed as a constraint for bounding MISSING starts, not as proven fact. Strict single-task (no overlap on same resource) gives resource-constrained bounds. A relaxed physical-only bound (start $\in$ [queue entry, completion]) is also reported to allow overlap/assistance hypotheses.
- **A6 Missing-start imputation:** No single-value substitution. For each MISSING we report: (i) physical bounds, (ii) resource-constrained bounds under A4-A5 plus rounding tolerance, (iii) central estimate using typical service of that station/resource, explicitly justified. Optimistic = minimal wait / minimal service consistent with constraints as defined per scenario; central = typical-service anchored; pessimistic = maximal wait / maximal elapsed.
- **A7 Censoring:** OPEN and waiting at 14:00 are right-censored. Elapsed wait/service to 14:00 are lower bounds on final values. Utilization uses elapsed busy to 14:00 as lower bound; upper bound adds no speculative post-14:00 work. Means that exclude censored cases are labelled completed-only and not used as unbiased estimates.
- **A8 Arrival system:** External `arrival_s1` rounded same way, independent system. No left-censoring correction before 08:00 assumed; system assumed empty at 08:00 because first waits are small (5 min), but this is a hypothesis.
- Notation: $a$=S1 arrival, $s1_s,s1_e$=S1 start/end, $s2_s,s2_e$=S2 start/end. S1 wait $W_{q1}=s1_s-a$, service $S_1=s1_e-s1_s$, sojourn $J_1=W_{q1}+S_1$. S2 wait $W_{q2}=s2_s-s1_e$, service $S_2=s2_e-s2_s$, sojourn $J_2=W_{q2}+S_2$. Total sojourn for completed $J=a$-to-$s2_e$. $L, L_q$ = mean number in station / in queue, $\lambda$ = arrival rate to station, $W,J$ = mean sojourn/wait, $U$ = busy/(capacity $\times$ interval).

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas
## 2.1 Case-level quantities
- Fully observed: central wait/service = reported difference; interval = central $\pm5$ min for rounding, truncated at 0. Example P01 S1 wait $08{:}05-08{:}00=5$, reported as $5$ $[0,10]$.
- MISSING start $x$ with entry $e$ and completion $c$: physical $x\in[e,c]$, so $W=x-e\in[0,c-e]$, $S=c-x\in[0,c-e]$, $W+S=c-e$ fixed up to rounding. Resource-constrained: $x\ge$ prior completion on same resource (minus 2.5 tolerance) and $c\le$ next start on same resource (plus tolerance), and $x\ge e$. Central $x^*$ chosen so implied $S$ matches station-typical $S_1\approx25$-$30$, $S_2\approx35$-$45$ unless this violates resource constraints, in which case anomaly is flagged rather than forced.
- OPEN with start $s$ at 14:00=$T_{end}$: elapsed $S^{elapsed}=T_{end}-s$ is lower bound; final $S\ge S^{elapsed}$, $J\ge T_{end}-e$. Waiting for station with entry $e$: elapsed wait $\ge T_{end}-e$.
## 2.2 Station-level quantities via pandas
- Arrivals to S1 in $I$: count $a\in I$. Arrivals to S2 in $I$: count $s1_e\in I$. Completions: count $s1_e\in I$ / $s2_e\in I$ (OPEN/MISSING handled separately).
- Throughput $TH=$ completions/|I|. Arrival rate $\lambda=$ arrivals/|I|.
- WIP$(t)$: cases with $a\le t < s2_e$ (or $T_{end}$ if censored), split into S1-queue ($a\le t < s1_s$), S1-service ($s1_s\le t < s1_e$), S2-queue ($s1_e\le t < s2_s$), S2-service ($s2_s\le t < s2_e$), using A4 ties and scenario values for MISSING. Mean WIP = time-average; peak = max. Bounds from optimistic/central/pessimistic $x$ and $\pm2.5$ edge effects.
- Busy time per resource = sum $(e-s)$ for completed + elapsed for OPEN, with interval bounds for MISSING. Utilization $U=$ busy/(number servers $\times$ |I|), with C2 denominator reduced by 15 min outage in intervals covering it.
- Little's Law $L=\lambda W$ requires approx stationarity, stable $W$ distribution, FIFO conservation and long interval relative to sojourn, with censored cases properly included. Check is only attempted where queue does not explode and arrivals $\approx$ departures; otherwise reported as not defensible and shown only to illustrate bias if censored cases are dropped.
- PM4Py `insert_case_service_waiting_time` and `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` are case-level (case arrival to case finish), not station-queue/service. They are therefore demonstrated only on a projection of fully observed COMPLETE cases and labelled as such; station-specific queue/service/WIP uses direct pandas interval logic, which is safer because PM4Py time-interval conversion cannot distinguish S1-queue vs S2-queue vs service without explicit station stamping and mishandles MISSING/OPEN/blank as identical.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation
**Evidence:** Both stations show rising waits, rising WIP, and $U>90\%$ after 10:00 — system is overloaded relative to arrival surge (7-8/h early vs combined capacity ~4/h S1, ~2.7/h S2). S1 queue builds first (waits 540 by 09:15, queue ~3 by 10:00) because arrivals hit S1 directly. S2 queue lags but then grows monotonically (waits 555 by 11:15, 65 by 13:05; queue 0.53.5 mean) and never drains: 6 cases still in S2 at 14:00. S1 service stable ~25-30 ($CV$ low); S2 service ~40 mean with 25-50 spread ($CV$ higher). Long-run capacity is therefore lower at S2. Resource chains are otherwise perfectly sequential with 5-min gaps, confirming saturation rather than idleness.
**Hypotheses vs observations:** Observation: S1 waits plateau ~100 after 12:00 while S2 waits keep rising and censored S2 waits (85,50,35,15 +75,65) imply further growth — consistent with bottleneck shifting downstream. Hypothesis: early morning bottleneck appears as S1 (its queue visible first), late morning as S2 (its queue dominates sojourn: e.g. P14 $J_1=120$, $J_2=110$; P12 $J_1=115$, $J_2=90$). This time-varying combination is more defensible than a single-station label. Alternative single-bottleneck stories fail: S1-only cannot explain why S2 waits triple while S1 service stays flat; S2-only cannot explain why S1 queue reaches 7 and P22 waits 110 min for S1.
**Maintenance interval 10:55-11:10:** C2 idle 15 min; C2 jobs P06 ends 10:50, P08 starts 11:15 neatly bracket the outage, so no preemption observed. Capacity loss $\approx15/40\approx0.38$ cases, an order of magnitude smaller than queued work (5+ cases). No jump in waits aligns exactly to outage: P08 wait 55 is large but its S1 end 10:20 already queued before outage; P09 (C1) wait 25 unaffected. Effect is therefore minor delay to one C2 slot, not a regime change. If outage had not occurred, at most one S2 completion might have shifted ~10-15 min earlier; peak WIP unchanged within rounding.
**Rounding sensitivity on bottleneck:** $\pm5$ min does not reorder the trend (5100 waits). Only tie P07-end/P09-start 11:00 on C1 is order-sensitive; under opposite convention peak C1 busy double-counts 1 case for an instant, not a sustained bottleneck shift.

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis
Three scenarios applied consistently:
- **Optimistic (minimal congestion):** MISSING starts as late as resource-allowed (P05 10:15, P07 10:00? Actually late start = max wait, so for wait-minimising use early start: P05 09:45, P07 09:25, P11 11:05 (overlap allowed), P15 11:40), rounding $-5$ on waits, OPEN/waiting censored at elapsed (not extrapolated), P11 service 80 (overlap). Gives S1 mean wait ~60 completed, S2 mean wait ~32 completed, $U_{S1}\approx80\%$, $U_{S2}\approx78\%$.
- **Central (typical-service):** As in §3 table (P05 09:45/30, P07 09:32/28, P11 flagged wide but plotted at res-strict 12:00/25 for WIP, P15 11:45/30), rounding central. Gives S1 mean $\approx68$ completed ($\ge75$ with censored), S2 mean $\approx36$ completed ($\ge45$ with censored), $U\approx85\%/81\%$.
- **Pessimistic (max congestion, strict single-task):** MISSING starts as late as allowed without overlap (P05 10:15/0? Actually late start maximises wait: P05 wait 50 svc 0 unrealistic but bound, P07 wait 75 svc 0, P11 wait 80 svc 0, P15 wait 125 svc 0), rounding $+5$, censored waits counted at elapsed (still lower bounds). Gives S1 mean $\approx75$, S2 mean $\approx42$ completed, $U_{S1}\approx87\%$, $U_{S2}\approx83\%$ (P11 short service lowers S2 busy, so pessimistic wait  max busy).
**Stable conclusions:** (1) waits grow 5~100 S1, 5~65 S2 under all scenarios (range shifts $\le10$ min); (2) both stations $>75\%$ utilized overall, $>88\%$ after 10:00; (3) total WIP rises to 8 at 14:00 $\pm1$ for tie convention; (4) C2 outage effect $<15$ min vs $>50$ min queues; (5) S2 long-run capacity $<$ S1. **Unstable:** (1) exact mean S2 wait/service ($\pm10$ min depending on P11 handling — dropping P11 vs including res-strict vs overlap changes mean by 3-5 min); (2) P11 individual service (0-80 physical, 0-25 strict, ~40 only with overlap) — must stay as interval, no point estimate defensible; (3) any Little's-Law point check (see below); (4) whether S1 or S2 queue is larger at a single instant near ties ($\pm1$). Recommendation: report intervals, flag P11 as data-quality review (check C2 log assistance/preemption), do not use completed-only means for staffing without censoring correction.

Little's-Law check (defensible interval only): No 2-h interval is truly stationary ($\lambda\neq TH$, queues trend up). The least-bad candidate is S1 10:00-12:00 where $\lambda=3.5/h$, $TH=4.0/h$ closest. Central $L_{S1}\approx6.5$, $W=J_1\approx(85+28)/60=1.88h$, $\lambda W\approx6.6$ — apparently consistent, but only because censored cases are few in that window and MISSING P15 central is used; using optimistic/pessimistic $W$ gives $6.0$-$7.2$, still overlapping $L$ bounds $[5.5,7.5]$ after rounding. For S2 12:00-14:00, $L\approx5$, $\lambda\approx4/h$, completed-only $W\approx(58+45)/60=1.72h$ gives $\lambda W\approx6.9>L$ (fails); including censored lower bounds raises $W$ further, worsening mismatch — correctly signalling violation of stationarity (queue exploding) and censoring bias, so Law is declared not defensible there. Lesson: dropping OPEN/waiting understates $L$ and $W$ in opposite directions; check only to demonstrate non-applicability in saturated tail.
