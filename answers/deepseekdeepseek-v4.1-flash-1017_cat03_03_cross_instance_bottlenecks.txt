## 1. Interaction-graph description

The relevant structure is a typed O2O/OCO graph, not a single case-notion process. Important node types are `Job` (J1–J6), `Component` (C1–C6), `ComponentLot` (L7, L8), `Machine` (CNC-2, OVEN-1, TEST-1), `Tool` (FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3), `Batch` (B1, B2), and `Shipment` (S1–S3). Event nodes such as `Batch Cure@12:05`, `Quarantine Lot@10:20`, `Release Lot@13:50`, and `Final Test` are also useful because they are where relations are observed.

Edge types/qualifiers include: `Job requires Component`; `ComponentLot contains Component`; `Batch batches Job`; `Shipment consolidates Job`; `Batch processed_on Machine with Tool`; `Job acquired/released Tool`; `Job processed_on Machine using Tool`; `ComponentLot quarantined/released affecting Component and Job`; and inferred `Job waits_for Batch/Tool/Shipment`. Direct observations include the relation tables, the B1 cure event relating B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J1, J2, J3, C1, C2, C3; FIX-A acquisition by J3 at 09:00 and release at 11:40; L7 quarantine at 10:20 and release at 13:50; CAL-3 recalibration 16:35–17:20; and packed timestamps. ## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

These are timing decompositions under explicit counterfactuals; they are not causal proofs.

1. **J1 B1 synchronization wait**: `12:05  08:10 = 3 h 55 min`. If J1 could have been cured immediately or in a smaller batch at 08:10 with OVEN/RACK available, up to 3 h 55 min is synchronization exposure. If B1 was waiting for J3 at 11:55, then J1’s wait-for-J3 component is `11:55  08:10 = 3 h 45 min`, plus 10 min to the recorded 12:05 start. Counterfactual assumes readiness is trusted and earlier OVEN/RACK availability existed.

2. **J2 B1 synchronization wait**: `12:05  08:25 = 3 h 40 min`. Under an immediate-batch counterfactual this is synchronization-induced waiting. J2 had a supervisor override and entered B1 before L7’s 13:50 release, so J2’s B1 wait is much more plausibly batch/tool coupling than lot-release gating. Counterfactual: no batch barrier and no earlier OVEN/RACK/tool block.

3. **J4 FIX-A contention exposure**: J4 was ready for CNC-2 at 09:50; FIX-A was held by J3 until 11:40; J4 started at 11:45. The overlap between J4 readiness and FIX-A unavailability is `11:40  09:50 = 1 h 50 min`. Because CNC-2 was idle 10:05–10:47, a conservative tool-specific exposure after CNC could have been free is `11:40  10:05 = 1 h 35 min`. J4 then started 5 min after FIX-A release. Counterfactual: FIX-A available when J4 reached CNC-2 and CNC scheduling otherwise unchanged. The actual delay is not provable because there is no `Wait for Tool` event and CNC-2 idle time is not fully attributable.

4. **J1 S1 shipment consolidation**: J1 packed at 13:30; S1 dispatched at 19:25, so `19:25  13:30 = 5 h 55 min` from J1 packed to shipment dispatch. If S1 were not waiting for J4 and followed an S3-like policy of dispatching 35 min after the last job packed, J1’s consolidation excess would be about `5 h 55  0 h 35 = 5 h 20 min`. If using the shipment-flattened median `Create Shipment  Load  Dispatch = 0.8 h + 0.3 h = 1.1 h`, the excess is `5 h 55  1 h 06  4 h 49 min`. Counterfactual: J1 could ship without J4 and transport capacity exists at that time.

5. **J6 CAL-3/TEST-1 delay**: J6 was ready at 16:05; J5 used TEST-1 until 16:35; CAL-3 was recalibrated 16:35–17:20; TEST-1 was idle until J6 started at 17:25. Compared with no recalibration after J5 and immediate testing, potential calibration-associated wait is `17:25  16:35 = 50 min`. Counterfactual: CAL-3 valid at 16:35 and TEST-1 available. This is strong because TEST-1 is explicitly idle during the recalibration.

6. **B2/RACK-7 and J4/J5 synchronization**: J4 ready 13:10; RACK-7 available after 14:35; J5 ready 14:40; B2 starts 14:50. J4’s total pre-batch wait is `14:50  13:10 = 1 h 40 min`. The RACK-related portion up to availability is `14:35  13:10 = 1 h 25 min`; after RACK availability, J4 waits 5 min for J5 and another 10 min to B2 start. Counterfactual: RACK available and batch could form earlier.

7. **L7 quarantine duration**: `13:50  10:20 = 3 h 30 min`. J5 did not proceed until after release, so if J5 were otherwise ready at 10:20, up to 3 h 30 min is lot-release exposure. J2’s override before release weakens a claim that L7 quarantined all associated jobs.

## 4. What flattening misses

- **Job viewpoint**: makes visible each job’s release, component staging, machine process, inspection, rework, packing, and dispatch. It hides batch readiness, tool acquisition/blocking, lot quarantine, and shipment-partner waiting. It duplicates job-level versions of batch/cure/shipment events or drops them entirely. Misleading conclusion: because no job has duration >1.8 SD above the six-job mean and `Pack  Dispatch` median is 1.1 h, J1 looks unexceptional even though it waited 5 h 55 min for S1.
- **Machine viewpoint**: makes visible CNC-2, OVEN-1, TEST-1 utilization and machine sequences. It hides which job is waiting for which tool, batch, or shipment. It can duplicate a single batch cure across J1/J2/J3 or collapse it into one machine event. Misleading conclusion: OVEN-1 at 44% and CNC-2 idle 10:05–10:47 suggest spare capacity, but a machine can be idle while jobs are blocked by RACK-7 quarantine or FIX-A.
- **Batch viewpoint**: makes visible synchronization start times and ready-vs-start waits. It hides why the batch started then, e.g. rack cleaning until 14:35 or OVEN availability. It may assign one cure timestamp to all jobs, hiding that J3 waited only 10 min while J1 waited 3 h 55 min. Misleading conclusion: batch utilization or OVEN utilization can look acceptable while early jobs wait a long time.
- **Tool viewpoint**: makes visible FIX-A acquire/release, RACK-7 cleaning/quarantine, and CAL-3 recalibration. It hides whether a job was truly blocked, because there is no explicit wait event. It may duplicate a tool hold across multiple related objects. Misleading conclusion: a busy tool is not automatically the bottleneck; FIX-A held by J3 09:00–11:40 only matters for J4 if J4 could otherwise run.
- **ComponentLot viewpoint**: makes visible L7/quarantine/release and component sharing. It hides inconsistent job linkage and supervisor overrides. It can duplicate the same lot event across J2/J5. Misleading conclusion: if integrated naively, L7 quarantine may be blamed for J2’s wait even though J2 had an override and entered B1 before release.
- **Shipment viewpoint**: makes visible create/load/dispatch and consolidation sets. It hides packed-to-load waiting for early jobs. It duplicates shipment events over J1/J4. Misleading conclusion: `Load  Dispatch` median 0.3 h suggests fast shipment, but J1 waited 5 h 55 min after packing before S1 dispatched.

Low machine utilization coexists with long job waiting because the machines are not the only gating objects. OVEN-1 can be idle while RACK-7 is quarantined; TEST-1 can be idle while CAL-3 is recalibrated; CNC-2 can be idle while FIX-A is held by another job. Waiting is generated by synchronization and eligibility constraints, not only by machine saturation.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**Narrative A: J1/J2/J3 through B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, and shipment.**

Downstream, J1 later waits for S1, while J2/J3 belong to S2, showing that one batch motif can feed different shipment motifs.

**Narrative B: J3/J4 through FIX-A, CNC-2, and C4.**

**Narrative C: J5/J6 through TEST-1, CAL-3, and final test.**

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

1. **Reduce batch synchronization with rolling/max-wait batching.** Target: B1/B2 batch synchronization. Expected benefit: lower J1/J2-style ready-to-batch waits. Helped: J1, J2, early-ready batch jobs. Potentially harmed: OVEN-1 utilization and energy efficiency if batches become smaller. New risk: more setup/load-unload events. Perverse metric: “average batch size” could encourage holding jobs longer. Object-centric evaluation: compare `ready_for_batch  batch_start` per job against batch size, OVEN-1 utilization, and downstream shipment timing in OCEL.

2. **Tool-aware scheduling and reservation for FIX-A/CNC-2.** Target: shared-tool contention. Expected benefit: reduce J4-style waits when FIX-A is held by another job. Helped: J4, CNC-2 flow. Harmed: J3 if preempted or delayed. New risk: scheduling complexity, setup loss, priority disputes. Perverse metric: “FIX-A utilization” could reward hoarding tools. Evaluation: reconstruct tool acquire/release intervals and link each job’s machine start to its tool wait; compare makespan with/without tool reservation.

3. **Move or stagger CAL-3 recalibration.** Target: calibration-induced TEST-1 idling. Expected benefit: avoid the 50 min J6 wait. Helped: J6 and later test jobs. Harmed: maintenance windows or calibration compliance if moved. New risk: invalid tests if calibration is not actually sufficient. Perverse metric: “calibrations per shift” could encourage skipping required calibration. Evaluation: object-centric `ready_for_test  test_start` decomposed by CAL-3 availability, with TEST-1 idle only as supporting evidence, not as a stand-alone wait measure.

4. **Revise shipment consolidation policy for S1-type shipments.** Target: shipment consolidation. Expected benefit: reduce J1’s 5 h 55 min packed-to-dispatch wait. Helped: early-packed jobs like J1. Harmed: J4 or logistics if splitting S1 raises freight cost. New risk: more shipments, lower consolidation efficiency. Potentially harmed: quality control if integration changes release decisions; not necessarily operations. New risk: logging overhead and inconsistent source data. Perverse metric: “lot release speed” could incentivize bypassing quarantine. 6. **Add explicit wait-state events and tool/shipment lifecycle logs.** Target: data-integration artifacts. Expected benefit: convert inferred waits into observable `WaitingForBatch`, `WaitingForTool`, `WaitingForShipment`, `QueueForCalibration` events. Helped: all objects and analyses. Harmed: none directly, but staff time. New risk: event spam or perfunctory logging. Perverse metric: “number of wait events” could reward noise. Evaluation: compare object-centric interaction summaries, flattened performance DFGs, and OCDFG with and without wait events; validate waits against timestamps and object identifiers.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

Observations that would weaken the top-ranked batch-synchronization explanation:

1. OVEN-1 or RACK-7 was actually busy or unavailable from 08:10 to 12:05; then J1/J2’s wait is machine/rack availability, not batch synchronization waiting for J3.
2. C1/C2 were not truly available at J1/J2 readiness times; then 08:10/08:25 timestamps are not meaningful readiness and batch-wait calculations overstate avoidable synchronization.
3. B1’s 12:05 start was a fixed schedule independent of J3 readiness, and earlier batch starts were prohibited by policy for reasons unrelated to synchronization.
4. J1’s and J2’s later inspection, rework, or shipment waits dominate their end-to-end duration, making B1 wait operationally secondary even if real.
5. Logs show J3 was deliberately delayed to 11:55 but B1 could have run earlier without J3; then the batch policy is not the barrier.
6. J4’s FIX-A wait is proven negligible or unrelated by a `Wait for Tool` event showing J4 waited on CNC-2 rather than FIX-A.
7. J5 proceeds before L7 release without an override, weakening lot-release coupling; or J2’s override is found to be a logging error.
8. CAL-3 recalibration after J5 is mandatory and cannot be moved without invalidating tests, weakening the calibration intervention even though the delay is real.

Additional fields or event types that would discriminate among hypotheses:

1. Explicit `Wait for Tool`, `Request Tool`, `Acquire Tool`, and `Release Tool` events linked to job, tool, and machine IDs.
2. Batch policy fields: per-job `Ready for Batch`, `Batch Candidate`, `Batch Start`, `Max Wait Threshold`, and batch-formation decision reason.
3. OVEN-1 and RACK-7 availability/cleaning/quarantine intervals with reason codes and authoritative source system.
4. Supervisor override records: timestamp, scope, approver, job, component lot, and reason for J2’s pre-release entry into B1.
5. Shipment policy and transport schedule: consolidation cutoff, planned departure, packed-to-load timestamp per job, and whether splitting is allowed.
6. CAL-3 calibration rules: required calibration after each test, allowed deferral, validity window, and technician availability.
7. Component-lot genealogy with confidence/source-system qualifiers and alternative job-lot integration links for L7/C2/C5.
8. Queue-state events such as `WaitingForBatch`, `WaitingForTool`, `WaitingForCalibration`, `WaitingForShipment`, plus priority/queue position.
9. Data-quality metadata: extraction pipeline, event source, late-arriving events, duplicate relations, and whether events were directly linked to Job at source.
