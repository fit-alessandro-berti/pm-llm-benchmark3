# 1. Mechanism map
The restructuring actions are mapped to expected processmining effects as follows:

| Restructuring Action | Expected ProcessMining Effect |
|----------------------|--------------------------------|
| Merge local technical specialists (N_Tech, S_Tech, E_Tech) into a **central Technical Shared Service** | **Central queue formation**  increased handovers to a single service node, higher waiting times for that node; **nominal handover reduction** for localtospecialist loops (36  41 cases/100) but shifted to the central hub; **specialist utilization** rises (~+15%) as specialists handle more cases from all teams; **local expertise loss** as early informal advice is removed; **role clarity** may suffer because local caseworkers now depend on a remote service; **resilience to absence** decreases for the central hub (a larger single point of failure) but improves for individual local teams (less need for intrateam backup); **fairness** could worsen for teams with weaker relationships to the central queue (e.g., South) if queue timing is inconsistent. |
| Centralize Specialist Review, Legal, Risk, and difficult Quality Checks in the same sharedservice queue | **Knowledge bottleneck** – highcomplexity or specialtyrich cases funnel through one queue  **queue/ waitingtime pattern** shift: median wait from 1.6h (local loop) to ~2–4h (central queue); **rework** may increase if cases are misrouted or if the central queue lacks contextual nuance  **rework rate** could rise for highcomplexity or regional cases; **learning** diminishes as local staff no longer see specialist reasoning on their own cases; **fairness** issues arise if some teams (e.g., East) receive faster service due to overlapping resource usage. |
| Keep Intake and simple Eligibility Review local; introduce routing rules (highcomplexity  Technical Shared Service; legal/risk  Specialist Review queue) | **Handover reduction** for localtolocal loops (e.g., local caseworker  local supervisor)  expected median wait shortens slightly (2.4h  3.0h) but **shared approval queue** introduces new handover (local caseworker  shared approval) with wait 3.0h; **role clarity** is partially clarified by standardized approval process but ambiguous ownership of returned specialist advice; **resilience** improves for routine intake but weakens for specialistheavy cases. |
| Remove one local supervisor; create shared approval queue for medium/highvalue cases | **Role clarity** becomes murkier – supervisors now function via a queue, potentially diluting decisionmaking authority; **fairness** may improve for highvalue cases (standardized review) but could disadvantage teams lacking direct supervisor input. |
| Expected specialist utilization increase (15%) | **Observed in** PM4Py **activitybased resource similarity** (higher overlap of specialist activity timestamps across cases) and **organizationalrole discovery** (fewer distinct specialist roles per team). |

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time
| Risk | Why it hides behind average cycle time | Processmining observability |
|------|---------------------------------------|------------------------------|
| **Tail latency for highcomplexity cases** | Average hides extreme wait times that affect a minority but crucial cases. | **95thpercentile wait** in central queue (`pm4py.get_service_time` with `quantile=0.95`). |
| **Local knowledge loss** | Uniform reduction in average time may mask loss of nuanced regional decision making. | **Local expertise flag** frequency (`pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values`). |
| **Fairness across teams/customer segments** | Global average ignores distributional shifts (e.g., North vs East). | **Teamlevel cycletime distribution** (`pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe` grouped by `team`). |
| **Learning & skill erosion** | Average throughput does not capture staff skill decay; learning curves flatten. | **Skillusage similarity** (`pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity`) for specialist roles. |
| **Accountability opacity** | Standardized queues may appear efficient but obscure who ultimately approves. | **Workingtogether network** (`pm4py.discover_working_together_network`) shows reduced cooccurrence of local supervisors with caseworkers. |
| **Exception handling robustness** | System may handle routine work faster, while rare exceptions (e.g., legal/risk spikes) remain unmeasured. | **Rework cases per activity** (`pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity`) and **highvolume outlier cases** (`pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values` on `priority`). |

# 5. Pilot design
```python
# Pilot design sketch (highlevel pseudocode)

pilot_scope = {
    "teams": ["North", "South", "East"],   # 3 out of 4
    "complexity_ranges": ["low", "medium", "high"],   # segment via `case_complexity`
    "specialty_segments": ["technical", "legal", "risk", "general"],   # via `required_specialty`
    "customer_segments": ["regional", "national"]   # via `customer_segment`
}

pre_observation_window = 4_weeks   # collect baseline logs
post_observation_window = 8_weeks  # after restructuring rollout

# Define guardrail metrics (to be monitored weekly)
guardrails = {
    "central_queue_wait_95": lambda log: pm4py.get_service_time(log, "Technical Shared Service", quantile=0.95) < 5.5,   # hours
    "local_expertise_flag_rate": lambda log: log["local_expertise_flag"].value_counts()["high"] / len(log) > 0.45,   # >45% high expertise
    "rework_rate": lambda log: pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(log)["all_activities"]["Rework"] < 0.09,
    "fairness_metric": lambda log: (log.groupby("team")["case_duration"].mean()).max() / (log.groupby("team")["case_duration"].mean()).min() < 1.2,
    "learning_signal": lambda log: len(set(log["required_specialty"])) == 4,   # all specialties still observed
}

# Data collection pipeline
for team in pilot_scope["teams"]:
    for complexity in pilot_scope["complexity_ranges"]:
        for specialty in pilot_scope["specialty_segments"]:
            variant_filter = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
                log,
                "team", values=[team],
                "case_complexity", values=[complexity],
                "required_specialty", values=[specialty]
            )
            extract_temporal_features(variant_filter, output_path=f"pilot_{team}_{complexity}_{specialty}.csv")

# Staff feedback collection
feedback_form = {
    "role_clarity": {"prompt": "How clear is your decisionmaking ownership now?", "scale": 1-5},
    "speed_of_advice": {"prompt": "How quickly do you receive specialist advice?", "scale": 1-5},
    "fairness": {"prompt": "Do you feel cases are treated fairly across teams?", "scale": 1-5}
}
collect_weekly_surveys(feedback_form)

# Rollback / adjustment triggers
if not all(guardrails[m](current_log) for m in guardrails):
    trigger_rollback_arm = True
    if "central_queue_wait_95" in [m for m in guardrails if not guardrails[m](current_log)]:
        adjust_routing_rules()
```
**Key points of the pilot**

- **Scope:** Include all three teams, all case complexities, and all required specialties.  
- **Control group:** A fourth team (excluded, e.g., “West”) continues unchanged, serving as a baseline.  
- **Observation windows:** 4weeks prechange to capture baseline; 8weeks postchange to assess stabilization.  
- **Segmentation:** Allows detection of differential impacts (e.g., East may show early benefit).  
- **Guardrail metrics:** Monitor central queue wait times at 95th percentile, local expertise flag rates, rework, fairness across teams, and learning signals (specialty coverage).  
- **Feedback:** Weekly staff surveys tied to role clarity, speed, and fairness.  
- **Rollback triggers:** Immediate rollback if any guardrail fails; otherwise, phasein additional supervisors or parallel local backup after week6.

# 6. Recommendation
**Conditional recommendation: Pilot first, then consider phased rollout.**  

- **Proceed with a **piloted, phased rollout** if the guardrail thresholds are met in the first month (central queue 95thpercentile wait 5.5h, local expertise flag 45%, rework 9%, fairness ratio 1.2, and all specialties observed).  
- **Modify** the routing rules before full rollout if early pilot data show emerging bottlenecks (e.g., legal/risk queue exceeds 5h).  
- **Do not infer** from early average cycletime improvements that all dimensions (fairness, learning, resilience) have improved; continue to monitor tail latencies and fairness metrics.  
- **Reject** a fullscale rollout without pilot evidence of handling highcomplexity cases and preserving local expertise.

Management should view the average cycletime as a **leading indicator only**; the **processmining indicators** (queue waits, rework, fairness, skill utilization) will reveal the hidden dimensions that could otherwise remain invisible.

**Explanation of the sketch**

- **Handover and subcontracting networks** reveal the new central bottleneck and reduction of local handovers.  
- **Workingtogether network** quantifies loss of local coaction (supervisor  caseworker) after the sharedservice introduction.  
- **Performance DFG** with filtered activity highlights the median waiting time inside the Technical Shared Service.  
- **Service/ waitingtime** extraction provides the core metric for tail latency.  
- **Resource similarity & role discovery** track specialist coverage and emergence of a unified role hierarchy.  
- **Segmentation filters** enable comparison across teams, complexity, specialty, and customer segment, directly feeding the leading and lagging indicators defined earlier.  
- **Rework and SLA** calculations confirm downstream quality impacts.

By running these analyses before and after the pilot, management can empirically validate the **mechanism map** and pivot based on concrete processmining evidence rather than relying solely on projected averages.
