## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The raw/mapped gap (0.35–0.40 in every month from March) shows that most of the conformance loss is explained by two known provenance changes: the `Eligibility Screen` label and duplicated `Open Case` events. Mapping recovers 85–96% of the loss, and in March–April the residual is 0.01–0.02, so the migration produced essentially no footprint change. What the gap does not imply is that the mapped series is "true": the mapping encodes an assumption that the renamed activity is semantically identical, that duplicate `Open Case` is purely technical, and that the old normative model is still the right reference. A mapping recovers conformance by construction; it cannot show that the screening procedure, its decision logic, or its resource behaviour stayed the same. Only equal position in the DFG, unchanged throughput (Register->Eligibility->CollectDocs at 100/98/94 per 100 cases) and unchanged cycle time in March make that reading plausible.

Duplicated technical events and missing resources hurt different analyses differently. Duplicates inflate event counts, event-frequency DFGs and alignment costs but barely affect case-level variants once removed; missing resources leave control-flow intact but break hand-over networks, resource-based segmentation and workload attribution, and because missingness is concentrated in migrated activities and not random by team, resource analyses after March are biased, not merely noisier.

The post-May pattern cannot be logging drift because the residual mapped gap grows (0.045–0.065) exactly when rework rises, while all logging indicators are flat (11 labels, 5–6% duplicates, 5.5–6.5% missing). The rework variant share from operational tables and the Assess->RequestInfo rate from the normalized DFG agree to the percentage point in every month, two independent sources. Cycle times changed while label semantics did not.

### 3.2 Normalized DFG-distance analysis

Measure: L1 distance between per-100-case relation rates over the seven supplied edges, relative to January; also total variation (TV) distance after turning each month's rates into an edge-share distribution. L1 is decomposed by edge group (main path: Register->Eligibility, Eligibility->CollectDocs, CollectDocs->Assess, Decision; rework: Assess->RequestInfo, RequestInfo->CollectDocs; escalation: Assess->SeniorAssessment). Assumption on omitted edges: they are treated as equal across months, so every distance is a lower bound; an unexplained Assess inflow imbalance of 6–12 per 100 cases from May (outflow RequestInfo + Decision exceeds CollectDocs->Assess inflow, whereas January and March balance) indicates omitted edges such as Assess->Assess reassignment, SeniorAssessment->Assess return, or Eligibility->Assess skips, or that the "occurrences" are actually case-coverage rates. Under the case-coverage reading, the 4–6 pp fall in Eligibility->CollectDocs and CollectDocs->Assess means 9–11% of cases never reach Assess within the extraction window (stalled in document collection or terminated), which is itself a supplier-consistent signal.

### 3.3 Cycle-time deterioration decomposition

Qualitative shares, justified from aggregates only. Rework moves the upper quantiles far more than the median because with <30% rework the median case is still a non-rework case; queueing and document waiting affect every case and therefore the median; service time is bounded by the observed 4.8→5.4 h per Assess pass.

| window | total deterioration | case mix | control-flow / rework | service | queueing (incl. waiting for documents) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jul–Aug vs baseline | median +13.5 h; p90 +39 h | HC +8 pp, Z share ×2.7 with more complex cases: median 5–15% (~1–2 h), p90 10–15% | rework +16 pp: median 10–20% (1.5–3 h), p90 30–45% (12–18 h) | +0.6 h × ~1.3 passes ≈ 0.8 h: median 5–8%, p90 ~3% | remainder: median 55–75% (7–10 h), p90 40–50%; consistent with reconstructed queue observation and a 1.5× demand index against reduced capacity |
| Oct–Nov vs baseline | median +17.3 h; p90 +50 h | HC +22 pp, volume +27%, SA path adds a stage: median 20–30%, p90 20–30% | rework +15 pp: median 10–15%, p90 25–35% | ~5% (Assess service not reported after August; SA service unknown) | median 45–60%, p90 35–45%; demand index ≈1.7× |
| Dec vs Nov | median −6.7 h; p90 −22 h | HC −11 pp and volume −13% predict most of the fall | rework −8 pp (campaign or seasonal ebb) | overtime raises capacity, not service speed | cannot be split from season without segment-level difference-in-differences |

Uncertainty: if the monthly cycle-time figures only include closed cases, high-rework months are right-censored and the true deterioration is understated; if they are indexed by closing month, October–November inherit long cases from the summer. The shares above should be read as ordering (queueing > rework > case mix > service for the median; rework ≈ queueing > case mix > service for p90), not as point estimates.

### 3.4 Candidate change points

- Logging drift: 2026-03 (label rename, duplicates, missingness — sudden step, standardised mean shift 8–13 in the single-split rule); 2026-05 (supplier_z_pct doubling — possible metric-definition change rather than population); 2026-07 (high-complexity flag becomes operationally consequential under the priority rule, possibly changing how it is recorded; missingness peak 6.5%); 2026-10 (Senior Assessment recorded at 22/100 for a historically inconsistent label).
- Behavioural drift: 2026-05 (rework 13→24%, Assess->RequestInfo 12→24; best single split for rework, fast variant and Z share); 2026-07 (rework peak 29%, priority ordering change); 2026-09 (partial reversion to 20%); 2026-10 (escalation 9→22/100, SA/HC ratio doubles); 2026-12 (rework 29→21% with the document-quality campaign).
- Performance drift: 2026-05 (median +14% month on month, first move); 2026-06 (best single split for median and p90; staffing cut mid-month); 2026-10 (median +6.3 h, p90 +20 h, seasonal load); 2026-12 (reversal).

### 3.5 One global change point or several

The data do not support a single global change point. The logging series step in March and then stay flat; the behavioural series step in May, partially revert in September and change composition in October; the performance series ramp gradually from May with the steepest single split in June, and the case-mix series step in October. Fitting one change point (any method would place it around May–June for a pooled score) would misattribute the March semantic break to behaviour and would hide the October population shift. Mechanism-specific change points aligned with the timeline (migration, supplier, staffing, priority rule, season, interventions) are the only reading consistent with all artifacts simultaneously.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 whole mapped log | month (or ISO week) | Trend and headline monitoring; reconcile with supplied aggregates | none (≥600 cases/month) | Global drift, change-point candidates | Overaggregation hides priority redistribution and supplier effects |
| L1 regime windows | Jan–Feb, Mar–Apr, May–Jun 14, Jun 15–Aug, Sep, Oct–Nov, Dec | Align artifacts with known events; before/after tests | ≥150 cases per window | Mechanism-specific before/after | Windows chosen from the timeline; risk of confirming the story |
| L2 supplier × regime | Supplier Z / other | Isolate supplier mechanism | ≥80 cases per cell per month, else pool to bi-monthly | Z vs non-Z rework, waiting, stall rates | Z serves specific regions and more complex customers: supplier is a proxy for region and complexity |
| L3 complexity × supplier × regime | HC flag (as recorded, with flag-stability check) | Separate case-mix from behaviour; support priority analysis | ≥50 cases per cell; merge HC-Z cells into quarters if below | SA/HC ratio, per-class waiting, rework within class | HC flag semantics may have changed in July; conditioning on an unstable variable |
| L4 priority class × complexity (post-July) | priority queue class, HC flag | Redistribution of delay | ≥50 cases per class per month | Standard vs HC waiting before Assess | Class is deterministic in HC after July, so class and complexity are collinear |
| L5 team / resource | team, resource where present | Staffing effect, hand-overs | ≥30 Assess events per resource per month; drop cells with >15% missing resource | Service time per resource, workload, hand-over edges | Missingness is non-random; cases with recorded resources are a biased sample |
| L6 region | region | Check whether Z effect is regional | ≥80 cases per region per month | Region vs supplier disentangling | Region and supplier overlap heavily |

Primary monitoring population: the mapped-label, closed-case log stratified into two panels — standard-complexity and high-complexity — with non-Z standard-complexity cases as the control cell. Reason: the priority rule and the seasonal case mix both act through complexity, so any unstratified aggregate mixes redistribution with drift; the non-Z standard cell is the population least exposed to the two identified shocks (supplier and season), so movement there is the cleanest indicator of capacity or behavioural change. Supplier, team and region segments are diagnostic layers, not primary monitors, because their cells are too small monthly and their attributes are of uncertain quality.

| M7 service time | Median and p90 Assess and Senior Assessment service (start–complete) per resource and class | Rolling 12 weeks | Median shift >15% or per-resource outliers >2 MAD | Case complexity within class, new-staff learning curve | Lifecycle logging changes | Team leads |
| M8 case mix | HC share, Z share (case-level), volume, region mix; flag stability over case lifetime | Same weeks previous year ± 2 weeks, plus rolling | Outside seasonal envelope (min–max of prior years widened by 10%) for 2 weeks | Distinguish recurrence (inside envelope) from new drift (outside); check flag semantics | Renewal season if envelope missing; metric redefinition | Planning / demand management |
| M9 resource completeness | Missing org:resource share by activity, team and system component | Post-migration baseline (4–5%) with target 1% | Any activity >8% missing or team gap >5 pp; monthly downward trend check | Interface logs, mapping tables | New activities without resource field | Process data engineering |
| M10 conformance (raw and mapped) | Alignment fitness and precision against baseline model, raw and mapped, plus residual gap | Baseline model from stable regime, refreshed after validated process changes | Mapped fitness down >0.03 vs reference, or raw-mapped gap changes >0.05 (new semantic break) | Alignment move types by activity | Model staleness, legitimate variants | Process owner |
| M11 escalation | Assess->SeniorAssessment coverage and SA/HC ratio by team | Rolling 8 weeks; seasonal | SA/HC ratio > 1.5× reference for 2 weeks | Escalation reasons; whether SA is being recorded where it was not before | Recording improvement of a historically inconsistent label | Assessment lead |
| M12 outcomes | Approve/Reject ratio, share closed without decision, reopen rate, by class and supplier | Rolling 12 weeks; seasonal | Reject or no-decision share moves >5 pp in any cell | Link to document completeness and rework | Right-censoring of open cases | Process owner |

Thresholds are metric- and segment-specific (percentile of the reference distribution or MAD-based bands, never one universal cut-off). Seasonality: every case-mix and performance monitor carries two references, a rolling window for new drift and a same-period-previous-year envelope for recurrence; an alert is labelled "recurrence" when the rolling reference is breached but the seasonal envelope is not, and "new drift" when both are breached. Multiple testing: with ~12 monitors × several segments per week, control the false discovery rate with Benjamini–Hochberg at 10% across the weekly family, require persistence over two consecutive windows for performance monitors, and organise monitors hierarchically so that M1/M2/M9 (logging) breaches suppress or annotate downstream conformance and DFG alerts until labels are reconciled.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 1. validation
def validate(monthly: pd.DataFrame, rates: pd.DataFrame) -> list:
    problems = []
    pct_cols = [c for c in monthly.columns if c.endswith("_pct")]
    for c in pct_cols:
        if not monthly[c].between(0, 100).all():
            problems.append(f"{c} outside [0,100]")
    for c in ["raw_model_fitness", "mapped_model_fitness"]:
        if not monthly[c].between(0, 1).all():
            problems.append(f"{c} outside [0,1]")
    if (monthly["mapped_model_fitness"] < monthly["raw_model_fitness"]).any():
        problems.append("mapped fitness below raw fitness (mapping should not hurt)")
    if (monthly["p90_cycle_h"] < monthly["median_cycle_h"]).any():
        problems.append("p90 below median")
    if ((monthly["fast_variant_pct"] + monthly["rework_variant_pct"]) > 100).any():
        problems.append("variant shares exceed 100")
    if not monthly.index.is_monotonic_increasing:
        problems.append("months not sorted")
    if monthly.isna().any().any() or rates.isna().any().any():
        problems.append("missing values present")
    if not (rates["Register->Eligibility"] == 100).all():
        problems.append("Register->Eligibility rate is not 100 in every month")
    # cross-source consistency: rework variant share vs Assess->RequestInfo rate
    common = rates.index.intersection(monthly.index)
    diff = (monthly.loc[common, "rework_variant_pct"] - rates.loc[common, "Assess->RequestInfo"]).abs()
    if (diff > 2).any():
        problems.append("rework_variant_pct and Assess->RequestInfo disagree by >2pp")
    # flow check at Assess: inflow CollectDocs->Assess vs outflow (RequestInfo + Decision)
    # ('Decision' already includes SeniorAssessment->Decision, so Assess->SeniorAssessment is not added twice)
    inflow = rates["CollectDocs->Assess"]
    outflow = rates["Assess->RequestInfo"] + rates["Decision"]
    imbalance = (outflow - inflow)
    for m, v in imbalance.items():
        if abs(v) > 3:
            problems.append(f"{m}: Assess outflow exceeds CollectDocs->Assess inflow by {v} per 100 cases "
                            f"(omitted edges or case-coverage semantics)")
    return problems


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 2. baseline-relative changes
def baseline_relative(monthly: pd.DataFrame, baseline_months=BASELINE_MONTHS) -> pd.DataFrame:
    base = monthly.loc[baseline_months].mean()
    abs_change = monthly - base
    pct_change = (monthly - base) / base.replace(0, np.nan) * 100
    out = pd.concat({"abs": abs_change, "pct": pct_change}, axis=1)
    return out.round(2)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 3. DFG-fragment distances
def dfg_distances(rates: pd.DataFrame, ref_month="2026-01") -> pd.DataFrame:
    """L1 on per-100-case rates, relative L1, total-variation on edge-share distribution,
    and L1 decomposed into edge groups. Omitted edges are assumed to be absent from every
    month equally; the distance is therefore a lower bound on the true DFG distance."""
    ref = rates.loc[ref_month]
    rows = []
    for m, row in rates.iterrows():
        d = (row - ref).abs()
        l1 = d.sum()
        p, q = row / row.sum(), ref / ref.sum()
        tv = 0.5 * (p - q).abs().sum()
        rec = {"month": m, "L1_per100": l1, "L1_relative": l1 / ref.sum(), "total_variation": tv}
        for g, cols in EDGE_GROUPS.items():
            rec[f"L1_{g}"] = d[cols].sum()
        rows.append(rec)
    return pd.DataFrame(rows).set_index("month").round(3)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 4. raw-to-mapped fitness recovery
def fitness_recovery(monthly: pd.DataFrame, baseline_months=BASELINE_MONTHS) -> pd.DataFrame:
    base = monthly.loc[baseline_months, "mapped_model_fitness"].mean()
    raw, mapped = monthly["raw_model_fitness"], monthly["mapped_model_fitness"]
    gap = mapped - raw
    lost = base - raw
    recovered_share = (gap / lost.where(lost > 0.02)).clip(upper=1.0)  # undefined when nothing was lost
    residual = base - mapped
    return pd.DataFrame({
        "raw": raw, "mapped": mapped, "raw_mapped_gap": gap,
        "recovered_share_of_loss": recovered_share.round(3),
        "residual_vs_baseline": residual.round(3),
    })


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 5. candidate change points (explainable rules)
MOM_THRESHOLDS = {  # metric-specific month-over-month jump thresholds (absolute units)
    "raw_model_fitness": 0.10, "mapped_model_fitness": 0.03, "distinct_activity_labels": 1,
    "missing_resource_pct": 2.0, "duplicate_open_case_pct": 2.0,
    "rework_variant_pct": 5, "fast_variant_pct": 5, "supplier_z_pct": 8, "high_complexity_pct": 6,
    "median_cycle_h": 3.0, "p90_cycle_h": 8.0, "cases": 60,
}


def mom_jumps(monthly: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    rows = []
    for c, thr in MOM_THRESHOLDS.items():
        diff = monthly[c].diff()
        for m, v in diff.items():
            if pd.notna(v) and abs(v) >= thr:
                rows.append({"metric": c, "month": m, "mom_change": round(v, 3), "threshold": thr})
    return pd.DataFrame(rows).sort_values(["month", "metric"]).reset_index(drop=True)


def best_single_split(series: pd.Series, min_seg=2) -> dict:
    """Binary segmentation with one split: month whose before/after mean difference,
    standardised by the pooled within-segment std, is largest."""
    x = series.values.astype(float)
    best = None
    for k in range(min_seg, len(x) - min_seg + 1):
        a, b = x[:k], x[k:]
        pooled = np.sqrt((a.var(ddof=1) * (len(a) - 1) + b.var(ddof=1) * (len(b) - 1)) / (len(x) - 2))
        pooled = max(pooled, 1e-9)
        score = abs(b.mean() - a.mean()) / pooled
        if best is None or score > best["score"]:
            best = {"split_month": series.index[k], "score": round(score, 2),
                    "mean_before": round(a.mean(), 2), "mean_after": round(b.mean(), 2)}
    return best


def change_points(monthly: pd.DataFrame, min_score=1.5) -> pd.DataFrame:
    rows = []
    for c in MOM_THRESHOLDS:
        s = best_single_split(monthly[c])
        s["metric"] = c
        s["flag"] = s["score"] >= min_score
        rows.append(s)
    return pd.DataFrame(rows).set_index("metric")

# PART B - reusable functions for a future event-level dataframe (PM4Py)

# Expected raw columns: case_id, activity, timestamp, optional resource, complexity,
# supplier, region, priority_class, team, lifecycle. Nothing below overwrites the raw log.
from typing import Dict, Iterable, Optional, Tuple

try:
    import pm4py
except ImportError:  # Part A stays executable without PM4Py
    pm4py = None

LABEL_MAP = {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"}
TECHNICAL_EVENTS = ("Open Case",)
CASE, ACT, TS, RES = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp", "org:resource"


def prepare_event_log(df: pd.DataFrame, case_col: str, act_col: str, ts_col: str,
                      start_ts_col: Optional[str] = None) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Standardise column names with pm4py.format_dataframe and keep the raw label."""
    kwargs = dict(case_id=case_col, activity_key=act_col, timestamp_key=ts_col)
    if start_ts_col:
        kwargs["start_timestamp_key"] = start_ts_col
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(df.copy(), **kwargs)
    log["concept:name_raw"] = log[ACT]
    return log.sort_values([CASE, TS]).reset_index(drop=True)


def normalize_labels(log: pd.DataFrame, mapping: Dict[str, str] = LABEL_MAP,
                     technical: Iterable[str] = TECHNICAL_EVENTS,
                     drop_all_technical: bool = False) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Return a NEW dataframe with mapped labels and duplicated technical events removed.
    The input log is not modified; 'concept:name_raw' keeps the original label."""
    out = log.copy()
    out[ACT] = out[ACT].replace(mapping)
    is_tech = out[ACT].isin(list(technical))
    if drop_all_technical:
        out = out[~is_tech]
    else:
        dup = out.duplicated(subset=[CASE, ACT], keep="first") & is_tech
        out = out[~dup]
    return out.reset_index(drop=True)


def slice_window(log: pd.DataFrame, start: str, end: str,
                 mode: str = "traces_contained") -> pd.DataFrame:
    """mode: 'events' | 'traces_contained' | 'traces_intersecting' (PM4Py semantics)."""
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(log, start, end, mode=mode)


def month_bounds(log: pd.DataFrame) -> Iterable[Tuple[str, str, str]]:
    months = pd.period_range(log[TS].min(), log[TS].max(), freq="M")
    for p in months:
        yield (str(p), p.start_time.strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S"),
               p.end_time.strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S"))


def monthly_dfgs(log: pd.DataFrame, mode: str = "traces_contained") -> Dict[str, tuple]:
    """month -> (dfg, start_activities, end_activities) from pm4py.discover_dfg (event frequency)."""
    out = {}
    for m, s, e in month_bounds(log):
        w = slice_window(log, s, e, mode)
        if len(w):
            out[m] = pm4py.discover_dfg(w)
    return out


def dfg_case_coverage(log: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.Series:
    """Share of cases (per 100) containing each directly-follows pair; comparable to the supplied rates."""
    df = log.sort_values([CASE, TS])
    nxt = df.groupby(CASE)[ACT].shift(-1)
    pairs = df.assign(nxt=nxt).dropna(subset=["nxt"])
    n_cases = df[CASE].nunique()
    cov = pairs.drop_duplicates([CASE, ACT, "nxt"]).groupby([ACT, "nxt"]).size()
    return (cov / n_cases * 100).rename("per_100_cases")


def dfg_rate_distance(a: pd.Series, b: pd.Series) -> Dict[str, float]:
    """L1 and total variation over the union of edges (missing edges treated as 0)."""
    idx = a.index.union(b.index)
    a2, b2 = a.reindex(idx, fill_value=0.0), b.reindex(idx, fill_value=0.0)
    l1 = float((a2 - b2).abs().sum())
    tv = float(0.5 * ((a2 / a2.sum()) - (b2 / b2.sum())).abs().sum())
    return {"L1_per100": l1, "total_variation": tv}


def monthly_variants(log: pd.DataFrame, mode: str = "traces_contained") -> Dict[str, pd.Series]:
    """month -> variant share (variants as activity tuples; handles str or tuple keys)."""
    out = {}
    for m, s, e in month_bounds(log):
        w = slice_window(log, s, e, mode)
        if not len(w):
            continue
        v = pm4py.get_variants(w)
        keys = [k if isinstance(k, tuple) else tuple(k.split(",")) for k in v.keys()]
        counts = pd.Series([len(x) if hasattr(x, "__len__") and not isinstance(x, str) else x for x in v.values()],
                           index=keys, dtype=float)
        out[m] = counts / counts.sum()
    return out


def rework_share(log: pd.DataFrame, activity: str = "Assess") -> float:
    counts = log[log[ACT] == activity].groupby(CASE).size()
    return float((counts > 1).sum() / log[CASE].nunique())


def monthly_temporal_profiles(log: pd.DataFrame, mode: str = "traces_contained") -> Dict[str, dict]:
    """month -> {(a,b): (mean_seconds, std_seconds)} via pm4py.discover_temporal_profile."""
    return {m: pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(slice_window(log, s, e, mode))
            for m, s, e in month_bounds(log)}


def temporal_deviations(window: pd.DataFrame, reference_profile: dict, zeta: float = 2.0):
    """Deviations of a window against a reference profile (list per trace)."""
    return pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile(window, reference_profile, zeta=zeta)


def discover_constraints(log: pd.DataFrame, noise_threshold: float = 0.05, use_declare: bool = True) -> dict:
    """Optional: log skeleton and DECLARE model of a (baseline) window for constraint-level drift checks."""
    out = {"log_skeleton": pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(log, noise_threshold=noise_threshold)}
    if use_declare and hasattr(pm4py, "discover_declare"):
        out["declare"] = pm4py.discover_declare(log, min_support_ratio=0.3, min_confidence_ratio=0.8)
    return out


def constraint_conformance(window: pd.DataFrame, models: dict) -> dict:
    out = {"log_skeleton": pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton(window, models["log_skeleton"])}
    if "declare" in models and hasattr(pm4py, "conformance_declare"):
        out["declare"] = pm4py.conformance_declare(window, models["declare"])
    return out


def waiting_before(log: pd.DataFrame, activity: str = "Assess") -> pd.Series:
    """Gap between an activity's timestamp and its predecessor in the case (hours).
    Without lifecycle start/complete this is waiting + predecessor service; use start_timestamp when available."""
    df = log.sort_values([CASE, TS])
    prev_ts = df.groupby(CASE)[TS].shift(1)
    gap = (df[TS] - prev_ts).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0
    return gap[df[ACT] == activity]


def compare_raw_vs_mapped(raw_log: pd.DataFrame, baseline_start: str, baseline_end: str,
                          eval_start: str, eval_end: str, mapping: Dict[str, str] = LABEL_MAP) -> dict:
    """Fitness of a window under raw and normalised labels against a model mined from the
    normalised baseline. raw_log is never modified."""
    mapped_log = normalize_labels(raw_log, mapping)
    base = slice_window(mapped_log, baseline_start, baseline_end)
    net, im, fm = pm4py.discover_petri_net_inductive(base, noise_threshold=0.2)
    res = {}
    for name, lg in {"raw": raw_log, "mapped": mapped_log}.items():
        w = slice_window(lg, eval_start, eval_end)
        res[name] = {
            "token_replay": pm4py.fitness_token_based_replay(w, net, im, fm),
            "alignments": pm4py.fitness_alignments(w, net, im, fm),
            "dfg_coverage": dfg_case_coverage(w),
            "rework_share": rework_share(w),
        }
    res["dfg_distance_raw_vs_mapped"] = dfg_rate_distance(res["raw"]["dfg_coverage"], res["mapped"]["dfg_coverage"])
    return res


def segment(log: pd.DataFrame, attribute: str, values: Iterable, level: str = "case") -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Case-level attribute filter (supplier, complexity, priority class, team) via PM4Py."""
    return pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(log, attribute, list(values), level=level, retain=True)

## 9. Provisional attribution

Strongest conclusions. The March fitness collapse is a semantic and provenance event, not a behavioural one (confidence ~90%): the mapped series stays within 0.02 of baseline, the normalized DFG distance is 3 per 100 cases, cycle times are flat, and every logging indicator steps in the same month while nothing else moves. The rework increase from May is real behaviour, not logging (~85%): two independent sources agree, logging indicators are flat, and only the rework edges of the DFG move. Cycle-time deterioration from May to November is dominated by queueing before Assess rather than by longer assessments (~80%): service changed about 0.6 h per pass against a 13–18 h median increase, and the demand index rose 50–70% while capacity was cut.

Plausible but not established. That Supplier Z's incomplete bundles caused the rework (~70%): timing and the September easing fit, but Z share more than doubling in one month is suspicious as a metric, and Z cases were already more complex. That the June staffing cut contributed to queueing beyond the rework-driven load (~60%): the median was already rising before June 15, and no capacity data are supplied. That the October–November shift is seasonal case-mix (~70%): consistent with history and with an escalation-heavy DFG distance, but no prior-year series is in the evidence, and the doubling of the SA/HC ratio may be recording, escalation as a capacity valve, or both. Priority-induced redistribution of delay is only weakly supported (~35%) by tail widening.

Where attribution is blocked by mediation or interaction. Supplier → rework → extra Assess passes → queue: the supplier effect on cycle time is mostly indirect, so "supplier" and "staffing" shares are not separable at aggregate level. Complexity mediates supplier, priority and season simultaneously. December confounds seasonal ebb with two interventions launched on the same date.

What would revise this account. Non-Z standard cases showing the same May rework step would move the cause from supplier to internal behaviour. Case-level Z share near 15% in May–June would reclassify the supplier_z_pct jump as a metric artefact. Waiting before Assess flat in May–June and stepping only after June 15 would rank staffing above rework as the queue driver. October 2025 with HC near 40% and SA/HC near 0.5 would confirm recurrence; HC near 30% would make 2026 new drift. March–April alignments with deviations beyond the two known label issues would reopen the migration as behavioural.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1.
