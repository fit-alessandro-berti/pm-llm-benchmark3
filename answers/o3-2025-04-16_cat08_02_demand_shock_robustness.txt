1. Stress scenarios
-------------------

S-1 “Skewed-Demand Wave”  
• +45 % total volume, mix shifts to 35 % Routine / 35 % Urgent / 22 % Complex-panel / 8 % Repeat.  
• All resources on-line.  
• Typical of winter infection surges.

S-2 “Assay Outage”  
• Normal volume and mix.  
• M1 down for 36 h; M2 runs but is 10 % slower because of higher load and more repeats.  
• Any urgent sample that waits >4 h past preparation incurs penalty risk.

S-3 “Clinical-Staff Gap”  
• Normal volume, but one medical validator plus the Friday specialist both absent for 3 working days.  
• Complex-panel share creeps up to 18 % because elective work is postponed elsewhere.

S-4 “Compressed Arrival”  
• Volume only +10 %, but 70 % of samples arrive in two courier waves (10:00 & 17:00).  
• No other resource outages.

2. Bottleneck-migration analysis
--------------------------------

S-1 Skewed-Demand Wave  
1. Pre-analytical check rises from 74 %  118 % utilisation; median queue would exceed 20 h.  
2. Once triaged, Preparation benches hit 125 %. Batching of Routine is slowed because Urgent now occupies >50 % of bench time.  
3. M1/M2 run 133 %; Urgentfirst (if applied) pushes Routine WIP to >2 days, filling refrigerator space and blocking new arrivals.  
4. Medical validation climbs to 140 %; queue >30 h.  
Relieving any one of the first three stations without extra validator capacity moves the bottleneck to medical validation within two shifts.

S-2 Assay Outage  
1. With M1 down, assay utilisation on M2 goes to 170 %, so the bottleneck is the single machine.  
2. Upstream WIP builds at Preparation (queue ~24 h) and Pre-analytical (12 h).  
3. When M1 returns, 16–18 h later Technical validation becomes the bottleneck (spike to 155 %), then Medical validation (120 %) 4 h later as two shifts of output arrive almost simultaneously.  
Unless a WIP cap is applied, cycle-time whiplash propagates 2–3 days downstream.

S-3 Clinical-Staff Gap  
1. Medical validation immediately becomes the primary bottleneck (utilisation 170 %, queue 48 h).  
2. Technical validation briefly starves (utilisation 60 %) but by day-2 the hold queue is full and Technical validation rises again to >110 % because technologists redo borderline results hoping to avoid specialist review, raising rework to >15 %.  
3. Assay machines idle 15–20 % during specialist-review back-ups, so the constraint cycles between the two clinical approval steps.

S-4 Compressed Arrival  
1. Registration jumps to 105 % for 2–3 h after each wave; error rates rise  more accession corrections ( +25 %).  
2. Pre-analytical check then exceeds 120 % for the next 3 h.  
3. By late evening, the backlog lands on M1/M2 (utilisation 110 %) and Technical validation (115 %), even though daily volume is modest.  
If courier peaks are left unsmoothed, every 1 % increase in wave amplitude adds 0.7 % to next-day Urgent cycle time.

3. Robust redesign package
--------------------------

R-A Early defect triage (implements I6)  
• 2 h fast-lane manned by a senior lab assistant screens sample volume equal to 40 % of daily intake.  
• Rejects poor specimens before they consume Preparation, Accession-correction and Assay capacity.  
Why robust: cuts recollection loops (760 300 normal; 780 350 peak) in **all** scenarios, freeing ~7 % assay time and 0.8 FTE clerk time.  
Down-side: if rejection criteria drift, repeat-after-failure share can rise  monitor repeat flags.

R-B Courier smoothing contract (I7)  
• Move two routes by ±45 min and add a micro-pickup at 13:00.  
• Reduces Registration and Pre-analytical 95-percentile load by 18 %.  
Works in S-1, S-2, S-3, essential for S-4.  
Risk: carrier punctuality; mitigation is a penalty/reward clause tied to arrival-spread KPI.

R-C Flexible validator pool (I4 plus partial I5)  
• Cross-train two Technical validators; they spend 40 % of week in Accession correction **unless** Technical validation backlog >8 h or Medical-validation queue >16 h.  
• Add a 16:00–22:00 remote medical-validation shift that activates when (Urgent backlog >60 cases OR Complex-panel backlog >30 cases).  
Coverage: prevents bottleneck lock-in in S-1 and S-3; smooths post-outage surge in S-2.  
Risk: reduces pure Technical validation capacity by 0.4 FTE if triggers are mis-set.

R-D Dynamic assay allocation (modified I3)  
• Reserve the first 25 % of M1 runtime each hour for Urgent/Complex; remaining 75 % is open.  
• If Urgent queue <10 samples and Routine queue >40, the reserve is released.  
• M2 keeps mixed work to balance repeat risk.  
Robust because it avoids starving Routine (mitigates I1 issue) yet guarantees SLA time for Urgent in S-1 and S-2.

R-E Registration automation with throttling (I8*)  
• Only auto-ingest up to a moving Daily-WIP cap so downstream stations are not flooded.  
• Estimate cap = (Pre-analytical WIP limit – on-hand manual queue).  
Benefits every scenario; danger is silent data errors, so auto-flag any case that enters Manual accession-correction loop twice.

4. Scenario-specific policies
-----------------------------

P-1 Surge protocol (trigger: Urgent backlog >80 OR Total WIP >1.8× normal)  
• Activate evening medical shift (R-C).  
• Increase M1 Urgent reserve from 25 %40 %.  
• Allow Preparation benches to run micro-batches of 6 for Urgent.

P-2 Assay-failure protocol (trigger: any machine down >4 h)  
• Freeze auto-registration (R-E) so intake matches diminished assay capacity.  
• Ship Complex-panel overflow >25 cases to partner lab (I10) once per day until local assay uptime >90 %.  
• Overtime two Preparation benches so WIP is ready for rapid catch-up when machine returns.

P-3 Clinical-gap protocol (trigger: Medical-validation queue >24 h AND validator absence >1 shift)  
• Cross-trained validators spend 100 % on Medical validation.  
• Technical validation adopts stricter auto-release rules for low-risk Routine assays to cut hand-offs by ~15 %.  
• Activate partner-lab option only for cases that already need specialist review to avoid double shipping.

P-4 Arrival-spike protocol (trigger: Registration utilisation >95 % for >90 min)  
• Phone courier service to hold next route by 30 min (contractual option from R-B).  
• Temporarily suspend Routine auto-registration and switch clerks to Urgent first.

6. Residual vulnerabilities
---------------------------

1. Single-point supplier risk: both M1 & M2 still same model; simultaneous failure would outstrip R-D and P-2 safeguards.  
2. Specialist review remains one-deep on Fridays; even with partner-lab fallback, immediate turnaround cannot be guaranteed.  
3. Early triage (R-A) may over-reject borderline paediatric samples, increasing physician complaint rate.  
4. Dynamic rules rely on accurate real-time WIP data; LIS latency >5 min would mis-fire triggers.  
5. Partner-lab shipping introduces chain-of-custody complexity; any lost sample creates legal exposure.

7. Minimum monitoring dashboard
-------------------------------

Leading indicators (checked 3× daily during peaks)  
• Current WIP by station vs WIP cap (alert if >110 %).  
• Urgent queue age 90-percentile (alert if >6 h).  
• Assay machine uptime last 4 h (alert if <85 %).  
• Medical-validation queue length (alert if >120 cases OR oldest >18 h).  
• Courier arrival spread  (alert if <1.2 h).  
• Early-triage reject rate (alert if outside 4–9 %).  

Lagging outcome metrics (reviewed weekly)  
• Cycle time median and p90 per case type.  
• Penalty incidents on Urgent SLA.  
• Overall rework %.
