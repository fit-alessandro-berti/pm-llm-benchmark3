# 3. Intervention options

We analyze eight interventions grounded in the DFG and performance data.

### S1. Green shipping recommender (SLA-aware)
*   **Target Path:** `Package standard -> Ship air` (specifically Standard-stock Service-contract).
*   **Mechanism:** Algorithm checks `promised_lead_time_hours` vs. `route_mode` reliability. Forces `parcel_ground` if probability of on-time delivery > 85%.
*   **Effect:** Reduces transport emissions significantly (Air ~18.5 vs Ground ~3.6 kgCO2e). Minimal service impact if SP1 compliance improves from 78% to >95%.
*   **Monitoring:** Track `late_delivery` rate for Standard-stock Service-contract cases post-implementation.

### S2. Dynamic batching with waiting caps
*   **Target Path:** `Plan production -> Batch wait -> CNC production`.
*   **Mechanism:** Enforce maximum wait time (e.g., 24h) for batches. If batch size < 12 after cap, release anyway to prevent excessive obsolescence.
*   **Effect:** Increases `energy_kwh` per item (loss of economies of scale) but drastically reduces `cycle_time` and obsolescence risk (currently 6% unsold within 14 days).
*   **Monitoring:** Correlation between `batch_size` and `late_delivery` risk.

### S3. Machine routing optimization (CNC_B to CNC_A)
*   **Target Path:** `Plan production -> CNC production` (Made-to-order).
*   **Mechanism:** Route MTO orders to `CNC_A` when queue slack exists, avoiding `CNC_B` despite its speed.
*   **Effect:** Reduces `rework_flag` incidence (CNC_B rework 18% vs CNC_A 9%). Lowers total energy/material waste from rework loops, even if single-pass energy is slightly higher.
*   **Monitoring:** Rework rate by `machine_group`; total energy per delivered part (including rework).

### S4. Priority picking vs. Air for Stock
*   **Target Path:** `Pick from stock -> Package standard -> Ship air`.
*   **Mechanism:** For Standard-stock Service-contract, replace `parcel_air` with `parcel_ground` + priority warehouse slotting.
*   **Effect:** Cuts transport CO2 by ~80% for this segment. Maintains service level by reducing internal pick time rather than external transit time.
*   **Monitoring:** `promised_lead_time_hours` adherence for Service-contract stock orders.

### S5. Fixed consolidation windows
*   **Target Path:** `Package standard -> Consolidated truck`.
*   **Mechanism:** Two daily departures for non-urgent stock orders. Requires 12-24h hold.
*   **Effect:** Reduces transport kgCO2e by 35-55% compared to individual parcels. Increases median cycle time for affected orders.
*   **Monitoring:** Truck fill rates; `late_delivery` rate for consolidated vs. parcel ground.

### S6. Packaging-route compatibility check
*   **Target Path:** `Package reinforced -> Rework packaging`.
*   **Mechanism:** Validate `part_geometry` vs. `route_mode` before labeling. Prevents incompatible packaging that triggers rework loops.
*   **Effect:** Eliminates `Rework packaging` events (currently 260 + 360 cases). Saves `package_material_kg` and avoids double handling energy.
*   **Monitoring:** Count of `Rework packaging` events; `package_material_kg` variance.

### S7. High-risk inspection gate
*   **Target Path:** `Inspection -> Rework production`.
*   **Mechanism:** Mandatory second inspection pass for parts produced on `CNC_B` or those with high historical defect rates before packing.
*   **Effect:** Reduces downstream rework loops (which add significant energy/scrap). Adds minor delay at inspection cell.
*   **Monitoring:** `rework_production` loop frequency; `cycle_time` p90 for MTO.

### S9. Critical-downtime segmentation
*   **Target Path:** `Emergency production -> ... -> Dedicated van`.
*   **Mechanism:** Classify orders into "True Downtime" (line stoppage) vs. "Precautionary Urgent". Only True Downtime qualifies for `dedicated_van`.
*   **Effect:** Reduces empty van returns and high CO2 trips (38.0 kgCO2e/order avg). Protects resilience for actual critical cases.
*   **Monitoring:** SP2 conformance (justification event presence); Customer satisfaction for Service-contract.

# 5. Rebound and displacement risks

Implementing sustainability measures can trigger unintended consequences elsewhere in the system:

1.  **Batching Obsolescence Rebound:** Implementing S2 (Batch Caps) reduces waiting time but forces smaller batches. This increases `energy_kwh` per item (losing economies of scale) and may increase `material_scrap_kg` if setup waste is frequent.
2.  **Service Displacement:** Aggressive application of S1 (Green Shipping) on marginally feasible orders may convert a "on-time ground" case into a "late order," triggering a customer-initiated emergency air shipment later, negating initial gains.
3.  **Queue Congestion:** Shifting MTO work to `CNC_A` (S3) to reduce rework may overload `CNC_A` queues, increasing `batch_wait` times for other jobs and pushing more orders toward `Emergency production`.
4.  **Consolidation Delay:** S5 (Consolidated trucks) introduces a fixed 12-24h wait. If inventory buffers are low, this could increase `Cancel due to delay` events (currently 410 cases), forcing emergency production which is highly energy-intensive.
5.  **Inspection Bottleneck:** Adding S7 (Inspection Gate) adds steps to the `Inspection -> Package reinforced` path. During peak demand, this could create a bottleneck, increasing cycle time and potentially causing late deliveries for Service-contracts.
6.  **Classification Gaming:** Under S9 (Critical Segmentation), sales teams might classify "Precautionary" orders as "True Downtime" to ensure faster service, undermining the emission reduction goal of dedicated vans.
