## 1. Decision rule
A case is eligible only if its current stage is before Diagnosis Complete and it either has no prior alert or has experienced a materially new event (new customer log, new field value, or workflow change) since the last alert. For repeated alerts without new events, suppress for 90 minutes or until a new event occurs. Adjust raw p_now for known miscalibration before scoring: add +0.065 to SME cases, subtract 0.085 from Strategic Enterprise cases, leave Public Sector unchanged, and apply a 0.10 conservative discount to Non-Native-Language Support cases. Compute a priority score = (midpoint adjusted risk reduction × value of avoiding breach  €450  false-alarm burden) / (1 + expected spillover cases affected). Intervene if score  €2,500 and at least one slot remains after reserving one for potential regulatory arrivals; otherwise wait or suppress. Re-evaluate every 30 minutes only on cases meeting the new-event rule.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy
A 90-minute cool-down applies after any alert unless a documented new event (new log, new field, or workflow change) occurs. Escalation to swarm is allowed only on the second qualifying alert. Suppression is automatic for unchanged repeated alerts. I-05 receives suppression because no new event has occurred and the model simply refreshed; I-07 is re-evaluated because the new customer diagnostic log constitutes a material evidence change even though p_now declined slightly.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks
Value-based prioritization risks starving lower-value SME or Public Sector cases when high-value Strategic Enterprise cases consume slots. Non-Native-Language Support cases carry extra uncertainty from small validation samples, increasing the chance of both missed interventions and wasteful ones. Segment-specific calibration drift can systematically mis-rank cases. Selective intervention changes future breach labels, biasing retraining toward intervened trajectories. Spillover delays fall disproportionately on untreated diagnosis-stage cases that are often lower-visibility. Safeguards: (a) maintain a minimum 25 % slot reservation for non-Strategic cases each day; (b) apply the 0.10 discount to Non-Native-Language Support and monitor its realized effect separately; (c) retrain only on cases whose intervention status is recorded; (d) track untreated diagnosis-stage breach rates weekly and trigger a spillover audit if they rise >0.02 above baseline.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan
Track daily: slot utilization, intervention rate by segment, realized breach rate within 2 h of alert, and median queue delay for non-intervened cases. Estimate causal effects via a rolling observational contrast of intervened versus eligible-but-not-intervened cases matched on stage, segment, and p_now, refreshed monthly; supplement with small randomized slot-allocation experiments when spare capacity exists. Monitor policy-induced drift by comparing pre-policy versus post-policy calibration plots stratified by segment. Change the priority-score threshold or allocation rule if the 30-day rolling net-impact estimate falls below €1,500 per intervention or if spillover-induced breach increase exceeds 0.025 on untreated cases for two consecutive weeks.
