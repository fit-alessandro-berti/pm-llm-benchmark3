## 1. What is observed

Automation availability began in April for Alpha and Beta and July for Gamma; Delta and Epsilon remained unavailable through August. Availability is not case-level treatment: even before formal adoption, 4–6% of cases are labeled automated fast path, while post-adoption use rises gradually from 25–27% to 48–50% in Alpha/Beta and from 25% to 32% in Gamma. This creates treatment-version and implementation-fidelity variation.

Rollout was targeted rather than randomized. Alpha and Beta were selected partly because of high volume and historically poor cycle times; Gamma followed a readiness pilot. Comparisons with later or never adopters therefore mix automation with branch selection, readiness, and possible regression to the mean.

Several operational changes coincide with rollout: a centralized fraud desk started in March; routing increased Alpha/Beta arrivals by about 10% and Gamma arrivals by about 5%; productive FTE changed in four branches; May’s product change raised complexity; and managers could alter local practices. These factors affect queues, workload, and outcomes independently of automation and may interact with it.

Outcomes are aggregated branch-month statistics. Medians and p90s do not permit case-level adjustment, pathway-specific comparison, or valid reconstruction of cycle-time distributions. Rework and escalation may reflect altered classification as well as performance. CSAT is a respondent mean with response rates varying from approximately 20–45%, but response counts and respondent composition are unavailable. Only five branches and eight months are observed, sharply limiting trend diagnostics and uncertainty estimation.

The observed intervention is not a single stable treatment: uptake, routing rules, training, managerial practices, and potentially model thresholds vary over time. Because all branches share the fraud desk, standard no-interference assumptions are also doubtful.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary: matched staggered-adoption event study using not-yet-treated controls and group-time effects** | Cohort-specific adoption dates; event time; no already-treated branch used as a control; separate early and Gamma cohorts | Delta/Epsilon and, before July, Gamma; matched/reweighted on pre-levels and slopes | Pre-outcome levels/slopes, arrivals/FTE, FTE, recorded complexity, calendar month; avoid conditioning on post-treatment uptake for ITT | E1, E2, E4, E6, E7 | Conditional parallel counterfactual trends; no anticipation; measured covariates adequate; stable outcomes; interference negligible or explicitly modeled | Event-time leads; leave-one-control-out; raw/matched trend plots; placebo adoption dates; cohort-specific estimates; weight diagnostics; randomization-style inference where possible | Only five branches, targeted rollout, shared fraud queue, and simultaneous shocks make parallel trends fragile |
| Comparative interrupted time series / controlled segmented regression | Level and slope changes at April and July, estimated separately by cohort | Never-treated branches plus not-yet-treated Gamma before July | Calendar shocks, workload, FTE, complexity; branch-specific pretrend sensitivity | E1, E2 and dynamic effects | Untreated level/slope would continue absent adoption; no coincident branch-specific intervention | Alternative break dates; residual plots; different windows; remove May/June; control-specific fits | Very short pre/post series and rollout coinciding with routing/staffing changes |
| Synthetic or weighted control for each adopter | Branch-specific intervention date; optimize pre-treatment outcome trajectories and covariates | Donor pool of untreated/not-yet-treated branches, excluding treated months | Outcome lags, arrivals/FTE, complexity, FTE, escalation/rework | Branch-specific availability effects | Weighted donor trajectory approximates counterfactual; no spillovers; adequate pre-fit | Pre-fit RMSPE; placebo-in-space/time; leave-one-donor-out; weight concentration | Tiny donor pool and poor extrapolation for Alpha/Beta’s high volume and weak baseline |
| Prospective randomized phased rollout | Randomized invitation/start month, preferably branch-team or operational unit level; encouragement if universal eventual access is required | Concurrent delayed-start units | Prespecified strata: baseline median/p90, workload, complexity, readiness; calendar effects | Prospective ITT; encouragement effect and IV effect for compliers if exclusion is credible | Assignment adhered to; no differential anticipation; interference measured/contained; IV exclusion and monotonicity for uptake effect | Balance, compliance, contamination, queue spillovers, blinded outcome definitions, preregistered event-time analysis | Too few whole branches unless randomization occurs within branches; shared queues can contaminate controls |

The primary retrospective strategy is preferable because it respects staggered timing and avoids using already-treated Alpha/Beta as controls for Gamma, which can produce misleading weights under heterogeneous dynamic effects. It also makes the relevant comparison explicit: each cohort versus branches not yet treated at that calendar time. Cohort-specific effects should be reported rather than collapsed into one coefficient, with event-time effects distinguishing implementation from mature use.

Matching or weighting on pre-outcome levels and slopes is important because early adopters were selected for poor historical performance. Calendar-time comparisons address common March fraud-desk and May product shocks, while workload, staffing, and recorded complexity improve conditional comparability. However, these adjustments do not repair unmeasured routing mix, local management changes, shared-queue interference, or the lack of pre-March history.

With three treated and two never-treated branches, conventional asymptotics are unreliable. Estimates should therefore be treated as structured sensitivity analyses, supplemented by raw contrasts, leave-one-branch-out results, placebo timings, and broad uncertainty intervals. A prospective randomized phase-in is superior for future learning; the event study is principally the best way to organize the existing evidence, not a guarantee of identification.

### Comparisons with untreated branches

For the April cohort, Delta/Epsilon averaged January–March versus April–August:

- Median: 37.35  42.60, **+5.25 h**.
- p90: 77.18  93.24, **+16.06 h**.
- Rework: 12.60%  14.63%, **+2.03 pp**.
- Escalation: 8.23%  9.77%, **+1.53 pp**.
- CSAT: 4.008  3.924, **0.084**.

A simple equal-branch difference-in-differences therefore gives approximately:

| outcome | Alpha/Beta mean change | untreated change | raw DiD |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Median | +0.75 h | +5.25 h | 4.50 h |
| p90 | +9.69 h | +16.06 h | 6.37 h |
| Rework | 0.06 pp | +2.03 pp | 2.09 pp |
| Escalation | +2.70 pp | +1.53 pp | +1.17 pp |
| CSAT | +0.003 | 0.084 | +0.087 |

For Gamma, comparing April–June with July–August against Delta/Epsilon gives a median DiD of roughly **4.4 h**, p90 **4.7 h**, rework **0.8 pp**, escalation **+0.8 pp**, and CSAT **+0.09**. These favorable relative contrasts partly reflect broad May–June deterioration followed by July–August recovery.

### Treatment intensity

Within post-adoption months, automated-fast-path share and outcomes move together over implementation time:

- Alpha, 25%48%: median 48.343.3 and p90 97.3101.2; endpoint slopes are about **2.2 median hours** and **+1.7 p90 hours per 10 pp** uptake.
- Beta, 27%50%: median 47.345.0 and p90 95.699.2; approximately **1.0 h** and **+1.6 h per 10 pp**.
- Gamma, 25%32%: median 38.436.0 and p90 87.182.7; approximately **3.4 h** and **6.3 h per 10 pp**.

These are not dose effects: uptake trends with tenure, arrivals, backlog, staffing, and seasonality; moreover, “completed through the automated fast path” is post-treatment and partly determined by case difficulty and successful completion.

### Pre-trends and regression to the mean

January–March medians rose by 3.0 h in Gamma and 1.8 h in Delta, were nearly flat in Beta/Epsilon, and fell 1.0 h in Alpha. March introduced the fraud desk, producing a common escalation jump of about 2 pp and generally worse cycle time. Thus trends are not convincingly parallel. Alpha/Beta were selected for weak performance, making regression to the mean plausible; Gamma was selected after readiness and adopted immediately after its worst pre-period.

### Sensitivity/ranged conclusions

- **Median:** Raw adopter DiDs are around 4 to 5 h. Excluding the disruption-heavy May–June comparison and comparing August with March gives Alpha/Beta improvements of 6.25 h versus 1.45 h in controls, again about 4.8 h. Nevertheless, unmeasured demand and readiness could plausibly erase much of this. A cautious causal scenario range is **0 to 6 h** under moderately credible comparability, broadened to **8 to +2 h** without it.
- **p90:** Raw DiDs are around 5 to 6 h, but Alpha/Beta’s own p90 averages increase nearly 10 h and remain above March in August. A cautious range is **8 to +8 h** relative to a common-shock counterfactual, with no defensible claim of absolute tail improvement.
- **Rework:** Relative estimates near 1 to 2 pp are suggestive, but displacement/reclassification permits a scenario range of **2.5 to +0.5 pp**.
- **CSAT:** Relative changes are +0.09 to +0.14 points, but unknown response selection makes even the sign uncertain; a reasonable sensitivity range is **0.10 to +0.15**.

## 6. Threats, tests, and decision consequences

| threat | why it matters here | direction of likely bias | test or additional evidence | consequence if threat is severe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Selection into adoption | Alpha/Beta were chosen for poor cycle time and volume; Gamma for readiness | Regression to mean may exaggerate gains; readiness may also exaggerate Gamma gains | Longer prehistory, selection score, documented rollout criteria, matched pretrends | Existing contrasts cannot support a general rollout effect |
| Nonparallel trends | Branch outcomes moved differently before treatment | Either direction | Event-study leads, branch-specific slopes, placebo dates | DiD estimates lose causal interpretation |
| Time-varying confounding | Routing, product, management, and learning changed near adoption | Either direction | Detailed intervention calendar and case-level covariates | Effects cannot be assigned uniquely to automation |
| Treatment intensity/endogeneity | Availability differs from actual use; use selects eligible/easy cases | Usually overstates per-case benefit | Eligibility, recommendation, acceptance, override, and route data; randomized encouragement | Binary ITT remains policy-specific; dose effect unidentified |
| Fraud-desk spillovers | Treated branches may add borderline referrals to a shared queue | Can make treated tails worse and controls worse, biasing either way | Referral timestamps, desk queue length/waits, origin branch, network load | SUTVA fails; use network exposure estimands |
| Staffing changes | Alpha/Gamma lost FTE and Beta/Delta gained FTE | Loss understates benefit; gains can overstate it | Productive hours by skill/day, absence, workload and backlog | Adjust or stratify; do not pool branches blindly |
| Case-mix change | Complexity rose in May and may be coarsely measured | Higher complexity likely masks benefit; routing of easy cases may exaggerate it | Case-level risk, product, source, severity, missingness audits | Aggregate changes are compositional |
| Routing campaign | Adopters received extra demand around rollout | Likely masks capacity benefit, but routing composition could reverse sign | Source-level arrivals, assignment rule, randomized routing | Estimate bundled policy effect or model routing explicitly |
| Anticipation | Training or work redesign may precede formal go-live | Biases pre-period toward treatment, often attenuating effect | Training dates, pilot logs, workflow changes; exclude anticipation window | Adoption date is misclassified |
| Survey nonresponse | Response rates vary and counts/composition are unknown | Either direction | Monthly invitations/responses, pathway and risk strata, inverse-probability bounds | CSAT sign may be unidentified |
| Measurement aggregation | Means of branch medians/p90s are not pooled case quantiles | Unknown; weighting changes interpretation | Case-level durations and quantile models; arrival-weighted sensitivity | Claims must remain branch-month-level |
| Small number of branches | Five clusters make clustered standard errors unstable | False precision | Wild-cluster bootstrap, randomization inference, leave-one-out estimates | Use broad intervals; avoid significance language |
| Dynamic treatment effects | Learning, backlog, and uptake evolve after adoption | Short-run averages can understate or overstate mature effect | Longer follow-up and event-time estimates by cohort | No single constant “X%” is appropriate |
| Treatment-version heterogeneity | Thresholds, training, overrides, and local work practices may differ | Either direction | Version/configuration logs and fidelity measures | Generalization requires version-specific estimates |
| Outcome-dependent pathway label | Automated fast path denotes completed path, not assignment | Likely makes intensity benefits look too favorable | Record assignment/recommendation before outcome and all subsequent paths | Per-protocol intensity regression is biased |
| Shared seasonality/product shock | May deterioration and summer recovery affect all branches | Can generate spurious adoption dynamics | Multiple years, richer controls, calendar placebo tests | Short-window Gamma estimate is especially fragile |
| Backlog and censoring | Monthly completions may represent earlier arrivals; unresolved cases may be absent | Usually makes recent periods look artificially fast | Arrival-cohort outcomes, censoring flags, WIP age distribution | Monthly cycle summaries may not be comparable |

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**expand with an embedded evaluation**

Expand next to **Delta**, while retaining Epsilon as a concurrent delayed-start comparator for a prespecified period. Delta has lower complexity and higher current productive capacity than Epsilon, making it a safer implementation setting. Epsilon should not be denied permanently; announce a randomized or fixed delayed start after the evaluation window. If operationally feasible, randomize Delta’s teams, queues, or eligible case-routing units to immediate versus delayed activation. Randomize **availability or encouragement**, not completed fast-path status. Stratify by baseline complexity, workload, and team performance, and prevent discretionary reassignment across arms.

Collect at least **six pre-rollout months**—preferably twelve to measure seasonality—and **six post-rollout months**, with primary short-run estimates at months 1–2 and mature estimates at months 4–6. Preserve arrival-cohort case-level event logs: timestamps for registration, triage recommendation, acceptance/override, specialist referral, fraud referral, information request, rework, resolution, and closure. Record eligibility, model version/score, risk at registration, source/product, assigned team, productive hours, WIP, queue entry/exit, queue age, abandonment, reopenings, and SLA breaches. Retain both pathway-specific and system-wide median, p90, and p95 outcomes.

Monitor fraud-desk load and waits by originating branch, including untreated Epsilon, because automation may create interference. Also monitor specialist utilization, cross-branch transfers, and routed arrivals. Customer guardrails should include no material increase in p90/p95, unresolved-case age, erroneous fast-path decisions, reopened cases, high-risk misses, or escalation waits. Capture survey invitations and responses so CSAT can be weighted and bounded for nonresponse.

Scale further only if the preregistered ITT analysis shows a practically meaningful median improvement—e.g., at least **3 hours or 7%**—without more than a **2-hour p90 deterioration**, **0.5 pp rework increase**, **1 pp escalation increase absent available specialist capacity**, or **0.10 CSAT decline**. Require concordant process evidence that gains arise from reduced triage work rather than displacement into specialist queues. Otherwise hold, adjust thresholds/capacity, and extend follow-up before Epsilon activation.

```python
    "median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h", "rework_pct",
    "escalation_pct", "csat_1to5"
]

# Print branch trends for all five outcomes. Each table is separate (no subplots).
for y in outcomes:
    print(f"\n=== TREND: {y} ===")
    print(df.pivot(index="month_str", columns="branch", values=y).round(2))

# Adoption dates and relative time
adoption = (
    df.loc[df["automation"].eq(1)]
      .groupby("branch")["month"].min()
      .to_dict()
)
df["adoption_month"] = df["branch"].map(adoption)

def month_diff(row):
    if pd.isna(row["adoption_month"]):
        return np.nan
    return (
        (row["month"].year - row["adoption_month"].year) * 12
        + row["month"].month - row["adoption_month"].month
    )

df["rel_time"] = df.apply(month_diff, axis=1)

# Relative-time indicators; omitted reference is event time -1.
for k in [-3, -2, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4]:
    label = f"rt_m{abs(k)}" if k < 0 else f"rt_p{k}"
    df[label] = (df["rel_time"] == k).astype(int)

print("\n=== RELATIVE-TIME COUNTS ===")
print(df.loc[df["adoption_month"].notna(), "rel_time"].value_counts().sort_index())

# Transparent pre/post windows:
# Alpha/Beta: Jan-Mar versus Apr-Aug.
# Gamma: Apr-Jun versus Jul-Aug.
windows = {
    "Alpha": (pd.Timestamp("2026-01-01"), pd.Timestamp("2026-03-01"),
              pd.Timestamp("2026-04-01"), pd.Timestamp("2026-08-01")),
    "Beta":  (pd.Timestamp("2026-01-01"), pd.Timestamp("2026-03-01"),
              pd.Timestamp("2026-04-01"), pd.Timestamp("2026-08-01")),
    "Gamma": (pd.Timestamp("2026-04-01"), pd.Timestamp("2026-06-01"),
              pd.Timestamp("2026-07-01"), pd.Timestamp("2026-08-01")),
}
contrast_vars = ["automated_fast_path_pct"] + outcomes

rows = []
for branch, (pre0, pre1, post0, post1) in windows.items():
    g = df[df["branch"].eq(branch)]
    pre = g[g["month"].between(pre0, pre1)]
    post = g[g["month"].between(post0, post1)]
    for y in contrast_vars:
        rows.append({
            "branch": branch,
            "outcome": y,
            "pre_mean": pre[y].mean(),
            "post_mean": post[y].mean(),
            "change": post[y].mean() - pre[y].mean(),
            "n_pre": len(pre),
            "n_post": len(post)
        })

contrasts = pd.DataFrame(rows)
print("\n=== ADOPTER-SPECIFIC PRE/POST CONTRASTS (DESCRIPTIVE) ===")
print(contrasts.round(3).to_string(index=False))

# A transparent early-cohort comparison against never-treated Delta/Epsilon.
early = df["branch"].isin(["Alpha", "Beta"])
control = df["branch"].isin(["Delta", "Epsilon"])
pre_ab = df["month"].between("2026-01-01", "2026-03-01")
post_ab = df["month"].between("2026-04-01", "2026-08-01")
print("\n=== SIMPLE EQUAL-BRANCH EARLY-COHORT DiD ARITHMETIC ===")
for y in outcomes:
    # First average within branch/window, then across branches.
    tpre = df[early & pre_ab].groupby("branch")[y].mean().mean()
    tpost = df[early & post_ab].groupby("branch")[y].mean().mean()
    cpre = df[control & pre_ab].groupby("branch")[y].mean().mean()
    cpost = df[control & post_ab].groupby("branch")[y].mean().mean()
    did = (tpost - tpre) - (cpost - cpre)
    print(f"{y:20s} treated_change={tpost-tpre:8.3f} "
          f"control_change={cpost-cpre:8.3f} raw_DiD={did:8.3f}")

warnings.warn(
    "Only five branch clusters are available. Conventional cluster-robust "
    "standard errors and p-values can be highly unreliable; use broad "
    "sensitivity analyses, leave-one-branch-out checks, or prospective randomization."
)
warnings.warn(
    "Branch-month medians and p90s are aggregated statistics. Regression on them "
    "does not estimate a case-level mean or quantile treatment effect, and branch "
    "medians cannot be pooled into an organization-wide median."
)

# DESCRIPTIVE panel model:
# Branch and month fixed effects organize adjusted associations.
# CAUSAL interpretation would require conditional parallel trends, no anticipation,
# stable treatment versions, and no interference through the shared fraud desk.
# Those assumptions are doubtful here. This is NOT labeled a causal estimate.
panel_results = {}
for y in ["median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h", "rework_pct",
          "escalation_pct", "csat_1to5"]:
    formula = (
        f"{y} ~ automation + workload + high_complexity_pct "
        "+ C(branch) + C(month_str)"
    )
    model = smf.ols(formula, data=df).fit(
        cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": df["branch"]}
    )
    panel_results[y] = model
    print(f"\n=== DESCRIPTIVE PANEL MODEL: {y} ===")
    print(model.summary().tables[1])

# TREATMENT-INTENSITY model:
# automated_fast_path_pct is endogenous: it rises with learning/time and is
# determined by eligibility, case difficulty, routing, and successful completion.
# Thus its coefficient is an adjusted descriptive dose association, not a
# per-protocol causal effect. The binary availability flag is included to separate
# level availability from within-version intensity only algebraically.
for y in ["median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h"]:
    formula = (
        f"{y} ~ automation + automated_fast_path_pct + workload "
        "+ high_complexity_pct + C(branch) + C(month_str)"
    )
    model = smf.ols(formula, data=df).fit(
        cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": df["branch"]}
    )
    print(f"\n=== DESCRIPTIVE TREATMENT-INTENSITY MODEL: {y} ===")
    print(model.summary().tables[1])

# Event-study specification shown descriptively.
# Already-treated comparisons, sparse event-time support, five clusters, and
# heterogeneous effects make this unsuitable as proof of causality. A formal
# retrospective analysis should compute cohort-time ATT using only not-yet-treated
# controls and report cohort-specific effects.
event_terms = ["rt_m3", "rt_m2", "rt_p0", "rt_p1", "rt_p2", "rt_p3", "rt_p4"]
event_formula = (
    "median_cycle_h ~ " + " + ".join(event_terms)
    + " + workload + high_complexity_pct + C(branch) + C(month_str)"
)
event_model = smf.ols(event_formula, data=df).fit(
    cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": df["branch"]}
)
print("\n=== DESCRIPTIVE RELATIVE-TIME PANEL MODEL: median_cycle_h ===")
print(event_model.summary().tables[1])
print("\nCAUTION: None of these regressions proves causality. Key assumptions are "
      "explicitly noted in comments and are not verifiable with this panel.")
```

## 10. Final claim ladder

- **Observed:** Availability was followed by increasing automated-fast-path use, late median improvements, higher escalation, and mixed p90 behavior across adopters.
- **Suggestive:** Relative to not-yet-treated or never-treated branches, automation may have reduced median cycle time and rework by modest amounts under the observed rollout package.
- **Conditionally causal:** If conditional parallel trends, no interference, stable measurement, and adequate adjustment for staffing, routing, and case mix hold, availability plausibly reduced median cycle time by roughly 0–6 hours without an established p90 benefit.
- **Not supported:** “Automation reduces cycle time by X%” as a universal, pathway-independent effect—or that it improves customer satisfaction—is not supported by the supplied data.
