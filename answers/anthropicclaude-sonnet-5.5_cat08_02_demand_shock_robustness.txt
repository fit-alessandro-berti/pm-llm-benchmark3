   - I4 (moving technical validators to accession correction) drains the 101% pool. In peak, even one or two FTE moved out means Technical validation collapses, so accession relief moves the queue two stations downstream.

**S6: Compound**
- Combined S1 + S2a + S3: Urgent wins assay time on M2 and then dies in Medical validation. Routine and Complex age. Backlog release hits Technical validation and Medical validation together.
- No single lever works. The system needs an explicit protection ordering and shedding (section 4, P-F).

# 3. Robust redesign package

**R1. Slack-based priority instead of strict Urgent-first (replaces I1)**
- Priority at preparation, assay, Technical validation and Medical validation is based on SLA slack relative to expected remaining work, not on class alone.
- Order of protection (only one class can be first):
  1. Urgent with slack under about 4–6h.
  2. Complex panels already started ("finish what you started", which cuts partial WIP).
  3. Routine older than 48h (an aging guard at two-thirds of the 72h target).
  4. Everything else.
- Why robust: it works across S1, S2 and S3 because it reacts to actual slack, not assumed mix.
- Depends on: a reliable SLA clock (assumed to start at registration), LIS worklist visibility, and discipline to apply it.
- Fails when: Urgent share exceeds about 40% (no slack left to reorder) or the clock is wrong. Then shedding is needed (P-F).
- Downstream risk: priority at assay alone pushes Urgent to Technical validation and Medical validation, so the same slack rules must apply there.

**R2. Defect prevention and early triage bundle (I6 + gated I8 + clerk redeployment)**
- A 2-hour triage at courier unload checks the minimum: ID/label match, tube type, volume, metadata completeness.
- Simple label/accession errors are fixed on the spot by trained staff ("Class A"). Only hard cases go to the two accession specialists ("Class B").
- Clerks freed by automated registration are reassigned to triage and a dedicated recollection desk. This protects intake from the recollection loop.
- Auto-registration starts with Urgent and Complex orders that have complete metadata, at a metered rate that respects pre-analytical headroom.
- Why robust: defects cost 60h per recollection loop and 26% of corrections fail anyway. Finding them before the 9.5h pre-analytical queue helps S4 and S5 directly and reduces rework in all scenarios.
- Depends on: a high-precision defect code list, and metadata quality at source.
- Fails when: false rejections add recollections, or triage becomes a new queue at the 10:00 and 17:00 spikes. Fallback: if triage queue exceeds 1h, switch to pass-through and let pre-analytical check everything.
- Downstream risk: the pre-analytical check (96%) and accession specialists. Metering keeps it from flooding.

**R3. Soft machine roles, repeat routing and pull-based batching (modifies I3 and I2)**
- M1 is the preferred machine for Urgent, Complex and all repeats. M2 is preferred for Routine, with explicit overflow in both directions. No hard dedication.
- Repeats go to M1 (repeating on the flakier machine wastes capacity twice). A QC gate applies to M2 night-shift first runs.
- Batches stay at 12 and are time-boxed (for example, close at 12 tubes or 4h, whichever comes first). Batches are formed when an assay slot opens ("pull"), not when preparation finishes, so priority can still be changed late.
- Urgent never waits for Routine batches. Batch size 24 is allowed only for high-volume Routine assay types, when assay queue is low and no Urgent slack is at risk.
- Why robust: it keeps flexibility under S2 and limits the failure blast radius (a failed 24-tube batch repeats 24 tubes).
- Depends on: assay-type-level batch data and reliable machine-level failure rates.
- Fails when: both machines are degraded or M2's failure rate rises. Then P-B applies.
- Downstream risk: better assay flow loads Technical validation. Coordinate with R4.

**R4. Validation flow redesign**
- **Technical validation:** prepare a flex pool of two cross-trained staff. Because training takes 4 weeks, start before winter. Deploy them as needed rather than reassigning them permanently (modified I4; see P-E). Consider a governance-approved auto-verification pilot for unflagged Routine results (beyond the candidate list). It reduces the 73.6% direct-release load on the 101% pool.
- **Medical validation and specialist review:**
  - Flag likely Complex panels early (they are 2.4× more likely to be held).
  - Run specialist review in parallel with Medical validation, not after it, since 92% of holds go to Medical validation anyway. Hold  Medical validation is 18h normal and 48h peak.
  - Hand off from Technical validation to Medical validation on a fixed 2-hour cadence, not at shift end.
  - Order the worklist by slack.
- Why robust: it attacks the two stations with the worst amplification (Medical validation ×11, specialist ×7.2) without adding headcount everywhere.
- Fails when: parallel review wastes specialist time on cases that did not need it, or specialist absence is long.

**R5. Courier smoothing (I7) at modest scope**
- Negotiate staggered pickup slots and a separate tagged lane for Urgent bags, coupled with R2 triage.
- Fails if the courier contract does not allow it or route delays dominate.
- Downstream risk: pre-analytical check. Pair it with R2.

**Not adopted:**
- I9 (hold Routine releases for batch report). Release is not a bottleneck (2h  5h), so it adds delay to already-late Routine for little gain.
- I2 at 24 as a blanket rule.
- I1 and I3 in their strict forms.

# 6. Residual vulnerabilities

- **Technical validation remains at about 101% in peak.** Even with flex staffing and possible auto-verification, it is the pool most likely to fail first.
- **Two accession specialists and the biweekly specialist absence** are single points of failure. Triage only reduces, not removes, the load.
- **Machine concentration.** M1 is the reliable path, and losing it forces Urgent onto M2 with its higher failure rate.
- **Urgent share above about 40%.** The slack rules have nothing to reorder, and penalties become unavoidable without shedding.
- **Data uncertainty.** The unbalanced DFG (unfinished cases, unmatched assay entries) means thresholds could be off. Validate them on a clean event log.
- **Vulnerabilities created by the redesign itself:**
  - **Rule complexity and gaming.** Slack-based priority is harder to understand and audit. Staff may game the "Urgent" flag, and the aging guard can consume capacity that Urgent needs.
  - **Triage false rejections** can increase recollections (already 42% cancel) and clerk load. Triage could become a new queue at the spikes.
  - **Parallel specialist review** wastes specialist effort on cases that turn out not to need review.
  - **Pull-based, time-boxed batching** loses some setup efficiency and could produce many small batches on M2.
  - **Flex pool dependency.** The cross-trained staff are a hidden shared resource: if they are needed in Technical validation and accession correction at the same time, the flex fails.
  - **Outsourcing** adds traceability and reconciliation risk.
  - **Policy flapping and alert fatigue** if thresholds are too tight.
