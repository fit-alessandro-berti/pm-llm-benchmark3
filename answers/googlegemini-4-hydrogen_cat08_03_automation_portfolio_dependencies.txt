## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

Automation choices in this process are tightly coupled across data, compliance, scarce human experts, external partners, and IT architecture:

1. **Upstream data quality dependencies (`A1/A2/A5 -> A6 -> A8 -> A9`)**
   - `Financial document review` (24% rework) and `Request missing documents` (3,300 cases, 2,800 looping back, 52% repeat rate) show that poor intake quality drives downstream rework. Automating `A6` (financial normalization) without improving document completeness (`A5`) causes extraction failures on missing or unreadable PDFs.
   - Similarly, `A8` (Committee pack auto-assembly) waits on four synchronized inputs (`KYC status`, `normalized financial statements`, `collateral valuation`, and `credit-analysis notes`). Deploying `A8` before `A6` and `A4` guarantees garbage-in/garbage-out assembly and perpetuates its 15% rework rate.
   - `A2` (classification) governs whether cases take the correct exposure path; misclassifying High-exposure SMEs undermines downstream `Committee decision` and four-eyes controls.

2. **Legal and compliance control dependencies (`A3/A4 -> A9 -> A10 -> A11`)**
   - Legally, `Customer signing` cannot precede KYC clearance, yet 6.5% of cases show provisional signing (1.1% requiring remediation). `A10` (e-signature automation) cannot be scaled safely until `A9` (or an equivalent hard gate) blocks contract issuance and signing links whenever KYC or `Resolve entity mismatch` is open.
   - `Contract preparation` experiences 8.5% remediation loops (14% for Credit-line renewals; 650510 DFG loop) because terms diverge from `Committee decision`. `A9` depends on structured committee decision outputs and a maintained rule library; `A11` (`Loan activation bot`) is strictly dependent on `A9`, `A4`, and four-eyes enforcement being airtight first.

3. **Human escalation capacity and shifted workload**
   - The process has two severe human bottlenecks: **Compliance specialists** (3 FTE, heavily concentrated on 2 senior specialists) and **Credit analysts** (9 FTE, central in the working-together network).
   - Automating high-frequency Back-office work (`A1`, `A2`) frees staff who are already underutilized (<65% after 15:00) while accelerating arrivals into `Credit analysts` and `Compliance ops/specialists`, inflating waiting times at `Financial document review` (18.0h), `Normalize financial statements` (20.0h), `Credit analysis` (28.0h), and `Resolve entity mismatch` (31.0h).
   - Conversely, `A3` (KYC pre-classifier) relies on human escalation for ambiguous hits; if false-positive escalation is poorly calibrated, it will overwhelm the 3 Compliance specialists unless paired with `A4`.

4. **External valuation synchronization dependency (`A2/triage -> A7 -> A8`)**
   - Currently, `Collateral valuation request` (2,400 cases) occurs *after* `Credit analysis`, creating a sequential 12.0h + 58.0h wait (19% late) before `Committee packaging` (1,980 DFG link).
   - Because `Financial document review` and `KYC check` already run concurrently in practice, `A7` depends on early eligibility signals (71% Equipment finance, 18% Working capital) from intake/classification so external valuations can run *in parallel* with financial and KYC checks rather than blocking `Committee packaging`.

5. **Core-banking integration constraints (2 deep integrations in Months 1–6)**
   - Deep core-banking integration is required where automation reads/writes authoritative customer masters, compliance holds, credit models, or binding contract/disbursement states (`A4`, `A6`, `A9`, `A11`).
   - Portal/workflow-layer modules (`A5` checklist/portal nudges, `A7` valuer routing rules, `A10` e-sign reminders) can initially operate via lightweight APIs or BPM workflow orchestration, reserving the two scarce H1 deep-integration slots for core control and bottleneck modules.

## 7. Final recommendation

**Recommend Portfolio B (Bottleneck / Synchronization: `A4`, `A6`, `A7`, `A9`), executed in the two-phase rollout above.**

### Why Senior Management Should *Not* Automate the Highest-Frequency Activity First
Senior management's instinct to automate `Receive application` (12,000 cases, already 72% digital, 0.2h wait) or `Register application` (`A1`, 11,880 cases, 8 min service time, 1.5h wait) mistakes **local task frequency** for **end-to-end process leverage**:

1. **Wrong resource pool:** `A1` saves labor in the **12-FTE Back office**, whose utilization already drops below 65% after 15:00. Saving minutes in an unconstrained pool yields little real throughput or cost-avoidance value.
2. **Worsens true bottlenecks:** Pushing registered applications faster into `Classify` and `Financial document review` increases arrival pressure on the **9-FTE Credit analysts** (central in the working-together network, already carrying 18.0h/20.0h/28.0h waits and 75–110 min tasks) and the **3-FTE Compliance specialists** (`Resolve entity mismatch`: 31.0h wait, 82% critical-path incidence). Automating `A1` first simply moves cases faster to a longer standstill.
3. **Leaves major waits and control risks untouched:** `A1` does nothing about the **58.0h external valuation synchronization wait** (71% of Equipment finance), the **6.5% illegal provisional signing**, **5.8% month-end four-eyes breaches**, or **8.5%/14% contract-remediation loops**.

### Defensible Trade-Off Argument for Portfolio B
Choosing **Portfolio B (`A4 + A6 + A7 + A9`)** deliberately trades away a visible, low-risk Back-office quick win (`A1`) and Year-1 portal nudges (`A5`, `A10`) in exchange for **systemic flow and control integrity**:
- **`A9` + `A4`** satisfy Compliance in Wave 1 by eliminating regulatory exposure (provisional signing, four-eyes breaches, contract mismatches) while unlocking the 31.0h `Resolve entity mismatch` critical path and relieving Legal ops month-end overload.
- **`A6`** directly expands capacity in the single most constrained internal group (Credit analysts), ensuring upstream improvements do not overload credit underwriting.
- **`A7`** attacks the single largest calendar-time delay (58.0h external valuation) through parallelization rather than expensive labor automation.

Once these four structural constraints and control gates are stabilized in Year 1, the bank can safely layer high-frequency intake (`A1`) and customer-nudging (`A5`, `A10`) in Year 2 into a process that can actually absorb the volume cleanly and compliantly.
