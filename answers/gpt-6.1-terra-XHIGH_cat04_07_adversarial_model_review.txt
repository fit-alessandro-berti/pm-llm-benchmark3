# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

The expressions are interpreted as standard process trees over complete, correctly ordered visible event sequences: `->` is sequence, `+` is parallel interleaving, `X` is exclusive choice, and `tau` is invisible. Acceptance means an exact complete visible trace is in the model language, not that an alignment returns a high score. Incomplete cases, ties in timestamps, missing events, and alignment repairs need separate treatment.

Exploration can tolerate a deliberately broad model if it is labeled as a hypothesis and exceptions remain visible. Enforcement needs a validated policy specification, reliable attributes and event capture, explicit uncertainty, and a low tolerance for both unlawful acceptance and unnecessary rejection. Simulation additionally needs calibrated arrival rates, branching probabilities, durations, resources, and rework probabilities; the control-flow expressions and reported metrics do not provide them. Stakeholder communication favors understandable, stable rules and examples, but perceived simplicity does not establish correctness.

Fitness measures accommodation of recorded behavior, whereas precision concerns behavior enabled beyond what the log supports; neither by itself establishes policy validity. A permissive exploratory model can trade precision for coverage. A compliance model cannot trade away mandatory checks just to improve fitness, and rejecting an unobserved combination requires a policy basis, not merely its absence from a sample. The log lacks the external/internal and affected-check attributes, so some decisions are inherently indeterminate without additional evidence.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

The strongest case for S is that its small structure handles both mandatory-check orders, the two approval choices, and combinations of supplier and expedited review without enumerating them. Optional branches can absorb absent events and keep exploration, alignment and discussion accessible. Its holdout fitness and runtime are encouraging for a clearly labeled exploratory summary.

The strongest case against S is that its language embeds missing-event tolerance as business permission. Every review branch can independently skip or occur in any interleaving, so the model cannot distinguish a missing recorded check from a genuinely unperformed check. This is a semantic defect for enforcement, and even an exploratory diagram can mislead if users mistake optionality for policy. It also has no correction activity, so it rejects legitimate rework; it cannot express the data condition on supplier review.

Examples of accepted visible traces, with Register Dossier and Release Order written in full:

1. `Register Dossier, Manager Approval, Release Order`: both mandatory checks skipped.
2. `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Auto Approval, Release Order`: Technical Review skipped.
3. `Register Dossier, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order`: Credit Check skipped.
4. `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order`: expedited review too early.
5. `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Expedite Review, Technical Review, Auto Approval, Release Order`: expedited review precedes completion of both checks.
6. `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Supplier Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` on a known internal order: supplier review is not authorized by P3 under the stated interpretation.
7. `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` on a known external order: required supplier review is missing.

Examples 6 and 7 are conditionally problematic, not universally forbidden based on the trace alone. Missing-event robustness is an intentional analytical accommodation only if represented as uncertainty or an alignment deviation. Treating actual mandatory checks as optional and allowing expedited review before them are accidental policy flaws if the model is presented as normative. S does enforce register first, exactly one approval and release last within its language, and it disallows repeated mandatory checks even when correction would justify them.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

The strongest case for C is that its explicit variants are easy to enumerate and audit individually. They enforce the mandatory checks, approval choice and release boundary in every listed branch, and the reported high training precision suggests limited enabled behavior relative to that log. A fixed approved variant catalogue can be useful for discovering changes that require review, especially where exceptions are consequential.

The strongest case against it is that the catalogue is not the policy: it confuses absence from training with prohibition. It omits, for example, alternate check order with Auto Approval, supplier review with Auto Approval, expedited review before supplier review, and most correction patterns. These are combinations of known choices, not necessarily new policy. The 0.229 training-to-holdout fitness drop is consistent with overfitting, although missing events, case mix, cost settings or split instability may also contribute. High precision on the training positives may reward memorizing variants while penalizing legitimate, unobserved ones; a curated negative suite and independently reviewed positive examples are needed to assess the error asymmetry.

Branch enumeration replicates activities and makes it easy for rules to drift between branches; the reported 33 duplicates are plausible but undefined. Duplicate visible labels complicate mapping alignments to business ownership, can increase routing ambiguity and structural size, and make edits and impact analysis harder. They are not automatically wrong and do not necessarily change visible trace acceptance. C has only one correction variant, which always repeats both checks in a fixed order, and cannot represent repeated corrections or selective rechecks. Neither its training precision nor its variant catalogue establishes audit readiness.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

The ranking is by potential effect on the governance decision, not a proven frequency in this dataset:

1. **Policy mismeasurement and optionality.** Fitness rewards S for skipping checks, even if that behavior is prohibited. Generalization can reward a broad model for reusing transitions even when its extra traces are unsafe. Training-only positive-log precision can reward C for suppressing rare compliant combinations. These can reverse a model choice if treated as policy scores.
2. **Missing-event and attribute bias.** Missing Supplier Review can make a compliant case appear noncompliant. Optionalizing supplier review hides the opposite case, an external order whose review really was omitted. Missing mandatory events produce the same ambiguity. No trace-only score recovers source type or the affected-check set; match to source systems and quantify capture rates.
3. **Split sensitivity or leakage.** Similar or related cases, case fragments, or future-derived preprocessing can cross the split; a single random split can also over- or under-represent rare combinations. No leakage is established here. Check case IDs, shared dossiers and temporal leakage, compare grouped and chronological splits, and report seed variability.
4. **Alignment cost and silent paths.** Different log-move, model-move and invisible-transition costs, normalization and tie-breaking can alter fitness, diagnostics and rankings. S's silent choices allow skipped mandatory actions at no visible model move; C's many routes can introduce alternative alignments. A high fitness alignment is not a direct statement that every event is allowed or performed.
5. **Duplicated labels and runtime confounding.** Duplicate labels may spread replay counts across transitions, complicate precision/generalization estimates and create multiple alignments; they do not by themselves imply lower precision. C's larger net and routing can drive runtime and memory, alongside trace length, algorithms, hardware and configuration. Define and measure these factors.
6. **Stakeholder-score methodology.** The informal ratings could reflect diagram size, familiarity, participant selection or leading questions rather than correct decisions. Use balanced tasks and blinded, randomized presentation; report accuracy, time and uncertainty as well as preference.

# 7. Third Alternative

Use a **procedural backbone with attribute-aware post-hoc policy checks**, named `model_third`. Its trace language is a conservative operational profile, not a claim to encode every ordering left unspecified by P1–P7:

```text
Register;
parallel(Credit, Technical);                    # both exactly once, either order
repeat zero or more times:
    Correct; one of {Credit, Technical, parallel(Credit, Technical)};
parallel(optional Supplier, optional Expedite); # either visible order
exactly one of Manager Approval or Auto Approval;
Release.
```

The optional Supplier branch is a *structural overapproximation* used because source type is absent from the discovery log. A separate check requires exactly one Supplier Review for an external case and none for an internal case. The expedited branch is optional, at most once, after both initial checks and all rechecks and before approval; it may precede or follow Supplier Review. Mandatory checks cannot repeat except inside a correction iteration. For each correction, the visible immediate recheck set must equal that correction's recorded affected set; one or both may be affected, with either order for both. Approval is exactly one of the two choices before release.

This profile assumes corrections occur after both initial checks and before Supplier or Expedite Review, and that every correction affects at least one mandatory check. P6 does not establish those restrictions or rule out corrections affecting only another review. Likewise P2–P4 do not require Supplier Review to wait for both initial checks. Thus an exact structural rejection can mean **outside the provisional profile**, not automatically a policy violation. Escalate such cases until stakeholders settle timing and affected-activity scope; revise the backbone if they are valid. The implementation also interprets each named review as occurring at most once per phase; determine explicitly whether repeated supplier/expedite reviews are allowed.

The following self-contained construction uses PM4Py's process-tree parser and loop semantics: the first loop child is the body, the second is the redo; a silent body permits zero or more `Correct`/recheck iterations. It assigns the process tree to `model_third`.

```python
import pm4py

model_third_string = """->(
  'Register Dossier',
  +('Credit Check', 'Technical Review'),
  *(tau, ->(
    'Correct Dossier',
    X('Credit Check', 'Technical Review',
      +('Credit Check', 'Technical Review'))
  )),
  +(X('Supplier Review', tau), X('Expedite Review', tau)),
  X('Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval'),
  'Release Order'
)"""
model_third = pm4py.parse_process_tree(model_third_string)
net_third, im_third, fm_third = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_third)
```

The following separate data checker is applied only after **exact structural acceptance**. Its input is an ordered list of event dictionaries, `source_type` equal to `external` or `internal`, and each `Correct Dossier` event carrying `affected_checks` as a list of exact activity names. A missing or malformed attribute yields `indeterminate`, not a pass. The checker returns explicit reasons; failures dominate uncertainty. It does not infer actual execution from absent log events or repair the trace.

```python
CHECKS = frozenset({'Credit Check', 'Technical Review'})

def check_data_policy(events, source_type, structural_acceptance):
    """Return (status, reasons) for the provisional profile.

    structural_acceptance must come from exact visible-trace membership,
    not a threshold on normalized alignment fitness.
    """
    if not structural_acceptance:
        return 'outside_profile', ['Trace is not in the procedural backbone']

    names = [event['concept:name'] for event in events]
    failures, unknowns = [], []
    suppliers = names.count('Supplier Review')
    if source_type == 'external':
        if suppliers != 1:
            failures.append('External order requires one Supplier Review')
    elif source_type == 'internal':
        if suppliers != 0:
            failures.append('Internal order must not have Supplier Review')
    else:
        unknowns.append('Missing or invalid source_type')

    for i, event in enumerate(events):
        if event['concept:name'] != 'Correct Dossier':
            continue
        raw = event.get('affected_checks')
        if (not isinstance(raw, (list, tuple, set)) or not raw
                or any(not isinstance(x, str) for x in raw)
                or not set(raw).issubset(CHECKS)
                or len(raw) != len(set(raw))):
            unknowns.append(f'Missing or invalid affected_checks at event {i}')
            continue
        j, observed = i + 1, []
        while j < len(names) and names[j] in CHECKS:
            observed.append(names[j])
            j += 1
        if set(observed) != set(raw) or len(observed) != len(raw):
            failures.append(f'Recheck set differs from affected_checks at event {i}')

    if failures:
        return 'fail', failures + unknowns
    if unknowns:
        return 'indeterminate', unknowns
    return 'pass', []
```

The list of rechecks immediately after each correction is unambiguous only within the provisional backbone; a wider policy may need explicit event-to-correction links. Structural rejection is routed for review, and data failure is a policy finding only after case attributes and event completeness have been verified. For historical logs missing these attributes, report a range of possible outcomes and the indeterminate count, rather than substituting guesses. For future execution, capture source type and affected set at decision time, including provenance and revisions. An external case lacking a visible supplier event cannot be adjudicated as skipped versus unlogged without independent evidence.

# 9. Validation Experiments

1. **Data and splits.** Publish a frozen event extract or governed reproducible access, case identifiers and grouping rules, time window, event mapping, lifecycle filters, missing-value rules, deduplication, timestamp tie-breaking, versioned code and PM4Py/dependency versions. Split by case and, where related cases share a dossier, by dossier; prevent preprocessing leakage. Publish exact split IDs under appropriate access controls and seeds, for example 17, 42 and 2026. Repeat grouped splits and an independently held-out chronological split; show variability and confidence intervals. Freeze model selection before the final test.
2. **Stratification and review.** Recover source type and affected-check sets from authoritative systems where possible, preserving provenance and missingness. Stratify by source, affected set, correction count, approval type and expedited/supplier combinations; include missing and disputed attributes as separate strata. Oversample rare groups for adjudication and report both subgroup rates and population-weighted rates, with uncertainty. Have independent policy experts label a blinded sample and resolve disagreement.
3. **Curated positive and negative cases.** Turn B1–B8 and A1–A5 into fixtures with explicit attributes and expected pass/fail/indeterminate/outside-profile outcomes. Add missing checks, both approvals, wrong endpoint, early expedite, internal supplier, external omission, unmatched or extra recheck and several corrections. Include permissible supplier/expedite and approval combinations. Record the policy authority and separate disputed cases. Measure false acceptance and false rejection, including critical violations separately. Test an unobserved combination of known choices, and entirely novel activity or genuinely new policy separately.
4. **Perturbations.** Inject controlled deletions of supplier, mandatory check and correction events, and duplicates of each, at documented rates and locations. Compare results against known unperturbed cases and independent execution records; report violations detected, false alarms and cases where evidence becomes indeterminate. Do not assume a deleted event implies execution. Vary event ordering when timestamps tie and test incomplete cases separately from completed cases.
5. **Costs and structural checks.** Fix and publish alignment algorithms, costs for log/model moves and invisible moves, normalization and timeouts. Repeat with multiple reasonable costs, including high cost for missing mandatory checks, and inspect per-event moves rather than only aggregates. Check workflow-net soundness, dead transitions, bounded reachability and expected exact traces; soundness is neither policy validity nor data completeness. Explore reachability with a documented state limit, reporting truncation. Validate each correction loop and both approval paths.
6. **Metrics and human use.** Report training and untouched holdout alignment fitness, alignment precision, token-based generalization and arc-degree simplicity separately, with definitions and settings. Include deviations by subgroup, negative-suite false acceptance, lawful-combination rejection, median and p95/p99 runtime, timeout and memory. Independently recount duplicate visible labels with a published rule. Assess comprehension in a preregistered, blinded, randomized crossover study using stakeholders from compliance, operations and IT; give equivalent legends and the same policy cases, measure answer accuracy, time and confidence, report sample sizes and uncertainty, and distinguish preference from correct interpretation.
7. **Decision rule.** Reject any model for compliance deployment, regardless of aggregate fitness or precision, if it accepts a verified critical forbidden behavior, fails a mandatory rule, silently treats a required attribute as optional, or cannot preserve indeterminate outcomes. Also block deployment for unacceptable false rejection of adjudicated valid cases, unsoundness, or unvalidated event/attribute completeness. Set explicit error tolerances and escalation ownership before evaluating the final holdout. Exploration may use a broader model with visible limitations and without automatic compliance decisions.

A PM4Py evaluation skeleton follows. Supply `train_log` and `test_log` as preprocessed PM4Py EventLogs, `model_s` and `model_c` parsed from the submitted expressions, and use exactly the same version and settings for all three. The soundness function's return shape and available alignment-cost configuration can vary by version; pin and document the installed API. The default metric calls below are a baseline, not a substitute for the cost-sensitivity experiments.

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.log.obj import Event, EventLog, Trace

models = {'S': model_s, 'C': model_c, 'third': model_third}

def make_log(cases):
    log = EventLog()
    for n, names in enumerate(cases):
        trace = Trace()
        trace.attributes['concept:name'] = f'fixture-{n}'
        for name in names:
            trace.append(Event({'concept:name': name}))
        log.append(trace)
    return log

probe_log = make_log([
    ['Register Dossier', 'Credit Check', 'Technical Review',
     'Manager Approval', 'Release Order'],
    ['Register Dossier', 'Manager Approval', 'Release Order'],
])

for name, model in models.items():
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    soundness = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=False)
    print(name, 'soundness:', soundness)
    print(name, 'training fitness:', pm4py.fitness_alignments(train_log, net, im, fm))
    print(name, 'holdout fitness:', pm4py.fitness_alignments(test_log, net, im, fm))
    print(name, 'training precision:', pm4py.precision_alignments(train_log, net, im, fm))
    print(name, 'holdout precision:', pm4py.precision_alignments(test_log, net, im, fm))
    print(name, 'token generalization:', pm4py.generalization_tbr(train_log, net, im, fm))
    print(name, 'arc-degree simplicity:',
          pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm, variant='arc_degree'))
    print(name, 'probe alignments:',
          pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(probe_log, net, im, fm))
```

For exact membership fixtures, inspect whether an alignment contains any **visible** log or model moves, permitting silent routing; a normalized fitness threshold is not equivalent. Alternatively use an exact reachability checker. Validate the checker's interpretation of move encodings for the pinned PM4Py version. For cost sensitivity use the lower-level alignment API for that version to explicitly set trace, visible model and silent transition costs; publish the configuration and compare decisions. Generate additional bounded traces through `pm4py.play_out` or reachability only after recording the sampling and loop bounds; sampled playout cannot prove absence of a forbidden trace. Apply `check_data_policy` separately to structurally accepted, attributed fixtures and report its three policy outcomes plus `outside_profile`.

# 10. Governance Recommendation

Allow S only as a prominently labeled exploratory view, with explicit warnings that mandatory checks are depicted as optional and that correction is absent. Do not use it to declare compliance or as an unqualified simulation specification. Use C only as a historical variant catalogue or drift-review aid, not as an enforcement model; its narrow branches are not a justified prohibition of all other combinations. Retire both from consideration as normative enforcement candidates in their present form, while retaining versioned artifacts and results for auditability.

Advance the third alternative as a candidate, not a deployed compliance model. Before deployment, stakeholders must resolve correction timing and scope, supplier timing, repetition and handling of missing or disputed events; the backbone must reflect those decisions. Validate the source and affected-check attributes against authoritative systems, establish complete and ordered event capture, run the stated structural, negative, positive, subgroup and sensitivity tests, and set reviewed error thresholds and escalation procedures. A pass requires both exact structural acceptance and a data-policy pass; indeterminate and outside-profile cases go to human review. A verified critical violation blocks automatic approval even if aggregate scores are excellent. If enforcement is prospective, ensure the actual operating controls can prevent or escalate violations, rather than relying only on retrospective log analysis.

# 11. Dissenting View

A compliance board could reasonably prefer C as a temporary controlled catalogue. In a regulated workflow, an unobserved combination may be a genuine change in operating practice, and the cost of accepting an unapproved order could exceed the cost of asking for review. C exposes each authorized route explicitly and its high training precision offers evidence that it does not casually enable many extra continuations. Its low holdout fitness may identify exactly the cases that require a process-owner decision, rather than demonstrate a defect. S, meanwhile, may be the most honest description of a noisy log if data capture is incomplete: forcing mandatory events into a model could turn integration defects into misleading accusations against employees. The proposed third model also imposes unapproved assumptions about when correction may occur and whether it must affect a mandatory check. Its attribute checker depends on data that are currently absent and potentially unreliable; even when present, affected-check values may be entered after the fact. A sound-looking backbone and more expressive choices could therefore create false confidence while hiding organizational disagreements. Until a reviewed policy and trustworthy execution data exist, the responsible course may be to keep both existing models as complementary investigative instruments, escalate deviations, and postpone selecting a single normative model.

I would change the recommendation if independent, representative adjudication showed that the missing combinations in C are in fact prohibited, that its holdout mismatches reliably identify material violations rather than incomplete logs, and that its operational and maintenance costs are acceptable. Conversely, validated source-system reconciliation, reliable affected-check capture, and stakeholder approval of the third model's timing assumptions would strengthen the case for deploying it. Evidence of frequent legitimate correction or supplier timing outside its profile would require changing the backbone first.
