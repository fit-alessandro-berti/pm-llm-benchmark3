**Least identifiable queue: QB.** A single visible preemption (B02) suggests preemptive priority, but B05 (same priority) does not preempt; B01 resumes twice despite lower priority; and B04 is selected over higher-priority and shorter jobs. Multiple observationally equivalent hybrids (FIFO + preemption exception + resumption mandate; non-preemptive priority + dispatcher override) fit the data. Confidence is lowest (60).

**Most dangerous policy to infer from these observations alone: “Preemptive priority” for QB.** The B02 event is highly salient—an explicit “urgent arrival” preemption is easy to observe and report. However, B05’s failure to preempt at 09:46, B01’s repeated resumption, and B04’s selection at 10:28 all show that the preemption rule is neither consistent nor dominant. Inferring pure preemptive priority would mis-predict every decision after 09:31. A close second is “SPT” for QC: the initial three jobs (C02, C03, C04) form a perfect SPT sequence, which could tempt an analyst to label the whole queue SPT, yet C05, C06, C01, and C07 all violate it in ways that require hidden constraints or a policy switch.

**Cross-queue pattern**: QA is best understood as a well-behaved FIFO line with a soft appointment insertion; QD is a multi-resource, skill-routed system with FIFO sub-queues and window-aware scheduling; QC behaves like SPT for a brief initial window then shifts to a reactive, deadline-sensitive mode; QB is the outlier—its visible events are dominated by interruptions and resumptions that defy any single documented mechanism. Any management claim that “each queue follows one documented rule” is least credible for QB and most credible for QD.
