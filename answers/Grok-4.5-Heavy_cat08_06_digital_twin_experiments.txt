**1. Model scope and state variables**

**Digital-twin boundary**  
The twin covers the municipal building-permit process from portal submission (“Submit application”) to final decision (“Issue permit” or “Reject application”). It includes completeness loops, parallel reviews (Planning, Fire-safety, Environmental), public-notice period (with duration proxy for unobserved objections), inspection scheduling and site visits (with travel and geographic batching), decision drafting, legal check, and rework/appeal loops.  

Out of scope: detailed pre-portal applicant document preparation (except the effect of intervention D1), post-rejection litigation beyond the modelled rework, internal processes of external consultants, precise GIS street-level routing (replaced by district-level travel times plus batch savings), and the rare emergency decisions already removed from the event log.

**Level of detail**  
Discrete-event simulation at the level of individual cases and activities. Control flow follows the supplied process tree (sequence `->`, exclusive choice `X`, parallel `+`, loops `*`) enriched by case-attribute guards and dynamic cross-case rules. Time is continuous (or minute-resolution). Cases are not independent: they compete for shared resources under calendars and interact via architect identity, district, and inspection bundles.

**Entities**  
- **Case**: identifier, `case_type` ∈ {Simple renovation, New residential, Commercial, Complex variance}, district, architect_id, application_value, requires_fire / requires_environment / requires_public_notice flags, submission time, current activity (or waiting state), rework counters (completeness, inspection, legal, appeal), list of related cases (same architect + district within a configurable time window), aging/priority flag, violation history (for D2), latent-objection risk factor.  
- **Resource pools**: Clerks (5 FTE), Planners (8 FTE, two restricted to Simple renovation), Fire reviewers (2 FTE), Environmental reviewers (3 FTE with 40 % non-permit capacity), Inspectors (4 FTE), Legal officers (2 FTE), System (unlimited). Each pool member carries a calendar, skill set and current assignment state.  
- **Queues**: activity- or pool-specific queues (completeness, planning, fire, environmental, inspection-scheduling, legal, …). Inspection-scheduling queue supports district batch formation. Optional aging-priority discipline (D6).  
- **External actors**: applicant response processes (missing-document return, inspection readiness) and third-party effects (public-notice extension).

**State variables**  
- Global: simulation clock, day-of-week, season, public-holiday flag, council-meeting-week flag, current arrival-intensity multiplier.  
- Per case: remaining service time (when in service), wait-start timestamp, accumulated service / waiting / external delay, status of parallel review branches, bundle membership.  
- Per resource pool: available capacity (accounting for part-time, reserved time, calendar), utilisation, busy/idle sets.  
- Queues: length, composition by case_type and district.  
- Cross-case: active inspection bundles (lists of cases per district/architect), recent architect submission counts, backlog by type and age.  
- Online KPIs: WIP, backlog aging distribution, pool utilisations, partial cycle-time statistics.

**Resources and calendars**  
Calendars are taken from the supplied HR data: Clerks Mon–Fri 08:00–16:00 (one clerk portal support on Mondays), Planners Mon–Fri 09:00–17:00, Fire Tue–Thu only, Environmental Mon–Fri with 40 % reserved, Inspectors field days Tue–Fri, Legal blacked out during council-meeting weeks (except urgent), System always available. Public holidays interrupt relevant activities. Capacity can be augmented temporarily (D4).

**Queues and cross-case dependencies**  
Municipal service queues are distinct from external applicant/third-party delays. Inspection scheduling implements optional geographic batching (D3): ready cases in the same district are grouped, travel time is reduced, but the first case in a bundle may wait for the others. Architect bursts are generated in the arrival process and can influence bundling preference. Neighborhood objection risk is a latent common factor that stochastically lengthens public-notice duration for cases sharing district and time window; no individual objection events are synthesised. Decision drafting is an explicit synchronisation point that waits for all required predecessors (Planning + applicable Fire/Env/Public-notice + Inspection).

This scope supports direct evaluation of all ten candidate interventions while respecting non-stationary demand and the listed data gaps.

**2. Parameter-estimation plan**

Use the provided event log (18 months, 31 200 cases) as primary source. PM4Py handles log/model discovery and descriptive statistics; a separate custom estimation layer produces simulation parameters (distributions, rates, calendars, dependency rules). Never rely on a nonexistent `pm4py.read_csv`; load via pandas then format.

**PM4Py log/model handling**  
1. Construct a pandas DataFrame from the CSV/excerpt (columns already match XES-style names).  
2. `df = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id="case:concept:name", activity_key="concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp")` (optionally supply start_timestamp_key if available).  
3. Parse the given process-tree string with `tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(process_tree_string)`. Note limitations of the simplified string: it omits multi-instance rework reasons, attribute-conditioned branches, and silent transitions for missing data; treat it as a control-flow skeleton that must be enriched by attribute-driven routing tables.  
4. Compute:  
   - variants = `pm4py.get_variants(df)` (or equivalent stats API)  
   - frequency DFG / start/end activities = `pm4py.discover_dfg(df)`  
   - performance DFG = `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(df)`  
   - case-arrival average = `pm4py.get_case_arrival_average(df)`  
   - if start timestamps exist for a subset: service-time statistics and waiting-time columns derived by lagging previous completion.  
5. Export variants, DFG edges, performance values and case-attribute distributions for the simulation parameter store. Keep PM4Py strictly for discovery/stats; the discrete-event engine never calls PM4Py at runtime.

**Arrivals (non-stationary)**  
Extract case start timestamps (first “Submit application”). Fit a non-homogeneous Poisson process (or piecewise-constant intensity) with seasonal multipliers (Mar–May & Sep–Nov ≈ 1.7× baseline Jan–Feb), day-of-week factors (Monday ≈ 2.2× Wednesday), and an architect-burst component (top-20 architect_id values = 31 % of cases; estimate weekly multi-submission probability and intra-week clustering). Stratify further by case_type mix. Generate future arrivals by thinning or time-varying rate functions.

**Service times**  
Where both start and complete timestamps exist, fit parametric distributions (log-normal, gamma, Weibull) stratified by activity, case_type, district and resource pool. Where only completion timestamps are recorded, service time cannot be observed cleanly; infer approximate service by residual analysis against resource calendars and estimated queue waits, or use expert/HR priors calibrated against observed sojourn. Explicitly document the inference bias and run sensitivity on the split ratio. System activities use near-deterministic short durations.

**Waiting times**  
Waiting = time from enabling (previous activity complete or AND-join of parallel reviews) until resource start (or completion if start missing). Estimate empirical waiting distributions per activity/queue conditioned on queue length, day-of-week and calendar availability. Applicant-driven waits are treated as external.

**Branching / routing probabilities**  
From frequency DFG and case attributes estimate conditional probabilities. Fire/Environmental/Public-notice use logistic or decision-tree models on observed combinations; incomplete fields (building area, sensitive district) use observed frequency as lower bound plus sensitivity ranges. Decision (Issue vs Reject) conditioned on preceding outcomes.

**Rework**  
Loop probabilities from the process-tree `*` operators and variant frequencies (Completeness, Inspection ≈ 9 %, legal, appeal). Condition on case_type; truncate maximum loops at observed quantiles.

**Resource calendars**  
Primary source is the supplied HR-derived calendars (not reconstructed from the event log). Map org:resource values to pools and validate utilisation against calendar availability. Public holidays and council weeks are explicit exceptions.

**Cross-case dependencies**  
Architect bundles: probability of multiple applications of the same architect_id + district in the same week estimated from co-occurrence. Inspection batching: potential travel-time reduction and extra wait for the first case of a geographic batch; fit empirical travel-time distributions by district (longer in D3/D5). Neighborhood objections: not observed as events; use Public-notice duration as a proxy (mixture of 14 d fixed minimum vs heavy-tailed extension) correlated with district and case_type; never invent objection event records.

**Applicant / third-party response times**  
Separate from municipal service: empirical distribution of Request → Receive missing documents (median 5 d, heavy tail). Fit log-normal or Pareto; allow D1 to shift this distribution.

All fitted distributions store parameters (and bootstrap uncertainty) in a configuration object consumed by the simulation engine. Missing-data bias is quantified by comparing subsets with vs without start timestamps and by sensitivity runs.

**3. Assumptions**

Assumptions are stated by category so that each can be sensitivity-tested or relaxed. They are deliberately conservative with respect to the known data gaps.

**Arrivals**  
Non-homogeneous Poisson (or piecewise-constant intensity) process whose rate is the product of a baseline, a seasonal multiplier (≈1.7× in March–May and September–November relative to January–February) and a weekday factor (Monday ≈2.2× Wednesday). Top-20 architects (31 % of volume) additionally generate clustered multi-case submissions within the same week; the remaining architects arrive more regularly. Case attributes (type mix 45/28/17/10, district, requires_* flags, application_value) are drawn from the empirical joint distribution, optionally conditioned on season and architect. No secular trend is assumed beyond the observed seasonality unless introduced in a stress scenario. Intervention D7 redistributes Monday peaks via appointment slots; D1 may slightly alter the quality mix of arriving cases.

**Service times**  
Where start and complete timestamps both exist, durations are fitted (log-normal or gamma) stratified by activity, case_type, district and resource. Where only completion timestamps are recorded, service time is inferred by residual analysis against resource calendars and estimated queue waits; the resulting split is treated as uncertain and is varied in sensitivity experiments. System tasks are near-deterministic. Travel time for Site inspection is district-dependent (longer for D3/D5) and is reduced by a calibrated factor under batching (D3). Parallel review services are drawn independently; the AND-join at Decision drafting waits for the slowest required branch.

**Routing**  
Control flow follows the given process tree. Branching probabilities (Fire, Environmental, Public-notice, Legal, Issue vs Reject) are estimated from observed frequencies conditioned on case attributes. Incomplete attributes (building area threshold, environmental sensitivity) are handled by probabilistic imputation or by using observed frequencies as lower bounds with explicit sensitivity ranges. Decision drafting is enabled only after all applicable predecessors have completed. Interventions alter guards or probabilities: D2 adds a deterministic skip of inspection for low-value Simple renovations without prior violations; D5 makes a legal pre-check parallel; D8 changes Environmental triage probabilities and service distributions.

**Resources / calendars**  
Exact FTE counts and shift patterns supplied by HR are used. Capacity is further reduced by the stated reservations (Environmental 40 %, Fire part-time, Legal council-week blackouts). Resources are multi-server pools with skill restrictions (two planners handle only Simple renovation). Work does not pre-empt at shift end; unfinished service resumes on the next available slot. Temporary capacity changes (D4) and priority reservation (D6) are implemented by dynamic adjustment of the available-capacity state variable.

**Rework**  
Loop probabilities are taken from the process-tree star operators and from variant frequencies (completeness, inspection ≈9 %, legal, appeal). Probabilities are conditioned on case_type (higher for Complex variance). Because rework reasons are unstructured, only frequency-based restart of the relevant sub-process is modelled; attribute updates (extra documents, etc.) may occur with small probability. Maximum loop counts are truncated at observed quantiles.

**Applicant / third-party response**  
“Receive missing documents” and inspection-readiness delays are external, heavy-tailed distributions (median 5 d from request) fitted independently of municipal clocks. Public-notice duration is a mixture: fixed 14-day minimum plus a heavy-tailed extension that proxies unobserved objections. The extension probability and magnitude are correlated with district and case_type; no individual objection events are generated. Intervention D1 shifts the missing-document probability and residual response distribution; D9 reduces the severity of the objection-extension component.

**Cross-case dependencies**  
Architect identity induces arrival clustering and optional bundling preference for inspection. District-level batching (D3) groups ready cases, reduces aggregate travel time, and introduces synchronisation delay for the first case of a batch. Neighborhood objection risk is a latent shared factor that lengthens public-notice for spatially and temporally proximate cases. Because the log is case-flattened, relatedness is reconstructed from attributes and time proximity rather than from observed links. Shared resource queues create additional statistical dependence.

**Non-stationary demand**  
The time-varying arrival intensity interacts with fixed calendars (Monday spikes meet limited Fire availability on Tue–Thu; seasonal peaks meet inspector capacity). Backlog can carry over between seasons. Stress scenarios amplify the seasonal multiplier or prolong high-demand periods.

**Missing data**  
- Start timestamps missing for many human activities → service/waiting split is inferred and subjected to sensitivity analysis; sojourn times are never treated as pure service.  
- Objection events absent → only the public-notice duration mixture is used as proxy; D9 acts on that mixture.  
- Rework reasons unstructured → frequency only.  
- Rare emergencies filtered → extremes may be under-represented.  
- Calendars from HR → assumed accurate but cross-checked via utilisation.  
- Incomplete attributes and flattened cross-case structure → probabilistic reconstruction with uncertainty bands.  

These assumptions permit relative comparison of D1–D10 under uncertainty; absolute predictions carry the acknowledged biases and are never claimed to be optimal or ground-truth.

**5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty treatment**

**Warm-up**  
Because demand is strongly non-stationary (seasonal + weekly + bursts) and the system starts empty, a pure steady-state assumption is invalid. Use a long warm-up covering at least one full seasonal cycle plus initial transient: recommended 180 days (or until WIP and age-of-backlog statistics stabilize within 5% by visual inspection / Welch’s method on cumulative averages of cycle time and queue lengths). Discard all statistics collected before warm-up end. Alternatively, initialize with a realistic starting backlog sampled from the empirical distribution of open cases at a representative date (if reconstructible from log). Prefer starting warm-up in a low-demand period when possible.

**Run length**  
After warm-up, simulate a horizon of 24–36 months to capture multiple seasonal cycles, council-meeting patterns, and rare long-tail variants. This allows reliable estimation of yearly averages, peak-period performance, and yearly SLA compliance.

**Replications**  
40–50 independent replications per experiment configuration. Use common random numbers (CRN) across policies for the same replication index to reduce variance of differences. Different seeds for arrival streams, service times, applicant delays, objection proxies, and resource interruptions.

**Uncertainty treatment**  
- Confidence intervals: 95% t-intervals on means of key metrics (median cycle time is reported via quantile estimation across replications; use batch means or replication means). For quantiles (p90, p95) report percentile-of-replication-quantiles or use bootstrap within replications.  
- Heavy-tailed durations: Applicant response (Receive missing docs), Environmental review, Public-notice extensions, and Complex rework exhibit heavy tails. Fit mixture distributions (lognormal + Pareto or empirical with kernel smoothing) or use phase-type approximations; avoid pure exponential. Sensitivity: re-run with truncated vs. untruncated tails and with bootstrap resampling of empirical residual times.  
- Parameter uncertainty: For quantities inferred from incomplete data (service/wait split when only completion timestamps exist, objection extension probability, travel-time savings under batching, eligibility fraction for D2), perform one-way and multi-way sensitivity analysis (low/base/high levels). Propagate via additional experimental factors or Latin-hypercube sampling of uncertain parameters.  
- Structural uncertainty: Compare process-tree driven routing vs. more flexible data-aware guards; test alternative batching windows.  
- Missing-data scenarios: Explicit scenarios that vary the inferred start-time model and the objection-proxy mixture weights.  
- Output: Report point estimates, 95% CIs, and sensitivity tornado diagrams for the most influential uncertain parameters. Do not claim statistical significance without the intervals; emphasize that results are conditional on the calibrated model and assumptions.

Random seeds: Fixed master seed per replication; sub-streams allocated to independent generators (arrivals, services, routes, external delays) for reproducibility and CRN.

**6. Validation strategy**

Validation acknowledges that the digital twin is an approximation under data gaps (missing starts, missing objection events, flattened cross-case links, reconstructed calendars). No claim of exact replication is made; the goal is sufficient fidelity for relative ranking of interventions.

**Face validation**  
Process owners and domain experts review the process-tree enrichment, routing rules, resource calendars, and intervention implementations (D1–D10). Walk-through of a few synthetic traces (Simple clean path, Complex with rework + objection proxy) to confirm logical plausibility of state transitions, parallel reviews, and batching logic.

**Event-log replay / trace-level validation**  
Align a held-out subset of real cases against the simulated control-flow using the process tree (PM4Py alignments or token-based replay on the converted Petri net). Measure fitness and precision; investigate systematic deviations (e.g., missing rare emergency paths that were filtered from the log). For timing, replay observed arrival sequences into the simulation with frozen stochastic seeds and compare sojourn times activity-by-activity where start timestamps exist.

**Distributional validation**  
Compare simulated vs empirical distributions of case duration (overall and by case_type / variant group), activity service times (where observable) and sojourn times, waiting times before key queues (Completeness, Planning, Inspection, Legal), and inter-arrival times stratified by season and weekday. Use Kolmogorov–Smirnov / Anderson–Darling tests or visual Q-Q / CDF plots; accept approximate match within confidence bands that reflect estimation uncertainty. Heavy tails (Complex variance p90 260 d, objection-extended public notice) receive special attention; report coverage of extreme quantiles.

**Resource-utilisation validation**  
Simulate the baseline under historical arrival intensity and compare average and peak utilisation of each pool (clerks, planners, fire, environmental, inspectors, legal) against calendar capacity and any available HR utilisation figures. Check that Monday portal spikes and seasonal peaks produce realistic queue build-ups without systematic under- or over-utilisation bias.

**Bottleneck validation**  
Rank activities/queues by simulated waiting contribution and by empirical median/p90 waiting from the log summary. Confirm that known bottlenecks (Planning review district/type-dependent queues, Inspection travel + availability, Legal council-week effects, Environmental variability) appear in the same order of magnitude. Cross-case batching should demonstrably reduce inspector travel time while increasing wait for the first case of a batch.

**Out-of-sample / temporal validation**  
Split the 18-month log into training (first 12–14 months) and test (last 4–6 months). Estimate all parameters on training only; simulate the test period under the observed non-stationary arrival pattern and compare predicted vs realised case-duration distributions, backlog trajectories, and SLA compliance by case_type. Additional stress: inject a synthetic demand surge or calendar disruption not present in training and verify qualitative behaviour matches expert expectation.

**Uncertainty and sensitivity**  
Propagate parameter uncertainty (bootstrap of arrival rates, service distributions, branching probs) through the validation metrics; report intervals rather than point matches. Sensitivity on critical assumptions: service/waiting split ratio, objection-extension probability, architect-burst intensity, incomplete environmental-sensitivity flags. If validation metrics change ranking of interventions under plausible ranges, flag those interventions as fragile.

All validation artefacts (alignment scores, distribution plots, utilisation tables, OOS error metrics) are stored and reviewed before any intervention ranking is accepted. The twin is considered “fit for comparative purpose” when relative ordering of D1–D10 under baseline and stress scenarios is stable across the sensitivity ranges, not when absolute durations match the log to the day.

**7. Decision criteria**

Primary decision metrics (estimated per replication then aggregated):  
- Cycle-time distribution by case type: median, p75, p90, p95 (days from Submit to Issue/Reject). Focus on reduction relative to baseline.  
- SLA compliance: % of cases finishing within type-specific targets (illustrative and stakeholder-tunable: Simple 30 d, New residential 90 d, Commercial 120 d, Complex 180 d). Also overall and by district.  
- Tail risk: expected excess over SLA, or CVaR of cycle time.  
- Resource utilization: average and peak (95th percentile daily) for each pool (Clerks, Planners, Fire, Env, Inspectors, Legal). Target band 60–85% average; avoid sustained >90%.  
- Backlog & aging: average WIP, max backlog, mean/p90 age of open cases, number of cases >2× SLA.  
- Rework incidence: fraction of cases with ≥1 completeness / inspection / legal / appeal rework; average rework loops.  
- Fairness: cycle-time disparity across districts (e.g., ratio p90_D3 / p90_D1 or Gini coefficient of case durations by district/architect size); avoid interventions that systematically disadvantage small architects or certain districts.  
- Inspection efficiency: average travel time, batch size realized, no-show impact (if modeled).  
- Robustness: performance degradation under stress scenarios S1–S3 (e.g., % increase in p90 under 2.5× demand).

Decision thresholds / selection rules (illustrative, to be refined with stakeholders):  
- Prefer intervention (or combo) that simultaneously: (a) reduces median cycle time ≥10% and p90 ≥15% for at least the two highest-volume types without increasing any type’s p90 by >5%; (b) raises SLA compliance by ≥8 percentage points overall; (c) keeps all resource utilizations ≤85% average and ≤95% peak; (d) does not worsen fairness metrics by >10%; (e) reduces rework rate or leaves it stable; (f) shows graceful degradation under S1–S3 (p90 increase <30% of baseline increase).  
- Cost-effectiveness proxy: rank by “days saved per added FTE-month” for capacity interventions (D4) and by cycle-time gain per complexity for process changes.  
- Multi-criteria: use weighted score (cycle-time 40%, SLA 20%, utilization/fairness 15% each, robustness 10%) or Pareto front of (p90 reduction, utilization, fairness). Present full distribution of outcomes rather than single ranking.  
- Sensitivity filter: discard any intervention whose ranking changes under plausible ranges of the top 3 uncertain parameters (objection rate, service/wait split, D2 eligibility).  
- Final recommendation must be framed as “under the calibrated model and stated assumptions, D10 (or X) yields the most robust improvement on the primary metrics; however, validation against hold-out periods and pilot implementation are required before full deployment. Simulation does not guarantee optimality.”

Secondary: applicant experience (external wait reduction), municipal cost (temp staff, system changes), and qualitative risk (quality under D2, data needs for D8/D9).

These criteria explicitly incorporate the non-stationary peaks, cross-case effects (batching fairness, architect impact), and data-gap uncertainty.

def parse_process_tree() -> ProcessTree:
    """
    Parse the simplified process-tree string.
    Limitations: the string is a control-flow skeleton only; it does not encode
    attribute-conditioned guards, multi-instance rework reasons, missing-start
    inference, or cross-case batching. These must be supplied by the routing
    and resource layers of the simulation engine.
    """
    tree_str = """->(
  'Submit application',
  *(
    ->(
      'Completeness check',
      X(
        tau,
        ->('Request missing documents','Receive missing documents')
      )
    ),
    'Completeness rework'
  ),
  +(
    'Planning review',
    X(tau,'Fire-safety review'),
    X(tau,'Environmental review')
  ),
  X(tau,'Public-notice period'),
  *(
    ->('Inspection scheduling','Site inspection'),
    'Inspection rework'
  ),
  'Decision drafting',
  X(tau,'Legal check'),
  X('Issue permit','Reject application')
)"""
    tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_str)
    return tree
