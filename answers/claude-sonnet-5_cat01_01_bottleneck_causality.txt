## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional verdict: partly causal through mechanisms — confidence 55.**

The crude comparison (Orion mean 21.96h vs Nova 15.24h) is confounded by case mix: Orion receives 92.9% night-shift, 42.9% high-complexity, 64.3% compliance-required cases versus Nova's 25%/12.5%/25%. Once complexity is stratified, the sign flips in every layer (High: 31.57 vs 35.00; Medium: 17.17 vs 19.38; Low: 7.50 vs 9.64 — Orion faster each time). A complexity-standardized contrast using pooled weights (Low 0.40, Medium 0.33, High 0.27) gives Orion 17.14h vs Nova 19.65h — a reversal of roughly 9 hours from the crude gap. This is classic Simpson's-paradox structure and it is the strongest evidence *against* a simple "Orion causes delay" story.

Stratification is not identification, however. Shift is almost collinear with group (13/14 Orion cases are night), so "Orion advantage" and "night-shift advantage" cannot be cleanly separated with this data; a within-complexity night-vs-day comparison independently shows night faster (e.g., Medium: 17.31h night vs 19.77h day), which could explain part of Orion's apparent edge without any causal contribution from the resource-group label itself. Review-service time is nearly identical across groups within complexity; the gap lives almost entirely in queue wait — pointing to a capacity/queueing mechanism, not reviewer competence.

The 50% reassignment could **backfire**: it would shift hard, night-heavy, compliance-heavy cases to a pool (Nova) that has historically not handled that mix, without addressing the queueing mechanism that actually produces Orion's within-stratum speed advantage.

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests

| rank | hypothesis | process mechanism | evidence supporting it | evidence contradicting it | discriminating analysis or falsification test | predicted observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Genuine harmful Orion effect | Orion's night-heavy operation or overload causes intrinsically slower processing regardless of case mix | Crude aggregate gap (21.96 vs 15.24) | Within every complexity stratum Orion is *faster*, not slower | Propensity-score matching on complexity+shift+load, then re-estimate group effect | If harmful, matched effect should stay positive (Orion slower); data suggests it will flip negative |
| 2 | Beneficial Orion effect masked by case-mix | More certified reviewers give Orion higher effective capacity, shown once complexity is held constant | Stratified/standardized reversal (std. Orion 17.14h vs Nova 19.65h); lower wait in every stratum | Small cell sizes (Orion Low n=2, Nova High n=2) limit confidence; unobserved case risk could still favor Orion within labeled complexity | Within-route comparison: compare cases with identical trace variant and compliance flag across groups; sensitivity analysis for unobserved case risk (e.g., Rosenbaum-bounds style) | If genuine, gap should survive tightening on trace-variant-level matches and be robust to plausible risk confounding strength |
| 3 | Shift/load-centered explanation | Night shift has structurally different queueing dynamics (less contention despite "High" load label), and Orion's near-total night coverage borrows this advantage | Within-complexity night cases faster than day cases (Medium: 17.31 vs 19.77) | Only 1 Nova night High case — very thin evidence at High complexity | Compare Orion-day (C02) and any future day-assigned Orion cases against Orion-night on wait/cycle; run pilot cases across both shifts for both groups | If shift, not group, drives speed, Orion-day cases should look like Nova-day cases, not Orion-night cases |
| 4 | Compliance/rework mediation | Compliance checks and Request-Info rework loops explain most of the wait/cycle differential, and are policy- not group-driven | Compliance required tracks complexity almost identically in both groups; rework rate matches complexity (50% High both groups) | Within-group compliance-required vs not comparison (Medium/Orion) shows *no* wait penalty for compliance | Front-door-style decomposition: cycle_time = wait(compliance path) + wait(no-compliance path) + service + rework, compared across groups holding complexity fixed | If mediation dominant, group differences in cycle time should shrink to near zero after conditioning on compliance_required and rework_count |
| 5 | Data selection / measurement artifact | Only completed cases observed; abandoned/still-open cases (possibly disproportionately hard Orion cases) are censored, biasing the visible Orion sample toward its faster completions | Explicit note that only completed cases are present; no throughput/backlog data | No direct evidence of differential abandonment — purely a plausibility argument | Negative control: check whether case volume/arrival counts (if obtainable) match completed counts per group per period; reconstruct queue-state from timestamps if available | If selection is material, completed-case average will diverge sharply from any reconstructed all-arrivals average, especially for Orion |

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Instrumentation | Add activity-level timestamps (queue entry/exit, compliance start/end, review start/end, request-info loop) and log arrival/abandonment counts, not just completions | Selection/measurement gap; queue_wait vs review_service decomposition | None (passive data capture) | Data-governance sign-off; no case routing changes | Reconstruct true DFG performance (not just control-flow), backlog/abandonment rates by group | Establishes covariates needed for matching and quantifies selection bias magnitude | N/A — purely observational, no operational risk |
| 2. Small randomized/quasi-random routing pilot | Randomize (or alternate-ID quasi-randomize) a capped share (~10–15%) of new Medium-complexity cases between Orion and Nova, stratified by shift where staffing allows | Direct effect of resource_group net of complexity/shift confounding | Case-level, stratified randomization within complexity×shift cells | Exclude urgent/high-risk claims from randomization; weekly monitoring with pre-set stopping thresholds (e.g., cycle time +20% sustained, appeal rate doubling) | Compare wait, service, rework, appeal rate by randomized arm; compare DFG variants | Highest — only design that breaks routing_ruleresource_group confounding and yields a defensible causal estimate | Halt immediately if pilot arm shows sustained quality degradation or cycle-time increase beyond threshold |
| 3. Queue/compliance capacity intervention | Add on-call certified reviewer capacity to Nova during high-load/night windows; parallelize compliance scheduling with review prep instead of relabeling cases | queue_wait, compliance_performed timing — independent of group label | Shift/time-block level (staffing), not case-level relabeling | Track staffing cost/overtime; ensure utilization targets met before judging effect | Pre/post wait_before_review_h by group and complexity; utilization and throughput rates | Tests whether capacity, not identity, explains the gap — informs whether scaling capacity beats reassignment | Roll back added staffing if wait times don't improve after a defined trial window despite adequate utilization |
| 4. Conditional rollout | Only after Phases 2–3, scale reassignment or capacity investment proportional to the estimated causal effect and its confidence interval | Whichever mechanism (skill, shift, capacity) is empirically dominant | Policy-level, phased | Pre-registered decision rule based on pilot CI | Ongoing DFG/variant monitoring for drift | Converts pilot learning into low-risk, evidence-based policy | Any sustained quality or backlog regression reverts to Phase-3 baseline |

**Final recommendation:** Do not implement the blanket 50% reassignment. The evidence shows a Simpson's-paradox-like reversal — Orion is slower in aggregate only because it absorbs harder, night-heavy, compliance-heavy cases; within every complexity stratum it is faster, and the gap is concentrated in queue wait rather than review service. Reassigning cases wholesale would move hard/night-conditioned work into a pool without matching capacity or shift coverage, risking a real cycle-time and quality regression rather than an improvement. Instead, run the three-phase plan: instrument the process to close the selection/measurement gap, execute a small randomized pilot to obtain an unconfounded direct-effect estimate, and separately test whether adding capacity/compliance efficiency to Nova narrows the gap without touching the resource-group label. Use the pilot's confidence interval, not the raw aggregate means, to size any eventual reallocation.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Unobserved case risk within recorded complexity bands.** Routing is described as "usually" — not always — sending harder cases to Orion, so even within a labeled complexity tier Orion cases could be systematically harder. *Most valuable addition:* a finer objective severity score (e.g., claim value, medical/legal flags) recorded independent of the coarse complexity label.

2. **Shift/resource-group collinearity.** With 13/14 Orion cases at night, shift and group effects cannot be separated in the current data. *Most valuable addition:* deliberately staff and record a batch of Orion-day and Nova-night cases (or the randomized pilot in Phase 2) to break this collinearity.

3. **True queueing exposure vs. coded arrival_load.** "High/Normal" arrival_load is a categorical snapshot, not a continuous measure of contention at the moment of assignment. *Most valuable addition:* timestamped queue-length or concurrent open-case counts per group per hour.

4. **Selection into the completed-case sample.** No information exists on abandoned, reassigned, or still-open cases. *Most valuable addition:* case inflow logs (all cases entering Classify, regardless of completion status) to compute true completion/abandonment rates by group.

5. **Sparse quality signal.** Only 2 of 30 cases show an appeal outcome, making any quality comparison underpowered. *Most valuable addition:* a longer observation window or a broader quality proxy (e.g., supervisor QA score, reopen rate) with higher event frequency than 30-day appeals.
