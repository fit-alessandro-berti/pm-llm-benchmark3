# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

**Increase concern:** Native access logs show the same authenticated human creating and approving a PO, or changing bank details and approving payment, after correcting bot/shared-account mappings. V-77x bank changes lack a valid maintenance ticket or vendor-authenticated instruction; callback records are missing; beneficiary accounts recur across unrelated vendors; payments reached a new account before verification. Original timestamps confirm unusually short review intervals and no intervening checks. Emergency flags were added after approval, delegated authority was absent, or original justifications were missing, signed by the override initiator, or completed more than three working days later. Repeated loops show no substantive correction, or a payment was released despite unresolved exceptions.

**Reduce concern:** Immutable transaction and login records identify separate authorized humans; Bot_PO and legacy_erp mapping versions account for the PO flags; the shared Approver_Gita account resolves to distinct people with proper authority. V-77x change tickets, vendor confirmations, callback logs, and bank-account validation explain the concentrated maintenance and payment timing. Source records show the 12 quarter-close justifications were independently signed on time before upload. Incident records and comparable urgent-case cohorts explain emergency speed, while approval comments and version histories show real review in subcontracting loops. Recalculate all rates after these corrections, with explicit denominators and overlap among flagged groups.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

The procurement-to-payment log contains control exceptions and concentrated work patterns that warrant targeted review, but it does not establish collusion or other misconduct. Frequent pairing among the routine buyer–approver teams is consistent with their workloads. The fast Carla–Gita and Dan–Hugo approvals also have plausible operational explanations: both groups handle urgent categories, and emergency handling can legitimately shorten elapsed time. Their apparent four-eyes exceptions and late or same-person retrospective justifications nevertheless require case-level verification against the stated policy. An emergency override permits same-day action; it does not, by itself, remove the requirement for independent review.

The most concerning subgroup is the V-77x bank-change/payment population associated with VendorMaster_Lena and AP_Kim. It combines vendor concentration, a high share of large amounts, manual uploads, short change-to-approval intervals, and possible bank-change/payment four-eyes violations. The nine flagged same-person cases must be linked to specific cases and authenticated users before they are attributed to this pair. The recent banking-platform migration may explain many changes, but its supporting tickets and case links are absent from the event log.

We recommend first reconciling identity and timing with native audit trails, then examining bank-change authorization, vendor confirmation, independent payment approval, and the emergency justifications. Review the remaining PO four-eyes flags using bot and legacy mapping records, and compare emergency timing with similar urgent work. Shared accounts, bot mappings, and quarter-close batch entry limit the reliability of current counts and timestamps. Findings should distinguish verified control breaches, documented emergency exceptions, and data-quality corrections before any conclusion about intent is considered.
