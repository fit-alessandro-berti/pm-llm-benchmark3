## 2. Rule Interpretation

**RR1 — Drafter/approver separation**
For every case c, if Draft_Change_Request(c, h) and Approve_Change(c, h) occur, then after alias normalization h  h.

**RR2 — Implementer/verifier separation**
If Implement_Change(c, h) and Verify_Implementation(c, h) occur, then h  h.

**RR3 — High-risk approval authority**
If Risk(c)=HIGH and Approve_Change(c, h) occurs at time t, then either:
- h holds the QM standing role at t (Asha from 2026-01-01; Eva never), or
- h is a ValidQMDelegate at t, defined as:
  1. Delegation D exists with principal P  h.
  2. Effective interval of D covers t.
  3. Scope of D covers c’s scope (e.g., production-equipment, training-document, or specific incident).
  4. P is absent or formally unavailable at t **when required by D** (D-QM-01 and D-QM-02 require this; D-IC-03 does not list it but is scope-bound).
  5. No prohibited subdelegation occurred (e.g., D-IC-03 explicitly prohibits subdelegation; D-IC-04 is therefore invalid).

A later database entry (e.g., D-QM-01 DB record 2026-08-12 09:15) **cannot** prove an earlier delegation existed before 2026-08-10. The signed PDF (2026-08-09 18:00) is the primary temporal evidence.

**RR4 — Emergency substitution**
If Risk=HIGH, Emergency=true, and approval/implementation occur:
- Approve_Change must be by IC or ValidICDelegate at t (D-IC-03 for Farid is valid for CI-44 within 12:00–18:00 on 2026-08-11; D-IC-04 is invalid subdelegation).
- Retrospective_QM_Review must occur within **two business days** of implementation. Business days = Monday–Friday excluding site holidays (none recorded 2026-08-10 to 2026-08-16). Counting starts on the first business day after implementation.
  - Example CHG-505 (implemented 2026-08-11): business day 1 = 2026-08-12; business day 2 = 2026-08-13. Deadline: 2026-08-13 23:59 UTC. Review on 2026-08-14 16:00 is **late**.
- The reviewer must be QM or a ValidQMDelegate at the review time, with matching scope.

**Principal on leave / acting during absence**
A principal retains standing authority regardless of leave status unless formally revoked. However, if a principal performs an action during a documented absence (e.g., Asha on annual leave 2026-08-10 to 08-14 with VPN session 08-13), the action is technically within role authority but creates an **identity/authentication ambiguity** that must be resolved (credential sharing cannot be excluded). It also undermines the absence condition required for concurrent delegations.

**RR5 — Identity evidence**
`svc_qms` is not a human. It supports conformance only if reliable session evidence (badge, FIDO token, workstation assignment, individual re-auth) maps the action to an authorized human at t. Aliases are normalized before SoD checks.

**RR6 — Suspicion versus proof**
Repeated resource pairs, reciprocal approvals, shared workstations, concentrated handovers, and absence conflicts are **indicators** requiring investigation. They do not, by themselves, prove collusion. Proof requires supporting session, credential, or documentary evidence linking actions to specific humans with intent to circumvent controls.

## 6. Control Improvements

1. **Delegation creation and immutability:** Require digitally signed, timestamped delegation documents **before** the effective interval begins. Store them in an append-only ledger; prohibit post-hoc database migration records from serving as evidence. The D-QM-01 PDF (08-09) should be the canonical record; the 08-12 DB entry must be flagged as non-authoritative for prior events.

2. **Effective-time recording and scope enforcement:** Encode delegation scopes explicitly (e.g., `production_equipment`, `training_document`, `CI-44`) in the authorization system. Block approval/verification actions automatically when the case scope does not match the delegate’s active scope.

3. **Principal availability and absence tracking:** Integrate HR leave/sick-leave systems with the QMS in real time. If a principal is on documented leave, the system should either (a) enforce delegation-only approvals or (b) require an explicit “return-to-work/override” authentication with enhanced logging when the principal acts. Asha’s VPN session during leave should trigger an automatic audit flag.

4. **Alias management:** Enforce a single canonical user ID per human. Reject logins or actions using deprecated aliases (`b.koch`) at the application layer; normalize centrally before any SoD evaluation.

5. **Individual authentication and shared workstation elimination:** Eliminate `svc_qms` for verification/approval activities. Require individual FIDO/token re-authentication for every critical action (`Approve_Change`, `Verify_Implementation`, `Retrospective_QM_Review`). Disable shared workstation default accounts; tie every session to a named human via token binding.

6. **Emergency review automation:** Automate the RR4 retrospective review schedule upon emergency approval. Calculate the 2-business-day deadline automatically (excluding weekends/holidays) and escalate if not completed. Block retrospective reviews by delegates whose scope does not cover the case (e.g., Ben on `cooling_system`).

7. **Pair rotation and concentration thresholds:** Implement rotation policies for HIGH-risk production-equipment approvals. If a drafter-approver pair exceeds a defined frequency (e.g., >30% of cases in 60 days), require an independent second review or automatic escalation to the principal QM.

8. **Network monitoring as indicator only:** Use `pm4py` handover and working-together networks for periodic monitoring, but configure alerts to trigger **investigations**, not automatic sanctions. Combine network alerts with session/auth evidence before any disciplinary action.

9. **Evidential retention and correlation:** Retain session logs, badge entry/exit, workstation assignment, VPN endpoint/geolocation, FIDO/token usage, and CCTV correlation for all HIGH-risk and emergency cases. Ensure retention covers at least the delegation interval plus the retrospective review window.
