# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

### Evidence that would **increase concern** (warranting escalation to forensic review or formal investigation)
* **Unverified or diverted bank details (`V-77x`):** Vendor master change logs showing `Change Vendor Bank Details` updated IBAN/account numbers to entities unrelated to `V-77x`'s documented banking-platform migration, or bank accounts reverted to the original account shortly after `Release Payment` (consistent with the 11 `Lena  Kim  Lena` loops).

* **Vendor distress or payment anomalies:** Accounts payable inquiries or vendor statements from `V-77x` reporting unpaid invoices for cases marked `Release Payment` and `Close Case`, or duplicate payments across regional vendor IDs in the `V-77x` family.
* **Deliberate control circumvention in emergency channels:** Non-emergency purchases repeatedly flagged `emergency_flag=True`, split just below `amount_band` approval thresholds, or lacking verifiable `Goods Receipt` / work-order evidence—particularly where `entered_by == approved_by` in non-`legacy_erp` records.

* **Verified `V-77x` migration documentation:** Official bank-migration letters from `V-77x`, authenticated via independent pre-existing vendor contacts, matched to IT/vendor-master mass-maintenance tickets covering the exact dates, regions, and `vendor_id`s in the 44 `Lena + Kim` cases, plus confirmation from `V-77x` that all payments were received.

Internal Audit reviewed last quarter’s procurement-to-payment event log to evaluate control adherence, approval turnaround times, and resource interaction patterns. Based on currently available log data, the observed anomalies are most consistent with a combination of legitimate operational specialization, emergency-handling pressures, data-quality limitations, and targeted segregation-of-duties control exceptions. Internal Audit recommends targeted substantive testing of the V-77x cases—inspecting bank-change tickets, independent vendor callbacks, manual-upload logs, and payment release records—alongside sampling non-emergency four-eyes exceptions and remediating shared-account, mapping, and justification-tracking controls.
