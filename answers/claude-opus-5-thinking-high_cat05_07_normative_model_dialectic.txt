## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### 1.1 What M0 actually says (semantics before argument)

Before anything can be called a "deviation," the model's semantics must be stated precisely, because several apparent findings dissolve under correct reading of POWL.

- `checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])` has **no edge** between its two nodes. In POWL, unordered nodes in a strict partial order are **concurrent**: any interleaving is permitted. Therefore `Quality_Review` before `Sanctions_Check` is **not** a deviation from M0. Any conformance report that flags the order of these two activities is reporting an artifact of a sequential replay heuristic, not a violation.
- An edge between two *submodels* (`M0.order.add_edge(checks, decision)`) means **every** activity in the source must complete before **any** activity in the target. So M0 requires both checks to complete before the XOR decision — which means before `Manual_Procurement_Approval`, before `Activate_Supplier`, and before `Reject_Supplier`.
- The XOR is between the *approved branch* and *reject*. There is no third route, no `Auto_Approve`, no provisional activation, no `Request_More_Info`, no `QA_Clearance`, no reactivation entry, and no withdrawal.
- Every node in M0 is mandatory. `Register_Supplier`, `Notify_Requester`, and `Close_Case` cannot be skipped; nothing is silent; nothing repeats. M0 has **zero** tolerance for optionality, iteration, or duplicate events.
- M0 encodes **only** control flow. It carries no risk class, no amount, no date, no actor identity, no rule version. CS-9's own segregation-of-duties rule is, by the question's stipulation, absent from M0.

Consequence: M0 is a *partial* formalization of CS-9, and CS-9 is itself incomplete with respect to observed case types. Conformance to M0 is therefore neither necessary nor sufficient for compliance with CS-9. SUP-712 fits M0 perfectly and violates CS-9; SUP-709 violates M0 and may violate nothing at all.

### 1.2 Stipulated facts (established, not seriously contestable)

1. CS-9 is a controlled, formally approved standard, in force from 2025-01-01 through the entire observation period. It was never amended.
2. PD-3 exists only as steering-committee minutes plus a follow-up email, with an effective window of 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31. It was never incorporated into CS-9 or M0.
3. FD-7 exists as a written directive with an explicit scope (critical relief suppliers), a hard window (2026-07-10 00:00 to 2026-07-12 23:59), a spend cap (EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval), and named compensating controls (daily monitoring, quality review within two business days). It expired automatically. No written extension exists.
4. Notification capture moved to CRM on 2026-07-01; onboarding-log notification missingness rose from 2% to 29%; roughly 70% of notifications are mirrored back.
5. Trace contents and their recorded orderings are as tabulated. Case dates are as stated.
6. SUP-703 involved a HIGH-risk new foreign supplier and a EUR 180,000 first purchase, and PD-3 eligibility is restricted to LOW-risk DE suppliers in an approved corporate group. The eligibility failure is a matter of attribute comparison, not interpretation.
7. SUP-708 records `Activate_Supplier` forty minutes before `Sanctions_Check`, with no pilot or emergency instrument applicable on its date.
8. SUP-711's two `Register_Supplier` rows carry the same source transaction ID.
9. 312 June–July `Auto_Approve` cases: 289 apparently eligible, 14 clearly ineligible, 9 with missing eligibility attributes. Six further `Auto_Approve` cases occurred in the first week of August, after PD-3 expired.
10. 47 cases contain `Request_More_Info`; none activated before the requested evidence arrived.

### 1.3 Disputed propositions (evidence exists on both sides; resolution depends on authority, scope, or identity)

- **D1 — PD-3's authority.** Can a steering committee, recording a decision in minutes, lawfully vary a controlled compliance standard? The Compliance observer's "may proceed as a monitored exception" is in tension with her later statement that she never approved weakening CS-9. Both statements are evidence; neither is a formal amendment.
- **D2 — FD-7's authority.** FD-7 is far better formed than PD-3, but we have not been shown either (a) a derogation clause in CS-9 permitting emergency instruments, or (b) FD-7's approval chain. Its legitimacy is probable but not proven on the record supplied.
- **D3 — Scope of M0 for rejected leads (SUP-709).** Process owner and model owner disagree; the scope document is ambiguous; CS-9 explicitly declines to resolve it.
- **D4 — Scope of M0 for reactivations (SUP-710).** CS-9 has no reactivation procedure. Whether "onboarding" includes reactivation of a previously onboarded supplier is undefined.
- **D5 — Identity in SUP-712.** Whether two aliases resolve to one human is an identity-resolution claim, currently asserted rather than demonstrated with an authoritative person registry.
- **D6 — Whether SUP-706's notification occurred.** A CRM message exists at +3 hours, but the link is a mutable text field populated two days later.
- **D7 — Label equivalence timing in SUP-713.** Content equivalence of `QA_Clearance` and `Quality_Review` is documented; the mapping's *approval* postdates the case by a week.
- **D8 — SUP-715's relationship.** Whether a spousal relationship within one reporting line, between quality reviewer and approver, falls inside the conflict-of-interest disclosure obligation.
- **D9 — Whether SUP-708 is a real ordering or a timestamp artifact.** Forty minutes is small enough to be produced by clock skew, batch posting, or late manual entry of a sanctions result obtained earlier.

### 1.4 Unproved propositions (asserted by one side without sufficient support)

- **U1** That notification did not occur in the 29% of cases where the onboarding log lacks it. Post-migration, absence of a `Notify_Requester` event is absence of *evidence*, and the log-completeness assumption that underpins conformance checking is demonstrably false for this activity from 2026-07-01.
- **U2** That the 289 "apparently eligible" pilot cases were genuinely eligible. Eligibility "appears" satisfied from attributes read at analysis time; no ex-ante eligibility snapshot is in evidence. The 9 attribute-missing cases are likewise unresolved in either direction.
- **U3** That the flood emergency's continued factual existence extends FD-7 (SUP-705). Facts do not extend instruments; instruments extend instruments.
- **U4** That the operation's high M0 deviation rate demonstrates widespread misconduct. Deviation rate against an admittedly obsolete and under-scoped model is a measure of model validity at least as much as a measure of behavior.
- **U5** That PD-3's own compensating control — automated master-data validation before activation — was performed. It appears in *no* trace, including the pilot-eligible ones. The pilot's safety argument is therefore unverifiable from the log, in both directions.
- **U6** That "permanent approval was imminent" (SUP-714) had any documentary basis.

### 1.5 Burden of proof

I apply a deliberately **asymmetric** standard, because the two claims are not symmetric in what they do to the control environment.

**To declare noncompliance**, the reviewer bears the burden and must establish four elements:
1. **Applicable rule** — a rule from a controlled instrument, in force on the case date, whose scope demonstrably covers the case type.
2. **Behavioral breach** — conduct contrary to that rule, established on evidence that survives challenge for logging artifacts, clock reliability, label drift, and identity resolution.
3. **Attribution** — the breach is a property of the *behavior*, not of the model's expressiveness (POWL cannot represent loops) or of the log's completeness (CRM migration).
4. **Standard of proof** — *preponderance* for minor/administrative findings; *clear and convincing evidence* for severe findings (those triggering discipline, restatement, or regulatory reporting). SUP-703 and SUP-708 must meet the higher bar; SUP-715's disclosure gap need only meet the lower one.

**To accept an exception**, the *operation* bears the burden, and must establish five elements:
1. **Authority** — a written instrument issued by a body empowered to vary the specific control, in force at the moment of the act. Authority to *run* a pilot is not authority to *disapply* CS-9.
2. **Ex-ante eligibility** — case-level eligibility determined and recorded before the deviating act, not reconstructed afterwards.
3. **Compensating controls actually executed and evidenced**, not merely prescribed.
4. **Boundedness** — scope, amount cap, and expiry, all respected.
5. **Registration** — the exception recorded as an exception, so it can be counted, monitored, and terminated.

The asymmetry is deliberate: an unproved allegation costs one case; an unproved exception, once normalized, silently rewrites the control environment. The August cases (SUP-714 and the five others) are exactly that failure mode realized.

Finally, a methodological commitment: every finding below is stated with the layer it belongs to — **behavior**, **model**, **data**, or **governance**. Collapsing these four into a single "conformance" verdict is the root error committed by *both* parties in this dispute.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

**The charge is not that the operation deviates from a diagram. The charge is that the operation has developed a habit of disapplying a controlled compliance standard on its own motion, and that the habit has already produced at least three cases of material harm exposure.**

### 2.1 Only CS-9 has the authority it is being asked to yield

CS-9 is controlled and formally approved. PD-3 is minutes and an email. The doctrine that governs this is elementary: an instrument can only be varied by an instrument of equal or superior authority, following the change-control procedure that governs it. Nothing in the record shows PD-3 went through CS-9's amendment process — indeed, the record states the opposite: "not incorporated into CS-9 or M0."

The Compliance observer's two statements are not contradictory once read carefully, and the defense's reading is the weaker one. "Pilot may proceed as a monitored exception" is a statement about *tolerating observation*; "I did not approve weakening CS-9" is a statement about *normative effect*. The first is consistent with the second. A monitored exception is, by construction, still an exception — a deviation that is watched, not a deviation that is legalized. The pilot therefore ran for two months on a foundation that its own compliance witness says was never laid.

And note what PD-3 purported to disapply: not a formatting rule, but **two** of CS-9's three substantive activation controls simultaneously — the manual approval requirement (replaced by `Auto_Approve`) and the check-before-activation requirement (Quality_Review pushed up to two business days *after* activation). That is not a marginal accommodation; it is the activation control gate itself.

### 2.2 Expiry is not a suggestion

FD-7 expired at 2026-07-12 23:59 by its own terms. SUP-705 (2026-07-13) reproduces the emergency pattern one day later. Operations' answer — that the flood still factually existed — proves too much. Every emergency instrument is issued because an emergency factually exists; if factual persistence were self-extending, no emergency instrument would ever need a window, and the window that FD-7's drafters deliberately wrote would be surplusage. The correct action on 2026-07-13 was a one-line extension request. The cost of that request was minutes. The fact that nobody made it is precisely the finding: **the operation had stopped experiencing authority as a constraint.**

SUP-714 is the same failure two days after PD-3's expiry, and the aggregate makes it systemic rather than incidental: **six** post-pilot `Auto_Approve` cases in the first week of August, with the stated rationale that operations "had normalized the pilot flow and believed permanent approval was imminent." Belief about a future amendment is not a present authority. This is exception creep in its textbook form: a time-boxed derogation is granted, becomes routine during the box, and then survives the box because the routine, not the instrument, is what people actually follow.

### 2.3 Deviations that M0 hides, and that the defense cannot claim as model defects

The defense will argue that M0's obsolescence explains the deviation rate. Two of the most serious findings are invisible to M0 altogether, and therefore cannot be explained by M0's obsolescence — they are *undercounts*, not overcounts.

- **SUP-712 fits M0 exactly and breaches CS-9.** Requester and manual approver are the same human behind two aliases. CS-9's segregation rule is explicit, controlled, and in force. The only reason this is not a conformance finding is that M0 does not encode resource rules — which is an argument for *extending* the control set, not for acquitting the case. Self-approval is the single most consequential control failure in a procurement process, because it defeats every downstream control that assumes an independent second pair of eyes.
- **SUP-715 also fits M0 exactly.** The relationship between reviewer and approver is not prohibited, and I do not allege that it should have been forbidden. But the conflict-of-interest policy requires *disclosure*, and none was recorded. The breach is the non-disclosure, not the marriage. Disclosure regimes exist so that the organization, not the involved individuals, decides whether independence is impaired.

If M0 is the compliance oracle, both cases are clean. That is a devastating fact about M0 — and it cuts against the defense's implicit claim that the true noncompliance rate is far lower than the deviation rate. The two rates are not nested; they are merely different.

### 2.4 The high-risk cases

- **SUP-703** is the strongest finding on the record. A HIGH-risk *new foreign* supplier — the exact opposite of PD-3's LOW-risk, German, approved-corporate-group profile on all three eligibility dimensions — was auto-approved because "the operator selected 'pilot'." The first purchase was EUR 180,000. There is no authority reading, however generous, under which PD-3 covers this case. The pilot's route was available in the tooling as a *selectable option with no enforced eligibility gate*, which converts a narrow derogation into a general-purpose bypass. That is a design finding as much as a conduct finding, and it is aggravated, not excused, by the tooling: a control that can be defeated by a dropdown is not a control.
- **SUP-708** is a HIGH-risk supplier activated forty minutes before sanctions screening, with no instrument in play. Sanctions screening before activation is the one requirement that *every* instrument in this file preserves — CS-9 keeps it, PD-3 keeps it, FD-7 keeps it. It is the irreducible control. The defense will say no transactions occurred before clearance. That is a statement about realized loss, not about control effectiveness: the organization was, for forty minutes, in a state where a transaction with an unscreened counterparty was possible and nothing in the system would have prevented it. Compliance is measured by exposure, not by luck.
- **SUP-705** carries lower intrinsic risk (EUR 12,000, monitoring present) but higher *institutional* risk, because it is the case that establishes that expiry dates do not bind.

### 2.5 The aggregate is worse than the sample suggests

Fourteen `Auto_Approve` cases are **clearly ineligible** — SUP-703 is not an isolated operator error but the visible instance of a fourteen-case pattern. Nine more have missing eligibility attributes, which under the burden allocation of §1.5 count *against* the exception claim, since the operation bears the burden of proving eligibility and cannot discharge it with absent data. And of the 289 "apparently eligible," none is supported by an ex-ante eligibility snapshot, and *none* shows the `Master_Data_Validation` event that PD-3 itself made a precondition of activation. The pilot's own compensating control is entirely unevidenced across 312 cases.

### 2.6 Desirable outcomes do not amend policy

`Request_More_Info` is genuinely good practice, and the aggregate is reassuring: 47 cases, none activating before evidence arrived. I concede its value without reservation. But it establishes nothing about auto-approval. The defense's move — reasoning from "some undocumented practice is beneficial" to "our undocumented practices are legitimate" — is the argument that makes control environments fail. The remedy for a good unwritten practice is to write it down; the remedy is not to conclude that writing things down is optional. The same applies to outcomes: SUP-702's next-day quality review was fine, SUP-705's spend was modest, SUP-708's exposure did not crystallize. Good outcomes under uncontrolled processes are evidence about this sample, not about the process.

### 2.7 The prosecution's concessions

I will not overcharge, and the following defects are real and are the reviewer's fault, not the operation's:

1. **M0 cannot express iteration.** SUP-707 is a model defect, full stop. No adverse inference of any kind.
2. **M0's concurrency is being misread by whoever built the deviation statistics.** Quality-before-sanctions ordering (SUP-708's first three events, SUP-702's structure) is permitted by the partial order. My charge against SUP-708 is narrow and specific: activation before sanctions, nothing else.
3. **SUP-711 is a migration artifact** with a deterministic resolution and no behavioral content.
4. **SUP-713 is label drift**, with documented content equivalence. The late mapping approval is a change-control housekeeping point, not a compliance finding.
5. **The 29% notification missingness makes non-notification unprovable** for post-migration cases. I withdraw any inference from SUP-706's missing event beyond "unproven."
6. **M0 conflates provisional and full activation** under one `Activate_Supplier` label. This is a serious model defect, because it makes a governed emergency activation structurally indistinguishable from an ungoverned early activation — which is why SUP-704 and SUP-708 produce the same alignment cost despite being, respectively, exemplary and severe.
7. **The scope disputes (SUP-709, SUP-710) are genuine.** Ambiguity that the standard-setter created cannot be charged to the operator.

The residue after all those concessions is what matters: three material findings (SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-712), two authority-expiry findings (SUP-705, SUP-714) sitting on top of a six-case August pattern, a fourteen-case ineligible-pilot population, and a disclosure failure (SUP-715). None of that is cured by rewriting the diagram.

## 3. Defense Brief

**The reviewer is measuring a 2026 process against a 2025 sketch that was never a complete statement of the rules, and then reporting the gap as if it were misconduct. Most of the "deviation rate" is a property of the instrument, not of the operation.**

### 3.1 M0 is not, and never was, the compliance standard

CS-9 is the standard. M0 is a control-flow abstraction of part of CS-9, and it is provably a lossy one: CS-9's requester/approver segregation rule is, by the reviewer's own admission, absent from M0. A model that omits the standard's own resource control cannot be treated as the standard's operational proxy. Every conformance number in this review inherits that defect.

Worse, M0 is *validated against nothing*. There is no evidence in the record that M0 was ever reconciled against the actual case-type population — new onboarding, reactivation, pre-screen rejection, withdrawal. It represents exactly one case type and treats the other three as violations.

### 3.2 Concurrency: a chunk of the reported deviation is a reading error

M0 makes sanctions and quality concurrent. Any finding that depends on their relative order is void. This matters beyond bookkeeping: it shows the conformance analysis was performed with an implicit sequential bias, which means the deviation statistics presented to management are not merely inflated but *methodologically unreliable*. When the prosecution cites a "high deviation rate," it is citing a number produced by that method.

### 3.3 The missing loop: 47 cases of good behavior recorded as noncompliance

`Request_More_Info` is used in 47 cases; in **zero** of them did activation precede the arrival of requested evidence. This is a practice that strictly *increases* control strength — it exists precisely to prevent approving on incomplete certificates, which is what happened in SUP-707 (missing certificates obtained before approval). M0 contains no loop and no such activity, so every one of these 47 cases is scored as a violation.

That is not a minor calibration issue. It means the model penalizes the operation for exercising more diligence than the model imagines, and the penalty is indistinguishable, in the conformance output, from the penalty for exercising less. A model whose deviation signal cannot separate "did extra verification" from "skipped sanctions screening" is not fit for the purpose of compliance monitoring, and management decisions taken on its output are unsafe.

### 3.4 Scope: three case types are outside M0's design, and the standard-setter knows it

- **Rejected leads (SUP-709).** CS-9 explicitly permits an abbreviated pre-screen for rejected leads and explicitly declines to say whether those leads are M0 cases. A lead that fails sanctions pre-screen is dismissed; performing a quality review on a supplier you have just rejected is pure waste, protecting nothing, because the terminal risk of the case is zero — nothing was activated, nothing was purchased, no exposure was created. The dispute between process owner and model owner is a dispute about *case notion*, which is a data-modeling decision, and it should not be resolved by charging the operator with the ambiguity. Note also the perverse incentive: if pre-screen rejections must carry the full check battery, the rational response is to stop logging pre-screens as cases at all — the conformance rule would destroy the very visibility it claims to want.
- **Reactivations (SUP-710).** CS-9 has *no* reactivation procedure. The operation nevertheless performed sanctions screening and manual procurement approval, and relied on a prior quality certification that remained valid. Charging a missing `Register_Supplier` against a supplier who was demonstrably already registered is a category error; the register step's purpose — establishing the supplier record — was satisfied six months earlier. Requiring re-execution of a still-valid certification is not additional assurance, it is ritual. The genuine gap here is the absence of a written reactivation route with a certification-validity test, and that gap is the standard-setter's.
- **Withdrawals** are similarly unrepresented, which will produce the same class of false findings as soon as one appears.

### 3.5 The pilot and the emergency were governed exceptions, not freelancing

**SUP-704 is the model of how this should work.** FD-7 was written, scoped to critical relief suppliers, capped at EUR 20,000, time-boxed to 72 hours, and equipped with two compensating controls. SUP-704 satisfied every element: EUR 18,000 (under cap), daily monitoring in place, quality review and procurement approval completed the next business day, sanctions screening before activation. The organization made a considered risk trade — a pump supplier's delay would have shut down flood response — and it made that trade *with controls attached*. Scoring SUP-704 as a deviation, with an alignment cost identical to SUP-708's, is the clearest possible demonstration that M0 is measuring the wrong thing.

**SUP-702 is a properly evidenced pilot case.** In-window, LOW-risk, DE, approved corporate group, eligibility fields complete, quality review the next business day, notification confirmed in CRM at +2 hours with a **stable** notification_id. And on authority: the Compliance function was in the room, observed, and wrote that the pilot "may proceed as a monitored exception." Whatever she meant afterwards, an operator reading that sentence in June 2026 had a reasonable basis for acting. If Compliance believed the pilot was unlawful, the remedy available to it was to stop the pilot — a remedy it did not use for two months. Charging front-line operators with the consequences of a governance failure at steering-committee level inverts responsibility: **the defect is at the top of this file, not at the bottom of it.**

### 3.6 The CRM migration destroyed the log-completeness assumption

Conformance checking assumes the log records what happened. From 2026-07-01 that assumption is false for `Notify_Requester`: missingness went from 2% to 29%. Any "missing notification" finding after that date is an artifact of an IT change, and the responsible reading is *unknown*, not *did not happen*. SUP-706 illustrates the second-order problem too: the CRM message exists, but the link is a mutable free-text field entered two days later, so the evidence is weak in *both* directions — it cannot prove notification, and its weakness is a design flaw in the integration, not a behavior of the case team. Compare SUP-702's stable notification_id, which is exactly the same underlying event supported by a better identifier. The difference in evidential value between those two cases is entirely attributable to system design.

### 3.7 Label drift and migration artifacts are not conduct

**SUP-713**: the procedure and the UI renamed `Quality_Review` to `QA_Clearance`, audit documents establish equivalent content, and the mapping was approved a week later. The activity was performed. The only defect is that the ontology change and the mapping change were not synchronized — a change-control timing issue in the *analytics* pipeline. Treating a rename as a missing control would mean no organization could ever rename anything.

**SUP-711**: two rows, one source transaction ID. One event happened. This is not even indeterminate; it is a deterministic deduplication with a key. Reporting it as a deviation is a failure of log preparation, and it should have been fixed before any conformance run.

### 3.8 The distinction the prosecution keeps eliding

A **trace deviation** is a mismatch between a recorded sequence and a formal model. **Business misconduct** is conduct that defeats a control's purpose. Of the fifteen cases, deviations arising purely from model expressiveness (SUP-707), model scope (SUP-709, SUP-710), label mapping (SUP-713), log preparation (SUP-711), and log completeness (SUP-706) are *six* cases with zero misconduct content. Two more (SUP-702, SUP-704) are exceptions that were substantively governed. That is more than half the sample. Meanwhile, the two cases with the sharpest control failure (SUP-712, SUP-715) generate **no** deviation at all. The correlation between M0 deviation and actual risk is, on this sample, close to zero and arguably negative.

Management is being asked to act on a signal with no discriminating power. The first remediation priority is the measurement system.

### 3.9 The defense's concessions

The defense does not, and cannot, defend the following:

1. **SUP-703 is indefensible.** HIGH-risk, new, foreign, EUR 180,000, ineligible on every criterion. PD-3 was narrow and this was outside it. The operator's selection of "pilot" was wrong, and the *system's* willingness to accept that selection without an eligibility gate is a design failure we own.
2. **SUP-708 is indefensible.** Sanctions-before-activation is preserved by every instrument in this file, including the ones we rely on. That no transactions occurred is mitigation of consequence, not of breach.
3. **The 14 clearly ineligible pilot cases are a real population**, and the 9 with missing eligibility attributes cannot be claimed as compliant by us — we bear that burden and cannot discharge it.
4. **Post-expiry continuation was wrong.** SUP-705 and SUP-714 should have been extension requests, not silent continuations. "The emergency was still real" and "permanent approval seemed imminent" are explanations, not authorities, and we accept that if the defense's own logic were extended to cover them, no instrument would ever bind anyone.
5. **The six August cases show normalization**, which is the specific pathology that makes informal practice dangerous, and it validates the prosecution's core institutional concern.
6. **PD-3's own precondition — automated master-data validation — is unevidenced in every trace.** We cannot claim the pilot's compensating control while being unable to show it ran.
7. **SUP-712's segregation issue is serious** if identity resolution confirms it, and the fact that M0 does not encode the rule is not a defense to CS-9.
8. **Obsolescence is not self-executing.** A model being wrong does not make any particular act right. The correct response to an obsolete model was to amend it in January, and we did not.

### 5.2 System-level judgment

**On operational compliance: the operation is not "materially noncompliant" as a whole, but it has a material control-environment failure in exception governance.**

Of fifteen cases, five are noncompliant, and of those, three (SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-712) are materially so. Two (SUP-705, SUP-714) are authority-window failures with modest case-level risk. That is not a picture of an operation that has abandoned its controls: sanctions screening ran in every single case; the emergency route stayed under its cap; the RMI practice is 47-for-47; SUP-704 was executed exactly as a governed exception should be.

But the *pattern* across those five is coherent and worrying, and it is not the pattern of scattered operator error. Three distinct mechanisms of authority erosion appear: an unauthorized instrument that ran for two months (PD-3), unenforced eligibility gating that let a narrow derogation become a general bypass (SUP-703 plus 14 more), and expiry that produced no behavioral change whatsoever (SUP-705, SUP-714, and five further August cases). The operation's exception *practices* are largely sound; its exception *governance* is not, and unmanaged, the second will eventually erode the first. The August cases show that erosion already underway.

**On model validity: M0 is invalid as a sole conformance oracle and must not be used as one for management or audit reporting.**

M0 is unfit on three independent grounds, any one of which would be sufficient:
- **Under-coverage.** It represents one case type out of at least four (new onboarding, reactivation, pre-screen rejection, withdrawal) and one route out of at least three.
- **Under-expression.** It cannot represent iteration, optionality, provisional versus full activation, or any data, time, amount, or identity condition — including CS-9's own segregation rule.
- **Non-discrimination.** Its deviation signal does not separate risk from non-risk. SUP-704 (exemplary) and SUP-708 (severe) produce comparable alignment costs, while SUP-712 and SUP-715 — the two cleanest control failures in the file — produce none. A metric that is uncorrelated with the thing it is meant to detect is worse than no metric, because it is acted upon.

Operations' claim that "M0's high deviation rate mostly reflects obsolescence and incomplete scope" is **substantially correct as an explanation of the rate** and **irrelevant as a defense of the five noncompliant cases**. Both halves of that sentence are load-bearing.

**Priority ordering for remediation** (severity-weighted, not frequency-weighted): (1) hard eligibility gating on any non-standard route, so no operator can select an inapplicable overlay; (2) identity resolution and enforced segregation of duties, which today is entirely unmonitored; (3) automatic expiry enforcement that disables overlay routes when their instrument lapses; (4) replacement of M0 with the layered architecture in §6; (5) immutable notification linkage.

### 5.3 Where I reject the prosecution

- **I reject the framing of "312 pilot cases" as a violation population.** The correct finding is 14 ineligible cases plus 9 unproven, and a governance defect at PD-3 level covering the rest. Charging 289 substantively eligible, documented, low-risk cases as individual misconduct would be a misallocation of the burden of proof and would guarantee the review is dismissed as noise.
- **I reject any inference from missing notification events after 2026-07-01.** Missingness rose to 29% for a documented systems reason. SUP-706 is indeterminate and stays indeterminate.
- **I reject order-based findings on sanctions versus quality.** They are concurrent in M0. Any deviation statistic containing them is defective and must be recomputed.
- **I reject SUP-707, SUP-709, SUP-711, and SUP-713 as compliance findings.** They are model, scope, log-preparation, and ontology defects respectively.
- **I reject deviation frequency as a severity proxy.** SUP-712 and SUP-715 deviate zero and rank among the most serious.
- **I reject the implicit charge against front-line operators in SUP-702.** Compliance observed the pilot and let it run for two months. The finding belongs to the steering committee and the compliance function.
- **I reject any suggestion that SUP-704 should be reported adversely.** It is the standard the rest of the operation should be held to.

### 5.4 Where I reject the defense

- **I reject "the model is obsolete" as a defense to any act.** Obsolescence explains deviation counts; it authorizes nothing. CS-9 was in force continuously and was never amended.
- **I reject "the emergency factually persisted" (SUP-705).** If facts extended instruments, effective windows would be meaningless — including FD-7's, which the defense elsewhere relies on for SUP-704's legitimacy. The defense cannot invoke FD-7's rigor on the 11th and its irrelevance on the 13th.
- **I reject "permanent approval was imminent" (SUP-714) and the six August cases.** Anticipated authority is not authority. This is the exact mechanism by which time-boxed pilots become permanent policy without anyone deciding they should be.
- **I reject "no transactions occurred" as curing SUP-708.** Control effectiveness is measured by exposure, not by realized loss.
- **I reject "M0 does not encode the rule" as curing SUP-712.** CS-9 encodes it, CS-9 is controlled, and CS-9 is the standard the defense itself invokes when it argues M0 is only a partial abstraction. That argument cuts both ways.
- **I reject the extension of "desirable practice" from RMI to auto-approval.** RMI adds a check; auto-approval removes two. Their moral valence under an identical documentation gap is not the same.
- **I reject the pilot's safety claim as currently evidenced.** PD-3 required automated master-data validation before activation and it appears in no trace. The defense cannot both rely on a compensating control and be unable to show it ran.
- **I reject the treatment of SUP-703 as an isolated operator error.** Fourteen clearly ineligible cases is a systemic gating failure.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The central design principle: **stop asking one artifact to carry control-flow, data, identity, time, and authority semantics simultaneously.** M0 failed not because it was drawn badly but because it was assigned a job no single control-flow model can do. The replacement is six layers, each holding only what it can hold soundly, and each independently versioned.

### Layer 0 — Scope, case notion, and activity ontology *(organizational control + data engineering)*

Nothing downstream is meaningful until these are fixed, and their absence is the direct cause of the SUP-709, SUP-710, SUP-711, and SUP-713 disputes.

- **Case-type register.** `NEW_ONBOARDING`, `REACTIVATION`, `PRE_SCREEN_LEAD`, `WITHDRAWN`, each with an explicit in-scope/out-of-scope ruling signed by the process owner *and* the model owner. This resolves D3 and D4 by decision rather than by argument, which is the only way ambiguity of this kind is ever resolved.
- **Rejected leads.** Ruling: `PRE_SCREEN_LEAD` cases are **in scope for monitoring, out of scope for the activation control set**. They must be logged (visibility) and are evaluated against a reduced obligation set that omits `Quality_Review` (proportionality). This keeps SUP-709 visible without manufacturing a false finding, and avoids the perverse incentive to stop logging pre-screens.
- **Reactivations.** Ruling: a distinct case type with its own route, entered by an explicit `Reactivate_Supplier` event, requiring fresh sanctions screening and manual approval, and permitting reuse of a prior quality certification only through an explicit, logged `Reuse_Valid_Certification` event carrying `certificate_id` and `valid_until`. Silence becomes an event; the waiver becomes auditable.
- **Withdrawals.** Explicit `Withdraw_Request` terminal route, so the next withdrawal does not become a finding.
- **Effective-dated activity ontology.** `QA_Clearance ≡ Quality_Review` with `valid_from` set to the UI change date, not the mapping-approval date. Ontology changes route through change control **before** go-live. This is the SUP-713 fix.
- **Deterministic deduplication** on `source_transaction_id` at ingestion, executed before any conformance run. This is the SUP-711 fix, and it belongs in the pipeline, not in the reviewer's judgment.

### Layer 1 — Control-flow model M1 *(POWL)*

POWL holds exactly what partial orders and operators can express soundly: route alternatives, concurrency, ordering, optionality, and iteration. M1 (coded in §7) adds a route XOR over standard / low-risk-auto / emergency-provisional / reject / withdraw, an intake XOR over registration and reactivation, a zero-or-more `Request_More_Info` loop, a one-or-more monitoring loop in the emergency route, and — importantly — a split of `Activate_Supplier` into `Activate_Supplier_Provisional` and `Confirm_Full_Activation` so that a governed provisional activation is structurally distinguishable from an ungoverned early one. That single split is what would have separated SUP-704 from SUP-708 automatically.

**What POWL must not be asked to do:** choose a route based on risk class, enforce a EUR 20,000 cap, check that an instrument was in force on the case date, compare two actors' identities, or bound elapsed time. Every one of those conditions is invisible to a control-flow model. Attempting to encode them by proliferating labels (`Activate_LowRisk_Under20k_InWindow`) produces a model that is unreadable, unmaintainable, and still unable to *verify* the condition it names — the label asserts the fact rather than checking it, which is worse than not modeling it, because it looks like a control.

### Layer 2 — Declarative constraints *(DECLARE / log-skeleton style)*

These express cross-cutting obligations that hold across routes, including the ones M1 deliberately approximates.

| Constraint | Form | Rationale / case |
|---|---|---|
| `Sanctions_Check` precedes every activation | `precedence(Sanctions_Check, Activate_*)` — **non-derogable** | The invariant every instrument preserves; SUP-708 |
| Provisional activation obliges quality review | `response(Activate_Supplier_Provisional, Quality_Review)` | PD-3, FD-7 |
| Provisional activation obliges approval | `response(Activate_Supplier_Provisional, Manual_Procurement_Approval)` | FD-7 retrospective approval; SUP-704 |
| Provisional activation obliges confirmation | `response(Activate_Supplier_Provisional, Confirm_Full_Activation)` | Prevents indefinite provisional state |
| Approval routes are mutually exclusive | `non_coexistence(Auto_Approve, Manual_Procurement_Approval)` | Prevents route laundering |
| Auto-approval requires validated master data | `precedence(Master_Data_Validation, Auto_Approve)` | PD-3's unevidenced control (U5) |
| Requests precede the approval decision | `precedence(Request_More_Info, {Auto_Approve, Manual_Procurement_Approval})` | Tightens M1's concurrency approximation (§7 limitation 1) |
| Notification precedes closure | `precedence(Notify_Requester, Close_Case)`, evaluated over log ∪ CRM | SUP-706 |
| Certification reuse excludes review | `non_coexistence(Reuse_Valid_Certification, Quality_Review)` | SUP-710 |
| Auto-approval absent for non-LOW risk | `absence(Auto_Approve)` under `risk_class != LOW` | SUP-703 |

Log-skeleton equivalents (`always_before`, `never_together`, activity-count bounds) are the right formalism where the check is over the whole case rather than a specific ordering — e.g. "at most one activation-class event per case," "exactly one intake event after deduplication."

### Layer 3 — Data-aware rules *(pandas / SQL checks over the event and case tables)*

Everything conditioned on values, amounts, dates, or elapsed time lives here, evaluated per case against the rule version in force on the case's own timestamps:

- **Eligibility**: `risk_class == LOW AND country == DE AND corporate_group_status == APPROVED`, evaluated against the **ex-ante eligibility snapshot** taken at route selection, never against current attribute values. This is the fix for U2 and the 289 unproven cases.
- **Amount**: cumulative committed spend ≤ EUR 20,000 between `Activate_Supplier_Provisional` and `Confirm_Full_Activation`; breach raises an immediate exception, not a month-end finding.
- **Time**: `Quality_Review` within two **business days** of provisional activation (with a holiday calendar); daily monitoring events present for every business day the case remains provisional.
- **Authority window**: the case's route selection timestamp falls inside `[effective_from, effective_to]` of the cited `rule_version_id`. This single check catches SUP-705 and SUP-714 *at the moment of the act*, which is the only time catching them is useful.
- **Notification**: satisfied by an onboarding-log event **or** an immutable CRM record joined on `notification_id`; missingness reported as a data-quality metric, never as a control failure.

### Layer 4 — Resource, identity, and conflict of interest *(data checks over a resolved identity graph + organizational control)*

- **Alias resolution** to a canonical `person_id` via an authoritative HR/IAM registry. Without it, SUP-712 is undetectable — which is precisely why it went undetected.
- **Segregation of duties**: `requester.person_id != approver.person_id` (CS-9, hard block at execution time, not detective after the fact).
- **Delegation**: where an approval is delegated, a `delegation_id` with grantor, grantee, scope, and validity window must be present; delegation must never collapse SoD.
- **Relationship register and CoI disclosure**: declared relationships (spousal, reporting-line, financial) between any two actors on the same case trigger a mandatory `CoI_Disclosure` record. SUP-715's gap is closed by making the disclosure a *blocking* precondition of approval rather than an unverified expectation. Note the design choice: the rule does not prohibit the relationship, it prohibits proceeding without disclosure — matching what the policy actually says.

### Layer 5 — Policy-overlay governance *(organizational control, with machine-readable representation)*

This layer is the substantive answer to this entire dispute. Every overlay — pilot or emergency — becomes a first-class, versioned object with:

`rule_id`, `version`, `issuing_body`, **`authority_basis`** (the clause of CS-9 or the delegated authority that permits the derogation), `derogated_clauses` (explicit list of what is being switched off), `eligibility_predicate` (machine-evaluable), `compensating_controls` (each mapped to a required event), `amount_cap`, `effective_from` / `effective_to`, `extension_procedure`, and `expiry_behavior`.

Four non-negotiable governance rules follow:

1. **No overlay without an `authority_basis` that traces to the controlled standard.** PD-3 would have failed this test on 2026-06-01, which is when it should have been failed. CS-9 must be amended to include an explicit derogation clause naming who may issue emergency and pilot instruments and on what terms — the absence of that clause is why even FD-7's legitimacy is only probable (D2).
2. **Hard expiry.** At `effective_to`, the system **disables the overlay route** in the UI. Expiry becomes a mechanical fact, not a matter of anyone remembering. SUP-705, SUP-714, and the six August cases all become impossible rather than detectable.
3. **Eligibility gating at selection time.** The overlay route is not offered unless `eligibility_predicate` evaluates true against the snapshot. SUP-703 becomes impossible; the 14 ineligible cases become zero.
4. **Exception register with mandatory sunset review.** Every overlay case is registered as an exception. Overlay volume is reported to the standard-setter monthly, and any overlay exceeding a defined share of volume triggers a mandatory decision: **amend the standard or terminate the pilot.** Normalization without a decision — what happened here — is structurally excluded.

**On PD-3 specifically:** the correct disposition is not retroactive validation and not blanket condemnation. It is (a) formal ratification or repudiation by the body that owns CS-9, applied prospectively; (b) if the low-risk auto-approval route is to survive — and on the evidence of 289 clean cases it probably should — a permanent CS-9 amendment with an enforced eligibility predicate and an evidenced `Master_Data_Validation` control; and (c) a finding against the steering committee and the compliance function for operating a two-month derogation without authority.

### Layer allocation summary

| Concern | Layer | Formalism |
|---|---|---|
| Routes, concurrency, iteration, optionality | 1 | POWL |
| Cross-route obligations, response/precedence, SLAs as obligations | 2 | DECLARE / log skeleton |
| Amounts, dates, risk class, eligibility, windows | 3 | pandas / SQL |
| Identity, SoD, delegation, conflict of interest | 4 | identity graph + data checks |
| Authority, expiry, ratification, exception registers | 5 | organizational control (machine-readable) |
| Case notion, scope, ontology, deduplication | 0 | governance decision + data engineering |

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

### 7.1 Executable model

```python
"""
Revised normative supplier-onboarding model M1 (POWL / PM4Py).

Layer 1 of the architecture in section 6. This file encodes CONTROL FLOW ONLY.
Route eligibility, amounts, dates, identities and instrument authority are
deliberately NOT encoded here; see the overlay table and DECLARE constraints
below. No external files are read.
"""

from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def act(label):
    return Transition(label=label)


def order(nodes, edges=()):
    """StrictPartialOrder helper. Unordered nodes are concurrent."""
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    for src, tgt in edges:
        po.order.add_edge(src, tgt)
    return po


def zero_or_more(node):
    """tau ( node tau )*  -> the node occurs zero or more times."""
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP,
                        children=[SilentTransition(), node])


def one_or_more(node):
    """node ( tau node )*  -> the node occurs at least once."""
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP,
                        children=[node, SilentTransition()])


def exclusive(*children):
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=list(children))


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Intake: new registration XOR reactivation of a dormant supplier (SUP-710).
# Reactivate_Supplier is a NEW required event; today reactivation cases simply
# start with Sanctions_Check, which is why they appear as missing-step
# violations rather than as a recognised case type.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
register = act("Register_Supplier")
reactivate = act("Reactivate_Supplier")
intake = exclusive(register, reactivate)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Request_More_Info: repeatable, and unordered with respect to the route body
# so it may interleave with the checks as observed in SUP-707.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
rmi_loop = zero_or_more(act("Request_More_Info"))

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# ROUTE 1 - standard manual route (CS-9 baseline; SUP-701, SUP-712, SUP-713,
# SUP-715). Sanctions and quality are concurrent; both precede approval;
# approval precedes activation. Quality may be replaced by an EXPLICIT reuse
# of a still-valid certification, which makes the SUP-710 waiver auditable
# instead of invisible.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
s_std = act("Sanctions_Check")
q_std = exclusive(act("Quality_Review"), act("Reuse_Valid_Certification"))
checks_std = order([s_std, q_std])

approve_std = act("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
activate_std = act("Activate_Supplier")
route_standard = order(
    [checks_std, approve_std, activate_std],
    [(checks_std, approve_std), (approve_std, activate_std)],
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# ROUTE 2 - low-risk auto-approval (PD-3; SUP-702, SUP-703, SUP-714).
# Master_Data_Validation is modelled as a REQUIRED event: PD-3 demands it, yet
# it appears in no observed trace, so today the pilot's own compensating
# control is unverifiable. Quality_Review is unordered w.r.t. provisional
# activation (PD-3 permits it up to two business days after) but must precede
# full confirmation. The two-business-day bound is a data rule, not POWL.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
s_auto = act("Sanctions_Check")
mdv_auto = act("Master_Data_Validation")
auto_approve = act("Auto_Approve")
prov_auto = act("Activate_Supplier_Provisional")
q_auto = act("Quality_Review")
confirm_auto = act("Confirm_Full_Activation")
route_auto = order(
    [s_auto, mdv_auto, auto_approve, prov_auto, q_auto, confirm_auto],
    [
        (s_auto, auto_approve),
        (mdv_auto, auto_approve),
        (auto_approve, prov_auto),
        (prov_auto, confirm_auto),
        (q_auto, confirm_auto),
    ],
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# ROUTE 3 - emergency / provisional route (FD-7; SUP-704, SUP-705).
# Sanctions still precedes activation - the non-derogable invariant. Quality
# review and retrospective approval both follow provisional activation and are
# concurrent with each other. At least one monitoring event is required before
# the case can be confirmed; "daily" is a data rule.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
s_emg = act("Sanctions_Check")
prov_emg = act("Activate_Supplier_Provisional")
monitoring = one_or_more(act("Daily_Transaction_Monitoring"))
q_emg = act("Quality_Review")
approve_emg = act("Manual_Procurement_Approval")   # retrospective by position
confirm_emg = act("Confirm_Full_Activation")
route_emergency = order(
    [s_emg, prov_emg, monitoring, q_emg, approve_emg, confirm_emg],
    [
        (s_emg, prov_emg),
        (prov_emg, monitoring),
        (prov_emg, q_emg),
        (prov_emg, approve_emg),
        (monitoring, confirm_emg),
        (q_emg, confirm_emg),
        (approve_emg, confirm_emg),
    ],
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# ROUTE 4 - rejection (SUP-709). Quality review is skippable ONLY for
# pre-screen rejections; the silent branch is guarded by a data rule keyed on
# reject_reason, not by the model.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
s_rej = act("Sanctions_Check")
q_rej_opt = exclusive(act("Quality_Review"), SilentTransition())
reject = act("Reject_Supplier")
route_reject = order([s_rej, q_rej_opt, reject],
                     [(s_rej, reject), (q_rej_opt, reject)])

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# ROUTE 5 - withdrawal before decision. Absent from M0; would otherwise
# generate false findings the first time it occurs.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
route_withdraw = act("Withdraw_Request")

route_choice = exclusive(
    route_standard,
    route_auto,
    route_emergency,
    route_reject,
    route_withdraw,
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Top level. Notification stays MANDATORY before closure: the 29% missingness
# is a data-collection defect and must not be laundered into an optional step.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
notify = act("Notify_Requester")
close = act("Close_Case")

M1 = order(
    [intake, rmi_loop, route_choice, notify, close],
    [
        (intake, rmi_loop),
        (intake, route_choice),
        (rmi_loop, notify),
        (route_choice, notify),
        (notify, close),
    ],
)

if __name__ == "__main__":
    print(M1)
```

### 7.2 Stated limitations and the approximations chosen

I state these rather than hiding them, because an unstated approximation in a normative model is how M0 became a liability.

1. **`Request_More_Info` placement is an over-approximation.** Making the loop concurrent with the route body is what allows it to interleave with the checks as SUP-707 actually did. The cost is that M1 also permits an RMI *after* activation. A POWL alternative — replicating the loop inside each route between checks and approval — would tighten this but quadruples the node count and still could not bound the iteration count. Chosen fix: keep the concurrent loop and enforce `precedence(Request_More_Info, {Auto_Approve, Manual_Procurement_Approval})` in Layer 2.
2. **Route selection is unguarded.** The XOR is free choice; POWL has no data conditions. Nothing in M1 stops a HIGH-risk case from taking the auto route — SUP-703 conforms to M1. This is *by design*: the guard belongs in the eligibility predicate at Layer 3 and in UI gating at Layer 5, where it can actually be evaluated. A model that appeared to encode the guard through label naming would be a false assurance.
3. **No time, amount, or identity semantics.** The two-business-day quality window, the EUR 20,000 cap, the daily cadence of monitoring, and requester ≠ approver are all outside POWL. `one_or_more(Daily_Transaction_Monitoring)` approximates "daily" as "at least once"; the cadence check is Layer 3.
4. **Instrument validity is not representable.** M1 cannot distinguish SUP-704 (in window) from SUP-705 (one day late) — their traces are identical. This is intrinsic to control-flow modeling and is exactly why Layer 5 must gate the route at execution time rather than detect it afterwards.
5. **Duplicate labels across routes are intentional.** `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` appear as distinct `Transition` objects per route because their ordering obligations differ by route. Alignment tooling handles duplicate labels; merging them into shared nodes would force a single ordering across all routes and reintroduce M0's core defect.
6. **`Activate_Supplier_Provisional` / `Confirm_Full_Activation` do not yet exist in the log.** They are proposed events (§7.4). Until they are emitted, the pilot and emergency routes must be evaluated against a compatibility mapping in which a single `Activate_Supplier` on an overlay case is read as provisional. That mapping is a temporary measure and should be retired, because it is the very conflation that makes SUP-704 and SUP-708 look alike.
7. **Reactivation intake is modeled as an XOR, not a skip.** Using `SilentTransition()` for a missing `Register_Supplier` would make "registration genuinely absent" and "registration legitimately not applicable" indistinguishable. Requiring an explicit `Reactivate_Supplier` event trades a small logging change for a permanent gain in auditability.

### 7.4 Event and data-collection proposal

**New or corrected events**

| Event | Purpose | Case that motivates it |
|---|---|---|
| `Activate_Supplier_Provisional` / `Confirm_Full_Activation` | Separate governed provisional activation from full activation, and end the conflation that makes SUP-704 and SUP-708 look alike | SUP-704, SUP-708 |
| `Master_Data_Validation` | Make PD-3's compensating control observable rather than assumed | all 312 pilot cases |
| `Daily_Transaction_Monitoring` | Make FD-7's monitoring evidenced in-log rather than asserted | SUP-704 |
| `Reactivate_Supplier`, `Reuse_Valid_Certification` | Turn reactivation and certification waiver into auditable events | SUP-710 |
| `Withdraw_Request` | Represent a terminal path that exists in reality | scope gap |
| `CoI_Disclosure` | Make disclosure a blocking, evidenced precondition of approval | SUP-715 |
| `Exception_Registered` | Bind each overlay case to its instrument at the moment of use | SUP-702, 705, 714 |

**Required attributes**

| Attribute | Where | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| `notification_id` | notification events | **Immutable**, system-generated, foreign key to CRM; retires the mutable text link that makes SUP-706 unresolvable |
| `rule_version_id` | every overlay-route event | Which instrument and version was relied on — the missing link that let PD-3 run unattributed for two months |
| `eligibility_snapshot` | route selection | JSON of `risk_class`, `country`, `corporate_group_status`, `criticality` **as evaluated at that instant**, hash-sealed; converts the 289 "apparently eligible" cases into provable ones |
| `actor_alias` → `person_id` | every event | Resolved against the authoritative registry at ingestion; without it, SUP-712 stays invisible |
| `delegation_id` | approval events | Grantor, grantee, scope, validity window |
| `emergency_ref` / `pilot_ref` | overlay cases | Link to the authorizing instrument and, where relevant, the extension record |
| `amount_committed` | activation and purchase events | Cumulative, for cap enforcement while provisional |
| `effective_timestamp` **and** `record_timestamp` | **every** event | Immutable, both stored. When an act occurred versus when it was recorded is the only way to settle SUP-708 (D9) and the only defense against backdating generally |
| `source_transaction_id` | every event | Deduplication key; resolves SUP-711 at ingestion |
| `case_type`, `activity_version` | case and event | Layer 0 scope routing and ontology versioning; resolves SUP-709, SUP-710, SUP-713 |

**Pipeline requirements**

1. Deduplicate on `source_transaction_id` **before** conformance replay.
2. Apply the effective-dated ontology to normalize `QA_Clearance` → `Quality_Review` for evaluation, retaining the original label for audit.
3. Left-join CRM notifications on immutable `notification_id`; report unmatched notifications as a **data-quality metric**, never as a control finding.
4. Evaluate conformance per `case_type`, per route, and per `rule_version_id` in force on the case's own effective timestamps — not against a single global model.
5. Publish two separate indicators and never merge them: **control-failure rate** (Layers 2–4 breaches) and **model/data-defect rate** (Layer 0–1 mismatches). The single blended "deviation rate" that produced this dispute must be discontinued.

### 7.5 Expected conformance behavior of M1

Replaying the observed patterns against M1 (converted to a Petri net, alignment-based fitness) gives the discrimination M0 lacked:

| Pattern | M0 | M1 | Correct? |
|---|---|---|---|
| SUP-701 standard route | fits | fits | yes |
| SUP-707 with two `Request_More_Info` cycles | deviates | fits | yes — the loop is now expressible |
| SUP-709 pre-screen rejection without quality review | deviates | fits | yes — the abbreviated reject branch exists |

The key improvement is the last two rows read against rows six and seven: M0 assigned SUP-704 and SUP-708 comparable alignment costs, whereas M1 accepts the governed emergency pattern and continues to reject activation before sanctions screening. M1 also deliberately still accepts SUP-703's control flow — an ineligible case on the auto route is a *data* violation, and pretending a control-flow model caught it would be the same false assurance that made M0 untrustworthy.
