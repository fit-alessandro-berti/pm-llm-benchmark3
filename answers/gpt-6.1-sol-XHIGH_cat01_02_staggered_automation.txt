## 1. What is observed

Automation became available in Alpha and Beta in April and Gamma in July; Delta and Epsilon remained without the rollout through August. Availability is a branch policy indicator, not case-level treatment. Recorded completed automated-fast-path shares rose to 48%, 50%, and 32% by August, respectively. Shares of 4–6% also appear before availability and in nonadopters, so the relationship between the availability flag and existing automation requires clarification. These shares exclude automated cases subsequently escalated and therefore do not measure all automated-triage exposure.

The first adopters were selected for high volume and poor historical cycle times. Gamma entered after a readiness pilot. Adoption dates consequently encode performance, readiness, and managerial choices.

A shared fraud desk opened in March. Routing increased demand at adopting branches, staffing changed unevenly, and May’s product change raised recorded complexity in several branches. Local practices and automation use also evolved. Alpha and Beta’s post-adoption p90 averages exceed their pre-adoption averages; Gamma’s median and p90 averages are lower after July. Untreated branches also show substantial changes over time.

Only 40 branch-month observations are available. Cycle-time summaries are quantiles, not individual durations, and their cohort definitions and censoring rules are unspecified. Arrivals do not establish completed-case denominators or throughput. Registration risk classes may incompletely represent difficulty. CSAT describes respondents, with variable response rates and unavailable counts. Event timestamps, queue states, fraud-desk waits, errors, and customer follow-up are absent.

## 2. Causal estimands

Let availability be a specified offer of the system, training, and escalation rules. Its total effect allows uptake and consequences induced by that offer to change naturally. Calendar shocks unrelated to the offer occur under both counterfactuals. Rollout-induced routing or management changes belong to this total policy effect; separating the technology from those changes requires a different intervention.

The historical rollout does not establish these boundaries. Consequently, historical comparisons most naturally concern the implementation bundle, rather than isolated software. With a shared fraud desk, outcomes can depend on other branches’ assignments. Each estimand must specify either other branches’ rollout and queue-capacity regime or a joint organization-wide policy. Training, thresholds, and routing rules define treatment versions.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary: cohort-specific group-time DiD with event-time reporting | Separate April and July adoption cohorts; estimate each cohort-month contrast against a pre-adoption baseline and retain event-time effects | Delta/Epsilon throughout; Gamma only before July and before any relevant pilot anticipation | Branch baseline outcome levels/trends, pre-treatment complexity, capacity, and load; calendar comparisons. The primary strategy preserves the rollout’s timing instead of treating all automated months as equivalent. It compares each adopter with branches still untreated at the relevant date, avoiding already-treated controls. An April-cohort estimate and a July-cohort estimate should remain separate: they cover different calendar conditions, treatment ages, uptake levels, and operating versions. A pooled two-way fixed-effects coefficient can mix these effects and use inappropriate comparisons when effects vary over time.

This approach is preferable here because the comparisons remain transparent with only five branches. It permits straightforward checks using Delta alone, Epsilon alone, and Gamma before July. Matching and synthetic control cannot create overlap or a long pre-period from this panel; interrupted time series would require estimating several simultaneous breaks from eight months. Neither is a shortcut around endogenous adoption.

Nevertheless, the primary design is a framework for conditional interpretation, not sufficient identification. Three pre-months for early adopters provide little evidence about parallel trends, and Gamma’s longer baseline contains major operational changes. Contemporary arrivals, FTE, and complexity should not automatically enter the primary total-effect model: some may be rollout consequences. Conditioning on them changes the target and can induce bias. Report minimally adjusted and operationally adjusted associations separately. Branch clustering addresses dependence only approximately; five branches cannot support precise conventional inference or strong causal attribution.

These are endpoint slopes, not dose-response effects. Between April and August, Alpha/Beta rework also rises 3.3/2.3 pp despite increasing fast-path share. In the code below, a separate intensity regression controlling descriptively for branch/month, arrivals/FTE, and complexity gives associations per +10 pp share of approximately −0.62 median hours, −1.01 p90 hours, −0.24 rework pp, +0.12 escalation pp, and +0.010 CSAT points. This change in apparent relationship illustrates specification dependence. Completed fast-path share selects on success, case mix, and downstream processing; it is neither randomized exposure nor a valid instrument. Availability is not a valid instrument without implausible exclusion restrictions concerning routing, managerial practices, and queues.

Pre-trends are not established. January–March median changes are Alpha −1.0, Beta +1.0, Gamma +3.2, Delta +1.8, and Epsilon −1.2 hours. Alpha’s February improvement reverses partly in March. Delta/Epsilon’s average January–March movement is only +0.3 hours, but cancellation conceals their opposing trajectories. March adds 2 pp escalation everywhere. Gamma’s median rises from 36.4 in January to 45.9 in June before declining in July. Selection of historically weak branches and a high pre-adoption Gamma month makes regression to the mean plausible. A failed pre-trend rejection would have very low power and would not validate the design.

Sensitivity to controls and baselines is substantial, even where relative signs agree:

A separate bias stress test makes the uncertainty explicit. Write D = effect + b, where b is the untreated adopter-versus-control difference in change. Across adopters and the two individual donors, primary-window D ranges are median −6.39 to −2.58 hours and p90 −8.80 to −4.77 hours. If one assumes |b| ≤ 5 median hours and |b| ≤ 10 p90 hours, the corresponding effect envelopes become approximately [−11.4, +2.4] and [−18.8, +5.2] hours. The 5/10-hour allowances approximately match the pooled controls’ June-to-July/August recovery of 4.98 median hours and 9.30 p90 hours: they test shocks on a scale actually observed in this panel. They are assumed stress allowances, not estimated bounds or confidence intervals. Differential shocks of this size can overturn the signs; without restrictions on b, no finite data-based causal interval follows.

Using the same illustrative approach, primary-window rework D spans −2.64 to −1.22 pp; allowing ±2 pp differential drift yields [−4.64, +0.78] pp. Escalation D spans +0.62 to +1.18 pp; allowing ±1 pp drift yields [−0.38, +2.18] pp. Respondent-CSAT D spans +0.062 to +0.111; allowing ±0.10 score drift yields [−0.038, +0.211]. These rate/score allowances are on the scale of observed activation changes and calendar variation, not calibrated bounds on confounding. These envelopes combine different branch targets, not one common treatment effect.

Survey selection creates further uncertainty. For respondent mean m and response fraction r, the full-customer mean lies between r m + (1−r)×1 and r m + (1−r)×5 without assumptions about nonrespondents. Using the approximate minimum r = 0.20, Gamma’s pre mean 4.003 gives [1.601, 4.801] and its post mean 4.090 gives [1.618, 4.818]. Even the equal-month full-customer before/after change can therefore range roughly [−3.18, +3.22]. These are selection bounds for a change, not a causal effect, and depend on treating 20% as a genuine lower response-rate bound. Actual branch-period response counts would sharpen them.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

expand with an embedded evaluation

The next subset should be readiness-qualified intake teams in both Delta and Epsilon, stratified by branch, baseline complexity, and workload. Epsilon provides a high-complexity setting; Delta provides a lower-complexity setting. Randomize matched queue-sharing team clusters to immediate availability or a delayed start after the evaluation endpoint. Keep a concurrent delayed group through the primary 12-week assessment. Use separate local queue allocations where feasible; if teams cannot be independent, randomize whole-branch order and recruit additional comparable branches. Two branch assignments alone cannot deliver precise inference. Before assignment, use baseline variance and cluster correlation to plan adequate units and follow-up.

Collect at least 12 weeks of pre-period event data and 12 weeks after activation, including the ramp; recover a year of historical data for seasonal checks where possible. Prespecify weeks 1–4 and 5–12 as dynamic contrasts. Hold planned routing, productive staffing, training, and escalation thresholds comparable across randomized groups, recording unavoidable deviations. Analyze availability by original assignment; measure actual assignment and completed variants separately.

Link registration, triage, specialist review, information requests, resolution, closure, transfers, and recontacts with case identifiers and timestamps. Capture registration eligibility/risk, software version, overrides, service and waiting times, productive hours, completions/FTE, WIP, aged backlog, and unfinished cases. Measure median, p90, SLA violations, loops, escalation appropriateness, errors, and 30-day recontacts. Record survey invitations, response counts, and case mix; sample nonrespondents for follow-up.

Monitor fraud-desk inflow, source branch, staffing, utilization, waiting-time distribution, and WIP across every branch. Predefine capacity responses and record them as part of the policy. The trial estimates effects at its assigned rollout saturation; full expansion may require another saturation step to assess network congestion. Monitor errors, overdue cases, complaints, and customer scores weekly. Stop a rollout arm for a credible serious customer incident or sustained queue deterioration; investigate before restarting.

Scale after the primary window only if the randomized availability contrast supports at least a 5% median improvement and improved throughput without growing backlog, while prespecified uncertainty limits exclude p90 worsening above 5%, rework worsening above 1 pp, and customer-mean CSAT worsening above 0.10. Require stable case-mix-adjusted quality and no material harm at untreated branches or the desk. Use inference appropriate to the randomized units; extend the delayed-start evaluation if precision or survey representativeness is insufficient.

    first, last = s.iloc[0], s.iloc[-1]

        "median_h_per_10pp": 10 * (last.median_cycle_h - first.median_cycle_h)

    })

print(pd.DataFrame(endpoint_rows).round(4).to_string(index=False))

# Donor sensitivity and explicit assumed bias envelopes; not confidence intervals.
donor_rows = []
for branch, start in adoption.items():
    s = df[df.branch.eq(branch)]
    change_s = (s[s.month >= start][outcomes].mean()
                - s[s.month < start][outcomes].mean())
    for donor in never:
        c = df[df.branch.eq(donor)]
        change_c = (c[c.month >= start][outcomes].mean()
                    - c[c.month < start][outcomes].mean())
        donor_rows.append({"adopter": branch, "donor": donor,
                           **(change_s - change_c).to_dict()})
donor_table = pd.DataFrame(donor_rows)
print("\nINDIVIDUAL-DONOR DESCRIPTIVE CONTRASTS")
print(donor_table.round(4).to_string(index=False))
assumed_drift = {"median_cycle_h": 5.0, "p90_cycle_h": 10.0,
                 "rework_pct": 2.0, "escalation_pct": 1.0, "csat_1to5": 0.10}
for outcome, allowance in assumed_drift.items():
    low = donor_table[outcome].min() - allowance
    high = donor_table[outcome].max() + allowance
    print(f"ASSUMED-DRIFT SCENARIO {outcome}: [{low:.4f}, {high:.4f}]; "
          f"assumed |differential drift| <= {allowance}; NOT a causal CI")

# These are customer-selection bounds for an equal-month before/after change,
# not bounds for a causal effect; 0.20 is treated as an assumed minimum response rate.
g = df[df.branch.eq("Gamma")]
m_pre = g[g.month < "2026-07"].csat_1to5.mean()
m_post = g[g.month >= "2026-07"].csat_1to5.mean()
def selection_bounds(m, r=0.20):
    return r * m + (1-r) * 1, r * m + (1-r) * 5
pre_lo, pre_hi = selection_bounds(m_pre)
post_lo, post_hi = selection_bounds(m_post)
print("\nGAMMA FULL-CUSTOMER CHANGE SELECTION BOUNDS, ASSUMING r>=0.20:",
      round(post_lo - pre_hi, 4), round(post_hi - pre_lo, 4))
print("\nNo raw event logs were supplied or fabricated. "
      "No process-discovery algorithm is applied to these aggregates.")

## 10. Final claim ladder

- Observed: Availability and completed automated-fast-path shares increased; Alpha/Beta’s broad-window p90 worsened, Gamma’s summaries improved, and untreated branches also changed substantially.
- Suggestive: Adoption coincides with lower median/p90 deterioration and lower recorded rework relative to selected comparison branches, plus additional escalation; case mix, capacity, seasonality, and shared queues remain competing explanations.
- Conditionally causal: If untreated trends would have been comparable, anticipation and spillovers were absent or correctly modeled, and versions/reporting were stable, these contrasts could estimate short-run rollout-bundle effects of roughly −3 to −6 median hours and −5 to −9 p90 hours for the observed adopters and windows.
- Not supported: A universal “automation reduces cycle time by X%,” a causal dose-response from completed fast-path share, demonstrated capacity creation, customer-wide satisfaction improvement, or a precise prediction for full expansion to Delta/Epsilon.
