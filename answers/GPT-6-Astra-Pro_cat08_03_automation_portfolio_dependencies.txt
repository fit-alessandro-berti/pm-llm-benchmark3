**Recommend a first-year portfolio of A12, A6, A4, and A5: control remediation, financial normalization, entity-mismatch assistance, and document completeness.** This prioritizes safe flow through scarce specialist and analyst capacity rather than the number of transactions automated.

Two comparisons explain the departure from frequency-based ranking. Registration has 11,880 occurrences, eight minutes of mean service, and 1.5 hours of median pre-activity waiting. Normalization has only 5,200 occurrences but 75 minutes of service and 20 hours of waiting. Multiplying frequency by mean service gives approximately **1,584 versus 6,500 recorded service-hours** over the supplied observation period—not annual savings, automatable hours, or staffing estimates. 

Entity mismatch is still less frequent, but has 31 hours of median waiting and is on the critical path in 82% of cases where it occurs. That is stronger evidence of end-to-end leverage than registration’s consistently early position. Actual benefit nevertheless depends on assistant coverage and specialist response capacity.  

## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

### Reconstruct readiness, not just recorded sequence

The operational dependency structure is approximately:

```text
Reliable intake and verified product/exposure classification
                  |
          +-------+----------------------+
          |                              |
    KYC branch                    Financial-information branch
    A3: hit assistance            A5: completeness and clear requests
    A4: mismatch assistance       Financial document review
          |                       A6: normalization
    Verified clearance            Credit analysis and notes
          |                              |
          +------------------------------+
                         |
          Required collateral valuation also ready
          A7: triage/routing, not elimination of external work
                         |
          Committee packaging readiness, where applicable
          A8: assembly assistance + independent human completion
                         |
          Committee decision / verified applicable approval
                         |
          A9: contract generation from authoritative terms
                         |
          Valid KYC clearance + identity/consent prerequisites
                         |
          A10: customer signing
                         |
          Complete and current control evidence
                         |
          A11: loan activation

A12: evidence, holds, remediation routing and accountable sign-off
     across these boundaries.
```

This is a **proposed readiness model**, not an executable model inferred from the directly-follows graph. In particular, KYC and financial review often overlap despite sequential system recording. Committee packaging requires several reconciled inputs, including valuation when required. 

For a committee-routed case, a useful measurement definition is:

$$
t_{\text{pack-ready}}
=
\max\left(
t_{\text{KYC-clear}},
t_{\text{normalized-financials-ready}},
t_{\text{credit-notes-ready}},
t_{\text{required-valuation-ready}}
\right).
$$

Exclude valuation from the maximum when it is not required. If a mandatory input is unresolved, the case is not ready. Distinguish this readiness time from packaging start and the subsequent committee slot.

An improvement to one branch shortens readiness only insofar as that branch was determining the maximum. Thus, **do not add activity-level median waits to estimate either cycle time or automation savings**.

### Data quality and approval dependencies

A5 requires an accurate product/exposure classification; otherwise, it can efficiently request the wrong documents. A6 requires adequate documents and traceable transformations. A8 requires consistent versions of all committee artifacts. A9 requires approved terms—not inferred terms from an unreconciled pack. These dependencies can be satisfied through reviewed manual processes; purchasing every upstream automation module is not necessary.

The selected DFG contains `Resolve entity mismatch → Contract preparation` 380 times. That edge warrants a state-and-evidence check, but is not, by itself, proof that KYC was bypassed: a directly-follows edge does not establish the full clearance history. Conversely, the explicitly reported provisional-signing finding is direct evidence of a control problem.  

Likewise, the activity frequencies do not establish that every application requires committee review. Any legitimate non-committee route must have its own verified approval requirements; absence from the selected DFG is not permission to bypass approval.

### Controls and human escalation are prerequisites

Under the supplied rules, KYC must precede signing, High-exposure SME analysis and committee packaging require different completing persons, and contract terms must match the committee decision. Automation must retain model suggestions, human overrides and final decisions. 

**A12 scoping assumption:** its funded implementation includes structured control evidence, unresolved-control holds and accountable remediation sign-off, connected to existing release controls or a controlled manual release procedure. A dashboard that merely lists violations would be insufficient. This scope does **not** silently include A3’s classifier, A9’s contract generator, or A11’s activation bot.

Three compliance specialists—and dependency on two senior specialists—make escalation capacity a hard operating constraint. A4 therefore needs protected review time and a qualified backup, not an unbounded queue of machine-generated recommendations. The nine analysts likewise need capacity for verification and exception handling, not just an assumed reduction in normalization work. 

### External valuation and integration constraints

Valuation remains outside direct control. A7 could improve triage, routing and permitted parallel initiation; it cannot guarantee shorter partner turnaround. Any earlier commissioning must respect approval requirements, information sufficiency and the risk of paying for valuations on cases that will not proceed. Its importance is especially plausible for Equipment finance, where valuation is required in 71% of cases, versus 18% for Working-capital.  

For the recommended portfolio, reserve the first six months’ **two deep integrations for A12 and A6**. Treat A4’s reviewed case-bundle assistance and A5’s checklist/instruction pilot as light integrations only after IT confirms that classification. If they need deep core connectivity, keep them offline or advisory until month seven rather than relabeling the work to evade the limit. The four-module annual ceiling still applies. 

## 3. Portfolio alternatives

All three portfolios contain four modules and include a control-strengthening module in the first wave. Sharing A12 is a deliberate design choice: the observed control failures cross several process boundaries. Another module could satisfy the compliance requirement if equivalently scoped, but that should be demonstrated rather than assumed.

| Portfolio                                    | Modules             | What it optimizes                                                                                       | What it sacrifices                                                                                                                          | Feasible first-six-month integration allocation                                                             |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Quick-win**                                | **A1, A5, A9, A12** | Visible intake efficiency, clearer document requests, contract productivity, and controlled remediation | Leaves specialist mismatch resolution and analyst normalization largely unchanged; downstream savings may be stranded behind upstream waits | A12 + A1 deeply integrated; A5 light pilot; A9 deep deployment after month six and only for verified inputs |
| **Bottleneck/synchronization — recommended** | **A4, A5, A6, A12** | KYC branch clearance, complete financial inputs, analyst capacity, and safe downstream readiness        | No registration showcase; no direct valuation-partner optimization, automated pack assembly or signing acceleration                         | A12 + A6 deeply integrated; A4 advisory and A5 light pilot subject to IT validation                         |
| **Compliance-first**                         | **A2, A3, A9, A12** | Correct control routing, supervised KYC hit handling, contract consistency, and accountable remediation | Does not directly fix missing-document behavior or normalization workload; may increase review and escalation demand                        | A12 + A2 deeply integrated; A3 shadow evaluation; A9 and any deep A3 integration after month six            |

**Why A5 rather than A7 in the recommendation?** A5 addresses a documented internal source of avoidable loops: incomplete instructions from relationship managers. It also improves A6’s input quality. A7 addresses a major delay, but partner responsiveness and its case-level critical-path contribution are not quantified. The selected portfolio therefore favors complementary internal changes with clearer ownership. 

A7 is the first reserve, not a dismissed opportunity. Before committing all four modules, an Equipment-finance-heavy cohort in which valuation is consistently the last missing prerequisite could justify substituting A7 for a still-uncommitted module. It must be a substitution, not an unfunded fifth module.

## 4. Expected process-level effects

The effects below are hypotheses to test. They are not additive savings forecasts, and faster activity execution does not necessarily mean faster completion.

### Quick-win portfolio: A1, A5, A9, A12

**Cycle time and waiting time.** A1 should reduce registration effort and may reduce intake queuing, but registration’s early position does not make it the decisive bottleneck. A9 could reduce contract preparation effort and some legal-operations peaks. Nevertheless, unresolved KYC, normalization and valuation can continue to determine when cases reach contracting. Legal ops is already underutilized when KYC is slow, so faster contract generation may have little effect during those periods.   

**Rework and handovers.** A5 should target `Financial document review → Request missing documents` and the return to review—3,300 and 2,800 observed edges respectively—by improving the first request, not merely increasing reminder frequency. A9 and A12 should target `Contract preparation → Remediate control issue → Contract preparation`, whose selected edge counts are 650 and 510. These are transition frequencies, not unique affected applications.  

**Resource demand and control violations.** Back-office touch work should fall, but time-of-day redeployment may be more valuable than assuming headcount savings: utilization already falls below 65% after 15:00. Cleaner intake could overload analysts because this portfolio omits A6. A9 must operate only on reviewed, consistent approval inputs; otherwise it accelerates incorrect contracts. A12 addresses release holds and independent completion, although recorded violations may initially rise as detection improves. 

### Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A4, A5, A6, A12

**Cycle time and waiting time.** A4 targets the 31-hour pre-mismatch wait and a branch reported as critical in 82% of affected cases. A6 targets normalization effort and its 20-hour wait, while A5 seeks to prevent avoidable document-return delays. Because KYC and financial review can overlap, the combined effect must be measured through branch readiness rather than summed. Customer response time, external valuation and committee cadence remain residual constraints.  

**Rework and handovers.** A4 should improve the evidence presented on `KYC check → Resolve entity mismatch` and reduce avoidable returns along `Resolve entity mismatch → KYC check`—1,750 and 1,160 observed transitions. A5 targets repeated document requests; A6 improves inputs along `Normalize financial statements → Credit analysis`, observed 5,000 times. Necessary specialist review and four-eyes handovers must remain. The objective is fewer failed transfers and clarification cycles, not the fewest possible handovers. 

**Resource demand and control violations.** Specialist work should shift from assembling evidence toward adjudicating exceptions. Analyst work should shift from transcription and normalization toward validation and credit judgment. Neither shift guarantees fewer total hours: previously blocked cases can increase demand for the 110-minute credit-analysis activity. More ready cases may also create packaging or legal peaks. A12 must prevent productivity gains from recreating month-end shortcuts, including the reported High-exposure four-eyes violations.  

### Compliance-first portfolio: A2, A3, A9, A12

**Cycle time and waiting time.** Correct classification and earlier identification of control problems could reduce late remediation. However, more accurate routing may deliberately send additional cases to specialist or high-exposure review. A3 false positives could worsen the specialist queue. Without A5 and A6, financial-document and normalization delays remain largely untreated.

**Rework and handovers.** A2 should reduce wrong-path rework at the classification split into KYC and document review. A9 is particularly relevant to Credit-line renewal, where contract-remediation loops are reported at 14%, compared with 8.5% overall. Yet more independent review handovers may be desirable rather than evidence of failure.  

**Resource demand and control violations.** Expect additional compliance adjudication, rule maintenance and quality-assurance work during deployment. This portfolio has the strongest direct coverage of routing, KYC suggestions and contract generation, but it should not be declared safer until false-negative risks and override behavior are tested. A3 remains supervised; compliance responsibility stays with accountable staff. A12 must still enforce separation between High-exposure credit analysis and packaging completion.

### Capacity accounting common to all portfolios

For each resource pool, assess:

$$
H_{\text{after}}
=
H_{\text{baseline}}
-
H_{\text{avoided effort}}
+
H_{\text{verification}}
+
H_{\text{exceptions and recovery}}
+
H_{\text{additional throughput}}
+
H_{\text{automation oversight}}.
$$

Use a consistent observation period and avoid double-counting eliminated rework. Waiting time removed is not automatically labor saved.

Monitor actual human work transfers separately from technical event transitions. Replacing human resource identifiers with a bot account can make a handover network appear simpler without reducing coordination effort.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

| Risk introduced or amplified                       | Compensating control                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Unsafe progression despite unresolved controls** | Use authoritative, current evidence for KYC, approval, signing and activation eligibility. Missing or contradictory evidence creates a hold. A ticket closure must not substitute for substantive clearance. Control every signing route, including manual or provisional workarounds.             |
| **False negatives and false positives**            | For A3/A4, retain human clearance authority and audit both escalated and non-escalated cases. Test false entity merges as well as missed matches. For A2, escalate ambiguous exposure rather than defaulting to a lower-control route. For A1, permit recovery of falsely suppressed applications. |
| **Incorrect financial extraction**                 | Preserve source-page provenance, units, currency, period and transformations. Use validation checks and human approval of material values. Route poor-quality documents to manual handling rather than silently accepting low-confidence outputs.                                                  |
| **Loss of auditability**                           | Record input and artifact versions, model/rule version, suggestion, confidence where applicable, reviewer identity, override reason and final decision. Preserve histories after correction. Protect customer documents through approved access and handling arrangements.                         |
| **Four-eyes erosion**                              | Check real human identities and completion responsibilities. A bot assembling a pack is not an independent second person. Require a qualified human other than the High-exposure credit analyst to complete the packaging responsibility.                                                          |
| **Drift and stale rules**                          | Monitor performance by product, exposure and document type. Revalidate after policy, template or model changes. Version and approve rule libraries; retain rollback. Invalidate downstream readiness when a material upstream artifact changes.                                                    |
| **Exception overload and automation failure**      | Assign queue owners, age-based escalation, qualified backups and protected review capacity. Provide tested manual fallback and duplicate-safe recovery. Do not interpret an expired timeout as approval.                                                                                           |
| **Customer friction and uneven access**            | Co-design A5 instructions with RMs; request only relevant documents, explain alternatives, limit reminder frequency and retain assisted channels. Track repeated requests, abandonment and complaints by segment and channel.                                                                      |
| **Shifted congestion and concentrated dependency** | Monitor analyst verification, specialist escalations, committee-ready backlog and legal month-end peaks. Slow expansion when downstream demand exceeds supported capacity; do not conceal delay by moving it to an unmeasured queue.                                                               |

The supplied control findings are acceptance-test priorities: reported month-end four-eyes violations, provisional signing, and contract-remediation loops. Their percentages have different meanings and potentially different denominators; they must not be combined into one “automation error rate.” 

These controls preserve responsibility rather than transferring it to software. A qualified control owner remains accountable for the clearance or decision; automation provides evidence, checks and workflow support.

A4’s strategic rank does not require it to be the first transactional deployment. Its specialist knowledge should be captured early, while A12 establishes controls and A6 prepares the analyst pool for additional clean inflow.

### Measurement and evaluation

**Process outcomes.** Measure receive-to-activation duration alongside time to other valid terminal outcomes. Retain still-open cases in backlog and aging measures; comparing only activated cases could make a faster-looking cohort conceal stranded applications. Report completed-case medians and tails separately from open-case aging, stratified by Micro/SME/High-exposure and product.

**Synchronization.** Record which prerequisite is last to become ready for each committee-routed case. Separate time spent awaiting KYC, documents, valuation or analysis from time spent waiting after the pack could have been prepared. Measure committee-ready waiting separately from preparation effort. This will show whether A7 or A8 becomes the next priority.

**Rework and customer effects.** Monitor the named DFG loops, document first-pass completeness, number of requests per application, mismatch reopening, contract remediation, overrides, abandonment and complaints. Preserve the distinction between event-level exception rates and case-level outcomes.

**Resource demand.** Track hands-on normalization and verification effort, exception arrivals and ages, specialist concentration, analyst backlog, qualified independent-checker availability, and legal month-end peaks. Back-office improvements should also be evaluated by time of day.

**Control quality.** Track KYC-before-signing violations, High-exposure same-person completion, terms mismatches, inappropriate control downgrades, audit completeness, and sampled false-positive/false-negative outcomes. Reconcile event timestamps with the effective time of clearance rather than relying solely on recording order.

### Expansion and stop criteria

Before each pilot, business and compliance owners should sign off the minimum evaluation sample, quality tolerances and acceptable resource guardrails. The prompt does not supply enough evidence to choose defensible numerical thresholds.

Expansion should require improvement in the module’s intended outcome with no unacceptable deterioration in queue-wide tail delay, control quality or customer burden. A12 may initially lengthen processing by enforcing previously bypassed checks; that should be reported separately from automation inefficiency.

Any confirmed severe misclearance, unauthorized signing, material contract discrepancy released to the customer, or invalid independent approval should stop the affected automated release path pending investigation. “No observed incident” in a small pilot is not proof of negligible risk.

Use a staggered evaluation with comparable product/exposure cohorts and explicit month-end coverage. Because cases share specialists and analysts, report effects on untreated cases and the whole queue—not just the automated subset.

## 7. Final recommendation

**Fund A12 + A6 + A4 + A5.** The portfolio combines an enforceable control foundation with analyst-capacity improvement, specialist KYC assistance and prevention of avoidable document loops. Its value comes from the interaction among the modules, not four independent labor-saving business cases.

The principal sacrifice is deliberate: external valuation, committee cadence and signing may still dominate some cases. Keep A7 as the first reserve, especially for Equipment finance, and use readiness measurements to determine whether it should replace a still-uncommitted module before spending the full budget.

The bank should **not automate the most frequent activity first merely because it is most frequent**. Receive application is the most frequent activity and is already digital for 72% of cases; A1 targets registration, the next-most-frequent activity. Both observations weaken a simple volume-based investment argument. 

A1 remains a credible later investment—or a conscious political trade-off—but the current evidence points toward scarce compliance expertise, analyst workload, synchronization dependencies and unreliable controls as more consequential constraints. The recommended sequence therefore establishes control integrity, prepares downstream capacity, and only then expands clean case flow. Success should mean **safer, more predictable end-to-end completion without exporting delay and exception work elsewhere**, not simply more automated events.
