# 1. Mechanism map

The proposed restructuring shifts the organization from a **unit-grouped, locally coupled topology** to a **functionally pooled, queue-mediated topology**. In process-mining terms, this replaces high-frequency, low-latency, high-context local edges with lower-frequency, potentially higher-latency, low-context cross-boundary edges.

| Restructuring Mechanism | Organizational Dynamic | Expected Process-Mining Effect (Observable in Event Log) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nominal Handover Reduction & Central Queue Formation**<br>*(Merging N/S/E_Tech into Technical Shared Service; shared return queue)* | Eliminates informal, undocumented "over-the-shoulder" consultations, but formalizes every specialist interaction into a ticketed queue transfer. | **DFG & Handover Network:** Local `caseworker -> embedded tech` edges disappear; replaced by a dense hub-and-spoke topology centering on `Technical Shared Service`. While *ad-hoc* cross-team handovers drop (from 6 to ~3/100), formal referrals *increase* (from 36 to 41+ per 100 cases) because local caseworkers lose informal triage help. |
| **Specialist Utilization & Queueing Dynamics**<br>*(Target +15% utilization; routing difficult Quality Check & Legal/Risk triage to shared pool)* | PoolingN_Tech (71%), S_Tech (68%), and E_Tech (82%) balances load initially. However, adding new tasks (difficult QC, Legal/Risk triage) pushes pooled utilization toward the >85% non-linear queuing threshold. | **Performance DFG & Temporal Features:** In Kingman’s VUT equation, pushing utilization from ~73% average to ~88%+ causes exponential growth in `waiting_time` prior to `Specialist Review` and `Quality Check`, even as `service_time` stays constant. |
| **Rework & Local Expertise Loss**<br>*(Returning advice via a shared pull-queue; decoupling specialists from regional teams)* | A specialist in North evaluating an East or South case lacks tacit knowledge of regional conventions (`local_expertise_flag = False`). Disproportionate increases in `waiting_time`, `reassignment_count`, and unfavorable `outcome` rates for vulnerable `customer_segment` cohorts requiring regional nuance. |

# 2. Scenario forecasts

### Optimistic Scenario (Pooling Dividend Realized)
* **Handover Pattern:** Formal referrals match management’s forecast (41/100 to Tech Shared Service, 12/100 to Legal/Risk). The `subcontracting_network` remains clean: even though any specialist can pick up a case, returned advice is cleanly integrated by the originating caseworker without ping-pong loops. Ad-hoc cross-team handovers drop to <3/100.
* **Queue/Waiting-Time Pattern:** Standardizing case notes before launch prevents context-switching penalties. Pooling N_Tech, S_Tech, and E_Tech absorbs East’s historical backlog. Median wait for Tech Shared Service settles at ~3.5h–4.0h; Shared Approval settles at ~2.8h.
* **Cycle-Time Distribution:** East’s median cycle time drops from 8.6d to ~7.2d, and its P90 drops from 22.8d to ~17.5d. North and South see negligible median changes (+0.2d), resulting in a tighter, more uniform organizational distribution.
* **Rework and Quality Pattern:** Standardized specialist and supervisor criteria reduce variance. Overall rework drops from ~9.2% weighted average to ~7.5%. Difficult Quality Checks catch errors early without clogging the queue.
* **Role Clarity and Accountability:** Clear routing rules (`case_complexity = High`, `required_specialty`) eliminate ambiguity. Caseworkers use standardized templates, and shared supervisors maintain clear audit trails.
* **Fairness Implications:** Regional equity improves dramatically as East customers receive the same service levels as North and South. Standardized decision rules eliminate regional leniency/strictness biases.
* **Early Warning Signs (Positive):** Stable queue depth at end-of-day; high first-time-right rate on cross-region specialist reviews (`rework_flag = False`).

### Central Scenario (The Redistribution Trade-off)
* **Handover Pattern:** Loss of informal local advice causes defensive referrals: `local caseworker -> Technical Shared Service` rises to 48/100 (above the 41/100 forecast). Return handovers frequently go to a *different* local caseworker via the pull queue, breaking the `discover_subcontracting_network` loop.
* **Queue/Waiting-Time Pattern:** Tech Shared Service utilization hits ~85% due to added QC and triage duties. Median wait times rise to 5.5h (Tech) and 4.5h (Shared Approval). Legal/Risk queue wait times jump from 9.6h to 14.0h+ as broader detection rules push volume up by 33% into a resource pool already at 91–93% utilization.
* **Cycle-Time Distribution:** Overall organizational *mean/median* cycle time appears flat (~7.4d), masking a structural divergence: simple cases accelerate (due to bot/local focus), while high-complexity cases suffer severe tail expansion (P90 across the org rises from ~18.5d to 23.0d+). East improves slightly at the median; North and South degrade by 0.8d–1.2d.
* **Rework and Quality Pattern:** Whenever N_Tech or S_Tech handles an East case (or vice versa), lack of regional context (`local_expertise_flag = False`) triggers clarification loops. Rework in North and South rises from 8.2%/9.0% to ~10.5%, offsetting gains in East.
* **Role Clarity and Accountability:** Moderate friction. Caseworkers blame the central queue for SLA misses; central specialists blame local caseworkers for poor documentation (`handover_reason = incomplete_notes`).
* **Fairness Implications:** East catches up to North/South, but complex customer segments (`customer_segment` requiring regional/language accommodation) experience 30% longer cycle times than standard segments due to context loss in the central pool.
* **Early Warning Signs:** Rising `reassignment_count` inside the shared queue; widening gap between median and 90th percentile waiting times on `Specialist Review`.

### Adverse Scenario (Central Bottleneck & De-skilling Spiral)
* **Handover Pattern:** Severe defensive routing and ping-ponging. Caseworkers dump every moderately ambiguous case into the central queue (60+ per 100 cases). Specialists reject poorly documented cases back to local queues, creating repetitive loops (`Local Technical Assessment <-> Specialist Review`).
* **Queue/Waiting-Time Pattern:** Pooling Tech + Legal/Risk + QC with one fewer supervisor creates a gridlocked central hub. Utilization exceeds 92% across shared queues. Waiting times compound at every stage: Tech wait >12h, Approval wait >9h, Legal/Risk wait >24h.
* **Cycle-Time Distribution:** Bimodal distribution. Trivial automated/local cases finish in <2 days, keeping the *mean* deceptive for the first few weeks, while anything touching the shared service blows past SLAs. Organizational P90 exceeds 28 days; SLA miss rates double across North and South (>14%) and fail to improve in East.
* **Rework and Quality Pattern:** Rework spikes toward the historical cross-team rate (15%–18%) across all specialist interactions because tacit local knowledge is severed. Quality Checks bottleneck implementation, causing batch-and-queue rework waves.
* **Role Clarity and Accountability:** Systemic diffusion of responsibility. Pull queues exhibit severe "cherry-picking" (easy cases pulled immediately; complex/regional cases aging at the bottom of the queue). Local caseworkers deskill rapidly, functioning as passive clerical routers.
* **Fairness Implications:** Severe regression. Customers with non-standard regional needs or high complexity face systemic neglect in the shared pull-queue. North and South customer satisfaction plummets from 4.3/4.1 toward 3.4, while East sees no net benefit due to central congestion.
* **Early Warning Signs:** Rapid growth in work-in-progress (WIP) in shared queues within week 1; spike in `reassignment_count >= 3`; waiting time on high-complexity cases diverging from medium-complexity cases inside the same queue.

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

Relying on mean or median organizational cycle time (e.g., observing a stable ~7.4-day average) can mask severe operational degradation due to seven structural phenomena:

1. **Bimodal Distribution and Tail Latency (P90/P99 Explosion):**
   Increasing automation (`Bot_Eligibility`) and keeping simple cases local will accelerate the bottom 50% of cases (e.g., reducing them from 4 days to 2 days). In an average calculation, this mathematically cancels out a severe blowout in the top 20% of cases (e.g., high-complexity cases stretching from 16 days to 28 days due to compounding central queues at Tech, Legal/Risk, and Approval).
2. **High-Complexity Case Starvation (Multi-Queue Compounding):**
   Simple cases traverse zero shared queues. High-complexity cases with `required_specialty` in `{legal, risk}` and high value must traverse *three* shared queues sequentially (`Technical Shared Service` $\to$ `Legal/Risk Queue` $\to$ `Shared Approval Queue`, plus difficult `Quality Check`). Because Legal (93%) and Risk (91%) are already saturated, adding 33% more volume (from 9 to 12 per 100 cases) will trigger non-linear queue blowups exclusively on complex cases.
3. **Tacit Local Knowledge Loss and Hidden Rework:**
   Currently, cross-team handovers suffer an 18% rework rate (vs. 8–9% locally) due to inconsistent regional notes and conventions. Centralizing specialists turns ~67% of specialist interactions into de facto cross-team handovers (`local_expertise_flag = False`). Even if initial queue routing is fast, cases will bounce back during `Implementation` or `Quality Check`, generating rework loops that inflate resource consumption and customer frustration without necessarily moving the median cycle time of all cases.
4. **Geographic and Demographic Fairness Inversion:**
   Improving East’s median cycle time (by relieving E_Tech’s 82% utilization) while degrading North and South creates a geographic redistribution that looks neutral in aggregate. Worse, `customer_segment` groups requiring regional conventions or non-standard accommodations lose their local advocate, suffering higher rejection rates (`outcome`), more `Local Customer Contact` friction, and longer waits.
5. **Local De-skilling and Long-Term Capacity Atrophy (Learning Loss):**
   When embedded specialists sit with local teams, informal exchanges upskill local caseworkers (`N_Case1`, `S_Case1`), keeping 64% of cases out of specialist queues entirely. Routing all hard cases to a central black box stops local learning. Over 6–12 months, medium-complexity cases that used to be handled locally will be escalated, steadily inflating central queue arrival rates.
6. **Accountability Dilution and Pull-Queue Cherry-Picking:**
   Shared pull-queues for specialist advice and supervisor approval sever end-to-end case ownership. Resources optimize their personal throughput metrics by pulling well-documented, low-complexity cases, leaving poorly documented or messy regional cases to stagnate. The *average* cleared case looks fast, while aging WIP accumulates silently.
7. **Fragile Exception Handling and Escalation Gridlock:**
   Removing one supervisor (from 3 to 2) while routing all medium/high-value approvals and difficult Quality Checks through shared queues removes local buffer capacity. When an exception occurs (e.g., an urgent high-complexity rework loop or a specialist absence), there is no local supervisor with end-to-end context to fast-track the case across functional silos.

# 5. Pilot design

Rather than an irreversible "big bang" reorganization, management should execute a **staggered, controlled 8-week pilot** designed to relieve East’s bottleneck while preserving North/South stability and protecting the saturated Legal/Risk pool.

* **Pilot Scope (Virtual Overflow Pool, Not Full Centralization):**
  * Form a *virtual* Technical Shared Service using **E_Tech** (currently 82% utilized) and **S_Tech** (currently 68% utilized), while keeping physical/team alignment for simple consultations.
  * **Exclude Legal and Risk (93%/91% utilization)** from increased routing rules until case-note standardization is proven and capacity is added.
  * **Retain all 3 local supervisors** during the pilot, testing the shared approval queue between South and East only.
* **Comparison Group (Quasi-Experimental Control):**
  * **Control Group:** **Team North** (`N_Intake`, `N_Case`, `N_Tech`, `N_Supervisor`) remains completely unchanged in its embedded local model. This controls for seasonal volume shifts and macro trends.
  * **Treatment Group:** **Team East** (under-resourced baseline) and **Team South** (well-resourced baseline). This tests whether South degrades when rescuing East.
* **Pre/Post Observation Window:**
  * **Pre-Pilot Baseline:** 8 weeks of historical event log data (plus 2 weeks of pre-pilot standardized case-note training across all teams to isolate documentation effects from structural effects).
  * **Pilot Execution:** 8 weeks total — Weeks 1–2 (ramp-up/stabilization), Weeks 3–8 (steady-state evaluation window, plus 2 weeks of run-out to capture lagging P90 cycle times of cohort cases).
* **Segmentation Strategy:**
  * Partition all metrics across a 4-dimensional matrix: `team` (North vs. South vs. East) $\times$ `case_complexity` (Low/Med/High) $\times$ `required_specialty` (Tech vs. Legal/Risk/Security) $\times$ `customer_segment` (Standard vs. Regional/Accommodated).
* **Guardrail Metrics (Hard Stop / Alert Thresholds):**
  * *South P90 Cycle Time:* Must not increase by $> 10\%$ (i.e., must stay $< 20.0\text{d}$).
  * *Cross-Location Rework Rate:* Rework on cases where `South_Tech` serves `East` (or vice versa, `local_expertise_flag == False`) must stay $< 12\%$ (well below the current 18% cross-team baseline).
  * *Referral Inflation:* South/East specialist referral rate must not exceed $42/100$ cases.
  * *Legal/Risk Queue Wait:* Median wait must not exceed $10.5\text{h}$.
* **Staff Feedback Collection:**
  * Weekly pulse survey for caseworkers and specialists tracking: (1) perceived clarity of case notes (`1–5 scale`), (2) frequency of informal 5-minute triage questions lost, and (3) qualitative tagging of `handover_reason` whenever a case is returned for rework.
* **Rollback or Adjustment Triggers:**
  * **Trigger 1 (Documentation Friction):** If cross-team rework exceeds 13% for two consecutive weeks $\to$ *Adjustment:* Mandate a structured intake/assessment checklist before a case can enter the shared queue.
  * **Trigger 2 (Defensive Dumping):** If South’s referral rate jumps from ~36% to >45% $\to$ *Adjustment:* Restore a dedicated 1-hour daily "local office hours" block where S_Tech/E_Tech do informal, un-queued consults with their home teams.
  * **Trigger 3 (Systemic Congestion):** If South’s P90 cycle time exceeds 21 days or SLA misses rise above 9.5% $\to$ *Rollback:* Revert to embedded local ownership and instead use a pure "overflow-only" routing rule (cases only route cross-team when local tech queue wait exceeds 4 hours).

# 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation: MODIFY AND PILOT FIRST (Reject immediate full rollout).**

Management should **not** proceed with the full restructuring as proposed, particularly the immediate removal of a local supervisor and the expansion of routing into the already-saturated Legal (93%) and Risk (91%) queues. Instead, management should **modify the design into a "Hybrid Local-First + Overflow Shared Pool" model** and test it via the South/East pilot above.

### Rationale and Modifications Required
1. **Solve East’s Capacity Deficit Without Breaking North and South:** East is struggling (8.6d median, 22.8d P90, 11.5% rework) because `E_Tech` is at 82% utilization, whereas `N_Tech` (71%) and `S_Tech` (68%) have slack. You do not need to destroy embedded local relationships in North and South to fix East; an **overflow routing rule** (local cases go to the embedded specialist first, and only spill over to a shared queue if local wait exceeds $X$ hours) captures 80% of the pooling benefit while preserving `local_expertise_flag = True` for the vast majority of cases.
2. **Protect the Legal/Risk Bottleneck:** Increasing Legal/Risk referrals from 9 to 12 per 100 cases (+33% volume) when those specialists are at 91–93% utilization violates basic queuing laws and will cause catastrophic tail delays. Legal/Risk capacity must be expanded or triaged via standardized checklists *before* broadening routing rules.
3. **Standardize Case Notes Before Centralizing Queues:** The current 18% rework rate on cross-team handovers proves that case context does not travel well across locations. Centralizing queues before standardizing documentation will import that 18% rework rate into the core process.
4. **Hold Supervisor Headcount Until Rework Stabilizes:** Cutting a supervisor while simultaneously shifting to shared approval and centralizing difficult Quality Checks removes leadership capacity right when exception handling and transition friction will peak.

### Conditional Go/No-Go Criteria for Scaling
Proceed from the pilot to full organizational adoption **only if** after 6 steady-state pilot weeks:
* East’s P90 cycle time drops below **19.5 days** and SLA misses drop below **8.0%**.
* South’s rework rate remains **$\le 9.5\%$** and P90 cycle time remains **$\le 19.0$ days**.
* Subcontracting return integrity (specialist advice returning to the *same* caseworker) remains **$\ge 80\%$**, and `reassignment_count` does not increase by more than **0.2 per case**.
* Vulnerable `customer_segment` cohorts show **no statistically significant increase** in rework or negative outcomes.

### What Management Must Not Infer from Early Averages
During the first 2–4 weeks post-launch, management **must not** celebrate a drop in average/median cycle time or an increase in specialist utilization to 85%+ as proof of success:
* **Right-Censoring Bias:** In weeks 1–3, completed cases consist disproportionately of fast, simple cases (`Bot_Eligibility` and local-only flows). High-complexity cases stuck in the new shared queues will not finish—and therefore will not enter closed-case cycle time averages—until weeks 4–6.
* **Utilization $\ne$ Throughput:** Hitting 86% specialist utilization may simply mean specialists are spending 20% more time deciphering unfamiliar regional case notes and processing rework loops rather than clearing more unique cases.

# 7. PM4Py analysis sketch

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py


def load_and_prepare_log(csv_path: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Loads event log, formats for PM4Py, and computes temporal/waiting features."""
    df = pd.read_csv(csv_path)
    df["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"], utc=True)

    # Format standard PM4Py dataframe columns
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        timest_format=None,
    )
    df["org:resource"] = df["org:resource"].astype(str)

    # Sort and compute service, waiting, and sojourn times per event
    df = df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"])
    df = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df)
    return df


def analyze_organizational_networks(df: pd.DataFrame, label: str) -> dict:
    """Discovers SNA networks, resource similarity, and organizational roles."""
    # 1. Handover of Work, Working Together, and Subcontracting Networks
    how_net = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(df)
    wt_net = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(df)
    sub_net = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(df)

    # 2. Activity-based resource similarity & Organizational Roles
    res_sim = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(df)
    roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(df)

    return {
        "period": label,
        "handover_of_work": how_net,
        "working_together": wt_net,
        "subcontracting": sub_net,
        "resource_similarity": res_sim,
        "roles": roles,
    }


def analyze_flow_and_queues(df: pd.DataFrame) -> dict:
    """Computes DFG, Performance DFG, Variant dispersion, and Queue Waiting metrics."""
    # 1. Frequency and Performance DFGs
    dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(df)
    perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(df)

    # 2. Variants analysis (detecting ping-pong loops & standardization)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(df)
    top_variants_df = pm4py.filter_variants(
        df, list(variants.keys())[:10]
    )  # Top 10 variants coverage

    # 3. Service vs. Waiting time on critical shared-service activities
    service_times = pm4py.get_service_time(df)
    target_acts = [
        "Specialist Review",
        "Supervisor Approval",
        "Quality Check",
        "Local Technical Assessment",
    ]
    queue_stats = (
        df[df["concept:name"].isin(target_acts)]
        .groupby("concept:name")
        .agg(
            event_count=("case:concept:name", "count"),
            median_wait_sec=("@@waiting_time", "median"),
            p90_wait_sec=("@@waiting_time", lambda x: np.nanpercentile(x, 90)),
            median_service_sec=("@@service_time", "median"),
            local_expertise_rate=("local_expertise_flag", "mean"),
        )
    )
    # Convert seconds to hours
    for col in ["median_wait_sec", "p90_wait_sec", "median_service_sec"]:
        queue_stats[col.replace("_sec", "_hr")] = queue_stats[col] / 3600.0

    return {
        "dfg": dfg,
        "perf_dfg": perf_dfg,
        "total_variants": len(variants),
        "top_10_variant_event_share": len(top_variants_df) / max(len(df), 1),
        "queue_stats": queue_stats,
        "activity_service_times": service_times,
    }


def compute_case_kpis_and_fairness(
    df: pd.DataFrame, sla_days: float = 15.0
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Extracts case-level cycle times, rework, SLA misses, and segments by team/complexity/specialty/customer."""
    # Rework cases per activity via PM4Py
    rework_per_act = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(df)
     temporal_features = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(df)

    # Aggregate case-level table for distributional and fairness slicing
    case_df = df.groupby("case:concept:name").agg(
        start_time=("time:timestamp", "min"),
        end_time=("time:timestamp", "max"),
        team=("team", "first"),
        location=("location", "first"),
        case_complexity=("case_complexity", "first"),
        required_specialty=("required_specialty", "first"),
        customer_segment=("customer_segment", "first"),
        reassignment_count=("reassignment_count", "max"),
        local_expertise_retained=("local_expertise_flag", "min"),
        rework_occurred=("rework_flag", "max"),
        outcome=("outcome", "last"),
    )
    case_df["cycle_time_days"] = (
        case_df["end_time"] - case_df["start_time"]
    ).dt.total_seconds() / 86400.0
    case_df["sla_miss"] = case_df["cycle_time_days"] > sla_days

    # Multi-dimensional summary (Team x Complexity x Specialty x Customer Segment)
    summary = case_df.groupby(
        ["team", "case_complexity", "required_specialty", "customer_segment"],
        dropna=False,
    ).agg(
        cases=("cycle_time_days", "count"),
        median_ct_days=("cycle_time_days", "median"),
        p90_ct_days=("cycle_time_days", lambda x: np.percentile(x, 90)),
        sla_miss_rate=("sla_miss", "mean"),
        rework_rate=("rework_occurred", "mean"),
        mean_reassignments=("reassignment_count", "mean"),
        local_expertise_share=("local_expertise_retained", "mean"),
    )
    return summary, rework_per_act, temporal_features


def compare_pre_post_segments(
    df_pre: pd.DataFrame, df_post: pd.DataFrame
) -> dict:
    """Demonstrates targeted PM4Py attribute filtering to isolate high-risk cohorts."""
    results = {}
    for period_name, log in [("Pre", df_pre), ("Post", df_post)]:
        # Filter 1: Team East vs Team North/South
        east_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            log, "team", ["East"], level="case", retain=True
        )
        north_south_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            log, "team", ["North", "South"], level="case", retain=True
        )

        # Filter 2: High-complexity cases requiring scarce Legal/Risk specialty
        high_comp_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            log, "case_complexity", ["High"], level="case", retain=True
        )
        scarce_spec_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            high_comp_log,
            "required_specialty",
            ["legal", "risk", "security"],
            level="case",
            retain=True,
        )

        # Filter 3: Vulnerable / regional accommodation customer segments
        regional_cust_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            log,
            "customer_segment",
            ["regional_accommodation", "language_support"],
            level="case",
            retain=True,
        )

        results[period_name] = {
            "org_networks": analyze_organizational_networks(log, period_name),
            "east_flow": analyze_flow_and_queues(east_log),
            "north_south_flow": analyze_flow_and_queues(north_south_log),
            "high_comp_scarce_flow": analyze_flow_and_queues(scarce_spec_log),
            "regional_customer_flow": analyze_flow_and_queues(
                regional_cust_log
            ),
            "kpi_matrix": compute_case_kpis_and_fairness(log)[0],
        }
    return results
```
