# 1. System boundary and assumptions

**Environmental scope included in this analysis:**

- Production-related energy from the event log: `energy_kwh` for CNC_A/CNC_B, heat treatment, inspection cells, and rework production loops.
- Material waste: `material_scrap_kg`, including scrap generated by normal production and rework.
- Packaging material: `package_material_kg`, including packaging rework and reinforced-packaging choices.
- Outbound transport emissions: `transport_kgco2e` for parcel ground, parcel air, dedicated vans, and consolidated trucks.
- Repeated handling and extra shipments linked to partial shipments, backorders, and packaging rework.
- Obsolescence caused by batching: batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days are treated as wasted embodied material and energy.

**Outside the quantitative boundary, but considered as constraints:**

- Upstream raw-material extraction and supplier inbound transport.
- Customer use-phase effects and avoided downtime benefits.
- End-of-life disposal of returned or scrapped parts.
- Warehouse facility energy, lighting, heating, and general overhead.
- Product design, demand volume, and strategic sourcing decisions.

**Uncertainty and assumptions:**

- The event-log averages and path summaries are observational, not causal. They may be confounded by urgency, product complexity, customer contract, and machine availability.
- The 85% service probability threshold in SP1 is treated as a decision rule, not a fixed legal guarantee.
- Made-to-order and Critical-downtime cases have different service constraints; “least emissions” is not a valid global objective.
- The discovered process model has low precision because emergency paths, partial shipments, and rework loops combine in many ways. Therefore, we recommend outcome- and attribute-based monitoring in addition to DFG conformance.

# 3. Intervention options

**S1. Green shipping recommender**

- Target path/segment: All ship paths; most valuable for Standard-stock Service-contract, MTO Service-contract, and cases where promised lead time has slack.
- Mechanism: At package completion, choose the least-emitting route that can meet the promised lead time with at least 85% probability. Default to ground or consolidated truck unless air or dedicated van is required.
- Service/quality/resilience effect: Reduces air/van use without breaking promises. Must keep an alternative route for Service-contract customers during disruption.
- Monitoring evidence: SP1 violation rate, route chosen vs recommended route, actual vs promised lead time, late-delivery rate, service-contract alternative-route availability.

**S2. Dynamic batching for CNC and heat treatment with waiting-time caps**

- Target path/segment: MTO production paths: Plan production  Batch wait  CNC production  Heat treatment  Inspection.
- Mechanism: Batch only when due-date slack allows. Use maximum waiting-time caps by segment, especially for heat treatment and CNC_A. This captures energy savings from batching without allowing batch wait to drive late deliveries.
- Service/quality/resilience effect: Energy per item falls when safe; late-delivery risk falls if caps prevent excessive waiting. Smaller-than-ideal batches may still be needed for urgent cases.
- Monitoring evidence: Batch size and wait distributions, energy per item, late-delivery risk by wait interval, obsolescence/unshipped stock.

**S3. Shift Made-to-order production from CNC_B to CNC_A when feasible**

- Target path/segment: MTO paths using CNC_B, especially the `MTO  batch wait  CNC_B  inspection  rework  ground` variant.
- Mechanism: Use CNC_A when its queue and due-date slack allow. CNC_A has a 9% rework rate versus 18% for CNC_B, reducing rework loops, scrap, and energy.
- Service/quality/resilience effect: Rework and material waste should fall. Cycle time may rise because CNC_A is slower, so this must only be used when slack exists.
- Monitoring evidence: Machine assignment, queue length, due-date slack, rework rate, scrap kg, energy kWh, cycle time.

**S4. Replace air for Standard-stock Service-contract orders with priority picking + ground unless slack is insufficient**

- Target path/segment: Standard-stock Service-contract orders often shipped on `Stock  standard pack  air`.
- Mechanism: Use promised lead time and route reliability to determine whether ground plus priority picking can deliver on time. If yes, switch from air to ground or consolidated truck.
- Service/quality/resilience effect: Directly addresses the 22% SP1 violation in this segment. Service can remain high because priority picking shortens internal lead time.
- Monitoring evidence: SP1 conformance, promised lead-time slack, priority-picking duration, late-delivery % by route, transport kgCO2e.

**S5. Consolidate truck departures twice daily for non-urgent stock orders**

- Target path/segment: Non-urgent Standard-stock Regular and Service-contract orders currently sent by individual ground parcels or air.
- Mechanism: Assign eligible orders to twice-daily consolidated truck departures. The 12–24 hour consolidation wait is acceptable only when promised lead time still allows delivery.
- Service/quality/resilience effect: Transport kgCO2e can fall by roughly 35–55% versus individual ground parcels. Cycle time increases by up to a day, so only non-urgent orders should be included.
- Monitoring evidence: Fill rates, departure schedule adherence, consolidation wait, late-delivery %, promised lead-time slack.

**S6. Improve packaging-route compatibility checks before shipment**

- Target path/segment: Packaging loops such as `Package standard  Rework packaging` and `Package reinforced  Rework packaging`, and MTO heavy parts over 8 kg.
- Mechanism: Before packaging, check part geometry, weight, route mode, and damage risk. Enforce Q1: heavy MTO parts must use reinforced packaging unless inspection certifies low damage risk.
- Service/quality/resilience effect: Reduces packaging rework, wasted packaging material, damage-related returns, and repeat handling.
- Monitoring evidence: Packaging-rework rate, package_material_kg per order, Q1 violation rate, return/damage events by route.

**S7. Add an inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations**

- Target path/segment: MTO paths after CNC production, especially CNC_B and inspection-cell queues that currently drive rework-production loops.
- Mechanism: Add a more rigorous inspection gate for combinations with historically high rework. This catches defects before packaging and shipping, reducing downstream rework and customer returns.
- Service/quality/resilience effect: Rework and return risk should fall. Inspection time may increase, so this must be balanced with queue management to avoid creating a new bottleneck.
- Monitoring evidence: `rework_flag`, machine_group, inspection wait time, rework loop frequency, return/damage rate, energy kWh overall.

**S8. Replace some dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes**

- Target path/segment: Critical-downtime Service-contract cases using `Dedicated van`, particularly the 31% of dedicated-van cases missing SP2 justification.
- Mechanism: Use shared emergency courier lanes for non-life-critical urgent orders. Keep dedicated vans for true downtime and cases with explicit justification.
- Service/quality/resilience effect: Reduces empty return miles and transport emissions. Must preserve a dedicated-van fallback for genuine emergencies.
- Monitoring evidence: SP2 justification events, van fill rates, empty-return share, transit time, critical-downtime outcomes.

**S9. Split Critical-downtime into true downtime and precautionary urgent categories**

- Target path/segment: Critical-downtime Service-contract segment, especially `Critical  emergency production  reinforced pack  dedicated van`.
- Mechanism: Define two subcategories: true downtime where equipment is stopped, and precautionary urgent where downtime is possible but not immediate. Use dedicated van/air for true downtime; use ground or shared courier for precautionary urgent.
- Service/quality/resilience effect: Protects genuine critical service while reducing high-emission transport for precautionary cases.
- Monitoring evidence: Category assignment, route chosen by category, late-delivery %, dedicated-van usage, downtime outcomes.

**S10. Introduce carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular made-to-order customers**

- Target path/segment: Made-to-order Regular customers, where the late-delivery rate is 18% and cycle time is high.
- Mechanism: Offer a slightly longer promised lead time when the customer accepts a lower-carbon production and shipping plan. The extra slack enables batching and ground transport.
- Service/quality/resilience effect: Reduces energy per item and transport emissions. Late-delivery risk should fall if promises become more realistic.
- Monitoring evidence: Negotiated promised lead time, customer acceptance rate, actual cycle time, late %, route mix, batch size distribution.

**S11. Reduce partial shipments by changing availability-check rules**

- Target path/segment: `Stock  partial shipment  ground + backorder`, and cancellation due to delay.
- Mechanism: During availability check, evaluate whether a partial shipment can actually meet the promised lead time for all line items. If not, hold the full order and set an accurate expected delivery date rather than creating a costly second shipment.
- Service/quality/resilience effect: Fewer duplicate shipments should reduce transport, packaging, and customer contact. There is a risk of longer order waits if availability is poor, so this must be combined with better promise-date communication.
- Monitoring evidence: Partial-shipment frequency, backorder wait, cancellation due to delay, line-item fill rate, transport per order.

**S12. Create a sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides**

- Target path/segment: All process paths where the sustainability policy is overridden.
- Mechanism: When a dispatcher chooses air despite SP1 allowing ground, or uses a dedicated van without SP2 justification, or violates Q1, require a structured override code, reason, and approver.
- Service/quality/resilience effect: Does not directly reduce emissions, but creates governance and visibility. It protects resilience by allowing justified overrides while discouraging unjustified ones.
- Monitoring evidence: Override counts by reason, approval rates, SP1/SP2/Q1 conformance trends, outcomes of overridden cases.

# 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching-induced obsolescence.** Larger batches reduce energy per item, but 6% of batch-produced items are already not shipped within 14 days when forecasts are wrong. If batching is increased without demand-linking, saved energy can be outweighed by wasted material, disposal, and lost value.

2. **Transport-mode displacement.** Replacing air with ground can add transit time. If a ground shipment then misses its promise, an emergency air or dedicated-van recovery shipment may emit more than the original air shipment. SP1 thresholds must therefore be based on reliability, not just average transit time.

3. **Consolidation and emergency rerouting.** Consolidated trucks cut emissions by 35–55%, but the 12–24 hour consolidation wait may push an order past its promise date. The resulting recovery shipment can negate the emissions saving and increase service risk.

4. **Packaging-material rebound.** Reinforced packaging reduces damage-related returns by 40% for heavy MTO parts but doubles package material. Over-applying reinforced packaging increases material use, transport weight/volume, and waste, without proportional return reductions.

5. **Inspection and rework displacement.** A stricter inspection gate may reduce rework, but it can also increase inspection queue time. Under pressure to clear the queue, inspections may again become rushed, recreating the same defect escapes and rework loops. Extra inspection energy may exceed the avoided rework energy for low-risk combinations.

6. **CNC machine substitution and load shift.** Moving CNC_B work to CNC_A lowers rework, but it may overload CNC_A, increase batch waits, and force smaller, less-efficient batches. That can raise energy per item and increase late-delivery risk unless the switch is conditional on queue and slack.

7. **Customer behavior and promise negotiation.** Carbon-aware promise dates may attract some Regular customers to accept longer lead times, while others may pay for faster delivery and increase air/van use. Demand segmentation can shift, reducing the environmental benefit of the program.

8. **Availability and completeness holds.** Reducing partial shipments may hold an order until everything is available. If replenishment is slow, cancellations due to delay can increase, and customers may switch to competitors. This shifts the environmental burden elsewhere and can strand inventory.

# 6. Balanced recommendation

A coherent redesign package, not a list of isolated green ideas, should be implemented in stages:

**Stage 0: Governance foundation**

- Deploy S12, the sustainability exception log, and a KPI dashboard first. Without governance, SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides will remain invisible and rebound effects will be hard to attribute.

**Stage 1: Transport and route selection**

- Implement S1 and S4 for Standard-stock Service-contract orders with lead-time slack.
- Implement S5 for non-urgent stock orders where consolidation wait fits within the promise.
- Implement S9 to separate true downtime from precautionary urgent Critical cases.
- Implement S8 only for precautionary urgent cases; retain dedicated vans for true downtime and for SP2-justified cases.

This is balanced because it does not eliminate air or dedicated vans. It merely prevents their use when the promised lead time can still be met with high reliability.

**Stage 2: Production and quality**

- Implement S2 dynamic batching with waiting-time caps.
- Implement S3 to shift CNC_B work to CNC_A only when queue and due-date slack permit.
- Implement S7 for high-rework machine/part combinations.
- Implement S10 for Made-to-order Regular customers to create the demand-side slack that makes S2 and S3 feasible.

This package reduces energy and scrap without letting batching delays or slower CNC_A processing degrade service. It also directly attacks rework, which is one of the largest hidden environmental costs.

**Stage 3: Packaging and order completeness**

- Implement S6 packaging-route compatibility checks.
- Implement S11 with updated availability-check rules.

These actions reduce packaging waste, packaging rework, second shipments, and backorder complexity. They also improve quality by reducing damage-related returns.

**Why this package is balanced:**

- **Environmental:** It targets the largest emission and waste drivers: unnecessary air freight, energy-intensive MTO production, rework, packaging waste, and partial shipment duplication.
- **Service:** It always checks promised lead time and uses the 85% reliability threshold. True Critical-downtime cases retain fast routes.
- **Quality:** S3, S6, and S7 reduce rework and returns rather than simply shifting emissions downstream.
- **Resilience:** S1 and S12 preserve alternative routes and justified overrides. S8 retains a dedicated-van fallback. S2 and S3 retain machine-path redundancy.

# 7. Validation and governance plan

**Baseline and pilot**

- Use the 12,000-order log as a baseline for each segment and path.
- Pilot the package for 8–12 weeks on a subset of orders or customers before full rollout.
- Set non-inferiority limits for service: for each segment, late-delivery, cancellation, and p90 cycle time should not worsen beyond acceptable thresholds. Exact thresholds should be calibrated from baseline percentiles.

**Event-log enrichment**

Add new process attributes:

- Recommended route and actual route selected.
- Override code and approver for SP1/SP2/Q1.
- Critical subcategory: true downtime vs precautionary urgent.
- Negotiated promised lead time for MTO Regular orders.
- Batching decision reason: batch assembled, batch cap reached, or urgent release.
- Inspection gate result and whether a high-rework combination was flagged.

**Conformance monitoring**

- **SP1:** For each ground/consolidated recommendation, compute the probability of on-time delivery. Flag cases where air or dedicated van is used despite the threshold being satisfied.
- **SP2:** Dedicated-van cases must have a justification event. Monitor the percentage of missing justifications; this should fall from the current 31%.
- **Q1:** Monitor MTO parts over 8 kg that ship without reinforced packaging and without a certified low-damage-risk inspection. Correlate violations with returns and rework loops.

**Segment-level service monitoring**

Track by `case_type × customer_type`:

- Median and p90 cycle time.
- Late-delivery %.
- Cancellation %.
- Partial-shipment %.
- Rework %.
- Average energy kWh, transport kgCO2e, scrap kg, and package material kg.

Use control charts to detect drift before service deterioration becomes severe.

**Rebound watch**

Monitor the risk indicators described in Section 5:

- Air/van recovery trips after ground or consolidated-truck shipments.
- Obsolescence and unshipped batch-produced items.
- Packaging material per order versus damage-return rate.
- Rework and return loop frequencies after the new inspection gate.
- CNC_A queue length and batch size after shifting work from CNC_B.
- Customer acceptance of carbon-aware promise dates and resulting route mix.

**Exception governance**

- Review the sustainability exception log weekly.
- Categorize overrides by reason: carrier disruption, customer mandate, true critical downtime, or incorrect policy threshold.
- If override rates become excessive, tune the recommender thresholds rather than weakening the process.
- Test R1 resilience annually with disruption simulations for Service-contract customers to ensure at least one alternative route remains available.

**Process-mining method**

Because the discovered model has low precision due to emergency paths and partial shipments, validation should not rely only on exact DFG conformance. Instead, combine:

- Attribute-based outcome analysis.
- Segment-level statistical monitoring.
- Exception-log audit.

This allows the organization to learn quickly, adjust thresholds, and maintain service, quality, and resilience while reducing environmental impact.
