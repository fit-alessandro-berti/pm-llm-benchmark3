Treatment-version issues: E1 bundles availability, the fast-path rule, the fraud-desk referral of borderline cases, and training and local practice changes. Automated cases and manual cases in one branch share the specialist and fraud queues, so the branch is not a clean unit of treatment.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary:** cohort-specific staggered DiD (group-time ATT, Callaway–Sant'Anna-style) plus event study | Cohorts: April (Alpha, Beta) and July (Gamma). Event time k = t  adoption month | Never-treated Delta and Epsilon; Gamma as not-yet-treated for Apr–Jun. Treated units never serve as controls | Branch and calendar-month effects (implicit in differencing); sensitivity with cases/FTE, complexity, FTE | E1, E3, E4, E5, E7 | Absent automation, treated branches would have moved like controls, allowing for level differences. No differential shocks (routing, FTE, complexity) and no anticipation. Controls are not affected by adopters | Pre-gaps to controls (flat?); Gamma-vs-controls placebo over Jan–Jun; alternative control (Delta only, Epsilon only); Jan–Feb-only baseline; log scale; leave-one-branch-out | Alpha/Beta were selected on poor performance and higher load, so trends may differ. Only 2 controls |
| Alt A: continuous-treatment DiD (dose-response) | Time-varying `automated_fast_path_pct` minus baseline, with lagged dose | Same controls (dose  0) | Cases/FTE, complexity, FTE (possible bad controls, since routing is part of the rollout bundle) | E2, E7 | Dose is as-good-as-random conditional on branch and month, with linear response. No dose-related time-varying confounders | Dose–response monotonicity; slope by branch; dose vs. time-since-adoption (collinear) | Use reflects how well the branch is coping. Dose and learning are confounded |
| Alt B: branch-specific ITS or synthetic control | Break at adoption; post-break level and slope | Weighted donors (2–3 branches) or pre-trend extrapolation | Seasonality via donor series | E1 per branch | Pre-trend continues, or donors span the treated series' convex hull | Pre-fit RMSE; placebo-in-time; placebo-in-space | Only two or three donors and 3–6 pre-points. Alpha/Beta lie outside the donors' support |
| Alt C (prospective): stepped-wedge or case-level randomized eligibility in Delta/Epsilon | Randomized start dates by team or queue, or randomized triage assignment for eligible cases | Concurrent not-yet-treated teams or cases | Stratify by risk class | E1–E6 with case-level inference | Randomization; no contamination across shared queues | Balance; uptake; queue-state monitoring | Interference through shared specialist and fraud queues. Requires design now, not analysis |

The primary strategy is preferable because it makes every comparison explicit, and there are only three adopters and two never-treated branches. Regression weights therefore do not hide the contrasts. Already-treated branches are never controls, which avoids the negative-weighting problem of two-way fixed effects when effects grow over time. That matters here because Alpha and Beta ramp for five months while Gamma has only two.

Gamma adds a placebo and a second timing. Before July, its gap to the never-treated controls is stable (median gap 1.5 to +1.4 h, no trend). Its adoption follows the March and May shocks, so its effect is separated from them in time. The design also lets us check for a common signature at three calendar dates. Rework falls about 2 points and escalation rises about 1 point at each adoption, while contemporaneous controls are flat. Seasonality or a single campaign would struggle to mimic that.

The main limit is that five clusters make cluster-robust p-values unreliable. I therefore report ranges and sensitivity to control choice, baseline window and pre-trend extrapolation, not significance. Synthetic control has too few donors. ITS lacks a counterfactual for the May shock. Intensity models are confounded with time since adoption.

**Treatment intensity.** I regress the gap deviation on (fast-path %  baseline), pooling the three adopters through the origin. The slope is about 0.13 h per pp for the median (branch range 0.10 to 0.16). For p90 it is about 0.19 h per pp. A 40 pp change would imply about 5 h (median) and 7.5 h (p90). Escalation and rework do not scale with dose: they jump at availability and then stay about flat. The dose is collinear with time since adoption, so this cannot separate use from learning or seasonality.

**Pre-trends and regression to the mean.**
- Gap to controls Jan–Mar: Beta median 7.5, 9.9, 8.2 (no trend). Alpha 12.5, 10.9, 11.2 (slightly falling, about 0.65 h/month, within noise). Gamma Jan–Jun is flat.
- Alpha and Beta were chosen on poor levels, so regression to the mean could contribute. But their pre-period was not a spike: March was a common fraud-desk bump, and the gaps had been stable. Their absolute outcomes then worsened, and improvement appears only relative to controls.
- Regression to the mean cannot easily explain the same rework and escalation steps at three dates. It could explain part of Gamma's July–August fall, which follows the May–June complexity and FTE shock. Controls also fell in July–August (seasonal), which the DiD nets out.
- If Alpha's pre-slope continued, its average median DiD would fall from 5.6 to about 3.4 h.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**expand with an embedded evaluation**

Next branches: Delta and Epsilon, but not together and not as an unmeasured switch-on. Epsilon has the worst trajectory (highest complexity, rework 16.2%, weakest CSAT), so it is the likely high-benefit test. Delta has low complexity and the best baseline, so it is the low-benefit contrast. Together they give heterogeneity information that Alpha, Beta and Gamma cannot.

Assignment mechanism: run a 4-week shadow mode in both branches, where the system scores cases without acting, to log eligibility and fast-path suitability. Then randomize eligible low- and medium-risk registrations 50/50 to automated or manual triage, stratified by branch and risk class. Ramp exposure by team-week if case-level assignment is impractical. If neither is possible, publicly draw the order of Delta and Epsilon at random and stagger them by at least 8 weeks.

Measurements: at least 12 weeks pre and 12 weeks post at weekly resolution (8 weeks minimum), so the post window covers learning and seasonality.

Data to collect (case-level event logs):
- timestamps for every activity, including the fraud desk and Specialist Review start and end;
- queue length and age at each arrival;
- path variant, rework loops, and reasons for escalation;
- risk class at registration;
- FTE hours and overtime;
- survey response indicators and reopen and complaint flags.

Spillover monitoring: compare specialist and fraud-desk waiting times for non-automated cases in all branches, including Alpha, Beta and Gamma. Track referral rates by source.

Guardrails: pause a branch if p90 for high-complexity cases rises more than 10% for two weeks, if fraud-desk or specialist waits rise more than 15%, if the complaint or reopen rate rises, or if CSAT falls more than 0.10 with stable response.

Scale-up rule: scale to the remaining eligible cases only if, in randomized comparisons, the 90% interval for the median change excludes zero and is at least 5% lower. The p90 upper bound must be no more than +3%. Combined rework plus escalation and specialist hours must not rise. Queue waits must stay within +10%. The CSAT interval must exclude a fall of more than 0.05.

print("\n=== Adopter pre/post and DiD vs never-treated (descriptive) ===")
print(prepost.round(2).to_string(index=False))

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 4. relative-time indicators
# Dummies for treated branches only; reference period k = -1. Gamma's k <= -4 rows and
# never-treated rows have all dummies = 0 and act as comparison observations.
# Alpha/Beta contribute k in [-3, 4]; Gamma k in [-6, 1], so k>=2 comes from Alpha/Beta only.
KS = [-3, -2, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4]
def kname(k): return f"k_m{abs(k)}" if k < 0 else f"k_p{k}"
for k in KS:
    df[kname(k)] = (df.rel == k).astype(int)

def fit_cluster(formula, data):
    # Branch-clustered covariance. WITH ONLY 5 CLUSTERS THESE SEs ARE UNRELIABLE.
    return smf.ols(formula, data=data).fit(
        cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": data["branch_id"].values})

def tab(res, keep, G):
    out = pd.DataFrame({"coef": res.params, "se_cluster": res.bse})
    out["p_t(G-1)"] = 2 * stats.t.sf(np.abs(out.coef / out.se_cluster), df=max(G - 1, 1))
    return out.loc[[k for k in keep if k in out.index]].round(3)

G = df.branch.nunique()

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 5a. descriptive panel models
# (i) Static TWFE with binary availability -- DESCRIPTIVE ONLY. With staggered adoption and
#     dynamic effects it can weight comparisons badly (already-treated as controls).
# (ii) Event-study version with never-treated / not-yet-treated observations as comparison.
# CAUSAL ASSUMPTIONS (not tested by these regressions): no differential shocks (routing, FTE,
# complexity), no anticipation, no spillover into controls, homogeneous branch responses.
with warnings.catch_warnings():
    warnings.simplefilter("ignore")
    for y in OUTCOMES:
        print(f"\n##### {y}: static TWFE (descriptive; biased if effects are dynamic/heterogeneous)")
        r = fit_cluster(f"{y} ~ automation + C(branch) + C(month)", df)
        print(tab(r, ["automation"], G))
        print(f"##### {y}: event-study (reference k=-1; cohort mix differs by k)")
        f = f"{y} ~ " + " + ".join(kname(k) for k in KS) + " + C(branch) + C(month)"
        r = fit_cluster(f, df)
        print(tab(r, [kname(k) for k in KS], G))

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 5b. treatment-intensity model
# Uses actual automated_fast_path_pct, not availability.
# ASSUMPTION: conditional on branch and month effects (and covariates), fast-path share is
# unrelated to unobserved shocks -- implausible: managers, case mix and coping drive use, and
# use is collinear with time since adoption (learning vs. seasonality unresolved).
# cases_per_fte and complexity are potentially "bad controls" (routing/product change are part of
# the rollout bundle), so both specifications are shown.
with warnings.catch_warnings():
    warnings.simplefilter("ignore")
    for y in OUTCOMES:
        print(f"\n##### {y}: intensity models (coefficient = per 1 pp fast-path share)")
        r1 = fit_cluster(f"{y} ~ automated_fast_path_pct + C(branch) + C(month)", df)
        r2 = fit_cluster(f"{y} ~ automated_fast_path_pct + cases_per_fte + high_complexity_pct"
                         " + C(branch) + C(month)", df)
        print("no covariates :\n", tab(r1, ["automated_fast_path_pct"], G))
        print("with covariates:\n", tab(r2, ["automated_fast_path_pct", "cases_per_fte",
                                             "high_complexity_pct"], G))
        loo = {}
        for b in df.branch.unique():                  # leave-one-branch-out stability check
            try:
                sub = df[df.branch != b]
                loo[b] = smf.ols(f"{y} ~ automated_fast_path_pct + C(branch) + C(month)",
                                 data=sub).fit().params["automated_fast_path_pct"]
            except Exception as e:
                loo[b] = np.nan
        print("leave-one-branch-out slope:", {k: round(v, 3) for k, v in loo.items()})

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 6. warnings
n_assign = int(round(np.math.comb(5, 3))) if hasattr(np, "math") else 10
print("\n" + "=" * 78)
print("WARNING 1: only 5 clusters (3 ever-treated, 2 never-treated). Cluster-robust SEs are")
print("  unreliable and the joint covariance is rank-deficient; p-values use t(G-1) only as a")
print("  rough guide. Permuting which 3 of 5 branches are treated gives 10 assignments, so the")
print("  smallest attainable permutation p-value is about 0.1. Treat estimates as descriptive ranges.")
print("WARNING 2: median_cycle_h and p90_cycle_h are branch-month summaries. Averaging medians or")
print("  percentiles across months/branches does not give the case-level median/p90 of the pooled")
print("  cases, and changes in case mix, volume or path mix can move them without any change in")
print("  case-level processing. Case-level event logs are needed for process claims.")
print("WARNING 3: CSAT response counts are unavailable; nonresponse can change with automation.")
print("WARNING 4: Nothing above proves causality. Identification rests on the untested assumptions")
print("  written in comments (parallel trends after shocks, no anticipation, no spillovers,")
print("  no differential routing/FTE/complexity effects).")

## 10. Final claim ladder

- Observed: In raw pre/post terms, Alpha's and Beta's medians barely moved (0.4 and +1.9 h) and their p90s rose about 10 h, while Delta and Epsilon deteriorated more (median +5 h, p90 +16 h). At each adoption date (April for Alpha and Beta, July for Gamma), rework fell about 2 points and escalation rose about 1 point, with controls flat in those months.
- Conditionally causal: If parallel trends hold after the fraud-desk, routing, staffing and complexity shocks, and the fraud-desk referral rule is part of what "automation" means, then adoption reduced rework by about 1–2.5 points and raised escalation by about 0.7–1.2 points at 25–50% use.
- Suggestive: Adopters had faster typical-case times than non-adopters (about 3–6 h, roughly 5–10%), a relatively smaller p90 deterioration (about 6–7 h), and a small positive CSAT gap (about +0.1). All are sensitive to control choice, pre-trends, and five-cluster uncertainty.
- Not supported: A single statement that "automation reduces cycle time by X%" across the organization, a claim of the same effect for Delta and Epsilon, a claim that tail risk and specialist or fraud-desk queues are unaffected, or a causal customer-satisfaction effect.
