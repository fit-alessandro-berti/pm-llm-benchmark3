# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1. **Tail latency.** A small share of cases (complex, legal/risk, escalated) can wait much longer while the mean is held down by many faster simple cases. Median and mean can be flat or better while P90/P95 rises 10–40%. Track P90/P95 and the share of cases above 2× baseline.
2. **High-complexity cases (mix effect).** All high-complexity cases now go through the pool, so they bear the queue. Routing more simple cases to automation or local handling can lower the average while complex cases get slower (Simpson's-paradox risk). Always report by complexity and specialty, and adjust for mix.
3. **Local knowledge loss.** Regional customer conventions and informal early advice prevented unnecessary referrals and rework in complex cases. The loss shows as referral inflation, "no change" advice, and later rework, none of which changes cycle time immediately. Informal consultation is not in the log, so instrument it.
4. **Fairness.**
   - East may gain and North/South may subsidize: the pool absorbs East's about 1.8× tech load per case, and the shared queue allocates capacity by rules that may favor volume, priority, or FIFO.
   - Language or regional accommodation customers may face more handovers and longer waits, yet are a small share of cases.
   - Averages over teams hide these gaps.
5. **Learning and skill erosion.** If all hard cases are routed away, local staff stop building technical skill. This produces resilience and capability decline months later. Track technical events per caseworker and escalation of medium-complexity cases.
6. **Accountability and role clarity.** Shared approval and pool queues anonymize decisions. Cycle time may fall while nobody owns a case, or decisions are relitigated. Look at distinct decision-makers per case, approval loops, and reassignments.
7. **Exception handling.** Absences, unusual requests, and customer-specific accommodations go through informal local fixes that vanish. Stalled exceptions may be rare but severe. Track stuck-case age, manual overrides, and the time to reroute after absence.
8. **Rework displacement and censoring.**
   - Rework is detected at Quality Check and later, so early post-change data undercounts it.
   - Early *closed* cases are systematically the fast ones. Open cases are censored, so early averages look better than reality.
9. **Resilience concentration.** Fewer supervisors and a consolidated pool create single points of failure. Even if average performance is fine, one absence at the pool or in Legal/Risk can stall many cases.
10. **Wait shifted, not removed.** Handover waits listed in the table rise about 23% under management's own estimates. Any average gain must come from elsewhere (automation, fewer local loops). Verify where, because a "stable average" could mean gains and losses offsetting.

# 5. Pilot design

## 5.1 Principle
Do not run the five changes as one bundle. Separate them so effects can be attributed:
- **A.** Technical Shared Service
- **B.** Legal/Risk/Specialist shared queue (with added capacity)
- **C.** Shared approval queue
- **D.** Routing rules
- **E.** Supervisor removal (**last**, only if C proves safe)

## 5.2 Pilot scope
- **Phase 0 (4–6 weeks):** instrumentation and baseline. Add queue and lifecycle events (enqueue, pull, start, complete), a stable case-owner field, a language/regional-accommodation flag, standardized case-note template, and an "informal consult" quick ticket. Create the harmonized activity mapping.
- **Phase 1 (12–16 weeks): East + a small share of shared tech (A + D only).** East has the most to gain (E_Tech 82%, highest SLA miss, longest cycle times). Keep local first-line consultation available. Route only high-complexity East cases plus overflow to the pool. Keep Legal/Risk and approval as-is.
- **Phase 2 (12 weeks): add South** (a mid-performance team), then add B with **added capacity** and C without removing a supervisor.
- **Phase 3:** North (the strongest local expertise and highest volume) only if guardrails held. Consider remove-supervisor (E) only after approval queue data.
- East is not representative of North/South. Its results should not be extrapolated without the later phases.

## 5.3 Comparison group
- **Stepped-wedge design:** teams not yet switched act as controls (difference-in-differences).
- Comparison: North (and South in Phase 1) against East pilot. Add a synthetic control built from pre-period, complexity, specialty, and segment mix.
- Also use the same period last year for seasonality.

## 5.4 Pre/post observation window
- **Pre:** at least 12 weeks of history (plus same-season prior year) with harmonized activity labels.
- **Post:** 12–16 weeks per phase, plus 4–8 weeks of follow-up for rework and outcome lag.
- Analyze by **intake cohort** (week of intake) and handle open cases with survival methods (Kaplan–Meier / open-case age). Do not compare close-date averages.
- Rough sample size: East has about 520 cases/month, so about 1,500 in 12 weeks. If about 20–30% are high complexity, that is about 300–450 cases, enough for medians and coarse P90, but weak for small segments. Extend the window or pool phases for rare segments.

## 5.5 Segmentation
Report every metric by:
- **Team** (home team at intake): North, South, East.
- **Complexity:** low, medium, high.
- **Required specialty:** none, tech, legal, risk, security.
- **Customer segment**, plus a language/regional flag.
- **Priority.**
- **Resource type:** local, pool, shared approver.

## 5.6 Guardrail metrics (initial thresholds, to be calibrated)

| Guardrail | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Pool queue P90 wait | > 2× baseline for 2 consecutive weeks |
| Pool utilization | > 85% (tech) or > 90% (legal/risk) for 3 weeks |
| Queue backlog age | Rising 3 weeks in a row |
| High-complexity P90 cycle time | > +15% vs. baseline / control |
| Portfolio P90 cycle time | > +10% |
| Rework rate | > +1.5pp |
| SLA miss rate | > +2pp |
| Referral rate for low/medium complexity | > +15% |
| Return-to-originator | < 70% |
| Reassignment / ping-pong | > +20% |
| Disparity (P90 ratio between teams or segments, complexity-adjusted) | > 1.25× baseline gap |
| Customer satisfaction (any team/segment) | 0.2 |
| Legal/Risk and approval load | > 100% of capacity |

## 5.7 Staff feedback collection
- Weekly 3–5 question pulse survey on clarity of ownership, advice quality, and workload.
- Fortnightly structured debriefs by role (caseworkers, techs, supervisors, legal/risk).
- Lightweight "informal consult" logging (quick-question tickets) to preserve the signal the log misses.
- Anonymous channel for concerns.
- Shadowing of hard cases and customer accommodation cases.
- Customer feedback, segmented by language and regional needs.

## 5.8 Rollback / adjustment triggers
- **Rollback:** any two of: pool P90 wait > 2× baseline, high-complexity P90 > +25%, SLA miss > +3pp, rework > +2pp, or a legal/risk/approval queue growing beyond capacity for 3 weeks.
- **Adjust (not rollback):** referral inflation (restore local first-line consult), low return-to-originator (enforce owner-return rule), disparity beyond threshold (change queue priority rules or add team-based capacity), role confusion (clarify ownership).
- **Pause phase expansion:** unresolved data quality gaps, or a staff-reported issue confirmed by log evidence.

# 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation: modify and pilot first. Do not roll out fully.**

The proposal has real potential, especially for East, where tech utilization is high, cycle times are long, and pooling could shorten waits. But management's own routing table shows more handovers, longer handover waits, and higher referral volumes, and the case for the shared model rests on assumptions about capacity that are not evidenced.

**Modifications before pilot:**
1. Keep a small local first-line consultation capacity (or a named local tech liaison) so informal early advice survives.
2. Add Legal/Risk capacity, or introduce triage/tiering. Do not route +33% more work into groups that are already at 91–93%.
3. Keep all local supervisors during the pilot. Reassess removal only after the shared approval queue is proven under load.
4. Make the local caseworker the persistent case owner, with advice returning to the originator.
5. Use complexity and priority-aware queue rules, and cap any team's share of pool capacity so East's demand does not crowd out North/South.
6. Standardize case notes across locations before shared handling.
7. Build a skills-rotation and pairing plan so local staff keep technical skills.
8. Keep a defined path for language and regional accommodation.

**Proceed to the next phase only if** the leading indicators stay within guardrails, the tail (P90/P95) is stable or improved for high-complexity cases, segment and team disparities are not widening, and staff evidence does not contradict the log.

**What management should not infer from early averages:**
- **A lower average cycle time does not mean the model works.** Early closed cases are the fast ones (censoring), and the mix may shift toward simpler cases.
- **Higher specialist utilization is not efficiency.** It may be added referrals (+14% matches the +15% claim), and above about 85–90% it predicts queue explosions.
- **Fewer local handovers is not fewer handovers.** Count total and queue-mediated handovers.
- **Fewer cross-team handovers is not better resilience.** Cases could be stuck rather than covered.
- **East's improvement does not generalize to North/South.** East had a different starting position.
- **No early rework increase is not proof of quality.** Rework and outcome effects lag.
- **No complaints in the first weeks is not proof of no local-knowledge loss or learning erosion.** These are slow.
- **A flat average with a rising P90 is a deterioration for the customers in the tail.**

# 7. PM4Py analysis sketch

The code below is a sketch. Check function signatures against your PM4Py version. It assumes a harmonized activity mapping between the old and new process, queue/lifecycle events if available, and a `period` label (pre/post) plus a `group` label (pilot/control).

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import networkx as nx
import pm4py

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 0. Load, format, harmonise, label
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
TECH_POOL = {"Tech_Shared_1", "Tech_Shared_2", "Tech_Shared_3"}     # post-change
LEGAL_RISK = {"Specialist_Legal", "Specialist_Risk"}
SHARED_APPROVERS = {"Approver_Shared_1", "Approver_Shared_2"}
ACT_MAP = {  # harmonise old/new labels so pre/post are comparable
    "Local Technical Assessment": "Technical Assessment",
    "Technical Shared Review": "Technical Assessment",
}
SLA_DAYS = {"high": 5, "medium": 8, "low": 12}   # assumption: replace with real SLA

def prep(path, period, group=None):
    df = pd.read_csv(path)
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id="case:concept:name",
                                activity_key="concept:name",
                                timestamp_key="time:timestamp")
    df["concept:name"] = df["concept:name"].replace(ACT_MAP)
    df = df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"])
    # home team = team at first event, so filters survive routing to shared units
    df["home_team"] = df.groupby("case:concept:name")["team"].transform("first")
    def unit(r):
        if r in TECH_POOL: return "TechShared"
        if r in LEGAL_RISK: return "LegalRisk"
        if r in SHARED_APPROVERS: return "ApprovalShared"
        if str(r).startswith("Bot_"): return "Bot"
        return str(r).split("_")[0]        # N / S / E
    df["unit"] = df["org:resource"].map(unit)
    df["period"] = period
    df["group"] = group
    return df

pre  = prep("log_pre.csv",  "pre")
post = prep("log_post.csv", "post")

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Filtering by team, complexity, specialty, customer segment
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
def slice_log(df, team=None, complexity=None, specialty=None, segment=None):
    out = df
    for col, val in [("home_team", team), ("case_complexity", complexity),
                     ("required_specialty", specialty), ("customer_segment", segment)]:
        if val is not None:
            vals = val if isinstance(val, list) else [val]
            out = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
                out, col, vals, level="case", retain=True)
    return out

SLICES = {
    "all": {},
    "East": {"team": "East"},
    "North": {"team": "North"},
    "South": {"team": "South"},
    "high_complexity": {"complexity": "high"},
    "legal_risk_sec": {"specialty": ["legal", "risk", "security"]},
    # add customer_segment slices, e.g. {"segment": "regional_language"}
}

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Handover-of-work, working-together, subcontracting networks
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
def net_to_df(sna):
    return pd.DataFrame([(a, b, w) for (a, b), w in sna.connections.items()],
                        columns=["src", "dst", "w"])

def graph_metrics(edges, directed=True):
    G = nx.DiGraph() if directed else nx.Graph()
    G.add_weighted_edges_from(edges.itertuples(index=False))
    bc = nx.betweenness_centrality(G)
    instr = pd.Series(dict(G.in_degree(weight="weight")))
    unit_of = lambda r: str(r).split("_")[0]
    cross = edges[edges.src.map(unit_of) != edges.dst.map(unit_of)].w.sum()
    return {"density": nx.density(G),
            "top_betweenness": max(bc.values()) if bc else np.nan,
            "top_in_strength_share": instr.max() / instr.sum() if len(instr) else np.nan,
            "cross_unit_weight_share": cross / edges.w.sum()}

def network_compare(df_pre, df_post):
    rows = []
    for name, fn, directed in [
        ("handover",        lambda d: pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(d), True),
        ("working_together", lambda d: pm4py.discover_working_together_network(d), False),
        ("subcontracting",  lambda d: pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(d, n=1), True)]:
        for label, d in [("pre", df_pre), ("post", df_post)]:
            m = graph_metrics(net_to_df(fn(d)), directed)
            rows.append({"network": name, "period": label, **m})
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Manual handover table: counts, waits, return-to-originator, ping-pong
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
def handover_table(df):
    d = df.copy()
    g = d.groupby("case:concept:name")
    for c in ["org:resource", "unit", "time:timestamp"]:
        d["next_" + c] = g[c].shift(-1)
    d["wait_h"] = (d["next_time:timestamp"] - d["time:timestamp"]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    h = d[d["org:resource"] != d["next_org:resource"]].dropna(subset=["next_org:resource"])
    h["type"] = h["unit"] + " -> " + h["next_unit"]
    n_cases = d["case:concept:name"].nunique()
    tab = h.groupby("type").agg(freq_per_100=("wait_h", lambda s: 100 * len(s) / n_cases),
                                median_wait_h=("wait_h", "median"),
                                p90_wait_h=("wait_h", lambda s: s.quantile(.9)))
    # weighted wait per 100 cases: compare against management's implied ~588 -> ~722
    tab["weighted_wait_per_100"] = tab.freq_per_100 * tab.median_wait_h
    return tab

def loop_metrics(df):
    """Return-to-originator (A -> pool -> A) and ping-pong (A->B->A) rates."""
    res = df.groupby("case:concept:name")["org:resource"].apply(list)
    rt = tot = pp = 0
    for seq in res:
        for i in range(len(seq) - 2):
            if seq[i] != seq[i + 1]:
                tot += 1
                if seq[i + 2] == seq[i]:
                    rt += 1
        pp += sum(1 for i in range(len(seq) - 3)
                  if seq[i] == seq[i + 2] and seq[i + 1] == seq[i + 3])
    return {"return_to_originator": rt / tot if tot else np.nan,
            "pingpong_per_100_cases": 100 * pp / len(res)}

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Variants and performance DFG (with diff)
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
def variant_stats(df, k=10):
    v = pm4py.get_variants(df)                       # {tuple(activities): count}
    s = pd.Series(v).sort_values(ascending=False)
    p = s / s.sum()
    return {"n_variants": len(s),
            f"top{k}_coverage": p.head(k).sum(),
            "variant_entropy": float(-(p * np.log2(p)).sum())}, s

def dfg_diff(df_pre, df_post):
    fp, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(df_pre)
    fq, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(df_post)
    pp, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(df_pre)     # mean seconds per edge
    pq, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(df_post)
    edges = set(fp) | set(fq)
    out = pd.DataFrame([{
        "edge": e,
        "freq_pre": fp.get(e, 0), "freq_post": fq.get(e, 0),
        "mean_h_pre": pp.get(e, np.nan) / 3600, "mean_h_post": pq.get(e, np.nan) / 3600}
        for e in edges])
    out["delta_mean_h"] = out.mean_h_post - out.mean_h_pre
    return out.sort_values("delta_mean_h", ascending=False)

def top_variants_only(df, s, n=20):
    return pm4py.filter_variants(df, list(s.head(n).index), retain=True)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Service and waiting times, queue length, utilization
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
def service_waiting(df):
    # Meaningful service/waiting split needs start + complete timestamps
    # (add enqueue/pull/start/complete events in Phase 0). Otherwise
    # "waiting" is just inter-event time.
    d = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df)
    # per-activity service time, only valid if start timestamps exist:
    # svc = pm4py.get_service_time(df, aggregation_measure="median")
    return d

def queue_length(df, pool="TechShared", freq="6H"):
    d = df.copy()
    g = d.groupby("case:concept:name")
    d["next_unit"] = g["unit"].shift(-1)
    d["next_ts"]   = g["time:timestamp"].shift(-1)
    q = d[d["next_unit"] == pool][["time:timestamp", "next_ts"]].dropna()
    # queue = time from handover event until next event completes at the pool
    events = pd.concat([pd.Series(1, index=q["time:timestamp"]),
                        pd.Series(-1, index=q["next_ts"])]).sort_index()
    return events.cumsum().resample(freq).max()

def weekly_features(df):
    # temporal features per week: arrivals, load, etc.
    return pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(df, grouper_freq="W")

def utilization(df, pool_units, capacity_hours_per_week):
    d = df[df["unit"].isin(pool_units)].copy()
    # requires start timestamps; else approximate service = gap to own next event
    d["svc_h"] = d.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].diff(-1).abs().dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    d["week"] = d["time:timestamp"].dt.to_period("W")
    return d.groupby("week")["svc_h"].sum() / capacity_hours_per_week

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Resource similarity and organisational roles
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
def role_clarity(df):
    sim = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(df)   # (r1, r2) -> similarity
    roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(df)                # list of Role objects
    n_res_in_multi = pd.Series(
        [r for role in roles for r in role.originator_importance]).value_counts()
    act_share = (df.groupby(["org:resource", "concept:name"]).size()
                   .groupby(level=0).apply(lambda s: s / s.sum()))
    entropy = act_share.groupby(level=0).apply(lambda p: float(-(p * np.log2(p)).sum()))
    return {"n_roles": len(roles),
            "resources_in_>1_role": int((n_res_in_multi > 1).sum()),
            "mean_activity_entropy": entropy.mean(),
            "mean_pairwise_similarity": float(np.mean(list(sim.connections.values())))}

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 7. Case table: cycle time, rework, SLA, fairness, learning
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
def case_table(df):
    g = df.groupby("case:concept:name")
    c = g.agg(start=("time:timestamp", "min"), end=("time:timestamp", "max"),
              team=("home_team", "first"), complexity=("case_complexity", "first"),
              specialty=("required_specialty", "first"),
              segment=("customer_segment", "first"), priority=("priority", "first"),
              reassign=("reassignment_count", "max"), rework=("rework_flag", "max"),
              outcome=("outcome", "last"),
              closed=("concept:name", lambda s: (s == "Close").any()))
    c["cycle_d"] = (c.end - c.start).dt.total_seconds() / 86400
    c["sla_miss"] = c.cycle_d > c.complexity.map(SLA_DAYS)
    c["intake_week"] = c.start.dt.to_period("W")
    return c

def summarize(c):
    # Use only mature cohorts (or handle open cases via survival) to avoid censoring bias
    return pd.Series({"n": len(c), "median_d": c.cycle_d.median(),
                      "p90_d": c.cycle_d.quantile(.9), "p95_d": c.cycle_d.quantile(.95),
                      "rework": c.rework.mean(), "sla_miss": c.sla_miss.mean(),
                      "reassign_mean": c.reassign.mean()})

def rework_by_activity(df):
    return pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(df)   # {activity: n_cases_with_rework}

def standardized_p90(c_pre, c_post, by="segment"):
    """Adjust post-change P90 to the pre-change complexity mix (fairness, mix effect)."""
    mix = c_pre.complexity.value_counts(normalize=True)
    out = {}
    for k, sub in c_post.groupby(by):
        per_cx = sub.groupby("complexity").cycle_d.quantile(.9)
        out[k] = float((per_cx * mix.reindex(per_cx.index).fillna(0)).sum() / mix.reindex(per_cx.index).fillna(0).sum())
    s = pd.Series(out)
    return s, s.max() / s.min()      # disparity ratio

def learning_metrics(df):
    """Technical activity performed by local resources over time + local expertise share."""
    t = df[(df["concept:name"] == "Technical Assessment") & (~df["unit"].isin(["TechShared"]))]
    by_month = t.groupby(t["time:timestamp"].dt.to_period("M"))["org:resource"].agg(["count", "nunique"])
    exp = df.groupby(df["time:timestamp"].dt.to_period("M"))["local_expertise_flag"].mean()
    return by_month, exp

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 8. Before/after comparison loop over all slices + difference-in-differences
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
def compare(pre, post, slices=SLICES):
    rows = []
    for name, f in slices.items():
        a, b = slice_log(pre, **f), slice_log(post, **f)
        ca, cb = case_table(a), case_table(b)
        sa, sb = summarize(ca), summarize(cb)
        rows.append(pd.concat([sa.add_suffix("_pre"), sb.add_suffix("_post")]).rename(name))
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

def did(pilot_pre, pilot_post, ctrl_pre, ctrl_post, metric="p90_d"):
    s = lambda d: summarize(case_table(d))[metric]
    return (s(pilot_post) - s(pilot_pre)) - (s(ctrl_post) - s(ctrl_pre))

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 9. Resilience: simulated absence
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
def top_resource_concentration(df, k=3):
    share = df["org:resource"].value_counts(normalize=True)
    return share.head(k).sum()

def absence_stress(df, resource):
    """Rough what-if: cases touched by `resource` and their current cycle-time tail."""
    touched = df[df["org:resource"] == resource]["case:concept:name"].unique()
    c = case_table(df[df["case:concept:name"].isin(touched)])
    return {"cases_touched": len(touched), "p90_d": c.cycle_d.quantile(.9)}

# ------------------------------------------------------------------

print(handover_table(pre)); print(handover_table(post))

print(compare(pre, post))
```

**Notes on interpretation:**
- **Harmonize before comparing.** Activity names and resource groupings must map cleanly, or network and variant differences will reflect renaming, not behavior.
- **Home team, not current team.** Filter on team at intake, since routing changes the `team` attribute mid-case.
- **Cohorts and censoring.** Compare by intake cohort with a maturity cut-off, or use survival analysis for open cases.
- **Waiting vs. service.** A true split requires lifecycle events. Without them, the "waiting" figures are inter-event gaps that include service time.
- **Attribution.** Use the pilot/control DiD, adjusted for complexity, specialty, and segment mix. A network change alone does not prove that restructuring caused it.
- **Capacity data.** Utilization needs real availability hours (including part-time Legal and Risk), which are not in the event log.
