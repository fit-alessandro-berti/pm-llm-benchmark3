## 1. Materiality Framework

Severity is a business-impact judgment; raw alignment cost is a syntactic distance. The two are decoupled deliberately. Severity here is scored 1 (negligible) to 5 (severe control failure) using eight factors:

- **Control objective at stake** – Is the deviation on a hard control (fraud screening, dual approval, payment sequencing) or a soft/administrative step (notification channel, UI telemetry)?
- **Amount / financial exposure** – Absolute EUR value and its proportion to review thresholds (5,000 / 50,000).
- **Reversibility** – Once `Pay Claim` executes, money movement is largely irreversible; anything before payment is more correctable.
- **Timing/sequence** – Whether a control happened *before* the irreversible step it is meant to gate (P3), not merely whether it happened at all.
- **Customer/regulatory harm** – Late/missing notification, wrongful denial, or wrongful payment delay affecting the claimant or breaching statutory timing.
- **Recurrence** – Single-case anomaly vs. systemic pattern (e.g., 74-case retry connector, 4-case auto-approve leak, 9-case adjuster gap).
- **Evidence reliability** – Is the corroborating evidence immutable and independently verifiable (signed report, bank record) or a mutable free-text field / verbal claim?
- **Model scope legitimacy** – Whether the "deviation" is really an operational failure or simply a case the process tree was never designed to cover (denials, withdrawals) — P6.

**Confidence** is a separate axis: it expresses how much we trust that the alignment (and the underlying event log) faithfully represents what actually happened, independent of how bad the scenario would be if true. A rare, severe scenario built on solid corroborating evidence (bank timestamps, signed reports) can carry high confidence; a severe-looking gap resting on a missing event and a verbal assurance carries lower confidence until investigated. Confidence should never be inflated by cost value — a cost of 1 says nothing about whether the missing/extra event is a real control failure, a labeling artifact, or unlogged-but-real activity.

## 3. Repeated Deviation Patterns

**A. Retry/duplicate notification telemetry** (CL-603 + 74 similar cases)
- Evidence: identical message ID, single retrying connector implicated across the full month.
- Risk: Low — no customer harm, purely a messaging-layer artifact.
- Cause: Connector retry logic without idempotency/dedupe check.
- Confidence: High.
- Owner: Integration/platform engineering.

**B. External-adjuster evidence gap for Assess Loss** (CL-606 + 9 high-value cases, 2 lacking stable doc-case IDs)
- Evidence: External signed reports exist but core log misses the internal Assess Loss event; document linkage inconsistent.
- Risk: Medium — for the 2 unlinked cases, cannot currently prove assessment occurred through durable evidence; audit/regulatory exposure on high-value claims.
- Cause: Assessment work performed by external, non-integrated system/party; document management not wired into core case log.
- Confidence: Medium (external evidence likely genuine, but linkage integrity unverified for a subset).
- Owner: Records management / claims operations data integration.

**C. Event-label mismatch** (CL-611)
- Evidence: mapping table updated after the case; label difference between "Assess Damage" and "Assess Loss" for what is procedurally one activity.
- Risk: Low intrinsic risk, but corrupts conformance statistics and may mask true anomalies in other cases sharing the old mapping.
- Cause: Incomplete/outdated activity taxonomy mapping.
- Confidence: High.
- Owner: Data engineering / process-mining center of excellence.

**D. Out-of-scope denial and withdrawal handling** (CL-607, CL-612)
- Evidence: Both produce maximal raw cost (4) purely from forcing non-payable paths through a payable-claim model; explicit scope dispute (P6) noted for denials.
- Risk: Low operationally (no payment made in either case) but high **governance** risk — inflated "worst" alignments could misdirect remediation effort or dashboards toward non-issues.
- Cause: Process tree M0 was scoped to payable claims; no representation of denial/withdrawal exits.
- Confidence: Low-Medium until governance ruling issued.
- Owner: Process governance board / model owner, with sign-off from process owner.

**E. Data-aware auto-approval failures** (CL-609, CL-610, plus 4 cases noted with fully synchronous Auto Approve despite P1/P2 violation)
- Evidence: Four cases in the monthly dataset are structurally perfect (cost 0) yet violate risk-tier or bank-change conditions for Auto Approve eligibility.
- Risk: High — this is the most dangerous pattern in the set because it is invisible to plain control-flow conformance; includes a plausible fraud vector (bank-change bypass).
- Cause: Auto-approval decision engine does not consult risk tier / bank-change flags that live outside the control-flow model.
- Confidence: High (facts of risk tier, amount, and bank-change timing are given directly, not inferred).
- Owner: Risk & controls engineering, in partnership with claims IT policy-rule owners.

**F. Payment-sequencing/control-bypass failures** (CL-602, CL-604)
- Evidence: One case skips Fraud Screen entirely before Manual Approve on a very large, high-risk, bank-changed claim; another shows payment executed before approval was recorded.
- Risk: Severe — irreversible payment either preceded or bypassed a mandatory gate.
- Cause: Possible workflow misconfiguration, manual override culture, or timestamp/synchronization issues between payment and approval systems.
- Confidence: Medium (facts are compelling but require forensic confirmation of clocks/channels).
- Owner: Claims operations leadership + internal audit / fraud investigations.

**G. Cross-system notification evidence weakness** (CL-608)
- Evidence: Only one case so far, but structurally significant (mutable text-field linkage) — likely part of a broader P4 evidentiary design gap.
- Risk: Medium-High for large claims; regulatory timing compliance cannot be proven robustly.
- Cause: Notification channel diversity (P4) without an immutable cross-system linkage standard.
- Confidence: Low on this specific instance's compliance status, High on the systemic weakness of the mechanism.
- Owner: CRM/portal integration team + compliance.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

**CL-602 (missing Fraud Screen)**
- H1: Fraud screening was genuinely skipped (control failure).
- H2: Screening was performed in a separate, unintegrated tool/channel and simply not logged.
- H3: A system integration fault dropped the event.
- Discriminating evidence: search fraud-screening system/API logs and email/ticket trails for the case ID; check approver's system access logs at the relevant timestamp; confirm whether "verbal fraud check" has any institutional recognition as valid evidence. A missing event alone does not prove H1 — it only proves absence *in this log*.

**CL-604 (payment before approval)**
- H1: Payment genuinely executed before approval (sequencing/control breach).
- H2: Clock skew between the bank system and the workflow system misrepresents true order.
- H3: Approval happened earlier but was recorded late (logging delay).
- Discriminating evidence: NTP/clock-sync audit between systems; check for any earlier informal approval trace (email, phone log); bank-side transaction initiation log vs. authorization timestamp.

**CL-606 (missing Assess Loss)**
- H1: Assessment happened externally and simply wasn't logged internally (integration gap).
- H2: Internal assessment never occurred; downstream steps proceeded on the adjuster report alone (process bypass).
- H3: Assessment was logged under a different label/system (similar to CL-611).
- Discriminating evidence: adjuster report metadata (signer ID, timestamp) cross-referenced with case assignment logs; check if a synonym-labeled event exists elsewhere in the case's full audit trail; confirm whether the 9-case pattern correlates with a specific external-adjuster workflow rollout.

**CL-607 (denied claim treated as model violation)**
- H1: The model is correctly scoped to payable claims, and this "deviation" is an artifact of forcing an out-of-scope case through it.
- H2: Denial handling should, per some stakeholders, be in scope, making this a genuine model gap.
- Discriminating evidence: written scope decision from model owner and process owner; count and pattern of denied-claim alignments; whether denial has its own downstream controls elsewhere.

**CL-609 / CL-610 / four-case Auto Approve pattern**
- H1: Risk score or bank-change flag was correctly available but ignored by the approval engine (rule-engine defect).
- H2: The relevant risk/bank-change data was updated *after* the Auto Approve decision (timing/latency issue, not a rule defect).
- H3: Manual override intentionally bypassed the automatic gate for legitimate reasons not captured in the log.
- Discriminating evidence: timestamp of risk-tier assignment and bank-change flag relative to the Auto Approve decision timestamp; configuration/version history of the approval rule engine; any override/justification field in the approval system.

**CL-611 (label mismatch)**
- H1: "Assess Damage" and "Assess Loss" are the same business activity under different system labels (mapping gap).
- H2: They are genuinely different sub-activities performed by different roles/systems, and treating them as equivalent would mask real process variants.
- Discriminating evidence: procedure documentation defining both terms; role/permission logs showing which actor performed the logged activity; whether the mapping-table update explicitly targeted this synonym pair, and whether other cases show both labels concurrently (which would support H2).

**CL-608 (notification evidence)**
- H1: Notification genuinely occurred via secure portal within the required window (P4-compliant).
- H2: The CRM text-field link is fabricated, stale, or unrelated to this case (non-compliant).
- Discriminating evidence: portal delivery/read receipts, message content hash matching claimant details, comparison of the linked timestamp against the mutable field's edit history/version log.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

1. **Uniform, non-business-weighted cost function.** As stated, every visible move costs exactly 1 regardless of which activity it is. This means a missing `Fraud Screen` on a EUR 480,000 high-risk case (CL-602, cost 1) is scored identically to a duplicate `Notify Claimant` caused by a retry connector (CL-603, cost 1). The cost function cannot see amount, risk tier, or control criticality — only move count.

2. **Data-aware conditions live outside the control-flow model.** CL-609 and CL-610 are fully synchronous (cost 0) because the process tree's Auto Approve branch is structurally always legal — it has no encoded knowledge of risk tier or bank-change timing (P1/P2). A perfect cost-0 alignment therefore actively conceals two of the most severe policy violations in the set. Any prioritization that trusted raw cost would rank these as "no issue" and CL-607 (scope mismatch, cost 4) as the worst case — precisely backwards.

3. **Hidden (silent) moves are fitting by construction.** CL-605's tau before Auto Approve costs 0 and is not a deviation at all — it is model routing logic. This must be explicitly reported as fitting, not folded indistinguishably into "low cost = low risk," because the same reasoning (low cost) applies for entirely different reasons in CL-609/610 (real risk hidden) versus CL-605 (no risk, non-issue).

4. **Granularity and duplication distort cost independent of severity.** CL-611's cost of 2 stems from treating a same-meaning relabeled event as delete+insert (L+M), inflating apparent deviation for what is very likely a harmless naming synonym — while CL-603's cost of 1 stems from genuine duplicate telemetry, not from anything resembling risk. Both produce non-zero cost through mechanical string-edit logic that has no awareness of semantic identity or duplication cause.

5. **Missing events are not proof of non-occurrence, and cost does not encode evidence quality.** CL-606's cost of 1 for `M(Assess Loss)` treats the missing event the same as any other missing move, yet strong external evidence (a signed adjuster report) suggests the underlying work likely happened — the real issue is evidentiary linkage, not the activity's existence. Conversely CL-608's cost of 1 for `M(Notify Claimant)` sits next to weak, mutable evidence on a EUR 250,000 claim — same cost, very different evidentiary confidence and stakes.

6. **Irreversibility is invisible to the cost function.** CL-604's cost of 3 is "mid-range," yet it represents money already paid out before approval was recorded — an irreversible event that categorically outranks equally- or higher-costed but reversible or inconsequential deviations (e.g., CL-607/CL-612 at cost 4, where no payment occurred at all). Cost has no concept of "can this still be undone."

Taken together, the raw_cost column should be read purely as a *technical edit-distance signal* — useful for detecting that *something* differs from the reference model — while severity, confidence, and remediation priority must be derived from the business narrative and policy overlay applied independently, exactly as separated in Section 2's table.

"""
Reproduction and aggregation scaffold for the insurance-claim alignment case set.

do not override the supplied alignments, which are treated as authoritative
diagnostic inputs for this analysis per the task instructions.

import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Parse the supplied process tree
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
tree_string = (
    "-> ( 'Register Claim', 'Validate Coverage', 'Assess Loss', "
    "X ( -> ( 'Fraud Screen', 'Manual Approve' ), 'Auto Approve' ), "
    "'Pay Claim', 'Notify Claimant' )"
)

process_tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_string)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Convert to an accepting Petri net
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(process_tree)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

case_traces = {

               "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-602": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss",
               "Manual Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-603": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss",
               "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-604": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss",
               "Pay Claim", "Manual Approve", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-605": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss",

    "CL-610": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss",
               "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"],

    for i, act in enumerate(activities):

        "case_id": case_id,

#    analysis (Sections 1-6). This is expected, not an error to reconcile away.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    {"case_id": "CL-605", "raw_cost": 0, "amount_eur": 800,    "risk": "LOW",    "bank_change": False, "policy_flag": "compliant",              "severity": 1, "confidence": "High"},
    {"case_id": "CL-606", "raw_cost": 1, "amount_eur": 75000,  "risk": "HIGH",   "bank_change": False, "policy_flag": "evidence_gap",            "severity": 3, "confidence": "Medium"},
