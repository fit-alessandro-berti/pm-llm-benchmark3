## 2. Counterfactual continuations

**1 — D: information gathering alongside preparation for S4.**

At time zero, resume Analyst Review of the validated evidence, check the risk-score timestamp and seek a current assessment, run one alternate screening call, and confirm S4’s actual availability and independence from R17. Screening and resource coordination can run alongside analyst work. The second officer’s final review must consider material new results.

`Evidence Validated → Analyst Review → independent Second Officer Review → decision`

- Adequate evidence supports payment: `Approve → Payment Executed`, if approval and execution finish in time. The observational ultimate-approval range of 0.55–0.75 is context, not a probability calibrated to this intervention.
- The match remains unresolved: second review remains mandatory; the officer must determine whether the evidence supports a lawful decision. A completed review is not automatic clearance. Branch to a justified decision or a hold for further work.
- Adverse findings meet the rejection threshold: document and reject through the required decision path.
- Findings justify reporting: hold and file a Suspicious Activity Report without customer disclosure. Reporting creates no execution authority.
- Evidence remains inadequate: use a targeted E loop if it can resolve the specific issue. Do not force approval to meet the deadline.

A negative alternate screen does not erase the original match by itself. Reconcile the results and document any resolution; even a resolved match leaves a current score of 72 independently triggering second review. If S4’s ETA is late or unreliable, compare C immediately rather than wait for the screening result or the nominal S4 arrival time before considering resources.

**2 — C: expedited independent review by S2.**

Approval permits subsequent execution. Rejection requires adequate grounds; hold may lead to the evidence loop or confidential reporting. If S2 finds the file incomplete, the 40–90-minute completion estimate no longer describes the full continuation.

**3 — E: targeted evidence loop.**

Relevant, reliable evidence may support approval or establish rejection grounds. Inadequate evidence may require another loop or a continued hold. **5 — G: attempted exception, with ordinary review preserved.**

`Assess genuine incident grounds → request director authorization + establish valid incident identifier`

- Both requirements are satisfied and the documented authorization covers the proposed manual override: use that exception only within its permitted scope, record the basis and subsequent actions, and execute only if the resulting authorization path permits it.
- Either requirement is absent, refused, invalid or late: no override. Continue the ordinary independent-review route or hold; expiry of the customer deadline creates no authority.

Director response time is not the probability of approval. A generated identifier without a genuine, justified incident does not satisfy the condition.

## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

Use conformance as an admissibility constraint before comparing operational outcomes. Within admissible plans, evaluate timely lawful execution, decision quality, customer harm and portfolio effects. A/B fail the constraint even under forecasts that predict excellent operational results. G cannot be treated as an available action until enabled.

For a lawful plan i, let q_i be the probability of valid approval and execution within 90 minutes. Its gross expected deadline benefit is q_i times the €20k–€35k avoided loss, allowing for the value’s uncertainty. Its incremental benefit against normal processing depends on the change in that joint probability. Neither q_i nor the change is supplied. The normal-processing breach forecast implies a 0.36–0.52 probability of avoiding breach, but avoiding breach need not equal timely lawful execution. Do not multiply this by the 0.55–0.75 ultimate-approval forecast: approval, evidence difficulty and completion time are dependent, and eventual approval may occur after the deadline.

As a narrow sensitivity calculation, assigning every normal-processing breach a €20k–€35k loss gives €9.6k–€22.4k expected deadline loss. This requires an assumption about losses on every breach and is not an estimate of what an intervention can recover. The supplied observational predictions may change under expedited review, preemption or alternative screening; no numerical reduction in breach probability is identified for C or D.

The €15k–€80k false-rejection/prolonged-hold range makes both F and E costly default responses. E preserves the possibility of later execution but almost certainly misses the current deadline. F terminates the payment and may be appropriate when evidence establishes rejection grounds; otherwise it risks discarding a case with a substantial predicted chance of eventual conformant approval. That forecast does not itself prove that rejection would be false. Identify the particular missing evidence before imposing either outcome.

The €100k–€500k unlawful-execution exposure is a possible consequence range, not its expected value or a capped penalty. Its asymmetry, together with sanctions-control failures, reputational damage, weakened audit credibility and potential harm from an improper transfer, reinforces the need for effective controls. Even a low modeled expected penalty would not authorize an unlawful path. Conversely, procedural conformance does not guarantee correct identity resolution or eliminate residual compliance risk.

D can improve decision quality without increasing elapsed time when its 15–30-minute screen fits inside unavoidable waiting for S4. That makes it attractive if the information is useful, but it does not strictly dominate waiting alone because the API call has an opportunity cost. C improves access to a reviewer but does not dominate D at the portfolio level because P-901 is harmed. E and F are evidence-dependent responses, rather than reliable means of rescuing the deadline. Avoid adding deadline loss, customer disruption and regulatory exposure mechanically: some components overlap, and non-monetary harms are not captured by a single euro total.

The risk score being 12 minutes old warrants checking currency and intervening information, but the prompt specifies no automatic expiry. Do not assume the score has fallen. - **A — rules 5, 6, 8–10:** skips the outstanding process and approval requirements, including mandatory second review, and has no enabled override. Its favorable deadline forecast cannot supply missing authority.
- **B — rules 6–7 and 8–9:** self-certification by R17 fails independence and cannot count as the required second review or support valid approval. A numerical speed advantage cannot satisfy the four-eyes principle.
- **C — rules 5–9:** valid when the analyst return step is completed and S2 independently performs the required review. Approval, rejection and hold must follow the appropriate evidence and decision requirements; execution follows valid approval.
- **D — rules 5–9:** alternate screening is supporting information. Preserve the analyst return step, reconcile conflicting results, obtain independent second review, and record the decision. Neither re-screening nor a lower vendor match score substitutes for these controls.
- **E — rules 5–9:** follow the complete evidence loop, including validation and return to Analyst Review. It cannot jump from Evidence Received to approval. The current high-risk/unresolved-match case retains second review before approval or execution.
- **F — rules 5 and 8, with rule 6 relevant to the review path:** establish the actual applicable rejection threshold, show how validated evidence satisfies it, record a defensible rationale and decision authority, and complete the required reviews. Rule 6 expressly gates approval/execution; it does not itself establish a special early-rejection permission. For this recommendation, retain independent review before rejection under rule 8 unless an applicable, valid earlier rejection path is established. The prompt supplies neither threshold satisfaction nor such a path, so do not implement the literal immediate rejection now.
- **G — rule 10:** require actual Compliance Director authorization, a genuine valid incident identifier, and records linking the incident and authorization to this payment and the permitted override. A request, expected response, or unjustified identifier is insufficient. Confirm the exception’s scope rather than inventing a general waiver of review or substantive sanctions obligations.

Rule 11 applies throughout: do not disclose a Suspicious Activity Report to the customer. A customer update may explain delay or request necessary evidence without revealing the report. Under rule 8, filing a report while held does not authorize execution and cannot replace independent review or approval.

Also, the actual review may last 35–75 minutes; do not apply the 45-minute estimate unchanged or scale it linearly without evidence. **API quota.** One D attempt leaves two expedited calls for other cases. Its value is decision-relevant identity information, not automatic permission to execute. Avoid repeated calls without a specific information gain. If the vendor cannot resolve the relevant ambiguity or another case has a stronger need for the quota, omit D’s screen and raise C or the ordinary S4 review plan accordingly.

**Exception delay.** Director outreach may take 20–60 minutes or longer and still fail to create a valid exception. Pursue G only with a credible incident basis and preserve ordinary review where feasible. Administrative work, director scarcity and lost reviewer opportunities are real costs even if outreach runs in parallel. An unsuccessful exception request must not leave the team dependent on an unlawful last-minute action.

## 6. Final recommendation and confidence

**Primary recommendation:** start D as a controlled parallel plan: resume Analyst Review, check the score’s currency, use one alternate-screening call, and immediately confirm an early S4 slot. Complete independent second review after considering material findings, then approve and execute only if justified; otherwise reject on established grounds or hold. Any reporting remains confidential. Favor waiting for S4 only while an early arrival and sufficiently short review make that choice credible relative to the harm of interrupting P-901.

**Fallback:** switch promptly to C when S4 cannot support a credible timely completion and the estimated portfolio benefit warrants preempting P-901. Do not wait for a nonessential screening result before arranging S2; the eventual decision must still incorporate material results. If neither reviewer option supports timely lawful execution, accept the deadline miss and continue the required review, using targeted E only for an identified evidence gap. The deadline does not justify A/B or enable G.

**Confidence:** high in the mandatory-control conclusions and exclusion of A/B; moderate in prioritizing independent review with parallel information gathering; low-to-moderate in D versus C because availability, review duration, information value and P-901’s loss are uncertain.

**Immediate reranking trigger:** the next confirmed S4 availability estimate—especially a shift from approximately 35 to 65 minutes. Also rerank immediately on a material screening/risk update or an adverse change in P-901’s ability to tolerate interruption. These observations change the feasible continuation and resource choice; the observational predictions alone do not establish the effect of intervention.
