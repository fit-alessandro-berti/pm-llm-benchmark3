## 2. Process-model consistency checks

A validation suite should generate assertions from a timestamped event log, the feature lineage graph, and a declarative version of the normative process.

1. **Establish prediction-time and feature-availability tests.**
   - Identify the first `Coverage Check Completed` by claim ID and freeze its event time, delivery time, and reliable local position as `t0`.
   - For every input, compare both its source-event times and its materialization/availability time with `t0`.
   - Admit only feature versions available by `t0`, including the actual point-in-time policy, queue snapshot, and roster versions.
   - Use synthetic tests for a field whose event time precedes `t0` but whose update was delivered afterward; that field must fail.
   - Reject nightly snapshots for intraday prediction unless the underlying event-time records are reconstructed.
   - For prior history, test both the temporal cutoff and the cohort, including accidental inclusion of the current claim.

2. **Run a complete-trace lineage audit.**
   - Maintain a directed lineage graph from each feature to its source events, transformations, join dimensions, and feature versions.
   - Assert that every source event used by an early explanation is observed by `t0`; reject a complete-trace path ID, final decision, closure field, or eventual count.
   - Detect mutable values by checking whether the feature store retains all historical versions rather than only the latest value.
   - Run a negative test in which a future outcome changes but all `t0` inputs remain fixed. The early prediction must not change.

3. **Check activity-order feasibility against the declared process.**
   - `Claim Registered` precedes the first coverage check.
   - After coverage, permit either the direct damage-assessment fast track or an evidence branch containing at least one request, receipt, and validation cycle.
   - Permit another request only in a valid subsequent evidence cycle. A repeated request count must not be mistaken for duplicate ingestion.
   - Require damage assessment to follow the selected track or completed evidence branch.
   - Allow fraud investigation only after its trigger, and represent it as potentially parallel rather than forcing an artificial total order between independent investigations and claim work.
   - Require settlement before closure, allow only the four defined decision types, and permit appeal only following Partial or Deny.
   - Maintain genuine uncertainty about the branch at coverage completion; passing a fast-track or evidence-branch check must not be treated as knowing which branch occurred.

4. **Handle same-timestamp ambiguity explicitly.**
   - Require a reliable source sequence index or event identity before asserting an order between tied events.
   - Never repair a missing index by arbitrary alphabetical sorting, ingestion recency, or an assumed DFG frequency.
   - Represent unresolved equal-time events as an unordered set or partial order.
   - For Case Z’s closed/damage events, test whether certified ordering would put closure first. If so, it is a genuine conformance violation; if order remains uncertifiable, report an unresolved source-order issue rather than claiming that the real claim necessarily violated the process.
   - A correct index for two settlement decisions, by itself, does not establish a valid process: an appeal and the appropriate predecessor decision must also be present.

5. **Classify explanation references as enabled, impossible, or future-possible.**
   - **Enabled at `t0`:** a transition or state whose required events and metadata are already observed. An appeal would require an earlier Partial or Deny decision, which is not present in these prefixes.
   - **Future-possible:** a process-valid continuation not yet observed, such as a later document request/receipt or damage/decision transition. It must not enter a `t0` explanation.
   - **Process-inconsistent:** a certified ordering forbidden by the process, such as damage assessment after closure. An ingestion-order artifact is not sufficient proof of this.
   - **Future-conditional:** fraud investigation after a trigger. The transition may be allowed, but a `t0` explanation cannot assert that the trigger or investigation occurred.
   - Validate the narrative as well as the numeric feature: a label such as “queue pressure” must be linked to an available queue observation, not an imagined later event.

6. **Test repeated settlement decisions semantically.**
   - Identify each decision by event ID, decision value, and reliable sequence position; do not deduplicate it merely because its coarse activity label is the same.
   - Permit a repeated decision following an appeal after Partial or Deny.
   - Reject an appeal after Approve or Withdrawn, and reject an additional decision without the required appeal lineage.
   - Do not require every appeal necessarily to produce another decision unless that requirement is explicitly added to the governing process; verify the actual decision and closure semantics.
   - At this model’s coverage-time prediction point, all such later decision behavior is future information regardless of whether it is legitimate.

7. **Use alignment, footprint, prefix, DFG, and declarative checks for different purposes.**
   - **Prefix checks** determine whether an explanation depends only on the observed state at its prediction time.
   - **Alignment checks** assess event correspondence and ordering, including uncertain ties; they cannot manufacture missing sequence evidence.
   - **Footprint checks** can assess whether observed behavior is compatible with allowed behavior. A footprint calculation that uses the actual future is an offline conformance test, not an admissible early feature.
   - **DFG statistics** are useful for discovering candidate branches, loops, and anomalies, but a frequent edge is not a normative rule and a rare edge is not automatically invalid.
   - **Declarative temporal rules** provide the authoritative feasibility constraints for this simplified process.

The suite should include gold-standard valid fast-track, multi-cycle, parallel-fraud, and appeal/reconsideration traces, plus deliberately invalid traces. It should be run both offline and in the serving feature pipeline, with failures blocking publication of an explanation.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

The relevant standards are distinct:

- **Predictive validity:** the feature is available when needed and improves temporally validated, calibrated predictions. This does not require a causal effect.
- **Causal interpretation:** an intervention on the feature would change the target under stated assumptions. SHAP, DFG frequency, or a plausible process story cannot establish this.
- **Operational actionability:** an authorized party can change the relevant conditions early enough, and doing so is expected to improve the outcome. A measured workload or demographic attribute need not be actionable.
- **Fairness review:** assess proxy effects, measurement quality, group errors, calibration, access to services, and the consequences of decisions. A feature can be predictively useful and still require restrictions or remedies.

### Defensible predictive signal

- `initial_damage_complexity` is a defensible early predictor because it is frozen before `t0`. It may support forecasting, but one cannot claim that changing the recorded complexity label would shorten settlement.
- `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` is a defensible past-derived state variable. Under the target’s time convention, it represents how much processing opportunity remains. Its model contribution is not a causal effect of accelerating registration.
- Registration metadata such as channel or initial reserve can be used **if** an immutable, available-at-`t0` version is verified.
- Genuine observed-prefix states and transitions are defensible signals when their prerequisite events are present. Future-possible paths are not.

### Process-state or congestion signal

- `queue_length_at_t0` describes observed workload. It can be predictive without being individually controllable; a handler cannot change the snapshot for the current claim by receiving a higher-risk prediction.
- Prefix document counts, completed evidence cycles, and outstanding work may describe process state. A document count is not itself proof of progress, progress quality, or causality.
- A correctly reconstructed prior-claim count can describe history, but it remains a proxy for circumstances and past system exposure rather than an intrinsic claimant characteristic.
- Prospective resource changes based on congestion require a separate intervention study; the current claim’s queue association is not evidence that such a policy will work.

### Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review

- `claimant_region` can represent repair-network availability, language access, or service coverage. It may improve forecast accuracy without being a cause of claimant behavior.
- Verified channel, prior history, and even complexity assessments can correlate with socioeconomic or service-access characteristics. Correlation alone is not proof of unfairness or discrimination.
- Compare temporally valid models with and without these variables, examine group and intersectional calibration/selection, audit how features are measured, and test robustness to missing or shifted inputs. A blanket deletion rule is not required.
- Region or channel must not become a basis for punitive claim treatment. Potential remedies should address service access or process design and require separate evidence.

### Drift/calendar proxy

- `calendar_week` may forecast workload, holidays, or policy regimes. It does not establish that the calendar week itself causes delay.
- Use policy and holiday annotations effective at `t0`, test year-to-year stability, and distinguish recurring seasonality from process or data drift.
- Prefer validated operational covariates over unexplained week labels when they are available in time. A change in attribution after a policy change may reflect process drift rather than a new causal relationship.

### Unusable until lineage is repaired

- `total_case_duration_hours`, realized appeals, complete document totals, and `full_trace_variant_id` are unavailable at this prediction time. They must be replaced by genuinely prefix-derived analogues or retired; relabeling the existing fields does not repair them.
- `final_handler_team` is unusable unless an actual historical assignment-at-`t0` exists. Closure-team agreement cannot repair the missing history.
- `prior_claim_count_before_t0` may be reconstructed from earlier claims and historical availability.
- `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, mutable current risk scores, and last-resource role require event-time reconstruction or point-in-time versions. If those versions do not exist, the current values cannot be backfilled.
- Employee experience requires an as-of-event roster snapshot.
- Future decisions and sequence patterns are never usable as realized `t0` features, even when their process behavior is legitimate.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **P0 — Quarantine future-derived features and implement a bitemporal feature store.**
   - Store, for every feature version, its event/effective time, computation time, delivery/availability time, valid interval, source event IDs, transformation version, and as-of prediction ID.
   - Preserve mutable history for risk scores, assignments, resources, and policy states. A timestamp saying when the current row was created is not enough.
   - Separate immutable events from mutable warehouse state. An assignment made at `t0` must be recorded separately from the team populated at closure.
   - Reconstruct queue, document, and employee-history features using point-in-time joins. Use the latest permitted snapshot at or before `t0`, never a later snapshot for convenience.
   - Fix the prior-claim SQL and version it. A present-day snapshot must never be copied backward to manufacture historical availability.
   - Where historical assignment evidence does not exist, retire the feature rather than estimate historical availability from correlation.

2. **P0 — Rebuild the model from prefix-only examples and recalibrate after removal.**
   - Generate one explicit coverage-time feature vector for every training and inference claim using the feature-store versions available on that date.
   - Remove closure fields, realized future paths, eventual appeals, document totals, downstream decisions, and unversioned mutable state.
   - Re-estimate both global importance and local explanations; do not preserve the old SHAP table or calculate a new probability by simply subtracting leaked terms.
   - Use chronological, outcome-mature training and validation splits. Ensure preprocessing, missing-value handling, background populations, and feature selection are fitted without later information.
   - Compare the repaired model with an admissible simple baseline. Report calibration curves, Brier score, log loss, discrimination, threshold-dependent utility, coverage, and abstention rate. Recalibrate on a separate temporal validation set after feature removal.
   - Treat fairness and operational costs as additional evaluation criteria, not merely secondary metrics.

3. **P0 — Make process conformance an executable data contract.**
   - Encode the branch, evidence-cycle, conditional fraud, appeal, repeated-decision, and closure rules as declarative temporal constraints.
   - Test valid synthetic traces and known invalid traces at ingestion, feature computation, model scoring, and explanation publication.
   - Quarantine uncertifiable tied event orders rather than inventing a sequence. Distinguish data-quality uncertainty from a proven process violation.
   - Preserve repeated settlement events as distinct events when appeal semantics are valid; do not deduplicate them solely by coarse activity label.
   - Version the process definition. Changes to appeal handling, evidence cycles, or event labels require conformance and model-impact review.

4. **P1 — Require provenance and stability in explanation objects.**
   - Every local explanation should identify the model version, exact `t0`, feature-store snapshot, source event IDs, output scale, baseline, and computation method.
   - Compute local explanations from the same admissible prefix as the prediction, with a background distribution drawn from comparable prefix-time claims.
   - Validate explanation fidelity through prefix-respecting feature-deletion tests and controlled synthetic changes. A negative document or queue explanation should disappear when the relevant observed state is removed, without consulting later events.
   - Test stability under small timestamp jitter, permitted snapshot staleness, equivalent feature encoding, missingness, and unresolved tie handling.
   - Correlated features can redistribute SHAP values without changing predictions. Report grouped or robust attributions where appropriate and do not overinterpret small rank changes.
   - Use terms such as “feature association” or “predictive contribution” by default. Reserve “driver,” “cause,” and “intervention” for separately supported analyses.

5. **P1 — Conduct proxy, fairness, robustness, and actionability reviews.**
   - Examine region, verified channel, prior history, and complexity assessment individually and in relevant intersections.
   - Compare selection rates, false-positive/false-negative patterns, calibration, missingness, and service-access consequences. Review whether the target and its measurement systematically disadvantage any group.
   - Compare models with and without the proxy, alternative representations, and fairness-constrained models. A performance benefit can justify retention only after governance review; it does not itself settle fairness questions.
   - Test robustness to regional case-mix shifts, missing snapshots, changing network coverage, and new process branches.
   - Do not equate an actionable explanation with changing the encoded feature. For congestion-based interventions, evaluate prospective staffing or scheduling policies with appropriate experimental or quasi-experimental evidence and operational safeguards.

6. **P1 — Monitor leakage, process drift, and explanation drift after deployment.**
   - Alert on source events or feature versions later than `t0`, late-arriving data, missing sequence indices, mutable values without history, changed snapshot cutoffs, and new extraction-time joins.
   - Monitor schema and event-label changes, tie frequency, branch frequencies, trigger availability, appeal/reconsideration rates, decision types, and closure conformance. Use mature full traces for offline conformance monitoring, never as new early-model inputs.
   - Track feature availability, freshness, missingness, distributions, and queue/document snapshot lag. Distinguish seasonal variation from structural process change.
   - Monitor calibration and performance only after the target horizon has matured, including subgroup and intersectional results.
   - Track changes in global ranks and local signs with their lineage and model version. A large shift may reflect new data, a schema change, correlation redistribution, or leakage.
   - Establish an incident process that disables affected explanations or predictions when a timestamp, lineage, or process-conformance check fails.
