# 2. Benign alternative explanations
**Pattern A: Buyer_Carla  Approver_Gita, 118 co-cases, 11 min Emergency Override  Approve PO, 88% emergency_flag, subcontracting loop 36.**

- Benign 1 – Emergency handling / formal specialization: Carla specializes in emergency medical supplies, Gita is delegated urgent approver. Fast time and repetition reflect rush channel design and weekend/urgent staffing.
  - Supports: emergency_flag=True, purchase_category=emergency medical, priority field, on-call rosters showing Gita rostered, low vendor concentration, low manual share (11%).
  - Refutes: absence of emergency documentation, non-urgent categories / low amounts approved fast, approvals outside Gita delegation authority, approver not actually on duty per access logs.
- Benign 2 – Data-quality / shared account: 23 approvals logged to shared Approver_Gita while entered_by shows assistants; speed reflects logging delay rather than review speed.
  - Supports: mismatch org:resource vs entered_by / approved_by, identical timestamps, assistant access logs.
  - Refutes: detailed audit trail confirms Gita herself acted quickly with independent review notes; entered_by = Gita.

**Pattern B: Buyer_Dan  Manager_Hugo, 96 co-cases, 14 min, 79% emergency_flag, facilities weekend cases.**

- Benign 1 – Emergency handling: Facilities repairs require immediate managerial override on weekends when Team A/B approvers unavailable.
  - Supports: timestamps on weekends, region/facility tickets, Manager_Hugo override authority in policy, low vendor concentration.
  - Refutes: weekday routine cases using same fast path, no repair ticket, policy does not authorize Hugo for PO approval.
- Benign 2 – Informal specialization / workload: Dan–Hugo dyad emerges from shift overlap, not preference; fast median reflects low queue, not skipped checks.
  - Supports: shift schedules overlapping, service/waiting-time decomposition showing waiting time short but service time normal, similar speed for other weekend approvers.
  - Refutes: selective routing to Hugo when Elena/Felix available, or systematic avoidance of standard approvers.

**Pattern C: VendorMaster_Lena  AP_Kim, 44 co-cases, 19 min median, Bank Details  Payment Approval 17 min, 72% V-77x, 68% high amount, 83% manual_upload.**

- Benign 1 – Legitimate mass maintenance: V-77x banking-platform change required bulk bank updates; linkage ticket missing in log creates false appearance of case-level change-to-payment.
  - Supports: maintenance ticket covering V-77x with oldnew account mapping, bank confirmation letters, identical new account across cases, change timestamps clustered by migration plan not by payment due dates.
  - Refutes: different new accounts per vendor, changes timed just before individual payments, no migration ticket, beneficiary not matching vendor master.
- Benign 2 – Informal specialization by risk: Kim handles higher-risk suppliers (per workload notes), Lena handles bank changes; pairing reflects queue assignment, and manual_upload reflects migration source, not intentional bypass.
  - Supports: assignment rules routing V-77x / high-risk to Lena/Kim, manual_upload expected for migration, AP_Iris comparator shows similar but slower pattern for Marc.
  - Refutes: routing rules do not assign this way, manual_upload used selectively for high-amount cases, lack of independent verification of bank letters.

**Pattern D: 17 same-person Emergency Override + Retrospective Justification, 9 min median, 12 batch-entered after quarter close; Temp_Nina  Buyer_Carla 65 cases at 4 min.**

- Benign 1 – Administrative backlog / data migration: Justifications done on time on paper but batch-entered from spreadsheet via quarter_close_upload with identical timestamp; Temp_Nina end-of-quarter support created entry lag.
  - Supports: spreadsheet with original dates 3 working days, email/paper justifications predating upload, source_system=quarter_close_upload, entered_by vs org:resource distinction.
  - Refutes: no contemporaneous justification document, original dates also late (>3 working days), late share 18-21% even excluding batch.
- Benign 2 – Role misunderstanding: Staff believed same manager could justify own override in emergencies; reflects training / control-design gap.
  - Supports: policy ambiguity, similar error across Hugo and Gita, no concealment (logged under own ID).
  - Refutes: training records show clear different-person requirement and prior compliance.

**Pattern E: Routine high-frequency pairs Buyer_Aline  Approver_Elena (510, 2.4h) and Buyer_Boris  Approver_Felix (495, 2.6h).**

- These are not treated as suspicious. Benign explanations are workload balancing (Team A vs Team B) and informal specialization in low/medium amounts.
  - Supports: 4-5% emergency, 5-6% manual, 1% late justification, median near global 2.1h, distinct teams.
  - Would become concerning only with vendor concentration, manual bypass, or systematic policy exception – none observed.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording
Internal Audit reviewed last quarter procurement-to-payment activity (approx X cases) to assess segregation-of-duties and emergency-handling controls. We identified control exceptions, not findings of misconduct. The rate of Create Purchase Order–Approve Purchase Order exceptions was 1.8% (42 cases), concentrated in emergency-flagged medical-supply and facilities-repair channels, and Change Vendor Bank Details–Payment Approval exceptions was 0.4% (9 cases). Retrospective Justification was late or completed by the same person in a small subset of emergency overrides, including a batch upload after quarter close that limits reliance on timestamps.

The most concerning subgroup for targeted follow-up is the 44-case cluster involving Change Vendor Bank Details followed rapidly by Payment Approval (median 17 min vs 7.8h globally), predominantly high-amount, manual_upload cases concentrated in vendor family V-77x. The pattern does not establish improper conduct; a recent banking-platform migration may explain legitimate mass maintenance, but missing ticket linkages, manual entry, and concentration warrant independent verification of beneficiary details, supporting documents, and system access logs.

Emergency-related fast approvals (e.g., Buyer_Carla–Approver_Gita, Buyer_Dan–Manager_Hugo) are broadly consistent with documented rush channels and weekend work, though late justifications should be remediated. High-volume routine pairs (e.g., Buyer_Aline–Approver_Elena) reflect informal specialization, not in themselves a concern. Data-quality limitations – shared use of Approver_Gita, legacy_erp bot-human mapping errors, and spreadsheet migration – prevent definitive conclusions. We recommend risk-based case review, master-data reconciliation, and clarification of emergency and role-design rules before broader conclusions.
