## 1. Decision rule
To determine intervention eligibility and priority, we move beyond raw `p_now` and use a Net Expected Value (Net EV) framework that accounts for calibration, stage, and queue externalities (spillovers).

1. **Eligibility:** The case must be in `Triage` or `Diagnosis Started`. Cases at `Diagnosis Complete` or later are strictly ineligible.
2. **Calibration Adjustment (`p_adj`):**
   - SME: `p_adj = p_now + 0.065` (correcting underprediction).
   - Strategic Enterprise: `p_adj = p_now - 0.085` (correcting overprediction).
   - Public Sector & Non-Native: `p_adj = p_now` (use raw, but note uncertainty).
3. **Alert Persistence:** Repeated alerts are suppressed unless a materially new event occurs (e.g., new logs) or `p_adj` jumps by >0.15 for non-imputed reasons.
4. **Prioritization (Net EV):**
   - `P_FalseAlarm = 1 - p_adj`
   - `Expected Direct Benefit (EDB) = (Mid_Effect × Value) - €450 - (P_FalseAlarm × FA_Burden)`
   - `Expected Spillover Cost (ESC) = 6 cases × 0.02 (mid_spillover) × €20,000 (avg portfolio value) ≈ €2,400 per slot`
   - **Rule:** Rank cases by `Net EV = EDB - ESC`. Intervene if Net EV > 0. For cases with high Net EV but >6 hours to SLA, consider waiting to preserve optionality, unless the early swarm provides outsized leverage.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy
- **Suppression vs. Re-entry (I-05 vs I-07):** I-05 is suppressed because its alert is driven purely by the model's 30-minute refresh cycle without new underlying events. I-07, despite a lower `p_now`, has a new customer diagnostic log; this is a materially new event that breaks suppression and forces a re-evaluation (though it ultimately fails the EV spillover hurdle).
- **Cool-down:** Cases evaluated and waitlisted (e.g., I-08) enter a 2-hour cool-down. They will not trigger a new alert unless `p_adj` increases by >0.15 or a new event occurs.
- **Escalation:** If a waitlisted case approaches within 4 hours of its SLA and `p_adj` remains high, it escalates past the cool-down for immediate re-evaluation.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks
- **Value-based prioritization starving lower-value cases:** Pure EV heavily favors Strategic Enterprise over SME/Public Sector. *Safeguard:* Implement a "value cap" in the EV formula or allocate a dedicated daily slot specifically for SME/Public Sector to ensure baseline service quality.
- **Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support:** Small sample sizes cause high uncertainty. *Safeguard:* Use an explicit exploration bonus (e.g., Upper Confidence Bound) for this segment to gather data, which justifies selecting I-09 today despite uncertainty.
- **Differential calibration across segments:** Strat Ent overpredicts, SME underpredicts. *Safeguard:* Explicitly apply segment-level calibration adjustments (as done in Section 1) before calculating EV to prevent Strat Ent from unfairly monopolizing capacity.
- **Selective intervention changing future training labels:** Swarming reduces breach rates, creating a "self-defeating prophecy" where the model learns to underpredict risk for similar cases. *Safeguard:* Log all interventions and use inverse probability weighting (IPW) or train a causal uplift model during retraining.
- **Spillover harms systematically falling on untreated groups:** Strat Ent swarms may cause 8-20 min delays for the general diagnosis queue (often SMEs). *Safeguard:* Monitor queue composition. If SMEs absorb >60% of spillover delay, dynamically increase the Spillover Cost penalty for Strat Ent cases.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan
- **Operational & Capacity Monitoring:** Track daily slot utilization, FA rates, and average delay added to the diagnosis queue. If congestion exceeds 20 mins per slot, halt discretionary swarms.
- **Predictive Monitoring:** Track Brier score and calibration curves weekly, stratified by segment, to detect if the SME underprediction or Strat Ent overprediction worsens.
- **Causal-Effect Monitoring:** We cannot observe the counterfactual directly. *Plan:* Implement a randomized holdout (e.g., 10% of eligible high-EV cases are randomly denied the swarm) to measure the true Average Treatment Effect on the Treated (ATT) and validate the supplied effect ranges.
- **Detecting Policy-Induced Drift:** Compare breach rates of cases just above the intervention threshold vs. just below. If threshold cases have lower breach rates, the intervention is working but labels are drifting.
- **Recalibration Triggers:** Update the EV parameters if the measured ATT falls below 0.10 for a segment, or if average diagnosis queue delay exceeds 15 minutes per slot. Remove the exploration bonus for Non-Native Support once confidence intervals tighten.
