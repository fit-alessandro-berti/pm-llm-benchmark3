## 1. Temporal Data Semantics

**State reconstruction.** For each case, events are ordered by the key `(time:timestamp, event_index)`. For event `e`, the state of attribute `a` is the value from the latest non-blank update `u` with `u  e` (inclusive). Updates after `e` are never visible at `e`.

**Blank cells.** A blank cell means "no update at this event." It never means null, zero, or low risk. A value is UNKNOWN only if no prior non-blank update exists. TX-305's amount is UNKNOWN at release because no amount was ever recorded before release, not because it was recorded as zero.

**Equal timestamps.** Ties are broken by `event_index`. Within TX-311 at 16:20, Enhanced_Review (index 3) precedes the jurisdiction change (index 4). The consequence is that the review was evaluated under US and the release is evaluated under DE.

**Material changes.** Three kinds of change are material:
- a change in jurisdiction, risk level, or customer type, since each can alter a trigger;
- a crossing of the EUR 100,000 threshold in either direction;
- an amount increase whose cumulative size, measured against the amount at the most recent review ("basis"), exceeds 20%. This uses the peak amount since the review, so several small increases cannot evade R4.

A decrease that does not cross the threshold is not material (R4). A 4.5% increase that does not cross the threshold is not material either.

**Final attributes.** Final case attributes are never copied backward. Forward-fill is restricted to within a case and moves forward in time only.

**Four time kinds, kept separate:**
- **Event time** is the `time:timestamp` of the log row.
- **Effective time** is when an attribute change or exemption takes legal effect. For updates this equals event time unless evidence says otherwise. For exemptions it is `effective_from`.
- **Record time** is when a fact was written to a system, such as Record_Exemption. It is evidence of later entry, not of earlier approval.
- **Evidence time** is the earliest time a supporting artifact could exist, such as a signing certificate creation time, a signature timestamp, or a settlement message. Evidence time bounds what can be inferred about earlier states.

**Currency.** The log does not state a currency. I assume that amounts are EUR-equivalent at the time of the event. If amounts are in another currency, R1's threshold must be converted at a stated FX rule.

**Post-release evidence.** Settlement or other later rows are evidence about later states. They do not overwrite the release-time state. They can only inform what the release-time state probably was, with an explicit caveat.

## 2. Normalized Rule Table

| Rule | Trigger event and condition | Required prior/subsequent event | Ordering constraint | State snapshot used | Exception | Missing-data treatment | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Release_Funds where J  DE and amount  100,000 | Enhanced_Review, or a valid R1-waiving exemption | ER  Release, and no material change m with ER  m  Release | J and amount at Release; last material change; peak amount since ER | Valid exemption under R5 | If J or amount is unknown and the other predicate does not already decide the trigger: INDETERMINATE | ER record with time; timestamped change history; amount basis at ER |
| R2 | Release_Funds where risk = HIGH or customer = PEP | Enhanced_Review and Compliance_Approve | ER  CA  Release | risk and customer type at Release; latest ER and CA | None in policy text | Unknown risk and non-PEP or unknown customer: INDETERMINATE | ER and CA records; approver role; what the CA reviewed |
| R3 | Release_Funds where J was added to the list after the latest screen | New Sanctions_Screen after listing and before release | Screen  Listing  Screen  Release | J at Release; listing time; latest screen | None in policy text | Unknown J: INDETERMINATE | List publication timestamp; screen record and time |
| R4 | Amount increase after a review | Review invalidated for amount-dependent obligations | Review  increase (and any later reliance on the review) | Basis at review; peak amount since review | Decreases do not invalidate; a 20% increase is not itself a trigger | Unknown basis or unknown amount: "not classifiable," flagged | Amount history with timestamps and provenance |
| R5 | Exemption relied on for trigger t | Approval existed as of t | approval-evidence  t; effective_from  t < expires; scope matches | Exemption register at t; evidence times | None | Unknown scope or unknown evidence time: not valid | Qualified approval record or signature with time  t; scope fields |
| R6 | Any decisive input unknown at its trigger | Not applicable | Not applicable | Attribute state at the event | Resolved only by timestamped evidence | Never default to low risk; classify INDETERMINATE | Evidence of the value and its effective time |
| R7 | All evaluations | Not applicable | Only updates u  e are visible at e | Per-event snapshot | Later evidence does not rewrite the decision | Not applicable | Stable event order and index |

**Pseudo-logic.** Notation: `e  f` means `(t(e), idx(e)) < (t(f), idx(f))` lexicographically. `S(c, e)` is the state at event `e`.

- **R1.** `Trig1(r)  Release(r)  J(S(c,r))  DE  Amt(S(c,r))  100000`. `Sat1(r)  ER: ER  r  ¬m: Mat(m)  ER  m  r  ¬Invalid(ER, r)`, or `x: Valid_R1(x, r)`. Here `Invalid(ER, r)  peak(ER, r) > 1.2 × basis(ER)`. Unknown J or Amt gives `Trig1 = `, which is INDETERMINATE.
- **R2.** `Trig2(r)  Release(r)  (Risk(S(c,r)) = HIGH  CT(S(c,r)) = PEP)`. `Sat2(r)  ER CA: ER  CA  r  Role(CA) = Compliance`. A Standard_Review is not an ER, and a CA that reviewed only a standard packet does not satisfy the ER requirement.
- **R3.** `Trig3(r)  Release(r)  J(S(c,r))  L  L_time(J)  lastScreen(c, r)`, where `L` is the set of listings. `Sat3(r)  SS: L_time(J)  SS  r`.
- **R4.** `Inv(v, f)  v  {Std, ER}  v  f  max{Amt(u): v  u  f} > 1.2 × Amt(v)`. A decrease does not satisfy this condition. Unknown basis gives "not classifiable."
- **R5.** `Valid(x, t)  Approver(x)=Head_Compliance  eff(x)  t  t < exp(x)  scope(x) matches (J, customer type, amount)   evidence e_x: time(e_x)  t`. Displayed fields alone do not satisfy the last conjunct. A record written later can corroborate an earlier approval, but it cannot establish it alone.
- **R6.** `Unknown(a, t)  Decisive(a, rule, t)  ¬Resolved(a, t)  INDETERMINATE(rule, t)`.
- **R7.** ` attribute a, S(c, e)[a] = val(u*)` where `u* = argmax{u : u  e  a(u) non-blank}`. No atom in `S(c, e)` may reference an update `u` with `u  e`.

## 4. Ambiguities and Alternative Readings

**TX-305 (INDETERMINATE).** The amount at release is unknown. R6 forbids defaulting it to a low value. The settlement amount of 150,000 is post-release evidence and is not a release payload.
- If the release-time amount is shown to be below 100,000, or J is DE, the result becomes COMPLIANT. R1 would not be triggered.
- If the release-time amount is shown to be at least 100,000, the outcome depends on whether the ER at 11:15 covered that amount. The review document has no amount, so coverage is unproven. If coverage is shown, and no increase occurred after the ER, the result becomes COMPLIANT. If the amount rose after the ER, the result becomes NONCOMPLIANT.
- If the settlement amount is evidence that the release amount was 150,000 and that the change occurred before 11:15, the result is COMPLIANT on the basis of a covering ER. A post-release settlement cannot establish the release-time amount without a release payload or bank authorization record.

**TX-302 (COMPLIANT).** The result rests on the 09:20 Enhanced_Review. Two alternatives follow.
- If the 09:20 review is in fact only a standard review, R1 is unsatisfied and the exemption must carry the case. EX-77 is supported by a qualified signature at 09:05 and is within scope and time. The result would then be JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.
- If the 11:00 DB insert were treated as the approval time, EX-77 would fail R5 because the approval would postdate release. That reading is not supported: the qualified signature at 09:05 and the documented outage establish the earlier time. The reading would matter only if the review were also non-qualifying.

**TX-310 (NONCOMPLIANT, provisional).** This case has contradictory evidence and I classify it as NONCOMPLIANT because R5 places the burden on the exemption and the strongest artifact postdates release. It would change as follows.
- To JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION: an independent, immutable approval-workflow log showing Head_Compliance approval at or before 10:00. Alternatively, an original document with a hash and timestamp that predates release, with the 10:30 certificate showing only a later re-signing.
- To a stronger NONCOMPLIANT: evidence that the 08:50 displayed date was created by a template or backdating, or that the oral approval was not from Head_Compliance.
- If the oral approval is treated as Head_Compliance approval, R5 still requires evidence of approval that existed at release. A witness who is the requester does not meet that standard without corroboration.

**TX-311 (COMPLIANT, provisional).** The result depends on whether the DE value is a correct reclassification or a correction of an earlier error.
- If DE was correct from creation (an earlier data error was later corrected), R1 is not triggered at release, and the result is COMPLIANT.
- If the change to DE is a reclassification effective at 16:20, the release state is DE. R1 is not triggered at release. The result is COMPLIANT. The review at index 3 was under US and does not need to satisfy R1, since R1 does not apply.
- If the change to DE was an erroneous correction and the true jurisdiction is US, then R1 applies at release. The 16:20 index-4 change is material and follows the review at index 3, so the review no longer satisfies R1. The result would be NONCOMPLIANT.
- The control weakness is real regardless: the same operator changed the jurisdiction in the same minute as the review, and no approval record exists for the change.

**Other cases with alternative readings.**
- **TX-306.** If "material data change" in R1 means any amount change, the 4.5% increase at 12:30 invalidates the ER for R1, and the result becomes NONCOMPLIANT. R2 would still be satisfied. The stated R4 threshold and the text "material" support the reading used here, but a stricter bank policy could adopt the broader reading.
- **TX-308.** If a decrease is treated as a change of basis, a re-review would be needed, and the result becomes NONCOMPLIANT. R4 explicitly says a decrease does not by itself invalidate a review, so the reading used here is COMPLIANT. The exact 100,000 boundary matters: at 99,999 R1 would not apply.
- **TX-307.** If KYC later clears the PEP match and the customer type reverts, the state at release would change. No such evidence exists, so the state at release is PEP.
- **TX-309.** If the 15:28 increase is a typo correction, the amount history still records the increase, and it would require documentary evidence to override. Without that evidence, the result stands.

CASE, ACT, TS, IDX = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp", "event_index"
ATTRS = ["amount", "risk_level", "jurisdiction", "customer_type"]
THRESH = 100_000.0
INC_LIMIT = 0.20

# Exemption register as data, not as a verdict. Times are UTC.
# evidence_time = earliest time the approval artifact could exist (e.g., signature/certificate time).
# record_time = when the database record was written (corroboration only).

def load_log():
    """Parse the CSV from memory, keep event_index, and optionally run pm4py formatting."""
    df = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(RAW_CSV), dtype=str)   # blank cells become NaN (= no update)
    df[IDX] = df[IDX].astype(int)
    df[TS] = pd.to_datetime(df[TS], utc=True)
    df["amount"] = pd.to_numeric(df["amount"])
    try:
        import pm4py
        df = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS)
    except Exception as exc:  # pm4py is optional
        print(f"[info] pm4py formatting skipped ({type(exc).__name__}); pandas path used")
    # re-impose types and order regardless of pm4py behaviour
    df[IDX] = df[IDX].astype(int)
    df["amount"] = pd.to_numeric(df["amount"])
    df[TS] = pd.to_datetime(df[TS], utc=True)
    return df


def add_states(df):
    """Forward-fill changing attributes within each case. Never backfill, never cross cases."""
    df = df.sort_values([CASE, TS, IDX], kind="mergesort").reset_index(drop=True)
    grp = df.groupby(CASE, sort=False)
    for a in ATTRS:
        df[a + "_state"] = grp[a].ffill()
    return df


def exemption_status(ex_id, t, jur, ctype, amt):
    """R5 check at trigger time t. Returns a status string, never silently 'valid' without evidence."""
    if ex_id is None:
        return "NONE"
    ex = REGISTER.get(ex_id)
    if ex is None:
        return "UNREGISTERED"
    if ex["approved_by"] != "Head_Compliance":
        return "NOT_HEAD_APPROVED"
    if not (isinstance(jur, str) and isinstance(ctype, str) and not pd.isna(amt)):
        return "SCOPE_UNKNOWN"
    if jur != ex["jurisdiction"] or ctype != ex["customer_type"] or amt > ex["amount_max"]:
        return "OUT_OF_SCOPE"
    if ex["effective_from"] > t:
        return "NOT_YET_EFFECTIVE"
    if t >= ex["expires"]:
        return "EXPIRED"
    if ex["evidence_time"] > t:
        return "NOT_EVIDENCED_AT_TRIGGER"
    return "VALID"


def tri_r1(J, AMT):
    if isinstance(J, str) and J == "DE":
        return False
    if not pd.isna(AMT) and AMT < THRESH:
        return False
    if isinstance(J, str) and not pd.isna(AMT):
        return True            # J known non-DE and amount >= threshold
    return None                # INDETERMINATE (R6)


def tri_r2(RK, CT):
    if RK == "HIGH" or CT == "PEP":
        return True
    if isinstance(RK, str) and isinstance(CT, str):
        return False
    return None


def tri_r3(J, listed, screen_key):
    if not isinstance(J, str):
        return None
    if J not in listed:
        return False
    return screen_key is None or listed[J] > screen_key


def invalid_flag(rv):
    """R4: cumulative increase of the peak amount since review, relative to the review basis."""
    if rv is None:
        return "NO_REVIEW"
    if pd.isna(rv["basis"]):
        return "UNKNOWN_BASIS"
    return bool(rv["peak"] > (1 + INC_LIMIT) * rv["basis"])


def diagnose(df):
    rows = []
    for cid, g in df.groupby(CASE, sort=False):
        g = g.sort_values([TS, IDX], kind="mergesort")
        std = enh = None
        ca_key = screen_key = last_material = last_jur = None
        listed = {}
        prev = None
        for _, r in g.iterrows():
            k = (r[TS], int(r[IDX]))
            cur = {a: r[a + "_state"] for a in ATTRS}

            # --- material-change detection against the previous state (R7: prior state only)
            if prev is not None:
                changed = any(
                    cur[a] != prev[a] and not (pd.isna(cur[a]) and pd.isna(prev[a]))
                    for a in ("risk_level", "customer_type", "jurisdiction")
                )
                pa, ca_amt = prev["amount"], cur["amount"]
                crossed = (not pd.isna(pa) and not pd.isna(ca_amt)
                           and ((ca_amt >= THRESH) != (pa >= THRESH)))
                if changed or crossed:
                    last_material = k
                if cur["jurisdiction"] != prev["jurisdiction"]:
                    last_jur = k

            # --- peak amount since each review (for R4)
            if not pd.isna(cur["amount"]):
                for rv in (std, enh):
                    if rv is not None:
                        rv["peak"] = cur["amount"] if pd.isna(rv["peak"]) else max(rv["peak"], cur["amount"])

            act = r[ACT]
            if act == "Standard_Review":
                std = {"key": k, "basis": cur["amount"], "peak": cur["amount"]}
            elif act == "Enhanced_Review":
                enh = {"key": k, "basis": cur["amount"], "peak": cur["amount"]}
            elif act == "Compliance_Approve":
                ca_key = k
            elif act == "Sanctions_Screen":
                screen_key = k
            elif act == "Sanctions_List_Update" and isinstance(r["jurisdiction"], str):
                listed[r["jurisdiction"]] = k
            elif act == "Release_Funds":
                J, CT, RK, AMT = cur["jurisdiction"], cur["customer_type"], cur["risk_level"], cur["amount"]
                ex_id = r["exemption_id"] if isinstance(r["exemption_id"], str) else None
                t_rel = r[TS]

                t1, t2, t3 = tri_r1(J, AMT), tri_r2(RK, CT), tri_r3(J, listed, screen_key)

                enh_fresh = (enh is not None and enh["key"] < k
                             and (last_material is None or last_material < enh["key"])
                             and not pd.isna(enh["basis"])
                             and enh["peak"] <= (1 + INC_LIMIT) * enh["basis"])
                ex_status = exemption_status(ex_id, t_rel, J, CT, AMT)
                r1_sat = enh_fresh or ex_status == "VALID"

                r2_sat = enh is not None and ca_key is not None and ca_key > enh["key"]
                r3_sat = (t3 is not True) or (screen_key is not None and screen_key > listed[J])

                devs = []
                if t1 is True and not r1_sat: devs.append("R1")
                if t2 is True and not r2_sat: devs.append("R2")
                if t3 is True and not r3_sat: devs.append("R3")
                unk = [n for n, t in (("R1", t1), ("R2", t2), ("R3", t3)) if t is None]

                jur_after_enh = enh is not None and last_jur is not None and last_jur > enh["key"]
                r4_std, r4_enh = invalid_flag(std), invalid_flag(enh)
                extra = []
                if jur_after_enh: extra.append("jurisdiction_changed_after_ER")
                if r4_enh == "UNKNOWN_BASIS": extra.append("ER_amount_basis_unknown")

                after = g[(g[TS] > t_rel) | ((g[TS] == t_rel) & (g[IDX] > r[IDX]))]
                later_amt_rows = int(after["amount"].notna().sum())

                if devs:
                    status = "DEVIATION_FLAG"
                elif unk or extra:
                    status = "REVIEW_REQUIRED"
                else:
                    status = "CLEAR_BY_CODE"
                contested = ex_id is not None and ex_status != "VALID"
                needs_human = bool(unk or extra or (devs and contested))

                rows.append(dict(
                    case=cid,
                    release_index=int(r[IDX]),
                    release_time=t_rel,
                    amount_at_release=AMT,
                    jurisdiction=J,
                    customer_type=CT,
                    risk_level=RK,
                    unknown_amount=bool(pd.isna(AMT)),
                    unknown_jurisdiction=not isinstance(J, str),
                    unknown_risk=not isinstance(RK, str),
                    unknown_customer_type=not isinstance(CT, str),
                    R1_trigger=t1, R1_satisfied=r1_sat,
                    R2_trigger=t2, R2_satisfied=r2_sat,
                    R3_trigger=t3, R3_satisfied=r3_sat,
                    R4_std_invalidated=r4_std,
                    R4_enh_invalidated=r4_enh,
                    last_standard_review=std["key"][0] if std else None,
                    last_enhanced_review=enh["key"][0] if enh else None,
                    last_compliance_approve=ca_key[0] if ca_key else None,
                    last_screen=screen_key[0] if screen_key else None,
                    list_update=listed[J][0] if isinstance(J, str) and J in listed else None,
                    last_material_change=last_material[0] if last_material else None,
                    exemption_id=ex_id,
                    exemption_status=ex_status,
                    exemption_record_lag_min=(
                        (REGISTER[ex_id]["record_time"] - t_rel).total_seconds() / 60
                        if ex_id in REGISTER else None),
                    later_amount_evidence_rows=later_amt_rows,
                    deviations=devs,
                    unknown_triggers=unk,
                    extra_flags=extra,
                    status=status,
                    needs_human_review=needs_human,
                ))
            prev = cur
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)


def coarse_precedence(df):
    """Unconditional control-flow check: every Release_Funds preceded by some Enhanced_Review.
    It ignores data conditions, so it both over- and under-detects (see Section 6)."""
    out = {}
    for cid, g in df.groupby(CASE, sort=False):
        acts = g.sort_values([TS, IDX], kind="mergesort")[ACT].tolist()
        enh_pos = [i for i, a in enumerate(acts) if a == "Enhanced_Review"]
        rel_pos = [i for i, a in enumerate(acts) if a == "Release_Funds"]
        out[cid] = all(any(e < r for e in enh_pos) for r in rel_pos)
    return pd.Series(out, name="unconditional_ER_precedes_release")

**Explanation of the code's design.**
- Steps 1–2: the CSV is read from `io.StringIO`, timestamps are parsed as UTC, and `event_index` is preserved. `pm4py.format_dataframe` is called if available, and types are re-imposed afterward.
- Step 3: changing attributes are forward-filled within each case only. Exemption and note columns are not filled.
- Step 4: each Release_Funds row is evaluated against the state up to that row, in `(time, event_index)` order.
- Step 5: the code tracks the latest Standard_Review, Enhanced_Review, Compliance_Approve, Sanctions_Screen, and listing, along with the most recent material change and peak amount since each review.
- Step 6: coarse R1–R3 flags are computed, and R4 uses the peak-since-review rule. The exemption status is computed by checking the register at trigger time.
- Step 7: the diagnostics frame includes evidence timestamps, unknown-value flags, exemption record lag, and post-release amount evidence.
- Step 8: the coarse precedence check is an unconditional control-flow test. It is not a faithful model of R1–R7; its purpose is to show the failure modes.
- The code labels cases `DEVIATION_FLAG`, `REVIEW_REQUIRED`, or `CLEAR_BY_CODE`. It does not assign the narrative classification. For example, TX-311 is flagged for review, and TX-310 is flagged as a deviation with a contested exemption, which requires human judgment about the approval evidence.

---

## 6. Why Control-Flow-Only Conformance Is Insufficient

1. **Final-state leakage.** A conformance check on the final attributes would judge TX-311 by DE and TX-308 by a final amount of 100,000 and ignore the state at release. Here, correct evaluation requires the release-time state. In TX-311 the final DE value is exactly the value that suppresses R1, which shows why reading the final state is dangerous.

2. **Post-hoc exemptions.** TX-302's exemption record appears at 11:00, after release at 09:40. TX-310's record appears at 11:00, and its certificate is dated 10:30, after release at 10:00. A control-flow check sees "Record_Exemption exists" and passes. Only the evidence time, compared to the trigger, shows that the exemption was not established at release.

3. **Material changes after review.** TX-309 contains Enhanced_Review at 15:25 followed by an increase of 22.9% at 15:28. The trace order is correct, but the review is invalidated under R4. TX-301's Standard_Review is similarly invalidated by a later increase. Control-flow shows only that the activities occurred.

4. **Conditional activation.** R1 and R2 are triggered only by data conditions. In TX-304, control-flow sees "screen then release" and passes, but the listing at 08:30 activates R3 and the screen is too early. In TX-308, the amount is exactly at the boundary. A trace does not show whether the obligation was active.

5. **Missing data.** TX-305 has no amount at release. A control-flow check sees "Enhanced_Review then Release" and passes. Only an explicit unknown-value rule shows that R1 cannot be decided, and that the settlement amount is not a release payload.

6. **Same-timestamp ordering.** In TX-311, Enhanced_Review and Update_Jurisdiction share 16:20. A timestamp-only sort would order them ambiguously. The `event_index` tie-breaker determines that the review preceded the change. The consequence depends on whether the change is a reclassification or a correction.

7. **Semantic type confusion.** TX-307 has a Compliance_Approve and a Standard_Review, and a control-flow check that looks for "approval before release" passes. The R2 obligation requires an Enhanced_Review specifically. TX-306 has a 4.5% increase that a naive "any update after review" check would flag, while the correct materiality test passes it.

8. **Sanctions-list temporal ordering.** R3 depends on the time of a list update relative to the latest screen. Control-flow can encode "screen before release" but cannot encode "screen before listing and no rescreen after listing" without the timestamps of the listing and the screen.

9. **Evidence quality and attribution.** TX-310's oral witness is the requester. Control-flow does not represent who gave evidence or whether it is independent.

10. **Unobservable release payloads.** Without an immutable release payload, the amount at release cannot be verified, so any amount-dependent obligation is at risk of being judged on later data.

---

## 7. Recommended Logging and Control Improvements

- **Immutable release payloads.** Record the complete payload at Release_Funds, including amount, currency, jurisdiction, customer type, risk level, and exemption ID, along with a payload hash. This resolves TX-305 and prevents state reconstruction from relying on the final record.

- **Separate event, effective, record, and evidence timestamps.** For each attribute change, record the event time, the effective time (if different), the record time, and the evidence time, for example the source system's commit time.

- **Exemption lifecycle events.** Log Exemption_Requested, Exemption_Approved (with an approver ID and a qualified signature timestamp), and Exemption_Recorded as separate events. Record the scope (jurisdiction, customer type, amount ceiling) and the expiry explicitly. Block release that references an exemption until the approval event has a signature timestamp at or before the release event. This would prevent TX-310 and would make TX-302 unambiguous.

- **Rule version.** Record the policy version active at each event, so that a later rule change cannot be applied retroactively.

- **Change provenance.** Require a reason code and an approver for each jurisdiction, risk, or customer-type change, and a separate approval for amount changes above a configured threshold. Record who made each change. In TX-311, the same operator changed the jurisdiction in the same minute as the review, which would have been flagged.

- **Materiality thresholds as explicit attributes.** Store the review basis amount on the review event, and compute and record the cumulative change since review on each subsequent amount update. This removes ambiguity about R4 and R1 materiality.

- **Stable event ordering.** Assign a monotonically increasing sequence number from a single source, and forbid equal sequence numbers. Where events share a timestamp, the sequence number must reflect causal order, not insertion order.

- **Distinguish Standard and Enhanced reviews in the activity vocabulary.** Use distinct activities with distinct approver roles, so that a Compliance_Approve on a standard packet cannot satisfy R2 silently.

- **Sanctions workflow.** Log list publication with an effective timestamp, and automatically schedule a rescreen for every open transaction that involves a newly listed jurisdiction. Require the rescreen to precede release.

- **Unknown-value handling.** Make amount, risk, jurisdiction, and customer type mandatory before the release gate. A release with an unknown decisive attribute should be blocked, not defaulted.

- **Evidence archival.** Archive signing certificates, approval workflow logs, and witness statements with immutable hashes, so that evidence times can be verified without relying on displayed fields.
