# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

**Time origin**: `Submit` (start of the case).
**Horizon**: 72 hours from `Submit`.
**Event(s)**: Operational success (`Resolve`), competing internal outcomes (`Withdraw`, `Reject`), and external transfer (`Transfer`).
**Censoring**: Cases marked `open` at cutoff are right-censored at `duration_or_age_h` (age at cutoff). `last_event_gap_h` is the time from the last recorded event to the terminal event or cutoff.

### Estimands:
1. **Probability of not being resolved by 72 hours (P(T > 72 | Resolve))**:
   - **Definition**: 1 - P(T  72 | Resolve), where `T` is time to `Resolve`.
   - **Contributions**:
     - S03 (resolved at 76h): Counts as a breach (T > 72).
     - S09 (open at 92h): Censored at 92h; contributes to risk set until 72h if still open.
     - S06 (withdrawn at 27h): Competing event; excluded from this estimand.
     - S08 (transferred at 68h): Treated as censored at 68h (see Section 2 for assumptions).

2. **Cumulative incidence of resolution after 72 hours (P(T > 72 and Resolve))**:
   - **Definition**: Probability of eventually resolving but after the SLA horizon.
   - **Contributions**:
     - S03: Counts as a late resolution.
     - S09: If assumed to eventually resolve, contributes to uncertainty.
     - S06: Excluded (competing risk).
     - S08: Excluded under "transfer = censoring" but included if assumed to resolve externally (see Section 2).

3. **Probability of no terminal disposition at 72 hours (P(no event by 72h))**:
   - **Definition**: P(T > 72 and no competing event by 72h).
   - **Contributions**:
     - S09: Counts if still open at 72h (age = 92h > 72h).
     - S10: Open at 54h + 19h gap = 73h > 72h; counts.
     - S08: Excluded if transferred before 72h (68h < 72h).

**Operational matching**:
- Estimand 1: Best for **SLA compliance monitoring** (e.g., "What % of cases miss the 72h target?").
- Estimand 2: Best for **resource planning** (e.g., "How many cases will resolve late, requiring post-SLA effort?").
- Estimand 3: Best for **workload forecasting** (e.g., "How many cases remain active at 72h?").

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

### Concrete examples:
- **Survivor bias**: Analyzing only resolved cases (e.g., S01–S04, S13) ignores:
  - Open cases (S09–S11, S15) that may resolve late or breach.
  - Competing risks (S05, S06, S12, S14) that reduce the population at risk.
- **Informative censoring**:
  - S08 (transferred at 68h) may have been transferred *because* it was at risk of breaching. Treating this as non-informative censoring underestimates breach risk.
  - S09 (open at 92h) has a 31h `last_event_gap_h`; long gaps may indicate stalling, making censoring informative.
- **Cohort imbalance**:
  - Cohort B has more `AutoCheck` (e.g., S16, S17) and fewer `Reject`/`Withdraw` (S23, S22, S30). Naive pooling masks policy effects.
- **Active open cases**:
  - **Below 72h**: S15 (35h + 2h gap = 37h) is still active at 72h.
  - **Above 72h**: S09 (92h) and S10 (54h + 19h = 73h) are already past 72h; their inclusion depends on the estimand (e.g., Estimand 3 counts them, Estimand 1 does not).

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

### Methods comparison:
| Method                          | Pros                                                                 | Cons                                                                 | Suitability                          |
|---------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **Kaplan-Meier (KM)**           | Simple for single-event survival.                                  | Ignores competing risks; biased if transfers/withdrawals are common. | Secondary (for Estimand 1 only).     |
| **Aalen-Johansen (CIF)**        | Handles competing risks; estimates marginal probabilities.         | Requires correct coding of competing events.                       | **Primary** (Estimands 1–3).         |
| **Cause-specific hazards**      | Models each event type separately.                                | Does not directly estimate marginal probabilities.                 | Secondary (exploratory).             |
| **Subdistribution hazards**     | Directly models cumulative incidence.                              | Complex; requires fine-gray model.                                  | Secondary (if CIF is primary).       |
| **Restricted mean time**        | Interpretable (area under survival curve).                         | Less intuitive for SLA breach probability.                         | Not recommended.                     |
| **Landmarking**                 | Addresses time-varying covariates (e.g., `Escalate`).              | Arbitrary landmark choice; loses early data.                       | Secondary (for process-aware features). |
| **Multi-state model**           | Captures transitions (e.g., `Screen`  `Review`  `Resolve`).     | Requires state definitions; complex with sparse data.              | **Primary** (for process-aware hazards). |

### Recommendations:
- **Primary**: **Aalen-Johansen (CIF)** for Estimands 1–3, with competing risks coded as in Section 2.
  - Justification: Directly estimates marginal probabilities under competing risks (e.g., P(Resolve > 72h) vs. P(Withdraw by 72h)).
- **Secondary**: **Multi-state model** (Section 5) to incorporate process stages (e.g., `Escalate` increases hazard of `Resolve` but may delay it).
  - Justification: Captures how process paths (e.g., `Rework`) affect SLA risk.

# 6. Sensitivity analysis

### Scenarios:
1. **Transfer assumptions**:
   - **Scenario A**: Transfers are censored (non-informative).
   - **Scenario B**: Transfers are treated as `Resolve` (optimistic).
   - **Scenario C**: Transfers are treated as competing risks (pessimistic).
   - *Qualitative impact*: Scenario B underestimates breach risk; Scenario C overestimates it.

2. **Competing risks**:
   - **Scenario D**: Treat `Withdraw`/`Reject` as censored (conservative).
   - **Scenario E**: Treat as competing risks (default).
   - *Impact*: Scenario D overestimates P(Resolve > 72h).

3. **Informative censoring**:
   - **Scenario F**: Assume open cases with `last_event_gap_h > 24h` (e.g., S09, S11) will breach.
   - *Impact*: Increases Estimand 1 (P(T > 72)).

4. **Cohort-policy effects**:
   - **Scenario G**: Stratify by cohort; test if Cohort B reduces breach risk.
   - *Impact*: If Cohort B has more `AutoCheck` (faster paths), breach risk may drop.

**Reversible conclusions**:
- If transfers are mostly resolved externally (Scenario B), the SLA breach rate could be **lower** than under Scenario A/C.
- If long gaps (`last_event_gap_h`) predict breaches (Scenario F), the breach rate could be **higher** than naive KM.

# 9. Limitations and additional data

### Key limitations:
1. **Missing timestamps**: Exact event times are unknown; `duration_or_age_h` and `last_event_gap_h` are aggregates. This prevents:
   - Precise time-to-event modeling (e.g., Cox regression).
   - Accurate multi-state models (transitions require timestamps).
2. **Transfer outcomes**: External resolution status is unknown. Assumptions (Section 2) are untestable.
3. **Cutoff date**: The observation cutoff is unspecified, so `duration_or_age_h` for open cases may include future time.
4. **Policy rollout**: Cohort B assignment was not randomized, and compliance was incomplete. This confounds cohort effects with unobserved factors.
5. **Workload dynamics**: Time-varying workload (e.g., queue lengths) is missing, which may affect `last_event_gap_h`.
6. **Activity durations**: Time spent in each stage (e.g., `Screen` vs. `Review`) is unknown, limiting stage-specific hazard modeling.

### Additional data needed:
- **Exact timestamps** for each event in `prefix`.
- **External outcomes** for transferred cases (e.g., resolution time post-transfer).
- **Cutoff date** and case start dates to compute precise ages.
- **Assignment mechanism** for Cohort B (e.g., eligibility criteria, compliance logs).
- **Resource availability** (e.g., number of reviewers) to contextualize `last_event_gap_h`.
- **Rework details**: Why cases were sent to `Rework` (e.g., error type).
