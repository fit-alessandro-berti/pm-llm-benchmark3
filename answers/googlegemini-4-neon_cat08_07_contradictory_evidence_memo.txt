# 1. Executive memo

**To:** Executive Leadership Team  
**From:** Process Transformation & Analytics Lead  
**Date:** Current  
**Subject:** Decision Recommendation on Fiber-Installation Redesign R  

## Recommendation
**Adopt a Hybrid Deployment (Alternative E):** scale the streamlined Redesign R workflow **only** to simple, low-risk houses (34% of pilot volume) with coordinator workload caps; **immediately suspend** post-billing activation across all segments and pause survey skipping for medium-risk apartments, older buildings, business, and rural orders; and **execute targeted measurements** (full 60-day cost tracking, predictive-rule validation, conformance guardrails, and team-resilience testing) before deciding whether to expand further.

## Why Neither Full Scaling Nor Full Rollback Is Defensible
Pilot R appears contradictory only when viewed in aggregate. Process-mining evidence reveals that Redesign R bundled four distinct interventions with opposing effects across customer segments:

1. **What worked:** Merging address validation with appointment scheduling and assigning end-to-end ownership dramatically improved **simple, low-risk houses** (-6.0 days median, -8.0 days p90, +3pp first-time-right [FTR], -1pp complaints). Rolling back completely would destroy proven value in a third of our order book.
2. **What failed:** Skipping field surveys on medium-risk apartments and older buildings did not eliminate physical or permit complexity—it deferred discovery until the installation visit. Consequently, failed visits rose from 13% to 19%, second visits rose from 11% to 18%, and p90 duration exploded (+12 days for apartments, +25 days for old buildings). Simultaneously, moving activation checks after billing start doubled manual activations (7% to 15%), triggered billing disputes for business customers, and created a new post-billing complaint surge (6.1% to 9.8% overall).
3. **Hidden operational and financial risks:** Lower upfront unit costs (EUR 184 to EUR 161) are offset by rework and downstream complaints, pushing estimated total cost up (EUR 198 to EUR 207)—and true cost is likely higher because complaint costs after week 4 are unrecorded. Furthermore, single-coordinator ownership pushed utilization to 93% in two regions, creating a bottleneck during rework and reducing resilience when coordinators are absent.

## The Strategic Trade-Off
Management faces a trade-off between **fast-path efficiency** (lower median cycle time, fewer handovers, lower upfront survey cost) and **end-to-end process control** (tail predictability, FTR quality, billing integrity, and operational resilience). Scaling Redesign R nationally today would trade visible administrative savings for uncontained rework, regulatory/billing exposure, and customer churn in complex buildings (66% of pilot volume). Rolling back entirely would forfeit clear gains in simple houses and fail to learn whether an improved risk rule and team-based coordinator model could unlock medium-risk segments safely.

## Caveats in the Current Evidence
Our decision must account for incomplete evidence: pilot regions had a disproportionately high share of apartments and older buildings (making aggregate pilot metrics look slightly worse than a national average might), building-risk attributes were incomplete before week 3, start timestamps are sparse, and downstream complaint costs are right-censored after week 4. However, segment-level deterioration in apartments and older buildings is too large and mechanistically consistent across alignments, DFG transitions, and resource queues to dismiss as sampling noise.

## Immediate Path Forward
1. **Scale with guardrails:** Deploy merged validate/schedule and coordinator ownership for simple, low-risk houses, adding backup pod coverage to keep utilization below 80–85%.
2. **Stop the bleed:** Re-enforce mandatory activation testing prior to billing start (all segments), restore field surveys for medium/high-risk and permit-sensitive buildings, and enforce mandatory permit checks for older apartments.
3. **Close decision-critical data gaps:** Within 60 days, measure true post-billing costs, validate the survey-skip risk model offline against actual survey outcomes (utilizing idle survey capacity), deploy conformance alerts, and test shared-team coordinator models before considering expansion into apartments or business orders.

# 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

## 1. High fitness (0.94) but low precision (0.51)
* **Why it happens:** Fitness measures whether the discovered process model can replay the observed event log; precision measures whether the model avoids allowing unobserved, arbitrary behavior. In Pilot R, many simple cases cleanly follow the shortened coordinator path, and exception paths are all captured—yielding high fitness. However, because Redesign R weakened sequential controls, different cases now skip surveys, repeat installation visits, jump to manual activation, trigger billing before activation, and escalate complaints in numerous ad-hoc orders. To fit all these inconsistent variants, the discovery algorithm must insert broad loops and parallel/skip constructs, producing an under-constrained ("spaghetti" or "flower-like") model. As a result, the model allows massive combinations of behavior never seen in reality, collapsing precision (0.79 to 0.51) and hurting generalization (0.70 to 0.56).

## 2. Improved median (14.2 to 10.8 days) but worse tail duration (p90: 31.0 to 38.5 days; p95: 45.0 to 62.0 days)
* **Why it happens:** The median reflects the "typical" fast-track order, whereas p90/p95 reflect failure and rework regimes. Merging validation/scheduling, removing handovers, and skipping surveys shaved days off simple houses (34%) and the subset of medium-risk orders that encountered no physical obstacles—pulling the median down. Conversely, whenever the survey-skip rule was wrong or a permit check was missed, the order failed on-site and entered a compounding rework loop (`Failed visit -> rescheduling delay under 93% coordinator load -> congested Second visit team at 92% utilization -> Manual activation -> Complaint escalation`). This created a severely right-skewed distribution where the middle sped up while the tail exploded.

## 3. Fewer handovers (34% to 12% with 3+) but lower resilience
* **Why it happens:** Reducing handovers simplifies the *handover-of-work* network, eliminating routine queue-to-queue transfer delays when everything runs smoothly. However, concentrating end-to-end case knowledge into a single coordinator operating at 93% utilization centralized the *working-together* network around overloaded hubs (coordinators and activation specialists). Without slack capacity or shared team visibility, tacit context was trapped with one individual. When a coordinator was absent—or when rework surged—no backup could seamlessly pick up stalled cases, turning efficiency gains into organizational fragility.

## 4. Lower visible cost (EUR 184 to EUR 161) but higher rework or downstream cost (EUR 198 to EUR 207+)
* **Why it happens:** Local cost optimization shifted work from cheap, preventive upstream tasks to expensive, reactive downstream failure recovery. Skipping field surveys immediately reduced visible upfront labor (field-survey utilization fell from 78% to 52%), and merged admin steps reduced front-office effort. However, failing to identify technical/permit blockers upfront caused expensive field truck rolls to fail (second visits jumped 11% to 18%, pushing second-visit utilization to 92%), doubled specialist manual activations (7% to 15%), and drove post-billing customer service escalations (6.1% to 9.8%). Because complaint-handling costs after week 4 are excluded from the EUR 207 estimate, the true financial reversal is almost certainly larger than reported.

## 5. Improvement for simple houses but deterioration for apartments/old buildings
* **Why it happens:** Process simplification works only when it removes genuine administrative waste, not necessary risk discovery. Simple, low-risk houses have standardized physical access and minimal regulatory barriers; bypassing surveys and merging scheduling eliminated pure bureaucracy, improving speed (-6d median, -8d p90), quality (+3pp FTR), and satisfaction (-1pp complaints). Apartments and older buildings have hidden structural complexity (shared risers, legacy cabling, landlord/municipal permits). Removing surveys and permit checks did not eliminate that complexity—it merely postponed its discovery to the costliest points in the lifecycle (installation day or after billing start). Note also that pilot regions had a higher mix of apartments and older buildings than baseline, amplifying the aggregate metric drop, yet the segment-level data proves the structural mismatch is real within those segments.

# 6. Final recommendation

**Recommended Decision:** Execute **Alternative E (Hybrid Deployment with Hard Controls and Targeted Measurement)**—combining controlled scaling of proven Redesign R elements for simple, low-risk houses with immediate cessation of premature billing and unvalidated survey skipping, supported by a 60-day measurement and validation program.

### 1. Immediate Action (Next 7–14 Days)
* **Enforce hard process guardrails:** Restore mandatory successful `Activation test` prior to `Billing start` across **all** segments (including business orders) and re-instate mandatory `Permit check` gates for older and multi-unit buildings.
* **Rebalance field capacity:** Redirect idle field-survey capacity (currently at 52% utilization) back to apartments, older buildings, and complex sites to relieve second-visit teams (92% utilization).
* **Stabilize coordinator load:** Cap coordinator utilization at <=80–85% and institute buddy/pod backup coverage so cases do not stall during absences.

### 2. What to Pause
* **Pause post-billing activation checks** completely until automated signal verification is proven near-100% reliable and legally/commercially cleared (never bill without confirmed working service).
* **Pause predictive survey skipping** for medium-risk apartments, old/permit-sensitive buildings, business customers, and rural long-distance installs.
* **Pause national expansion** of Redesign R to the complex segments (66% of pilot volume) until root causes and unit economics are resolved.

### 3. What to Scale (Controlled Rollout)
* **Scale Redesign R's core workflow**—specifically merged `Address validation / Appointment scheduling`, streamlined survey bypass, and coordinator ownership (with pod backup)—**exclusively to verified Simple, Low-Risk Houses**.
* Require complete upfront building-risk and customer-segment classification at order intake; any order with missing or ambiguous risk attributes defaults to the safeguarded standard path (mandatory survey/permit review).

### 4. What to Measure Next (60-Day Learning Sprint)
Execute the highest-VoI measurements immediately in sequence:
1. **M4 (60-day full cost tracking)** across all segments, including simple houses, to confirm net unit-cost savings after late complaints and rework.
2. **M7 (Automated conformance monitoring)** tracking `Billing start` before `Activation test` and missing `Permit check` events in real time.
3. **M6 (Offline risk-rule validation)** comparing predictive-rule classifications against actual field-survey findings to build a higher-precision model without harming customers.
4. **M5 (Coordinator vs. shared-pod trial)** and **M3 (Structured exception reason codes)** to resolve operational resilience and root causes of failed visits/manual activations.
5. **Conditional Phase 2 (M1):** Only *if* M6 produces a validated risk rule, run a stratified randomized pilot of survey skipping for the lowest-risk subset of apartments.

### 5. Thresholds and Evidence Required to Justify Broader Rollout
Management should expand Redesign R elements beyond simple low-risk houses **only on a per-segment basis** when a segment meets **all** of the following evidence gates over a full 60-day observation window (with >=95% complete risk attributes):

* **Compliance & Conformance (Hard Gate):** <0.5% move-on-log rate for `Billing start` before successful `Activation test`; >=98% conformance on `Permit check` for permit-sensitive buildings; discovered model precision restored to >=0.75.
* **Tail & Quality Control:** Segment p90 cycle time improves or remains within +5% of baseline (no tail explosion); First-Time-Right >=82% (at or above baseline); Second-visit rate <=12%; Manual activation rate <=8%.
* **Customer Trust:** 30-day complaint rate <=6.0% (at or below baseline) with near-zero "billing before activation" disputes; segment NPS >=+21.
* **True Economics:** Full 60-day end-to-end mean cost (including revisits, manual activation, and complaint handling) is strictly lower than baseline (<=EUR 190/case equivalent on a risk-adjusted segment basis).
* **Operational Resilience:** Coordinator/pod utilization remains <=85%, second-visit team utilization <=80%, and absence-induced rescheduling delays (`Failed visit -> Second visit`) show no statistically significant degradation.
