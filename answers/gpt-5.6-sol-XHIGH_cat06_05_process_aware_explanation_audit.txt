## 2. Process-model consistency checks

A concrete validation suite should apply the following tests before training and again before serving explanations:

1. **Define an exact, reproducible cutoff**
   - Select the first `Coverage Check Completed` event.
   - Include events with an earlier timestamp and the coverage event itself.
   - For events sharing its timestamp, include another event only if a reliable sequence index places it no later than the cutoff.
   - If the sequence index is missing, treat the tied events as partially ordered. Do not manufacture a directional pattern through row order, ingestion order, or alphabetical sorting.
   - Check both event occurrence time and `available_at`. An event that occurred before `t0` but was ingested later was not operationally available.

2. **Apply point-in-time feature contracts**
   - Every feature should have source records, aggregation window, event timestamp, availability timestamp, and version.
   - As-of joins must use `available_at <= t0`; nearest-neighbor joins that can select future records should fail.
   - Mutable values—risk score, reserve, team, resource role, employee experience—must be reconstructed from historical versions rather than extraction-date state.

3. **Run prefix/suffix invariance tests**
   - Truncate each trace at `t0` and recompute all features.
   - Delete, permute, or replace the suffix while holding the prefix fixed. No `t0` feature or local explanation should change.
   - This test directly detects full-trace variants, final teams, total request counts, appeals, final decisions, last roles, and future sequence patterns.
   - SQL lineage scanning should separately flag aggregations lacking a cutoff predicate and fields sourced from closure tables.

4. **Replay prefixes against the normative model**
   - Use token replay or alignment against a BPMN/Petri-net model, supplemented by declarative constraints such as:
     - coverage requires prior registration;
     - document receipt requires a preceding request;
     - settlement decision requires completed damage assessment;
     - appeal requires a preceding Partial or Deny decision;
     - closure requires a final decision or withdrawal.
   - Alignment deviations identify impossible-looking or missing behavior, but they do not prove whether the log or the normative model is wrong. Deviations require investigation.

5. **Classify activity references by process state at `t0`**
   - **Observed:** Claim Registered and the first Coverage Check Completed.
   - **Potentially enabled next:** Damage Assessment for fast-track cases or Documents Requested for evidence-cycle cases.
   - **Conditionally enabled:** Fraud Investigation Opened if a trigger exists; it may then run in parallel.
   - **Not currently enabled but future-possible:** Documents Received, Settlement Decision, Appeal, and Claim Closed, subject to their prerequisites.
   - **Impossible in the stated current state:** an already-observed appeal, final decision, or closure.
   
   An activity being future-possible does not make its eventual occurrence an admissible feature.

6. **Handle concurrency and same-timestamp events explicitly**
   - Fraud investigation may run in parallel with evidence collection or assessment, so a single total order can overstate precedence.
   - Where no reliable sequence exists, use partial-order or unordered co-occurrence representations rather than directional edges.
   - The `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` edge should therefore be considered unsupported by the tied timestamps and inconsistent with the normative lifecycle if interpreted literally.

7. **Validate repeated settlement decisions semantically**
   - One `Settlement Decision Recorded` is expected in the ordinary path.
   - A second occurrence is legitimate when the first outcome is Partial or Deny, an Appeal follows, and the second event records reconsideration.
   - A repeated coarse label should not automatically be marked as a duplicate or conformance violation. Conversely, a second decision without an eligible first decision and appeal should be investigated.

8. **Use DFGs as diagnostics, not normative specifications**
   - A DFG can identify common, rare, and suspicious adjacencies, but frequency does not establish legal order.
   - A frequent edge may reflect timestamp sorting or logging artifacts; a rare edge may be normatively allowed.
   - DFGs also poorly represent prerequisites, repeated labels, and concurrency. Alignment, declarative constraints, lifecycle attributes, and domain review are needed alongside them.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

Predictive validity, causality, actionability, and fairness are separate standards:

- **Predictive validity** requires point-in-time availability and stable out-of-sample performance. A correlated proxy can satisfy this standard.
- **Causal interpretation** requires an appropriate causal design, assumptions about confounding, and usually intervention evidence. SHAP importance does not provide this.
- **Operational actionability** requires a feasible intervention and evidence that changing the feature or related process will improve outcomes. A non-controllable feature can still support triage.
- **Fairness acceptability** requires legal and subgroup-impact review. Removing a sensitive proxy automatically can sometimes hide rather than reduce disparities.

The classes below are primary classifications; some signals require more than one review.

| Class | Signals | What may be claimed | What may not be claimed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Defensible predictive signal** | Frozen `initial_damage_complexity`; corrected point-in-time `prior_claim_count`; verified initial reserve; a fully versioned `risk_score_as_of_t0` whose inputs are also time-safe | These attributes are associated with breach risk under temporal validation and may improve ranking or calibration. | Complexity, claim history, reserve, or risk scores “cause” delay. Their SHAP values do not prescribe an intervention. |
| **Process-state or congestion signal** | `queue_length_at_t0`; elapsed registration-to-coverage time; current team/role after historical repair; documents actually available by `t0`; observed prefix timings such as `Claim Registered -> Coverage Check Completed` duration | The model observes a busy or aged process state at the prediction time. These are legitimate early signals if reconstructed online. | That adding staff, transferring the case, or changing queue length will necessarily reduce breaches. Such actions require intervention evaluation and can have displacement effects. |
| **Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review** | `claimant_region`, claimant channel, corrected prior-claim count, reserve, and potentially assigned team or handler experience | The feature is known at `t0` and may provide stable predictive information. It may also be useful for auditing disparities or identifying missing operational variables. | Region or channel is inherently a “delay driver,” or service should be restricted because of group membership. Use requires subgroup calibration, error, stability, and legal review. |
| **Drift/calendar proxy** | `calendar_week`, and any derived holiday or policy-period indicators | A time period is associated with different observed risk in the historical environment. Explicit holiday or policy indicators can be used when known at `t0`. | The week number itself causes delay or that the effect will repeat next year. Random cross-validation is insufficient for this feature. |
| **Unusable until lineage is repaired** | Current prior-claim count, nightly same-day document snapshot, current risk score, extraction-date employee experience, unverified initial reserve, final team, and last complete-case role | After repair, a point-in-time counterpart may move into one of the classes above. | No current local contribution from these versions should be presented as an early driver. |
| **Fundamentally inadmissible suffix summaries at this checkpoint** | Total duration, appeal anywhere, total document requests, full-trace variant, final decision, all reported future sequence patterns, and complete-case last role | A redesigned prefix counterpart may be used if it has meaningful variation. | These retrospective values cannot explain what the model could legitimately know immediately after coverage completion. |

Legitimate prefix-derived features should be favored: elapsed time to coverage, queue state, frozen registration attributes, the actual assignment at `t0`, and counts or timestamps of records demonstrably available before the cutoff. At this checkpoint, a prefix variant is essentially the observed registration-and-coverage path, not the eventual process route.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **Immediately quarantine suffix-derived features**
   - Remove total duration, complete-trace variants, future appeals and decisions, total document-request counts, final teams, complete-case roles, and future sequence patterns.
   - Retrain rather than merely hiding these fields from explanations. A model trained with leakage remains contaminated even if the leaked contributions are suppressed.

2. **Build a bitemporal, point-in-time feature store**
   - Store `event_time`, `recorded_at`, `available_at`, `valid_from`, `valid_to`, source version, and extraction time.
   - Require as-of joins to select only records available by `t0`.
   - Version assignments, resource roles, reserves, risk scores, employee attributes, document snapshots, and claimant history.
   - Retain the exact cutoff event identifier and reliable sequence index.

3. **Establish online/offline feature parity**
   - Recompute every training feature from a trace physically truncated at `t0`.
   - Run suffix-invariance tests: changing the suffix must not alter any feature or explanation.
   - Test boundary cases involving equal timestamps, late arrivals, nightly refreshes, and queue-snapshot staleness.
   - Keep label construction in a separate future-aware pipeline inaccessible to feature queries.

4. **Repair historical lineage before reintroducing mutable features**
   - Correct prior-claim SQL to use claims created and available before `t0`.
   - Rebuild risk scores from their historical model version and point-in-time inputs.
   - Use assignment and employment histories for team, role, and experience.
   - Confirm when initial reserve and claimant channel first became available and whether their initial values were preserved.

5. **Make conformance checks part of CI/CD**
   - Replay sampled and adversarial prefixes against the normative model.
   - Add declarative tests for appeal prerequisites, decision repetition, closure prerequisites, evidence-cycle ordering, and branch feasibility.
   - Alert on missing sequence indices, increasing timestamp ties, impossible edges, and unexplained repeated decision events.
   - Use DFG changes as diagnostics, not as automatic normative violations.

6. **Retrain and recalibrate after feature removal**
   - Evaluate on forward temporal splits, including holdouts spanning the policy change and holiday periods.
   - Report discrimination, Brier score, calibration curves, threshold performance, and operational utility.
   - Expect performance to decrease after leakage removal; the honest lower score is preferable to an invalid high score.
   - Regenerate SHAP baselines and local contributions from the repaired model.

7. **Test explanation stability**
   - Measure contribution sign and rank stability across resamples, time windows, model seeds, and reasonable correlated-feature groupings.
   - Group strongly correlated features where appropriate because SHAP-like importance can be redistributed arbitrarily among proxies.
   - Reject local explanations that change when only the future suffix changes.
   - Present uncertainty when several correlated features support interchangeable explanations.

8. **Perform proxy and fairness analysis rather than automatic deletion**
   - Review region, channel, prior claims, reserve, team, and handler-related features for direct and intersectional subgroup effects.
   - Compare models with and without each proxy, including subgroup calibration, false-positive rates, service-allocation impacts, and temporal robustness.
   - Consider using sensitive attributes for auditing or fairness adjustment even where policy restricts their use for scoring.
   - Replace broad proxies with direct operational variables, such as network availability or language-support need, when those variables are lawful and reliably captured.

9. **Monitor schema, process, and explanation drift**
   - Track activity-label changes, event lifecycle semantics, missing timestamps or sequence indices, new trace variants, queue-refresh schedules, and source-table changes.
   - Monitor feature distributions, calibration, subgroup performance, conformance rates, and explanation-rank drift.
   - Treat policy changes as explicit model-regime changes rather than allowing calendar week to absorb them silently.

10. **Govern causal and actionable wording**
   - Explanation templates should say “associated with” or “the model used,” not “caused.”
   - Label controllable process-state signals separately from immutable risk markers and fairness-sensitive proxies.
   - Validate interventions such as staffing, routing, or expedited evidence collection through controlled rollout or credible quasi-experimental analysis before presenting them as remedies.
