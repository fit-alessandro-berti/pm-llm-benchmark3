## 1.3 Delay definition and the parallel/sequential decision

## 1.5 Two objective formulations

**O1 — SLA-risk objective (tail-aware).** With `SLA_p` the fraction of priority-*p* cases inside target:

`J1 = 10·(0.90SLA_P1) + 3·(0.90SLA_P2) + 1·(0.90SLA_P3) + 0.002·E[delay] + 0.004·CVaR(delay)`

subject to 1 preemption/case and 30 min overtime/resource. The 10:3:1 weights encode that a P1 breach is an order of magnitude worse than a P3 breach; CVaR prevents "good mean, catastrophic tail" solutions. A lexicographic variant **O1-lex** (P1, then P2, then P3, then delay) is more auditable for operators and is what Policy C implements at dispatch time.

**O2 — robustness + fairness objective.** Let `short(s) = _p w_p(0.90SLA_p(s)) / 0.90` (dimensionless) and `_r` planned utilization:

`J2 = max_{s{base,U1..U5}} short(s) + 0.5·(max_r _r  0.90) + 1.0·Gini() + 0.01·E[handovers/case] + 0.05·(backlog_min_at_20:00 / 60)`

O2 is minimax over the *supplied* scenarios (not the base mean), penalizes the 90% fairness rule explicitly, and charges coordination churn and end-of-day backlog (the instability signal). **Robustness definition used here:** a policy is robust if (i) worst-case P1 shortfall across U1–U5 is smallest, (ii) P1 SLA *retention* `SLA(U)/SLA(base)` is highest, (iii) backlog does not grow across days, and (iv) no resource is a single point of failure whose absence collapses a whole skill lane. Mean base delay is a tie-breaker only.

## 2.4 Bounds vs estimates (uncertainty handling)

- **Work lower bound** (all ordinary minima, multiplier 1.0): 42·5 + 18.09·18 + 24.3·22 + 12.85·25 + 42·10 = **1811 min**  =0.73. So feasibility is *possible* on a lucky day; it is the **mean** that is infeasible.
- **Work upper bound** (all tails, slowest qualified multipliers): >4500 min  >1.8. Not a planning case, but it bounds the tail.
- **Distribution-shape sensitivity:** replacing uniform by a right-skewed density with the same range and mean leaves §2.1 unchanged but raises p95 flow by roughly 10–25% (long activities block resources longer). All p90/p95 figures in §4 carry this caveat.

## 2.5 P1 attainability ceiling (a bound that reframes the problem)

Best-case P1 flow with **zero queueing**: M  50, E  56, S  62, ME-parallel  77, ES-parallel  76 min. Worst tails: ES with a software tail + intake tail + validation tail = `12 + 80 + 8 + 28.5 + 14 = 142.5` > 120; sequential ES with both tails = 194. So a small fraction of P1 cases **cannot** meet 120 min even with infinite capacity.

- Base ceiling  **0.97–0.99** (breach requires simultaneous tails; A4).
- Under correlated tails (A4 relaxed) the ceiling drops to 0.94–0.96; under U4 to 0.94–0.97.
- Under A3 relaxed (P1 concentrated in ES) the ceiling can fall below 0.90.

**Interpretation:** the 90% P1 target is *theoretically* reachable in base/U1/U2/U4 with near-zero P1 queueing (median P1 queueing budget  45–60 min). Therefore **P1 failures are almost entirely a queueing/capacity phenomenon  reservation and preemption are the correct levers**, while P2/P3 failures are a *capacity* phenomenon that routing cannot fix.

# 3. Candidate policies

All four use the same physics (§1, §8). They differ only in dispatch rules that a team lead can read aloud.

## Policy A — Lane specialization, fixed queues (incumbent-style)

- **Eligibility:** a fixed rota owner per activity/time window, with a pre-approved deputy chain used only if the primary is *absent* (not merely busy). Rota: Intake Farah(08–12)/Ben(12–16)/Deepa(16–18)/Emil(18–20); Mechanical Ada for M, Ben for ME, Deepa(16–18), none(18–20); Electrical Chen for E, Deepa for ME, Emil for ES; Software Chen for ES and all pre-12, Emil for S post-12; Validation Ben(M,ME)/Ada(E)/Deepa(S,ES)/Emil(16–20). Deputy map ChenDeepaEmil, AdaBen, BenAda, FarahBen.
- **Priority:** P1 to head of its lane queue, otherwise FIFO. **No preemption. No cross-lane help.**
- **Parallelization:** always parallel when the two branches have distinct rota owners (lanes are independent); sequential only if no second owner exists.
- **Handover minimization:** none (structural: intake, diagnostics and validation are almost always different resources).
- **Overflow trigger:** none. If the owner is busy, the case waits.

## Policy B — Full pooling, "earliest expected finish" (transparent greedy)

- **Eligibility:** any on-shift, qualified, non-break resource (overtime only for continuation/P1/own case).
- **Dispatch rule (auditable one-liner):** *take the highest-priority waiting activity (P1P2P3, FIFO within class) and give it to the qualified idle resource with the smallest `now + handover + setup + remaining_base × multiplier`; ties to the least-loaded resource.*
- **Reservation:** none. **Preemption:** P1 may preempt P2 or P3 diagnostics after 3 min of P1 waiting; victim = lowest priority, largest remaining (>10 min); 10% restart penalty; once per case.
- **Parallelization:** compare estimated Validation-ready time of the best parallel pair (incl. +7/+5/+8) against the best sequential cross-skilled resource; pick the smaller.
- **Pin-break:** a paused task may leave its resource after 60 min of waiting.

## Policy C — Hybrid: reserved P1 responders + controlled overflow + fairness cap (**recommended routing layer**)

- **P1 responders (soft reservation, recomputed each minute):** the fastest present resource per family — Mechanical: Ada (Deepa after 16); Electrical/Software: Chen (Emil after 16, Farah pre-12 if Chen absent); Intake/Validation: Ben (Emil after 16). A responder accepts non-P1 work **only if** planned active time  15 min (short-job backfill) **or** it has been idle  20 min with no P1 waiting (idle-release valve). This avoids paying for pure idleness.
- **Priority score:** `(class_rank, slack, arrival)` with `slack = deadline  now  estimated_remaining_flow`. P1 rank 0; **aging guard:** P2 promoted at 7 h age, P3 at 18 h age (prevents silent starvation).
- **Controlled cross-skill overflow:** a resource with multiplier > 1.35 is eligible only if it is the lane owner **or** the activity has waited > 5 min (P1) / 20 min (P2) / 45 min (P3). Lane owner = fastest present qualified resource (score bonus 4 min).
- **Handover minimization:** sticky ownership bonus 12 min for the resource that already holds the case; **skill-dwell batching** — a resource that switches diagnostic family keeps that family for 45 min (score bonus 5 for same-family), which suppresses the +5 setup churn.
- **Fairness cap:** rolling 120-min utilization > 90%  +25 min score penalty, and the resource is skipped for P3 work if any other eligible resource exists (unless wait > 30 min).
- **Parallelization threshold:** parallel if (P1 and both branches startable within 10 min) or (shorter branch  30 base-min and a second qualified resource is idle now); else sequential on a cross-skilled resource available within 15 min; else parallel.  **ES parallel by default, ME sequential by default** (matches §1.3 arithmetic).
- **Preemption:** P1 over **P3 only**, after 5 min P1 wait, victim remaining > 10 min, victim slack > 60 min, 3 preemptions/hour, once per case. Never spend a case's single preemption token on a branch that is not on the critical path.
- **Pin-break:** 30 min.

## Policy D — C + targeted staffing-envelope change (explicitly a *cost* decision, A14)

Same rules as C, plus: Farah 08:00–16:00 (contract extension, +210 paid min at 1.40 on diagnostics) and a cross-skilled 16:00–20:00 floater "Gus" (1.0–1.1 multipliers, +240 min). Total +450 min (+7.5 paid hours  0.94 FTE) which simultaneously closes the aggregate gap (§2.1) and the 18–20 mechanical hole (§2.4). Included to separate *routing* gains from *capacity* gains — it is not a free policy.

*Additional variants worth logging but not tabled:* pure SRPT (minimizes mean flow, starves long P3 tails), threshold-parallelization-only (C without reservation), and dynamic skill reservation with a rolling P1 arrival forecast.
