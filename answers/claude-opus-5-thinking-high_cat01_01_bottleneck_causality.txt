## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Verdict: `mainly confounded`. Confidence: 72/100.**

The claim that Orion *causes* longer completion times is not supported. Orion's crude +6.72 h disadvantage (21.96 vs 15.24 h) reverses inside every complexity stratum: Orion is 2.14 h faster at Low, 2.21 h faster at Medium, 3.43 h faster at High. Standardising to the pooled complexity mix gives −2.51 h; standardising to Orion's own mix (the mix actually at stake in the proposal) gives −2.72 h. Orion carries 42.9% high-complexity work against Nova's 12.5%, and 64.3% compliance-flagged against 25%; that mix fully accounts for the crude gap.

Strongest evidence for the verdict: the reversal is monotone across all three strata; the routing rule is explicitly complexity-driven, so complexity is a genuine confounder; and residual confounding within coarse strata (Orion plausibly takes the harder cases *inside* each level) biases against Orion, making its within-stratum advantage conservative.

Strongest evidence against: group is nearly collinear with shift (Orion 92.9% night) and load (13/14 High), with zero Orion daytime Medium or High cases — no complexity×shift cell has three cases in both arms. Within Nova, night cases wait ~1.5 h less than comparable day cases, so some of Orion's advantage may be a night effect. Only 30 completed cases; abandoned and open cases are censored.

The 50% reassignment is more likely to backfire than succeed. Applying Nova's own stratum means to Orion's caseload predicts roughly +2.7 h per moved case *before* congestion. Nova would absorb hard, compliance-heavy, night-arriving work with fewer certified reviewers and mainly day coverage, while Orion capacity idles. Queueing is convex, so realised loss would likely exceed 2.7 h.

## 2. Causal graph

```
unobserved_case_risk  -> complexity, compliance_required, rework, quality_outcome, case_completed
complexity            -> routing_rule, compliance_required, review_service, rework, queue_wait,
                         quality_outcome, case_completed
shift                 -> routing_rule, arrival_load, queue_wait
arrival_load          -> routing_rule, queue_wait
routing_rule          -> resource_group
resource_group        -> queue_wait, review_service, rework, unobserved_reviewer_skill,
                         case_completed
unobserved_reviewer_skill -> review_service, rework, quality_outcome
compliance_required   -> compliance_performed
compliance_performed  -> queue_wait, cycle_time, quality_outcome
rework                -> queue_wait, review_service, cycle_time
queue_wait            -> cycle_time
review_service        -> cycle_time
cycle_time            -> quality_outcome, case_completed
```

Supported by supplied facts: `complexity -> routing_rule -> resource_group` (routing engine, Orion gets difficult cases); `shift -> resource_group` (Orion covers nights); `complexity -> compliance_required`; `compliance_required -> compliance_performed` (performed whenever required); `queue_wait -> cycle_time` and `review_service -> cycle_time` (definitional, and the data satisfy cycle ≈ 0.8 + wait + service + 1.4·compliance + 1.45·rework).

Assumptions: everything involving `unobserved_case_risk` and `unobserved_reviewer_skill`; `resource_group -> rework`; `cycle_time -> quality_outcome`; all arrows into `case_completed`.

Suppressed dynamics: congestion feedback. Today's `review_service` and `rework` consume capacity that raises tomorrow's `arrival_load` and `queue_wait`, and the routing engine plausibly reacts to observed backlog, so `queue_wait(t−) -> routing_rule(t) -> resource_group(t)`. A static DAG cannot represent this loop, and it is exactly the loop the 50% reassignment would perturb.

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests

| rank | hypothesis | process mechanism | evidence supporting it | evidence contradicting it | discriminating analysis or falsification test | predicted observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **H1 — Case-mix confounding.** The crude gap is entirely routing-induced selection; Orion has no adverse effect | The routing engine conditions on complexity and required-compliance status, so Orion's arrival stream is structurally heavier. Cycle time is dominated by `complexity -> queue_wait + review_service` | Complete reversal in all three strata; crude +6.72 h vs standardised −2.51 h; Orion 42.9% High vs 12.5%, 64.3% compliance vs 25%; service time is a near-deterministic function of complexity in both groups | Complexity has only 3 levels, so adjustment is incomplete; the reversal itself is not proof that the *remaining* difference is null | Exact match on (complexity, compliance_required, rework, shift, arrival_load); then estimate the propensity for Orion from the routing engine's own input features and check calibration and common support. Compute an E-value for the standardised −2.51 h | If H1 is the whole story, the matched contrast collapses toward 0 ± noise and the estimated propensity is near 1 for Orion's Medium/High cases (confirming near-deterministic assignment). If matched Orion cases stay ~2 h faster with a large E-value, H1 is incomplete |
| 2 | **H2 — Genuine beneficial Orion effect masked by mix (certified-reviewer capacity).** Orion is causally *faster* per comparable case | More certified reviewers means a shorter wait for a qualified reviewer, especially where certification is binding: high-complexity and compliance-flagged work. Mechanism acts on `queue_wait`, not `review_service` | Advantage grows with complexity (−1.61, −1.87, −3.28 h of wait at Low/Medium/High); 86% of the standardised gap is queueing; service times are near-identical across groups; residual within-stratum confounding runs *against* Orion, so −2.5 h is conservative | Nova's High arm is n=2; Orion's Low arm is n=2; the pattern is equally consistent with a night-capacity story (H3); zero daytime Orion Medium/High cases | Reconstruct wait as *time to first certified-reviewer availability* vs *time queued after availability*, using reviewer rosters and certification flags. If all three sub-durations are equal across groups, the mediation story is dead and the difference is generic queueing |
| 5 | **H5 — Selection and measurement artefacts.** The comparison is manufactured by conditioning on completion and by derived, not measured, fields | `case_completed` is a collider: complexity, group and cycle time all affect whether a case finished inside the extraction window. Right-truncation removes long Orion cases (or long Nova cases) differentially | Only completed cases are present, with no abandonment or WIP data; `review_service_h` is documented as including the repeated review yet rework cases have identical service times to their non-rework peers in every stratum, with the rework cost appearing in an unexplained residual — the field is derived, not observed | Nothing in the data contradicts H5, which is itself the problem; the tidy accounting identity is *consistent* with a template-generated extract | Re-extract with all cases, including open and abandoned, and re-run the contrast on an intention-to-route basis with cycle time as a time-to-event outcome (Kaplan–Meier / competing risks). ## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Instrument before intervening (2–3 weeks, no routing change)** | Freeze routing. Log full event timestamps (start/complete per activity), reviewer ID and certification status, routing-engine input features and score, queue-entry/exit times for the compliance step, and **all** cases including abandoned and still-open. Rebuild `review_service_h` from raw events | None — this targets measurement, not the process. Resolves the `review_service` semantics defect and the `case_completed` selection problem | No assignment; observation only | Zero operational risk. Data-privacy review for reviewer-level identifiers; report reviewer metrics only in aggregate | Full DFG and performance DFG with real durations; variant analysis by group; queue-state reconstruction (concurrent WIP, servers on duty, backlog age per minute); conformance checking against the reference model; completion-rate curves by group×complexity | Converts an unidentifiable 30-case comparison into an analysable one. Enables H1/H3/H5 tests: propensity estimation from the engine's own features, WIP-band matching, survival analysis on non-completed cases | Abort only if instrumentation degrades throughput by >5%. If reviewer identity cannot be logged, proceed but drop within-reviewer designs from later phases |
| **1. Relieve the queue, not the label (4–6 weeks)** | Add certified-reviewer coverage and compliance-step capacity on the **day** shift (where waits are longest): a rostered certified reviewer for compliance hand-backs, a pull-based work queue replacing push assignment, and a WIP cap per reviewer. Directly tests whether the difference is capacity/queueing rather than group identity | Time-based: stepped-wedge across weeks, staggered by day-of-week block, with both groups exposed | Cap concurrent changes to one lever at a time; keep the reference process model unchanged so conformance stays interpretable; on-call escalation if the compliance backlog exceeds two days | Wait decomposed into time-to-certified-availability vs time-queued-after-availability; compliance sub-durations (queue-for-compliance, service, hand-back); waiting-time distributions per DFG edge; P50/P90 wait per complexity | If wait falls in **both** groups and the Orion–Nova gap shrinks, the gap was a capacity artefact and the reassignment proposal is answering the wrong question (supports H2/H3, undercuts H6). | **2. Small randomised routing pilot, restricted to the overlap region (6–8 weeks)** | Randomise **20%** of eligible cases to Orion vs Nova, with a hard eligibility filter: only cases both groups can genuinely serve (Low and Medium complexity, arriving during hours when both groups are staffed). Randomisation runs *inside* the routing engine, so the engine's score is recorded but overridden for the pilot arm | The compound treatment `resource_group` itself, within the region where positivity actually holds. Gives an unbiased ATE for the eligible subpopulation | Individual case, stratified by (complexity, shift, arrival_load), with balanced blocks | Never randomise High-complexity or compliance-flagged cases into a group without adequate certified coverage — the certification constraint is a hard clinical/regulatory guardrail. It also directly measures the **transportability limit**: an ATE on Low/Medium cases does not license moving High-complexity work, and the pilot makes that boundary explicit rather than assumed | Stop for harm if the randomised arm's median cycle time exceeds control by >15% at the pre-planned interim, or if any single case breaches the SLA because of the override. | **3. Conditional, capped scale-up with a switchback (8–12 weeks)** | If phase 2 shows Nova is non-inferior on eligible cases, shift **at most 15–20%** of Orion's eligible volume, in a switchback design alternating weekly between the new and old routing ratios. High-complexity, compliance-flagged and night-arriving work stays with Orion unless certified night coverage is added at Nova first | System-level effects that per-case randomisation cannot see: congestion spillover, utilisation shifts, and the routing feedback loop suppressed by the static DAG | Week-level cluster (switchback), with the whole system as the unit | Volume cap and a one-week reversion switch; monitor Orion under-utilisation as well as Nova overload; explicitly re-derive SUTVA assumptions — at 15% the interference is small, at 50% it is not | Time-series of WIP, utilisation and P90 wait per group; performance DFG per switchback week; drift detection on variant mix; queue-length vs wait curves to detect the convex regime | Estimates the **general-equilibrium** effect, which the per-case ATE cannot. This is the only phase that tests management's actual proposal, at a survivable dose | Revert immediately if system-wide P90 cycle time rises in two consecutive exposed weeks, if Nova's certified-reviewer queue exceeds the 90th-percentile baseline, or if any complexity stratum's median worsens by >1 h |

**Final recommendation.** Do not move 50% of Orion's cases. The evidence points the other way: after complexity standardisation Orion is about 2.5 h *faster* per case, and under Orion's own case mix, applying Nova's stratum means predicts roughly **+2.7 h per moved case** — before any congestion effect. Because queueing is convex and 86% of the group difference sits in waiting rather than service, the realised loss would likely be larger, and the moved work is precisely the hard, compliance-flagged, night-arriving work that Nova is least staffed to absorb.

Equally, do not conclude that Orion is causally better. Group is nearly collinear with shift and load, no complexity×shift cell has three cases in both arms, and only completed cases are visible.

Instead: instrument first (phase 0), attack day-shift queueing and compliance hand-back capacity (phase 1), and buy the causal knowledge with a 20% randomised pilot confined to Low/Medium cases where both groups are genuinely feasible (phase 2). Scale only if the pilot and a switchback both hold (phase 3). This learns what management wants to know while keeping the downside to a few hours of cycle time rather than the whole book of difficult claims.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Residual confounding by unobserved case severity within the three complexity labels.** The routing engine sends "difficult" cases to Orion, and difficulty is certainly finer-grained than a three-level label, so Orion's within-stratum cases are probably harder than Nova's. This biases the −2.51 h contrast toward zero or beyond, and no adjustment on the supplied fields can fix it. *Most valuable addition:* **the routing engine's continuous assignment score and its full input feature vector, logged per case.** That converts an unmeasured confounder into a measured propensity, enables overlap diagnostics on the actual assignment mechanism, and makes near-deterministic assignment visible rather than assumed.

2. **Complete collinearity of resource group with shift and arrival load.** Orion is 92.9% night with zero daytime Medium or High cases, so "Orion effect", "night effect" and "high-load effect" are the same statistical object in the strata that matter. *Most valuable addition:* **deliberate cross-shift routing for a small randomised slice of eligible cases** (the phase-2 pilot). This is a design change rather than a data field, because no additional observational variable can create overlap that the routing policy structurally forbids.

3. **Selection into the completed-case sample.** Abandoned and still-open cases are absent, and `case_completed` is a collider on complexity, group and cycle time. If Orion's hardest cases are disproportionately still open, its apparent advantage is an artefact; if Nova abandons hard cases, the bias runs the other way. Nothing in the data signs the direction. *Most valuable addition:* **a case-status field with a case-open timestamp for every registered case, including abandoned and in-progress ones**, allowing the contrast to be re-estimated as a time-to-event analysis with competing risks rather than on survivors only.

4. **The queueing mechanism is unobserved; `arrival_load` is a two-level proxy for it.** Waiting carries ~86% of the standardised group gap, yet we cannot distinguish waiting for *any* reviewer from waiting for a *certified* reviewer, or from waiting around the compliance hand-back — and `wait_before_review_h` deliberately bundles all three. *Most valuable addition:* **per-activity start and complete timestamps plus a roster of on-duty reviewers with certification status**, which permits queue-state reconstruction (concurrent WIP, servers available, backlog age at classification) and splits wait into time-to-certified-availability versus time-queued-after-availability.

5. **Quality is unmeasurable at this scale, and one of its inputs is definitionally suspect.** Two appeals in thirty cases give no power at all for a speed–quality tradeoff, the 30-day appeal flag "may be affected by post-process factors", and `review_service_h` contradicts its own documented definition (rework cases show no extra service time, while ~1.45 h appears in an unexplained residual). *Most valuable addition:* **a richer, earlier quality signal recorded per case — such as a reviewer-independent QA audit score or a rework-reason code sampled on a fixed fraction of cases** — together with an audit that rebuilds `review_service_h` from raw event pairs so that mediation through service time becomes analysable at all.
