# 1. Objectives and constraints

## 1.1 Purpose

The policy is a **decision-support and audit framework**, not an autopilot. Every recommendation must be explainable in one sentence to a worker, reproducible from logged data, and overridable by a team lead with a recorded reason.

The policy optimises a **vector of objectives**, not a single number:

| Objective | What it means | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| SLA protection | Finish before `sla_deadline`, with a safety buffer | Maximise |
| Skill fit / first-time-right | Match the required skill and complexity | Maximise |
| Continuity | Avoid unnecessary owner changes and context loss | Maximise |
| Workload fairness & well-being | Spread work; protect people with high overtime risk | Maximise |
| Skill development | Support documented training priorities safely | Maximise (bounded) |
| Resilience | Avoid single points of failure and specialist queues | Maximise |
| Throughput | Minimise queueing and handling time | Maximise, but never alone |

An illustrative utility used for ranking (weights are **starting values to be co-designed with workers and recalibrated from the log**, not truth):

```
Score(r,c) = 0.30·SLA_fit + 0.25·SkillFit + 0.15·Continuity
           + 0.15·WorkloadFairness + 0.05·Training + 0.10·Resilience
```

Training has a small weight because it is enforced mainly as a **batch-level quota**, not as a trade-off against safety. SLA and skill safety act as **gates**, not as compensable terms.

## 1.2 Hard constraints (safety, compliance, and non-negotiable fairness)

These are applied as **filters before any scoring**. A candidate that violates one is removed from the candidate set; if no candidate survives, the case is escalated rather than silently mis-assigned.

| ID | Hard constraint | Enforcement |
|---|---|---|

| H3 | Final Approval: level 2–3 finalises; level 1 may **prepare only** | Filter + role split |
| H4 | High-priority (urgent/high) work: no assignee below level 2 unless supervised | Filter + supervision check |
| H5 | Quality batching only for normal/low priority **and** SLA remaining > 24h | Filter |

| H11 | Batch-level training quota:  20% of normal/low assignments support a documented training priority, provided SLA risk stays acceptable | Batch repair step |
| H12 | No knowingly unsafe dispatch: if no candidate can meet the deadline, escalate with a risk note rather than hide the risk | Escalation |

**Calibration thresholds used here (to be re-estimated from the log):** high complexity = `case_complexity  0.75`; high handover = `handover_cost_band = high`; urgent = priority urgent/high with  8h remaining; supervision load counts as 0.5 assignment, shadowing as 0.25.

## 1.3 Preference trade-offs (soft, explicitly negotiable)

- **Throughput vs. rework:** a fast dispatch to a level-1 worker may look productive but raises rework (log evidence: >2 reassignments  1.8× rework, 1.4× appeal; high-complexity owner change  +2.7h median waiting).
- **Continuity vs. fairness:** keeping a case with a capable but overloaded owner can preserve context but concentrate load.
- **Training vs. error risk:** a trainee assignment is only acceptable with slack, supervision, and a bounded task scope.
- **Specialisation vs. queueing:** specialist-only routing reduces errors but creates Security/Approval queues.
- **Batching vs. responsiveness:** batching saves effort but delays individual feedback.
- **Team continuity vs. cross-team learning:** cross-team supervision adds coordination cost but builds resilience and skill transfer.

## 1.4 Socio-technical guardrails

- Workers can **override** with a reason code; overrides are reviewed for *system* problems, not punished.
- Training assignments are **consented, supervised, and bounded**; they are not a substitute for staffing.
- Complexity, overtime, and appeal indicators are used for **system improvement**, not individual performance ranking.
- The skill matrix, supervision roster, and training priorities are reviewed quarterly with team representatives; the log may reveal that the matrix is wrong (see C015 in Section 3).

# 2. Assignment policy

## 2.1 Inputs and pre-processing

1. **Timestamped snapshot:** use `workload_snapshot`, `current open cases`, and `hours_remaining_today` *as of the decision time*; re-read them at commit time (stale snapshots are a known failure mode).
2. **Effort estimate:** `e(c) = median_service_time(activity) × complexity_factor(c)`, learned from PM4Py service times; if unavailable, fall back to conservative activity defaults and flag the estimate as provisional.
3. **Historical risk parameters:** error rate by (skill level, activity, complexity band), handover waiting penalty, rework/appeal uplift — all learned from the event log, not assumed.

## 2.2 Scoring components

**SLA urgency (case-level, drives ordering):**

```
U(c) = 0.45·P + 0.30·T + 0.15·Complexity + 0.10·min(reassignment_count,3)/3
P: urgent 1.0 | high 0.75 | normal 0.40 | low 0.15
T (time pressure, slack = remaining  e):
   slack  0  1.0 | 2h  0.8 | 4h  0.6 | 8h  0.35 | 24h  0.15 | else 0.05
```

**SLA fit (candidate-level, a gate):** `SLA_fit = 1` if `now + e + expected_wait  deadline  buffer`, else `0` (case flagged for escalation).

**Skill match:**

```
SkillFit = 0.5·(skill/3 adjusted) + 0.5·(1  normalised historical error rate)
adjustments: level 1 and complexity  0.75  0.25 (0.10 if supervised)
             level 2 and complexity  0.85  0.10
```

**Continuity / handover cost:**

```
Continuity = 1.0                                   if candidate is the current owner and is eligible
            = band_factor × (1  0.1·min(reassign,3)) + 0.05·same_team_bonus   otherwise
band_factor: low 0.75 | medium 0.45 | high 0.15
```

**Workload and overtime fairness:**

```
WorkloadFairness = 0.5·(1  projected_open/max_open)
                 + 0.3·(1  projected_hours/max_hours)
                 + 0.2·OT_factor  0.1·team_concentration_penalty
OT_factor: low 1.0 | medium 0.7 | high 0.3
```

**Training / development:**

```
Training = 1.0  if training_priority(resource) == required_skill and skill  2 and supervision available
         = 0.6  if supervised learning off the documented priority (still logged as development)
         = 0    otherwise
   (only applied to normal/low priority with acceptable SLA slack)
```

**Resilience / bottleneck avoidance:**

```
Resilience = 0.4·(1  specialist_share_after_assignment)
           + 0.3·backup_available
           + 0.2·(1  team_concentration_after)
           + 0.1·cross_team_supervision_learning_bonus
```

## 2.3 Algorithm

```python
def assign_batch(queue, resources, log_stats, policy):
    load = {r.id: {"open": r.open, "hours": r.hours, "new": 0,
                   "supervision": 0, "shadow": 0} for r in resources}
    decisions = []

    # 1) Order by SLA urgency, but keep a look-ahead reserve for normal/low
    #    cases that can carry documented training (prevents all urgent work
    #    consuming the same specialist before training cases are placed).
    ordered = sorted(queue, key=lambda c: -urgency(c, log_stats))

    for c in ordered:
        effort = estimate_effort(c, log_stats)
        cands = []
        for r in resources:
            # 2) Hard filters
            if not skill_allowed(c, r):                 continue          # H1,H2,H3,H4
            if not rule_allowed(c, r, load):            continue          # H5,H6,H7,H8
            if load[r.id]["new"] >= policy.max_new:     continue          # H6
            if load[r.id]["hours"] < effort:            continue          # H9
            if needs_supervision(c, r) and \
               not supervisor_available(c, r, load):    continue          # H10
            if not sla_fit(c, r, effort, log_stats):    continue          # H12

            # 3) Score
            s = score(c, r, effort, load, log_stats)
            cands.append((s, r))

        if not cands:
            decisions.append(escalate(c, reason="no_feasible_candidate"))
            continue

        # 4) Deterministic selection with explicit tie-breaks
        best = max(cands, key=lambda x: tiebreak(x[0], x[1], c))
        commit(best, load, effort)
        decisions.append(log_decision(c, best, cands, load))

    # 5) Batch-level training quota (H11) with SLA-safe repair
    repair_training_quota(decisions, queue, resources, load, log_stats)

    # 6) Report unresolved exceptions for human review
    return decisions
```

**Tie-breaking order (deterministic and auditable):** higher SLA_fit  higher SkillFit  higher Continuity  lower projected open cases  lower overtime risk  same team  documented training priority  lexicographic resource ID.

**Exception handling**

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| No feasible candidate | Escalate to team lead; keep in queue; record which constraint blocked each candidate. Never relax H1–H4. |
| Only a high-overtime-risk candidate | Assign only if SLA is urgent **and** no safer alternative exists; require a recorded exception and duty-manager sign-off. |
| Only a skill-0 candidate | Not assignable. Escalate and request a skill-matrix review. |
| Supervision unavailable | Assign a qualified specialist, or escalate; never dispatch an unsupervised trainee on high-priority work. |
| Projected hours insufficient | Defer the lowest-urgency case, split the task, or request a deadline change; log the decision. |
| Training quota infeasible | Log the structural cause (e.g., only quality-priority trainee has high overtime risk; security trainees have skill 0) and propose a roster/matrix change. |
| Emergency override | Reason code + reviewer; included in the weekly deviation review. |

**Logging requirement:** each decision stores `policy_version`, accountable owner, supervisor/shadow, batch id, effort estimate, SLA risk band, the top three score components, the two runner-up candidates, and any exception code. This is what makes the policy auditable rather than merely plausible.

**Trade-off reading.** First-available wins on *instantaneous dispatch count* and loses on everything that shows up later in the case lifecycle. If the log later shows that supervision overhead dominates, the fix is to adjust supervision ratios or the skill matrix, not to revert to first-available.

# 5. Explanation to workers

> **How we assign work**
>
> We try to give each case to the person who can do it well, finish it on time, and keep the story of the case together — while sharing work fairly and creating safe learning opportunities.
>
> **The order we use:**
> 1. **Can you legally and safely do it?** Some work has hard rules. Security Review must be done by a level-3 specialist, or by a level-1/2 colleague with a named specialist supervising. Final Approval must be signed by level 2 or 3; a level-1 colleague may prepare the file but not finalise it. High-priority work is not given to a trainee without supervision.
> 2. **Will it meet the deadline?** Urgent and close-to-deadline cases are placed first, and we check that the work fits in the hours you have left today.
> 3. **Best skill fit.** We prefer the smallest skill gap that still fits the complexity of the case.
> 4. **Keeping the case with you.** If you already own a complex case, we keep it with you unless you are not allowed to do it and no supervision can be arranged. Handing over a complex case costs about 2.7 hours of waiting on average and raises rework.
> 5. **Sharing work fairly.** We look at your open cases, your remaining hours, and your overtime risk. People with high overtime risk are protected unless a deadline is genuinely at stake and there is no safer option.
> 6. **Learning, safely.** If you have a documented training priority for that skill and the case has time to spare, we may give it to you **with a named supervisor**. Training assignments are never used to cover urgent work without supervision.
> 7. **Not making one person the only option.** We spread specialist work so that holidays, sickness, or a busy afternoon do not stop the queue.
>
> **What you will see on every assignment:** who owns it, who supervises, the main reason, the trade-off we accepted, and what we will watch. If you think the reason is wrong, **override it and write why** — overrides are how we improve the policy, and they are never used against you. If a supervision pair is proposed, the supervisor's time is counted as work, so supervision is not invisible labour.
>
> **Batching:** some low-risk quality checks are grouped to save time. Batching only happens for normal/low priority with more than 24 hours left, and you can always ask to unbatch a case that needs faster feedback.

# 6. Process-mining audit indicators

All indicators are computed per case and per assignment event, segmented by team, priority, complexity band, required skill, assignee skill level, and `policy_version`. Targets are starting points to be agreed with workers and adjusted after a baseline period.

| Indicator | Definition | Source | Target / alert |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Handover frequency** | Handovers per case and per assignment; share of assignments changing owner | Consecutive `org:resource` changes within a case | Alert if > 30% of assignments change owner, or if high-complexity handovers exceed 10% |
| **Reassignment count** | Mean, median, P90 of `reassignment_count`; % cases > 2 | Event log attribute | Alert if % > 2 exceeds the historical baseline (linked to 1.8× rework / 1.4× appeal) |
| **Skill mismatch** | % assignments with skill gap > 1, or below the activity minimum; separate rate of **unsupervised** trainee assignments | Skill matrix vs. `required_skill` | Target 0 for Security/Approval/priority violations; overall mismatch trending down |
| **Workload distribution** | Gini/CV of new assignments and of open cases per resource; max-min ratio; cap breaches | `workload_snapshot` + assignment events | Cap breaches = 0; monitor CV and team-level concentration |
| **Overtime risk exposure** | % assignments to high/medium overtime-risk staff; hours worked beyond shift; consecutive-day load | `overtime_risk`, timestamps, roster | High-risk share  5% and only with a recorded exception |
| **SLA misses** | % cases with last activity after `sla_deadline`, by priority; predicted-vs-actual slack error | `sla_deadline`, case end time | Miss rate by priority band; alert on any increase in urgent misses |
| **Rework / appeal after assignment** | Rework and appeal rates within N days of assignment, split by skill match, continuity, and supervision | Activities "Rework Correction" / appeal events | Compare against pre-policy baseline; investigate any uplift |
| **Training assignment share** | % of normal/low assignments with a documented training-priority match (owner-level), plus a separate shadowing rate | `training_priority` vs. `required_skill` |  20% owner-level; report shadowing separately to avoid inflating the metric |
| **Specialist bottleneck indicators** | Queue length, wait time, and utilisation for level-3 Security (R7/R8), Approval (R6), Diagnosis (R3/R4); HHI of specialist load; % of a skill's cases held by one person | Performance DFG, service/waiting times, assignment counts | No specialist > 80% of that skill's load for a sustained period; wait-time trend |
| **Deviations from policy** | % logged assignments differing from the replayed recommendation, split into justified override / data change / unexplained | `assignment_reason` + replay | Unexplained deviations  0; review weekly |
| **Supervision coverage** | % of level-1/2 assignments with a named, available supervisor; supervisor load per case | Assignment + supervision events | Target 100% for high-priority and Security; supervisor load within agreed cap |
| **Continuity rate** | % of cases retaining their owner, split by complexity and handover band | `current_owner` vs. assignee | 100% for high-complexity/high-handover unless an exception is logged |
| **Batch quality** | % of batched cases meeting the >24h/normal-low rule; batch-induced delay; rework within batches | `discover_batches` + timestamps | Zero rule breaches; batch delay within agreed buffer |
| **Data quality** | Missing timestamps, stale `workload_snapshot`, missing `assignment_reason`, skill-matrix conflicts (e.g., skill 0 with a training priority) | Log completeness checks | > 2% stale snapshots triggers a policy pause |

**Governance:** weekly operational review of SLA misses, deviations, and overrides; monthly fairness and training review with worker representatives; quarterly recalibration of weights, thresholds, and the skill matrix. Indicators are used to improve the system, not to rank individuals.

# 7. PM4Py implementation sketch

PM4Py provides the **descriptive and audit layer**; the scoring policy itself is custom Python over the same dataframe. sim = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log, resource_attribute="org:resource")

pm4py.save_vis_sna(hon, "handover_network.html")        # or pm4py.view_sna(hon)

batches   = pm4py.discover_batches(log)                 # validate the C006/C012 batch pattern
dfg_perf  = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)         # bottleneck activities
variants  = pm4py.get_variants(log)                     # rework-loop variants

# ---------- 6. Learn policy parameters from the log ----------
cases = (log.groupby("case:concept:name")
            .agg(priority=("case_priority", "first"),
                 skill=("required_skill", "first"),
                 complexity=("case_complexity", "first"),
                 deadline=("sla_deadline", "first"),
                 owner=("current_owner", "first"),
                 team=("team", "first"),
                 handover_band=("handover_cost_band", "first"),
                 reassignments=("reassignment_count", "max"),
                 start=("time:timestamp", "min"),
                 end=("time:timestamp", "max"),
                 resources=("org:resource", lambda s: list(s))))
cases["owner_changes"] = cases["resources"].apply(
    lambda seq: sum(1 for a, b in zip(seq, seq[1:]) if a != b))
cases["sla_miss"] = cases["end"] > cases["deadline"]
cases["rework"] = cases["resources"].apply(lambda s: "Rework Correction" in s)

# effort model: median service time per activity x complexity factor
effort = (log.groupby("concept:name")["service_time"].median().to_dict())

# validate the stated historical effects (handover penalty, rework/appeal uplift)
hi_cx = cases[cases["complexity"] >= 0.75]
handover_penalty = (hi_cx[hi_cx["owner_changes"] > 0]["service_time"].median()
                    - hi_cx[hi_cx["owner_changes"] == 0]["service_time"].median())
rework_uplift = (cases[cases["reassignments"] > 2]["rework"].mean()
                 / cases[cases["reassignments"] <= 2]["rework"].mean())

# ---------- 7. Replay the policy and audit deviations ----------
def replay(case_row, snapshot, resources, params):
    """Return recommended assignment + score components for one decision point."""
    cands = []
    for r in resources:
        if not skill_allowed(case_row, r): continue
        if not rule_allowed(case_row, r, snapshot): continue
        if snapshot[r.id]["new"] >= 4: continue
        if needs_supervision(case_row, r) and not supervisor_available(case_row, r, snapshot):
            continue
        s = score(case_row, r, snapshot, params)   # SLA/Skill/Continuity/Workload/Training/Resilience
        cands.append((s, r))
    return max(cands, key=lambda x: tiebreak(x[0], x[1])) if cands else None

# counterfactual comparison of the three policies on the same decision points
for policy in ["first_available", "specialist_only", "dynamic"]:
    results = replay_all(policy, log, snapshots, resources)
    summarise(results)   # cycle time, SLA misses, handovers, rework, workload dispersion

# ---------- 8. Audit indicators ----------
audit = (cases.assign(hand_over=cases["owner_changes"] > 0)
              .groupby(["team", "priority"])
              .agg(sla_miss_rate=("sla_miss", "mean"),
                   handover_rate=("hand_over", "mean"),
                   mean_reassignments=("reassignments", "mean"),
                   rework_rate=("rework", "mean")))
training_share = (assignments[(assignments["priority"].isin(["normal", "low"]))]
                  .assign(training_match=lambda d: d["training_priority"] == d["required_skill"])
                  .groupby("team")["training_match"].mean())
deviations = assignments.merge(replayed, on="case:concept:name", suffixes=("", "_replay"))
deviations["deviation"] = deviations["org:resource"] != deviations["recommended_resource"]

**How PM4Py maps to the policy:** `discover_handover_of_work_network` exposes where handovers concentrate (the C007/C016 pattern); `discover_working_together_network` validates supervision pairs; `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` and `discover_organizational_roles` flag role drift (e.g., R1 performing approvals) and suggest skill-matrix corrections; `discover_batches` checks that batching only occurs for normal/low cases with >24h slack; `get_service_time` and `insert_case_service_waiting_time` supply effort and waiting-time estimates for SLA scoring; `discover_performance_dfg` locates bottleneck activities; `get_variants` exposes rework-loop variants; `filter_event_attribute_values` and `filter_case_performance` produce the priority/team/complexity/SLA-late segments used for fairness and SLA auditing. PM4Py does not natively implement resource-assignment optimisation, so the scoring, constraint filtering, and counterfactual replay are custom code that consumes PM4Py-derived features.
