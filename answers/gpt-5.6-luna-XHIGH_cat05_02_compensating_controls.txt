## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 is a separation-of-duties control, but its underlying objective is broader than comparing two resource IDs. The relevant objectives and threat scenarios are:

| Objective or threat scenario | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prevent one person from initiating and authorizing a completed payment | Can prevent some erroneous payments before release, but cannot create human independence | A conflict-free L3+ review before release supplies a genuine second decision-maker | Detects the same-user event after the fact; it does not restore pre-release separation | Permits same-user handling only under all listed conditions; it is an exception mechanism, not abolition of the objective |
| Detect duplicate, sanctions, limit, bank-ownership, recent-bank-change, and basic PO mismatches | Directly preventive when it blocks; detective/mitigating when it only warns and is overridden | Can independently challenge the result if the manager actually examines the underlying details | Can identify a missed or overridden check, and can support a recall if still timely | Requires a full, non-overridden AV-2 pass for an emergency exception |
| Prevent diversion to a changed or unauthorized beneficiary account | Bank ownership and seven-day change checks can block or warn | Independent review of bank details before release or settlement can break the diversion chain | Can discover the diversion, but recovery depends on recall timing and bank cooperation | Excludes a bank change in the prior seven days, which is a strong eligibility gate |
| Establish that the payment is for genuine goods or services and is commercially reasonable | Does not establish receipt, service performance, or commercial reasonableness | Can do so only if the manager reviews the invoice, bank details, receipt/service evidence, and commercial reasonableness rather than rubber-stamping | Can detect fabricated receipts or unsupported payments; normally cannot prevent settlement retrospectively | Does not substitute for the manager's substantive review; it relies on that review for the emergency case |
| Resist compromised credentials, unauthorized overrides, and operator abuse | Blocks or warns on covered conditions, but an override defeats a warning and does not identify who controls a compromised account | A separately authenticated, conflict-free manager can challenge the operator; credential sharing destroys that assurance | Immutable audit and rapid investigation can identify abuse; late audit leaves more exposure | Controls who may use the exception, but only if token issuance precedes preparation and all conditions are evidenced |
| Resist collusion or correlated independence failures | Does not detect collusion or shared control of accounts | Two human IDs are insufficient if the pair colludes, shares a workstation, or has an unresolved conflict; evidence of independent judgment matters | Pattern analysis and substantive audit can detect collusion indicators, but generally after release | Does not make a colluding pair safe; the independent manager and other conditions are compensating barriers |
| Contain and correct a defect after release | A block is preventive; a warning alone is not corrective | Approval before settlement can still stop settlement if the manager has that capability | A review within one business day is detective and can be partly corrective only while cancellation, hold, or recall remains possible | Requires timely audit, but does not guarantee recovery after settlement |
| Govern emergency use and preserve evidence of the exception | Provides one eligibility input, not authorization by itself | Provides the required independent approval | Provides the required post-event audit | Establishes the conditions: active token before preparation, amount and vendor-age limits, no recent bank change, full AV pass, independent approval before settlement, and timely audit |

Thus, a clean AV-2 result is evidence only about the individual automated tests. It is not evidence of receipt, commercial purpose, independence, or absence of collusion. Likewise, distinct resource values show nominal FEP-1 conformance but do not alone establish that the second person exercised independent judgment.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

I would assess a compensating package in this order:

1. **State the threat and the required decision point.** The package must address the same objective as FEP-1: stopping or challenging an erroneous, fraudulent, diverted, or unauthorized payment before it becomes irrevocable. A control that only checks duplicates cannot be treated as a substitute for human review of receipt and commercial purpose.

2. **Test timing.** The strongest substitute is a conflict-free manager's substantive approval before Release_Payment. Approval after release but before bank settlement is a hybrid: it is detective as to the release that already occurred, but preventive as to settlement if the manager can place a real hold or cancel instruction. Approval after settlement is detective only, with corrective capacity dependent on recall or recovery. An audit outside the recall window is not a timely substitute for prevention.

3. **Test independence.** The reviewer must be L3 or above, neither preparer nor releaser, and free of unresolved conflicts. Separate usernames are not enough where a credential is shared, the reviewer is the direct supervisor with a conflicting incentive, the same workstation is used in a suspicious pattern, or the two resources may be acting as a collusive cell. The evidence should show who authenticated, what was reviewed, and what decision options were available.

4. **Test coverage.** AV-2 covers only its listed assertions. MG-4 must cover the invoice, bank details, receipt or service report, and commercial reasonableness when those matters are material. The package must cover the threat in question, not merely have an activity labelled “validation,” “ratification,” or “audit.”

5. **Test overrideability and attribution.** A block is materially stronger than a warning. A warning overridden by the preparer or releaser is not an independent compensating control. Any override needs a non-shared, attributable credential, a stated reason, and an independent review. A manager's credential being assigned to another operator is a major evidence and independence failure.

6. **Test recoverability.** RA-7 is meaningful only when the audit is 100% for same-user emergency payments, completed within one business day, and able to trigger a hold, cancellation, or recall. Recovery after the bank's normal 24-hour window is uncertain and should be given much less weight than a pre-release control.

7. **Test evidence quality.** Use immutable, synchronized timestamps; identity and authentication evidence; token issuance and scope; rule-level AV results; manager review contents; settlement status; audit conclusions; and any recall action. “Ratified” without evidence of a substantive review is weak.

8. **Test correlated failure and collusion resistance.** The package should remain effective if the first operator is careless or malicious. It need not make collusion impossible, but it should make collusion harder through separate authentication, workstations or devices where appropriate, conflict checks, substantive review, and monitoring of repeated pairs. A repeated pair is an alert, not proof, but it prevents an automatic conclusion that FEP-1 was substantively achieved.

I use four sufficiency outcomes:

- **Fully equivalent:** The package addresses the same material threats, has adequate coverage, independent human judgment, attributable evidence, and a decision point before irrevocable settlement. Same-user processing can then be an authorized exception while the control objective is met, even though the literal FEP-1 resource comparison is not.
- **Partially equivalent:** The package blocks or challenges important parts of the threat, but has a bounded gap such as post-release approval, incomplete evidence, or weak collusion resistance. It may be tolerable for a low-risk case, but it is not equivalent to ordinary pre-release separation.
- **Merely mitigating:** The package reduces likelihood or consequences but cannot stop the material threat at the required decision point—for example, a late audit or an AV-2 check that says nothing about receipt.
- **Ineffective:** A key control is unavailable, overridden without independent authorization, performed by a conflicted or shared actor, unsupported by evidence, or too late for both prevention and realistic recovery.

“Same expected loss” is not the sole standard. Control objectives also protect authorization integrity, independence, auditability, regulatory and contractual obligations, tail-risk exposure, and confidence that the control would work under a different but plausible fraud. Two designs can have similar average loss while one violates a mandatory segregation rule, produces no reliable evidence, or leaves a low-frequency, high-impact diversion uncontained. A low-value transaction can therefore still be a governance failure, while a high-value transaction with genuinely independent pre-settlement review can be substantively well controlled.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control | Preventive effect | Detective effect | Corrective effect and limits | Semantic classification used here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AV-2 full pass | Strong for duplicates, sanctions, bank ownership/change, limits, and basic PO match when it blocks release | A pass or warning can evidence that those rules ran; it cannot evidence receipt or commercial reasonableness | Usually none after release; a block prevents loss, while a warning only enables a decision | Preventive for covered assertions; not a substitute for human independence |
| AV-2 warning/override | Weak prevention because the risk remains unless an independent decision-maker reviews the override | Records that a covered condition required attention | Can support investigation if the override is attributable; shared or preparer-controlled overrides weaken this sharply | Detective/mitigating unless the override is independently adjudicated before release |
| MG-4 before Release_Payment | Preventive: the reviewer can reject or hold the payment before the operator's release | Also detects errors in invoice, bank details, receipt, or reasonableness | Can correct source data before release and prevent the transfer | Fully preventive and the closest substitute for ordinary FEP-1 |
| MG-4 after Release_Payment but before bank settlement | It cannot prevent the release event that already occurred | Detects what the operator released and can challenge it | Hybrid: preventive for the later settlement only if the manager has a real, logged hold/cancel capability; otherwise merely detective | Hybrid in this assessment, with the unreleased settlement as the preventable stage |
| MG-4 after bank settlement | No ordinary preventive effect | Detects defects after the transfer became internally irrevocable | Corrective only if a recall, freeze, legal recovery, or other remedy actually remains available; “ratification” alone is not correction | Detective, possibly partly corrective in the exceptional recovery window |
| RA-7 within one business day | Not preventive for the original release | 100% review of same-user emergency payments detects control violations and transaction defects | Partly corrective only when it triggers a hold, cancellation, recall, or recovery before those options expire | Detective with conditional corrective capacity |
| RA-7 after the recall window | No preventive effect | Can identify root cause and support remediation or disciplinary action | Normally no transaction-level corrective capacity; recovery is uncertain | Detective only for the payment, though useful for system remediation |
| ED-1 | Prevents unauthorized use of the single-operator path by enforcing eligibility conditions | The token and condition logs enable exception monitoring | Does not itself recover a payment; it depends on MG-4 and RA-7 | A conditional governance gate that makes an exception potentially equivalent; not an elimination of FEP-1's purpose |

The relevant timing boundary is the bank settlement, not merely the label attached to an event. A manager who can still stop settlement is providing a real barrier to loss, but the case remains nonconforming to the literal “different human for release” rule unless ED-1 authorizes the exception.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

Evidence should be collected at the level of each payment and retained in an immutable, time-synchronized trail:

- Preserve the full state transition from preparation through release, settlement, cancellation, recall, and recovery, including UTC timestamps and the effective human identity behind every action.
- Record emergency-token issuance, activation, scope, issuer, authentication method, and whether activation preceded preparation. Reconcile token-service, payment-system, and bank clocks before deciding whether AP-206 is a late-token breach or a logging delay.
- Store AV-2 results by rule, not just a summary pass/fail: duplicate, sanctions, ownership, bank-change, limit, PO match, warning, block, override, override reason, and override identity. Prevent the preparer or releaser from silently overriding a warning.
- Require a structured MG-4 attestation identifying the invoice, bank details, receipt or service report, commercial purpose, reasonableness assessment, conflict check, and decision. Capture L3+ status, authentication, and the manager's ability to hold or cancel settlement.
- Retain bank-account history, confirmation of ownership, change initiator and approver, vendor age, and the time from any bank change to preparation and settlement.
- Require RA-7 to record audit start and completion, findings, whether settlement or recall remained possible, action taken, amount recovered, and root cause. Same-user emergency payments should be a 100% population, not a sample.
- Capture device, session, workstation, network, and credential-delegation evidence for preparers, releasers, managers, and overrides. Shared service accounts must be resolved to accountable human actions or treated as non-independent.

At a minimum, monitoring should calculate and trend the following by operator, business unit, vendor, manager, and time period:

- Same-user rate: same-user completed payments divided by completed payments, separated into authorized ED-1 cases, ordinary cases, blocked attempts, and cases missing token evidence.
- Override rate: warnings overridden divided by warnings, with the rule, operator, purported manager, credential, amount, vendor age, and bank-change proximity. An override is an alert, not proof of misconduct.
- Late manager approval: manager action after Release_Payment, after settlement, and outside the recall window; distinguish an actual hold-capable approval from a passive ratification.
- Audit-before-recall-window: percentage of same-user emergency cases audited within one business day and within the operational 24-hour recall window, with an alert for any gap or unsuccessful recall.
- Repeated resource pairs: count and concentration of preparer/releaser pairs, especially pairs repeated across many payments or associated with anomalies. Compare against the legitimate staffing design before drawing conclusions.
- Shared workstations and correlated sessions: repeated pairings from one workstation, overlapping sessions, credential delegation, or unusual device reuse. These are indicators requiring corroboration, not proof of collusion.
- Bank-change proximity: payments prepared within seven days of a bank change, with stricter escalation for new vendors, high amounts, or a change initiated by a person connected to the payment.
- Control outages: duration, affected population, fallback checklist completion, high-value payments released during the outage, and whether the outage disabled an otherwise mandatory block. An unsigned manual checklist should not be counted as equivalent coverage.

Alerts should create a review queue and preserve evidence; they should not be stated as findings of fraud without corroboration. A repeated pair may be a legitimate dedicated two-person cell, a supervisor relationship may be benign, and a legitimate payment may still have inadequate control evidence. Conversely, a clean outcome does not retroactively make a missing control effective. Thresholds should be calibrated to the group's normal staffing and payment volume, with immediate escalation for recent bank changes, unavailable AV-2 on high-value payments, unauthorized overrides, post-settlement manager approval, or an audit outside the recall window.

PM4Py versions differ in whether convenience filters are exported at the top level, what type a filter returns, and which parameter names the lower-level four-eyes filter accepts. The code uses the requested pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle call first and includes a compatibility fallback. The fallback and the dataframe-derived checks should be validated against the installed PM4Py version. In all versions, the output is only reconstructed event evidence and preliminary flags: it cannot establish the substantive quality of a manager review, the truth of a receipt, credential control, collusion, or the final control-objective classification.
