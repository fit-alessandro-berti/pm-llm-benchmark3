# Process Redesign for Low-Impact Spare-Parts Fulfillment

## 1. System boundary and assumptions

**Inside the boundary (measured via event attributes):**

- **Production energy**: `energy_kwh` consumed at CNC_A, CNC_B, heat_treat, and inspection_cell, including energy burned in rework loops (`Inspection -> Rework production -> CNC production`, 470/390 events).
- **Transport emissions**: `transport_kgco2e` for parcel_ground, parcel_air, dedicated_van, consolidated_truck, including the second leg created by partial shipments/backorders (1,010 partial-shipment events) and empty van returns.
- **Material impacts**: `material_scrap_kg` from first-pass production and rework loops, plus **obsolescence** (6% of batch-produced items unshipped >14 days under forecast error), treated as wasted embodied material/energy.
- **Packaging materials**: `package_material_kg`, including doubled material from reinforced packaging and waste from the packaging-rework loop (620 rework-packaging events).

**Outside the boundary:** upstream supplier footprints, machine embodied carbon, warehouse HVAC, customer-site use phase, end-of-life treatment, and the *economic* cost of customer downtime (which motivates, but is not part of, the environmental accounting for Critical-downtime service).

**Key uncertainties:**

- Transport factors are order-level averages; actual per-shipment kgCO2e varies with weight, distance, and load factor. Empty-return share for dedicated vans is qualitative ("often return empty").
- Obsolescence disposal emissions are not logged; we proxy them via the 14-day unshipped rate.
- The discovered model has **low precision** in disruption contexts, and the DFG shows 530 `Cancel due to delay -> END` endings versus only ~470 visible incoming edges — some cancellation paths are unobserved. Findings about emergency/partial-shipment combinations carry wider error bars.
- Route reliability probabilities (needed for SP1-style decisions) must be estimated empirically; they are not given.

## 3. Intervention options

### S1 — Green shipping recommender
- **Target:** All 1,420 air shipments (`Package standard/reinforced -> Ship air`), especially Standard-stock Service-contract.
- **Mechanism:** At packaging, compute P(on-time | route, segment, current hour) from historical event data; select the lowest-emission route with P  85%, operationalizing SP1.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Neutral-to-positive if reliability estimates are well-calibrated; keeping air capacity free for genuinely urgent cases *improves* resilience. Risk: miscalibrated probabilities convert savings into late deliveries.
- **Monitor:** SP1 violation rate (baseline 22%), route-mode mix by segment, late % and p90 cycle time per segment, kgCO2e/order.

### S2 — Dynamic batching with segment-specific waiting caps
- **Target:** `Plan production -> Batch wait -> CNC production` (2,910) and heat-treatment batches.
- **Mechanism:** Accumulate Regular-MTO orders toward the 12–20 batch sweet spot (1.3 vs. 2.8 kWh/item) but hard-cap waits by segment (tighter for Service-contract MTO, tightest around heat treatment where waiting carries 1.7× late risk).
- **Effects:** Large energy gain; cycle time and late risk rise unless caps bind; obsolescence grows with batch size (6% unshipped >14 days).
- **Monitor:** batch_size histograms, batch-wait percentiles by segment, kWh/item, late % for batched cases, M2 obsolescence rate, post-batch `expedite_flag` rate.

### S3 — Shift Made-to-order from CNC_B to CNC_A where slack allows
- **Target:** The `MTO -> CNC_B -> inspection -> rework -> ground` variant (5% share; 32 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap, 32% late).
- **Mechanism:** Route to CNC_A (9% vs. 18% rework) when queue depth and due-date slack permit; each avoided rework loop saves a full extra CNC pass, inspection, and scrap.
- **Effects:** Scrap and energy fall sharply; cycle time falls for diverted cases but may rise if CNC_A queues build; resilience risk from single-machine dependency.
- **Monitor:** Queue time and utilization by machine_group, rework_flag by machine, scrap kg, due-date slack distribution at routing decision.

### S4 — Priority pick + ground instead of default air for Standard-stock Service-contract
- **Target:** The 22% SP1-violating subset; these orders are often shipped air despite sufficient promised lead time.
- **Mechanism:** Default to ground with priority picking; require explicit slack-shortfall evidence for air.
- **Effects:** ~4–6× transport reduction on switched volume (18.5 vs. 3.6 kgCO2e anchor); cycle time rises toward—but stays within—promise; frees air capacity for genuine urgency.
- **Monitor:** SP1 conformance, SC-stock late % and p90 (baselines 5%/41h), transport kgCO2e for the segment.

### S5 — Twice-daily consolidated truck departures for non-urgent stock orders
- **Target:** The dominant `Stock -> standard pack -> ground` path (42% share), Regular segment first.
- **Mechanism:** Consolidation cuts transport 35–55% vs. individual parcels at the cost of a 12–24h departure wait.
- **Effects:** Transport down; cycle time up; late risk rises if applied to tight-promise orders — restrict by slack. Mild resilience dependency on the consolidation schedule (keep parcel fallback).
- **Monitor:** Consolidation wait distribution, on-time % for consolidated orders, modal split, kgCO2e/order.

### S6 — Packaging–route compatibility check before shipment
- **Target:** The 620 `Rework packaging` events (260 from standard, 360 from reinforced packaging).
- **Mechanism:** Validate packaging choice against route_mode and part geometry at packing time (also enforces Q1 for >8 kg MTO parts), eliminating repack loops and their material waste.
- **Effects:** Package material down, cycle time down (loop removed), damage returns down; minimal service risk.
- **Monitor:** Packaging-rework rate by route_mode × geometry, package_material_kg/order, Q1 conformance, damage-return rate.

### S7 — Inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations
- **Target:** `Inspection -> Rework production` (470) driven by rushed inspections; worst on CNC_B combinations.
- **Mechanism:** Mandatory enhanced inspection for high-risk combos before packaging, catching defects before value-added packaging/shipping.
- **Effects:** Rework loops, scrap, and the 160h/32%-late rework variant shrink; risk: inspection_cell is already congested and its queues contribute to rework — the gate needs capacity, else it displaces the problem upstream.
- **Monitor:** First-pass yield, inspection wait times, rework-loop count, downstream returns, inspection-cell utilization.

### S8 — Shared emergency courier lanes replacing some dedicated vans
- **Target:** `Package reinforced -> Dedicated van` (620), Critical-downtime segment (52 kgCO2e/order, empty returns).
- **Mechanism:** Pool urgent shipments into shared hot-shot lanes to raise load factor; retain exclusive vans only for SP2-justified true downtime.
- **Effects:** Transport down meaningfully; cycle time roughly preserved; resilience risk if shared capacity contends during disruptions — R1 alternatives must remain bookable.
- **Monitor:** Van load factor/empty-return proxy, SP2 justification coverage (31% missing), Critical late % (baseline 3–4%), R1 alternative-route availability.

### S9 — Split Critical-downtime into "true downtime" vs. "precautionary urgent"
- **Target:** The 11% Critical segment mixing genuine stoppage cases with precautionary rush orders (760 emergency productions).
- **Mechanism:** True downtime keeps emergency production + dedicated van; precautionary urgent gets priority ground/consolidated (the `Critical -> stock/ground exception` path: 23h, 9% late, 4.8 kgCO2e vs. 52).
- **Effects:** Large transport and energy reduction on the precautionary share; true-downtime service protected or improved through freed capacity; requires a trustworthy classification step.
- **Monitor:** Category mix, late % per sub-category, emergency-production count, transport kgCO2e per sub-category, gaming signals (category downgrade/upgrade rates).

### S10 — Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular made-to-order
- **Target:** MTO Regular (22% share, 18% late, 94h median).
- **Mechanism:** Offer slightly longer promised lead times at order entry in exchange for reliability; the added slack enables larger batches (S2), ground/consolidated shipping, and CNC_A routing (S3).
- **Effects:** Energy and transport down via enabled structural changes; late risk falls because promises match reality; obsolescence risk rises if slack induces over-production.
- **Monitor:** Acceptance rate of offered dates, promise-slack distribution, batch eligibility after negotiation, late %, M2 obsolescence.

### S11 — Availability-check rules to reduce partial shipments
- **Target:** 1,010 partial shipments; the `partial -> backorder` path (79h, 24% late, 7.2 kgCO2e, second shipment).
- **Mechanism:** Ship-complete rules or smarter ATP promising so orders either ship whole or are re-promised once, eliminating second transport legs and duplicate packaging.
- **Effects:** Transport and packaging material down; cycle time mixed (holding complete orders delays available lines); backorder cancellations (60) should fall; needs inventory positioning to avoid triggering emergency production to complete kits.
- **Monitor:** Partial-shipment rate, backorder wait and cancels, transport/order, cycle-time p90 for multi-line orders, emergency-production rate for kit completion.

### S12 — Sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides
- **Target:** All override decisions across policies.
- **Mechanism:** Structured reason codes + approver for every override; makes the 31% missing SP2 justifications and Q1 violations auditable and measurable.
- **Effects:** No direct physical impact, but enables distinguishing justified from habitual violations — the feedback loop that keeps S1/S4/S8 honest.
- **Monitor:** Override counts by reason code and approver, outcomes of overridden cases (late %, returns), override-rate trends.

## 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching  expediting rebound.** Larger batches cut kWh/item but add 4–38h of wait; cases that breach promise then attract `expedite_flag` and air upgrades, converting energy savings into high-emission transport. Watch the expedite rate conditional on batch wait.
2. **Batching  obsolescence.** At batch sizes optimized for energy, 6% of batch-produced items go unshipped >14 days when forecasts miss — the embodied energy and material of those items is pure loss, potentially offsetting per-item gains.
3. **Transport  urgency migration and re-ship loops.** Removing air/van from precautionary cases invites relabeling ("this is really critical") to regain speed, eroding S9; separately, the 12–24h consolidation wait can push borderline orders past promise, triggering emergency re-shipment — two transport legs instead of one.
4. **Packaging  damage-return reversal.** Downgrading reinforced packaging to save `package_material_kg` risks losing the 40% reduction in damage returns for heavy MTO parts; a returned part means reverse transport, repackaging, and possibly remanufacture — far exceeding the material saved. Q1 violations already correlate with return/rework loops.
5. **Rework  bottleneck displacement.** Shifting work to CNC_A and adding an inspection gate concentrates load on CNC_A and inspection_cell — and inspection queues are themselves a documented cause of missed defects and rework loops. Fixing one constraint can recreate the failure at the next one.
6. **Customer behavior.** Longer negotiated promise dates (S10) may teach Regular customers to overstate urgency on the next order or to split orders; Service-contract customers who experience slower stock shipments (S4) may escalate complaints or churn — displaced demand does not disappear, it moves (possibly to a higher-footprint competitor or channel).
7. **Partial-shipment suppression  emergency production.** Strict ship-complete rules can force emergency production runs (760 today) to fill missing kit lines, raising energy and scrap per delivered order even as transport falls.
8. **Exception normalization.** If S12 overrides become routine approvals, SP1/SP2 conformance improves on paper while physical air/van use persists — a paper-rebound that hides the original problem.

## 6. Balanced recommendation

Deploy a **tiered, segment-aware redesign package**, sequenced so that conformance fixes and quality gates come before structural changes that depend on them:

**Tier 1 — Conformance quick wins (weeks, low risk):** S1 + S4 + S6 + S12.
These attack the clearest evidence-backed waste: 22% SP1 violations in Standard-stock Service-contract (air used despite adequate slack), 31% missing SP2 justifications, and 620 packaging-rework events. They touch no resilience-critical path — express modes remain available, just justified. Expected effect: meaningful transport reduction concentrated in the highest-gap pairs (18.5 vs. 3.6 kgCO2e), zero design change to production.

**Tier 2 — Structural production and quality redesign (quarters):** S7  S3  S2, plus S9 + S8.
Sequence matters: install the inspection gate (with added inspection capacity) *first*, so that shifting volume from CNC_B to CNC_A (S3) does not just relocate defects; then introduce dynamic batching with hard segment-specific wait caps (S2), using the CNC_A energy curve (2.81.3 kWh/item) while capping heat-treatment waits because of the 1.7× late multiplier. In parallel, split Critical-downtime (S9) so dedicated vans and emergency production are reserved for true downtime, with shared courier lanes (S8) absorbing precautionary urgency. This tier cuts the two biggest per-order impact pools — rework-dominated MTO paths (32 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap, 32% late) and van transport (52 kgCO2e) — while *improving* resilience by freeing express capacity for genuine stoppages.

**Tier 3 — Demand-side levers (pilots):** S10 + S5 + S11.
Negotiate modestly longer promise dates for Regular MTO (creating the slack Tier 2 batching needs), consolidate non-urgent Regular stock orders onto twice-daily trucks, and tighten availability-check rules to eliminate second shipment legs. Pilot on Regular segments only; extend to Service-contract segments only after late-delivery guardrails prove stable.

**Why this balances the four objectives:**

- **Environment:** Targets every major measured pool — misrouted air, empty vans, batch energy curves, rework scrap, packaging rework, obsolescence — rather than optimizing one metric.
- **Service:** Every intervention is gated on slack or segment caps; the fastest paths (true-downtime van, emergency production) are preserved explicitly, honoring the constraint against choosing the lowest-emission option when service/resilience would break.
- **Quality:** Rework is treated as an environmental problem (energy + scrap + cycle time), attacked at its root (machine routing, inspection timing, packaging compatibility) rather than end-of-pipe.
- **Resilience:** Dual-routing for Service-contract customers (R1) is a hard guardrail; consolidation and shared lanes keep fallback modes; capacity freed from precautionary use is deliberately reserved for disruptions — which also addresses the low-precision emergency-path tangle by making those paths rarer and better-labeled.

## 7. Validation and governance plan

**Baseline and instrumentation.** Freeze a pre-intervention event-log baseline for all Section 2 indicators, segmented by case_type × customer_type. Enrich the log with the new decision attributes the redesign creates: recommended vs. chosen route, batch-cap headroom, criticality class (true/precautionary), inspection-gate outcome, exception reason codes. This also fixes the current blind spots (unobserved cancellation paths, low model precision in disruptions).

**Rollout design.** Staggered pilots with control groups: Tier 1 on Standard-stock Service-contract first; Tier 2 on Regular MTO; Tier 3 pilots limited to named accounts. Use difference-in-differences on late %, p90 cycle time, kWh/order, kgCO2e/order, and scrap/order versus matched control segments. Pre-register guardrails, e.g.: no segment's late % may deteriorate beyond a defined threshold; p90 cycle time for Service-contract stock must stay within promise; heat-treatment batch waits may not exceed the cap in >X% of cases.

**Conformance monitoring (continuous).**
- **SP1:** violation rate per segment, with the 22% Standard-stock SC baseline as reference; alert on regression; every violation auto-links to an S12 exception record.
- **SP2:** justification coverage for every dedicated-van dispatch (closing the 31% gap); unexplained van trips block settlement.
- **Q1:** reinforced-packaging compliance for >8 kg MTO parts, cross-checked against damage-return and rework-loop rates to catch silent downgrades.
- **R1:** weekly synthetic check that at least one alternative route is bookable for Service-contract profiles, including during simulated carrier disruptions.

**Segment-level service and quality monitoring.** Weekly dashboards: late %, p90 cycle time, cancellations, partial-shipment and backorder rates per segment; rework_flag rate by machine_group, first-pass yield, packaging-rework rate, damage returns. Pair each environmental KPI with its paired service KPI (e.g., kWh/item with batch-wait p90; kgCO2e/order with late %) so trade-offs are visible, not hidden.

**Rebound watchlist.** Explicitly track the Section 5 indicators: expedite rate after batch wait, obsolescence (>14-day unshipped batch items), consolidation-wait-induced re-ships, van empty-return proxy, inspection-cell queue depth, category-upgrade (gaming) rate on Critical splits, and override-approval rates by approver.

**Model and governance cadence.** Re-discover the process quarterly and compare variants against the redesigned target flow; investigate any newly frequent emergency or partial-shipment combinations immediately. A monthly sustainability-operations review (process mining, production, logistics, quality, customer service) approves exception-policy changes, sunsets interventions that fail guardrails, and signs off on extending pilots to stricter segments. Overrides without valid reason codes are treated as conformance defects, not exceptions — keeping the redesign accountable to both the environmental and the service commitments simultaneously.
