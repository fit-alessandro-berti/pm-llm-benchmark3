```python
# Approximation: POWL cannot express unbounded loops. This helper allows at most
# one Request_More_Info event per route. Multiple cycles are treated as one
# evidence-collection activity or tolerated by conformance replay.
def optional_request_more_info(silent_label: str = "No_Request_More_Info") -> OperatorPOWL:
    rmi = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
    no_rmi = SilentTransition(label=silent_label)
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[rmi, no_rmi])

register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")

# Standard route: sanctions and quality concurrent; both and optional RMI before manual approval; manual approval before activation.
sanctions_std = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality_std = Transition(label="Quality_Review")

activate_std = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
rmi_std = optional_request_more_info("No_RMI_Standard")
standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions_std, quality_std, rmi_std, manual_approval, activate_std])
standard_route.order.add_edge(sanctions_std, manual_approval)

standard_route.order.add_edge(rmi_std, manual_approval)
```

**To declare case-level noncompliance**, the reviewer should require:

1. an applicable controlled rule at the event time;

It is that CS-9 remains the controlled baseline, M0 is the normative control-flow oracle, and the operation contains material unauthorized deviations that cannot be cured by informal pilot practice, expired emergency authority, or post-hoc normalization.

### Formal authority is not optional

CS-9 is effective from 2025-01-01 and is controlled. It requires both `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` before activation, and it requires `Manual_Procurement_Approval` before `Activate_Supplier`. M0 implements those requirements for the approved branch.

PD-3 is not incorporated into CS-9 or M0. It is a steering-committee decision recorded in minutes and email. The Compliance observer’s later statement that she had not approved weakening CS-9 is important. Even if PD-3 was intended as a monitored exception, it does not automatically amend the controlled standard. SUP-702 and SUP-714 therefore cannot be treated as ordinary compliance with M0. SUP-702 may be an exception, but only if the pilot had delegated authority and all conditions are proven. SUP-714 is after the pilot window and has no permanent amendment.

FD-7 is controlled, but it expired. SUP-704 is within the directive window and appears to satisfy its conditions. SUP-705 is not. The fact that flood recovery continued does not extend an emergency directive. Authority is not established by operational necessity after expiry.

### The deviations are not merely cosmetic

SUP-703 is severe: a HIGH-risk new foreign supplier used `Auto_Approve` despite ineligibility, and the first purchase was EUR 180,000. This is not a model artifact. It is an unauthorized bypass of manual procurement approval and eligibility controls.

SUP-708 is also severe. The supplier was activated without the required approval sequence. Even if no transactions occurred before sanctions clearance, the control was defeated. The narrative conflict about whether activation preceded sanctions by 40 minutes does not rescue the case; either way, activation occurred without the required pre-activation control chain.

SUP-712 and SUP-715 show why M0 fit is not enough. SUP-712 fits M0 but violates CS-9’s segregation-of-duties rule because requester and approver resolve to the same human through aliases. SUP-715 fits M0 but violates conflict-of-interest disclosure because the quality reviewer and manual approver are spouses in the same reporting line and no disclosure was recorded. M0 is blind to these rules, but CS-9 and COI policy are not.

### Normalization of exceptions is a governance failure

The aggregate evidence shows a pattern, not isolated noise. In June and July, 312 cases used `Auto_Approve`. Only 289 appear PD-3-eligible; 14 are clearly ineligible; 9 have missing eligibility attributes. Six post-pilot cases used `Auto_Approve` in the first week of August. SUP-714 is one of them. Operations “normalized” the pilot flow and believed permanent approval was imminent, but no permanent amendment existed. Belief is not authority.

Similarly, the flood emergency practice continued after FD-7 expired. SUP-705 shows that operations treated factual emergency conditions as a substitute for written authority. That is exactly how temporary controls become permanent control erosion.

### Desirable outcomes do not retroactively amend policy

SUP-707 and the 47 aggregate `Request_More_Info` cases are operationally desirable: none activated before the requested evidence arrived. But desirability does not make the practice normative. M0 has no loop and no explicit `Request_More_Info`. Until the process and policy are revised, extra activities are deviations from the approved model. They may be good deviations, but they are still deviations.

The same logic applies to low-risk auto-approval. Operations wants it permanently, and some cases may be sensible, but no permanent policy amendment exists. The absence of harm does not establish authority.

### M0 has defects, but they do not excuse the high-risk failures

The prosecution acknowledges genuine model and data defects:

- M0 omits `Request_More_Info`.  
- M0 omits resource and COI rules.  
- M0 overconstrains rejected leads by requiring quality before rejection.  
- M0 does not define reactivation.  
- Notification logging is unreliable after the CRM migration.  
- `QA_Clearance` is a label-mapping defect.  
- SUP-711 is a duplicate event defect.

But these defects do not justify SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-705, SUP-714, SUP-712, or SUP-715. Those cases involve missing authority, ineligible auto-approval, expired emergency authority, segregation violations, or undisclosed conflicts. The correct response is to revise M0 and the data model, not to relax CS-9.

### Prosecution conclusion

The operation is materially noncompliant in several important respects. M0 should be enforced as the baseline for cases where no valid overlay applies. PD-3 and FD-7 should be treated as governed overlays only when authority, scope, dates, eligibility, and conditions are proven. Unauthorized pilot and emergency behavior must be stopped, ineligible auto-approvals investigated, post-expiry flood activations remediated, and resource/COI rules implemented outside POWL.

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest defense is that M0 is an obsolete, incomplete, and sometimes over-scoped oracle. Many “deviations” are not business misconduct; they are artifacts of an unrepresentative model, ambiguous scope, inadequate event collection, or valid temporary overlays.

### M0 does not represent the approved operating model

M0 lacks several controls that stakeholders and policy sources recognize:

- It has no `Request_More_Info` loop, although 47 observed cases used it and none activated before evidence arrived.  
- It has no `Auto_Approve` route, although PD-3 explicitly authorized it for eligible cases.  
- It has no emergency/provisional activation route, although FD-7 explicitly authorized it.  
- It has no resource rule, although CS-9 requires requester and approver to be different humans.  
- It has no COI disclosure rule, although COI policy requires disclosure.  
- It does not handle reactivation, although SUP-710 shows a realistic case type.  
- It overconstrains rejected leads by requiring `Quality_Review` before `Reject_Supplier`, although CS-9 only requires checks before activation.

A conformance oracle that cannot express these rules will generate false deviations. SUP-707, SUP-709, SUP-710, SUP-713, and many notification cases are better explained as model or data defects than as misconduct.

### PD-3 and FD-7 are better evidence than M0 for some cases

SUP-702 is a LOW-risk DE supplier in an approved corporate group on 2026-06-15. PD-3 eligibility fields are complete. `Sanctions_Check` occurred before activation. `Quality_Review` occurred the next business day. CRM shows notification with a stable `notification_id`. The only reason this case appears nonconformant is that M0 predates or ignores PD-3.

SUP-704 is a critical pump supplier activated on 2026-07-11 under FD-7. Initial spend was EUR 18,000, within the EUR 20,000 cap. Daily monitoring exists. Quality and approval occurred the next business day. This is not an unexplained deviation; it is an emergency overlay.

The defense acknowledges that PD-3 is not a formal CS-9 amendment. But a steering-committee decision can still function as a monitored operational exception if it has authority. SUP-702 should not be treated as business misconduct merely because M0 was not updated.

### Notification missingness is largely a logging problem

The aggregate notification missingness jumped from 2% to 29% after the CRM migration. That correlation strongly suggests a collection defect, not a sudden collapse of requester notification.

SUP-706 is a classic indeterminate case: the onboarding log lacks `Notify_Requester`, but CRM contains a message at +3 hours. The link is a mutable text field entered two days later. That is insufficient to declare noncompliance, but it is also insufficient to declare compliance. The correct classification is data-quality indeterminate.

SUP-702 is stronger: CRM shows notification with a stable `notification_id`. The primary log omission is likely a mirror failure.

### Scope is ambiguous for rejected leads and reactivations

SUP-709 is a rejected lead that failed sanctions pre-screen. The process owner says rejected leads are intake cases. The model owner says M0 begins only after pre-screen acceptance. CS-9 does not resolve the ambiguity. M0 requires quality before rejection, which is not required by CS-9 for activation. Treating SUP-709 as noncompliance forces M0 into a scope it was never clearly assigned.

SUP-710 is a reactivation of a supplier closed six months earlier. Prior quality certification remained valid. CS-9 has no explicit reactivation procedure. The event log lacks `Register_Supplier` and `Quality_Review`. That may be a genuine gap, but it may also mean M0 is the wrong model for reactivation.

### Label mapping and duplicates are not substantive control failures

SUP-713 uses `QA_Clearance` instead of `Quality_Review`. The procedure and UI renamed the activity, audit documents show equivalent content, and mapping was approved one week later. This is an ontology/mapping defect, not evidence that quality review did not occur.

SUP-711 contains two `Register_Supplier` events with the same source transaction ID. That is almost certainly a migration duplicate. The process likely registered the supplier once. Declaring noncompliance from a duplicate event is a logging artifact.

### Trace deviation is not the same as business misconduct

M0 is a control-flow model. It does not encode:

- eligibility;  
- risk class;  
- country;  
- corporate group membership;  
- spend caps;  
- effective dates;  
- business-day deadlines;  
- actor identity;  
- delegation;  
- COI disclosure;  
- notification reliability.

Therefore, a trace that fails M0 may be a serious violation, a justified exception, a model defect, a data defect, or an out-of-scope case. The defense’s point is not that deviations are harmless. The point is that M0 alone is an invalid oracle for judging all cases.

### Defense concession

The defense does not excuse SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-705, SUP-714, SUP-712, or SUP-715. Those cases show real risk: ineligible auto-approval, activation without required controls, expired emergency authority, expired pilot authority, segregation violations, and undisclosed conflicts. Informal normalization, desirable outcomes, and post-hoc mapping cannot substitute for authority.

### Defense conclusion

M0 should not be enforced as the sole normative model. The correct response is a layered architecture: revised control flow for standard, pilot, emergency, rejection, and RMI; data-aware eligibility and time rules; resource and COI rules; governed overlays; explicit scope for rejected leads and reactivations; and stable event collection.

**Operational compliance:** The operation is materially noncompliant in several high-risk areas, but not uniformly noncompliant. Confirmed serious control failures include SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-705, SUP-714, SUP-712, and SUP-715. PD-3 is weaker because it is not incorporated into CS-9 or M0 and compliance authority is disputed. SUP-702 is accepted as a justified exception with medium confidence; SUP-714 is not, because it is outside the window.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The architecture must separate four concerns that M0 conflates:

1. **Control flow** — what activity may follow what.  
2. **Data conditions** — risk, country, group, amount, date, eligibility.  
3. **Resource and identity conditions** — actor uniqueness, segregation, delegation, COI.  
4. **Governance overlays** — pilots, emergencies, scope exceptions, sunsets.

### Layered model

| Layer | Purpose | Best implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Scope layer | Define case types: new onboarding, rejected lead, reactivation, withdrawal. | Organizational rules + `case_type`/`scope_id` data fields. |
| Control-flow layer | Represent ordering, concurrency, exclusive choice, notification before closure. | Revised POWL. |
| Data-aware rule layer | Enforce eligibility, dates, amounts, time windows, master-data validation. | DECLARE/log-skeleton constraints + pandas/data checks. |
| Resource/COI layer | Enforce actor identity, requester/approver separation, disclosure, delegation. | Pandas/identity resolution + organizational controls. |
| Overlay governance layer | Manage pilots and emergencies as versioned exceptions with authority and sunset. | Policy registry + event fields + audit controls. |
| Event-collection layer | Provide stable IDs, immutable timestamps, CRM links, label mappings. | Data platform governance. |

### What belongs in POWL

POWL should encode only activity ordering and choice:

- `Register_Supplier` precedes the route.  
- Standard route: `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` concurrent before manual approval.  
- Standard route: `Manual_Procurement_Approval` precedes `Activate_Supplier`.  
- Auto route: `Sanctions_Check` precedes `Auto_Approve`, which precedes activation.  
- Auto route: `Quality_Review` may occur after activation.  
- Emergency route: `Sanctions_Check` precedes provisional activation.  
- Emergency route: `Quality_Review` and `Manual_Procurement_Approval` may occur after activation.  
- Reject route: rejection may follow sanctions without quality.  
- Notification follows activation or rejection.  
- Closure follows notification.  
- Optional `Request_More_Info` before approval/activation, as an approximation.

### What belongs in DECLARE or log-skeleton constraints

DECLARE-style constraints are suitable for activity-level rules with data parameters:

- `Sanctions_Check` must precede `Activate_Supplier`.  
- `Manual_Procurement_Approval` or `Auto_Approve` must precede `Activate_Supplier` for non-emergency routes.  
- `Activate_Supplier` must precede `Notify_Requester`.  
- `Notify_Requester` must precede `Close_Case`.  
- For emergency/pilot routes, `Quality_Review` must occur within two business days after `Activate_Supplier`.  
- For emergency route, `Manual_Procurement_Approval` must occur within a defined retrospective window.  
- For eligible pilot route, automated master-data validation must precede activation.

### What belongs in pandas/data checks

Pandas or equivalent data checks are needed for attributes POWL cannot express:

- risk = LOW;  
- country = DE;  
- approved corporate group membership;  
- eligibility snapshot at decision time;  
- overlay effective window;  
- directive expiry;  
- spend amount and cap;  
- daily monitoring evidence;  
- notification linkage by stable `notification_id`;  
- actor alias resolution;  
- requester/approver distinctness;  
- COI disclosure;  
- duplicate event suppression;  
- label mapping canonicalization.

### What belongs in organizational controls

Organizational controls are required for authority and governance:

- steering-committee authority for pilots;  
- compliance sign-off for exceptions;  
- emergency declaration and expiry;  
- exception register;  
- sunset review;  
- retrospective approval workflow;  
- conflict-of-interest disclosure workflow;  
- delegation approval;  
- audit sampling of ineligible or missing-eligibility cases.

### Revised POWL code

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def _silent_transition(label: str):
    """
    Create a silent transition. Some PM4Py versions accept a label; others do not.
    The label is only for debugging and should not appear in event logs.
    """
    try:
        return SilentTransition(label=label)
    except TypeError:
        return SilentTransition()


def optional_request_more_info(silent_label: str = "No_Request_More_Info") -> OperatorPOWL:
    """
    Approximation for Request_More_Info.

    POWL cannot express unbounded loops. This helper allows at most one
    Request_More_Info event per route. For cases such as SUP-707 with two
    Request_More_Info events, conformance replay should either:
      - map multiple Request_More_Info events to this single optional transition, or
      - treat additional Request_More_Info events as tolerated optional evidence
        collection before the decision.
    """
    rmi = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
    no_rmi = _silent_transition(silent_label)
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[rmi, no_rmi])


register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Standard route:
# - Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review are concurrent.
# - Both, and optional Request_More_Info, precede Manual_Procurement_Approval.
# - Manual_Procurement_Approval precedes Activate_Supplier.
# - Notification precedes closure.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
sanctions_std = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality_std = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
rmi_std = optional_request_more_info("No_RMI_Standard")
manual_std = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
activate_std = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
notify_std = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close_std = Transition(label="Close_Case")

standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[
        sanctions_std,
        quality_std,
        rmi_std,
        manual_std,
        activate_std,
        notify_std,
        close_std,
    ]
)

standard_route.order.add_edge(sanctions_std, manual_std)
standard_route.order.add_edge(quality_std, manual_std)
standard_route.order.add_edge(rmi_std, manual_std)
standard_route.order.add_edge(manual_std, activate_std)
standard_route.order.add_edge(activate_std, notify_std)
standard_route.order.add_edge(notify_std, close_std)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Low-risk auto-approval route:
# - Sanctions_Check precedes Auto_Approve.
# - Optional Request_More_Info precedes Auto_Approve.
# - Auto_Approve precedes Activate_Supplier.
# - Quality_Review may occur after provisional activation.
# - Notification may occur before or after Quality_Review, but before closure.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
sanctions_auto = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
rmi_auto = optional_request_more_info("No_RMI_Auto")
auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
activate_auto = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
quality_auto = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
notify_auto = Transition(label("Notify_Requester"))
close_auto = Transition(label="Close_Case")

auto_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[
        sanctions_auto,
        rmi_auto,
        auto_approve,
        activate_auto,
        quality_auto,
        notify_auto,
        close_auto,
    ]
)

auto_route.order.add_edge(sanctions_auto, auto_approve)

auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_approve, activate_auto)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Emergency/provisional route:
# - Sanctions_Check precedes provisional Activate_Supplier.
# - Optional Request_More_Info may precede activation.
# - Quality_Review and Manual_Procurement_Approval may occur after activation.
# - Notification may occur before or after post-activation checks, but before closure.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
sanctions_em = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
rmi_em = optional_request_more_info("No_RMI_Emergency")
activate_em = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
quality_em = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
manual_em = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
notify_em = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close_em = Transition(label="Close_Case")

emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[
        sanctions_em,
        rmi_em,
        activate_em,
        quality_em,
        manual_em,
        notify_em,
        close_em,
    ]
)

emergency_route.order.add_edge(sanctions_em, activate_em)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(rmi_em, activate_em)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(activate_em, notify_em)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(activate_em, quality_em)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(activate_em, manual_em)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(notify_em, close_em)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(quality_em, close_em)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(manual_em, close_em)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Reject route:
# - A lead may be rejected after sanctions without Quality_Review.
# - Notification precedes closure.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
sanctions_rej = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
notify_rej = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

    nodes=[
        sanctions_rej,
        reject,

reject_route.order.add_edge(sanctions_rej, reject)
reject_route.order.add_edge(reject, notify_rej)
reject_route.order.add_edge(notify_rej, close_rej)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Route choice:
# POWL cannot enforce eligibility, risk, country, group, amount, date,
# authority, actor identity, or COI. Those are data/resource rules.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
route = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[
        standard_route,
        auto_route,
        emergency_route,
        reject_route,

M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, route])
```
