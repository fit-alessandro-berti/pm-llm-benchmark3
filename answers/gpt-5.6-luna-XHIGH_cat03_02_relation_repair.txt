## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

I treat referential integrity, relation direction, and preservation of observed data as hard invariants. Every added E2O edge must use an existing event and object ID; every added O2O edge must use two existing object IDs. An O2O qualifier is directional: `DEL-Y carries PK-8` cannot be silently represented as `PK-8 carries DEL-Y`. Exact duplicate relations are not useful repairs. I also do not invent events, objects, or identifier values, and I retain all observed relations.

The following are strong defaults, but not absolute rules:

- `Create Order` has one Order, and `Add Item` has one Order plus one Item.
- `Pack Item` has one Item and normally one Package. An item-only packing event is allowed by the stated scanner-outage exception.
- `Dispatch Package` has one Package and normally one Delivery, although a bulk dispatch can weaken that cardinality.
- `Confirm Delivery` can be order-level, delivery-level, or multi-delivery, so its cardinality is intentionally permissive.
- `Issue Invoice` has one Invoice and at least one Order; a consolidated invoice may have several Orders.
- `Record Payment` has one Payment and normally one Invoice, but an unapplied payment is legitimate.
- Lifecycle order is a strong default: creation/addition precedes packing, packing precedes dispatch, dispatch precedes confirmation, and invoicing precedes settlement.

Identifier prefixes, close timestamps, route, customer, and amount are weak-to-moderate heuristics. `PK8-2` is meaningful evidence for PK-8, `N-421?` is meaningful evidence for DEL-Y, and `N-42*` deliberately does not distinguish DEL-X from DEL-Y. The common customer C17 supports a consolidated transaction hypothesis but does not identify an order or delivery by itself. The exact 230 invoice total equals the two declared order values, and 100 plus 130 equals the invoice total; these are useful abductive clues, not proof, because fees, discounts, taxes, credits, and unapplied cash are allowed.

Evidence against a repair includes a direct identifier conflict, a violation of the normal cardinality without a bulk or outage explanation, a reversed lifecycle timestamp, a duplicate role for the same event, or an edge that requires a chain of earlier guesses. For example, DEL-X has carrier reference N-420, so assigning the N-421 dispatch to DEL-X is weak unless the event is reinterpreted as bulk dispatch. Conversely, an object remaining without a complete lifecycle is not by itself evidence that an edge must be invented.

I combine evidence by preferring the smallest set of edges that explains the strongest direct clues, then checking whether the resulting object paths are temporally and cardinality-consistent. Existing O2O edges increase confidence only when they agree with independently observed E2O roles; an O2O edge inferred solely from an E2O edge is recorded as dependent evidence, not double-counted. Confidence values below express judgment about a candidate relation, not statistical probabilities.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

Bundle A is the conservative/minimum-change repair selected below. It accepts the two relatively strong identifier-based repairs, but keeps the ambiguous confirmation and financial allocations unresolved. It adds ev08–PK-8 and `PK-8 packs IT-B1`, both provisional because the scanner reference is the best available clue but the item-only exception is real. It adds ev10–DEL-Y and `DEL-Y carries PK-8`, also provisionally. Thus PK-8 has two packed items and a dispatch through DEL-Y, while DEL-X continues to carry PK-7 from ev09.

For ev11, Bundle A retains only the observed ORD-A relation. It does not choose DEL-X, DEL-Y, both deliveries, or ORD-B: the wildcard and customer field establish plausible candidates but not a unique answer. For INV-44, it retains only the observed ORD-A E2O/O2O relationship. It leaves the possible ORD-B invoice allocation unresolved despite the exact total match. PAY-2 remains an unapplied payment event; it is not linked to INV-44 merely because 130 is the apparent residual. This bundle leaves some lifecycle fragments incomplete deliberately.

Bundle B is a plausible consolidated-customer interpretation. It includes all Bundle A package and dispatch repairs. It additionally links ev11 to both DEL-X and DEL-Y as a multi-delivery acknowledgment, while retaining its observed ORD-A link. It adds ev12–ORD-B and `INV-44 bills ORD-B`, interpreting INV-44 as a consolidated invoice for the two C17 orders. It also adds ev14–INV-44 and `PAY-2 settles INV-44`, interpreting the two payments as the 100 and 130 components of that invoice. It need not add ev11–ORD-B: the order-A relation can be the portal's anchor while the delivery relations represent the batch acknowledgment. The resulting graph has a coherent consolidated financial lifecycle, but its strongest links still rest on amount arithmetic and wildcard matching.

A further, lower-ranked Bundle C could treat ev10 as a bulk dispatch under DEL-X and add `DEL-X carries PK-8`, leaving DEL-Y unused. That interpretation explains a possible bulk operation but conflicts with N-421 and makes DEL-Y more orphan-like, so it is not selected. It illustrates why the carrier prefix should be treated as evidence rather than an unconditional key.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

For Orders, ORD-A has create, two item additions, an order-level delivery confirmation, and invoice issuance. Its lifecycle is plausible, although package and delivery confirmation are not directly attached. ORD-B has create and item addition, and under Bundle A its item is packed into PK-8 and dispatched through DEL-Y indirectly through item/package relations. It has no confirmed delivery or invoice edge. That is an incomplete observation, not an orphan that must be repaired. The existing `contains` edges are independently supported by the Add Item E2O rows.

For Items, IT-A1 and IT-A2 each have an Add Item event and one Pack Item event. IT-B1 has ev05 and ev08; A1/A2/B1 each have exactly one package under the selected repair. The new `PK-8 packs IT-B1` edge agrees with the proposed ev08 relation, but is not independent confirmation. No item is assigned to PK-7 without evidence.

For Packages, PK-7 has ev06 and ev09, with the observed `DEL-X carries PK-7` edge. PK-8 has ev07, the provisional ev08, and ev10, with two packed items and one delivery. A package containing items from two orders is unusual but not prohibited; both orders share customer C17 and the data supplies no package exclusivity rule. The package-to-item O2O structure is cardinality-one-to-many, not a contradiction.

For Deliveries, DEL-X has a dispatch event and one carried package. DEL-Y gains a dispatch event and one carried package under Bundle A. Neither receives a confirmation E2O edge, because ev11 is left order-level. There are no delivery or package orphans caused by the selected additions, although DEL-Y would be an orphan with respect to events in the unrepaired log.

For Invoice and Payment, INV-44 has issue ev12, bills ORD-A, and is settled by PAY-1. PAY-2 has a Record Payment event and an E2O relation to PAY-2 itself, but no invoice allocation. That is a valid unapplied-cash state under the domain rules. The invoice's 230 total is suspicious relative to ORD-A alone, but not a hard lifecycle failure. Bundle B would make INV-44 one-to-many over orders and payments; Bundle A does not.

All events have at least their observed expected object role, except that ev08 is intentionally item-only in the raw log and provisionally gains a package in A. ev11 remains a valid order-only confirmation, ev14 a valid payment-only record. No event is duplicated; no normal one-to-one rule is violated without a stated exception. Existing O2O and E2O independently agree for ORD-A/IT-A1, ORD-A/IT-A2, ORD-B/IT-B1, PK-7/IT-A1, PK-8/IT-A2, DEL-X/PK-7, and INV-44/ORD-A. The two new O2O edges do not have independent evidence: each is partly inferred from its new E2O edge and the analogous observed pattern. Treating them as independent would be circular reasoning.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

The unrepaired log has 14 events, so event-count activity frequencies do not change in either bundle: two Create Order, three Add Item, three Pack Item, two Dispatch Package, one Confirm Delivery, one Issue Invoice, and two Record Payment events. Repairs change object participation and graph connectivity, not the event count.

In the unrepaired OC-DFG, the item path for IT-B1 ends at ev08 while the PK-8 path runs through ev07 to ev10; DEL-Y has no event path. Bundle A joins ev08 to PK-8, so PK-8 can show a `Pack Item -> Pack Item -> Dispatch Package` fragment across ev07, ev08, and ev10, and IT-B1 still shows `Add Item -> Pack Item`. It adds the concrete event/object edges ev08–PK-8 and ev10–DEL-Y. Bundle B additionally creates `Dispatch Package -> Confirm Delivery` paths for both deliveries and a second `Record Payment` occurrence on the invoice after `Issue Invoice`. It also gives ORD-B an `Issue Invoice` continuation. These may make the consolidated process look more complete than the raw evidence warrants.

In the OTG, the object-type directions already include Order-to-Item, Package-to-Item, Delivery-to-Package, Invoice-to-Order, and Payment-to-Invoice instances where observed. Bundle A increases the Package-to-Item instance count through PK-8 and the Delivery-to-Package instances through DEL-Y, even if the type-level edges already existed. Bundle B increases Invoice-to-Order and Payment-to-Invoice instances and creates a multi-delivery confirmation pattern. The direction must remain, for example, Delivery-to-Package, not Package-to-Delivery.

The ET–OT graph gains two edges in A: ev08 to PK-8 and ev10 to DEL-Y, plus the selected O2O topology if that graph incorporates O2O links. B further adds ev11 to DEL-X and DEL-Y, ev12 to ORD-B, and ev14 to INV-44. In the raw graph, ev11 is connected only to ORD-A, ev12 only to INV-44 and ORD-A, and ev14 only to PAY-2.

With direct E2O flattening, Order flattening is unchanged in A: ORD-A has its observed order trace and ORD-B still ends after Add Item. B adds Issue Invoice to ORD-B; any order-level confirmation for B remains absent unless that optional edge is chosen. Package flattening gains ev08 for PK-8 in A and B. Delivery flattening has DEL-X/ev09 in the raw log, gains DEL-Y/ev10 in A, and gains the ev11 confirmation on both deliveries in B. Invoice flattening is ev12 followed by ev13 in the raw log and A, but includes ev14 in B. Payment flattening already contains ev13 for PAY-1 and ev14 for PAY-2; B additionally makes PAY-2 part of the invoice-centered view. Implementations that first propagate O2O links can create further derived inclusions, but that is an enrichment choice rather than proof of business semantics.

Unique-object counts also change. For Pack Item, the raw log and A/B have three distinct items; the directly associated package objects remain two distinct packages in A/B because ev08 reuses PK-8 rather than creating a third package. The total package incidences rise from two to three. Dispatch has one distinct delivery in the raw log and two in A/B. Confirm Delivery has zero distinct delivery objects in the raw log and A, and two in B. Issue Invoice has one distinct Order in the raw log/A and two in B. Record Payment has one distinct invoice in the raw log/A and two in B. Total E2O incidences rise in A by two edges (ev08's package and ev10's delivery), and B adds four further E2O incidences: two delivery confirmations, ORD-B on ev12, and INV-44 on ev14. Confusing event counts with these incidence counts would produce false frequency changes.

Equivalent-execution clustering around Order sees ORD-B as a short Create/Add trace in the raw log and still mostly so in A under direct flattening. A may nevertheless make B1's package context resemble ORD-A's package context if clustering uses O2O reachability. B makes ORD-B more similar to ORD-A by adding the invoice, and possibly shared delivery/payment context through enrichment; it can therefore merge executions that are merely same-customer or same-invoice cases. Concrete new fragments include `ev08 Pack Item -> ev10 Dispatch Package` on PK-8 in A and `ev10 Dispatch Package -> ev11 Confirm Delivery` plus `ev12 Issue Invoice -> ev14 Record Payment` in B.

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Preserve the raw tables and never mutate the caller's originals.
events_raw = events.copy(deep=True)
objects_raw = objects.copy(deep=True)
relations_raw = relations.copy(deep=True)
o2o_raw = o2o.copy(deep=True)

# These are the selected Bundle A additions. E2O qualifiers are local role labels.
e2o_add = pd.DataFrame([
    {"ocel:eid": "ev08", "ocel:oid": "PK-8", "ocel:qualifier": "package"},
    {"ocel:eid": "ev10", "ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:qualifier": "delivery"},
])
o2o_add = pd.DataFrame([
    {"ocel:oid": "PK-8", "ocel:oid_2": "IT-B1", "ocel:qualifier": "packs"},
    {"ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:oid_2": "PK-8", "ocel:qualifier": "carries"},
])

events_r = events_raw.copy(deep=True)
objects_r = objects_raw.copy(deep=True)
relations_r = pd.concat([relations_raw, e2o_add], ignore_index=True)
o2o_r = pd.concat([o2o_raw, o2o_add], ignore_index=True)

assert {"ocel:eid", "ocel:oid"}.issubset(relations_r.columns)
assert {"ocel:eid"}.issubset(events_r.columns)
assert {"ocel:oid"}.issubset(objects_r.columns)
assert {"ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2"}.issubset(o2o_r.columns)

event_ids = set(events_r["ocel:eid"])
object_ids = set(objects_r["ocel:oid"])
assert set(relations_r["ocel:eid"]) <= event_ids
assert set(relations_r["ocel:oid"]) <= object_ids
assert set(o2o_r["ocel:oid"]) <= object_ids
assert set(o2o_r["ocel:oid_2"]) <= object_ids

e2o_key = ["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"]
o2o_key = ["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"]
assert not relations_r.duplicated(e2o_key).any(), "duplicate E2O relation"
assert not o2o_r.duplicated(o2o_key).any(), "duplicate O2O relation"

# VERSION-DEPENDENT: constructor location/signature differs across PM4Py releases.
# In releases exposing pm4py.OCEL, this is the corresponding OCEL 2.0 constructor.
ocel = pm4py.OCEL(
    events=events_r,
    objects=objects_r,
    relations=relations_r,
    o2o=o2o_r,
)

ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)
order_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type="Order")
delivery_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type="Delivery")

# Deliberately do not call pm4py.ocel_o2o_enrichment as semantic validation.
# If used for an exploratory view, its propagated edges must be labeled derived.
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings

- Assigning ev08 to PK-8 can falsely put IT-B1 from ORD-B in the same package as IT-A2 and create a spurious cross-order packing and dispatch path.
- Assigning ev10 to DEL-X because the timestamps and route match can duplicate a package under DEL-X, leave DEL-Y unexplained, and corrupt carrier-level throughput.
- Assigning ev11 to both deliveries from `N-42*` can turn an order-level portal acknowledgment into two confirmed delivery lifecycles and inflate on-time delivery metrics.
- Assigning ev11 to ORD-B merely because the customer is C17 can create a false order confirmation and make two executions appear equivalent.
- Adding ORD-B to INV-44 solely from 230 = 130+100 can manufacture a consolidated invoice when the difference could be a fee, tax, adjustment, or unrelated charge.
- Linking PAY-2 to INV-44 solely from the residual 130 can convert legitimate unapplied cash into a false settlement and create an unsupported payment sequence.
- Treating an inferred O2O edge as independent confirmation of the E2O edge double-counts the same scanner, prefix, or amount evidence.
- Applying O2O enrichment and then flattening without marking propagated events can duplicate package, delivery, invoice, or payment activities in Order traces.
- Counting total event-object incidences as event frequencies can make the same 14-event log appear to have more packing, confirmation, or payment activity after repair.
- Reversing a directional qualifier, such as storing `PK-8 carries DEL-Y`, changes the object graph's meaning and can create impossible downstream traversal paths.
