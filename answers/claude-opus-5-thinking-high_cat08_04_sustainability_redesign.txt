# Sustainability Redesign for the Spare-Parts Order-to-Delivery Process

## 1. System boundary and assumptions

**Functional unit.** All comparisons are per *delivered, on-time, damage-free order line* — not per order and not per shipment. This matters for three reasons visible in the log: (a) the log is flattened by order, so a "low kWh/order" figure may simply mean fewer line items; (b) partial shipments produce two shipping legs and two packaging events for one order, so a per-order transport metric understates them; (c) a cancelled order (410 + 60 cases upstream, 530 reaching END) looks emission-*free* per order while actually being 100% waste of whatever was already consumed.

**Inside the boundary (measurable from current attributes).**
- Own/contracted production energy: `energy_kwh` per event, attributable to `machine_group` (CNC_A, CNC_B, heat_treat, inspection_cell). Treated as Scope 2 (purchased electricity) plus Scope 1 for any on-site fuel in heat treatment.
- Transport: `transport_kgco2e` per `route_mode` (parcel_ground, parcel_air, dedicated_van, consolidated_truck). Scope 3 category 4/9 for purchased carriers; Scope 1 if dedicated vans are own fleet — this split is unknown and matters for who owns the reduction.
- Packaging materials: `package_material_kg`, including the *repeated* consumption caused by `Rework packaging` (620 entries into the loop) and by second shipments after `Partial shipment`.
- Production waste: `material_scrap_kg`, including the multiplied scrap of `Inspection -> Rework production -> CNC production` loops (470 / 390 edges).
- Obsolescence: the 6% of batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days. Their *entire* embodied energy, scrap and material is a loss with zero delivered function.

**Outside the boundary (acknowledged, not optimised).**
- Cradle-to-gate burden of purchased raw material and of supplier-manufactured stock parts. This is likely the single largest lifecycle term for Made-to-order metal parts, which means `material_scrap_kg` and obsolescence are *proxies for a much larger upstream footprint* than their kilogram mass suggests. Avoided scrap should be weighted more heavily than its mass implies; avoided kWh should not be over-weighted.
- The part's use phase and end-of-life.
- **The customer's avoided impact.** A Critical-downtime spare part that arrives late leaves an industrial asset stopped: idle energy, spoiled work-in-progress, restart scrap, and sometimes a substitute part flown in by the customer. This is a deliberate boundary asymmetry: our ledger sees only the 52 kgCO2e of a dedicated van, never the avoided downtime waste. It is the main reason a naive "minimise our own emissions" rule is wrong for the Critical segment, and it is why the constraint against recommending the lowest-emission option where service or resilience fails is not merely a business constraint but a *lifecycle* argument.
- Leakage: a `Cancel due to delay` case where the customer sources elsewhere shifts, not removes, impact.

**Assumptions.**
1. `transport_kgco2e` is already a carrier/mode-based estimate (distance × mode factor), not measured; absolute levels are uncertain, relative ordering between modes (air 4–6x ground; van 52 vs ground 4.8 on the Critical paths) is credible.
2. Batch energy is allocated by dividing a batch-level meter reading by `batch_size`, which is why per-item energy falls with batch size (fixed heat-up/setup load amortised). The *marginal* energy of adding one item to a batch is therefore much lower than the average — and, symmetrically, a half-empty batch does not save what the curve suggests. Batch fill ratio must be measured, not assumed.
3. `promised_lead_time_hours` is trustworthy as the service commitment; slack must be *computed* (promise minus projected remaining time), and that field does not exist yet.
4. No grid emission factor is given, so **kWh and kgCO2e are not combined into one score**. Interventions are ranked on both axes separately, with a note where a factor would be needed to trade them off (e.g., S2 saves energy while risking transport; the sign of the net effect is factor-dependent).

**Residual uncertainty.**
- Data quality: the DFG does not close. Shipping-to-delivery edges total 9,410 while `Confirm delivery -> Close` is 11,200; cancellation inflows are 470 against 530 outflows; `Rework production -> CNC production` (390) is less than `Inspection -> Rework production` (470). Selected paths cover only 89% of volume. So some emitting activity is unrecorded, and the model's own precision is stated as low around emergency and partial-shipment behaviour. Baselines must be rebuilt on a repaired log before any claim of improvement.
- Damage returns are referenced in the evidence (reinforced packaging cuts damage returns 40%) but there is **no return event and no return leg in the DFG**. Return transport and re-manufacture are currently invisible, which biases every packaging-lightweighting idea to look better than it is.
- Case-mix drift will confound before/after comparison; segment-level and variant-level analysis is mandatory.

**Where the impact actually sits** (order of magnitude from the case-mix table, deliberately rounded):
- Made-to-order is ~34% of orders but ~85–90% of energy and ~85% of scrap.
- Critical-downtime Service-contract is ~11% of orders but ~40% of transport CO2e.
- The `MTO -> batch wait -> CNC_B -> inspection -> rework -> ground` variant is ~5% of orders but carries roughly a fifth of all energy and a quarter of all scrap, at 32% late delivery. It is bad on *every* dimension simultaneously — no trade-off is required to attack it.
- Avoidable air in Standard-stock Service-contract (22% SP1 violation rate, ~420 cases, air ≈ 18.5 vs ground ≈ 3.6 kgCO2e) is a low-single-digit percentage of the total transport footprint that costs essentially no service, since the evidence states the promise still allows ground plus priority picking.

## 2. Sustainability-related process indicators

Indicators are defined so each can be computed from the event log (existing attributes marked, new attributes flagged **[new]**), and each environmental indicator is paired with a service/quality counter-indicator to prevent one-sided optimisation.

**Energy**
- E1 Energy per delivered line: `energy_kwh` summed per case / delivered lines. Split by `case_type` and `machine_group`.
- E2 Energy per item vs `batch_size` curve, per machine group — refreshed monthly, because the 2.8→1.3 kWh CNC_A curve is the basis of the batching policy and will shift with part mix.
- E3 **Rework energy share**: energy consumed on cases containing `rework_flag = true` or a `Rework production` event, as a share of total energy. This isolates energy that produced no additional delivered function.
- E4 **Obsolescence energy share [new]**: energy embodied in batch items with no shipment within 14 days (target denominator for the stated 6%).
- E5 Setup/idle energy proxy: energy on `Plan production`/`Batch wait`/`Inspection` events, i.e. non-transforming energy.

**Transport**
- T1 kgCO2e per delivered line and per shipped kg, by `route_mode` and segment.
- T2 **Avoidable-expedite emissions [new]**: emissions difference between the mode used and the greenest mode that met the promise with ≥85% probability, summed over cases where slack was sufficient. This is the operational form of SP1 and the single most decision-relevant transport metric.
- T3 Mode mix per segment, and `expedite_flag` rate — separated into *planned* expedite (Critical) and *recovery* expedite (used to rescue a case that fell behind, detectable as an expedite decision occurring after `Batch wait` or `Backorder wait`).
- T4 **Load factor / empty-return share for dedicated vans [new]** (`carrier_id`, `stops_per_trip`, `return_load_flag`): the evidence says vans often return empty, so emissions per delivered part depend far more on trip sharing than on the mode label.
- T5 Consolidation fill rate and consolidation wait actually incurred (p50/p90) for `consolidated_truck`.
- T6 **Second-leg emissions [new]**: transport CO2e attributable to `Partial shipment` follow-up deliveries and to damage returns.

**Materials**
- M1 `package_material_kg` per delivered line, and per *shipment* (exposes partial shipments).
- M2 **Packaging rework material [new]**: material consumed on the 620 `Rework packaging` entries, most of which is discarded packaging.
- M3 `material_scrap_kg` per good item by `machine_group` × part family; separate first-pass scrap from rework scrap.
- M4 Obsolescence write-off mass **[new]**, in kg and in embodied-energy terms.
- M5 Reinforced-vs-standard packaging split for MTO >8 kg, cross-tabulated with damage returns (feeds Q1).

**Rework and returns**
- R1 First-pass yield at `Inspection`, by `machine_group` (baseline: CNC_B 18% vs CNC_A 9% rework for MTO).
- R2 Rework loop count per case (`Rework production -> CNC production`, `Rework packaging -> Package *`), and loop *depth* (repeat offenders).
- R3 **Inspection dwell time vs defect escape rate [new]**: correlation of inspection duration/queue length with downstream rework or return. The evidence says rushed inspections cause later loops; this indicator makes that causal claim testable.
- R4 **Damage-return rate [new]**, by packaging type × `route_mode` × weight band.

**Batching**
- B1 `batch_size` distribution and **batch fill ratio [new]** (actual size / target size).
- B2 Batch wait p50/p90 by segment, and share of cases exceeding the segment wait cap.
- B3 **Order-backed batch share [new]**: proportion of batch items backed by a firm order rather than a forecast — the leading indicator for obsolescence.
- B4 Heat-treatment batch wait and the associated late-delivery multiplier (baseline 1.7x).
- B5 Batch-wait-to-expedite conversion rate: share of batched cases that later require air/van rescue.

**Service**
- S1 Median and p90 cycle time by segment (baselines: 31/62, 18/41, 94/180, 78/156, 15/30 hours).
- S2 Late-delivery rate vs `promised_lead_time_hours` by segment (7/5/18/13/4%).
- S3 **Slack at routing decision [new]** (`promised_lead_time_hours` minus projected remaining time) and predicted on-time probability — the input SP1 requires and that currently cannot be audited.
- S4 Cancellation-due-to-delay rate and `Partial shipment` rate (currently ~14% of stock picks; 27% of backorder waits end in cancellation).

**Quality**
- Q1c Q1 conformance: MTO >8 kg with reinforced packaging or an explicit inspection low-damage-risk certification event.
- Q2c Packaging/route compatibility failure rate before shipment (root cause of `Rework packaging`).
- Q3c Return and complaint rate per segment, correlated with Q1 violations.

**Resilience**
- X1 R1 compliance: count of *currently feasible* alternative `route_mode` options per Service-contract case **[new]**; alarm when it drops to one.
- X2 Carrier and lane concentration (Herfindahl-type index) for Service-contract volume.
- X3 Machine substitutability: share of MTO parts routable to both CNC_A and CNC_B, and CNC_A queue headroom — the binding constraint on S3.
- X4 Recovery performance: p90 cycle time and late rate during flagged disruption windows versus normal windows.
- X5 Emergency-capacity slack: share of `Emergency production` capacity consumed by *precautionary* rather than true-downtime cases.

**Governance**
- G1 SP1/SP2/Q1 violation rates by segment, with justification-event completeness.
- G2 Override volume and reason-code distribution in the sustainability exception log, plus override *concentration* by user/customer (rubber-stamping detector).

## 3. Intervention options

### S1 — Green shipping recommender (slack-, reliability- and segment-aware)
- **Target.** All shipping decisions; highest value on `Package standard -> Ship air` (620) and `Package reinforced -> Ship air` (800), and the 5% `Stock -> standard pack -> air` variant.
- **Mechanism.** Replaces habit-based mode choice with a computed decision: pick the lowest-emission mode whose on-time probability exceeds the SP1 threshold, using lane-level historical reliability from the log rather than nominal transit times. Converts the 4–6x air penalty into an emission only where slack genuinely requires it.
- **Service/quality/resilience.** Service-neutral by construction *if* the probability model is calibrated; the failure mode is an overconfident model that silently converts green choices into late deliveries. Resilience improves because a ranked mode list is exactly the alternative-route inventory R1 demands — provided the recommender is not allowed to collapse every case onto one cheapest carrier.
- **Evidence to monitor.** T2 avoidable-expedite emissions, calibration curves (predicted vs actual on-time by decile), S2 late rate per segment per lane, X2 carrier concentration, and the recommender's override rate.

### S2 — Dynamic batching with segment wait caps for CNC and heat treatment
- **Target.** `Plan production -> Batch wait` (3,020 of 3,780 planned productions) and heat-treatment batching; the 13% MTO batch variant at 118h/21% late.
- **Mechanism.** Exploits the amortisation curve (CNC_A 2.8→1.3 kWh/item; heat treatment optimal above size 10) but *bounds* the waiting that produces it: a batch releases at the earlier of target fill or the tightest due date in the batch. Caps differ by segment (no batching for Critical, tight cap for Service-contract, wider for MTO Regular), so energy savings are harvested only from cases that own real slack.
- **Service/quality/resilience.** Reduces the 38h batch-wait tail and the 1.7x heat-treatment late multiplier relative to unbounded batching, while keeping most of the energy benefit. Quality-neutral. Resilience improves: shorter committed batch windows mean less work-in-progress frozen mid-process when a disruption hits.
- **Evidence to monitor.** B1 fill ratio (guard against caps producing many half-empty batches that lose the energy benefit *and* the wait benefit), B2 wait p90 vs cap, E2 curve, B5 batch-wait-to-expedite conversion, E4/M4 obsolescence.

### S3 — Shift MTO from CNC_B to CNC_A when queue and due-date slack allow
- **Target.** The 5% `batch wait -> CNC_B -> inspection -> rework -> ground` variant (32 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap, 32% late) and MTO work on CNC_B generally (18% vs 9% rework).
- **Mechanism.** Halving the rework rate removes an entire second pass through CNC and inspection: it cuts energy, scrap *and* cycle time at once. This is waste elimination, not a trade-off — the rework path is the worst performer on every column in the path table.
- **Service/quality/resilience.** Quality clearly better. Service better on average but with a real caveat: CNC_B is faster, so for tight-slack cases CNC_A plus a longer queue can be late where CNC_B would not be. The rule must be conditional on both CNC_A queue length and case slack, not a blanket reassignment. Resilience risk is the important one: concentrating MTO on CNC_A raises single-machine dependency, so CNC_B must be retained as a qualified fallback with periodic validation runs rather than allowed to decay.
- **Evidence to monitor.** R1 first-pass yield per machine × part family (to confirm the 18/9 gap is machine-caused rather than part-mix confounded), CNC_A queue and utilisation, X3 substitutability, S1/S2 for tight-slack MTO cases.

### S4 — Replace air with priority pick + ground for Standard-stock Service-contract
- **Target.** The ~22% SP1 violations in Standard-stock Service-contract (~420 cases); the segment's 7.8 kgCO2e/order versus 4.2 for Standard-stock Regular.
- **Mechanism.** The evidence states these orders are air-shipped while the promise still allows ground plus priority picking. Cycle time is bought with *internal* speed (queue priority at picking, earlier carrier cut-off) instead of with a 4–6x transport mode. Roughly a low-single-digit percentage of the total transport footprint at essentially no service cost — the highest-certainty win available.
- **Service/quality/resilience.** Median cycle time rises somewhat (the segment's 18h median partly reflects air), but the commitment that matters, `promised_lead_time_hours`, is still met; the 5% late rate is the guardrail, not the median. Quality-neutral. Resilience unchanged, and arguably improved because priority picking is a capability that does not depend on one air carrier.
- **Evidence to monitor.** SP1 conformance in this segment, S2 late rate and p90 (not just median), picking-queue impact on *other* segments (priority for one segment is delay for another — a displacement to watch), customer complaints per Service-contract account.

### S5 — Consolidated truck departures twice daily for non-urgent stock orders
- **Target.** `Package standard -> Ship ground` (5,100) and the 42% `Stock -> standard pack -> ground` variant; grows `Consolidated truck` (currently 850).
- **Mechanism.** 35–55% transport CO2e reduction versus individual ground parcels, by raising vehicle load factor. Twice-daily rather than daily departures is the deliberate compromise: it keeps the consolidation wait near the low end of the stated 12–24h band, which is what makes the wait affordable for Standard-stock Regular (31h median, 62h p90).
- **Service/quality/resilience.** Adds 12–24h to affected cases, acceptable only where measured slack exceeds the wait plus a buffer; eligibility must be computed per case, not per segment. Quality-neutral. Resilience risk: concentrating volume onto one consolidation lane and one carrier creates a single failure point, so a parcel-ground fallback must stay contracted and periodically exercised.
- **Evidence to monitor.** T5 fill rate and actual consolidation wait, S2 late rate for consolidated cases, the share of consolidated cases that later need expedite rescue (B5/T3), X2 concentration.

### S6 — Packaging–route compatibility check before shipment
- **Target.** `Rework packaging` (620 entries: 260 from standard, 360 from reinforced) and the loops back into packaging (420 + 130).
- **Mechanism.** Validates packaging choice against `route_mode` and part geometry *before* packing, eliminating double material consumption, double labour and the delay of a repack. Pure waste elimination on material and cycle time with no service trade-off, which is why it belongs in the first wave.
- **Service/quality/resilience.** Service improves (repack delay removed); quality improves (fewer incompatible shipments reaching a carrier); resilience improves slightly, since the check makes mode switching during a carrier disruption safe rather than repack-prone.
- **Evidence to monitor.** M2 packaging rework material, Q2c compatibility failure rate, rework-packaging loop frequency in the DFG, and the check's false-positive rate (over-specifying reinforced packaging would inflate M1).

### S7 — Inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations
- **Target.** `Inspection -> Rework production` (470) and `inspection_cell` queues; concentrated on the CNC_B and heavy-part combinations.
- **Mechanism.** The evidence is explicit that rushed inspections miss defects that later cause packaging rework or customer returns. A risk-targeted gate — longer or dual inspection only for combinations with demonstrated escape rates — converts a small amount of extra inspection time into avoided full re-manufacture. Because a rework loop costs a second CNC pass plus additional scrap, the energy and material arithmetic strongly favours catching the defect early.
- **Service/quality/resilience.** Cycle time rises slightly for gated cases and falls substantially for the cases that would otherwise loop (the 160h/32% rework variant). Quality clearly improves; returns fall. Resilience improves because fewer unplanned rework loops means less capacity consumed by surprises.
- **Evidence to monitor.** R3 dwell time vs escape rate (the core hypothesis), R1 first-pass yield, inspection queue length and its effect on non-gated cases, Q1 violation correlation with returns, R4 damage-return rate.

### S8 — Shared emergency courier lanes replacing some dedicated vans
- **Target.** The 8% `Critical -> emergency production -> reinforced pack -> dedicated van` path at ~52 kgCO2e/order, and specifically the 31% of dedicated-van cases lacking SP2 justification.
- **Mechanism.** The van's emissions problem is a load-factor problem — vans often return empty. Scheduled shared urgent lanes (multi-stop, backhaul-loaded) preserve speed while spreading the trip across several deliveries. The addressable pool is the unjustified subset, not the whole path.
- **Service/quality/resilience.** Speed is largely preserved on fixed lanes but flexibility is not: a shared lane has departure windows, whereas a dedicated van leaves now. For genuine machine-down cases that difference can be decisive, so dedicated vans must remain available on justification. Resilience is two-sided: an additional lane type is an extra option under R1, but outsourcing urgent capacity reduces direct control during disruption.
- **Evidence to monitor.** T4 load factor and empty-return share (without it, "shared" is a label, not a reduction), SP2 justification completeness and quality, Critical-segment late rate and p90 (4%/30h baseline as a hard floor), X5 emergency-capacity slack.

### S9 — Split Critical-downtime into true downtime and precautionary urgent
- **Target.** The 11% Critical-downtime segment (~40% of transport CO2e) and the 760 `Emergency production` cases.
- **Mechanism.** Today one label buys the most expensive routing and production treatment for a heterogeneous population. Distinguishing an asset that is actually stopped from a precautionary urgent order lets the truly premium treatment be concentrated where the (out-of-boundary but real) avoided-downtime benefit justifies it, while precautionary cases move to fast-but-shared options. Note the log already shows a lower-impact alternative exists — `Critical -> stock/ground exception` at 4.8 kgCO2e — but at 9% late versus 3%, which is why it must be applied selectively rather than broadly.
- **Service/quality/resilience.** Service preserved for true downtime; deliberately relaxed for precautionary cases, which is acceptable only if the classification is accurate. Resilience improves materially: freeing emergency production and van capacity from precautionary demand means real capacity is available when a genuine multi-customer disruption occurs.
- **Evidence to monitor.** Classification drift over time (share labelled "true downtime"), late rate and escalation/complaint rate within each new sub-segment, X5, reclassification-after-the-fact events, and correlation between label and actual customer downtime where reported.

### S10 — Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular made-to-order customers
- **Target.** MTO Regular (22% of orders, 94h median, 18% late) at the `Receive order` / `Availability check` decision point.
- **Mechanism.** Attacks the root cause rather than the symptom: much expedite and un-batched production exists because promises are tighter than the customer needs. Offering a slightly later, explicitly greener date creates the slack that S2 and S5 then convert into real energy and transport savings. It also reduces the 18% late rate by making promises achievable, which is itself an emissions win because late cases trigger recovery expedite.
- **Service/quality/resilience.** Formal service metrics improve (promises are kept), though perceived speed may fall for customers who decline the trade. Quality-neutral. Resilience improves through less schedule stress and fewer rescue shipments.
- **Evidence to monitor.** Acceptance rate of green dates, realised slack actually used for batching/consolidation rather than absorbed by queues, cancellation rate (a rise signals the offer is being pushed too hard), S2 late rate, and revenue/order-loss signals.

### S11 — Reduce partial shipments by changing availability-check rules
- **Target.** The 7% `Stock -> partial shipment -> ground + backorder` variant (79h median, 24% late, 7.2 kgCO2e ≈ exactly double the single-leg ground figure) and `Pick from stock -> Partial shipment` (1,010, about 14% of stock picks), including the 220 backorder waits of which 60 cancel.
- **Mechanism.** A partial shipment duplicates the whole downstream chain: two packaging events, two transport legs, two delivery confirmations. Better availability logic at the check — real-time inbound visibility, short hold-for-completion where slack allows, substitute-part offers — collapses two shipments into one.
- **Service/quality/resilience.** Nuanced: partial shipment sometimes *protects* a customer who urgently needs the available line item, so the rule must hold for completion only when slack permits and no line is urgent. Done crudely it converts a 24%-late partial into a 100%-late complete shipment. Quality-neutral; fewer handling events slightly reduce damage exposure. Resilience: holding for completion increases exposure to supply delay, so a forced-release timer is required.
- **Evidence to monitor.** Partial-shipment rate, shipments per order, backorder-wait duration and its cancellation conversion (currently ~27%), late rate for held-complete orders, M1 per shipment, T6 second-leg emissions.

### S12 — Sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides
- **Target.** All three policy violation populations: SP1 (22% in Standard-stock Service-contract), SP2 (31% missing justification on vans), Q1 (correlated with return/rework loops).
- **Mechanism.** No direct emissions reduction; it is the control layer that makes every other intervention durable. A structured override with reason codes turns invisible policy erosion into a measurable, attributable stream, and — importantly for low model precision — makes exception behaviour during disruptions explicit in the log instead of appearing as unexplained variant sprawl.
- **Service/quality/resilience.** Neutral to positive; the one risk is friction that pushes urgent decisions off-system. Justification must be a fast structured reason code, not free text, and must never block a genuine downtime response — record-and-review rather than approve-then-act.
- **Evidence to monitor.** G1/G2 override volume, reason-code mix, override concentration by user/customer (rubber-stamping), and the recurrence rate of the same override, which indicates a policy that needs changing rather than overriding.

## 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching rebound: waiting converts into expediting.** The energy curve (2.8 → 1.3 kWh/item) is real, but the 38h median batch wait consumes the slack that would have allowed ground shipping. A case that saves ~1.5 kWh and then ships air instead of ground trades a small energy gain for a 4–6x transport penalty. The `MTO -> CNC immediately -> ... -> air` variant (24 kWh, 23 kgCO2e) shows the combination already occurs. *Detection:* B5 conversion rate — measure `expedite_flag` and mode choice conditional on realised batch wait, not in aggregate.

2. **Batching-driven obsolescence.** Filling batches to reach the efficient size invites make-to-forecast items, and 6% of batch-produced items are not shipped within 14 days when forecasts are wrong. Those items carry 100% of their energy, scrap and packaging with zero delivered function — the embodied burden of one obsolete part can exceed the per-item savings of many correctly forecast ones, especially given that upstream material production sits outside the boundary and is the dominant lifecycle term. *Guard:* B3 order-backed batch share, and forbid forecast-fill for high-variance parts.

3. **Transport modal displacement through failed consolidation.** Consolidation cuts 35–55% per parcel but adds 12–24h. Applied to cases without genuine slack, it produces late deliveries whose recovery is a dedicated van or air shipment — plus goodwill costs. The net effect can be higher emissions than doing nothing, concentrated in a minority of cases and therefore invisible in a mode-mix average. *Guard:* track emissions of the *case*, end to end, including rescue legs, not emissions per shipping event.

4. **Packaging lightweighting displaced into damage returns.** Halving `package_material_kg` by dropping reinforced packaging looks like an immediate material win, but reinforced packaging cuts damage returns 40% for MTO heavy parts. A return means a reverse transport leg, a re-package, often a re-manufacture, and the log has *no return event*, so the saving is measured while the cost is not. Q1 violations already correlate with return/rework loops. Relaxing Q1 for green reasons is the single most tempting false economy in this process. *Guard:* create R4 damage-return tracking *before* touching packaging specifications.

5. **Rework displacement from a rushed inspection cell.** If cycle-time pressure from batching caps or consolidation cut-offs lands on `inspection_cell`, inspections are rushed, defects escape, and the evidence states this feeds packaging rework and customer returns. The process then pays a full second CNC pass — the most energy- and scrap-intensive event available — to save minutes of inspection. *Guard:* R3 dwell-vs-escape correlation with inspection queue length as a leading indicator.

6. **Capacity displacement from machine reassignment.** S3 does not create CNC_A capacity; it moves demand. The CNC_B queue shortens, the CNC_A queue lengthens, batch waits on CNC_A grow, and the batching rebound (risk 1) is amplified. Worse, if CNC_B volume falls far enough that its qualification, tooling or operator skill decays, the fallback disappears and a CNC_A outage becomes a full MTO stoppage — a resilience loss caused by a quality improvement. *Guard:* X3 substitutability plus mandatory minimum CNC_B qualification volume.

7. **Customer-behaviour rebound and urgency inflation.** If green defaults slow the standard offer, customers learn to declare urgency, invoke Service-contract escalation, or renegotiate contracts toward tighter SLAs. The result is structurally *more* air and van volume than before, locked into contracts. S9's reclassification is exposed to the same drift: "precautionary" quietly migrates back to "true downtime". Similarly, an SP2 justification field with no audit becomes a dropdown selected by reflex. *Guard:* monitor segment-share drift, escalation frequency, override concentration by user and customer, and label-change events over time.

8. **Cancellation as phantom sustainability.** Cancelled cases (530 reaching END, plus 60 from backorder wait) improve every per-delivered-order metric while representing pure loss and probable leakage to a competitor who ships the part anyway. Any intervention that increases waiting can "improve" the dashboard by shedding demand. *Guard:* make cancellation and obsolescence explicit terms in the sustainability scorecard, with wasted energy and material charged to them.

9. **Intensity-versus-absolute rebound.** Every indicator in §2 is a per-line intensity. Volume growth, or a case-mix shift toward Standard-stock, can improve every intensity metric while total kWh, kgCO2e and scrap rise. *Guard:* report absolute totals alongside intensities, and analyse per segment and per variant to avoid Simpson's-paradox conclusions.

10. **Accounting displacement to third parties.** Replacing an own dedicated van with a 3PL shared lane may move emissions from Scope 1 to Scope 3 without reducing anything physical, particularly if the 3PL runs its own empty returns. *Guard:* require T4 load-factor evidence from the carrier; treat unverified shared-lane claims as unproven.

## 6. Balanced recommendation

The redesign is one coherent package with a single organising principle: **eliminate waste loops first, then spend measured slack — never assumed slack — on lower-impact modes and batches, with hard service floors per segment and every green default expressed as a governed, reversible decision rule.** Isolated green measures fail here precisely because the evidence shows the process trades impact between stages; the package therefore pairs each impact-reducing move with the slack that funds it and the fallback that protects it.

**Wave 1 — Waste elimination (no service trade-off; start immediately).** S6 packaging–route compatibility, S7 targeted inspection gate, S3 conditional CNC_B → CNC_A, and S12 exception logging with fixed data-quality repairs. Rationale: the rework variant is ~5% of orders yet carries roughly a fifth of energy and a quarter of scrap *and* is the worst performer on cycle time (160h) and lateness (32%). Removing it improves environment, service and quality simultaneously, so it faces no stakeholder trade-off and builds credibility for the harder waves. S12 goes first, not last, because without a baseline exception stream the later waves cannot be evaluated.

**Wave 2 — Slack-aware routing (service-neutral by construction).** S4 for Standard-stock Service-contract, S1 recommender, S11 tightened availability rules, then S5 twice-daily consolidation. Sequencing matters: S4 is the highest-certainty win because the prompt states the promise is already satisfiable by ground plus priority picking, so it is essentially free emissions reduction and demonstrates that a green default need not hurt service. S1 generalises it, and only once the on-time probability model is calibrated does S5 use it to gate consolidation eligibility. All three depend on a new computed `slack_at_routing_hours`; **no case is diverted to a slower mode without measured slack exceeding the added wait plus a buffer**. S11 sits here because eliminating a duplicated shipping-and-packaging chain is large-effect, but it needs a forced-release timer so held orders cannot become worse than the 24%-late partials they replace.

**Wave 3 — Structural demand and capacity (highest value, highest care).** S9 Critical segmentation, S2 dynamic batching with segment wait caps, S10 carbon-aware promises for MTO Regular, S8 shared lanes limited initially to the SP2-unjustified van population. S9 addresses the largest transport pool and S2 the largest energy pool, but both depend on Wave 2's slack measurement and Wave 1's loop elimination to be safe: capping batches without a calibrated shipping recommender simply relocates emissions into rescue air freight, and batching a process with a 9–18% rework rate batches the defects too.

**Deliberate non-actions, and why.**
- **Do not route true downtime to ground.** The `Critical -> stock/ground exception` path at 4.8 kgCO2e is the lowest-emission Critical option and it triples lateness (9% vs 3%). Beyond the service constraint, the lifecycle argument in §1 applies: the avoided customer downtime waste sits outside our boundary but is real, and it plausibly exceeds the ~47 kgCO2e difference. Dedicated vans stay available for justified downtime; only the unjustified 31% and the precautionary cases identified by S9 are in scope.
- **Do not relax Q1 or lightweight MTO heavy-part packaging.** With no return event in the log, any material saving would be booked while the 40% damage-return penalty stayed invisible.
- **Do not ban air.** Air stays legitimate where slack is genuinely insufficient — a Service-contract case recovering from a supply delay is a different decision from a Standard-stock order shipped by air out of habit. The distinction is slack and segment, computed per case, not the mode label.
- **Do not let CNC_B decay** into an unusable machine, and do not consolidate all ground volume onto one lane. Both would buy environmental gains with resilience.

**Service, quality and resilience floors** (any breach triggers automatic relaxation of the responsible rule, not a review meeting): per-segment late-delivery rate must not exceed its baseline (7 / 5 / 18 / 13 / 4%); per-segment p90 cycle time must not exceed baseline plus a pre-agreed tolerance, with the Critical p90 of 30h treated as non-negotiable; cancellation and partial-shipment rates must not rise; every Service-contract case must retain at least one feasible alternative route (R1) at all times; and rework/return rates must fall, never merely hold.

**Why this is balanced.** It attacks impact where impact actually is — Made-to-order for energy and scrap, Critical-downtime for transport, and habitual air in Standard-stock Service-contract — rather than spreading uniform green rules across a heterogeneous case mix. It funds every environmental gain from one of three sources that do not consume service: eliminated defect loops, eliminated duplicate shipments, and genuine measured slack (including slack newly created by honest promise negotiation). It protects quality by *strengthening* rather than trading away Q1 and inspection. It protects resilience by keeping premium options alive as governed exceptions with recorded justifications, maintaining machine and carrier fallbacks deliberately, and freeing emergency capacity from precautionary demand. And it treats the trade-offs the evidence actually documents — batching versus waiting, air versus lateness, packaging versus damage — as bounded, monitored decision rules with automatic reversal, instead of as one-off design choices.

## 7. Validation and governance plan

**Step 0 — Fix the measurement system before claiming anything.** The DFG does not reconcile (9,410 shipping-to-delivery edges against 11,200 `Confirm delivery -> Close`; 470 cancellation inflows against 530 outflows; 470 rework entries against 390 returns to CNC), and selected paths cover 89% of volume. Repair event capture, then freeze a clean pre-change baseline at segment *and* variant level, including absolute totals. Add the attributes the redesign depends on: `slack_at_routing_hours`, predicted on-time probability and the mode actually recommended versus chosen, `route_alternatives_count`, `batch_fill_ratio` and `order_backed_flag`, `carrier_id` with load factor and empty-return flag, an SP2 `justification` event with reason codes, a damage/return event with a reverse-transport record, and an obsolescence write-off event.

**Validation design.** Stage the rollout so causality is recoverable: split by carrier lane, machine group and region rather than switching globally, keeping matched control populations. Compare like-for-like *variants*, never aggregates, because case-mix drift and the deliberate segment reclassification in S9 will otherwise manufacture both false wins and false alarms. Flag disruption windows and analyse them separately — the model's precision is lowest exactly there. Use variant analysis and conformance checking on the post-change log to confirm the intended paths are the ones actually being executed, since low precision means new behaviour may appear as variant sprawl rather than as the designed route.

**Paired metrics to defeat rebound.** Every environmental indicator is reviewed next to its rebound counterpart, and no gain is accepted on a single-sided reading: energy per item *with* absolute energy and obsolescence write-offs; batch wait *with* the batch-wait-to-expedite conversion rate; consolidation share *with* the rate of subsequent air or van rescue on those same cases; packaging mass *with* damage returns; scrap *with* rework loop counts; per-delivered-line intensity *with* cancellation volume. The controlling question for Waves 2 and 3 is always case-level: did total case emissions fall, including rescue legs and return legs?

**Conformance monitoring.**
- *SP1* — automated alignment between the routing decision and the ≥85% on-time-probability rule, reported weekly by segment. Two things are audited, not one: the violation rate (baseline 22% in Standard-stock Service-contract) and the *calibration* of the 85% probability estimate itself, via predicted-versus-actual on-time curves per lane. A falling violation rate on a miscalibrated model is a service failure in disguise. Recalibrate quarterly and after any carrier change.
- *SP2* — the justification event becomes mandatory at van booking, using structured reason codes, and is recorded rather than approval-gated so a genuine downtime response is never delayed. Track completeness (baseline 31% missing) toward near-zero, and separately audit justification *quality*: reason-code distribution, concentration by requester and by customer, and repeat use for the same account — the rubber-stamping signature.
- *Q1* — conformance check that Made-to-order parts over 8 kg carry reinforced packaging or an explicit inspection low-damage-risk certification event. Because Q1 violations already correlate with return/rework loops, report Q1 conformance jointly with R4 damage returns and packaging-rework frequency so the causal chain is continuously re-tested rather than assumed.

**Segment-level service monitoring.** A weekly dashboard of median and p90 cycle time, late-delivery rate, partial-shipment rate and cancellation rate for each of the five original segments plus the two new Critical sub-segments, each shown against its floor from §6. Breaching a floor for two consecutive periods triggers an automatic circuit breaker that relaxes the responsible rule — widening a batch wait cap, restoring parcel-ground eligibility, or returning a customer group to its prior routing — with the relaxation itself logged as an exception. Also monitor the displacement that priority picking creates for non-prioritised segments, and watch S9 label drift as a first-class service risk.

**Exception governance.** The S12 exception log is triaged weekly by a standing group with operations, quality, logistics and sustainability representation. Reason codes are reviewed for concentration and for recurrence: a repeatedly used override is evidence that the policy, not the behaviour, needs changing, and should feed a quarterly policy revision rather than an escalating enforcement campaign. Decision rights are explicit — who may override SP1, who may authorise a dedicated van, and what evidence is required — with overrides visible to the requester's manager. Resilience is verified actively rather than assumed: periodic simulated carrier and machine outages confirm that R1 alternatives are genuinely available and that CNC_B remains qualified, and route-mode diversity per Service-contract customer is reported monthly alongside carrier concentration.

**Review cadence.** Weekly service and conformance monitoring; monthly environmental indicators with absolute totals and rebound pairs; quarterly recalibration of route-reliability and batch-energy curves plus policy revision from override patterns; annual boundary review to reconsider what remains outside scope — in particular upstream material burden, damage returns, and the avoided customer-downtime impact, since these are precisely the terms that currently make our internal ledger a biased guide to real lifecycle impact.
