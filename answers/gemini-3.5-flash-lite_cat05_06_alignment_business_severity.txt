## 1. Materiality Framework

To evaluate process deviations in insurance-claim payments, raw conformance cost (edit distance under a uniform cost function of $c=1$) is insufficient. This framework defines how business severity is evaluated independently of technical alignment cost.

*   **Control Objective:** Prevent unauthorized, fraudulent, or erroneous financial disbursements while maintaining operational efficiency and regulatory compliance.
*   **Amount/Financial Exposure:** The absolute monetary value at risk (EUR) in the transaction. High-value claims (>EUR 50,000) carry catastrophic exposure if controls fail.
*   **Reversibility:** Once a payment (`Pay Claim`) is executed and funds leave the house, reversal is difficult, costly, and frequently impossible. Pre-payment control failures are irreversible once executed.
*   **Timing:** Regulatory and policy mandates require specific actions within strict windows (e.g., claimant notification within 24 hours). Timing failures compound customer harm and regulatory exposure.
*   **Customer/Regulatory Harm:** Direct negative impact on the claimant (e.g., delayed notifications, unhandled withdrawals) or breach of statutory/fiduciary mandates (e.g., paying a high-risk claim without fraud screening).
*   **Recurrence:** Whether an anomaly is an isolated human error, a recurring technical artifact, or a systemic design flaw in the automation logic.
*   **Evidence Reliability:** The trustworthiness and immutability of the audit trail (e.g., core system logs vs. mutable text fields in CRM vs. external adjuster documents).
*   **Model Scope:** Whether the normative control-flow model is designed for all intake events or strictly for successfully processed, payable claims.

### How Confidence Differs from Severity
**Severity** measures the potential financial, regulatory, or operational damage of a control breakdown. **Confidence** measures the reliability, completeness, and immutability of the evidence underpinning that assessment. A case can have *high severity* (e.g., a massive payout bypassing fraud screens) and *high confidence* (fully logged in the core system), or *high severity* and *low confidence* (a payment recorded with ambiguous or mutable metadata where actual execution details are obscured). Conversely, a minor logging artifact has *low severity* despite *high confidence*.

## 3. Repeated Deviation Patterns

1.  **Data-Aware Auto-Approval Policy Violations (Silent Compliance Breakers)**
    *   *Cases:* CL-609, CL-610.
    *   *Frequency Evidence:* 4 cases in the dataset exhibit fully synchronous paths while violating monetary limits, risk thresholds, or bank-change rules (P1/P2).
    *   *Business Risk:* High financial exposure and fraud vulnerability (paying out high amounts or diverted funds without human review).
    *   *Causes:* Process model and alignment tooling evaluate control flow only, remaining blind to data payloads (amount, risk score, bank change timestamps).
    *   *Confidence:* High.
    *   *Recommended Owner:* Risk & Compliance / Rules Engine Product Owner.

2.  **Notification Transport & Logging Duplication**
    *   *Cases:* CL-603.
    *   *Frequency Evidence:* 74 cases monthly feature a duplicate `L(Notify Claimant)` immediately following a synchronous notification with identical message IDs.
    *   *Business Risk:* None (purely technical telemetry artifact).
    *   *Causes:* Message broker retry logic logging duplicate delivery acknowledgments.
    *   *Confidence:* High.
    *   *Recommended Owner:* Integration Engineering / IT Infrastructure.

3.  **External Adjuster Document Decoupling**
    *   *Cases:* CL-606.
    *   *Frequency Evidence:* 9 high-value cases feature external adjuster sign-offs without core `Assess Loss` logs; 2 lack stable document-to-case identifiers.
    *   *Business Risk:* Audit trail opacity and potential unverified assessments.
    *   *Causes:* External adjusters operate in external portals without direct API integration into core event logs.
    *   *Confidence:* Medium.
    *   *Recommended Owner:* Claims Operations / Vendor Management.

4.  **UI and Workbench Telemetry Pollution**
    *   *Cases:* CL-601.
    *   *Frequency Evidence:* Widespread across user-facing workstations.
    *   *Business Risk:* Clutters audit logs, distorts process mining models.
    *   *Causes:* Unfiltered capture of frontend lifecycle events (`Open Workbench`, `Autosave`, `Close Workbench`).
    *   *Confidence:* High.
    *   *Recommended Owner:* Process Mining Center of Excellence (COE) / Data Engineering.

5.  **Out-of-Scope Intake Exceptions (Denials and Withdrawals)**
    *   *Cases:* CL-607, CL-612.
    *   *Frequency Evidence:* Account for the majority of alignments with raw cost >= 4.
    *   *Business Risk:* Skews process efficiency metrics and inflates anomaly counts.
    *   *Causes:* Normative model built exclusively for successful, payable claims; fails to capture negative/terminating paths.
    *   *Confidence:* High.
    *   *Recommended Owner:* Process Architecture / Business Process Management (BPM) Team.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

1.  **CL-602 (Verbal Override vs. Unrecorded System Bypass):**
    *   *Hypothesis A:* The manual approver performed an unrecorded offline fraud check and improperly bypassed the system flag.
    *   *Hypothesis B:* The fraud screening module suffered an API outage, and management forced a manual sign-off without fallback documentation.
    *   *Evidence Test:* Check system availability logs for the fraud screening microservice at timestamp $T-10$ minutes, and inspect the approver's email/chat archives for verbal authorization records.

2.  **CL-604 (Premature Payment vs. Timestamp Skew):**
    *   *Hypothesis A:* The payment gateway executed settlement prematurely before manual approval was captured in the core database.
    *   *Hypothesis B:* Clock synchronization drift between the payment server and the workflow engine created a false sequence order.
    *   *Evidence Test:* Inspect NTP server logs across payment gateway and workflow engines; audit transaction transaction-isolation logs in the database.

3.  **CL-608 (Missing Notification vs. Alternative Channel Delivery):**
    *   *Hypothesis A:* The notification failed to reach the claimant, but a CRM-linked portal message satisfied policy P4.
    *   *Hypothesis B:* The CRM link is a retrospective manual entry fabricating compliance after an audit query.
    *   *Evidence Test:* Examine the immutable message-delivery receipt timestamp from the secure portal gateway and verify database audit trails on the CRM text field modification history.

4.  **CL-610 (Bank-Change Blindness in Rules Engine):**
    *   *Hypothesis A:* The auto-approval rules engine does not query the beneficiary bank change ledger.
    *   *Hypothesis B:* The bank change event was logged *after* the auto-approval evaluation due to race conditions.
    *   *Evidence Test:* Review the JSON payload evaluated by the rules engine during CL-610 against the master customer database audit log for account modification timestamps.

5.  **CL-611 (Label Divergence vs. Process Variation):**
    *   *Hypothesis A:* `Assess Damage` is an obsolete UI label that was renamed in the core system but left unmapped in process mining dictionaries.
    *   *Hypothesis B:* `Assess Damage` represents a specialized preliminary engineering review distinct from normative `Assess Loss`.
    *   *Evidence Test:* Inspect the standard operating procedure (SOP) document referenced in CL-611 to determine if damage assessment is a formal pre-requisite or synonym for loss assessment.

6.  **CL-606 (External Adjuster Traceability):**
    *   *Hypothesis A:* External adjuster reports are systematically unlinked from core cases due to missing case reference IDs in vendor portals.
    *   *Hypothesis B:* Adjusters manually attach documents days later, causing temporary log gaps.
    *   *Evidence Test:* Audit PDF metadata of the 9 external assessment reports for embedded case ID strings and cross-reference upload timestamps with core system creation times.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Raw alignment cost measures the edit distance (insertions, deletions, substitutions) between an event log trace and a normative process model under a uniform cost function ($c=1$). It is fundamentally a structural graph metric, not a business risk score. 

Five specific supplied cases illustrate why treating alignment cost as risk fails:

1.  **CL-610 (Zero Cost, Maximum Risk):** Raw cost is $0$ because the trace perfectly matches the normative model's happy path (`Auto Approve`). However, business risk is catastrophic: a beneficiary bank change occurred one hour before payment, violating policy P1 and exposing the firm to potential wire fraud. Alignment cost is blind to data payloads.
2.  **CL-607 (High Cost, Zero Risk):** Raw cost is $4$ due to multiple model-required steps (`Assess Loss`, `Auto Approve`, `Pay Claim`) that never occurred. Yet, business risk is zero because the claim failed coverage pre-screen and was legitimately denied. The cost reflects model scoping mismatch (P6), not operational failure.
3.  **CL-601 (Moderate Cost, Harmless Noise):** Raw cost is $3$ due to UI telemetry events (`Open Workbench`, `Autosave`, `Close Workbench`). These events represent frontend interaction noise (P5), carrying zero financial, compliance, or customer risk.
4.  **CL-602 (Low Cost, Severe Control Failure):** Raw cost is $1$ because the model expected a `Fraud Screen` event and registered a missing model move ($M$). In business terms, this single missing step represents a catastrophic violation of policy P2 on a EUR 480,000 high-risk claim. A low numerical cost masks a severe control breakdown.
5.  **CL-612 (High Cost, Operational Drop-Out):** Raw cost is $4$ because the claimant withdrew after assessment, triggering model-side penalties for skipped approvals and payments. The business reality is a benign customer withdrawal, but the process model lacks an exception branch, converting a standard business drop-out into an apparent high-cost anomaly.
