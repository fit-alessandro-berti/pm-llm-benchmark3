# 1. Risk indicators

**Overall reading before the detail.** The evidence does not point to a single explanation. It is best read as a mixture: (a) two emergency channels (Buyer_Carla/Approver_Gita, Buyer_Dan/Manager_Hugo) whose four-eyes exceptions and fast approvals are largely explained by designed emergency handling plus informal specialization, with a genuine but procedural weakness in retrospective justification; (b) one cluster (VendorMaster_Lena/AP_Kim on vendor family V-77x) whose pattern is *not* explained by emergency handling and carries the highest payment-diversion risk profile, while still having a plausible benign explanation (banking-platform migration) that the log cannot currently confirm; (c) several indicators that are partly or wholly artefacts of data quality (batch-uploaded justifications, shared Approver_Gita account, Bot_PO/human mapping error in legacy_erp). High co-working frequency on its own (Aline/Elena, Boris/Felix, Bot_Match/AP_Kim) is not treated as a risk indicator.

Severity is assessed per subgroup, not per indicator type, because the same indicator means different things in different channels.

# 2. Benign alternative explanations

## Pattern A: Carla/Gita and Dan/Hugo – fast same-person or near-same-person approvals in emergency cases

1. **Designed emergency channel operating as intended.** Medical supplies and weekend repairs require same-day commitments; Gita is the delegated urgent approver and Hugo is the exceptions manager. Supporting evidence: emergency_flag=True in 79–88%, low vendor concentration, low manual share, purchase_category consistent with medical/facilities, weekend timestamps for Dan/Hugo. Refuting evidence: emergency_flag=True cases with non-urgent purchase categories, emergency cases created Monday–Friday during business hours with no time pressure, or high-amount cases just under the next approval threshold.
2. **Informal specialization and pre-agreed rush rules.** Carla and Dan are specialists; Gita and Hugo may pre-approve categories or vendors (framework contracts) so that individual approvals are genuinely quick. Supporting: contract_type = framework/call-off for most fast cases, approvals that reference a standing authorization, amounts within the framework limit. Refuting: contract_type = spot/one-off with new vendors and fast approvals.
3. **Attribution artefact.** Some "same-person" Create/Approve pairs are Bot_PO creations mislabelled in legacy_erp, or assistant-entered approvals under the shared Gita account. Supporting: source_system=legacy_erp and approved_by ≠ org:resource on the Create PO event; entered_by ≠ "Approver_Gita" on the approval. Refuting: violations persist after recomputing on entered_by/approved_by.

## Pattern B: Same-person Emergency Override + Retrospective Justification (Hugo, Gita)

1. **Documentation backlog captured late in bulk.** The justification was decided or discussed at the time (e-mail, ticket) but only typed into the system at quarter close by the same person who did the override. Supporting: contemporaneous e-mails or tickets dated within 3 working days of the override; upload metadata showing the batch was prepared from an earlier document. Refuting: no contemporaneous record exists; justification text is generic or copied across cases.
2. **Misunderstanding of the rule.** Managers may believe the override holder is the correct person to document the justification. Supporting: consistent same-person pattern across *all* of that manager's overrides (not selective), training records showing the rule was not communicated. Refuting: same-person justification only for a subset with specific vendors or high amounts.

## Pattern C: Lena/Kim – Change Vendor Bank Details → Payment Approval within minutes, V-77x concentration, manual uploads

1. **Vendor family V-77x banking-platform migration.** Legitimate mass maintenance of bank details for a multi-region vendor family, done by upload, with payments already queued and approved as soon as master data was corrected. Supporting: a maintenance ticket dated before the changes listing the affected vendor records; new bank details matching a vendor-issued letter/portal notification; new IBANs belonging to the same legal entity and bank as the vendor's declared new platform; changes affecting all V-77x records, not just those with pending high-value payments. Refuting: bank changes only on vendor records with imminent high-value payments; new account holder name differing from vendor; accounts changed and then changed back after payment (the Lena→Kim→Lena loop).
2. **Role design plus workload routing.** AP_Kim is the designated approver for higher-risk suppliers; V-77x may be classified higher-risk, so every V-77x payment lands with Kim regardless of who changed the bank details. Lena is the bank-change specialist. Their co-occurrence is then structural. Supporting: supplier risk classification listing V-77x as high-risk; Kim approving V-77x payments with no preceding bank change at the same rate. Refuting: Kim approving V-77x payments only when Lena changed the details.
3. **Payment-file rejection and correction.** The Lena→Kim→Lena loop may be a correction after a payment file bounced due to format issues on the new platform. Supporting: bank return/rejection messages, corrections that change format (e.g., BIC) not account holder. Refuting: corrections that revert to a different account after funds were released.

## Pattern D: Vendor Check + Change Vendor Bank Details by same person (Lena, Marc)

1. **Role design.** Vendor-master team performs both. Supporting: job descriptions, access-role matrix. Refuting: a formal SoD policy that assigns these to different roles.
2. **Small-team practicality.** Two-person team cannot always split. Supporting: leave calendars coinciding with the 31 cases.

## Pattern E: Subcontracting loops (Gita→Carla→Gita etc.)

1. **Clarification loop.** Approver returns the PO for a correction, buyer amends, approver approves. Supporting: change history on the PO between the two approver events; approval comments. Refuting: no PO change between events and identical content.
2. **Normal process shape.** Routine pairs (Aline/Elena, Boris/Felix) show the same loop at similar frequency, so the shape is ordinary. Supporting: comparable loop rates per 100 cases across all pairs.

## Pattern F: Temp_Nina → Buyer_Carla handover in 4 minutes

1. **End-of-quarter backlog clearance** with a temp keying requests and the emergency buyer picking them up. Supporting: concentration in the final weeks of the quarter; Temp_Nina's access limited to Create Request. Refuting: Temp_Nina able to modify vendor or amount fields, or requests flagged emergency without an emergency category.

# 4. Network and conformance analyses

All snippets assume `pandas` and `pm4py` (2.7+). The first step is to build two resource views: the raw `org:resource` and a corrected resource that uses `entered_by`/`approved_by` and fixes the legacy_erp bot mapping. Every subsequent analysis is run on both views; differences between them quantify how much of each indicator is a data-quality artefact.

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

df = pd.read_csv("p2p_events.csv", parse_dates=["time:timestamp"])
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id="case:concept:name",
                            activity_key="concept:name",
                            timestamp_key="time:timestamp")
# keep one event per activity instance
df = df[df["lifecycle:transition"].fillna("complete").eq("complete")]

BOTS = {"Bot_PO", "Bot_Match"}

# corrected resource view
df["res_raw"] = df["org:resource"]
df["res_fixed"] = df["org:resource"]
# 1) legacy_erp mapping error: bot-created POs mislabelled with the approver
m = (df["source_system"] == "legacy_erp") & (df["concept:name"] == "Create Purchase Order") \
    & df["approved_by"].notna() & (df["org:resource"] == df["approved_by"])
df.loc[m, "res_fixed"] = "Bot_PO"
# 2) shared account: use entered_by where it differs from the account
m = (df["org:resource"] == "Approver_Gita") & df["entered_by"].notna() \
    & (df["entered_by"] != "Approver_Gita")
df.loc[m, "res_fixed"] = df.loc[m, "entered_by"]
df["shared_account_flag"] = m
# 3) approvals: prefer approved_by when present
m = df["concept:name"].isin(["Approve Purchase Order", "Payment Approval"]) & df["approved_by"].notna()
df.loc[m, "res_fixed"] = df.loc[m, "approved_by"]

def view(df, col):
    d = df.copy()
    d["org:resource"] = d[col]
    return d

raw_log, fixed_log = view(df, "res_raw"), view(df, "res_fixed")
```

## 4.1 Four-eyes violations with `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle`

```python
PAIRS = [("Create Purchase Order", "Approve Purchase Order"),
         ("Change Vendor Bank Details", "Payment Approval"),
         ("Emergency Override", "Retrospective Justification"),
         ("Vendor Check", "Change Vendor Bank Details")]  # last one is a design check, not a rule

def four_eyes_report(log, label):
    humans = log[~log["org:resource"].isin(BOTS)]
    rows = []
    for a1, a2 in PAIRS:
        viol = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
            humans, a1, a2,
            activity_key="concept:name", resource_key="org:resource",
            timestamp_key="time:timestamp", case_id_key="case:concept:name")
        cases = viol["case:concept:name"].unique()
        attrs = (log[log["case:concept:name"].isin(cases)]
                 .groupby("case:concept:name")
                 .agg(emergency=("emergency_flag", "max"),
                      amount=("amount_band", "first"),
                      vendor=("vendor_id", "first"),
                      manual=("source_system", lambda s: (s == "manual_upload").mean()),
                      legacy=("source_system", lambda s: (s == "legacy_erp").any()),
                      resources=("org:resource", lambda s: sorted(set(s)))))
        attrs["pair"] = f"{a1} + {a2}"
        attrs["view"] = label
        rows.append(attrs)
    return pd.concat(rows)

fe_raw = four_eyes_report(raw_log, "raw")
fe_fixed = four_eyes_report(fixed_log, "fixed")
# artefact share = cases violating in raw view but not in fixed view
artefacts = set(fe_raw.index) - set(fe_fixed.index)
print(len(fe_raw), len(fe_fixed), len(artefacts))
```

Interpretation rule: a violation that disappears in the fixed view is reported as a data-quality finding; a violation that persists is a control exception and goes to the prioritization in Section 3.

## 4.2 Handover, working-together, and subcontracting networks

```python
# whole log, then per segment (emergency / non-emergency / by source_system)
def sna_bundle(log, tag):
    hw = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log, beta=0)      # direct handovers
    wt = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log)
    sc = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(log, n=2)          # A -> B -> A

hw_all, wt_all, sc_all = sna_bundle(fixed_log, "all")

## 4.5 Declarative and log-skeleton checks for retrospective justification

# The 3-working-day and different-resource conditions are not expressible in Declare/log
# skeleton directly, so complete the rule in pandas:
ov = fixed_log[fixed_log["concept:name"] == "Emergency Override"] \
        .groupby("case:concept:name").agg(t_ov=("time:timestamp", "min"), r_ov=("org:resource", "first"))
rj = fixed_log[fixed_log["concept:name"] == "Retrospective Justification"] \
        .groupby("case:concept:name").agg(t_rj=("time:timestamp", "min"), r_rj=("org:resource", "first"),
                                           src_rj=("source_system", "first"))
rule = ov.join(rj, how="left")
rule["missing"] = rule["t_rj"].isna()
rule["busdays"] = [np.busday_count(a.date(), b.date()) if pd.notna(b) else np.nan
                   for a, b in zip(rule["t_ov"], rule["t_rj"])]
rule["late"] = rule["busdays"] > 3
rule["same_person"] = rule["r_ov"] == rule["r_rj"]
rule["batch_upload"] = rule["src_rj"] == "quarter_close_upload"
# batch-uploaded events: timestamp unusable -> mark timing as 'unknown' rather than 'late'
rule.loc[rule["batch_upload"], "late"] = np.nan
summary = rule.groupby(["same_person", "batch_upload"]).agg(n=("late", "size"), late=("late", "sum"), missing=("missing", "sum"))
```

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

## Evidence that would increase concern

- **Bank account ownership.** New IBAN/account holder on V-77x records not matching the vendor's legal name, a bank country different from the vendor's, or accounts that were changed and later reverted after Release Payment (the Lena→Kim→Lena loop).
- **Missing or post-dated maintenance ticket.** No V-77x migration ticket predating the bank changes, or a ticket that lists different vendor records than those changed.
- **Selective changes.** V-77x bank changes only on records with pending high-value payments, rather than across the full vendor family; change dates aligned to individual payment runs rather than a migration window.
- **Access-log anomalies.** Lena and Kim logging in from the same workstation/IP, Kim's approvals originating from Lena's session, or approvals under the shared Approver_Gita credential at times when Gita's badge/VPN records show absence.
- **Payment-file evidence.** Bank confirmations showing beneficiary names different from vendor master, or payments to the new account returned and re-routed.
- **Emergency misuse.** Carla/Gita or Dan/Hugo cases where emergency_flag=True but purchase_category is non-urgent, contract_type is spot with a new vendor, timestamps are mid-week business hours, and amounts cluster just below the next approval threshold.
- **Boilerplate justifications.** Retrospective Justification texts that are identical across cases or written after audit notification; no contemporaneous e-mail or ticket for the 12 batch-uploaded justifications.
- **Persistent violations after cleaning.** Create/Approve same-person violations that survive the res_fixed recomputation, especially outside legacy_erp.
- **Vendor-master anomalies.** V-77x entities sharing addresses, contacts or tax IDs with employees, or created shortly before first payment.

## Evidence that would reduce concern

- **Migration documentation.** A dated V-77x banking-platform ticket, vendor notification letter, and call-back verification records covering the 44 bank changes; new account details matching the vendor's published banking information; changes covering all V-77x records irrespective of payment status.
- **Structural routing.** Supplier risk classification showing V-77x as higher-risk (hence always routed to AP_Kim), and Kim approving V-77x payments at the same rate when no bank change preceded.
- **Format-only corrections.** Lena→Kim→Lena loops where the second change modifies BIC/format fields, not the account or holder, with a matching bank rejection message.
- **Attribution fixes.** entered_by showing that the 23 shared-account approvals were made by authorised assistants under documented delegation; legacy_erp mapping table correction reducing the 42 Create/Approve violations substantially.
- **Contemporaneous justifications.** E-mails or tickets within 3 working days of each override for the 17 same-person cases, with a second person's acknowledgement.
- **Emergency validity.** Medical-supply stock-out records, weekend incident tickets for facilities repairs, and framework contracts with pre-agreed pricing for the fast Carla/Gita and Dan/Hugo approvals.
- **Amounts within delegation.** Gita's and Hugo's delegation limits covering the high-amount emergency cases, with no threshold clustering.
- **Independent bank confirmation.** Positive confirmation from V-77x's treasury that the accounts on file are theirs.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

The review of last quarter's procurement-to-payment event log identified several control exceptions and unusual timing patterns. None of the evidence establishes misconduct; the findings describe deviations from the segregation-of-duties and emergency-justification controls that require explanation, not conclusions about intent.

Most of the detected four-eyes exceptions and fast approvals concentrate in two emergency channels (Buyer_Carla/Approver_Gita for medical supplies and Buyer_Dan/Manager_Hugo for weekend facilities repairs). These clusters show high emergency_flag rates, low vendor concentration and predominantly system-generated records, which is consistent with a designed rush process operating with incomplete documentation. The principal weakness there is procedural: roughly one in five emergency cases lacks a timely, independently entered Retrospective Justification, and 17 justifications were entered by the overriding person, 12 of them in a single post-quarter batch.

The subgroup that warrants the most immediate attention is the VendorMaster_Lena/AP_Kim cluster (44 cases). It combines bank-detail changes followed within minutes by payment approval, 68% high-value cases, 72% concentration on vendor family V-77x and 83% manual uploads, and is not explained by emergency handling. A legitimate explanation exists, namely V-77x's recent banking-platform migration, but the event log does not link these changes to the maintenance ticket, so this cannot currently be verified.

We recommend targeted follow-up: reconcile all V-77x bank changes to maintenance tickets, vendor confirmation letters and bank-account ownership evidence; retrieve entered_by and access logs for the shared Approver_Gita account; and confirm the Bot_PO resource mapping in legacy_erp before treating Create/Approve PO exceptions as human violations. Findings should be read with these data-quality limitations in mind; several indicators may shrink once the mapping and linkage gaps are corrected.
