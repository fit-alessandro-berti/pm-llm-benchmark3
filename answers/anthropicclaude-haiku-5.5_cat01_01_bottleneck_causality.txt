## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional verdict: `mainly confounded`. Confidence: 60/100.** The verdict concerns the crude Orion gap. It does not establish that Orion is beneficial or harmful.

**Strongest evidence for "mainly confounded":**
- The crude gap is +6.7 h (mean 21.96 vs 15.24 h; median 18.2 vs 10.6 h).
- Case mix differs sharply. Orion handles 43% high-complexity cases vs 12.5% for Nova, and 93% night cases vs 25%.
- All 17 night cases have High arrival load, so night and load cannot be separated.
- About 60% of the gap is queue wait (12.8 vs 8.8 h). Queue wait is partly driven by load and compliance.

**Strongest evidence against treating the crude gap as the whole story:**
- Within every complexity stratum, Orion is faster: Low 2.1 h, Medium 2.2 h, High 3.4 h. The pooled-weight standardized contrast is 2.5 h.
- The reversal is not explained by complexity alone.

**Why this does not establish an Orion benefit:**
- Orion has no day Medium or High cases. Nova has only one night Medium case and one night High case.
- The within-stratum contrasts therefore rest on very thin overlap and are not identified.
- Orion's within-stratum advantage is mainly queue wait, which is also a load-sensitive mediator.

**Why the 50% reassignment may fail or backfire:**
- If the within-stratum pattern is real, each moved case adds roughly 2–3 h, so the mean worsens. This is the opposite of what management expects.
- If the crude gap is mainly case mix, moving cases changes nothing for those cases and adds load to Nova.
- Nova has only four night cases, so night capacity is thin. Moving night High-load work may increase queueing, which is the dominant mechanism.
- Nova's high-complexity evidence (n = 2) is too sparse to show that Nova can absorb difficult work.

**Supported by facts:** `complexity -> routing_rule` and `routing_rule -> resource_group` (Orion is selected for difficult and night work). `complexity -> compliance_required` (policy). `shift -> arrival_load` (all night cases are High load). `queue_wait`, `review_service`, and `rework` each feed `cycle_time`, since the cycle-time decomposition is observed.

**Assumptions:** `resource_group` effects on queueing, service, and rework (these reflect capacity and certification). `unobserved_reviewer_skill` edges. `compliance_performed` equals `compliance_required`, as stated. `cycle_time -> quality_outcome`.

**Time-varying feedback suppressed by the static DAG:** `queue_wait -> routing_rule`. When the Orion queue backs up, overflow to Nova may occur, so current queue state affects future assignment, which in turn affects future queues. The static DAG hides this loop and the dependence of assignment on time.

**Final recommendation (120–200 words):** Do not reassign 50% of Orion cases. The crude gap is mostly driven by case mix and night load. Within complexity strata, Orion looks faster, mainly through shorter queue waits. The within-stratum evidence is too thin to establish that Orion is beneficial, and moving half its work to Nova risks worsening performance for the difficult, night-shift cases that now dominate queueing.

Start with a measurement repair that adds timestamps, reviewer IDs, and open-case status. Then pilot capacity changes for compliance and night shifts, which target the queue directly and leave the resource-group label unchanged. Only after that, run a small randomized routing pilot restricted to Medium-complexity cases where both groups have capacity, with the stop rules above in place. Use the results to decide whether any routing change is justified. Until then, keep current routing, and track queue wait, compliance waiting, rework, and open-case counts weekly.

```python
        events.append({"case_id": r["case_id"], "activity": act,
                       "seq_stamp": base + pd.Timedelta(seconds=pos),
                       "resource_group": r["resource_group"]})
ev = pd.DataFrame(events)
log = pm4py.format_dataframe(ev, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity",
                             timestamp_key="seq_stamp")

dfgs, cases_per_group = {}, {}
for g in GROUPS:
    sub = log[log["resource_group"] == g]
    cases_per_group[g] = sub["case:concept:name"].nunique()
    if cases_per_group[g] < 3:
        print(f"WARNING [{g}]: only {cases_per_group[g]} cases. DFG comparison is weak.")
    dfgs[g] = pm4py.discover_dfg(sub)[0]  # returns (dfg, start_activities, end_activities)

pairs = sorted(set(dfgs["Orion"]) | set(dfgs["Nova"]))
rows = []
for a, b in pairs:
    o = dfgs["Orion"].get((a, b), 0)
    n = dfgs["Nova"].get((a, b), 0)
    rows.append({"pair": f"{a} -> {b}", "Orion_count": o, "Nova_count": n,
                 "Orion_per_case": round(o / cases_per_group["Orion"], 2),
                 "Nova_per_case": round(n / cases_per_group["Nova"], 2)})
dfg_table = pd.DataFrame(rows)
print(dfg_table.to_string(index=False))
for _, r in dfg_table.iterrows():
    if max(r["Orion_count"], r["Nova_count"]) < 3:
        print(f"WARNING [DFG {r['pair']}]: low support ({r['Orion_count']}/{r['Nova_count']}).")

print("\nNote: DFG differences describe routing and rework paths. They do not establish causality.")
```

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Positivity and overlap.** Orion has no day Medium or High cases, and Nova has only one night Medium and one night High case, so the within-stratum contrasts are not identified. The single most valuable addition is a randomized or overlap-generating routing sample in the empty cells, such as Medium-complexity cases routed to both groups across shifts.

2. **Shift and arrival load are nearly collinear.** All 17 night cases are High load. The most valuable field is the queue length and reviewer availability at the moment of classification, which separates load from shift.

3. **Unobserved case risk.** Complexity is a coarse label, and it drives routing, compliance, and rework. The most valuable field is a risk score recorded at registration, before routing.

4. **Completed-case selection and censoring.** Open and abandoned cases are absent, and Orion's highest-load cases are the most likely to be unfinished. The most valuable field is a case-status flag with extraction timestamp and case age for open and abandoned cases.

5. **Unlogged and unattributed time.** About 2 h per case is unexplained by logged wait or service. Request Info external waiting and compliance-check service are not separated. The most valuable field is separate start and end timestamps for Request Info and compliance activities, together with reviewer ID.
