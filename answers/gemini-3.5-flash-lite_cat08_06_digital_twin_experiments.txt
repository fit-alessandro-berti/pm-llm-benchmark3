# Simulation-Based Digital Twin for Building-Permit Process

---

## 1. Model Scope and State Variables

### System Boundary
The digital twin encompasses the end-to-end municipal building-permit lifecycle, beginning with application submission via the online portal and concluding with permit issuance, rejection, or withdrawal. It explicitly models upstream applicant interactions, internal municipal review queues, inter-departmental handoffs (planning, fire, environment, legal), site inspection logistics, and council calendar constraints.

### Level of Detail
- **Granular Activity Level:** Every node in the process tree (including checks, reviews, notices, scheduling, inspections, drafting, and legal gates) is modeled as an explicit operational step.
- **Micro-State Representation:** Individual case tokens retain dynamic attributes (case type, district, architect ID, application value, required reviews, current queue position, historical rework count).
- **Resource Level:** Finite human and system resources are modeled with explicit state machines (Idle, Busy, Break/Off-shift, Unavailable due to council meetings or part-time schedules).

### Entities and Attributes
- **Cases ($c \in \mathcal{C}$):** Applications entering the system. Attributes: `case_id`, `case_type` $\in \{\text{Simple renovation, New residential, Commercial, Complex variance}\}$, `district` $\in \{D_1, \dots, D_5\}$, `architect_id`, `application_value`, `requires_fire`, `requires_environment`, `requires_public_notice`, `arrival_time`, `urgency_score`, `rework_count`.
- **Resources ($r \in \mathcal{R}$):** Clerks, Planners, Fire Reviewers, Environmental Reviewers, Inspectors, Legal Officers, and the automated Portal/System. Attributes: `resource_id`, `pool_type`, `schedule_calendar`, `current_state`, `assigned_case_id`.

### State Variables
- **Global Clock ($t$):** Continuous or discrete-event time step (modeled in minutes).
- **Arrival State ($\Lambda(t)$):** Non-stationary Poisson arrival rate modulated by time-of-day, day-of-week (Monday spikes), and seasonal factors (March–May, Sept–Nov bursts).
- **Queue Lengths ($Q_{act}(t)$):** Number of cases waiting for each activity $act$, disaggregated by district and case type where applicable.
- **Resource Utilization ($\rho_r(t)$):** Instantaneous utilization and cumulative busy time.
- **Spatial/Dependency States:** Inspector geographical clusters, architect active submission counts within a 7-day sliding window, and active neighborhood objection indicators.

### Queues, Calendars, and Cross-Case Dependencies
- **Queues:** Priority queues governed by First-In-First-Out (FIFO) with dynamic re-ranking (e.g., aging-based prioritization in intervention D6, or batched spatial sorting for inspections).
- **Calendars:** Hard constraints derived from HR data (e.g., Fire reviewers available Tue–Thu; Legal officers idle during council-meeting weeks; public holidays).
- **Cross-Case Dependencies:**
  1. *Architect Bursts:* Top 20 architects ($A_{top}$) generate correlated batch submissions; arrival generator samples burst sizes from an empirical joint distribution.
  2. *Geographic Inspection Bundling:* Inspections in adjacent districts ($D_3, D_5$) are grouped into daily batch schedules to minimize travel time, forcing some cases to wait for spatial co-occurrence.
  3. *Shared Neighborhood Objections:* A single objection event on a contentious variance case extends the public-notice duration for spatially proximate applications.

## 2. Parameter-Estimation Plan

### Estimation Methodology
- **Arrivals:** Fit non-stationary Poisson processes using time-varying intensity functions $\lambda(t) = \lambda_0 \cdot \gamma_{season}(t) \cdot \beta_{day}(t) \cdot \alpha_{architect}(t)$, estimated via kernel density estimation on event timestamps.
- **Service and Waiting Times:** Fit parametric distributions (Log-Normal, Weibull, or Gamma to capture right-skewness and heavy tails) to empirical service-duration data. Where start timestamps are missing for human activities, employ expectation-maximization (EM) or regression imputation conditioned on resource availability and case attributes.
- **Branching Probabilities & Rework:** Estimate multinomial logistic regression models for routing choices and rework loops using case attributes (`case_type`, `application_value`, `district`, `rework_count`).
- **Resource Calendars & Efficiencies:** Directly ingest HR-reconstructed shift schedules and calibrate effective processing rates using resource-activity join logs.

### PM4Py Integration
PM4Py will be used exclusively for process discovery, event-log formatting, variant analysis, and Directly-Follows Graph (DFG) extraction. 
- `pm4py.format_dataframe()` standardizes columns (`case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, `time:timestamp`).
- `pm4py.discover_petri_net_inductive()` or `pm4py.parse_process_tree()` extracts the reference process model topology and routing branch probabilities.
- `pm4py.get_variants_as_dataframe()` and `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg()` provide baseline routing shares and waiting/processing time distributions.
- **Separation of Concerns:** PM4Py handles descriptive analytics, log parsing, and structural process mining. Simulation execution, queueing dynamics, state propagation, and intervention scenario testing are handled by a dedicated discrete-event simulation (DES) engine (e.g., SimPy or a custom event loop).

## 3. Assumptions

### Arrivals
- Arrivals follow a non-stationary Poisson process with deterministic daily/seasonal scaling factors and modulated stochastic bursts from top architects.

### Service Times
- Service durations follow Log-Normal distributions parameterized by activity, resource group, and case type. Missing start timestamps are imputed by subtracting median service times from completion timestamps during warm-up calibration.

### Routing
- Routing decisions at exclusive choices ($X$) are governed by multinomial logit probabilities conditioned on case attributes (`case_type`, `application_value`, `requires_fire`, etc.).

### Resources / Calendars
- Resources adhere strictly to reconstructed HR calendars. Multi-skilling is limited (e.g., two planners restricted to Simple renovations; environmental reviewers operate at 60% capacity due to competing duties).

### Rework
- Rework probability is a function of prior review findings and case complexity. Because rework reasons in the log are unstructured text, rework loops are modeled as stochastic retries with inflated service times.

### Applicant / Third-Party Response
- Applicant response times (e.g., waiting for missing documents or appeal filings) are modeled as heavy-tailed Pareto or Log-Logistic distributions independent of municipal resource availability.

### Cross-Case Dependencies
- Spatial proximity ($D_3, D_5$) introduces a deterministic scheduling delay (up to 48 hours) to allow geographic batching of site inspections. Architect clustering is modeled via correlated batch arrival streams.

### Non-Stationary Demand
- Seasonal surges (Spring/Fall) and day-of-week effects (Monday submission spikes) are explicitly modeled via time-varying arrival rate multipliers.

### Missing Data
- Unobserved neighborhood objections are treated as latent stochastic variables; their impact is modeled by drawing public-notice durations from a mixture distribution where the heavy tail represents unrecorded objections.

## 4. Experiment Matrix

## 5. Warm-up, Replications, and Uncertainty Treatment

- **Warm-up Period:** 90 simulated days (burn-in phase) to populate all internal queues and eliminate transient startup artifacts before data collection commences.
- **Run Length:** 730 days (2 full annual cycles) per replication to capture complete seasonal non-stationarity.
- **Replications:** 30 independent macro-replications using antithetic variates and distinct pseudo-random number generator (PRNG) seeds to ensure statistical stabilization.
- **Uncertainty Treatment:** 
  - Report 95% Confidence Intervals (CI) for parametric means and bootstrap percentile intervals (1,000 resamples) for heavy-tailed metrics (p90 and p99 cycle times).
  - Sensitivity analysis performed via Morris Screening and Sobol variance-based global sensitivity analysis to identify which input parameters (e.g., applicant response delays, arrival burst intensity) drive outcome variance.
- **Heavy-Tailed Durations:** Modeled using heavy-tailed distributions (Log-Normal, Weibull with shape $k < 1$, or Pareto) without truncation, ensuring extreme delays (e.g., complex variance appeals) are faithfully propagated.

---

## 6. Validation Strategy

1. **Face Validation:** Review simulation logic, routing rules, and resource schedules with municipal department heads, domain experts, and process planners in periodic workshops.
2. **Event-Log Replay Validation:** Feed historical arrival sequences into the digital twin and compare simulated case trajectories against historical event logs using PM4Py conformance checking (token-based replay, fitness, and precision metrics).
3. **Distributional Validation:** Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) tests comparing simulated cycle-time and waiting-time distributions against empirical event-log distributions across all four case types.
4. **Resource-Utilization Validation:** Cross-reference simulated resource utilization rates against reconstructed HR calendars and empirical timesheet summaries.
5. **Bottleneck Validation:** Verify that simulated bottleneck frequencies and queue lengths match observed process-mining performance DFG bottlenecks (e.g., Legal check and Environmental review queues).
6. **Out-of-Sample Validation:** Calibrate parameters on event-log data from Year 1 and validate predictive performance on unseen Year 2 data.

---

## 7. Decision Criteria

Interventions will be evaluated and ranked using a multi-criteria decision analysis (MCDA) framework based on the following weighted metrics:

- **Cycle Time Performance:** Median total duration ($\le 30$ days for Simple, $\le 60$ for Residential, $\le 90$ for Commercial, $\le 150$ for Complex) and p90 tail duration.
- **SLA Compliance:** Percentage of cases completed within statutory time limits ($\ge 95\%$ compliance per case type).
- **Fairness:** Maximum absolute disparity in median cycle time across districts ($D_1$–$D_5$) and architect submission tiers ($\le 15\%$ variance).
- **Resource Utilization:** Average resource utilization maintained between 75% and 85% to balance productivity against burnout and queue instability.
- **Rework & Backlog:** Total volume of rework loops and end-of-year open case backlog.
- **Robustness:** Performance degradation ratio under stress scenarios (S1–S3) compared to baseline.
