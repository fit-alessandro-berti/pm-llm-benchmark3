**Opening-hours convention.** The service center is open 07:00–19:00, so the observed window is 12 hours = 720 minutes. Unless stated otherwise, all daily rates below are **per opening hour**, not per calendar day or per 24-hour day.

**Primary observed quantities and units.**

| Quantity | Symbol / column | Unit | Definition used here |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Arrivals | `arrivals` | cases/day | Cases arriving during opening hours. |
| Completions | `completed` | cases/day | Cases completed during opening hours. |
| Arrival intensity | `arrivals / 12` | cases/hour | Opening-hour arrival rate proxy. |
| Completion intensity | `completed / 12` | cases/hour | Opening-hour departure/throughput proxy. |
| Median active service time | `median_service_min` | minutes/case | Median handling time excluding waiting. |
| p90 active service time | `p90_service_min` | minutes/case | 90th percentile handling time excluding waiting. |
| Mean WIP | `mean_wip` | cases | Mean number of cases in system from 15-minute snapshots during opening hours. |
| Staffing capacity | `staff_fte` | FTE | Mean concurrently available full-time-equivalent capacity. |
| Complex case share | `complex_pct` | % of arrivals | Share of arrivals following multi-step complex variant. |
| Morning peak share | `peak_08_10_pct` | % of arrivals | Share of arrivals in 08:00–09:59, as defined in the prompt. |
| Outage | `outage_min` | minutes/day | Total outage minutes; exact timing only partially inferred from representative vector. |

**Derived estimates and proxies.**

- **Net backlog change estimate:**  
  \[
  \Delta B_d = \text{arrivals}_d - \text{completed}_d
  \]
  in cases/day. This assumes no abandonment and that arrivals enter the system and remain until completion. This is an **estimate**, not an observed backlog level.

- **Indicative load proxies.** Let \(A\) be arrivals/day, \(S_{med}\) median service minutes, \(S_{p90}\) p90 service minutes, and \(F\) staff FTE. I use:
  \[
  \rho_{med} = \frac{A \times S_{med}}{720 \times F}, \quad
  \rho_{p90} = \frac{A \times S_{p90}}{720 \times F}
  \]
  These are **capacity-pressure proxies**, not true utilization. True utilization would require mean service time and a staffing/productivity model. Under a right-skewed service-time assumption, median-based load tends to understate mean load, while p90-based load tends to overstate mean load, but these are not strict bounds because the mean can exceed p90 in heavy-tailed distributions.

- **WIP per FTE:**  
  \[
  L/F
  \]
  is an observed ratio of mean opening-hours WIP to mean concurrent FTE. It is a crude pressure indicator, not a queueing probability.

**Estimands not directly observed.**

- True mean active service time.
- True mean case flow time.
- True arrival process distribution and interarrival times.
- True service-time distribution and variance.
- Backlog at 07:00 and 19:00.
- Abandonment, if any.
- Exact timing of the staffing intervention on 2026-04-29.
- Whether `complex_pct` reflects real case mix, coding practice, or both.

**Threats to naive inference.** At least the following five are material:

1. **Aggregation hides event-level structure.** Daily counts, medians, and p90s cannot recover case-level arrival/departure matching, waiting times, or service-time variance. A pooled queueing model fitted to daily averages may describe no real day.

2. **Boundary and carry-over effects.** The 07:00 backlog is not supplied. Completed cases may have arrived on prior days. Daily completions are therefore not the same cohort as daily arrivals, and daily mean WIP is only an opening-hours snapshot.

3. **Outage distortion.** 2026-04-15 has 180 outage minutes. Opening-hour completion rate is not comparable to normal days. The representative vector suggests completions collapse around 11:00–14:00, but arrivals continue. Without abandonment and re-entry rules, the outage day cannot be modeled as a normal high-WIP day.

4. **Case-mix and coding ambiguity.** Complex share jumps from about 25% to 41% on 2026-04-20. This may be a real demand-mix shift, a coding change, or both. Service-time medians and p90s also jump, but the aggregates cannot separate mix effect from process change.

5. **Peak metric and hourly vectors are inconsistent.** The supplied `peak_08_10_pct` is defined as 08:00–09:59, but the representative hourly vectors do not reconcile with it. For example, 2026-04-07 vector arrivals in bins 08–09 and 09–10 are \(5+12=17\) of 101, about 16.8%, while the daily table reports 31%. This requires a raw timestamp audit before using either metric for peak modeling.

6. **Serial dependence and regime mixture.** Daily rows are not independent: backlog carries over, weekdays/weekends alternate, and there are apparent level shifts. Pooling across regimes can create aggregation bias.

7. **Staffing intervention timing is unknown.** The intervention began during 2026-04-29, but exact implementation time is not logged. Daily mean `staff_fte` may mask partial-day staffing and cannot identify the causal timing of backlog drainage.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

For a chosen window, I use:

\[
\hat W = \frac{L}{\gamma}
\]

where:

- \(L\) = mean `mean_wip` over the window, cases, observed only during opening hours;
- \(\gamma\) = mean completions per opening hour, cases/hour;
- \(\hat W\) = implied average time in system during the observed opening-hours window, hours/case.

I prefer **completions** as the throughput proxy because Little’s law uses departure rate. Arrivals are used only as a check when arrivals and completions are close. These implied values are not mean flow times if backlog changes materially, if overnight carry-over is important, or if the window is non-stationary. They also do not include unobserved overnight waiting unless the window and assumptions are extended.

**Key caution.** Median service time is not a substitute for mean flow time. For example, baseline weekday median service is 25 minutes, but the implied opening-window time from Little’s-law diagnostics is about 2.4 hours. That difference is a warning that waiting, carry-over, and boundary effects matter; it is not a valid decomposition without event-level data.

# 7. Operational implications

## Staffing

**Baseline weekday.** Median-based load proxy is 0.59, but p90-based load proxy is 1.10. The system may be adequate on average but tail-constrained. Maintain at least current weekday capacity and monitor hourly queues.

**High-complex weekday.** Median-based load proxy is 0.93; p90-based proxy is 2.22; WIP/FTE is 7.5; net backlog grows by about 13 cases/day. This is the clearest capacity-pressure regime.

A rough staffing need can be estimated from the net backlog swing:

- high-complex weekday completions: 100.6 cases/day,
- high-complex weekday arrivals: 114.0 cases/day,
- backlog growth: 13.4 cases/day.

To turn net change negative, completions must exceed arrivals by the desired drain rate. If mean service were near the median, 35 minutes, one FTE could theoretically process about \(720/35 = 20.6\) cases/day, so an extra 0.7–1.0 FTE might offset median-based pressure. If mean service is closer to the p90 tail, required additional capacity is larger. The intervention period added roughly 0.9 FTE relative to high-complex weekdays and moved net change from +13.4 to 6.0 cases/day. That is suggestive but not causal because service times also fell and exact timing is unknown.

**Weekend.** Baseline weekend load proxy is moderate, but high-complex weekend has median-based load 0.85 and p90-based load 1.96, with WIP/FTE 7.6. Weekend staffing should not be set from pooled weekend averages.

## Backlog recovery

Treat 2026-04-16/17 and 2026-04-29/05-01 as recovery regimes, not normal throughput benchmarks.

Observed cumulative net change, assuming no abandonment:

- baseline weekday +4,
- baseline weekend +1,
- outage +47,
- recovery 30,
- high-complex weekday +94,
- intervention 18,
- high-complex weekend +12,
- total +110 cases over the 28-day window.

The intervention appears to have begun draining backlog, but the remaining backlog level is unknown because the 07:00 backlog on 2026-04-06 and final backlog on 2026-05-03 are not supplied. Recovery should be judged by WIP trend and net change, not by single-day completion counts.

## Peak smoothing

Peak share rises from baseline weekday about 29.6% to high-complex weekday about 45.9%. The 2026-04-21 representative vector is bursty, with one hour containing 34 arrivals against a mean of 9.6 arrivals/hour. Even though the vector conflicts with the daily peak metric, the qualitative story is consistent: morning/early-day concentration matters.

Recommendations:

- smooth appointments or intake channels around 08:00–12:00,
- stagger staff breaks away from observed peaks,
- create overflow capacity for the first several hours,
- monitor hourly WIP, not only daily WIP.

## Data collection

The most important upgrades are:

1. **Event-level timestamps** for arrival, service start, service end, completion, and abandonment.
2. **Backlog snapshots** at 07:00 and 19:00, or full 24-hour WIP if cases carry over overnight.
3. **Case-level complex-variant flags** and service-step counts to distinguish real mix change from coding change.
4. **Hourly staffing and outage logs**, including exact intervention start time on 2026-04-29.
5. **Hourly arrivals and completions** reconciled with `peak_08_10_pct`.

With raw event data, process-mining tools could estimate arrival rates, throughput rates, and temporal features more directly. The daily aggregates supplied here are not an event log and cannot be passed directly to PM4Py as such.

## Policy that looks beneficial pooled but risky segmented

**Pooled-average policy:** “Weekend mean WIP is only 17.4 cases and weekend arrivals are low, so reduce weekend staffing or keep it flat.”

**Segmented risk:** high-complex weekend days have mean WIP 21.0, WIP/FTE 7.6, median-based load proxy 0.85, and p90-based load proxy 1.96. They also accumulate net backlog by about 3 cases/day. A pooled weekend average hides that the relevant weekend regime is tail-loaded. Reducing weekend staffing based on pooled averages could worsen Monday morning carry-over.

Another pooled-average risk: “Overall median-based load is 0.75, so no capacity action is needed.” This hides high-complex weekday median-based load 0.93 and p90-based load 2.22, plus sustained backlog growth.
