## 2. Competing explanations and timeline  

**January–March – Stable baseline**  
The model performs well (AUROC 0.81–0.82, Brier ~0.13, ECE <0.025). Data quality is excellent, labels mature, variant mix stable. No drift is evident.

**April – Datapipeline incident, not model failure**  
The migration renames `Initial Diagnosis Completed`  `Diagnostic Assessment Completed`. The feature pipeline does not recognise the new label, causing prediction prefixes to be built at wrong events or missing entirely. Duplicate events and `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` appear. Unknownactivity share spikes to 17%, missing queue feature to 14%. Mean predicted risk jumps to 0.28 (artefact of broken features), AUROC collapses to 0.67. The spike in unmapped variants (18% “unmapped/other”) confirms the eventlog distortion. This month is overwhelmingly a **pipeline failure**, not genuine model decay.

**May – Partial recovery with residual contamination**  
The mapping patch restores most labels, backfills some records, but “number of events before diagnosis” remains unrecomputed for April. Unknown share falls to 2%, missing queue to 3%. Metrics improve substantially (AUROC 0.78, ECE 0.05). A small amount of residual dataquality drag remains; model predictions for Maystarted cases are usable but not pristine.

**June – Policyinduced process and covariate shift**  
A new remoterouting policy intentionally increases V2 usage from 34% to 52%. Because V2 has a low breach rate (0.09–0.11), the observed breach rate drops to 0.15 while the model still predicts 0.20 — a classic **covariate shift** causing calibration error (ECE 0.06). AUROC stays at 0.79, showing the model still ranks cases correctly within each variant. No concept drift is visible: V1, V2, V3 breach rates are unchanged from earlier months. The episode highlights that a change in process mix alone degrades overall calibration without requiring retraining.

**July – Early concept drift emerges in V3**  
Supplier delivery problems begin (partwait median 65h). V3 mature breach rate climbs to 0.46, while V1/V2 remain stable. Overall true breach rate stays near 0.19 due to stillhigh V2 share, but the model’s mean prediction (0.18) fails to reflect the growing V3 risk. AUROC ticks down to 0.76; ECE modest (0.042). Data quality is good. The start of **concept drift** is clear, though its impact on aggregate metrics is dampened by the variant mix.

**August – Fullblown concept drift, now visible in aggregate**  
Parts delays spike to 130h; V3 breach rate reaches 0.64. With V3 at 35% of volume, the weighted true breach rate is ~0.31, but the model predicts only 0.19. AUROC plunges to 0.68, Brier to 0.197, ECE to 0.12. Label maturity is still 93%, so these figures reflect real degradation. The model **cannot capture the new relationship** between part delay and breach. This is textbook concept drift, concentrated in V3.

**September – Concept drift compounded by population shift**  
Partner Z onboards, contributing 24% of cases. Partner Z cases are more rural, follow V3 more often (58% vs 36%), and bring deviceage and distance patterns absent from training. Mature V3 breach stays high at 0.61. The population shift introduces **additional covariate shift (missing features) and a subpopulation with different baseline risk**. Metrics continue to erode (AUROC 0.66, ECE 0.14). Label maturity drops to 82%, so some metric noise is present, but the deterioration is largely real.

**October – Dominated by label censoring; metrics are unreliable**  
Label maturity falls to 55%, and the outcome API backlog selectively delays breach labels for longerrunning V3 cases. The observed breach rate is an implausible 0.16 – a heavy **censoring bias**. Reported AUROC (0.64) and Brier (0.205) mix true model weakness with artefactual degradation from immature outcome labels. No reliable assessment of concept drift or population shift can be made until labels mature. October must be treated as an “evaluation holiday”; any retraining on October data would bake in censoring bias.

**Why these months are not homogeneous**  
- April = pipeline breakage.  
- June = covariate (mix) shift without concept drift.  
- August–September = real concept drift + early population shift, with adequate label maturity to diagnose.  
- October = labelcensoring contamination, making metrics uninterpretable.

## 3. Diagnostic tests  

### 3.1 Labelmaturity curves and fixedvintage backtesting  
**Test:** For each monthly cohort, plot observed breach rate as a function of the number of days after case start (maturity curve). Compare the rate at the reporting cutoff with the asymptotic rate after 120 days.  
- *Under censoring hypothesis (Oct):* Curve will climb well beyond 0.16 as maturity increases; the final rate will approach 0.28–0.35, consistent with the variant mix.  
- *Under genuine lowbreach hypothesis:* Curve would flatten near 0.16.  

**Fixedvintage:** Recompute AUROC/Brier on the October cohort after waiting for 95% maturity.  
- *Censoring hypothesis:* Metrics will recover (AUROC  0.66–0.68 still degraded, but not 0.64).  
- *If model truly worsened further:* Metrics remain low even after label maturation.

### 3.2 Reevaluation after activitylabel normalisation and duplicate cleaning  
**Test:** Rerun the April feature pipeline with (a) `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` mapped to `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, (b) duplicates removed, and (c) “events before diagnosis” recomputed. Then recalculate April metrics.  
- *Pipelinefailure hypothesis:* AUROC returns to ~0.80, Brier ~0.13, ECE <0.03, predicted mean ~0.19.  
- *If genuine drift occurred in April:* Metrics remain depressed.  

### 3.3 Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source, and month  
**Test:** Build reliability diagrams and pergroup AUROC for V1, V2, V3 and for PartnerZ vs. nonPartnerZ within each month.  
- *Conceptdrift (V3) hypothesis:* From July onward, V3 shows a large gap between predicted risk (low) and observed (high); V1/V2 remain wellcalibrated.  
- *Purely covariate/population shift hypothesis:* All variants show similar calibration error direction after reweighting.  
- *PartnerZ effect:* If missing features are key, PartnerZ segments will exhibit worse calibration than nonPartnerZ even after adjusting for variant.

### 3.4 ProcessDFG edge performance and variantpath analysis  
**Test:** Correlate `Order Part  Part Received` median duration with V3 breach rate. Compare performanceDFG edges across months.  
- *Concept drift via supplier delay:* V3 breach rate rises with partwait time, but model predictions stay flat, indicating the model fails to translate longer delays into higher risk.  
- *If model captured the delay feature correctly:* Predictions would rise with partwait time; the error would be from distribution shift alone.  

### 3.5 Conditional outcome relationship test  
**Test:** Fit logistic regression `breach ~ part_delay_time + month_indicator` on V3 cases only.  
- *Pure covariate (delay distribution) shift:* `month_indicator` is not significant; the increase in breach is fully explained by longer delays.  
- *Concept drift beyond delay:* `month_indicator` remains significant  even for the same delay duration, breach probability increased (e.g., supplier reliability changed urgency handling).  

### 3.6 Population reweighting / transportstyle check for PartnerZ  
**Test:** Weight the nonPartnerZ September population to match PartnerZ’s observed feature distribution (variant, queue wait, etc., but not rural/distance). Assess whether performance gap persists.  
- *Missingfeatures cause:* The gap remains large, indicating unobserved rural/distance/deviceage characteristics are driving the discrepancy.  
- *If purely variant mix & known features:* The gap closes after reweighting.  

### 3.7 Feature missingness and lineage audit  
**Test:** Trace the “number of events before diagnosis” feature for April cases before and after backfill. Confirm that its missingness correlates with prediction outliers.  
- *Pipelinefailure hypothesis:* Cases with missing feature show inflated predicted risk (0.28); after backfill, predictions normalise.  
- *If not pipeline related:* Missingness does not explain the prediction shift.

## 4. Recalibrationversusretraining decision  

### Staged plan  

**Stage 0 – Immediate containment (now)**  
- Quarantine **October** data from any training, evaluation, or calibration. Set a rule: only cohorts with 90% label maturity are used for model updates.  
- Reprocess **April** with corrected label mapping, deduplication, and full feature backfill. Validate that metrics return to baseline before considering its use.  
- For live scoring, flag V3 cases as highrisk based on postJuly matured breach rates (0.46), overriding the model’s probabilistic output for decision thresholds.

**Stage 1 – Shortterm recalibration (weeks 12)**  
- Build a variantspecific calibration map using mature, clean data from **May–July** (postpatch, precensoring, and containing the early partdelay signal).  
  - For V1 and V2, recalibrate the existing model (Platt scaling or isotonic regression) because the relationship between features and outcome is stable, only the mix changed.  
  - For V3, recalibrating the existing model is insufficient; instead, develop a lightweight V3specific risk model that uses partdelay duration and queue features on the same feature set. Train on V3 cases from **July–September** (labels already 82% mature; accept slight noise).  
- Do **not** use June alone for recalibration because it contains only mixshift, not concept shift; mixing June with July+ would blur the necessary V3 correction.

**Stage 2 – Feature repair (weeks 36)**  
- Ingest rural distance, device age, and PartnerZ identifier into the feature pipeline.  
- Backfill these features for September and October cases.  
- Reevaluate model performance on PartnerZ vs. others; confirm that adding these features improves calibration and discrimination.

**Stage 3 – Full retraining (once labels mature)**  
- Wait until the **October cohort reaches 90% maturity** ( early December 2026).  
- Assemble a training set from:  
  - **Safe, representative months:** Jan–Mar (historical), May–July (modern, clean), and **August–September** (concept drift + PartnerZ).  
  - Exclude April (unless fully corrected and verified) and October until mature.  
- Consider a **variantspecific model ensemble**: one model per variant (V1, V2, V3) given the divergent dynamics. This simplifies monitoring and recalibration. A unified model with variant indicator and interaction terms is a lighter alternative.  
- Incorporate PartnerZ features and evaluate fairness across sources.

### Which months are safe for training?  
| Month | Safe for training? | Condition |
|-------|-------------------|------------|
| Jan–Mar | Yes | Stable baseline; can be used as historical anchor. |
| April | No | Only after full pipeline repair and validation; otherwise contaminated. |
| May | Yes (with caution) | Minor residual missingness; verify feature completeness. |
| June | Yes | Only mixshift; labels perfect. |
| July | Yes | Early concept drift; valuable for V3 learning. |
| August | Yes | Strong concept drift signal; labels 93% mature. |
| September | Conditional | Label maturity 82% – usable but expect slight label noise for V3. |
| October | No | Await maturity 90%. |

## 5. Monitoring architecture  

### Monitors and alert dependencies  

- **Predictive-performance monitors**  
  *AUROC, Brier, ECE per variant and overall, computed on mature cohorts.*  
  - Trigger investigation if AUROC drops >0.05 from baseline.  
  - Block retraining alert if label maturity <90% or dataquality alert active.

- **Calibration monitors**  
  *Reliability curves, ECE stratified by variant and source.*  
  - Alert if ECE >0.08 for any variant while data quality is healthy.  
  - Suppress if label maturity <90% for that cohort.

- **Labelmaturity monitors**  
  *Cohort maturity%, median label delay, outcomeAPI backlog size.*  
  - If maturity <90%, automatically suppress all “model deteriorated” alarms and block training pipelines for that month.  
  - Issue a “labeldelay warning” for the operations team.

- **Dataquality monitors**  
  *Unknown activity%, missing feature%, duplicate rate, schemachange detection.*  
  - If unknown activity >2% or missing feature >5%, block evaluation and model updates; trigger datapipeline repair alert.

- **Processvariant monitors**  
  *Variant distribution (V1/V2/V3/other) per month, per source.*  
  - Sudden shift (>10pp) triggers a “covariate shift” advisory, prompting recalibration check without forcing retraining.

- **PerformanceDFG monitors**  
  *Median duration for edges “Order Part  Part Received” and “Diagnosis  Remote Fix”.*  
  - If partwait time exceeds 70h (or 2× historical median), escalate to conceptdrift investigation for V3.

- **Population monitors**  
  *PartnerZ share, rural/distance feature distributions (once added).*  
  - Notify when a new subpopulation exceeds 10% of volume; verify model performance on that segment separately.

- **Intervention / deployment monitors**  
  *Model version, prediction distribution (mean, variance), threshold changes.*  
  - Log every deployment; raise alert if prediction distribution shifts abruptly without explanation.

**Alert dependency logic**:  
1. Latest month maturity <90%  **do not evaluate model quality** for that month; only expose labelmaturity and dataquality dashboards.  
2. Dataquality alarm active  **suppress** all modelperformance alerts and training jobs.  
3. Processvariant or performanceDFG alarm  trigger **calibration/recalibration suitability** analysis, but **do not** autoretrain.  
4. Only when maturity 90% AND data quality is healthy AND predictive/calibration monitors breach thresholds  **recommend retraining**.

### Python code sketch: monthly processevidence extraction  

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.algo.filtering.log.timestamp import timestamp_filter
from pm4py.statistics.traces.generic.log import case_statistics
from pm4py.statistics.variants.log import get as variants_get
from pm4py.algo.discovery.dfg import algorithm as dfg_discovery
from pm4py.algo.discovery.performance_dfg import algorithm as perf_dfg_discovery
from pm4py.objects.conversion.log import converter as log_converter

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Load and prepare log
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
df = pd.read_csv("field_service_events.csv", parse_dates=["timestamp"])
df.rename(columns={"case_id": "case:concept:name", "activity": "concept:name",
                    "timestamp": "time:timestamp"}, inplace=True)

# Normalise diagnosis labels (handle both old and new names)
old_label = "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
new_label = "Diagnostic Assessment Completed"
df["concept:name"] = df["concept:name"].replace(new_label, old_label)

# Remove exact duplicate events (same case, activity, timestamp)
n_before = len(df)
df.drop_duplicates(subset=["case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"],
                   inplace=True)
duplicates_removed = n_before - len(df)

# Format for PM4Py
log = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id="case:concept:name",
                             activity_key="concept:name",
                             timestamp_key="time:timestamp")
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Monthly casestart cohort and eventcalendar summaries
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
case_durations = pm4py.get_all_case_durations(log)
# get case start time
case_starts = (df.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"]
                .min().dt.to_period("M"))

monthly_summaries = []
for month_period in pd.period_range(start="2026-01", end="2026-10", freq="M"):
    month_label = str(month_period)
    # --- Case-start cohort: cases that began this month ---
    cohort_cases = case_starts[case_starts == month_period].index.tolist()
    cohort_log = pm4py.filter_log(lambda trace: trace.attributes["case:concept:name"]
                                  in cohort_cases, log)

    if len(cohort_log) == 0:
        continue

    # Variants
    variants = variants_get.get_variants(cohort_log)
    variant_counts = {v: len(cases) for v, cases in variants.items()}
    total_cases = len(cohort_log)

    # DFG and performance DFG on the whole traces of the cohort
    activities_count = pm4py.get_event_attribute_values(cohort_log, "concept:name")
    unknown_share = activities_count.get("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY", 0) / sum(activities_count.values()) if activities_count else 0

    dfg = dfg_discovery.apply(cohort_log)
    perf_dfg = perf_dfg_discovery.apply(cohort_log, parameters={"aggregation_measure": "median"})

    # Extract specific edges
    part_edge = perf_dfg.get(("Order Part", "Part Received"), None)
    remote_edge = perf_dfg.get((old_label, "Remote Fix"), None)

    # --- Eventcalendar window: all events in this month ---
    start_ts = pd.Timestamp(month_period.start_time)
    end_ts = pd.Timestamp(month_period.end_time) + pd.Timedelta(days=1) - pd.Timedelta(seconds=1)
    event_log_month = pm4py.filter_time_range(log, start_ts, end_ts, mode="events")
    # The eventcalendar DFG reflects the process fragments occurring in the month
    event_dfg = dfg_discovery.apply(event_log_month)
    event_perf_dfg = perf_dfg_discovery.apply(event_log_month)

    monthly_summaries.append({
        "month": month_label,
        "cases_started": total_cases,
        "variant_distribution": variant_counts,
        "unknown_activity_share": unknown_share,
        "case_cohort_part_wait_median_h": part_edge / 3600 if part_edge else None,
        "case_cohort_remote_fix_median_h": remote_edge / 3600 if remote_edge else None,
        "duplicates_removed_this_period": duplicates_removed,  # global, can be tracked per extraction
        "event_calendar_top_edges": list(event_perf_dfg.items())[:10],
        # additional temporal features can be extracted via pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe
    })

monthly_df = pd.DataFrame(monthly_summaries)
print(monthly_df[["month", "cases_started", "unknown_activity_share",
                  "case_cohort_part_wait_median_h", "case_cohort_remote_fix_median_h"]])
```

*Notes on interpretation*:  
- The **case-start cohort** answers “for cases created in this month, what was their eventual process performance?” – aligns with model evaluation.  
- The **eventcalendar slice** answers “what process behaviour occurred during this calendar month?” – useful to detect realtime degradation of a specific edge.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis  

1. **Retraining on immature labels (October)**  
   *Failure mode*: The model learns that October’s breach rate is only 0.16 – a severe underestimation because most true breaches are not yet labelled. Deployed model would assign low risk to longrunning V3 cases, dramatically increasing operational breaches.  
   *Mitigation*: Enforce a **minimum labelmaturity gate** (90%) before any cohort is used. Produce survivaladjusted outcome estimates for partially mature months.  

2. **Calibrating around a pipeline bug (April)**  
   *Failure mode*: Applying recalibration (e.g., Platt scaling) on April’s contaminated data would make the model “learn” the inflated 0.28 predicted mean as correct, permanently distorting the probability scale. It masks the underlying pipeline failure rather than fixing it.  
   *Mitigation*: Always run **dataquality checks** (unknownactivity rate, missing features) before calibration. If checks fail, reprocess the data through the corrected pipeline first.  

3. **Ignoring genuine supplierdriven concept drift (V3, July–October)**  
   *Failure mode*: The AUROC drop is attributed entirely to label delay or population shift; the team recalibrates overall probabilities or simply waits for labels to mature. Meanwhile, the model continues to underpredict V3 risk for months, leading to latestage repair escalations and SLA breaches.  
   *Mitigation*: Monitor **variantstratified breach rates** and **performanceDFG edges**. When a variant’s realised breach deviates >20% from predicted without a corresponding rise in predicted risk for identical paths, trigger a conceptdrift investigation regardless of aggregate metrics.  

4. **Attributing PartnerZ’s population shift to unfair model behaviour without stratified evidence**  
   *Failure mode*: Observing higher breach rates and worse calibration for PartnerZ, the team assumes the model is biased and launches a fairness remediation – e.g., reweighting samples or adjusting thresholds – while the root cause is missing features (rural distance, device age) and a genuinely more challenging process environment. This misdirected effort wastes time and may harm performance for other segments.  
   *Mitigation*: Conduct **transportstyle checks** and compare performance conditional on available features. If the gap persists only because of unobserved characteristics, prioritise feature engineering and data collection over fairness postprocessing.
