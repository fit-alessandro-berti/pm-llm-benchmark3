# 1. Executive Memo

**TO:** Senior Management, Telecom Provider
**FROM:** [Your Name/Title], Process Analysis & Strategy
**DATE:** [Current Date]
**SUBJECT:** Recommendation on Redesign R: A Controlled, Segment-Specific Path Forward

Management must decide how to proceed with the fiber-installation pilot, Redesign R. The evidence is conflicting: it delivered speed for simple orders but introduced severe tail risks, compliance gaps, and rework for complex installations. This memo recommends a calibrated hybrid strategy to capture the proven benefits while containing the demonstrated harms.

The core of Redesign R—the **coordinator ownership model**—shows clear value. It reduced handovers from 34% to 12% and accelerated median completion for the large "simple house" segment. However, the pilot also introduced two high-risk changes: **(1) the predictive rule that skips field surveys** and **(2) moving activation checks after billing start**. These changes systematically failed for apartments and old buildings, causing a 7-11 percentage point drop in first-time-right rates, a dramatic worsing of the tail (p95 cycle time from 45 to 62 days), and a 50% relative increase in complaints.

The conflicting metrics are not noise; they signal a clear pattern. The redesign optimized the process for the 34% of low-risk cases at the expense of the 47% of cases involving apartments, old buildings, or business customers. This created a fragile system where a single coordinator absence stalled cases and where "installed but not working" became the dominant complaint.

**Therefore, we recommend a hybrid approach:**

1.  **Scale the Coordinator Model, but with Guardrails:** Immediately expand the single-owner coordinator model to all regions, but **only for simple, low-building-risk residential orders**. This segment showed a -6 day median improvement and +3pp in first-time-right.
2.  **Suspend High-Risk Changes:** Do **not** scale the predictive survey-skip rule or the post-billing activation protocol. Reinstate mandatory field surveys for all medium-risk, old, or apartment buildings and require activation verification before billing for all segments. This directly addresses the tail risks and compliance issues.
3.  **Run Targeted Experiments:** Launch a controlled 8-week experiment in one region to properly validate the survey-skip rule and the coordinator absence resilience, using the measurement investments outlined below.
4.  **Invest in Measurement:** Allocate budget for the critical next-phase measurements (M1, M2, M4, M6, M8) to resolve the cost and quality uncertainties for complex segments before any wider decision.

This path avoids the false choice between full-scale and rollback. It harvests the proven efficiency gains for the majority of straightforward orders while containing the process instability and cost overruns in the critical tail. We will have the data to make a more confident, broader rollout decision within 10 weeks.

# 3. Explanation of Metric Conflicts

The conflicting evidence arises because Redesign R traded **process consistency and compliance for speed on a subset of cases**, exacerbating risk in others.

*   **High Fitness, Low Precision:** Fitness (0.94) means the model accurately *replays* the pilot logs. Low precision (0.51) means the model is overly permissive—it allows many pathways not actually observed, indicating a lack of enforced structure. The redesign created more ad-hoc variants (e.g., skipping surveys, billing before activation), so the log has many unique paths, lowering precision.
*   **Improved Median, Worse Tail:** The merged task and skip rule sped up the "happy path" for simple cases, improving the median. However, for complex cases (apartments, old buildings), skipping surveys led to installation failures and rework, pushing the worst-case durations far out, thus inflating the 90th and 95th percentiles.
*   **Fewer Handovers, Lower Resilience:** Consolidating work under coordinators reduced formal handovers. However, it also eliminated natural checkpoints and alternative pathways. When a coordinator was absent or overloaded (at 93% utilization), there was no fallback, causing cases to stall and reducing system resilience.
*   **Lower Visible Cost, Higher Downstream Cost:** The immediate cost per case (pre-complaint) fell, likely from fewer tasks. However, the increase in failed installations (18% second visits), manual activations (15%), and complaints (9.8%) generated significant hidden downstream costs in rework, service calls, and credits, eroding the initial savings.
*   **Improvement for Simple, Deterioration for Complex:** The redesign's shortcuts (merged tasks, survey skip) were designed with a simple, low-risk case in mind. For this segment, it worked. For complex cases (medium/high building risk), these shortcuts removed necessary safeguards (site surveys, careful activation), directly causing errors and delays. The process was "one-size-fits-most," but the "most" was only the simple segment.

# 5. Value-of-Information Analysis

Ranked by decision value (ability to resolve critical uncertainties in the recommended hybrid path):

1.  **M6: Validate the building-risk predictive rule.** **Highest Value.** Directly tests the safety of the survey-skip rule. If the rule can be made accurate for medium-risk apartments, it could be safely re-enabled for that sub-segment, expanding the benefits. If not, it must be permanently retired.
2.  **M1: Randomized pilot of survey skipping.** **High Value.** Provides causal evidence (not just correlation) on the impact of skipping, stratified by risk. This is the gold standard to validate M6's findings and quantify the true effect on rework and tail risk.
3.  **M4: Measure full downstream cost.** **High Value.** Resolves the most critical financial uncertainty. Determines if the visible cost savings are real or merely an illusion created by shifting costs to complaint handling and rework. Essential for any future ROI calculation.
4.  **M8: Collect separate customer experience metrics.** **Medium-High Value.** Disentangles speed-of-appointment satisfaction from service-reliability satisfaction. Prevents misguided optimization of a single metric (NPS) that conflates different experiences.
5.  **M2: Instrument start timestamps.** **Medium Value.** Provides a clearer picture of where time is actually spent in the process, especially in the problematic Installation-to-Activation phase. Helps diagnose bottleneck causes.
6.  **M3: Add structured reason codes.** **Medium Value.** Moves diagnostics from observation ("Failed visit") to actionable insight ("Failed due to access issue" vs. "customer not home"). Enables targeted process improvement.
7.  **M7: Conformance monitoring rules.** **Medium Value.** An automated control to flag the two most dangerous non-conformances (Billing before Activation, Missing Permits) in real-time. A necessary operational control regardless of path chosen.
8.  **M5: Compare coordinator absence models.** **Medium-Low Value.** Important for organizational design but less urgent than resolving the core process design flaws (survey skipping, activation sequencing). Can be run in parallel.

# 6. Final Recommendation

**Recommended Decision: Adopt Alternative E (Hybrid) immediately, informed by targeted experiments.**

*   **Immediate Action (Next 2 Weeks):**
    *   **Scale** the **coordinator ownership model** nationally, **exclusively for the "Simple house, low building risk" segment** (approx. 34% of orders).
    *   **Suspend** the **predictive survey-skip rule** and the **post-billing activation protocol** for all segments. Reinstate mandatory field surveys for all non-simple, apartment, and old building addresses.

*   **What to Measure Next (Concurrent Program):**
    *   Immediately launch experiments **M1, M6, and M4** (randomized survey skip test, rule validation, full cost tracking) in a dedicated control region.
    *   Implement measurement **M7** (conformance monitoring for billing-before-activation) as a real-time control across all regions.

*   **What to Pause:**
    *   All national scaling of Redesign R beyond the specified simple-house segment until experimental results are available.

*   **Threshold for Broader Rollout:**
    *   After 8 weeks, the survey-skip rule may be reconsidered for a wider rollout **only if**:
        1.  **M6** shows the building-risk rule achieves >95% precision for identifying low-risk apartments.
        2.  **M1** shows that for validated low-risk sub-segments, skipping surveys does **not** increase first-time-right failure rates by more than 2 percentage points.
        3.  **M4** shows the total lifecycle cost (including complaints and rework) for simplified-process cases is at least 5% lower than the baseline process cost for those same segments.

This approach decisively captures the redesign's proven value, contains its proven harms, and uses targeted experimentation to unlock further potential in a data-driven, risk-managed manner.
