**Section 1 Decision Rule** components:
1. Eligibility gate: current stage strictly before Diagnosis Complete AND feasible slot time before expected Diagnosis Complete; p or corrected p above de-minimis; effect range non-trivial.
2. Probability robustification: segment-specific additive corrections (SME +0.04..0.09  central +0.065; SE 0.05..0.12  central 0.085; PS none; NNL symmetric widening of interval). Compute adjusted p with intervals carried forward. ## 1. Decision rule

**Core principle.** A high `p_now` is a description of exposure, not a reason to act. The decision variable is the *expected movable loss*: the model's calibrated breach probability times the risk reduction we can plausibly create, net of cost, disturbance, and harm pushed onto other cases.

**R1 — Eligibility gate.** A case is swarm-eligible only if (a) its stage precedes `Diagnosis Complete`, (b) the expected time until `Diagnosis Complete` exceeds the 45-minute swarm duration plus a buffer, and (c) the lower bound of the estimated risk reduction is > 0. Stage-ineligible cases exit the swarm channel entirely regardless of risk (the I-03 clause) and are routed to resolution-phase levers (staffing, customer communication, validation acceleration).

**R2 — Calibration robustification.** All probabilities entering the rule are adjusted, not raw:

- SME: add midpoint +0.065 (range +0.04/+0.09 carries through as an interval).
- Strategic Enterprise: subtract midpoint 0.085 (interval 0.05/0.12), reflecting the post-redesign regime change.
- Public Sector: no shift; carry moderate sampling uncertainty.
- Non-Native-Language Support: no directional knowledge, so widen symmetrically (±~0.10) rather than shift.

Adjusted `p` is always tracked as an interval. Decisions that are hard to reverse must survive the *lower* end of the interval, not the point estimate.

**R3 — Observationality warning.** `p_now` is fit on observational histories. The r ranges are priors on causal effect, not measurements. We never substitute `p_now` for r, and we treat r ranges as hypotheses to be tested (§6).

**R4 — Alert persistence and evidence change.** Each alert receives an event fingerprint (cause codes, artifacts attached, stage transitions). Repetition of an identical fingerprint — e.g., the 30-minute refresh re-firing with no state change — contributes **zero** to priority. Escalation requires at least one of: (i) a materially new event (new diagnostic log, config change, customer contact, stage regression); (ii) a decision-milestone crossing (time-to-deadline < 3h / 2h / 1h); (iii) interval-adjusted drift of `p`  0.10 attributed to real state changes, not imputation. Stale sequences (3 identical alerts) are demoted to a watched deferral queue.

**R5 — Prioritization score.**

> N(case)  r_center × V  €450  (1  p) × F

where (1  p) weights the false-alarm burden by the probability the intervention was unnecessary. Portfolio assembly then charges each *additional* used slot an externality   6 × 0.02 × V_dx  **€2,400** (sensitivity €1.2k–7.2k, using V_dx  €20k). Decision horizon H = time until eligibility lapses:

- **Tier 1 — act now:** H  3h and N robustly positive across the interval. Options expire; spend the slot.
- **Tier 2 — scheduled trigger:** high N but large H. Attach explicit triggers (diagnosis start, p persistence, reserve release at 15:00). Do not burn slots early.
- **Tier 3 — watch:** stale alerts, unresolved data-quality flags, or N    0. Re-scored at fixed hourly epochs (12/13/14/15:00) under a published re-entry rule (§4).

Illustrative induced operating region: with r  0.2, V = €15k, F = €300, a case must carry p  0.5 to clear cost + burden + externality share. There is no universal p-threshold; the threshold emerges from value, effect, stage, and load.

**R6 — Capacity logic.** Swarms serialize (both seniors are consumed together). Maximum one concurrent interruption of the diagnosis queue. Stop filling slots when the best marginal N    0. Keep a reserve slot while P(regulatory arrival before 15:00) × E[net benefit if it arrives] exceeds the best forgone alternate — here that alternate is I-02 at roughly +€0.6k after externality, which a regulatory scenario with even ~25% arrival probability beats easily.

**R7 — Robustness constraints.** Tier-1 auto-service requires lower-interval N > 0; data-quality-flagged cases need feature verification or a haircut p before competing for slots; human overrides are logged.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

- **Cool-down:** after an alert is dispositioned (serve, defer, dismiss), identical-fingerprint re-alerts are suppressed for 2h or until a milestone grid (T3h/2h/1h) is crossed. Purpose: neutralize the artificial urgency created by 30-minute refreshes.
- **Suppression:** 3 identical fingerprints  the case moves to a watched deferral queue with priority frozen at its original evaluation. Priority rises again **only** through: new evidence fingerprint, §1-R2 interval drift attributable to real state change, or a hard safety valve — time-to-deadline < 2h (because option value collapses independently of information content).
- **Escalation:** new evidence restarts the lifecycle with full standing; milestone crossings force a fresh decision; a verified cause (e.g., I-06's field repaired confirming the surge) reinstates or upgrades standing.
- **Re-entry:** all deferred cases recompete at fixed public epochs (hourly, :00) under the same N   rule. Predictable cadence protects queue managers and prevents lobbying-driven queue-jumping.
- **Effort cap:** number of decision reviews per case per day capped, protecting specialist attention from chronic re-litigation.

**I-05 vs I-07 (explicit contrast).** Both are third alerts with no prior swarm. *I-05*: identical fingerprints, static state — the system is repeating itself; repetition must not buy priority, or every high-frequency refresher learns to spam its way to capacity. It stays deferred with a starvation-watch flag; its genuinely positive N means it will be served if late-day capacity frees up. *I-07*: the customer uploaded a new diagnostic log — a material new event within the meaning of R4 — so its third alert is evaluated as if fresh. Combined with p  0.785 after SME correction and 90 minutes of life left in the option, it jumps to Tier 1. Same alert count, opposite dispositions: the differentiator is evidence and expiring option value, not frequency.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

- **Value-based starvation.** SME/Public Sector cases lose ties to Strategic Enterprise euro amounts (today: I-05 and I-01 wait behind €60–100k cases). *Safeguards:* (1) an explicit daily equity budget — one swarm slot benchmarked against a value-blind ordering (urgency × effect only), reported publicly, so deviations are visible decisions rather than invisible drift; (2) a "silent cohort" dashboard tracking eligible-but-never-served cases and eventual breach outcomes by segment and value band; (3) quarterly counterfactual audit rerunning the rule with V removed, quantifying the gap; (4) in deferral decisions, note that low false-alarm burden cases (like I-05, I-07) are cheap to disturb — the discriminator should be evidence and effect, not invoice size.
- **Non-Native-Language Support uncertainty.** Sparse data means both p and r are unreliable — risking both over- and under-service, and confounding language support with severity mix. *Safeguards:* symmetric interval widening in scoring (applied to I-09); every NLL decision reviewed by a human until segment n crosses a minimum threshold; NLL cases exempt from automatic suppression; effects estimated for NLL with hierarchical (partial-pooling) shrinkage rather than raw segment rates; results fed back as a labeled pilot.
- **Differential calibration.** Corrections exist in three directions (SME up, Enterprise down, PS flat, NLL unknown). *Safeguards:* only pre-registered backtest-derived corrections, recomputed monthly with minimum-sample gates; intervals retained end-to-end so downstream decisions inherit the doubt; irreversibility asymmetry — actions taken on the lower confidence bound, escalations considered on the upper bound; any ad hoc manual override logged and audited to prevent gaming.
- **Selective intervention corrupting future labels.** Swarming high-risk cases hides their counterfactual breaches; the next model trained on post-policy logs concludes "these cases weren't that risky," degrading the very signal the policy depends on, and shrinking apparent intervention value in observed data. *Safeguards:* retain a small ethics-permissible randomized holdout (~10–20% of marginal Tier-1/Tier-2 eligibles) for measurement; log shadow (pre-decision) predictions for every case regardless of action; analyze intent-to-treat versus treatment-received separately; train predictive models only on pre-policy windows or propensity-weighted data; never feed "post-swarm success" back as evidence the alert was a false alarm.
- **Systematic spillover incidence.** Untreated diagnosis-stage cases absorb the delay; if that queue skews toward particular segments, unseen harms track group membership. *Safeguards:*  internalizes expected harm in the decision rule (done); cap of one concurrent dx interruption with batched releases; per-segment dwell-time deltas measured on swarm days vs quiet days; harmed untreated cases receive compensation (priority requeue or service credit); a governance tripwire if incidence disparity across segments exceeds a pre-set bound.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational monitoring.** Slot utilization vs policy (eligibility violations — anything attempted post-`Diagnosis Complete` — should read zero); alert-to-decision latency; percentage of days the reserve survived to 15:00 and what claimed it; cool-down/suppression compliance; senior interruption minutes and dx-throughput losses per swarm day (target: inside the 8–20 min/slot band).

**Predictive monitoring.** Rolling per-segment calibration curves, Brier scores, and ECE with bootstrap intervals, plus a dedicated PSI drift monitor on the imputed-field population (so an I-06-type artifact pattern is detected portfolio-wide, not case-by-case). Alert precision measured on the randomized holdout, not on intervened outcomes.

**Causal-effect estimation.** Maintain a registry of intended-vs-delivered swarms. Primary estimator: randomized/lottery-based holdout among eligible cases (capacity limits make controlled denial feasible and defensible), analyzed as difference in breach outcomes at matched horizons, stratified by segment and stage; secondary quasi-experiments from natural slot scarcity. Update r priors quarterly; shrink sparse segments hierarchically. Flag any segment whose measured effect misses its supplied range.

**Capacity monitoring.** Dx-dwell-time distributions on swarm vs non-swarm days; spillover coefficient re-estimated empirically (does one slot really move six untreated cases by 0.01–0.03, and on whom?); next-24h arrival and backlog carryover to detect "whack-a-mole" dynamics (breaches displaced, not prevented); base rate of unscheduled regulatory incidents to recalibrate the reserve prior P(arrival).

**Fairness monitoring.** Service rates, breach rates, and starve-rates by segment, language-support status, and value quartile; spillover incidence split the same way; complaint/disruption counts; quarterly parity report with the counterfactual (V-blind) policy comparison.

**Change control — when to move thresholds or allocation.** Pre-registered triggers force a policy review: (i) any segment's ECE worsens by >0.03; (ii) the measured average r's confidence interval excludes the assumed midpoint by >0.05 for four consecutive weeks; (iii) portfolio net-per-slot negative two weeks running; (iv) fairness disparity beyond the governance bound. Calibration layers re-fit weekly; effect estimates only from identified designs, never from naive post-policy outcome diffs. Allocation-rule changes ship as gated experiments (alternating time-block A/B where volume permits) with rollback plans, and every manual override feeds a monthly review so human discretion is measured, not silently absorbed into the model.
