# 1. Assumptions and notation

Times are reported clock times on 2026-05-12. Durations are in minutes unless stated otherwise.

- **A1 — Interval convention.** An activity occupies the half-open interval $[\text{start},\text{end})$. A case is waiting for a station on $[\text{queue-entry},\text{start})$, in service on $[\text{start},\text{end})$, and in station sojourn from queue entry to end. Simultaneous rounded events therefore do not create double-counting: an end at $t$ and a start at $t$ contribute zero-length overlap. Queue/WIP at an instant is measured just after processing events at that instant under this convention.
- **A2 — Queue entries.** S1 queue entry is `arrival_s1`. S2 queue entry is `s1_end`, consistent with the stated consecutive mandatory stations and no intentional bypass.
- **A3 — Rounding.** A reported time $t$ represents approximately $[t-2.5,t+2.5]$ minutes. Consequently, a duration computed from two independently rounded times has a first-order rounding uncertainty of roughly $\pm5$ min, before any missingness. Bounds below are first shown on reported times; rounding can widen a feasible missing-start interval by a few minutes at each end. I do not treat $\pm5$ min as a hard guarantee because the prompt says “approximately”.
- **A4 — Capacity.** S1 has I1,I2; S2 has C1,C2; each is single-task. C2 capacity is removed 10:55–11:10. No preemption is assumed for the main reconstruction. Apparent overlaps after rounding are flagged, not automatically treated as physical double-booking.
- **A5 — Missing starts.** `MISSING` means the activity definitely occurred, its resource and completion are known, but start is unknown. It is **not** zero service and **not** equal to completion. Feasible start must satisfy: $\max(\text{queue entry},\text{same-resource previous end},\text{any outage constraint if applicable}) \le \text{start} \le \text{end}$, with nonnegative service. For S2, entry is S1 end. For C2, a start inside 10:55–11:10 is treated as infeasible in the central scenario; starting before and spanning the outage would violate A4 unless service was paused, which is not documented.
- **A6 — Open and blank.** `OPEN` means started and unfinished at 14:00: elapsed service at 14:00 is a lower bound on final service. Blank S2 for completed S1 means still waiting for S2 at 14:00: elapsed S2 wait is a lower bound. Blank S1 means still waiting for S1: elapsed S1 wait is a lower bound. These are right-censored, not missing-at-random.
- **A7 — No hidden arrivals/departures.** The 22 cases are the observed case universe in 08:00–14:00. The absence of arrivals after 12:10 is treated as observed, but it could also reflect extraction truncation; therefore late-window arrival rates are especially fragile.
- **A8 — Central imputations are scenarios, not replacements.** For missing starts I report mathematical feasible bounds first, then scenario imputations. “Optimistic” means earliest feasible start / shortest wait. “Pessimistic-wait” means latest mathematically feasible start, which drives service toward zero and is usually operationally implausible; I therefore also discuss a “plausible” narrowed range using typical observed service times where helpful.
- **A9 — PM4Py semantics.** PM4Py case-level service/waiting and arrival/finish-rate functions describe whole cases from first to last event, not station queues. They are demonstrated only on a fully observed complete-case projection and must not be read as S1/S2 station metrics.

Notation for a case at station $k\in\{1,2\}$: queue entry $a_k$, start $s_k$, end $e_k$, wait $w_k=s_k-a_k$, service $p_k=e_k-s_k$, station sojourn $d_k=e_k-a_k=w_k+p_k$. Total case sojourn for completed cases is $e_2-\text{arrival}_{S1}$.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

**Observed durations.** Where both timestamps exist, $w_k$ and $p_k$ are computed directly and carry roughly $\pm5$ min rounding uncertainty. Station sojourn $d_k$ computed from entry to end is often more robust for missing-start cases because it does not depend on the unknown split between wait and service.

**Missing-start bounds.** For missing $s_k$ with known $a_k,e_k$, resource $r$, previous end on $r$ equal to $prev_r$, and next start on $r$ equal to $next_r$:

$$
L=\max(a_k, prev_r, \text{outage lower constraints}),\quad U=\min(e_k,next_r)
$$

with $s_k\in[L,U]$, $w_k\in[L-a_k,U-a_k]$, $p_k\in[e_k-U,e_k-L]$, and $w_k+p_k=e_k-a_k=d_k$ on reported times. Midpoint imputation uses $s_k^{mid}=(L+U)/2$ only as a scenario; it is not privileged physically. A service-informed plausible scenario chooses $p_k$ near the station’s observed service distribution, then sets $s_k=e_k-p_k$ if feasible; if that violates resource availability, it is truncated to the feasible interval and flagged.

**OPEN / waiting censoring.** At censoring time $T=14{:}00$: if open, elapsed service $=T-s_k$ and final service $>T-s_k$; if waiting, elapsed wait $=T-a_k$ and final wait $>T-a_k$. Open cases are included in WIP and busy-time up to $T$, excluded from completed-service means, and never assigned final durations.

**Throughput and rates.** For interval $[u,v)$: arrivals/entries are cases with $a_k\in[u,v)$; completed services are cases with $e_k\in[u,v)$. Rates are counts divided by hours. Because the window starts empty for practical purposes but ends with substantial WIP, period rates are descriptive flows, not steady-state capacities.

**WIP and queue length.** Using A1, for each minute grid or event sweep:

$$
Q_k(t)=\#\{a_k\le t<s_k\},\quad B_k(t)=\#\{s_k\le t<e_k \text{ or censored}\},\quad N_k(t)=Q_k(t)+B_k(t)
$$

with missing starts contributing interval-valued $Q_k,B_k$ but a more stable $N_k$ because $N_k$ depends on entry and end, not on the unknown split. Total WIP is $N_1+N_2$ counting a case once by its current station; a completed-S1 case waiting for S2 is S2 WIP, not S1 WIP.

**Busy time and utilization.** For resource $r$ over interval $[u,v)$, busy time is the union length of $[s,e)\cap[u,v)$ over cases assigned to $r$, using elapsed portions for OPEN cases and feasible ranges for missing starts. Utilization is busy resource-minutes divided by available resource-minutes. S2 available minutes subtract C2’s 15 outage minutes when the denominator is “available capacity”; I also note the gross 08:00–14:00 denominator where useful. Completed cases alone understate utilization because OPEN cases P15/P17 were busy at 14:00 and missing-start services still occupied resources before their known ends.

**Little’s Law.** The steady-state form $L=\lambda W$ is used only as a truncated accounting identity check over 08:00–14:00: time-averaged WIP equals the sum of within-window station sojourn portions divided by 360 min, and entry-rate times mean truncated sojourn reproduces it by construction when entries, exits, and censored portions are all included. A departure-only steady-state interpretation is not defended because queues grow and the end state is loaded. This distinction matters: agreement of the identity is not evidence of stationarity.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

**Evidence.** Early, S1 is the first constraint: seven cases arrive in the first hour while two inspectors with ~25–30 min services can complete only about 4–4.5 cases/hour if continuously busy. S1 waits rise from 5 min for P01/P02 to 40–50 min by P06/P08 and 60–100 min for the late-morning cohort. S2 waits are initially small (5–15 min for P01–P04) because S1 meters flow downstream.

From roughly 10:20 onward, the evidence shifts downstream. S2 waits become 25 min (P06/P09), then 45–65 min (P10–P14 cohort), and by 14:00 S2 holds six WIP cases: four waiting and two in service. S1 still matters—P22 never starts S1 and P21 is still in S1—but the marginal accumulation at the end is larger at S2. Completions over the whole window are 20 at S1 versus 14 at S2, with seven cases having entered S2 but not completed by 14:00 if open/waiting downstream cases are counted by station state. Nominal capacity also favors this interpretation: S2 service times are longer on average (~40 min) than S1 (~28 min), so two S2 resources have lower nominal throughput (~3/hour) than two S1 resources (~4.3/hour), before the C2 outage.

**Time-varying bottleneck statement.** The defensible statement is not “S1 or S2” globally. It is: S1 is the initial/entry bottleneck in the first 60–90 minutes; the system becomes jointly saturated late morning; by 12:00–14:00 S2 is the dominant bottleneck because S1 backlog is being transferred into a larger S2 queue while S2 resources remain busy and four cases cannot even start calibration. Hypotheses beyond evidence—e.g., operator fatigue, case complexity drift, priority insertion—are possible but unsupported by the supplied fields. There is no priority, case-type, or rework field, so I do not infer them.

**C2 outage.** The 10:55–11:10 outage is real but small: 15 resource-min, roughly 0.3–0.5 of one S2 service. It plausibly contributes to P08’s 11:15 C2 start after being ready at 10:20 and after C2 finished P06 at 10:50; without the outage C2 might have started around 10:50–10:55, though rounding and handoff could still delay to ~11:00. It cannot explain waits of 45–85 min or the six-case S2 WIP at 14:00. Treating the outage as the main cause would be a causal overreach.

**Maintenance-window sensitivity.** If C2 had started P08 at 10:50 and service was 45 min, P08 would end ~11:35 instead of 12:00, freeing C2 earlier for P11. But P11’s feasible start is still bounded by C2 availability and its own 11:05 entry; the queue behind P08/P11 would remain because multiple cases entered S2 faster than two calibrators could clear them.

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

Three scenarios are compared. “Mathematical bounds” are wider than all three when a start could approach its end; the pessimistic-wait mathematical bound is included to show the limit, not as a believable operation.

| scenario | imputation rule for MISSING starts | effect on split wait/service | conclusions affected |
|---|---|---|---|
| Optimistic-wait / earliest feasible | Start at $\max(\text{entry},\text{previous resource end})$: P05 S2 09:45; P07 S1 09:25; P11 S2 12:00; P15 S1 11:40 | Minimizes waits, maximizes services: P05 S2 service 30, P11 25, P07/P15 S1 service 35 | Gives shortest missing-case waits. Still S2 waits for P11 55 and P05 20. Bottleneck shift remains. |
| Central midpoint | Start at midpoint of feasible interval | P05 wait/service 35/15; P11 67.5/12.5; P07 57.5/17.5; P15 107.5/17.5 | Convenient but produces implausibly short services relative to observed distributions. It overstates missing-case waits by ~10–17 min and understates busy time. I do not use it for headline utilization. |
| Pessimistic-wait mathematical | Start arbitrarily close to end | Waits approach full sojourn: P05 50, P11 80, P07 75, P15 125; services approach 0 | Useful only as a bound. If interpreted literally, it would falsely imply resources were idle while cases waited despite resource-busy neighbors. Reject as an operational scenario. |
| Service-informed plausible (preferred sensitivity) | Choose service near observed station distribution if feasible: S1 ~25–30; S2 ~40 preferred but capped by feasibility | P07 S1 start 09:30–09:35, wait 45–50; P15 start 11:45–11:50, wait 95–100; P05 S2 service capped 30, wait 20–30; P11 service capped 25, wait 55–60 | Narrows ranges greatly. Raises S1 utilization to low-80s%. Makes P05/P11 look like short-service or logging-anomaly cases rather than long-wait cases. |

**Stable conclusions.** Across all non-degenerate scenarios: waits increase over the morning; S2 sojourn for missing-start cases is fixed by entry/end (P05 50, P11 80) even though the wait/service split is uncertain; the end-of-window S2 WIP is six and exceeds S1 WIP two; the C2 outage is too short to explain systemic congestion; completed-case-only analysis understates late congestion and S2 busy time; total completed sojourn rises mainly through waiting.

**Unstable or fragile conclusions.** Exact mean S2 wait is sensitive to P05/P11 splits by up to 30 and 25 min respectively for those cases, though their station sojourns are not sensitive. Exact S1 utilization is sensitive at the few-percent level to P07/P15. The P11 service time is intrinsically suspicious: on reported times it cannot exceed 25 min if C2 was truly free only after P08 at 12:00 and single-tasked. Rounding could stretch this slightly, undocumented help could invalidate the resource constraint, or the end/resource could be mislogged; the data cannot distinguish these. Any claim that the outage “caused” the afternoon backlog is unstable; any claim that one station was the bottleneck for the entire window is also unstable.

**Censoring effect.** If OPEN/waiting cases are dropped, S1 loses P21/P22 and S2 loses P15–P20 except completed history, creating a survivorship bias toward shorter waits. If OPEN elapsed times are treated as final, S2 service is biased down by P17 especially and S1 service by P21. The correct treatment is lower bounds plus WIP/busy inclusion up to 14:00.

**Rounding effect.** A ±5 min duration uncertainty can reorder nearly simultaneous events and create/remove small apparent resource gaps, but it cannot explain 50–110 min waits. It can explain why some consecutive same-resource jobs show 5-min gaps that may be zero actual handoff time.

**Little’s-Law consistency check.** Using truncated within-window sojourns over 08:00–14:00: S1 truncated sojourn sum is 2065 min over 22 entries, mean 93.9 min, entry rate 3.667/h, product $=5.74$ cases, matching time-averaged S1 WIP $2065/360=5.74$. S2 truncated sojourn sum is 1340 min over 20 entries, mean 67.0 min, entry rate 3.333/h, product $=3.72$, matching $1340/360=3.72$. This is an accounting identity with censored portions included, not proof of steady state. A steady-state departure-based check is not defended in any subinterval because arrivals, queues, and end WIP change materially.

T_END = pd.Timestamp("2026-05-12 14:00")
OUTAGE = (pd.Timestamp("2026-05-12 10:55"), pd.Timestamp("2026-05-12 11:10"))

df = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(RAW), dtype=str).fillna("")

def parse_ts(x):
    """Status-aware parsing: blank/MISSING/OPEN are preserved as NaT here,
    but the original string remains available in df for flag logic."""
    if x in ("", "MISSING", "OPEN"):
        return pd.NaT
    return pd.Timestamp("2026-05-12 " + x)

for c in ["arrival_s1", "s1_start", "s1_end", "s2_start", "s2_end"]:
    df[c + "_ts"] = df[c].map(parse_ts)

def mins(a, b):
    return (b - a).total_seconds() / 60.0 if pd.notna(a) and pd.notna(b) else np.nan

# Previous/next known activity on same resource, used for missing-start bounds.
def resource_bounds(row, station):
    if station == 1:
        res, end = row["s1_resource"], row["s1_end_ts"]
        start_col, end_col, res_col = "s1_start_ts", "s1_end_ts", "s1_resource"
        entry = row["arrival_s1_ts"]
    else:
        res, end = row["s2_resource"], row["s2_end_ts"]
        start_col, end_col, res_col = "s2_start_ts", "s2_end_ts", "s2_resource"
        entry = row["s1_end_ts"]
    same = df[df[res_col] == res]
    prev_ends = same.loc[same[end_col].notna() & (same[end_col] <= end), end_col]
    next_starts = same.loc[same[start_col].notna() & (same[start_col] >= end), start_col]
    lo = entry
    if len(prev_ends):
        lo = max(lo, prev_ends.max())
    if station == 2 and res == "C2":
        # Central assumption: no C2 start inside maintenance window.
        if lo < OUTAGE[1] and end > OUTAGE[0]:
            lo = max(lo, OUTAGE[1]) if lo >= OUTAGE[0] else lo
    hi = end
    if len(next_starts):
        hi = min(hi, next_starts.min())
    return lo, hi

    rec = {"case": r["case"], "state": r["state_at_14_00"]}
    # S1
    if pd.notna(r["s1_start_ts"]) and pd.notna(r["s1_end_ts"]):
        rec["S1_wait"] = mins(r["arrival_s1_ts"], r["s1_start_ts"])
        rec["S1_service"] = mins(r["s1_start_ts"], r["s1_end_ts"])

    else:
        rec["S1_wait"] = f">={mins(r['arrival_s1_ts'], T_END):.0f} censored, not started"
        rec["S1_service"] = "not started"
    # S2
    if pd.notna(r["s1_end_ts"]):
        if pd.notna(r["s2_start_ts"]) and pd.notna(r["s2_end_ts"]):
            rec["S2_wait"] = mins(r["s1_end_ts"], r["s2_start_ts"])
            rec["S2_service"] = mins(r["s2_start_ts"], r["s2_end_ts"])
        elif r["s2_start"] == "MISSING":
            lo, hi = resource_bounds(r, 2)
            rec["S2_wait"] = f"[{mins(r['s1_end_ts'], lo):.0f},{mins(r['s1_end_ts'], hi):.0f}]"
            rec["S2_service"] = f"[{mins(hi, r['s2_end_ts']):.0f},{mins(lo, r['s2_end_ts']):.0f}]"
        elif r["s2_end"] == "OPEN":
            rec["S2_wait"] = mins(r["s1_end_ts"], r["s2_start_ts"])
            rec["S2_service"] = f">={mins(r['s2_start_ts'], T_END):.0f} censored"
        else:
            rec["S2_wait"] = f">={mins(r['s1_end_ts'], T_END):.0f} censored, not started"
            rec["S2_service"] = "not started"
    else:
        rec["S2_wait"] = "not entered"
        rec["S2_service"] = "not entered"
    rows.append(rec)

case_table = pd.DataFrame(rows)
print("\nCASE-LEVEL RECONSTRUCTION (reported times; durations in min)")
print(case_table.to_string(index=False))

# pm4py.convert_log_to_time_intervals is not used for station WIP because it converts
# a whole event log into case/activity intervals; station queue length needs explicit
# entry/start/end/censoring semantics, resource outage handling, and missing-start
# bounds. A direct pandas sweep with half-open intervals is safer for that part.

# Direct pandas WIP / truncated-sojourn accounting identity (Little check).
def station_frame(station):
    out = []
    for _, r in df.iterrows():
        if station == 1:
            entry, end = r["arrival_s1_ts"], r["s1_end_ts"]
            end_raw = r["s1_end"]
        else:
            entry, end = r["s1_end_ts"], r["s2_end_ts"]
            end_raw = r["s2_end"]
        if pd.isna(entry):
            continue
        trunc_end = end if pd.notna(end) else T_END
        out.append({"case": r["case"], "entry": entry,
                    "trunc_sojourn_min": mins(entry, trunc_end),
                    "completed": pd.notna(end) and end_raw != "OPEN"})
    return pd.DataFrame(out)

for station in [1, 2]:
    sf = station_frame(station)
    area = sf["trunc_sojourn_min"].sum()
    lam_per_h = len(sf) / 6.0
    mean_trunc_h = sf["trunc_sojourn_min"].mean() / 60.0
    print(f"\nS{station} truncated Little identity: entries={len(sf)}, "
          f"completed={int(sf['completed'].sum())}, area_min={area:.0f}, "
          f"time_avg_WIP={area/360:.2f}, lambda*Wtrunc={lam_per_h*mean_trunc_h:.2f}")

# Busy-time ranges: observed/OPEN elapsed at face value; missing starts vary service
# between feasible bounds. This is interval arithmetic, not a single imputation.
def busy_range(station):
    lo = hi = 0.0
    for _, r in df.iterrows():
        if station == 1:
            s_raw, e_raw, s, e = r["s1_start"], r["s1_end"], r["s1_start_ts"], r["s1_end_ts"]
        else:
            s_raw, e_raw, s, e = r["s2_start"], r["s2_end"], r["s2_start_ts"], r["s2_end_ts"]
        if pd.notna(s) and pd.notna(e):
            lo += mins(s, e); hi += mins(s, e)
        elif pd.notna(s) and e_raw == "OPEN":
            lo += mins(s, T_END); hi += mins(s, T_END)  # elapsed only; final is larger
        elif s_raw == "MISSING" and pd.notna(e):
            blo, bhi = resource_bounds(r, station)
            lo += mins(bhi, e); hi += mins(blo, e)
    return lo, hi

for station, avail in [(1, 720), (2, 705)]:
    lo, hi = busy_range(station)
    print(f"S{station} elapsed busy range = [{lo:.0f},{hi:.0f}] resource-min; "
          f"utilization vs available = [{100*lo/avail:.1f}%, {100*hi/avail:.1f}%] "
          f"(OPEN finals would increase the upper side beyond window-elapsed accounting)")
