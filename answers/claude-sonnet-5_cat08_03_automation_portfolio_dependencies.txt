# 2. Dependency and Prerequisite Map

**Upstream data-quality dependencies.** A6 (normalization) is only as good as document intake, which depends on A5 fixing the Financial-document-review  Request-missing-documents loop; feeding unnormalized or incomplete statements into Credit analysis or Committee packaging (A8) reproduces the loop downstream rather than removing it. A8 is the sharpest example of compounded dependency: it AND-joins KYC status, normalized financials, collateral valuation (if required), and credit-analysis notes — automating assembly before A3/A4/A6/A7 are trustworthy just automates the delivery of bad packets to the Committee.

**Legal/compliance controls.** A9 must operationalize the "contract terms must match Committee decision" rule, not just template text. A8/A9 tooling must preserve the four-eyes rule (Credit analysis and Committee packaging cannot be the same person for High-exposure SME) even when steps are software-assisted — the audit trail must show two distinct human sign-offs, not one operator clicking through an automated pipeline. A10/A11 are legally blocked by the "KYC clearance precedes signing" rule; any reminder/e-sign automation must read a clearance flag, not case status alone, or it will scale the existing 6.5% provisional-signing leakage.

**Human escalation capacity.** A3 and A4 both terminate in human escalation to Compliance specialists — only 3 FTE, already the most fragile resource in the handover network. Expanding *intake* to this pool (e.g., via A1 pushing more cases through classification and KYC faster) without first giving specialists better tooling (A4) or filtering (A3) simply moves the queue, it doesn't shrink it. Any KYC-related automation must be sized against this 3-FTE ceiling.

**External valuation dependency.** The 58-hour "Wait for external valuation" is largely outside internal control. A7 can only reduce the internal handling (20 min mean) and reduce the 19% late-valuation rate through triage/parallel routing; it cannot compress the fundamental external SLA. Committee packaging (A8) therefore cannot be fully "fixed" by internal automation alone when collateral is required (71% of Equipment finance, 18% of Working-capital) — expectations must be set that A7 improves *synchronization reliability*, not raw cycle time.

**Core-banking integration constraints.** With only two deep integrations possible in six months, the natural split is: modules touching system-of-record transactions and legal/contract data (A9, and eventually A1/A11) need deep integration; modules that are primarily decision-support/workflow layers sitting alongside core banking (A4's mismatch assistant, A12's workbench, A6's extraction pipeline reading documents) can be integrated more lightly first and hardened later. This ordering also reduces risk: lighter-footprint modules can be piloted and monitored before committing scarce deep-integration slots.

# 3. Portfolio Alternatives

**Portfolio Q — Quick-win:** A1, A5, A9, A10
Optimizes: visible labor savings on high-volume/high-feasibility steps, fast political win, customer-facing improvement (e-sign), some control benefit via A9's rule checks.
Sacrifices: does not touch the entity-mismatch (31h wait) or external-valuation (58h wait) synchronization points; risks pushing more volume into the 3-FTE compliance and 9-FTE analyst pools (A1) and risks scaling provisional-signing violations (A10) unless explicitly KYC-gated. Does not fully satisfy the compliance mandate for a control-first module.

**Portfolio B — Bottleneck/synchronization:** A4, A6, A7, A3
Optimizes: the actual drivers of end-to-end duration and rework — entity-mismatch delay/rework, analyst overload risk, collateral synchronization reliability, and KYC hit-handling volume into the specialist pool.
Sacrifices: little immediate high-frequency labor-saving story for management; all modules are medium feasibility, so time-to-value is slower and requires more compliance/analyst involvement in design; does not by itself guarantee a "controls, not just labor" module unless A3's audit-trail design is emphasized.

**Portfolio C — Compliance-first:** A2, A3, A9, A12
Optimizes: control integrity — reduced misrouting (A2), auditable KYC screening (A3), contract-decision alignment (A9), and a dedicated remediation/traceability layer (A12) that satisfies the explicit compliance requirement.
Sacrifices: slower, less visible throughput improvement on the highest-frequency, highest-wait activities (entity mismatch, external valuation, signing); management's initial "automate the biggest number" expectation is not met.

**Portfolio R — Recommended/Balanced (synthesis):** A4, A6, A9, A12
Combines the bottleneck fix with the least specialist-capacity risk (A4), the analyst-overload safeguard (A6), a dual labor/control win with manageable integration risk (A9), and the mandatory pure-control module (A12) — see Section 7.

# 4. Expected Process-Level Effects

**Portfolio Q (Quick-win).**
- Cycle time: modest reduction at the front end (Registration, Contract prep) but little change in the 31h/58h/44h waits that dominate total duration.
- Waiting time: largely unchanged at Resolve-entity-mismatch, Wait-for-external-valuation, Customer signing.
- Rework: reduced at Financial-document-review loop (via A5) and Contract-remediation loop (via A9); entity-mismatch rework (38%) untouched.
- Handovers: A1 increases throughput into Classify/KYC handover without increasing their capacity, likely increasing queue depth there (visible in Back-office/Compliance handover network).
- Resource demand: Compliance specialists (3 FTE) and Credit analysts (9 FTE) see *increased* pressure from faster upstream intake.
- Control violations: risk of provisional-signing rate rising if A10 is not hard-gated on KYC clearance.

**Portfolio B (Bottleneck/synchronization).**
- Cycle time: largest expected reduction in *median* end-to-end duration, since Resolve-entity-mismatch (31h, 82% critical-path presence) and collateral synchronization (58h wait feeding Committee packaging) are the biggest single contributors.
- Waiting time: direct reduction at KYC  Resolve-entity-mismatch loop (1750/1160 DFG pair) and improved reliability of the Collateral-valuation-request  Wait-for-external-valuation  Committee-packaging chain.
- Rework: entity-mismatch rework (38%) and KYC hit false-positive rate both drop; analyst rework indirectly reduced because A6 removes manual normalization errors feeding Credit analysis.
- Handovers: fewer round-trips between Compliance specialists and KYC ops; fewer re-escalations.
- Resource demand: Compliance specialists freed to handle only genuinely hard cases; Credit analysts protected from overload as normalization inflow becomes cleaner (directly addresses the resource-network warning).
- Control violations: indirectly reduced, since fewer rushed/incomplete entity resolutions feed into Contract preparation and signing.

**Portfolio C (Compliance-first).**
- Cycle time: smaller direct effect; benefits are concentrated in avoiding *rare but severe* delays from misclassification, KYC false negatives, and contract-decision mismatches rather than shrinking the largest medians.
- Waiting time: minor improvement at Classify/KYC entry; Committee-packaging wait unaffected since A8's real prerequisites (A4/A6/A7) aren't in this portfolio.
- Rework: strong reduction in Contract-remediation loop (8.5%/14%) via A9; misclassification-driven rework reduced via A2.
- Handovers: cleaner audit trail reduces informal handovers for clarification/exception chasing.
- Resource demand: relatively neutral; Legal ops load smoothed by A9.
- Control violations: direct, measurable reduction — this portfolio is explicitly built to lower four-eyes and KYC-signing violation rates and stale-rule contract risk.

**Portfolio R (Recommended).** Combines B's cycle-time/bottleneck relief (A4, A6) with C's control assurance (A9, A12), at the cost of deferring A1/A10/A3/A7/A8/A11 to Year 2 — see Section 6/7 for sequencing and monitoring.

# 5. Risks and Compensating Controls

| Risk | Where it arises | Compensating control |
|---|---|---|
| Loss of auditability when suggestions become "invisible" | A2, A3, A6, A8, A9 | Mandatory logging of model version, input snapshot, human override, and final decision for every automated suggestion (explicit requirement) |
| False negatives in KYC screening | A3 | Conservative thresholds, mandatory human review of borderline hits, periodic sampling audits by Compliance |
| False clears in entity-mismatch handling | A4 | Coverage limits enforced (only clear-cut matches auto-resolved); ambiguous cases always escalate to specialists with reason codes logged |
| Model drift in extraction/classification accuracy | A2, A6 | Scheduled recalibration, drift dashboards comparing model output vs. analyst overturn rate, retraining trigger thresholds |
| Compliance-specialist saturation from mis-sized automation coverage | A3, A4 | Cap automation coverage to specialist absorption capacity; track specialist utilization weekly; scale coverage only as backlog data confirms headroom |
| Four-eyes violations from automated packaging | A8 (and any future rollout) | Enforce role-based segregation in workflow tooling: preparer  approver for High-exposure SME, even when both steps are software-assisted; automated flag if same user ID appears in both roles |
| Provisional-signing scale-up | A10 | Hard gate: signing workflow cannot trigger unless KYC-clearance flag = cleared; any override requires documented Compliance sign-off, logged and sampled |
| Legal risk from stale contract rules | A9 | Versioned rule library with mandatory legal review cadence; automatic flag when rules haven't been reviewed within policy window |
| Garbage-in/garbage-out at Committee packaging | A8 | Do not deploy until A4/A6/A7 quality metrics cross agreed thresholds (gate criteria, Section 6) |
| External-partner quality disputes | A7 | SLA tracking, dispute log, fallback manual routing path preserved |
| Customer friction from rigid document/e-sign rules | A5, A10 | RM-in-the-loop override option; friction metrics (complaint rate, drop-off rate) monitored before scaling |

# 6. Recommended Phased Rollout

**Phase 0 (Month 1): Governance baseline** — define audit-trail schema (model suggestion / override / final decision), define four-eyes enforcement rules in workflow tooling, baseline current metrics (entity-mismatch wait, analyst utilization, contract-remediation rate, provisional-signing rate). No automation live yet.

**Phase 1 (Months 1–6): Two deep-integration modules**
1. **A4 – Entity-mismatch resolution assistant** (integrates with KYC/AML case data). Monitor: median wait before Resolve-entity-mismatch, specialist escalation rate, rework rate (target: reduce 38% rework and 31h median wait materially before Phase 2).
2. **A12 – Control remediation workbench** (integrates with KYC, contract, and signing exception logs — the mandated control-strengthening module). Monitor: visibility/closure time on all three remediation loops (entity mismatch, contract, provisional signing).
Gate to Phase 2: entity-mismatch specialist backlog reduced by an agreed margin; audit-trail completeness at or near 100% for A4/A12 decisions; no rise in false-clear complaints.

**Phase 2 (Months 7–12): Remaining two modules**
3. **A6 – Financial-statement extraction/normalization**, deployed once A5-style document-completeness practices are at least informally improved (even without full A5 automation, RM instruction templates can be tightened). Monitor: extraction accuracy vs. human baseline, analyst time-per-case, analyst utilization (must not exceed pre-automation levels — explicit resource-network concern).
4. **A9 – Contract generation with control-rule checks**, deployed once KYC-clearance and Committee-decision data quality from Phase 1 modules is confirmed reliable. Monitor: contract-remediation loop rate (target: reduce from 8.5%/14%), Legal-ops utilization variance (month-end overload should ease).

Gate to Year 2 candidates (A1, A2, A3, A5, A7, A8, A10, A11): only after Phase 1–2 metrics show (a) compliance-specialist utilization stabilized, (b) analyst utilization stabilized or improved, (c) control-violation rates trending down, (d) audit trail fully operational. A8 specifically gated on A4+A6+A7 reliability; A10/A11 gated on demonstrated KYC-clearance-flag reliability from A4/A3.

# 7. Final Recommendation

**Recommended portfolio: R — A4 (Entity-mismatch resolution assistant), A6 (Financial-statement normalization), A9 (Contract generation with control-rule checks), A12 (Control remediation workbench).**

This portfolio is chosen over the Quick-win portfolio because frequency and cycle-time impact are not the same thing in this process. Register application (11,880 cases, 8-minute task, already 72% digital) has the largest volume but the smallest marginal effect on end-to-end duration and risk: it feeds capacity-constrained downstream resources — Compliance specialists (3 FTE) and Credit analysts (9 FTE) — that are already the bottlenecks. Speeding an upstream, non-constraining step without first fixing the constraints (Resolve-entity-mismatch's 31-hour wait and 38% rework, on the critical path in 82% of the cases where it occurs; the analyst-overload risk explicitly flagged in the resource network) simply pushes more work, faster, into the same narrow pipes — a textbook case of optimizing a non-bottleneck. Automating Registration remains a good candidate, but for Year 2, once A4 and A6 have created headroom downstream.

The recommended set also satisfies every stated constraint: it stays within the four-module budget; A12 explicitly satisfies compliance's requirement for a control-strengthening module (and A9 reinforces this by directly targeting the contract-decision-mismatch control finding); it respects the two-deep-integration limit by sequencing A4/A12 (lighter-footprint, case-management-level integration) in the first six months and A6/A9 (which depend on cleaner upstream data and slightly deeper integration) in the second half; and it explicitly avoids the two anti-patterns called out in the constraints — it does not automate a downstream bot (A11, A10) ahead of upstream control reliability, and it does not treat any exception path as eliminated: A4 and A3-style escalation, A6's low-quality-document fallback, and A9's legal review sampling all preserve human judgment and compliance accountability rather than assuming automation removes the need for it.
