### K-337

**Factual mechanism:**
The legacy system created only the identity task at Triage (Tue 09:35), so D2 had no work to start until I2 completed Identity (13:20). **100-case weekly rollout analysis (250–400 words):**

Applying these four interventions to 100 similar cases in one week produces severe interference effects that cannot be predicted by single-case counterfactuals.

**C1 reservation (K-104):** Reserving provisional compliance slots for 100 cases commits C1's entire capacity (the sole certified reviewer) to a pre-committed schedule. Any non-reserved urgent case faces the full queue without the ability to jump ahead, creating a two-tier system. If reservations exceed C1's practical throughput (100 cases × 4 h review  400 h  one week's capacity), the reservation system fails: slots queue behind each other, and the "early reservation" becomes a formalized waitlist with no real benefit over the normal queue. The nonlinear effect is that reservation overhead (scheduling, slot management) itself consumes C1's time.

**D1D2 substitution (K-219):** Moving 100 standard-handwritten cases from D1 to D2 creates an inversion: D1's queue drops to near-zero while D2's queue absorbs 100 × 6 h  600 h of work — far exceeding one resource's weekly capacity (~40–50 h). D2 becomes the dominant bottleneck. Meanwhile, D1 sits idle with no work. The optimal policy would split cases between D1 and D2; routing all to D2 is systematically suboptimal. If 15% trigger rework on handwriting, that's 15 additional external-response cycles (8–36 h each), further clogging both the document queue and the supplier channel.

**Rework suppression (K-337):** Suppressing 100 second-loop rework cycles eliminates ~100 external responses, ~100 document checks, and ~100 re-assessments — a massive reduction in document and assessment queue pressure. External suppliers benefit from fewer requests, speeding responses to non-suppressed cases. However, 100 decisions on incomplete evidence in high-risk cases represent an unacceptable quality risk: even a 5% error rate yields five compromised high-value decisions with nonlinear regulatory and appeal consequences. The weekend effect is particularly relevant: suppressed loops avoid the FriMon external delay, but genuine loops from non-suppressed cases still stall over weekends.

**Assessment bottleneck (K-411):** 100 cases parallelizing verification enters assessment 1.5–3.5 h earlier each, adding 150–350 h of earlier readiness to the assessment queue. A1 and A2's combined throughput (~2 resources × 2.5 h avg  20 cases/day) cannot absorb this influx — assessment queue grows by hours for every case. True concurrency (real-time timestamps) reveals the actual throughput; batch entry may show apparent parallelism that doesn't exist, inflating metrics and masking the bottleneck shift.

## 7. Final synthesis

Of the four interventions, **K-411 (parallel verification at Triage)** appears most robust. It preserves every compliance and four-eyes constraint, changes no routing or decision policy, and delivers a modest but reliable cycle-time improvement by eliminating artificial serialization. Its benefit is bounded (0.5–3 h per case) but consistent: the identity and document checks genuinely run concurrently, and even when the assessment queue absorbs some of the saving, the case loses nothing relative to the control. It is trivially reversible and generates valuable timestamp data.

The most dangerous intervention despite potential speed is **K-337 (suppressing the second rework loop)**. It offers the largest single-case saving (up to 111 hours by avoiding a weekend-delayed external response cycle) but converts a timing question into a decision-policy question. Deciding high-risk, high-value cases on incomplete evidence erodes compliance defensibility and introduces appeal risk that is nonlinear — one adverse outcome on a high-value case can exceed all cumulative time savings from the intervention. Once a decision is issued on compromised evidence, no later process change can restore the missing verification. The intervention is also the least reversible: you cannot "undo" a decision made without the data that the rework loop would have provided.

Local case counterfactuals are least transportable to system-wide policy for **K-104 (early compliance reservation)**. The single-case benefit depends on C1 having latent capacity on that specific week (C1 queue was 20 h Mon, dropping to 5 h Thu — the reservation exploits a clearing backlog). At 100-case scale, the reservation becomes a rationing mechanism that starves non-reserved cases. What helps one case in isolation may worsen the overall compliance queue disproportionately.

The most valuable additional data would be: (1) per-resource actual start times versus queue-estimate accuracy over several weeks to calibrate timing uncertainty; (2) D2's historical rework rate on handwritten documents to quantify K-219's downside; (3) C1's real-time capacity utilization under reservation to model spillover convexity; and (4) historical appeal and regulatory-audit rates for decisions issued on partial evidence to bound K-337's quality risk.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. `structural` — After Request More Info, both a new Document Check and a new Assessment are mandatory before Decision (Rule 7), so suppressing the second RMI loop eliminates the need for both downstream activities.

2. `structural` — High-risk or high-value cases require Compliance Review strictly between the latest Assessment and Decision (Rule 5), so K-104 and K-337 must include a Compliance Review before Decision regardless of preliminary work or evidence completeness.

3. `temporal` — Queue estimates from system snapshots approximate expected wait times but may deviate by ±2–4 hours; actual queue dynamics are stochastic and may not follow the snapshot trend linearly.

4. `resource` — C1 is the sole fully certified compliance reviewer in the window and cannot parallelize review work; any reservation of C1's capacity directly subtracts from other cases' access (Rule 11).

5. `resource` — D1 is faster on complex corporate documents (3–6 h standard, 5–9 h complex) while D2 is a generalist with longer standard times (4–8 h) and potentially higher rework probability on difficult handwriting.

6. `behavioral` — S1's preliminary escalation advice (from the early packet) reduces post-Assessment escalation from the factual 2 h to 1 h because S1 already has the case context; this assumes S1's preliminary work is retained and referenced.

7. `quality` — Suppressing a rework loop on a high-risk, high-value case with an unresolved discrepancy increases the probability of an adverse decision outcome (wrong approval, upheld appeal, or regulatory finding) by at least 10–20% relative to the factual execution where the discrepancy was resolved.

8. `interference` — Parallelizing verification tasks shifts queue pressure downstream to Assessment; if the assessment queue is the binding bottleneck (as suggested by growing A1/A2 queues), the local readiness gain is partially or fully absorbed by downstream congestion rather than translating to proportional cycle-time reduction.
