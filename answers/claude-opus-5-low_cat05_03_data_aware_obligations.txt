## 1. Temporal Data Semantics

**State reconstruction.** For each case I build a per-attribute step function over time. Attributes tracked: `amount`, `risk_level`, `jurisdiction`, `customer_type`, plus derived registers (sanctions list membership, exemption approvals). The state *at* an event e is the value carried by the latest event e' with (timestamp, event_index) <= (timestamp, event_index) of e, where e' actually carried a value for that attribute. Formally: `val(a, e) = value of a at the ⊑-maximal e' ⊑ e with a-cell non-blank`, where `⊑` is the lexicographic order on `(time:timestamp, event_index)`.

**Blanks.** A blank cell is *no update*, not a null assignment. Blanks are therefore filled by forward-fill within the case only. There is no way in this log to express "this attribute became unknown again"; if that were needed, a sentinel token (e.g. `__UNKNOWN__`) would be required. Attributes that are still blank after forward-fill (TX-305 `amount` from case start) are genuinely **unknown**, not zero and not low-risk (R6).

**Equal timestamps.** Ordering is `(timestamp, event_index)`. Ties on timestamp are resolved by `event_index`, and I never resolve ties by CSV row position, by activity name, or by resource. Consequence in TX-311: Enhanced_Review (index 3) precedes Update_Jurisdiction to DE (index 4) at 16:20, so the review was performed under jurisdiction US and the jurisdiction is DE only from index 4 onward. Any obligation evaluated *at* index 4 or later sees DE; any obligation evaluated at index 3 sees US. Had the indices been reversed, the review would have been performed under DE and the R1 trigger analysis at 16:20 would flip. Because `event_index` is only an ordering *within* a timestamp, I do not use it to compare events at different timestamps.

**Material changes.** A *material data change* for the amount-dependent obligations is (per R4) an amount **increase of more than 20 percent** relative to the amount that was current at the latest completed Standard_Review or Enhanced_Review: `amount_at_release > 1.20 * amount_at_review`. A decrease, or an increase of at most 20 percent, does not invalidate the review. Independently, I treat a change of `jurisdiction`, `risk_level` or `customer_type` after a review as a **scope change**: it does not fall under R4's 20-percent test, but it can newly *activate* R1/R2/R3 after the review, in which case the review may predate the trigger and therefore not discharge it. If the amount at the review is unknown, the ratio is undefined and R4 yields `INDETERMINATE`, never `not material` (R4 last sentence, R6).

**Final attributes are not backdated.** The final row of a case is only the state after the last event. I never propagate a later value backward: TX-305's `150000` recorded at `Settlement_Confirmed` (2026-04-07 08:00) is *not* the amount at the 2026-04-06 11:30 release; TX-306's `230000` is not the amount that the 12:15 Enhanced_Review examined (that was 220000); TX-311's `DE` is not the jurisdiction under which the 16:20 Enhanced_Review was performed. A pandas `groupby(...).last()` or a case-attribute view of this log would silently produce all three errors.

**Four distinct clocks.** These are conflated in this CSV and must be separated:
- **Event time** — when the business act occurred (`time:timestamp`). Governs ordering constraints "before release", "after the review".
- **Effective time** — when a fact starts to hold in the business world (`effective_from` of an exemption; the moment a jurisdiction is on the sanctions list). Default rule for this task: effective time = event time, *unless* the evidence explicitly establishes another effective time (e.g. EX-77's `effective 09:00`).
- **Record time** — when the fact was written into a system (EX-77 and EX-88 `Record_Exemption` rows at 11:00). Record time may legitimately lag event time (outage); lateness of the record alone neither validates nor invalidates the underlying fact.
- **Evidence time** — when the *proof* of the fact came into existence (EX-77's qualified signature at 09:05; EX-88's signing certificate created at 10:30). Evidence time bounds what can be *demonstrated*. A displayed/backdated field is a claim; a cryptographically bound timestamp is proof. Where evidence time is after the obligation trigger and no independent, disinterested corroboration exists, the fact is not established for compliance purposes (R5 final clause).

Burden of proof: obligations are discharged by positive evidence. Missing evidence for a *trigger* → INDETERMINATE (R6). Missing evidence for an *exception* (exemption) → the exception does not apply; whether that makes the case NONCOMPLIANT or INDETERMINATE depends on whether the underlying obligation was otherwise breached.

## 2. Normalized Rule Table

| Rule | Trigger event and condition | Required prior/subsequent event | Ordering constraint | State snapshot used | Exception | Missing-data treatment | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R1** Foreign high-value | `Release_Funds` r with `jurisdiction(r) ≠ DE` ∧ `amount(r) ≥ 100000` | `Enhanced_Review` x (completed) | `x ≺ r` ∧ `x ≻ lastMaterialChange(r)`, i.e. no invalidating change in `(x, r)` | State at r for the trigger; state at x for R4 baseline | Valid exemption per R5 covering (jurisdiction, customer_type, amount) at r and waiving R1 | `amount` or `jurisdiction` unknown at r → INDETERMINATE unless the outcome is the same for every admissible value | Review completion record with the amount/jurisdiction it examined; change log between x and r |
| **R2** High-risk / PEP | `Release_Funds` r with `risk_level(r)=HIGH` ∨ `customer_type(r)=PEP` | `Enhanced_Review` x **and** `Compliance_Approve` c | `x ≺ c ≺ r`; x must be the review relevant to the trigger, i.e. `x ≻ triggerOnset` where practicable | State at r for the trigger; c's packet scope from evidence | none stated (exemptions in this register waive R1 only) | `risk_level`/`customer_type` unknown at r → INDETERMINATE | Approval record identifying which review packet it approved |
| **R3** Sanctions update | `Release_Funds` r, and a `Sanctions_List_Update` u adding `jurisdiction(r)` with `lastScreen(r) ≺ u ≺ r` | new `Sanctions_Screen` s | `u ≺ s ≺ r` | jurisdiction at r and at u | none | jurisdiction unknown at r or at u → INDETERMINATE; absence of any `Sanctions_Screen` at all makes `lastScreen` undefined → treat u as post-screen (worst case) and flag | List-update feed with effective time; screening result record |
| **R4** Material amount change | any review x (Standard or Enhanced), amount at obligation point | — (modifier rule, not an obligation) | applies to the window `(x, r]` | `amount(x)` vs `amount(r)` | decrease or ≤20 % increase → review remains valid | `amount(x)` or `amount(r)` unknown → material = INDETERMINATE (never "false") | Amount at the moment of review, in the review artefact |
| **R5** Exemption validity | invocation of an exemption E against an obligation triggered at r | `Record_Exemption`/register entry for E | `effective_from(E) ⪯ r ≺ expires(E)`; `record_time` may be `≻ r`; `approval` must *exist* at `⪯ r` | scope of the case at r | — | any scope field unknown → exemption cannot be matched → not applicable | `approved_by = Head_Compliance`; scope match; evidence binding the approval to a time `⪯ r` (qualified signature, sealed log). Backdated displayed field alone: insufficient. Late DB insert with independent proof of earlier approval: sufficient |
| **R6** Missing data | any decisive attribute unknown at the evaluation point | — | — | — | resolved if *all* admissible values give the same verdict | classify INDETERMINATE; never default to LOW/DE/RETAIL/0 | Source-system evidence of the value at the event time |
| **R7** Event-time semantics | every evaluation | — | `⊑` = lexicographic `(timestamp, event_index)`; strictly `≺` means earlier in `⊑` | latest known value at or before the evaluated event | — | — | Stable, total, source-system ordering |

**Formal pseudo-logic.** Let `E(c)` be the events of case c totally ordered by `⊑` on `(time, event_index)`. `e1 ≺ e2` means `e1 ⊑ e2 ∧ e1 ≠ e2` (e1 strictly earlier). `val(a,e)` is the last-known value of attribute a at e, defined only if some `e' ⊑ e` carries a. `⊥` denotes unknown. All quantifiers range over `E(c)`. Verdicts are three-valued (Kleene): a rule body containing `⊥` in a decisive position evaluates to `U` (unknown) and propagates to INDETERMINATE unless the head is already determined by another disjunct.

```
% --- ordering (R7) ---
before(e1,e2) :- (time(e1) < time(e2)) ;
                 (time(e1) = time(e2), index(e1) < index(e2)).

% --- last-known value ---
val(A,E,V)   :- carries(E',A,V), before_eq(E',E),
                not (carries(E'',A,_), before(E',E''), before_eq(E'',E)).
unknown(A,E) :- not val(A,E,_).

% --- R4 (modifier), direction: change strictly AFTER the review, at or before release ---
material_increase(X,R) :- review(X), before(X,R),
                          val(amount,X,A0), val(amount,R,A1), A1 > 1.20*A0.
material_U(X,R)        :- review(X), before(X,R),
                          (unknown(amount,X) ; unknown(amount,R)).
valid_for_amount(X,R)  :- review(X), before(X,R), not material_increase(X,R),
                          not material_U(X,R).

% --- R1: trigger at release, discharge strictly before release, review not invalidated ---
r1_trigger(R) :- act(R,'Release_Funds'), val(jurisdiction,R,J), J \= 'DE',
                 val(amount,R,A), A >= 100000.
r1_trigger_U(R) :- act(R,'Release_Funds'), (unknown(amount,R) ; unknown(jurisdiction,R)).
r1_sat(R)     :- r1_trigger(R), act(X,'Enhanced_Review'), completed(X),
                 before(X,R), valid_for_amount(X,R),
                 not scope_change_between(X,R).      % new trigger arose after X
r1_sat(R)     :- r1_trigger(R), exempt(R,'R1').      % R5
r1_viol(R)    :- r1_trigger(R), not r1_sat(R).
r1_indet(R)   :- r1_trigger_U(R), not r1_sat(R).     % cannot establish or exclude

% --- R2: conjunctive obligation, chained ordering X < C < R ---
r2_trigger(R) :- act(R,'Release_Funds'),
                 (val(risk_level,R,'HIGH') ; val(customer_type,R,'PEP')).
r2_trigger_U(R):- act(R,'Release_Funds'),
                 (unknown(risk_level,R) ; unknown(customer_type,R)).
r2_sat(R)     :- r2_trigger(R), act(X,'Enhanced_Review'), act(C,'Compliance_Approve'),
                 before(X,C), before(C,R), approves(C,X).
r2_viol(R)    :- r2_trigger(R), not r2_sat(R).

% --- R3: re-screen strictly after the list update and strictly before release ---
r3_trigger(R) :- act(R,'Release_Funds'), val(jurisdiction,R,J),
                 act(U,'Sanctions_List_Update'), adds(U,J), before(U,R),
                 last_screen(R,S), before(S,U).
r3_sat(R)     :- r3_trigger(R), act(S2,'Sanctions_Screen'),
                 before(U,S2), before(S2,R).
r3_viol(R)    :- r3_trigger(R), not r3_sat(R).

% --- R5: exemption, defeasible; note effective_from <= trigger, record time unconstrained ---
exempt(R,Rule) :- ref_exemption(R,E), approved_by(E,'Head_Compliance'),
                  waives(E,Rule),
                  effective_from(E,F), before_eq_t(F, time(R)),
                  expires(E,T),        before_t(time(R), T),
                  scope_match(E,R),                    % jurisdiction, cust. type, amount at R
                  approval_existed_at(E, time(R)).     % evidential predicate, see below
approval_existed_at(E,T) :- bound_evidence(E,TS), TS =< T.        % qualified signature etc.
approval_existed_at(E,T) :- late_record(E), independent_proof(E,T).
¬approval_existed_at(E,T) :~ displayed_backdate_only(E).          % defeasible: defeated only
                                                                  % by disinterested corroboration
% --- R6 ---
verdict(R, indeterminate) :- (r1_indet(R) ; r2_trigger_U(R) ; material_U(_,R)),
                             not r1_viol(R), not r2_viol(R), not r3_viol(R).
verdict(R, noncompliant)  :- r1_viol(R) ; r2_viol(R) ; r3_viol(R).
verdict(R, justified_exc) :- r1_trigger(R), exempt(R,'R1'), not plain_sat(R),
                             not r2_viol(R), not r3_viol(R).
verdict(R, compliant)     :- not verdict(R,_other).
```

Direction conventions, stated explicitly: `before/2` is strictly earlier in `⊑`; every "must occur after X and before R" is encoded as the conjunction `before(X, Y), before(Y, R)`; "no later than" (`effective_from ⪯ trigger`) is non-strict; "has not expired" is strict (`time(R) < expires`); R4's window is half-open `(X, R]` — a change at the same timestamp as the review is *after* it only if its `event_index` is larger.

## 4. Ambiguities and Alternative Readings

**TX-305 (INDETERMINATE — the only one).** Two readings compete. (a) *Trigger-first*: R1 fires only if the amount at release is ≥100 000; the amount is unknown, so under R6 the trigger can neither be established nor excluded, and the R4 baseline is likewise undefined. (b) *Obligation-first*: whether or not R1 fires, an Enhanced_Review completed at 11:15, before the 11:30 release, with no recorded data change in between; therefore the obligation is discharged for every admissible amount and R6's "unless other evidence resolves it" applies, giving COMPLIANT. I report INDETERMINATE because reading (b) assumes the review actually covered the released amount, and the review document explicitly has no amount — so a silent amount change between review and release (exactly the TX-309 pattern) is not excluded, and the 150 000 at settlement makes such a change plausible rather than fanciful. What would change the verdict: an immutable release payload carrying the amount authorised at 11:30; the review artefact stating the amount examined; a bank ledger entry binding the 150 000 debit to the 11:30 release instruction; or a positive statement from the amount feed that no update occurred between 11:00 and 11:30. If the released amount were shown to be 150 000, the case becomes COMPLIANT under R1 (review after last change, before release) but the R4 question re-opens: 150 000 against an unknown reviewed amount is precisely R4's "increase from an unknown amount is not safely classifiable", which would keep the case INDETERMINATE unless the reviewed amount is also recovered. Conversely, if the amount at release were shown to be below 100 000, R1 never fires and the case is trivially COMPLIANT.

**TX-302.** Verdict COMPLIANT, but on which ground? On the merits (Enhanced_Review at 09:20) the exemption is redundant, so the case is COMPLIANT rather than JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. A reviewer who held that the 09:20 Enhanced_Review was not "after the latest material data change" — e.g. if evidence emerged of an unlogged amount or scope change between 09:20 and 09:40 — would have to fall back on EX-77, and the classification would become JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION rather than NONCOMPLIANT, because EX-77's evidence is strong: a qualified signature at 09:05, hash valid, and a *documented* outage explaining the 11:00 record. What would flip it to NONCOMPLIANT: the amount at release exceeding 250 000 (out of the exemption's scope) *together* with an invalidated review; or the signature's certificate chain proving to have been created after 09:40, which would reduce EX-77 to EX-88's situation. Note also that EX-77 waives R1 only — had the risk been reclassified to HIGH before release, R2 would fire and the exemption would be powerless.

**TX-310.** Verdict NONCOMPLIANT, with INDETERMINATE as the serious alternative. The exemption fails only on evidence, and one can argue that "not conclusive" (R5) means "not proven either way", which under R6 argues for INDETERMINATE. I resolve against that because R6 governs missing *trigger* data, whereas R5 places the burden for an *exception* on the party invoking it: an unproven exemption simply does not apply, and the underlying R1 obligation is then plainly unmet (a Standard_Review is not an Enhanced_Review), so the case is NONCOMPLIANT on the primary rule. What would change it to JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION: a qualified signature or sealed audit-log entry timestamped ≤ 10:00; a disinterested witness (Head_Compliance themself, or a compliance officer with no stake in the transaction) corroborating the 08:45 oral approval; a mail-server or ticketing record showing the approval message existed before release; or a documented outage explanation of the kind that rehabilitates EX-77. What would harden the NONCOMPLIANT finding: evidence that the certificate at 10:30 was the *first* key material issued to that signer, or that the displayed 08:50 was edited after the fact. Purely INDETERMINATE would be the right call if the underlying Enhanced_Review had actually occurred and only the exemption's role were in doubt — it did not, so the outcome does not hinge on EX-88 at all.

**TX-311.** Verdict COMPLIANT, and the interesting point is that the same-timestamp tie-break is the *only* thing that decides whether R1 is even triggered. With idx 3 ≺ idx 4 the jurisdiction at release is DE and R1 sleeps. Reverse the two indices and the review would have been performed under DE while the release is still under DE — R1 still sleeps. Delete the jurisdiction update entirely and the release is US at 115 000, R1 fires, and the 16:20 Enhanced_Review discharges it (no amount change after 16:10). So all three readings converge on COMPLIANT, which is worth stating explicitly: contested ordering matters only when the readings diverge, and here they do not. The reading that *would* change the result: treating a jurisdiction change as itself a "material data change" that invalidates the prior review and requires re-review under the new scope — but here the new scope (DE) is strictly less demanding, so re-review would be pointless; the rule should be read as invalidating only when the change *increases* the obligation. The genuinely dangerous variant is the mirror image: DE at review, non-DE at release. Under a strict release-time reading that would fire R1 with a review performed under the wrong scope, and the case should then be NONCOMPLIANT even though the control flow is identical.

**TX-306 (additional).** COMPLIANT hinges on measuring the increase from the *reviewed* amount (220 000), giving 4.55 %. Measured from some earlier or later baseline the answer could differ, and a reviewer who required the Compliance_Approve to postdate every material change would note that the 12:30 amount update came *after* the 12:25 approval — under that stricter reading of R2's "after the relevant Enhanced_Review", a re-approval would be needed and the case becomes NONCOMPLIANT. R4 explicitly makes sub-20 % increases non-invalidating, so I do not adopt that reading, but a policy that bounded PEP approvals to an exact authorised amount would.

**TX-307 (additional).** NONCOMPLIANT rests on the semantic fact that a Compliance_Approve of a standard packet does not cure a missing Enhanced_Review. If the 13:12 approval could be shown to have considered the PEP status and the enhanced-review criteria in substance — e.g. the approval artefact containing enhanced-due-diligence findings — a JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION or even COMPLIANT reading becomes defensible on a substance-over-form basis. Conversely, if the PEP update at 13:05 were shown to have an *effective* time earlier than the case (a beneficial owner who was always a PEP), the breach extends backwards and the severity rises.

  * exemption evidence quality is data, not a hard-coded verdict per case.
"""

## 6. Why Control-Flow-Only Conformance Is Insufficient

1. **Final-state leakage.** Control-flow conformance is normally paired with case-level attributes obtained by `groupby().last()` or by reading the trace attributes of an XES case. That silently backdates the final value onto every earlier decision point. Here it would tell you TX-306's Enhanced_Review examined 230 000 (it examined 220 000), that TX-311's review was performed under DE (it was performed under US), and that TX-305's release amount was 150 000 (it was unknown, and the 150 000 is a next-day settlement figure). Two of those three errors happen to be harmless and one is not — but you cannot tell which without the event-time reconstruction, so the method gives no reliable signal at all.

2. **Post-hoc exemptions and the record/evidence-time gap.** TX-302 and TX-310 have the same trace shape: review → release → `Record_Exemption` at 11:00. A control-flow model sees the exemption record *after* the release in both and must either reject both (wrong for TX-302, where a documented outage explains the lag) or accept both (wrong for TX-310, where the signing certificate did not exist until 10:30). The discriminating facts — signature timestamp, hash validity, outage documentation, the witness's independence — are not events at all. No ordering constraint over activity labels can encode "the approval existed at time t even though its record does not".

3. **Material changes after a valid review.** TX-308 and TX-309 both contain Enhanced_Review → Update_Amount → Release_Funds. The control-flow projections are identical. TX-308 is compliant (a decrease) and TX-309 is a breach (+22.9 %). The rule is a *predicate over two attribute values separated in time* (`amount_at_release > 1.2 × amount_at_review`), which is outside the expressive power of activity-sequence languages including DECLARE templates and Petri-net alignments over labels.

4. **Conditional (data-activated) obligations.** R1, R2 and R3 are all guarded: they only fire for certain jurisdictions, amounts, risk levels, customer types, and list states. A control-flow model must choose between an unconditional constraint — which produces false positives on every low-value domestic case (TX-303's release is fine without an Enhanced_Review under R1, since 90 000 < 100 000) — and no constraint, which misses every real breach. Worse, in TX-303 and TX-307 the guard *becomes true mid-case* (`Risk_Reclassify`, `Update_Customer_Type`), so even a per-case guard chosen at case start is wrong; the activation point is itself data- and time-dependent.

5. **Missing data.** TX-305 has a textbook-clean trace: Create → Enhanced_Review → Release. Every control-flow constraint passes with a perfect fitness of 1.0. The actual situation is that the decisive attribute is absent, the trigger can be neither established nor excluded, and the review artefact cannot be shown to have covered the released amount. Control-flow conformance has no representation for "unknown" — it conflates *absent value* with *value that does not trigger the rule*, which is exactly the low-risk defaulting R6 forbids. A fitness score of 1.0 here is actively misleading: it reports confidence where none exists.

6. **Same-timestamp ordering.** In TX-311 the Enhanced_Review and the jurisdiction change share the timestamp 16:20 and are separated only by `event_index`. Most conformance pipelines sort by timestamp alone, with a non-deterministic or insertion-order tie-break, so the reconstructed state at the review — and therefore whether R1 was triggered under US or DE — depends on an implementation detail of the sort. The verdict happens to be stable here, but that stability is a property of this case, not of the method, and the method cannot tell you which is which.

7. **Cross-entity and external events.** R3 depends on `Sanctions_List_Update`, an event about a *jurisdiction*, not about the case. In TX-304 it happens to be interleaved into the case's trace, but that is a logging convenience; in general the list is a separate object with its own timeline. Control-flow conformance over a single case notion cannot express "an event in another object's history invalidates an earlier event in mine" — this is an object-centric / temporal-database question.

8. **Rule versioning and the identity of obligations.** "The relevant Enhanced_Review" (R2) and "the review that the Compliance_Approve approved" (TX-307) are *links between events*, not orderings. TX-307's approval is correctly positioned in time — after the review, before the release — and still fails, because it approved the wrong packet. Sequence-based conformance cannot distinguish an approval that discharges the obligation from one that merely occupies the right slot.

## 7. Recommended Logging and Control Improvements

**Immutable release payload.** Every `Release_Funds` event should carry a signed, hash-chained snapshot of the full decision state it authorised: `amount`, `currency`, `jurisdiction`, `risk_level`, `customer_type`, the identifiers of the reviews and approvals relied upon, the exemption relied upon (if any), and the rule-set version. This single change resolves TX-305 outright, makes TX-309's breach self-evident from one row, and removes any need to reconstruct state by forward-fill for the decisive event. The payload must be write-once and hash-chained to the preceding case event so that later edits are detectable.

**Four explicit timestamps on every fact-bearing row.** Replace the single `time:timestamp` with `event_time` (when it happened), `effective_from`/`effective_to` (when the fact holds in the business world — bitemporal validity), `record_time` (when it was written), and `evidence_time` (when the binding proof came into existence), plus `evidence_type` (`qualified_signature`, `sealed_log`, `oral_attestation`, `manual_entry`) and `evidence_subject_independence` (is the attestor an interested party?). This is precisely the distinction that separates TX-302 from TX-310, and it should be a column, not a footnote in a register. Signature verification status (`hash_valid`, `cert_not_before`, `cert_not_after`) should be materialised at ingestion, not re-derived at audit time.

**Explicit null semantics.** Distinguish "no update" from "value became unknown" with a sentinel (`__NO_UPDATE__` vs `__UNKNOWN__`), and add a per-attribute `source_system` and `feed_status` (`OK` / `UNAVAILABLE` / `STALE`). TX-305's `amount feed unavailable` is currently prose in a `note` column; as a machine-readable `feed_status=UNAVAILABLE` it would automatically block the release under a pre-release completeness gate rather than surfacing as an audit finding a day later.

**Stable, total event ordering.** Keep `event_index`, but strengthen it: a monotonic per-case sequence number assigned by the source system, or a Lamport/hybrid-logical clock, and sub-second timestamp precision. Document and enforce that all consumers sort by `(event_time, sequence_number)`. Better still, log causal edges — `caused_by_event_id` — so that the TX-311 question ("was the jurisdiction change before or after the review?") is answered by an explicit reference rather than by a tie-break convention.

**Change provenance on every attribute update.** Each `Update_*` event should carry `attribute`, `old_value`, `new_value`, `change_reason`, `initiator`, `authorising_control`, and a `materiality` flag computed by the source system against the rule then in force. Recording `old_value` alongside `new_value` makes R4 checkable from a single row and removes the dependence on a correct forward-fill; recording the initiator would have flagged that TX-309's second increase came from the same `ops09` who requested the transaction.

**Structured review and approval artefacts.** `Standard_Review` and `Enhanced_Review` should record `review_type`, `reviewed_amount`, `reviewed_jurisdiction`, `reviewed_risk`, `reviewed_customer_type`, `review_scope_hash`, and `valid_until_amount` (an explicit authorised ceiling). `Compliance_Approve` should carry `approves_review_id` and `packet_type`. TX-307 then fails automatically and unambiguously — the approval points at a standard packet while the obligation demands an enhanced one — instead of requiring a human to read a free-text note.

**Machine-readable exemption register with scope predicates.** Store exemptions as first-class objects: `exemption_id`, `approver_role`, `approval_evidence` (with the four timestamps above), `effective_from`, `expires`, `scope` as an explicit predicate (`jurisdiction ∈ {...} ∧ customer_type ∈ {...} ∧ amount ≤ X`), `rules_waived` (an explicit list — both register entries here waive R1 only, and that must be structural, not prose), and `revocation_status`. Require the exemption to be *resolvable at release time*: a release may reference only an exemption whose approval evidence already exists, enforced as a system control rather than checked afterwards.

**Rule-set versioning.** Every evaluated event should carry `policy_version` and the engine should retain the rule text per version, so that a 2027 audit of an April 2026 release applies April 2026's thresholds. Without it, changing the 20 percent or EUR 100 000 threshold silently rewrites the compliance history.

**Preventive controls, not just better logs.** Make the release gate synchronous and data-aware: block `Release_Funds` when the authorised state differs from the reviewed state beyond tolerance, when a decisive attribute is unknown, when the jurisdiction has been listed since the last screen, or when a referenced exemption lacks verified approval evidence. Emit an explicit `Release_Blocked` event with a reason code, so that the log records prevented breaches too. Also emit `Sanctions_List_Update` as a first-class object-centric event with its own effective time, linked to all open cases in the affected jurisdiction, and auto-raise a `Rescreen_Required` obligation — TX-304's breach then becomes an open task rather than a released payment.
