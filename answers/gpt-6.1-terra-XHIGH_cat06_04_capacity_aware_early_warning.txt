## 1. Decision rule

At each decision point, first identify cases still before Diagnosis Complete for which a 45-minute swarm can plausibly start in time to help. Confirm stage, deadline, queue state and the specific diagnostic obstacle with the case owner. A high breach probability after Diagnosis Complete prompts a different escalation, not a diagnostic swarm. Check contractual and regulatory obligations separately; the economic score does not override them.

Maintain one active alert per case. A first alert opens a review, not an automatic swarm. Keep it open until the owner records a decision, the case leaves the eligible stage, or the information expires. Refreshing a prediction alone does not create a new task. Reconsider an active case when a material event changes its likely response or feasibility—for example, new diagnostic evidence, a changed deadline, a stage transition, or a verified substantial change in queue conditions. A risk change alone warrants review if it exceeds a pre-specified noise band, provisionally 0.10 absolute probability, and is confirmed by reliable inputs; it does not by itself authorize a repeat swarm.

For each eligible case, estimate incremental avoided breaches from a swarm versus ordinary care, with an uncertainty interval and remaining time accounted for. Separately estimate its direct net value as incremental risk reduction times breach value, less €450 and the case's false-alarm burden. For a conservative operational comparison, charge the full listed burden per swarm; actual burden may be smaller if it is incurred only in a subset of cases. Rank feasible portfolios by their plausible net benefit after queue spillovers and opportunity cost, giving weight to the lower end of the effect range, urgency, service obligations and fair access. Reassess marginal value before each start. Use a reserve when its expected option value for a credible arrival exceeds the best currently available marginal intervention; release it if that is no longer true. A case with low estimated net value can still receive support under an explicit contractual or fairness safeguard, with the reason logged.

Use calibrated untreated-risk estimates as inputs to urgency and plausibility checks, not as estimates of treatment benefit. Provisionally add 0.04–0.09 to SME predictions and subtract 0.05–0.12 from Strategic Enterprise predictions, clipping to [0, 1]; retain a range rather than choosing a falsely precise correction. Public Sector estimates have moderate sampling uncertainty. For Non-Native-Language Support, use a wider uncertainty range and human review; do not apply an invented numerical correction. Check the missing queue feature for I-06 before trusting its rise. These segment adjustments are approximate backtest corrections, not proof of current individual probabilities; test sensitivity to the unadjusted values as well. Effect ranges are uncertain, potentially observational estimates, not known causal effects, and any assumed reduction must be feasible relative to the untreated risk.

## 3. Expected-impact table

Illustration for the three selected cases only, assuming all three swarms occur and each 45-minute slot has its own set of six untreated diagnosis-stage recipients. Overlap between sets is allowed; sum the incremental risks across slots, subject to feasible probabilities. For a transparent monetary example, assume those recipients have an average breach value of €10,000. Their actual identities and values are unknown, so this is a sensitivity assumption, not a fact about the queue. With a common incremental risk of 0.01–0.03 per recipient per slot, expected harm is 3 × 6 × €10,000 × 0.01–0.03 = €1,800–€5,400. In general, calculate harm as the sum, over each slot and its untreated recipients, of incremental risk times that recipient's breach value, plus any material non-monetary harm. Validate the estimate against current congestion before each start; do not add the delay minutes again as a separate monetary loss if they already drive the estimated risk increase.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

Use a 60-minute notification cool-down after an alert review, with one persistent case record and an owner. Suppress unchanged 30-minute refreshes, while continuing to update the internal estimate and monitor deadlines. Escalate immediately outside the cool-down for a material new diagnostic event, a critical deadline or contractual issue, verified substantial deterioration, or a data-quality failure that needs action. Route data-quality failures for verification rather than automatically treating the changed score as a real risk increase.

A case can re-enter allocation when its actionable evidence, stage, remaining time, queue conditions or capacity changes materially, or at the scheduled next review. Document the event and the revised incremental benefit. A previous alert is not a previous swarm: a case can receive its first swarm after several alerts. After an actual swarm, normally forbid another; require a materially new event, a fresh assessment of incremental benefit and queue harm, and senior approval before any exception. Close or redirect the alert when Diagnosis Complete makes the swarm ineffective, the case closes, or an owner documents that it is no longer actionable.

For I-05, suppress the third unchanged notification and do not count three alerts as three independent reasons to act. If the original alert has not received a substantive review, perform that review; suppression must not strand the case. For I-07, the new customer log is a material event: review it immediately despite the slightly lower probability and cool-down, and decide whether it identifies a useful swarm action before the short deadline. Neither a repeat alert nor the log alone guarantees a slot.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

Pure monetary ranking can repeatedly favor high-value Enterprise cases while SMEs and other lower-value cases wait. Track selection, time to review and breach outcomes by segment and severity, including eligible cases that were never selected. Set a documented maximum review delay for urgent cases and periodically audit cases passed over repeatedly. Consider a minimum service-access constraint or protected review capacity if disparities persist, subject to legal obligations and a transparent assessment of effects on the whole queue; do not promise identical outcomes where case mixes differ.

For Non-Native-Language Support, provide interpreters or translated diagnostic material as needed, include this segment in effect-estimation studies, and report uncertainty alongside outcomes. Sparse evidence must trigger review and data collection, not an automatic discount that systematically deprioritizes the group. Compare calibration and error rates by segment, stage and deadline horizon, with sample counts and uncertainty intervals; investigate the SME underprediction and Enterprise overprediction before using the corrections as stable facts.

Interventions change outcomes, so a treated case's observed breach label is not its untreated counterfactual. Log predictions, eligibility, decisions, reasons, treatment timing and outcomes, including overrides and untreated cases. Do not retrain an untreated-risk model as if all observed labels came from ordinary care. Monitor whether the policy changes who enters diagnosis, receives attention, or has complete data.

Identify the untreated cases sharing each queue and attribute estimated incremental delay and breach risk by segment as well as to selected cases. Monitor cumulative and per-case burdens, not just average portfolio value. If a group repeatedly bears disproportionate spillovers, investigate staffing and routing, limit concurrent or back-to-back swarms where warranted, and change the allocation or protect that queue. Review both intervention recipients and spillover recipients for accessibility and contractual impacts.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

For every alert and swarm, record the prefix and stage, deadline, inputs and missingness, calibrated and raw predictions, effect estimate and uncertainty, reason for alert, decision and override, staff, start and finish, new evidence, disruption, queue state, and eventual breach and resolution times. Track review latency, duplicate alerts, missed stage windows, intervention uptake, reserve use, realized duration, delay to untreated cases and specialist workload. Alert operations when data feeds fail or queue delays exceed locally agreed limits.

Monitor predictive calibration and discrimination by stage, horizon and segment, with sample sizes and uncertainty; compare observed outcomes with predicted untreated risk using appropriate untreated or otherwise adjusted cohorts. Check missing-feature rates, population and workflow shifts, and changes in score distributions and case mix. Re-estimate calibration on recent representative data, validate out of time, and retain conservative ranges when segment samples are too small.

Estimate causal effects prospectively where operationally and ethically feasible: randomize among similarly eligible cases near the allocation boundary, or use a stepped rollout with recorded assignment probabilities. Capture treatment timing and noncompliance, and measure outcomes for both treated cases and untreated queue peers. Because one swarm can affect other cases, use queue- or time-block randomization when feasible, or explicitly model interference and congestion; individual randomization alone may mix direct and spillover effects. Where randomization is infeasible, use matched or weighted comparisons with pre-intervention covariates and sensitivity analysis for unmeasured confounding. Report effect intervals by stage and relevant groups, not just a pooled average.

Review operational and queue indicators daily, and predictive, causal and fairness results at a regular governance review, provisionally monthly or sooner after a workflow or data change. Compare current outcomes and case mix with pre-policy and concurrent comparison periods to detect policy-induced drift, including selective labels and shifted spillovers. Recalculate marginal portfolio value and the reserve's option value as evidence accumulates. Change the alert noise band, review triggers or allocation constraints when validated calibration, effect, congestion or fairness evidence shows sustained degradation or a better expected outcome; document the decision, test it prospectively where possible, and roll back if adverse effects emerge. In the meantime, use human review and conservative effect and spillover bounds when evidence is weak.
