# 1. Queue-by-queue diagnosis

Reading conventions used throughout (labelled assumptions):

- **A1 — decision time ≈ service start.** I treat each service start (and each preemption instant) as a decision opportunity, because no dispatch-decision timestamp is supplied. Needed to construct eligibility sets at all; it is the single most consequential assumption and I test its effect explicitly in §4 and §5.
- **A2 — eligibility = arrived (scanned) at or before the decision time, not yet started or completed, and skill-compatible with the deciding resource.** Needed because "waiting" is not a recorded state.
- **A3 — dispatch latency of about 2 minutes.** Observed repeatedly: A01 arrives 08:00 and starts 08:02; A1 idles 08:30–08:32 with three jobs queued; B01 is stopped 09:16, exactly 2 minutes after the priority-1 arrival B02 at 09:14; B01 is stopped again 09:48, exactly 2 minutes after the priority-1 arrival B05 at 09:46. This matches the stated arrival-scan tolerance and means small (≤2 min) discrepancies carry no evidential weight.
- **A4 — realized duration equals `est_min` for all 32 jobs.** This is a fact of the data (e.g. C05 40 min, B01's segments 14+17+14 = 45 = est). It makes SPT-on-estimate and SRPT-on-actuals indistinguishable and raises a data-provenance worry (§5).

```python
import pandas as pd

qc = pd.DataFrame([
    ("C01","10:00","13:00",3,36,"11:40"), ("C02","10:03","14:00",3, 8,"10:10"),
    ("C03","10:05","12:30",2,14,"10:18"), ("C04","10:07","14:30",3,22,"10:32"),
    ("C05","10:11","10:50",1,40,"10:54"), ("C06","10:25","15:00",3, 6,"11:34"),
    ("C07","10:40","12:00",2,18,"12:16"),
], columns=["job","arrival","deadline","priority","est_min","start"])
for c in ["arrival","deadline","start"]:
    qc[c] = pd.to_datetime("2024-01-01 " + qc[c])

rows = []
for _, r in qc.sort_values("start").iterrows():
    t = r.start
    e = qc[(qc.arrival <= t) & (qc.start >= t)].copy()          # scanned, not yet started
    e["idx"] = (t - e.arrival).dt.total_seconds()/60 - e.est_min  # age minus estimate
    rows.append(dict(t=t.strftime("%H:%M"), observed=r.job, eligible=" ".join(e.job),
        FIFO=e.sort_values("arrival").job.iloc[0],
        PRIO=e.sort_values(["priority","arrival"]).job.iloc[0],
        SPT=e.sort_values(["est_min","arrival"]).job.iloc[0],
        EDD=e.sort_values(["deadline","arrival"]).job.iloc[0],
        AGE_MINUS_EST=e.sort_values("idx", ascending=False).job.iloc[0]))
print(pd.DataFrame(rows).to_string(index=False))
```

PM4Py is deliberately not used here: the object of inference is a per-decision choice over an eligibility set, not a control-flow structure, and PM4Py's conformance semantics would answer a different question. Its useful role would be limited to importing/normalising a XES log and computing waiting-time statistics once queue-entry and dispatch events exist as first-class events (§6).

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

## QA — FIFO plus soft appointments

- **Supports:** A02 (08:32, chosen over the priority-1 A04 and over the shorter A04/A05 — FIFO's signature), A05 (09:05, chosen over priority-1 A08 and over the shorter A07, and at the cost of overrunning A06's appointment), A07 (09:32, chosen over priority-1 A08), A01 (trivially). A03 supports the appointment component: it arrived 08:08, waited even though it was the shortest-but-one job, and started at 08:20 on the minute.
- **Challenges:** A03 is the only order violation against pure FIFO — fully explained by a *recorded* field (appointment 08:20), so it does not require an undocumented exception. A04 and A08 challenge any priority reading, not my reading. The A1 idle 08:30–08:32 challenges strict work conservation but sits inside the stated 2-minute tolerance (A3); the 08:52–08:58 gap is the logged calibration.
- **Residual:** A06's appointment being served 5 min late tells us the appointment mechanism is soft. Whether the tolerance is +5 or +10 min, and whether an early-arriving appointment would ever be started early, cannot be decided: at 08:20 A1 had just finished A01, so "A03 was not started before 08:20" is unfalsifiable — the server was busy the whole time.

## QB — pooled FIFO with a priority-1 interrupt

- **Supports FIFO backbone:** B01's resumption at 09:31 over the waiting priority-2 B03; B01's second resumption at 10:14 over the priority-2 B06 and the 6-minute B07; B04 at 10:28 over the priority-2 B06; B07 served last purely by arrival order despite a 10:30 appointment.
- **Supports the priority-1 overlay:** B02 (interrupt within the 2-minute dispatch lag) and B05 (10:00, jumping the older B01 and B04).
- **Challenges:** B05 itself is the sharpest problem. If priority 1 preempts, B05 should have taken the server at 09:48 — the stop even occurred 2 min after its arrival — yet B03 ran and B05 waited 14 min. Two readings: (i) the trigger was B05 and the *selection* was overridden by the dispatcher (the log's own words: "dispatcher reassignment"), which means the escalation rule exists but is not reliably executed; (ii) the 09:48 stop was an anti-starvation intervention for B03 (28 min waiting, the longest wait then) and the 2-minute coincidence with B05's arrival is chance. Reading (i) is an undocumented *exception*; reading (ii) is an undocumented *rule*. Neither is a known constraint, so QB genuinely requires an unlogged mechanism, which is why its confidence is the lowest.
- **Also unexplained by anything documented:** B01 accrues two resumption overheads (three segments for a 45-minute job, 88 minutes of flow time) with no visible setup cost in the timestamps — the segments sum exactly to `est_min`, i.e. the log implies zero-cost preemption, which is operationally implausible and suggests reconstructed rather than measured segment boundaries.

## QC — SPT with aging plus an SLA escalation

- **Supports:** C02 (10:10) and C03 (10:18) are the SPT picks against the oldest job, the highest priority and the earliest deadline simultaneously; C06 (11:34, 6 min, chosen over the priority-2, earlier-deadline C07). C05 (10:54) supports the escalation component and is explicitly annotated as deadline-endangered.
- **Challenges pure SPT:** C04 at 10:32, chosen over the 6-minute C06. Explainable by known constraints *only if* the decision was committed before 10:25 (nothing in the data records decision times); otherwise it needs the aging term, which reproduces it exactly (+3 vs +1).
- **Challenges everything except aging:** C01 at 11:40 over C07. This one cannot be rescued by scan delays (C07 arrived 10:40, an hour earlier), by skills (all general), by resource state (C1 was free) or by deadlines (C07 first would have met both deadlines; C01 first made C07 34 min late). It requires either a starvation cap/promised-date rule or a human decision to clear the oldest job — the note "oldest job, executed last among initial backlog" reads like an operator rationalisation of exactly that.
- **Note on C07's tardiness:** the observed sequence produces one avoidable deadline violation. That is strong evidence that *no* deadline-aware rule (EDD, minimum slack, critical ratio) governs QC, despite deadlines being recorded — the same pattern as `priority` in QA and QD: a field is captured but not enacted.

## QD — skill routing with FIFO inside the eligible set

- **Supports:** D04 over the priority-1 D08 (11:25) and D07 over the priority-1 D10 (11:48) are the two decisions that could have shown priority or SPT and did not. Routing support: all four mechanical-only jobs (D01, D04, D08, D09) went to the mechanical specialist D-M even though D-X could do them, while D-X was spent on the jobs only it could do (D07 mechanical+electrical) or that needed its electrical skill (D06, D10); D02 and D05 went to D-F because nothing else qualifies. D-X's idle 11:20–11:30 followed by an exact 11:30 start supports hard appointment reservation. D05 starting at 12:20 supports calendar-aware, non-splittable scheduling.
- **Challenges:** D-F idle 11:36–12:00 while D10 (priority 1, electrical, 12 min, waiting since 11:25) and D05 (priority 2, waiting since 11:07) were both eligible for it. D05's wait is explainable by a known constraint if firmware work cannot be split across the 12:00–12:20 meeting — a domain-plausible assumption I label explicitly, and one the QB queue shows is *not* a center-wide prohibition, since B01 was split three times. D10's wait is **not** explainable by any recorded constraint and requires an undocumented exception (unlogged D-F unavailability, pre-meeting preparation, a routing rule that hard-partitions electrical work to D-X once D-X is up, or a resource reservation held for D05).
- Also note D-M idle 12:03–12:30: correctly explained, since the only remaining work (D07) requires an electrical skill D-M lacks.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

**Test 1 — QB at 09:48 (B01's second interruption).**
- H1, *preemptive priority-1 escalation*: the stop was triggered by B05's 09:46 arrival, so the next job should be **B05**, and B01 should resume only after B05 completes.
- H2, *FIFO with a manual anti-starvation override*: the stop was a dispatcher decision to unblock the longest-waiting job, so the next job should be **B03** (28 min waiting) and B05 should wait for a completion boundary.
- Observed: B03 — H2. But H1 is not dead, because B05 *did* jump the queue at the very next boundary (10:00), which H2 alone does not predict.
- Discriminating observable: a **preemption event with a reason code and a triggering-job reference** ("preempted_by = B05" vs "manual_reassignment, initiator = dispatcher_id"), together with B05's readiness timestamp (parts, customer authorisation). If B05 was not ready until 10:00, H1 survives intact and the queue is a clean preemptive-priority-1 queue. Behaviourally: observe a priority-1 arrival while a priority-3 job is in service and *no* aged priority-2 job is waiting. H1 predicts an interrupt within ~2 min; H2 predicts the priority-1 job waits for the next completion.

**Test 2 — QC at 10:32 (C04 chosen over the 6-minute C06).**
- H1, *dynamic SPT with a decision committed before C06's 10:25 scan*: at any commitment time ≤ 10:24 the eligible set is {C01 36, C04 22, C05 40} and the pick is **C04**; if the decision were genuinely made at 10:32 the pick would be **C06**.
- H2, *SPT with linear aging (maximise age − est)*: with full information at 10:32 the pick is still **C04** (+3) over **C06** (+1), because C04 has waited 25 min.
- Both explain the observation, so this episode alone cannot separate them; the two diverge sharply elsewhere, which is what makes the test worth running.
- Discriminating observable: the **dispatch-decision timestamp with the eligibility snapshot** attached (does the record show C06 in the candidate set?). A behavioural discriminator that needs no new fields: collect decisions where a long job has been waiting much longer than a short one — the configuration that separated the two hypotheses in this log at 11:40 (C01, 36 min, waiting 100 min, against C07, 18 min, waiting 60 min). H1 takes the short job every time regardless of age; H2 flips to the long job once the age advantage exceeds the estimate gap. A handful of such episodes, with the flip occurring near the predicted crossover, confirms aging; consistent short-job wins refute it and reinstate plain dynamic SPT.

**Test 3 — QD at 11:20 (D-X idles for 10 minutes).**
- H1, *hard appointment reservation*: D-X is held for D06's 11:30 slot. Prediction: any job that provably finishes before 11:30 may be inserted, so the priority-1, 8-minute D08 (finishing 11:28) *should* have been taken; the fact that it was not implies a conservative reservation buffer of ≥ 10 min.
- H2, *specialist-protection routing*: D-X is barred from mechanical-only work as long as a dedicated mechanical resource exists, so idling is a consequence of the routing rule, not the appointment. Prediction: D-X would idle at 11:20 even with no appointment pending.
- Observed data cannot separate them, because the only insertable jobs at 11:20 (D04, D08, D09) were all mechanical-only — exactly the class H2 forbids.
- Discriminating observable: a **resource-state log distinguishing `reserved_for_appointment` from `idle_eligible`**, plus the queue's **appointment tolerance and insertion-buffer parameters**. Behavioural test: an episode where D-X is free, the next appointment is 40 min away and only mechanical-only jobs wait. If D-X takes one, H2 is refuted and H1 with a buffer is confirmed; if D-X still idles, routing (or a non-work-conserving rule) is doing the work.

**Test 4 — QA at 08:20/08:32 (FIFO + appointment vs. an aging index).**
- H1, *FIFO with appointment insertion*: predicts A03 at 08:20 (appointment due) and **A02** at 08:32 (oldest).
- H2, *maximise age − est, no appointment concept*: also predicts A03 at 08:20 (+2 vs A04's 0 and A02's −4) but **A04** at 08:32.
- Observed A02 at 08:32 favours H1, and this is the *only* decision in QA that separates them — a thin margin for a 74-confidence claim.
- Discriminating observable: an episode with an **idle server and an appointment job that arrived early**. H1 holds the slot until the appointment time (or starts a job that fits before it); H2 starts the index-maximising job immediately and ignores the appointment. Recording the appointment tolerance and whether early starts are permitted settles it in one case.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked by plausibility (most plausible first).

1. **Decision time ≠ service start (dispatch latency and early commitment).** Directly evidenced: a consistent ~2-minute lag at A01, the 08:30–08:32 idle, and both QB preemptions occurring 2 min after a priority-1 arrival. If commitment can precede the start by more than 2 min, QC's 10:32 anomaly dissolves into plain dynamic SPT and the aging hypothesis loses its best independent support; QB's 09:48 selection could also be a decision taken before B05 was scanned. Effect: QC's confidence would shift from "SPT with aging" toward "dynamic SPT with a starvation cap"; nothing else changes much.
2. **Service durations equal `est_min` for all 32 jobs.** Either durations were imputed from estimates or estimates were back-filled from actuals. This is the most dangerous data-quality issue in the set: if `est_min` was written after the fact, the QC diagnosis becomes circular (we would be inferring "SPT on estimates" from a field that encodes outcomes), and the prompt's warning about noisy or strategic estimates becomes untestable. Effect: QC's confidence would fall to roughly 40 and the honest label would become "short-job-first family, mechanism of estimation unknown". It also makes QB's zero-cost preemptions suspect, since real resume overhead would show up as segment sums exceeding the estimate.
3. **Priority assigned or escalated after arrival (post-hoc labelling).** Nothing in the data proves the recorded priority was visible at the decision time. If A04's and A08's priority 1 was set after 08:32, or D08's and D10's after their decisions, then "priority is inert" is wrong and the truth is "priority was unknown when it mattered". Effect: QA would become FIFO+appointment *with* an unobservable priority overlay (confidence down ~10), QD similarly; QB's account would be strengthened, since its priority-1 effects would then be genuine and the queue would look consistently escalatory. A priority-change audit trail settles this immediately.
4. **Undocumented resource unavailability or setup.** Candidates: D-F 11:36–12:00 (24 min), the 2-minute A1 gaps, and B1's "dispatcher reassignment". The prompt warns explicitly that absence of an interruption reason does not prove absence of a reason. Effect: this is the single change that would most improve QD — it removes the only real work-conservation violation and leaves a clean "skill routing + FIFO + hard appointments + calendar-aware non-splittable jobs" story (confidence up to ~82).
5. **Non-splittable job classes.** Firmware flashing plausibly cannot be interrupted, which alone explains D05's 73-minute wait for a contiguous 30-minute window after the 12:00–12:20 meeting, without invoking any queue policy. Note the tension with QB, where a 45-minute job was split three times: splittability is a job-type attribute, not a site policy. Effect: converts a QD "anomaly" into a constraint and supports reading QD as calendar-aware planning rather than reactive dispatching.
6. **Human override / anti-starvation intervention as an unlogged rule.** Needed for QB at 09:48 and QC at 11:40 under any pure-policy reading. If overrides are frequent, the right description of both queues is "documented base rule plus discretionary expediting", which is a materially different management conclusion: the base rule cannot be validated by outcome data alone. Effect: QB's confidence would drop further and QC's aging term would be re-labelled as discretion rather than policy.
7. **Per-queue appointment enforcement rather than a single appointment policy.** QD reserves capacity and starts on the minute; QA honours appointments in slot order with ~5 min tolerance and no reservation; QB ignores B07 by 26 min. This is consistent with one nominal policy implemented with three different tolerances, or with three different policies. Effect: does not change any primary label but reframes §7's recommendation — appointment behaviour is a configuration parameter, not a discipline.
8. **Delayed arrival scans (≤2 min).** Marginally relevant at three points only: C05 at 10:10, B05 at 09:48, D09 at 11:20. Every anomaly I rely on involves gaps far larger than 2 min (45, 51, 61, 73 minutes), so scan delay cannot rescue any refuted hypothesis. Effect: none of the diagnoses change; it only forbids reading fine ordering distinctions inside a 2-minute band.
9. **SLA-triggered policy switch.** Documented for C05 and consistent with its 10:50 deadline having already passed. Also worth noting that C05 was arithmetically infeasible from arrival (10:11 + 40 > 10:50), so the alternative reading is a tardiness-minimising rule that deliberately shelves doomed jobs. Effect: distinguishes "escalate when endangered" from "de-prioritise when already lost" — opposite management implications from the same observation.
10. **Sample size.** With 7 to 10 jobs per queue and at most 6 contested decisions each, several diagnoses rest on one or two decisive episodes (QD's FIFO claim on two; QA's FIFO-vs-aging separation on one). Any confidence above ~80 would be rhetorical, not evidential.

# 6. Additional event data needed

Prioritised for the specific ambiguities above, not as a generic wish list.

1. **Dispatch-decision event with an eligibility snapshot** — decision timestamp, deciding actor (rule engine vs. named dispatcher), the candidate set considered, and the chosen job. Resolves exception 1, Test 2 and Test 1 in one field. Highest value per unit of effort, because almost every remaining ambiguity is really "what was known, by whom, when".
2. **Priority provenance:** `priority_assigned_at`, `priority_before`, `priority_after`, `assigned_by`. Decides whether QA's A04/A08 and QD's D08/D10 refute priority scheduling or merely reveal late escalation (exception 3), which is the difference between "priority is not implemented" and "priority is implemented but arrives too late to matter".
3. **Separate `planned_duration` and `measured_duration` fields, with estimate provenance and version history.** Needed to break the est-equals-actual circularity (exception 2) before any SPT claim about QC can be trusted, and to test whether estimates are strategically shaded (compare estimate revisions to realized values by job type and by requester).
4. **Preemption/resume records:** stop reason code, triggering job reference, `estimated_remaining_time` at the stop and at each resume, plus resume setup time. Distinguishes preemptive priority from SRPT from manual reassignment in QB, and quantifies the resumption cost that the current segment arithmetic implies is zero.
5. **Appointment semantics per queue:** appointment window start/end, tolerance for early and late start, `reserve_capacity` flag, insertion-buffer rule, and no-show handling. Explains the QA/QB/QD divergence and settles Test 3 and Test 4.
6. **Resource state timeline at one-minute granularity:** available, reserved-for-appointment, setup/changeover, staging, break, meeting, travel, blocked-on-parts, with reason codes. Targets the D-F 11:36–12:00 idle, the A1 08:30–08:32 gap, and C1's pre-10:10 job.
7. **Queue-entry time distinct from the arrival scan**, plus job-readiness timestamps (parts available, customer authorisation, diagnostics complete). Tests whether D10, D05 and B05 were genuinely eligible when they appear to have been passed over — the cheapest way to convert three "violations" into constraints or confirm them as exceptions.
8. **Deadlines/SLA targets and escalation events for QA, QB and QD as well.** Currently only QC carries deadlines, so EDD and slack-based rules cannot even be evaluated elsewhere; this asymmetry, not the behaviour, is why QA's and QD's diagnoses exclude deadline rules.
9. **Policy version and configuration change log**, timestamped, including which rule set each queue ran and whether an SLA-endangered mode was active. Directly tests QC's suspected mode switch at 10:54 and rules out the possibility that QA and QB differ only because of a configuration change during the observation window.
10. **Skill/certification matrix as data:** required skill sets per job, resource certifications with validity dates, and any routing constraint such as "prefer the least flexible qualified resource". Confirms or refutes the specialist-protection reading of QD.
11. **Volume.** Several hundred decisions per queue, so that per-rule hit rates can carry confidence intervals and rank correlations (e.g. Kendall's tau between the observed service order and each rule's ranking, computed per decision rather than over the whole day) can be tested rather than eyeballed. Analysis plan: reconstruct eligibility sets from the fields above in pandas, score each candidate rule per decision, and report accuracy restricted to *discriminating* decisions — the all-rules-agree cases (QA 08:58, QD 11:35) must be excluded or they inflate every rule alike.

# 7. Final synthesis

**Most identifiable: QC.** Its service order violates FIFO, priority and EDD so massively — the oldest job runs sixth of seven, the priority-1 job (which also carries the earliest deadline) fourth, and the second-earliest deadline last — that the whole class of due-date and arrival-order rules can be rejected on the evidence, and a short-job-first rule wins the two cleanest tests (10:10 and especially 10:18, where SPT beats priority and EDD simultaneously). The refinement — plain dynamic SPT plus a starvation cap versus SPT with linear aging — is not settled, and the fitted "age − est" index that matches 6 of 7 decisions should be read as a well-performing hypothesis on 7 data points, not a discovered formula. QA is a close second: FIFO explains 7 of 8 picks and the eighth is explained by a recorded appointment, but only one decision (08:32) separates FIFO from the aging alternative.

**Least identifiable: QB.** It is the only queue with preemption, which should make it the most informative, and instead it is the least: both interruptions occur exactly 2 minutes after a priority-1 arrival, yet one is labelled a dispatcher reassignment and is followed by a selection (B03) that contradicts the very trigger that seems to have caused it. Priority 2 never yields a pick that FIFO would not have produced anyway, so class 2 is unobservable in principle here, and the appointment field is simply not enforced. QD is a different kind of unidentifiable: its *routing* is nearly certain because skills are hard constraints, but those same constraints reduce most eligible sets to one or two jobs, so the sequencing rule rests on two decisions and one unexplained 24-minute idle.

**Most dangerous policy to infer from these observations alone: priority scheduling** — in either the preemptive or the non-preemptive form, and especially as a site-wide claim. Every queue records a priority field, and it is enacted at best for class 1 in a single queue. Three of the four queues let priority-1 jobs wait 45, 51 and 61 minutes behind priority-3 work, and the two episodes most likely to be cited as evidence of priority (QA at 08:58, QD at 11:35) are decisions where FIFO, priority and SPT all pick the same job and therefore carry no information at all. Declaring the center "priority-driven" would validate a rule that the logs mostly refute, institutionalise interruptions whose resumption cost the data implausibly reports as zero, and mask the fact that QB's escalations may be one dispatcher's discretion rather than any policy. The second most dangerous inference is "SPT for QC": if `est_min` was back-filled from realized durations, the SPT finding is an artifact of the log's construction rather than a property of the queue, and rolling SPT out on genuinely noisy estimates would degrade exactly the deadline performance that QC already fails (C07, 34 minutes late, avoidably).
