## 1. Materiality Framework

**Severity** measures the *business consequence if the narrative behind the alignment is confirmed*. **Confidence** measures the *strength of the evidence that the narrative is true*. They are deliberately decoupled: CL-602 is severity 5 even though confidence that the fraud screen was *skipped* is only medium (confidence that *no evidence exists* is high); CL-607 is severity 1 with high confidence. High severity + low confidence triggers **investigation first**; high severity + high confidence triggers **containment plus remediation**.

Severity is assessed on eight dimensions, not on edit distance:

1. **Control objective affected** — payment integrity, fraud prevention, approval authority, and the notification duty are the material controls; UI hygiene is not.
2. **Amount / financial exposure** — €480,000 (CL-602) and €250,000 (CL-608) demand different weight than €800 (CL-605).
3. **Reversibility** — an executed bank payment (CL-604, CL-610) is far less reversible than a logging gap that can be backfilled (CL-606, CL-611).
4. **Timing / sequence** — paying before approval (P3, CL-604) is categorically worse than recording approval late.
5. **Customer / regulatory harm** — missed or unverifiable notification (P4), wrongful denial, or fraud exposure outweighs internal KPI distortion.
6. **Recurrence** — 74 retry duplicates signal a systemic (if low-harm) defect; 4 zero-cost auto-approval violations signal a systemic *high-harm* defect.
7. **Evidence reliability** — a signed external report (CL-606) is stronger than a mutable CRM text link (CL-608).
8. **Model scope** — deviations that exist only because the model cannot express an outcome (P6: denials, withdrawals) are model defects, not operational ones.

| Level | Label | Meaning (consequence if confirmed) |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Severe | Breakdown of payment-integrity or fraud controls on executed/irreversible payments, or mandated checks missing on high exposure. Immediate containment + investigation. |
| 4 | High | Realized breach of approval authority on an executed payment, without evidence substantive checks were skipped. |
| 3 | Moderate | Control-evidence weaknesses that would fail audit/regulatory scrutiny and could mask abuse. |
| 2 | Low | Data, label, or integration quality issues; indirect risk via distorted conformance signal. |
| 1 | Minimal | Noise, fitting variants, model-scope artifacts; no customer or financial impact. |

   - *Discriminators:* workflow task creation/claim timestamps; payment release-queue operator ID (and whether releaser = approver — SoD test); approver session logs; bank value-date vs. acceptance timestamp; NTP/clock-sync records.

3. **CL-606 / Pattern B — external adjuster.**
   - H1: assessment performed externally; ingestion failed (evidence gap). H2: document backdated/post-hoc to justify payment. H3: pro-forma document, no substantive assessment.
   - *Discriminators:* adjuster work-order and invoice timestamps; document metadata (creation time, signature-certificate time, hash); adjuster's own system logs; claimant contact records. Prioritize the 2 cases lacking stable IDs for forensic review — they discriminate H1 from H2/H3.

4. **CL-603 / Pattern A — retry duplicates.**
   - H1: connector at-least-once retry without idempotency (most likely — all 74 tied to one connector). H2: upstream re-emission on restart. H3: user re-triggered notification.
   - *Discriminators:* identical message IDs (already observed  machine retry, not user); connector retry configuration and error logs; inter-arrival times (seconds  machine; minutes  human); portal delivery logs showing whether customers actually received duplicates.

5. **CL-609/CL-610 + two unnamed — data-aware auto-approval failures.**
   - H1: rules engine never evaluates P1 predicates (design gap). H2: predicates evaluated on stale data — bank-change event arrived after the decision (feed latency). H3: manual override routed the claim to the auto path. H4: policy version not deployed to this product line.
   - *Discriminators:* decision-service logs with rule-set version and the actual evaluated inputs; bank-change event ingestion timestamp vs. decision timestamp ( H2 if ingestion postdates decision); override audit table ( H3); deployment/config history ( H4); deterministic replay of the decisions under current rules.

6. **CL-607/CL-612 / Pattern D — scope.**
   - H1: legitimate non-payable outcomes outside model scope (model incomplete). H2: mishandled cases that should have continued (wrongful denial, pressured withdrawal). H3: mis-registered claim type at intake.
   - *Discriminators:* denial letter citing the coverage clause; customer-initiated withdrawal confirmation (signed form or call recording); complaint/re-open history; intake taxonomy audit. Resolving P6 is a *governance* decision and should be evidenced by a signed scope statement, not inferred from alignments.

7. **CL-608 — notification evidence.**
   - H1: notification genuinely sent at +3h via portal; only the linkage is weak. H2: CRM record created after the fact to cover a miss. H3: sent to a wrong/stale channel — customer never saw it.
   - *Discriminators:* portal immutable delivery/read receipts; CRM record creation metadata (created before or after any audit query, and by whom); message-store hash; customer login/acknowledgment events.

8. **CL-611 — label mismatch.**
   - H1: pure synonym. H2: genuinely different granularity ("damage" as subtype of "loss"). H3: post-hoc rename after procedure change.
   - *Discriminators:* mapping-table diff timestamps; UI/procedure version history; whether event payloads are identical except the activity string ( H1).

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Alignment cost is a property of the triple *(event log, process model, cost function)* — not of the business event. The supplied costs were configured **uniformly at 1 per visible move with no business weighting**, so cost literally counts edit operations. Six structural consequences follow, each demonstrated by supplied cases:

1. **Cost-function design inverts the true ordering.** CL-601 costs 3 for three harmless UI telemetry events; CL-602 costs 1 for a missing fraud screen on €480,000 with a day-old bank change. The cost ranking and the exposure ranking are inverted by roughly five orders of magnitude. Any queue sorted by raw cost would triage CL-607 and CL-612 (cost 4, zero control risk) above every genuine failure in this portfolio.

2. **Synchronous  compliant (data conditions are invisible).** The process tree encodes an XOR branch; it does not encode P1's amount/risk/bank-change guards. CL-609 and CL-610 are perfectly fitting at cost 0 while breaching approval-authority policy — CL-610 on the canonical fraud vector of a bank change one hour before payment. Zero cost certifies only that *some path through the net reproduces the visible sequence*; it says nothing about whether that path was permitted for this claim's data.

3. **Costly  noncompliant (model scope).** CL-607 and CL-612 tie for the highest cost (4) because the model cannot express denial or withdrawal (P6). Both cases were handled *correctly* — no payment was made or intended. Their cost measures the model's silence, not operational behavior.

4. **Hidden moves are free and meaningless.** CL-605's `T(tau,c=0)` is Petri-net routing glue. That it costs nothing is a modeling convenience; symmetrically, an alignment that repairs a control failure cheaply (CL-604) does not make the failure cheap — P3 states this explicitly.

5. **Activity granularity makes one unit of cost incommensurable across moves.** CL-611 pays 2 (one log move + one model move) for a *single real activity* recorded under a synonym — pure naming artifact. Conversely, one unit of `M(Fraud Screen)` in CL-602 may conceal an entire investigative function on a €480k exposure. The same unit of cost spans zero business content and enormous business content.

6. **Duplicated telemetry and missing-but-external evidence conflate distinct phenomena.** CL-603's retry duplicate (1 of 74) and CL-601's UI events inflate cost without business content (P5). Meanwhile `M()` merges two very different worlds: CL-606 (assessment done, signed externally at 10:05) and CL-608 (notification sent via portal, weakly linked) are priced identically to CL-602 (screen possibly never performed). Cost cannot distinguish *absence of activity* from *absence of record* from *absence of immutable linkage*.

7. **No axis for irreversibility, timing, or recurrence.** CL-604's cost of 3 equals CL-601's cost of 3, but the bank accepted €90,000 at 10:03, before approval — irreversible. CL-602's €480k and CL-610's fraud-vector payment likewise moved money that edit distance cannot weigh. And a single case's cost cannot express that 74 duplicates indicate one connector defect while 4 silent auto-approvals indicate one rules defect of far greater consequence.

8. **Optima are not unique.** CL-604 admits a second optimal alignment (synchronize Pay Claim, treat approval as late-recorded) with identical cost but a materially different business story. The supplied alignments are rightly treated as authoritative diagnostic inputs — but cost alone could never have selected between those stories, which is precisely why narrative judgment sits above, not inside, the cost function.

try:
    tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(TREE_STR)

    # Fallback: build the same tree programmatically (PM4Py version differences)
    from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator
    def leaf(lbl): return ProcessTree(label=lbl)
    fraud_seq = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE)
    fraud_seq.children = [leaf("Fraud Screen"), leaf("Manual Approve")]
    xor = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR)
    xor.children = [fraud_seq, leaf("Auto Approve")]
    tree = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE)
