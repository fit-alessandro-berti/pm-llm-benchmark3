## 1. Materiality Framework

**Severity Framework Definition**
Severity is determined by the intersection of Control Objective Violation, Financial Exposure, Regulatory Harm, and Reversibility. We define five severity levels:
*   **Severity 1 (Low):** Technical noise, UI telemetry, or benign reordering with no policy breach.
*   **Severity 2 (Medium):** Missing evidence (e.g., Assessment) with external corroboration, or notification delays within policy limits.
*   **Severity 3 (High):** Control bypass (e.g., Payment before Approval) or Policy Violation (e.g., Auto-approve on High Risk) where financial exposure exists.
*   **Severity 4 (Very High):** Material financial loss risk, regulatory breach (e.g., Fraud bypass), or irreversible payment without approval.
*   **Severity 5 (Critical):** Systemic logic failure, repeated high-value policy bypasses, or evidence of intent to circumvent controls.

**Confidence vs. Severity**
*   **Confidence** reflects the reliability of the evidence provided in the log or alignment (e.g., is the CRM link stable? Is the verbal check documented?). High confidence means the data is trustworthy; low confidence means the event *might* have happened but isn't logged.
*   **Severity** reflects the business impact regardless of logging quality. A missing log entry (`L` or `M`) with high confidence in the external system is a Severity 5 failure if the control was bypassed. A missing log entry with no external evidence is a Severity 4 failure.
*   **Confidence** is about the *trace*; **Severity** is about the *risk*. A trace can be "clean" (Cost 0) but "risky" (Policy Violation).

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Alignment costs measure edit distance (technical effort to align log to model), not business impact.

1.  **Policy Blindness (CL-609, CL-610):** The model allows `Auto Approve` on all low-risk claims. The cost function sees `S(Auto Approve)` as a perfect match (Cost 0). However, Policy P1 forbids Auto Approve if a bank change occurred (CL-610) or if risk is not strictly Low (CL-609). The cost is 0, but the risk is Severity 5.
2.  **Severity Weighting (CL-602):** A missing `Fraud Screen` event is a Cost 1 deviation. However, this relates to a 480k EUR claim with a bank change. The financial exposure is massive. The cost function treats a missing fraud check the same as a missing UI autosave.
3.  **Hidden Moves (CL-605):** A hidden transition `T(tau,c=0)` costs nothing. If this transition bypassed a validation step, the risk is hidden. The cost function rewards "clean" logs even if the logic is opaque.
4.  **Noise vs. Signal (CL-601):** UI events like `Open Workbench` cost 3 in raw alignment. These are administrative artifacts, not business risks. Penalizing them distracts from real issues like `Pay Claim` before `Fraud Screen`.
5.  **Irreversibility (CL-604):** `Pay Claim` before `Approve` is a Severity 5 failure. The alignment shows `L(Pay Claim)` then `M(Fraud Screen)`. The cost is 3. If the cost function were business-aware, this specific reordering would cost significantly more than a label mismatch (CL-611, Cost 2).
6.  **Scope Ambiguity (CL-607):** A denied claim generates Cost 4 because the model expects a payment path. This is a model design flaw, not a compliance failure. The cost function penalizes the model owner for a design limitation rather than the operator for a risk.
