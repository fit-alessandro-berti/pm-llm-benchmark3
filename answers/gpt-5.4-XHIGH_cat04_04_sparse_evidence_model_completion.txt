| S2 | Another stakeholder believes the two checks normally run in parallel and logs show whichever completes first. | That they always overlap; that no cases are sequential. | Medium because it is plausible but also unverifiable from completion-only data. | Supports leaving `Credit Check` and `Engineering Review` unordered in balanced/permissive models. |
| S3 | `Supplier Confirmation` is mandatory for every external component. | That every case contains external components; that missing `Supplier Confirmation` means non-compliance. | Medium. | Optionality of `Supplier Confirmation` may be attribute-conditioned, not unconditional. |
| S4 | Stocked components legitimately skip `Supplier Confirmation`. | That the observed no-`Supplier Confirmation` traces are exactly the stocked-component cases. | Medium. | Represent no-`Supplier Confirmation` behavior, but disclose the latent stocked/external attribute. |
| S5 | The stocked/external attribute is missing and 5-10% of `Supplier Confirmation` events may be absent because of integration problems. | The exact missingness rate in this sample; that every missing `Supplier Confirmation` is a logging error. | Medium-high for the existence of missingness; medium for the rate estimate. | Model no-`Supplier Confirmation` behavior as a mixture of true optionality and missing data. |

Both directions in a DFG do not by themselves prove concurrency. `Credit Check -> Engineering Review` and `Engineering Review -> Credit Check` only show that both completion orders occur across cases. The same pattern can arise from true overlap, from flexible sequencing, from different routing conditions, or from equal-time completions broken by ingestion order (S7). With completion-only logs, a bidirectional DFG is evidence of ordering variability, not proof of a parallel block.

# 2. Shared Assumptions
- The log records only activity completion events. Every edge or partial-order relation below is therefore about completion order, not about start times or resource overlap.
- `Register Order` is treated as the unique intended start and `Archive Case` as the unique intended end, following D1.
- `Approve Configuration`, `Release Production`, and `Archive Case` are modeled as single-occurrence activities with the hard chain `Approve Configuration < Release Production < Archive Case`.
- `Credit Check` and `Engineering Review` are assumed to occur at most once per case because no repetition evidence is provided.
- `Expedite Review` is optional, occurs at most once, and must be after both main checks and before approval. This is one of the stronger cross-source constraints.
- The absence of `Supplier Confirmation` in a trace is treated as an abstracted control-flow alternative that conflates two possibilities: legitimate stocked-component cases (S4) and logging loss (S5). This is a modeling convenience, not a claim that those two causes are behaviorally identical.
- Because the external/stocked attribute is unavailable, none of the three candidates conditions `Supplier Confirmation` on a case attribute; they approximate that missing attribute through branch choice.
- Optionality is represented either by explicit XOR branch enumeration or by `XOR(tau, activity)` in POWL.
- No candidate includes rework loops, cancellations, or repeated approvals, because none are observed and none are supported by the domain rules.

# 3. Conservative Candidate
**Inductive bias.** This candidate is intentionally extensional: it treats the seven observed variant strings as the language. That gives maximal protection against over-generalizing from sparse evidence and sidesteps the concurrency ambiguity by refusing to infer any extra interleavings. The downside is classic overfitting: duplicate labels are repeated across many XOR branches, the model scales poorly as more variants arrive, and it cannot distinguish genuine optionality from missing `Supplier Confirmation` data.

**Compact formal expression.**
```text
XOR(
  seq(R,C,E,S,A,P,AR),
  seq(R,E,C,S,A,P,AR),
  seq(R,C,E,A,P,AR),
  seq(R,E,C,A,P,AR),
  seq(R,C,S,E,A,P,AR),
  seq(R,C,E,S,X,A,P,AR),
  seq(R,E,C,X,S,A,P,AR)
)
```
where `R=Register Order`, `C=Credit Check`, `E=Engineering Review`, `S=Supplier Confirmation`, `X=Expedite Review`, `A=Approve Configuration`, `P=Release Production`, `AR=Archive Case`.

**PM4Py code.**
```python
import pm4py

model_conservative = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "X("
    "->('Register Order','Credit Check','Engineering Review','Supplier Confirmation','Approve Configuration','Release Production','Archive Case'),"
    "->('Register Order','Engineering Review','Credit Check','Supplier Confirmation','Approve Configuration','Release Production','Archive Case'),"
    "->('Register Order','Credit Check','Engineering Review','Approve Configuration','Release Production','Archive Case'),"
    "->('Register Order','Engineering Review','Credit Check','Approve Configuration','Release Production','Archive Case'),"
    "->('Register Order','Credit Check','Supplier Confirmation','Engineering Review','Approve Configuration','Release Production','Archive Case'),"
    "->('Register Order','Credit Check','Engineering Review','Supplier Confirmation','Expedite Review','Approve Configuration','Release Production','Archive Case'),"
    "->('Register Order','Engineering Review','Credit Check','Expedite Review','Supplier Confirmation','Approve Configuration','Release Production','Archive Case')"
    ")"
)
```

**Deliberately excluded behavior.**
- Any trace not seen in the seven observed variants, even if it is compatible with D3-D6.
- `Expedite Review` without `Supplier Confirmation`, because no such case was observed.
- `Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check` and `Supplier Confirmation`-first completions, because they are unobserved.
- Any interpretation of missing `Supplier Confirmation` as hidden logging loss rather than an explicit branch.

**Known underfitting risks.**
- If S5 is materially true, this model wrongly treats some missing `Supplier Confirmation` cases as real control-flow variants rather than data loss.
- It cannot admit plausible but unseen traces such as `Register Order, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case`.
- It hard-codes the accidental co-occurrence patterns of rare `Expedite Review` traces and will break immediately when a new but valid interleaving appears.
- Duplicate labels across XOR branches make the tree bulky and less scalable as a long-run process representation.

# 4. Balanced Candidate
**Inductive bias.** This candidate generalizes where multiple sources jointly justify it, but stops short of the weakest interpretation. It treats `Credit Check` and `Engineering Review` as both required yet unordered in completion terms, models `Supplier Confirmation` as conditionally optional under a latent attribute/missingness abstraction, and encodes the rare `Expedite Review` only where domain rules are fairly clear. The goal is not to prove concurrency, but to preserve uncertainty without collapsing into arbitrary freedom.

**Compact formal expression.**
```text
model_balanced = XOR(B_none, B_afterC, B_afterE)
optX = XOR(tau, X)

B_none   = PO({R,C,E,optX,A,P,AR} |
              R<C, R<E, C<A, E<A, C<optX, E<optX, optX<A, A<P, P<AR})

B_afterC = PO({R,C,E,S,optX,A,P,AR} |
              R<C, R<E, R<S, C<S, S<A, C<A, E<A,
              C<optX, E<optX, optX<A, A<P, P<AR})

B_afterE = PO({R,C,E,S,optX,A,P,AR} |
              R<C, R<E, R<S, E<S, S<A, C<A, E<A,
              C<optX, E<optX, optX<A, A<P, P<AR})
```

**How sequence, concurrency, and partial order are decided.**
- `Credit Check` versus `Engineering Review`: I do not encode a strict sequence because V1/V2 plus D7/S7 are inconsistent with a single completion order, and I do not encode a strong parallel operator because the log does not prove actual overlap. I therefore use partial order: both must finish before approval, but neither completion is forced before the other.
- `Supplier Confirmation`: I do not make it universally mandatory because V3/V4 plus S4/S5 contradict that. I also do not allow it to complete before both main checks, because V5 and D5 suggest at least one main check should have progressed enough first. The compromise is a three-way XOR: absent, present after `Credit Check`, or present after `Engineering Review`.
- `Expedite Review`: I model it as `XOR(tau, Expedite Review)` with hard predecessors `Credit Check` and `Engineering Review` and hard successor `Approve Configuration`, because D6/S6 are comparatively strong.
- `Supplier Confirmation` versus `Expedite Review`: I leave them unordered when both exist, because V6 and V7 jointly show both completion orders and S6 explicitly permits either one.

**PM4Py code.**
```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def _balanced_branch(kind: str):
    R = Transition("Register Order")
    C = Transition("Credit Check")
    E = Transition("Engineering Review")
    X = Transition("Expedite Review")
    A = Transition("Approve Configuration")
    P = Transition("Release Production")
    AR = Transition("Archive Case")
    opt_x = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [SilentTransition(), X])

    nodes = [R, C, E, opt_x, A, P, AR]
    S = None
    if kind != "none":
        S = Transition("Supplier Confirmation")
        nodes.append(S)

    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes)
    for u, v in [
        (R, C),
        (R, E),
        (C, A),
        (E, A),
        (C, opt_x),
        (E, opt_x),
        (opt_x, A),
        (A, P),
        (P, AR),
    ]:
        po.order.add_edge(u, v)

    if S is not None:
        po.order.add_edge(R, S)
        po.order.add_edge(S, A)
        if kind == "after_c":
            po.order.add_edge(C, S)
        elif kind == "after_e":
            po.order.add_edge(E, S)

    return po


model_balanced = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.XOR,
    [
        _balanced_branch("none"),
        _balanced_branch("after_c"),
        _balanced_branch("after_e"),
    ],
)
```

**Deliberately excluded behavior.**
- `Supplier Confirmation` completing before both main checks, for example `Register Order, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check, Engineering Review, ...`.
- `Expedite Review` before both checks or after approval.
- `Approve Configuration` before either `Credit Check` or `Engineering Review`.
- Universal mandatory `Supplier Confirmation`; no-`Supplier Confirmation` remains allowed because of S4/S5.

**Known underfitting risks.**
- If S1 is actually a hard policy and the opposite completion order is only a logging artifact, the unordered `Credit Check`/`Engineering Review` relation is too permissive.
- If `Supplier Confirmation` can genuinely complete before both principal checks because of hidden start-time overlap, the model is too strict.
- If `Expedite Review` is semantically tied to supplier involvement in practice, the model over-separates those choices.
- The missing stocked/external attribute means the model still conflates a real business choice with data loss.

# 5. Permissive Candidate
**Inductive bias.** This candidate admits the broadest behavior still consistent with the stronger domain rules. It treats completion ordering among `Credit Check`, `Engineering Review`, and `Supplier Confirmation` as largely unresolved, uses approval as the main synchronization point, and keeps `Expedite Review` constrained only where the rules are clearest. The point is exploratory sensitivity analysis: show what becomes possible if the ambiguous evidence is interpreted in the weakest defensible way.

**Compact formal expression.**
```text
optS = XOR(tau, S)
optX = XOR(tau, X)

model_permissive = PO({R,C,E,optS,optX,A,P,AR} |
  R<C, R<E, R<optS,
  C<A, E<A, optS<A,
  C<optX, E<optX, optX<A,
  A<P, P<AR
})
```

**Reasonable-generalization boundary.** Reasonable here means: start/end are fixed, both principal checks must complete before approval, `Supplier Confirmation` is optional and if present must finish before approval, `Expedite Review` remains optional but only after both principal checks and before approval, and no activity repeats. Behavior remains forbidden when it violates those gates. What I intentionally relax is the completion ordering between `Supplier Confirmation` and the two main checks: because D5 speaks about when supplier work may begin, not when it must complete, and because only completion timestamps exist, this candidate allows `Supplier Confirmation` to complete before both main-check completions.

**PM4Py code.**
```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

R = Transition("Register Order")
C = Transition("Credit Check")
E = Transition("Engineering Review")
S = Transition("Supplier Confirmation")
X = Transition("Expedite Review")
A = Transition("Approve Configuration")
P = Transition("Release Production")
AR = Transition("Archive Case")

opt_s = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [SilentTransition(), S])
opt_x = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [SilentTransition(), X])

model_permissive = StrictPartialOrder([R, C, E, opt_s, opt_x, A, P, AR])
for u, v in [
    (R, C),
    (R, E),
    (R, opt_s),
    (C, A),
    (E, A),
    (opt_s, A),
    (C, opt_x),
    (E, opt_x),
    (opt_x, A),
    (A, P),
    (P, AR),
]:
    model_permissive.order.add_edge(u, v)
```

**Deliberately excluded behavior.**
- `Approve Configuration` before either principal check.
- `Supplier Confirmation` after approval.
- `Expedite Review` before either principal check or after approval.
- Loops, repeated checks, repeated approvals, and post-release work.

**Known underfitting risks.**
- It still excludes any rework/cancellation loops that may exist in reality but are not evidenced here.
- It does not condition `Supplier Confirmation` on the missing stocked/external attribute; it only approximates that uncertainty.
- If future data show `Expedite Review` can trigger additional checks or re-approval, this model is too shallow.
- If `Supplier Confirmation` is truly mandatory in almost all cases and missingness dominates, even this model remains under-specified because it lacks an explicit missing-event mechanism.

**Intentionally excluded traces.**
- Conservative:
  - `Register Order, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case` is excluded at the third event because no enumerated branch has `Supplier Confirmation` there.
  - `Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Expedite Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case` is excluded at `Expedite Review` because the exact language never contains expedite without either prior `Supplier Confirmation` or the specific observed rare branch.
- Balanced:
  - `Register Order, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case` is excluded at `Supplier Confirmation` because every `Supplier Confirmation`-present branch requires it after either `Credit Check` or `Engineering Review`.
  - `Register Order, Credit Check, Expedite Review, Engineering Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case` is excluded at `Expedite Review` because D6/S6 require both principal checks first.
- Permissive:
  - `Register Order, Credit Check, Expedite Review, Engineering Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case` is excluded at `Expedite Review` for the same earliest reason: `Engineering Review` has not yet completed.
  - `Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Approve Configuration, Supplier Confirmation, Release Production, Archive Case` is excluded at `Approve Configuration` because present `Supplier Confirmation` must precede approval.

# 9. Recommendation and Data-Collection Plan
Recommend `model_balanced` for immediate use. It fits the observed log, respects the stronger domain rules, and encodes the main ambiguities explicitly instead of pretending they are resolved. Use `model_permissive` for sensitivity analysis. If downstream conformance or KPI conclusions change materially between balanced and permissive, the remaining uncertainty is operationally important and should be resolved before making strong compliance claims.

Concrete data-collection plan:
- Export the missing stocked/external attribute at the order or component level, so `Supplier Confirmation` optionality can be conditioned instead of guessed.
- Capture lifecycle `start` and `complete` timestamps for `Credit Check`, `Engineering Review`, `Supplier Confirmation`, and `Expedite Review`. This is the most direct way to distinguish strict sequence, overlap, and completion-order artifacts.
- Add a case flag for whether `Supplier Confirmation` was expected, and another flag for whether the supplier integration reported a transmission failure. That separates true optionality from missing events.
- Record the trigger reason and policy basis for `Expedite Review` so its rarity can be interpreted as business selectivity rather than data sparsity alone.
- Preserve event-source identifiers or message IDs for supplier events to estimate actual missingness instead of relying on a rough 5-10% stakeholder estimate.

Future traces that would be maximally informative:
- Cases with known start/end times where `Credit Check` and `Engineering Review` overlap in real time despite different completion order.
- Cases with `Supplier Confirmation` present and completion order `Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check`, which would support the `after_e` branch directly.
- Cases with `Supplier Confirmation` apparently completing before both principal-check completions; these would distinguish balanced from permissive.
- Cases with `Expedite Review` present but `Supplier Confirmation` absent, especially when the stocked/external attribute is known.
- Cases where an external-component order has no logged `Supplier Confirmation` but the integration audit shows a missing message; those would justify replacing a control-flow branch with an explicit data-quality treatment.

# 10. Uncertainty Statement
- `Register Order` as start, `Archive Case` as end, and `Approve Configuration < Release Production < Archive Case`: high confidence.
- `Credit Check` and `Engineering Review` both required before approval: medium-high confidence.
- `Credit Check` versus `Engineering Review` as unordered rather than strictly sequential: medium confidence. The strongest alternative is S1's strict precedence claim, but it conflicts with observed reverse completion order and completion-only logging.
- `Supplier Confirmation` as sometimes absent in the model language: medium confidence. The observed no-`SupplierConfirmation` cases are real in the log, but their interpretation as optionality versus missing data remains unresolved.
- Balanced rule that present `Supplier Confirmation` should follow at least one principal-check completion: medium-low confidence. It is a disciplined reading of D5, but D5 is about when supplier work may begin, not when it must complete.
- `Expedite Review` as optional, after both principal checks, and before approval: high confidence relative to the other uncertain parts.
- `Expedite Review` unordered relative to `Supplier Confirmation` when both are present: medium-high confidence because V6, V7, and S6 all point the same way despite low counts.
- Excluding loops and repeated activities: medium confidence. There is no evidence for rework, but the sample is small and sparse.

A plausible alternative interpretation that would materially change the recommendation is this: if future lifecycle timestamps show that `Credit Check` always finishes before `Engineering Review` starts and the apparent reverse completion order is purely an ingestion/timestamp artifact, then the balanced partial-order treatment is too permissive and a stricter sequential model should replace it. Another equally important alternative is that almost all no-`Supplier Confirmation` cases are integration failures rather than stocked-component cases; that would make the conservative and permissive control-flow branches too literal and would shift the modeling problem from behavior discovery toward missing-event handling.
