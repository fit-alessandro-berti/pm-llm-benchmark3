# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

I treat the expressions as PM4Py process-tree expressions under their standard semantics: -> is sequence, + is parallelism, X is exclusive choice, and tau is silent behavior. Thus X(a, tau) is an optional activity, not merely a way of tolerating a noisy observation. Model C is an exclusive choice among the eight complete sequences shown.

The review separates four uses:

- Exploration and variant discovery: broad coverage and understandable summaries are useful. Some false positives can be acceptable if they are clearly labelled as hypotheses.
- Conformance enforcement and release control: mandatory checks, approval, and high-risk ordering rules must be explicit. False acceptance of a forbidden trace is more serious than rejection for manual review.
- Simulation and capacity planning: the model must represent plausible behavior and frequencies, not just align well. An over-permissive model can simulate impossible work and give unsafe throughput estimates.
- Stakeholder communication: the notation must expose policy concepts such as “mandatory,” “optional,” and “conditional,” rather than hiding them in silent paths or a long list of variants.

Consequently, there is no universally best fitness/precision trade-off. For exploration, I would accept lower precision in return for transparent uncertainty and broad compositional coverage. For enforcement, I require policy-level tests and a fail-closed treatment of missing attributes; a high aggregate fitness cannot compensate for accepting B3 or B4. For simulation, both the language and event frequencies need validation. All conclusions below are conditional on the stated traces and on standard PM4Py semantics; the missing preprocessing, seeds, costs, and attributes prevent stronger claims.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

The strongest case for S is that it is a compact compositional envelope. It captures the two possible approval labels, puts registration and release at the boundaries, allows Supplier Review and Expedite Review to be absent, and can accept new interleavings of activities already present. That explains its high holdout fitness, high token generalization, simplicity, runtime, and comprehension. If the goal were exploratory discovery in a log known to contain missing events, this flexibility could be useful as a deliberately broad first hypothesis.

The strongest case against it is that its broadness is not controlled uncertainty. The parallel block contains optional versions of both mandatory checks. It also makes Expedite Review concurrent with the checks, so the model has no way to distinguish a missing observation from an actual policy violation. The model’s high fitness can therefore be obtained by declaring required work optional and by allowing illegal orderings.

Under standard process-tree semantics, S accepts at least these undesirable or policy-ambiguous traces:

1. Register Dossier, Manager Approval, Release Order — both mandatory checks are skipped.
2. Register Dossier, Credit Check, Manager Approval, Release Order — Technical Review is skipped.
3. Register Dossier, Technical Review, Auto Approval, Release Order — Credit Check is skipped.
4. Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order — Expedite Review precedes both checks, directly contradicting P4.
5. Register Dossier, Credit Check, Expedite Review, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order — Expedite Review occurs after only one mandatory check.
6. Register Dossier, Supplier Review, Expedite Review, Manager Approval, Release Order — both checks are absent and Expedite Review is early.
7. Register Dossier, Supplier Review, Technical Review, Credit Check, Auto Approval, Release Order — Supplier Review can occur before either check, and S cannot know whether it was required by the source type.
8. Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Supplier Review, Release Order is rejected because its supplier event is after approval; this is a useful boundary, but the model has no explicit policy explanation for why Supplier Review must be before approval.

The first six are accidental semantic flaws relative to the stated policy, not a defensible noise strategy. Treating an absent Credit Check as a silent model move may be useful for data-quality triage, but it must produce “unresolved/missing evidence,” not “conformant.” Optional Supplier Review is a more legitimate abstraction because source type is missing, but it still cannot enforce P3. S also has no Correct Dossier transition at all, so it rejects every correction trace and cannot express P6. Its XOR approval is a genuine strength: it does not accept both approvals or no approval, assuming the parser’s standard XOR semantics.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

The strongest case for C is that its listed branches encode several important constraints directly: registration is first, release is last, both mandatory checks appear in the ordinary branches, their two orders are represented in some cases, Supplier Review and Expedite Review occur before approval where they are present, and exactly one approval occurs. Its high training precision and low simplicity score are consistent with a narrow, auditable catalog of known variants. For an approved finite set of already reviewed variants, that can be an intentional control.

The strongest case against C is that it is a variant list, not a policy model. An exclusive choice among eight complete sequences rejects any legal combination that was not enumerated. The branches do not form a small grammar over independent policy dimensions:

- Alternate check order is represented only for branches without Supplier Review, Expedite Review, or Auto Approval. A Technical-then-Credit trace with Auto Approval is absent.
- Supplier Review plus Expedite Review is represented only with Credit-then-Technical and Manager Approval.
- Auto Approval has only one branch, with Credit-then-Technical and neither Supplier Review nor Expedite Review.
- The sole correction branch repeats both checks, in one order, and uses Manager Approval. It does not represent a correction affecting only one check, multiple correction rounds, or alternate recheck order.
- Because the external/internal attribute was not in the discovery log, C has no condition that says when Supplier Review is required. It merely lists some supplier-containing variants.

Thus F2 and F4 describe a real generalization failure, not just a cosmetic update burden. An unseen legal combination is not automatically noncompliant. That is only a valid decision if the organization has explicitly adopted a closed-world approval policy in which every new combination must be separately approved; P1–P7 instead describe compositional rules that imply many combinations.

The 0.744 holdout fitness and 0.412 token generalization are consistent with branch memorization. The gap from 0.973 training fitness and 0.941 training precision should be treated as a warning, although its magnitude cannot be attributed without the split and cost settings. High precision may be an artifact of memorizing frequent positive traces and refusing unseen combinations. F5 is decisive here: precision calculated only against observed positive traces is not a test of forbidden behavior.

Duplicate labels and branch enumeration also create operational risk. If repeated labels refer to distinct transition objects, alignments and diagnostics may lose phase identity; if they refer to the same activity name in many branches, ownership and impact analysis become difficult. The correction path necessarily repeats labels, but 33 undocumented duplicates may include much more than that. A longer branch list multiplies maintenance: adding Auto Approval to every legal Supplier/Expedite/check-order combination requires many edits and creates opportunities for inconsistent policy. Runtime and low stakeholder comprehension are predictable consequences of that structure. C may be retained as a historical catalog of frequent variants, but “audit-ready” does not follow from being detailed.

# 7. Third Alternative

I recommend a block-structured POWL backbone plus explicit data and policy checks. It is a language model for structural behavior, not the sole compliance oracle. Let R, L, C, T, S, E, M, A, and K denote Register Dossier, Release Order, Credit Check, Technical Review, Supplier Review, Expedite Review, Manager Approval, Auto Approval, and Correct Dossier.

The compact semantics are:

~~~text
model_third =
  R ; PO{ U(C,T), S_mode, Q, Opt(E) } ; (M XOR A) ; L

U(C,T): both C and T occur exactly once initially, in either order
S_mode: S is mandatory when external=True; otherwise it is not required
Opt(E): E may be absent, but if present it is after both initial checks and before approval
Q: zero or more K ; (C' XOR T' XOR U(C',T')) correction rounds
~~~

Here Q is ordered after the initial checks and before E; E is ordered after both the initial checks and all correction rounds. S has no order relation with the checks in the partial-order block, because P3 gives no such order; all of the pre-approval block remains before the approval choice. If the operating procedure requires Supplier Review after the checks, that edge should be added as a documented policy decision rather than inferred from frequency.

The generic model uses an explicit optional Supplier Review only as an unknown-source envelope. It is not a compliance pass: an unknown source produces UNRESOLVED_SOURCE. An external case uses a model with mandatory Supplier Review. An internal case uses a model in which Supplier Review is not required; whether it is permitted as an extra activity is a separate policy flag. This prevents the missing source attribute from being silently converted into a universal optional rule.

The following is a self-contained PM4Py construction skeleton. It uses one partial-order block instead of enumerating cross-products of variants. The POWL LOOP is used for an unbounded sequence of correction rounds; the outer XOR supplies zero rounds. The repeated recheck labels are a small semantic loop, not one branch per trace variant.

~~~python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    Transition, SilentTransition, OperatorPOWL, StrictPartialOrder
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def xor(*nodes):
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, operands=list(nodes))


def partial_order(*nodes):
    return StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))


def sequence(*nodes):
    p = partial_order(*nodes)
    for left, right in zip(nodes, nodes[1:]):
        p.order.add_edge(left, right)
    return p


def optional(label):
    return xor(Transition(label=label), SilentTransition())


def unordered(*nodes):
    # No edge between nodes: every node is mandatory, but their order is free.
    return partial_order(*nodes)


def zero_or_more(body):
    # LOOP(body, tau) represents repeatable correction rounds; the XOR adds 0.
    return xor(
        SilentTransition(),
        OperatorPOWL(
            operator=Operator.LOOP,
            operands=[body, SilentTransition()],
        ),
    )


def correction_round():
    # Each alternative is a separate occurrence family with the same log label.
    only_credit = Transition(label="Credit Check")
    only_technical = Transition(label="Technical Review")
    both_credit_first = sequence(
        Transition(label="Credit Check"),
        Transition(label="Technical Review"),
    )
    both_technical_first = sequence(
        Transition(label="Technical Review"),
        Transition(label="Credit Check"),
    )
    affected_rechecks = xor(
        only_credit,
        only_technical,
        both_credit_first,
        both_technical_first,
    )
    return sequence(Transition(label="Correct Dossier"), affected_rechecks)


def build_model(source_mode="unknown"):
    register = Transition(label="Register Dossier")
    credit = Transition(label="Credit Check")
    technical = Transition(label="Technical Review")
    checks = unordered(credit, technical)

    if source_mode == "external":
        supplier = Transition(label="Supplier Review")
    elif source_mode in ("internal", "unknown"):
        # Internal does not require S. Unknown is only an exploratory envelope.
        supplier = optional("Supplier Review")
    else:
        raise ValueError("source_mode must be external, internal, or unknown")

    corrections = zero_or_more(correction_round())
    expedite = optional("Expedite Review")

    pre_approval = partial_order(checks, supplier, corrections, expedite)
    pre_approval.order.add_edge(checks, corrections)
    pre_approval.order.add_edge(checks, expedite)
    pre_approval.order.add_edge(corrections, expedite)

    approval = xor(
        Transition(label="Manager Approval"),
        Transition(label="Auto Approval"),
    )
    return sequence(
        register,
        pre_approval,
        approval,
        Transition(label="Release Order"),
    )


# The main model is deliberately an unknown-source envelope. Use the
# attribute-conditioned models for enforcement whenever source is available.
model_third = build_model(source_mode="unknown")
model_third_by_source = {
    "external": build_model(source_mode="external"),
    "internal": build_model(source_mode="internal"),
}

# Standard PM4Py conversion/checks for the selected main model:
net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_third)
pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True)
~~~

If the pinned PM4Py release requires the three-operand loop form, the Operator.LOOP constructor should use that release’s documented body/redo/exit convention while preserving the stated zero-or-more-round language. The version and exact constructor form belong in the reproducibility record.

The policy layer is required because POWL alone cannot infer a missing event attribute. A minimal explicit policy dictionary is:

~~~python
POLICY = {
    "first": "Register Dossier",
    "last": "Release Order",
    "mandatory_before_approval": {"Credit Check", "Technical Review"},
    "exactly_one_approval": {"Manager Approval", "Auto Approval"},
    "conditional_supplier": {"attribute": "external", "required_value": True},
    "expedite": {
        "optional": True,
        "after": {"Credit Check", "Technical Review"},
        "before": {"Manager Approval", "Auto Approval"},
    },
    "correction_attribute": "affected_checks",
    "allowed_affected_checks": {
        frozenset({"Credit Check"}),
        frozenset({"Technical Review"}),
        frozenset({"Credit Check", "Technical Review"}),
    },
}
~~~

The checker below is intentionally fail-closed for missing decision attributes. events is an ordered list of activity names and event_attributes[i] contains attributes for event i; in production, the source and affected-check values should be immutable case-level/event-level fields, not inferred from the model’s best alignment.

~~~python
def audit_case(events, case_attributes, event_attributes):
    errors = []
    unresolved = []

    if not events or events[0] != POLICY["first"]:
        errors.append("Register Dossier is not first")
    if not events or events[-1] != POLICY["last"]:
        errors.append("Release Order is not last")

    approval_positions = [
        i for i, a in enumerate(events)
        if a in POLICY["exactly_one_approval"]
    ]
    if len(approval_positions) != 1:
        errors.append("exactly one approval is required")
    else:
        approval_i = approval_positions[0]
        if any(a == POLICY["last"] for a in events[:approval_i]):
            errors.append("release precedes approval")

    first = {}
    for i, activity in enumerate(events):
        if activity in POLICY["mandatory_before_approval"] and activity not in first:
            first[activity] = i
    if set(first) != POLICY["mandatory_before_approval"]:
        errors.append("a mandatory check is absent")

    expedite_positions = [i for i, a in enumerate(events)
                          if a == "Expedite Review"]
    if len(expedite_positions) > 1:
        errors.append("Expedite Review occurs more than once")
    if expedite_positions and set(first) == POLICY["mandatory_before_approval"]:
        e = expedite_positions[0]
        if not all(first[a] < e for a in POLICY["mandatory_before_approval"]):
            errors.append("Expedite Review is before both mandatory checks")
        if approval_positions and e > approval_positions[0]:
            errors.append("Expedite Review is after approval")

    external = case_attributes.get("external")
    supplier_present = "Supplier Review" in events
    if external is None:
        unresolved.append("external/internal source is missing")
    elif external is True and not supplier_present:
        errors.append("external order lacks Supplier Review")
    # If internal Supplier Review is forbidden by local policy, add that as a
    # configured error. P3 alone says it is not required internally.

    # Every repeat must be authorized by a preceding correction. After a
    # correction, exactly the declared affected set may occur once.
    seen = set()
    pending = None
    for i, activity in enumerate(events):
        if activity == "Correct Dossier":
            if pending is not None and pending["seen"] != pending["expected"]:
                errors.append("a correction round was not completed")
            affected = event_attributes[i].get("affected_checks")
            if affected is None:
                unresolved.append("affected_checks is missing after Correct Dossier")
                pending = {"expected": set(), "seen": set(), "unknown": True}
            else:
                expected = set(affected)
                if frozenset(expected) not in POLICY["allowed_affected_checks"]:
                    errors.append("invalid affected-check set")
                pending = {"expected": expected, "seen": set(), "unknown": False}
        elif activity in POLICY["mandatory_before_approval"]:
            if activity not in seen:
                seen.add(activity)
            elif pending is None:
                errors.append("repeated mandatory check without Correct Dossier")
            elif pending.get("unknown"):
                unresolved.append("cannot validate a recheck without affected_checks")
            else:
                if activity not in pending["expected"]:
                    errors.append("unaffected check repeated after correction")
                elif activity in pending["seen"]:
                    errors.append("same check repeated twice in one correction round")
                else:
                    pending["seen"].add(activity)
                    if pending["seen"] == pending["expected"]:
                        pending = None

    if pending is not None and not pending.get("unknown"):
        errors.append("declared affected checks were not all repeated")

    return {
        "status": "REJECT" if errors else ("UNRESOLVED" if unresolved else "ACCEPT"),
        "errors": errors,
        "unresolved": unresolved,
    }
~~~

This hybrid has explicit semantics for all known rules: no silent omission of the mandatory checks, conditional Supplier Review, an optional Expedite Review ordered after the checks and before approval, an XOR approval, and correction rounds that authorize only one or both affected checks. The source and affected-check omissions remain real limitations. An unknown source or missing affected-check field is an unresolved data-quality state, not evidence of conformance. If the organization is willing to treat missing affected-check data as a manual exception, that exception must be recorded and audited.

# 9. Validation Experiments

The teams should rerun the comparison as a reproducible experiment rather than report one score per model.

1. Freeze the environment and preprocessing. Publish Python and PM4Py versions, model serialization, activity classifier, case identifier, lifecycle handling, timestamp tie-breaking, event filtering, deduplication rules, attribute availability, and all alignment parameters. Preserve the exact train and holdout case IDs. Use case-level splits, not event-level splits, and publish several seeds, for example 7, 19, 42, 73, and 101. If cases can share a dossier or customer episode, split by that group instead.

2. Use attribute- and structure-stratified holdouts. Report separate results for check order, Supplier Review presence, Expedite Review presence, approval type, correction count, correction affected set, source type, and missingness pattern. Hold out legal Cartesian combinations such as alternate check order plus Supplier Review plus Auto Approval; this directly tests compositional generalization. Keep a temporal holdout if the process has drift.

3. Build a curated negative suite independently of the positive log. Include B3, B4, B8, both approvals, no approval, release before approval, missing each mandatory check, E before each mandatory check, repeated checks without K, an unaffected recheck after K, and external orders without S. Measure false acceptance and false rejection at the trace/policy layer. A precision value computed only from positive cases is not a substitute.

4. Run perturbation tests. Starting from known valid cases, delete C, T, S, E, or approval events; duplicate C/T with and without K; insert K with missing affected_checks; and permute S/E around the checks. Run both models and the third policy checker. The desired result for a deletion is an explicit missing-evidence or nonconformant status, not a silently “good” alignment. Test missingness separately from genuine negative traces so data quality is not conflated with behavior.

5. Sweep alignment costs and thresholds. At minimum vary log-move, model-move, and silent-move costs over a documented grid, and report the classification of every probe and negative trace at each setting. A model should not pass governance because a cheap tau path hides a mandatory omission. Report fitness, alignment precision, generalization, simplicity, and runtime separately, with confidence intervals across cases and seeds.

6. Check model soundness and reachability. Verify that Register Dossier can reach Release Order, that both approval alternatives are reachable, that external Supplier Review paths are reachable, that each correction alternative is reachable, and that no dead transitions or approval bypasses exist. Run the PM4Py conversion and diagnostics:

~~~python
import pm4py


def pm4py_metrics(log, model):
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True)
    return {
        "fitness": pm4py.fitness_alignments(log, net, im, fm),
        "precision": pm4py.precision_alignments(log, net, im, fm),
        "generalization": pm4py.generalization_tbr(log, net, im, fm),
        "simplicity": pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(
            net, im, fm, variant="arc_degree"
        ),
        "diagnostics": pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(
            log, net, im, fm
        ),
    }


metrics_third = pm4py_metrics(log, model_third)
~~~

For the third model, the PM4Py result must be joined with audit_case results. A trace with a good alignment but UNRESOLVED_SOURCE or UNRESOLVED affected-check evidence is not compliant. Conversely, a trace rejected by the generic unknown-source envelope should be reevaluated using the source-conditioned model before being called nonconformant.

7. Measure runtime under matched conditions. Use the same machine, warmed and cold runs, equal case-length bins, identical alignment settings, and p50/p95/p99 latency plus memory. Report preprocessing time separately from conformance time. C’s reported median alone is not enough to make an operational decision.

8. Evaluate comprehension with a documented protocol. Blindly randomize model labels, give compliance, operations, audit, and data-quality participants the same short training, and ask them to classify trace cards including B1–B8 and the new correction cases. Score accuracy by rule, critical-error count, time, confidence, and inter-rater agreement. Predefine a threshold such as zero critical false approvals and at least 90% accuracy on noncritical cases; report the raw confusion matrix rather than one informal 1–5 score.

9. Define rejection gates before looking at aggregate results. Reject a candidate if it is unsound, has an unreachable mandatory/approval path, accepts any critical negative such as B3/B4 or both approvals, treats missing source or affected-check data as accepted compliance, or changes probe classifications under a reasonable alignment-cost sweep. Also reject it if critical false-acceptance rate is nonzero in the curated suite, even if fitness, precision, or generalization is highest. Aggregate metrics are secondary to these gates.

# 10. Governance Recommendation

Model S may be used for exploratory discovery and interactive variant brainstorming, provided every visualization is labelled “permissive exploratory model” and it is not used to approve release, certify compliance, or drive a simulation presented as policy-constrained. Its optional checks and early Expedite Review make it unsafe for enforcement.

Model C may be retained as a read-only catalog of frequent historical variants or as a finite approved-variant control if management intentionally adopts a closed-world policy. It should not be presented as a generalized policy model, and it should not be the default conformance gate. I would retire both originals as production compliance models: S for false acceptance, C for brittle false rejection and maintenance risk. Retiring here means removing them from automated decision authority, not deleting their historical artifacts.

The third alternative is the recommended candidate for compliance, but only after it passes the validation gates. Before deployment, the organization must either add a reliable external/internal attribute or route unknown-source cases to manual review. It must also capture affected_checks at each Correct Dossier event, or route every correction case to manual review. The source-conditioned model and policy checker must reject critical negatives, preserve all stated valid probes, pass soundness/reachability checks, remain stable under cost sensitivity, and meet the documented comprehension and runtime thresholds. Release automation should consume the policy status (ACCEPT, REJECT, or UNRESOLVED), never an alignment fitness number alone.

# 11. Dissenting View

The recommendation may be too conservative and may impose a data-collection project before delivering value. Model S’s excellent holdout fitness, generalization, simplicity, runtime, and comprehension suggest that it captures the real process more robustly than C. The two apparent violations may be artifacts of the compact notation, event loss, or an intended exploratory use rather than actual release behavior. Model C’s poor holdout score may be a feature: if unseen combinations require explicit approval, rejecting them is exactly what governance wants. The proposed third model also introduces substantial complexity: POWL loops, source-conditioned variants, duplicate-label handling, and a separate policy checker can be harder to operate than either original. Missing source and affected-check attributes mean that many cases become UNRESOLVED, potentially creating a manual-review bottleneck. If the business cannot instrument those attributes reliably, a theoretically safer model may be less useful than S’s resilient operational coverage. A finite C-style whitelist may also be the correct control in a highly regulated, low-volume process.

I would change the decision if a seeded, cost-published negative-suite evaluation showed that S never accepts critical violations in the actual production classifier, or if policy owners explicitly amended P2/P4 to permit those behaviors. I would allow C for enforcement if the organization confirmed a closed-world approval policy and demonstrated that all legal combinations are intentionally enumerated. Evidence that source and affected-check attributes are unavailable permanently would favor a formally documented manual-exception workflow rather than claiming full automation.
