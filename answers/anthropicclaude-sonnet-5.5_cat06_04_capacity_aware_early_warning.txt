## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**Cool-down.** After a swarm, no repeat on the same case for at least 4h, or until a material event has occurred. Material events are a stage transition, new customer evidence, reassignment or scope change, or an SLA clock change.

**Suppression.** An alert is a duplicate if there is no material event since the last alert and p_adj < 0.10. Duplicates are logged, not re-scored, and the same alert is not surfaced again for 2h.

**Escalation.** A suppressed case escalates if any of the following occurs:
- p_adj rises by at least 0.10 on non-imputed features;
- the time-to-deadline drops below the feasibility gate while the case is still eligible;
- it hits a non-monetary obligation flag.

Escalation goes to a human reviewer, not straight to a slot.

**Re-entry.** A material event resets the case for re-evaluation. The rule then runs a fresh Step 0–4 with the new information. A swarm is not automatic.

**Waiting.** A "wait" decision expires at 60 minutes or at the next stage transition, whichever is first.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Value-based starving.** Today all three SME cases (I-05, I-07, I-10) are deprioritized: they are low value and their risk is underpredicted, a double disadvantage.
- Safeguard: a weekly floor so that at least one slot in five goes to the lowest-value tercile, using the best robust-positive NV within that tercile.
- Safeguard: an "aging credit" that raises a case's score by a set amount for every hour it is passed over while its p_adj stays above the alert band.
- Safeguard: lower-cost non-swarm actions (a senior review, an information request) are available to all deprioritized cases.
- Report intervention rates by value tier, holding risk band constant.

**NNL uncertainty.** I shrank the NNL effect by 30% but did not exclude these cases, because exclusion would perpetuate a data gap.
- Safeguard: NNL swarms are logged as "learning cases" with outcomes recorded, and use language-capable specialists.
- Safeguard: an NNL decision requires human sign-off until at least 100 validated cases exist.
- Safeguard: NNL cases join the randomized near-threshold pool (below).
- Safeguard: the interval is widened, not the point estimate inflated.

**Differential calibration.**
- Recalibrate per segment on a rolling window, and report segment-level calibration slope and intercept with intervals.
- If a segment's intercept error exceeds 0.05 over at least 50 cases and four weeks, update the shift.
- Never use one global threshold across segments.

**Selective intervention changing training labels.** Treated cases that avoid a breach look like "low-risk" cases in future data, which biases retraining.
- Safeguard: log the decision, its NV, the propensity and the treatment flag for every case.
- Safeguard: randomize 10–20% of near-threshold cases (|NV| < €1k) into treat/no-treat, stratified by segment.
- Safeguard: train uplift or doubly-robust models, not raw breach classifiers.
- Safeguard: include the intervention as a feature or exclude treated outcomes from "natural risk" training.

**Spillover falling on untreated groups.** Exposed cases in the diagnosis queue bear the cost of each swarm, and they may cluster in particular segments.
- Safeguard: cap swarms at two per rolling 4h per queue, with a congestion stop rule.
- Safeguard: track exposed cases' delay and breach rate by segment and value tier.
- Safeguard: give exposed cases compensating priority, such as a queue bump or reassignment, when their p_adj rises.
- Safeguard: charge spillover in NV, and re-estimate  from actual exposure data.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational**
- Slot usage, time from alert to swarm start, specialist utilization, and alert volume.
- Suppression rate and override rate.
- Feature latency and imputation rate (I-06 shows this matters).
- Review of any non-swarm actions triggered.

**Predictive**
- Rolling calibration by segment (intercept, slope, Brier score).
- Drift in the inputs, and the fraction of alerts based on imputed features.
- Alert precision and lead time.
- Calibration of the p_adj shifts against outcomes.

**Causal effect**
- Estimate ARR per segment from the randomized near-threshold pool, using a Bayesian hierarchical model with the supplied ranges as priors.
- Cross-check with difference-in-differences or matched controls, and with switchback or stepped windows where feasible.
- Track the realized ARR against ARR*. If the lower credible bound falls below break-even for a segment, tighten or pause swarms there.
- Compare wrong-reason alerts (model alerted, no real problem) by segment.

**Capacity**
- Measured queue delay per swarm (test against the 8–20 minute base and 15–30 minute adverse estimates).
- Breach rate of exposed cases versus matched unexposed cases (test against  = 0.01–0.03).
- Reserve-slot hit rate: how often it was needed for the regulatory case.
- Re-estimate V from the actual value of exposed cases.

**Fairness**
- Intervention rate, uplift and net benefit by segment and value tier.
- Burden of false alarms by segment.
- Breach rate conditional on risk band.
- Spillover exposure by segment.

**Detecting policy-induced drift.** Compare feature distributions and calibration between treated and untreated groups, and before and after policy launch. Run a shadow model trained on pre-policy data as a reference. Retrain with treatment indicators, and hold back a random slice for unbiased evaluation.

**When to change the rule**
- Recalibrate a segment's shift when its intercept is off by more than 0.05 across at least 50 cases.
- Update  or V when measured spillover falls outside 0.01–0.03.
- Update h (the effect haircuts) when realized ARR has credible intervals that exclude the current values.
- Tighten or relax the 1.25× margin and the reserve hurdle after a full review cycle (for example four weeks), with sign-off from operations and a fairness owner.
- Reduce the reserve when the regulatory incident hit rate is low, and increase it when high.
- Every change is logged and reviewed against the randomized pool, not against selectively treated outcomes.
