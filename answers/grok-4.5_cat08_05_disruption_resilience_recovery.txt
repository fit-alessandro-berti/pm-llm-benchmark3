# Cyber Incident Adaptation Analysis: Pharmaceutical Wholesaler

## 1. Disruption timeline

**T0 — Baseline (Day 30 to Day 1)**  
Operational state: Full WMS and order-to-delivery stack available. Dominant variant is the normative path Receive  Validate  Reserve  Pick  Pack  Quality/release  Dispatch  PoD  Invoice (fitness 0.96; 91% fitting traces). Exception loops are small (compliance hold ~700, backorder ~900, repack ~450). Risk posture: controlled. Traceability exceptions 0.8%, cold-chain doc missing 0.4%, dual-control missing 0.2%, rework 3.5%, cancellation 2.1%. Hospital-critical median 8h / p90 15h.

**T1 — Onset (Day 0 morning)**  
WMS unavailable after cyber incident. Inventory reservation, scanning, and electronic authorization stop. Process enters emergency mode; no stable variant yet. Risk posture flips from controlled to unknown within hours.

**T2 — Manual workaround (Day 0 afternoon–Day 2)**  
Operational state: Printed snapshots, phone confirmations, spreadsheet pick lists, manual courier calls. Dominant variants (from DFG): Emergency triage  Manual authorization check | Skip system authorization | Hospital-critical fast lane  Manual inventory snapshot  Manual pick list  Manual pack  Temporary release control | Dispatch without scan  Phone PoD  Spreadsheet invoice hold; plus Defer noncritical  Backlog. Fitness collapses to 0.71 (44% fitting). Move-on-log: skip auth, dispatch without scan, phone PoD, spreadsheet invoice. Move-on-model: missing quality/release, missing scan, missing PoD, missing temperature record.  
Metrics: 5,600 cases. Hospital-critical median 10h (fill rate held 91%) but Pharmacy-standard median 54h / fill 61%, Wholesale p90 180h. Traceability exceptions 9.5%, cold-chain missing 4.8%, dual-control missing 2.9%, rework 12.5%, cancel 7.8%. Risk posture: **mission-protective for hospitals, high concealed noncompliance elsewhere**, inventory snapshots aging by the hour.

**T3 — Partial recovery (Day 3–Day 6)**  
Order intake returns; inventory sync and scanning unreliable. Hybrid variants: electronic path with frequent reconcile-manual-reservation and elevated compliance holds. Fitness 0.82 (59% fitting). Metrics improve but remain elevated vs baseline: traceability 5.4%, cold-chain 2.3%, dual-control 1.4%, rework 9.0%, cancel 5.2%. Hospital-critical near-normal (median 9h); Pharmacy/Wholesale still stretched. Risk posture: **improving operations, residual control debt accumulating**.

**T4 — Stabilization (Day 7–Day 21)**  
Systems mostly restored. DFG shows return toward normative path plus new recovery-only activities: Backlog prioritization, Reconcile manual reservation, Proof-of-delivery remediation, Audit remediation (2,300 cases into audit tail). Fitness 0.90 (76% fitting)—still below baseline. Cycle times approach baseline for Hospital-critical; Pharmacy/Wholesale lag (p90 74h / 120h). Traceability 2.2%, cold-chain 0.9%, dual-control 0.5%, rework 5.8%, cancel 3.0%—all above baseline. **Operational recovery precedes compliance recovery**; audit-remediation queue is the hidden workload after cycle-time metrics look “recovered.”

## 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

**Distinction.**  
Resilience is an adaptation that **preserves mission outcomes while retaining a designed minimum of risk control**, with evidence that can be defended contemporaneously or within a defined capture window. Concealed noncompliance is an adaptation that **preserves or restores throughput/cycle-time appearance while dropping required controls**, leaving gaps that are invisible in operational dashboards until audit, recall, or regulator review. After-the-fact audit remediation (L) can reduce residual risk but is **not equivalent** to real-time compliance: custody, temperature, and dual-control are time-sensitive facts; reconstruction is partial and contestable.

**True resilience (keep / keep-with-structure)**  
- **A (with controls)**: Segment-aware triage that held Hospital-critical fill at 91% and median near baseline under collapse—mission-aligned.  
- **B**: Offline authorization by trained staff—control quality preserved under degraded tools.  
- **F (controlled-substance scope)**: Human dual-control substitute where system gates failed.  
- **I + J (policy-bound)**: Capacity rationing and backlog ordering by criticality—absorbs shock without pretending all demand is equal.  
- **K**: Explicit inventory truth repair before re-entering normal ATP—prevents silent corruption.  

These show up as protected critical service, bounded control loss on the highest-risk products, and deliberate recovery work in the DFG—not only faster averages.

**Risky shortcuts (retire or strictly fence)**  
- **C**: Skip system authorization on assumed standing auth—direct eligibility gap; major move-on-log deviation.  
- **G (bare)**: Dispatch without scan and without a hard manual custody record—largest traceability hole.  
- **D past freshness**: Snapshot picking after inventory drift—looks like flow, hides wrong-item/short-expiry risk.  
- **H for controlled products**: Phone PoD as sole evidence—insufficient chain-of-custody.  
- Treating **L** as proof that T2 was “compliant”—cycle time recovered faster than fitness and exception rates; 2,300 audit-tail cases prove hidden workload and deferred risk.

**Ambiguous / appetite-dependent**  
- **E**: Necessary to move product, but only resilient if reconstruction SLA and identifiers are mandatory; otherwise it manufactures scan debt (rework 12.5% in T2).  
- **F sampling on cold-chain**: Dual-control focus helped controlled drugs; cold-chain temp missing 4.8% shows partial design—acceptable only if temp logging is independently mandated.  
- **A without B**: Fast lane is resilient under explicit emergency auth rules; without them it collapses into C.  
- **I**: Resilient for hospital protection; harmful if Pharmacy-standard clinical needs are misclassified as “noncritical” without clinical input.  
- **J**: Resilient if priority rules are written, auditable, and fair; arbitrary prioritization is reputational and possibly regulatory risk.

**Conformance lens.** Fitness 0.960.710.820.90 and fitting traces 91%44%59%76% show the process never fully returned to baseline control density in the observation window. Throughput and hospital medians are therefore insufficient resilience indicators; control completeness and audit-tail age must co-define recovery.

## 4. Recovery process model

Redesigned **Degraded-Mode Order-to-Delivery (DM-OTD)** for future WMS / cyber degradations.

### 4.1 Trigger conditions (enter DM-OTD)
Enter when **any** hold for 30 minutes:  
1. WMS inventory reservation or scan services unavailable;  
2. Electronic authorization/compliance service unavailable;  
3. Cold-chain monitoring or controlled-substance logging path unavailable;  
4. Detection of integrity/cyber event affecting warehouse systems (even if partially up).  

**Severity levels**  
- **S1 Partial**: intake up, scan/inventory flaky (like T3)  
- **S2 Full WMS loss**: manual warehouse (like T2)  
- **S3 Auth/compliance service loss concurrent with S1/S2**

Exit DM-OTD only when: live reservation+scan stable for defined soak period, K backlog below threshold, and open L items risk-scored with owners—not when median cycle time alone normalizes.

### 4.2 Process model (BPMN-style structured text)

```
START
   Receive order (electronic or phone/fax intake)
   [Trigger active?] 
       No   Normative path (baseline)
       Yes  DM_TRIAGE

DM_TRIAGE
   Score order: CustomerCriticality × ProductRisk × ClinicalUrgency × Age
   Gateway: priority band
       P0 Hospital-critical
       P1 Pharmacy-standard (clinical / cold-chain / controlled)
       P2 Pharmacy-standard ambient
       P3 Wholesale-bulk / deferrable
   P3  DEFER_POLICY  (max age / cancel-or-renegotiate)  END or back to DM_TRIAGE
   P0–P2  MIN_CONTROLS_GATE

MIN_CONTROLS_GATE  (minimum controls by risk—cannot skip)
  Product/Customer matrix:
    Controlled-substance (any customer):
      MUST: Manual authorization (B) + two-person release (F full) 
            + manual custody dispatch log (G') + hard PoD artifact (H')
    Cold-chain (any customer):
      MUST: Auth (B or electronic) + temp device/logger ID recorded at pack
            + temp evidence at dispatch + PoD with receipt condition
    Recall-sensitive:
      MUST: Auth + lot capture on manual pick list + quarantine check
    Hospital-critical ambient:
      MUST: Auth (B; emergency waiver only with timed B backfill 4h)
            + lot/expiry on pick list + dispatch log
    Pharmacy ambient / Wholesale ambient:
      MUST: Auth via B or pre-registered standing-auth list with hash/version
            + pick list identifiers; no bare C
   Fail gate  HOLD (compliance) or DEFER
   Pass  INVENTORY_MODE

INVENTORY_MODE
   If snapshot age  T_fresh (e.g. 4h) AND SKU not high-drift:
       Manual inventory snapshot check (D)
    Else:
       Physical/bin count or phone count delta (D+)
   Manual reservation log (paper/spreadsheet with version, user, timestamp)
   Manual pick list (E) with: order ID, SKU, lot, expiry, qty, picker ID
   Manual pack
   RELEASE_PATH

RELEASE_PATH
   Controlled: Temporary release control full dual-control (F)
   Cold-chain: Temp evidence capture mandatory
   Other: Risk-based sample release (F') + exception escalate
   DISPATCH_PATH

DISPATCH_PATH
   Forbidden: bare Dispatch without scan (G)
   Required: Scan if available OR Manual Dispatch Record G':
       seal/tote ID, courier ID, vehicle, timestamp, dispatcher ID
       (+ second person for controlled)
   PoD_PATH

PoD_PATH
   Electronic PoD if available
   Else ambient: Phone PoD allowed with callback ID + time + counterparty name (H)
   Controlled / hospital cold-chain: photo/signature/e-sign artifact (H')—phone alone insufficient
   Invoice hold queue (spreadsheet OK) with evidence completeness flag
   END (operational) AND enqueue RECONCILE_AUDIT

RECONCILE_AUDIT  (parallel, first-class—not optional tail)
   K: Reconcile manual reservation vs restored WMS (block ATP on unresolved)
   Scan/lot reconstruction from pick lists
   PoD remediation
   L: Audit remediation prioritized by:
       controlled > cold-chain > hospital > recall-sensitive > others
   Regulatory threshold check (report if evidence cannot be restored)
   Invoice close only when evidence policy met OR waiver logged
   END
```

### 4.3 Backlog prioritization (J codified)
Score = w1·CustomerCriticality + w2·ProductRisk + w3·HoursWaiting + w4·ClinicalFlag  w5·DeferCount.  
Publish weights pre-incident. Recompute each shift. Cap P3 defer age; auto-escalate to P1 if clinical flag arrives.

### 4.4 Manual evidence capture standard
Every degraded-mode case carries an **Evidence Envelope**: auth method & actor; inventory source & snapshot age; lot/expiry; release actor(s); dispatch custody; temp logger IDs; PoD artifact type. Incomplete envelope  cannot leave RELEASE/DISPATCH without HOLD or documented emergency waiver with expiry.

### 4.5 Notation note
Above is a hierarchical process tree with XOR gateways by priority and product risk, AND-split between operational close and reconcile/audit. It can be implemented as BPMN with a conditional event subprocess “DM-OTD” interrupting the normative process on triggers.

## 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should **accept** deliberate service differentiation under disruption: hospital-critical protection (A), policy-bound deferral of bulk (I), slower Pharmacy/Wholesale cycle times, and a visible investment in manual authorization and dual-control capacity (B/F)—even if unit cost and median cycle time for noncritical segments worsen. It should **not accept** bare authorization skips (C), scanless dispatch without a hard custody record (G), phone-only PoD for controlled substances (H misuse), indefinite reliance on stale snapshots (D), or declaring recovery when cycle times normalize while traceability exceptions, fitness, and the audit-remediation queue remain materially above baseline (as in T4). After-the-fact remediation is necessary debt repayment, not a substitute for real-time minimum controls on controlled, cold-chain, and recall-sensitive product. **Evidence that would change this recommendation** includes: (i) regulator-accepted standing-order frameworks that make a narrow, logged ambient auth cache equivalent to B; (ii) independent offline scan/custody tech that drives dispatch-without-scan rate to zero with proven integrity under cyber assumptions; (iii) sustained post-exit metrics at baseline (traceability 0.8%, dual-control miss 0.2%, fitness 0.95, L-queue near empty) showing the playbook’s minimum controls are oversized; or (iv) clinical data showing Pharmacy-standard deferral caused patient-level harm comparable to hospital stockouts—in which case priority weights in J/I must be redesigned, not abandoned.
