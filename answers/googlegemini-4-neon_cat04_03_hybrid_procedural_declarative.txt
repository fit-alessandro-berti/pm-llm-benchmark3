# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

## Material Assumptions

To keep all five modeling layers internally coherent without hiding unstated interpretations, the implemented design rests on the following explicit assumptions:

1. **Scope of `Request Clarification` / `Receive Clarification` (R3):** Clarification cycles may occur zero, one, or more times at any point after `Receive Onboarding Request` and before the final onboarding decision (`Activate Vendor` or `Reject Vendor`). Every `Receive Clarification` corresponds to a preceding unmatched `Request Clarification`, and no clarification may remain open when `Activate Vendor` or `Reject Vendor` executes.
2. **Scope of `Sanctions Escalation` / `Obtain Legal Opinion` (R4, R5, R7):** `Sanctions Escalation` may occur zero, one, or more times only after `Run Sanctions Screening`. Every `Obtain Legal Opinion` answers a preceding `Sanctions Escalation`. In the implemented procedural model, any open escalation must be resolved by `Obtain Legal Opinion` prior to the final onboarding decision (`Activate Vendor` or `Reject Vendor`).
3. **Exclusive Risk Routing (Backbone #4, R2, R8):** After `Classify Risk`, a case follows exactly one of three mutually exclusive branches: (i) `Reject Vendor`, (ii) `Manager Approval` $\rightarrow$ `Activate Vendor`, or (iii) `Enhanced Due Diligence` $\rightarrow$ `Compliance Approval` $\rightarrow$ `Activate Vendor`. Because the event log carries no `risk_level` attribute, conformance checking verifies structural adherence to one of the three valid branches, not whether the chosen branch matched the vendor's true risk score.
4. **Post-Activation Bank Detail Changes (R11):** The activity `Change Bank Details` models a post-activation master-data mutation occurring after `Activate Vendor` and before `Release First Payment` (pre-activation bank corrections are absorbed into `Capture Master Data` / `Verify Bank Account`). Once `Change Bank Details` occurs, both `Suspend Vendor` and a fresh `Verify Bank Account` must occur before `Release First Payment`. Per Backbone #5 (`Close Onboarding` follows either rejection or first payment), a suspended vendor must be re-verified and paid before closing; a separate "terminate while suspended" exit path is not assumed unless explicitly added by policy.
5. **Interpretation of R6's 24-Hour Deadline:** R6 states that after emergency pre-opinion activation, *"the opinion must then arrive within 24 hours."* Because the wording does not specify whether the 24-hour clock starts at `Activate Vendor` or at `Sanctions Escalation`, the resource/data layer evaluates `Obtain Legal Opinion - Activate Vendor <= 24h` as the primary emergency-window check and additionally reports `Obtain Legal Opinion - Sanctions Escalation` for audit transparency. (In T6, both intervals are within 24 hours: 10 hours post-activation and 17 hours post-escalation.)
6. **Temporal Profile Semantics (R12):** Duration expectations are normative alerting thresholds (`warning`), never automatic control-flow rejections (`fail`).

## Resolving the R5 / R6 Policy Conflict

R5 (Legal: `Obtain Legal Opinion` must precede `Activate Vendor` under all circumstances after `Sanctions Escalation`) and R6 (Operations: emergency activation may precede `Obtain Legal Opinion` if jointly authorized outside the event log and followed by the opinion within 24 hours) are **directly contradictory** on any trace where `Sanctions Escalation` $\prec$ `Activate Vendor` $\prec$ `Obtain Legal Opinion` (such as T6). Moreover, R6's authorizing condition is **unobservable in the event log**.

Three coherent resolutions are possible:

1. **Resolution A — Legal Supremacy / Fail-Closed Default (Chosen for Implementation):**
   - Enforce R5 unconditionally in the automated control-flow layers (Accepting Petri Net and DECLARE). Any trace in which `Activate Vendor` precedes `Obtain Legal Opinion` after `Sanctions Escalation` fails control-flow conformance.
   - Model R6 in the Resource/Data-Aware Layer as a **waiver-eligibility diagnostic**: it checks the 24-hour post-activation SLA and checks for external authorization metadata (`case:emergency_override`, `case:joint_authorization_ref`). Because those attributes are absent from the log, the data layer returns `policy-dependent`, requiring offline attestation to clear the compliance breach.
2. **Resolution B — Permissive Control-Flow + Deferred Policy Adjudication (Fail-Open Control Flow):**
   - Relax the procedural and DECLARE models to enforce only R4 (`Sanctions Escalation` eventually followed by `Obtain Legal Opinion` before `Close Onboarding`), allowing `Activate Vendor` to precede `Obtain Legal Opinion`.
   - Delegate all R5/R6 enforcement to the Resource/Data-Aware Layer, which marks pre-opinion activations as `fail` if the opinion exceeds 24 hours or is explicitly unauthorized, and `policy-dependent` (manual review hold) when external authorization attributes are missing.
3. **Resolution C — Schema Instrumentation (Target-State Remediation):**
   - Prohibit R6 in production until the onboarding system emits verifiable event/case attributes (`case:emergency_override`, `case:joint_authorization_ref`, or a distinct activity `Emergency Activate Vendor`), after which R5 and R6 become non-contradictory conditional branches.

## Choice and Compliance Risk

**Implemented Choice:** **Resolution A (Legal Supremacy / Fail-Closed Default)**, paired with a recommendation to migrate toward Resolution C.

- **Rationale:** Sanctions compliance carries strict-liability regulatory and legal exposure. Because the event log cannot prove that Operations actually obtained joint authorization, silently accepting T6 as control-flow compliant would create an unmonitored bypass of sanctions controls.
- **Compliance and Operational Risk of Resolution A:** Resolution A minimizes regulatory exposure (zero silent sanctions bypasses) but introduces **false-positive friction** whenever legitimate, externally authorized emergency activations occur. If Operations frequently uses emergency activations without instrumenting the log, compliance teams may suffer alert fatigue and rubber-stamp overrides, eroding real governance. Conversely, had Resolution B been chosen, the risk would invert: downstream teams monitoring only procedural or DECLARE fitness would see T6 as `pass` and could miss unauthorized pre-opinion activations if manual-review queues are ignored.

# 3. Procedural Backbone

## 1. Compact Formal Description

We use a **1-safe, sound Accepting Petri Net** $N = (P, T, F, \ell, M_0, M_f)$ rather than a ProcessTree because two cross-cutting requirements—clarification cycles bounded by the final decision (R3) and sanctions escalation/opinion interleaved with `Classify Risk`, `Enhanced Due Diligence`, and `Compliance Approval` (R4, R5, R7)—are **non-block-structured** and cannot be expressed in a ProcessTree without either over-permissive flower loops or rigid placement approximations.

- **Initial & Final Markings:** $M_0 = \{p_{\text{start}}: 1\}$, $M_f = \{p_{\text{end}}: 1\}$.
- **Parallel Onboarding Checks:** `Receive Onboarding Request` forks $\{p_{\text{md\_todo}}, p_{\text{ss\_todo}}, p_{\text{vb\_todo}}\}$ for concurrent execution of `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, and `Verify Bank Account` (`t_verify_bank_init`), joined by `Classify Risk`.
- **Exclusive Primary Routing:** From $p_{\text{classified}}$, three mutually exclusive transitions fire:
  - Rejection: `Reject Vendor` $\rightarrow p_{\text{close\_ready}}$;
  - Low-risk acceptance: `Manager Approval` $\rightarrow p_{\text{approved}}$;
  - High-risk acceptance: `Enhanced Due Diligence` $\rightarrow p_{\text{edd\_done}} \rightarrow$ `Compliance Approval` $\rightarrow p_{\text{approved}}$.
- **Decision-Bounded Clarification Gate (R3):** `Receive Onboarding Request` also marks $p_{\text{clar\_idle}}$. `Request Clarification` moves the token to $p_{\text{clar\_open}}$; `Receive Clarification` returns it to $p_{\text{clar\_idle}}$. Both `Activate Vendor` and `Reject Vendor` consume $p_{\text{clar\_idle}}$, guaranteeing strict alternation and blocking final outcomes while a clarification is unanswered.
- **Screening-Unlocked Escalation Gate (R4, R5, R7 — Resolution A):** `Run Sanctions Screening` marks $p_{\text{ss\_done}}$ and $p_{\text{esc\_idle}}$. `Sanctions Escalation` moves the token to $p_{\text{esc\_pending}}$; `Obtain Legal Opinion` returns it to $p_{\text{esc\_idle}}$. Both `Activate Vendor` and `Reject Vendor` consume $p_{\text{esc\_idle}}$, enforcing R7 (only after screening), R4 (every escalation gets an opinion), and R5 (opinion precedes activation) while permitting arbitrary interleaving with `Classify Risk`, `Enhanced Due Diligence`, and `Compliance Approval` (as in T7).
- **Post-Activation Bank-Change Loop (R11) & Closure:** `Activate Vendor` marks $p_{\text{activated}}$, enabling either `Release First Payment` $\rightarrow p_{\text{close\_ready}} \rightarrow$ `Close Onboarding` $\rightarrow p_{\text{end}}$, or `Change Bank Details`, which consumes $p_{\text{activated}}$ and forks $p_{\text{need\_suspend}}$ (`Suspend Vendor`) and $p_{\text{need\_reverify}}$ (duplicate-labeled `Verify Bank Account` transition `t_verify_bank_post`). A silent join transition $\tau_{\text{r11}}$ restores $p_{\text{activated}}$ only after both complete.

```python
        ("p_md_done", "t_classify"),
        ("p_ss_done", "t_classify"),
        ("p_vb_done", "t_classify"),
        ("t_classify", "p_classified"),

        ("p_classified", "t_reject"),
        ("p_clar_idle", "t_reject"),
        ("p_esc_idle", "t_reject"),
        ("t_reject", "p_close_ready"),

        ("p_classified", "t_mgr_approve"),
        ("t_mgr_approve", "p_approved"),

        ("p_classified", "t_edd"),
        ("t_edd", "p_edd_done"),
        ("p_edd_done", "t_comp_approve"),
        ("t_comp_approve", "p_approved"),

        # Activation requires approval, no open clarification, and no open escalation
        ("p_approved", "t_activate"),
        ("p_clar_idle", "t_activate"),
        ("p_esc_idle", "t_activate"),
        ("t_activate", "p_activated"),

        # Post-activation bank-detail change recovery loop (R11)
        ("p_activated", "t_change_bank"),
        ("t_change_bank", "p_need_suspend"),
        ("t_change_bank", "p_need_reverify"),
        ("p_need_suspend", "t_suspend"),
        ("t_suspend", "p_suspended_done"),
        ("p_need_reverify", "t_verify_bank_post"),
        ("t_verify_bank_post", "p_reverify_done"),
        ("p_suspended_done", "t_tau_r11_rejoin"),
        ("p_reverify_done", "t_tau_r11_rejoin"),
        ("t_tau_r11_rejoin", "p_activated"),

        # Payment and closure
        ("p_activated", "t_release_payment"),
        ("t_release_payment", "p_close_ready"),
        ("p_close_ready", "t_close"),
        ("t_close", "p_end"),
    ]

    nodes = {**places, **transitions}
    for src, dst in arcs:
        petri_utils.add_arc_from_to(nodes[src], nodes[dst], net)

    initial_marking = Marking({places["p_start"]: 1})
    final_marking = Marking({places["p_end"]: 1})
    return net, initial_marking, final_marking


procedural_model = build_procedural_model()
```

## 3. Structural Explanation and Scope Boundaries

- **Concurrency:** `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, and `Verify Bank Account` execute in any interleaving after `Receive Onboarding Request`, synchronizing at `Classify Risk`. Likewise, within R11, `Suspend Vendor` and the re-verification `Verify Bank Account` execute concurrently after `Change Bank Details`.
- **Choices:** `p_classified` implements a strict deferred/exclusive choice among `Reject Vendor`, `Manager Approval`, and `Enhanced Due Diligence`. Because `p_classified` holds one token, R1, R2, and R8 are structurally guaranteed.
- **Clarification Loops:** Modeled via `p_clar_idle`/`p_clar_open` concurrently with checks, classification, and approval. This avoids forcing clarifications into a single fixed slot (such as only before `Classify Risk`) while preventing unclosed clarifications from reaching `Activate Vendor` or `Reject Vendor` (catching T5).
- **Terminal Outcomes:** Both `Reject Vendor` and `Release First Payment` converge into `p_close_ready`, ensuring `Close Onboarding` terminates every valid case cleanly with zero leftover tokens.
- **Included vs. Excluded Post-Activation Behavior:** R11's bank-detail change recovery loop is **deliberately included** because its stateful re-verification requirement cannot be enforced in DECLARE or Log Skeleton. Conversely, post-activation `Obtain Legal Opinion` (R6) is **deliberately excluded** under Resolution A (enforcing R5 fail-closed), and permanent closure of a suspended vendor without `Release First Payment` is excluded per Backbone #5.

## Rule Semantics and Key Template Distinctions

| Template | Key(s) | Direction & Semantics | Normative Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| `init` | `"Receive Onboarding Request"` | First event of every case must be `Receive Onboarding Request`. | Backbone #1 |
| `exactly_one` | `Receive Onboarding Request`, `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, `Classify Risk`, `Close Onboarding` | Each occurs strictly once per case (`count == 1`). `Verify Bank Account` is omitted because R11 permits `count >= 2`. | Backbone #1–#3, #5 |
| `existence` | `"Verify Bank Account"` | Occurs at least once (`count >= 1`), preserving compatibility with R11 re-verification. | Backbone #2–#3, R11 |
| `noncoexistence` | `(Reject, Activate)`, `(Reject, Manager)`, `(Reject, Compliance)`, `(Reject, EDD)`, `(Reject, Payment)`, `(Manager, Compliance)`, `(EDD, Manager)` | **Symmetric:** the two activities may never appear together in the same case in *any* order. | R1, R2, R8, Backbone #4–#5 |
| `altresponse` | `("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification")` | **Forward (`src -> tgt`):** every `Request Clarification` must be followed by `Receive Clarification` *before* another `Request Clarification` occurs. | R3 |
| `altprecedence` | `("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification")` | **Backward (`src -> tgt`):** every `Receive Clarification` must be preceded by `Request Clarification` *without* another `Receive Clarification` in between. | R3 |
| `response` | `(Escalation, Opinion)`, `(Manager, Activate)`, `(Compliance, Activate)`, `(Change Bank, Suspend)`, `(Change Bank, Verify Bank)` | **Forward (`src -> tgt`):** whenever `src` occurs, `tgt` must eventually occur later in the case. | R4, Backbone #5, R11 |
| `precedence` | `(Capture/Screening/Verify, Classify)`, `(Screening, Escalation)`, `(Escalation, Opinion)`, `(Classify, Reject/Manager/EDD)`, `(Activate, Change Bank)` | **Backward (`src -> tgt`):** `tgt` may occur only if `src` occurred earlier; `src` occurring does *not* force `tgt` to occur. | Backbone #3–#4, R7, R11 |
| `succession` | `(EDD, Compliance)`, `(Activate, Payment)` | **Bidirectional (`response` + `precedence`):** `src` must eventually be followed by `tgt`, and `tgt` may occur only after `src`. | Backbone #4–#5 |
| `nonsuccession` | `(Activate, Opinion)`, `(Activate, ReqClar/RecClar)`, `(Reject, ReqClar/RecClar)` | **Directed negative (`src -/-> tgt`):** `tgt` may never occur *after* `src` (unlike `noncoexistence`, `tgt` *is* allowed *before* `src`). | R5 (Resolution A), R3 |

**Critical Template Distinctions:**

- **`response` vs. `precedence`:** `response(A, B)` is triggered by `A` (requiring `B` later), whereas `precedence(A, B)` is triggered by `B` (requiring `A` earlier). For R7, `precedence("Run Sanctions Screening", "Sanctions Escalation")` is correct because screening does not force escalation, whereas escalation requires prior screening. Conversely, for R4, `response("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion")` enforces that escalation demands a later opinion.
- **`altresponse` vs. `chainresponse`:** `chainresponse` would require `Receive Clarification` to be the *immediately next* event after `Request Clarification`, which breaks whenever concurrent checks (`Capture Master Data`, `Verify Bank Account`) or `Classify Risk` interleave between request and reply. `altresponse` permits arbitrary interleaving while still forbidding two consecutive `Request Clarification` events without an intervening `Receive Clarification`. Likewise, `succession` (not `chainsuccession`) is used for `("Enhanced Due Diligence", "Compliance Approval")` so `Obtain Legal Opinion` may interleave (T7).
- **`noncoexistence` vs. `nonsuccession`:** `noncoexistence(A, B)` forbids `A` and `B` from appearing in the same trace in *any* order (correct for R1 `Reject Vendor` vs. `Activate Vendor`). `nonsuccession("Activate Vendor", "Obtain Legal Opinion")` allows `Obtain Legal Opinion` *before* `Activate Vendor` (passing T7) while forbidding it *after* `Activate Vendor` (failing T6). Paired with `response("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion")`, it encodes R5 without falsely failing non-escalated cases (T1, T2).
- **PM4Py DECLARE Limitations:** PM4Py's template inventory has no `absence2` / at-most-once template (delegated to Log Skeleton `activ_freq` and the Petri net for R10) and no disjunctive precedence (delegated to the Petri net for R2's mandatory approval before activation).

## Pair Orientation, Overlaps, and Disagreements

- **Critical Orientation Inversion (`always_before` vs. DECLARE `precedence`):**
  - In **Log Skeleton `always_after`**, `(A, B)` means *"after `A`, `B` always occurs"* — matching DECLARE `response(A, B)`.
  - In **Log Skeleton `always_before`**, `(A, B)` means *"before `A`, `B` always occurs"* (`A` is the later anchor, `B` is the earlier prerequisite) — **the exact reverse** of DECLARE `precedence(B, A)`. For example, R7 is `("Run Sanctions Screening", "Sanctions Escalation")` in DECLARE `precedence`, but `("Sanctions Escalation", "Run Sanctions Screening")` in Log Skeleton `always_before`.
  - Both `equivalence` and `never_together` are symmetric relations (one canonical pair per unordered relation is shown; PM4Py checks exact count equality and co-occurrence per pair).
- **Unique Strength (R10):** `activ_freq` enforces `{0, 1}` bounds for `Activate Vendor`, `Reject Vendor`, and `Release First Payment`, filling PM4Py DECLARE's lack of an at-most-once template. Activities subject to unbounded loops (`Request Clarification`, `Receive Clarification`, `Sanctions Escalation`, `Obtain Legal Opinion`, `Change Bank Details`, `Suspend Vendor`, and `Verify Bank Account` under R11) are deliberately omitted from `activ_freq` so valid multi-cycle traces are not falsely rejected.
- **Disagreement with Procedural & DECLARE on R5 (T6):** Log Skeleton **cannot express R5**. Adding `("Activate Vendor", "Obtain Legal Opinion")` to `always_before` would break non-escalated accepted cases (T1, T2); adding `("Obtain Legal Opinion", "Activate Vendor")` to `always_after` would break escalated cases that end in `Reject Vendor`; adding the pair to `never_together` would break T7. Because Log Skeleton has no `never_after` relation, **T6 passes Log Skeleton even under Resolution A**.
- **R11 Adjustments:** `("Verify Bank Account", "Classify Risk")` is deliberately excluded from `always_after` and `equivalence` because a post-activation re-verification occurs *after* `Classify Risk`.
- **Empty `directly_follows`:** Concurrency among checks, clarification interleaving, legal-opinion interleaving between `Enhanced Due Diligence` and `Compliance Approval` (T7), and post-activation R11 loops mean no activity pair is universally adjacent across all valid traces.

## Calibration and Warning-Only Semantics

With $\zeta = 2.0$, PM4Py flags a pair $(A, B)$ with observed elapsed time $\Delta t$ when $z = |\Delta t - \mu| / \sigma > 2.0$. Because $\mu = 2\sigma$ in all three normative pairs, the lower bound $\mu - 2\sigma = 0\text{s}$ never penalizes fast positive turnarounds (for example, T7's 2-hour activation-to-payment interval yields $z = |2 - 72|/36 = 1.94 \le 2.0$, remaining within bounds), while upper-bound warnings fire strictly above $96\text{h}$ (clarification), $24\text{h}$ (legal opinion — triggering T7's $34\text{h}$, $z = 3.67$), and $144\text{h}$ (first payment).

**Why temporal deviations are warnings, not automatic control-flow violations:**

1. **Statistical Norm vs. Legal Validity:** A late legal opinion (T7: 34h vs. 12h mean) or slow vendor reply does not invalidate the legality of the onboarding sequence; auto-rejecting a case whose legal opinion legitimately took 34 hours due to complex sanctions research would halt valid business operations.
2. **Noise in Elapsed-Time Measurements:** Event timestamps omit business calendars (weekends, public holidays), timezone boundaries, and waiting-vs-processing distinctions.
3. **Separation from Hard SLAs:** Where time *is* a hard compliance rule (R6's 24-hour emergency legal-opinion limit), it is enforced explicitly in the Resource/Data-Aware Layer rather than conflated with statistical $z$-score profiling.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

Activity-only formalisms cannot evaluate actor identity (`org:resource`), out-of-log authorization flags (`case:emergency_override`, `case:joint_authorization_ref`), or hard conditional SLA cutoffs.

## Executable-Style Validation Code

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py


def _clean_resources(sub_df: pd.DataFrame, res_col: str) -> tuple[set[str], bool]:
    """Return (non-empty resource set, has_missing_resource)."""
    if res_col not in sub_df.columns:
        return set(), len(sub_df) > 0
    vals = sub_df[res_col]
    missing = vals.isna() | vals.astype(str).str.strip().eq("")
    valid = set(vals[~missing].astype(str).str.strip())
    return valid, bool(missing.any())


def evaluate_resource_and_data_layer(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    policy_mode: str = "resolution_a",  # "resolution_a" (R5 strict) or "resolution_b" (R6 deferred)
    case_col: str = "case:concept:name",
    act_col: str = "concept:name",
    res_col: str = "org:resource",
    ts_col: str = "time:timestamp",
    emerg_col: str = "case:emergency_override",
    auth_col: str = "case:joint_authorization_ref",
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Evaluate R9a, R9b, and the R5/R6 emergency-activation policy per case.

    Note on pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, act1, act2):
    We intentionally perform explicit set-disjointness checks rather than relying
    solely on pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle, because PM4Py's LTL four-eyes
    filter (1) checks existential non-equality across pairs (allowing a repeated
    Verify Bank Account under R11 by a second actor to mask a same-actor breach),
    (2) drops cases where an optional branch activity did not occur, and
    (3) can treat missing NaN resources as unequal.
    """
    records = []

    for case_id, grp in df.sort_values(ts_col).groupby(case_col, sort=False):
        verdicts = []
        findings = []

        # --- R9a: Verify Bank Account vs. {Manager Approval, Compliance Approval} ---
        vb_df = grp[grp[act_col] == "Verify Bank Account"]
        app_df = grp[grp[act_col].isin(["Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval"])]
        if not vb_df.empty and not app_df.empty:
            vb_res, vb_miss = _clean_resources(vb_df, res_col)
            app_res, app_miss = _clean_resources(app_df, res_col)
            if vb_miss or app_miss:
                verdicts.append("fail")
                findings.append("R9a: missing org:resource on Verify Bank Account or approval.")
            elif vb_res & app_res:
                verdicts.append("fail")
                findings.append(f"R9a violation: shared actor(s) {sorted(vb_res & app_res)}.")

        # --- R9b: Capture Master Data vs. Activate Vendor ---
        md_df = grp[grp[act_col] == "Capture Master Data"]
        act_df = grp[grp[act_col] == "Activate Vendor"]
        if not md_df.empty and not act_df.empty:
            md_res, md_miss = _clean_resources(md_df, res_col)
            act_res, act_miss = _clean_resources(act_df, res_col)
            if md_miss or act_miss:
                verdicts.append("fail")
                findings.append("R9b: missing org:resource on Capture Master Data or Activate Vendor.")
            elif md_res & act_res:
                verdicts.append("fail")
                findings.append(f"R9b violation: shared actor(s) {sorted(md_res & act_res)}.")

        # --- R5 / R6: Emergency Pre-Opinion Activation & 24-Hour Hard SLA ---
        esc_df = grp[grp[act_col] == "Sanctions Escalation"]
        op_df = grp[grp[act_col] == "Obtain Legal Opinion"]
        if not esc_df.empty and not act_df.empty:
            if act_df[ts_col].isna().any() or esc_df[ts_col].isna().any() or op_df[ts_col].isna().any():
                verdicts.append("fail")
                findings.append("R5/R6: missing timestamp prevents ordering/SLA verification.")
            else:
                act_ts = act_df[ts_col].iloc[0]
                esc_ts = esc_df[ts_col].iloc[0]
                if op_df.empty:
                    verdicts.append("fail")
                    findings.append("R4/R5/R6 violation: Obtain Legal Opinion never occurred after escalation.")
                else:
                    op_ts = op_df[ts_col].iloc[-1]
                    if op_ts > act_ts:
                        hours_post_act = (op_ts - act_ts).total_seconds() / 3600.0
                        hours_post_esc = (op_ts - esc_ts).total_seconds() / 3600.0
                        if hours_post_act > 24.0:
                            verdicts.append("fail")
                            findings.append(
                                f"R5/R6 violation: opinion arrived {hours_post_act:.1f}h after activation (>24h)."
                            )
                        else:
                            has_emerg = emerg_col in grp.columns and grp[emerg_col].notna().any()
                            has_auth = auth_col in grp.columns and grp[auth_col].notna().any()
                            if not (has_emerg and has_auth):
                                verdicts.append("policy-dependent")
                                findings.append(
                                    f"R5/R6 conflict: Activate Vendor preceded Obtain Legal Opinion "
```

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

1. **Duplicated Enforcement Across Layers:** Requirements R1, R2, R4, R7, and R8 are enforced across the Accepting Petri Net, DECLARE, and Log Skeleton simultaneously. While useful for defense-in-depth and fast screening, duplication creates **policy-drift risk**: for instance, switching from Resolution A to Resolution B requires synchronized edits to both the Petri net (`p_esc_idle` arc on `t_activate`) and DECLARE (`nonsuccession`). Without a single authoritative source per requirement, layers will emit contradictory verdicts on identical traces.
2. **Inconsistencies Under Loops and Post-Activation Changes (R11 & Interleaving):** Constraints that appear sound on happy-path traces become inconsistent once loops or R11 are admitted:
   - Adding `Verify Bank Account: {1}` to `activ_freq`, or adding `("Verify Bank Account", "Classify Risk")` to `equivalence` or `always_after`, silently breaks valid R11 traces containing a second post-activation bank verification.
   - Adding `chainresponse` or `directly_follows` between `Enhanced Due Diligence` and `Compliance Approval` breaks T7 (where `Obtain Legal Opinion` interleaves).
   - Standard DECLARE `precedence("Verify Bank Account", "Release First Payment")` and Log Skeleton `always_before` suffer from **stale satisfaction** under R11: the initial pre-classification verification satisfies them even if no fresh verification follows `Change Bank Details`.
3. **Vacuity in Declarative and Temporal Rules:** Declarative constraints pass *vacuously* whenever their activation event never occurs. In T3 (rejected), nearly all approval, activation, escalation, and R11 rules pass vacuously, and zero temporal pairs execute; in T5, the temporal profile reports `pass` despite an unanswered `Request Clarification` because incomplete pairs are never scored. Aggregating raw pass rates across vacuous rules dramatically inflates apparent compliance.
4. **Missing Case and Event Attributes:**
   - **R6 Blind Spot:** Without `case:emergency_override` and `case:joint_authorization_ref`, no automated checker can distinguish a duly authorized emergency activation from an unauthorized sanctions bypass (T6).
   - **Risk-Tier Blind Spot:** Because `Classify Risk` carries no logged `risk_tier` attribute (`low`/`high`/`reject`), none of the five layers can detect a high-risk vendor improperly routed through `Manager Approval` instead of `Enhanced Due Diligence` $\rightarrow$ `Compliance Approval`.
   - **Missing `org:resource`:** Naive equality filters (`res1 != res2`) treat missing `NaN` values as distinct actors, silently passing SoD violations unless explicitly guarded fail-closed.
5. **Timestamp-Only Limitations:** Atomic event timestamps record only completion instants, not `start`/`complete` lifecycle intervals. Consequently, R12 cannot distinguish active review delays from external vendor waiting time, ignores weekends/holidays and business hours, and is vulnerable to clock skew, batch-job timestamp rounding, and timezone misconfigurations.
6. **Treating `support` / `confidence` Metadata as Normative Truth:** In PM4Py DECLARE, `support` and `confidence` originate from empirical log discovery, not legal authority. Hand-authored normative dictionaries populate `support=1.0, confidence=1.0` purely as structural placeholders. Confusing empirical metrics with normative truth is dangerous in both directions: rare but legally mandatory rules (such as R11 bank-change suspension or R4/R5 escalation controls) exhibit low empirical support in real logs, whereas widespread non-compliant habits (such as repeated SoD overrides in an understaffed branch) can exhibit high empirical support and confidence.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

## 1. Canonical Procedural Representation & Authoritative Policy Layers

- **Canonical Procedural Model:** Maintain the **Accepting Petri Net** (`procedural_model`) as the canonical executable control-flow specification (version-controlled in code/PNML and rendered to BPMN for business stakeholders) because it cleanly represents concurrency, exclusive risk routing, decision-bounded clarifications, and R11's stateful post-activation re-verification loop without non-block-structured approximations.
- **Authoritative Layer Assignment (Single Source of Truth per Policy Class):**
  - *Lifecycle & Stateful Loops (Backbone, R2 existence, R11):* Accepting Petri Net.
  - *Cross-Cutting Ordering & Exclusion Policies (R1, R3, R4, R5, R7, R8):* DECLARE (governed by Compliance, with explicit policy-version flag for Resolution A vs. B).
  - *Cardinality Caps & Fast Structural Filtering (R10):* Log Skeleton.
  - *Actor Segregation & Conditional SLA/Override Governance (R6, R9):* Resource/Data-Aware Policy Engine.
  - *Soft Operational Timing Monitoring (R12):* Temporal Profile (`zeta = 2.0`).

## 2. Recommended Execution Order

Run conformance checks as a tiered, short-circuit-aware (yet audit-complete) pipeline:

1. **Stage 1 — Schema & Attribute Completeness Gate:** Verify required columns (`case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, `time:timestamp`, `org:resource`) and flag missing actor/timestamp/override metadata immediately.
2. **Stage 2 — Fast Log-Skeleton Pre-Screening:** Linear-time cardinality (`activ_freq`), `never_together`, and global ordering screening to catch blatant structural defects (e.g., T5, T8) cheaply at high volume.
3. **Stage 3 — Resource/Data-Aware Policy Engine:** Evaluate R9a/R9b SoD and R5/R6 hard 24h SLA + authorization attributes (catching T4 and isolating T6's missing override evidence).
4. **Stage 4 — DECLARE Evaluation:** Evaluate all cross-cutting declarative policies (`altresponse`, `nonsuccession`, `precedence`, `noncoexistence`), producing human-readable rule-level diagnostics (catching T5, T6, T8).
5. **Stage 5 — Accepting Petri Net Alignments:** Compute optimal alignments on `procedural_model` (especially for traces with R11 bank changes or complex interleavings) to pinpoint exact earliest move-on-log / move-on-model deviations.
6. **Stage 6 — Temporal Profile Scoring:** Compute $z$-scores against `temporal_profile` (`zeta = 2.0`) to emit non-blocking `warning` alerts (e.g., T7) and feed operational SLA dashboards.

## 3. Surfacing Contradictory Findings to Users

Never collapse multi-layer results into a single opaque Boolean without provenance. Instead, emit a **structured case compliance dossier** with four distinct severity lanes:

- **Hard Control-Flow / SoD Blockers (`fail`):** Highlight the earliest decisive violation and offending layer(s) (e.g., T4: Resource/Data R9a; T5: R3 across Procedural/DECLARE/Log Skeleton; T8: R1/R2).
- **Governance / Policy-Conflict Holds (`policy-dependent`):** Whenever layers disagree due to the R5/R6 conflict or missing external evidence (e.g., T6 failing Procedural/DECLARE under Resolution A while passing Log Skeleton/Temporal and returning `policy-dependent` in the Data Layer), surface an explicit **"Policy Conflict — External Attestation Required"** banner showing both the R5 breach and the R6 24h eligibility calculation, routing the case to a joint Legal/Operations Waiver Queue.
- **Soft Timing Anomalies (`warning`):** Surface Temporal Profile deviations (e.g., T7's 34h legal opinion, $z=3.67$) separately from compliance failures so operations managers investigate bottlenecks without wrongly invalidating compliant cases.
- **Vacuity & Coverage Indicators (`not evaluated` / vacuous pass):** Explicitly report how many rules were *activated* versus *vacuously satisfied* per case so auditors can distinguish genuinely verified controls from inactive branches. Finally, prioritize **Resolution C** (instrumenting `case:emergency_override`, `case:joint_authorization_ref`, and `risk_tier` in the source system) to eliminate the R5/R6 observability gap permanently.
