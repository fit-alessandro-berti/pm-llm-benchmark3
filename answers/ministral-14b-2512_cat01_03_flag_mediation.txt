## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk**: If `U_reviewer_perception` perfectly predicts risk, suppressing the flag (P1) could worsen outcomes despite faster cycle times.
2. **Queue interference**: If protected capacity (P3) is overwhelmed by unobserved high-risk cases, delays may persist.
3. **Decision quality**: If flagged cases require deeper review for compliance (e.g., legal), P2’s faster cycle time could increase errors.
4. **Timestamp semantics**: If `triage_start` includes pre-processing time, the 12m vs. 120m contrast underestimates the true triage effect.
5. **Positivity**: If all high-risk cases are flagged, P1’s effect cannot be estimated for the highest-risk subgroup.
6. **Behavior change**: If reviewers in P3 game the system by assigning low-risk cases to protected lanes, capacity spillover increases.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed**:
- Flagged cases have **faster triage** (12m vs. 120m) due to priority lanes.
- They experience **longer cycle times** (1200m vs. 372m) driven by **mandatory manual review** and **shared queue delays**.
- **Escalation** occurs only for flagged cases, further extending cycle time.

**Plausible mechanism**:
The flag acts as a **policy bundle**: it (1) triggers priority triage (direct effect), (2) mandates manual review (mediated via occurrence), and (3) induces queue interference (mediated via waiting). The **longer cycle time** is primarily a **mediated effect** of manual review occurrence and shared queues, not a direct consequence of the flag itself.

**Unidentified**:
- The **causal effect of the flag on decision quality** (e.g., does deeper review improve outcomes?).
- The **true underlying risk** not captured by `risk_score` or `complexity`.
- **Reviewer behavior changes** in response to the flag (e.g., risk compensation).

**Next evidence**:
**Policy P3 (protected manual-review capacity)** is the most actionable pilot because it targets the **queue interference** pathway while preserving the flag’s intended signal. A randomized encouragement design (P1 vs. P0) would test whether the flag’s **direct effect on triage** persists when hidden from reviewers. **Minimum viable data**: timestamps for all stages, `risk_score`, `complexity`, and reviewer identity.
