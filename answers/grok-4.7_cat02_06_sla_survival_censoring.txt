# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

**Time origin.** For every case, \(t=0\) is `Submit`. **Assumption A1:** `duration_or_age_h` is elapsed hours on that same clock. The SLA horizon is the fixed time \(\tau=72\). No business-hours calendar is supplied, so the clock is elapsed time as recorded.

**Clock and information at the cutoff.** The extract is one frozen observation. Terminal rows (`resolved`, `rejected`, `withdrawn`, `transferred`) record time from `Submit` to that terminal activity. `open` rows are still internal and unresolved; their recorded time is age at the cutoff, which is an administrative censoring time for any event not yet seen. `last_event_gap_h` is 0 on every terminal row, because the terminal activity is the last event. On open rows it is the time since the last prefix activity. **Assumption A2:** reject, withdraw, and, under the primary transfer rule below, transfer are absorbing and block later internal `Resolve`. **Assumption A3:** priority is fixed at `Submit`. **Assumption A4:** these 32 cases are the analysis population. Extending the figures to future inflow is a separate claim.

All 32 recorded times are distinct integer hours, so the life table below has no ties.

**Two layers of outcome must stay separate.** A *binary status at 72 hours* is known for every case whose follow-up reaches 72 or whose absorbing outcome occurs earlier. A *continuous time to resolution* remains right-censored for cases still open, including those already older than 72. An open case at age 92 is a fully observed failure of “resolved by 72” and, at the same time, a censored observation for “when, if ever, will `Resolve` occur?”

### Estimands

**E1. Probability of internal resolution by 72 hours.**  
\[
\theta_{\mathrm{res}}(72)=P(T_{\mathrm{res}}\le 72),
\]  
where \(T_{\mathrm{res}}=\infty\) if the case is absorbed by reject, withdraw, or transfer and never resolves internally. The natural estimator is the Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence of `Resolve`. Its complement, \(1-\theta_{\mathrm{res}}(72)\), is the probability of not being internally resolved by the SLA.

**E2. Probability of still having no terminal internal disposition at 72 hours.**  
\[
S_{\mathrm{open}}(72)=P(T_{\mathrm{term}}>72),
\]  
the probability of remaining inside the process with no `Resolve`, `Reject`, `Withdraw`, or `Transfer` yet. This is the workload estimand: share of cases still on the internal desk when the SLA clock matures. Every late resolution travels through this state. E2 is smaller than \(1-\theta_{\mathrm{res}}(72)\), because competing exits are not still open and are also not resolutions.

**E3. Cause-specific, competing-event-removed probability of resolution by 72.**  
\[
1-S_{\mathrm{cs}}(72),
\]  
the Kaplan–Meier quantity obtained by censoring reject, withdraw, transfer, and open rows. This is the resolution probability in a hypothetical process where those other exits do not happen, and only under an independence assumption. It is the estimand that answers “how fast is the resolution mechanism when we keep every other exit in the risk set as if it were an uninformative dropout?” It is not the share of submitted cases that the operation actually resolves.

**E4. On-time compliant disposition, clock stopped at handoff.**  
A governance variant: transfer at \(t\le 72\) counts as clearing the internal clock, while transfer after 72 does not. This is a different event from `Resolve`. It is reported only as a sensitivity estimand.

**Which decision uses which estimand.**

| Decision | Estimand |
|---|---|
| SLA success: did this submission reach internal `Resolve` within 72h? | E1 |
| Backlog at the deadline: how much work is still internal and undisposed? | E2 |
| Staffing the resolution path while treating other exits as removals | E3, and only with the independence caveat stated |
| “Did we hand the case off or finish it before the clock?” | E4 |
| Alert on a live case | A landmark residual version of E1 given the state at a fixed age, which this extract cannot fit (no intermediate timestamps) |

**Primary estimand for SLA tail risk is E1, with E2 reported beside it.** E1 matches the problem’s definition that operational success is `Resolve`. E2 stops leadership from reading every non-resolution as a case that is still occupying the team. E3 is a diagnostic, not the SLA number. E4 is a policy choice about whether handoff is success.

### How four concrete cases contribute

Follow-up through 72 hours is complete for the first, second, and fourth of these situations when the terminal time or the open age is past 72. Only a transfer *before* 72 makes the 72-hour resolution status assumption-dependent.

**Resolved at 76 hours (S03, cohort A, P2, escalated, rework 0).**  
Internal resolution is observed at 76, so at 72 the case was still unresolved and still without a terminal disposition. Under E1 it is a failure of on-time resolution and it enters the resolution incidence at 76, not at 72. Under E2 it is still open at 72 and leaves to `Resolve` at 76. Under E3 it is a resolution event at 76. The breach of the binary SLA is already observed. The final prefix shows `Escalate` and a second `Review`, but the stage occupied *at hour 72* is not identified, because only the total time and the order are known.

**Open at 92 hours (S09, cohort A, P3, prefix `Submit>Screen>Review`, gap 31).**  
Age \(92>72\) and the case is still unresolved, so E1 is an observed failure. It stays in the resolution risk set through 72 and is censored at 92 for the continuous resolution time. E2 is also an observed “still open.” Nothing about reject, withdraw, or transfer has happened. The 31-hour gap supports a *hypothesis* that the residual resolution hazard is lower than that of recently touched cases; that hypothesis changes extrapolation past 92, not the fact of the miss at 72.

**Withdrawn at 27 hours (S06, cohort A, P2).**  
Under A2 the case will not resolve. E1 counts it as not resolved, via a competing event at 27, not via censoring. E2 counts a terminal disposition at 27, so the case is not in the 72-hour backlog. E3, if withdraw is censored, drops the case from the risk set at 27 and reallocates its probability onto later resolutions and open cases. The elapsed time 27 is inside the window; the miss is a miss of *resolution*, while the queue itself was cleared.

**Transferred at 68 hours (S08, cohort A, P1, escalated).**  
Internal observation stops at 68, four hours before the horizon, with no `Resolve`.

- Under E1 with transfer as a competing terminal (TR1), S08 is an observed non-resolution. It adds to transfer incidence at 68 and is not in the 72-hour backlog.
- Under E3 / transfer-as-censoring (TR2), status at 72 is unknown and the case leaves the risk set at 68.
- Under E4 (TR3), 68 is an on-time handoff, so the disposition clock is met and the resolution clock is not.
- External resolution after handoff is not in the extract. For S08 the residual gap to 72 is only 4 hours, so even an optimistic external credit is a strong extra assumption. S21 (transfer at 26) has 46 residual hours; S32 (transfer at 96) was still internal and unresolved at 72, so no external outcome after 96 can make S32 an on-time internal resolution.

### Estimates in this extract

Under TR1 (transfer competing), open cases censored at their age, and **Assumption A5** (censoring independent of the remaining hazards *given the stratum*), the Aalen–Johansen values at 72 hours for all 32 cases are:

| Component at 72h | Estimate |
|---|---:|
| E1 cumulative incidence of `Resolve` | 0.304 |
| Cumulative incidence of `Reject` | 0.101 |
| Cumulative incidence of `Withdraw` | 0.140 |
| Cumulative incidence of `Transfer` | 0.075 |
| E2 still without terminal disposition | 0.380 |
| E1 complement, \(P(T_{\mathrm{res}}>72)\) | 0.696 |
| E3, \(1-S_{\mathrm{cs}}(72)\) with non-resolution censored | 0.361 |

These four incidences and the event-free probability sum to 1. The cause-specific calculation assigns about 5.6 percentage points more probability to resolution by 72 than the cumulative incidence does (0.361 versus 0.304). That gap is the competing-event mass being pushed back into the resolution curve.

**Audit point for the recursion.** The first censoring is S31 at 23 hours. Before that, six single events occur with no censoring, so each adds \(1/32\) to its own incidence: three resolutions (S18, S16, S01), two rejections (S05, S23), one withdrawal (S12). Resolution incidence is exactly \(3/32=0.094\) just after 18 hours. Later increments use the current event-free probability divided by the current risk set.

**Restricted mean time to any terminal disposition, cut at 72,** is the area under the event-free curve: about **50.4 hours**. Reading: under TR1 and A5, the average case spends about 50 hours of the first 72 still without a terminal internal disposition. This is not the mean time to resolution.

**Identification bounds for E1**, which do not use A5. Nine cases are observed resolutions at or before 72 (S01, S02, S04, S07, S16, S17, S18, S19, S27). Sixteen cases cannot be on-time internal resolutions under any coding of the two pre-72 transfers: four resolutions after 72 (S03 76, S13 110, S20 74, S28 88), four opens older than 72 (S09, S11, S25, S26), four withdrawals (S06, S12, S22, S30), three rejections (S05, S14, S23), and S32 transferred at 96. The only E1-ambiguous cases under TR1 are the five opens younger than 72 (S10 47, S15 35, S24 67, S29 51, S31 23).

\[
\theta_{\mathrm{res}}(72)\in\left[\frac{9}{32},\frac{14}{32}\right]=[0.281,0.438]
\]  
if a pre-72 transfer is not success. If pre-72 transfer may count as success because an unobserved external resolution might occur, the upper end becomes \((9+5+2)/32=0.50\). **A majority internally resolved by 72 hours is outside these bounds**, as long as withdrawal and rejection are not relabeled as resolution.

The AJ point 0.304 sits inside \([0.281,0.438]\), close to the floor: the fitted hazard gives the five early open cases only a small chance of resolution in their residual window. A binomial-scale illustration of uncertainty for a proportion near 0.30 on \(n=32\) is about 0.08. Stratum-specific standard errors are larger. The third decimal of the AJ figures is for audit against the life table, not a claim of that precision. No formal Greenwood or bootstrap interval is attached here.

# 2. Event and censoring encoding

Right censoring means follow-up stopped while the case was still eligible for the event and the event time is only known to exceed the censor time. A competing event means an absorbing outcome that makes the event of interest impossible or no longer the operational event. An already-observed breach means the data determine the 72-hour status, so the case is not censored for that binary estimand.

| Observed status | \(n\) | Primary code for E1 / E2 | Time used | Competing-risk code | Censoring rule | Sensitivity alternative |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| `resolved`, time \(\le 72\) (9) | 9 | Resolution event | `duration_or_age_h` | Cause = resolve | None | None for the binary SLA |
| `resolved`, time \(>72\) (4) | 4 | Resolution event after the horizon | full duration | Cause = resolve | Not censored. Binary E1 failure is observed; the case is in the risk set at 72 | Can be called a clock breach and a late success at once |
| `rejected` (3), all at 9, 11, 63 | 3 | Competing exit | duration | Cause = reject | Censor only in a cause-specific resolution hazard | Drop from the SLA denominator if screen-reject is defined as out of scope |
| `withdrawn` (4), all at \(\le 70\) | 4 | Competing exit | duration | Cause = withdraw | Same as reject | Same scope option. S30 at 70 exits just before the horizon |
| `open`, age \(<72\) (5) | 5 | Unresolved and internal | age | No cause yet | Right-censored at age for every event. Binary E1 status unknown | Pessimistic: count as E1 failures. Optimistic: count as on-time resolutions. These two ends are the bounds |
| `open`, age \(>72\) (4) | 4 | Unresolved through 72 | age | No cause yet | Censored at age for the *future* resolution time. Binary E1 failure is already known because age \(>72\). Censoring them at any time \(\ge 72\) leaves E1(72) unchanged | Residual life after the cutoff may be informatively censored when the gap is long. That affects forecasts after 72, not E1 at 72 |
| `transferred` (3) | 3 | See the three rules below | duration | Cause = transfer under TR1 | Under TR2, censored at transfer time | TR3 recodes the *disposition* estimand, not `Resolve` |

### Three treatments of `transferred`

**TR1, competing internal terminal (primary).**  
`Transfer` is an absorbing internal outcome. External resolution is unknown and is not credited. S08 (68) and S21 (26) are E1 failures and E2 clearances at the transfer time. S32 (96) is an E1 failure already, because at 72 it had neither resolved nor transferred; transfer at 96 is a post-horizon competing event. This matches “the internal system stops observing the case” plus “success is `Resolve`.”

**TR2, censor at transfer.**  
The resolution time is treated as unobserved after handoff, and the censoring is treated as independent. S08 and S21 become censored before 72. S32 remains an observed E1 failure at 72, because the transfer time itself is after 72. This is the usual software default if transfer is stored as “lost to follow-up.” It is not credible as a primary rule: all three transfers are P1 and all three prefixes end in `Escalate>Transfer`, so the exit is a selected pathway.

**TR3, on-time handoff stops a disposition clock.**  
If transfer time \(\le 72\), the case is a success for E4 and a non-resolution for E1. S08 and S21 become E4 successes. S32 remains an E4 failure because 96 is already outside the window. TR3 answers a queue-clearance policy. It does not answer the resolution SLA.

A fourth, more extreme, reading (external credit with unknown delay) only widens E1’s upper bound from 0.438 to 0.500, as in section 1. It is not a third encoding so much as an ignorance bound around TR1 versus TR2.

**Long gaps are not ordinary censoring.** Gap is uninformative on terminal rows because it is structurally 0. Among the nine open cases it is a property of the current state at a single cutoff. The four opens already past 72 have gaps 31, 55, 48, and 72 hours. Four of the five opens under 72 have gaps of 2, 3, 4, and 6 hours; S10 has gap 19. **Hypothesis H_stall:** a long current gap marks a lower residual resolution hazard. The cross-section is consistent with that hypothesis and also with the simpler fact that older unresolved cases have had more time to go quiet. H_stall is a reason to refuse TR2 and to refuse independent-censoring forecasts *after* 72. It is not, by itself, a bias in the binary status of the four opens older than 72.

**Primary analysis file, one row per case, TR1.**

- `time` = `duration_or_age_h` (do not truncate open cases at 72).
- `cause` ∈ {resolve, reject, withdraw, transfer, censor}.
- Open → censor. Resolved / rejected / withdrawn / transferred → that cause.
- Binary `y_res_by_72` ∈ {1, 0, unknown}: 1 only for resolve at time \(\le 72\); 0 for resolve after 72, open after 72, reject, withdraw, and any transfer; unknown only for open with age \(<72\).

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

Restricting the SLA rate to the 13 resolved cases gives \(9/13=0.692\) resolved by 72 hours, a median resolution time of 41 hours, and a mean of \(647/13=49.8\) hours. Those three numbers describe cases that already reached `Resolve` before the freeze. They are not E1, E2, or E3.

**Survivor / completion bias, with the cases that get dropped.** The resolved-only rate drops all 9 open cases, all 4 withdrawals, all 3 rejections, and all 3 transfers. Among the dropped opens, S09 (92), S11 (81), S25 (79), and S26 (104) are already observed misses of resolution-by-72. Deleting them removes breaches from the denominator and from the numerator’s failure count. The same deletion removes S03, no: S03, S13, S20, and S28 *are* in the resolved set, so the late resolutions that have already finished are kept, while the late cases that have not finished are discarded. Fast resolutions are over-represented because a resolution must occur before the cutoff to be labeled `resolved`. A case that will resolve at 200 hours can appear here only as `open` or not yet. Conditioning on `resolved` therefore shortens the apparent duration and raises apparent compliance.

**Open and below 72 is not the same object as open and above 72.**

- Still inside the window: S31 (23, gap 3), S15 (35, gap 2), S10 (47, gap 19), S29 (51, gap 6), S24 (67, gap 4). Their E1 outcome is unknown. They remain in the risk set until their age. S24 has five hours of SLA left and was touched four hours ago; S31 is early and active. Counting either as an automatic failure, or deleting either, changes E1.
- Already outside the window: S25 (79), S11 (81), S09 (92), S26 (104), plus resolved-late S20, S03, S28, and S13. For the binary SLA these eight are known results. The four opens are censored only for residual life after the cutoff.

A 27-case “known outcome” proportion that drops the five young opens is \(9/27=0.333\). That happens to lie near the AJ estimate, but it is still a complete-case shortcut: it answers a different question (the share among cases whose 72-hour status is already determined) and it will drift as the cutoff moves and the young opens mature.

**Competing risks.** S05 and S23 are `Screen>Reject` at 9 and 11 hours. S12 is `Screen>Withdraw` at 15. These are completed processes with a short cycle time and a non-resolution outcome. A completed-case duration analysis that keeps only resolutions ignores a fast, designed exit. A Kaplan–Meier that *censors* them treats that exit as an ordinary dropout and moves their probability mass onto resolution. In this extract that inflation is the difference between 0.361 (E3) and 0.304 (E1). On P1 it is a difference of interpretation more than of the 72-hour point: the two observed P1 resolutions are S18 at 12 and S04 at 29, so a cause-specific Kaplan–Meier stays near 0.73 “still unresolved” after hour 29, while the cumulative incidence of resolution stays at 0.25 and transfer incidence is about 0.28. Reading the P1 Kaplan–Meier plateau as “most P1 cases are still on the way to `Resolve`” is inconsistent with the event-free probability, which is about 0.47 at 72.

**Informative censoring.** Transfer is clustered: S08, S21, and S32 are all P1, all escalated, and all move `Escalate>Transfer`. Censoring them as if they were a random slice of the risk set (TR2) assumes the resolution hazard they would have had equals the hazard of cases that stayed. The pathway evidence points the other way: escalation is where transfer enters the outcome set. Long open gaps have the same qualitative problem for residual-life estimates. The young opens that actually threaten E1(72) under A5 mostly have short gaps, which is more compatible with a freeze cutting through active work. A5 is most strained for S10 (gap 19 at age 47, sitting in `Escalate`) and for any forecast about S09, S11, S25, and S26 after the cutoff.

**Cohort imbalance.** Cohort B is the era after an automated-screening and escalation-policy change. Assignment was not randomized, and compliance was incomplete. In the extract the imbalance is visible and patterned:

- Every cohort A case starts `Submit>Screen`. AutoCheck appears in A only on S01 and S15, both after `Screen`, both P2.
- Every B case with priority P1 or P2 has AutoCheck. Of five B P3 cases, only S17 contains AutoCheck, and it is after `Screen`. The four B cases with no AutoCheck are S20, S23, S26, and S30, all P3.
- P1 share is 3/15 in A and 5/17 in B. All three transfers fall on P1, and P1’s resolution incidence is low, so B carries a heavier share of the priority that exits through transfer.
- Escalation *as an end-of-case flag* is 6/15 in A and 6/17 in B. That flag is not a baseline compliance measure (section 5).

Priority-specific on-time resolution in the extract: P3 is \(1/5\) in A (S02) and \(1/5\) in B (S17), with no P3 case censored before 72. P1 is 1 known on-time resolution in A (S04, out of 3, none censored before 72) and 1 in B (S18, out of 5, with S29 censored at 51). The entire crude cohort contrast sits in P2, where the identification bounds are \([2/7,4/7]\) for A and \([3/7,5/7]\) for B. Those intervals overlap. A cohort comparison that ignores priority, entry path, and calendar time is not an effect of the policy change.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

The estimand is fixed before the estimator. E1 is a cumulative incidence at a predeclared horizon. E2 is an all-cause event-free probability at that same horizon. The sample has 32 cases, 13 resolution events, no tied times, and a risk set of 9 still event-free just after 70 hours (the last event before 72). Strata are smaller: P3 has 10 cases and, usefully, no censoring before 72; P1 has 8.

**Kaplan–Meier, cause-specific.** Appropriate for \(S_{\mathrm{cs}}\) and therefore for E3. Censoring competing events is the right algebra *for that hazard* and the wrong algebra for the SLA proportion. With these data it overstates resolution by 72 hours (0.361 vs 0.304 overall; on P1 it produces a flat curve after hour 29 that is easy to misread as a large surviving fraction). Use it as a side-by-side diagnostic, not as the reported SLA rate.

**Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence.** Matches E1 and the stacked decomposition of E2. It keeps every case until its first of resolution, competing exit, or true censoring, so open cases stay in the risk set. It uses the small sample directly, with no proportional-hazards assumption. At horizon 72 the overall risk set is still large enough for a crude curve; the tail after 72 is not. In particular, once a single late case remains, a final resolution (S13 at 110 in the pooled curve) absorbs all leftover probability mass. Report the curve through 72, and do not interpret incidence after the risk set falls below about 8 as a stable tail.

**Cause-specific hazards regression.** The right *intensity* story inside a multi-state model: the hazard of each exit among cases still internal. A Cox model from \(t=0\) with only priority and cohort is the most the baseline data can support, and 13 resolution events cannot carry priority (3 levels), cohort, and their interaction. It also cannot include escalation, rework, or stage, because those are not baseline and their times are missing. Not the primary analysis.

**Subdistribution (Fine–Gray) hazards.** They target covariate effects on the CIF directly, which is attractive for E1. In this extract they are a poor primary tool. The weighted risk set keeps withdrawn, rejected, and transferred cases notionally at risk of resolution, which is hard to explain to the operation and sits badly with the process fact that those states are absorbing. With 13 events the model is also unstable. Revisit Fine–Gray when event times and a larger census exist and the question is a covariate contrast on the CIF, not the CIF itself.

**Restricted mean time.** Report \(\int_0^{72} S_{\mathrm{open}}(t)\,dt \approx 50.4\) hours as the mean time to terminal disposition, censored at the SLA. Do not report a restricted mean of latent time-to-resolve beside the 49.8-hour completed-case mean without a label; the numerical coincidence will be quoted as if it were the SLA. RMST is secondary workload context, not a substitute for \(\theta_{\mathrm{res}}(72)\).

**Landmarking.** The right design for an alert: at a prespecified age such as 24 or 48, among cases still internal, estimate residual E1 through 72 using only the prefix and gap known at the landmark. That conditions on survival to the landmark and removes immortal time. **It is not identified here.** A resolved case’s prefix is known only at the end, so an activity cannot be placed before versus after the landmark. Specify landmarks now; fit them when event timestamps arrive.

**Multi-state model.** The generative model that matches the prefixes: transient states `Screen`, `AutoCheck`, `Review`, `Escalate`, and `Rework`, with absorbing `Resolve`, `Reject`, `Withdraw`, and `Transfer`, and with the cause set changing after escalation (section 5). Transition intensities are not estimable, because sojourn times are not observed—only total age, order, and the current open gap. Fit the multi-state model on the next extract. On this extract, use the state map as the control-flow description and as the list of covariates that must be time-varying.

**Primary procedure for these data.** Nonparametric Aalen–Johansen under TR1, horizon 72, for the pooled process and stratified by priority. Publish E1, the stacked competing incidences, and E2. Publish the assumption-light bounds \([0.281, 0.438]\) next to the AJ point so A5 is visible. Stratify cohort the same way, and do not difference the crude curves into a policy effect.

**Secondary procedure.** (1) E3 Kaplan–Meier beside E1, to show the competing-risk gap, with the P1 plateau called out. (2) Event-free RMST to 72. (3) The same AJ under TR2, expected to move E1 only slightly and to move E2 more. (4) A snapshot of the nine open cases by stage, age, and gap for immediate work allocation, labeled as a census of who is open now, not as a scored risk model.

**What is deliberately not fit.** No pooled logistic model on completed cases. No baseline Cox model using `escalated` or `rework_count`. No multi-state intensities on invented sojourns. No claim that cohort B shifted the CIF.

# 5. Process-aware features and stage hazards

### What the prefixes support

There are 27 distinct full prefixes in 32 cases. Five prefixes occur twice (`Submit>Screen>Review>Resolve` on S02 and S20; `Submit>Screen>Reject` on S05 and S23; `Submit>Screen>Review>Withdraw` on S06 and S30; `Submit>Screen>Review` on open S09 and S26; `Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Escalate>Transfer` on S21 and S32). The rest are unique. Variant identity is too sparse to be a survival covariate. S02 and S20 share a variant and land on opposite sides of the SLA (41 and 74). **Control-flow identity does not determine the clock.**

Coarsen to features whose timing class is explicit.

| Feature | Class | Use | Leakage or immortal-time note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Priority | Baseline under A3 | Stratum for E1 and E2 | If priority can be upgraded later, the column is not baseline. The extract gives one value per case |
| Cohort | Baseline *era* label, not a treatment | Separate description | Entangled with calendar time, P1 mix, and rollout |
| First activity after `Submit` (`Screen` vs `AutoCheck`) | First transition, slightly after \(t=0\) | Descriptive mediator of the B-era path | Known only once the first activity has happened. In this file every AutoCheck-first case is in B. Using it as a covariate in a cohort model adjusts away the policy path |
| “Ever AutoCheck”, “ever Escalate”, final `rework_count`, full variant, prefix length | End-of-observation summaries | Control-flow description only | Post-treatment and immortal-time biased if used as baseline predictors of time to resolve. A case that resolves at `Screen` never becomes escalated. The exposed group is immortal from 0 until the activity occurs |
| Current stage | Time-varying; known at the cutoff for open cases | Snapshot triage | For completed cases, stage at an earlier hour is unknown |
| Escalation indicator switched on at escalation time | Time-varying | Correct exposure for a hazard model | Requires the timestamp. The yes/no column is the end-of-case version |
| Rework indicator or count process | Time-varying | Same | Observed on only S07, S13, S27, all P2, all eventually resolved (at 54, 110, and 39). Too rare for a stable effect |
| Inactivity gap | Time-varying; one snapshot at the cutoff for open cases | Stall alert | Structurally 0 on terminal rows, so it cannot be trained as a precursor of resolve vs withdraw. Open gaps are also length-biased: a cutoff over-represents long sojourns |
| Directly-follows pair, for example `Escalate → Transfer` | Path feature, time-varying once the pair completes | Process rules and state-dependent causes | The pair includes the outcome, so it cannot predict that outcome. Use the state `Escalate` to predict *future* transfer, not the completed pair |

**Immortal-time illustration.** S18 is `Submit>AutoCheck>Escalate>Resolve` at 12 hours and S04 is `Submit>Screen>Escalate>Resolve` at 29. S01 resolves at 18 with no escalation. If the fitted covariate is “escalated = yes” measured at the end, S01 is classified as unexposed partly because resolution ended the opportunity to escalate. The hours before escalation are guaranteed not to contain a resolution that would have removed the case from the escalated group. That mechanical association makes escalation look slow even when escalation has no causal delay. The data already show both directions: escalated resolutions at 12 and 29, and escalated resolutions at 76, 88, and 110. Escalation is neither necessary for a miss (S20 resolves at 74 on `Submit>Screen>Review>Resolve` with no escalation and no rework) nor sufficient for one.

### Stage-dependent competing risks, observed in this extract

Terminal edges, counted once per terminal case (23 terminal cases, 23 edges):

| Edge | Count | Cases |
|---|---:|---|
| `Review → Resolve` | 6 | S02, S03, S07, S17, S19, S20 |
| `Escalate → Resolve` | 4 | S04, S13, S18, S28 |
| `Review → Withdraw` | 3 | S06, S22, S30 |
| `Escalate → Transfer` | 3 | S08, S21, S32 |
| `Screen → Reject` | 2 | S05, S23 |
| `Screen → Resolve` | 1 | S16 |
| `Screen → Withdraw` | 1 | S12 |
| `AutoCheck → Resolve` | 1 | S01 |
| `Review → Reject` | 1 | S14 |
| `Rework → Resolve` | 1 | S27 |

Every transfer is immediately preceded by `Escalate`. Across all 12 escalated cases the observed endings are resolve (5), transfer (3), or still open (4): S03, S04, S13, S18, S28; S08, S21, S32; S10, S11, S25, S29. None is rejected or withdrawn. **Hypothesis H_esc:** after escalation the competing set changes, from reject/withdraw toward transfer. Alternative: those exits are allowed and merely absent in 12 cases. Either way, a single competing-risk model with a fixed cause set from \(t=0\) is coarser than the path. Rejection and withdrawal are properties of `Screen` and `Review`. Transfer is a property of `Escalate`.

Sojourn hazards are not estimated. The only sojourn lower bound in the file is the open gap, equal to observed time in the current stage under A7 (the last prefix label is the last event). # 6. Sensitivity analysis

Three analyses are enough to see which sentences survive. All of them keep open cases in the file.

**S-a. Primary.** TR1, reject and withdraw competing, open cases censored at age, E1 via Aalen–Johansen, plus the bounds that relax A5.  
Pooled \(\hat\theta_{\mathrm{res}}(72)=0.304\), bounds \([0.281, 0.438]\), E2 \(=0.380\). By priority: P1 0.250 resolved and 0.469 still open (\(n=8\)); P2 0.431 resolved and 0.404 still open (\(n=14\)); P3 0.200 resolved, 0.300 rejected, 0.200 withdrawn, 0.300 still open (\(n=10\), and the P3 figures are ordinary proportions because nobody in P3 is censored before 72). Cohort AJ: A 0.292 (\(n=15\)), B 0.316 (\(n=17\)).

**S-b. Transfer censored (TR2), other competing events unchanged.**  
Resolution incidence at 72 moves only from 0.304 to about 0.313. Two pre-72 transfers are not enough to reshape the pooled resolution curve. Event-free probability moves more, from 0.380 to about 0.435, because those transfers are no longer counted as dispositions. On P1, TR2 is a material *interpretation* change: two of eight cases leave the resolve/transfer decomposition and become dropouts. Qualitative conclusion that is stable: pooled on-time resolution stays near 30 percent and below half. Qualitative conclusion that is not stable: “P1 cases are cleared before 72 hours.”

**S-c. Redefine the success event, do not redefine the math silently.**  
- E4 (TR3): pre-72 transfer counts as clock clearance. S08 and S21 become disposition successes. P1 then has two on-time resolutions plus two on-time handoffs, against three known non-clearances (S11, S25, and S32) and one unknown (S29). P1 can be described as often cleared and poorly *resolved*. Both descriptions are true of different estimands.  
- Scope-out of fast ineligible rejects: if `Screen>Reject` (S05, S23) is defined as not an SLA case, the denominator changes and P3’s rejection incidence is no longer an SLA failure. P3’s on-time resolution count stays two cases (S02, S17). The sentence “P3 rarely resolves inside 72 hours” stays true; the sentence “P3 is where the SLA fails” gets weaker, because half of P3’s pre-72 exits are reject or withdraw, including two rejects at 9 and 11 hours.  
- Optimistic imputation: the five young opens all resolve in time, and, jointly, external partners resolve S08 and S21 in time. Upper value \(16/32=0.50\). The pooled “below half” statement does not flip to a majority. It would flip only if withdrawals or rejections were counted as successes, which contradicts the problem’s definition of success.

**Cohort-policy sensitivity.** Standardizing crudely on priority does not reveal a B-era gain: P3 is \(1/5\) versus \(1/5\), P1 is one on-time resolution on each side, and P2 bounds overlap. A sign flip is still possible if the two censored A P2 cases (S10, S15) miss and the two censored B P2 cases (S24, S31) both resolve before 72. That allocation is inside the bounds and is not what the short residual window on S24 (five hours) makes probable. **Hypothesis H_rollout:** AutoCheck-first was applied to B’s P1 and P2 cases, while P3 rollout is the incomplete part. If that hypothesis is right, a cohort dummy is not “the policy,” and a compliance analysis has to be priority-specific. No compliance analysis here identifies an effect: entry path is post-baseline, noncompliance is entangled with P3, and there is no concurrent control.

**Conclusions that are stable under S-a through S-c.**  
On-time internal resolution is at most one half of this extract and is estimated near 30 percent. The resolved-only 69 percent figure does not survive contact with open and competing cases. P2 has the highest resolution incidence; P3’s low resolution incidence is tied to reject and withdraw; P1’s low resolution incidence is tied to escalation and transfer. Open cases older than 72 are observed resolution misses. Cohort B is not shown to have reduced SLA tail risk.

**Conclusions that can reverse.**  
“P1 is the worst priority” reverses when the estimand changes from E1 (P1 0.25, P3 0.20, P2 0.43) to E2 (P1 still-open about 0.47, P2 about 0.40, P3 0.30): P1 holds the most unfinished internal work, P3 resolves the least because it exits. “Transfer is rare enough to ignore” reverses for any P1-only report. “B improved the SLA” can be produced by selective imputation of four P2 censorings and is not supported by the observed statuses. “A long gap is an ordinary censoring indicator” reverses residual-life forecasts for the stalled opens and does not reverse E1 at 72 for cases already older than 72.

# 7. Operational interpretation

Use the estimates as a description of this frozen census under an explicit estimand. They are not effects of priority, of escalation, or of the cohort B policy.

**Governance number.** Report E1 and its bounds together: about 30 percent internally resolved by 72 hours, and not more than 44 percent under TR1 no matter how the five young opens finish. Put the stacked split next to it so 70 percent “not resolved” is not spoken as if it were 70 percent still in queue. About 38 percent are estimated still undisposed at 72 (E2); about 32 percent have already left through reject, withdraw, or transfer. The action attached to E2 is capacity. The action attached to competing incidence is decision quality (were the rejects and withdrawals correct?) and handoff quality (what happens after transfer, which this system does not see).

**Do not manage to the completed-case 69 percent.** It will move whenever the cutoff moves, even if the process does not, because it waits for slow cases to finish before it counts them.

**Two different live actions, corresponding to two different open patterns.**

1. **Deadline watch, short gap, age near 72.** S24 is 67 hours old, in `Review`, touched 4 hours ago, with 5 hours of SLA left. The useful action is immediate ownership and a finish-or-exit decision before hour 72. A stall model will under-rank S24 because the gap is short.
2. **Restart watch, long gap, breach already recorded.** S11 and S25 have been in `Escalate` for about two days of silence; S09 and S26 have been in `Review` for 31 and 72 hours of silence. They have already missed E1. Further waiting is not an SLA strategy. The action is reassignment, a withdrawal/transfer decision, or an escalation that actually changes the queue—whichever the policy allows. Attaching a survival probability to these four cases requires H_stall and is not calibrated here.

S29 (P1, back in `Review` after escalation, age 51, gap 6, 21 hours left) is ordinary high-priority work with time remaining. S31 and S15 are active and early. S10 (age 47, gap 19, in `Escalate`) is the ambiguous one: inside the window, but the gap is the longest among the pre-72 opens, and the state is the same state in which transfers and long stalls appear.

**Alerts should be state rules with a documented estimand, not points on a score.** A provisional rule, justified only as a description of this snapshot, is: if age \(\ge 60\) and the case is internal, raise a deadline alert regardless of gap; if gap \(\ge 24\) and the stage is `Review` or `Escalate`, raise a stall alert even when the case is already past 72. When timestamps exist, replace each rule with a landmark cumulative incidence: \(P(T_{\mathrm{res}}>72 \mid \text{still internal at } t_0, \text{stage}(t_0), \text{gap}(t_0), \text{priority})\), estimated only from information available at \(t_0\). Until those probabilities exist, do not print a pseudo-probability on the case. Do not train that model on the final `escalated` flag or on a prefix that already ends in `Resolve`.

**Cohort and priority are strata for calibration and for fairness checks, not treatment effects.**

- **Calibration.** On 32 cases there is no fitted score to calibrate. If a score is later deployed, calibrate predicted E1 against observed resolution-by-72 inside priority × cohort cells, on a later time window, and include open cases via the same censoring rules. A score that ignores competing exits will look well calibrated against a Kaplan–Meier and poorly calibrated against the operational miss rate.
- **Fairness / consistency with the priority label.** Equal resolution rates across priorities are not the target: P1, P2, and P3 are different intended severities, and a fast correct rejection of a P3 submission is not a harm of the same kind as a stalled P1. The relevant check is consistency with the stated priority. In this extract every P1 case is escalated, and the long `Escalate` silences are P1 cases (S11, S25). Every P3 case avoids escalation, including S09 and S26, which are the oldest open cases. **Hypothesis:** an escalation-centric dashboard hides P3 stalls and parks P1 work in `Escalate` without shortening the clock. Alternative: P1 cases are harder, and P3 cases are appropriately left on a manual review path that often ends in reject or withdraw. The data can show the disparity in pathway and in gap; they cannot decide between those two readings. Publish E1, E2, and median open gap by priority. Investigate any future extract in which P1’s event-free curve and P1’s inactivity exceed P2’s while P1 is labeled the highest priority.
- **Cohort reporting.** Report B versus A inside priority, with the AutoCheck-first rate shown as a process metric of rollout (here: AutoCheck-first on 0/15 in A and on 12/17 in B, and on 0 of the B P3 cases except the non-first AutoCheck on S17). Do not convert the CIF difference 0.316 versus 0.292 into a claim that the policy worked.

**Customer and partner communication.** For a transferred case, the internal system cannot state an external resolution time. S32 had already missed 72 hours before handoff. S08 missed resolution and, under E4 only, met a handoff clock by four hours. Those sentences should stay distinct in the SLA report so a rise in transfer cannot improve the resolution KPI.

def e1_binary(row, transfer_mode):
    """1 = resolved by 72, 0 = known not, None = unknown. TR3 is not E1."""
    t, s = row.duration_or_age_h, row.observed_status
    if s == "resolved":
        return 1 if t <= HORIZON else 0
    if s in ("rejected", "withdrawn"):
        return 0
    if s == "open":
        return 0 if t > HORIZON else None
    if s == "transferred":
        if t > HORIZON:
            return 0  # still internal and unresolved at 72
        if transfer_mode == "competing":
            return 0
        if transfer_mode == "censor":
            return None
        raise ValueError(transfer_mode)
    raise ValueError(s)

def e4_disposition(row):
    """Clock-stop estimand: resolve or transfer at t<=72 counts as clearance."""
    t, s = row.duration_or_age_h, row.observed_status
    if s == "resolved":
        return 1 if t <= HORIZON else 0
    if s == "transferred":
        return 1 if t <= HORIZON else 0
    if s in ("rejected", "withdrawn"):
        return 0
    if s == "open":
        return 0 if t > HORIZON else None
    raise ValueError(s)

def cause_column(frame, transfer_mode):
    out = []
    for r in frame.itertuples(index=False):
        if r.observed_status == "open":
            out.append("censor")
        elif r.observed_status == "transferred" and transfer_mode == "censor":
            out.append("censor")
        else:
            out.append(r.observed_status)
    return out

def aalen_johansen(frame, transfer_mode, horizon=HORIZON):
    """CIF at `horizon`. Events before censoring on any tied time.
    Open cases are retained as censorings. No case is dropped.
    """
    work = frame.copy()
    work["cause"] = cause_column(work, transfer_mode)
    rank = {"resolved": 0, "rejected": 1, "withdrawn": 2, "transferred": 3, "censor": 4}
    work["_r"] = work["cause"].map(rank)
    work = work.sort_values(["duration_or_age_h", "_r"])
    n = len(work)
    surv = 1.0
    cif = {k: 0.0 for k in ("resolved", "rejected", "withdrawn", "transferred")}
    at_risk = n
    history = []
    for r in work.itertuples(index=False):
        if r.duration_or_age_h > horizon:
            break
        if at_risk <= 0:
            break
        if r.cause == "censor":
            at_risk -= 1
            history.append((r.duration_or_age_h, r.case, "censor", at_risk, surv, dict(cif)))
            continue
        inc = surv / at_risk
        cif[r.cause] += inc
        surv *= (at_risk - 1) / at_risk
        at_risk -= 1
        history.append((r.duration_or_age_h, r.case, r.cause, at_risk, surv, dict(cif)))
    return {
        "n": n,
        "cif_resolve": cif["resolved"],
        "cif_reject": cif["rejected"],
        "cif_withdraw": cif["withdrawn"],
        "cif_transfer": cif["transferred"],
        "still_open": surv,
        "p_not_resolved": 1.0 - cif["resolved"],
        "at_risk_at_horizon": at_risk,
        "history": history,
    }

def bounds(frame, count_pre72_transfer_as_failure=True):
    """Identification bounds for E1. Young opens are the ambiguity under TR1."""
    known_success, known_fail, unknown = [], [], []
    for r in frame.itertuples(index=False):
        y = e1_binary(
            r, "competing" if count_pre72_transfer_as_failure else "censor")
        if y == 1:
            known_success.append(r.case)
        elif y == 0:
            known_fail.append(r.case)
        else:
            unknown.append(r.case)
    n = len(frame)
    lo = len(known_success) / n
    hi = (len(known_success) + len(unknown)) / n
    return lo, hi, known_success, known_fail, unknown

def naive_resolved_only(frame):

    on_time = (done.duration_or_age_h <= HORIZON).sum()
    return on_time, len(done), on_time / len(done), done.duration_or_age_h.median()

# 9. Limitations and additional data

The bounds on on-time internal resolution are the firmest result this file can support. The Aalen–Johansen point depends on independent censoring for five young open cases and on transfer being an absorbing non-resolution. Priority-specific curves depend on single-digit event counts. Nothing in the file identifies the effect of the cohort B policy.

Data that would change the analysis, in the order they would reduce uncertainty:

1. **Event timestamps on the SLA clock**, one per prefix activity, plus the calendar start time and the cutoff timestamp. These identify sojourn times, make landmarking possible, let escalation and rework be time-varying covariates, and separate administrative censoring (cutoff minus start) from stalling (gap). They also show whether cohort B simply had less calendar time to finish.
2. **External outcome and external time after `Transfer`.** Needed to close TR1 versus an external-credit estimand for S08 and S21. S32 is already a pre-transfer miss at 72 hours even without this field.
3. **Cutoff and start dates for every case**, including cases that never reached the extract if any exist. Needed to confirm there is no left truncation and to test whether open ages are an administrative pattern or a difficulty pattern.
4. **Time-varying workload and staffing** (queue length, reviewer load) at each event. Without them, a long `Review` or `Escalate` gap is observationally confounded with an overloaded desk.
5. **Assignment and priority-setting mechanism**, including whether priority can change and why a case is escalated or transferred. Needed before any fairness conclusion moves past “the pathways differ.”
6. **Policy rollout compliance at case level**: whether AutoCheck and the new escalation rule were supposed to apply, and whether non-use on B P3 cases was designed or missed. The prefix pattern is consistent with priority-targeted rollout; the file does not say so.
7. **A repeat census after the five young opens mature** (S10, S15, S24, S29, S31). Their eventual causes would shrink the E1 bounds directly. Until then, those five are the estimation risk at the horizon; the four opens already past 72 are not.

Sojourn times must not be imputed to unlock a multi-state intensity model. A directly-follows description is available now. Stage-specific hazards wait on the timestamps.
