### Analysis of DFG Edges and Concurrency
Observed DFG edges show bidirectional frequencies between Credit Check and Engineering Review: (`Register Order`, `Credit Check`: 19, `Register Order`, `Engineering Review`: 11), (`Credit Check`, `Engineering Review`: 17, `Engineering Review`, `Credit Check`: 11), and (`Supplier Confirmation`, `Engineering Review`: 2, `Engineering Review`, `Supplier Confirmation`: 13). 

**Why both directions in a DFG do not by themselves prove concurrency:** 
A directed graph records precedence relationships of *completions*, not executions. If two activities (e.g., Credit Check and Engineering Review) execute concurrently, their completion timestamps depend on their relative durations. If Credit Check finishes faster in 17 cases, the log records $CC \rightarrow ER$. If Engineering Review finishes faster in 11 cases, it records $ER \rightarrow CC$. While this bidirectional flow is *indicative* of concurrent execution (as supported by stakeholder S2), it can equally be explained by an unconstrained sequential XOR choice ($CC \rightarrow ER$ vs. $ER \rightarrow CC$) or overlapping independent tasks. Therefore, structural choices (Sequence, XOR, or Concurrency/Partial Order) must be justified by domain testimony (S1 vs. S2) and risk appetite rather than raw DFG topology alone.

# 2. Shared Assumptions

1. **Event-Completion Semantics:** All observed traces and generated behaviors operate on completion timestamps. Activities are assumed to represent completed work units, and logs capture the completion order, not the start order.
2. **Missing Supplier Confirmation Events:** Due to data integration issues (S5) and legitimate business skipping (S4), the absence of Supplier Confirmation in certain observed variants (e.g., variants 3, 4) is treated as a valid structural variation rather than a pure data corruption anomaly.
3. **Absent External/Stocked Attribute:** Because the attribute distinguishing stocked components from external components is missing from the data export, all candidate models must accommodate both paths (Supplier Confirmation present or absent) structurally via control-flow optionality.
4. **Mandatory Bookends:** All models enforce `Register Order` as the absolute start activity and `Archive Case` as the absolute end activity, in compliance with high-confidence rule D1.
5. **Representation of Optionality:** Optional activities (such as Supplier Confirmation and Expedite Review) are modeled using explicit choice operators (XOR/optional branches) or partial order skips, avoiding unconstrained loops that could lead to infinite or unintended behavior.

# 3. Conservative Candidate

### Inductive-Bias Statement
The conservative model prioritizes underfitting over overfitting by strictly adhering to observed variant structures and high-confidence rules while rejecting unobserved interleavings. It uses an explicit XOR enumeration of the observed variants wrapped between the mandatory start and end milestones. This minimizes the risk of admitting unauthorized control-flow behaviors (such as arbitrary concurrency) at the expense of structural simplicity and generalization, treating unobserved paths as non-compliant.

### Formal Expression
$$\text{Model}_{\text{conservative}} = \text{Register Order} \rightarrow \left( \bigoplus_{i=1}^{7} \text{Variant}_i \right) \rightarrow \text{Archive Case}$$
where each $\text{Variant}_i$ is the strict sequence of activities corresponding to the 7 observed empirical variants.

### Behavior Deliberately Excluded
- Any permutation of Credit Check and Engineering Review combined with Supplier Confirmation not explicitly captured in the 7 variants (e.g., Engineering Review $\rightarrow$ Supplier Confirmation $\rightarrow$ Credit Check).
- Any occurrence of Expedite Review without Credit Check and Engineering Review preceding it, or multiple Expedite Reviews.
- Cases where Supplier Confirmation occurs *after* Approve Configuration.

### Known Underfitting Risks
- Fails to generalize to unobserved but valid combinations (e.g., an external component order where Engineering Review precedes Credit Check and Supplier Confirmation happens before both).
- Fails to capture the underlying concurrency between Credit Check and Engineering Review, treating order swaps as entirely distinct process variants rather than parallel execution.
- Scalability bottleneck: Enumerating every minor variation via XOR creates an exponentially growing tree that is unmaintainable for larger logs.

# 4. Balanced Candidate

### Inductive-Bias Statement
The balanced model strikes a principled compromise between precision and generalization by capturing stakeholder-attested concurrency (S2) and optionality (S4, S5) while respecting core sequencing guardrails (D2, D3, D4, D6). It models Credit Check and Engineering Review as concurrent execution blocks, treats Supplier Confirmation as an optional parallel/conditional branch, and inserts Expedite Review as an optional step gated behind both checks, avoiding both rigid trace enumeration and over-permissive loop structures.

### Decision Rationale: Sequence vs. Concurrency vs. Partial Order
- **Credit Check & Engineering Review:** Modeled as **concurrent** ($\land$). While Finance (S1) insists on strict sequence ($CC \rightarrow ER$), Engineering (S2) states they run in parallel and completion order reflects race conditions. The bidirectional DFG frequencies ($CC \rightarrow ER: 17$, $ER \rightarrow CC: 11$) strongly support parallel execution with variable completion times.
- **Supplier Confirmation:** Modeled as an **optional branch** ($\times(\text{tau}, \text{Supplier Confirmation})$) operating concurrently with or between the checks and approval, satisfying Procurement (S3) and Production (S4/S5) regarding missing data and stocked component skipping.
- **Expedite Review:** Modeled as an **optional sequential block** ($\times(\text{tau}, \text{Expedite Review})$) constrained to occur after both principal checks and before approval, adhering to Compliance (S6).

### Formal Expression
$$\text{Model}_{\text{balanced}} = \text{Register Order} \rightarrow (\text{Credit Check} \parallel \text{Engineering Review}) \rightarrow [ \text{Supplier Confirmation} ] \rightarrow [ \text{Expedite Review} ] \rightarrow \text{Approve Configuration} \rightarrow \text{Release Production} \rightarrow \text{Archive Case}$$

### Python Code (`model_balanced`)
```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator

# Balanced model using Process Tree operators:
# Register Order -> (CC concurrent ER) -> [Supplier Confirmation] -> [Expedite Review] -> Approve Configuration -> Release Production -> Archive Case

reg = ProcessTree(name="Register Order")
cc = ProcessTree(name="Credit Check")
er = ProcessTree(name="Engineering Review")
sc = ProcessTree(name="Supplier Confirmation")
exp = ProcessTree(name="Expedite Review")
app = ProcessTree(name="Approve Configuration")
rel = ProcessTree(name="Release Production")
arc = ProcessTree(name="Archive Case")

# Optional Supplier Confirmation: XOR(tau, Supplier Confirmation)
opt_sc = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[ProcessTree(None), sc])

# Optional Expedite Review: XOR(tau, Expedite Review)
opt_exp = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[ProcessTree(None), exp])

# Concurrency between Credit Check and Engineering Review
checks_par = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.PARALLEL, children=[cc, er])

model_balanced = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[
    reg,
    checks_par,
    opt_sc,
    opt_exp,
    app,
    rel,
    arc
])
```

### Behavior Deliberately Excluded
- Supplier Confirmation occurring *after* Approve Configuration.
- Expedite Review occurring *before* either Credit Check or Engineering Review completes.
- Multiple instances of Expedite Review in a single trace.
- Activities occurring outside the Register Order $\rightarrow$ Archive Case bracket.

### Known Underfitting Risks
- The parallel operator ($\parallel$) between Credit Check and Engineering Review permits unobserved interleavings with other concurrent branches if not carefully scoped, potentially allowing Supplier Confirmation to execute in arbitrary relation to internal check sub-steps.
- Treating optionality independently may generate combinations that violate subtle business rules if dependencies between stocked components and supplier confirmations are strictly tighter than assumed.

# 5. Permissive Candidate

### Inductive-Basis Statement
The permissive model maximizes generalization and robustness against missing data (S5) and unobserved process variations by employing a relaxed partial order (POWL). It lifts rigid strict-sequencing assumptions and allows Credit Check, Engineering Review, and Supplier Confirmation to execute in flexible partial orders as long as causal milestones (start, approval prerequisites, and termination) are preserved. This represents the upper bound of exploration, admitting plausible unobserved pathways while caging clearly forbidden violations.

### Boundary Definition
- **Permitted (Reasonable Generalization):** Flexible ordering of Credit Check and Engineering Review, optional Supplier Confirmation placed anywhere before approval, and single or zero Expedite Reviews occurring after both checks.
- **Forbidden (Strictly Enforced Boundaries):** `Register Order` must always be the absolute first event; `Archive Case` must always be the absolute last event; `Approve Configuration` must strictly precede `Release Production`; `Release Production` must precede `Archive Case`. Any trace violating these causal precedence edges is strictly forbidden.

### Python Code (`model_permissive`)
```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, Transition, SilentTransition

# Permissive POWL model encoding partial order between checks, optional supplier confirmation, and expedite review
reg = Transition(label="Register Order")
cc = Transition(label="Credit Check")
er = Transition(label="Engineering Review")
sc = Transition(label="Supplier Confirmation")
exp = Transition(label="Expedite Review")
app = Transition(label="Approve Configuration")
rel = Transition(label="Release Production")
arc = Transition(label="Archive Case")

tau_sc = SilentTransition()
tau_exp = SilentTransition()

nodes = {reg, cc, er, sc, tau_sc, exp, tau_exp, app, rel, arc}
order = StrictPartialOrder(nodes)

# Register Order initiates everything
order.order.add_edge(reg, cc)
order.order.add_edge(reg, er)
order.order.add_edge(reg, tau_sc)
order.order.add_edge(reg, exp) # via tau choice or direct

# Both CC and ER must precede Approve Configuration
order.order.add_edge(cc, app)
order.order.add_edge(er, app)

# Supplier Confirmation is optional (choice via silent transition)
# If SC happens, it precedes App; tau_sc bypasses SC
order.order.add_edge(sc, app)
order.order.add_edge(tau_sc, app) # tau_sc represents skipping SC

# Expedite Review is optional, requires both checks, precedes app
order.order.add_edge(cc, exp)
order.order.add_edge(er, exp)
order.order.add_edge(exp, app)
order.order.add_edge(tau_exp, app)

# Mandatory tail
order.order.add_edge(app, rel)
order.order.add_edge(rel, arc)

model_permissive = order
```

### Behavior Deliberately Excluded
- Traces where `Archive Case` occurs before `Register Order`.
- Traces where `Release Production` occurs without prior `Approve Configuration`.
- Traces where `Expedite Review` occurs without both `Credit Check` and `Engineering Review` completing first.

### Known Underfitting Risks
- High risk of low precision: Permits a vast combinatorial explosion of trace variants that were never observed in production, some of which might violate unstated operational dependencies.
- Difficult to audit for compliance officers due to the high degree of structural freedom in partial order execution.

# 9. Recommendation and Data-Collection Plan

### Recommendation
- **Immediate Operational Use:** **Balanced Candidate (`model_balanced`)**. It provides an optimal trade-off by capturing stakeholder-attested concurrency between Credit Check and Engineering Review while maintaining clean structural controls for optional supplier confirmation and expedite review. It avoids the rigidity of the conservative model and the excessive precision risks of the permissive model.
- **Sensitivity Analysis:** **Permissive Candidate (`model_permissive`)**. Use this model in simulated stress tests to evaluate how downstream queue times and compliance bottlenecks shift if procurement and engineering tasks are allowed maximum operational flexibility.

### Concrete Data-Collection Plan
1. **Attribute Logging:** Mandate the export of the `external_stocked` component attribute in the ERP integration layer to definitively distinguish legitimate skips (S4) from data integration bugs (S5).
2. **Lifecycle Timestamps:** Capture both `start` and `complete` lifecycle timestamps for all activities—especially `Credit Check` and `Engineering Review`—to resolve D7 and prove whether they execute concurrently or sequentially.
3. **Targeted Trace Experiments:** Introduce controlled process variations (e.g., forcing strict sequential credit checks followed by engineering reviews on a pilot batch of orders) to measure cycle-time impact and empirically validate the concurrency hypothesis (S2).

# 10. Uncertainty Statement

### Calibrated Confidence Assessment
- **Start/End Bookends (D1):** **High Confidence (95%)**. Universal business consensus supports Register Order and Archive Case as absolute boundaries.
- **Credit Check & Engineering Review Concurrency (S2 vs. S1):** **Medium Confidence (60%)**. While DFG frequencies and engineering testimony suggest concurrency, finance policy demands sequential execution.
