# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

Baseline facts about Mira_Coordinator that every network must be read against: 960 events in 905 distinct cases (about 1.06 events per case she touches), 22% of all claims (905 / ~4,100), a 4.2-minute median service time, and a 48% exception_flag share against a population baseline of roughly 19–31%. She is a low-volume, low-duration, high-reach resource. Most of the "contradictions" below dissolve once the normalisation of each metric is made explicit.

**Handover-of-work network (directed, sequence-adjacency based)**

- What it implies: she is the main human landing point for the automated router (Bot_Rules → Mira 920, versus 610 to Alice and 590 to Bruno, i.e. roughly 43% of Bot_Rules' human handovers). She hands over to at least six distinct human resources with a remarkably flat distribution (Carla 350, Diego 330, Elise 290, Farid 280, Omar 210, Priya 180), and she is the return address for specialists (Omar → Mira 160, Priya → Mira 140) and for temporary staff (Temp_1 → Mira 190, Temp_2 → Mira 170). Out-degree centrality 0.82 on a 15-node graph means she passes work to roughly 11–12 of the 14 other resources; betweenness 0.77 makes her the top human bridge, far above Bot_Rules (0.41) and the team leads (0.31–0.33).
- What it does not imply: (a) that she performs the work; handover counts adjacency, not effort or duration. (b) That the 16–51 minute mean elapsed times after her handovers are "her" time; the elapsed time between two events is the receiver's queue wait plus the sender's completion lag, and cannot be attributed without service/waiting decomposition. (c) That she exercises discretion; Bot_Rules may already determine the target, and she may merely execute an assignment event. (d) That the centrality is personal; any resource placed at the fan-out point of an exception branch would have high betweenness. Betweenness here measures the process design position at least as much as the individual.
- Measurement caveat: the listed edges alone give roughly 1,580 in-handovers and 1,640 out-handovers involving her, both exceeding her 960 events. This is only possible if the network was computed with an indirect-succession window (PM4Py's beta parameter greater than 0), if lifecycle start/complete events were both counted, or if the tables come from different filters. The degree and betweenness values should therefore be read as relative rankings, not as absolute counts.

**Working-together network (undirected, per-case co-occurrence)**

- What the metric actually measures: whether two resources both appear in a case, counted once per case and normalised by cases. It does not weight by number of events, by service duration, or by order. The note in the evidence attributing her low co-occurrence to "short routing events" is technically incorrect for the standard metric: a 4-second event and a 4-hour event count identically. The fact that this explanation was offered is itself a small signal that the metric is being misread inside the organisation.
- What it implies: her co-case rates with adjusters (6–8%) are approximately what her handover counts predict (350 / 4,100 ≈ 8.5% for Carla). There is no contradiction; it is the same set of cases seen through a different normaliser. Because she is present in only 22% of cases, no pair involving her can exceed 22%, and her cases are split across six or more partners, so each dyad is necessarily small. By contrast Alice–Carla (27%) is a repeated, fixed dyad inside a team. The very low rates with the team leads (5% and 4%) are informative: Bot_Rules routes a case either to her or to a team lead, so they sit on alternative branches. She is not embedded in either team; she is a parallel channel.
- What it does not imply: that she is peripheral, unimportant, or "works alone". Low clustering combined with high betweenness is the textbook signature of a broker occupying a structural hole, not of a marginal actor. It also does not imply that the notes' labels ("Team North core pair", "surprisingly peripheral") are validated; those are interpretations layered onto the numbers.

**Subcontracting network (A → B → A within n = 2 events)**

- What it implies: she delegates and receives back. Of 210 medical referrals, 126 (60%) return to her within two events; of 180 legal referrals, 98 (54%) do. Her subcontracting betweenness (0.69) is by far the highest. This is a dispatcher / case-ownership pattern: specialists act as advisors, and the onward decision (Assign Specialist, Reopen) flows back through her. The Bot_Rules → Mira → Bot_Rules fragment (Fraud Screen → manual exception → Fraud Screen update, 62 cases) shows a human-in-the-loop override or enrichment of an automated control.
- What it does not imply: hierarchy. Subcontracting in PM4Py is purely sequential and says nothing about who reports to whom. It also does not distinguish designed consultation loops from unplanned rework, and it cannot say whether the Reopen in the fragment is a quality catch or a clerical registration.
- Measurement caveat: 224 subcontracting instances with her as resource A require at least 224 cases in which she appears twice, but 960 − 905 leaves at most 55 surplus events. The subcontracting table therefore must have been computed on a different log slice (for example exception cases with lifecycle events retained), or resource attribution differs between tables. This should be reconciled before any figure is cited to management.

**Performance / time statistics**

- What they imply: her direct processing footprint is small (4.2 minutes median). The "high variance" cycle-time contribution therefore describes waiting in front of her events, not her service time. The long mean elapsed times after her handovers to specialists (42 and 51 minutes) largely reflect specialist availability. The absence episode is the strongest single piece of evidence that the exception branch depends on her seat: exception P90 rose from 8.4 to 13.9 days (+65%) while ordinary claims were unaffected, which is exactly the scoped effect a single-owner exception queue would produce.
- What they do not imply: that she is the branch's bottleneck under normal conditions (specialists at 37–51 minutes of service and Vendor_Verify at 2.1 hours dominate the branch's touch time), or that the absence effect is about unique knowledge rather than an unstaffed queue. It is one six-day episode with no control period, P90 on perhaps a hundred exception cases is statistically noisy, and concurrent events (specialist load, volume spikes) are unreported.

**Role discovery / activity-profile similarity**

- What it implies: her activity set (Request Missing Information, Reopen Claim, Escalate Complaint, Assign Specialist) is unusual. Two activities appear to be hers alone (Escalate Complaint, Assign Specialist), one is shared with juniors and Temp_1 (Request Missing Information), and one with Alice (Reopen Claim). `discover_organizational_roles` would probably return her as a singleton role or merge her with Elise, Farid and Temp_1 on the strength of Request Missing Information, a merge that would completely hide her broker position. Note that "Assign Specialist" is not in the 14-activity process model given; it is an undocumented activity label and possibly a system-generated event.
- What it does not imply: anything about sequence, timing or centrality. Activity profiles systematically under-describe coordinators, whose value lies in where and when they act rather than in what activity label they produce.

**Reconciling the apparent contradiction.** Handover and subcontracting are sequence metrics; working-together is a set-membership metric normalised by cases. A resource that appears once per case, in 22% of cases, immediately after a router and immediately before one of many downstream resources, will maximise the first two and minimise the third by construction. Her position is a hub in a star; the team leads sit in dense triangles (lead–senior–junior). Both are central, in different topological senses. Nothing here is contradictory; the metrics are answering different questions.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

**H1. Formal coordinator / exception desk (the designed role)**

- Mechanism: Bot_Rules routes flagged or ambiguous cases to an exception queue that Mira owns. She clarifies, requests missing information, dispatches to specialists or adjusters, and registers reopens and complaints. Her centrality is a property of the branch design.
- Supporting evidence: the Bot_Rules → Mira edge (920) is the largest automated-to-human handover and is labelled as exception routing; 48% exception share versus a 19–31% baseline; 64% of reopens and 71% of complaints; the near-uniform fan-out to all four adjusters suggests rule-based rather than preferential assignment; the operations manager's description.
- Contradicting or limiting evidence: about half of her cases are not exception-flagged, so either the desk also handles unflagged missing-information work or the flag is applied inconsistently (Bot_Ingest and Bot_Rules touch almost the same cases yet show 19% versus 31%, which indicates the flag is set or changed after ingestion). Temps escalate to her rather than to team leads, which is outside a pure exception-desk mandate. The process owner explicitly denies that she is a lead, and no formal routing rulebook is mentioned.
- Predictions: a documented routing rule set exists and her assignment choices can be reproduced from case_type, amount_band, region and channel; her in-edges are almost exclusively from Bot_Rules, returning specialists and temps; exception-case variants show a small number of stable dispatch patterns; a formally assigned backup reproduces her edge profile during future absences.

**H2. Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency**

- Mechanism: throughput of the exception branch is limited by one person's availability and tacit knowledge; short service times conceal a long queue wait in front of her events.
- Supporting evidence: the absence effect (+5.5 days at P90 for exception cases, none for ordinary cases); "high variance" cycle-time contribution; the highest handover and subcontracting betweenness; adjusters' statement that she "knows where hard cases should go"; two activities apparently performed by nobody else.
- Contradicting or limiting evidence: 4.2 minutes of service and about one event per case mean she cannot be the dominant time consumer in the branch; specialists, Vendor_Verify (2.1 hours) and downstream waits are. The absence effect may reflect an unstaffed queue (a design flaw fixable by cover) rather than irreplaceable knowledge. It is a single, short, uncontrolled observation.
- Predictions: waiting time before her events grows with her daily inflow (queue behaviour); exception cycle time correlates with the length of her queue; a trained backup eliminates the absence effect (design bottleneck) or fails to (knowledge bottleneck); her events cluster into daily batches rather than being spread through the day.

**H3. Organizational boundary spanner / triage broker**

- Mechanism: she bridges structurally disconnected groups—automation, Team North, Team South, the two specialists, temporary staff, and vendor-driven verification failures—converting ambiguous cases into routable ones. She occupies a structural hole: high betweenness, low clustering.
- Supporting evidence: equal edges into both teams (350/290 North, 330/280 South); only 4–5% co-case with either team lead, so she belongs to neither team; specialists return to her rather than to the teams; temps escalate to her; Temp_1 → Vendor_Verify → Temp_1 → Request Missing Information → Mira suggests she absorbs the fallout of vendor verification failures.
- Contradicting or limiting evidence: a broker with end-to-end ownership would reappear at approval or closure; she "disappears for several events" and rarely co-occurs with approvers, so she is a dispatcher, not an owner. Brokerage implies discretion, but Bot_Rules may pre-encode much of the routing.
- Predictions: deleting her node splits the human handover graph into near-disconnected components (North, South, specialists, temps); for cases with identical case_type, amount_band and region her routing target varies (discretion) or is fixed (rules); specialists would receive direct handovers from adjusters and team leads if she were removed.

**H4. Informal supervisor for temporary staff and juniors**

- Mechanism: temps and junior adjusters lack a formal content supervisor and use her as the de facto lead for uncertain cases.
- Supporting evidence: Temp_1 and Temp_2 hand over more to her (190, 170) than to anyone else; juniors receive a larger fraction of their caseload from her (290/610 ≈ 48%, 280/590 ≈ 47%) than seniors do (≈ 40%); juniors' exception share (0.35–0.36) is slightly above seniors' (0.29–0.33).
- Contradicting or limiting evidence: she performs no Triage or Approve Settlement; co-case with juniors is only 6%; the pattern could equally be Bot_Rules assigning simpler rework to juniors via her.
- Predictions: temp and junior handovers to her fall when a formal supervisor is designated; her service times on temp-originated cases are longer (explanation, not routing).

**H5. Partial data / measurement artifact**

- Mechanism: system or queue events are attributed to the queue owner's org:resource; lifecycle start and complete are both retained; "Assign Specialist" is an unmodelled system activity; the handover network was built with indirect succession.
- Supporting evidence: three arithmetic inconsistencies (handover in/out exceed her event count; subcontracting instances exceed her surplus events; exception share differs between Bot_Ingest and Bot_Rules on the same cases); an activity outside the process model; the "appears immediately after Bot_Rules, then disappears" pattern is typical of queue-assignment records.
- Contradicting or limiting evidence: the absence effect and staff testimony are real-world signals that cannot be produced by attribution errors; her events have non-trivial service times.
- Predictions: her events occur outside her working hours or during her absence (system attribution); lifecycle:transition distribution per resource is skewed; recomputing with beta = 0 and a single lifecycle transition materially changes her centrality.

**H6. Quality gatekeeper / second-line control**

- Mechanism: her Reopen and Fraud Screen update loops function as a human control catching errors from automation, temps and vendors.
- Supporting evidence: 64% of reopens; the Bot_Rules ↔ Mira fraud loop (62); complaint handling.
- Contradicting or limiting evidence: Reopen could be clerical registration on behalf of others; no outcome data links her reopens to corrected decisions.
- Predictions: outcomes after her reopens differ from the original decision more often than chance; her fraud-loop cases show changed Fraud Screen results.

These hypotheses are not mutually exclusive. The evidence is most consistent with a combination of H1 and H3 (a designed exception desk that has grown into an informal brokerage role), with H2 as a latent condition exposed by the absence, H4 as a side effect of temp staffing, and H5 as a caveat on every figure.

# 3. Risk assessment

- **Bottleneck risk (moderate, conditional).** Under normal operation her service time is small and the branch's touch time is dominated by specialists and Vendor_Verify. The risk is queue-based and materialises when she is unavailable or when exception volume spikes. The absence episode is direct but single-instance evidence. Ordinary claims are insulated, which limits the blast radius.
- **Coordination risk (moderate to high).** The specialist-return loops and the flat fan-out show that the exception branch's coordination logic lives in her actions rather than in Bot_Rules or in team structures. If that logic is undocumented, coordination quality is unobservable and unrepeatable. The low working-together with team leads means team leads may have limited visibility of what happens to their cases while in the exception branch.
- **Key-person risk (high on availability, uncertain on knowledge).** Two activities appear to be performed only by her, and the absence effect is scoped exactly to her branch. Whether the dependency is on knowledge or merely on a seat being filled is not yet distinguishable; the mitigation differs (codification versus cover).
- **Compliance / control risk (moderate, no evidence of wrongdoing).** The compliance concern is structurally valid: one resource participates in identifying an exception, choosing the specialist, reopening closed claims, and updating fraud screen results. This concentrates discretion without a recorded second pair of eyes. The evidence shows a segregation-of-duties gap and a possible audit-trail gap; it shows no misuse. Any claim beyond "uncontrolled discretion is possible" would be unsupported.
- **Workload fairness risk (low to moderate).** Her outbound distribution is balanced across teams. However, juniors receive a proportionally larger share of her rework and missing-information cases than seniors, and juniors' exception shares are slightly higher. Whether this is appropriate delegation or offloading of difficult rework onto less experienced staff cannot be decided from counts alone. Specialists at 62–71% exception share with long service times face overload if her filtering is weakened.
- **Data-quality risk (high, and it conditions everything above).** Three internal inconsistencies, an unmodelled activity, an unstable exception flag between ingestion and triage, and a misdescription of the working-together metric all indicate that the organisational analysis was assembled from heterogeneous computations. Until the log is re-derived with explicit parameters (lifecycle filter, beta, denominator), the centrality figures should be treated as ordinal.

```python

log = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id="case:concept:name", activity_key="concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp")
log = log[log["lifecycle:transition"] == "complete"]
how = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log, beta=0)
G = nx.DiGraph(); G.add_weighted_edges_from([(a, b, w) for (a, b), w in how.connections.items()])
```

# 5. Recommendation

**Change immediately (low regret, reversible):**
- Designate and train a named backup for the exception queue and record who acts as backup in the log (this addresses the single-seat dependency regardless of which hypothesis is true).
- Fix the log semantics before any further reporting: one lifecycle transition per analysis, explicit beta for handover networks, one denominator for working-together, a decision on whether "Assign Specialist" is an activity or a system event, and a stable per-case exception_flag. Publish the parameters alongside the figures.
- Add a lightweight recording requirement: when a specialist is selected or a claim is reopened from the exception desk, the reason code is captured. This closes the audit-trail gap raised by compliance without changing who does the work.

**Monitor (weekly, for at least one quarter):**
- Waiting time in front of exception-desk events and exception P90 cycle time, with an alert threshold derived from the pre-absence baseline.
- Distribution of her outbound handovers by receiver, seniority and team, and the exception share and cycle time of cases juniors receive from her versus from their team lead.
- Specialist inflow that bypasses her (a rising share would indicate either a new formal path or leakage).
- Frequency of the Bot_Rules ↔ Mira fraud-screen loop and of reopens with a subsequent changed outcome.
- Temp-to-Mira handover volume, as a proxy for unmet supervision needs of temporary staff.

**Do not change yet:**
- Do not remove or reassign Mira_Coordinator's role, and do not route exceptions directly to specialists. The evidence indicates that she filters and returns work to specialists who already operate at 62–71% exception share with the longest service times; removing the filter without a replacement (rule-based routing that has first been validated against her historical choices, plus a staffed exception desk) would predictably increase formal escalations and specialist load, as the operations manager argues.
- Do not formally elevate her to team lead or re-label her as "queue cleaner". Both labels prejudge a role the data does not yet identify.
- Do not split "detect" from "assign" until the service/waiting analysis and the routing-predictability analysis show whether her assignment decisions are discretionary at all; if Bot_Rules effectively determines the target, the compliance concern is resolved by documentation rather than by reorganisation.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira_Coordinator handles about one in five claims but is involved in most reopened claims and complaints. The network analysis is not contradictory: she is highly central in the handover and subcontracting views because she sits at the point where the automated router sends difficult cases to be dispatched to specialists and adjusters, and she looks peripheral in the working-together view simply because she appears briefly in a minority of cases and spreads them across many colleagues. She is a hub, not a team member. This makes the exception branch dependent on her seat, as the six-day absence showed, and it concentrates discretion over specialist selection, reopens and fraud-screen updates in one person without a recorded second check. There is no evidence of misuse, only of a control gap. The immediate steps are a trained backup, cleaner event data, and reason codes for her decisions. Her role should not be removed or redesigned until the analysis shows whether her routing choices follow rules or judgement.
