# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

## Handover-of-work network
Mira_Coordinator is the most central resource in the handover network (highest out-degree and betweenness centrality), indicating she is a critical routing node where work frequently transitions to or from her. This suggests she acts as a **triage broker** who:
- Receives delegated work from Bot_Rules (920 handovers)
- Routes exceptions to specialists (Omar_Medical, Priya_Legal)
- Reassigns cases after missing information (to adjusters)
- Handles reopenings and escalations

The network does not imply she performs the actual work—her events are often short routing/assignment tasks—but she is the **nexus of coordination**. The high mean elapsed time (3.5–51 min) between her handovers suggests she may introduce delays when cases require her input.

## Working-together network
Mira_Coordinator is **peripheral in the working-together network** (low weighted degree) despite high handover centrality. This indicates:
- She rarely co-occurs with the same resource on the same case for extended periods (her events are typically short, discrete actions).
- Her role is **transactional** (routing, assignment) rather than collaborative (e.g., she doesn’t work alongside adjusters on the same claim for long).
- The low co-case rates (4–8%) with specialists/adjusters reflect her **ephemeral involvement** in cases.

This does not mean her role is unimportant—just that her work is **fragmented and distributed** rather than concentrated.

## Subcontracting network
Mira_Coordinator dominates subcontracting betweenness (0.69), meaning she is frequently the **intermediary in sequences of 2+ handovers**. The top patterns show:
- She routes cases to Omar_Medical or Priya_Legal, then reassigns them afterward (126 and 98 occurrences).
- She is involved in **exception handling fragments** (e.g., Request Missing Information  Medical Review  Reopen).

This suggests she is a **hidden bottleneck** in complex cases, where her involvement spans multiple steps. The subcontracting network implies her role is **cascading**—she doesn’t just hand off once but may re-engage later.

## Performance/time statistics
- **High case count percentile (91%)**: She touches most claims, but only 22% of all claims (likely the most complex ones).
- **High variance in cycle-time contribution**: Her involvement correlates with unpredictable delays, especially for exception_flag=True cases (90th percentile cycle time spikes during her absence).
- **Exception flag concentration (48%)**: She is heavily involved in problematic cases, suggesting she is either **detecting exceptions** or **handling fallout**.

## Role-discovery or activity-profile similarity
- **Activity profile**: Her main activities (Request Missing Information, Reopen Claim, Escalate Complaint, Assign Specialist) are **meta-activities**—they manage other work rather than perform core claim processing.
- **Similarity to coordinators**: Her activity set resembles other coordinators (Alice_TeamLead, Bruno_TeamLead) but with a stronger focus on **exception handling** and **cross-team routing**.
- **No strong similarity to specialists**: Her activity profile is distinct from adjusters or medical/legal specialists, reinforcing her **coordinative role**.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

## Hypothesis 1: Formal Coordinator / Exception Desk
**Mechanism**:
Mira_Coordinator is an officially designated role responsible for routing exceptions, reassigning cases with missing information, and escalating complaints. She operates a formal "exception desk" that handles cases Bot_Rules cannot auto-resolve.

**Evidence supporting**:
- High handover centrality (82% out-degree) and subcontracting betweenness (0.69).
- Focus on exception_flag=True cases (48% share) and complaints (71%).
- Process owner’s description: "queue cleaner."
- Compliance reviewer’s note: she detects problems and selects specialists.

**Evidence contradicting/limiting**:
- Low working-together co-occurrence suggests her role is **transactional**, not collaborative.
- Absence experiment shows her role is **not purely mechanical**—cases accumulate when she’s gone, implying **tacit knowledge** or **discretion** is required.
- Operations manager’s claim that removing her would increase formal escalations suggests her role is **not fully replaceable by rules**.

**Observable predictions**:
- If her role is formal, we should see **consistent patterns** in how she routes cases (e.g., specific rules for when to send to Omar vs. Priya).
- Her absence should correlate with **increased formal escalations** (e.g., more "Escalate Complaint" events initiated by others).

## Hypothesis 2: Hidden Bottleneck / Key-Person Dependency
**Mechanism**:
Mira_Coordinator is an **unintentional bottleneck** whose absence creates delays, but her role is not formally recognized as critical. She is the **only person** who knows how to route certain complex cases, making her a single point of failure.

**Evidence supporting**:
- High betweenness centrality (0.77) and subcontracting betweenness (0.69) indicate she is a **critical path** in many cases.
- 90th percentile cycle time for exception_flag=True cases **increases by 65%** during her absence.
- Adjusters describe her as "the person who knows where hard cases should go," implying **tacit knowledge**.
- Compliance reviewer worries about **uncontrolled discretion**, suggesting her role is **not fully documented**.

**Evidence contradicting/limiting**:
- If she were purely a bottleneck, we’d expect **consistent delays** in all her handovers—but her median service time (4.2 min) is shorter than specialists (e.g., Omar_Medical: 37 min).
- The operations manager argues **removing her would increase formal escalations**, suggesting her role has **value beyond just routing**.

**Observable predictions**:
- Her absence should correlate with **increased rework** (e.g., cases being sent back to her for re-routing).
- Cases she touches should have **higher variance in cycle time** (some fast, some slow) due to her **discretionary routing**.

## Hypothesis 3: Organizational Boundary Spanner / Triage Broker
**Mechanism**:
Mira_Coordinator acts as a **boundary spanner** who bridges teams (North/South), specialists (medical/legal), and automated systems (Bot_Rules). She is not a formal manager but a **triage broker** who navigates organizational silos.

**Evidence supporting**:
- She routes between **Team North (Alice_TeamLead) and Team South (Bruno_TeamLead)** (handover edges to both).
- She connects **automated systems (Bot_Rules) to specialists (Omar_Medical, Priya_Legal)**.
- She handles **cross-functional cases** (e.g., medical + legal + adjusters).
- Low working-together co-occurrence is **expected for a boundary spanner**—she doesn’t work within a single team but **across teams**.

**Evidence contradicting/limiting**:
- If she were purely a boundary spanner, we’d expect **more balanced co-case rates** with teams—but she has low co-occurrence with Alice_TeamLead (5%) and Bruno_TeamLead (4%).
- Her **high exception_flag involvement (48%)** suggests she is **not just a neutral broker** but actively **manages exceptions**.

**Observable predictions**:
- Cases involving **multiple teams/specialists** should have **higher Mira_Coordinator involvement**.
- Her routing decisions should **reduce specialist overload** (e.g., cases routed to Omar_Medical are pre-screened by her).

**Observable predictions**:
