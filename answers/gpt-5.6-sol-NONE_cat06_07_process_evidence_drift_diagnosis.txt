**Falsification criteria**

- **Pipeline explanation:** falsified if fully reconstructed April records still show the same degradation with normal feature distributions and correct prediction timestamps.
- **Process drift:** falsified if raw event audits show stable path frequencies and part-edge times after correcting timestamps and censoring.
- **Concept drift:** weakened if performance is restored by composition reweighting and added supplier/lead-time covariates, with no within-stratum relationship change.
- **Population shift:** weakened if Partner Z/non-Z differences disappear before adjustment or are caused by label-vintage imbalance alone.
- **Label censoring:** weakened if fully matured October retains an observed rate near 0.16 and metrics remain unchanged across vintages.
- **Covariate drift:** falsified for a proposed feature if its distribution is stable within source/variant and its aggregate change is only a measurement artifact.

## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

### January–March: stable reference period

These are suitable baseline months. Labels are fully mature, unknown activities and missing queue features are near zero, variant shares are stable, and predictive metrics vary little: AUROC 0.81–0.82, Brier about 0.13, and ECE about 0.02. V3 is consistently riskier than V1 and V2, but the model appears to handle the prevailing mix reasonably well.

### April: schema and feature-construction incident

April should primarily be classified as a **data-pipeline failure**, not organic model drift. The source renamed the diagnosis activity, causing prefixes to be created at the wrong event or not created. Unknown activity rises to 17%, missing queue features to 14%, and 18% of cases become unmapped/other. The model consequently overpredicts on average—0.28 predicted versus 0.19 observed—and both discrimination and calibration deteriorate.

April labels are 100% mature, so label delay is not a plausible primary explanation. Its apparent variant-distribution change is also unreliable because event mapping itself failed. The residual cached feature and inconsistent deduplication mean that the May patch did not automatically make April-derived data valid.

### May: partial recovery with residual lineage risk

After the mapping patch, unknown activities fall to 2%, missing queue features to 3%, AUROC recovers to 0.78, and ECE to 0.050. This supports the pipeline diagnosis for April. However, incomplete backfilling of the cached event-count feature means reconstructed April cases—and potentially May records derived from stale caches—require explicit recomputation.

May is not yet proof that the model has fully returned to baseline, but it does not show the later supplier-driven pattern.

### June: real routing and case-mix change, not necessarily concept drift

The remote-routing policy changes the process mix: V2 increases to 52%, while V1 falls to 30%. The diagnosis-to-remote-fix edge remains stable, and V2 breach rate falls to 0.09. Aggregate breach rate declines to 0.15.

This is clear **process drift and covariate/mix drift**, but it need not be concept drift. The same V2 path appears to retain stable timing and outcome behavior. The mildly high mean risk of 0.20 and ECE of 0.060 could result from applying an old aggregate calibration to a safer mix. A variant-conditioned analysis is required before retraining.

### July: early supplier signal

The part edge rises from 42 to 65 hours and V3 breach rate from 0.35 to 0.46. Aggregate mean prediction is 0.18 versus observed breach 0.19; AUROC slips to 0.76. With 98% maturity, label bias is limited but not zero.

July is the transition from routing-mix drift to a likely **V3 performance/process change**. It provides an early warning that the old V3 relationship may be deteriorating.

### August: supplier-driven process and deployed-concept drift

The part edge doubles from July to 130 hours, V3 share rises to 35%, and V3 breach reaches 0.64. Aggregate breach is 0.28 while mean prediction remains 0.19; AUROC drops to 0.68 and ECE reaches 0.120.

The temporal alignment and path specificity strongly support supplier-driven **process drift**. From the deployed model’s perspective, this is also **concept drift**: a case identified at diagnosis as V3 now has much greater breach risk than historically. The change could become ordinary covariate drift if supplier/lead-time information were represented well, but it is an unmodeled relationship change in the current feature space.

At 93% maturity, August metrics are not perfectly final, but censoring cannot plausibly account for the 130-hour edge or the magnitude of the V3 increase.

### September: supplier deterioration plus population shift

The V3 edge reaches 148 hours, V3 is 42% of cases, and Partner Z enters at 24% of volume. Partner Z has more rural, older-device, and V3 cases; these characteristics were absent from the original model. AUROC falls to 0.66, ECE rises to 0.140, and predictions substantially understate currently observed risk.

September therefore combines:

1. continuing supplier/process deterioration;
2. V3-specific deployed-concept drift;
3. a major population/covariate shift from Partner Z;
4. some label-delay bias, since only 82% of labels are mature.

Partner Z is not the sole explanation: supplier problems began in July and were severe before onboarding. Conversely, attributing all September deterioration to the supplier would miss the new source distribution and unmodeled rural/device characteristics.

### October: primarily an evaluation-validity problem

Only 55% of October labels are mature, and the API backlog disproportionately excludes long-resolution paths. The observed breach rate of 0.16 is therefore not comparable with mature months. Mean prediction of 0.23 cannot safely be interpreted as overprediction; late breaches may raise the final rate materially. AUROC, Brier, and ECE are also selection-biased because the evaluated subset excludes difficult, long-path cases.

The continuing V3 share, Partner Z volume, and 155-hour part edge suggest the underlying operational problems did not disappear. Nevertheless, October’s predictive metrics should be marked **provisional**, not used as evidence for a reversal, a new calibration direction, or retraining targets.

The tests should report confidence intervals and sample counts. Aggregate ECE alone is especially unsafe because it changes with prevalence and binning.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

### Immediate containment

1. Mark April metrics as pipeline-contaminated and October metrics as provisional.
2. Repair October outcome ingestion and publish maturity by month, variant, and source.
3. Normalize diagnosis activities, reconstruct prediction anchors, clean duplicates uniformly, and invalidate stale feature caches.
4. Add a temporary operational alert for V3 cases involving the affected supplier or excessive expected part lead time.
5. Avoid a global threshold reduction based solely on September/October; it could overload interventions for V1/V2 without addressing V3.

### Short-term recalibration

After pipeline repair and adequate maturity:

- Re-estimate calibration intercept/slope on clean, mature data.
- Prefer **variant-specific calibration**, especially a V3 overlay, if there is sufficient support and persistent calibration separation.
- V2 may need separate calibration after the June routing expansion because its stable, lower breach rate changed the aggregate base rate.
- Do not calibrate October until fixed-vintage analysis shows stability.
- Recalibration alone is inadequate if AUROC remains materially lower within V3; calibration cannot restore lost ranking.

A temporary V3 recalibrator may be reasonable using mature July–August data, with conservative shrinkage and frequent review. September should enter only after maturity correction and source-stratified validation.

### Feature repair and enrichment

Before full retraining:

- add supplier, part type, expected and current lead-time signals available at prediction time;
- add rural distance, device age, source/partner, region, and service accessibility;
- ensure routing/path features are known at diagnosis and do not leak future decisions;
- add feature-presence indicators where absence is operationally meaningful;
- version normalization, deduplication, and feature logic.

If V3 routing is not known at diagnosis, use planned route/part-order likelihood rather than a future realized variant.

### Full retraining decision

Retrain only when:

1. April has been fully reconstructed or excluded;
2. October and late September have reached a defined maturity threshold;
3. label-vintage sensitivity is acceptable;
4. the supplier regime is expected to persist rather than represent a brief outage;
5. evaluation uses temporal and source holdouts;
6. the new features are available consistently at scoring time.

Full retraining is justified if same-path tests show persistent V3 relationship change, ranking remains degraded after feature repair/recalibration, or Partner Z occupies a durable region of feature space not covered by training.

### Training-month safety

- **Jan–Mar:** safe baseline training months.
- **April:** unsafe until complete raw-to-feature reconstruction; otherwise exclude.
- **May:** generally usable after verifying cache version and duplicate lineage.
- **June:** safe and useful for the new routing mix; include intervention indicators and validate V2 separately.
- **July:** usable as the beginning of supplier transition, but label the regime explicitly.
- **August:** usable after final maturation; important for learning the severe V3 regime.
- **September:** unsafe for ordinary training at current 82% maturity; usable later with Partner Z/source-aware sampling and transport checks.
- **October:** unsafe for supervised training and performance selection at 55% maturity. It may be used for unlabeled covariate/process monitoring.

### Segment- or variant-specific models

Variant-specific **monitoring and calibration** are justified immediately. Separate predictive models are justified only if:

- the variant is knowable at prediction time;
- there are enough observations and events per segment;
- relationships materially differ after shared-feature adjustment;
- temporal/source holdout performance improves;
- operational ownership can support multiple models.

A hierarchical/shared model with variant, supplier, and source interactions is preferable to completely separate models at first. A Partner-Z-only model is premature: the source is new, labels mature slowly, and overlap with non-Z cases must be established. A source-specific calibrator may later be warranted if residual differences persist.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

### Monitoring layers

1. **Predictive monitor**
   - AUROC, AUPRC, Brier, log loss, sensitivity/precision at operational thresholds.
   - Report by case-start cohort and fixed label vintage.
   - Stratify by variant, source, supplier, region, and intervention status.

2. **Calibration monitor**
   - Mean prediction versus prevalence, calibration intercept/slope, reliability plots, ECE with uncertainty.
   - Require minimum maturity and sample size before paging.
   - Maintain both aggregate and variant/source views.

3. **Label-maturity monitor**
   - Percentage labeled by cohort age; delay distribution; API queue size; outcome reconciliation rate.
   - Compare curves by outcome, variant, source, and path length.
   - Detect informative censoring when long paths mature more slowly.

4. **Data-quality and lineage monitor**
   - Unknown/new activity rate, missingness, duplicates, out-of-order timestamps, schema changes, prefix-anchor success, cache/feature version, and raw-to-feature reconciliation.
   - Enforce a contract that exactly one valid diagnosis anchor is selected under documented tie rules.

5. **Process-variant monitor**
   - V1/V2/V3/unmapped shares, new variants, transition frequencies, conformance deviations.
   - Annotate policy changes such as 1 June.

6. **Performance-DFG monitor**
   - Edge elapsed-time medians and upper quantiles by supplier/source.
   - Alert on localized changes such as `Order Part -> Part Received`, not just whole-case duration.
   - Monitor event completeness so open cases do not masquerade as faster paths.

7. **Population monitor**
   - Source/partner volume, region/rurality, device age, product/part mix, promise duration, and support/overlap with training data.
   - Report weighted and unweighted performance for new populations.

8. **Intervention/deployment monitor**
   - Model, calibration, threshold, schema, feature, and code versions.
   - Routing-policy and supplier-change markers.
   - Treatment rate, capacity, overrides, and outcome effects to detect feedback loops.

### Alert dependencies

Use a dependency-aware triage graph:

- **Label-maturity alarm active:** suppress automatic retraining and hard performance pages for immature cohorts; issue a data/label incident and continue unlabeled covariate/process monitoring.
- **Schema/lineage alarm active:** quarantine affected scores and require recomputation before interpreting model metrics.
- **Process alarm with stable same-path model metrics:** classify as operational/process change; do not automatically retrain.
- **Population alarm with stable reweighted performance:** investigate coverage and policy, not model decay.
- **Mature, clean, within-segment calibration drift but stable discrimination:** candidate for recalibration.
- **Mature, clean, within-segment discrimination and calibration deterioration:** candidate for feature changes/full retraining.
- Retraining requires approval after label, lineage, and deployment gates pass.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

PATH = "field_service_events.csv"
CASE, ACT, TS = "case_id", "activity", "timestamp"

raw = pd.read_csv(PATH)
raw[TS] = pd.to_datetime(raw[TS], utc=True, errors="coerce")

# Normalize semantically identical diagnosis events before anchors/features/variants.
raw[ACT] = raw[ACT].replace({
    "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
})

# Auditable duplicate strategy: retain raw counts, then remove exact source-event IDs
# when available; otherwise use a documented composite key.
raw["_raw_row"] = range(len(raw))
raw["_duplicate"] = (
    raw.duplicated("event_id", keep="first")
    if "event_id" in raw.columns
    else raw.duplicated([CASE, ACT, TS], keep="first")
)
dedup = raw.loc[~raw["_duplicate"]].copy()

log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    dedup, case_id=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS
)
case_start = log.groupby(CASE)[TS].min().rename("case_start")
log = log.merge(case_start, on=CASE, how="left")

def monthly_process_summary(log, start, end, cohort_type):
    """
    event_calendar: events occurring in month; useful for operational workload/edge
    conditions, but can combine cases started in different months.
    case_start: complete observed traces for cases that began in month; aligns with
    predictive cohorts, but later events occur outside the month and may be censored.
    """
    if cohort_type == "event_calendar":
        cohort = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            log, start, end, mode="events",
            timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE
        )
    elif cohort_type == "case_start":
        ids = case_start[(case_start >= pd.Timestamp(start, tz="UTC")) &
                         (case_start < pd.Timestamp(end, tz="UTC"))].index
        cohort = log[log[CASE].isin(ids)].copy()
    else:
        raise ValueError("cohort_type must be event_calendar or case_start")

    dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(
        cohort, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE
    )
    perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
        cohort, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE
    )
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(
        cohort, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE
    )
    temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
        cohort, case_id_key=CASE, timestamp_key=TS
    )

    edge = ("Order Part", "Part Received")
    variant_counts = {
        str(v): (len(cases) if hasattr(cases, "__len__") else cases)
        for v, cases in variants.items()
    }
    return {
        "cohort_type": cohort_type,
        "cases": int(cohort[CASE].nunique()),
        "events": int(len(cohort)),
        "duplicate_rows_removed_total": int(raw["_duplicate"].sum()),
        "unknown_share": float((cohort[ACT] == "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean()),
        "part_edge_count": int(dfg.get(edge, 0)),
        # PM4Py performance DFG durations are typically seconds.
        "part_edge_mean_hours": (
            float(perf_dfg[edge]) / 3600 if edge in perf_dfg else None
        ),
        "variant_counts": variant_counts,
        "temporal_feature_rows": int(len(temporal)),
        "start_activities": dict(start_acts),
        "end_activities": dict(end_acts),
    }

rows = []
for month_start in pd.date_range("2026-01-01", "2026-10-01", freq="MS", tz="UTC"):
    month_end = month_start + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1)
    for cohort_type in ("event_calendar", "case_start"):
        s = monthly_process_summary(
            log, month_start.isoformat(), month_end.isoformat(), cohort_type
        )
        s["month"] = month_start.strftime("%Y-%m")
        rows.append(s)

monthly_audit = pd.DataFrame(rows)
print(monthly_audit.to_json(orient="records", indent=2))
```

The exact `filter_time_range` mode and function signatures should be verified against the installed PM4Py version. For performance monitoring, retain edge-duration distributions from raw consecutive-event pairs as well as PM4Py’s aggregate performance DFG; a single mean should not replace the supplied median/quantile evidence.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

1. **Retraining on immature September–October labels**
   - **Failure:** Long, breach-prone V3 cases are underrepresented, so retraining teaches the model that recent cases are safer and can reinforce underprediction.
   - **Mitigation:** Enforce maturity gates, fixed-vintage validation, delayed-label backfills, and censoring sensitivity analyses. Exclude October from supervised training for now.

2. **Calibrating around April’s pipeline bug**
   - **Failure:** A recalibrator learns compensation for wrong anchors, missing queues, and duplicate-driven event counts. Once repaired, predictions become systematically distorted.
   - **Mitigation:** Recompute raw-to-score data first; calibration artifacts are not accepted unless they reproduce on normalized, deduplicated records.

3. **Ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept drift**
   - **Failure:** Treating August solely as a temporary prevalence change leaves V3 cases severely underpredicted and delays operational action.
   - **Mitigation:** Monitor V3 and the part edge by supplier; apply a temporary V3 overlay and add diagnosis-time supplier/lead-time features while testing persistence.

4. **Treating June’s routing mix change as global model failure**
   - **Failure:** Unnecessary retraining may degrade stable V2 ranking or undo useful historical learning when the main change is a safer process mix.
   - **Mitigation:** Evaluate within variant and reweight to common mixes. Prefer calibration adjustment if discrimination remains stable.

5. **Attributing Partner Z’s raw gap directly to unfair model behavior**
   - **Failure:** The comparison confounds source with V3 routing, rurality, device age, supplier exposure, and slower label maturity. Conversely, composition can also be used to dismiss a genuine residual disparity.
   - **Mitigation:** Use equal-vintage, within-variant, overlap-restricted and reweighted comparisons; then assess errors and interventions by source and relevant customer groups.

6. **Believing October’s observed rate indicates recovery**
   - **Failure:** Operational controls may be relaxed because 0.16 appears low, even though unresolved long paths are selectively absent.
   - **Mitigation:** Display maturity prominently, suppress final performance status, and retain supplier/process alerts until the cohort matures.

7. **Deploying a source-specific model too early**
   - **Failure:** A Partner Z model trained on small, censored samples may overfit and encode source as a proxy without learning actionable risk.
   - **Mitigation:** Begin with shared/hierarchical models and interactions, require overlap and temporal holdout evidence, and consider source-specific calibration only after maturation.

8. **Using future process information at diagnosis**
   - **Failure:** Retraining on realized V3 path or actual part-receipt delay would create leakage and excellent offline but unusable online performance.
   - **Mitigation:** Freeze features at the normalized diagnosis timestamp and use only planned routing, supplier, part, and expected lead-time information available then.
