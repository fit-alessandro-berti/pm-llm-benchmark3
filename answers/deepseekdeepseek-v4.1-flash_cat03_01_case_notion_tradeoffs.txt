## 1. Analytical assumptions

I interpret a **case notion** as the chosen object type used to key a flattened trace: an event enters that trace when it is directly E2O-related to the case object, unless I explicitly define a propagation transformation. I treat **convergence** as multiple business objects or lifecycles meeting in one event/object relation, e.g. P3 packs items for O2 and O3 in e13, D2 carries P2 and P3 in e16/e24, and V2 bills O2 and O3 in e19. I treat **divergence** as one object’s lifecycle being split across multiple objects or cases, e.g. O1 is split across P1/P2 and D1/D2, and O3 is split across P3/P4, D2/D3, and V2.

I treat copied shared events as **both evidence and duplication**. They are evidence of real coordination: e13 shows O2 and O3 converge in P3; e24 shows O1, O2, and O3 converge in D2. They are duplication when flattening by a participating object: e13 appears once in the OCEL but would appear in both O2-order and O3-order traces, inflating Pack Package frequency. I permit relation propagation through O2O links only as an explicit analytical variant, never silently. For example, I might propagate Order to Item via `contains` to attach invoice/payment events to items, or propagate Package to Order via `packs`, but I would compare that against the direct E2O baseline.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

1. **Order flattening and cross-order package P3.** In e13, Pack Package relates O2, O3, I21, I31, P3. Flattening by Order creates e13 in both O2 and O3 traces. This inflates Pack Package frequency by two and may make O2 and O3 appear to converge on the same activity, while hiding that the actual convergence object is P3. Harmful for order-level frequency; informative for shared logistics if P3 is inspected.

2. **Order or Item flattening and cross-order package P4.** In e14, Pack Package relates O3, O4, I32, I41, P4. Order flattening duplicates e14 for O3 and O4; Item flattening duplicates it for I32 and I41. The DFG may show both O3 and O4 having a Pack Package step after Add Item, but it does not show that P4 physically combines their items. This is context-dependent: useful for cross-order detection, harmful for independent order counting.

3. **Delivery flattening and consolidated delivery D2.** e16 Dispatch and e24 Confirm Delivery relate D2 with O1, O2, O3, I12, I21, I31, P2, P3. Flattening by Delivery gives D2 a short DispatchConfirm trace. That is accurate for D2’s delivery lifecycle, but it hides that D2 consolidates three orders and two packages. If compared with Order flattening, O1, O2, and O3 all appear to have delivery events at the same timestamps, creating apparent synchronization that is really one consolidated delivery.

4. **Invoice flattening and consolidated invoicing V2.** e19 Issue Invoice relates V2 with O2 and O3; e22 and e25 Record Payment relate V2 with M2 and M3. Invoice flattening yields V2: Issue InvoiceRecord PaymentRecord Payment. This preserves the two-payment settlement but loses the fact that V2 services two orders. Order flattening duplicates e19/e22/e25 into O2 and O3, making both orders appear to have two payments, while the partial/complete settlement qualifiers are outside the activity labels.

5. **Payment flattening and partial payments M2/M3.** M2 is `partially_settles` V2 in e22; M3 is `completes_settlement` V2 in e25. Flattening by Payment gives M2 and M3 each a single Record Payment trace. No DFG edge distinguishes partial from complete settlement. This is harmful for process discovery because the same activity represents two different business meanings; it is informative only if the O2O qualifier is joined back.

6. **Payment flattening and near-singleton lifecycles.** M1 e21, M2 e22, M3 e25, and M4 e26 are each related to exactly one Record Payment event. Every Payment case is length one, so DFG discovery has no edges and performance analysis has no duration. This is not a true process lifecycle; it is a ledger event. The apparent shortness is an artifact of the object type, not evidence of a simple process.

7. **Item flattening truncation at delivery.** I11 appears in e05 Add Item, e11 Pack Package, e15 Dispatch, and e23 Confirm Delivery, but not in e18 Issue Invoice, e21 Record Payment, or e28 Close Order. The item DFG ends at Confirm Delivery even though the order continues financially. This is safe for fulfilment questions about I11 but harmful for end-to-end order-to-cash analysis.

8. **Package flattening missing invoice, payment, and close.** P3 appears in e13 Pack Package, e16 Dispatch, and e24 Confirm Delivery, but not in e19 Issue Invoice, e22/e25 Record Payment, or e30 Close Order. The package DFG is therefore PackDispatchConfirm. It hides that P3 contains O2/O3 items and that V2 consolidates O2/O3 billing. Useful for package logistics, misleading for end-to-end.

9. **Order flattening and O1 split across P1/P2 and D1/D2.** O1 has two Pack events e11/e12, two Dispatch events e15/e16, and two Confirm Delivery events e23/e24. Its order trace also has Invoice e18 and Payment e21 before the first Confirm Delivery e23. The DFG can show multiple Pack/Dispatch/Confirm branches and an Invoice/Payment-before-Delivery pattern. This is informative about split fulfilment, but performance metrics may double-count delivery confirmation and misstate O1’s delivery completion time.

10. **Order flattening and O3 split across P3/P4, D2/D3, and V2.** O3 participates in e13 with P3, e14 with P4, e16 with D2, e17 with D3, e19 with V2, e22 with M2, e24 with D2, e25 with M3, and e27 with D3. An O3 trace can contain two Pack, two Dispatch, two Confirm Delivery, and two Record Payment events. This creates a complex variant that is real but heavily driven by cross-order consolidation. It is informative for detecting shared fulfilment, but harmful if the analyst treats O3 as a simple independent order.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

I would still create flattened logs, but with explicit scope. **Order-flattened** logs are useful for customer-facing order milestones, order cycle time, and order close status (e28–e31). They can answer “which orders have multiple Pack/Dispatch/Confirm events?” but not “why did O2 and O3 share P3?” **Item-flattened** logs are useful for item fulfilment and package assignment, especially I11 vs I12 and I31 vs I32. **Package-flattened** logs are useful for pack-to-delivery logistics, especially P3/P4 cross-order packages. **Delivery-flattened** logs are useful for D2 consolidation and dispatch-to-confirm duration. **Invoice-flattened** logs are useful for billing and settlement duration, especially V2. **Payment-flattened** logs are useful only as payment-level records, not as process traces.

Questions requiring object-centric views include: how O2 and O3 converge in P3 (e13), how D2 consolidates P2/P3 (e16/e24), how V2 consolidates O2/O3 (e19), how M2 partially settles and M3 completes V2 (e22/e25), and how O3 diverges across P3/P4 and D2/D3. These need an OC-DFG, OTG, ET–OT graph, object interaction summary, or equivalent-execution clustering. The interaction summary should expose P3 linking O2/O3, D2 linking P2/P3, and V2 linking O2/O3. Clustering equivalent OCEL executions can separate O1’s split-package pattern from O2/O3’s consolidated-package pattern.

Contradictions should be reconciled by treating direct E2O as baseline and O2O as explanatory layer. If Order flattening says O2 and O3 each have two Record Payment events, the OCEL explains that V2 is one invoice paid by M2 and M3. If Delivery flattening says D2 has a simple two-event trace, Order flattening says three orders participated in that delivery. Both are true at different granularities. Do not average duplicated shared-event performance without deduplication.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Position B: Use a primary lead object plus explicitly secondary lifecycles.**

**Decision criteria.** The lead should align with business accountability, span the most end-to-end activities, and support stable KPIs. Order is the only object type directly related to every event from e01 to e31. O1–O4 cover Create, Add Item, Pack, Dispatch, Invoice, Payment, Delivery, and Close. Other object types truncate: Item stops before invoice/payment/close; Package stops at delivery; Delivery has only Dispatch and Confirm; Invoice stops before close; Payment is singleton.

**Strongest supporting evidence.** Every event has at least one Order relation, so Order is the best primary case notion for order-to-cash. It preserves O1’s split across P1/P2 and D1/D2, O2/O3’s shared P3/D2/V2, and O3/O4’s shared P4/D3. It also exposes O1’s Invoice/Payment before Confirm Delivery, which matters for end-to-end performance.

**Strongest counterargument.** Order flattening systematically duplicates shared events. P3, P4, D2, V2, M2, and M3 cause cross-order convergence that Order traces cannot represent as first-class objects. A pure Order lead can inflate Pack, Dispatch, Confirm Delivery, Issue Invoice, and Record Payment frequencies for O2/O3, and can hide that D2 is one delivery covering three orders. Therefore Order must not be the only view.

**Residual risk.** Secondary lifecycles may contradict Order KPIs. For example, O3’s order-level delivery completion may be e27, while e24 also confirms D2. Payment partial/complete distinction is lost unless V2/M2/M3 qualifiers are inspected. Cross-order resource contention is invisible in pure Order DFGs.

**Conditions to change decision.** If the analysis is purely customer-order journey and shared events are deduplicated or down-weighted, position A could be acceptable. If the main question is logistics consolidation, Package or Delivery should lead. If the main question is settlement, Invoice or Payment should lead. If no single object can represent the multi-object process without material distortion, position C would be justified. For this snapshot, B is the most defensible balance.
