## 1. Interaction-graph description

**Node types.** Job (J1–J6), Component (C1–C6), ComponentLot (L7, L8), Machine (CNC-2, OVEN-1, TEST-1), Tool (FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3), Batch (B1, B2), Shipment (S1–S3). Events are a second node class (`Batch Cure`, `Quarantine Lot`, `Release Lot`, `Machine Process`, `Final Test`, `Pack`, `Dispatch`) whose incident edges to objects are what actually create coupling; the object graph is a projection of the event–object bipartite graph.

**Edge types.** (a) Structural O2O: `requires` (Job→Component), `contains` (Lot→Component), `batches` (Batch→Job), `consolidates` (Shipment→Job). These are static and directly given. (b) Event-mediated E2O co-participation: two objects share an event. (c) Derived resource-holding intervals: Job–Tool holds inferred from acquire/release pairs. (d) Policy edges: "B1 cannot start until all batched jobs are ready", "Final Test requires CAL-3" — these are constraints, not observations.

**Higher-order motifs (≥3 object types).**

1. *Batch synchronization barrier* — {Job, Job, Job, Batch, Machine, Tool, Component}: the single `Batch Cure` at 12:05 touches B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J1, J2, J3, C1, C2, C3. One event makes three job lifecycles rigidly simultaneous; a delay in the last-ready job propagates backwards in *waiting* to the earliest-ready jobs.
2. *Lot–batch–shipment cascade* — {ComponentLot, Component, Job, Batch, Shipment}: L7 contains C2 and C5; C5 gates J5; J5 gates B2; B2 gates J4; J4 gates S1; S1 gates J1. J1 and J5 share **no** direct relation yet are coupled through a five-hop path.
3. *Tool-as-hidden-server* — {Job, Job, Tool, Machine}: FIX-A held by J3 (09:00–11:40) while J4 waits at CNC-2, and CAL-3 unavailable 16:35–17:20 while TEST-1 idles. The tool, not the machine, is the constrained server; machine-flattened views cannot see it.
4. *Shared secondary resource across batches* — {Batch, Batch, Tool, Machine}: RACK-7 is used by both B1 and B2; its cleaning/quarantine to 14:35 makes OVEN-1's idleness non-usable capacity.

**Direct vs inferred.** Direct: all O2O rows; the E2O sets of `Batch Cure` (12:05), `Quarantine Lot` (10:20), `Release Lot` (13:50), `Machine Process` for J4 (11:45), the test and pack/dispatch timestamps. Inferred: FIX-A holding interval as a *blocking* relation for J4 (there is no `Wait for Tool` activity); the L7→J5 causal gating (only precedence is observed); "readiness" timestamps in the B1 table (the source explicitly conditions them on "if readiness is trusted"); any Job–Lot edge for J2/J5 (present only under an alternative integration pipeline).

**Compact edge list.**

```
J1 requires C1; J2 requires C2; J3 requires C3; J4 requires C4; J5 requires C5; J6 requires C6
L7 contains C2, C5;  L8 contains C1, C3
B1 batches J1, J2, J3;  B2 batches J4, J5
S1 consolidates J1, J4;  S2 consolidates J2, J3;  S3 consolidates J5, J6
B1 uses OVEN-1, RACK-7 (event: Batch Cure @12:05)
B2 uses OVEN-1, RACK-7 (event: Batch Cure @14:50)
J3 holds FIX-A [09:00,11:40];  J4 uses CNC-2 + FIX-A @11:45
J5 uses TEST-1 + CAL-3 [16:10,16:35];  CAL-3 recalibrated [16:35,17:20];  J6 uses TEST-1 + CAL-3 [17:25,17:50]
L7 --Quarantine@10:20 / Release@13:50--> {C2,C5,J2,J5}   [job endpoints INFERRED/pipeline-dependent]
Derived: J1 ~sync~ J3 (via B1); J1 ~sync~ J4 (via S1); J5 ~sync~ J4 (via B2); J1 ~5-hop~ J5
```

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

All figures are *attributions under an explicit counterfactual*, not causal proof; each assumes the rest of the schedule is unchanged (no re-queueing), which is itself optimistic.

**(1) B1 synchronization, J1.** Ready 08:10, cure 12:05 → 3 h 55 min. Counterfactual A (unit-of-one curing, oven free on arrival): all 3 h 55 min avoidable. Counterfactual B (batching retained, but batch closes when the last member is ready): the binding member is J3 at 11:55, so 3 h 45 min is *attributable to waiting for J3* and 10 min to load/close overhead. Counterfactual C: if J3 had instead been placed in B2 or a later batch, J1+J2 could have cured at ~08:35, saving ≈3 h 30 min.

**(2) B1 synchronization, J2.** Ready 08:25 → 3 h 40 min; under counterfactual B, 3 h 30 min is J3-attributable. Important caveat: J2's component C2 was in quarantined lot L7 (10:20–13:50) and J2 only entered B1 because of a **supervisor override** logged in another system. So J2's 3 h 40 min is arguably not the real cost — the real exposure is a quality/compliance risk, and had the override not occurred J2 would have waited until 13:50 (5 h 25 min from readiness) and B1 would either have split or delayed J1 and J3 further.

**(3) FIX-A and J4.** J4 at CNC-2 09:50; `Machine Process` starts 11:45 → 1 h 55 min. FIX-A released 11:40, so 1 h 50 min of that coincides with J3's hold and 5 min is changeover. Counterfactual: a second fixture ⇒ start ≈09:55, saving ≈1 h 50 min. **But** the machine was idle only 10:05–10:47 (42 min), so ~73 min of J4's wait is not explained by "machine busy" either — the residual is unexplained by both machine and tool records and may be setup, operator, or missing events. Critically, this saving does **not** propagate: B2 started at 14:50 gated by J5 (ready 14:40), so J4's 1 h 50 min was absorbed by slack. FIX-A cost J4 local time but ~0 min of flow time.

**(4) CAL-3 and J6.** J6 ready 16:05, tested 17:25 → 1 h 20 min wait. Decomposition: 30 min waiting behind J5's test (16:05→16:35) — pure sequential contention on a single calibration tool — plus 45 min of recalibration (16:35–17:20) plus 5 min setup. Counterfactual (recalibration deferred or a second CAL-3): J6 starts ≈16:40, saving 45 min. TEST-1 idled 50 min in this window, so the flattened 58% utilization understates the constraint.

**(5) S1 consolidation and J1.** J1 packed 13:30, S1 dispatched 19:25 → 5 h 55 min held. Benchmark closing lag from siblings: S2 = 25 min, S3 = 35 min, mean 30 min. Counterfactual (J1 shipped alone at the observed mean closing lag): dispatch ≈14:00, saving ≈5 h 25 min. S1's own closing lag after J4's pack (19:10) was only 15 min, i.e. shipment *execution* was fast; the loss is entirely partner-waiting, which the shipment flattening does not measure.

**(6) Cascade arithmetic for J1 (composed, not additive-proof).** L7 released 13:50 → J5 ready 14:40 (50 min later) → B2 cures 14:50 → J4 packed 19:10 → S1 dispatch 19:25. If L7 had never been quarantined and J5 were ready at, say, 12:30, B2 could have cured ~12:40 and J4 might have packed ~17:00, dispatching S1 ~17:15 — J1 saves ≈2 h 10 min *from a lot event on a component it never touches*. This chain is the strongest illustration of cross-instance coupling and the weakest in evidence (J5's readiness lag of 50 min after release is precedence only).

**(7) RACK-7 vs OVEN-1 for B2.** Oven idle 12:55–14:50 (1 h 55 min); rack unavailable until 14:35. Earliest feasible B2 with both members present is 14:40 (J5 readiness); with the rack free, ≈14:45 instead of 14:50 → ≈5 min. RACK-7 was **not** binding for B2; it merely explains why oven idleness was unusable. This is a good example of a plausible-looking mechanism that the timing arithmetic demotes.

## 4. What flattening misses

**Job.** Shows: per-job activity order, rework loops (2 of 6). Hides: all synchronization waiting, since a job's waiting for *another* job's readiness is recorded as ordinary sojourn time; also hides `Quarantine Lot`/`Release Lot` entirely (extraction used only events directly related to Job at source). Misleading conclusion: "no job exceeds 1.8 SD above the mean, so variation is normal" — the coupling makes the six durations non-independent, so the SD itself is inflated and the z-test is near-meaningless; and `Pack → Dispatch` median 1.1 h is a median over six values of which J1's is 5 h 55 min.

**Machine.** Shows: utilization, setup–process–release sequences. Hides: *why* the machine is idle; duplicates nothing but silently attributes cross-job waits to nobody. Misleading: "CNC-2 idle 10:05–10:47 ⇒ spare capacity", when the queue was blocked on FIX-A; "TEST-1 idle 16:35–17:25 ⇒ add work", when CAL-3 was the server.

**Batch.** Shows: the barrier explicitly — the ready-time spread within B1 (08:10 → 11:55) is the bottleneck signature. Hides: what made J3 late; duplicates the single `Batch Cure` event across three job lifecycles, so naive frequency counts triple-count one physical operation. Misleading: cure duration looks like the batch's cost while the real cost is the 3 h 45 min pre-cure spread.

**Tool.** Shows: hold intervals, RACK-7 cleaning/quarantine, CAL-3 recalibration as a first-class occupancy. Hides: which job was *blocked* (no `Wait for Tool` event), so blocking must be inferred by interval overlap. Misleading: "RACK-7 unavailable 1 h 40 while the oven idled ⇒ rack is the bottleneck" — arithmetic shows only ~5 min of true impact on B2.

**ComponentLot.** Shows: the 10:20–13:50 hold on L7 spanning two unrelated jobs. Hides: the job linkage under the current pipeline; also hides the supervisor override. Misleading: because the lot flattening has 4 Job–Lot interactions and the Job flattening has 0, any cross-view comparison of "how often lots delay jobs" is pipeline-dependent, not empirical.

**Shipment.** Shows: Create → Load → Dispatch, 0.8 h and 0.3 h medians — an efficient-looking process. Hides: the pack-to-dispatch dwell of member jobs (J1: 5 h 55). Misleading: "shipments add ~1.1 h" when S1 added nearly six hours to one member.

**Low utilization + long waiting.** Utilization measures the *server*; waiting here is created by (i) synchronization constraints that require multiple jobs to be simultaneously ready, (ii) secondary resources (FIX-A, CAL-3, RACK-7) that gate access to an idle primary resource, (iii) state holds on objects (L7 quarantine) that are not resources at all, and (iv) downstream consolidation that holds finished work. None of these consume machine time; three of them actively *create* machine idleness. Utilization can therefore fall while waiting rises — they are not complements.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**Narrative A — L7 → C5 → J5 → B2 → J4 → S1 → J1** (ComponentLot, Component, Job, Batch, Shipment; 5 types).
*Temporal precedence:* Quarantine 10:20, Release 13:50, J5 ready 14:40, B2 cure 14:50, J4 packed 19:10, S1 dispatch 19:25, J1 packed 13:30.
*Resource feasibility:* B2 physically cannot cure before both members are staged; OVEN-1 was free from 12:55 and RACK-7 from 14:35, so the oven was never the limiter.
*Policy:* the batch-completeness rule and the S1 = {J1, J4} pairing rule.
*Speculation:* that the quarantine *caused* J5's readiness at 14:40 (50 min of post-release lag is unaccounted for), and that an earlier J5 would have pulled J4's pack earlier by a similar amount. The end-to-end claim — a lot event on C5 cost J1, which requires C1 from a different lot L8, roughly two hours — is the most consequential and least provable statement in this analysis.

**Narrative B — J3 → FIX-A → CNC-2 → J4 → B2** (Job, Tool, Machine, Job, Batch; 4 types).
*Precedence:* FIX-A held 09:00–11:40; J4 at CNC-2 09:50; process 11:45.
*Feasibility:* CNC-2 idle 10:05–10:47 proves the machine was not the constraint for at least 42 min; FIX-A is required by J4's `Machine Process` event.
*Policy:* single fixture, no preemption, no `Wait for Tool` recording.
*Speculation:* that the full 1 h 55 min is fixture-attributable — 73 min lies outside the observed machine-idle window and outside any recorded state. Note the causal irony: J3's fixture hold made J3 ready for B1 at 11:55, which cost J1 and J2 ~3 h 45 min. FIX-A's real victim is plausibly J1/J2 via B1, not J4 via B2.

**Narrative C — J5 → TEST-1 → CAL-3 → J6 → S3** (Job, Machine, Tool, Job, Shipment; 5 types).
*Precedence:* J5 tests 16:10–16:35; CAL-3 recalibrated 16:35–17:20; J6 tests 17:25–17:50; S3 dispatches 35 min after its last pack.
*Feasibility:* TEST-1 idle 16:35–17:25 — capacity existed, the calibration tool did not.
*Policy:* every `Final Test` requires CAL-3; recalibration is triggered by usage or schedule.
*Speculation:* that recalibration was avoidable or deferrable; if it is usage-triggered by J5's test, then J5 caused J6's wait and the two are coupled through a tool-state lifecycle rather than a queue. S3 then carries J6's 45 min forward to J5's own delivery — a loop where J5 delays J6 which delays J5's shipment.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

**I1 — Batch closing rule: cure on time-window or minimum-fill, not full completeness (reduces synchronization).**
*Target:* rank 1. *Benefit:* J1/J2-type waits drop from ~3 h 45 to a bounded window (e.g. 45 min). *Helps:* early-ready jobs (J1, J2, J4). *Harms:* late-ready jobs (J3, J5) now miss batches and wait for the next oven slot; OVEN-1 cycles rise. *Cost/risk:* more cures, more RACK-7 turnarounds and cleaning windows, higher energy. *Perverse metric:* "average job wait" — improved by splitting batches even when total oven capacity is exceeded; "oven utilization" would rise while throughput falls. *Evaluation:* object-centric — measure the within-batch readiness spread (max−min ready time) per Batch object and the count of jobs bumped to a later batch, not per-job means.

**I2 — Second FIX-A and a second/faster CAL-3, plus off-peak recalibration (tools).**
*Target:* rank 5. *Benefit:* ≈1 h 50 min for J4-type cases, 45 min for J6-type. *Helps:* J4, J6, and indirectly J3 (no incentive to hoard). *Harms:* nobody directly; but the FIX-A gain is absorbed by B2 slack, so measured flow time may not move — an intervention that looks like a failure. *Cost/risk:* capital, plus calibration-drift risk if recalibration is deferred, which is a quality exposure on TEST-1 results. *Perverse metric:* "tool wait time" → encourages deferring calibration indefinitely. *Evaluation:* per-Tool occupancy vs per-Tool *blocking* (jobs whose next event required the tool during its hold); pre/post on the Job–Tool interaction count and on batch entry times, not on job totals alone.

**I3 — Shipment policy: dispatch-on-ready with a cutoff, or re-pair shipments by expected completion (shipment).**
*Target:* rank 2. *Benefit:* ≈5 h 25 min for J1. *Helps:* early-packed jobs. *Harms:* freight cost, more partial loads; the partner (J4) may lose consolidation benefits; carrier slots may not exist at 14:00. *Cost/risk:* transport spend, possible customer-side receiving overhead. *Perverse metric:* "pack-to-dispatch dwell" → dispatch half-empty trucks. *Evaluation:* per-Shipment cost per unit shipped alongside per-Job dwell; compare shipments formed by the old vs new pairing rule as Shipment-object cohorts.

**I4 — Log lot events with job linkage, add explicit `Wait for Tool` / `Blocked` events, and ingest supervisor overrides (logging, not operations).**
*Target:* rank 4, and it is a precondition for evaluating I1–I3 honestly. *Benefit:* makes J2/J5's lot exposure, J4's 73 min residual, and the override auditable. *Helps:* all analysis; quality/compliance (the J2 override on quarantined C2 is a live risk). *Harms:* overrides become visible, which may make supervisors reluctant to record them — a reporting-suppression risk. *Cost/risk:* integration work, event volume, retraining. *Perverse metric:* "number of overrides" → drives them underground. *Evaluation:* re-run `ocel_objects_interactions_summary` before/after and check that Job–ComponentLot interactions rise from 4 and that Job-flattened DFGs gain wait activities; validate a sample against shop-floor observation.

**I5 — Decouple lot quarantine at component granularity (segregate/re-test C5 independently of C2).**
*Target:* rank 3. *Benefit:* removes the L7 → J5 → B2 → J4 → S1 → J1 cascade (≈2 h to J1 under the stated counterfactual). *Helps:* J5, J4, J1. *Harms:* quality assurance — partial release weakens the lot-level containment logic; if the defect is lot-wide, escaped defects reach customers. *Cost/risk:* per-component inspection cost. *Perverse metric:* "quarantine duration" → pressure to release early. *Evaluation:* ComponentLot-object cohort study: hold duration, number of downstream Job/Batch/Shipment objects touched per hold (the *blast radius*), and escaped-defect rate — the last is the veto metric.

**I6 (optional) — Sequence-aware release: schedule fixture and staging so within-batch readiness spread is minimized.** Cheapest of all; targets rank 1 without adding capacity; risk is scheduler fragility when lot holds occur.

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd

# ---------- 1. Interaction structure: two independent frequency metrics ----------
inter = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)   # per object-pair, event-level
otg   = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)                        # object-type graph

objg_desc = pm4py.discover_objects_graph(ocel, graph_type="object_descendants")

# Metric A: number of distinct EVENTS in which a type-pair co-occurs
pairs = inter.copy()
pairs["type_pair"] = pairs.apply(
    lambda r: tuple(sorted((r["ocel:type_1"], r["ocel:type_2"]))), axis=1)
metric_A = pairs.groupby("type_pair")["ocel:eid"].nunique().rename("events")

# Metric B: number of distinct OBJECT-INSTANCE pairs (breadth, not repetition)
metric_B = (pairs.groupby("type_pair")
                 .apply(lambda d: d[["ocel:oid_1", "ocel:oid_2"]]
                        .drop_duplicates().shape[0])
                 .rename("object_pairs"))

cmp = pd.concat([metric_A, metric_B], axis=1)
cmp["repetition"] = cmp["events"] / cmp["object_pairs"]
# Batch-Machine has few events but high breadth per event (one Batch Cure -> 3 jobs):
# low frequency does NOT imply low impact.  Rank by breadth, not by count.
print(cmp.sort_values("object_pairs", ascending=False))

# ---------- 2. Object-centric DFG: keep the barrier events intact ----------
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
pm4py.view_ocdfg(ocdfg, annotation="performance", performance_aggregation="median")
pm4py.view_ocdfg(ocdfg, annotation="frequency")   # compare: perf hotspots != frequency hotspots

# ---------- 3. Flattenings, explicitly as a LOSS experiment ----------
flat = {t: pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, t)
        for t in ["Job", "Machine", "Batch", "Tool", "ComponentLot", "Shipment"]}
for t, log in flat.items():
    dfg, st, et = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)
    print(t, "activities:", log["concept:name"].nunique(), "cases:", log["case:concept:name"].nunique())
    # duplication check: one Batch Cure event replicated across J1,J2,J3 in the Job view
print("Batch Cure occurrences, Job view:", (flat["Job"]["concept:name"] == "Batch Cure").sum(),
      "vs OCEL events:", (ocel.events["ocel:activity"] == "Batch Cure").sum())

# ---------- 4. Waiting measured from OBJECT readiness, never from machine gaps ----------
ev, rel = ocel.events, ocel.relations
def sync_wait(batch_id, ready_activity="Stage Component", batch_activity="Batch Cure"):
    """Waiting = batch start - each member's own readiness timestamp.
       Machine idle gaps are NOT used: OVEN-1 idle 12:55-14:50 was rack/lot blocked."""
    members = rel[(rel["ocel:oid"] == batch_id)]["ocel:eid"]
    start = ev[ev["ocel:eid"].isin(members) &
               (ev["ocel:activity"] == batch_activity)]["ocel:timestamp"].min()
    jobs = rel[(rel["ocel:eid"].isin(members)) & (rel["ocel:type"] == "Job")]["ocel:oid"].unique()
    out = {}
    for j in jobs:
        jev = rel[rel["ocel:oid"] == j].merge(ev, on="ocel:eid")
        ready = jev[jev["ocel:activity"] == ready_activity]["ocel:timestamp"].max()
        out[j] = (start - ready).total_seconds() / 3600.0
    return start, out            # B1 -> {J1: 3.92, J2: 3.67, J3: 0.17}

for b in ["B1", "B2"]:
    print(b, sync_wait(b))

def consolidation_dwell(shipment_id):
    """Pack -> Dispatch dwell per member job; invisible in the Shipment flattening."""
    sev = rel[rel["ocel:oid"] == shipment_id].merge(ev, on="ocel:eid")
    disp = sev[sev["ocel:activity"] == "Dispatch"]["ocel:timestamp"].min()
    jobs = sev[sev["ocel:type"] == "Job"]["ocel:oid"].unique()
    return {j: (disp - rel[rel["ocel:oid"] == j].merge(ev, on="ocel:eid")
                .query("`ocel:activity` == 'Pack'")["ocel:timestamp"].max()).total_seconds()/3600
            for j in jobs}
print("S1 dwell:", consolidation_dwell("S1"))     # J1 ~ 5.92 h, J4 ~ 0.25 h

def tool_blocking(tool_id):
    """Overlap of a tool hold with another job's next-event gap -> INFERRED blocking.
       There is no Wait for Tool activity, so this is a hypothesis generator only."""
    tev = rel[rel["ocel:oid"] == tool_id].merge(ev, on="ocel:eid").sort_values("ocel:timestamp")
    return tev[["ocel:timestamp", "ocel:activity", "ocel:eid"]]
for t in ["FIX-A", "CAL-3", "RACK-7"]:
    print(t); print(tool_blocking(t))

# ---------- 5. Features + graph export ----------
feat, feat_names = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(ocel, "Job")   # also run with "Batch", "Shipment"
G = pm4py.convert_ocel_to_networkx(ocel)
# Path query: is there a J1 <-> J5 path?  (expected via C1/L8? no -> via B2/S1/L7 chain)
import networkx as nx
print(nx.shortest_path(G, source="J1", target="J5")) if nx.has_path(G, "J1", "J5") else None
```

## 8. Falsification and additional data

**Observations that would weaken the top-ranked explanation (batch synchronization).**

1. Evidence that "ready for batch" timestamps are recorded at *scheduling* rather than physical staging — J1's 08:10 would then be fictitious and the 3 h 55 min largely an artifact.
2. An oven-capacity or cure-recipe constraint showing OVEN-1 could not have cured J1/J2 earlier (e.g. shared recipe requiring a minimum of three parts, or a mandatory preheat from 08:10).
3. Records showing J1 and J2 had unfinished upstream work (rework loops, inspection queue) after 08:10, so they were not truly waiting on B1.
4. A history in which batch composition is dynamic and typical within-batch readiness spread is under 30 min, making B1 an outlier rather than a structural pattern.
5. Evidence that J3's lateness — not the batching rule — is the recurring driver, and that J3's lateness is itself caused by FIX-A; the mechanism would then re-rank to tool contention.
6. Customer due dates showing J1 and J2 had no downstream urgency (their delivery was gated by S1/S2 anyway), so the batch wait consumed slack rather than flow time.
7. A corrected extraction in which the lot-hold on C2 (10:20–13:50) means J2 could not legitimately have cured before 13:50, converting most of J2's "batch wait" into lot wait.

**Additional fields or event types that would discriminate among hypotheses.**

1. Explicit `Wait for Tool` / `Blocked` / `Queue Enter` / `Queue Exit` events with the blocking object as a second E2O reference — resolves J4's unexplained 73 min and separates FIX-A from setup.
2. Lot events consistently linked to Jobs at extraction (or an explicit `Component → Job` allocation timestamp), so `Quarantine Lot`/`Release Lot` appear in Job flattenings.
