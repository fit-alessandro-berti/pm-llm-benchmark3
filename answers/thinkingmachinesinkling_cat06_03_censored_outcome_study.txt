## 1. Target definition

**Primary formulation (selected)**
- **Unit of analysis:** One credit application (`case:concept:name`).
- **Population:** Cases that reach `Account Activated` (the funded/activated cohort). Rejected, withdrawn, transferred (unlinked), open, and not-yet-mature applications are excluded from the risk set for default because they never received credit exposure; labeling them “non-default” is undefined and misleading.
- **Prediction time (`t_pred`):** The timestamp of the 3rd canonical event (or 72 h after `Application Started`, whichever occurs first), restricted to events occurring before `Account Activated`. This is an “early warning” point.
- **Outcome definition:** `default_90d` — a binary indicator defined only after activation: whether a default event occurs within 90 days after the `Account Activated` timestamp.
- **Horizon:** 90 days post-activation.
- **Estimand:** The conditional cumulative incidence (or 90-day hazard) of default within 90 days *given activation*:
  \[
  F_{\text{def}}(90 \mid X, \text{Activated}) = P(T_{\text{def}} \le 90 \mid X, \text{Activated})
  \]
  where `X` is the feature vector extracted at `t_pred`. In operational terms: “Among applications that become active, what is the 90-day default risk based on the first few process events?”

This is defensible because it respects the data-generating mechanism: default is only observable under the activation policy. It avoids counterfactual labeling of rejected applicants and aligns with the mature warehouse feed.

**Alternative formulation**
- **Multi-state competing-risk target for all 48,000 applications:** From `Application Started`, the terminal states are `{ActivatedDefault (90d), ActivatedNo Default (90d), Rejected, Withdrawn, Transferred, Censored (open/not mature)}`. The estimand is the cause-specific cumulative incidence of reaching `ActivatedDefault` by time `t` (e.g., 180 days from start, allowing 90 days post-activation plus feed delay).
- **Why it answers a different question:** It estimates the unconditional probability that an arbitrary incoming application will eventually become an activated default. This requires counterfactual assumptions about what a rejected applicant would have done if activated, and it depends on the selection/activation policy. It is valuable for portfolio-level forecasting but is not the same as the conditional risk used for post-approval monitoring.

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

**Mechanisms (distinguished explicitly)**

1. **Ordinary right censoring:** Activated cases where the 90-day post-activation window extends beyond 15 Feb 2026 (e.g., activated in Dec 2025). The event (default) has not had time to occur.
2. **Delayed labels:** Activated cases where the 90-day window has closed but the external feed has not arrived (7–35 day delay). These are administratively censored at extraction, but the true outcome likely exists.
3. **Selection through activation:** Only applications that pass `Credit Review`  `Accepted`  `Account Activated` enter the risk set. The selection is not random; it depends on `risk_score`, `requested_amount`, region, and policy thresholds.
4. **Transfer as competing / missing mechanism:** Transferred cases leave the local system. If linked and activated elsewhere, the outcome feed is often permanently missing (`partner_origin` elevated missingness). Unlinked transfers are lost.
5. **Permanently missing feeds:** A subset of partner-originated activated accounts never receive the `default_90d` feed. This is likely MNAR (missing not at random) because partner selection correlates with risk.

**Primary analysis strategy**
- Restrict training and evaluation to the mature activated cohort (21,600 cases) with complete `default_90d`.
- For the 3,600 immature activated cases, build a parallel **survival / competing-risk training set** using right-censoring: censor at `min(activation + 90d, extract_date)` for ordinary censoring; for delayed-label cases where 90d has passed but feed is missing, treat as censored at 90d and flag `delayed_feed` for sensitivity weighting.
- Do **not** impute `default_90d` for rejected/withdrawn/open/transferred cases.

**Sensitivity analyses**
- **S1 (Censoring inclusion):** Train a survival model (e.g., Cox or discrete-hazard) including the 3,600 immature activated cases with appropriate censoring indicators.
- **S2 (Transfer linkage):** For the 60% linked transfers, attempt to recover activation/default status via `linkage_table`. If recovered, include as activated; if not, treat transfer as a competing state.
- **S3 (Partner MNAR):** Re-weight mature partner-originated cases using inverse-probability-of-observation weights based on `partner_origin`, region, and amount, to correct for permanently missing feeds.

**Testable / partially testable / untestable assumptions**
- **Testable:** Covariate balance between mature and immature activated cases (check for selection on `risk_score`, region, post-policy period).
- **Partially testable:** Whether missing feed is random conditional on observed features (test correlation of `label_available_at` delay with `partner_origin`, amount, region). If delay correlates strongly with risk features, MNAR is plausible.
- **Fundamentally untestable:** The counterfactual default risk for rejected applicants (they never received credit). Any model that assigns them a default risk requires an untestable structural assumption about selection into activation.

## 7. Validation design

**Why random split is misleading**
- Temporal trends: Credit policy changed (1 Jul 2025), migration changed activities (15 Oct 2025), and economic/seasonal effects vary across 2024–2025.
- Label maturity: Late-starting cases (Oct–Dec 2025) are more likely to be immature and excluded, so a random split would place immature, non-evaluable cases in the test set, creating an ill-defined evaluation population.
- Selection dynamics: The activated cohort in 2025 may have different risk profiles due to policy shifts; random splitting mixes these regimes and hides calibration drift.

**Temporal split (by `Application Started`)**
- **Train:** 1 Jan 2024 – 30 Jun 2025 (pre-policy-change, mostly mature).
- **Validation:** 1 Jul 2025 – 30 Sep 2025 (post-policy, pre-migration; mature activated only).
- **Test:** 1 Oct 2025 – 31 Dec 2025 (post-migration; evaluate only on mature activated subset, acknowledging that this biases the test cohort toward faster-process cases).

**Backtesting and evaluation rules**
- For each split, construct prefixes at `t_pred` (3rd event / 72 h).
- Apply canonical mapping for migration-period cases.
- **Evaluation subset for performance metrics:** Only cases with mature `default_90d`. The 3,600 immature activated cases are excluded from metric computation but included in cohort-flow reporting.
- **Metrics (primary binary target):** AUC-ROC, AUC-PR (important given 2,700/18,900  12.5% prevalence), Brier score, calibration slope/intercept, and C-index if using survival formulation.
- **Calibration:** Plot predicted 90-day default probability (binned) vs. observed rate, separately for train/val/test and for `post_policy` vs. pre-policy.
- **Subgroup performance:** By `case:region`, `case:channel`, amount quartile, `post_policy`, and `partner_origin` (if included in sensitivity).
- **Distribution shift check:** Compare feature distributions (`risk_score`, `requested_amount`, loop counts) across splits using population stability index (PSI) or Kolmogorov–Smirnov. Flag shifts > 0.1 for review.

**Sensitivity backtest**
- Repeat evaluation on the survival cohort (including 3,600 censored activated cases) using a discrete-time hazard model. Report concordance and calibration at 90 days with censoring adjustments.

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

**What the study can claim**
- Predictive association: Features available at the 3rd event are associated with 90-day default among activated accounts.
- Calibration and discrimination within the mature activated cohort, separately pre/post-policy.
- Relative risk ranking for funded applications (conditional on activation).

**What it cannot claim**
- **Causal effect:** Changing `requested_amount` or loop count does not necessarily change default risk (confounding by unobserved applicant quality and selection).
- **Selection-corrected unconditional risk:** The model does not predict default for rejected applicants because the counterfactual “activated then defaulted” is never observed.
- **Partner/default feed completeness:** For partner-linked or partner-originated accounts, permanently missing feeds mean performance estimates are lower-bound and potentially biased.

**Three deployment-induced data-generating changes**
1. **Selection feedback loop:** If the model is used to reject high-risk applications, the activated cohort becomes lower-risk over time. The observed default rate will drop, and the feature-target relationship may shift (collapsing the risk distribution near the decision boundary).
2. **Resource reallocation:** If predictions trigger faster document requests or specialist routing, queue times (`inter_event_mean`) and loop dynamics change, altering the very process features used for prediction.
3. **Partner and policy dynamics:** If partner linkage improves or partner risk appetite changes, the transfer/activation pathway and missing-feed rate change, breaking the competing-risk structure and censoring assumptions.

**Decision-use boundary**
- Deploy only for activated (or post-activation-monitoring) use cases where the conditional interpretation is explicit.
- Do not use to justify rejecting applications solely on this model’s score without a separate selection/activation model.
- Re-train and re-calibrate after any policy or system change; do not apply pre-July-2025 models post-policy without validation.
