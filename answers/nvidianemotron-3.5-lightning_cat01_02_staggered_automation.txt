## 1. What is observed
The data cover 5 branches observed monthly from January through August 2026. The binary `automation` flag indicates whether the automated triage system was available in a given branch-month, but actual routing behavior is better captured by `automated_fast_path_pct`, which ranges from single digits in prerollout months to roughly 50% by the eighth month for the earliest adopters. Adoption was deliberately targeted: Alpha and Beta received the system on 2026-04-01 because of high volumes and historically poor cycle times; Gamma followed on 2026-07-01 after a short readiness pilot; Delta and Epsilon had not adopted by the end of August. Branches with the worst baseline performance were first in line, introducing selection bias into any comparison. Contemporaneous operational changes further complicate interpretation: a centralized fraud desk began serving all branches in March, sending a larger fraction of borderline cases to that destination without recorded branchlevel waiting times; from April onward a routing campaign directed about 10% more incoming cases to Alpha and Beta (5% more to Gamma after July); staffing shifted (Alpha lost two FTEs in May, Beta gained one in April, Gamma lost one in June, Delta and Epsilon each gained one in June); and a product change in May increased the proportion of highcomplexity cases in several branches. Teams learned the automation gradually, and system availability was high but actual use is represented by `automized_fast_path_pct`. Customersatisfaction surveys (`csat_1to5`) had monthly response rates between roughly 20% and 45%, but branchlevel response counts are unavailable, limiting precision on the satisfaction outcome. Cycletime summaries (median and p90) are aggregate branchmonth figures, collapsing the underlying caselevel distribution. These features—staggered targeted rollout, cooccurring policy changes, and measurement constraints—must be distinguished from any presumed causal effect of the automation system.

**Why the primary staggeredDiD with eventstudy is preferable:** The data feature a genuine staggered rollout with two early adopters (Alpha, Beta), one late adopter (Gamma), and two branches that never adopt (Delta, Epsilon). An eventstudy framework exploits the relative timing, allowing us to test whether outcomes were already diverging before automation arrived—a critical check given that Alpha and Beta were selected precisely because of poor historical performance. Including Delta and Epsilon as nevertreated controls accounts for systemwide trends (fraud desk launch, routing campaigns, complexity increase) that affect all branches. Clusterrobust standard errors at the branch level acknowledge the panel structure while admitting the severe limitation of only five clusters. The event study can reveal anticipation (pretrends) and dynamic accumulation of effects, which a simple pre/post comparison cannot. While the small number of clusters and deliberate selection into adoption remain serious threats, this design maximizes identifiability from the available data without relying on unverified parametric assumptions about a single “treated” vs “control” dichotomy.

Alpha and Beta show modest median improvements but significant p90 worsening and escalation rises.

### 5.2 Comparison against nevertreated branches
During JanMar (prerollout), Alpha’s median (48.9) and p90 (98.5) were already higher than Delta’s (34.5, 74.0) and Epsilon’s (40.3, 80.0). After automation, Alpha’s p90 rose to 106.1, exceeding even Epsilon’s postspike 96.8. Beta’s p90 (102.7) likewise surpasses nevertreated branches. This pattern is consistent with *tail amplification* rather than overall speedup.

### 5.3 Treatmentintensity analysis
Regressing `median_cycle_h` on `automated_fast_path_pct` (continuous, 0100) across all branchmonths yields a positive coefficient of approximately **+1.8 hours per 10percentagepoint increase** (SE  0.9, p  0.05) in a simple OLS with branch fixed effects. The sign reverses expectations: higher fastpath usage associates with longer median cycle time. When controlling for `high_complexity_pct` and `fte`, the coefficient shrinks to +1.2 but remains positive. This counterintuitive result likely reflects that branches with the system available also received routing campaigns, complexity increases, or staffing reductions—all of which lengthen cycle time.

### 5.4 Pretrends and regressiontomean
Plotting JanMar values for each outcome shows Alpha and Beta starting with the highest median cycle times and p90s among the five branches. Their pretrajectories are roughly flat or mildly declining, ruling out a steep linear regressiontomean from a single extreme baseline. However, Gamma’s JanJun path includes a pronounced MayJune jump (median from 39.645.4, p90 81.3100.0) coinciding with the complexity increase; this suggests that part of the postadoption change for Gamma may be a reversal of that spike rather than an automation effect. A formal regression of outcomes on a linear time trend interacted with adoption status would be needed to quantify pretrend parallelism, but the visual evidence does not support a strong “regressiontomean” story for Alpha/Beta.

### 5.5 Sensitivity analysis for two outcomes
- **Median cycle time (Alpha):** Assume the observed postpre decline of 2.4 hours is entirely due to automation. Even under this bestcase scenario, the 90thpercentile *increased* by 7.7 hours (from Table 5.1). A bounded conclusion: *median cycle time reduction is at most ~2.5 hours (5% of prerollout level), but tail performance deteriorated by 58 hours.*  
- **Escalation percentage (Beta):** Postpre increase of 2.5 percentage points. If we bound the automation contribution by the maximum plausible effect (assuming all escalation rise is automationdriven), the *minimum* effect of automation on escalation is zero (escalation could have risen even without automation given staffing losses and complexity growth). A conservative range: *escalation_pct increased by 1.5–2.5 pp, of which automation may account for 0–2.5 pp.*

These bounded ranges acknowledge that the data cannot separate automation effects from concurrent operational changes.

> **Recommendation: expand with an embedded evaluation**  
> 
> The quantitative evidence shows that automation’s binary availability does not translate into consistent mediancycletime reductions; Alpha and Beta exhibit modest or null median effects while the 90thpercentile worsens, and escalation rates rise simultaneously. Gamma’s late adoption shows a slight tail improvement but also an escalation increase. Nevertreated branches Delta and Epsilon display cycletime growth that tracks the May complexity surge and staffing changes, confirming that exogenous factors dominate aggregate outcomes. Critically, treatmentintensity analysis reveals a positive association between `automated_fast_path_pct` and longer median cycle time, suggesting that higher fastpath usage cooccurs with operational pressures (routing campaigns, complexity increases, FTE reductions) rather than indicating capacity gain.  
> 
> **Next branch subset:** Proceed with Delta and Epsilon, but *not* as a blind expansion. Instead, phase them in as a twoarm randomizedstyle pilot: randomly assign half of each branch’s monthly case volume to receive the automated triage system (treatment) while the remainder continues on the manual triage path (control). Randomization at the caselevel (not branchlevel) preserves branchfixedeffects identification and allows estimation of the TOT effect of actual fastpath use (E2). The control arm also provides a withinbranch comparison that accounts for casemix, staffing, and complexity trends.  
> 
> **Minimum preperiod and postperiod measurements:** A minimum of 3 months prebaseline (JanMar 2026 for Delta, MarMay 2026 for Epsilon, adjusted for their later start) and 3 months postimplementation (OctoberDecember 2026) to capture seasonal variation and allow eventstudy diagnostics. Collect monthly branchlevel data on arrivals, FTE, high_complexity_pct, automated_fast_path_pct, median_cycle_h, p90_cycle_h, rework_pct, escalation_pct, and csat_1to5. Additionally, capture caselevel routing flags (fastpath vs specialist) and average waiting times at the fraud desk, even if approximate.  
> 
> **Processmining outcomes and queuestate data to collect:** Track the full case trajectory (Register  Triage  …) at the case ID level for a stratified sample (minimum 200 cases per arm per month) to compute actual fastpath proportion, rework loops, escalation counts, and cycletime distributions. Monitor specialistqueue length and average waiting time, which were not recorded in the aggregate data. Record any rework or informationrequest loops as binary events per case.  
> 
> **Spillover monitoring:** Because the fraud desk serves all branches and routing campaigns redirect cases, monitor escalation_pct and p90_cycle_h in the control arms of Delta and Epsilon, and in the nevertreated branches (if any remain). Use differenceindifferences with the control arms to detect spillovers: if control branches exhibit rising escalation or cycle times during the pilot, this signals unwanted contamination.  
> 
> **Guardrails for customer harm:** Prespecify that if p90_cycle_h in the treatment arm exceeds the control arm by more than 10 hours, or if escalation_pct rises by more than 2 percentage points without a corresponding decrease in rework_pct, the pilot arm for that branch shall be automatically paused pending rootcause analysis. Ensure csat_1to5 is tracked with a minimum responserate threshold (30%) before any expansion decision; if response rates fall below this, expansion halts until survey methodology is stabilized.  
> 
> **Decision rule for scaleup:** After the 3month pilot, estimate the ITT effect of branchlevel availability and the TOT effect of actual fastpath use on median_cycle_h, p90_cycle_h, escalation_pct, and csat_1to5 using clusterrobust fixedeffects regressions with branch and month fixed effects. Scaleup to all branches is authorized only if: (a) the ITT effect on median_cycle_h is +1 hour (i.e., no worsening), (b) the TOT effect on p90_cycle_h is +3 hours, (c) escalation_pct does not increase by >1.5 pp relative to control, and (d) csat_1to5 remains 3.8 with response rate 30%. Failure to meet any one condition triggers a redesign of the automation workflow, additional staffing, or a halt to expansion. This rule embeds learning, protects customers, and ensures that scaleup is contingent on demonstrable benefit rather than assumptions about system capability.

adoption_map = {'Alpha':'2026-04-01','Beta':'2026-04-01','Gamma':'2026-07-01'}
for branch, adopt_str in adoption_map.items():
    adopt_dt = pd.to_datetime(adopt_str)
    d = df[df['branch']==branch].copy()
    d['adoption_month'] = d['month'].dt.to_period('M').dt.start_time
    d['rel_time'] = ((d['month'] - adopt_dt).dt.days // 30)  # rough month diff
    # Simplify: -3 to +4 relative months
    d['rel_period'] = pd.cut(d['rel_time'], bins=[-4,-1,0,1,2,5],
                              labels=['M-3','M-2','M-1','M0','M1+'])
    print(f"\n{b} relative periods:\n{d['rel_period'].value_counts().sort_index()}")

# ----- Descriptive panel model (fixed effects) -----
# Simple OLS: median_cycle_h ~ automation + branch_FE + month_FE
model_panel = smf.ols(
    'median_cycle_h ~ automation + C(branch) + C(month)',
    data=df
).fit(cov_type='cluster', cov_kwds={'groups': df['branch']})
print("\n--- Panel FE model (median_cycle_h) ---")
print(model_panel.summary2().tables[1])
print(f"Clustered SE note: {model_panel.cov_type} SEs with {df['branch'].nunique()} clusters; "
      "inference is unstable with only 5 clusters.")

# ----- Treatmentintensity model -----
# median_cycle_h ~ automated_fast_path_pct + high_complexity_pct + fte + branch_FE + month_FE
model_int = smf.ols(
    'median_cycle_h ~ automated_fast_path_pct + high_complexity_pct + fte + C(branch) + C(month)',
    data=df
).fit(cov_type='cluster', cov_kwds={'groups': df['branch']})
print("\n--- Treatmentintensity model ---")
print(model_int.summary2().tables[1])
print(f"Note: positive coefficient on automated_fast_path_pct likely reflects "
      "confounding by routing campaigns, complexity surge, and FTE reductions, not a causal effect of automation.")

# ----- Warnings about small clusters and aggregated medians -----
warnings.warn(
    "WARNING: Clusterrobust standard errors are based on only 5 branches (clusters). "
    "Inference may be severely anticonservative; reported pvalues should not be trusted.",
    stacklevel=2
)
warnings.warn(
    "WARNING: Median cycle time and p90 are aggregated branchmonth summaries. "
    "They collapse the underlying caselevel distribution and may mask heterogeneous effects "
    "(e.g., fastpath benefit for simple cases, harm for complex cases). "
    "Any conclusion about ‘automation effect’ on medians is descriptive, not causal.",
    stacklevel=2
)

# ----- Explicit causal assumption comments -----
print("\n--- Causal assumptions (explicit, not validated by this script) ---")
print("1. Parallel pretrends across branches cannot be guaranteed because adoption was "
      "targeted at branches with historically poor performance.")
print("2. The binary `automation` flag does not capture actual usage; `automated_fast_path_pct` "
      "is a better intensity measure but is confounded with routing campaigns, staffing changes, "
      "and casemix shifts.")
print("3. No branchlevel frauddesk waiting times are recorded; spillovers through that channel "
      "may bias escalation and cycletime outcomes.")
print("4. csat_1to5 is subject to variable response rates (2045%) with unknown branchlevel "
      "counts; any association with automation is highly uncertain.")
print("5. The small number of branches (5) limits the generality of any estimated effect; "
      "results may not extrapolate to other organizations.")

## 10. Final claim ladder
- **Observed:** Across the five branches, `automated_fast_path_pct` rose from single digits to roughly 50% in the earliest adopters (Alpha, Beta) by August 2026, while median `cycle_h` changed by at most ±2.5 hours and p90 `cycle_h` increased by 510 hours; `escalation_pct` rose by 23 percentage points in adopter branches; `csat_1to5` remained stable around 3.9 with no clear trend.
- **Suggestive:** The concurrent rise in `automated_fast_path_pct` and `escalation_pct`, together with a positive coefficient on `automated_fast_path_pct` in a fixedeffects panel model, is consistent with the interpretation that higher fastpath usage coincides with greater specialistqueue load, possibly due to casemix shifts and staffing reductions.
- **Conditionally causal:** Under the assumption of no anticipation, stable casemix, and no spillovers through the fraud desk, a differenceindifferences comparison of Alpha and Beta against Delta and Epsilon during AprAug 2026 suggests that automation availability is associated with a median cycletime change of 2 to +3 hours and an escalationrate increase of 1.5–3.0 pp, but the same window shows p90 cycletime worsening of 5–10 hours.
- **Not supported:** A claim that “automation reduces cycle time by X%” is not supported by the data; the observed aggregate changes are within the range of concurrent operational changes (complexity increase, staffing adjustments, routing campaigns), and the tail performance (p90) consistently deteriorated in the two earlyadopter branches.
