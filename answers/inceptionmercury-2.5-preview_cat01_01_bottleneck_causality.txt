

## 1. Executive causal assessment
Verdict: mainly confounded. Confidence: 75.

Evidence For: Within every complexity stratum, Orion’s mean cycle time is lower than Nova’s (High: 31.6 vs 35.0; Medium: 17.2 vs 19.4; Low: 7.5 vs 9.6). This reversal of the aggregate trend suggests case mix drives the difference.

Evidence Against: We lack data on unobserved case risk or reviewer skill. Additionally, Orion’s night shift concentration (93% vs 25%) may independently affect queue dynamics not fully captured by arrival_load.

Proposal Risk: Reassigning 50% of Orion cases to Nova likely targets high-complexity cases. Nova handles mostly low-complexity work (87.5% of cases). Flooding Nova with difficult cases could spike queue waits, negating efficiency gains and degrading quality. The proposal assumes group identity causes time, ignoring that groups are proxies for case characteristics. A targeted pilot is safer than a blanket swap.

Arrows from complexity to routing/group are supported by "Orion is usually selected for difficult cases". Shift and compliance paths reflect operational facts. The unobserved skill and risk arrows are necessary assumptions to acknowledge hidden confounding. A time-varying feedback not in this DAG is that prolonged cycle_time reduces case_completed likelihood, biasing the sample toward faster completed cases.

## 6. Intervention and learning plan
| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Randomized routing (10% of eligible cases) | Direct group effect | Case arrival | Max 2-hour cycle time increase | Compare wait vs service split | Isolate causal impact from confounding | Rollback if quality scores drop |
| 2 | Add compliance capacity for night shift | Queue wait mechanism | Night arrival | Ensure no increase in backlog | Queue state reconstruction | Determine if delay is staffing vs process | Pause if queues grow >20% |
| 3 | Full redistribution based on evidence | Process optimization | Routing rules | Monitor Nova load balance | Variant comparison and DFG | Scale proven improvements | Rollback to status quo if Nova SLA breaches |

Recommendation: The proposal to move 50% of Orion cases is risky because it ignores case complexity. Orion is likely faster within strata but over-represented in difficult work. Instead, conduct a small randomized routing pilot (Phase 1) to isolate group effects. Simultaneously, assess if night shift staffing limits throughput (Phase 2). Use process-mining to track queue wait vs service time changes. If results show Orion’s complexity advantage is real and night queues are manageable, redistribute cases gradually. This avoids flooding Nova with complex cases that exceed their current operational capacity.

## 8. Residual uncertainty
1.  **Unobserved Case Risk:** We lack data on specific risk factors driving complexity. **Solution:** Integrate risk scores from intake systems.
2.  **Reviewer Skill:** Group differences may reflect skill heterogeneity rather than process. **Solution:** Track reviewer IDs and perform within-reviewer analysis.
3.  **Selection Bias:** Abandoned cases are missing. **Solution:** Capture all intake cases (opened/closed/abandoned) for analysis.
4.  **Night Shift Load:** "High" arrival load is coarse. **Solution:** Collect hourly queue volume data.
5.  **Compliance Timing:** We do not know exact timing of compliance checks. **Solution:** Record timestamps for each trace activity.
