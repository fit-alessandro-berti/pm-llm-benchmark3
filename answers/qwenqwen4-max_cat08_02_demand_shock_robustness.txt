| Urgent >18h (penalty) | 2% | 5% | 12%, all breached cases pre-notified |
| Routine p90 | 72h | 96h (published relaxation) | 120h + proactive ETA |
| Complex-panel p90 | 120h | 168h | 192h |
| Repeat p90 | 132h | 192h | 216h |
| Rework / >12h-queue incidence | 7.5% / 22% | 9% / 30% | 11% / 40% |
| Invariant | Urgent cases in Batch wait = **0** | 0 | 0 |

Current peak performance fails all of these (Urgent p90 33h; 42% of Urgent cases no longer bypass Batch wait, i.e. ~1,200 of 2,871 peak Urgent cases exposed to a 14h + 22h = 36h path).

**Scenarios** (varying by case type *and* disruption type):

- **S1 — Winter infection wave (mix shift).** 3,300/wk; Routine 38 / Urgent 33 / Complex 21 / Repeat 8. No station outage. Stress lands on credentialed humans (MV, specialist) and on M1/M2 at 98%.
- **S2 — Assay-machine degradation.** M2 loses ~50% effective capacity for 5 days (calibration drift, not hard down); mix near peak; M2 repeat rate rises above the observed 14.8% blended peak rate; repeats cluster after night shift. Total volume only +15%.
- **S3 — Authority absence.** Every-second-Friday specialist unavailable (48h) **plus** 2 medical validators sick during a wave. Volume/mix at peak. Note the specialist queue is already 36h at a *reported* 87% utilisation with only ~203 Hold entries/wk (5.9× normal) — a single-server capability bottleneck, not a capacity one.
- **S4 — Courier compression / delay.** Storm compresses 60% of daily intake into 15:00–18:00 (vs. the existing 10:00/17:00 spikes), or slips arrivals 6h. Mix Routine-heavy (55%) with 20% Urgent. Sample stability windows, not just queues, become binding; pre-analytical defect rate (7%  12.3% at peak) rises further.
- **S5 — Compound "black Friday".** S1 + partial M2 loss + Friday specialist gap + compressed couriers. Disruptions are **correlated** in winter (same storm causes absence, delay and demand), so independence assumptions are invalid.
- **S6 — Flat volume, wrong mix.** 2,300/wk (normal volume) but Routine 35 / Complex 35 / Urgent 20 / Repeat 10 after a hospital referral surge. Volume-based staffing looks green; specialist review and MV collapse. This is the explicit anti-average test.

## 2. Bottleneck-migration analysis

**Baseline migration chain (peak):** Courier intake (88%, 7.5h)  Pre-analytical (96%, 9.5h)  Accession correction (91%, 14h, **2 people**)  Preparation (89%, 11h)  Assay (98%, 18h)  **Technical validation (101%, 16h)**  **Medical validation (93%, 22h)**  Specialist review (87%, 36h).

Two distinct bottleneck *types* matter: **capacity** bottlenecks (assay, TV — batchable, cross-trainable, automatable) and **authority/capability** bottlenecks (MV, specialist — per-case, credentialed, non-batchable, not fixable by scheduling). At peak the binding constraint migrates *downstream into authority*, while the front end becomes a **rework generator** (defects 7%  12.3%; recollection requests ×2.9/wk; recollection cancels 35%  42%; repeat-assay share of completions 6.7%  14.8%).

**S1 (mix shift).** MV saturates first (2.6× load). Because TV cannot hand off, TV becomes **blocked, not busy** — its 101% "utilisation" partly reflects a WIP dam, and assay output backs up behind it. Relieving TV alone (I4-style help, more machines) pushes cases into a 22h MV queue and converts machine congestion into *credentialed* congestion, which is the expensive kind (Urgent penalties, Complex risk). Relieving MV (I5) migrates the constraint to **specialist review** (Hold entries ×4.1 in share, 18h  48h edge) and then to release/dispatch. Meanwhile Urgent priority at prep/assay (I1) starves Routine and Complex: Complex needs *multiple* assays, so partial priority leaves half-finished panels occupying WIP and re-entering queues.

**S2 (M2 degradation).** Constraint = assay capacity, then the **repeat loop** (Repeat assay  Assay run 10h  26h; repeats consume 2× capacity). Effective capacity loss is worse than nominal: repeats at ~10.5% of peak machine runs mean ~1 in 10 runs produces no validated result. I3 as written (all Routine  M2) is actively harmful here: it feeds the failure-prone machine and manufactures repeats, which then overload **TV** (each repeat needs re-validation) and later MV. Relieving assay by outsourcing (I10) does nothing for Routine assay load unless the partner takes Routine, and its 12h transport is disqualifying for Urgent.

**S3 (authority absence).** Specialist review is a **single server with a calendar-known outage**; queue 36h and Hold  MV 48h means Complex-panel p90 (210h) is dominated by one person's roster. MV absence makes TV blocked within one shift; TV blocking makes assay output to go; the dam propagates *upstream* to preparation, where WIP sits and samples age. Adding assay or prep capacity in S3 strictly worsens flow time (Little's law with a fixed downstream drain). The only real levers: reduce demand for the authority (autoverification-eligible subset, fewer Holds via better technical flags), borrow authority remotely, or export the work (I10 for non-urgent Complex).

**S4 (courier compression).** Intake burst  pre-analytical (96%) is the first wall; defect triage capacity and accession correction (2 specialists, 14h queue) become the real constraint. Recollection loops (42h/60h) re-enter **Receive sample**, so intake true load is 18,660 normal / 10,250 peak events, and clerks lose time explaining errors to physicians — a *self-reinforcing* loop: burst  defects  recollection  clerk time lost  bigger burst backlog. I8 (registration automation) in this scenario is counterproductive if ungated: it removes the accidental throttle upstream of a 96%-utilised pre-analytical station and relocates the queue into a place where samples degrade.

**S5 (compound).** Constraints co-exist rather than migrate: assay capacity , authority , intake variability . Priority inversion appears: Repeat-after-failure cases (worst p90, 240h) consume 2× assay capacity, so *deferring* them to protect Urgent reduces total assay capacity — the classic "starve rework, lose throughput" trap. Explicit rationing is required; you cannot prioritise all four classes.

**S6 (flat volume, wrong mix).** Volume-triggered policies never fire; specialist review (Complex 2.4× more likely to Hold) and MV (Complex 3.1× more likely to need MV) fail at "normal" total demand. Triggers must be **mix- and load-based, not volume-based**.

**Evidence caveats that change the sizing.** The log is flattened by order, so Complex-panel multi-assay load is invisible — machine and prep sizing must be rebuilt at tube/assay level. Peak TV outflow (8,090) exceeds inflow (7,240), and 1,320 peak cases (13.3%) have no END: the peak DFG is **right-censored and draining backlog**, so peak p90 is understated and steady-state queueing formulas are invalid at 101% util. Utilisation figures also cannot distinguish working from blocked. All capacity claims below are offered-load estimates, to be re-derived from resource-annotated, enqueue-timestamped events.

## 3. Robust redesign package

No-regret moves, chosen because they **reduce work or variability** rather than buy capacity everywhere. Each lists its downstream overload risk.

**R1 — Level arrivals (I7) + pull-gated intake.** Renegotiate courier slots to spread the 10:00/17:00 spikes; admit work into pre-analytical at a rate matched to its demonstrated drain. *Robust because:* it attacks the variability that makes 96% utilisation produce 9.5h queues, at zero capacity cost, and helps S1/S4/S5. *Assumes:* courier contracts are adjustable and samples remain stable in transit. *Fails:* in S4 storms, when compression is exogenous. *Overloads:* smoothing removes the idle gaps that accession-correction specialists covertly used to clear backlog — sustained pre-analytical load rises; must ship with R2 and shift re-timing.

**R2 — Early defect triage (I6) + recollection liaison.** Decide sample viability within 2h; reject only genuinely non-viable samples (urgent/irreplaceable draws get a conditional-run-with-flag path); a dedicated clerk handles physician explanations so intake clerks stay on intake. *Robust because:* the 42h/60h recollection loop and 35–42% recollection-cancel rate are the largest tail and wasted-capacity terms in the log; preventing defects beats any downstream speed-up. *Assumes:* defects are early-detectable; false-reject audit in place. *Fails:* over-rejection destroys Urgent SLA and clinician trust. *Overloads:* faster pass-through pushes more cases sooner into preparation/assay; re-draw demand lands on couriers.

**R3 — Rules-based autoverification + cross-training in the *reverse* direction (I4 inverted).** Autoverify a defined low-risk subset (non-Complex, non-Repeat, delta-check pass, no flags, stable QC). Peak already sends 4,050 cases straight from TV to Release (1,350/wk); autoverifying ~60% of those removes ~20% of TV demand  TV offered load ~101%  ~81%. Cross-train accession-correction specialists and senior clerks **into** TV, never TV staff into correction (I4 as written drains the only >100% pool). *Assumes:* regulatory permission, QC stability, 4-week lead time completed **before** the wave. *Fails:* rule drift — a winter wave raises abnormal-result share, so eligibility falls exactly when needed. *Overloads:* MV, immediately; R3 is only admissible paired with R4.

**R4 — Medical-validation capacity shaping (I5 as pre-scheduled flex) + class lanes.** Evening MV shift booked in advance of forecast waves, reserved MV lanes for Urgent/Complex, no batching of MV (it is per-case authority work), fatigue caps and structured handover. *Robust because:* MV is the binding peak constraint (2.6× load, 22h queue) in S1/S3/S5/S6. *Assumes:* credentialed humans exist and can be rostered. *Fails:* when the licence itself is absent (S3) — no roster fixes that; needs remote cross-site MV cover. *Overloads:* specialist review (Hold  MV 48h) and release/dispatch.

**R5 — Machine-routing doctrine + repeat prevention (replaces I3).** M1 reserved for time-critical and repeat-prone work; M2 for robust Routine assays only, with mandatory QC gating, night-shift double-check, and a ban on first-run Complex panels on M2 at night. Preventive maintenance scheduled pre-wave. *Robust because:* repeats double machine consumption and cluster on M2/night; recovering half the peak repeat rate returns ~3–5% machine capacity and cuts TV re-validation load — cheaper than a machine, and it helps S1/S2/S5. *Assumes:* M2 failures are preventable/diagnosable. *Fails:* hard M2 outage (S2)  routing flips to scenario policy. *Overloads:* reserving M1 shrinks Routine assay capacity  Routine tail worsens (already p90 120h vs 72h target); mitigate with R6 aging, not with more batching.

**R6 — Slack-aware dispatch with aging and capped batching (replaces I1/I2).** Dispatch by remaining slack (deadline  elapsed  predicted remaining p90), not by class label; hard invariant **Urgent never enters Batch wait**; Routine batches capped at 12 with a 4h fill window (24 only when forecast Urgent share is low and Routine slack is abundant); group setups by assay/reagent family rather than by class; age-promote any case past 60% of its target. *Robust because:* it fixes the measured 42%-Urgent-in-batch leak without starving Routine/Complex, across all mixes. *Assumes:* LIS can compute per-case deadlines and remaining-time estimates. *Fails:* Complex multi-assay remaining time is under-estimated in the flattened log  use conservative tube-level estimates. *Overloads:* less batching = more setups  preparation effective capacity falls (89%  ~95%).

**R7 — Stability-aware WIP caps + blocking board.** CONWIP caps at the MV inlet and specialist-review inlet, with caps expressed in *sample-stability hours*, not just counts; log blocked-time separately from busy-time. *Robust because:* it makes the dam visible and prevents the 54% >12h-queue incidence from hiding inside "utilisation". *Fails / creates risk:* holding work upstream can convert queue time into sample degradation (see §6).

**R8 — Pre-agreed switchboard and pre-wave readiness.** Triggers, owners, hysteresis and dwell times agreed in advance; cross-training (4 weeks), partner-lab contract and traceability dry-run, courier renegotiation and M2 maintenance all completed before the wave, because their lead times exceed disruption onset.

**Explicitly rejected:** **I9** (hold Routine releases for batch reports) — adds waiting to a class already at p90 120h to save work at Release, which is not a constraint (MV  Release 2h/5h); it optimises a non-bottleneck and inflates WIP. **I1, I2, I3, I4 as written** — each fixes one station by overloading a scarcer one. **I8, I10** — admitted only as gated scenario policies below.

## 4. Scenario-specific policies

All triggers use mix/load, not raw volume (S6). Every policy has an owner, an off-trigger and a minimum dwell time of 24h to prevent thrash.

| Policy | Activate when | Actions | Deactivate when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P-WAVE** (S1, S6) | Urgent+Complex share >33% for 3 days **or** MV offered-load/capacity >0.85 **or** MV queue p50 >6h for 2 shifts | Evening MV shift (I5); MV lanes for Urgent/Complex; expand autoverification to pre-approved rule tier; lock Urgent-no-batch | load <0.70 and MV p50 <3h for 3 days |
| **P-MACHINE** (S2, S5) | M2 repeat rate >10%/24h **or** 2 consecutive QC failures **or** uptime <85% | Quarantine M2 to validated robust assays; all Urgent/Complex/repeat  M1; night-shift double-check; offer Routine bulk to partner (12h transport fits 72h target) | repeat <7% and QC clean 48h |
| **P-ABSENCE** (S3) | Roster shows specialist gap 8h (known every 2nd Friday) **or** specialist oldest item >12h **or** 2 MV absent | Book remote specialist cover by Thu 12:00; remote cross-site MV rota; pre-classify Hold cases into "must-review-now" vs "deferrable with clinician notification" | cover confirmed and oldest <6h |
| **P-COURIER** (S4) | >35% of daily volume in any 2h window **or** courier ETA slip >3h | Burst intake shifts; triage by **stability clock**, not FIFO; suspend non-essential registration work; correction specialist roams to intake | concentration <25% for 2 days |
| **P-REG** (I8, gated) | Pre-analytical queue <4h **and** correction queue <6h **and** metadata completeness >90% | Auto-register complete-metadata orders, with a rate limiter feeding pre-analytical | pre-analytical queue >8h  throttle/off (otherwise the queue merely relocates) |
| **P-OUTSOURCE** (I10, gated) | Specialist oldest >24h **and** absence 1 day **and** case = Complex, non-Urgent, remaining slack >36h | Send 10% of Complex volume to partner; dual-ID traceability reconciliation on return; **never Urgent** (12h transport) | specialist oldest <8h |
| **P-COMPOUND** (S5) | 2 policies active **or** forecast Urgent breaches >5% of Urgent WIP | Publish explicit rationing order: (1) Urgent clinical/18h, (2) time-sensitive Complex, (3) **Repeat-after-failure early** (frees 2× capacity), (4) Routine with aging floor; stop Routine batching; activate partner overflow; daily 15-min ops huddle; notify clinicians of relaxed Routine target | 1 policy active for 48h |

Under P-COMPOUND we state openly that Routine and non-urgent Complex **will** be slower; that is the price of holding the Urgent invariant, and it is communicated rather than discovered.

## 6. Residual vulnerabilities

**Inherent, not removed:**
- **Credentialed authority is a single point of failure.** MV and specialist review cannot be cross-trained in 4 weeks; S3/S6 still bite. Remote cover and I10 mitigate but do not eliminate.
- **Recollection depends on external physicians/patients** (42h/60h, 35–42% cancel). We can reduce defect entry, not control re-draw speed.
- **M2's intrinsic failure rate** may not be preventable; R5 then just concentrates load on M1.
- **Partner lab is correlated capacity** — the same winter wave hits it; 12h transport plus traceability reconciliation is a new failure surface.
- **Measurement fragility:** order-flattened log hides Complex multi-assay load; peak censoring (13.3% open cases) understates tails; TV outflow > inflow means rates are unsafe without WIP correction.

**Created by the redesign itself (must be owned):**
- **R7 WIP caps convert waiting into biological loss.** Holding samples upstream of pre-analytical to protect MV can expire stability windows  *more* recollection and rework, i.e. our flow-control fix feeds the defect loop. Mitigation: caps in stability-hours, viability-first triage, and a hard "no cap may push a sample past 70% of its stability window" rule.
- **R3 autoverification drift and automation complacency.** Winter raises abnormal-result share; rules tuned in normal periods may auto-release something they should not. This is a *clinical* risk, not a delay risk — sentinel monitoring of post-release corrections is mandatory, with an instant kill-switch.
- **R6 de-batching costs setup capacity** at preparation, creating a new upstream bottleneck (89%  ~95%).
- **R1 levelling removes hidden slack** that the 2-person accession-correction pool relied on; a single absence there (91% util) is still catastrophic.
- **R4 evening shifts + fatigue** echo the documented registration error rise after 9h shifts — capacity bought with rework.
- **Policy thrash and fairness drift:** overlapping triggers can oscillate; aging floors can silently erode under sustained Urgent pressure.
