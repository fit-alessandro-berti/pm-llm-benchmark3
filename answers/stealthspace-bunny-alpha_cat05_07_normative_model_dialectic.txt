## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### 1.1 What is established

**M0 and the governing sources**

- M0 requires registration, concurrent Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review, an exclusive approved/rejected decision, and notification before closure.
- Within the standard approved branch, Manual_Procurement_Approval precedes Activate_Supplier.
- M0 does **not** impose an ordering between sanctions and quality review. A sequential trace showing one before the other is not, by itself, a violation.
- M0 does not encode low-risk auto-approval, emergency activation, Request_More_Info loops, resource separation, actor identity, policy effectiveness, or event provenance.
- CS-9 is formally approved and effective from 2025-01-01. It requires both checks and manual approval before ordinary activation, and requires the requester and manual approver to be different humans.
- PD-3 is evidence of a time-limited pilot authorization, not evidence of a permanent amendment to CS-9. Its eligibility conditions and two-business-day quality-review allowance must be preserved.
- The Compliance observer’s statement that the pilot might proceed as a monitored exception does not establish approval to weaken CS-9. Equally, her lack of approval does not automatically invalidate a properly authorized steering-committee pilot.
- FD-7 is a time-limited emergency authorization. It permits provisional activation without either prior quality review or prior manual approval, subject to its sanctions, spend, monitoring, and retrospective-review conditions.
- The flood emergency’s factual continuation did not, on the supplied evidence, extend FD-7’s legal effective period.
- The CRM migration changed notification evidence collection. Missingness in the onboarding log is not equivalent to proof that no notification occurred.
- Desire for Request_More_Info and permanent low-risk auto-approval does not itself amend a controlled policy.

**Operational evidence**

- SUP-701 follows the ordinary control sequence and satisfies the stated requester/approver separation requirement.
- SUP-702 presents substantial evidence of pilot eligibility and timely deferred quality review; its notification is supported by a stable CRM identifier.
- SUP-703 was outside the stated pilot criteria and used Auto_Approve for a HIGH-risk new foreign supplier.
- SUP-704 falls within FD-7’s effective window and reports an EUR 18,000 initial spend, monitoring, and next-business-day review and approval.
- SUP-705 occurred after FD-7 expired, without a written extension.
- SUP-706’s trace lacks notification, but its only positive notification evidence is a mutable text link.
- SUP-707’s additional information requests preceded activation and were followed by receipt of missing certificates.
- SUP-708 activated 40 minutes before Sanctions_Check completed, outside any supplied exception.
- SUP-711 contains duplicate registration rows with the same source transaction identifier.
- SUP-712 resolves the requester and approver to the same human through aliases.
- SUP-713 uses a later-mapped, audit-supported equivalent of Quality_Review.
- SUP-714 used Auto_Approve after PD-3 expired and without another supplied authorization.
- SUP-715 supplies no recorded conflict-of-interest disclosure, but no supplied rule prohibits the reviewer/approver relationship merely because they are spouses.
- Of 312 June–July Auto_Approve cases, 289 appear eligible, 14 are clearly ineligible, and 9 lack eligibility attributes.
- The 47 Request_More_Info cases show no activation before requested evidence arrived.
- Onboarding-log notification missingness increased from 2% to 29% after the CRM migration.
- Six additional Auto_Approve cases occurred after the pilot.

### 1.2 Disputed or unproved propositions

| Proposition | Assessment |
|---|---|
| All sanctions and quality checks in M0 must occur concurrently. | **Disputed and unsupported by M0.** They are concurrent with each other, not necessarily with manual approval. |
| Every deviation from the displayed M0 is noncompliance. | **Unsupported.** Time-bound exceptions, semantic equivalence, lifecycle scope, and logging defects can change the conclusion. |
| M0 is entirely obsolete and should be discarded. | **Unsupported.** Its ordinary route remains a useful baseline and appears valid where no authorized overlay applies. |
| The operation is broadly compliant because the observed desired outcomes were generally safe. | **Unsupported.** Good outcomes do not establish authorization, correct sequencing, or required disclosure. |
| M0 is a sufficient compliance oracle for the 2026 operation. | **Supported as a criticism.** Important conditions and lifecycle variants are absent or unrepresented. |
| The high deviation rate is “mostly” caused by obsolete or incomplete modeling. | **Unproved.** The denominator for all onboarding cases is not supplied, and the known severe cases are not explained by model obsolescence. |
| SUP-703 was justified because the operator selected “pilot.” | **Rejected.** An operator label is not an eligibility decision or an authorization. |
| SUP-705 remained covered because the flood emergency continued. | **Unsupported.** Continued need is not a written extension of authority. |
| SUP-708 is harmless because no transactions occurred. | **Unsupported as a compliance defense.** It mitigates impact, not the sequencing violation. |
| SUP-706 proves notification was omitted. | **Unproved.** The CRM message is evidence of sending, but the mutable case association is weak. |
| SUP-709’s lead was outside CS-9 and M0. | **Unproved.** CS-9 permits abbreviated rejected leads but does not define their boundary. |
| SUP-710’s prior quality certification automatically satisfied CS-9. | **Unproved.** No reuse, freshness, or reactivation rule is supplied. |
| SUP-712’s different labels establish different approvers. | **Rejected.** The supplied alias resolution identifies the same human. |
| QA_Clearance must be treated as unauthorized solely because of its name. | **Unsupported where equivalent content and an approved mapping are evidenced. |
| The spouse relationship in SUP-715 is prohibited by CS-9. | **Unsupported.** The supplied policy concern is disclosure; no categorical relationship prohibition is stated. |
| Non-supply of an event proves nonperformance. | **Generally unsupported.** It may establish a logging deficiency, but a nonperformance finding needs adequate evidence or a properly defined documentary obligation. |

### 1.3 Burden-of-proof approach

I would use an **obligation-specific, evidence-sensitive approach**, not a blanket presumption of compliance or noncompliance.

1. **Establish the obligation.** Identify the controlling policy version, lifecycle scope, event time, delegated authority, and applicable overlay.
2. **Establish the relevant fact.** Use authoritative event, identity, authorization, spend, and notification evidence. A complete log is persuasive evidence of nonperformance; an incomplete log alone is not.
3. **Require the party asserting an exception to establish it.** For an exception, the relevant evidence is the authority record, eligible-case facts at the decision point, mandatory controls, amount/time conditions, and post-activation obligations.
4. **Do not require impossible proof for an ordinary compliance finding.** The process owner must produce adequate logs for obligations that should be observable. If those logs are missing, the correct result may be “not proved,” rather than either automatic compliance or automatic breach.
5. **Separate documentary and substantive findings.** Failure to record a required disclosure can be established even if disclosure occurred elsewhere. It should not be inflated into proof of deliberate concealment or an unproved substantive prohibition.
6. **Separate process failure from harm.** A control can be seriously violated without transaction loss. Conversely, desirable outcomes do not cure a violated prerequisite.

For a definitive NONCOMPLIANT finding, I would require clear authority plus reliable evidence of the breached condition. For JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, I would require affirmative evidence of valid authority, scope, and fulfillment of the exception’s conditions. Otherwise, DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE is preferable to rhetorical certainty.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

### 2.1 Formal authority, not operational preference, controls the decision

CS-9 is the strongest formally approved source. It requires:

- both checks before activation;
- manual approval before activation; and
- separation of requester and manual approver.

Nothing in CS-9 creates a standing low-risk or high-risk exemption. PD-3 was expressly temporary, and FD-7 was expressly time-limited. Operations’ belief that a permanent auto-approval policy was “imminent” is not an effective policy.

The prosecution should acknowledge that PD-3 and FD-7 are genuine forms of authority when their conditions are met. But neither supports treating all Auto_Approve use as lawful merely because a pilot once existed.

### 2.2 The most serious cases do not depend on ambiguous model scope

**SUP-703** is not an ordinary M0-version mismatch. The case was HIGH risk and new, expressly outside the pilot’s criteria, and the operator nevertheless selected “pilot.” Auto_Approve replaced the required manual approval for a high-risk supplier. The EUR 180,000 first purchase substantially increases the potential impact of the control failure. The pilot has no applicable spending cap; more importantly, neither the risk nor eligibility conditions were met.

**SUP-708** directly contradicts a central CS-9 prerequisite: activation occurred 40 minutes before sanctions clearance. No pilot, emergency, or other supplied authority applied. The absence of transactions before clearance mitigates actual exposure; it does not convert the violation into compliance.

**SUP-712** satisfies the displayed control flow but violates an express CS-9 rule: the requester and manual approver are the same human. M0’s silence is a modeling defect, not a defense to the underlying standard.

**SUP-714** occurred after PD-3’s July 31 expiry. Its low-risk status and successful checks do not satisfy the ordinary manual-approval requirement. Six first-week-August cases increase the concern that a temporary exception became normalized practice.

These cases distinguish a genuine objection to model obsolescence from an attempt to excuse control failures through retrospective policy arguments.

### 2.3 M0 can hide relevant failures and misstate operational coverage

A M0-only review misses or underrepresents:

- PD-3 and FD-7 route differences;
- requester/approver identity in SUP-712;
- CRM notification evidence in SUP-706;
- duplicate event provenance in SUP-711;
- actor alias resolution;
- approved label equivalence in SUP-713;
- Request_More_Info cycles in SUP-707; and
- lifecycle scope questions in SUP-709 and SUP-710.

The issue is not that M0 permits sanctions after activation. It does not. Rather, the unmodeled rules and overlays make a simple trace comparison an unreliable system compliance measure. Matching M0 must not be mistaken for full compliance, and failing to match M0 must not be mistaken for proof of misconduct.

### 2.4 Expiry is an objective control condition, not an administrative technicality

SUP-704 is a plausible authorized emergency case. SUP-705 is different: the directive expired on July 12, and no written extension is supplied. The supplier’s continuing criticality and the relatively small initial spend may mitigate the decision to deviate, but they do not extend the directive.

The six August Auto_Approve cases are a second normalization signal. Combining temporary practice, operational expectations, and persistent use without a controlling amendment would replace governance with habit.

### 2.5 The aggregate evidence demonstrates both exception volume and real misuse

The 312 Auto_Approve cases cannot all be called violations:

- 289 appear eligible;
- 14 are clearly ineligible; and
- 9 remain unadjudicated.

That is important restraint. At the same time, the 14 known ineligible cases prove that eligibility was not reliably enforced. The nine missing-attribute cases should not be silently counted as compliant or as proven violations.

Likewise, the rise in notification missingness from 2% to 29% is not a 29% proven non-notification rate. It is strong evidence of a post-migration evidence-control problem, compounded by roughly 70% mirroring rather than a complete authoritative notification integration.

### 2.6 Desirable behavior does not retroactively amend policy

The 47 information-request cases contain no evidence of premature activation. Compliance agrees that the requests are desirable. That supports a model improvement, not a prosecution for 47 unauthorized control deviations.

But the same principle cuts both ways:

- a certificate arriving before activation does not retroactively legalize an otherwise unauthorized approval route;
- a critical flood need does not retroactively extend FD-7;
- a safe low-risk activation does not create permanent auto-approval authority; and
- successful sanctions clearance after activation does not cure activation before clearance.

The prosecution’s strongest position is therefore **targeted and process-aware**: preserve the ordinary standard, reject unauthorized overrides, and refuse to let favorable outcomes rewrite the governing rules.

## 3. Defense Brief

### 3.1 M0 is incomplete, but its ordinary route remains meaningful

M0 correctly expresses a defensible standard design:

- sanctions and quality review may run concurrently;
- either check may finish first;
- a selected approved path requires manual approval before activation; and
- a rejected path need not perform quality review if rejection occurs at sanctions pre-screen.

It would therefore be wrong to classify SUP-701 or the sequential ordering of checks in an ordinary case as noncompliance merely because the trace is not a literal simultaneous pair.

The defense should not claim that the entire model is obsolete. A more accurate conclusion is:

- the ordinary CS-9 route remains valid;
- PD-3 and FD-7 require conditional overlays or route variants;
- lifecycle scope, clarification loops, identity, and provenance require additions; and
- a single, unversioned M0 trace comparison is not a sufficient 2026 compliance model.

### 3.2 PD-3 and FD-7 are evidence of controlled operational change

The defense can reasonably argue that:

- SUP-702 satisfies the supplied pilot criteria;
- quality review occurred the next business day, within the permitted delay;
- sanctions and auto-approval preceded activation; and
- the stable CRM notification identifier is stronger evidence than the absence of a mirrored onboarding-log event.

Likewise, SUP-704 occurred within FD-7, had initial spend below EUR 20,000, and included the reported monitoring and next-business-day reviews.

These cases show that a M0-only oracle would misclassify authorized exceptions as deviations. They support a layered architecture, not abandonment of control.

The defense must nevertheless concede that pilot eligibility, validity dates, spend aggregation, retrospective deadlines, and monitoring cannot be inferred merely from a branch label or an event order.

### 3.3 Request_More_Info is a model omission, not demonstrated misconduct

M0 has no loop or Request_More_Info activity. SUP-707 therefore does not conform literally to M0, but CS-9 does not prohibit clarification requests. The requests obtained missing certificates before approval, and all 47 observed cases behaved similarly.

The appropriate conclusion is that M0 understates a desirable and apparently controlled intake practice. A new model should represent repeatable requests and an evidence-sufficiency exit condition. Historic cases should not be marked noncompliant merely because a new model now represents behavior that previously occurred outside the old syntax.

### 3.4 The CRM migration weakens negative inferences from the onboarding log

The increase from 2% to 29% missingness, against an approximate 70% mirroring rate, shows that the log ceased to be a complete notification source.

For SUP-702, a stable CRM identifier is credible linked-event evidence. For SUP-706, a mutable text field entered two days later is weaker, but it is still some evidence of a message. Neither case supports a confident finding that notification never occurred.

The correct remedy is authoritative event integration and immutable linkage—not converting every absent mirror into an allegation of misconduct.

### 3.5 Lifecycle scope and semantic equivalence defeat naïve trace judgments

**Rejected leads — SUP-709.** CS-9 permits abbreviated pre-screen processing, and the trace precisely matches M0’s rejected branch. The process owner’s intake interpretation is plausible, although the model owner’s contrary interpretation prevents treating exclusion as established. A scope document is required.

**Reactivations — SUP-710.** A previously valid certification may make event repetition unnecessary for some legitimate lifecycle process. That does not prove CS-9 exempts reactivation, but it does mean that absence of a Quality_Review event cannot automatically be equated with absence of quality assurance. The case lacks the lifecycle and freshness evidence needed to decide.

**Label equivalence — SUP-713.** A literal M0 label match is not the same as control equivalence. The procedure and UI were renamed, and the audit record supports equivalent content. A mapping approved after the event can be valid historical classification evidence when it documents equivalence; it should not be presented as a policy amendment.

### 3.6 Trace deviation and business misconduct are different findings

The defense is strongest on:

- SUP-706’s weak notification linkage;
- SUP-711’s migration duplicate;
- SUP-713’s label change;
- SUP-707’s permitted clarification behavior; and
- the uncertainty over SUP-709 and SUP-710.

It is weak on:

- SUP-703’s acknowledged ineligibility;
- SUP-708’s activation before sanctions clearance;
- SUP-712’s same-person approval;
- SUP-714’s post-pilot auto-approval; and
- SUP-705’s reliance on an expired directive.

Neither the prosecution nor the defense can explain these away by saying that M0 was incomplete. The defense’s strongest point is narrower: **M0’s incompleteness makes crude deviation statistics misleading, while the known breaches still require case-level findings.**

### 5.2 System-level judgment

**Operational compliance: materially noncompliant in identifiable, systemic respects — severity HIGH, confidence MODERATE.**

This is not a finding that all 312 Auto_Approve cases were violations:

- 289 appear eligible;
- 14 are clearly ineligible;
- 9 require evidence resolution; and
- the 289 still require individual verification of pre-activation validation, deadlines, and notification.

The systemic concern is nevertheless real: eligibility enforcement failed in known cases, temporary pilot practice continued after expiry, and at least one HIGH-risk activation bypassed sanctions. The CRM transition also created a substantial evidence-control weakness.

**Model validity: M0 is incomplete and invalid as the sole operational compliance model, but it is not wholly obsolete — severity HIGH, confidence HIGH.**

M0’s ordinary topology is sound as a baseline. Its deficiencies are:

- no conditional low-risk or emergency route;
- no policy/effective-date binding;
- no resource or identity constraint;
- no clarification loop;
- no authoritative notification provenance;
- no lifecycle scoping for rejected leads or reactivations;
- no explicit withdrawal/cancellation treatment; and
- no demonstrated safe mechanism for distinguishing branch selection from skipped XOR alternatives.

A revised model is needed, but adopting it must not retrospectively convert historical deviations into authorized conduct. Each case must be evaluated under the authority effective when the relevant act occurred.

### 5.3 What I accept and reject from each side

**Accepted from the prosecution**

- Formal authority and expiry matter.
- Known ineligibility, activation before sanctions, same-person approval, and post-pilot auto-approval are genuine violations.
- M0-only matching misses material non-control-flow requirements.
- Good outcomes and stakeholder preferences cannot amend policy.

**Rejected from the prosecution**

- That every M0 deviation is a breach.
- That all Auto_Approve cases are unlawful.
- That missing notification mirrors prove missing notifications.
- That concurrent checks require simultaneous execution or a particular sequential order.
- That emergency and pilot exceptions are nonexistent merely because CS-9 was not amended.

**Accepted from the defense**

- M0 is incomplete and overapplied as a universal oracle.
- Pilot and emergency cases require conditional routes.
- Clarification cycles and semantic label equivalence are legitimate model needs.
- CRM and lifecycle evidence prevent naïve trace-to-breach conclusions.
- Trace deviation is not itself evidence of bad faith or harmful conduct.

**Rejected from the defense**

- That continued flood need extends an expired directive.
- That normalized practice or anticipated permanent approval creates authority.
- That a missing quality event proves—or cures—missing quality assurance.
- That M0’s incompleteness explains SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-712, or SUP-714.
- That favorable outcomes or a small initial spend erase the need for authority and controls.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

### 6.1 Layered control model

The normative architecture should distinguish:

1. **Base policy:** CS-9 and the ordinary M0 route.
2. **Authorized temporary overlays:** PD-3 and FD-7, each independently versioned and bounded.
3. **Procedural and semantic mappings:** equivalent activity names, evidence-request procedures, and data corrections.
4. **Lifecycle scope rules:** intake, accepted onboarding, reactivation, reactivation with reusable evidence, and withdrawal.
5. **Runtime controls:** identity, delegation, amounts, deadlines, notifications, monitoring, and evidence completeness.

A pilot or emergency overlay must not overwrite history. It should be attached to a case through a recorded policy binding, with its authority, eligibility snapshot, and conditions preserved.

### 6.2 Revised control-flow structure

For the **ordinary accepted-onboarding route**:

- register or record an accepted onboarding entry event;
- perform any necessary clarification loop;
- authorize the ordinary route;
- run Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review concurrently;
- record both results;
- choose manual approval followed by activation, or rejection;
- reject without the approval activity being executed;
- converge through a route-specific completion join;
- notify the requester; and
- close.

For the **low-risk pilot route**:

- require a pilot eligibility snapshot before route authorization;
- require both Sanctions_Check and Master_Data_Validation before activation;
- use Auto_Approve as the approval event;
- activate provisionally;
- perform Quality_Review within the deadline;
- verify post-activation conditions;
- notify; and
- close.

For the **emergency route**:

- require critical-relief eligibility and emergency authority;
- require Sanctions_Check before provisional activation;
- record activation restrictions and applicable spend controls;
- activate provisionally without prior manual approval or prior quality review;
- perform Quality_Review and retrospective manual approval within their applicable deadlines;
- verify monitoring and spend evidence;
- notify; and
- close through an emergency closeout verification.

These routes must be mutually exclusive for a case and must return to a common notification/closure tail. A naïve XOR that permits activation to be skipped while notification and closure proceed independently is insufficient.

A future permanent low-risk branch should be added only after a controlled amendment. The model can represent the route, but the engine must not enable it merely because pilot history exists.

### 6.3 Policy-overlay table

The following table normalizes the supplied authority. Dates are inclusive; the engine should convert the final date into an exclusive boundary using the authoritative policy calendar and time zone.

|---|---|---|---|
| **CS-9-BASE** | CS-9; effective 2025-01-01 onward; no supplied expiry | All in-scope supplier onboarding; Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review complete before activation. Notify/close follow the applicable lifecycle model. |
| **PD3-PILOT** | PD-3; 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31 | LOW-risk DE supplier belonging to an approved corporate group, established by a pre-decision snapshot; Sanctions_Check and Master_Data_Validation complete before activation. | Auto_Approve instead of prior manual approval; provisional activation allowed; Quality_Review complete by the second business day afterward. The emergency spending rule should be normalized as an **aggregate case-level spend constraint until retrospective approval** if that is the intended approved meaning. If the source owner intended a per-order or other allocation rule, that ambiguity must be resolved explicitly rather than silently changing the obligation.

### 6.4 Data-aware eligibility, amount, and time rules

A case should be bound to a policy version using:

- event/fact time, not data-migration time;
- lifecycle scope;
- risk and country as-of the decision;
- approved corporate-group membership as-of the decision;
- master-data dataset version;
- authority reference and issuer;
- applicable rule/version IDs; and
- explicit exception conditions and outcomes.

The engine should enforce, among other conditions:

- standard check completion times no later than activation start;
- manual approval completion no later than ordinary activation start;
- pilot eligibility before Auto_Approve;
- pilot Sanctions_Check and Master_Data_Validation before provisional activation;
- quality completion no later than two business days after provisional activation, using an authoritative business calendar;
- emergency retrospective approval and quality completion within the applicable deadline;
- aggregate eligible emergency spend not exceeding EUR 20,000 before retrospective approval;
- complete required daily monitoring records; and
- a valid effective authority at the relevant act time.

Missing data should not default to eligibility. For example, the nine Auto_Approve cases with missing eligibility attributes should remain unresolved or fail a pre-activation eligibility gate, not be silently accepted through a permissive default.

### 6.5 Resource and conflict-of-interest rules

The system should distinguish:

- requester;
- reviewer;
- manual approver;
- operational executor;
- delegate;
- authorizing official; and
- monitoring/control reviewer.

The ordinary separation test is between the **actual human requester and actual human manual approver**. Different usernames or aliases do not establish separation.

Delegation evidence should identify:

- delegator and delegate;
- authority scope;
- effective period;
- task/case coverage;
- approval limits; and
- immutable recorded time.

Delegation does not turn one human into two. Whether a delegated requester may act as approver must be governed explicitly, not inferred.

For conflicts:

- preserve the existing disclosure obligation;
- record disclosure, review, and disposition separately;
- do not invent a categorical spouse prohibition;
- do not infer a conflict from surname or address alone; and
- escalate if the requester/approver separation rule is also implicated.

### 6.6 Explicit lifecycle scope

**Rejected leads**

- Create a separately identified Lead_Pre_Screen scope if CS-9’s abbreviated process is formally adopted.
- Record registration/intake, pre-screen outcome, notification where applicable, and closure.
- A rejected lead need not perform ordinary Quality_Review.
- Acceptance into supplier onboarding must trigger a distinct, recorded onboarding-entry event; a lead cannot avoid checks by remaining classified as a lead.

Until this scope is approved, SUP-709 remains an unresolved scope case rather than a unilateral exemption.

**Reactivations**

- Distinguish new onboarding, reactivation, renewal, and reactivation requiring fresh checks.
- Record the lifecycle decision and evidence-reuse decision.
- A prior quality certification may count only if a controlled rule establishes freshness, equivalence, acceptable changed facts, and the required control treatment.
- Absent such a rule, assume the base check obligations rather than infer exemption.

**Withdrawals**

- A withdrawal before activation is a cancellation, not a sanctions rejection.
- A provisional supplier that is withdrawn requires a recorded cancellation/deactivation state and any required communications.
- Withdrawal after full activation is a supplier deactivation process, not merely closure of the onboarding case.
- No supplied policy authorizes bypassing notification or evidence preservation; any new exception requires approval.

### 6.7 Allocation of controls

| Control type | POWL / process model | DECLARE or log-skeleton constraints | pandas/data validation | Organizational controls |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Concurrency, sequence, loops, XOR, closure | Natural fit for standard check concurrency, route topology, RFI loop, and notification-before-close. | Exactly one route authorization and one completed decision outcome; correct activity membership; RFI cardinality. | Replay legal orders without incorrectly requiring a fixed sanctions/quality sequence. | Procedure ownership, training, and route configuration review. |
| Risk, country, group, dates | Not reliably expressible as ordinary POWL structure. | Require route-authority and snapshot evidence events before dependent activity. | Join effective policy versions, eligibility snapshots, and event timestamps. | Approved policy ownership, authority delegation, change control, and sunset review. |
| Amounts and monitoring | Approximate route placement only. | Require spend/monitoring evidence before emergency closeout. | Aggregate spend, reconcile authorization and settlement events, calculate deadlines and monitoring coverage. | Procurement blocks, transaction monitoring, exception escalation, and reconciliation. |
| Identity and resources | Not a native control-flow guarantee. | Require requester, approver, and identity-resolution evidence. | Resolve aliases with effective dates; validate delegation and separation. | Segregation of duties, access administration, identity ownership, and conflict review. |
| Notification | Require Notify_Requester before Close_Case. | Require a stable linked notification evidence event. | Validate provider linkage, delivery/send status, and completeness. | CRM authoritative-event integration, retention, and notification service ownership. |
| Historic corrections | Should not fabricate activities. | Allow append-only corrections and supersession links. | Deduplicate by source identity, retain originals, and label derived events. | Audit approval for corrections and migration reconciliation. |

DECLARE/log-skeleton rules can express event presence, absence, counts, and mutual exclusion, but they do not replace a temporal, arithmetic, identity, or authority-aware evaluator.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

### 7.1 Executable model

The code below builds:

- concurrent standard Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review;
- a standard manual-approval route;
- a pilot Auto_Approve route with pre-activation master-data validation;
- an emergency provisional route with retrospective approval;
- a genuine Request_More_Info loop;
- rejection and route-specific completion joins;
- notification before closure; and
- route-specific post-activation checks.

**Limitations are intentional and explicit:**

- POWL alone does not enforce risk, country, corporate-group, amount, effective-date, identity, delegation, or authority conditions.
- The XOR is a possible-choice construct; the executing engine must permit only one authorized route and must not treat an unexecuted branch as evidence that the other branch occurred.
- The clarification loop is front-loaded. It is a defensible approximation for requests before the terminal approval decision. A request that changes an already evaluated check result should return to the relevant check or invalidate the route authorization; that semantic routing is outside a single static POWL.
- The code builds a model and rule manifest. It does **not** claim to decide real cases from static rows.
- No historical event is synthesized merely to fit this model.
- Repeated emergency monitoring is represented by a closeout evidence task whose referenced monitoring ledger must cover every required period. The data engine, not one POWL activity occurrence, enforces per-day completeness.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    ProcessModel,
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    SilentTransition,
    Transition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Helpers: all model construction is local; no external files are read.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------

def sequence(nodes):
    """A fully ordered partial-order fragment."""
    result = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    for source, target in zip(nodes, nodes[1:]):
        result.order.add_edge(source, target)
    return result


def parallel(nodes):
    """Concurrent activities: deliberately no edges between them."""
    return StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))


def intake_clarification_loop():
    """
    A real cycle:
        Assessment -> choose request or continue
        Request -> Response -> Assessment

    The static graph permits repetition. Runtime guards must require:
      - a valid open request before Response;
      - response/evidence references;
      - a finite continuation decision; and
      - evidence sufficiency before route authorization.
    """
    assessment = Transition("Intake_Assessment")
    request = Transition("Request_More_Info")
    response = Transition("More_Info_Response")
    loop_head = SilentTransition()
    continue_silently = SilentTransition()
    continuation_join = SilentTransition()

    choice = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[request, continue_silently],
    )

    loop = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[
            assessment,
            loop_head,
            choice,
            continuation_join,
            request,
            response,
        ]
    )

    for source, target in [
        (assessment, loop_head),
        (loop_head, choice),
        (choice, continuation_join),
        (request, response),
        (response, assessment),
    ]:
        loop.order.add_edge(source, target)

    return loop


def authorized_outcome(preconditions, approval_label, route_name):
    """
    Preconditions -> exclusive approved/rejected outcome.
    Each outcome reaches its own silent join before the common tail.
    Activation is therefore on the approved path, not a skippable
    side branch while notification proceeds independently.
    """
    approval = Transition(approval_label)
    activation = Transition("Activate_Supplier")

    approved_path = sequence([approval, activation])
    rejected_path = Transition("Reject_Supplier")

    decision = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[approved_path, rejected_path],
    )

    approved_join = SilentTransition()
    rejected_join = SilentTransition()

    decision_fragment = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[decision, approved_join, rejected_join]
    )
    decision_fragment.order.add_edge(decision, approved_join)
    decision_fragment.order.add_edge(decision, rejected_join)

    result = sequence([preconditions, decision_fragment])

    if route_name == "STANDARD":
        return result

    if route_name == "PILOT":
        postconditions = sequence(
            [
                activation,
                Transition("Quality_Review"),
                Transition("Pilot_Postconditions_Verified"),
            ]
        )
    elif route_name == "EMERGENCY":
        retrospective_approval = Transition("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
        postconditions = sequence(
            [
                activation,
                parallel(
                    [
                        Transition("Quality_Review"),
                        retrospective_approval,
                    ]
                ),
                Transition("Emergency_Monitoring_Attestation"),
                Transition("Emergency_Closeout_Verified"),
            ]
        )
    else:
        raise ValueError("Unknown route")

    return sequence([result, postconditions])
```

### 7.2 Event and data-collection proposal

#### Core event table

`case_event` should be append-only:

- `event_id`
- `case_id`
- `activity_name`, `activity_version`
- `event_type`
- `occurred_at`
- `recorded_at`
- `source_system`, `source_event_id`
- `actor_id`
- `route_id`, `policy_overlay_id`
- `rule_version_ids`
- `eligibility_snapshot_id`
- `authority_record_id`
- `activation_type`
- `amount`, `currency`
- `parent_event_id`
- `source_transaction_id`
- `event_hash`
- `supersedes_event_id`, if corrected
- `derivation_type`, if a linked projection rather than a native event

**occurred_at and recorded_at must both be immutable and distinct.** For example, a CRM event entered into the onboarding system two days later has its original CRM send time as `occurred_at` and its onboarding ingestion time as `recorded_at`.

Corrections should append a new linked event or correction record. They should not overwrite the original notification, activation, or source transaction.

#### Stable notification identity

Create an immutable `notification_link` or `notification_event` table containing:

- `notification_id` generated by the authoritative sending service;
- `case_id`;
- provider and channel;
- provider message/event ID;
- send time;
- recipient reference;
- payload hash;
- mirrored ingestion event ID, if applicable;
- link confidence and verification method.

For SUP-702, the stable CRM identifier can support a linked event without inventing a second business notification. For SUP-706, a mutable text field should be retained as weak evidence but not treated as an immutable authoritative link. A later correction may establish the link without rewriting the original onboarding event.

#### Authority and policy binding

`policy_authority` should contain:

- authority/rule version ID;
- issuer and issuer role;
- delegation or statutory authority reference;
- approval and publication timestamps;
- effective start and end;
- scope predicates;
- monetary limits;
- obligations and deadlines;
- supersession relationship; and
- immutable document hash.

`case_policy_binding` should record:

- case and route;
- authority record;
- eligibility snapshot;
- selection time;
- decision result;
- rule versions;
- reviewer/approver;
- exception conditions and outstanding postconditions; and
- any denial or escalation.

A policy amendment cannot use `recorded_at` to backdate `effective_from`. An exception discovered late remains late unless a competent authority expressly grants a prospective or legally valid retroactive remedy.

#### Eligibility and master-data snapshots

`eligibility_snapshot` should preserve:

- risk classification and basis;
- supplier country;
- corporate-group ID and approval status;
- new, existing, or reactivation lifecycle;
- critical-relief classification where applicable;
- emergency necessity evidence;
- master-data version and validation result;
- fact-effective time;
- snapshot/record time;
- source provenance; and
- policy/rule version used.

External group or risk data can change later; the decision must remain reproducible using its original snapshot and source version.

#### Identity, aliases, and delegation

Maintain:

- `actor`
- `actor_alias`, with effective interval and source
- `case_participant`, recording role and actual human
- `delegation`, with delegator, delegate, scope, period, and approval evidence

For SUP-712, alias resolution should yield one human and produce a separation failure. For a genuine alias mapping error, append a verified correction while retaining the original resolution history.

#### Spend, deadlines, and monitoring

Create:

- `spend_transaction`
- `spend_authorization`
- `policy_deadline`
- `monitoring_period`
- `monitoring_observation`
- `retrospective_approval`

The emergency closeout evaluator should reconcile all relevant transactions through retrospective approval, not just an “initial spend” field. Multi-currency transactions require a controlled conversion source and rate timestamp.

Monitoring records should cover each required day/period. An attestation without underlying observations is insufficient. Where historic monitoring exists outside the onboarding log, link its evidence; do not fabricate monitoring events that were never collected.

#### Scope and semantic mappings

Maintain controlled tables for:

- lead versus accepted onboarding;
- new supplier versus reactivation;
- reactivation requiring fresh checks versus authorized evidence reuse;
- pending withdrawal versus supplier deactivation;
- activity aliases and procedure versions; and
- equivalence-test results.

For SUP-713, a semantic catalog can classify the historical QA_Clearance event. For SUP-710, the absence of a quality event should produce a requirement-specific evidence gap until the reactivation rule and certification validity are established.

#### Conformance output

Each obligation should receive its own result rather than one binary case verdict:

- `SUPPORTED_COMPLIANT`
- `SUPPORTED_NONCOMPLIANT`
- `SUPPORTED_EXCEPTION`
- `NOT_APPLICABLE`
- `EVIDENCE_INDETERMINATE`

The output should include:

- obligation and policy version;
- route and scope;
- actual condition;
- supporting and contrary evidence;
- assumptions;
- missing evidence;
- severity and confidence; and
- whether any conclusion depends on migration, alias resolution, or label mapping.

This design preserves the strongest parts of both positions: it does not dismiss genuine unauthorized conduct, and it does not convert imperfect models, incomplete logs, or authorized temporary routes into fabricated violations.
