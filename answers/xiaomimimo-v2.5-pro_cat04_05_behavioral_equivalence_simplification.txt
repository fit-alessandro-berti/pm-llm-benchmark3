# 1. Equivalence Notions

**Visible trace equivalence (after hiding ):** Two models are visibly trace equivalent iff the sets of visible traces (sequences of activity labels with all  removed) are identical. Each occurrence of a visible label is distinguished by its position in the trace; duplicate labels are distinguished by occurrence identity (first occurrence vs. second occurrence, etc.), not by label alone. Two traces are equal iff they are identical sequences of labels.

**Bounded visible-language equivalence under P3:** Two models are P3-equivalent iff, restricting to complete traces (reaching a final marking) with at most 12 visible events and at most 3 occurrences of "Analyze Revision," the visible trace sets (with  hidden) are identical.

**Business-relevant equivalence under P1:** Two models are P1-equivalent iff for every visible trace of one model, there exists a trace of the other model that satisfies the same compliance predicates:
- For every occurrence of "Analyze Revision," there is a "Check Revision" before the next "Rework Case" or "Close Case."
- "Rework Case" separates consecutive analysis attempts.
- The number and placement of "Approve Revision" occurrences match.

**Abstracted dashboard equivalence under P2:** Two models are P2-equivalent iff, after applying the following abstraction to every visible trace, the abstract trace sets are identical:
1. Remove all occurrences of "Approve Revision."
2. Replace all occurrences of "Check Revision" with a single Boolean flag (true if 1 occurrence, absent if 0).
3. Record the count of "Analyze Revision" and the presence/absence of "Rework Case."

Under this abstraction, two abstract traces are equal iff they have the same sequence of "Analyze Revision" and "Rework Case" labels (in order), the same Boolean flag for check, same AR count, and same Rework presence.

**Label identity convention:** Duplicate labels are distinguished by occurrence position in the trace. Two traces `[AR, CR, AR, CR]` and `[AR, CR, AR, CR]` are equal only if the labels appear in the same positions. However, when comparing across models, we match traces as sequences.

# 1. Equivalence Notions

**Occurrence identity.** Every occurrence of a visible label in a trace is distinguished by its *position* (index) in the trace sequence. Two traces are equal iff they are identical label sequences element-by-element. When we say "a second Check Revision," we mean a *distinct occurrence* of the label `'Check Revision'` at a later position.

**Visible trace equivalence (after hiding ).** Models *M* and *M* are visibly trace equivalent iff the sets of maximal visible traces—complete traces from initial to final marking with every  removed—are identical as sets of label sequences.

**Bounded visible-language equivalence under P3.** *M* and *M* are P3-equivalent iff, restricting to complete visible traces of length  12 with at most 3 occurrences of `'Analyze Revision'`, the visible trace sets are identical. This is evidence of a bounded language match, not a proof of full equivalence.

**Business-relevant equivalence under P1.** *M* and *M* are P1-equivalent iff every visible trace of one model admits a visible trace of the other that satisfies all three compliance predicates simultaneously:
1. For every occurrence of `'Analyze Revision'`, a `'Check Revision'` occurs *before* the next `'Rework Case'` or before `'Close Case'`.
2. `'Rework Case'` separates consecutive analysis attempts (appears between iterations but not inside one).
3. The number and relative ordering of `'Approve Revision'` occurrences is preserved.

**Abstracted dashboard equivalence under P2.** Define the P2 abstraction function ** applied to any visible trace:
1. Remove all `'Approve Revision'` occurrences.
2. Replace all occurrences of `'Check Revision'` with the single Boolean flag `check = true` (if  1 occurrence) or `check = false` (if 0); remove individual CR positions.
3. Retain the subsequence of `'Analyze Revision'` and `'Rework Case'` labels in order, and record `#AR` and `has_Rework`.

Models *M* and *M* are P2-equivalent iff `{(t) : t  L(M)} = {(t) : t  L(M)}`.

**How optional Approve Revision affects the verdict.** In Model A, `X('Approve Revision', )` is the *last* operator within each iteration, *after* `+(AR, CR)`. # 6. Stochastic and Footprint Comparison

## Footprints

`pm4py.discover_footprints(...)` extracts structural relations from a process tree or accepting Petri net: **start/end activities**, **direct successions** (*A then B*), **causal / parallel / exclusive** pairs. Two models with *different* footprints are provably non-equivalent. Two models with *equal* footprints may still differ in trace language (e.g., different loop iteration counts, different concurrency scope).

```python
import pm4py

tree_a = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "->('Open Case', *(->(+('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision'), "
    "X('Approve Revision', tau)), 'Rework Case'), 'Close Case')"
)
tree_b = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "->('Open Case', +('Check Revision', *(->('Analyze Revision', "
    "X('Approve Revision', tau)), 'Rework Case')), 'Close Case')"
)

fp_a = pm4py.discover_footprints(tree_a)
fp_b = pm4py.discover_footprints(tree_b)

print("=== Model A footprints ===")
for k, v in fp_a.items():
    print(f"  {k}: {v}")

print("\n=== Model B footprints ===")
for k, v in fp_b.items():
    print(f"  {k}: {v}")

print(f"\nFootprints structurally equal: {fp_a == fp_b}")
```

**Expected difference.** In Model A, `'Check Revision'` has a direct-succession relationship with `'Rework Case'` (CR  Rework within the loop), while in Model B, CR is *concurrent* with Rework (CR  Rework). This changes the parallel/direct-succession classification. Therefore `fp_a  fp_b`, which is a *sufficient* condition for trace non-equivalence. However, equal footprints would *not* be sufficient for trace equivalence.

## Stochastic Language and EMD

`pm4py.get_stochastic_language(...)` and `pm4py.compute_emd(...)` require a probability distribution over traces. Since neither model specifies transition probabilities, we would need to assume uniform choices at each XOR and loop-exit/redo point. Even then:

- The two models produce *different supports* (different sets of possible traces), so the EMD between their stochastic languages is bounded below by the probability mass on traces that exist in one but not the other.
- A low EMD (which would not occur here due to the support mismatch) would still not prove trace equivalence — it only measures distributional distance.

**Why equal footprints or low EMD would not establish trace equivalence.**
Footprints abstract away *multiplicity* and *scope*: they record *that* two activities are parallel, not *how many times* each can occur. Two models with the same parallel/sequence/exclusive structure can have very different trace languages (e.g., one allows activity X twice, the other only once). EMD measures distributional closeness but cannot distinguish models whose supports differ; and even with identical supports, low EMD only means the probability mass is *close*, not that the supports are *equal*.

```python
            cs = tree_stats(child, depth + 1)
            stats['nodes'] += cs['nodes']
            stats['max_depth'] = max(stats['max_depth'], cs['max_depth'])
            stats['visible_leaves'] += cs['visible_leaves']
            stats['silent_leaves'] += cs['silent_leaves']
            stats['operators'] += cs['operators']
            stats['labels'].extend(cs['labels'])
    elif node.label is not None:
        stats['visible_leaves'] = 1
        stats['labels'] = [node.label]
    else:
        stats['silent_leaves'] = 1
    return stats

tree_a = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "->('Open Case', *(->(+('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision'), "
    "X('Approve Revision', tau)), 'Rework Case'), 'Close Case')"
)
tree_b = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "->('Open Case', +('Check Revision', *(->('Analyze Revision', "
    "X('Approve Revision', tau)), 'Rework Case')), 'Close Case')"
)

sa = tree_stats(tree_a)
sb = tree_stats(tree_b)

label_counts_a = Counter(sa['labels'])
label_counts_b = Counter(sb['labels'])
dup_a = {k: v for k, v in label_counts_a.items() if v > 1}
dup_b = {k: v for k, v in label_counts_b.items() if v > 1}

# Petri net simplicity
net_a, im_a, fm_a = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_a)
net_b, im_b, fm_b = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_b)

try:
    simp_a = pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net_a, im_a, fm_a)
    simp_b = pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net_b, im_b, fm_b)
except Exception as e:
    simp_a = f"Error: {e}"
    simp_b = f"Error: {e}"

print("| Metric                  | Model A | Model B |")
print("|-------------------------|---------|---------|")
print(f"| Total nodes             | {sa['nodes']:>7} | {sb['nodes']:>7} |")
print(f"| Max depth               | {sa['max_depth']:>7} | {sb['max_depth']:>7} |")
print(f"| Visible leaves          | {sa['visible_leaves']:>7} | {sb['visible_leaves']:>7} |")
print(f"| Silent leaves ()       | {sa['silent_leaves']:>7} | {sb['silent_leaves']:>7} |")
print(f"| Internal operator nodes | {sa['operators']:>7} | {sb['operators']:>7} |")
print(f"| Duplicate labels        | {str(dup_a):>7} | {str(dup_b):>7} |")
print(f"| Unique visible labels   | {len(set(sa['labels'])):>7} | {len(set(sb['labels'])):>7} |")
print(f"| PN simplicity           | {str(simp_a):>7} | {str(simp_b):>7} |")
print(f"| PN places               | {len(net_a.places):>7} | {len(net_b.places):>7} |")
```

# 8. Simplified Model

**Target semantics: P1 (operational compliance).** P1 is the most stringent view and the most appropriate for a process model that drives compliance checking. Model A already satisfies P1, but its `X('Approve Revision', )` adds structural complexity without serving the core compliance requirement (which treats Approve as optional and does not require distinguishing approved from auto-approved revisions).

## 8.1 Compact Process Tree

```text
->(
  'Open Case',
  *(
    +('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision'),
    'Rework Case'
  ),
  'Close Case'
)
```

This is strictly simpler than **both** originals:

| Metric | Simplified | Model A | Model B |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total nodes | **8** | 12 | 12 |
| Max depth | **3** | 4 | 5 |
| Visible leaves | 4 | 6 | 6 |
| Silent leaves () | **0** | 1 | 1 |
| Operator nodes | **3** | 5 | 5 |
| XOR operators | **0** | 1 | 1 |

## 8.2 PM4Py Code

```python
import pm4py

simplified_tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "->('Open Case', *(+('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision'), "
    "'Rework Case'), 'Close Case')"
)

net_s, im_s, fm_s = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(simplified_tree)
sound_result = pm4py.check_soundness(net_s, im_s, fm_s, print_diagnostics=False)
is_sound = sound_result[0] if isinstance(sound_result, tuple) else sound_result
print(f"Simplified model sound: {is_sound}")

# Enumerate bounded traces for verification
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import Marking
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.semantics import ClassicSemantics
from collections import deque

def enumerate_visible_traces_bfs(net, im, fm, max_visible=12, max_ar=3):
    sem = ClassicSemantics()
    AR_LABEL = "Analyze Revision"
    fm_k = frozenset(fm.items())

    def reconstruct(key):
        m = Marking()
        for p, c in key:
            m[p] = c
        return m

    initial = (frozenset(im.items()), (), 0)
    queue = deque([initial])
    visited = {initial}
    complete = set()

    while queue:
        mk, vis, arc = queue.popleft()
        m = reconstruct(mk)
        for t in pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, m):
            new_m = sem.execute(t, net, m)
            new_mk = frozenset(new_m.items())
            if t.label is None:
                s = (new_mk, vis, arc)
                if s not in visited:
                    visited.add(s)
                    queue.append(s)
            else:
                nv = vis + (t.label,)
                na = arc + (1 if t.label == AR_LABEL else 0)
                if len(nv) > max_visible or na > max_ar:
                    continue
                s = (new_mk, nv, na)
                if s not in visited:
                    visited.add(s)
                    queue.append(s)
                    if new_mk == fm_k:
                        complete.add(nv)
    return complete

traces_s = enumerate_visible_traces_bfs(net_s, im_s, fm_s)
print(f"Simplified model: {len(traces_s)} bounded visible traces")
```

# 9. Final Recommendation

| Business view | Recommended model | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **P1 — Operational compliance** | **Model A** (or simplified) | Only Model A (and its simplification) enforces the 1:1 AR-to-CR pairing that P1 demands. Model B structurally violates this. The **simplified model** drops optional Approve without weakening P1 compliance, reducing node count from 128 and eliminating all silent transitions. **Use the simplified model for P1.** |
| **P2 — Executive dashboard** | Either original model, or simplified | Under P2's abstraction (``), both originals and the simplified model produce identical abstract languages. Choose the simplest: the **simplified model**. |
| **P3 — Bounded testing** | **Model A** (if Approve traces needed) | P3 retains all visible labels including Approve Revision. Only Model A (or Model B, which has different traces) can generate the full P3-visible language. The simplified model loses Approve traces, so **do not use it for P3** if Approve coverage matters. |

**Should the simplified model replace either original?** The simplified model should replace Model A for P1 and P2 purposes, because it preserves all P1 and P2 properties with less structural complexity. It should *not* replace Model A for P3 purposes where Approve Revision traces are needed. Model B should not be used for P1 (it violates the compliance invariant) but could serve P2 if already deployed.

**Warning about bounded comparison (P3).** Bounded testing with 12 events and 3 AR is *evidence*, not *proof*. In this case, the bounded counterexamples (e.g., traces with 2 CRs) happen to also be unbounded counterexamples because the `#CR = #AR` vs. `#CR = 1` invariant is a structural property that manifests at all scales. In general, however, two models can agree on all traces up to a bound and diverge beyond it (e.g., a loop that differs only on the 4th iteration). Bounded testing is a necessary but not sufficient condition for unbounded equivalence. Always complement bounded testing with structural analysis (footprints, node-level comparison) when a definitive verdict is required.
