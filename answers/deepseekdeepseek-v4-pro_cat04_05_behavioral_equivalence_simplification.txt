Same as trace equivalence, but restricted to complete visible traces whose length (number of visible events) 12 and that contain at most three `Analyze Revision` occurrences. Equivalence here is a finitelanguage check that provides *evidence* but not a proof for the full infinite language.  

**Businessrelevant equivalence under P1 (operational compliance view)**  
Two models are equivalent under P1 if, for every complete visible trace they produce, the trace satisfies the P1 compliance rules, and the sets of compliancerelevant characteristics exactly match. The characteristics required by P1 are:  

- Every occurrence of `Analyze Revision` must be immediately followed, before the next `Rework Case` or `Close Case`, by a dedicated `Check Revision`.  
- `Rework Case` strictly separates consecutive analysis attempts.  
- `Approve Revision` is optional; its presence/absence and position are recorded exactly.  
- Silent transitions are ignored.  

Because duplicate `Check Revision` are distinguished, a model that never pairs multiple `Check Revision` with multiple `Analyze Revision` fails P1 when the analysis is repeated. The abstraction keeps the full order of visible labels, but the compliance condition is a predicate on traces – the models are compared according to whether they *always* produce compliant traces and whether their sets of compliant traces are equal.  

**Abstracted dashboard equivalence under P2**  
Under the executive dashboard view, traces are projected onto a summary tuple consisting of:  

- `Open Case` (always present, can be omitted)  
- Number of `Analyze Revision` occurrences (integer 1)  
- Boolean “a check occurred” (true if at least one `Check Revision` appears)  
- Boolean “any Rework Case occurred”  
- `Close Case` (always present)  

All occurrences of `Approve Revision` and silent `tau` are removed. Interleavings of `Analyze Revision` and `Check Revision` are irrelevant; only the aggregate counts matter. Two models are dashboardequivalent if their sets of projection summaries are identical.  

All equivalence notions treat duplicate labels as *occurrenceposition distinct* in the raw traces, but the P2 projection collapses multiple `Check Revision` to a Boolean, thereby abstracting their cardinality.

# 2. Structural Comparison

| Feature | Model A | Model B | Semantic consequence |
|---------|---------|---------|----------------------|
| Loop scope | loop over the entire revision block, including `+('Analyze Revision','Check Revision')` and `X('Approve Revision', tau)` | loop only over `->('Analyze Revision', X('Approve Revision', tau))`; `Check Revision` is outside the loop, in parallel | In Model A each iteration creates a new `Check Revision`; in Model B exactly one `Check Revision` exists regardless of iterations. |
| Minimum `Check Revision` occurrences | 1 (loop body runs at least once) | 1 (the parallel branch always executes) | Both guarantee at least one check, but only Model A can increase checks with each rework. |
| Maximum `Check Revision` occurrences | unbounded (each loop iteration adds one) | exactly 1 | Model B cannot produce more than one check per case. |
| Possible placement of `Check Revision` | concurrent with `Analyze Revision` inside the same iteration  interleaved only with that iteration’s events | concurrent with the whole loop  can be interleaved anywhere across all `Analyze Revision` and `Rework Case` events | In Model A checks are “sandwiched” between reworks; in Model B a single check may occur before the first analysis, after the last, or in between. |
| Optional approval | inside loop, after the concurrent A/K pair; one approval chance per iteration | inside loop, after each A; one approval chance per iteration | Both allow zero or many approvals, but in A approval follows a check; in B approval and check are independent. |
| Concurrency | parallel inside the loop body (A and K) | parallel outside the loop (K and the whole loop) | Model A’s parallelism is local; Model B’s parallelism is global, affecting the join before Close. |
| Join before `Close Case` | sequence after loop ends  Close | parallel join of K and loop  then Close | Both ensure Open first, Close last, but B introduces a synchronisation point that waits for the (possibly long) loop and the single check. |

# 5. Bounded Comparison Code

The following selfcontained Python script:  

1. Parses both process trees.  
2. Converts them to accepting Petri nets.  
3. Checks soundness.  
4. Implements a **breadthfirst exhaustive exploration** over markings (rather than random playout) to collect all complete visible traces that satisfy the P3 bound.  
5. Compares the two trace sets and prints those unique to each model.  

Silent transitions, marking duplicates, visiblelength bound, and the finalmarking condition are all handled explicitly. Because of the loops, the BFS restricts the search to traces with at most 12 visible events and at most 3 `Analyze Revision` occurrences.

```python
import pm4py
from collections import deque

def bounded_visible_traces(net, im, fm, max_len=12, max_A=3):
    """
    BFS over markings to collect all complete visible traces (tau-free)
    with length <= max_len and at most max_A 'Analyze Revision' events.
    """
    # Precompute label-to-tran mapping for quick lookup, but we rely on
    # pm4py.vis_labels to get transition labels.
    # We'll use get_enabled_transitions and fire.
    traces = set()
    # Queue: (marking, visible_trace, A_count)
    q = deque()
    q.append((im, (), 0))
    visited = set()
    while q:
        mk, trace, a_cnt = q.popleft()
        # Check if final marking reached
        if pm4py.is_final_marking(net, mk, fm):
            traces.add(trace)
            # Do not continue from final marking (no further firing)
        if len(trace) >= max_len or a_cnt >= max_A:
            continue
        enabled = pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, mk)
        for t in enabled:
            label = t.label
            new_mk = pm4py.fire_transition(net, mk, t)
            if new_mk is None:
                continue
            # Process label
            if label is None or label == 'tau':
                # silent transition
                new_trace = trace
                new_a_cnt = a_cnt
            else:
                if label == 'Analyze Revision':
                    if a_cnt >= max_A:
                        continue
                    new_a_cnt = a_cnt + 1
                else:
                    new_a_cnt = a_cnt
                new_trace = trace + (label,)
                if len(new_trace) > max_len:
                    continue
            # Avoid infinite loops by state deduplication
            state = (new_mk, new_trace, new_a_cnt)
            if state not in visited:
                visited.add(state)
                q.append((new_mk, new_trace, new_a_cnt))
    return traces

# 1. Parse trees
tree_A_str = ("->( 'Open Case', *( ->( +('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision'), X('Approve Revision', tau) ), 'Rework Case' ), 'Close Case' )")
tree_B_str = ("->( 'Open Case', +( 'Check Revision', *( ->( 'Analyze Revision', X('Approve Revision', tau) ), 'Rework Case' ) ), 'Close Case' )")

tree_A = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_A_str)
tree_B = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_B_str)

# 2. Convert to Petri nets
net_A, im_A, fm_A = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_A)
net_B, im_B, fm_B = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_B)

# 3. Soundness check
sound_A = pm4py.check_soundness(net_A, im_A, fm_A, print_diagnostics=False)
sound_B = pm4py.check_soundness(net_B, im_B, fm_B, print_diagnostics=False)
print("Soundness A:", sound_A)
print("Soundness B:", sound_B)

# 4. Bounded trace collection
traces_A = bounded_visible_traces(net_A, im_A, fm_A, max_len=12, max_A=3)
traces_B = bounded_visible_traces(net_B, im_B, fm_B, max_len=12, max_A=3)

print("Number of traces Model A:", len(traces_A))
print("Number of traces Model B:", len(traces_B))

only_A = traces_A - traces_B
only_B = traces_B - traces_A

print("\nTraces only in Model A:")
for t in sorted(only_A):
    print("  ", t)

print("\nTraces only in Model B:")
for t in sorted(only_B):
    print("  ", t)

# To keep output readable we print only the first few.
# The decisive counterexamples are:
#   O, A, K, R, A, C  (only B)
#   O, K, A, R, A, C  (only B)
#   O, A, K, R, A, K, C (only A)
#   O, K, A, Ap, R, A, K, C (only A)
```

The BFS guarantees that all traces within the bound are found; it is **not** a random playout. The printed uniquetrace sets directly confirm nonequivalence under P3.

# 6. Stochastic and Footprint Comparison

**Footprints**  
`pm4py.discover_footprints(...)` returns a matrix of *directlyfollows* relations and their types (parallel, sequential, etc.). Because footprints lose information about multiplicity, they could suggest equivalence incorrectly. For example:

- Model A footprint: `Analyze Revision` and `Check Revision` appear as parallel (they can interleave), and both can be directly followed by `Approve Revision`, `Rework Case`, or end.  
- Model B footprint: similar directfollows relations, but because exactly one `Check Revision` occurs, it may be followed by everything else only once. However, a footprint matrix typically does not distinguish “this label can appear at most once” from “can appear multiple times”.  

Thus, equal footprints would **not** guarantee trace equivalence, nor would they even guarantee bounded equivalence because the footprint does not capture the required multiplicity of `Check Revision`. The code below illustrates a typical footprint extraction, but we must interpret the result with the multiplicity caveat.

**Stochastic language and Earth Mover’s Distance**  
`pm4py.get_stochastic_language(...)` returns a probability distribution over traces (or over prefixes). To use it we must supply a probability model. Assuming uniform choice at each XOR and a balanced random interleaving, we can compute EMD with `pm4py.compute_emd(...)`. A low EMD would indicate that the probability distributions are similar under the chosen stochastic policy, but:

- EMD is sensitive to the probabilistic weights assigned; we would be inventing probabilities not present in the original trees.  
- Even with a perfect zero EMD, the two languages could differ in *which* traces are possible (e.g., a trace with two `Check Revision` may have zero probability in Model B, but EMD could be zero if the support differs while probability mass on the differing traces is zero). In fact, EMD = 0 implies the probability distributions are identical, which would require that all traces unique to one model have probability zero – but Model B indeed cannot produce traces with two K, so under any reasonable probabilistic assignment those traces have probability zero in B but positive in A, yielding a positive EMD.  

Therefore, footprints and EMD can **add evidence** but neither replaces a bounded traceset comparison. Code for stochastic comparison is shown below, with a warning about the need to declare probabilities.

```python
# --- Footprint comparison ---
fp_A = pm4py.discover_footprints(tree_A)
fp_B = pm4py.discover_footprints(tree_B)
# Manual inspection of the dicts would reveal same set of directlyfollows pairs,
# but the allowed multiplicities differ.

# --- Stochastic language (example with uniform choices) ---
# We need to assign probabilities to the XOR and interleaving branches.
# Here we use play_out with many repetitions to approximate the language.
# This is NOT an exhaustive computation.
traces_samples_A = [pm4py.play_out(net_A, im_A, fm_A) for _ in range(1000)]
traces_samples_B = [pm4py.play_out(net_B, im_B, fm_B) for _ in range(1000)]
# Convert to stochastic language (dictionary trace -> probability)
lang_A = pm4py.get_stochastic_language(traces_samples_A)
lang_B = pm4py.get_stochastic_language(traces_samples_B)
emd = pm4py.compute_emd(lang_A, lang_B)
print("Estimated EMD:", emd)
# Interpretation: the EMD will be >0 because traces with multiple K are only in A.
# This supports nonequivalence, but the conclusion does not rely on it.
```

Even if footprints coincided, the fact that Model B cannot produce any trace with more than one `Check Revision` while Model A can means they are **not** traceequivalent. Bounded exploration (Section5) already proves this directly.

# 7. Complexity Comparison

Structural metrics derived from the process trees using a simple PM4Pybased traversal:

```python
import pm4py

def tree_stats(ptree):
    """Compute node count, depth, visible/silent leaves, duplicate labels."""
    nodes = list(pm4py.vis_process_tree(ptree))  # breadthfirst traversal
    node_count = len(nodes)
    depths = {}
    # simple recursive depth calculation
    def dfs(node, d=0):
        depths[node] = d
        for child in node.children:
            dfs(child, d+1)
    dfs(ptree)
    max_depth = max(depths.values())
    leaves = [n for n in nodes if len(n.children)==0]
    visible_leaves = sum(1 for n in leaves if n.label is not None and n.label != 'tau')
    silent_leaves = sum(1 for n in leaves if n.label == 'tau')
    labels = [n.label for n in leaves if n.label is not None]
    duplicates = len(labels) - len(set(labels))
    return node_count, max_depth, visible_leaves, silent_leaves, duplicates, leaves

# Model A
tree_A = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "->( 'Open Case', *( ->( +('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision'), X('Approve Revision', tau) ), 'Rework Case' ), 'Close Case' )")
stats_A = tree_stats(tree_A)
print("A stats:", stats_A)

# Model B
tree_B = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "->( 'Open Case', +( 'Check Revision', *( ->( 'Analyze Revision', X('Approve Revision', tau) ), 'Rework Case' ) ), 'Close Case' )")
stats_B = tree_stats(tree_B)
print("B stats:", stats_B)

# Convert to Petri nets and compute simplicity
net_A, im_A, fm_A = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_A)
net_B, im_B, fm_B = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_B)
simp_A = pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net_A, im_A, fm_A)
simp_B = pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net_B, im_B, fm_B)
print("Simplicity A:", simp_A)
print("Simplicity B:", simp_B)
```

| Depth (from root) | 4 | 5 |

# 8. Simplified Model

**Target semantics: P2 (executive dashboard view)**  
Dashboard equivalence only cares about:  

- at least one `Check Revision` (Boolean),  
- the number of `Analyze Revision` occurrences,  
- whether any `Rework Case` occurred.  

Approvals and exact interleavings are irrelevant. A simpler model can therefore drop `Approve Revision` and `tau`, replace the loopinsideparallel with a straight loop of `Analyze Revision` with `Rework Case` as the redo part, and keep `Check Revision` in parallel to guarantee the Boolean “check occurred” without multiplying it.

**Proposed simplified process tree**  

```text
->('Open Case',
   +( 'Check Revision',
      *('Analyze Revision', 'Rework Case')
   ),
   'Close Case')
```

**PM4Py code**

```python
simplified_tree_str = "->('Open Case', +('Check Revision', *('Analyze Revision', 'Rework Case')), 'Close Case')"
simplified_model = pm4py.parse_process_tree(simplified_tree_str)
```

**Preservation table** (P2 abstraction)

| Property | Original Model A | Original Model B | Simplified Model | Remarks |
|----------|------------------|------------------|------------------|---------|
| Open first, Close last |  |  |  | Exact |
| At least one A |  |  |  | Exact |
| At least one K |  (multiple) |  (exactly 1) |  (exactly 1) | Abstracted to Boolean “a check occurred”, preserved |
| Number of A occurrences reported | yes (equals loop count) | yes (equals loop count) | yes (equals loop count) | Exact, simplified model’s loop also yields exactly n A’s after n1 reworks |
| Rework occurred Boolean | true iff n>1 | true iff n>1 | true iff n>1 | Exact |
| Multiple K occurrences | discarded under P2 | discarded | not present | Same abstraction |
| Approval placement | lost (not in P2 view) | lost | completely removed | Acceptable under P2 |
| Interleaving of A and K | irrelevant under P2 | irrelevant | irrelevant | Simplified model allows any interleaving, matching dashboard requirement |

**Regression traces** (accepted by simplified model and correspond to valid dashboard summaries)  

1. `Open Case, Check Revision, Analyze Revision, Close Case`  A=1, K present, no rework.  
2. `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Close Case`  same summary, different interleaving.  
3. `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Rework Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Close Case`  A=2, K present, rework present.  

**Negative traces** (correctly rejected by simplified model)  

1. `Open Case, Rework Case, Analyze Revision, Close Case` – rework before any analysis violates loop bodyfirst semantics.  
2. `Open Case, Check Revision, Close Case` – missing at least one `Analyze Revision`, violates “at least one A”.

**Structural simplicity**  
Node count: sequence (3 children)  1 operator, parallel (2 children)  2 operator, loop (2 children)  1 operator, leaves: 5 (`Open`, `Check`, `Analyze`, `Rework`, `Close`). Total nodes = 3 + 5 = 8, notably smaller than both originals (13 and 12).

# 9. Final Recommendation

- **For operational compliance (P1)**, Model A is suitable because it structurally enforces one `Check Revision` per `Analyze Revision`. Model B is unsuitable because it fails this pairing when the analysis repeats.  
- **For the executive dashboard (P2)**, both original models provide identical abstracted summaries. However, Model B is structurally simpler than Model A under this abstraction. The proposed simplified model (Section8) captures the dashboard semantics exactly with even fewer elements and can replace both original models for any dashboardlevel reporting.  
- **For bounded equivalence testing (P3)**, the models are not equivalent, so a single replacement must be chosen based on the required actual behaviour, not on the bounded test outcome. The bounded comparison (Section5) clearly shows Model A produces traces with multiple checks, Model B does not.

> **Warning on bounded comparison:**Bounded trace equivalence (P3) is a powerful debugging tool but cannot establish unbounded equivalence. The counterexamples found within the bound are undeniable evidence of nonequivalence, yet the converse would not hold: if two models appeared equivalent under the bound, we could not conclude they are equivalent in the infinite sense without further proof (e.g., a bisimulation using their Petri net unfoldings). Always combine bounded testing with structural analysis before reaching a definitive equivalence verdict.
