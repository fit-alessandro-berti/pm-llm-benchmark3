# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

## Intended uses distinguished

The two submissions are being judged as if one metric profile could serve every purpose. It cannot. I distinguish at least four uses, because the acceptable fitness/precision trade-off reverses between them:

| Intended use | What the model is for | Fitness/precision implication | Main danger |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Exploration / hypothesis generation** | Suggest plausible flows, discover variants, prompt questions | High fitness and communicability are valuable; low precision is tolerable if the model is explicitly non-normative and nobody can release an order from it | Users mistake a permissive map for permission |
| **Conformance enforcement** | Gate actual orders; accept/reject a trace before release | Precision against *forbidden* behaviour and sound handling of mandatory/recheck logic dominate; a false acceptance of a skipped mandatory check is worse than a false rejection of a novel valid combination | S-type false accepts; C-type false rejects handled badly |
| **Simulation / capacity analysis** | Estimate throughput, waiting, resource load | Need a stochastic/attribute-aware model, not just language; neither submission supplies durations, resources, probabilities, or the external/internal split | Treating a control-flow skeleton as a simulator |
| **Stakeholder communication** | Shared vocabulary, training, process ownership | Simplicity and comprehensibility matter, but only if the communicated model does not teach the wrong policy | A simple picture that normalises skipping checks |

Model S is, at best, a communication/exploration artefact. Model C is, at best, a descriptive catalogue of observed variants. Neither, as submitted, is an enforcement model. The governance question is therefore not “which score is bigger?” but “which language, plus which auxiliary checks, is safe for which decision?”

## Assumptions and evidential limits

1. **Acceptance means strict language membership** under standard process-tree semantics: `->` sequence, `+` parallel, `X` choice, `tau` silent/skip, no loops in either submission. Alignment fitness is not acceptance; a trace can have high fitness and still be outside the language.
2. No raw log, seed, preprocessing code, alignment costs, or held-out cases were supplied. All metric comparisons are therefore provisional.
3. Policy statements P1–P7 are treated as stakeholder-agreed constraints, but several are under-specified: Supplier Review’s exact position relative to the mandatory checks is not fixed; P3 does not state whether Supplier Review on an *internal* order is forbidden or merely unnecessary; P6 does not identify the affected check.
4. Because the external/internal and affected-check attributes are absent, any unconditional verdict on B5, B6, B7, or a no-Supplier trace is beyond the evidence. My third alternative returns **ACCEPT / REJECT / CONDITIONAL** rather than hiding that gap.
5. “Valid” in the probe table is a policy assessment given for the probe’s stated context. For B1/B2, validity presumably assumes the no-Supplier context is legitimate; for B5/B7, an external context is stated.

# 2. Metric Interpretation

No composite score is computed. Averaging these measures would require defended weights across incommensurable constructs — alignment fit, language size, token replay, graph degree, wall-clock search time, and an informal opinion score — and would conceal exactly the trade-offs the board must decide.

| Measure | What it suggests | What it does not establish | Concern about comparability | Decision relevance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Training alignment fitness: S 0.992, C 0.973** | Both replay the training log with few edits; S slightly more so | That S is “more correct” — fitness rewards deleting/skipping expensive log behaviour, and S has no Correct Dossier node at all, yet still scores 0.992 | Undisclosed alignment costs and preprocessing; S gets `tau` skips for mandatory activities, C does not | Weak evidence for enforcement; useful only as a replay diagnostic |
| **Holdout alignment fitness: S 0.981, C 0.744** | S’s language covers held-out traces almost completely; C’s enumerated language does not | That S *generalises correctly* — a near-universal language also covers held-out traces. The gap is consistent with C memorising training variants | Unknown split strategy; if the split was random by case, C’s variants leak into training; if it was variant-aware, C is penalised for unseen combinations by design. F4 says holdout contains Supplier+Auto+alternate-order combinations absent from training | Strong warning against C as a general gate; near-zero discrimination value for S |
| **Alignment precision on training log: S 0.584, C 0.941** | C’s language is much closer to the observed training behaviour; S admits a large surplus of unobserved behaviour | That C forbids the *right* things. Precision here was computed only against observed positive traces (F5); no curated negatives were used. A model can reject B4 by accident while also rejecting B5 | Same training log for both helps comparability, but precision is still positive-only and cost-dependent | Relevant to enforcement, but only when recomputed against a negative suite |
| **Token-based generalization: S 0.907, C 0.412** | S rarely encounters infrequent tokens during replay; C often does | Genuine semantic generalisation. Token-replay generalisation is known to reward permissive models that never get stuck | Token replay is sensitive to duplicate labels and silent transitions, which differ sharply between submissions | Should not be used to promote S to enforcement |
| **Petri-net simplicity, arc-degree: S 0.918, C 0.281** | S’s net is structurally smaller; C’s is larger | Maintainability, comprehensibility, or ease of change. Arc degree does not measure branch-enumeration debt or cognitive load across 8 sequences | The net is derived from very different constructs (`tau` skips vs. 8-way choice); arc degree counts structure, not semantics | Supports S for communication; does not support C’s retirement or S’s adoption by itself |
| **Median alignment runtime per case: S 0.018 s, C 0.440 s** | Aligning against C is ~24× slower in this setup | Operational runtime, throughput, or user latency. Alignment search time explodes with model size, duplicate labels, and branching | Confounded by model size, log-move costs, case length, implementation, and hardware; no variance or machine reported | Relevant to tooling cost, not to policy correctness |
| **Reported duplicate-label count: S 0, C 33** | C has substantial label duplication in some representation | Anything precise: the definition is undocumented and the count cannot be reconciled from the expression alone (C has 48 leaf occurrences across 8 branches; 33 matches no obvious leaf/transition count I can derive) | Not comparable until the counting rule is published; may count net transitions, process-tree leaves, or log labels | A red flag for C’s maintainability, but not a usable metric as reported |
| **Stakeholder comprehension, informal 1–5: S 4.4, C 1.9** | Stakeholders found S easier to grasp | Measured comprehension. No protocol, sample, task, blinding, order randomisation, or inter-rater agreement is reported; advocates may have scored their own model | Informal, unblinded, undocumented — not comparable to the quantitative measures at all | A hypothesis about communication value, to be re-tested under the protocol in §9 |

**Overall reading:** The metric profile is a textbook permissive-vs-enumerative trade-off. S wins every measure that rewards coverage and smallness; C wins the one measure that rewards narrowness on observed positives. Neither profile answers the enforcement question, because the decisive evidence — behaviour on forbidden and unseen-but-valid traces — is largely absent from the reported numbers.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

## Strongest case for Model S

Model S is small, communicable (4.4/5 informal), fast to align, and covers held-out behaviour with fitness 0.981. Its backbone gets several things right: Register first, Release last, exactly one of Manager/Auto Approval, and free interleaving of the check/review activities. As an exploratory skeleton it can absorb missing events and novel combinations without the brittle false rejections that plague C (F2). For a workshop, it is the only one of the two that a business user can hold in their head.

## Strongest case against Model S

The model violates the agreed policy in its core, not its margins. F1 is dispositive for enforcement: it can skip Credit Check and Technical Review, and can place Expedite Review before them. Its high fitness is achieved precisely by making mandatory behaviour optional (`X('Credit Check', tau)` etc.), and its parallel block places no precedence between Expedite Review and the mandatory checks. It also omits Correct Dossier entirely, so the correction/recheck policy (P6, B6) cannot even be expressed. F7 — “low precision is fine for exploration” — is an argument for not deploying it for enforcement, not for deploying it.

## Concrete undesirable behaviours admitted by S

Since `+` interleaves the four optional branches arbitrarily and each can be skipped, S admits at least:

1. **B3:** `Register Dossier, Manager Approval, Release Order` — both mandatory checks skipped.
2. **B4:** `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` — Expedite before the mandatory checks it must follow (P4).
3. **Partial mandatory skip:** `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Manager Approval, Release Order` — Technical Review skipped.
4. **Supplier-only order:** `Register Dossier, Supplier Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` — no credit or technical assurance at all.
5. **Expedite-only order:** `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Auto Approval, Release Order` — urgency substituted for assurance.
6. **Silent external non-compliance:** `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` is accepted unconditionally, yet if the order is externally sourced, P3 makes Supplier Review mandatory. S cannot distinguish this from a legitimate internal case because it models Supplier Review as unconditionally optional.

## Intentional noise tolerance vs. accidental semantic flaws

Some permissiveness is defensible noise tolerance: allowing a Supplier Review event to be missing from a noisy log, or allowing check order to vary, are reasonable responses to imperfect data. But three flaws are accidental, not designed tolerance:

- **Mandatory activities wrapped in `X(·, tau)`** conflates “event missing from the log” with “check not required.” A noise-tolerant model should still *require* the check and let alignment report a missing event as a violation; S instead makes the violation unrepresentable.
- **Parallel placement of Expedite Review** ignores P4 entirely. Free interleaving is not noise tolerance; it is the absence of a precedence constraint the stakeholders explicitly agreed.
- **No Correct Dossier node** is not tolerance of missing events — it is omission of an agreed activity. S cannot represent, reward, or penalise correction behaviour at all.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

## Strongest case for Model C

C is conservative in a way auditors can defend: every accepted trace contains both mandatory checks before approval, Expedite (where present) follows them, and exactly one approval occurs. It rejects B3, B4, and B8. Its training precision (0.941) shows it does not license unobserved behaviour, and F8’s position — unseen behaviour is non-compliant until approved — is a coherent fail-closed policy. As an inventory of what was actually observed, it is traceable: each branch is a real variant.

## Strongest case against Model C

C mistakes enumeration for specification. Eight hand-listed sequences cannot cover the combinatorial space that P2–P5 define, and the holdout fitness of 0.744 is the predictable consequence. The model rejects B5, a plausible valid external/urgent case, and B6, a policy-dependent partial recheck. Its problems compound:

- **Branch enumeration:** Adding one legitimate combination (e.g., Supplier + Auto Approval, alternate check order + Expedite) requires a new branch. The space of combinations of {check order} × {Supplier?} × {Expedite?} × {Manager/Auto} × {correction variant} is far larger than 8. F2’s complaint that C “rejects legitimate new combinations and is difficult to update” is structural, not incidental.
- **Duplicate labels:** The same activity label appears as separate leaves in many branches, producing many same-labelled transitions in the Petri net. This creates alignment ambiguity (which branch’s Credit Check was replayed?), complicates impact analysis and ownership (F3), and may distort token-replay and precision measures. The undocumented count of 33 cannot be audited.
- **Correction handling:** C allows exactly one correction pattern — Correct Dossier followed by Credit then Technical, in that order, before Manager Approval. It cannot express B6 (Technical only), a Credit-only recheck, a reordered recheck, or correction combined with Supplier/Expedite/Auto. F6’s requirement — recheck only the affected check — is unrepresentable.
- **Holdout degradation:** The fall from 0.973 (train) to 0.744 (holdout) is the signature of memorisation. F4 confirms the mechanism: holdout combines Supplier Review, Auto Approval, and alternate check order in ways absent from training.
- **Maintainability and runtime:** Simplicity 0.281, informal comprehension 1.9, and 0.440 s median alignment make C expensive to align and hard to change. Every policy clarification becomes a branch-editing exercise with regression risk across the other branches.
- **Precision may be an artefact:** C’s 0.941 precision is computed against observed positives only. A model that enumerates the training variants will look precise on training data by construction. High precision here measures *narrowness*, not *correctness of the boundary*; C rejects B5 (valid) and B4 (invalid) with the same mechanism — non-membership in the list — so the metric cannot distinguish principled exclusion from accidental exclusion.

C’s saving grace is that its errors are mostly false *rejections*, which are visible and routable to an exception process. That makes it safer than S for enforcement, but still not a specification.

# 5. Behavior-Probe Matrix

Acceptance is strict language membership. Neither model has a loop operator, so there is no loop-semantics uncertainty; the only silent element is S’s `tau` skip inside `X(·, tau)`, whose meaning is standard. Alignment fitness for rejected traces is not acceptance.

| Probe | Model S accepts? | Model C accepts? | Policy assessment | Consequence for review |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1: R, C, T, M, Rel | **Yes** | **Yes** (branch 1) | valid | Both pass the happy path |
| B2: R, T, C, Auto, Rel | **Yes** | **Yes** (branch 2) | valid | Both handle alternate check order + Auto |
| B3: R, M, Rel | **Yes** — all four optionals take `tau` | **No** | forbidden | S commits a hard policy violation; C is correct here |
| B4: R, Exp, C, T, M, Rel | **Yes** — parallel interleaving | **No** | forbidden | S violates P4; C correct, but by enumeration, not by a stated precedence rule |
| B5: R, T, C, S, Exp, Auto, Rel | **Yes** | **No** — no branch has alternate order + Supplier + Expedite + Auto | plausible valid external/urgent | S accepts for the wrong reason (everything is optional); C falsely rejects a legitimate combination |
| B6: R, C, T, Correct, T, M, Rel | **No** — Correct Dossier and the second Technical Review are absent from S | **No** — branch 7 requires Credit *and* Technical after correction | valid only if correction affected Technical alone | Both fail the key correction case; S cannot represent it, C over-specifies it |
| B7: R, C, T, S, M, Rel | **Yes** | **Yes** (branch 3) | valid external | Both accept, but neither can check the external condition that makes it valid |
| B8: R, C, T, C, M, Rel | **No** — only one Credit Check node exists | **No** — no branch repeats a check without correction | suspicious/forbidden without correction | Both reject, but S rejects only because it has a single node, not because it encodes P7; neither can distinguish B8 from a legitimate recheck |

The matrix shows the asymmetry clearly: S’s failures are *false accepts of forbidden behaviour*; C’s failures are *false rejects of valid behaviour*. For enforcement, false accepts of skipped mandatory checks are the more serious failure class.

# 7. Third Alternative

## Design choice

I propose a **block-structured procedural backbone plus DECLARE-style constraints and an explicit correction checker** — i.e., a procedural model with explicit uncertainty and post-hoc policy checks. For first-pass activities, the backbone’s language is exactly the partial order (POWL-style) defined by P1–P5; the declarative layer enforces the precedence the parallel block cannot, and a small state-machine checker handles correction episodes. Nothing is silently made optional: mandatory checks remain mandatory, Supplier Review is *conditionally* required, and correction is a validated episode, not a parallel activity.

## 1. Compact formal description

- **First-pass partial order:** `Register < {Credit, Technical, Supplier?, Expedite?} < Approval < Release`, with `Credit` and `Technical` incomparable (either order), `Supplier` incomparable with `Credit`, `Technical`, and `Expedite` (position relative to the mandatory checks is *not* fixed by policy — flagged as ambiguous, not forbidden), `Expedite` strictly after both `Credit` and `Technical`, `Approval  {Manager, Auto}` exactly one, immediately before `Release`.
- **Correction episodes:** a `Correct Dossier` must be immediately followed by a non-empty recheck set  {Credit, Technical}, each at most once, in either order, before the next non-recheck activity and before Approval. Episodes may repeat; each is validated independently.
- **Verdicts:** ACCEPT / REJECT / CONDITIONAL (CONDITIONAL when the external/internal or affected-check attribute needed for a definitive verdict is missing).

## 2. PM4Py code

```python
import pm4py
from collections import Counter

# --- Procedural backbone (first-pass language) -------------------------------
model_third_string = """
->(
  'Register Dossier',
  +(
    'Credit Check',
    'Technical Review',
    X('Supplier Review', tau),
    X('Expedite Review', tau)
  ),
  X('Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval'),
  'Release Order'
)
"""
model_third = pm4py.parse_process_tree(model_third_string)

net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_third)
pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True)

# --- Declarative constraints enforced on top of the backbone ----------------
THIRD_CONSTRAINTS = {
    "precedence": [
        ("Register Dossier", "Credit Check"),
        ("Register Dossier", "Technical Review"),
        ("Credit Check", "Expedite Review"),     # P4: both mandatory first
        ("Technical Review", "Expedite Review"),
        ("Expedite Review", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Expedite Review", "Auto Approval"),
        ("Credit Check", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Technical Review", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Supplier Review", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Supplier Review", "Auto Approval"),
    ],
    "exactly_one": ["Manager Approval", "Auto Approval"],       # P5
    "mandatory_first_pass": ["Credit Check", "Technical Review"],  # P2
    "optional_but_conditional": {
        "Supplier Review": "required iff external attribute is True",  # P3
        "Expedite Review": "optional, but after both mandatory checks",  # P4
    },
    "correction": {
        "trigger": "Correct Dossier",
        "recheck_subset_of": ["Credit Check", "Technical Review"],
        "recheck_nonempty": True,
        "recheck_each_at_most_once": True,
        "must_precede_approval": True,
        "duplicate_mandatory_without_correction": "forbidden",  # P7
    },
}

def analyse_third(labels, external=None, affected=None):
    """labels: list of activity names. external: True/False/None (unknown).
    affected: set of checks the correction affected, or None if unknown.
    Returns (verdict, reasons)."""
    reasons, conditional = [], False
    if not labels or labels[0] != "Register Dossier" or labels[-1] != "Release Order":
        return "REJECT", ["P1: Register first, Release last"]
    if labels.count("Manager Approval") + labels.count("Auto Approval") != 1:
        return "REJECT", ["P5: exactly one approval"]
    approval_idx = labels.index("Manager Approval") if "Manager Approval" in labels \
        else labels.index("Auto Approval")

    # --- extract and validate correction episodes (P6) ---
    cleaned, episodes, i = [], [], 0
    while i < len(labels):
        if labels[i] == "Correct Dossier":
            if i > approval_idx:
                return "REJECT", ["Correction after approval"]
            recheck = []
            i += 1
            while i < len(labels) and labels[i] in ("Credit Check", "Technical Review"):
                recheck.append(labels[i]); i += 1
            episodes.append(recheck)
        else:
            cleaned.append(labels[i]); i += 1
    for ep in episodes:
        if not ep or len(set(ep)) != len(ep):
            return "REJECT", ["P6: correction without valid recheck subset"]
        if affected is None:
            conditional = True
            reasons.append("Affected check unknown: recheck accepted only conditionally")
        elif set(ep) != set(affected):
            return "REJECT", ["Recheck set does not match affected checks"]

    # --- first-pass checks on the cleaned trace ---
    counts = Counter(cleaned)
    if counts["Credit Check"] != 1 or counts["Technical Review"] != 1:
        return "REJECT", ["P2/P7: mandatory count != 1 in first pass"]
    for lab in ("Register Dossier", "Release Order", "Supplier Review", "Expedite Review"):
        if counts[lab] > 1:
            return "REJECT", [f"Duplicate {lab}"]
    pos = {lab: cleaned.index(lab) for lab in set(cleaned)}
    if "Expedite Review" in pos and (pos["Expedite Review"] < pos["Credit Check"]
                                     or pos["Expedite Review"] < pos["Technical Review"]):
        return "REJECT", ["P4: Expedite before a mandatory check"]
    if pos.get("Manager Approval", pos.get("Auto Approval")) != len(cleaned) - 2:
        return "REJECT", ["Approval must immediately precede Release"]

    # --- Supplier Review conditionality (P3) ---
    if "Supplier Review" not in pos:
        if external is True:
            return "REJECT", ["P3: external order without Supplier Review"]
        if external is None:
            conditional = True
            reasons.append("External/internal unknown: Supplier absence is conditional")
    else:
        if external is None:
            conditional = True
            reasons.append("External/internal unknown: Supplier presence is conditional")
        elif external is False:
            conditional = True
            reasons.append("Supplier on internal order: clarify if forbidden or merely unnecessary")
    return ("CONDITIONAL" if conditional else "ACCEPT"), reasons
```

Note: raw PM4Py alignment fitness on `model_third` alone will penalise correction traces (the backbone has no Correct Dossier node). That is intentional and documented — the *system* verdict is `analyse_third` combined with the backbone, and §9 requires reporting the two separately so this is never mistaken for a conformance score.

## 3. Declarative/external checks required

The `THIRD_CONSTRAINTS` dictionary above (precedence, exactly-one approval, mandatory set, conditional optionals, correction-episode rules) plus the `analyse_third` checker. These must be versioned and tested as part of the model, not treated as informal notes.

## 4. Explicit semantics

- **Mandatory checks:** `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` are backbone nodes without `tau`; exactly one first-pass occurrence each; either order.
- **Optional Supplier Review:** optional in the backbone, but its *requirement* is conditional on the external attribute. Absence on a known external order is a REJECT; presence on a known internal order is flagged CONDITIONAL because P3 does not say whether it is forbidden.
- **Expedite Review ordering:** optional, but DECLARE precedence requires both mandatory checks before it, and the backbone requires it before Approval. It is incomparable with Supplier Review, so both relative orders are accepted.
- **Exactly-one approval:** the `X('Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval')` choice has no `tau`; zero or two approvals are rejected. No criterion for *which* approval applies is modelled (that needs resource/data attributes — see limitations).
- **Correction/recheck:** episodes as coded above; a repeated mandatory check is legitimate only inside a validated episode (P7 enforced by construction).

## 5. Limitations from missing attributes

- Without the external/internal attribute, every no-Supplier trace is CONDITIONAL, and the true Supplier-mandatory rate cannot be estimated. Missing Supplier events in the log cannot be distinguished from genuinely internal orders.
- Without the affected-check attribute, B6-type traces are CONDITIONAL: the checker verifies the recheck is a valid subset, but cannot verify it is the *right* subset. A Credit-only correction rechecked with Technical only would pass the syntactic check and fail only when the attribute is captured.
- The Manager-vs-Auto criterion, Supplier’s position relative to the mandatory checks, correction timing relative to Supplier/Expedite, and any timing/resource constraints are all unmodelled. The checker flags the first of these as ambiguity rather than inventing a rule.

# 9. Validation Experiments

No new metric results are reported here. The programme below is what must be run — with published artefacts — before any deployment decision.

## Required components

1. **Reproducible splits:** publish the log hash, preprocessing/mapping code, and seeds. Use case-level (never event-level) splits; report both a seeded random 80/20 split *and* a variant-grouped split (all cases of a variant in one fold) *and* a temporal split. Example skeleton:
   ```python
   import random, hashlib
   rng = random.Random(42)
   cases = list(log)
   rng.shuffle(cases)
   n = int(0.8 * len(cases)); train, test = cases[:n], cases[n:]
   ```
2. **Attribute-stratified holdout:** once the external/internal and affected-check attributes are captured, stratify by them and by check order, Supplier presence, Expedite presence, and approval type. Report metrics per stratum, not only pooled.
3. **Curated negative trace suite:** B3, B4, B8, A4, A7, A8 plus generated violations of every P1–P7 rule (missing each mandatory check, Expedite before each mandatory check, zero/two approvals, duplicate without correction, correction without recheck, Register/Release misplacement). Report **false-accept rate per rule** for each model/system.
4. **Unseen-combination tests:** hold out entire combinations (e.g., all Supplier+Auto cases; all alternate-order+Expedite cases) as test folds — combination holdout, not random cases — to measure compositional generalisation directly.
5. **Missing/duplicate-event perturbation:** delete each activity type with probability p  {0.05, 0.1, 0.2} and duplicate random events; measure verdict stability. A mandatory check deleted from the log must produce a *violation*, not silent acceptance.
6. **Alignment-cost sensitivity:** recompute fitness/precision under a grid of log-move/model-move costs (including high cost for skipping mandatory activities and for duplicate labels). Report whether model rankings flip.
7. **Soundness and reachability:** `pm4py.check_soundness`, plus bounded `pm4py.play_out` to verify Register-first/Release-last, that both approvals are reachable, and that no dead activity exists. For the checker, exhaustively test all permutations of the activity multiset up to a bounded length against the intended language.
8. **Separate reporting:** fitness, precision (against positives *and* negatives), token generalisation, and simplicity reported disaggregated with case-bootstrap confidence intervals. No composite score.
9. **Stakeholder comprehension protocol:** pre-registered tasks (judge trace validity, predict the next activity, locate a policy change), blinded model order, randomised presentation, role-stratified sample, scored rubric with inter-rater agreement — replacing the informal 1–5.
10. **Decision rule for rejection despite good aggregates:** a model/system is rejected if (a) it accepts *any* hard-policy negative in the curated suite, (b) it rejects a plausible-valid probe without a routable exception path, (c) soundness fails, (d) rankings flip under plausible alignment costs, or (e) CONDITIONAL verdicts affect more than a pre-agreed share of live cases. Aggregate fitness cannot override (a)–(e).

# 10. Governance Recommendation

| Model | Exploration | Compliance enforcement | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Model S** | Permitted, clearly labelled *non-normative*, in a sandbox with no release authority | **Prohibited** — accepts B3/B4 and skipped mandatory checks | Retire as a normative artefact; keep only as a communication sketch if re-labelled, or withdraw |
| **Model C** | Permitted as a descriptive variant catalogue for audit of observed behaviour | **Not approved as a general gate** — false-rejects B5/B6 and unseen combinations; unsafe to treat as policy | Retire as the enforcement model; retain the 8 branches as an evidence inventory, with the duplicate-label and count documentation fixed |
| **Third alternative (backbone + constraints + checker)** | Approved | Approved only in **shadow mode** until the conditions below are met; then approved as the enforcement system, with CONDITIONAL cases routed to human review | Adopt provisionally; deployment gated |

**Conditions the third alternative must meet before deployment:**

1. Capture the external/internal and affected-check attributes in the case data (or formally ratify default interpretations for their absence) and re-run the attribute-stratified evaluation.
2. Pass the curated negative suite with zero false accepts on hard rules, and pass the unseen-combination and perturbation tests within pre-registered tolerances.
3. Demonstrate ranking stability under the alignment-cost grid, and publish seeds, preprocessing, log hash, and cost settings.
4. Pass soundness/reachability checks and exhaustive checker tests on bounded permutations.
5. Complete the documented stakeholder-comprehension protocol with business, operations, IT, and audit roles.

# 11. Dissenting View

## Rebuttal

The board should distrust this elegant hybrid. It is not a validated process model but three artefacts—backbone, dictionaries, and bespoke checker—whose combined behaviour has no reported fitness, precision, soundness, or stakeholder evidence. Renaming Model S’s permissiveness as “conditional acceptance” does not resolve the missing external and affected-check attributes; it merely transfers hard decisions to overloaded case handlers and guarantees inconsistent verdicts under operational pressure. The checker’s precedence and correction rules were invented from ambiguous probes, not learned or ratified, so their apparent rigour may be false precision. A binary enforcement gate is what auditors, suppliers, and automated release systems need. Model C, for all its ugliness, has a defensible rule: approve only behaviour actually observed, and route novel variants through explicit exception approval. That creates a visible queue of policy decisions instead of silently normalising combinations nobody has authorised. Model S at least communicates the intended backbone honestly to business users. The supposedly balanced third model may be the worst of both worlds—too complex for users, too permissive for compliance, and too novel to trust without the very validation programme proposed. Postponing deployment until attributes, negative suites, and cost-sensitivity results exist is prudent; pretending the hybrid is already safer is not.

## What would change my decision

I would withdraw the third alternative and endorse a fail-closed Model C-style gate if: (a) stakeholders formally ratify that only observed variants are ever legitimate, making B5-type combinations genuinely non-compliant; (b) attribute capture proves infeasible, so CONDITIONAL verdicts would dominate live traffic; (c) the negative-suite and combination-holdout results showed the checker’s precedence/correction rules false-accepting real violations; or (d) the comprehension protocol showed the hybrid is harder for stakeholders to apply correctly than either original. Conversely, if the validation programme in §9 passes with published seeds and costs, the dissent’s “unvalidated” objection is answered on evidence, not elegance.
