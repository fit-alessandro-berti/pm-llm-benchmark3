### 1. Executive memo  *( 640 words)*  
To: SVP Operations & Transformation  
From: Process-Mining Review Task-force  
Subject: Fiber-installation Redesign R – next step  

Redesign R demonstrably speeds up the *typical* customer: median lead time dropped 24 % and visible cost 13 %. These wins are concentrated in simple low-risk houses, which account for one-third of orders. However, the pilot also exposed significant downside risk:  

• Long-tail cases got markedly worse (p95 +17 days) and SLA breaches rose to 16 %.  
• First-time-right fell 6 pp, mainly in apartments and permit-sensitive buildings.  
• Complaints shifted from “waiting” to “installed but not working” and “billed before working”, indicating quality problems masked by faster early steps.  
• Process-mining shows high fitness (the model explains what we saw) but low precision (it would allow many unobserved, non-compliant paths), flagging weak controls around survey skipping and billing before activation.  
• Coordinator utilization reached 93 %; when a coordinator was off, cases stalled and second-visit demand spiked.  

In short, R contains *two different changes*:  
1. **Case ownership simplification.** It cuts handovers from 34 % to 12 % and clearly benefits simple houses.  
2. **Risk-based skipping** (field survey and pre-billing activation). This is where errors, rework, and tail duration originate; the predictive rule was never validated, and permit checks were silently skipped.  

Rolling the whole redesign back would forfeit proven benefits. Scaling it untouched would magnify quality, cost, and compliance liabilities. Therefore we recommend a **segment-specific scale-and-control strategy** supported by immediate targeted measurement.

Recommended decision – see section 6 for detail  
• **Scale** coordinator ownership nationwide **only for simple, low-risk houses** (35 % of volume).  
• **Pause** survey-skip and post-billing activation everywhere; restore mandatory field survey and pre-billing activation until the rule is validated.  
• **Run a controlled experiment** on the risk-based skipping logic with solid instrumentation (start-times, reason codes, 60-day cost, customer experience) to decide whether the rule ever pays off.  
• **Add conformance monitors** that block Billing Start before a successful Activation Test and enforce Permit Check for high-risk buildings.  
• **Resource guardrails**: cap coordinator load at 80 %; when absent, fall back to shared-team ownership to preserve resilience.  

Executing this hybrid path preserves the 6-day median advantage for one-third of customers, halts quality drift, and buys the information we need to decide on broader adoption within a 12-week horizon.

### 3. Explanation of metric conflicts  

• High fitness but low precision: the mined model matches the *observed* pilot paths, yet its many optional edges (skip survey, billing before activation) also accept behaviour that never should occur, signalling weak governance.  

• Improved median but worse tail: simple low-risk cases fly through; when the predictive rule misclassifies, problems surface late, causing long rework cycles that inflate the p90/p95.  

• Fewer handovers but lower resilience: ownership reduces transfers, but with one coordinator per case, any absence or overload stalls work because tacit knowledge isn’t shared.  

• Lower visible cost but higher downstream cost: field surveys are cheaper upfront, but added second visits, manual activations, and complaints raise end-to-end cost that current accounting misses.  

• Segment divergence: low-risk houses benefit because issues are rare; apartments and old buildings have hidden permit or wiring problems that the skip rule fails to catch, hence more failures and complaints.

### 4. Decision alternatives  

A. Scale R nationally  
+ Locks in 24 % median speed, 13 % visible cost saving  
– Exposes 60 % of cases to higher tail times, complaints, and compliance risk  
– Coordinator bottleneck likely to break at scale  
 Only viable if risk-based skipping proved reliable—currently false.  

B. Full rollback  
+ Removes new quality issues quickly  
– Discards confirmed benefits for simple houses; demotivates change agents  
 Reasonable only if regulatory penalty for early billing becomes acute and quick fix impossible.  

C. Scale coordinator ownership, suspend skips (our core recommendation)  
+ Keeps median speed gain while addressing rework driver  
+ Reduces complexity of pilot governance changes  
– Some benefit lost for genuine low-risk cases that did not need survey  
 Needs utilisation guardrails and fall-back procedure.  

D. Controlled regional experiment with better data  
+ Scientifically clean result  
– Delays benefit realisation; does not separate low-risk segment that is already proven  
 Better as complement, not sole path.  

E. Hybrid segment rollout + measurement (C + D)  
+ Harvests proven wins immediately for 35 % volume  
+ Limits exposure for risky segments; generates data to refine rule  
– Requires segmentation accuracy and dual process variants  
 Most balanced; implementation complexity acceptable.  

### 5. Value-of-information analysis (highest to lowest)  

1. M6 – Validate building-risk rule: Directly decides whether survey skipping can ever scale; high impact on quality & cost.  
2. M4 – 60-day downstream cost: Clarifies true economics; may swing decision towards or away from skipping.  
3. M1 – Randomised skip test by stratum: Provides causal evidence of benefit vs harm; complements M6.  
4. M7 – Real-time conformance monitors: Prevent regulatory/compliance breaches; high risk mitigation value.  
5. M3 – Reason codes for rework & complaints: Pinpoints root causes; medium decision value.  
6. M2 – Start timestamps instrumentation: Improves cycle-time precision; moderate value.  
7. M8 – Split customer-experience metrics: Helps marketing but unlikely to change go/no-go on process design.  
8. M5 – Ownership vs shared team under absence: Operational fine-tuning; lowest strategic impact now.  

### 6. Final recommendation  

Immediate action (next 4 weeks)  
• Adopt Alternative E. Nationally deploy *only* the coordinator-ownership pathway **for simple low-risk houses**.  
• Reinstate mandatory Field Survey and pre-billing Activation Test for all other segments.  
• Configure workflow rule that blocks Billing Start until Activation Test passes; enforce Permit Check for old buildings.  
• Cap coordinator active-case load at 80 %; when exceeded or absent, auto-route to shared team.  

Pause  
• Survey-skip rule and post-billing activation for medium/high-risk segments.  

Scale  
• Coordinator ownership plus handover reduction where segment risk  low and permit class = green.  

Measure next (start within 2 weeks)  
• Execute M6, M4, M1, M7 concurrently; add M3 reason codes.  

Decision threshold for broader rollout (review after 12 weeks, 3000 experimental cases per stratum)  
• For every segment: p90 cycle time  baseline + 5 %, first-time-right  80 %, complaints  baseline + 2 pp, precision of conformance model  0.70, and no SLA or billing-before-activation breach >1 %.  
If met, scale survey skipping and post-billing activation to that segment; otherwise retain survey and activation checks.
