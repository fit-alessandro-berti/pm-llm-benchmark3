# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

### handover-of-work network
This network maps the direct sequence of resources handling a case. For Mira_Coordinator, the high out-degree (0.82) and betweenness (0.77) indicate she sits at a structural choke point. She receives high volumes from automation (`Bot_Rules`) and distributes work to a wide range of specialists and adjusters. This implies she is a primary routing node for exceptions, not a primary processor of claim value. The low mean elapsed time (4.2 min) suggests her handovers are administrative or triage actions rather than long processing tasks.

### working-together network
This network measures co-occurrence in the same case. Mira_Coordinator has a low weighted degree (0.16) compared to Team Leads (Alice/Bruno > 0.45). This does not imply disengagement; rather, it suggests her role is distinct from the "doing" work. Her events are often short "routing" or "assignment" tags that do not overlap temporally with the long-duration work of specialists (Omar, Priya) or adjusters. She manages the *interface* between the specialist and the rest of the system, rather than working alongside them on the same claim steps.

### subcontracting network
This network identifies loops where a resource returns a case to a previous handler. Mira_Coordinator shows high subcontracting betweenness (0.69), specifically in patterns like `Mira  Specialist  Mira`. This implies she manages the lifecycle of exceptions, including the return of cases after specialist review (e.g., `Medical Review  Mira_Coordinator`). She effectively orchestrates the "return traffic" of the process, ensuring exceptions loop back for resolution or closure rather than stalling.

### performance/time statistics
Mira_Coordinator's high case count percentile (0.91) and high variance indicate she handles outlier cases with unpredictable durations. Her absence proves her criticality: median cycle time for ordinary claims remained stable, but the 90th percentile for exception cases spiked (8.4 to 13.9 days). This confirms she is the buffer for high-complexity workload; without her, exceptions accumulate rather than flow through standard queues.

### role-discovery or activity-profile similarity
Activity-based similarity suggests Mira_Coordinator is distinct from Team Leads. While Alice and Bruno approve settlements directly (high working-together with adjusters), Mira focuses on triage and rework. She is functionally similar to `Bot_Rules` in terms of volume but human in terms of decision variance, acting as a "humanized" version of the rules engine for exceptions.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

### Formal Coordinator / Exception Desk
*   **Mechanism:** A designated role responsible for filtering claims that do not meet standard automation criteria, assigning them to appropriate human experts, and managing rework loops.
*   **Evidence:** High handover centrality, explicit routing to specialists (Omar, Priya), and operational evidence that she handles 71% of complaints.
*   **Contradiction:** The "queue cleaner" label suggests she may be informal or reactive rather than a planned formal role. The low working-together network suggests she does not manage the *process* of the specialist, only the access to them.
*   **Predictions:** If formalized, she will have a defined SLA for exception handling. If removed, formal escalations to specialists will increase immediately.

### Hidden Bottleneck / Key-Person Dependency
*   **Mechanism:** The process relies on her tacit knowledge to identify "hard cases," creating a single point of failure for exception management.
*   **Evidence:** The spike in 90th percentile cycle time during her absence. Adjusters describe her as "the person who knows where hard cases should go."
*   **Contradiction:** Routine claims (low exception flag) are unaffected by her absence, implying automation is fine for standard flow.
*   **Predictions:** Training or automation of her decision logic will reduce cycle time variance. If left unaddressed, staff turnover will cause significant process disruption.

### Organizational Boundary Spanner / Triage Broker
*   **Mechanism:** She bridges the gap between automated/virtual resources (`Bot_Rules`, `Vendor_Verify`) and human specialists, translating system signals into human tasks.
*   **Evidence:** High subcontracting betweenness (loops) and interaction with vendors. She handles the "hand-off" friction between different organizational units.
*   **Contradiction:** Compliance concerns suggest she exercises uncontrolled discretion in this boundary role, potentially creating audit risks.
*   **Predictions:** Introducing a formal handover protocol (e.g., standardized criteria) will reduce her betweenness centrality by automating the boundary crossing.

### Quality Gatekeeper
*   **Mechanism:** She acts as a filter to prevent low-quality or non-compliant claims from reaching expensive specialists, protecting organizational resources.
*   **Evidence:** High share of cases with `exception_flag=True` (0.48) compared to other human resources.
*   **Contradiction:** This could lead to "gaming" where she flags cases as exceptions to avoid assignment to others, or conversely, hides errors to protect her own metrics.
*   **Predictions:** Audit trails on her `exception_flag` decisions will reveal patterns of discretion that differ from automated expectations.

### Key-Person Risk
**High.** The process relies heavily on `Mira_Coordinator`'s specific judgment. There is no evidence of a backup resource with similar centrality in the subcontracting or handover networks.

### Compliance/Control Risk
**Moderate.** The compliance reviewer's concern is valid. If Mira decides which cases are "exceptions" without transparent rules, she introduces uncontrolled discretion into a regulated process. This creates a risk of inconsistent treatment of similar claims.

This suggests a functional segregation rather than unfair distribution, though it risks burnout if the exception volume grows.

### Data-Quality Risk
