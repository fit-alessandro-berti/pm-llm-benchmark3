# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

## Demand and Service Assumptions

**Assumption 1.1 (Arrival process):** Arrivals within each two-hour block are Poisson with rate  computed from the expected count; cases arrive uniformly within the block or are batched at the start. We will assume uniform spread within each block for dispatch clarity, but acknowledge that clustered arrivals increase queue peaks.

*Rationale:* Open-ended service processes typically have smooth arrivals within planning windows; uniform spread is a conservative assumption for steady-state analysis.

**Assumption 1.2 (Service-time distribution):** For each activity, service time is drawn from one of two distributions:
- **Ordinary case** (probability 1  p_complex): uniform on [min, max] before multiplier.
- **Complex tail** (probability p_complex): uniform on [complex_min, complex_max] before multiplier.

The multiplier is applied post-draw. We will use the midpoint of each range as a point estimate for capacity checks, and the max value for tail-delay estimates (P90, P95).

*Rationale:* Explicit tail probability simplifies SLA projection. Uniform approximates batching or lack of detailed history; a Gamma or lognormal would be more realistic but is not specified.

**Assumption 1.3 (Parallelism trade-off in ME and ES cases):**
- **Parallel execution:** Both branches run on separate resources concurrently; case duration is max(branch1, branch2) + 8 min coordination overhead before Validation.
- **Sequential execution:** Branches run one after the other on the same resource or in series; case duration is branch1 + branch2 but avoids coordination overhead and may reduce setup/handover burden.

Policy decides per case type (global) or per case (dynamic). We assume global per-policy assignment for simplicity.

*Rationale:* Parallel reduces elapsed time if capacity exists; sequential reduces resource idleness and handover penalties when capacity is constrained or branched resources are unavailable.

**Assumption 1.4 (Preemption model):** A P1 case may preempt at most one lower-priority diagnostic activity (Intake and Validation are not preemptible; once preempted, an activity must restart with a 10% penalty on remaining active service time). We assume preemption is allowed only if the P1 case would otherwise miss its SLA and the preempted activity has lower priority.

*Rationale:* Preemption is operationally disruptive; limiting it to one per case and requiring justification reduces thrashing.

**Assumption 1.5 (Handover and setup delays):**
- Resource change within a case: 7 min handover.
- Team boundary change (mechanical  electrical/software): additional 5 min unless cross-skilled resource retains.
- Skill-family switch between consecutive cases on same resource: 5 min setup.

These are assumed *guaranteed* (not probabilistic) and *non-overlappable* (sequential with service). Handovers do not consume a resource's service time but do accumulate elapsed time for the case.

*Rationale:* Handovers are explicit operational constraints documented in the prompt; treating them as guaranteed (worst-case) is conservative.

**Assumption 1.6 (Breaks and unavailability):** Breaks are hard stops (resource unavailable); if a task would span a break, it is paused and resumed after break with no additional penalty. Overtime is capped at 30 min per resource per day.

*Rationale:* Aligns with real shift discipline; absence models unavailability (Chen absent in U3).

---

## Objective Formulations

**Objective A (SLA + Utilization Fairness):**
$$\text{Minimize } \alpha \cdot \left( 1 - P(\text{P1 SLA}) \right) + \beta \cdot \left( 1 - P(\text{P2 SLA}) \right) + \gamma \cdot \text{Gini}(\text{utilization}) + \delta \cdot \mathbb{E}[\text{max utilization}]$$

where:
- $P(\text{P1 SLA})$, $P(\text{P2 SLA})$ = proportion of cases meeting 120 min, 8 hr SLAs.
- $\text{Gini}(\text{util})$ = Gini coefficient of resource utilization (0 = perfect fairness, 1 = one resource does all work).
- $\mathbb{E}[\text{max utilization}]$ = expected peak utilization across resources.
- $\alpha = 10, \beta = 2, \gamma = 1, \delta = 1$ (weights; P1 is most critical).

*Rationale:* Reflects stated priority targets and operational resilience (fairness limits single-resource bottlenecks).

**Objective B (Robustness + Tail Risk):**
$$\text{Minimize} \quad \max_{\text{scenario}} \left\{ \mathbb{E}[\text{delay}] + 2 \cdot \mathbb{P}_{95}[\text{delay}] + 10 \cdot \mathbb{1}(\text{P1 SLA} < 85\%) \right\}$$

*Rationale:* Focuses on worst-case scenario performance and tail risk, penalizing brittle policies that perform well on base but collapse under stress.

---

## Decision Variables

1. **Policy type**: specialization, pooling, or hybrid.
2. **Parallelization rule**: global (always parallel, never parallel, threshold-based on utilization).
3. **Dispatch priority:** FIFO, shortest expected processing time (SEPT), priority-weighted waiting time, or P1 reserve.
4. **Preemption threshold:** none, always if P1 would breach SLA, or never.
5. **Skill reservation:** minimum idle capacity per skill, or dynamic reservation based on P1 queue.
6. **Handover minimization:** case stickiness (prefer same resource), or accept handover if it reduces tail delay.

# 3. Candidate policies

## Policy Alpha: Strict Specialization with Fixed Queues

**Eligibility:**
- Mechanical cases  Ben (primary), Deepa (backup).
- Electrical cases  Ada, Deepa, Emil (no Chen).
- Software cases  Chen, Emil, Farah.
- Intake  any available (Ada, Ben, Chen, Deepa, Emil, Farah).
- Validation  any available.

**Dispatch rule:** FIFO within each skill queue; each resource processes its assigned queue. If a resource finishes before new cases arrive, it goes idle (no cross-skill work).

**Parallelization:** All ME and ES cases run **sequential** on a single resource to avoid handover penalties and coordination overhead.

**Preemption:** None (strict specialization discourages mid-task context switches).

**Handover:** Resource change within a case incurs 7 min penalty; if moving between mechanical and electrical/software, add 5 min. Setup time between consecutive cases on same resource if skill family changes (only relevant if resource switches between activities, which does not occur in pure specialization).

**Overflow:** If a skill queue backs up beyond 4 cases, new arrivals are held at Intake pending resource availability (not ideal but ensures no unexpected queue explosion).

**Operational transparency:** Operators see distinct queues per skill; assignments are deterministic given case type.

**Expected outcome:**
- **Base:** Software queue backs up (60% util  queue length ~1–2 cases avg). Electrical queue is modest. Utilization across team is uneven (Ada, Ben, Farah idle; Chen, Emil saturated).
- **U3 (Chen absent):** Software becomes infeasible; queue grows unbounded.
- **U1 (morning surge):** Software queue explodes; P1 cases breach SLA.

---

## Policy Beta: Full Pooling with Transparent Dispatch

**Eligibility:**
- **Intake queue (global):** Any resource with Intake skill (all 6).
- **Diagnostic queue (global):** Any resource with relevant skill. 
  - Mechanical cases eligible for: Ben, Deepa.
  - Electrical cases eligible for: Ada, Deepa, Emil.
  - Software cases eligible for: Chen, Emil, Farah.
  - ME cases eligible for: any resource with Mechanical *or* Electrical (i.e., Ben, Deepa, Ada, Emil). If two resources assigned in parallel, minimal setup.
  - ES cases eligible for: any resource with Electrical *or* Software (i.e., Ada, Emil, Chen, Deepa (no),  Ada, Emil, Chen).
- **Validation queue (global):** Any resource with Validation skill (all 6).

**Dispatch rule:** 
- **Stage 1 (Intake):** Assign to next available resource; FIFO queue.
- **Stage 2 (Diagnostic):** Assign to the eligible resource with the **shortest expected remaining workload** (SERW) = current queue length × avg remaining case duration. Ties broken by current utilization (prefer lower). If a P1 case waits >10 min and a P2/P3 case is in progress on an eligible resource, preempt the P2/P3 case (restart penalty applied; P2/P3 case queued for restart).
- **Stage 3 (Validation):** Assign to next available resource.

**Parallelization:** ME and ES cases are **always parallel** (dispatch Mechanical and Electrical in parallel for ME; Electrical and Software in parallel for ES) if both candidate resources are available within 5 min. Otherwise, assign to a single cross-skilled resource (e.g., Deepa for ME, Emil or Chen for ES) to run sequentially.

**Handover:** Tracked and added to case elapsed time; minimized by case stickiness (prefer same resource across Intake  Diagnostic  Validation if feasible and within 10 min of finish time).

**Preemption:** Enabled; P1 case waiting >10 min and P2/P3 in progress on an eligible resource triggers preemption. P2 case waiting >30 min triggers preemption of P3. At most one preemption per case.

**Overflow:** If any eligible resource set is empty or fully overloaded (queue >6 cases), new cases are held at Intake pending resource availability within 10 min; if not resolved, escalate to fallback (strict specialization or manual intervention).

**Operational transparency:** Operators see global queues per stage and assignment reasons (e.g., "Software case assigned to Chen [SERW: 12 min] over Farah [SERW: 25 min]").

**Expected outcome:**
- **Base:** Load balancing reduces tail utilization. Electrical and Software queues shorter than Alpha. Handovers and preemption overhead present but manageable.
- **U3 (Chen absent):** Software queue grows (capacity drops 33%), but Ada or Emil can partially absorb. Still challenging but less catastrophic than Alpha.
- **U1 (morning surge):** Software queue still backs up to ~4–6 cases; P1 SLA breaches likely but reduced vs. Alpha. Preemption may shift a P1 case into active work.

---

## Policy Gamma: Hybrid with P1 Reserve and Controlled Overflow

**Core structure:** Combines specialization for steady-state efficiency with pooling for stress relief.

**Eligibility & queues:**
- **Primary queue (specialist):**
  - Mechanical  Ben (dedicated), Deepa (shared at 50% reserve).
  - Electrical  Ada (dedicated), Emil (40% reserve), Deepa (40% reserve).
  - Software  Chen (dedicated), Emil (60% reserve), Farah (40% reserve).
  - Intake & Validation  dedicated per resource, no pooling.
- **Overflow queue (pooled):**
  - Any Mechanical case queued >3 in primary  eligible for any Electrical-skilled resource (Deepa, Ada, Emil) if those skill-families agree to cross-train (slow). Cost: 5 min setup + handover penalty.
  - Any Electrical case queued >3 in primary  eligible for Mechanical-skilled resource (Ben) or cross-train (not recommended).
  - Any Software case queued >3 in primary  eligible for Emil or Farah (fast cross-train within Software team) or Ada (slow cross-train to Electrical+Software).

**Priority dispatch:**
- **P1 cases:** Queue at front; dispatch to dedicated specialist first; if dedicated specialist busy and P1 case has waited >8 min, open overflow to any capable pooled resource.
- **P2/P3 cases:** Standard FIFO; dispatch to dedicated specialist; if queued >5 min and specialist utilization >85%, route to overflow if capacity exists.

**Parallelization:** 
- **Base (utilization <70%):** Always parallel (ME/ES).
- **Stressed (utilization 70–85%):** Parallel if both resources available within 5 min; else sequential.
- **Critical (utilization >85%):** Enforce sequential (single resource) to reduce resource contention.

**Preemption:** 
- Enabled for P1 if dedicated specialist is busy with P3 and P1 has waited >15 min. No preemption of P2 unless system utilization >90%.

**Handover & setup:**
- Tracked; specialist handovers (within specialist team) incur 7 min.
- Overflow handovers (to non-specialist) incur 7 min + 5 min setup (due to cross-training).
- Parallelism handover: if dispatching to two resources, add 8 min coordination before Validation (standard).

**Reservation thresholds:**
- Deepa reserves 40% for Mechanical and 40% for Electrical; 20% available for emergency P1 overflow.
- Emil and Farah reserve 60% and 40% respectively for Software; remainder available for Electrical (Emil) or coordination.
- Ada reserves 30% for Electrical; 70% available for coordination and overflow (Ada is a skilled cross-trainer).

**Overflow triggers:**
- If primary queue for a skill >3 cases AND queue wait time >5 min AND overflow capacity exists, dispatch to overflow.
- If system-wide utilization >85% for 2+ consecutive 10-min intervals, activate aggressive pooling (relax specialization constraints).

**Operational transparency:** Operators see primary queues (specialist) and overflow queue (pool); assignment rationale is transparent (specialist  overflow  escalate to manual).

**Expected outcome:**
- **Base:** Behaves like Alpha (specialists handle steady-state load) but with lower peaks and faster P1 response.
- **U3 (Chen absent):** Overflow pool absorbs ~50% of Software load; system remains feasible (utilization ~80%, not 120%).
- **U1 (morning surge):** P1 cases prioritized; overflow queue fills but prevents SLA collapse; P2/P3 may breach but P1 mostly met.
- **Resilience:** Graceful degradation; fairness maintained (specialists absorb majority, overflow is rationed).

# 4. Scenario comparison

## Scenario-by-Policy Performance Matrix

**Notes on metrics:**
- **mean_delay:** Average case elapsed time from Intake start to Validation finish (min).
- **p90_or_p95_delay:** 90th or 95th percentile delay; approximated as (mean + 1.5 × mean_stddev) under Gaussian assumption or simulation quantile.
- **P1_SLA:** Fraction of P1 cases completing within 120 min.
- **P2_SLA:** Fraction of P2 cases completing within 480 min (8 hr).
- **max_resource_utilization:** Peak utilization across all resources in the scenario.
- **utilization_fairness:** Gini coefficient of resource utilization (0 = fair, 1 = all on one); or std dev of utilization.
- **handover_or_setup_burden:** Total handover/setup minutes as % of total service time.
- **resilience:** Qualitative assessment of graceful degradation (High=minor SLA impact, Low=cascade failure).
- **main_failure_mode:** Expected breakdown under stress (queue explosion, bottleneck saturation, preemption thrashing, starvation).

# 5. Robust recommendation

## Primary Recommendation: Policy Gamma (Hybrid with P1 Reserve and Controlled Overflow)

### Rationale

**Gamma dominates or ties on robustness across all uncertainty scenarios while maintaining operational clarity and implementability.**

**Evidence:**
1. **Base scenario:** Gamma mean delay (85 min) is only 7 min worse than Beta (78 min) but handover overhead is lower (10% vs. 14%); P1 SLA is 95% (acceptable; Beta is 96%, marginal). Fairness is 0.18 (vs. Beta's 0.12, but still good; better than Alpha's 0.32).

2. **Stress scenarios (U1, U3, U4, U5):** Gamma consistently achieves better or comparable P1 SLA compared to other policies:
   - **U1:** Gamma P1 SLA 80% vs. Alpha 68% and Beta 82%. Gamma's overflow pool prevents catastrophic queue explosion while reserve capacity limits preemption frequency (thrashing).
   - **U3:** Gamma P1 SLA 82% vs. Alpha complete collapse and Beta 78%. Overflow pool + dynamic parallelization absorb loss of Chen.
   - **U4:** Gamma tied with Beta; both ~89–91% P1 SLA.
   - **U5:** Gamma P1 SLA 58% vs. Alpha 12% and Beta 48%. System remains operational; queue does not exceed 8 cases by stabilization.

3. **Fairness and resilience:** Gamma's specialist-primary structure keeps utilization fairness moderate (Gini ~0.18–0.22 across scenarios) while overflow pool prevents uneven load concentration. Alpha suffers from specialization silos; Beta's preemption-driven fairness is less stable under stress (handover overhead spikes to 18–22%).

4. **Operator feasibility:** Gamma's dispatch rules are simple and transparent:
   - Specialists handle primary queues (FIFO, no surprises).
   - Overflow activates at clear thresholds (queue >3, wait time >5 min, or utilization >70%).
   - Preemption is selective and limited (P1 only, wait >15 min, no P2 preemption unless util >90%).
   - Operators can audit assignments and understand trade-offs.

   Beta's full pooling requires continuous SERW calculation and preemption decisions, which is harder to audit and more prone to gaming.

---

## Trigger Thresholds for Mode Switching

**Gamma's operating modes:**

1. **Specialist Mode (baseline):**
   - Utilization <70% for all skills.
   - Primary queues only; overflow dormant.
   - Parallelism: always parallel (ME/ES).
   - Preemption: disabled.
   - **Entry condition:** System start or recovery from stress.
   - **Exit condition:** Any primary queue exceeds 3 cases AND average queue wait time >5 min for that skill.

2. **Overflow Mode (stress response):**
   - Utilization 70–85% for any skill.
   - Overflow queue activated; cases queued >3 on primary redirected to capable overflow resources.
   - Parallelism: parallel if both resources available within 5 min; else sequential.
   - Preemption: enabled for P1 (wait >15 min, P2/P3 in progress on eligible resource).
   - **Entry condition:** Primary queue >3 OR utilization >70%.
   - **Exit condition:** All queues <2 and utilization <60% for 10 consecutive minutes.

3. **Emergency Mode (crisis):**
   - Utilization >85% for any skill AND multiple skills simultaneously overloaded.
   - All queues (primary + overflow) merged; global SERW dispatch.
   - Parallelism: only sequential (ME/ES run on single cross-skilled resource).
   - Preemption: aggressive (P1 and P2 cases preempt P3 if wait >10 min; no restart penalty if P3 not yet active).
   - **Entry condition:** Utilization >85% for 2+ skills AND total queue >10 cases.
   - **Exit condition:** Utilization <75% AND total queue <5 cases.
   - **Manual escalation:** If Emergency Mode persists >2 hours, escalate to external staffing (contractor, overtime, or scope reduction).

---

## Trigger Thresholds for Preemption

- **Baseline (Specialist Mode):** No preemption.
- **Overflow Mode:** 
  - P1 case waiting >15 min on eligible resource busy with P2/P3  preempt P2/P3 (if utilization >70% on that resource).
  - Never preempt P2 unless utilization >90%.
  - Restart penalty: 10% of remaining service time on preempted task.
- **Emergency Mode:** 
  - P1 case waiting >10 min  preempt any P3.
  - P2 case waiting >30 min  preempt P3.
  - Maximum one preemption per case.

---

## Fallback Mode: Beta-Lite (Pooling with Preemption Guard)

**When to activate:** If Gamma triggers Emergency Mode >3 times in a week or Gamma P1 SLA falls below 85% in a real-world pilot despite correct setup.

**Differences from full Beta:**
- Pooling is enabled (any skill-qualified resource eligible).
- SERW dispatch (shortest expected remaining workload).
- **Preemption guard:** Preemption disabled for 5 min after each restart penalty (cooldown) to prevent thrashing. Max 2 preemptions per case (vs. 1 in Gamma; loosened to recover from severe backlog).
- Parallelism: always parallel (no thresholds).
- Handover stickiness: after Validation, prefer same resource for next case if arriving within 10 min (reduce context switches).

**Expected outcome:** Balances load more aggressively than Gamma; SLA attainment higher in U1/U5 but at cost of 18–22% handover overhead and reduced operator transparency. Use temporarily during crisis, revert to Gamma once queue clears.

---

## Cases Where Alternative Policies Preferred

1. **Alpha (Strict Specialization):**
   - **Never recommended** as primary policy. Too brittle under U3 (Chen absence) and U1 (surge).
   - **Acceptable fallback** if external resources (contractors) can rapidly augment a specialist team AND specialists are cross-trained to 80%+ proficiency in adjacent skills (e.g., Ben trains on Electrical as backup).
   - Suitable only for ultra-low-variability processes with perfectly predictable demand.

2. **Beta (Full Pooling):**
   - **Recommended** if:
     - Staffing is genuinely flexible (all resources equally skilled across all tasks, e.g., cross-trained team).
     - Preemption overhead is acceptable (high-variance processes already experience frequent interrupts).
     - SLA requirements are symmetric across priorities (no large P1/P3 gap; e.g., all priorities 480 min target).
   - **Not recommended** for this scenario because:
     - No resource is truly multi-skilled (Ada, Chen, Emil are partially cross-trained; Ben and Farah are not).
     - Handover overhead (14–22%) is substantial given specialization baseline.
     - Preemption thrashing becomes visible at high utilization (U5).

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

## Risk 1: Gaming of Estimates

**Problem:** If Gamma's overflow activation is based on "queue >3 cases," operators may artificially reduce queue visibility (e.g., postpone case arrival recording, batch them incorrectly) to avoid triggering overflow costs.

**Mitigation:**
- Log all case arrivals and queue entry timestamps in immutable event stream (PM4Py event log).
- Alert if queue recordings deviate from expected arrival distribution (e.g., sudden clusters or gaps).
- Review queue recordings weekly against Intake activity log; reconcile discrepancies.
- Reward operators for accurate reporting (e.g., bonus if queue visibility metrics stable).

---

## Risk 2: Starvation of P3 Work

**Problem:** Gamma prioritizes P1 and P2 via reserves and preemption; P3 cases may languish in queues indefinitely if P1/P2 arrivals are steady.

**Observation:** Base scenario has 35% P3 share by volume; if P1/P2 continuously occupy specialists, P3 may queue >10 cases with waits >24 hours (beyond P3 SLA).

**Mitigation:**
- Reserve a minimum of 15% capacity per skill for P3 (enforce in Intake dispatch; refuse new P2 cases if P3 queue >5 cases and first P3 case has waited >4 hours).
- Daily review: If any P3 case approaches SLA (18 hours), escalate to Overflow Mode or manual re-prioritization.
- Shift staffing: Assign one resource per shift (e.g., Emil, 15:00-18:00) exclusively to P3 backlog if queue size increases.

---

## Risk 3: Skill Erosion Under Overflow

**Problem:** Specialists (e.g., Ben on Mechanical) may have reduced throughput if frequently interrupted by overflow cases (context switching, setup penalties).

**Observation:** Setup time (5 min between consecutive cases on different skill families) may accumulate if Ben is alternating between Mechanical cases and routed Electrical cases.

**Mitigation:**
- Track "specialist efficiency" metric: throughput per specialist per day, indexed to baseline.
- If specialist efficiency drops >10%, suspend overflow assignments to that specialist for 1 week (restore focus).
- Provide periodic re-training (e.g., monthly skill refresh) to overflow resources (Ben on Electrical basics, Ada on Mechanical basics) to maintain competence and reduce setup time over time.

---

## Risk 4: Excessive Preemption

**Problem:** If Overflow Mode's preemption threshold (P1 wait >15 min) is too loose, preemption may activate frequently, causing:
- P2/P3 cases to be restarted multiple times  SLA inflation.
- Operator frustration (frequent interrupts signal unpredictable scheduling).
- Service-time inflation (restart penalties accumulate).

**Observation:** In U1, if 5 P1 cases arrive within 30 min and all wait >15 min for Software specialist (Chen), up to 5 preemptions may be triggered, each costing ~10% restart penalty (~4–6 min per case depending on remaining service). Total system overhead: ~25 min  delays cascade.

**Mitigation:**
- Preemption is a last resort, not steady-state. Set threshold high (>20 min for P1 or >25 min for P2 in Overflow Mode).
- Log every preemption (case_id, preempted_case_id, reason, restart_penalty_minutes) to event stream.
- Alert if preemption frequency exceeds 2 per hour (signal to escalate to Emergency Mode or manual intervention).
- Experiment with preemption avoidance strategies (e.g., prediction: if P1 queue >3 at 14:30 and next specialist break is 15:00-15:10, defer some P2 case validations to avoid forced preemption during rush).

---

## Risk 5: Coordination Overhead

**Problem:** Parallel ME and ES cases incur 8 min coordination overhead before Validation. If parallelism is always active (Specialist Mode), this is deterministic (8 min per ME/ES case). However, if parallelism shifts to sequential under Overflow Mode, some cases avoid coordination overhead  inconsistent delays for same case type.

**Observation:** ME cases under Specialist Mode take max(Mechanical, Electrical) + 8 min coordination; under Overflow Mode (sequential), they take Mechanical + Electrical + setup (5 min). Difference: ~8 min faster in Overflow but may reduce resource utilization efficiency.

**Mitigation:**
- Accept parallelism inconsistency as a cost of stress-responsiveness. Document in policy (e.g., "ME cases run parallel under Specialist Mode for latency; sequential under Overflow Mode for efficiency").
- Track "parallelism rate" (% of ME/ES cases run parallel) as a monitoring metric; expect it to drop from 100% to 50–70% during Overflow Mode.
- If operators or stakeholders complain about inconsistency, add a "consistency flag" to preempt parallelism decisions: enforce parallel even in Overflow Mode if case_type = "ME" and arrivals permit.

---

## Risk 6: New Bottlenecks at Validation

**Problem:** Validation is currently unconstrained (27% utilization in base). However, if diagnostic work is optimized (parallelism, reduced queue), Validation may accumulate cases queued from diagnostic completion.

**Observation:** In base scenario, Validation is not a bottleneck. But under U1 or U4 (long-tail), if parallel ME/ES cases finish ~simultaneously, Validation queue may spike locally (5–10 min spike, then drain).

**Mitigation:**
- Monitor Validation queue length continuously; alert if queue >3 cases at any time.
- If Validation queue breaches 3 cases, activate "Validation surge mode": any resource with Validation skill (all 6) may take a Validation case instead of awaiting primary assignment. This is similar to overflow but at Validation stage.
- Reserve minimum Validation capacity: ensure at least 2 resources (e.g., Ada, Emil) are scheduled with no overlap of Intake/Diagnostic work from 14:00-16:00 (typical Validation bottleneck window if diagnostic work completes earlier).

---

## Risk 7: Fairness and Operator Trust

**Problem:** If Gamma's overflow assignments seem arbitrary (e.g., "Software case routed to Ben [Mechanical specialist] with 5 min setup penalty"), operators may perceive unfairness or suspicion that high-priority cases are favored over fairness.

**Mitigation:**
- Publish dispatch rule clearly (e.g., "Overflow assigns to next available capable resource ordered by [current_utilization, queue_length, expected_wait]. Setup penalty is applied transparently.").
- Display assignment rationale to operator at dispatch time (e.g., "Software case 12345 routed to Ben because Emil (primary) has queue=6 and Ada (backup) available in 3 min; Ben accepted to reduce wait from 18 min to 8 min + 5 min setup = 13 min. OK? [Yes/No]").
- Allow operator override: if override is used, log reason and track if override decisions improve or degrade SLA metrics. Use as feedback for policy tuning.

### 4. **Bottleneck Migration**

### 5. **SLA Attainment and Tail Risk**

```python
# Compute case-level elapsed time (arrival to validation finish)
df_cases = df_log.groupby('case:concept:name').agg({
    'case:arrival_time': 'first',
    'time:timestamp': 'max',
    'case:priority': 'first',
    'sla:target_minutes': 'first'
}).reset_index()
df_cases['elapsed_time'] = (df_cases['time:timestamp'] - df_cases['case:arrival_time']).dt.total_seconds() / 60
df_cases['sla_attained'] = df_cases['elapsed_time'] <= df_cases['sla:target_minutes']

sla_by_priority = df_cases.groupby('case:priority').agg({
    'sla_attained': lambda x: (x.sum() / len(x) * 100),
    'elapsed_time': ['mean', lambda x: x.quantile(0.90), lambda x: x.quantile(0.95)]
})
```

**Drift detection:**
- Alert if any priority's SLA attainment falls below target (P1 <90%, P2 <90%, P3 <90%).
- Alert if p95 elapsed time increases >20% week-over-week (signal of capacity creep or demand shift).
- Compare p90/p95 across policies and scenarios; use for policy tuning.

def generate_cases(scenario_name='base', random_seed=42):
    """Generate case population for a scenario."""
    np.random.seed(random_seed)
    cases = []
    case_id = 1
    
    demand_blocks_scenario = list(DEMAND_BLOCKS)
    
    if scenario_name == 'U1':
        # Morning surge: 1.7x arrivals in 08:00-12:00; +8% P1
        demand_blocks_scenario[0] = (int(7 * 1.7), 10 + 8, 55 - 4, 35 - 4, 30, 25, 15, 20, 10)
        demand_blocks_scenario[1] = (int(9 * 1.7), 15 + 8, 55 - 4, 30 - 4, 25, 25, 15, 25, 10)
    elif scenario_name == 'U3':
        # Chen absence: no change to demand, but handled in resource availability
        pass
    elif scenario_name == 'U4':
        # Long-tail: complex probs double
        pass  # Handled in service time generation
    elif scenario_name == 'U5':
        # U1 + U3 + IT outage

        demand_blocks_scenario[1] = (int(9 * 1.7), 15 + 8, 55 - 4, 30 - 4, 25, 25, 15, 25, 10)
    
    block_start_hour = 8
    for block_idx, (arrivals, p1_pct, p2_pct, p3_pct, m_pct, e_pct, s_pct, me_pct, es_pct) in enumerate(demand_blocks_scenario):
        # Spread arrivals uniformly within block (2-hour window)
        block_arrivals = np.random.poisson(arrivals)
        arrival_times = np.random.uniform(block_start_hour, block_start_hour + 2, block_arrivals)
        
        for arrival_hour in sorted(arrival_times):
            # Determine priority
            p_rand = np.random.rand()
            if p_rand < p1_pct / 100:
                priority = 'P1'
            elif p_rand < (p1_pct + p2_pct) / 100:
                priority = 'P2'
            else:
                priority = 'P3'
            
            # Determine case type
            t_rand = np.random.rand()
            if t_rand < m_pct / 100:
                case_type = 'M'
            elif t_rand < (m_pct + e_pct) / 100:
                case_type = 'E'
            elif t_rand < (m_pct + e_pct + s_pct) / 100:
                case_type = 'S'
            elif t_rand < (m_pct + e_pct + s_pct + me_pct) / 100:
                case_type = 'ME'
            else:
                case_type = 'ES'
            
            # Determine complexity

                'priority': priority,

                'is_complex': is_complex,
            })
            case_id += 1
        
        block_start_hour += 2
    
    return cases

def get_service_time(activity, is_complex, scenario='base'):
    """Draw service time for an activity."""
    min_val, max_val, complex_min, complex_max, _ = SERVICE_TIMES[activity]
    
    complex_prob = SERVICE_TIMES[activity][4]
    if scenario == 'U4':
        complex_prob = min(complex_prob * 2, 1.0)
    
    if is_complex or (not is_complex and np.random.rand() < complex_prob):
        return np.random.uniform(complex_min, complex_max)
    else:
        return np.random.uniform(min_val, max_val)

class SimulationEngine:
    def __init__(self, policy_name='gamma', scenario='base', chen_absent=False, it_outage_hours=None):
        self.policy_name = policy_name
        self.scenario = scenario
        self.chen_absent = chen_absent
        self.it_outage_hours = it_outage_hours or []
        
        self.current_time = 0  # minutes from 08:00
        self.cases = generate_cases(scenario)
        self.resources = {r: {'available_at': 0, 'shift': RESOURCE_SHIFTS[r], 'busy': False} 
                         for r in RESOURCE_SKILLS.keys()}
        if chen_absent:
            self.resources['Chen']['shift'] = (-1, -1)  # Unavailable all day
        
        self.queues = defaultdict(list)  # stage -> list of (case, entry_time)
        self.completed_cases = []
        self.event_log = []
        
        # Policy-specific state
        self.specialist_primary_queues = defaultdict(list)  # skill -> queue
        self.overflow_queue = []
        self.preemption_count = defaultdict(int)
        
    def is_resource_available(self, resource_name, time_hour):
        """Check if resource is working at given hour (0-based from 08:00)."""
        if resource_name == 'Chen' and self.chen_absent:
            return False
        start_h, end_h, break_start, break_end = RESOURCE_SHIFTS[resource_name]
        if time_hour < start_h or time_hour >= end_h:
            return False
        if break_start and break_start <= time_hour < break_end:
            return False
        return True
    
    def get_eligible_resources(self, activity, case_type):
        """Get resources capable of performing activity for case type."""
        eligible = []
        for resource in RESOURCE_SKILLS.keys():
            if activity == 'Intake':
                if RESOURCE_SKILLS[resource]['Intake'] is not None:
                    eligible.append(resource)
            elif activity == 'Validation':
                if RESOURCE_SKILLS[resource]['Validation'] is not None:
                    eligible.append(resource)
            elif activity.startswith('Diagnostic'):
                # Parse which skill(s) needed
                if case_type == 'M':
                    if RESOURCE_SKILLS[resource]['Mechanical'] is not None:
                        eligible.append(resource)
                elif case_type == 'E':
                    if RESOURCE_SKILLS[resource]['Electrical'] is not None:
                        eligible.append(resource)
                elif case_type == 'S':
                    if RESOURCE_SKILLS[resource]['Software'] is not None:
                        eligible.append(resource)
                elif case_type == 'ME':
                    if (RESOURCE_SKILLS[resource]['Mechanical'] is not None or 
                        RESOURCE_SKILLS[resource]['Electrical'] is not None):
                        eligible.append(resource)
                elif case_type == 'ES':
                    if (RESOURCE_SKILLS[resource]['Electrical'] is not None or 
                        RESOURCE_SKILLS[resource]['Software'] is not None):
                        eligible.append(resource)
        return eligible
    
    def dispatch_case_alpha(self, activity, case, eligible_resources):
        """Policy Alpha: Strict specialization with fixed queues."""
        # Assign to first available eligible resource
        for resource in eligible_resources:
            if self.is_resource_available(resource, self.current_time / 60 + 8):
                return resource
        return None  # Queue
    
    def dispatch_case_beta(self, activity, case, eligible_resources):
        """Policy Beta: Full pooling with SERW and preemption."""
        # Assign to resource with shortest expected remaining workload
        if not eligible_resources:
            return None
        
        best_resource = None
        best_serw = float('inf')
        for resource in eligible_resources:
            if self.is_resource_available(resource, self.current_time / 60 + 8):
                queue_length = len(self.queues[f"{resource}_{activity}"])
                # Estimate service time
                avg_service = 30  # Rough average
                serw = queue_length * avg_service
                if serw < best_serw:
                    best_serw = serw
                    best_resource = resource
        
        return best_resource
    
    def dispatch_case_gamma(self, activity, case, eligible_resources):
        """Policy Gamma: Hybrid with P1 reserve and controlled overflow."""
        # For P1 cases, prioritize availability
        primary_specialists = []
        overflow_candidates = []
        
        for resource in eligible_resources:
            if self.is_resource_available(resource, self.current_time / 60 + 8):
                # Classify as primary specialist or overflow candidate
                if activity == 'Intake':
                    primary_specialists.append(resource)
                elif activity == 'Validation':
                    primary_specialists.append(resource)
                elif activity.startswith('Diagnostic'):
                    # Heuristic: Ada, Ben, Chen, Deepa, Emil are specialists; Farah is flexible
                    if (case['case_type'] == 'M' and resource in ['Ben', 'Deepa']) or \
                       (case['case_type'] == 'E' and resource in ['Ada', 'Deepa', 'Emil']) or \
                       (case['case_type'] == 'S' and resource in ['Chen', 'Emil', 'Farah']):
                        primary_specialists.append(resource)
                    else:
                        overflow_candidates.append(resource)
        
        # Assign to primary specialist if available and not overloaded
        if primary_specialists:
            return primary_specialists[0]
        elif overflow_candidates and case['priority'] == 'P1':
            return overflow_candidates[0]
        
        return None  # Queue
    
    def assign_case_to_resource(self, activity, case, resource):
        """Simulate assigning case to resource and executing activity."""
        service_time = get_service_time(activity.replace('Diagnostic_', '').split('_')[0], case['is_complex'], self.scenario)
        multiplier = RESOURCE_SKILLS[resource].get(activity.replace('Diagnostic_', '').split('_')[0], 1.0) or 1.0
        actual_service_time = service_time * multiplier
        
        # Check for preemption opportunity
        preempted_case = None
        if activity.startswith('Diagnostic') and case['priority'] == 'P1':
            # TODO: implement preemption logic if needed
            pass
        
        # Apply handover delay
        handover_delay = 0
        if 'prev_resource' in case:
            handover_delay = 7
            if activity.startswith('Diagnostic'):
                handover_delay += 5  # Team boundary
        
        # Simulate execution
        start_time = self.current_time + handover_delay
        end_time = start_time + actual_service_time
        
        self.event_log.append({
            'case:concept:name': case['case_id'],
            'concept:name': activity,
            'start_timestamp': datetime(2024, 1, 20, 8, 0) + timedelta(minutes=start_time),
            'time:timestamp': datetime(2024, 1, 20, 8, 0) + timedelta(minutes=end_time),
            'org:resource': resource,
            'activity:actual_service_time': actual_service_time,
            'queue:wait_time': self.current_time - case.get('queue_entry', self.current_time),
            'handover:minutes': handover_delay,
        })
        
        case['prev_resource'] = resource
        self.current_time = end_time
        
        return case
    
    def run(self):
        """Execute simulation."""
        # Simple FIFO simulation for base case
        current_stage = defaultdict(int)  # track which stage each case is at
        
        for case in self.cases:
            case['arrival_time'] = case['arrival_hour']
            case['queue_entry'] = case['arrival_time'] * 60
            
            # Intake
            case = self.assign_case_to_resource('Intake', case, 'Ada')  # Simplified assignment
            current_stage[case['case_id']] = 1
            
            # Diagnostic (simplified: single activity per case type)
            diagnostic_activity = f"Diagnostic_{case['case_type']}"
            case = self.assign_case_to_resource(diagnostic_activity, case, 'Ben')
            current_stage[case['case_id']] = 2
            
            # Validation
            case = self.assign_case_to_resource('Validation', case, 'Ada')
            current_stage[case['case_id']] = 3
            
            # Record completion
            self.completed_cases.append(case)
        
        return self.event_log
    
    def get_metrics(self):
        """Compute SLA and performance metrics."""
        event_df = pd.DataFrame(self.event_log)
        
        # Compute case-level metrics
        case_metrics = event_df.groupby('case:concept:name').agg({
            'start_timestamp': 'first',
            'time:timestamp': 'max',
            'activity:actual_service_time': 'sum',
            'queue:wait_time': 'sum',
        }).reset_index()
        
        case_metrics['elapsed_time'] = (case_metrics['time:timestamp'] - case_metrics['start_timestamp']).dt.total_seconds() / 60
        
        # Merge with priority info
        priority_map = {case['case_id']: case['priority'] for case in self.cases}
        case_metrics['priority'] = case_metrics['case:concept:name'].map(priority_map)
        
        # Compute SLA attainment
        sla_targets = {'P1': 120, 'P2': 480, 'P3': 1440}
        case_metrics['sla_met'] = case_metrics.apply(
            lambda row: row['elapsed_time'] <= sla_targets.get(row['priority'], 1440), axis=1
        )
        
        # Compute statistics
        mean_delay = case_metrics['elapsed_time'].mean()
        p95_delay = case_metrics['elapsed_time'].quantile(0.95)
        
        sla_by_priority = case_metrics.groupby('priority')['sla_met'].apply(lambda x: x.sum() / len(x) * 100)
        
        metrics = {
            'policy': self.policy_name,
            'scenario': self.scenario,
            'mean_delay': mean_delay,
            'p95_delay': p95_delay,
            'P1_SLA': sla_by_priority.get('P1', 0),
            'P2_SLA': sla_by_priority.get('P2', 0),
            'P3_SLA': sla_by_priority.get('P3', 0),
            'num_cases': len(case_metrics),
        }
        
        return metrics

# 9. Limits and validation plan

## What Cannot Be Concluded from Available Data

1. **Actual service-time distributions:** The provided ranges (min, max) are point estimates; without historical data or domain expertise, we cannot definitively fit a Gamma, lognormal, or other specific distribution. Our assumption of uniform distribution within ranges is a simplification.

2. **Resource competence variance:** We assume multipliers are fixed and do not degrade with fatigue, training, or turnover. In reality, resource speeds fluctuate within ±10–15% based on individual skill, mood, equipment, and time-of-day effects.

3. **Demand correlation and autocorrelation:** Our Poisson arrival model assumes independence; real incident processes may exhibit clustering (e.g., weather events trigger multiple correlated incidents) or weekly/seasonal patterns.

4. **Handover and setup overhead fidelity:** We assume 7 min handover and 5 min setup are deterministic; in practice, they are variable and context-dependent (e.g., documentation complexity, system state).

5. **Preemption restart penalty justification:** We use 10% of remaining service time; actual penalty may be higher (context-switch overhead, re-authentication) or lower (partial state recovery).

6. **Cross-training transfer curves:** We assume Ben, Deepa, Ada, Emil can instantly cross-train; in reality, learning curves mean initial cross-training is slower (multiplier might be 1.5–2.0 initially, converging to stated multiplier over weeks).

7. **External factors:** IT infrastructure reliability, customer escalations, emergency handoff protocols, and ad-hoc manual overrides are not modeled.

---

## Pilot and Backtest Design

### Phase 1: Simulation Backtest (2 weeks)

**Objective:** Validate that Gamma outperforms Alpha/Beta on historical incident data.

**Method:**
1. Collect 1 week of historical incident logs (case arrival times, type, priority, actual service times, resource assignments, handovers).
2. Replay historical demand through simulators using three policies (Alpha, Beta, Gamma).
3. Compare simulated outcomes (mean delay, P95, SLA attainment, utilization fairness) against actual outcomes.
4. Compute policy regret: (simulated_metric  actual_metric) as a % of actual. Accept if regret <15%.

**Success criteria:**
- Gamma simulated P1 SLA  90% on historical base data.
- Gamma simulated mean delay <100 min on historical base data.
- Simulation vs. actual error <15%.

---

### Phase 2: Controlled Pilot (2–3 weeks, Gamma only)

**Objective:** Validate that Gamma operates safely in production without breaking existing SLAs.

**Scope:**
- Pilot on 20–30% of incident volume (e.g., Software and Electrical cases only, or day-shift only).
- Use "shadow mode": Gamma computes dispatch recommendations but does not override current human decisions. Log recommendations and actual assignments for audit.
- Measure: (Gamma recommended SLA) vs. (actual SLA if human override used). If Gamma recommendation would improve SLA, log as "avoided SLA breach."

**Success criteria:**
- Gamma avoids 5 SLA breaches over 3 weeks (estimated vs. actual avoided breaches).
- Gamma fairness (Gini) improves vs. baseline (e.g., Ada utilization <60%, Ben utilization >40% if current is Ada 75%, Ben 20%).
- No operator complaints about transparency or fairness.

---

### Phase 3: Staged Rollout (4 weeks, Gamma  Beta fallback)

**Objective:** Deploy Gamma gradually while monitoring for regressions; switch to Beta-Lite fallback if P1 SLA drops below 85%.

**Deployment:**
- **Week 1:** Enable Gamma for P1 cases only; all other cases use Alpha. Monitor P1 SLA (target 90%).
- **Week 2:** Enable Gamma for P1 + P2 cases; P3 uses Alpha. Monitor P1 SLA (target 90%), P2 SLA (target 90%).
- **Week 3:** Enable Gamma for all priorities. Monitor all SLA targets.
- **Week 4:** Observe steady state; if any SLA drops below target, activate Beta-Lite fallback for that priority.

**Rollback triggers:**
- If P1 SLA <85% for 2 consecutive days, rollback to Alpha.
- If max utilization >90% for any resource, activate Overflow Mode (preemption allowed).
- If handover overhead >15%, investigate and consider sequential parallelism.

---

### Phase 4: Stress Test and Scenario Exercises (ongoing, post-rollout)

**Objective:** Validate Gamma's resilience under U1–U5 conditions and refine policy parameters.

**Methods:**
1. **Demand surge drill:** Manually increase arrivals by 50% for 1 hour; observe Gamma response (queue depth, utilization, SLA impact). Target: queue clears within 3 hours, P1 SLA >80%.
2. **Resource absence drill:** Simulate Chen absence; disable her resource and run for 2-hour window. Target: Software still processed (overflow activates), P1 SLA >75%.
3. **Long-tail exercise:** Run with complex-tail probabilities doubled for 4-hour window. Target: P1 SLA stays 88% (acceptable degradation of 2%).
4. **Policy A/B test:** On low-volume day, run Alpha on morning shift, Gamma on evening shift; compare SLA outcomes, mean delay, utilization fairness. If Gamma wins, extend to full day.

### Phase 5: Continuous Monitoring and Adaptation (post-rollout)

**KPIs to track (daily/hourly):**
- P1/P2/P3 SLA attainment (vs. targets).
- Mean and p95 delay (vs. baseline).
- Resource utilization distribution (Gini coefficient).
- Handover + setup overhead (% of total service time).
- Preemption frequency and restart penalties.
- Bottleneck migration (which resource/skill is constrained each hour?).

**Monthly review:**
- If any SLA target missed 2 times in a month, investigate root cause and adjust policy thresholds (e.g., increase P1 reserve from 15% to 20%, or lower preemption wait threshold from 15 min to 12 min).
- If fairness degrades (Gini >0.25), review resource assignments and consider manual rebalancing or hiring.
- If handover overhead >12%, audit case stickiness logic and reduce cross-team assignments.

**Quarterly policy tuning:**
- Run simulation with updated service-time distributions (from actual logs) and updated demand (seasonal adjustment).
- Compare Gamma vs. Beta to check if Beta has become competitive (e.g., in low-variance scenario, full pooling may dominate strict specialization).
- If Gamma P1 SLA consistently 95%, consider optimizing for utilization fairness or cost (e.g., reduce staffing while maintaining SLAs).

### Risk Mitigation During Rollout

1. **P1 Protection:** Always reserve at least 15% capacity for P1 cases; preemption is only allowed to protect P1, never the reverse.
2. **Gradual escalation:** Start with Specialist Mode (no preemption); activate Overflow Mode only after manual validation. Escalate to Emergency Mode only after operator confirmation.
3. **Human oversight:** Require human approval for preemption decisions; log every preemption with reason and outcome for audit.
4. **Rollback safety:** Maintain Alpha dispatch rules as fallback; if Gamma system fails, automatically switch to Alpha.
5. **Communication:** Inform operators of policy change; provide training on Gamma dispatch rules and what to expect (increased queue visibility, dynamic preemption, cross-skill assignments).

# Conclusions

**Policy Gamma (Hybrid with P1 Reserve and Controlled Overflow)** is the recommended robust policy for this incident process. It:

1. **Meets SLA targets** on base scenario (P1 SLA 95%, P2 SLA 98%) with acceptable mean delay (~85 min).
2. **Degrades gracefully** under uncertainty scenarios:
   - **U1 (morning surge):** P1 SLA 80% (acceptable given 70% demand spike; system does not collapse).
   - **U3 (Chen absence):** P1 SLA 82% (feasible; overflow absorbs loss; better than Alpha's 35% or Beta's 78% complexity).
   - **U5 (combined stress):** P1 SLA 58% (system remains operational; can clear backlog by evening; better than Alpha's 12%).
3. **Balances specialist efficiency** (low handover in base) with **pooling resilience** (overflow capacity under stress).
4. **Is operationally transparent:** Clear dispatch rules, auditable preemption decisions, manual override capability.
5. **Limits preemption and thrashing:** Selective preemption (P1 only, wait >15 min) avoids excessive restart penalties and unfairness.

**Fallback:** Beta-Lite (pooling with preemption guard) for crisis scenarios; expect higher handover overhead but more stable load balancing if Gamma's specialist model breaks down.

**Implementation:** Staged rollout over 4 weeks, continuous monitoring, quarterly tuning. Invest in event logging, PM4Py analysis, and operator training to maximize benefits and catch regressions early.
