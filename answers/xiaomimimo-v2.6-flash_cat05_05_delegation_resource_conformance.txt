## 2. Rule Interpretation

**Notation.** `H(x)` = alias-normalized human for resource `x` ( for a service account); `role(h,t)` = set of roles held by `h` at instant `t` from the role-interval table; `t` = event timestamp (UTC); `sc(c)` = case scope field; `emg(c)` = emergency flag.

**RR1 (drafter/approver separation).** `H(draft(c))  H(approve(c))` after alias normalization. Applies at **all** risk levels; no exception is written into the rules for LOW risk or for emergency cases. If either side is , the test is INDETERMINATE unless Tier-A attribution exists. Evaluating `u_cs` vs `u_cs`, or `b.koch` vs `u_bk`, both **fail** after normalization — RR5 explicitly requires normalization *before* RR1/RR2.

**RR2 (implementer/verifier separation).** `H(implement(c))  H(verify(c))`, same normalization rule. A service account used for verification yields: Tier A  evaluate against the attributed human; Tier B/C  **INDETERMINATE** (not a violation, not conformance). Note the asymmetry: a failed identity binding can *hide* a real RR2 breach (CHG-503), so INDETERMINATE must never be reported as PASS.

**RR3 (high-risk approval authority).** For `risk=HIGH  ¬emg(c)` at time `t`: the approver must satisfy `QM  role(H(approve),t)` **or** there exists a delegation `d` with `delegate = H(approve)` and:

1. `d.start  t  d.end` (interval coverage), **and**
2. `d.scope` covers **both** the change's scope field **and** the function being exercised (approval vs. retrospective review), **and**
3. the principal is absent/formally unavailable at `t` **when the delegation text requires it**, **and**
4. no prohibited subdelegation occurred, **and**
5. evidence tier  {A, B} (a record created after `t` cannot, alone, prove a delegation existed at `t`).

All five are conjunctive. Failure of any one  the delegate had **no authority** at that instant  UNAUTHORIZED, unless RR4 applies.

**RR4 (emergency substitution) and precedence.** RR4 is a **conditional carve-out from RR3**, available only when `risk=HIGH  emergency=true  emergency flag and safety rationale are credible`. When engaged: approval *and* implementation may be performed by the IC or a valid IC delegate; in exchange a QM or valid QM delegate must perform `Retrospective_QM_Review` within **two business days** of implementation. If any RR4 condition (including the retrospective review) fails, the case **falls back to RR3**, where it fails because the approver was an IC, not a QM.

**Business-day counting for RR4 (formalized).** Business day = calendar day excluding Saturday, Sunday, and site-calendar holidays (site calendar shows **no** holiday 08-1008-17, so only weekends bite). Counting starts at 00:00 UTC on the **next calendar day after the implementation event**; day *n* ends 23:59:59 UTC. Implementation Tue 2026-08-11  day 1 = Wed 08-12, day 2 = Thu 08-13  deadline **2026-08-13 23:59:59Z**. A review on Fri 08-14 16:00 (CHG-505) is **one business day late**. Implementation 08-11 with review 08-12 10:00 (CHG-510) is timely.

**Action by a principal who is on leave.** Leave status does **not** suspend a standing role: authority derives from role intervals, not from the rota. Therefore Asha's approval of CHG-504 satisfies RR3 *if the actor is Asha*. However, a principal-while-on-leave event creates an **identity/authenticity flag** (RR5): the event is presumed attributable only where session evidence binds it to her. Two consequences: (i) if the credential was shared, the real approver had no role and the case becomes UNAUTHORIZED; (ii) an active principal during the leave window weakens the "principal absent" precondition for any *concurrent* delegate action — but only as a factual question to be resolved, not automatically: the recorded absence register remains the controlling source for delegation conditions, with the VPN window logged as a contradictory signal. Note also the anomaly that an approval was executed as the **principal** on 08-13 when a **validly scoped delegate (Ben) existed for exactly that scope** — a routing/behavioral anomaly worth explaining.

**Can a later database entry prove an earlier delegation?** **No, not by itself.** A record whose creation timestamp post-dates the event can only be *corroborative*; it cannot establish that the delegation existed, was authorized, or was known at event time (it may be a migration artifact or a back-dated entry). Proof requires an artifact whose creation is provably  `t` — a signed, trusted-timestamped PDF (08-09 18:00) or a system record "registered before use". Consequence for D-QM-01: CHG-501 (approved 08-11 09:00) rests on the pre-dated PDF alone (tier B — sufficient, but with a documentary chain-of-custody weakness); CHG-503 (approved 08-12 11:30) is supported by both the PDF and the DB row created 08-12 09:15 (tier A).

**RR5 / RR6 as decision rules.** RR5 sets the attribution tiers above. RR6 requires that every concentration/reciprocity/shared-device observation be labeled an **indicator** and be paired with at least one benign and one adverse hypothesis plus discriminating evidence; no finding in this report asserts collusion.

## 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

Every item below is an **indicator under RR6**. None is treated as proof.

**A. Clara  Ben: 18 of 23 HIGH-risk production approvals in 60 days (78%).**
- *Benign:* both sit in the same production cell; the principal QM (Asha) was on leave for much of the window; D-QM-01 named Ben specifically; **no rotation policy exists**, so concentration is the structural default, not a choice.
- *Adverse:* a stable approval duopoly in which drafter and approver stop testing each other; approvals become procedural rather than substantive.
- *Discriminating evidence:* availability of alternative QM delegates on each date; distribution of Asha's 3 approvals (were they the *controversial* ones?); approval latency and comment/condition content (substantive review vs. rubber stamp); rate of post-approval rework, deviations, or audit findings by pair vs. others; whether any change in the 23 was ever rejected or returned.
- *Caution:* in a strictly sequential process, **drafterapprover handover edges are structurally inevitable** — handover weight alone is uninformative. The meaningful metric is concentration **relative to available alternatives** and relative to a cell-normalized baseline.

**B. Reciprocal: Ben drafted 4 LOW training changes that Clara approved.**
- *Benign:* small, low-risk, mutually-assisted coverage within one cell; no financial interest; no known benefit.
- *Adverse:* reciprocity as implicit quid-pro-quo reinforcing A; reciprocal approval creates a pattern where neither party's work is independently checked.
- *Discriminating evidence:* whether the training documents govern procedures these two perform (self-regulation); content overlap between Ben's drafts and Clara's approvals; whether the same 4 cases also show RR-relevant shortcuts; comparison with other cells' reciprocity rates.

**C. Shared workstation WS-DM-04 + `svc_qms` verification (CHG-503) — Tier B.**
- *Benign:* an automated QMS verification transaction executed under a service identity as part of normal system behaviour, with Diego present only because he had just implemented; a *different* verifier used the shared seat.
- *Adverse:* the implementer (or a colluding party) verified his own HIGH-risk work through a service account precisely because a human service-account action leaves no personal trace — an SoD breach concealed by identity design.
- *Discriminating evidence:* service-account run-as/owner mapping and whether interactive logon is even permitted; per-session MFA records; database audit showing which session invoked the verify transaction; terminal login history showing who held the console; badge records for other staff in the room; CCTV retention; whether CHG-503's verification has any substantive content (checklist completion) attributable to a verifier.

**What the code decides vs. what stays manual.** Decided mechanically: alias-merged SoD (which is what exposes CHG-508), four-eyes filters, interval coverage/absence/scope conjuncts for each approval, RR4 business-day deadlines, and network weight extraction. **Manual:** whether `cooling_system` reviews are within D-QM-01's "production-equipment approval" wording; whether CHG-505/510 are CI-44; whether the emergency flags are credible; who really used `u_as` (CHG-504) and `u_bk` (CHG-506); whether `svc_qms` in CHG-503 was Diego; and every collusion judgment. Service-account actions are represented by keeping the raw `org:resource`, annulling `human_resource`, and filling `attributed_human` **only** on Tier-A session evidence — so weak attribution degrades a check to `INDETERMINATE_UNATTRIBUTED` rather than silently passing it.

## 6. Control Improvements

1. **Delegation creation.** Dual-authorized creation with mandatory machine-readable scope tags drawn from the same taxonomy as the change `scope` field (so `cooling_system` cannot silently sit outside a "QM coverage" delegation). Cover the *whole* QM function set during absence — including `Retrospective_QM_Review` — so RR4 deadlines are attainable (the D-QM-01 gap made CHG-505's deadline structurally unreachable). Encode "no subdelegation" as a system-enforced flag, not prose; block routes that depend on email-only authority. Auto-expiry, no open-ended coverage, and a named successor when the delegate is unavailable.
2. **Effective-time recording.** Separate `effective_from/to` from `recorded_at` in an append-only audit store with trusted timestamps (RFC 3161); never allow migration to overwrite creation provenance; produce an exception report for any delegation record created *after* its first use (as with D-QM-01/CHG-501).
3. **Principal availability.** Integrate the absence register with approval routing: a delegation conditioned on absence must be validated against the register at evaluation time and **blocked** when the condition fails (would have stopped CHG-509). Suspend a delegation automatically when the *delegate* goes on sick/leave (would have stopped CHG-506). Raise an alert when a principal transacts during recorded leave (CHG-504) and reconcile the leave record. Maintain a weekend/on-call QM rota so emergency review deadlines can be met.
4. **Alias management.** Single identity source (SSO) with an authoritative alias table; run **all** SoD checks on normalized human IDs; prohibit duplicate/local accounts (`b.koch` vs `u_bk`); quarterly account inventory and recertification; keep the alias table versioned so event-time normalization is reproducible.
5. **Individual authentication.** Prohibit interactive use of service accounts for human decisions: approvals and verifications require per-user step-up authentication (FIDO2), bound to the session and written into the change record; disable interactive logon for `svc_qms`; for HIGH-risk actions require badge or biometric co-verification; retain session recording for HIGH-risk approvals; make shared terminals unusable for approval/verification without individual login (addresses CHG-503).
6. **Emergency review.** Emergency flag requires a free-text safety rationale **and** a mandatory incident-ID link (CI-44) validated against the incident register; second-person confirmation of the flag; hard block on drafter=approver even in emergency mode (CHG-510); automated reminders at T+1 business day and escalation at T+2 with a defined in-scope fallback approver; post-event review of every emergency change regardless of timing.
7. **Pair rotation.** Publish and enforce a rotation policy with a maximum share of approvals per drafter/approver pair (e.g., >60% over 60 days triggers review); pre-designate alternates; make Asha's/QM coverage continuous so a single delegate is never the only route. Frame this as a *resilience* control, explicitly not as a misconduct control (RR6).
