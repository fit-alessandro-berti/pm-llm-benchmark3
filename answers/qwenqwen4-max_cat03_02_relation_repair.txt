## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

**Hard invariants (never violated by any bundle)**
- H1 Referential integrity: every added E2O points to an existing `eid` and `oid`; every O2O to two existing objects. No new events or objects are invented.
- H2 Qualifier typing/direction: `contains` OrderItem, `packs` PackageItem, `carries` DeliveryPackage, `bills` InvoiceOrder, `settles` PaymentInvoice. `PK-8 carries DEL-Y` is not a legal rewrite of `DEL-Y carries PK-8` (rule 8).
- H3 No duplicate identical relation rows; no self-loops in O2O.
- H4 An E2O repair may not contradict an *observed* relation (e.g. ev05 already ties IT-B1 to ORD-B).

**Strong defaults (defeasible, cost attached to violation)**
- S1 Activity cardinalities from rules 1, 2, 3, 6, 7 (Create Order 1 Order; Add Item 1+1; Pack Item 1+1 with the documented item-only outage case; Issue Invoice 1 Invoice + 1 Order; Record Payment 1 Payment + normally 1 Invoice).
- S2 An Item is packed into at most one Package; a Package is dispatched at most once; a Payment normally settles at most one Invoice.
- S3 Lifecycle order: Add Item  Pack Item  Dispatch  Confirm; Create Order  Issue Invoice  Record Payment. Violations are possible (backdating, clock skew) but expensive.
- S4 O2O structure should be *derivable from* observed events, not freely invented to close a lifecycle.

**Weak heuristics**
- W1 Identifier similarity: `scanner_ref=PK8-2`  PK-8; `external_ref=N-421?`  DEL-Y (`carrier_ref=N-421`); `N-42*` matches N-420 **and** N-421 equally, so it is non-discriminative.
- W2 Attribute hints: `order_hint`, `route_hint`.
- W3 Arithmetic: 130+100=230 (invoice), 100+130=230 (payments), PAY-2=130=ORD-A.declared_value. Explicitly *evidence, not proof* (rule 6): fees, taxes, discounts, partial settlements and unrelated coincidences all produce the same numbers.
- W4 Timestamp proximity (ev09/ev10 both 10:00).
- W5 Low-information attributes: `route=NORTH` and `customer=C17` are constant across the whole log; they cannot discriminate between candidate deliveries, orders or invoices. They only rule *out* candidates with different values.

**Evidence against a repair**
- X1 PK-8 already `packs` IT-A2, which `ORD-A contains`. Putting IT-B1 (ORD-B) into PK-8 creates a cross-order package — legal, but a structural change with large downstream consequences.
- X2 Two distinct carrier refs (N-420, N-421) and two Delivery objects argue against folding both packages into DEL-X.
- X3 PAY-1 (100) already settles INV-44; 100 also equals ORD-B.declared_value. This is a trap: payments settle invoices, not orders.
- X4 The `?` and `*` tokens mark degraded source values; treating them as exact identifiers overstates confidence.

**Combination method.** I score a candidate by (i) direct identifier/attribute match, discounted for degradation markers; (ii) lifecycle and cardinality feasibility; (iii) *structural parsimony* — how many new one-to-many/many-to-many edges the repair introduces; (iv) whether support is **independent** of other repaired edges. Route/customer contribute almost nothing here. When two candidates score within ~0.15 I leave the relation unresolved rather than pick a winner. Crucially, a repair that makes every lifecycle complete gets no bonus: completeness is a modelling preference, not evidence.

**Bundle B — consolidation interpretation.**
The story: C17's two orders were shipped as a mixed load and billed together. ev08  PK-8 (B-01) makes PK-8 a cross-order package (IT-A2 + IT-B1); `PK-8 packs IT-B1` follows. ev10  DEL-Y as in A. ev11 is a joint acknowledgment of DEL-X **and** DEL-Y under the wildcard `N-42*` (A-03 + B-03), keeping ORD-A. INV-44 bills ORD-A *and* ORD-B (B-04/B-05), and both PAY-1 and PAY-2 settle INV-44 (B-06/B-07). This bundle is internally coherent and yields complete lifecycles for every object, including ORD-B: Create  Add Item  Pack (via PK-8)  Dispatch (via DEL-Y)  Confirm  Invoice  Payment. Its weakness is precisely its attractiveness: the only *independent* evidence for the financial half is arithmetic plus a constant customer attribute, and the only evidence for the logistics half is one degraded scanner token. The two halves then appear to confirm each other, which is circular (§5). B also silently changes three cardinality profiles (PackageItem 1:n, InvoiceOrder 1:n, PaymentInvoice n:1).

**Bundle C — consolidated billing, separate logistics.**
Financial and physical consolidation are logically independent. C accepts B-04/B-05/B-06/B-07 (one invoice for both orders, both payments applied) but keeps shipments separate: ev08 unresolved (or, in variant C, IT-B1  PK-7 as a mis-scan, R-04 promoted to provisional 0.25), ev10  DEL-Y, ev11  DEL-X only with DEL-Y unresolved. C shows that "230 = 130+100" carries **no** information about package contents, and it avoids the cross-order package while still explaining the invoice total. Its cost: ORD-B has billing but no logistics closure, and the 100 gap between INV-44's total and ORD-A's declared value is explained by scope rather than fees — an assumption, not a finding.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

**Order.** ORD-A: Create  Add Item ×2  Confirm Delivery  Issue Invoice; plausible even though packing/dispatch are not directly order-related (normal in OCEL). ORD-B in Bundle A: Create  Add Item, then nothing — a truncated but non-contradictory lifecycle. No Order is an orphan object. Suspicious relation avoided: `ORD-A contains IT-B1` (R-05) would contradict ev05.

**Item.** IT-A1 and IT-A2 have Add Item  Pack Item with package membership confirmed by *both* E2O (ev06/ev07) and O2O (`PK-7 packs IT-A1`, `PK-8 packs IT-A2`) — genuinely double-supported. IT-B1 in A has Add Item  Pack Item but no Package link: an OTG-level orphan relative to Package, and the honest representation of the outage. In B it acquires a package, but only via a single repaired token.

**Package.** PK-7: Pack  Dispatch, fully observed. PK-8 in A: Pack  Dispatch (via A-01), no confirmation. No package is dispatched twice (R-03/R-10 rejected). B introduces the only one-to-many instance relation: PK-8 packs two items from two different orders. Legal, but it changes the empirical PackageItem cardinality from 1:1 to 1:n and must not be promoted to a schema constraint.

**Delivery.** DEL-X: Dispatch  Confirm, supported independently by ev09 + `DEL-X carries PK-7` (both original) and, after A-03, by ev11. DEL-Y in A: Dispatch only — no Confirm, correctly left open. In B both deliveries are confirmed by the *same* event, making ev11 a three-object, two-type event; that is a legitimate bulk ack but inflates per-object activity counts.

**Invoice.** INV-44 in A bills ORD-A only; total 230 vs declared 130 leaves 100 unexplained. I record this as an *open discrepancy*, not as evidence that ORD-B must be added. In B/C, INV-44 bills two orders (1:n) and is settled by two payments (n:1). Both are schema-legal; neither is determined by the data.

**Payment.** PAY-1 settles INV-44 with independent E2O (ev13) + O2O support. PAY-2 in A is an orphan with respect to Invoice — plausible unapplied cash. R-07/R-08 (paymentorder shortcuts) are rejected on qualifier typing, despite seductive amount matches.

**Circular reasoning, explicitly.** A-02, B-02, B-05 and B-07 are *functions of* A-01, B-01, B-04 and B-06. They must never be counted as corroboration for their parent E2O edge, nor as a second "independent path" in confidence propagation. A second, subtler cycle: using the 230 invoice to justify `PK-8 packs IT-B1`, then using the mixed package to justify `INV-44 bills ORD-B`. Both rest on the same two weak signals (constant customer C17, arithmetic); iterating them manufactures confidence. Bundle C exists to break that loop.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**OC-DFG.** Unrepaired: IT-B1's path ends at Pack Item; PK-8's path ends at Pack Item; DEL-Y contributes no node at all (no events); ORD-B's trace is Create Order  Add Item; PAY-2 forms a single-activity trace. Bundle A adds `Pack Item  Dispatch Package` on PK-8 and creates a Dispatch Package node for DEL-Y, plus `Dispatch Package  Confirm Delivery` on DEL-X. ORD-B's OC-DFG path is **unchanged** in A, because OC-DFG follows objects of the same type and no repaired edge touches an Order — a point often missed. Bundle B additionally extends ORD-B to Confirm Delivery  Issue Invoice  Record Payment, adds `Dispatch Package  Confirm Delivery` on DEL-Y, and adds a `Record Payment  Record Payment` succession on INV-44 that looks like duplicate-payment rework but is merely two instalments.

**OTG.** Type-level edges are the same in all three variants (Order–Item, Package–Item, Delivery–Package, Invoice–Order, Payment–Invoice); what changes is instance multiplicity and connectivity. A connects DEL-Y into the graph; B additionally merges the ORD-A and ORD-B components through PK-8 and INV-44, turning two weakly connected subgraphs into one. Any cardinality annotation learned from instances shifts (1:1  1:n on packs/bills, n:1 on settles).

**ET–OT graph.** A adds the `Confirm Delivery–Delivery` edge (absent unrepaired) and strengthens `Dispatch Package–Delivery`. B raises the weight of `Pack Item–Package` (3 vs 2 events), `Issue Invoice–Order`, `Record Payment–Invoice`, and `Confirm Delivery–Delivery` (2 links from one event). If ET–OT weights are used to infer mandatory participation, B makes Confirm Delivery look delivery-anchored when the source may have been order-anchored.

**Flattenings.** Order flattening drops ev06–ev10 in *all* bundles (no Order object on those events); ev11 survives via ORD-A. So the Order-flattened trace is Create Order  Add Item  Confirm Delivery  Issue Invoice  Record Payment, with logistics invisible — repairs on Package/Delivery do not fix this. In B, ev11 also carries ORD-B, so ORD-B's flattened trace gains a Confirm Delivery with no preceding packing/dispatch: a fabricated shortcut. Delivery flattening: DEL-X Dispatch  Confirm; DEL-Y empty (unrepaired), Dispatch-only (A), Dispatch  Confirm (B). Invoice flattening: one Record Payment (A) vs two (B/C) — B changes the "invoice settled" rate from partial to complete. Payment flattening is identical in all bundles (one Payment per event), so settlement semantics are only observable on Invoice; a Payment-centric analysis will silently miss the difference between A and B.

**Activity frequencies.** Event-based counts are invariant (14 events, 3 Pack Item, 2 Record Payment). Unique-object counts change: Confirm Delivery covers 1 delivery in A, 2 in B. Total-object (relation-row) counts change more: Confirm Delivery goes from 1 row to 2 (A) to 4 (B: ORD-A, DEL-X, DEL-Y). Any "per-object throughput" KPI therefore moves without a single new event.

**Equivalent-execution clustering around Order.** In A, ORD-A and ORD-B remain separate executions with distinct variants (long vs truncated). In B they collapse into one equivalence class containing both orders, all three items, both packages, both deliveries, the invoice and both payments. Variant count drops from 2 to 1, the consolidated variant's support doubles, and ORD-B's genuinely different (short) behaviour disappears from the variant statistics — a real analytical loss disguised as data cleaning.

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# --- 0. Preserve originals -------------------------------------------------
events0, objects0 = events.copy(deep=True), objects.copy(deep=True)
relations0, o2o0 = relations.copy(deep=True), o2o.copy(deep=True)

EID, OID, OID2 = "ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2"
QUAL, ACT, TS, TYP = "ocel:qualifier", "ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp", "ocel:type"

# --- 1. Bundle A additions (confidence kept OUT of the OCEL tables) --------
e2o_add = pd.DataFrame([
    {EID: "ev10", OID: "DEL-Y", QUAL: "delivery"},   # 0.60 provisional
    {EID: "ev11", OID: "DEL-X", QUAL: "delivery"},   # 0.55 provisional
])
o2o_add = pd.DataFrame([
    {OID: "DEL-Y", OID2: "PK-8", QUAL: "carries"},   # 0.50, DERIVED from ev10 assumption
])
# Unresolved candidates are logged, not inserted:
unresolved = ["ev08->PK-8", "PK-8 packs IT-B1", "ev11->DEL-Y", "ev11->ORD-B",
              "ev12->ORD-B", "INV-44 bills ORD-B", "ev14->INV-44", "PAY-2 settles INV-44"]

rel_new = pd.concat([relations0, e2o_add], ignore_index=True)
o2o_new = pd.concat([o2o0, o2o_add], ignore_index=True)

# --- 2. Referential integrity + duplicates --------------------------------
assert set(rel_new[EID]) <= set(events0[EID]), "dangling event id"
assert set(rel_new[OID]) <= set(objects0[OID]), "dangling object id"
for c in (OID, OID2):
    assert set(o2o_new[c]) <= set(objects0[OID]), f"dangling {c}"
assert not rel_new.duplicated([EID, OID, QUAL]).any(), "duplicate E2O"
assert not o2o_new.duplicated([OID, OID2, QUAL]).any(), "duplicate O2O"
assert (o2o_new[OID] != o2o_new[OID2]).all(), "self-loop O2O"

# Qualifier domain/range check (H2) - direction matters.
sig = {("contains"): ("Order", "Item"), ("packs"): ("Package", "Item"),
       ("carries"): ("Delivery", "Package"), ("bills"): ("Invoice", "Order"),
       ("settles"): ("Payment", "Invoice")}
tmap = objects0.set_index(OID)[TYP].to_dict()
for r in o2o_new.itertuples(index=False):
    d, g = sig[getattr(r, "_2")] if False else sig[r.ocel_qualifier] if hasattr(r, "ocel_qualifier") else sig[r[-1]]
    assert (tmap[r[0]], tmap[r[1]]) == (d, g), f"bad qualifier direction: {r}"

# Cardinality *report* (strong defaults, not hard failures).
pk = rel_new.merge(events0[[EID, ACT]], on=EID)
pack_rows = pk[pk[ACT] == "Pack Item"].merge(objects0, on=OID)
print(pack_rows[pack_rows[TYP] == "Package"].groupby(EID)[OID].nunique())
print("item-only Pack Item rows:",
      set(pk[pk[ACT] == "Pack Item"][EID]) - set(pack_rows[pack_rows[TYP] == "Package"][EID]))

# --- 3. Build OCEL (VERSION-DEPENDENT CONSTRUCTOR) ------------------------
# OCEL 2.0 builds: pm4py.objects.ocel.obj.OCEL(events=..., objects=..., relations=..., o2o=...)
# Older builds require parameters={"ocel:global-log": ...} and/or use ocel:o2o column names.
from pm4py.objects.ocel.obj import OCEL
try:
    ocel = OCEL(events=events0, objects=objects0, relations=rel_new, o2o=o2o_new)
except TypeError:
    ocel = OCEL(parameters={}, events=events0, objects=objects0,
                relations=rel_new, o2o=o2o_new)   # version-dependent fallback

# --- 4. Discovery ---------------------------------------------------------
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
try:
    otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
except AttributeError:
    otg = None            # version-dependent discovery name
try:
    etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)
except AttributeError:
    etot = None           # version-dependent discovery name

# --- 5. Flattenings (note the convergence/divergence caveats) -------------
flat_order = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Order")
flat_delivery = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Delivery")
for name, fl in (("Order", flat_order), ("Delivery", flat_delivery)):
    print(name, len(fl), fl[ACT].value_counts().to_dict() if ACT in fl else fl.columns.tolist())

# --- 6. Do NOT treat o2o enrichment as semantic validation ----------------
# pm4py.ocel_o2o_enrichment(ocel) merely propagates existing O2O structure;
# it cannot confirm that DEL-Y really carries PK-8. Compare A vs B instead:
ocdfg_unrepaired = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(
    OCEL(events=events0, objects=objects0, relations=relations0, o2o=o2o0))
print("unresolved candidates kept open:", unresolved)
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Manufacturing a cross-order package.** Adding `PK-8 packs IT-B1` from one degraded scanner token merges ORD-A and ORD-B into a single execution, halves the Order variant count, and makes mixed-order packing look like standard practice.
2. **Turning instalments into rework.** Applying PAY-2 to INV-44 creates a `Record Payment  Record Payment` loop on the invoice, which conformance checkers may flag as duplicate payment or rework, while erasing the genuinely interesting "unapplied cash" pattern.
3. **Arithmetic-driven invoice scope.** Adding `INV-44 bills ORD-B` because 130+100=230 asserts a consolidated-billing process that fees or taxes could equally explain, and inflates InvoiceOrder cardinality in the OTG.
4. **Duplicated confirmations.** Attaching ev11 to both deliveries (and to ORD-B) multiplies Confirm Delivery per-object frequency without any new event, and gives ORD-B a confirmation with no packing or dispatch behind it in Order flattening.
5. **Bulk-dispatch over-collapse.** Folding PK-8 into DEL-X (R-01/R-02) leaves DEL-Y as a permanently event-less object, invents a two-package delivery, and hides the two-carrier-ref distinction that the source deliberately preserved.
6. **Circular confirmation.** Deriving O2O edges from assumed E2O edges and then citing them as independent support (A-02/B-02/B-05/B-07) produces high-confidence artefacts with a single weak root; likewise mutual boosting between the 230 invoice and the mixed package.
7. **Qualifier inversion or type shortcuts.** `PK-8 carries DEL-Y` or `PAY-2 settles ORD-A` break directional/typing invariants and create invoice-bypassing payment paths that no observed event supports.
8. **Completeness as an objective.** Any repair justified mainly by "now every object has a full lifecycle" is fitting the model to an expectation; the outage clause and unapplied-cash clause exist precisely so that incomplete lifecycles can be legitimate.
