# 2. Benign alternative explanations

**Pattern A: `Buyer_Carla  Approver_Gita` fast emergency PO approvals and PO four-eyes exceptions**

1. **Genuine emergency medical-supply channel.**  
   - **Supporting evidence:** `emergency_flag=True`, `purchase_category` showing medical supplies, urgent request forms, hospital or clinic need dates, on-call approval roster, approval comments referencing urgency, goods receipts showing items were received and used.  
   - **Refuting evidence:** emergency flag added after the fact, missing request documentation, non-medical or routine items processed through the fast channel, high-value purchases without urgency, absence of goods receipt or usage evidence.

2. **Delegated approval or shared-account data entry.**  
   - **Supporting evidence:** `entered_by` showing assistants or delegates, approved delegation matrix for `Approver_Gita`, access logs showing authorized delegate login, system audit trail showing the actual human who clicked approve, policy allowing delegated urgent approvals.  
   - **Refuting evidence:** no delegation authority, assistants using shared credentials without authorization, same individual performing both creation and approval, missing login or audit-trail detail.

**Pattern B: `Buyer_Dan  Manager_Hugo` fast weekend facilities-repair approvals**

1. **On-call manager escalation for urgent repairs.**  
   - **Supporting evidence:** facilities incident tickets, weekend work orders, contractor call-outs, safety or operational urgency, photos or site reports, goods receipt or service confirmation, retrospective justification by a different person.  
   - **Refuting evidence:** routine maintenance disguised as emergency, no incident ticket, repairs not tied to operational need, repeated weekend approvals without corresponding facilities activity.

2. **Mobile or batch approval with system timestamp effects.**  
   - **Supporting evidence:** approval comments showing review occurred after site visit, lifecycle transitions showing approval recorded later but decision documented elsewhere, mobile approval logs, manager on-call records.  
   - **Refuting evidence:** timestamps inconsistent with login records, approvals recorded before purchase request or goods receipt, no evidence of actual review.

**Pattern C: `VendorMaster_Lena  AP_Kim` bank-detail changes followed quickly by payment approval for vendor family `V-77x`**

1. **Legitimate mass-maintenance operation due to banking-platform change.**  
   - **Supporting evidence:** mass-maintenance ticket, vendor notification letters, bank confirmation documents, list of affected `V-77x` entities, independent validation of new bank details, change log showing bulk update scope, linkage between maintenance ticket and procurement cases.  
   - **Refuting evidence:** missing maintenance ticket, changes outside the stated vendor family, payments to newly changed accounts without independent verification, beneficiary names inconsistent with vendor master, changes made after invoice submission but before payment.

2. **Documented manual exception process for urgent supplier payments.**  
   - **Supporting evidence:** AP exception forms, invoice match results, goods receipts, contract references, second reviewer or approver in source system, manual upload file with preparer and approver fields, evidence that `entered_by` and `approved_by` differ.  
   - **Refuting evidence:** same person preparing and approving, missing exception reason, manual upload file without owner or approval, repeated exceptions for the same vendor without remediation, payments released despite failed invoice match.

**Pattern D: `Emergency Override` followed by same-person `Retrospective Justification`, often batch-entered**

1. **Quarter-close spreadsheet migration of contemporaneous justifications.**  
   - **Supporting evidence:** original spreadsheet, upload ticket, file creation/modification timestamps, migration log, original decision timestamps, justification text matching emergency request forms, independent reviewer sign-off stored outside the event log.  
   - **Refuting evidence:** no source spreadsheet, identical generic justifications, timestamps all identical after quarter close, no evidence that justifications existed before upload, no independent reviewer.

2. **Emergency policy allowed manager to record justification, with later independent review.**  
   - **Supporting evidence:** written emergency policy, post-event review, second signature, risk or compliance approval, evidence that retrospective justification was reviewed by someone other than the override approver.  
   - **Refuting evidence:** policy requires a different human resource, but no different person appears; retrospective justification is missing, late beyond three working days, or self-approved.

**Pattern E: `Vendor Check` and `Change Vendor Bank Details` by same person**

1. **Vendor-master role design includes both activities.**  
   - **Supporting evidence:** role matrix, standard operating procedure, access-control design, periodic user-access review showing this combination was approved.  
   - **Refuting evidence:** policy requires segregation between vendor check and bank change, but role design permits both without compensating review.

2. **Mass data cleanup or bank-migration activity.**  
   - **Supporting evidence:** change ticket, validation report, list of changed records, independent review of bank-change file, vendor confirmation.  
   - **Refuting evidence:** ad hoc changes with no ticket, changes limited to high-risk suppliers, missing validation, repeated changes shortly before payments.

**Pattern F: Repeated subcontracting loops**

1. **Normal correction or clarification loops.**  
   - **Supporting evidence:** rejection comments, revised quotes, changed PO fields, rework variants, goods receipt corrections, invoice-match exceptions with documented resolution.  
   - **Refuting evidence:** loops with no field changes, no comments, and immediate approval each time.

2. **Automated exception feedback or system retries.**  
   - **Supporting evidence:** `Bot_Match` exception codes, bot logs, interface retry logs, AP exception queue records, system-generated timestamps.  
   - **Refuting evidence:** human overrides of bot exceptions without explanation, repeated manual release of failed matches, same person resolving and approving exceptions.

def four_eyes_violation_cases(
    event_df,
    activity_1,
    activity_2,
    case_key="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource"
):
    act1 = event_df[event_df[activity_key] == activity_1]
    act2 = event_df[event_df[activity_key] == activity_2]

    res1 = act1.groupby(case_key)[resource_key].agg(set).rename("resources_act1")
    res2 = act2.groupby(case_key)[resource_key].agg(set).rename("resources_act2")

    merged = pd.concat([res1, res2], axis=1).dropna()
    merged["same_resource"] = merged.apply(
        lambda row: row["resources_act1"] & row["resources_act2"],
        axis=1
    )
    merged["violation"] = merged["same_resource"].map(len) > 0

    return merged[merged["violation"]]

    eo = event_df[event_df[activity_key] == "Emergency Override"][
        [case_key, timestamp_key, resource_key]
    ].copy()

    rj = event_df[event_df[activity_key] == "Retrospective Justification"][
        [case_key, timestamp_key, resource_key]
    ].copy()

    eo = eo.rename(columns={
        timestamp_key: "override_time",
        resource_key: "override_resource"
    })

    rj = rj.rename(columns={
        timestamp_key: "justification_time",
        resource_key: "justification_resource"
    })

    merged = eo.merge(rj, on=case_key, how="left")

    merged["days_to_justification"] = np.busday_count(
        merged["override_time"].values.astype("datetime64[D]"),
        merged["justification_time"].values.astype("datetime64[D]")
    )

    merged["missing_justification"] = merged["justification_time"].isna()
    merged["late_justification"] = merged["days_to_justification"] > 3
    merged["same_resource"] = merged["override_resource"] == merged["justification_resource"]

    merged["violation"] = (
        merged["missing_justification"] |
        merged["late_justification"] |
        merged["same_resource"]
    )

    return merged

retro_check = retrospective_justification_check(log_complete)
retro_violations = retro_check[retro_check["violation"]]

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

Based on the event-log review, the evidence does not establish fraud or collusion. It shows a mixture of emergency handling, informal specialization, control exceptions, and data-quality issues. The routine buyer-approver pairs, particularly `Buyer_Aline` with `Approver_Elena` and `Buyer_Boris` with `Approver_Felix`, appear consistent with normal workflow specialization: volumes are high, timings are measured in hours, emergency flags are rare, and vendor concentration is low. The fast `Buyer_Carla` to `Approver_Gita` and `Buyer_Dan` to `Manager_Hugo` patterns are largely associated with emergency flags, medical supplies, weekend repairs, and delegated escalation; they warrant verification of emergency documentation and retrospective approvals, but they are not, by themselves, indicative of misconduct.

The subgroup requiring the most attention is `VendorMaster_Lena` and `AP_Kim` activity involving vendor family `V-77x`. The combination of bank-detail changes followed quickly by payment approval, a high share of `manual_upload` records, concentrated vendor exposure, and a small number of four-eyes exceptions creates an elevated control risk. A plausible benign explanation is the documented banking-platform migration and manual exception processing; this should be confirmed before any adverse inference.

Data-quality limitations affect the assessment. Shared approver accounts, assistant entries, batch quarter-close uploads, and bot-to-human mapping errors can create apparent violations that do not reflect actual individual behavior. Accordingly, the review should reconcile `org:resource` with `entered_by`, `approved_by`, access logs, and source documents.

Recommended follow-up: targeted testing of `V-77x` bank-change and payment cases; validation of emergency override justifications; review of shared-account delegation; and correction of source-system resource mapping. Control exceptions should be reported as control-design and compliance issues unless documentary evidence indicates intentional circumvention or personal benefit.
