- A05 at 09:05: FIFO over A08 (priority 1) and A07 (est 9). Strong FIFO support.

- A06 at 09:20: both FIFO and overdue appointment. Consistent with FIFO plus non-preemptive appointment override.

**Contradicting / challenging**
- A03 at 08:20 violates FIFO (A02 earlier) and priority/SPT (A04 priority 1, est 7). Explained by A03’s appointment at 08:20. This is a known constraint, not an undocumented exception.
- A06 appointment 09:15 started at 09:20. This challenges strict appointment scheduling; explained by non-preemptive service of A05 (started 09:05, ran 15 min).
- A04 and A08 are priority 1 but never jump the queue. This contradicts priority scheduling and supports FIFO.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

## Episode 1: QB 09:16 — B02 preempts B01

**Competing explanations**
- Preemptive priority: B02 is priority 1, B01 is priority 3.
- SEPT/SPT: B02 est 15 is shorter than B01’s remaining ~31 min.

Both predict B02 at 09:16.

**Discriminating observable**
A future arrival where priority and expected remaining time conflict. For example, a priority 1 job with est 40 arrives while a priority 3 job with est 5 is running. Preemptive priority would preempt; SEPT would not. Conversely, a priority 2 job with est 5 arriving while a priority 3 job with est 20 is running would be preempted under SEPT but not under priority. The existing 10:00 choice of B05 (priority 1, est 14) over B04 (est 8) already favors priority over SPT, but it is not a preemption.

## Episode 2: QC 10:54 — C05 selected over C06

**Competing explanations**
- Priority override: C05 is priority 1.
- EDD/SLA override: C05’s deadline 10:50 is already missed.

Both predict C05.

**Discriminating observable**
A case where priority and deadline conflict. For example, a priority 1 job with deadline 15:00 versus a priority 3 job with deadline 11:00. Priority override selects the priority 1 job; EDD selects the imminent-deadline job. Additional data needed: dispatch decision when a high-priority job has slack and a low-priority job is about to miss its deadline.

## Episode 3: QD 11:20 — D-X idle until D06 appointment

**Competing explanations**
- Appointment scheduling: D-X is reserved for D06’s 11:30 appointment.
- Undocumented D-X unavailability until 11:30.

Both predict D-X idle 11:20–11:30.

**Discriminating observable**
Resource state log for D-X 11:20–11:30. If D-X was idle and could have taken D03/D04/D08, appointment reservation is supported. If D-X was unavailable, the gap is not a dispatch decision.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked by plausibility.

1. **Delayed arrival scans, up to two minutes** — documented, high plausibility. Affects waiting sets at decision times: A05 at 08:20, B04 at 09:31, C05 at 10:10, D10 at 11:25. Could make some apparent FIFO violations disappear or appear. Alters diagnosis: minor, but requires re-checking exact waiting sets.

2. **Manual dispatcher overrides** — high plausibility. B01’s second stop at 09:48 is explicitly “dispatcher reassignment”; QD’s D-F idle gap may also be manual. Explains anomalies that no standard rule predicts. Alters diagnosis: QB and QD would be cleaner FIFO/SPT if overrides were removed.

3. **Undocumented resource unavailability** — medium-high plausibility. D-F idle 11:36–12:00, D-X idle 11:20–11:30, and A1 calibration 08:52–08:58 could reflect unrecorded breaks or meetings. Alters diagnosis: QD gaps could be availability, not policy; QA gap is already explained by calibration.

4. **Estimates noisy or strategically inaccurate** — medium plausibility. `est_min` is the estimate visible at arrival, not realized duration. Could affect SPT in QC and SEPT in QB. Alters diagnosis: SPT may be misclassified if estimates are biased; priority or EDD may be the true rule.

5. **Appointment tolerance and non-preemptive appointments** — medium plausibility. A03 appointment 08:20 was exact; A06 appointment 09:15 started 09:20; D06 appointment 11:30 was exact; B07 appointment 10:30 was not honored. Alters diagnosis: appointment scheduling may be non-preemptive or have tolerance; QA/QD appointment override may be weaker than it appears.

6. **SLA-driven policy switch** — medium plausibility. C05 at 10:54 was selected when its deadline was already missed; C07’s deadline was missed later. Alters diagnosis: QC could be SPT with an SLA override, or a different policy entirely.

# 6. Additional event data needed

Prioritized collection plan.

1. **Actual queue-entry time, separate from scan time, with scan delay** — needed to resolve waiting sets at decision times (A05 at 08:20, B04 at 09:31, C05 at 10:10, D10 at 11:25).
2. **Dispatch-decision time and eligibility set** — at each service start or preemption, record which jobs were considered and which resources were eligible. Needed to distinguish FIFO, priority, SPT, and appointment rules, especially in QD.
3. **Resource state log** — available/busy/unavailable with reason for A1, B1, C1, D-M, D-F, D-X. Needed to explain idle gaps (D-F 11:36–12:00, D-X 11:20–11:30).
4. **Appointment type and tolerance** — exact time versus window, and whether appointments are preemptive. Needed for QA (A03/A06), QD (D06), and QB (B07).
5. **Current estimated remaining time and estimate updates** — for preempted jobs (B01) and SPT decisions (QC). Needed to distinguish SPT from priority and to detect strategic estimates.
6. **Preemption reason and policy version** — whether preemption is allowed, limited, or manual. Needed for QB (B02 preemption, B05 non-preemption).
7. **SLA/deadline status and policy-switch triggers** — for QC (C05 deadline missed, C07 deadline missed). Needed to identify SLA-driven overrides.
8. **Skill eligibility matrix and routing rules** — which resources can serve which jobs, and reservation rules. Needed for QD.

# 7. Final synthesis

**Most identifiable queue: QA.** It is best explained as FIFO with a non-preemptive appointment override. After the single appointment override at 08:20, the remaining service order is exactly FIFO, and priority and SPT are repeatedly violated.

**Least identifiable queue: QB.** It is a hybrid of FIFO, priority-1 preemption/override, resumption of preempted work, and at least one manual dispatcher override. The 09:16 preemption is observationally equivalent to SEPT; B05 did not preempt B01 at 09:46 despite being priority 1; and the 09:48 B03 start is a manual override.

**Most dangerous policy to infer: preemptive priority for QB.** The evidence consists of one preemption (B02) and one priority override (B05), but the rest of the queue follows FIFO. Inferring preemptive priority would predict that B05 should have preempted B01 at 09:46, which did not happen, and would misclassify the manual override at 09:48.
