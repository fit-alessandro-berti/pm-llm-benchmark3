## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The raw/mapped fitness gap shows that much of the March conformance collapse is syntactic rather than operational. When `Eligibility Screen` is mapped to `Validate Eligibility` and duplicated `Open Case` events are removed, fitness returns from about 0.55 to 0.90, while raw fitness remains low. This implies the normative model no longer recognizes renamed or duplicated technical events; it does not imply that the underlying eligibility process remained unchanged. A mapping restores token-based or alignment-based replay only under the assumption that the new label denotes the same business step, same gatekeepers, and same position in the lifecycle. It cannot verify unobserved policy changes, workarounds, or altered decision thresholds.

Duplicated technical events mainly distort ordering, variants, and conformance, creating artificial loops or extra starts; missing resource identifiers mainly corrupt resource analysis, workload attribution, and miner bias, but need not change case duration. After May, however, mapped fitness still declines modestly, rework edges and rework variants rise sharply, supplier-Z exposure rises in parallel, and cycle-time deterioration is driven more by queue waiting than by assessment service time. Those changes persist after label normalization and appear in business-relevant relations, so they cannot be dismissed as logging drift alone.

\[

### 3.5 One global change point or multiple?

The data do not support one global change point. Different artifacts move at different times:

- Raw conformance and schema break in March, but mapped conformance largely recovers.
- Rework-related DFG edges and variants break in May.
- Queue waiting and cycle-time tail worsen from June-July onward.
- Case mix and senior-assessment usage break sharply in October.
- Partial improvement appears in September and December for different reasons.

A single global change point would require one common cause to explain asynchronous schema, control-flow, queueing, and seasonal changes. Multiple mechanism-specific change points are more consistent with the evidence. Formal event-level change-point detection would still be needed to date them more precisely.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Case stratum | Supplier Z yes/no; high complexity yes/no; renewal season yes/no; priority class if available | Separate known exogenous mix drivers from process behavior | At least 30 cases per month-cell or 1% of monthly volume; otherwise roll up to one dimension | Within-stratum DFG, rework, waiting, and fitness over time | Supplier Z is non-random; complexity and priority may be correlated |
| Time epoch | Period labels: baseline, post-migration, supplier disruption, staffing/priority, recovery, renewal, intervention | Align analysis with known change points and seasonal regimes | Each epoch should contain 100 cases and at least one full month unless event is abrupt | Epoch-over-epoch changes and interrupted time series | Epoch boundaries may be arbitrary; shocks overlap |
| Organizational/resource | Assessor team, region, channel, supplier responsibility | Detect resource drift, redistribution, and missingness | 50 cases per team-month or 100 cases per team-quarter | Team-level queue waiting, service time, and handoffs | Missing resource identifiers may bias or prevent segmentation |
| Variant/control-flow family | Fast, rework, senior, other; mapped and deduplicated activity sequences | Monitor behavioral drift without overaggregating rare paths | 20 cases per month per variant family; rare variants pooled | Variant share, duration, and conformance changes | Variant labels can hide meaningful subpaths |
| Activity/schema level | Raw label, mapped label, technical/business event type | Separate logging drift from business drift | Activity observed in 5 cases per month before alerting | Raw vs mapped conformance, label novelty, duplicate events | Low-frequency labels may be unstable |

Primary monitoring population: the primary sentinel segment should be **mapped and deduplicated standard-complexity, non-Supplier-Z, non-renewal cases**. This segment is likely the largest stable core, is least contaminated by the known exogenous shocks, and is therefore sensitive to genuine process-control, queueing, or policy drift. High-complexity, Supplier Z, priority, and renewal segments should be monitored separately because they are operationally important but more exposed to exogenous case-mix and supplier effects.

Seasonality and multiple testing should be handled explicitly. For seasonality, maintain separate baselines for renewal and non-renewal periods, use year-over-year comparisons where available, and alert on residuals from seasonal decomposition rather than raw levels. For multiple testing, avoid one universal threshold. Use metric-specific robust baselines, minimum-support filters, and persistence requirements. During periodic scans, apply a false-discovery-rate control such as Benjamini-Hochberg or a hierarchical gatekeeping approach: schema/logging alerts are triaged first, then control-flow alerts, then performance alerts. Correlated alerts should be suppressed or grouped when an upstream case-mix or logging monitor already explains the shift.

# These functions are intended for a future event-level dataframe. They are not
# executed here because the raw event log is not supplied.

PM4PY_CASE_ID = "case:concept:name"
PM4PY_ACTIVITY = "concept:name"
PM4PY_TIMESTAMP = "time:timestamp"


def prepare_formatted_log(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    case_id: str = "case_id",
    activity: str = "activity",
    timestamp: str = "timestamp",
):
    """
    Copy the dataframe, coerce timestamps, and format it for PM4Py.
    The original dataframe is not modified.
    """
    import pm4py

    out = df.copy()
    out[timestamp] = pd.to_datetime(out[timestamp], utc=True, errors="coerce")
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(
        out,
        case_id=case_id,
        activity_key=activity,
        timestamp_key=timestamp,
    )


def slice_time_window(
    log,
    start,
    end,
    timestamp_key: str = PM4PY_TIMESTAMP,
):
    """
    Slice a formatted PM4Py log or dataframe by time range.
    """
    import pm4py

    return pm4py.filter_time_range(
        log,
        start_date=pd.Timestamp(start),
        end_date=pd.Timestamp(end),
        mode="inclusive",
        timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
    )


def compute_monthly_dfgs(
    log,
    start,
    end,
    case_id_key: str = PM4PY_CASE_ID,
    activity_key: str = PM4PY_ACTIVITY,
    timestamp_key: str = PM4PY_TIMESTAMP,
):
    """
    Compute one DFG per calendar month using pm4py.filter_time_range and
    pm4py.discover_dfg.
    """
    import pm4py

    results = {}
    periods = pd.period_range(start=start, end=end, freq="M")

    for period in periods:
        month_start = period.start_time
        month_end = period.end_time

        month_log = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            log,
            start_date=month_start,
            end_date=month_end,
            mode="inclusive",
            timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
        )

        if len(month_log) == 0:
            continue

        dfg, start_activities, end_activities = pm4py.discover_dfg(
            month_log,
            activity_key=activity_key,
            timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
            case_id_key=case_id_key,
        )

        results[str(period)] = {
            "dfg": dfg,
            "start_activities": start_activities,
            "end_activities": end_activities,
            "event_count": len(month_log),
        }

    return results


def compute_variants(
    log,
    case_id_key: str = PM4PY_CASE_ID,
    activity_key: str = PM4PY_ACTIVITY,
):
    """
    Compute case variants from a formatted PM4Py log.

    return pm4py.get_variants(

        activity_key=activity_key,
        case_id_key=case_id_key,
    )


def compute_temporal_profile(

    activity_key: str = PM4PY_ACTIVITY,
    timestamp_key: str = PM4PY_TIMESTAMP,

    return pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(
        log,
        activity_key=activity_key,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
        case_id_key=case_id_key,
    )


def discover_log_skeleton_model(
    log,
    noise_threshold: float = 0.05,
    activity_key: str = PM4PY_ACTIVITY,
):
    """
    Discover a log skeleton. Uses the high-level PM4Py API if available,
    otherwise falls back to the algorithm module.
    """
    import pm4py

    try:
        return pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(
            log,
            noise_threshold=noise_threshold,
            activity_key=activity_key,
        )
    except AttributeError:
        from pm4py.algo.discovery.log_skeleton import algorithm as log_skeleton_discovery

        return log_skeleton_discovery.apply(
            log,
            parameters={
                "noise_threshold": noise_threshold,
                "activity_key": activity_key,
            },
        )


def discover_declare_model(
    log,
    activity_key: str = PM4PY_ACTIVITY,
):
    """
    Discover a DECLARE model. Uses the high-level PM4Py API if available,
    otherwise falls back to the algorithm module.
    """
    import pm4py

    try:
        return pm4py.discover_declare(
            log,
            activity_key=activity_key,
        )
    except AttributeError:
        from pm4py.algo.discovery.declare import algorithm as declare_discovery

        return declare_discovery.apply(
            log,
            parameters={
                "activity_key": activity_key,
            },
        )


def normalize_labels_without_overwriting(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    activity_col: str = "activity",
    mapping: dict | None = None,
    duplicate_technical_activity: str | None = "Open Case",
    case_id: str = "case_id",
    timestamp: str = "timestamp",
):
    """
    Create a normalized copy of the event dataframe:
      - preserve original activity as activity_raw;
      - create activity_mapped using the supplied mapping;
      - remove duplicated technical events within a case, keeping the earliest.

    The original dataframe is not modified.
    """
    if mapping is None:
        mapping = {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"}

    original = df.copy()
    normalized = df.copy()

    normalized["activity_raw"] = normalized[activity_col]
    normalized["activity_mapped"] = normalized[activity_col].replace(mapping)

    normalized = normalized.sort_values([case_id, timestamp], kind="mergesort")

    if duplicate_technical_activity:
        technical_mask = normalized["activity_mapped"] == duplicate_technical_activity
        if technical_mask.any():
            technical_order = (
                normalized.loc[technical_mask]
                .groupby(case_id)["activity_mapped"]
                .cumcount()
            )
            duplicate_indices = technical_order[technical_order > 0].index
            normalized = normalized.drop(index=duplicate_indices)

    return original, normalized


def compare_raw_and_normalized_logs(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    activity_col: str = "activity",
    mapping: dict | None = None,
    duplicate_technical_activity: str | None = "Open Case",
    case_id: str = "case_id",
    timestamp: str = "timestamp",
):
    """
    Produce raw and normalized formatted logs side by side without overwriting
    the original dataframe.
    """
    original, normalized = normalize_labels_without_overwriting(
        df,
        activity_col=activity_col,
        mapping=mapping,
        duplicate_technical_activity=duplicate_technical_activity,
        case_id=case_id,
        timestamp=timestamp,
    )

    raw_input = normalized[[case_id, timestamp, "activity_raw"]].rename(
        columns={"activity_raw": activity_col}
    )
    mapped_input = normalized[[case_id, timestamp, "activity_mapped"]].rename(
        columns={"activity_mapped": activity_col}
    )

    raw_log = prepare_formatted_log(
        raw_input,
        case_id=case_id,
        activity=activity_col,
        timestamp=timestamp,
    )
    mapped_log = prepare_formatted_log(
        mapped_input,
        case_id=case_id,
        activity=activity_col,
        timestamp=timestamp,
    )

    return {
        "original_dataframe": original,
        "normalized_dataframe": normalized,
        "raw_formatted_log": raw_log,
        "mapped_formatted_log": mapped_log,
    }

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest attribution is that the March collapse in raw conformance is primarily logging/semantic drift caused by migration. The sudden appearance of `Eligibility Screen`, duplicated `Open Case`, and resource missingness, together with recovery of mapped fitness to about 0.90 and stable normalized DFG rates in March-April, supports this with high confidence. I would not call March a business-process change unless mapped, segmented artifacts show otherwise.

The next strongest conclusion is that the May-June deterioration includes supplier-driven control-flow drift. The simultaneous jump in Supplier Z exposure, `Assess -> Request Info` rate, rework variants, and cycle-time tail, followed by partial September recovery when Supplier Z reported recovery, is coherent. Confidence is moderate-high, but Supplier Z cases were already more complex, so confounding remains.

Staffing-driven queueing is plausible and likely contributed from mid-June onward. The observation that assessment service time increased only modestly while reconstructed queue waiting increased substantially is important. However, staffing is confounded with supplier-induced rework and the July priority rule. I would assign moderate confidence to staffing as a capacity mechanism and lower confidence to any precise share.

The July priority rule is plausible as a redistribution mechanism, not necessarily a net capacity loss. It may explain why p90 worsened while high-complexity cases could be protected, but the monthly aggregate cannot confirm it without priority-class waiting. Seasonal renewal drift is strongly supported for October-November by volume, high-complexity share, senior-assessment usage, and cycle-time peaks; December improvement is plausibly a mixture of lower seasonal load, overtime, document-quality campaign, and supplier recovery.

Mediation prevents clean attribution in several places: supplier incompleteness creates rework, rework increases assessment demand, staffing cuts reduce capacity to absorb that demand, and priority changes allocate the resulting delay. I would revise this account if event-level data showed that rework increases occurred mainly outside Supplier Z, that queue waiting did not rise after controlling for rework and priority, or that October senior-assessment growth disappeared after correcting recording practices.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF raw fitness drops while mapped fitness remains stable and unmapped labels or duplicated technical events increase, THEN audit label mapping and event schema before investigating process behavior, BECAUSE the signature indicates semantic/logging drift rather than operational drift.

2. IF mapped `Assess -> Request Info` and rework variants rise, especially within Supplier Z cases, THEN trigger supplier document-completeness investigation and pre-validation of bundles, BECAUSE incomplete supplier documents generate repeated collection and assessment loops.

3. IF reconstructed queue waiting before `Assess` rises while `Assess` service time remains nearly stable, THEN review assessor capacity, backlog, and staffing plan, BECAUSE delay is being generated by queueing/capacity rather than slower individual work.

4. IF high-complexity waiting decreases while standard-case waiting increases after the priority rule, THEN recalibrate priority thresholds and add safeguards for standard-case service levels, BECAUSE the priority rule redistributes delay rather than removing it.

5. IF volume, high-complexity share, and senior-assessment usage rise together during renewal season, THEN activate seasonal capacity and evaluate alerts against a renewal-specific baseline, BECAUSE seasonal population drift changes expected cycle time and control-flow mix.

6. IF missing resource percentage rises in migrated activities, THEN suspend resource-based conclusions and repair resource provenance, BECAUSE missing identifiers bias workload, team, and handoff analyses.

7. IF cycle time and rework improve after overtime and a document-quality campaign while seasonal load also falls, THEN run an interrupted time-series analysis stratified by season, supplier, and complexity before claiming intervention success, BECAUSE multiple concurrent mechanisms can produce the same improvement.
