# 3. Intervention options

Grouped by the process area they target.

### Transport and route choice

**S1. Green shipping recommender (slack, route reliability, segment)**
- *Target:* Package  Ship decisions for all segments. The main targets are Stockair (5%, 18.5 kgCO2e vs 3.6 for ground) and MTOair (6%, 23 kgCO2e).
- *Mechanism:* Recommends the lowest-emission route whose predicted on-time probability is above 85% (SP1). Takes carrier reliability and slack into account, so it does not default to cheap or green blindly.
- *Effect:* Service is neutral if the threshold is honored. Quality risk comes from route–packaging mismatch, so it should be coupled with S6. Resilience needs a rule that keeps a second viable route for Service-contract (R1).
- *Monitor:* Recommendation vs actual route, override reason, on-time outcome by recommended route, calibration of predicted vs actual on-time probability.

**S4. Replace air with priority pick + ground for Standard-stock Service-contract**
- *Target:* Standard-stock Service-contract (16% of orders; 7.8 kgCO2e/order; median 18h; 5% late). SP1 is violated in 22% of these cases.
- *Mechanism:* Priority picking recovers time; ground/consolidated replaces air when slack allows. Air drops sharply in kgCO2e (4–6× per order).
- *Effect:* Cycle time rises only where air was unnecessary, and a slack test keeps air for cases that need it. Risk: Service-contract customers are sensitive, so late delivery must be monitored at segment level.
- *Monitor:* Air share, late rate and p90 for this segment, pick-to-ship time after priority-pick, slack at decision, reversion to air after the initial choice.

**S5. Twice-daily consolidated truck departures for non-urgent stock orders**
- *Target:* Stock  standard pack  ground (42%, 3.6 kgCO2e). Consolidated truck is used by only 850 orders at present.
- *Mechanism:* Consolidated trucks cut transport kgCO2e by 35–55% vs individual parcels. Fixed departures cap the 12–24h wait at roughly half a day.
- *Effect:* Adds wait, so it is only for orders with slack greater than wait plus transit. Regular orders are first candidates. Service-contract orders need a slack check and an alternative route.
- *Monitor:* Consolidation wait vs slack, truck fill rate, late rate of consolidated vs ground cases, carrier dependency.

**S8. Replace some dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes**
- *Target:* Critical  emergency production  reinforced pack  dedicated van (8%, 52 kgCO2e, 3% late). Vans often return empty.
- *Mechanism:* Shared lanes, pooled regional runs and backhaul cut empty running.
- *Effect:* Only for cases where the shared lane meets the promised time. True downtime keeps dedicated vans. A shared lane also adds an alternative route for R1.
- *Monitor:* Late rate and delivery-time variance by lane, van load factor, SP2 justification event present, kgCO2e per Critical order.

### Production and batching

**S2. Dynamic batching with maximum waiting-time caps**
- *Target:* MTO  Batch wait  CNC_A  heat  inspection (13%, 16 kWh). Batch wait feeds 2,910 of the CNC starts.
- *Mechanism:* Energy per item at CNC_A falls from 2.8 to 1.3 kWh going from batch size 1–3 to 12–20, and heat treatment is cheapest above batch size 10. Release rule: size reached *or* oldest job's slack hits a segment-specific cap, so the batch is not held longer than the order can tolerate.
- *Effect:* Keeps most of the energy gain but removes the worst waits (median 4h  38h, heat-treatment waiters 1.7× late risk). Batches should contain only order-backed items to avoid the 6% obsolescence problem. Critical is never batched.
- *Monitor:* Batch size at release, release reason, wait by segment, late rate conditional on batch wait, kWh/item by batch band, 14-day unshipped share.

**S3. Shift MTO from CNC_B to CNC_A when queue and slack allow**
- *Target:* MTO  batch wait  CNC_B  inspection  rework (5%, 160h, 32% late, 32 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap).
- *Mechanism:* CNC_A has half the rework rate (9% vs 18%), so fewer rework loops and less scrap and energy per conforming item.
- *Effect:* CNC_B stays for tight-slack jobs. The risk is overloading CNC_A and lengthening batch waits. Part-family confounding must be checked.
- *Monitor:* Rework rate by machine *and* part family, CNC_A queue length, slack at assignment, late rate of shifted jobs.

**S10. Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular MTO**
- *Target:* MTO Regular (22%, 94h median, p90 180h, 18% late, 11% rework).
- *Mechanism:* Offers a longer, *more reliable* promise date (or a default choice) that makes full batches, ground and consolidated transport feasible and avoids late air upgrades.
- *Effect:* Service is measured against the promise, so late rate may improve, but customer acceptance is uncertain. Not for Service-contract or Critical.
- *Monitor:* Acceptance rate, cancellation due to delay, reorder behavior, air upgrade rate, batch size achieved.

### Quality, packaging and availability

**S6. Packaging–route compatibility check before shipment**
- *Target:* Package  Rework packaging (620 events: about 4% of standard, about 9% of reinforced packaging).
- *Mechanism:* Checks packaging against `route_mode`, weight and part geometry *before* packing, which avoids repackaging and reshipment material. Reinforced packaging stays where Q1 applies (MTO >8 kg), since it reduces damage returns by 40% even though it doubles package material.
- *Effect:* Less rework and delay; quality protected. Resilience: check that a switch to an alternative route does not invalidate packaging.
- *Monitor:* Packaging rework rate by route and part, Q1 conformance, damage returns per package kg.

**S7. Inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations**
- *Target:* CNC  Heat treatment  Inspection  Rework production (470 rework events, 390 loops back to CNC). Rework is concentrated on CNC_B.
- *Mechanism:* Targeted in-process check before the heat-treatment step, so defective parts do not consume energy-intensive processing, plus protected inspection capacity so inspections are not rushed. Rushed inspections miss defects that later cause packaging problems or customer returns.
- *Effect:* Adds a small inspection time on risky combinations, expected to be offset by fewer loops and returns.
- *Monitor:* Defect detection stage, inspection queue time, rework loops per order, returns, p90 cycle time on gated cases.

**S9. Split Critical-downtime into "true downtime" and "precautionary urgent"**
- *Target:* Critical-downtime Service-contract (11%, 38 kgCO2e/order).
- *Mechanism:* Precautionary cases can use stock/ground or a shared lane (4.8 kgCO2e vs 52 for emergency production + van) when slack exists. True downtime remains unchanged.
- *Effect:* Service risk only for the precautionary class (9% late on the ground exception path vs 3%). Risk of gaming: customers may label everything as true downtime.
- *Monitor:* Class proportion over time, verification evidence, late rate by class, van/air share.

**S11. Reduce partial shipments through availability-check rules**
- *Target:* Stock  Partial shipment  ground + backorder (7%, 79h, 24% late, 7.2 kgCO2e vs 3.6).
- *Mechanism:* Hold-and-ship-complete when missing items arrive within the slack; otherwise ship split only for Service-contract or urgent cases. Avoids a second shipment and extra packaging.
- *Effect:* Waiting can increase cancellations (60 already cancel from Backorder wait). Needs line-level data.
- *Monitor:* Partial rate, second-shipment share, cancellation due to delay, late rate after rule change.

**S12. Sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides**
- *Target:* The override points: SP1 (22% violations), SP2 (31% missing justification), Q1 loops.
- *Mechanism:* Mandatory reason-coded event for each override, with slack, alternatives considered and approver. This makes exceptions visible and gives data to tune S1.
- *Effect:* Little direct environmental change, but it enables learning and controls the gaming risk. Slight administrative overhead.
- *Monitor:* Override rate by segment and reason, repeat overriders, justification completeness.

# 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching  waiting  expediting.** Bigger batches save CNC/heat-treat kWh/item but increase batch wait. Late cases then get upgraded to air (MTOair: 23 kgCO2e, 24 kWh) or emergency production, which can cancel the energy gain. Waiting for a heat-treat batch already carries 1.7× late risk. *Mitigation:* slack-based caps (S2) and tracking of post-batch air upgrades.
2. **Batching  obsolescence.** Filling batches with forecast-based items creates the 6% not-shipped-in-14-days waste. *Mitigation:* batch only order-backed items, track unshipped share by batch.
3. **Modal shift  lateness  re-shipment/cancellation.** Moving air/van to ground can cause late deliveries. Cancellation due to delay or a replacement shipment wastes both the original and the new emissions. *Mitigation:* 85% on-time threshold, reversal rules, segment guardrails.
4. **Consolidation  waiting  customer workaround.** A 12–24h hold can push customers to request express, split orders, or order from other suppliers. Single consolidated lanes also concentrate carrier risk (R1). *Mitigation:* apply only where slack exists, keep alternative route.
5. **Packaging reduction  damage returns.** Cutting package material on Q1 parts (heavy MTO) removes the 40% damage-return protection, and a return costs a second shipment plus new packaging. Conversely, reinforced-by-default doubles material on parts that don't need it. *Mitigation:* S6 compatibility logic, Q1 stays mandatory, measure package kg per damage-free delivery.
6. **Rework shifting.** Moving work to CNC_A without checking capacity can lengthen queues and trigger rushed inspections, which raise rework loops. A stronger inspection gate can itself create an inspection queue. Rework is also hidden material waste (MTO via CNC_B: 1.8 kg scrap, 32 kWh). *Mitigation:* monitor inspection queue time, CNC_A load, rework by part family.
7. **Customer behavior gaming.** If precautionary urgency is penalized, customers may declare everything as true downtime; Regular MTO customers may decline longer promise dates or place more, smaller orders, which lowers batching efficiency and raises packaging and transport per item. *Mitigation:* verification, contract allowances, feedback on the cost of urgency.
8. **Displacement to customers and resilience.** Replacing vans with shared lanes may move emissions and risk to the customer (downtime, expedited workarounds). A single green default route reduces R1 resilience during carrier disruption. *Mitigation:* keep true downtime protected, validate reliability, test disruptions.
9. **Partial shipment rule  holds and cancellations.** Holding for complete orders can increase backorder waits and cancellations (60 of 220 backorder waits cancelled already). Stock reserved for held orders can cause more partials elsewhere.

# 6. Balanced recommendation

**Package: "slack-aware routing, capped batching and quality at the source", segment-specific.**

**Design principles**
- Environmental effort goes where it is concentrated: MTO for energy, scrap and rework; Critical plus Service-contract stock for transport.
- Every green default is conditional on slack and reliability, with protected exceptions for true downtime.
- Quality levers (S6, S7) come first because they reduce waste without a service trade-off.

**Segment design**

| Segment | Redesign |
|---|---|
| Standard-stock Regular (39%) | S5 twice-daily consolidation for non-urgent orders; S1 recommends ground by default; S11 for partial shipment; S6 |
| Standard-stock Service-contract (16%) | S4 priority pick + ground when slack is sufficient; air stays when not; SP1 enforced with logged slack; R1 second route retained |
| MTO Regular (22%) | S10 reliable promise dates; S2 batching with longer caps; S3 CNC_A preference; S7 gate; S6/Q1 |
| MTO Service-contract (12%) | S2 with shorter caps; S3 when slack; S7; ground/consolidated only if SP1 probability met; air retained for tight slack |
| Critical-downtime (11%) | S9 split; true downtime keeps emergency production and dedicated vans (with SP2 justification); precautionary class uses stock/ground or shared lane (S8); no batching |

**Sequencing**
1. *Foundation (first):* S12 exception log; add missing attributes (slack, predicted on-time probability, override reason, van load, defect stage); reconcile DFG.
2. *Low-regret:* S6 packaging check, S7 targeted gate, S4 and the SP1 part of S1.
3. *Conditional pilots:* S2 and S3 on CNC_A/heat-treat; S5; S11.
4. *Guarded pilots:* S8, S9, S10, rolled out only after reliability evidence exists.

**Why this balances the objectives**
- *Environmental:* acts on the MTO energy/scrap hotspot (batching, machine choice, rework avoidance) and the transport hotspots (Critical's 38 kgCO2e, unnecessary air for Service-contract), and cuts hidden waste in rework, repackaging and second shipments.
- *Service:* slack-gated rules, segment-specific caps, and no change to true downtime paths. The 85% SP1 threshold ties each decision to on-time probability.
- *Quality:* Q1 and reinforced packaging retained where they prevent returns; inspection strengthened where defects originate.
- *Resilience:* R1 built into the recommender; shared lanes add alternative routes; no dependence on a single consolidated lane.
- *Explicit trade-off:* the package does not choose the lowest-emission option everywhere. It accepts some air, dedicated-van and non-batched production where slack or downtime justify it.

# 7. Validation and governance plan

**Baseline and design**
- Rebuild the pre-change baseline with a reconciled DFG and line-level data where possible.
- Stage the rollout by segment/site (or by matched weeks). Compare with control groups, adjusting for part weight, geometry, distance, slack and demand mix. Use difference-in-differences or matched-case comparison, because the batch-wait and CNC_B effects may be confounded.
- Report outcomes per segment and per variant. Aggregate averages would hide segment harm, given the low precision of the discovered model.

**Conformance monitoring**
- Use declarative constraints rather than one discovered model, given the many combinations of emergency paths and partial shipments.
- **SP1:** for each Standard-stock Service-contract and other non-urgent case, if logged P(on-time by ground/consolidated) > 85%, then route  {ground, consolidated}. Target: reduce the 22% violation rate; every remaining violation has a reason code.
- **SP2:** every Dedicated van event is preceded by a Critical-downtime justification event. Target: close the 31% gap; include SP2 checks on shared-lane switches.
- **Q1:** MTO >8 kg must have reinforced packaging or an inspection certification event. Monitor for Q1 violations that correlate with rework packaging, damage returns or reshipments.
- **R1:** for Service-contract, log available alternate routes at dispatch, and run disruption drills or replay to test capacity.

**Service and quality guardrails (segment level)**
- Track late delivery, median and p90 cycle time, and cancellation due to delay for all five segments, compared to the baseline. Examples: Standard-stock Service-contract 5% late, Critical 4% late / p90 30h, MTO Regular 18% late.
- Pre-agree stop or rollback triggers for any segment (for example, a sustained rise above baseline tolerance, or a worse p90). Critical true-downtime is a hard guardrail.
- Track batch wait and late-delivery conditional on batch wait, kWh/item by batch band, and obsolescence (14-day unshipped).
- Track rework by machine and part family, inspection queue time, packaging rework, damage returns (including after Close), and package kg per damage-free delivery.

**Environmental verification**
- Report kWh per conforming item, scrap kg per conforming order, transport kgCO2e per on-time order, and package kg per damage-free delivery. These metrics include rework, reshipment and returns, so a shift of impact to a hidden loop becomes visible.
- Check for displacement: air upgrade after batch wait, reshipments after ground switch, repeat orders, customer declaration of Critical.
- Review LCA factors (grid, transport method) periodically. Quantified claims need sensitivity ranges.

**Exception governance**
- Named owners by area: logistics (SP1/SP2, routes), production (batching, machine allocation), quality (Q1, gate), customer operations (promise dates, Critical classification).
- Weekly operational review of overrides and guardrails; monthly review of trend, recommender calibration and route reliability; quarterly review of policies and thresholds (85% rule, batch caps, consolidation departure times).
- Overrides must be reason-coded with slack and alternatives; repeat overrides trigger root-cause review. Overrides are allowed, particularly for true downtime and disruptions, but they are not silent.
- Customer-facing governance: audit Critical classification, review Service-contract feedback, and adjust rules if recurring harm appears.
- Sunset or adapt rules: any intervention that fails its guardrail is reduced in scope or retired, and the decision is documented.
