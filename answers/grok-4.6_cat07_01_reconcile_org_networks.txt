# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

## Handover-of-work network

Handover-of-work is a directed graph of sequential resource succession: an edge from A to B means B executed the next recorded event after A on the same case. Frequency and elapsed time describe volume and latency of that succession, not collaboration quality, authority, or skill.

What it implies about Mira_Coordinator:
- She is the main human sink after `Bot_Rules` (920 edges; next are Alice 610 and Bruno 590). The automated layer treats her as the default exception/overflow destination, not as a peer of the two team leads.
- She is a high-volume dispatcher. Outbound edges go to both specialists (Omar, Priya) and all four adjusters, so she redistributes work across organizational silos rather than inside one team.
- She is also a return sink: specialists and temps hand back to her (Omar 160, Priya 140, Temp_1 190, Temp_2 170). Combined with out-degree 0.82, in-degree 0.51, and betweenness 0.77, she sits on many shortest paths between bots, specialists, temps, and line teams.
- Short elapsed times after `Bot_Rules` (3.5 min) and from temps (8–9 min) fit a routing desk; longer outbound times to Omar/Priya (42–51 min) fit waiting for specialist capacity, not Mira’s own service time.

What it does not imply:
- Centrality is not proof that she is a leader, a bottleneck, or a control failure. Handover counts events, not decisions.
- High out-degree does not mean she performs the downstream work or remains on the case.
- Edges to vendors are almost absent, so she is not the payment or verification operator; `Vendor_Pay` is fed mainly by the team leads.
- Handover cannot distinguish assignment, rework, escalation, and log artifacts such as “Assign Specialist” written by the coordinator after the specialist already started.

## Working-together network

Working-together is an undirected co-case graph: edge weight is the share of cases in which both resources appear at least once. It measures case-level overlap, not sequence, intensity, or duration.

What it implies:
- Mira is peripheral. Co-case rates with Carla/Diego/Elise/Farid are only 6–8% despite hundreds of handovers; with Alice/Bruno only 4–5%. Weighted degree 0.16 is closer to Omar/Priya than to team-lead–adjuster cores (0.43–0.49).
- The North/South pairs (Alice–Carla 27%, Bruno–Diego 26%, plus junior support 22–24%) are the real standing teams. Mira is not a member of those cliques.
- Low overlap is consistent with brief, case-local interventions: she appears, routes or reopens, then leaves while the case continues with others.

What it does not imply:
- Low co-occurrence does not mean she is unimportant. A resource who touches 22% of cases, 64% of reopens, and 71% of complaints can be operationally critical and still rare as a co-worker.
- It does not measure whether her intervention changed the case path.
- It is biased against short-lived roles and toward resources who stay on a case from assessment through approval.

## Subcontracting network

Subcontracting (here n=2) finds A→…→B→…→A within a short event window: A delegates and later reappears. It is the network that best captures broker/return-desk behavior.

What it implies:
- Mira is the dominant broker. Top patterns are Mira–Omar–Mira (126: missing information → medical review → assign/reopen) and Mira–Priya–Mira (98: escalate → legal review → reopen/assign). Subcontracting betweenness 0.69 is far above team leads (0.19–0.21).
- She is not only a one-way router; she often remains accountable for the case after the specialist answers.
- The Bot_Rules–Mira–Bot_Rules pattern (62: fraud screen → manual exception → fraud-screen update) shows she also closes a loop with automation, not only with humans.
- Team-lead–adjuster subcontracting (Alice–Carla 86, Bruno–Diego 82) is a different, local approve–negotiate–approve loop. Mira’s loops are cross-skill and exception-shaped.

What it does not imply:
- n=2 subcontracting does not prove she “owns” the specialist’s work or that the return event is a real managerial review. “Assign Specialist” or “Reopen Claim” after a review can be a logging convention.
- Frequency is not delay. Specialist loops can be long even if the count is moderate.
- It does not show whether she chooses the specialist freely or follows a rules table.

## Performance/time statistics

These describe duration, volume, and exception mix, not social role.

What they imply:
- Median service time 4.2 minutes is routing-scale, not assessment-scale (adjusters 17–24 min, Omar 37 min, Priya 51 min, vendors hours). She is not doing the heavy cognitive work on most events.
- Exception share 0.48 is the highest among non-specialists; specialists are higher (0.62–0.71) because they receive the already-flagged tail. Mira’s mix is exception-enriched relative to team leads (0.21–0.26) and temps (0.13–0.14).
- She touches 905 cases / 960 events ≈ 1.06 events per case: typically a single short intervention, which explains low working-together.
- Cycle-time contribution is “high variance”: her presence is associated with heterogeneous, often long cases, but association is not causation. Exception cases are long because they are hard, and she is assigned to hard cases.
- The 6-day absence is the strongest causal-ish evidence: ordinary low-value cycle time unchanged; exception queues grew; p90 for `exception_flag=True` rose 8.4 → 13.9 days. She is not on the happy path; she is on the exception path’s critical chain.
- Bots absorb volume (Bot_Ingest 4100, Bot_Rules 4750) with seconds of service time. Vendors absorb waiting time (`Vendor_Verify` 2.1 h, `Vendor_Pay` 1.3 h) and sit at the ends of coverage and payment. Mira is neither a volume bot nor a waiting-time vendor; she is a thin, high-betweenness human layer between them and the line.

What they do not imply:
- High exception rate on her cases does not mean she creates exceptions.
- Short service time does not mean low skill; brokers are fast by design.
- Absence impact does not prove she is irreplaceable as a person; it proves the *function* she currently performs has no slack.

## Role-discovery or activity-profile similarity

Organizational-role discovery and activity-based resource similarity group resources by the activities they perform, not by who they sit with.

What they imply (from activity profiles, even without running the algorithm):
- Mira’s signature {Request Missing Information, Reopen Claim, Escalate Complaint, Assign Specialist} is unique. Team leads do {Triage, Approve Settlement, (Reopen)}. Adjusters do {Assess Damage, Negotiate} or {Assess Damage, Request Missing Information}. Specialists are almost single-activity. Temps do {Validate Coverage, Request Missing Information / Close Claim}. Bots and vendors are machine/vendor profiles.
- Similarity should place her closest to an “exception/coordination” role, not to Alice/Bruno (approval authority) and not to Omar/Priya (deep review). Juniors share “Request Missing Information” with her, so a naive similarity metric may pull Elise/Farid toward Mira and hide that juniors request information *inside* assessment, while Mira requests it as a desk function.
- She is not a substitute team lead on the evidence of activities: she rarely if ever Approves Settlement or Issues Payment; those stay with Alice/Bruno → Vendor_Pay.

What they do not imply:
- Distinct activity profile ≠ official job title. “Assign Specialist” may be a logged workaround.
- Similarity can merge or split roles depending on activity granularity (e.g. if “Assign Specialist” is treated as a real process step versus a system note).
- Role discovery will not resolve whether she is a formal desk, an informal broker, or a logging artifact; it only clusters behavior.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

## H1. Formal coordinator / exception desk

**Mechanism.** After automated ingest and rules, exception-like cases are routed to a designated desk. Mira triages missing information, reopens, escalates complaints, and assigns specialists, then exits. Team leads keep North/South line authority and payment release.

**Supporting evidence.** Activity set matches an exception desk; 920 Bot_Rules→Mira edges; 48% exception share; 64% of reopens and 71% of complaints; 4.2 min service time; ~1 event per case; absence hurts only exception cases; ops manager’s claim that removing her raises formal escalations.

**Limiting evidence.** Process owner calls her a “queue cleaner,” not a desk owner. Compliance concern implies discretion beyond a scripted desk. “Assign Specialist” is not in the official 14-activity list, suggesting an informal or system-only step. Working-together with team leads is only 4–5%, odd if she were a recognized coordination layer. Bot_Rules also routes hundreds of cases directly to Alice/Bruno, so she is not the unique post-automation coordinator.

**Future-log predictions.** If formalized, Mira (or a named Exception_Desk role) should keep high handover/subcontracting centrality, low working-together, stable activity profile, and a documented rule linking `exception_flag` / complaint / reopen to her. If the desk is only informal, activity names and routing rules will keep drifting.

## H2. Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency

**Mechanism.** Exception flow has a single human serialization point. Work waits for Mira’s assignment or reopen action. During load or absence, exception WIP and tail cycle time rise even if her own events are short.

**Supporting evidence.** Highest handover betweenness (0.77) and subcontracting betweenness (0.69); high-variance cycle-time contribution; absence: exception p90 8.4→13.9 days while ordinary claims unchanged; specialists return to her before the case proceeds; temps escalate to her rather than to team leads.

**Limiting evidence.** Short median service time argues against a processing bottleneck; the constraint is availability and uniqueness, not minutes per event. She touches only 22% of claims, so she cannot bottleneck the main flow. Vendor_Verify (2.1 h) and Vendor_Pay (1.3 h) plus specialist service times are larger time sinks on the cases she touches. Betweenness can be high because she is a star router, not because a queue sits on her desk every day. We lack her queue-length and waiting-time-before-Mira series except during the absence window.

**Future-log predictions.** If she is a key-person bottleneck, waiting time from Bot_Rules/temps/specialist-return *to Mira’s next event* should dominate her service time, spike when she is offline, and fall if a second exception resource is added. If she is only a star logger, waiting time to her events will stay small and absence will mainly change *who* is recorded, not tail cycle time.

## H3. Organizational boundary spanner / triage broker

**Mechanism.** North and South are closed teams (Alice–Carla–Elise; Bruno–Diego–Farid). Hard cases need a broker who can send work to medical, legal, either team, or back to automation. Mira spans those boundaries, then drops off the case. Adjusters experience her as “the person who knows where hard cases should go.”

**Supporting evidence.** Outbound handovers to both teams and both specialists; specialist return loops; low working-together with everyone (brokers do not co-inhabit cases); Omar–Priya co-case rate only 4% (complex cases are rare and go through her, not through a standing multi-specialist team); Mira–adjuster co-case 6–8% despite 280–350 handovers each (many brief crossings); process owner vs adjuster quotes match a broker who looks like a cleaner from above and a pathfinder from below.

**Limiting evidence.** Bot_Rules already spans teams (edges to Alice, Bruno, and Mira). A broker with real case knowledge would be expected to co-occur more with specialists than  the working-together table shows (Omar/Priya are not even listed as Mira pairs, implying co-case rates below the table cut or simply omitted; handover exists, but sustained joint work may not). High exception share could be assignment policy rather than spanning skill. Compliance worry is exactly that a broker without a rulebook becomes an uncontrolled gate.

**Future-log predictions.** If she is a true broker, cases that touch both a specialist and an opposite-team adjuster should almost always include Mira; removing or splitting her function should increase either specialist mis-assignment or Alice/Bruno cross-team handovers. If she is only an exception logger, cross-boundary cases will continue via Bot_Rules and team leads without her.

## H4. Quality gatekeeper (secondary)

**Mechanism.** She inspects incomplete or risky work (missing information, fraud-screen exceptions, complaints, reopens) and refuses to let the case proceed until a specialist or a cleaner junior path is attached.

**Supporting evidence.** Bot_Rules–Mira–Bot_Rules fraud-screen loop; Temp_1–Vendor_Verify–Temp_1 / Temp→Mira missing-info escalations; high reopen and complaint coverage; specialists return to her.

**Limiting evidence.** She does not sit on Approve Settlement or Vendor_Pay; Alice/Bruno are the financial gates. She is absent from 78% of claims, including many with `exception_flag=True` at ingest (Bot_Ingest exception share 0.19). Gatekeepers usually have higher working-together with the people they gate.

**Future-log predictions.** A real gate would show rejected or bounced variants (Mira → request missing info → same activity again) and lower rework after her intervention. A logger would show no quality effect after controlling for case type.

## H5. Data artifact / automation-monitor shadow (secondary)

**Mechanism.** Some “Mira” events are system actions attributed to the exception-desk login: assignment stamps, reopen buttons, fraud-screen overrides. Centrality is partly a logging convention.

**Supporting evidence.** “Assign Specialist” is extra-model; ~1.06 events/case; disappearance after Bot_Rules routing; working-together far below handover; process-owner “queue cleaner” framing.

**Limiting evidence.** Absence changed real exception cycle times, which a pure label cannot do unless *nobody else can press the buttons*. Specialist and temp handbacks with 8–12 min latency look like human responses, not batch stamps. Adjusters describe her as a knowledgeable person.

**Future-log predictions.** If artifactual, `org:resource` for assignment/reopen will flip to Bot_Rules or a generic desk ID after a RACI/logging cleanup, while operational delays stay. If human, those activities remain named to a person and absence effects persist.

## H6. Informal team lead / specialist scheduler (secondary)

**Mechanism.** She de facto schedules Omar and Priya and overrides North/South allocation, functioning as a third lead without approval rights.

**Supporting evidence.** Almost exclusive human path onto medical/legal; subcontracting loops; compliance discretion concern; ops-manager fear of specialist overload if she is removed.

**Limiting evidence.** No Approve Settlement / Vendor_Pay path; working-together with leads only 4–5%; Alice/Bruno retain routine assignment to Carla/Diego and payment release. She looks more like a scheduler *of exceptions* than a general lead.

**Future-log predictions.** If she is the specialist scheduler, Omar/Priya utilization and waiting times will track her presence; team-lead→specialist edges will remain rare. If scheduling is rules-based, Bot_Rules or a work-list engine can replace those edges without her.

# 3. Risk assessment

**Bottleneck risk — moderate for exceptions, low for the main flow.**  
The happy path is Bot_Ingest → Bot_Rules → team lead → adjuster → team lead → Vendor_Pay. Absence left ordinary low-value cycle time unchanged, so she is not the plant bottleneck. For `exception_flag=True`, the p90 jump of 5.5 days in six days is material. The bottleneck is uniqueness and queueing before a short action, not her 4.2 min service time. Vendors and specialists remain larger time components on the same cases.

**Coordination risk — high, but double-edged.**  
She is the only resource with a star-shaped interface to both specialist skills, both regional teams, temps, and the rules engine. That reduces coordination failures on hard cases (adjuster view) and concentrates routing knowledge in one head (ops-manager view). Low working-together means coordination is *episodic*, not embedded in teams: when she is away, teams do not automatically inherit the spanning ties.

**Key-person risk — high for the exception/complaint/reopen slice.**  
22% of claims, 64% of reopens, 71% of complaints, betweenness 0.77/0.69, no evident deputy. Temps escalate to her; specialists return to her; Bot_Rules prefers her for the exception bucket. Loss of the *function* is already observed. That is not the same as “Mira is indispensable as a heroic individual”; it is an unbuffered role.

**Compliance / control risk — plausible and material, not proven misconduct.**  
Segregation concern is real: the same resource appears to classify the problem (missing info, complaint, reopen, fraud-screen exception) and to choose the next specialist or adjuster. There is no direct evidence of favoritism, suppression of complaints, or biased specialist loading. There *is* structural discretion. Alice/Bruno still own settlement approval and payment release, which limits financial SoD failure, but complaint and medical/legal routing are weaker controls. Uncontrolled discretion is a design finding, not an allegation.

**Workload fairness risk — moderate, asymmetric.**  
Mira’s own events are short; her load is interrupt-driven variety, not long casework. Fairness risk sits more on *recipients*: she can overload Omar/Priya or dump rework on juniors (Elise/Farid already carry more “Request Missing Information”). Exception share on her outbound cases is high by construction. Temps have *low* exception share (0.13–0.14) and bounce hard cases to her, which protects temps and concentrates difficulty. Senior adjusters receive both routine lead assignments and Mira reassignments; juniors receive more Mira rework. Without assignment-reason codes, we cannot separate skill-based routing from dumping.

**Data-quality risk — material and it inflates her centrality.**  
“Assign Specialist” is outside the stated activity catalog. Lifecycle transitions are unused in the narrative. Short routing events plus disappearance produce high handover/subcontracting and low working-together — which is a real organizational signal *and* a logging signature. If bots or a shared mailbox sometimes write events under her name, betweenness is overstated. Conversely, if she advises in ways not logged, spanning is understated. SNA conclusions should be treated as upper bounds on recorded routing, not as a complete picture of influence.

Automated and vendor resources change the risk picture: the organization already automated ingest, triage/fraud, coverage verification, and payment. Mira is the residual *human exception API* between those systems and scarce specialists. Removing her without replacing that API would push exceptions onto Alice/Bruno (who are approval gates) or onto specialists (who are already long-service, high-exception resources).

A broker hypothesis predicts Mira almost always present when Omar or Priya co-occur with an adjuster; a desk hypothesis predicts she is replaceable by a flag.

6. **Service and waiting time.**  
   Apply `get_service_time` and `insert_case_service_waiting_time` at resource and activity grain. Predict H2 if wait-for-Mira dominates the absence effect; predict H3/H1 if wait-for-specialist dominates and Mira only changes *who* is next. Complementary (still PM4Py-adjacent): dotted-chart by resource over the absence week; handover network visualization (`view_sna` / `save_vis_sna`) side-by-side for the three SNA semantics so stakeholders see that “central” is definition-dependent; simple SoD check (same resource on Escalate Complaint and the following Legal Review assignment versus a second resource).

# 5. Recommendation

**Change immediately**
- Treat the exception/complaint/reopen path as an official, staffed function with a deputy. The absence test already failed; do not wait for Mira’s next leave.
- Split *detection* from *destination choice* on paper and in the system: Bot_Rules or a documented matrix should propose Omar vs Priya vs team vs vendor; Mira (or the desk) confirms or records a reason code. This answers the compliance concern without accusing anyone.
- Log “Assign Specialist” as a first-class activity with `lifecycle:transition` start/complete, reason, and proposed vs accepted resource. Stop using a personal login as the exception mailbox if more than one person can perform the function.
- Do **not** route ordinary low-value claims through her; the data say that path does not need her.

**Monitor**
- Exception p90 cycle time, wait-for-desk, wait-for-Omar/Priya, and Bot_Rules→desk vs Bot_Rules→team-lead shares (weekly).
- Specialist utilization and junior rework share after her assignments (fairness).
- Handover betweenness vs working-together degree: if betweenness stays high while a second person joins the desk, key-person risk is falling. If working-together with leads stays near 4–5%, she is still not being absorbed into line management — which is acceptable if the desk is meant to be separate.
- Vendor waiting times, so exception delays are not mis-blamed on the desk.

**Do not change until more evidence exists**
- Do not remove Mira or “collapse the exception desk into the team leads.” Leads are already the approval/payment gate; loading them with spanning and complaint intake would mix control roles and, on present evidence, recreate the bottleneck on Alice/Bruno. Any removal must first clone the function (second desk resource + rules matrix + specialist work lists).
- Do not promote her into a third team lead or give her Approve Settlement / Vendor_Pay rights on this evidence. That would worsen SoD.
- Do not redesign North/South team structure solely because SNA shows her as a star. Those teams look healthy on working-together.
- Do not treat high centrality as a performance score (good or bad) in HR processes.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira_Coordinator is the human exception interface between bots, vendors, two regional teams, and scarce medical/legal specialists. She appears on about one in five claims but on most reopens and complaints. Her events are short, so she rarely “works together” with others; she hands work on and often takes it back after a specialist answers. That is why she looks central in handover and subcontracting and peripheral in working-together — not a contradiction, but a broker/desk signature.

She is not the bottleneck for ordinary claims. She is a key-person risk for exceptions: when she was away for six days, hard cases queued and the slowest exception cases got much slower. There is no evidence of misconduct, but the same person currently flags problems and chooses the next specialist, which is a control-design issue.

Do not remove her. Officially staff the exception desk with a deputy, put routing rules and reason codes in the system, and keep settlement approval with the team leads. Measure exception waiting time, not her personal heroics.
