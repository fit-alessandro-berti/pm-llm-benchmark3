# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

This review treats the two submissions as candidate **controllable artifacts** for a model-governance board, not as scoreboard winners. Intended uses are separated as follows.

| Use case | Goal | Acceptable fitness/precision stance |
|---|---|---|
| Exploration / discovery communication | Explain mainstream flow to business users; support ideation | High fitness and simplicity preferred; moderate precision OK if over-permissiveness is disclosed |
| Conformance enforcement / audit blocking | Reject noncompliant cases at runtime or in audit | Precision and explicit mandatory semantics dominate; holdout fitness must not be bought by optionalizing mandates |
| Simulation / capacity planning | Generate plausible futures | Needs well-founded optionality and attribute guards; pure variant enumeration under-generates |
| Stakeholder communication / training | Teach policy | Comprehensible structure + probe traces beat arc-degree scores |
| Impact analysis / IT ownership | Change management | Duplicate labels and branch explosion are first-class defects |

**Assumptions**

- The control-flow log lacks `external/internal` and `affected_check` attributes (P3, P6). Models must not invent those attributes as silent always-true guards.
- Alignment metrics are **evidence about replay under a cost function**, not policy truth (F5).
- “Suspicious” (P7, B8) is not identical to “syntactically unfitting”; governance may flag rather than hard-reject.
- Same claimed 80/20 split and PM4Py build do **not** imply comparable experiments without seeds, preprocessing, and alignment costs.
- Use-case mix for this board: enforcement and audit are in scope; pure exploration is insufficient justification for shipping Model S alone (contra F7).

**No single aggregate score is computed.** Any weighted sum would hide that S fails hard compliance probes while C fails maintainability and holdout combination tests. Fitness and precision here move in opposite directions for structural reasons (optional  vs. variant XOR), not because one model is uniformly “better.”

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

**Strongest case for Model S.**  
The structure is a short block: register  concurrent optionalized activities  exclusive approval  release. It is robust when events are missing ( in each `X(activity, )`), easy to draw, fast to align, and scores well on fitness, generalization, simplicity, and informal comprehension. For pure exploration on noisy logs, that is a defensible engineering choice. The advocate claim (F7) is coherent *if and only if* the model is labeled non-enforcing and paired with external rules.

**Strongest case against Model S.**  
The same -optional parallel block is a **semantic flaw**, not mere noise tolerance: it systematically deletes mandatory control points (F1). Parallelism puts Expedite unordered w.r.t. checks. There is **no** Correct Dossier, so P6/P7 are unrepresented. “Permissive for missing events” and “permits skipping Credit Check on a complete trace” are different phenomena; Model S collapses them.

**At least five undesirable or policy-ambiguous behaviors (concrete traces)**

1. **Skips both mandatory checks** (forbids B3-class):  
   `Register Dossier  Manager Approval  Release Order`  
   Admitted via  on Credit and Technical.

2. **Skips Technical Review only:**  
   `Register Dossier  Credit Check  Auto Approval  Release Order`

3. **Expedite before mandatory checks** (B4 / F1):  
   `Register Dossier  Expedite Review  Credit Check  Technical Review  Manager Approval  Release Order`  
   Parallel `+` does not enforce Expedite after checks.

4. **Double approval path not blocked if model were extended—but within S, approval is XOR so OK; however S allows approval with zero checks and optional chaos:**  
   `Register Dossier  Supplier Review  Expedite Review  Auto Approval  Release Order`  
   External-looking path with no Credit/Technical.

5. **Suspicious re-check without correction** (B8) — and worse, unbounded re-execution in the parallel block is *not* in S; actually each activity appears at most once in the `+` of XORs. So B8 is **rejected** by S, but for the wrong reason (no loops), while **legitimate correction** is also impossible:  
   `Register Dossier  Credit Check  Technical Review  Correct Dossier  Technical Review  Manager Approval  Release Order` (B6) is **rejected** because `Correct Dossier` is absent from the alphabet of S.

6. **Extra policy-ambiguous interleaving:**  
   `Register Dossier  Expedite Review  Supplier Review  Manager Approval  Release Order`  
   No checks; Expedite first.

**Intentional noise tolerance vs accidental flaws**

| Mechanism | Intentional? | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `X(a, )` on Credit/Technical | Marketed as missing-event robustness | Accidentally makes mandates optional on complete traces |
| Parallel `+` over four activities | Flexibility of order | Accidentally allows Expedite  checks |
| No Correct Dossier | Omission | Cannot express P6/P7 at all |
| XOR approvals + final Release | Intentional P1/P5 fragment | Correct fragment inside an incorrect envelope |

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

**Strongest case for Model C.**  
It is an explicit XOR of observed (or frequent) full paths. Mandatory checks appear on almost every branch before approval; Expedite is never before checks in the listed paths; several external and expedite variants are spelled out; training precision is high; the team can point auditors at “the path that fired.” For a frozen, low-variety process with rare change, path enumeration can be audit-friendly (team claim).

**Strongest case against Model C**

- **Branch enumeration = memorization.** High training precision and low holdout fitness (0.744) plus low TBR generalization (0.412) are the signature of a variant union, not a generative policy model (F2, F4). Unseen but legal combinations (Supplier + Auto Approval + alternate check order) fall out of the XOR.
- **Duplicate labels (reported 33).** Same activity name on many disjoint paths destroys single-node ownership, DPIA/impact analysis, and maintainability (F3).
- **Incomplete policy coverage inside the XOR.** Only one Auto Approval path is listed (no Supplier/Expedite/Correct combinations with Auto). Manager paths dominate. B2-like and B5-like traces are at risk.
- **Correction handling is a single hard-coded path** forcing *both* checks after one Correct Dossier, and only once, in one order—transparent neither for “credit only” nor “technical only” (F6, B6).
- **No loop:** repeated corrections or repeated suspicious checks are mostly out of band.
- **Holdout degradation** is not automatically “good enforcement” (F8). Without a curated negative suite, low holdout fitness equally indicates under-generalization of *legitimate* behavior (F4, F5).
- **Runtime and simplicity collapse** follow from unfolded paths—operational cost without semantic completeness.
- **Precision artifact:** precision measured only on positive observed traces against a model built as the union of those traces is partly circular (F5).

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

Ranked by **likely decision impact** (highest first):

1. **Fitness rewarded by optionalizing mandatory behavior (S)**  
   `X(Credit, )` and `X(Technical, )` buy training/holdout fitness and generalization while violating P2. This is the dominant reason S “wins” numeric fitness.

2. **Positive-log-only precision (both; masks S, flatters C)**  
   F5: no curated negatives. S’s true enforcement precision is far worse than 0.584 suggests once B3/B4-class traces are counted. C’s 0.941 partly measures “are you one of the memorized paths?”

3. **Unseen-combination / variant-enumeration brittleness (C)**  
   Holdout 0.744 and generalization 0.412 reflect missing legal products of independent choices (check order × supplier × expedite × approval), not necessarily fraud.

4. **Missing-event bias**  
   S is tuned for missing events; F4 notes possible missing Supplier in holdout. Metrics conflate “event dropped by logging” with “activity skipped in reality.” Without attribute-aware incomplete-logging models, S is over-credited.

5. **Train/holdout leakage or split sensitivity**  
   No seeds, no published case lists, same claimed 80/20. C’s branches look training-derived; if frequent variants leaked into model construction beyond the training split, precision/fitness are optimistic. Even without leakage, a single split overstates C’s stability.

6. **Duplicated-label effects (C)**  
   Inflates size, confuses precision implementations that key on labels, breaks ownership (F3), and makes “33 duplicates” an undefined metric.

7. **Generalization rewarded by excessive permissiveness (S)**  
   TBR generalization 0.907 is consistent with many enabled unused edges ( skips, free order)—the same edges that admit B3/B4.

8. **Silent-transition effects**  
    edges improve fitness under alignment when activities are absent and expand the language. Not documented how many -moves were costed in reported alignments.

9. **Alignment-cost sensitivity**  
   Standard unit costs vs. custom costs change whether synchronous moves on optional paths are preferred. Neither team published costs—comparability of 0.992 vs 0.973 is soft.

10. **Runtime confounding by model size**  
    0.440 s vs 0.018 s mostly says “XOR of long paths is big,” not “more correct.”

11. **Stakeholder-score methodology**  
    Informal 1–5 without tasks, probes, or inter-rater protocol; likely penalizes any diagram with many branches regardless of compliance quality.

# 7. Third Alternative

## Design choice

A **block-structured POWL backbone** (mandatory skeleton, clear partial orders) **plus a small DECLARE-style / post-hoc policy layer** for constraints the log cannot support with attributes (`external`  Supplier; affected-check after Correct). This is safer than S (mandates not -optional), more general than C (no path XOR explosion), and honest about missing data.

### 1. Compact formal description

**Procedural backbone (`model_third`):**

```
Register Dossier
  ;
  (Credit Check || Technical Review)          // both required, either order
  ;
  LOOP
    body:  OptionalSupplier || OptionalExpedite
           // each optional; either order; both after checks
    // exit loop without correction, OR:
    redo:  Correct Dossier
           ;
           XOR(
             Credit Check,
             Technical Review,
             (Credit Check || Technical Review)
           )                                   // at least one recheck
  ;
  XOR(Manager Approval, Auto Approval)
  ;
  Release Order
```

Equivalently: corrections are zero or more; after each Correct, 1 mandatory check repeats; Expedite/Supplier sit in a post-check partial order and are re-allowed after rechecks so urgent external paths remain possible; exactly one approval; Release last.

**Declarative / post-hoc layer (enforcement mode):**

- `Expedite Review` must not precede the *latest completing pair-start* of mandatory checks in the same “cycle” (implemented as: no Expedite before at least one CC and one TR have occurred since Start/last Correct).
- If resource/data attribute `sourcing = external` is present, require 1 Supplier Review before approval; if `internal`, forbid Supplier (when attribute exists).
- If `affected_check` present after Correct, require exactly those rechecks; if absent, accept any non-empty recheck set but **flag** for audit when only one check repeats.
- Flag (not necessarily hard-fail) second Credit/Technical without intervening Correct (P7).
- Exactly-one approval already in backbone; hard-fail duplicate approvals if log shows them.

### 2–3. PM4Py code and declarative dictionaries

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# --- Activities (unique nodes; labels may repeat only for recheck stages) ---
register = Transition(label="Register Dossier")
credit = Transition(label="Credit Check")
technical = Transition(label="Technical Review")
supplier = Transition(label="Supplier Review")
expedite = Transition(label="Expedite Review")
correct = Transition(label="Correct Dossier")
manager = Transition(label="Manager Approval")
auto = Transition(label="Auto Approval")
release = Transition(label="Release Order")

tau_sup = SilentTransition()
tau_exp = SilentTransition()
tau_sup2 = SilentTransition()
tau_exp2 = SilentTransition()

# Initial mandatory checks: both, either order
checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[credit, technical])
# no edges => concurrent (interleaving), both required

# Optional Supplier / Expedite after checks (either order)
opt_supplier = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[supplier, tau_sup])
opt_expedite = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[expedite, tau_exp])
post_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[opt_supplier, opt_expedite])
# no edge => either order when both present

# Recheck after correction: credit only | technical only | both
re_credit = Transition(label="Credit Check")
re_technical = Transition(label="Technical Review")
re_both = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[re_credit, re_technical])
recheck = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[re_credit, re_technical, re_both])

# After recheck, allow optional supplier/expedite again (same policy window before approval)
supplier_r = Transition(label="Supplier Review")
expedite_r = Transition(label="Expedite Review")
opt_supplier_r = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[supplier_r, tau_sup2])
opt_expedite_r = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[expedite_r, tau_exp2])
post_recheck = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[opt_supplier_r, opt_expedite_r])

correct_cycle = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[correct, recheck, post_recheck])
correct_cycle.order.add_edge(correct, recheck)
correct_cycle.order.add_edge(recheck, post_recheck)

# LOOP(body=post_checks, redo=correct_cycle):
#   execute post_checks; optionally Correctrecheckpost again; then exit
loop_po = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[post_checks, correct_cycle])

approval = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[manager, auto])

model_third = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[register, checks, loop_po, approval, release]
)
model_third.order.add_edge(register, checks)
model_third.order.add_edge(checks, loop_po)
model_third.order.add_edge(loop_po, approval)
model_third.order.add_edge(approval, release)

# Optional: convert for alignment-based metrics
# net_third, im_third, fm_third = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_third)

# --- Declarative / attribute-aware post-hoc checks (enforcement companion) ---
declare_constraints = {
    "existence_after_register": ["Credit Check", "Technical Review"],
    "exactly_one_of": [["Manager Approval", "Auto Approval"]],
    "precedence": [
        # Expedite only after both checks have occurred at least once
        ("Credit Check", "Expedite Review"),
        ("Technical Review", "Expedite Review"),
        ("Register Dossier", "Credit Check"),
        ("Register Dossier", "Technical Review"),
        # Approval after checks
        ("Credit Check", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Technical Review", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Credit Check", "Auto Approval"),
        ("Technical Review", "Auto Approval"),
        # Release last among named endpoints
        ("Manager Approval", "Release Order"),
        ("Auto Approval", "Release Order"),
    ],
    "succession_flags": {
        # P7: repeat check without Correct  suspicious
        "repeat_without_correct_suspicious": ["Credit Check", "Technical Review"],
    },
    "attribute_guards": {
        # Applied only when attributes exist on the case
        "external_requires_supplier": {
            "when": {"sourcing": "external"},
            "require_activity": "Supplier Review",
        },
        "internal_forbids_supplier": {
            "when": {"sourcing": "internal"},
            "forbid_activity": "Supplier Review",
        },
        "affected_check_recheck": {
            "when_activity": "Correct Dossier",
            "attribute": "affected_check",  # e.g. {"Credit Check"}, {"Technical Review"}, or both
            "mode": "enforce_if_present_else_flag_partial_recheck",
        },
    },
    "response": [
        # After Correct Dossier, at least one of CC/TR before Approval (backbone already ensures)
        ("Correct Dossier", ["Credit Check", "Technical Review"]),
    ],
}

def post_hoc_policy_check(trace, case_attrs=None, constraints=declare_constraints):
    """
    Returns dict: hard_fail (bool), flags (list), reasons (list).
    Backbone fitness is necessary but not sufficient for enforcement mode.
    """
    case_attrs = case_attrs or {}
    events = [e["concept:name"] for e in trace]
    hard_fail, flags, reasons = False, [], []

    # Mandatory checks exist
    if "Credit Check" not in events or "Technical Review" not in events:
        hard_fail = True
        reasons.append("missing_mandatory_check")

    # Exactly one approval
    n_app = sum(1 for a in events if a in ("Manager Approval", "Auto Approval"))
    if n_app != 1:
        hard_fail = True
        reasons.append("approval_cardinality")

    # Expedite not before first occurrence of both checks
    if "Expedite Review" in events:
        i_exp = events.index("Expedite Review")
        prefix = events[:i_exp]
        if not ("Credit Check" in prefix and "Technical Review" in prefix):
            hard_fail = True
            reasons.append("expedite_before_checks")

    # P7 suspicious repeats without correct
    def flagged_repeats(act):
        idxs = [i for i, a in enumerate(events) if a == act]
        for a, b in zip(idxs, idxs[1:]):
            if "Correct Dossier" not in events[a:b]:
                flags.append(f"suspicious_repeat:{act}")

    flagged_repeats("Credit Check")
    flagged_repeats("Technical Review")

    # Attribute guards
    src = case_attrs.get("sourcing")
    if src == "external" and "Supplier Review" not in events:
        hard_fail = True
        reasons.append("external_missing_supplier")
    if src == "internal" and "Supplier Review" in events:
        hard_fail = True
        reasons.append("internal_has_supplier")

    # affected_check: enforce when present
    # (simplified scan: each Correct paired with following rechecks before next Correct/Approval)
    # Full implementation omitted for brevity — see Validation Experiments.

    return {"hard_fail": hard_fail, "flags": flags, "reasons": reasons}
```

### 4. Explicit semantics

| Topic | Backbone semantics | Enforcement add-on |
|---|---|---|
| Mandatory checks | Both CC and TR occur after Register, concurrent, before loop exit toward approval | Hard-fail if either missing |
| Optional Supplier | XOR(Supplier, ) in post-check partial order; may sit either side of Expedite | If `sourcing` known: require/forbid; if unknown: allow both (do **not** silent-optionalize mandates) |
| Expedite ordering | Only enabled in nodes after initial checks (and after rechecks); XOR with  | DECLARE precedence + post-hoc: Expedite index after both checks |
| Exactly-one approval | XOR(Manager, Auto) once before Release | Cardinality check hard-fail |
| Correction / recheck | LOOP redo = Correct then XOR(CC, TR, both); partial recheck allowed | If `affected_check` present, match it; else accept non-empty recheck and flag single-sided recheck for audit (F6 honesty) |
| P7 suspicious | Not hard-coded as silent skip | Flag repeats without Correct |

### 5. Limitations from missing attributes

- Without `sourcing`, Supplier remains **optional in the net**, so internal cases with Supplier and external cases without are still control-flow-fitting; only the post-hoc layer can enforce when attributes arrive.
- Without `affected_check`, B6-class traces are **accepted by the net** with a **governance flag**, not silently declared fully compliant—avoiding S’s under-constraint and C’s false certainty.
- Duplicate labels appear only for staged rechecks (bounded), not 33-path explosion; still a mild IT caveat—prefer one logical activity id with stage attributes in a production BPMN mapping.
- LOOP allows multiple corrections; rare pathological long loops need SLA guards outside the model.

# 9. Validation Experiments

## Program design

1. **Reproducible splits**  
   Fixed seeds `{23, 47, 101}`; 80/20 case-level splits stratified by: has_Supplier, has_Expedite, has_Correct, approval type, check order (CCTR vs TRCC). Publish case-id lists and preprocessing (duplicate consecutive events, lifecycle consolidation).

2. **Attribute-stratified holdout**  
   Even if attributes were absent in discovery, join from source systems for evaluation: external/internal, affected_check. Report fitness **per stratum**.

3. **Curated negative suite** (minimum)  
   - Skip checks (B3 and variants)  
   - Expedite before checks (B4)  
   - Two approvals  
   - Release not last  
   - Approval before checks  
   - Supplier on known-internal  
   - Missing Supplier on known-external  
   - B8-class repeats  
   Precision := rejection rate on negatives  escaping-edges on positives (report both).

4. **Perturbations**  
   - Drop each non-endpoint event with p  {0.05, 0.1}  
   - Duplicate random event  
   - Swap adjacent CC/TR  
   Measure fitness delta (S will look robust; require mandate preservation checks).

5. **Unseen-combination tests**  
   Cross product: check order × {, Sup} × {, Exp} × {MA, AA} × {0,1 Correct with each recheck mode}, filter by policy oracle; compare accept/reject to oracle.

6. **Alignment-cost sensitivity**  
   Grid: standard unit cost vs. higher cost on log moves for mandatory labels; report rank stability of models.

7. **Soundness / reachability**  
   `check_soundness` on Petri-net conversion; dead transition report; boundedness.

8. **Metrics disaggregated**  
   Fitness (alignments), precision (alignments on positives **and** negative suite), generalization TBR, simplicity arc-degree—**never averaged without pre-registered weights**.

9. **Stakeholder comprehension protocol**  
   n12 stakeholders; tasks: (i) mark B1–B8 allow/deny, (ii) edit “add mandatory insurance check,” (iii) time-to-answer; score accuracy not likability alone.

10. **Rejection rule despite good aggregates**  
    Reject for **enforcement** if any of: accepts B3 or B4; soundness fail; negative-suite recall &lt; 0.99 on critical forbids; duplicate-label count &gt; threshold without mapping table; holdout stratum (external+auto+expedite) fitness &lt; 0.9 **and** those traces are oracle-valid; no published seed/split.

# 11. Dissenting View

The recommendation overweights theoretical policy elegance and underweights operational reality. Model C, whatever its aesthetic crimes, is the only artifact that currently **refuses** the worst forbids (B3, B4) without depending on an untested secondary Python policy layer. Governance boards have seen “backbone plus DECLARE” projects stall for months while attributes never land; meanwhile auditors will ask which model blocked the skipped credit check **yesterday**. Model S’s fitness and comprehension scores are not vanity—they show the log is noisy and that business users already think in optional parallel steps; forcing both checks in the net may flood operations with false noncompliance when Technical Review is logged late or under another name. A stricter third model could become a political failure if holdout fitness drops toward C’s 0.744 after mandates are restored. Prefer shipping C **plus** a short blacklist of anti-patterns, and invest in data attributes before a clever POWL LOOP. The third model’s recheck XOR and dual-stage labels reintroduce the duplicate-label problem it claims to solve, only more quietly.

**Evidence that would change the decision.** (1) A production-ready attribute feed for `sourcing` and `affected_check` with &gt;95% case coverage, plus six months of post-hoc rules with measured false-positive rates. (2) Negative-suite and unseen-combo results showing `model_third` oracle agreement  C on forbids and  S on valid holdout strata. (3) If experiments show real logs almost never contain legal combinations outside C’s branches, C’s brittleness claim weakens—then a maintained variant family might be acceptable for enforcement. (4) If alignment under realistic missing-event models restores S’s precision once  is restricted to logging-noise only, revisit a noise-aware S variant—not the submitted S.
