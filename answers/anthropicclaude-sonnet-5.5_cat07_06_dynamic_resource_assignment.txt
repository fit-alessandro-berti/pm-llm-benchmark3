# 1. Objectives and constraints

## 1.1 Objectives

| # | Objective | Operational meaning |
|---|---|---|
| O1 | Throughput and flow | Short time-to-start and low queueing. Count only finished work, not work that later comes back as rework. |
| O2 | SLA risk | Minimise the projected probability of missing `sla_deadline`. Urgent cases weigh far more than low-priority ones. |
| O3 | Quality | Low rework and appeal rates after assignment. |
| O4 | Continuity | Few owner changes, especially on high-complexity cases with high `handover_cost_band`. |
| O5 | Fairness and wellbeing | Balanced load relative to capacity, protection of high-overtime-risk people, and rotation of "hard" cases over time. |
| O6 | Skill development | Documented training priorities are served through supervised, low-risk work. |
| O7 | Resilience | No single point of failure, a named backup per skill, and specialists reserved for work that needs them. |

These objectives conflict, so the policy does not claim a single optimum:

- Specialist routing helps O3 but hurts O1, O5 and O7.
- First-available routing helps average wait but hurts O3 and O4.
- Continuity can conflict with both skill fit and fairness.

## 1.2 Constraints, in three tiers

**Tier A – Safety and compliance (never traded for score; only a named human can grant an exception)**

| ID | Constraint |
|---|---|
| A1 | Skill 0 means not allowed. A skill-0 person may only *observe* (no system action), never perform or finalize. |
| A2 | Urgent or high-priority cases go to skill 2. Skill 1 is allowed only under explicit supervision by a skill-3 person who reviews or signs off. |
| A3 | Security Review requires skill 3. Skill 1–2 is allowed only under explicit supervision by R7_Gita or R8_Hugo. |
| A4 | Final Approval is finalized by skill 2–3. Skill 1 may only prepare. |
| A5 | Quality Check may be batched only for normal/low priority with SLA remaining >24h. |
| A6 | No resource gets more than 4 new assignments per decision batch. |
| A7 | Capacity: committed estimated effort must not exceed hours remaining today (with a 15% buffer for non-urgent work). |

**Tier B – Conditional rules (default-on, can be overridden only with a logged exception code and a reason)**

| ID | Rule |
|---|---|
| B1 | High-overtime-risk resources receive new work only if SLA risk is urgent and no safer alternative exists. Training work for them may be scheduled only into protected slack. |
| B2 | For high-complexity cases (assumed 0.70) with high handover cost, the current owner is kept unless the owner has skill 0–1 and no supervision is feasible. |
| B3 | At least 20% of normal/low-priority assignments support a documented training priority, if SLA risk stays acceptable. |
| B4 | Reassignment guard, derived from the evidence that >2 reassignments give 1.8× rework and 1.4× appeals. A case with 2 reassignments changes owner only with team-lead approval. A case with 3 is locked to its owner unless the owner is absent or unsafe. |

**Tier C – Preference trade-offs (weighted and explainable)**

These are SLA fit, skill fit beyond the minimum, continuity beyond the locks, workload balance, training value, and bottleneck avoidance. Weights shift with urgency (Section 2).

## 1.3 Data issues the policy makes explicit

1. The training priorities of **R4 David and R8 Hugo (Approval)** and **R5 Eva and R10 Jonas (Security)** point at skills where their level is **0 (not allowed)**.
   - The policy cannot assign them this work.
   - It offers *observer/shadow exposure* and recommends a formal governance step to enrol them at level 1.
   - Exposure does not count toward the 20% training share until then.
2. "Supervision" must be defined and logged: supervisor name, sign-off, and reviewed-before-close.
3. "High priority" means `urgent` or `high`. "High complexity" is assumed to be 0.70 and should be calibrated from data.

# 2. Assignment policy

## 2.1 Parameters

**Urgency.** Let `u` be the case urgency in [0,1].

- Priority score: urgent = 1.0, high = 0.7, normal = 0.4, low = 0.2.
- Time score: `clip(1  SLA_remaining/48h, 0, 1)`.
- `u = max(priority score, time score)`.

**Weights** (they sum to 1 at both ends and are interpolated linearly in `u`):

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| SLA fit | 0.15 + 0.20u |
| Skill fit | 0.20 + 0.05u |
| Continuity | 0.15 + 0.05u |
| Workload/overtime fairness | 0.20  0.10u |
| Training | 0.20  0.20u |
| Resilience | 0.10 |

**Estimated effort** is a planning model to be replaced by mined service times (Section 7): `0.5h + 2h × complexity`, × 0.85 for skill 3, × 1.0 for skill 2, × 1.3 for skill 1.

**Other parameters:**
- `need_level` is 3 if complexity 0.75 or the activity is Security. It is 2 if complexity 0.40, otherwise 1.
- Handover band factor `hb`: low 0.25, medium 0.50, high 0.90.
- Slack ratio: `(SLA_rem  queue_delay  service_est  handover_delay) / SLA_rem`. Amber is <0.25 and red is <0.

## 2.2 Pseudocode

```text
function assign_batch(Q, R, S, state):
    # ---- Step 0: case features
    for c in Q:
        c.u         = urgency(c)                          # priority + time pressure
        c.need      = need_level(c)
        c.locked    = (c.reassign >= 3)                                      # B4
                      or (c.complexity >= 0.70 and c.handover == "high"      # B2
                          and c.owner != none)

    # ---- Step 1: eligibility filtering (hard filter, produces modes)
    for c in Q:
        cand[c] = []
        for r in R:
            if not r.present: continue
            lvl = S[r][c.skill]
            if lvl == 0:                                   # A1
                continue                                   # (observer option only, see Step 5)
            if hours_left(r) - committed(r) < effort(c, r) * 1.15 and c.u < 1: continue   # A7
            if assigned_in_batch(r) >= 4: continue                                         # A6
            mode = "SOLO"
            if c.skill == "Security" and lvl < 3:                                          # A3
                mode = "SUPERVISED" if supervisor in {R7_Gita, R8_Hugo} has headroom else skip
            if c.priority in {urgent, high} and lvl < 2:                                   # A2
                mode = "SUPERVISED" if skill3_supervisor available and c.slack_ratio > 0.25 else skip
            if c.skill == "Approval" and lvl < 2: continue  # may prepare only (A4)
            if c.skill == "Quality" and batched(c) and not (c.priority in {normal, low}
                                                            and c.sla_remaining > 24h): unbatch(c)   # A5
            if r.overtime == "high" and not (c.u >= 0.9 and no_safer_alternative(c)):      # B1
                mark r as OT_EXCEPTION_NEEDED (keep as candidate of last resort;
                allow only training-in-slack items, scheduled next shift)
            if c.locked and r != c.owner and not owner_unsafe(c):
                continue                                   # keep owner (B2, B4); may add
                                                           # a peer-review/pair, not a transfer
            cand[c].append((r, mode))

    # ---- Step 2: order cases, most-constrained first
    order = sort(Q, key = (len(cand[c]) ascending, slack_ratio ascending, u descending))

    # ---- Step 3: score and select
    for c in order:
        best = none
        for (r, mode) in cand[c]:
            SLA   = clip(slack_ratio(c, r) / 0.5, 0, 1)
            SKILL = 1.0 if S[r][c.skill] >= c.need else 1 - 0.4*(c.need - S[r][c.skill])
            if mode == "SUPERVISED": SKILL *= 0.7
            hcost = hb[c.handover] * (0.6 + c.complexity) * (1 + 0.5*max(0, c.reassign - 1))
            CONT  = 1.0 if r == c.owner else 1 - min(1, hcost)
            load  = (committed(r) + effort(c, r)) / hours_left(r)
            FAIR  = 1 - load - OT_pen[r.overtime]          # low 0, medium 0.10, high 0.40
                      - 0.15*assigned_in_batch(r)/4
                      - 0.10*rolling_4wk_hard_case_share(r) # rotate "hard" work
            FAIR  = clip(FAIR, 0, 1)
            TRAIN = 1.0 if (training_priority(r) == c.skill and S[r][c.skill] >= 1
                            and c.priority in {normal, low} and c.complexity <= 0.5
                            and c.slack_ratio >= 0.5 and reviewer_available(c))
                    else 0.5 if (training_priority(r) == c.skill and S[r][c.skill] == 0
                                 and observer_ok(c))      # exposure only, not counted
                    else 0
            RES   = 1 - 0.3*scarce_waste(c, r)    # scarce skill-3 used on work a competent person can do
                      - 0.2*urgent_stack(r)       # already holds >=2 urgent cases
                      + 0.2*bench_building(c, r)  # widens thin backup bench, low risk only
            score = wS*SLA + wK*SKILL + wC*CONT + wF*FAIR + wT*TRAIN + wR*RES
            score -= exception_penalty(mode, OT_EXCEPTION, ...)
            best = argmax(best, score) using tie-break below
        if best is none: escalate_to_duty_lead(c, top3_infeasible_options); continue
        commit(c, best)
        add named_backup(c)          # skill >=2 person other than assignee (A7/resilience)

    # ---- Step 4: repair passes
    training_share = count(normal/low cases with counted training) / count(normal/low cases)
    if training_share < 0.20:
        for each normal/low case with slack_ratio >= 0.5 and a reviewer:
            try swapping to trainee with training priority; accept if no A-tier breach
            and SLA amber count does not increase
    for r with assigned_in_batch > 4 or load > 0.85: move lowest-value item to next best candidate
    for urgent cases with slack_ratio < 0.25: require start-now flag, checkpoint at 50% SLA and named backup
    for urgent cases with slack_ratio < 0: re-route (even at continuity cost if handover is low)

    # ---- Step 5: observer/shadow exposure for skill-0 training priorities
    # Offer only on non-urgent cases, with a performer who is skill 3 and has headroom.
    # Observation is logged separately and never counted as work or training share.

    # ---- Step 6: explanation and logging
    for each assignment write assignment_reason as short codes plus plain text:
        "POL-v1;CONT=owner;SKILL=3;SUP=-;TRN=0;EXC=-" + "You know this case and are our strongest match."
```

## 2.3 Tie-breaking and exceptions

**Tie-break order** (applied when scores differ by <0.03):
1. Lower projected SLA risk.
2. Higher continuity.
3. Lower rolling 4-week overtime exposure.
4. Fewer assignments in the batch.
5. Longest time since this person last received this type of work (rotation).
6. Lowest resource ID, flagged for human review. There is no random choice, so the result is reproducible and auditable.

**Exception codes** (logged, reviewed weekly):

| Code | Exception |
|---|---|
| X1 | High-overtime resource used. |
| X2 | Supervised high-priority assignment. |
| X3 | No feasible candidate, escalated to the duty lead. |
| X4 | Absence or unavailability re-run. Locks persist, and a pair or peer-review replaces a transfer where possible. |
| X5 | Override of the reassignment lock (team-lead approval). |
| X6 | Observer-only exposure for a skill-0 training priority. |

**Re-run triggers:**
- A new urgent case arrives.
- Any resource is absent.
- A case turns amber or red.
- A high-overtime resource has <2.5h left.

**Human in the loop:** the duty lead can override any assignment, and the override and its reason are logged. Overrides feed the audit and weight review.

## 4.1 Where the baselines win, and sensitivity

- **First-available** wins on simplicity and average waiting time when staff are fully interchangeable. That does not hold here.
- **Specialist-only** wins on immediate accuracy for complex cases. It would be competitive if specialist capacity were plentiful and handovers were cheap. The evidence shows neither.
- **Sensitivity of the proposed policy:**
  - If SLA weight is raised, C014 and C013 would shift toward Celine or Amal, and more training items would be postponed.
  - If fairness and training weight rise, C010 would return to Bruno despite his overtime risk, and C006 would stay with Iris. This shows why weights are a governance choice.
  - If mined data show handover delay is much smaller than 2.7h, C016 and C001 could go to specialists outright.
  - If mined service times show trainees are far slower than ×1.3, C009 should move to a low-stakes alternative.
- This replay is a single queue snapshot. The real comparison should come from A/B or shadow-mode runs over weeks.

# 5. Explanation to workers

**How we decided who gets what today**

We try to do five things at once: serve customers on time, give you work you can do well, avoid passing cases around, share the load fairly, and help people grow. Sometimes these pull in different directions, so there is no perfect answer. Here is how we balanced them today.

**What we looked at**
- **Who already knows the case.** If you already own it, you usually keep it. Every extra handover costs time and risk for the customer and for you.
- **Who can do it well and safely.** Some work (security, final approval) may only be done by certain people. For other work we look at complexity and your skill level.
- **How urgent it is.** Urgent cases come first, and we place them where the chance of a delay is lowest.
- **How much you already have.** We look at open cases, hours left today, and overtime risk. If you are already stretched, we try to take work off your plate and avoid adding more.
- **Learning goals.** When there is no time pressure, we try to offer work that matches the development areas you and your lead agreed. A skilled colleague supports you and reviews before the case closes.
- **Backups.** We try not to rely on just one person for a critical skill.

**What this means for you**
- **If you keep a case:** it is because you know it and a change would add risk. It is not a judgement about your workload or performance.
- **If a case moved away from you:** this is to protect your time, not a verdict. For example, Bruno's approval was moved because he is close to overtime.
- **If you have a training item:** it is low-risk, has time slack, and a named person is there to help. It is not a test. For example, Amal's diagnosis case has Celine as coach, and Bruno's quality check is scheduled next shift with Jonas.
- **If you are "shadowing":** some training goals (Security for Eva and Jonas, Approval for David and Hugo) are not yet formally open to you. For now you can watch and ask questions, and we are asking for formal enrolment so you can move to hands-on work.
- **If you feel overloaded or uncomfortable with an assignment:** tell the duty lead. You can ask for a swap or a helper. Every override is recorded, with no blame, and used to improve the rules.

**What we do not do**
- We do not use these assignments to rank individuals.
- We do not give you urgent or sensitive work without a qualified person involved.
- We do not treat overtime as normal.

**Transparency**
Every assignment carries a short reason, for example "you own it, strong skill fit, low handover cost" or "training with Celine as coach". We will review the rules with team representatives every four weeks, looking at where the rules felt unfair, and adjust.

# 6. Process-mining audit indicators

Initial thresholds are placeholders to be calibrated on at least 4–8 weeks of data. Indicators are read as a set, because optimising any one (for example low handover) can be gamed or harmful alone.

| Indicator | Definition (log fields) | Initial alert | Interpretation notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Handover frequency** | Events where `org:resource`  previous resource in the same case. Report per case, per team pair, and split by `handover_cost_band` and complexity. | Rate >0.4 per case, or any high-band/high-complexity handover without an exception code | Also inspect the handover-of-work network for ping-pong and hubs. |
| **Reassignment count** | `max(reassignment_count)` per case. Share of cases with >2, and cases with 2 that changed owner without X5. | >5% of cases >2, or any X5-free override | Compare rework and appeal for >2 vs 2 to recheck the 1.8× and 1.4× evidence. |
| **Skill mismatch** | Join `org:resource` × `required_skill` to the skill matrix. Hard mismatch = skill 0, or skill 1 on urgent/high without supervision, or Security <3 without `SUP=`, or Approval <2. Soft mismatch = skill below `need_level`. | Hard mismatch must be 0. Soft mismatch >10% | Mismatch with exception code is tracked separately. |
| **Workload distribution** | Assignments and estimated effort per resource per day and week, relative to hours remaining. Gini coefficient, max/median ratio, share above 85% load. `workload_snapshot` for actual load. | Gini >0.35, any resource >85% load on >2 days per week | Adjust for part-time and overtime risk. Watch team-level differences. |
| **Overtime risk exposure** | Share of assignments (and effort) going to `overtime risk=high` resources, split urgent vs non-urgent. | Non-urgent high-overtime share >5%. Any high-overtime resource with >1 new item without X1 | Include training items scheduled into slack. |
| **SLA misses** | `case end > sla_deadline`. Also near-miss (<10% slack at finish) and amber/red flags at assignment. | Urgent misses >0 need review. Ambermiss conversion rate | Split by priority, assigned skill, and whether an exception occurred. |
| **Rework/appeal after assignment** | Occurrence of `Rework Correction` (after Quality Check) or an appeal event per case, linked to the assigned resource, skill mismatch and handovers. | Rework rate >baseline + 20% | Use logistic regression with handovers, skill gap, load and complexity, so case difficulty is not blamed on individuals. |
| **Training assignment share** | Normal/low assignments with `TRN=1` or assigned skill = documented training priority, divided by all normal/low assignments. Separate observer/exposure. | <20% for two consecutive weeks, or training items with no review record | Also audit the supervisor sign-off and whether trainee cases cause SLA or rework problems. |
| **Specialist bottleneck indicators** | For Security and Approval (and Diagnosis and Rework): waiting time before start by skill, specialist utilisation (effort ÷ hours), queue length per specialist, p90 wait, share of work done by skill 3, number of skill 2 people with headroom. | Utilisation >85%, p90 wait >2× median, coverage 2 people | Include absence scenarios (what happens if the top specialist is missing). |
| **Deviations from assignment policy** | Policy replay on each batch. Assignments differing from the recommendation, hard-rule breaches without exception code, missing `assignment_reason`, overrides by reason. | Unexplained deviations >5%, any Tier A breach | Overrides are not automatically bad. Review whether they reveal a missing rule. |
| **Continuity ratio** | Share of cases where the owner at `case` start equals the performer of major steps. | Falling trend | Interpret with handover cost and SLA. |
| **Supervision evidence** | Supervised assignments with named supervisor and review event before close. | 100% required | No evidence = treat as unsupervised. |
| **Batch compliance** | `Quality Check` batches where members had 24h SLA at release. | 0 violations | |
| **Wellbeing/fairness counter-check** | Share of hard/urgent cases per person over 4 weeks, overtime hours, and worker feedback/override requests. | Persistent concentration on one person | Protects against "specialist burn-out". |

**Reading the indicators together:**
- Low handovers with rising SLA misses may mean continuity is over-weighted.
- Good skill match with extreme specialist utilisation means the policy is drifting toward specialist-only.
- Good throughput with rising overtime exposure means the fairness weight is being bypassed.

The sketch assumes the columns listed in the task. Check function signatures and attribute names against your installed PM4Py version. Service and waiting times need start timestamps, or at least a consistent approximation.

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

ACT2SKILL = {
    "Eligibility Review": "Eligibility", "Technical Diagnosis": "Diagnosis",
    "Customer Negotiation": "Negotiation", "Security Review": "Security",
    "Final Approval": "Approval", "Quality Check": "Quality", "Rework Correction": "Rework",
}
CASE, ACT, TS, RES = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp", "org:resource"

# ---------- 0. Load and format ------------------------------------------------
raw = pd.read_csv("service_log.csv")
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(raw, case_id=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS)
df["sla_deadline"] = pd.to_datetime(df["sla_deadline"], utc=True)

# Reference tables: skills(org:resource, required_skill, skill_level),
# res_attrs(org:resource, training_priority, overtime_risk, team)
skills = pd.read_csv("skill_matrix_long.csv")
res_attrs = pd.read_csv("resource_attributes.csv")

# ---------- 1. Resource networks, roles, similarity ---------------------------
kw = dict(resource_key=RES, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)
sna_handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(df, **kw)   # who passes work to whom
sna_together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(df, **kw)   # who works on the same cases
sna_similar  = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(df, **kw)  # similar activity profiles
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(df, resource_key=RES, activity_key=ACT,
                                            timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)

pm4py.save_vis_sna(sna_handover, "handover.html")   # or pm4py.view_sna(sna_handover)
for role in roles:   # attribute names may differ across PM4Py versions
    print(role.activities, getattr(role, "originator_importance", None))

# Use: (a) hubs or ping-pong pairs in the handover network, (b) observed vs declared skills
# (e.g. residents doing Security although matrix says 0), (c) candidates for backup bench
# from similarity and role overlap, (d) suggested training pairs (high similarity, different level).
handover_edges = pd.Series(sna_handover.connections)   # (src, dst) -> weight

# ---------- 2. Service/waiting times and calibration ---------------------------
# If start timestamps exist (e.g. 'start:timestamp'), pass them; otherwise treat as approximate.
svc_by_act = pm4py.get_service_time(df, aggregation_measure="mean")      # dict: activity -> seconds
df_st = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df)                       # case-level service/waiting columns

d = df.sort_values([CASE, TS]).copy()
d["prev_res"] = d.groupby(CASE)[RES].shift()
d["is_handover"] = d["prev_res"].notna() & (d["prev_res"] != d[RES])
d["wait_h"] = d.groupby(CASE)[TS].diff().dt.total_seconds() / 3600
d["required_skill"] = d["required_skill"].fillna(d[ACT].map(ACT2SKILL))
d = (d.merge(skills, on=[RES, "required_skill"], how="left")
       .merge(res_attrs, on=RES, how="left"))

# Calibrate: handover delay by complexity band (replaces the 2.7h constant)
d["cx_band"] = pd.cut(d["case_complexity"], [0, .4, .7, 1.0], labels=["low", "mid", "high"])
handover_delay = d[d.is_handover].groupby(["cx_band", "handover_cost_band"])["wait_h"].median()

# Calibrate: service time multiplier by skill level (replaces 0.85 / 1.0 / 1.3)
d["service_h"] = d["wait_h"]   # placeholder: use real service time if available
skill_mult = (d.groupby(["required_skill", "skill_level"])["service_h"].median()
                .div(d.groupby("required_skill")["service_h"].median()))

# ---------- 3. Performance DFG and variants on filtered subsets ----------------
urgent = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(df, "case_priority", ["urgent", "high"],
                                             level="case", retain=True)
perf_dfg, start_act, end_act = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(urgent, perf_aggregation_key="median")

slow = pm4py.filter_case_performance(df, min_performance=48*3600, max_performance=10**9)
variants = pm4py.get_variants(df)       # look for Diagnosis loops and repeated Rework variants

# Only Security events, to study specialist queues
sec = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(df, ACT, ["Security Review"], level="event", retain=True)
batches = pm4py.discover_batches(
    pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(df, ACT, ["Quality Check"], level="event", retain=True),
    merge_distance=900, min_batch_size=2)

# ---------- 4. Audit indicators ------------------------------------------------
def gini(x):
    x = np.sort(np.asarray(x, float)); n = len(x)
    return 0.0 if x.sum() == 0 else float((2*np.arange(1, n+1) - n - 1).dot(x) / (n*x.sum()))

def audit(d):
    d = d.copy()
    d["high_prio"]  = d["case_priority"].isin(["urgent", "high"])
    d["supervised"] = d["assignment_reason"].fillna("").str.contains("SUP=")
    d["has_exc"]    = d["assignment_reason"].fillna("").str.contains("EXC=X")
    lvl = d["skill_level"].fillna(0)

    # hard mismatch: skill 0, urgent/high below 2 without supervision, Security <3 unsupervised, Approval <2
    d["hard_mismatch"] = (
        (lvl == 0)
        | (d.high_prio & (lvl < 2) & ~d.supervised)
        | ((d["required_skill"] == "Security") & (lvl < 3) & ~d.supervised)
        | ((d["required_skill"] == "Approval") & (lvl < 2))
    )
    assigned = d[d["assignment_reason"].notna()]          # assignment events

    case = d.groupby(CASE).agg(
        end=(TS, "max"), sla=("sla_deadline", "first"),
        max_reassign=("reassignment_count", "max"),
        n_handover=("is_handover", "sum"),
        rework=(ACT, lambda s: (s == "Rework Correction").any()),
        appeal=(ACT, lambda s: (s == "Appeal").any()),    # if an appeal event exists
        priority=("case_priority", "first"))
    case["sla_miss"] = case["end"] > case["sla"]

    out = {}
    out["handover_per_case"]   = case["n_handover"].mean()
    out["share_reassign_gt2"]  = (case["max_reassign"] > 2).mean()
    out["hard_mismatch_rate"]  = assigned["hard_mismatch"].mean()
    out["hard_mismatch_no_exc"] = (assigned["hard_mismatch"] & ~assigned["has_exc"]).sum()
    per_res_day = assigned.groupby([RES, assigned[TS].dt.date]).size()
    out["workload_gini"] = gini(per_res_day.groupby(level=0).sum())
    out["ot_high_share"] = (assigned["overtime_risk"] == "high").mean()
    out["ot_high_nonurgent"] = ((assigned["overtime_risk"] == "high") & ~assigned.high_prio).mean()
    out["sla_miss_by_prio"] = case.groupby("priority")["sla_miss"].mean()
    out["rework_appeal_by_reassign"] = case.groupby(case["max_reassign"] > 2)[["rework", "appeal"]].mean()
    nl = assigned[assigned["case_priority"].isin(["normal", "low"])]
    out["training_share"] = ((nl["training_priority"] == nl["required_skill"])
                             & (nl["skill_level"].fillna(0) >= 1)).mean()
    out["specialist_wait_p90"] = (d[d["skill_level"] == 3]
                                  .groupby("required_skill")["wait_h"].quantile(.9))
    out["specialist_load_share"] = (assigned.groupby("required_skill")["skill_level"]
                                    .apply(lambda s: (s == 3).mean()))
    return out

# ---------- 5. Policy replay for deviation detection ---------------------------
def hard_ok(row):
    lvl = row["skill_level"] if pd.notna(row["skill_level"]) else 0
    if lvl == 0: return False
    sup = "SUP=" in str(row["assignment_reason"])
    if row["case_priority"] in ("urgent", "high") and lvl < 2 and not sup: return False
    if row["required_skill"] == "Security" and lvl < 3 and not sup: return False
    if row["required_skill"] == "Approval" and lvl < 2: return False
    return True

def deviations(assigned_events, recommended):   # recommended: DataFrame case,activity -> resource
    m = assigned_events.merge(recommended, on=[CASE, ACT], suffixes=("", "_rec"))
    m["deviated"] = m[RES] != m[RES + "_rec"]
    m["unexplained"] = m["deviated"] & ~m["assignment_reason"].fillna("").str.contains("OVR=")
    m["hard_breach"] = ~m.apply(hard_ok, axis=1) & ~m["assignment_reason"].fillna("").str.contains("EXC=")
    return m[["deviated", "unexplained", "hard_breach"]].mean()

# ---------- 6. Learn or adjust weights with governance (not automatically) -----
# Fit interpretable outcome models, and show the result to worker representatives before changing weights.
from sklearn.linear_model import LogisticRegression
feat = d.assign(skill_gap=lambda x: (2 - x["skill_level"].fillna(0)).clip(lower=0),
                handover=lambda x: x["is_handover"].astype(int))
X = feat.groupby(CASE)[["skill_gap", "handover", "reassignment_count", "case_complexity"]].agg("max")
y = d.groupby(CASE)[ACT].apply(lambda s: (s == "Rework Correction").any()).astype(int)
model = LogisticRegression(max_iter=1000).fit(X.fillna(0), y.loc[X.index])
print(dict(zip(X.columns, model.coef_[0])))   # sign and size suggest which penalties to strengthen
```

**How the pieces are used:**
- **Networks and roles (`discover_handover_of_work_network`, `discover_working_together_network`, `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity`, `discover_organizational_roles`, `view_sna` / `save_vis_sna`).**
  - They reveal observed collaboration, compare observed roles with the declared skill matrix, and suggest backup candidates and coaching pairs.
  - They also show ping-pong handovers and hubs such as Celine.
- **Service/waiting times (`get_service_time`, `insert_case_service_waiting_time`).** They calibrate the effort model, the trainee slowdown, specialist queues, and the handover delay constant.
- **Performance DFG and variants (`discover_performance_dfg`, `get_variants`).** They locate slow transitions (for example Diagnosis loops and Rework repetition) on urgent subsets and show whether the policy shortens them.
- **Filtering (`filter_event_attribute_values`, `filter_case_performance`).** They slice by `case_priority`, `team` or `required_skill` and by slow cases, and compare before/after the policy launch.
- **Batches (`discover_batches`).** They check that Quality Check batching occurs only on eligible cases.

**Operating caveats:**
- Run the policy in shadow mode first (recommend only) and compare to human decisions before it drives assignments.
- Recheck weights after each review cycle with the workers' representatives.
- Do not use individual-level indicators for performance management.
