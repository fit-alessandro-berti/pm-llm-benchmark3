# 2. Reconstructed dispatch decisions

## Reconstruction conventions and uncertainty bounds

The tables use the following explicit assumptions for comparison purposes.

1. **Eligibility assumption.** A job scanned at or before a decision time is treated as plausibly ready unless a recorded skill, availability, appointment, or active-service constraint excludes it. This is an analytical assumption, not an observation. Hidden repair prerequisites, customer-contact holds, or dispatch commitments could invalidate it.

2. **Scan-delay bound.** A scan at time \(t\) may represent physical arrival in the interval \([t-2,t]\). Jobs marked `?` could therefore have been present just before the displayed scan time. A job scanned more than two minutes later is treated as not yet arrived.

3. **FIFO prediction.** FIFO means original queue-entry order, with a resumed job treated as retaining its old position unless otherwise stated. This convention is especially important for QB.

4. **Priority prediction.** Priority 1 is highest. At a fresh decision point, priority chooses the smallest numeric priority; ties are assumed FIFO only for comparison. During service, non-preemptive priority continues the active job, whereas preemptive priority interrupts it for a higher-priority arrival.

5. **SPT prediction.** SPT uses the arrival-time estimate. At a service completion it selects the smallest estimate among waiting jobs. At a mid-service interruption, literal non-preemptive SPT would not interrupt; a shortest-remaining-processing-time variant would compare the new job with an estimated remaining time. For B01, remaining estimates shown below are retrospective approximations, not known dispatch data.

6. **Appointment prediction.** A hard appointment would protect the scheduled time or a specified tolerance window. A soft appointment may merely receive preference once due. The data do not state the tolerance, whether early service is allowed, or whether a resource is reserved before the appointment.

7. **Skills.** Skill-based routing determines feasibility, not necessarily priority among feasible jobs. Thus a “skill” prediction often identifies a set rather than a unique next job.

A useful retrospective observation is that every listed estimate equals the displayed total service time in this sample, including B01’s \(14+17+14=45\) minutes. That does **not** prove estimates were trusted, current remaining-time estimates were available, or future estimates would be accurate; it only means there is no observed estimation error here to explain conflicting choices.

- B07’s appointment is not treated as a hard appointment.

There is an SPT-like initial pattern: among C01–C04, the observed starts are C02 (8), C03 (14), C04 (22), with C01 (36) delayed. C05 complicates that story because it is priority 1 and has the earliest deadline, yet C03 is selected ahead of it at 10:18.

The strongest qualification is the 10:32 C04 decision. C06 had arrived at least seven minutes earlier and has estimate 6, so **dynamic** SPT predicts C06. A defensible alternative is that C04 had already been committed as the next member of an initial SPT batch at 10:18, before C06 arrived. The data do not record such a commitment.

C05’s start at 10:54 is observationally equivalent between a priority-1 override and a deadline/SLA escalation. It is already late, so it does not demonstrate a preventive deadline policy. The C01-over-C07 decision at 11:40 is especially inconsistent with active deadline protection: serving C07 first would complete it at 11:58, before its 12:00 deadline, and would still allow C01 to finish by 12:34, before its 13:00 deadline.

QD shows two different phenomena that should not be conflated:

- The allocation of D05 and D07 is strongly constrained by skills. Those assignments are not evidence that a single global FIFO, SPT, or priority rule controls the pooled queue.
- The start of D06 exactly at 11:30, after D-X had been idle since 11:20, is consistent with appointment reservation. It is not decisive by itself, because D06 is also the oldest eligible job at 11:30 after currently active work is excluded.

The unexplained D-F idle interval from 11:36 remains important. A non-preemptive calendar-feasibility rule explains deferring D05: it needs 30 minutes, but only 24 minutes remain before the meeting. It does not explain why D10, which needs 12 minutes and fits exactly in 11:36–11:48, was not assigned to D-F.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

## QA

**Support for FIFO baseline**

- **A02 at 08:30:** A02 is older than A04, A05, and A06. It is selected despite A04 being priority 1 and estimated at only 7 minutes. This is strong FIFO evidence.
- **A05 at 09:05:** A05 is selected ahead of A08, priority 1, and A07, estimated 9 minutes. This rejects both active priority and SPT at that decision.
- **A07 at 09:32:** A07 precedes A08. This matches FIFO and SPT, but conflicts with priority because A08 is priority 1.

**Challenges and disposition**

- **A03 at 08:20:** A03 bypasses older A02 and priority-1/shorter A04. This is not a FIFO choice. The recorded 08:20 appointment explains it without requiring an unknown exception, provided appointments have real dispatch force.
- **A06 at 09:15/09:20:** A06 begins five minutes after its appointment. The recorded fact that A1 is processing A05 until 09:20 explains the immediate delay, but not why A05 was started at 09:05 if the appointment was hard. This requires either a soft appointment tolerance, inaccurate use of the 15-minute estimate, or an undocumented appointment exception.

## QB

**Support for FIFO/resumption baseline**

- **B01 at 09:31:** B01 resumes ahead of B03, despite B03 being priority 2 and estimate 12. This is directly inconsistent with a strict priority or SPT rule, but consistent with resumed work retaining FIFO precedence.
- **B01 at 10:14:** B01 resumes ahead of B04 and priority-2 B06. Again, this supports an old-position/resume convention.
- **B04 at 10:28 and B06 at 10:36:** Once B01 is complete, the observed B04 then B06 then B07 sequence follows arrival order.

**Challenges and disposition**

- **B02 at 09:16:** B02, priority 1, interrupts B01. The recorded “urgent arrival” reason makes this a known operational override and supports selective emergency preemption. It is not enough to establish general preemptive priority.
- **B05 at 10:00:** B05, priority 1, bypasses paused B01 and older B04. This supports a priority preference at a completion point, but B05 was not allowed to interrupt B03 after arriving at 09:46. It is more consistent with selective non-preemptive priority preference than with strict preemptive priority.
- **B03 at 09:48:** B03 is selected despite B05 already being present and priority 1. “Dispatcher reassignment” explains why B01 stopped, but not why B03 rather than B05 was selected. This requires an undocumented eligibility hold, a prior commitment, a special reassignment rule, or erroneous/incomplete event semantics.
- **B07 appointment:** B07 is not selected at 10:28, when starting B04 guarantees a late appointment, nor at 10:36 when it is already overdue. A hard appointment policy is contradicted.

## QC

**Support for an SPT-like component**

- **C02 at 10:10:** Among the certain initial backlog C01–C04, C02 has the shortest estimate, 8 minutes. C01 is the FIFO choice and C03 is the priority choice, so C02 is specifically SPT-like.
- **C03 at 10:18:** C03 has the shortest estimate among C01, C03, C04, and C05. It is selected despite C05 being priority 1 and having the earliest deadline.
- **C01’s deferment:** C01, the 36-minute oldest initial job, is executed after C02, C03, and C04. This is the opposite of FIFO for the initial backlog and is consistent with a shortest-job ordering.

**Challenges and disposition**

- **C04 at 10:32 over C06:** C06 has estimate 6 and was definitely scanned by 10:25. Dynamic SPT should choose C06. This requires either an advance commitment of C04 made before C06 arrived, a hidden eligibility restriction on C06, or an undocumented hybrid.
- **C05 at 10:54:** C05 is both priority 1 and the earliest-deadline job, so this event cannot distinguish priority override from SLA escalation. Since C05 is already late, it does not prove proactive deadline scheduling.
- **C01 at 11:40 over C07:** C07 is shorter, higher priority, and has the earlier deadline. No recorded resource or skill constraint explains C01’s selection. A plausible but undocumented explanation is aging, preservation of a precommitted initial-batch order, or a hidden hold on C07.
- **C07’s missed deadline:** C07 could have finished by 11:58 if selected at 11:40. Its eventual completion at 12:34 challenges any claim that QC consistently changes to deadline-first scheduling when an SLA is endangered.

## QD

**Support for skill-based routing and constrained-job protection**

- **D02 on D-F:** D02 requires firmware, which only D-F possesses. This is a hard feasibility constraint rather than a policy preference.
- **D05 on D-F:** D05 explicitly requires the only fully qualified resource, D-F. Its eventual assignment is skill-forced.
- **D07 on D-X at 11:48:** D07 requires mechanical+electrical and therefore D-X. D10 is priority 1 and shorter, yet D07 is selected. This is consistent with protecting a highly constrained job or using resource-local FIFO.
- **D10 on D-X at 12:16:** D-F is in its meeting until 12:20, making D-X the only available qualified resource. This is a recorded availability constraint, not evidence for a global priority decision.

**Challenges and disposition**

- **D04 over D08 at 11:25:** D08 is priority 1 and shorter, but D04 is selected. This is consistent with local FIFO on D-M, not with global priority or SPT. It does not challenge skill routing, because both jobs are skill-feasible on D-M.
- **D-F idle at 11:36:** D10 is electrical and fits before the 12:00 meeting, yet D-F remains idle. The meeting explains why D05 cannot be started non-preemptively, but it does not explain D10. This likely requires an undocumented resource state, routing hold, cross-queue work, job-release restriction, or incomplete availability data.
- **D06 at 11:30:** Its exact appointment start supports resource reservation, but D06 was also FIFO among the currently unassigned D-X-eligible jobs. The appointment contribution is therefore plausible rather than uniquely identified.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

| ambiguous episode | explanation 1 and next-job prediction | explanation 2 and next-job prediction | additional observable that would discriminate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **QB, 09:48 B01 interruption** | **Strict preemptive priority:** B05 should start immediately because it is priority 1 and had arrived no later than 09:46. | **FIFO with forced reassignment:** if B01 had to stop for an external reassignment, B03 should be selected because it is the oldest waiting job. | B05’s true queue-entry and release time; a dispatcher override/reassignment code; whether B03 was committed before B05 became eligible; and the rule for retaining B01’s queue position after interruption. |
| **QC, 10:32 C03 completion** | **Dynamic SPT:** C06 should start because estimate 6 is lower than C04’s 22, C01’s 36, and C05’s 40. | **Batch SPT with precommitment:** C04 should start if it was committed as C03’s successor at 10:18, before C06 arrived. | A “decision made” versus “service started” timestamp, planned-next-job identifier, C06’s actual release time, and any event that marks a job as locked to a resource. |
| **QD, 11:20 D-X becomes available** | **Work-conserving queue discipline after skill filtering:** D-X should take a currently eligible job immediately; queue-wide FIFO suggests D03, while a constrained-skill heuristic could choose D07. | **Appointment reservation:** D-X should remain idle and begin D06 at or near 11:30. | D-X’s ready/busy state, appointment-reservation flag, appointment tolerance rule, whether D06 was preassigned to D-X, and whether D-X was allowed to begin a non-appointment job that would overlap 11:30. |

The QC test is especially important. Without dispatch-commit timestamps, a start-order analysis can falsely reject SPT merely because the system makes decisions before service starts. Conversely, if C04 was not precommitted, the same episode is strong evidence against dynamic SPT.

| P2 | **Deadline and SLA state** | deadline, SLA target, projected completion time at decision, “at-risk” flag, escalation timestamp, penalty class, customer commitment class | Tests whether C05 was selected because of priority, deadline escalation, or manual intervention; evaluates whether C07’s miss was avoidable under the operating policy. |
| P2 | **Skill and routing metadata** | atomic skill requirements, composite-skill interpretation, qualified-resource set at decision time, preferred-resource rule, load-balancing target, task transfer restrictions | Confirms that D05 and D07 are truly uniquely routable and reveals whether D10 was intentionally reserved for D-X despite D-F feasibility. |
| P3 | **Data-quality reconciliation fields** | event producer, ingestion delay, correction flag, sequence number, event-time versus processing-time, missing-event indicator | Establishes whether apparent gaps, especially D-F’s idleness and QB’s reassignment sequence, are operational facts or logging artifacts. |

# 7. Final synthesis

QA is the most identifiable queue: repeated choices favor arrival order over both priority and shorter estimates, while A03 provides a clear appointment-based exception. The best description is FIFO with imperfect or soft appointment protection.

QC is the least identifiable queue. Its initial order is SPT-like, but later choices require either advance commitment, aging, SLA escalation, hidden eligibility states, or an undocumented hybrid. Calling it simple dynamic SPT would overstate the evidence.

QB is the queue for which it would be most dangerous to infer **strict preemptive priority** from observations alone. B02’s urgent preemption is real, but the later B01 resumptions, B03-over-B05 decision, and delayed B07 appointment show that priority is selective and event-dependent rather than a universal preemptive rule.

QD is best understood first as a resource-feasibility and skill-routing problem, with appointment reservation and probable local sequencing rules layered on top. Its hard skill constraints are clear, but its within-resource dispatch rule remains partly obscured by D-F’s unexplained idle period.
