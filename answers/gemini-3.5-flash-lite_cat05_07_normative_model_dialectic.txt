## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### Stipulated Facts
- **Normative Base:** The canonical control-flow model M0 and Compliance Standard CS-9 require strict sequential or concurrent enforcement of `Sanctions_Check`, `Quality_Review`, `Manual_Procurement_Approval`, and `Activate_Supplier` prior to `Notify_Requester` and `Close_Case`. M0 does not natively contain loops (`Request_More_Info`), alternative approval branches (`Auto_Approve`), emergency overlays (`FD-7`), or resource-separation rules.
- **Operational Overlays & Pilots:** Steering Committee Decision PD-3 established a temporary, monitored pilot window (2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31) allowing `Auto_Approve` for LOW-risk DE suppliers in approved corporate groups with delayed `Quality_Review`. Flood Emergency Directive FD-7 (2026-07-10 to 2026-07-12) permitted provisional activation prior to quality/manual approval for critical relief suppliers under a €20,000 spend cap.
- **Data & Infrastructure Changes:** On 2026-07-01, supplier notification processing migrated to a CRM service (Source N4), causing a drop in direct event-log capture and introducing mutable text links. UI and procedure updates renamed `Quality_Review` to `QA_Clearance` (Source N3/N5 context).
- **Observed Deviations:** Cases SUP-701 through SUP-715 demonstrate distinct variations from M0, including timing shifts, unauthorized auto-approvals, missing log events, duplicate registrations, data quality artifacts, and unmodeled loops.

### Disputed Propositions
1. **Scope and Applicability:** Whether M0 governs pre-screen rejected leads (SUP-709) and supplier reactivations (SUP-710) as part of the core process or if they sit outside the baseline process boundary.
2. **Authorization Boundaries & Temporal Scope:** Whether operational normalization of `Auto_Approve` past the PD-3 sunset date (SUP-714) or post-FD-7 expiration under subjective emergency claims (SUP-705) constitutes valid operational necessity or unauthorized policy breach.
3. **Control-Flow vs. Data Integrity vs. Resource Rules:** Whether violations of segregation of duties (SUP-712, SUP-715) or logging gaps (SUP-706, SUP-711) invalidate conformance when the core control-flow sequence matches M0.
4. **Model Completeness:** Whether unmodeled business realities (e.g., iterative `Request_More_Info` loops in SUP-707, label renaming in SUP-713) represent noncompliant process execution or structural defects in M0.

### Burden of Proof
- **For Noncompliance:** The accuser must demonstrate that an observed trace or execution violates an active, formally promulgated, and legally binding normative constraint (CS-9, valid statutory/emergency directive) without a verified, contemporaneous exemption valid at the precise timestamp of execution. Informal operational desires, unwritten extensions, or retrospective justifications do not discharge this burden.
- **For Accepting an Exception:** The actor or defender must provide verifiable, immutable administrative evidence (e.g., active policy text, valid pilot eligibility flags, emergency activation timestamp within effective bounds, valid delegation-of-authority records) proving that the deviation was authorized under a legally sound governance override at the moment of execution.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The organization is experiencing systemic procedural drift, governance erosion, and material noncompliance disguised as operational agility. The normative baseline established by Compliance Standard CS-9 and model M0 is not merely an abstract visualization; it represents the audited control architecture required for legal and financial compliance in supplier onboarding.

### Formal Authority and Expiry Violations
Operating outside approved policy windows is a fundamental breach of internal controls. 
- **PD-3 Sunset Violation (SUP-714):** The automation pilot PD-3 expired on 2026-07-31. Cases executed in August 2026 using `Auto_Approve` (such as SUP-714) have no legal validity. Operations cannot "normalize" an expired pilot into permanent policy. 
- **FD-7 Overreach (SUP-705):** Flood Emergency Directive FD-7 expired on 2026-07-12 at 23:59. SUP-705 occurred on 2026-07-13. Operations' subjective assertion that the flood emergency "factually existed" does not override executive governance limits. Furthermore, SUP-703 abused the pilot mechanism for a high-risk foreign supplier with an initial spend of €180,000—violating both risk criteria and spend thresholds.

### High-Risk Exposures and Governance Collapses
- **SUP-708:** Bypassed the sanctions check entirely during initial activation, leaving the company exposed to severe regulatory penalties under international trade law. 
- **SUP-715:** Highlights a breakdown in segregation of duties, where the quality reviewer and manual approver are spouses in the same reporting line without a disclosed conflict of interest.

### Incomplete Evidence and Accountability
The transition to CRM notification logging (Source N4) has created an accountability vacuum, evidenced by the jump in missing notifications (e.g., SUP-706, where notification was unreliably linked via mutable text fields). Stakeholders cannot claim compliance when audit trails are incomplete or altered retroactively. 

Desirable operational outcomes (like gathering extra information in SUP-707) do not retroactively amend binding compliance standards. Unsanctioned process mutations break traceability, invalidate internal audits, and expose the firm to regulatory sanctions.

## 3. Defense Brief

The prosecution's insistence on treating M0 and CS-9 as immutable tablets ignores operational reality, process evolution, and the inherent limitations of static compliance modeling. M0 is obsolete, incomplete, and fundamentally misaligned with the dynamic, high-throughput environment of modern supply chain operations.

### Model Obscurity and Missing Constructs
M0 fails to represent standard, highly desirable business workflows:
- **`Request_More_Info` Loops (SUP-707):** Operations frequently need to request missing compliance certificates. Forcing a binary reject-or-approve choice without a retry loop is counterproductive and forces legitimate suppliers into administrative rejection. Stakeholders universally agree this behavior is desirable.
- **Label Mappings & UI Updates (SUP-713):** Renaming `Quality_Review` to `QA_Clearance` is a surface-level semantic update, not a compliance failure. Penalizing traces due to UI terminology shifts is an artifact of rigid model parsing.
- **Reactivations and Scope Boundaries (SUP-709, SUP-710):** Pre-screen rejected leads (SUP-709) and supplier reactivations (SUP-710) sit outside the narrow scope of initial M0 intake. M0 does not accommodate onboarding lifecycles beyond first-time activation, rendering compliance evaluations of these traces meaningless.

### Valid Overlays and Emergency Adaptability
- **Emergency Responsiveness (SUP-704, SUP-705):** During the flood emergency (FD-7), supply chain continuity was paramount to prevent catastrophic shutdowns. Provisional activation preceding formal reviews saved the operation. Even post-FD-7 (SUP-705), supply chain disruptions persisted; treating operational adaptations as malicious noncompliance ignores the duty of business continuity under force majeure.
- **Pilot Efficacy (SUP-702, SUP-714):** The `Auto_Approve` pilot (PD-3) successfully streamlined low-risk domestic onboarding. SUP-714 reflects minor lag in administrative sign-off for a substantive policy that was universally acknowledged as beneficial and subsequently formalized.

### Data Artifacts vs. Material Noncompliance
- **Logging Artifacts (SUP-706, SUP-711):** CRM migration issues (Source N4) and database migration duplicates (`Register_Supplier` duplicated in SUP-711 with identical transaction IDs) are technical data anomalies, not operational malfeasance. Similarly, resource alias resolution (SUP-712) proves that M0 lacks the semantic capacity to model human actor resolutions correctly. 

The high deviation rate is not evidence of systemic fraud, but of a brittle model failing to keep pace with an adaptive, compliant, and necessary business operation.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

To reconcile operational agility with rigorous compliance, the onboarding architecture must be refactored into a **layered governance framework**:

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------+
|               LAYER 1: ORGANIZATIONAL & RESOURCE CONTROLS       |
|  - Segregation of Duties (Requester != Approver)                |
|  - Conflict of Interest / Nepotism / Spousal Rules              |
|  - Delegation of Authority & Actor Alias Resolution             |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------+
                                  |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------+
|               LAYER 2: POLICY OVERLAY GOVERNANCE                |
|  - Active Pilot Rules (PD-3 parameterized)                      |
|  - Emergency Directives (FD-7 automated scoping & spend caps)   |
|  - Effective Date / Timestamp Validation Engine                 |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------+
                                  |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------+
|               LAYER 3: DATA-AWARE DECLARATIVE & SCRIPT CONSTRAINTS|
|  - Eligibility checks (Risk == 'LOW', Corporate Group == True)  |
|  - Spend limits (< EUR 20k for provisional emergency activation)|
|  - Retrospective review SLA enforcement (<= 2 business days)     |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------+
                                  |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------+
|               LAYER 4: REVISED POWL CONTROL-FLOW MODEL          |
|  - Core Strict Partial Order with Iterative Request_More_Info   |
|  - Exclusive Choice branching (Standard, Auto-Approve, Emergency)|
+-----------------------------------------------------------------+
```

- **POWL Model:** Encodes the structural routing (standard concurrent checks, iterative info requests, auto-approval, emergency provisional paths, and formal rejection/closure).
- **DECLARE / Log-Skeleton Constraints:** Enforces mandatory precedence (e.g., `Sanctions_Check` *responded-existence* before `Activate_Supplier`) and lifecycle closure rules.
- **Pandas / Data Checks:** Enforces numeric thresholds (spend caps), risk categorizations, effective date windows, and immutable CRM notification linkages (`notification_id`).
- **Organizational Controls:** Enforces segregation of duties, actor alias resolution, and conflict-of-interest disclosures via enterprise identity management (IAM) integration.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# Define core process transitions
register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")

# Iterative Request More Info Loop Construction
req_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
skip_info = SilentTransition()
info_loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[skip_info, StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[req_info])])
# Note: POWL models sequence via partial order edges; iterative behavior is approximated via optional/choice blocks or loops.

# Standard Route Checks (Concurrent Sanctions and Quality, with Info Loop)
standard_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality, info_loop])
standard_checks.order.add_edge(sanctions, info_loop)
standard_checks.order.add_edge(quality, info_loop)

manual_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")

# Branch 1: Standard Approved Route
approved_standard = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[standard_checks, manual_approval, activate])
approved_standard.order.add_edge(standard_checks, manual_approval)
approved_standard.order.add_edge(manual_approval, activate)

# Branch 2: Low-Risk Auto-Approve Route (Pilot PD-3 approximation)
auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
approved_auto = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, auto_approve, activate, quality])
approved_auto.order.add_edge(sanctions, auto_approve)
approved_auto.order.add_edge(auto_approve, activate)
approved_auto.order.add_edge(activate, quality) # Quality review deferred up to 2 days

# Branch 3: Emergency Provisional Route (Flood Directive FD-7 approximation)
emergency_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier") # Provisional
emergency_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, emergency_activate, quality, manual_approval])
emergency_branch.order.add_edge(sanctions, emergency_activate)
emergency_branch.order.add_edge(emergency_activate, quality)
emergency_branch.order.add_edge(quality, manual_approval)

# Decision XOR choice across branches or Rejection
reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
decision = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[approved_standard, approved_auto, emergency_branch, reject])

notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

# Root Model M_Revised Construction
M_Revised = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, decision, notify, close])
M_Revised.order.add_edge(register, decision)
M_Revised.order.add_edge(decision, notify)
M_Revised.order.add_edge(notify, close)
```
