3. **Arithmetic-as-proof cascade:** adding `INV-44 bills ORD-B` because 130+100=230, then allocating PAY-1ORD-B and PAY-2ORD-A by amount, double-counts one datum and — if the invoice is actually single-order with fees — creates phantom order-level payments and a false "ORD-B is paid" signal.
4. **Bulk-dispatch over-repair:** adding PK-8 to ev09 while keeping ev10DEL-Y gives PK-8 two dispatch events, producing an impossible `Dispatch Package  Dispatch Package` package trace. The one-dispatch-per-package guard above exists for this.
5. **Inventing O2O qualifiers** (`PK-8 ships ORD-B`, `DEL-Y serves C17`) to "connect" the OTG adds object-type relations with no event co-occurrence behind them; downstream users will read them as business structure.
6. **Completeness-driven repair of DEL-Y or ORD-B's finances** (e.g., always closing PAY-2INV-44 in every reading) can hide genuine unapplied cash or off-log billing that finance would otherwise investigate.
7. **Deleting "dirty" observed relations** (ev11ORD-A, or the item-only ev08 row) removes evidence; repair adds grounded structure, it does not erase inconvenient rows.
