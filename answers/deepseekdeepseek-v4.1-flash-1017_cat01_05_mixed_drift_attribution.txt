## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The raw/mapped fitness gap is primarily evidence about labels and provenance, not a real process-conformance collapse. Raw model fitness falls from about 0.92 to 0.55 in March while mapped fitness recovers to about 0.90; this means the old normative model cannot understand the new label and technical-event vocabulary. It does not imply that activities disappeared or that case handling suddenly became non-compliant. Mapping `Eligibility Screen` to `Validate Eligibility` and removing duplicated `Open Case` events can recover conformance because alignments no longer count expected activities as missing and spurious technical events as deviations. But recovering fitness under a mapping only shows that the mapped abstraction fits the old model; the business process could still change in rework, queueing, priority, or service. Duplicated technical events mainly distort case-level conformance, trace length, variant frequency, and process discovery unless removed; missing resources mainly affect organizational/social analyses and resource-performance segmentation, not necessarily control-flow fitness. The post-May DFG and performance patterns cannot be dismissed as logging drift alone: raw fitness is already poor, but mapped fitness also declines modestly while `Request Info` relation rates, rework variant share, median/P90 cycle time, Supplier Z share, and high-complexity share move together. Logging explains the March level shift and some later measurement noise; it does not explain the May rework jump, the July/August tail, or the October complexity spike. Therefore separate semantic normalization from performance and behavioral monitoring, and retain both raw-schema monitors and mapped-process monitors.

**Normalized DFG-distance analysis.** I use the seven supplied normalized relation rates per 100 cases. For month \(m\), the fragment L1 distance from January is \(\sum_e |r_{m,e}-r_{\mathrm{Jan},e}|\), and fragment MAD is \(L1/7\). This is a transparent partial-distance measure, not a full conformance distance, because omitted edges are absent and rates are rounded. Omitted edges are assumed not compared; therefore the distance is a lower-bound/partial indicator of full DFG change.

**Candidate change points.**

- Logging/provenance drift: (1) 2026-03-01 sudden label/schema migration, raw fitness collapse, duplicated Open Case, missing resources; (2) 2026-03 to 2026-06 gradual resource-provenance deterioration as missing-resource percentage rises from ~4.8% to ~6.2%.
- Behavioral/control-flow drift: (1) 2026-05-05 Supplier Z rework/request-info jump; (2) 2026-07-01 priority-rule onset with queue redistribution and Senior Assessment changes; (3) 2026-06-15 staffing-driven adaptation is plausible but mediated by queueing.
- Performance drift: (1) 2026-05 median/P90 begins rising; (2) 2026-06-07 acceleration after staffing loss and priority policy; (3) 2026-10 seasonal volume/complexity peak.

**Global versus mechanism-specific change points.** The data support multiple mechanism-specific change points, not one global change point. March is mainly a logging/semantic change: raw fitness collapses while mapped fitness recovers and the mapped DFG fragment is nearly stable. May-July are behavioral and performance changes: rework, Request Info loops, Supplier Z share, queueing, and P90 rise. October is strongly seasonal/population-driven but is amplified by staffing and backlog. December improvement is mixed because lower seasonal load and interventions coincide. A single changepoint model would misattribute semantic drift to process deterioration and would miss the later supplier, capacity, priority, and seasonal mechanisms.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Month/week time window | month, ISO week, event timestamp | Monitor onset and recovery; separate sudden from gradual | At least 50 cases/week or 200 cases/month; pool rare cells | Rolling baseline, same-season comparison, pre/post event windows | Seasonality and interventions can be mistaken for drift |
| Semantic era | pre/post 2026-03-01, label-normalized flag | Separate migration/logging effects from process effects | At least 100 cases per era within major strata | Raw vs mapped DFG, alignments, event ordering | Migration coincides with later supplier and staffing events if eras are too wide |
| Supplier-Z exposure phase | supplier Z yes/no/unknown; pre-May, May-Sep, post-Sep | Isolate supplier disruption and recovery | At least 30 cases per Supplier-Z phase cell; pool regions if needed | Z vs non-Z within phase; recovered vs never-Z | Supplier Z is non-random by region and complexity |
| Complexity/priority | high_complexity flag, priority class if available, Senior Assessment need | Separate population drift from priority redistribution | At least 30 cases per complexity/priority/month cell | High vs standard; priority-class queue divergence | Complexity recording is inconsistent |
| Resource/team | team, assessor, reassignment status, missing-resource flag | Diagnose capacity and organizational drift | At least 20 cases per assessor-quarter or aggregate to team | Departed vs remaining teams; per-assessor queue and service | Missing resources create selection bias |
| Variant/outcome | variant category, rework flag, final decision | Link behavior to rework and outcomes | At least 10 cases per variant cell or collapse rare variants | Rework vs non-rework; approve vs reject; pre/post event | Conditioning on outcome can induce collider bias |
| Region/customer | region, customer type, Supplier Z region | Test non-random supplier composition | At least 30 cases per region-phase cell | Region-stratified DFG and cycle time | Region may be collinear with supplier and complexity |

**Primary monitoring population.** The primary population should be the monthly mapped event log after technical duplicate removal, stratified by Supplier Z and high-complexity/priority class. The clean reference substream should be non-Supplier-Z, standard or unknown-complexity cases in a comparable season, because this best approximates the intended process and is less structurally affected by supplier disruption or priority redistribution. Overall metrics should still be reported for the full population so vulnerable or disrupted cases are not hidden; the clean substream is for detecting new drift, not for estimating total operational load.

## 7. Monitoring architecture

| monitor | statistic/artifact | reference window | alert logic | diagnostic follow-up | likely false-positive source | owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Label/schema monitor | New/retired activity labels, distinct label count, mapping rate for `Eligibility Screen` | Rolling 6-12 months and same-month prior year | Alert when a new label appears, mapping rate changes materially, or label set change exceeds robust baseline variability; require schema audit | Review mapping dictionary and system release notes | Legitimate migration or retraining | Data steward |
| Event-ordering and duplicates monitor | Duplicate `Open Case` rate, invalid transitions, duplicate event timestamps, case-ID anomalies | Mapped baseline by month and system version | Alert on duplicate rate above robust control limits or new invalid transition patterns | Trace provenance; inspect ETL/system logs | Batch processing or technical reconciliation | Data engineer |
| Normalized DFG monitor | Fragment L1/MAD against seasonal baseline; top deviating edges | Seasonal baseline plus rolling 6 months | Use EWMA/CUSUM on edge rates; require corroborating edge deviations, not a single universal cutoff | Drill into DFG contributors and alignments | Small monthly samples; rounded aggregates | Process analyst |
| Variant distribution monitor | Variant entropy, fast-variant %, rework-variant %, Jensen-Shannon divergence | Same-season rolling baseline | Alert when JS divergence or rework-variant share shifts beyond stratified robust limit; require two consecutive periods | Inspect top variant changes; split by supplier/complexity | Case-mix changes | Process owner |
| Rework monitor | `AssessRequest Info`, `RequestInfoCollectDocs`, repeat `Assess`, rework share | Rolling 6 months stratified by Supplier Z and complexity | Alert on upward CUSUM in rework stratified by strata; avoid one global threshold | Audit document completeness and reassessment reasons | Seasonal complexity or supplier recovery | Operations |
| Temporal-profile monitor | Directly-follows duration profiles, especially before Assess and after Request Info | Same-season and by priority class | Alert on temporal-profile deviations by activity pair using robust z-scores/EWMA | Compare queue and service fragments by class/team | Case mix and short observation windows | Process analyst |
| Queue-wait monitor | Reconstructed waiting before `Assess`, by priority class and team | Rolling 8-12 weeks plus same-season baseline | Alert if high-complexity and standard-case waits diverge or P90 wait exceeds stratum-specific control band | Capacity review; priority-rule audit | Priority policy effects, seasonal volume | Capacity planning |
| Service-time monitor | Median/P95 service time for `Assess` and `Senior Assessment` | Same complexity and team stratum | Alert on EWMA shift in service time or its P95 | Staffing/training review; case sampling | Complexity mix and senior assessment recording | Team lead |
| Case-mix monitor | High-complexity %, Supplier Z %, region/customer mix, priority mix | Same-month prior year and rolling trend | Alert when composition changes beyond seasonal bands; stratify before interpreting performance alerts | Recompute dashboards with case-mix adjustment | Annual renewal and regional campaigns | Population analyst |
| Resource-completeness monitor | Missing-resource %, activity-specific missingness, by team/system version | Rolling 6 months and migration-era baseline | Alert when missingness exceeds activity-specific robust limit or is non-random by team | Data-quality audit; sensitivity analysis | Migration side effects | Data steward |
| Conformance/alignment monitor | Raw and mapped fitness, alignment deviations, DECLARE constraint violations | Mapped pre-migration baseline and seasonal baseline | Track raw and mapped separately; alert on mapped fitness drop with corroborating DFG/variant deviation | Model review; label audit; inspect alignment deviations | Normative model drift rather than process drift | Process-mining team |
| Outcome monitor | Approve/reject rates, overturn rate, time-to-decision | Same-season stratified reference | Alert on outcome-rate shifts within strata using SPC or logistic monitoring | Review eligibility and assessment criteria | Case-mix and eligibility-policy changes | Quality owner |
| Seasonality/intervention monitor | Volume, high-complexity share, known interventions, same-month prior-year ratios | Prior year and announced intervention calendar | Use seasonal adjustment; alert only on residual deviation from expected seasonal pattern | Compare to intervention timing and campaign logs | Holidays, renewal season, one-off campaigns | Analytics owner |

I would not use one universal threshold. Each monitor should use a stratum-specific robust threshold or model—median/MAD bands, EWMA/CUSUM, or quantile control limits—by season, Supplier Z status, complexity/priority class, and resource team where support allows. For multiple testing, group monitors into families, apply a false-discovery-rate procedure such as Benjamini-Hochberg, and require corroboration from at least two related artifacts before operational action. Seasonal monitors should compare against the same month of prior years or seasonal residuals; intervention periods should be flagged and analyzed separately rather than silently resetting the baseline.

# Part B: Reusable PM4Py event-level functions

import pm4py

def format_event_log(df, case_id="case_id", activity="activity", timestamp="timestamp"):
    """
    Return a formatted copy; the original dataframe is not overwritten.
    """
    work = df.copy()
    work[timestamp] = pd.to_datetime(work[timestamp], errors="coerce")
    work = work.dropna(subset=[case_id, activity, timestamp]).copy()
    formatted = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        work,
        case_id=case_id,
        activity_key=activity,
        timestamp_key=timestamp
    )
    return formatted

def slice_time_range(formatted_df, start, end):
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(
        formatted_df,
        start,
        end,
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
    )

def monthly_dfg(formatted_df, freq="M"):
    work = formatted_df.copy()
    work["__month"] = work["time:timestamp"].dt.to_period(freq)
    out = {}
    for m, sub in work.groupby("__month"):
        out[str(m)] = pm4py.discover_dfg(
            sub,
            case_id_key="case:concept:name",
            activity_key="concept:name",
            timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
        )
    return out

def monthly_variants(formatted_df, freq="M"):
    work = formatted_df.copy()
    work["__month"] = work["time:timestamp"].dt.to_period(freq)
    out = {}
    for m, sub in work.groupby("__month"):
        out[str(m)] = pm4py.get_variants(
            sub,
            case_id_key="case:concept:name",
            activity_key="concept:name",
            timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
        )
    return out

def temporal_profile(formatted_df, start=None, end=None):
    sub = formatted_df
    if start is not None and end is not None:
        sub = slice_time_range(formatted_df, start, end)
    return pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(
        sub,
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
    )

def discover_optional_log_skeleton(formatted_df):

def drop_exact_duplicate_activity_events(formatted_df, activity_name="Open Case"):
    """
    Drop exact duplicate events of a technical activity using case, activity, timestamp.
    Returns a copy and never mutates the original log.
    """
    df = formatted_df.copy()
    mask = df["concept:name"].eq(activity_name)
    if not mask.any():
        return df
    duplicate_subset = ["case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"]
    cleaned = pd.concat([
        df[~mask],
        df[mask].drop_duplicates(subset=duplicate_subset, keep="first")
    ]).sort_index()
    return cleaned

def compare_raw_vs_normalized_label_mappings(
    df,
    mapping,
    case_id="case_id",
    activity="activity",
    timestamp="timestamp",
    drop_duplicates_for_activity="Open Case"
):
    """
    Compare raw and label-normalized logs without overwriting the input df.
    mapping example: {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"}
    """
    raw = format_event_log(
        df, case_id=case_id, activity=activity, timestamp=timestamp
    )
    normalized = raw.copy()
    normalized["concept:name"] = normalized["concept:name"].replace(mapping)

    if drop_duplicates_for_activity:
        normalized = drop_exact_duplicate_activity_events(
            normalized, activity_name=drop_duplicates_for_activity
        )

    raw_dfg = pm4py.discover_dfg(
        raw,
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
    )
    normalized_dfg = pm4py.discover_dfg(
        normalized,
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
    )
    raw_variants = pm4py.get_variants(
        raw,
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
    )
    normalized_variants = pm4py.get_variants(
        normalized,
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
    )

    return {
        "raw_dfg": raw_dfg,
        "normalized_dfg": normalized_dfg,
        "raw_variants": raw_variants,
        "normalized_variants": normalized_variants
    }

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest attribution is March’s raw-fitness collapse: raw fitness falls from 0.92 to 0.55 while mapped fitness recovers to about 0.90, with 11 activity labels, duplicated `Open Case`, and missing resources. This is high-confidence semantic/provenance or logging drift, not a real conformance collapse. The mapped DFG fragment changes little in March. Moderate-to-high confidence: the Supplier Z disruption from May increased incomplete bundles, `Request Info`/recollection, and rework; Supplier Z share rose from about 18% to 43-46%, rework from 13% to 24-27%, and cycle time began rising. September recovery partially reverses these patterns, although backlog and persistent adaptation remain plausible. The June staffing cuts plausibly explain much of the later queueing and P90 tail: recorded Assess service time changed only modestly while reconstructed queue waiting increased much more. The July priority rule likely redistributed delay, helping high-complexity cases and worsening standard cases, but the aggregates cannot prove magnitude. Seasonal renewal plausibly explains the October-November volume and high-complexity peak, amplified by staffing and Supplier Z backlog; the Senior Assessment rise is consistent with complexity but confounded by inconsistent historical recording. December improvement is mixed seasonal relief, Supplier Z recovery, backlog reduction, overtime, and the document-quality campaign, so it is not clean evidence of permanent recovery. Interaction is central: Supplier Z is non-random and more complex, high-complexity cases were prioritized, and staffing and seasonality overlap. The data support multiple mechanism-specific change points, not one global change. I would revise the account if event-level analyses showed that excess rework persists after Supplier Z recovery and after complexity adjustment; if queue increases concentrate in teams after assessor departures; if high-complexity waits fall while standard waits rise after July 1; or if label remapping plus duplicate removal leaves no substantive DFG or performance change.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. `IF raw_model_fitness drops sharply while mapped_model_fitness recovers and new labels or duplicated Open Case events appear, THEN audit label mapping and technical-event provenance before treating conformance as deteriorated, BECAUSE this signature indicates semantic or logging drift rather than a real process change.`

2. `IF Supplier Z share rises together with Assess->Request Info and rework variants, THEN stratify cycle time and DFG by supplier before adjusting capacity, BECAUSE incomplete bundles can generate rework and queueing while confounding by case mix must be checked.`

3. `IF median Assess service time is stable but queue waiting before Assess and P90 cycle time increase after assessor departures, THEN treat capacity and queueing as the primary mechanism and rebalance work, BECAUSE service-time stability suggests delay is accumulating outside the service activity.`

4. `IF high-complexity waiting falls while standard-case waiting rises after a priority rule, THEN report queue metrics separately by priority class and revise the rule if standard cases breach service levels, BECAUSE aggregate medians can hide priority-induced redistribution.`

5. `IF October-November volume and high-complexity share rise in a repeating annual pattern, THEN compare against same-season historical baselines and adjust thresholds for renewal season, BECAUSE seasonal population drift can mimic behavioral deterioration.`

6. `IF mapped DFG, variant, or rework metrics remain elevated after supplier recovery and after controlling for complexity and staffing, THEN investigate persistent behavioral adaptation and backlog-clearing rules, BECAUSE recovery alone should not leave the same rework signature if the original disruption mechanism has truly ended.`

7. `IF resource missingness is concentrated in migrated activities and biases team comparisons, THEN analyze organizational metrics with missingness sensitivity and label-provenance strata, BECAUSE measurement missingness can create apparent resource or organizational drift or hide real effects.`
