## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

**Hard invariants (must not be violated by any repair):**
- H1 Referential integrity: every added E2O and O2O must use existing `eid/oid` and existing types. No new events or objects.
- H2 Type compatibility: e.g., `Record Payment` must relate to a `Payment`; `Dispatch Package` must relate to a `Package`; `Issue Invoice` to an `Invoice`. Qualifier direction matters: `DEL carries PK` is not `PK carries DEL`.
- H3 Temporal feasibility: cause must precede effect in lifecycle order: `Create Order < Add Item < Pack Item < Dispatch Package < Confirm Delivery`, and `Issue Invoice` normally after orders/items, `Record Payment` after or concurrent with invoice. A later timestamp cannot create an earlier lifecycle precondition.
- H4 No contradiction of observed records: observed E2O/O2O are taken as present; repair only adds or explicitly declines to add. It does not delete or rewrite `scanner_ref`, `external_ref`, amounts, routes.

**Strong defaults (violable only with positive evidence):**
- S1 Cardinality norms from domain semantics 1-7: `Create Order:1 Order`, `Add Item:1 Order+1 Item`, `Pack Item:1 Item+1 Package`, `Dispatch:1 Package+1 Delivery` unless bulk, `Record Payment:1 Payment+normally 1 Invoice`.
- S2 Lifecycle completeness expectation, but not requirement: Orders normally `Create->Add->Pack->Dispatch->Confirm->Invoice->Payment`; Items `Add->Pack->Dispatch(via package)->Delivery(via delivery)`; Packages `Pack->Dispatch->Delivery`; Deliveries `Dispatch->Confirm`; Invoices `Issue->Payment`; Payments `Record` with invoice link unless unapplied cash.
- S3 Customer/route coherence: same-customer objects can share invoice/payment; same-route package/delivery combinations are more plausible than cross-route.
- S4 Existing O2O constrains E2O: if `PK-8 packs IT-A2` is observed, then any event linking `IT-A2` and `PK-8` is redundant but coherent; conversely inventing an E2O without O2O support (or vice versa) needs independent justification.

**Weak heuristics (informative but not decisive):**
- W1 Identifier similarity: `N-421?` vs `N-421`, `N-420` vs `N-420`, `N-42*` vs both, `order_hint`, `route_hint NORTH`, `scanner_ref PK8-2`. Useful for candidate generation only.
- W2 Timestamp proximity: simultaneous `ev09/ev10 at 10:00` suggests shared operational context but does not distinguish one bulk delivery vs two parallel deliveries.
- W3 Amount arithmetic: `130+100=230` and `100+130=230`, `PAY-1=100=ORD-B`, `PAY-2=130=ORD-A`. This is consistency evidence, not proof, per task constraint. Fees, discounts, partial payments, coincidental equality can produce same pattern.
- W4 Attribute co-occurrence: same `customer=C17` across orders/invoice/portal, same `route=NORTH` across packages/deliveries.

**Evidence against a repair:**
- C1 Time inversion, route/customer mismatch, unexplained 1:N or N:M explosion, mixing distinct customer orders in one package/delivery without operational support.
- C2 Orphan creation or orphan elimination solely for aesthetic completeness.
- C3 Double-counting: using an assumed E2O to justify an O2O and then citing the O2O as independent confirmation.

**Combination model:** No single cue decides. A proposal is stronger when at least two independent dimensions agree (e.g., identifier + time + route + cardinality + existing O2O), and weaker when it relies on one weak heuristic alone (amount only, prefix only). Absence of evidence is treated as `unresolved`, not negative proof. Confidence is calibrated downward when alternatives are mutually exclusive (ev10 can satisfy single-delivery norm with DEL-Y *or* bulk norm with DEL-X, not both without creating 1:N) and when a wrong addition would merge lifecycles downstream.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

ORD-B has `Create ev02  Add ev05`, then gap: IT-B1 lacks package/delivery in Bundle A, and ORD-B lacks invoice/confirm links. **Item:** IT-A1 (`Add ev03  Pack ev06  Dispatch ev09  Delivery DEL-X`) and IT-A2 (`Add ev04  Pack ev07  Dispatch ev10  Delivery DEL-Y in Bundle A`) are clean 1:1:1 chains with dual E2O+O2O support (ev06+packs, ev07+packs). Adding P01/P02 would give it continuity but creates Package 1:N (PK-8 packs two items from different orders) — suspicious many-to-one that needs operational proof of mixed picking, not just scanner prefix. Circularity warning: do not cite `PK-8 packs IT-B1` O2O as confirming `ev08PK-8` E2O when both were inferred from the same `PK8-2` string; they are one hypothesis, not two.

**Package:** PK-7 (`Pack IT-A1  Dispatch DEL-X`) is fully supported dually. PK-8 (`Pack IT-A2  Dispatch DEL-Y in A`) is plausible but Dispatch support hinges on provisional P03/P04. In Bundle B PK-8 becomes 1:N packs (IT-A2+IT-B1) — allowed physically but cardinality-suspicious because no other package in log mixes orders. In Bundle C PK-8 shares DEL-X, making Delivery 1:N.

**Delivery:** DEL-X (`Dispatch ev09  carries PK-7`) is solid. DEL-Y is orphan in unrepaired log (no E2O/O2O). Bundle A activates it provisionally via ev10; confirmation still missing (ev11 unresolved). This is honest: a Delivery with Dispatch but no Confirm is common when portal loses carrier id. Bundle B closes it via joint confirmation; Bundle C re-orphans it via bulk. Explicitly, DEL-Y lifecycle completeness must not drive the choice between A/B/C.

**Invoice:** INV-44 (`Issue ev12 with ORD-A  Payment PAY-1`) is observed. Scope extension to ORD-B (P11/P12) would make Invoice 1:N bills — permitted by semantics (`one or more Orders`) so not suspicious per se, but support is only customer+amount. **Payment:** PAY-1 (`Record ev13  settles INV-44`) is solid. PAY-2 (`Record ev14` alone) is orphan upstream in A — allowed as unapplied cash. Linking it (P13/P14) creates Invoice 1:N payments (PAY-1+PAY-2INV-44), which is plausible for split payments but again amount-only support. Must not treat sum-equality as closing the loop.

Across types, Bundle A preserves independent E2O/O2O corroboration where it exists (IT-A1, PK-7, PAY-1) and withholds it where it would be self-generated.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Forcing IT-B1 into PK-8 creates a false multi-order package.** Scanner prefix `PK8-2` alone does not prove physical packing; if wrong, Package-flattened log shows `Pack IT-A2  Pack IT-B1  Dispatch` as one case and Order-equivalence merges ORD-A/B, inventing joint fulfillment that never happened and distorting weight/capacity analysis (3.1 vs 2.9 kg).

2. **Asserting bulk `DEL-X carries PK-8` on timestamp equality hides a delivery.** Same 10:00 and NORTH route also fit two parallel trucks. If wrong, DEL-Y becomes a false orphan to be deleted, Delivery flattening undercounts deliveries, and Confirm Delivery appears to cover both packages with one carrier_ref, corrupting carrier performance metrics.

3. **Assigning `ev11` uniquely to DEL-X (or DEL-Y) erases joint-acknowledgement possibility.** `N-42*` matches both, and ORD-A spans two packages. Unique assignment makes OC-DFG show `DispatchConfirm` for one delivery only, suggesting the other failed, triggering false exception handling or SLA breach alerts.

4. **Adding `INV-44 bills ORD-B` because 130+100=230 invents consolidated billing.** Fees, taxes, or coincidence can yield 230. If wrong, Invoice flattening duplicates all ORD-B Add/Pack/Dispatch events into INV-44's case, revenue attribution overstates invoice scope, and Bundle B's neat `two Ordersone Invoicetwo Payments` OTG diamond is pure artifact.
