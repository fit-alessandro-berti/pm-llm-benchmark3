## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

**Stipulated facts established by the materials**

- CS-9 is a controlled, formally approved standard effective from 2025-01-01. It requires Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review before activation, Manual_Procurement_Approval before Activate_Supplier, and different humans for requester and manual approver.
- M0 is the current normative POWL construction. It encodes register, concurrent sanctions/quality checks, XOR between approved branch and rejection, Manual_Procurement_Approval before activation, Notify_Requester, and Close_Case.
- M0 does **not** encode the CS-9 requester/approver separation rule, PD-3 pilot eligibility, FD-7 emergency logic, Request_More_Info loops, CRM notification migration problems, label aliases, reactivation, rejected-lead scope, or data/amount/time conditions.
- PD-3 is a steering-committee pilot decision with minutes and follow-up email, effective 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31, but it was not incorporated into CS-9 or M0. It authorizes a narrow low-risk DE group auto-approval exception and permits delayed Quality_Review under conditions.
- FD-7 is a formal emergency directive effective 2026-07-10 through 2026-07-12, then expired automatically. It permits provisional activation before Quality_Review and manual approval for critical relief suppliers, but requires sanctions first, a EUR 20,000 cap, daily monitoring, and retrospective approval/quality within two business days.
- The CRM notification migration from 2026-07-01 caused substantial notification under-capture: about 70% mirrored into the onboarding log; missingness rose from 2% to 29%.
- Operations uses Request_More_Info cycles; Compliance regards them as desirable; none of the 47 observed such cases activated before requested evidence arrived.
- Aggregate operational evidence shows 312 Auto_Approve cases in June/July: 289 apparently eligible, 14 clearly ineligible, 9 with missing eligibility attributes. Six post-pilot Auto_Approve cases occurred in the first week of August.

**Propositions supported, but only under an authority or interpretation assumption**

- SUP-702 is pilot-eligible on its face and within the PD-3 window. If PD-3 is accepted as a competent temporary exception, it is a justified exception. If CS-9 can be relaxed only by formal CS-9 amendment, it remains a deviation.
- SUP-704 is within FD-7 scope/time/amount conditions as described. If the evidence for “critical relief supplier,” monitoring, and retrospective approval is accepted, it is a justified emergency exception.
- SUP-706 likely had a notification, because CRM shows a message at +3 hours, but the link is mutable text. The substantive event is supported; the evidentiary chain is weak.
- SUP-713’s QA_Clearance label is supported as semantically equivalent by audit documents, but the approved label mapping postdates the case.

**Disputed propositions**

- Whether rejected leads such as SUP-709 are governed by M0. The process owner says they are intake cases; the model owner says M0 begins only after pre-screen acceptance; the scope document is ambiguous.
- Whether reactivations such as SUP-710 are new onboardings requiring full register/quality treatment, or a separate lifecycle with reused certification.
- Whether PD-3 had authority to suspend CS-9 for eligible pilots. The steering-committee form and “monitored exception” language support temporary authority; the absence of CS-9 incorporation and the Compliance observer’s later statement weaken any claim of permanent amendment.
- Whether factual flood recovery after FD-7 expiry can extend the directive. Operations asserts continued factual emergency; the directive expired automatically and no written extension exists.
- Whether post-pilot Auto_Approve in August reflects legitimate reliance, pending approval, or control drift.

**Unproved propositions**

- That every Auto_Approve case with missing eligibility attributes was eligible.
- That automated master-data validation occurred in every pilot case where M0 shows only Auto_Approve.
- That SUP-715’s absent COI disclosure record proves no disclosure occurred anywhere, though the record as given says none was recorded.
- That all CRM notifications after migration have stable linked identifiers.
- That SUP-708’s sanctions result existed before activation but was timestamped late; no such evidence is provided.

**Burden-of-proof approach**

- For **noncompliance**, the proponent should establish:  
  1. the case was in scope of a valid rule;  
  2. the rule was effective at event time;  
  3. reliable evidence shows the required control did not occur, occurred too late, or involved the wrong actor/data condition; and  
  4. no valid exception covered the case.  
  For high-severity findings, the evidence should be especially clear because the consequence is significant.
- For **accepting an exception**, the burden lies on the party invoking it:  
  1. identify a competent authority;  
  2. produce a written, versioned, effective, scoped rule;  
  3. show case-level eligibility from a contemporaneous snapshot;  
  4. show all exception conditions were met; and  
  5. show the exception had not expired or been revoked.
- A raw M0 deviation is not automatically misconduct. Conversely, a desirable outcome, informal normalization, or missing model support does not erase a substantive control breach.
- Where evidence is incomplete in a way that prevents a reliable conclusion, the correct classification is **DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE**, not “compliant.”
- Where the issue is primarily that the model or case scope cannot represent the actual process, the correct classification is **MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT**, unless substantive harm or a clear rule breach dominates.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The strongest prosecution case starts from authority, not from PM4Py syntax. CS-9 is the controlled standard. It was formally approved, is effective from 2025-01-01, and imposes three hard requirements relevant here: sanctions and quality before activation, manual procurement approval before activation, and separation between requester and approver. M0 is the current intended control-flow representation. Deviations from M0 are not automatically meaningless merely because M0 is incomplete; many deviations are deviations from CS-9 itself.

**Formal authority and expiry.** PD-3 did not amend CS-9 or M0. It was a meeting-minutes/follow-up-email pilot decision, and the Compliance observer expressly did not approve weakening CS-9. At most, PD-3 could support a strictly bounded monitored exception. It cannot support post-expiry behavior or ineligible cases. FD-7 was stronger and more formal, but it expired automatically at 2026-07-12 23:59. A continuing factual flood condition is not a continuing legal authorization. SUP-705 therefore lacks authority after expiry.

**Deviations hidden or understated by M0.** M0’s incompleteness sometimes hides substantive breaches rather than excusing them. SUP-712 fits the control-flow trace but violates CS-9’s different-human rule: requester and approver resolve to the same human through aliases. SUP-715 fits M0 but has no recorded COI disclosure despite a disclosure policy. SUP-713’s label change is explainable, but exact raw conformance would miss the semantic issue. SUP-706’s missing notification is not merely cosmetic because notification before closure is both in M0 and operationally important, even though CRM migration gives a defense. SUP-711’s duplicate Register event is a data artifact, but if not deduplicated it distorts conformance.

**Normalization of exceptions is itself a control symptom.** Six post-pilot Auto_Approve cases in the first week of August show that a temporary pilot became an operating habit. Operations’ claim that the pilot flow was normalized and that permanent approval was believed imminent is not a defense: belief without amendment is not authority. This is exactly the kind of drift that CS-9 and overlay governance are meant to prevent.

**High-risk cases are materially serious.** SUP-703 is the clearest severe case: a HIGH-risk new foreign supplier used Auto_Approve despite pilot ineligibility, and the first purchase was EUR 180,000. Quality occurred after activation, and no emergency applied. This is not a mere logging defect. SUP-708 is also severe: a HIGH-risk supplier was activated 40 minutes before Sanctions_Check, with no pilot or emergency. The absence of transactions before sanctions clearance reduces harm but does not cure the breach of a preventive gate. Sanctions screening is designed to occur before exposure, not to be validated retrospectively after luck.

**Aggregate evidence confirms systemic exposure.** Fourteen clearly ineligible Auto_Approve cases and nine cases with missing eligibility attributes are not trivial. Missing eligibility attributes mean the operation cannot discharge the burden for an exception. The rise in notification missingness to 29% weakens the audit trail precisely for a required control. The 47 Request_More_Info cases may be benign, but their absence from M0 shows that conformance reporting has been underestimating or misclassifying real process variants.

**Desirable outcomes do not retroactively amend policy.** Request_More_Info may be desirable, but a desirable loop still needs model and policy representation. QA_Clearance may be equivalent, but semantic mapping must be governed. Flood recovery may be real, but FD-7 expired. Operations may prefer permanent auto-approval, but no permanent amendment exists.

**Concession required of the prosecution.** The prosecution should not overclaim. M0 genuinely lacks resource rules, overlays, loops, data conditions, label mapping, and scope rules. SUP-702 and SUP-704 are strong justified-exception candidates. SUP-707, SUP-709, SUP-710, SUP-711, and SUP-713 contain real model/data/scope issues rather than obvious misconduct. The correct prosecution conclusion is therefore not “every raw deviation is noncompliant,” but “material noncompliance exists alongside genuine model defects, and those defects do not legalize substantive breaches.”

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest defense begins with model semantics. Conformance results are only meaningful if the reference model, data, and scope are valid. M0 is not a complete or current representation of the governed process. It encodes a simplified CS-9 view and then silently treats everything outside that view as deviant. That conflates at least five different phenomena: true policy breach, justified overlay, data loss, scope mismatch, and harmless but desirable practice.

**Concurrency and missing loops.** M0 makes Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review concurrent by leaving them unordered inside `checks`. It does not represent Request_More_Info, despite 47 observed cases and Compliance agreement that the behavior is desirable. SUP-707 is therefore not evidence of misconduct; it is evidence that the model is obsolete. None of those 47 cases activated before required evidence arrived, so the control objective was met.

**Pilot overlay.** SUP-702 is within the PD-3 window, low-risk, DE, approved corporate group, and quality occurred next business day. CRM shows notification with stable notification_id. If PD-3 is treated as what it says it is—a recorded steering-committee monitored exception—then SUP-702 is not a breach of operational policy; it is the intended pilot behavior. The Compliance observer’s statement that she did not approve weakening CS-9 is consistent with a non-permanent pilot: the pilot did not amend CS-9, but it could still authorize monitored deviations for a defined period and population. The lack of an automated master-data validation event in the trace is a data-collection issue to investigate, not proof that the validation did not occur.

**Emergency overlay.** SUP-704 is the paradigmatic FD-7 case: critical pump supplier, 2026-07-11, sanctions first, initial spend EUR 18,000 under the cap, daily monitoring exists, and quality plus approval occurred next business day. Treating that as ordinary CS-9 noncompliance ignores a valid emergency rule. Emergencies are precisely situations where the normal sequence is suspended for narrowly defined cases.

**CRM migration and notification evidence.** After 2026-07-01, notification collection changed. The log captures only about 70% of notifications, and missingness rose from 2% to 29%. SUP-706 has no onboarding-log notification, but CRM contains a message at +3 hours. The weak point is the mutable text link, not proof that requester notification failed. A raw trace missing Notify_Requester after the migration is weak evidence of noncompliance.

**Reactivation and rejected leads.** SUP-710 concerns a supplier closed six months earlier with prior quality certification still valid. CS-9 has no reactivation procedure. A reactivation is not necessarily a new onboarding, and M0’s requirement for Register_Supplier and Quality_Review may be misapplied. SUP-709 likewise falls into a scope gap: the lead failed sanctions pre-screen, and CS-9 expressly contemplates abbreviated pre-screen for rejected leads. The process owner calls these intake cases. If the model owner’s boundary is not in an approved scope document, classifying SUP-709 as an M0 deviation is not enough to establish misconduct.

**Label equivalence.** SUP-713’s QA_Clearance is supported by procedure/UI renaming, an approved mapping, and audit documents showing equivalent content. The mapping was approved after the case, but the event itself is semantically traceable. A rigid label mismatch finding would be a data-normalization problem, not a substantive Quality_Review failure.

**Resource and COI defects.** SUP-712 and SUP-715 expose an important distinction: M0 is a control-flow model, but CS-9 and COI policy include resource and organizational constraints. If the conformance system lacks canonical actor IDs and COI disclosure checks, it will misstate compliance. For SUP-715 specifically, no rule prohibits spouses per se; the recorded failure is absence of disclosure. That may be a disclosure-control gap or evidence gap, not proof of an improper approval decision.

**Aggregate record is mixed.** Of 312 Auto_Approve cases, 289 appear eligible; 47 Request_More_Info cases had no early activation; SUP-702 and SUP-704 fit valid overlays. This is not a process where all controls collapsed. It is a process where the conformance architecture failed to keep pace with approved exceptions, scope boundaries, and data migration.

**Concession required of the defense.** The defense cannot excuse genuine risk. PD-3 does not cover high-risk ineligible suppliers or first purchases of EUR 180,000, so SUP-703 is noncompliant. FD-7 did not continue after expiry, so SUP-705 lacks written authority. SUP-708’s late sanctions check is a real preventive-control failure, even without transactions. Six post-pilot Auto_Approve cases show normalization of an expired exception. Missing eligibility attributes cannot be treated as eligibility. Informal practice, desirable outcomes, and operational belief cannot replace formal amendment or written extension.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

**Layer 1: Scope and case taxonomy.** Define case type before applying a process model:

- **New onboarding:** starts after pre-screen acceptance; standard CS-9 onboarding model applies.
- **Rejected lead / intake:** starts at lead capture; may end at sanctions pre-screen failure, withdrawal, or rejection; abbreviated pre-screen applies; no Quality_Review required unless policy says so.
- **Reactivation:** closed supplier returning; requires prior certification validity check, sanctions refresh, approval, notification, closure; Quality_Review may be waived only if an approved rule accepts prior certification.
- **Withdrawal:** requester or candidate withdraws before decision; no activation; notification and closure still required.

This layer belongs in organizational scope documents and case-type data checks, not only in POWL.

**Layer 2: Revised control-flow model.** POWL should represent the broad control-flow alternatives:

- register and optional repeatable Request_More_Info;
- standard route with concurrent Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review, then Manual_Procurement_Approval, then Activate_Supplier;
- low-risk auto route with automated sanctions/master-data gates and Auto_Approve;
- emergency/provisional route with sanctions before activation and retrospective quality/approval;
- Reject_Supplier route;
- Notify_Requester before Close_Case.

POWL is appropriate for precedence, concurrency, XOR routing, and loop approximation. It is not appropriate for amount thresholds, eligibility snapshots, actor identity, authority, or expiry logic.

**Layer 3: DECLARE/log-skeleton-style constraints.** Use declarative/log-skeleton rules for invariants across variants:

- Sanctions_Check must precede Activate_Supplier in every onboarding, pilot, and emergency activation.
- Quality_Review must precede activation except when an active overlay explicitly permits delayed quality.
- Manual_Procurement_Approval must precede activation except under an active low-risk auto or emergency overlay.
- Reject_Supplier and Activate_Supplier cannot both occur in the same case.
- Notify_Requester must precede Close_Case.
- If Request_More_Info occurs, activation must occur after the latest information-resolution event.
- In emergency cases, Quality_Review and retrospective approval must occur within two business days of activation.
- In pilot cases, quality must occur within two business days if delayed.

These are better expressed as constraints than forced into a single procedural tree because overlays change which rules are active.

**Layer 4: pandas/data-aware checks.** Use data checks for:

- pilot eligibility: risk = LOW, country = DE, approved corporate group, effective PD-3 version, eligibility snapshot at decision time;
- amount: emergency spend cap EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval;
- time: rule effective timestamps, expiry, business-day SLAs, immutable event timestamps versus record timestamps;
- actor identities: canonical requester/approver IDs, alias resolution, delegation evidence;
- notification completeness: stable notification_id, CRM linkage, migration flag;
- duplicate detection: source_transaction_id deduplication;
- label normalization: QA_Clearance mapped to Quality_Review with versioned mapping;
- case scope: onboarding versus intake/reactivation/withdrawal.

**Layer 5: Organizational controls.** Some rules cannot be reduced to event-log conformance:

- authority to approve pilots, emergencies, amendments, and extensions;
- COI disclosure and mitigation;
- delegation and manual approval rights;
- approval of retrospective emergency spend;
- formal amendment process for making low-risk auto-approval permanent;
- monitoring and audit review of exception populations.

These require policy records, access-control systems, and governance evidence.

**Layer 6: Overlay governance.** Every exception rule should carry rule_id, version, authority, scope, effective_from, effective_to, created_at, recorded_at, precedence, eligibility conditions, required evidence, expiry behavior, and revocation status. Expired overlays must fail closed. Factual similarity to an expired emergency is not an overlay.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

**Model limitation before the code.** A single POWL cannot enforce data-dependent eligibility, amount caps, actor identity, effective dates, authority, or business-day SLAs. The code below therefore implements the most defensible approximation: route alternatives are structural; guards live in the overlay table and data checks. The Request_More_Info loop uses a PM4Py-style LOOP with a silent “do” child and Request_More_Info as the repeatable child, approximating zero or more cycles. Delayed pilot/emergency quality is left unordered relative to activation inside the branch; the overlay enforces the timing SLA. Because parent edges wait for the entire branch to complete, notification is modeled after route completion; if a future policy permits notification before delayed quality, use a declarative constraint instead of this POWL coupling.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# ---------------------------
# Shared pre-decision section
# ---------------------------
register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")

# Approximation of 0..n Request_More_Info cycles:
# LOOP child 0 executes once; LOOP child 1 may repeat.
request_more_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
rmi_loop = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[SilentTransition(), request_more_info]
)

# ---------------------------
# Standard CS-9 manual route
# ---------------------------
std_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
std_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")

# No order edge between these two means concurrency in StrictPartialOrder.
std_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[std_sanctions, std_quality])

std_manual_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
std_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")

standard_branch = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[std_checks, std_manual_approval, std_activate]
)
standard_branch.order.add_edge(std_checks, std_manual_approval)
standard_branch.order.add_edge(std_manual_approval, std_activate)

# ---------------------------
# Low-risk auto-approval route
# Guards are not in POWL; see overlay PD3 / permanent amendment table.
# ---------------------------
auto_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
auto_master_data = Transition(label="Automated_Master_Data_Validation")
auto_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[auto_sanctions, auto_master_data])

auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
auto_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
auto_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")

auto_branch = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[auto_checks, auto_approve, auto_activate, auto_quality]
)
auto_branch.order.add_edge(auto_checks, auto_approve)
auto_branch.order.add_edge(auto_approve, auto_activate)
# auto_quality is intentionally unordered relative to auto_activate:
# PD-3 allows delayed quality, subject to a two-business-day SLA overlay.

# ---------------------------
# Emergency/provisional route
# Guards live in overlay FD7: critical relief, time window, amount cap,
# monitoring, retrospective approval, and quality SLA.
# ---------------------------
emergency_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
emergency_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
emergency_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
emergency_manual_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")

emergency_branch = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[
        emergency_sanctions,
        emergency_activate,
        emergency_quality,
        emergency_manual_approval,
    ]
)
emergency_branch.order.add_edge(emergency_sanctions, emergency_activate)
emergency_branch.order.add_edge(emergency_activate, emergency_quality)
emergency_branch.order.add_edge(emergency_activate, emergency_manual_approval)
# Daily monitoring and EUR cap are data/audit controls, not POWL transitions here.

# ---------------------------
# Rejection route
# ---------------------------
reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
reject_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[reject])

# ---------------------------
# XOR over governed routes
# ---------------------------
route_choice = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[standard_branch, auto_branch, emergency_branch, reject_branch]
)

# ---------------------------
# Notification and closure
# ---------------------------
notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

M1 = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[register, rmi_loop, route_choice, notify, close]
)
M1.order.add_edge(register, rmi_loop)
M1.order.add_edge(rmi_loop, route_choice)
M1.order.add_edge(route_choice, notify)
M1.order.add_edge(notify, close)
```

**Event/data-collection proposal**

- Add immutable event fields: `case_id`, `case_type`, `activity`, `event_timestamp`, `record_timestamp`, `source_system`, `source_transaction_id`, `rule_version_id`, `overlay_id`, `actor_canonical_id`, `actor_alias_used`, `actor_role`, `delegation_id`.
- Add stable notification fields: `notification_id`, `crm_message_id`, `notification_sent_at`, `notification_linked_at`, `link_method`, `link_immutable`, `notification_payload_hash`.
- Add eligibility snapshot fields at decision/activation time: `risk_level`, `country`, `corporate_group_id`, `group_approval_status`, `pilot_eligibility_snapshot_id`, `master_data_validation_id`.
- Add emergency/pilot authority fields: `authority_document_id`, `overlay_rule_id`, `overlay_version`, `effective_from`, `effective_to`, `emergency_classification`, `spend_cap_eur`, `spend_to_date_eur`, `daily_monitoring_id`.
- Add resource/COI fields: `requester_canonical_id`, `approver_canonical_id`, `segregation_check_result`, `relationship_disclosure_id`, `coi_mitigation_status`.
- Maintain separate normalized tables for actor aliases, notification records, overlay rules, label mappings, eligibility snapshots, and source-transaction deduplication.
- Use immutable effective timestamps for rules and immutable event timestamps for activities; never infer validity from mutable record timestamps.
- Deduplicate events by `source_transaction_id` before conformance checking, especially for Register_Supplier.
- Treat missing eligibility, missing master-data validation, or missing stable notification linkage as indeterminate unless an approved alternate evidence source satisfies the overlay.
- Fail closed when no active rule version matches the case type and event time.
