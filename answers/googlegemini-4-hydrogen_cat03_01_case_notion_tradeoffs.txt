## 1. Analytical assumptions

In object-centric process mining, a **case notion** is a projection rule that selects a single object type $OT$ as the grouping key, retains only events directly linked to at least one object $o \in OT$ via the event-to-object (E2O) relation, and orders those events chronologically per object $o$ to form traditional traces. Flattening does not merely "filter" an OCEL; it imposes a single-level hierarchy onto a many-to-many graph.

Under this projection, **convergence** occurs when a single event references multiple objects of the *chosen* case-notion type (e.g., `e16` referencing `O1`, `O2`, `O3` under `Order` flattening, or `P2`, `P3` under `Package` flattening). Flattening copies that shared event into every participating object's trace. Cross-type convergence (e.g., `V2` billing `O2` and `O3` in `e19`, `e22`, `e25`) is invisible when flattening on `Invoice`, yet induces same-type convergence when flattening on `Order`. **Divergence** occurs when a single object of the chosen case-notion type participates in multiple instances of the same activity or interleaved independent sub-processes—typically via $1:N$ links to finer-grained objects (e.g., `O1` executing `Add Item` twice via `I11`/`I12` and `Dispatch` twice via `D1`/`D2`, or `V2` executing `Record Payment` twice via `M2`/`M3`).

I treat copied shared events as **both evidence and duplication**. Operationally, copying `e16` into `O1`, `O2`, and `O3` is valid evidence that one physical dispatch affected all three orders. Analytically, however, treating those three rows as three independent `Dispatch` executions constitutes duplication that inflates activity frequencies, edge weights, and apparent resource workload (expanding 31 unique events into 42 rows under `Order` flattening).

By default, all flattenings use **strictly direct E2O relations**. I permit two explicit analytical transformations solely as documented sensitivity variants: (1) **controlled O2O propagation** (e.g., propagating `Order` or `Invoice` financial events to `Item` via `bills` and `contains`), and (2) **fractional event weighting** ($1/k$ per object when an event is shared by $k$ objects of the case type) to separate operational participation from execution counts.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

### 1. Cross-order package packing creates false multi-order precedence (`Order` flattening; `P3`, `O2`, `O3`)
In `e13` (10:12), `P3` packs `I21` (`O2`) and `I31` (`O3`). Flattening on `Order` copies `e13` into both `O2` and `O3`. In `O2`, `e13` directly follows `Add Item (e07)` and precedes `Dispatch (e16)`, whereas in `O3`, `e13` follows `Add Item (e09)` and precedes `Pack Package (e14, P4)`. This duplicates `e13` (+100% frequency for `P3` packing) and forces an apparent `Pack Package (e13) -> Pack Package (e14)` sequence in `O3` that looks like sequential rework on `O3`, rather than two independent parcels (`P3` shared with `O2`, `P4` shared with `O4`). **Assessment: Harmful** for workload accounting and variant analysis if unannotated.

### 2. Cross-order package `P4` causes false `Pack -> Dispatch` cross-wiring (`Order` flattening; `P4`, `O3`, `O4`, `e14`, `e16`, `e17`)
`P4` packs `I32` (`O3`) and `I41` (`O4`) in `e14` (10:20), and is dispatched on `D3` in `e17` (11:15). Meanwhile, `O3` also participates in `D2` via `P3` in `e16` (11:05). In the `Order`-flattened trace for `O3`, chronological ordering yields `e13 (Pack P3) -> e14 (Pack P4) -> e16 (Dispatch D2) -> e17 (Dispatch D3)`. Consequently, `O3` generates a DFG edge `Pack Package (e14) -> Dispatch (e16)` and measures a 45-minute lag between packing `P4` and dispatching `D2`, even though `P4` is never carried by `D2` (`D2` carries `P2` and `P3`). Simultaneously, `e14` and `e17` are duplicated across `O3` and `O4`. **Assessment: Harmful**, as it fabricates a causal link and false cycle-time metric across disjoint logistics branches.

### 3. Order splitting across packages induces logistics self-loops (`Order` flattening; `O1`, `P1`, `P2`, `e11`, `e12`, `e15`, `e16`, `e23`, `e24`)
Because `O1` contains two items (`I11`, `I12`) packed into separate packages (`P1` in `e11`, `P2` in `e12`) and dispatched on separate deliveries (`D1` in `e15`, `D2` in `e16`), the `Order` trace for `O1` contains `Add Item -> Add Item`, `Pack Package -> Pack Package`, `Dispatch -> Dispatch`, and `Confirm Delivery -> Confirm Delivery` self-loops. While `O1` genuinely experiences split fulfilment, a traditional DFG interprets these self-loops as repeated attempts or rework on the same item/shipment rather than parallel fulfilment of `P1` and `P2`. Moreover, `e12 -> e15` creates a false 50-minute transition between packing `P2` and dispatching `D1` (`P1`). **Assessment: Context-dependent**—informative as a symptom of split orders, harmful as a control-flow or rework KPI.

### 4. Consolidated delivery `D2` triples apparent dispatch and delivery volume (`Order` and `Item` flattenings; `D2`, `e16`, `e24`)
`D2` consolidates `P2` (`O1`, `I12`) and `P3` (`O2`/`I21`, `O3`/`I31`) in `e16` and `e24`. Under `Order` flattening, `e16` and `e24` are each copied 3 times (`O1`, `O2`, `O3`); under `Item` flattening, they are copied 3 times (`I12`, `I21`, `I31`); under `Package` flattening, they are copied 2 times (`P2`, `P3`). Across the whole dataset, only 3 `Dispatch` events occur (`e15`, `e16`, `e17`), yet an `Order`- or `Item`-flattened DFG reports 6 `Dispatch` executions (a 100% inflation) and makes `D2` account for 50% of dispatches (`3/6`) instead of 33.3% (`1/3`). Performance averages are similarly skewed toward `D2`'s 4h55m transit time (`e16->e24`). **Assessment: Harmful** for transport capacity or carrier-frequency reporting; **informative** only for measuring how many customer orders/items were impacted by `D2`.

### 5. Consolidated invoicing inflates billing events and masks shared credit exposure (`Order` vs `Invoice` flattenings; `V2`, `O2`, `O3`, `e19`)
`V2` bills both `O2` and `O3` (`e19`, 12:05). ### 6. Partial payments create spurious finance–delivery oscillations (`Order` flattening; `V2`, `M2`, `M3`, `e22`, `e24`, `e25`, `e27`)

Meanwhile, `e22` and `e25` are duplicated across `O2` and `O3`, inflating `Record Payment` from 4 actual transactions to 6 rows (+50%). **Assessment: Harmful**, as it misrepresents asynchronous parallel threads as coupled control-flow loops.

### 8. Near-singleton lifecycle truncation in `Delivery` flattening hides upstream batching delays (`Delivery` flattening; `D1`–`D3`, `e15`–`e17`, `e23`, `e24`, `e27`)

Waiting time between packing and dispatch (`e12->e16`: 55m; `e13->e16`: 53m; `e14->e17`: 55m) disappears completely. **Assessment: Context-dependent**—ideal for pure carrier transit SLA, harmful if mistaken for total fulfilment speed.

### 9. High-coverage omission and variant erasure in `Item` and `Package` flattenings (`Item` and `Package` flattenings; `I11`–`I41`, `P1`–`P4`)
Both `Item` and `Package` flattenings collapse the entire observed process into a single linear variant (`Add Item -> Pack Package -> Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery` for `Item`; `Pack Package -> Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery` for `Package`). While this cleanly isolates physical flow, it drops 48.4% (`Item`) and 67.7% (`Package`) of events, hiding the fact that in `O1`, `O2`, `O3`, and `O4`, invoicing (`e18`–`e20`) and at least initial payment (`e21`, `e22`, `e26`) occur *before* final delivery confirmation (`e23`, `e24`, `e27`). An analyst viewing only `Item` or `Package` logs could falsely conclude that billing happens post-delivery or that all orders follow one homogeneous workflow. **Assessment: Harmful** if used to characterize end-to-end compliance; **informative** solely for physical step ordering.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

No single flattened log is sufficient, so the analysis portfolio should deliberately pair **scoped traditional logs** with **native object-centric artefacts**.

### Recommended flattened logs and their safe questions
1. **`Delivery`-flattened log** (6 events, 3 cases `D1`–`D3`): Safe for carrier transit-time SLAs (`Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery`: 3h00m–22h45m) and delivery completion counts, because it has 0% event duplication and zero divergence.
2. **`Invoice`-flattened log** (7 events, 3 cases `V1`–`V3`): Safe for billing-to-settlement duration (`V1`: 30m, `V2`: 20h55m, `V3`: 21h20m), instalment incidence (`V2` self-loop via `M2`/`M3`), and accounts-receivable aging.
3. **`Package`-flattened log** (12 rows / 10 events, 4 cases `P1`–`P4`): Safe for pack-to-dispatch dwell times (`e11->e15`: 1h00m; `e12->e16`: 55m; `e13->e16`: 53m; `e14->e17`: 55m) and pack-to-confirm durations, provided `Dispatch` and `Confirm Delivery` counts are **not** summed across `P2`/`P3` (`e16`, `e24` duplicated).
4. **`Order`-flattened log** (42 rows / 31 events, 4 cases `O1`–`O4`): Safe *only* for order-level boundary KPIs (`Create Order -> Close Order` lead times: `O1` 26h00m, `O2` 26h03m, `O3` 26h01m, `O4` 26h04m) and checking whether all orders reach `Close Order`. Do **not** use it unadjusted for DFG edge discovery, rework detection, or activity frequency reporting.
5. **Do not build `Payment`-flattened logs** (4 length-1 traces provide no behavioural insight) or unadjusted `Item`-flattened logs for operational counting (24 rows from 16 events heavily over-weight `D2`).

### Questions requiring object-centric artefacts
- **OC-DFG**: Essential to view distinct typed flows simultaneously (e.g., `Order` closing after `Delivery` and `Invoice` branches converge, without merging `e22->e24->e25->e27` into a single-object cycle) and to compare event counts (`E`: 3 `Dispatch` events) against unique object counts (`UO`: 4 `Package`s, 3 `Delivery`s, 4 `Order`s, 6 `Item`s).
- **Object Type Graph (OTG)** and **Event Type–Object Type (ET–OT) graph**: Required to verify structural O2O cardinalities (e.g., `Package`–`Item` $1:N$ vs `Order`–`Package` $M:N$ via `P3`/`P4`) and which activities bridge which object types (`Pack Package` binds `Order`–`Item`–`Package`, whereas `Issue Invoice` binds only `Order`–`Invoice`).
- **Object interactions summary**: Required to quantify cross-order coupling (specifically identifying that `O2` and `O3` co-occur on `P3`, `D2`, `V2`, `M2`, `M3`, while `O3` and `O4` co-occur on `P4`, `D3`).
- **Equivalent-execution clustering**: Useful diagnostically to test whether connected components of interacting objects separate into clean process-execution clusters (here revealing that `D2` and `P4` chain `O1`–`O4` into one connected network).

### Reconciling contradictory findings
When flattened and object-centric metrics disagree—for example, `Order` flattening reports 6 `Dispatch` events, `Package` reports 4, `Delivery` reports 3, and `OC-DFG` reports `E=3`—reconciliation proceeds in three steps: (1) anchor execution volume to the object type with 1:1 cardinality for that activity (`Delivery` for `Dispatch`: 3 runs; `Package` for `Pack Package`: 4 parcels; `Invoice` for `Issue Invoice`: 3 invoices); (2) interpret flattened excess rows (`42 - 31 = 11` in `Order`) explicitly as *co-participation touchpoints* rather than executions; and (3) resolve contradictory control-flow loops (`Record Payment <-> Confirm Delivery` in `Order` vs acyclic `Invoice` and `Delivery` lifecycles in `OC-DFG`) by checking the ET–OT graph to confirm that those activities share `Order` only as a common commercial container, not as an operational synchronization gate.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Selected Position: C. Reject a single lead object for this process.**

### Decision criteria
A defensible single lead object must: (1) cover the end-to-end activity spectrum without dropping core operational or financial phases; (2) preserve true execution counts without severe convergence inflation; (3) avoid fabricating control-flow loops or false causal edges via divergence; and (4) respect natural operational boundaries (physical batches and financial settlements).

### Strongest supporting evidence
None of the six object types satisfies all four criteria. Five object types (`Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`, `Payment`) fail criterion (1) immediately: they omit between 48.4% (`Item`, 15/31 events) and 87.1% (`Payment`, 27/31 events) of the process, making end-to-end analysis impossible. The sole candidate covering all 31 events, `Order`, fails criteria (2), (3), and (4):
- It suffers **+35.5% event duplication** (42 rows vs 31 events) across 9 shared events (`e13`, `e14`, `e16`, `e17`, `e19`, `e22`, `e24`, `e25`, `e27`).
- It fabricates **self-loops** (`Add Item`, `Pack Package`, `Dispatch`, `Confirm Delivery` on `O1`/`O3`), **false cross-parcel precedence** (`e14 -> e16` in `O3`, `e12 -> e15` in `O1`), and **spurious finance–logistics cycles** (`Record Payment <-> Confirm Delivery` via `e22 -> e24 -> e25 -> e27`).
- It violates natural operational boundaries because `P3` (`O2`/`O3`), `P4` (`O3`/`O4`), `D2` (`O1`/`O2`/`O3`), and `V2` (`O2`/`O3`) are genuine multi-order entities, not attributes of a single order. Moreover, because `D2` links `O1`–`O3` and `P4` links `O3`–`O4`, the entire 24-object inventory forms a single connected component—confirming this is a coupled fulfilment network rather than independent order traces.

### Strongest counterargument
`Order` is the only object type present in every single event (`e01`–`e31`) and anchors both the contractual start (`Create Order`) and commercial completion (`Close Order`). Therefore, Position B (designating `Order` as a primary lead object paired with secondary lifecycles) is attractive to business stakeholders who organize KPIs "per customer order." However, elevating `Order` to *lead* status encourages analysts to treat `Order`-flattened DFGs and conformance checks as the baseline truth and secondary views as mere drill-downs, whereas in this snapshot the primary execution mechanics (batch packing, consolidated transport, consolidated multi-instalment billing) live on `Package`, `Delivery`, and `Invoice`.

### Residual risk
Rejecting a single lead object increases analytical complexity: stakeholders accustomed to single-table case logs must interpret multi-dimensional OC-DFGs and O2O graphs, and naive connected-component clustering (`cluster_equivalent_ocel`) collapses all 4 orders into one cluster unless edges are scoped. We mitigate this by pairing the OC-DFG with the three clean scoped projections (`Delivery`, `Invoice`, and deduplicated `Package`).

### Conditions under which this decision would change
I would switch to **Position A or B (selecting `Order` as lead)** if: (a) business rules prohibited cross-order consolidation (`P3`, `P4`, `D2`, `V2` eliminated so convergence drops to zero), or (b) the analytical question were strictly restricted to commercial order-status duration (`Create Order` to `Close Order`) with all intermediate multi-object events aggregated or abstracted away.

    .unstack(fill_value=0)

        "cases": n_cases,
        "flattened_event_rows": n_rows,
        "unique_events_covered": n_unique_covered,
        "event_omission_rate": 1.0 - (n_unique_covered / total_unique_events),
        "duplication_factor": (n_rows / n_unique_covered) if n_unique_covered else 0.0,
        "dfg_edge_count": len(dfg),
    })

flattening_diagnostics_df = pd.DataFrame(flattening_diagnostics)

# 3. Object-centric discovery (preserving multi-object semantics rejected by single-lead flattening)
ocdfg_model = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
otg_model = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot_model = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)
object_interactions = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)

# 4. Justified by Position C: test whether connected components separate executions.
# Semantic caveat: because D2 links O1-O3 and P4 links O3-O4, graph clustering is expected
# to merge O1-O4 into a single coupled cluster unless cross-order object types are excluded first.

# 5. Quantitative sensitivity: compare Order-flattened activity counts vs true OCEL event counts
order_flat_act_counts = flattened_logs["Order"]["concept:name"].value_counts()
inflation_comparison = pd.DataFrame({

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

1. **Shared-event removal and fractional down-weighting**: Recompute `Order`- and `Item`-flattened DFGs under two perturbations: (a) assigning weight $1/k$ to any event shared by $k$ objects of the case type (e.g., `e16` weighted $1/3$ across `O1`–`O3`), and (b) partitioning the log into *order-exclusive* events (`e01`–`e12`, `e15`, `e18`, `e20`, `e21`, `e23`, `e26`, `e28`–`e31`) versus *shared batch* events (`e13`, `e14`, `e16`, `e17`, `e19`, `e22`, `e24`, `e25`, `e27`). Validation confirms whether apparent bottlenecks (such as `Dispatch` volume) vanish once the +11 duplicated rows in `Order` are normalized.

2. **Direct E2O versus explicit O2O relation propagation**: Construct a sensitivity OCEL where `Order`-level and `Invoice`-level events are explicitly propagated downward to `Item` via `Order --contains--> Item` and `Invoice --bills--> Order` (so `I11` inherits `e01`, `e18`, `e21`, `e28`, and `I21`/`I31` inherit `e19`, `e22`, `e25`). Comparing direct vs propagated `Item` flattening quantifies how much artificial convergence is introduced: `I11`/`I12` would suddenly duplicate `e01`, `e18`, `e21`, `e28`, and `I31`/`I32` would duplicate `e03`, `e19`, `e22`, `e25`, `e30`, turning clean `Item` traces into divergent, inflated traces mirroring `Order`.

3. **Systematic lead-object rotation**: Rotate the flattening across all six object types and benchmark four invariants: event-coverage rate (`Order` 100%, `Item` 51.6%, `Package` 32.3%, `Invoice` 22.6%, `Delivery` 19.4%, `Payment` 12.9%), row inflation factor (`Order` 1.35x, `Item` 1.50x, `Package` 1.20x, `Delivery`/`Invoice`/`Payment` 1.00x), variant count, and cycle-time median/max. Any KPI whose conclusion flips when rotating between `Order` and `Delivery`/`Invoice` (e.g., `Record Payment <-> Confirm Delivery` cycling) is flagged as a projection artefact rather than a process invariant.

4. **OC-DFG metric triangulation (`E`, `UO`, `TO`)**: Compare OC-DFG node and arc frequencies across three counting semantics: unique events (`E`), unique objects (`UO`), and total object appearances (`TO`). For `Dispatch` (`e15`–`e17`), `E = 3` (actual carrier departures), `UO(Package) = 4` (parcels shipped), `UO(Order) = 4` (distinct orders touched), and `TO(Order) = 6` (`O1` counted twice for `D1` and `D2`, `O3` counted twice for `D2` and `D3`). Divergences where `TO > UO` diagnose within-object **divergence** (`O1`, `O3`), whereas `TO > E` diagnoses cross-object **convergence** (`D2`, `P3`, `P4`, `V2`).
