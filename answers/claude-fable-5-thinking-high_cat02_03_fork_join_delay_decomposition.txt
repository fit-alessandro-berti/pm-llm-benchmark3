# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

Let c index cases and i ∈ {A, B, C} index the parallel branches. All quantities are in minutes, computed from complete timestamps accurate to one minute.

**Component formulas**

- Upstream elapsed: `upstream(c) = fork_release(c) − arrival(c)`
- Branch queue wait: `queue_i(c) = start_i(c) − fork_release(c)` (valid because `fork_release` is, by definition, the earliest time branch work is permitted, so any gap to the branch start is branch-local waiting)
- Branch service: `service_i(c) = end_i(c) − start_i(c)`
- Join readiness (not logged; derived from the stated AND-join rule): `join_ready(c) = max_i end_i(c)`
- Branch synchronization wait: `sync_i(c) = join_ready(c) − end_i(c)` ≥ 0, with `sync_i(c) = 0` for at least one branch (the critical one)
- Elapsed fork-join stage time: `forkjoin(c) = join_ready(c) − fork_release(c) = max_i [queue_i(c) + service_i(c)]`
- Critical branch: `crit(c) = argmax_i end_i(c)`; ties broken deterministically (alphabetical A < B < C) and flagged
- Downstream queue: `downstream(c) = final_start(c) − join_ready(c)` (a queue only if the model assumptions hold; see §7)
- Final service: `final(c) = final_end(c) − final_start(c)`
- Total flow time: `T(c) = final_end(c) − arrival(c)`

**Additive identity (no double counting)**

`T(c) = upstream(c) + forkjoin(c) + downstream(c) + final(c)`

This holds exactly for all 12 cases (verified in the code, §6).

**Why summing synchronization waits would double count.** The three branches run on the same wall clock in parallel. The elapsed fork-join time is already fully accounted for by the *critical* branch alone: `forkjoin(c) = queue_crit(c) + service_crit(c)`, and `sync_crit(c) = 0`. Every non-critical branch satisfies

`queue_i(c) + service_i(c) + sync_i(c) = forkjoin(c)` for each i,

i.e., each branch's queue + service + sync is a *different decomposition of the same elapsed interval* `[fork_release, join_ready]`. Adding `sync_A + sync_B + sync_C` to the stage time (or summing branch durations across branches) would count that interval up to three times. Synchronization waits are therefore reported as per-branch diagnostic measures of slack (how much a branch could slow down without moving `join_ready`), never as additive stage components. For example, in F07 the elapsed stage is 75 min, but queue+service+sync sums to 75 for each of the three branches separately (A: 0+50+25, B: 30+45+0, C: 5+18+52) — a naïve sum would produce 225 min of "time" inside a 75-minute window.

**Downstream sub-split (used in §4).** Because the final-station resource is logged, `downstream(c)` can be split further:

- resource blocking: `block(c) = max(0, prev_release_r(c) − join_ready(c))`, where `prev_release_r(c)` is when case c's assigned resource (F1 or F2) finished its previous job;
- residual gap: `resid(c) = downstream(c) − block(c)` — waiting not explained by the two logged resources (handoff, batching, or a release policy; see §7).

# 3. Aggregate critical-path analysis

| branch | mean queue | max queue | mean service | max service | mean queue+service | max queue+service | critical freq. | mean sync wait |
|:--:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 0.0 | 0 | 50.0 | 50 | 50.0 | 50 | 2 / 12 | 11.0 |
| B | 19.2 | 30 | 37.5 | 45 | 56.7 | 75 | **8 / 12** | 4.3 |
| C | 10.4 | 35 | 20.2 | 30 | 30.6 | 57 | 2 / 12 | 30.4 |

Fork-join elapsed time: mean 61.0 min, min 50 (F01, F11 — the A-floor), max 75 (F07, F12 — both B-driven). Downstream queue: total 176 min (mean 14.7/case), of which 73 min is attributable to F1/F2 being busy and 103 min is a residual gap of 8–15 min that appears even when the assigned resource is idle.

**Influence ranking (with reasoning):**

1. **B (Machine Stress Test) — most influential.** B is the last-completing branch in 8 of 12 cases, including *both* tail cases (fork-join = 75 in F07 and F12). Although B's mean service (37.5) is below A's (50), its queue+service mean (56.7) exceeds A's 50, and its queue+service is highly variable (35–75). Influence on `join_ready` comes from queue+service at the max operator, not service alone — B dominates precisely because its queue (mean 19.2, up to 30) stacks on top of a mid-sized, variable service.
2. **A (Laboratory Assay) — structural second, near-tie in a specific sense.** A has the largest mean service and zero queue, giving a *deterministic* 50-minute completion offset from fork release. A is critical only twice, but it is always the runner-up when B is critical (mean A sync is only 11 min). This is an honest near-tie for "what limits further improvement": once B's queue is reduced (§4, CF2), A immediately becomes the binding constraint in most cases, capping the achievable fork-join stage at 50 min. B controls today's tail; A controls tomorrow's floor.
3. **C (Documentation Review) — least influential but not negligible.** C is critical in 2 of 12 cases (F04, F08), and only when its queue spikes (35 and 25 min) coincide with elevated service (22 and 30). Its mean sync wait of 30.4 min says C usually finishes far ahead of the join. C is a variance problem (occasional administrative backlog), not a mean problem.

A final, non-branch factor deserves equal billing: the **final station adds 14.7 min/case of waiting on average** — comparable to the entire benefit obtainable from fixing B — and in 4 cases (F05, F08, F09, F11) waiting is partly or wholly caused by F1/F2 being busy, which masks branch improvements (demonstrated concretely in §4).

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

Method: `join_ready` is recomputed under each intervention; the final station is then *replayed* keeping the observed F1/F2 assignment and order, with `final_start = max(new join_ready, time the assigned resource frees up) + residual_gap`, where the residual gap (the unexplained 0–15 min handoff/policy delay per case) is conservatively held constant. This replay reproduces the observed schedule exactly under no intervention, so all savings are attributable to the intervention. (Assumption, labeled: the residual gap is a fixed per-case overhead independent of timing; needed because its cause is unidentifiable from the log — see §7.)

**CF1: Reduce A service by 20% (50 → 40 min).**

- Direct effect on `join_ready`: only in the 2 cases where A is critical — F01 (−5 min, B becomes critical) and F11 (−10 min, join drops to 11:20, creating an A/B tie). Total join saving: 15 min.
- Zero-benefit cases: the other 10 cases save nothing because B or C remains critical; A merely gains slack (its sync wait grows).
- Flow-time effect after downstream replay: **5 min total (0.42 min/case)**. F01 keeps its 5 min; F11's 10 min is *entirely absorbed* because F1 is busy until 11:50 regardless. This is the clearest demonstration that downstream final-station congestion masks branch improvements.
- Secondary effects: none positive at this load; freeing lab capacity 10 min/case could matter at higher volume, but nothing in this log shows an A queue to relieve.

**CF2: Reduce B queue delay by 50%.**

- Direct effect: B's completion moves earlier by half its queue in the 8 B-critical cases, but the saving is capped where A becomes the new critical branch (e.g., F02: B would finish 09:15, exactly tying A; F06: new join ties A at 10:15). Join savings per case: F02 10, F03 10, F05 10, F06 15, F07 15, F09 12.5, F10 5, F12 15 — total 92.5 min. (The half-minute in F09 breaches the 1-minute granularity; rounding to 12 or 13 changes nothing material.)
- Zero-*join*-benefit cases: F01, F04, F08, F11 (B not critical).
- Flow-time effect after replay: **122 min total (10.2 min/case)** — *larger* than the join saving because earlier joins let F1/F2 finish earlier, which cascades to unblock later cases: F04, F08, F10 each gain 8 min and F11 gains 15 min *without any change to their own join_ready*. Every case except F01 benefits.
- Secondary effects: A becomes critical in most cases afterward (critical frequency shifts from B:8 → roughly A-dominant with ties), so further B improvement is nearly worthless — the next constraint is A's 50-minute service. Also, faster joins increase the arrival rate at F; at ~57% observed F utilization this is absorbable, but it should be checked.

**CF3: Add final-station capacity (third resource F3, greedy assignment, residual gaps kept).**

- Direct effect: eliminates resource blocking (the 73-min component). Flow savings: F04 8, F05 10, F08 15, F09 15, F10 5, F11 20 — **73 min total (6.1 min/case)**.
- Zero-benefit cases: F01, F02, F03, F06, F07, F12 — their downstream waiting is entirely the residual gap, which more capacity cannot touch. `join_ready` is untouched by construction.
- Secondary effects: F utilization drops from ~57% (240 service min over two resources across the 09:08–12:40 span) to ~38% over three — expensive idle capacity.
- **Cheaper variant worth comparing:** keep two resources but eliminate the residual gap (i.e., fix the handoff/release policy so F starts the moment a case is ready and a resource is free). Replayed savings: **171 min total (14.25 min/case)** — 97% of all downstream waiting — at zero capacity cost. The data (strict F1/F2 alternation; recurring 10–15 min gaps while a resource sits idle) suggests policy, not capacity, is the real downstream problem.

Ranking of interventions by verified flow-time impact: policy fix at F (171) > B queue halving (122) > third F resource (73) > A acceleration (5).

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

**Redesign 1: Remove the final-station release policy / handoff delay (start F as soon as join_ready and a free resource coincide).**

- Throughput: modest gain; F was never saturated, so throughput is limited upstream. The gain is almost purely flow time (~14 min/case, §4).
- Flow-time tail: shrinks the tail uniformly; F07 (130 min) would drop to ~115.
- Resource utilization: F1/F2 utilization is unchanged in minutes but becomes burstier; no new hires needed.
- Possible new bottlenecks: if the 8–15 min gap is actually mandatory work (result transfer, mandatory cooling/verification time before release decisions), removing it is impossible or unsafe — this is the key risk, because the log cannot distinguish policy from necessity (§7).
- Synchronization effects: none on the fork-join itself; joins are upstream of the change.
- Pilot/measure first: instrument what happens between `join_ready` and `final_start` (log a "ready-for-decision" event and resource idle time); interview F1/F2 about the alternating assignment rule; run the policy change on a sample of cases and compare `final_start − join_ready` before/after.

**Redesign 2: Halve B's queue (e.g., stagger machine-test slots, add setup crew coverage, or reserve test-rig time at fork release), combined with monitoring A as the emerging constraint.**

- Throughput: helps if the stress-test rig is a shared, contended asset; the 19-min mean queue implies contention today.
- Flow-time tail: the strongest branch-side lever — removes B from the tail (F07/F12 drop by 15 min each) and cascades downstream (122 min total, §4).
- Resource utilization: B resources see the same work, differently timed; watch for idle slivers if slots are reserved but branches release late.
- Possible new bottlenecks: A becomes the critical branch in most cases; after this change, A's fixed 50-minute service is the binding floor, so the *next* investment should target A (CF1 shows A acceleration is worthless *before* B is fixed but becomes the main lever *after*).
- Synchronization effects: sync waits redistribute — B's slack grows, A's shrinks to near zero; the join becomes more deterministic (less variance in `join_ready`), which itself smooths the final station.
- Pilot/measure first: measure B's queue cause (rig busy with other product lines? operator availability?) since the log shows only when B starts, not why it waited; pilot slot reservation on a subset and track `B_start − fork_release` and the critical-branch frequency weekly.

Explicitly *not* recommended as a first move: adding a third F resource (dominated by the policy fix) and accelerating A in isolation (5 min total benefit while B and downstream waiting persist).

# 6. Reproducible Python

```python
import io
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Construct the supplied table in memory
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
RAW = """case,arrival,fork_release,A_start,A_end,B_start,B_end,C_start,C_end,final_start,final_end,final_resource
F01,07:55,08:10,08:10,09:00,08:15,08:55,08:20,08:38,09:08,09:28,F1

F09,09:50,10:10,10:10,11:00,10:35,11:15,10:15,10:35,11:30,11:50,F1
F10,10:05,10:25,10:25,11:15,10:45,11:20,10:30,10:50,11:40,12:00,F2
F11,10:20,10:40,10:40,11:30,10:50,11:20,10:45,11:03,11:50,12:10,F1
F12,10:35,10:55,10:55,11:45,11:25,12:10,11:00,11:18,12:20,12:40,F2
"""
df = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(RAW))
TS = ["arrival", "fork_release", "A_start", "A_end", "B_start", "B_end",
      "C_start", "C_end", "final_start", "final_end"]
for c in TS:
    df[c] = pd.to_datetime("2026-05-19 " + df[c])

def mins(td):
    return td.dt.total_seconds() / 60 if hasattr(td, "dt") else td.total_seconds() / 60

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Decomposition components (minutes). These are CUSTOM pandas
#    calculations: join_ready, per-branch synchronization waits and the
#    critical branch require max()-based fork-join logic over parallel
#    intervals. Standard directly-follows performance metrics aggregate
#    time between completion events of ordered activity pairs and cannot
#    recover synchronization waiting: a non-critical branch's wait lies
#    on no directly-follows edge -- it overlaps, in parallel wall-clock
#    time, with the critical branch's service.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
BR = ["A", "B", "C"]
out = pd.DataFrame({"case": df["case"]})
out["upstream_elapsed"] = mins(df["fork_release"] - df["arrival"])
for b in BR:
    out[f"{b}_queue"]   = mins(df[f"{b}_start"] - df["fork_release"])
    out[f"{b}_service"] = mins(df[f"{b}_end"]   - df[f"{b}_start"])

df["join_ready"] = df[["A_end", "B_end", "C_end"]].max(axis=1)  # AND-join rule
out["join_ready"] = df["join_ready"].dt.strftime("%H:%M")

for b in BR:  # sync waits: parallel-clock diagnostics, NOT additive components
    out[f"{b}_sync"] = mins(df["join_ready"] - df[f"{b}_end"])

# critical branch: argmax of branch completion; deterministic tie handling:
# numpy argmax returns the FIRST maximum, i.e. alphabetical A < B < C wins,
# and a boolean flag marks genuine ties for honest reporting.
ends = df[["A_end", "B_end", "C_end"]].to_numpy()
out["critical_branch"] = [BR[i] for i in ends.argmax(axis=1)]
out["tie"] = (ends == ends.max(axis=1, keepdims=True)).sum(axis=1) > 1

out["forkjoin_elapsed"] = mins(df["join_ready"] - df["fork_release"])
out["downstream_queue"] = mins(df["final_start"] - df["join_ready"])
out["final_service"]    = mins(df["final_end"] - df["final_start"])
out["total_flow_time"]  = mins(df["final_end"] - df["arrival"])

# additivity check -- proves the four stage components do not double count
chk = (out["upstream_elapsed"] + out["forkjoin_elapsed"]
       + out["downstream_queue"] + out["final_service"])
assert (chk == out["total_flow_time"]).all(), "decomposition not additive!"

# split downstream waiting: blocking by the assigned resource vs residual gap
df["prev_release"] = df.groupby("final_resource")["final_end"].shift(1)
blocked = (df["prev_release"] - df["join_ready"]).clip(lower=pd.Timedelta(0)).fillna(pd.Timedelta(0))
out["resource_blocking"] = mins(blocked)
out["residual_gap"] = out["downstream_queue"] - out["resource_blocking"]

pd.set_option("display.width", 250)
print("=== Case-level decomposition (minutes) ===")
print(out.to_string(index=False))

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Aggregate branch comparison
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
agg = pd.DataFrame({
    "mean_queue":    [out[f"{b}_queue"].mean() for b in BR],
    "max_queue":     [out[f"{b}_queue"].max() for b in BR],
    "mean_service":  [out[f"{b}_service"].mean() for b in BR],
    "max_service":   [out[f"{b}_service"].max() for b in BR],
    "mean_q+s":      [(out[f"{b}_queue"] + out[f"{b}_service"]).mean() for b in BR],
    "max_q+s":       [(out[f"{b}_queue"] + out[f"{b}_service"]).max() for b in BR],
    "critical_freq": [(out["critical_branch"] == b).sum() for b in BR],
    "mean_sync":     [out[f"{b}_sync"].mean() for b in BR],
}, index=BR).round(2)
print("\n=== Branch comparison ===")
print(agg.to_string())
print("\nfork-join elapsed: mean %.1f, min %.0f, max %.0f"
      % (out["forkjoin_elapsed"].mean(), out["forkjoin_elapsed"].min(),
         out["forkjoin_elapsed"].max()))
print("downstream queue: total %.0f (resource blocking %.0f, residual %.0f)"
      % (out["downstream_queue"].sum(), out["resource_blocking"].sum(),
         out["residual_gap"].sum()))

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Counterfactual replay engine.
#    final_start = max(new join_ready, assigned resource freed) + residual_gap
#    Residual gap held constant (assumption: fixed per-case handoff/policy
#    overhead). Replaying the base scenario reproduces observed times
#    exactly, so all reported savings are attributable to the intervention.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
def replay_final(join_new, keep_assignment=True, n_resources=2, residual=None):
    res_gap = out["residual_gap"].to_numpy() if residual is None else residual
    service = out["final_service"].to_numpy()
    if keep_assignment:  # observed F1/F2 assignment and order preserved
        free, starts, ends_ = {}, [], []
        for i in range(len(df)):
            r = df["final_resource"].iloc[i]
            base = max(join_new.iloc[i], free.get(r, join_new.iloc[i]))
            s = base + pd.Timedelta(minutes=res_gap[i])
            e = s + pd.Timedelta(minutes=service[i])
            free[r] = e
            starts.append(s); ends_.append(e)
        return pd.Series(starts, index=df.index), pd.Series(ends_, index=df.index)
    # greedy earliest-available assignment over n_resources, ordered by join
    order = np.argsort(join_new.to_numpy())
    free = [pd.Timestamp("2026-05-19 00:00")] * n_resources
    starts, ends_ = [None] * len(df), [None] * len(df)
    for i in order:
        k = int(np.argmin(free))
        base = max(join_new.iloc[i], free[k])
        s = base + pd.Timedelta(minutes=res_gap[i])
        e = s + pd.Timedelta(minutes=service[i])
        free[k] = e
        starts[i], ends_[i] = s, e
    return pd.Series(starts, index=df.index), pd.Series(ends_, index=df.index)

s0, e0 = replay_final(df["join_ready"])  # sanity: must reproduce the log
assert (s0 == df["final_start"]).all() and (e0 == df["final_end"]).all()

def summarize(name, join_new, **kw):
    s, e = replay_final(join_new, **kw)
    save_join = mins(df["join_ready"] - join_new)
    save_flow = mins(df["final_end"] - e)
    res = pd.DataFrame({"case": df["case"],
                        "join_saving": save_join.round(1),
                        "flow_saving": save_flow.round(1)})
    print(f"\n=== Counterfactual: {name} ===")
    print(res.to_string(index=False))
    print("total join saving %.1f | total flow saving %.1f | mean %.2f min/case"
          % (save_join.sum(), save_flow.sum(), save_flow.mean()))
    return res

# CF1: A service -20% (50 -> 40 min)
A_end_new = df["A_start"] + (df["A_end"] - df["A_start"]) * 0.8
summarize("A service -20%",
          pd.concat([A_end_new, df["B_end"], df["C_end"]], axis=1).max(axis=1))

# CF2: B queue -50% (B start, hence B end, moves earlier by half its queue)
B_shift = (df["B_start"] - df["fork_release"]) / 2
summarize("B queue -50%",
          pd.concat([df["A_end"], df["B_end"] - B_shift, df["C_end"]], axis=1).max(axis=1))

# CF3a: third final-station resource (greedy assignment), residual gaps kept
summarize("third final resource", df["join_ready"],
          keep_assignment=False, n_resources=3)

# CF3b: two resources but the residual (policy/handoff) gap removed
summarize("2 resources, residual gap removed", df["join_ready"],
          residual=np.zeros(len(df)))

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. PM4Py interval-event log: one row per activity instance with a
#    start_timestamp and a completion time:timestamp.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
rows = []
for _, r in df.iterrows():
    rows += [
        dict(case=r["case"], activity="Preparation",            start=r["arrival"],     end=r["fork_release"]),
        dict(case=r["case"], activity="A_Laboratory_Assay",     start=r["A_start"],     end=r["A_end"]),
        dict(case=r["case"], activity="B_Machine_Stress_Test",  start=r["B_start"],     end=r["B_end"]),
        dict(case=r["case"], activity="C_Documentation_Review", start=r["C_start"],     end=r["C_end"]),
        dict(case=r["case"], activity="F_Release_Decision",     start=r["final_start"], end=r["final_end"]),
    ]
ev = pd.DataFrame(rows).rename(columns={"start": "start_timestamp",
                                        "end": "time:timestamp"})
ev = pm4py.format_dataframe(ev, case_id="case", activity_key="activity",
                            timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
                            start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")

# get_service_time correctly recovers per-activity service from intervals ...
svc = pm4py.get_service_time(ev, aggregation_measure="mean",
                             start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")
print("\n=== PM4Py mean service time per activity ===")
for k, v in sorted(svc.items()):
    print(f"  {k}: {v/60:.1f} min")

# ... but the performance DFG measures time between COMPLETIONS of ordered
# pairs, which is meaningless inside a parallel block (it even yields
# negative means here, because branch completions interleave). This is why
# join_ready, sync waits, critical branches and the downstream split are
# computed with custom pandas max()/shift() logic above rather than taken
# from directly-follows performance.
dfg_perf, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(ev)
print("\n=== PM4Py performance DFG (mean min between completions) ===")
for (a, b), m in sorted(dfg_perf.items()):
    print(f"  {a} -> {b}: {m['mean']/60:.1f} min")
```

The script runs end-to-end (pandas 3.x, PM4Py 2.7.x); both assertions pass, confirming additivity and that the replay reproduces the observed schedule. The PM4Py performance DFG indeed reports negative mean durations between parallel branch completions (e.g., A → B ≈ −43 min), empirically confirming the comment that directly-follows semantics cannot recover fork-join waiting.

# 7. Limits of inference

- **Causality.** All decompositions are accounting identities on timestamps, not causal claims. The counterfactuals assume interventions shift completions without changing anything else (no behavioral response, no resequencing upstream, residual gaps invariant). The replay model is one defensible reconstruction; the true scheduling rule at F is unknown.
- **`join_ready` is derived, not observed.** It equals the latest branch completion *only if* the stated AND-join rule holds. If F may in fact start with pending documentation (C) under some override, both the downstream queue and C's criticality in F04/F08 are misstated.
- **Branch resource capacity is invisible.** We see queue waits but not why: B's queue could be one shared rig, operator shifts, or priority rules; C's two queue spikes could be one absent clerk. Halving B's queue (CF2) is stated as an outcome, not a mechanism — the log cannot tell us what mechanism achieves it or what it costs.
- **Work preemption and multitasking.** `start`/`end` pairs are treated as continuous service. If work was interrupted (lunch, preemption by other cases), "service" mixes touch time with hidden waiting, and the 50-minute A constant would suggest a scheduled slot rather than measured effort — indistinguishable here.
- **Queue vs. release policy at F.** The residual 103 minutes of downstream waiting occurs largely while a logged resource is idle, and cases alternate strictly F1/F2. This is consistent with (a) a batching/release policy, (b) mandatory post-join handoff work, or (c) an unlogged shared prerequisite (e.g., a supervisor sign-off). Options (a)–(c) have very different remedies; the timestamps cannot separate them, which is why §5 recommends instrumenting the join-to-start interval before acting.
- **Sample size and stationarity.** Twelve cases from a single morning, with suspiciously regular arrivals (15-minute spacing) and constant upstream (20 min) and final service (20 min). Means, maxima and critical-branch frequencies are descriptive of this window only; tail statements (e.g., "B drives the 75-minute stages") rest on two observations each and should not be extrapolated without more data.
- **One-minute granularity.** Waits of 0 may be up to ±1 min; the CF2 half-minute artifact (12.5 min) exceeds the log's precision and any conclusion depending on sub-minute differences is unsupported.
