## 2. Rule Interpretation

All rules use the normalized human. H(a,c) is the set of humans performing activity a in case c.

- **RR1:** H(Draft,c)  H(Approve,c) = . It applies to every risk level and to emergencies. No delegation or emergency waives it.
- **RR2:** H(Implement,c)  H(Verify,c) = .
  - An unresolved service-account verifier gives a result of "not established". It is neither a pass nor a fail.
  - I test the adverse candidate as well.
  - No rule forbids one person approving and verifying (CHG-509). I record it as a control-objective concern only.
- **RR3 (HIGH, non-emergency):** the approver must hold standing QM at time t, or be a delegate d who meets all of these:
  - t falls inside [start, end];
  - the scope and activity are covered;
  - the principal is absent or unavailable at t if the delegation requires it;
  - no prohibited subdelegation occurred;
  - a delegation instrument existed at t;
  - the delegate personally acted.
- **RR4 (HIGH, emergency):**
  - Approval comes from the IC or a valid IC delegate, using the same validity test plus the incident link.
  - A QM or valid QM delegate must do Retrospective_QM_Review within two business days.
  - The emergency flag and safety rationale must be credible. This is a manual judgment, with the flag as a necessary but not sufficient signal.
  - RR1 and RR2 still apply. Delegate scope is read strictly (an "approval" delegation does not automatically cover a review), and strictness is flagged for manual override.
- **RR5:** Normalize aliases before SoD checks. A service account supports conformance only with reliable session mapping to an authorized human.
- **RR6:** Repeated pairs, reciprocity, shared workstations and concentrated handovers are indicators only. They are never a violation by themselves.

**Business days (RR4).**
- A business day is Monday to Friday by UTC calendar date. The site calendar shows no holidays.
- The clock starts at the Implement_Change timestamp. The implementation day is day 0.
- The deadline is 23:59:59 UTC on the second business day after day 0.
- For an implementation on Tuesday 08-11, the deadline is Thursday 08-13. Friday 08-14 is business day 3. Saturday 08-15 and Sunday 08-16 are not counted.
- Sensitivity check: 48 elapsed hours from 13:10 on 08-11 also ends before 08-14 16:00. The CHG-505 lateness is robust to the reading.

**Action by a principal on leave.**
- Leave is an availability fact. It does not revoke standing role authority, so Asha's act is authority-valid if it was really Asha.
- It raises an authentication question, because it is an anomalous use.
- Delegation absence is judged per event. A principal demonstrably active at the moment (Asha's VPN session) means a delegate could not rely on the absence condition during that window.
- A delegate's sick leave does not revoke the delegation, but it makes account use anomalous. Delegations are personal and non-subdelegable. If the delegate did not act, the delegation was never validly exercised.

**Can a later DB entry prove an earlier delegation?** Not by itself. I separate validity time (the effective interval) from evidence time (when it was recorded).
- A later record is admissible as corroboration of a pre-existing instrument. The instrument itself needs a verified signature and timestamp, an e-mail or DMS audit trail, or confirmation by the principal.
- A later record alone cannot establish pre-event existence.
- Applied here:
  - For CHG-501, the DB record postdates the event by about 24 hours, so the PDF carries the weight.
  - For CHG-503 and CHG-506, the record (09:15) precedes the events, which is stronger but still derived from the PDF.

## 6. Control Improvements

- **Delegation creation.** Use a workflow with principal e-signature, a cryptographic timestamp, an explicit scope, and required fields for incident ID, activities allowed and subdelegation flag. Block approvals that lack an active, registered delegation. Delegations must be machine-readable and versioned.
- **Effective-time recording.** Record three separate timestamps: signed, entered and effective. Migration or backfill entries must keep the original timestamp and be marked as backfilled. Never overwrite history.
- **Principal availability.** Integrate the HR leave calendar and on-call status, and require an explicit "unavailable" attestation at the time of use. Log weekend and holiday coverage. Auto-suspend the delegate's authority while the principal is actively logged in.
- **Alias management.** Give one canonical human ID per person and disable legacy aliases after migration. Run a periodic alias-collision report.
- **Individual authentication.** Retire shared service accounts for human actions. Require per-action re-authentication (FIDO or badge plus PIN) for verification and approval. Block approvals from accounts on certified leave.
- **Emergency review.** Make the review clock visible, add escalation before the deadline, and require QM-side coverage plans. Define explicitly whether cooling-system and review activities are within delegate scope. Require an incident ID on every emergency change.
- **Pair rotation.** Rotate drafter–approver pairs or add periodic second review where one pair dominates. Review cells with thin QM coverage.
- **Network monitoring.** Produce monthly handover and working-together reports against a baseline. Treat them as triage indicators, not findings.
- **Evidential retention.** Retain VPN, badge, FIDO, workstation and delegation-document records for a defined period, tied to case IDs. Preserve CCTV where safety-relevant. Keep the delegation audit trail immutable.
