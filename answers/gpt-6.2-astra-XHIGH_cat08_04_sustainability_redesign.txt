# 1. System boundary and assumptions

Use **one complete, conforming order delivered against its original promise** as the unit of service. Count all resources spent serving the order, not just its successful final path. For portfolio intensity, divide the cohort's total burden, including failed attempts and cancellations, by the number of complete, conforming deliveries. Also report absolute totals, burden per received order, and per good line or item within comparable part families. Fewer deliveries or a shift to smaller orders is not an environmental saving.

Include picking, CNC setup and production, required heat treatment, inspection, packaging and repacking, all shipment legs, and linked returns, replacements and disposal. Attribute batch energy and unshipped output to the production cohort; include empty vehicle mileage where it is not already in the carrier estimate. Track purchased material and packaging burdens where factors exist. The log measures operational energy, mass and estimated freight emissions, **not** a complete lifecycle footprint. Keep kWh, kg of material by type and kgCO2e separate. Convert electricity and materials only with documented site/time and material-specific factors, with uncertainty ranges. Capital equipment, customer use and the environmental consequences of customer downtime are outside the quantified boundary; downtime remains a service constraint, not zero harm.

The flattened order log cannot establish which line was urgent, how a shared batch was metered, or whether a first parcel completed the order. Link order lines, batches, shipments, inventory lots and returns before claiming net savings. DFG arcs are event counts, not unique orders or a balanced flow: 11,200 closes plus 530 cancellation ends do not reconcile to 12,000 starts. Check observation windows, missing events and open cases rather than treating the gap as success or failure. Selected variant averages are comparisons, not causal effects.

# 2. Sustainability-related process indicators

| Indicator | Definition and diagnostic cut |
|---|---|
| Production electricity | Sum metered `energy_kwh` once per operation/batch, allocate shared runs by processing time or comparable units, and reconcile to meter totals. Report kWh/good item by `machine_group`, part family and `batch_size`, including repeat CNC/heat cycles. |
| Freight | Sum `transport_kgco2e` across outbound, split, reverse and replacement legs; allocate a shared trip once across its loads. Track legs/order, mode, load factor, empty km and `expedite_flag` by lane and segment. |
| Materials and quality losses | Separate `material_scrap_kg` from `package_material_kg`, and original from repeat consumption. Track first-pass inspection yield, repeat passes, repacks, and damage/defect returns per delivered line over a fixed follow-up window. |
| Batching and inventory | By `batch_id`, machine and batch size: wait from ready to start, setup energy/good item, fill against firm orders, share of units not shipped at 14 days, eventual sale or write-off. The 14-day signal is exposure, not proof of disposal. |
| Service | By `case_type` × `customer_type`: receipt-to-first and receipt-to-complete delivery, median/p90, on-time-in-full against `promised_lead_time_hours`, late and delay-cancellation rates. For Critical orders also time to receipt of the line that restores operation. |
| Resilience and exceptions | Service-contract share with a tested, independently available fallback route; capacity and time to switch during disruption; SP1/SP2/Q1 compliance and overrides by reason. Track inspection queues and spare production capacity as early warnings. |

# 3. Intervention options

| Change and target | Decision, environmental mechanism and safeguard | Evidence to monitor |
|---|---|---|
| **S1 + S4: priority pick and route choice**, Standard-stock Service-contract | At availability check, reserve a pick slot and estimate end-to-end arrival by route using remaining promise slack, pick/pack queues, cutoff and lane reliability. Prefer qualifying ground to routine air; retain air if needed to meet the contractual service target. Priority picking must not simply make Regular orders late. | Log options and probabilities at decision time, actual pick completion, missed cutoffs, air avoided, transport emissions and lateness in **both** stock segments. SP1 currently fails in 22% of stock contract cases. |
| **S5: twice-daily consolidation**, non-urgent stock | Offer the next truck only if its actual departure, load and transit distribution fit the promise. Book a ground fallback before its cutoff if the truck is unlikely to run. A lightly loaded extra truck need not save freight emissions. | Truck wait, fill, km, route-level kgCO2e/order, missed departures and fallback mode. |
| **S11: line-aware availability check**, stock partial/backorder | Reserve available lines and obtain a credible date for missing lines. Ship together when the customer can use the order on that date; split only the line needed sooner when waiting would breach a promise or prolong downtime. Avoid a second parcel and pack, not the recording of a backorder. | Compare first useful and complete delivery, parcels/order, repeated picks, backorder cancellations and customer acceptance. The partial/ground variant is 79h median, 24% late and 7.2 kgCO2e, versus 34h, 8% and 3.6 on stock/standard/ground. |
| **S2: capped batching**, scheduled MTO CNC and heat | Batch compatible, firm-demand work. Set a latest release for each order by subtracting a conservative remaining CNC/heat, inspection, packing and transit time from its due time. Release when the efficient load is reached **or** the earliest release deadline arrives; give tight contract orders shorter caps and true downtime an immediate path. Never manufacture speculative units just to fill a batch. | Batch-ready/start times, kWh/good item, waits at both machines, downstream expedites, late orders and unshipped units. Heat is most efficient above 10 items but heat-batch waiting is associated with 1.7× late risk. |
| **S3: queue-aware CNC assignment**, MTO | Prefer qualified CNC_A for rework-prone part families if its queue and all required downstream operations fit. CNC_B is faster and remains available for tight slack and outages; do not bypass a required heat cycle to make a route look better. | Compare like geometry, specifications and batch sizes: A/B rework (observed 9%/18%), incremental kWh per accepted item, scrap, queue p90 and remaining qualified capacity. |
| **S7: targeted first-piece/in-process inspection**, high-risk machine/part pairs | Check setup and the first piece before repeating a suspect run; stop and correct the source of defects. Reserve inspection capacity for these checks rather than adding a universal final queue. Early detection can raise recorded rejects while reducing later rework and returns. | `inspection_cell` wait, first-pass yield, inspection → rework → CNC repeats, discarded kg and downstream defect escapes. The CNC_B/rework/ground variant averages 32 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap and 32% late. |
| **S6: route/geometry check before packing**, especially heavy MTO | Choose the route, then validate the pack against weight, geometry and handling; revalidate on route change. Enforce Q1 at line level. Do not remove reinforcement from heavy parts merely to cut packaging mass: it doubles pack material but reduces damage returns by 40% in the evidence. | Pack choice and weight, low-risk certificate, package → rework loops (260 from standard, 360 from reinforced), material per intact delivery and damage by route. |
| **S8 + S9: triage emergency transport**, Critical-downtime | Verify which line is stopping equipment, the latest useful arrival and whether stock can cover it. For precautionary demand use reliable scheduled service; use a shared emergency lane when it meets the recovery deadline. Reserve a justified dedicated van for genuine downtime when alternatives are too slow or unreliable; retain independent backup capacity. | Urgency evidence, customer-confirmed recovery deadline, dispatch-to-useful-delivery p90, shared-lane reliability, deadhead km and SP2 justification. The dedicated emergency path is 14h/3% late versus 23h/9% late on the stock/ground exception; these are different cases. |
| **S10: voluntary promise choices**, Regular MTO | Before commitment, offer a credible date aligned with an existing production run and ground departure alongside the faster feasible option. Use only customer-accepted flexibility; never reset the clock on late work. This can avoid small runs and rescue air. | Requested, offered, accepted and original committed dates; take-up, refusals/cancellations, batch utilization, expedites and actual elapsed time. |
| **S12: decision and exception log**, all policy-sensitive paths | Capture options, predicted arrival, decision, reason, authorizer and expiry at the decision, with rapid emergency authorization. This has no direct physical saving; it makes avoidable premium freight and preventable damage identifiable without removing contingency routes. | Link each exception to decision context and outcome; distinguish absent evidence from a demonstrably bad decision. SP2 justification is missing in 31% of dedicated-van cases. |

# 5. Rebound and displacement risks

- **Batching → freight:** CNC savings disappear if waiting forces air or emergency production. Give CNC and heat a shared end-to-end time budget, not independent allowances that consume the same slack twice.
- **Fill → obsolescence:** 6% of batch-produced items can remain unshipped at 14 days under forecast error. Tie releases to firm demand and follow aged units through sale or disposal; do not allocate unused units' energy away from the comparison.
- **Consolidation → extra trips:** a missed departure can mean a lightly loaded truck *and* a rescue parcel. Count both, including return mileage; check contracted carriers have not just moved the empty leg elsewhere.
- **Light packing → damage:** packaging mass saved on an outbound leg can cost an entire replacement part, another production run and two freight legs. Compare intact deliveries after the return window, not kg of pack at dispatch.
- **Rework → another station or customer:** shifting CNC_B work to A can congest A; extra checks can rush other inspections. Watch both queues, repeat energy and field returns, not only the new gate's rejection rate.
- **Priority → displaced lateness:** faster contract picking may consume the last ground cutoff for Regular orders or induce their cancellation. Audit all segments and total air trips on the same shift.
- **Customer behavior → off-log demand:** customers may flag precautionary orders as downtime, split orders themselves or buy expedited replacements elsewhere after a longer quote. Check repeat orders, urgency mix, lost quotes and customer feedback; retain a real fast option.
- **Pooling → fragile service:** declining dedicated volume may cost standby capacity precisely when a shared carrier fails. Reserve and periodically exercise an independent alternative; count its cost and any repositioning emissions.

# 6. Balanced recommendation

Start with **priority pick plus slack-tested ground for contract stock, line-level availability, and route-compatible packaging**. These fix decisions made too late in today's process. Use the next consolidated truck for slack-rich stock, not as a required hold. At order receipt, record the original promise and the needed line; at dispatch, update the arrival prediction. SP1's >85% test is a policy floor, not permission to worsen a segment currently only 5% late. If the greener service cannot satisfy a stricter commitment, protect the commitment and record the exception.

Next pilot **capped firm-demand batching, selective CNC_A routing and targeted inspection together** for MTO. This segment is 34% of orders but accounts for roughly 87% of order-attributed energy using the segment shares and means. A larger clean batch is valuable only if it yields shipped, conforming parts without rescue air. Give Regular customers a genuine promise choice; reserve capacity on CNC_B and in inspection for urgent work. For Critical service, prioritize time to restore operation, then minimize freight within that constraint. The 52 kgCO2e dedicated variant merits review, not abolition. Do not retire a carrier or machine on the strength of a normal-week pilot.

# 7. Validation and governance plan

1. **Establish a trustworthy baseline.** Reconcile timestamps, promises, open/cancelled cases and DFG coverage. Link line weights/geometry, batch meters, stock ageing, inspection certificates, shipment legs and returns; record carrier, available alternatives, load and departure cutoffs. Deduplicate batch/trip totals; report missingness, estimated empty mileage and sensitivity to allocation and emissions factors. Use directly observed variants and decision points: low discovered-model precision during disruptions is not evidence that every model-allowed path is appropriate.
2. **Test decisions before dispatch.** Replay historical decisions using only information available then; check predicted versus realized on-time probabilities by lane, segment and disruption state on held-out periods. Shadow the recommender, then stagger adoption by comparable lanes, shifts or part families with concurrent controls. Adjust comparisons for case mix, urgency, weight, load, season and carrier disruption. Test fallback switches and queue surges separately; normal-period averages do not validate resilience.
3. **Check policy at each eligible decision.** For **SP1**, reconstruct whether ground or truck had >85% on-time probability; report eligible decisions, actual use, documented exceptions, unexplained breaches and unknowns. For **SP2**, require an explicit, linked Critical-downtime justification at van dispatch. For **Q1**, check each MTO line over 8 kg for reinforced pack or prior inspection certification of low damage risk; a free-text override is not certification. For **R1**, verify at least one usable independent alternative for each Service-contract lane during disruption, including booking capacity and a tested switch time. A recorded waiver is not automatic compliance.
4. **Set gates and owners before rollout.** Operations and account owners agree non-inferiority margins by segment for late delivery, p90 complete delivery and cancellation against baseline and contract limits. Use the Critical baseline (15h median, 30h p90, 4% late) separately, with a hard check on each true downtime deadline. Quality owns first-pass yield and mature return/damage rates; logistics owns R1 and dispatch exceptions; production owns queues, scrap and aged stock. Compare pilot/control differences with uncertainty intervals, including absolute resource totals and per-good-delivery intensity. Extend observation for rare disruptions, returns and write-offs rather than declaring success from a small sample.
5. **Act on exceptions.** Alert on a missed critical deadline, Q1 failure, unavailable fallback or emerging queue; switch affected orders to the qualified fallback and pause the offending rule. Review overrides and missing evidence weekly across operations, quality and customer service; assign corrective actions with an owner and expiry. Scale only where measured environmental gains survive return/inventory follow-up and service, quality and resilience gates hold. Otherwise tighten eligibility or revert that segment, not the entire contingency network.
