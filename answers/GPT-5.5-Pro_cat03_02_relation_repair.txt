## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

I treat the log as incomplete rather than simply dirty. The aim is not to maximize lifecycle completeness, but to add relations only where several independent signals point in the same direction.

**Hard invariants.** I do not invent new events or objects. Every added E2O or O2O edge must reference an existing event and object. O2O qualifiers are directional: `DEL-Y carries PK-8` is not equivalent to `PK-8 carries DEL-Y`. Duplicate identical relations should not be created. I also avoid adding direct relations that contradict the activity semantics, such as payment events directly relating to Orders merely because a payment amount equals an order value.

**Strong defaults.** Activity cardinalities are strong but defeasible. `Pack Item` normally has one Item and one Package; `Dispatch Package` normally has one Package and one Delivery; `Record Payment` normally has one Payment and one Invoice, although unapplied cash is possible. Lifecycle order is also strong evidence: Add Item before Pack Item before Dispatch Package before Confirm Delivery is plausible; reverse lifecycle edges would be suspicious. Existing O2O structure is useful: if an event links an Item and Package during `Pack Item`, a `Package packs Item` O2O relation is plausible, but this is not independent proof if both are inferred from the same scanner reference.

**Weak heuristics.** Identifier similarity is useful but not decisive: `PK8-2` suggests `PK-8`; `N-421?` suggests `DEL-Y`; `N-42*` matches both `N-420` and `N-421`. Same timestamp at 10:00 may indicate either separate simultaneous dispatches or one operational batch. Amount arithmetic is only weak-to-moderate evidence: `130 + 100 = 230` and `100 + 130 = 230` support possible consolidated billing/payment, but fees, discounts, taxes, or missing invoices could explain the same numbers.

**Evidence against a repair.** Observed absence is weak evidence against an edge because the dataset is incomplete, but it still matters when the existing relation is semantically sufficient. For example, ev12 already relates `INV-44` to `ORD-A`; adding `ORD-B` only to make the invoice total equal the two declared values risks over-repair. The wildcard delivery confirmation ev11 is especially ambiguous because it has `N-42*`, not a full carrier reference, and only `ORD-A` is observed.

I combine evidence by requiring at least one strong semantic reason plus one independent attribute, time, or identifier signal for selected additions. Where the support mainly comes from arithmetic or broad customer/route matching, I leave the relation unresolved or move it to an alternative bundle.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A: conservative/minimum-change repair.**
This bundle repairs strong cardinality holes where identifier and lifecycle evidence are also present. It adds `ev08 → PK-8` and `PK-8 packs IT-B1`. That makes ev08 a normal `Pack Item` event and gives `IT-B1` a package without inventing a package. The cost is that `PK-8` now contains items from both `ORD-A` and `ORD-B`; this is unusual but not prohibited, especially because both Orders belong to customer C17 and the route is NORTH.

For ev10, Bundle A links `DEL-Y` and adds `DEL-Y carries PK-8`. This is preferred over `DEL-X` because `N-421?` is closer to `DEL-Y.carrier_ref=N-421` than to `DEL-X.carrier_ref=N-420`. The question mark keeps confidence below certainty.

For ev11, Bundle A makes no delivery addition. ev11 remains an Order/customer-level confirmation related only to observed `ORD-A`. Both `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y` are plausible candidates, but `N-42*` is not enough to choose one or both.

For `INV-44` and `ORD-B`, Bundle A leaves the relation unresolved. The invoice total supports possible joint billing, but ev12 already has one Invoice and one Order, satisfying the minimum semantics. For `PAY-2`, Bundle A adds `ev14 → INV-44` and `PAY-2 settles INV-44`, because payment events normally relate to an invoice and the temporal/amount evidence is stronger than for adding `ORD-B` to the invoice.

**Bundle B: consolidated customer shipment and invoice.**
This bundle interprets the day as two same-customer Orders consolidated operationally. It accepts the same ev08 and ev10 repairs as Bundle A. It also treats ev11 as a customer-level acknowledgment of both carrier references matching `N-42*`, so it adds `ev11 → DEL-X` and `ev11 → DEL-Y`. I would still hesitate to add `ev11 → ORD-B` unless the analysis requires order-level delivery acknowledgments, because the event already names only `ORD-A`; however, a stronger version of Bundle B could add `ORD-B` as a low-confidence order edge.

Bundle B adds `ev12 → ORD-B` and `INV-44 bills ORD-B`. The coherent story is: two C17 orders, values 130 and 100, are packaged into PK-7 and PK-8, dispatched through two NORTH deliveries, invoiced together for 230, and settled by two payments of 100 and 130. This gives much cleaner lifecycles, but that cleanliness is exactly why it should be treated cautiously.

**Bundle C: pessimistic identifier-quality repair.**
A stricter alternative accepts only `PAY-2 → INV-44` and leaves ev08’s package, ev10’s delivery, ev11’s delivery candidates, and `INV-44 → ORD-B` unresolved. This is coherent if scanner references such as `PK8-2` denote devices rather than packages, or if `N-421?` is too uncertain to bind to `DEL-Y`. It preserves ambiguity but leaves `IT-B1`, `DEL-Y`, and PK-8’s full contents less explainable.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

**Orders.** `ORD-A` has a plausible but split lifecycle: Create Order, two Add Item events, a Confirm Delivery event, and Issue Invoice. Its items are packed into two packages, PK-7 and PK-8, but the package and delivery events are not directly E2O-related to the Order. `ORD-B` has Create Order, Add Item, and, through selected repairs, its item is packed into PK-8 and dispatched through DEL-Y. It has no selected invoice or delivery-confirmation edge. That is incomplete but acceptable.

**Items.** All three Items have an Add Item and Pack Item lifecycle. `IT-B1` becomes normal only after adding `ev08 → PK-8` and `PK-8 packs IT-B1`.

**Packages.** `PK-7` has Pack Item then Dispatch Package via `DEL-X`. `PK-8` has two Pack Item events and one Dispatch Package via `DEL-Y`. The suspicious point is one package packing items from two Orders. This is plausible for same-customer consolidation but should be tested operationally.

**Deliveries.** `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y` each have dispatch evidence under Bundle A, but neither has a selected Confirm Delivery event. `DEL-Y` was originally orphan-like; the repair connects it. The `DEL-Y carries PK-8` O2O edge is not independent evidence for `ev10 → DEL-Y`; both are inferred from the same `N-421?` dispatch interpretation.

**Invoices.** `INV-44` is issued for `ORD-A` and settled by PAY-1 plus repaired PAY-2. The invoice total remains suspicious relative to only `ORD-A.declared_value=130`. That is evidence for possible `ORD-B` billing, but Bundle A does not treat it as determined.

**Payments.** PAY-1 is observed settling INV-44. PAY-2 is repaired as also settling INV-44. Multiple payments to one invoice are plausible. Direct payment-to-order edges are rejected.

The main circularity risk is inferring O2O edges from repaired E2O edges and then treating those O2O edges as independent confirmation. For selected additions, the O2O edges are consequences of the same repair hypothesis, not separate proof.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

In the **unrepaired log**, OC-DFG behavior is fragmented. `IT-B1` has Add Item → Pack Item, but ev08 has no Package, so PK-8 does not show that second packing step. `DEL-Y` has no events. `PAY-2` is a Payment-only event, so the Invoice trace has Issue Invoice → Record Payment only through PAY-1.

Under **Bundle A**, the event count does not change, but object participation does. PK-8’s Package lifecycle becomes Pack Item → Pack Item → Dispatch Package, adding a repeated `Pack Item` fragment for the Package type. DEL-Y appears in the Delivery flattening with Dispatch Package. INV-44 gains a second payment event, so the Invoice flattening becomes Issue Invoice → Record Payment → Record Payment. The ET–OT graph gains higher incidences for `Pack Item–Package`, `Dispatch Package–Delivery`, and `Record Payment–Invoice`, although those activity–object-type pairs already existed elsewhere.

Under **Bundle B**, the Delivery OC-DFG gains concrete Dispatch Package → Confirm Delivery edges for DEL-X and DEL-Y. If `ev12 → ORD-B` is added, Order flattening for ORD-B extends from Create Order → Add Item to Create Order → Add Item → Issue Invoice, and possibly to Confirm Delivery if ev11 is also related to ORD-B. This can make ORD-A and ORD-B appear much more similar in equivalent-execution clustering.

For the **OTG**, Bundle A does not introduce new type-level relation kinds, but increases counts: Package–Item via `packs`, Delivery–Package via `carries`, and Payment–Invoice via `settles`. Bundle B additionally increases Invoice–Order `bills` counts if `ORD-B` is linked.

For **flattenings**, Order flattening changes little in Bundle A; Package, Delivery, and Invoice flattenings change materially. Bundle B changes Order and Delivery flattenings much more. Activity frequencies by event remain unchanged in all bundles. Frequencies by unique objects or total object incidences change: ev11 is one event but may count as two Delivery participations in Bundle B; ev12 is one invoice event but may count against two Orders if ORD-B is added.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. Adding `ev11` uniquely to `DEL-X` because `DEL-X` is already observed at ev09 would ignore that `N-42*` also matches `DEL-Y`.

2. Adding both `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y` to ev10 just because ev09 and ev10 share timestamp 10:00 would create a false bulk-dispatch event.

3. Treating `INV-44.total=230` as proof that it bills both Orders would ignore fees, discounts, taxes, or missing invoice objects.

4. Adding direct `Record Payment → Order` E2O edges because `PAY-1=100` and `PAY-2=130` match declared order values would violate the stated payment semantics.

5. Using inferred O2O edges as independent confirmation of the E2O edges that produced them would create circular validation.

6. Forcing `DEL-Y` to have a Confirm Delivery edge merely to complete its lifecycle would overstate delivery evidence.

7. Propagating ev11 to every object with customer C17 would turn a vague portal confirmation into a customer-wide event.

8. Adding inverse O2O edges such as `IT-B1 packs PK-8` with the same qualifier would corrupt the directional OTG semantics.
