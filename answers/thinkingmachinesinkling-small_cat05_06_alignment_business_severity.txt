## 1. Materiality Framework

Severity is not edit distance; it is the product of control-objective importance, financial exposure, reversibility, timing, customer/regulatory harm, recurrence, evidence reliability, and whether the deviation occurs inside the model’s intended scope.

| Axis | How it drives severity |
|---|---|
| **Control objective** | Payment authorization and approval integrity > notification fulfillment. Paying before approval (P3) is irreversibly severe; a missing notification is recoverable. |
| **Amount / exposure** | EUR >50k and HIGH-risk claims amplify any control gap; EUR <5k LOW-risk claims reduce exposure but do not eliminate fraud indicators (e.g., bank-change). |
| **Reversibility** | Pay Claim can be recalled; an approval after payment cannot retroactively secure authorization. Sequence violations dominate. |
| **Timing / sequence** | Pay before approve is worse than approve without documented fraud screen because time order determines causality. |
| **Customer / regulatory harm** | Beneficiary-bank changes + high value invite fraud; denied/withdrawn claims have no customer harm if no payment occurs. |
| **Recurrence** | Four fully synchronous Auto Approve violations and 74 duplicate notifications indicate systemic causes, not isolated errors. |
| **Evidence reliability** | External signed reports (CL-606) reduce severity versus missing documentation with no corroboration (CL-602). |
| **Model scope** | Denied/withdrawn claims (CL-607, CL-612) are out of scope for the payable-claim process tree (P6); high alignment cost reflects model incompleteness, not operational failure. |

**Confidence vs. severity.** Severity is the potential harm if the event is real. Confidence is our evidentiary certainty that the harmful event occurred. A missing Fraud Screen log with a manual approver admitting “verbal only” (CL-602) is high-severity, high-confidence. A missing core Notify event with a CRM message at +3 hours and a mutable link (CL-608) is moderate-severity, medium-confidence because functional compliance may exist but forensic proof is weak. A zero-cost fully synchronous Auto Approve on a 18k/Medium claim (CL-609) is high-severity (data-aware breach), high-confidence (fields are explicit), but the alignment assigns zero cost because the control-flow model ignores data guards.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

For each, competing hypotheses (H) and discriminating evidence (E) are given. A missing event is never proof that an activity did not occur.

**CL-602 — Missing Fraud Screen / verbal-only approval**
- H1: Fraud screen performed but not logged (system gap).
- H2: Fraud screen never performed; approver relied solely on verbal check.
- H3: Fraud screen performed informally (phone/email) and evidence lost.
- E: Fraud-system audit logs independent of claim log; physical bank-change and amount evidence; approver interview consistency with other cases; presence/absence of fraud-check documentation in external systems.

**CL-604 — Pay at 10:03, approval at 10:20**
- H1: Batch/state-machine released payment before approval recorded.
- H2: Manual override by user with dual authorization/pay role bypassed gate.
- H3: Approval timestamp backdated to cover sequence error.
- E: User session/access logs; authorization workflow state-machine history (before/after); bank transaction timestamp vs approval audit trail; user action logs at 10:03.

**CL-606 — Missing `Assess Loss` with external signed report**
- H1: Assessment never performed (report fabricated/unrelated).
- H2: Performed by external adjuster; core integration failed.
- H3: Performed but event deleted/mistranscribed.
- E: Physical signed report with stable case identifier; external adjuster system logs; core database audit trail (deletion flags); comparison with the 2 cases lacking stable identifiers.

**CL-609 / CL-610 — Zero-cost Auto Approve violations**
- H1: Data conditions (amount, risk, bank-change) never evaluated by workflow engine.
- H2: Evaluated incorrectly due to stale data at decision time.
- H3: Deliberate user or automated rule exception.
- E: Decision-engine input snapshot at approval time (amount field, risk score, bank-change timestamp); rule-config version history; override audit trail; retrospective data-state reconstruction.

**CL-608 — Core `Notify Claimant` missing; CRM portal at +3h**
- H1: Notification never delivered.
- H2: Delivered via CRM/portal; core log never synchronized.
- H3: Link broken/updated; evidence lost.
- E: CRM message timestamp and recipient; portal access/authentication logs; link metadata/versioning history; claimant confirmation/acknowledgment.

**CL-607 / CL-612 — Scope mismatch (high alignment cost, no payment)**
- H1: Cases incorrectly routed into payable-claim model.
- H2: Payable-only model is correct, but denied/withdrawn paths should be excluded or added.
- E: Intake classification rules; process owner documentation; case outcome flag at intake vs. alignment path length; whether notification content indicates denial/withdrawal.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Alignment cost is the edit distance under a uniform cost function (all visible moves = 1, hidden = 0). It measures structural distance between a log trace and a process tree, not control failure, exposure, or evidence quality. The following cases demonstrate why technical cost and business risk diverge.

**Cost-function design ignores business weight (CL-602 vs. CL-601).** CL-602 (missing Fraud Screen on EUR 480k, HIGH risk, bank change) has raw cost 1; CL-601 (UI telemetry on EUR 1,200 LOW claim) has raw cost 3. The missing fraud evidence is a far more severe control failure than three unfiltered workbench events, yet the uniform function assigns lower cost to the severe gap because it counts moves, not exposure.

**Data conditions are invisible to control-flow alignment (CL-609 and CL-610).** Both have raw cost 0 and fully synchronous paths. However, CL-609 violates P1 (EUR 18,000, MEDIUM risk; Auto approval allowed only 5k/LOW) and CL-610 violates P1 (bank changed 1 hour before payment; requires no recent change). The process tree does not encode amount, risk, or bank-change guards, so the alignment sees perfect conformance while a severe data-aware policy failure occurs. Zero cost does not mean zero risk; it means the model lacks the relevant dimensions.

**Scope mismatch inflates cost without harm (CL-607 and CL-612).** Both have raw cost 4 because the payable-claim model demands `Assess Loss`, `Auto Approve`, `Pay Claim`, and `Notify`, none of which occurred (claim denied / withdrawn). There was no intended payment; no customer harm. The high cost reflects model incompleteness (P6 dispute), not an operational deviation.

**Hidden / silent moves can be benign; missing moves can be benign or severe (CL-605 vs. CL-606 vs. CL-602).** CL-605 contains a hidden routing transition (`T(tau,c=0)`) and cost 0; it is a harmless conversion artifact. CL-606 also has cost 1 for `M(Assess Loss)`, but an external signed report exists, so the business risk is moderate (evidence gap, not missing work). CL-602 has the same cost 1 for `M(Fraud Screen)`, yet the approver admits no fraud evidence exists, making it critical. Same numerical cost, different evidence reliability.

**Activity granularity and telemetry duplication inflate cost (CL-601 and CL-603).** CL-601’s cost 3 comes from `Open Workbench`, `Autosave`, and `Close Workbench`, which are not business activities per P5. CL-603’s cost 1 comes from a retry duplicate of `Notify Claimant`. Both are technically unmatched moves, but neither indicates control failure; the cost captures logging artifacts, not risk.

**Missing-but-external evidence lowers true risk below cost (CL-606, CL-608).** CL-606 has cost 1 but external evidence exists; CL-608 has cost 1 but functional notification occurred via CRM at +3 hours with linked evidence. Technical alignment penalizes missing core events regardless of whether the business control is satisfied elsewhere.

**Irreversibility and timing are invisible to edit distance (CL-604).** The alignment repairs CL-604 with `L(Pay)` and `M(Pay)` at cost 3, but it cannot express that a bank accepted payment at 10:03 before manual approval at 10:20. The sequence violation is irreversible: funds have left the account. The edit distance measures structural repairability, not temporal causality or recovery difficulty. Thus a moderate cost accompanies the highest operational severity.

**Conclusion:** Raw cost should be reported alongside business severity, policy-overlay flags, evidence reliability, and scope classification. Remediation and investigation priority must be driven by control-objective importance (P3 > P2 > P4 > P1 in irreversibility) and exposure (amount, risk, bank-change), not by minimizing edit distance.
