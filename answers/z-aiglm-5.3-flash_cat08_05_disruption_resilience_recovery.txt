Definition + three tests:
1. Control-objective test: does the deviation preserve or credibly substitute the control's objective (eligibility, release, custody, temperature, traceability), or merely preserve flow?
2. Transparency test: is the deviation visible, attributable, and evidenced at the time, or only discoverable later by auditors?
3. Reversibility/governability test: is it time-boxed, rule-bound, and exit-able, or open-ended?

True resilience: B (substituted control with named accountability), F (risk-based release with 100% high-risk coverage), I and J (explicit capacity rationing), K (truth-restoring gate), plus the spreadsheet invoice hold (billing gated on evidence — a deviation that added control), and triage itself. ## 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

Apply three tests to any deviation:

1. **Control-objective test** — does the deviation preserve or credibly *substitute* the control's objective (eligibility, release, custody, temperature, traceability), or does it merely preserve flow?
2. **Transparency test** — is the deviation visible, attributable, and evidenced *at the time*, or only discoverable later by auditors?
3. **Governability test** — is it time-boxed, rule-bound, and exitable, or open-ended?

**True resilience** — deviations that passed the tests: **B** (substituted control with named accountability), **F** (risk-based release with 100% high-risk coverage), **I** and **J** (explicit, rule-based capacity rationing), **K** (a truth-restoring gate), and — notably — the **spreadsheet invoice hold**, a deviation that *added* a control by refusing to bill without delivery evidence. These are all deviations from the baseline model that made the process *better* under constraint. The DFG shift confirms this: compliance holds *rose* in T4 (1.7%  3.7%), the signature of reactivated controls catching re-verified work — a deviation that is a good sign, not a bad one.

**Risky shortcuts (concealed noncompliance)** — throughput purchased by silently deleting controls: **C** (authorization removed, no substitute, no record) and **G** (custody and temperature handoff removed at scale), plus **E** in its unrepaired form and **D** past its time-box. The conformance evidence is diagnostic here: the *move-on-log* deviations (improvised activities like skip-authorization, dispatch-without-scan) are visible new behaviors that *could* have been governed; the *move-on-model* deviations (missing release check, missing scan, missing POD, missing temperature record) are where noncompliance actually hides — required activities absent with no substitute.

**Ambiguous, depending on risk appetite:** **A** (a fast lane is legitimate if it reorders work; noncompliant if it waived authorization for regulated items — depends on what actually rode the lane), **H** (fine for ambient with a named-person log; unacceptable for controlled), **D** (acceptable on Day 0, not Day 2 — the risk was a *function of time*, which is the essence of temporal reasoning here), **F** (the sampling rate is defensible only if documented), **L** (acceptable for ambient documentary gaps; never a substitute for real-time cold-chain or custody evidence).

Two abductive readings sharpen the picture. First, C emerged *alongside* B: where trained compliance staff were available, controls were substituted; where capacity ran out, they were skipped. The driver was capacity, not intent — so the fix is pre-positioned capacity and rules, not blame. Second, controlled-substance dual-control failures rose to 2.9% even though F states pharmacists checked *all* controlled orders: the physical check most likely happened, but its **evidence capture and transfer failed**. The concealment was in the documentation layer — which is exactly why "no missing records" cannot be read as "no missing controls," and vice versa.

Finally, the throughput illusion: T2 sustained roughly baseline daily volume while exceptions rose 12–14.5×. That throughput was partly financed by control debt, and the debt was real — it surfaced as 12.5% rework, 7.8% cancellations, and a remediation queue outliving cycle-time recovery. And hospital protection (91% fill) was partly paid for by pharmacies (61% fill). Resilience for one segment at another's expense must be a *decision*, never an accident of informal triage.

### 4.4 Manual evidence capture
A **Contingency Evidence Envelope** per order: pre-numbered form capturing order ID, customer, items with lot/expiry, authorization checker + list version + timestamp, release checker, temperature readings, dispatch manifest with courier signature/time/photo, and POD record. Numbering makes gaps detectable; photos/scans sync to an incident evidence repository when connectivity returns; envelope closure is a **precondition of invoice close** (formalizing the good instinct behind the spreadsheet invoice hold).

### 4.5 Backlog prioritization, reconciliation, and audit flow
On restoration: (1) **inventory reconciliation gate** — void the stale snapshot, count, adjust, *then* auto-release reservations (K repositioned as a gate); (2) **evidence matching** — envelopes  system orders, gaps  remediation queue; (3) **backlog prioritization** — single node with the published score, fairness caps, and a cancel-with-communication path for deferred stock failing risk re-screen; (4) **staged ramp** — throttle intake to reconciliation capacity (T3 resumed intake while scanning was unreliable, which is why deviations persisted into T4); (5) **remediation queue** with a 10-business-day SLA, aging dashboard, and per-case classification: *evidence reconstructed* (documentary gap) vs. *deviation confirmed* (triggers quality event/CAPA, possibly regulatory notification) vs. *unrecoverable* (disclosure decision).

### 4.6 Process-tree notation
Legend: **SEQ** = strict sequence; **XOR** = exclusive choice; **PAR** = concurrency; **LOOP** = repetition; **tau** = silent skip (false alarm); `[brackets]` = attributes.

```
DEGRADED_MODE_PROCESS =
SEQ(
  DetectTrigger,
  XOR( tau,                      -- spurious trigger: resume NORMAL
    SEQ(
      PAR( DeclareDegradedMode[ops+compliance co-sign, notify QA],
           ActivateContingencyKit[forms, offline auth list vN, photo pipeline],
           FreezeAutoPromises,
           NotifyCustomers[deferral rules] ),
      LOOP( PER_ORDER:
        SEQ(
          Triage[S = criticality x product risk + urgency],
          XOR( PriorityLane, StandardLane, DeferToBacklog,
               QAHold[controls unsatisfiable -> hold, do not ship] ),
          SEQ( ManualAuthorization[evidence mandatory, no skip],
               RiskTierControls[per matrix],
               PickWithEvidence, PackWithEvidence,
               ReleaseCheck[100% high-risk / documented sample ambient],
               XOR( DispatchWithManifest,
                    HoldAtDock[no custody/temp document -> no dispatch] ),
               TieredPOD[risk-based],
               CloseEvidenceEnvelope ) ) ) ),
      ON_RESTORE:
        SEQ( InventoryReconciliationGate[before auto-release],
             EvidenceMatching[envelope <-> order],
             BacklogPrioritization[published score, fairness caps, risk re-screen],
             StagedRamp[throttle intake to reconciliation capacity],
             PAR( PODRemediation,
                  AuditRemediation[SLA, reconstructed-vs-confirmed flag, QA signoff] ),
             ExitCheck,
             DeclareNormal ) ) ) )
```

**Exit criteria:** fitness  0.95 and  85% fitting traces against the *extended* normative model; high-risk control completion = 100%; traceability exceptions < 1.2× baseline for 5 consecutive days; remediation queue age < SLA and shrinking.

## 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should accept slower cycle times, deferral of noncritical pharmacy-standard and bulk demand, and documented manual *substitution* of controls as the legitimate price of protecting hospital supply — provided substitution is risk-tiered, evidenced at the moment of action, and applied by rule rather than improvisation. It should not accept, under any throughput pressure, the silent removal of authorization, release, custody, or temperature controls for controlled, cold-chain, or recall-sensitive products; nor should it accept after-the-fact remediation as an equivalent to real-time compliance, or declare recovery on cycle-time metrics alone — T4 proved that medians can return to baseline while traceability exceptions sit at 2–3× baseline and 8.5% of closed invoices require remediation. The recommendation is evidence-revisable in both directions: if validation of the T2 skipped-authorization population shows zero confirmed deviations, a narrow, pre-approved fast lane for long-standing ambient accounts with documented sampling becomes a defensible risk appetite; a single confirmed diversion, temperature excursion, or failed recall-trace drill reverses it to 100% gating for all risk classes, even at materially lower throughput. Standing tripwires: high-risk control completion below 100%, hospital fill below ~85%, or remediation queue age beyond SLA should trigger slowing intake — never shedding controls.
