## 1. Conformance semantics

The norm is closed-world only in two zones: rows marked **forbidden** (N3, T6, E10) and the **mandatory** rows within their stated scope (N1, N4, T1, T2, E1–E8). Everywhere else it is open-world: absence of a relation from the normative tables licenses no conclusion.

I use seven deviation classes:

- **Forbidden behavior**: an observed relation matching a forbidden row within its scope. Forbidden rows are closed-world, so one occurrence is a prima facie violation — but only after ruling out that the observation itself is an artifact and confirming the scope applies.
- **Missing mandatory behavior**: an in-scope instance lacking the relation a mandatory row requires. Mandatory rows are closed-world within scope, so a count shortfall is a candidate violation — but scope and the abstraction encoding the rule must be verified first.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior**: a relation matching no norm row or a row marked unspecified. Under the open-world reading this is not a deviation and carries no compliance weight, though it may inform norm revision.
- **Typical-pattern absence**: an instance lacking a relation marked typical. Typical rows admit legitimate exceptions, so absence is at most a follow-up signal, never a violation; severity should track business consequence, not count.
- **Likely data-quality error**: an observation whose most plausible mechanism is a recording or integration defect (a reused-identifier join, or an event whose attributes contradict its missing relation). This class can masquerade as any other, so it must be an explicit alternative in every finding.
- **Abstraction mismatch**: the norm means "eventually" but the summary encodes directly-follows. An instance can violate the encoding while satisfying the rule — an unrecorded intermediate event breaks directly-follows without breaking "eventually". N1 flags itself as such an approximation.
- **Insufficient evidence**: the observation is consistent with both a compliant and a non-compliant reading and the summary cannot separate them; the honest verdict is "undecided, evidence X needed".

The open/closed split does the work: closed-world zones let violations be inferred from presence (forbidden) or absence (mandatory in scope); open-world zones forbid inferring anything from absence-from-the-norm. Hence `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72), the 7 codeath edges, `Reopen Order -> Close Order` (1, N6), and Manual Compliance Review–Customer (3) are unregulated behavior, not violations.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

The OC-DFG establishes per-object-type direct-succession frequencies. It can show a forbidden succession occurred (N3: 2 Orders) and a mandatory succession falling short of its scope count (N1: 98/100; N4: 198/200). It cannot establish why an edge is missing, what happened between two activities, whether the "missing" successor occurs later, or whether timestamps are trustworthy.

Scope and counts matter directly. N1's denominator (100 created Orders) is stated, so the 2-order gap is well-defined. N4's scope is "packages intended for external shipment"; the summary asserts all 200 qualify, but if some were internal the gap would dissolve — the scope test precedes any verdict. N5 is typical: 92 paid, 4 written off, 14 open is not a gap; the 14 sit inside the rule's exception space (open credit terms).

The central danger is encoding "eventually expected" as directly-follows. N1 says orders should eventually enter validation; the summary approximates with a directly-follows edge. Any unlogged intermediate event — the context note suggests an unrecorded review — breaks the edge while the rule still holds, so a checker treating the edge as the rule produces false violations precisely on compliant-but-richer traces. The two flagged Orders should be re-tested under eventually-follows semantics. The same logic protects N2: intermediates are "incompletely modeled", so `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72) is expected structure, not a detour. Conversely, directly-follows is too weak for prohibitions: N3 means no dispatch at any time after cancellation, so a dispatch two steps later would not appear as this edge — the observed 2 instances are a lower bound on N3 violations.

### OTG

The object-type graph shows which object types interact, co-birth, or co-die, with aggregate weights. It shows a forbidden type-level relation exists (T6: cobirth weight 1) and mandatory relations exist with plausible weights (T1: 1450, T2: 200). It cannot localize which instances produced an edge, distinguish a systematic problem from an accident, or express per-event requirements — T1 and T2 are really per-event rules ("required for an Add Item / Dispatch Package interaction"), testable only in the ET–OT view.

This produces "conforming at one abstraction, suspicious at another": the OTG shows `(Package, object_interaction, Delivery)` at weight 200, apparently satisfying T2; the ET–OT view shows only 198 of 200 Dispatch events carry a Delivery, so two events fail the per-event rule even though the type-level edge looks healthy (the 200 interactions evidently include non-dispatch confirmation events, consistent with the 7 codeath edges). Likewise T1 looks satisfied at 1450 while ET–OT reveals two non-conforming Add Item events (1448 vs. 1450). Aggregate weights hide per-instance shortfalls. On the open-world side, T4 (Order–Customer, 100) and T5 (Order–RiskCase, 3) are unspecified, so their presence is uninformative, and the codeath relationship type is outside the norm's vocabulary and must not be scored.

### ET–OT

The ET–OT model gives, per event type, participating object types and frequencies — the right granularity for E1–E10. It quantifies per-event-type shortfalls exactly (Add Item–Order 1448/1450; Dispatch–Delivery 198/200; Record Payment–Invoice 92/93) and detects forbidden participations (E10: weight 1). It cannot say which event instances are affected, whether the missing object exists but is unlinked (the carrier-reference note suggests exactly this for E6), or whether an exception clause applies. E8's unapplied-cash exception depends on a label never set in this snapshot — the model shows the exception is formally unavailable, not whether it should apply. That is the "insufficient evidence" class: strictly the 1 unmatched payment is in scope and non-conforming; charitably it is an unlabeled recognized exception.

The Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice edge conforms at no abstraction, but here the issue runs the other way: the edge is real in the log and almost certainly false in the world, given the identified join defect — a defect of the log against reality, not of the process against the norm.

## 5. PM4Py conformance code sketch

```python
import pm4py

# 1. Discover observed models from the OCEL
obs_ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
obs_otg   = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
obs_etot  = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

# 2. Raw conformance diagnostics against the normative artifacts.
#    These are structural diffs ONLY: they do not know mandatory /
#    typical / forbidden / unspecified. Preserve them verbatim.
diag_ocdfg = pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(ocel, norm_ocdfg)
diag_otg   = pm4py.conformance_otg(ocel, norm_otg)
diag_etot  = pm4py.conformance_etot(ocel, norm_etot)

raw_diagnostics = {"ocdfg": diag_ocdfg, "otg": diag_otg, "etot": diag_etot}

# 3. Normative status metadata.
#    IMPORTANT: the bare graph structures (norm_ocdfg dict, norm_otg
#    tuple, norm_etot tuple) encode only edge presence/absence. The
#    mandatory/typical/forbidden/unspecified statuses, scopes, and
#    exception clauses MUST be maintained in this separate registry,
#    keyed compatibly with the graph elements.
NORM_STATUS = {
    # OC-DFG: (object_type, source_act, target_act) -> metadata
    ("Order",   "Create Order",  "Validate Order"):  {"status": "mandatory",
        "scope": "newly created standard Orders",
        "note":  "eventually-expected; DF is an approximation"},   # N1
    ("Order",   "Validate Order","Close Order"):     {"status": "typical"},    # N2
    ("Order",   "Cancel Order",  "Dispatch Package"):{"status": "forbidden"},  # N3
    ("Package", "Pack Package",  "Dispatch Package"):{"status": "mandatory",
        "scope": "external shipments"},                                        # N4
    ("Invoice", "Issue Invoice", "Record Payment"):  {"status": "typical"},    # N5
    ("Order",   "Reopen Order",  "Close Order"):     {"status": "unspecified"},# N6
    # OTG: (type1, relationship, type2) -> metadata
    ("Order",   "object_interaction", "Item"):     {"status": "mandatory"},    # T1
    ("Package", "object_interaction", "Delivery"): {"status": "mandatory"},    # T2
    ("Invoice", "object_interaction", "Payment"):  {"status": "typical"},      # T3
    ("Order",   "object_interaction", "Customer"): {"status": "unspecified"},  # T4
    ("Order",   "object_interaction", "RiskCase"): {"status": "unspecified"},  # T5
    ("Order",   "object_cobirth",     "Payment"):  {"status": "forbidden"},    # T6
    # ET-OT: (event_type, object_type) -> metadata
    ("Create Order",     "Order"):    {"status": "mandatory"},                 # E1
    ("Add Item",         "Order"):    {"status": "mandatory"},                 # E2
    ("Add Item",         "Item"):     {"status": "mandatory"},                 # E3
    ("Pack Package",     "Package"):  {"status": "mandatory"},                 # E4
    ("Dispatch Package", "Package"):  {"status": "mandatory"},                 # E5
    ("Dispatch Package", "Delivery"): {"status": "mandatory"},                 # E6
    ("Record Payment",   "Payment"):  {"status": "mandatory"},                 # E7
    ("Record Payment",   "Invoice"):  {"status": "mandatory",
        "exception": "events explicitly typed as unapplied cash"},             # E8
    ("Manual Compliance Review", "RiskCase"): {"status": "unspecified"},       # E9
    ("Sensor Heartbeat", "Invoice"):  {"status": "forbidden",
        "dq_hypothesis": "join on reused numeric identifier"},                 # E10
}

# 4. Semantic classification layer: interprets the raw diffs through
#    the status registry WITHOUT mutating raw_diagnostics.
def classify(element, raw_finding, norm_status):
    meta = norm_status.get(element)
    if meta is None:
        # Open-world default: not covered by the norm at all.
        return {"element": element, "class": "unregulated",
                "raw": raw_finding, "action": "none (open world)"}
    status = meta["status"]
    if status == "forbidden" and raw_finding.get("observed", 0) > 0:
        return {"element": element, "class": "forbidden-candidate",
                "raw": raw_finding,
                "action": "verify data quality before declaring violation",
                "dq_hypothesis": meta.get("dq_hypothesis")}
    if status == "mandatory" and raw_finding.get("missing", 0) > 0:
        return {"element": element, "class": "mandatory-gap-candidate",
                "raw": raw_finding,
                "action": "check scope '%s' and abstraction (eventually vs DF)"
                          % meta.get("scope", "all in-scope instances"),
                "exception": meta.get("exception")}
    if status == "typical" and raw_finding.get("missing", 0) > 0:
        return {"element": element, "class": "typical-absence",
                "raw": raw_finding,
                "action": "monitor; legitimate exceptions allowed"}
    if status == "unspecified":
        return {"element": element, "class": "explicitly-unregulated",
                "raw": raw_finding, "action": "none"}
    return {"element": element, "class": "conforming", "raw": raw_finding}

# _iter_*_diffs adapt each PM4Py diagnostic structure into
# (element, finding) pairs; exact shape depends on the PM4Py version,
# which is another reason raw outputs are kept intact.
semantic_findings = {
    "ocdfg": [classify(el, f, NORM_STATUS) for el, f in _iter_ocdfg_diffs(diag_ocdfg)],
    "otg":   [classify(el, f, NORM_STATUS) for el, f in _iter_otg_diffs(diag_otg)],
    "etot":  [classify(el, f, NORM_STATUS) for el, f in _iter_etot_diffs(diag_etot)],
}
```

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1 encodes "eventually" as directly-follows.** Risk: systematic false positives on compliant orders with unlogged intermediate events, eroding trust in reports. Clarification: restate N1 as eventually-follows with an optional time bound. Status: mandatory.
2. **N4's scope ("intended for external shipment") has no operational test.** Risk: every gap becomes a scope dispute; the 2 flagged packages cannot be adjudicated. Clarification: define scope via an explicit attribute (shipment_type = external). Status: mandatory within that scope.
3. **E8's unapplied-cash exception depends on a label never used.** Risk: legitimate payments become formal violations, or analysts silently waive the rule. Clarification: require typing unapplied-cash events at capture; untyped invoice-less payments become violations after a grace period. Status: mandatory (the labeling duty).
4. **Codeath (and cobirth beyond T6) is unmentioned.** Risk: analysts improvise — scoring the 7 benign codeath edges as deviations, or missing a future problematic co-lifecycle pattern. Clarification: add a row for (Package, object_codeath, Delivery). Status: explicitly unspecified (or typical, if co-termination in a confirmation event is intended design).
5. **No tolerance bands or minimum frequencies.** Risk: a 98% and a 60% mandatory-compliance rate are formally identical; severity becomes ad hoc. Clarification: per-rule thresholds and escalation tiers (mandatory: investigate each instance; typical: alert on trend shifts). Status: documented meta-policy.
6. **N3 is written edge-style but means an eventually-follows prohibition.** Risk: dispatches two or more steps after cancellation escape detection — under-detection of the severest class. Clarification: "no Dispatch Package at any time after Cancel Order on the same Order". Status: forbidden (strengthened).
7. **Interplay of `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review` with N1 is undefined.** Risk: unclear whether the 3 reviewed orders still require validation and count in N1's denominator. Clarification: state whether reviewed orders must still be validated. Status: mandatory if validation remains required; otherwise typical alternative path.
8. **Sensor Heartbeat's legitimate object scope is undefined.** Risk: E10 forbids Invoice links only; contamination of, say, Payment is unregulated. Clarification: forbid Sensor Heartbeat interaction with all financial object types. Status: forbidden.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

**Immediate containment** — prioritizing the two highest-severity findings (N3, T6). For the 2 dispatch-after-cancel Orders: pull order, package, and delivery IDs; attempt carrier recall if in transit; freeze invoicing and collection; notify the process owner same day. Quarantine the Order–Payment cobirth event from financial reporting until its event boundary is verified. The Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice edge needs no operational containment, only exclusion from analytics.

**Data repair.** Fix the reused-identifier join behind E10 and re-extract; audit that key for further collisions. Backfill the 2 missing Delivery objects from carrier references (E6). Restore the 2 missing Add Item–Order references if integrity checks show extraction loss (E2). Retro-label the invoice-less payment once finance classifies it (E8). Log each repair so raw diagnostics stay reproducible.

**Process investigation.** N3: reconstruct both order timelines from source timestamps to separate genuine violation from recording disorder; if genuine, identify the actor and failed control. N4: determine whether the 2 packages lack Pack, lack Dispatch, or only the record, and verify their external-shipment scope. N1: run eventually-follows on the 2 orders and ask operations about unlogged review steps. Do **not** investigate the reopen path (1, N6), write-offs (4, named exception), compliance reviews (3, allowed), or codeath edges (7): rarity alone is not suspicion.

**Norm revision.** Adopt Section 6's clarifications, prioritizing the eventually-follows restatements of N1 and N3 and the operationalization of E8's exception — these three directly change the classification of open findings.

**Monitoring.** Alert immediately on any forbidden-row instance (N3, T6, E10-class links), each alert paired with a data-quality checklist. Track mandatory satisfaction ratios (N1, N4, E2, E6, E8) as trend metrics with the new thresholds. Track the 14 open invoices via receivables aging, not conformance alerting. Re-run extraction-integrity checks (identifier reuse, referential completeness) on every refresh.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

**Robust:** the 2 `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` observations are the most serious findings and warrant case-level investigation regardless of root cause; the mandatory ET–OT shortfalls (Add Item–Order 2, Dispatch–Delivery 2, Record Payment–Invoice 1) are real gaps in the log; the typical and unspecified observations (write-offs, open invoices, reopen path, risk reviews, customer links, codeath edges) are not violations.

**Dependent on the norm's incompleteness:** whether the 2 non-validated Orders and the 2 Pack–Dispatch gaps are violations turns on N1's directly-follows encoding and N4's undefined scope test; whether the invoice-less payment violates E8 turns on an exception clause whose triggering label is never applied.

**Dependent on suspected data quality:** the Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice edge (identified join defect), the Order–Payment cobirth (possible event-boundary merge), the missing Delivery links (carrier references contradict absence), and the interpretation of the N3 finding (timestamp integrity) all hinge on source-data verification.

**Undecidable from graph summaries alone:** which specific instances are affected, the temporal order within each case, whether missing relations reflect missing reality or missing recording, actor and intent behind the forbidden observations, and whether unlogged activities exist between summarized edges. Every classification marked "candidate" or "insufficient evidence" requires trace-level and source-system evidence before a final verdict.
