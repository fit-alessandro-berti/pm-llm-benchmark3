# 2. Adjusted comparison strategy

The strategy must separate four things that the preliminary model blends: (a) **disparity** (unexplained difference), (b) **discrimination** (disparate treatment in decision-making discretion), (c) **legitimate routing** (rule-concordant complexity handling), and (d) **uncertainty** (small overlap, biased covariates, unobservables). I propose a **layered decomposition**, reporting all layers side by side rather than a single "adjusted" number.

### 2.1 Layer 0 — Raw gap, decomposed by path composition
Report the raw gap and then re-weight each group's variant distribution to a common distribution (standardization / Oaxaca–Blinder on the variant mix). This answers: *how much of the raw gap is simply that groups traverse different documented paths?* Key comparators: restrict to V1-only cases and to V2-only cases across groups. If A and B differ in approval *within* V2 (a path with the same named steps), the difference cannot be attributed to path composition alone.

### 2.2 Layer 1 — Legitimate-complexity adjustment (the "conservative" estimate)
Adjust only for variables that are plausibly **pre-treatment, exogenous, and not themselves products of the process**:
`risk_score_band` (with an explicit sensitivity flag, §2.5), `household_size_band`, `region`, `month`, `prior_case_count` (flagged — partly endogenous).

Matching/stratification design:
- **Coarsened exact matching / stratification cells** on `risk_score_band × documentation_completeness × household_size_band × month × region`.
- Within cells, compare A vs B/C/D on approval, on reaching each activity, and on case duration.
- **Overlap (positivity) diagnostics first.** Report the share of each group's mass in cells with no Group A counterpart (e.g., high-risk + minority-language + in-person cells). Any estimate for those cells is extrapolation and must be labelled as such.
- Use **overlap weights** (or propensity-score weighting on group membership given covariates) as a continuous check on the cell-based results.
- Report **standardized mean differences** before and after weighting; if SMD > 0.1 persists on any covariate, the comparison is not balanced.

### 2.3 Layer 2 — Mediator-adjusted estimate (the "direct-effect" estimate)
Add `documentation_completeness` and `channel`, which are **partly caused by group membership and by the accessibility of the process itself** (language barriers reduce documentation completeness; lack of online access or trust drives in-person use). These are mediators/contaminated covariates, so:
- **Total effect** = Layer 1 estimate (do not control for mediators).
- **Direct effect** = Layer 2 estimate (control for mediators).
- **Mediated portion** = difference between the two, reported as *"disparity operating through documentation completeness and channel"*.
The gap between Layers 1 and 2 is precisely the portion a naive "adjusted" analysis would mislabel as "explained by case mix." It must not be dismissed as legitimate.

### 2.4 Path/variant, resource, and waiting-time decomposition

**Path/variant comparison.** Match cases into *variant strata* (same activity sequence), then compare within stratum on: probability of approval, service vs waiting time per transition, handover count per case, and probability of Supervisor Review. Also compute a **path-burden index** per case = (number of activities) + (number of handovers) + (number of applicant-facing requests) + (number of days waiting in applicant-caused loops), and regress it on group with Layer 1 controls.

**Resource-assignment comparison.** Compare, per case and per group:
- number of distinct resources touched (concentration/diversity),
- handover count and **handover-of-work network** position (bridging vs. peripheral),
- proportion of cases assigned to top-decile-loaded workers (queue pressure proxy),
- effective capacity per case: cases-per-worker within the specialized queues (bilingual, accommodation) vs. the general pool.

**Waiting-time decomposition.** Use `insert_case_service_waiting_time` and `get_service_time` to split every transition into **service time** (resource active) vs **waiting time** (resource idle/absent). Then decompose the adjusted cycle-time gap into:
1. **Complexity share** — extra steps (Fraud Review, extra document loops) legitimately required;
2. **Support-step queue share** — waiting at Language Support  Eligibility (3.8 d) and Accommodation Review  Eligibility (4.7 d);
3. **Handover share** — waiting at general  bilingual  general and general  specialist  supervisor transitions;
4. **Discretion/backlog share** — waiting at Supervisor Review  Decision Draft (differential 1.0 vs 1.9 d), localized in two regions;
5. **Residual.**

*Preliminary arithmetic worth testing:* B's adjusted delay (+2.6 d) is smaller than the single language-support queue (3.8 d), and C's (+4.1 d) is comparable to the accommodation queue (4.7 d). If the decomposition confirms this, then **the residual "group effect" on time is largely the queue cost of exercising a right**, i.e., a resourcing/SLA problem rather than caseworker-level disparate treatment. This is a materially different finding and a different remedy.

### 2.5 Sensitivity analysis for possibly biased covariates
- **Leave-one-covariate-out.** Re-estimate excluding `risk_score_band`; excluding `documentation_completeness`; excluding `prior_case_count`. Report the range.
- **Misclassification sensitivity for `risk_score_band`.** Assume the band is a noisy proxy for true risk with differential error by group (e.g., 5–20% of cases mis-scored upward for Groups B/D). Re-score via probabilistic bias analysis and re-estimate the residual approval gap. Report the *tipping point*: how much differential scoring error is needed to drive the residual to zero?
- **E-values / Rosenbaum-style bounds** for unmeasured confounding on the residual estimates (5.5 pp B, +5.7 pp appeal D).
- **Placebo/negative controls.** `Decision Draft  Applicant Notification` (0.5–0.8 d) is described as relatively uniform; if a group effect appears there, the model is absorbing noise.
- **Attrition sensitivity.** The log contains only *submitted* applications. If language or accommodation needs deter submission, the analyzed population is already selected; results describe survivors and likely understate barriers.

### 2.6 Subgroup checks by region and channel
- **Region × group interactions.** The backlogs and handover patterns are reported in *two* regions. Estimate the group gap separately in those two regions versus the rest. A gap concentrated in two regions points to local staffing/routing design (correctable, and weak evidence of organization-wide disparate treatment). A uniform gap points to process-wide design or discretion.
- **Channel × group.** Restrict to online-only cases (Group A 91%, Group B 62%, Group C 76%, Group D 82%). If B's gap persists among online-only, the in-person/wait mechanism is not the explanation. If it collapses, the mechanism is channel/access related.
- **Intersectional cells.** Language-support-need × accommodation-request; language-support-need × high-risk band. These are the highest-burden, likely smallest cells — report counts and intervals honestly, and use them as *signal generation*, not as precise estimates.

### 2.7 Classification output
For each finding, tag it as one of: **(i) explained by documented, plausibly legitimate complexity**; **(ii) explained by process design (capacity, SLA, handover) — not "legitimate complexity," and fixable**; **(iii) unexplained residual, consistent with disparate discretion**; **(iv) indeterminate** because of measurement or overlap limits. Only (i) is "no concern." The preliminary model conflates (i) and (ii); separating them is the central analytic task.

# 4. Plausible mechanisms

**M1 — Legitimate complexity routing (exculpatory).** Higher measured risk genuinely warrants Fraud Review and Supervisor Review (D: 51% high-risk, 28% on V3, +2.0 pp supervisor review only). Accommodation requests and language needs genuinely warrant specialized review. Part of every raw gap is this.

**M2 — Scarcity-induced queueing behind rights (disparate impact, design-level).** 7 bilingual caseworkers and 5 accommodation specialists are hard capacity ceilings. When the only route to an entitlement is a scarce queue, the entitlement carries a delay penalty that falls on defined groups. No SLA exists to even measure the breach. Generates exactly the observed pattern: large timing penalties (3.8 d, 4.7 d) concentrated on the groups exercising rights.

**M3 — Accessibility mismatch inflating `documentation_completeness` (contaminated covariate).** If forms, evidence requirements, and notifications are majority-language and web-first, minority-language and in-person applicants submit "incomplete" files at higher rates for reasons unrelated to their actual eligibility. The process then treats the symptom (request more documents, +9.5 pp) instead of the cause. Controlling for `documentation_completeness` then *credits the process for a barrier it created*, which is why the 5.5 pp residual for B should be read as a floor, not a ceiling.

**M4 — Handover-induced escalation and duplicated verification (concerning, structural).** General  bilingual  general and general  specialist  supervisor chains lose context, so the receiving worker re-verifies and escalates. This generates Supervisor Review uplift (+8.0 pp for B), extra document requests, and rework (22–24% for B/C) *without any individual acting with bias*.

**M5 — Discretion-threshold asymmetry (concerning, possibly discriminatory).** Caseworkers may set lower thresholds for escalation or for document requests when communication is harder, when there is no shared language, or when prior cases exist ("more scrutiny where more friction"). This would appear as adjusted residual differences in *discretionary* activities (Request Missing Documents, Supervisor Review) while *rule-based* activities (Fraud Review after high risk) remain proportionate — which is broadly the observed pattern (B: +9.5 pp request-docs and +8.0 pp supervisor review vs. D: +1.5 pp and +2.0 pp despite far higher risk).

**M6 — Feedback loop of cumulative disadvantage (concerning, dynamic).** Adverse or delayed outcomes  appeal  `prior_case_count`   `risk_score_band`   Fraud Review route  longer cases  more appeals (D: 16% raw, +5.7 pp adjusted). A neutral-sounding scoring rule can encode and amplify historical disparity across cases.

**M7 — Access/self-selection channel effect (mixed).** In-person use (B 38%) reflects limited digital access, literacy, or trust rather than preference; in-person steps have longer identity-verification waits (1.4 d vs 0.8 d). This is partly a legitimate operational difference and partly an accessibility failure.

**M8 — Explanation quality driving appeal/rework (concerning, unmeasured).** Higher appeal and reopen rates at lower approval rates (B, D) is the classic signature of decisions that were not well explained, not translated, or not appealable in practice. If the same evidence produced a clear, translated explanation, some appeals would be unnecessary and some rework would disappear. This mechanism is entirely consistent with the data and entirely unmeasured.

# 5. Limitations of available attributes

1. **Measurement bias in `documentation_completeness`.** It is recorded by the agency against agency-defined requirements, in the agency's working language. It therefore measures *compliance with an accessibility-contingent standard*, not applicant diligence. As a control variable it is **endogenous and contaminated**: using it removes part of the very disadvantage under examination. It should be treated as a mediator, not a confounder.

2. **`risk_score_band` is not neutral.** It is derived from `prior_case_count` and other historical behavior. If prior case counts reflect prior access barriers, prior miscommunication, or prior differential enforcement, the score inherits and re-encodes that history. Its measurement error is also plausibly *differential* (fewer interpretable signals for minority-language applicants  more conservative scoring). Treating it as an unquestioned complexity control would launder historical disparity into "explained variance."

3. **Missing applicant-side needs.** Nothing captures literacy, digital access, caregiving obligations, health status, transportation, housing stability, immigration-related caution, or whether a disability was *disclosed*. `disability_accommodation_requested` is a request indicator, not a need indicator; hidden and undisclosed needs are invisible, so Group C is not "applicants with needs" but "applicants who asked." Conversely `language_preference` records a preference, not proficiency or the presence of a family interpreter.

4. **Unobserved resource workload.** Workload is available only at daily granularity. We cannot compute true queue length at the moment of assignment, caseworker-level case mix, part-time/FTE status, seniority, language certification level, or simultaneous non-log work (training, cover). Therefore the 3.8 d and 4.7 d queues cannot be cleanly attributed to staffing scarcity vs. case difficulty vs. work distribution, and per-worker load figures (§3.4) are indicative only.

5. **Appeal and rework quality is unmeasured.** `appeal_flag` records that an appeal happened, not whether it was substantively successful, how much effort it cost the applicant, whether legal aid was available, or whether the appeal reviewed the merits versus procedure. A high appeal rate can mean poor first-instance decisions *or* an accessible and functioning appeal system — the log cannot distinguish these.

6. **`case_outcome` is thin.** It is not stated to record benefit level, duration, conditionality, partial awards, time-to-benefit, or the quality/explainability of the decision. Two cases with `case_outcome = denied` can be procedurally very different. **Dignity, respect, and comprehensibility of treatment are not in the log at all** — yet these are core components of procedural fairness.

7. **Selection and survivorship.** Only submitted applications appear. Deterred, abandoned, or never-attempted applicants (likely over-represented among language and accommodation needs) are absent. All estimates describe *survivors* and likely understate barriers.

8. **Granularity and censoring.** Activity names collapse real interactions ("Language Support Contact" could be a voicemail or a 60-minute interpreted interview). Waiting-time splits depend on `org:resource` being correctly stamped. Right-censoring of open cases and the appeal loop (V6 at 30.7 d) can bias duration comparisons.

9. **Interactions and small cells.** Intersectional subgroups (language × accommodation × high risk) will be small; estimates there are exploratory. Absence of evidence in those cells is not evidence of absence of harm.

# 6. Mitigation options and side effects

| # | Mitigation | Rationale | Side effects / trade-offs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Add capacity for language support and accommodation review** (raise bilingual caseworkers above 7 and specialists above 5; remove the 2 specialists' dual supervisor-review role) | Attacks the 3.8 d / 4.7 d queues and the ~100-case-per-worker concentration, the clearest actionable disparity | Cost; recruitment lead time for credentialed bilingual/accommodation staff is long; risk of a temporary quality dip from rapid hiring; removing dual roles reduces supervisor-review throughput elsewhere and may shift the backlog to other groups. Must be paired with capacity *elsewhere* to avoid reallocating delay rather than removing it. |
| 2 | **Service-level targets for support steps** (e.g., language support contact within X days; accommodation review decided before eligibility assessment; translated notification within Y days of decision draft) | The policy calls these rights but sets no timeliness threshold, so they are invisible to management. SLAs make the delay measurable and enforceable | Targets create new reporting burden and can become compliance theatre ("contact logged"  need met); risk of *degrading* the substance of support to hit the clock (shorter, less adequate calls); risk of gaming (marking contact complete at first attempt). Must define SLAs in terms of need resolved, not step performed. |
| 3 | **Routing redesign to reduce handovers** (single case owner who retains the file across bilingual/specialist contact, with the specialist acting as embedded consultant; escalation-only supervisor review) | Cuts duplicated verification, re-documentation, and handover-induced escalation (+8.0 pp supervisor review for B), and removes the generalspecialistsupervisor chain | Requires cross-training and job redesign; concentrates authority in fewer hands (new concentration/conflict risk); case owners may become bottlenecks; may reduce the beneficial "fresh eyes" check that catches errors. Do **not** remove Language Support Contact or Accommodation Review — move them earlier and keep them (as V4 already begins to do). |
| 4 | **Decision-explanation improvements** (translated, plain-language decision letters specifying evidence relied on, reasons for each requirement, and appeal rights; pre-decision explanation call for denials) | Plausibly targets the largest unexplained residual (D's +5.7 pp appeal; B's 22% rework) and the dignity/explainability gap | Production cost and turnaround time for translation; templated letters can read as bureaucratic and *reduce* perceived legitimacy; if letters lengthen `Decision Draft  Notification`, timeliness worsens slightly. Measure appeal *volume* and appeal *outcome*, not volume alone — a well-explained system may see appeals rise then fall. |
| 5 | **Monitoring adjusted disparities** (standing dashboard: raw + Layer 1 + Layer 2 estimates, per region and channel, with overlap diagnostics and the four-way classification (i)–(iv)) | Converts one-off analysis into governance; prevents the two failure modes (dismissing gaps as "case mix", or treating raw gaps as proof) | Analyst capacity; multiple-comparison and false-alarm risk across many cells; risk of **metric fixation** (managing the residual rather than the process); adjustments can drift in meaning as covariates change — the dashboard must version its specification. |
| 6 | **Audit `risk_score_band` and `documentation_completeness`** (test predictive validity by group; quantify differential misclassification; run the leave-one-covariate-out and tipping-point analyses as standing checks) | Both variables plausibly encode historical and accessibility bias and are used as *justification* for routing | Audit may reveal that a widely used control is unreliable, destabilizing current case-mix explanations and requiring operational change; limited ground truth for "true risk"; legitimate risk management could be weakened if bands are diluted — mitigate by auditing *measurement* and *error rates* rather than simply lowering weights, and keep human review of fraud-review routing. |
| 7 *(supplementary)* | **Case-ownership at intake + proactive accessibility outreach** (requirements check-in at submit, pre-emptive translation of the evidence checklist) | Targets M3 upstream: fewer avoidable "incomplete" files, fewer document-request loops | Requires earlier contact (small delay at intake); risk of over-collecting sensitive information; if requirements are simplified for some groups and not others, consistency questions arise. |
| 8 *(supplementary)* | **Load-balancing for specialized queues and regional backlogs** | Addresses the two-region concentration | Can move delay to general queues; may pull specialists away from complex cases; must be monitored with the same subgroup dashboard to detect burden-shifting. |

Sequencing note: 1, 3 and 7 reduce *delay*; 4 and 6 address *discretion and explanation*; 2 and 5 make the system *accountable*. Doing only 2 or 5 risks producing a well-measured unfair process.

# 8. Bottom-line assessment

**Assessment: the process is procedurally fair in part, but shows real and correctable procedural-burden disparities; on discrimination the evidence is indeterminate, and on design-level disparate impact it is fairly strong.** Confidence: moderate on the timing/resource findings, low-to-moderate on the discrimination question.

What the evidence supports reasonably well:
- Raw outcome differences are substantially attributable to documented case mix. It would be wrong to read the 20 pp raw approval spread as discrimination.
- Rule-based routing is broadly consistent with policy: Group D's heavier Fraud Review and Supervisor Review load is proportionate to its measured risk profile and modest after adjustment (+2.0 pp).
- There is a genuine **burden and timeliness disparity concentrated at rights-preserving steps**. Groups B and C face 3.8- and 4.7-day queues that exist only for people exercising language or accommodation rights, plus longer document-request loops and heavier handover chains. Adjusted delays (+2.6 d and +4.1 d) are of similar magnitude to those queues — so the timing gap is largely **scarcity and routing design**, not case complexity. Since there are no service-level targets for these steps, the harm is unmeasured as well as unaddressed. This is a fairness concern even if no decision-maker intends disadvantage.
- Group D faces the largest **adjusted appeal gap** (+5.7 pp) alongside the lowest approval rate; combined with 20% rework, this raises a fair concern about the adequacy of explanations and about the `prior_case_count`/`risk_score_score` feedback loop.

What the evidence does **not** support:
- It does not establish discrimination. Residual adjusted differences (5.5 pp for B, 2.2 pp for C, 3.1 pp for D) are consistent with several alternatives: unmeasured applicant needs, accessibility-driven documentation gaps, handover-induced escalation, and differential measurement error in `risk_score_band`. Two of the most decision-relevant covariates are themselves possibly biased in the direction of explaining away disadvantage, so the residuals should be read as lower bounds on non-complexity-driven disparity, not as proof of disparate treatment.
- It does not establish that support steps are the problem. Language Support Contact and Accommodation Review are rights-preserving and their *sequencing* is broadly sound (V4 places language support before the completeness check). The problem is that these steps are thinly staffed, un-SLA'd, and hand back the case to a generalist.
- It cannot speak to dignity, comprehensibility, or the quality of support delivery — dimensions where the largest unobserved harms may sit.

Calibrated conclusion: call the process **"partly fair on outcomes, procedurally uneven in burden and timeliness, indeterminate on disparate treatment, and demonstrably under-resourced at the points where it protects rights."** The priority finding is not the approval gap but the fact that **exercising a right currently costs the applicant days of delay and extra handovers**. The indicated response is capacity and service-level targets for support steps, routing continuity to reduce handovers, better decision explanations, and continued adjusted monitoring — **not** the removal or streamlining of the support steps themselves, which would improve cycle time by withdrawing procedural protection. Any conclusion should be revised after the waiting-time decomposition, the within-variant comparisons, the risk-score misclassification sensitivity analysis, and the region/channel subgroup runs are completed.
