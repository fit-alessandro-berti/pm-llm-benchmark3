## 2. Rule Interpretation

RR1 — Drafter/approver separation: For each case, after alias normalization and reliable service-account attribution, the human who performs `Draft_Change_Request` must not perform `Approve_Change`. A single human using different aliases counts as the same actor.

RR2 — Implementer/verifier separation: For each case, after normalization/attribution, the human who performs `Implement_Change` must not perform `Verify_Implementation`. A service account cannot satisfy this by itself; reliable session evidence must map the action to an authorized human.

RR3 — High-risk approval authority: For HIGH-risk non-emergency cases, `Approve_Change` must be performed by the QM or a valid QM delegate. A QM delegation is valid only if:
- the effective interval covers the approval timestamp;
- the delegation scope covers the case scope/change type;
- the principal-absence or unavailability condition is satisfied when required;
- no prohibited subdelegation occurred.

RR4 — Emergency substitution: For a HIGH-risk emergency, initial approval may be by the IC or a valid IC delegate if the emergency flag and safety rationale are credible. A QM or valid QM delegate must then perform `Retrospective_QM_Review` within two business days. I count business days as Monday–Friday, excluding site holidays, starting with the first business day after the emergency approval/implementation. For 2026-08-11 Tuesday, the two-business-day deadline is end of 2026-08-13 Thursday. CHG-505 review on 2026-08-14 Friday is late; CHG-510 review on 2026-08-12 is within time but still needs valid QM authority.

Action by a principal while on leave: Leave does not automatically remove the principal’s standing role. If Asha personally performs a QM action and identity is authenticated, the action can be technically authorized. However, leave evidence weakens the principal-absence condition for delegations and creates an identity/credential-sharing caveat.

Later database entry versus earlier delegation: A later QMS database record created during a migration cannot by itself prove that a delegation existed earlier. A contemporaneous signed PDF or other pre-event evidence can. D-QM-01 is supported by a signed PDF timestamped 2026-08-09; the later DB record is corroborative, not primary.

RR5 — Identity evidence: Service accounts are not human identities. They support conformance only if reliable session evidence maps the action to an authorized human. Unresolved service-account actions should be classified as data/identity problems, with a secondary unauthorized flag only if attribution is sufficiently strong.

RR6 — Suspicion versus proof: Repeated resource pairs, reciprocal approvals, shared workstations, and concentrated handovers are indicators. They are not proof of collusion without corroborating evidence such as session, badge, MFA, communication, financial, or process-quality evidence.

## 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

Repeated pair: Clara drafted and Ben approved 18 of 23 HIGH-risk production changes. Benign explanation: they work in the same production cell, Ben had valid D-QM-01 coverage during Asha’s leave, and the pair is operationally stable. Adverse explanation: routine concentration bypasses independent approval, creates reciprocal obligation, or masks collusion. Discriminating evidence: approval comments, review duration, whether Ben actually assessed risk, email/chat traffic, rotation policy, comparison to other cells, and whether approvals occurred only when Asha was truly absent.

Reciprocal pattern: Ben drafted LOW-risk training changes that Clara approved. Benign explanation: peer review of low-risk training documents in the same operational area. Adverse explanation: mutual approval trading to avoid independent review. Discriminating evidence: frequency versus peers, whether Clara had authority/competence, content sensitivity, financial or personal benefit, and whether approvals were substantive.

Shared workstation WS-DM-04 for CHG-503: Benign explanation: shared terminal, Diego present, another authorized human used `svc_qms`, or a system process ran verification. Adverse explanation: Diego implemented and then verified using a service account to hide identity. Discriminating evidence: re-authentication logs, session token source, CCTV, badge timeline, command-line provenance, and whether the verification had human judgment content.

Absence conflicts: Asha approves while on leave; Ben’s account approves during sick leave and Ben denies. Benign explanations: urgent work despite leave, stale leave record, unlocked session, or office workstation use before departure. Adverse explanations: credential sharing, proxy approval, stolen credentials, or deliberate account misuse. Discriminating evidence: VPN IP/device, MFA token logs, badge access, workstation session start/end, file artifacts, and contemporaneous communications.

Credential compromise: CHG-506 is the strongest single identity concern. Ben says he did not perform the approval, and the IP was an office workstation during sick leave. Benign explanation: Ben logged in earlier and someone reused an unlocked session; adverse explanation: shared credentials or unauthorized account use. Discriminating evidence: MFA challenge, workstation login time, badge entry, session termination, and keyboard/session recording if available.

Service accounts: CHG-503 is unresolved and should not be treated as human conformance. CHG-509 is more strongly tied to Eva. Even then, a service account is not a human identity; it only supports conformance if session evidence is reliable and the mapped human is authorized. Benign explanation: shared terminal or automated support. Adverse explanation: identity concealment or self-verification. Discriminating evidence: individual authentication, FIDO token use, process owner, and session re-authentication.

Network interpretation: handover and working-together edges are indicators. They should be correlated with absence, delegation scope, service-account attribution, and event-level SoD failures. Repeated collaboration alone is not proof of collusion.

## 6. Control Improvements

Delegation creation: Move all delegations into one controlled QMS workflow with immutable audit trail, mandatory scope, activity, start/end, principal, delegate, and no-subdelegation flag. Require pre-approval before use.

Effective-time recording: Record both the signed effective timestamp and the system-entry timestamp. Do not let migration-created database records replace contemporaneous evidence. Version delegation records and preserve source PDFs.

Principal availability: Integrate leave and unavailability status with the QMS. If a principal performs an action while marked absent, trigger a review. If a delegation requires principal absence, block delegate use unless absence is confirmed at event time.

Alias management: Maintain a central identity map and normalize aliases at ingestion. Prevent the same human from drafting and approving through different user IDs.

Individual authentication: Prohibit shared accounts for controlled change activities. Require MFA/FIDO and re-authentication for approval and verification. Terminate inactive sessions and tie sessions to individual humans.

Emergency review: Automatically calculate the two-business-day retrospective QM deadline. Require emergency safety rationale, IC/IC-delegate evidence, and a QM delegate whose scope covers the emergency change type. Escalate overdue reviews.

Pair rotation: Monitor repeated drafter-approver and implementer-verifier pairs. Rotate pairs or require secondary independent review when concentration exceeds thresholds.

Network monitoring: Use PM4Py handover/working-together networks as indicators only. Correlate with session, badge, MFA, absence, and delegation data before drawing conclusions.

Evidential retention: Retain session logs, MFA, badge access, workstation assignments, CCTV where available, delegation PDFs, QMS audit trails, and approval comments under a defined retention/legal-hold policy.
