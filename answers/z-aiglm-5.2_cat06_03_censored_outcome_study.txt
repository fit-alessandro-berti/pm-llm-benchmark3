## 1. Target definition

**Primary target formulation:** Time-to-default after activation with right censoring (survival analysis).
- **Prediction time:** Either after the 3rd event of the application process or after 48 hours have elapsed since `Application Started`, whichever occurs first.
- **Horizon:** 90 days after `Account Activated`.
- **Unit of analysis:** The credit application case (`case:concept:name`).
- **Population:** All applications that eventually reach `Account Activated`. Rejected, withdrawn, and transferred cases are excluded from the primary training population because the target (default) is undefined without credit exposure.
- **Estimand:** The survival function $S(t | X_{prefix}) = P(T > t | X_{prefix}, \text{Activated})$, specifically evaluating $1 - S(90)$ to obtain the probability of default within 90 days. 

This formulation is statistically defensible because it respects the fact that default is only observed post-activation and naturally handles delayed labels and right-censored unmatured accounts. 

**Alternative target formulation:** A multi-state competing-outcome model.
- **Target:** The probability of transitioning to one of several absorbing states (`Default`, `No Default (Matured)`, `Rejected`, `Withdrawn`, `Transferred`) from the observed prefix. 
- **Why it answers a different question:** This answers “what is the likely trajectory and final disposition of this application?” rather than “given the account is activated, what is its default risk?”. It avoids the selection bias of conditioning on activation but requires modeling pathways (e.g., rejection) that are outside the business question’s immediate focus on activated defaults.

## 2. Cohort construction

**As-of rules:** 
- The data extraction date is 15 February 2026. 
- An account is considered “matured” if `Account Activated` occurred at least 90 days before the extraction date (i.e., on or before 17 November 2025) and the outcome feed is present. 
- Applications still in process (not accepted/rejected/withdrawn/activated) at the extraction date are right-censored at their last observed event time.

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

- **Ordinary right censoring:** The 3,600 activated accounts whose 90-day window has not matured by 15 Feb 2026. These are included in the survival dataset with their last known event time as the censoring time.
- **Delayed labels:** The 7–35 day feed delay means an account might be matured in reality but missing the label in the warehouse. Survival analysis accommodates this naturally; if the feed arrives later, the record can be updated.
- **Selection through activation:** By conditioning the primary model only on activated accounts, we explicitly avoid the undefined counterfactual of default for rejected/withdrawn cases. We acknowledge this estimates risk *among the selected*.
- **Transfer as a competing event:** Applications transferred before activation never face our credit exposure. They are excluded from the primary survival model. 
- **Permanently missing outcome feeds:** Partner-originated accounts with missing feeds must be treated as right-censored. If missingness is informative (e.g., missing feeds correlate with risk), this introduces bias.

**Primary analysis strategy:** A discrete-time survival model (e.g., logistic regression or gradient boosted trees on person-period formatted data) predicting the hazard of default at each time step post-activation. Prefixes are used to predict this baseline risk.

**Sensitivity analysis:** Inverse Probability of Treatment Weighting (IPTW) to estimate what the default rate would be if all applications were activated. This requires modeling the probability of activation given the prefix (a selection model). This is *partially testable* (we can validate the activation propensity model) but the counterfactual outcome for non-activated cases is *fundamentally untestable*.

## 4. Prefix extraction

- **Fixed event-count prefixes:** We will use a prefix length of 3 events. For cases with fewer than 3 events, we pad with a special `<PAD>` activity or exclude them if too short to be informative.
- **Elapsed-time prefixes:** As an alternative, we will define a cutoff at 48 hours after `Application Started` and extract all events occurring within that window.
- **Repeated document loops:** The prefix extraction must include these loops (e.g., `Credit Review -> Request Documents -> Credit Review`) as they carry strong predictive signal. The event count naturally captures the number of loops.
- **Activity renaming:** Pre-15 October 2025 activity names are mapped to post-migration names using a dictionary. Duplicate events created by the migration are removed by retaining only the first event per `(case:concept:name, concept:name, time:timestamp)`.
- **Future prevention:** Events occurring after the 3rd event or after the 48-hour mark are completely discarded for feature generation.

## 5. Process-aware features

- **Control-flow:** Activity sequence (one-hot encoded or embeddings), count of rework loops, presence of specific activities (e.g., `Identity/KYC Check`).
- **Timing:** Time elapsed between `Application Started` and the 3rd event, average time between events.
- **Resource/queue:** Resource ID at the prefix events, historical throughput of that resource (calculated using a rolling window of *past* cases only), current queue length at the resource level at the time of the event.
- **Rework:** Number of document requests, time spent in rework.
- **Cohort/calendar features:** Month of application start, day of week, pre/post 1 July 2025 policy flag, pre/post 15 October 2025 migration flag.
- **Static case attributes:** `case:channel`, `case:region`, `case:requested_amount`.
- **Features requiring historical reference:** Moving average of approval rates for a given `case:region` or `org:resource` in the preceding 30 days.

## 7. Validation design

A random case split is highly misleading here because it would mix cases from the pre-policy, post-policy, and post-migration eras, allowing the model to learn era-specific artifacts and falsely generalize. 

**Temporal Validation Scheme:**
- **Training set:** Applications started between 1 January 2024 and 30 June 2025 (pre-policy change).
- **Validation set:** Applications started between 1 July 2025 and 14 October 2025 (post-policy, pre-migration).
- **Test set:** Applications started between 15 October 2025 and 31 December 2025 (post-migration).

**Evaluation:**
- **Backtesting:** Simulate predictions at the prediction point for the test set and evaluate against eventually observed outcomes (accounting for right censoring).
- **Calibration:** Assess calibration of predicted default probabilities using the Brier Score and calibration plots, specifically for the 90-day horizon.
- **Discrimination:** Use the time-dependent Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC) or Concordance Index (C-index) suitable for censored survival data.
- **Subgroup performance:** Evaluate calibration and AUC across `case:channel` and `case:region` to ensure the model does not exacerbate disparities, especially for partner-originated accounts.

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

- **What the study can claim:** The model predicts the association between early application prefixes and 90-day default risk *among accounts that are eventually activated*. 
- **What it cannot claim:** It cannot estimate the counterfactual default risk for an applicant if they had been accepted, because rejected/withdrawn cases are excluded. It is a model of selection-conditioned risk, not a model of inherent applicant creditworthiness.
- **Deployment risks (data-generating process changes):**
  1. If the model is used to automatically reject high-risk applications, the population of activated accounts will shift, reducing the observed default rate but invalidating the model’s baseline calibration.
  2. Applicants may adapt their behavior if they know the process is automated, altering the frequency of early events like document uploads.
  3. Resources may change their workflow (e.g., spending less time on `Credit Review` for flagged cases), altering the timing and rework features the model relies on.
