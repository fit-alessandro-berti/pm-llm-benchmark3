# 1. System boundary and assumptions

- **Order handling and availability:** `Receive order -> Availability check`, including partial shipment, backorder wait, and cancellation due to delay.

- **Made-to-order production:** `Plan production -> Batch wait -> CNC production -> Heat treatment -> Inspection -> Rework production -> Package reinforced -> Ship`.

- **Rework and quality loops:** inspection rework, packaging rework, rework packaging loops, and damage-related returns where they can be inferred from rework, returns, or policy violations.

**Outside the boundary**

- Corporate facilities, employee commuting, IT infrastructure, and non-allocated warehouse overhead.
- Product-design changes that are not represented in the event log.

**Key assumptions**

1. `promised_lead_time_hours` is a reasonable proxy for the customer’s service requirement.

**Remaining uncertainty**

# 3. Intervention options

### S1. Green shipping recommender using promised lead time, route reliability, and customer segment

- **Target path or segment:** Shipping transitions from `Package standard -> Ship ground/air/consolidated truck` and `Package reinforced -> Ship ground/air/dedicated van`; especially Standard-stock Service-contract air shipping and MTO air shipping.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Recommend ground or consolidated truck when the promised lead time can still be met with probability above 85%, per SP1. Air becomes an exception only when slack is insufficient or reliability is poor.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Transport emissions fall without automatically degrading service if the recommender uses reliability and slack. Cycle time may increase relative to air, but only when the promise is still achievable. Resilience improves if the recommender also requires an alternative route for Service-contract customers.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `route_mode` by segment, `transport_kgco2e`, `promised_lead_time_hours`, actual cycle time, late-delivery rate, `expedite_flag`, SP1 violations, and override reasons.

### S2. Dynamic batching for CNC and heat treatment with maximum waiting-time caps by segment

- **Target path or segment:** `Plan production -> Batch wait -> CNC production -> Heat treatment -> Inspection`, especially MTO Regular and MTO Service-contract orders.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Form batches only when energy savings are material and due-date slack exists. CNC_A energy per item drops from 2.8 kWh at batch 1–3 to 1.3 kWh at batch 12–20; heat-treatment energy per item is lowest above batch 10.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Energy falls, but batch wait can rise from 4h to 38h and heat-batch waiting is associated with 1.7x higher late-delivery risk. Therefore, segment-specific maximum waiting-time caps are required. Large batches also increase obsolescence risk, so forecast confidence should gate batch size.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `batch_size`, `batch_id`, batch wait duration, `energy_kwh`, late delivery by segment, heat-treatment waiting paths, and items not shipped within 14 days.

### S3. Shift Made-to-order production from CNC_B to CNC_A when queue and due-date slack allow

- **Target path or segment:** `MTO -> batch wait -> CNC_B -> inspection -> rework -> ground`, which has 32 kWh/order, 1.8 kg scrap/order, 32% late delivery, and rework-dominated impact.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** CNC_B is faster but has 18% rework versus 9% for CNC_A. Routing quality-sensitive or rework-prone MTO parts to CNC_A reduces scrap, rework energy, and downstream handling.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Material and rework impact fall, but cycle time may increase if CNC_A queues are longer or batching waits are required. Late-delivery risk rises if CNC_A capacity is insufficient, so the rule must include due-date slack and queue-time caps.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `machine_group`, rework loops from `Inspection -> Rework production`, `Rework production -> CNC production`, queue times, batch wait, late delivery, and scrap by machine.

### S4. Replace air for Standard-stock Service-contract orders with priority pick + ground unless slack is insufficient

- **Target path or segment:** `Stock -> standard pack -> air`, especially Standard-stock Service-contract cases where SP1 is violated in 22% of cases.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Evidence says many Standard-stock Service-contract orders are shipped by air even when promised lead time still allows ground plus priority picking. Air emits roughly 4–6x more than ground, while ground remains serviceable if picking is prioritized.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Transport emissions fall. Median cycle time may increase relative to air because air cuts median transport time by 24h, but if the promise still allows ground, late-delivery risk should remain controlled. Resilience is neutral or improved if priority picking does not consume all emergency capacity.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `customer_type`, `case_type`, `route_mode`, priority-pick flag if added, `promised_lead_time_hours`, late delivery, SP1 violations, and air share among Standard-stock Service-contract orders.

### S5. Consolidate truck departures twice daily for non-urgent stock orders

- **Target path or segment:** `Package standard -> Ship ground` for non-urgent Standard-stock Regular and some Standard-stock Service-contract orders with sufficient slack.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Consolidated trucks reduce transport kgCO2e by about 35–55% versus individual ground parcels.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Transport emissions fall, but consolidation requires a 12–24h wait. This can raise cycle time and late-delivery risk if applied to urgent or promise-sensitive orders. Resilience risk increases if too many orders depend on only two departures, so alternative routes must remain available for Service-contract customers.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `route_mode = consolidated_truck`, transport emissions, consolidation wait, late delivery by segment, alternative-route availability, and cancellation due to delay.

### S6. Improve packaging-route compatibility checks before shipment

- **Target path or segment:** `Package standard -> Rework packaging`, `Package reinforced -> Rework packaging`, and `Rework packaging -> Package reinforced/standard`.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Packaging rework occurs when packaging choice is incompatible with route mode or part geometry. Preventing incompatible packaging reduces extra handling, packaging material, and possible re-shipment.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Material use and rework fall. Cycle time may improve because packaging rework loops are avoided. Late-delivery risk may fall if rework packaging previously caused shipment delays. Resilience is neutral if the compatibility rules do not remove viable packaging options.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** packaging rework loops, `package_material_kg`, `route_mode`, part geometry or weight attributes if available, rework flag, and downstream returns.

### S7. Add inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations

- **Target path or segment:** `Inspection -> Rework production`, especially CNC_B parts and MTO parts with high rework rates.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Rushed inspections miss defects that later cause packaging rework or customer returns. A quality gate catches defects earlier, reducing rework loops, scrap, and return transport.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Quality improves and hidden material waste falls, but inspection queues may lengthen and cycle time may increase. Late-delivery risk can rise in the short term if inspection capacity is not adjusted. Resilience improves if fewer customer returns and emergency reworks occur.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** inspection queue time, `rework_flag`, `Inspection -> Rework production`, `Rework production -> CNC production`, damage returns, Q1 certification events, and late delivery.

### S8. Replace some dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes

- **Target path or segment:** `Critical -> emergency production -> reinforced pack -> dedicated van`, where transport averages 52 kgCO2e/order and dedicated vans often return empty.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Shared emergency lanes can reduce empty van returns and improve load utilization while preserving faster-than-normal service for true downtime events.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Transport emissions may fall, but if the shared lane becomes scheduled or capacity-constrained, Critical-downtime service may deteriorate. Resilience risk increases if one shared lane becomes a single point of failure; an alternative route or backup van capacity is needed.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `route_mode = dedicated_van`, Critical-downtime justification events, empty-return proxy if available, late delivery for Critical cases, expedite flags, and alternative-route availability.

### S9. Split Critical-downtime into true downtime and precautionary urgent categories

- **Target path or segment:** `Critical-downtime Service-contract`, especially emergency production and dedicated-van paths.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Not all Critical-downtime orders have the same urgency. True downtime can retain dedicated van or emergency production; precautionary urgent orders can use priority pick, air only if needed, or ground if slack exists.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Transport emissions fall for precautionary urgent orders. True-downtime service is protected. Misclassification risk is real: if urgent orders are incorrectly downgraded, late delivery and customer dissatisfaction rise; if orders are incorrectly upgraded, emissions rebound.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** Critical-downtime justification, `expedite_flag`, `route_mode`, late delivery by true versus precautionary urgent category, SP2 violations, and cancellation due to delay.

### S10. Introduce carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular made-to-order customers

- **Target path or segment:** `Made-to-order Regular`, which has 94h median cycle time, 18% late delivery, 18.5 kWh energy, and 0.90 kg scrap per order.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Offer a longer but more realistic promise date in exchange for ground shipping, consolidated truck, or batching. This reduces the need for air, emergency production, and rushed low-quality production.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Environmental impact falls, but promised cycle time increases. Late-delivery risk may fall if promises become more realistic. Resilience may improve if fewer emergency paths are triggered, but customer behavior risk exists if customers repeatedly request expedite after negotiating longer promises.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `promised_lead_time_hours`, actual cycle time, late delivery, route mode, batch size, expedite requests, cancellations, and acceptance of negotiated promises.

### S11. Reduce partial shipments by changing availability-check rules

- **Target path or segment:** `Pick from stock -> Partial shipment -> Backorder wait -> Pick from stock/Cancel due to delay`, and the path `Stock -> partial shipment -> ground + backorder`, which has 79h median cycle time and 24% late delivery.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Avoidable partial shipments create second shipments, extra packaging, and higher customer contact. Changing availability rules can reduce split shipments when a complete shipment is feasible within the promise or when the promise can be renegotiated.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Transport and packaging impact fall. Cycle time may increase if orders wait for complete availability, but late-delivery risk may fall because the partial/backorder path is already high-risk. Resilience may improve if fewer backorder loops occur, but urgent line items must not be delayed unnecessarily.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** partial shipment frequency, backorder wait duration, second shipment count, late delivery, cancellations, and `route_mode` for partial versus complete shipments.

### S12. Create a sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides

- **Target path or segment:** All shipping, van, packaging, and quality decisions where SP1, SP2, or Q1 is overridden.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** The exception log does not directly reduce emissions, but it makes unjustified overrides visible and enables policy tuning. It prevents “green” rules from being silently bypassed.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Neutral to positive. It preserves flexibility for genuine service, quality, or resilience exceptions while reducing drift. It also improves governance during disruptions, when precision is low.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** override events, reason codes, approver, carbon impact, service impact, late delivery, rework, and repeat violations by customer, route, machine, or packaging type.

# 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching rebound: lower energy per item but higher waiting and obsolescence**  
   Larger CNC_A or heat-treatment batches reduce energy per item, but batch wait can rise from 4h to 38h, and heat-batch waiting is associated with 1.7x higher late-delivery risk. Large batches also increase obsolescence: 6% of batch-produced items are not shipped within 14 days when forecasts are wrong. Mitigation: segment-specific maximum batch wait, forecast-confidence gates, and obsolescence tracking.

2. **Transport rebound: reducing air may increase emergency vans or future expedite requests**  
   If ground or consolidated truck causes missed promises, customers or staff may respond by requesting air, dedicated vans, or emergency production more often. This can shift emissions from parcel air to vans or emergency paths. Mitigation: use SP1’s 85% reliability logic, monitor expedite flags, and require exception justification.

3. **Packaging rebound: reinforced packaging reduces returns but increases material use**  
   Reinforced packaging reduces damage-related returns by 40% for heavy MTO parts, but it doubles package material. Over-applying reinforced packaging can increase material impact without reducing returns. Mitigation: enforce Q1 only where required, allow inspection certification for low damage risk, and monitor package material versus returns.

4. **Quality-gate rebound: better inspection reduces rework but may create late-delivery pressure**  
   An inspection quality gate can reduce hidden rework and returns, but if inspection queues grow, staff may rush inspections again or customers may demand air to compensate for production delay. Mitigation: monitor inspection queue time, first-pass yield, and late delivery together.

5. **Machine-routing rebound: moving work from CNC_B to CNC_A may overload CNC_A**  
   CNC_A has lower rework, but if all MTO work is shifted to CNC_A, queues and batch waits may increase, raising late-delivery risk. Mitigation: use due-date slack, queue-time caps, and capacity-aware routing rather than a blanket machine switch.

6. **Partial-shipment rebound: holding orders for complete shipment may delay urgent line items**  
   Reducing partial shipments can lower second-shipment emissions, but if a complete shipment is held too long, a critical line item may miss its promise and trigger cancellation or emergency transport. Mitigation: allow partial shipment only when promised, or split promise dates by line item where possible.

7. **Critical-downtime segmentation rebound: misclassification can harm service or emissions**  
   If precautionary urgent orders are incorrectly classified as true downtime, emissions remain high. If true downtime orders are incorrectly downgraded, service and customer risk increase. Mitigation: require explicit justification events for dedicated vans and monitor late delivery by true versus precautionary urgent categories.

8. **Consolidation and shared-lane rebound: fewer departures can reduce resilience**  
   Consolidated trucks and shared emergency courier lanes reduce emissions, but they can create dependency on few departures or lanes. During carrier disruption, Service-contract customers may lose alternative routes. Mitigation: enforce R1, maintain backup capacity, and monitor alternative-route availability.

# 6. Balanced recommendation

The recommended redesign is a **slack-aware, quality-first fulfillment package**. It does not pursue the lowest-emission option in isolation. Instead, it changes default paths only when promised lead time, reliability, quality, and resilience can be protected.

## Core redesign principles

1. **Default to ground or consolidated truck when slack is sufficient.**  
   Use SP1’s probability rule: ground or consolidated truck is preferred when the promised lead time can still be met with probability above 85%.

2. **Treat air and dedicated vans as justified exceptions, not automatic choices.**  
   Air may be appropriate for true Critical-downtime or insufficient slack. Dedicated vans require explicit Critical-downtime justification and an alternative route.

3. **Do not optimize energy per item if it creates waiting, obsolescence, or late delivery.**  
   Batching is beneficial only with maximum waiting-time caps by segment and forecast confidence.

4. **Protect quality first.**  
   Rework, returns, and packaging rework are hidden environmental costs. The redesign should reduce rework loops even if that adds short-term inspection time.

5. **Preserve resilience for Service-contract and Critical-downtime customers.**  
   Consolidation and shared emergency lanes must not remove alternative routes or emergency capacity.

## Recommended package

### A. Promise and criticality governance

- **Split Critical-downtime into true downtime and precautionary urgent orders** using S9.
  - True downtime: emergency production, reinforced packaging, and dedicated van are allowed when justified.
  - Precautionary urgent: priority pick, air only if needed, or ground if slack exists.
- **Introduce carbon-aware promise negotiation for Regular MTO customers** using S10.
  - Offer longer realistic promises in exchange for batching and ground shipping.
- **Reduce avoidable partial shipments** using S11.
  - Change availability-check rules so partial shipments are not used when backorder wait would cause high late risk.
- **Create a sustainability exception log** using S12.
  - Record SP1, SP2, and Q1 overrides with reason, approver, service impact, and environmental impact.

### B. Production redesign

- **Use dynamic batching with segment-specific waiting caps** using S2.
  - Batch CNC_A and heat treatment only when due-date slack exists.
  - Cap batch wait to prevent 38h batch waits from destroying service.
  - Track obsolescence for items not shipped within 14 days.
- **Prefer CNC_A over CNC_B when queue and due-date slack allow** using S3.
  - Target the high-impact `MTO -> batch wait -> CNC_B -> inspection -> rework -> ground` path.
  - Keep CNC_B for genuinely urgent, low-rework parts when CNC_A cannot meet the promise.
- **Add an inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations** using S7.
  - Reduce rushed inspections that create later packaging or customer-return rework.
  - Monitor inspection queues to avoid creating new late-delivery bottlenecks.

### C. Logistics redesign

- **Deploy a green shipping recommender** using S1.
  - Recommend ground or consolidated truck when SP1 probability is above 85%.
  - Use customer segment and route reliability to prevent service deterioration.
- **Replace unjustified air for Standard-stock Service-contract orders with priority pick + ground** using S4.
  - Target the 22% SP1 violations in this segment.
  - Keep air when ground plus priority picking cannot meet the promise.
- **Use consolidated truck departures twice daily for non-urgent stock orders** using S5.
  - Apply only where slack absorbs the 12–24h consolidation wait.
  - Maintain alternative routes for Service-contract customers.
- **Add packaging-route compatibility checks before shipment** using S6.
  - Reduce `Package standard/reinforced -> Rework packaging` loops.
  - Prevent packaging choices that are incompatible with route mode or part geometry.
- **Replace some dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes** using S8.
  - Reduce empty van returns while preserving emergency service.
  - Require backup routing to protect resilience.

## Segment-level playbook

| Segment | Recommended default path | Allowed exceptions | Key guardrails |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Standard-stock Regular** | Pick stock -> standard packaging -> ground; consolidated truck if slack | Air only if ground reliability or promise slack is insufficient | Monitor late delivery, partial shipments, and cancellations |
| **Standard-stock Service-contract** | Priority pick -> standard packaging -> ground; consolidated truck if slack | Air only when SP1 probability falls below 85% or customer SLA requires it | Reduce SP1 violations; preserve alternative route for R1 |
| **Made-to-order Regular** | Negotiated promise -> batched CNC_A/heat -> inspection -> reinforced or standard packaging -> ground/consolidated | Air or CNC_B only when due-date slack is genuinely insufficient | Cap batch wait; monitor obsolescence and late delivery |
| **Made-to-order Service-contract** | Batched CNC_A when slack allows -> inspection -> reinforced packaging if Q1 applies -> ground/air based on slack | Air only if promise risk is high; CNC_B only for urgent low-rework parts | Monitor rework, returns, Q1 compliance, and late delivery |
| **Critical-downtime Service-contract** | True downtime: emergency production/reinforced packaging/dedicated van with justification; precautionary urgent: priority pick + air/ground based on slack | Dedicated van only for true downtime or explicit exception | SP2 justification, alternative route, late delivery, and customer impact |

## Why this package is balanced

- It targets the highest-impact paths without banning fast transport:
  - `Stock -> standard pack -> air`
  - `MTO -> CNC immediately -> inspection -> reinforced pack -> air`
  - `Critical -> emergency production -> reinforced pack -> dedicated van`
  - `MTO -> batch wait -> CNC_B -> inspection -> rework -> ground`
- It reduces hidden waste from rework and packaging loops, not just visible energy or transport.
- It protects service by using probability, slack, and segment-specific caps rather than blanket rules.
- It preserves resilience by requiring alternative routes, exception governance, and backup emergency capacity.
- It avoids the lowest-emission trap: for example, it does not replace all air with ground, all vans with shared lanes, or all production with maximum batching.

## Conformance monitoring

### SP1: ground or consolidated truck when promise probability is above 85%

- **Check:** For each order, compare `route_mode` against predicted probability that ground or consolidated truck can meet `promised_lead_time_hours`.
- **Baseline signal:** SP1 is violated in 22% of Standard-stock Service-contract cases.
- **Monitor after implementation:**
  - SP1 violation rate by segment.
  - Air share by customer type.
  - Late delivery among orders shifted from air to ground.
  - Override reasons for air use.
- **Decision rule:** If ground substitution increases late delivery materially, tighten the probability threshold or restrict the policy to segments with more slack.

### SP2: dedicated vans require explicit Critical-downtime justification

- **Check:** Every `Dedicated van` event must have a linked Critical-downtime justification event.
- **Baseline signal:** SP2 is missing justification in 31% of dedicated-van cases.
- **Monitor after implementation:**
  - Missing justification rate.
  - Dedicated-van share by true downtime versus precautionary urgent.
  - Late delivery for Critical-downtime orders.
  - Empty-return proxy or van utilization.
- **Decision rule:** If missing justifications remain high, require system-enforced justification before van booking. If true-downtime service deteriorates, restore dedicated-van capacity or add backup courier lanes.

### Q1: Made-to-order parts over 8 kg must use reinforced packaging unless inspection certifies low damage risk

- **Check:** For MTO orders with part weight above 8 kg, verify `Package reinforced` or a valid inspection certification.
- **Baseline signal:** Q1 violations correlate with return/rework loops.
- **Monitor after implementation:**
  - Q1 violation rate.
  - Reinforced packaging share.
  - `package_material_kg`.
  - Damage-related returns and packaging rework.
  - Late delivery caused by inspection certification delays.
- **Decision rule:** If reinforced packaging increases material too much, refine the low-damage certification model. If returns rise, tighten Q1 enforcement.

## Segment-level service monitoring

For each segment, monitor before/after:

- Median and p90 cycle time.
- Late-delivery rate.
- Cancellations due to delay.
- Partial shipment rate.
- Backorder wait duration.
- Expedite requests.
- Rework rate.
- Transport kgCO2e per order.
- Energy kWh per order and per line item where available.
- Scrap kg per order.
- Package kg per order.
- Obsolescence within 14 days for batch-produced items.

Critical segments require stricter guardrails:

- **Standard-stock Service-contract:** protect promised service while reducing SP1 violations.
- **Made-to-order Service-contract:** protect due dates while reducing rework and scrap.
- **Critical-downtime Service-contract:** protect true downtime service; do not allow emissions reduction to create unacceptable downtime risk.

## Process-mining validation

After implementation, rerun process mining and compare:

- **DFG changes:**
  - Reduction in `Package standard/reinforced -> Ship air`.
  - Reduction in `Critical -> emergency production -> reinforced pack -> Dedicated van`.
  - Reduction in `MTO -> batch wait -> CNC_B -> inspection -> rework -> ground`.
  - Increase in `Package standard -> Ship consolidated truck` only where slack is sufficient.
- **Rework loops:**
  - Reduction in `Inspection -> Rework production`.
  - Reduction in `Rework production -> CNC production`.
  - Reduction in `Package standard/reinforced -> Rework packaging`.
- **Partial shipment paths:**
  - Reduction in `Pick from stock -> Partial shipment -> Backorder wait`.
  - Reduction in second shipments and cancellations from backorder wait.
- **Variant analysis:**
  - Monitor emergency and partial-shipment variants separately because model precision is low during disruptions.
  - Use conservative thresholds for automated routing when variants are unstable.

## Rebound dashboards

Create dashboards that flag displacement risks:

1. **Transport rebound:** air or van share rises after ground/consolidation policy.
2. **Batching rebound:** batch wait, late delivery, or 14-day obsolescence rises.
3. **Quality rebound:** inspection queue time rises while rework or returns do not fall.
4. **Packaging rebound:** package material rises without a corresponding reduction in damage returns.
5. **Resilience rebound:** alternative-route availability falls for Service-contract customers.
6. **Customer-behavior rebound:** expedite requests or Critical-downtime classifications rise after carbon-aware promise changes.

## Exception governance

- Require all SP1, SP2, and Q1 overrides to be logged with:
  - reason code,
  - approver,
  - expected service impact,
  - expected environmental impact,
  - customer segment,
  - route mode,
  - batch or machine context.
- Review exceptions weekly during rollout and monthly afterward.
- Use exception data to tune:
  - probability thresholds,
  - batch-wait caps,
  - reinforced-packaging rules,
  - Critical-downtime classification,
  - emergency lane capacity.

## Rollback and adjustment rules

Pause or adjust an intervention if any of the following occurs:

- Critical-downtime late delivery increases materially.
- Service-contract SLA risk rises without offsetting environmental gain.
- Rework or returns increase after packaging or batching changes.
- Obsolescence within 14 days increases without energy savings.
- Alternative-route availability falls for Service-contract customers.
- Override rates become high enough to undermine SP1, SP2, or Q1.

The goal is not to minimize one metric. The redesign should be accepted only if the combined lifecycle and service outcome improves: lower energy, transport, scrap, packaging, and rework impact without unacceptable deterioration in cycle time, late delivery, quality, or resilience.
