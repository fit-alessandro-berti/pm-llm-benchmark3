## 1. Equivalence design space

Treat each execution as a view centered on its designated Order. Object identity matters within a view because it connects events and relations. Across views, only the object type, the lead role, and the local or external status constrain a renaming. An external object can be renamed as long as its external status and every reference to it are preserved.

**Activity-sequence equivalence.** Replace each event with its activity label and compare the resulting sequences for equality. With tied timestamps, compare sequences of sorted label multisets, one multiset per timestamp. This preserves activity names, occurrence counts, and the order between timestamp blocks. It discards elapsed time, object types and identities, event-to-object (E2O) incidence, and object-to-object (O2O) relations. It is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive by equality. Each listed line in the examples is treated as a separate time position, since no ties are specified. Events with distinct times remain ordered even if they could have run concurrently.

**Typed structural equivalence.** Compare graphs by isomorphism. Color each object by its type, external status, and lead status, and color each event by its activity and ordinal timestamp block. Require bijections on objects and events that preserve colors, E2O incidence, and all directed, qualified O2O relations. Preserve any additional role in a relation, such as the item that scopes the replacement of one package by another in X8. This retains object counts, repeated events, shared references, and the placement of relations in the execution. It discards literal object and event identifiers, elapsed time, and attributes outside the graph. Use the stated O2O notes, expanding explicit references to the baseline. Omitted relations are unobserved in this comparison. Identity, inverse, and composition of isomorphisms give reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity. Same-time events can be permuted within their block if incidence and labels agree.

**Business-semantic equivalence (preferred).** Compare the profile in Section 2. It counts distinct local items added, packages packed, deliveries dispatched, invoices issued, and payments recorded. It also records cross-order logistics, cross-order finance, staged settlement, item substitution or removal, and a return followed by inspection and redispatch of the same item. These features retain the main fulfillment and settlement distinctions in the examples. The profile discards exact event order outside the return motif, full incidence after feature extraction, absolute timing, and payment amounts. Equal profiles give a reflexive, symmetric, and transitive relation. The return chain requires observed precedence, so a timestamp tie alone does not establish an order. With a supplied or reconstructed dependency partial order, use precedence in that order instead. The other features are insensitive to tie-breaking.

## 2. Canonical signatures

Use the events incident to the lead Order and the O2O notes available in its view. Counts use distinct object identities within the view. In particular, a payment seen on multiple relation rows contributes one payment object. The flags are based on positive evidence. A missing flag records the absence of evidence in the supplied view. A single payment record alone gives no settlement amount or proof of completion.

The pseudocode uses directed triples for the O2O relations queried by the profile. In the input, normalize the descriptions of partial and completing payments to the qualifiers shown below. `strict_chain` checks consecutive event pairs for strict precedence. `products` enumerates tuples taking one event from each listed activity.

```text
semantic_key(events, links, lead, object_type, external):
    E = group_by_activity(e for e in events if lead in e.objects)
    # Missing activity groups are empty.
    ids(e, kind, ext=False) =
        {o for o in e.objects if object_type[o] == kind and external[o] == ext}
    seen(activity, kind) = union(ids(e, kind) for e in E[activity])
    items     = seen("Add Item", "Item")
    packages  = seen("Pack Package", "Package")
    deliveries = seen("Dispatch", "Delivery")
    invoices  = seen("Issue Invoice", "Invoice")
    payments  = seen("Record Payment", "Payment")

    shared_logistics =
        any(ids(e, "Order", True) or ids(e, "Item", True)
            for e in E["Pack Package"] + E["Dispatch"])
        or any(p in packages and external[i] and object_type[i] == "Item"
               and q in {"packs", "initially_packs", "repacks"}
               for (p, q, i) in links)
    shared_finance =
        any(ids(e, "Order", True)
            for e in E["Issue Invoice"] + E["Record Payment"])
        or any(v in invoices and q == "bills" and external[o]
               and object_type[o] == "Order" for (v, q, o) in links)
    staged = any(
        (m1, "partially_settles", v) in links
        and (m2, "completes_settlement", v) in links
        for v in invoices for m1 in payments for m2 in payments if m1 != m2)
    substitution = any(
        (q == "replaces" and s in items and t in items)
        or (q == "removed_before_dispatch" and s in packages and t in items)
        for (s, q, t) in links)

    returned = False
    for (p0, d0, r, i, p1, d1) in products(
        E["Pack Package"], E["Dispatch"], E["Return Item"],
        E["Inspect Return"], E["Pack Package"], E["Dispatch"]
    ):
        first_packages = ids(p0, "Package") & ids(d0, "Package")
        next_packages = ids(p1, "Package") & ids(d1, "Package")
        first_deliveries = ids(d0, "Delivery") & ids(r, "Delivery")
        next_deliveries = ids(d1, "Delivery")
        same_items = ids(p0, "Item") & ids(r, "Item") & ids(i, "Item") & ids(p1, "Item")
        if strict_chain(p0, d0, r, i, p1, d1) and same_items & items:
            if any(a != b for a in first_packages for b in next_packages) and
               any(a != b for a in first_deliveries for b in next_deliveries):
                returned = True

    counts = (len(items), len(packages), len(deliveries), len(invoices), len(payments))
    return (counts, shared_logistics, shared_finance, staged, substitution, returned)
```

The key contains only integers and Booleans. Renaming objects bijectively preserves set cardinalities, joins on object identity, and all feature predicates. For the typed structural relation, a separate exact canonical key can be obtained by serializing the colored incidence graph with sorted relation records under every permutation within each vertex color and taking the lexicographic minimum. This exhaustive construction is suitable for these compact examples. Larger graphs call for a graph canonicalization or isomorphism algorithm.

## 4. Cluster interpretations

- **Baseline, one package, one delivery, one payment record.** The two items move together through packing. This is a reference flow for comparing packing and dispatch effort. An Item-case flattening can count the same Pack Package occurrence twice and omit downstream events without direct item links. The grouping is stable under identifier and timestamp tie cleanup that preserves incidence.
- **Consolidated delivery, two packages on one delivery.** Packing is split, while the dispatch groups both packages. Consolidation can reduce delivery overhead and introduces a coordination point if one package is late. An Order-case trace shows two pack occurrences but omits their common delivery membership. The signature is stable under reordering of the two packs and deduplication of repeated relation rows.
- **Split delivery and staged settlement, two deliveries and two payments.** Fulfillment proceeds separately by package, and completing shipment and completing settlement are different milestones. Monitoring can track both fulfillment coverage and outstanding balances. An Order-case trace loses the package-to-delivery pairing and the meaning of the payment stages. Repeated rows for one payment can be deduplicated safely. Losing the settlement qualifiers changes the signature.
- **Shared logistics, one lead package with external participation.** The package and delivery couple two orders, creating an opportunity for consolidation and a shared exposure to delay or misrouting. Independent Order-case views can duplicate the shared dispatch and obscure the dependency between orders. The cluster is stable under external-ID normalization that preserves the boundary, and sensitive to dropping the external links.
- **Shared finance, one invoice spanning orders.** The invoice is settled at invoice level. Lead-order cash attribution is unresolved in the supplied records. Order-case flattening can count the invoice and payment twice or associate each order with the entire payment. The cluster survives deduplication of invoice relation rows while both billed Orders remain identifiable.
- **Item substitution, replacement and removal before dispatch.** The original packing membership differs from the dispatch state. Inventory reconciliation and fulfillment checks need the qualified links. A Package-case trace retains the pack and dispatch milestones but loses which item was removed and why. Either item-level qualifier suffices for the flag. Removing both makes this grouping unstable.
- **Return and redispatch, same item on a second delivery after inspection.** The second pack and dispatch represent rework. The return loop supports measurements of rework time and workload. Delivery-case flattening separates the original dispatch from the new delivery and can omit inspection. Deduplicating relation rows preserves the cluster. Repair that loses the return or breaks the item link or precedence can change it.

## 5. Borderline cases

- **X1 versus X2.** Typed isomorphism maps I11 to I22 and I12 to I21. Thus the first and second Add Item events and all later incidence agree. Literal record equality separates them and is defensible for testing source-record reproducibility. The local identifiers supply no business distinction.
- **X1 versus X5.** Label sequences agree. The semantic profile separates them because the same package and dispatch involve another order, creating a logistics dependency even though O5 is billed individually.
- **X1 versus X6.** Label sequences again agree. A logistics-only criterion also groups them. The preferred profile separates them because the same invoice covers both orders and the payment has no lead-level allocation in the supplied data.
- **X1 versus X7.** Label sequences agree, and local object counts alone agree. The semantic profile distinguishes item substitution and removal. The typed graph also detects the extra Item incidence at Dispatch independently of the O2O qualifiers.
- **X3 versus X4.** A packing-only criterion based on item and package counts groups them as two items in two packages. The preferred profile separates one consolidated delivery from two deliveries and retains the staged settlement in X4.
- **X4 versus X8.** A criterion based on package-to-delivery topology groups them as two packages with one distinct delivery per package. Following item identity and the return events shows that X8's second dispatch is rework. In X4 it is original fulfillment for the second item, and the invoice has two settlement stages.

## 6. Relation to `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel`

PM4Py's exact key describes a split execution through its object representation and ordered event representation. The event entries retain the activity and the related object references. The references retain object type and identity reuse across events, so repeated activities, participation counts, and different E2O incidence can affect the key. Timestamp order can affect the event sequence. Absolute time gaps, financial attributes, and qualified O2O edges are outside the key's activity-and-incidence comparison. The key also has no benchmark-specific interpretation of the external boundary.

A key built directly from raw object IDs would split X1 and X2. PM4Py's type/time/lexicographic object renaming reduces this dependence by assigning type-based names. A lexical tie-break can still depend on the original IDs when same-type objects start together, so ordering-based renaming offers a different guarantee from graph isomorphism. To aggregate, recompute a canonical key for each returned execution and pool members with equal keys, keeping the original exact key for drill-down. Compute the semantic key from the sublog and its qualified source relations, since the exact key alone cannot recover those relations. Exact matching is preferable for checking whether exports preserve the same detailed event participation or for investigating incidence differences within a business variant.

The outline uses the benchmark's `EXT` convention and a unique local Create Order anchor in each sublog. In production, lead and external status should come from the extraction provenance or ownership data. The bound limits the size of the extractions considered. Large or highly connected neighborhoods can make extraction and comparison expensive or leave some leads outside the bound. Audit coverage against the intended lead population, and account for shared objects or events that can appear in overlapping views.

```python
from collections import defaultdict
from types import SimpleNamespace

import pm4py

lead_type = "Order"
exact = pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel, lead_type, max_objs=100)

eid, oid = ocel.event_id_column, ocel.object_id_column
activity, time = ocel.event_activity, ocel.event_timestamp
otype, qualifier = ocel.object_type_column, ocel.qualifier
object_type = dict(zip(ocel.objects[oid], ocel.objects[otype]))
external = {obj: str(obj).startswith("EXT") for obj in object_type}
source_links = [] if ocel.o2o is None else [
    (row[oid], row[qualifier], row[oid + "_2"])
    for row in ocel.o2o.to_dict("records")
]

semantic_clusters = defaultdict(list)
for exact_key, sublogs in exact.items():
    for sublog in sublogs:
        by_event = sublog.relations.groupby(eid)[oid].agg(set).to_dict()
        events = [
            SimpleNamespace(activity=row[activity], time=row[time],
                            objects=by_event.get(row[eid], set()))
            for row in sublog.events.to_dict("records")
        ]
        anchors = {
            obj for event in events if event.activity == "Create Order"
            for obj in event.objects
            if object_type[obj] == lead_type and not external[obj]
        }
        if len(anchors) != 1:
            raise ValueError("Use the split's recorded lead for ambiguous anchors")
        lead, = anchors
        view = set(sublog.objects[oid])
        links = {(s, q, t) for s, q, t in source_links if s in view and t in view}

        # Implement semantic_key as in Section 2, with strict_chain comparing times.
        key = semantic_key(events, links, lead, object_type, external)
        semantic_clusters[key].append((lead, exact_key, sublog))
```

## 7. Robustness analysis

1. **Ignore all O2O qualifiers.** Retain unqualified adjacency and all E2O incidence. X7 loses the substitution flag and joins the preferred Baseline cluster. The typed graph still distinguishes its item-to-item adjacency and the Item on Dispatch. X4 loses the staged flag but remains a singleton by its delivery and payment counts. X5 and X6 keep their sharing flags through event incidence, and X8 keeps its return chain.
2. **Ignore external objects.** Project out external nodes and their incident links while retaining the events and lead-owned objects. X5 and X6 join the preferred Baseline cluster because the sharing flags disappear. Activity assignments are unchanged. The projected E2O patterns of X5 and X6 match X1. Full typed equality after projection still depends on the remaining reported O2O edges. For example, X5's abbreviated notes omit baseline links such as the lead's item containment.
3. **Collapse repeated same-label events.** Merge consecutive runs of the same activity, retaining the union of their E2O participants and all O2O edges. This folds X3 into A1, since its consecutive Pack Package events become one. The separated dispatches in X4 and the return loop in X8 keep their activity sequences distinct. Preferred assignments survive this repair. Distinct packages and payments remain countable, and X4's two settlement qualifiers still refer to distinct payment objects. A collapse that retained only one event's participants could erase these distinctions.
4. **Use a dependency partial order.** Constrain events on a shared non-Order object by their observed order, then take the transitive closure. Order creation may precede all other events without making every pair of Order events comparable. In X4 the first dispatch and the second pack can become incomparable because they use different packages. X3 still has one dispatch downstream of both packs. X8 retains precedence through the first delivery, the returned item, and the new package. The typed partition here remains the same because the incidence and qualifiers already distinguish its clusters. The preferred profiles also stay the same.

## 8. Limitations and recommendation

- **Compliance monitoring.** Use qualified typed structural equivalence with the precedence required by the rule. Inspect precise timestamps or financial attributes separately when the rule concerns a deadline or an amount. X7 illustrates why a valid-looking activity sequence is insufficient evidence.
- **Operational segmentation.** Use the preferred semantic profile. Consolidation, shared logistics, shared billing, and rework correspond to different coordination needs and can be measured without fragmenting groups by local IDs or incidental interleavings.
- **Root-cause analysis.** Use typed incidence and qualified relations with object-specific precedence, and inspect the source attributes and neighboring executions. In X8 the delivery and item links identify where rework begins. Timing and inspection findings would be needed to explain its cause.
- **Descriptive reporting.** Use activity sequences for order-level milestone frequencies. Use semantic counts for questions whose denominator is packages, deliveries, or shared invoices, since case-level event counts can obscure the unit being measured.

These levels retain different evidence. Exact matching separates patterns that can be combined for planning, while a compressed profile can merge cases that differ on an audit rule or a causal dependency. The notes contain no payment amounts and do not establish the completeness of all O2O links, so the clustering supports comparisons on observed structure and behavior.
