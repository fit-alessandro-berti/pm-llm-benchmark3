## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts relied on, not assumptions:** GPIP-4 is board-approved and applies to all Northbridge purchases. DE-AAC-PROC-17 Rev. 6 is a controlled local SOP for Aachen maintenance/production-support purchases. ECD-2 is an incident directive with a fixed window and stated scope. The CFO email is an ordinary email, not a controlled policy document. The ERP outage affects evidential weight but is not itself a normative exception. Case timestamps, amounts, supplier status, purpose, and recorded events are taken as stated unless challenged by stronger contemporaneous evidence.

**Interpretive assumptions, defeasible:**

1. **Authority assumption:** A board-approved policy has high presumptive authority. A local SOP may be stricter but may not silently weaken GPIP-4. An incident directive can override local SOP workflow steps where it is clear, within scope, and within time. It is not treated as automatically equivalent to a Chief Risk Officer exception under A7.
2. **Specificity assumption:** ECD-2 is highly specific to the contamination event, but specificity does not create authority to waive GPIP-4 core integrity controls unless the waiver is clear or a valid CRO exception exists.
3. **Later-in-time assumption:** ECD-2 and the CFO email are later than GPIP-4 and the SOP, but later-in-time is not decisive absent valid authority.
4. **Emergency necessity assumption:** Emergency status is assessed at the time of the relevant act. Necessity does not itself create authority if the emergency definition, scope, delegation, or exception authority is not met.
5. **Retrospective evidence assumption:** ERP timestamps during or after the outage are rebuttable. Supplier acknowledgments, call records, signed PDFs, and access logs may establish the real time of an act or record. A late database entry may be a logging defect, not necessarily a policy breach.
6. **Burden of proof assumption:** Northbridge must demonstrate compliance or a valid exception. Post-commitment approval can mitigate consequences but does not automatically cure a missing pre-commitment control under A6.
7. **Role assumption:** Plant_Approve is treated as the local final business approval unless evidence shows otherwise. Finance_Approve is treated as the local Finance approval. Incident Command authorization is treated as a substitute for ordinary business approval only within ECD scope/time, but not automatically as an “independent budget owner” under A2.
8. **Scope assumption:** “Critical sterilization-line lots” is interpreted narrowly for ECD purposes: parts needed to restore or contain the affected sterilization line within the emergency window. Backup-line components with multi-day stock are not presumptively within scope, although Operations’ broader reading is a credible alternative.
9. **Severity scale:** Low = local timing/evidence defect with limited financial/integrity exposure; Medium = missing or late financial/authority control; High = missing pre-commitment compliance, segregation of duties, or scope breach.
10. **Confidence scale:** High = clear facts and controlling norm; Medium = material interpretive ambiguity; Low = missing evidence prevents proof either way.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Proposed replacement hierarchy and exception clause:**

1. **Normative hierarchy:** GPIP-4 prevails over local SOPs and incident directives. A Chief Risk Officer written, scoped, time-limited exception prevails over GPIP-4 controls where issued. Incident directives may override local SOP timing and approved-supplier workflow only within their stated scope and time. CFO emails may interpret Finance controls but are not valid waivers of Compliance clearance, segregation of duties, or independent budget-owner requirements unless countersigned by the CRO.

2. **Emergency exception authority:** Only the CRO, or a delegate expressly authorized in writing by the CRO, may waive GPIP-4 A1, A2, or A3. A COO/Incident Commander directive is valid as an exception only if it includes a CRO countersignature or is ratified by the CRO within 24 hours.

3. **Scope definition:** “Critical sterilization-line lot” means a component or service needed to restore or contain the affected sterilization line within the next 24 hours. Backup-line components are in scope only if existing stock is insufficient for 24 hours and the backup line is required for containment. Site continuity unrelated to sterilization or production is not in scope.

4. **Thresholds and timing:** For purchases within the emergency scope and window, commitment may precede Create_PO and an unapproved supplier may be used only up to EUR 150,000. For purchases above EUR 100,000 or from a new supplier, Compliance_Clearance is always required before commitment. For purchases  EUR 50,000, independent budget-owner and Finance Controller approval are required before commitment. The Finance Controller element may be substituted by two distinct Incident Command members only if the CFO issues a written, scoped, time-limited designation for that emergency and the amount is  EUR 150,000. The independent budget-owner element may not be substituted unless the CRO expressly authorizes it.

5. **Segregation of duties:** The requester or person who commits the company to a supplier may not be one of the two Incident Command authorizers or the final business approver. No incident directive or CFO email may waive this rule absent a CRO written exception.

6. **Retrospective records and evidence:** Verbal Incident Command authorization must be recorded within 4 hours. ERP timestamps during system outages are rebuttable. Contemporaneous supplier acknowledgments, telephone recordings, signed PDFs, and access logs may establish the real time of acts and records. A late ERP entry is a logging defect if stronger evidence shows timely action.

7. **Expiry and extension:** Emergency directives expire automatically at the stated time. No verbal extension is valid. Any extension requires a written CRO exception identifying scope, threshold, duration, and controls waived.

8. **Safeguard against emergency normalization:** Emergency directives may not exceed 72 hours without CRO renewal. Every emergency purchase must be reported weekly to the CRO and Internal Audit, including amount, supplier status, scope justification, authorizers, and record timeliness. If emergency purchases exceed a defined threshold by count, value, or duration, the directive is suspended pending independent review.
