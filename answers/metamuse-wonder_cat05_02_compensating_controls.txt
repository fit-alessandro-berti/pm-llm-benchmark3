## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 - `Prepare_Payment` human $\ne$ `Release_Payment` human, shared service accounts not independent - is a proxy rule. Its objective is not separation for its own sake, but to ensure no single actor can create **and** make irrevocable a payment that is erroneous, unauthorised, or fraudulent, and that a second, independent mind intervenes before loss becomes unrecoverable. A nominal violation and an objective failure are therefore related but not identical: AP-203/AP-207 can violate the rule yet satisfy the objective under ED-1; AP-204/AP-209 can satisfy the rule yet leave the objective in doubt.

**P = prevent before irrevocable settlement, D = detect, C = correct/recall/recover, F = fails / does not address**

| # | Objective / threat scenario | AV-2 Automated validation | MG-4 Independent manager approval | RA-7 Retrospective audit and recall | ED-1 Emergency permission |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Can block; warning+override weakens to D. | **P** if before release/settlement and genuinely reviewing invoice/amount; **F** if rubber-stamp. | **D**, **C** only if within ~24h recall window and before irrevocable settlement in practice. | Not a control itself; **permits** single-operator only if AV-2 + pre-settlement MG-4 + timely RA-7 substitute the second eye. |

Key implication: AV-2, MG-4 and RA-7 are not fungible units to be counted. They cover different threats, at different times, with different failure modes. Three weak/late controls do not sum to one timely independent preventive control, especially once settlement is irrevocable for internal purposes.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

**Structured test - apply in order, no compensating across a failed gate by counting other controls:**

**Gate A - Completion and timing:** Was intervention possible before `Release_Payment`, or at latest before `Bank_Settlement`/irrevocability? Preventive = before release; hybrid settlement-preventive = after release but before settlement with a real power to stop settlement; detective = after settlement or after recall window. Timing is measured against settlement and the 24h recall window, not merely against the log's end.

**Gate B - Independence:** Actor distinct in human terms (not shared/service account), L3+ for MG-4, not preparer/releaser, no unresolved conflict (direct performance-rating line in AP-202 fails), no shared credential, no evidence of a captured repeated pair or shared workstation that plausibly merges two IDs into one decision.

**Gate C - Coverage:** Map the control's actual checks to O1-O6. AV-2 full pass covers duplicate/sanctions/ownership/recent change/limit/PO only. It says nothing about receipt, reasonableness, collusion, or account compromise. MG-4 covers receipt only if invoice + bank details + receipt/service report were demonstrably reviewed.

**Gate D - Overrideability and operational state:** Was AV-2 operational, a block rather than a warning, and un-overridden? Was an override by the preparer themselves, or using a manager-assigned credential? Was validation unavailable (AP-208) - then coverage is zero for O2, not "manual equivalent" without signed evidence.

**Gate E - Recoverability:** If prevention fails, could the defect still be corrected? Audit within 1 business day / recall within 24h gives partial corrective capacity; audit after settlement + recall window is detective only. Amount and vendor risk scale the consequence of failed recoverability, but do not by themselves classify conformance.

**Gate F - Evidence quality:** Contemporaneous, attributable, timestamped-before-the-act evidence (token before `Prepare`, approval content, audit start) vs. post-hoc ratification, unsigned checklists, unresolved logging-delay claims (AP-206). Unresolved material ambiguity $\rightarrow$ INDETERMINATE, not benefit of the doubt.

**Gate G - Collusion resistance and case risk:** New vendor, bank change hours/days earlier, high value, relative-linked account, repeated pair, outage/emergency pressure. Higher inherent risk raises the bar for equivalence; it does not automatically fail a low-risk case (AP-201) nor save a high-risk one because payment later proved legitimate (AP-208).

**Classification of the package:**

- **Fully equivalent:** All of A-G satisfied for the material threats: an independent, informed intervention before irrevocability, covering the threats AV-2 misses, un-overridden, with timely audit as backstop. Under ED-1, all seven conditions evidenced. Objective can be MET despite nominal violation (AP-203, AP-207).
- **Partially equivalent:** Independent intervention before settlement and main preventive checks intact, but a leg is late, narrow, or slightly outside window (e.g. manager after release but before settlement, audit at 25h). Risk materially reduced, residual tolerable given low exposure/established vendor. Not a precedent for convenience use.
- **Merely mitigating:** Detective controls exist but after release/settlement, or preventive coverage has a material gap (no receipt review, warning overridden by a non-conflicted manager with later review). Loss is less likely or more discoverable, but a single actor could still cause an irrevocable loss.
- **Ineffective for the objective:** No timely independent intervention, AV-2 absent/overridden, manager conflicted or post-settlement, audit after recall window, or collusion credibly alleged. Counting activities present does not rescue it (AP-202, AP-208).

**Why "same expected loss" is not the only standard:** (i) Expected loss averages away tail/irrevocability - a rare EUR 900k unrecoverable diversion is not equivalent to frequent small recoverable errors with the same mean; (ii) fraud is adversarial and adaptive, not a stationary probability - a known overridable path will be selected by a bad actor, so population-average loss understates conditional risk; (iii) the rule also serves deterrence, accountability, non-repudiation and segregation-of-knowledge, which a post-hoc audit does not reproduce; (iv) recoverability is asymmetric and time-decaying, so timing changes the *kind* of control, not just its probability weight; (v) evidence for regulators/auditors must show prevention was *possible and exercised*, not that ex post no loss happened - legitimate outcome in AP-208 does not retroactively create bank-ownership evidence at release time.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

**Chosen semantics:** Classify a control by its position relative to the *loss-irrevocability point* (bank settlement), not only relative to the preceding log event. A manager action after `Release_Payment` but before `Bank_Settlement` is **hybrid: detective as to the release decision, preventive as to the loss** - provided the manager can actually stop settlement and reviewed substance. If settlement cannot be stopped, or review is a rubber stamp, it collapses to detective. I label this "settlement-preventive (hybrid)" in Section 3 rather than forcing a single bucket.

| Control | Preventive capacity | Detective capacity | Corrective capacity | Timing sensitivity | Independence requirement | Coverage strengths | Structural failures |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AV-2 | **Strong when blocking** before release (AP-205); weak when warning-only | Flags duplicate, sanctions, ownership, <7d bank change, limit, PO mismatch | None itself; block enables human correction | Must be operational *at* release; outage = no control (AP-208) | Systemic - independent of preparer, but not of compromised credentials/accounts | O1, O2 objective checks, fast and consistent | Receipt/reasonableness (O3), collusion (O5), compromised-account control (O4); overrideable warnings |
| MG-4 before release | **Strongest human preventive** - independent second mind, can cover receipt (AP-203) | Can detect errors during review | Prevents loss rather than correcting it | Before release = preventive; degrades rapidly after | L3+, not preparer/releaser, no rating/conflict line, no shared credential | O1-O5 potentially, incl. commercial substance if evidence reviewed | Rubber-stamping; conflicted supervisor (AP-202); review content often unevidenced |
| MG-4 after release, before settlement (hybrid) | **Preventive re loss** if settlement-stop power is real (AP-201, AP-207) | Detective re the already-taken release decision | Partial - can stop settlement, equivalent to correction before irrevocability | Narrow window; value vanishes at settlement timestamp | Same as above; m04/m15 independent, hence credited | Can still break causal chain to irrevocable transfer | Not compliant with a strict pre-release reading of ED-1 except where ED-1 itself says "before bank settlement"; audit trail must prove stop-power |

## 5. Counterfactual and Residual-Risk Analysis

Qualitative ranges only; no precise probabilities are estimated from nine cases.

**AP-202 - diversion via recent bank change + self-override.** Most plausible mechanism: single actor creates/releases payment to a newly changed account linked to a relative, overriding the exact warnings designed to stop this. AV-2 *detected* but did not break the chain because the warning was overrideable by the preparer with m09's credential; MG-4 and RA-7 arrived after recoverability expired. Residual exposure was total and realised - funds unrecovered. *With a truly independent releaser:* high likelihood the chain breaks - a second person facing a 2-hour-old bank change, new vendor and limit warning has both the opportunity and the accountability to refuse. This is the case where four-eyes has maximum marginal value.

**AP-208 - ungated high-value release during validation outage.** Mechanism (potential, not realised): EUR 900k to an account changed 3 days earlier with no ownership verification, manager and audit both after irrevocability. No control broke the chain at release time; legitimacy was discovered later. Residual risk at the moment of release was high and unrecoverable in kind, even though ex post loss was zero. *With an independent releaser:* plausibly would have demanded AV-2 restoration or signed ownership evidence, especially at this value - but note a second human without AV-2 data still cannot verify ownership by inspection alone, so independent release is necessary-not-sufficient here; the correct counterfactual is releaser *plus* restored validation.

**AP-204 - collusive or captured pair fabricating service evidence.** Mechanism: intangible consulting, fabricated receipt, pair acting together across 37 payments; AV-2 passed because receipt truth is outside its coverage. Audit detected, late, and recovery is unevidenced. *With a "truly independent" second releaser* - i.e. not merely a different ID but a rotated, non-paired, separately supervised reviewer checking receipt provenance - the fabrication is materially more likely to be challenged before payment. Simply adding the same u13 signature changes little; independence quality, not headcount, is the counterfactual variable.

**AP-201 - convenience same-user on a low-risk payment.** Mechanism: honest-error or small diversion on EUR 1,800 to a 6-year vendor; AV-2 covered duplicate/bank checks and independent m04 reviewed before settlement, so the chain to irrevocable loss was substantially interrupted. Residual: the precedent risk - normalising convenience exceptions erodes Gate D/G discipline more than this payment's direct exposure. *With an independent releaser:* direct loss reduction modest (low base risk, controls already present), main gain is normative - removing the exception pathway. Hence partially met/tolerable, but explicitly not a template.

**AP-203 - genuine emergency, ED-1 satisfied.** Mechanism: time-critical repair creates pressure to skip receipt checks; m02's pre-release review of invoice, bank details and service report broke exactly that chain, with AV-2 covering bank/duplicate threats and same-day audit as backstop. Residual: emergency-pressure and single-preparer framing risk remain, bounded by the <100k cap and vendor history. *With an independent releaser instead of MG-4-approver:* marginal change small - the manager already performed the substantive second-eye function before release; this is why nominal violation $\ne$ objective failure.

**AP-207 - emergency with hybrid manager timing.** Mechanism: safety repair, token pre-dated, AV-2 passed, manager reviewed all evidence after release but before settlement. Chain breaks at settlement, not at release - so residual risk is the window 14:05-16:00 and dependence on settlement-stop actually working. *With an independent releaser:* risk in that window falls to near the AP-203 level; expected difference is small for this fact pattern (established vendor, no changes, full evidence review) but the control is structurally less robust than pre-release approval, which is why ED-1's before-settlement wording matters.

**AP-209 - pattern risk without a defect.** Mechanism (hypothetical): if AP-204 reflects pair capture rather than a sanctioned payment cell, AP-209's clean audit may simply mean this payment was genuine or the fabrication was not in audit scope. No control currently breaks a collusion chain here except timely audit. *With rotation/independent releaser:* would convert indeterminate pattern evidence into a test - defects surfacing under rotation, or clean results under varied pairs, are both informative. Until then residual risk is genuinely unquantifiable from the log.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

**Evidence to collect contemporaneously (not reconstructed after audit):** emergency token issuance timestamp from the token system's own clock (resolves AP-206); MG-4 review checklist artefact showing invoice, bank details and receipt/service report were opened/reviewed, approver L3 and reporting-line snapshot, and settlement-stop confirmation; AV-2 version/result payload including warning vs block, override credential ID and its assignee (resolves AP-202 credential question); bank-change timestamp, old/new account ownership verification; workstation/session/device IDs for preparer and releaser (AP-204/AP-209); vendor master-data change history; settlement timestamp on every case (missing in several log traces); audit start *and* completion times; organisational design document for any dedicated payment cell.

**Monitoring rules - alert $\ne$ proof of misconduct.** An alert triggers evidence preservation and independent review; proof requires documents, access logs and interviews. Thresholds below are starting points to calibrate, not verdicts:

| Metric | Suggested alert rule | What it indicates / does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Same-user rate | Any same-user completed payment without full ED-1 evidence pack = alert; trend rate by user/unit monthly | Exception discipline failure; low rate with full ED-1 packs may be legitimate emergency use |
| Override rate | Any preparer self-override or override with another's credential = immediate alert; override rate per 100 validations by user | Override may be justified (e.g. false bank-change warning) - but never by the preparer alone; credential sharing must be investigated separately |
| Late manager approval | `Manager_Approve/Ratify` after `Release` = amber; after `Bank_Settlement` = red; measure release$\rightarrow$approval and approval$\rightarrow$settlement gaps | Post-settlement ratification (AP-202, AP-208) is not an approval control; pre-settlement lateness may still be hybrid-effective (AP-207) |
| Audit-before-recall-window | % emergency/same-user cases audited within 1 business day and within 24h of release; alert on first miss | Measures whether RA-7 has any corrective capacity; a completed late audit is detective only |
| Repeated resource pairs | Pair (preparer, releaser) count in rolling 30 days, e.g. >10 or >3$\times$ expected under rotation; alert on concentration | May reflect a sanctioned two-person cell (AP-209) - require the org-design document rather than inferring collusion (AP-204) |
| Shared workstations | Same device/workstation for prepare+release within a case or across a pair's cases = alert | Could be hot-desking; combined with pair concentration + fabricated evidence it becomes strong collusion *evidence to investigate*, still not standalone proof |
| Bank-change proximity | Bank change within 7 days of payment = hold/alert; within 24h = block pending independent ownership verification | Legitimate changes occur; proximity shifts burden of proof to contemporaneous ownership evidence (AP-202: 2h; AP-208: 3d) |
| Control outages | Any release during `Validation_Service_Unavailable` = red alert; outage minutes and payments released during outage reported daily | Legitimate payment during outage (AP-208) still represents ungated exposure; releases should default to queue, not manual unsigned checklist |
| Additional | ED-1 token-after-prepare count; amount just below EUR 100k clustering; vendor age <180d in emergencies; manager = direct supervisor flags | Detects permission gaming and threshold structuring before they become losses |

Escalation: red alerts freeze further same-user/override privileges for the involved resources pending review, preserve logs, and require audit inside the recall window where settlement has not occurred - act on recoverability first, attribution second.

```python
AP-207,Manager_Ratify,2026-05-04T14:40:00Z,m15

AP-207,Audit_Review,2026-05-05T12:00:00Z,a02
AP-208,Prepare_Payment,2026-05-04T15:00:00Z,u72
AP-208,Validation_Service_Unavailable,2026-05-04T15:01:00Z,SYSTEM
AP-208,Release_Payment,2026-05-04T15:04:00Z,u72

AP-209,Bank_Settlement,2026-05-04T18:00:00Z,SYSTEM
AP-209,Audit_Review,2026-05-05T10:00:00Z,a07
"""

# 1-2. Reconstruct CSV, parse UTC timestamps, format as PM4Py event log
df = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(CSV_DATA))
df["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"], utc=True)
df["org:resource"] = df["org:resource"].fillna("SYSTEM")
log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df, case_id="case:concept:name", activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp")

# 3. Nominal four-eyes violations among COMPLETED payments.
# PM4Py semantics (version-dependent, see note below): returns the log
# filtered to cases where the two activities were performed by the
# same resource. Convert back to a dataframe defensively.
violating_log = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
    log, "Prepare_Payment", "Release_Payment")
try:
    violating_df = pm4py.convert_to_dataframe(violating_log)
    nominal_violation_cases = set(violating_df["case:concept:name"])
except Exception:
    nominal_violation_cases = set()

# 4. Attempted but blocked releases (e.g. AP-205): an attempt/block/cancel
#    with NO completed Release_Payment must not count as a violation.
acts = df.groupby("case:concept:name")["concept:name"].agg(set)
blocked_cases = {
    c for c, a in acts.items()
    if "Release_Payment" not in a
    and ({"Attempt_Release", "Automated_Validate_Block"} & a)
}

# 5. Case-facts dataframe constructed from the case table (no external files)
facts = pd.DataFrame([
    # case, amount, vendor_days, bank_change_days (None = none), token_before_prepare
    ["AP-201",   1800, 2190, None, False],
    ["AP-202", 480000,   10, 0.08, False],
    ["AP-203",  75000, 1460, None, True ],
    ["AP-204", 220000,  730, None, False],
    ["AP-205",  12000, 1000, None, False],
    ["AP-206",  95000, 1095, None, False],  # token 13:02 AFTER prepare 13:00
    ["AP-207",  35000, 3285, None, True ],
    ["AP-208", 900000,  240, 3.0,  True ],
    ["AP-209",  42000, 1825, None, False],
], columns=["case", "amount_eur", "vendor_days",
            "bank_change_days", "token_before_prepare"])

# Event-derived helpers
def first_time(case, activities):
    sub = df[(df["case:concept:name"] == case) &
             (df["concept:name"].isin(activities))]
    return sub["time:timestamp"].min() if len(sub) else pd.NaT

def resource_of(case, activity):
    sub = df[(df["case:concept:name"] == case) &
             (df["concept:name"] == activity)]
    return sub["org:resource"].iloc[0] if len(sub) else None

rows = []
for _, f in facts.iterrows():
    c = f["case"]
    prep, rel = resource_of(c, "Prepare_Payment"), resource_of(c, "Release_Payment")
    t_prep, t_rel = first_time(c, ["Prepare_Payment"]), first_time(c, ["Release_Payment"])
    t_mgr = first_time(c, ["Manager_Approve", "Manager_Ratify"])
    t_settle = first_time(c, ["Bank_Settlement"])
    t_audit = first_time(c, ["Audit_Review"])
    case_acts = acts.get(c, set())

    av_state = ("BLOCK" if "Automated_Validate_Block" in case_acts else
                "UNAVAILABLE" if "Validation_Service_Unavailable" in case_acts else
                "WARNING_OVERRIDE" if "Override_Warning" in case_acts else
                "PASS" if "Automated_Validate" in case_acts else "NONE")

    mgr_timing = ("NONE" if pd.isna(t_mgr) else
                  "BEFORE_RELEASE" if not pd.isna(t_rel) and t_mgr < t_rel else
                  "AFTER_RELEASE_BEFORE_SETTLEMENT"
                  if not pd.isna(t_settle) and t_mgr < t_settle else
                  "AFTER_SETTLEMENT")
    audit_hours = (None if pd.isna(t_audit) or pd.isna(t_rel)
                   else round((t_audit - t_rel).total_seconds() / 3600, 1))

    rows.append({
        "case": c,
        "preparer": prep, "releaser": rel,
        "nominal_pm4py_violation": c in nominal_violation_cases,
        "blocked_attempt_no_release": c in blocked_cases,
        "av_state": av_state,
        # --- Preliminary ED-1 / evidence flags (NOT a verdict) ---
        "flag_token_before_prepare": bool(f["token_before_prepare"]),
        "flag_amount_lt_100k": f["amount_eur"] < 100_000,
        "flag_vendor_ge_180d": f["vendor_days"] >= 180,
        "flag_no_bank_change_7d": pd.isna(f["bank_change_days"]) or f["bank_change_days"] > 7,
        "flag_av_pass_no_override": av_state == "PASS",
        "flag_mgr_timing": mgr_timing,
        "flag_mgr_before_settlement": mgr_timing in
            ("BEFORE_RELEASE", "AFTER_RELEASE_BEFORE_SETTLEMENT"),
        "flag_audit_within_1bd_approx_24h":
            audit_hours is not None and audit_hours <= 24,
        "audit_hours_after_release": audit_hours,
        "pair": f"{prep}->{rel}",
    })

evidence = pd.DataFrame(rows)
pair_counts = evidence["pair"].value_counts()
evidence["flag_repeated_pair_gt1_in_log"] = evidence["pair"].map(pair_counts) > 1

print("=== PRELIMINARY EVIDENCE FLAGS - FOR JUDGMENT, NOT A COMPLIANCE VERDICT ===")
print(evidence.to_string(index=False))
print("\nNominal PM4Py violating completed cases:", sorted(nominal_violation_cases))
print("Blocked attempts without completed release:", sorted(blocked_cases))
```

**Version caveats I cannot guarantee:** `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` has varied across PM4Py versions in whether it returns violating or compliant cases and whether it expects an `EventLog` versus a formatted dataframe; some versions also require the resource column to be discoverable after `format_dataframe`. The code therefore converts the result defensively inside `try/except` and cross-checks against directly computed preparer/releaser resources and the `blocked_attempt` logic - verify the filter's direction on your installed version with AP-201 (known same-user) and AP-204 (known distinct-user) before relying on it. Timestamp parsing, activity names, and the absence of `Bank_Settlement` events for several cases (settlement times then come from the facts table, not the log) also limit what the code can time-stamp. All ED-1, independence, collusion and sufficiency judgments in Sections 3-5 remain human assessments on top of these flags.
