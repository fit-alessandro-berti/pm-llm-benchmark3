## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

**January–March: reference period.** AUROC is about 0.81, ECE is 0.022–0.025, observed breach is 0.18–0.19, and mean predicted risk matches observed. The variant mix is about 45/35/20 and matured V3 breach is about 0.31. Treat this period as the calibration and discrimination reference.

**April: schema and pipeline incident, not a real regime change.**
- The renamed diagnosis label caused prefixes to be built at the wrong event or not at all. Unknown activity rose to 17% and missing queue to 14%.
- 18% of cases were unmapped. The apparent drop in V1, V2 and V3 shares (37/29/16) is an artefact of this.
- Mean predicted risk was 0.28 against observed 0.19, which is a nine-point overprediction. The likely cause is corrupted event-count and prefix features.
- Outcomes are intact. Matured breach is V1 0.18, V2 0.13 and V3 0.34, close to the baseline. The problem sits in the features, not the outcome relationship.
- April's calibration and discrimination metrics should be treated as invalid, not as evidence of drift.

**May: partial recovery.** Unknown activity fell to 2% and missing queue to 3%. AUROC recovered to 0.78. ECE stayed at 0.050, still twice baseline. The likely cause is the cached "events before diagnosis" feature, which was not backfilled for April and may still affect May features. Variant mix returned to roughly 43/34/22 and matured V3 breach was 0.33, so process and outcomes look normal.

**June: real routing and process mix change.**
- V2 share rose to 52% after the remote-routing policy.
- Observed breach fell to 0.15, the lowest value in the series. Matured V2 breach dropped to 0.09, while V1 was 0.16 and V3 0.35.
- Mix-implied breach is about 0.16, which matches the observed 0.15. The fall is therefore explained by the mix, not by a change in outcomes.
- Mean predicted risk stayed at 0.20, overpredicting by about five points, and ECE rose to 0.06. The model does not fully reflect the lower-risk V2 share, which suggests calibration-in-the-large sensitivity to mix or selection on unobservables created by the policy.
- Order Part to Part Received had already risen to 42 h. This is an early supplier signal, not yet a clear break.
- June is not evidence of concept drift. It is a process mix change that exposes calibration sensitivity.

**July: supplier onset, hidden by the aggregate.**
- The supplier problem began on 10 July. Order Part to Part Received rose to 65 h.
- Matured V3 breach rose to 0.46, up from 0.35 in June.
- The aggregate looks normal: observed 0.19, mix-implied about 0.20, predicted 0.18. A high V2 share (50%) masks the V3 deterioration.
- AUROC dropped only to 0.76. The first genuine signal appears only in variant-stratified metrics.

**August: overlapping process, concept and censoring effects.**
- The supplier problem became severe, with Order Part to Part Received at 130 h.
- V3 share rose to 35%, and matured V3 breach reached 0.64.
- Observed breach is 0.28 at 93% maturity. The true rate is probably somewhat higher, and the mix-implied rate is about 0.31.
- Mean predicted risk stayed at 0.19, so the model underpredicts by roughly nine to twelve points. AUROC fell to 0.68, ECE rose to 0.12 and Brier to 0.197.
- Partner Z was not yet onboarded, so August is not a population shift. The model's failure to respond to supplier delay is the main issue.

**September: three overlapping shifts.**
- Partner Z was onboarded, reaching 24% of cases. The supplier edge remained at 148 h and V3 share was 42%.
- Observed breach is 0.31 at 82% maturity. Partner Z shows 0.45 against 0.27 for others, but Partner Z labels are immature.
- Mature-implied breach is about 0.35. Mean predicted risk is 0.21, an underprediction of about 10–14 points.
- AUROC is 0.66 and ECE 0.14, the worst in the series.
- Non-Partner-Z breach at 0.27 remains well above baseline, so the supplier-driven degradation was not caused by Partner Z. AUROC was already declining in August, before Partner Z arrived.
- Part of Partner Z's excess is V3 mix (58% V3 against 36%). The rest needs stratified evidence.

**October: confounded by label maturity.**
- Only 55% of labels are mature, with a median delay of 52 days.
- Observed breach of 0.16 looks like recovery but is censoring. Mix-implied breach is about 0.33 if September's matured rates persist.
- Unresolved positives are labelled negative, which biases AUROC, ECE and Brier downward or in unpredictable directions. These metrics are unreliable.
- The supplier edge at 155 h shows no recovery.
- The backlog disproportionately affects long V3 paths, so V3 observed breach is biased downward more than other variants.
- October should be reported as provisional. It should not be treated as recovery or as a new regime.

**Why these periods should not be treated as one failure.**
- April is a pipeline incident affecting features, with intact outcomes.
- June is a policy-driven mix change with stable within-variant outcomes.
- August–September is overlapping process change, conditional change in V3, and population change, with censoring in the background.
- October is primarily a censoring artefact.

Each requires a different response. Applying one label to the whole period would lead to retraining on contaminated data, or to reacting to a recovery that has not happened.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

**T1. Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting.**
- Method: for each case-start month, compute breach at 30, 60 and 90 days after case start. Freeze the model trained on Jan–Mar. Score every month, and evaluate all months at the same horizon so label maturity is equal.
- If censoring explains October: the October curve lags and converges to September's matured rate (about 0.33–0.35) once mature.
- If supplier degradation is real and not censoring: Aug and Sep curves are above baseline at the same horizon, with V3 near 0.6.
- If April is a pipeline issue only: April's outcome curve matches Jan–Mar while its predictions do not.

**T2. Recomputation after activity-label normalisation and duplicate cleaning.**
- Method: rebuild April and May prefixes and features from raw events, with normalised labels, deduplication applied to all sources, and the cached count recomputed.
- If the pipeline explanation holds: April AUROC returns to about 0.79–0.82, ECE falls below 0.03, and unknown activity is near zero.
- If something else is also happening: AUROC remains near 0.67 after correction. That would point to a real change in April features or outcomes.

**T3. Feature lineage and missingness audit.**
- Method: diff stored feature values against recomputed values per feature and per month. Check missingness by source and month. Check duplicates by ingest date.
- If pipeline failure is the cause: mismatches are concentrated in event-count features and in the period 1 April to 6 May. Duplicates cluster before and after 20 April.
- If the residual Jul–Oct missingness is a Partner Z feed issue: queue missingness is concentrated in Partner Z. This would be lineage, not drift.
- If there is no pipeline problem: the stored–recomputed difference is near zero.

**T4. Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source and month.**
- Method: report mature-only metrics by variant × source × month. Also report mix-standardised metrics that reweight to the baseline variant mix.
- Under a mix or process explanation (June): within-variant calibration is stable, and the miscalibration largely disappears when standardised.
- Under a supplier concept explanation (Jul–Sep): V3 calibration deteriorates (predicted about 0.2–0.3 against observed about 0.6), while V1 and V2 remain stable.
- Under a pipeline explanation (April): miscalibration appears in all variants, not only V3.
- Under a population explanation: Partner Z miscalibration appears in Partner Z strata only, possibly concentrated at high distance or device age.

**T5. Process-variant and DFG/performance-DFG comparison.**
- Method: compare the Order Part to Part Received edge (median and 90th percentile) across months and by source. Track Diagnosis to Remote Fix and Part Received to Dispatch. Check for implausible edges, such as zero or negative durations.
- Under the supplier explanation: the Order to Received edge rises across all sources, including non-Partner-Z cases, starting around July.
- Under the policy explanation: V2 share changes, but edge times are stable.
- Under the pipeline explanation: April shows artefact edges, such as near-zero durations from duplicates or wrongly ordered events.

**T6. Is the outcome relationship conditional on the same path changed?**
- Method: hold path (V3) and supplier-lag bucket fixed, then compare breach across time. Use stratified or fixed-effect models with maturity-adjusted labels. Test whether the time effect remains after adding lag.
- If supplier lag is an omitted covariate and the mechanism is unchanged: the time effect vanishes once lag is included.
- If there is genuine concept drift: breach is higher than baseline within the same lag bucket in August and September, even after adjustment.
- If the process explanation is correct: breach within the same lag bucket is stable, and only the lag distribution moves.

**T7. Population reweighting and transport checks for Partner Z.**
- Method: fit a classifier of Partner Z membership on pre-September covariates (distance, device age, variant, fault type). Use inverse-probability weights to reweight non-Partner-Z cases to Partner Z's covariate distribution. Compare weighted breach with mature Partner Z breach. Check calibration at the same predicted risk.
- If the gap is explained by mix and maturity: weighted non-Partner-Z breach is within about three points of mature Partner Z breach.
- If Partner Z has a real operational difference, such as rural parts logistics: the gap persists after weighting. This is a real risk factor, not evidence of unfair model behaviour.
- If the model is miscalibrated for Partner Z: the calibration gap appears at the same predicted risk, not only in the aggregate.
- Caveat: if Partner Z's distance and device-age values are outside the support of the training data, the weights will be unstable and transport is limited.

**T8. Censoring-aware breach estimation.**
- Method: Kaplan–Meier or cumulative-incidence estimates of time to breach, with closure as a competing event.
- If censoring explains October: the adjusted estimate is about 0.33–0.36, close to September.
- If there is real recovery: the adjusted estimate is about 0.16–0.20.

**T9. Intervention and feedback check.**
- Method: check whether predicted risk influenced routing, for example through the remote-routing policy. Compare predicted risk by variant.
- If routing used the score: predicted risk is systematically lower for V2 cases, and V2 cases are selected on observables. This creates a feedback loop that must be modelled.
- If not: predicted risk is similar across variants, and the June effect is purely policy-driven.

**T10. Resolution of unknown activity.**
- Method: check whether `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` events can be resolved against `activity_raw`, and whether unresolved events cluster in V3 or in a specific supplier status.
- If pipeline mapping: most events are resolvable.
- If a genuine new supplier status event: unresolved events cluster in V3 and in the period after July.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Stage 0: immediate containment (now).**
- Freeze retraining. Do not recalibrate on any window containing April, May (before backfill), September or October.
- Mark September and October metrics as provisional. Publish the maturity gate alongside every metric.
- Add an advisory flag for V3 and Partner Z predictions. Add a manual override for supplier-delayed parts.
- Run the model in shadow mode with the T1–T7 monitors active.

**Stage 1: short-term recalibration (2–4 weeks).**
- Fit an interim calibration map per variant (V1, V2, V3). Use mature labels from Jan–Mar, June and July, plus August cases that are mature at the chosen horizon, with censoring weights.
- Do not include April or May.
- Keep the V3 map on a short expiry, because supplier recovery would make it wrong.
- Treat this as a stopgap. Recalibrating around degraded features can conceal the problem, so the map must be checked against the T2 recomputation before deployment.

**Stage 2: feature repair (4–8 weeks).**
- Recompute April and May features from raw data, including the backfilled event-count feature.
- Apply deduplication and label normalisation to all sources.
- Resolve unknown activity and add a Partner Z queue feed if it is missing.
- Add supplier-lag features: Order Part to Part Received elapsed time, open part orders, and supplier-level lead time.
- Add rurality and device age after support validation.

**Stage 3: full retraining.**
- Wait until September labels reach about 90% maturity, which will take several weeks given the 42–52 day median delay, and until feature repair is validated.
- Training window: Jan–Mar, June (with routing version as a feature), and July–Oct once matured. Use a censoring-aware horizon target.
- Validation: time-based, using fixed-vintage backtests and T4 stratified checks.

**Safe and unsafe months for training.**

| Month | Status | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Jan–Mar | Safe | 100% matured, pipeline valid, stable regime. Reference period. |
| Apr | Unsafe until recomputed | Features corrupted. Safe only after T2 recomputation and validation. |
| May | Unsafe until backfilled | Cached feature not recomputed. |
| Jun | Safe, with a routing-version covariate | Labels mature. Mix change must be modelled, not absorbed. |
| Jul | Conditionally safe | 98% matured and pipeline valid. Supplier regime is starting. Use a regime indicator. Safe for evaluation. |
| Aug | Conditionally unsafe | 93% matured. Censoring bias remains. Use only with censoring-aware targets. |
| Sep | Unsafe | 82% matured, Partner Z labels immature, feature gaps. |
| Oct | Unsafe | 55% matured. Evaluation only as provisional. |

**Segment-specific or variant-specific models.**
- **V3-specific calibration or sub-model:** justified, but only after supplier-lag features exist and interaction tests show that feature effects differ by variant. V3 is about 40% of cases, so volume is sufficient (about 2,100 cases per month in September). Prefer a single model with variant interactions unless validation favours separation.
- **Partner Z-specific model:** not justified yet. It has about one month of data, its labels are immature, and its features are outside the training support. Use stratified calibration and feature additions instead. Revisit after at least three mature months, with roughly 1,000 or more mature Partner Z cases.
- **Variant as a feature:** justified. Variant is a process-level state that changed materially, and the model should be told about it rather than inferring it.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

**Monitor layers.**

| Monitor | Metric | Gate or dependency | Example threshold | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Data quality** | Unknown activity share, missing queue, duplicates, feature lineage hash | Upstream of all others | Unknown >0.5% (warn), >1% (block); missing queue >2% | Blocks performance and drift attribution for affected windows |
| **Label maturity** | Share matured at horizon, median delay, by variant and source | Gates performance and training | <90% provisional; <80% suppressed | Converts performance alerts to provisional; no retraining trigger |
| **Predictive** | AUROC, Brier, on mature cohorts only, with bootstrap CI | Requires DQ green and maturity 90% | AUROC drop >0.03 against reference, two consecutive vintages | Investigate; candidate for retraining only after the checks below |
| **Calibration** | Mature calibration-in-the-large (mean predicted minus KM-adjusted breach); ECE by variant | Requires maturity 90% | \|gap\| >0.03 on two vintages | Recalibration candidate (low cost) |
| **Process variant** | Variant shares; V3 and V2 share; reason codes | Context annotation | Share change >5 points | Annotates model regime; triggers mix-standardised reporting |
| **Performance DFG** | Median and p90 of OrderReceived, DiagnosisRemote Fix, ReceivedDispatch | Context for predictive alarms | Edge >1.5× 8-week median for 2 weeks | Supplier or operations ticket; explains predictive alarms |
| **Population** | Source share; covariate distributions; out-of-support rate | Triggers stratified evaluation | Out-of-support >5%; new source >5% of cases | Stratified evaluation, not retraining |
| **Intervention and deployment** | Model version, mapping patches, routing policy version, supplier status events | Annotation log | Any event | Opens a 30-day explanation window; feedback check (T9) |

**Alert dependency rules.**
1. A data-quality alarm blocks predictive and drift attribution for the affected window. The alarm is recorded as "performance invalid", not "performance degraded".
2. A label-delay alarm does not trigger retraining. It triggers a backfill or data-engineering ticket and converts performance alerts for the affected cohorts to provisional.
3. A performance-DFG alarm, such as the supplier edge, combined with a V3 share change, explains predictive alarms. The response is feature enrichment or an operations action, not retraining alone.
4. A calibration alarm on mature, DQ-green cohorts with no active intervention triggers recalibration.
5. A population alarm triggers stratified evaluation. It does not trigger retraining.
6. Retraining requires all of the following: mature-label degradation persisting for two vintages; DQ green; no unexplained mapping patch; mix-standardised metrics still degraded; and a positive same-path test (T6).

**Monthly process-evidence extraction.** This sketch produces auditable summaries for the monitors above. It does not fit a model. Verify the `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` signature against the installed PM4Py version.

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

RAW_PATH = "field_service_events.csv"   # case_id, activity, timestamp, source, queue
OUTCOME_PATH = "outcomes.csv"           # case_id, breach (0/1/NaN), label_available_ts
CUTOFF = pd.Timestamp("2026-10-31 23:59:59")   # reporting cutoff
RENAME = {"Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"}
UNKNOWN = "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY"
CASE, ACT, TS = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
MONTHS = pd.period_range("2026-01", "2026-10", freq="M")

# 1. Load; keep raw label for audit, then normalise BEFORE any feature extraction
raw = pd.read_csv(RAW_PATH)
raw["ts_sec"] = pd.to_datetime(raw["timestamp"]).dt.floor("s")
raw["activity_raw"] = raw["activity"]
raw["activity"] = raw["activity"].replace(RENAME)
raw["month"] = raw["ts_sec"].dt.to_period("M")

# 2. Duplicate handling, applied to all sources (not only post-20-April).
#    Rule A: exact duplicate on (case, normalised activity, second).
#    Rule B: same case and activity within 60 s (re-emitted events). Parameterise and log.
raw = raw.sort_values(["case_id", "activity", "ts_sec"])
gap_s = raw.groupby(["case_id", "activity"])["ts_sec"].diff().dt.total_seconds()
raw["is_dup"] = raw.duplicated(["case_id", "activity", "ts_sec"], keep="first") | (gap_s <= 60)
dup_by_month = raw.groupby("month")["is_dup"].sum()
by_source_dup = raw.groupby(["source", "month"])["is_dup"].agg(dup_events="sum", events="size")
clean = raw.loc[~raw["is_dup"]].copy()

# 3. Format for PM4Py (pm4py renames columns to case:concept:name / concept:name / time:timestamp)
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    clean, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="ts_sec"
).sort_values([CASE, TS])

# 4. Cohorts
#    Event-calendar cohort: events that occurred in the month. Describes process load
#    and performance conditions in that month, including work on earlier cases.
#    Case-start cohort: cases whose first event fell in the month. This is the unit
#    for predictions and labels; use it for model evaluation.
case_start = df.groupby(CASE)[TS].min()
case_month = case_start.dt.to_period("M")
seqs = df.groupby(CASE)[ACT].agg(list)

def variant_of(seq):
    if "Order Part" in seq:
        return "V3"
    if "Remote Fix" in seq:
        return "V2"
    if "Dispatch Technician" in seq:
        return "V1"
    return "other"

case_variant = seqs.map(variant_of)
outcomes = pd.read_csv(OUTCOME_PATH, parse_dates=["label_available_ts"]).set_index("case_id")

def median_edge_hours(events, a, b):
    """Case-level median elapsed time from first `a` to first `b`."""
    t = events[events[ACT].isin([a, b])]
    first = t.groupby([CASE, ACT])[TS].min().unstack()
    if not {a, b}.issubset(first.columns):
        return np.nan
    return ((first[b] - first[a]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600).dropna().median()

# 5. Monthly summaries (auditable; no model is fitted)
rows = []
for m in MONTHS:
    start, end = m.start_time, m.end_time

    # Event-calendar cohort: process evidence for the month
    ev = pm4py.filter_time_range(df, start, end, mode="events")
    dfg_ev, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(ev)          # mean edge durations, seconds
    perf_mean_h = dfg_ev.get(("Order Part", "Part Received"), np.nan) / 3600

    # Case-start cohort: prediction and label unit
    ids = case_month[case_month == m].index
    cs = df[df[CASE].isin(ids)]
    n_variants_distinct = len(pm4py.get_variants(cs))          # cross-check on variant count

    v = case_variant.reindex(ids)
    out = outcomes.reindex(ids)
    matured = out["label_available_ts"] <= CUTOFF

    def matured_rate(mask):
        sel = matured & mask
        return out.loc[sel, "breach"].mean() if sel.any() else np.nan

    queue_first = cs.groupby(CASE)["queue"].first()

    rows.append({
        "month": str(m),
        "cases_started": len(ids),
        "label_maturity": matured.mean(),
        "share_V1": (v == "V1").mean(),
        "share_V2": (v == "V2").mean(),
        "share_V3": (v == "V3").mean(),
        "share_other": (v == "other").mean(),
        "matured_breach_all": matured_rate(pd.Series(True, index=ids)),
        "matured_breach_V1": matured_rate(v == "V1"),
        "matured_breach_V2": matured_rate(v == "V2"),
        "matured_breach_V3": matured_rate(v == "V3"),
        "missing_queue_share_cases": queue_first.isna().mean(),
        "unknown_share_events_casestart": (cs[ACT] == UNKNOWN).mean(),
        "unknown_share_events_calendar": (ev[ACT] == UNKNOWN).mean(),
        "dup_events_removed": int(dup_by_month.get(m, 0)),
        "distinct_variants_casestart": n_variants_distinct,
        "case_median_order_to_received_h": median_edge_hours(cs, "Order Part", "Part Received"),
        "calendar_mean_order_to_received_h": perf_mean_h,
        "case_median_diag_to_remote_h": median_edge_hours(
            cs, "Initial Diagnosis Completed", "Remote Fix"),
    })

summary = pd.DataFrame(rows).set_index("month")
summary.to_csv("monthly_process_evidence_audit.csv")
print(summary.round(3).to_string())
print(by_source_dup)

# Optional lineage comparison (API varies by PM4Py version; check the installed signature).

#                                                      timestamp_column=TS)

```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

**1. Retraining on immature September and October labels.**
- Risk: unresolved long-path cases are labelled non-breach, so the model learns that V3 and Partner Z risk is lower than it is. The next model underpredicts further.
- Mitigation: enforce the maturity gate. Use censoring-aware or horizon-based targets. Exclude cohorts below 90% maturity from training and recalibration.

**2. Calibrating around a pipeline bug.**
- Risk: a fixed intercept learned from April or May absorbs the feature corruption. It appears to fit, then breaks when the feature state changes.
- Mitigation: recompute features from raw data before any calibration (T2). Do not calibrate on windows flagged by the data-quality monitor. Log every calibration map with the feature-lineage hash it depends on.

**3. Ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept drift.**
- Risk: August–September degradation is attributed to Partner Z, to label noise, or to a routing artefact. The supplier edge goes unaddressed, and V3 predictions keep underpredicting.
- Mitigation: run the lag-stratified same-path test (T6) and the non-Partner-Z V3 check. Add supplier-lag features. Monitor the performance DFG independently of the model.

**4. Attributing Partner Z's population shift to unfair model behaviour without stratified evidence.**
- Risk: Partner Z's higher breach is treated as model bias. The response is an unwarranted redesign, or a Partner Z-specific model built on one month of immature data.
