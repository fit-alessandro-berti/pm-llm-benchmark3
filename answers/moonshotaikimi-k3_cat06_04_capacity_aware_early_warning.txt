## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

- **Cool-down:** once an alert is assessed, the case keeps a *standing assessment*. Re-alerts without a material event are suppressed for 2h (4 refresh cycles): they do not re-enter the queue, reset priority, or consume analyst attention. The case is automatically re-scored at cool-down expiry.
- **Material event (re-entry trigger):** stage transition; new artifact (log, diagnostic file, customer message); \|p\|  0.10 between refreshes; restoration of a failed feature; deadline crossing T2h. A material event bypasses cool-down and re-opens assessment immediately, but eligibility (stage, prior-swarm rule) is re-checked.
- **Escalation safety valve (non-monetary):** duty-manager review is mandatory if p_adj  0.80 at any refresh, or if deadline < 2h with p_adj  0.6 — even inside cool-down. The manager may override capacity decisions, but the override and rationale are logged for the fairness audit (§5).
- **Repeat-swarm rule:** at most one swarm per case unless a material event occurs *and* the first swarm's outcome is documented; hard cap of two swarms per case.
- **I-05 vs. I-07 (the explicit comparison):** I-05's third alert carries p = +0.02 with no new event — statistically indistinguishable from refresh noise. It is **suppressed**: its standing score (+€0.3k, T3) persists but it neither jumps the queue nor gets a slot; because SME underprediction means its true risk is plausibly 0.61–0.66, it receives a scheduled human glance at cool-down expiry rather than silent burial. I-07's third alert accompanies a **new customer diagnostic log** — a material event — so it **re-enters** with a fresh assessment despite p ticking *down* (0.750.72); the new evidence, not the score movement, justifies re-processing. Its fresh score (€0.6k after spillover charge) does not clear capacity, and the escalation valve fires (p_adj  0.79, deadline 13:30) but the duty manager confirms the reserve is retained — logged. Had I-07 cleared the cut, the same material event would also have satisfied the repeat-intervention gate.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

- **Value-based starvation.** Pure EV ranking systematically skips low-value cases (I-07, I-10, I-01, I-05 all sit below the cut today; SME cases will rarely beat €25k–100k Strategic cases). Safeguards: (a) a **service floor** — when daily capacity allows, ~15–20% of slots go to the highest-scoring EV-positive case under €15k value, chosen by weighted rotation rather than strict rank; (b) cheaper tiered mitigations (async expert review, templated diagnostics) for cases that never justify a swarm, so "no slot"  "no help"; (c) monthly dashboard of intervention rate vs. breach rate by value band, with a cap on consecutive skips per segment.
- **Non-Native-Language Support uncertainty.** With sparse validation, the model could be silently mis-serving these customers in either direction. Safeguards: interval inflation (§1) rather than exclusion; **deliberate inclusion** — I-09's slot is partly an information investment, and NNLS cases are prioritized in the equipoise-band evaluation design (§6) so the evidence base grows; check that language support is never used as an informal triage proxy by reviewers.
- **Differential calibration.** Unadjusted scores would over-serve Strategic (overpredicted) and under-serve SME (underpredicted) — a *policy-induced* allocation bias on top of a measurement bias. Safeguards: the segment adjustments in §1, re-estimated monthly with shrinkage toward the global curve for small segments; adjustments published to reviewers; frozen if drift is detected.
- **Selective intervention corrupts future labels.** Breach outcomes of swarmed cases reflect treatment, so naive retraining conflates policy with risk and will progressively understate the risk of cases we routinely save. Safeguards: log the full decision context (score, capacity state, segment, dispatch) with every outcome; always train with a treatment indicator and/or inverse-propensity weighting; maintain an **equipoise band** (score within ±€500 of the cut) where intervention is randomized or deferred one refresh cycle — ethically defensible because we are genuinely uncertain there — to preserve an unbiased evaluation stream.
- **Spillover incidence.** The 0.01–0.03 breach-probability increase lands on six *untreated* diagnosis-stage cases; if those are disproportionately SME/low-value, the policy's costs are regressive even when its benefits look meritorious. Safeguards: attribute measured queue delay and breach deltas by segment monthly ("spillover burden share"); cap concurrent swarms when the congestion index exceeds a threshold, since spillover is congestion-dependent; if any segment bears >40% of excess delay for two consecutive months, rebalance the portfolio rule or the scheduling of swarms.
- **Specialist load.** Interruptions displace ordinary diagnosis work and concentrate burden on two people; rotate swarm duty and track displaced-work hours as a real capacity cost, not just the €450.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational monitoring (daily/weekly):** slot utilization and reserve expirations; alert-to-dispatch latency; share of swarms dispatched pre-Diagnosis-Complete (target 100% — re-check at dispatch); measured aggregate queue delay per slot vs. the assumed 8–20 min; suppression counts and override log review.

**Predictive monitoring (weekly, by segment):** reliability diagrams, ECE and Brier score with confidence intervals for SME / Strategic / Public Sector / NNLS separately; re-estimate the segment offsets monthly with shrinkage (trigger: \|bias\| > 0.05 for two consecutive months  update the adjustment); population-stability index on features and score distribution to catch drift from the Strategic workflow redesign (the known cause of current overprediction); feature-availability rate to catch I-06-type failures faster.

**Causal-effect estimation (ongoing, quarterly readouts):** (a) exploit the policy's own variation — regression discontinuity around the priority cut and around slot-exhaustion days compares near-identical cases with and without swarms; (b) randomized encouragement / one-cycle deferral in the equipoise band, oversampling NNLS and SME to close the evidence gap fastest; (c) IPW-matched comparisons as fallback; (d) estimate the spillover coefficient directly (untreated-case breach delta per concurrent swarm, instrumented by congestion) to replace the assumed 0.01–0.03 and the €12k monetization. Update each segment's effect range quarterly; retire ranges that three designs contradict.

**Capacity monitoring:** shadow price of the marginal slot (how often the reserve is used vs. expires — if it expires unused > 60% of days, shrink the reserve; if regulatory arrivals regularly find no slot, enlarge it); congestion index vs. realized spillover; specialist utilization and displaced-work hours.

**Fairness monitoring (monthly):** intervention rate, breach rate, false-alarm burden incidence, and spillover-delay share by segment, language-support flag, and value band; alert-fatigue metrics (alerts per case, suppression rate, override rate).

**Drift and policy-change governance:** because intervention changes the data-generating process, run a monthly **shadow-mode sample** (policy scores logged, no action) to measure natural breach rates and detect policy-induced drift. Monthly review board with pre-registered triggers: realized net impact negative on > 1 in 6 portfolio-days  tighten the cut or enlarge the reserve; a segment's estimated effect midpoint falls below its cost-inclusive break-even  revise its range and tier thresholds; calibration adjustments stop improving out-of-sample Brier  revert to global calibration. Change one parameter at a time, with a 4-week champion–challenger evaluation window and full decision logs so every change is attributable and reversible.
