# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

**R07_Giorgio: commercial shadow role**

Giorgio is a senior front-office agent with a pricing certification. He handles about 72 A7 and 88 A8 events in the 10-week window, which is roughly 7 pricing events per week. That is a regular function, not just emergency cover.

- Benefits: He reduces the bottleneck created by Fatima's single-point availability. He has the seniority (7.4 years) and the complexity exposure to make reasonable pricing decisions on front-office cases. His hybrid profile makes front-office and commercial handoffs faster.
- Risks: Pricing exceptions made through a front-office channel may not have the same review rigor as the Commercial function. His median pricing duration during substitution (74 minutes) suggests that cover costs time. If his pricing authority is informal, there is no clear audit trail for decisions, and his certification may not match what he actually does.
- Interpretation: He is a shadow commercial specialist, not an improper one. Formalizing his authority with explicit limits, certification and audit records would convert shadow capacity into a documented resilience asset.

**R13_Mateo: cross-functional escalation broker**

Mateo does not fit any formal cluster. He is an Incident Coordinator with a diffuse profile of about 415 events and a very high share of high and critical cases (about 52%).

- Benefits: He hops work between functions (fragments 3 and 5), reduces unnecessary escalations (the false escalation complaints fell in Weeks 7–8), and gives cross-functional continuity. His preliminary approvals and reopen handling keep work moving when Hana is absent.
- Risks: His filtering of Erik's referrals lowered specialist exposure, and two critical cases waited more than 2 days. His preliminary approvals were rechecked 62% of the time. Mateo is a single point of failure for triage and an informal approval channel. Without a decision-rights matrix, his interventions are hard to audit.
- Interpretation: He is a bridging role that performs routing, triage and provisional authority. This is useful but needs formal boundaries.

**R05_Erik: rare but critical specialist**

Erik has the highest severity exposure in the organization (median complexity 0.86; 55 critical cases). Total volume is low (about 210 events), so frequency clustering understates his role.

- Benefits: He is the only security certification holder in the observed data. His involvement reduces security risk on the highest-severity cases.
- Risks: He is a single point of failure for security review. Weeks 7–8 show that both excessive triage (reducing his volume) and insufficient triage (critical cases waiting) are risks. Low volume can lead management to under-resource the role.
- Interpretation: He is a rare-intervention specialist whose importance is in the case severity, not the event count.

**Additional shadow and bridging roles**

- **Hana as approval-with-rechecks broker:** Her week-5 rechecks show she is also the final control point for provisional decisions made by others. Her authority is a bridge between Mateo's triage and formal approval.
- **Ivan and Julia as remediation gatekeepers:** Their Configure and Implement events show that Quality has partly become a remediation function. This is a quality-control benefit (catch and fix) with a risk of independence loss (the team that checks also changes).
- **Temporary workforce as an edge role:** Kai and Lina handle high-volume low-complexity work. Their Validate notes feed reopens traced to others. They provide capacity but their rework cost is not visible in their own counts.
- **Nora as automation boundary:** Nora is a system resource performing human-like intake. Its precheck can create false negatives and reduce human visibility into initial entitlement quality.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

The code below is illustrative. Verify function signatures and return structures against your installed PM4Py version. The structure of the discovered roles should be adapted to the returned object.

```python
import pandas as pd
import networkx as nx
import pm4py

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Load and format the event data
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
events = pd.read_csv("order_resolution_events.csv")

# Standard PM4Py-style columns
events = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    events,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)

# Ensure temporary_substitution and escalation_level are present and typed
events["temporary_substitution"] = events["temporary_substitution"].fillna(False).astype(bool)
events["escalation_level"] = events["escalation_level"].fillna(0).astype(int)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Case-level attributes (joined back to events later)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
case_attrs = (
    events.groupby("case:concept:name")
    .agg(
        case_complexity=("case_complexity", "first"),
        customer_segment=("customer_segment", "first"),
        product_line=("product_line", "first"),
        any_substitution=("temporary_substitution", "max"),
        max_escalation=("escalation_level", "max"),
    )
    .reset_index()
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Organizational role discovery
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# Discovers groups of resources that share sets of activities.
# Parameter names may vary by PM4Py version; verify with help(pm4py.discover_organizational_roles).
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(
    events,
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource",
)

# Assume roles is a list of (resource_set, activity_set) tuples.
# Convert to a resource -> role_id map. Overlapping memberships are allowed,
# so keep a multi-valued mapping rather than forcing one role per resource.
role_membership = {}
for role_id, (res_set, act_set) in enumerate(roles):
    for r in res_set:
        role_membership.setdefault(r, []).append(f"role_{role_id}")

role_table = pd.DataFrame(
    [(r, rid) for r, rids in role_membership.items() for rid in rids],
    columns=["org:resource", "discovered_role"],
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Activity-based resource similarity
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
sim_raw = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(
    events,
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource",
)
# Assume it returns a dict keyed by (res_a, res_b) or a matrix; normalize to a DataFrame.
sim_df = pd.DataFrame(sim_raw).T if isinstance(sim_raw, dict) else pd.DataFrame(sim_raw)

# Flag cross-title similarity: similarity high but formal titles differ
# (titles would come from a separate HR/resource table)
resource_titles = pd.read_csv("resource_titles.csv")  # resource, formal_title, home_team

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Handover-of-work and working-together networks
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
    events,
    case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    resource_key="org:resource",
)
working_together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(
    events,
    case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource",
)

# Convert to networkx for centrality analysis (betweenness flags bridging roles)
def to_nx(network_dict):
    G = nx.DiGraph()
    for (src, tgt), weight in network_dict.items():
        G.add_edge(src, tgt, weight=weight)
    return G

G_hand = to_nx(handover)
G_wt = to_nx(working_together.items() and {(a, b): w for (a, b), w in working_together.items()})

bridging = pd.DataFrame({
    "betweenness_handover": pd.Series(nx.betweenness_centrality(G_hand, weight="weight")),
    "betweenness_together": pd.Series(nx.betweenness_centrality(G_wt, weight="weight")),
}).fillna(0).reset_index().rename(columns={"index": "org:resource"})

# Expect Mateo and Hana to score high on betweenness; Nora on incoming handover.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Join role output with complexity and substitution indicators
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
events_roles = events.merge(role_table, on="org:resource", how="left")
events_roles = events_roles.merge(case_attrs, on="case:concept:name", how="left")

# Substitution share per discovered role
substitution_profile = (
    events_roles.groupby(["discovered_role", "case_complexity"])
    .agg(
        events=("concept:name", "size"),
        substitution_share=("temporary_substitution", "mean"),
        escalation_mean=("max_escalation", "mean"),
    )
    .reset_index()
)

# Resource-level profile: complexity exposure and substitution
resource_profile = (
    events.merge(case_attrs, on="case:concept:name", how="left")
    .groupby("org:resource")
    .agg(
        events=("concept:name", "size"),
        high_or_critical_share=(
            "case_complexity",
            lambda s: s.isin(["high", "critical"]).mean(),
        ),
        substitution_share=("temporary_substitution", "mean"),
    )
    .reset_index()
    .merge(bridging, on="org:resource", how="left")
    .merge(resource_titles, on="org:resource", how="left")
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 7. Guardrail: do not over-interpret activity-frequency clusters
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# Flag mismatches between discovered roles and formal titles
resource_profile["discovered_role_mismatch"] = False
# Set True when a resource's dominant discovered role differs from its formal home team,
# e.g. Giorgio (Front Office) grouped with the Commercial pricing role.

# Flag resources with low volume but high severity exposure (rare-critical signal)
resource_profile["rare_critical_flag"] = (
    (resource_profile["events"] < resource_profile["events"].quantile(0.25))
    & (resource_profile["high_or_critical_share"] > 0.5)
)

# Flag substitution-driven volume: pricing events during substitution weeks
# should not be treated as evidence of permanent competence without outcome data.
resource_profile["substitution_caveat"] = resource_profile["substitution_share"] > 0.05

print(resource_profile.sort_values("high_or_critical_share", ascending=False))
```

**Guardrails in the code**
- Overlapping role membership is retained because Giorgio, Mateo and Amira legitimately belong to more than one functional pattern.
- Role output is joined to formal titles, complexity and substitution, so a discovered cluster can be checked against decision rights.
- Low-volume, high-severity resources are flagged so they are not discarded as noise.
- Substitution share is reported as a caveat, not as evidence of competence.
