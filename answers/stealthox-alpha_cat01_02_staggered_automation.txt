## 1. What is observed

The panel records whether the triage system was **available** (`automation=1`), not how intensively it was used: actual use (`automated_fast_path_pct`) rose only gradually from a ~4% baseline to 25–50% over 2–5 months, and even in August roughly half of cases in adopters still followed manual paths. Adoption was **targeted, not random**: Alpha and Beta went live in April because they had high volumes and poor historical cycle times; Gamma followed a readiness pilot in July. Treated branches therefore differ systematically from never-treated Delta and Epsilon. Several **contemporaneous changes** coincide with rollout: a centralized fraud desk from March (escalation_pct jumps ~2pp in *every* branch that month), a routing campaign adding ~10% of arrivals to Alpha/Beta from April and ~5% to Gamma from July, staffing changes (Alpha 2 FTE in May, Beta +1 in April, Gamma 1 in June, Delta +1 in June), and a May product change raising high-complexity shares in all branches. **Outcome measurement is limited**: medians and p90s are branch-month aggregates without dispersion or counts; rework/escalation definitions are unstated; CSAT is a monthly mean from surveys with 20–45% response rates and unknown respondent counts. A data anomaly — 4–6% "automated fast path" in months with `automation=0` — suggests measurement noise or pre-existing partial automation. No causal claim follows from these observations alone.

Interference note: if adopters' extra fraud-desk demand slows the shared desk, controls deteriorate too, biasing DiD *toward* finding adopter improvement; if desk congestion hits adopters harder, it biases *against*. Treatment-version note: E1 (availability) and E3 (use) answer different questions; leadership's "X%" statement implicitly assumes E1  scaled E3, which the data do not establish.

## 3. Identification strategy

**Primary strategy**

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Heterogeneity-robust staggered-adoption event study (Callaway–Sant'Anna group-time ATTs / Sun–Abraham interaction weights), never-treated comparison | Adoption month per branch (Apr: Alpha/Beta; Jul: Gamma); event-time dummies binned (4, 3/2, ref 1, 0/1, 2/3, 4); never-treated coded 0 | Delta & Epsilon (never treated through Aug-2026); Gamma doubles as not-yet-treated control for Apr–Jun | Calendar-month FE (absorbs fraud desk, product change); descriptively condition on load (arrivals/FTE) and complexity; wild cluster bootstrap for inference | E1, E2, E7 (group-time ATTs, aggregable to summary ATT; dynamic path) | Parallel trends conditional on month FE; no anticipation; no spillovers to controls (E8=0); common shocks affect groups proportionally | Pre-period event-time coefficients  0; placebo go-lives; per-branch synthetic-DiD cross-check; compare shock exposure (complexity) across groups | Differential shock exposure (routing +10%, Alpha's FTE loss, larger complexity jump in adopters) + regression to the mean from selection  overstates improvement; 5 clusters  fragile inference |

**Alternative 1 — synthetic control / synthetic DiD per adopter**

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Per-adopter SCM / synthetic DiD | Go-live indicator at branch-specific date | Weighted donor pool: Delta, Epsilon (plus Gamma pre-July for Alpha/Beta) | Pre-adoption outcome fit weights; approximate covariate balance | E1, E2 per branch | Donor pool spans the counterfactual; no treated-unique shocks | Pre-fit RMSPE; in-time placebo; leave-one-donor-out | Only 2–3 donors  poor fit, no valid inference |

**Alternative 2 — comparative interrupted time series (CITS)**

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CITS per adopter vs blended control; level + slope change at go-live | Step and trend-break at adoption month | Delta/Epsilon composite | Linear pre-trend, month FE, autocorrelation-robust (Newey–West) SEs | E1 (level shift at 3 months post) | Linear counterfactual extrapolation; shocks captured by control series | Pre-period trend stability, residual autocorrelation, break-date sensitivity | Only 3 clean pre-months; March fraud-desk break sits *inside* the pre-period |

**Alternative 3 — treatment-intensity (dose) DiD**

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Continuous treatment: `automated_fast_path_pct` (change from 4–6% baseline), branch + month FE | Dose 025–50% | Within-branch over time and across branches | Branch FE, month FE, load, complexity | E3 | Uptake variation exogenous after FE — **implausible** | Event-study of uptake; residualized scatter | Uptake endogenous (learning, case selection)  biased dose effect; descriptive only |

**Why the primary strategy is preferable.** With five branches, eight months, and staggered timing, the binding constraints are (i) not comparing already-treated units as controls, (ii) not averaging over dynamically different post-periods, and (iii) being honest that inference rests on 3 treated clusters. The heterogeneity-robust event study with never-treated comparison groups satisfies all three: it uses every branch-month, never uses an already-treated branch as a control, replaces the static post×treated term (which a naïve TWFE uses and which is biased under staggered adoption with heterogeneous effects) with event-time dummies that expose the honeymoon-then-reversion pattern visible in rework, and yields group-time ATTs that can be aggregated or reported separately for the April and July cohorts. Month fixed effects absorb the two common shocks (March fraud desk, May product change) to the extent they hit branches proportionally — a testable-but-underpowered condition we check by comparing shock exposure. Synthetic DiD and CITS are retained as transparent per-branch robustness checks rather than primaries because a 2–3 unit donor pool cannot support credible counterfactual weights, and trend extrapolation from three pre-points (one of which contains a structural break) is fragile. The dose model is kept strictly descriptive because uptake is a choice. All strategies share the same ultimate weakness — endogenous rollout — which no estimator can repair; only the embedded evaluation in Section 8 can.

The dose model in Section 9 estimates this association with branch and month FE; because uptake is chosen (learning, case selection), this is a descriptive dose–response, not an identified per-10pp effect.

**4. Pre-trends and regression to the mean.** JanMar median changes: Alpha 1.0h, Beta +1.0h, Gamma +3.2h, Delta +1.8h, Epsilon 1.2h — no clear divergence, but three points and a March structural break (fraud desk) make pre-trend tests uninformative. RTM is a live threat: Alpha/Beta were *selected* for poor historical performance, so mechanical improvement would inflate DiD. Mitigating observation: adopters' **raw** medians did not improve (Alpha 3.3h late post but +1.9h full post; Beta +0.9h late post); the improvement exists only *relative to controls that deteriorated* (May product change), a pattern more consistent with common-shock recovery than RTM — but not decisive, because the routing campaign and Alpha's FTE loss pushed adopters' raw numbers the other way.

**5. Sensitivity / bounded conclusions.** Varying windows (late post Jul–Aug vs full post Apr–Aug; pre Jan–Mar vs Jan–Apr vs Jan–Jun) and controls (Delta+Epsilon vs Gamma-as-not-yet-treated): **median** DiD stays in **[6.9, 2.8] h (14% to 6%)** for every adopter and permutation — a relative improvement of at least ~3h (~6%) survives every scenario we could construct; the point value is uncertain between ~3 and ~7h. **p90**: raw changes are **+6.9 to +9.5h in every adopter** (tails unambiguously worsened in absolute terms); relative DiD is 5.2 to 7.8h, but this cannot be separated from the common May–June shock (controls' p90 rose +12.6 to +16.9h). **Rework**: raw 0.5 to +1.3pp, DiD 1.2 to 2.6pp, with a front-loaded (month-1 dip, then reversion) pattern. **CSAT**: DiD +0.06 to +0.13 — within plausible survey noise; not identified. Median and p90 are deliberately **not** collapsed: the defensible story is "medians held flat-to-better under rising load; tails worsened in absolute terms and are unresolved relative to counterfactual."

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**`expand with an embedded evaluation`**

**Next sites:** Epsilon first, Delta second. Epsilon resembles the treated regime (complexity 30–38%, elevated cycle times, ~850–960 arrivals/month), so it tests whether the apparent benefit generalizes where it matters; Delta (high-performing, low complexity, recently +1 FTE) tests generalizability to healthy branches at low operational risk.

**Assignment mechanism:** randomized stepped-wedge — both branches adopt, but the *order and dates* (2026-11-01 vs 2027-02-01) are drawn by an external party. Where case-level eligibility can be defined, add a within-branch encouragement design (randomized prompts toward automated triage) to create exogenous treatment-version variation for the dose estimand.

**Measurement:** 6 additional clean pre-months (Sep–Feb) with no other policy changes; post-period 6 months per branch, since month 1–2 effects (Alpha/Beta's rework dipped then reverted) differ from steady state. Collect case-level event logs with activity timestamps (register, triage type/start, specialist-review enter/exit, rework loops, resolve, close); per-case automated-path **eligibility** and **actual path** (separating eligibility from uptake); specialist and **fraud-desk queue lengths and waiting times by originating branch** — today's critical blind spot; SLA breaches and abandonments; complexity class at registration and at resolution.

**Spillover monitoring:** fraud-desk waits by branch of origin, specialist utilization, routed volumes, and continued publication of control-branch outcomes so cross-branch contamination is quantified rather than assumed away.

**Customer-harm guardrails:** pause expansion if p90 exceeds the branch's own baseline by >10% for two consecutive months, rework exceeds baseline +2pp, escalation exceeds +3pp, or CSAT response rate falls below 20%; report CSAT response counts monthly.

**Decision rule (pre-registered, evaluated at month 6 post, wild-cluster-bootstrap inference):** scale to remaining sites if median DiD  3h ( 6%) versus synthetic control with p90, rework, and escalation inside guardrails; if medians improve but tails breach guardrails, remediate routing/escalation before scaling; if the median effect is null but arrivals-per-FTE improves 15% with guardrails met, decide as a capacity case on cost grounds; otherwise hold. This design buys leadership both a cleaner answer and a safer rollout.

## 10. Final claim ladder

- **Observed:** After go-live, automated fast-path use rose from a ~4–6% baseline to 25–50% over 2–5 months; adopters absorbed 13–29% more arrivals per FTE (vs ~6–10% in controls) while raw median cycle times stayed roughly flat (3.4 to +1.9h), raw p90 worsened +7 to +10h, rework ended near pre-adoption levels after a transient month-1 dip, escalations rose +2.0 to +3.1pp, and CSAT was flat.
- **Suggestive:** Relative to never- and not-yet-treated branches over the same calendar windows, adopter median cycle times ran ~3–7h (~6–14%) below the no-adoption trend and rework ~1–2.6pp lower in every window/control permutation we constructed, while escalations ran ~0.7–1.2pp higher — a pattern consistent with automated triage substituting for manual handling while rerouting borderline cases to specialists.
- **Conditionally causal:** If parallel trends, no anticipation, and no fraud-desk/routing spillovers hold — i.e., if the May product change, routing campaign, staffing shifts, and shared-desk congestion affected adopters and controls comparably — then branch availability plausibly reduced steady-state median cycle time by roughly 5–10% relative to the counterfactual without further tail damage; this is the strongest statement the current design supports, it does not extend to p90 or CSAT, and it carries wide uncertainty from three treated branches.
- **Not supported:** A single universal figure like "automation reduces cycle time by X%" across branches, outcomes, and horizons; any claim of p90 (tail) improvement; any claim about CSAT effects; any case-level or per-10pp-of-use causal effect; and any assumption that Delta or Epsilon would experience the same effects, since adopters were selected for poor performance and high volume and no branch was randomly assigned.
