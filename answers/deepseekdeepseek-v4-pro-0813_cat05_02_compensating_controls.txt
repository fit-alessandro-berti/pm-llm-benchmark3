## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 is not merely “different names in two columns.” Its control objective is to prevent one actor or compromised identity from moving an erroneous or fraudulent payment through to irrevocable value transfer without independent challenge.

| Objective/threat scenario | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prevent one person from both initiating and authorizing a payment | Preventive for specific data errors pre-release; does not prevent all errors or collusion | Strong preventive only if independent and before release or before irrevocable settlement | Detective after the fact; corrective only if recall still feasible | Allows a constrained same-user exception, but does not remove the objective |
| Prevent payee/bank-account tampering after validation | Checks bank-account ownership and bank changes in previous 7 days | Can prevent if manager verifies bank details independently | Detective; often too late for recall | Requires no bank change in previous 7 days and AV-2 full pass |
| Prevent duplicate or sanctions/operator-limit violations | Covers duplicate invoice, sanctions, operator limit, PO match | Can review, but should not be primary for these automated checks | Detective after release | Requires AV-2 fully operational and passed without override |
| Prevent payment without receipt of goods/services or commercial reasonableness | Not covered | Potentially preventive if manager reviews receipt and commercial reasonableness | Detective after release; may find fabricated receipt later | Only as strong as manager’s actual review |
| Prevent collusion, shared-credential, or shared-workstation defeat of separation | Not designed to detect collusion | Can resist if independent, not conflicted, and not part of collusion | Detective through patterns, but after the fact | Not designed to resist collusion |
| Prevent loss from becoming irrevocable before correction | Preventive before release | Hybrid if after release but before settlement; weak if after settlement | Detective; partly corrective only before recall window | Requires manager before settlement and audit within one business day |
| Prevent abuse of emergency exceptions | Not relevant to emergency need | Can validate emergency need if truly independent | Audit can detect false emergency after the fact | Requires token before Prepare_Payment, caps amount, vendor age, bank-change absence, AV pass, manager approval, and timely audit |

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A compensating package should not be judged by “was any control present,” but by whether it reduced the control objective to at least the level of a genuinely independent second releaser, or, for allowed exceptions, to a level justified by ED-1.

| Dimension | Fully equivalent | Partially equivalent | Merely mitigating | Ineffective |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Timing | Independent release or independent manager review before release and before irrevocable settlement | Manager review after release but before settlement | Manager review after settlement but while recall may still be possible | After settlement and after recall window, or no review |
| Independence | No conflict, no shared credentials, no shared workstation, no same actor | Formal independence but residual pair/workstation or design ambiguity | Supervisor or conflicted manager, or rubber-stamp review | Same actor, shared credential, or reviewer in conflict |
| Coverage | AV-2 full pass/no override plus manager verifies invoice, bank details, receipt where relevant | AV-2 pass but manager review incomplete or audit late | AV-2 warning overridden, unsigned checklist, or AV unavailable | AV unavailable or overridden and no effective manager review |
| Overrideability | No warning override, or override by independent manager before release with documented rationale | Same-user override but later independent manager before settlement | Override using another person’s credential or authority | Same-user override with no pre-settlement independent review |
| Recoverability | Audit started within recall window and recall/recovery capacity confirmed | Audit started after settlement but within one business day; recovery possible | Audit after recall window; recovery uncertain | No timely audit, no recall, or funds lost |
| Evidence quality | System logs plus signed manager review and supporting invoices/bank/services evidence | Good system logs but incomplete manager/audit workpapers | Unsigned manual checklist, missing invoice/bank support, or unresolved access logs | No reliable evidence for key assertions |
| Collusion resistance | Rotated independent releaser, separate access, no repeated suspicious pair | Distinct users but repeated pair and shared workstation | Known supervisory or family/vendor-related conflict | Same actor, shared service account, or confirmed collusion |

“Same expected loss” is not the only possible standard because control failures can be tail events with non-quantifiable probability, concentrated single-payment exposure, systematic tolerance of exceptions, undetected collusion, and regulatory or governance per se requirements. A control package can have a low average expected loss because most payments are legitimate, yet still be inadequate because it normalizes uncontrolled exceptions or leaves catastrophic tail risk open.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control | Type | Effective timing | Coverage | Overrideability and limits | Main limitation | Same-user case role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AV-2 | Preventive, with detective warning elements | Before release unless unavailable | Duplicate invoice, sanctions, bank ownership, 7-day bank changes, operator limit, basic PO match | Can emit warning override; cannot detect collusion, receipt fraud, commercial reasonableness, or account control | Narrow automated checks; clean pass is not evidence of receipt or innocence | First-line automated gate |
| MG-4 | Hybrid: preventive if before release or before irrevocable settlement; detective if later | Strongest before release or before bank settlement | Broad if manager reviews invoice, bank account, receipt, and reasonableness | Human judgment, conflict of interest, rubber-stamp risk | Not effective if after settlement or conflicted | Independent human check or authorization |
| RA-7 | Detective; partly corrective only before recall | After release/settlement | Forensic review of payment support, anomalies, patterns | Cannot restore lost funds after recall window | After-the-fact; depends on audit quality and speed | Backstop detection and possible recall |
| ED-1 | Conditional permission package, not a standalone control | Conditions span before Prepare_Payment, before settlement, and after | Emergency need, amount cap, vendor age, bank-change absence, AV pass, manager approval, audit | If any condition fails, exception is not authorized | Still permits same-user release; not collusion-resistant | Defines when same-user release can be tolerable |

A manager action after `Release_Payment` but before `Bank_Settlement` is best classified as **hybrid** in this process logic. It is detective with respect to the release decision already made, but preventive with respect to irreversible bank settlement. That distinction matters because internal `Release_Payment` does not necessarily mean funds have moved immediately.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

- **Same-user rate**: Monitor same-user completed payments by case, amount, and business area. A small number of low-risk ED-1-compliant cases can be tolerable; an increase in same-user convenience cases should alert governance.
- **Override rate**: Track all AV-2 warnings/overrides. Any override of bank-change, sanctions, duplicate, or operator-limit warnings should require a separate incident review and evidence of manager pre-release approval. Overrides using another person’s credential should be treated as a control breach, not routine.
- **Late manager approval**: Distinguish manager approval before release, after release but before settlement, and after settlement. After-settlement ratification should trigger an incident. After-release pre-settlement ratification is a hybrid control and should be measured separately.
- **Audit-before-recall-window**: For same-user emergency payments, require RA-7 audit to begin within one business day. Measure audit start time from release or settlement, not from working-day end. Any late audit should be an alert.
- **Repeated resource pairs**: Count prepare/release pairs, especially values over time. A pair recurring at high frequency, or after a prior defect, should trigger rotation and independence review. This is an alert, not proof of misconduct.
- **Shared workstations**: Log workstation, session, user account, and escalation permissions. Shared workstations for payment preparation/release should be rare, risk-rated, and monitored. A shared workstation weakens four-eyes even when resource names differ.
- **Bank-change proximity**: Any payment with a bank-account change within seven days should be high-risk, regardless of amount. Require AV-2 full pass, no override, independent manager before release, and confirmation of bank ownership before settlement.
- **Control outages**: Track AV-2 or validation-service unavailability. During an outage, no same-user release should occur; high-risk or high-value releases should require signed manual checklist and independent manager before release or settlement.
- **Alert versus proof**: A monitoring alert is an indicator that a control may have failed or an exception may be unauthorized. Misconduct or control failure requires specific evidence such as access logs, credential use records, manager review workpapers, bank verification, or audit findings.
