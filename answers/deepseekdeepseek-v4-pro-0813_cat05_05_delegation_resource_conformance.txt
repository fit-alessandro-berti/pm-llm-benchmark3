## 2. Rule Interpretation

**RR1 — Drafter/approver separation**  
For each case, after alias normalization, the human identity of `Draft_Change_Request` must differ from the human identity of `Approve_Change`. If they are equal, RR1 is violated regardless of risk level.

**RR2 — Implementer/verifier separation**  
For each case, after alias normalization, the human identity of `Implement_Change` must differ from the human identity of `Verify_Implementation`. A service account cannot satisfy this unless reliable session evidence maps it to an authorized human.

**RR3 — High-risk approval authority**  
For HIGH-risk cases, `Approve_Change` must be performed by:

- the standing QM, **Asha Rao**, at event time; or
- a valid QM delegate.

A delegation is valid only if:

- event timestamp is inside the effective interval;
- scope covers the case;
- principal is absent or formally unavailable where required;
- no prohibited subdelegation occurred.

Direct action by a principal while on leave is not automatically invalid for RR3 if the action is truly performed by that principal. But leave creates an identity/availability conflict when the evidence cannot confirm the actor.

**RR4 — Emergency substitution**  
A HIGH-risk emergency change may be approved by the IC or valid IC delegate, but a QM or valid QM delegate must perform `Retrospective_QM_Review` within two business days. The emergency exception also requires a credible safety rationale.

Business-day rule: I count UTC business days Monday–Friday, excluding holidays, starting from the first full business day after approval. Example: approval on Tuesday 2026-08-11 gives Day 1 Wednesday 2026-08-12 and Day 2 Thursday 2026-08-13; the review deadline is Thursday 23:59 UTC. A review on Friday 2026-08-14 is late.

**RR5 — Identity evidence**  
A service account is not human. It supports conformance only when reliable session evidence maps it to an authorized human. Aliases belonging to the same human must be normalized before segregation checks.

**RR6 — Suspicion versus proof**  
Repeated pairs, reciprocal approvals, shared workstations, and concentrated handovers are indicators, not proof. They require supporting evidence before collusion can be concluded.

**Later database entry**  
A later QMS database entry cannot by itself prove an earlier delegation. But if signed evidence predates the effective interval, as with D-QM-01’s signed PDF, a later migration record does not invalidate the delegation.

## 6. Control Improvements

- **Delegation creation**: Require structured, pre-approved delegation records with mandatory fields; prohibit email-only delegations; explicitly block subdelegation unless separately authorized.
- **Effective-time recording**: Use immutable, timestamped delegation records; alert when a database record is created after the effective start; distinguish original signed evidence from later migration records.
- **Principal availability**: Integrate leave, sick leave, and on-call status with delegation validity; suspend delegation automatically if the principal is active or if absence cannot be established.
- **Alias management**: Maintain a single enterprise identity per human; prevent multiple active aliases for one person; run regular identity reconciliation.
- **Individual authentication**: Require MFA/FIDO for human accounts; for service accounts, require individual re-authentication; prohibit unattributed shared-terminal sessions for regulated actions.
- **Emergency review**: Enforce RR4 with workflow timers; require a structured safety-rationale field; assign emergency QM review scope explicitly, including cooling-system or other operational categories.
- **Pair rotation**: Monitor repeated drafter/approver pairs and reciprocal approvals; rotate reviewers where practical; send periodic risk reports to independent QA.
- **Network monitoring**: Use handover and working-together networks as continuous monitoring indicators, not as proof; trigger audits when threshold concentrations appear.
- **Evidential retention**: Retain badge, FIDO, CCTV, VPN, MFA, and system logs for at least the life of the change record; ensure service-account sessions can be attributed.
