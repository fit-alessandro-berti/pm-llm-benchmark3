# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

**Automated Intake (Nora)**  
- Core: A1/A2 at very high volume, low complexity, 24/7.  
- Peripheral: none. Nora never leaves intake.  
- Authority: system precheck only; humans still re-validate on non-trivial paths (fragment 2).  
- Substitutions: not applicable; she is the automation boundary.

**Front-Office Generalist (Amira, Ben)**  
- Core: Register/Validate (human), Standard Response, Close — high frequency, low–medium complexity.  
- Peripheral: Diagnose/Configure/Implement (low counts), occasional Pricing/Negotiate (Amira 18/42 vs Ben 5/25), rare Quality/KB.  
- Complexity: Amira is the stretch generalist (more medium/high/critical); Ben is high-volume routine.  
- Substitutions: they absorb temp rework (fragment 6), not commercial absence.

**Seasonal Temp Support (Kai, Lina)**  
- Core: same intake/response/close skeleton as front office but almost exclusively low complexity.  
- Peripheral: essentially none (near-zero configure/implement/quality/pricing).  
- Evidence against “same role as Amira/Ben”: reopen spike from incomplete Validate notes; median complexity ~0.18; temporary contracts.  
- Temporary load support  demonstrated entitlement to permanent generalist duties.

**Technical Resolution (Chen, Dina)**  
- Core: Diagnose, Configure, Implement.  
- Peripheral: light intake/response/close; KB and reopen; Chen’s A6 (24) is meaningful adjacency, Dina’s A6 (6) is marginal.  
- Complexity separates them operationally: Chen owns high/critical technical work; Dina similar activity mix at lower risk. Same activity profile  same risk exposure.

**Security Specialist (Erik)**  
- Core: A6 Security Review on high/critical cases.  
- Peripheral: sparse diagnose/configure/implement/quality — often on the same critical cases he secures, not a second job family.  
- Frequency is low; organizational importance is high. Week 7–8 shows Mateo filtering reduced Erik’s A6 load but created multi-day waits on two critical cases — classic rare-specialist bottleneck.

**Commercial Specialist (Fatima)**  
- Core: A7 Pricing Exception, A8 Negotiation.  
- Peripheral: Validate, occasional Approval participation, Close.  
- Authority: pricing certification and commercial home team.  
- Substitution by Giorgio raised median exception duration (3874 min): evidence of coverage without equivalent fluency.

**Senior Hybrid / Commercial Shadow (Giorgio)**  
- Core (formal): front-office entitlement/response/close at senior volume.  
- Core (shadow): A7/A8 on complex front-office-originated cases; some A9.  
- Peripheral: light technical and quality.  
- Tenure + dual certifications (entitlement, pricing) explain why he can shadow Fatima; duration degradation shows shadow  primary commercial role.

**Supervisory Authority (Hana)**  
- Core: A9 Approval; ownership of escalations and reopen governance.  
- Peripheral: light touches on validate/diagnose/pricing/negotiate — situational, not production work.  
- Week 5: Mateo performed 21 preliminary A9 but 13 were rechecked  Mateo can draft/coordinate, Hana retains decision authority.

**Quality & Knowledge Gate (Ivan, Julia)**  
- Core: A11, A14.  
- Peripheral: occasional configure/implement/close/reopen — typically gatekeeping on technical outputs, not primary resolution.  
- Late vs day shift is scheduling, not role split; profiles and complexity nearly match.

**Escalation Broker (Mateo)**  
- Core: cross-functional routing, reopen coordination, escalation packaging, security referral filtering, multi-party alignment (fragments 3 and 5).  
- Peripheral: almost every activity at moderate count — including preliminary A9 and some A6/A7.  
- High critical-case share (76) with only moderate similarity to Hana or Erik shows brokerage, not replacement of specialist or supervisor authority.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

**1) R07_Giorgio — commercial shadow (and senior front-office bridge)**  
- Benefits: resilience when Fatima is absent; faster path from complex front-office cases into pricing/negotiation without hard handoff; dual certifications make this intentional informal capacity.  
- Risks: longer pricing-exception cycle time under substitution; possible inconsistent commercial judgment; metrics may understate true commercial workload if only Fatima is staffed as “pricing.”  
- Not improper: it is a documented informal substitute pattern, but it should be recognized in training and SLA design rather than left tacit.

**2) R13_Mateo — cross-functional escalation broker**  
- Bridges technical, security, commercial, quality, and supervisory lanes; owns reopen orchestration and filters low-risk security referrals.  
- Benefits: reduces false escalations to Erik; single throat-to-choke for multi-party cases; absorbs coordination overhead others should not carry.  
- Risks: hidden single point of failure; preliminary approvals create false confidence until Hana rechecks; over-filtering can delay true critical security cases (>2 days).  
- Similarity scores (0.54 Hana, 0.39 Erik) confirm he is not “junior supervisor” or “deputy security.”

**3) R05_Erik — rare but critical specialist**  
- Low event count, highest complexity concentration.  
- Benefits: concentrated expertise on Enterprise Security / critical path; clear accountability for A6.  
- Risks: brittle capacity; load-smoothing via Mateo helps volume but can mask queue risk; naïve mining underweights him because frequency is small.  
- Staffing implication: protect capacity and define explicit intake criteria for security referral—do not “balance” his utilization against front-office agents.

**Additional bridging / boundary roles**

**4) R14_Nora — automation boundary**  
- Bridges customer channel and human work. Over-interpreting her A1/A2 volume as a human “role peer” would distort FTE planning. Humans still perform Validate on non-bot paths; bot precheck  full entitlement authority.

**5) R11_Kai / R12_Lina — temporary workforce buffer**  
- Bridge seasonal demand and permanent front office.  
- Benefit: cost-flexible capacity on low-complexity fragment 1.  
- Risk: incomplete entitlement notes  reopen  expensive rework by Amira/Ben/Mateo. Temps share activity labels with generalists but not quality outcomes or complexity license.

**6) R09_Ivan / R10_Julia — quality gatekeeping bridge**  
- Sit between Implement and Close; also maintain KB (organizational learning).  
- Benefit: decouple technical throughput from closure quality.  
- Risk: if treated as “another technical pair,” management may overload them with configure/implement and weaken the gate.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.algo.organizational_mining.roles import algorithm as roles_discovery
from pm4py.algo.organizational_mining.sna import algorithm as sna
from pm4py.objects.conversion.log import converter as log_converter

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1) Load & format
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
df = pd.read_csv("order_to_resolution_event_log.csv")

df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    resource_key="org:resource",
)

# Ensure analytic attributes retained
extra_cols = [
    "case_complexity", "customer_segment", "product_line",
    "temporary_substitution", "escalation_level"
]
for c in extra_cols:
    if c not in df.columns:
        raise ValueError(f"Missing expected column: {c}")

event_log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2) Organizational roles (activity–resource grouping)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# pm4py.discover_organizational_roles groups resources with similar activity profiles
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(event_log)
# roles: list of role objects / dicts with members and activity sets

print("=== Discover organizational roles ===")
for i, role in enumerate(roles):
    print(f"Role {i}: {role}")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3) Activity-based resource similarity
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
act_sim = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(event_log)
# matrix or dict keyed by resource pairs (cosine-like over activity frequencies)

sim_df = pd.DataFrame(act_sim) if not isinstance(act_sim, pd.DataFrame) else act_sim
print("=== Activity-based resource similarity (slice) ===")
print(sim_df.head())

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4) Handover-of-work & working-together networks
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(event_log)
together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(event_log)

# Optional: subcontracting network for broker/specialist patterns
try:
    subcontract = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(event_log)
except Exception:
    subcontract = None

# SNA metrics via algo API (illustrative)
handover_metric = sna.apply(
    event_log,
    variant=sna.Variants.HANDOVER_LOG
)
together_metric = sna.apply(
    event_log,
    variant=sna.Variants.WORKING_TOGETHER_LOG
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5) Join role-discovery with complexity & temporary_substitution
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Per-resource activity counts
res_act = (
    df.groupby(["org:resource", "concept:name"])
      .size()
      .unstack(fill_value=0)
)

# Per-resource complexity profile
complexity_profile = (
    df.drop_duplicates(subset=["case:concept:name", "org:resource"])
      .groupby("org:resource")
      .agg(
          median_complexity=("case_complexity", "median"),
          mean_complexity=("case_complexity", "mean"),
          n_cases=("case:concept:name", "nunique"),
      )
)

# Temporary substitution intensity: events flagged while covering others
if df["temporary_substitution"].dtype == bool:
    sub_flag = df["temporary_substitution"]
else:
    sub_flag = df["temporary_substitution"].astype(str).str.lower().isin(
        ["true", "1", "yes", "substitute"]
    )

substitution_load = (
    df.loc[sub_flag]
      .groupby("org:resource")
      .agg(
          substitution_events=("concept:name", "size"),
          substitution_activities=("concept:name", lambda s: s.value_counts().to_dict()),
      )
)

# Escalation exposure
escalation_profile = (
    df.groupby("org:resource")["escalation_level"]
      .value_counts()
      .unstack(fill_value=0)
)

# Build resource feature table (do NOT cluster on frequency alone)
resource_features = (
    res_act.join(complexity_profile, how="left")
           .join(substitution_load, how="left")
           .join(escalation_profile.add_prefix("esc_"), how="left")
           .fillna({"substitution_events": 0})
)

# Attach naïve role labels for comparison only
def role_label_map(roles_obj):
    """Map resource -> mined role id (structure depends on pm4py version)."""
    mapping = {}
    for idx, role in enumerate(roles_obj):
        # role may expose .members or dict keys — normalize defensively
        members = getattr(role, "members", None) or role.get("members", [])
        for m in members:
            mapping[m] = f"mined_role_{idx}"
    return mapping

resource_features["mined_role"] = resource_features.index.map(
    role_label_map(roles)
).fillna("unassigned")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6) Guardrails against over-interpreting frequency clusters
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6a Relative activity distribution (row-normalize) before any clustering
rel_act = res_act.div(res_act.sum(axis=1).replace(0, 1), axis=0)

# 6b Complexity-weighted activity preference
df_cw = df.copy()
df_cw["cw"] = df_cw["case_complexity"].fillna(df_cw["case_complexity"].median())
cw_act = (
    df_cw.groupby(["org:resource", "concept:name"])["cw"]
         .sum()
         .unstack(fill_value=0)
)
cw_rel = cw_act.div(cw_act.sum(axis=1).replace(0, 1), axis=0)

# 6c Compare frequency similarity vs complexity-weighted similarity
from sklearn.metrics.pairwise import cosine_similarity

freq_cos = pd.DataFrame(
    cosine_similarity(rel_act.fillna(0)),
    index=rel_act.index,
    columns=rel_act.index,
)
cw_cos = pd.DataFrame(
    cosine_similarity(cw_rel.fillna(0)),
    index=cw_rel.index,
    columns=cw_rel.index,
)
similarity_gap = (freq_cos - cw_cos).abs()

print("=== Pairs where frequency similarity overstates role sameness ===")
print(similarity_gap.stack().sort_values(ascending=False).head(15))

# 6d Substitution caveat: high substitute volume != permanent competence
# Flag resources whose A7/A8/A9 spikes only under temporary_substitution
special_acts = ["A7 Pricing Exception Review", "A8 Customer Negotiation",
                "A9 Supervisor Approval", "A6 Security Review"]
# (names may match log spelling — adjust to concept:name values)
special_acts = [c for c in df["concept:name"].unique()
                if any(k in c.lower() for k in ["pricing", "negotiat", "approval", "security"])]

for act in special_acts:
    base = df.loc[~sub_flag & (df["concept:name"] == act), "org:resource"].value_counts()
    sub = df.loc[sub_flag & (df["concept:name"] == act), "org:resource"].value_counts()
    comp = pd.DataFrame({"steady_state": base, "during_substitution": sub}).fillna(0)
    comp["substitution_share"] = comp["during_substitution"] / (
        comp["steady_state"] + comp["during_substitution"] + 1e-9
    )
    print(f"\n=== Authority caution for activity: {act} ===")
    print(comp.sort_values("substitution_share", ascending=False).head(10))

# 6e Optional: cluster_log on case features, not on resources alone
# Avoid claiming formal authority from activity-frequency clusters
try:
    clustered = pm4py.cluster_log(event_log)  # illustrative; version-specific
except Exception:
    clustered = None

# 6f Service / waiting times to contextualize shadow-role cost
df_st = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
    df,
    case_id_column="case:concept:name",
    timestamp_column="time:timestamp",
)
# Compare pricing exception service times: Fatima vs Giorgio under substitution
pricing_mask = df_st["concept:name"].str.contains("Pricing", case=False, na=False)
pricing_rt = (
    df_st.loc[pricing_mask]
         .groupby(["org:resource", sub_flag.loc[pricing_mask].rename("is_sub")])
         .size()
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 7) Interpretation helper (manual overlay — not automatic truth)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
manual_role_overlay = {
    "R14_Nora": "Automated Intake",
    "R01_Amira": "Front-Office Generalist",
    "R02_Ben": "Front-Office Generalist",
    "R11_Kai": "Temporary Intake Support",
    "R12_Lina": "Temporary Intake Support",
    "R03_Chen": "Technical Resolution (senior/critical bias)",
    "R04_Dina": "Technical Resolution (standard)",
    "R05_Erik": "Security Specialist",
    "R06_Fatima": "Commercial Specialist",
    "R07_Giorgio": "Senior FO + Commercial Shadow",
    "R08_Hana": "Supervisory Authority",
    "R09_Ivan": "Quality & Knowledge Gate",
    "R10_Julia": "Quality & Knowledge Gate",
    "R13_Mateo": "Cross-Functional Escalation Broker",
}
resource_features["interpreted_role"] = resource_features.index.map(manual_role_overlay)

# Final join for process owner review
review_table = resource_features[
    [c for c in resource_features.columns
     if c in ("mined_role", "interpreted_role", "median_complexity",
              "mean_complexity", "n_cases", "substitution_events")
     or c.startswith("esc_")]
].copy()
print(review_table.sort_values("median_complexity", ascending=False))

# CAUTION NOTE (encoded as assertions for analysts)
assert review_table["substitution_events"].sum() >= 0
# Do not promote resources to permanent commercial/supervisory roles
# solely because mined_role or freq_cos clusters them with Fatima/Hana.
```

**How this code avoids naïve mistakes**  
- Uses `discover_organizational_roles` and similarity as **inputs**, not final org design.  
- Joins **case_complexity**, **escalation_level**, and **temporary_substitution**.  
- Contrasts frequency cosine vs complexity-weighted cosine (`similarity_gap`).  
- Separates steady-state vs substitution-time performance of sensitive activities (A6/A7/A8/A9).  
- Keeps a **manual interpreted_role** overlay for shadow/broker distinctions algorithms under-specify.

# 6. Operational interpretation

**Staffing**  
- Size Automated Intake and Temporary buffers for low-complexity fragment-1 volume; do not count Nora’s events as human FTE.  
- Maintain a permanent Front-Office tier (Amira/Ben) distinct from temps; staff temps only with hardened Validate checklists after the entitlement-note reopen failures.  
- Technical capacity should be tiered: Chen-level critical headroom is not interchangeable with Dina-level standard throughput on a 1:1 activity basis.  
- Commercial and Security need explicit on-call/backup rosters; Giorgio is a partial commercial backup, Mateo is a filter/router—not a full Erik substitute.  
- Quality gate (Ivan/Julia) should be staffed to implementation volume, not treated as slack technical labor.

**Training**  
- Cross-train selected senior generalists (Amira path  Giorgio path) on pricing **with** measured mentoring, targeting closure of the 3874 minute exception gap—not informal sink-or-swim substitution.  
- Train Mateo-role holders on security triage criteria to reduce both false escalations and critical waits.  
- Temp onboarding must emphasize Validate Entitlement documentation; monitor reopen rates as a training KPI.  
- Preserve Erik’s specialist depth; pair Chen for security-adjacent technical work without diluting A6 ownership.

**Risk control**  
- Binding Supervisor Approval stays with Hana (or formal delegates); Mateo’s preliminary A9 must remain visibly non-final in the system (`escalation_level` / recheck flags).  
- Security referral SLAs: track age of critical cases waiting on Erik; Mateo filtering needs audit samples.  
- Shadow commercial work should be sampled for pricing consistency and compliance.  
- Automation boundary: audit Nora prechecks vs human Validate disagreements.

**Resilience**  
- Map single points of failure: Erik (critical security), Fatima (pricing), Hana (authority), Mateo (coordination).  
- Pre-authorize shadow playbooks (Giorgio for A7/A8; limited Mateo packaging for A9) with expected service-time degradation baked into SLAs.  
- Keep a named backup broker for Mateo’s coordination patterns during high Enterprise Security load.  
- Use working-together and handover networks to verify that backups actually maintain healthy handoff paths—not only similar activity histograms.

**Cautions about changing staffing from mined roles only**  
1. **Similar activity profiles  equal authority or risk license.** ChenDina (0.94) and AmiraBen (0.93) hide material complexity and critical-case differences; collapsing them for “efficiency” can overload juniors and under-protect critical paths. Frequency clusters also pull Giorgio toward Fatima or Amira and erase the hybrid shadow design.  
2. **Temporary substitution is evidence of emergency coverage, not permanent competence.** Giorgio’s A7/A8 surge and Mateo’s A9 surge both came with quality/latency costs (longer exceptions; rechecked approvals). Promoting substitutes to full-time role peers—or cutting primary specialists because “someone else already does the activity”—confuses resilience theater with capacity. Likewise, Kai/Lina matching front-office activity labels while driving reopens shows that mined “same role” can increase downstream cost if used as a hiring or rightsizing blueprint without outcome metrics.
