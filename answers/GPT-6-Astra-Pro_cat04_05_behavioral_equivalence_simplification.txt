# 1. Equivalence Notions

## Observation boundary

The comparison concerns **complete, finite executions of one case**, from `Open Case` through `Close Case`. It does not observe timestamps, activity durations, resources, data values, or infinite executions. Concurrency is observed through its permitted interleavings, not through execution intervals or causal identities.

Use the following abbreviations:

$$
\begin{aligned}
o&=\text{Open Case}, & a&=\text{Analyze Revision},\\
c&=\text{Check Revision}, & p&=\text{Approve Revision},\\
r&=\text{Rework Case}, & z&=\text{Close Case}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Write \(\#_x(w)\) for the number of occurrences of label \(x\) in trace \(w\).

**Occurrence policy.** A visible trace is an ordered word over activity labels. Repeated labels remain separate occurrences at separate positions: \(a,r,a\) contains two analyses. Process-tree node identities and Petri-net transition identities are not observed. Under P1, revision membership is inferred from the occurrence’s position between rework boundaries; it is not established using a revision identifier.

## Visible trace equivalence after hiding `tau`

Let

$$
L(T)=
\{\operatorname{erase}_{\tau}(\sigma):
\sigma\text{ is a complete finite execution of }T\}.
$$

For an accepting Petri net, completion means reaching **exactly the final marking**, not merely placing a token in its final place.

Two models are visibly trace-equivalent precisely when

$$
T_1\equiv_{\mathrm{vis}}T_2
\iff L(T_1)=L(T_2).
$$

Only silent behavior is erased. Approval events, repeated checks, and all visible ordering remain observable. This does not assert bisimulation or partial-order equivalence.

## Bounded visible-language equivalence under P3

Define

$$
L_{12,3}(T)=
\{w\in L(T): |w|\leq 12\land \#_a(w)\leq 3\}.
$$

The length includes **all visible events**, including opening, closing, rework, and approval. Silent transitions consume no visible-event budget.

Then

$$
T_1\equiv_{\mathrm{P3}}T_2
\iff L_{12,3}(T_1)=L_{12,3}(T_2).
$$

An exhaustively established difference disproves unbounded visible equivalence. Equality within these bounds would not establish unbounded equality.

## Business-relevant equivalence under P1

Split each complete visible trace at its rework events:

$$
w=o\,u_1\,r\,u_2\,r\cdots r\,u_n\,z.
$$

The **revision window** \(u_i\) is the visible activity sequence for attempt \(i\). Define the operational observation as

$$
\alpha_1(w)=(o;u_1,\ldots,u_n;z),
$$

retaining the ordered occurrences inside each window, including every approval.

The compliance condition is that each window contains one analysis and its own check before that window ends:

$$
\forall i:\quad \#_a(u_i)=1
\quad\land\quad \#_c(u_i)\geq 1.
$$

Distinct windows cannot reuse one check occurrence. Business-relevant equivalence means equality of the sets of operational observations:

$$
T_1\equiv_{\mathrm{P1}}T_2
\iff \alpha_1(L(T_1))=\alpha_1(L(T_2)).
$$

This is stronger than merely asking whether both models satisfy one compliance predicate. Approval histories remain relevant.

**Interpretation of “before.”** The stated rule requires checking before the next rework or closure; it does not explicitly require checking *after analysis*. I therefore allow \(ca\) within a revision window. A stronger “analyze, then check” rule would require a different model. Also, window membership does not prove that a check inspected the correct revision data.

## Abstracted dashboard equivalence under P2

Let \(b(w)\) indicate that the trace starts with \(o\) and ends with \(z\). Define

$$
\alpha_2(w)=
\left(
b(w),\
\#_a(w),\
[\#_c(w)>0],\
[\#_r(w)>0]
\right).
$$

This observation hides approvals and silent behavior, collapses check occurrences to a Boolean, and discards internal interleaving.

Then

$$
T_1\equiv_{\mathrm{P2}}T_2
\iff
\{\alpha_2(w):w\in L(T_1)\}
=
\{\alpha_2(w):w\in L(T_2)\}.
$$

This compares **possible dashboard records**, not their probabilities or frequencies.

# 2. Structural Comparison

For either model, let \(n\geq1\) be the number of analysis attempts. Both have exactly \(n-1\) rework events.

| Feature                                        | Model A                                                                                                      | Model B                                                                                                                                                        | Semantic consequence                                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Loop scope                                     | Analysis, check, and optional approval are inside the loop body.                                             | Analysis and optional approval are inside the loop; one check is outside it.                                                                                   | A repeats checking with every attempt; B does not.                                      |
| Minimum and maximum Check Revision occurrences | Minimum 1; no finite maximum across complete traces. Exactly \(n\) checks for \(n\) attempts.                | Minimum 1 and maximum 1, regardless of \(n\).                                                                                                                  | With two or more attempts, the check multiplicities differ.                             |
| Possible placement of Check Revision           | Within its revision window, before or after analysis, but before that window’s approval, rework, or closure. | At any insertion position among the revision-loop events: before the first analysis, between attempts, between analysis and approval, or after the whole loop. | B can rework an unchecked attempt and can approve before its sole check.                |
| Optional approval occurrences                  | Between 0 and \(n\), at most one per attempt; follows both analysis and check for that attempt.              | Between 0 and \(n\), at most one per attempt; follows analysis but need not follow checking.                                                                   | Equal approval-count ranges do not imply equal approval placement.                      |
| Concurrency                                    | Analysis and check are concurrent **within each iteration**.                                                 | One check is concurrent with the **entire loop**.                                                                                                              | The synchronization boundary moves from each revision to the whole case.                |
| Join before Close Case                         | Each iteration’s analysis/check join is completed before its approval decision; closure follows loop exit.   | The outer parallel join waits for both the sole check and the revision loop to finish.                                                                         | Both prevent closing before checking, but only A enforces checking before every rework. |

These differences can be expressed as exact visible languages.

For A, a single revision window belongs to

$$
D=\{ac,\ ca,\ acp,\ cap\}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{L(A)=o\,D\,(rD)^*\,z.}
$$

For B, the revision-loop body belongs to

$$
E=\{a,\ ap\}.
$$

Using \(\shuffle\) for order-preserving shuffle,

$$
\boxed{
L(B)=
o\left(\{c\}\shuffle\left(E(rE)^*\right)\right)z.
}
$$

Because \(c\) does not occur in \(E(rE)^*\), this shuffle simply inserts exactly one check at any position in a loop word.

# 3. Verdicts

| View                        | Verdict                                      | Confidence                                                | Decisive reason                                                                                  | Caveat                                                                                            |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Full visible trace language | **not equivalent**                           | Certain under the supplied semantics                      | A repeats checks; B permits approval before its sole check. Neither language contains the other. | Transition identities and silent execution histories are hidden.                                  |
| P1: operational compliance  | **not equivalent**                           | Certain under the revision-window interpretation          | A checks every attempt; every B execution with \(n>1\) necessarily has an unchecked attempt.     | A permits checking before analysis within a window and does not verify revision-data identity.    |
| P2: executive dashboard     | **equivalent only under stated abstraction** | Certain; constructive unbounded argument                  | Both produce exactly the same dashboard records for every \(n\geq1\).                            | Check multiplicity, approval histories, and internal ordering are deliberately discarded.         |
| P3: bounded testing         | **not equivalent**                           | Certain; explicit witnesses and exact language derivation | The bounded languages have 76 and 85 traces, with only 4 in common.                              | The counts below were independently enumerated; the PM4Py implementation itself was not run here. |

**P1 reasoning.** A has one check in every revision window. B has only one check for the entire case. For \(n>1\), that single occurrence cannot supply a distinct check to each of the \(n\) windows. This difference survives even an abstraction that ignores analysis/check order within each window.

**P2 proof.** Both models produce exactly

$$
\boxed{
\alpha_2(L(A))=\alpha_2(L(B))
=
\{(\mathrm{true},n,\mathrm{true},[n>1]):n\geq1\}.
}
$$

Every complete execution produces such a record because both have \(n\) analyses, \(n-1\) reworks, at least one check, and the required boundaries. Conversely, either model can execute any finite number \(n\geq1\) of attempts, so every record in this set is achievable.

# 4. Counterexample Traces

All four witnesses are complete and satisfy the P3 bounds.

| Trace ID | Trace                              | Model A      | Model B      | First decisive semantic difference                                                                           | Relevant view             |
| -------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------ | ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------- |
| T1       | \(\langle o,a,c,r,a,c,z\rangle\)   | Admitted     | Not admitted | The second \(c\), at position 6, is impossible in B.                                                         | P1, P3                    |
| T2       | \(\langle o,c,a,p,r,a,c,z\rangle\) | Admitted     | Not admitted | The second \(c\), at position 7, is impossible in B. The earlier approval is legal in both up to that point. | P1, P3                    |
| T3       | \(\langle o,a,p,c,z\rangle\)       | Not admitted | Admitted     | At position 3, A cannot approve because its analysis/check join has not completed.                           | P1 approval placement, P3 |
| T4       | \(\langle o,a,r,a,c,z\rangle\)     | Not admitted | Admitted     | At position 3, A cannot rework the first attempt without its check. B can.                                   | P1 compliance, P3         |

T1 and T4 have identical P2 observations:

$$
\alpha_2(T1)=\alpha_2(T4)
=(\mathrm{true},2,\mathrm{true},\mathrm{true}).
$$

Their operational difference is therefore intentionally invisible to P2.

## Neither inclusion holds; the intersection is exactly known

T1 proves \(L(A)\nsubseteq L(B)\), and T3 proves \(L(B)\nsubseteq L(A)\).

Moreover, any common trace must have one analysis: A has \(\#c=\#a\), whereas B has \(\#c=1\). For one analysis, all four A windows are possible in B. Thus the **unbounded** intersection is exactly

$$
\boxed{
L(A)\cap L(B)
=
\{oacz,\ ocaz,\ oacpz,\ ocapz\}.
}
$$

## Effect of optional approval and hidden `tau`

Selecting `tau` in `X('Approve Revision', tau)` means that no visible approval is recorded. It does not skip analysis, checking, or an entire loop iteration.

Consequently, missing approvals in T1 and T4 are legal; they are not the reason those traces distinguish the models. T3 shows why visible approval placement matters even with only one attempt.

Hiding approvals in addition to `tau` would remove the T3 distinction, but would **not** establish ordinary trace equivalence: the two-attempt check-count distinction remains. P2 equates the models only through its additional Boolean and ordering abstractions.

# 5. Bounded Comparison Code

The following script performs exhaustive bounded exploration, not sampling. It uses the documented parser and accepting-net conversion API. The conversion returns a net together with initial and final markings. ([PM4Py Documentation][1])

The consulted PM4Py 2.7.22 documentation specifies that `check_soundness` returns a `(Boolean, diagnostics)` tuple. The script extracts the Boolean rather than testing the tuple’s truth value, and uses `get_enabled_transitions` for exploration. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][2])

**Execution status:** PM4Py was unavailable in this environment, and installation failed. The script was syntax-checked, but its Petri-net operations were not executed here. The trace counts were separately verified by standard-library enumeration of the exact languages in Section 2.

```python
from __future__ import annotations

from collections import Counter, deque
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.petri_net import semantics
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import Marking, PetriNet

OPEN = "Open Case"
ANALYZE = "Analyze Revision"
CHECK = "Check Revision"
APPROVE = "Approve Revision"
REWORK = "Rework Case"
CLOSE = "Close Case"

MODEL_A = (
    "->('Open Case',*(->(+('Analyze Revision','Check Revision'),"
    "X('Approve Revision',tau)),'Rework Case'),'Close Case')"
)

MODEL_B = (
    "->('Open Case',+('Check Revision',*(->('Analyze Revision',"
    "X('Approve Revision',tau)),'Rework Case')),'Close Case')"
)

VisibleTrace = tuple[str, ...]


def bounded_language(
    net: PetriNet,
    im: Marking,
    fm: Marking,
    max_visible: int = 12,
    max_analyses: int = 3,
) -> set[VisibleTrace]:
    """Exhaustive bounded language for these finite-state ordinary nets.

    Acceptance requires exact final-marking equality.
    No sampling, silent-step limit, firing limit, or state cutoff is used.
    """
    if max_visible < 0 or max_analyses < 0:
        raise ValueError("Bounds must be nonnegative.")

    # Use place objects, not possibly non-unique place names.
    places = tuple(net.places)

    def marking_key(marking: Marking) -> tuple[int, ...]:
        # Full token vector: omitted and explicit zero entries are equal.
        return tuple(marking.get(place, 0) for place in places)

    final_key = marking_key(fm)
    initial = Marking(im)

    queue = deque([(initial, (), 0)])
    seen = {(marking_key(initial), ())}
    complete: set[VisibleTrace] = set()

    while queue:
        marking, word, analyses = queue.popleft()

        if marking_key(marking) == final_key:
            complete.add(word)

        # Silent transitions remain explorable at the visible-length limit.
        for transition in pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, marking):
            label = transition.label
            next_word = word
            next_analyses = analyses

            if label is not None:
                # Only None is invisible. A literal label "tau" is visible.
                next_word = word + (label,)
                next_analyses += int(label == ANALYZE)

                if (
                    len(next_word) > max_visible
                    or next_analyses > max_analyses
                ):
                    continue

            successor = semantics.execute(transition, net, marking)
            if successor is None:
                raise RuntimeError("An enabled transition failed to fire.")

            # Marking-only or (marking, length)-only deduplication is wrong.
            # The analysis count is already determined by next_word.
            state_key = (marking_key(successor), next_word)

            if state_key not in seen:
                seen.add(state_key)
                queue.append(
                    (successor, next_word, next_analyses)
                )

    return complete


def print_traces(title: str, words: set[VisibleTrace]) -> None:
    print(f"\n{title}: {len(words)}")
    for word in sorted(words, key=lambda w: (len(w), w)):
        print("  <" + " | ".join(word) + ">")


print("PM4Py version:", pm4py.__version__)

trees = {
    "A": pm4py.parse_process_tree(MODEL_A),
    "B": pm4py.parse_process_tree(MODEL_B),
}

accepting_nets = {}
languages = {}

for name, tree in trees.items():
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)
    accepting_nets[name] = (net, im, fm)

    report = pm4py.check_soundness(
        net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True
    )
    # Documented API returns a tuple; also handle a Boolean return.
    sound = bool(report[0]) if isinstance(report, tuple) else bool(report)

    print(f"{name}: sound={sound}")
    if not sound:
        raise RuntimeError(
            f"Model {name} failed soundness: {report!r}"
        )

    languages[name] = bounded_language(net, im, fm)

    print(name, "complete bounded traces:", len(languages[name]))
    print(
        name,
        "by analysis count:",
        dict(sorted(Counter(
            word.count(ANALYZE) for word in languages[name]
        ).items())),
    )

L_A, L_B = languages["A"], languages["B"]

print("EXHAUSTIVE within P3; not random/heuristic playout.")
print("P3 equivalent:", L_A == L_B)
print("Intersection:", len(L_A & L_B))

print_traces("A only", L_A - L_B)
print_traces("B only", L_B - L_A)

# Independent expectations derived from the tree-language equations.
assert (len(L_A), len(L_B), len(L_A & L_B)) == (76, 85, 4)
assert (len(L_A - L_B), len(L_B - L_A)) == (72, 81)
```

## Why the exploration is exhaustive within P3

The search state contains both the **marking and the visible prefix**. Different prefixes reaching the same marking must remain distinct because they generate different complete words. Conversely, two executions reaching the same marking with the same visible prefix have identical possible continuations in these ordinary Petri nets, so merging them is safe.

Silent transitions do not increase either bound. Silent cycles returning to the same marking and prefix are handled by `seen`, without an arbitrary silent-step cutoff. Acceptance uses complete token-vector equality, so residual tokens cannot be mistaken for successful completion.

These process-tree-derived nets have finite reachable marking spaces. Combining finitely many markings with finitely many bounded visible prefixes makes the exploration finite. That termination argument would not apply automatically to an arbitrary net with unbounded silent token generation.

Structurally, both original trees have an option to complete from every reachable execution state: finish active branches, choose loop exit, and close. Every branch can participate in a completion, and the joins prevent residual parallel work at closure. This supports soundness, but the script separately checks the converted nets. **Soundness is not P1 compliance and does not establish equivalence.**

## Derivation of the bounded counts

For \(n\) analyses and \(k\) approvals, A has

$$
|w|=3n+1+k,
\qquad
N_A(n,k)=2^n\binom nk.
$$

There are two analysis/check orders per attempt and \(\binom nk\) choices of which attempts have approval.

For B,

$$
|w|=2n+2+k,
\qquad
N_B(n,k)=(2n+k)\binom nk.
$$

Before inserting the sole check, its loop word has \(2n-1+k\) events, giving \(2n+k\) distinct insertion positions.

Applying both P3 bounds yields:

| Analyze Revision occurrences | Model A traces | Model B traces |
| ---------------------------: | -------------: | -------------: |
|                            1 |              4 |              5 |
|                            2 |             16 |             20 |
|                            3 |             56 |             60 |
|                    **Total** |         **76** |         **85** |

For A with \(n=3\), the eight traces with three approvals have length 13 and are excluded. All B traces with \(n\leq3\) fit the length bound.

The four intersection traces give

$$
\boxed{
|L_{12,3}(A)\setminus L_{12,3}(B)|=72,\qquad
|L_{12,3}(B)\setminus L_{12,3}(A)|=81.
}
$$

# 6. Stochastic and Footprint Comparison

## Footprints

`pm4py.discover_footprints(...)` supports process trees and Petri-net inputs and returns a high-level behavioral summary. It is useful for inspecting sequence and parallel relations. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][3])

For these models, a decisive pairwise relation concerns checking and approval: A permits adjacent \(cp\), but not \(pc\); B permits both. A footprint retaining those relations should expose this difference.

The following extends Section 5:

```python
fp_A = pm4py.discover_footprints(trees["A"])
fp_B = pm4py.discover_footprints(trees["B"])

print("Full returned footprints equal:", fp_A == fp_B)

for relation in ("sequence", "parallel"):
    rel_A = fp_A.get(relation, set())
    rel_B = fp_B.get(relation, set())
    print(relation, "A only:", sorted(rel_A - rel_B))
    print(relation, "B only:", sorted(rel_B - rel_A))

# Alternatively, inspect accepting-net footprints:
# fp_A_net = pm4py.discover_footprints(*accepting_nets["A"])
# fp_B_net = pm4py.discover_footprints(*accepting_nets["B"])
```

**Equal footprints would not generally prove trace equivalence.** Pairwise summaries omit global multiplicity and dependency constraints. For example,

$$
\{ab,aba\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\{ab,aba,ababa\}
$$

have identical activity sets, directly-follows pairs, start/end sets, and minimum trace length, but different languages.

## Stochastic languages

Neither supplied tree specifies business probabilities. In particular, it does not specify loop-continuation probabilities, approval probabilities, or a scheduling distribution for parallel behavior.

`pm4py.get_stochastic_language(log)` derives trace frequencies from a log. When passed a model, the consulted implementation calls `pm4py.play_out(...)` and then derives frequencies from that playout; it is not an exact enumeration of the unbounded model language. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][4])

Ordinary random playout can miss rare legal traces. A trace appearing in one sample but not another is therefore not, by itself, a model-language counterexample: non-membership in the other model still requires verification. Model playout can also use a supplied stochastic map, but such weights must be justified or explicitly declared as assumptions. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][5])

A reproducible diagnostic here is to assign **uniform probability to each distinct complete P3 trace**. This is an artificial distribution over bounded words—not a claim about business probabilities or uniform transition choices:

```python
from pm4py.objects.log.obj import EventLog, Trace, Event


def uniform_bounded_test_language(words):
    """One synthetic case per distinct word: uniform finite trace weights."""
    if not words:
        raise ValueError("Cannot normalize an empty language.")

    log = EventLog()

    for case_id, word in enumerate(sorted(words)):
        trace = Trace(attributes={"concept:name": str(case_id)})
        for activity in word:
            trace.append(Event({"concept:name": activity}))
        log.append(trace)

    return pm4py.get_stochastic_language(log)


prob_A = uniform_bounded_test_language(L_A)
prob_B = uniform_bounded_test_language(L_B)

assert abs(sum(prob_A.values()) - 1.0) < 1e-9
assert abs(sum(prob_B.values()) - 1.0) < 1e-9

print(
    "EMD under uniform bounded-trace test weights:",
    pm4py.compute_emd(prob_A, prob_B),
)
```

Under these declared test weights, the probability of three analyses is \(56/76\) for A and \(60/85\) for B. Thus their **possible dashboard records** are equal even though these particular induced dashboard distributions differ.

## Earth Mover’s Distance

`pm4py.compute_emd(...)` compares two stochastic languages. Its value provides distributional evidence under the chosen trace probabilities and ground distance, not an operational-equivalence verdict. No numeric EMD result is asserted here. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][2])

A low distance can coexist with a compliance violation: a missing check might require only a small edit, or a prohibited behavior might receive little probability.

The qualification concerning zero is important: **exact zero EMD between exact full-support distributions, using a distance that distinguishes different words, would imply equal supports**. For the artificial distributions above, that would concern only the bounded languages. Zero obtained from finite samples or from already-abstracted observations would not establish unbounded visible-language equivalence.

# 7. Complexity Comparison

Count each operator and each leaf occurrence as one process-tree node. Define depth as the maximum number of **edges** from the root to a leaf, so the root has depth 0.

Define duplicate-label excess as

$$
\sum_{\ell}\max(\operatorname{leafcount}(\ell)-1,0),
$$

over visible labels. Dynamic repetitions caused by a loop are not duplicate tree leaves.

| Metric                         | Model A | Model B |
| ------------------------------ | ------: | ------: |
| Operator nodes                 |       5 |       5 |
| Visible leaves                 |       6 |       6 |
| Silent leaves                  |       1 |       1 |
| **Total process-tree nodes**   |  **12** |  **12** |
| **Depth in edges**             |   **4** |   **5** |
| Duplicate visible-label excess |       0 |       0 |

Both have two sequence operators, one parallel operator, one loop, and one exclusive choice. Their six visible leaves and one silent leaf give \(5+6+1=12\) nodes.

A’s deepest paths are of the form sequence → loop → sequence → choice/parallel → leaf. B adds the outer parallel level above the loop on its deepest approval path.

The following structural counter traverses occurrences rather than deduplicating equal-looking nodes:

```python
from collections import Counter


def tree_metrics(tree):
    stack = [(tree, 0)]
    nodes = operators = silent = depth = 0
    visible_labels = []

    while stack:
        node, current_depth = stack.pop()
        nodes += 1
        depth = max(depth, current_depth)

        if node.operator is not None:
            operators += 1
            stack.extend(
                (child, current_depth + 1)
                for child in node.children
            )
        elif node.label is None:
            silent += 1
        else:
            visible_labels.append(node.label)

    counts = Counter(visible_labels)

    return {
        "nodes": nodes,
        "operators": operators,
        "depth_edges": depth,
        "visible_leaves": len(visible_labels),
        "silent_leaves": silent,
        "duplicate_label_excess": sum(
            count - 1 for count in counts.values()
        ),
    }


def petri_metrics(accepting_net):
    net, im, fm = accepting_net
    return {
        "places": len(net.places),
        "transitions": len(net.transitions),
        "silent_transitions": sum(
            transition.label is None
            for transition in net.transitions
        ),
        "arcs": len(net.arcs),
        "arc_degree_simplicity": pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(
            net, im, fm, variant="arc_degree"
        ),
    }


for name in ("A", "B"):
    print(name, "tree:", tree_metrics(trees[name]))
    print(name, "net:", petri_metrics(accepting_nets[name]))
```

For the documented arc-degree variant,

$$
\operatorname{simplicity}(N)
=
\frac{1}{1+\max(\bar d-2,0)},
\qquad
\bar d=\frac{2|F|}{|P|+|T|}.
$$

The source computes average incident-arc degree over places and transitions, with default baseline 2. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][6])

**No exact converted-net simplicity scores are claimed without executing the conversion.** The code reports them for the installed converter/version. Equal tree sizes do not determine equal net scores, and fewer tree nodes do not guarantee a strictly higher arc-degree score.

# 8. Simplified Model

## Target: P2 dashboard semantics

I choose P2 as the target for a **shared simplified representation** because it is the observation under which both originals are equivalent. This is not an operational workflow replacement.

Use one canonical ordering: perform the analysis/rework loop, record one check, then close.

```text
->(
  'Open Case',
  *('Analyze Revision', 'Rework Case'),
  'Check Revision',
  'Close Case'
)
```

Self-contained PM4Py construction:

```python
import pm4py

simplified_model = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "->('Open Case',*('Analyze Revision','Rework Case'),"
    "'Check Revision','Close Case')"
)
```

Call this model \(S\). Its language is

$$
L(S)=
\{o\,a(ra)^{n-1}c\,z:n\geq1\}.
$$

## Preservation proof

For any \(w\in L(A)\cup L(B)\), define its canonical representative

$$
N(w)=o\,a(ra)^{\#_a(w)-1}c\,z.
$$

Then \(N(w)\in L(S)\), and

$$
\alpha_2(N(w))=\alpha_2(w).
$$

Conversely, for each \(n\geq1\), the S trace

$$
o\,a(ra)^{n-1}c\,z
$$

is itself admitted by B. A admits

$$
o\,ac(rac)^{n-1}z,
$$

which has the same dashboard record. Therefore,

$$
\boxed{
\alpha_2(L(S))
=
\alpha_2(L(A))
=
\alpha_2(L(B)).
}
$$

This is an unbounded abstraction-preservation proof, not an inference from the regression tests.

Concretely,

$$
L(S)\subsetneq L(B),
$$

but \(L(S)\nsubseteq L(A)\): S permits unchecked earlier attempts when rework occurs.

## Structural simplification

| Metric                 | Model A | Model B | Simplified S |
| ---------------------- | ------: | ------: | -----------: |
| Total tree nodes       |      12 |      12 |        **7** |
| Operator nodes         |       5 |       5 |        **2** |
| Depth in edges         |       4 |       5 |        **2** |
| Visible leaves         |       6 |       6 |        **5** |
| Silent leaves          |       1 |       1 |        **0** |
| Duplicate-label excess |       0 |       0 |            0 |

S has one sequence operator, one loop operator, and five visible leaves: \(2+5=7\) nodes. It is therefore smaller and shallower than **both** originals.

## Regression and negative traces

These tests concern **concrete membership in S**.

| Test | Trace                              | Expected result | Reason                                                |
| ---- | ---------------------------------- | --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| R1   | \(\langle o,a,c,z\rangle\)         | Accept          | One attempt, no rework, one check.                    |
| R2   | \(\langle o,a,r,a,c,z\rangle\)     | Accept          | Two attempts and one rework.                          |
| R3   | \(\langle o,a,r,a,r,a,c,z\rangle\) | Accept          | Three attempts and two reworks.                       |
| N1   | \(\langle o,a,z\rangle\)           | Reject          | Missing mandatory check.                              |
| N2   | \(\langle o,c,z\rangle\)           | Reject          | Missing mandatory analysis.                           |
| N3   | \(\langle o,a,a,c,z\rangle\)       | Reject          | Two analyses without the required intervening rework. |

The earlier A trace T2, containing two checks and an approval, maps to R2. B trace T3, with approval before checking, maps to R1. Their original ordering is **not preserved**; their P2 records are.

The following extends Section 5 after constructing `simplified_model`:

```python
def dashboard_signature(word):
    boundary_ok = (
        bool(word)
        and word[0] == OPEN
        and word[-1] == CLOSE
    )
    return (
        boundary_ok,
        word.count(ANALYZE),
        CHECK in word,
        REWORK in word,
    )


L_S = bounded_language(
    *pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(simplified_model)
)

positive = {
    (OPEN, ANALYZE, CHECK, CLOSE),
    (OPEN, ANALYZE, REWORK, ANALYZE, CHECK, CLOSE),
    (
        OPEN, ANALYZE, REWORK, ANALYZE,
        REWORK, ANALYZE, CHECK, CLOSE,
    ),
}

negative = {
    (OPEN, ANALYZE, CLOSE),
    (OPEN, CHECK, CLOSE),
    (OPEN, ANALYZE, ANALYZE, CHECK, CLOSE),
}

assert positive <= L_S
assert negative.isdisjoint(L_S)
assert len(L_S) == 3

assert (
    {dashboard_signature(w) for w in L_A}
    == {dashboard_signature(w) for w in L_B}
    == {dashboard_signature(w) for w in L_S}
)

# These must remain false: preservation is dashboard-only.
assert L_S != L_A
assert L_S != L_B
```

# 9. Final Recommendation

**For P1, use Model A.** It ensures a distinct check within every revision window, before rework or closure, and retains optional approval histories. Model B is unsuitable when revisions require their own checks. Neither model establishes data-level revision identity, and neither enforces an additional rule that checking must occur after analysis.

**For P2, either original is suitable as a source of dashboard observations.** Their possible dashboard records are exactly equal under \(\alpha_2\). Use S as a smaller, canonical dashboard representation, while making clear that its ordering is a normalization rather than a faithful operational timeline.

**For P3, keep the originals distinct.** They disagree well within the specified bounds, and S preserves neither bounded visible language. When testing an implementation intended to satisfy P1, A—not B or S—should be its behavioral reference.

A single model cannot be P3-equivalent to **both** originals: transitivity would then imply that A and B are P3-equivalent, contradicting the explicit witnesses.

**Bounded-comparison warning:** exhaustive bounded equality establishes equality only within the bounds; random playout does not even establish that. In this case, however, the bounded counterexamples already suffice to disprove unbounded visible trace equivalence.

**Retain A for operational control; use S only for the explicitly abstracted P2 dashboard view.**
