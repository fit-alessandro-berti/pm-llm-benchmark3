## 1. Decision rule

A deployable portfolio-level early-warning policy must separate **observational breach risk** $P(Y(0)=1 \mid X)$ from **causal intervention value** $\Delta r_i = P(Y(0)=1 \mid X) - P(Y(1)=1 \mid X)$, while pricing **specialist capacity opportunity cost**, **diagnosis-queue spillover**, and **option value of waiting**. At each 30-minute decision epoch $t$, every alerting case $i$ passes through four sequential gates:

### Gate 1: Stage, Lead-Time, History, and Telemetry Eligibility (`Eligible_now`)
A case $i$ is eligible for a 45-minute Senior Diagnostic Swarm at epoch $t$ if and only if all four operational conditions hold:
1. **Process-Stage Gate**: $\text{Stage}_i \in \{\text{Incident Opened}, \text{Triage}, \text{Diagnosis Started}\}$. Once $\text{Stage}_i \ge \text{Diagnosis Complete}$, the swarm's causal mechanisms (accelerating root-cause isolation, surfacing missing diagnostic data, or redirecting diagnostic paths) no longer operate ($\Delta r_i \approx 0.00\text{–}0.03$; e.g., `I-03` is strictly ineligible).
2. **Feasible Execution Lead-Time**: $\text{TimeToSLA}_i \ge 0.75\text{ h}$ (the 45-minute swarm duration itself), with highest urgency when $\text{TimeToSLA}_i \in [1.25\text{ h}, 3.5\text{ h}]$ so the swarm completes before the case exits `Diagnosis Started` or breaches SLA.
3. **Prior-Swarm Constraint**: $\text{PriorSwarms}_i = 0$, OR ($\text{PriorSwarms}_i = 1$ AND a verified, materially new structural event has invalidated the first swarm's diagnostic trajectory).
4. **Data-Integrity Gate (Telemetry Hold)**: If an alert jump ($\Delta p_i = p_{\text{now}, i} - p_{\text{prev}, i} \ge 0.15$) coincides with a missing or imputed high-leverage live feature (such as `I-06`'s imputed live queue field), the case is placed on a **15-minute Telemetry Verification Hold** (`Wait — Verify Data`) rather than immediately consuming a specialist slot on a potential imputation artifact.

### Gate 2: Segment Calibration Adjustment and Uncertainty Robustification
Because raw predictions $p_{\text{now}, i}$ are observational and exhibit known segment miscalibration, we compute a **calibrated breach probability** $\hat{p}_{\text{cal}, i}$ and a **robust causal risk reduction** $\Delta r_{\text{rob}, i}$:
1. **Segment Calibration Correction ($\delta_{\text{seg}}$)**:
   $$\hat{p}_{\text{cal}, i} = \text{clip}\!\left(p_{\text{now}, i} + \delta_{\text{seg}},\; [0.01, 0.99]\right)$$
   - **SME** (underpredicted by $0.04\text{–}0.09$): apply midpoint offset $\delta_{\text{SME}} = +0.065$ (interval $[+0.04, +0.09]$).
   - **Strategic Enterprise** (overpredicted by $0.05\text{–}0.12$ post-workflow redesign): apply midpoint offset $\delta_{\text{SE}} = -0.085$ (interval $[-0.12, -0.05]$).
   - **Public Sector**: $\delta_{\text{PS}} = 0.00$ (acceptable calibration; moderate sample size).
   - **Non-Native-Language Support (NNLS)**: $\delta_{\text{NNLS}} = 0.00$ point offset, but attach a wider epistemic uncertainty interval ($\pm 0.10$) due to small validation sample size.
2. **Minimum Calibrated Risk Floor**: To avoid disrupting customers on likely non-breaches, immediate allocation requires $\hat{p}_{\text{cal}, i} \ge 0.50$. Cases with $\hat{p}_{\text{cal}, i} < 0.50$ in `Triage` (`I-04` at $0.36\text{–}0.43$ and `I-08` at $0.31\text{–}0.38$) are deferred until `Diagnosis Started` unless $\text{TimeToSLA}_i \le 2.5\text{ h}$.
3. **Robust Causal Effect ($\Delta r_{\text{rob}, i}$)**:
   Given supplied effect ranges $[\Delta r_{\min, i}, \Delta r_{\max, i}]$ with midpoint $\Delta r_{\text{mid}, i}$, we guard against model over-optimism and bound the achievable absolute risk reduction by baseline calibrated risk:
   $$\Delta r_{\text{rob}, i} = \min\!\Big(\hat{p}_{\text{cal}, i},\; (1 - \lambda_i)\,\Delta r_{\text{mid}, i} + \lambda_i\,\Delta r_{\min, i}\Big)$$
   where $\lambda_i \in [0.25, 0.40]$ is an ambiguity-aversion weight ($\lambda_i = 0.25$ for well-calibrated segments; $\lambda_i = 0.35$ for Strategic Enterprise where baseline overprediction compresses room for absolute risk reduction and for sparse NNLS validation samples, capped at $0.35$ so sparse segments are not starved).

### Gate 3: Alert Persistence and Minimum Evidence Change
Because the model refreshes every 30 minutes, elapsed time alone creates small upward probability drift without new diagnostic content. An alert is **actionable** at epoch $t$ only if at least one condition holds:
- **First Actionable Alert**: First alert with $\hat{p}_{\text{cal}, i} \ge 0.50$ and verified telemetry.
- **Material Risk Acceleration**: $\Delta p_i = p_{\text{now}, i} - p_{\text{prev}, i} \ge +0.05$ accompanied by real process progression (e.g., entering `Diagnosis Started`, as in `I-02` with $+0.08$ and `I-09` with $+0.07$).
- **Material New Diagnostic Evidence**: A new substantive event is logged (e.g., new customer diagnostic log uploaded as in `I-07`, new failure trace, or stage transition), **even if $\Delta p_i \le 0$** (`I-07` has $\Delta p = -0.03$).
- **Clock-Drift Suppression**: If $\text{AlertCount}_i \ge 2$, $|\Delta p_i| < 0.05$, no new event or stage change has occurred since the prior alert, and $\text{TimeToSLA}_i > 2.5\text{ h}$ (`I-05` with $\Delta p = +0.02$), the alert is suppressed in cool-down (`Wait`).

### Gate 4: Portfolio Prioritization, Spillover Hurdle, Option Value of Waiting, and Reserve Policy
For eligible, actionable cases, we compute **Direct Net Economic Benefit** ($\text{EV}_{\text{direct}, i}$) and adjust for **queue externalities** and **window urgency**:
1. **Direct Net Benefit**:
   $$\text{EV}_{\text{direct}, i} = \Delta r_i \times V_i - C_{\text{swarm}} - B_{\text{FA}, i}$$
   where $C_{\text{swarm}} = €450$ and $B_{\text{FA}, i}$ is the false-alarm burden (evaluated both under the full-burden baseline formula above and under probability-weighted false-alarm burden $(1 - \hat{p}_{\text{cal}, i})B_{\text{FA}, i}$).
2. **Spillover Hurdle ($S_{\text{slot}}$)**:
   Each used slot pulls two senior specialists off ordinary diagnosis work, adding $8\text{–}20\text{ min}$ of aggregate delay and increasing breach probability by $0.01\text{–}0.03$ across $6$ untreated diagnosis-stage cases ($0.06\text{–}0.18$ expected spillover breaches per slot, midpoint $0.12$ breaches; worth $€750\text{–}€2,250$, midpoint $€1,500$, at a representative untreated queue breach value $\bar{V}_{\text{queue}} = €12,500$).
3. **Urgency / Option-Value Multiplier ($U_i$)**:
   Cases in `Diagnosis Started` with $\text{TimeToSLA}_i \le 3.0\text{ h}$ (`I-09`, `I-02`) or `Triage` with $\text{TimeToSLA}_i \le 2.0\text{ h}$ (`I-07`) are in a **now-or-never window** ($U_i = 1.0$): waiting another cycle risks crossing `Diagnosis Complete` or breaching SLA. Cases in `Triage` with $\text{TimeToSLA}_i \ge 7.0\text{ h}$ (`I-01`, `I-04`, `I-08`, `I-10`) retain high option value to wait ($U_i = 3.5 / \text{TimeToSLA}_i \le 0.50$), because normal triage may resolve uncertainty without incurring immediate false-alarm costs or diagnosis-queue spillover.
4. **Two-Track Slot Allocation & Strategic Reserve**:
   - **Track A (Portfolio Economic Value)**: Allocate slots to now-or-never cases ($U_i = 1.0$) where $\text{EV}_{\text{direct}, i}^{\text{mid}} > S_{\text{slot}}$ (`I-09` and `I-02`).
   - **Track B (Urgent SLA-Fairness & Breach-Reduction Floor — max 1 slot)**: To prevent high-$V$ enterprise cases from permanently starving lower-$V$ SME cases, allocate up to $1$ slot to an SME/Public Sector case with $\hat{p}_{\text{cal}, i} \ge 0.75$, $\text{TimeToSLA}_i \le 2.0\text{ h}$, new diagnostic evidence, $\text{EV}_{\text{direct}, i} > 0$, and net portfolio breach reduction $\Delta r_{\text{mid}, i} > 0.12$ (`I-07`, where $\Delta r_{\text{mid}} = 0.26 > 0.12$).
   - **Strategic Reserve (Slot 4)**: Hold $1$ slot in reserve for the anticipated regulatory incident before 15:00, with a **timed release gate**: (a) at 12:15, if `I-06`'s missing queue telemetry is verified as a real congestion spike and regulatory risk can be covered, or (b) at 14:30, if no regulatory incident has arrived, release Slot 4 to the highest-priority verified standby case (`I-06` if verified real, otherwise `I-01` or `I-04` upon entering `Diagnosis Started`).

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

Because the prediction pipeline scores every active case every 30 minutes while specialist capacity is allocated in discrete waves, the policy must distinguish **passive clock-driven persistence** from **information-driven escalation**, and **repeated alerts on unswarmed cases** from **repeated swarms on already-treated cases**.

### 1. Cool-Down and Suppression Logic (Unswarmed Cases)
When an alerting case is evaluated and placed in `Wait`, it enters a **90-minute (3-refresh-cycle) Alert Cool-Down**:
- **Suppression Rule**: During cool-down, subsequent 30-minute model refreshes are **suppressed from triggering dispatcher interrupts** (logged silently in telemetry) if all three conditions hold:
  1. **Bounded Probability Drift**: $|\Delta p_t| = |p_{\text{now}, t} - p_{\text{prev}, t-1}| < 0.05$ and cumulative drift since deferral $< 0.08$.
  2. **Stage Invariance**: The case remains in the same process stage (`Incident Opened` or `Triage`).
  3. **Event Invariance**: No new diagnostic event, customer attachment, or infrastructure signal has been recorded since the deferral timestamp.

### 2. Escalation and Re-Entry Triggers (Breaking Cool-Down)
A suppressed case immediately breaks cool-down and re-enters active slot contention if **any** of four triggers fire:
- **Trigger 1 — Material New Evidence (Content Override)**: A new diagnostic artifact or event is logged (e.g., customer log upload, diagnostic test output, subsystem status change). This trigger overrides $|\Delta p_t| < 0.05$ and even $\Delta p_t < 0$, because new diagnostic data changes the *causal tractability* ($\Delta r_i$) of a swarm.
- **Trigger 2 — Process Stage Transition**: The case transitions from `Triage` into `Diagnosis Started` (as in `I-02` and `I-09`), narrowing the remaining window before `Diagnosis Complete` and clarifying initial triage routing.
- **Trigger 3 — Verified Risk Surge**: $\Delta p_t \ge +0.08$ in a single cycle (or $\ge +0.12$ cumulative since deferral) after passing the Gate 1 Data-Integrity check (ruling out imputation spikes like `I-06`).
- **Trigger 4 — Critical Horizon Escalation (Last-Chance Floor)**: $\text{TimeToSLA}_i$ drops to $\le 2.0\text{ h}$ while $\text{Stage}_i < \text{Diagnosis Complete}$ and $\hat{p}_{\text{cal}, i} \ge 0.60$.

### 3. Post-Swarm Lockout and Second-Swarm Re-Entry Policy
Once a case receives a Senior Diagnostic Swarm ($\text{PriorSwarms}_i = 1$), it enters a **Hard Post-Swarm Lockout**:
- **Post-Swarm Suppression**: For 60 minutes after swarm completion, observational risk alerts are suppressed because the observational model does not immediately reflect off-system diagnostic redirection or pending customer/engineering actions initiated by the swarm.
- **Second-Swarm Exception Rule**: A second swarm is forbidden unless (i) a **materially new structural event** occurs after the first swarm (e.g., a secondary subsystem failure or contradictory test result that invalidates the first swarm's hypothesis), (ii) $\text{Stage}_i$ is still prior to `Diagnosis Complete` with $\text{TimeToSLA}_i \ge 1.5\text{ h}$, and (iii) the lead specialist verifies that a second 45-minute swarm has positive marginal risk reduction $\Delta r_{\text{marginal}} \ge 0.15$.

### 4. Explicit Comparison: `I-05` vs. `I-07`
`I-05` and `I-07` illustrate why temporal policy cannot rely on alert count or raw $\Delta p$ direction alone:

| Dimension | `I-05` (Suppressed / `Wait`) | `I-07` (Re-Entered / `Intervene`) |
|---|---|---|
| **Surface Similarity** | SME segment ($\delta_{\text{SME}} = +0.04\text{ to }+0.09$), `Triage` stage, **third alert**, data complete. | SME segment ($\delta_{\text{SME}} = +0.04\text{ to }+0.09$), `Triage` stage, **third alert**, data complete. |
| **Probability Trajectory** | $p_{\text{prev}} = 0.55 \to p_{\text{now}} = 0.57$ ($\Delta p = +0.02$); calibrated $\hat{p}_{\text{cal}} \approx 0.61\text{–}0.66$. | $p_{\text{prev}} = 0.75 \to p_{\text{now}} = 0.72$ ($\Delta p = -0.03$); calibrated $\hat{p}_{\text{cal}} \approx 0.76\text{–}0.81$. |
| **Underlying Cause of Alert** | **Pure 30-minute clock refresh**: Zero new events have occurred since the first alert. Elapsed time slightly reduced remaining slack, nudging the observational score up by $+0.02$. | **Material new evidence (Trigger 1)**: Customer uploaded a new diagnostic log. In observational data, `"log_uploaded"` correlates with customer responsiveness, causing the classifier to shave $0.03$ off raw risk. |
| **Causal Swarm Leverage** | Unchanged since the 1st and 2nd alerts; swarming now interrupts specialists without any new diagnostic input to analyze. | **Increased**: A newly uploaded customer diagnostic log gives two senior specialists concrete telemetry to parse immediately during a 45-minute swarm ($\Delta r = 0.20\text{–}0.32$). |
| **Temporal Window (`TimeToSLA`)** | `5.0 h` remaining — ample buffer to wait for `Diagnosis Started` entry or new triage findings. | `1.5 h` remaining (**Trigger 4 — Last-Chance Floor**) — if deferred one more 30-minute cycle, the case cannot complete a 45-minute swarm plus resolution before breaching. |

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

Deploying a capacity-constrained, value-weighted early-warning system introduces five structural fairness and statistical feedback-loop hazards. Below is the mechanism of each risk and the concrete operational and algorithmic safeguards required.

### 1. Value-Based Prioritization Starving Lower-Value Cases (SME Starvation)
- **Risk Mechanism**: Contract values in the portfolio span a $20\times$ range ($€5,000$ for SME `I-07` to $€100,000$ for Strategic Enterprise `I-08`). Under unconstrained $\Delta r_i \times V_i$ ranking, a Strategic Enterprise case with a minor $0.06$ risk reduction ($€6,000$ gross) will always outrank an SME case with a $0.30$ risk reduction ($€1,500\text{–}€2,100$ gross), even when the Strategic Enterprise case has low calibrated breach risk ($\hat{p}_{\text{cal}} \approx 0.35$) and `8–10 h` of slack while the SME case has an $80\%$ calibrated breach risk and `1.5 h` left (`I-07`). Over time, SMEs experience systematic SLA starvation.
- **Concrete Safeguards**:
  1. **Dual-Track Capacity Quota (25% SLA-Fairness Floor)**: Ring-fence up to 1 of 4 daily slots (Track B in Gate 4) for imminent-breach cases ($\hat{p}_{\text{cal}} \ge 0.75$, $\text{TimeToSLA} \le 2.0\text{ h}$, $\Delta r_{\text{mid}} \ge 0.20$) whose direct net EV is positive and whose expected breach reduction exceeds expected spillover breaches ($\Delta r_{\text{mid}} > 0.12$), regardless of raw contract value $V_i$.
  2. **Within-Segment Normalized Priority Component**: Score Tier-2 standby contention using a blended index $S_i = 0.6\,\text{EV}_{\text{direct}, i} + 0.4\,\big(\Delta r_{\text{rob}, i} \cdot \bar{V}_{\text{portfolio}} - C - B_{\text{FA}, i}\big)$ so high causal efficacy ($\Delta r_i$) cannot be completely swamped by contract size.
  3. **Calibrated Risk Floor ($\hat{p}_{\text{cal}} \ge 0.50$) & Slack Deferral**: Bar high-$V$ cases with $\hat{p}_{\text{cal}} < 0.50$ and $\text{TimeToSLA} \ge 7.0\text{ h}$ (`I-04`, `I-08`) from pre-empting slots while still in `Triage`.

### 2. Epistemic Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support (NNLS)
- **Risk Mechanism**: Because validation sample sizes for `Non-Native-Language Support` (`I-09`) are small, both calibration error bars and treatment-effect intervals are wide. If the policy applies heavy ambiguity aversion (e.g., evaluating cases strictly at their $5\text{th}$-percentile lower confidence bound), small-sample segments like NNLS will be systematically penalized for data sparsity (**epistemic starvation**), which in turn prevents collecting the very intervention data needed to shrink their uncertainty. Conversely, ignoring language context may pair an NNLS case with specialists who cannot resolve the communication bottleneck.
- **Concrete Safeguards**:
  1. **Capped Ambiguity Penalty ($\lambda_{\text{NNLS}} \le 0.35$)**: Prohibit sample-size variance from discounting $\Delta r_{\text{mid}}$ by more than $35\%$ when midpoint net EV is positive (`I-09` retains $+€2,350$ net direct value even at its $0.18$ lower bound).
  2. **Hierarchical Empirical-Bayes Shrinkage**: Estimate calibration and treatment effects for NNLS using multilevel partial pooling across incident complexity and technical domain rather than noisy standalone cell means.
  3. **Bilingual / Protocol-Structured Swarm Routing**: Tag NNLS swarms to include a language-matched specialist or structured diagnostic checklist so the 45-minute intervention directly resolves translation ambiguity.

### 3. Differential Calibration Across Segments
- **Risk Mechanism**: Backtests show the model **underpredicts SME breach risk by $0.04\text{–}0.09$** while **overpredicting Strategic Enterprise risk by $0.05\text{–}0.12$** after a workflow redesign. Using raw $p_{\text{now}}$ compounds economic inequality with statistical bias: Strategic Enterprise cases receive artificially inflated probabilities *and* $10\times\text{–}20\times$ higher euro weights, while SME cases are systematically under-flagged.
- **Concrete Safeguards**:
  1. **Mandatory Segment Post-Calibration Layer**: Apply segment-specific isotonic/Platt recalibration offsets ($\hat{p}_{\text{cal}} = p_{\text{now}} + \delta_{\text{seg}}$) upstream of all alert thresholds, cool-down rules, and priority rankings.
  2. **Workflow-Change Quarantine**: Automatically trigger segment-specific shadow recalibration whenever an operational workflow redesign occurs (such as the Strategic Enterprise redesign), isolating that segment's calibration curve from the rest of the portfolio.

### 4. Selective Intervention Corrupting Future Training Labels (Performative Feedback Loops)
- **Risk Mechanism**: The early-warning model is supposed to estimate untreated breach risk $P(Y(0)=1 \mid X)$. However, once swarms ($A=1$) are systematically deployed on high-risk cases (`I-09`, `I-02`, `I-07`), successful interventions flip their realized outcomes to non-breach ($Y_{\text{obs}} = Y(1) = 0$). Meanwhile, untreated diagnosis-stage cases suffer spillover delays that flip some of their realized outcomes to breach ($Y_{\text{obs}} = 1$). If future model retraining treats $Y_{\text{obs}}$ as ground-truth baseline risk without accounting for $A$ and queue spillover exposure $E$:
  - Swarmed case profiles (`I-09`, `I-02`, `I-07`) will appear artificially safe in retraining, causing the next model version to **underpredict** their risk and withdraw swarms.
  - Untreated cases exposed to swarm spillovers will appear intrinsically riskier, distorting segment calibration.
- **Concrete Safeguards**:
  1. **Explicit Potential-Outcomes Logging**: Log $(X_{i,t}, A_{i,t}, \pi_{i,t}, E_{i,t}, Y_i)$ at every decision epoch, where $A_{i,t} \in \{0,1\}$ is swarm assignment, $\pi_{i,t} = P(A_{i,t}=1 \mid H_{i,t})$ is the policy assignment propensity, and $E_{i,t} \in \{0,1,2,3,4\}$ is the number of concurrent swarms delaying the diagnosis queue.
  2. **Doubly Robust / IPTW Retraining for Baseline Risk**: Train the prognostic baseline model $\hat{P}(Y(0)=1 \mid X, E=0)$ using Inverse Probability of Treatment and Exposure Weighting (IPTW) or a Doubly Robust (DR) outcome model that explicitly partials out both direct treatment $A$ and spillover exposure $E$.

### 5. Spillover Harms Falling Systematically on Untreated Groups (Regressive Queue Externalities)
- **Risk Mechanism**: Each swarm pulls two senior specialists from ordinary diagnosis work, adding $8\text{–}20\text{ min}$ ($15\text{–}30\text{ min}$ in Adverse congestion) and $+0.01\text{–}0.03$ breach risk to $6$ untreated diagnosis-stage cases. If high-value Strategic Enterprise cases capture most swarms while Public Sector (`I-06`) and SME cases sit untreated in `Diagnosis Started`, **lower-value segments bear the delay and induced SLA breaches that finance risk reduction for high-value segments**.
- **Concrete Safeguards**:
  1. **Cumulative Spillover Exposure Cap ($E_j \le 2$)**: Track how many swarm delays each untreated case $j$ in `Diagnosis Started` has absorbed. Any untreated case exposed to $\ge 2$ concurrent swarms receives an automatic **ordinary-queue priority bump** (protected specialist assignment for standard diagnosis so it cannot be delayed by a 3rd swarm).
  2. **Cross-Segment Spillover Tax in Slot Scoring**: Compute the spillover hurdle $S_{\text{slot}}$ dynamically from the live `Diagnosis Started` queue and apply a $1.25\times$ penalty weight when $>50\%$ of untreated diagnosis-queue cases belong to under-served segments (SME / Public Sector / NNLS).

### 2. Plan for Estimating Causal Intervention Effects and Spillovers
Because swarms are assigned non-randomly to high-priority cases, naive differences between swarmed and unswarmed breach rates are severely confounded. We identify $\Delta r_i$ and spillover $s$ through three mechanisms:
1. **Stratified Randomized Exploration on Borderline Tier-2 Cases (5–10% Capacity Budget)**:
   When the reserve slot is released at 14:30 (or when multiple Tier-2 cases such as `I-01`, `I-04`, and `I-05` compete within $15\%$ of the priority cutoff), assign the marginal slot using **logged randomized propensities** $\pi_i \in [0.25, 0.75]$. Combining these randomized tie-breaks with **Doubly Robust (AIPW) estimators** yields unbiased estimates of $\hat{\tau}(X) = E[Y(0) - Y(1) \mid X]$.
2. **Quasi-Experimental Capacity & Shift Instrumental Variables**:
   Exploit exogenous variation in specialist availability and concurrent regulatory arrivals (cases with identical $\hat{p}_{\text{cal}}$ and stage arriving when 3 slots remain vs. 0 slots remain) alongside **within-case hazard-rate changes** (reduction in remaining `Diagnosis Started` duration immediately following a 45-minute swarm relative to matched untreated controls).
3. **Interference / Exposure Mapping for Queue Spillovers**:
   For every untreated case $j$ in `Diagnosis Started`, record its exposure $E_j(t) \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}$ (number of concurrent swarms active during its diagnosis window) and baseline queue backlog $Q_t$. Estimate two structural equations:
   $$\text{Delay}_j = \alpha_0 + \gamma\,E_j + \beta\,Q_t + \epsilon_j, \qquad P(\text{Breach}_j \mid A_j=0, X_j, E_j, Q_t) = g(\hat{p}_{\text{cal}, j}) + \theta(Q_t)\,E_j$$
   This directly estimates whether $\theta(Q_t)$ is in the Optimistic ($\le 0.01$), Base ($0.01\text{–}0.03$), or Adverse ($>0.03$) regime as a function of real-time congestion $Q_t$.

### 3. Detecting Policy-Induced Drift
We run three automated drift detectors to catch feedback loops before they degrade allocations:
1. **Counterfactual vs. Observational Divergence Test**: Compare predictions of a shadow model trained naively on all realized outcomes $Y_{\text{obs}}$ against the production IPTW/DR model trained on $Y(0)$. A widening gap ($\hat{p}_{\text{IPTW}} - \hat{p}_{\text{naive}} > 0.05$ on high-priority profiles) confirms that successful swarms are masking baseline risk in raw labels.
2. **Stage-Duration & Gaming Drift**: Monitor median time spent in `Triage` and `Diagnosis Started` by segment. If teams delay marking `Diagnosis Complete` to keep cases eligible for senior swarms, or if workflow changes shift stage durations (as in Strategic Enterprise), a CUSUM change-point detector flags the distribution shift.
3. **Alert-Reason Attribution Audit**: For every swarmed case, log the specialist team's post-swarm disposition (`"true bottleneck accelerated"`, `"already resolving / false alarm"`, or `"telemetry artifact"`). If `"already resolving / false alarm"` exceeds $25\%$ in any segment, that segment's calibration offset $\delta_{\text{seg}}$ and false-alarm penalty $B_{\text{FA}}$ are immediately adjusted.

### 4. Decision Rules for Changing Thresholds or Allocation Policy
- **Trigger A — Real-Time Congestion Circuit-Breaker (Threshold Escalation)**: If live rolling diagnosis-queue delay per active swarm crosses **$20\text{ minutes}$** (Adverse spillover boundary), automatically raise the minimum net EV hurdle $S_{\text{slot}}$ from $€1,500$ to **$€3,000$**, suspend Track B low-$V$ allocations if net breaches turn positive, and cap concurrent swarms at **$2\text{ slots}$** (`I-09` and `I-02`) until queue delay drops below $15\text{ minutes}$.
- **Trigger B — Segment Offset Recalibration ($\delta_{\text{seg}}$ Update)**: If rolling bi-weekly untreated O/E calibration bias exceeds $\pm 0.03$ in any segment, update $\delta_{\text{seg}}$ via Bayesian shrinkage without waiting for full model retraining; if `NNLS` accumulated sample size reaches $N \ge 150$ untreated cases, transition `NNLS` from pooled shrinkage to its own isotonic calibration curve.
- **Trigger C — Effect-Range Shrinkage & Stage Gate Revision**: Update each segment's prior effect range $[\Delta r_{\min}, \Delta r_{\max}]$ monthly using posterior credible intervals from the Doubly Robust estimator. If `Triage`-stage swarms show $\ge 30\%$ lower causal effect than `Diagnosis Started` swarms (due to premature swarming before basic triage facts are gathered), restrict `Triage` interventions exclusively to cases with $\text{TimeToSLA} \le 2.5\text{ h}$ (`I-07`) and require all longer-horizon cases (`I-01`, `I-04`, `I-05`, `I-08`, `I-10`) to complete `Triage` before becoming eligible.
