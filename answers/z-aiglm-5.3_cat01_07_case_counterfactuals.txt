D1/D2 substitution (K-219): moving 100 handwritten-standard cases to D2 adds roughly 100–300 net document hours (D2 is 1–6 h slower per case) while freeing ~400 D1 hours for complex corporate work. Once D2's queue equals D1's, the policy inverts. The decisive term is rework: at a 15–30 percent clarification rate, 15–30 loops appear, each adding a document check, an assessment, and an 8–36 hour external response that can span a weekend — expected rework dominates the 2–4 hour first-pass saving. Rework suppression (K-337): capping second loops removes perhaps 20–40 external waits including weekend spans — the single largest cycle-time lever — and unloads D1 and A1. But every suppressed case still consumes C1 and M1, so the bottleneck migrates to compliance review and decision signing, and suppressed discrepancies become conditional approvals whose appeal and reconciliation workload arrives later and unbudgeted. Parallelization (K-411): creating both tasks at triage is capacity-neutral and removes artificial serialization, but 100 earlier-ready cases pour into an assessment queue that was already growing (A1 1012 h, A2 47 h on Monday). With fixed assessor capacity, earlier arrivals convert into queue wait rather than earlier completion. Finally, if workaround timestamps are batch-entered, the log will show concurrency that never occurred; the 100-case evaluation would then overstate benefits and misdirect capacity decisions. Real-time timestamping is not a logging detail — it is the measurement.

## 7. Final synthesis

K-411's parallelization is the most robust intervention. It removes an artificial serialization between two different resource pools, is capacity-neutral, and preserves every normative rule — concurrency without originals is explicitly permitted. Its mandated real-time timestamps also improve the measurement system itself. The gains are modest (0–6 hours) and will compress under assessment-queue congestion, but the intervention rarely makes anything worse.

K-337 is the most dangerous despite the largest saving. It does not merely retime the process; it changes what the organization is allowed to know at decision time. Suppressing the second information request converts an evidence problem into a risk-acceptance problem: the case must be approved with conditions or rejected while a discrepancy is open. The time saving is near-certain; decision quality is not. A signed decision is effectively irreversible — appeals and reopening cost more than the loop they replace, and capping loops teaches assessors to stop documenting discrepancies.

The least transportable local counterfactual is K-104's compliance reservation. For one case, a reserved C1 slot looks like scheduling intelligence; for a hundred, it pre-commits a single certified reviewer's week against noisy assessment ETAs, idles slots, and displaces exactly the urgent compliance cases the rule exists to protect. K-219's rerouting is similarly fragile: its gain depends on D2 overnight slack and an afternoon M1 slot, while its failure mode — clarification loops with 8–36 hour external responses — is unbounded relative to the saving.

The most valuable additional data are queue join/leave timestamps (not just service starts) to separate waiting from processing; C1 slot utilization and no-show rates; clarification and rework rates by resource and document type; external response-time distributions including weekend spans; and decision and appeal outcomes for conditional approvals. Without these, every counterfactual here is a forecast; with them, the queue estimates stop being folklore.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. `structural` — All normative precedences (RegisterTriage; both checks before Assessment; final Compliance Review after the final Assessment and before Decision; Decision before Notify) hold in every counterfactual; only K-337's explicitly specified suppression of the second Request More Info removes an activity, and it does so as a policy change, not a rule violation.
2. `temporal` — Queue snapshot values are forecasts with roughly ±30–50% error; single-case counterfactuals hold other cases' arrivals and service completions approximately fixed (ceteris paribus), which is exactly what breaks at 100-case scale.
3. `resource` — Service durations fall within the stated bands, and the documents and assessment pools have evening/overnight coverage in this window (D1 worked 21:40–05:40; A2 worked 20:00–22:30); extending overnight coverage to D2 is the weakest link in K-219's counterfactual.
4. `behavioral` — M1 signs within ~0.5–2 h when a case becomes ready during business hours and otherwise next business morning (observed 08:30, 14:00, 18:00 signings), which caps how much upstream acceleration reaches the customer.
5. `quality` — A triage-time escalation packet is inherently incomplete (verified documents exist only after Document Check), so preliminary compliance work saves only 1–3 h, and only when triage notes are accurate; scheduling the deep read after document verification partially mitigates this.
6. `behavioral` — D2 carries a 15–30% probability of requesting clarification on difficult handwriting; triage's "handwriting  D1" rule encoded real expertise rather than arbitrary preference.
7. `interference` — Reserved C1 slots, D2 reassignments, and earlier assessment arrivals displace non-target cases; at scale, displacement and induced rework dominate direct per-case savings (Little's-law compression at the assessment pool).
8. `temporal` — External responses to Request More Info take 8–36 h and, when triggered late on a Friday, span the weekend (K-337 factual: Fri 10:30  Mon 09:00 = 70.5 h), making rework-related timings the most volatile quantity in all counterfactuals.
