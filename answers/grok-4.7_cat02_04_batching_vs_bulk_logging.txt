# 1. Cluster diagnoses

Assumptions used throughout:

- **A1.** Clocks are complete as printed. A value with no seconds is exact at `:00`. The extract shows seconds where it has them (`09:03:02`, `12:09:04`), so recording lag and adjacency are computed from the printed strings.
- **A2.** Within a cluster, `queue_arrival`, lifecycle times, and `recorded_at` share a clock, except the safety-check clock in G8, which is explicitly a different clock. G7’s upstream arrivals are finer than the MES lattice; they are treated as a separate, finer source.
- **A3.** Each row is one case of one activity. Clusters are separate work areas. No end-to-end case linkage is inferred.
- **A4.** Naive wait = start − `queue_arrival`. Naive service = complete − start. Strict overlap uses a half-open interval: endpoint contact is adjacency, not concurrency.
- **A5.** Fixture size, the begin-group button, the folder-submit practice, the shared login, MES rounding, and the safety check are source context. They are not treated as proven causes. They break ties; they do not replace the timestamps.
- **A6.** For G7 bounds only: work cannot start before that case’s `queue_arrival`, and the stored pair `(14:00, 14:15)` is the image of an execution inside the quarter-hour under an outward (floor start, ceil complete) rule. That bound is hypothesis-dependent.

# 2. Expected temporal signatures

PM4Py batch labels are names for temporal patterns. “Simultaneous” means stored starts match and stored ends match. It does not mean people or machines worked at the same time.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

# 3. Consequences for performance analysis

Cross-cluster ranking of bottlenecks is not supported. These are eight activities, eight sources, and 4–7 rows each, with no shared case id. Bias below is **within** a cluster, for the naive KPIs in A4.

**G1 — fixture cycle.** Case sojourn on the station is 3 min for every item if the cycle bounds are real. That sojourn is the right input to lead time. Summing the four intervals yields 12 resource-minutes for 3 minutes of wall clock (sum/union = 4) and inflates utilization by the batch size. A single-server formula 1/3 per minute understates cycle throughput (4 items in 3 min) by about that same factor. Waits of 6, 5, 4, and 2 min are batch-formation waits: the first arrival waits longest for the fixture to fill. Those waits are operationally real under the simultaneous-cycle reading and should stay in queue time. Conformance models that forbid parallelism will flag a normal fixture load. Correction: keep the interval as case sojourn; compute robot busy time as the union; do not divide the 3 min by 4 unless a per-item touch time is required. That division is the rival, serial-inside-the-cycle, reading.

**G2 — group start, individual finishes.** If the begin-group stamp is not a true per-item start, naive service (8, 14, 21, 27; mean 17.5) absorbs queueing inside the group. Rebuilt serial gaps are 8, 6, 7, 6 (mean 6.75). Wall clock is 27 min, equal to the sum of those gaps. Naive wait (mean 12.25) is then too small by the time each item sat after 10:00 while an earlier item was inspected. Sojourn = complete − arrival can still be about right, because the late start and the long service cancel. Summed utilization uses 70 min for 27 min of one inspector (sum/union = 2.59) and overstates load. Throughput observed from the wall clock is 4 items in 27 min; the naive mean service implies only about 3.4 items per hour. Bottleneck methods that rank on mean service will overstate this station. A conformance rule “one inspection at a time” will mark an artifactual overlap. The parallel-inspection reading reverses the correction: 17.5 min and the overlap would be real, and busy time would be closer to the sum. Those two readings must not be averaged.

**G3 — individual starts, folder complete.** This is the mirror image of G2. Starts step by 8, 7, and 7 min; the last naive duration is 6 min. If starts are true begin-review times and 11:30 is folder submit, naive service (28, 20, 13, 6; mean 16.75) and sojourn are both long, while naive wait (mean 19.5, peak queue 3) is the usable queue measure. Sum/union = 67/28 = 2.39 overstates one reviewer’s busy time if the union (28 min, from 11:02 to 11:30) is the real effort. Throughput from the wall clock is about 4 items in 28 min; naive mean service implies about 3.6 per hour. An SLA on review duration will record false breaches for E01–E03. An SLA on “released by 11:30” will look perfectly met. The multitasking-until-11:30 reading would instead treat the overlap as real and the sojourn as real. As in G2, the two readings move service and utilization in opposite directions.

**G4 — clean chain.** Naive service is 4 min on every case, union equals the sum (20 min), overlap is 0, peak concurrency is 1. Wait is 7, 10, 13, 16, 19 (mean 13): all five items are in queue before 12:05, then each extra position adds service − interarrival = 3 min. That is ordinary transient overload (arrivals about one per minute, service 4 min), not a timestamp defect. Insert lag of 3–6 sec is about 2% of service. Throughput 5 per 20 min matches 1/4 min. Conformance to FIFO single-piece flow fits. The only analytical risk is calling a busy period a “batch,” or treating a 4-minute arrival burst as the daily rate. No duration correction is indicated.

**G5 — one login, overlapping elapsed times.** Naive waits (mean 15.25, peak queue 4) may be real pickup delay. Naive services (25, 15, 20, 10; mean 17.5) are elapsed open times with an unknown touch fraction. Sum/union = 70/30 = 2.33. If `analyst_pool` is modeled as one server, offered load above 1 is either impossible utilization, a multitasking upper bound, or a sign that the model is wrong. Union busy time (30 min) understates team effort if several people actually worked; the sum (70 min) overstates effort if intervals contain idle. Peak concurrency 4 exceeds the stated cap of 3, so even a 3-server pool does not absorb the overlap unless one person carries two cases for about 8 min (13:12–13:20) or the cap is wrong. Bottleneck detection on one resource id will call root-cause analysis a single overloaded server and will hide which analyst, if any, is slow. Conformance to “one analyst, one case” fails; conformance to “pooled queue” is plausible and untestable without badges. Throughput of 4 cases in 30 min is feasible for a pool and infeasible for one non-overlapping analyst at these durations.

**G6 — why bulk point masses inflate waiting and zero the service.** The form writes one clock time into both lifecycle fields. Service = complete − start = 0 for all 7 cases, so every indicator that uses service (mean, capacity 1/service, resource busy time) treats Close ticket as instantaneous. Wait = start − arrival uses that same late stamp as the moment work began. With arrivals from 14:05 through 16:25, naive waits are 175, 148, 122, 110, 76, 58, and 35 min (mean 103.4, median 110). The entire path from arrival to the 17:00 stamp is labeled queue, and none of it is labeled service. Sojourn equals that inflated wait. The insert at 17:01 shows the database itself was prompt; the distortion sits in the lifecycle fields, one minute before the insert, hours after the arrivals.

Effects that follow directly:

- Queue analysis reports a large afternoon queue and a zero service rate. Methods that rank bottlenecks by waiting or queue length flag Close ticket. Methods that rank by utilization see about 0 busy time and ignore it. The two families disagree because the same defect moves time from service into wait.
- A capacity model with service 0 cannot form a utilization ratio (union busy time is 0). Filtering zero-duration events out of the log deletes the activity and makes the cases look unfinished.
- Throughput is a spike of 7 completions on one timestamp and nothing earlier. That spike is not a demonstrated processing rate.
- Conformance against “close within an hour of arrival” records seven breaches whose size is the logging delay under the late-typing reading, and a real policy delay under the end-of-day-sweep reading. Either way, service-time conformance against a positive standard time is undefined, because no positive duration was stored.

G4 shows that a live scanner does not produce this shape. G6’s shape matches the source name.

**G7 — quarter-hour censoring.** Naive service is exactly one precision unit, five times. Sum/union = 75/15 = 5, so summed utilization multiplies one bin by the number of rows. All five intervals overlap, so a concurrency analysis reports a 5-wide batch. Peak queue computed from the nominal start is 1, because M02–M05 have arrival after start and never enter the queue. Nominal waits are +2, −1, −4, −7, −11 min (mean −4.2). Negative wait is a data-quality alarm, not a fast process. Under A6, arrival times censor the latest possible start, so maximum durations are 15, 14, 11, 8, and 4 min, and one worker’s durations must also fit jointly inside the bin. Publishing 15 min as the standard time overstates every case, especially M05. Throughput of “5 per 15 min” is the bin width, not a measured rate. The insert stagger (one row per minute from 14:16 to 14:20) is an insertion sequence. Conformance to a specification of “15-minute measure” will look exact because the lattice value equals the bin. Interval-censor this cluster in any queue, service, utilization, or bottleneck figure.

**G8 — real hole, then a clean drain.** Service of 7 min is internally consistent: union = sum = 35 min, overlap 0, lag a few seconds, FIFO preserved. Throughput after 15:38 matches 1/7 min. Naive waits are 38, 43, 48, 54, and 58 min (mean 48.2, peak queue 5). Conditional on a resume at 15:38, those waits split exactly into time-until-resume plus 0, 7, 14, 21, and 28 min of FIFO congestion:

- time from arrival to 15:38 is 38, 36, 34, 33, 30
- plus position × 7 min
- sums are the observed waits

The hole from the last arrival (15:08) to the first start (15:38) is 30 min **on the scanner clock**. It does not depend on the safety system. Attributing it to the safety check does: that event is 15:00–15:35 on another clock, leaving a 3 min residual to 15:38 that skew can absorb. If the whole wait is reported as congestion, triage looks like a severe queueing bottleneck despite a regular 7 min service. If the whole wait is deleted, true FIFO delay of up to 28 min for Q05 disappears. Utilization from 15:00 to 16:13 mixes unavailable time with productive time and understates load during the open period, when the station is busy for the entire 35 min drain. Conformance waits will look breached mostly because the station was not in service. The correction is segmentation, not deletion of the service intervals.

def pair_overlap_rate(g):
    starts = g["start_timestamp"].tolist()
    ends = g["time:timestamp"].tolist()
    n = len(g)
    if n < 2:
        return float("nan")
    hits = 0
    pairs = 0
    for i in range(n):
        for j in range(i + 1, n):
            pairs += 1
            if starts[i] < ends[j] and starts[j] < ends[i]:
                hits += 1
    return hits / pairs


def union_minutes(g):
    intervals = sorted(zip(g["start_timestamp"], g["time:timestamp"]))
    cur_s, cur_e = intervals[0]
    total = pd.Timedelta(0)
    for s, e in intervals[1:]:
        if s > cur_e:
            total += cur_e - cur_s
            cur_s, cur_e = s, e
        elif e > cur_e:
            cur_e = e
    total += cur_e - cur_s
    return total.total_seconds() / 60.0


def peak_concurrency(g):
    events = []
    for s, e in zip(g["start_timestamp"], g["time:timestamp"]):
        if e <= s:
            continue
        events.append((s, 1))
        events.append((e, -1))
    events.sort(key=lambda x: (x[0], x[1]))  # release before acquire at the same instant
    cur = peak = 0
    for _, delta in events:
        cur += delta
        peak = max(peak, cur)
    return peak


def peak_queue(g):
    peak = 0
    for t in set(g["qa"]).union(set(g["start_timestamp"])):
        q = int(((g["qa"] <= t) & (g["start_timestamp"] > t)).sum())
        peak = max(peak, q)
    return peak


def adjacency_count(g):
    s = g.sort_values(["start_timestamp", "sequence_no"])
    st = s["start_timestamp"].tolist()
    en = s["time:timestamp"].tolist()
    return sum(st[i + 1] == en[i] for i in range(len(s) - 1))


def max_equal_group(series):
    return int(series.value_counts().max()) if len(series) else 0


def reference_type(g, merge_distance_sec, min_batch_size):
    recs = g.sort_values(["start_timestamp", "time:timestamp", "sequence_no"]).to_dict("records")
    batches = []
    current = [recs[0]]
    for rec in recs[1:]:
        max_end = max(r["time:timestamp"] for r in current)
        gap = (rec["start_timestamp"] - max_end).total_seconds()
        if gap <= merge_distance_sec:
            current.append(rec)
        else:
            batches.append(current)
            current = [rec]
    batches.append(current)
    kept = [b for b in batches if len(b) >= min_batch_size]
    if not kept:
        return "none"
    labels = []
    for b in kept:
        starts = {r["start_timestamp"] for r in b}
        ends = {r["time:timestamp"] for r in b}
        if len(starts) == 1 and len(ends) == 1:
            labels.append("Simultaneous")
        elif len(starts) == 1:
            labels.append("Start batch")
        elif len(ends) == 1:
            labels.append("End batch")
        else:
            overlap = False
            for i in range(len(b)):
                for j in range(i + 1, len(b)):
                    a, c = b[i], b[j]
                    if a["start_timestamp"] < c["time:timestamp"] and c["start_timestamp"] < a["time:timestamp"]:
                        overlap = True
            labels.append("Concurrent batch" if overlap else "Sequential batch")
    return ";".join(labels)

# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

**Resource ids trusted as one worker or one machine.**

- G1 stays a machine cycle. Trusting `RBT-7` does not create a shared-account story. Capacity 4 is what makes concurrency legal.
- G2 and G3 become strong sequential-work readings. Four-wide physical overlap for a named person is a poor fit, so the equal stamp is logging. Utilization should use the union (27 and 28 min), not the sums (70 and 67).
- G4 and G8 agree with one server: peak concurrency 1, sum/union = 1.
- G5 becomes a contradiction. One person would have to overlap four root-cause cases, with 70 elapsed minutes inside 30 wall-clock minutes. Trusting the id turns a capacity signal into an apparent data error.
- G6 and G7 gain no concurrency. Zero duration and a 15 min lattice do not become more physical if T-F and T-G are single people.
- G7 becomes more clearly “one person, times rounded,” because five-wide simultaneous measuring is then an extra claim.

**Resource ids treated as shared accounts.**

- G5 is the only cluster with positive evidence for sharing (`analyst_pool`, `shared_VDI`, stated headcount). Distrusting the id makes mean concurrency 2.33 look like two or three analysts. The peak of 4 still needs multitasking, a fourth person, or an overstated open interval.
- Applying the same distrust to T-B, T-C, T-D, T-F, T-G, and T-H has no supporting field. It would reclassify G2 and G3 overlaps as legal multi-person work and would hide the logging batch. G4 and G8 would be misread as parallel stations that happened to produce a perfect chain.
- `RBT-7` is a cell, not a login. Sharing does not apply.

**Timestamps treated as exact.**

- G1 simultaneity, G4/G8 adjacency, and G6’s zeros are then literal.
- G2’s common start and G3’s common end are literal events, not rounding, because the other edge of each interval is staggered by more than the 1 min precision.
- G5’s four-way overlap lasts about 8 min (13:12–13:20), so it is literal at 1 min resolution.
- G7’s identical intervals are then misread as a real 15 min simultaneous measure. Exactness is the assumption that does the damage.

**Timestamps interval-censored by the stated precision.**

- 1 sec (G1, G4, G8): a one-second grain is negligible beside 3–7 min services. G4/G8 adjacency might hide a sub-second gap or overlap. The sequential reading stands.
- 1 min (G2, G3, G5, G6): moving every edge by up to a minute does not create G2’s 6–8 min completion steps, G3’s 7–8 min start steps, or G5’s 8 min four-way overlap. For G6, both fields already share one minute, so censored service is at most on the order of that minute. An afternoon of work cannot hide inside precision of 1 min. Waiting remains on the order of hours.
- 15 min (G7): the stored interval is the bin. Under A6 the per-case upper bounds are 15, 14, 11, 8, and 4 min, with a joint one-worker constraint that the durations share one quarter-hour. Overlap rate 1.0 and service = 15 are properties of the lattice.

Widening the equality tolerance up to the documented precision does not move G4 or G8 into Simultaneous (4 min and 7 min gaps versus a 1 sec grain) and does not split G7. The fragile step is reading exact equality as physical simultaneity.

Three patterns that each support more than one physical story:

**1. G1, identical positive-duration interval with n = capacity.** The same `[09:00, 09:03]` on four PLC rows fits a true four-wide cycle, serial indexing inside the fixture with only cycle bounds stored, or a fan-out of one cycle record onto four real arrivals. Case sojourn is 3 min under the first two. Robot-minutes are the 3 min union if the cycle is parallel, and could be a partition of the 3 min if it is serial. Summing to 12 min overstates parallel effort and can accidentally resemble total touch time under a serial partition. Lead time and costing want opposite corrections from the same timestamps. Per-slot signals are what would separate them.

**2. G2, one start and a monotone completion ladder.** This fits a begin-group stamp over serial inspections (rebuilt services 8, 6, 7, 6), a real parallel inspection that finishes in case order (services 8, 14, 21, 27 are then true), or a shared setup at 10:00 folded into the first gap. The cadence favors serial work. It does not prove it. Naive mean service is 17.5 min either as an inflated serial sojourn or as a real parallel elapsed time. A published standard time is wrong under one reading and usable under the other. G3 is the same ambiguity with the bad field reversed: ends are the shared stamp, starts may be the real edge. One recipe that “fixes equal timestamps” will repair the wrong column on one of these two clusters.

**3. G6, seven zero-duration stamps at 17:00.** This fits late typing of work done earlier, a real end-of-day close with click-level service, or a form that defaults both fields to save-time during a short 17:00 sweep. All three put a point mass at 17:01 insert time. They disagree on the mean wait of 103 min: logging delay, policy delay, or policy delay plus a sweep censored into one minute. They agree that service 0 must not enter a capacity model and that 7-at-17:00 is not a throughput rate. The source name supports a form stamp and is silent on what T-F did between 14:05 and 17:00.

Two further cases belong to the same point. **G7**’s single lattice interval fits coarsened serial work, a coarsened group measurement, or clock skew between the queue and the MES. Coarsening is the only one of those that also explains both the lattice and the arrival inversions with a documented rule; the within-bin schedule stays unidentified, so touch-time totals from “at most 15 min, one person” to “five items exposed together” remain open. **G5**’s overlap matrix is a pooled-capacity signal if the login is distrusted, and a multitasking or bad-interval alarm if the login is trusted. Peak 4 against a cap of 3 means a single explanation is short either way. The matrix itself does not change; the resource assumption does.

# 6. Data-collection redesign

Priority follows the size of the KPI error these fragments already produce.

1. **Execution timestamps separate from recording timestamps.** Require a true work start, a true work end, and an insert time. Do not copy the save time into both lifecycle fields. Add a timestamp role: execution, group-button, submission, defaulted. This is the G6 fix, and it stops G3’s folder submit from being stored as four completion times.
2. **Keep the pre-rounded value.** Persist the original clock time, the rounded value, and the rounding mode (floor, ceil, nearest), not only a precision label. G7 already has `precision = 15 min` and an undocumented rule, and the original times are gone. Analysis can censor a raw timestamp; it cannot un-round a lattice value.
3. **Group identifiers.** Write fixture id, begin-group id, folder id, or bulk-submit id onto every child row, with the group’s own start and end. Equality of timestamps then stops being the only way to see a batch. This separates G1’s cycle, G2’s button, G3’s folder, and G6’s form.
4. **Badge distinct from station and login.** Store person, station, and account as three fields at both start and complete. A shared VDI login may remain for access; it cannot remain the only resource key. G5 is unusable for individual utilization until this exists. Do not spread a shared-account interpretation onto named ids that have no such evidence.
5. **Downtime in the same stream and on the same clock.** Safety checks, setup, pause, and resume should be events with the resource id, linked to the queue they block. G8’s safety interval lives in another system, so the 30 min scanner hole can be seen and its cause cannot.
6. **Clock identity and offset.** Queue, PLC, scanner, tablet, MES, and safety should share a synchronized source, and each event should carry `clock_id`. The G8 residual of a few minutes is exactly an unsynced pair of clocks. A stored offset turns that residual into a bound.
7. **Capacity as master data.** Fixture slots, allowed parallel headcount, and “login is shared” belong on the resource record joined to the log. G1’s 4 and G5’s “up to 3” then become checks on measured concurrency instead of comments outside the extract.
8. **Precision metadata kept, and used as censoring width.** Continue storing source precision. Add accuracy where it differs from granularity. Queue analysis should widen G7 by 15 min and should not widen G4 by anything operationally meaningful.

Imputing G7 as 15/5 = 3 min, deleting every equal-timestamp row, or raising `merge_distance` until a preferred batch label appears are not substitutes for these fields.

# 7. Final adjudication

High confidence in the timestamp mechanism: G4, G7, G2, G3, G8, and G6, with the residuals named below. G1 is high confidence that four items share one fixture-cycle interval, and only moderate confidence (74) that motion inside the cycle is parallel. G5 is provisional (68).

Usable, with a stated semantics:

- **G4** can enter queue, service, utilization, throughput, and FIFO conformance as recorded. It is the control that the other patterns are not “just how this plant logs.”
- **G1** can enter case sojourn and fixture-formation wait. Robot utilization is the union, 3 min for this cycle. Per-item touch time needs an intra-cycle trace before it is published. One cycle does not support an hourly rate.
- **G8** service intervals can enter a service-time and productive-throughput calculation (7 min, one piece at a time). Waiting must be split into time-until-resume and FIFO congestion. The safety-check story is the leading cause of the hole and is not confirmed until the clocks are compared.

Correct before any standard time or bottleneck rank:

- **G2:** completions and sojourn are the usable side if a serial reading is accepted; the shared start is not a per-item start. Shadow one session on the begin-group screen before publishing a rebuilt 6–8 min standard time. The parallel reading is still open.
- **G3:** starts and the resulting waits are the usable side if the folder-submit reading is accepted; 11:30 is not four completion times. Same requirement for one observed folder. Do not apply the G2 correction to G3, or the reverse.

Exclude or interval-censor:

- **G6** is out of service-time, utilization, and throughput profiles. The 17:00 stamp is a bulk submission. Naive waits are time-until-stamp, which may mix policy delay and typing delay. Report them only under that name. Dropping the rows entirely hides the activity; keeping service as 0 manufactures both an infinite capacity and a phantom queue bottleneck.
- **G7** is interval-censored. Point service, overlap, summed utilization, and nominal wait (including the negative waits) do not enter queue analysis. The only duration statement the extract supports is a bound inside the quarter-hour, and only under A6.
- **G5** is out of any single-server queue or utilization model. Case-level elapsed intervals may be kept as upper bounds on open time, with resource identity marked unknown. Peak 4 against a cap of 3 is unresolved.

Operational validation before a performance conclusion, in order:

- G6: what the bulk form writes, and whether any handling preceded 17:00.
- G7: the MES rounding rule, in writing, and whether the five measures are serial.
- G5: badge-level identity for that half-hour, and whether open time is touch time.
- G8: the offset between the safety clock and the scanner clock, before the 3 min residual or the safety label is published.
- G1: a fixture trace, only if the decision needs touch time rather than case sojourn.
- G2 and G3: one shadowed session each, because the UI note and the worker remark were not guaranteed to describe this hour.

No plant-level bottleneck order can be read from these eight fragments.
