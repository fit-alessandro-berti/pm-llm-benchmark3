# 1. Data audit and estimands

**Scope.** The table covers 28 calendar days (2026-04-06 to 2026-05-03): 20 weekdays and 8 weekend days. It is a daily aggregate of one queue. It is not an event log, and it cannot establish a full stochastic model on its own.

**Quantities and units**

| Symbol | Definition used here | Units |
|---|---|---|
| A (arrivals) | Cases entering the queue during opening hours | cases/day |
| D (completions) | Cases leaving the system during opening hours; may have arrived on earlier days | cases/day |
| _arr | A / 12 h (arrivals occur during the outage too, so the full 12 h is the denominator) | cases/h |
| _dep | D / effective open hours (720  outage_min) | cases/h |
| L | `mean_wip`, a time-average of 15-min snapshots over opening hours | cases |
| W | Mean sojourn (flow) time, implied by Little's Law as L/ | min or h |
| S | Active handling time per case, excluding waiting. Only median and p90 are given. | min |
| c | `staff_fte`, treated as the number of effective parallel servers | servers |
| _est | Estimated utilisation = D·S / (open minutes × c) | dimensionless |
| Wq | Waiting time = W  S | min |

**Opening-hours convention.** I assume the service is open 07:00–19:00 (720 min) on every calendar day, including weekends (**A1**). Effective open minutes are 720  outage_min. Arrivals are not reduced during the outage, because the hourly vector for 2026-04-15 shows 37 arrivals between 11:00 and 14:00 (**A4**).

**Service-time shape.** The mean S is not supplied. I estimate it with a lognormal fit to the median and p90 (**A2**):

-  = ln(p90/median) / 1.2816
- S  median · exp(²/2)

Because this is an assumption, I report utilisation as a range. The lower bound uses S = median, and the lognormal estimate is the upper-central value.

**Limitations of the aggregates**

1. **Flow non-conservation.** Over the 28 days, (A  D) = 2519  2409 = +110 cases. Mean WIP is 18 on 2026-04-06 and 19 on 2026-05-03. Under conservation, A  D should equal N plus abandonments, which implies about 1 case. Roughly 109 cases are unaccounted for. Within regimes the imbalance is small at baseline (week 1: +1) but large in the mix-shift period (weekdays 2026-04-20 to 04-28: +94).
2. **Peak-share mismatch.** `peak_08_10_pct` cannot be reproduced from the representative hourly vectors. For 2026-04-07 the hourly vector gives 17/101 = 16.8% against a daily value of 31%. For 2026-04-21 it gives 11/115 = 9.6% against 47%. For 2026-04-25 it gives 6/51 = 11.8% against 22%. This is either a definitional difference or a data error, and it must be resolved before any peak-smoothing claim.
3. **Complex-variant step.** `complex_pct` rises from about 22% to about 44% on 2026-04-20, on weekdays and weekends alike. This could be a real case-mix change, a coding or classification change, or a change in how multi-step cases generate arrivals. The data cannot separate these.
4. **Staffing history is not clean.** The intervention is said to begin on 2026-04-29, but `staff_fte` is already 7.0 on 2026-04-16 and 6.5 on 04-17, during the outage recovery. Friday values are also lower (5.5 on 04-10 and 04-24), which suggests a standing schedule.
5. **Medians and percentiles, not means.** Daily median and p90 are per-case distribution summaries, so averaging them across days is not a mean service time. No mean flow time is given, so Little's Law can only be applied as an implied W.
6. **Carry-over and unknown boundary stock.** The 07:00 backlog is not supplied on any day. Completions can belong to earlier arrivals, and WIP can include overnight stock. Each daily L/ therefore has a boundary error of roughly ±|N|/(T).
7. **Outage distortion.** On 2026-04-15, completions are zero from 11:00 to 14:00, so WIP and completions are not representative of normal operation.
8. **Serial dependence and small samples.** Consecutive days share staffing and mix state, so they are not independent. The weekend and outage subsets have n = 1–4 each.
9. **Load-dependent service.** Median service falls from 35 to 33 min on the intervention weekdays while complex share is unchanged. Active time therefore appears to depend on congestion or staffing, which violates the usual assumption of an invariant service distribution.
10. **Coarse precision.** WIP and FTE are rounded to whole numbers or half-units. Snapshot-based WIP gives an average, not a distribution.

**Threats to naive inference (summary list)**

- Pooling across regimes that differ in mix, capacity and day type.
- Unreconciled flow balance (+110 cases).
- Unreconciled peak-share definition.
- Ambiguous complex-variant change: real or coding?
- Staffing changes that predate the logged intervention.
- Carry-over and unknown start-of-day backlog.
- Using median or p90 as if they were means.

# 2. Primary regime segmentation

**Primary segmentation: day type × operating state.** Weekday and weekend are split first, because their capacity levels differ (about 6 FTE against 2.5–3 FTE) and so does their utilisation. Within each, the outage, recovery, mix-shift and intervention states are separated. Boundaries are at day granularity, because the intervention time is unlogged and the outage time is only approximate.

**Assumptions for this table:** A1 (opening hours every day), A2 (lognormal service), A3 (FTE = servers), A4 (arrivals continue during the outage). Arrival and completion rates are per open hour.
