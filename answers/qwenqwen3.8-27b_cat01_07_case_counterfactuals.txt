Temporal propagation: I1 and D1 remain governed by their own queues, so document completion still lands around Tue 05:40 and A1 still starts around Tue 15:10. The intervention changes the compliance tail: if C1 performs 1-3h of preliminary work before or during A1, the final C1 interval can be shorter. Earlier compliance preparation therefore creates earlier readiness, but earlier final completion depends on C1 availability and queue displacement.

Process-feasibility check: The path remains feasible because final C1 still occurs after A1 and before Decision. Preliminary C1 work does not replace final compliance. C1 cannot perform preliminary and final intervals simultaneously, so they must be separate non-overlapping intervals.

Rolling out all four to 100 similar cases per week would not simply multiply single-case savings. K-104’s provisional C1 reservations are the most systemically fragile: C1 is the only certified reviewer, so 100 high-risk cases each reserving 1–3 h of C1 capacity can move the compliance bottleneck from Wednesday to Tuesday or Monday. Even if each target case saves 2 h in final review, displaced urgent cases may gain 0 h or lose several hours, and the net system effect can be negative. Preliminary packets also create a quality nonlinearity: incomplete early evidence may add final C1 rework.

K-219 substitutes D2 for D1. For 100 standard handwritten cases, D1’s queue may shrink, helping complex corporate cases, while D2’s queue grows. If D2’s clarification rate is low, the system gains by moving load off D1; if handwriting difficulty triggers 10–20% rework, external responses of 8–36 h can push cases into the next week and create a new D2/A2 bottleneck. The effect is therefore conditional on D2 accuracy and D1/D2 queue levels.

K-337 removes a rework loop and can cut 24–48 h per case, but it changes the decision policy. At scale, it may reduce D1/A1 load and avoid weekend external delays, yet it shifts pressure to C1 immediately after Assessment 2 and increases appeal/loss exposure. If C1 cannot absorb earlier reviews, the cycle-time saving disappears; if decisions are later reversed, the process may incur longer external rework not visible in the original case path.

K-411 is the safest local change. Parallelizing I2 and D2 can free 2–3 h of verification time, but if 100 cases become assessment-ready earlier, A2/A1 queues may peak earlier and absorb the gain. The critical distinction is timestamp behavior versus true concurrency: real-time logs showing non-overlapping resource intervals are necessary; batch entry can create false concurrency, audit exceptions, and hidden rework. Overall, the rollout should prioritize K-411 and controlled D2 substitution, pilot C1 reservation only with capacity guardrails, and avoid suppressing RMI without decision-quality safeguards.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is K-411, parallelizing identity and document verification. It respects the no-originals concurrency rule, uses separate resources, does not touch the compliance bottleneck, and is easy to reverse. Its main uncertainty is whether earlier verification readiness translates into earlier assessment completion; if A2’s queue absorbs the case, the end-to-end gain is small, but the downside is limited to logging and workload management.

The most dangerous intervention despite potential speed is K-337, suppressing the second Request More Info loop. It can remove a weekend external delay and two D1/A1 intervals, but it changes the allowed decision policy. A decision made on an unresolved discrepancy may be compliant in form if followed by C1, yet it can be substantively weaker, increasing appeal, loss, and remediation risk. At scale, the saved D1/A1 time may be offset by C1 congestion or later external rework.

The local counterfactual is least transportable for K-104. In one case, a provisional C1 slot may save 1–3 hours of final review. In a weekly cohort, C1 is a single scarce resource, so reservations can move the compliance bottleneck earlier and delay other urgent cases. The same is true, though less severely, for K-219 when D2 substitution shifts load from D1 to D2 and creates rework.

The most valuable additional data are event-level C1 queue positions and reservation flags, D2 clarification/rework reasons, assessment start lag after verification readiness, and timestamp integrity metrics. Also useful are external-response lead times by supplier, M1 signing availability, and post-decision appeal/reversal rates. These data would distinguish true cycle-time savings from queue displacement and would show whether a locally faster path creates system-wide interference.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. `structural`: Register/Triage first and Identity/Document before Assessment are hard prerequisites that cannot be skipped in any counterfactual path.
2. `structural`: High-risk/high-value cases require final C1 after the latest relevant Assessment and before Decision; preliminary C1 work does not replace final compliance.
3. `temporal`: Queue estimates are forecasts, not guarantees; actual starts may differ by hours due to priority, arrivals, and resource availability.
4. `resource`: C1 is the only fully certified compliance reviewer in the window, so C1 demand shifts directly affect all high-risk/high-value cases.
5. `resource`: D1 and D2 have different service distributions; D2 may start earlier but can be slower on difficult handwriting.
6. `behavioral`: External suppliers respond slowly and inconsistently; RMI response times can span weekends and are not controlled by internal resources.
7. `quality`: D2 on handwritten standard documents has a higher clarification/rework probability, and suppressing RMI increases appeal, loss, or compliance-defensibility risk.
8. `interference`: Local interventions can change queue positions of non-target cases; earlier readiness may be absorbed by downstream assessment or compliance bottlenecks.
