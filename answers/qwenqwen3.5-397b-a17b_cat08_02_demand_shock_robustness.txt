# 1. Stress scenarios

To ensure robustness, we define four scenarios that stress different parts of the process based on the provided data variants and capacity metrics. These scenarios move beyond simple volume increases to target specific structural weaknesses.

*   **Scenario A: Winter Infection Wave (High Mix Volatility)**
    *   **Description:** Matches the "Peak-period summary" data. Total volume increases ~1.4x, but the mix shifts aggressively toward Urgent (18%  29%) and Complex-panel (13%  20%).
    *   **Stress Point:** Medical Validation and Specialist Review. Complex panels require Medical Validation (3.1x likelihood) and Specialist Review (2.4x likelihood). The data shows Medical Validation queue time exploding from 2h to 22h median in peak conditions.
    *   **Risk:** Urgent cases miss the 18h contractual penalty threshold (Peak p90 is 33h).

*   **Scenario B: Assay Machine Degradation (M2 Failure Spike)**
    *   **Description:** M2 machines experience higher failure rates. During high load, Routine cases routed to M2 (per candidate I3) trigger excessive repeats.
    *   **Stress Point:** Assay Run and Technical Validation. The DFG shows "Assay run -> Repeat assay" increases from 1140 (Normal) to 1260 (Peak). Repeat loops consume capacity twice.
    *   **Risk:** Technical Validation utilization hits 101% in peak; added repeats cause a backlog that blocks Release Result for all case types.

*   **Scenario C: Specialist Absence (The "Friday Gap")**
    *   **Description:** One specialist is unavailable every second Friday. Complex-panel cases accumulate at "Hold for specialist review".
    *   **Stress Point:** Hold for specialist review and Medical Validation. The DFG shows "Hold for specialist review -> Medical validation" drops in efficiency during peaks (median time 18h  48h).
    *   **Risk:** Complex-panel cycle time exceeds 240h (p90 peak). Medical Validators sit idle waiting for Specialist sign-off, then get overwhelmed when the Specialist returns.

*   **Scenario D: Courier Arrival Compression (Intake Spike)**
    *   **Description:** Courier routes converge, causing arrival spikes at 10:00 and 17:00 rather than smooth flow.
    *   **Stress Point:** Registration and Pre-analytical Check. Pre-analytical utilization jumps from 74% to 96%. Queue time jumps from 1.8h to 9.5h.
    *   **Risk:** Early bottlenecks delay sample access for Urgent cases. High pressure on Registration clerks increases error rates (Manual accession correction rises from 500 to 420 absolute, but higher % of total), triggering recollection loops later.

# 2. Bottleneck-migration analysis

Relieving a bottleneck often shifts the constraint downstream. Based on the DFG and queue metrics, here is the migration path for each scenario.

*   **Scenario A (Winter Wave):**
    *   *Primary Bottleneck:* **Medical Validation** (Queue 22h, Util 93%).
    *   *Migration:* If we add Medical Validation capacity (e.g., I5), cases flow faster to Release. However, this exposes the **Specialist Review** bottleneck (Queue 36h peak). If Specialists cannot keep up, Medical Validators will again face starvation followed by floods.
    *   *Secondary Risk:* **Assay Machines** (Util 98%). If Medical Validation clears faster, demand pulls harder on Assay runs. Since Assay is already at 98% utilization, any increase in throughput demand here will cause immediate queue buildup at "Test preparation" (Queue 11h peak).

*   **Scenario B (Machine Degradation):**
    *   *Primary Bottleneck:* **Assay Run / Repeat Loop**.
    *   *Migration:* If we fix M2 reliability or reduce Routine load on M2, Assay throughput improves. This pushes volume into **Technical Validation** (Util 101% peak).
    *   *Secondary Risk:* Technical Validators are already over-utilized. Clearing the Assay queue faster will simply fill the Technical Validation queue (currently 16h median peak) unless Technical Validation capacity is also adjusted.

*   **Scenario C (Specialist Absence):**
    *   *Primary Bottleneck:* **Hold for Specialist Review**.
    *   *Migration:* If we outsource Complex panels (I10) to bypass the Specialist, we remove load from Internal Specialist Review.
    *   *Secondary Risk:* This increases load on **Courier/Logistics** (12h transport delay) and creates a dependency on external **Medical Validation** processes at the partner lab. Internally, it frees Medical Validators, potentially making them underutilized unless Routine/Urgent volume fills the gap.

*   **Scenario D (Courier Compression):**
    *   *Primary Bottleneck:* **Pre-analytical Check** (Queue 9.5h).
    *   *Migration:* If we smooth courier arrivals (I7), Pre-analytical queue drops. This pushes samples faster to **Test Preparation**.
    *   *Secondary Risk:* Test Preparation utilization is 89% in peak. A sudden smooth influx could spike this queue (currently 11h) if batching logic (I2) isn't adjusted dynamically. Also, faster intake means **Recollection** loops (Request recollection -> Receive sample) return faster, potentially clashing with the next courier wave.

# 3. Robust redesign package

The following interventions are selected for robustness across multiple scenarios. They focus on flow stability and waste reduction rather than raw capacity addition.

*   **R1. Early Pre-analytical Defect Triage (Based on I6)**
    *   **Action:** Implement a rapid "fitness-for-purpose" check within 2 hours of receipt, rejecting poor samples before Test Preparation.
    *   **Robustness:** Reduces waste in Assay and Validation steps across all scenarios. In Peak (Scenario A), it prevents high-value resources (Assay/Med Val) from processing doomed samples.
    *   **Downstream Impact:** Reduces load on Assay Run and Technical Validation.
    *   **Upstream Risk:** Increases workload on Pre-analytical Check (already 96% util in peak). Must be balanced with R2.
    *   **Assumption:** Defects can be identified visually or via basic metadata without full assay.

*   **R2. Courier Arrival Smoothing (Based on I7)**
    *   **Action:** Negotiate staggered pickup slots with courier partners to flatten the 10:00/17:00 spikes.
    *   **Robustness:** Directly addresses Scenario D. Prevents the "Queue over 12h" spike (54% of cases in peak) at intake.
    *   **Downstream Impact:** Stabilizes flow into Pre-analytical Check, allowing better staffing planning.
    *   **Upstream Risk:** None internally. External dependency on courier compliance.
    *   **Assumption:** Couriers can adhere to slots without delaying clinic collections.

*   **R3. Dynamic Machine Routing (Modified I3)**
    *   **Action:** Instead of strict dedication (I3), use a threshold-based router. Route Urgent/Complex to M1. Route Routine to M2 *unless* M2 queue > 4h, then spill to M1.
    *   **Robustness:** Protects Urgent SLA (Scenario A) while preventing M2 overload (Scenario B). Avoids starving M1 completely.
    *   **Downstream Impact:** Balances load into Technical Validation.
    *   **Upstream Risk:** Increased scheduling complexity at Test Preparation.
    *   **Assumption:** M1 reliability is significantly higher than M2 (as noted in metrics).

*   **R4. Flexible Medical Validation Shift (Based on I5)**
    *   **Action:** Activate a temporary evening Medical Validation shift during Peak periods or when Complex-panel queue > 10 cases.
    *   **Robustness:** Directly attacks the 22h median queue in Peak (Scenario A) and clears backlog after Specialist absence (Scenario C).
    *   **Downstream Impact:** Accelerates Release Result.
    *   **Upstream Risk:** Requires Technical Validation to hand off cases in the evening. If Tech Val closes at 5 PM, Med Val evening shift sits idle. Must align Tech Val handoff times.
    *   **Assumption:** Staff are available for evening shifts without excessive overtime fatigue.

# 4. Scenario-specific policies

These policies activate only when specific triggers are met to avoid unnecessary complexity or trade-off costs during normal operations.

*   **Policy P1: Urgent Fast-Track (Triggered Activation of I1)**
    *   **Trigger:** Urgent case queue at Test Preparation > 2 hours OR projected cycle time > 14 hours.
    *   **Action:** Preempt Routine batching. Urgent cases bypass Batch Wait immediately.
    *   **Deactivation:** When Urgent queue < 1 hour.
    *   **Reasoning:** Strict Urgent-first (I1) starves Routine cases continuously. Only activate when SLA is at risk.

*   **Policy P2: Cross-Train Surge (Triggered Activation of I4)**
    *   **Trigger:** Accession Correction queue > 10 hours AND Technical Validation queue < 5 hours.
    *   **Action:** Deploy cross-trained Technical Validators to Accession Correction pool.
    *   **Deactivation:** When Accession queue < 4 hours.
    *   **Reasoning:** Tech Val utilization is 101% in peak. Only move them if Accession is the blocking constraint and Tech Val has temporary breathing room.

*   **Policy P3: Complex-Panel Outsourcing (Triggered Activation of I10)**
    *   **Trigger:** Specialist Review queue > 20 cases OR Specialist Absence confirmed.
    *   **Action:** Route new Complex-panel orders to partner lab.
    *   **Deactivation:** When internal Specialist queue < 5 cases.
    *   **Reasoning:** Outsourcing adds 12h transport delay. Only use when internal delay exceeds 12h + processing time.

*   **Policy P4: Batch Size Reduction (Reverse I2)**
    *   **Trigger:** Assay Machine Utilization > 95% AND Urgent Mix > 25%.
    *   **Action:** Reduce Routine batch size from 24 back to 12.
    *   **Deactivation:** When Utilization < 85%.
    *   **Reasoning:** Large batches (I2) efficiency is outweighed by Urgent delays during high-mix volatility. Smaller batches allow more frequent Urgent insertion.

# 6. Residual vulnerabilities

Despite the redesign, specific fragilities remain:

1.  **The "Repeat-after-Failure" Black Hole:**
    *   **Vulnerability:** Repeat cases consume assay capacity twice and cluster after night shifts. The redesign reduces initial defects (R1) but does not eliminate assay failures. During Scenario B (Machine Degradation), repeat loops can still consume >15% of capacity (based on 1260 repeats in peak DFG).
    *   **Impact:** Unpredictable capacity loss that standard staffing cannot absorb.

2.  **Cross-Training Expertise Dilution (Caused by Redesign):**
    *   **Vulnerability:** Policy P2 moves Technical Validators to Accession Correction. While this relieves the Accession queue, it risks increasing error rates in Accession Correction (less specialized) and creates a "hollow core" in Technical Validation if the surge lasts too long.
    *   **Impact:** Potential increase in downstream Technical Validation rework if Accession Corrections are done incorrectly by non-specialists.

3.  **Outsourcing Traceability Gap:**
    *   **Vulnerability:** Policy P3 relies on a partner lab. While it relieves Specialist Review, the 12h transport delay adds variability. If the partner lab experiences their own outage, visibility is lost.
    *   **Impact:** Loss of control over Complex-panel SLAs during regional-wide incidents.

4.  **Medical Validator Dependency:**
    *   **Vulnerability:** R4 adds evening shifts, but Medical Validation is still a single point of failure for Urgent and Complex cases. If the evening staff call out sick during a Peak period, the queue will double overnight.
    *   **Impact:** Catastrophic SLA failure for Urgent cases overnight.
