## 3. Causal-abductive explanation
```text
UI_release: recorded_labels, technical_events
policy_change: true_process, normative_model
ETL_change: recorded_order
reviewer_behavior: true_process
true_process: recorded_labels, recorded_order, business_outcomes
technical_events: recorded_labels, recorded_order
recorded_labels: alignment_moves
recorded_order: alignment_moves
normative_model: alignment_moves, measured_fitness
alignment_moves: measured_fitness
measured_fitness: governance_actions
business_outcomes: governance_actions, detection_quality
detection_quality: measured_fitness
```

The conformance collapse is best explained by interacting mechanisms along measurement, modeling, and behavior. The UI release relabels `Classify` as `Risk Screen` and the gateway begins emitting `Queue Routed`. These change recorded labels and add technical events while the true process for classification and routing remains mostly stable. Simultaneously, the ETL pipeline starts using warehouse-ingestion time for `Decision` and `Notify`, corrupting recorded order for some cases. When these altered logs are aligned against an unchanged normative model, the algorithm registers many extra or out-of-order moves on the log, producing a sharp drop in measured fitness even though the underlying control flow has not collapsed. Later, the high-value sign-off policy genuinely updates the true process, but the normative model is not extended, creating further apparent deviations specifically in sign-off traces. Reviewer behavior (occasional manual overrides and possible bypasses of checks) may contribute localized real noncompliance, yet business outcomes and appeals remain stable, suggesting these are not the dominant driver of global fitness changes. Crucially, unchanged outcomes neither prove that the data are wrong nor guarantee perfect compliance: measurement defects can mask real pockets of risk, while policy-driven additions can look like noncompliance until the model and log semantics are harmonized.

def variants_to_log_string(variants_dict, prefix):
    """
    Build a simple CSV-like string for pm4py.parse_event_log_string.
    Format (per line): case_id,activity,timestamp
    Here we use a synthetic integer timestamp based on the event position.
    """
    lines = []
    case_id = 0
    for variant, count in variants_dict.items():
        activities = [a.strip() for a in variant.split(",")]
        for _ in range(count):
            case_id += 1
            for pos, act in enumerate(activities):
                # use position as a stand-in timestamp
                ts = pos + 1
                lines.append(f"{prefix}_{case_id},{act},{ts}")
    return "\n".join(lines)

def normalize_log(log, remove_queue_routed=False):
    """
    Return a deep-copied log where:
    - 'Risk Screen' is relabeled to 'Classify'
    - 'Queue Routed' events can optionally be removed
    """
    norm_log = deepcopy(log)
    for trace in norm_log:
        new_events = []
        for event in trace:

                name = "Classify"
            if remove_queue_routed and name == "Queue Routed":
                continue
            new_events.append(event)
        trace.clear()
        for e in new_events:
            trace.append(e)
    return norm_log

## 7. Provisional conclusion
The primary explanation for the measured conformance collapse is a combination of logging and extraction changes, plus a lag in updating the normative model, rather than a genuine breakdown of the claims process. The introduction of `Risk Screen`, `Queue Routed`, and warehouse-based timestamps, together with unchanged business KPIs and large fitness gains after normalization, all point to technical-event artifacts, label semantics, and ordering defects as the dominant drivers of the apparent fitness deterioration. The August sign-off policy then creates a real, policy-driven extension of the process that the control-flow model does not capture, further lowering measured fitness for otherwise reasonable behavior until the model is updated. Residual anomalies—enhanced reviews without logged checks and some use of `Manual Override`—may indicate pockets of real noncompliance or missing events, but they are numerically modest and not yet tied to worse business outcomes.

My confidence is high (around 85%) that measurement artifacts and model obsolescence jointly explain most of the drop in conformance metrics. I have moderate confidence (around 50%) that a non-trivial, but localized, compliance risk remains around missing checks and overrides, because these traces persist after normalization and coincide with a supervisor memo. Immediate IT actions should include hardening event semantics (explicit contracts for technical vs business events), fixing ETL ordering, and implementing a tested normalization pipeline. Process owners should update and version the normative model to include high-value sign-off, then re-baseline fitness and precision on normalized logs. Compliance should run targeted audits on all enhanced-review cases without recorded checks, focusing on high-value and reviewer hotspots, and avoid either clearing the process or alleging systemic misconduct until audit evidence is in. What must not yet be claimed is that the process is either fully compliant (because residual deviations are unexplained) or broadly noncompliant (because many deviations were clearly measurement artifacts and incidents detection is incomplete).

## 8. Model-governance recommendation
1. Maintain explicit, versioned normative models that are updated whenever policies, thresholds (such as the EUR 50,000 sign-off rule), or major routing rules change, and record which model version was used for each conformance run.  
2. Establish formal event-semantic contracts between operations, IT, and analytics that define each activity label, its business meaning, and whether UI relabeling or backend refactoring is allowed without prior process-mining impact assessment.  
3. Define and enforce a technical-event policy that classifies events like `Queue Routed` and certain overrides as non-executive or auxiliary, documenting how they should be logged, filtered, or annotated before process mining.  
4. Govern timestamp and ordering provenance by specifying authoritative sources (e.g., source-system sequence numbers), forbidding silent switches to warehouse-ingestion time, and routinely checking for order anomalies such as `Notify` preceding `Decision`.  
5. Encode critical data-aware constraints—such as high-value status for Supervisor Sign-off—either in the process model (via guards or attributes) or in complementary monitors, and never rely on control-flow-only models to prove adherence to value-based policies.  
6. After every major release or policy change, run a post-release validation cycle that compares pre/post DFGs, variants, fitness, and precision on a provenance-checked log, coupled with a small manual audit sample, before using new conformance numbers for compliance assertions.
