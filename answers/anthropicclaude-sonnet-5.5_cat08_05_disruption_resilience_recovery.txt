# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

**Definition in this case.**

- **Resilience** is maintaining the critical function (hospital supply) while preserving each essential control objective, by an alternative mechanism that is:
  - explicit and visible in the log;
  - scoped and time-boxed;
  - evidenced at the time of action;
  - reversible or reconcilable to equal quality;
  - owned by someone accountable.
- **Concealed noncompliance** maintains the *appearance* of performance (cycle time, dispatch counts, invoice closure) while the control or its evidence disappears, and the debt is pushed downstream. The visible metrics improve while the assurance worsens.

**Tests used.** (1) Is the control objective preserved by an equivalent? (2) Is the deviation declared and bounded? (3) Can evidence be recreated at equal quality? (4) Who bears the residual risk (patients, regulators)? (5) Does the cycle-time metric hide it?

**Classification**

| Category | Adaptations | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **True resilience** | B (manual authorization), F (release control, with gaps), J (backlog prioritization, if formalized), K (reconciliation), A (hospital priority, if authorization was provisional) | They preserved a control objective by an alternative, with the deviation visible. A's priority logic matches mission. |
| **Risky shortcuts / concealed noncompliance** | G (dispatch without scan), C (skip authorization as practised), D after the early window, H for CS, E without lot capture, and L when used as a substitute for real-time control | They removed controls or evidence. G explains the largest traceability gap. D's effect appears later as reconciliation and cancellations. The fast path still completed cycle time, so the damage appears only as downstream exceptions. |
| **Ambiguous (depends on risk appetite)** | C (for ambient, known customers), I (deferral), H (non-CS), sampling in F for ambient, L (audit remediation for low-risk cases), cancellations in J | Acceptable if governed by explicit criteria, and unacceptable if informal. The decision depends on regulatory tolerance and on the patient impact of delays versus documentation gaps. |

**The critical distinction.** The log shows "Skip system authorization check" and "Dispatch without scan" as labelled deviations, so they are visible in the event log. What is *concealed* is what is absent: temperature records, dual-control, dispatch custody, and lot-level recall traceability. Each has no activity, so it cannot be seen as a deviation, only as a move-on-model gap. The hospital outcome (91% fill, near-baseline cycle) is a real achievement, but it cannot be used to excuse the unobserved control loss. No adaptation in A-L specifically protected recall-sensitive items, which is a notable gap: stale snapshots and unscanned dispatches make recall execution unreliable.

**Hospital-critical service was real, but not proof of safe operation.** Throughput preserved alongside a 14.5× rise in CS dual-control failures is risk transfer, not resilience.

# 4. Recovery process model

**Notation.** `` sequence, `×` exclusive choice, `` parallel, `` loop, `[mode]` attribute on events. All manual activities keep the *standard activity name* and carry attributes `mode=manual`, `provenance=native|reconstructed`, `evidence_ref`. This keeps process mining comparable and avoids a new vocabulary that hides deviations.

### 4.1 Trigger conditions and modes

| Mode | Trigger | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| **Mode 0 Normal** | Scan success 98%, inventory sync variance <0.5% | — |
| **Mode 1 Degraded** | Scan success <90% for 15 min, or sync latency >2h, or variance >2% on cycle counts | Shift lead plus QA |
| **Mode 2 Manual continuity** | WMS unavailable >30 min, or security declares a cyber incident | Incident commander with **two-key** sign-off from Quality/Responsible Person |
| **Mode 3 Recovery/reconcile** | Intake/WMS partly back but sync or scanning unreliable | Incident commander |
| **Exit to Mode 0** | Scan 98% and variance <0.5% for 24h, evidence debt below threshold | Quality sign-off |

Every deviation has an **expiry time** and an owner, and is reviewed every 6h.

### 4.2 Triage rules

1. **Priority class.** P1 = Hospital-critical, plus any CS/cold-chain for critical care. P2 = Hospital routine and Pharmacy-standard. P3 = Wholesale-bulk.
2. **Capacity reservation.** Reserve about 20% of capacity for P2/P3 so they are not starved (T2 Pharmacy fill fell to 61%).
3. **Deferral.** Defer only P3 first; defer P2 only when P1 is at risk, with customer notice within 4h and a maximum deferral age.
4. **Authorization.** Use provisional authorization via pre-approved rosters (hospitals and registered standing-authorization customers). Anything outside the roster requires manual authorization (B).
5. **Stop rule.** An order that cannot meet the minimum controls for its product class is held, not shipped. This applies to CS and cold-chain even for hospitals, with an escalation path.

### 4.3 Minimum controls by product/customer risk

| Product | Authorization | Inventory | Release | Dispatch/custody | POD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ambient** | Roster or standing register; verify within 24h | Snapshot 6h old, holdback buffer | Risk-based sample | Manual manifest (lot/tote, time, signature) | Structured callback, entered within 24h |
| **Cold-chain** | Manual or roster check before dispatch | Fresh count of lot/expiry | 100% pharmacist/QA release including logger ID and temperature reading | Manifest with temperature at handoff, courier acceptance, transit limit | Receiver temperature/condition confirmed |
| **Controlled-substance** | Full manual authorization, no standing-assumption path | Count verified by two persons | 100% pharmacist release **plus** independent second person (true dual control) | Two-person sign-off, seal, named courier ID | Signed e-POD or photo of signature with recipient ID. Phone POD not acceptable |
| **Recall-sensitive** | As above | Exclude lots unless recall status confirmed from the latest recall list | 100% lot check | Lot-level manifest | Lot/time recorded for recall trace |

**Customer modifiers.** Hospital-critical: may ship on provisional authorization, but product-class controls are unchanged. Pharmacy and Wholesale: standard controls; Wholesale may wait for full verification.

### 4.4 Manual evidence capture

- An **evidence kit** is pre-staged: serialized paper forms, locked-template ledger, and devices for timestamped photos of labels, totes, and temperature loggers.
- An **append-only evidence ledger** per case captures case ID, activity, timestamp, actor, lot/expiry, temperature and signatures.
- **Two-person rule** for CS and cold-chain entries.
- A **case evidence-completeness score** is computed at pack and dispatch. Tier 1 cases (CS/cold-chain/recall-sensitive) cannot dispatch below 100%.
- A **no evidence, no compliance close** gate separates *operational close* (delivered) from *compliance close* (evidence complete). Invoice close for high-risk tiers waits for compliance close. Low-risk tiers may close with an evidence-debt flag and a due date.

### 4.5 Backlog prioritization

Score = clinical criticality + product risk (cold-chain remaining shelf-life, CS) + age + evidence debt + customer commitment. Rules are **published** and logged. An **age cap** escalates any order over 48h, so orders cannot sit behind the p95. **Customer consent** is required for cancellation, and alternate sourcing or partial fill is offered first. Backlog work starts in T3, not T4.

### 4.6 Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow (POWL-style)

```
Mode3 = ( R1_reconcile_inventory , R2_reconstruct_evidence , R3_adjudicate )   R4_close
R1 = Freeze stale snapshot  cycle-count high-risk SKUs first  Reconcile manual reservation  release to Pick
R2 = match manual ledger to system events  tag provenance   (gap found  retrieve source evidence)
R3 = × ( evidence recovered | compensating evidence accepted | unrecoverable  deviation/CAPA,
         customer/regulator notification assessment, recall-readiness check )
R4 = QA sign-off  Compliance close  Invoice close (high-risk) / close with debt flag (low-risk)
```

- **Run in parallel with operations** from the start of T3, with a dedicated remediation team sized from expected volume (T2+T3 excess exceptions  1,000+ cases, plus the T4 tail).
- **SLAs:** CS and cold-chain within 24-72h, recall-sensitive immediate, ambient within 2 weeks.
- **Unrecoverable evidence** is reported as a deviation and never recreated to look native.

# 5. Resilience indicators

| Dimension | Leading indicators (predictive, real-time) | Lagging indicators (outcome) |
|---|---|---|
| **Throughput** | Orders picked/packed per hour against plan; manual-lane queue length | **Evidence-complete dispatches** per day (not raw dispatches) vs baseline; end-to-end completion rate (T2: ~37.5% of cases reached END via invoice vs ~88% baseline) |
| **Service protection** | Hospital-critical order age at each stage; stock-out alerts for P1 | Hospital fill rate (target 90%); Hospital p90 1.5× baseline |
| **Traceability** | Scan-equivalent capture rate at handoff; % reconstructed vs native events | Traceability exception rate (baseline 0.8%; T2 9.5%, T4 2.2%); recall-trace time for a sample lot |
| **Control completeness** | % Tier-1 orders with authorization evidence before dispatch; CS dual-control captured at release; temperature logger ID at pack | Missing temperature documentation (baseline 0.4%); missing CS dual-control (baseline 0.2%); % dispatches without release check |
| **Backlog age** | Oldest order age; backlog growth rate; % over age cap | Backlog p90 age; Backlog  Manual pick list vs p95; time to clear backlog |
| **Rework** | Repack/recheck rate; compliance-hold rate per hour | Rework/correction (baseline 3.5%; T2 12.5%); reconciliation cancellations |
| **Fairness** | Capacity share to P2/P3 vs reserve; deferral count by segment | Fill-rate and cancellation **ratio** across segments (Pharmacy 61% vs Hospital 91%, a 30-point gap); cancellations with documented customer consent |
| **Evidence debt** | Open evidence gaps by age; remediation team utilization | Audit-remediation queue size and age; % closed cases later reopened; time to compliance close |
| **Process conformance** | Share of cases in declared vs undeclared variants | Fitness (0.96  0.71  0.82  0.90) and % fitting traces (91  44  59  76) |

**Interpretation rule.** Never declare recovery on cycle time alone. Recovery requires cycle times, control rates, and the evidence-debt queue all to be at or near baseline.

# 6. Recommendations for future disruptions

| # | Recommendation | Effort | Expected benefit | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Replace "Dispatch without scan" with a mandatory manual custody manifest** (lot/tote, time, temperature, signatures, seal) | Low | Closes the largest traceability gap; enables recall tracing; cheap to implement | Manual errors; relies on courier cooperation |
| 2 | **Hard stop rules for Tier-1 products** (CS, cold-chain, recall-sensitive): no dispatch without authorization, 100% release, true dual control and temperature evidence | Low-Med | Prevents the CS (2.9%) and cold-chain (4.8%) failure spikes; clear accountability | Possible delays to critical hospital orders; needs escalation path and pre-authorized hospital roster |
| 3 | **Formal incident modes with two-key trigger, expiry on each deviation, and a deviation register** | Low-Med | Makes adaptations visible and bounded; avoids informal bypass | Incident commander bottleneck; requires drills |
| 4 | **Pre-approved standing-authorization register and offline authorization lists** (versioned), with 24h post-verification, replacing ad hoc "skip" | Medium | Keeps ambient throughput without the authorization gap; reduces pressure on compliance staff | Register staleness; customer status changes inside the window |
| 5 | **Evidence kit and ledger** (serialized forms, photo capture, provenance flag) plus a compliance-close gate before invoice close for high-risk tiers | Medium | Reduces evidence debt, and turns audit remediation from recreation to confirmation | Reconstructed evidence is still weaker than real-time; staff burden |
| 6 | **Decay-based manual inventory control**: snapshot age limits, holdback, cycle-count high-risk SKUs, start reconciliation in T3 | Medium | Fewer overcommits and late cancellations (T4: 1,400 reconciled, 300 cancelled) | Some stock-outs and under-utilization |
| 7 | **Formal triage and fairness rules**: capacity reserve for P2/P3, age caps, customer consent for cancellation, alternate sourcing | Medium | Narrows the Pharmacy/Hospital gap (61% vs 91% fill); reduces cancellations and complaints | Reduced hospital headroom under extreme constraint |
| 8 | **Dedicated remediation team and evidence-debt dashboard**; operational vs compliance close; report evidence debt as a KPI alongside cycle time | Medium-High | Prevents the hidden long tail (2,300 audit cases, ~200 open) and gives honest recovery status | Some evidence unrecoverable, so regulator or customer notification may still be needed |
| 9 | **Logging improvements**: product-tier, temperature, and dual-control events as explicit activities; resilience drills that test the manual mode | Medium-High | Makes concealed gaps visible in conformance checking; validates readiness | Drill fatigue; cost |

# 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should **accept** prioritising hospital-critical supply, time-boxed deferral of bulk orders, slower pharmacy and wholesale service, and provisional authorization for rostered hospitals and registered ambient customers, provided each is declared, bounded and followed by verification. It should **not accept** unscanned dispatch without a custody record, release of controlled-substance, cold-chain or recall-sensitive products without real-time evidence and true dual control, stale-inventory reservations beyond a defined age, or declaring recovery based on cycle time while the audit-remediation queue and exception rates stay above baseline. Audit remediation is a recovery tool, not a substitute for real-time compliance, because a missed temperature reading or dual-control cannot be recreated. The recommendation would change if evidence showed that: (a) skipped-authorization cases were all ambient and later verified with no authorization failures, which would justify a broader standing-authorization path; (b) CS and cold-chain failures were concentrated in a few paths (for example, the 50% unreleased orders), which would narrow the fix; (c) delays from hard stop rules risked patient harm, which would call for regulator-agreed emergency provisions; or (d) the audit queue turns out to be larger than the reported exception rates, which would argue for more remediation capacity and possible regulator notification.
