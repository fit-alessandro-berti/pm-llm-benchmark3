Site A's before/after contrast was shifted by co-occurring changes (`S_API_latency`, `S_queue_huddles`, `S_document_capacity`, and `S_case_complexity`) that are not part of the proposed Site B change. The primary effect modifiers of `treatment_parallelization` are `originals_required`, `digital_documents`, `IT_case_lock`, `role_specialization`, `case_complexity`, `assessment_capacity`, and `legal_capacity`. When `originals_required` or `IT_case_lock` binds, `treatment_parallelization` fails to generate lawful `true_overlap`. Even when `true_overlap` rises, scarce `assessment_capacity` or `legal_capacity` converts upstream verification speedups into downstream queueing rather than net `cycle_time` reduction.

Consequently, a similar control-flow diagram does not guarantee transportability: control-flow syntax captures allowable task orders, not data-dependent legal prerequisites, IT locking, resource silos, queueing saturation, or case-mix shifts that determine realized causal effects.

## 6. Candidate Site B intervention

Rather than copying Site A's blanket rollout, Site B should implement a **Segmented, Lock-Refactored Partial-Order Workflow** governed by the following process specification:

1. **Eligibility rules:**
   - At `Register`, automated triage classifies each case on two mandatory data attributes: `originals_required` (`True` = 62%, `False` = 38%) and `legal_required` (`True` = 40%, `False` = 60%), plus `complexity_tier` (`standard` vs `high`).
   - **Pathway D (Digital-Only, 38%):** Eligible for concurrent `Identity Check` and `Document Check` immediately after `Register` (phased: standard-complexity non-legal cases first; high-complexity and legal-review digital cases second).
   - **Pathway O (Originals-Required, 62%):** Ineligible for pre-receipt `Document Check`; eligible only for concurrent `Identity Check` and `Originals Receipt` after `Register`, with `Document Check` strictly gated until `Originals Receipt` completes.
2. **Concurrent task creation and execution:**
   - In **Pathway D**, `Register` spawns two independent child verification sub-tasks—`Identity Check` and `Document Check`—assigned simultaneously to their respective role queues.
   - In **Pathway O**, `Register` spawns `Identity Check` and opens the `Originals Receipt` tracking milestone concurrently; `Document Check` is auto-instantiated only upon verified completion of `Originals Receipt`.
   - If `Identity Check` flags a disqualifying error or identity mismatch, an automated event alert immediately notifies the assigned `Document Check` owner (or pauses an unstarted `Document Check`) to prevent wasted work.
3. **Statutory prerequisites remaining mandatory:**
   - `Originals Receipt < Document Check` is strictly enforced by the workflow engine for all `originals_required = True` cases (zero override permitted).
   - `Identity Check < Assessment` and `Document Check < Assessment` remain mandatory AND-join prerequisites for 100% of cases.
   - `Assessment < Legal Review` remains mandatory for all `legal_required = True` cases prior to final `Approve` or `Reject`.
4. **Queue and WIP controls:**
   - Institute per-reviewer WIP caps (4–5 active tasks per Identity or Document reviewer) to prevent multitasking inflation.
   - Add an **Assessment-paced release throttle (CONWIP guardrail)**: when the pre-Assessment queue exceeds a calibrated threshold (e.g., >24 h median wait or >1.5x baseline queue count), prioritize already-started verification completions and temporarily throttle new non-urgent dual-task releases rather than flooding Assessment.
   - Adopt Site A's low-cost **daily 15-minute cross-role queue huddle** across Identity, Document, Assessment, and Legal leads to clear blocked cases and rebalance queue priorities.
5. **Resource responsibilities:**
   - Preserve statutory role specialization (4 Identity, 7 Document, 3 Assessment, 2 Legal reviewers); no unauthorized cross-role sign-off.
   - Assign clear case-level ownership for triage/exception routing at `Register` and for post-verification completeness verification before `Assessment`.
6. **Handling of legal-review and supplement loops:**
   - When `Legal Review` issues `Request Supplement`, the case re-enters a scoped supplement path: if supplementary physical originals are legally required, `Originals Receipt` precedes supplementary `Document Check`; otherwise supplementary `Document Check` starts immediately.
   - Completed supplement checks must pass through `Assessment` and return to `Legal Review` before `Approve` or `Reject`. Supplement-loop cases receive aging priority in `Document Check` and `Assessment` to protect the p90 tail from starvation.
7. **Logging requirements:**
   - Refactor the legacy case-management system to support **task-level ownership locks** (separate lockable child sub-tasks under one parent case ID) instead of whole-case locking.
   - Automatically record immutable `assign`, `start`, and `complete` timestamps, `resource_id`, `role`, `case_id`, `originals_required`, `legal_required`, `complexity_tier`, and an explicit `manual_back_entry_flag` (with batch back-entry restricted to audited exceptions).

**Compact textual POWL-style description (partial-order specification):**

```text
PO_SiteB = StrictPartialOrder(
    Nodes = {
        Register,
        Identity_Check,
        Opt_Originals = XOR(Originals_Receipt, tau),     # Originals_Receipt iff originals_required=True; tau iff Digital-Only
        Document_Check,
        Assessment,
        Opt_Legal     = XOR(tau, Legal_Loop),            # Legal_Loop iff legal_required=True; tau otherwise
        Decision      = XOR(Approve, Reject),
        Notify
    },
    Order (<) = {
        Register      < Identity_Check,
        Register      < Opt_Originals,
        Opt_Originals < Document_Check,
        Identity_Check < Assessment,
        Document_Check < Assessment,
        Assessment    < Opt_Legal,
        Opt_Legal     < Decision,
        Decision      < Notify
    }
)

where:
Legal_Loop = LOOP(
    do   = Legal_Review,
    redo = StrictPartialOrder(
        Nodes = { Request_Supplement, Opt_Supp_Originals = XOR(Originals_Receipt, tau), Document_Check_Supp, Assessment_Supp },
        Order = { Request_Supplement < Opt_Supp_Originals < Document_Check_Supp < Assessment_Supp }
    )
)
```

*(Note: Under `Opt_Originals = tau` [Pathway D], `Identity_Check` and `Document_Check` are unordered and fully concurrent after `Register`, synchronizing at `Assessment`. Under `Opt_Originals = Originals_Receipt` [Pathway O], `Identity_Check` and `Originals_Receipt` may proceed concurrently after `Register`, while `Document_Check` strictly awaits `Originals_Receipt` and `Assessment` awaits both checks.)*

**Separating mechanism failure from implementation failure:**
Implementation failure occurs when the intended parallel partial order is never faithfully executed—for instance, because residual IT locks serialize work, reviewers wait informally for `Identity Check` to finish before opening `Document Check`, or off-system back-entry corrupts event logs. Mechanism failure occurs when faithful parallel execution *is* achieved yet fails to improve—or actively degrades—net operational outcomes because upstream speedup shifts queues into the three-FTE Assessment pool or raises rework and supplement loops.

The pilot separates these regimes through staged instrumentation and instrumental-variable process-mining diagnostics. Phase 0 verifies lock-free multi-owner execution and authentic `start`/`complete` logging prior to randomization. During the randomized pilot, conformance checking measures realized compliance—the proportion of treated digital cases achieving verified, non-back-entered `true_overlap` without statutory violations. Comparing intention-to-treat (ITT) effects against complier average causal effects (CACE, using random assignment as an instrument for `true_overlap`) distinguishes the two failures: low realized overlap with a null ITT signals **implementation failure** (fix IT routing or queue prompts before judging concurrency), whereas high realized overlap (>65%) accompanied by null or negative net cycle-time effects, rising `waiting_before_assessment`, or elevated `rework` signals **mechanism failure**, warranting workflow modification or abandonment.

## 9. Recommendation

`conditional go`

Site B should pursue a **conditional go** for a segmented, IT-enabled pilot rather than copying Site A's blanket intervention. What should be transferred from Site A is the *structural principle* of concurrent `Identity Check` and `Document Check` strictly where no statutory prerequisite blocks `Document Check`, paired with operational hygiene—per-reviewer WIP caps, daily cross-role queue huddles, and automated task-level timestamping. What must **not** be copied is management's unsegmented proposal to spawn both verification tasks immediately after `Register` for every case, Site A's big-bang rollout without a control group, or Site A's confounded nine-hour median reduction as a performance target.

Implementation must begin with a mandatory **Phase 0** technical prerequisite: refactoring the legacy single-owner IT case lock into lockable verification sub-tasks and validating authentic `start`/`complete` timestamp logging. Once Phase 0 passes conformance audits, **Phase 1** should enroll only low- and medium-complexity **digital-only** cases that do not require `Legal Review` (a clean subset of the 38% non-originals volume). Originals-required cases (62%) must remain permanently gated by `Originals Receipt -> Document Check`, while high-complexity and legal-review digital cases should enter only in **Phase 2** after Phase 1 demonstrates safe quality and stable downstream queues.

Expected benefits are meaningful but modest at the site level: roughly 2.5–4.2 hours of median cycle-time reduction within treated digital cases, translating to approximately 0.8–1.8 hours across Site B overall. The principal risks are bottleneck shifting into the three-FTE Assessment and two-FTE Legal pools, p90 tail inflation if concurrency elevates rework or `Request Supplement` loops, and statutory or audit breaches if lock workarounds persist.

Decision triggers should be pre-committed: **expand** to all digital-only strata if Phase 1 achieves >=65% genuine overlap, >=2.0 hours median reduction in treated digital cases, <=+1.5 pp rework change, and no p90 Assessment-wait deterioration; **modify** (adding Assessment-paced CONWIP throttling or staffing adjustments) if verification shortens reliably but Assessment queues absorb the savings; and **abandon** if IT lock reliability cannot keep workarounds below 5%, if any statutory ordering violation occurs, or if rework rises >=+4 pp or p90 cycle time degrades materially despite faithful execution.

## 10. Assumption register

1. **Digital-only cases (38%) have no hidden statutory or data dependency requiring `Identity Check` to complete before `Document Check` starts** — *why needed:* parallelization is only lawful and operationally valid if both tasks can begin concurrently after `Register` on digital claims — *how to test:* legal/compliance sign-off plus audit of 200 historical digital claims checking how often `Document Check` relies on outputs produced by `Identity Check` — *consequence if false:* even the 38% digital segment cannot be safely parallelized without redesigning task inputs or risking compliance violations.
2. **The legacy case-management system can be refactored in Phase 0 to support task-level ownership locks and accurate real-time timestamps at reasonable cost** — *why needed:* single-owner whole-case locking physically blocks true concurrency and drives off-system back-entry — *how to test:* IT architecture prototype and 4-week Phase 0 shadow stress test measuring lock-conflict and back-entry rates — *consequence if false:* attempting parallelization triggers Scenario 4 (IT-lock failure), degrading both cycle time and audit integrity and forcing a `no-go`.
3. **Site B's 62% originals-required cases normally complete `Identity Check` (median 3.4 h) before `Originals Receipt` (median 18 h) arrives** — *why needed:* justifies excluding originals cases from Document-Check parallelization and treating 38% as the primary eligible share for concurrency gains — *how to test:* process-mining audit of timestamped intervals comparing `Identity Check` completion against `Originals Receipt` arrival across the 2,108 originals cases — *consequence if false:* if `Identity Check` frequently stalls past `Originals Receipt`, an additional low-risk improvement exists by expediting `Identity Check` during the originals wait.
4. **The identity-failure or early-rejection rate during `Identity Check` is low enough (<5%) that concurrent `Document Check` does not waste substantial Document Reviewer capacity** — *why needed:* parallelizing verification forfeits the sequential early-exit option that avoids an 8.1 h `Document Check` when identity verification fails — *how to test:* quantify historical share of cases aborted, rejected, or materially altered by `Identity Check` findings before `Document Check` — *consequence if false:* wasted labor overloads the 7-FTE Document Reviewer pool, increasing queues and potentially lengthening cycle times for all cases.
5. **Reported median service times (3.4 h Identity, 8.1 h Document) reflect elapsed task durations rather than continuous dedicated single-FTE labor hours, and verification pools have modest slack to absorb dual-task concurrency without severe multitasking penalties** — *why needed:* ensures that introducing simultaneous verification tasks does not immediately push Identity (4 FTE) or Document (7 FTE) reviewers into hyper-congestion — *how to test:* time-motion sampling and active-vs-elapsed duration analysis alongside per-reviewer utilization measurements during Phase 0 — *consequence if false:* WIP rises without throughput relief, lengthening `Document Check` service times and erasing overlap gains.
6. **Site B's 3-FTE Assessment pool and 2-FTE Legal pool operate below critical saturation (<90% effective utilization) so that smoother or slightly earlier verification arrivals do not trigger runaway queueing** — *why needed:* upstream parallelization only reduces end-to-end cycle time if downstream bottlenecks do not absorb the entire gain or blow up tail waits — *how to test:* measure Assessment and Legal utilization, arrival burstiness, and queue-length elasticity during Phase 0 and Phase 1 — *consequence if false:* triggers Scenario 3 (bottleneck shift), where verification looks faster but p90 cycle time worsens until downstream capacity is expanded or intake is throttled.
7. **Concurrent execution of `Identity Check` and `Document Check` does not materially increase defect rates, `Request Supplement` loops, or 90-day appeals once cross-check exception alerts are implemented** — *why needed:* Site A's slight rise in rework (+0.6 pp) and appeals (+0.3 pp) amid falling complexity warns of a possible quality penalty that Site B's legal-supplement loop would amplify — *how to test:* track rework, supplement-loop incidence, and QA audit error rates across T1 vs T0 in Phase 1 and Phase 2 with pre-specified stop rules — *consequence if false:* triggers Scenario 5 (legal/rework congestion), overloading Assessment and Legal pools and forcing abandonment or restriction to simple non-legal cases.
8. **Queueing spillover (interference) between randomized digital treatment and control cases within shared pools is small enough to be bounded using arrival queue-depth covariates and the originals-required sentinel stratum** — *why needed:* case-level randomization within shared server pools violates strict SUTVA, so valid causal inference requires spillover to be modest and measurable — *how to test:* monitor sentinel originals-required waiting times and compare treatment/control differences across low-load vs high-load weeks; switch to weekly cluster-crossover if spillover is large — *consequence if false:* case-level RCT estimates are biased (either understating or overstating net site-wide benefits), requiring a cluster-crossover design before scaling.
