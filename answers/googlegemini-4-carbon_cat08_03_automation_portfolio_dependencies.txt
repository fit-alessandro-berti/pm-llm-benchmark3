# 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

Automation candidates form a tightly coupled system governed by five structural dependencies:

### 1. Upstream data quality dependencies
* **Intake to Checklists (`A2 -> A5 & A7`):** Product and exposure tier determine which financial documents are required (`A5`) and whether collateral valuation applies (71% of Equipment finance, 18% of Working-capital; `A7`). Even without `A2` ML automation, `A5` requires deterministic intake rules so customers are not nudged for irrelevant documents.
* **Document Completeness to Extraction (`A5 -> A6`):** `Financial document review -> Request missing documents` occurs 3,300 times with a 52% repeat rate. Deploying `A6` (Financial-statement extraction) without `A5` causes extraction pipelines to fail or run multiple times on partial uploads, wasting analyst review effort.
* **Upstream Artifact Reconciliation to Committee & Contracts (`A4 + A6 + A7 -> A8 -> A9`):** `Committee packaging` is a hard synchronization join waiting for KYC status, normalized financials, collateral valuation, and credit notes. `A8` (Committee pack auto-assembly) suffers from garbage-in/garbage-out unless `A6` standardizes financial spreads and `A4` resolves entity identity. Similarly, `A9` (Contract generation) requires structured outputs from `Committee decision` and verified legal entity data from `KYC check`/`Resolve entity mismatch`.

### 2. Legal and compliance control dependencies
* **Hard KYC Gate Before Signing (`A4 + A9 -> A10 -> A11`):** Legally, `Customer signing` cannot precede `KYC check` clearance, yet provisional signing occurs in 6.5% of cases (with `Resolve entity mismatch -> Contract preparation` occurring 380 times directly, bypassing clean early closure). Deploying `A10` (E-signature automation) or `A11` (Loan activation bot) before enforcing a hard system block in `A9` and clearing entity backlogs via `A4` would automate regulatory violations at scale.
* **Decision-to-Contract Integrity & Four-Eyes Enforcement (`A9 -> A11`):** Contract remediation loops hit 8.5% overall and 14% in Credit-line renewals, while High-exposure SME cases suffer a 5.8% four-eyes violation rate between `Credit analysis` and `Committee packaging` during month-end rushes. `A9` must act as an automated control gate—verifying distinct user IDs on `Credit analysis` and `Committee packaging` and locking approved committee terms—before any downstream booking (`A11`) can ever be considered.

### 3. Human escalation capacity dependencies
* **Compliance Specialist Funnel (`A3 -> A4`):** The social network reveals extreme handover concentration from Compliance ops onto just **2 senior specialists** (within a 3-FTE pool) handling `Resolve entity mismatch` (31.0h wait, 38% rework). Deploying `A3` (KYC hit pre-classifier) tuned conservatively to avoid false negatives will increase rapid escalations into this 3-FTE pool. Therefore, `A4` (Entity-mismatch assistant) is a strict prerequisite to expand specialist throughput before `A3` accelerates upstream KYC screening.
* **Credit Analyst Centrality (`A1 / A2 / A5 -> A6`):** Credit analysts (9 FTE) sit at the center of the working-together network, carrying `Financial document review` (45 min), `Normalize financial statements` (75 min), `Credit analysis` (110 min), and shared `Committee packaging` (50 min). Any upstream automation (`A1`, `A2`, or `A5`) that pushes cleaner cases faster into the analyst pool without `A6` relieving the 75-minute normalization burden will spike the 28.0h pre-credit-analysis queue.

### 4. External valuation dependency
* **Sequential Trigger Trap (`Classify / Credit analysis -> A7`):** Currently, the DFG shows `Credit analysis -> Collateral valuation request` (2,400 cases), followed by `Wait for external valuation` (58.0h median wait, 19% late) feeding `Committee packaging` (1,980 cases). Because valuation is triggered *after* credit analysis rather than parallelized upon product classification or initial financial completeness, external partner latency sits directly on the critical path to `Committee packaging`. `A7` depends on clean collateral/product intake data and external valuer SLA integration, but cannot eliminate external turnaround time—only parallelize and route around slow valuers.

### 5. Core-banking integration constraints
* **Two-Module Deep Integration Cap (Months 1–6):** Deep two-way core-banking integration is required for `A1` (customer master/account creation), `A9` (pulling authoritative limits, pricing, covenants, KYC flags, and role-separation logs), and `A11` (loan booking/disbursement). By contrast, `A5` (portal/workflow layer), `A6` (document-AI workbench), `A4` (external registry/entity workspace), and `A7` (valuer portal routing) rely primarily on workflow/API/document layers and require light or read-only core touchpoints initially. Consuming a H1 deep-integration slot on `A1` or `A11` starves `A9`, blocking Compliance's Wave 1 control-strengthening requirement.

# 3. Portfolio alternatives

All four portfolios respect the four-module first-year budget cap and represent distinct strategic philosophies.

### Portfolio 1: Quick-Win (Front-End Volume & Visibility) Portfolio
* **Modules:** `A1` (Auto-registration), `A2` (Product/exposure recommender), `A5` (Missing-doc checklist & nudging), `A10` (E-signature & reminder automation).
* **What it optimizes:** Political visibility, high-frequency front-end task automation (11,880 registrations and 11,820 classifications), reduced Back office queue optics before 15:00, and faster tail-end signing reminders.
* **What it sacrifices:** Violates Compliance's mandate unless `A2`/`A10` have bolt-on checks (neither truly fixes existing four-eyes, contract-mismatch, or provisional-signing breaches). Worse, it automates non-bottleneck Back office slack while starving overloaded Credit analysts (9 FTE) and Compliance specialists (3 FTE), actively worsening middle-office queues and risking automated provisional-signing breaches via `A10`.

### Portfolio 2: Pure Flow (Bottleneck & Synchronization) Portfolio
* **Modules:** `A5` (Missing-doc checklist & nudging), `A6` (Financial-statement extraction & normalization), `A4` (Entity-mismatch resolution assistant), `A7` (Collateral valuation triage & routing).
* **What it optimizes:** End-to-end throughput, critical-path synchronization before `Committee packaging`, and capacity relief for the two scarcest specialist pools (9 Credit analysts and 3 Compliance specialists). Attacks the three longest internal/external bottlenecks: external valuation wait (58.0h), entity mismatch wait (31.0h), and credit/normalization queues (20.0h + 28.0h).
* **What it sacrifices:** Fails to address downstream `Contract preparation` control loops (8.5% overall, 14% Credit-line renewal), Legal ops month-end overload, the 5.8% month-end four-eyes breach rate, and the 6.5% illegal provisional-signing rate. High-velocity upstream flow will pile up at the 4-FTE Legal ops team at month end.

### Portfolio 3: Compliance-First (Control & Risk) Portfolio
* **Modules:** `A2` (Product/exposure recommender), `A3` (KYC hit pre-classifier), `A4` (Entity-mismatch resolution assistant), `A9` (Contract generation with control-rule checks).
* **What it optimizes:** Regulatory defensibility, accurate exposure/control-path routing (`A2`), end-to-end AML/KYC auditability (`A3` + `A4`), elimination of provisional signing and contract-term mismatches, and hard enforcement of four-eyes separation (`A9`).
* **What it sacrifices:** Leaves the core operational engine untouched. Credit analysts remain buried under 24% document-review rework (3,300 missing-doc loops) and 75-minute manual financial normalizations (5,200 cases). Median end-to-end cycle time for clean SME loans improves modestly because the parallel financial-review/credit-analysis branch remains the dominant time path once `A4` clears KYC outliers.

### Portfolio 4: Control-Anchored Bottleneck Portfolio (Balanced Hybrid)
* **Modules:** `A9` (Contract generation with control-rule checks), `A5` (Missing-document checklist & portal nudging), `A4` (Entity-mismatch resolution assistant), `A6` (Financial-statement extraction & normalization).
* **What it optimizes:** End-to-end cycle time, scarce specialist capacity (Credit analysts and Compliance specialists), rework loop elimination across both concurrent branches (KYC and Financials), month-end Legal ops stability, and hard preventative compliance gating. Fully satisfies IT's H1 deep-integration cap and Compliance's Wave 1 mandate.
* **What it sacrifices:** Foregoes politically flashy front-end registration automation (`A1`) and defers external collateral triage (`A7`) and e-signature nudging (`A10`) to Year 2 (mitigated operationally via process-rule adjustments for early valuation requests in Equipment finance).

# 5. Risks and compensating controls

 automation changes the risk profile from individual human inconsistency to systematic, high-speed failure if unguarded. Every deployed module must incorporate the following controls:

### 1. Auditability and Decision Lineage
* **Risk:** Black-box extraction (`A6`), entity-matching suggestions (`A4`), or automated contract clause selection (`A9`) obscure accountability during regulatory exams or credit defaults.
* **Compensating Control:** Implement an immutable, append-only audit log capturing four states for every automated step: *(1) raw input artifact hash, (2) model/rule version and suggestion with confidence score, (3) human reviewer ID, timestamp, and explicit override rationale if modified, and (4) final committed state*. Compliance responsibility remains explicitly assigned to named human role-holders.

### 2. False Negatives vs. False Positives
* **Risk:** In `A4` (Entity mismatch) or `A3` (if ever deployed), a **false negative** clears a mismatched/sanctioned legal entity, creating severe AML and enforceability exposure. Conversely, excessive **false positives** in `A4`, `A6` (low-confidence PDF extraction), or `A9` (overly strict control flags) flood the 3 Compliance specialists, 9 Credit analysts, and 4 Legal ops FTEs with alert fatigue.
* **Compensating Control:**
  * *Asymmetric thresholds:* Prohibit auto-clearance on `A4`; position it strictly as an evidence-gathering and entity-graph resolution assistant where a human specialist executes final sign-off.
  * *Field-level confidence gating in `A6`:* Auto-populate financial spread fields only above calibrated confidence thresholds; highlight low-confidence/scanned PDF line items in amber requiring mandatory Credit analyst click-through verification before the credit model can run.
  * *Tiered exception routing:* Route formatting/completeness flags in `A5`/`A6` to underutilized post-15:00 Back office clerks rather than scarce Credit analysts or senior Compliance specialists.

### 3. Model Drift and Rule-Library Obsolescence
* **Risk:** Changes in SME tax/accounting formats degrade `A6` extraction accuracy; shifts in corporate registry schemas degrade `A4`; uncoordinated credit policy or product changes make `A9`'s contract rule library outdated (especially dangerous for Credit-line renewals, which already suffer 14% remediation).
* **Compensating Control:** Establish monthly stratified quality sampling (5% of auto-extracted spreads in `A6` re-spread blind by senior analysts) to detect drift. For `A9`, institute a mandatory **Legal-Product Change Control Gate**: no new product feature, covenant template, or renewal rule can go live in Committee workflows without dual sign-off from Legal Ops and Compliance updating the `A9` rule repository.

### 4. Exception Handling and Customer/RM Workarounds
* **Risk:** If `A5` enforces rigid, generic document checklists, customers stall and Relationship Managers bypass the portal by emailing incomplete PDFs directly to Back office or Credit analysts—recreating the 52% repeat loop outside system visibility.
* **Compensating Control:** Co-design `A5` with RMs using dynamic, product-specific checklists (Working-capital vs. Equipment finance vs. Credit-line renewal) with an explicit "RM Exception / Alternative Document Upload" path that requires structured justification rather than blocking submission outright. Track off-portal email bypass rates per RM.

### 5. Four-Eyes Principle and Pre-Signing KYC Enforcement

  2. `A9` locks the "Send for Signature / Print Contract" action until `KYC_status == CLEARED` and `Entity_Mismatch_Open == FALSE`, eliminating provisional signing structurally rather than relying on policy reminders.
  3. Require exact field-level parity between `Committee decision` parameters (amount, pricing, collateral conditions, renewal covenants) and generated contract schedules unless re-approved by Committee.

# 7. Final recommendation

**Recommend Portfolio 4: the Control-Anchored Bottleneck Portfolio (`A9, A5, A4, A6`), sequenced as `A9 + A5` (Wave 1) -> `A4` (Phase 2) -> `A6` (Phase 3).**

### Why NOT automate the highest-frequency activity (`A1: Register application`) first
Senior management's instinct to automate `Register application` (11,880 cases, position 2 in 98% of variants, very high feasibility) is a textbook **local-efficiency trap** exposed by the process-mining evidence:
1. **It optimizes slack, not a bottleneck:** `Register application` takes 8 minutes with a 1.5h median wait in a 12-FTE Back office pool whose utilization **falls below 65% after 15:00**. Its visible morning queue is an arrival-timing artifact, not a true capacity constraint. Automating it yields negligible end-to-end cycle-time improvement (shaving ~1.5h off a multi-day/multi-week journey) and produces phantom FTE savings unless staff are laid off—which would hurt afternoon exception handling.
2. **It worsens downstream queues:** Pushing 11,880 cases instantaneously into `Classify`, `KYC check`, and `Financial document review` accelerates work arrival into the **true bottlenecks**—the 9 Credit analysts (facing 18.0h, 20.0h, and 28.0h waits) and 3 Compliance specialists (facing a 31.0h wait)—inflating downstream waiting times and intensifying month-end rush errors (such as the 5.8% four-eyes violation rate).
3. **It wastes scarce core-banking IT capacity:** Consuming one of only **two** H1 deep core-banking integration slots on `A1` crowds out `A9` or `A4`, directly jeopardizing Compliance's Wave 1 control mandate.

### Defensible trade-off argument for Portfolio 4
* **Balances speed across concurrent paths:** Process mining shows `KYC check` and `Financial document review` execute concurrently in practice before synchronizing at `Committee packaging` and `Contract preparation`. Speeding up only one branch leaves end-to-end latency pinned by the slower branch. Portfolio 4 attacks **both** branches: `A4` collapses the 31.0h critical-path `Resolve entity mismatch` blockage on the KYC branch, while `A5` + `A6` eliminate the 2,800 missing-document re-entry loops and 75-minute manual spreading bottleneck on the Credit analyst branch.
* **Satisfies Compliance structurally rather than cosmetically:** By leading with `A9` in Wave 1, the bank eliminates its three sharpest regulatory exposures—the 6.5% illegal provisional signing rate, the 5.8% High-exposure four-eyes breach rate, and the 8.5%/14% contract-mismatch rework rate—while simultaneously breaking Legal ops' month-end bottleneck so upstream speed gains translate directly into safe, compliant loan activations.
* **Explicitly accepts manageable Year-1 trade-offs:** Deferring `A7` (Collateral triage, 58.0h external wait) and `A10` (E-signature nudges, 44.0h customer wait) is the right trade-off: `A10` is unsafe to automate until `A9` and `A4` prove 0% provisional signing in production, and `A7`'s external synchronization delay can be partially mitigated at zero IT cost in Year 1 simply by triggering valuation requests parallel to `Financial document review` for Equipment finance cases rather than waiting until `Credit analysis` finishes.
