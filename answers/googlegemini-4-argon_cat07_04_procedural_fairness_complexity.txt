# 1. Fairness dimensions

Assessing fairness in public-benefits administration requires evaluating how the process treats applicants, not merely whether final approval rates are equal. We evaluate eight distinct dimensions:

*   **Outcome parity:** The raw comparison of approval rates across groups. Group A has a 78% approval rate compared to 63% for Group B, 69% for Group C, and 58% for Group D. Because case mix, underlying eligibility, and risk profiles differ across groups, raw outcome gaps do not by themselves prove discrimination, nor does their presence automatically indict the process.
*   **Adjusted outcome disparity:** Differences in approval probability after controlling for observable case complexity and eligibility proxies (`risk_score_band`, `documentation_completeness`, `channel`, `household_size_band`, `prior_case_count`, `region`, and month). Even after adjustment, Group B (-5.5 percentage points), Group D (-3.1 pp), and Group C (-2.2 pp) experience lower approval probabilities than Group A. This suggests that observable case mix alone does not fully explain outcome gaps.
*   **Procedural burden:** The administrative friction, effort, and compliance cost imposed on applicants. Group B (+9.5 pp adjusted) and Group C (+4.2 pp adjusted) face significantly higher probabilities of receiving a `Request Missing Documents` action, as well as higher reopen/rework rates (22% and 24% vs. 11% for Group A). When administrative hurdles fall disproportionately on minority-language or disabled applicants, they act as barriers to access.
*   **Timeliness:** The speed of benefit delivery, measured via median and tail (90th percentile) cycle times. Group B (13.7d median / 34.2d 90th) and Group C (15.9d median / 38.8d 90th) wait much longer than Group A (8.1d / 21.4d). Even after controlling for covariates, Group C waits +4.1 days longer and Group B waits +2.6 days longer, penalizing applicants who require legally mandated support.
*   **Consistency of routing:** Whether similarly situated cases follow predictable, rule-abiding process paths. While routing Group D to `Fraud Review` or Group C to `Accommodation Review` aligns with policy, Group B (+8.0 pp) and Group C (+5.5 pp) face elevated adjusted rates of `Supervisor Review` that are not transparently explained by baseline complexity variables.
*   **Quality/accessibility of support:** The efficacy of rights-preserving steps (`Language Support Contact` and `Accommodation Review`). Fairness requires that accessing these accommodations does not degrade service quality or trigger punitive delays. Currently, utilizing these supports subjects applicants to severe waiting-time bottlenecks (3.8 days for language support; 4.7 days for accommodation review) and fragmented handovers.
*   **Appeal/rework burden:** The frequency with which applicants must contest decisions or endure corrective cycles to achieve an accurate outcome. Adjusted appeal probabilities are higher for Group D (+5.7 pp) and Group B (+4.4 pp), and raw rework rates are roughly double for Groups B, C, and D (20–24%) compared to Group A (11%), indicating higher rates of initial administrative error or miscommunication.
*   **Resource-allocation fairness:** The equitable distribution of staff capacity and expertise across applicant populations. Group A is served by a broad pool of 52 caseworkers, whereas Group B is bottlenecked through just 7 bilingual staff and 3 overloaded regional officers, and Group C is restricted to 5 accommodation specialists (two of whom have split duties). Structural under-resourcing of specialized queues creates institutional disadvantage.

# 2. Adjusted comparison strategy

To separate raw disparity, systemic or interpersonal discrimination, legitimate policy-based routing, and statistical uncertainty, the analysis must follow a structured, multi-stage strategy:

### Matching and Stratification Variables
Construct matched cohorts or coarse exact strata using baseline case characteristics: `channel`, `household_size_band`, `region`, and calendar month. Because `language_preference` and `disability_accommodation_requested` almost perfectly define Groups B and C, we must evaluate two separate estimands: (1) within-need comparisons (e.g., comparing minority-language applicants across well-resourced vs. overloaded regions), and (2) cross-group comparisons net of legitimate support step service times.

### Path and Variant Comparison
Segment the event log into distinct structural pathways using variant analysis:
*   *Standard path (V1):* Compare approval rates and cycle times across groups for cases that require no extra documentation, risk review, or support steps. Disparities in V1 isolate baseline processing inequity.
*   *Support paths (V4, V5):* Isolate cases routed through `Language Support Contact` or `Accommodation Review`. Compare their post-support trajectory (e.g., subsequent `Request Missing Documents` or `Supervisor Review` rates) against comparable cases in V1/V2 to see if entering a support track triggers cascading scrutiny.

### Resource-Assignment Comparison
Map the organizational network to quantify workload concentration, queue depth, and handover friction:
*   Calculate the number of unique resources per case and the frequency of ping-pong handovers (e.g., `General Caseworker  Bilingual Caseworker  General Caseworker`).
*   Use resource-level fixed effects or multilevel modeling to test whether Group B and C disparities are driven by specific overloaded officers/regions or occur uniformly across the agency.

### Waiting-Time Decomposition
Decompose total cycle time into **active service time** (time spent actively working on an activity) and **waiting/queue time** (elapsed time between activity completion and the start of the next activity):
$$\text{Total Cycle Time} = \sum \text{Service Time}_{\text{standard}} + \sum \text{Wait Time}_{\text{standard}} + \sum \text{Service Time}_{\text{support/risk}} + \sum \text{Wait Time}_{\text{support/risk}}$$
Adding legitimate active service time for translation or accommodation assessment is procedurally justifiable; however, excess *waiting time* in understaffed specialist queues (`Language Support Contact  Eligibility Assessment` of 3.8d; `Accommodation Review  Eligibility Assessment` of 4.7d) represents a structural fairness failure.

### Sensitivity Analysis for Possibly Biased Covariates
`risk_score_band` and `documentation_completeness` are "tainted" or endogenous controls:
*   `documentation_completeness` may be an artifact of inaccessible forms for minority-language (Group B) or disabled (Group C) applicants, or inconsistent grading by intake staff.
*   `risk_score_band` is strongly correlated with `prior_case_count` (Group D has 49% high prior cases and 51% high risk) and may penalize recurring poverty or past wrongful denials.
*   **Action:** Estimate sequential models: (a) controlling only for exogenous demographics/geography (`region`, `household_size_band`, `month`), (b) adding `channel` and `prior_case_count`, and (c) adding `documentation_completeness` and `risk_score_band`. Comparing coefficients across specifications reveals how much of the disparity is absorbed by potentially biased administrative labels.

### Subgroup Checks by Region and Channel
Perform interaction analyses across `region` $\times$ `applicant_group` and `channel` $\times$ `applicant_group`. Since two regions have severe supervisor backlogs and three regional officers are overloaded, subgroup checks will determine whether Group B and C penalties are localized capacity failures or systemic policy failures. Similarly, stratifying by `channel` separates the 0.8d vs. 1.4d `Submit  Identity Verification` delay caused by in-person submission from true group-level discrimination.

# 4. Plausible mechanisms

1.  **Structural Capacity Rationing in Support Queues (Concerning):** The agency instituted `Language Support Contact` and `Accommodation Review` to protect applicant rights, but failed to staff them proportionally (7 bilingual workers, 5 specialists) or establish dedicated SLA targets. Consequently, exercising a legal right to language or disability support mechanically forces applicants into starved queues, adding 3.8 to 4.7 days of pure waiting time.
2.  **Sequence Misalignment and Communication Friction (Concerning):** For Group B, `Completeness Check  Request Missing Documents` takes 4 times longer than for Group A (1.6d vs. 0.4d), and adjusted document requests are +9.5 pp higher. If initial intake forms or deficiency notices are poorly translated, or if general caseworkers initiate completeness checks before bilingual staff intervene, applicants will misunderstand evidentiary requirements, driving the 22% rework rate and lower approval rate (-5.5 pp adjusted).
3.  **Handover Tax and Role Contention (Concerning):** In two regions, cases bounce between generalists and specialists (`General  Bilingual  General` and `General  Specialist  Supervisor`). Each handover introduces queue delay and loss of tacit case context. Furthermore, because two accommodation specialists also serve as supervisors, spikes in Group C volume cannibalize supervisory capacity, inflating `Supervisor Review  Decision Draft` wait times for Groups B (1.9d) and C (1.6d).
4.  **Legitimate Risk-Stratified Routing Coupled with Algorithmic Feedback Loops (Mixed Legitimate/Concerning):** The routing of Group D into Variant V3 (`Risk Screening  Fraud Review  Supervisor Review`) is a faithful execution of formal policy given their 51% high `risk_score_band` share. However, because `risk_score_band` correlates heavily with `prior_case_count` (49% in Group D), the risk model likely treats prior benefit applications, past administrative closures, or income volatility as indicators of fraud. This creates a punitive feedback loop where vulnerable repeat applicants face elevated denials (-3.1 pp adjusted) and must resort to appeals (+5.7 pp adjusted) to secure benefits.
5.  **Defensive Escalation to Supervisor Review (Concerning):** Caseworkers facing complex accommodation rules, unfamiliar foreign-language documents, or high regional workloads may defensively escalate Group B (+8.0 pp adjusted) and Group C (+5.5 pp adjusted) cases to `Supervisor Review` to offload liability, even when policy does not require it.

# 5. Limitations of available attributes

Relying solely on the event log columns to adjudicate fairness is insufficient due to five structural data limitations:

*   **Endogeneity and Measurement Bias in `documentation_completeness`:** This attribute is recorded by the system/caseworker, not an objective ground truth. If an online portal is only partially translated or incompatible with screen readers, Group B and C applicants will submit "incomplete" packets due to system design flaws. Moreover, a generalist caseworker unable to read a non-English document may erroneously flag it as incomplete prior to `Language Support Contact`. Controlling for this variable masks the procedural unfairness that caused the incompleteness.
*   **Historical and Proxy Bias in `risk_score_band`:** The risk score is a black-box composite that appears heavily weighted toward `prior_case_count`. If historical enforcement disproportionately targeted low-income regions or specific demographics, or if past wrongful denials due to language barriers forced applicants to reapply (inflating `prior_case_count`), then `risk_score_band` encodes historical disparity. Treating it as an objective control variable legitimizes past institutional bias.
*   **Unobserved Applicant Needs and Socioeconomic Complexity:** `household_size_band` and binary flags (`disability_accommodation_requested`, `language_preference`) are coarse proxies. They do not capture severity of disability, literacy levels, housing instability, informal employment (which is harder to document), or whether an applicant *needed* language/accommodation support but failed to request it due to stigma or confusing intake forms.
*   **Coarse Resource Workload and Invisible Queues:** Resource workload is observed only at daily granularity, and timestamps typically capture activity completion rather than exact start-of-work vs. queue placement. We cannot observe caseworker multitasking, external interruptions, time spent waiting on third-party verifications vs. internal staff availability, or variation in individual caseworker skill and bias.
*   **Absence of Appeal Outcomes and Qualitative Treatment Metrics:** While `appeal_flag`, `Appeal Intake`, and `Appeal Review` exist, the schema does not distinguish *overturned denials* (agency error) from *upheld denials* in `case_outcome`, nor does it capture whether the applicant abandoned the process out of frustration. Furthermore, event logs cannot measure the clarity of `Decision Draft` explanations, the tone of `Language Support Contact`, or the dignity afforded to applicants during in-person interactions.

# 6. Mitigation options and side effects

| Mitigation Option | Operational Description | Potential Side Effects & Trade-offs |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Add capacity for language support & accommodation review** | Hire additional bilingual caseworkers and dedicated accommodation specialists; remove supervisory duties from the 2 split-role accommodation specialists; rebalance caseloads away from the 3 overloaded regional officers. | **Trade-offs:** High budget impact and hiring/training lag. If generalists are reassigned to specialist pools, Group A median wait times (currently 8.1d) may increase slightly. Over-specialization can also lead to siloed teams if cross-training is neglected. |
| **2. Establish service-level targets (SLAs) for support steps** | Create explicit, monitored timeliness thresholds for `Language Support Contact` and `Accommodation Review` (e.g., max 24-hour queue wait), distinct from overall case cycle-time targets. | **Trade-offs:** Goodhart's Law risk—staff may rush consultations, conduct superficial accommodation reviews, or prematurely mark support steps complete just to stop the SLA clock, degrading support quality and dignity of treatment. |
| **3. Redesign routing to reduce handovers (End-to-End Ownership)** | Route applicants indicating minority language preference or accommodation needs *at intake* directly to cross-trained bilingual or accommodation-certified caseworkers who handle the case end-to-end (eliminating `General  Specialist  General` ping-ponging), and decouple routine V5 accommodation cases from mandatory `Supervisor Review`. | **Trade-offs:** Requires upskilling specialist workers to handle full eligibility assessments. If intake screening misclassifies language/accommodation needs, initial misrouting could still occur. |
| **4. Improve decision explanations and intake accessibility** | Standardize, translate, and simplify `Request Missing Documents` and `Applicant Notification` templates; provide upfront multilingual checklists and accessible digital upload tools at `Submit Application`. | **Trade-offs:** Upfront translation and UX redesign costs. More transparent, detailed denial explanations may initially *increase* `Appeal Intake` rates as empowered applicants better understand how to contest errors, temporarily increasing appeal review workloads. |
| **5. Continuous monitoring of adjusted disparities** | Deploy automated process-mining dashboards tracking adjusted approval rates, waiting-time decompositions, and `Request Missing Documents` rates by group, region, and resource pool. | **Trade-offs:** Risk of "quota gaming"—caseworkers or supervisors pressured on disparity metrics might rubber-stamp marginal applications or suppress legitimate `Request Missing Documents` actions, leaving files incomplete and vulnerable to future audit failures. |
| **6. Audit and recalibrate `risk_score_band` and `documentation_completeness`** | Conduct an algorithmic bias audit of `risk_score_band` (Specifically evaluating the weight of `prior_case_count` and false-positive rates in `Fraud Review`), and standardize objective rubrics for `Completeness Check` across languages. | **Trade-offs:** Removing `prior_case_count` or raising risk thresholds may slightly reduce fraud detection recall. Tightening completeness definitions may temporarily lengthen `Completeness Check` active service times. |

# 7. PM4Py analysis plan

The following Python implementation plan demonstrates how to use `pm4py` ( alongside `pandas` and `statsmodels`) to execute the fairness assessment on the event log dataframe `df`.

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
import statsmodels.api as sm
import statsmodels.formula.api as smf

# ==============================================================================
# 1. FORMAT DATAFRAME & ENRICH WITH SERVICE / WAITING TIMES
# ==============================================================================
# Ensure standard PM4Py column formatting
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    timest_format="%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S",
)

# Insert case service and waiting times (computes elapsed & waiting intervals between events)
df_enriched = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df)

# Extract case-level durations and service times per activity
case_durations = pm4py.get_all_case_durations(df_enriched)
service_times = pm4py.get_service_time(df_enriched)

# ==============================================================================
# 2. SUBLOG SPLITTING & VARIANT ANALYSIS BY APPLICANT GROUP
# ==============================================================================
groups = ["Group A", "Group B", "Group C", "Group D"]
sublogs = {}
variant_summaries = {}

for grp in groups:
    # Filter event log by applicant_group
    sublog = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        df_enriched,
        attribute_key="applicant_group",
        values=[grp],
        level="case",
        retain=True,
    )
    sublogs[grp] = sublog

    # Extract top variants and calculate frequency shares
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(sublog)
    total_cases = sublog["case:concept:name"].nunique()
    variant_summaries[grp] = {
        var: (len(cases) / total_cases) for var, cases in variants.items()
    }

# Filter specific high-interest variants (e.g., V1 clean path vs V4/V5 support paths)
v1_path = [
    (
        "Submit Application",
        "Identity Verification",
        "Completeness Check",
        "Eligibility Assessment",
        "Decision Draft",
        "Applicant Notification",
        "Benefit Activation",
    )
]
df_v1_only = pm4py.filter_variants(df_enriched, variants=v1_path, retain=True)

# ==============================================================================
# 3. DISCOVER FREQUENCY & PERFORMANCE DFGs (WAITING-TIME DECOMPOSITION)
# ==============================================================================
dfg_freq_by_group = {}
dfg_perf_by_group = {}

for grp, sublog in sublogs.items():
    # Frequency DFG to inspect routing probabilities (e.g., Completeness -> Request Docs)
    dfg_freq, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(sublog)
    dfg_freq_by_group[grp] = dfg_freq

    # Performance DFG (median/mean sojourn & waiting times on edges)
    dfg_perf, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(sublog)
    dfg_perf_by_group[grp] = dfg_perf

# Filter cases suffering extreme bottleneck delays on support steps (> 4 days / 345,600 seconds)
df_long_accomm_wait = pm4py.filter_paths_performance(
    df_enriched,
    path=("Accommodation Review", "Eligibility Assessment"),
    min_performance=345600,
    max_performance=float("inf"),
)

# Filter tail-end cases (90th percentile cycle time outliers, e.g., > 30 days)
df_tail_cases = pm4py.filter_case_performance(
    df_enriched, min_performance=30 * 86400, max_performance=float("inf")
)

# ==============================================================================
# 4. ORGANIZATIONAL & RESOURCE FAIRNESS ANALYSIS
# ==============================================================================
for grp, sublog in sublogs.items():
    # Handover of Work Network: detects General -> Bilingual -> General ping-ponging
    hw_net = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(sublog)

    # Working Together Network: evaluates team cohesion vs fragmented processing
    wt_net = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(sublog)

# Discover organizational roles & resource similarity across the entire log
# Identifies the 7 bilingual staff, 5 accommodation specialists, and dual-role overlaps
org_roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(df_enriched)
res_similarity = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(df_enriched)

# ==============================================================================
# 5. PROCEDURAL CONSTRAINTS (DECLARE / LOG SKELETON) FOR CONSISTENCY CHECKS
# ==============================================================================
# Discover log skeleton and declarative rules on baseline Group A vs Groups B, C, D
skeleton_A = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(sublogs["Group A"])
declare_all = pm4py.discover_declare(df_enriched)


# Custom verification of policy compliance and anomalous escalation:
# 1. Legitimate rule: language_preference == 'Minority' -> existence('Language Support Contact')
# 2. Legitimate rule: disability_accommodation_requested == 1 -> existence('Accommodation Review')
# 3. Fairness check: Does 'Accommodation Review' force 'Supervisor Review' even when risk_score_band == 'Low'?
def check_procedural_rules(df_log):
    case_acts = (
        df_log.groupby("case:concept:name")["concept:name"]
        .apply(set)
        .reset_index()
    )
    case_meta = df_log.groupby("case:concept:name").first().reset_index()
    merged = pd.merge(
        case_meta, case_acts, on="case:concept:name", suffixes=("", "_set")
    )

    merged["has_sup_review"] = merged["concept:name_set"].apply(
        lambda x: int("Supervisor Review" in x)
    )
    merged["has_req_docs"] = merged["concept:name_set"].apply(
        lambda x: int("Request Missing Documents" in x)
    )
    merged["has_rework"] = (
        df_log.groupby("case:concept:name")["concept:name"]
        .apply(lambda s: int(len(s) != len(set(s))))
        .values
    )
    return merged


case_df = check_procedural_rules(df_enriched)

# ==============================================================================
# 6. ADJUSTED REGRESSION & SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS (EXOGENOUS VS TAINTED CONTROLS)
# ==============================================================================
# Model 1: Exogenous controls only (avoids tainted risk_score_band & documentation_completeness)
mod_exog = smf.ols(
    "case_outcome_binary ~ C(applicant_group, Treatment(reference='Group A')) "
    "+ C(household_size_band) + C(region) + C(channel)",
    data=case_df,
).fit()

# Model 2: Full specification (comparing shift in Group B, C, D coefficients)
mod_full = smf.ols(
    "case_outcome_binary ~ C(applicant_group, Treatment(reference='Group A')) "
    "+ C(risk_score_band) + C(documentation_completeness) + C(channel) "
    "+ C(household_size_band) + prior_case_count + C(region)",
    data=case_df,
).fit()

print("Exogenous Adjusted Disparities:\n", mod_exog.summary())
print("Full Adjusted Disparities:\n", mod_full.summary())
```

# 8. Bottom-line assessment

Based on the converging evidence across paths, resource allocations, waiting times, and adjusted models, **the case-management process is procedurally unfair with respect to timeliness, administrative burden, and resource allocation for Groups B and C, while the presence of direct outcome discrimination and the fairness of Group D’s treatment remain indeterminate without further auditing.**

*   **What is procedurally unfair:** While routing applicants to `Language Support Contact` (Group B) and `Accommodation Review` (Group C) is a legitimate, rights-preserving policy, the agency has failed to provision adequate staff capacity (only 7 bilingual workers and 5 accommodation specialists, 2 of whom have split supervisory duties) or establish SLA protections for those steps. As a result, exercising a right to language or disability accommodation penalizes applicants with 3.8 to 4.7 days of queue delay, excessive handovers (`General  Specialist  General/Supervisor`), elevated rework (22–24% vs. 11%), and unexplained increases in `Supervisor Review` (+8.0 pp and +5.5 pp adjusted) and `Request Missing Documents` (+9.5 pp and +4.2 pp adjusted). Process protections that impose severe time taxes and administrative friction on protected groups violate procedural fairness.
*   **What is indeterminate:** We cannot definitively conclude that the remaining adjusted approval disparities (-5.5 pp for Group B, -3.1 pp for Group D, -2.2 pp for Group C) represent intentional caseworker prejudice. Conversely, we cannot absolve the process by pointing to case-mix differences. Because `documentation_completeness` may be directly degraded by language/accessibility barriers, and `risk_score_band` may encode historical disparities via `prior_case_count`, controlling for these attributes may *understate* systemic bias against Groups B, C, and D, whereas unobserved socioeconomic complexity might explain part of the residual gap. Resolving this indeterminacy requires auditing the risk algorithm, standardizing completeness criteria, and evaluating decision quality on overturned appeals.
