## 2. Rule Interpretation

- **RR1.** For each case, compare the humans who drafted with the humans who approved. Any overlap violates separation, including across aliases and multiple executions. The rule applies at every risk level and during emergencies. Where there is no known overlap but a relevant actor is unresolved, separation is undetermined.
- **RR2.** Apply the same case-level test to implementation and verification. Distinct verified actors satisfy the identity separation test. The supplied rules do not specify a standing verifier-role requirement. Check any separate eligibility policy independently, particularly for a human attributed through a service account under RR5. Neither RR1 nor RR2 prohibits the drafter from closing or implementing, or an approver from verifying, merely because those actions occur in the same case.
- **RR3.** Evaluate HIGH-risk approvals at the approval timestamp. The human must hold the active standing QM role or an effective QM grant covering the activity and change scope, with the principal's required absence or formal unavailability supported and the delegation chain permitted. Use the effective interval and the separate execution and recording timestamps. A database entry created later cannot by itself prove earlier authorization. Here the signed Aug 9 instrument is contemporaneous evidence predating the Aug 11 approval. Confirm its authenticity and continuity through migration. Treat day-end 23:59 as covering that final minute and the stated IC end instants as inclusive.
- **Principal availability.** Leave supports a condition of absence but does not revoke a standing role. If Asha personally approved CHG-504, her QM authority applied during leave. If the site regards a documented return to duty as availability, assess its actual interval for any delegate actions then. A VPN login by an account neither establishes a sustained return to work nor overturns the leave record for the afternoon. The weekend after leave ended does not establish the Aug 15 absence condition. Ben's sick leave and denial raise attribution concerns. No supplied rule automatically suspends his grant for being on sick leave.
- **RR4.** For the IC exception, establish a credible emergency flag and safety rationale, an authorized IC or IC delegate approval before implementation, and an authorized QM review after implementation and by the deadline. A delegate needs review authority for the activity and change scope. The two supplied QM grants support the stated approvals within scope. Do not assume either authorizes retrospective review without controlled evidence. Count the first two site business days after the implementation date, excluding weekends and holidays, and allow through the end of the second business day in UTC. Both Aug 11 implementations were due by end of Thu Aug 13, with Aug 12 and 13 counted. A timely but unauthorized review fails. A late review cannot make the exception timely. The emergency exception does not waive RR1, RR2 or RR5.
- **RR5.** Require reliable action-to-session-to-human evidence and applicable authority for a service-account event. A shared workstation, IP address or badge entry alone cannot identify the operator. Keep unresolved events visible, neither granting separation nor assuming self-verification. Resolve account-to-human attribution independently of authority.
- **RR6.** Use repetition, reciprocity, shared endpoints and handover concentration to select records for review. Evaluate these signals against assignments, workload, available authorized staff, case mix and event-level corroboration. Technical conformance concerns the logged obligations. The control objective also depends on meaningful independent scrutiny and genuine authorization of the underlying change.

## 6. Control Improvements

- Require a controlled, signed delegation before use. Capture principal, named delegate, specific permitted activities, risk and change scope or incident ID, conditions, subdelegation and revocation. Make the approval engine enforce those fields, including the prohibition on onward delegation.
- Store separate UTC signature, effective, expiry and ingestion timestamps with immutable version history. Preserve the original signed instrument during migration. Reconcile records created after their effective start against the original signatures and migration log.
- Record authoritative, time-bounded absence, formal unavailability and return to duty, including on-call weekend coverage and one-off returns. Use this record for conditional delegates and retain the decision snapshot used for each approval.
- Maintain an effective-dated identity directory with a durable human ID, alias and account lifecycle history. Enforce SoD on the human ID at action time. Require an individually authenticated, transaction-bound signature for controlled actions even on shared terminals. Tie service-generated records to the initiating human, session and exact action.
- Require a linked incident and contemporaneous safety rationale for emergency substitution. Validate IC authority before implementation and schedule the retrospective QM review using the site business calendar. Escalate before the deadline to a QM or an explicitly qualified, in-scope review delegate and track any unresolved review through closure.
- Rotate qualified approvers for recurring high-risk pairs where staffing allows. Compare network concentration and reciprocal approval to eligible staffing, case mix, shifts and delegation windows. Sample underlying reviews for substantive challenge and use graph signals to prioritize review.
- Retain signed approvals, delegation versions, availability records, case and incident audit histories, session/endpoint, badge and FIDO evidence under controlled access, synchronized UTC clocks and auditable retention. Preserve the evidence and its chain of custody when an attribution conflict is detected.
