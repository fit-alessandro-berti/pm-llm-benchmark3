# 2. Integrated explanation

A simplistic reading of the aggregate shift table produces two false narratives: either *"workers learn and speed up throughout the shift (`18.8 min` $\to$ `10.8 min`) but get tired and sloppy at the end (`2.5%` $\to$ `7.9%` error)"*, or *"case complexity explains everything."* Neither holds up when the event log is disaggregated across sequence position, WIP, complexity, resource experience, shift dynamics, and batching. Instead, the work period evolves through **three distinct operational regimes** governed by interacting mechanisms:

### Regime 1: Early-Shift Backlog Absorption and Warm-Up (Positions 1–15; 0–90 minutes)
At the start of a shift (bin `1–5`, mean `22 min`), mean service time is at its maximum (`18.8 min`) and error/rework rates are elevated (`3.4%` error, `6.1%` 7-day rework). This is primarily a **case-mix, carryover, and deliberate triage selection effect**, overlaid with modest **cognitive warm-up**:
* **Backlog inheritance and selection:** Shifts—especially `Early` (`48%` high-complexity in hour 1, starting queue `61`) and `Night` (`56%` high-complexity in hour 1)—begin by confronting complex, high-priority carryover exceptions left unfinished or escalated by the prior shift (`case_complexity_score = 0.72`, high-priority share = `41%`). Senior staff (`Senior_A`, `Senior_B`, `Senior_C`) intentionally pull these complex backlog cases early.
* **Complexity vs. warm-up across experience levels:** High-complexity cases inherently require nearly twice the processing time (`22.4 min` vs. `12.7 min` at low WIP) and carry double the baseline error risk (`4.2%` vs. `2.1%`). For `Seniors` and `Mid-level` staff, once complexity is accounted for, early quality is already at its baseline floor (`2.1%` and `2.8%` in the first 20 events vs. `2.2%` and `2.9%` later under low WIP). However, `Juniors` (`Junior_H–K`) experience both genuine **cognitive warm-up** and early complexity vulnerability: their error rate starts at `5.1%` (`19.6 min` service time) and improves to `4.4%` (`13.7 min`) later in the shift when WIP is low.

### Regime 2: Mid-Shift Stabilization and True Baseline Execution (Positions 16–50; 90–300 minutes)
Through bins `16–30` (`151 min`) and `31–50` (`257 min`), the initial complex backlog is cleared (`queue_length_at_start` drops from `46` to `31–33`), incoming work normalizes toward routine cases (`case_complexity_score` falls from `0.72` to `0.50`; high-priority share drops from `41%` to `25%`), and workers are fully warmed up.
* **Parallel improvement in speed and quality:** Because case difficulty declines and queue congestion is low (`~31–33`), service time and error rate decline *together*, reaching the system's healthy operational equilibrium (`12.9–13.6 min` service time, `2.5%–2.7%` error rate, `4.8%–5.0%` rework).

### Regime 3: Late-Shift Queue Pressure, Batching Workarounds, and Automation Cascading (Positions 51–76+; 300–480 minutes)
In bins `51–75` (`378 min`) and `76+` (`459 min`), a dramatic divergence occurs: **service time continues to fall (`12.9` $\to$ `11.4` $\to$ `10.8 min`), while error rates (`2.7%` $\to$ `4.6%` $\to$ `7.9%`) and 7-day rework (`5.0%` $\to$ `8.2%` $\to$ `13.7%`) explode—even though average case complexity is at its lowest point of the entire shift (`0.46` $\to$ `0.43`, high-priority `19%`).**

Why does service time fall while errors rise only in specific conditions? Four reinforcing mechanisms explain this divergence:
1. **Queue pressure triggers a speed-quality tradeoff, NOT learning:** In the `Day` shift (`09:00–17:00`), external arrivals surge late in the afternoon, driving mean queue length from `38` at shift start to `75` at shift end (and `88` in bin `76+`). Faced with high WIP (`>70`) and end-of-shift target pressure, workers accelerate their pace by ~25% within both complexity bands (`12.7` $\to$ `9.4 min` for low complexity; `22.4` $\to$ `16.8 min` for high complexity) by abbreviating verification steps. Crucially, the resource experience table proves that **late-shift position alone does not cause high errors**: when late-shift events occur under **low WIP**, error rates stay flat at `2.2%` (`Seniors`), `2.9%` (`Mid-level`), `4.4%` (`Juniors`), and `3.7%` (`Night`). Errors spike *only* when late-shift position coincides with **high WIP** (`4.9%`, `6.8%`, `12.4%`, and `8.2%`, respectively). Fatigue acts as a **vulnerability amplifier** (reducing cognitive resilience under stress, particularly for `Juniors` jumping to `12.4%`), rather than an unconditional driver.
2. **Sequential batching by juniors artificially depresses mean service time while degrading quality:** Under late `Day`-shift queue pressure, `Junior_H` and `Junior_I` batch `9–18` consecutive `Document Review` tasks together. Rapid batch clicking slashes recorded per-event service time (pulling the bin `76+` mean down to `10.8 min`) but induces habituation, copy-paste mistakes, and missed document discrepancies.
3. **Automation (`Bot_Risk`) amplifies congestion rework:** `Bot_Risk` executes `Risk Classification` instantaneously, feeding cases rapidly into downstream queues without throttling. When `queue_length_at_start > 70`, upstream batched/rushed `Initial Triage` and `Document Review` feed noisier structured inputs to `Bot_Risk` (or surge periods bring edge-case claims), causing higher bot misclassification rates that require human override and correction—or slip past rushed reviewers to become 7-day rework (`13.7%`).
4. **End-of-shift `Quality Check` batching creates a measurement burst:** Because `Quality Check` is routinely deferred to the last 45 minutes of the shift, errors committed earlier in the afternoon are logged and corrected in concentrated bursts right before shift handover, exaggerating the apparent spike in raw late-shift error/correction rates. Meanwhile, on the `Night` shift (`22:00–06:00`), genuine **circadian slowing** keeps service times high (`15.2 min` in the last two hours) while lower average WIP limits the late-shift error rate (`5.1%`) compared to the `Day` (`7.5%`) and `Late` (`6.8%`) shifts.

# 3. Proposed analyses

To rigorously disentangle these mechanisms from observational event-log data, we combine statistical econometric modeling with process-mining structural diagnostics in PM4Py.

### 3.1 Statistical and Econometric Analyses

1. **Within-Resource Fixed-Effects Models (`org:resource` demeaned):**
   * *Objective:* Eliminate compositional confounding from resource skill/experience (`Seniors` vs. `Juniors`) and shift assignment (`Night_L/M` vs. `Day` staff).
   * *Specification:* Fit linear mixed/fixed-effects models for $\log(\text{service\_time}_{ij})$ and panel logit / linear probability models for $\text{error\_flag}_{ij}$ and $\text{rework\_flag}_{ij}$ for event $i$ handled by human resource $r$ in shift $s$:
     $$\begin{aligned}
     Y_{irs} = \alpha_r &+ \gamma_{\text{act}(i)} + f_1(\text{seq\_pos}_{irs}) + f_2(\text{min\_since\_start}_{irs}) + f_3(\text{wip\_at\_start}_{irs}) \\
     &+ \beta_1 \text{complexity}_i + \beta_2 \text{priority}_i + \beta_3 \left(\text{min\_since\_start}_{irs} \times \mathbb{I}[\text{wip} > 70]\right) \\
     &+ \beta_4 \text{is\_batched}_{irs} + \delta_{\text{clock\_hour}(i)} + \theta_{\text{shift}(s)} + \varepsilon_{irs}
     \end{aligned}$$
   * Including resource fixed effects $\alpha_r$ and activity fixed effects $\gamma_{\text{act}(i)}$ ensures we compare the *same worker* executing the *same activity* across varying levels of shift elapsed time, WIP, and case complexity.

2. **Within-Shift Position Effects (Warm-up vs. Fatigue Separation):**
   * *Objective:* Isolate early-shift warm-up (`sequence_position_within_shift` 1–30) from late-shift fatigue (`minutes_since_shift_start > 300`).
   * *Method:* Use restricted cubic splines or piecewise linear splines with knots at sequence positions `5`, `15`, `30`, `50`, and `75`. To test **pure warm-up**, evaluate the slope over positions `1–25` on the subset where `wip_at_start < 30` and `case_complexity_score` is restricted to `[0.45, 0.55]`. To test **pure fatigue**, compare position `51–75` and `76+` against `16–30` specifically within the `wip_at_start < 30` stratum, also testing cumulative prior cognitive load ($\sum_{k < i} \text{service\_time}_k \times \text{complexity}_k$).

3. **WIP/Queue Stratification and Non-Linear Threshold Testing:**
   * *Objective:* Test whether queue acceleration and quality collapse are continuous or triggered above a congestion tipping point (e.g., `wip_at_start > 70`).
   * *Method:* Stratify data into fine WIP deciles (`<20, 20–30, ..., >80`) crossed with shift phase (`Early-in-shift <120m`, `Mid-shift 120–300m`, `Late-in-shift >300m`). Compare `Late` shift start (where initial queue is `70` when workers are *fresh* at `minutes_since_shift_start < 60`) against `Day` shift end (where queue is `75` when workers are *tired* at `minutes_since_shift_start > 360`). The difference at `WIP ~ 70` between fresh `Late`-shift workers and tired `Day`-shift workers cleanly separates **pure queue pressure** from the **fatigue $\times$ queue pressure interaction**.

4. **Complexity Adjustment and Selection Bias Audit:**
   * *Objective:* Quantify how much of the `18.8 min` $\to$ `10.8 min` drop is mechanical case-mix change (`0.72` $\to$ `0.43` complexity) vs. behavioral speed change, and test for non-random case pulling.
   * *Method:* Perform propensity-score/direct standardization (reweighting every sequence position bin to a constant reference distribution of `case_complexity_score`, `priority`, and `concept:name`). Run a multinomial choice model predicting which `org:resource` claims the next case from the queue as a function of `case_complexity_score`, `priority`, and `minutes_since_shift_start` to quantify early-shift cherry-picking of complex cases by `Senior_A/B/C`.

5. **Shift and Time-of-Day (Circadian) Controls via Shift-Overlap Windows:**
   * *Objective:* Separate circadian clock time (`hour_of_day`) from time-on-task (`minutes_since_shift_start`).
   * *Method:* Exploit the overlapping shift schedule:
     * **Window A (`09:00–14:00`):** `Early` shift workers are in hours `3–8` of their shift, while `Day` shift workers are in hours `0–5`.
     * **Window B (`14:00–17:00`):** `Day` shift workers are in hours `5–8` (final stretch), while `Late` shift workers are in hours `0–3` (freshly started).
     Comparing `Day` vs. `Late` workers between `15:00` and `17:00` on identical `wip_at_start` and `case_complexity_score` holds clock time, demand surge, and system queue state strictly constant while varying `minutes_since_shift_start` by 5 hours.

6. **Batching Detection (`Document Review` and `Quality Check`):**
   * *Objective:* Identify sequential and concurrent task batching (`Junior_H`, `Junior_I`, and late-shift `Quality Check` bursts) and quantify its distortion of service time and error rates.
   * *Method:* Use `pm4py.discover_batches` and compute per-resource activity run lengths (`consecutive identical concept:name with inter-start gap < 30s`). Test whether events inside batch runs (`run_length >= 9`) have significantly lower `service_time` and higher downstream `rework_flag` than unbatched `Document Review` events at the same WIP and complexity.

7. **Resource-Experience Interactions:**
   * *Objective:* Test how `resource_experience_months` and tier (`Senior`, `Mid`, `Junior`, `Night`) moderate both early warm-up and late high-WIP degradation.
   * *Method:* Estimate three-way interactions (`resource_experience_months` $\times$ `minutes_since_shift_start` $\times$ `wip_at_start`). Quantify the experience slope showing that `Juniors` suffer a `+8.0` percentage point error surge under high WIP (`4.4%` $\to$ `12.4%`) compared to `+2.7` percentage points for `Seniors` (`2.2%` $\to$ `4.9%`).

8. **Rework and Error Downstream Path Analysis (including `Bot_Risk`):**
   * *Objective:* Separate *when an error is created* from *when it is detected* (during late-shift `Quality Check`), and trace how `Bot_Risk` classifications under high queue length (`>70`) propagate into `Rework Correction`.
   * *Method:* Link each `Quality Check` failure and `Rework Correction` event back to the upstream resource and shift state (`wip_at_start`, `minutes_since_shift_start`, `is_batched`) of the *originating* `Initial Triage`, `Document Review`, `Risk Classification` (`Bot_Risk`), or `Eligibility Decision` event within the same `case:concept:name`.

### 3.2 PM4Py-Oriented Implementation Code

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py
import statsmodels.formula.api as smf

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Format Event Log & Enrich with PM4Py Case/Event Temporal Features
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
df = pd.read_csv("claims_triage_event_log.csv")

# Format standard PM4Py dataframe columns (case ID, activity, completion & start timestamps)
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
    timest_format="%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S"
)

# Compute explicit event service time (in minutes) as well as case-level service/waiting times
df["event_service_time_min"] = (
    df["time:timestamp"] - df["start_timestamp"]
).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0

df = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
    df,
    case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
)
df = pm4py.insert_case_arrival_finish_rate(
    df,
    case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
)

# Extract temporal features (hour of day, day of week, etc.) for circadian controls
temporal_features_df = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp"
)
df["clock_hour"] = df["start_timestamp"].dt.hour

# High-level activity service times and rework case counts via PM4Py
activity_service_times = pm4py.get_service_time(
    df,
    start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
)
rework_cases_by_act = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(df)
print("Mean service time per activity (sec):", activity_service_times)
print("Rework cases per activity:", rework_cases_by_act)

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Visual Diagnostics: Dotted Chart & Events Distribution for Batching/Peaks
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Save dotted chart colored by activity to inspect Junior_H/I Document Review batches
# and last-45-minute Quality Check bursts across shifts
pm4py.save_vis_dotted_chart(
    df,
    "dotted_chart_resource_shift.png",
    attributes=["org:resource", "start_timestamp", "concept:name"]
)

# Save events distribution over hour of day to visualize Day-shift late demand peaks
pm4py.save_vis_events_distribution_graph(
    df,
    "events_distribution_hour.png",
    distr_type="hours"
)

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Automated Batching Detection (Junior_H/I Document Review & Quality Check)
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
batches = pm4py.discover_batches(
    df,
    case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource",
    start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    merge_distance=60  # group consecutive/concurrent executions within 60 seconds
)

# Inspect batching frequency per (activity, resource)
for act_res, batch_types in batches:
    print(f"Activity-Resource: {act_res} -> Batch summary: {batch_types}")

# Flag sequential same-activity batch runs on the event dataframe
df = df.sort_values(["org:resource", "shift_id", "start_timestamp"])
df["same_act_as_prev"] = (
    (df["org:resource"] == df["org:resource"].shift(1)) &
    (df["shift_id"] == df["shift_id"].shift(1)) &
    (df["concept:name"] == df["concept:name"].shift(1))
)
df["batch_group"] = (~df["same_act_as_prev"]).cumsum()
batch_sizes = df.groupby("batch_group")["case:concept:name"].transform("count")
df["seq_batch_size"] = batch_sizes
df["is_sequential_batch"] = (df["seq_batch_size"] >= 9).astype(int)

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Stratification via PM4Py Filtering & Performance DFG Comparison
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Exclude Bot_Risk when analyzing human resource pacing
human_df = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df,
    attribute_key="org:resource",
    values=["Bot_Risk"],
    level="event",
    retain=False
)

# Compare Performance DFG under Low WIP (<30) vs High WIP (>70)
low_wip_cases = df[df["wip_at_start"] < 30]["case:concept:name"].unique().tolist()
high_wip_cases = df[df["wip_at_start"] > 70]["case:concept:name"].unique().tolist()

df_low_wip = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df, "case:concept:name", low_wip_cases, level="case", retain=True
)
df_high_wip = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df, "case:concept:name", high_wip_cases, level="case", retain=True
)

perf_dfg_low, sa_low, ea_low = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
    df_low_wip,
    start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    perf_aggregation="mean"
)
perf_dfg_high, sa_high, ea_high = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
    df_high_wip,
    start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",

)

# Filter anomalous/slow cases or specific high-duration rework paths (Bot_Risk -> Rework Correction)
slow_cases_df = pm4py.filter_case_performance(

    path=("Risk Classification", "Rework Correction"),
    min_performance=0,

# Cluster traces by structural & performance profiles to isolate complex exception archetypes
clustered_sublogs = pm4py.cluster_log(df)

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Attribution of Downstream Rework/Errors to Originating Upstream Events
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Map case-level rework or Quality Check failure back to the upstream processing event
case_has_rework = df.groupby("case:concept:name")["rework_flag"].max().rename("case_rework_7d")

bot_cases = df[

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
human_df["high_wip"] = (human_df["wip_at_start"] > 70).astype(int)
human_df["late_shift"] = (human_df["minutes_since_shift_start"] >= 360).astype(int)
human_df["warmup_phase"] = (human_df["sequence_position_within_shift"] <= 15).astype(int)

mod_speed = smf.ols(

        "np.log(event_service_time_min + 0.1) ~ "
        "warmup_phase + late_shift * high_wip + wip_at_start + "
        "case_complexity_score + C(priority) + is_sequential_batch + "
        "late_shift:high_wip:resource_experience_months + "

    ),

    ),
    data=human_df
).fit(cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": human_df["shift_id"]})

print(mod_speed.summary())
```

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

Using raw worker-level event metrics (`service_time`, `error_flag`, `rework_flag`) for performance appraisal, compensation, or disciplinary action in this claims center is both **statistically invalid** and **ethically indefensible** for five interlocking reasons:

1. **Endogenous Selection Bias and Cooperative Triage Behavior:**
   * Work assignment is not randomized. `Senior_A`, `Senior_B`, and `Senior_C` intentionally take high-complexity (`0.72` mean in bin `1–5`) and high-priority (`41%`) backlog cases early in the shift, and `Night_L` and `Night_M` handle a `56%` high-complexity mix of urgent overnight exceptions. High complexity naturally doubles service time (`22.4 min` vs. `12.7 min`) and doubles baseline error rates (`4.2%` vs. `2.1%`). Evaluating workers on unadjusted speed or raw error counts penalizes cooperative staff who step up to absorb the hardest claims and rewards cherry-picking easy cases.

2. **Measurement Error and Temporal Distortion between Error Creation and Error Detection:**
   * Because `Quality Check` is systematically delayed until the final 45 minutes of the shift, the event log records bursts of error flags and `Rework Correction` activities at the end of the shift. Attributing those errors to the *timestamp of detection* or to the *reviewer/worker active in the last 45 minutes* conflates **who discovered/corrected the defect** with **who originated the defect** (and under what earlier shift conditions). Furthermore, 7-day rework (`rework_flag`, reaching `13.7%` for bin `76+`) can be triggered by multiple touches across a case's lifecycle (`Initial Triage` $\to$ `Document Review` $\to$ `Risk Classification` $\to$ `Eligibility Decision`), making naive single-event blame attribution noisy and biased.

3. **Management-Induced Queue Pressure and Uncontrolled Demand Shocks:**
   * The dramatic rise in late-shift errors (`4.6%` $\to$ `7.9%` overall; `7.5%` in the `Day` shift's last two hours) is driven by systemic capacity-demand mismatch (`queue_length_at_start` surging from `38` to `75–88`). When `wip_at_start` is low (`<30`), late-shift error rates for `Seniors` (`2.2%`), `Mid-level` (`2.9%`), `Juniors` (`4.4%`), and `Night staff` (`3.7%`) remain virtually unchanged from shift start. When management fails to staff for afternoon demand peaks and implicitly or explicitly pressures workers to clear the queue before shift end, workers compress service times (`12.7` $\to$ `9.4 min` and `22.4` $\to$ `16.8 min`). Penalizing workers for errors resulting from management-created congestion shifts accountability for capacity planning failures onto frontline employees.

4. **Automation Quality Degradation (`Bot_Risk`) and Human-AI Blame Shifting:**
   * `Bot_Risk` performs `Risk Classification` instantly, flooding downstream human stages without pacing control. When `queue_length_at_start > 70`, `Bot_Risk` classifications require human correction much more frequently. This creates a double bind for human workers under high WIP: if a worker takes extra time to verify and override `Bot_Risk`, their `service_time` metric suffers and their recorded rework/correction touch count increases; if a rushed worker trusts `Bot_Risk`'s faulty classification under high queue pressure, the error escapes into `Eligibility Decision` and is charged against the human worker as a quality failure or 7-day rework.

5. **Perverse Behavioral Incentives from Punitive Use of Error Rates:**
   * In accordance with Goodhart's Law, using raw late-shift error rates punitively will corrupt both operational flow and event-log integrity:
     * **Defensive Cherry-Picking & Cream-Skimming:** Workers will avoid pulling complex cases (`case_complexity_score > 0.6`) or `Bot_Risk`-flagged exceptions during the second half of the shift, leaving high-priority claims stranded in the queue.
     * **Strategic Slowdown at Shift End:** Workers will deliberately stall on a single case during the final hour (`minutes_since_shift_start > 420`) to avoid starting new cases when `WIP > 70`, dumping the entire afternoon surge onto the `Late` shift (`14:00–22:00`).
     * **Suppression of Quality Checks:** Reviewers will rubber-stamp `Quality Check` in the final 45 minutes rather than logging `error_flag = 1`, reducing visible in-shift error rates while driving up costly external customer callbacks and 7-day post-close rework.
