

# 1. Executive memo

**To:** Senior Leadership Team
**From:** Process Optimization Lead
**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Subject:** Recommendation on Fiber Installation Redesign R

**Decision Required:**
Do not scale Redesign R nationally at this time. Instead, adopt a Hybrid approach (Option E) with immediate rollout for low-risk cases only, while pausing risky simplifications for complex segments.

**Rationale:**
The pilot demonstrates that Redesign R creates value for simple installations but introduces significant risk for complex ones. Median cycle time improved by 3.4 days, driven primarily by the "Simple house" segment (34% of volume), which saw faster appointments and fewer handovers. However, the process tail worsened significantly: p95 cycle time increased from 45 to 62 days. This degradation is concentrated in apartments and older buildings, where predictive skip rules failed, leading to missing permit checks, failed visits, and downstream billing disputes.

The process mining data reveals structural fragility. While model fitness improved (0.87  0.94), precision dropped (0.79  0.51), indicating the new process allows too many exception paths (e.g., billing before activation, skipped surveys). Complaints rose from 6.1% to 9.8%, and estimated full costs increased despite lower visible labor costs. Furthermore, organizational resilience decreased; coordinator utilization hit 93%, creating a single point of failure where cases stalled during absences.

**Trade-off Summary:**
We face a tension between speed for standard cases and quality/compliance for complex ones. Scaling universally maximizes speed but risks reputation damage and higher rework costs. Rolling back forfeits efficiency gains for 34% of cases. The optimal path is segmented deployment. We will scale the coordinator model for low-risk homes but suspend survey skipping and post-billing activation for medium/high-risk properties until predictive rules are validated.

**Next Steps:**
Immediate action includes restricting Redesign R to Simple Low-Risk segments. We will pause survey skipping and billing activation changes for Apartments and Old Buildings. We will instrument downstream cost measurement and validate risk rules before any broader rollout.

**Conclusion:**
Redesign R is not universally successful. By segmenting deployment, we protect the gains for simple cases while mitigating the compliance and quality risks in complex segments. This approach balances operational efficiency with customer experience and financial risk.

# 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

**High fitness but low precision:**
Fitness measures how well the model explains observed traces; high fitness (0.94) means most pilot cases fit the simplified coordinator path. Precision measures how much behavior the model allows; low precision (0.51) means the model permits many unlikely paths (e.g., billing before activation), reflecting poor control over exceptions.

**Improved median but worse tail duration:**
Simple houses benefited from merged validation/scheduling, lowering the median. Complex segments (apartments/old buildings) suffered from skipped surveys and missing permits, causing delays that stretched the tail (p90/p95).

**Fewer handovers but lower resilience:**
Coordinator ownership centralized work, reducing handovers and increasing accountability. However, this created a dependency on individual coordinators. When absent, cases stalled because context was not shared, reducing organizational resilience.

**Lower visible cost but higher rework/downstream cost:**
Front-end labor costs dropped due to fewer surveys. However, increased complaints and manual activations generated hidden costs after billing start, which were excluded from visible cost data but appear in downstream estimates.

**Improvement for simple houses but deterioration for others:**
The redesign optimized for standard cases where risk prediction works well. For medium/high-risk segments, skipping surveys or permitting checks introduced errors that required rework (second visits, escalations), negating speed gains.

# 5. Value-of-information analysis

**Ranking by Decision Value:**

1.  **M6 (Validate building-risk rule):** Resolves whether skipped surveys are the root cause of rework. If accuracy < 90%, suspend skipping universally.
2.  **M4 (Measure full downstream cost):** Determines true profitability of the redesign. If downstream costs exceed visible savings, the redesign is financially neutral or negative.
3.  **M7 (Conformance monitoring for Billing/Permits):** Identifies compliance risks. If billing precedes activation frequently, it triggers immediate policy changes.
4.  **M3 (Reason codes for failures):** Helps identify specific causes of failed visits. Useful for root cause analysis but secondary to risk rule validation.
5.  **M2 (Instrument start timestamps):** Clarifies process duration. Helpful for reporting but less critical than cost/quality decisions.
6.  **M8 (Customer experience split):** Validates NPS drivers. Useful for PR but does not change operational strategy.
7.  **M5 (Resilience experiment):** Tests coordinator absence impact. Important for operations but lower priority than customer complaints.

# 6. Final recommendation

**Recommended Decision:** Option E (Hybrid/Segmented deployment).

**Immediate Action:**
*   **Scale:** Deploy coordinator ownership to "Simple house, low building risk" cases nationally.
*   **Pause:** Suspend predictive survey skipping and post-billing activation for all medium/high-risk segments (apartments, old buildings).
*   **Measure:** Implement M6 (rule validation) and M4 (full cost) immediately in remaining pilot regions.

**Threshold for Broader Rollout:**
Proceed to national scaling only when:
1.  Building-risk predictive rule precision exceeds 90%.
2.  Full downstream cost is  Baseline cost.
3.  Complaint rate remains  6.1% across all segments.

This approach captures efficiency gains for low-risk volumes while preventing systemic failures in complex segments.
