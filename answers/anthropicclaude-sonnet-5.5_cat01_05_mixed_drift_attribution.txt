## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The raw–mapped gap is 0.35 in March and stays between 0.32 and 0.40 afterwards. Mapping recovers roughly 85–93% of the raw fitness loss relative to the 0.925 baseline. That indicates the March collapse is mainly a vocabulary/provenance artifact:
- 500 of 640 March cases carry `Eligibility Screen`.
- Mapped edge rates sit within ±1 per 100 of January.
- Median cycle time is unchanged (31.2 vs 31.15 h).

It does not show that the migration was operationally neutral, that the mapping is lossless, or that the residual 0.025–0.065 mapped loss is noise. A mapping asserts semantic equivalence. The new screen may differ in trigger, automation or content, which only timing, resource and temporal-profile checks can probe. Fitness is also nearly blind to rework, because the normative model already permits `Request Info` loops.

Duplicated `Open Case` events inflate event and variant counts. They create spurious edges (26 `Open Case->Register` in March) and corrupt start-activity and ordering analyses, yet barely affect case-level cycle time. Missing resources leave control flow intact but break workload, handover and team attribution. They also bias stratified results if missingness is non-random.

Post-May changes are not logging drift:
- The label set was constant at 11 from March.
- The shifts appear after mapping (`Assess->Request Info` 1229 per 100).
- They appear in cycle metrics computed from operational tables (median 3245 h, p90 71110 h).
- They track the supplier Z share.
- They partly reverse after Z's recovery, which labels and missing-resource rates do not do.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 global | month/week | Reporting, global alarms | 300 cases per window | Overall trend, fitness | Masks priority and mix effects |
| L1 core | supplier group (Z/non-Z at registration) × complexity (high/standard) | Separates supplier, mix and behavioral drift | 100 cases and 30 expected events per edge; else widen window (weeklymonthlyquarterly) | Z vs non-Z within complexity; standard-case trend | Complexity flag may be set after assessment; Z flag must not derive from observed incompleteness |
| L2 operational | L1 × priority class × staffing era (pre/post 06-15, 07-01) | Redistribution of delay, capacity effects | 100 cases; 200 for p90 (tail of 20 cases) | Queue wait by class; before/after rule | Priority correlates with complexity; era overlaps with supplier era |
| L3 resource/team | team × activity (only where resource known) | Practice variation, workload | 100 events per team×activity; report missingness share | Handover, service time, practice differences | Missing resources are non-random, so excluded events bias teams |
| L4 provenance | label version (old/new) × Open Case duplicate flag × channel | Logging diagnostics | 50 cases; diagnostic only | Timing equivalence of relabelled activity | Label use may correlate with channel/team |
| L5 drill-down | Z region/customer cluster; Senior vs non-Senior | Triggered investigations | 30 cases, exploratory only | Hypothesis generation | Multiple comparisons, tiny cells |

Primary monitoring population: standard-complexity, non-Z cases on mapped labels (65–70% of baseline volume; 300–450 per month).
- It is shielded from the two largest composition shocks (supplier mix, renewal complexity), so rework rises there indicate behavioral change and waiting rises indicate capacity or priority displacement.
- It is large enough for weekly or monthly DFG and p90 monitoring.
- It is exposed to the priority rule by design, which makes it a sensitive capacity indicator.
- It is supplemented by the Z×complexity strata and a global customer-facing view, never replaced by them.

**Seasonality.** Compare to prior-year same-period values after direct standardization to baseline complexity/supplier mix. Only flag "new drift" when the seasonal-adjusted residual leaves the prediction band. With one year of history, label such alerts "uncertain seasonal expectation". Report expected-seasonal vs unexpected separately, and keep cohorts by case start date to avoid censoring bias.

**Multiple testing.** Organise monitors into families (schema, flow, time, mix, outcomes). Apply gatekeeping (schema alerts pause downstream interpretation), Benjamini–Hochberg at q0.10 across segments within a family, and a weekly alert budget. Use sequential methods (CUSUM/EWMA) calibrated to a target false-alarm rate. Require persistence (2 windows) or a minimum effect size before escalating. Set thresholds per metric and stratum from their own variance, not one universal cut-off.

def validate(monthly, dfg):
    """Return a list of data-quality issues (empty list = passed)."""
    issues = []
    if monthly.index.duplicated().any():
        issues.append("duplicate months in monthly table")
    if len(monthly) != 12:
        issues.append(f"expected 12 months, got {len(monthly)}")

            issues.append(f"{c} outside [0,100]")

    if ((monthly["fast_variant_pct"] + monthly["rework_variant_pct"]) > 100).any():

    if not dfg.index.isin(monthly.index).all():

    if (dfg["ri_cd"] > dfg["assess_ri"]).any():
        issues.append("RequestInfo->CollectDocs exceeds Assess->RequestInfo")
    if (dfg > 100).any().any():
        issues.append("a DFG rate exceeds 100 per 100 cases")
    return issues

def dfg_distances(dfg, ref_month="2026-01"):
    """Transparent L1 distance on per-100-case rates. Omitted edges are
    assumed unchanged (contribute 0), so distances are lower bounds."""
    ref = dfg.loc[ref_month]
    diff = (dfg - ref).abs()
    out = pd.DataFrame({"L1": diff.sum(axis=1)})
    out["mean_per_edge"] = out["L1"] / dfg.shape[1]
    out["relative_L1"] = out["L1"] / ref.sum()
    for g, cols in EDGE_GROUPS.items():
        out[f"L1_{g}"] = diff[cols].sum(axis=1)
    arr = dfg.values.astype(float)
    pair = np.abs(arr[:, None, :] - arr[None, :, :]).sum(-1)
    pair = pd.DataFrame(pair, index=dfg.index, columns=dfg.index)
    return out, pair


def fitness_recovery(monthly):
    base_fit = monthly.loc[BASE_MONTHS, "raw_model_fitness"].mean()
    out = pd.DataFrame(index=monthly.index)
    out["gap_mapped_minus_raw"] = monthly["mapped_model_fitness"] - monthly["raw_model_fitness"]
    raw_loss = base_fit - monthly["raw_model_fitness"]
    out["raw_loss"] = raw_loss
    out["mapped_loss"] = base_fit - monthly["mapped_model_fitness"]
    out["share_raw_loss_recovered"] = np.where(raw_loss > 1e-9,
                                               out["gap_mapped_minus_raw"] / raw_loss, np.nan)
    return out


def _sse(x):
    x = np.asarray(x, float)
    return float(((x - x.mean()) ** 2).sum()) if len(x) else 0.0


def binary_segmentation(x, offset=0, min_seg=2, min_gain_frac=0.15, total=None, depth=3):
    """Explainable rule: accept a mean-shift split if it removes at least
    `min_gain_frac` of the total series SSE. Returns [(index_of_first_new_regime, gain_frac)]."""
    x = np.asarray(x, float)
    total = _sse(x) if total is None else total
    if depth == 0 or len(x) < 2 * min_seg or total == 0:
        return []
    base = _sse(x)
    best = max(((k, base - _sse(x[:k]) - _sse(x[k:])) for k in range(min_seg, len(x) - min_seg + 1)),
               key=lambda t: t[1])
    k, gain = best
    if gain / total < min_gain_frac:
        return []
    return (binary_segmentation(x[:k], offset, min_seg, min_gain_frac, total, depth - 1)
            + [(offset + k, gain / total)]
            + binary_segmentation(x[k:], offset + k, min_seg, min_gain_frac, total, depth - 1))


def baseline_excursions(series, tol_frac=0.10, k=3.0):
    """Flag months outside baseline +/- max(k*std, tol_frac*|mean|)."""
    b = series.loc[BASE_MONTHS]
    band = max(k * b.std(ddof=1), tol_frac * abs(b.mean()))
    return series[(series - b.mean()).abs() > band].index.tolist()

def prepare_event_log(df, case_col, act_col, ts_col, res_col=None, ts_format=None):
    """Format with PM4Py; keep the original label in `activity_raw`."""
    d = df.copy()
    d["activity_raw"] = d[act_col]
    d = pm4py.format_dataframe(d, case_id=case_col, activity_key=act_col,
                               timestamp_key=ts_col, timest_format=ts_format)
    if res_col and res_col in d.columns and res_col != RES:
        d = d.rename(columns={res_col: RES})
    return d.sort_values([CASE, TS], kind="stable").reset_index(drop=True)


def add_normalized_labels(d, label_map=LABEL_MAP, technical=TECHNICAL):
    """Add activity_norm and a duplicate-technical-event flag; original columns untouched."""
    d = d.copy()
    d["activity_norm"] = d["activity_raw"].replace(label_map)
    is_tech = d["activity_norm"].isin(technical)
    d["is_dup_technical"] = is_tech & d.duplicated(subset=[CASE, "activity_norm"], keep="first")
    return d


def raw_view(d):
    return d.copy()  # concept:name still holds the raw label


def normalized_view(d, drop_technical=True):
    """Returns a NEW dataframe whose concept:name is the normalized label."""
    out = d.loc[~d["is_dup_technical"]].copy()
    if drop_technical:
        out = out.loc[~out["activity_norm"].isin(TECHNICAL)]
    out[ACT] = out["activity_norm"]
    return out


def time_slice(d, start, end, mode="traces_contained"):
    """PM4Py time-range filter (modes: events, traces_contained, traces_intersecting).
    Note: timestamps from format_dataframe are UTC-aware; pass strings 'YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS'."""
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(d, start, end, mode=mode,
                                   case_id_key=CASE, timestamp_key=TS)


def _naive(s):
    return s.dt.tz_convert(None) if s.dt.tz is not None else s


def cohort_by_start_month(d, month):
    """Cases started in `month` (e.g. '2026-05'); avoids cut-off censoring of partial cases."""
    start = d.groupby(CASE)[TS].min()
    ids = start[_naive(start).dt.to_period("M").astype(str) == month].index
    return d[d[CASE].isin(ids)]


def months_in(d):
    start = d.groupby(CASE)[TS].min()
    return sorted(_naive(start).dt.to_period("M").astype(str).unique())


def monthly_dfgs(d, activity_key=ACT):
    out = {}
    for m in months_in(d):
        sub = cohort_by_start_month(d, m)
        dfg_, sa, ea = pm4py.discover_dfg(sub, activity_key=activity_key,
                                          timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)
        out[m] = {"dfg": dfg_, "start": sa, "end": ea, "n_cases": sub[CASE].nunique()}
    return out


def dfg_rates(entry):
    n = entry["n_cases"]
    return {e: 100.0 * c / n for e, c in entry["dfg"].items()}


def l1_distance(rates_a, rates_b, edges=None):
    edges = edges or set(rates_a) | set(rates_b)  # missing edge counts as 0
    return sum(abs(rates_a.get(e, 0.0) - rates_b.get(e, 0.0)) for e in edges)


def variant_distribution(d, activity_key=ACT):
    v = pm4py.get_variants(d, activity_key=activity_key, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)
    n = sum(v.values())
    return {k: c / n for k, c in v.items()}


def js_divergence(p, q):
    keys = set(p) | set(q)
    P = np.array([p.get(k, 0.0) for k in keys])
    Q = np.array([q.get(k, 0.0) for k in keys])
    M = 0.5 * (P + Q)
    kl = lambda a, b: float(np.sum(np.where(a > 0, a * np.log2(a / b), 0.0)))
    return 0.5 * kl(P, M) + 0.5 * kl(Q, M)


def temporal_profile(d, activity_key=ACT):
    return pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(d, activity_key=activity_key,
                                           timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)


def temporal_profile_shift(tp_ref, tp_new, min_std=1e-9):
    """z-shift of mean eventually-follows durations relative to the reference std."""
    rows = []
    for pair in set(tp_ref) & set(tp_new):
        (m0, s0), (m1, _) = tp_ref[pair], tp_new[pair]
        rows.append((pair, m0, m1, (m1 - m0) / s0 if s0 > min_std else np.nan))
    return pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["pair", "mean_ref_s", "mean_new_s", "z_shift"]) \
             .sort_values("z_shift", key=abs, ascending=False)


def temporal_conformance(d, tp_ref, zeta=3.0, activity_key=ACT):
    return pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile(d, tp_ref, zeta=zeta, activity_key=activity_key,
                                              timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)


def discover_constraints(d, kind="skeleton", noise=0.0, activity_key=ACT):
    """Optional: log skeleton or DECLARE (DECLARE needs a recent PM4Py)."""
    try:
        if kind == "skeleton":
            return pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(d, noise_threshold=noise, activity_key=activity_key,
                                               timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)
        return pm4py.discover_declare(d, activity_key=activity_key,
                                      timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)
    except Exception as exc:
        print("constraint discovery unavailable:", exc)
        return None


def diff_constraints(ref, new):
    """Generic diff of dict-of-collections models (skeleton keys or DECLARE templates)."""
    out = {}
    for k in set(ref) | set(new):
        a, b = set(ref.get(k, [])), set(new.get(k, []))
        out[k] = {"lost": sorted(map(str, a - b)), "gained": sorted(map(str, b - a))}
    return out


def rework_and_queue_metrics(d, act="Assess", activity_key=ACT, start_col=None):
    """Loops per case and waiting before `act`. With only completion timestamps the
    'wait' includes the predecessor's duration; pass start_col if start times exist."""
    d = d.sort_values([CASE, TS], kind="stable")
    loops = d[d[activity_key] == "Request Info"].groupby(CASE).size()
    prev_end = d.groupby(CASE)[TS].shift(1)
    begin = d[start_col] if start_col else d[TS]
    wait_h = ((begin - prev_end).dt.total_seconds() / 3600)[d[activity_key] == act]
    return loops, wait_h.dropna()


def compare_raw_vs_normalized(d, ref_start, ref_end, tgt_start, tgt_end):
    """Raw vs normalized labels without overwriting the source log; uses a reference
    Petri net discovered on the normalized baseline window."""
    d = add_normalized_labels(d)
    views = {"raw": raw_view(d), "normalized": normalized_view(d)}
    ref_log = time_slice(views["normalized"], ref_start, ref_end)
    net, im, fm = pm4py.discover_petri_net_inductive(ref_log, noise_threshold=0.0,
                                                     activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)
    rows = {}
    for name, v in views.items():
        tgt = time_slice(v, tgt_start, tgt_end)
        fit = pm4py.fitness_token_based_replay(tgt, net, im, fm, activity_key=ACT,
                                               timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE)
        rows[name] = {"labels": tgt[ACT].nunique(), "cases": tgt[CASE].nunique(),
                      "variants": len(variant_distribution(tgt)),
                      "fitness": fit.get("average_trace_fitness", fit.get("log_fitness"))}
    return pd.DataFrame(rows).T


def segment(d, case_attr, values):
    """Case-level segmentation via pandas (e.g. 'case:supplier' in ['Z'])."""
    return d[d[case_attr].isin(values)]

- The March raw-fitness collapse is a semantic/provenance artifact of the migration (confidence 95%). Labels, duplicate `Open Case` events and missing resources changed suddenly, mapped edges stayed within ±1 per 100, and the median did not move.
- The May onset of rework is real behavioral/control-flow drift consistent with Supplier Z incomplete bundles (80% real; 65% attributable mainly to the supplier). It began with the disruption and partly reversed after 09-10.
- The performance deterioration from May to Aug is mostly queueing, not service. Assess service moved only 0.6 h while waits grew much more (70% confidence, with queueing 40–60% of the Jul–Aug delta).
- Mapped fitness barely moves (0.065), because the normative model already allows the loops, so fitness is a weak detector of the operational drift.

**Only plausible**
- Staffing exits are a driver of the Jun–Aug tail growth (60–65%), but the MayJun step began before 06-15 and capacity N is unknown.
- Oct–Nov complexity/volume growth is seasonal (75%), pending prior-year comparison.
- Senior Assessment growth is partly real practice and partly recording change (40% real practice; the per-high-complexity-case rate 0.190.54 exceeds what mix explains).
- The priority rule redistributes delay (30%), which is unobservable at this aggregation.
- December's improvement is mainly load decay (55%). The intervention effect is 25% and undetectable beyond mix-predicted rework.

**Where mediation/interaction blocks clean attribution**
- Rework is mediated through Assess workload (+33% in July, 53% from rework), so supplier and staffing effects on queueing are entangled.
- Season, reduced capacity and priority all act on the same queue in Oct–Nov.
- The Z flag jumped from 118 to 292 cases while non-Z cases fell 150, suggesting rerouting or redefinition. Z-attributed effects are therefore uncertain.
- Residual rework in Sep (2 pts above the mix prediction) could be backlog, practice persistence, or noise.

**What would make me revise the account**
- Stratified data showing rework rising equally in non-Z, standard-complexity cases (shifts weight to staffing/behavior).
- Queue wait rising before 06-15, or assessor throughput unchanged.
- Standard-case waits falling, or high-complexity waits rising, after 07-01.
- Prior-year renewal data showing similar Senior rates (season) or none (new practice or recording).
- Temporal-profile or resource evidence that `Eligibility Screen` has different timing or content, which would reopen March as a real change.
- A discovered change in Z-flag or customer-assignment logic.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF a label outside the registry exceeds 0.5% of events, or the raw–mapped fitness gap moves by more than 0.05 from its own trailing range, THEN freeze raw-fitness alerts, update the mapping registry after checking the temporal profile and resource pattern, and re-baseline, BECAUSE the change is probably a semantic/provenance shift whose equivalence must be tested, not assumed.

2. IF `AssessRequest Info` per 100 cases rises beyond its stratum band in Z cases and in non-Z cases, THEN investigate assessor practice and capacity and widen beyond supplier remediation, BECAUSE rework that is not supplier-specific points to behavioral or workload-driven change.

3. IF rework rises only in Z strata while bundle completeness falls, THEN escalate to supplier management and pre-position extra assessment capacity for the expected loop volume, BECAUSE supplier incompleteness generates Request Info loops that feed the Assess queue.

4. IF wait before `Assess` (p90) rises while Assess service time stays within ±15%, THEN examine the workload/capacity ratio, arrival rates and the priority rule by class, BECAUSE the delay is queueing, not slower work.

5. IF standard-class waits rise while high-complexity waits fall after a priority rule or complexity surge, THEN report class-specific SLAs and review the rule's thresholds, BECAUSE the aggregate hides redistribution of delay.

6. IF high-complexity share and volume move outside the prior-year seasonal band, THEN treat as population drift (standardize metrics, add seasonal capacity), but IF the shift matches prior-year patterns, THEN label it recurring and do not raise a drift alarm, BECAUSE recurring mix shifts require planning, not process-change investigation.

7. IF Senior-per-high-complexity-case, resource completeness or duplicate-event rates change while outcomes and service times do not, THEN audit recording and event emission before any behavioral conclusion, BECAUSE recording conventions for Senior Assessment and migrated events have been inconsistent.
