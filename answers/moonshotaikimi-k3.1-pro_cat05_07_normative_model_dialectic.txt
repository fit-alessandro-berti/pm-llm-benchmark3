## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

The unit of analysis is not merely a raw trace. Four questions must be separated:

1. **Observation:** What events are reliably attributable to the case, and in what order?
2. **Conformance:** Does that projection of the log fit the applicable model?
3. **Applicability:** Which scope, policy version, and temporary overlay governed the case?
4. **Compliance:** Were all applicable control-flow and non-control-flow conditions satisfied?

### Established propositions

- **M0’s semantics are clear.** `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` are both required but concurrent: either sequential order, or actual parallelism, can conform. On the approved branch, manual approval precedes activation. Notification precedes closure. M0 contains no auto-approval, emergency, information-request, or reactivation route.
- **CS-9 remains the controlled baseline.** It requires sanctions and quality review before activation, manual approval before activation, and different humans as requester and manual approver. The last rule is not represented in M0.
- **PD-3 and FD-7 are different kinds of evidence.** PD-3 establishes that a pilot decision existed, but its authority to derogate from CS-9 is disputed. FD-7 has a precise emergency scope, window, conditions, and automatic expiry; no comparable authority challenge is supplied.
- **Neither factual emergency nor expected policy approval extends an instrument.** FD-7 did not cover a new activation on 2026-07-13, and PD-3 did not cover one on 2026-08-02.
- **The data collection changed materially.** The CRM migration, approximately 70% notification mirroring, and the rise in notification missingness from 2% to 29% make “missing from the onboarding log” an unreliable universal proxy for “did not happen.”
- **The aggregate figures are evidence, not case-level verdicts.** Of 312 June/July auto-approval cases, 289 only *appear* eligible, 14 are clearly ineligible, and 9 lack eligibility attributes. These figures neither establish 289 compliant cases nor prove that every deviation was misconduct.

### Disputed or unproved propositions

- Whether the steering committee had delegated authority to make PD-3 a CS-9 derogation, rather than merely authorize a monitored operational pilot. The Compliance observer’s two statements weaken, but do not conclusively resolve, that question.
- Whether pre-screen rejected leads and reactivations are M0/CS-9 onboarding cases. The process owner’s and model owner’s assertions are not substitutes for an authoritative scope rule.
- Whether SUP-706’s CRM message belongs to that case and preceded closure. Its late, mutable text link is materially weaker than SUP-702’s stable `notification_id`.
- Whether all mandatory pilot conditions were performed in SUP-702. In particular, pre-activation automated master-data validation is not shown merely by stating that eligibility fields are complete.
- Whether the 47 information-request cases prove that a permanent auto-approval policy is safe. They support modeling the information-request practice, not a separate approval relaxation.
- Whether SUP-715 involved bias or an invalid activation. The supported finding is nondisclosure under the conflict-of-interest policy; the relationship itself is not expressly prohibited.

### Burden-of-proof approach

- **For a noncompliance finding:** require reliable evidence of the relevant events or their ordering, an applicable rule, and resolution of scope and any facially applicable exception. Severe findings warrant particularly reliable timing, identity, and scope evidence.
- **For accepting an exception:** its proponent must establish the instrument’s authority, effective interval, case eligibility, and satisfaction of its conditions. An operator selecting “pilot,” a low amount spent, or a good eventual outcome is insufficient.
- **For missing evidence:** distinguish “the control did not occur” from “the available record cannot establish that it occurred.” Missing mandatory records may themselves be an assurance or recordkeeping finding, but should not automatically be converted into proof of business misconduct.
- **For model and data corrections:** deduplication, reliable activity-label equivalence, and scope clarification may be applied retrospectively because they improve interpretation. A later approval relaxation may not be applied retrospectively to erase a historical breach.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The strongest prosecution case does not depend on enforcing every literal feature of M0. It rests on CS-9 controls that remain applicable even after reasonable model and data allowances are made.

1. **There are clear, material activation-control breaches.**
   - **SUP-703** concerned a HIGH-risk new foreign supplier whose operator selected an inapplicable pilot route. Activation occurred without prior manual approval and before quality review. The EUR 180,000 first purchase demonstrates material exposure, although it is not an FD-7 cap violation and does not itself prove loss or intent.
   - **SUP-708** involved activation 40 minutes before sanctions clearance, with manual approval also after activation. No pilot or emergency applied. The absence of transactions before sanctions clearance mitigates consequences, not the breach of the activation control.
   - These cases cannot be explained by CRM missingness, desirable information requests, label renaming, or rejected-lead scope.

2. **Expiry is a control, not an administrative technicality.**
   - **SUP-705** was activated after FD-7 expired. Continuing flood conditions may explain the operator’s decision and mitigate discipline, but cannot create a written extension. The EUR 12,000 spend would have been below an emergency cap only if a valid emergency overlay applied.
   - **SUP-714** occurred after PD-3 expired. Low risk, actual eligibility, eventual quality review, and belief that permanent approval was imminent do not supply authority for that date.
   - The six post-pilot auto-approval cases in early August are consistent with failure to retire the pilot configuration, not merely isolated model deviation.

3. **The pilot population contains unresolved and adverse evidence.** Fourteen clearly ineligible cases require case-level review and cannot be defended by aggregate pilot eligibility. Nine cases with missing attributes remain unproved. Even the 289 apparently eligible cases require verification of dates, eligibility at decision time, master-data validation, and quality-review deadlines. Normalization of an exception can weaken control even where most individual suppliers appear low risk.

4. **M0 conformance can conceal noncompliance.**
   - **SUP-712** fits M0 but violates CS-9 because requester and manual approver are the same human through aliases. This defeats independent approval even though every visible activity is correctly ordered.
   - **SUP-715** also fits M0, but the required conflict-of-interest disclosure was not recorded. This is narrower than SUP-712: no rule supplied expressly prohibits the spousal/reporting relationship or automatically invalidates the activation.

5. **Desirability and good outcomes do not retroactively amend policy.** Information requests may be a beneficial addition that does not relax any CS-9 activation control. Permanent auto-approval, by contrast, displaces a required approval and therefore needs formal authorization. Similarly, completing quality review after activation does not make an expired or inapplicable exception effective.

6. **A responsible prosecution must concede genuine defects.** It should not treat SUP-704’s facially supported emergency exception, SUP-707’s information requests, SUP-709’s ambiguous pre-screen scope, SUP-710’s reactivation scope, SUP-711’s known duplicate, or SUP-713’s evidenced label equivalence as equivalent to SUP-703 or SUP-708. Nor should SUP-706 be declared a notification failure solely from the onboarding log. These concessions strengthen, rather than weaken, the prosecution’s core case.

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest defense is not that practice automatically overrides CS-9. It is that M0 is being used beyond its semantic and evidentiary competence.

1. **M0 is neither a complete compliance rule nor a faithful description of all legitimate cases.**
   - Concurrency does not require simultaneous timestamps or a particular sequential order; SUP-701 is conformant despite sequential checks.
   - M0 cannot express SUP-707’s repeatable information requests. Projecting away the two unmodeled requests leaves the standard controlled sequence, and the requested certificates were obtained before approval.
   - M0 requires quality review even before rejection, whereas CS-9 expressly requires quality review before *activation* and allows abbreviated processing of rejected leads. SUP-709 therefore exposes a scope/model question, not an activation-before-quality problem.
   - M0 has no reactivation semantics. SUP-710 had sanctions and manual approval before activation and a previously valid quality certification. Whether that suffices requires a reactivation policy; silently treating it as either a new onboarding or an automatic exemption is unjustified.

2. **Temporary overlays explain why raw M0 deviation is not always business noncompliance.**
   - SUP-702 has strong pilot facts: LOW risk, DE, approved group, within the PD-3 window, sanctions before activation, quality review the next business day, and comparatively strong CRM notification evidence.
   - SUP-704 falls within FD-7’s stated window and has sanctions before activation, next-business-day quality review and retrospective approval, daily monitoring, and stated initial spend below EUR 20,000.
   - The defense should nevertheless acknowledge that PD-3’s derogation authority and SUP-702’s master-data validation remain open, and that neither overlay helps SUP-703, SUP-705, or SUP-714.

3. **The notification data are not missing at random.** The migration and missingness shift make a single-log conformance test unreliable. SUP-702’s stable identifier supports a cross-source reconstruction. SUP-706’s late mutable link does not prove notification, but it also does not prove that no notification occurred. The correct result may be unknown rather than either compliance or misconduct.

4. **Several apparent deviations are data or dictionary problems.**
   - SUP-711’s duplicate registration rows share a source transaction ID; deduplication produces the standard sequence and preserves the raw evidence for audit.
   - SUP-713’s `QA_Clearance` label is supported by a procedure/UI rename and content-equivalence audit. Later approval of the mapping is a dictionary-governance weakness, but not automatically proof that quality review was omitted. Unlike a policy amendment, a semantic mapping can recognize what an earlier event actually was.

5. **Trace deviation and business misconduct must be distinguished.** No evidence establishes fraudulent intent in SUP-703, loss in SUP-712, bias in SUP-715, or transactions before sanctions clearance in SUP-708. Conversely, absence of loss or malicious intent does not eliminate a control breach. SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, and SUP-714 remain genuine adverse findings even after the strongest model and data defense.

| SUP-712 | **NONCOMPLIANT** | High | High | M0 conformity conceals a direct CS-9 segregation-of-duties violation. |
| SUP-713 | **MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT** | Low on current evidence | Medium-high | The dominant problem is the activity dictionary, not a demonstrated omission of quality review. |
| SUP-714 | **NONCOMPLIANT** | Medium-high | High | Eligibility and normalization cannot revive an expired pilot. |
| SUP-715 | **NONCOMPLIANT** | Moderate, limited to the COI disclosure finding | High for nondisclosure; low for any inference of bias | The trace is valid, but the required disclosure is absent; the relationship itself is not prohibited by the supplied rules. |

SUP-702 is deliberately not treated like SUP-703 or SUP-714. I would not issue a proven business-noncompliance finding on its current record, but neither would I give it unqualified assurance. Proof of both authority and pre-activation master-data validation would make it **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**; reliable proof that either condition failed would make it **NONCOMPLIANT**. SUP-704 receives a firmer exception classification because FD-7 has a specific emergency scope and expiry and no comparable authority conflict, although ordinary audit verification of criticality and cumulative spend remains appropriate.

### System-level judgment

- **Material operational noncompliance exists.** SUP-703 and SUP-708 are especially serious; SUP-705 and SUP-714 show failure to respect exception expiry; SUP-712 shows an independent-approval failure invisible to M0. The aggregate ineligible and post-pilot cases make this more than a purely theoretical concern.
- **The prevalence of noncompliance is unproved.** The sample is not stated to be representative, the aggregate categories may overlap with other defects, and 289 apparently eligible pilot cases cannot be counted as either fully compliant or noncompliant without further evidence. M0’s deviation rate is not a compliance rate.
- **M0 is not wholly obsolete.** It remains a reasonable standard-route expression of CS-9’s control flow. It is, however, **invalid as the sole enterprise conformance oracle**: it omits overlays, scope boundaries, information requests, resource rules, label mappings, and cross-source evidence.
- **CS-9 should continue to be enforced unless validly amended or overlaid.** Revising M0 or adopting the proposed architecture must not retrospectively authorize SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, or SUP-714.
- **The data platform is not yet adequate for reliable assurance.** Notification linkage, eligibility snapshots, actor identity, and policy-version evidence require remediation. Unknown cases should remain visibly unknown.

I therefore reject the prosecution’s argument if it equates every M0 deviation, every missing mirrored notification, or every unfamiliar scope with business misconduct. I also reject any suggestion that SUP-715’s relationship alone proves a prohibited approval. Conversely, I reject the defense’s argument if it treats M0’s incompleteness as a general waiver, treats “appears eligible” as proof of pilot compliance, or allows factual emergency, desirable practice, absence of loss, or imminent policy approval to replace authority and effective dates.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

### 1. Versioned scope and policy routing

Every case should receive an immutable `case_type`, `model_version_id`, and `policy_profile_id` before consequential processing:

- **PRE_SCREEN_LEAD:** a separate intake model and identifier. Sanctions failure may lead directly to rejection, notification, and closure without quality review. Acceptance creates or links a full onboarding case. This prospectively resolves SUP-709 without rewriting the ambiguous historical scope.
- **FULL_ONBOARDING:** the standard, authorized auto, or authorized emergency profile.
- **REACTIVATION:** a separate activation cycle linked to the prior supplier case. Sanctions and authorized approval remain mandatory before activation. A prior quality certification may substitute for a new review only if a versioned rule defines acceptable validity, scope, supplier changes, and risk. No synthetic historical `Quality_Review` event should be invented.
- **WITHDRAWAL:** permitted before activation by an authorized actor, with reason, notification, and closure. Open information requests are cancelled or completed. After activation, the matter is deactivation/offboarding, not withdrawal.

A pandas rule can perform the routing, but the **scope decision and its authority are organizational controls**.

### 2. Revised control flow

The POWL layer should express only what it expresses well:

- Standard sanctions and quality checks are concurrent and both required.
- Standard manual approval precedes activation.
- An auto route contains sanctions and automated master-data validation before auto-approval/activation, with quality review required eventually.
- An emergency route always requires sanctions before activation but permits quality review and retrospective manual approval on either side of provisional activation.
- Repeatable information requests are permitted before standard approval.
- Rejection and withdrawal cannot be activation routes.
- Notification precedes closure.

The auto and emergency branches in a structural POWL are **over-approximations**, not standing permissions.

### 3. DECLARE/log-skeleton-style constraints

Profile-specific declarative constraints should supplement POWL:

- Sanctions precede every activation.
- Activation, rejection, and withdrawal are mutually exclusive within an activation cycle.
- Standard activation requires prior quality review and manual approval.
- Auto/emergency activation requires eventual quality review before case completion.
- Notification precedes closure; closure occurs at most once and no operational activity follows it.
- A blocking information request must be evidence-received or formally cancelled before approval.
- Manual approval and auto-approval cannot be selected opportunistically for the same activation decision.

These constraints should be versioned by profile. A single global “manual approval before every activation” rule would wrongly reject valid overlays; a single global “manual approval optional” rule would be unsafe.

### 4. Data-aware eligibility, time, amount, and evidence rules

These belong primarily in **pandas/data checks**, supported by source evidence:

- **PD-3 eligibility:** LOW risk, DE, approved group, all evaluated from an immutable snapshot at decision time; auto-approval and activation within the effective window.
- **PD-3 controls:** sanctions and automated master-data validation before activation; if quality review follows activation, activation is provisional and quality is due within two business days using a versioned business calendar.
- **FD-7 eligibility:** emergency scope and critical-relief/shutdown-prevention evidence, with activation inside the exact directive interval.
- **FD-7 controls:** sanctions before activation; cumulative spend, not merely each individual transaction, at most EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval; daily monitoring; quality within two business days; retrospective approval completed.
- Expiry prevents **new** activations. Validly initiated cases retain their necessary tail obligations after expiry; expiry does not erase monitoring, quality, or retrospective approval duties.
- No EUR 20,000 cap should be imported into PD-3, and SUP-703’s EUR 180,000 purchase should be treated as exposure evidence rather than a fictitious cap breach.

Rule results should be three-valued—`PASS`, `FAIL`, `UNKNOWN`, with `NOT_APPLICABLE` where appropriate—rather than converting missing attributes into either eligibility or ineligibility.

### 5. Resource and conflict-of-interest controls

- Resolve requester, approver, reviewer, and delegates to canonical human identities valid at the event time. SUP-712 must fail even if two user IDs differ.
- Verify procurement authority and delegation scope, amount, and validity interval. Delegation cannot circumvent requester/approver separation.
- For auto-approval, control rule deployment, eligibility-data modification, and service ownership; auto-approval must not become self-approval through data manipulation.
- Treat spousal, reporting-line, and similar relationships according to the COI policy. At minimum require timely disclosure and an authoritative disposition. Unless the policy expressly requires recusal or prohibits the relationship, nondisclosure should not automatically be equated with biased review or an invalid activation.

Identity, amount, relationship, and authority predicates should not be multiplied into unmanageable POWL XOR branches.

### 6. Overlay and model governance

Organizational controls must require:

- Named issuer, approver, delegation chain, authority document, scope, effective interval, expiry behavior, monitoring, and rollback for every pilot or emergency.
- Explicit distinction between authority to operate a pilot and authority to derogate from CS-9.
- Automatic workflow disablement of expired routes and alerts on attempted post-expiry use.
- Formal approval and an effective date for any permanent low-risk auto route.
- Separate change control for policy relaxations, POWL revisions, activity dictionaries, and data repairs.
- Aggregate reporting separated into true control failures, justified exceptions, model-only deviations, and data-unknown cases.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

The following is a **proposed structural union**, not a retrospective authorization. In particular, its auto and emergency branches must be disabled by the policy profile unless an authorized, effective overlay applies. Without those external guards, the model would wrongly make ineligible or post-expiry cases structurally acceptable.

The information-request construction uses process-tree loop semantics: `LOOP(do, redo)` represents `do (redo do)*`; the silent redo makes each additional iteration produce another visible request, and the outer XOR permits zero requests. It models requests, not evidence receipt, because no historical receipt activity was supplied. The data layer must therefore ensure that blocking requests were satisfied or cancelled. If a selected PM4Py exporter or conformance implementation cannot execute this loop with a silent redo, a documented finite chain of optional requests may be used for analysis; cases exceeding the bound must be reported as a model limitation, not silently filtered or failed.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def partial_order(nodes, edges=()):
    """Construct a POWL partial order and add only the stated precedence edges."""
    result = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    for source, target in edges:
        result.order.add_edge(source, target)
    return result


def optional(node):
    return OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[SilentTransition(), node],
    )


# Zero or more Request_More_Info occurrences.
# LOOP(do, redo) = do (redo do)*; the outer optional() supplies zero occurrences.
rmi_loop = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[
        Transition(label="Request_More_Info"),
        SilentTransition(),
    ],
)
rmi_optional = optional(rmi_loop)

# Standard route:
# - Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review have no edge between them: both required,
#   concurrent in the POWL sense.
# - Both checks and all information requests finish before manual approval.
# - Manual approval precedes activation.
s_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
s_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
s_manual = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
s_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")

standard_route = partial_order(
    nodes=[s_sanctions, s_quality, rmi_optional, s_manual, s_activate],
    edges=[
        (s_sanctions, s_manual),
        (s_quality, s_manual),
        (rmi_optional, s_manual),
        (s_manual, s_activate),
    ],
)

# Low-risk auto route:
# - sanctions and automated master-data validation precede Auto_Approve and
#   therefore activation;
# - Quality_Review is required but deliberately has no edge to activation,
#   allowing it before or after provisional activation;
# - eligibility, provisional status, and the two-business-day deadline are
#   external data rules.
a_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
a_master_data = Transition(label="Automated_Master_Data_Validation")
a_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
a_auto = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
a_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")  # route/mode stored in data

auto_route = partial_order(
    nodes=[a_sanctions, a_master_data, a_quality, a_auto, a_activate],
    edges=[
        (a_sanctions, a_auto),
        (a_master_data, a_auto),
        (a_auto, a_activate),
    ],
)

# Emergency/provisional route:
# - sanctions always precede activation;
# - quality review and retrospective manual approval are required eventually,
#   but may occur before or after provisional activation;
# - cap, daily monitoring, deadlines, emergency scope, and expiry are external.
e_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
e_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
e_manual = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
e_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")  # provisional mode in data

emergency_route = partial_order(
    nodes=[e_sanctions, e_quality, e_manual, e_activate],
    edges=[
        (e_sanctions, e_activate),
    ],
)

# Termination without activation. Optional checks accommodate work already
# performed before rejection/withdrawal without making quality review mandatory
# merely to reject a lead. Scope rules still distinguish pre-screen leads.
t_opt_sanctions = optional(Transition(label="Sanctions_Check"))
t_opt_quality = optional(Transition(label="Quality_Review"))
t_choice = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[
        Transition(label="Reject_Supplier"),
        Transition(label="Withdraw_Onboarding"),
    ],
)
termination_route = partial_order(
    nodes=[t_opt_sanctions, t_opt_quality, t_choice],
    edges=[
        (t_opt_sanctions, t_choice),
        (t_opt_quality, t_choice),
    ],
)

decision = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[
        standard_route,
        auto_route,
        emergency_route,
        termination_route,
    ],
)

register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

M1_proposed = partial_order(
    nodes=[register, decision, notify, close],
    edges=[
        (register, decision),
        (decision, notify),
        (notify, close),
    ],
)
```

Finally, conformance evaluation should: deduplicate only with source evidence; map `QA_Clearance` through an audited, effective-dated dictionary; reconstruct notification as a logical cross-source activity only when its case link and timing are reliable; select rules by scope and effective time; and retain both the **raw M0 deviation** and the **profile-specific business-rule result**. This prevents data repair from becoming policy revision, while also preventing an incomplete log or model from manufacturing noncompliance.
