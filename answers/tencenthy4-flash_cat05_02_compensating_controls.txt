## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

Legend: **P** = prevents, **D** = detects, **C** = corrects/stops before loss becomes irrevocable, **F** = fails or materially fails to address.

| FEP-1 objective / threat scenario | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 | Key gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. No single human both initiates and authorizes the same irrevocable transfer | F for segregation itself; may block known bad payments | P if independent and before release/settlement; D if ratification only | D; C only if still recallable | P if token and all conditions are genuinely enforced | Automated validation does not restore human authorization; late ratification is not full four-eyes |
| 2. Correct payee, bank account, amount, duplicate status, sanctions status, PO match | P/D for the listed AV-2 checks; warning can be overridden | P/D if manager substantively reviews invoice, bank details, and support | D after payment; C only in recall window | P via no recent bank change, low amount, AV-2 pass conditions | AV-2 does not prove who controlled a compromised account or that goods/services were received |
| 3. Commercial reasonableness and evidence of receipt | F except basic PO match | P/D only if the manager reviews receipt/service evidence; weak if rubber-stamped | D; fabricated receipts may be found later | F by itself; emergency does not prove commercial validity | AP-204 and AP-202 show why payment-integrity checks alone are insufficient |
| 4. Operator authority, limits, and override governance | P/D for limit checks, but warning overrides can defeat the control | P if independent L3+ manager with no conflict; F if subordinate, conflicted, or credential-sharing unresolved | D of override patterns; C rarely after settlement | P if amount below EUR 100,000 and conditions met | Same-user override using a manager credential is not independent authorization |
| 5. Emergency legitimacy and necessity | F except rule execution | P if manager review happens before settlement and is substantive | D/C if audit within one business day and recall still possible | P if token was active before Prepare_Payment and all other conditions hold | ED-1 is a conditional permission, not a waiver of the four-eyes objective |
| 6. Independence against collusion, shared workstation, or dedicated two-person cell | F; rules do not assess social/organizational independence | P only if manager is outside the pair and conflict-free | D via pattern analytics; C usually limited | F unless paired with genuine independent MG-4 | Nominal two-user separation can be ineffective if the “two” users are not operationally independent |
| 7. Auditability, recoverability, and non-repudiation | D through logs; useful only if logs are complete | D through review evidence and approval artifacts | D/C depending on timing and recall | P/D only through required MG-4 and RA-7 conditions | Audit after settlement or recall window is detective only; it does not restore authorization |

ED-1 is best treated as a governed exception path. It reduces risk only when all conditions are satisfied and evidenced; it does not make the underlying FEP-1 objective disappear.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A compensating-control package should be assessed against the control objective, not merely against the presence of control activities.

| Dimension | Fully equivalent | Partially equivalent / tolerable | Merely mitigating | Ineffective |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Timing** | Independent review or block occurs before release or at least before irrevocable settlement | Independent review after release but before settlement, with ability to stop payment | Detective audit after settlement but within recall window | Ratification/audit after settlement and outside practical recall |
| **Independence** | Reviewer is L3+, independent of preparer/releaser, conflict-free, and not part of the same pair | Mostly independent, but minor documentation gaps | Manager is formally independent but review is cursory or relationship unclear | Reviewer is the same user, subordinate, conflicted, or override credential is controlled/used by operator |
| **Coverage** | Covers bank ownership, recent bank change, duplicate invoice, sanctions, amount limits, PO match, commercial support, and receipt evidence | Covers most automated checks plus substantive review of key high-risk facts | Covers only AV-2 checks or only post-payment audit | AV-2 unavailable, unsigned checklist, no receipt/bank ownership evidence |
| **Overrideability** | Overrides impossible or require independent dual approval with evidence | Warnings exist but override governance mostly controlled | Warning can be overridden by same operator with weak follow-up | Same user overrides bank-change or limit warning using assigned/accessibly manager credential |
| **Recoverability** | Defect can be stopped before settlement; otherwise recall path is timely and evidenced | Some recall chance remains | Recall uncertain | Funds settled and unrecovered; audit only documents failure |
| **Evidence quality** | Logs show token timing, individual AV checks, reviewer identity/level, reviewed artifacts, and audit completion | Some artifacts missing but core facts evidenced | Event names exist without review content | Contradictory, late, unsigned, or missing evidence |
| **Collusion resistance** | Independent reviewer is outside repeated pair and shared workstation context | Repeated pair exists but no adverse evidence and independent review present | Pattern alert exists but no resolution | Shared workstation, repeated pair, fabricated support, or unresolved relationship strongly indicate objective failure |

**Why “same expected loss” is not the only standard:**  
Expected loss may look similar under different controls, but controls differ in irreversibility, deterrence, independence, evidence quality, fraud intent, detectability, recoverability, and organizational design. A detective audit that recovers the same average amount does not restore the authorization objective. A preventive control that stops defects before settlement also reduces opportunities and creates deterrence in a way a later audit cannot. Conversely, a low-value same-user payment can still be objectionable if the exception was convenience-based and undocumented, while a nominally four-eyes-compliant payment can fail the objective if the two users are functionally a single cell.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control pattern | Classification | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Separate preparer and releaser before Prepare/Release | Preventive | Direct FEP-1 control; prevents one person from holding both initiation and authorization rights |
| AV-2 full pass before release | Preventive, with detective elements | Strong for covered automated checks; weak for receipt, commercial reasonableness, collusion, and compromised-account control |
| AV-2 warning that the same user can override | Weak preventive / mostly illusory if override is uncontrolled | The system detects a condition, but the operator can defeat it; override governance becomes the real control |
| AV-2 block | Preventive | Stops completion before release; AP-205 shows loss prevention even with same-user attempt |
| Independent MG-4 before release | Preventive and strongly equivalent when substantive | Independent human authorization occurs before the payment action |
| Manager action after Release_Payment but before Bank_Settlement | **Hybrid**, chosen here as detective relative to release and preventive relative to irrevocable loss | The release already happened, so it does not fully restore ex ante segregation. However, because settlement is the internal-control irreversibility point and the manager can still stop/cancel, it prevents erroneous or fraudulent irrevocable transfer if review is substantive |
| Manager action after settlement | Detective only | It may document or investigate the defect but does not prevent the controlled loss event |
| RA-7 audit within 24 hours / recall window | Detective with partial corrective capacity | Detect defect early enough to stop, recall, or recover |
| RA-7 audit after recall window | Detective only | Useful for learning, discipline, and pattern detection; not corrective for the payment |
| ED-1 with all conditions satisfied | Preventive governance package | Permits single-operator handling only because emergency necessity, AV-2, MG-4, and RA-7 jointly reduce the risk |
| ED-1 with missing token, unavailable AV-2, or late manager | Ineffective as permission | The label “emergency” does not cure absent conditions |

The chosen semantics for “after release but before settlement” are objective-dependent. For narrow FEP-1 activity sequencing, it is not a pure preventive substitute. For the broader objective of preventing an erroneous or fraudulent irrevocable transfer, it is hybrid and can be sufficient when the reviewer is independent, substantive, and able to stop settlement.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

**Evidence collection**

- Capture raw emergency-token logs: token creation time, activation time, creator, approver, justification, and whether it preceded `Prepare_Payment`.
- Preserve AV-2 detail: each individual check, rule version, warning/block result, override actor, override reason, and whether the override credential belonged to another person.
- For MG-4, collect reviewer identity, level, relationship/conflict declaration, timestamp relative to release and settlement, and the actual artifacts reviewed: invoice, bank details, receipt/service report, vendor master data.
- For RA-7, collect audit start and completion timestamps, defect found/not found, recall attempt, bank response, and recovery outcome. Do not treat “audit started” as “audit completed.”
- Collect bank-change evidence: effective date, requester, approver, callback verification, account ownership proof, and payments to the vendor within seven days of the change.
- Collect workstation and session evidence: login ID, device, IP, concurrent sessions, shared desktop use, and whether two “users” used the same terminal.
- Collect organizational design evidence for dedicated payment cells; repeated pairs may be legitimate role design, but missing documentation leaves independence unresolved.
- Collect full population evidence for repeated pairs, not only the flagged cases.

**Monitoring rules**

- **Same-user rate:** monitor completed payments where `Prepare_Payment` and `Release_Payment` share a human resource; exclude system events and blocked attempts from the denominator.
- **Override rate:** alert on any override of AV-2 bank-change, sanctions, duplicate, or amount-limit warnings, especially where the override credential belongs to a manager but the operator uses it.
- **Late manager approval:** distinguish before-release approval, after-release-before-settlement hybrid approval, and after-settlement ratification. Alert on the latter two for completed payments.
- **Audit-before-recall-window:** require 100% audit within one business day for same-user or ED-1 payments; separately flag whether audit occurred before the practical recall window.
- **Repeated resource pairs:** flag pairs appearing repeatedly in a short window, such as u12/u13 in AP-204/AP-209. This is an alert for review, not proof of misconduct.
- **Shared workstations:** flag prepare/release pairs executed from the same workstation, terminal, or concurrent session.
- **Bank-change proximity:** alert when first payment to a vendor occurs shortly after a bank-account change, especially with new vendors or emergency tokens.
- **Control outages:** alert when AV-2 is unavailable and payment proceeds with unsigned or incomplete manual checklist.
- **Emergency misuse:** alert when token activation is after Prepare, vendor age is under 180 days, amount is at/above EUR 100,000, bank changed within seven days, or audit is late.
- **Conflict and credential governance:** alert when an approver directly supervises the operator or when override credentials are used by anyone other than the assigned manager.

All such rules generate **investigative alerts**, not conclusions. A repeated pair may be a legitimate dedicated cell; no defect found may mean no defect existed; denial of collusion is neither proof nor disproof.
