## 8. Overrepair warnings (5 specific ways an aggressive repair could create false process behavior)

1. **Forcing a unique delivery for `Confirm Delivery`** – If we added an O2O edge linking `ORDA` (or both orders) to `DELX` or `DELY`, the OTG might show a mandatory `Delivered` transition from `Confirm Delivery` to the delivery object, implying that the portal never lost the carrier identifier.  In reality the portal may acknowledge the order without carrier info, leading to a spurious “delivery confirmed” step.

2. **Adding `PK7 packs ITB1`** – Because scanner outage could produce an itemonly row, linking ITB1 to PK7 would incorrectly suggest that the item was packed into the wrong package before PK8 existed, potentially creating a backward pack transition in the OCDFG.

3. **Linking `PAY2` to `INV44` without checking cash application** – Aggressive repair might assume PAY2 settles the remaining invoice amount.  If the business actually holds PAY2 as unapplied cash (e.g., for a later shipment), the flattened payment trace would show an early settlement, misleading downstream cashflow analysis.

4. **Adding both `PK8 packs ITB1` and `DELY carries PK8` while keeping `ev08` only linked to PK8** – If the scanner outage produced an itemonly row that later gets packed into PK7 (instead of PK8), the O2O `PK8 packs ITB1` would contradict the actual pack event, causing the OTG to contain a nontemporal packcarry edge that appears before the pack event (since O2O edges ignore timestamps).  This could be interpreted as the package carrying the item before it was packed.

5. **Treating amount equality (`total=230` = `130+100`) as proof that the invoice bills both orders** – Adding `INV44 bills ORDB` based solely on arithmetic could hide a legitimate scenario where two separate invoices are issued (e.g., one per order).  The enriched OTG would then merge the two orders under a single invoice node, obscuring orderlevel billing details.

6. **Creating a circular O2O edge** – If we inferred `PK7 carries DELX` (in addition to `DELX carries PK7`) or `ITA1 carries PK7`, the O2O graph would contain a 2cycle.  Some processmining tools detect cycles and may either ignore them or produce ambiguous traces, leading to incorrect process discovery.

7. **Overclustering orders due to shared package** – Adding O2O `PK8 packs ITA2` (already present) and `PK8 packs ITB1` plus `DELY carries PK8` might cause a flattening algorithm that merges packages with orders to group ORDA and ORDB together under PK8, producing a single “orderpackage” cluster even though the orders are distinct.  This could mask the true twoorder workflow.

These warnings highlight that aggressive repairs—i.e., adding all plausible O2O edges without marking them provisional, or forcing unique delivery links—can introduce spurious transitions, ignore scanneroutage possibilities, and create cycles or overclustering.  The conservative bundles (A and B) explicitly mark added edges as provisional and keep unresolved candidates, thereby reducing the risk of false process behavior.
