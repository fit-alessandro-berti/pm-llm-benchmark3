## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands
We use **interventional (stochastic) indirect effects and controlled / separable effects**, not natural direct/indirect effects. Natural effects require cross-world independence `Y(a,m)  M(a')` and no exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounding and no interference. All fail here: flag induces queue congestion that changes `manual_queue_wait` for other cases (interference), `arrival_load` and triage duration are mediator-outcome confounders affected by flag via priority lane (exposure-induced confounding), and setting `flag=0`  changing the organization’s response to the flag. Separable effects decompose flag into `flag_priority` and `flag_review_rule` components that can in principle be set independently.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification
| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| does flag increase total cycle_time net of case-mix? | within risk_score RDD / local threshold: compare cases just above vs below flag threshold, using risk_score forcing variable | risk_score, complexity, arrival_load slice, time-of-day | continuity of potential outcomes at threshold; no manipulation of risk_score; compliance sharp | McCrary density test on risk_score, placebo threshold, covariate balance at cutoff | no jump in cycle_time or manual_review at threshold would imply observed gap is case-mix not flag |
| does queue state mediate or confound? | queue-state-aware analysis: model manual_queue_wait as function of flag-induced queue length at triage_done time (number waiting, senior utilization) |arrival_load, clock time, complexity, triage_resource, queue length at moment of triage_done | queue length is pre-mediator confounder not collider; no measurement error in timestamps | compare estimates with/without queue conditioning; test if flag still predicts wait after exact queue length adjustment | flag effect on wait disappears after queue adjustment  supports queue-interference mechanism over flag-direct delay |
| what is total policy effect of flag bundle? | policy-change or randomized encouragement: A/B hide flag (P1) or randomize priority lane while holding info constant; cluster randomization by day to manage interference | stratification by arrival_load, complexity | encouragement affects outcome only via flag visibility (exclusion), no Hawthorne; interference contained within cluster | intention-to-treat, balance checks, first-stage (flag displayreview rate) | small ITT on cycle_time despite strong first-stage would refute large causal claim from naive contrast |
| occurrence vs waiting mediation | mediation-oriented interventional decomposition: compare P0 vs P2 on hypothetical stochastic mediator distributions; compute IIE for manual_review occurrence and manual_queue_wait separately via g-computation with queue simulation | risk_score, complexity, arrival_load, triage outcomes (as exposure-induced confounder handled via interventional formula) | interventional identifiability: no unmeasured mediator-outcome confounder conditional; consistency for stochastic interventions | bootstrap, sensitivity to mediator model, compare CDE when fixing manual_review=0 via simulation | large CDE (flag still lengthens cycle even with no review) would implicate priority-induced behavior change, not just occurrence |
| is association spurious due to time/location? | negative-control outcome/exposure: use outcome that cannot be affected by flag (e.g., pre-flag historical cycle_time for same hour-of-week) or exposure that cannot affect triage (future flag status) | same adjustments as main | sharp null for controls; exchangeability holds | negative control should show zero effect; if it shows similar flagged advantage/delay, indicates temporal confounding | positive association on negative control  observed gap is calendar/operational confounding |
| how sensitive to hidden risk? | sensitivity analysis for U_underlying_risk: simulate unmeasured confounder that drives both risk_score error and processing time; bound total effect using Rosenbaum or VanderWeele bias formulas | observed risk_score, complexity | plausible range for U prevalence and effect size informed by domain | tipping-point analysis: how strong must U be to explain 8h total gap and 100% review rate? | small tipping strength would mean naive mediation collapses; large required strength would increase credibility |
| does spillover bias individual estimate? | interference-aware design: two-stage randomization (first randomize cluster-level flag prevalence, then individual flag within cluster) + queue simulation; estimate direct and spillover effects | cluster, arrival_load, complexity | partial interference within cluster-day, stratified interference | test for spillover: does unflagged cycle_time increase with cluster flagged proportion? Dose-response with queue length | significant spillover slope would reverse recommendation to simply suppress flag without adding capacity |

## 6. Policy comparison
| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 keep current | faster triage (-2h) | high: +4-6h manual wait, +7h escalation wait for many | long: ~16.5h mean, high variance with escalation | over-review may catch risk but causes fatigue/queue delay; compliance okay | congests shared queues for all | moderate: consistent with process logs but confounded, small n | baseline for comparison only |
| P1 suppress display, leave score/human info | triage slows to unflagged baseline (+2h) | reduces but not eliminates: reviewers still use risk_score/human judgment; queue pressure falls partially | ambiguous: saves manual queue but loses triage speed; net maybe 10-12h if review rate drops only modestly | high: flag blindness may miss high-risk if human judgment imperfect | moderate spillover benefit | weak: assumes flag is only display, ignores that underlying risk still prompts review | risky without evidence human judgment substitutes |
| P2 retain priority triage, remove mandatory review | triage fast preserved | large drop: manual incidence from 100% to ~30-40% flagged, queue wait cut ~50% | likely biggest cycle-time reduction: flagged without escalation ~9h | moderate-high: must ensure high-risk not under-reviewed; need training/audit | positive spillover: frees capacity for all | moderate: directly targets observed biggest mediator (occurrence) | promising if escalation rule preserved; needs quality guardrail |
| P3 retain routing, add protected manual capacity for flagged | triage fast + parallel lane keeps wait low | flagged wait falls (maybe 6.52-3h), unflagged may worsen if zero-sum unless net capacity added | flagged ~12-13h, unflagged may rise slightly; net system maybe neutral unless funded | low: maintains compliance/quality signal | negative spillover if capacity taken from pool; requires true extra staffing | moderate-strong if capacity funded | attractive where compliance required but needs investment |
| P4 retain review, change escalation criteria/capacity | minimal triage change | escalation wait falls if senior capacity added; review queue unchanged | trims tail: flagged-with-escalation 2115h, modest mean improvement | moderate: stricter escalation may miss true positives; looser may overload | concentrated on scarce senior layer | moderate: escalation is smaller n but long tail driver | complementary to P2/P3; alone insufficient |

**Pilot for P2 (retain priority triage, remove mandatory review):** Eligibility: cases flagged by current risk_score threshold arriving Normal and High load, medium+high complexity where flag support exists. Randomization: individual-level 1:1 flag-blind review encouraged vs mandatory, cluster by day to limit cross-contamination, with queue-capacity guardrail capping concurrent manual reviews per reviewer and pausing enrollment if manual_queue_wait >6h or senior backlog >8h. Primary outcome total cycle_time; secondary triage-time, manual_review rate, escalation rate, decision accuracy via independent audit and downstream appeal/error rate. Minimum data: case arrival, all start/done timestamps, resource IDs, queue length at each transition, risk_score, complexity, flag, outcome, reviewer notes. Stopping: safety stop if audit shows >3pp miss-rate increase; efficacy stop if mean cycle_time reduction >25% with non-inferior quality; futility if review rate unchanged.

## 8. Reversal conditions
1. **Hidden risk dominates:** if `U_underlying_risk` strongly predicts both flag and processing need, and `risk_score` omits key risk, then within-threshold or covariate-adjusted effects vanish and the observed longer cycle is case-mix, not flag causation; mandatory review may be appropriate.
2. **Queue interference underestimated:** if spillover test shows unflagged waits rise steeply with flagged prevalence (e.g., +4h per 10pp flagged share), then suppressing only individual flags yields little benefit; system-level cycle_time remains long and P3 with added capacity would reverse P2’s apparent advantage.
3. **Decision quality depends on review:** if audit shows manual/escalation prevents material errors (reject accuracy, downstream rework) and P2 pilot increases miss-rate or appeal rate, then shorter cycle_time is not welfare-improving and the quality-adjusted recommendation reverses toward P0/P3.
4. **Timestamp semantics misread:** if `triage_start` reflects queue assignment rather than work start, or `manual_start` is logged when request is sent rather than work begins, then arrival-to-triage vs manual-wait decomposition is invalid and the triage-acceleration finding reverses.
5. **Positivity / lack of overlap:** if there are zero flagged low-complexity and zero unflagged high-risk with risk_score >0.85, then threshold-comparison and adjusted estimates have no support; extrapolation would reverse under alternative modeling assumptions that penalize flagged complexity.
6. **Reviewer behavior change (Hawthorne/offset):** if removing mandatory review causes reviewers to prolong human-triggered reviews for flagged cases (compensatory scrutiny) or to rush and degrade service, then P2’s predicted reduction in manual incidence/wait fails to translate into cycle-time gains.

## 9. Final synthesis
What is directly observed: flagged cases start triage ~2.2h earlier and have ~0.35h shorter triage service, but average total cycle ~8h longer (16.5h vs 8.2h) with high variance. All 10 flagged cases receive manual review vs 4/10 unflagged, and 6/10 vs 2/10 escalate; conditional manual wait is 6.5h vs 1.9h and escalation adds ~9h tail. High complexity drives long totals independent of flag, and small-n subgroups are sparse, limiting precision. What is plausible mechanism: flag bundle combines beneficial priority lane with downstream congestion via mandatory review and scarce senior escalation using shared queues. Longer cycle likely reflects occurrence mediation plus queue-interference rather than flag itself slowing work; interference amplifies individual delays into system delays. What remains unidentified: whether flag causes review or proxies hidden risk, the separate contributions of occurrence vs waiting, cross-world mediator effects under exposure-induced confounding, magnitude of spillover, and quality implications of faster throughput. Which intervention yields most useful evidence: a limited P2 pilot retaining priority lane but removing mandatory review, cluster-randomized with capacity guardrails and audit of decision quality, directly tests the largest mediator while preserving triage gains and measuring spillover, providing an interventional total effect without requiring untestable natural-mediation assumptions.
