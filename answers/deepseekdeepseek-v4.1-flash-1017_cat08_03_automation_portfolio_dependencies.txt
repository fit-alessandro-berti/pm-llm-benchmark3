## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

**Upstream data quality dependencies.** A9 depends on clean KYC status, committee decision data, and accurate contract terms; it should not generate contracts while the `KYC -> Resolve entity mismatch -> KYC` loop is unresolved. A8 committee-pack assembly depends on A6-normalized statements, A4-cleared KYC status, collateral valuation status, and credit-analysis notes; otherwise it automates garbage-in/garbage-out. A2 classification affects routing: DFG shows `Classify -> KYC` 7,250 and `Classify -> Financial document review` 3,940, so classification errors misdirect work and increase later rework. A5 improves input documents but should enforce RM/customer completeness; it can reduce the `Financial document review -> Request missing documents` 3,300 and return 2,800 loop.

**Legal/compliance controls.** KYC clearance must precede customer signing; the observed 6.5% provisional signing and 1.1% remediation mean A10/A11 must be hard-gated by KYC status and cannot rely on reminders alone. A9 must enforce that contract terms match the committee decision, address the `Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue` 650 and return 510 loop, and preserve model suggestion, human override, and final-decision audit. Four-eyes for High-exposure SME credit analysis/committee packaging must be enforced in workflow and cannot be automated away; month-end 5.8% violations show the risk. A3 and A4 require conservative escalation and immutable audit trails.

**Human escalation capacity.** A4 increases reliance on the 3 FTE compliance specialists and two senior specialists who already appear central in the handover network. A3 with conservative thresholds will also escalate to compliance ops/specialists; if false positives increase, KYC and signing queues worsen. A6 must route low-quality PDFs to credit analysts; if extraction quality is poor, it simply moves work rather than reducing it. A5 must route unresolved customer documents to RMs/back office without creating customer friction. A9 exceptions go to legal ops, which is underutilized when KYC is slow but overloaded at month end.

**External valuation dependency.** A7 can triage and parallelize valuation requests, but the `Wait for external valuation` activity has 58h waiting time and is outside direct control. A7 should not be treated as a substitute for valuer capacity management. A8 committee packaging waits for valuation when required; equipment finance has 71% valuation incidence and working capital 18%. A7 is a synchronization and routing play, not a full automation of valuation judgment.

**Core-banking integration constraints.** A9 contract generation/control-rule checks and A11 activation are likely deep core-banking/legal integrations. A1, A2, A4, and A5 can be delivered through workflow, portal, or CRM-type integrations. A6 requires document-management and credit-model integration and may also count as a deep integration. The first six months can deeply integrate only two modules, so A5 and A4 should use lighter workflow/portal integration first; A6 can be deep module #1; A9 should be deep module #2 after month six or be introduced first in shadow/rule-check mode.
