## 1. Conformance semantics

A **forbidden** relation is explicitly prohibited within its stated scope. An observed instance is a high-priority violation candidate; it becomes a confirmed process violation when the event identity, object links and ordering are reliable. A **missing mandatory** relation is a breach candidate only when the relevant event or object is in scope, the required observation window has elapsed, and the required behavior or association is actually absent. Graph-wide presence does not prove that every applicable instance complies.

An **unexpected but unregulated** relation is outside the norm's coverage; it is not a violation merely because it is absent from a graph. A **typical-pattern absence** calls for contextual review, not an automatic breach. A **likely data-quality error** is a suspected defect in recorded identifiers, links, timestamps or labels; it can produce an apparent deviation without establishing that the business action happened. An **abstraction mismatch** occurs when a graph proxy differs from the rule, such as adjacency standing in for eventual validation. **Insufficient evidence** applies whenever the summary cannot resolve scope, identity, timing, completeness or an exception.

The norm is closed-world for explicit prohibitions and mandatory obligations in their stated scope, not for all graph edges. It is open-world for unmentioned and explicitly unspecified relations. An edge that PM4Py calls additional, missing or different in frequency is therefore a diagnostic to interpret, not a legal or business verdict. No frequency threshold is supplied.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

An OC-DFG summarizes activity pairs that directly follow one another along objects of a given type. It can establish that the specified adjacent patterns were observed and provide their reported counts, but it cannot reconstruct all complete lifecycles or reliably determine scope, deadlines, missing events or reasons. Counts of different edges can overlap; they should not be added or subtracted as though they were disjoint cases. The 83 Validate → Close and 72 Validate → Issue Invoice observations therefore do not determine the number of orders closed, or lacking closure.

N1 requires eventual validation for each newly created **standard** Order. Direct adjacency is only a proxy: an intervening Manual Compliance Review can make Create → Validate absent even when validation occurs later. The two missing direct pairs are candidates for investigation, not two proven violations; the three review paths are not necessarily the same two orders. Establish standard-order scope, the observation cutoff and later validation on each lifecycle. Similarly, 198/200 for N4 warrants checking both external-shipment package paths and what the norm means by requiring the direct pair; intervening events or incomplete logs could account for it. By contrast, an accurately ordered Cancel → Dispatch for the same Order satisfies the evidence needed for N3's forbidden temporal pattern. N3 also prohibits later dispatch with intermediate events, which this single direct edge does not exhaustively detect.

For N5, 92/110 direct payment successors, four write-offs and 14 no-successor observations describe patterns, not payment compliance or delinquency. Write-offs and open terms may be legitimate; ascertain financial outcomes and time windows. A direct Issue → Payment edge may be typical at the graph level yet an individual Record Payment may still lack its mandatory Invoice association under E8.

### OTG

The OTG summarizes type-level relationships and weights, including interaction, cobirth and codeath. Its existence and frequency do not identify which event has which links, prove all Add Item or Dispatch events meet T1 or T2, or establish the real-world identities and lifecycle boundaries of the objects. Weight semantics and possible multiple objects per event matter; do not assume a weight equals a count of compliant events.

T1 and T2 edges are present, yet the ET–OT summary raises possible E2 and definite reported E6 event-link gaps. T3's 92 Invoice–Payment interactions are typical, while E8 imposes a stricter event-level association unless explicitly typed as unapplied. T6's single cobirth is a prohibited graph observation, subject to validation of the common birth event and IDs. T4 and T5 are unspecified, and the seven codeaths are explained by confirmation events; none is a breach solely because another graph omits it.

### ET–OT

ET–OT reports event-type/object-type associations and weights. It identifies that associations exist and, with known counting conventions, highlights mismatches. It does not by itself show which individual events lack each required type, whether two type links belong to the same event, whether duplicate or multiple object links inflate weights, or whether an exception applies. E1–E8 must be checked per event, not merely by finding each edge globally.

The context establishes two Dispatch events without Delivery and one Record Payment event without Invoice; E6 and, on the recorded typing, E8 are mandatory gaps. A carrier reference may help recover a Delivery, but does not itself satisfy E6. The 1,448 versus 1,450 Add Item weights raise an E2/E3/T1 concern without establishing exactly two missing Order links. Review–RiskCase is explicitly unspecified and Review–Customer is unregulated. Sensor–Invoice violates E10 at the recorded-link level, yet the suspected reused-ID join means it is not evidence of an actual sensor/finance process interaction until validated.

- **Mandatory:** N1 Create → eventual Validate for standard Orders: 98 direct, two direct gaps pending lifecycle and scope checks. N4 Pack → Dispatch: 198/200 scoped packages, two direct gaps pending sequence interpretation. T1/T2 and E1–E8 apply per scoped interaction or event: their aggregate edges exist, but E6 has two known missing links, E8 one untyped missing link, and E2/E3/T1 require event-level checking. These are confirmed recorded patterns, with underlying process breaches conditional on reliable links, identities and ordering; E10 is particularly likely to be a join error.
- **Typical:** N2 Validate → Close: 83 direct occurrences, with intermediate invoice, review or fulfilment permitted. N5 Issue → Payment: 92/110 direct, four Issue → Write Off and 14 with no observed successor; legitimate exceptions and observation windows matter. ## 5. PM4Py conformance code sketch

The bare graphs do not encode normative status, scope, exceptions, deadlines or data provenance. Maintain those in a separately versioned policy registry; the example below reports graph-level candidates and explicitly defers instance-level decisions. It uses the OC-DFG `event_couples` layout of the discovery result; adapt the extractor if the installed PM4Py version uses a different layout.

```python
import pm4py

# Inputs supplied by the caller: ocel, norm_ocdfg, norm_otg, norm_etot.
observed = {
    "ocdfg": pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel),
    "otg": pm4py.discover_otg(ocel),
    "etot": pm4py.discover_etot(ocel),
}
raw_diagnostics = {
    "ocdfg": pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(observed["ocdfg"], norm_ocdfg),
    "otg": pm4py.conformance_otg(observed["otg"], norm_otg),
    "etot": pm4py.conformance_etot(observed["etot"], norm_etot),
}

# Policy metadata lives outside the graph structures. Store scope, exceptions,
# eventuality, identity rules and policy version alongside these statuses.
policy = {
    "ocdfg": {
        ("Order", "Create Order", "Validate Order"): ("N1", "mandatory"),
        ("Order", "Validate Order", "Close Order"): ("N2", "typical"),
        ("Order", "Cancel Order", "Dispatch Package"): ("N3", "forbidden"),
        ("Package", "Pack Package", "Dispatch Package"): ("N4", "mandatory"),
        ("Invoice", "Issue Invoice", "Record Payment"): ("N5", "typical"),
        ("Order", "Reopen Order", "Close Order"): ("N6", "unspecified"),
    },
    "otg": {
        ("Order", "object_interaction", "Item"): ("T1", "mandatory"),
        ("Package", "object_interaction", "Delivery"): ("T2", "mandatory"),
        ("Invoice", "object_interaction", "Payment"): ("T3", "typical"),
        ("Order", "object_interaction", "Customer"): ("T4", "unspecified"),
        ("Order", "object_interaction", "RiskCase"): ("T5", "unspecified"),
        ("Order", "object_cobirth", "Payment"): ("T6", "forbidden"),
    },
    "etot": {
        ("Create Order", "Order"): ("E1", "mandatory"),
        ("Add Item", "Order"): ("E2", "mandatory"),
        ("Add Item", "Item"): ("E3", "mandatory"),
        ("Pack Package", "Package"): ("E4", "mandatory"),
        ("Dispatch Package", "Package"): ("E5", "mandatory"),
        ("Dispatch Package", "Delivery"): ("E6", "mandatory"),
        ("Record Payment", "Payment"): ("E7", "mandatory"),
        ("Record Payment", "Invoice"): ("E8", "mandatory"),
        ("Manual Compliance Review", "RiskCase"): ("E9", "unspecified"),
        ("Sensor Heartbeat", "Invoice"): ("E10", "forbidden"),
    },
}

# Preserve object type for OC-DFG edges; do not rely on graph-comparison
# diagnostics alone, which may aggregate away that distinction.
oc_edges = observed["ocdfg"].get("edges", {}).get("event_couples", {})
oc_keys = {(ot, a, b) for ot, flows in oc_edges.items() for a, b in flows}
otg_keys = set(observed["otg"][1])
etot_keys = set(observed["etot"][2])
observed_keys = {"ocdfg": oc_keys, "otg": otg_keys, "etot": etot_keys}

semantic_findings = []
for family, keys in observed_keys.items():
    for edge in keys | set(policy[family]):
        ref, status = policy[family].get(edge, (None, "unspecified"))
        present = edge in keys
        if status == "forbidden" and present:
            result = "forbidden recorded edge; validate instances and provenance"
        elif status == "mandatory":
            result = ("edge present; per-instance compliance still unverified"
                      if present else
                      "edge absent; check applicable instances, scope and completeness")
        elif status == "typical":
            result = "typical edge present" if present else "typical edge absent; review context"
        elif present:
            result = "observed but unregulated by this policy"
        else:
            result = "unregulated edge not observed"
        semantic_findings.append({"family": family, "edge": edge, "norm_id": ref,
                                  "status": status, "present": present,
                                  "assessment": result})

# Next, inspect individual event/object links and ordered lifecycles in ocel.
# Evaluate N1 eventual validation, N3 all later dispatches, external-shipment
# scope for N4, and E8's explicit unapplied-cash type. Compare source records
# and cutoff times; do not infer compliance from graph-wide presence or weights.
# Return both products without changing or replacing PM4Py's diagnostics.
result = {"observed_graphs": observed, "raw_diagnostics": raw_diagnostics,
          "semantic_findings": semantic_findings}
```

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

The following are proposed policy decisions, not rules already established by the snapshot. Owners should approve them and reconcile them with other policies.

1. **Validation scope and timing (N1).** “Standard” and “eventually” lack an operational definition; cases can remain unresolved indefinitely. Define eligible orders, start time, deadline, exceptions and treatment of open cases. Proposed status: **mandatory** within the defined scope.
2. **Validation adjacency (N1).** A direct-edge check can falsely flag legitimate reviews. State explicitly that intervening activities are allowed and validate the later event on the same Order. Proposed status: **mandatory** eventual validation, with adjacency **explicitly unspecified**.
3. **Cancellation and dispatch (N3).** Reinstatement, partial cancellation and timestamp ties are unclear; an overly broad or narrow check can mishandle shipments. Define cancellation scope, effective time, ties and any authorized reinstatement. Proposed status: **forbidden** dispatch while the applicable cancellation remains effective.
4. **Packing sequence (N4).** The direct-edge proxy does not explain whether intermediate events are permitted, how re-packing works or how external-shipment intent is determined. Clarify those details and the required preceding Pack event for each shipment. Proposed status: **mandatory** packing under defined scope; specify adjacency separately.
5. **Delivery identity (T2/E6).** A carrier reference may exist without a Delivery object link, creating traceability gaps and inconsistent enforcement. Define Delivery creation, identity, required association and acceptable reconciliation delay. Proposed status: **mandatory** association at the defined point.
6. **Unapplied cash (E8/T3).** An unlabeled exception is indistinguishable from a missing link and may misstate receivables. Define who may classify it, event typing, evidence and later matching. Proposed status: **mandatory** explicit typing for the exception and Invoice linkage otherwise.
7. **Invoice outcomes (N5).** “Typical” has no due-date or completion rule; legitimately open invoices and missed collections look alike. Define acceptable closure outcomes, terms and aging measures. Proposed status: **typical** direct payment; any separate settlement deadline requires an explicit business decision.
8. **Reopening and review (N6/T5/E9).** Lack of lifecycle and ownership rules can leave reopened orders or risk cases unresolved. Decide who owns them and whether validation or closure is required after review or reopening. Proposed status: **explicitly unspecified** until owners adopt a rule; avoid inferring one from rarity.
9. **Lifecycle boundaries and keys (T6/E10; codeath).** Unspecified ID namespaces, birth/death definitions and join rules can create false forbidden edges or mask genuine ones. Define source-qualified identifiers, canonical event linkage and lifecycle boundary semantics. Proposed status: **mandatory** data-identity and provenance controls; codeath remains **explicitly unspecified** unless a separate policy governs it.
10. **Customer and other relations (T4 and omissions).** Separate policy ownership and cross-policy precedence are unclear, risking contradictory judgments. Identify the authoritative Customer policy and how it is applied. Proposed status here: **explicitly unspecified**, with governance assigned elsewhere.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

- **Immediate containment:** First trace the two N3 Orders and stop or intercept any still-pending affected dispatch where feasible. Next isolate the T6 cobirth event and preserve its source records. Quarantine the suspected E10 join output so it is not propagated. Trace the two E6 shipment records using their carrier references.
- **Data repair:** Verify source-qualified IDs, joins, timestamps and links before correcting the Sensor–Invoice association. Reconcile Delivery, Order and Invoice associations against source systems; add a missing link or correct an unapplied-cash type only with supporting evidence and audit history. Recompute graphs after repair.
- **Process investigation:** For N3 and T6, inspect original events, object identities and operational outcomes; determine whether process controls failed. Examine individual Add Item events, both N1 and N4 gaps, and the unlinked payment. Reconcile invoice write-offs and open cases to ledger status and cutoff. Escalate only confirmed or material unresolved risks.
- **Norm revision:** Assign owners to the proposed clarifications, particularly N1 eventuality, N3 cancellation scope, N4 sequencing and E8 exception typing. Version the policy metadata separately from graph models and record approvals and effective dates.
- **Monitoring:** Check explicit prohibitions and scoped mandatory requirements per event or object, including nonadjacent later dispatch and eventual validation after a suitable observation window. Track data-link completeness and provenance separately from process outcomes. Monitor typical patterns descriptively with context and agreed baselines; do not invent tolerance bands or escalate rare unregulated reviews, reopenings or codeaths.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

The recorded forbidden edges and the explicitly reported missing Delivery and Invoice associations are robust observations of this snapshot; graph presence alone does not establish universal compliance. Conclusions about unregulated or typical paths depend on this deliberately incomplete norm and may change under another applicable policy. The Sensor–Invoice interpretation, and potentially other findings, depends on verifying data quality. Graph summaries alone cannot determine individual scopes, complete event sequences, exception eligibility, source truth or whether a recorded deviation caused a real business violation.
