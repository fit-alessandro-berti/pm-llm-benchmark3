# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

**Resilience** in this case is not simply “orders still moved.” It is the ability to preserve the wholesaler’s core obligations under disruption: hospital supply, product integrity, controlled-substance security, cold-chain integrity, recall traceability, and auditable chain of custody. **Concealed noncompliance** occurs when visible throughput or cycle-time metrics improve while required controls are weakened, delayed, reconstructed, or moved into an after-the-fact remediation queue.

Abductively, the T2 event log suggests a rational response under uncertainty: teams optimized the most visible and mission-critical outcomes—hospital fill rate and physical flow—by substituting human judgment, phone confirmations, spreadsheet holds, and later reconstruction. The fast lane protected hospitals: Hospital-critical median cycle time was only 10h and fill rate remained 91%. But the same period produced 9.5% traceability exceptions, 4.8% missing cold-chain temperature documentation, 2.9% missing controlled-substance dual control, and 1800 direct dispatch-without-scan traces plus 1450 dispatch-without-scan traces after temporary release. That combination indicates that some adaptations preserved service while shifting compliance evidence downstream.

### True resilience

These adaptations are defensible when bounded, documented, and supported by contemporaneous evidence:

- **B. Manual authorization check**: a real control, not a missing control. It should be the default fallback for eligibility checks.
- **F. Temporary release control for controlled substances**: pharmacist review of all controlled-substance orders is appropriate, but it must be extended to cold-chain temperature evidence.
- **A. Hospital-critical fast lane**: valid for prioritizing hospital supply, provided it does not bypass authorization or high-risk product controls.
- **I. Defer noncritical order**: valid capacity management if criteria are explicit, communicated, time-bound, and fair.
- **J. Backlog prioritization**: valid recovery control if the prioritization rules are documented and auditable.
- **K. Reconcile manual reservation**: necessary post-incident recovery, but not a substitute for real-time inventory control.
- **L. Audit remediation**: necessary for cleanup, but only if it restores evidence from valid contemporaneous controls; it must not become a mechanism for backfilling missing compliance.

### Risky shortcuts and concealed noncompliance

These adaptations may preserve throughput but create hidden regulatory, safety, or traceability risk:

- **C. Skip system authorization check**: throughput was preserved, but authorization was not. This is not resilience unless replaced by an approved standing authorization with evidence.
- **G. Dispatch without scan**: the largest traceability gap. It weakens chain of custody and is unacceptable for controlled, cold-chain, and recall-sensitive products.
- **D. Manual inventory snapshot check**: reasonable early, but risky as inventory moved. Stale snapshots can cause wrong lots, overselling, and failed recall traceability.
- **E. Manual pick list with reconstructed scans**: physical flow was maintained, but traceability evidence was delayed and possibly reconstructed.
- **H. Phone proof of delivery**: useful for noncritical ambient deliveries, but weak for controlled-substance or cold-chain deliveries where custody and temperature evidence must be contemporaneous.

### Ambiguous depending on risk appetite

Some adaptations are neither clearly resilient nor clearly noncompliant; their status depends on governance:

- **A. Hospital-critical fast lane**: resilient if it is a triage rule with parallel authorization; risky if it becomes a bypass rule.
- **D. Manual inventory snapshot check**: resilient for the first few hours; noncompliant if used beyond a defined snapshot age.
- **E. Manual pick list**: resilient if the picker records lot, serial, time, quantity, and label evidence; risky if scans are silently reconstructed later.
- **H. Phone POD**: acceptable for low-risk ambient deliveries as a temporary measure; unacceptable for controlled or cold-chain products.
- **I. Defer noncritical order**: resilient if it protects hospital supply under explicit rules; unfair and potentially noncompliant if it silently degrades pharmacy service or lets high-risk orders age.
- **J. Backlog prioritization**: resilient if rules are explicit and auditable; concealed noncompliance if informal staff judgment creates arbitrary customer treatment.

The stabilization metrics make the distinction important. By T4, Hospital-critical median cycle time returned to 8h and fitness improved to 0.90, but traceability exceptions remained 2.2% versus 0.8% baseline, cold-chain documentation missing remained 0.9% versus 0.4%, controlled-substance dual control missing remained 0.5% versus 0.2%, and an audit-remediation queue persisted. Therefore, “normal-looking” throughput after recovery does not prove that compliance was preserved during the incident.

# 4. Recovery process model

**Notation**: `->` means sequence; `[decision]` is a gate; `(activity)` is a process step; `|` means alternative path; `{evidence}` means mandatory capture; `HARD STOP` means the order cannot proceed without the stated control or approved exception.

| Mode | Trigger conditions | Allowed behavior |
|---|---|---|
| **Normal** | WMS, scanning, inventory synchronization, authorization, POD, and invoice systems are available; traceability exceptions below agreed threshold; no cyber suspicion. | Standard process: validate, reserve, pick, pack, quality/release, dispatch, POD, invoice. |
| **Controlled-Degraded** | WMS unavailable for more than 30 minutes, or scanning unavailable for more than 15 minutes for high-risk products; order intake or offline authorization is still possible. | Manual authorization, time-limited inventory snapshots, manual evidence packets, controlled fallback release, no high-risk dispatch without custody evidence. |
| **Manual-Emergency** | No system authorization or reliable inventory synchronization for more than 2 hours; cyber incident suspected; inventory snapshot age exceeds limit. | Restrict to hospital-critical and emergency pharmacy orders; hard stops for controlled-substance, cold-chain, and recall-sensitive products; no ad hoc skip authorization. |
| **Partial-Recovery** | Order intake returns, but inventory synchronization and scanning remain unreliable. | Backlog prioritization, reconciliation of manual reservations, POD remediation, controlled exception workflow. |
| **Stabilization** | Systems mostly stable, but flagged incident cases, reconciliation work, or audit-remediation queue remain above threshold. | Audit remediation, inventory counts, compliance review, regulator notification assessment, playbook update. |

## High-level recovery process tree

```text
START
-> [Incident trigger?]
   -> (Normal)
   -> (Controlled-Degraded)
   -> (Manual-Emergency)
   -> (Partial-Recovery)
   -> (Stabilization)
```

## Minimum controls by product and customer risk

| Risk class | Minimum real-time controls | Acceptable fallback | Hard stop |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Controlled-substance, any customer** | Dual authorization; dual control at pick/pack/release; serialized lot or serial evidence; chain-of-custody; scan or tamper-evident seal plus courier manifest; POD with recipient signature/photo/e-signature. | Manual serialized register, photo of labels/seals, immediate scan when system returns. | No dispatch without dual control and custody evidence; no phone-only POD. |
| **Cold-chain, any customer** | Temperature logger attached before dispatch; temperature record available at release; POD includes temperature export or logger evidence. | Manual logger reading with timestamped photo and courier acknowledgement. | No dispatch without temperature documentation. |
| **Recall-sensitive, any customer** | Lot/serial verification; expiry verification; quarantine if lot or serial cannot be confirmed. | Manual lot capture with photo and dual verification. | No dispatch without lot/serial evidence. |
| **Hospital-critical ambient** | Manual authorization check; inventory snapshot age within 4 hours; pick/pack evidence; POD evidence. | Sealed manifest if scan unavailable; immediate scan on system return. | No dispatch if authorization is missing or snapshot is stale beyond 4 hours. |
| **Pharmacy-standard ambient** | Manual authorization; inventory snapshot age within 8 hours; pick/pack evidence; defer allowed only under explicit rules. | Batch authorization for known customers; photo POD. | No defer beyond 72 hours without escalation and customer communication. |
| **Wholesale-bulk ambient** | Batch manual authorization; inventory snapshot age within 12 hours; defer allowed under explicit rules. | Batch evidence packet; scheduled reconciliation. | No defer beyond 120 hours without cancellation or escalation. |

## Manual evidence capture requirements

For every case processed in Controlled-Degraded or Manual-Emergency mode, create an incident evidence packet:

- `{Incident ID, case ID, timestamp, mode, approver}`
- `{Authorization evidence: offline list version, phone log, customer/product confirmation, approver name}`
- `{Inventory evidence: snapshot timestamp, bin/location, quantity, lot, serial, expiry, counter name}`
- `{Pick evidence: picker name, time, labels, photo of pick location or label}`
- `{Pack evidence: packer name, time, package count, seal number if used}`
- `{Release evidence: pharmacist name, controlled-substance dual signatures, cold-chain logger ID, temperature status, release decision}`
- `{Dispatch evidence: courier name, seal number, manifest, scan status or exception reason}`
- `{POD evidence: recipient name, photo/e-signature/signed paper, time, temperature export if cold-chain}`
- `{Invoice hold evidence: hold reason, approver, release decision, timestamp}`

Handwritten packets must be scanned or photographed into an immutable log daily. Spreadsheet invoice holds should be replaced by a controlled workflow with versioning, approval, and audit trail.

## Backlog prioritization rules

Use explicit risk-based scoring rather than informal staff judgment.

```text
Backlog case
-> [Calculate priority score]
   -> Customer criticality: Hospital-critical highest; Pharmacy-standard medium; Wholesale-bulk lower.
   -> Product risk: Controlled-substance, cold-chain, recall-sensitive highest.
   -> Aging: score increases with age.
   -> Service breach: score increases if SLA is breached.
   -> Expiry/recall risk: score increases if lot or product is at risk.
-> [Apply hard maximum age]
   -> Controlled-substance / cold-chain / recall-sensitive: max 24 hours.
   -> Hospital-critical: max 48 hours.
   -> Pharmacy-standard: max 72 hours.
   -> Wholesale-bulk: max 120 hours.
-> [If max age exceeded]
   -> Escalate, source substitute, quarantine, or cancel with documented customer notification.
```

Fairness guardrails:

- Deferral rules must be published to operations, compliance, and customer service.
- Customer service must communicate expected delay when a case is deferred.
- If Pharmacy-standard fill rate falls below 80% for more than one business day, escalate to incident command.
- If Wholesale-bulk cancellations rise above an agreed threshold, review capacity allocation.
- No controlled-substance, cold-chain, or recall-sensitive order may be deferred solely because of capacity.

## Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow

```text
Operational close
-> [Was case touched in degraded mode or missing scan/authorization/temperature/POD evidence?]
   -> No -> END
   -> Yes -> Event reconstruction
      -> Inventory reconciliation
      -> Quality/release review
      -> Compliance review
      -> [Is contemporaneous evidence sufficient?]
         -> Yes -> Upload evidence, close exception, update WMS -> END
         -> No -> Quarantine / recall assessment / regulator notification assessment
            -> Corrective action -> Close with documented exception -> END
```

Reconciliation and audit remediation must be treated as a capacity-constrained exception process:

1. **Auto-flag** every case processed in degraded mode or with missing scan, authorization, temperature, dual-control, or POD evidence.
2. **Reconstruct events** from manual evidence packets, not from memory or informal spreadsheets.
3. **Reconcile inventory** for affected SKUs, lots, bins, and reservations.
4. **Review quality and compliance** before closing high-risk cases.
5. **Do not backfill missing high-risk controls** as if they occurred in real time. Missing controlled-substance dual control, missing cold-chain temperature record, or missing lot evidence should trigger quarantine, product assessment, and possible regulatory notification.
6. **Assign owners and aging thresholds** for remediation: high-risk cases within 24–48 hours; lower-risk cases within a defined service window.
7. **Close only with evidence** showing either contemporaneous control, approved compensating control, or a documented corrective action.
8. **Update the playbook** based on root causes and residual gaps.

# 5. Resilience indicators

| Category | Leading indicator | Lagging indicator | Target / threshold | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Throughput** | Snapshot age; manual evidence packet completion rate; dispatch exception approval rate. | Median and p90 cycle time by customer segment. | Hospital-critical median <=10h and p90 <=20h during T2; after stabilization, p90 within 1.25x baseline: Hospital <=19h, Pharmacy <=61h, Wholesale <=90h. | Shows whether speed is being preserved without letting inventory or evidence become stale. |
| **Service protection** | Percentage of hospital-critical orders with completed authorization and custody evidence. | Fill rate by customer segment. | Hospital-critical fill >=90%; Pharmacy-standard fill >=80%; Wholesale-bulk fill >=70% during incident. | Protects mission-critical supply without hiding service degradation in other segments. |
| **Traceability** | Dispatch-without-scan rate; reconstructed-scan rate; missing-lot/serial rate. | Traceability exception rate. | High-risk dispatch without scan = 0%; overall dispatch without scan <1%; traceability exceptions <=1%. | Prevents the T2 pattern where physical flow continued but custody evidence was lost. |
| **Control completeness** | Controlled-substance dual-control completion at time of dispatch; cold-chain logger attachment before dispatch. | Controlled-substance dual-control missing; cold-chain temperature documentation missing. | Controlled-substance missing <=0.2%; cold-chain missing <=0.4%. | Ensures that regulated controls are contemporaneous, not remediated after the fact. |
| **Backlog age** | Backlog by segment and product risk; number of defer-rule exceptions. | Cancellations caused by backlog age; orders exceeding maximum age. | Controlled/cold/recall <=24h; Hospital-critical <=48h; Pharmacy-standard <=72h; Wholesale-bulk <=120h. | Makes the T2 backlog and T4 remediation tail visible before they become customer or compliance failures. |
| **Rework** | Spreadsheet invoice hold count; manual event correction count; repack/recheck rate. | Rework / correction case rate. | Rework <=4%; reconstructed scans = 0; spreadsheet holds replaced by workflow. | High rework indicates that early shortcuts were not adequately controlled. |
| **Fairness across customer groups** | Documented defer approvals; customer communication completion; informal priority exceptions. | Fill-rate ratio across segments; cancellation rate by segment. | No segment fill below 70% for more than 24h without escalation; Pharmacy-standard below 80% triggers review. | Prevents “hospital-first” decisions from becoming arbitrary or unfair without governance. |
| **Hidden compliance tail** | Audit-remediation queue size; high-risk remediation open cases; aging of remediation items. | Audit findings; regulator notifications; unresolved missing-control cases. | Remediation queue <1% of cases; high-risk remediation closed within 48h; no backfill of missing controlled/cold/recall controls. | Addresses the stabilization finding that operational metrics recovered while audit remediation persisted. |

# 6. Recommendations for future disruptions

| Priority | Recommendation | Implementation effort | Expected benefit | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Adopt a risk-based degraded-mode playbook** with explicit triggers, triage rules, and hard stops for controlled-substance, cold-chain, and recall-sensitive products. | Medium | Reduces ad hoc skipping, preserves hospital supply, and makes deviations intentional rather than hidden. | Requires training and enforcement; staff may still improvise under pressure. |
| 2 | **Pre-approve an offline authorization package** with versioned customer/product eligibility lists, emergency approvers, and immutable phone-log capture. | Medium | Replaces “skip system authorization check” with a defensible manual control. | Offline lists can become stale if not refreshed. |
| 3 | **Require manual evidence packets or digital photo capture** for every degraded-mode order, including lot, serial, expiry, picker, packer, release, seal, courier, and POD. | Low to medium | Converts manual work into contemporaneous traceability evidence. | Handwriting errors, incomplete forms, and scanning burden. |
| 4 | **Replace dispatch without scan with tamper-evident seals, courier manifests, and immediate POD capture** for all high-risk products. | Medium to high | Closes the largest T2 traceability gap while preserving handoff speed. | Courier noncompliance; need for seals, manifests, and mobile capture. |
| 5 | **Enforce hard stops for controlled-substance, cold-chain, and recall-sensitive products**: no dispatch without dual control, temperature documentation, or lot/serial verification. | Medium | Prevents regulatory exposure and patient-safety risk. | May reduce throughput for high-risk orders during severe outages. |
| 6 | **Build a reconciliation and audit-remediation workflow** with ownership, aging, segregation of duties, and regulator-notification criteria. | High | Makes hidden post-recovery workload visible and prevents after-the-fact evidence from being treated as real-time compliance. | Requires system integration, data quality, and dedicated compliance capacity. |
| 7 | **Formalize backlog prioritization and fairness monitoring** with maximum age rules, customer communication, and segment-level dashboards. | Low to medium | Protects critical customers while reducing arbitrary service degradation and cancellations. | Customer dissatisfaction may persist when capacity is constrained. |
| 8 | **Run tabletop exercises and snapshot refresh drills** for WMS outage, cyber incident, and scanning failure. | Low | Improves activation speed, clarifies roles, and reduces stale inventory use. | Exercises cannot cover every disruption scenario. |

# 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should accept temporary loss of noncritical throughput, higher manual labor, and a visible remediation queue when doing so preserves hospital supply and maintains contemporaneous controls for controlled-substance, cold-chain, and recall-sensitive products. Management should not accept skipped authorization, dispatch without scan, phone-only proof of delivery, stale inventory snapshots, or after-the-fact audit remediation as substitutes for real-time compliance, because those shortcuts can make the process look recovered while leaving hidden regulatory and patient-safety exposure. The recommendation would change if evidence showed that alternative controls produced auditable, contemporaneous authorization, custody, temperature, and lot/serial records; hospital and pharmacy service remained within agreed thresholds; and the remediation queue aged out without regulatory findings. Conversely, if controlled-substance or cold-chain gaps persist, if pharmacy cancellations rise, or if audit remediation becomes a normal hidden workload, the playbook must tighten and high-risk orders must be held rather than expedited.
