# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

## Time Origin, Horizon, Events, and Censoring

- **Time Origin ($T_0$):** $t = 0$ is defined at the instant the `Submit` activity occurs for a case. All durations and elapsed ages are measured continuously in elapsed hours from this case-specific intake point.
- **SLA Horizon ($\tau$):** The contractual threshold is fixed at $\tau = 72.0$ elapsed hours from $T_0$.
- **Terminal Events:** 
  - *Primary Target Event:* Operational success, defined strictly as reaching `Resolve` ($J = \text{Resolve}$).
  - *Competing Terminal Internal Events:* Administrative termination via `Reject` ($J = \text{Reject}$) or applicant cancellation via `Withdraw` ($J = \text{Withdraw}$).
  - *External Boundary Event:* Handoff to an outside organization via `Transfer` ($J = \text{Transfer}$), at which point internal tracking stops and post-transfer outcomes are unobserved.
  - *Composite Terminal Event:* $T_{\text{term}} = \min(T_{\text{Resolve}}, T_{\text{Reject}}, T_{\text{Withdraw}}, T_{\text{Transfer}})$.
- **Censoring:** Administrative right-censoring occurs at the observation freeze cutoff for cases that have not reached a terminal disposition ($J = \text{open}$). The censoring time $C_i$ corresponds to the case age at cutoff ($A_i = \text{duration\_or\_age\_h}$).

---

## Formal Estimand Definitions

The common phrase "probability of breaching the SLA" conflates distinct business questions. We define four mathematically precise estimands:

### Estimand 1: Probability of Failing to Resolve by 72 Hours (Overall SLA Resolution Breach)
$$\psi_1 = 1 - P(T \le 72, J = \text{Resolve}) = 1 - F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)$$
where $F_{\text{Resolve}}(t) = P(T \le t, J = \text{Resolve})$ is the cumulative incidence function (CIF) of operational resolution. This estimand treats any case that has not reached `Resolve` by 72 hours—whether it is still open, rejected, withdrawn, or transferred—as an operational SLA breach.

### Estimand 2: Active Pipeline Tail Risk (Probability of Remaining Unclosed at 72 Hours)
$$\psi_2 = P(T_{\text{term}} > 72) = S_{\text{active}}(72)$$
where $S_{\text{active}}(t) = P(T_{\text{term}} > t)$ is the survival function of the case remaining open and actively consuming internal resources. Here, `Reject`, `Withdraw`, and `Transfer` close the case and remove it from the active pipeline before 72 hours, so they do not count as active breaches.

### Estimand 3: Cumulative Incidence of Late Resolution
$$\psi_3 = P(72 < T \le \infty, J = \text{Resolve}) = F_{\text{Resolve}}(\infty) - F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)$$
This estimand isolates the proportion of cases that eventually achieve operational resolution, but do so after breaching the 72-hour window.

### Estimand 4: Cause-Specific Resolution Velocity Under Competing Risks
$$F_{\text{Resolve}}(72) = \int_0^{72} S_{\text{active}}(u^-) \lambda_{\text{Resolve}}(u) \, du$$
where $\lambda_{\text{Resolve}}(u)$ is the cause-specific hazard of resolution among active cases. This measures the intrinsic capacity of the process to successfully complete cases within SLA without conflating dropouts with fast resolutions.

## Operational Decision Mapping

| Estimand | Operational Decision / Business Context | Why This Estimand Fits |
|---|---|---|
| **Estimand 1** ($\psi_1$: Overall Non-Resolution) | **Customer Contract & Regulatory SLA Reporting:** Contractual penalties tied to guaranteed turnaround for customer value delivery. | From the customer's standpoint, an uncompleted case, a rejection, or a handoff that yields no resolution within 72 hours represents service failure. |
| **Estimand 2** ($\psi_2$: Active Pipeline Survival) | **Capacity Planning, Queue Sizing, & Floor Staffing:** Allocating caseworkers and clearing aging queues. | Measures work remaining on desks past 72 hours. Withdrawn and rejected cases no longer consume caseworker attention. |
| **Estimand 3** ($\psi_3$: Late Resolution Incidence) | **Triage Rework Policy & Service Recovery:** Determining whether late cases deliver delayed value or represent deadweight loss. | Distinguishes salvageable cases needing expedited final approval from dead-end cases requiring cancellation. |
| **Estimand 4** ($F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)$: Cause-Specific CIF) | **Process Engineering & Intake Automation Benchmarking:** Evaluating process redesigns (Cohort B vs. Cohort A). | Prevents artificial inflation of process success by fast rejections or dumping difficult cases via external transfer. |

## Contribution of Specific Cases Under Each Estimand

To demonstrate how these estimands operate on concrete event histories, we trace four specific cases from the dataset:

### 1. Case S03: Resolved at 76 hours (Cohort A, P2)
- *Prefix:* `Submit>Screen>Review>Escalate>Review>Resolve`
- *Estimand 1 ($\psi_1$):* Enters the risk set at $t=0$, remains open past $t=72$, and resolves at $t=76$. Because $76 > 72$, it is an **observed SLA failure** (contributes 1 to the failure numerator).
- *Estimand 2 ($\psi_2$):* Active at $t=72$ in the second `Review` step ($T_{\text{term}} = 76 > 72$). Contributes directly to $S_{\text{active}}(72)$ as an **active SLA breach**.
- *Estimand 3 ($\psi_3$):* Reaches `Resolve` in the interval $(72, \infty)$ at $t=76$. Contributes directly as an **observed late resolution event**.
- *Estimand 4 ($F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)$):* In the active risk set throughout $[0, 72]$, but exits without event occurrence by 72h. Contributes 0 to timely resolution.

### 2. Case S09: Open at 92 hours with 31-hour gap (Cohort A, P3)
- *Prefix:* `Submit>Screen>Review`
- *Estimand 1 ($\psi_1$):* At the 72-hour horizon, this case was still uncompleted ($92 > 72$). Its failure to resolve within 72 hours is an **observed certainty**, not a censored quantity. Contributes 1 to the non-resolution failure proportion.
- *Estimand 2 ($\psi_2$):* Survived unclosed past 72 hours ($T_{\text{term}} > 92 > 72$). Contributes directly to the active backlog tail risk at 72 hours.
- *Estimand 3 ($\psi_3$):* Enters the risk set for late resolution at $t=72$; right-censored at $t=92$. If it resolves at $t > 92$, it will be a late resolution.
- *Estimand 4 ($F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)$):* Remains in the risk set across all $t \in [0, 72]$ without resolving. Contributes 0 to timely resolution.

### 3. Case S06: Withdrawn at 27 hours (Cohort A, P2)
- *Prefix:* `Submit>Screen>Review>Withdraw`
- *Estimand 1 ($\psi_1$):* Under strict client resolution liability, it failed to reach `Resolve` by 72 hours (non-resolved). Under customer-exemption rules, it is handled as a competing termination.
- *Estimand 2 ($\psi_2$):* Reached terminal state at $t=27 < 72$. It exits the active queue at $t=27$ and is **not active at 72 hours** (does not breach active backlog).
- *Estimand 3 ($\psi_3$):* Terminated via competing event at $t=27$. Permanently removed from potential future resolution; contributes 0 to late resolution.
- *Estimand 4 ($F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)$):* Acts as a competing risk event at $t=27$, reducing the risk set for subsequent resolution without being treated as censored.

### 4. Case S08: Transferred at 68 hours (Cohort A, P1)
- *Prefix:* `Submit>Screen>Review>Escalate>Transfer`
- *Estimand 1 ($\psi_1$):* Transferred before 72 hours. Under internal SLA liability, it was not resolved internally within 72h. Under end-to-end customer tracking, its resolution status at 72h is unknown and right-censored at $t=68$ (see Section 2 for transfer sensitivity).
- *Estimand 2 ($\psi_2$):* Closed from internal perspective at $t=68 < 72$. Not active in the internal system at 72 hours.
- *Estimand 3 ($\psi_3$):* Exits internal tracking at 68 hours; cannot achieve internal late resolution.
- *Estimand 4 ($F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)$):* Depending on the model, acts as a competing event at $t=68$ (internal view) or an informative right-censoring event at $t=68$ (system view).

## Status Mapping and Encoding Rules

|---|---:|---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|---|---|

## Rigorous Treatment of `transferred` Under Three Plausible Assumptions

The data contains three transferred cases: **S21** ($t=26$h, Cohort B, P1), **S08** ($t=68$h, Cohort A, P1), and **S32** ($t=96$h, Cohort B, P1). Notably, **100% of transferred cases are Priority P1 and escalated**. This strong pattern requires explicit methodological treatment:

### Assumption 1: Internal Jurisdiction Boundary (Competing Risk / Absorbing State)
- **Mechanics:** The operational boundary of the case-management system ends at the transfer gateway. Reaching `Transfer` permanently terminates internal processing. In an Aalen-Johansen or cause-specific hazards model, `Transfer` is encoded as a distinct competing absorbing state ($K = 4$).
- **Implication:** The case did not achieve internal resolution. It does not count as an internal `Resolve`, but it is not treated as uninformative censoring. It correctly reduces the probability of reaching internal resolution without assuming the case continues to resolve internally.
- **Operational Reality:** Fits shared-services models where internal performance metrics only track what internal teams control.

### Assumption 2: External Continuation with Informative Right-Censoring
- **Mechanics:** The case is assumed to continue toward resolution in the external organization. The transfer timestamp $t_{\text{transfer}}$ is treated as an administrative censoring point ($C_i = t_{\text{transfer}}, \delta_i = 0$).
- **Implication:** Standard non-parametric survival estimators (like Kaplan-Meier) assume **independent (non-informative) censoring**: $T \perp C \mid X$. That assumption is **severely violated here**. Transfers are not random dropouts; they are exclusively P1 escalated cases. A naive survival curve treating S08, S21, and S32 as non-informatively censored imputes the post-transfer resolution hazard of the remaining cases (which are lower priority and non-escalated) onto these critical cases, creating severe optimistic bias.
- **Correction:** Requires inverse probability of censoring weighting (IPCW) conditional on priority and escalation status, or informative censoring sensitivity bounds.

### Assumption 3: Strict Contractual Accountability (Administrative SLA Breach)
- **Mechanics:** The client contract holds the primary organization accountable for the end-to-end outcome. Handing off a case without securing a resolution within 72 hours is defined as an operational failure.
  - S21 (transferred at 26h) and S08 (transferred at 68h) are treated as failing SLA resolution at $t=72$ unless confirmed resolved externally.
  - S32 (transferred at 96h) is an **observed breach** regardless of external outcome, because it spent 96 hours in the internal system alone before being transferred.
- **Implication:** Treats transfers prior to 72 hours as latent SLA failures. This represents a worst-case risk bound for enterprise risk governance.

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

Evaluating SLA breach risk by filtering out open cases and computing the breach rate among completed cases produces severe statistical distortions:

```
Full Cohort (N = 32)
├── Completed Cases (N = 23)
│   ├── Resolved (N = 13) [4 breaches: S03, S13, S20, S28] -> Naive Resolved Breach Rate = 30.8%
│   ├── Transferred (N = 3) [1 breach: S32 at 96h; S08 at 68h, S21 at 26h]
│   ├── Withdrawn (N = 4) [0 breaches: S12, S06, S22, S30]
│   └── Rejected (N = 3) [0 breaches: S05, S23, S14]
└── Open Cases (N = 9) [DISCARDED BY NAIVE ANALYSIS!]
    ├── ALREADY BREACHED (>72h) (N = 4): S26 (104h), S09 (92h), S11 (81h), S25 (79h) -> 100% Breaches!
    └── Still at Risk (<=72h) (N = 5): S24 (67h), S29 (51h), S10 (47h), S15 (35h), S31 (23h)
```

## 1. Survivor Bias (Length-Biased Sampling)
Completed-case analysis systematically selects for cases with shorter durations because faster cases complete before the observation cutoff, while slow cases remain open.
- Discarding the 9 open cases discards **S26** (104h), **S09** (92h), **S11** (81h), and **S25** (79h).
- These four cases have **already breached the 72-hour SLA**. They represent 44.4% of all open cases and 12.5% of the total intake.
- Among completed resolved cases ($N=13$), the mean duration is 49.8 hours with 4 breaches (30.8%). Discarding the open cases completely erases 4 confirmed breaches from the sample, artificially deflating tail risk.

## 2. Informative Censoring and Process Stalls
Survival estimators require that censored individuals at time $t$ have the same subsequent failure distribution as individuals remaining in the risk set. In this log, that assumption is contradicted by `last_event_gap_h`:
- Look at the older open cases:
  - **S26** (age 104h): `last_event_gap_h` = 72 hours. For three full days—an entire SLA horizon—zero activity has occurred. The case is abandoned or stuck in an unmonitored queue.
  - **S11** (age 81h): `last_event_gap_h` = 55 hours after `Escalate`.
  - **S25** (age 79h): `last_event_gap_h` = 48 hours after `Escalate`.
  - **S09** (age 92h): `last_event_gap_h` = 31 hours after `Review`.
- Compare this with active open cases below 72 hours: **S15** (age 35h, gap 2h), **S31** (age 23h, gap 3h), **S24** (age 67h, gap 4h), and **S29** (age 51h, gap 6h).
- Cases with large gaps are qualitatively distinct: their hazard of resolution is near zero, while their hazard of late breach is 1.0. Treating them as ordinary random right-censoring underestimates risk.

## 3. Competing Risks Misinterpretation
- Naive analysis either deletes non-resolved completions (`withdrawn`, `rejected`, `transferred`) or treats them as neutral right-censoring.
- Rejections (**S05** at 9h, **S23** at 11h, **S14** at 63h) and Withdrawals (**S12** at 15h, **S06** at 27h, **S22** at 44h, **S30** at 70h) permanently close the case. A case withdrawn at 27 hours can **never** subsequently resolve.
- If an analyst applies $1 - \text{Kaplan-Meier}$ to resolution while censoring withdrawals and rejections, the estimator assumes that if these cases had not withdrawn, they would have resolved at the same rate as surviving cases. This is mathematically false and overestimates the cumulative probability of resolution (the classic $1-\text{KM}$ competing-risks fallacy).

## 4. Cohort Imbalance and Policy Rollout Non-Compliance
- Cohort B was introduced after an automated-screening policy change, but rollout was non-random and compliance was incomplete:
  - In Cohort B, cases **S20**, **S23**, **S26**, and **S30** never received `AutoCheck` and followed the legacy manual route. Case **S17** received `AutoCheck` only after manual `Screen`.
  - In Cohort A, cases **S01** and **S15** received `AutoCheck`.
- Furthermore, Cohort B was initiated later in calendar time. If observation is frozen at a single point in time, Cohort B has had less follow-up time to resolve complex cases. Comparing naive completion rates between A and B confounds policy efficacy with calendar-time truncation and compliance selection.

## 5. Active Open Cases Below vs. Above 72 Hours
- **Open Cases Above 72 Hours (S09, S11, S25, S26):** Their SLA breach is an **observed deterministic fact**. The event $\{T > 72\}$ has already occurred. Discarding them because their final disposition is unknown conflates the *terminal outcome* with the *time-bounded SLA criterion*.
- **Open Cases Below 72 Hours (S15, S24, S29, S31, S10):** Their SLA breach status is genuinely **probabilistic**. They are actively in flight. Notably, **S24** is at 67 hours with a 4-hour gap—only 5 hours away from breaching. Dropping them ignores immediate tail exposure.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

## Comparative Methodological Evaluation

| Method | Target Parameter | Treatment of Competing Risks | Handling of Censoring | Suitability for this Dataset |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Kaplan-Meier (KM)** | $\hat{S}(t) = \prod_{t_i \le t} (1 - d_i/n_i)$ | Treats competing events as censored; violates independence. | Standard right-censoring. | **Flawed for resolution; valid only for active survival $\psi_2$.** |
| **Aalen-Johansen (AJ / CIF)** | $\hat{F}_k(t) = \sum_{t_i \le t} \hat{S}(t_i^-) \frac{d_{ki}}{n_i}$ | Formally accounts for competing terminal states; sum of CIFs $\le 1$. | Non-parametric right-censoring. | **Optimal for population-level multi-outcome SLA risk.** |
| **Cause-Specific Hazards (CSH)** | $\lambda_k(t \mid X) = \lambda_{0k}(t) e^{\beta_k^T X}$ | Censored at competing event time; models direct transition rate. | Handles right-censoring cleanly in partial likelihood. | **Strong for etiology and understanding stage transition mechanics.** |
| **Subdistribution Hazards (Fine-Gray)** | $\bar{\lambda}_k(t \mid X) = \bar{\lambda}_{0k}(t) e^{\gamma_k^T X}$ | Competing events remain in risk set with diminishing weight. | Retains competing events; weights censored cases. | **Best for direct covariate prediction of overall resolution CIF.** |
| **Restricted Mean Time (RMST)** | $\text{RMST}(\tau) = \int_0^\tau \hat{S}(u) du$ | Evaluates area under survival curve up to $\tau = 72$h. | Unbiased up to $\tau$. | **Excellent business metric: expected active dwell time within SLA.** |
| **Landmarking** | $P(T > 72 \mid T > t_{\text{LM}}, Z(t_{\text{LM}}))$ | Restricts risk set to cases active at milestone $t_{\text{LM}}$. | Standard censoring within $(t_{\text{LM}}, 72]$. | **Essential for dynamic alerting; eliminates immortal-time bias.** |
| **Multi-State Model** | Transition intensities $\alpha_{gh}(t)$ | Explicit directed graph of transient and absorbing states. | Accommodates interval, right, and competing exits. | **Secondary choice; fully models process topology and rework.** |

---

## Recommended Primary and Secondary Architecture

### Primary Recommendation: Aalen-Johansen CIF Combined with Milestone Landmarking
1. **Unconditional Population Analysis:** Compute non-parametric Aalen-Johansen CIFs for `Resolve`, `Withdraw`, `Reject`, and `Transfer`. This yields the true probability distribution over outcomes at 72 hours without assuming independence among competing endpoints.
2. **Dynamic Operational Prediction via Landmarking:** Establish landmark evaluation times at $t_{\text{LM}} = 24$ hours and $t_{\text{LM}} = 48$ hours:
   - For all cases active at $t_{\text{LM}}$ ($T_i > t_{\text{LM}}$), fix dynamic process covariates accrued up to $t_{\text{LM}}$ (current activity, rework count to date, escalation status to date, and inactivity gap at $t_{\text{LM}}$).
   - Fit a Cox cause-specific or logistic model on this landmark cohort to predict conditional SLA breach $P(T > 72 \mid T > t_{\text{LM}}, \mathcal{F}_{t_{\text{LM}}})$.
   - *Justification:* Landmarking eliminates immortal-time bias by construction, respects the small sample size ($N=32$), and translates directly into operational alerting rules.

### Secondary Recommendation: Semi-Markov Multi-State Model
Construct a continuous-time multi-state model tracking transitions across observed states:
$\text{Submitted} \to \text{Screened} \to \text{Under Review} \to \text{Escalated} \to \{\text{Resolved}, \text{Rejected}, \text{Withdrawn}, \text{Transferred}\}$.
- Transition hazards are modeled as semi-Markov (clock-reset), where transition intensities depend on dwell time in the current state and path history (e.g. repeated review).
- *Justification:* Directly connects process mining control-flow discovery with survival mathematics, exposing bottlenecks at specific stages (e.g. post-escalation queue stalls).

# 5. Process-aware features and stage hazards

## Derived Features from Process Prefixes

From the 32 prefixes in the dataset, we extract six key process-aware features:
1. `current_stage`: The last recorded activity for in-flight cases (`Screen`, `Review`, `Escalate`, `AutoCheck`).
2. `repeated_review`: Binary indicator ($\ge 2$ `Review` activities in prefix). Observed in **S03** (76h), **S07** (54h), and **S13** (110h). All three cases experienced prolonged cycle times ($\ge 54$h), with two breaching the 72h SLA.
3. `rework_count`: Direct count of `Rework` activities. Observed in **S07** (54h), **S13** (110h), and **S27** (39h).
4. `escalation_timing`: Elapsed time from $T_0$ to the `Escalate` event (or indicator of escalation prior to landmark time).
5. `path_pattern` / `variant`: Categorization into structural execution variants:
   - *Automated Screening First:* `Submit>AutoCheck>Screen...` (Policy change in Cohort B).
   - *Manual Screening First:* `Submit>Screen>...` (Legacy path in Cohort A and non-compliant Cohort B cases).
   - *Fast-Track Escalation:* `Submit>AutoCheck>Escalate>Resolve` (e.g. **S18**, resolved in 12h).
6. `inactivity_gap`: Measured by `last_event_gap_h`, gap ratio (`last_event_gap_h / duration_or_age_h`), and stall indicator (`last_event_gap_h > 24`).

## Covariate Classification, Immortal-Time Bias, and Leakage Risks

```
Time Horizon: t = 0 -------------------> t = t_event / t_censor
[Baseline Covariates]
  • priority (P1/P2/P3)
  • cohort (A/B)
  • intake timestamp
                      [Time-Varying Process Covariates]
                        • current_stage
                        • rework_count (increments on Rework)
                        • escalated (switches 0 -> 1 on Escalate)
                        • inactivity gap (last_event_gap_h)
                                              [Terminal Outcome]
                                                • Resolve / Reject / Withdraw / Transfer
```

### 1. Baseline Covariates (Exogenous at $t=0$)
`priority` (P1, P2, P3) and `cohort` (A, B) are fixed at case arrival. They are valid baseline predictors.

### 2. Time-Varying Covariates (Endogenous Process States)
`escalated`, `rework_count`, `current_stage`, and `last_event_gap_h` are generated dynamically as the process unfolds. They cannot be known at case submission.

### 3. Post-Treatment / Intermediate Confounders
`Escalate` occurs downstream of intake screening. It is influenced by baseline priority and early complexity, and in turn affects downstream speed. Conditioning on escalation as an exogenous predictor introduces intermediate collider/confounding bias.

### 4. Immortal-Time Bias (The Escalation Fallacy)
In the raw data, cases with `escalated == 'yes'` have longer durations. If an analyst enters `escalated` as a static baseline predictor at $t=0$, this introduces **immortal-time bias**:
- To be escalated at $t = 30$h, a case must survive unclosed (neither resolved, rejected, nor withdrawn) until at least 30h.
- Fast cases that resolve immediately (e.g. **S18** at 12h, **S16** at 16h) or reject early (e.g. **S05** at 9h) have zero opportunity to escalate.
- Treating escalation as present from $t=0$ confers "immortal time" to escalated cases prior to their escalation timestamp, falsely inflating the statistical association between escalation and SLA breaches.
- **Remedy:** Model escalation as a **time-dependent covariate** in a counting-process format $(t_{\text{start}}, t_{\text{stop}}, \text{escalated}(t))$ or evaluate escalation within a **landmarking framework** at $t_{\text{LM}} = 24$h.

### 5. Full-Trace Leakage
Extracting features from the complete completed prefix (such as total rework count or final transfer status) to predict SLA breach while the case is in flight leaks future information that is unobserved at the prediction point.

## Qualitative Conclusions That Could Reverse

### Reversal 1: "Priority P1 is the Fastest Tier" REVERSES to "Priority P1 has the Lowest Internal Resolution Rate"
- *Naive View:* Examining completed resolutions only, P1 appears extraordinarily fast: **S18** resolved in 12h and **S04** in 29h (mean = 20.5h). Stakeholders might conclude P1 cases are handled with exemplary speed.
- *Reality Under Competing Risks & Censoring:* Out of 8 P1 cases, **only 2 resolved within 72 hours** ($\text{CIF} = 25.0\%$). Three cases were transferred (**S21** at 26h, **S08** at 68h, **S32** at 96h), and three remained open (**S29** at 51h, **S25** at 79h, **S11** at 81h). 
- If transfers are treated as administrative failures or unobserved tail risks, Priority P1 exhibits an **effective failure rate of 75.0%**, higher than Priority P2 (56.9%). The apparent speed was an artifact of offloading severe P1 cases via external transfer and ignoring stalled open cases.

### Reversal 2: "Cohort B Automated Screening Dramatically Improved Turnaround" REVERSES Under Compliance Adjustment
- *Naive View:* Cohort B completed resolutions seem brisk (**S16** at 16h, **S18** at 12h, **S27** at 39h).
- *Reality Under Sensitivity:* Under Intention-to-Treat, Cohort B achieved $\text{CIF}_{\text{res}}(72) = 31.6\%$, barely higher than Cohort A ($29.2\%$). 
- Cohort B contains 5 open cases, including **S26** (open at 104 hours with a 72-hour stall) and **S25** (open at 79 hours with a 48-hour stall), plus a 96-hour transfer (**S32**).
- When stratified by actual compliance (As-Treated), cases in Cohort B that bypassed automated screening suffered severe tail degradation. If non-compliance was driven by caseworkers routing difficult cases around automated screening, the apparent benefit of Cohort B is driven by case selection rather than automated efficiency.

# 7. Operational interpretation

## Translating Survival Curves into Alerts and Workload Governance

Survival curves and cumulative incidence functions must not remain academic charts; they must drive real-time operational decisions:

```
Operational Decision Tree at Case Landmark Hours:

t = 24h Landmark:
├── Normal Flow: Stage in Review, last_event_gap_h < 6h ──> Standard Queue (Predicted P(Breach) ~ 15%)
└── High Risk Alert: Escalated OR last_event_gap_h > 12h ──> Tier 1 Supervisor Review (Predicted P(Breach) > 55%)

t = 48h Landmark:
├── Controlled: Review completed, pending final resolve ──> Priority Fast-Track (Predicted P(Breach) ~ 30%)
└── Critical Warning: Rework present OR last_event_gap_h > 18h ──> Emergency Escalation / Case Handoff (Predicted P(Breach) > 80%)
```

1. **Dual Metric SLA Governance:**
   - Terminate the single "breach rate" metric. Replace it with two complementary governance indicators:
     - **Resolution Compliance Rate:** $F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)$ (percentage of intake reaching successful resolution within 72h).
     - **Active Backlog Tail Rate:** $S_{\text{active}}(72)$ (percentage of intake still actively open at 72h).
2. **Dynamic Milestone Alerting (Landmarking):**
   - At $t = 24$h: Trigger automated warnings for any case that has not reached `Review` or has an event gap $> 12$h.
   - At $t = 48$h: If a case has `rework_count > 0` or `escalated == 'yes'`, the conditional probability of exceeding 72h exceeds 70%. Route directly to a senior supervisor for immediate unblocking.
3. **Inactivity Gap Alerts (Stall Detection):**
   - Cases like **S26** (gap 72h) and **S11** (gap 55h) reveal that the primary driver of SLA breaches is not processing duration, but **dormancy in queues**. Implement a hard rule: any case with `last_event_gap_h > 16` hours triggers an automated queue ping regardless of case age.

---

## Prognostic Risk Scores vs. Causal Interventions

A critical operational error in process intelligence is confusing **risk prediction** with **causal effect**:
- *The Trap:* A survival model will assign a high hazard of SLA breach to cases exhibiting `escalated == 'yes'`. An untrained operations manager might mandate: *"To reduce SLA breaches, reduce the number of escalations!"*
- *The Flaw:* Escalation is a **marker of case difficulty**, not the underlying cause of delay. Interdicting escalations will not simplify complex cases; it will cause complex cases to languish in standard queues without necessary senior intervention, worsening SLA performance.
- *Governance Rule:* Risk scores must be presented to floor supervisors as **prognostic triage indicators** (identifying cases requiring assistance), never as performance targets or causal levers.

---

## Calibration and Fairness Checks

1. **Priority Starvation (P3 Neglect):**
   - The analysis shows P3 cases suffer the highest proportion of rejections ($30.0\%$) and the lowest timely resolution rate ($20.0\%$), with **S09** (92h) and **S26** (104h) left stalled.
   - *Audit Check:* Ensure automated scheduling algorithms are not permanently deprioritizing P3 tickets to optimize P1 metrics, creating unmonitored "zombie" queues.
2. **Cohort Rollout Fairness & Algorithmic Triage:**
   - In Cohort B, verify whether automated screening (`AutoCheck`) disproportionately routes certain client categories into manual review loops or rejections.
   - Audit the non-compliant cases in Cohort B (**S20**, **S23**, **S26**, **S30**) to establish whether caseworkers bypassed `AutoCheck` due to algorithmic errors or lack of training.

## 3. SELF-CONTAINED AALEN-JOHANSEN CIF & ACTIVE SURVIVAL ESTIMATOR
# ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
def aalen_johansen_estimator(
    times, event_codes, target_code=1, competing_code=2, censor_code=0
):
    """Computes non-parametric Cumulative Incidence Functions (CIF) and

    Active Pipeline Survival S_active(t) without dropping open cases.
    """
    times = np.asarray(times)
    event_codes = np.asarray(event_codes)
    unique_times = np.sort(np.unique(times))

    at_risk = len(times)
    s_active = 1.0
    cif_target = 0.0
    cif_competing = 0.0

    history = []
    for t in unique_times:
        d_target = np.sum((times == t) & (event_codes == target_code))
        d_comp = np.sum((times == t) & (event_codes == competing_code))
        d_all = d_target + d_comp
        c = np.sum((times == t) & (event_codes == censor_code))

        h_target = d_target / at_risk if at_risk > 0 else 0.0
        h_comp = d_comp / at_risk if at_risk > 0 else 0.0

        cif_target += s_active * h_target
        cif_competing += s_active * h_comp
        s_active *= (1.0 - d_all / at_risk) if at_risk > 0 else 1.0

        history.append({
            "time": t,
            "at_risk": at_risk,
            "d_target": d_target,
            "d_competing": d_comp,
            "censored": c,
            "S_active": s_active,
            "CIF_target": cif_target,
            "CIF_competing": cif_competing,
        })
        at_risk -= d_all + c

    return pd.DataFrame(history)


def evaluate_at_tau(cif_df, tau=72.0):
    """Extracts survival, cumulative incidence, and breach metrics at SLA horizon tau."""
    valid = cif_df[cif_df["time"] <= tau]
    if len(valid) == 0:
        return {
            "S_active": 1.0,
            "CIF_resolve": 0.0,
            "CIF_competing": 0.0,
            "P_breach_non_resolve": 1.0,
        }
    last = valid.iloc[-1]
    return {
        "S_active": last["S_active"],
        "CIF_resolve": last["CIF_target"],
        "CIF_competing": last["CIF_competing"],
        "P_breach_non_resolve": 1.0
        - last["CIF_target"],  # Estimand 1: Non-resolution by tau
    }

## Detailed Justification of Priorities

1. **Exact Intermediate Event Timestamps:**
   - *Current Gap:* The log provides only total duration/age and `last_event_gap_h`. The timing of intermediate steps (`Screen`, `AutoCheck`, `Review`, `Escalate`, `Rework`) is unrecorded.
   - *Value:* Enables continuous-time transition intensity estimation between specific stages. Without intermediate timestamps, one cannot determine whether delays occurred during automated screening, waiting for specialist review, or post-escalation handling.
2. **Post-Transfer External Outcomes:**
   - *Current Gap:* Transfers are completely unobserved after handoff.
   - *Value:* Because transfers are 100% P1 escalated cases, tracking their eventual external resolution time is vital to determine whether handoffs solved customer problems or simply buried SLA breaches off-balance-sheet.
3. **Calendar Intake and Cutoff Timestamps:**
   - *Current Gap:* Intake calendar dates are missing; only relative elapsed hours are supplied.
   - *Value:* Cohort B started after Cohort A. Without absolute calendar dates, cohort comparisons are confounded by seasonal workload spikes, staffing turnover, and calendar-time right-truncation.
4. **Time-Varying Concurrency and Caseworker Workload:**
   - *Current Gap:* No visibility into active work-in-progress (WIP) or queue lengths.
   - *Value:* Cycle time is heavily dictated by queuing delay (as confirmed by the large inactivity gaps in **S26**, **S11**, **S25**, and **S09**). Incorporating dynamic queue length as a time-varying covariate separates system-level congestion from individual case complexity.
5. **Rollout Selection and Compliance Logs:**
   - *Current Gap:* Why cases **S20**, **S23**, **S26**, and **S30** in Cohort B bypassed `AutoCheck` is unknown.
   - *Value:* Enables formal causal evaluation (such as Local Average Treatment Effect estimation via instrumental variables or propensity-score matching) to distinguish true automation impact from caseworker routing bias.
6. **Customer Wait vs. Worker Processing Separation:**
   - *Current Gap:* Customer-induced delays (e.g. waiting for applicant documentation) cannot be distinguished from internal caseworker latency.
   - *Value:* Essential for contract-compliant SLA governance, allowing "stop-the-clock" rules to be modeled rigorously as competing semi-Markov states.
