## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

**P-501 — Alternative: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**

*Premise change:* Read B4's "commitment may precede the PO" as permitting not just PO reordering but also deferral of Plant and Finance approvals in a genuine emergency, treating B5 as preserving the *existence* of the approval requirement rather than its *pre-commitment timing*.

*Why credible:* B4 is an emergency provision and the 4-hour stoppage prediction creates real urgency. One could argue the SOP's drafters intended B5 to mean that Finance approval must still occur (not be skipped entirely), not that it must occur before commitment.

*Why weak:* B5 states "The emergency procedure does not expressly waive Finance_Approve or the approved-supplier rule." This language, combined with A6 ("does not automatically cure a missing pre-commitment approval"), strongly suggests pre-commitment timing is preserved. Additionally, the Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate SHIFT-1, so even the Shift Lead substitution under B4 is unavailable.

**P-502 — Alternative: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**

*Premise change:* Interpret ECD-2 C4's "supplier-onboarding requirements" as encompassing the GPIP-4 A3 Compliance-clearance requirement for new suppliers, such that ECD-2 authorizes commitment before Compliance clearance provided IC dual authorization is obtained.

*Why credible:* From an operational perspective, new-supplier compliance vetting and supplier-onboarding are closely linked—a new supplier must be onboarded, and compliance clearance is part of that process. ECD-2 C2 explicitly allows unapproved suppliers, suggesting the directive intended to sweep aside all new-supplier barriers.

*Why weak:* A3 is a GPIP-4 board-approved integrity control, not a local onboarding step. C5 explicitly notes ECD-2 does not mention Compliance clearance, the €100k threshold, or the CRO. GPIP-4 A4 limits emergency waiver to "competitive tendering only," and A7 reserves further exceptions to the CRO. The CRO did not ratify ECD-2. Treating "supplier-onboarding" as including A3 would effectively allow a COO directive to override a board-approved integrity control without CRO involvement, undermining the governance structure.

**P-503 — Alternative: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**

*Premise change:* Interpret ECD-2 C4 ("takes precedence over ordinary purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding requirements") as a blanket override of all procurement controls below GPIP-4, including A2 Finance and B2 Plant, and treat the IC dual authorization as satisfying all operational and financial approval requirements. Further, adopt Operations' broad interpretation of "critical sterilization-line lots" to include backup-line contingency parts.

*Why credible:* ECD-2 was jointly signed by the COO and Incident Commander, both senior executives. The organization has previously treated signed incident directives as temporary policy overlays (per the incomplete organizational facts). In a severe contamination event, backup-line pumps labeled "critical contingency" by Incident Command have a plausible operational urgency claim.

*Why weak:* Three independent defeats apply. First, A1 segregation of duties is violated regardless of any override—PROC-3 was requester, committer, and IC authorizer, and no source below CRO can waive A1. Second, ECD-2 C5 explicitly omits mention of segregation, Finance, Compliance, and the CRO, indicating the directive's authors did not intend to override these. Third, Internal Audit's narrower reading of "critical sterilization-line lots" (parts needed within 24 hours) is more textually defensible for parts with 3 days of existing stock. Even under the broad scope reading, the A1 violation alone makes this case noncompliant.

**P-505 — Alternative: NONCOMPLIANT**

*Premise change:* Treat the ERP timestamp of 18:06 as authoritative, disregard the "may have been nine minutes fast" caveat as unproven, and conclude the commitment occurred after ECD-2 expired at 18:00. Without ECD-2, standard SOP B and GPIP-4 apply, and the absence of Create_PO before commitment violates B1, while Finance_Approve at 19:00 violates B2/B5 pre-commitment timing.

*Why credible:* The ERP is the official system of record. The "may have been" language is speculative, and the system-outage ON-44 (which affected timestamp reliability) occurred on 07-14, not 07-18. If the ERP clock is accurate, 18:06 is after 18:00, and ECD-2 has expired.

*Why weak:* The supplier acknowledgment email at 17:59:31 is contemporaneous third-party evidence that the commitment was communicated before 18:00. The supplier call started at 17:57. An ERP timestamp recording a manual entry can lag the real-world act, especially if the user was finalizing the record after the call. The instruction to prefer stronger contemporaneous evidence over database timestamps favors the 17:59:31 evidence. This alternative would require rejecting the supplier's own acknowledgment as non-authoritative, which is difficult to justify.

**P-508 — Alternative: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**

*Premise change:* Read CFO email D2 as establishing that above €150,000, the required controls are IC dual authorization *plus* Legal clearance—with IC authorization still substituting for Finance_Approve (per D1's logic) and Legal being the incremental addition. Under this reading, the combination of IC auth (16:05) + Legal_Clearance (16:00) + Compliance_Clearance (15:50) before commitment (16:10) satisfies all applicable requirements.

*Why credible:* D2 says "obtain Legal clearance as well," which can be read as adding Legal to the existing IC-authorization framework rather than restoring all standard controls. The CFO owns the Finance framework and may interpret how Finance controls apply in this tier. Legal clearance was obtained, as was Compliance.

*Why weak:* D1's threshold of €150,000 creates a bright line. The natural reading is that below €150k, IC authorization alone suffices for operational approvals; above €150k, something *more* is needed. If IC authorization still sufficed for Finance above €150k, the threshold would be meaningless. More fundamentally, A2 is a GPIP-4 board policy requiring "a Finance Controller" before commitment—this is not merely a Finance-control interpretation the CFO can waive by email, and D3 itself disclaims authority over GPIP-4 integrity controls.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

**Distinguishing four categories:**

1. **Policy breach** — A control required by an applicable normative source was not satisfied before the relevant act, and no valid exception applies. *Examples: P-501 (pre-commitment approvals), P-503 (segregation, Finance, Compliance), P-504 (approved supplier, scope), P-506 (all post-expiry controls).*

2. **Logging defect** — A control was satisfied in the real world but not properly recorded in the system of record. *Example: P-507, where the signed PDF at 12:40 likely constitutes timely C3 recording but the ERP entry at 14:30 appears late due to a document-system outage.*

3. **Late evidence of a timely act** — The act occurred on time but evidence of it surfaced or was entered later. *Example: P-505, where the supplier acknowledgment at 17:59:31 and the possibly fast ERP clock suggest the commitment was before 18:00, even though the ERP shows 18:06.*

4. **Inability to prove compliance** — The evidence is insufficient to determine whether a required control was satisfied. *Example: P-502, where the identity of the committer is not stated, making an A1 segregation assessment impossible; P-505, where A2's independent-budget-owner requirement is unverifiable.*

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Replacement clause: Emergency Procurement Authority and Exception Framework**

1. **Authority to suspend controls.** Only the Chief Risk Officer (CRO), or the CRO's designated deputy in writing, may suspend, waive, or modify GPIP-4 controls during an incident. A COO-issued incident directive may reorder operational workflow steps (e.g., PO-after-commitment, approved-supplier-register exceptions) but may not waive GPIP-4 integrity controls, including segregation of duties (A1), independent budget-owner approval (A2), or Compliance clearance (A3). Incident directives must state expressly which GPIP-4 controls, if any, the CRO has authorized for suspension.

2. **Scope and expiry.** Any incident directive must define: (a) the specific assets, lines, or lots covered; (b) the categories of purchases eligible (e.g., "parts required within 24 hours to restore the affected sterilization line"); (c) a hard expiry date and time, in the site's local timezone; and (d) whether the directive applies retroactively (default: no). Extensions require a new written, jointly signed instrument before the prior directive expires.

3. **Thresholds.** During an active, in-scope incident directive:
   - Purchases  €150,000: dual Incident Command authorization may substitute for Finance_Approve, provided the CFO has confirmed this substitution in a controlled document (not an ad hoc email). The independent-budget-owner requirement of A2 remains; Incident Command members may be designated as budget owners only by a written delegation from the relevant functional head.
   - Purchases > €150,000: Finance Controller approval and Legal clearance are both required before commitment, in addition to dual Incident Command authorization.
   - Any new or unapproved supplier: Compliance clearance is required before commitment regardless of amount. The incident directive may allow use of an unapproved supplier but may not bypass the compliance vetting gate.

4. **Segregation of duties.** No emergency provision suspends A1. A person who requests a purchase or commits the company to a supplier may not also serve as an authorizer or approver of the same purchase. Incident Command dual authorization must come from two members who are neither the requester nor the committer.

5. **Recording and evidence.** Verbal authorizations must be recorded within four hours by any durable means (signed PDF, email, or system entry). A document-system outage does not excuse the recording requirement; a contemporaneous signed record created within the four-hour window satisfies the requirement even if system entry is delayed. The authoritative timestamp is the real-world act time, supported by the best available contemporaneous evidence; ERP timestamps are rebuttable.

6. **Anti-normalization safeguard.** An emergency procurement may not be made under an incident directive unless (a) the purchase is for items required to address the specific incident within the directive's defined urgency horizon, and (b) the requester documents the operational consequence that would occur without the purchase within that horizon. Purchases for convenience, contingency stock beyond the defined horizon, or non-production purposes (e.g., facility amenities) are excluded. A quarterly audit of all emergency-designated purchases must verify that each met the urgency and scope criteria, with results reported to the Audit Committee. Repeated misuse of emergency authority by any individual triggers automatic revocation of emergency procurement privileges for that individual.
