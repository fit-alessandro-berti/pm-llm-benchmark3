# 1. Assumptions and notation
- **A1 (Event-order convention):** When events share a rounded timestamp, we assume departures/completions precede arrivals/starts to maximize capacity and avoid artificial concurrency violations.
- **A2 (Timestamp rounding):** Timestamps are rounded to the nearest 5 minutes. A recorded time $t$ represents an actual time in $[t-2.5, t+2.5]$ minutes. Durations $t_2 - t_1$ have a rounding uncertainty of $\pm 5$ minutes.
- **A3 (Capacity & Routing):** S1 has resources I1, I2. S2 has C1, C2. C2 was down 10:55–11:10. No bypassing occurs.
- **A4 (Imputation of MISSING starts):** For MISSING starts, the earliest possible start is the maximum of the case's arrival at the station and the resource's availability from its previous task. The latest possible start is the activity's completion time. We use the earliest possible start as the central estimate, assuming no unnecessary idle time.
- **A5 (Censoring):** OPEN activities and WAITING states are right-censored at the end of the observation window (14:00). Service and waiting times for these are bounded below by $14:00 - \text{start\_time}$ and $14:00 - \text{arrival\_time}$, respectively.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas
- **Waiting time ($W_q$):** $t_{start} - t_{arrival\_at\_station}$. For S1, $t_{arrival\_at\_station}$ is `arrival_s1`. For S2, it is `s1_end`.
- **Service time ($S$):** $t_{end} - t_{start}$.
- **Sojourn time ($W$):** $W_q + S$. Case-level sojourn time is $t_{s2\_end} - t_{arrival\_s1}$.
- **WIP:** Count of cases that have arrived at a station but not yet completed.
- **Throughput ($X$):** Number of completions divided by the analysis interval duration.
- **Utilization ($\rho$):** Total active service time divided by total available resource time.
- **Little's Law:** $L = \lambda W$. We check this for the 08:00–10:00 interval. Since the system is not in steady state (queues are growing), Little's Law will only approximate the cumulative averages.
- **Estimates:** Central estimates use the nominal rounded timestamps. Lower/upper bounds adjust for the $\pm 5$ minute rounding uncertainty and the range of possible imputed missing starts.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation
Both S1 and S2 act as bottlenecks because the arrival rate exceeds the capacity of both stations.
- **S1 Capacity:** 2 resources * (60 mins / ~28 mins/case) $\approx 4.3$ cases/hour.
- **S2 Capacity:** 2 resources * (60 mins / ~38 mins/case) $\approx 3.1$ cases/hour.
- **Arrival Rate:** 22 cases / 6 hours $\approx 3.7$ cases/hour.
Since the arrival rate (3.7) is greater than S2's capacity (3.1), S2 is the primary long-term bottleneck. S1 is also a bottleneck initially because arrivals are bursty in the morning (14 cases in the first 2 hours = 7 cases/hour), overwhelming S1's capacity.
The C2 maintenance interval (10:55–11:10) temporarily reduced S2 capacity by 50%, exacerbating the S2 queue precisely when the bulk of S1 completions were arriving.

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis
- **Optimistic Imputation:** Missing starts occur as late as possible. This minimizes service time and maximizes waiting time, suggesting resources were idle despite queues.
- **Central Imputation:** Missing starts occur as early as possible. This maximizes service time and minimizes waiting time, assuming resources are fully utilized when work is available.
- **Pessimistic Imputation:** Central imputation plus 5 minutes of rounding penalty.
**Stability of conclusions:** The conclusion that S2 is the primary bottleneck and that queues grow monotonically is highly stable. Even under optimistic imputations (shorter service times), the service times at S2 remain longer than at S1, and the WIP at 14:00 confirms the backlog. Censoring (OPEN/WAITING) only hides the true extent of the delays, meaning our wait time estimates for later cases are strict lower bounds.

Little's Law ($L = \lambda W$) assumes a stable, stationary system. Since the arrival rate exceeds capacity, the system is unstable and queues grow monotonically. Therefore, Little's Law is not strictly applicable for steady-state estimation here. However, we can perform a cumulative check for the first two hours (08:00–10:00) as a rough consistency bound:
- Arrival rate $\lambda = 14 \text{ cases} / 120 \text{ mins} \approx 0.117 \text{ cases/min}$.
- Mean S1 sojourn time for these cases $W \approx 76 \text{ mins}$.
- Predicted average WIP $L = \lambda W \approx 8.9 \text{ cases}$.
- Observed average WIP is lower (around 5-6) because the 76-minute average sojourn time includes waiting time that occurs *after* 10:00 for cases arriving near the end of the interval, violating the steady-state assumption.

df = pd.read_csv(StringIO(data))

# 2. Parse status-aware timestamps
def parse_time(t):
    if pd.isna(t) or t == '' or t == 'MISSING' or t == 'OPEN':
        return pd.NaT
    return pd.to_datetime('2026-05-12 ' + t)

for col in ['arrival_s1', 's1_start', 's1_end', 's2_start', 's2_end']:
    df[col + '_dt'] = df[col].apply(parse_time)

# 3. Create PM4Py-compatible interval-event DataFrame
interval_events = []
for idx, row in df.iterrows():
    case = row['case']
    if pd.notna(row['s1_start_dt']) and pd.notna(row['s1_end_dt']):
        interval_events.append({
            'case:concept:name': case,
            'concept:name': 'S1',
            'start_timestamp': row['s1_start_dt'],
            'time:timestamp': row['s1_end_dt'],
            'org:resource': row['s1_resource']
        })
    if pd.notna(row['s2_start_dt']) and pd.notna(row['s2_end_dt']):
        interval_events.append({
            'case:concept:name': case,
            'concept:name': 'S2',
            'start_timestamp': row['s2_start_dt'],
            'time:timestamp': row['s2_end_dt'],
            'org:resource': row['s2_resource']
        })

interval_df = pd.DataFrame(interval_events)
# 4. call pm4py.format_dataframe
interval_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(interval_df, case_id='case:concept:name', activity_key='concept:name', timestamp_key='time:timestamp')

# 5. Pandas calculation for station-specific metrics
print("--- Station-level Pandas Calculations ---")
df['s1_service'] = (df['s1_end_dt'] - df['s1_start_dt']).dt.total_seconds() / 60
df['s1_wait'] = (df['s1_start_dt'] - df['arrival_s1_dt']).dt.total_seconds() / 60
df['s2_service'] = (df['s2_end_dt'] - df['s2_start_dt']).dt.total_seconds() / 60
df['s2_wait'] = (df['s2_start_dt'] - df['s1_end_dt']).dt.total_seconds() / 60

print(df[['case', 's1_wait', 's1_service', 's2_wait', 's2_service']].head())

# 6. Demonstrate pm4py case-level metrics on valid projection

                 (df['s1_start'] != 'MISSING') & 
                 (df['s2_start'] != 'MISSING')]['case'].tolist()

proj_df = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(proj_df)
