# 1. Mechanism map

The restructuring changes *who* does Specialist Review, Legal/Risk Review, difficult Quality Check and Supervisor Approval, and *how* work reaches them (pooled queues instead of embedded colleagues). Every claimed benefit and every staff concern can be expressed as a change in a process-mining measure. The map below is organised as action → causal mechanism → observable PM measure → direction expected if the mechanism is real.

Before the map, three arithmetic observations from management's own tables, because they anchor several mechanisms:

- **Nominal handovers do not fall in management's own forecast.** Summing the two routing tables: current = 92+88+36+31+42+39+9+6 = **343 handovers per 100 cases**; expected = 92+83+41+36+49+43+12+3 = **359 per 100 cases (+4.7%)**. The "reduction" is a reduction of *local* loops (caseworker ↔ embedded tech, caseworker → local supervisor), replaced by more queue handovers. Handovers do not disappear; they change type, and the new type has a queue in front of it.
- **Handover-attributable waiting rises in the management estimate.** Frequency × median wait: current ≈ 588 h per 100 cases (≈ 5.9 h per case); expected ≈ 722 h per 100 cases (≈ 7.2 h per case), **+23%**. Six of the eight expected medians go up; only cross-team (a 3–6% flow) goes down. If cycle time is to improve at all, it must come from mechanisms *not* in the table (less rework, fewer wrong referrals, faster implementation), which are exactly the mechanisms the staff say are at risk.
- **Legal/Risk is arithmetically infeasible without added capacity.** Legal at 93% and Risk at 91% utilization, with referrals rising 9 → 12 per 100 cases (+33%), implies demand of roughly 120–125% of capacity. A queue whose arrival rate exceeds service rate does not have a "median wait of 10.5 h"; it grows without bound until work is deflected, deferred or silently downgraded. Similarly, approval demand rises 42 → 49 per 100 cases (+17%) while supervisor headcount falls 3 → 2 (−33%): supervisor load per head rises ~1.75×.

# 2. Scenario forecasts

The scenarios differ mainly in (a) whether shared-service capacity is actually increased or just re-labelled, (b) whether note standardisation and routing rules are good enough to stop clarification loops, and (c) whether local informal triage survives in some form.

## Optimistic scenario (pooling benefits realised, capacity added, routing rules well-tuned)

- **Handover pattern:** Handovers per case roughly flat (~3.5), but the handover network becomes a hub-and-spoke: local intake → local caseworker → shared pool → same caseworker → approval → implementation. Cross-team edges nearly vanish (≤ 3 per 100). Return loops (shared → local → shared) rare (< 5% of shared cases). `handover_reason` dominated by "routing rule", not "clarification".
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern:** Pooling lowers wait variance; median wait into Technical Shared Service ~3–4 h (as forecast), p90 ≤ 12 h. Legal/Risk gets capacity or a triage filter so its utilization returns to ≤ 85% and wait falls below today's 9.6 h. Waiting share of sojourn falls for East, roughly unchanged for North/South.
- **Cycle-time distribution:** East median 8.6 d → ~7.2 d, p90 22.8 d → ~17 d. North/South median roughly flat (6.8 → 6.9 d, 7.4 → 7.3 d), p90 flat or slightly better. Overall p90 tightens; tails shrink because the single overloaded server (E_Tech) is gone.
- **Rework and quality pattern:** East rework 11.5% → ~9%; North/South rework flat; high-complexity rework flat because specialists gain volume-driven expertise and standardised notes compensate for lost regional knowledge. Rework after shared handover ≤ 9% (today's local specialist figure).
- **Role clarity and accountability:** Caseworker remains case owner end-to-end; shared service is explicitly a service *to* the caseworker with SLA, not a co-owner. Approval queue records decision owner; `discover_organizational_roles` returns clean, non-overlapping roles (intake, casework, technical specialist, legal/risk, approver).
- **Fairness implications:** Team gap closes (East converges toward North/South). Within-queue wait by `customer_segment` and by `team` for the same priority class differs by < 10%. Regional/language cases handled by caseworker with specialist advice, not re-routed to a stranger.
- **Early warning signs (that this scenario is *not* happening):** shared-queue p90 wait above 2× median in week 2–3; return-loop rate above 10%; Legal/Risk backlog growing week over week; East improving while North/South p90 degrades more than 10%.

## Central scenario (mixed: East gains, North/South lose a little, tails widen, averages look fine)

- **Handover pattern:** Handovers per case rise from ~3.4 to ~3.7–3.9. New loop appears: shared specialist → local caseworker → shared specialist ("clarification" / "missing context") in 10–15% of shared cases. Unique resources per case rises by about one. `reassignment_count` mean rises ~20–30%.
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern:** Technical Shared Service utilization lands at 85–90% (target plus absorbed QC/triage). Median wait 4–5 h, p90 15–25 h, with strong day-of-week pattern (Monday/Tuesday backlog). Legal/Risk backlog grows until an informal rule emerges ("only send if really necessary") — referrals drop back toward 9–10 per 100, undoing the "broader detection" goal. Approval queue with two supervisors: median 3 h as forecast but p90 well over a day.
- **Cycle-time distribution:** Overall median improves slightly (7.3 d → 7.0 d) driven by East (8.6 → 7.6 d). North/South medians drift up 0.3–0.6 d. Overall p90 flat or slightly worse; p95 for high-complexity cases worsens by 2–4 days. This is the scenario where management declares success from the mean while the tail and North/South customers say otherwise.
- **Rework and quality pattern:** Aggregate rework roughly flat (~9%). Composition changes: East low/medium complexity rework falls; North/South high-complexity rework rises (loss of regional conventions, context lost in queue handover). Rework after shared handover ~11–13%, between today's local-specialist (9%) and cross-team (18%) figures — consistent with "partially cross-team" work.
- **Role clarity and accountability:** Ambiguity at two seams: who decides whether a case is "difficult" for QC, and who owns a case waiting for approval. `handover_reason` shows rising "wrong queue"/"clarification". Some caseworkers stop taking ownership of waiting cases; customers call and nobody has the file.
- **Fairness implications:** Team convergence achieved partly by levelling down North/South. Within the shared queue, cases needing language/regional accommodation wait longer (specialists prefer standard cases they can close quickly); low-priority segments' p90 wait grows more than high-priority's. East customers gain, North/South customers with complex needs lose.
- **Early warning signs:** North/South local customer satisfaction slipping in month 1–2 while averages improve; growing gap between median and p90 of shared-queue wait; rising share of events where `handover_reason ∈ {clarification, missing_context, wrong_queue}`; caseworker referral rate rising while specialist "no action" returns also rise; Legal/Risk referrals *falling* after an initial rise (silent deflection).

## Adverse scenario (hidden bottleneck, ownership loss, skill erosion)

- **Handover pattern:** Handovers per case > 4.2. Handover network shows a dense star around the shared pool with many return edges and subcontracting patterns (specialist hands to another specialist, back to caseworker, back to queue). Cross-team handovers formally vanish but every complex case is effectively cross-team because the specialist is remote. Variants explode: `get_variants` shows the top-10 variants covering < 50% of cases (vs ~70% before).
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern:** Technical Shared Service utilization > 90%; queue never drains within the week; median wait 6–8 h but p90 > 2 days. Legal/Risk queue in permanent backlog; risk/legal cases are aged, then escalated, creating a second-order wait for approval. Approval queue with 2 supervisors becomes a second bottleneck; p90 approval wait > 1.5 days. Waiting share of sojourn time exceeds 80%.
- **Cycle-time distribution:** Median overall may *still* look flat or slightly better in month 1 because simple cases sail through local intake/eligibility. High-complexity median +2–3 d, p90 +5–8 d. SLA miss rate for high-complexity and legal/risk cases doubles. East improvement stalls after the initial gain because the shared pool inherits East's backlog plus the others'.
- **Rework and quality pattern:** Rework rate climbs to 11–13% overall; rework after shared handover approaches the current cross-team figure (18%), because context is now lost on *every* specialist referral. Difficult QC in the shared queue creates QC → Rework → QC loops with long waits between them; `get_rework_cases_per_activity` shows Quality Check and Specialist Review as top rework activities.
- **Role clarity and accountability:** Nobody owns complex cases; caseworkers "fire and forget" into the queue; specialists process tickets without customer contact; the approval queue rubber-stamps or bounces. `discover_organizational_roles` produces blurred roles; `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` shows caseworkers converging to an intake-like profile.
- **Fairness implications:** Complex, regional-language, and low-priority segments become the queue's residual class with the longest waits. North/South customers see clear service degradation; East gains modestly but its customers also lose local tech contact. Staff fairness: shared specialists carry three teams' hard cases at > 90% utilization with no local relief; attrition risk.
- **Early warning signs:** In the first 2–4 weeks: shared-queue arrival rate persistently above completion rate; specialist overtime; referral-return loops > 20%; high-complexity p90 cycle time worsening in the *pilot* team while its median holds; `local_expertise_flag` share falling sharply; sudden drop in Legal/Risk referrals (deflection) or sudden rise in cases closing without Specialist Review despite `required_specialty` set.

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1. **Tail latency (p90/p95).** Pooling and higher utilization typically improve or hold the median while stretching the tail: a few cases sit behind a backlog for days. The management table itself already implies +23% handover waiting per case; averaged over 100 cases this may be absorbed by faster simple cases, but the cases that wait are concentrated. Monitor p90 and p95 by activity edge, not just case mean.

2. **High-complexity cases.** They are exactly the cases routed away from local knowledge and into the busiest queue. Simple cases (kept local, more automation) get faster and dominate the mean; complex cases get slower. A flat or improving average with a worsening high-complexity distribution is the most likely outcome and the least visible in a KPI dashboard that reports one number.

3. **Local knowledge loss and unnecessary referrals.** Informal advice from an embedded specialist never generated an event; its disappearance appears only as *more* referrals (36 → 41 is already planned), more "no action needed" returns, and higher rework in regional/complex cases. Average cycle time can stay flat while the organisation is spending more specialist hours to achieve the same outcome.

4. **Fairness across teams and customer segments.** East improving by 1.5 days while North and South each worsen by 0.5 days produces a better weighted mean (East is the smallest team but had the worst baseline) and a worse experience for 75% of customers. Within the shared queue, segments needing language or regional accommodation, or low `priority` classes, can be systematically deprioritised without moving the average.

5. **Learning and skill erosion.** If local caseworkers never see hard cases through, their skill profile narrows over 6–18 months. This has no cycle-time signature at all in the first quarter; it appears later as higher referral rates, lower `local_expertise_flag`, homogenised resource profiles, and a shared service that cannot ever be de-centralised because the local capability is gone (irreversibility).

6. **Accountability and ownership.** A case with six resources and three queues can meet its cycle-time target while nobody can answer the customer's call. Symptoms are in `handover_reason`, `reassignment_count`, return loops and unique resources per case — none of which are cycle time.

7. **Exception handling and resilience.** Cross-team cover "works poorly because notes are inconsistent"; the shared model makes *every* specialist interaction depend on notes. Exceptions (customer escalation, legal hold, urgent security case) previously handled by walking to the embedded specialist now go through the queue. Exceptions are rare, so their delay barely moves the mean; but they are the cases with the highest consequence.

8. **Silent deflection at Legal/Risk.** With Legal/Risk demand above capacity, the system stabilises only by not sending cases. Average cycle time then *improves* because risk/legal cases are closed without review — an outcome-quality and compliance risk that appears as a mismatch between `required_specialty` and actual Specialist Review events, and later as rework or incidents.

9. **Hidden utilization and burnout.** Specialist "utilization up 15%" is reported as success; the cost — overtime, sick leave, attrition, cherry-picking of easy cases — appears months later and is not a cycle-time measure.

# 5. Pilot design

**Principle:** test the mechanism where it is most likely to help (East, overloaded E_Tech) *and* where it is most likely to hurt (a strong local team), with the other strong team as an untouched control, before removing any supervisor or merging Legal/Risk.

- **Pilot scope (Phase 1, 12 weeks):**
  - Create the Technical Shared Service from E_Tech + S_Tech serving **East and South**; **North keeps its embedded N_Tech and local loop as the control**.
  - Route according to the proposed rules for East and South only (high-complexity → shared tech; `required_specialty ∈ {legal, risk, security}` → shared specialist queue).
  - **Do not** remove a supervisor in Phase 1; instead run the shared approval queue as an *additional* option for medium/high-value cases in East/South, with the decision owner logged as `org:resource`.
  - **Do not** add difficult QC to the shared queue until tech wait metrics are stable for 4 consecutive weeks; QC centralisation is Phase 2.
  - Legal/Risk: add capacity (or a triage step with a 30-minute service-level) *before* widening detection; otherwise cap the referral change and monitor for deflection.
  - Mandate a structured case-note template in the pilot teams from day one; track template compliance as a leading indicator.
- **Comparison group:** North (business as usual) is the concurrent control; each pilot team is also compared to its own 6-month pre-period. Because East's baseline is worse, use difference-in-differences: Δ(East pilot − East pre) versus Δ(North pilot period − North pre), and likewise for South. South is the key test: if a strong local team degrades, North/South rollout should be reconsidered.
- **Pre/post observation window:** 6 months pre (to capture seasonality, absences and the current handover figures), 2-week run-in excluded from analysis, 12 weeks measurement, then decision. Lagging indicators (rework, satisfaction, outcomes) need at least 8 weeks of *closed* cases, so the formal review is at week 14.
- **Segmentation:** every indicator reported by `team` × `case_complexity` × `required_specialty` × `customer_segment` (and `priority`). Minimum reported cells: East/South/North × {low, medium, high} × {none, technical, legal/risk/security} × {standard, regional/language, other}. Report medians *and* p90; report cell counts so small cells are not over-read.
- **Guardrail metrics (with thresholds relative to pre-period, evaluated weekly):**
  - Shared-queue p90 wait ≤ 2.5× pre-period p90 wait for the corresponding local handover; sustained breach 2 weeks → adjustment.
  - Technical Shared Service utilization ≤ 85%; Legal/Risk ≤ 90%.
  - High-complexity cycle-time p90 in South not worse than pre by more than 10%.
  - Rework after shared handover ≤ 12% (between local 9% and cross-team 18%).
  - Return-loop rate (shared → local → shared) ≤ 10% of shared cases.
  - SLA miss rate in any segment cell not worse than pre by more than 2 percentage points.
  - Within-queue wait gap between customer segments at the same priority ≤ 15%.
  - Legal/Risk referrals not below `required_specialty` incidence (deflection check).
  - `local_expertise_flag` share in South not falling more than 20% relative.
- **Staff feedback collection:** weekly 3-question pulse (ownership clarity, workload, ability to get informal advice) for caseworkers, specialists and supervisors in all three teams (North as control); biweekly 30-minute structured interviews with 2 caseworkers + 1 specialist per pilot team; a "friction log" where staff record clarification loops and wrong-queue events (cross-checked against `handover_reason`). Collect customer satisfaction per closed case with segment tag.
- **Rollback or adjustment triggers:**
  - *Adjust* (add capacity, tighten routing, restore local pre-triage): any guardrail breached for 2 consecutive weeks.
  - *Pause expansion* (no QC centralisation, no supervisor removal): South high-complexity p90 or rework worsens beyond guardrail, or return-loop rate > 15%.
  - *Rollback* to embedded model for the affected team: SLA miss rate for high-complexity or legal/risk cases up > 4 points for 3 weeks, or Legal/Risk deflection detected, or shared-service utilization > 90% for 3 weeks with growing backlog, or staff pulse "I know who owns this case" falls below a pre-agreed floor.
  - *Proceed to Phase 2* (add North; add difficult QC; consider supervisor consolidation): all guardrails held for 8 consecutive weeks, East improves on median *and* p90, South does not degrade on p90/rework/satisfaction, and segment fairness gaps stay within threshold.

# 6. Recommendation

**Modify, then pilot; do not proceed to full rollout as specified.**

Reasoning:

- The pooling logic is genuinely sound for East: E_Tech at 82% is the single most constrained resource and East has the worst cycle time, tail, rework and SLA performance. Pooling three specialists reduces variability and gives East access to spare North/South capacity. This is the part of the plan most likely to deliver.
- The plan as written has three quantitative problems that make it unsafe to roll out unmodified: (1) Legal/Risk demand exceeds capacity (≈ 120%+), so "10.5 h median wait" is not an achievable number; (2) approval load per supervisor rises ~1.75× while the shared approval queue also becomes the accountability seam; (3) management's own routing table shows *more* handovers and *more* waiting per case, so the projected benefit depends entirely on unmodelled second-order gains (less rework, fewer wrong referrals) that staff plausibly argue will move the other way.
- Two effects are slow and hard to reverse — local skill erosion and loss of regional knowledge — so they should be tested on one strong team (South) with the other (North) as control before being imposed on both.

Conditions attached to the recommendation (monitoring logic):

- Proceed with the modified Phase 1 pilot (East + South tech pooling, North control, no supervisor removal, QC and Legal/Risk widening deferred until capacity is added).
- Expand only if, for 8 consecutive weeks, the guardrails in Section 5 hold: shared-queue p90 wait within bound, utilization ≤ 85%/90%, South high-complexity p90 and rework not degraded, return-loop rate ≤ 10%, segment fairness gap ≤ 15%, no Legal/Risk deflection, staff ownership-clarity pulse stable.
- Adjust or roll back on the triggers in Section 5; the decision to remove a supervisor should be made only after the shared approval queue has demonstrated p90 approval wait and decision-owner logging for a full quarter.

What management should **not** infer from early averages:

- A better overall mean cycle time in month 1 does not mean the model works. It is the expected result of East improving from a poor baseline plus simple cases moving faster locally, and it is consistent with the central and even the adverse scenario. Look at p90/p95 by complexity and team, and at South specifically.
- "Fewer local handovers" is not "fewer handovers": count resource switches per case and return loops, not the number of distinct edges.
- Higher specialist utilization is a cost signal as much as a benefit; utilization above 85% with a growing backlog is failure, not success.
- Falling Legal/Risk referrals after an initial rise is likely deflection, not improved routing.
- Improvements in the first quarter say nothing about learning loss, which takes 6–18 months to show up and is the least reversible effect. Track `local_expertise_flag`, referral rates and caseworker role profiles quarterly for at least a year even if the pilot passes.
- Team convergence is only good if it is convergence upward; East improving while North/South degrade is redistribution, and 75% of customers are in North/South.

import pm4py

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# Step 0 - Load and format; split into before / after cohorts
# ---------------------------------------------------------------

df["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"], utc=True)
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df, case_id="case:concept:name", activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
)
# resource key is already 'org:resource'

RUN_IN_END  = CHANGE_DATE + pd.Timedelta(weeks=2)

before_ids = case_start[case_start <  CHANGE_DATE].index
after_ids  = case_start[case_start >= RUN_IN_END].index
before = df[df["case:concept:name"].isin(before_ids)].copy()
after  = df[df["case:concept:name"].isin(after_ids)].copy()

def seg(d, team=None, complexity=None, specialty=None, segment=None, priority=None):
    out = d
    if team:       out = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(out, "team", set(team), level="case", retain=True)
    if complexity: out = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(out, "case_complexity", set(complexity), level="case", retain=True)
    if specialty:  out = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(out, "required_specialty", set(specialty), level="case", retain=True)
    if segment:    out = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(out, "customer_segment", set(segment), level="case", retain=True)
    if priority:   out = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(out, "priority", set(priority), level="case", retain=True)
    return out

def handover_summary(d, label):
    how = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(d, beta=0)   # {(res_a, res_b): weight}
    n_edges = len(how)
    # resource switches per case (direct measure of "nominal handovers")
    d = d.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"])
    prev = d.groupby("case:concept:name")["org:resource"].shift()
    switches = ((d["org:resource"] != prev) & prev.notna()).groupby(d["case:concept:name"]).sum()
    uniq_res = d.groupby("case:concept:name")["org:resource"].nunique()
    shared = d["org:resource"].str.startswith(("TSS_", "Shared_", "Specialist_"))
    # return loop: shared -> local -> shared within a case
    is_shared = shared.astype(int).groupby(d["case:concept:name"]).apply(list)
    def has_return(seq):
        s = "".join(map(str, seq))
        return "101" in s.replace("00", "0").replace("11", "1")
    return {
        "cohort": label,
        "distinct_handover_edges": n_edges,
        "handovers_per_case_mean": switches.mean(),
        "handovers_per_case_p90": switches.quantile(0.9),
        "unique_resources_per_case": uniq_res.mean(),
        "return_loop_rate": is_shared.apply(has_return).mean(),
        "reassignment_count_mean": d.groupby("case:concept:name")["reassignment_count"].max().mean(),
        "top_edges": sorted(how.items(), key=lambda kv: -kv[1])[:15],
    }

def variant_stats(d):
    v = pm4py.get_variants(d)                           # {variant: count or traces}
    counts = sorted([len(x) if hasattr(x, "__len__") and not isinstance(x, int) else x
                     for x in v.values()], reverse=True)

    return {"n_variants": len(counts), "top10_coverage": sum(counts[:10]) / total}

# compute edge-level durations directly from consecutive events:

def edge_durations(d):
    """Per-edge waiting time (hours) between consecutive events; used for p50/p90/p95."""
    d = d.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"])
    g = d.groupby("case:concept:name")
    d = d.assign(prev_act=g["concept:name"].shift(), prev_ts=g["time:timestamp"].shift())
    d = d.dropna(subset=["prev_act"])
    d["wait_h"] = (d["time:timestamp"] - d["prev_ts"]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    return d.groupby(["prev_act", "concept:name"])["wait_h"].quantile([0.5, 0.9, 0.95]).unstack()

# If a start timestamp exists (e.g. 'start_timestamp'), service time is real;
# otherwise service time collapses to 0 and sojourn == waiting, which is still
# useful for waiting-share comparisons.
def case_times(d):
    ct = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
        d, service_time_column="@@service", sojourn_time_column="@@sojourn",
        waiting_time_column="@@waiting",
        start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp" if "start_timestamp" in d.columns else "time:timestamp",
    )
    per_case = ct.groupby("case:concept:name").agg(
        sojourn=("@@sojourn", "first"), waiting=("@@waiting", "first"), service=("@@service", "first"),
        team=("team", "first"), complexity=("case_complexity", "first"),
        specialty=("required_specialty", "first"), segment=("customer_segment", "first"),
        priority=("priority", "first"),
        rework=("rework_flag", "max"), outcome=("outcome", "last"),
        local_exp=("local_expertise_flag", "max"),
    )
    per_case["cycle_d"] = per_case["sojourn"] / 86400
    per_case["wait_share"] = per_case["waiting"] / per_case["sojourn"].replace(0, pd.NA)
    return per_case

# Resource utilization proxy per week: busy time (sum of service) / available hours
def utilization(d, resources, hours_per_week=37.5):
    d = d[d["org:resource"].isin(resources)]
    if "start_timestamp" in d.columns:
        busy = (d["time:timestamp"] - pd.to_datetime(d["start_timestamp"], utc=True)).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    else:
        busy = pd.Series(0.5, index=d.index)   # fallback: assumed handling time per event; replace with measured
    wk = d["time:timestamp"].dt.to_period("W")
    return (busy.groupby([wk, d["org:resource"]]).sum() / hours_per_week).unstack()

# Queue growth (leading indicator): arrivals into vs completions of shared activities per day
def queue_growth(d, activity="Specialist Review"):
    d = d.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"])
    g = d.groupby("case:concept:name")
    nxt = g["concept:name"].shift(-1); nxt_ts = g["time:timestamp"].shift(-1)
    arrivals = d.loc[nxt == activity, "time:timestamp"].dt.floor("D").value_counts().sort_index()
    completions = d.loc[d["concept:name"] == activity, "time:timestamp"].dt.floor("D").value_counts().sort_index()
    q = pd.DataFrame({"arrivals": arrivals, "completions": completions}).fillna(0)
    q["backlog"] = (q["arrivals"] - q["completions"]).cumsum()
    return q

# Rework rate AFTER a shared-service handover (analogue of the 9% / 14% / 18% column)
def rework_after_handover(d, from_pred, to_pred):
    d = d.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"])
    g = d.groupby("case:concept:name")
    prev_res = g["org:resource"].shift()
    from_ok = prev_res.notna() & prev_res.fillna("").map(from_pred)
    mask = from_ok & d["org:resource"].map(to_pred)
    cases = d.loc[mask, "case:concept:name"].unique()
    rw = d[d["case:concept:name"].isin(cases)].groupby("case:concept:name")["rework_flag"].max()
    return rw.mean(), len(cases)

# Legal/Risk deflection check: required_specialty set but no Specialist Review by a legal/risk resource
def deflection(d):
    need = d[d["required_specialty"].isin(["legal", "risk", "security"])]["case:concept:name"].unique()
    reviewed = d[(d["concept:name"] == "Specialist Review") &
                 d["org:resource"].str.startswith("Specialist_")]["case:concept:name"].unique()
    return 1 - len(set(need) & set(reviewed)) / max(1, len(need))

# Within-queue fairness: wait into Specialist Review by segment at the same priority
def queue_wait_by(d, activity, by):
    d = d.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"])
    g = d.groupby("case:concept:name")
    d = d.assign(prev_ts=g["time:timestamp"].shift())
    x = d[(d["concept:name"] == activity) & d["prev_ts"].notna()].copy()
    x["wait_h"] = (x["time:timestamp"] - x["prev_ts"]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    return x.groupby(by)["wait_h"].quantile([0.5, 0.9]).unstack()
