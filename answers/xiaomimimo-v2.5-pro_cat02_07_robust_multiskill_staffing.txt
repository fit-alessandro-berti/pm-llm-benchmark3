# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

## Demand and service assumptions
- Service times are drawn uniformly from the specified ranges. Complex-tail cases are drawn from the complex-tail range with the given probability. Each case independently draws whether it's complex-tail at arrival time.
- Arrival times within each 2-hour block are uniformly distributed.
- Priority and case type are independently assigned according to the block percentages.
- ME and ES cases require both branches (not either/or).
- Resource multipliers apply to the drawn base service time.
- Breaks are fixed and non-negotiable; resources cannot work during breaks.
- Overtime is limited to 30 minutes per resource per day.

## Parallel vs sequential ME/ES
- **Parallel mode**: both branches start as soon as qualified resources are available; the case waits for both to complete; 8-min coordination delay before Validation.
- **Sequential mode**: a single cross-skilled resource performs both branches in sequence; no coordination delay; no cross-team handover penalty.
- The policy determines which mode is used, potentially case-by-case.

## Objective formulations

**Objective 1: SLA-Risk-Weighted Delay**
Minimize: _cases (w_priority * max(0, completion_time - SLA_target))
where w_P1=100, w_P2=10, w_P3=1. This heavily penalizes P1 SLA breaches.

**Objective 2: Fairness-and-Robustness Objective**
Minimize:  * mean_delay +  * max_resource_utilization +  * utilization_variance +  * worst_case_P1_SLA_gap
where , , ,  are weights. This balances efficiency, overload risk, fairness across resources, and worst-case P1 performance.

**Objective 3 (for reference): Minimize Mean Completion Time**
Minimize average elapsed time across all cases. This is the standard efficiency metric but ignores priorities and fairness.

## Decision variables
