**Explicit note on DFG both-directions.** The presence of both CC→ER and ER→CC (and the limited SC interleaving edges) is consistent with (a) true concurrency whose completions are serialized by the logger, (b) exclusive choice between two sequential orders, or (c) overlapping executions. Without start timestamps, resource data or the external/stocked attribute, none of these interpretations can be proven. D7 and S7 make the limitation explicit; therefore every model treats concurrency as an inductive bias choice, not a fact derived from the DFG.

# 2. Shared Assumptions

All three candidates share the following:

- **Event-completion semantics only.** Models constrain the order of completion events. They make no claim about start times, durations or true simultaneity (D7, S7). When a model uses concurrent or partial-order nodes, the intended meaning is unconstrained interleaving of completions.
- **SC optionality abstraction.** SC is represented by a control-flow alternative (XOR with a silent transition). This single construct abstracts over two observationally indistinguishable causes: (a) legitimate skip for stocked components (S4) and (b) missing events caused by the known integration problem (S5, estimated 5–10 %). The observed absence rate (~23 %) exceeds the estimated missing rate, so true optionality is the primary explanation, yet the models explicitly disclose that the attribute needed to separate the two causes is absent.
- **XR optionality.** XR is likewise optional (XOR silent), occurs at most once, and is constrained to occur only after both CC and ER have completed and before AC (D6, S6).
- **Mandatory spine.** RO is the unique start activity; both CC and ER complete before AC; AC → RP → Arch is the unique end sequence (D1–D3 and all 30 traces).
- **No loops, no duplicate executions, no other activities.** Every activity appears at most once per case.
- **Missing-data treatment.** Only SC (and XR) optionality is made explicit. Other events are assumed present in the log; residual missingness risk is acknowledged but not modelled by additional silent transitions.
- **Stakeholder conflicts resolved by evidence weight.** Observed both orders of CC/ER outweigh S1; observed skips outweigh S3. Models remain hypotheses, none claimed “true”.

# 3. Conservative Candidate

**Inductive-bias statement.** The conservative model refuses any completion sequence that is not among the seven observed variants. It therefore prioritises high precision and exact fitness on the given log at the expense of generalisation. High-confidence rules (D1–D3) are enforced; medium- and low-confidence rules are realised only to the extent they appear in the data. Concurrency is never assumed—orders are treated as exclusive sequential alternatives.

**Compact formal expression.**  
Sequence( RO , XOR(  
  Sequence(CC, ER, SC),  
  Sequence(ER, CC, SC),  
  Sequence(CC, ER),  
  Sequence(ER, CC),  
  Sequence(CC, SC, ER),  
  Sequence(CC, ER, SC, XR),  
  Sequence(ER, CC, XR, SC)  
) , AC , RP , Arch )

**Self-contained PM4Py code.**

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator, ProcessTree  # for reference

model_conservative = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "->('Register Order', "
    "X( "
    "->('Credit Check', 'Engineering Review', 'Supplier Confirmation'), "
    "->('Engineering Review', 'Credit Check', 'Supplier Confirmation'), "
    "->('Credit Check', 'Engineering Review'), "
    "->('Engineering Review', 'Credit Check'), "
    "->('Credit Check', 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Engineering Review'), "
    "->('Credit Check', 'Engineering Review', 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Expedite Review'), "
    "->('Engineering Review', 'Credit Check', 'Expedite Review', 'Supplier Confirmation') "
    "), "
    "'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case')"
)
```

**Behaviour deliberately excluded.**  
Any interleaving not listed above (e.g. ER-SC-CC, SC first after RO, XR without the exact observed flanking activities, XR after only one check, SC after AC, missing CC or ER, any activity after Arch or before RO).

**Known underfitting risks.**  
- Cannot accept a newly observed legitimate interleaving of SC with the checks or a different XR/SC order.  
- Treats the two XR cases as rigid sequences rather than an optional activity under D6.  
- Scalability of pure XOR enumeration is poor for larger or noisier logs; duplicate activity labels across branches can also become problematic (not an issue here because each path uses each label at most once).  
- Over-fits the particular 30-case sample and therefore has low robustness to the known 5–10 % SC missingness.

# 4. Balanced Candidate

**Inductive-bias statement.** The balanced model encodes the most plausible generalisation that still respects all high- and medium-confidence rules and fits every observed trace. CC and ER are treated as concurrent (no order edge) because both completion orders are frequent, SC interleaving is observed, and S2/D7 permit the concurrency hypothesis. SC is optional and may interleave freely with the two checks (partial order with no edges among the trio). XR is optional, forced after both checks, and free to interleave with SC. The residual uncertainty about true concurrency versus sequential alternatives, and about missing versus skipped SC, is managed by explicit disclosure rather than by forcing a single sequential order.

**Compact formal expression (POWL).**  
StrictPartialOrder nodes = {RO, CC, ER, sc_opt = XOR(SC, τ), ex_opt = XOR(XR, τ), AC, RP, Arch}  
edges: RO → {CC, ER, sc_opt},  
CC → ex_opt, ER → ex_opt,  
{CC, ER, sc_opt, ex_opt} → AC → RP → Arch  
(no edges among CC, ER, sc_opt ⇒ any interleaving; XR after both).

**Self-contained PM4Py code.**

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

RO = Transition(label="Register Order")
CC = Transition(label="Credit Check")
ER = Transition(label="Engineering Review")
SC = Transition(label="Supplier Confirmation")
XR = Transition(label="Expedite Review")
AC = Transition(label="Approve Configuration")
RP = Transition(label="Release Production")
AR = Transition(label="Archive Case")
skip_sc = SilentTransition()
skip_xr = SilentTransition()

sc_opt = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[SC, skip_sc])
ex_opt = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[XR, skip_xr])

model_balanced = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[RO, CC, ER, sc_opt, ex_opt, AC, RP, AR])
model_balanced.order.add_edge(RO, CC)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(RO, ER)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(RO, sc_opt)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(CC, ex_opt)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(ER, ex_opt)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(CC, AC)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(ER, AC)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(sc_opt, AC)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(ex_opt, AC)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(AC, RP)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(RP, AR)
```

**Decision between sequence / concurrency / partial order.**  
- CC vs ER: concurrent (partial order, no edge). Evidence: both orders frequent (V1/V2), interleaving via SC (V5), S2, D7. A forced sequence would arbitrarily prefer one stakeholder view; an XOR of the two sequences is more conservative and is left to the conservative model.  
- SC relative to the checks: free interleaving (no edges). Evidence: V5 and D5 (“after either”). Forcing SC after both would under-fit V5; forcing SC after a specific check is unsupported.  
- XR: partial-order successors of both CC and ER (edges). Evidence: D6 and S6 are medium-to-high confidence and the two observed cases obey them; free interleaving with SC is retained because both orders appear.

**Behaviour deliberately excluded.**  
Missing CC or ER; XR before both checks; SC after AC; any activity outside the spine; loops or duplicate executions.

**Known underfitting risks.**  
- Admits the unobserved linear extension “SC before both checks” (mild over-generalisation of D5).  
- Does not capture possible resource or attribute conditions that would make SC mandatory for a subset of cases.  
- Relies on the concurrency hypothesis; if future start-timestamp data refute overlapping executions, the model will be too permissive.

# 5. Permissive Candidate

**Inductive-bias statement.** The permissive model admits every linear extension that is still consistent with the high-confidence rules (D1–D3) and the broad medium-confidence constraints, even when those extensions are unobserved. CC, ER, optional SC and optional XR may complete in any order after RO and before AC. This maximises generalisation and robustness to the known SC missingness while still forbidding clear violations of the mandatory spine.

**Compact formal expression.**  
Sequence( RO ,  
  Parallel( CC , ER , XOR(SC, τ) , XOR(XR, τ) ) ,  
  AC , RP , Arch )

**Self-contained PM4Py code.**

```python
model_permissive = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "->('Register Order', "
    "+('Credit Check', 'Engineering Review', "
    "X('Supplier Confirmation', tau), "
    "X('Expedite Review', tau)), "
    "'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case')"
)
```

**Boundary between reasonable generalisation and forbidden behaviour.**  
Reasonable: any interleaving of the four activities (including XR before one or both checks, SC completely first, XR without SC, SC without XR).  
Still forbidden: missing CC or ER; SC or XR after AC; RP or Arch before AC; any activity before RO or after Arch; loops; duplicate activity executions. These violate D1–D3 (high confidence) and are therefore excluded even under a permissive bias.

**Behaviour deliberately excluded.**  
Any trace that omits CC or ER, places SC/XR after AC, violates the RO-start / Arch-end spine, or introduces loops/duplicates.

**Known underfitting risks.**  
Almost none on the observed log (perfect fitness expected). The principal risk is over-generalisation: XR-before-both is admitted although D6/S6 discourage it, and SC-before-both is admitted although never observed. The model is therefore more suitable for exploration than for compliance checking.

# 9. Recommendation and Data-Collection Plan

**Immediate-use recommendation:** `model_balanced`.  
It achieves perfect fitness on the observed log, respects every high- and medium-confidence rule, admits only a modest set of unobserved but plausible interleavings, and makes the uncertainty about concurrency and SC missingness explicit. It is suitable both for operational monitoring and for moderate generalisation.

**Sensitivity-analysis recommendation:** `model_conservative`.  
Use it whenever a compliance or audit stakeholder requires a model that cannot produce any unobserved behaviour. Compare its diagnostics with those of the balanced model to quantify the precision/generalisation trade-off. The permissive model can be retained for pure exploration / “what-if” scenario generation under missing-data hypotheses.

**Concrete data-collection plan to resolve the main ambiguities**

1. **Lifecycle timestamps.** Record both start and complete events for CC, ER, SC and XR. Overlapping intervals would corroborate the concurrency hypothesis; strict non-overlap would favour sequential alternatives.  
2. **External / stocked attribute.** Export the missing attribute for every order. This separates true SC skips (S4) from integration losses (S5) and allows a guarded optional construct instead of a pure control-flow XOR.  
3. **Instrumentation of the SC integration.** Reduce the estimated 5–10 % missing rate; any residual absences can then be treated as true optionality with higher confidence.  
4. **Targeted future traces.** Prioritise collection of cases that contain XR, cases that omit SC, and any new interleavings of SC with the two checks. These traces maximise the information gain for distinguishing XOR-of-sequences versus concurrent versus partial-order hypotheses.  
5. **Resource / queue data.** Identify whether CC and ER share a resource pool or can truly execute in parallel; this supplies independent evidence for the concurrency bias.

After 50–100 new cases with the additional attributes, re-discover and re-evaluate the three candidates; the balanced model is expected to remain stable, while the conservative model will either stay exact or require a controlled expansion of its XOR.

# 10. Uncertainty Statement

| Major modelling decision | Calibrated confidence | Notes |
|--------------------------|-----------------------|-------|
| Unique RO start / Arch end / AC→RP→Arch spine | ~95 % | Universal in data + D1/D2 |
| Both CC and ER required before AC | ~90 % | Universal in data + D3 |
| SC represented as optional (control-flow XOR) | ~70 % | Observed 23 % absence vs claimed 5–10 % missing; S4 vs S3 conflict |
| CC ‖ ER (concurrent / free interleaving) | ~60 % | Both orders frequent, S2, D7; contradicted by S1 |
| XR only after both checks and before AC | ~75 % | D6 + S6 + two observed cases; sparse data |
| Free interleaving of SC with the checks | ~55 % | Supported by V5 and D5 (low-med); SC-before-both remains unobserved |
| Exact XOR enumeration for conservative | ~85 % (for precision) / ~30 % (for usefulness) | Perfect on current log; brittle to any new legitimate variant |

**Plausible alternative interpretation that would materially change the recommendation.**  
If the Finance manager’s rule (S1) is an enforced organisational policy and the observed ER-before-CC orders are logging artefacts or rare exceptions, then a sequential CC → ER backbone becomes the preferred hypothesis. In that case the balanced and permissive models would be rewritten with a forced sequence CC → ER (or an XOR that heavily prefers that order), the concurrency bias would be abandoned, and the conservative model (or a sequential variant of it) would become the primary recommendation for compliance use. The present data cannot rule this interpretation out; only start-timestamp or policy-audit evidence can.
