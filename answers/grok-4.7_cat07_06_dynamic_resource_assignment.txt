# 1. Objectives and constraints

The policy chooses a feasible assignment for each waiting case under several objectives at once. No single objective is allowed to dominate. Short-term speed, correct work, learning, fairness, stable ownership, and the ability to absorb an absence are all in scope. Historical evidence is used as a governed input, not as a hidden weight: cases with more than two reassignments have shown about 1.8× rework and 1.4× appeals; changing owner on a high-complexity case has added about 2.7 hours of waiting; keeping a weak skill on the case has added about 3.3 hours of service time and more rework; specialist-only routing has piled up Security and Approval; first-available routing has cut waiting time and raised mismatches and reassignments. Those quantities should be re-estimated from the log on a set cadence. Until they are revised in a reviewed change, the live policy uses them as explicit thresholds.

Urgency classes, used everywhere below:

- **Critical:** priority is urgent, or SLA time remaining is 4 hours or less.
- **Elevated:** priority is high, or SLA time remaining is 8 hours or less, and the case is not critical.
- **Standard:** priority is normal and the case is not critical or elevated.
- **Flexible:** priority is low, or SLA time remaining is at least 48 hours, and the case is not in a tighter class.

High complexity means `case_complexity >= 0.70`. Cases from 0.60 to 0.69 receive a partial continuity preference only.

## Optimization objectives

These are preferences. They are traded off in the scoring and close-call rules. They are not absolute.

1. **SLA reliability.** Critical cases start with someone who can finish inside the remaining time. Elevated cases are next. Flexible cases may wait.
2. **Correctness.** Prefer a skill that can complete the activity without a predictable rework penalty. Security and Approval carry stricter sign-off rules than the other activities.
3. **Continuity.** Keep the current owner when a handover would be expensive, when complexity is high, or when the case has already changed hands twice.
4. **Fairness and sustainable load.** Spread new work toward people with room, and stop adding work to people already at high overtime risk.
5. **Skill development.** On standard and flexible cases, give real ownership to people whose documented training priority matches the activity, with a named supervisor and a checkpoint.
6. **Resilience.** Keep Security and Approval from depending on one person in this batch. Keep a second specialist able to absorb a critical arrival. Prefer coherent blocks of similar work so one absence does not scatter a whole activity.
7. **Throughput as a consequence.** Less waiting is valuable. It is pursued by giving people work they can finish, and by batching suitable Quality Checks. It is not pursued by filling the first free person.

## Hard constraints (safety, compliance, and stop-rules)

These remove candidates before any score is computed. A close call cannot override them.

1. **Skill 0 is not assignable.** A score of 0 means the person must not perform the activity and must not be the accountable resource. They may shadow only if a separate attribute records the shadow. The event that completes the activity still carries the accountable resource.
2. **Security Review sign-off.** The accountable performer has skill 3, or has skill 1–2 with explicit supervision by R7_Gita or R8_Hugo. Skill 0 cannot perform it under supervision. On a critical Security case, skill below 3 is also excluded when the remaining SLA is shorter than the 3.3-hour weak-skill service penalty.
3. **Final Approval sign-off.** Only skill 2 or 3 may complete the activity. Skill 1 may prepare material and must not be the completing resource. Skill 0 may not prepare.
4. **High-priority skill floor.** Urgent and high cases go to skill 2 or better. Skill 1 is allowed only with a named supervisor of skill 2 or better, and only when the SLA can absorb slower handling.
5. **Batch cap.** At most 4 new assignments per resource in this decision batch. Supervision and a time-boxed shadow do not count as one of the 4. They do count as real load and are visible in the reason.
6. **Quality batching gate.** Two or more Quality Checks may be given to the same person as a batch only when every case in the batch is normal or low priority and has more than 24 hours of SLA left.
7. **Overtime stop-rule.** A resource with overtime risk `high` gets no new case unless the case is urgent and every safer eligible person is impossible. Safer means skill-legal, under the batch cap, and not high overtime risk.
8. **Unsafe continuity stop-rule.** If complexity is high and the handover cost is high, keep the owner when the owner is still legal. Drop the owner when their skill is 0, or their skill is 1 and either supervision is impossible or the SLA is shorter than the weak-skill penalty. Do not “keep” someone the overtime stop-rule has already removed.
9. **Hours gate.** Critical cases require at least 2.5 hours remaining, elevated cases 2.0, standard cases 1.5, flexible cases 1.0. This is a floor, not an effort estimate.
10. **One accountable owner.** A supervisor, buddy, or shadow is named in attributes. They are not a second owner. Pre-authorized release valves name the alternate before the day starts.

## Soft constraints and trade-offs the score may accept

- A standard case may move off its owner to protect overtime risk or to form a legal Quality batch, when handover cost is low or medium.
- A third reassignment may be accepted only when the owner is illegal under a hard rule. The extra rework and appeal risk is then flagged, not smoothed over.
- The training share target is at least 20% of normal and low cases, when SLA risk stays acceptable. If the only way to hit 20% breaks a hard rule, the share is missed and the gap is logged. Shadowing a skill-0 person can support a documented training priority; it does not make them the performer.
- On Security and Approval, a non-critical case should not all sit with the only skill-3 person when a legal skill-2 person has room. That diversion is blocked if the skill-2 person already holds two elevated or critical cases.
- When two legal options are close, prefer less context-switching, keep a waiting customer with the negotiator who already holds the case, and keep a flexible case of an already-mastered skill with that specialist if the alternative would crowd a scarce-skill reserve.
- A person who holds a critical scarce-skill case is not also given unrelated new work while another competent person exists.
- Flexible cases carry a deferral right: the owner may start them the next day if remaining SLA stays above 24 hours and today’s tighter work, including a scheduled shadow or training checkpoint, needs the time.

# 2. Assignment policy

The policy is a filter, then a sort, then a small score with named reason codes. Workers see the reason codes. They do not see a single opaque total unless an audit asks for the tie-break arithmetic.

Reason codes: `OWNER_CONTINUITY`, `SLA_SPECIALIST`, `OT_PROTECTION`, `TRAINING_OWNERSHIP`, `TRAINING_SHADOW`, `BATCH_QUALITY`, `LOAD_AND_FOCUS`, `SCARCE_SKILL_SPLIT`, `MASTERED_SKILL_FLEXIBLE`, `FORCED_REASSIGN_OT`, `RELEASE_VALVE`, `HOT_BACKUP`, `CLOSE_CALL_CONTEXT`.

```text
function assign(queue, people, skills):

    for case in queue:
        case.class ← urgency_class(case)
        case.high_cx ← (case.complexity ≥ 0.70)

    # Bundle legal Quality Checks so they are scored as a group.
    bundles ← group batchable Quality Checks
               (priority in {normal, low} AND sla_remaining > 24h)
    ordered ← sort queue by
               class rank (critical, elevated, standard, flexible),
               sla_remaining ascending,
               complexity descending,
               handover cost (high before low)
    # A Quality bundle is inserted at the position of its most urgent member.

    new_load[r] ← 0 for every resource
    scarce[activity, r] ← 0
    elevated_or_critical[r] ← 0

    for item in ordered:                      # item is a case or a quality bundle
        candidates ← [r for r in people if eligible(item, r)]
        if candidates is empty:
            candidates ← relax_once(item)     # overtime only, and only if urgent
        if candidates is empty:
            park item for the dispatcher with the failed rules listed
            continue

        if item has an owner still inside candidates
           AND (handover is high OR reassignment_count ≥ 2 OR owner.skill ≥ 2)
           AND not (item.class = critical AND owner.skill < 2):
            chosen ← owner
            code ← OWNER_CONTINUITY
        else:
            chosen, code ← argmax score(item, r) over candidates
            apply close-call margin if the top two scores are within 2 points

        commit(item, chosen, code, runner_up)
        new_load[chosen] += size(item)
        if item.class in {critical, elevated}:
            elevated_or_critical[chosen] += size(item)
        if item.activity in {Security Review, Final Approval}:
            scarce[item.activity, chosen] += size(item)

    # Training repair. Never breaks a hard rule.
    if training_share(normal and low cases) < 0.20:
        try one SLA-safe swap onto a matching training priority
        if still short:
            log STRUCTURAL_TRAINING_GAP with the blocked people and the rule that blocked them

    # Attach shadows only after loads are known.
    for case where a skill-0 person has this training priority
        and the performer is skill 3
        and the shadow's new_load ≤ 3
        and the shadow holds fewer than 2 elevated/critical cases
        and the shadow can take a ≤ 45 minute review:
            set shadow_resource, code TRAINING_SHADOW
            # org:resource stays the performer

    return assignments
```

```text
function eligible(case, r):
    skill ← skills[r, case.required_skill]
    if skill = 0: return false
    if new_load[r] ≥ 4: return false
    if r.hours_remaining < hours_floor(case.class): return false
    if r.overtime_risk = high:
        if not (case.priority = urgent and no other legal candidate exists):
            return false

    if case.activity = Security Review:
        if skill < 3 and not supervised_by(r, {R7_Gita, R8_Hugo}): return false
        if case.class = critical and skill < 3: return false
    if case.activity = Final Approval and skill < 2:
        return false                              # preparers are not candidates for ownership
    if case.priority in {urgent, high} and skill < 2:
        if not named_supervisor(skill ≥ 2): return false

    if r = case.owner and skill ≤ 1:
        weak_penalty ← 3.3h
        if supervision impossible: return false
        if case.sla_remaining ≤ weak_penalty: return false
        if case.activity = Security Review and case.class = critical: return false

    return true
```

```text
function score(case, r):          # only legal candidates; higher is better
    points ← 0
    reasons ← []

    # Skill match (0–3). On a true training-ownership case, skill 1 is the intended match.
    points += skill
    if training_ownership_ok(case, r):
        points += 3
        reasons += TRAINING_OWNERSHIP

    # Continuity / handover.
    if r = case.owner:
        points += {high: 4, medium: 2, low: 1}[case.handover] if skill ≥ 2 else 1
        reasons += OWNER_CONTINUITY
    else:
        if case.reassignment_count ≥ 2: points -= 3
        if case.high_cx and case.handover = high: points -= 3
        else if case.handover = high: points -= 2
        else if case.handover = medium: points -= 1

    # Fairness. High overtime is already gone.
    if r.overtime_risk = low: points += 2
    if r.open_cases ≤ 6: points += 2
    else if r.open_cases ≤ 9: points += 1
    else if r.open_cases ≥ 12: points -= 2
    if r.hours_remaining ≥ 6: points += 1
    else if r.hours_remaining < 4: points -= 1
    if new_load[r] = 0: points += 1
    else if new_load[r] = 2: points -= 1
    else if new_load[r] ≥ 3: points -= 3
    if elevated_or_critical[r] ≥ 1 and case.activity family differs:
        points -= 2                        # protect focus; related work does not take this hit

    # Resilience on scarce activities.
    if case.activity in {Security Review, Final Approval}:
        if skill = 3 and case.class = critical: points += 1
        projected ← scarce[case.activity, r] + 1
        other_legal_exists ← true if some other legal person remains
        if projected ≥ 2 and other_legal_exists and case.class ≠ critical:
            points -= 2
        # Do not "save the backup" by loading a person who already has two tight cases.
        if other person is the resilience alternate
           and elevated_or_critical[alternate] ≥ 2:
            do not apply the diversion; keep the stronger available skill

    # Quality batch.
    if case is in a legal quality bundle and r already holds another member:
        points += 2
        reasons += BATCH_QUALITY

    # Same activity family today, on standard or flexible work.
    if same_family(r, case) and case.class in {standard, flexible}:
        points += 1

    return points, reasons
```

```text
function training_ownership_ok(case, r):
    return case.class in {standard, flexible}
       and r.training_priority = case.required_skill
       and skills[r, case.required_skill] = 1
       and a supervisor with skill ≥ 2 (prefer 3) has a 20-minute checkpoint available
       and case.complexity < 0.60
       and case.sla_remaining > 12h
       and case.reassignment_count < 2
       and case.handover ≠ high
       and case.priority ≠ high and case.priority ≠ urgent

function close_call(top, second):
    if abs(top.points - second.points) > 2: return top
    prefer, in order:
        1. the person who does not already hold two elevated or critical cases
        2. the current owner
        3. the person who would stay inside the same activity family
        4. more hours remaining
        5. stable resource id
```

Tie-break when points are equal after the close-call rule: fewer new assignments today, then lower overtime risk, then higher skill, then current owner, then more hours remaining, then resource id.

Exception and release handling:

- **Relaxation order when nobody is legal:** first confirm the case is urgent; then allow one high-overtime resource if they are skill-legal. Never relax skill 0, Security sign-off, Approval finalize level, the batch cap of 4, or the high-priority skill floor.
- **Hot backup:** if the chosen person on a critical case has not started within 45 minutes, the named backup takes it. For Security, the other skill-3 person may preempt their own less-urgent Security case. The preempted case keeps its owner and its place in that person’s day.
- **Release valves, named at decision time:** a standard case may move to its named alternate if it has not started by the stated time and the SLA still has room. Flexible cases may be deferred to the next day by the owner while remaining SLA stays above 24 hours. A shadow is cancelled rather than allowed to displace accountable work; cancellation is logged and lowers that day’s training share on purpose.
- **Forced reassignment:** if the owner is removed by the overtime rule or the unsafe-continuity rule, write `FORCED_REASSIGN_OT` or `SLA_SPECIALIST`, require a structured handover, and flag the case when `reassignment_count` will exceed 2.
- **Dispatcher park:** empty candidate lists, conflicting sign-off, or a worker flag that the handover band is wrong. Parking is an outcome, not a failure of the worker.
- **Training gap:** if documented training priorities sit at skill 0, or only on high-overtime people, do not invent an undocumented trainee and do not break the overtime rule to hit 20%. Log the gap. Development for skill 0 happens as shadowing or off-queue coaching until the skill matrix is raised to 1 through a separate assessment.

Governance: reason codes, release valves, and the numbers 2.7 hours, 3.3 hours, complexity 0.70, and the 20% share are readable parameters. A weekly review may propose changes. The live batch uses the current parameters only.

# 4. Comparison with baseline policies

Illustrative encodings, so the comparison can be audited:

- **First-available:** among people with skill above 0 and enough hours, pick the fewest open cases; break remaining ties by more hours remaining. Specialization, handover, overtime labels, training, and the cap of 4 are ignored.
- **Specialist-only:** pick the highest skill for the required activity; break ties by fewest open cases, then more hours remaining. In this snapshot that encoding assigns C004 and C013 to Amal; C007, C016, C003, and C009 to Celine; C002, C011, C005, and C010 to Farid; C001, C008, C014, and C015 to Hugo; C006 and C012 to Jonas. Eva, Gita, Bruno, David, and Iris receive nothing new. A different secondary tie-break on C015 could hand that one case to Gita; the pile-up on Hugo remains either way.
- **Proposed policy:** the assignment in section 3.

These baselines are coherent policies with real advantages. The proposed policy does not win every column. It refuses some short-term speed, and it refuses some specialist purity, in order to stay inside the hard rules and to keep tomorrow staffable.

Owners are kept only when they already have the fewest cases | Kept only when the owner is also the highest skill and wins the load tie. That happens to protect C002, C007, C004, and C012. Ten cases keep their owner (C002, C004, C005, C007, C008, C009, C011, C012, C015, C016). | Resilience to absence or bottlenecks | Poor. Security skill 3 can sit idle while skill 1 holds the Security queue. Approval can be signed by someone who is not allowed to finalize. Diagnosis is split between Eva (hard, continuous) and Celine (loop), with a consult path. Jonas can release Celine from C014. The remaining fragility is Farid’s dual urgent load and Celine’s technical cluster. Both are visible and capped |

First-available is the speed policy, and it spends compliance and rework to get that speed. Specialist-only is the error policy, and it spends queues, continuity, and idle peers to get that error rate. The proposed policy is the auditable middle: it will look slower than first-available on the first hour, and it will look less pure than specialist-only on C016 and on C003’s reassignment count. Those are accepted costs.

# 5. Explanation to workers

This is the note that can go out with today’s allocations.

We assigned the queue as a team, with the same rules for everyone. The aim is to meet today’s deadlines, keep difficult cases with people who already know them, avoid handing anyone a fifth new case, and still leave room for people to learn. Speed matters. It is not the only thing that matters.

You can see a short reason on each case. If the reason says the customer is waiting, or the deadline is inside a few hours, that case comes first. If it says we kept the current owner, it is because changing owner on that case has been expensive: another handover, more waiting, and a higher chance the case comes back. If a case moved, the note says why. Usually it is because the current owner is already at high overtime risk and we are not adding to that load, or because the case needs a Security or Approval sign-off the current owner is not in a position to give.

High overtime risk today is Bruno, David, and Iris. No new case went to them. That is about the load they already carry. It is not a judgment of their work. The cases they already own stay with them. David’s diagnosis loop (C003) and rework (C014), Bruno’s approval (C010), and Iris’s quality check (C006) did move, because those items were still waiting to be assigned and there was a legal colleague with room. C003 has already changed hands twice, so moving it again is not something we do lightly. Celine gets a proper handover from David, and the case is flagged so we can see if it bounces back.

Security and final approval have sign-off rules. Security is completed by Gita or Hugo, who are the specialists. Approval is completed by Farid today. A colleague can sit with them to learn. Learning does not transfer the signature. On C015, Hugo completes the review. Jonas is invited to a short shadow because security is his development focus and the case is not on fire. If Jonas’s quality work needs that time, the shadow is cancelled. Cancelling it is a normal outcome. Eva’s development focus is also security; she is not asked to shadow today because she already has a customer waiting and a hard diagnosis.

Amal keeps the eligibility work he is already strong in, and he keeps C009 as a real diagnosis assignment because that is the skill he is building. Celine will take one short checkpoint with him. Amal remains the owner. If the checkpoint shows the case needs a specialist, we will say so and change it openly. C013 is small and can move to tomorrow if the diagnosis work needs the day. That deferral is his to use.

Celine’s new work is the technical cluster: the rework she already owns, the diagnosis that had to leave David, and a second rework. If the first two are not moving by midday, the second rework goes to Jonas. That release is already agreed. It is not a rescue and it is not a criticism.

Farid keeps the urgent approval and the VIP negotiation he already owns, and he has a calmer approval behind them. If the two urgent cases take the day, the calmer approval moves to Eva. Eva keeps the negotiation where the customer is waiting and the diagnosis she already knows. We did not take the hard diagnosis away for a small skill difference, because the handover would spend hours the deadline does not have. She can call Celine in for a consult without giving the case up.

Jonas has the quality pair, which can be done as a batch, and the optional shadow. He was also a fair home for some of the small leftover cases. We left those elsewhere so his day stays understandable. Gita keeps the Security case her team already holds, and she is the person we call if Hugo cannot start the urgent Security case quickly. If that happens, her own Security case waits, because three hours is tighter than nine. She will know that before it happens.

Nobody is ranked by this allocation. A question about a reason, a handover note that does not match the case, or a release valve you think we should pull now is part of running the policy. Raise it with the dispatcher. We would rather park a case for a few minutes than quiet a real objection.

# 6. Process-mining audit indicators

Audit the policy from the event log. Do not audit it from memory of the meeting. Write the decision back into the log at assignment time: `policy_recommended_resource`, `policy_accountable_resource`, `supervisor_resource`, `shadow_resource`, `assignment_reason`, `urgency_class`, `release_valve_resource`, `policy_version`. The completing event’s `org:resource` is the accountable performer. A shadow or a supervisor must not overwrite that field, or handover and role discovery will describe a process we did not run.

Review operational indicators each day, and pattern indicators each week. Retune the 2.7h, 3.3h, 0.70, and 20% parameters only through the weekly review, using the cohorts below.

| Indicator | Definition | What to watch | PM4Py / log basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Handover frequency | Directed handovers per completed case, and the share of handovers whose `handover_cost_band` was high | A rise versus the pre-policy baseline, especially on complexity ≥ 0.70. Separation by `assignment_reason` so continuity-keeping days look different from forced-reassignment days | `discover_handover_of_work_network` on the full log and on the post-policy slice. Compare edge weight from the previous `current_owner` to `org:resource` |
| Reassignment count | Distribution of `reassignment_count` at completion. Share of cases that cross above 2 after this policy’s decision | C003’s cohort: cases assigned with `FORCED_REASSIGN_OT` and count crossing 2. The policy is off if that cohort’s rework rate is worse than the 1.8× history and we keep forcing the move anyway | Case attribute, grouped by `assignment_reason`. Variants that reach Rework Correction after the crossing |
| Skill mismatch | For each completed activity, accountable resource’s skill versus `required_skill`. Mismatch = skill 0, or skill 1 on urgent/high without `supervisor_resource`, or Security completed below the sign-off rule, or Approval completed below skill 2 | Any non-zero count on the hard rules is a compliance deviation, not a soft miss. Also track competent-but-not-specialist share (skill 2 on complexity ≥ 0.70), which the policy allows on purpose for C016-like cases | Join the skill matrix to `org:resource` and `required_skill`. `filter_event_attribute_values` on activity and on priority |
| Workload distribution | New assignments per resource per batch, and open cases from `workload_snapshot` plus new assignments. Spread across the team, not a single average | Any resource above 4 new assignments. Repeated days where the same two people absorb all flexible work. High-overtime resources gaining new work while a safer person existed | Counts by `org:resource` and `team`. `discover_batches` to see whether load is batch structure or one-by-one dumping |
| Overtime risk exposure | Share of new assignments whose resource had overtime risk `high` at decision time, split by urgency class | Non-urgent assignments to high overtime risk. Urgent ones should exist only with reason `SLA_SPECIALIST` or the urgent relaxation, and only when the log shows no safer legal candidate | `workload_snapshot` and a resource-day overtime attribute stored at decision time. Cohort by `case_priority` |
| SLA misses | Completion timestamp after `sla_deadline`. Also first-touch lateness: start timestamp minus assignment timestamp, against the 45-minute hot-backup rule and the class | Misses on critical cases. Misses where first touch blew the handover budget on C001-like moves. Misses on cases whose release valve was available and not used | `filter_case_performance` for long cases. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` and `get_service_time` to separate waiting from service. Compare both to the 2.7h and 3.3h assumptions |
| Rework and appeal after assignment | Share of cases that later show Rework Correction or an appeal activity, by assignment reason, by whether the owner changed, and by reassignment band (0–1, 2, ≥3) | The ≥3 band and the `FORCED_REASSIGN_OT` band. Also weak-skill keeps: skill 1 performer, no training reason, followed by rework inside the same case | `get_variants` and a performance DFG filtered to paths that enter Rework Correction. Cohort on `reassignment_count` and `assignment_reason` |

A day that misses 20% with a `STRUCTURAL_TRAINING_GAP` is a pass if the blocked rule was real | `filter_event_attribute_values` on `assignment_reason` and `case_priority`. Numerator and denominator stored, not just the percentage |
| Specialist bottleneck indicators | Share of Security completions by each skill-3 resource; same for Approval. Queue time and service time in those activities. Critical cases waiting behind standard work on the same specialist. The other specialist idle | `discover_performance_dfg` filtered to Security Review and Final Approval. `discover_working_together_network` to see whether backups actually pair. `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` to see whether the skill matrix still matches who really does the work |
| Deviations from assignment policy | Actual accountable `org:resource` differs from `policy_recommended_resource`, or a hard rule is violated, or a release valve fires with no log line, or reason code is missing | Silent overrides. Repeated overrides of the same rule, which means the rule is wrong or the queue data are wrong. Worker flags on handover cost are deviations of the *input*, and they are welcome | Direct attribute comparison. `filter_event_attribute_values` for missing `assignment_reason`. Role discovery versus the official teams, to catch shadowing that was logged as performance |

Supporting views, not extra targets: `discover_organizational_roles` against the official teams (A, B, Risk, Quality); `view_sna` / `save_vis_sna` on the handover network and the working-together network for the weekly review; `discover_batches` checked against the Quality gate (priority normal/low and SLA above 24h). If a batch contains an urgent or high Quality Check, that is a deviation.

Two interpretation rules keep the audit from becoming a league table. First, indicators are read as policy health. Individual drill-down is for a compliance breach, a coaching request, or a case the worker has already flagged. Second, a bad week on one indicator is a trade-off signal. More continuity can raise specialist queue time. More training can raise service time on standard cases. The review asks which hard rule, if any, moved, and only then whether a soft weight should change.

# 7. PM4Py implementation sketch

The log is the source for retuning and for the audit. The skill matrix, overtime band, and training priority are master data joined at decision time and stored on the event, so later mining does not depend on a spreadsheet that has since changed.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# --- prepare the log ---
df = pd.read_csv("event_log.csv")
df["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"], utc=True)
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)

# Master data captured on the assignment event so audits stay reproducible.
# Expected columns already in the log plus policy write-back:
#   org:resource, required_skill, case_priority, sla_deadline, case_complexity,
#   current_owner, team, assignment_reason, reassignment_count,
#   workload_snapshot, handover_cost_band,
#   policy_recommended_resource, supervisor_resource, shadow_resource,
#   resource_skill_at_decision, overtime_risk_at_decision, training_priority

skills = pd.read_csv("skill_matrix.csv")  # resource, activity, skill, overtime_risk, training_priority

# --- service, waiting, SLA ---
service = pm4py.get_service_time(df)  # per activity / resource, used to re-estimate the 3.3h weak-skill effect
df_wait = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df)

def sla_miss_table(frame):
    done = (
        frame.sort_values("time:timestamp")
        .groupby("case:concept:name")
        .tail(1)
    )
    done = done.copy()
    done["sla_miss"] = done["time:timestamp"] > pd.to_datetime(done["sla_deadline"], utc=True)
    return done[["case:concept:name", "sla_miss", "case_priority", "reassignment_count", "assignment_reason"]]

# Long-running cases for the weekly pack. Threshold is a review parameter, in seconds.
long_cases = pm4py.filter_case_performance(df, min_performance=24 * 3600)

# --- scarce activities and sign-off compliance ---
security = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df, "concept:name", ["Security Review"], level="event", retain=True
)
approval = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df, "concept:name", ["Final Approval"], level="event", retain=True
)
high = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df, "case_priority", ["urgent", "high"], level="case", retain=True
)

def signoff_breaches(events, min_skill):
    merged = events.merge(
        skills,
        left_on=["org:resource", "concept:name"],
        right_on=["resource", "activity"],
        how="left",
    )
    # Shadow must not appear as org:resource. supervisor_resource is an attribute only.
    illegal = merged[merged["resource_skill_at_decision"].fillna(0) < min_skill]
    return illegal

security_breaches = signoff_breaches(security, min_skill=3)
# skill 1–2 is still a breach unless supervisor_resource is Gita or Hugo;
# apply that exception before counting security_breaches as compliance failures
approval_breaches = signoff_breaches(approval, min_skill=2)

# --- rework / appeal cohort, to refresh the 1.8x and 1.4x evidence ---
variants = pm4py.get_variants(df)

def ends_with_rework(variant_tuple):
    return "Rework Correction" in variant_tuple

rework_cases = {vid for vid, v in variants.items() if ends_with_rework(v)}

cohort = (
    df.groupby("case:concept:name")
    .agg(
        reassignment_count=("reassignment_count", "max"),
        owner_changed=("assignment_reason", lambda s: "FORCED_REASSIGN_OT" in set(s.dropna())),
        priority=("case_priority", "last"),
    )
    .reset_index()
)
# Join rework_cases, group by reassignment band (0-1, 2, >=3) and by owner_changed.

# --- resource networks: did the policy do what the SNA shows? ---
handover_net = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(df)
together_net = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(df)
similarity = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(df)
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(df)

# Visuals for the weekly review. Working-together should show Amal with Celine
# on training days, and Hugo with Gita as Security backups — not Jonas as a
# Security performer.
pm4py.view_sna(handover_net)
# pm4py.save_vis_sna(handover_net, "audit_handover.png")
# pm4py.save_vis_sna(together_net, "audit_working_together.png")

# If similarity says Eva and Farid no longer cluster on negotiation, or Hugo and
# Gita no longer cluster on Security, the skill matrix is stale. Update it
# through the same review that retunes thresholds. Do not silently let the
# similarity score override sign-off rules.

# --- bottlenecks ---
perf_dfg = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(df)
# Inspect median waiting and service on Security Review and Final Approval.
# A specialist bottleneck is: one resource, most of the completions, rising wait
# on the arc into that activity. Cross-check with scarce-activity counts below.

def concentration(events):
    counts = events.groupby("org:resource").size()
    return (counts / counts.sum()).sort_values(ascending=False)

# --- batches vs the Quality gate ---
batches = pm4py.discover_batches(df)
quality = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df, "concept:name", ["Quality Check"], level="event", retain=True
)
# Flag a discovered batch whose member cases include priority urgent/high
# or sla_deadline - batch_start <= 24h. Those batches violate the gate
# even if discover_batches groups them.

# --- deviations ---
assigned = df[df["assignment_reason"].notna()].copy()
deviations = assigned[
    assigned["org:resource"] != assigned["policy_recommended_resource"]
]
missing_reason = df[df["concept:name"].isin([
    "Eligibility Review", "Technical Diagnosis", "Customer Negotiation",
    "Security Review", "Final Approval", "Quality Check", "Rework Correction",
])]
missing_reason = missing_reason[missing_reason["assignment_reason"].isna()]

# Training share, two definitions, normal/low only.
std = df[df["case_priority"].isin(["normal", "low"])]
owners = std[std["assignment_reason"] == "TRAINING_OWNERSHIP"]["case:concept:name"].nunique()
shadows = std[std["assignment_reason"] == "TRAINING_SHADOW"]["case:concept:name"].nunique()
denom = std["case:concept:name"].nunique()
performer_share = owners / denom if denom else None
support_share = (owners + shadows) / denom if denom else None

# Weak-skill penalty refresh:
# median service time where resource_skill_at_decision == 1 and reason is not
# TRAINING_OWNERSHIP, minus median service time where skill >= 2, same activity.
# Handover penalty refresh:
# median waiting time on cases with complexity >= 0.70 whose owner changed,
# minus median waiting time on the same activities where owner was kept.
# Bring both numbers to the weekly review before any weight changes.
```

What this sketch is for: it recomputes the evidence the policy already quotes, checks that today’s assignments still satisfy the hard rules, and shows whether continuity, training, and specialist load moved together. It does not assign the next batch by itself. Discovery of roles, similarity, and social networks informs the skill matrix. The sign-off rules stay in the filter in section 2 until a person changes them. If the mined handover penalty or weak-skill penalty moves materially away from 2.7 and 3.3 hours, the thresholds are out of date, and the honest response is to retune them in review rather than to keep defending today’s numbers.
