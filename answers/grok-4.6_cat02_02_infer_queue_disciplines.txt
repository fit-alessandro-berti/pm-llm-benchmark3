# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

## QA — primary: non-preemptive FIFO + when-free appointment override

**Supports.** A01→A02→A04→A05→A06→A07→A08 is arrival order except A03. A03 is the only job whose appointment coincides with a free instant, and it is the only overtake. P1 jobs A04 and A08 wait behind older P3/P2 work (A02, then A05–A07). No semicolon appears, so preemptive priority is not used. SPT is contradicted by A02 (20) before A04 (7) and A05 (15) before A07 (9).

**Challenges.** A06’s 09:15 appointment starts at 09:20. This does **not** require an undocumented exception if appointments are an override only when the server is free *at* the appointment time: at 09:05 A06 is 10 minutes early, A05 is the oldest fully eligible job, and the queue is non-preemptive, so A05’s 15-minute estimate overruns by construction. It *would* be an exception if QA reserved capacity or refused jobs that overlap an appointment (that is the QD/D-X behavior, not QA).

The 08:32 choice of A02 over P1 A04 looks like a priority violation; under FIFO it is the predicted act. Calibration packing is an alternative sufficient explanation, not a needed exception.

## QB — primary: P1-preemption + FIFO + resume

**Supports.** B02 is the only automatic overtake and is labeled “urgent arrival.” After B02, B01 is resumed rather than B03 (P2), which matches resume-plus-FIFO and contradicts ordinary preemptive priority. After B05, B01 is resumed again rather than B06 (P2). Once B01 is finished, B04→B06→B07 is arrival order. B07’s appointment is ignored, so this is not an appointment queue. SPT is contradicted repeatedly (B04 est 8 and B07 est 6 sit behind the long B01).

**Challenges.** At 09:48 the observed start is B03, not waiting P1 B05, and not resume of B01. The log itself names this “dispatcher reassignment,” i.e. an operational exception, not the baseline rule. If B05’s 09:46 arrival was still unscanned, the visible set is {B01, B03, B04} and a dispatcher moving off the long P3 job onto the oldest higher-priority waiting job (B03) is coherent. They then run B03 to completion: P1 B05 does **not** preempt a just-started P2. So P1 preemption is not unconditional; it is evidenced only against an in-progress P3. At 10:00, choosing B05 over resume/FIFO is a second P1 privilege (selection at a free instant), which is consistent with the hybrid but is a contradiction for pure FIFO.

## QC — primary: non-preemptive SPT, with later hybrid patches

**Supports.** Opening decision set at 10:10 is decisive: FIFO predicts C01, priority and EDD predict C03, SPT predicts C02 — observed C02. At 10:18 SPT predicts C03 and that is observed, while priority/EDD predict C05. Those two episodes are the cleanest identification in QC.

**Challenges.** Three later choices need extra machinery, not just SPT:

- **C04 before C06 (10:32).** C06 has been present since 10:25 (well beyond a 2-minute scan delay) and is the shortest job. This is a true SPT miss unless the next job was bound earlier (assumption: C04 was assigned when C03 started at 10:18, before C06 existed). That is a data-generating exception, not a different ranking rule.
- **C05 before C06 (10:54).** Predicted by priority and by overdue-deadline elevation, not by SPT. C05’s deadline was already unattainable at arrival, so “start when the SLA becomes endangered” is only loosely timed. This is best read as a policy add-on, not as EDD (EDD would have taken C05 at 10:18).
- **C01 before C07 (11:40).** FIFO/aging of the leftover initial job; contradicts SPT, priority, and EDD. Starting C07 first would still have met both deadlines (C07 by 11:58, C01 by 12:34). This is the hardest QC contradiction and is **not** explained by known resource constraints.

No preemption of C02–C04 occurs while P1 C05 waits, so this is not preemptive priority.

## QD — primary: hard skill routing + FIFO inside the eligible set + appointment hold

**Supports.** Parallel starts at 11:00/11:01 are skill-separated (mechanical vs firmware), not a single FIFO line. D05 is withheld from D-X and D-M and executed only by D-F after 12:20, matching the supplied note. D07 is withheld from D-M (no electrical) and D-F (no mechanical) and executed by D-X. D-X’s 10-minute idle and exact 11:30 start of D06 is an appointment hold: even D08 (P1, est 8) would have finished by 11:28 and was *not* inserted. D-M’s own sequence D01→D04→D08→D09 is FIFO among mechanical-only jobs and is non-preemptive (D08 arrives during D04).

**Challenges.** D-F idle 11:36–12:00 with D10 waiting is **not** explained by the recorded meeting (meeting begins 12:00; D10 fits). This requires an undocumented exception: early departure / meeting prep, unrecorded unavailability, or a reservation heuristic that refuses to start anyone but D05 even when D05 does not fit. Scarce-skill reservation is itself contradicted at 11:21, when D-F takes D03 (which D-X could have done) instead of exclusive D05. D-M taking D04 over D08 contradicts priority-within-skill; it supports FIFO-within-skill and is therefore not an exception to the primary diagnosis.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

## Episode A — QA at 09:05 (A05 started)

Competing explanations still standing after 08:20: (i) FIFO with appointments eligible only at/after the stated time; (ii) a stronger appointment scheduler that protects the 09:15 slot.

| rule | predicted start at 09:05 |
|---|---|
| (i) FIFO, A06 not eligible before 09:15 | A05 (then A06 cannot start before 09:20) |
| (ii) reserve capacity for 09:15 | idle until 09:15, or start a job with est ≤10 (A07, est 9) so the slot survives |

Observed: A05, which is exactly (i). Residual ambiguity is whether A06 *could* have been started early (A03 never started early). **One additional observable that would discriminate:** appointment tolerance / earliest-start field, or a reject/idle flag at 09:05 (“held for A06” vs “A06 not yet eligible”). If A07 had been started at 09:05 and finished ≈09:14, that would have supported (ii) even without a new field.

## Episode B — QC at 10:32 (C04 started while C06 waited)

Competing explanations: (i) live SPT on the full waiting set; (ii) SPT with next-job bound at the previous start (C04 assigned at 10:18, C06 not yet present).

| rule | predicted start at 10:32 |
|---|---|
| (i) live SPT | C06 (est 6) |
| (ii) frozen next job from 10:18 | C04 (shortest among {C01, C04, C05} at 10:18) |

Observed: C04, which matches (ii) and rejects live SPT. **One additional observable:** dispatch-decision timestamp and the eligibility set actually shown to the dispatcher at that instant (or a “job already assigned / locked” flag). If the decision time is 10:18 and C06 is absent from the set, (ii) is identified; if the decision time is 10:32 and C06 is listed, then QC is not SPT at all and the hybrid/SLA story must be widened.

(A third useful discriminator, if only one more field can be collected on QB instead: at 09:48, whether B05 was in the eligibility set. If yes, the primary QB rule cannot include “P1 always ranks first”; if no, the dispatcher reassignment is compatible with P1-preempt + FIFO.)

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked most to least plausible for *this* log.

1. **Documented dispatcher reassignment (QB 09:48).** Already in the event data. It is the cleanest way to keep a simple P1-preempt+FIFO+resume rule without claiming that P2 preempts P3 automatically. If this exception is denied and treated as ordinary policy, QB’s primary diagnosis collapses toward an undocumented multi-rule hybrid and confidence should drop into the 40s.

2. **Arrival-scan delay up to two minutes.** Explains 08:02, 09:02, 09:16, and possibly why B05 (09:46) is missing from a 09:48 decision. It does **not** explain C06 (present 7 minutes) or D-F’s 24-minute idle. Using it more broadly would falsely rescue live SPT on QC.

3. **Early binding / next-job assigned before the server is free.** Especially QC 10:18→10:32 (C04 locked before C06 arrives) and possibly QA’s 2-minute gaps. If this is the norm, several “SPT violations” are artefacts of decision time ≠ service-start time, and QC’s SPT confidence should rise; QB’s 09:48 story also becomes a decision made on a stale set.

4. **Unrecorded D-F unavailability after 11:36 (meeting prep or early leave).** Converts the worst QD contradiction into a resource-state problem rather than a ranking-rule problem. Diagnosis of QD as skill+FIFO+appointment then becomes *more* identifiable. If instead D-F was free and choosing, QD has an unexplained refusal of P1 D10 and confidence should fall.

5. **Policy switch when an SLA is already broken (QC C05 at 10:54).** Plausible given the note and the P1 field, but poorly timed (C05 was hopeless from 10:11). If accepted, QC is explicitly hybrid and should not be labeled “SPT” in documentation. If rejected, C05-before-C06 is an unexplained priority insert.

6. **Appointment windows rather than exact clock times.** Can absorb A06 starting at 09:20 (5 minutes late) without calling it a violation, but cannot absorb B07 at 10:56 versus 10:30 unless the window is huge. Treating windows as wide would overstate appointment influence on QA/QB.

7. **Noisy or strategic `est_min`.** Least plausible here: every job’s segments sum exactly to `est_min`. Invoking noisy estimates would be an overfitting excuse, not an explanation.

Absence of an interruption reason on QA/QC/QD does not prove work could not have been stopped; but no mid-job arrival of a P1 produces a split segment outside QB, which is positive evidence of non-preemption rather than missing reasons.

# 6. Additional event data needed

Priority is driven by the actual ambiguities above, not a generic wish list.

1. **Dispatch-decision time and the eligibility set at that time** (job ids, their then-visible arrival, priority, `est_min`, remaining estimate, appointment, deadline, skill). This single artefact separates live ranking from early binding and tells us whether B05 and C06 were on the screen.
2. **Queue-entry / scan-complete time** separate from business arrival, to bound the two-minute delay instead of using it as a free parameter.
3. **Resource state with reason** (idle, in-service, calibration, meeting, travel/prep, assigned-but-not-started), especially D-F 11:36–12:00 and A1 08:30–08:32 and 08:52–08:58.
4. **Appointment semantics:** earliest start, latest start, whether the slot reserves a named resource, and whether a job may start early (A03 vs A06 vs D06 vs B07 disagree).
5. **Preemption reason coded as a closed set** (urgent P1, dispatcher override, skill reassignment, estimate revision, SLA break) plus who initiated it. QB has free text for two stops only.
6. **Remaining estimate at each decision**, not only arrival `est_min` (needed if resume vs SPT-on-remaining is ever in play; B01’s remaining is currently imputed).
7. **Assignment lock / promised resource** (D05→D-F, D07→D-X, D06→D-X at 11:30, C04 locked at 10:18). Distinguishes skill constraints from human reservation.
8. **Policy version or rule in force**, and whether a “SLA endangered” switch exists, with the threshold (QC C05 vs ignored C07).
9. **Offered-but-declined jobs** (especially D-F declining D10; A1 declining to idle for A06). Refusal is currently indistinguishable from “not in the choice set.”

Items 1–3 would resolve most of the residual uncertainty; 4–8 are needed only after those are in place.

# 7. Final synthesis

QA is the most identifiable queue: one decision set (08:20) isolates an appointment override, and every later contrast set rejects priority, SPT, preemption, and slot reservation. QC is the least identifiable: the opening backlog is a clean SPT fingerprint, but C04/C06, C05/C06, and C01/C07 cannot be jointly explained without stacking early binding, P1/SLA elevation, and aging, and those add-ons are under-identified. The most dangerous claim to infer from these observations alone is that the center runs **priority scheduling** (preemptive or not). Priority is populated on every job and is tempting to read into any P1 that is not last, yet decision-set reconstruction shows P1 losing on QA (A04, A08), on QC until after several SPT choices (C05), and inside QD skill groups (D08 behind D04, D10 behind D07). Acting as if priorities dispatch work would mis-predict waiting times and would hide the mechanisms that actually move the needle: FIFO plus a when-free appointment on QA, P1 interrupt plus resume on QB, noisy hybrid SPT on QC, and hard skills plus one reserved appointment on QD.
