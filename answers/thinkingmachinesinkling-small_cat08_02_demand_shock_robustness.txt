- **Dynamic batching with protected lanes (replaces I1/I2)**: Do not use strict Urgent-first (I1 starves Routine/Complex and increases WIP) or batch size 24 (I2 delays Urgent unless exceptions are perfect). Instead, schedule Routine in fixed batches of 12–16 with protected start windows; allow Urgent bypass only up to 30% of assay slot capacity; reserve Complex-panel windows with pre-booked medical validator time. This protects contract penalties (18 h) without fragmenting schedules. Robust because it balances throughput and latency. Assumption: schedule discipline enforced by system, not just staff; failure: if Urgent share exceeds 30%, bypass fails.

- **Modified I3 — Failure-aware machine routing (not strict dedication)**: M1 primary for Urgent/Complex; M2 for Routine. Monitor M2 failure rate continuously; if failure > baseline +20%, reroute routine to M1 at reduced volume or delay to next window. Avoids overloading M2 when it is degraded. Robust because it adapts. Assumption: real-time failure detection; failure: if M1 is also stressed, rerouting overloads it.

- **Modified I4 — Flex cross-training pool**: Cross-train two technical validators for accession correction, but only activate when correction queue > 10 h and validation queue < 8 h. This addresses correction overload (91% peak) without draining validation (101% peak). Robust because it uses slack when it exists. Assumption: training completed; failure: during 4-week training, both pools are weak.

- **Triggered I5 — Evening medical-validation shift**: Activate only when specialist review > 20 h or medical validator queue > 12 h, not continuously. Adds capacity exactly when bottleneck migrates to the end. Robust because it targets the migration point. Assumption: staff available on short notice; failure: if activated too late, the 36 h specialist queue takes too long to recover.

Why this is robust: The package attacks variability (I7), quality/rework (I6), intake speed (I8) with guardrails, and protects all case types with time-sliced scheduling rather than absolute priority. It does not optimize historical average; it builds slack at bottleneck migration points.

Assumptions: Digital metadata completeness is high; courier contracts are flexible; assay failure is detectable; specialist absence is predictable (every second Friday).

Failure conditions: Combined S1+S2+S3 (high mix + machine degradation + specialist absence) may overwhelm sequential policies because Assay and Validation block simultaneously; no redesign eliminates the need for physical capacity at extremes.

4. Scenario-specific policies

Only activate when triggers are met; switch off when conditions normalize for > 4 hours.

| Policy | Trigger condition | Actions | Switch-off |
|--------|-------------------|---------|------------|
| **Assay Surge & Repeat Containment** (S2 / high assay load) | Assay median queue > 15 h OR M2 failure rate > baseline + 20% OR utilization > 95% for > 2 h | Halt routine batch accumulation; switch to continuous flow for Urgent; restrict Routine to pre-booked assay slots; fast-track Repeat assay (clear double-consumption within 4 h); do NOT increase batch size | Queue < 8 h and failure rate normal for 4 h |
| **Medical & Specialist Reserve** (S3 / absence) | Medical validator queue > 18 h OR specialist review > 30 h OR validator util > 95% OR specialist unavailable | Activate evening shift (I5); reduce non-critical hold criteria; for Complex only (not Urgent, due to 18 h penalty), activate partner lab overflow (I10) with 12 h transport; notify physicians to manage expectations | Queue < 12 h and specialist available; partner shipments paused |
| **Courier & Intake Compression** (S4 / logistics) | Courier median queue > 6 h OR arrival spike > 2× average in 1 h OR registration queue > 2 h | Open temporary intake station; prioritize Urgent/Complex for pre-analytical check; delay Routine batch prep by one slot; use I8 for complete orders; initiate I6 triage at intake | Courier queue < 3 h and arrival rate normal for 2 h |
| **Case-Mix Shift Response** (S1 / high urgency/complex) | Urgent % > 25% OR Complex % > 18% OR combined > 40% | Enforce 30% Urgent assay slot cap; reserve validator blocks for Complex; reduce Routine release batch reporting; activate flex pool (I4) if correction queue grows | Mix returns to normal (< 20% Urgent, < 15% Complex) for 6 h |

Important: I1 (strict Urgent-first) is not used as a permanent policy; it is explicitly avoided because it starves Routine/Complex and increases total WIP. I2 (batch 24) is only permitted during low-urgency windows, never during S1.

6. Residual vulnerabilities

- **Specialist single-point failure**: Even with I5 and I10, one specialist unavailable every second Friday is hard to cover fully. The partner lab adds 12 h, which is acceptable for Complex (64 h median) but risky if the absence coincides with peak mix.
- **M2 degradation dynamics**: The redesigned routing relies on real-time failure detection. If failure is sudden (not gradual), routine cases already in M2 may generate a repeat burst (Repeat assay  Assay 1,080 cases) before rerouting activates.
- **Cross-training transition gap**: The 4-week training window for I4 means both technical validation and accession correction pools are thinner during transition; if a disruption occurs then, the flex pool cannot respond.
- **Redesign-induced vulnerability — “Good-flow acceleration”**: By reducing recollection loops (I6) and automating registration (I8), more clean, high-quality orders move quickly into Assay and Technical validation. Historical logs show Technical validation already hits 101% in peak; faster good flow can cause a **sharper bottleneck migration to Medical validation / Specialist review** than historical averages suggest. The process may appear healthier at intake/pre-analytical but collapse abruptly at release because downstream capacity was never resized—only protected by scheduling.
- **Combined multi-bottleneck scenario**: If S1 (mix shift) + S2 (M2 failure) + S3 (specialist absence) occur together, sequential policies cannot fully parallelize. Assay and Validation block simultaneously; the redesign has no “magic” capacity, only migration management.
- **Courier external dependency**: I7 reduces variance but cannot eliminate external transport disruptions; a winter weather event can still cause compression that overwhelms even automated registration.

7. Minimum monitoring dashboard

Distinguish **leading indicators** (actionable before cycles complete) from **lagging outcome metrics** (confirm impact after release).

**Leading indicators — monitored hourly / daily during peaks**

| Indicator | Normal / Green | Yellow warning | Red / Trigger | Scenario / Policy link |
|-----------|----------------|----------------|---------------|------------------------|
| Case mix (% Urgent + Complex) | < 35% | 35–40% | > 40% | S1 / Case-Mix Shift Response |
| Courier arrival rate vs forecast | ±10% | +10–30% spike | > 2× in 1 h or queue > 6 h | S4 / Courier Compression Policy |
| Pre-analytical defect / recollection rate (per 100) | Low | Rising | > baseline + 30% | I6 / Source-quality |
| Assay M1 / M2 utilization | < 85% | 85–95% | > 95% or M2 failure > +20% | S2 / Assay Surge Policy |
| Accession correction queue | < 8 h | 8–14 h | > 14 h (or 10 h + val < 8 h) | I4 / Flex pool trigger |
| Batch wait time (Routine) | < 8 h | 8–14 h | > 14 h or bypass < 58% | Dynamic batching policy |
| Technical validation queue | < 8 h | 8–16 h | > 16 h | S1/S3 migration warning |
| Medical validator queue / Specialist review | < 10 h / < 10 h | 10–20 h / 20–30 h | > 18 h / > 30 h or specialist unavailable | S3 / Medical & Specialist Reserve |
| Urgent bypass % at Assay | ~74% | 60–70% | < 58% (fragmentation risk) or > 30% slot cap broken | Dynamic scheduling discipline |

**Threshold bands (qualitative):**  
- **Green**: All queues < 4 h; mix < 20% Urgent; M2 failure normal; specialist present.  
- **Yellow**: Any queue 4–12 h; mix 25–30% Urgent; M2 failure +10–20%; specialist unavailable next 24 h; correction queue > 10 h. Activate flex pool / smoothing / monitoring.  
- **Red**: Any queue > 12 h (matches historical “% cases with at least one queue >12h” metric at 54%); mix > 30% Urgent; M2 failure high / down; specialist review > 30 h; validation > 16 h. Activate scenario-specific policies immediately.

**Lagging outcome metrics — reviewed daily / weekly**
- Median / p90 cycle time by case type (Routine 3862 h; Urgent 1117 h; Complex 64111 h; Repeat 70145 h)
- Overall rework / repeat rate (7.5%  11.8% in peak; target < 8% in redesign)
- % cases with at least one queue > 12 h (22%  54%; target < 30% under stress)
- Urgent > 18 h penalty count (contractual)
- Cancel / recollection rate and manual correction rate

The key design of this dashboard is to watch **where the bottleneck is migrating** (assay  validation  specialist) rather than only watching total cycle time, because historical averages hide the case-type-specific migration that drives failures during winter waves.
