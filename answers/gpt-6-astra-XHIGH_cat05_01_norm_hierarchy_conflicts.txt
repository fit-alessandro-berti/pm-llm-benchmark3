## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

The most defensible hierarchy is issue-specific: GPIP-4 governs reserved integrity controls; the Aachen SOP supplies additional local controls; ECD-2 can displace specified local workflow and supplier-register restrictions within its scope and window; and the CFO can interpret Finance controls without acquiring an unstated power to waive other board-policy requirements. Under the interpretation below, P-501 is a JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION; P-502, P-503, P-504, and P-506 are NONCOMPLIANT; and P-505, P-507, and P-508 are INDETERMINATE. These are assessments of the supplied record, not findings of actual loss, product unsafety, or individual misconduct.

**Facts versus interpretive choices.** The stated documents, dates, activities, outage evidence, and organizational facts are the factual record. The following are my interpretive choices, not additional organizational facts:

- **Authority is limited by subject matter.** A board policy is presumptively controlling on its explicit integrity requirements. A7 identifies the CRO as exception authority. The COO's operational authority and the practice of using incident overlays support operational exceptions, but do not establish an equivalent power to waive A1–A3. CRO unavailability does not transfer that power. No qualifying A7 exception is established here.
- **Specificity and later enactment operate within authority.** B survives the later global policy because A5 permits stricter local rules. C's express permissions, read with B7, displace B1 and B3 for qualifying purchases. C's later date and emphatic wording do not independently defeat A. D applies prospectively from 15 July at 07:20, while ECD-2 is active. I read its subject matter as ECD-2 purchases, rather than every site purchase made during the same dates.
- **An approval activity can satisfy more than one control, but its label is insufficient.** A joint Incident Command authorization could also embody business and Finance approvals if the signers actually possess the necessary authority, make the relevant decisions, and meet independence requirements. A2 does not demand a separately named ERP event. Nevertheless, an operational charter does not establish budget ownership or Finance Controller capacity. D supports combined processing; it does not, on this record, establish the appointments needed to satisfy A2. Whether the CFO could authorize a broader functional substitution is a substantial alternative interpretation.
- **I do not insert an unstated deadline into B2.** For EUR 25,000 to below EUR 50,000, B2 requires Plant and Finance approvals but does not expressly require either before commitment. Above that range, A2 independently imposes its explicit pre-commitment requirements. This textual choice materially affects P-501. A purposeful interpretation treating all B2 approvals as prior approval gates is credible and assessed separately.
- **B's maintenance scope includes repairing site equipment, including the cafeteria freezer.** Production support is an additional scope category. A narrower interpretation restricting B to production-related maintenance is possible and would affect P-504.
- **Emergency routes have different triggers.** B4 requires imminent safety harm or a production stoppage expected within six hours. C requires a demonstrated connection to containing this contamination event and an in-window commitment. Neither Operations' broad view nor Audit's 24-hour view is an enacted definition. The six-hour test is not automatically imported into C, and the continued existence of contamination does not extend C.
- **Recording obligations survive.** I retain B6 for local purchases using an emergency route, including C, because C does not expressly replace the emergency form. A single document can satisfy both B6 and C3 if it contains the required information and is accepted as equivalent operational evidence. Joint authorization alone is not automatically the emergency form. I measure C3's four hours from the authorization, the more protective natural reading of “initially be verbal but must be recorded within four hours.”

**Time and evidence.** Apply a control at the act it governs. Commitment means the actual act creating the supplier obligation, not necessarily the call's start, an ERP posting, or receipt of an acknowledgment. An acknowledgment can establish a latest possible commitment time if its contents confirm an already binding order. Later PO creation and documentation can remain permissible after a directive expires when the commitment was validly made during the window; that does not extend permission for new commitments.

ON-44 permits recovery postings on 14 July to be up to 12 minutes later than the action. It neither excuses controls nor establishes a general clock problem on other dates. P-505's claimed clock offset and P-507's document outage require their own evidence. Contemporaneous recordings, signed documents, acknowledgments, and access logs can establish earlier actions more reliably than ingestion timestamps. A later record of an earlier approval differs fundamentally from an approval first given later. A6 prevents automatic retrospective cure in the latter situation.

**Burden and classification.** A claimed exception needs positive support for authority, scope, timing, and its conditions. A demonstrated mandatory departure without an established exception supports NONCOMPLIANT. A plausible combined approval whose necessary capacities remain unresolved supports INDETERMINATE. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION means an otherwise applicable requirement was validly displaced; it is not a synonym for management accepting a breach afterward.

I accept positively described approvals and signed records as evidence of the stated acts, subject to identified contradictions. An omitted event is not invariably an omitted act: the supplied traces are selective. An expressly absent clearance with no identified equivalent, as in P-503, supports an apparent failure of the required clearance; an identified joint authorization that might embody Finance approval warrants investigating its signers' capacities. Missing routine records are disclosed rather than silently treated as either completed or breached. “Satisfied” below refers to the identified rule or element, not certification of the entire file. Confidence measures support for the stated finding; severity measures the control exposure, not proven harm.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

The following is a proposed prospective replacement clause, not a description of powers already established during July 2026:

> **Authority and scope.** GPIP-4 governs integrity controls; applicable local SOPs add compatible controls. Only the CRO, or an acting CRO designated in writing by the board, may issue a written, purchase- or lot-specific, time-limited A7 exception identifying each affected clause, rationale, safeguards, and accountable owner. A COO/Incident Commander directive may change only the local steps expressly listed. CFO interpretations must be recorded in the controlled repository and may define Finance approval methods; they do not themselves waive Compliance, segregation, or independent budget authority.
>
> **Emergency permissions.** An operational directive may permit commitment before PO creation and use of an unregistered supplier only for identified lots directly needed to contain or restore the affected sterilization operation. Backup-line lots qualify only with documented engineering necessity within the next 24 hours; longer-horizon contingencies require express inclusion by the CRO. Every use requires two distinct IC members to authorize before commitment. Supplier-register relief does not waive Compliance.
>
> **Approvals and thresholds.** At EUR 25,000 or more, Plant and Finance approval must precede commitment. At EUR 50,000 or more, an independent budget owner and Finance Controller must approve beforehand. Above EUR 100,000, or for any new supplier, Compliance clearance must precede commitment. Above EUR 150,000, Legal clearance is additionally required. For the incident route, a joint record may satisfy these approvals only when it identifies competent signers acting in each required capacity. Requesters and committers may provide information but may not participate as final business approvers. Any A7 departure from a stated control must name that control explicitly; silence supplies no waiver. “New supplier” means a supplier without valid prior Northbridge Compliance clearance, independently of register status.
>
> **Evidence and expiry.** Commitment occurs at the first binding external order, acceptance, or instruction to undertake chargeable work. Verbal IC authorization must be recorded within four hours of authorization; the emergency form is due within 24 hours of commitment. One complete, authenticated artifact may satisfy both duties. Record actual action time and posting time separately; outages permit alternative contemporaneous evidence, not suspended controls or deadlines. Later approval mitigates but does not retrospectively satisfy a prior-approval requirement. Each directive states an inclusive start and exclusive expiry, named lots, and permitted departures. Commitment must occur before expiry; a call started or authorization obtained earlier does not extend the window. Existing documentation deadlines survive expiry. Extensions require a written instrument issued before expiry by the competent authority; verbal or presumed extensions are ineffective.
>
> **Return to ordinary controls.** Each operational directive lasts no more than 72 hours without a fresh, documented necessity review and competent reauthorization. Review scope daily, aggregate related spending to prevent threshold avoidance, and end permissions when their necessity ceases. Perform an independent review within five business days of closure and assign a dated plan to restore ordinary purchasing controls.
