## 2. Counterfactual continuations

The lawful decision branches below depend on evidence and reviewer judgment. The observational 0.55–0.75 ultimate-approval forecast is context, not a probability guaranteed for each intervention. The complementary probability includes rejection and hold, not necessarily rejection alone.

1. **C — independent S2 review**

   `Evidence Validated → R17 Analyst Review → S2 Second Officer Review → justified disposition`

   - S2 finds sufficient clearance evidence → valid Approve → Payment Executed. Execution before minute 90 preserves the timing benefit; otherwise this becomes a lawful late execution.
   - Evidence satisfies the rejection threshold → documented Reject after required reviews.
   - Material uncertainty remains → Hold; obtain targeted evidence or investigate further. File a Suspicious Activity Report only if warranted. A filed report does not authorize execution and must not be disclosed to the customer.

   Main uncertainties: how quickly R17 can complete the resumed review, S2's actual service time, whether the unresolved match can be adjudicated, and the resulting effect on P-901. Expedited scheduling must preserve independent judgment.

2. **D — parallel screening and S4 preparation**

   `Start alternate screening ∥ resume R17 review/refresh current risk information ∥ confirm and wait for S4`

   `Validated screening result + completed Analyst Review + officer available → independent Second Officer Review → Approve/Reject/Hold`

   - Alternate screening supports a false-match explanation → R17 and the second officer assess it with the existing evidence. Approval may become more supportable, but screening alone does not authorize execution.
   - Screening corroborates a material concern → evaluate documented rejection or hold/report criteria; do not force a clearance to preserve the deadline.
   - Screening is inconclusive → continue the independent review using the full evidence; request additional evidence if needed.
   - S4 cannot credibly complete within the deadline → promptly consider C rather than spending the entire availability window waiting. If interruption is unjustified or S2 is unavailable, maintain a lawful hold and use S4 when available.

   Main uncertainties: vendor accuracy and disagreement with the original screen, S4's actual availability and review duration, and whether new evidence requires further analysis. Any S2 review started while screening runs must consider the returned result before final disposition; parallel work must not conceal unresolved contradictory evidence.

3. **E — further customer evidence**

   `Analyst Review → Evidence Requested → Evidence Received → Evidence Validated → Analyst Review → required Second Officer Review → disposition`

   Evidence may establish a false match and support approval, substantiate grounds for rejection, or remain inadequate and lead to another hold/evidence cycle. The current deadline is almost certainly missed. The main uncertainty is whether the requested evidence is obtainable and decision-relevant; another request should name the missing fact rather than repeat an uninformative cycle. Customer communications must not reveal a report.

4. **F — conditional rejection**

   `Resume Analyst Review → establish documented rejection threshold → complete required disposition reviews → Reject`

   If the threshold is not met, there is no valid “reject now” branch: continue C/D, or hold and obtain targeted evidence through E. Match confidence 0.46, geographic concern and the risk score do not, by themselves, establish a rejection standard. Rule 8 places disposition after required reviews; the 10–20-minute estimate must not be interpreted as permission to bypass them. Main uncertainties are the applicable evidentiary threshold and whether the validated evidence actually meets it.

5. **G — conditional exception**

   `Preserve normal review/hold path ∥ assess genuine incident and seek director authorization`

   - Actual authorization covering this payment + a valid, justified incident identifier → record both → follow the authorized exception's scope → execute only if that exception supplies valid execution authority.
   - Director unavailable, authorization denied, incident unjustified, or either prerequisite missing → no override execution; continue independent review or hold.

   The deadline and commercial loss do not themselves establish an incident. Main uncertainties are reachability, genuine incident justification, authorization scope and time remaining. Creating a token identifier to bypass the process is not a valid incident.

For completeness, A produces execution without a valid approval path, while B produces a purported second review by the first reviewer. Neither continuation becomes lawful merely because the transfer succeeds or no sanctions violation is subsequently discovered.

## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

Use a constrained decision: first remove actions that violate mandatory controls, then compare the remaining plans. A favorable predicted financial result cannot compensate for missing approval authority or reviewer independence. This separates control eligibility from optimization within the eligible set.

For a lawful plan j, the deadline-related component of expected value depends on the intervention-specific probability of **valid approval and execution before minute 90**, multiplied by €20k–€35k. Its incremental benefit uses the difference between that joint probability under j and under normal processing. Those joint probabilities are not supplied. Neither the baseline breach range of 0.48–0.64 nor the approval range of 0.55–0.75 can simply be multiplied together: approval and completion time can be dependent, and interventions change resource allocation and potentially decision outcomes. The model ranges also provide no distribution over review times or calibration guarantee.

Timely payment is not an entitlement if clearance evidence is insufficient. Conversely, unresolved uncertainty does not justify a reflex rejection. The €15k–€80k false-rejection/prolonged-hold range makes E and F potentially expensive despite avoiding immediate execution risk. Prefer an independent, informative review that can distinguish lawful clearance from justified rejection or hold. Additional evidence dominates an unsupported rejection on control grounds, even when slower; a sufficiently evidenced rejection can dominate a prolonged, uninformative hold.

The €100k–€500k regulatory range is a possible consequence of unlawful execution, not a known expected cost. Match confidence 0.46 is not an established probability that the beneficiary is sanctioned or that a regulatory loss will occur. Do not multiply it by that harm range. Mandatory controls remain binding even if an analyst invents probabilities that make A or B appear numerically profitable.

Non-monetary consequences include facilitating a prohibited transfer, legal exposure, damage to compliance governance and trust, and loss of auditable accountability. An executed transfer may be difficult to recover, making premature clearance especially asymmetric. A conformant review reduces avoidable control failure; it does not guarantee that the final judgment is correct or that external sanctions obligations are satisfied. Approval still requires an adequately supported substantive clearance decision.

Do not automatically add €20k–€35k missed-timing value to €15k–€80k customer harm: the estimates may describe overlapping losses. Apply the same caution to regulatory monetary loss and associated reputation costs. The defensible comparison is a sensitivity analysis across plausible consequences and portfolio priorities, not a fabricated precise expected-utility score.

## 5. Resource spillovers

S2 provides the shortest available conformant route, but transferring delay to P-901 is a real portfolio cost. P-901's 70-minute deadline is earlier than P-884's. If L901 is its incremental consequence of breach, the supplied 45-minute interruption estimate contributes approximately `0.12–0.28 × L901` in expected spillover loss, before considering other effects. L901 is not supplied and cannot be replaced with P-884's loss estimate. The 0.12–0.28 range also must not be extrapolated unchanged to a 35- or 75-minute interruption. Confirm P-901's remaining work, the actual interruption duration and available mitigation, and release S2 promptly after the decision.

S4 preserves P-901's immediate capacity, but its availability delay can make P-884's deadline unattainable. Let a be S4's availability time, h the time when required analyst work and screening results are ready, r the second-review duration, and x the disposition/execution duration, all measured from the current decision point. Completion is approximately `max(a, h) + r + x`; handoffs or additional investigation can extend it.

- **S4 available at minute 35:** If r is 35–75 minutes as a working assumption and x is 5–15, completion is 75–125 minutes when preceding work is ready. Timely execution requires `r + x ≤ 55`; early availability alone does not establish this. With a credible short review, D can become rank 1 because screening fits into the waiting period and P-901 avoids interruption. Confirm S4's own service estimate rather than treating C's estimate as guaranteed for S4.
- **S4 available at minute 65:** Under the same assumption, even the fastest completion is minute 105, with a 105–155-minute envelope. C therefore remains first for deadline feasibility. D with S4 remains a lawful option if P-901's expected harm makes interruption unacceptable, but the decision must explicitly accept P-884's likely miss.

C's 40–90-minute envelope has little or no margin at its upper end and excludes unspecified preceding work. Waiting before escalating to S2 shifts that whole window later. Minute 50 is the latest start compatible even with the optimistic 40-minute service path, not a prudent escalation deadline. Do not wait until then hoping for the shortest service time.

D consumes one-third of the remaining expedited API quota. Its value depends on whether an independent vendor can resolve a consequential uncertainty and change the disposition; correlation between vendors may limit that value. Compare this information benefit with other cases' likely need for the two remaining calls. If screening is selected, run it alongside the analyst's work and officer scheduling. A strictly serial screen-then-S2 path takes approximately 55–120 minutes, plus unquantified preceding work. Overlap may reduce delay, but final review must incorporate the result and any contradictions; a guaranteed zero-delay improvement is not established.

G should not occupy the critical path or cause release of a viable officer slot on the assumption that authorization will arrive. A 20–60-minute unsuccessful pursuit can destroy an otherwise achievable completion window and also consume scarce director capacity. Consider it only for a genuine incident, with a firm parallel conformant path and actual prerequisites verified before any exception execution.

## 6. Final recommendation and confidence

**Primary recommendation: C.** Promptly resume R17's review of the validated evidence and verify current risk/match information, secure S2 for an independent expedited review, and record approval, rejection or hold on the evidence. Execute only after valid approval. Check P-901's interruption consequences immediately and preserve substantive review quality. Alternate screening can supplement this plan in parallel if its likely information value justifies one scarce call; it cannot replace the review or justify an unsupported approval.

**Fallback: D with S4.** Run alternate screening and resumed analyst review in parallel while confirming S4's availability and review duration. Prefer this over interrupting P-901 if confirmed early completion can meet the deadline, or if P-901's expected harm makes interruption disproportionate. If timely independent clearance cannot be achieved, maintain a lawful hold and complete the reviews; request additional evidence only for an identified gap. Do not automatically reject or execute to meet the clock.

**Confidence:** high in the mandatory-control conclusions; moderate in C's current priority over D; low in exact intervention effects and monetary expectations. The ordering is sensitive to unknown portfolio losses and reviewer completion times.

**Immediate reranking trigger:** a reliable S4 availability-and-completion update. Availability at 35 minutes with a credible review/disposition time of at most 55 minutes can promote D above C; availability at 65 minutes strengthens C's deadline advantage. Material new sanctions evidence, a changed current risk score, or a materially worse P-901 interruption forecast also requires immediate reassessment. None of these observations enables A or B while their mandatory controls remain unsatisfied.
