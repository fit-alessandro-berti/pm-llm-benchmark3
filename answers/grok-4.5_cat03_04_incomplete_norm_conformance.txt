## 1. Conformance semantics

Distinction of deviation classes rests on the four normative statuses and on open- versus closed-world reading of each family.

**Forbidden behavior** is any observed relation whose status is explicitly `forbidden` (N3, T6, E10). The norm is closed-world for these edges: presence alone is noncompliant, subject only to data-quality rebuttal.

**Missing mandatory behavior** is absence of a relation marked `mandatory` inside its stated scope (N1, N4, T1–T2, E1–E8). Closed-world applies only inside that scope; outside the scope, absence is uninformative. Counts below the population of in-scope objects (e.g., 98/100, 198/200) are candidate gaps, not automatic violations, until abstraction and recording completeness are checked.

**Unexpected but unregulated behavior** is any observed edge or path whose status is `unspecified` or that never appears in the norm at all. Open-world applies: observation is allowed unless a separate policy forbids it. Examples include ReopenClose (N6), Order–Customer (T4), Order–RiskCase (T5), Manual Compliance Review–Customer, ValidateIssue Invoice, Package–Delivery codeath, and Issue InvoiceWrite Off.

**Typical-pattern absence** is shortfall on a `typical` relation (N2, N5, T3). Shortfall is noteworthy for operations but is not noncompliance; legitimate exceptions are already acknowledged in the norm text.

**Likely data-quality error** is an observation that (a) contradicts a hard rule or produces an impossible business binding and (b) has independent evidence of join error, reused identifiers, missing labels, or alternative attribute carriers. E10’s Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice link and the unlabeled unapplied-cash payment are primary candidates.

**Abstraction mismatch** occurs when the norm states an eventual or lifecycle expectation while the summary only materializes directly-follows (DF) counts. N1 is the canonical case: “should eventually enter validation” is approximated by CreateValidate DF; intervening unrecorded review events can suppress the DF edge without suppressing the eventual behavior.

**Insufficient evidence** covers cases where graph aggregates cannot decide scope membership, object intent (e.g., “externally shipped”), event typing (unapplied cash), or whether a missing mandatory partner is absent from the log or merely unbound.

Open-world versus closed-world therefore partitions the analysis: forbidden and in-scope mandatory edges are closed; typical, unspecified, and all unmentioned relations are open. PM4Py-style diagnostics that flag “edge not in norm” must be re-interpreted through this lens; raw diagnostic presence/absence is not a legal verdict.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

OC-DFG establishes directly-follows frequency between activities on the same object type. It can show presence of forbidden DF edges (N3: CancelDispatch, count 2) and approximate mandatory eventualities (N1, N4) and typical continuations (N2, N5).

It cannot establish true eventual reachability, parallelism, or that an activity never occurred later; DF absence  lifecycle absence. It cannot see cross-object constraints or birth/death coupling.

Counts and scopes: N1 is scoped to “newly created standard Order” with an explicit note that the summary only approximates eventual validation via DF. 98/100 is therefore not automatically two violations; the additional context (possible unrecorded review between create and validate) is a classic abstraction mismatch. N4’s 198/200 must be read against “packages intended for external shipment”; without an intent flag in the graph, the two gaps may be out-of-scope or mis-typed. N3’s two cases need no frequency threshold—forbidden is binary—but still need object-identity integrity. N2’s 83 ValidateClose and the 72 ValidateIssue Invoice paths illustrate that typical DF is optional and that intermediate invoice paths are acknowledged as incompletely modeled. N5’s 92/110 plus 4 write-offs plus 14 opens matches the norm’s own exception language. N6 ReopenClose (1) is unspecified and therefore non-violating.

**Danger of DF-as-eventual:** Treating N1 as a strict DF rule over-flags orders that validate after an intervening compliance review (the 3 CreateManual Compliance Review orders may partly explain the DF shortfall). Business rules of the form “should eventually” require eventually-follows or alignment diagnostics, not only DFG edges. Conversely, a present DF edge does not prove the business intent behind mandatory controls (e.g., pack quality) was satisfied—only that the activities were adjacent on the object.

### OTG

OTG establishes typed object-to-object relationships (interaction, cobirth, codeath, etc.) and their weights. It can confirm mandatory interactions (T1 Order–Item 1450; T2 Package–Delivery 200) and detect forbidden cobirth (T6 Order–Payment cobirth 1). Unspecified edges (T4, T5) and unmentioned codeath remain open-world.

It cannot prove that every event instance carried the required partners—only that the relationship type exists with some weight—nor whether weights match event cardinalities. It cannot by itself sequence cancellations versus dispatches (that is OC-DFG/behavior).

Counts: T1/T2 look satisfied at aggregate level, but ET–OT will show finer gaps (Dispatch–Delivery 198). T3 Invoice–Payment 92 is typical, not mandatory. T6 weight 1 is a hard finding. Package–Delivery codeath 7 is silent in the norm and should not be raised as nonconformance; it is a lifecycle synchronization pattern.

A relation can be conforming in OTG (Package–Delivery interaction 200) yet suspicious in ET–OT (two Dispatch events without Delivery objects), showing family disagreement that must be reconciled with attribute-level carrier references.

### ET–OT

ET–OT binds event types to object types per event occurrence aggregates. It is the right family for E1–E10: every Create Order must touch an Order (100/100), every Add Item must touch Order and Item (1448 vs 1450—near miss), Dispatch must touch Package and Delivery (200 and 198), Record Payment must touch Payment and Invoice (93 and 92), and Sensor Heartbeat must never touch Invoice (1 observed).

It cannot express activity order, cobirth, or whether the linked Invoice is the commercially correct one. Scope exceptions (E8 unapplied cash) require event subtypes that the snapshot does not carry—hence the single Payment-without-Invoice case is ambiguous between breach and unlabeled legitimate exception.

Cross-family consistency: N4 DF gaps (2) align in spirit with possible dispatch control issues but are distinct from E6’s two missing Delivery links; context attributes that a carrier reference exists, so E6 may be representation DQ rather than missing delivery. E10 aligns with forbidden status but context points to join DQ. MCR–RiskCase (E9) is unspecified; MCR–Customer is unregulated. Add Item Order/Item skew (1448/1450) is a small mandatory gap worth binding repair, not process redesign.

Again, DF-versus-eventual: ET–OT will not repair N1’s abstraction problem; only richer temporal queries will.

## 5. PM4Py conformance code sketch

```python
import pm4py

# ocel: observed object-centric event log
# norm_ocdfg, norm_otg, norm_etot: bare normative structures (edges only)
# Maintain status metadata OUTSIDE bare graphs:
#   norm_status_ocdfg[(ot, a, b)] in {"mandatory","forbidden","typical","unspecified"}
#   norm_status_otg[(t1, rel, t2)] analogously
#   norm_status_etot[(et, ot)] analogously
#   norm_scope: dict of scope predicates / notes (e.g., N1 eventual vs DF)

obs_ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
obs_otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
obs_etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

# Raw PM4Py diagnostics (do not treat as final business verdict)
raw_ocdfg = pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(obs_ocdfg, norm_ocdfg)
raw_otg = pm4py.conformance_otg(obs_otg, norm_otg)
raw_etot = pm4py.conformance_etot(obs_etot, norm_etot)

def classify_ocdfg(raw, status_map, scope_map, obs):
    layer = []
    for finding in raw:
        edge = finding.get("edge")  # (object_type, act_from, act_to)
        st = status_map.get(edge, "unspecified")
        sc = scope_map.get(edge, {})
        cls = "unregulated"
        if st == "forbidden" and finding.get("observed", False):
            cls = "forbidden_behavior"
        elif st == "mandatory" and finding.get("missing", False):
            cls = ("abstraction_mismatch"
                   if sc.get("eventual_not_df") else "missing_mandatory")
        elif st == "typical" and finding.get("missing", False):
            cls = "typical_pattern_absence"
        elif st in ("unspecified",) or edge not in status_map:
            cls = "unexpected_unregulated"
        layer.append({**finding, "norm_status": st, "semantic_class": cls})
    return layer

def classify_otg(raw, status_map):
    layer = []
    for finding in raw:
        edge = finding.get("edge")  # (t1, rel, t2)
        st = status_map.get(edge, "unspecified")
        cls = "unregulated"
        if st == "forbidden" and finding.get("observed", False):
            cls = "forbidden_behavior"
        elif st == "mandatory" and finding.get("missing", False):
            cls = "missing_mandatory"
        elif st == "typical" and finding.get("missing", False):
            cls = "typical_pattern_absence"
        layer.append({**finding, "norm_status": st, "semantic_class": cls})
    return layer

def classify_etot(raw, status_map, dq_hints):
    layer = []
    for finding in raw:
        edge = finding.get("edge")  # (event_type, object_type)
        st = status_map.get(edge, "unspecified")
        cls = "unregulated"
        if st == "forbidden" and finding.get("observed", False):
            cls = ("likely_data_quality_error"
                   if edge in dq_hints else "forbidden_behavior")
        elif st == "mandatory" and finding.get("missing", False):
            cls = "missing_mandatory"
        layer.append({**finding, "norm_status": st, "semantic_class": cls})
    return layer

# Example external metadata (maintain outside graph structures)

    ("Order", "object_interaction", "RiskCase"): "unspecified",

    ("Add Item", "Order"): "mandatory",
    ("Add Item", "Item"): "mandatory",
```

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1 DF versus eventual validation** — Risk: false nonconformance on orders with intervening reviews; auditors chase ghosts. Clarification: state eventually-follows Validate within a SLA, and list allowed interveners (Manual Compliance Review). Status: **mandatory** (eventual), with explicit allowed intermediates.

2. **External-shipment predicate for N4** — Risk: internal transfers flagged or true external skips excused. Clarification: define intent attribute/object subtype for external packages. Status: **mandatory** within clarified scope.

3. **E6 Delivery object versus carrier attribute** — Risk: dual representations cause intermittent mandatory gaps. Clarification: require Delivery object on dispatch **or** name allowed attribute fallback and its audit strength. Status: **mandatory** object link (preferred) or **typical** attribute fallback with DQ rule.

4. **E8 unapplied cash typing** — Risk: indistinguishable breach vs legitimate cash. Clarification: mandatory event subtype or Payment reason code when no Invoice is linked. Status: exception path **explicitly unspecified** or **typical** when subtype = unapplied; else **mandatory** Invoice link.

5. **N3 cancellation semantics (partial cancel, multi-package orders)** — Risk: over/under-flagging when one line cancels and another ships. Clarification: forbid Dispatch only for packages bound to cancelled order lines/objects. Status: **forbidden** with refined object-level scope.

6. **Codeath and shared confirmation events** — Risk: future tools may mark codeath anomalous. Clarification: declare Package–Delivery codeath allowed when both end in the same confirmation. Status: **explicitly unspecified** or **typical**.

7. **T6 cobirth definition (timestamp equality vs single event ID)** — Risk: batch loads create false cobirth. Clarification: cobirth = same event ID creating both objects, not merely equal timestamps. Status: **forbidden** under event-ID definition.

8. **Frequency tolerance / materiality** — Risk: single DQ edges escalate like systemic fraud. Clarification: investigation thresholds (e.g., any forbidden instance investigates; mandatory gaps below 1% sample-first). Status: governance rule alongside statuses, not a graph edge—document as **mandatory** review policy for forbidden, **typical** sampling for small mandatory gaps.

9. **Manual Compliance Review multi-object pattern (RiskCase+Customer)** — Risk: privacy or dual-control ambiguity. Clarification: allow MCR–Customer as supporting link. Status: **explicitly unspecified** or **typical**.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

**Robust conclusions:** N3’s two CancelDispatch observations are forbidden under closed-world N3 and warrant containment regardless of tolerance bands; T1/T2 aggregate interactions and E1/E4/E5/E7 anchors conform; N6, T4, T5, E9, codeath, ValidateIssue Invoice, MCR–Customer, and ReopenClose are not violations under open-world reading; N5 shortfalls including write-offs and opens are within typical exception language.

**Dependent on suspected data-quality issues:** E10 Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice (reused ID join); possibly T6 cobirth (batch/timestamp); possibly E6 missing Delivery links (carrier attribute present); possibly Add Item Order/Item skew.

**Cannot be decided from graph summaries alone:** whether the two N1 orders ever validated; whether the two N4 packages were in scope; commercial correctness of invoice–payment matches; actor intent on MCR; true shipment status of N3 packages; and any statistical “normal” rate—because the norm supplies neither full traces nor tolerance bands. Multi-family alignment plus source-system lineage is required before legal or disciplinary use of these diagnostics.
