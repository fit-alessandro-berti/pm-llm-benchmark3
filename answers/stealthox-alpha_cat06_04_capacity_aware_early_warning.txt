## 1. Decision rule

**Principle.** `p_now` ranks risk; it does not decide interventions. Decisions are made on **calibration-adjusted risk × estimated causal risk reduction × value  costs  externalities**, subject to stage eligibility, evidence freshness, and live congestion.

**Step 0 — Probability robustification (always applied).** Convert `p_now` to `p_adj` with segment offsets carried as intervals, not points:

| Segment | Adjustment | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| SME | +0.065 [+0.04, +0.09] | documented underprediction |
| Strategic Enterprise | 0.085 [0.05, 0.12] | overprediction post-redesign |
| Public Sector | 0 | acceptable calibration |
| Non-Native-Language Support | 0, band ±0.10 | sparse validation; widen, don't shift |
| Any case with imputed key features | decision band = [p_prev, p_now] until source field verified | imputation may manufacture the signal |

**Step 1 — Eligibility gates (all must pass).**
- **Stage:** current stage strictly before `Diagnosis Complete`, and time-to-deadline  90 min (swarm 45 min + coordination buffer). This permanently disqualifies I-03 regardless of its 0.82 risk.
- **Cool-down:** no swarm on the case within the last 4 h.
- **Evidence freshness:** first alert; **or** repeat with a *material new event* (new diagnostic artifact, scope/severity change, invalidated hypothesis, ownership change); **or** repeat with no prior swarm this episode and `p_adj`  0.5. A third-plus alert with **no new event is auto-suppressed** (manual override requires written justification).

**Step 2 — Economics.** Compute `EV_dir = ARR_mid × V  450  FA_burden` (full FA charge per the supplied convention; probability-weighting FA is a reported sensitivity). Then test against **live marginal spillover** `S = 6 × p_cong × V_q`, where `V_q` is the *measured* average value-at-risk of the six untreated diagnosis-queue cases (not assumed — pulled from the queue at decision time).
- **Green:** `EV_dir > 0` at *pessimistic* calibration (low ARR, worst offset) **and** `EV_dir > S`.
- **Amber:** passes only at midpoints  senior coordinator sign-off.
- **Red:** otherwise.

**Step 3 — Portfolio assembly.** Rank green/amber cases by `EV_net = EV_dir  S`; tie-break by *time to window closure* (a case whose eligibility expires soon gains priority because waiting destroys the option; a case with hours of runway gains nothing from instant action). Stagger swarm starts ~30 min apart. **Congestion brake:** after 2 slots, each additional slot must clear `1.5 × S` and show no congestion signals (queue wait-time trend, specialist interruption budget  ~90 min/senior/day).

**Step 4 — Reserve.** One slot held while the regulatory-incident window is open (until 15:00), with scheduled release checkpoints (12:20, 13:00, 14:00, 15:00) and preemption hierarchy: regulatory incident > triggered watchlist case > marginal-evidence cases.

**Step 5 — Logging.** Every decision records `p_prev/p_now`, offsets, ARR draw, spillover estimate, and signer. This is the raw material for Section 6.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

- **Cool-down:** after any swarm, 4 h during which alerts are logged but cannot trigger action. Elevated `p` after a swarm is *expected* — judge the trajectory (slope of `p_adj` across refreshes), not the level.
- **Suppression:** the third consecutive alert with no material new event is auto-suppressed for the episode and routed to a weekly "chronic alert" review that hunts systematic causes (stuck features, segment drift, refresh artifacts). Suppression is per-episode: a material event resets it.
- **Escalation to mandatory human review (within 15 min):** `p_adj`  0.70 with  4 h runway; value  €50k with `p_adj`  0.50; any NNLS or imputed-feature flag near a decision boundary.
- **Re-entry:** a *material new event* — enumerated: new diagnostic artifact/log, scope or severity change, invalidated root-cause hypothesis, failed workaround, ownership/handover change. Re-entry restores full eligibility once per event; max 2 swarms per incident, the second requiring coordinator sign-off that the event is genuinely new.

**I-05 vs I-07 — the contrast the policy is built around.** *I-05* is a third alert with **no new event**: it carries zero marginal information, so it is suppressed regardless of its respectable EV_dir (€1,725). Its SME-adjusted risk (~0.64) is real, so it is not ignored — it gets ordinary queue hygiene and a spot in the chronic-alert review to find out why the model keeps refiring on static features. *I-07* is also a third alert, but the **new customer log is a material event**: it re-enters eligibility with restored priority and an expedited checkpoint, because its 90-minute window is closing. The difference in treatment is evidential, not rhetorical — but re-entry restores *eligibility*, not entitlement: I-07 still must clear the economics, and at EV_dir €800 against a base spillover of ~€2.4k/slot it defaults to "do not deploy" unless the live congestion estimate is unusually low. Treating I-07 seriously and still declining is the system working, not failing.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

- **Value-weighting starves low-value segments.** Pure €EV ranking systematically directs swarms to Strategic Enterprise and away from SME/Public Sector cases, compounding over time. *Safeguards:* (a) an **equity floor** —  25% of weekly swarm capacity allocated by ARR-per-case alone, ignoring €V; (b) **winsorize V at €50k** in the scoring formula so a single €100k account cannot monopolize slots; (c) monthly report of intervention rate ÷ eligibility rate by segment, alarmed outside [0.8, 1.25].
- **Non-Native-Language Support uncertainty.** Small validation samples mean both `p_adj` and ARR are soft; silent auto-denial would under-serve these customers invisibly. *Safeguards:* widened bands (already in Step 0), mandatory human review near boundaries, deliberate label collection on NNLS outcomes, quarterly re-estimation of NNLS effects, and language-matched swarm staffing where possible.
- **Differential calibration.** Unadjusted, SE overprediction crowds out SME cases whose true risk is understated — a double injustice. *Safeguards:* institutionalize the offset table as per-segment isotonic recalibration refreshed weekly; publish the offsets; auto-alarm when any segment's backtest bias exceeds 0.05 for two weeks.
- **Selective intervention corrupts future training labels.** Treated cases that survive are labeled "no breach," teaching the model that cases resembling treated ones are safe — a classic feedback loop that degrades ranking exactly where the policy acts. *Safeguards:* store the treatment flag and propensity score in the training pipeline; train with uplift/doubly-robust methods rather than naive outcomes; maintain a small (**~10%**) randomized holdout among eligible amber cases (safety-constrained) to preserve counterfactual signal; never retrain on post-intervention outcomes without adjustment.
- **Spillover falls systematically on the untreated.** The six diagnosis-queue cases that bear delay are invisible in case-level EV, and if that queue skews toward particular segments (e.g., SMEs lingering in diagnosis), harm concentrates. *Safeguards:* a daily **spillover ledger** tagging exposed cases by segment; difference-in-differences of diagnosis-stage breach rates on swarm vs non-swarm days; congestion caps and rotation of queue priority; a next-day priority boost for heavily exposed cases; exposed-case outcomes on the fairness dashboard, not buried in queue metrics.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

- **Operational (daily):** slot utilization; actual swarm duration vs 45 min; specialist interruption minutes vs the ~90 min/day budget; queue-delay P50/P95 on swarm days vs baseline; alertdecision latency; suppression and override rates; whether the reserve was actually available when the regulatory incident arrived.
- **Predictive (weekly):** per-segment calibration curves, Brier score, ECE; drift tests (PSI) on top features with special tracking of **imputed-field failure rates** (the I-06 failure mode); burden-weighted false-alarm rate; alert precision@k. Trigger: any segment bias > 0.05 sustained two weeks  recalibrate that segment immediately.
- **Causal-effect (rolling):** maintain an intervention registry (pre-intervention `p_adj`, ARR draw, outcome). Estimate realized risk reduction via propensity-matched treated-vs-similar-untreated comparisons plus the 10% randomized holdout; produce per-segment, per-stage ARR posteriors with CIs. Trigger: if realized ARR < 50% of the prior midpoint across 10 consecutive interventions, halve ARR priors and raise all gates; watch for decay after workflow changes (the SE redesign is the cautionary tale).
- **Capacity (weekly):** re-estimate the congestion-response curve (aggregate delay and p per slot vs slots used) to replace the assumed 0.01–0.03; track regulatory-incident frequency to validate the reserve policy — if incidents prove rare, shorten the reserve to earlier release checkpoints rather than a full slot held all day.
- **Fairness (monthly + quarterly audit):** intervention, eligibility, suppression, and spillover-exposure rates by segment, value band, and language support; disparity-ratio alarms outside [0.8, 1.25]; quarterly review of refused cases (I-03-type and I-05-type) to confirm suppression isn't hiding true positives, especially under the SME offset.
- **Policy-induced drift detection:** compare pre- vs post-policy `p` distributions by segment (selection shift), monitor for alert-rate inflation or threshold-gaming (cases clustering just below the line), and re-run backtests on holdout periods untouched by interventions.
- **Change rules (pre-registered, not ad hoc):** raise the deployment bar if measured spillover exceeds 0.03/case for a week; relax it if below 0.015 with stable congestion; adjust thresholds via staged rollout on a rolling cohort rather than a global flip; every rule change is logged with the metric that motivated it, so the policy itself remains auditable.
