# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

## 1.1 Scope and Intended Use Cases
In evaluating process models discovered from event logs, model utility cannot be assessed in a vacuum. A model that is optimal for open-ended exploratory analytics can be catastrophic if deployed for automated compliance monitoring or robotic process orchestration. We distinguish four distinct operational use cases:

1. **Exploratory Process Discovery & Bottleneck Analysis:** The model acts as an analytical lens to uncover actual execution paths, variant frequencies, queuing bottlenecks, and throughput times. Here, the model must be resilient to sensor noise, missing log events, and minor logging anomalies to reveal broad operational patterns.
2. **Conformance Enforcement & Operational Auditing:** The model serves as an authoritative normative specification (a compliance gate). It detects unauthorized bypasses of regulatory controls (e.g., credit risk or technical safety gates), flags suspicious duplicate tasks, and enforces audit invariants. In this setting, false negatives (failing to flag an illegal bypass) represent critical compliance liabilities, while false positives (blocking valid orders) create operational friction.
3. **Simulation & What-If Capacity Planning:** The model provides a generative execution engine for discrete-event simulation, capacity planning, and stress-testing under shifting order volumes. The model must preserve structural soundness, avoid deadlocks, properly capture concurrency, and avoid unbounded state explosion in loops.
4. **Stakeholder Communication & System Architecture:** The model functions as a shared contract between business process owners, IT architects, compliance officers, and software engineers. It must exhibit low visual complexity, modular governance boundaries, clear activity ownership, and unambiguous control-flow semantics.

## 1.2 The Fitness–Precision Trade-off Across Use Cases
The acceptable trade-off between fitness (the proportion of observed behavior admitted by the model) and precision (the degree to which the model forbids unobserved or invalid behavior) varies fundamentally across these use cases:

* **In Exploratory Discovery:** Moderate precision is acceptable to accommodate real-world variance and measurement noise, provided high fitness is achieved without completely erasing control-flow structure.
* **In Conformance Enforcement:** High precision against curated negative business rules is paramount. A model that achieves high fitness by permitting all interleavings (an under-constrained "flower-like" structure) is useless for compliance because it cannot reject forbidden traces. Conversely, an overfitted model that achieves high precision by explicitly enumerating observed training traces will generate excessive false-positive compliance alerts when valid, unseen combinations occur.
* **In Simulation:** Both fitness and precision must be balanced with structural generalization to ensure simulated traces reflect realistic, valid behaviors without generating deadlocking states or impossible bypasses.

## 1.3 Key Assumptions and Data Limitations
Our review operates under three core domain and data realities:
* **Missing Attribute for Order Sourcing ($P3$):** The discovery event log lacks the `is_external` attribute. While policy dictates that `Supplier Review` is mandatory for external orders and absent for internal ones, a purely control-flow model cannot condition on data attributes. Thus, in the control-flow abstraction, `Supplier Review` must be modeled as structurally optional, while remaining strictly bound to its valid execution window.
* **Missing Attribute for Correction Scope ($P6$):** The log does not record which specific check failed when `Correct Dossier` occurs. Policy requires that after a correction, only the affected checks repeat. The control-flow model must therefore permit repeating `Credit Check` alone, `Technical Review` alone, or both, while strictly forbidding arbitrary repeated checks in the absence of a preceding `Correct Dossier` ($P7$).
* **Positive-Only Log Limitation:** The training and holdout logs contain only observed positive executions. Standard automated precision metrics computed solely against positive logs are vulnerable to severe bias. Conformance evaluation must therefore be augmented with curated negative behavior probes.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

## 3.1 Strongest Case for Model S
Model S provides a compact, structured representation of the dossier lifecycle. By placing the review activities inside a parallel block (`+`), it captures the true concurrency of independent reviews without forcing an artificial sequential ordering. It achieves high training fitness (0.992), exceptional holdout fitness (0.981), high token-based generalization (0.907), fast alignment computation (0.018 s/case), and strong stakeholder comprehension (4.4/5). For high-level executive presentations or coarse process discovery, it visualizes the end-to-end milestone progression (`Register` $\rightarrow$ `Reviews` $\rightarrow$ `Approval` $\rightarrow$ `Release`) cleanly and without duplicate transitions.

## 3.2 Strongest Case Against Model S
Model S is fundamentally flawed as a normative or operational process model. Its parallel block wraps every single review activity in an optional exclusive choice with a silent transition (`X(Activity, tau)`). This transforms the review phase into an unconstrained "flower-like" subsystem.

Because all four review branches are concurrent and completely optional, Model S admits severe policy violations:
1. **Mandatory Safety Gates Can Be Bypassed ($P2$):** Both `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` can choose the `tau` branch, allowing dossiers to proceed directly to approval and release with zero verification.
2. **Premature Expedite Review ($P4$):** `Expedite Review` is executed concurrently with mandatory checks rather than sequentially after them. It can fire first, or execute alone without the mandatory checks ever occurring.
3. **Total Absence of Rework / Correction Semantics ($P6, P7$):** Model S contains no `Correct Dossier` transition. Any case requiring correction will fail alignment or incur heavy non-synchronous penalty costs.
4. **False Generalization:** Model S's high generalization (0.907) and holdout fitness (0.981) are illusory; they are mathematical artifacts of a model that permits almost any subset of review activities in any arbitrary order.

## 3.3 Concrete Undesirable and Policy-Ambiguous Traces Admitted by Model S

1. **Trace T1 (Complete bypass of all mandatory risk controls — Violates $P2$):**
   $$\langle \text{Register Dossier}, \text{Manager Approval}, \text{Release Order} \rangle$$
   *Mechanism:* In the parallel block, `Credit Check`, `Technical Review`, `Supplier Review`, and `Expedite Review` all execute their silent `tau` transitions. The process proceeds directly from registration to approval.
2. **Trace T2 (Premature Expedite Review before mandatory checks — Violates $P4$):**
   $$\langle \text{Register Dossier}, \text{Expedite Review}, \text{Credit Check}, \text{Technical Review}, \text{Manager Approval}, \text{Release Order} \rangle$$
   *Mechanism:* Parallel interleaving allows the `Expedite Review` branch to fire before the mandatory check branches fire.
3. **Trace T3 (Expedite Review with complete omission of mandatory checks — Violates $P2, P4$):**
   $$\langle \text{Register Dossier}, \text{Expedite Review}, \text{Auto Approval}, \text{Release Order} \rangle$$
   *Mechanism:* `Expedite Review` fires, while `Credit Check`, `Technical Review`, and `Supplier Review` take `tau` skips.
4. **Trace T4 (Omission of Technical Review in an automated approval — Violates $P2$):**
   $$\langle \text{Register Dossier}, \text{Credit Check}, \text{Auto Approval}, \text{Release Order} \rangle$$
   *Mechanism:* `Technical Review` takes a `tau` skip, allowing automated release of unvetted custom engineering orders.
5. **Trace T5 (Supplier Review executed without mandatory reviews — Violates $P2$):**
   $$\langle \text{Register Dossier}, \text{Supplier Review}, \text{Manager Approval}, \text{Release Order} \rangle$$
   *Mechanism:* External supplier review occurs, but internal financial credit and technical feasibility checks are skipped via `tau`.
6. **Trace T6 (Expedite Review executed before Supplier Review without mandatory checks — Violates $P2, P4$):**
   $$\langle \text{Register Dossier}, \text{Expedite Review}, \text{Supplier Review}, \text{Manager Approval}, \text{Release Order} \rangle$$
   *Mechanism:* Reviews execute in reverse dependency order with core controls omitted.

## 3.4 Noise Tolerance vs. Accidental Semantic Flaws
Permissiveness in Model S is not a deliberate, calibrated noise-filtering strategy; it is an **accidental semantic flaw** caused by combining an AND operator (`+`) with unrestrained XOR skips (`X(A, tau)`). While intentional noise tolerance accommodates occasional missing logging timestamps or minor recording dropouts, Model S destroys the fundamental business invariants of the enterprise ($P2$ and $P4$) while entirely omitting the rework lifecycle ($P6$).

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

## 4.1 Strongest Case for Model C
Model C provides an explicit whitelist of historical variants observed during the training window. On those exact 8 paths:
* It strictly prevents mandatory check skipping (every path contains both `Credit Check` and `Technical Review`).
* It guarantees that `Expedite Review` never precedes `Credit Check` or `Technical Review`.
* It achieves an exceptional training alignment precision score of 0.941 and 0.973 training fitness.
* For an auditor seeking an exact trace-replay model of the 8 most frequent historical execution paths, Model C provides deterministic, sequentially unambiguous execution traces with zero silent transitions.

## 4.2 Strongest Case Against Model C
Model C is a classic textbook example of catastrophic process-discovery overfitting. Rather than learning the modular, concurrent rules governing the process, it memorized 8 specific sequential permutations under a top-level exclusive choice operator (`X`).

The structural and operational consequences are severe:
1. **Combinatorial Brittleness and Holdout Collapse:** By modeling independent concurrent checks as rigid sequential sequences, it fails on any legitimate combination not present in the training set. When evaluated on holdout data containing unseen valid combinations (e.g., `Technical Review` before `Credit Check` paired with `Auto Approval`, or `Supplier Review` paired with `Auto Approval`), its fitness collapses to 0.744.
2. **Label Duplication Pathology (33 Duplicates):** Visible activities are duplicated up to 8 times across branches (e.g., `Register Dossier` appears in all 8 branches, `Release Order` in all 8, `Manager Approval` in 7). This creates 33 duplicate labels in the Petri net representation, destroying structural simplicity (0.281) and triggering severe A* search-space explosion during conformance checking (increasing alignment runtime from 0.018 s to 0.440 s per case—a 24-fold slowdown).
3. **Rigid and Flawed Rework Semantics ($P6$):** Model C includes exactly one rework branch (Branch 7):
   $$\text{Branch 7: } \to(\text{Register}, \text{CC}, \text{TR}, \text{Correct Dossier}, \text{CC}, \text{TR}, \text{Manager Approval}, \text{Release})$$
   This forces *both* `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` to repeat in an exact fixed order, exactly once. It strictly rejects cases where only `Technical Review` or only `Credit Check` needs repeating ($F6$), rejects cases with multiple correction cycles, and forbids any dossier involving a correction from using `Auto Approval`, `Supplier Review`, or `Expedite Review`.
4. **Maintenance Impossibility:** If IT or compliance modifies the policy for `Release Order` or updates `Manager Approval`, changes must be manually copied across 7 or 8 separate branches. A change in one branch that is omitted in another creates silent compliance regressions.
5. **False Precision Artifact:** Model C's 0.941 precision is an artifact of memorizing training variant sequences rather than enforcing business rules. It appears precise only because it refuses to generalize.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

Process-mining evaluation metrics are subject to known pathologies when applied to flawed model topologies or uncurated logs.

## 6.1 Detailed Pathology Analysis

1. **Fitness Rewarded by Optionalizing Mandatory Behavior:**
   Model S achieves 0.992 training fitness and 0.981 holdout fitness because wrapping every activity in `X(Activity, tau)` guarantees that alignment cost is minimized regardless of what is missing in the log. The metric rewards the model for relinquishing control flow.
2. **Generalization Rewarded by Excessive Permissiveness:**
   Token-based replay (TBR) generalization calculates how evenly transitions and places are visited. Because Model S has an open, unconstrained parallel structure with minimal places that never deadlock, tokens flow freely without getting trapped. This earns Model S an inflated generalization score of 0.907 despite its poor semantic precision.
3. **Positive-Log-Only Precision Bias:**
   Precision metrics (such as alignment precision or ETC precision) measure how much behavior allowed by the model was unobserved in the log. However, when logs contain common variants, Model C's explicit memorization of those variants earns an artificially inflated precision score of 0.941. Without a curated negative trace suite containing known forbidden violations (such as B3 and B4), the precision metric fails to detect whether the model actually learned the governing rules or merely memorized strings.
4. **Duplicated-Label Effects on Simplicity and State-Space Explosion:**
   Model C enumerates 8 branches, duplicating visible labels 33 times. In Petri nets, duplicate transitions destroy the 1-to-1 mapping between labels and transitions, splitting places into redundant paths. This collapses simplicity to 0.281 and drastically degrades A* heuristic bounds during alignment computation, causing median alignment runtime to balloon from 0.018 s to 0.440 s per trace (a 24-fold performance penalty).
5. **Silent-Transition Inflation and Semantic Distortion:**
   Model S relies on four silent transitions (`tau`) inside a parallel construct. While silent transitions are standard for modeling skips, placing them in parallel allows any subset of tasks to execute in any order. The alignment algorithm exploits these `tau` transitions to match traces with minimal cost, masking severe nonconformance.
6. **Alignment-Cost Sensitivity & Uniform Cost Masking:**
   The standard alignment configuration assigns a uniform cost of 1 to all model moves and log moves. Under this setting, skipping a mandatory compliance check (e.g., `Credit Check`) incurs the exact same cost penalty as skipping an optional review (`Supplier Review`). Consequently, alignment fitness treats fatal compliance breaches as minor deviations, obscuring critical policy violations.
7. **Train/Holdout Leakage and Split Sensitivity:**
   The discovery team executed an unstratified 80/20 train/test split without publishing random seeds or variant distributions. If low-frequency valid combinations (e.g., `Technical Review` followed by `Credit Check` with `Auto Approval`, or `Supplier Review` + `Auto Approval`) were placed into the holdout set, Model C's holdout fitness drops precipitously (to 0.744). Model C's degradation reflects split sensitivity rather than a fundamental flaw in those holdout traces.
8. **Missing-Event and Missing-Attribute Bias:**
   In real-world operations, data attributes often govern routing. Because the `is_external` attribute was omitted from the log, `Supplier Review` appears in some traces and is absent in others. Model S responds by making it completely optional and unconstrained; Model C responds by duplicating branches with and without `Supplier Review`. Neither model captures the true attribute-dependent invariant.
9. **Informal Stakeholder Scoring Methodology:**
   The reported comprehension scores (4.4 vs. 1.9) reflect subjective, unvalidated 1–5 ratings without a structured assessment rubric. Business users rated Model S highly because its compact 4-activity visual diagram was easy to digest, completely unaware that it permitted orders to bypass all financial and engineering controls.
10. **Runtime Confounding by Branch Enumeration:**
    Model C's runtime slowdown (0.440 s) is directly confounded by model size and duplicate labels rather than underlying process complexity.

## 6.2 Impact Ranking of Metric Pathologies

The pathologies are ranked below by their potential to distort executive and governance decision-making (from most dangerous to least):

1. **Fitness Rewarded by Optionalizing Mandatory Behavior (Rank 1):** Creates the false impression that Model S is highly accurate when it is actually non-compliant.
2. **Positive-Log-Only Precision Bias (Rank 2):** Masks Model C's brittle overfitting and rewards memorization over rule induction.
3. **Alignment-Cost Uniformity Masking (Rank 3):** Equates fatal control-bypass violations with benign optional skips.
4. **Train/Holdout Split Sensitivity & Variant Omission (Rank 4):** Drastically distorts holdout performance metrics due to unstratified sampling.
5. **Generalization Rewarded by Permissiveness (Rank 5):** Gives an unconstrained model an artificially high structural score.
6. **Duplicated-Label A* State Explosion (Rank 6):** Degrades computational scalability and prevents real-time auditing.
7. **Silent-Transition Semantic Distortion (Rank 7):** Hides control-flow vacuums behind silent execution paths.
8. **Missing-Event / Missing-Attribute Bias (Rank 8):** Forces control-flow algorithms to guess data-driven routing.
9. **Informal Stakeholder Scoring Methodology (Rank 9):** Biases governance toward cosmetically simple models.
10. **Runtime Confounding by Model Topology (Rank 10):** Obscures algorithmic performance drivers.

# 7. Third Alternative

To resolve the fatal permissiveness of Model S and the brittle memorization of Model C, we design **`model_third`**: a structured, sound, and balanced process model that strictly enforces business policies $P1$ through $P7$ while maintaining maintainability and high generalization.

## 7.1 Compact Formal Description

`model_third` is designed as a block-structured Process Tree and equivalent Partially Ordered Workflow Language (POWL) model:

### Process Tree Formulation:
```text
->(
  'Register Dossier',
  +( 'Credit Check', 'Technical Review' ),
  +( X('Supplier Review', tau), X('Expedite Review', tau) ),
  *(
    tau,
    ->(
      'Correct Dossier',
      X( 'Credit Check', 'Technical Review', +( 'Credit Check', 'Technical Review' ) ),
      +( X('Supplier Review', tau), X('Expedite Review', tau) )
    )
  ),
  X( 'Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval' ),
  'Release Order'
)
```

### POWL and Execution Semantics:
1. **Milestone Progression ($P1$):** `Register Dossier` is the strict entry point; `Release Order` is the strict exit sink.
2. **Mandatory Concurrency Gate ($P2$):** `+( 'Credit Check', 'Technical Review' )` is a strict parallel block without `tau` transitions. Both checks MUST execute (in any interleaved order) before the process can advance. Skipping either check is structurally impossible.
3. **Constrained Post-Check Reviews ($P3, P4$):** Followed sequentially by `+( X('Supplier Review', tau), X('Expedite Review', tau) )`. 
   * Both reviews are optional (`X(..., tau)`).
   * Both are strictly sequenced *after* `Credit Check` and `Technical Review`. `Expedite Review` cannot fire prematurely.
   * If both execute, they can occur in either order (`SR` then `ER`, or `ER` then `SR`), satisfying $P4$.
4. **Correction and Targeted Rework Loop ($P6, P7$):** Modeled via the standard process tree loop construct `*( do, redo )` instantiated as `*( tau, redo_block )`.
   * Under standard process tree semantics, `*( tau, redo )` evaluates to $\text{tau} \cdot (\text{redo} \cdot \text{tau})^* \equiv (\text{redo})^*$.
   * If no correction is needed, the loop executes `tau` and exits immediately to approval.
   * If rework occurs, `Correct Dossier` fires first, followed by an exclusive choice of targeted rechecks:
     * Option A: `Credit Check` only.
     * Option B: `Technical Review` only.
     * Option C: Both `+( 'Credit Check', 'Technical Review' )`.
     * Followed by optional re-execution of `Supplier Review` / `Expedite Review`.
   * An unprovoked repeat of a mandatory check without a preceding `Correct Dossier` is structurally forbidden ($P7$).
5. **Exclusive Approval Gate ($P5$):** `X( 'Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval' )` ensures exactly one approval fires before release.

## 7.4 Explicit Semantic Mapping

* **Mandatory Checks:** Enforced by the non-optional parallel operator `+( 'Credit Check', 'Technical Review' )`.
* **Optional Supplier Review:** Governed by `X('Supplier Review', tau)` placed strictly downstream of mandatory checks.
* **Expedite Review Ordering:** Placed downstream of mandatory checks in parallel with `Supplier Review`. Interleaving permits either order, but both must precede approval.
* **Approval Choice:** Strictly guarded by `X('Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval')`.
* **Rework / Loop Semantics:** Handled by `*( tau, ->('Correct Dossier', ...) )`, allowing zero, one, or multiple correction cycles with targeted rechecks.

## 7.5 Missing-Data Limitations and Warning
* **Omission of `is_external`:** In the control-flow log, external and internal orders look identical except for the presence/absence of `Supplier Review`. `model_third` handles this safely by making `Supplier Review` structurally optional without allowing it to violate dependency ordering.
* **Omission of `affected_checks`:** The model permits any subset of mandatory checks to repeat after `Correct Dossier`. Full compliance requires capturing the data attribute in the operational schema.
* **Methodological Admonition:** *We explicitly do not solve missing data by silently treating mandatory checks as optional.* Doing so (as Model S did) destroys process integrity and invalidates compliance monitoring.

# 9. Validation Experiments

To validate `model_third` without relying on ungrounded metric averages or unseeded splits, the governance board must execute the following concrete evaluation program.

## 9.1 Experimental Protocol
1. **Reproducible Stratified Case Splits:** Use 5-fold cross-validation with fixed pseudo-random seeds (`seed=42`). Stratify splits across trace length, presence of `Supplier Review`, presence of `Correct Dossier`, and approval type.
2. **Curated Negative Behavior Suite:** Maintain a dedicated test suite containing explicit illegal traces (probes B3, B4, ADV3, and variants with skipped approvals or unauthorized releases). Measure negative precision ($1.0 - \text{False Positive Rate}$).
3. **Synthetic Noise & Perturbation Testing:** Inject controlled perturbations (missing timestamps, sensor dropouts, swapped events) at rates from 1% to 10% to measure graceful metric degradation.
4. **Unseen Orthogonal Combination Testing:** Hold out specific valid feature combinations (e.g., all traces combining `Supplier Review` and `Auto Approval`) from training and evaluate holdout fitness.
5. **Alignment-Cost Sensitivity Analysis:** Evaluate alignments under standard uniform costs ($1:1$) versus a **Rule-Weighted Cost Matrix** where skipping mandatory safety gates incurs a penalty cost of 100.
6. **Formal Soundness Verification:** Execute reachability analysis to prove the model is a sound 1-safe workflow net with no deadlocks, no livelocks, and proper completion.
7. **Task-Based Stakeholder Comprehension Testing:** Replace informal 1–5 scoring with a standardized test: measure response time and error rate when process participants answer questions regarding path legality and activity ownership.
8. **Hard Rejection Decision Rule:** Reject any candidate model if:
   * It accepts any trace from the Curated Negative Suite (Negative Precision $< 1.00$);
   * It exhibits a Holdout Fitness drop $> 0.05$ on stratified holdout sets;
   * It fails formal Petri net soundness;
   * Its median alignment runtime exceeds $0.050\text{ s/case}$.

import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.log.obj import EventLog, Trace, Event
from sklearn.model_selection import StratifiedKFold
import numpy as np

def build_trace(activity_list, case_id="0", attributes=None):
    trace = Trace(attributes={"concept:name": case_id, **(attributes or {})})
    for act in activity_list:
        trace.append(Event({"concept:name": act}))
    return trace

def create_curated_negative_suite():
    negative_traces = [
        # B3: Skip mandatory checks
        ['Register Dossier', 'Manager Approval', 'Release Order'],
        # B4: Premature Expedite Review
        ['Register Dossier', 'Expedite Review', 'Credit Check', 'Technical Review', 'Manager Approval', 'Release Order'],
        # ADV3: Premature Supplier Review

        # Unprovoked repeat check (B8)
        ['Register Dossier', 'Credit Check', 'Technical Review', 'Credit Check', 'Manager Approval', 'Release Order']
    ]
    log = EventLog()
    for idx, acts in enumerate(negative_traces):
        log.append(build_trace(acts, case_id=f"neg_{idx}"))
    return log

def evaluate_negative_suite_precision(log_neg, net, im, fm):
    """Calculates true negative rejection rate (must be 1.0 for compliance)."""
    alignments = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(log_neg, net, im, fm)
    accepted_count = sum(1 for a in alignments if a['fitness'] == 1.0)
    rejection_rate = 1.0 - (accepted_count / len(log_neg))
    return rejection_rate

def run_comprehensive_evaluation(log_full, model_tree):
    # [A] Convert and check soundness
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_tree)
    soundness_diag = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm)
    print(f"Soundness Verification: {soundness_diag[0]}")
    
    # [B] Simplicity (arc-degree variant)

    # [C] Negative Suite Evaluation
    log_neg = create_curated_negative_suite()
    neg_rejection_rate = evaluate_negative_suite_precision(log_neg, net, im, fm)
    print(f"Negative Suite Rejection Rate: {neg_rejection_rate:.4f} (Threshold: 1.0000)")
    
    # [D] Stratified K-Fold Cross Validation
    # (Stratification vector based on trace attributes / length)
    labels = [len(t) for t in log_full]
    skf = StratifiedKFold(n_splits=5, shuffle=True, random_state=42)
    
    train_fitnesses, test_fitnesses, precisions, generalizations = [], [], [], []
    
    for fold, (train_idx, test_idx) in enumerate(skf.split(log_full, labels)):
        train_log = EventLog([log_full[i] for i in train_idx])
        test_log = EventLog([log_full[i] for i in test_idx])
        
        fit_train = pm4py.fitness_alignments(train_log, net, im, fm)['log_fitness']
        fit_test = pm4py.fitness_alignments(test_log, net, im, fm)['log_fitness']
        prec_train = pm4py.precision_alignments(train_log, net, im, fm)
        gen_train = pm4py.generalization_tbr(train_log, net, im, fm)

        test_fitnesses.append(fit_test)
        precisions.append(prec_train)
        generalizations.append(gen_train)
        
    print(f"Stratified CV Train Fitness: {np.mean(train_fitnesses):.4f} +/- {np.std(train_fitnesses):.4f}")
    print(f"Stratified CV Holdout Fitness: {np.mean(test_fitnesses):.4f} +/- {np.std(test_fitnesses):.4f}")
    print(f"Stratified CV Alignment Precision: {np.mean(precisions):.4f} +/- {np.std(precisions):.4f}")

# 11. Dissenting View

## 11.1 Adversarial Rebuttal to the Governance Recommendation
A rigorous defense can be mounted for adopting Model C—or a strict declarative whitelist—over any generalized procedural model in high-liability, zero-trust environments. In heavily regulated domains governed by strict product liability and financial compliance (such as bespoke aerospace engineering or high-value financial underwriting), any unvetted execution path is inherently noncompliant until explicitly verified by engineering leadership. 

Generalization in control-flow models is not a pure asset; it is an assumption that unobserved combinations of known tasks are safe. By permitting combinations such as concurrent `Supplier Review` and `Expedite Review` preceding `Auto Approval` ($B5$), `model_third` presumes that an automated system can safely release expedited custom dossiers involving external third-party suppliers without managerial oversight. If historical operational policy deliberately reserved manager approval for all expedited external orders, Model C's strict rejection of $B5$ was not an overfitting error, but an accurate enforcement of an unwritten, risk-averse organizational invariant. 

From this perspective, Model C’s low holdout fitness (0.744) correctly halted unvetted execution variants for human review. In contrast, `model_third` introduces procedural flexibility that may inadvertently approve untested, high-risk process permutations.

## 11.2 Evidence That Would Alter the Decision
We would reconsider our recommendation and support a strict whitelist architecture (similar to Model C's philosophy, though refactored without duplicate-label bloat) if the following empirical evidence is established:
1. **Risk Analysis of Automated Expedited External Orders:** Audit evidence proving that combining `Auto Approval` with `Expedite Review` and `Supplier Review` leads to statistically significant defect or default rates, requiring a hard control-flow barrier rather than a data-attribute check.
2. **Exhaustive Variant Saturation:** Production log evidence over a 12-month period demonstrating that the business process is strictly variant-saturated (i.e., no valid new combinations ever occur in legitimate operations), meaning any unseen variant is in fact an operational error.
3. **Regulatory Mandate for White-Box Whitelisting:** Legal or regulatory mandates requiring explicit pre-authorization of every allowable sequential execution permutation before execution in automated ERP workflows.
