# 1. Model scope and state variables

## System boundary and paradigm
The digital twin is designed as a **hybrid Discrete-Event and Object-Centric Simulation (DES + relational state)** covering the municipal lifecycle from **portal submission** (`Submit application`) to **final administrative disposition** (`Issue permit` or `Reject application`), including post-decision **applicant appeals** where rework loops re-enter planning or legal review. 
* **In scope:** Municipal intake, completeness verification, missing-document loops, parallel/conditional technical reviews (Planning, Fire, Environment), statutory public-notice periods, district-routed inspection scheduling and field visits (including travel overhead), decision drafting, legal review, council-week freezes, and cross-case interactions. For Intervention D1 (pre-submission checker) and D7 (architect slots), a lightweight **pre-portal staging buffer** is included at the entry boundary to capture arrival smoothing and shifted applicant effort.
* **Out of scope:** Pre-design architectural work, internal consultant report authoring (modeled as exogenous stochastic delays), physical construction after permit issuance, and rare emergency hazard overrides removed from the historical log.

## Entities
1. **`PermitCase` (Primary dynamic entity):** An individual building-permit application progressing through workflow activities and synchronization barriers.
2. **`ArchitectSubmissionGroup` (Transient batch entity):** Links related applications submitted by the same `architect_id` within a rolling time window, inducing correlated arrivals, shared missing-document behavior, and inspection bundling eligibility.
3. **`InspectionTour` (Operational batch entity):** A daily itinerary for a specific inspector on a field day (Tue–Fri), grouping one or more scheduled inspections by `district` to amortize travel time.
4. **`NeighborhoodZone` (Spatial environment entity):** Sub-district spatial state tracking active public notices and latent objection waves that can simultaneously delay co-located `New residential` and `Complex variance` cases.
5. **`ResourceUnit` (Capacity entity):** Individual municipal staff members mapped to skill pools, shift schedules, task eligibilities, and non-permit duty blocks.

## State variables, queues, and calendars
* **Case-level state ($s_i(t)$):**
  * *Static attributes:* `case_id`, `case_type` $\in \{\text{Simple renovation, New residential, Commercial, Complex variance}\}$, `district` $\in \{D1..D5\}$, `architect_id` (flagged if top-20 high-volume), `application_value`, latent `prior_violations` flag, latent `env_sensitive_site` flag, `requires_fire`, `requires_environment`, `requires_public_notice`.
  * *Dynamic attributes:* `submission_time`, `current_active_nodes` (set of enabled parallel branches), `sync_barrier_status` (boolean completion flags for Planning, Fire, Env, Notice, Inspection prior to `Decision drafting`), `completeness_loop_count`, `inspection_rework_count`, `planning_legal_rework_count`, `has_objection` (latent indicator), `is_urgent`, `case_age(t)`.
* **System queues ($Q(t)$):**
  * Skill-partitioned priority/FIFO queues: $Q_{\text{clerk}}$, $Q_{\text{planner\_simple}}$, $Q_{\text{planner\_general}}$, $Q_{\text{fire}}$, $Q_{\text{env}}$, $Q_{\text{scheduler}}$, $Q_{\text{inspector}}[d]$ (indexed by district $d$), $Q_{\text{legal}}$.
  * External/time-delay holding sets (infinite-server delay states): $H_{\text{missing\_docs}}$, $H_{\text{public\_notice}}$, $H_{\text{appeal}}$.
* **Resource pools and calendars ($C_r(t)$):**
  * **Clerks (5 FTE):** Mon–Fri 08:00–16:00; on Mondays, 1 FTE is Masked/Unavailable for permit queues due to portal support (leaving 4 FTE during peak 2.2x Monday arrivals).
  * **Planners (8 FTE):** Mon–Fri 09:00–17:00; 2 FTE restricted strictly to `Simple renovation`, 6 FTE flexible across all case types (with aging-case reservation under D6).
  * **Fire reviewers (2 FTE eq.):** Active Tue–Thu standard hours; offline Mon and Fri.
  * **Environmental reviewers (3 FTE):** Mon–Fri standard hours, overlaid with stochastic/scheduled **non-permit duty blocks** consuming 40% of weekly hours (modeled as calendar unavailability rather than inflated service times).
  * **Inspectors (4 FTE):** Mon office/admin only; Tue–Fri field availability; capacity consumed by both `Site inspection` duration and sequence-dependent district `travel_time`.
  * **Legal officers (2 FTE):** Mon–Fri standard hours; blocked during scheduled **council-meeting weeks** and **public holidays** except for cases where `is_urgent == True`.
  * **System (`portal`, `system`):** 24/7, infinite capacity.

## Cross-case dependencies
1. **Architect burst & readiness correlation:** Top-20 architects generate compound batch arrivals; cases in the same `ArchitectSubmissionGroup` share correlated missing-document probabilities and synchronized applicant response behavior.
2. **Geographic inspection bundling:** Cases waiting in $Q_{\text{inspector}}[d]$ (especially sharing `architect_id` and `district`) can be grouped into a single `InspectionTour`. This saves inter-district travel time (notably in high-travel districts D3 and D5) at the cost of holding the earliest-ready case until a batch threshold or max-wait timer expires.
3. **Contagious neighborhood objections:** Active cases in the same `district`/sub-zone undergoing `Public-notice period` concurrently experience positively correlated objection probabilities (e.g., organized neighborhood opposition targeting adjacent filings).

# 2. Parameter-estimation plan

## Role of PM4Py for log and model extraction
PM4Py serves as the **offline analytical and structural extraction layer**, strictly decoupled from the runtime simulation engine:
1. **Ingestion & standardization:** Load raw CSV/Parquet extracts via `pandas`, standardize column types and UTC/local timezones with `pm4py.format_dataframe`.
2. **Control-flow & variant profiling:** Extract trace variants (`pm4py.get_variants_as_tuples`) and Directly-Follows Graphs (`pm4py.discover_dfg`) to quantify loop frequencies, rare exception paths (the 7% tail), and observed ordering of nominally parallel reviews.
3. **Empirical timing baselines:** Compute `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg` and case inter-arrival statistics (`pm4py.get_case_arrival_average`) stratified by season, day-of-week, and `case_type` to anchor sojourn-time targets.
4. **Conformance checking & model reconciliation:** Parse the normative process tree (`pm4py.parse_process_tree`), convert to a Petri net (`pm4py.convert_to_petri_net`), and run token-based replay or alignments (`pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments`) to detect deviations—Crucially, reconciling differences between the normative tree (which names explicit loop transitions like `'Completeness rework'` and places reviews before public notice) and the event log (which repeats `'Completeness check'` directly and records completion-only timestamps).

## Estimating specific model components
* **Non-stationary and bursty arrivals:** Decompose the arrival stream into (a) an independent background **Non-Homogeneous Poisson Process (NHPP)** with intensity $\lambda_{\text{bg}}(t) = \lambda_0 \cdot m_{\text{month}}(t) \cdot w_{\text{dow}}(t) \cdot h_{\text{hour}}(t)$ capturing the 1.7x spring/autumn peaks and 2.2x Monday effect, and (b) a **Compound NHPP / Hawkes-style cluster process** for top-20 `architect_id` entities (31% of volume), estimating batch-size distributions and intra-week submission lags via Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) on grouped architect timestamps.
* **Deconvolving service times vs. waiting times (Missing start timestamps):** Because human activities mostly log completion timestamps only, raw inter-event intervals $\Delta t_{A \to B} = t_{\text{complete}}(B) - t_{\text{complete}}(A)$ conflate queueing delay, off-shift calendar hours, and actual touch time. We avoid collapsing them by:
  1. Using the subset of logs *with* start timestamps plus SME/empirical anchors (the provided median and p90 service times) to fit parametric touch-time families (Log-normal or Gamma matching the 50th and 90th percentiles, e.g., `Planning review` median 3.5h, p90 14h).
  2. Running **calendar-aware resource timeline reconstruction** (bounding busy intervals between sequential completions by the same `org:resource` during active shift hours) to refine covariate multipliers (e.g., `Commercial` vs. `Simple renovation` review effort).
  3. Treating residual calendar-adjusted time before start as **queue waiting + synchronization delay**, which is an *emergent output* of the simulation used for calibration rather than a hardcoded input delay.
* **Branching probabilities and hidden routing drivers:** Train regularized logistic regression or gradient-boosted classification models for conditional routing (`requires_fire`, `requires_environment`, `requires_public_notice`, `Legal check` necessity, and `Reject application` vs. `Issue permit`) conditioned on observable features (`case_type`, `district`, `log(application_value)`, top-20 `architect_id`). Unobserved regulatory triggers (building area, environmental sensitivity) are captured in the calibrated stochastic residuals by district and value tier.
* **Rework loops:** Estimate geometric vs. state-dependent decay probabilities for loop counts ($P(\text{loop } k+1 \mid \text{loop } k, \text{case\_type}, \text{architect\_id})$). Differentiate `Completeness` loops (observed via `Request missing documents`), `Inspection` rework (repeated `Site inspection`), and `Planning/Legal/Appeal` rework (backward edges in the DFG). Fit shorter touch-time multipliers (e.g., $0.4\times$–$0.6\times$ initial touch time) for subsequent passes.
* **Latent objection mixture in Public-notice period:** Fit a two-component censored mixture model to `Public-notice period` sojourn times:
  $$f(d \mid x) = (1 - \pi_{\text{obj}}(x)) \cdot f_{\text{clean}}(d) + \pi_{\text{obj}}(x) \cdot f_{\text{obj}}(d)$$
  where $f_{\text{clean}}$ is concentrated just above the 14-day statutory minimum, $f_{\text{obj}}$ is a shifted Log-normal/Generalized Pareto capturing the 60-day p90 tail, and $\pi_{\text{obj}}(x)$ depends on `case_type`, `district`, and concurrent notices via an Expectation-Maximization (EM) routine.
* **Resource calendars & inspector travel:** Cross-check HR schedules against empirical timestamp heatmaps (`org:resource` $\times$ hour-of-week) to verify actual shift adherence, holiday blackouts, and council-meeting dates. Estimate district travel matrices ($\tau_{d_1, d_2}$, with elevated means for D3 and D5) from back-to-back same-day `Site inspection` completions by the same inspector.

# 3. Assumptions

* **Arrivals:** Baseline seasonal (1.7x Mar–May and Sep–Nov) and day-of-week (2.2x Monday) multipliers remain structurally stable across policies unless explicitly altered by D7 (architect slots) or stress scenarios. Case attributes (`case_type`, `district`, `application_value`) are drawn conditionally on the submitting architect profile (commercial/residential specialists vs. single-homeowners).
* **Service times:** Human touch times follow right-skewed parametric distributions (Log-normal/Weibull) parameterized by the empirical median and p90 summaries, conditioned on `case_type`. Active tasks interrupted by shift end are **preempt-resume** without rework penalty (work pauses at 17:00 and resumes next active shift).
* **Routing:** All applicable technical reviews (`Planning`, `Fire`, `Environmental`), `Public-notice period`, and `Site inspection` must complete at a synchronization join (`AND`-join for active branches) before `Decision drafting` begins. Because missing covariates (building floor area, prior violations) are unobserved, probabilistic eligibility conditioned on `application_value` and `district` faithfully proxies true statutory thresholds.
* **Resources/calendars:** HR FTE counts reflect net productive staff. Environmental reviewers' 40% non-permit allocation occurs as scheduled half-day/full-day blocks rather than uniform slowdowns. During council-meeting weeks, Legal officers process only urgent cases (assumed top 5–10% priority/aging exceptions). Planners prefer case continuity (the planner who did `Planning review` handles `Decision drafting` unless queued $> X$ days).
* **Rework:** Rework probabilities decrease after the first failure (applicants correct cited defects) unless an appeal occurs. Unstructured rework reasons are aggregated into routing probabilities without modeling granular text topics.
* **Applicant/third-party response:** `Receive missing documents` (median 5d, heavy-tailed), consultant inputs affecting `Environmental review`, and inspection site readiness are **exogenous infinite-server delays** unaffected by municipal staffing levels, clocked on continuous calendar days.
* **Cross-case dependencies:** Inspection bundling is restricted to cases within the same `district` ready within a configurable holding window; travel time per additional bundled inspection in the same district/architect group drops by an estimated fraction (e.g., 60–75% reduction vs. standalone trips). Neighborhood objection contagion decays exponentially with temporal separation between public-notice start dates in the same district.
* **Non-stationary demand:** Queue backlogs accumulated during Mar–May and Sep–Nov peaks carry over naturally into summer and winter troughs; the system never instantaneously resets to steady state.
* **Missing data:** Missing start timestamps do not systematically bias case ordering (FIFO within priority class holds on average). Excluded emergency decisions represent $<1\%$ of resource load and have negligible impact on standard permit queues.

# 5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty treatment

## Warm-up and horizon design
Because arrivals vary seasonally (1.7x peaks) and Complex variance cases take 140–260+ days, starting the digital twin empty (`WIP = 0`) creates severe downward bias in waiting times for the first several months, while standard steady-state truncation rules (e.g., Welch's method) do not strictly apply to periodic non-stationary systems.
* **Hybrid initialization:** Initialize at $t = -6\text{ months}$ (July 1) with an empirical **WIP state snapshot** sampled from historical active cases, run a **6-month warm-up** through one autumn peak and December holiday trough so queues, rework loops, and aging distributions equilibrate naturally, and then collect metrics over an **18-month evaluation horizon** (Jan 1 Year 1 to Jun 30 Year 2, matching the 18-month historical log profile).
* **Censoring treatment:** Track both *completed-case metrics* for all cases arriving within Months 1–12 (allowing up to 6+ months run-out to avoid right-censoring long-tail Complex variances) and *time-persistent queue/backlog metrics* across the full 18 months.

## Replications, seeds, and variance reduction
* **Macro-replications:** Run $R = 60$ independent replications per experiment (expanded if relative half-width of the 95% CI on p90 Complex duration exceeds 5%).
* **Common Random Numbers (CRN):** Partition pseudo-random number streams (`numpy.random.SeedSequence` child streams) strictly by source: Stream 1 for NHPP/architect arrivals, Stream 2 for intrinsic case attributes, Stream 3 for applicant missing-doc delays, Stream 4 for touch-time quantiles, Stream 5 for objections, and Stream 6 for routing uniforms. Thus, Intervention $D_k$ and Baseline $E_0$ encounter the *exact same* applicants and seasonal shocks in replication $r$, enabling **paired difference confidence intervals** ($\Delta_{k,0}^{(r)} = Y_k^{(r)} - Y_0^{(r)}$) with dramatically reduced variance.

## Input uncertainty and sensitivity analysis
Aleatory simulation noise is often smaller than **epistemic/input uncertainty** (inferred touch times, unobserved building area/sensitivity thresholds, reconstructed calendars).
* **Two-level nested uncertainty (Bayesian / Parametric Bootstrap):** Draw $M = 25$ parameter vectors $\theta_m \sim p(\theta \mid \text{Log, HR})$ across uncertain inputs (e.g., varying the service/waiting split ratio, objection mixture parameters, and D1/D8 behavioral adoption efficacy), running $R_{\text{inner}} = 10$ CRN replications per $\theta_m$ ($250$ total runs per top candidate policy). Report both **within-parameter 95% CIs** and **total predictive credible intervals** decomposing parameter vs. stochastic variance via ANOVA.
* **Global sensitivity analysis:** Apply Sobol indices or Morris elementary effects over uncertain nuisance parameters: (1) inspector travel savings per batched stop ($40\%\text{–}80\%$), (2) actual Environmental permit availability ($50\%\text{–}70\%$), (3) planner continuity strictness, and (4) missing-start service-time scale factor.

## Heavy-tailed duration treatment
* Never fit normal/exponential distributions or cap outliers at empirical maxima for `Receive missing documents`, `Environmental review`, `Public-notice period`, or `Appeal` delays.
* Fit **spliced/composite distributions** (Log-normal body up to the 85th percentile + **Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD)** tail estimated via Peaks-Over-Threshold, bounded only by statutory administrative expiration limits such as 365 days for abandoned missing-doc requests).
* Summarize outcomes using **order statistics (median, p80, p90, p95)** with non-parametric bootstrap/binomial CIs rather than sample means, which are unstable under heavy tails.

# 6. Validation strategy

1. **Face validation (SME walk-throughs):** Present animated queue traces, calendar heatmaps, and sample case timelines (e.g., Monday morning clerk spikes, council-week legal stalls, D3/D5 inspector tours) to municipal clerks, planners, inspectors, and legal officers to verify that dispatch logic and exception handling reflect real floor behavior.
2. **Event-log replay validation (Trace & structural fidelity):** Replay historical arrival timestamps and observed case attributes through the Baseline ($E_0$) routing engine. Verify that simulated **variant-group shares** stay within $\pm 2\text{ percentage points}$ of the empirical benchmark (e.g., Simple clean path $\approx 26\%$, Simple missing docs $\approx 19\%$, Inspection rework $\approx 9\%$) and compare DFG edge frequencies.
3. **Distributional validation (Cycle-time curves):** Compare simulated vs. empirical end-to-end duration distributions **stratified by `case_type` and variant group** across the full distribution—checking p50 and p90 targets (e.g., Simple clean 12d/24d; Commercial 68d/130d; Complex variance 140d/260d) using **Two-sample Wasserstein-1 (Earth Mover's) distance** and quantile relative error ($|\hat{q}_\tau - q_\tau|/q_\tau \le 10\text{–}15\%$), rather than relying on Kolmogorov-Smirnov p-values which over-reject at $N = 31,200$.
4. **Resource-utilization validation:** Cross-check simulated resource busy ratios against HR workload records and implied daily completion rates per FTE (e.g., ensuring planners are not unrealistically at 99% or 25% utilization, and verifying that Environmental reviewers' permit work fits within their 60% allocation).
5. **Bottleneck validation (Activity waiting signatures):** Compare simulated activity-level pre-service waiting medians against the empirical timing summary (e.g., verifying `Planning review` median wait $\approx 6\text{d}$, `Environmental review` $\approx 8\text{d}$, `Site inspection` $\approx 8\text{d}$, `Legal check` $\approx 7\text{d}$, and Monday `Completeness check` queue spikes). If end-to-end durations match but wait is concentrated at the wrong activity, recalibrate touch times and calendar constraints.
6. **Out-of-sample temporal validation:** Calibrate all parameters exclusively on **Months 1–12** of the historical log, freeze model parameters, and simulate **Months 13–18** (which includes the spring March–May 1.7x surge) driven only by Month 13–18 arrival volumes/case mix to verify that the twin accurately predicts backlog accumulation and recovery dynamics out of sample.

# 7. Decision criteria

Interventions are evaluated as a multi-criteria decision problem with **hard operational guardrails** (constraints that disqualify fragile or unfair policies) and **graded performance metrics**. No policy is claimed "globally optimal"; instead, we select the Pareto-undominated policy with minimum regret across parameter and stress uncertainty.

| Criterion Category | Operational Metric | Target / Improvement Goal | Hard Disqualification Guardrail |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Central & Tail Lead Time** | Median (p50) and p90 end-to-end duration by `case_type` (including pre-portal prep delay for D1/D7) | $\ge 15\%$ reduction in weighted p50 and $\ge 20\%$ reduction in p90 across all case types | Any `case_type` experiences $>5\%$ degradation in p90 duration vs. Baseline $E_0$ |
| **SLA Compliance** | % completed within statutory targets: Simple $\le 21\text{d}$, New Res $\le 60\text{d}$, Commercial $\le 90\text{d}$, Complex $\le 180\text{d}$ | Reach $\ge 85\%$ SLA compliance for Simple/New Res and $\ge 80\%$ for Commercial/Complex | SLA compliance drops below $E_0$ for any segment |
| **Spatial & Applicant Fairness** | (a) Max-min gap in median inspection/total wait between peripheral districts (D3, D5) and core (D1, D2, D4); (b) Wait ratio between one-off applicants and top-20 architects | Shrink D3/D5 inspection wait penalty by $\ge 25\%$ (via D3/D4); maintain neutral treatment across architect sizes | D3/D5 waiting time increases $>2\text{d}$ due to under-batched starvation, or small-applicant p90 worsens $>10\%$ |
| **Resource Utilization & Burnout** | Mean active-shift utilization $\rho_r$ per pool (excluding off-permit blocks) during peak months | Balanced $\rho_r \in [65\%, 82\%]$ across Clerks, Planners, Fire, Env, Inspectors, Legal | Any human pool exceeds $\rho_r > 88\%$ sustained over a 3-month peak (triggers non-linear queue instability & staff burnout) |
| **Process Quality & Rework** | Completeness loop rate, inspection rework rate, and downstream legal/planning conflict rate | Reduce completeness rework by $\ge 35\%$ (under D1); no spillover increase in downstream rejections | Downstream Legal rework increases $>3\%$ (e.g., from premature D5 pre-checks or overly aggressive D8 triage) or uninspected hazard proxy exceeds risk cap |
| **Backlog Stability** | End-of-peak WIP count and time-to-recover after Mar–May / Sep–Nov surges | Peak backlog reduced $\ge 20\%$; queues recover to baseline trough levels within 4 weeks post-peak | Structural drift: end-of-horizon WIP grows $>10\%$ YoY under Baseline arrival volume |
| **Stress Robustness** | 90th-percentile regret across stress scenarios S1–S3 and bootstrap parameter draws $\theta_m$ | Positive paired improvement ($\Delta \text{p90} < 0$ with $>90\%$ posterior probability) across S1, S2, and S3 | Catastrophic queue divergence (p90 $> 1.8\times$ Baseline) under S1 (+25% boom) or S2 (staff absence) |

class ProcessMiningAnalyzer:
    """Handles PM4Py formatting, discovery, process-tree parsing, and timing extraction."""

    def __init__(self, csv_source: io.StringIO | str):
        # Load via pandas (never using a nonexistent pm4py.read_csv)
        raw_df = pd.read_csv(csv_source)
        self.df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
            raw_df,
            case_id="case:concept:name",
            activity_key="concept:name",
            timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        )

    def parse_normative_tree(self, tree_str: str) -> ProcessTree:
        """
        Parses the process tree string with pm4py.parse_process_tree.
        Notes two critical domain limitations of the simplified tree:
        1. The tree specifies explicit redo activities ('Completeness rework',
           'Inspection rework'), whereas the event log (e.g., P001) loops silently
           back to 'Completeness check' after 'Receive missing documents' (tau redo).
        2. The tree omits backward rework arcs from Planning, Legal check, and Appeals.
        """
        normalized_str = " ".join(tree_str.split())
        tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(normalized_str)
        return tree

    def extract_process_diagnostics(self) -> Dict[str, object]:
        """Computes variants, frequency DFG, performance DFG, and arrival average."""
        variants = pm4py.get_variants_as_tuples(self.df)
        dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(self.df)
        perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(self.df)
        case_arrival_avg_sec = pm4py.get_case_arrival_average(self.df)

        return {
            "variants": variants,
            "dfg": dfg,
            "start_activities": start_acts,
            "end_activities": end_acts,
            "performance_dfg": perf_dfg,
            "case_arrival_avg_seconds": case_arrival_avg_sec,
        }

    def augment_timing_columns(
        self, empirical_median_service_sec: Optional[Dict[str, float]] = None
    ) -> pd.DataFrame:
        """
        Computes service_time and waiting_time columns if start_timestamp exists;
        otherwise computes observed sojourn_time (completion-to-completion) and
        documents the missing-start-timestamp limitation.
        """
        df_timed = self.df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"]).copy()
        prev_ts = df_timed.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].shift(1)
        df_timed["sojourn_seconds"] = (
            df_timed["time:timestamp"] - prev_ts
        ).dt.total_seconds().fillna(0.0)

        if "start_timestamp" in df_timed.columns and df_timed["start_timestamp"].notna().all():
            start_ts = pd.to_datetime(df_timed["start_timestamp"], utc=True)
            df_timed["service_seconds"] = (df_timed["time:timestamp"] - start_ts).dt.total_seconds()
            df_timed["waiting_seconds"] = (
                (start_ts - prev_ts).dt.total_seconds().clip(lower=0.0).fillna(0.0)
            )
            df_timed["timing_quality"] = "exact_from_start_ts"
        else:
            # Start timestamps are absent in the excerpt: sojourn_seconds conflates
            # queue wait, off-calendar hours, parallel branch completion lag, and touch time.
            df_timed["service_seconds"] = np.nan
            df_timed["waiting_seconds"] = np.nan
            df_timed["timing_quality"] = "completion_only_requires_deconvolution"

            if empirical_median_service_sec is not None:
                est_service = df_timed["concept:name"].map(empirical_median_service_sec).fillna(0.0)
                df_timed["approx_service_seconds"] = np.minimum(est_service, df_timed["sojourn_seconds"])
                df_timed["approx_waiting_and_calendar_seconds"] = (
                    df_timed["sojourn_seconds"] - df_timed["approx_service_seconds"]
                )

        return df_timed

class NonStationaryArrivalGenerator:
    """Generates seasonal + day-of-week NHPP arrivals and top-20 architect bursts."""

    def __init__(self, rng: np.random.Generator, config: PolicyConfig):
        self.rng = rng
        self.config = config

    def arrival_intensity_multiplier(self, dt: datetime) -> float:
        seasonal = 1.7 if dt.month in {3, 4, 5, 9, 10, 11} else 1.0
        if dt.weekday() == 0 and not self.config.smooth_architect_mondays:
            dow = 2.2  # Monday portal spike
        elif dt.weekday() < 5:
            dow = 1.15 if self.config.smooth_architect_mondays else 1.0
        else:
            dow = 0.25
        return seasonal * dow * self.config.arrival_scale_multiplier

    def sample_architect_batch(self, current_time: datetime, start_idx: int) -> List[PermitCase]:
        """Placeholder for compound burst generation when a top-20 architect submits."""
        is_top_architect = self.rng.random() < 0.31
        batch_size = int(self.rng.choice([1, 2, 3, 4], p=[0.4, 0.3, 0.2, 0.1])) if is_top_architect else 1
        arch_id = f"A_TOP_{self.rng.integers(1, 21)}" if is_top_architect else f"A_STD_{self.rng.integers(21, 500)}"
        district = str(self.rng.choice(["D1", "D2", "D3", "D4", "D5"]))

        batch: List[PermitCase] = []
        for k in range(batch_size):
            c_type = str(
                self.rng.choice(
                    ["Simple renovation", "New residential", "Commercial", "Complex variance"],
                    p=[0.45, 0.28, 0.17, 0.10],
                )
            )
            val = float(self.rng.lognormal(mean=10.5 if c_type == "Simple renovation" else 13.0, sigma=0.6))
            batch.append(
                PermitCase(
                    case_id=f"SIM_{start_idx + k:06d}",
                    submission_time=current_time + timedelta(minutes=15 * k),
                    case_type=c_type,
                    district=district,
                    architect_id=arch_id,
                    application_value=val,
                    requires_fire=(c_type in {"Commercial", "New residential"} and self.rng.random() < 0.65),
                    requires_environment=(c_type == "Commercial" or (district in {"D3", "D5"} and self.rng.random() < 0.3)),
                    requires_public_notice=(c_type == "Complex variance" or (c_type == "New residential" and self.rng.random() < 0.5)),
                    no_prior_violations=(self.rng.random() < 0.85),
                )
            )
        return batch


class ResourceCalendarManager:
    """Evaluates shift schedules, role restrictions, peak headcount, and council freezes."""

    def __init__(self, config: PolicyConfig, council_weeks: Set[int], holidays: Set[datetime.date]):
        self.config = config
        self.council_weeks = council_weeks
        self.holidays = holidays

    def available_fte(self, pool: str, dt: datetime, urgent_case: bool = False) -> int:
        if pool == "system":
            return 9999
        if dt.date() in self.holidays or dt.weekday() >= 5:
            return 0

        hour = dt.hour
        is_peak_month = dt.month in {3, 4, 5, 9, 10, 11}

        if pool == "clerks":
            if 8 <= hour < 16:
                # 1 of 5 clerks dedicated to portal support on Mondays
                return 4 if dt.weekday() == 0 else 5
            return 0
        if pool == "planners_simple_only":
            return 2 if 9 <= hour < 17 else 0
        if pool == "planners_general":
            return 6 if 9 <= hour < 17 else 0
        if pool == "fire":
            return 2 if (dt.weekday() in {1, 2, 3} and 9 <= hour < 17) else 0
        if pool == "environment":
            # 3 FTE with 40% non-permit duty calendar blocks (modeled e.g. Thu-Fri PM blocked)
            in_permit_block = not (dt.weekday() in {3, 4} and hour >= 12)
            return 3 if (9 <= hour < 17 and in_permit_block) else 0
        if pool == "inspectors_field":
            if dt.weekday() in {1, 2, 3, 4} and 8 <= hour < 16:
                return 5 if (self.config.peak_temp_inspector and is_peak_month) else 4
            return 0
        if pool == "legal":
            iso_week = dt.isocalendar()[1]
            if iso_week in self.council_weeks and not urgent_case:
                return 0
            return 2 if 9 <= hour < 17 else 0
        return 0

    def district_travel_hours(self, district: str, batched_stop: bool) -> float:
        base_travel = {"D1": 0.4, "D2": 0.5, "D3": 1.1, "D4": 0.5, "D5": 1.25}[district]
        return base_travel * (0.35 if batched_stop else 1.0)


class RoutingAndDependencyEngine:
    """Governs conditional branching, rework loops, synchronization barriers, and policy rules."""

    def __init__(self, rng: np.random.Generator, config: PolicyConfig):
        self.rng = rng
        self.config = config

    def needs_missing_docs(self, case: PermitCase) -> bool:
        base_p = 0.38 * (0.5 ** case.completeness_loops)
        if self.config.pre_submission_checker:
            base_p *= 0.45
        return bool(self.rng.random() < base_p)

    def requires_site_inspection(self, case: PermitCase) -> bool:
        if (
            self.config.skip_low_risk_simple_inspection
            and case.case_type == "Simple renovation"
            and case.application_value < 50_000
            and case.no_prior_violations
        ):
            return False
        return True

    def required_sync_branches(self, case: PermitCase) -> Set[str]:
        required = {"Planning review"}
        if case.requires_fire:
            required.add("Fire-safety review")
        if case.requires_environment:
            required.add("Environmental review")
        if case.requires_public_notice:
            required.add("Public-notice period")
        if self.requires_site_inspection(case):
            required.add("Site inspection")
        return required

    def ready_for_decision_drafting(self, case: PermitCase) -> bool:
        return self.required_sync_branches(case).issubset(case.completed_branches)

    def sample_public_notice_days(self, case: PermitCase, active_district_notices: int) -> float:
        """Models unobserved objections via a latent spatial-contagion mixture."""
        contagion_boost = min(0.25, 0.03 * active_district_notices)
        base_obj_prob = 0.35 if case.case_type == "Complex variance" else 0.12
        case.has_latent_objection = bool(self.rng.random() < (base_obj_prob + contagion_boost))
        if not case.has_latent_objection:
            return 14.0 + float(self.rng.exponential(0.5))
        tail_scale = 0.65 if self.config.objection_early_warning else 1.0
        return 14.0 + tail_scale * float(self.rng.lognormal(mean=3.2, sigma=0.55))


class DigitalTwinExperimentRunner:
    """Coordinates warm-up, CRN streams across policies, and KPI aggregation."""

    def __init__(self, fitted_diagnostics: Dict[str, object]):
        self.fitted_diagnostics = fitted_diagnostics

    def run_single_replication(self, config: PolicyConfig, seed: int) -> Dict[str, float]:
        # Independent child RNG streams preserve Common Random Numbers across interventions
        ss = np.random.SeedSequence(seed)
        rng_arrivals, rng_routing = [np.random.default_rng(s) for s in ss.spawn(2)]

        arrival_gen = NonStationaryArrivalGenerator(rng_arrivals, config)
        routing = RoutingAndDependencyEngine(rng_routing, config)
        calendars = ResourceCalendarManager(config, council_weeks={4, 8, 12, 16}, holidays=set())

        # Placeholder event-loop execution over 6m warm-up + 18m evaluation window
        t0 = datetime(2026, 1, 5, 9, 0)
        sample_batch = arrival_gen.sample_architect_batch(t0, start_idx=1)
        monday_clerks = calendars.available_fte("clerks", t0)
        needs_insp = routing.requires_site_inspection(sample_batch[0])

        return {
            "monday_available_clerks": float(monday_clerks),
            "first_case_inspected": float(needs_insp),
            "simulated_p50_days": np.nan,  # Populated by discrete-event loop
            "simulated_p90_days": np.nan,
        }

    def compare_interventions(
        self, policies: List[PolicyConfig], n_replications: int = 50, master_seed: int = 2026
    ) -> pd.DataFrame:
        base_seeds = [master_seed + r for r in range(n_replications)]
        records = []
        for policy in policies:
            for rep_idx, rep_seed in enumerate(base_seeds):
                kpis = self.run_single_replication(policy, seed=rep_seed)
                records.append({"policy_id": policy.policy_id, "rep": rep_idx, **kpis})
        return pd.DataFrame(records)
