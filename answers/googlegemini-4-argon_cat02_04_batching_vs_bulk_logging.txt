# 3. Consequences for performance analysis

Misinterpreting logging mechanisms or shared identifiers as literal physical execution introduces severe, directional biases across standard process-mining metrics:
- **Waiting Time:** $W_i = start_i - queue\_arrival_i$
- **Service (Processing) Time:** $S_i = complete_i - start_i$
- **Sojourn (Cycle) Time:** $T_i = complete_i - queue\_arrival_i = W_i + S_i$
- **Resource Utilization:** $U_r = \frac{\text{Total Active Time of } r}{\text{Observation Window}}$ (or naive $\sum S_i / \text{Window}$)

### Cluster-by-Cluster Bias Mechanisms

1. **G1 (Genuine simultaneous batch on 4-item fixture):**
   - *Waiting Time:* Unbiased (2 to 6 mins; mean 4.25 mins).
   - *Service Time:* Unbiased at the *item* level (each item spends 3 mins in the cell), **but** if summed naively across 4 cases ($4 \times 3 = 12$ resource-mins), resource effort is **overestimated by 400%** (actual machine active time is 3 mins).
   - *Utilization & Bottleneck:* Naive summation inflates RBT-7 utilization by $4\times$, creating a false bottleneck. Union-of-intervals utilization correctly yields 3 mins of active work.

2. **G2 (Start batching / common setup via "begin group" button):**
   - *Waiting Time:* **Underestimated** for S02, S03, and S04. Because S01–S04 all record $start = 10:00$, recorded waiting is 18, 14, 11, and 6 mins (mean 12.25 mins). Physically, S02 waited until 10:08 (22 mins), S03 until 10:14 (25 mins), and S04 until 10:21 (27 mins). True mean waiting is **23.0 mins** (underestimated by ~47%).
   - *Service Time:* **Severely overestimated** for later items in the batch. Recorded service times are 8, 14, 21, and 27 mins (mean 17.5 mins, sum 70 mins). True sequential service times ($complete_i - complete_{i-1}$) are 8, 6, 7, and 6 mins (mean **6.75 mins**, sum 27 mins).
   - *Utilization & Bottleneck:* Naive $\sum S_i$ yields 70 mins of work in a 27-min window (259% utilization!), flagging T-B as an extreme bottleneck with heavy multitasking. Union-of-intervals gives the correct 27 mins of active work.

3. **G3 (End batching / bulk folder submission at 11:30):**
   - *Waiting Time:* **Unbiased** (assuming `start` was logged when each file was opened: 22, 18, 19, 19 mins; mean 19.5 mins).
   - *Service Time:* **Severely overestimated** for early items in the folder. Recorded service times are 28, 20, 13, and 6 mins (mean 16.75 mins, sum 67 mins) because early items sit "open" until the 11:30 batch submit. True sequential service times ($start_{i+1} - start_i$) are 8, 7, 7, and 6 mins (mean **7.0 mins**, sum 28 mins).
   - *Utilization & Bottleneck:* Naive $\sum S_i$ (67 mins in a 28-min window) inflates utilization by $2.4\times$. Furthermore, throughput appears spiky (zero completions for 28 mins, then 4 instantaneous completions at 11:30).

4. **G4 (Sequential batching / back-to-back flow):**
   - *Waiting Time:* Unbiased (modulo $\pm 30\text{ s}$ minute-rounding), growing linearly from 7 mins (P01) to 19 mins (P05) as later arrivals wait for earlier items in the batch.
   - *Service Time:* Unbiased (4.0 mins per item, 20 mins total).
   - *Utilization & Bottleneck:* Unbiased; T-D is 100% utilized from 12:05 to 12:25. High waiting time for P05 is correctly explained by serial queueing behind P01–P04.

5. **G5 (Shared-account artifact `analyst_pool`):**
   - *Waiting & Service Time:* Unbiased at the individual case level ($W_i \in [15, 16]$ mins, $S_i \in [10, 25]$ mins, total work = 70 analyst-mins).
   - *Utilization & Bottleneck:* **Distorted.** If `analyst_pool` is treated as 1 worker, naive utilization over the 30-min active window (13:00–13:30) is $70 / 30 = 233\%$, or union-of-intervals utilization is $30 / 30 = 100\%$. In reality, with 3 analysts (capacity = 90 analyst-mins over 30 mins), true pool utilization is $70 / 90 = 77.8\%$ (or $70 / 120 = 58.3\%$ if 4 analysts). Treating a pool as one person hides true capacity and distorts queueing models ($M/M/1$ vs $M/M/c$).

6. **G6 (End-of-shift bulk data entry — Explicit Mechanism Analysis):**
   - *Why G6 creates BOTH inflated waiting and near-zero service:* In G6, tickets arrive between 14:05 and 16:25. The worker processes them during the afternoon (or closes them in a batch at shift end) using `manual_bulk_form` at 17:00, which sets both `start` and `complete` to the form submission timestamp (`17:00`).
     1. **Inflated Waiting Time:** Because the true execution start $t_{\text{start, true}}$ was never recorded and was overwritten by $t_{\text{submit}} = 17:00$, the entire elapsed time between arrival and shift-end submission—including both *true queue waiting*, *true service time*, and *post-completion administrative lag*—is dumped into the waiting time formula:
        $$W_{\text{recorded}, i} = 17:00 - queue\_arrival_i = W_{\text{true}, i} + S_{\text{true}, i} + \text{Lag}_{\text{admin}, i}$$
        This yields a massive apparent mean wait of **108.9 minutes** (ranging from 35 mins for C07 up to 175 mins for C01).
     2. **Near-Zero Service Time:** Because the bulk form populates `start` = `complete` = `17:00`, the recorded service time collapses identically to zero:
        $$S_{\text{recorded}, i} = 17:00 - 17:00 = 0\text{ mins}$$
     3. **Downstream Bottleneck Distortion:** Queueing analysts looking at G6 will see an extreme waiting-time bottleneck (nearly 2 hours of wait!) paired with 0% resource utilization (0 mins of service time) and infinite instantaneous throughput at 17:00. This leads to the false diagnosis of "starvation / unstaffed queue with trivial work effort," when in reality work effort is simply unobserved.

7. **G7 (Timestamp coarsening on 15-min grid):**
   - *Waiting Time:* **Biased and produces negative values (causal violations).** Because `start` is rounded down to `14:00` while upstream `queue_arrival` has 1-min precision (13:58 to 14:11), recorded waiting times are $+2, -1, -4, -7, -11$ mins (mean **$-4.2$ mins**). Negative waiting corrupts conformance checking and queueing distributions.
   - *Service Time:* **Overestimated.** Every item is snapped to `14:00`–`14:15` (15 mins each; 75 mins total). Yet `recorded_at` ticks up by 1 min per item (14:16, 14:17, 14:18, 14:19, 14:20), indicating sequential processing taking ~1 min per item (or ~3 mins per item across the 15-min window).
   - *Utilization:* Naive sum gives 75 mins of work in 15 mins ($500\%$); true active time is $\le 20$ mins.

8. **G8 (Queue accumulation during safety check 15:00–15:35):**
   - *Waiting Time:* Numerically accurate as *total delay* (38, 43, 48, 54, 58 mins; mean **48.2 mins**), **but attributionally biased** if unavailability is ignored. Each item waited ~30–38 mins due to *planned downtime* (safety check) and only 0–28 mins due to *serial congestion* behind preceding items.
   - *Service Time:* Unbiased (exact 7.0 mins per item, 35 mins total).
   - *Bottleneck Detection:* Without downtime logs, T-H is flagged as a chronic capacity bottleneck with 48-min average waits, prompting unnecessary staffing increases when the true driver was a one-off 35-min safety stoppage.

# 5. Compute independent diagnostics in pandas
def compute_cluster_diagnostics(group):
  n = len(group)
  starts = group["start_dt"].values
  ends = group["complete_dt"].values
  arrs = group["queue_arrival_dt"].values
  recs = group["recorded_at_dt"].values

  # Pairwise overlap rate (including zero-duration point equality at same timestamp)
  overlap_pairs = 0
  adjacent_pairs = 0
  total_pairs = n * (n - 1) // 2
  for i in range(n):
    for j in range(i + 1, n):
      # Check strict interval overlap or identical point timestamps
      s_max = max(starts[i], starts[j])
      e_min = min(ends[i], ends[j])
      if s_max < e_min or (
          starts[i] == starts[j] and ends[i] == ends[j]
      ):
        overlap_pairs += 1
      if ends[i] == starts[j] or ends[j] == starts[i]:
        adjacent_pairs += 1
  overlap_rate = (
      overlap_pairs / total_pairs if total_pairs > 0 else 0.0
  )

  # Start / End equality groups (size of largest shared timestamp group)
  max_start_eq = group["start_dt"].value_counts().max()
  max_end_eq = group["complete_dt"].value_counts().max()

  # Recording lag in seconds
  rec_lags_sec = (
      group["recorded_at_dt"] - group["complete_dt"]
  ).dt.total_seconds()
  mean_rec_lag_s = rec_lags_sec.mean()
  rec_distinct = group["recorded_at_dt"].nunique()

  # Waiting & Service times (minutes)
  wait_mins = (
      group["start_dt"] - group["queue_arrival_dt"]
  ).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
  serv_mins = (
      group["complete_dt"] - group["start_dt"]
  ).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
  causal_violations = (
      wait_mins < 0
  ).sum()  # start < queue_arrival

  # Queue build-up at first start: how many items arrived before start.min()
  first_start = group["start_dt"].min()
  queue_at_first_start = (
      group["queue_arrival_dt"] <= first_start
  ).sum()

  # Granularity check: do start/complete contain non-zero seconds or non-15m multiples?
  has_sec_start_end = (
      (group["start_dt"].dt.second != 0)
      | (group["complete_dt"].dt.second != 0)
  ).any()
  all_15m_grid = (
      (group["start_dt"].dt.minute % 15 == 0)
      & (group["complete_dt"].dt.minute % 15 == 0)
      & (~has_sec_start_end)
  ).all()
  granularity_flag = (
      "15m-grid"
      if all_15m_grid
      else ("1s-actual" if has_sec_start_end else "1m-actual")
  )

  act_res = (group["activity"].iloc[0], group["resource"].iloc[0])

  return pd.Series({
      "res": group["resource"].iloc[0],
      "n": n,
      "ovlp_rate": round(overlap_rate, 2),
      "adj_pairs": adjacent_pairs,
      "eq_start/end": f"{max_start_eq}/{max_end_eq}",
      "wait_m(mean/min)": (
          f"{wait_mins.mean():.1f}/{wait_mins.min():.0f}"
      ),
      "serv_m(mean)": f"{serv_mins.mean():.1f}",
      "rec_lag_s(mean)": f"{mean_rec_lag_s:.1f}",
      "rec_uniq": rec_distinct,
      "q_build": f"{queue_at_first_start}/{n}",
      "causal_err": causal_violations,
      "obs_gran": granularity_flag,
      "pm4py(md=0,mbs=2)": batch_results["md=0s, mbs=2"].get(
          act_res, "None"
      ),
      "pm4py(md=0,mbs=5)": batch_results["md=0s, mbs=5"].get(
          act_res, "None"
      ),
      "pm4py(md=900,mbs=2)": batch_results["md=900s, mbs=2"].get(
          act_res, "None"
      ),
  })


diag_df = df.groupby("cluster", as_index=True).apply(
    compute_cluster_diagnostics
)
print(diag_df.to_markdown())

# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

### Axis 1: Resource IDs Trusted vs. Treated as Shared Accounts
- **If Resource IDs are strictly trusted as single individuals:**
  - **G5 (`analyst_pool`)** must be interpreted as a single hyper-multitasking analyst juggling 4 root-cause analyses simultaneously between 13:12 and 13:20. Service times (10–25 mins) would be heavily inflated by context-switching and suspended work time.
  - **G2 (`T-B`) and G3 (`T-C`)** could also be interpreted as single-worker multitasking across 4 items, rather than UI start-batching or end-batching.
- **If Resource IDs are treated as potentially shared accounts (station/terminal logins):**
  - **G5** immediately resolves into 3–4 independent analysts working in parallel on individual cases with zero or minimal multitasking.
  - **G1 (`RBT-7`)** could represent 4 parallel labeling heads operating independently under one PLC controller ID.
  - **G2 (`T-B`) and G3 (`T-C`)** *could* theoretically represent 4 inspectors sharing a single tablet (`T-B`) who all started at shift/hour start (`10:00`) and finished at different speeds (8, 14, 21, 27 mins), or 4 reviewers sharing a desktop login (`T-C`) who started as items arrived and all logged off together at `11:30`. However, in G2, the steady ~6.75-min cadence between completions strongly favors 1 human working serially after pressing "begin group," and in G3, the steady ~7-min cadence between starts (`11:02, 11:10, 11:17, 11:24`) strongly favors 1 human opening files sequentially.

### Axis 2: Timestamps Exact vs. Interval-Censored by Stated Precision
If a timestamp $t$ with stated precision $\Delta$ represents an interval $[t - \Delta, t + \Delta]$ (or $[t, t + \Delta)$ under floor rounding):
- **G1 & G4 & G8 (Stated `1 sec`, Observed `1 min` on `start`/`complete`):** Despite `1 sec` source capability (proven by `recorded_at` having `:02`–`:06` seconds), `start` and `complete` were truncated/formatted to whole minutes (`:00`). Treating them as interval-censored on $[t, t+60\text{s})$ means:
  - In **G1**, the 4 items might not have started at the exact same millisecond, or could even be rapid sequential operations taking $45\text{ s}$ each across the 3-minute span ($4 \times 45\text{ s} = 180\text{ s} = 3\text{ mins}$, e.g., 09:00:00 to 09:03:00)! However, `recorded_at` shows L01–L04 were all committed at `09:03:02`–`09:03:03`, which refutes sequential completions at 09:00:45, 09:01:30, etc., and confirms they finished together at 09:03.
  - In **G4 and G8**, adjacency ($complete_i = start_{i+1}$) holds up to $\pm 60\text{ s}$ censoring; small physical handoff gaps (2–10 seconds) are masked by minute formatting.
- **G7 (Stated `15 min` precision):** Every `14:00` start and `14:15` completion is censored over a 15-minute window. Once treated as interval-censored:
  - The apparent causal violation ($start = 14:00 < queue\_arrival = 14:11$ for M05) vanishes because M05's true start $t_{\text{start, M05}} \in [14:11, 14:15]$ rounds down to `14:00`.
  - The apparent simultaneous batch completely dissolves into sequential execution inside $[13:58, 14:20]$.

### Three Cases Where the Same Temporal Pattern Supports Multiple Physical Explanations

1. **Identical Start and Identical Complete ($start_i = start_0, \; complete_i = complete_0$) — Seen in G1, G6, G7:**
   - *Physical Explanation A (True Simultaneous Batch — G1):* Multi-item fixture or oven processes $k$ items together.
   - *Physical Explanation B (Coarse Grid Rounding of Serial Work — G7):* Fast sequential tasks (1–3 mins each) occur within a single 15-min clock bucket (`14:00`–`14:15`) and snap to the bucket boundaries.
   - *Physical Explanation C (Deferred Bulk Administrative Entry — G6):* Items are completed over several hours, but a user or script closes all tickets in one bulk transaction (`17:00`), copying the submission time into both `start` and `complete`.

2. **Identical Start with Staggered Completions ($start_1 = \dots = start_k, \; complete_1 < \dots < complete_k$) — Seen in G2:**
   - *Physical Explanation A (Start Batching / Single Setup + Serial Execution):* One worker stages 4 items, clicks "begin group" at `10:00`, and inspects them one by one (taking 8, 6, 7, and 6 mins respectively).
   - *Physical Explanation B (Parallel Execution with Heterogeneous Task Complexity):* Four inspectors under a shared station login (or 1 inspector running 4 automated test rigs) start 4 items truly in parallel at `10:00`, where S01 is an easy item (8 mins) and S04 is a complex item (27 mins).
   - *Disambiguation:* Uniform inter-completion spacing ($\approx 6.7\text{ mins}$ between every consecutive completion) strongly favors serial execution of homogeneous items over parallel execution of coincidentally linearly-spaced complex items.

3. **Zero-Gap Back-to-Back Execution after a Long Wait ($arrival_i \ll start_1$, then $complete_i = start_{i+1}$) — Seen in G4 and G8:**
   - *Physical Explanation A (Queue Accumulation During Unplanned/Planned Downtime — G8):* Resource is blocked by a safety check or machine breakdown (15:00–15:38); once restored, the operator works continuously through the backlog.
   - *Physical Explanation B (Deliberate Threshold/Pull Batching Policy — G4/G8):* The operator intentionally waits until a batch of $k=5$ items accumulates in the input bin before switching over to the packing/triage station to amortize cognitive or physical setup cost.
   - *Disambiguation:* Event logs alone cannot separate downtime from a deliberate batch-accumulation threshold of $k=5$ without external availability/downtime logs (like the 15:00–15:35 safety check record).

# 7. Final adjudication

Based on the combined evidence from PM4Py temporal batch detection, `recorded_at` commit lags, `queue_arrival` ordering, and operational context, we classify clusters G1–G8 into three actionable governance tiers:

### Tier 1: Interpret with High Confidence (Ready for Process & Queue Modeling)
- **G1 (RBT-7 — Genuine Simultaneous Batch):** High confidence. Model as a simultaneous batch of 4 items on a 4-item fixture. Use union-of-intervals (3 mins) for machine utilization and 3 mins for item cycle time.
- **G4 (T-D — Sequential Batching / Back-to-Back Flow):** High confidence. Model as strict serial processing (4 mins/item, $\pm 30\text{ s}$ rounding bounds). Waiting time and service time are reliable.
- **G2 (T-B — Start Batching + Serial Execution) & G3 (T-C — Serial Execution + End Batching):** High confidence *after deterministic timestamp reconstruction*. Because `recorded_at` in G2 proves sequential completion (`10:09, 10:15, 10:22, 10:28`) and `start` in G3 proves sequential initiation (`11:02, 11:10, 11:17, 11:24`), reconstructing sequential handoffs ($start_i = complete_{i-1}$ in G2; $complete_i = start_{i+1}$ in G3) yields clean, reliable service times (6–8 mins/item) and true waiting times.

### Tier 2: Exclude or Interval-Censor in Queue Analysis (Severe Logging Distortion)
- **G6 (T-F — End-of-Shift Bulk Data Entry):** **Exclude from waiting-time and service-time KPIs.** Both `start` and `complete` (`17:00`) are administrative submission artifacts. Retain `17:00` only as an upper bound on completion ($complete_{\text{true}} \le 17:00$) for coarse daily throughput counting.
- **G7 (T-G — 15-Minute Timestamp Coarsening):** **Interval-censor or reconstruct from `recorded_at`.** Raw `start` (`14:00`) and `complete` (`14:15`) must not be used directly because they generate negative waiting times ($-1$ to $-11$ mins) and $5\times$ inflated service times. Either treat timestamps as interval-censored on $15\text{-min}$ bounds or use the 1-minute increments in `recorded_at` (`14:16`–`14:20`) to approximate serial completion times (~1–3 mins/item).

### Tier 3: Require Operational Validation Before Performance Conclusions
- **G5 (`analyst_pool` — Shared-Account Concurrency):** **Requires staffing validation.** Individual case waiting times (15–16 mins) and durations (10–25 mins) are valid, but resource utilization and queueing capacity cannot be computed without confirming how many physical analysts were logged into `analyst_pool` between 13:00 and 13:30 (specifically whether 4 analysts were present at 13:12–13:20, or 3 analysts with 1 multitasking).
- **G8 (T-H — Post-Downtime Sequential Catch-Up):** **Requires clock-offset and policy validation.** Serial service time (7 mins/item) is highly reliable, but before attributing the 38–58 min waiting times to either planned safety checks or deliberate batch accumulation, investigators must verify the clock skew between the safety system (`15:00`–`15:35`) and the scanner (`15:38` start).
