## 1. Materiality Framework

Severity is assessed on a 1–5 scale independent of raw alignment cost:

1 = Harmless technical noise or fully fitting silent/model-internal moves with no control impact (UI telemetry, transport retries, tau routing).  
2 = Model incompleteness or labeling/mapping issues for out-of-scope or synonym activities where the business outcome was correct and exposure low (denied/withdrawn cases, post-hoc label mapping).  
3 = Missing core-system evidence for a required activity where external or alternative evidence exists, or minor timing/notification issues; exposure moderate; reversibility high or impact limited.  
4 = Data-aware policy violations (P1/P2) that the control-flow tree permits, or incomplete fraud/approval evidence on elevated-risk or elevated-amount claims; potential regulatory/customer exposure; partial reversibility.  
5 = Actual operational control failures that defeat a key objective (especially P3 pay-before-approval), high financial exposure, recent bank-change or HIGH risk without reliable fraud screening, low reversibility, or high recurrence potential with weak evidence.

Factors weighted in severity:  
- Control objective defeated (fraud prevention, dual control/approval before payment, timely accurate notification, coverage validation).  
- Amount / financial exposure and risk rating (HIGH or >EUR 50k elevates).  
- Reversibility (payment already executed is harder than pre-payment deviation).  
- Timing/order violations vs pure absence.  
- Customer or regulatory harm potential (notification SLA, incorrect payee via bank change).  
- Recurrence frequency from additional pattern evidence.  
- Model scope disputes (P6) lower severity for pure out-of-scope paths.  
- Evidence reliability modulates confidence, not the severity score itself.

Confidence (High / Medium / Low) is orthogonal to severity:  
- High: clear log + policy match/mismatch, stable identifiers, consistent with additional month-level patterns.  
- Medium: external documents or CRM links exist but linkage is imperfect (mutable fields, post-case mapping updates).  
- Low: verbal claims only, disputed scope ownership, or missing stable document-to-case ID.

Severity reflects potential business harm assuming the alignment semantics; confidence reflects how reliably we know the facts from the supplied evidence. A high-severity case can have low confidence (e.g., verbal fraud screen) and still warrant investigation; a low-severity noise case has high confidence.

Zero-cost alignments can still be severity 4 when they represent data-aware policy failures the Petri-net tree cannot see. Silent T(tau,c=0) moves are treated as fitting and severity 1.

## 3. Repeated Deviation Patterns

**Pattern A: Retry/duplicate Notify Claimant (harmless technical noise)**  
- Frequency: CL-603 + 74 month-wide cases with L(Notify Claimant) immediately after S(Notify) sharing same message ID; all tied to one retrying connector.  
- Business risk: Negligible; notification occurred (P4 satisfied).  
- Likely cause: Transport-layer retry / connector misconfiguration.  
- Confidence: High.  
- Owner: Integration / middleware team.

**Pattern B: Data-aware Auto Approve policy failures (zero-cost tree fits but P1/P2 violated)**  
- Frequency: CL-609, CL-610 + two additional cases (total 4 fully synchronous Auto paths violating P1 or P2).  
- Business risk: High. Auto Approve bypasses required Fraud Screen + Manual for elevated amount/risk/bank-change. Irreversible once paid.  
- Likely cause: Process tree encodes only control-flow; data conditions not enforced.  
- Confidence: High.  
- Owner: Process owner + IT for data-aware guards.

**Pattern C: External adjuster / missing Assess Loss in core log**  
- Frequency: CL-606 + 9 high-value cases with M(Assess Loss) and external licensed-adjuster document; 2 lack stable document-to-case identifier.  
- Business risk: Medium-High on high-value claims (audit-trail incompleteness).  
- Likely cause: External adjusters outside core logging; no automated import.  
- Confidence: Medium-High for occurrence; lower for linkage.  
- Owner: Claims operations + document management / integration.

**Pattern D: Out-of-scope denied and withdrawn claims (model incompleteness)**  
- Frequency: CL-607, CL-612 + majority of alignments with raw_cost ≥ 4.  
- Business risk: Low operational (no payment); high diagnostic noise and scope dispute (P6).  
- Likely cause: Tree designed only for payable claims; no Deny/Withdraw branches.  
- Confidence: High on mismatch.  
- Owner: Process owner + model steward (resolve P6).

**Pattern E: Missing mandatory Fraud Screen on high-risk / high-value / bank-change cases**  
- Frequency: CL-602 (and related to Pattern B).  
- Business risk: Critical (P2 violation + high exposure + bank change + weak evidence).  
- Likely cause: Verbal/informal check or system allowed Manual Approve without prior Fraud Screen event.  
- Confidence: Medium.  
- Owner: Fraud / compliance + claims control (immediate investigation of CL-602).

**Pattern F: Pay-before-approval timing violation (P3 severe control failure)**  
- Frequency: CL-604.  
- Business risk: Severe (irreversible funds movement without authorization).  
- Likely cause: Payment system not gated on approval status; race or override.  
- Confidence: High (explicit timestamps).  
- Owner: Claims payment controls + system architecture.

**Pattern G: Label / activity-name mismatch**  
- Frequency: CL-611.  
- Business risk: Low (activity occurred).  
- Likely cause: Terminology inconsistency; delayed mapping-table update.  
- Confidence: High.  
- Owner: Data governance / process-mining data steward.

**Pattern H: Missing Notify in core with external CRM evidence of uncertain quality**  
- Frequency: CL-608.  
- Business risk: Medium (P4 time satisfied but mutable linkage weakens audit).  
- Likely cause: Notification in CRM/portal without immutable core correlation.  
- Confidence: Medium.  
- Owner: Customer communications + CRM/core integration.

**Pattern I: Pure UI telemetry noise**  
- Frequency: CL-601 (likely common).  
- Business risk: None (P5).  
- Likely cause: UI instrumentation of non-business events.  
- Confidence: High.  
- Owner: Process-mining filter configuration.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

Missing log event is never treated as proof that an activity did not occur.

**1. CL-602 / Pattern E (missing Fraud Screen)**  
- H1: Formal Fraud Screen never performed; verbal claim is post-hoc.  
- H2: Screen occurred off-system or in non-logged tool; evidence not retained/correlated.  
- Discriminators: Search secondary fraud-tool logs, email/chat, contemporaneous notes around Manual Approve; check whether Manual Approve UI currently enforces Fraud Screen prerequisite; review other HIGH/bank-change cases.

**2. CL-604 / Pattern F (Pay before Manual Approve)**  
- H1: Payment system not hard-gated; race/override allowed Pay before approval recorded.  
- H2: Approval occurred earlier or via secondary channel; core timestamp delayed.  
- Discriminators: Cross-system timestamps and transaction IDs (payment gateway vs approval workflow); user session logs of approver; presence of any force-pay/override flag; NTP/clock consistency.

**3. Pattern B / CL-609 & CL-610 (Auto Approve despite P1/P2)**  
- H1: Runtime routing ignores amount/risk/bank-change data (structure-only).  
- H2: Data attributes were incorrect/stale at decision time.  
- Discriminators: Reconstruct exact attribute values at Auto Approve timestamp; examine decision-rule/code that enables Auto path; compare bank-change event time vs Auto time for CL-610.

**4. Pattern C / CL-606 (M(Assess Loss) + external report)**  
- H1: Assessment performed only externally; no core activity.  
- H2: Core Assess Loss occurred but was not logged (or logged under different label).  
- Discriminators: Search core activity/user-session logs around report time; verify document-to-case ID stability and any integration job that should create core events; for the 2 unstable-ID cases, forensic linkage via claimant/policy numbers.

**5. Pattern D / CL-607 & CL-612 (Deny / Withdraw)**  
- H1: Model scope intentionally limited to payable claims; cases should be filtered.  
- H2: Denied/withdrawn are in-scope intake; tree must be extended.  
- Discriminators: Formal scope document / RACI between process owner and model steward; volume of denied/withdrawn vs payable; stakeholder decision on whether fitness metrics should penalize these paths.

**6. Pattern H / CL-608 (M(Notify) + CRM message)**  
- H1: Notification performed solely in CRM/portal; core never generated event.  
- H2: Core notification attempted but failed to persist; CRM is residual trace.  
- Discriminators: CRM message metadata (immutable ID, delivery receipt, sender); core notification service logs near +3 h; whether architecture is supposed to write core event on portal send; change history of the mutable link field.

**7. Pattern G / CL-611 (Assess Damage vs Assess Loss)**  
- H1: Pure label synonymy; activity identical.  
- H2: Distinct activities; required Assess Loss truly skipped.  
- Discriminators: Compare data payloads/fields updated; UI labels vs logged names at case time; exact timing of mapping-table update; frequency of “Assess Damage” after the fix.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Raw alignment cost equals the sum of uniform unit costs (c=1) on every visible log or model move under the supplied cost function. It therefore measures purely syntactic edit distance against the control-flow tree and systematically diverges from business risk for multiple independent reasons illustrated by the cases:

1. Cost-function design is business-blind. Every L or M move costs 1 regardless of whether the move is UI telemetry (CL-601: three L moves for Open/Autosave/Close Workbench → cost 3, severity 1) or a missing Fraud Screen on a EUR 480 k HIGH-risk claim with recent bank change (CL-602: cost 1, severity 5). Uniform weighting cannot encode P1–P3 materiality.

2. Scope mismatch. The process tree was designed for payable claims (P6). Denied (CL-607) and withdrawn (CL-612) cases generate multiple M moves (cost 4) simply because the model lacks Deny/Withdraw branches; the business outcome (no payment) is correct and exposure is zero. High cost reflects model incompleteness, not operational failure.

3. Hidden/silent moves are technically perfect. CL-605 inserts T(tau,c=0) before Auto Approve; raw cost 0 and the alignment is fitting. The converted Petri net’s internal routing is invisible to the log yet produces no control deviation.

4. Data conditions are invisible to the control-flow tree. CL-609 (EUR 18 k MEDIUM, Auto Approve) and CL-610 (EUR 4.5 k with bank change one hour earlier, Auto Approve) both receive fully synchronous alignments and raw cost 0. The tree’s XOR allows Auto Approve, yet both violate P1 (and the additional evidence notes four such cases). Cost 0 conceals data-aware policy failures of severity 4.

5. Activity granularity and label synonymy. CL-611 records “Assess Damage” while the model and signed procedure use “Assess Loss”. The alignment pays cost 2 for an L+M pair; after the mapping table was updated the same events would align at cost 0. The deviation is terminological, not behavioral.

6. Duplicated telemetry and transport noise. CL-603 (and the 74-case monthly pattern) produces an extra L(Notify Claimant) with identical message ID caused by a retrying connector. Cost 1, yet P4 and P5 classify it as harmless; notification occurred within the 24-hour window.

7. Missing-but-external evidence and irreversibility. CL-606 (M(Assess Loss), cost 1) has a contemporaneous external licensed-adjuster report; CL-608 (M(Notify), cost 1) has a CRM secure-portal message at +3 h. In both cases the activity almost certainly occurred, but core-log completeness failed. Conversely CL-604 shows an irreversible order violation (Pay at 10:03 before Manual Approve at 10:20) that the alignment “repairs” with L(Pay)+M(Pay) at total cost 3; the business fact is a severity-5 P3 control failure that cost under-states relative to lower-cost missing-evidence cases.

Thus raw cost ranks noise and scope issues above (or equal to) genuine high-exposure control breaches, ignores data guards, treats external evidence as pure absence, and cannot distinguish reversible from irreversible deviations. Business risk prioritization must therefore be performed by the narrative and materiality framework, not by sorting on raw_cost.
