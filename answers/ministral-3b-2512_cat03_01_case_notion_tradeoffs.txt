### **1. Analytical assumptions**
**Case notion and convergence/divergence:**
- A “case notion” here refers to the *focused unit of analysis* (e.g., an order, item, or transaction) when flattening the OCEL. Convergence risk arises when flattening obscures cross-object interactions (e.g., shared packages, consolidated deliveries). Divergence risk occurs when flattening isolates objects that must be coordinated (e.g., partial payments spanning multiple invoices).
- **Shared events as evidence vs. duplication:**
  - Events like `e13` (Pack Package) or `e16` (Dispatch) involve multiple objects. Flattening on an object type that is *directly* related to these events (e.g., Order) will *copy* the event into each relevant case, creating duplication. We treat this as *semantic duplication* (not noise) because it preserves the original event’s scope but distorts the flattened trace’s granularity.
  - We *do not* propagate relations through the O2O graph unless explicitly justified (e.g., to reconstruct cross-order dependencies).
- **Analytical transformations allowed:**
  - **Relation propagation** is permitted only if it clarifies a business question (e.g., reconstructing a package’s item composition from O2O links). Otherwise, we treat E2O relations as *primitive* and avoid implicit propagation.
  - **Event clustering** (e.g., merging partial payments) is only done if it resolves ambiguity (e.g., `M2` and `M3` for `V2` could be treated as a single “partial settlement” event).

### **3. Concrete distortion analysis**

#### **A. Cross-order packages (flattening Order)**
- **Event:** `e13` (Pack Package)  `O2,O3,I21,I31,P3`
  - **DFG distortion:** In the Order-flattened log, `e13` is duplicated for `O2` and `O3`, creating two traces with identical activities but diverging item compositions.
  - **Effect:** **Frequency inflation** (e.g., `P3` appears twice in the DFG), and **artificial branching** (e.g., `O2` and `O3` diverge after `e13` while sharing `P3`).
  - **Harmless?** Context-dependent: Useful for analyzing *per-order packaging*, but obscures cross-order dependencies (e.g., why `P3` was shared).

#### **B. Consolidated delivery (flattening Order)**
- **Event:** `e16` (Dispatch)  `O1,O2,O3,I12,I21,I31,P2,P3,D2`
  - **DFG distortion:** The Order-flattened log splits `e16` into three traces (`O1`, `O2`, `O3`), each carrying a subset of `P2,P3`. This creates **artificial divergence** (e.g., `O1` has `P2` but not `P3`, while `O2` has both).
  - **Effect:** **Performance bias** (e.g., DFG edges for `D2` are overcounted), and **lifecycle truncation** (e.g., `O1`’s dispatch ends prematurely without `P3`).
  - **Harmless?** Useful for analyzing *per-order dispatch*, but hides the fact that `D2` is a *consolidated* delivery.

#### **C. Consolidated invoicing (flattening Invoice)**
- **Event:** `e19` (Issue Invoice)  `O2,O3,V2`
  - **DFG distortion:** In the Invoice-flattened log, `e19` is duplicated for `V2`, but the Order-flattened log splits `V2` into `O2` and `O3`. This creates **conflicting traces** (e.g., `O2`’s invoice includes `I21` while `O3`’s includes `I31`).
  - **Effect:** **Convergence risk** (e.g., `V2`’s payments `M2,M3` are merged into a single trace, obscuring partial settlement).
  - **Harmless?** Useful for analyzing *per-invoice payments*, but loses order-level item context.

#### **D. Partial payments (flattening Payment)**
- **Event:** `e22` (Record Payment)  `O2,O3,V2,M2`
  - **DFG distortion:** In the Payment-flattened log, `e22` is duplicated for `M2`, but `V2` spans `O2` and `O3`. This creates **artificial lifecycle truncation** (e.g., `M2` appears in `O2` but not `O3` unless `M3` is merged).
  - **Effect:** **Performance bias** (e.g., `V2`’s payments are split into two traces, inflating the DFG).
  - **Harmless?** Useful for analyzing *per-payment history*, but obscures the fact that `V2` was *partially settled*.

#### **E. Near-singleton lifecycle (flattening Item)**
- **Event:** `I11`  `O1`  `P1`  `D1`
  - **DFG distortion:** In the Item-flattened log, `I11`’s lifecycle is isolated, but its O2O links (`O1`, `P1`, `D1`) are *lost*. This creates **divergence risk** (e.g., `I11`’s dispatch `e15` is missing from the Item DFG).
  - **Effect:** **Fragmented DFG** (e.g., `I11` and `I12` share `P1,P2` but diverge in `D1,D2`).
  - **Harmless?** Useful for analyzing *item-specific failures*, but lacks order context.

#### **F. Cross-order package (flattening Package)**
- **Event:** `e13` (Pack Package)  `O2,O3,I21,I31,P3`
  - **DFG distortion:** In the Package-flattened log, `e13` is duplicated for `P3`, but `O2` and `O3` are *lost*. This creates **convergence risk** (e.g., `P3`’s items are merged into a single trace, obscuring order-specific packaging).
  - **Effect:** **Artificial convergence** (e.g., `P3`’s items are treated as a single event, hiding why `I21` was in `O2` and `I31` in `O3`).
  - **Harmless?** Useful for analyzing *package consolidation*, but loses order context.

#### **G. Shared event duplication (flattening Order)**
- **Event:** `e19` (Issue Invoice)  `O2,O3,V2`
  - **DFG distortion:** In the Order-flattened log, `e19` is duplicated for `O2` and `O3`, creating **frequency inflation** (e.g., `V2` appears twice in the DFG).
  - **Effect:** **Performance bias** (e.g., DFG edges for `V2` are overcounted), and **artificial branching** (e.g., `O2` and `O3` diverge after `e19` while sharing `V2`).
  - **Harmless?** Useful for analyzing *per-order invoicing*, but obscures the fact that `V2` was *consolidated*.

#### **H. Lifecycle truncation (flattening Invoice)**
- **Event:** `e25` (Record Payment)  `O2,O3,V2,M3`
  - **DFG distortion:** In the Invoice-flattened log, `e25` is duplicated for `V2`, but `O2` and `O3` are *lost*. This creates **lifecycle truncation** (e.g., `V2`’s payments are split into two traces, inflating the DFG).
  - **Effect:** **Performance bias** (e.g., `V2`’s payments are overrepresented), and **artificial divergence** (e.g., `M2` and `M3` are merged into a single trace).
  - **Harmless?** Useful for analyzing *per-invoice payments*, but loses order-specific item context.

### **4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio**

#### **Recommended flattened logs:**
- **Create Order-flattened logs** for:
  - End-to-end order analysis (items, packages, deliveries).
  - Cross-order package analysis (if propagated via O2O links).
- **Create Package-flattened logs** for:
  - Package consolidation analysis (e.g., `P3`’s items).
- **Avoid flattening Item, Delivery, or Payment** unless:
  - Item-flattened logs are used with explicit O2O resolution (e.g., to reconstruct `I11`’s lifecycle).
  - Payment-flattened logs are used to analyze partial settlements (e.g., `M2,M3` for `V2`).

#### **Questions each flattened log can answer safely:**
| Flattened log | Safe questions                                                                 |
|---------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Order         | Per-order fulfillment, item packaging, delivery routing.                     |
| Package       | Package consolidation, cross-order item distribution.                          |
| Invoice       | Invoice consolidation, partial payment tracking.                              |

#### **Questions requiring OC-DFG/OTG/ET–OT:**
- **Cross-order dependencies** (e.g., why `P3` was shared between `O2` and `O3`).
- **Consolidated deliveries** (e.g., `D2`’s item composition).
- **Partial payments** (e.g., `M2,M3`’s relationship to `V2`).
- **Near-singleton item lifecycles** (e.g., `I11`’s dispatch).

#### **Reconciliation strategy:**
- Use **OC-DFG** to reconstruct cross-order packages (e.g., `P3`’s items).
- Use **OTG** to map flattened logs to object interactions (e.g., `O2`’s items in `P3`).
- Use **ET–OT** to link events to objects (e.g., `e13`’s relation to `O2,O3`).
- For contradictions (e.g., `V2` in `O2` vs. `O3`), use **equivalent-execution clustering** to group related traces.

### **5. Lead-object decision**
**B. Use a primary lead object plus explicitly secondary lifecycles.**

#### **Decision criteria:**
1. **Primary lead object:** **Order** (as the most natural unit of analysis for fulfillment).
2. **Secondary lifecycles:** Package, Invoice, and Payment (to capture cross-order dependencies).
3. **Justification:**
   - Orders are the *business boundary* (e.g., `O1` vs. `O2`).
   - Packages, invoices, and payments are *secondary* but critical for cross-order analysis (e.g., `P3`’s items in `O2` and `O3`).
   - Flattening Order preserves the most meaningful lifecycle while allowing secondary flattening for dependencies.

#### **Strongest supporting evidence:**
- **Business alignment:** Orders are the primary unit of fulfillment (items, packages, deliveries).
- **Convergence risk mitigation:** Secondary flattening (e.g., Package) can reconstruct cross-order dependencies via O2O links.
- **Performance:** Order-flattened logs are less duplicated than Item/Delivery/Payment.

#### **Strongest counterargument:**
- **Artificial branching:** Order-flattened logs duplicate shared events (e.g., `e13`), inflating the DFG.
- **Lack of granularity:** Items and payments are lost unless explicitly propagated.

#### **Residual risk:**
- **Cross-order dependencies** may still be obscured unless explicitly reconstructed via OC-DFG/OTG.
- **Partial payments** (e.g., `M2,M3` for `V2`) require secondary Invoice-flattened logs.

#### **Conditions for change:**
- If **cross-order analysis** is the primary goal, switch to **Package as lead** with Order as secondary.
- If **end-to-end order analysis** is the goal, accept **duplication** and use secondary flattening for dependencies.
