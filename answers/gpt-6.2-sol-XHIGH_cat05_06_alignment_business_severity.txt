## 1. Materiality Framework

Start with the control objective. Claim funds should go to the authorized beneficiary only after the applicable checks and approval. Claimants should receive timely notice, with evidence that can be retrieved for audit or a dispute. Assess the amount actually released and any credible loss exposure, whether the bank can stop or recover the transfer, how long the exposure lasted, and any customer or regulatory consequence. Consider how often the same defect occurs and how many other cases it could affect. First establish whether the case belongs to the payable-claim model and whether the evidence covers the systems where the business activity could occur.

| Severity | Business significance |
|---|---|
| 5 | Critical payment-control incident or credible high-exposure failure requiring immediate investigation and containment. A booked loss is unnecessary for this rating. |
| 4 | Major failure of an applicable approval or payment safeguard, including release on an ineligible approval. Investigate promptly and contain the affected route. |
| 3 | Material control, service, or evidence weakness with a meaningful consequence or an unresolved prerequisite. The evidence supports a narrower or more recoverable impact than at levels 4–5. |
| 2 | Evidence or data-quality defect that weakens assurance. Independent evidence supports the business activity, or the immediate operational impact appears limited. |
| 1 | Fitting behavior, harmless technical noise, or an apparent deviation explained by the scope of the payable model, with no material business failure established. |

Severity rates the consequence of the observed issue in light of credible exposure. Confidence rates how strongly the evidence supports the diagnosis. Use high confidence for direct or corroborated observations, moderate confidence for a plausible interpretation that still needs linking, timing, or authenticity checks, and low confidence where material evidence is absent or conflicting. A missing audit trail can be certain while the underlying control failure remains unresolved. Amounts below are claim amounts and, for paid cases, indicate the scale of funds at issue. Actual loss and recoverability are separate facts.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Under the supplied cost function, the raw score is the number of visible L and M moves because each costs one. S and T cost zero. These twelve supplied alignments have eight L moves and twelve M moves, for a total raw cost of 20. The score depends on the chosen model, observed log, event vocabulary, and error prices. It describes how many edits reconcile those representations. The costs contain no input for amount, evidence reliability, time relative to approval, or the bank's ability to reverse a release.

- **Scope changes the expected path.** CL-607 and CL-612 each cost 4 because the payable model forces continuation past a terminal outcome. A denial or withdrawal can legitimately end without approval or payment. Any closure-notice obligation needs its own test. Applying a scope-aware model changes the diagnosis without changing what happened.
- **Zero has two different business interpretations.** In CL-605 the hidden tau move is fitting internal routing. In CL-609 and CL-610 the model matches every visible event while the selected branch or release fails policy. An activity-only trace lacks the amount, risk, and point-in-time beneficiary data needed to evaluate those rules.
- **Granularity and naming affect edit counts.** CL-601 costs 3 because the event feed logs three UI actions. CL-603 costs 1 when an attempt for the same logical message appears as another activity. CL-611 costs 2 because one likely alias appears as an unmatched log label and an unmatched model activity. The score helps locate normalization work, while attempt-level and source records remain useful evidence.
- **One missing match can represent different evidence states.** CL-606 has a pre-approval external assessment and CL-608 has timely CRM evidence with weaker linkage. CL-602 has the same cost of 1, yet the record cannot substantiate a required fraud screen on a EUR 480,000 payment after a bank change. Event absence alone does not establish the activity's absence.
- **A modeled repair cannot undo a bank-accepted release.** In CL-604 the cost-3 alignment consumes the actual early payment as L and advances a later model payment as M. It identifies where the trace departed from the documented path. It cannot authorize or reverse the EUR 90,000 transfer. The timing of the bank acceptance and the prospects for recovery drive the priority.

Business-specific weights could improve the ordering of technical review, especially for frequent low-information moves. They would still operate on the selected trace and model. Eligibility and timing need policy checks, off-system work needs evidence reconciliation, and severity needs business judgment.

non_sync = moves.loc[moves["move"].isin(["L", "M", "T"])].copy()

def sum_known(amounts):
    return amounts.sum(min_count=1)


def summarize_cases(frame, group_columns):
    return frame.groupby(group_columns, as_index=False, dropna=False).agg(
        cases=("case", "size"),
        total_supplied_raw_cost=("supplied_raw_cost", "sum"),
        cases_with_known_amount=("claim_amount_eur", "count"),
        total_known_claim_amount_eur=("claim_amount_eur", sum_known),
        total_known_paid_case_amount_eur=("paid_case_amount_eur", sum_known),
    )


pattern_summary = summarize_cases(case_summary, ["ordered_move_pattern", "fraud_risk"])
