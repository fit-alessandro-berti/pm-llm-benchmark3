## 1. Executive causal assessment

Provisional verdict: **partly causal through mechanisms**. Confidence: **58/100**. Orion’s crude mean is 21.96 hours versus Nova’s 15.24, a 6.72-hour gap, and Orion also has higher observed mean wait (12.80 versus 8.79 hours), service time (7.04 versus 5.04), and rework prevalence (28.6% versus 18.8%). Those facts are compatible with harm, but Orion receives disproportionately difficult work: high complexity is 42.9% versus 12.5%, compliance is 64.3% versus 25.0%, and 92.9% of Orion cases are at night. Thus the aggregate is heavily confounded by case mix and operating conditions.

The strongest counter-evidence is the reversal within every complexity stratum: Orion is faster by about 2.14 hours for Low, 2.21 for Medium, and 3.43 for High cases. Its wait is also lower within each stratum in the supplied aggregates. This is consistent with certified-reviewer expertise or better handling of difficult work, but it is not causal identification: shift, load, routing rules, reviewer composition, and risk remain poorly overlapped, and only completed cases are observed. The quality comparison is uninformative: one appeal occurs in each group.

A 50% reassignment could improve time if Orion’s apparent advantage transfers and Nova has spare capacity. It could fail if the advantage is a selection effect, or backfire by moving high-risk/compliance work into Nova queues, saturating capacity, increasing rework, or degrading quality. A guarded, randomized pilot is safer.

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests

| rank | hypothesis | process mechanism | evidence supporting it | evidence contradicting it | discriminating analysis or falsification test | predicted observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Orion is genuinely harmful for at least some eligible cases | Orion’s workload, handoffs, or reviewer mix creates longer service, rework, or downstream delay | Orion’s crude cycle is 6.72 hours longer, service is 2.00 hours longer, and rework is more frequent | Orion is faster in every complexity stratum and has lower stratum mean waits | Run a small randomized routing pilot within complexity × shift × load × compliance cells, with time-stamped queue and service outcomes | If harmful, the randomized Orion-minus-Nova effect remains positive in comparable cells and is accompanied by longer queue/service or rework |
| 2 | Orion is beneficial, but case mix masks the benefit | Certified reviewers or specialized expertise reduce handling time for difficult cases | Orion beats Nova by 2.14, 2.21, and 3.43 hours in Low, Medium, and High strata; service is also lower within strata | Orion’s observed aggregate service and cycle are higher; residual risk and shift are uncontrolled | Exact/propensity matching with overlap diagnostics, then within-route and, where possible, within-reviewer comparisons; use mediation only with explicit assumptions | A benefit hypothesis predicts a negative Orion contrast after measured adjustment, especially in service/rework, not just in crude cycle time |
| 3 | Night shift and arrival load, not the label, drive the bottleneck | Night coverage and high arrival load raise backlog and queue wait; routing co-occurs with both | 13/14 Orion cases are night and high load; pooled night mean is 21.74 versus day 13.98 | Nova also has some night/high-load cases, and Orion’s within-complexity wait is lower | Reconstruct queue state from event timestamps; estimate effects within shift/load/backlog bands and use a staggered coverage change or interrupted routing experiment | If load/shift dominates, group differences shrink after queue-state adjustment, and queue wait—not service—tracks backlog |

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Instrument and baseline | Preserve current routing while instrumenting every initiated case and auditing eligibility | Separate queue, compliance, service, rework, completion, and quality mechanisms | Case plus hourly queue snapshot | No reassignment; retain certification and safety rules; pre-specify outcome and subgroup definitions | Event timestamps for Classify, queue entry, compliance start/end, Review starts/ends, Request Info, Close; backlog, variants, DFGs, rework, censoring, appeals | Establishes denominators, overlap, baseline queue state, and whether completed-case selection is material | Roll back instrumentation changes only if they delay operations or create missing events above a pre-set threshold |

| 4. Conditional scale-up | Increase reassignment only for strata where the pilot shows a stable benefit and no quality harm | Scale a validated mechanism, not an aggregate correlation | Weekly policy cohort with stratum-specific allocation | Ramp in small increments; preserve Orion reserve for certified high-risk work; review weekly | Treatment effects, overlap, p50/p90/p95 cycle, queue/service decomposition, variant drift, completion and quality | Tests external validity and detects capacity saturation or treatment-effect decay | Return to the last safe allocation if the confidence interval crosses the no-benefit threshold, SLA fails, or quality noninferiority fails |

Recommendation: do not implement the proposed 50% reassignment from the current table alone. The crude gap is dominated by Orion’s case mix and night/load exposure, while the within-complexity reversal is encouraging but not identified causally. Keep the existing policy as the operational control, instrument all initiated cases, and run the stratified randomized pilot with explicit safety overrides and pre-specified time-and-quality guardrails. In parallel, test night queue capacity and compliance capacity without changing group labels; this distinguishes a bottleneck intervention from a staffing-label intervention. Analyze intention-to-treat effects, queue/service mediation, rework, completion, and quality, with separate estimates for complexity, shift, load, and compliance. If Orion’s advantage persists under overlap and Nova does not saturate, ramp selectively rather than moving half of all Orion work. If the effect disappears after queue-state or reviewer adjustment, target capacity or routing policy instead.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Unobserved case risk:** A single pre-routing risk/severity score calibrated before assignment would reduce residual case-mix confounding beyond the three-level complexity field.
2. **Non-random routing:** A logged randomized-assignment indicator for a small eligible pilot would directly reduce uncertainty about the group effect and reveal noncompliance with the assignment.
3. **Reviewer capability:** A reviewer identifier linked to certification, workload, and prior performance would separate group effects from reviewer-skill composition.
4. **Duration semantics:** Event-level timestamps for queue entry, compliance start/end, each Review start/end, Request Info, and Close would decompose queueing, compliance, service, and handoff time.
5. **Completed-case selection:** Prospective inclusion of every initiated case with an observation-time status (completed, open, or abandoned) and censoring time would reduce selection bias from analyzing completed cases only.
