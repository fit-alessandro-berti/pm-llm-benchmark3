# 2. Integrated explanation

The observed performance dynamics in the claims triage and resolution center reflect an interplay of **case-mix selection, queue-pressure trade-offs, cognitive fatigue, and procedural workarounds**, rather than any single mechanism.

```

               High Priority Backlog (41%)

               [ MID-SHIFT STABILIZATION ]
```

### 1. Shift Start: Selection Bias and Warm-Up (Sequence Positions 1–15)
At the start of a shift, mean service time is highest (18.8 min), but this is primarily a **case-mix and selection artifact**. Early-shift queues are dominated by complex carryover cases and overnight backlogs (mean complexity 0.72, 41% high priority in seq 1–5; 48% high complexity in hour 1 of Early shift). Senior resources actively select or are assigned these high-complexity cases early in the shift. As workers clear these initial complex cases, average complexity drops to 0.61 (seq 6–15) and 0.54 (seq 16–30), causing average service times to decline rapidly (15.2 min to 13.6 min). While minor task re-familiarization (warm-up) may occur, the primary driver of this early drop in service time is the shift in case complexity.

### 2. Mid-Shift Stabilization (Sequence Positions 16–50)
During the middle of the shift, the process reaches a steady state. Complexity stabilizes between 0.50 and 0.54, queue lengths remain manageable (31–33), and service times settle between 12.9 min and 13.6 min. Error rates reach their lowest point (2.5%–2.7%), and 7-day rework is minimal (4.8%–5.0%). Under these baseline conditions (Low WIP <30), workers maintain high quality across all experience levels (Seniors 2.2%, Mid-level 2.9%, Juniors 4.4%).

### 3. Late Shift: Queue Pressure, Rushing, and Batching (Sequence Positions 51+)
In the latter third of the shift, queue pressure escalates significantly, particularly during the Day shift where demand peaks late (queue rises from 38 to 75). As `wip_at_start` exceeds 70 (seq 76+ mean queue is 88), worker behavior changes radically:
- **Speed-Quality Trade-Off:** To clear accumulating queues before shift end, workers rush. Even for low-complexity cases, service time drops from 12.7 min under low WIP to 9.4 min under high WIP. 
- **Error Exacerbation:** Rushing under cognitive fatigue creates a surge in errors. Under high WIP and high complexity, the error rate spikes to 10.9%, and 7-day rework reaches 13.7% for sequence 76+.
- **Experience Differential:** Senior staff absorb queue pressure with minimal quality degradation (error rate rises from 2.2% to 4.9%), whereas Junior staff suffer severe degradation (error rate surges from 4.4% to 12.4%).
- **Workarounds and Batching:** Junior workers (e.g., Junior_H, Junior_I) resort to opportunistic batching—processing 9–18 Document Review events sequentially with minimal execution time—and deferring Quality Checks to the final 45 minutes of the shift.
- **Automation Strain:** Under high queue conditions (>70), human workers override or correct `Bot_Risk` classification outputs at higher rates, indicating that fast-moving automated triage introduces errors that downstream human workers must fix under pressure.

### Summary of Service Time vs. Error Rate Trade-off
Service time falls while errors rise late in the shift because workers **adaptively shrink their depth of processing** in response to queue pressure and fatigue. Reduced service time late in the shift is not genuine learning; it represents truncated thoroughness and procedural shortcuts, which directly fuel downstream error and rework rates.

# 3. Proposed analyses

To disentangle these competing mechanisms rigorously, the following statistical and process-mining analyses must be conducted.

### Analytical Design
1. **Within-Resource Controlled Fixed-Effects Regression:** Model `service_time` and `error_flag` using resource fixed effects to control for individual baseline ability. Include `sequence_position_within_shift`, `case_complexity_score`, `wip_at_start`, `minutes_since_shift_start`, and clock hour as covariates.
2. **WIP and Complexity Stratification:** Evaluate performance in 3×2 grid cells (`wip_at_start`: Low/Med/High × `case_complexity_score`: Low/High) to isolate the pure effect of queue pressure independent of case mix.
3. **Batching Identification:** Run batch detection algorithms on sequential activity logs to separate legitimate individual work from batched workarounds.
4. **Downstream Impact Tracing:** Map initial error flags to downstream `Rework Correction` events and 7-day rework indicators using trace-level path analysis.

### PM4Py Script Implementation

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Load and Format Event Log
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
df = pd.read_csv("claims_triage_event_log.csv")

# Ensure required timestamp columns are datetimes
df['time:timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(df['time:timestamp'])
df['start_timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(df['start_timestamp'])
df['shift_start_time'] = pd.to_datetime(df['shift_start_time'])

# Format dataframe for PM4Py compliance
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id='case:concept:name',
    activity_key='concept:name',
    timestamp_key='time:timestamp',
    start_timestamp_key='start_timestamp'
)

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Enrich Log with Performance & Temporal Features
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Calculate service time and waiting time per activity
df = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df)

# Calculate arrival and finish rates across time windows
df = pm4py.insert_case_arrival_finish_rate(df, time_interval=1800)

# Extract temporal features (hour of day, day of week)
df = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(df)

# Calculate explicit service time in minutes
df['service_time_min'] = pm4py.get_service_time(df) / 60.0

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Batching Detection
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Discover batch activities (e.g., sequential Document Reviews by Juniors)
batches = pm4py.discover_batches(
    df,
    activity_key='concept:name',
    resource_key='org:resource',
    max_step_duration=300,  # max 5 min gap between batched events
    min_batch_size=5
)
print(f"Discovered {len(batches)} batch instances.")

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Stratified Filtering & Performance DFG
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Sub-log 1: Early shift position, High Complexity
df_early_complex = df[
    (df['sequence_position_within_shift'] <= 15) & 
    (df['case_complexity_score'] > 0.6)
]

# Sub-log 2: Late shift position, High WIP (>70)
df_late_high_wip = df[
    (df['sequence_position_within_shift'] > 50) & 
    (df['wip_at_start'] > 70)
]

# Discover Performance DFG for High WIP late-shift conditions
dfg_late_perf, sa_late, ea_late = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(df_late_high_wip)

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Rework & Error Analysis
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Get rework statistics per activity
rework_per_activity = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(df)
print("Rework cases per activity:", rework_per_activity)

# Path filtering: Filter traces containing Quality Check followed by Rework Correction
rework_paths = pm4py.filter_paths_performance(
    df,
    [('Quality Check', 'Rework Correction')],
    min_performance=0,
    max_performance=86400  # within 24 hours
)

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Visualization Export
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Save events distribution graph over time
pm4py.save_vis_events_distribution_graph(
    df, 
    file_path="events_distribution.png", 
    distribution_type="days_week"
)

# Save dotted chart colored by resource experience
pm4py.save_vis_dotted_chart(
    df, 
    file_path="dotted_chart_experience.png", 
    attributes=['resource_experience_months']
)
```

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

### 1. Unfair Worker-Level Conclusions Due to Selection Bias
Evaluating individual worker performance using unadjusted metrics produces flawed conclusions. Senior staff show higher initial service times (15.9 min) because they disproportionately select or are assigned complex carryover claims (complexity 0.72) at shift start. Attributing slow execution to Senior inefficiency, or evaluating Juniors without controlling for queue pressure, fails to isolate worker skill from case-mix variance.

### 2. Operational Systemic Drivers vs. Individual Failure
The dramatic error rate surge among Juniors under high WIP (rising from 4.4% to 12.4%) is an **operational system failure**, not an individual performance deficiency. When management allows queue lengths to reach 88 (seq 76+) without load balancing, workers are forced to trade accuracy for speed. Penalizing workers for errors generated under systemic overload misallocates accountability.

### 3. Measurement Error and Log Manipulation
Plausible punitive policies (e.g., Option 8) induce gaming behaviors. Workers under performance penalties alter event logging practices by delaying activity close timestamps, performing off-system processing, or artificial batching to artificially inflate throughput while masking errors.

### 4. Flawed Automated Triage (`Bot_Risk`)
The increase in human overrides of `Bot_Risk` classifications during high queue conditions (>70 WIP) indicates that automated classification rules degrade under specific arrival conditions or pass ambiguous cases downstream. Blaming human reviewers for slow re-classification overlooks upstream automation limitations.

### 5. Integrity of Quality Control Mechanisms
Proposing the elimination or deferral of `Quality Check` steps to artificially maintain throughput treats process quality as disposable. Quality controls are critical safeguards; deferring them shifts operational friction into customer-facing rework, escalating 7-day rework rates and long-term costs.

### Dashboard Indicator Definitions

1. **Service Time Dynamics (Complexity-Adjusted):** Tracks mean execution duration stratified by `case_complexity_score` and `sequence_position_within_shift`. *Goal:* Distinguish learning speed-up from quality-degrading rushing.
2. **Waiting Time & Queue Bottlenecks:** Measures duration cases spend idle in queues before activity initiation (`pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time`). *Goal:* Identify shift-change handoff delays and queue accumulation points.
3. **Real-Time WIP Threshold Indicator:** Live count of active cases in the center. Triggers visual amber alerts at WIP > 30 and red alerts at WIP > 70. *Goal:* Prevent queue pressure reaching critical error zones.
4. **7-Day Rework Rate:** Proportion of cases requiring `Rework Correction` within 7 days of completion, linked to original executing resource and shift position. *Goal:* Measure long-term quality decay.
5. **Stratified Error Rate:** Immediate error percentage split by shift position, WIP level, and resource experience tier. *Goal:* Monitor operational quality without misattributing overload to worker deficiency.
6. **Complexity Allocation Balance Index:** Ratio of high-complexity cases handled by resource experience tier over sequence positions. *Goal:* Ensure high-complexity carryover is not concentrated excessively at shift start or on specific tiers.
7. **Batching Workaround Detector:** Flags resource activity logs exhibiting >5 consecutive short-duration events of identical type within <5 minutes (derived from `pm4py.discover_batches`). *Goal:* Detect procedural shortcuts and artificial logging patterns in real time.
8. **Workload Fairness & Handover Equity:** Gini coefficient of WIP assigned across active staff and total queue length passed between shifts (e.g., Day to Late). *Goal:* Prevent shift-end dumping and ensure equitable task distribution across resources.
