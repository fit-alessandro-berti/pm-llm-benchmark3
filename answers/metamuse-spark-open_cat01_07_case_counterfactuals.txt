R6: Assessment  ComplianceReview_final  [iff highRisk=TRUE  highValue=TRUE]  Decision
R7: ComplianceReview_prelim  ComplianceReview_final // prelim does not satisfy R6; only final/reaffirmed does
R8: Assessment_i / ComplianceReview_i  RequestMoreInfo_i  DocumentCheck_{i+1}  Assessment_{i+1} [ ComplianceReview_{i+1 or reaffirm}]
R9: Decision  Notify
R10: ResourceNonOverlap: r, intervals ij on r: ¬overlap(i,j); except S1.advisory may || other pools

```

```
CONDITIONAL & DECLARATIVE CONSTRAINTS:
C1: IF highRiskhighValue THEN ComplianceReview_final  path  ComplianceReview_final  max(Assessment_*)  ComplianceReview_final  Decision

**100-case weekly rollout analysis (322 words):**

Single-case evaluations hold queue backgrounds fixed. At 100 cases/week scaled uniformly, that approximation collapses via interference.

*Scarce compliance reservation.* C1 is unit capacity (only fully certified). Reserving 3-4h per high-risk case for 20-30 high-risk weekly cases (25% mix) consumes 60-120h of C1 weekly demand against ~40h supply. Strict FIFO breaks; priority reservations cannibalize the two urgent Monday adds seen Mon16:00 and every subsequent urgent. Forecast 5-8h Wed compliance queue would inflate to 20-30h for non-reserved cases, inverting K-104’s local gain into zero-sum redistribution. Benefit persists only if C1 capacity expanded, delegation enabled, or triage filters who merits prep (high sanctions score).

*D1D2 substitution.* Shifting 100 handwritten-standard cases from D1 to D2 relieves D1’s 11-13h Monday pileup (6h mean wait) but loads D2. Current D2 2-5h queue suggests slack for perhaps 20-30 redirects; beyond that D2’s longer tail (8-14h complex) drives service-variance-induced queuing (Pollaczek-Khinchine). Rework amplification multiplies: if D2 rework p=25% vs D1 5%, each 100 cases adds ~20 extra Document+Assessment loops (160-240h) and external 8-36h lags, potentially erasing D1 relief and congesting Assessment. Gains are concave then negative.

*Avoided vs additional rework.* K-337’s truncation at scale dramatically cuts Document/Assessment loops and eliminates weekend external waits that dominate elapsed time. That frees A1/D1 from repeat visits (Thu 12-17h slots) and is the only intervention with superlinear system-throughput gain. Conversely K-219 may add loops.

*Assessment bottleneck migration.* Both K-219 and K-411 push completions earlier into Assessment. Snapshots show A1 10-12h, A2 4-7h peaks. Synchronized Tuesday-afternoon convergence creates a Wednesday morning surge; assessment wait absorbs upstream savings (Little’s law). Locally 4h saved verification translates to 0-1h real exit unless Assessment staffing scales evenings/weekends.

*Weekend/external effects.* External supplier responses drive calendar time; rework weekend penalties multiply with batches (Fri requests sit until Mon). Truncation gains scale with calendar, not workhours.

*Timestamp behavior vs true concurrency.* K-411’s batch entry masked serialized logging; at scale poor workaround logging creates illusory  inflating reported concurrency KPIs while true contention remains. Validate with real-time heartbeat logs else capacity planning misled.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is K-411 parallel verification: it restores legally allowed concurrency by fixing an IT lock rather than betting on human judgment. Its upside is moderate and largely predictable—removing a 1.7–3.5h artificial gap—while downside is contained to migratory congestion at Assessment that can be managed with WIP caps. It is fully reversible, preserves all compliance and four-eyes rules, and a pilot yields clean causal evidence by contrasting logged versus real concurrency.

Most dangerous despite apparent speed is K-337 suppressing the second rework loop. Stripping ~90h dominated by weekend external waits is seductive on cycle-time dashboards, but it achieves time by redefining risk tolerance, not removing waste. The factual second request was genuine quality control for inconsistent supplier totals; truncating forces a conditional approve or reject under residual uncertainty. The loss function is asymmetric and lagged: regulatory findings, default exposure, and systematic supplier signal erosion appear quarters later, dwarfing any throughput gain. Once decided, approval cannot be recalled, and culture quickly learns to tolerate discrepancies.

Local counterfactuals are least transportable to system policy wherever they rely on *priority* or *queue repositioning*. K-104’s reservation looks beneficial holding others fixed, but at 100 cases C1—unit capacity—becomes a priority queue where every advance displaces another high-risk case; the net system effect is near zero unless capacity expands. Similar burst synchronization undermines K-411 and K-219, whose local waits vanish only to reappear at Assessment.

Most valuable additional data are fine-grained, second-resolution event logs with resource heartbeat and calendar availability, true queue-length trajectories per pool by hour and skill subtype, handwriting-legibility scores linked to D2 error rates, and downstream loss/appeal panels linking truncated due-diligence to long-run credit and compliance outcomes, plus explicit manager signing windows to separate readiness from completion. ## 8. Assumption audit

1. **structural** – Business-rule partial order (RegisterTriage{Identity,Document}AssessmentComplianceDecisionNotify) reflects normative process and preliminary screens do not satisfy final compliance; no hidden precedence omitted.

2. **temporal** – Service-time guidance (Identity 1.5-3.5h, D1/D2 ranges, Assessment 2-5h, Compliance 3-6h minus 1-3h prelim saving, external 8-36h) are distributional anchors with high variance; point forecasts unreliable.

3. **resource** – C1 single-certified and S1 advisory resources have finite capacity; preliminary work by C1/S1 consumes real hours that cannot overlap with their own other service intervals (except S1 advisory overlap exception).

4. **behavioral** – Managers and assessors adhere to assigned decision thresholds and do not implicitly raise/lower bar when observed routing changes; truncation prohibition is enforceable and logged rather than informally bypassed.

5. **quality** – Residual unresolved discrepancies carry non-trivial predictive power for downstream loss, appeal, or regulatory deficiency; conditional approval does not fully mitigate information gap.

6. **interference** – Single-case analyses use *ceteris paribus* queue forecasts; multi-case scaling introduces spillovers via shared D1/D2/A1/A2/C1 pools that invalidate independence.

7. **resource** – Night/weekend availability differs by pool: Compliance and Assessment capacity drops sharply outside office hours, making calendar elapsed time non-proportional to work hours and amplifying weekend-blocked rework loops.

8. **interference** – Batch logging can masquerade as concurrency; failure to enforce real-time timestamping creates measurement artifact where reported flow improvement does not correspond to true resource-time consumption.
