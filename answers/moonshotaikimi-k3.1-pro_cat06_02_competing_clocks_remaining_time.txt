- **Branch stages and join:** determine when the investigation can start; a contamination case therefore starts its investigation later than a smooth case.
- **Documentation-only:** 8–20 working hours is roughly 0.7–1.7 full QA weekdays of pure delay, but re-review, queues, and weekends expand the elapsed time. Starts Monday/Tuesday commonly resolve Wednesday–Friday; later starts can roll through Saturday/Sunday.
- **Laboratory investigation:** the 3–7-calendar-day clock runs through weekends, but final QA work does not. A three-day investigation opened Monday can finish Thursday; a five- to seven-day investigation can finish Friday/Saturday or roll from Sunday into Monday/Tuesday.
- **Final step:** release takes 0.25–0.75 h after acceptance. If the investigation instead confirms a critical defect, rejection recording takes 0.5–1.0 h after confirmation.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

1. **QA disposition and investigation type — mainly path uncertainty.**  
   First-pass acceptance leaves only hours; a documentation query adds working hours; and a laboratory investigation adds 3–7 calendar days. This is the largest source of terminal-time dispersion.

2. **Resolution of the temperature-excursion flag — observation uncertainty and dependence uncertainty.**  
   Confirmation simultaneously worsens the outlook for both test branches and selects the higher investigation-probability regime. It therefore changes both the median and the correlated tail, rather than merely adding an independent delay.

3. **Chemistry queue discipline, backlog, and service spillover — primarily queue uncertainty, with service-time and calendar uncertainty.**  
   Risk-priority can produce a Monday-morning result; FIFO can move chemistry to Monday afternoon or Tuesday. A rerun magnifies the effect of the 18:00 laboratory close.

4. **Microbiology weekend staffing and contamination — calendar uncertainty for the smooth path, path uncertainty for contamination.**  
   Weekend authorization changes readout from Monday to Sunday, although chemistry and QA often prevent a correspondingly large terminal-time improvement. It matters more when contamination starts another 20–30 h clock.

5. **QA queue at the actual future join — queue uncertainty.**  
   The current five batches are an imperfect predictor. Weekend service, overdispersed arrivals, long reviews, and two Monday reviewers can produce anything from nearly immediate service to an overnight spillover. Later branch completion does not necessarily mean longer QA waiting.

## 6. Most forecast-changing next event

The single most informative future observation would be the **first substantive QA disposition, including the investigation type if one is opened**:

- **Clean acceptance:** the forecast collapses to approximately **0.25–0.75 h** until electronic release.
- **Documentation query:** the forecast shifts later by **4–12 working hours**, plus reviewer availability, re-review, and any intervening calendar closure.
- **Documentation-only investigation:** the forecast moves to an **8–20-working-hour** delay regime, with weekend rollover possible.
- **Laboratory investigation:** the forecast moves immediately to a **3–7-calendar-day** regime, making late this week or early next week more plausible than Monday/Tuesday release.
- **Confirmed critical defect:** the terminal event changes to **Batch Rejected**, with recording approximately 0.5–1.0 h after confirmation.

The secondary update trigger is **Engineering’s resolution of the temperature-excursion flag**. Confirmation would increase the joint plausibility of chemistry and microbiology anomalies and apply the 0.30–0.55 investigation regime, shifting the central forecast later and fattening the tail. Clearing the flag would remove that common-cause uplift, return unobserved branch risks toward their base ranges, and increase confidence in the Monday/Tuesday release envelope—without ruling out an independently observed rerun, contamination, query, or investigation.
