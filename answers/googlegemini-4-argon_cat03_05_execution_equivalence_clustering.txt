## 1. Equivalence design space

To analyze object-centric executions centered on a lead object of type `Order`, we represent each execution $X = (E, O, L, \tau, \prec, R_{E2O}, R_{O2O})$ as a directed, edge- and vertex-labeled incidence structure. Here, $E$ is the set of events, $O$ is the set of objects, $L: E \to \Sigma$ assigns activity labels, $\tau: O \to \mathcal{T} \times \{\text{LOC}, \text{EXT}\}$ assigns an object type and a scope flag (local vs. external to the lead `Order`), $\prec$ is a strict partial or total order over $E$, $R_{E2O} \subseteq E \times O$ is the event-to-object incidence relation, and $R_{O2O} \subseteq O \times O \times Q$ is the qualified object-to-object relation with qualifier vocabulary $Q$.

We define four equivalence relations that form a strict refinement hierarchy, moving from control-flow projections to rich relational semantics.

### 1. Activity-sequence equivalence ($\equiv_{\text{act}}$)
* **Formal Definition:** Let $\pi_L(X) = \langle L(e_1), L(e_2), \dots, L(e_n) \rangle$ be the sequence of activity labels ordered by the timestamp total order $\prec$. Two executions $X_a$ and $X_b$ are activity-sequence equivalent ($X_a \equiv_{\text{act}} X_b$) if and only if $\pi_L(X_a) = \pi_L(X_b)$.
* **Invariants Preserved:** Event cardinality $|E|$, exact activity label multiset, and sequential control-flow order at the level of the lead `Order`.
* **Information Discarded:** All object sets $O$, object types and scope flags $\tau$, $R_{E2O}$ cardinalities and topologies, and all $R_{O2O}$ relations and qualifiers. It flattens the execution onto the lead `Order` before comparison.
* **Equivalence Properties:** Strictly reflexive, symmetric, and transitive because it is defined by exact equality of strings over $\Sigma^*$.
* **Handling Concurrency / Same-Time Events:** If timestamps induce only a weak order (ties exist), $\pi_L(X)$ is replaced by a sequence of multisets of labels $\langle M_1, M_2, \dots, M_k \rangle$ representing simultaneous step buckets. If true concurrency is modeled via an independence relation $I \subseteq \Sigma \times \Sigma$, $\equiv_{\text{act}}$ is defined over Mazurkiewicz trace equivalence classes rather than total strings.

### 2. Local Typed-Structural Isomorphism ($\equiv_{\text{loc-iso}}$)
* **Formal Definition:** Let $X|_{\text{LOC}}$ be the sub-structure induced by restricting $O$ to local objects $O_{\text{LOC}} = \{o \in O \mid \text{scope}(o) = \text{LOC}\}$ and stripping qualifiers from $R_{O2O}$ (retaining only unlabeled directed edges $(o_i, o_j)$). Two executions satisfy $X_a \equiv_{\text{loc-iso}} X_b$ iff there exist bijections $\phi_E: E_a \to E_b$ and $\phi_O: O_{a,\text{LOC}} \to O_{b,\text{LOC}}$ such that:
  1. $\forall e \in E_a: L_a(e) = L_b(\phi_E(e))$ and $e_1 \prec_a e_2 \iff \phi_E(e_1) \prec_b \phi_E(e_2)$.
  2. $\forall o \in O_{a,\text{LOC}}: \text{type}_a(o) = \text{type}_b(\phi_O(o))$.
  3. $\forall (e, o) \in E_a \times O_{a,\text{LOC}}: (e, o) \in R_{E2O,a} \iff (\phi_E(e), \phi_O(o)) \in R_{E2O,b}$.
  4. $\forall (o_1, o_2) \in O_{a,\text{LOC}}^2: \exists q (o_1, o_2, q) \in R_{O2O,a} \iff \exists q' (\phi_O(o_1), \phi_O(o_2), q') \in R_{O2O,b}$.
* **Invariants Preserved:** Everything in $\equiv_{\text{act}}$, plus local object counts per type, exact local E2O bipartite topology, and unlabeled local O2O graph topology.
* **Information Discarded:** Specific object identifiers (e.g., `I21` vs `I22`), external objects (`EXT-*`) and their incident relations, and semantic qualifiers on O2O edges (e.g., `partially_settles`, `removed_before_dispatch`, `replaces`).
* **Equivalence Properties:** Reflexive, symmetric, and transitive, as typed graph isomorphism is a valid mathematical equivalence relation.
* **Handling Concurrency / Same-Time Events:** Bijection $\phi_E$ must be an order-isomorphism over the partial order $(E, \prec)$. Events with identical timestamps and identical neighborhood signatures can be permuted freely by $\phi_E$, naturally resolving artificial tie-breaking in logs.

### 3. Boundary- and Qualifier-Aware Typed Isomorphism ($\equiv_{\text{full-iso}}$)
* **Formal Definition:** Extending $\equiv_{\text{loc-iso}}$ to the full object set $O$ and the qualified relation $R_{O2O}$. $X_a \equiv_{\text{full-iso}} X_b$ iff there exist bijections $\phi_E: E_a \to E_b$ and $\phi_O: O_a \to O_b$ satisfying the conditions of $\equiv_{\text{loc-iso}}$ over all $O$, plus:
  1. Scope preservation: $\forall o \in O_a, \text{scope}_a(o) = \text{scope}_b(\phi_O(o))$.
  2. Qualifier preservation: $\forall o_1, o_2 \in O_a, q \in Q: (o_1, o_2, q) \in R_{O2O,a} \iff (\phi_O(o_1), \phi_O(o_2), q) \in R_{O2O,b}$.
* **Invariants Preserved:** Exact structural, boundary (external object sharing), and relational-qualifier fidelity up to alpha-renaming of object and event IDs.
* **Information Discarded:** Only raw string literals of local and external object identifiers.
* **Equivalence Properties:** Reflexive, symmetric, and transitive.
* **Handling Concurrency / Same-Time Events:** Handled via partial-order isomorphism on $(E, \prec)$ identically to $\equiv_{\text{loc-iso}}$.

### 4. Business-Semantic Equivalence ($\equiv_{\text{sem}}$) — *Preferred*
* **Formal Definition:** A coarse-grained abstraction of $\equiv_{\text{full-iso}}$ obtained by quotienting out non-critical operational cardinalities while preserving architectural and exception motifs. Each execution is mapped to a feature tuple $\mathcal{F}(X) = (\text{Arch}_{\text{log}}, \text{Arch}_{\text{fin}}, \text{Bound}_{\text{log}}, \text{Bound}_{\text{fin}}, \text{Exc})$, where:
  * $\text{Arch}_{\text{log}} \in \{\text{Consolidated}, \text{Split-Pack-Cons-Del}, \text{Split-Del}\}$ captures the local cardinality ratio $(|O_{\text{Package}}|, |O_{\text{Delivery}}|)$ as $(1,1)$, $(>1, 1)$, or $(>1, >1)$ without rework.
  * $\text{Arch}_{\text{fin}} \in \{\text{Single-Settle}, \text{Installment-Settle}\}$ captures whether $|O_{\text{Payment}}| = 1$ or $>1$ (with `partially_settles` qualifiers).
  * $\text{Bound}_{\text{log}}, \text{Bound}_{\text{fin}} \in \{\text{Isolated}, \text{Shared}\}$ indicate whether logistics events (`Pack`, `Dispatch`) or financial events (`Issue Invoice`, `Record Payment`) reference `EXT` objects.
  * $\text{Exc} \in \{\text{None}, \text{Pre-Dispatch-Sub}, \text{Post-Dispatch-Return}\}$ captures lifecycle anomaly motifs evidenced by O2O replacement qualifiers (`removed_before_dispatch` vs. `Return Item` + `replaces`).
  $X_a \equiv_{\text{sem}} X_b \iff \mathcal{F}(X_a) = \mathcal{F}(X_b)$.
* **Invariants Preserved:** Fulfillment consolidation strategy, financial settlement structure, cross-order dependency boundaries, and item/package exception lifecycles.
* **Information Discarded:** Exact item counts (e.g., 2 vs. 3 items in a single box), exact installment counts beyond binary $>1$, and exact interleaved ordering of independent parallel branches (e.g., `Pack P1 -> Pack P2 -> Dispatch` vs. `Pack P1 -> Dispatch -> Pack P2`).
* **Equivalence Properties:** Reflexive, symmetric, and transitive, since it is induced by the kernel of a deterministic function $\mathcal{F}$ into a discrete feature space.
* **Handling Concurrency / Same-Time Events:** Because $\mathcal{F}(X)$ relies on graph-level cardinalities, boundary flags, and motif presence rather than fine-grained event interleavings, it is naturally invariant to concurrency and timestamp jitter between independent sub-processes.

## 2. Canonical signatures

Below is a reproducible Python-like implementation that computes both the exact structural canonical signature ($\equiv_{\text{full-iso}}$) via Weisfeiler-Lehman-style iterative color refinement and projects it onto the preferred business-semantic signature ($\equiv_{\text{sem}}$).

```python
from collections import Counter, defaultdict
import hashlib


def compute_canonical_signatures(execution):
    """Computes both the Full Typed-Structural Signature (equiv_full_iso)

    and the Preferred Business-Semantic Signature (equiv_sem).

    execution format:
      .events: list of dicts {'id': str, 'act': str, 'ts': int, 'objs':
      list[(type, id)]}
      .o2o: list of tuples (src_id, tgt_id, qualifier)
      .obj_meta: dict {obj_id: {'type': str, 'is_ext': bool}}
    """
    events = sorted(execution.events, key=lambda e: e["ts"])
    obj_meta = execution.obj_meta
    o2o = execution.o2o

    # 1. Build Event-to-Object incidence & Object lifecycles
    obj_event_hist = defaultdict(list)
    for idx, ev in enumerate(events):
        for o_type, o_id in ev["objs"]:
            obj_event_hist[o_id].append((idx, ev["act"]))

    # 2. Compute deterministic initial object colors (invariant to local ID renaming)
    obj_colors = {}
    for o_id, meta in obj_meta.items():
        scope = "EXT" if meta["is_ext"] else "LOC"
        hist_sig = tuple(obj_event_hist[o_id])
        obj_colors[o_id] = f"{meta['type']}:{scope}:{hist_sig}"

    # 3. Refine object colors using O2O qualifiers (2 rounds of WL refinement)
    for _ in range(2):
        new_colors = {}
        for o_id in obj_meta:
            out_edges = sorted(
                [
                    (q, obj_colors[tgt])
                    for src, tgt, q in o2o
                    if src == o_id
                ]
            )
            in_edges = sorted(
                [
                    (q, obj_colors[src])
                    for src, tgt, q in o2o
                    if tgt == o_id
                ]
            )
            combined = (
                obj_colors[o_id],
                tuple(out_edges),
                tuple(in_edges),
            )
            new_colors[o_id] = hashlib.md5(
                str(combined).encode()
            ).hexdigest()[:8]
        obj_colors = new_colors

    # 4. Canonicalize Event Sequence with refined object color multisets
    canon_events = []
    for idx, ev in enumerate(events):
        participating_colors = sorted(
            [
                f"{obj_meta[o_id]['type']}_{'EXT' if obj_meta[o_id]['is_ext'] else 'LOC'}#{obj_colors[o_id]}"
                for _, o_id in ev["objs"]
            ]
        )
        canon_events.append(
            f"{idx}:{ev['act']}[{','.join(participating_colors)}]"
        )

    # 5. Canonicalize O2O Graph
    canon_o2o = sorted(
        [
            f"{obj_colors[src]}-{q}->{obj_colors[tgt]}"
            for src, tgt, q in o2o
        ]
    )

    full_iso_signature = (
        "EVENTS("
        + "; ".join(canon_events)
        + ") | O2O("
        + "; ".join(canon_o2o)
        + ")"
    )

    # 6. Project to Business-Semantic Signature (equiv_sem)
    loc_types = Counter(
        meta["type"]
        for meta in obj_meta.values()
        if not meta["is_ext"]
    )
    acts = [e["act"] for e in events]
    qualifiers = {q for _, _, q in o2o}

    # Logistics Architecture
    if "Return Item" in acts:
        log_arch = "REWORK_DELIVERY"
    elif loc_types["Package"] == 1 and loc_types["Delivery"] == 1:
        log_arch = "1P_1D"
    elif loc_types["Package"] > 1 and loc_types["Delivery"] == 1:
        log_arch = "MP_1D"
    else:
        log_arch = "MP_MD"

    # Financial Architecture
    fin_arch = (
        "INSTALLMENT_PAY"
        if (
            loc_types["Payment"] > 1
            or "partially_settles" in qualifiers
        )
        else "SINGLE_PAY"
    )

    # Boundary Scope
    ext_in_log = any(
        obj_meta[o_id]["is_ext"]
        for e in events
        if e["act"] in {"Pack Package", "Dispatch"}
        for _, o_id in e["objs"]
    )
    ext_in_fin = any(
        obj_meta[o_id]["is_ext"]
        for e in events
        if e["act"] in {"Issue Invoice", "Record Payment"}
        for _, o_id in e["objs"]
    )
    boundary = f"LOG:{'EXT' if ext_in_log else 'LOC'}_FIN:{'EXT' if ext_in_fin else 'LOC'}"

    # Exception / Lifecycle Motif
    if "Return Item" in acts or "Inspect Return" in acts:
        exc_motif = "EXC:POST_DISPATCH_RETURN"
    elif "removed_before_dispatch" in qualifiers:
        exc_motif = "EXC:PRE_DISPATCH_SUB"
    else:
        exc_motif = "EXC:NONE"

    sem_signature = (
        f"[{log_arch} | {fin_arch} | {boundary} | {exc_motif}]"
    )
    return full_iso_signature, sem_signature
```

## 4. Cluster interpretations

### SEM-1: Isolated Baseline Fulfillment (`{X1, X2}`)
* **Concise Signature:** `[1P_1D | SINGLE_PAY | LOG:LOC_FIN:LOC | EXC:NONE]`
* **Business Behavior:** The "happy path" order-to-cash execution. All ordered items are packed into a single container, shipped in a single delivery, billed on one dedicated invoice, and settled via a single full payment, completely isolated from other customer orders.
* **Operational Risks / Opportunities:** Lowest operational overhead and clean audit trail. Opportunity for straight-through processing (STP) and automated invoice matching.
* **Flattening Blind Spots:** Well-represented by traditional `Order`-flattened logs because all subordinate object types (`Package`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`, `Payment`) stand in 1:1 correspondence with `Order`. However, flattening still loses the 1:2 `Order`-to-`Item` fan-out.
* **Stability under Data Repair:** Highly stable. Missing O2O links between `Package` and `Delivery` can be deterministically inferred from shared participation in `Dispatch`.

### SEM-2: Split-Package Consolidated Delivery (`{X3}`)
* **Concise Signature:** `[MP_1D | SINGLE_PAY | LOG:LOC_FIN:LOC | EXC:NONE]`
* **Business Behavior:** Items within the order are separated into multiple physical packages (`P31`, `P32`)—for instance, due to weight limits, item fragility, or picking from different warehouse zones—but staged together at the loading dock to leave in a single consolidated shipment (`D31`).
* **Operational Risks / Opportunities:** Warehouse picking/packing labor increases, and staging delays may occur while `D31` waits for the slowest package to be packed. However, shipping freight cost is optimized by avoiding split carrier dispatches.
* **Flattening Blind Spots:** Flattening on `Order` duplicates `Pack Package` (convergence/divergence distortion), making it look like rework on the order. Flattening on `Package` duplicates `Create Order`, `Dispatch`, `Issue Invoice`, and `Record Payment`.
* **Stability under Data Repair:** Moderately stable. If warehouse scanners fail to log one `Pack Package` event, repair algorithms seeing two packages in `Dispatch` would reconstruct the missing pack event, preserving `SEM-2`.

### SEM-3: Multi-Shipment & Installment Settlement (`{X4}`)
* **Concise Signature:** `[MP_MD | INSTALLMENT_PAY | LOG:LOC_FIN:LOC | EXC:NONE]`
* **Business Behavior:** The order undergoes full logistics splitting—each item is packed and dispatched independently (`P41->D41`, `P42->D42`), typical of backorders or multi-warehouse fulfillment. Financially, a single invoice `V41` is settled across multiple partial payments (`M41`, `M42`).
* **Operational Risks / Opportunities:** High freight cost from multiple deliveries and elevated credit/collections risk due to partial settlement delays. Opportunity to align invoicing with per-delivery milestones rather than issuing a single consolidated invoice.
* **Flattening Blind Spots:** Catastrophic under single-case flattening. Flattening on `Order` interleaves two independent `Pack -> Dispatch` threads and turns two distinct `Payment` lifecycles into a self-loop on `Record Payment`.
* **Stability under Data Repair:** Sensitive to timestamp repair and payment aggregation. If an ETL pipeline aggregates partial payments on the same day into one ledger entry, `SEM-3` shifts toward a logistics-only split cluster.

### SEM-4: Cross-Order Logistics Consolidation (`{X5}`)
* **Concise Signature:** `[1P_1D | SINGLE_PAY | LOG:EXT_FIN:LOC | EXC:NONE]`
* **Business Behavior:** While billing remains strictly order-specific (`V51` bills only `O5`), physical fulfillment is pooled across orders: `P51` and `D51` combine items from `O5` and an external order `EXT-OA` (e.g., same customer and destination address merged at packing).
* **Operational Risks / Opportunities:** Major freight and packaging cost savings. Risk of customs, delivery, or return complications if a physical delay on `EXT-IA` holds up the dispatch of `O5`, or if freight cost allocation across orders is distorted.
* **Flattening Blind Spots:** Completely invisible when flattened on `Order:O5` (looks identical to `X1`). Flattening on `Package` or `Delivery` merges two separate orders into one trace, duplicating `Create Order` and `Issue Invoice`.
* **Stability under Data Repair:** Vulnerable to boundary truncation. If an extraction script filters the event log strictly to objects created by `O5` and drops `EXT-*` foreign keys, `X5` collapses into `SEM-1`.

### SEM-5: Cross-Order Consolidated Billing (`{X6}`)
* **Concise Signature:** `[1P_1D | SINGLE_PAY | LOG:LOC_FIN:EXT | EXC:NONE]`
* **Business Behavior:** Physical fulfillment is dedicated solely to `O6`, but financial execution is pooled: a single collective invoice `V61` and payment `M61` cover both `O6` and `EXT-OB` (e.g., monthly summary invoicing for a B2B buyer).
* **Operational Risks / Opportunities:** Reduces accounts-receivable transaction fees and matches B2B customer preferences. High risk in dispute management: a dispute on `EXT-OB` can block payment `M61`, delaying revenue recognition for `O6` because `M61` lacks order-level allocation.
* **Flattening Blind Spots:** Invisible when flattened on `Order:O6` (indistinguishable from `X1`). Flattening on `Invoice` merges the logistics histories of `O6` and `EXT-OB` into a single convoluted trace.
* **Stability under Data Repair:** Like `SEM-4`, unstable if extraction scopes drop external `Order` references on shared financial events, collapsing `X6` into `SEM-1`.

### SEM-6: Pre-Dispatch Item Substitution (`{X7}`)
* **Concise Signature:** `[1P_1D | SINGLE_PAY | LOG:LOC_FIN:LOC | EXC:PRE_DISPATCH_SUB]`
* **Business Behavior:** Both `I71` and `I72` are added and initially associated with `P71`, but prior to shipment, `I71` is pulled from the package (`removed_before_dispatch`) and replaced by `I72` (`replaces`), which is explicitly confirmed on the `Dispatch` event.
* **Operational Risks / Opportunities:** Indicates inventory inaccuracy (stockout or damaged stock discovered at packing/staging) or last-minute order modification. Risk of billing mismatch if `V71` bills the original item price rather than the substituted item.
* **Flattening Blind Spots:** Completely hidden by `Order`-flattened control-flow discovery (`X7` has the exact same 7-activity sequence as `X1`). Only visible when retaining E2O item links on `Dispatch` and O2O relational qualifiers.
* **Stability under Data Repair:** Fragile. If a data-cleaning routine drops E2O item links on `Dispatch` (assuming `Dispatch` only connects to `Order`, `Package`, `Delivery`) or strips O2O qualifiers, `X7` merges with `SEM-1`.

### SEM-7: Post-Dispatch Return & Reshipment (`{X8}`)
* **Concise Signature:** `[REWORK_DELIVERY | SINGLE_PAY | LOG:LOC_FIN:LOC | EXC:POST_DISPATCH_RETURN]`
* **Business Behavior:** Initial fulfillment completes (`P81`, `D81`), but item `I82` is returned (`Return Item`), inspected (`Inspect Return`), repacked into a new container (`P82`), and reshipped via a second delivery (`D82`) before final invoicing and payment.
* **Operational Risks / Opportunities:** Direct margin erosion from reverse logistics, inspection labor, double shipping, and delayed invoicing (`V81` waits until after `D82`). Signals quality defects, picking errors, or transit damage on `D81`.
* **Flattening Blind Spots:** Flattening on `Order` shows the rework loop at the activity level but obscures *which* item returned (1 of 2 items vs. whole order) and hides the creation of a distinct replacement `Package` (`P82`) and `Delivery` (`D82`).
* **Stability under Data Repair:** Very high stability. The presence of dedicated exception activities (`Return Item`, `Inspect Return`) and extra object instances makes this cluster robust even if O2O qualifiers (`replaces`) are lost.

## 5. Borderline cases

### X1 versus X2 (Identifier Order vs. Structural Symmetry)
* **Why definitions agree/disagree:** Raw string-based hashing (such as uncanonicalized object-ID serialization) separates `X1` and `X2` because in `X1` the first `Add Item` binds `I11` and the second binds `I12`, whereas in `X2` the first binds `I22` and the second binds `I21`, while `Pack Package` lists `I21,I22`. However, every defensible equivalence relation ($\equiv_{\text{act}}$, $\equiv_{\text{loc-iso}}$, $\equiv_{\text{full-iso}}$, $\equiv_{\text{sem}}$) **merges** `X1` and `X2`. Under graph isomorphism, the bijection $\phi_O(I_{11}) = I_{22}$ and $\phi_O(I_{12}) = I_{21}$ makes the two executions identical. Treating `I21 < I22` lexicographically as semantically distinct violates identifier-invariance.

### X1 versus X5 (Local Subgraph Isolation vs. External Logistics Coupling)
* **Why definitions disagree:** $\equiv_{\text{act}}$ and $\equiv_{\text{loc-iso}}$ **merge** `X1` and `X5`. From the strictly local perspective of `Order:O5`, there is 1 Order, 2 Items, 1 Package, 1 Delivery, 1 Invoice, and 1 Payment following the exact same 7 activities. Conversely, $\equiv_{\text{full-iso}}$ and $\equiv_{\text{sem}}$ **separate** them because `Pack Package` and `Dispatch` in `X5` are incident to `EXT-OA` and `EXT-IA`. A warehouse manager needs them separated (shared physical container `P51`), whereas a single-order customer portal tracker might treat them as equivalent.

### X1 versus X6 (Dedicated vs. Pooled Financial Settlement)
* **Why definitions disagree:** Just like `X1` vs `X5`, $\equiv_{\text{act}}$ and $\equiv_{\text{loc-iso}}$ **merge** `X1` and `X6` because the local subgraph induced by `O6` and its local objects is isomorphic to `X1`. However, $\equiv_{\text{full-iso}}$ and $\equiv_{\text{sem}}$ **separate** them because `Issue Invoice` and `Record Payment` bind `EXT-OB`, and `M61` settles `V61` without order-level allocation. For logistics KPIs, `X1` and `X6` are identical; for financial auditing and credit risk, they belong to fundamentally different regimes.

### X1 versus X7 (Control-Flow Similarity vs. Qualified E2O/O2O Semantics)
* **Why definitions disagree:** $\equiv_{\text{act}}$ **merges** `X1` and `X7` because both execute the exact same 7-activity sequence (`Create -> Add -> Add -> Pack -> Dispatch -> Issue -> Record`) with identical local object counts per type (1 Order, 2 Items, 1 Package, 1 Delivery, 1 Invoice, 1 Payment). However, $\equiv_{\text{loc-iso}}$, $\equiv_{\text{full-iso}}$, and $\equiv_{\text{sem}}$ **separate** them on two grounds: (1) in `X7`, the `Dispatch` event has an explicit E2O link to `Item:I72` (absent in `X1`), breaking E2O bipartite isomorphism, and (2) the O2O graph in `X7` contains the `replaces` edge between items and the `removed_before_dispatch` qualifier between `P71` and `I71`.

### X3 versus X4 (Warehouse Split vs. End-to-End Logistics & Payment Split)
* **Why definitions disagree:** A coarse "Multi-Package Fulfillment" business rule that only asks whether $|O_{\text{Package}}| > 1$ would **merge** `X3` and `X4`. However, all four of our formal relations ($\equiv_{\text{act}}$, $\equiv_{\text{loc-iso}}$, $\equiv_{\text{full-iso}}$, $\equiv_{\text{sem}}$) **separate** them. `X3` packs two boxes (`P31`, `P32`) and ships them in a single delivery (`D31`) with a single payment (`M31`), whereas `X4` interleaves packing and dispatching into two separate deliveries (`D41`, `D42`) and splits settlement across two partial payments (`M41`, `M42`).

### X4 versus X8 (Planned Multi-Delivery vs. Exception-Driven Rework Delivery)
* **Why definitions disagree:** An object-count summary clustering that only counts local logistics multiplicities ($|O_{\text{Item}}|=2, |O_{\text{Package}}|=2, |O_{\text{Delivery}}|=2, |O_{\text{Invoice}}|=1$) would **merge** the logistics footprint of `X4` and `X8`. However, $\equiv_{\text{act}}$, $\equiv_{\text{loc-iso}}$, $\equiv_{\text{full-iso}}$, and $\equiv_{\text{sem}}$ all **separate** them. In `X4`, the two deliveries carry disjoint items (`I41` in `P41/D41`, `I42` in `P42/D42`) as parallel forward fulfillment. In `X8`, `I82` is shipped twice (`P81/D81` then `P82/D82`) via an explicit reverse-logistics rework loop (`Return Item`, `Inspect Return`) with a `replaces` O2O relation between `P82` and `P81`.

## 6. Relation to `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel`

### How `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel` works and its limitations
In PM4Py, `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel, obj_type, max_objs=...)` extracts an object-centric sub-log (execution) around each lead object of `obj_type` (via connected components or ego-network extraction in the object interaction graph) and groups executions into equivalence classes.
1. **What the PM4Py key structure preserves:** It preserves the sequence of activity labels, the exact typed E2O incidence structure relative to the extracted subgraph, and the structural relationships between objects of different types participating in the events.
2. **Why raw object identifiers can make exact clusters too fine:** If an implementation or naive post-processor serializes event-object relations using raw object IDs or sorts participating objects lexicographically by raw string ID before assigning canonical indices (e.g., sorting `I21` before `I22` in `X2` even though `I22` appeared in the first `Add Item` event), `X1` and `X2` fail to match. Furthermore, if external objects (`EXT-*`) are pulled into the subgraph, any difference in the size or history of the external order's component splits clusters into singleton classes.
3. **Canonicalization and post-processing:** To obtain stable business clusters, we must (a) canonicalize local object IDs purely by their *first appearance timestamp and activity role* (or Weisfeiler-Lehman structural color) rather than string sorting, and (b) project the fine-grained PM4Py structural clusters onto semantic feature vectors (e.g., collapsing exact package/payment counts into architectural motifs and enriching with O2O qualifiers that standard E2O-based OCEL clustering may ignore).
4. **When exact equivalence is preferable:** Exact structural equivalence ($\equiv_{\text{full-iso}}$) is preferable for **automated test-case generation, digital-twin simulation replay, and strict process-mining conformance checking**, where any variation in E2O wiring or object cardinality changes token replay semantics.
5. **Impact of `max_objs` and large connected executions:** When orders share packages (`X5`) or invoices (`X6`), the full connected component in the object interaction graph can snowball into a giant component spanning thousands of orders ("super-executions"). Setting `max_objs` bounds the ego-network expansion around the lead `Order`, preventing memory blowup and ensuring that `O5` and `O6` are clustered by their immediate boundary interaction (`EXT` stubs) rather than merging the entire company database into one massive cluster.

### Executable Python Outline

```python
from collections import defaultdict
import pm4py


def cluster_and_canonicalize_orders(ocel, max_objs=15):
    """Uses pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel centered on 'Order' and post-processes

    the structural clusters into canonical business-semantic clusters.
    """
    # Step 1: Extract structural clusters around lead object type 'Order'
    # PM4Py returns a dict mapping representative keys/clusters to lists of sub-OCELs
    raw_clusters = pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(
        ocel, "Order", max_objs=max_objs
    )

    semantic_clusters = defaultdict(list)

    for struct_key, ocel_list in raw_clusters.items():
        for sub_ocel in ocel_list:
            # Identify the lead Order ID in this extracted sub-execution
            orders = sub_ocel.objects[
                sub_ocel.objects[sub_ocel.object_type_column] == "Order"
            ]
            lead_order_id = orders[sub_ocel.object_id_column].iloc[0]

            # Step 2: Custom canonicalization invariant to local ID sorting
            events_df = sub_ocel.events.sort_values(by=sub_ocel.event_timestamp)
            relations_df = sub_ocel.relations.sort_values(
                by=sub_ocel.event_timestamp
            )
            o2o_df = getattr(sub_ocel, "o2o", None)

            # Distinguish local vs external objects relative to lead Order creation/scope
            all_objs = sub_ocel.objects.set_index(sub_ocel.object_id_column)[
                sub_ocel.object_type_column
            ].to_dict()
            ext_objs = {
                oid for oid in all_objs if str(oid).startswith("EXT-")
            } | {
                oid
                for oid, otype in all_objs.items()
                if otype == "Order" and oid != lead_order_id
            }

            # Count local object multiplicities by type
            local_type_counts = defaultdict(int)
            for oid, otype in all_objs.items():
                if oid not in ext_objs:
                    local_type_counts[otype] += 1

            # Extract activity sequence and boundary flags
            act_seq = list(events_df[sub_ocel.event_activity])
            log_rels = relations_df[
                relations_df[sub_ocel.event_activity].isin(
                    ["Pack Package", "Dispatch"]
                )
            ]
            fin_rels = relations_df[
                relations_df[sub_ocel.event_activity].isin(
                    ["Issue Invoice", "Record Payment"]
                )
            ]

            has_ext_log = any(
                oid in ext_objs for oid in log_rels[sub_ocel.object_id_column]
            )
            has_ext_fin = any(
                oid in ext_objs for oid in fin_rels[sub_ocel.object_id_column]
            )

            # Extract O2O qualifiers if present in the OCEL 2.0 table
            qualifiers = set()
            if o2o_df is not None and not o2o_df.empty:
                qualifiers = set(o2o_df["ocel:qualifier"].dropna().unique())

            # Step 3: Aggregate into semantic signature
            log_arch = (
                "REWORK"
                if "Return Item" in act_seq
                else (
                    "1P_1D"
                    if local_type_counts["Package"] == 1
                    and local_type_counts["Delivery"] == 1
                    else (
                        "MP_1D"
                        if local_type_counts["Delivery"] == 1
                        else "MP_MD"
                    )
                )
            )
            fin_arch = (
                "INSTALLMENT"
                if local_type_counts["Payment"] > 1
                or "partially_settles" in qualifiers
                else "SINGLE_PAY"
            )
            exc_flag = (
                "RETURN"
                if "Return Item" in act_seq
                else (
                    "PRE_DISP_SUB"
                    if "removed_before_dispatch" in qualifiers
                    else "NONE"
                )
            )

            sem_sig = (log_arch, fin_arch, has_ext_log, has_ext_fin, exc_flag)
            semantic_clusters[sem_sig].append((lead_order_id, sub_ocel))

    return semantic_clusters
```

## 7. Robustness analysis

We evaluate how the 7 preferred semantic clusters (`SEM-1` through `SEM-7`) and structural clusters behave under five specific perturbations:

1. **Ignore all O2O qualifiers:**
   * *Impact:* Strips `partially_settles`, `completes_settlement`, `removed_before_dispatch`, and `replaces`.
   * *Cluster changes:* `X7` still remains distinct from `X1` under **typed-structural equivalence** ($\equiv_{\text{loc-iso}}$) *only* because `Dispatch` in `X7` retains an explicit E2O link to `Item:I72`. However, if E2O item links on `Dispatch` are not treated as an exception flag in $\mathcal{F}(X)$, `X7` **merges into `SEM-1` (`{X1, X2, X7}`)**. `X4` remains in `SEM-3` because the multiplicity of two distinct `Payment` objects (`M41`, `M42`) still signals installment settlement even without the `partially_settles` O2O string. `X8` remains in `SEM-7` because `Return Item` and `Inspect Return` events explicitly mark the return loop.

2. **Ignore external objects (`EXT-*` dropped from E2O and O2O):**
   * *Impact:* Truncates each execution strictly to objects locally instantiated for the lead `Order`.
   * *Cluster changes:* `X5` (`SEM-4`) and `X6` (`SEM-5`) lose their external scope flags (`LOG:EXT` and `FIN:EXT`) and become locally isomorphic to `X1` and `X2`. Consequently, `SEM-1`, `SEM-4`, and `SEM-5` **collapse into a single cluster `{X1, X2, X5, X6}`**, reducing the total number of semantic clusters from 7 to 5.

3. **Collapse repeated same-label events (stutter-equivalence on consecutive identical activities):**
   * *Impact:* Consecutive occurrences of `Add Item`, `Pack Package`, and `Record Payment` are compressed at the control-flow level.
   * *Cluster changes:* Under **activity-sequence equivalence ($\equiv_{\text{act}}$)**, `X3` (`Create -> Add+ -> Pack+ -> Dispatch -> Issue -> Record`) **merges with `X1, X2, X5, X6, X7`**, because `X3`'s two consecutive `Pack Package` events collapse into one! However, `X4` does *not* merge with `X3` even under stutter-equivalence because `X4` interleaves `Pack -> Dispatch -> Pack -> Dispatch` (non-adjacent repetitions). Under $\equiv_{\text{loc-iso}}$ and $\equiv_{\text{sem}}$, `X3` remains separate from `X1` as long as distinct `Package` object instances (`P31`, `P32`) are preserved in the collapsed event's E2O set.

4. **Treat `Pack` and `Dispatch` event order as a partial order rather than a total order:**
   * *Impact:* For independent packages $P_a, P_b$, we only enforce causal order $e_{\text{Pack}}(P_i) \prec e_{\text{Dispatch}}(P_i)$ per package, ignoring whether $e_{\text{Pack}}(P_2)$ occurs before or after $e_{\text{Dispatch}}(P_1)$.
   * *Cluster changes:* On `X1–X8`, cluster assignments **remain unchanged** because `X3` has only 1 `Dispatch` event (`D31` carrying both `P31` and `P32`) while `X4` has 2 `Dispatch` events (`D41`, `D42`). However, if we had an execution `X4'` that executed `Pack P1 -> Pack P2 -> Dispatch D1 -> Dispatch D2`, total-order structural equivalence would separate `X4` and `X4'`, whereas partial-order structural equivalence and $\equiv_{\text{sem}}$ merge them into the same cluster.

5. **Treat payment multiplicity as an amount attribute rather than structure:**
   * *Impact:* Collapses `M41` and `M42` in `X4` into a single `Record Payment` event with an attribute `installments=2`.
   * *Cluster changes:* `X4` now has a single `Payment` object and a single `Record Payment` event. Its financial dimension changes from `INSTALLMENT_PAY` to `SINGLE_PAY`. While `X4` still stays separate from `X1–X3` due to its split deliveries (`D41`, `D42`), its structural signature simplifies to a pure logistics-split archetype.

## 8. Limitations and recommendation

### Recommendations by analytical purpose

| Analytical Purpose | Recommended Equivalence Relation | Justification |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Compliance Monitoring** | **Boundary- & Qualifier-Aware Typed Isomorphism ($\equiv_{\text{full-iso}}$)** | Auditing segregation of duties, revenue recognition, and regulatory invoicing rules requires zero loss of structural fidelity. Shared invoices (`X6`), unshipped/substituted items (`X7`), and partial payments (`X4`) must never be masked by high-level abstractions. |
| **2. Operational Segmentation** | **Business-Semantic Equivalence ($\equiv_{\text{sem}}$)** | Process owners and warehouse/finance leads need a manageable number of interpretable process variants (e.g., consolidated vs. split delivery, isolated vs. pooled orders, clean vs. rework). $\equiv_{\text{sem}}$ avoids fragmenting clusters over minor item-count variations or parallel interleavings while preserving all 7 core operational regimes. |
| **3. Root-Cause Analysis** | **Boundary- & Qualifier-Aware Typed Isomorphism ($\equiv_{\text{full-iso}}$) with Partial-Order Concurrency** | Diagnosing bottlenecks (e.g., why `O5` was delayed by `EXT-OA` in `X5`, or why `I71` failed in `X7`) requires preserving exact E2O/O2O causality and external boundary links, while using partial orders to avoid false splits from timestamp concurrency noise. |
| **4. Descriptive Reporting** | **Business-Semantic Equivalence ($\equiv_{\text{sem}}$) (with $\equiv_{\text{act}}$ as a secondary L1 rollup)** | Executive dashboards require stable, mutually exclusive KPI buckets (e.g., "% Happy Path `SEM-1`", "% Cross-Order Pooled `SEM-4/5`", "% Return Rework `SEM-7`"). $\equiv_{\text{sem}}$ gives accurate volume shares without drowning stakeholders in hundreds of isomorphic graph variants. |

### Why no single equivalence notion is best for all four purposes
An inherent tension exists between **discriminative fidelity** and **generalization power** in object-centric process mining:
1. **The Granularity-Sparsity Trade-off:** In real-world OCEL logs, orders have variable numbers of items ($1 \dots 50$), packages ($1 \dots 10$), and payments. Exact structural isomorphism ($\equiv_{\text{loc-iso}}$ or $\equiv_{\text{full-iso}}$) treats an order with 2 items in 1 box as non-isomorphic to an order with 3 items in 1 box. For **descriptive reporting** and **operational segmentation**, this causes combinatorial cluster explosion (the "uniqueness problem"), hiding macro-level patterns.
2. **The Abstraction-Blindness Trade-off:** Conversely, coarse relations like $\equiv_{\text{act}}$ or local-only abstractions solve sparsity by discarding E2O cardinalities, O2O qualifiers, and `EXT` boundaries. While clean for high-level flowcharts, they catastrophically fail **compliance monitoring** and **root-cause analysis** by conflating normal execution (`X1`) with cross-order co-mingled billing (`X6`) and silent pre-dispatch item removals (`X7`). Therefore, mature object-centric analytics must maintain a multi-tiered equivalence hierarchy, storing $\equiv_{\text{full-iso}}$ canonical graphs at the base layer and projecting them dynamically onto $\equiv_{\text{sem}}$ for segmentation and reporting.
