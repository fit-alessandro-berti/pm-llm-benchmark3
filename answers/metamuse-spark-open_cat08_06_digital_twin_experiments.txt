# 1. Model scope and state variables

**Boundary of the digital twin:** `municipal service system + applicant/third-party response system + environment` that generates demand and policy constraints. The twin simulates from `Submit application` arrival through `Issue permit / Reject` including queues, resource contention, calendars, cross-case bundling and objection spillover. Outside scope: fee payment ledger, construction-phase inspections, appeals beyond log horizon, macro-economic drivers of demand (treated exogenously).

**Level of detail:** Discrete-event, stochastic, resource-constrained, non-stationary, with entity attributes and cross-case dependencies. Not independent-case replay. Individual activity execution modeled as `waiting for resource/calendar -> service -> routing / rework` with explicit distinction of municipal service time vs. waiting vs. applicant/third-party response time.

**Entities:**
- `Case` (token): attributes `case_id, case_type, district, architect_id, application_value, requires_fire, requires_environment, requires_public_notice, building_area_flag, prior_violations, related_group_id, age, SLA class, objection_latent_state`.
- `Application bundle`: ephemeral entity linking `k>=1` cases from same `architect_id + district + submission week` for batched inspection scheduling.
- `Objection episode`: latent neighborhood-level event affecting `n` cases in `district × time window` requiring public-notice extension.
- `Resource` agents: `clerk, planner, fire_reviewer, env_reviewer, scheduler, inspector, legal_officer, system` with individual calendars and skill masks.
- `Third party`: applicant response, consultant report.

**State variables (system state `S_t`):**
- Queues per activity: `Q_completeness, Q_planning, Q_fire, Q_env, Q_public_notice, Q_inspection_sched, Q_inspection_exec, Q_drafting, Q_legal` with per-case `entry_time, priority, waiting_class`.
- Resource state: `available/busy/off-calendar/on-non-permit-duty` per resource, `next_available_time`, `current_allocation`, `travel_position` for inspectors.
- Calendar state: `is_working_hour(resource, t)`, `council_meeting_week(t)`, `public_holiday(t)`, `effective_capacity(t) = nominal_capacity × calendar_open × non_permit_duty_fraction`.
- Case progress vector: `enabled_activities, completed_activities, join_counter` for AND-split `+{Planning, Fire, Env}` and synchronization before `Decision drafting`.
- Backlog & age: `WIP(t), WIP_by_type/district, queue_length(t), age_distribution, overdue_flag`.
- Grouping state: `open_bundle_buffer[district]` with cases waiting for batch window; `last_bundle_dispatch_time`.
- Latent processes: `objection_risk_score(district,t)`, `architect_burst_state[architect_id]`.
- Metrics state: `throughput(t), utilization(t), SLA_breach_count, rework_count`.

**Queues & dependencies:**
- Parallel branch join: `Decision drafting` enabled only when all required of `{Planning review completed, Fire if required completed, Env if required completed, Public-notice period elapsed, Site inspection completed if required}`.
- Cross-case dependencies: (1) inspection bundling: co-waiting in scheduler queue, (2) pooled objection: extension duration correlated within district-time window, (3) architect capacity: submission burst increases applicant response delay mean, (4) legal council calendar blocks multiple cases simultaneously.

# 2. Parameter-estimation plan

All estimation starts from PM4Py log/model handling; simulation parameters are derived from those statistics, not hand-coded. The plan separates *observable log facts* from *inferred model parameters* due to missing start timestamps.

**PM4Py foundation (log/model handling):**
1. `pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id='case:concept:name', activity_key='concept:name', timestamp_key='time:timestamp')` after ensuring `time:timestamp` is `datetime64[ns, UTC]`.
2. Extract case- and trace-level stats: `pm4py.get_start_activities / get_end_activities`, `pm4py.statistics.traces.generic.log.case_statistics.get_median_case_duration`, filtering by `case_type, district`.
3. Variant analysis: `pm4py.statistics.variants.log.get.get_variants(log)` + `pm4py.get_variants_as_tuples` to map 7 variant groups in prompt to empirical shares. Validate process tree via `pm4py.discover_process_tree_inductive` and compare to given tree; use token-replay/conformance for fitness.
4. DFG & performance DFG: `dfg, sa, ea = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)` and `perf_dfg = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)` (`business_hours=False` then recalc with calendars). Use `perf_dfg` to get `median/mean/p90 sojourn between activities`.
5. Arrival handling: derive `case_arrival_times = log.groupby(case:concept:name).first()['time:timestamp'].sort_values()` then inter-arrival times; stratify by `month, weekday, architect_id`.

**Arrivals (non-stationary):**
- Base seasonal rate: Poisson/NHPP via piecewise-constant rate `_0(m)`: Jan-Feb baseline, Mar-May/Sep-Nov `1.7×`, other months interpolated from log. Estimate `_0 = cases_per_day / working_days` per month with `case_arrival_average = total_cases / 540 days` as reference.
- Day-of-week multiplier: `w_dow[d]`: Monday `2.2×` Wed, compute `w_dow = empirical submissions on d / mean submissions` via PM4Py case start time histogram.
- Architect burst: two-level model: (a) background NHPP for small architects, (b) clustered bursts for top-20 architects: estimate burst arrival as Hawkes or Poisson cluster: infer `burst_inter-arrival ~ Exp` and `burst_size ~ Geometric(p)` from same `architect_id` with `t 7d`. Compute `31%` share verification.
- Validation: compare simulated Monday share and seasonal ratio to log via ².

**Service times vs. waiting times (with missing start timestamps):**
- If log has only completion (`time:timestamp`), *do not equate sojourn to service*. Compute `performance DFG` sojourn `T_sojourn = t_{i+1}_complete - t_i_complete`.
- Inference split: For resources with observed start/completion subset, estimate `service ~ LogNormal/Fitted` directly; for missing-start activities, deconvolve: `service = T_sojourn - waiting_calendar_adjusted` using queueing inference: `median waiting before activity` from prompt as prior; estimate `waiting = max(0, T_sojourn - service_p50)` and calibrate waiting via queue length simulation iteratively (simulation-based calibration).
- Fit per `activity × case_type × resource_pool`: use `scipy`/`distfit` on service samples; select among `LogNormal, Gamma, Weibull` via AIC + tail QQ. Report `median/p90` matches to prompt table. Heavy-tailed `Receive missing documents, Env review, Public-notice` fit with `LogNormal` or `Pareto-lognormal mixture`; do not truncate tail without sensitivity.
- Calendar correction: subtract non-working hours using reconstructed calendars to get `business_time service`.

**Branching probabilities & routing:**
- Completeness rework: `P(Request missing docs | case_type)` from variant shares: `Simple with missing docs 19%` + part of `Other`. Estimate conditional on `application_value, architect tier` via logistic regression on log attributes.
- Fire/Env/Public-notice requirement: `P(requires_fire=yes  Fire review)` 1, but estimate hidden rule `P(Fire | area_threshold)` via proxy: `case_type=Commercial/Complex variance` has higher empirical Fire rate than flag suggests; model as `OR(flag, district_sensitive, type_prior)` with Bayesian calibration. Similarly for Env: sensitivity data incomplete  treat as latent Bernoulli with prior `Beta(,)` updated by `requires_environment` proxy + district random effect.
- Public-notice objection: never treat objection as observed. Estimate `P(objection | Public-notice required, district)` from *duration proxy*: `Public-notice period >14d + `. Fit mixture: `14d fixed + X` where `X=0` with `1-p_obj`, `X~ LogNormal(_obj,_obj)` with `p_obj`. `p_obj` identified from excess mass above 14d (e.g., median 14d vs p90 60d). Report as latent.
- AND-join for parallel reviews: routing `+{Planning, Fire, Env}`: `+` means parallel; enable via simulation join counter; estimate parallel overlap from timestamps: `overlap = intersection of [start, complete]` where available.

**Rework:**
- Types: `Completeness rework` (loop `*{Completeness check}`), `Inspection rework` (`*{Scheduling,Inspection}`), `Planning/Legal rework` implicit. Estimate `P(rework | stage, case_type, outcome)` from variant table: `Inspection rework 9%`, `Complex variance rework 8%`. For each loop iteration, fit `geometric iteration count` with case-type specific `p_loop`.
- Rework reasons unstructured  code into `applicant-driven / inspection-failure / legal_conflict` via text clustering small sample for simulation labeling, but keep routing stochastic with covariates `district, inspector, application_value`.

**Resource calendars:**
- Reconstruct per pool: `is_available(t)`. Estimate nominal FTE from HR data; validate against observed completion timestamps density per hour/weekday (PM4Py dotted chart `pm4py.view_dotted_chart`). Observed busy density gives `effective capacity`.
- Fire reviewers `Tue-Thu only`, Inspectors `Tue-Fri + travel`, Legal `blocked council meeting weeks`: encode as calendar mask; calibrate `effective FTE` (Env 40% non-permit = `0.6×3=1.8` effective).
- System tasks unconstrained.

**Cross-case dependencies:**
- Bundling: estimate `P(bundle | district, n_waiting)` from scheduler delay vs. batch size trade-off: simulation parameter `batch_window_hours` and `max_batch_size` tuned so simulated `Inspection scheduling waiting 3d median` matches log conditional on district D3/D5 travel penalty `+ X minutes`.
- Architect concurrent capacity: for top architects, `Receive missing documents` delay shows `corr(burst_size, delay)`  estimate multiplicative factor `delay = base_delay × (1+·burst_size)`.

# 3. Assumptions

**Arrivals:**
- NHPP with piecewise-constant monthly and day-of-week multipliers; independence across months except architect burst clustering. Assume 18-month log is representative of annual seasonality; no long-term trend beyond observed 1.7× factor unless stress-tested. Portal submissions occur per applicant local time; inter-arrival within day non-uniform (morning peak) estimated but if not in log assume uniform within portal hours for baseline.

**Service times:**
- Service time distributions stationary conditional on `activity × case_type × district` and resource skill; calendar time vs. business time clearly separated. Heavy-tailed where indicated (applicant delays, Env, Legal) modeled as lognormal, not exponential. For activities with missing start timestamps, inferred split is uncertain and treated as calibration parameter, not ground truth. No assumption that `p50 sojourn = p50 service`.

**Routing:**
- Fire/Environmental/Public-notice requirement follows observable flags `requires_*` *or* latent rules; incomplete sensitivity data means routing probabilities have epistemic uncertainty (±). Decision drafting join waits for all enabled predecessors (true AND). `X(tau, task)` is optional inclusive choice; `*` is Kleene loop with re-entry probability independent of history beyond case type (unless evidence of escalation).

**Resources/calendars:**
- HR calendars approximate true availability; Legal zero capacity during council weeks except `urgent` tag (rare emergency cases removed from log  assume <2% of volume). Clerk Mondays: 1 FTE diverted to portal support reduces Completeness capacity 20% that day. Env reviewers 40% non-permit time is random uniformly distributed across weeks, not synchronized. No overtime unless explicitly in intervention. Unlimited system capacity.

**Rework:**
- Rework loops are memoryless geometric with case-type specific `p`, except Complex variance shows increasing hazard with age (escalation). Rework incurs full re-service time, not delta. Applicant appeal after Legal maps to `Completeness rework` equivalent extension.

**Applicant/third-party response:**
- `Receive missing documents` and consultant Env reports are exogenous delays separate from municipal queues; applicant delay heavy-tailed (median 5d, long tail) and correlated with architect workload and case value. No municipal resource busy during applicant wait (case in `waiting for external`). Applicant no-show for inspection ~5% modeled as re-schedule.

**Cross-case dependencies:**
- Cases from same `architect_id` submitted within 7d and same district are *related* with probability ``; if related, inspection scheduling can be bundled for travel saving but first case incurs waiting for last case to be ready. Objection episodes affect groups of cases: public-notice extensions are spatially correlated (neighborhood), not i.i.d. per case. Inspector travel depends on district (D3/D5 penalty +15-30 min per leg) and batch geography.

**Non-stationary demand:**
- Seasonal 1.7× and Monday 2.2× multipliers persist; peak months (Mar-May, Sep-Nov) also increase `rework` and `objection` arrival modestly due to contention, not because case mix changes alone. Consider climate/holiday shocks as separate stress scenarios; baseline does not extrapolate beyond observed seasonality.

**Missing data:**
- Start timestamps missing at random conditional on resource, not informative censoring. Objection events unobserved  modeled as latent mixture, not assumed absent when duration =14d (some 14d+epsilon may still be no objection). Rework reason text not coded  assume routing estimate variance higher, use expert prior. Rare emergency decisions excluded  tail not understated; sensitivity includes injection of 1-3% urgent cases that bypass queues. Log flattening underrepresents dependencies  assume empirical DFG underestimates bundling potential by 10-20%.

# 5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty treatment

**Warm-up & run length:**
- Initial state `empty` is biased low vs. historical WIP (~600-900 open cases estimated from Little's Law with 46d mean throughput). Use warm-up via `Welch method` on `WIP(t)` and `average queue length Planning+Inspection`. Test `10 synthetic runs` to plot moving average; select warm-up `T_w = 90 days` (covers one full seasonal peak + Monday cycles) or until `WIP` stabilizes within ±10% of steady-state for baseline. For stressed scenarios longer `T_w=120d`.
- Alternative `MSER-5` (Marginal Standard Error Rule) on `throughput time series` to automate truncation.
- Run length: `540 days` (18 months) post-warm-up to match log horizon; report both `transient backlog from historical initial WIP` (initialized backlog sampled from empirical age distribution) and `steady-state` empty-start conditioned on warm-up discard. For policy horizon, also run `2-year` projection with seasonal cycling.
- Initialization: inject `initial WIP` by sampling `WIP_0` cases with ages from empirical remaining time distribution; resources start empty.

**Replications & random seeds:**
- Replications `n` adaptive: start `n=30`, compute half-width of 95% CI for ` median duration` and `SLA compliance difference`. Require `half-width  8% of effect size` or `±1.5 days` for median, `±3 days` for p90. For heavy-tail metrics increase to `n=80-120`. Use `law.n  (z·/)²` estimate after pilot.
- Variance reduction: Common Random Numbers (same arrival streams, service draws correlated) for paired `B vs Dx`; Antithetic variates optional.
- Separate seed streams per stochastic element: `arrival_seed, routing_seed, service_seed, calendar_seed, applicant_seed` via `numpy.random.SeedSequence` spawning to allow sensitivity without confounded RNG.

**Confidence / credible intervals:**
- Primary: bootstrap percentile 95% CI for `median, p75, p90, mean` via `10k bootstrap` on replication means (accounts for non-normality). For differences, `paired bootstrap` on per-replication deltas.
- For heavy-tailed durations: report `median [IQR] + p80, p90, p95, CV` not just mean; use `Harrell-Davis quantile estimator` for stable tail quantiles. Visualize `CCDF on log-scale` and `bootstrap CI bands`.
- For binomial SLA compliance: Wilson 95% CI.
- If Bayesian calibration of latent `p_obj, Env sensitivity`, report posterior credible intervals and propagate via posterior predictive simulations (sample ` ~ posterior` per replication).

**Sensitivity analysis:**
- Global: Morris screening on `10 key parameters` (Monday multiplier, Env mean service, legal calendar block length, applicant delay tail , batch window, rework p, objection p). Follow with Sobol indices on top 5 for `p90 Commercial`.
- Heavy-tail handling: (a) Winsorization sensitivity `99th vs 99.9th percentile cap` to show mean instability; (b) fit alternative `Pareto-lognormal vs LogNormal` and compare tail predictions; (c) EVT `Peaks-over-Threshold` `GPD` for durations >150d to estimate `P(duration > 200d)` with CI.
- Missing data sensitivity: multiple imputation for `service/waiting split` (3 plausible splits: optimistic short service / pessimistic long service / calendar-adjusted). Report interval of intervals.
- Non-stationarity sensitivity: `+ / - 20%` seasonal amplitude test.

**Robustness reporting:** Every `Dx` reported as `E[] [95% CI] (median), E[ p90], robustness rank under S1-S3` (worst-case degradation) to avoid spurious optimality claims.

# 6. Validation strategy

No claim of optimality before calibration + validation.

- Conformance: token-replay fitness `0.85` for baseline tree vs. log (`pm4py.conformance.fitness_token_based_replay`, `alignments`). Precision check to ensure model not over-general.
- Variant share reproduction: ² test for 7 variant groups; `26%,19%,18%,13%,8%,9%,7%` within `±3% absolute` per group after calibration.
- Temporal replay: deterministic replay using historical arrival timestamps + FCFS per resource pool; compare simulated `median/p90/median waiting before activity` to prompt table; target `median within ±20%`, `p90 within ±25%`. Dotted chart overlay `observed vs simulated event density`.

**Distributional validation:**
- For each activity sojourn and case duration (overall + by case type): `Kolmogorov-Smirnov, Anderson-Darling, QQ plots` per `case_type`. Heavy tails: compare `p50,p80,p90,p95` rather than relying on KS which is insensitive to tails; use `Cramér-von Mises weighted for tails`.
- Public-notice duration mixture: verify bimodal histogram (spike at 14d + long tail) reproduced; latent `p_obj` posterior predictive check.
- Inter-arrival: compare `seasonal (t), Monday effect, architect burst size distribution` via `² goodness-of-fit`.

**Resource-utilization validation:**
- Estimate observed utilization proxy from completion densities: `busy_rate  completions_per_hour / capacity_per_hour` during working hours. Simulated `utilization per pool` (Planners, Fire, Env, Inspectors, Legal) should rank `Legal  75-85%, Inspectors 70-80%, Env 60-70%` consistent with bottleneck cues (Planning wait 6d, Env 8d). Validate daily/weekly patterns: Monday clerk spike, Tue-Fri inspector peak, Legal dip in council weeks.
- Queue length / waiting time correlation: `Planners queue  Planning review waiting 6d`, etc. Use `Spearman` between `WIP_by_pool(t)` and `waiting_before(t)` in log-derived estimates.

**Bottleneck validation:**
- Identify bottleneck via `queueing theory + simulation`: mean queue before `Environmental review (8d) + Inspection (8d) + Legal (7d)`. Validate that simulated bottleneck shifts to `Inspection` when D2 removed vs. `Legal` for Commercial/Complex under S2. Bottleneck drift across seasons must match intuition (Legal in council-heavy months, Inspection in summer construction season).
- Check Little's Law consistency: `WIP  ·W` using log `31,200/540d 57.8 arrivals/d` and mean duration `~46d`  `WIP ~2,650` total including applicant wait; vs. municipal WIP ~800-1000. Discrepancy flags missing applicant-wait modeling.

**Out-of-sample validation:**
- Temporal hold-out: train on first 12 months, test on last 6 months with actual arrival stream; predict `weekly throughput, median duration by case_type, queue lengths Planning & Inspection` out-of-sample. Metrics `MAPE <15% for medians, <20% for p90`.
- Cross-district hold-out: train on `D1-D4`, test on `D3,D5` inspection travel penalty transferability.
- Counterfactual plausibility: test simulated D2 effect vs. external benchmark (other municipality that waived inspections) for order-of-magnitude reasonableness; not calibration target.

Failure to pass triggers recalibration loop adjusting `service/waiting split, effective FTE, objection latent parameters` before any policy recommendation.

# 7. Decision criteria

Decision is multi-criteria, Pareto-aware, with robustness and fairness constraints. No single optimum; interventions are ranked by `dominance + robustness`.

**Primary effectiveness metrics (per case_type and overall):**
- ` median duration` (days) and ` p90 duration`: threshold `meaningful if 95% CI excludes 0 and | median|  3d or | p90|  7d`. Flag `tail improvements` separately; a policy that only improves median but worsens p95 is not preferred for Complex variance.
- `SLA compliance %`: define SLA per type e.g. `Simple 21d 80%`, `New residential 60d 75%`, `Commercial 85d 70%`, `Complex 180d 60%` (illustrative, to be set by council). Require `+5 percentage points` with CI lower bound >0 to be *operationally significant*. Report Wilson CI.
- `Rework rate %`: `Rework cases / total`; goal `` but ensure D2/D8 not artificially  `Inspection rework` or `Legal rework`.

**Efficiency & capacity:**
- `Resource utilization %` per pool (busy_time / available_calendar_time): healthy `60-85%`; flag `>90%` as fragile, `<40%` as waste. Temporary inspector D4 acceptable if peak utilization drops 8572% without year-round <45%.
- `Throughput per month` and `Backlog WIP(t)` at horizon end: policy must not increase steady-state WIP vs baseline `+5%`.
- `Travel time per inspection` (for D3):  `15%` to justify bundling delay.

**Fairness / equity:**
- `District fairness`: `max_district p90 - min_district p90` for Simple & New residential; require not increase `>5d` vs baseline; ideally . D3/D5 penalty should not worsen under D3 batching (first-in-batch delay check).
- `Architect fairness`: Gini of delay by architect tier; large vs small architects ` median` difference 3d unless justified by bundle efficiencies.
- `Case-type protection`: Complex variance tail must not degrade under policies targeting Simple (D2/D3); constraint ` p90_Complex  +5d`.

**Robustness:**
- `Robustness score`: worst-case `SLA compliance` under S1-S3 vs baseline worst-case; Pareto-optimal if `dominates baseline in 2 of 3 stress tests` without >5% extra cost.
- `Sensitivity interval width`: policies with conclusions flipping under alternative heavy-tail or service/waiting split imputations are flagged `uncertain`.

**Selection rule (threshold + ranges):**
1. Filter: passes face + replay + distributional validation (Section 6).
2. Filter: meets `fairness & rework not worse` constraints.
3. Rank by `weighted benefit`: `0.35· median_normalized + 0.25· SLA_pp + 0.20·(- p90_tail_weighted) + 0.10·resource_efficiency + 0.10·robustness`. Weight elicited via council workshop.
4. Present Pareto frontier ` median vs cost vs fairness` with 95% CI ellipses; recommend *portfolio*: `D1+D2` low-cost quick wins, `D4` if backlog >1000, `D10` only if `tail risk S1` acceptable and legal coordination feasible.

**Uncertainty-aware decision language:** "Evidence suggests D2 reduces Simple median 6-9d (95% CI 4-11d) with 1-2% additional rework risk under mis-classification S3; recommend pilot with cap 50% eligibility + monitoring" rather than "optimal".
