## 2. Process-model consistency checks

A credible validation suite should be automated, run on every training extract and explanation artifact, and should treat missing sequence indices as a blocker for order-sensitive features.

**1. Prefix-time availability ledger and point-in-time tests.**  
Create a feature metadata registry containing: source system, computation logic, input event timestamps, `valid_from`, `valid_to`, transaction time, and snapshot time. For every case, assert that each feature’s observation time is no later than `t0`. For snapshots, do not accept a generic ±5 minute tolerance as proof of validity; require either `snapshot_time <= t0` or a documented, immaterial tolerance with sensitivity analysis. For derived features, recursively validate all upstream inputs. Limitation: if source timestamps or valid-time metadata are missing, the test can only quarantine the feature, not prove correctness.

**2. Prefix reconstruction and enabled-state checking.**  
Rebuild the prefix up to and including the first reliably ordered `Coverage Check Completed` event. At this `t0`, the normally occurred events are `Claim Registered` and `Coverage Check Completed`. Enabled or next-possible activities include `Documents Requested`, fast-track `Damage Assessment Completed`, and possibly `Fraud Investigation Opened` if a trigger already existed before or at `t0`. Future-possible but not yet enabled activities include `Documents Received`, `Documents Validated`, `Settlement Decision Recorded`, `Appeal`, and `Claim Closed`. An explanation referring to `Appeal`, `Claim Closed`, final decision, last resource, or full-trace variant is referring to information that is not prefix-available. Limitation: process deviations may be legitimate, so conformance failures should trigger review, not automatic deletion of the case.

**3. Activity-order feasibility and declarative constraints.**  
Use declarative rules such as: damage assessment precedes settlement decision; appeal only after Partial or Deny; a second `Settlement Decision Recorded` is legitimate only after an appeal; claim closure follows a final decision or withdrawal. At `t0`, completed `Claim Closed`, completed `Appeal`, and completed `Settlement Decision Recorded` are not feasible prefix states. A pattern such as `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` should fail unless a reliable sequence index proves an unusual but valid ordering. Limitation: declarative rules encode the normative model; they may flag rare but legitimate exceptions.

**4. Same-timestamp ambiguity checks.**  
For events with identical timestamps, require a reliable ingestion or causal sequence index. If the index is missing, order-sensitive patterns must be treated as unordered or invalid. Specifically, the Case Z `Claim Closed` and `Damage Assessment Completed` same-timestamp pair cannot support a directed `Closed -> Damage` pattern. Run stability tests by permuting same-timestamp events and checking whether pattern features or explanations change. Limitation: even a sequence index can be wrong if source systems post-process events; therefore, same-timestamp cases should be sampled manually.

**5. Complete-trace information detection.**  
Statically scan feature definitions and explanation outputs for complete-trace markers such as `total_`, `final_`, `last_`, `_anywhere`, `full_trace`, variant over entire case, and joins using `extraction_date`. At runtime, compare feature values computed as of `t0` with values computed at extraction; material differences indicate future contamination. Limitation: business names can hide future logic, so manual lineage review remains necessary.

**6. Explanation-to-process-state mapping.**  
Each explanation noun should be mapped to one of: already available at `t0`, enabled at `t0`, future-possible, process-inconsistent, or complete-trace-only. For example, `queue_length_at_t0` and `initial_damage_complexity` are available; `Documents Requested` is enabled or next possible; `Fraud Investigation Opened` is future-possible unless a trigger already exists; `Settlement Decision Recorded` is future-possible but not enabled; `Appeal` is future-possible only after a later Partial/Deny; `Claim Closed` is not an admissible completed activity at `t0`. Limitation: coarse labels can hide sub-activities, so mapping should use event attributes where available.

**7. Repeated `Settlement Decision Recorded` validation.**  
Because the label is coarse and may occur twice, validate that multiple decision events are semantically legitimate: an initial decision, then an appeal after Partial or Deny, then a reconsideration decision. If two decisions occur without an appeal, or if an appeal occurs without a prior Partial/Deny, flag as data-quality or process deviation. If the events share timestamps, require sequence indexing. Limitation: missing appeal events or coarse logging can make legitimate reconsiderations look invalid.

**8. Role and limits of DFG, footprint, alignment, and prefix checks.**  
Directly-follows graphs can reveal common paths, but a frequent DFG edge is not automatically a normative rule. Footprint matrices can detect impossible precedence relations, alignment checks can measure conformance to the normative model, and prefix checks can verify what is known at `t0`. None of these methods by itself proves causal relevance or operational actionability. They should be used together: prefix checks for availability, declarative checks for feasibility, and alignment/footprint checks for anomaly detection.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

The audit should not equate SHAP magnitude with truth, causality, or actionability. Different standards apply:

- **Predictive validity**: the feature is available at `t0`, reliably measured, and associated with the target in temporally honest validation. A feature can be predictively valid without being causal or fair.
- **Causal interpretation**: requires temporal precedence, absence of confounding, a plausible mechanism, and ideally intervention or quasi-experimental evidence. SHAP importance does not establish causality.
- **Operational actionability**: requires a controllable lever, evidence that changing it improves outcomes, and an understanding of side effects. A predictive signal may be real but not actionable.
- **Fairness review**: features correlated with protected or socioeconomic characteristics may be predictively useful but require disparate-impact analysis, necessity assessment, alternatives testing, and human oversight.

| Class | Surviving or repairable examples | What may be claimed | What may not be claimed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defensible predictive signal | `initial_damage_complexity`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, verified `initial_reserve`, repaired `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, repaired `same_day_document_count_snapshot` | These prefix-available attributes are statistically associated with higher or lower breach risk in historical data. | They do not prove causation, do not by themselves justify adverse treatment, and may not remain stable under process changes. |
| Process-state or congestion signal | `queue_length_at_t0`, elapsed time since registration, repaired `assigned_team_at_t0`, open prefix tasks if available | The claim enters a congested or time-constrained state, which is associated with reduced slack. | Congestion alone is not proven to cause breach, and staffing changes should not be justified solely by SHAP values. |
| Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review | `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, possibly team or reserve if they proxy sensitive factors | The feature may improve prediction but should be treated as a proxy and reviewed for disparate impact and necessity. | The feature must not be described as an individual’s responsibility, an inherent delay driver, or a reason for denial without fairness review. |
| Drift / calendar proxy | `calendar_week`, holiday indicators, policy-change indicators | Certain periods historically show different breach risk, possibly due to holidays, staffing, or policy changes. | The week itself is not causal; the signal may decay when policy or holiday patterns change. |
| Unusable until lineage is repaired | current `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_handler_team`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, current `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, current `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, `risk_score_current`, `last_resource_role`, future sequence patterns, `final_decision` | Nothing should be claimed from these features for `t0` explanation until they are removed, repaired, or recomputed as prefix-only equivalents. | They must not be presented as early warnings, causal reasons, or operational explanations. Some, such as total duration and final decision, should never be used in a `t0` model. |

Some features overlap: `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` is both a defensible predictive signal and a process-state signal. `claimant_region` is temporally available but should not be treated as a simple operational reason without fairness analysis.
