### Why the primary strategy is preferable

With only five branches, synthetic control suffers from a donor pool of two and cannot reliably reproduce Gamma’s pre-trend, which was already diverging due to the readiness pilot and case-mix shifts. Interrupted time series assumes a stable pre-period and a single interruption; here, the fraud desk (March), routing campaigns (April/July), staffing changes (May/June), and the May product shift all create multiple concurrent interruptions, making slope-level estimates fragile. The staggered event-study framework is preferable because it uses all available data—both never-treated controls and the pre-adoption windows of eventual adopters—to estimate a time-varying ATT rather than a single average. By including branch-specific linear trends and observable case-mix/staffing controls, it directly addresses the nonparallel-trend threat that is visible in the data (Gamma’s rising pre-period, Alpha/Beta’s volume/complexity shocks). Relative-time indicators allow explicit separation of learning dynamics (early shock vs. later improvement) from a static effect. Although standard two-way fixed effects with heterogeneous timing can be biased, an event-study specification with never-treated controls is more transparent: pre-coefficients serve as an internal placebo test, and interaction with intensity (`automated_fast_path_pct`) addresses treatment-version concerns. The main cost is small-cluster inference; but with only five branches, any design is descriptive. The event-study makes these limitations visible rather than hiding them in a single regression coefficient, and it can incorporate spillover proxies (fraud-desk period indicators) explicitly rather than assuming them away.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**Choose one:** `expand with an embedded evaluation`

**Specification:** Given endogenous rollout, small clusters, unmeasured fraud-spillover, and mixed median-vs-tail patterns, immediate universal expansion to Delta and Epsilon would forfeit the last credible opportunity for causal learning and risks cementing a suboptimal process design. Instead, roll out in a phased, embedded-evaluation design.

**Branch/subset for next step:** Promote Delta to automated availability in September 2026, not Epsilon. Delta is preferred because it has lower historical complexity (23% vs 38% in Epsilon), a smaller arrival base allowing cleaner measurement, and it gained one productive FTE in June, providing a capacity buffer to absorb automation-related routing adjustments without the severe staffing compression seen in Alpha. Epsilon should remain a pure holdout control through November, preserving a never-treated contrast for at least three months of post-adoption comparison.

**Treatment assignment / phase-in mechanism:** Use a standardized branch-level protocol rather than manager discretion. In Delta, adopt on 2026-09-01 with a two-phase intensity ramp: (1) Weeks 1–4 at “pilot intensity” requiring manual verification on all automated outputs and restricting automated fast-path to lower-complexity cases only; (2) Weeks 5+ at full protocol with gradual relaxation of verification as `automated_fast_path_pct` reaches 30%+. Within Delta, randomize case-level routing for the first month (e.g., alternate-day manual/automated assignment for borderline cases) only if operationally feasible; if not, use time-based phase-in with intensive process logging.

**Minimum measurements:** Require 3 months pre-period (Jun–Aug) fully documented with case-level event logs already available; 6 months post-period (Sep–Feb 2027). Do not evaluate before 3 months post, because learning curves (evident in Alpha/Beta) mean early months are likely to show shock rather than steady-state effects.

**Process-mining outcomes and queue-state data to collect:** Move beyond branch-month aggregates. Collect event-level traces (Register, Automated Triage, Manual Triage, Resolve, Specialist Review, Rework, Close) to compute:
- Path-specific cycle-time distributions (fast-path vs escalated vs manual);
- Branch-level specialist-queue wait times (not just total branch time);
- Central fraud-desk entry/exit times by case origin branch;
- Rework loop counts and stages (information-request vs review vs correction);
- Actual routing decision (automated vs manual override) for each case.
Also collect weekly FTE, arrival mix, and fraud-desk queue length.

**Spillover monitoring:** Track centralized fraud-desk wait times and case volume by originating branch; if wait times rise for Delta cases after adoption, the apparent branch-level cycle-time effect is partly spillover. Monitor whether routing campaigns redirect additional cases to Delta, separating routing effects from automation effects.

**Guardrails for customer harm:** Halt or redesign if any of the following occur for 2 consecutive months:
- Branch median cycle time exceeds Delta’s pre-period baseline +10%;
- P90 exceeds pre-period +15%;
- Escalation % exceeds 15% and is rising;
- `automated_fast_path_pct` <20% by month 3 (implementation failure);
- CSAT drops >0.2 pts from Delta’s baseline (with response-rate documentation required).

**Decision rule for scale-up:** Scale fully to Epsilon and consider redesign for Alpha/Beta only if Delta meets all of the following for 3 consecutive months by month 6:
1. `automated_fast_path_pct`  35%;
2. Branch-level median cycle time is at or below Epsilon’s trend-adjusted control level (accounting for complexity and arrivals);
3. P90 is not above Delta’s pre-period +10%;
4. Specialization-queue waits (new data) are stable or falling;
5. Rework and escalation are not rising relative to Delta’s pre-period;
6. Fraud-desk spillover does not explain >30% of any observed cycle-time change.

If Delta fails on intensity or if median/p90 harm persists despite case-mix adjustment, redesign routing rules and staffing ratios before any further expansion.

- **Observed:** After branch-level automation became available, the share of cases completing the automated fast path rose gradually from roughly 4–6% to 32–50% over three to five months, but never reached universal adoption, and adoption was staggered (Alpha/Beta in April; Gamma in July).
- **Observed:** Branch-month median and p90 cycle times increased in the first 2–3 months after adoption for Alpha and Beta, then partially declined in later months, while escalation percentages and rework rates remained elevated or increased slightly relative to pre-period levels.
- **Suggestive:** Later months (July–August) for all three adopters show lower median cycle times and lower p90 relative to the immediate post-adoption peak, coinciding with higher automated-fast-path use and possible organizational learning, though concurrent case-mix, routing, staffing, and fraud-desk changes prevent isolation of an automation benefit.
- **Not supported:** A precise causal statement such as “automation reduces cycle time by X%” is not supported because rollout was endogenous, treatment intensity varied and was not randomly assigned, control branches experienced nonparallel trends, and unmeasured queue spillovers through the centralized fraud desk confound branch-level outcomes.
