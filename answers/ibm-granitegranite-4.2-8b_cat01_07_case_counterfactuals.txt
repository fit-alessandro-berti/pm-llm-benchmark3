- **Temporal propagation:** D2 is likely free at Mon17:30 (D2 queue at Mon08:00 was only 2h), so D2 can start Document Check immediately, finishing within the 4–8h typical range. This removes the ~4.5h gap between Identity Check completion (Mon16:20) and Document Check start (Tue09:30) seen in fact. Assessment (A2) can begin shortly after Document Check finishes, potentially on the same day or the next morning.
- **Processfeasibility check:** Originals are required, so Originals Receipt (Mon17:30) precedes Document Check, satisfied. Identity Check and Document Check may be concurrent (no original needed for concurrency rule), so D2 and I2 can work together. The foureyes rule does not apply (no compliance review). Routing to D2 is permissible; D2’s slower pace is allowed.
- **Best/base/worst case:**  
  - Best: D2 completes Document Check in ~5h, finishes Mon22:30; Assessment starts Mon22:30–23:30 and ends Tue01:30–02:30; Decision can be made Tue09:00–10:00, reducing total cycle to ~1.5days.

- **Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If D2’s first review finds an inconsistency that requires external confirmation, a RequestMoreInfo would be issued, recreating the rework pattern; alternatively, if D2 is already busy with another case at originals arrival, the intervention would wait, negating the benefit.

*Rollout of the same policy to 100 similar cases in one week.*  
- **K104:** Reserving a provisional C1 slot for every highrisk/highvalue case would quickly saturate C1’s limited capacity (C1 queue fell from 20h to 5h over the week). Even if the reservation is “soft,” the mental commitment often leads operators to hold the slot, delaying the two urgent compliance cases added on Monday. The benefit of earlier preliminary screening is modest (0.1–0.3day reduction) but the interference—delaying other urgent compliance work—could be noticeable and reduce overall compliance throughput. The nonlinearity arises because a single reservation can block several cases if C1 is already queued.

- **K219:** Assigning all standard handwritten cases to D2 would shift ~20% of documentcheck load from D1 to D2. D1’s queue (already short at 5h by Thursday) would shrink, while D2’s queue (normally 4–8h) could rise if more than ~30% of cases are routed to D2, especially if some require clarification. The substitution is elastic; D2’s slower pace means that under moderate load the queue may increase modestly but assessment queue would likely shrink because document checks finish earlier on average. The risk is higher if external clarification rates are higher than anticipated (e.g., >30%). Weekend effects are minimal because D2 is not scheduled over weekends in the snapshots.

- **K337:** Suppressing the second RequestMoreInfo loop eliminates one Document Check, one Assessment, and one extra request cycle for each highrisk/highvalue case. Overall D1 and A1 queues would shrink noticeably, reducing assessment bottleneck pressure. However, compliance reviews would still need to fit into the existing C1 schedule; if many cases adopt the rule, C1 might receive compliance reviews clustered after Assessment2 rather than after Assessment3, potentially causing a temporary spike in C1 queue if several cases finish Assessment2 on the same day. The decisionpolicy change means auditors must accept decisions without the second confirmation; this could raise perceived compliance risk if the omitted confirmation is later deemed material.

- **K411:** Creating both tasks at Triage reduces the typical ~4–5h gap between Identity and Document tasks. Assessment can start earlier, which tends to shrink the Assessment queue if many cases parallelize, but if a large batch of cases simultaneously create concurrent tasks, the Assessment pool (A1/A2) could face higher concurrent demand in the early part of the day. Timestamp accuracy matters: realtime timestamps avoid falseconcurrency claims and help operators see true overlap; poor logging could lead operators to mistakenly think tasks are sequential, causing them to queue unnecessarily.

Overall, the most predictable interference comes from the scarce compliance resource (C1) in K104 and from the slower D2 in K219. Nonlinearity appears when the percentage of cases adopting an intervention crosses a small threshold (e.g., >20% for D2 or >30% for C1 reservation), causing queue jumps. Weekend/externalresponse effects are mainly felt in K337 because the second request often arrives on a weekday after a weekend delay; suppressing the loop avoids that delay but does not eliminate the inherent uncertainty of supplier response timing.

## 7. Final synthesis
The most robust intervention among the four is **K219 (assign D2 for standard handwritten documents)** because it changes only routing based on an observable condition (originals arrival) and has clear, measurable benefits (shorter cycle time) with a straightforward risk profile (possible rework). It does not reserve a scarce resource or alter decision policy, making it less sensitive to variations in C1 availability or external response timing.

The most dangerousdespitepotentialspeed intervention is **K337 (suppress the second RequestMoreInfo loop)**. While it eliminates a lengthy rework loop and can shorten the calendar cycle, it changes the decision policy (forcing a decision without the second supplier confirmation). This introduces compliance audit risk and may lead to higher appeal or loss exposure if the omitted confirmation turns out to be material. The benefit hinges on assumptions about evidence sufficiency that are uncertain.

A local counterfactual that works for one case (e.g., K104’s early compliance screen) is least transportable to a systemwide policy because it requires reserving a provisional C1 slot for every highrisk/highvalue case, which quickly saturates C1’s limited capacity and can delay other urgent compliance cases. The same reasoning holds for K411’s parallel verification: creating both tasks at Triage works for a single case but could cause Assessmentqueue congestion if many cases adopt the workaround simultaneously.

The most valuable event or queue data to collect for evaluating any of these interventions would be: (1) realtime taskstart timestamps (to confirm true concurrency vs perceived overlap), (2) actual queue lengths at each snapshot compared to forecasts (to assess forecast accuracy), (3) externalsupplier response latency (to gauge whether the second confirmation in K337 is typically needed), and (4) C1 reservation usage (whether the provisional slot is held or released) for K104. These four data points directly address the key uncertainties (C1 availability, D2 clarification rate, supplier timing, and forecast reliability) and enable calibration of the modestrange servicetime estimates.

## 8. Assumption audit
1. Register must be first and complete before Triage. *(structural)*
2. Identity Check and Document Check must both finish before Assessment. *(structural)*
3. When originals are required, Originals Receipt must precede Document Check. *(structural conditional)*
4. Highrisk or highvalue cases require a Compliance Review after the latest relevant Assessment and before Decision. *(structural conditional)*
5. Issuing a RequestMoreInfo creates a new Document Check and Assessment; highrisk/highvalue cases also need a new or reaffirmed Compliance Review. *(structural conditional)*
6. Queue estimates given at each timestamp are within ~20% of actual remaining processing time for the next 24h. *(temporal)*
7. Service times for Identity, Document (D1/D2), Assessment and Compliance follow the stated ranges and are not systematically biased by concurrent work on other tasks. *(resource)*
8. Using realtime timestamps in the workaround (K411) does not create false concurrency and is logged reliably. *(quality)*
