## 1. Decision rule

Use a **gated robust portfolio rule**, not a single `p_now` threshold.

1. **Stage and timing gate**
   - Eligible only at `Triage` or `Diagnosis Started`.
   - Not eligible after `Diagnosis Complete`; high risk there does not justify a swarm.
   - Require enough remaining SLA time for a 45-minute swarm to alter diagnosis, normally 60 minutes.

2. **Alert persistence and minimum evidence gate**
   - First alert: can enter review if data quality is acceptable; persistent high-risk first alerts may act immediately.
   - Repeated alert without prior swarm: re-enter only if there is a **material evidence change** since the last alert: new customer log/diagnostic artifact, stage change, adjusted-risk jump 0.05, SLA compression 30 minutes, changed value/regulatory status, or data-quality resolution.
   - Repeated alert after a prior swarm: normally barred unless there is a new event plus stronger evidence, e.g., adjusted-risk jump 0.10 or a major new diagnostic finding.
   - If the data source is imputed or failed, require verification before allocating a slot unless the adjusted risk and deadline are extreme.

3. **Calibration robustification**
   - Treat `p_now` as observational, not causal under intervention.
   - Use adjusted risk intervals:
     - SME: add `+0.04–0.09`, central about `+0.06`.
     - Strategic Enterprise: subtract `0.05–0.12`, central about `0.08`.
     - Public Sector: broadly accept, but keep wider uncertainty due moderate sample.
     - Non-Native-Language Support: widen interval substantially; do not point-correct without better validation.
     - Data-quality flags: widen further and require verification.
   - Use adjusted risk for urgency and persistence, **not** as the intervention effect.

4. **Prioritization score**
   - For each eligible case compute:
     - `LB direct net = RR_low × value  450  false-alarm burden`
     - `MID direct net = RR_mid × value  450  false-alarm burden`
   - Require positive lower-bound direct net for immediate selection unless a documented non-monetary override applies.
   - Then choose the portfolio maximizing robust net value after queue externality:
     - `spillover harm  used slots × 6 × spillover probability × value-at-risk of untreated diagnosis cases`
   - Apply reserve option value: hold one slot when an unlisted regulatory/high-obligation case is likely and marginal cases are uncertain.
   - Tie-breakers: adjusted risk, SLA imminence, regulatory/safety/contractual obligations, and fairness floor.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

Each alert should carry an **evidence fingerprint**: stage, adjusted risk, SLA remaining, data version, reason codes, new artifacts, data-quality status, and prior action.

- **Cool-down after no-swarm review:** suppress materially identical alerts for at least 2 hours or 4 refreshes, whichever is longer, unless a material evidence change occurs.
- **Cool-down after swarm:** no repeat swarm on the same case for at least 4 hours and normally not within the same diagnosis phase. Re-entry requires a new event plus senior approval.
- **Suppression:** unchanged repeated alerts go to the case dashboard/owner, not to scarce swarm-capacity ranking.
- **Escalation:** bypass cool-down if there is a new diagnostic artifact, stage regression, SLA compression, verified data-quality correction, regulatory status change, or adjusted risk jump 0.10.
- **Re-entry:** compare against the last reviewed fingerprint; require at least one material change. For data-quality-triggered alerts, require source verification before re-entry.

**I-05 vs I-07:**

- **I-05:** third alert, `0.55  0.57`, no new event. Despite SME underprediction and a high effect range, the incremental evidence is nearly zero. Treat as duplicate alert; suppress from capacity allocation unless a new customer log, material risk jump, or SLA change appears.
- **I-07:** third alert, but a new customer diagnostic log was uploaded. The slight p decrease does not invalidate the new evidence. Re-entry is allowed and the case is urgency-relevant, though its low economic value keeps it behind the selected portfolio unless a slot frees very early.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

- **Value-based starvation:** Ranking by value of avoiding breach can systematically starve lower-value customers, SMEs, and Public Sector cases. Safeguard: audit swarm rate by value quintile and segment; maintain a human-review floor for high adjusted-risk low-value cases; allow non-monetary overrides for regulatory, safety, vulnerability, and critical contractual obligations. The reserve slot is one concrete safeguard against purely economic rationing.

- **Non-Native-Language Support uncertainty:** I-09’s segment has sparse validation and uncertain effect estimates. Do not exclude the segment because evidence is weak; instead select with explicit uncertainty flags, require bilingual case review, record reason codes, and monitor outcomes separately. Use wider confidence intervals and an effect haircut until enough data accumulate.

- **Differential calibration:** SME underprediction and Strategic overprediction mean a global p threshold would over-alert some Strategic cases and under-alert SMEs. Use segment-specific calibration intervals, monitor false positives and false negatives by segment, and revisit correction factors on a rolling basis.

- **Selective intervention changes future labels:** Swarmed cases that avoid breach become misleading “low-risk” training examples if treated as untreated observations. Safeguard: log treatment status, swarm timing, reason for alert, and whether diagnosis actually changed. Train and calibrate on untreated or properly reweighted outcomes; use propensity/inverse-weighting or randomized encouragement where feasible.

- **Spillover harms on untreated groups:** Each slot can raise breach risk for six untreated diagnosis-stage cases. Those harmed cases may be lower-value or already disadvantaged and do not receive the intervention benefit. Safeguard: compute a spillover map before allocation, cap cumulative risk increase per untreated segment/group, stagger swarms to reduce queue shocks, monitor breach rates among untreated diagnosis-stage cases, and trigger queue-relief actions when spillover exceeds thresholds.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

- **Operational monitoring**
  - Track slots used, reserve utilization, swarm start/end time, stage at intervention, specialist interruption time, queue delay minutes per slot, false-alarm burden realized, and repeated-alert suppressions/overrides.
  - Hard check: any swarm started after `Diagnosis Complete` should be treated as process failure.

- **Predictive monitoring**
  - Weekly calibration by segment: SME, Strategic, Public Sector, NNLS, and data-quality-imputed cases.
  - Track Brier score, calibration-in-the-large, alert precision, missed-breach rate, `p_prev  p_now` jump distribution, and imputation rate.
  - Segment correction rules are re-estimated when calibration error exceeds about 0.05 for two consecutive review windows.

- **Causal-effect monitoring**
  - Record intervention effect proxies: time saved in diagnosis, new information found, route changed, breach avoided/not avoided, and expert judgment on whether the alert reason was correct.
  - Estimate effects using matched untreated controls, difference-in-differences, and randomized tie-breaking among near-identical eligible cases when capacity is scarce.
  - Update the effect ranges by stage, segment, reason code, and data-quality status. If observed effects fall below the supplied lower bounds repeatedly, reduce the effect haircut or suspend low-value swarm reasons.

- **Capacity and spillover monitoring**
  - For every slot used, log the six affected diagnosis-stage cases, estimated value at risk, actual delay, and subsequent breach outcome.
  - If median delay exceeds 20–30 minutes per slot or estimated spillover harm exceeds direct net benefit, reduce swarm volume, add specialist capacity, or stagger interventions.

- **Policy-induced drift**
  - Maintain a shadow model trained only on untreated outcomes.
  - Compare pre-policy and post-policy alert rates, calibration, and breach outcomes.
  - Detect whether “alerted but not swarmed” cases are becoming systematically different from historical alerted cases.

- **Fairness monitoring**
  - Dashboard swarm rate, false-alarm burden, breach rate, and spillover burden by segment, value tier, language-support status, and data-quality status.
  - Investigate if any group receives less than its agreed review floor or disproportionately absorbs spillover harm.

- **Change control**
  - Change the allocation rule when: calibration drifts materially, causal effects move outside supplied ranges, spillover harm exceeds direct benefit in the base scenario, adverse-case losses become unacceptable, or fairness floors are breached.
  - Before deployment, re-run the three scenarios and require the revised rule to have positive base-case net impact, acceptable adverse-case downside, and no group-level safeguard breach.
