## 1. Conformance semantics

In an object-centric process environment with an intentionally incomplete norm, conformance classification cannot rely on a binary match/mismatch against a reference graph. Instead, every observation must be evaluated under a hybrid open/closed-world semantics:

- **Closed-world** applies strictly to relations marked **forbidden** (violation whenever observed) and to **mandatory** relations within their explicitly stated scope (violation whenever omitted within scope).
- **Open-world** applies to everything else—relations marked **typical**, **unspecified**, or entirely absent from the norm are presumed permissible unless contradicted by another explicit rule or scope constraint.

Using this baseline, observations are separated into seven analytical classes:

1. **Forbidden behavior**: An observed edge or sequence explicitly disallowed by the norm (e.g., N3, T6, E10). Unless disproved as a logging artifact, this represents active noncompliance.
2. **Missing mandatory behavior**: A required relation within its stated scope fails to occur in the observed counts (e.g., E2, E6, N4). Scope conditions must be checked before concluding a process failure.
3. **Unexpected but unregulated behavior**: An observation absent from the normative graph or marked **unspecified** (e.g., N6, T4, T5, E9, `Package object_codeath Delivery`). Under open-world semantics, rarity or omission from the norm does not make it noncompliant.
4. **Typical-pattern absence**: A **typical** relation does not occur for some objects, and legitimate business exceptions are anticipated by the norm (e.g., N2, N5, T3).
5. **Likely data-quality error**: An apparent violation or gap caused by identifier collisions, dropped foreign keys, missing attribute-to-object extraction, or timestamp/event-merging defects rather than actual operational behavior (e.g., E10 suspected join error).
6. **Abstraction mismatch**: Behavior conforms to the underlying business intent (such as eventual precedence) but violates a stricter graph projection (such as directly-follows edges in an OC-DFG) or lacks an expected flat-to-object mapping (e.g., N1).
7. **Insufficient evidence**: Summary counts alone cannot distinguish between open/in-flight cases, right-censored observation windows, and true omissions (e.g., 14 invoices with no observed successor).

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

An Object-Centric Directly-Follows Graph (OC-DFG) establishes per-object-type sequential adjacency (`a -> b` for a shared object) and edge frequencies across object instances. However, it cannot establish global synchronization across distinct object types without inspecting shared events, nor can it distinguish between an event *never* occurring versus occurring *indirectly* after an intermediate step. It also cannot reveal open/right-censored lifecycles when an object simply stops before the log cutoff.

Counts and scopes are decisive here. For N1 (`Create Order -> Validate Order`, 98/100), treating the directly-follows summary as the business rule creates false positives: the norm explicitly notes N1 is an *eventually-follows* requirement approximated as directly-follows. With `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review` occurring 3 times, an intermediate review step naturally breaks directly-follows adjacency without violating eventual validation. Similarly, N4 (`Pack Package -> Dispatch Package`, 198/200) looks like 2 missing packs in OC-DFG, yet ET–OT proves all 200 `Package` objects participated in both `Pack Package` (200) and `Dispatch Package` (200). Thus, the 2-package gap is either an unmodeled intermediate event (abstraction mismatch) or a sequence inversion (`Dispatch` before `Pack`), rather than an omitted packing step.

Conversely, N3 (`Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`, 2 Orders) is **forbidden** regardless of intermediate steps, and N5 (`Issue Invoice -> Record Payment`, 92/110) must be read alongside its scope: 4 `Write Off` transitions are explicitly permitted exceptions, and 14 successorless invoices may simply be open credit terms. Interpreting an "eventually expected" rule (N1, N2, N5) as a strict directly-follows rule is dangerous because any legitimate detour—such as compliance screening, invoicing (`Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`: 72), or carrier hold—triggers spurious violations.

### OTG

An Object Type Graph (OTG) establishes which object types co-occur within shared events (`object_interaction`), share creation events (`object_cobirth`), or share termination events (`object_codeath`), along with interaction volume. What OTG cannot establish is *which specific event type* produced the interaction, temporal ordering, or per-event completeness.

Because OTG aggregates across all event types, a relation can look conforming in OTG while being suspicious in ET–OT, or vice versa. For example, `(Order, object_interaction, Item)` has weight 1450 and `(Package, object_interaction, Delivery)` has weight 200—both matching the total item additions (1450) and dispatches (200) and appearing to satisfy T1 and T2. Yet ET–OT exposes that 2 `Add Item` events lack an `Order` and 2 `Dispatch Package` events lack a `Delivery`. Conversely, `(Invoice, object_interaction, Payment)` = 92 is **typical** under T3 (permitting unapplied cash), whereas in ET–OT the 93rd `Record Payment` without an `Invoice` triggers an E8 mandatory gap due to missing event typing. Meanwhile, `(Package, object_codeath, Delivery)` = 7 and `(Order, object_interaction, Customer)` = 100 / `RiskCase` = 3 are open-world **unspecified** or unmodeled behaviors, not violations, while `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)` = 1 violates T6's closed-world **forbidden** rule.

### ET–OT

The Event Type–Object Type (ET–OT) graph establishes mandatory and forbidden cardinality/participation links between each event type and object type, exposing missing object references that OTG aggregates hide. However, ET–OT cannot establish event sequence (e.g., whether `Pack` preceded `Dispatch`), eventual lifecycle completion, or whether missing object links exist elsewhere as flat event attributes.

ET–OT counts expose four key anomalies:
1. `Add Item` links 1450 `Item`s but only 1448 `Order`s, revealing 2 E2 breaches invisible in OTG.
2. `Dispatch Package` links 200 `Package`s but 198 `Delivery`s (E6 gap), even though carrier references exist as event attributes—an abstraction/representation mismatch where an object was stored as an attribute rather than instantiated.
3. `Record Payment` links 93 `Payment`s and 92 `Invoice`s (E8 gap because the unapplied cash event lacks its required explicit label).
4. `Sensor Heartbeat – Invoice` = 1 violates E10 (**forbidden**), though context indicates a reused-ID join defect rather than an operational payment-telemetry process.

Finally, `Manual Compliance Review – Customer` = 3 is absent from the norm but represents the legitimate customer dimension of an explicitly permitted rare review (E9/T5).

    obs_etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

    # 2. Compute raw PM4Py diagnostics and preserve them untouched
    raw_diagnostics = {
        "ocdfg": pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(ocel, norm_ocdfg),
        "otg": pm4py.conformance_otg(ocel, norm_otg),
        "etot": pm4py.conformance_etot(ocel, norm_etot),
    }

    # 3. Separate semantic-classification layer (open-world except forbidden & scoped mandatory)
    classified_findings = []

    def classify_observed_edge(family, edge_key, observed_weight):
        meta = norm_meta[family].get(edge_key)
        if meta is None:
            return {
                "family": family, "edge": edge_key, "weight": observed_weight,
                "norm_id": None, "class": "unexpected_unregulated_open_world",
                "requires_review": False,
            }
        status = meta["status"]
        if status == "forbidden" and observed_weight > 0:
            return {
                "family": family, "edge": edge_key, "weight": observed_weight,
                "norm_id": meta["id"], "class": "forbidden_behavior_or_dq_candidate",
                "requires_review": True,
            }
        if status == "unspecified":
            return {
                "family": family, "edge": edge_key, "weight": observed_weight,
                "norm_id": meta["id"], "class": "unspecified_benign",
                "requires_review": False,
            }
        return {
            "family": family, "edge": edge_key, "weight": observed_weight,
            "norm_id": meta["id"], "class": f"observed_{status}",
            "requires_review": False,
        }

    # Example traversal over discovered OC-DFG edges (object_type -> {(act1, act2): events/objects})
    for obj_type, edge_map in obs_ocdfg.get("edges", {}).get("event_couples", {}).items():
        for (src, tgt), instances in edge_map.items():
            classified_findings.append(
                classify_observed_edge("ocdfg", (obj_type, src, tgt), len(instances))
            )

    # Example traversal over discovered OTG and ET-OT tuple structures
    otg_edges = obs_otg[0] if isinstance(obs_otg, tuple) else obs_otg
    for edge_key, weight in (otg_edges.items() if isinstance(otg_edges, dict) else []):
        classified_findings.append(classify_observed_edge("otg", edge_key, weight))

    etot_edges = obs_etot[0] if isinstance(obs_etot, tuple) else obs_etot
    for edge_key, weight in (etot_edges.items() if isinstance(etot_edges, dict) else []):
        classified_findings.append(classify_observed_edge("etot", edge_key, weight))

    # Separate check for mandatory gaps (e.g., comparing ET-OT event counts against expected OTs)
    # Kept independent from raw PM4Py outputs so scope/attribute exceptions can be evaluated.
    mandatory_gap_candidates = []
    for edge_key, meta in norm_meta["etot"].items():
        if meta["status"] == "mandatory":
            # Placeholder for comparing total events of edge_key[0] vs linked edge_key[1]
            # and checking event-attribute exceptions (e.g., carrier ref for E6, unapplied_cash for E8).
            mandatory_gap_candidates.append({"norm_id": meta["id"], "edge": edge_key, "meta": meta})

    return {
        "raw_pm4py_diagnostics": raw_diagnostics,  # Preserved verbatim
        "discovered_models": {"ocdfg": obs_ocdfg, "otg": obs_otg, "etot": obs_etot},
        "semantic_classification": classified_findings,
        "mandatory_gap_checks": mandatory_gap_candidates,
    }

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1 directly-follows vs. eventually-follows mismatch (`Create Order -> Validate Order`)**:
   - *Operational risk*: Every legitimate compliance detour (`Manual Compliance Review`) triggers false noncompliance alerts, causing alert fatigue and masking real unvalidated orders.
   - *Clarification*: Specify eventual precedence (`Create Order ~> Validate Order`) with a maximum validation SLA.
   - *Status*: **Mandatory** (for standard orders within SLA).

2. **N4 intermediate-step ambiguity (`Pack Package -> Dispatch Package`)**:
   - *Operational risk*: Teams cannot tell whether the 2 non-DF packages suffered an un-packed shipment (control breach) or simply passed through an unmodeled scan/staging step.
   - *Clarification*: Require `Pack Package` eventually-before `Dispatch Package` for external shipments while permitting documented warehouse staging events in between.
   - *Status*: **Mandatory** (precedence rule).

3. **E8 vs. T3/N5 inconsistency on unapplied cash (`Record Payment – Invoice`)**:
   - *Operational risk*: Valid unapplied cash without an explicit subtype flag registers as a mandatory breach, while true misapplied payments can be dismissed informally as "probably unapplied cash."
   - *Clarification*: Mandate a structured `payment_classification` attribute (`invoice_applied` vs `unapplied_cash`); link to `Invoice` is required when `invoice_applied` and omitted only when `unapplied_cash` is explicitly set.
   - *Status*: **Mandatory** (attribute typing); **Typical** (invoice link existence).

4. **Missing aging thresholds and snapshot-window rules (N2, N5, 14 successorless invoices)**:
   - *Operational risk*: Delinquent or abandoned invoices cannot be separated from healthy open credit terms or right-censored cases at the end of the log window.
   - *Clarification*: Define credit-term SLAs (e.g., escalate invoices >30 days past due) and exclude events within a trailing censoring buffer.
   - *Status*: **Typical** (settlement within term), with **Mandatory** escalation beyond SLA.

5. **Unmodeled `object_codeath` and `Write Off` boundaries (OTG, N5)**:
   - *Operational risk*: Analysts waste capacity investigating benign lifecycle terminations (`Package object_codeath Delivery`: 7) or normal bad-debt handling (`Write Off`: 4), or fail to catch unauthorized write-offs.
   - *Clarification*: Document `Package–Delivery` codeath on confirmation as permissible; define approval limits for `Write Off`.
   - *Status*: **Explicitly unspecified** (for `Package–Delivery` codeath in this process norm); **Typical** (for `Write Off` within policy thresholds).

6. **Undefined scope predicates ("standard Order" in N1; "external shipment" in N4)**:
   - *Operational risk*: Evaluators cannot determine whether exceptions belong to excluded order types or genuine failures.
   - *Clarification*: Add formal object-attribute predicates (e.g., `order_type == 'standard'`, `delivery_mode == 'external'`) to the norm specification.
   - *Status*: **Mandatory** (scope governance).

7. **Event-attribute vs. object-reference equivalence (E6 carrier attribute)**:
   - *Operational risk*: Recording carrier tracking as a flat attribute instead of a `Delivery` object breaks object-centric traceability and generates false E6 violations.
   - *Clarification*: Prohibit `Dispatch Package` events carrying a flat carrier attribute without a corresponding instantiated `Delivery` object.
   - *Status*: **Mandatory** (data-model contract).

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

Prioritizing the top two findings—**Rank 1 (`Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`, N3)** and **Rank 2 (`Order object_cobirth Payment`, T6)**—while also containing Rank 3's financial data-lineage defect (`Sensor Heartbeat – Invoice`, E10), and deliberately **not** escalating benign rare events (`Reopen Order -> Close Order`: 1, `Manual Compliance Review`: 3, `Package object_codeath Delivery`: 7):

- **Immediate containment**:
  - For the 2 N3 Orders: verify physical shipment status immediately; issue carrier intercept/recall if in transit and block further fulfilment on cancelled orders.
  - For the 1 T6 cobirth event: place an accounting hold/reconciliation review on the Order–Payment pair.
  - For E10: quarantine the `Sensor Heartbeat – Invoice` link in the analytics/audit mart.
- **Data repair**:
  - Fix the ETL identifier-namespace collision causing `Sensor Heartbeat – Invoice` (E10) by scoping keys with source-system prefixes.
  - Update the OCEL extractor to instantiate `Delivery` objects from carrier attributes on the 2 `Dispatch Package` events (E6), repair the 2 dropped `Order` foreign keys on `Add Item` (E2) if present in source tables, and backfill `unapplied_cash` typing on the 1 untyped `Record Payment` event (E8).
- **Process investigation**:
  - Audit the 2 N3 Orders to determine whether warehouse scanners lack real-time cancellation locks (race condition) or if manual overrides bypassed controls.
  - Trace the 1 T6 cobirth event in source transaction logs to distinguish a real direct-pay bypass from an ETL event-coalescing defect.
  - Inspect full event timelines for the 2 N4 Packages (`Pack` vs `Dispatch` ordering) and the 2 N1 Orders (confirming eventual validation).
- **Norm revision**:
  - Implement the clarifications from Section 6: convert N1 to eventually-follows, formalize scope predicates (`standard Order`, `external shipment`), require `unapplied_cash` typing for E8, and document benign unmodeled paths (`Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, `Package object_codeath Delivery`, `Manual Compliance Review – Customer`).
- **Monitoring**:
  - Deploy automated real-time rules for **forbidden** patterns (N3, T6, E10) and scoped **mandatory** cardinality gaps (E2, E6, E8), while suppressing alerts on **unspecified** patterns unless thresholds or fraud indicators change.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

- **Robust conclusions**: The existence of 2 `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` transitions (N3), 1 `Order object_cobirth Payment` edge (T6), and 1 `Sensor Heartbeat – Invoice` edge (E10) represents genuine violations of explicit closed-world prohibitions (either operationally or as pipeline defects). Equally robust is that `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72), `Manual Compliance Review – Customer` (3), `Package object_codeath Delivery` (7), and `Reopen Order -> Close Order` (1) are benign under open-world semantics and must not be flagged as noncompliance.
- **Conclusions dependent on norm incompleteness**: Whether the 2 non-DF `Create Order -> Validate Order` (N1) and 2 non-DF `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package` (N4) cases are benign intermediate-step detours or actual missing/inverted steps depends on clarifying directly-follows vs. eventually-follows rules and scope predicates. Similarly, the 1 unlinked `Record Payment` (E8/T3) sits on an ambiguity between missing mandatory typing and a typical unapplied-cash exception.
- **Conclusions dependent on suspected data-quality issues**: Whether `Sensor Heartbeat – Invoice` (E10), `Order object_cobirth Payment` (T6), `Add Item` missing `Order` (E2), and `Dispatch Package` missing `Delivery` (E6) reflect real operational defects or ETL/logging bugs depends on source-system verification.
- **Undecidable from graph summaries alone**: Aggregate OC-DFG, OTG, and ET–OT counts cannot determine true event ordering when intermediate steps occur (N1, N4), whether the 14 successorless invoices (N5) are overdue or right-censored, whether N3 was caused by clock skew versus physical mis-shipment, or whether the 2 orphan `Add Item` events belong to already-linked Orders via another event. Object-level trace inspection and raw event payloads are required to resolve these ambiguities.
