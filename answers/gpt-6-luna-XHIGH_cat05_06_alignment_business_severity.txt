## 1. Materiality Framework

Severity describes the consequence if the indicated condition is real; confidence describes how well the supplied evidence establishes that condition. A high-severity possibility can have only medium confidence when the evidence is incomplete. Conversely, a high-confidence duplicate transport event can be low severity. A missing event establishes a logging gap in the supplied trace, not by itself that the business activity did not occur.

Use this case-level severity scale:

- **1 — Negligible:** expected telemetry, a harmless retry, an explainable hidden routing step, or a scope/model mismatch with no apparent control or claimant harm.
- **2 — Low:** an evidence, linkage, or auditability weakness, or a possible service obligation gap, with no demonstrated missed payment control or material harm.
- **3 — Moderate:** a confirmed policy-ineligible route or control weakness with meaningful exposure, but without evidence of a high-risk gate being bypassed or a payment preceding approval.
- **4 — High:** a material payment or fraud-control concern, such as a high-exposure required check lacking reliable evidence, or a recent bank-change case paid without its required gate.
- **5 — Critical:** an irreversible or difficult-to-recover payment made before an applicable approval, or a comparable confirmed failure with substantial exposure and immediate control implications.

The assigned level reflects the case evidence and policy, not just the claim amount. Amount affects potential exposure; reversibility and timing determine whether the exposure is still containable. Also consider the control objective, claimant and regulatory consequences, recurrence, evidence reliability, and whether the trace belongs in this payable-claim model at all. Recurrence can raise the priority of a portfolio fix even when each instance is low severity. Where scope is disputed, avoid labeling expected termination as a business failure until the model boundary is agreed.

Confidence below refers to confidence in the business interpretation, with any important limit stated explicitly. The alignments are authoritative diagnostics for this exercise. Synchronous moves establish a logged model match, not necessarily that every policy condition was met. Likewise, a model move means no matching event appears in this trace; it does not prove the activity never happened.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

The supplied raw cost is an edit distance under a uniform cost of 1 for every visible log or model move. It does not weight the purpose of a control, claim amount, payment reversibility, customer impact, evidence quality, or whether a trace is in scope. It is useful as a measure of deviation under that particular representation and cost function; it is not a risk score.

- **CL-604 has cost 3 but critical business meaning.** A EUR 90,000 payment was accepted at 10:03 and approval recorded at 10:20. P2/P3 make payment before approval a severe control failure. Three unit-cost edits cannot express irreversibility, timing, or exposure.
- **CL-609 and CL-610 have cost 0 but violate policy.** Both traces fit the XOR branch. The tree has no amount, risk, or bank-change predicates. CL-609 breaks P1; CL-610 breaks P1 and P2. Synchronous conformance is not data-aware policy compliance.
- **CL-602 has cost 1 but a high-exposure evidence exception.** The missing Fraud Screen event costs one model move even though the case is EUR 480,000, high risk, and has a recent bank change. The score also cannot decide whether screening happened verbally/off-system or was omitted.
- **CL-607 and CL-612 have cost 4 largely because of scope.** A denied case and a withdrawn case terminate before approval/payment. The payable-claim model inserts activities that are inappropriate for those outcomes. Their high costs mainly diagnose a model boundary/branch problem, not four acts of noncompliance.
- **CL-603’s cost 1 reflects a duplicate event, not necessarily a second notice.** Same message ID and connector retry evidence point to transport duplication. A uniform event cost treats it like a new business action.
- **CL-606’s cost 1 is a missing core event despite an external signed report.** The alignment cannot see linked evidence in another system, and a missing event is not proof that assessment did not occur. Evidence linkage still matters, especially for the two portfolio cases without stable document IDs.
- **CL-608’s cost 1 treats a core-log notification omission as one edit.** P4 permits a linked secure-portal message within 24 hours. The message is at +3 hours, but a mutable CRM link weakens durable proof. Channel and evidence reliability change the interpretation without changing edit distance.
- **CL-611’s cost 2 records label difference, not necessarily different work.** Assess Damage may be the Assess Loss activity under a late mapping update. Activity granularity and taxonomy determine whether this is a substantive process deviation.
- **CL-605’s hidden tau move has cost 0 and is technically fitting.** A silent routing transition is not visible business evidence and cannot be counted as a performed screening or approval. Here the business context separately supports an eligible auto approval.
- **CL-601’s cost 3 counts UI events.** Open, autosave, and close events are not claim activities under P5; their inclusion inflates distance while adding no apparent business harm.

Thus, alignment cost supports technical conformance diagnosis. Business severity requires policy and context, event-source and scope interpretation, and an explicit account of uncertainty. Keep the raw cost and severity in separate fields and use the alignment as evidence, not as a substitute for judgment.

## 7. PM4Py Reproduction and Aggregation Code

The example builds an event log from the visible log-side activities in the supplied traces. Model-only and tau moves are not log events. The supplied move summaries remain authoritative for the case interpretation; PM4Py may return alternative optimal alignments or format alignment moves differently depending on version, parameters, and event-log conventions.

~~~python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Process-tree string follows the notation in the question.
tree_text = (
    "->('Register Claim', 'Validate Coverage', 'Assess Loss', "
    "X(->('Fraud Screen', 'Manual Approve'), 'Auto Approve'), "
    "'Pay Claim', 'Notify Claimant')"
)
tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_text)
net, initial_marking, final_marking = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)

# Each trace contains the visible log events, including log-only events.
# Model-only activities and the silent tau transition are not log events.
traces = [
    ("CL-601", ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Open Workbench",
                "Assess Loss", "Autosave", "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim",
                "Notify Claimant", "Close Workbench"]),
    ("CL-602", ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss",
                "Manual Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"]),
    ("CL-603", ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss",
                "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant",
                "Notify Claimant"]),
    ("CL-604", ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss",
                "Pay Claim", "Manual Approve", "Notify Claimant"]),
    ("CL-605", ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss",
                "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"]),
    ("CL-606", ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Fraud Screen",
                "Manual Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"]),
    ("CL-607", ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Deny Claim",
                "Notify Claimant"]),
    ("CL-608", ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss",
                "Fraud Screen", "Manual Approve", "Pay Claim"]),
    ("CL-609", ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss",
                "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"]),
    ("CL-610", ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss",
                "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"]),
    ("CL-611", ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Damage",
                "Fraud Screen", "Manual Approve", "Pay Claim",
                "Notify Claimant"]),
    ("CL-612", ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss",
                "Withdraw Claim"]),
]

event_rows = []
base_time = pd.Timestamp("2026-01-01T00:00:00Z")
for case_id, activities in traces:
    for position, activity in enumerate(activities):
        event_rows.append({
            "case:concept:name": case_id,
            "concept:name": activity,
            "time:timestamp": base_time + pd.Timedelta(seconds=position),
        })

event_df = pd.DataFrame(event_rows)
event_log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(event_df)
diagnostics = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(
    event_log, net, initial_marking, final_marking
~~~
