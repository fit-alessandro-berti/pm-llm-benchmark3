## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

**Stipulated Facts:**
- CS-9 is the formal, controlled compliance standard effective 2025-01-01.
- M0 defines the normative control flow but omits resource rules (e.g., separation of duties) and data conditions.
- PD-3 (Automation Pilot) and FD-7 (Flood Emergency) were temporarily authorized overlays with specific eligibility criteria and effective windows.
- A CRM migration on 2026-07-01 altered the data collection for notifications, reducing event log completeness and introducing mutable linking.
- Operations utilizes a "Request_More_Info" cycle not present in M0.

**Disputed Propositions:**
- Whether rejected leads (SUP-709) and reactivations (SUP-710) fall under the intended scope of M0.
- Whether the Compliance observer's retroactive note invalidated the PD-3 pilot.
- Whether the factual continuation of the flood emergency justified extending FD-7 (SUP-705).
- Whether the normalization of PD-3 justified post-pilot auto-approvals (SUP-714).
- The reliability of mutable CRM text fields for establishing notification compliance (SUP-706).

**Burden of Proof:**
- **Declaring Noncompliance:** The prosecution must prove that a trace deviates from M0/CS-9, that the deviation is not an artifact of data collection (e.g., CRM migration, label aliasing) or model incompleteness (e.g., missing loops), and that it is not covered by a valid, active policy overlay.
- **Accepting an Exception:** The defense must prove that the deviation was authorized by a documented policy overlay (PD-3, FD-7) active at the time of the event, and that the specific case met all eligibility criteria (e.g., LOW-risk, critical supplier).

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The operation is materially noncompliant, exhibiting a dangerous normalization of exceptions and a disregard for formal policy boundaries. 

**Formal Authority and Expiry:** CS-9 is the only permanent, approved standard. PD-3 and FD-7 were temporary exceptions. Operations unilaterally extended FD-7 (SUP-705) and PD-3 (SUP-714) without formal authority. Desirable outcomes or operational convenience do not retroactively amend policy; expiration dates exist precisely to force governance review.

**High-Risk Violations:** The operation fails to enforce critical safeguards. SUP-708 activated a HIGH-risk supplier before the sanctions check, a severe compliance breach that exposes the company to legal liability. SUP-703 applied the pilot auto-approval to an ineligible HIGH-risk supplier, demonstrating a failure of controls around exceptions.

**Hidden Deviations:** M0's lack of resource constraints hides severe violations. SUP-712 violates the core CS-9 separation of duties because the requester and approver are the same human using aliases. SUP-715 involves a conflict of interest that was not disclosed. These cases fit M0 but violate the spirit and letter of compliance.

**Normalization of Deviations:** The aggregate evidence shows 14 clearly ineligible cases used Auto_Approve during the pilot, and 6 cases used it after the pilot expired. This indicates systemic control failures and a culture that treats formal policy as optional.

## 3. Defense Brief

M0 is an obsolete, incomplete, and incorrectly scoped model that fails to reflect the reality of a dynamic, risk-aware operation.

**Model Incompleteness and Concurrency:** M0 cannot express the highly desirable `Request_More_Info` loop (SUP-707), falsely flagging diligent compliance work as a deviation. It also fails to capture legitimate label changes like `QA_Clearance` (SUP-713) and data duplication artifacts (SUP-711).

**Pilot and Emergency Overlays:** PD-3 and FD-7 were documented, authorized realities. SUP-702 and SUP-704 are not compliance failures; they are successful executions of agile business processes. The Compliance observer's retroactive hesitation does not invalidate a steering-committee decision.

**Incorrect Scope:** M0 is being applied to cases it was never designed for. SUP-709 is a pre-screen rejection, not a full onboarding case. SUP-710 is a reactivation, which CS-9 does not explicitly govern.

**CRM Migration:** The missing notifications (SUP-706) are a data-engineering failure, not a process failure. The 29% missingness rate post-migration proves the event log is an unreliable witness for the `Notify_Requester` activity.

**Trace Deviation vs. Business Misconduct:** Most deviations represent model rigidity or data issues, not material risk. Operations is acting in good faith to maintain supply chains during a flood (SUP-705) and improve efficiency (PD-3), while the model remains static and disconnected from reality.

3. **System-Level Judgment:**
The operation is materially noncompliant, but M0 is simultaneously invalid. The compliance failures are not primarily trace deviations (which are mostly justified exceptions or model defects), but rather severe breakdowns in data-aware and resource-aware controls (SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-712). 4. **Explanation of Rejections:**
- **Prosecution rejected:** I reject the argument that PD-3 and FD-7 were invalid; steering committee decisions and emergency directives carry operational authority, even if not codified in CS-9. I also reject penalizing SUP-707 and SUP-713, which are clear model/data defects.
- **Defense rejected:** I reject the argument that factual emergencies or normalized practices justify ignoring expiration dates (SUP-705, SUP-714). Expiration dates exist to force governance review. I also reject the defense of SUP-703 and SUP-708; these are material risks, not mere timing or eligibility errors.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

A revised architecture must separate control flow from data/resource constraints.
- **Revised Control-Flow Model (POWL):** Handles the core routing: standard, auto-approval, emergency, and rejection. It must include a `Request_More_Info` loop and accommodate early rejections.
- **Data-Aware Rules (DECLARE/pandas):** Enforces eligibility (e.g., `Auto_Approve` requires `risk == LOW` and `date <= 2026-07-31`). Enforces temporal constraints (e.g., `Quality_Review` within 2 days of `Activate_Supplier` for emergencies).
- **Resource and CoI Rules (Organizational Controls/pandas):** Enforces Separation of Duties (`requester_id != approver_id` after resolving aliases) and Conflict of Interest checks (reporting lines, relationships).
- **Policy-Overlay Governance:** A central registry of active pilots/emergencies with strict effective windows and eligibility criteria, evaluated at runtime.
- **Explicit Scope Rules:** Intake pre-screens and reactivations must have distinct case types or sub-processes, filtering them out of the main M0 evaluation.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

**Limitation Statement:** Encoding early rejection (e.g., failing sanctions pre-screen without quality review) and emergency routes cleanly in a single block without duplication is awkward in POWL. The most defensible approximation requires duplicating the `Sanctions_Check`, `Quality_Review`, `Manual_Procurement_Approval`, and `Activate_Supplier` nodes for the distinct branches to avoid PM4Py node-reuse errors.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
request_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
manual_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

# Branch-specific transitions to avoid node reuse in POWL
sanctions_emg = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality_emg = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
manual_approval_emg = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
activate_emg = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
sanctions_rej = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")

# 1. Standard Checks with Loop
checks_concurrent = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])
checks_loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[checks_concurrent, request_info])

# 2. Standard approved branch
std_approval = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[checks_loop, manual_approval, activate])
std_approval.order.add_edge(checks_loop, manual_approval)
std_approval.order.add_edge(manual_approval, activate)

# 3. Auto-approve branch
auto_approval = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[checks_loop, auto_approve, activate])
auto_approval.order.add_edge(checks_loop, auto_approve)
auto_approval.order.add_edge(auto_approve, activate)

# 4. Emergency branch (Sanctions -> Activate -> Quality & Manual Approval)
emg_post_act = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[quality_emg, manual_approval_emg])
emg_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions_emg, activate_emg, emg_post_act])
emg_branch.order.add_edge(sanctions_emg, activate_emg)
emg_branch.order.add_edge(activate_emg, emg_post_act)

# 5. Reject branches (Early reject after sanctions, or reject after full checks)
early_reject_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions_rej, reject])
early_reject_branch.order.add_edge(sanctions_rej, reject)

late_reject_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[checks_loop, reject])
late_reject_branch.order.add_edge(checks_loop, reject)

# Decision XOR
decision = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[
    std_approval, 
    auto_approval, 
    emg_branch, 
    early_reject_branch, 
    late_reject_branch
])

# End sequence
end_seq = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[notify, close])
end_seq.order.add_edge(notify, close)

# Full Model
M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, decision, end_seq])
M1.order.add_edge(register, decision)
M1.order.add_edge(decision, end_seq)
```

**Data-Collection Proposal:**
- **Normalized Policy-Overlay Table:** `overlay_id`, `type` (PILOT/EMERGENCY), `start_date`, `end_date`, `eligibility_rules` (JSON logic).
- **Event Enhancements:**
  - `notification_id`: Immutable UUID generated at source (CRM), preventing mutable text field tampering.
  - `rule_version_id`: Attached to each case at registration to freeze the applicable policy.
  - `actor_id`: Resolved to a canonical human ID at the data-warehouse layer (handling aliases).
  - `delegation_evidence`: Logged if approval is delegated.
  - `effective_timestamp` and `record_timestamp`: Immutable timestamps for all events to distinguish system delays from actual execution times.
  - `eligibility_snapshot`: A JSON snapshot of supplier attributes (risk, region) at the time of decision.
