## 2. Process-model consistency checks

### 2.1 Validation Suite Design

A credible explanation audit must implement these checks in sequence:

#### **Check A: Prefix-Time Availability (PTA)**
For every feature and every local case, verify:
- **Event timestamp  `t0` (Coverage Check Completed timestamp)**
- **No reference to events 3–8 (Damage Assessment onward, Settlement Decision, Appeal, Claim Closed)**
- **No mutable attributes populated after `t0`**

*Application to this audit:*
- `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`: **FAIL** (post-closure).
- `final_handler_team`: **FAIL** (populated at closure, warehouse join has no historical timestamp).
- `risk_score_current`: **FAIL** (mutable, no timestamp).
- `last_resource_role`: **FAIL** (role assigned at closure, not at `t0`).
- `initial_damage_complexity`, `claimant_region`, `initial_reserve` (if registered): **PASS** (event 1  `t0`).
- `queue_length_at_t0`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`: **PASS** (timestamped snapshots at or before `t0`).

#### **Check B: Activity-Order Feasibility (AOF)**
Verify that any sequence pattern is feasible at or before `t0` given the normative process order:
1. Claim Registered
2. Coverage Check Completed  **`t0` is here**
3. (Damage Assessment OR Document Cycles)
4. Settlement Decision, Appeal, Claim Closed

*Application:*
- `Documents Requested -> Documents Received`: Activities 3a–3b, both post-`t0`. **Not feasible in prefix at `t0`.** Pattern contribution 0.12 is post-hoc.
- `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded`: Activities 7–8, post-`t0`. **Impossible in prefix.** Pattern contribution +0.18 is post-hoc.
- `Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened`: Activities 25a. Fraud Investigation may begin post-Coverage Check but is not mandatory in the normative order. At `t0`, Fraud Investigation has not yet occurred. **Not observable in prefix.** Contribution +0.11 is forward-looking conditional probability, not a realized sequence.
- `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` (Case Z): Violates process order (8 before 4). **AOF FAIL.** Additionally, same timestamp with missing sequence index makes true order ambiguous. **Do not trust this pattern.**
- `Damage Assessment Completed -> Settlement Decision Recorded`: Activities 46, both post-`t0`. **Not feasible in prefix.** Contribution 0.09 is post-hoc.

**Conclusion:** Every reported sequence pattern is either post-`t0`, impossible at `t0`, or both. None are legitimate prefix signals.

#### **Check C: Same-Timestamp Ambiguity (STA)**
For events with identical timestamps, verify:
- A reliable sequence index exists (e.g., `event_id`, `event_order`).
- If missing, flag the case as non-deterministic.
- Do not use features derived from ambiguous orderings in predictions.

*Application:*
- Case Z: `Claim Closed` and `Damage Assessment Completed` have the same timestamp and no sequence index. **STA FAIL.** The direction of the pattern `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` is unreliable. Do not use the pattern contribution (+0.18) without resolving the ambiguity.

#### **Check D: Complete-Trace Dependencies (CTD)**
Identify features that require the complete, finalized trace to compute:
- `total_case_duration_hours`: Requires case closed. **Fails CTD.**
- `appeal_seen_anywhere`: Requires full event log (appeal may occur anytime in events 7–8). **Fails CTD.**
- `num_document_requests_total`: Requires all document cycles. **Fails CTD.**
- `full_trace_variant_id`: Requires all events. **Fails CTD.**

These features are valid for *post-hoc analysis* (root cause, audit, compliance) but not for *prospective prediction at `t0`*.

#### **Check E: Enabled / Impossible / Future-Possible (EIF)**
For each feature or activity reference, classify its status at `t0`:
- **Enabled:** Observable in the prefix at `t0`. E.g., `claimant_region`, `initial_damage_complexity`, elapsed time from registration.
- **Impossible:** Forbidden by process order or logic. E.g., `appeal` before `Coverage Check Completed`; `Damage Assessment` at `t0`.
- **Future-Possible:** Conditionally likely to occur later. E.g., `Documents Requested` (likely but not certain), `Fraud Investigation` (depends on claim triggers).

*Application:*
- `queue_length_at_t0`: **Enabled** (observable at `t0`).
- `appeal_seen_anywhere`: **Impossible** at `t0` (appeal is event 7–8).
- `Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened`: **Future-Possible** (Fraud Investigation is enabled post-`t0` but depends on triggers).
- `final_decision = Partial`: **Impossible** at `t0` (decision is event 6).

#### **Check F: Appeal and Repeated Settlement Decisions (ARD)**
The normative process permits *one* `Settlement Decision Recorded` event initially (event 6). If an `Appeal` occurs (event 7), a *second* `Settlement Decision Recorded` is semantically legitimate.

- A feature tracking "whether appeal occurred" or a sequence edge `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is **post-`t0` only** (appeal is future).
- A feature tracking "whether a decision was recorded" at `t0` is **not useful** (always false; no decision yet).
- Post-hoc: A trace with two `Settlement Decision Recorded` edges is valid; one without an intervening appeal is a schema error.

*Application:*
- Case X: `appeal_seen_anywhere = 1` means an appeal was recorded (post-`t0`). At `t0`, this is unknown. The contribution +0.26 is post-hoc.
- Sequence pattern `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` with +0.18 is post-hoc and legitimate post-closure.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

### Classification Schema

Features are classified into five categories. For each, the allowed claims and limitations are strict.

---

### **Category 1: Defensible Predictive Signals (Prefix-Based, Frozen, Causal)**

These features are observable and frozen at `t0`, and causal interpretation is plausible.

| Feature | Admissibility | Mechanism | Caveats |
|---|---|---|---|
| `initial_damage_complexity` | **RETAIN** | Entered at registration; captures inherent claim difficulty. Higher complexity  longer investigation, more cycles. | Validate that complexity is entered before `t0`, not updated later. May confound with claim value (more expensive = more complex). |
| `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` | **RETAIN** | Time since claim started; claims that have already consumed time have less slack to meet SLA. | Assumes SLA is from registration, not from Coverage Check. If SLA is from Coverage Check, this is a confound. |
| `claimant_channel (Broker)` | **RETAIN (with fairness review)** | Broker vs. direct; channel may correlate with claim routing, settlement style, communication friction. | Mechanism is unclear: Is broker-channel inherently slower, or does it proxy for claim type or region? Conduct stratified analysis. Fairness risk if channel proxies for socioeconomic status. |
| `initial_reserve (€25,000)` | **RETAIN (with mechanism review)** | If set at registration: Reserve amount proxies for estimated claim severity and payout scope. | Validate timing: Is reserve set before or after `t0`? If set during Damage Assessment (event 4), it is post-`t0` and must be removed. Confound: Reserve may be set after complexity assessment, making it a response variable. |

---

### **Category 2: Process-State and Congestion Signals**

These features measure the state of the system or workload at `t0`, not claim attributes.

| Feature | Admissibility | Interpretation | Caveats |
|---|---|---|---|
| `queue_length_at_t0` | **RETAIN (with caveats)** | **NOT:** "Queue causes breaches." **YES:** "High queue at `t0` correlates with breach (claims in busy periods may experience longer total residence time)." | Join tolerance ±5 min must be validated. Confound: High-queue days may also have staffing changes, holidays, or policy delays. Do not claim queue length is causal without controlling confounds. Separate signal: "Queue is a state proxy; it does not determine breach risk directly but may correlate with operational capacity." |
| `calendar_week` | **RETAIN (drift/policy proxy only)** | **NOT:** "Certain weeks are operationally worse." **YES:** "Weeks 52 and surrounding periods align with holiday and policy changes; claims filed in these weeks may experience different processing." | Document which weeks have policy changes, holidays, staffing reductions. Replace raw week number with explicit flags: `is_holiday_week`, `is_post_policy_change_week`. Prevents fairness-neutral seasonal patterns from masking operational decisions. |

Document findings. If (e) is substantial after controlling (a)–(d), escalate to fairness review. Do not claim region is a breach driver without this analysis. |
| `prior_claim_count_before_t0` | **RETAIN (after SQL repair)** | May proxy for: (a) customer type (legitimate), (b) known-problematic claimant (operational), (c) data quality artifact (bug in the SQL: current logic includes claims after `t0`). | Fix SQL to `claim_created_at < t0`. Recompute importance. Separately analyze: Does prior claim count proxy for legitimate claim difficulty (customers with multiple prior claims file more complex claims), or is it a fairness proxy (repeat claimants treated differently)? If latter, require escalation. |

### **Summary: What May and May Not Be Claimed**

| Claim Type | Defensible | Not Defensible | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| **"Feature X causes breach"** | `initial_damage_complexity`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` (with SLA validation). | `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `final_handler_team` (future/post-hoc). | Causality requires timeline: cause must precede effect. Post-`t0` features are outcomes or confounds. |
| **"Feature X is an early warning"** | `queue_length_at_t0`, `calendar_week` (with drift documentation). | `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total` (not "early"; they are the delay itself). | Early warning must be observable at `t0` and precede the outcome. Confusing outcome with warning signal is circular logic. |
| **"Feature X correlates with breach"** | All prefix-available features, post-hoc features (for post-hoc analysis). | None (correlation is agnostic to timing, but must be stated as "post-hoc" if feature is post-`t0`). | Correlation can hold regardless of timing; attribution must distinguish post-hoc from prospective. |
| **"Feature X is actionable"** | `initial_damage_complexity` (not actionable; already assigned), `queue_length_at_t0` (partially; hints at staffing/capacity), `calendar_week` (actionable if policies are discretionary). | `final_handler_team` (handler assigned post-`t0`), `appeal_seen_anywhere` (appeal is claimant/legal decision). | Actionable means: (a) within operator's control, (b) available at decision time, (c) changes the outcome if changed. Pre-`t0` features cannot be altered; post-`t0` features are future unknowns. |
| **"Feature X indicates fairness risk"** | `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (all require stratified analysis). | None (fairness analysis requires explicit digging; absence of analysis is not absence of risk). | Proxies for sensitive attributes or group membership must be decomposed. Use stratified analysis and residual tests. |

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

### **Phased Remediation Plan**

#### **Phase 1: Immediate Feature-Store Audit (Weeks 1–2)**

**1.1 Complete-Trace Feature Quarantine**
- Identify and remove from prospective models: `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`.
- Keep these features in a separate post-hoc analysis module for root-cause investigation, calibration, and audit.
- Update the feature store metadata to flag these as "post-hoc only."
- **Deliverable:** Feature catalog with temporal classification (prefix vs. post-hoc).

**1.2 Mutable-Attribute Timestamps**
- Audit all mutable features in the model: `risk_score_current`, `final_handler_team`, `reserve_amount`, `handler_assignment`.
- For each, check if a historical snapshot table with `valid_at_timestamp` exists.
- If not, remove the feature or create a historical snapshot (see 1.3).
- **Deliverable:** Lineage document for each mutable feature; identify gaps.

**1.3 Historical Feature Snapshots**
- Design and populate time-series tables for:
  - `risk_score_at_t0` (timestamp-indexed)
  - `handler_assignment_at_t0` (initial assignment, frozen at event 1 or event 2)
  - `reserve_amount_at_registration` (frozen at event 1, if set then)
- Implement point-in-time (PIT) join logic: For each case prediction at `t0`, join features using `feature_valid_at_time >= t0 AND feature_valid_at_time < next_feature_update_time`.
- **Deliverable:** Snapshot tables in production; PIT join validation queries.

---

#### **Phase 2: Temporal-Leakage Repair (Weeks 2–4)**

**2.1 SQL Logic Corrections**
- **`prior_claim_count_before_t0`:** Rewrite to `claim_created_at < t0` instead of `<=`. Re-run on historical data.
- **`same_day_document_count_snapshot`:** Replace with point-in-time snapshot: `documents_received_at <= t0`, ignore calendar-day boundaries.
- **Test:** Run before/after SQL on 100 random cases; verify counts decrease (more conservative).

**2.2 Prefix-Only Variant Definition**
- Replace `full_trace_variant_id` (post-hoc) with `prefix_variant_id_at_t0` (observable at `t0`).
- Examples: At `t0`, all cases are `REG-COV` (no variance). As cases progress, variants branch (e.g., `REG-COV-DOC`, `REG-COV-DMG` if fast-track).
- Rationale: Prefix variants are much coarser but honest; they reflect the actual information available.
- **Test:** Confirm that prefix variants at each event time match the process order and are deterministic.

**2.3 Source Data Quality: Same-Timestamp Ambiguity**
- Audit event ingestion for same-timestamp collisions.
- **Case Z finding:** Damage Assessment and Claim Closed have the same timestamp; sequence index is missing.
- Implement: (a) Add event sequence index at the source (e.g., microsecond precision, or explicit event order column). (b) Validate all event pairs with the same timestamp; if order cannot be determined, flag case as non-conformant.
- **Deliverable:** Event audit report; Case Z reprocessed with corrected timestamps.

---

#### **Phase 3: Process-Conformance Testing (Weeks 4–6)**

**3.1 Declare and Validate Activity Order**
- Implement a declarative process-conformance checker using a business-process library (e.g., PM4Py or custom rules).
- Declare normative rules:
  - `Claim Registered` must precede all other activities.
  - `Coverage Check Completed` must follow `Claim Registered` and precede `Settlement Decision Recorded`.
  - `Damage Assessment Completed` must precede `Claim Closed`.
  - `Appeal` must follow the first `Settlement Decision Recorded` (event 6).
  - `Fraud Investigation` may start after `Claim Registered` and run in parallel (no strict order constraint).
- Run on the full historical trace database. Flag non-conformant cases.
- **Deliverable:** Conformance report; list of non-conformant traces (prioritize for investigation).

**3.2 Prefix-Availability Validation**
- For each feature, assert a rule: "This feature must be derivable from events up to and including `t0` (Coverage Check Completed)."
- Implement automated checks:
  - **PTA check:** For every feature and case, verify `max(event_timestamp) <= t0_timestamp` for all events used in feature computation.
  - **Trace check:** For every case, confirm that the feature can be computed from `trace[0:index_of_coverage_check]` only.
- Run on test set; flag violations.
- **Deliverable:** PTA validation report; refine feature definitions as needed.

**3.3 Sequence-Pattern Feasibility (SPF)**
- For each sequence pattern reported by the explainer (e.g., `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded`), verify:
  - Both activities are pre-`t0` in the test set (necessary but unlikely for most patterns).
  - The pattern occurs in at least 5% of cases (to ensure it's not noise).
  - The pattern is not a schema artifact (e.g., always appears due to post-processing).
- For patterns that fail SPF (both activities post-`t0`), remove from prospective explanations. Retain for post-hoc analysis.
- **Deliverable:** SPF audit report; updated list of acceptable patterns.

---

#### **Phase 4: Feature-Importance Recalibration (Weeks 6–8)**

**4.1 Retrain on Cleaned Features**
- Remove or repair features identified in Phases 1–3.
- Features to remove: `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `risk_score_current` (if no snapshot available), `last_resource_role`, `final_handler_team` (if no reliable initial-assignment timestamp).
- Features to repair: `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (SQL fix), `same_day_document_count_snapshot` (PIT redefinition).
- Retrain the breach-risk model on the cleaned feature set.
- Compare new model performance (AUC, F1, precision, recall) to the original model. Document delta.
- **Deliverable:** Retrained model; performance comparison report.

**4.2 Recompute SHAP Importance**
- Compute SHAP values on the retrained model.
- Compare to original importance ranking. Identify features that:
  - Drop in importance (likely were leaked correlates).
  - Rise in importance (were suppressed by correlated leakage).
  - Change sign (suspicious; investigate lineage).
- **Deliverable:** New importance ranking; delta analysis.

**4.3 Stability Testing**
- For the new model, compute SHAP importance on 10 random subsets of the test set (cross-validation).
- Plot importance distributions and confidence intervals.
- If importance varies wildly across subsets (e.g., ±50%), the feature is unstable; consider removal.
- **Deliverable:** Stability report; flag unstable features.

---

#### **Phase 5: Proxy and Fairness Analysis (Weeks 8–10)**

**5.1 Stratified Breach Analysis by Protected Attributes**
- For features that are proxies or sensitivities (e.g., `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0`), compute:
  - Breach rate by region: `E[breach | region = R] - E[breach]` for each R.
  - Breach rate by channel: `E[breach | channel = C] - E[breach]` for each C.
  - Breach rate by prior count: `E[breach | prior_count = k]` for k  {0, 1, 2, 3+}.
- Visualize as heat maps or stratified plots.
- For each group, investigate potential drivers:
  - **Operational drivers:** repair-network capacity, staffing, policy (actionable, legitimate).
  - **Case-characteristic drivers:** claim complexity, claim value (legitimate if not discriminatory).
  - **Handler-behavior drivers:** average settlement time, approval rate (potential fairness concern if disparate).
  - **Residual:** unexplained gap after controlling operational and case factors (potential disparate impact).
- **Deliverable:** Stratified analysis report; fairness risk flags.

**5.2 Residual Fairness Test**
- For each protected attribute (region, channel), fit a linear regression:
  - `breach ~ initial_damage_complexity + initial_reserve + queue_length_at_t0 + [other non-proxy features]`
  - Add the protected attribute as a covariate: `breach ~ ... + protected_attr`.
  - If `protected_attr` coefficient is statistically significant and large (e.g., > 5% of baseline breach rate), this suggests disparate impact.
- Document findings and escalate if needed.
- **Deliverable:** Regression table; fairness assessment.

---

#### **Phase 6: Explanation Stability and Schema Monitoring (Weeks 10–12)**

**6.1 Local Explanation Reproducibility**
- For a sample of historical cases (e.g., 50 random cases with known outcomes), recompute local SHAP explanations using the retrained model.
- Compare to the original explanations: Do the top 3 drivers remain the same? Do sign and magnitude align?
- If explanations are unstable, investigate: (a) Feature changes (lineage), (b) Model retraining (randomness, hyperparameter drift), (c) Data drift (new cases have different distributions).
- **Deliverable:** Reproducibility report; stability metrics.

**6.2 Operational Monitoring for Drift**
- Set up data-quality and drift monitors:
  - **Distribution drift:** Monitor feature distributions (e.g., queue_length_at_t0, initial_reserve) over time. Alert if mean or variance shifts.
  - **Schema drift:** Monitor for new values in categorical features (e.g., new regions, channels, process variants).
  - **Temporal drift:** Compare breach rate by calendar period (month, quarter). Alert if breach rate shifts materially.
  - **Process drift:** Check process-conformance rate monthly. Alert if % non-conformant cases rises.
- **Deliverable:** Monitoring dashboard; alert configuration.

**6.3 Model Recalibration Schedule**
- Establish a recalibration cadence: Retrain the model every 3 months or upon significant drift alert.
- Document rationale: "Retraining ensures SHAP values remain consistent with the current data distribution and operational process."
- Upon retraining, re-audit top features using the same process-conformance checks (Phase 3).
- **Deliverable:** Recalibration policy; calendar.

---

#### **Phase 7: Documentation and Governance (Weeks 12+)**

**7.1 Feature Lineage Document**
- Create a data dictionary for every feature in the model:
  - **Name, definition, unit, range.**
  - **Source table, join logic, temporal availability.**
  - **Calculation formula or SQL snippet.**
  - **Classification:** Prefix vs. post-hoc, proxy status, fairness review required (yes/no).
  - **Timestamp handling:** When is the feature valid? Is it frozen or mutable?
  - **Last updated, owner, review date.**
- **Deliverable:** Feature lineage document (living); publish in the feature store.

**7.2 Explanation Audit Checklist**
- Create a checklist for model developers and data scientists:
  -  Feature is prefix-available (derivable before `t0`).
  -  Feature has valid timestamp or is immutable.
  -  Feature does not violate process order (no pre-event activities).
  -  Sequence patterns are feasible in the prefix.
  -  Proxy features (region, channel, prior count) have fairness analysis.
  -  SHAP importance is stable across subsets.
  -  Causal claims are limited to features with strong temporal and logical precedence.
- **Deliverable:** Checklist; integrate into model validation workflow.

**7.3 Explanation Communication Guidelines**
- Establish standards for how explanations are presented to business stakeholders:
  - ** DO:** "Claims with high complexity correlate with breach; complex claims often require extended investigation."
  - ** DO:** "High queue length at Coverage Check correlates with breach; congestion may slow downstream processing."
  - ** DON'T:** "This case will breach because it has an appeal" (appeal is future, unknown).
  - ** DON'T:** "Region R4 drives breach" (without mechanism analysis; potential fairness issue).
  - ** DO (with caveats):** "Region R4 claims have higher breach rates (71% vs. 65% baseline); potential drivers include repair-network capacity, claim type distribution, and handler experience. Further investigation required."
- **Deliverable:** Explanation style guide; training for users.

## Summary

This audit identified **critical temporal-leakage issues** affecting 8 of the top 12 global features and all 5 reported sequence patterns. Key findings:

1. **`total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`** are complete post-hoc leakage; remove from prospective explanations immediately.

2. **`final_handler_team`** is populated at closure with no historical initial-assignment timestamp; either repair with timestamped initial assignment or remove.

3. **Sequence patterns** (Appeals, Document Cycles, Damage Assessment  Settlement Decision) are all post-`t0` and impossible in the prefix; retain for post-hoc root-cause analysis only.

4. **Mutable features** (`risk_score_current`, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`) require historical snapshots with precise timestamps; implement or remove.

5. **Proxy features** (`claimant_region`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, `claimant_channel`) are predictive but confounded; require stratified fairness analysis before confident deployment.

6. **Defensible drivers** include `initial_damage_complexity`, `queue_length_at_t0` (as a state signal, not causal), and `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`.

**Revised explanations for Cases X and Y** are provided above, with explicit boundaries on what can and cannot be claimed as causal. **Case Z** is flagged as non-recomputable until source data is repaired (same-timestamp ambiguity, mutable feature timestamps).

The **7-phase remediation plan** provides a roadmap for feature-store audit, temporal-leakage repair, process-conformance validation, model retraining, fairness analysis, and operational monitoring, with clear success criteria and risk priorities.
