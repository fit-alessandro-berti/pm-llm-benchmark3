# 1. Data audit and estimands

**Tagging convention used throughout:** `[O]` = observed in the supplied tables; `[E]` = estimate derived here; `[B]` = bound; `[A]` = assumption (needed because the aggregate is insufficient); `[H]` = hypothesis; `[R]` = recommendation.

**Queueing quantities, units, conventions**

- Window: 28 days, 2026-04-06  2026-05-03. Service center open 07:00–19:00 (12 opening hours/day). Arrivals and completions are counted during opening hours only `[O]`; no service occurs while closed; cases may carry over overnight and completions need not be same-day arrivals `[O]`.
- Arrival intensity : cases/day, or cases per opening hour (÷12). Completion (throughput) intensity X: completions/day; this is the departure rate of the *system*, including carry-over cases — it is a throughput, not a same-day arrival rate.
- Service time: **active handling only**, summarized by daily median and p90. The **mean service time is not supplied**; it must be estimated for any capacity arithmetic. `[A]` I assume a within-day lognormal shape, under which p90/median identifies  (mean = median·exp(²/2)); implied mean/median multipliers are 1.12 (baseline) to 1.27 (strained era) `[E]`. Medians must never be substituted for means or for flow times.
- WIP (`mean_wip`): mean number in system from 15-minute snapshots. `[A]` Snapshots cover opening hours and include queueing + in-service cases. Overnight stock is therefore not directly observed.
- Capacity: `staff_fte` × 720 staffed minutes/day; an outage of *m* minutes removes  `staff_fte` × m staffed minutes `[A]` (assumes full stop, all staff affected).
- Utilization : workload/capacity = (cases × E[S]) / staffed minutes, computed both on completions ("realized") and arrivals ("demand pressure") `[E]`.
- A **regime** is defined here as a maximal interval over which (, service distribution, capacity, backlog trajectory) is approximately constant for planning purposes.

**Threats to naive inference (eight; first five are the most damaging)**

1. **Flow-conservation gap.** arrivals  completions = 2519  2409 = **110 cases (4.4%)** `[O]`, yet daily mean WIP changes only 18  19 `[O]`. By 7-day blocks the net flow is +1, +21, +78, +10 `[O]`; same-weekday WIP comparisons (Mon Apr 13 21  Mon Apr 20 37  Mon Apr 27 49) imply true stock rose far less than net flow in the strained week (gap  60–66 cases) `[E]`. Candidate explanations `[H]`: (i) complex multi-step cases spend time in states the WIP snapshots do not cover (external waits), (ii) abandonment/redirection before joining the measured queue, (iii) a WIP definition/coverage change, (iv) completion under/over-counting. Any Little's-Law claim inherits this systematic uncertainty.
2. **No mean service time.** p90/median ratios of 1.83–2.44 `[O]` imply strong right skew; capacity plans built on medians understate workload by ~12–27% `[E, A]`.
3. **Structural non-stationarity.** At least three breaks: outage (Apr 15), case-mix step (Apr 20), staffing step (Apr 29) `[O]`. Pooled statistics describe **no actual day**.
4. **Carry-over backlog and boundary conditions.** Estimated 07:00 stock is never near zero (14–27 cases) `[E]`, so daily "flow" mixes multi-day residence; the representative hourly vectors also fail to reconcile with daily WIP at day boundaries (e.g., implied Fri Apr 24 close  50 vs estimated Sat Apr 25 open  18) `[O, E]` — the hourly vectors are "representative", not exact logs.
5. **Composition/Simpson effects.** Weekday/weekend mixture and era shifts create pooled relationships that reverse or vanish within regimes (§4).
6. **Intraday burstiness is invisible in daily rows** (peak_08_10 up to 50%; one representative day shows 34 arrivals in a single hour) `[O]`.
7. **Serial dependence.** Weekly cycle and trend give lag-1/lag-7 autocorrelations of +0.4–0.6 `[E]`; the 28 daily points are far from 28 independent observations.
8. **Ambiguous semantics.** `complex_pct` may be a real mix shift or a coding change `[O-caution]`; the Apr-29 intervention's within-day timing is unlogged; `staff_fte` is already elevated (7.0, 6.5) on Apr 16–17, *before* the declared intervention `[O]` — an unexplained ad-hoc capacity bump.

**Why one pooled queueing model misleads (preview):** a single G/G/c-style fit at the pooled means (  90/day, E[S]  37 min `[A]`, c  5.2 FTE,   0.86 `[E]`) matches no day: baseline days ran   0.65 with small queues; strained weekdays ran   1.0 with a growing backlog; the outage is a supply shock the pooled model cannot represent at all.
