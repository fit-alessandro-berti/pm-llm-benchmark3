# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

**Use Legal's policy for the implemented model.** The disputed decision is whether to activate while an escalation still lacks an opinion. R4 requires the opinion eventually under either policy. It does not settle whether activation can happen first.

| Policy version | Decision at activation with an outstanding escalation | Compliance risk |
|---|---|---|
| Legal, implemented | Fail R5 immediately. A subsequent opinion can satisfy R4 but cannot repair the activation order. R6's exception is disabled. | An emergency authorized by Operations will still be blocked or recorded as a violation. |
| Operations, alternative | Adopt an explicit exception to R5. Require evidence of joint emergency authorization valid at activation and a subsequent opinion within 24 elapsed hours of activation, inclusive. | An activity trace alone cannot establish authorization. Automatically accepting a timely opinion as evidence of authorization would admit unauthorized activations. This version requires policy-owner approval of the exception. |

Modeling assumptions for both versions follow.

- Each trace concerns one onboarding case. Events record completed activities in reliable case order. The eight traces are complete, with elapsed hours on a common clock. In production, an unfinished prefix can have pending obligations. Closing a case ends the opportunity to discharge them.
- A final vendor decision for R3 is either activation or rejection. The decision must find clarification idle. Only one request may be outstanding, requests may span classification or approval, and a reply needs an open request. New cycles may start until the decision. Administrative closure is a separate event.
- Branch choice records the route taken. The input contains no independent classification value against which to audit the route. Rejection requires no approval. A complete accepted case includes activation, first payment and closure.
- Each escalation opens an obligation. The first subsequent opinion in the same case discharges all escalations outstanding at that point. An opinion before an escalation gives no clearance for it. A fresh escalation reopens the obligation. An escalation after activation still triggers R4 and has no retroactive effect on that activation.
- R9 compares people, including anyone who re-verifies the bank account after activation. R11 applies while the case is open, including after first payment. A suspension or verification can cover several earlier changes. A fresh change requires fresh action. R11 specifies no relative order between suspension and verification.
- For R12, elapsed times refer to matched activity occurrences. The 24-hour emergency deadline in the Operations version is a separate hard rule. Missing evidence on an applicable hard rule requires review and prevents automatic clearance.

Require the first clear at closure and the second clear at each subsequent Release First Payment. | Remembers which changes have been covered and checks two different deadlines. | Regular control-flow condition, also encodable with net places. An unconditional change-to-verification response would be too strong. |

# 3. Procedural Backbone

On projection to the initial checks and the primary route, the net has the following structure. Here `;` means sequence, `||` means parallel composition with a join, and `⊕` means exclusive choice.

```text
Receive Onboarding Request ;
(Capture Master Data || Run Sanctions Screening || Verify Bank Account) ;
Classify Risk ;
(Reject Vendor ⊕
 ((Manager Approval ⊕ (Enhanced Due Diligence ; Compliance Approval)) ;
  Activate Vendor ; Release First Payment)) ;
Close Onboarding
```

The following accepting Petri net extends that description to **all 18 visible labels**. Exact acceptance means replaying every event from the initial marking and reaching exactly the final marking.

```python
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import Marking, PetriNet
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.utils.petri_utils import add_arc_from_to

net = PetriNet("Controlled vendor onboarding")
places = {}

def place(name):
    if name not in places:
        places[name] = PetriNet.Place(name)
        net.places.add(places[name])
    return places[name]

def step(name, label, inputs, outputs):
    transition = PetriNet.Transition(name, label)
    net.transitions.add(transition)
    for p in inputs:
        add_arc_from_to(place(p), transition, net)
    for p in outputs:
        add_arc_from_to(transition, place(p), net)

step("receive", "Receive Onboarding Request", ["start"],
     ["need_master", "need_screen", "need_bank", "clar_idle", "open"])

step("capture", "Capture Master Data", ["need_master"], ["have_master"])
step("screen", "Run Sanctions Screening", ["need_screen"], ["have_screen"])
step("initial_bank", "Verify Bank Account", ["need_bank"], ["have_bank"])
step("classify", "Classify Risk", ["have_master", "have_screen", "have_bank"],
     ["classified"])

step("ask", "Request Clarification", ["clar_idle"], ["clar_pending"])
step("reply", "Receive Clarification", ["clar_pending"], ["clar_idle"])

step("reject", "Reject Vendor", ["classified", "clar_idle"], ["rejected"])
step("manager", "Manager Approval", ["classified"], ["approved"])
step("enhance", "Enhanced Due Diligence", ["classified"], ["enhanced"])
step("compliance", "Compliance Approval", ["enhanced"], ["approved"])
step("activate", "Activate Vendor", ["approved", "clar_idle"], ["active", "unpaid"])

step("pay", "Release First Payment", ["unpaid"], ["paid"])
step("repeat_bank", "Verify Bank Account", ["active"], ["active"])
step("close_paid", "Close Onboarding", ["active", "paid", "open"], ["end"])
step("close_rejected", "Close Onboarding", ["rejected", "open"], ["end"])

for name, label in [
    ("escalate", "Sanctions Escalation"),
    ("opinion", "Obtain Legal Opinion"),
    ("change", "Change Bank Details"),
    ("suspend", "Suspend Vendor"),
]:
    step(name, label, ["open"], ["open"])

procedural_model = (net, Marking({place("start"): 1}), Marking({place("end"): 1}))
```

Receipt enables three independent checks. Classification consumes all three completion tokens. Taking any route consumes the single classified token. Clarification has independent state, so other work can intervene between request and reply. Activation and rejection both disable further clarification.

The initial and repeat bank transitions have the same visible label and different enabling conditions. The active token survives payment so a bank change and its suspension can still be recorded before closure. A repeat verification is permitted without a change. The four open-case self-loops deliberately leave detailed ordering to the other layers. For example, an early escalation can replay in the net and fail R7. Closure on either route consumes the open token and leaves no work enabled.

# 4. DECLARE Model

```python
def rules(*keys):
    return {key: {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0} for key in keys}

declare_model = {
    "init": rules("Receive Onboarding Request"),
    "altresponse": rules(("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification")),
    "response": rules(
        ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"),
        ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"),
    ),
    "precedence": rules(("Run Sanctions Screening", "Sanctions Escalation")),
    "noncoexistence": rules(
        ("Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor"),
        ("Reject Vendor", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Reject Vendor", "Compliance Approval"),
        ("Enhanced Due Diligence", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Compliance Approval", "Manager Approval"),
    ),
}
```

| Template | Activity or ordered pair as stored | Trace semantics |
|---|---|---|
| init | Receive Onboarding Request | Must be the first event. |
| altresponse | Request Clarification → Receive Clarification | Each request needs a later reply before the next request, including a reply to the last request. Other activities may intervene. Chain response would require the very next event to be the reply. |
| response | Sanctions Escalation → Obtain Legal Opinion | Each escalation needs a strictly later opinion. The same opinion can witness several obligations. |
| response | Activate Vendor → Release First Payment | Activation creates a future payment obligation. Response alone makes no demand on a payment that has no preceding activation. |
| precedence | Run Sanctions Screening → Sanctions Escalation | Each occurrence of the **second** activity requires a strictly earlier occurrence of the **first**. Screening without escalation satisfies the rule. |
| noncoexistence | Reject Vendor, Activate Vendor | Either label may occur alone. Their joint presence violates the rule in either order. |
| noncoexistence | Reject Vendor, Manager Approval | Rejection and the low-risk approval cannot share a case. |
| noncoexistence | Reject Vendor, Compliance Approval | Rejection and the high-risk approval cannot share a case. |
| noncoexistence | Enhanced Due Diligence, Manager Approval | High-risk due diligence cannot share a case with the low-risk approval. |
| noncoexistence | Compliance Approval, Manager Approval | The two approval types cannot share a case. |

Nonsuccession excludes a particular ordering and would still permit the reversed pair. Alternate response imposes neither a decision deadline nor a ban on unsolicited replies. The clarification places supply both properties. The R5/R6 decision is absent from this dictionary so the same activity constraints can be run under either policy version.

# 5. Log-Skeleton Model

```python
log_skeleton = {
    "equivalence": {("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment")},
    "always_before": {("Release First Payment", "Activate Vendor")},
    "always_after": {("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment")},
    "never_together": {
        ("Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor"),
        ("Enhanced Due Diligence", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Compliance Approval", "Manager Approval"),
    },
    "activ_freq": {
        "Manager Approval": {0, 1},
        "Enhanced Due Diligence": {0, 1},
        "Compliance Approval": {0, 1},
        "Activate Vendor": {0, 1},
        "Reject Vendor": {0, 1},
        "Release First Payment": {0, 1},
        "Close Onboarding": {1},
    },
    "directly_follows": set(),
}
```

Project each case onto the seven activities in `activ_freq` for this skeleton. The following adapter accepts a PM4Py EventLog and preserves case boundaries, including empty projections. Its alphabet covers every label used in a skeleton pair. Giving each label in the projection an allowed count avoids unexpected-activity diagnostics from PM4Py for labels omitted from `activ_freq`. This also avoids assigning an arbitrary upper bound to repeatable activities.

```python
from pm4py.objects.log.obj import EventLog, Trace

def skeleton_view(log):
    alphabet = set(log_skeleton["activ_freq"])
    return EventLog([
        Trace([e for e in case if e["concept:name"] in alphabet],
              attributes=dict(case.attributes))
        for case in log
    ])
```

Pass `skeleton_view(log)` and `log_skeleton` to `pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton`. Equivalence compares the two counts in a completed case, symmetrically. Both are zero on rejection and one on acceptance. For an `always_before` pair (A, B), each **A** requires an earlier **B**. Thus payment requires prior activation. The dependent activity comes first in this tuple and second in a DECLARE precedence tuple. In `always_after`, each activation requires a later payment. Equal counts alone would allow them in reverse order.

Each `never_together` pair prohibits the two listed labels in either order. One orientation suffices for that diagnosis. Frequencies count each named activity separately, with exactly one closure on either route. No immediate adjacency is prescribed. This skeleton gives independent count and order diagnostics but does not implement the fork/join, select an approval, or pair clarification cycles.

# 6. Temporal Profile

```python
temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (172800, 86400),
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"): (43200, 21600),
    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"): (259200, 129600),
}
zeta = 2.0
```

For elapsed seconds Δ, flag a warning when `abs(Δ - mean) > zeta * standard_deviation`. The inclusive in-band intervals are respectively **0–96**, **0–24**, and **0–144 hours**. Use the reply in the same clarification cycle, the first subsequent opinion for each escalation, and the first payment following activation. Skip ambiguous or absent matches and report any underlying sequence violation separately.

For repeated activities, construct one two-event sample per matched pair, retaining both original event IDs, then apply the profile or compute the formula directly. An all-ordered-pairs calculation on the entire trace can, for example, time the first request against the third reply. A completed match supplies a duration. A missing opinion supplies no duration and can still fail R4. Under Operations, a hard 24-hour deadline starts at emergency activation, whereas the statistical opinion interval starts at escalation.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

The pseudocode operates on the whole case in trusted event order. A failure records the rule and the witnessing event IDs. An unresolved required check records `not evaluated` and the missing evidence. Independent checks continue after either result.

**R9.** Use a case ID, activity (`concept:name`), event ID or stable index, and canonical person ID resolved from `org:resource`. Apply each comparison once, even when the verification follows approval.

```text
for (left, right) in [
    ({"Verify Bank Account"}, {"Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval"}),
    ({"Capture Master Data"}, {"Activate Vendor"}),
]:
    for x in events with activity in left:
        for y in events with activity in right:
            if either person ID is missing or maps ambiguously:
                record unresolved comparison(x, y)
            else if person(x) == person(y):
                fail R9 at the later event in (x, y), citing both
```

A missing counterpart creates no identity comparison. The procedural model checks whether that activity was required. Two usernames mapping to the same person fail the rule. A shared service account with unknown human provenance leaves it unresolved.

**R5/R6.** Select exactly one policy version. The strict guard needs ordered case activities. The exception additionally needs event times and a trusted, linked authorization record from outside the log. Evidence must identify the case and activation covered, emergency scope, the two authorized parties, and approval effective by the instant of activation. The following monitor distinguishes evidence of invalid authorization from an unavailable register.

```text
pending_escalations = empty set
early_activations_awaiting_opinion = empty list
for e in case order:
    if e.activity == "Sanctions Escalation":
        add e to pending_escalations

    if e.activity == "Activate Vendor" and pending_escalations is nonempty:
        if policy == Legal:
            fail R5 at e, citing the outstanding escalations
        else:  # Operations
            decision = lookup_joint_emergency_authorization(case, e, pending_escalations)
            if decision is denied, invalid, or absent in a verified complete register:
                fail R6 authorization at e
            if decision is unknown (including a missing link or unavailable register):
                record unresolved R6 authorization at e
            append e to early_activations_awaiting_opinion

    if e.activity == "Obtain Legal Opinion":
        for a in early_activations_awaiting_opinion:
            if time(a) or time(e) is unavailable or unreliable:
                record unresolved R6 deadline for (a, e)
            else if elapsed_hours(a, e) is outside [0, 24]:
                fail R6 deadline for (a, e)
        clear early_activations_awaiting_opinion
        clear pending_escalations

at completed case end:
    any pending_escalations are R4 failures (also diagnosed by DECLARE)
    any early_activations_awaiting_opinion are unfulfilled R6 obligations
```

On live cases, a trusted clock and complete event feed can establish that the deadline has expired even before closure. A later opinion retains the deadline failure. A missing emergency flag in this log supplies no evidence either way. A declaration by Operations that the case was an emergency also needs the joint authorization record.

**R11.** Use case, activity and reliable event order. This check needs no resource or clock interval. The two outstanding obligations have different discharge events.

```text
active = suspension_due = verification_due = false
for e in case order:
    if e.activity == "Activate Vendor":
        active = true
    if active and e.activity == "Change Bank Details":
        suspension_due = verification_due = true
    if e.activity == "Suspend Vendor":
        suspension_due = false
    if e.activity == "Verify Bank Account":
        verification_due = false
    if e.activity == "Release First Payment" and verification_due:
        fail R11 at e, citing the most recent uncovered change
    if e.activity == "Close Onboarding" and suspension_due:
        fail R11 at e, citing the most recent unsuspended change
at confirmed case end, also report any outstanding suspension obligation
```

A change after payment still requires suspension. No later payment means no further verification deadline under R11. Evidence of suspension does not clear the verification flag.

**Input quality and route data.** Missing case IDs prevent grouping affected events. Missing labels, ambiguous order, or unknown case-completion status leave dependent sequence or end-of-case obligations unresolved. Preserve findings whose witnesses are already decisive. Missing timestamps prevent affected elapsed-time checks even if sequence numbers establish order. If an independent risk classification is supplied, compare its value at Classify Risk with the observed acceptance route (low with Manager Approval, high with Enhanced Due Diligence and Compliance Approval). A mismatch is a data finding. That additional audit is unevaluated for all eight supplied traces. Their verdicts assess the specified branch structure.

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

- **Policy conflict.** T6 is a witness that separates the policies. Exactly the same activity sequence and duration can arise with valid, invalid or missing authorization. No tightening of an activity-only precedence rule recovers that fact.
- **Duplicate findings.** In T8 the branch violation and the two exclusion checks share the same rejection event. The missing payment has a different witness at closure. Group the first findings together and retain the second finding. Counting layer votes would obscure both causes.
- **Loops and lifecycle.** Equating clarification request and reply counts would accept two requests followed by two replies. An opinion, then a fresh escalation, then activation violates Legal's policy despite the earlier opinion. Requiring exactly one Verify Bank Account per case, or requiring every verification to precede classification or approval, would conflict with legitimate post-activation verification. A direct-follow rule would unnecessarily forbid intervening work.
- **Vacuity.** Response with no escalation and precedence with no escalation both hold. Noncoexistence also holds if neither label occurs. Those results supply no evidence that a required route was completed. The net's final marking tests completion.
- **Observability.** The route does not independently certify the recorded risk, usernames may conceal a common performer, and a case ID alone may be insufficient to match opinions to individual escalation incidents. An incident-specific clearance policy would require incident IDs and per-incident matching. Case-relative timestamps alone establish neither authority nor causality. Equal times need a trusted sequence for strict precedence, and clock skew, batching or missing lifecycle events can distort elapsed times. The procedural AND-split permits concurrency even when only completion events are logged.
- **Metadata.** The DECLARE support and confidence values are supplied model annotations. They are neither measurements from T1–T8 nor a vote that a policy is correct. A frequent historical exception does not repeal a hard rule. A statistical outlier alone does not create one.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

1. Keep the accepting Petri net and its two markings as the canonical procedural representation. Version a policy specification alongside it that identifies the owner and authoritative check for each R-number, the Legal or Operations decision, evidence requirements, matching conventions and severity. Activate one approved policy version per case.
2. Normalize identities and timestamps, establish case boundaries and event order, link external evidence when needed, and mark unavailable inputs. Run exact net replay or an alignment that exposes deviations, then DECLARE on the full trace and the Log Skeleton on its seven-activity projection. Derive both inputs from the **original** log. An alignment's inserted reply or payment is never observed evidence.
3. Run the versioned escalation, resource and bank-change checks, then score completed temporal matches. Continue independent checks after a failure. For live decisions, evaluate the applicable clarification and opinion guards and the authorization lookup at activation, and the verification guard at payment.
4. Report the case verdict with rule ID, policy version, earliest decisive event, relevant earlier events, and outstanding or missing evidence. Show distinct downstream failures and advisory durations with their expected bands. T6 should display Legal's failed ordering guard alongside the satisfied eventual-opinion rule. If Operations is evaluated as a counterfactual, label that version and show the timely opinion and unresolved authorization separately.
