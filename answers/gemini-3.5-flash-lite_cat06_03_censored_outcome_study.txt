## 1. Target definition

### Primary Prediction Target
- **Unit of Analysis:** Individual credit applications (`case:concept:name`).
- **Prediction Time ($T_{pred}$):** The point in time when the case completes the second occurrence of `Credit Review` (or alternatively, the earliest fixed-length prefix of 3 events).
- **Target Horizon:** 90 days following successful `Account Activated`.
- **Target Definition (Conditional Default among Activated Accounts):** Let $Y_i \in \{0, 1\}$ be an indicator of whether an activated account experiences a 90-day default. The estimand is the conditional probability $P(Y_i = 1 \mid \text{Activated}_i = 1, \mathcal{F}_i(T_{pred}))$, where $\mathcal{F}_i(T_{pred})$ represents the observed event prefix history up to $T_{pred}$.
- **Operational Scope:** This model acts as an **early-warning intake screen**. It estimates the risk *conditional upon activation*, evaluating whether applicants currently progressing through underwriting warrant tighter credit limits, manual escalation, or denial, *given that they survive selection*.

### Alternative Targets Considered

1. **Multi-State / Competing-Outcome Target for All Applications:**
   - *Definition:* A categorical or multi-label target assigned to all 48,000 applications: (0) Withdrawn/Rejected/Transferred, (1) Activated with no 90-day default, (2) Activated with 90-day default.
   - *Why it answers a different question:* This target addresses portfolio-level throughput and business operations (e.g., "Will this incoming application convert to a performing loan?"). However, it conflates applicant self-selection (withdrawals), credit policy decisions (rejections), and post-activation credit risk (defaults). An applicant who is rejected has a zero probability of default *by definition*, not because they are creditworthy.

2. **Time-to-Default after Activation with Right Censoring (Survival Target):**
   - *Definition:* Estimating the hazard function $\lambda(t \mid \mathcal{F}_i(T_{pred}))$ over the 90-day post-activation window using Cox Proportional Hazards or Gradient Boosting Survival Trees (e.g., DeSurv/XGBoost-Survival).
   - *Why it answers a different question:* This target provides granularity on *when* within the 90 days a default occurs, accommodating right-censoring for accounts activated recently whose 90-day window has not fully elapsed. While superior for financial loss-given-default modeling, it adds complexity that may exceed the lender's current operational requirements.

## 2. Cohort construction

To prevent survival and selection bias, the cohort construction must establish strict as-of boundaries, explicitly categorize all 48,000 applications, and avoid treating non-activated cases as "non-defaults."

### As-Of and Inclusion Rules
- **Observation Window:** 1 January 2024 to 31 December 2025.
- **Extraction Cutoff:** 15 February 2026.
- **Inclusion Criteria:** All applications starting within the observation window.
- **Censoring Rule:** An activated account is considered *mature* if its `Account Activated` timestamp precedes the extraction cutoff minus 90 days minus the maximum observed delayed feed lag (35 days). Thus, activation cutoff = 15 February 2026 - 125 days = 13 October 2025.

### Cohort-Flow Table (Total 48,000 Applications)

| Cohort Category | Count | Status in Primary Model (Conditional Default) | Status in Competing-Risk Model |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Activated & Mature** (Clean Labeled) | 18,000 | **Included** (Observed $Y=0$ or $Y=1$) | **Included** (Event = Default or Right-Censored) |
| **Activated & Mature** (Default = 1) | 2,700 | **Included** ($Y=1$) | **Included** (Event = Default) |
| **Activated & Immature** (Unmatured 90d window) | 3,600 | **Right-Censored / Excluded** from training | **Included** with right-censoring at current age |
| **Rejected Applications** | 9,100 | **Excluded** from primary population (Undefined $Y$) | **Competing Risk / Terminal State** |
| **Withdrawn Applications** | 5,700 | **Excluded** from primary population (Undefined $Y$) | **Competing Risk / Terminal State** |
| **Transferred Applications** | 2,900 | **Excluded** (or handled via linkage table imputation) | **Competing Risk / Lost to Follow-up** |
| **Still-Open Applications** (In-flight at extraction) | 5,100 | **Excluded** from training (Right-censored at extraction) | **In-flight / Right-Censored** |
| **Total Applications** | **48,000** | — | — |

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

1. **Ordinary Right Censoring:** Accounts that activated successfully but whose 90-day observation window has not completed by the extraction date (the 3,600 immature cases plus any in-flight cases) are right-censored. In survival analysis, their survival time is bounded by their current age. In binary classification, they must be excluded from training unless survival weights (e.g., Inverse Probability of Censoring Weighting - IPCW) are applied.
2. **Delayed Labels:** The external outcome feed arrives 7–35 days *after* the 90-day horizon. We enforce a **label lag buffer**: training labels are only pulled from accounts whose activation date is $\ge 125$ days prior to data extraction. This completely eliminates delayed-label truncation bias.
3. **Selection Through Activation:** Only ~45% of applications reach activation. Conditioning on activation introduces selection bias (Berkson’s bias / collider stratification bias) if unobserved confounders affect both approval and default. 
   - *Primary Strategy:* Explicitly model the conditional probability $P(\text{Default} \mid \text{Activated}, X)$ while acknowledging the population restriction in the deployment boundary.
   - *Sensitivity Analysis:* Implement a two-stage Heckman selection model or a joint propensity-weighted outcome model where Stage 1 estimates $P(\text{Activated} = 1 \mid \text{Features})$ across all 48,000 applications, and Stage 2 models default using Inverse Mills Ratio or propensity weights (IPW).
4. **Transfers as Competing Events / Missing Follow-Up:** Transferred cases (2,900) disappear from the local system. For the 60% with linkage tables, we ingest cross-platform outcome feeds. For the remaining 40%, we treat them as Missing at Random (MAR) conditional on observable channel/region features, using multiple imputation or treating transfer as a competing terminal state.
5. **Permanently Missing Outcome Feeds (Partner-Originated):** Partner-originated accounts have an elevated missing outcome probability. We test the Missing Not at Random (MNAR) assumption by adding a binary indicator feature (`is_partner_originated`) and evaluating performance stability under tree-based models that natively handle missing splits.

## 4. Prefix extraction

To build a valid predictive monitoring model, prefixes must be extracted strictly as of the prediction time $T_{pred}$, mirroring real-time scoring conditions.

- **Prefix Definition:** 
  - *Fixed Event-Count Prefixes:* The sequence of the first $k$ events (e.g., $k \in \{3, 5, 7\}$) per case.
  - *Elapsed-Time Prefixes:* All events occurring within a sliding time window from case start (e.g., first 24 hours, first 72 hours).
- **Handling Data Risks & Artifacts:**
  - *Short Cases:* Cases terminating before reaching length $k$ (e.g., rejected at event 2) are padded with special null tokens or evaluated in separate sub-models stratified by case length.
  - *Repeated Document Loops:* The process allows multiple document request/return cycles. We collapse redundant consecutive identical loops or retain loop counters as numeric features to prevent sequence length explosion.
  - *Activity Renaming (Migration on 15 Oct 2025):* A harmonization dictionary maps legacy activity names to post-migration canonical names (e.g., mapping `"KYC_Verify_v1"` and `"KYC_Check_v2"` to `"Identity/KYC Check"`) prior to prefix extraction. Duplicate events generated during migration are de-duplicated by keeping the first occurrence within a 1-second window.

## 5. Process-aware features

We extract the following feature categories from the event log as of $T_{pred}$:

1. **Control-Flow Features:**
   - Current activity name, active variant ID, total unique activities executed so far, frequency of `Credit Review` revisits.
2. **Timing Features:**
   - Elapsed time from `Application Started` to $T_{pred}$, waiting time in the current state (`pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time`), service time vs. idle time.
3. **Resource & Queue Features:**
   - Resource workload at event timestamps, resource historical default rate (calculated strictly out-of-fold to prevent leakage). *Note: Resource experience is stored at extraction time, so we back-calculate historical tenure or rely on event-log-derived resource frequency.*
4. **Rework Features:**
   - Count of document request loops, number of times returned to `Credit Review`.
5. **Cohort & Calendar Features:**
   - Day of week of application start, calendar month (to capture seasonality and the credit-policy change on 1 July 2025).
6. **Data Type Distinction:**
   - *Static Case Attributes:* `case:channel`, `case:region`, `case:requested_amount`.
   - *Prefix-Derived Dynamic Features:* Cumulative document count, running maximum `risk_score`, variance of `risk_score` updates.
   - *Historical Reference Features:* Out-of-fold resource default rates and regional approval rates.

## 6. Leakage audit

| Feature or data element | Leakage or bias risk | Valid availability rule | Keep/repair/drop | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`final_limit`** | Direct target leakage | Populated *after* final credit decision. | **Drop** (or repair as decision output) | Exists only post-decision; reveals approval/limit tier linked to risk. |
| **`decision_reason`** | Target / outcome leakage | Populated at/after Accept/Reject. | **Drop** | Contains explicit rejection or approval rationales correlated with default. |
| **`collection_status`** | Direct target leakage | Updated *after* activation; directly records default. | **Drop** | Directly encodes the default state or collection actions. |
| **Future Events** (Contract Signed, Collections, etc.) | Future leakage | Must occur strictly $\le T_{pred}$. | **Drop** | Events occurring after $T_{pred}$ represent future states. |
| **`documents_total`** (nightly snapshot) | Look-ahead bias | Includes docs uploaded later on the same calendar day. | **Repair** | Must be replaced by event-log-derived cumulative document counts timestamped $\le T_{pred}$. |
| **Updated `risk_score`** | Valid if as-of; leakage if re-scored post-$T_{pred}$ | Only include score revisions occurring $\le T_{pred}$. | **Keep (with audit)** | Valid dynamic predictor if restricted to timeline. |
| **Resource Experience** (extraction time) | Retrospective bias | Employee tenure at extraction $\neq$ tenure at event time. | **Repair** | Must calculate resource tenure dynamically from their first recorded event in the log. |
| **`label_available_at`** | Information leakage | Future timestamp indicating when label arrived. | **Drop** | Administrative metadata known only post-outcome maturation. |

## 7. Validation design

- **Why Random Split Fails:** A random case split mixes pre- and post-policy change data (1 July 2025) and pre- and post-migration data (15 October 2025) across training and testing folds, leading to optimistic performance estimates and covariate shift failure.
- **Temporal Validation Scheme:**
  - **Training Set:** Applications started between 1 January 2024 and 30 June 2025 (incorporating the pre-policy change period).
  - **Validation Set (Hyperparameter Tuning):** Applications started between 1 July 2025 and 31 August 2025.
  - **Test Set (Out-of-Time Backtest):** Applications started between 1 September 2025 and 31 December 2025 (spanning the migration period and testing generalization under distribution shift).
- **Calibration & Subgroup Performance:**
  - Evaluate Brier Score and Expected Calibration Error (ECE) alongside ROC-AUC and PR-AUC (due to class imbalance: 2,700 defaults out of 21,600 labeled cases $\approx 12.5\%$).
  - Subgroup performance audit across `case:channel`, `case:region`, and pre/post-migration periods.

---

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

- **What the Study Can Claim:** The model predicts the *conditional risk of 90-day default given that an application reaches account activation*, based on historical prefix features observed up to $T_{pred}$.
- **What the Study Cannot Claim:** It does *not* claim to predict population-wide default risk across all 48,000 applications, nor does it capture counterfactual default risk for rejected applicants (who were never exposed to the loan terms).
- **Ways Deployment Changes the Data-Generating Process (Goodhart's Law / Feedback Loops):**
  1. *Policy Feedback:* If loan officers use the early warning score to deny high-risk applications, those applicants will no longer activate, starving the training loop of positive instances in the high-risk region (censoring-by-selection shift).
  2. *Applicant Behavioral Adaptation:* Applicants aware of automated scoring may alter their document upload velocity or interaction cadence to game the prefix features.
  3. *Operational Workflow Changes:* Introducing real-time alerts may alter employee behavior, changing service times and queue dynamics.

## 9. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation skeleton

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.log.util import dataframe_utils

# ==========================================
# 1. DATA INGESTION & PM4Py FORMATTING
# ==========================================
# ASSUMPTION: 'applications_events.csv' is present in the working directory.
csv_path = "applications_events.csv"
print(f"Loading event log from {csv_path}...")
df = pd.read_csv(csv_path)

# Parse timestamps
df['time:timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(df['time:timestamp'])
df['label_available_at'] = pd.to_datetime(df['label_available_at'], errors='coerce')
df['data_extract_time'] = pd.to_datetime(df['data_extract_time'], errors='coerce')

# Harmonize activity names to handle migration (Post-15 Oct 2025 cleanup)
migration_date = pd.Timestamp("2025-10-15")
activity_mapping = {
    "KYC_Verify_v1": "Identity/KYC Check",
    "KYC_Check_v2": "Identity/KYC Check",
    "Credit_Review_Start": "Credit Review"
}
df['concept:name'] = df['concept:name'].replace(activity_mapping)

# Format dataframe for PM4Py
df = dataframe_utils.convert_timestamp_columns_in_df(df)
event_log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id='case:concept:name',
    activity_key='concept:name',
    timestamp_key='time:timestamp'
)

# ==========================================
# 2. COHORT DEFINITION & MATURITY FILTERING
# ==========================================
EXTRACTION_CUTOFF = pd.Timestamp("2026-02-15")
MATURITY_BUFFER_DAYS = 125 # 90 days horizon + 35 days max feed lag

# Determine case start and activation status
case_summary = event_log.groupby('case:concept:name').agg(
    case_start=('time:timestamp', 'min'),
    activated=('concept:name', lambda x: 'Account Activated' in x.values),
    activation_time=('time:timestamp', lambda x: x[x.eq('Account Activated')].min() if 'Account Activated' in x.values else pd.NaT),
    default_recorded=('default_90d', lambda x: x.max())
).reset_index()

# Maturity flag
case_summary['is_mature'] = case_summary['activation_time'] <= (EXTRACTION_CUTOFF - pd.Timedelta(days=MATURITY_BUFFER_DAYS))

print(f"Total applications: {len(case_summary)}")
print(f"Activated cases: {case_summary['activated'].sum()}")
print(f"Mature activated cases: {case_summary['is_mature'].sum()}")

# ==========================================
# 3. TEMPORAL TRAIN/VALIDATION/TEST SPLIT
# ==========================================
# Temporal split by case start time to prevent leakage across policy/migration periods
train_mask = case_summary['case_start'] < pd.Timestamp("2025-07-01")
val_mask = (case_summary['case_start'] >= pd.Timestamp("2025-07-01")) & (case_summary['case_start'] < pd.Timestamp("2025-09-01"))
test_mask = case_summary['case_start'] >= pd.Timestamp("2025-09-01")

train_cases = case_summary[train_mask]['case:concept:name']
val_cases = case_summary[val_mask]['case:concept:name']
test_cases = case_summary[test_mask]['case:concept:name']

print(f"Train cases: {len(train_cases)}, Val cases: {len(val_cases)}, Test cases: {len(test_cases)}")

# ==========================================
# 4. PREFIX EXTRACTION & LEAKAGE AUDIT CHECK
# ==========================================
# Filter log to training cases and extract fixed-length prefixes (e.g., prefix length = 3)
train_log = event_log[event_log['case:concept:name'].isin(train_cases)]
prefixes_log = pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(train_log, 3)

# Process discovery & temporal feature extraction on prefix log
dfg, start_activities, end_activities = pm4py.discover_dfg(prefixes_log)
variants = pm4py.get_variants(prefixes_log)

# Temporal feature extraction (PM4Py native)
prefix_features = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
    prefixes_log, 
    case_id_key='case:concept:name', 
    activity_key='concept:name', 
    timestamp_key='time:timestamp'
)

# Leakage Audit Assertion Check
leaky_columns = ['final_limit', 'decision_reason', 'collection_status', 'label_available_at']
for col in leaky_columns:
    if col in prefix_features.columns:
        raise AssertionError(f"LEAKAGE DETECTED: Column '{col}' is present in feature set!")

print("Leakage audit passed: No forbidden post-prediction columns found in prefix feature set.")
print("Pipeline skeleton executed successfully. Ready for model training integration.")
```
