# Forensic Conformance Review — Insurance-Claim Payments
*Note on framing: `raw_cost` is an edit-distance metric against a structural model under a uniform unit cost. It is treated here as diagnostic telemetry about model-fit, **not** as a risk, loss, harm, or evidence score. Business severity and technical cost are reported in separate columns throughout.*

## 1. Materiality Framework

### 1.1 Why a bespoke framework is required

The alignment cost function supplied for this exercise assigns **cost 1 to every visible log or model move and 0 to sync/silent moves, with no business-specific weighting** (confirmed in the Additional Pattern Evidence). Under this function, a duplicate transport-level notification (CL-603) costs exactly as much as an unevidenced mandatory fraud screen on a EUR 480,000 claim with a one-day-old bank-change (CL-602), and a case in which the organization *correctly refused to pay* scores **worse** (CL-607, cost 4) than the payment-diversion case (CL-610, cost 0). Therefore severity must be derived from the business semantics of the move, the policy overlay (P1–P6), the case data, and the evidence base — never from `raw_cost`.

### 1.2 Severity dimensions (each scored, then consolidated)

| # | Dimension | What it measures | Scoring guidance for this process |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | **Control objective** | Which objective the step serves: authorization/approval, fraud prevention, evidence & records, customer communication | Authorization and fraud objectives dominate communication and records objectives |
| D2 | **Amount / exposure** | Claim value, concentration, and whether funds have left the estate | Tiers:  EUR 5,000 (P1 auto band) · 5,001–50,000 (grey band) · > 50,000 (P2 mandatory route) |
| D3 | **Reversibility** | Can the outcome be undone? | Settled bank payment = effectively irreversible; notification = reversible/repeatable; log gap = retrospectively correctable |
| D4 | **Timing / sequence** | Order violations and SLA breaches | P3 (Pay Claim before the applicable approval) is by definition severe; P4 is a 24-hour SLA |
| D5 | **Customer / regulatory harm** | Payment diversion (APP fraud), wrongful payment, audit/records defensibility, notification rights | Fraud-indicator cases (beneficiary-bank change) rank above pure amount |
| D6 | **Recurrence** | Isolated vs systemic; shared connector/systemic cause | Systemic patterns escalate one level and receive their own remediation line, even if each instance is mild |
| D7 | **Evidence reliability** | Independent, immutable, attributable evidence vs mutable field vs verbal assertion | Independent bank timestamps and signed external reports are strong; free-text mutable CRM links are weak |
| D8 | **Model scope** | Is the case in the intended population (P6)? | Out-of-scope behavior is de-escalated at case level but tracked as model-coverage severity, because scope artifacts mask real findings |

### 1.3 Consolidated severity scale (1–5)

| Level | Label | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **5 — Critical** | Mandatory authorization or fraud control **violated or not evidenced** on a materially exposed claim and/or a claim carrying an independent fraud indicator; or a P3-type pay-before-approval failure; or an irreversible payment made through an unauthorized route. Immediate containment + investigation. |
| **4 — High** | Data-aware policy violation with payment already executed but **without** an independent fraud indicator and with moderate exposure; or a control-design defect that creates a **repeatable** unauthorized-approval route. |
| **3 — Moderate** | The control objective was **met in substance**, but the evidence retained is not sufficient to demonstrate it for a material claim (records/audit/regulatory defensibility failure). |
| **2 — Low-material** | No control objective unmet in substance; the deviation is evidentiary, naming, logging, or integration-related and is retrospectively correctable. |
| **1 — Minimal / noise** | No control objective unmet; the deviation is UI telemetry, transport duplication, silent model routing, or out-of-scope behavior where the business outcome was correct (including *correct* non-payment). |

**Escalation rules (any one applies):** (a) fraud indicator present (recent beneficiary-bank change, HIGH risk with bank change)  +1; (b) irreversible settlement  +1; (c) recurrence  10 occurrences sharing one systemic cause  +1 at pattern level; (d) evidence reliability is weak *and* the claim is material (D2 high)  +1.

**De-escalation rules:** (a) behavior confirmed out of scope under P6  case-level severity drops to 1, but model-coverage severity is recorded separately; (b) missing core-log event with **independent, signed, attributable** external evidence in correct temporal order  drops to 2–3.

### 1.4 Confidence is orthogonal to severity

Severity answers *"how much does this matter if true?"* Confidence answers *"how strongly does the evidence support the facts and the interpretation asserted?"* They must never be collapsed.

Confidence is reported on three separate axes:
- **C1 — Fact confidence**: are the data points (amount, risk score, timestamps, bank-change date, presence/absence of a record) reliable? Bank-side settlement timestamps and signed external documents give high C1; mutable text fields give low-to-medium C1.
- **C2 — Cause confidence**: how well is the root cause established? Usually lower than C1 (e.g., we know Fraud Screen is unevidenced — high C1 — but not whether it was skipped or merely unlogged — low C2).
- **C3 — Interpretation confidence**: how settled is the governing rule? High where P1–P5 are explicit; **low where P6 is disputed** (CL-607, CL-612) and where the policy overlay leaves a grey band (CL-609: P1 forbids Auto Approve above 5,000/LOW, but P2's mandatory route only starts at 50,000/HIGH/bank-change — so the required route for 5,001–49,999 is genuinely undefined).

Critical governing principle: **a missing event is evidence of a logging gap, not proof that an activity did not occur.** Every "activity not performed" claim below is expressed as a hypothesis and paired with discriminating evidence tests (Section 4). High severity + low confidence  investigate and contain first; do not treat as an established breach. Low severity + high confidence  still fix the systemic cause, because recurrence is itself a severity driver.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

> Governing rule applied throughout: **a missing event is a logging observation, not proof of non-performance.** Each test is written to *discriminate*, not to confirm a preferred story.

### 4.1 CL-602 — `M(Fraud Screen)` on EUR 480,000, HIGH risk, one-day-old bank change
| Hypothesis | Implication | Discriminating evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **H1** Fraud check performed verbally/ad hoc and never recorded | Record-keeping breach; control may have operated | Dated written attestations from approver + alleged screener; calendar/telephony/Teams records for a call in the Assess-LossManual-Approve window; absence of any screen ID anywhere |
| **H2** Screening performed in a standalone fraud tool whose results never reached the core log | Integration gap; control operated | Fraud-tool query/decision logs keyed to claim or claimant; screen ID referenced in the approval note; correspondence between fraud-tool volume and `Fraud Screen` event volume |
| **H3** Screening was skipped under time pressure or an undocumented override | Genuine control failure; potential fraud loss | Override/exception register entries (expected: none — which is itself informative); time-to-approve distribution vs peers; approver workload that day; whether the same approver waived screens elsewhere |
| **H4** The screen is subsumed under `Manual Approve` (activity-granularity artifact, as in CL-611) | Model artifact only | Approval checklist/record showing fraud-screen fields completed inside the approval artefact; UI task IDs; procedure wording on whether screen and approval are separate sign-offs (P2 wording "Fraud Screen **and** Manual Approve" argues against H4) |

**Key test:** cross-reference the **nine** external-adjuster cases and the **four** auto-approve violations for the same "control performed elsewhere / not at all" ambiguity. If a fraud-tool ID exists for most claims but not CL-602/CL-604, H2 weakens and H1/H3 strengthen.

### 4.2 CL-604 — `Pay Claim` accepted by the bank at 10:03, approval at 10:20
| Hypothesis | Implication | Discriminating evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **H1** Payment released before authorization (auto-release / batch job not gated) | Severe control failure (P3) | Payment-release scheduler/batch logs; the release job's precondition check; whether other >50k claims show the same release-without-approval signature |
| **H2** Approval decision was taken earlier but only *recorded* at 10:20 (retro-documentation) | Control operated; evidence-integrity failure | Approval system's decision-time vs record-time fields; draft/save history of the approval record; approver's session and keystroke telemetry; e-mail/chat acknowledging approval before 10:03 |
| **H3** Cross-system clock skew (bank interface ahead of core system) | Apparent, not real, sequence failure | NTP synchronisation status and drift logs for both systems; a control sample of same-day timestamps on a known-ordered activity pair across the two systems |
| **H4** Emergency/manual release used (e.g., urgent payment to avoid claimant hardship) | Documented exception expected | Emergency-release authorisation records; incident tickets; a standing exception policy for hardship payments (P3 leaves no such carve-out, so even a documented exception would be a policy gap) |

**Key test:** H3 is the cheapest to eliminate and must be eliminated first, because it is the only hypothesis that would remove the P3 classification. Note that H2, even if true, does **not** resolve the case — it converts an authorization failure into an evidence-integrity failure at severity 3–4.

### 4.3 CL-608 — `M(Notify Claimant)` with a mutable CRM link to a portal message at +3h
| Hypothesis | Implication | Discriminating evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **H1** Notification sent via secure portal/CRM only (P4-permitted channel) | P4 satisfied; core-log gap only | Immutable message store record with message ID, send timestamp and delivery receipt; portal send logs; claimant login/download record for the message |
| **H2** Message never sent; the CRM record is inaccurate or aspirational | P4 failure on a EUR 250,000 claim | Absence of any message in the immutable store; mail-gateway logs; claimant confirmation |
| **H3** Message sent, but the CRM link points to another case (mis-binding) | P4 satisfied for *this* case but a systemic linkage defect | Compare message content/claim number inside the message with the case; CRM **field-history/audit trail** for edits to the link field; scan for other cases whose link resolves to the same message ID |

**Key test:** the **field-history of the mutable link field**. If the field has no audit history, the organization cannot prove the linkage at all — which is the actual finding, independent of whether the notification occurred.

### 4.4 CL-606 / Pattern B — `M(Assess Loss)` with external adjuster evidence (9 cases; 2 unstable IDs)
| Hypothesis | Implication | Discriminating evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **H1** Assessment performed externally and correctly not logged in core | Evidential/integration gap only | Adjuster assignment order and contract scope; signed report metadata (author, licence, timestamp) consistent with the case facts |
| **H2** Core logging dropped the event (logging defect) | Systemic data-quality defect | Adjuster channel event volumes vs core `Assess Loss` counts; error logs on the document-to-event interface; other cases from the same channel |
| **H3** The report is not for this case (mis-attributed document) | Assessment effectively absent | Report's internal claim number vs case key; document hash and creation metadata; the **two cases lacking a stable document-to-case identifier** are the priority sample |

**Key test:** do **not** assert that `Assess Loss` did not occur. Assert instead that the organization cannot currently bind the assessment to the case with sufficient reliability — and prove or disprove binding via document metadata, not via the process log.

### 4.5 CL-609 / CL-610 / Pattern E — zero-cost data-aware Auto Approve failures (4 cases/month)
| Hypothesis | Implication | Discriminating evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **H1** The P1/P2 rule was never enforced at the decision point (control-design gap) | Repeatable unauthorized-approval route | Presence/absence of a rule-engine decision log; UI validation rules for Auto Approve; whether the `X`-branch is user-selectable without guard |
| **H2** The data attribute was wrong *at decision time* (e.g., risk score refreshed later, bank-change flag set late) | Data-latency defect rather than an authorization breach | **Point-in-time attribute snapshots** at the approval timestamp; change-data-capture logs with flag-set times (CL-610: was the bank change known at 1 hour prior to payment?); risk-score recalculation history |
| **H3** A legitimate, authorised exception was granted but not documented | Governance/records gap | Exception/override registers; delegation-of-authority matrix for 5k–50k approvals; the approver's authority level |
| **H4** P1 is advisory and the binding rule is different (overlay mis-stated) | Interpretation error by this review | Version-controlled policy documents and effective dates; the rule specification used by any existing decision engine |

**Key test:** the **point-in-time snapshot**. For CL-610 specifically, H2 is falsifiable in one query: if the bank-change flag was already set when Auto Approve fired, the failure is a rule-enforcement failure (H1), not a data-latency failure. This test also determines whether the other 3 monthly cases share one cause.

### 4.6 CL-611 / Pattern C — `Assess Damage` vs `Assess Loss`
| Hypothesis | Implication | Discriminating evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **H1** Straightforward activity rename without log-schema migration | Naming artifact | Data-dictionary change log with effective dates; UI version history showing the rename; identical task IDs before/after |
| **H2** Two genuinely distinct activities conflated (e.g., damage assessment vs loss assessment by different roles) | Real control-coverage gap hidden by an assumed 1:1 mapping | Role/segregation-of-duties data on the events; whether both labels ever co-occur in one case; procedure wording distinguishing the two |
| **H3** Logging field-mapping error only (wrong column populated) | Pure data-pipeline defect | ETL/mapping job configuration and its change history; the fact that the mapping table was updated *after* the case |

**Key test:** population-level co-occurrence. If `Assess Damage` and `Assess Loss` **never co-occur** in any case and their role/timestamp distributions are identical, H1/H3 are supported. If they co-occur or are performed by different roles, H2 is supported and the 1:1 alias mapping must **not** be applied — doing so would mask a real gap.

### 4.7 CL-603 / Pattern A — 74 retry duplicates from one connector
| Hypothesis | Implication | Discriminating evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **H1** Transport-level redelivery duplicates the **event** but not the **message** | Pure logging noise (P5) | Message-ID identity across the pair; exactly one delivery receipt; connector retry logs with matching correlation IDs |
| **H2** The claimant genuinely received two notifications | Minor customer harm / reputational risk (duplicate claim notices can imply duplicate payments) | Claimant complaints; portal inbox showing two entries; SMTP/SMS gateway delivery counts per message ID |
| **H3** Log-side deduplication failure (message sent once, stored twice) | Data-quality defect | Uniqueness constraint absent on (case, message ID); storage-layer insert logs |

**Key test:** one delivery receipt per message ID supports H1/H3 (noise); two delivery receipts supports H2 (customer-visible). Either way the **systemic** cause is the same connector, so remediation is unambiguous — which is why confidence is High despite the hypothesis split.

### 4.8 CL-607 / CL-612 / Pattern D — scope of denied and withdrawn claims (P6)
| Hypothesis | Implication | Discriminating evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **H1** These traces are out of scope for M0 | Filter them; cost 4 is meaningless | Model charter / design document stating "payable claims only"; the model owner's specification of the initial marking's population |
| **H2** They are in-scope intake outcomes requiring a model extension | Model-coverage defect producing systematic false findings | Process owner's operating model defining "claim" and "intake"; regulatory/reporting definitions of claim handling; whether denial/withdrawal appear in KPIs reported to regulators |

**Key test:** this is a **governance decision, not an empirical question** — but it is decidable by evidence: if denial/withdrawal outcomes are included in externally reported claim-handling statistics, H2 is strongly supported and the model must be extended (or conformance must be run on a documented, filtered population).

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

### 6.1 What the number actually measures

`raw_cost` is the minimum total weight of edit operations (log-move, model-move) needed to reconcile one trace with one model, under a **cost function that assigns weight 1 to every visible log or model move and 0 to synchronous and silent moves, with no business-specific weighting** (stated explicitly in the Additional Pattern Evidence). Formally it is a *structural distance to the specification as encoded*. Business risk, by contrast, is a function of at least: probability and magnitude of loss, control objective served, reversibility, timing, fraud indicators, customer and regulatory harm, evidence reliability, model validity, and recurrence. The two quantities share no monotone relationship, and this sample contains a **complete rank inversion** between them.

**Inversion demonstration.** Ranking by `raw_cost` descending gives: CL-607/CL-612 (4)  CL-601/CL-604 (3)  CL-611 (2)  CL-602/CL-603/CL-606/CL-608 (1)  CL-605/CL-609/CL-610 (0). Ranking by business severity gives CL-602/CL-604/CL-610 (5)  CL-609 (4)  CL-608 (3)  CL-606/CL-611 (2)  the rest (1). Three of the four *least* costly cases are the three severity-5 cases, and the two *most* costly cases are the two zero-exposure, zero-harm cases. Any resource allocation driven by `raw_cost` would investigate correct non-payments and cosmetic naming issues while leaving an unverified EUR 480,000 payment-diversion profile and an irreversible payment to a one-hour-old bank account untouched.

### 6.2 Failure modes, each with a case

**(a) Cost-function design — unit weights encode a false equivalence.** CL-602 (`M(Fraud Screen)`, cost 1) and CL-603 (`L(Notify Claimant)` duplicate, cost 1) receive identical scores. One is an unevidenced mandatory fraud control on EUR 480,000 with a one-day-old bank change; the other is a retrying connector redelivering a message the claimant was already correctly notified about. With uniform unit costs, the metric asserts that one unmatched move is one unmatched move — a claim with no business content. Worse, CL-611's pure **naming** artifact costs **2**, i.e. *double* the unevidenced fraud screen. A cost function that weights label substitution above a missing control is not merely uninformative, it is systematically perverse.

**(b) Model scope — the metric punishes correct behaviour outside the model's population.** CL-607 (denied lead, EUR 0 paid) and CL-612 (claimant withdrew, EUR 0 paid) each cost **4**, the joint-highest in the sample. Four of their moves are `M()` moves demanding `Assess Loss`, `Auto Approve`, `Pay Claim` and `Notify Claimant` — in other words, the model scores the organization down for **not** making a payment that coverage screening or the claimant's own withdrawal made improper. Under P6 this population is scope-disputed; under any business reading, non-payment is the compliant outcome. Cost also degrades the *statistical* base: "denied and withdrawn cases account for most alignments with raw cost  4" means the metric's top decile is dominated by artifacts, which corrupts KPI denominators (e.g. P4 timeliness rates computed over traces that contain no required notification) and trains reviewers to discount high-cost traces.

**(c) Hidden moves — fitting silence is invisible to risk, and cost 0 is not risk-free.** CL-605 shows `T(tau,c=0)`: a silent routing transition introduced by converting `X( ->(Fraud Screen, Manual Approve), Auto Approve )` to a Petri net. This is technically **fitting** — an invisible activity, cost 0 — and carries no business content whatsoever. The problem is that cost 0 is shared with CL-609 and CL-610 (below). A metric that assigns the same value to a conversion artifact and to a payment-diversion exposure cannot be used as a triage key. Silent moves are also structurally unfalsifiable: because they can fire at cost 0 anywhere the model permits, alignment will happily "absorb" sequencing questions that the business needs answered.

**(d) Data conditions — the model has no data semantics, so data-aware policy failures cost nothing.** This is the most consequential gap. P1 permits `Auto Approve` only when amount  EUR 5,000 **and** risk is LOW **and** no bank change within 7 days; P2 mandates `Fraud Screen` + `Manual Approve` for > EUR 50,000, HIGH risk, **or any recent bank change**. The process tree encodes an `X`-choice with **no guard at all**: both CL-609 (EUR 18,000, MEDIUM, auto-approved) and CL-610 (EUR 4,500, bank changed one hour before payment, auto-approved) are **fully synchronous, `raw_cost = 0`**. CL-610 additionally triggers P2's mandatory manual route and was paid to a just-changed account — the strongest fraud indicator in the sample, on an irreversible settlement. Both cases also expose a genuine **policy grey band**: P1 forbids Auto Approve above 5,000/LOW, while P2's mandatory route begins only at 50,000/HIGH/bank-change, so the required approval for EUR 5,001–49,999 is undefined — a control-design gap no alignment, at any cost setting, could ever reveal. And the pattern evidence confirms this is not anecdotal: **four cases in the month** show fully synchronous Auto Approve paths violating P1 or P2. Zero cost, four policy failures.

**(e) Activity granularity and labels — cost measures naming, not behaviour.** CL-611 logs `Assess Damage` where the signed procedure and UI say `Assess Loss`; the alignment charges `L(Assess Damage,c=1)` + `M(Assess Loss,c=1)` = 2 for a **1:1 label substitution** of one real activity. Substantively the case is fully compliant with P2 (Fraud Screen  Manual Approve  Pay Claim in the mandated order on a EUR 62,000 HIGH-risk claim). Cost here measures schema drift and is additionally dangerous because it is **two-sided**: the same mechanism that creates false findings can *mask* real ones, since a genuine deviation can be reinterpreted as a naming artifact and normalised away.

**(f) Duplicated telemetry and transport noise — cost accumulates on non-business events.** CL-601 costs **3** — the joint-highest in the sample — entirely from `Open Workbench`, `Autosave` and `Close Workbench`, which P5 explicitly excludes from business activity. CL-603's retry duplicate is the same class at smaller scale. With 74 retry duplicates per month from a single connector, the metric generates roughly 75 spurious findings monthly: not merely uninformative but actively harmful, because audit attention is a scarce resource and this noise crowds out severity-5 cases.

**(g) Missing-but-external evidence — cost cannot tell "not done" from "done elsewhere".** CL-606 (`M(Assess Loss)`, cost 1) is numerically identical to CL-602 (`M(Fraud Screen)`, cost 1). In CL-606 the assessment **did** occur: a licensed external adjuster signed a report at 10:05, with fraud screening and approval following in the correct order before payment — P2 satisfied in substance. In CL-602 the mandatory fraud control cannot be demonstrated at all on a EUR 480,000 claim with a one-day-old bank change. CL-608 is a third variant at cost 1: notification evidence exists in a permitted channel well inside the 24-hour SLA, but through a **mutable text field**, so P4's retention condition is only weakly met. Identical cost; three materially different evidentiary situations (strong external evidence / no evidence / weak evidence). The metric is blind to the *quality* of the evidence base, which is the decisive dimension here.

**(h) Irreversibility and timing — the metric rewards repairability instead of penalising exposure.** CL-604 is the clearest case. P3 states in terms that `Pay Claim` before the applicable approval is a severe control failure **"even if the model alignment can repair it cheaply"** — and the alignment does exactly that: it consumes the early `Pay Claim` as a log move and leaves a model `Pay Claim` unmet later, at a total cost of 3, of which the misordering accounts for only 2 units. What the business experienced is EUR 90,000 leaving the estate at 10:03 and being authorized at 10:20 — an irreversible, unauthorized payment. The metric measures the *editability* of the trace; risk is governed by the *irreversibility* of the real-world act. These are not merely different — they are close to inversely related, because the cheapest alignments are precisely those where the model can quietly reposition the offending event.

### 6.3 Additional structural reasons

- **Additivity without interaction.** Cost sums independent unit penalties, so it cannot express that a bank change **plus** auto-approval **plus** HIGH risk is not three small things but one catastrophic conjunction (CL-610's P1P2 breach).
- **Alignment non-uniqueness.** `raw_cost` is a *minimum* over alignments; ties are resolved arbitrarily, and different optimal alignments distribute the same cost across different move positions (compare the supplied CL-604 alignment with the equally plausible variant that consumes `Pay Claim` later). The cost is stable across ties; the *business interpretation* of where the deviation sits is not. The supplied alignments are therefore treated here as authoritative diagnostic inputs, per the brief, while being read for semantics rather than for their numeric total.
- **Model validity is assumed, not tested.** Cost is defined relative to a model; if the model is wrong (P6 scope gaps, the missing 5k–50k approval route, the absent data guards), then a low cost is evidence of agreement with a defective specification. CL-609 and CL-610 are exactly this: they are *conformant* to a tree that under-specifies the policy.
- **No probability, no magnitude.** Cost contains no claim amount, no fraud-indicator state, no settlement finality — so it cannot rank EUR 480,000 against EUR 4,500 in either direction, and in CL-610 it fails to rank a EUR 4,500 fraud-pattern payment above a EUR 800 compliant one.

**Conclusion.** `raw_cost` is retained in this report as a *diagnostic* column — useful for detecting where the log and the model disagree, for sizing model-repair effort, and for spotting systematic logging defects — but severity is assigned from control objective, exposure, reversibility, timing, harm, recurrence, evidence reliability and scope. The two columns are never combined arithmetically.

**Notes on reproduction fidelity (as required):**
1. The tree string is parsed with `pm4py.parse_process_tree` and converted with `pm4py.convert_to_petri_net`. The conversion introduces at least one **silent routing transition** for `X( ->(Fraud Screen, Manual Approve), Auto Approve )` — this is the exact mechanism behind CL-605's `T(tau,c=0)`, which is **technically fitting and business-irrelevant**.
2. The log is built as a **formatted pandas dataframe** (`pm4py.format_dataframe` + `convert_to_event_log`) so timestamps and case attributes (amount, risk level, bank-change flag) survive. The `pm4py.parse_event_log_string` alternative expects one delimited line per case (`case_id;act1,act2,...`) and carries no timestamps, which is why the dataframe route is used.
3. The event log contains the **log side only**: `S(A)` and `L(A)` contribute events; `M(A)` has no log event by construction; `T(tau)` never appears. CL-606 therefore has no `Assess Loss`, CL-608 has no `Notify Claimant`, CL-611 carries the legacy `Assess Damage`, and CL-604 logs `Pay Claim` at 10:03 before `Manual Approve` at 10:20.
4. `pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments` output is printed for transparency only. **PM4Py may select a different optimal alignment and formats moves differently from the supplied `S()/L()/M()/T()` notation** (notably for CL-604's out-of-order `Pay Claim`, and for tau handling). The supplied alignments remain the authoritative diagnostic inputs for the business narrative in Sections 2–6.
5. The supplied move summaries are reconstructed in a pandas dataframe with **`raw_cost_technical` and `severity_business` in separate columns**; an assertion verifies that the `L`/`M` counts reproduce `raw_cost` exactly (1 unit per visible move, 0 for `S` and `T`). Aggregations cover move signatures, deviation types, raw costs, amounts, risk levels, amount bands (including the undefined 5k–50k grey band) and P1–P6 policy flags.
6. The final risk-prioritisation table is ordered by **narrative severity**, with technical cost displayed alongside but **never used to rank, weight, or compute severity**. The divergence check at the end is presented as *evidence* of the metric's failure mode, not as an alternative scoring formula.
