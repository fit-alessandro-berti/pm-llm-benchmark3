1. System boundary and assumptions

The boundary is the order-fulfillment process captured in the event log, from Receive order through Close or Cancel due to delay. Inside it are the physical flows the log can already measure: production and handling energy (`energy_kwh` by `machine_group`), outbound transport (`transport_kgco2e` by `route_mode`), process scrap (`material_scrap_kg`), packaging mass (`package_material_kg`), and the multipliers that repeat those flows — rework production, packaging rework, partial shipments, emergency production, expedites, cancellations after work has started, and batch items that remain unshipped. Service, quality, and resilience sit inside the decision boundary even though they are not emissions: late delivery against `promised_lead_time_hours`, `rework_flag`, cancellations, and the continued availability of an alternate route for Service-contract customers.

Outside the boundary, and therefore not converted into a footprint here, are embodied impacts of purchased Standard-stock materials, upstream production of packaging resins and metals, inbound freight to the warehouse, building energy, grid-to-CO2e conversion of `energy_kwh`, customer downtime, use-phase, and end-of-life. Standard-stock looks low-impact only because its 0.8–0.9 kWh and ~0.05 kg scrap are operational additions. A product-level life-cycle assessment would likely be dominated by the part itself, which this log does not describe.

Units stay separate. There is no emission factor in the evidence that would let kWh, scrap kilograms, packaging kilograms, and transport kgCO2e be added into one score.

The segment table is a complete mix (shares sum to 100%). The selected paths are not: they cover about 89% of orders. Stock paths (42% + 5% + 7%) line up with the 55% stock segments. The two Critical paths (8% + 3%) line up with the 11% Critical segment. The three Made-to-order paths cover 24 of the 34 percentage points of Made-to-order volume, so Made-to-order conclusions are strongest for those variants and weaker for the residual 10%. Discovered-model precision is low where emergency production and partial shipments interleave; attribute checks (`route_mode`, `case_type`, `expedite_flag`, `rework_flag`, batch size) are more reliable than trace alignment alone. DFG counts and path shares are close but not identical (for example 620 dedicated-van shipments versus an 8% variant share), so both are used as corroboration rather than forced into one ledger.

A mix-weighted index from the segment table is an accounting identity of the given averages, not a new measurement. Per order it is about 10.3 kgCO2e transport, 7.4 kWh, and 0.35 kg scrap. Critical-downtime is 11% of orders and about 41% of the transport index (11% × 38 kg). Made-to-order is 34% of orders and about 87% of the energy index and about 85% of the scrap index. Those two concentrations set the redesign: transport governance on Critical and on unjustified air, energy and scrap governance on Made-to-order machines, batches, and rework.

Remaining uncertainty: whether `transport_kgco2e` already contains empty van returns; whether batch `energy_kwh` is allocated per item or per order; the distribution of `promised_lead_time_hours` behind the cycle-time medians; how often “forecasts are wrong” in the 6% obsolescence statement; and which share of the 31% of dedicated-van cases with no justification event are truly non-urgent rather than undocumented emergencies.

3. Intervention options

**S1. Green shipping recommender at promise time, rechecked at ship.**
Target path: every ship decision, with the first control point on Standard-stock Service-contract and on stock → standard pack → air (5% of orders, 18.5 kg, 17 h, 3% late).
Mechanism: choose `parcel_ground` or `consolidated_truck` when historical lane reliability and the remaining slack to `promised_lead_time_hours` still give more than 85% on-time probability (SP1). Keep `parcel_air` when that probability fails. Make the choice before packaging so `route_mode` and pack type are compatible.
Service, quality, resilience: neutral where the probability gate is honest. Air remains available, which is what R1 requires for Service-contract customers during a ground or truck disruption. Quality is unchanged.
Evidence to monitor: recommended versus actual `route_mode`, SP1 violation rate, late rate and p90 by segment, `expedite_flag` after a ground recommendation, and transport kgCO2e on the stock–air path.

**S4. Priority pick plus ground for Standard-stock Service-contract orders that still have slack.**
Target: the Standard-stock Service-contract segment (16% of orders, median 18 h, late 5%, 7.8 kg transport versus 4.2 kg for Regular) and, within it, the 22% SP1 violations.
Mechanism: warehouse priority removes queue time so the slower mode can still meet the promise. Air is about 24 h faster and about 4–6× the kgCO2e; the stock–air path is 18.5 kg against 3.6 kg on stock–ground. Switching only the slack-feasible subset avoids paying that multiple where the promise does not require it.
Service, quality, resilience: designed to hold the segment near its current 5% late rate. The SP1 ceiling of 15% late is too loose to use as an operating target for Service-contract customers. Quality is unchanged. Resilience stays intact because air remains the recorded alternate and is still used when priority pick cannot create enough slack.
Evidence: cycle-time distribution of pick-to-ship for this segment, share still on air, conditional late rate of orders moved to ground, and override frequency.

**S5. Twice-daily consolidated truck for non-urgent stock.**
Target: stock → standard pack → ground (42%, median 34 h, late 8%, 3.6 kg, 0.9 kWh), limited to Regular orders whose slack absorbs a 12–24 h consolidation wait. Service-contract stock joins only through the S1/S4 gate.
Mechanism: consolidated truck is 35–55% lower kgCO2e than an individual ground parcel. Twice daily caps the wait, unlike holding freight until a truck is full.
Service, quality, resilience: median cycle time lengthens by up to a day on participating orders. Standard-stock Regular already has p90 of 62 h, so orders near the promise are excluded. Quality is unchanged. A truck lane must not become the only lane for Service-contract customers (R1); parcel ground and air stay bookable.
Evidence: load factor and kgCO2e per order on truck versus parcel ground, cutoff spillover into `parcel_air`, late rate for Standard-stock Regular, and cancellation rate.

**S2. Dynamic batching with segment wait caps, firm orders only.**
Target: Plan production → Batch wait → CNC (3020 waits, 2910 released to CNC) and Heat treatment (about half of CNC output: 1820 of roughly 3530 CNC exits). Primary beneficiary is Made-to-order Regular on the efficient path (13%, 16.0 kWh, 5.8 kg, 0.70 kg scrap, median 118 h, 21% late) when slack exists. Made-to-order Service-contract uses a much shorter cap. Critical-downtime does not enter batch wait.
Mechanism: CNC_A energy per item falls from 2.8 kWh at batch size 1–3 to 1.3 kWh at 12–20, while median wait rises from about 4 h to 38 h. Heat treatment is least energy-intensive above batch size 10 and raises late risk by 1.7× while orders wait. The rule pools firm orders up to the cap and releases a smaller batch when the cap is reached. It does not pad batches with forecast demand. The 6% fourteen-day unshipped rate is tied to wrong forecasts, so forecast-fill is the part to drop.
Service, quality, resilience: orders that today wait too long are released earlier, which should cut the 21% late rate on the long batch path. Orders with real slack wait longer and absorb the energy benefit. A single global batch of 12–20 would push Made-to-order Service-contract (already 13% late, p90 156 h) and any heat-treat wait in the wrong direction. Resilience of emergency capacity is preserved by keeping Critical out of the batch queue.
Evidence: kWh per item by batch-size band, wait hours versus the segment cap, late rate conditional on heat-treat wait, 14-day unshipped rate, and `expedite_flag` raised during batch wait.

**S3. Prefer CNC_A for Made-to-order work when queue and due-date slack allow.**
Target: Made-to-order cases now started on CNC_B, especially the rework variant (5%, median 160 h, 32% late, 32 kWh, 6.0 kg transport, 1.8 kg scrap).
Mechanism: on Made-to-order, CNC_B’s rework rate is 18% versus 9% on CNC_A. The 5% path is already the rework-conditioned variant, so it illustrates the cost of a rework case rather than the average of all CNC_B output. Moving slack-tolerant work to CNC_A is a way to cut the incidence of that loop. Each loop returns to CNC (390 of 470 rework exits) and repeats energy and scrap. CNC_B remains the fast resource for emergency production (720 emergency cases reach CNC) and for Made-to-order orders whose slack cannot absorb the CNC_A queue.
Service, quality, resilience: first-pass quality should move toward CNC_A’s 9% rework rate on the volume that moves. Cycle time improves for orders that avoid a 160 h rework loop and worsens for orders that merely change queue. Concentrating slack work on CNC_A is viable only while a queue cap still leaves surge room; otherwise emergency and Service-contract Made-to-order lose the faster machine.
Evidence: rework rate and scrap kg by `machine_group`, queue time and due-date slack at the routing decision, share of emergency orders that still find a free machine, and late rate of Made-to-order Service-contract.

**S7. Inspection hold-point for high-rework machine and part combinations.**
Target: Inspection → Rework production (470) → CNC production (390), and the downstream packaging and customer-return defects the log links to rushed inspection. Priority combinations are CNC_B output and Made-to-order parts that later fail Q1 or packaging.
Mechanism: inspection_cell queues cause missed defects that become machining loops, packaging rework, or returns. A hold-point on the high-risk subset adds a real check before Package reinforced. The efficient Made-to-order path already spends 0.70 kg scrap and 16 kWh; the rework path spends 1.8 kg and 32 kWh. Catching the defect once, before ship, avoids a second machining cycle and a second transport leg.
Service, quality, resilience: good parts pick up a short inspection delay. Parts that would have looped should finish sooner than the 160 h rework variant. The gate needs protected capacity; otherwise the same queue that causes rushing simply gets longer. Resilience impact is small if emergency and Critical orders use a staffed fast lane through the same cell rather than skipping the check entirely.
Evidence: first-pass yield by `machine_group`, rework-loop count, inspection queue time, damage-return rate, and late-rate change on Made-to-order split by whether the case entered the hold-point.

**S6. Packaging–route compatibility check before the first pack.**
Target: Rework packaging after Package standard (260) and after Package reinforced (360); Q1 on Made-to-order parts over 8 kg.
Mechanism: packaging rework happens when the pack does not match `route_mode` or part geometry. Choosing the pack from weight, geometry, and the already-selected route eliminates most of those 620 repack events and the material they consume. Reinforced packaging stays mandatory for Made-to-order parts over 8 kg unless inspection certifies low damage risk. That choice doubles packaging mass and is what cuts damage-related returns by about 40% on heavy parts. The environmental gain is the avoided repack and the avoided return shipment, while the extra packaging mass on true heavy parts is accepted.
Service, quality, resilience: fewer repack delays and fewer damage returns. No route is removed, so resilience is unchanged. Q1 conformance should rise, and the present correlation between Q1 violations and return loops is the quality test.
Evidence: Rework packaging count, `package_material_kg` per order split by first pack versus repack, Q1 violation rate, damage-return rate, and share of repacks whose reason is route or geometry mismatch.

**S9. Split Critical-downtime into confirmed downtime and precautionary urgent.**
Target: the whole Critical segment (11%, median 15 h, p90 30 h, late 4%, 4.5 kWh, 38 kg, 0.20 kg scrap), which is composed of emergency → reinforced → dedicated van (8%, 14 h, 3% late, 7 kWh, 52 kg, 0.25 kg) and the stock/ground exception (3%, 23 h, 9% late, 1.2 kWh, 4.8 kg, 0.07 kg).
Mechanism: the segment average of 38 kg is mostly the van path. The stock/ground exception shows that some orders labeled critical already travel at 4.8 kg, with a visible service penalty (late 9% versus 3%, median 23 h versus 14 h). A two-class label, recorded as an event before Emergency production or dedicated van, separates orders that justify the van from orders that were upgraded preventively. Precautionary orders can then use priority stock, a short production release, and air or ground according to slack. Confirmed downtime keeps the emergency path.
Service, quality, resilience: confirmed downtime keeps the current 14 h / 3% late protection. The classification rule is biased toward the fast lane whenever evidence is ambiguous, because sending a true line-down order onto the stock/ground pattern is the unacceptable outcome. Precautionary orders accept a slower, lower-emission mode. Resilience improves if van capacity is no longer diluted by undocumented upgrades (SP2 is already missing on 31% of van cases).
Evidence: justification event present or absent, late rate and p90 inside each subclass, transport kgCO2e by subclass, and the rate at which precautionary orders are later reclassified during fulfillment.

**S8. Shared emergency courier lanes for the precautionary subclass, dedicated capacity retained for confirmed downtime.**
Target: dedicated-van shipments (620 in the DFG; path average 52 kgCO2e), specifically the precautionary subclass from S9 and the cases that today fail SP2.
Mechanism: dedicated vans protect a 14 h median and often return empty. A shared courier lane raises load factor on the precautionary volume. Confirmed downtime keeps a dedicated vehicle so its service level does not depend on a shared queue. This is not a substitution of the 52 kg path by the 4.8 kg stock/ground path. Shared courier remains an express mode; the emission cut is the empty-return and exclusivity premium on trips that do not need exclusivity. The size of that cut is not in the log and should be measured, not assumed.
Service, quality, resilience: confirmed-downtime late rate and p90 stay on the current van baseline. Precautionary service should stay inside what that subclass was promised, which is looser than line-down. Shared capacity can become contested in a disruption, so R1 is met by holding both the courier lane and a retained dedicated pool, and by pre-assigning the alternate on each Service-contract order.
Evidence: kgCO2e and empty-return indicator per van and per courier trip, on-time rate of confirmed downtime versus precautionary, courier saturation, and SP2 conformance.

**S11. Availability-check rules that cut unnecessary partial shipments.**
Target: Pick from stock → Partial shipment (1010), then either Package standard (790) or Backorder wait (220, of which 160 are picked later and 60 are cancelled). The aggregated path is 7% of orders, median 79 h, 24% late, 1.4 kWh, 7.2 kg, 0.08 kg scrap, against stock–ground at 34 h, 8% late, 3.6 kg.
Mechanism: at Availability check, if the missing lines will be pickable inside the slack of a Regular order, hold one shipment instead of launching a second logistics cycle. The transport gap (7.2 versus 3.6 kg) is the signature of that second cycle. For Service-contract and Critical orders, ship the lines that are available now when those lines are the ones keeping a customer running, and only then open a backorder for the rest. Early, explicit reschedule for Regular orders that cannot be completed replaces a late backorder and a possible cancel.
Service, quality, resilience: Regular customers should see fewer 79 h / 24% late cases. Service-contract customers keep partial shipment where a partial delivery has operational value. Quality is unchanged. Cancellation of the 60 backorder cases should fall if the promise is reset before the wait, while the 410 cancels already taken at Availability check remain the low-waste place to stop an infeasible Regular order.
Evidence: partial-shipment rate by segment, second-ship kgCO2e, backorder-wait duration, cancel rate split by before versus after production, and late rate of orders held for a single ship.

**S10. Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular Made-to-order only.**
Target: Made-to-order Regular (22%, median 94 h, p90 180 h, late 18%, 18.5 kWh, 8.0 kg, 0.90 kg scrap), especially orders that would otherwise follow immediate CNC → air (6%, 70 h, 9% late, 24 kWh, 23 kg) or be expedited off a batch wait.
Mechanism: offer a promise that has room for a CNC_A batch, a heat-treatment batch above size 10 where the 1.7× late factor still fits, and ground or consolidated transport. The immediate-air path spends both the higher energy (24 versus 16 kWh) and air-scale transport (23 versus 5.8 kg). A negotiated promise lets those orders use the efficient path without being scored late against an unachievable date. Service-contract and Critical promises are contractual and are not opened by this rule.
Service, quality, resilience: the customer-visible promise changes by agreement; adherence to the new promise is the service measure. Quality follows the CNC_A and inspection rules above. Resilience is unchanged. The behavioral risk, treated in section 5, is customers relabeling Regular orders as Critical to escape the negotiation.
Evidence: original versus accepted `promised_lead_time_hours`, mode and batch actually used on negotiated orders, late rate against the accepted promise, and the rate of case_type changes from Made-to-order Regular to Critical after the offer.

5. Rebound and displacement risks

**Batching displaced into expedited transport.** A CNC_A batch that saves 1.5 kWh/item, or a heat-treatment batch above size 10, can still lose on the fulfillment footprint if the extra wait (up to ~38 h, or the 1.7× late factor on heat treatment) sets `expedite_flag` and moves the order from ground at 5.8 kg onto air at 18.5–23 kg or a van at 52 kg. One expedited recovery shipment outweighs the machining-energy gain under any plausible grid factor, which is why the wait cap has to be a hard release, not a target.

**Forecast-fill obsolescence.** Padding a batch to size 12–20 with forecast demand spends the 1.3 kWh/item and the material up front. When the forecast is wrong, 6% of batch-produced items are unshipped at 14 days. That is energy and material already inside the boundary, plus later scrap or a distressed shipment. Pooling firm orders avoids this rebound; chasing the minimum kWh/item does not.

**Lighter packaging displaced into returns.** Dropping reinforced packaging halves `package_material_kg` and reverses the 40% reduction in damage-related returns on heavy Made-to-order parts. A returned heavy part re-enters production, inspection, packaging, and transport, which is the same structure as the rework path at 32 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap, and a second `transport_kgco2e`. Q1 violations already correlate with those loops. Extra packaging mass on parts over 8 kg is the lower-impact side of this trade.

**Mode shift displaced into a recovery shipment.** Moving a Standard-stock Service-contract order from air (18.5 kg, 3% late) to ground without enough slack can miss the promise and trigger a second, faster shipment. Two legs plus a complaint-driven expedite can exceed the original air leg. The same pattern appears at the consolidation cutoff: a twice-daily truck saves 35–55% only on freight that actually rides the truck; freight bumped to air at cutoff can erase the day’s saving. The partial path is the existing proof, at 7.2 kg versus 3.6 kg.

**Partial-shipment suppression displaced into false urgency.** If Availability check stops partials for everyone, Service-contract customers who needed the in-stock lines will escalate the order to Critical or call for a dedicated van. That moves volume from the 7.2 kg partial path toward the 52 kg van path. Holding Regular orders too long also feeds the backorder cancel route (60 of 220 backorder waits already cancel) after pick effort has been spent.

**CNC_A preference displaced into queue delay and lost surge capacity.** Routing Made-to-order away from CNC_B cuts a population whose rework rate is 18% rather than 9%. If the CNC_A queue exceeds due-date slack, Made-to-order Service-contract late delivery rises from an already high 13%, and planners will expedite. If emergency production can no longer find CNC_B free, confirmed downtime loses the 14 h path. The displacement shows up as queue time, `expedite_flag`, and emergency wait, not as CNC_A’s own kWh/item.

**Critical reclassification as customer behavior.** Promise negotiation and stricter van justification create an incentive to label Regular or precautionary orders as Critical-downtime. The transport index is dominated by the 11% Critical segment at 38 kg. A small leakage of Made-to-order Regular (8 kg, sometimes 23 kg on the air path) into the van path increases fleet emissions and crowds out true downtime. The stock/ground Critical exception, at 9% late and 23 h, is what customers are trying to avoid, so they will use the label that still unlocks the van.

**Shared courier saturation.** Moving precautionary volume onto a shared lane improves load factor at first. Once the lane is full, new precautionary orders charter ad-hoc vans, and confirmed downtime that was placed on the same lane misses the 30 h p90. Empty-return emissions then reappear under a different vehicle type. Retaining a dedicated pool for confirmed downtime is what keeps this from becoming a resilience failure.

**Inspection-gate queue.** A hold-point that is not staffed will lengthen the inspection_cell queue. The log already ties that queue to rushed checks, missed defects, packaging rework, and customer returns. Orders skipped “to recover the promise” recreate the 470-case rework exit. The rebound monitor is first-pass yield falling at the same time as inspection wait rising.

**Consolidation and recommender lock-in during disruption.** A ground and truck default that is excellent under SP1 on a normal day violates R1 if Service-contract orders have no pre-booked alternate when that carrier fails. Emissions then spike anyway, because the recovery mode will be air or van, and service fails at the same time. Alternate `route_mode` has to be stored when the promise is made.

6. Balanced recommendation

Adopt one slack-gated design with three lanes, plus a quality loop that sits on top of all three. The lanes use the same attributes at Availability check: `case_type`, `customer_type`, `promised_lead_time_hours`, stock position, and historical reliability of each `route_mode`. Isolated green rules will fight each other; batching, packaging, and mode have to be chosen from the same slack number.

**Lane A — stock, economy where the promise allows.** Standard-stock is 55% of orders and a small share of energy and scrap. Its lever is transport and extra shipments. For Regular stock with slack, ship on the twice-daily consolidated truck or parcel ground, and hold a partial only long enough to complete one shipment. For Service-contract stock, priority-pick first, then ground if the on-time probability stays high enough to protect today’s roughly 5% late rate; air if it does not. Record air as the disruption alternate. This uses S1, S4, S5, and S11. It does not put Service-contract stock onto a truck by default, and it does not spend the full SP1 headroom (on-time probability of 85%) as if 15% late were an acceptable Service-contract result.

**Lane B — Made-to-order, quality first and batch second.** This lane is about 87% of process energy and about 85% of scrap. Route slack-tolerant Regular work to CNC_A, pool firm orders only, and release at a wait cap well inside the observed 38 h upper end. Use a tighter cap for Made-to-order Service-contract, and keep CNC_B for that tighter work and for emergencies. Heat-treatment batches above size 10 are allowed only inside the tighter cap, because of the 1.7× late factor. Inspection holds high-rework combinations before pack. Parts over 8 kg take reinforced packaging. Regular customers may be offered a longer promise when that promise is what makes the efficient path real: the efficient path is 16 kWh, 5.8 kg, and 0.70 kg scrap, against 24 kWh and 23 kg on immediate CNC plus air, and against 32 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap, 160 h, and 32% late on the CNC_B rework path. Service-contract Made-to-order promises are not negotiated away. This uses S2, S3, S7, S6, and S10.

**Lane C — Critical, split before any vehicle is called.** Confirmed downtime keeps emergency production, reinforced pack, and a dedicated van or equivalent exclusive resource, aiming at the current 14 h median, 3–4% late, and 30 h p90. Precautionary urgent orders leave that path and use priority stock or a short production release plus the express mode their slack actually needs. Shared courier is introduced for precautionary volume only after the subclass exists, with a dedicated pool still sized for confirmed downtime and for R1 disruption cover. Missing SP2 justification is treated as a process defect on the van, not as proof that the underlying order was safe to slow down. The stock/ground Critical pattern (23 h, 9% late, 4.8 kg) is a legitimate outcome for some precautionary orders and an unacceptable default for confirmed downtime. This uses S9, S8, and S1.

**Quality loop on every lane.** Packaging is chosen from part weight, geometry, and the route already selected, so the 620 packaging-rework events shrink. Reinforced mass stays on heavy Made-to-order parts because the return it prevents is a second production and transport cycle. Inspection capacity is added at the high-rework combinations rather than as a blanket slowdown.

**Sequence.** Start with the compatibility check, the exception log, and Q1 enforcement, which remove waste without slowing promises. Next turn on the recommender for Standard-stock Service-contract air and the twice-daily truck for Regular stock that passes the slack test. Then change CNC routing and batch caps for Regular Made-to-order, with promise negotiation as an option rather than a silent change of due dates. Last, split the Critical label and only then move precautionary volume off exclusive vans. The largest transport pool is also the one where a mistake is a line-down event, so it changes after the classification evidence is real.

**Guardrails that stop the rollout.** Confirmed-downtime late rate and p90 stay at today’s Critical levels (about 4% and 30 h). Standard-stock Service-contract late rate stays near 5%. Made-to-order Service-contract is not pushed from 13% late toward the 21–32% of the long batch and rework paths. Every Service-contract order keeps a recorded alternate route. Van and courier overrides carry a justification event. Fourteen-day unshipped batch items and `expedite_flag` after batch wait are rollback triggers for the batch-size settings. Damage-return rate is a rollback trigger for any packaging change.

The package deliberately leaves emissions in place where they buy a required outcome: air for stock promises that ground cannot hold, reinforced packaging for heavy parts, CNC_B and dedicated vans for confirmed downtime, and partial shipment when a Service-contract line needs the available items now. The reductions sit in the documented waste: SP1 violations on Standard-stock Service-contract, empty exclusive vans without a downtime justification, CNC_B rework loops, forecast-padded batches, packaging that does not match the route, and second shipments created by the availability check.

7. Validation and governance plan

Validate on the event log after implementation, in the same segments and paths used above. A fleet-wide drop in average kgCO2e is not evidence, because a mix shift toward Standard-stock would produce it with no process change.

**Conformance.**
- SP1: for every shipped order, store the recommended mode, the probability that ground or consolidated truck would have met `promised_lead_time_hours`, and the actual `route_mode`. The current 22% violation rate on Standard-stock Service-contract is the baseline to bring down. Count overrides by reason.
- SP2: a dedicated-van case is conformant only when a preceding event records confirmed Critical-downtime and the reason. The current gap is 31% of dedicated-van cases. Report documentation failures separately from precautionary orders that still took a van.
- Q1: Made-to-order parts over 8 kg on standard pack without an inspection certificate are nonconformant. Track that rate next to damage returns and Rework packaging, which is the correlation already observed.
- R1: every Service-contract order has a non-null alternate `route_mode` at promise time. During any carrier-disruption window, report the share that actually had a usable alternate and their late rate.
- Because model precision is low on combined emergency and partial traces, score conformance on attributes (`case_type`, `customer_type`, `route_mode`, `expedite_flag`, justification event, pack type, weight), not only on DFG alignment.

**Segment service monitoring.** Keep a control chart, or at least a pre/post comparison with stable case-mix, for late rate, median, p90, and cancel-due-to-delay, for all five segments. Add path-level views for the eight variants in the prompt. Judge S10 only against the accepted promise, and publish the original promise beside it so a looser date cannot appear as a service improvement. Hold the guardrails in section 6 as stop conditions. Specific early-warning pairs: Critical p90 above 30 h, Standard-stock Service-contract late rate moving from 5% toward the stock–ground 8% or worse, Made-to-order batch-path late rate moving from 21% toward the rework path’s 32%, and partial-path late rate remaining near 24% after the availability rule changes.

**Environmental monitoring, same cuts.** Transport kgCO2e by `route_mode` and segment; energy kWh per item by `machine_group` and batch-size band; scrap and `package_material_kg` with and without `rework_flag`; count of Rework production → CNC loops; count of Rework packaging; second-shipment rate; 14-day unshipped rate on `batch_id`. Review the mix-weighted index only as a secondary rollup. Keep kWh, kilograms, and kgCO2e in separate columns.

**Rebound dashboard, reviewed with the same cadence.** Expedites opened after batch wait; air shipments created after a truck cutoff; orders whose `case_type` changes to Critical after promise negotiation or after a refused van; inspection queue time versus first-pass yield; CNC_A queue time versus emergency wait; courier saturation versus ad-hoc van charters; damage returns versus packaging mass. A green trend on one indicator with an adverse trend on its rebound pair is a failed intervention, even if average kgCO2e fell.

**Exception governance.** One sustainability exception log covers SP1, SP2, and Q1 overrides. Each entry has case id, segment, the rule overridden, the reason, the person or role, and the later outcome (on-time or late, rework, actual kgCO2e, actual kWh). Review overrides weekly by reason code. Reasons that dominate and show no better service outcome than the conformant path become candidates to close. Reasons that consistently protect Critical downtime or a Service-contract promise stay as standing, auditable exceptions. Shadow processes — expedites that bypass the recommender, vans booked outside the justification event — are visible as attribute patterns ( `expedite_flag` with no recommender event, `dedicated_van` with no justification) and are in scope for the same review.

**Rollout evidence.** Pilot Lane A on one region or one carrier pair before network-wide truck consolidation. Pilot CNC_A routing and batch caps on Regular Made-to-order only, with CNC_B still staffed for surge. Run the Critical subclass label in parallel with the old van policy until justification quality is high and the confirmed-downtime late rate has not moved; only then shift precautionary volume to shared courier. Compare each pilot to its own prior period and to a held-out slice of the same segment, so a seasonal mix change is not read as a process effect.

**Decision review.** After a volume sufficient to refill each segment (the baseline log is 12,000 orders; Standard-stock Service-contract is 16% and Critical is 11%, so small pilots will be noisy on those two), accept, adjust the cap, or roll back each lane against the joint test: transport and energy and scrap moved in the intended direction on the targeted path, late rate and p90 stayed inside the guardrail, rework and damage returns did not rise, obsolescence did not rise, and an alternate route was still present for Service-contract orders.
