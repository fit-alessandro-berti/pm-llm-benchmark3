# 1. Current-process diagnosis

The warranty process is not one process. It is a poorly segmented mix of an attractive fast path, a slower reviewed/repair path, and a rework-heavy missing-evidence loop. The most frequent fast path looks good on localized speed and pre-rework cost, but process-mining and outcome data show it often shifts cost and harm downstream.

Key problems:

- **The fast path is frequent and fast but not reliably good.** The DFG shows `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval` 2,550 times and `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement` 2,300 times. Local edge times are short: `Auto eligibility -> Fast approval` mean 1.0h/median 0.4h, `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement` mean 3.0h/median 1.8h, and `Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close` mean 5.0h/median 3.2h.
- **The contradiction is rework and compliance.** Fast approval cases are 31% cheaper before rework, but only 9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments, and service credits. `Customer complaint/reopen` is 9.2% overall but 14.1% for Fast approval versus only 3.3% for the `Repair quote -> Customer approval -> Repair device` path. Long-tail edges are severe: `Fast approval -> Customer complaint/reopen` mean 72h/p90 150h, and `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` mean 96h/p90 180h.
- **Conformance is materially violated.** Token-based replay gives log fitness 0.91 but only 68% fitting traces. Alignment diagnostics show move-on-log deviations for `Fast approval before Evidence completeness`, `Customer complaint/reopen after close`, and `Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices`. Low precision means many low-frequency variants combine auto approval, risk review, and repair in policy-disallowed ways.
- **Missing evidence is a major rework engine.** `Request missing evidence` occurs at least twice in 26% of cases that enter it. The DFG has `Auto eligibility -> Request missing evidence` 650, `Manual eligibility -> Request missing evidence` 780, `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility` 890, and `Request missing evidence -> Claim abandoned` 320. The unclear/missing-evidence segment is 22% of cases with median cycle time 140h and the highest abandonment.
- **The slow path is not uniformly better, but it is better for high-value and risk.** Premium high-value device fast path has 22% complaint/reopen versus 5% on reviewed/repair; Standard high-value device fast path has 18% versus 6%. Wrong replacement cost is EUR 95 for low-risk accessories but EUR 410 for high-value devices.
- **The fast path can still be right for some low-risk cases.** Premium low-risk accessory fast path median is 8h versus 46h manual, with complaint/reopen 6% versus 3%, but service-credit exposure is high if cycle time exceeds 24h. Standard low-risk accessory fast path is 13h versus 52h manual, with 8% versus 4% complaint/reopen, and these customers tolerate 72h if status visibility is good.
- **Resource constraints prevent simply routing more work to human review.** Manual eligibility review has 7 reviewers and queue grows sharply above 82% utilization. Fraud/risk review has 2 specialists and is a Monday/campaign bottleneck. Repair quote delay is mostly waiting for part-price confirmation. Quality check is underused except after repair campaigns. The escalation board is a daily 45-minute slot with good customer-satisfaction effect but poor scalability.
- **Compliance rules are explicit and currently at risk.** R1 requires above EUR 400 claims to pass Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review before Fast approval. R2 forbids replacement dispatch before evidence completeness except for Premium low-risk accessory claims. R3 requires any reopened claim to pass Manual eligibility review before final close or denial.

The diagnosis is therefore: stop treating the frequent fast path as the default. Use it for genuinely low-risk, low-value, well-evidenced or Premium-accessory cases; require risk/evidence gates for high-value, high-cost, and unclear cases; and redesign the fulfillment/repair loop because the worst downstream rework is often created after approval, not before it.

# 2. Redesign A: Risk-tiered lanes with hard policy gates

**Redesigned process flow**
- Intake enriches each claim with segment, device value, defect type, and risk score.
- The auto screen becomes segment-aware and routes into three lanes:
  - **Low-risk accessory fast lane:** Premium low-risk accessories can use the R2 exception and auto-approve/dispatch, with a 24h service-credit timer. Standard low-risk accessories can auto-approve if evidence is complete and no fraud signal exists; otherwise they go to the evidence lane, with status visibility supporting the 72h tolerance.
  - **High-value/high-risk review lane:** Any claim above EUR 400 or high-value device must pass Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review before Fast approval. Evidence completeness must be confirmed before dispatch.
  - **Exception/reopen lane:** Any reopened claim is forced through Manual eligibility review before final close or denial, satisfying R3.
- Hard system gates enforce R1, R2, and R3 instead of relying on local workarounds.

**Targets**
- High-value devices: 14% Premium high-value and 21% Standard high-value.
- Claims above EUR 400.
- Fast-approval deviations: `Fast approval before Evidence completeness` and `Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices`.
- Preserve speed for low-risk accessories, especially Premium low-risk where the 24h service-credit threshold matters.

**Mechanism of improvement**
- The fast path is deliberately narrowed to cases where its complaint/reopen rate and wrong-replacement cost are acceptable.
- High-value cases move to the reviewed/repair path, which already shows much lower complaint/reopen: 5% for Premium high-value and 6% for Standard high-value versus 22% and 18% on the fast path.
- Hard gates reduce conformance deviations, improve fitting-trace percentage, and prevent R1/R2 violations before they create downstream complaints.
- Manual and fraud resources are focused on high-risk cases rather than broadly slowing every claim.

**What it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved**
- It sacrifices fast replacement speed for many high-value customers. The reviewed/repair path may be 96h to 120h or longer.
- It may increase manual-review inflow by 15–35% if auto-screen rules are updated aggressively, risking the 82% utilization threshold.
- It does not fully solve missing-evidence loops, low-digital-literacy abandonment, or wrong replacement selection once a high-value replacement is correctly approved.

# 3. Redesign B: Evidence-first concierge and no-loop routing

**Redesigned process flow**
- At intake, the process starts an adaptive evidence checklist based on defect type, device value, and segment.
- Customers use a new evidence portal for uploads, status tracking, and reminders.
- If evidence is still missing after one request, or if the customer is flagged as low digital literacy or high abandonment risk, the case routes to a human **evidence concierge** rather than bouncing back to Manual eligibility review.
- Once evidence is complete, the case routes to auto-approval, Manual eligibility review, or Fraud/risk review using risk rules.
- Premium low-risk accessories can still use the R2 exception, but with post-hoc evidence and a 24h clock. Standard low-risk accessories can wait up to 72h if status visibility is good.

**Targets**
- Unclear defect/missing evidence: 22% of cases with median cycle time 140h and highest abandonment.
- Repeated evidence requests: 26% of cases entering `Request missing evidence`.
- The loop `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review -> Request missing evidence`.
- Standard low-risk accessories where 72h tolerance is acceptable if visibility is strong.
- Premium cases where evidence delays trigger service-credit exposure.

**Mechanism of improvement**
- Front-loading evidence completeness reduces downstream rework and removes repeated handoffs.
- The portal is expected to reduce repeated evidence requests by 30–45%, but the concierge is essential to prevent abandonment for low-digital-literacy customers.
- Manual reviewers receive more complete cases, reducing the `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` mean of 34h/p90 88h and the `Manual eligibility -> Request missing evidence` mean of 14h/p90 36h.
- R2 compliance improves because dispatch is gated on evidence completeness.

**What it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved**
- It initially slows missing-evidence cases because evidence is completed before approval.
- It adds portal and concierge operating cost.
- If the concierge is understaffed, portal-induced abandonment may rise.
- It does not fix high-value risk approval or wrong replacement selection by itself.

# 4. Redesign C: Repair-first high-value track with pre-dispatch quality gate

**Redesigned process flow**
- High-value devices enter a diagnostic/repair-first track unless the device is irreparable, total loss, or the customer chooses replacement with transparent cost/quality trade-offs.
- Repair quote part-price confirmation runs in parallel with technician diagnosis where possible, reducing the current `Manual eligibility -> Repair quote` delay and the repair quote wait.
- For any high-value replacement, a lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate checks part compatibility, photos, barcode, and prior defect history. This adds 6–18h but targets wrong replacement selection.
- Repaired high-value devices go through a closed-loop quality check with rework tracking and 30-day follow-up.
- The escalation board is reserved for Premium high-value exceptions, not routine complaints.

**Targets**
- High-value devices: 14% Premium high-value and 21% Standard high-value.
- `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` long-tail pattern linked to incorrect replacement part selection.
- Rework repair: 12.5% of repaired high-value devices versus 4.0% of repaired low-risk devices.
- Wrong replacement cost: EUR 410 for high-value devices.

**Mechanism of improvement**
- The repair quote path has only 3.3% complaint/reopen versus 14.1% for Fast approval, so shifting appropriate high-value cases toward repair/diagnosis improves quality.
- The pre-dispatch gate catches wrong-part selections before they create 72–96h complaint/reopen tails.
- Quality inspectors are currently underused except after repair campaigns, so this redesign uses latent capacity instead of creating a new permanent queue.
- Better first-time-right repair reduces reshipments, service credits, and repeated repair loops.

**What it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved**
- It sacrifices replacement speed: the gate adds 6–18h, and repair-first can lengthen high-value cycle time.
- It increases load on technicians and the repair quote process, which already waits on part-price confirmation.
- Customer approval already averages 30h/p90 80h, so repair-first may frustrate customers who expect replacement.
- It does not solve low-risk accessory speed or the missing-evidence loop.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

## Redesign A

- **Failure mode 1:** Updated auto-screen rules increase manual-review inflow by 15–35%, pushing Manual eligibility review above 82% utilization and creating long queues for high-value and Premium cases.
- **Failure mode 2:** Misclassified or missing segment attributes let high-value claims enter the low-risk fast lane, so R1/R2 violations persist despite the new gates.
- **Likely bottleneck migration:** The bottleneck moves from auto approval/dispatch to Manual eligibility review and Fraud/risk review, especially on Mondays or after campaigns. If high-value cases then go to repair, the next bottleneck becomes repair quote part-price confirmation.

## Redesign B

- **Failure mode 1:** The evidence portal increases abandonment for low-digital-literacy customers, and the concierge team is too small to absorb the exceptions.
- **Failure mode 2:** Evidence-first handling slows Premium low-risk accessories beyond the 24h service-credit threshold, increasing credits even if evidence quality improves.
- **Likely bottleneck migration:** The bottleneck moves to the evidence concierge/support queue. Once evidence is complete, manual review may receive bursts of cases, temporarily shifting pressure back to Manual eligibility review.

## Redesign C

- **Failure mode 1:** The pre-dispatch quality gate adds 6–18h and causes Premium high-value customers to complain about replacement delay, even if wrong-part errors fall.
- **Failure mode 2:** Repair-first overloads technicians and part-price confirmation, worsening the existing `Repair quote -> Customer approval` wait of mean 30h/p90 80h.
- **Likely bottleneck migration:** The bottleneck moves to repair quote/technician capacity and the pre-dispatch quality gate. If the gate is centralized, Quality check inspectors may become the new constrained resource.

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**First pilot: Redesign B on unclear/missing evidence**
- Why first: Missing evidence is cross-cutting, affects 22% of cases, drives 140h median cycle time, repeated evidence requests in 26% of cases entering the loop, and 320 abandoned cases after `Request missing evidence`.
- Pilot design: Randomize unclear/missing-evidence cases into portal + concierge versus current process. Stratify by Standard/Premium and low-risk/high-value.
- Metrics: repeated evidence requests, time from first missing evidence to complete, abandonment, end-to-end median/p90 cycle time, 30/60/90-day complaint/reopen, R2 conformance, manual review queue utilization, service credits, portal abandonment.
- Stop/go: If repeated requests fall by roughly 30–45% and abandonment does not rise by more than about 2 percentage points, expand. If abandonment rises by more than 3 percentage points or evidence-concierge utilization exceeds sustainable capacity, stop and redesign the concierge/portal mix.

**Second pilot: Redesign A on high-value and >EUR 400 claims**
- Why second: Evidence completeness from the first pilot makes risk gates cleaner. High-value fast-path complaints are 22% Premium and 18% Standard, and R1/R2 deviations are explicit.
- Pilot design: Stepped-wedge by region or randomized by case within high-value segments. Compare risk-tiered lanes with hard gates versus current routing.
- Metrics: R1/R2/R3 violations, fitting traces, move-on-log deviations, Premium high-value complaint/reopen, Standard high-value complaint/reopen, Manual/Fraud utilization, cycle time, service credits.
- Stop/go: If compliance violations drop by more than half and high-value complaint/reopen does not worsen, continue. If Manual or Fraud utilization exceeds 82% or Premium high-value cycle time exceeds acceptable transparent-service thresholds, add capacity or narrow the high-value gate criteria.

**Third pilot: Redesign C on high-value replacements and repaired high-value devices**
- Why third: It addresses the long-tail fulfillment failures after routing has been improved. It also uses currently underused Quality check capacity, but must be tested before scaling because it adds 6–18h.
- Pilot design: Compare pre-dispatch quality gate + repair-first diagnostic track versus current high-value replacement/repair flow. Focus on high-value devices and wrong-part-prone replacements.
- Metrics: wrong replacement rate, `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen`, rework repair for high-value devices, cost per case including reshipment/service credits, cycle time, technician utilization, repair quote queue, Quality check utilization.
- Stop/go: If wrong replacement/complaints fall by at least 20% with added cycle time no more than about 18h and no technician/quality backlog beyond capacity, expand. If repair quote queue grows sharply or Premium high-value replacement SLAs breach repeatedly, adjust the gate or add repair capacity.

**Sequencing logic**
- Start with Redesign B because evidence completeness is a prerequisite for clean risk decisions and reduces rework across all later redesigns.
- Then test Redesign A because it protects high-value compliance and stops the most damaging fast-path deviations.
- Then test Redesign C because it fixes the remaining long-tail fulfillment and repair-quality failures after intake and routing are cleaner.

**How to learn whether improvements are real rather than shifted downstream**
- Measure end-to-end cost including rework, reshipments, service credits, complaint handling, and abandoned claims, not just pre-rework cost.
- Track 30/60/90-day complaint/reopen, not just time-to-close.
- Use process-mining conformance metrics: fitting traces, move-on-log deviations, precision, and the specific deviations `Fast approval before Evidence completeness`, `Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value`, and `Customer complaint/reopen after close`.
- Monitor bottleneck migration: Manual eligibility, Fraud/risk, evidence concierge, repair quote, Quality check, and escalation board. If a queue exceeds sustainable utilization or p90 wait grows, the redesign may be moving cost rather than removing it.
- Keep segment-level reporting: Premium low-risk, Standard low-risk, Premium high-value, Standard high-value, and unclear/missing evidence. Aggregate averages would hide the very trade-offs that matter.
- Treat cost reductions driven mainly by abandonment or denials as suspect until customer outcomes and compliance are checked.
