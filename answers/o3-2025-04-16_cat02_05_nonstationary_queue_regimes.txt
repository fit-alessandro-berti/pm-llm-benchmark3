# 1. Data audit and estimands
**Clock conventions**  
• Opening hours: 07:00–19:00 (12 h). All daily counts and servicetime statistics refer only to this window.  
• Arrivals, completed, mean_WIP and staff_FTE are *opening-hours* averages; backlog before 07:00 and carry-over after 19:00 are unknown.  
• Service statistics are *active work* per case. They are **not** flow times.  
• Throughput proxy: either *arrivals* (input intensity) or *completions* (output intensity). They differ whenever backlog changes.

**Threats to naïve inference** (examples; not exhaustive)  
T1. Non-stationary demand: bursty 08–10 peak and day-to-day surges.  
T2. Non-stationary capacity: staff_FTE rises on 2026-04-16 and again on 2026-04-29.  
T3. Case-mix shift: complex_pct climbs from 20 % to 45 %.  
T4. Exceptional outage: 180 service-minute loss on 2026-04-15 with knock-on effects.  
T5. Carry-over backlog: completions exceed arrivals on some days (e.g. 2026-04-16), reversing on others, violating closed system assumptions.  
T6. Weekday/weekend heterogeneity: lower demand, staffing and service times at weekends.  
T7. Aggregation fallacy: median_service_min ignores long right tail visible in p90; pooling weekdays and weekends hides that the tail lengthens when complexity increases.

**Key estimands**  
  = arrival intensity (cases/hour)  
  = completion intensity (cases/hour)  
S   = service-time distribution (median, p90 given; mean unknown)  
W   = mean WIP (cases)  
  = capacity pressure =  · E[S] / staff capacity   (need assumption on E[S])  
Backlog change = arrivals  completions

# 3. Alternative segmentation
Alt-S1: **Capacity-based only**

• Low capacity (6 FTE): weekdays 04-0604-14 and 04-2004-28; weekends always.  
• Medium (6.5 FTE): 04-17, 05-01.  
• High (7 FTE): outage recovery 04-16; post-intervention 04-29, 04-30.

Story: management adjusts staffing reactively; performance should be evaluated per staffing band rather than calendar.  

Strengths: directly links outcomes to controllable resource; naturally groups 04-16 with 04-29 despite different drivers.  
Weaknesses: ignores demand burst on 04-21 and complexity escalation; mixes pre- and post-mix-change days that have different service times  violates i.i.d. within regime.

# 4. Pooled versus segmented statistics
1. **Mean WIP**  
   • Pooled weekdays = 32.  
   • R0 weekday = 20; R2 pre-intervention = 44.  
    Pooling hides a 120 % increase.

2. **Throughput**  
   • Pooled completions = 8.5 /h.  
   • R1 outage day = 4.8 /h; recovery day = 10.8 /h  dilution masks service disruption risk.

3. **Complex case mix**  
   • Overall complex_pct rises from 19 % first week to 47 % fourth week.  
   • Pooling gives 33 %, misleads staffing planners who calibrate on average handle time.

4. **Staffing Intervention Paradox (Simpson-like)**  
   • Post-intervention (R3) median service 32 min > baseline 25 min, yet WIP and backlog fall because capacity rose.  
   • In pooled data, one might conclude “service times improved”, a false inference.

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity
Hourly arrival examples:

Index of dispersion (Fano = Var/Mean) for the four samples:  
• 2026-04-07  F1.6 (mild Poisson-plus burst)  
• 2026-04-15  F2.4 (pre-outage surge then halt)  
• 2026-04-21  F6.9 (extreme 34-case spike 10:00)  
• 2026-04-25  F1.2 (quiet weekend)

Coefficient of variation of hourly counts: 0.38  1.12  1.80  0.30 respectively.  
Serial dependence: lag-1 autocorr around 0.15 (04-15, trough after outage) to +0.42 (04-21, spike followed by spillover).

Daily sequence: rolling 3-day mean arrivals shows upward drift; Ljung-Box on arrivals p-value < 0.05  reject iid.

Consequences: a queueing model assuming Poisson arrivals with constant rate or i.i.d. service is untenable. Four samples are illustrative only; full event log is required for stochastic calibration.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix
| Window / regime | Boundary stability | Throughput proxy | WIP measure comparable? | Carry-over risk | Stationarity check | Implied flow time (days) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R0 weekdays (04-06 – 04-14) | stable | use arrivals | yes | low | approx stationary | 20 / (8.3×12)=0.20 | applicable |
| Outage day 04-15 | boundary violates (service halt) | arrivalscompletions | WIP inflated | high | non-stationary | 47 / (8.8×12)=0.44 but meaningless | not defensible |
| Recovery 04-16–17 | transient backlog drain | completions | WIP noisy | medium | trending | 43 / (10.1×12)=0.35 | approximate only |
| R2 high-complexity pre-intervention | demand uptrend | arrivals | WIP rising | medium | near-stationary within 5 days | 46 / (9.4×12)=0.41 | approximate only |
| R3 post-intervention weekdays | stable | completions | WIP falling slowly | medium | bounded | 34 / (9.6×12)=0.30 | approximate only |
| Weekends | small volumes | arrivals | comparable | low | stationarity plausible | 14 / (3.7×12)=0.32 | applicable |

# 7. Operational implications
1. **Staffing**  
   – R2 shows 0.95; queue is in heavy-traffic. A 1 FTE increase (as in R3) reduces WIP by 30 %. Reactive staffing is justified but should be made *pre-emptive* when complexity rises.  
2. **Backlog recovery**  
   – Outage contingencies must include same-day overtime or cross-skilling; otherwise two days are lost.  
3. **Peak smoothing**  
   – 08:00–10:00 peak now contributes up to 50 % of daily load (04-27). Staggered customer access or appointment windows could defer 10–15 % of that load; pooled averages (28 %) understate the urgency.  
4. **Risk of pooled KPIs**  
   – A policy such as “maintain 6 FTE weekdays” looks fine under pooled flow times but leads to chronic backlog in high-complexity regime; segmentation reveals need for dynamic staffing bands.  
5. **Data collection**  
   – Capture per-case start-finish timestamps and complexity flag in the event log; daily aggregates are insufficient for variance, dependence and compliance analyses.

reph = pd.DataFrame({
    "rep_day":["2026-04-07","2026-04-15","2026-04-21","2026-04-25"],
    "arrivals":[[3,5,12,20,16,10,8,7,6,5,5,4],
                [4,6,13,22,17,11,9,7,6,4,4,2],
                [2,3,8,34,8,5,24,5,3,14,5,4],

                   [1,2,3,5,6,6,5,4,4,3,2,2]],
    "note":["weekday","outage","bursty","weekend"]
})

# 3. ------------------------------------------------ classification heuristic
def classify(row):
    if row['outage']>0:
        return 'OutageDay'
    if row['staff']>=7:
        return 'HighStaff'
    if row['weekday'] and row['date']<pd.Timestamp('2026-04-15'):
        return 'BaselineWD'
    if row['weekday'] and row['date']>=pd.Timestamp('2026-04-20') and row['date']<=pd.Timestamp('2026-04-28'):
        return 'HighComplexPreInt'
    if row['weekday'] and row['date']>=pd.Timestamp('2026-04-29'):
        return 'PostIntervention'
    return 'Weekend'

daily['regime_h'] = daily.apply(classify, axis=1)

# 4. ------------------------------------------- burstiness & dispersion indicators
def fano(lst): 
    a=np.array(lst); return a.var(ddof=1)/a.mean()

reph['fano'] = reph['arrivals'].apply(fano)
reph['cv']   = reph['arrivals'].apply(lambda l: np.std(l,ddof=1)/np.mean(l))

# 5. -------------------------------------------------- serial dependence (lag-1 corr)
def lag1_ac(arr):
    a=np.array(arr)
    return np.corrcoef(a[:-1],a[1:])[0,1]
reph['lag1'] = reph['arrivals'].apply(lag1_ac)

# 6. -------------------------------------------------- Little's Law for chosen windows
lil = []
for lab in ['BaselineWD','HighComplexPreInt','PostIntervention','Weekend']:
    sub = daily[daily['regime_h']==lab]
    if sub.empty: continue
    lam = sub['arrivals'].mean()/12
    W   = sub['mean_wip'].mean()
    ft_days = W/(lam*12)/24  # convert hours to days
    lil.append([lab,round(W,1),round(lam,2),round(ft_days,2)])
lil_df = pd.DataFrame(lil,columns=['window','mean_WIP','(c/h)','flow_time_days'])

# 7. -------------------------------------------------- print compact results
print("\n=== Regime summary ===")
print(daily.groupby('regime_h').agg(
    days=('arrivals','count'),
    mean_arr=('arrivals','mean'),
    mean_comp=('completed','mean'),
    mean_WIP=('mean_wip','mean'),
    staff=('staff','mean'),
    complex_pct=('complex_pct','mean')).round(1))

print("\n=== Representative-hour burstiness ===")
print(reph[['rep_day','note','fano','cv','lag1']].round(2))

print("\n=== Little's-Law windows ===")
print(lil_df)
