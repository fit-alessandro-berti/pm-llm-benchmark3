## 1. Model scope and state variables

**Purpose and boundary.** The twin is a calibrated, stochastic discrete-event simulation (DES) used to compare interventions, not to replay history. It ranks policies under stated assumptions; it does not produce an "optimal" policy.

- **In scope:** everything from portal submission to the final decision (Issue permit / Reject application), including completeness loops, parallel reviews, public notice, inspection, drafting, legal check, rework after Planning review, Inspection and Legal check, and appeal-triggered re-entry.
- **Upstream extension:** a small pre-submission module (document checker for D1, appointment slots for D7), because these interventions move delay and effort to the applicant side and that must stay visible.
- **Exogenous actors, modelled as delay/behaviour processes rather than as queues:** applicants, architects, external consultants, objecting neighbours, and the council (as a calendar).
- **Out of scope:** construction-phase inspections, enforcement, and emergency decisions. Emergency decisions were removed from the log, so they appear only as an uncertain deduction from capacity.

**Level of detail.**

| Aspect | Choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Control flow | Activity level, following the process tree as a skeleton, with data-aware rule overrides | The tree lacks planning, legal and appeal rework and the "wait for all results" rule |
| Resources | Individual units grouped into skill pools, each with its own calendar | Part-time pools, specialised planners, a seasonal inspector |
| Time | Continuous, with calendars resolved to the hour | Monday bursts and Tue–Thu pools need sub-day resolution |
| Inspection | Day-route sub-model at district level (travel matrix, visits per trip) | D3 and D4 act on travel and batching; street-level routing is not supported by the data |
| External behaviour | Stochastic response models with architect-level random effects | Applicant time dominates several variants |
| Objections | Latent shared-shock process per notice cluster | Objections are not observed in the log |

**Entities.** Application (case); architect; application bundle (related cases of one architect); neighbourhood notice cluster (district × time window); work item (activity instance); inspection trip; resource unit; calendar events (public holiday, council-meeting week); latent objection.

**State variables.**

| Level | State variables |
|---|---|
| Case | Case type, district, architect, application value, requirement flags (fire, environment, notice), latent attributes (floor area over threshold, environmentally sensitive site, prior violations), current position(s) in the model, outstanding results in the AND-join, counters of completeness, planning, inspection and legal loops, age, separate municipal and applicant clocks, bundle and notice-cluster membership, appeal status, priority class |
| Resource unit | Idle / busy / off-shift / absent, current work item, skills, calendar, next-free time, last district visited (inspectors), cumulative busy and travel hours |
| Queue | Content and ages per pool, split by case type and district; discipline; open batches and their release conditions |
| External wait states | Cases waiting for applicant documents, consultant reports, notice expiry, objection handling, applicant readiness for inspection, appeal decisions |
| Cross-case | Active bursts per architect, open inspection bundles per architect × district, objection status per notice cluster, council calendar position |
| System | Simulation clock, calendar regime (peak month, council week, holiday), demand regime, active policy switches, WIP by stage |

**Resources and calendars.**

| Pool | Capacity and calendar | Modelling notes |
|---|---|---|
| Clerks | 5 FTE, Mon–Fri 08:00–16:00; one on portal support on Mondays | Completeness check and Request missing documents; effective capacity of 4 on the busiest day |
| Planners | 8 FTE, Mon–Fri 09:00–17:00; 2 restricted to Simple renovation | Planning review and Decision drafting; the same planner tends to do both for a case (P001: planner_3, P002: planner_1), so case stickiness is modelled |
| Fire reviewers | 2 FTE equivalent, Tue–Thu | Work arriving Thu afternoon to Monday accumulates |
| Environmental reviewers | 3 FTE, Mon–Fri, 60% available for permits | Modelled as availability factor or stochastic interruptions; consultant reports are an external delay |
| Scheduler(s) | Small pool (scheduler_1 in the excerpt) | Holds the batching logic |
| Inspectors | 4 FTE, field visits Tue–Fri; optional temporary unit in peak months | Service plus district-dependent travel; no-shows; day routes |
| Legal officers | 2 FTE, closed in council-meeting weeks except urgent cases | Urgent bypass rule with its own (uncertain) definition |
| System / portal | Unlimited | Issue permit, notice timer, portal events |

Public-notice time runs on calendar days with a 14-day statutory minimum. Holiday and council-meeting effects on notice expiry and objection handling are separate calendar rules.

**Queues.** One queue per pool, with the as-is discipline estimated from the log (FIFO as default, tested against observed overtaking). Additional constructs: a priority lane for aging cases (D6), per-district inspection queues with batch release (D3), and a synchronisation buffer before Decision drafting. Time in that buffer is waiting for the slowest applicable result, not waiting for a planner, and is reported separately.

**Cross-case dependencies explicitly modelled.**

1. **Shared resources:** all cases compete in the same calendar-constrained queues, so a Monday burst or a council week propagates to unrelated cases.
2. **Architect bursts:** clustered arrivals of related applications, with correlated document quality and response speed per architect.
3. **Inspection bundling:** cases of the same architect and district, or of the same district, share a trip. Travel per case falls; the first case in the bundle waits longer.
4. **Neighbourhood objections:** one latent objection event extends the notice period of all cases in the same notice cluster.
5. **Legal–environmental interaction:** Commercial cases need both scarce pools, and council weeks release legal work in waves.
6. **Council calendar:** Complex variance decisions and appeals are gated in batches, which creates downstream bursts for planners and legal officers.

## 2. Parameter-estimation plan

**PM4Py for log and model handling.** PM4Py prepares and describes logs and models; it is not the simulation engine. Its play-out has no resources, calendars or cross-case logic.

- Load CSV with pandas, convert timestamps, then call `pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id='case:concept:name', activity_key='concept:name', timestamp_key='time:timestamp')`.
- Cut calibration and hold-out windows with `pm4py.filter_time_range`. Keep incomplete cases as right-censored observations rather than dropping them, because dropping them biases long durations downward.
- Describe control flow and timing with `pm4py.get_variants`, `pm4py.discover_dfg`, `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg`, `pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity` and `pm4py.discover_temporal_profile`.
- Parse the given tree with `pm4py.parse_process_tree`, convert it with `pm4py.convert_to_petri_net`, and measure how well it explains the log with `pm4py.fitness_token_based_replay` and `pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments`. Compare with trees discovered per case type by `pm4py.discover_process_tree_inductive`.
- Use `pm4py.discover_organizational_roles` and `pm4py.discover_batches` as starting points for pools and batching evidence.
- Run every simulated log through the same functions, so real and simulated behaviour are compared like for like.

**Limitations of the simplified tree.** `'Completeness rework'` and `'Inspection rework'` are model labels that never occur as log activities (in P001 the second Completeness check follows Receive missing documents directly), so they are treated as silent redo markers. The tree has no planning, legal or appeal rework, makes inspection mandatory (D2 changes that), and places Public notice strictly after the reviews. It carries no data conditions, time or resources. Alignments on the full log determine which deviations become explicit simulation rules and which go into the "rare variants" residual (7%).

**Arrivals.**

- Fit a non-homogeneous Poisson process on hourly/daily counts with a log-linear model: month effect, weekday effect, hour-of-day profile, holiday effect and a trend term. Test for overdispersion and move to negative-binomial counts if needed.
- Split demand into two streams. Ordinary applicants follow the NHPP. The top-20 architects (31% of cases) follow a cluster process: burst start times, burst size distribution, spread within the week, and shared district.
- Sample case attributes jointly (case type × district × architect × value × flags) by conditional resampling from the log, not from independent marginals.
- `pm4py.get_case_arrival_average` gives one stationary average and is used only as a sanity check (31,200 cases in 18 months is about 57 per calendar day, about 400 per week).
- With 18 months, each season is seen once or twice, so seasonality and trend are partly confounded. The seasonal factors therefore carry wide uncertainty intervals.

**Service times.**

- Where start and completion timestamps exist: service = completion − start, measured in working time of the resource's calendar. Fit per activity × case type (and district for inspection) and compare lognormal, gamma, Weibull and mixture fits by AIC and tail diagnostics.
- Where only completion exists: estimate the start as the later of the enablement time and the resource's previous completion, inside the calendar. This gives bounds, not observations. The two-quantile summaries (median, p90) serve as priors. The final split is fixed by simulation-based calibration (matching simulated to observed sojourn and cycle-time distributions).
- Each event carries a timing-quality flag (observed / inferred / sojourn only). Results are reported with a sensitivity band over the inferred split.
- **Capacity reconciliation.** Taken at face value, the summary does not close. At about 400 cases per week, Planning review plus Decision drafting at their medians (3.5 h + 2 h) imply about 2,200 planner hours per week against 8 × 40 = 320 staffed hours. Site inspection implies about 400 h plus travel against 4 × 4 × 8 = 128 h. Completeness checks imply about 230 h against under 200 h. So the reported "service times" are probably elapsed activity spans that include interruptions and multitasking, or the staffing list is incomplete. This must be resolved with the municipality (timesheets, a short time-and-motion sample) before any experiment; until then, per-pool effective-effort factors are calibration parameters.

**Waiting times.** Waiting is an output of the twin, not an input. Observed waits are calibration and validation targets. Each observed wait is decomposed into: resource queueing, off-shift calendar time, synchronisation at the AND-join, batching delay, external response time, and statutory minimum. Enablement times come from the model, not from the previous event in the trace: inside the review block the enabler is the last Completeness check.

**Branching probabilities.**

- Rule-based branches use the flags directly (requires_fire, requires_environment, requires_public_notice).
- Hidden rule inputs (floor-area threshold, environmental sensitivity) become residual probabilities: P(review | flag = no, case type, value, district), estimated from cases that received the review without the flag.
- Missing documents, legal check, and permit/reject are fitted with logistic models or decision trees on case type, value, district, architect history and weekday. Sparse cells (for example Complex variance × district) use hierarchical shrinkage.

**Rework.**

- Loop continuation probabilities are estimated per iteration (first, second, third pass), not as one geometric probability, since repeat probability usually falls with each pass.
- Re-entry points come from alignments and DFG back-edges. Rework service times are estimated separately from first-pass times.
- Unstructured rework reasons are coded into a few coarse classes on a manually audited sample. The coding error is carried as uncertainty; reasons are not used as if reliably observed.
- Appeals: probability by case type and outcome, delay distribution, and re-entry activity, tied to the council calendar.

**Resource calendars.** Start from the HR calendars, then reconcile with event timestamps: hour-of-week activity profiles per resource, gaps indicating leave or absence, and observed work outside nominal hours. Estimate effective availability per pool (leave, sickness, non-permit duties, removed emergency cases). Council weeks and holidays come from the official calendars. Because calendars come from HR and not from the log, availability gets an uncertainty range, not a point value.

**Dependencies.**

- *Architect effects:* burst structure from per-architect inter-arrival times; correlation of missing-document rates and response times within architect via mixed models.
- *Inspection batching and travel:* consecutive Site inspection completions by the same inspector on the same day give visits per trip and same-district sequences; gaps between them, by district pair, give travel estimates. D3 and D5 parameters are estimated separately.
- *Objections (latent):* fit a two-component mixture to Public-notice duration (near the 14-day minimum versus extended). Test whether extended durations cluster by district and week. The objection probability and extension are posterior distributions, anchored where possible by a manual sample from the objection register.
- *Queue interactions:* cross-correlation of legal and environmental backlogs for Commercial cases.
- *Flattening bias:* rebuild an object-centric view (application, architect, inspection trip, notice cluster) from attributes and timestamps to recover relations the case-flattened log hides.

## 3. Assumptions

**Arrivals**
- Arrivals follow an NHPP plus an architect cluster process; the intensity is piecewise constant by hour.
- Demand is exogenous to municipal performance (no applicants deterred by long queues), except where an intervention explicitly changes submission behaviour (D1, D7).
- Case mix is stable within a season unless a scenario changes it.

**Service times**
- Service time depends on activity, case type, and (for inspection) district; it is independent of queue length. This is tested, because staff may speed up under pressure.
- Tasks are non-preemptive, pause outside working hours, and resume with the same resource.
- Where start timestamps are missing, the service/waiting split is inferred and uncertain; durations are not collapsed into one number without that label.
- Reported service spans are not pure effort until the capacity reconciliation is done.

**Routing**
- The process tree is the skeleton; documented rules override it.
- Decision drafting starts only when all applicable results (Planning, Fire, Environmental, Public notice, Inspection) are in.
- Rare variants (7%) are reproduced as an aggregated exception path with empirical duration, not modelled mechanistically.
- Interventions change only the routing they target (D2 removes inspection for eligible cases; D5 adds a parallel legal pre-check).

**Resources/calendars**
- HR calendars are the baseline; effective availability is lower by a fitted factor.
- Environmental reviewers have 60% of time for permits; legal officers handle only urgent cases in council weeks.
- Baseline queue discipline is FIFO within pool unless the log shows systematic prioritisation.
- No overtime or cross-pool substitution unless a scenario specifies it.

**Rework**
- Rework probability depends on case type, loop iteration and architect, not on the resource who did the first pass (tested).
- Rework returns to the activity that triggered it and repeats downstream steps only where rules require.
- A maximum number of loops applies, after which the case follows the rejection or withdrawal path at an empirical rate.

**Applicant/third-party response**
- Applicant, consultant and objector times are separate heavy-tailed processes on their own clocks, unaffected by municipal capacity.
- Response speed and document quality are correlated within architect.
- Site no-shows and applicant readiness delay inspections independently of inspector capacity.
- D1 shifts effort to before submission; that time counts in applicant-experienced duration.

**Cross-case dependencies**
- Cases interact through shared queues, architect bursts, inspection bundles and notice clusters; outside these mechanisms they are conditionally independent.
- Bundles form only within architect × district (or district, for D3) and within a time window.
- One objection event affects all cases in its notice cluster with the same extension, plus case-level noise.

**Non-stationary demand**
- The seasonal pattern (1.7× in March–May and September–November relative to January–February) and weekday pattern (Monday 2.2× Wednesday) repeat each year; levels for the remaining months and weekdays are estimated, not assumed.
- There is no steady state; results are reported by calendar period and arrival cohort.
- Trend and structural breaks are handled by scenarios, not extrapolated silently.

**Missing data**
- Objections are latent. Public-notice duration is a proxy used to infer a distribution over objection occurrence; it is never recoded as an observed objection event.
- Missing start timestamps, hidden rule attributes (floor area, sensitivity, prior violations) and removed emergency cases are treated as uncertain parameters with ranges.
- The flattened log understates cross-case links, so estimated dependency strengths are lower bounds and are varied upward in sensitivity runs.
- Intervention effect sizes are not in the log. They come from pilots, comparable municipalities or expert elicitation, and are always varied over a range.

## 5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty treatment

**Warm-up.** Demand is seasonal, so there is no steady state and no single truncation point in the classical sense.
- Primary approach: start each run from a realistic state, namely the WIP snapshot reconstructed from the log at a chosen date (cases, positions, ages), followed by 6 months of simulated warm-up.
- Check: start empty with an 18-month warm-up and compare. Use Welch-style plots of replication-averaged WIP per pool, comparing the same calendar months of successive simulated years; warm-up is sufficient when they coincide.
- The warm-up must exceed the long tail of Complex variance (p90 = 260 days), so at least 12 months when starting empty.

**Run length.** 24 months of measurement after warm-up (two full seasonal cycles). Statistics are collected for cases arriving in that window and followed to completion. Arrivals continue during a 12-month run-off, so late cohorts face realistic congestion; cases still open at the end are treated as right-censored (Kaplan–Meier quantiles).

**Replications.** Pilot with 30 replications per scenario, then add replications until the 95% confidence-interval half-width is at most 5% of the median, 10% of p90, and 1.5 percentage points of SLA compliance, per case type. Complex variance tails will drive the count; 100–200 replications are plausible.

**Random seeds.** One documented master seed. Each replication derives independent streams per source (arrivals, attributes, routing, service, external responses, objections, absences) via `numpy.random.SeedSequence`. The same seeds are reused across scenarios (common random numbers), so comparisons are paired and the arrival sample path is identical.

**Intervals.** Three sources of uncertainty are kept apart.
- *Simulation noise:* 95% confidence intervals on paired differences from baseline; quantiles are computed within a replication and then summarised across replications. Multiple comparisons use a Bonferroni correction or a ranking-and-selection procedure.
- *Parameter uncertainty:* a nested design with 40–50 parameter draws (block bootstrap of the log by week and architect; posterior draws for hierarchical and latent parameters) × 10–20 replications each. Reported as 90% credible/prediction intervals and as the probability that an intervention beats baseline by a stated margin.
- *Structural and effect-size uncertainty:* alternative model forms (objection mechanism, service/waiting split, queue discipline) and intervention effect ranges, reported as scenario bands and break-even values, not folded into one interval.

**Sensitivity analysis.**
- One-at-a-time tornado charts for: seasonal factors, effective availability per pool, service/waiting split, objection probability and extension, architect clustering strength, travel times, rework probabilities, each intervention's effect size.
- Global analysis with a Latin hypercube over 10–15 parameters and a regression or Morris/Sobol summary, to find interactions.
- For each shortlisted intervention, the report states which parameter value would reverse the recommendation.

**Heavy-tailed durations.**
- Fit body and tail separately (lognormal or mixture body, generalised Pareto tail above a threshold) for applicant response, environmental review, public notice with objections, and appeals; check with mean-excess plots.
- Use empirical resampling where data are dense. Two-quantile fits are fallbacks only.
- Truncate at administratively justified limits (for example withdrawal after a fixed non-response period), stated explicitly.
- Decide on medians and quantiles (p50, p90, p95), not means or maxima; give bootstrap intervals for quantiles; vary the tail index in sensitivity runs; add replications or stratify on rare events (objection waves) for tail metrics.

## 6. Validation strategy

Validation supports the baseline. It cannot confirm intervention effects, which the log has never observed; those need pilots.

**Face validation.** Structured walkthroughs with clerks, planners, fire and environmental reviewers, inspectors, schedulers and legal officers: routing rules, calendars, batching practice, priority handling, urgent-case rule. Staff review animated traces and are asked to tell simulated case timelines from real ones. Extreme-condition tests (zero arrivals, double staff, no council weeks) must behave sensibly.

**Event-log replay validation.**
- Conformance of the real log on the simulation's control-flow model, and of simulated logs on the same model, using PM4Py token-based replay and alignments.
- Variant-group shares compared with the summary (26 / 19 / 18 / 13 / 8 / 9 / 7%), target within ±3 percentage points; DFG edge frequencies and the stochastic language of variants compared between real and simulated logs.
- Trace-driven run B1: with historical arrivals and attributes fixed, simulated queues and completions should follow history. This separates errors in the arrival model from errors in the resource model.

**Distributional validation.**
- Cycle time by case type and variant group: median within ±10% and p90 within ±15–20% of the log; Kolmogorov–Smirnov and Wasserstein distances; QQ plots with emphasis on the upper tail.
- Per-activity sojourn times against the performance DFG and the summary table, by weekday (the Monday completeness spike must appear) and by month.
- Loop counts, missing-document rate, legal-check share, permit/reject share.

**Resource-utilisation validation.** Simulated utilisation per pool compared with independent evidence: timesheets where available, events per resource per day, hour-of-week activity profiles. Because start timestamps are missing, observed utilisation is a range; the simulated value must fall inside it and stay below 100% in the baseline. The capacity reconciliation in Section 2 is a precondition.

**Bottleneck validation.** The twin must reproduce where and when waiting occurs: ranking of activities by share of waiting, weekly backlog series per pool, peak-season build-up and recovery, the council-week effect on Legal check, longer inspection lead times in D3 and D5, and the legal–environmental interaction for Commercial cases. Target: correct ranking and weekly WIP error below about 15–20%.

**Out-of-sample validation.**
- Temporal split: calibrate on months 1–12, predict months 13–18 (which include a peak), once with actual arrivals and once with forecast arrivals. Repeat with a rolling origin.
- Check coverage: about 90% of held-out monthly KPIs should fall inside the 90% prediction intervals.
- Hold out one district or one case type as a transfer check.
- Use natural experiments in the log (a past staffing change, a long absence, a holiday cluster) as quasi-intervention tests.
- After go-live, run the twin in shadow mode, compare forecasts monthly with new log data, and recalibrate when drift exceeds tolerance.

## 7. Decision criteria

The SLA targets below are proposals for the municipality to confirm; thresholds are starting points, not statutory values. All comparisons are paired against the baseline under the same demand and parameter draws.

| Metric | Definition | Selection threshold or acceptable range |
|---|---|---|
| Median duration | Median cycle time per case type, on municipal clock and on total (applicant-experienced) clock | At least 10% reduction for the targeted types with the 95% interval of the paired difference excluding zero; no type worse by more than 5% |
| Tail duration | p90 and p95 per case type | At least 10% reduction in p90 for the targeted types; no type's p90 worse by more than 5% |
| SLA compliance by case type | Share decided within: Simple renovation 30 days, New residential 75, Commercial 110, Complex variance 210 | At least 85% per type (90% for Simple), or a gain of at least 5 points; no type losing more than 2 points |
| Fairness across districts | Ratio of worst to best district in case-mix-adjusted p90; SLA gap between districts | Ratio at most 1.20 and not rising by more than 0.05; D3 and D5 not worse off |
| Fairness across architects | Median and p90 for cases of non-top-20 architects versus top-20 | Small applicants not worse by more than 5%; gap not widening (relevant to D3 and D7) |
| Resource utilisation | Busy share of available hours per pool, yearly and per peak week | Yearly 70–85%; peak weeks at most 90%; no pool above 95% for more than 4 consecutive weeks |
| Rework | Missing-document loop rate, inspection rework, legal rework, loops per case | No rework rate up by more than 1 point; D1 should cut missing-document loops by at least 30% relative |
| Backlog | Peak WIP, share of open cases older than twice their SLA, recovery time after a peak | Aged cases at most 2% of WIP; backlog back to pre-peak level within 6 weeks; no year-on-year growth |
| Robustness | Share of parameter draws and stress scenarios in which the gain persists | Gain in at least 80–90% of draws; in every stress scenario no worse than baseline under the same stress; low worst-case regret |
| Cost and burden | Staff cost, applicant effort before submission, audit effort | Reported per day of cycle time saved; D1 and D7 judged on applicant-experienced time |

**Selection rule.**
1. *Gate:* drop any option that breaches a guardrail (fairness, utilisation, rework, backlog) in the central case.
2. *Rank:* order the rest by tail and SLA gains per unit of cost, with the probability of meeting each threshold under parameter uncertainty.
3. *Classify:* adopt (thresholds met and robust), pilot (met, but dependent on an unobserved effect size, as for D1, D8 and D9), defer, or reject.
4. *Combined policies* are preferred only if the factorial shows a gain beyond the best single component that justifies the cost.

D2 additionally needs a risk assessment outside the twin, since the simulation measures time, not undetected violations. Recommendations are worded as "preferred under the calibrated model and stated assumptions", with the break-even values that would change them; no option is claimed to be optimal.

## 8. Python skeleton

The skeleton has three layers: (A) PM4Py log and model handling, (B) a simulation engine that never calls PM4Py, and (C) experiments plus re-analysis of simulated logs with the layer-A functions. It runs end to end with PM4Py 2.7.x on the excerpt. All values marked PLACEHOLDER are illustrative, so its outputs support no conclusions.

```python
"""Building-permit digital twin -- skeleton.

Layer A (PM4Py)  : log / model handling and parameter extraction.

# LAYER A -- PM4Py-based log / model handling (no simulation logic here)

def load_log(csv_text: str = LOG_EXCERPT_CSV, path: Optional[str] = None) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Load with pandas (PM4Py has no read_csv) and normalise with pm4py.format_dataframe."""
    raw = pd.read_csv(path) if path else pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(csv_text))
    raw[TS] = pd.to_datetime(raw[TS])
    if START in raw.columns:                      # the excerpt has completion timestamps only
        raw[START] = pd.to_datetime(raw[START])
        raw[START_OBSERVED] = raw[START].notna()
    else:
        raw[START_OBSERVED] = False
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(raw, case_id=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS)


def parse_tree(tree_string: str = PROCESS_TREE_STRING, silent_labels: Tuple[str, ...] = ()):
    """Parse the simplified tree. Limitations: 'Completeness rework' / 'Inspection rework' are model
    labels that never occur as log activities (pass them as silent_labels for conformance); planning,
    legal and appeal rework are absent; Public notice is sequential after the reviews; inspection is
    mandatory. The tree is a control-flow skeleton only -- no data, time, resources or cross-case logic."""
    tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_string)

    def _silence(node):
        if node.label in silent_labels:
            node.label = None  # label None == tau in PM4Py process trees
        for child in node.children:
            _silence(child)

    _silence(tree)
    return tree


def add_timing_columns(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Service / waiting columns as far as timestamps allow.
    - With a start timestamp: service = complete - start; waiting = start - previous completion in the case.
    - Without: only the sojourn since the previous event is observable (waiting + service + off-shift time);
      service_h stays NaN and must be inferred (resource-availability heuristic + calibration), never assumed.
    NOTE: 'previous event in the case' is the correct enabler only for sequential parts; inside the review
    AND-block the enabler is the last Completeness check (derive enablement from the model in production code)."""
    out = df.sort_values([CASE, TS]).copy()
    prev_complete = out.groupby(CASE)[TS].shift(1)
    out["sojourn_h"] = (out[TS] - prev_complete).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0
    out["service_h"], out["waiting_h"], out["timing_quality"] = np.nan, np.nan, "sojourn_only"
    if START in out.columns and out[START_OBSERVED].any():
        seen = out[START_OBSERVED]
        out.loc[seen, "service_h"] = (out[TS] - out[START]).dt.total_seconds()[seen] / 3600.0
        out.loc[seen, "waiting_h"] = ((out[START] - prev_complete).dt.total_seconds()[seen] / 3600.0).clip(lower=0)
        out.loc[seen, "timing_quality"] = "observed"
    return out


def describe_log(df: pd.DataFrame, tree=None) -> Dict[str, object]:
    """Control-flow and performance descriptives, used identically for real and simulated logs."""
    timing = add_timing_columns(df)                                 # before PM4Py calls (some add helper columns)
    case_span = df.groupby(CASE)[TS].agg(["min", "max"])
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(df)                               # {tuple(activities): count}
    dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(df)              # {(a, b): frequency}
    perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(df)             # {(a, b): {'mean','median',...}} in seconds
    summary: Dict[str, object] = {
        "variants": variants,
        "dfg": dfg,
        "start_activities": start_acts,
        "end_activities": end_acts,
        "performance_dfg": perf_dfg,
        "case_arrival_avg_s": pm4py.get_case_arrival_average(df),  # stationary average: sanity check only
        "case_durations_s": (case_span["max"] - case_span["min"]).dt.total_seconds(),
        "timing": timing,
    }
    if tree is not None:
        net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)
        summary["tbr_fitness"] = pm4py.fitness_token_based_replay(df, net, im, fm)
    return summary


def lognormal_from_quantiles(median: float, p90: float) -> Tuple[float, float]:
    """Two-quantile lognormal: prior / fallback only; fit full distributions on the real log."""
    return math.log(median), math.log(p90 / median) / 1.2816

def month_of(t_hours: float) -> int:
    return (EPOCH + pd.Timedelta(hours=float(t_hours))).month

class Calendar:
    """Weekly working pattern with closed days, optional active months and an availability factor."""

    def __init__(self, weekdays=(0, 1, 2, 3, 4), start=8.0, end=16.0, closed_days=(), months=None, availability=1.0):
        self.weekdays, self.start, self.end = set(weekdays), start, end
        self.closed_days, self.months, self.availability = set(closed_days), months, availability

    def _day_open(self, day: int) -> bool:
        if day % 7 not in self.weekdays or day in self.closed_days:
            return False
        return self.months is None or month_of(day * 24.0) in self.months

    def next_open(self, t: float) -> float:
        day = int(t // 24)
        for _ in range(800):
            if self._day_open(day):
                lo, hi = day * 24 + self.start, day * 24 + self.end
                if t < hi:
                    return max(t, lo)
            day += 1
            t = day * 24.0
        return math.inf

    def add_work(self, t: float, effort_h: float) -> float:
        """Finish time of effort_h working hours started at t (work pauses outside the calendar)."""
        remaining = effort_h / self.availability
        while True:
            t = self.next_open(t)
            if math.isinf(t):
                return t
            shift_end = (t // 24) * 24 + self.end
            if remaining <= shift_end - t:
                return t + remaining
            remaining -= shift_end - t
            t = shift_end


@dataclass
class ResourceUnit:
    name: str
    calendar: Calendar
    skills: Optional[set] = None               # e.g. planners restricted to Simple renovation
    free_at: float = 0.0
    last_district: Optional[str] = None
    busy_h: float = 0.0


class ResourcePool:
    """FIFO pool of individually calendared units. Requests arrive in event-time order, so a per-unit
    'free_at' clock yields FIFO. TODO: explicit queues for priority/aging (D6), batching and stickiness."""

    def __init__(self, units: List[ResourceUnit]):
        self.units = units

    def book(self, now: float, effort_h: float, case_type: str) -> Tuple[ResourceUnit, float, float]:
        eligible = [u for u in self.units if u.skills is None or case_type in u.skills]
        unit = min(eligible, key=lambda u: u.calendar.next_open(max(now, u.free_at)))
        start = unit.calendar.next_open(max(now, unit.free_at))
        end = unit.calendar.add_work(start, effort_h)
        unit.free_at, unit.busy_h = end, unit.busy_h + effort_h
        return unit, start, end


def build_pools(p: TwinParameters, sc: Scenario) -> Dict[str, ResourcePool]:
    hol, wk = p.closed_days, (0, 1, 2, 3, 4)
    mk = lambda prefix, n, cal_fn, skills=None: [ResourceUnit(f"{prefix}_{i + 1}", cal_fn(), skills) for i in range(n)]
    planners = mk("planner", 6, lambda: Calendar(wk, 9, 17, hol)) + [
        ResourceUnit(f"planner_{i}", Calendar(wk, 9, 17, hol), {"Simple renovation"}) for i in (7, 8)]
    inspectors = mk("inspector", 4, lambda: Calendar((1, 2, 3, 4), 8, 16, hol)) + [
        ResourceUnit(f"inspector_temp_{i + 1}", Calendar((1, 2, 3, 4), 8, 16, hol, months=PEAK_MONTHS))
        for i in range(sc.peak_extra_inspectors)]                                              # D4
    return {
        "clerks": ResourcePool(mk("clerk", 4, lambda: Calendar(wk, 8, 16, hol)) + [
            ResourceUnit("clerk_5", Calendar((1, 2, 3, 4), 8, 16, hol))]),      # portal support on Mondays
        "planners": ResourcePool(planners),
        "fire": ResourcePool(mk("fire", 2, lambda: Calendar((1, 2, 3), 8, 16, hol))),
        "environment": ResourcePool(mk("env", 3, lambda: Calendar(wk, 8, 16, hol, availability=0.6))),
        "schedulers": ResourcePool(mk("scheduler", 1, lambda: Calendar(wk, 8, 16, hol))),
        "inspectors": ResourcePool(inspectors),
        # TODO: urgent-case bypass during council weeks
        "legal": ResourcePool(mk("legal", 2, lambda: Calendar(wk, 8, 16, tuple(hol) + tuple(p.council_days)))),
    }


@dataclass
class Case:
    case_id: str
    arrival: float
    case_type: str
    district: str
    architect_id: str
    application_value: float
    requires_fire: bool
    requires_environment: bool
    requires_public_notice: bool
    doc_loops: int = 0
    inspection_loops: int = 0
    pending: int = 0
    done: Optional[float] = None


class ArrivalGenerator:
    """Piecewise-constant NHPP (month x weekday) plus architect bursts of related applications.
    TODO: hour-of-day profile, negative-binomial overdispersion, trend, joint attribute resampling."""

    def __init__(self, p: TwinParameters, sc: Scenario, rng: np.random.Generator):
        self.p, self.sc, self.rng = p, sc, rng

    def _attributes(self, cid: str, t: float, architect: Optional[str] = None, district: Optional[str] = None) -> Case:
        p, rng = self.p, self.rng
        ctype = rng.choice(list(p.case_mix), p=list(p.case_mix.values()))
        top = rng.random() < p.top_architect_share
        architect = architect or (f"A{rng.integers(1, 21):02d}" if top else f"A{rng.integers(21, 2000)}")
        value = float(rng.lognormal(math.log({"Simple renovation": 35e3, "New residential": 4.5e5,
                                              "Commercial": 1.5e6, "Complex variance": 8e5}[ctype]), 0.7))
        return Case(cid, t, ctype, district or rng.choice(p.districts), architect, value,
                    requires_fire=ctype == "Commercial" or (ctype == "New residential" and rng.random() < 0.6),
                    requires_environment=ctype == "Commercial" and rng.random() < 0.75,
                    requires_public_notice=ctype == "Complex variance"
                    or (ctype == "New residential" and rng.random() < p.p_notice_new_residential))

    def generate(self, horizon_days: int) -> List[Case]:
        p, rng, cases, ids = self.p, self.rng, [], itertools.count(1)
        mean_w = float(np.mean(p.weekday_factor))
        mean_m = float(np.mean(list(p.month_factor.values())))
        burst_inflation = 1 + p.top_architect_share * p.burst_extra_mean
        for day in range(horizon_days):
            lam = (p.base_rate_per_day * self.sc.demand_multiplier / burst_inflation
                   * p.month_factor[month_of(day * 24.0)] / mean_m * p.weekday_factor[day % 7] / mean_w)
            for t in np.sort(day * 24 + rng.uniform(0, 24, rng.poisson(lam))):
                seed_case = self._attributes(f"S{next(ids):06d}", float(t))
                cases.append(seed_case)
                if int(seed_case.architect_id[1:]) <= 20:           # top architect -> related burst in same week
                    for _ in range(rng.poisson(p.burst_extra_mean)):
                        cases.append(self._attributes(f"S{next(ids):06d}", float(t + rng.uniform(0, 120)),
                                                      seed_case.architect_id, seed_case.district))
        return sorted(cases, key=lambda c: c.arrival)


class Router:
    """Data-aware routing; hidden rule inputs (floor area, sensitivity, violations) are probabilistic."""

    def __init__(self, p: TwinParameters, sc: Scenario, rng: np.random.Generator):
        self.p, self.sc, self.rng = p, sc, rng

    def missing_docs(self, c: Case) -> bool:
        prob = self.p.p_missing_docs[c.case_type] * self.sc.missing_doc_multiplier * (0.5 ** c.doc_loops)
        return self.rng.random() < prob                             # TODO: architect random effect

    def reviews(self, c: Case) -> List[Tuple[str, str]]:
        acts = [("Planning review", "planners")]
        if c.requires_fire or self.rng.random() < self.p.p_hidden_fire:
            acts.append(("Fire-safety review", "fire"))
        if c.requires_environment or self.rng.random() < self.p.p_hidden_env:
            acts.append(("Environmental review", "environment"))
        return acts

    def skip_inspection(self, c: Case) -> bool:                    # D2
        limit = self.sc.skip_inspection_simple_below
        return (limit is not None and c.case_type == "Simple renovation" and c.application_value < limit
                and self.rng.random() < self.sc.p_no_prior_violation)

    def inspection_rework(self, c: Case) -> bool:
        return c.inspection_loops < 2 and self.rng.random() < self.p.p_inspection_rework

    def needs_legal(self, c: Case) -> bool:
        return c.case_type in ("Commercial", "Complex variance") or self.rng.random() < self.p.p_legal_other

    def legal_rework(self, c: Case) -> bool:
        return self.rng.random() < self.p.p_legal_rework

    def outcome(self, c: Case) -> str:
        return "Reject application" if self.rng.random() < self.p.p_reject else "Issue permit"


class ObjectionModel:
    """LATENT cross-case shock: one draw per (district, week) notice cluster extends every related case.
    Objections are not in the log; p and extension are priors calibrated on the notice-duration mixture."""

    def __init__(self, p: TwinParameters, sc: Scenario, rng: np.random.Generator):
        self.p, self.sc, self.rng, self._clusters = p, sc, rng, {}

    def notice_duration_h(self, c: Case, now: float) -> float:
        key = (c.district, int(now // (24 * 7)))
        if key not in self._clusters:
            hit = self.rng.random() < self.p.p_objection_cluster * self.sc.objection_multiplier
            mu, sigma = lognormal_from_quantiles(*self.p.objection_extension_d)
            self._clusters[key] = float(self.rng.lognormal(mu, sigma)) if hit else 0.0
        return 24.0 * (14.0 + self._clusters[key])                  # TODO: holiday / council-calendar rules


class DigitalTwin:
    """Event loop + case state machine following the process-tree skeleton (plus rule overrides)."""

    def __init__(self, p: TwinParameters, sc: Scenario, seed: np.random.SeedSequence):
        s_arr, s_route, s_service, s_external, s_objection = [np.random.default_rng(s) for s in seed.spawn(5)]
        self.p, self.sc = p, sc
        self.arrivals = ArrivalGenerator(p, sc, s_arr)              # separate streams -> common random numbers
        self.router = Router(p, sc, s_route)
        self.objections = ObjectionModel(p, sc, s_objection)
        self.rng_service, self.rng_external = s_service, s_external
        self.pools = build_pools(p, sc)
        self.events: List[tuple] = []
        self.records: List[dict] = []
        self.now = 0.0
        self._seq = itertools.count()

    # ---- engine primitives -----------------------------------------------------------------
    def _at(self, t: float, fn: Callable, *args) -> None:
        heapq.heappush(self.events, (t, next(self._seq), fn, args))

    def _log(self, c: Case, activity: str, start: float, end: float, resource: str) -> None:
        self.records.append({CASE: c.case_id, ACT: activity, START: start, TS: end, RES: resource,
                             "case_type": c.case_type, "district": c.district, "architect_id": c.architect_id,
                             "application_value": round(c.application_value)})

    def _work(self, c: Case, activity: str, pool: str, now: float, then: Callable, extra_h: float = 0.0) -> None:
        mu, sigma = lognormal_from_quantiles(*self.p.service_h[activity])
        effort = float(self.rng_service.lognormal(mu, sigma)) * self.p.effort_scale[pool] + extra_h
        unit, start, end = self.pools[pool].book(now, effort, c.case_type)
        if pool == "inspectors":
            unit.last_district = c.district
        self._log(c, activity, start, end, unit.name)
        self._at(end, then, c)

    def _delay(self, c: Case, activity: str, now: float, hours: float, then: Callable, resource: str) -> None:
        self._log(c, activity, now, now + hours, resource)          # external / system time, no municipal resource
        self._at(now + hours, then, c)

    # ---- case flow ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
    def _submit(self, c: Case) -> None:
        self._log(c, "Submit application", c.arrival, c.arrival, "portal")
        self._completeness(c)

    def _completeness(self, c: Case) -> None:
        self._work(c, "Completeness check", "clerks", self.now, self._after_completeness)

    def _after_completeness(self, c: Case) -> None:
        if self.router.missing_docs(c):
            c.doc_loops += 1
            self._work(c, "Request missing documents", "clerks", self.now, self._await_documents)
        else:
            acts = self.router.reviews(c)
            c.pending = len(acts)                                   # AND-split; join in _review_done
            for activity, pool in acts:
                self._work(c, activity, pool, self.now, self._review_done)

    def _await_documents(self, c: Case) -> None:
        mu, sigma = lognormal_from_quantiles(*self.p.applicant_response_d)
        wait_h = 24.0 * float(self.rng_external.lognormal(mu, sigma))   # applicant clock, heavy-tailed
        self._delay(c, "Receive missing documents", self.now, wait_h, self._completeness, "portal")

    def _review_done(self, c: Case) -> None:
        c.pending -= 1
        if c.pending > 0:
            return                                                  # synchronisation wait, not a resource queue
        if c.requires_public_notice:
            hours = self.objections.notice_duration_h(c, self.now)
            self._delay(c, "Public-notice period", self.now, hours, self._inspection, "system")
        else:
            self._inspection(c)

    def _inspection(self, c: Case) -> None:
        if self.router.skip_inspection(c):                          # D2
            return self._drafting(c)
        self._work(c, "Inspection scheduling", "schedulers", self.now, self._site_visit)

    def _site_visit(self, c: Case) -> None:
        travel, release = self.p.travel_h[c.district], self.now
        if self.sc.district_batching:                               # D3 PLACEHOLDER: weekly district day
            district_day = 1 + self.p.districts.index(c.district) % 4          # Tue..Fri
            day = int(self.now // 24) + 1
            while day % 7 != district_day:
                day += 1
            release, travel = day * 24.0, travel * self.sc.batch_travel_factor
        self._at(release, self._book_visit, c, travel)

    def _book_visit(self, c: Case, travel: float) -> None:
        # TODO: applicant readiness / no-shows, architect+district bundles, route-level travel model
        self._work(c, "Site inspection", "inspectors", self.now, self._after_visit, extra_h=travel)

    def _after_visit(self, c: Case) -> None:
        if self.router.inspection_rework(c):
            c.inspection_loops += 1
            return self._at(self.now + 24.0 * float(self.rng_external.exponential(7.0)), self._inspection, c)
        self._drafting(c)

    def _drafting(self, c: Case) -> None:
        self._work(c, "Decision drafting", "planners", self.now, self._after_drafting)

    def _after_drafting(self, c: Case) -> None:
        if self.router.needs_legal(c):
            self._work(c, "Legal check", "legal", self.now, self._after_legal)
        else:
            self._decide(c)

    def _after_legal(self, c: Case) -> None:
        if self.router.legal_rework(c):
            return self._drafting(c)                                # legal rework loop (not in the simplified tree)
        self._decide(c)

    def _decide(self, c: Case) -> None:
        self._log(c, self.router.outcome(c), self.now, self.now, "system")   # TODO: appeal -> rework re-entry
        c.done = self.now

    # ---- run ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    def run(self, horizon_days: int) -> Tuple[pd.DataFrame, List[Case]]:
        cases = self.arrivals.generate(horizon_days)
        for c in cases:
            self._at(c.arrival, self._submit, c)
        while self.events:                                          # run-off: open cases are completed, not censored
            self.now, _, fn, args = heapq.heappop(self.events)
            if math.isinf(self.now):
                break
            fn(*args)
        log = pd.DataFrame(self.records)
        for col in (START, TS):
            log[col] = EPOCH + pd.to_timedelta(log[col], unit="h")
        return log, cases

SLA_DAYS = {"Simple renovation": 30, "New residential": 75, "Commercial": 110, "Complex variance": 210}  # proposal


def kpis(cases: List[Case], warmup_days: int, horizon_days: int) -> Dict[str, float]:
    rows = [(c.case_type, (c.done - c.arrival) / 24.0) for c in cases
            if c.done is not None and warmup_days * 24 <= c.arrival < horizon_days * 24]
    frame = pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["case_type", "days"])
    out = {"median_all": frame["days"].median(), "p90_all": frame["days"].quantile(0.9)}
    for ctype, grp in frame.groupby("case_type"):
        out[f"median_{ctype}"], out[f"p90_{ctype}"] = grp["days"].median(), grp["days"].quantile(0.9)
        out[f"sla_{ctype}"] = float((grp["days"] <= SLA_DAYS[ctype]).mean())
    return out   # TODO: municipal vs applicant clock, district/architect fairness, WIP, utilisation, rework


class ExperimentRunner:
    """Scenario x replication grid with common random numbers and paired comparison to the baseline."""

    def __init__(self, params: TwinParameters, master_seed: int = 20261112):
        self.params, self.master_seed = params, master_seed

    def run(self, scenarios: List[Scenario], replications: int, horizon_days: int, warmup_days: int) -> pd.DataFrame:
        rows = []
        for rep in range(replications):
            for sc in scenarios:
                seed = np.random.SeedSequence([self.master_seed, rep])      # same seed for every scenario (CRN)
                sim_log, cases = DigitalTwin(self.params, sc, seed).run(horizon_days)
                rows.append({"scenario": sc.name, "replication": rep, **kpis(cases, warmup_days, horizon_days)})
        return pd.DataFrame(rows)

    @staticmethod
    def paired_vs_baseline(results: pd.DataFrame, metric: str, baseline: str = "B0_baseline") -> pd.DataFrame:
        wide = results.pivot(index="replication", columns="scenario", values=metric)
        diff = wide.drop(columns=baseline).sub(wide[baseline], axis=0)
        half = 1.96 * diff.std(ddof=1) / math.sqrt(len(diff))       # use t-quantiles / bootstrap in production
        return pd.DataFrame({"mean_diff": diff.mean(), "ci_low": diff.mean() - half, "ci_high": diff.mean() + half})


def simulated_log_to_pm4py(sim_log: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Simulated logs go through the same PM4Py pipeline as the real log (validation by symmetry)."""
    sim_log = sim_log.assign(**{START_OBSERVED: True})
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(sim_log, case_id=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS)
```

**What the skeleton deliberately leaves open.**
- `estimate_parameters` returns placeholders; the estimation plan of Section 2 belongs there, including bootstrap or posterior draws.
- The excerpt has completion timestamps only, so `service_h` and `waiting_h` stay empty and only the sojourn since the previous event is reported. They are filled when a `start_timestamp` column exists, as in the simulated logs.
- `ResourcePool` is first-come-first-served. Aging priority (D6), planner stickiness, the urgent-case rule in council weeks, and true batch formation for inspection (D3 is approximated by a weekly district day) need explicit queues.
- D5, D7, D8 and D9 need their own routing and arrival hooks; the objection model is a prior on a latent process, to be calibrated on the notice-duration mixture.
- The per-pool effort factors exist because the summarised service spans exceed staffed capacity at face value; they must be replaced by calibrated values before any result is interpreted.
