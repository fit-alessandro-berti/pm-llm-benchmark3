# 1. Stress Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Demand Mix Shift
- **Trigger:** Significant spike in Urgent and Complex-panel cases during peak winter infection waves.
- **Impact:** Higher volume of high-priority cases increases contention at Medical validation, Specialist review, and Assay machines. Routine cases face longer delays as resources are diverted. Pre-analytical check is also strained due to stricter defect tolerance for high-priority cases.

---

### Scenario 2: Assay-Machine Degradation or Outage
- **Trigger:** M2 failure or prolonged downtime; M2 already has a higher failure rate.
- **Impact:** Reduced assay machine capacity heavily impacts Routine, Urgent, and Repeat cases. Urgents would bypass batching more frequently, leading to fragmentation and higher queue times. M1 becomes a severe bottleneck.

---

### Scenario 3: Validator Absences
- **Trigger:** Absence of Medical validators or Specialist reviewers (e.g., Friday absences or illness during peaks).
- **Impact:** Delays in Urgent and Complex-panel cases due to validation bottlenecks. Specialist-review queue backing up cascades delays into medical validation and Release result.

---

### Scenario 4: Courier Arrival Compression
- **Trigger:** Severe early-morning arrival peak caused by courier rescheduling or weather disruptions.
- **Impact:** Registration clerks are overwhelmed in short bursts, delaying sample intake. Follow-on bottlenecks form at Pre-analytical check and Preparation benches, leading to delayed batching and irregular flow further downstream.

---

# 2. Bottleneck-Migration Analysis

### Scenario 1: Demand Mix Shift
- **Bottlenecks:**  
  1. **Medical validation** struggles with higher volumes of Complex-panel cases.  
  2. Backlog increases **Specialist review** queues.  
  3. Delays cascade back to **Assay machines**, causing higher repeat assay volumes.
  4. **Preparation benches** experience batching inefficiency as urgent cases bypass queues.  

- **Risk of overload migration:** Fixing medical validation by prioritizing only Urgents leads to Routine and Complex-panel cases waiting too long, leading to rework and compounding delays upstream at assay stages.

---

### Scenario 2: Assay-Machine Degradation
- **Bottlenecks:**  
  1. **M1 becomes overloaded** due to redirection of Routine cases from M2.  
  2. If sampling batches cannot be processed on time, backup occurs at **Preparation benches** and Pre-analytical checks.  
  3. Urgent cases bypass batching, disrupting resource synchronization downstream.  

- **Risk of overload migration:** Relieving M1/M2 by splitting workloads (e.g., dedicating M1 to high-priority cases) increases batch fragmentation and delays Routine orders. Additionally, Repeat-after-failure cases rise due to higher pressure on M1.

---

### Scenario 3: Validator Absences
- **Bottlenecks:**  
  1. **Medical validation** and **specialist review** see cascading queues from unaddressed high-priority cases.  
  2. **Release results** delays for Routine cases accumulate as higher priority cases monopolize downstream resources.  

- **Risk of overload migration:** Redirecting validators from Technical validation or temporarily increasing evening shifts relieves bottlenecks but risks overloading Technical validation earlier in the flow.

---

### Scenario 4: Courier Arrival Compression
- **Bottlenecks:**  
  1. **Registration clerks** struggle to handle compressed intake, leading to dropped or delayed samples.  
  2. **Pre-analytical check** receives uneven workloads leading to underutilization during gaps.  
  3. Overloaded clerks or gaps disrupt demand pacing at **Preparation benches** and amplify batching delays further downstream.  

- **Risk of overload migration:** Smoothing courier arrival helps early-stage flow but risks overwhelming later stages like Preparation benches and Assay runs if capacity isn't balanced.

---

# 3. Robust Redesign Package

### Key Interventions
#### I4: Cross-train two technical validators to perform accession correction.
- **Why robust:** Relieves high-pressure bottlenecks at Accession correction without adding FTEs. Frees up specialists during peaks.  
- **Risk:** Creates a temporary bottleneck at Technical validation until validators are fully cross-trained.  

#### I5: Temporary evening medical-validation shifts during peaks.
- **Why robust:** Handles spikes in Medical validation workload during peak periods to clear Urgent and Complex-panel backlogs.  
- **Risk:** Relies on adequate staffing and scheduling flexibility. Not sustainable for long durations.  

#### I7: Shift courier pickup slots.
- **Why robust:** Smoothing courier intake evens Registration and Pre-analytical load, improving flow synchronization across the process.  
- **Risk:** May create resistance from couriers or expose uneven later-stage capacity (e.g., Assay machines).  

#### I8: Automate registration for orders with digital metadata.
- **Why robust:** Reduces workload spikes on Registration clerks during compressed arrivals, ensuring smoother downstream pacing.  
- **Risk:** Exposes Pre-analytical and Preparation bottlenecks if automation efficiency increases intake rates without downstream readiness.  

#### I10: Outsource Complex-panel overflow to a partner lab.
- **Why robust:** Reduces stress on Medical validation and Specialist review queues. The transport delay is offset by freeing onsite resources for higher-priority cases.  
- **Risk:** Adds traceability complexity and may delay the resolution of non-outsourced cases.

# 4. Scenario-Specific Policies

### Policy 1: Emergency Assay Machine Allocation 
- **Trigger:** M2 failure or >90% sustained utilization at Assay machines.
- **Action:** Dedicate M1 to Urgents and Complex-panel. Route Routines to M2 or batch wait.  

---

### Policy 2: Accelerated Medical Validator Shifts
- **Trigger:** Medical validator absence or >24h median Medical validation queue time.  
- **Action:** Activate evening shifts or redirect cases for overflow outsourcing.  

---

### Policy 3: Intake Triage Priority
- **Trigger:** >85% Registration clerk utilization or >9h median Pre-analytical check queue.  
- **Action:** Prioritize digital orders for fast-track Registration and early pre-analytical defect triage (I6).

# 6. Residual Vulnerabilities

1. **Sudden Assay Machine Failures:** Redesign depends heavily on flexible M1/M2 allocation. Complete failure of M1 would overwhelm the system.  
2. **Human Resource Availability:** Cross-training validators and evening medical-validation shifts depend on staff availability and willingness.  
3. **Traceability Complexity from Outsourcing:** Increased reliance on partner labs may create communication and handoff risks.  

---

# 7. Minimum Monitoring Dashboard

### Leading Indicators
- **Registration clerk utilization:** Monitor daily peaks >85% and queue >8h.  
- **Pre-analytical check queue:** Track median delays >10h.  
- **Assay machine utilization:** Monitor >90% M1/M2 usage or outages.  
- **Medical validation queues:** Watch for median delays >24h or sustained increases.  
- **Specialist review queues:** Flag growth over 24h median delay.  

---

### Lagging Outcome Metrics
- **Cycle time targets met (Routine/Urgent/Complex):** Weekly trend analysis.  
- **Overall rework rate:** Compare weekly against normal baseline.  
- **Queue metrics at each station:** High-level summary visualized daily.
