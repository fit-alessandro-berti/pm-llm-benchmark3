## 1. Current-process diagnosis

The current warranty process has **good average speed and low up‑front cost on the dominant fast path**, but this comes at the price of **higher rework, compliance deviations, and unstable customer outcomes**, especially for high‑value devices and unclear cases.

- **Fast path dominance and contradiction**
  - The most frequent route is: START → `Intake claim` → `Auto eligibility screen` (3,600 cases) → `Fast approval` (2,550) → `Dispatch replacement` (2,300) → `Quality/compliance close` (2,320).
  - Performance data shows this path is very quick: `Intake → Auto eligibility` has mean 0.8 hours; `Auto eligibility → Fast approval` mean 1.0 hours; `Fast approval → Dispatch replacement` mean 3.0 hours; `Dispatch replacement → Quality/compliance close` mean 5.0 hours.
  - Cost-wise, **Fast approval cases are 31% cheaper before rework**, but only **9% cheaper after accounting for complaints, reshipments, and service credits**, showing that apparently cheap speed is partially illusionary once rework is considered.

- **Rework and complaint burden**
  - Reopens are substantial: `Customer complaint/reopen` occurs in **9.2% of all cases**, but **14.1% of cases using Fast approval** versus **only 3.3% of cases following `Repair quote → Customer approval → Repair device`**.
  - The DFG shows reopens from both `Fast approval` (140 cases) and `Dispatch replacement` (260 cases), and performance data indicates these links are slow: `Fast approval → Customer complaint/reopen` has mean 72 hours, and `Dispatch replacement → Customer complaint/reopen` has mean 96 hours with long p90 tails (up to ~180 hours). This means the “fast” path often becomes slow over the full case lifecycle once complaints are included.

- **Compliance and conformance issues**
  - Normative rule **R1** requires that claims above EUR 400 receive `Manual eligibility review` or `Fraud/risk review` before `Fast approval`, and **R2** requires evidence completeness prior to replacement (except for Premium low‑risk accessories). However, alignment diagnostics show frequent deviations such as **Fast approval before evidence completeness** and **Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices**.
  - Normative rule **R3** requires any reopened claim to pass `Manual eligibility review` before final close or denial, yet there are **reopens after close** in the alignment diagnostics, indicating non‑compliant loops.
  - Token-based replay shows **log fitness 0.91 but only 68% of traces are fitting**, and discovered-model precision is low, implying many actual paths combine auto approval, risk review, and repair in ways not allowed by policy.

- **Evidence and abandonment problems**
  - `Request missing evidence` has problematic rework: it occurs **at least twice in 26% of cases that enter it**, indicating repeated evidence chases rather than a clean, single clarification loop.
  - The **“Unclear defect / missing evidence” segment (22% of cases)** has a median cycle time of **140 hours**, with the **highest abandonment rate** and repeated evidence requests.
  - Edges such as `Auto eligibility screen → Request missing evidence` (mean 5.2 hours) and `Request missing evidence → Manual eligibility review` (mean 34 hours, p90 88 hours) build very long tails, particularly for unclear cases.

- **Segment-specific contradictions**
  - **Premium low-risk accessories (18% of cases)**: Fast path median cycle time is 8 hours vs. 46 hours on the manual path, with complaints 6% (fast) vs. 3% (manual). Service-credit exposure is high if cycle time exceeds 24 hours, so the fast path is strongly attractive and mostly acceptable on quality.
  - **Standard low-risk accessories (25% of cases)**: Fast path median is 13 hours vs. 52 hours manual, with complaints 8% (fast) vs. 4% (manual). These customers tolerate up to 72 hours if status visibility is good, so speed is helpful but not at any cost.
  - **Premium high-value devices (14% of cases)**: Fast path median is 20 hours vs. 96 hours for the reviewed/repair path, but complaints are **22% (fast)** vs. **5% (reviewed/repair)**, and compliance risk is high if R1/R2 are violated.
  - **Standard high-value devices (21% of cases)**: Fast path median 28 hours vs. 120 hours reviewed/repair, with complaints 18% vs. 6% and **wrong replacements costing on average EUR 410**.
  - Thus the **same fast path that is ideal for low-risk accessories is problematic for high-value devices**, creating the central contradiction.

- **Resource imbalances and latent bottlenecks**
  - `Auto eligibility screen` is extremely cheap (EUR 0.20 per case) and nearly unconstrained, but its rules are outdated and not segment-aware, contributing to misrouted high-value and unclear cases.
  - `Manual eligibility review` (7 reviewers, EUR 34/h) and `Fraud/risk review` (2 specialists, EUR 52/h) are slower steps with utilization-sensitive queues; manual-review edges have mean durations of 7.5–22 hours with high p90s (up to 60 hours), and queues grow sharply beyond 82% utilization.
  - `Quality check` resources are underused except after repair campaigns, while rework repair occurs in **12.5% of repaired high-value devices vs. 4.0% of low-risk devices**, pointing to both quality and capacity optimization potential in the repair pathway.

In summary, the **current design over-uses a very fast and cheap auto-approval replacement channel**, which works well for low-risk accessories but **generates disproportionate rework, complaints, and compliance risk for high-value and unclear cases**, and **wastes capacity in quality and repair while overloading evidence and review steps**.

## 2. Redesign A: Segmented fast track with strict high-value gating

### 2.1 Redesigned process flow

**Core idea:** Preserve and refine the fast replacement path for truly low-risk, clear, sub‑EUR 400 claims, while enforcing **strict gating for high-value and unclear cases** using updated auto-screen rules and targeted pre-dispatch checks.

High-level flow:

1. **Enhanced intake and segmentation**
   - At `Intake claim`, classify each case using enhanced rules:
     - Segment: Premium vs. Standard.
     - Risk/value: low-risk accessory (< EUR 400) vs. high-value device (≥ EUR 400).
     - Defect clarity: clear vs. unclear/missing evidence.
   - Implement this as an **updated, segment- and value-aware Auto eligibility screen**, built on the “rules update” capability that can be deployed in ~2 weeks.

2. **Fast lane for low-risk accessories**
   - For **Premium and Standard low-risk accessories with clear defects and good history**:
     - `Intake claim → Auto eligibility screen → Fast approval`.
     - Before `Dispatch replacement`, perform a **lightweight evidence-completeness confirmation** driven by structured inputs (e.g., photo uploads, proof-of-purchase capture). For Premium low-risk accessories, this fits the exception allowed by R2.
     - `Fast approval → Dispatch replacement → Quality/compliance close`.
   - For these claims, the evidence portal is primarily a **single, early capture step** rather than multiple `Request missing evidence` loops, using the “new evidence portal” to **reduce repeated evidence requests by 30–45%**.

3. **Gated path for high-value and ≥ EUR 400 claims**
   - For **high-value devices or any claim above EUR 400**:
     - Auto-screen rules block the direct `Auto eligibility screen → Fast approval` path.
     - Route instead to `Manual eligibility review` or `Fraud/risk review` in compliance with R1:
       - `Intake claim → Auto eligibility screen → Manual eligibility review` for most high-value / standard-risk cases.
       - `Intake claim → Auto eligibility screen → Fraud/risk review` for high-risk patterns or suspicious histories.
     - Only after a **positive review** and evidence completeness may `Fast approval` be used, and **Fast approval → Dispatch replacement** is allowed **only if R2 is satisfied**.

4. **Targeted pre-dispatch quality gate for high-value replacements**
   - For **high-value devices only**, insert a **pre-dispatch quality gate** before `Dispatch replacement`:
     - Confirm device model, configuration, and applicable remedy (repair vs. replacement).
     - This is the “lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate” with an added 6–18 hours, focusing on high-value devices where **wrong replacement costs around EUR 410** and are a key driver of reopens.

5. **Unclear/missing-evidence routing**
   - For cases flagged “Unclear defect/missing evidence” at intake:
     - Route early to `Request missing evidence` once, followed immediately by `Manual eligibility review` after evidence is received.
     - Avoid sending unclear cases through `Fast approval`, reducing repeat `Request missing evidence` loops.

### 2.2 Targets (segments, paths, activities)

- **Primary targets**
  - Premium and Standard **high-value devices** and **claims > EUR 400**, which currently see high complaint/reopen rates (22%/18%) and heavy compliance risk.
  - The frequent path `Auto eligibility screen → Fast approval → Dispatch replacement` when applied to high-value cases.

- **Secondary targets**
  - Premium and Standard **low-risk accessories**: preserve and clean up the fast path that works reasonably well but still has a modestly elevated complaint rate (6–8%).
  - Unclear evidence flows that currently generate repeated evidence requests.

### 2.3 Mechanism of improvement

- **Reduced misuse of fast approvals for high-value devices**
  - Enforcing R1 and R2 via updated auto-screen logic and review gating reduces the number of high-value cases that get **Fast approval without adequate review or evidence**.
  - This directly tackles the observed alignment deviations (Fast approval before evidence completeness; Dispatch without risk review for high-value devices).

- **Lower reopens and wrong replacements for high-value devices**
  - For high-value devices currently seeing high complaint/reopen rates (22% Premium, 18% Standard on the fast path), routing them through `Manual eligibility review`/`Fraud/risk review` and a **pre-dispatch gate** should push their performance profile closer to the **5–6% complaint rate of the repair/review path**, and reduce costly EUR 410 wrong replacements.

- **Preserved speed and service-credit protection for low-risk accessories**
  - Premium low-risk accessories keep a **sub‑24-hour median cycle time** on the fast lane, protecting against service-credit exposure.
  - Standard low-risk accessories retain the fast 13-hour median path, with better evidence capture to avoid unnecessary reopens.

- **Less evidence rework for clear cases**
  - By capturing needed evidence cleanly up front via the portal, **repeated `Request missing evidence` loops (26% of cases that enter)** should be significantly reduced, especially for clear low-risk cases.

### 2.4 Deliberate sacrifices and unresolved issues

- **Higher load and longer times on review resources**
  - Updating auto-screen rules is expected to **increase Manual eligibility review inflow by 15–35%**, pushing utilization toward or beyond the 82% threshold where queues lengthen sharply.
  - High-value cases will generally have **longer cycle times** (closer to 96–120 hours for reviewed/repair paths) versus current 20–28 hour fast-path medians.

- **Partial treatment of unclear and reopened cases**
  - While unclear cases are steered away from fast approvals, **the core issue of 140-hour median cycles and high abandonment for the “Unclear defect/missing evidence” segment is not fully solved**—only mitigated.
  - Reopened cases still rely on the existing complaint/reopen and escalation mechanisms without a dedicated redesign.

- **Complexity in rule maintenance**
  - The auto-screen logic becomes more complex and must be maintained as product portfolios evolve; if rules drift out of date again, the benefits will erode.

## 3. Redesign B: Repair-first lane for high-value devices with strong quality gates

### 3.1 Redesigned process flow

**Core idea:** For high-value devices, **pivot from “replace-first fast approval” to a “repair-first, quality-assured lane”**, using repair and quality-check capacity more fully while still allowing fast replacement for low-risk accessories.

High-level flow:

1. **Eligibility and risk triage**
   - At `Intake claim`, segment as before by value (low-risk accessory vs. high-value device), customer segment, and defect clarity.
   - For **high-value devices**:
     - Route from `Intake claim` to `Auto eligibility screen`, but configure it so that high-value cases are **not allowed** to go directly to `Fast approval`.
     - Instead, high-value devices flow as:
       - `Auto eligibility screen → Fraud/risk review` for risk-screening (satisfying R1).
       - Then `Fraud/risk review → Repair quote` (rather than `Fast approval` where possible).

2. **Repair-first execution**
   - For high-value devices:
     - `Repair quote → Customer approval → Repair device`.
     - `Repair device → Quality check` is enforced for **all repaired high-value devices**, using underutilized quality inspectors.
     - `Quality check → Rework repair` where necessary, then back to `Repair device`.
     - `Quality check → Quality/compliance close`.
   - Only if repair is **not viable or uneconomical** (e.g., based on technician assessment or customer preference) should the case branch to a **replacement path with pre-dispatch gate**.

3. **Pre-dispatch quality gate for all high-value replacements**
   - For any high-value case that ultimately goes to replacement:
     - Insert the same **pre-dispatch quality gate** step before `Dispatch replacement`.
     - This gate focuses specifically on device configuration, eligibility, and past complaint history to reduce the `Dispatch replacement → Customer complaint/reopen` long-tail pattern linked to wrong replacement selection.

4. **Low-risk accessories remain mostly unchanged**
   - For low-risk accessories:
     - Retain `Auto eligibility screen → Fast approval → Dispatch replacement → Quality/compliance close` as the main path, with minor evidence capture improvements.
     - Repair is reserved for atypical or complex accessory cases.

### 3.2 Targets (segments, paths, activities)

- **Primary targets**
  - **Premium and Standard high-value devices (35% of cases)**, which currently have:
    - Very fast but error-prone fast paths (20–28 hour medians, but 18–22% complaint rates).
    - High cost of wrong replacements (EUR 410 expected).
  - Edges and paths:
    - `Fast approval → Dispatch replacement` for high-value devices.
    - `Dispatch replacement → Customer complaint/reopen` with long tails and wrong-part issues.

- **Secondary targets**
  - Underused **Quality check** capability, which becomes a central control for high-value repairs.
  - `Rework repair` loops, especially the 12.5% rate in repaired high-value devices—these should decrease once quality checks are systematic, not optional.

### 3.3 Mechanism of improvement

- **Shift from expensive wrong replacements to controlled repairs**
  - High-value cases are moved from the high-reopen fast path to a **reviewed repair path** where complaints are currently only 5–6%.
  - Even though technician time (EUR 45/h) and inspector time (EUR 38/h) add cost, **reductions in EUR 410 wrong replacements and service credits** can make the end-to-end cost per high-value case more stable and potentially lower.

- **Systematic use of Quality check**
  - Mandatory `Quality check` for high-value repairs should reduce the 12.5% rework repair rate closer to the 4% observed for low-risk devices, improving first-time-right rates and customer satisfaction.

- **Better compliance with R1 and R2**
  - R1 is naturally satisfied since high-value devices always pass through `Fraud/risk review` or `Manual eligibility review` before any approval.
  - R2 is supported both by repair-first logic (repair inherently requires evidence and diagnosis) and the pre-dispatch gate for any unavoidable replacements.

### 3.4 Deliberate sacrifices and unresolved issues

- **Longer cycle times for high-value customers**
  - Median cycle times for high-value devices will move toward the **96–120 hour range** of the reviewed/repair path, away from the 20–28 hours of the fast path.
  - Premium high-value customers may perceive this as a service downgrade, even if complaint rates fall.

- **Increased pressure on technicians and risk specialists**
  - Fraud/risk specialists (already bottlenecked on Mondays and after campaigns) and technicians for `Repair quote` and `Repair device` will see more volume and may become the dominant bottlenecks unless capacity or prioritization is adjusted.

- **Limited improvement for unclear/evidence-poor cases**
  - This redesign focuses on high-value devices; **unclear defect and missing-evidence cases (22% of volume, 140-hour medians)** may not benefit much, and repeated `Request missing evidence` loops may persist.

## 4. Redesign C: Evidence-centric and reopen-control cell

### 4.1 Redesigned process flow

**Core idea:** Create a dedicated **“Evidence and Reopen Cell”** combining a new digital evidence portal and specialized handling of reopened cases, to **break the repeated evidence-request loops and reduce long-tail reopens**, without heavily modifying the main approval vs. repair decisions.

High-level flow:

1. **Evidence portal as a controlled, single loop**
   - For any case where intake or auto-screen detects **missing or unclear evidence**:
     - `Intake claim → Auto eligibility screen → Request missing evidence → Evidence portal/concierge`.
   - The Evidence Cell:
     - Guides customers through uploading photos, documents, and clarifications.
     - Provides real-time assistance (chat/call) for those with lower digital literacy.
   - After this step:
     - If sufficient evidence: `Evidence portal → Manual eligibility review`.
     - If the customer disengages: `Evidence portal → Claim abandoned` (explicitly marked).
   - Critically, **subsequent “Request missing evidence” events are disallowed**; the goal is **at most one evidence-request cycle per case**, targeting the current pattern where `Request missing evidence` occurs at least twice in 26% of cases.

2. **Structured handling of reopened claims**
   - For any `Customer complaint/reopen`:
     - `Customer complaint/reopen → Evidence and Reopen Cell`.
   - The cell:
     - Ensures R3 compliance by routing every reopened claim through `Manual eligibility review` before final `Quality/compliance close` or `Claim denied`.
     - Classifies root cause (wrong part, misunderstood decision, policy exception, poor communication, etc.).
     - If wrong replacement: route to `Repair device` or the pre-dispatch gate redesigned in A/B for a corrected replacement.
     - If communication failure: quickly confirm or adjust the decision, with proactive status updates.

3. **Status visibility and expectations for low-risk segments**
   - Standard low-risk customers tolerate **72-hour completion if status visibility is good**.
   - The evidence portal and Reopen Cell push status updates (SMS/email/portal) to avoid reopens caused solely by uncertainty or perceived inaction.

4. **Minimal changes to the main approval vs. repair logic**
   - Fast approval vs. reviewed/repair decisions remain largely as in the current process (or as adjusted by A/B when those are combined), but **the upstream evidence quality and downstream reopen handling are significantly improved**.

### 4.2 Targets (segments, paths, activities)

- **Primary targets**
  - The **“Unclear defect / missing evidence” segment (22% of cases)** with 140-hour median cycle time and high abandonment.
  - Paths involving:
    - `Request missing evidence` (especially repeated occurrences).
    - `Customer complaint/reopen → Manual eligibility review` and `Customer complaint/reopen → Escalation board`.

- **Secondary targets**
  - Low-risk segments where poor communication/status drives unnecessary reopens.
  - Conformance issues where **reopens occur after close without proper manual review**, violating R3.

### 4.3 Mechanism of improvement

- **Fewer and cleaner evidence loops**
  - The portal plus concierge support should **reduce repeated `Request missing evidence` by 30–45%**, especially by turning multiple scattered requests into a single focused interaction.
  - This should materially shorten the 140-hour medians for unclear cases and reduce abandonment driven by frustration.

- **Lower reopen rates and quicker resolution when reopens occur**
  - Reopens become a **structured sub-process** with root-cause classification and guaranteed `Manual eligibility review`, rather than an ad hoc combination of `Customer complaint/reopen`, `Manual eligibility review`, and the `Escalation board`.
  - This is expected to lower the 9.2% overall reopen rate and shorten the very long p90 tails associated with `Customer complaint/reopen` edges (e.g., 100–130 hours).

- **Better customer experience for Standard customers**
  - With status visibility and proactive communication, **Standard low-risk customers’ tolerance of 72 hours** can be leveraged, reducing complaints that are driven by lack of transparency rather than actual delay.

### 4.4 Deliberate sacrifices and unresolved issues

- **Increased digital and organizational complexity**
  - Building and operating the evidence portal and Reopen Cell is non-trivial; **implementation complexity and cross-functional coordination are high**.
  - If not carefully designed with assisted channels, the portal can **increase abandonment for customers with low digital literacy**, as explicitly warned in the data.

- **Limited impact on pure eligibility/repair decisions**
  - While evidence and reopens are better managed, the **underlying trade-offs between fast approvals and repairs for high-value devices remain**, unless C is combined with the gating or repair-first approaches of A or B.

## 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

### 6.1 Redesign A – Segmented fast track

- **Failure mode 1: Manual review overload and new delays**
  - If updating auto-screen rules increases Manual eligibility inflow toward the upper end of the **15–35% range**, manual-review utilization may exceed the 82% level where queues spike.
  - This can push median times for `Intake → Manual eligibility review` and downstream steps well beyond current 5–18-hour medians, generating **longer tail times and new reopens** driven by perceived slowness, especially for Premium high-value customers.

- **Failure mode 2: Misclassification in auto-screen rules**
  - If segmentation thresholds or data quality are imperfect, some high-value or > EUR 400 cases may still be **misrouted to the low-risk fast lane**, perpetuating R1/R2 violations and high complaint rates.
  - Conversely, too conservative rules could push many low-risk accessories into the slower manual path, creating unnecessary cost and service-credit exposure.

- **Likely displaced bottlenecks**
  - Bottlenecks move from misused fast approvals and evidence loops to **Manual eligibility review and Fraud/risk review**:
    - These steps become the throughput limit for high-value cases.
    - Queue growth will manifest in increased `Manual eligibility review → Repair quote` and `Manual eligibility review → Request missing evidence` delays.

### 6.2 Redesign B – Repair-first high-value lane

- **Failure mode 1: Technician and quality capacity saturation**
  - With more high-value devices routed to `Repair quote → Repair device → Quality check`, technicians and inspectors may become overloaded.
  - If no capacity is added or prioritized, **mean times of 21 hours for `Fraud/risk review → Repair quote` and 20 hours for `Customer approval → Repair device` could increase further**, pushing high-value cycle times beyond customer and service-credit tolerances.

- **Failure mode 2: Customer resistance to longer, repair-heavy resolution**
  - Premium customers used to fast replacements (20-hour medians) may react negatively to **96–120-hour repair cycles**, even if quality is higher.
  - This can cause **more `Customer complaint/reopen` events and higher use of the Escalation board**, whose single 45-minute daily slot does not scale, undermining perceived service quality.

- **Likely displaced bottlenecks**
  - The main bottlenecks shift to **Fraud/risk review, Repair quote, and Repair device**:
    - Fraud/risk specialists already experience peak bottlenecks on Mondays and after campaigns.
    - Repair quote delays (currently driven by part-price confirmation) become more critical to end-to-end performance.

### 6.3 Redesign C – Evidence & Reopen Cell

- **Failure mode 1: Increased abandonment for low digital-literacy segments**
  - The evidence portal can **increase abandonment** if customers with low digital literacy find it hard to use, as explicitly noted.
  - This may lead to **inequitable outcomes**, where some valid claims are abandoned, reducing measured reopens and cycle time but actually harming customer fairness and potentially increasing regulatory scrutiny.

- **Failure mode 2: New bottleneck in the Evidence and Reopen Cell**
  - If a large share of the 22% unclear cases and 9.2% reopens are funneled into the Cell without adequate staffing or automation, it may become a **heavily overloaded step**.
  - Mean times of 40–54 hours from `Customer complaint/reopen` to review or Escalation may rise further, simply relocating the delay rather than resolving it.

- **Likely displaced bottlenecks**
  - Bottlenecks migrate from scattered repeated `Request missing evidence` and `Customer complaint/reopen` edges to:
    - The **Evidence portal/concierge** and associated support staff.
    - The `Manual eligibility review` step for reopened cases, which will now see all reopens by design (R3 enforcement).

## 7. Recommended experiment sequence

### 7.1 Overall strategy

Given the absence of a single obvious optimum and the risk of displaced bottlenecks, the redesigns should be **tested in staged pilots**, with **segment-specific metrics** and **end-to-end (claim-to-final-close) views** to detect downstream shifts.

### 7.2 Step 1 – High-value gating and pre-dispatch gate pilot (elements of Redesign A)

- **Scope**
  - Pilot in **one region or selected product lines of high-value devices**, including both Premium and Standard customers.
  - Implement:
    - Updated auto-screen rules to enforce R1 (no direct Fast approval for > EUR 400 without review).
    - A pre-dispatch quality gate for high-value replacements.

- **Key metrics**
  - Segment-level:
    - Complaint/reopen rates for high-value devices.
    - Share of traces deviating from R1/R2 (e.g., Fast approval before review or evidence completeness).
  - Performance:
    - Median and p90 end-to-end cycle time for high-value devices (from `Intake claim` to final `Quality/compliance close` or `Claim denied`).
    - Queue lengths and utilization for Manual eligibility and Fraud/risk review.
  - Cost:
    - Rate and cost of wrong replacements (high-value).
    - Service-credit payouts for high-value segments.

- **Stop/go thresholds (qualitative)**
  - **Go/scale** if:
    - Complaint/reopen rates for high-value devices fall materially (e.g., toward the 5–6% range of the repair path) without pushing median cycle time beyond a tolerable band (e.g., below or around current 96–120-hour reviewed-path medians).
    - Manual review and Fraud/risk utilization stay below the danger zone (e.g., stable around or below 85% with acceptable p90 times).
    - Wrong replacement incidents and related costs show a clear downward trend.
  - **Stop/adjust** if:
    - Manual or Fraud/risk queues explode (p90 durations grow sharply).
    - Premium high-value customers incur unacceptable delays or service-credit penalties.

### 7.3 Step 2 – Repair-first high-value lane pilot (Redesign B) on top of Step 1

- **Scope**
  - On the same high-value pilot domain where gating works acceptably, pilot the **repair-first lane**:
    - Route most high-value approvals to `Repair quote → Repair device → Quality check` before any replacement.

- **Key metrics**
  - Compare **A/B cohorts** of high-value devices:
    - One cohort with replacement-first (but gated) logic, and one with repair-first.
  - Metrics:
    - End-to-end cycle time (median, p90) for each cohort.
    - Rework repair rate, especially for high-value devices (aiming to reduce the current 12.5% rate).
    - Complaint/reopen rates and Escalation board usage.
    - Cost per resolved high-value case, including technician/inspector labor and replacement cost.

- **Stop/go thresholds**
  - **Go/extend** if:
    - Repair-first cohorts exhibit **meaningfully lower complaint/reopen rates and wrong replacement incidents**, with total cost per case comparable or better, and cycle times still within acceptable service/credit thresholds.
  - **Stop/limit** if:
    - Technician/Quality check capacity becomes a sustained bottleneck.
    - Customer satisfaction or NPS for Premium high-value segments deteriorates despite better technical quality.

### 7.4 Step 3 – Evidence and Reopen Cell pilot (Redesign C)

- **Scope**
  - Start with the **“Unclear defect / missing evidence” segment and reopened cases** in one or two markets.
  - Deploy:
    - The evidence portal with concierge support.
    - The Reopen Cell routing for all `Customer complaint/reopen` events in scope.

- **Key metrics**
  - Evidence loops:
    - Frequency of multiple `Request missing evidence` events per case (targeting a **30–45% reduction**).
    - Median and p90 cycle times for unclear cases (targeting substantial reductions from current 140-hour median).
  - Reopens:
    - Overall reopen rate (9.2% baseline), and by fast-path vs. repair-path cohorts.
    - Time from reopen to final resolution.
  - Customer behavior:
    - Abandonment rates by segment and channel, to detect **increased abandonment among low digital-literacy groups**.
    - NPS or complaint sentiment for unclear and reopened cases.

- **Stop/go thresholds**
  - **Go/scale** if:
    - Repeated evidence loops drop significantly without a corresponding spike in abandonment for vulnerable segments.
    - Reopen handling becomes faster and more compliant (clear R3 adherence) without increasing downstream escalation.
  - **Stop/adjust** if:
    - Abandonment increases for specific demographic or channel segments.
    - The Evidence/Reopen Cell becomes a major bottleneck with long queues.

### 7.5 Learning whether improvements are real vs. shifted downstream

- **End-to-end outcome tracking**
  - For all pilots, measure **cycle time, cost, and complaint/reopen rates from initial Intake to final close**, not just intermediate steps.
  - Monitor whether reductions in particular transitions (e.g., fewer `Fast approval → Customer complaint/reopen`) are offset by increases in others (e.g., more Escalation board usage or delayed repairs).

- **Conformance and alignment monitoring**
  - Re-run token-based replay and alignment diagnostics after each pilot stage:
    - Track changes in the proportion of fitting traces (currently 68%).
    - Monitor deviations related to R1–R3.

- **Segment-specific performance views**
  - Always slice metrics by **segment (Premium vs. Standard)** and **risk/value class (low-risk accessory vs. high-value device vs. unclear evidence)** to avoid hiding regressions for particular groups behind overall averages.

- **Progressive scaling**
  - Only scale from pilots to broader deployment when **both quality/compliance and customer metrics improve or remain acceptable**, and when **new bottlenecks are identified and mitigated**, ensuring that observed gains are not just local optimizations that worsen performance elsewhere.
