## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test
A compensating package is:

* **Fully equivalent** when for each material threat the package provides prevention before irrevocable settlement with independence, coverage and evidence comparable to distinct preparer/releaser, and residual collusion risk is not increased. Timing  settlement, independence verified, overrideability limited, recoverability not required.
* **Partially equivalent** when prevention is achieved for some threats but not all, or timing is after release but before settlement, or independence is plausible but not proven, or coverage has gaps e.g. receipt not checked.
* **Merely mitigating** when controls are detective only or corrective only, or prevention exists but is overrideable without independent check, or audit occurs after recall window. Risk is reduced but objective not met.
* **Ineffective** when preventive coverage is absent, manager is conflicted or rubber-stamps, AV-2 is bypassed/unavailable, audit is late, or ED-1 conditions are violated.

Dimensions: timing vs release vs settlement vs recall window; independence of reviewer vs preparer/releaser and absence of conflict; coverage of the specific threat; overrideability and credential sharing; recoverability and residual loss after detection; evidence quality of review; collusion resistance.

Same expected loss is insufficient: two packages can have equal loss expectation but differ in prevention of fraud vs error, in deterrence, and in ability to stop an incident before it becomes irrevocable. Control objective is about preventing one person from both initiating and authorising, not only about loss amount.

AP-203: ED-1 compliant emergency. Loss mechanism would be error in emergency repair. AV-2 + pre-release independent manager break chain. Independent releaser would add marginal benefit; current package is already equivalent for this risk profile.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

Monitor and alert, not prove misconduct.

* Same-user rate per operator and per business unit; alert > threshold with trend.
* Override rate per operator, per warning type, per credential owner; alert spikes and override by preparer.
* Late manager approval: time between Release_Payment and Manager_Ratify/Approve and vs Bank_Settlement; alert if approval after settlement or >4h before settlement.
* Audit-before-recall-window compliance for emergency same-user payments; alert if RA-7 >24h post-release.
* Repeated resource pairs: count distinct preparer/releaser pairs per 30 days; alert on high frequency pairs like u12/u13.
* Shared workstation evidence from login/session logs; alert on co-location.
* Bank-change proximity: payments with bank change in previous 7 days; alert if released without manager pre-release approval.
* Control outages: AV-2 unavailable or degraded; alert on any release during outage, especially >100k.
* ED-1 condition completeness: token timestamp vs Prepare_Payment, amount limit, vendor age, bank change, AV pass, manager timing, audit timing; alert on any missing condition.

Alert indicates increased risk, not proof of fraud. Investigation requires additional evidence of intent, review quality, and independence.
