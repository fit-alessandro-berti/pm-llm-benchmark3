## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**A. Treatment of source authority.** I treat authority as a function of (i) institutional standing, (ii) formal entry into the controlled policy system, (iii) scope specificity, and (iv) temporal fit. A board-approved policy (GPIP-4) carries the highest *institutional* weight for matters it governs; a controlled local SOP (DE-AAC-PROC-17 Rev. 6) governs operational mechanics within its scope but cannot silently weaken a board policy (A5). An incident directive (ECD-2) is an extraordinary, time- and scope-bounded instrument, not a standing policy. An email from the CFO (D) is guidance on Finance controls within the CFO's authority, but it is not a controlled instrument and cannot waive what the CFO does not own (compliance, segregation, CRO exceptions).

**B. Specificity vs. generality.** A more specific norm governs the matter it specifically addresses; however, specificity does not authorize the instrument to override a norm of higher institutional authority on issues outside its granted scope. Thus ECD-2's specific authorization mechanics can govern the question "who must authorize before commitment" within its window, but cannot lawfully extinguish GPIP-4 segregation or Compliance clearance unless (a) an A7 exception exists or (b) GPIP-4 itself is read to permit the derogation — which it does not.

**C. Later-in-time.** A later-in-time rule of equal or higher institutional standing may supersede an earlier one for overlapping scope. ECD-2 is later than B and contemporaneous with D. But GPIP-4 was not amended or suspended; it remains in force. Later-in-time does not equal higher-authority.

**D. Emergency necessity.** Emergency status narrows certain controls (competitive tendering under A4; the PO-before-commit order and Plant Manager presence under B4), but the texts I have do not silently waive: segregation of duties (A1, A7), Finance_Approve (A2, D), Compliance_Clearance for new suppliers or >EUR 100k (A3), or the approved-supplier rule (B3, with only B4 partially relaxing it during a defined emergency).

**E. Retrospective evidence.** Per A6, a later approval does not automatically cure a missing pre-commitment approval. I therefore evaluate compliance at the moment of supplier commitment, using the strongest contemporaneous evidence (telephone recordings, signed PDFs, access logs, supplier acknowledgments) over ERP timestamps where the system outage affects reliability (EVIDENCE NOTE).

**F. Burden of proof.** The organization bears the burden of demonstrating that an exception applies. Ambiguity in the directive (C6) and the absence of a written CRO exception are resolved against the claim of exception.

**Separation of facts from assumptions.** Facts: amounts, supplier statuses, ERP timestamps, outage window, CRO unavailability, absence of written CRO exception, audit-vs.-operations disagreement over "critical." Assumptions: (i) "incident directive" is not a controlled policy instrument under GPIP-4; (ii) GPIP-4 segregation and Compliance controls are not "ordinary workflow steps" within the meaning of B7; (iii) ECD-2's silence on CRO, segregation, Compliance, and Finance is deliberate omission, not inadvertent waiver; (iv) Incident Command members are not, on the present record, recognized budget owners for A2 purposes.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

**P-501.** Alternative: **COMPLIANT**. Premise to change: read B4's "absent Plant Manager" liberally to include "not formally on-site" and treat SHIFT-1 as implicitly delegated by shift protocol; read B6's 24-hour form as substantially satisfied by the 07-15 07:50 filing. Why credible: B4 plainly contemplates substitution; on-call delegation is common. Why weak: B4 text requires "actively delegated"; evidence shows Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate. Net: alternative is plausible but unproven.

**P-503.** Alternative: **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**. Premise to change: accept Operations' broad reading of C6 ("critical" includes backup-line components) and read ECD-2 together with the CFO email D1 as a *combined* derogation reaching Finance and Compliance under emergency necessity. Why credible: ECD-2 is signed by COO and Incident Commander; operations did face contamination; CFO email arguably stretches to "operational approvals." Why weak: C5 expressly omits A1/A2/A3; D3 expressly bars the CFO from waiving Compliance; CRO is the only designated waiver authority (A7) and did not act; audit reading of "critical" is textually tighter. Net: alternative fails under the explicit text.

**P-505.** Alternative: **COMPLIANT**. Premise to change: rely on supplier acknowledgment (17:59:31) and outage-adjusted ERP clock to place the real commit at 17:57, within the ECD-2 window; treat Compliance_Clearance 07-18 17:40 as the governing pre-commit gate (A3 not triggered because <EUR 100k; A2 not triggered because <EUR 100k — wait, EUR 88k < EUR 100k, so A2 also not triggered). Why credible: contemporaneous external evidence is strong; outage note explicitly downgrades ERP timestamps. Why weak: any residual uncertainty keeps it INDETERMINATE rather than COMPLIANT. Net: alternative is genuinely credible and could flip the case.

**P-506.** Alternative: **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**. Premise to change: accept that Incident Command verbally extended ECD-2 overnight and that the buyer's statement, combined with the continuity-of-purpose (cleaning chemicals), establishes a *de facto* extension. Why credible: contamination recovery plausibly continued; verbal authorizations are contemplated by C3. Why weak: C3 requires recording within 4h, and A4/A7 require written exceptions for non-tendering controls; self-report without corroboration is weak evidence; the organization bears the burden. Net: alternative is weak.

**P-507.** Alternative: **COMPLIANT**. Premise to change: treat the CFO email D1 (EUR 150k) as reaching Finance pre-commit approval through "joint Incident Command authorization is sufficient for all operational approvals" — i.e., read "all operational approvals" as encompassing Finance for amounts EUR 150k. Why credible: D1's plain wording; recipients include Finance. Why weak: D3 explicitly limits CFO to Finance-control interpretation; D4 hedges; A2 requires an *independent* budget owner and a Finance Controller — substituting IC members changes the control architecture; absent an A7 exception, A2 cannot be waived by email. Net: alternative fails the text but is not frivolous.

**P-508.** Alternative: **COMPLIANT**. Premise to change: read D2's "above EUR 150k, obtain Legal clearance as well" as implicitly *replacing* Finance with Legal for amounts above EUR 150k during ECD-2, on the theory that the CFO is reframing the approval stack. Why credible: D2 reads naturally as a layered rule; "as well" could be read in context. Why weak: D2 does not say Legal *replaces* Finance; A2 requires a Finance Controller; the CFO cannot waive what he does not own (segregation, compliance). Net: alternative is weak under the explicit hierarchy.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

**Distinguishing breach types.**
- *Policy breach:* conduct contrary to the controlling rule at the time of the act (e.g., P-502, P-503, P-504, P-506, P-507, P-508).
- *Logging defect:* conduct likely compliant at the time, but records missing or mis-timestamped (e.g., P-501 B6 timing, P-505 ERP clock).
- *Late evidence of a timely act:* contemporaneous external evidence (call, signed PDF, supplier ack) supports pre-commit compliance even where ERP shows otherwise (P-505, P-507).
- *Inability to prove compliance:* contemporaneous external evidence absent and only self-report remains (P-506 verbal extension).

**Immediate case-level remediation.**
1. **P-502, P-503, P-507, P-508:** Obtain retroactive Compliance and/or Finance documentation where possible, open CAPA entries, and reassign segregation. For P-503, formally rotate PROC-3 out of the IC authorizer pool for this purchase and any further purchase of which he is requester/committer.
2. **P-501:** Issue a written delegation protocol between Plant Manager and Shift Leads; file the emergency form contemporaneously in future incidents; treat any later approvals as mitigation evidence, not cure (A6).
3. **P-504:** Disqualify the cafeteria purchase from any incident-related procurement reporting; recover the policy deviation through standard procurement discipline (no emergency exception applies).
4. **P-505:** Preserve the telephone recording, supplier acknowledgment, and any access logs; commission an independent forensic timeline; if the real commit was post-18:00, treat as breach with mitigation.
5. **P-506:** Decline to recognize the verbal extension; require written CRO exception process for any future overnight continuity; re-run approval under normal thresholds.
6. **All cases:** Insert audit trail annotations distinguishing ERP timestamps from contemporaneous evidence during outage windows; mark outage-affected entries.

**Monitoring actions.**
- Daily reconciliation of ERP vs. external timestamps for any commitment made during declared incident windows.
- Independent Compliance spot-check of every new-supplier commitment above EUR 50,000.
- Segregation-of-duties report run against incident-window transactions using full role-assignment data.
- A specific CRO review of whether ECD-2 should be ratified as an A7 exception; absent ratification, the deviations stand.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

> **GPIP-4 Amendment — Section A7 and new Section A8 (Emergency Procurement Overlay).**
>
> **A7 (unchanged in substance, clarified).** The Chief Risk Officer may issue a written, scoped, time-limited exception. An exception must (i) name the policy clauses displaced, (ii) name the persons or roles covered, (iii) state the time window not to exceed 72 hours absent renewal, (iv) identify compensating controls, and (v) require post-incident ratification by the Audit Committee within 14 days. *No other instrument — including a CFO email, a local SOP, or a joint COO/IC directive — constitutes an A7 exception.*
>
> **A8 (new).**
> 1. **Authority of incident directives.** An incident directive signed by the COO and Incident Commander may, within its declared window and declared scope, override only the *timing* of the purchase order, the *approved-supplier register*, and the *default authorization actors*. It may **not** override (a) segregation of duties (A1), (b) Finance Controller approval thresholds (A2), (c) Compliance_Clearance for new suppliers or purchases above EUR 100,000 (A3), or (d) any rule that requires an A7 exception.
> 2. **Scope discipline.** The directive must enumerate the asset, lot, or line covered. Items not enumerated are governed by the default policy. "Critical" means components whose absence would, with reasonable foreseeability, cause a sterilization or production stoppage within 24 hours.
> 3. **Thresholds.** A directive may not authorize commitments above EUR 150,000. Above EUR 150,000, Legal_Clearance and a contemporaneous Finance Controller approval are mandatory in addition to directive authorization.
> 4. **Segregation safeguard.** No person who is the requester or committer of a purchase may serve as one of the two required authorizers under the directive. Incident Command members are *not* deemed budget owners for A2 purposes absent a written budget-owner designation on file.
> 5. **Recording.** Any verbal authorization under a directive must be recorded within four hours, with an immutable copy (signed PDF or recorded line) preserved. ERP timestamps during declared ERP outages are not conclusive as to real time.
> 6. **Expiry and non-renewal by silence.** A directive expires at its stated end time. Continuation requires a written renewal that meets A8.1–A8.5; verbal continuations are null.
> 7. **CFO scope.** The CFO may issue interpretive guidance on Finance controls only. No CFO communication may waive Compliance_Clearance, segregation of duties, or substitute for an A7 exception.
> 8. **Safeguard against emergency normalization.** Any directive covering purchases above EUR 250,000 in aggregate, or extending beyond 72 hours, requires (i) prior CRO consultation (or, if unavailable, a documented attempt) and (ii) Audit Committee notification within 24 hours of issuance. A post-incident review must report on each directive, the deviations observed, the CAPAs taken, and whether further directives of similar shape should be pre-authorized as standing policy rather than re-issued ad hoc.

This replacement clause (i) pins authority to the CRO for substantive waivers, (ii) confines directives to timing, supplier onboarding, and default authorizers, (iii) preserves segregation, Finance, and Compliance controls, (iv) resolves the "critical" ambiguity, (v) caps directive amounts and durations, (vi) disciplines the evidentiary record against ERP clock uncertainty, and (vii) builds in a structural brake on emergency normalization.
