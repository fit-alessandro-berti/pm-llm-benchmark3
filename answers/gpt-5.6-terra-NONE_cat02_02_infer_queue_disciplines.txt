# 2. Reconstructed dispatch decisions

Assumptions used below:

- **A1 — arrival uncertainty:** an arrival scan may be up to two minutes late, so a job scanned at time \(t\) may have physically arrived in \([t-2,t]\). I treat jobs as “plausibly waiting” if their recorded arrival is no later than the decision time, while noting close cases.
- **A2 — non-preemptive unless shown otherwise:** a job remains in service unless service segments show an interruption.
- **A3 — appointment interpretation:** an appointment time is not automatically an exact mandatory start. It could represent a protected time, a target within a tolerance window, or a lower-strength preference.
- **A4 — SPT basis:** SPT means smallest `est_min` among eligible waiting jobs, not realized service duration.
- **A5 — QD eligibility:** composite skill requirements require a resource with all listed skills, because D05 explicitly says D-F is the only fully qualified resource.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

## QA — appointment-aware non-preemptive priority/FIFO hybrid

**Supporting cases**

- **A03 at 08:20:** A03 begins exactly at its scheduled appointment after arriving early. A02 had waited longer, and A04 had higher priority and a shorter estimate. This is strong support for appointment protection.
- **A06 at 09:20:** A06 begins shortly after its 09:15 appointment despite A05 and A07 being ordinary waiting work, and despite A08 having priority 1. This supports an appointment-overrides-ordinary-work component.
- **A04 at 08:58:** After calibration, A04 starts ahead of A05–A08. This is compatible with FIFO because A04 is oldest, but also with priority because it is priority 1 and shortest estimated. It does not independently identify the rule, but it is not inconsistent with the proposed hybrid.

**Challenging cases**

- **A02 at 08:32 over A04:** A04 is priority 1 and has a 7-minute estimate, while A02 is priority 3 with a 20-minute estimate. Strict priority or SPT would select A04. FIFO selects A02. This is a genuine challenge to priority as the universal secondary rule, not explained by skills or appointments.
- **A05 at 09:05 over A08:** A08 is priority 1 and A05 is priority 3. FIFO supports A05; strict priority does not. This supports FIFO among ordinary jobs more strongly than priority.
- **A07 at 09:32 over A08:** Again, FIFO/SPT favor A07, whereas strict priority favors A08. This rejects strict priority as a sole QA policy.
- **A03’s early arrival:** The fact that A03 arrived at 08:08 does not itself prove that the 08:20 appointment was binding. It could have been selected due to an undocumented “appointment window opens early” rule. The exact window is unknown.

**Conclusion on violations.** The apparent violations of strict priority are not well explained by known constraints. They require either FIFO fallback, a rule that priority is advisory, or an undocumented exception. By contrast, appointment selection is directly supported by the observed timings.

## QB — preemptive priority with dispatcher interventions

**Supporting cases**

- **B01 interrupted for B02 at 09:16:** B02 is priority 1, arrives at 09:14, and immediately replaces B01. The stated reason, “urgent arrival,” directly supports priority-triggered preemption.
- **B05 at 10:00 over B01/B04:** B05 is priority 1 and is selected ahead of older priority-3 work. This supports priority ordering after B03 completes.
- **B01’s segmented service:** Multiple service segments are direct evidence that this queue permits interruption and resumption, unlike QA and QC.
- **B06 before B07 at 10:36:** B06 is priority 2 while B07 is priority 3, and B07’s appointment has already passed. The observed choice supports priority dominance over a weak appointment target.

**Challenging cases**

- **B01 resumes at 09:31 before B03:** B03 is priority 2, while B01 is priority 3. Under a strict preemptive-priority queue, B03 should normally start at B02 completion. The observed resumption could be explained by a “resume interrupted job first” rule, retained resource context, or a dispatcher decision. It is not explained by pure priority.
- **B03 at 09:48 instead of B05:** B05 had arrived at 09:46 and is priority 1. At the minute-level timestamp resolution, B03’s 09:48 start occurs two minutes after B05’s recorded arrival. Since arrival scans can be delayed by up to two minutes, B05 may have physically arrived at or just after the dispatch decision. Thus this is only a moderate contradiction, not decisive evidence against priority.
- **B01 interrupted at 10:14 without immediate replacement:** The second interruption reason includes “dispatcher reassignment,” and B04 does not begin until 10:28. This requires an operational exception or missing event rather than a simple queueing-rule explanation.

**Conclusion on violations.** The 09:31 and 10:14 episodes show that preemptive priority is not sufficient as a complete operational model. They are plausibly explained by explicit dispatcher control, resumption policy, setup/context preservation, or missing unavailable time. They do not erase the strong preemption evidence.

## QC — SPT with priority/SLA escalation

**Supporting cases**

- **C02 at 10:10 over C01/C03/C04:** C02 has the smallest estimate, 8 minutes. FIFO would select C01 and priority would select C03. This is the strongest direct support for SPT.
- **C03 at 10:18:** C03 is both shortest among remaining jobs and highest priority. This is compatible with SPT and priority, so it supports neither uniquely but is consistent with the hybrid.
- **C04 at 10:32 over C01:** C04 is shorter than C01, and it begins before the long oldest job. This continues the broad “do shorter work before C01” pattern, although strict SPT would have selected C06.
- **C06 at 11:34:** C06 is the shortest remaining estimate and is selected ahead of C01 and C07. This supports an SPT component after C05 is completed.

**Challenging cases**

- **C04 at 10:32 over C06:** C06’s estimate is 6 minutes, versus C04’s 22. Strict SPT selects C06. Priority would select C05, not C04. Thus C04 requires either a decision made before C06 was visible/eligible, an unobserved assignment commitment, noisy estimates, or a further rule not supplied.
- **C05 at 10:54 over C06:** C05 is the longest available job but priority 1 and overdue relative to its 10:50 deadline. This strongly contradicts pure SPT and supports urgency/SLA escalation.
- **C01 at 11:40 over C07:** C07 is priority 2 and estimated at 18 minutes; C01 is priority 3 and estimated at 36. Both SPT and priority choose C07. This is the strongest challenge to the stated hybrid and may indicate FIFO/fairness restoration or an undocumented constraint.
- **C05’s selection after deadline:** If the policy were pure earliest-deadline-first, C05 should have been selected earlier, before it became late. Its late selection may mean the deadline was not actively used until a threshold was crossed, or that the stated deadline was informational rather than a dispatch constraint.

**Conclusion on violations.** QC cannot be identified as a stable single policy. The evidence supports a short-job tendency, but the exceptions require an urgency escalation and perhaps a fairness/age rule. Delayed arrival scans alone cannot explain C06’s 10:32 non-selection because it was recorded at 10:25, seven minutes earlier; a missing eligibility or dispatch-commitment event would be needed.

## QD — skill-based routing with local queue logic

**Supporting cases**

- **D05 starts at 12:20 on D-F:** D05 needs firmware+electrical, and D-F is explicitly the only fully qualified resource. D-F’s meeting ends at 12:20, exactly when D05 begins. This is decisive support for skill and availability constraints.
- **D02 on D-F at 11:01:** D02 requires firmware; D-F is the only listed firmware-skilled resource.
- **D01/D04/D08/D09 on D-M:** These mechanical jobs are naturally handled by the mechanical resource. This supports routing, though not uniquely because D-X also has mechanical skill after 11:20.
- **D06 on D-X at 11:30:** D06 requires electrical and has a 11:30 appointment. D-X becomes available at 11:20 and starts D06 at the appointment time. This supports appointment-aware resource assignment within the skill feasible set.
- **D10 on D-X at 12:16:** D-F is unavailable until 12:20, while D-X is electrical-qualified. Routing D10 to D-X is operationally consistent.

**Challenging cases**

- **D08 waits until 11:35:** D08 is priority 1 and arrives at 11:16, but D-M is occupied until 11:35 and D-X only appears at 11:20. If D-X could freely take mechanical work, D08 might have been started before 11:35. However, D-X serves D06 at 11:30 because of appointment timing. This is explainable by resource occupancy and appointment protection, not necessarily a violation.
- **D07 selected before D10 at 11:48 on D-X:** D10 is priority 1 and estimated 12 minutes; D07 is priority 3 and estimated 28. Strict priority and SPT favor D10. FIFO favors D07, which arrived earlier. An early assignment of D07 to D-X, before D10’s arrival or before its scan was recorded, is also possible. This is a real challenge to strict local priority/SPT.
- **D04 on D-M at 11:25 instead of D08:** At that time D08 is already waiting and priority 1 with a lower estimate. FIFO selects D04, the older mechanical job. Thus D-M’s local rule at 11:25 looks FIFO-like rather than strict priority/SPT.

**Conclusion on violations.** The contradictions affect the secondary local ordering rule, not the primary skill-routing diagnosis. The observed data strongly establish resource-skill feasibility as a first-order determinant, while priority, FIFO, appointments, and possibly pre-assignment determine choices after eligibility is established.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

## Episode 1: QA at 08:20 — appointment protection versus priority/SPT

**Observed:** A03 starts at 08:20. Waiting jobs include A02, A03, and A04.

| competing explanation | predicted next job at 08:20 | fit to observation |
|---|---|---|
| Protected appointment rule | A03, because its appointment is at 08:20 and it arrived early | Direct fit |
| Strict priority | A04, priority 1 | Rejected by observed A03 |
| SPT | A04, estimated 7 minutes | Rejected by observed A03 |
| FIFO | A02, earliest arrival | Rejected by observed A03 |

**Additional observable that would discriminate most directly:** the configured **appointment tolerance/protection window**, including whether an early-arriving appointment customer may start before the exact time and whether a due appointment outranks priority-1 walk-ins. A dispatch log with `appointment_status_at_decision` (“due,” “within early window,” “late,” “not protected”) would be especially decisive.

A useful follow-up natural experiment would be a future decision where an appointment is due and a priority-1 job is waiting, but estimates and arrival order do not favor the appointment. If the appointment still wins systematically, appointment protection is confirmed.

## Episode 2: QC at 10:54 — SLA escalation versus SPT

**Observed:** C05 starts at 10:54, although C06 is waiting with estimated duration 6 minutes. C05 is priority 1, estimated 40 minutes, and has a 10:50 deadline.

| competing explanation | predicted next job at 10:54 | fit to observation |
|---|---|---|
| Strict SPT | C06, estimate 6 | Rejected |
| Priority/SLA escalation | C05, because it is priority 1 and deadline-at-risk/late | Direct fit |
| FIFO | C01, oldest waiting job | Rejected |
| Earliest deadline first | C05, deadline 10:50 | Direct fit, but it does not explain why C05 was not selected earlier |

**Additional observable that would discriminate most directly:** the **decision-time SLA state**, including current projected completion, lateness threshold, urgency score, and whether C05 was explicitly escalated. If C05 had a “breach imminent” or escalation flag at 10:54 but not at 10:32, that supports threshold-triggered SLA escalation. If no such flag existed and dispatch used a computed shortest-processing-time score, then the selection would require another explanation.

A secondary discriminator is the **estimate version history**. If C05’s 40-minute estimate was revised downward before 10:54, the apparent SPT violation could be partially illusory. The provided data say `est_min` is visible at arrival, but do not state whether it can later be revised.

If D07 was assigned before D10 entered the dispatchable set, the decision may be consistent with a non-preemptive assignment policy rather than FIFO. | 1 | **Appointment tolerance or protected windows rather than exact appointment starts** | High | QA, QB, QD | Explains A03’s early service and A06/D06’s timing without assuming appointments are strict exact-time reservations. Also explains why B07’s 10:30 appointment may not be honored exactly: appointments may be lower-priority or only target times. |
| 2 | **Dispatcher overrides, reassignment, or pre-commitment of jobs to resources** | High | QB, QD, possibly QC | Explicitly supported in QB by “dispatcher reassignment.” It can explain B01’s second interruption and D07’s start ahead of D10 if D07 had been assigned earlier. It lowers confidence in inferring a fully automatic queue discipline from start order. |
| 3 | **Resource-state and qualification constraints are more granular than the table shows** | High | QD | The stated skills establish major constraints, but may omit certification levels, setup compatibility, equipment location, or work-in-progress handoff rules. This could explain local choices that appear to violate priority/SPT. It does not weaken the skill-routing conclusion; it strengthens the caution against a global-order interpretation. |
| 4 | **Delayed arrival scans and minute-resolution simultaneity** | Medium-high | All queues, especially QB and QD | A job scanned at 09:46 may physically have arrived as early as 09:44, but a dispatch decision at 09:48 could have been committed before the scan became visible. This weakens close-timing contradictions such as B03 before B05. It cannot explain large gaps, such as C06 being recorded at 10:25 but not chosen at 10:32, without additional missing state. |
| 5 | **Estimates are noisy, stale, or strategically inaccurate** | Medium | QA, QC, QB, QD | Weakens inferences for and against SPT. It is particularly relevant in QC, where observed order mostly resembles SPT but has material exceptions. Because `est_min` is explicitly visible at arrival, the relevant issue is not whether an estimate existed but whether dispatch used that recorded estimate, a revised estimate, or an unobserved expected remaining time. |
| 6 | **Preemption/resumption cost or context preservation** | Medium | QB | Could explain why B01 resumes at 09:31 ahead of higher-priority B03: resuming a partially completed job may avoid setup loss. However, this is not explicitly documented and would make the policy a hybrid rather than pure preemptive priority. |
| 7 | **SLA threshold escalation rather than continuous deadline ordering** | Medium | QC | Explains C05’s late selection at 10:54: the system may only override normal SPT when projected breach crosses a threshold. It does not explain C01 over C07 at 11:40 unless there is an additional fairness or commitment rule. |
| 8 | **Unrecorded breaks, setup, quality checks, or administrative holds** | Medium-low | QB, QC, possibly QA | Particularly relevant to QB’s 10:14–10:28 interval. If B1 was unavailable or B01 was administratively reassigned, the apparent dispatch sequence is incomplete. In QC, an unrecorded job-specific hold could explain why C06 was not eligible at 10:32, but there is no direct evidence. |
| 9 | **Documented policy changed during the observed period** | Low-medium | QA, QB, QC, QD | A policy version or incident mode could create apparent hybrids. This is plausible in principle, especially around SLA breaches, but not directly evidenced. It should be tested before treating all deviations as random exceptions. |

Two cautions follow from these alternatives:

1. A queue can look like priority scheduling merely because high-priority jobs are also appointments, urgent SLA cases, or the only eligible jobs for a resource.
2. Conversely, a queue can look FIFO-like because non-preemptive assignments and pre-commitments preserve an older job even after a higher-priority job arrives.

# 6. Additional event data needed

The collection plan should prioritize decision-state reconstruction over adding more completion timestamps. Start order alone is insufficient because the decisive missing variables are what was eligible, visible, committed, and protected at each dispatch instant.

| priority | data field / event | why it is needed for these queues | implementation guidance |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Queue-entry time and scan time, separately** | Resolves whether close arrivals, especially B05 versus B03 and D10 versus D07, were truly present when a decision was made. | Record `physical_arrival_time`, `scan_time`, `queue_visible_time`, and source system. Preserve uncertainty if physical time is inferred. |
| 2 | **Dispatch-decision timestamp** | A service start may occur after an earlier assignment decision. This is central for QB interruptions, QC’s C04/C06 choice, and QD’s D07/D10 order. | Log each decision event with `decision_time`, `selected_job`, `resource`, and whether the decision was provisional or final. |
| 3 | **Eligibility set at each dispatch decision** | Required to distinguish policy from hidden holds, unavailable parts, appointment rules, and skill constraints. | Capture the full candidate list with `job_id`, `eligible=true/false`, and a coded `ineligibility_reason`. |
| 4 | **Resource state timeline** | Needed for calibration, meetings, breaks, setup, reassignment, and unrecorded idle intervals. | Record `available`, `busy`, `break`, `meeting`, `calibration`, `setup`, `administrative_hold`, `off_queue`, plus start/end times. |
| 5 | **Formal skill and certification matrix** | Essential for QD. Current broad labels do not show whether D-X can substitute for D-M or whether composite skills have special requirements. | Versioned matrix: `resource`, `skill`, `level/certification`, effective interval, and whether the skill is sufficient for each job type. |
| 6 | **Appointment semantics and tolerance** | Needed to identify QA and QD, and to interpret B07. | Store appointment type, promised window, early-start tolerance, late-start grace, protection rank versus priority, and no-show/early-arrival policy. |
| 7 | **Priority value plus policy meaning** | Priority labels alone do not reveal whether they are strict, advisory, preemptive, or only used at SLA escalation. | Record `priority_class`, reason/source, whether it may preempt, and tie-break rule. |
| 8 | **Current estimate and current estimated remaining time at every decision** | Necessary to test SPT/SEPT, especially QB after partial service and QC if estimates were revised. | Retain immutable arrival estimate, revised estimate history, remaining-work estimate, revision source, and timestamp. |
| 9 | **Preemption and resumption events with reasons** | QB’s diagnosis depends on distinguishing urgent-arrival preemption from reassignment, break, setup, or system error. | Log `preempted_job`, `incoming_job`, `reason_code`, `decision_actor`, expected resumption condition, and setup/context loss. |
| 10 | **Assignment versus service-start events** | Particularly important in QD, where jobs may be reserved for a resource before that resource becomes free. | Separate `assigned_to_resource_time`, `queued_at_resource_time`, `service_start_time`, and whether reassignment was permitted. |
| 11 | **Deadline/SLA state and projected breach metrics** | Needed to distinguish QC’s SPT interpretation from threshold-driven urgency escalation. | At each decision, log deadline, target, predicted completion, lateness, urgency score, escalation flag, and applicable SLA rule. |
| 12 | **Policy version and override indicator** | Required to determine whether apparent hybrids are stable policy or human exceptions. | Log `policy_version`, active mode, automatic/manual decision, dispatcher ID/role, override reason, and approval trail. |
| 13 | **Tie-break and fairness state** | Needed for QA’s FIFO-like behavior and QC’s C01-before-C07 outcome. | Record waiting age, prior deferrals, starvation-prevention flag, aging score, and whether a job is “must serve next.” |
| 14 | **Job readiness dependencies** | Could explain hidden ineligibility in QC or QD. | Capture parts availability, diagnostic completion, customer authorization, documentation completeness, test-bench availability, and handoff status. |

A practical minimum event schema for process-mining analysis would therefore include:

`case_id, queue_id, physical_arrival_time, scan_time, queue_visible_time, decision_time, assignment_time, service_start_time, service_pause_time, service_resume_time, completion_time, resource_id, resource_state, eligibility_set, ineligibility_reason, skills_required, qualification_status, appointment_window, priority, deadline, est_at_arrival, est_current, est_remaining, preemption_reason, override_flag, policy_version`.

# 7. Final synthesis

QB is the **most identifiable** queue because observed service segmentation and the explicit urgent-arrival interruption provide direct evidence of preemption. Its most plausible discipline is preemptive priority, qualified by dispatcher-controlled reassignment and resumption behavior.

QC is the **least identifiable** queue. It has meaningful SPT-like episodes, but priority, deadlines, SLA escalation, FIFO/fairness, and unobserved job readiness can each explain portions of the same sequence. Calling it simply “SPT” would overstate the evidence.

QA is best understood as **appointment-aware dispatch with FIFO-like handling of ordinary work**, not as pure priority. QD is primarily **skill-based routing**, with appointment and local ordering rules operating only after resource feasibility is determined.

The claimed policy most dangerous to infer from these observations alone is **strict priority scheduling**. In QA and QD, high-priority jobs are repeatedly deferred for appointments, FIFO-like local order, or resource constraints; in QC, urgency may only activate when an SLA is endangered; and even QB’s strong priority evidence is modified by resumption and reassignment behavior.
