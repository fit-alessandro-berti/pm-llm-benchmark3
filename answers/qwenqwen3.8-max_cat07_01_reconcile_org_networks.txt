# 3. Risk assessment

**Bottleneck risk**

- The bottleneck risk is concentrated in the exception segment, not the entire claims process. Routine low-value claims continued normally during Mira’s absence, but exception cases deteriorated. This suggests a localized but operationally important bottleneck.
- The risk is not that Mira is slow personally; her median event service time is short. The risk is that exception cases wait for her triage decision because no equivalent backup exists.
- Secondary bottlenecks may also exist in `Omar_Medical`, `Priya_Legal`, `Vendor_Verify`, and `Vendor_Pay`. Medical and legal service times are long, and vendor waiting times are high. Mira may be masking or managing these bottlenecks rather than causing them.

**Coordination risk**

- Mira appears to coordinate between automation, teams, specialists, and temporary staff. If this coordination is informal, there is a risk of unclear ownership, repeated handoffs, or inconsistent routing.
- The low working-together scores with Alice and Bruno may indicate a gap between the exception desk and formal team leadership. If Mira routes directly to adjusters or specialists without team-lead visibility, there may be coordination ambiguity.
- However, coordination through Mira may also reduce misrouting and specialist overload. The operations manager’s concern that removing her would increase formal escalations and specialist overload is plausible and should be tested, not dismissed.

**Key-person risk**

- The absence-period evidence strongly suggests key-person risk for exception handling. Mira appears to hold tacit knowledge about where hard cases should go.
- This is not evidence of wrongdoing. It is a resilience issue. If Mira is unavailable, the process loses triage capacity for reopened claims, complaints, medical/legal exceptions, and possibly vendor-triggered missing-information cases.
- Mitigation should focus on documentation, cross-training, and delegation, not on blaming the individual.

**Compliance/control risk**

- The compliance concern is legitimate but should be calibrated. Mira may both identify problems and choose the next handler, which can create discretion. However, there is no direct evidence of inappropriate decisions, favoritism, fraud, or policy violation.
- Mira does not appear to approve settlements or release payments. The payment path runs through team leads to `Vendor_Pay`. This reduces direct financial-control risk but does not eliminate upstream influence risk.
- High-risk cases involving fraud, legal review, medical review, complaints, or reopening may require documented routing rationale, dual review, or audit trails.

**Workload fairness risk**

- Exception work may be unevenly distributed. Mira carries a high share of complaints and reopenings; medical and legal specialists carry high exception rates; junior adjusters receive rework or missing-information cases.
- There is a potential fairness or skill-development risk if the same junior adjusters repeatedly receive difficult rework without support.

**Data-quality risk**

  - `Assign Specialist` appears in the log but not in the official activity catalog.

  - Short routing events may inflate handover and subcontracting centrality while understating coordination value in working-together networks.
  - `lifecycle:transition` may need to be used to separate substantive work from scheduling, assignment, or system updates.
- Until these issues are addressed, network metrics should be treated as directional evidence, not definitive proof.
