# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

**Operational definition used** (three independent tests, all must be considered):
1. **Share test** — activity  ~15% of the resource's own events (not of the log).
2. **Persistence test** — activity survives when substitution windows (`temporary_substitution == True`, weeks 3–4, 5, 7–8) are removed.
3. **Authority test** — the resource's decision is *final* for that activity, or is it rechecked/overturned downstream?

A resource can pass (1) and (2) and still fail (3): that is exactly the shadow-role case.

### R-A Automated intake (Nora)
Core: A1, A2 — 100% of events, but semantically **precheck**, not human adjudication. Peripheral: none. Complexity is structurally truncated (no high/critical), which is a routing rule, not a capability. Authority: none — the bot cannot own an entitlement decision. Treating its 610 A2 events as equivalent to a certified human validation is the single largest measurement error available in this log.

### R-B Front office (Amira, Ben)
Core: A1/A2/A4/A12. Peripheral: A3 (5.5%/4.4%) is *triage-level* diagnosis before handover, A5/A10 are minor fixes on simple cases, A8 is low-stakes clarification. Amira's A7 (18) and A8 (42) are a genuine periphery-with-growth-potential (Tier 1.5), not commercial ownership: she has no pricing certification. Neither has any A9 or A6 — a hard authority boundary. Complexity: they own the low/medium mass and pass high/critical onward.

### R-C Temps (Kai, Lina)
Core: the same four activities as Tier 1 — this is why frequency clustering fails. Peripheral: A3 (8/12) and A8 (3/4) are almost certainly misrouted or assisted touches, worth auditing. **The periphery that matters is the absent one**: zero A13, A11, A5, A10 means they never see the consequences of their own A2 work; the 17 reopens are the feedback loop the role structure has cut. Authority: they perform A2 without entitlement certification — 23.1% of all A2 events, and bot + temps together = **50.2% of all entitlement validation events executed by uncertified resources**.

### R-D Giorgio (hybrid)
Core: front office (68%) **and** commercial review/negotiation (19.5%, persistent: 41 A7 outside the absence window). Peripheral: A9 (20) — provisional at best; A3 (40) is senior triage. Complexity 0.58 sits between Tier 1 (0.29–0.34) and Fatima (0.67): he absorbs *complex front-office cases with a commercial dimension*. Substitution evidence cuts both ways: he is real surge capacity, but 38  74 minutes (2× cycle time) proves **substitution  equivalent competence**.

### R-E Technical (Chen, Dina)
Core: A3/A5/A10 (~81–84%). Peripheral: A2/A4/A12 (case hygiene), A11 (self-check), A13/A14 (rework and knowledge contribution — Chen 15/14 vs Dina 7/11 tracks seniority). Chen's A6 = 24 is peripheral in frequency but **not in risk**: it is either legitimate security-adjacent triage or unlicensed security review. Complexity, not activity mix, is the real separator: identical vocabulary, 42 vs 9 critical involvements.

### R-F Erik
Core: A6 only. Everything else (A3 16, A10 18, A5 10, A11 8, A14 9) is peripheral *consultation around* the security decision — he diagnoses and implements only where security constrains the fix. Frequency here is inversely related to criticality: 1.6% of events, highest median complexity (0.86), 32% of his involvements are critical. Authority: blocking/veto, which no count-based method encodes.

### R-G Fatima
Core: A7/A8 (73%). Peripheral: A2 (22 — entitlement re-verification for pricing eligibility), A12 (18 — closing commercial-only cases), A9 (16 — **ambiguous**: delegated threshold approval or mislabelled request). Complexity 0.67 with 25 critical involvements shows pricing exceptions are not administrative trivia.

### R-H Quality (Ivan, Julia)
Core: A11 + A14 (75–77%). Peripheral-but-material: A10 (73 combined) and A5 (48 combined) — 12.6% and 7.9% of all implementations/configurations. If these follow their own A11 in the same case, the role silently contains a **fix-forward implementation sub-role** with a segregation-of-duties exposure. A14 makes them the de facto knowledge owner (76.6% of KB updates), an authority that is invisible in the formal title "Quality Analyst".

### R-I Mateo
The share test **fails by construction**: max activity share 12.5%, all 14 activities present. Core must be defined functionally: reopen orchestration (30% of all A13), provisional approval (44 A9, 62% rechecked in week 5), and referral gating (16 A6 + the weeks 7–8 filtering that reduced Erik's load). Peripheral: routine A1/A4/A12 touches that are by-products of walking cases through the process. Authority: **provisional and conditional** — 13/21 rechecks is direct evidence of *borrowed*, not held, authority. Yet he touches more critical cases (76) than the supervisor (62).

### R-J Hana
Core: A9 (56%). Peripheral: A13 (24 — reopen adjudication), A8 (22 — high-stakes negotiation), A11 (12 — assurance sampling). Authority is the role: 2.3% of events, but the terminal node of fragments 4 and 5 and the recheck authority over Mateo. Substitution during the audit workshop degraded decision quality, not throughput.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

None of these are improper. They are the organisation's adaptive capacity; the risk is that they are **unmanaged, uncertified, or unmeasured**, not that they exist.

### 3.1 R07_Giorgio — commercial shadow reviewer (dual-membership bridge)
- **Evidence:** 30.6% of all A7 and ~34% of A8 outside the commercial team; 41 A7 events outside the substitution window; pricing certification held; sim 0.71 to Fatima, 0.84 to Amira.
- **Benefit:** keeps complex front-office cases with a commercial dimension in one pair of hands; provides genuine continuity (fragment 4 shows negotiation staying with either Fatima or Giorgio).
- **Risk:** cycle time doubled during full substitution (38  74 min); 20 A9 events of unclear authority; if Giorgio is loaded as a Tier-1 agent by a frequency-based staffing model, the commercial buffer silently disappears.
- **Management stance:** recognise a *formal secondary role* ("delegated commercial reviewer, threshold X") rather than either ignoring it or reassigning him to Commercial.

### 3.2 R13_Mateo — cross-functional escalation broker (and two hidden gatekeeper roles)
- **Evidence:** all 14 activities; 30% of reopens; 76 critical involvements; 44 A9; 16 A6; weeks 7–8 he filtered low-risk security referrals, reducing false escalations **but** two critical cases waited >2 days.
- **Three distinct powers in one resource:** (a) *routing* power — who gets the case; (b) *provisional approval* power — 21 week-5 A9, 13 rechecked; (c) *security triage* power — he effectively redefined Erik's demand curve.
- **Benefit:** reduces specialist interruption and false escalations; single accountable owner for messy cross-functional cases.
- **Risk:** an uncalibrated triage rule applied to a rare-critical activity produces **false negatives with long tails** (>2-day critical waits). Provisional approvals can drift into de facto final approvals if recheck rates fall when Hana is busy.
- **Management stance:** publish triage criteria with a mandatory bypass for `escalation_level` critical; make provisional approvals expire unless countersigned.

### 3.3 R05_Erik — rare but critical specialist (latent-capacity role)
- **Evidence:** 65.8% of all A6, 1.6% of events, median complexity 0.86, 55 critical involvements; 34% of A6 done by others (Chen 24, Mateo 16, Dina 6, Ivan 4).
- **Benefit:** concentrated expertise, consistent security judgement, KB contributions (9 A14).
- **Risk:** bus-factor 1. No substitution episode for Erik appears in 10 weeks — his resilience is **untested**, unlike Fatima's and Hana's. Any efficiency drive that reads "170 events in 10 weeks" as underutilisation would remove the process's only security veto.
- **Management stance:** measure this role in *risk-weighted coverage and response latency*, never in event counts; build a certified deputy (Chen is the plausible candidate given 24 A6 events).

### 3.4 R09_Ivan / R10_Julia — quality gatekeepers with a shadow implementation role
73 A10 and 48 A5 events. Benefit: faster closure, no handover loss, KB written by the people who see defects. Risk: checking your own fix; also, whoever writes 76.6% of KB articles controls how future discretion is exercised — a real, unlisted authority.

### 3.5 R11_Kai / R12_Lina — bounded temporary workforce as a control boundary
Benefit: absorbs 1,692 seasonal events of low-complexity load, protecting certified staff for complex work. Risk: they execute 23.1% of entitlement validations without certification and never see reopens; fragment 6 is a measurable defect pipeline (temp A2  close  reopen  Amira/Ben/Mateo rework). This is a **role-design defect, not a people defect**.

### 3.6 R14_Nora — automation boundary role
The bot is a *precheck* actor whose A2 label collides with human adjudication. Benefit: 45.6% of registrations automated. Risk: handover-of-work networks become bot-dominated, human collaboration structure is masked, and downstream staff may anchor on the bot's precheck (automation bias) — a plausible mechanism for the incomplete-entitlement reopens.

### 3.7 Emergent micro-bridges worth watching
- **R01_Amira as Tier 1.5:** A7 18 / A8 42 / A3 75 with complexity 0.34 vs Ben 0.29 — an informal escalation absorber inside Tier 1.
- **R03_Chen as security-adjacent triager:** 24 A6 events; either an sanctioned pre-screen or an uncertified security decision.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

```python
"""
Role discovery for an order-to-resolution log.
Design principle: activity-frequency clusters are HYPOTHESES about roles.
Authority, complexity and substitution behaviour are separate evidence layers.
PM4Py API names drift across versions -> defensive wrappers are used deliberately.
"""
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import networkx as nx
import pm4py
from sklearn.metrics.pairwise import cosine_similarity
from sklearn.cluster import AgglomerativeClustering

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Load and format
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
raw = pd.read_parquet("o2r_log.parquet")

# If columns are already PM4Py-conformant, keep them; otherwise format.
if "case:concept:name" not in raw.columns:
    raw = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        raw,
        case_id="case_id",
        activity_key="activity",
        timestamp_key="timestamp",
    )
raw["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(raw["time:timestamp"], utc=True)

CASE, ACT, RES, TS = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "org:resource", "time:timestamp"
log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(raw)

# Case-level attributes are constant per case in this log -> build a case table.
case_attrs = (raw.groupby(CASE)
                 .agg(case_complexity=("case_complexity", "max"),
                      customer_segment=("customer_segment", "first"),
                      product_line=("product_line", "first"),
                      escalation_level=("escalation_level", "max"))
                 .reset_index())

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Baseline PM4Py organizational mining (treat output as a hypothesis)
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
def safe(fn, *a, **k):
    try:
        return fn(*a, **k)
    except Exception as e:                      # version / signature drift
        print(f"[warn] {fn.__name__}: {e}")
        return None

pm4py_roles = safe(pm4py.discover_organizational_roles, log)          # list of resource sets
sim_pm4py   = safe(pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity, log)
how_net     = safe(pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network, log)      # nx.DiGraph
wt_net      = safe(pm4py.discover_working_together_network, log)      # nx.Graph
sub_net     = safe(pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network, log)        # nx.DiGraph
variants    = safe(pm4py.get_variants, log)

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Resource x activity profiles: three different spaces
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
M = pd.crosstab(raw[RES], raw[ACT])
P = M.div(M.sum(axis=1), axis=0)                       # row-normalised (frequency space)

def cluster(profile, k):
    D = 1 - cosine_similarity(profile.values)
    lab = AgglomerativeClustering(n_clusters=k, metric="precomputed",
                                  linkage="average").fit_predict(np.clip(D, 0, 2))
    return pd.Series(lab, index=profile.index)

freq_roles = cluster(P, 7)

# (a) complexity-weighted space: rare-but-critical work is up-weighted
w = raw["case_complexity"].clip(0, 1) ** 2             # convex weight on complexity
Mw = pd.crosstab(raw[RES], raw[ACT], values=w, aggfunc="sum").fillna(0)
Pw = Mw.div(Mw.sum(axis=1).replace(0, np.nan), axis=0).fillna(0)
complexity_roles = cluster(Pw, 7)

# (b) critical-only space: who actually carries critical risk?
crit = raw[raw["escalation_level"].astype(str).str.lower().isin(["critical", "3", "high"])]
Pc = pd.crosstab(crit[RES], crit[ACT])
Pc = Pc.div(Pc.sum(axis=1).replace(0, np.nan), axis=0).fillna(0)

print("Chen/Dina freq cosine:",
      cosine_similarity(P.loc[["R03_Chen", "R04_Dina"]])[0, 1])       # ~0.94 -> merged
print("Erik share of events:", M.loc["R05_Erik"].sum() / M.values.sum())
print("Erik share of A6:", M.loc["R05_Erik", "A6. Security Review"] / M["A6. Security Review"].sum())

# Divergence between the two clusterings is the interesting object, not either one.
comparison = pd.DataFrame({"freq_cluster": freq_roles,
                           "complexity_cluster": complexity_roles})
print(comparison[comparison.freq_cluster != comparison.complexity_cluster])

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Automation boundary: discover HUMAN roles on a bot-free log
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
human_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    log, RES, {"R14_Nora"}, level="event", retain=False)
human_raw = raw[raw[RES] != "R14_Nora"]
human_roles = safe(pm4py.discover_organizational_roles, human_log)

# Bot distortion of the handover network
if how_net is not None:
    deg_all = pd.Series(dict(how_net.degree_weight if hasattr(how_net, "degree_weight")
                             else how_net.degree()))
    print("Top handover degree (with bot):", deg_all.sort_values(ascending=False).head())
how_human = safe(pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network, human_log)
if how_human is not None:
    btw = nx.betweenness_centrality(how_human, weight="weight")
    print("Broker candidates (human-only betweenness):",
          sorted(btw.items(), key=lambda x: -x[1])[:4])   # expect R13_Mateo high

# Shift confound check on working-together ties
shift = raw.groupby(RES)["usual_shift"].first() if "usual_shift" in raw else None

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Substitution-aware core/periphery split  (core must PERSIST)
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
sub_flag = raw.get("temporary_substitution", pd.Series(False, index=raw.index)).astype(bool)
base = raw[~sub_flag]
subs = raw[sub_flag]

def share(df):
    m = pd.crosstab(df[RES], df[ACT])
    return m, m.div(m.sum(axis=1), axis=0)

Mb, Pb = share(base)
Ms, Ps = share(subs)

core = Pb.ge(0.15)                       # share test
persist = Mb.reindex_like(M).fillna(0).gt(0)
core_persistent = core & persist         # share + persistence tests

# Giorgio: is commercial work structural or only substitution?
g_a7_all = M.loc["R07_Giorgio", "A7. Pricing Exception Review"]
g_a7_sub = Ms.loc["R07_Giorgio", "A7. Pricing Exception Review"] if "R07_Giorgio" in Ms.index else 0
print("Giorgio A7 baseline vs substitution:", g_a7_all - g_a7_sub, g_a7_sub)  # 41 vs 31

# Mateo: same logic for provisional approvals
m_a9_sub = Ms.loc["R13_Mateo", "A9. Supervisor Approval"] if "R13_Mateo" in Ms.index else 0
print("Mateo A9 baseline:", M.loc["R13_Mateo", "A9. Supervisor Approval"] - m_a9_sub)

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. AUTHORITY test: performing A9 != owning A9
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
appr = raw[raw[ACT] == "A9. Supervisor Approval"].sort_values([CASE, TS])
appr["next_approver"] = appr.groupby(CASE)[RES].shift(-1)
appr["is_final"] = appr["next_approver"].isna()
authority = (appr.groupby(RES)
                 .agg(n_A9=("is_final", "size"),
                      final_share=("is_final", "mean"),
                      rechecked_by_Hana=("next_approver",
                                         lambda s: (s == "R08_Hana").mean())))
print(authority)     # expect Mateo/Giorgio/Fatima: low final_share, high recheck

# Segregation of duties: quality checking its own fix
q = raw.sort_values([CASE, TS]).copy()
q["prev_act"] = q.groupby(CASE)[ACT].shift()
q["prev_res"] = q.groupby(CASE)[RES].shift()
self_fix = q[(q[ACT].isin(["A10. Implement Solution", "A5. Configure Solution"])) &
             (q["prev_act"] == "A11. Quality Check") &
             (q[RES] == q["prev_res"])]
print("Self-check-then-fix by resource:\n", self_fix[RES].value_counts())

# Entitlement control exposure: uncertified resources performing A2
certified = {"R01_Amira", "R02_Ben", "R07_Giorgio"}
a2 = raw[raw[ACT] == "A2. Validate Entitlement"]
print("A2 by uncertified/system:",
      a2[~a2[RES].isin(certified)][RES].value_counts(normalize=True))

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# 7. Rework attribution and rare-specialist latency
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
```

**Cautions about changing staffing based only on mined roles.**
1. **Mined roles describe behaviour under the current routing policy, not capability or authority.** Chen/Dina look identical (0.94) because cases are *assigned* differently; reassigning them on that basis confuses cause and effect and creates a feedback loop that invalidates the next mining round.
2. **Frequency-based efficiency penalises exactly the capacity you cannot afford to lose.** Erik's 170 events and Hana's 245 look like slack; they are the security veto and the final approval. Cutting or merging them optimises the metric and destroys the control.
3. **Substitution is not competence.** Giorgio's 31 A7 events prove availability, not equivalence (2× duration); Mateo's 21 approvals prove delegation under supervision, not authority (62% rechecked). Do not convert either into a permanent reassignment without certification and quality monitoring.

5. **People, not clusters.** Role labels derived from logs can quietly become performance judgements (e.g., temps' reopen rate is a role-design defect). Use them for process redesign and coaching, with the affected staff involved in validating the interpretation.
