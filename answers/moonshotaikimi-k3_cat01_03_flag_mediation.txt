## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

Framework choice: I use (a) total policy (ITT) effects for the P-contrasts, (b) controlled direct effects (CDEs) for stage-level manipulations, (c) interventional (stochastic) indirect effects for mediation via occurrence and waiting, and (d) a separable-effects framing, because the flag is a *policy bundle* (label + priority lane + review mandate), not an atomically manipulable cause. I deliberately avoid natural direct/indirect effects: their cross-world counterfactuals are untestable, and they are undefined here because complexity/`U_underlying_risk` are exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounders and shared queues violate SUTVA.

Interference: priority lanes and shared review/senior queues mean one case's flag changes other cases' waits; unflagged triage wait is 3.1h under high load vs 1.9h under normal load, consistent with displacement. Exposure-induced confounding: the flag routes risk/complexity-selected cases into review, so complexity and `U_underlying_risk` confound the mediator–outcome links (reviewcycle time, escalationdecision) *because of* the exposure. Treatment–confounder feedback: queue state at each stage entry is a consequence of earlier flagged arrivals and influences later review/escalation decisions and waits; `arrival_load` is only an arrival snapshot, so congestion is a time-varying confounder affected by prior treatment. This defeats naive regression and single-world mediation formulas, and voids NDE/NIE cross-world assumptions. Overnight gaps additionally suggest unlogged staffing calendars as a hidden common cause of multiple waiting stages.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk:** if the P2 pilot shows elevated overturns/losses among un-reviewed flagged cases — i.e., `U_underlying_risk`, not the queue, drove both duration and outcomes — the recommendation reverses from P2 toward P0/P3 (keep or expand mandatory review).
2. **Queue interference:** if the priority lane turns out to *add* capacity rather than reallocate it (unflagged waits invariant to flag-share ahead in queue), the claimed displacement spillover disappears and P1's unflagged-side benefits reverse.
3. **Decision quality:** if escalated Rejects are validated as loss-avoiding (low overturns, high confirmed-risk yield), P4's cycle-time gains reverse into net harm; conversely, high overturns would strengthen P4 by showing escalation buys delay without quality.
4. **Timestamp semantics:** if the long triage-donemanual-start gaps are business-calendar or clock artifacts (nights/weekends in local time) rather than queue congestion, P3 does nothing and the fix is calendrical; verified by overlaying gaps on staffing rosters.
5. **Positivity/overlap:** currently P(review|flag=1)=1 and risk_score nearly separates the groups (0.61 vs 0.64 boundary); if future data fail to create overlap — observationally or via the pilot — all flagged-vs-unflagged contrasts remain extrapolations and the causal reading collapses to description.
6. **Reviewer behavior change:** if under P2 reviewers re-add informal reviews (high review-called-anyway rate) or under P3 protected capacity slows work, the mechanical effects shrink or reverse; process mining of override behavior is the check.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed:** flagged cases reach triage 10× faster (0.2h vs 2.5h) and are served faster; 10/10 flagged enter manual review (vs 4/10 unflagged) and then wait 6.5h from triage-done to manual-start (vs 1.9h for the reviewed unflagged); escalated flagged cases add 7h of senior-queue wait; totals average 16.5h flagged vs 8.2h unflagged; all Rejects are escalated High-complexity cases; triage resource is perfectly collinear with the flag. **Plausible mechanism:** the flag is a policy bundle whose priority-lane component accelerates triage while its mandatory-review component routes nearly all flagged cases into a shared, congested review queue, feeding a scarce senior queue — longer cycles arise from occurrence mediation (more stages) plus waiting mediation (queueing), amplified by morning high-load clustering. **Unidentified:** how much is case mix (complexity and unmeasured risk, given the score's near-separation), how much of the "waiting" is staffing calendar rather than congestion, whether review and escalation buy decision quality, and the size of spillovers onto unflagged cases. **Most useful next evidence:** the cluster-randomized P2 pilot on medium-complexity flagged cases with queue-length logging and quality follow-up — it separates occurrence from waiting mediation, quantifies interference, protects quality via guardrails, and is fully reversible if the mechanism story is wrong.
