FEP-1 has both a **form test**—different human resources for preparation and release—and a **purpose test**—no person should be able to initiate and finally authorize an erroneous or fraudulent transfer without meaningful independent challenge. ED-1 can authorize a departure from the form test; it does not make one person into two independent actors.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. ## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A package should be tested against the **case’s material threats**, not scored by counting control activities.

### Structured test

1. **Establish the transaction state.**
   - Was there a completed `Release_Payment`, or only an attempt that was blocked?
   - When were release, irrevocable settlement, audit, and any recall opportunity?
   - FEP-1’s nominal completed-payment test, ED-1 eligibility, and broader payment-objective achievement remain separate questions.

2. **Test causal timing.**
   - Before release: potentially preventive of both release and loss.
   - After release but before settlement: preventive of irrevocable loss only if the reviewer could actually stop settlement.
   - After settlement: ordinarily detective; corrective capacity depends on recall or recovery.
   - An audit or ratification does not become preventive merely because it occurred.

3. **Test independence and evidence quality.**
   - Confirm distinct human control, not merely distinct user IDs.
   - For MG-4, verify L3 status, independence, conflicts, credential control, and evidence that invoice, bank details, and receipt or service support were reviewed.
   - Shared workstations and repeated pairs reduce assurance and justify investigation, but do not prove collusion.

4. **Test threat coverage.**
   - Map each material case risk to a control capable of addressing it.
   - A clean AV-2 pass cannot compensate for missing receipt evidence.
   - A vendor-age check cannot replace bank-ownership verification.
   - Higher consequences and weaker recoverability require stronger assurance; amount alone does not determine conformance.

5. **Test overrideability and outages.**
   - Determine whether AV-2 blocked, merely warned, was overridden, or was unavailable.
   - Establish who controlled the override credential and whether the warning was resolved.
   - An unsigned manual checklist is weak evidence, particularly during an outage and for ED-1, which expressly requires clean AV-2 operation.

6. **Test recoverability.**
   - Verify stop-payment authority, actual settlement time, bank-specific recall deadline, recall attempts, and recovery.
   - “Within one business day” and “within the bank’s recall window” are related but distinct tests.

7. **Identify evidence that could change the conclusion.**
   - Missing evidence about a causally material property—such as whether one person controlled both accounts—can make the case indeterminate.
   - Missing administrative metadata should not automatically outweigh otherwise strong substantive evidence.

### Equivalence categories

| Package assessment | Required interpretation |
|---|---|
| **Fully equivalent** | Independent, substantive challenge occurs before release or before a demonstrably stoppable settlement; material non-AV threats are covered; validations are operational and not improperly overridden; emergency audit and recoverability requirements are satisfied where applicable; and no material independence or collusion concern remains unresolved. This can establish achievement of the objective without asserting that FEP-1 was literally performed. |
| **Partially equivalent** | The package supplies meaningful independent challenge or covers several material threats, but review quality, timing, audit, or coverage gaps prevent full equivalence. It may be tolerable after the fact only where the remaining exposure is limited and no aggravating evidence exists. |
| **Merely mitigating** | Controls reduce some likelihood or impact—such as automated checks or late audit—but do not establish effective independent authorization and leave a material unrecoverable window. This is insufficient for high-consequence or red-flag cases. |
| **Ineffective** | The relevant control is absent, unavailable, controlled by the person needing challenge, overridden without resolution, too late to affect the transaction, or inherently incapable of addressing the threat. |

For **policy conformance under ED-1**, every ED-1 condition is also a necessary gate. Thus, substantive objective achievement and formal emergency eligibility can diverge.

“Same expected loss” is not the only reasonable sufficiency standard. Expected loss averages likelihood and impact and can obscure irreversibility, rare catastrophic outcomes, uncertain recovery, collusion, risk appetite, auditability, and non-delegable authorization requirements. It can also treat prevention and hoped-for recovery as interchangeable even when they are not. A low expected loss therefore does not automatically excuse a control outage or unauthorized override.

| Control or timing | Preventive role | Detective role | Corrective role and principal limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AV-2 operational before release** | Strong for the enumerated checks when it blocks or when release is conditioned on a clean pass. | **AV-2 warning with override** | Weak unless an independent, authorized reviewer resolves the warning before release. | The warning and override log provide evidence of the unresolved condition. | An override does not correct the underlying bank-change, limit, or other defect. | **AV-2 unavailable** | No AV-2 preventive coverage. | The outage is itself a monitoring signal. | A later retrospective check cannot retroactively become release-time prevention. Manual fallback requires separate, reliable evidence. |
| **Independent MG-4 before release** | Strongest substitute for independent release authorization, provided the review is substantive. | Can identify defective support during review. | Can prevent the transaction entirely; no recovery is then necessary. |
| **Independent MG-4 after release but before settlement** | **Hybrid** under the semantics used here: detective with respect to the already-released instruction, but preventive with respect to irrevocable economic loss if the manager could stop settlement. | Reviews an action that has already occurred. | Can stop settlement or initiate another remedy only if that capability genuinely exists. | **MG-4 after settlement** | Not preventive for the completed payment. | Retrospective ratification or review. | Corrective only if recall or recovery remains possible; otherwise mainly useful for systemic remediation. |

The labels `Manager_Approve` and `Manager_Ratify` are therefore not decisive. Timing, review substance, independence, and the technical ability to stop settlement determine the control’s effect.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

### Evidence to retain

- **Authoritative event chronology:** preparation, attempted release, actual release, cancellation, manager decision, settlement, audit start and completion, recall request, and recovery. Synchronize clocks and preserve immutable timestamps.
- **Human and credential identity:** preparer, releaser, authenticated session, credential owner, MFA evidence, workstation or device, and any shared-service-account mapping. Distinct IDs must not be treated as conclusive proof of distinct human control.
- **Emergency evidence:** token creation, activation, expiry, scope, approver, emergency reason, and the source of each timestamp. Distinguish activation from later workflow logging.
- **AV-2 evidence:** service health, version, individual check results, warning or block disposition, override reason, override actor, and credential owner. Preserve evidence that no override occurred for claimed clean passes.
- **MG-4 evidence:** manager level, independence and conflicts, invoice and bank details reviewed, receipt or service evidence reviewed, decision time, and stop-settlement authority. Approval duration or checklist completion alone should not be treated as proof of review quality.
- **Vendor and bank evidence:** independently sourced bank-ownership verification, bank-change timestamp and approver, vendor age, sanctions result, receipt or service confirmation, and commercial-reasonableness support where relevant.
- **RA-7 and recovery evidence:** audit scope, completion, findings, affected-payment population, recall deadline used, recall attempt, recovery, and corrective-action closure.
- **Organizational evidence:** approved payment-cell design, delegation matrix, credential-access reviews, and explanations for repeated pairings or shared workstations.

### Monitoring rules

| Measure or rule | Recommended monitoring treatment |
|---|---|
| **Same-user completed-payment rate** | Use completed payments as the denominator and report blocked attempts separately. Stratify by operator, team, emergency status, and vendor/bank risk. Alert on every completed same-user case without a supportable exception and monitor trends even where ED-1 applies; the rate is not a tolerance threshold. |
| **Override rate** | Report overrides per warning and per validated payment, by operator, credential owner, vendor, and warning type. Require independent resolution before release. Same-user release combined with use of a manager’s override credential warrants immediate access-control investigation. |
| **Late manager approval** | Measure decision relative to both release and settlement. Post-release/pre-settlement approval counts only as hybrid when stop capability is evidenced. Post-settlement approval must not be counted as preventive. Report approval latency and missing review evidence separately. |
| **Audit before the recall window** | For emergency same-user payments, test 100% completion within one business day. Separately measure completion against the actual bank recall deadline and target completion with a safety margin. Missing either deadline should trigger review; neither timing fact guarantees recall. |
| **Repeated resource pairs** | Track rolling pair counts, each person’s concentration with the pair, value handled, overrides, and defect rates. Compare against team norms and approved cell design. AP-204’s 37 payments in 30 days and the recurrence in AP-209 justify targeted review, not an automatic collusion conclusion. |
| **Shared workstations or sessions** | Correlate preparation and release with workstation, login, session, and MFA records. Investigate shared credentials or evidence that one person controlled both steps. A shared workstation alone is an alert and may have an innocent operational explanation. |
| **Bank-change proximity** | Measure time from bank change to preparation, release, and settlement, with separate same-day/24-hour and one-to-seven-day bands. Apply ED-1’s seven-day exclusion strictly. Recent change plus new vendor, AV-2 warning, outage, or override should require independently sourced out-of-band verification before settlement. |
| **Control outages** | Measure outage duration and all payments prepared or released during it. Reconcile those payments to independent manual evidence. For ED-1, an AV-2 outage is ineligible rather than something an unsigned checklist can cure. Outside ED-1, high-risk payments should ordinarily stop until validation resumes or a documented independent equivalent control operates. |
| **Manager quality and audit effectiveness** | Sample supporting-document access and review content, track post-approval defects, and distinguish audit start from completion. Do not infer rubber-stamping solely from a short approval interval or high approval volume. |

An **alert selects cases for examination; it is not proof of misconduct**. In particular, AP-202’s event establishes use of an override credential assigned to m09, not who disclosed it or who participated in the vendor relationship. AP-204 establishes a fabricated receipt, not the identity or intent of every participant. Conversely, legitimate outcomes in AP-208 and no-defect outcomes in AP-207 or AP-209 do not establish that their control packages were sufficient.

nominal_violating_completed = pm4py_fep_ids & completed_ids

# AP-205 must not be treated as a completed-payment violation.
attempt_ids = set(
    events.loc[events[ACT].eq("Attempt_Release"), CASE].astype(str)

block_ids = set(
    events.loc[events[ACT].eq("Automated_Validate_Block"), CASE].astype(str)

cancel_ids = set(

attempted_blocked_not_completed = sorted(

# Reconstruct first-event timing and resource evidence without relying on
# version-specific PM4Py filtering internals.
timing_rows = []
for cid, group in events.groupby(CASE, sort=True):
    group = group.sort_values(TS)

    def first_event(names):
        hit = group.loc[group[ACT].isin(names)]
        return hit.iloc[0] if not hit.empty else None

    prepare = first_event(["Prepare_Payment"])
    release = first_event(["Release_Payment"])
    manager = first_event(["Manager_Approve", "Manager_Ratify"])
    settlement = first_event(["Bank_Settlement"])
    audit = first_event(["Audit_Review"])

    timing_rows.append({
        "case": str(cid),
        "prepare_at": prepare[TS] if prepare is not None else pd.NaT,
        "release_at": release[TS] if release is not None else pd.NaT,
        "manager_at": manager[TS] if manager is not None else pd.NaT,
        "settlement_at": settlement[TS] if settlement is not None else pd.NaT,
        "audit_at": audit[TS] if audit is not None else pd.NaT,
        "prepare_resource": prepare[RES] if prepare is not None else None,
        "release_resource": release[RES] if release is not None else None,
    })

timings = pd.DataFrame(timing_rows)

# Small case-facts table. None means unknown/ambiguous, not "no".
# Settlement fallbacks are supplied only where the narrative adds a time
# absent from the event CSV.
def cf(case, amount_eur, **updates):

        "shared_workstation": False,
        "pair_30d_count": np.nan,

    row.update(updates)
    return row

case_facts = pd.DataFrame([

def manager_timing(row):
    manager = row["manager_at"]
    release = row["release_at"]
    settlement = row["settlement_evidence_at"]
    if pd.isna(manager):
        return "none"
    if pd.notna(release) and manager < release:
        return "before_release"
    if pd.notna(settlement) and manager < settlement:
        return "after_release_before_settlement"
    if pd.notna(settlement):
        return "after_settlement"
    return "after_release_settlement_unknown"

e["manager_timing_flag"] = e.apply(manager_timing, axis=1)
e["manager_pre_settlement_evidence"] = e["manager_timing_flag"].isin([
    "before_release",
    "after_release_before_settlement",

    (e["audit_at"] - e["release_at"]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
).round(2)

def audit_flag(row):
    if pd.isna(row["release_at"]):
        return "not_applicable_no_completed_release"
    if pd.isna(row["audit_at"]):
        return "audit_missing"
    if row["audit_hours_after_release"] <= 24:
        return "within_24h_screen_not_business_day_proof"
    return "after_24h_screen_recall_not_assured"

e["audit_timing_flag"] = e.apply(audit_flag, axis=1)

def av_flag(row):
    status = row["av_status"]
    if status == "pass" and not row["av_override"]:
        return "clean_pass_for_enumerated_AV2_checks_only"
    if status == "warning" and row["av_override"]:
        return "warning_overridden_no_clean_pass"
    if status == "warning":
        return "warning_without_clean_pass"
    if status == "block":
        return "preventive_block_before_completion"
    if status == "unavailable":
        return "outage_no_AV2_assurance"
    return "AV2_status_unknown"

e["av_coverage_flag"] = e.apply(av_flag, axis=1)

def yes(value):
    return isinstance(value, (bool, np.bool_)) and bool(value)

def no(value):
    return isinstance(value, (bool, np.bool_)) and not bool(value)

def bank_flag(row):
    recent = row["bank_change_within_7d"]
    hours = row["bank_change_hours"]
    if yes(recent):
        if pd.notna(hours) and hours <= 24:
            return "recent_change_within_24h"
        if pd.notna(hours):
            return "recent_change_within_7d"
        return "recent_change_within_7d_time_unknown"
    if no(recent):
        return "no_recent_change_reported"
    return "bank_change_evidence_unknown"

e["bank_change_flag"] = e.apply(bank_flag, axis=1)

def preliminary_ed1_flag(row):
    """Screen selected ED-1 facts; deliberately not a compliance verdict."""
    cid = row["case"]
    if cid not in completed_ids:
        return "not_applicable_no_completed_payment"
    if cid not in nominal_violating_completed:
        return "not_assessed_for_nominal_distinct_resource_case"

    failures = []
    review = []

    if not yes(row["token_before_prepare"]):
        if yes(row["token_active"]) and not no(row["token_before_prepare"]):
            review.append("token_activation_timing")
        else:
            failures.append("no_token_before_prepare")

    if not row["amount_eur"] < 100000:
        failures.append("amount_cap")
    if not yes(row["vendor_180d_ok"]):
        failures.append("vendor_age")

    if yes(row["bank_change_within_7d"]):
        failures.append("recent_bank_change")
    elif not no(row["bank_change_within_7d"]):
        review.append("bank_change_history")

    clean_av = (
        row["av_status"] == "pass" and not bool(row["av_override"])
    )
    if not clean_av:
        failures.append("AV2_clean_unoverridden_pass")

    if not row["manager_pre_settlement_evidence"]:
        if row["manager_timing_flag"] == "after_release_settlement_unknown":
            review.append("manager_before_settlement_unproven")
        else:
            failures.append("manager_timing")
    if not yes(row["manager_independent"]):
        review.append("manager_independence")

    hours = row["audit_hours_after_release"]
    if pd.isna(hours):
        failures.append("audit_missing")
    elif hours > 24:
        review.append("audit_after_24h_screen")

    if failures:
        result = "known_ineligibility_flags:" + ",".join(failures)
        if review:
            result += "; review:" + ",".join(review)
        return result
    if review:
        return "indeterminate_or_review:" + ",".join(review)
    return (
        "core_conditions_present;"
        "verify_L3_review_quality_and_one_business_day_audit"
    )

e["ed1_preliminary_evidence"] = e.apply(preliminary_ed1_flag, axis=1)

e["nominal_result"] = e["case"].apply(
    lambda cid: (
        "PM4PY_NOMINAL_VIOLATION"
        if cid in nominal_violating_completed
        else (
            "COMPLETED_NOT_RETURNED_BY_FEP_FILTER"
            if cid in completed_ids
            else "NO_COMPLETED_RELEASE"
        )
    )
)
e["same_resource_crosscheck"] = (
    e["case"].isin(completed_ids)
    & e["prepare_resource"].eq(e["release_resource"])
)

# Repeated-pair evidence: both this log and supplied history are relevant.
pair_base = e.loc[
    e["case"].isin(completed_ids)
    & e["prepare_resource"].notna()
    & e["release_resource"].notna()
]
pair_counts = (
    pair_base.groupby(["prepare_resource", "release_resource"])["case"]
    .nunique()
    .reset_index(name="pair_log_count")
)
e = e.merge(
    pair_counts,
    on=["prepare_resource", "release_resource"],
    how="left",
)
e["pair_log_count"] = e["pair_log_count"].fillna(0).astype(int)
e["repeated_pair_flag"] = (
    e["pair_log_count"].gt(1)
    | e["pair_history_flag"].fillna(False).astype(bool)
)

print(

print(

print("Cases with cancellation events:", sorted(cancel_ids))

Across PM4Py versions, the return container, default resource-key handling, and treatment of missing resources or repeated activities can differ. The code handles the usual DataFrame/EventLog return forms and explicitly intersects the filter result with cases containing `Release_Payment`, but production use should still verify the installed version’s `org:resource` behavior. The filter establishes nominal resource-based evidence only; it does not establish human independence or compensating-control sufficiency. Likewise, the code’s 24-hour audit field is a screening proxy, not a business-day calendar calculation or proof that a bank recall remained available.
