# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

**Assumptions I make explicit before reviewing anything.**

- The two models are candidate *process specifications* for the custom-order dossier, discovered from a control-flow log that lacks two decisive attributes: the external/internal sourcing flag (P3) and the affected-check indicator (P6). Any model that claims to decide P3/P6 questions from control flow alone is over-claiming.
- The reported table is *evidence produced by the submitting teams*, not an independent measurement. No seeds, no preprocessing code, no alignment cost function, no held-out case list were supplied. Every number in it is therefore a *hypothesis about the model*, not a fact about it.
- P1–P7 are the normative reference. The behavior probes B1–B8 are policy test cases, deliberately incomplete, and B6 is explicitly under-determined by the available data.

**Intended use cases I distinguish.** These are not interchangeable, and the fitness/precision trade-off flips between them.

| Use case | What the model is used for | Cost of over-permissiveness (low precision) | Cost of over-restrictiveness (low holdout fitness) | Acceptable trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **U1 Exploration / discovery communication** | Understanding what happens, spotting variants, briefing business users | Low — an analyst reading a permissive map is mildly misled | Moderate — genuine behavior invisible | Favor fitness and simplicity; precision ≥ ~0.5 tolerable |
| **U2 Conformance enforcement / audit** | Deciding whether a case violated policy; producing findings a supplier or auditor can be confronted with | **Severe** — policy violations silently declared conforming (false negatives) | **Severe in the other direction** — legitimate cases flagged, alert fatigue, auditors stop trusting the tool | Neither may be sacrificed; must be near-1 fitness on *legitimate* behavior and near-0 acceptance on *curated illegitimate* behavior |
| **U3 Simulation / performance prediction** | What-if staffing, cycle time, queueing | Moderate — permissive models generate impossible paths and bias KPIs | Moderate — restrictive models under-represent the tail | Needs correct branching *probabilities*, which neither submission carries at all |
| **U4 Stakeholder communication / process design** | Workshops, sign-off, onboarding | Low | Low | Favors simplicity and comprehension, but the artifact must not be mistaken for U2 |
| **U5 Runtime guidance / next-step recommendation** | Telling a clerk what may be done next | Severe (suggests forbidden steps) | Severe (blocks valid work) | Same bar as U2, plus latency requirements |

**How use case changes the trade-off.** For U1/U4, Model S's low precision (0.584) is a *design property*, not a defect — F7 is a coherent position. For U2/U5, low precision is exactly the failure mode that matters, because a conformance checker that accepts everything produces zero findings and creates false assurance. Conversely, Model C's holdout fitness of 0.744 is only defensible under F8 if "unseen ⇒ noncompliant" is genuinely the organization's policy — but P2–P5 explicitly permit combinations (either check order × optional Supplier Review × optional Expedite Review × either approval) that the log need not have exhibited. So F8 confuses *unseen* with *unauthorized*, which is a category error: the policy already authorized those combinations.

**Scope limits.** I do not have the logs, so I cannot reproduce any number. I evaluate the models by reading their expressions and reasoning about their language, and I evaluate the metrics by reasoning about what they can and cannot establish. I do not fabricate replacement measurements; Section 9 specifies how to obtain them.

# 2. Metric Interpretation

I deliberately do **not** produce a single aggregate score. Aggregation here would require weights that depend on the use case (Section 1), and the two models are optimized at opposite ends of the same trade-off, so any weighted mean simply encodes the reviewer's prior as if it were a measurement.

| Measure | What it suggests | What it does not establish | Concern about comparability | Decision relevance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Training alignment fitness** — S 0.992, C 0.973 | Both replay the training log almost perfectly. C's 0.973 < 1.0 is itself informative: an enumeration of 8 variants that does *not* reach 1.0 means the training log contains variants outside the enumeration (or the split moved them), i.e. C already fails to cover its own training data. | Nothing about correctness. S reaches 0.992 partly by making everything skippable — a flower-like model attains high fitness trivially. High fitness is necessary, never sufficient. | Unknown alignment cost function; unknown whether standard (1,1) move costs were used; unknown log filtering. If either team filtered infrequent variants before training, the numbers are not on the same log. | Low as a discriminator. Only useful as a floor: a model below ~0.95 here is broken. |
| **Holdout alignment fitness** — S 0.981, C 0.744 | S generalizes to unseen cases; C does not. Combined with F4, C's 0.744 is consistent with holdout cases containing Supplier Review + Auto Approval + alternate check order — a combination absent from C's 8 branches. | Does not establish that S is *right* about those cases: S accepts them because it accepts nearly everything, not because it models the policy. Two very different mechanisms produce a high number. | Held-out cases were never published; no seed. A different split could move C to 0.9 or to 0.5 depending on whether external orders land in holdout. This is the single least reproducible number in the table. | **High** for C: 0.744 with a documented cause (unseen legal combinations) is a genuine defect under P2–P5. Moderate for S, because the mechanism is degenerate. |
| **Alignment precision on training log** — S 0.584, C 0.941 | S admits a great deal of behavior never observed; C admits little beyond what it enumerates. | Does not establish that C's admitted behavior is the *policy-permitted* behavior, nor that S's extra behavior is all illegitimate. Precision measures escaping edges relative to the log, so a model that memorizes the log scores high *by construction*. | Precision-by-alignment is highly sensitive to how the log was preprocessed and to silent transitions in the model (S has four τ's, C has none) — see Section 6. Not comparable across models with different τ structure. | **High**, but must be re-read as: "S is unusable for enforcement" and "C's number is partly an artifact of memorization." |
| **Token-based generalization** — S 0.907, C 0.412 | S's transitions are each exercised by many cases; C's are exercised by few, because 8 duplicated branches split the traffic. | Does not measure generalization to *unseen behavior* in any predictive sense. `generalization_tbr` is essentially a function of how often each transition fires; duplicating a transition across branches mechanically depresses it. | Directly confounded by model size and duplicate labels. C is penalized for having 33 duplicate-labeled transitions, which is an *encoding* choice, not a behavioral one. | **Low as reported.** Its real content ("C shatters the log across branches") is better read off the duplicate count. |
| **Petri-net simplicity, arc-degree** — S 0.918, C 0.281 | C's net is large and tangled; S's is small. | Says nothing about semantic quality. A single-place flower model would score superbly. Simplicity is a *maintainability* proxy, not a correctness one. | Depends on the tree→net conversion (τ handling, reduction). If one team ran reduction and the other did not, the values differ for no semantic reason. | Moderate — supports F3 (IT architecture) on maintainability, nothing more. |
| **Median alignment runtime** — S 0.018 s, C 0.440 s | C is ~24× slower per case. | Not a model-quality measure. It is a function of state-space size and of how far each trace is from the model — C's poor fit *causes* longer alignment searches, so runtime is partly re-reporting holdout fitness. | Hardware, PM4Py version, and variant (A*, decomposed) unstated. Median hides the tail, which is what actually breaks batch jobs. | Moderate for U5 (runtime guidance) and for large-scale audit; low otherwise. Report p95/p99, not median. |
| **Reported duplicate-label count** — S 0, C 33 | C repeats activity labels across branches; S does not. | "Definition undocumented" means we do not know whether τ's were counted, whether the count is on the tree or the converted net, or whether it counts labels-with-multiplicity or excess-occurrences. 33 is plausible for C by inspection (Register Dossier alone appears 8 times, Credit Check 10, Release Order 8 — already ≫33 by one definition, so the reported definition must be narrower). | **Not comparable at all** until defined. The discrepancy between 33 and a by-hand count is itself a red flag about the reporting pipeline. | High for maintainability (F3), and high as a *credibility* signal about the submission. |
| **Stakeholder comprehension, informal 1–5** — S 4.4, C 1.9 | Business users find S readable and C impenetrable. | Nothing about correctness, and nothing reliable at all: "informal" means no sample size, no rater population, no anchors, no blinding, no inter-rater agreement. Users may have rated S highly *because* it says less. | Two different audiences may have rated the two models. Ratings of a picture depend on the layout tool used. | Moderate for U4; **must not** be traded against U2 correctness. A comprehensible wrong model is worse than an opaque right one for compliance. |

**Cross-cutting comparability concern.** Both teams claim "the same 80/20 split and the same PM4Py installation" but published neither the split nor the code. Two independently drawn 80/20 splits are *not* the same split, and F4 tells us the holdout composition drives C's headline weakness. Until the split is fixed and published, the S-vs-C comparison on holdout fitness, generalization and runtime is not a controlled comparison.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

```
->( 'Register Dossier',
    +( X('Credit Check', τ), X('Technical Review', τ), X('Supplier Review', τ), X('Expedite Review', τ) ),
    X('Manager Approval','Auto Approval'),
    'Release Order' )
```

**Language of Model S (read precisely).** Register Dossier first; then a parallel block of four independent optional activities, each of which may occur exactly once or not at all, in *any* interleaving; then exactly one approval; then Release Order. Crucially: **no activity in the middle block may occur twice**, and **Correct Dossier does not exist in the model at all**.

## Strongest case for Model S

1. It is *sound* and trivially so: block-structured, no deadlocks, every transition reachable, clean final marking. Governance-friendly in the structural sense.
2. It correctly encodes P1 (Register first, Release last), P5 (exactly one approval, and the approval is after all checks and before release), and the "checks precede approval" half of P2/P4.
3. It is robust to missing events — and F4 says Supplier Review events *may* be missing. A model used to *explore* a log with known instrumentation gaps should not shatter on absent events; S will still replay those cases and let the analyst see the rest of the behavior.
4. Its 4.4 comprehension score and one-page footprint make it a legitimate workshop artifact for U4.
5. Fast alignment (0.018 s) makes it usable for exploratory replay over large logs.
6. F7 is internally coherent: *for exploration*, precision 0.584 is a deliberate choice, not an error.

## Strongest case against Model S

For any enforcement use, S is not merely imprecise — it contradicts mandatory policy. F1 is correct and is the decisive finding. Worse, S is permissive in exactly the places where policy is strict, and *restrictive* in exactly the place where policy is permissive (corrections), which is the opposite of a well-designed noise-tolerant model.

### Concrete undesirable or ambiguous behaviors admitted (traces)

- **S-1 — Both mandatory checks skipped** (violates P2, matches probe B3):
 `Register Dossier, Manager Approval, Release Order`
 Accepted: both check XORs take τ, Supplier and Expedite take τ. An order is approved and released with no credit and no technical assessment. This is the single most serious defect: a conformance run using S would report this trace as perfectly fitting.
- **S-2 — Credit Check skipped, auto-approved** (violates P2):
 `Register Dossier, Technical Review, Auto Approval, Release Order`
 Unassessed credit risk, automatically approved. Directly contradicts P2.
- **S-3 — Expedite Review before both mandatory checks** (violates P4, matches probe B4):
 `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order`
 The parallel operator imposes no ordering among the four, so expediting can precede the checks it is supposed to follow.
- **S-4 — Expedite Review as the *only* review** (violates P2 and P4 simultaneously):
 `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Auto Approval, Release Order`
 An "optional" activity becomes the sole review activity in the case.
- **S-5 — Supplier Review interleaved before any check, on an order of unknown sourcing** (P3/P4 ambiguous):
 `Register Dossier, Supplier Review, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Auto Approval, Release Order`
 Policy never says Supplier Review must follow the mandatory checks, so this is *ambiguous* rather than clearly forbidden — but S cannot express the difference, and S also cannot express P3's conditioning on external sourcing.
- **S-6 — Correction behavior is entirely unrepresentable** (violates P6/P7 by omission):
 `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Correct Dossier, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` (probe B6)
 S *rejects* this: Correct Dossier is not in the model, and Technical Review cannot occur twice. So a policy-legitimate corrective case is reported as nonconforming, while trace S-1 (a genuine violation) is reported as conforming. A model whose false-positive and false-negative sets are both non-empty *and* inverted relative to policy severity is worse than useless for audit.
- **S-7 — Repeated check without correction is rejected for the wrong reason** (probe B8):
 `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Credit Check, Manager Approval, Release Order`
 S rejects this, which coincidentally matches P7 — but the rejection comes from "no activity may repeat," not from "a repeat requires a preceding correction." The right answer for the wrong reason is not a control; it fails as soon as a legitimate repeat (B6) appears.

### Intentional noise tolerance vs. accidental semantic flaw

This distinction matters for the board, because only the second category is a modeling error.

| Behavior | Category | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| Supplier Review optional | **Intentional and defensible** | P3 makes it genuinely conditional; the conditioning attribute is missing, so leaving it optional in control flow is honest — *provided* the condition is enforced elsewhere (S does not do this, but the choice itself is sound). |
| Expedite Review optional | **Intentional and correct** | P4 says optional. |
| Credit Check / Technical Review optional | **Accidental semantic flaw** | P2 says mandatory. Making them skippable is not noise tolerance — it deletes the control. If the motivation was tolerating missing *events*, the correct instrument is a cost-based/probabilistic tolerance at conformance time, or an explicit "missing data" annotation, not an XOR-with-τ in the specification. |
| Expedite Review unordered w.r.t. checks | **Accidental semantic flaw** | P4 states an ordering constraint that the `+` operator simply discards. This is a modeling omission, not tolerance. |
| Correct Dossier absent, no repetition | **Accidental semantic flaw, and the inverse of "permissive"** | S is described as robust; on corrections it is the *strictest* of all three models. This shows the permissiveness was not designed, it was an artifact of using one flat parallel block. |
| Exactly-one approval | **Correct** | Faithful to P5. |

**Verdict on S:** acceptable as a U1/U4 artifact with a prominent "not a control" label; unacceptable for U2/U5 in its current form. Its low precision is *partly* the intentional property F7 describes and *partly* three concrete modeling errors that no amount of "it's only for exploration" excuses, because they misstate the policy to the very business users who rate it 4.4.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

```
X( 8 fully enumerated sequences )
```

**Language of Model C (read precisely).** Exactly eight traces. Nothing else. Every branch is a total order; there is no concurrency, no loop, no silent transition, no optionality except by branch selection.

## Strongest case for Model C

1. **It is unambiguous.** Every accepted trace is written down. For an auditor, "the specification is this list" is an attractive property — no operator semantics to argue about, no hidden interleavings.
2. **It enforces P2 in every branch**: Credit Check and Technical Review appear before approval in all eight. It never admits S-1, S-2 or S-4.
3. **It enforces P4 correctly where it appears**: Expedite Review always follows both mandatory checks and precedes approval. It rejects B4.
4. **It enforces P5**: exactly one approval per branch.
5. **It enforces P7 in effect**: the only repeated check occurs in the branch that contains Correct Dossier; a bare repeat (B8) is rejected.
6. It scores 0.941 precision honestly in the sense that it really does admit very little — this is not a measurement artifact in the direction of flattering C's *restrictiveness*.
7. F8 is a defensible *governance stance* in a high-risk domain: a whitelist of approved variants, with an explicit change-control process for adding branches, is a real control pattern (it is how many payment and safety systems are actually governed).

## Strongest case against Model C

**Branch enumeration does not scale and does not express the policy.** The policy P2–P5 is a product of independent choices: check order (2) × Supplier Review (2) × Expedite Review (2) × approval (2) = **16 base combinations before any correction**, and corrections multiply this further. C enumerates 8, i.e. **less than half of the legal base behavior**, and the omissions are not random:

- Every branch containing Supplier Review or Expedite Review uses **Manager Approval only** — Auto Approval appears in exactly one branch, the bare `CC, TR` one. Nothing in P3/P4/P5 links Auto Approval to the absence of Supplier/Expedite Review. This is a **spurious dependency memorized from the training log**, and it is precisely what F4 reports breaking on holdout.
- The alternate check order (`TR` before `CC`) exists only in branches 2 and 4; branches 5–8 (Expedite, Correction, Auto) silently forbid it. Again spurious: P2 says either order, unconditionally.
- **Probe B5** (`Register, TR, CC, Supplier, Expedite, Auto, Release`) is a legal external/urgent case under P2–P5 and C rejects it. **Probe B2** (`Register, TR, CC, Auto, Release`) is flagged *valid* in the probe table and C rejects it too — C fails a probe that the stakeholders already labeled valid.

**Duplicate labels and impact analysis (F3).** Register Dossier appears 8 times, Credit Check 10, Technical Review 10, Manager Approval 7, Release Order 8. Any change to a single activity — renaming, adding a required predecessor, assigning an owner, attaching a KPI — must be applied consistently to every copy. There is no single node that "is" Credit Check, so ownership, cost annotation, and impact analysis all have to be done by textual search. The reported duplicate count of 33 does not match any obvious by-hand count (excess occurrences over the label set is far larger), which is an independent reason to distrust the submission's reporting pipeline rather than the model itself.

**Correction handling is wrong, not merely limited (F6).** C's single correction branch mandates `Correct Dossier` followed by **both** `Credit Check` and `Technical Review`, in that fixed order. P6 says only the *affected* checks should repeat. So C:
- rejects B6 (correction affecting Technical Review only) — a policy-valid case under one reading;
- rejects a correction affecting Credit Check only;
- rejects `TR, CC` re-check order;
- rejects two corrections in a row (P6 says corrections *may repeat*, C allows exactly one);
- rejects any correction on a case that also had Supplier Review or Expedite Review or Auto Approval.
C therefore has **no loop at all** while the policy explicitly contains repetition. This is the deepest structural defect: an unbounded behavior modeled by a single enumerated instance.

**Holdout degradation is explained, and the explanation is damning.** 0.744 is not noise. F4 identifies the cause: legal combinations of already-known behavior. F8 says "treat unseen as noncompliant until approved" — but these combinations were already approved, in P2–P5, before any log was collected. C is not enforcing policy; it is enforcing the *sampling distribution of the training log*. That is the classic overfitting failure, and it converts every legitimate rare case into an audit finding.

**Is high precision an artifact of memorizing training variants?** Largely yes, and it is testable. Alignment precision counts model-enabled activities not observed in the log at each prefix state. A model whose language is exactly the set of frequent training traces has almost no escaping edges *by construction*; precision → 1 as the model → the log. The diagnostic is that C's precision on the *training* log is 0.941 while its *holdout* fitness is 0.744: the same enumeration that suppresses escaping edges also suppresses unseen legal traces. Two numbers, one mechanism. A precision figure measured on the log the model was fitted to cannot distinguish "precise" from "memorized"; only precision against a **curated negative suite** (F5) can, and none was run.

**Also note C's training fitness is 0.973, not 1.0.** An enumeration of training variants should replay its own training log perfectly. It does not — so either the enumeration omits training variants (self-inconsistent), or the split/preprocessing differs from what was described. Either way the submission's internal consistency is in question.

**Maintainability and operations (F2).** Adding one legitimate combination (say `TR, CC, Supplier, Expedite, Auto`) requires a new branch; adding the full legal product requires ~16 branches before corrections and a combinatorial blow-up with them. Each addition changes the duplicate count, the simplicity score, and the alignment runtime. The model cannot be reviewed by diff.

**Verdict on C:** structurally safe on P2/P4/P5 and genuinely auditable as a whitelist, but it encodes spurious dependencies, cannot express corrections or repetition, rejects behavior the stakeholders themselves call valid (B2, B5), and its headline precision is not independent evidence of quality. Unusable as-is for U2 without a change-control burden the organization is unlikely to sustain; unusable for U1/U3/U4 outright.

# 5. Behavior-Probe Matrix

Reasoning is from the expressions. For Model S: middle block is `+` over four `X(a, τ)` — each activity occurs **at most once**, any interleaving, and `Correct Dossier` is **not in the alphabet**. For Model C: membership is literal string matching against the eight branches.

| Probe | Model S accepts? | Model C accepts? | Policy assessment | Consequence for review |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **B1** `Reg, CC, TR, MA, Rel` | **Yes** — CC and TR take their labeled branch, SR/ER take τ, MA chosen | **Yes** — branch 1 verbatim | valid | Agreement; no discriminative power. Baseline sanity only. |
| **B2** `Reg, TR, CC, AA, Rel` | **Yes** — parallel block allows TR before CC; AA is one of the approval alternatives | **No** — branch 2 uses MA; the only AA branch fixes order `CC, TR`. No branch matches `TR, CC, AA`. | valid | **Decisive against C.** C rejects a stakeholder-confirmed valid trace because it memorized a spurious order↔approval dependency. This is the mechanism behind the 0.744 holdout figure. |
| **B3** `Reg, MA, Rel` | **Yes** — all four XORs take τ | **No** — no branch omits the checks | **forbidden** (violates P2) | **Decisive against S.** S declares a control-free approval fully conforming. Alone this disqualifies S for enforcement. |
| **B4** `Reg, ER, CC, TR, MA, Rel` | **Yes** — `+` imposes no order between ER and the checks | **No** — every ER branch places ER after both checks | **forbidden** (violates P4) | Confirms S loses P4's ordering entirely; confirms C encodes it correctly. |
| **B5** `Reg, TR, CC, SR, ER, AA, Rel` | **Yes** — all four labeled branches taken in that interleaving, AA chosen | **No** — no branch combines SR + ER + AA, and no ER/SR branch uses the `TR, CC` order | plausible valid external/urgent case | **Decisive against C.** Exactly F4's scenario: a legal product of known behavior, rejected. |
| **B6** `Reg, CC, TR, CD, TR, MA, Rel` | **No** — `Correct Dossier` is not in S's alphabet, and `Technical Review` cannot occur twice | **No** — C's correction branch requires `CD, CC, TR`, not `CD, TR` | **policy-dependent**: valid iff the correction affected Technical Review alone (P6); undecidable from the log because the affected-check attribute is absent | **Both models fail, in opposite ways.** S cannot represent corrections at all; C represents exactly one rigid correction shape. Neither surfaces the *decision* the process owner describes in F6. The honest answer for any model is "structurally accepted, flagged as requiring the affected-check attribute to adjudicate." |
| **B7** `Reg, CC, TR, SR, MA, Rel` | **Yes** — SR takes its labeled branch | **Yes** — branch 3 verbatim | valid external case | Agreement; note that S accepts it for no reason connected to external sourcing (P3 is unrepresented in both). |
| **B8** `Reg, CC, TR, CC, MA, Rel` | **No** — `Credit Check` cannot occur twice under `X(CC, τ)` | **No** — no branch repeats CC without CD | **suspicious/forbidden without correction** (P7) | Both reject, but **S rejects for the wrong reason** (blanket no-repetition), which is why it also rejects B6. C rejects for a reason that coincides with P7 but is not stated as a rule — it is a side effect of enumeration. Neither model *implements* P7; a model that genuinely implements P7 must accept B6-style repeats and reject B8-style repeats, which requires a loop guarded by Correct Dossier. |

**Stated uncertainties (rather than guesses).**

- **Silent-transition semantics in S.** `X(a, τ)` is unambiguous for language membership (a occurs or does not), so B1–B8 acceptance for S is certain. What is *not* certain is how the τ's affect alignment **cost** and hence the reported fitness/precision numbers: a skip via τ is usually cost-0, which is exactly why fitness can be inflated. My acceptance verdicts are language-level and do not depend on cost settings; the reported metrics do.
- **Parallel-block interleaving in S.** The `+` operator is interpreted as true concurrency (arbitrary interleaving of the enabled children), which is standard process-tree semantics in PM4Py. Under an alternative "shuffle with additional synchronization" reading the B4/B5 verdicts would change; I flag this as an operator-semantics dependency the board should pin down in writing.
- **Correction repetition bound in C.** C's correction branch is a fixed sequence, so "may repeat" (P6) is definitively not supported — no uncertainty here.
- **B6 policy status** is genuinely undecidable from data, not from model semantics. No model can resolve it; only the missing attribute can.
- **Trailing/lifecycle events and duplicate timestamps** in the real log could change *replay* outcomes for all of these; the probes are treated as clean control-flow sequences.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

Each pathology below is stated with the mechanism, the evidence in this submission, and a falsifiable test (tests are specified in Section 9).

**1. Fitness rewarded by optionalizing mandatory behavior.**
Mechanism: wrapping a mandatory activity in `X(a, τ)` lets alignment skip it at zero cost, so any case missing that activity replays perfectly. Fitness rises, and *policy content falls to zero*. Evidence: S's 0.992/0.981 coexists with acceptance of B3. This is not a subtle bias — it is the metric rewarding the removal of the control. Test: a curated negative suite (below) plus a "mandatory-activity ablation" check that verifies no legal trace omits CC or TR.

**2. Positive-log-only precision (F5).**
Mechanism: alignment precision counts escaping edges relative to *observed* behavior. A model that is the log has ≈0 escaping edges. Therefore precision is maximized by memorization, and "precision" measured this way is a *similarity-to-training-log* statistic, not a policy-conformance statistic. Evidence: C 0.941 on the training log with 0.744 holdout fitness — the same mechanism producing both. Test: measure acceptance rate on a curated **negative** suite; a genuinely precise model accepts 0 of them; C would score well here, S badly, but the point is that neither team ran it, so the 0.584-vs-0.941 gap currently means less than the board thinks.

**3. Train/holdout leakage and split sensitivity.**
Mechanism: no seed, no published split, no published case IDs. With enumerated-variant models, whether a rare-but-legal combination lands in train or holdout swings holdout fitness enormously — C's score is essentially a function of how many external/Auto-Approval/alternate-order cases fell into the 20%. Leakage in the other direction (variant-level rather than case-level splitting, or filtering applied before splitting) would inflate both models. Evidence: F4 explicitly describes the holdout composition driving the result; nothing was published. Test: fixed seed, published case-ID lists, and repeated splits (k≥10) reporting mean ± spread rather than one number.

**4. Missing-event bias.**
Mechanism: F4 says some Supplier Review events *may be missing*. A model that makes SR optional (S) replays those cases perfectly; a model that requires SR in some branches (C) pays alignment cost. So part of S's fitness advantage and part of C's holdout deficit measure **instrumentation quality, not model quality**. Symmetrically, a *correct* future model that conditions SR on the external flag will look worse on fitness than S purely because of the missing events. Test: inject/remove SR events at known rates and observe the fitness delta per model (perturbation test, Section 9).

**5. Duplicated-label effects.**
Mechanism: duplicate labels split traffic across transitions, mechanically depressing token-based generalization; they also enlarge the state space, inflating alignment runtime; and they make simplicity metrics collapse. All three of C's bad numbers (0.412, 0.281, 0.440 s) share this single cause, so the table **triple-counts one property**. Evidence: 33 reported duplicates with an undocumented definition that does not obviously reconcile with the model text. Test: publish the counting definition; recompute generalization on a label-collapsed net.

**6. Silent-transition effects.**
Mechanism: τ's are invisible to token-based measures and free in alignments. S has four τ's in the middle block plus conversion-introduced ones; C has none. Precision and generalization are therefore computed over structurally incomparable nets. Evidence: the 0.584 vs 0.941 and 0.907 vs 0.412 gaps are inflated by this asymmetry, in opposite directions. Test: report metrics both on the raw conversion and on a reduced net, and state which variant was used.

**7. Alignment-cost sensitivity.**
Mechanism: fitness and precision depend on move-on-log / move-on-model costs. Non-default or asymmetric costs (e.g. cheap model-moves) systematically favor permissive models; expensive model-moves favor restrictive ones. Neither team disclosed settings. Evidence: none supplied — that *is* the finding. Test: sweep cost ratios and report metric curves, not points.

**8. Generalization rewarded by excessive permissiveness.**
Mechanism: `generalization_tbr` rises when every transition is frequently visited. A small flower-like model achieves this trivially. S's 0.907 is therefore substantially a restatement of "S is small and permissive," which is already reported as simplicity 0.918. Evidence: S's two highest scores are near-duplicates of each other. This is the mirror image of pathology 5. Test: compute generalization for a deliberate flower model as a control; if the flower beats S, the metric is not discriminating.

**9. Stakeholder-score methodology.**
Mechanism: "informal 1–5" with no protocol. Unknown N, unknown raters, unknown anchors, no blinding, no counterbalancing, likely presented as different-sized diagrams. Comprehension of a *diagram* also depends on the rendering tool. Additionally, users may rate S highly *because* it omits complexity that reflects real policy — comprehension and correctness are anti-correlated here. Evidence: 4.4 vs 1.9 with no method description. Test: documented protocol in Section 9.

**10. Runtime confounding by model size (and by misfit).**
Mechanism: alignment runtime grows with state space *and* with distance from the model. C is slower both because it is bigger and because it fits worse — so runtime partially re-reports holdout fitness (pathology 3/4). Median also hides the tail that matters operationally. Evidence: 0.440 s median with no p95, no hardware, no variant. Test: report p50/p95/p99 on fixed hardware with the alignment variant named, and separately on traces stratified by fitness.

# 7. Third Alternative

## 7.1 Strategy

I propose a **hybrid**: a **balanced POWL backbone** (partial order + explicit choice + explicit loop) that encodes everything the control-flow log *can* justify, plus a **declarative constraint set** and **data-aware post-hoc checks** that encode what control flow alone *cannot* (P3's external condition, P6's affected-check condition, P7's suspicion rule).

Rationale: P2 (unordered mandatory pair) is a partial order, not a choice; P4 (optional, after both checks, before approval) is an optional node with precedence edges; P5 is an XOR; P6 is a **loop**, which is the structure both submissions lack. POWL expresses partial orders and loops natively and converts to a sound WF-net. What POWL cannot express — "Supplier Review iff external", "only affected checks repeat" — is *not silently optionalized away*; it is moved to an explicit, named, testable check that fails loudly when the required attribute is absent.

## 7.2 Compact formal description

```
model_third =
  PO{ Register ≺ FirstPass ≺ CorrectionLoop ≺ Approval ≺ Release }

  FirstPass  = PO{ nodes: CreditCheck, TechnicalReview,
                          OptSupplier = X(SupplierReview, τ),
                          OptExpedite = X(ExpediteReview, τ)
                   edges: CreditCheck ≺ OptExpedite,
                          TechnicalReview ≺ OptExpedite }
               # CC and TR are MANDATORY and mutually unordered (P2)
               # OptSupplier unordered w.r.t. everything in the block (P3, P4-"either side")
               # OptExpedite strictly after both mandatory checks (P4)

  CorrectionLoop = LOOP( τ , Redo )                       # zero or more corrections (P6)
  Redo = PO{ nodes: CorrectDossier,
                    Recheck = X( CreditCheck ,
                                 TechnicalReview ,
                                 PO{CreditCheck, TechnicalReview} ),   # affected subset (P6/F6)
                    OptSupplier2 = X(SupplierReview, τ),
                    OptExpedite2 = X(ExpediteReview, τ)
             edges: CorrectDossier ≺ Recheck,
                    CorrectDossier ≺ OptSupplier2,
                    Recheck        ≺ OptExpedite2 }

  Approval = X( ManagerApproval , AutoApproval )          # exactly one (P5)
```

Key properties by construction: Register first and Release last (P1); CC and TR cannot be skipped anywhere (P2) — there is no τ alternative for them; both check orders allowed unconditionally, in the first pass *and* in rechecks; Supplier Review optional and order-free (P3, pending the attribute); Expedite Review optional but never before both mandatory checks and never after approval (P4); exactly one approval (P5); corrections repeatable with an **explicit three-way choice** of which checks repeat — the decision F6 asks for, made visible in the model (P6); and a repeated check is **only** reachable inside the loop, i.e. only after a `Correct Dossier` (P7), which is exactly the rule, not a side effect.

## 7.3 Self-contained PM4Py code

```python
"""
model_third: balanced POWL backbone + declarative constraints + data-aware post-hoc checks.
Self-contained. Assigns the main model to `model_third`.
"""

import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    Transition, SilentTransition, OperatorPOWL, StrictPartialOrder,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- labels
REGISTER = 'Register Dossier'
CREDIT   = 'Credit Check'
TECH     = 'Technical Review'
SUPPLIER = 'Supplier Review'
EXPEDITE = 'Expedite Review'
CORRECT  = 'Correct Dossier'
MANAGER  = 'Manager Approval'
AUTO     = 'Auto Approval'
RELEASE  = 'Release Order'

MANDATORY_CHECKS = (CREDIT, TECH)
APPROVALS        = (MANAGER, AUTO)


def T(label):
    """Fresh labeled POWL transition (fresh object per use: POWL nodes are identity-based)."""
    return Transition(label=label)


def opt(label):
    """Explicit optionality: X(label, tau). Used ONLY where policy says optional."""
    return OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [T(label), SilentTransition()])


# ------------------------------------------------------- FIRST PASS (P2,P3,P4)
def build_first_pass():
    cc = T(CREDIT)          # mandatory - deliberately NOT wrapped in X(.,tau)
    tr = T(TECH)            # mandatory - deliberately NOT wrapped in X(.,tau)
    sup = opt(SUPPLIER)     # P3: conditional on external sourcing (attribute missing)
    exp = opt(EXPEDITE)     # P4: optional
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[cc, tr, sup, exp])
    po.order.add_edge(cc, exp)   # P4: Expedite after both mandatory checks
    po.order.add_edge(tr, exp)
    # no edge involving `sup`: P3/P4 leave Supplier Review order free
    return po


# ------------------------------------------------- CORRECTION / RECHECK (P6,P7)
def build_recheck_choice():
    """P6: after a correction, only the AFFECTED checks repeat.
    Modelled as an explicit 3-way decision, making the choice auditable (F6)."""
    only_credit = T(CREDIT)
    only_tech   = T(TECH)
    both = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[T(CREDIT), T(TECH)])   # no edges -> either order
    return OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [only_credit, only_tech, both])


def build_redo():
    corr = T(CORRECT)
    recheck = build_recheck_choice()
    sup2 = opt(SUPPLIER)     # a correction may also invalidate the supplier assessment
    exp2 = opt(EXPEDITE)     # expedite may be (re)requested after a correction
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[corr, recheck, sup2, exp2])
    po.order.add_edge(corr, recheck)     # P7: any repeat is preceded by a correction
    po.order.add_edge(corr, sup2)
    po.order.add_edge(recheck, exp2)     # P4 preserved inside the loop
    return po


def build_correction_loop():
    """LOOP(tau, Redo) = tau (Redo tau)*  ->  zero or more corrections (P6 'may repeat')."""
    return OperatorPOWL(Operator.LOOP, [SilentTransition(), build_redo()])


# ------------------------------------------------------------ ROOT (P1, P5)
def build_model_third():
    reg = T(REGISTER)
    first = build_first_pass()
    loop = build_correction_loop()
    approval = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [T(MANAGER), T(AUTO)])   # P5: exactly one
    rel = T(RELEASE)

    root = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[reg, first, loop, approval, rel])
    root.order.add_edge(reg, first)        # P1
    root.order.add_edge(first, loop)
    root.order.add_edge(loop, approval)    # all checks precede approval (P2/P4)
    root.order.add_edge(approval, rel)     # P1/P5
    return root


model_third = build_model_third()

# -------------------------------------------------- conversion & soundness
net_third, im_third, fm_third = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_third)
# pm4py.check_soundness(net_third, im_third, fm_third, print_diagnostics=True)
```

## 7.4 Declarative dictionary and external checks

The POWL backbone is the *control-flow* half. The following two artifacts carry the constraints control flow cannot express. They are part of the specification, not optional extras — a deployment that ships the net without them is not `model_third`.

```python
# --------------------------------------------------- (a) DECLARE constraint set
# Redundant-by-construction constraints are kept ON PURPOSE: they are the
# regression suite that detects a future edit silently weakening the backbone.
declare_third = {
    "existence":   {REGISTER: {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},
                    CREDIT:   {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},   # P2
                    TECH:     {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},   # P2
                    RELEASE:  {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0}},
    "init":        {REGISTER: {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0}},  # P1
    "exactly_one": {REGISTER: {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},
                    RELEASE:  {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0}},
    "noncoexistence": {                                              # P5
        (MANAGER, AUTO): {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0}},
    "precedence": {                                                  # P2/P4 orderings
        (CREDIT,   MANAGER):  {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},
        (TECH,     MANAGER):  {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},
        (CREDIT,   AUTO):     {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},
        (TECH,     AUTO):     {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},
        (CREDIT,   EXPEDITE): {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},

        (CREDIT,   RELEASE):  {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},
        (TECH,     RELEASE):  {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0}},
    "response":   {                                                  # P1/P5
        (REGISTER, RELEASE):  {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0}},
    "nonsuccession": {                                               # P4: no post-approval review
        (MANAGER, EXPEDITE):  {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},
        (AUTO,    EXPEDITE):  {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},
        (MANAGER, CREDIT):    {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},
        (AUTO,    CREDIT):    {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},
        (MANAGER, TECH):      {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0},
        (AUTO,    TECH):      {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0}},
}
# usage: pm4py.conformance_declare(log, declare_third)   # API name may vary by version;
#        otherwise evaluate the dictionary with the rule checks in (b), which are exact.


# ------------------------------------- (b) data-aware post-hoc policy checks
def policy_checks(trace_labels, case_attrs=None):
    """Checks that the control-flow model provably cannot carry.

    trace_labels : list[str]   activity labels in time order
    case_attrs   : dict        may contain 'is_external' (bool) and
                               'affected_checks' (subset of {CREDIT, TECH}) per correction

    Returns (violations, undecidable). `undecidable` items are NOT passes:
    they are unresolved obligations caused by missing attributes.
    """
    case_attrs = case_attrs or {}
    violations, undecidable = [], []

    # --- P7: every repeated mandatory check must be preceded by a Correct Dossier
    seen, corrections_so_far = {}, 0
    for a in trace_labels:
        if a == CORRECT:
            corrections_so_far += 1
        if a in MANDATORY_CHECKS:
            seen[a] = seen.get(a, 0) + 1
            if seen[a] > 1 and corrections_so_far == 0:
                violations.append(f"P7: repeated {a} with no preceding {CORRECT}")

    # --- P5: exactly one approval
    n_appr = sum(trace_labels.count(x) for x in APPROVALS)
    if n_appr != 1:
        violations.append(f"P5: {n_appr} approval events, expected exactly 1")

    # --- P3: Supplier Review iff externally sourced
    has_sup = SUPPLIER in trace_labels
    if 'is_external' in case_attrs:
        if case_attrs['is_external'] and not has_sup:
            violations.append("P3: external order without Supplier Review")
        if not case_attrs['is_external'] and has_sup:
            violations.append("P3: internal order with Supplier Review")
    else:
        undecidable.append("P3: external/internal attribute absent - cannot adjudicate "
                           f"Supplier Review {'presence' if has_sup else 'absence'}")

    # --- P6: only the AFFECTED checks repeat after a correction
    affected = case_attrs.get('affected_checks')
    n_corr = trace_labels.count(CORRECT)
    if n_corr:
        if affected is None:
            undecidable.append(f"P6: {n_corr} correction(s) but no affected-check attribute - "
                               "recheck scope unverifiable")
        else:
            # affected: list of sets, one per correction, in order
            idx = [i for i, a in enumerate(trace_labels) if a == CORRECT] + [len(trace_labels)]
            for k in range(n_corr):
                seg = trace_labels[idx[k] + 1: idx[k + 1]]
                redone = {a for a in seg if a in MANDATORY_CHECKS}
                expected = set(affected[k]) if k < len(affected) else set()
                if redone != expected:
                    violations.append(
                        f"P6: correction #{k+1} affected {sorted(expected)} "
                        f"but rechecked {sorted(redone)}")
    return violations, undecidable


def review_case(trace_labels, case_attrs=None):
    """Combined verdict: control-flow conformance is necessary but not sufficient."""
    violations, undecidable = policy_checks(trace_labels, case_attrs)
    if violations:
        return "NONCONFORMING", violations, undecidable
    if undecidable:
        return "CONFORMING-PENDING-DATA", violations, undecidable
    return "CONFORMING", violations, undecidable
```

## 7.5 Explicit semantics

| Requirement | How `model_third` realizes it | Guarantee |
|---|---|---|
| **Mandatory checks (P2)** | `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` are bare `Transition`s inside `FirstPass`, with **no τ alternative** anywhere. Both must fire before the loop, hence before approval. | Hard structural guarantee: no accepted trace omits either check. B3 and S-1/S-2/S-4 are rejected by the net itself, not by a side check. |
| **Check order (P2)** | No edge between `cc` and `tr` in the partial order; the recheck `both` node likewise has no edge. | Both orders accepted **unconditionally**, in first pass and in rechecks — removing C's spurious order↔approval coupling. |
| **Optional Supplier Review (P3)** | `X(Supplier Review, τ)` in the first pass and again inside `Redo`; unordered with respect to every other node. | Control flow permits presence or absence, in any position within the check block. The *condition* (external sourcing) is enforced by `policy_checks`, which returns an explicit `undecidable` when the attribute is missing — never a silent pass. |
| **Expedite Review ordering (P4)** | Optional node with incoming edges from **both** mandatory checks; the whole block precedes `Approval`, which precedes `Release`. Inside `Redo`, an edge `Recheck ≺ OptExpedite2` preserves the same rule. DECLARE `nonsuccession(approval, Expedite)` restates it as a regression guard. | B4 rejected structurally. Expedite can never precede a mandatory check, nor follow approval. Expedite may occur before or after Supplier Review (P4) since they are mutually unordered. |
| **Exactly one approval (P5)** | `X(Manager Approval, Auto Approval)` as a single node between the loop and Release; `noncoexistence(MA, AA)` and the `n_appr != 1` check as guards. | Exactly one approval event, of either kind, on every accepted trace — independent of which reviews occurred, which is precisely the coupling C got wrong. |
| **Correction / recheck (P6, P7)** | `LOOP(τ, Redo)` gives **zero or more** corrections. `Redo` forces `Correct Dossier` first, then an explicit `XOR(credit-only, tech-only, both-in-either-order)`. | Repetition of a mandatory check is reachable **only** inside `Redo`, i.e. only after a `Correct Dossier` — P7 becomes a structural property, so B8 is rejected and B6 is accepted. The affected-subset decision is a *named XOR node* in the model, which is exactly the transparency F6 asks for; the branch taken is recoverable from alignments and can be cross-checked against the attribute once it exists. |
| **Register first / Release last (P1)** | Root partial-order edges `reg ≺ first ≺ loop ≺ approval ≺ rel`. | Structural. |

## 7.6 Limitations caused by the missing attributes

I state these as **open obligations**, not as resolved design choices, because the honest failure mode here is to quietly optionalize and call it done.

1. **External/internal flag (P3) is absent.** Consequence: in control flow, `model_third` accepts both the presence and the absence of Supplier Review on *every* case. It therefore cannot, by itself, detect either (a) an external order that skipped supplier vetting — a real compliance risk — or (b) an internal order that ran an unnecessary review — a cost/efficiency issue. `policy_checks` returns `CONFORMING-PENDING-DATA` for every such case rather than `CONFORMING`, so the gap is visible in every report. **Until the attribute is joined in, P3 is unenforced and must be listed as an uncovered control in the audit scope.** This limitation is shared by Models S and C; C merely *appears* to handle it by memorizing which training variants had SR.
2. **Affected-check attribute (P6) is absent.** Consequence: `model_third` accepts credit-only, tech-only, and both-checks rechecks after any correction. It cannot verify that the *right* subset was repeated — so B6 is accepted structurally but returns `CONFORMING-PENDING-DATA`. A correction that affected only Credit Check but was followed by a Technical Review recheck (wasteful) or by no Credit Check recheck (a genuine control gap) is indistinguishable today. The explicit XOR node at least makes the *observed* subset a first-class, reportable quantity, which is the precondition for adding the check later.
3. **F4's possibly missing Supplier Review events** interact with limitation 1: absent SR events and legitimately-absent SR are indistinguishable without the flag. `model_third` deliberately does **not** buy fitness by pretending this is resolved; instead, Section 9's perturbation test quantifies how much of any fitness figure is attributable to instrumentation.
4. **No repetition of `Register Dossier` or `Release Order`** is modeled; if the real log contains restarts or cancellations, they are out of scope and will surface as nonconformance — I prefer that to widening the model on speculation.
5. **No frequencies/probabilities** are attached, so `model_third` is not yet usable for U3 (simulation) without a stochastic annotation step.
6. **Loop cardinality is unbounded.** P6 says corrections "may repeat" without a limit. If the business has an escalation threshold (e.g. more than two corrections requires management sign-off), that is a further rule to add to `policy_checks`; it is currently unmodeled and I flag it as an open question for the process owner.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

SEED = 20260806
random.seed(SEED)

def log_fingerprint(df):
    payload = df.sort_values(['case:concept:name', 'time:timestamp']) \
                [['case:concept:name', 'concept:name', 'time:timestamp']] \
                .to_csv(index=False).encode()
    return hashlib.sha256(payload).hexdigest()

def split_cases(df, seed=SEED, train_frac=0.8):
    cases = sorted(df['case:concept:name'].unique())      # sorted -> deterministic
    rnd = random.Random(seed)
    rnd.shuffle(cases)
    k = int(round(train_frac * len(cases)))
    train_ids, test_ids = set(cases[:k]), set(cases[k:])
    with open(f'split_{seed}.json', 'w') as f:                 # PUBLISH THIS
        json.dump({'seed': seed, 'train': sorted(train_ids),
                   'test': sorted(test_ids)}, f, indent=2)
    return (df[df['case:concept:name'].isin(train_ids)],
            df[df['case:concept:name'].isin(test_ids)])
```

**Repeat over k≥10 seeds** and report mean ± min/max for every metric. A single split is not a measurement.

## E1 — Disaggregated metric suite (never averaged)

```python
def evaluate(model_or_net, log, name):
    if hasattr(model_or_net, 'order') or hasattr(model_or_net, 'operator'):
        net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_or_net)
    else:
        net, im, fm = model_or_net
    return {
        'model': name,
        'fitness':        pm4py.fitness_alignments(log, net, im, fm),
        'precision':      pm4py.precision_alignments(log, net, im, fm),
        'generalization': pm4py.generalization_tbr(log, net, im, fm),
        'simplicity':     pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm, variant="arc_degree"),
        'places': len(net.places), 'transitions': len(net.transitions), 'arcs': len(net.arcs),
        'silent': sum(1 for t in net.transitions if t.label is None),
        'duplicate_labels': _duplicate_label_count(net),
    }

def _duplicate_label_count(net):
    """PUBLISHED DEFINITION: number of visible transitions minus number of distinct
    visible labels (i.e. excess copies). Silent transitions excluded."""
    labels = [t.label for t in net.transitions if t.label is not None]
    return len(labels) - len(set(labels))
```

Report the four quality metrics **separately, per split, per model**, together with size, silent-transition count, and the duplicate count *under the published definition* — this closes the "definition undocumented" gap.

```python
def to_log(cases):
    rows = []
    for cid, trace in cases:
        for i, act in enumerate(trace):
            rows.append({'case:concept:name': cid, 'concept:name': act,
                         'time:timestamp': pd.Timestamp('2026-01-01') + pd.Timedelta(minutes=i)})
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(pd.DataFrame(rows),
                                  case_id='case:concept:name',
                                  activity_key='concept:name',
                                  timestamp_key='time:timestamp')

def acceptance_report(net, im, fm, cases):
    """Per-trace: perfectly fitting or not. Report counts; do not average with anything."""
    log = to_log(cases)
    diag = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(log, net, im, fm)
    return [(cases[i][0], d['fitness'] == 1.0) for i, d in enumerate(diag)]
```

**Two headline figures, reported separately and never blended:**
- **False-acceptance rate** = fraction of `NEGATIVES` accepted (target **0**);
- **False-rejection rate** = fraction of `POSITIVES_REQUIRED` rejected (target **0**).

These replace training-log precision as the enforcement-relevant measurement.

## E4 — Missing-event and duplicate-event perturbation

Quantifies pathology #4 (missing-event bias) directly.

```python
def perturb(df, activity, mode, rate, seed=SEED):
    """mode='drop' removes `activity` events with probability `rate`;
       mode='duplicate' repeats them."""
    rnd = random.Random(seed)
    out = []
    for _, row in df.iterrows():
        if row['concept:name'] == activity:
            r = rnd.random()
            if mode == 'drop' and r < rate:
                continue
            out.append(row)
            if mode == 'duplicate' and r < rate:
                out.append(row.copy())
        else:
            out.append(row)
    return pd.DataFrame(out)
```

Run for `Supplier Review` (F4's suspected gap), `Credit Check`, and `Correct Dossier`, at rates 0.05/0.10/0.20. Report **Δfitness per model per rate**. Interpretation rule: a model whose fitness is *insensitive* to dropping a mandatory check is not tolerant — it is not enforcing that check. This test converts pathology #1 into a number.

## E6 — Alignment-cost sensitivity

```python
from pm4py.algo.conformance.alignments.petri_net import algorithm as ali

def cost_sweep(log, net, im, fm, model_move_costs=(1, 2, 5, 10)):
    results = {}
    for c in model_move_costs:
        params = {ali.Variants.VERSION_STATE_EQUATION_A_STAR.value.Parameters
                    .PARAM_MODEL_COST_FUNCTION:
                      {t: (0 if t.label is None else c) for t in net.transitions},
                  ali.Variants.VERSION_STATE_EQUATION_A_STAR.value.Parameters
                    .PARAM_STD_SYNC_COST: 0}
        results[c] = ali.apply(log, net, im, fm, parameters=params,
                               variant=ali.Variants.VERSION_STATE_EQUATION_A_STAR)
    return results
```

Report fitness as a **curve over the cost ratio**, and state the operating point used for headline numbers. Note explicitly whether silent-transition moves are free; if they are, S's fitness advantage is partly an artifact and must be labelled as such.

## E8 — Stakeholder comprehension with a documented protocol

Replaces the "informal 1–5" figure:

- **Participants:** ≥12 per model, drawn from three named roles (clerk, compliance officer, IT owner), randomly assigned, **counterbalanced order**.
- **Materials:** identical rendering tool, identical layout algorithm, identical legend, identical page size.
- **Task-based measurement, not opinion:** each participant answers 8 comprehension items — e.g. "can this process approve an order without a credit check?", "may the technical review precede the credit check?", "what must happen before a check may repeat?" — scored for **correctness against P1–P7**, plus time-to-answer.
- **Also collect** a subjective 1–5 rating, but report it separately from the objective score.
- **Report** per-item accuracy, inter-rater agreement, N, and confidence intervals.
- **Key contrast:** a model that scores high on subjective clarity but low on objective comprehension correctness is *misleading*, which is worse than merely complex. I expect this contrast to be the most informative result for Model S.

## E9 — Runtime, properly measured

Report p50/p95/p99 alignment time per case on fixed hardware, with the alignment variant named, stratified by whether the trace fits. Report separately for the negative suite (worst case for restrictive models).

## E10 — Decision rule: rejection despite good aggregate metrics

A model is **rejected for compliance use regardless of any aggregate score** if any of the following holds:

1. **Any** trace in the curated negative suite is accepted (false-acceptance rate > 0). *Model S fails on N1–N5 as analyzed; this alone is disqualifying.*
2. **Any** trace in `POSITIVES_REQUIRED` is rejected. *Model C fails on P_b, P_c, P_d, P_e, P_f.*
3. A mandatory activity (P2) is skippable — verified structurally, not statistically.
4. Holdout fitness < 0.95 **in any stratum with ≥30 cases**, unless the shortfall is traced to a documented data-quality defect rather than a model defect.
5. The model is not sound.
6. Metrics are not reproducible from the published seed, split, preprocessing and cost function.
7. Perturbation test shows fitness is insensitive to dropping a mandatory check.
8. The duplicate-label count under the published definition exceeds an agreed maintainability threshold *and* no single-source-of-truth mapping from activity to owner exists.

Conversely, a model may **not** be approved merely because it wins on a weighted average. The board should record that criteria 1 and 2 are *lexicographically prior* to all scalar metrics.

# 10. Governance Recommendation

**Neither Model S nor Model C may be used for conformance enforcement.** This is not a close call, and it does not depend on any of the disputed numbers.

| Use case | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Exploration (U1)** | **Model S — conditionally approved**, with a mandatory header on every artifact: *"Descriptive map. Does not encode mandatory controls. Not a conformance model."* It must not be published on any governance page that also hosts compliance artifacts. |
| **Stakeholder communication (U4)** | **Model S may be shown only alongside a written statement of P1–P7**, because in its current form it actively miscommunicates P2 and P4 to the very audience that rates it 4.4. Preferred replacement: a rendering of `model_third`, re-tested under E8. |
| **Conformance enforcement / audit (U2)** | **Neither S nor C.** S accepts B3/N1–N5 (control-free approvals). C rejects B2 and B5, which stakeholders confirm are valid, and cannot represent corrections at all. Use `model_third` **after** it clears the conditions below. |
| **Runtime guidance (U5)** | **Neither**, for the same reasons; plus C's latency profile is unsuitable. |
| **Simulation (U3)** | **None of the three** without a stochastic annotation step; no model here carries branching probabilities. |

**Retirement decisions.**

- **Model C: retire as a model; retain as a test asset.** Its eight branches are a useful *positive* regression suite — any successor must accept all eight. But it must not be maintained as a specification: the combinatorial growth (F2), the duplicate labels (F3), and the absence of any loop make it unmaintainable, and its precision advantage is not independent evidence of quality (Section 4).
- **Model S: retire from any governance context; retain as an exploratory artifact** under the label above. Its three semantic flaws (optional mandatory checks, lost Expedite ordering, no corrections) should be corrected even in the exploratory version, because the fixes cost nothing in comprehensibility.
- **Reject F7 and F8 as deployment arguments.** F7 is valid *only* if S is fenced off from enforcement — which requires the labeling above, not just an intention. F8 conflates "unseen in the training log" with "not authorized by policy"; P2–P5 authorized the combinations C rejects before any data was collected.

**Conditions `model_third` must meet before deployment.**

1. **E0 reproducibility package published** — seed, split case IDs, preprocessing code, PM4Py version, alignment cost function. Blocking.
2. **E3 passed with false-acceptance rate = 0 and false-rejection rate = 0** on the curated suites, with the suites signed off by Compliance and the Process Owner.
3. **E7 soundness confirmed** and the four structural invariants asserted as automated tests in CI.
4. **E2 stratified holdout fitness ≥ 0.95 in every stratum with ≥30 cases**, over ≥10 seeds.
5. **E4 perturbation test** demonstrating that dropping `Credit Check` or `Technical Review` events measurably reduces fitness — proving the controls are live.
6. **E8 comprehension study** showing objective comprehension correctness at least equal to Model S's, using the documented protocol. If `model_third` is harder to read, ship a simplified *view* for workshops with an explicit "view, not specification" label — never a second, weaker model.
7. **Data remediation plan with owners and dates** for the two missing attributes. Until the external/internal flag exists, **P3 is an uncovered control** and must appear as such in the audit scope, with `CONFORMING-PENDING-DATA` cases counted and reported, not silently folded into "conforming." Same for the affected-check attribute and P6.
8. **Change control**: the POWL source, the DECLARE dictionary, and `policy_checks` are versioned together in one repository; any change requires re-running E3/E7 and Process Owner sign-off. The declarative constraints that are redundant with the backbone are retained deliberately as regression detectors.
9. **Open questions resolved with the Process Owner** before go-live: (a) may a correction interrupt the first pass (trace A5)? (b) is there a maximum number of corrections? (c) may Supplier Review repeat after a correction independently of the checks (trace A6)? Each answer either confirms the current semantics or triggers a specific, small structural edit.
10. **Two-phase rollout**: run `model_third` in *shadow mode* against live cases for one reporting period, comparing its findings to current manual audit outcomes, before it is used to generate binding findings. Any disagreement is triaged as either a model defect or a genuine finding, and the triage log becomes the next round's negative/positive suites.

# 11. Dissenting View

**The strongest rebuttal to my own recommendation.**

This review rejects both delivered models and replaces them with a design that has never been measured. That is a governance risk dressed as rigor. Model C is in front of the board *now*, it enforces every mandatory control, it rejects every one of my twelve negative traces, and its weakness — rejecting legal-but-unseen combinations — produces **false positives**, which surface as reviewable audit findings that a human closes in minutes. Model S's weakness produces **false negatives**: violations that are never seen by anyone. Between a model that over-reports and one that under-reports, a compliance function should take over-reporting every time. My own decision rule concedes this: C fails only on criterion 2, and criterion 2 is a rule I wrote. F8 is therefore not a category error but a deliberate whitelist posture with a change-control process attached — a recognized control pattern in payments and safety-critical systems, where "unseen until approved" is the *point*. Meanwhile `model_third` introduces a loop with an unbounded correction count and a three-way recheck choice that *no attribute can currently validate*; I have replaced C's over-restriction with a new, unmeasured region of permissiveness (traces A5 and A6 show I know this) and labeled it `CONFORMING-PENDING-DATA`, which in practice becomes a queue nobody works. Ten preconditions and a shadow-mode rollout will take quarters. During that time the organization has no enforcement model at all. Approving C today with a documented backlog of branches to add is faster, safer, and reversible.

**What would change my decision.**

I would approve Model C for enforcement now if: (1) the E5 unseen-combination test showed C rejecting fewer than ~5% of holdout cases, and the process owner confirmed each rejection is genuinely reviewable rather than routine — i.e. the false-positive volume is operationally absorbable; (2) a change-control process with a named owner and a demonstrated turnaround of days, not quarters, for adding branches existed in practice, not on paper; and (3) the correction gap were closed, since C cannot represent repeated corrections at all — this is the one defect no branch-addition backlog fixes at acceptable cost, and it is why I do not expect condition (3) to be met. Conversely, I would soften my rejection of Model S only if the E4 perturbation test showed its permissiveness is confined to activities with documented instrumentation gaps — which B3 already refutes, since Credit Check and Technical Review are not missing from the log. Finally, if `model_third` fails E3 (any negative accepted or any required positive rejected), I would withdraw it immediately and re-open the C-with-change-control option rather than defend the design.
