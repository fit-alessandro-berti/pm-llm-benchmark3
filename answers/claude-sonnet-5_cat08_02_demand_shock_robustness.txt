# 1. Stress scenarios

**S1 – Demand-mix shift toward Urgent + Complex-panel** (the observed peak pattern: Urgent 18%29%, Complex 13%20%). Volume may or may not rise much, but the *stress mix* changes: more assays per case (Complex-panel = multi-assay + mandatory medical validation), more urgent-bypass fragmentation of batches.

**S2 – Assay-machine degradation/outage** (e.g., M2's known higher failure rate materializes, or M1 goes down for maintenance). Effective assay capacity drops sharply while repeat-assay demand rises (Repeat-after-failure cases already consume assay capacity twice and cluster after M2 use / night shifts).

**S3 – Medical validator / specialist-review absence** (the documented every-second-Friday specialist gap, or a flu-season validator shortage coinciding with a Complex-panel surge). Technical work continues but the validation "gate" narrows.

**S4 – Courier arrival compression/delay** (10:00/17:00 spikes get worse, or a delayed run pushes two waves into one). Intake and pre-analytical check absorb a burst rather than a smooth arrival stream.

**S5 – Compound "winter wave"** (S1 + S3 + S4 simultaneously): mix shift toward Urgent/Complex, a Friday specialist gap, and courier bunching all at once — the realistic worst case that historical averages hide.

# 2. Bottleneck-migration analysis

**S1 (mix shift).** Urgent-bypass-of-batch-wait rises to 58%, fragmenting Prep-bench and Assay scheduling. Assay utilization goes 71%98% (peak data), and the AssayRepeat-assay share roughly doubles (6.6%13.7% of assay throughput), which recycles work back through the same constrained machines. This pushes the bottleneck to **Technical validation**, which crosses 100% utilization (76%101%) with queue 3.5h16h. Because Complex-panel is 3.1× more likely to need Medical validation and 2.4× more likely to need Specialist review, the extra Complex load doesn't stop at Technical validation — it cascades into **Medical validation** (58%93%, queue 2h22h) and **Specialist review** (43%87%, queue 5h36h). Net effect: even though Urgent is "prioritized" upstream, it queues behind Complex-panel at the shared validation gates, and the 18h SLA breaks downstream of where any dispatch rule was applied. Relieving Assay alone (e.g., I3) without touching validation just moves the queue two steps to the right.

**S2 (assay degradation/outage).** If M2 is lost, all volume — Urgent bypass and Routine batches alike — competes for M1. Queue at Assay (already 4h18h at peak-average) spikes non-linearly because arrivals become bursty once the surviving machine catches up (batch-wait queue of 6h14h stacks up further). Repeat-assay rate increases further because Repeat-after-failure cases already cluster around M2 use — if M2 itself is the failure, the remaining M1 volume includes proportionally more first-time failures needing repeats too, tightening the loop. The bottleneck stays at Assay but its *severity* changes the calculus for I3: a **static** M1-dedication rule assumed two machines exist; with one machine down, dedicating M1 to Urgent/Complex only just starves Routine completely (Routine already at 62h median, 120h p90 at peak) and does nothing to fix aggregate capacity. Downstream, Technical validation receives arrivals in bursts rather than a smooth stream, so its queue spikes even if average utilization looks unchanged.

**S3 (validator/specialist absence).** Technical validation clears normally, but Medical validation and Specialist review queues balloon beyond the already-fragile peak baseline (22h/36h). Complex-panel cycle time (already 111h median/210h p90 at peak) is hit hardest because it structurally requires both gates. Urgent cases that need Medical validation (a minority, but present) also get trapped behind Complex-panel WIP because validators are a shared pool. As turnaround slips past SLA, physicians call in about overdue Urgent/Complex results — this reroutes clerk time into "explaining delays," which the log already shows depresses effective intake capacity (recollection-loop variant) — so the bottleneck **migrates backward** into Registration/Pre-analytical check even though nothing changed there physically. This is a second-order migration purely from the disruption's social/communication load.

**S4 (courier compression/delay).** Burst arrivals hit Receive sample  Pre-analytical check, whose queue already reaches 9.5h at peak. Higher local congestion increases contact/handling errors, pushing more volume into Manual accession correction — a 2-person-only pool already at 91% utilization / 14h queue at peak. Correction backlog delays Test preparation entry (median 5h/16h edge), and the Request-recollection loop (42h/60h round-trip) removes samples from the pipeline for days, disproportionately hurting Repeat-after-failure and Urgent orders whose SLA windows can't absorb a 60h side-loop. If couriers are *delayed* rather than compressed, the opposite risk appears: idle windows at Prep benches encourage staff to wait for a "full batch," which — combined with I2-style larger batch sizes — increases average sample dwell time before assay even starts.

**S5 (compound).** Each single-scenario migration path activates together: Assay/Technical-validation congestion (S1/S2 pattern) collides with a Friday specialist gap (S3) and courier bunching (S4). The gates most exposed by all four scenarios simultaneously are **Technical validation, Medical validation, and Specialist review** — they are the structural choke points every scenario eventually migrates to, regardless of where it starts.

# 3. Robust redesign package

These are proposed as **structural, always-on** changes because they reduce load or variance at points common to all scenarios, rather than optimizing one case type.

**R1. Early pre-analytical defect triage (modified I6), 2h SLA.**
Rejects/flags bad samples before they consume Manual accession correction time or Prep-bench slots. Robust because recollection loops (S4) and correction-specialist scarcity (only 2 people, hit in S3/S4) are stressed in *every* scenario, not just courier-related ones. *Assumption:* triage can reliably detect defects from early metadata/visual check without a full pre-analytical pass. *Failure mode:* false rejections raise the Cancel-order/Request-recollection edge volume, adding load exactly where I6 tries to save it — must be monitored, not assumed correct.

**R2. Courier slot smoothing (I7), applied year-round not just at peak.**
Reduces burst variance into Pre-analytical check for all scenarios (directly fixes S4, buffers S1/S3 by preventing simultaneous stress). *Assumption:* collection sites/couriers can adhere to revised slot windows. *Failure mode:* if external sites ignore the schedule, the intended smoothing doesn't materialize and Registration/Pre-analytical still spike — need a fallback surge-queue policy (Section 4).

**R3. Conditional registration automation (modified I8) — only for orders with complete digital metadata, with a mandatory light manual spot-check.**
Registration is rarely the primary bottleneck (68–85% utilization) so automating it is low-risk on its own, but per the conflict hint it *will* push volume faster into Pre-analytical check and Assay. Robust only when paired with R1 (triage) and R2 (smoothing) so the freed capacity doesn't just relocate the queue. *Assumption:* "complete digital metadata" correlates with low downstream error rate — must be validated, not assumed.

**R4. Dynamic, capped priority (modified I1) instead of a strict Urgent-first rule.**
Urgent gets priority at Prep/Assay only up to a WIP cap for Routine/Complex-panel; once Routine or Complex-panel WIP-age exceeds a threshold, the rule yields a slot to prevent starvation (per the I1 conflict hint). Robust across S1/S2/S5 because it explicitly trades off the starvation risk instead of ignoring it. *Assumption:* real-time WIP-age visibility exists to enforce caps. *Failure mode:* under S2 (machine outage) even capped priority cannot create capacity that doesn't exist — it only reallocates scarcity.

**R5. Dynamic M1/M2 allocation (modified I3) with an outage fallback to pooled, class-reserved dispatch.**
Default: M1 favors Urgent/Complex when both machines are healthy. Automatic fallback: if either machine's downtime/error rate crosses a threshold, revert to a single pooled queue with a fixed reservation (e.g., 30% of slots) for Urgent/Complex, rest FIFO — preventing the total-Routine-starvation failure mode identified for S2. *Assumption:* machine health is monitored in near-real time. *Failure mode:* frequent flapping between modes could itself destabilize scheduling; needs hysteresis (don't switch modes more than once per shift).

**R6. Flexible cross-trained surge pool (modified I4) — activated, not permanent.**
Cross-train technical validators for accession correction, but only pull them into correction duty when correction-queue thresholds are breached, and cap the number reassigned so Technical validation (already prone to hitting >100% utilization) is not itself starved. This directly addresses the I4 conflict hint by making the trade explicit and bounded rather than a blanket reassignment.

Why this package is "robust": each element reduces either (a) variance feeding the shared validation gates, or (b) starvation risk from rigid prioritization — the two failure patterns common to all four scenarios — while explicitly naming the downstream station each could overload.

# 4. Scenario-specific policies

| Trigger condition | Policy activated | Off-ramp |
|---|---|---|
| Rolling 24h Urgent+Complex share > ~40% of registrations (vs ~31% baseline) | Activate R4 capped-priority reservation at Prep/Assay; activate I5 evening medical-validation shift | Deactivate when share <35% for 48h |
| Assay machine downtime >2h OR error/repeat rate on a machine >2× its 7-day baseline | Switch R5 to pooled single-machine mode with fixed Urgent/Complex reservation; **suspend** any active large-batch policy (I2) to avoid worsening fragmentation; temporarily halt Urgent bypass-of-batch-wait (forces short controlled batches instead) | Restore dual-machine dedicated mode once downtime cleared and error rate normal for 24h |
| Medical validation + Specialist review combined queue trend projects >12h median (Little's-law projection from arrival rate and known specialist absence calendar) | Activate I5 surge shift; for **non-urgent** Complex-panel cases only, activate I10 outsourcing to partner lab (12h transport is acceptable only when remaining SLA budget >24h) | Deactivate I10 routing once in-house queue <10h for 2 consecutive days; never route Urgent cases to I10 |
| Courier arrival bunching index (share of daily volume arriving within any 30-min window) exceeds threshold, or Pre-analytical queue >6h at either daily peak | Enforce R2 smoothed slots strictly; temporarily pull R6 flex staff into accession correction | Deactivate when Pre-analytical queue <3h at both peaks for 2 days |
| Two or more triggers active simultaneously (S5 compound) | All above policies active concurrently **plus** explicit WIP cap on new Complex-panel intake (soft diversion to partner lab for non-urgent overflow) to prevent validator-gate collapse | Step down one policy at a time as each trigger clears, monitored daily |

Note: I2 (batch size 24) and R4/strict-Urgent modes should **never** run simultaneously — large Routine batches plus Urgent priority reservation together are what fragments schedules and inflates Routine p90 the most (per I2 conflict hint). Adaptive batch sizing (smaller batches when Urgent+Complex share is high, larger only when share <20% and machines healthy) replaces a blanket I2.

# 6. Residual vulnerabilities

- **Specialist review remains a structural single point of failure.** Even with I10 outsourcing and surge shifts, the every-second-Friday absence plus 2.4× Complex-panel hold-rate means some Complex-panel/Urgent-adjacent cases will still queue behind an irreducible one-person capacity limit on those Fridays.
- **Self-inflicted vulnerability from R6:** cross-training technical validators to help accession correction, if over-used, can push Technical validation utilization (already prone to exceeding 100% at peak) into the exact overload the redesign is trying to avoid. This is a direct trade created by the redesign itself, not an external shock.
- **Self-inflicted vulnerability from R3:** conditional automation of registration could reduce the human "catch" of malformed orders that previously got fixed informally at registration, silently increasing volume into Pre-analytical check/Manual accession correction if "complete metadata"  "correct metadata."
- **Dependence on real-time monitoring for all dynamic/triggered policies (R4, R5, and Section 4 triggers).** If process-mining/dashboard data lags, triggers fire late, and the system reverts to the same rigid failure modes the static interventions had (e.g., I1 starving Routine, I3 starving Routine during an undetected outage).
- **Outsourcing dependency (I10)** ties Complex-panel resilience to a partner lab's own capacity and to courier reliability — the very disruption modeled in S4 — so it is not a fully independent mitigation for S5.
- **Behavioral drift risk:** under sustained pressure, staff may revert to habitual large-batch behavior or ignore capped priority rules ("just push the urgent ones through") — adaptive batch sizing and R4 require ongoing enforcement/monitoring, not just policy publication.

# 7. Minimum monitoring dashboard

**Leading indicators (predictive, check multiple times daily during peak):**
- Oldest-sample age at Pre-analytical check queue — warn >5h, critical 9.5h (peak-observed median).
- Manual accession correction queue depth relative to 2-specialist capacity — warn >6h, critical 14h.
- Rolling 24h repeat-assay rate — warn >10%, critical 13.7% (peak-observed).
- Technical validation utilization trend — warn >90%, critical 100% (over-capacity signal).
- Combined Medical validation + Specialist review queue — warn >10h, critical 22h/36h split by station.
- Rolling 24h case-mix ratio (Urgent+Complex share of new registrations) — warn >35%, critical >40% (S1 trigger).
- Courier arrival-bunching index (share of daily volume landing within any 30-min window) — warn above historical smoothed baseline.
- Machine error/downtime flag per assay unit (binary/near-real-time) — any sustained flag >2h triggers S2 fallback mode.

**Lagging indicators (outcome, reviewed weekly, used to validate whether leading-indicator responses worked):**
- % Urgent released 18h; % Routine 72h (SLA compliance).
- p90 cycle time by case type (not just median — the redesign must not just fix the average).
- Overall rework % (baseline 7.5%, peak historical 11.8% — used to check whether triage/allocation changes are actually reducing repeats or just relocating them).
- % of cases with any queue >12h (baseline 22%, peak historical 54% — an aggregate proxy for whether bottleneck migration is under control).

The leading set exists specifically to catch bottleneck **migration** before it shows up in the lagging SLA/rework numbers — since, as the scenario analysis shows, relieving one station's average utilization can still leave the system fragile if the queue simply reappears one or two steps downstream.
