## 1. Equivalence design space

For analysis we isolate eight *lead-centric executions*: the subgraph reachable from a focal `Order:Oi` by following `E2O` (event-object) and then one-hop `O2O` links shown in the O2O notes. All definitions below are stated on this scoping. Local identifiers `O1,P11,I11...` are arbitrary surrogates; only a prefix `EXT-` carries semantics: membership in another Order's lifecycle.

### R1 — Activity-sequence equivalence (`~seq`)

**Operational definition:** `X ~seq Y` iff the projection `pi_act(X)` onto activity labels sorted by timestamp is equal as a sequence (multiset with total order). i.e. `|events|` identical and `label(e_i)=label(f_i)` for position `i`. Object types, cardinalities, `E2O`/`O2O`, repetition counts beyond labels, and identifiers are ignored.

*Invariants preserved:* control-flow skeleton from the Order perspective: what activities occurred and in what order.
*Information discarded:* everything object-centric: how many Packages, Deliveries, Invoices, Items per event; whether a Pack packed 1 or 2 Items; sharing with `EXT-` objects; replacement qualifiers; `M42` vs `M11`; `Return Item`.
*Properties:* Reflexive, symmetric, transitive — equality on strings, thus an equivalence relation.
*Concurrency handling:* As given, timestamps are total. If two events share a timestamp we must choose: (a) *total-order variant* — break ties by string sort of serialized event IDs, preserving determinism but creating spurious differences; (b) *poset variant* — treat same-time events as unordered and compare DAGs up to linear extensions. We adopt (a) for reproducibility and note that X2's two `Add Item` events would be considered equivalent under (b) even if order differed, but identical under (a) as well because labels are identical. R1 cannot distinguish planned concurrency from arbitrary ordering.

R1 is deliberately coarse and *not* object-centric; we use it only as baseline.

### R2 — Typed structural equivalence under identifier renaming (`~struct`)

**Operational definition:** `X ~struct Y` iff there exists a type-preserving bijection `h_O` on non-external objects and a bijection `h_E` on events such that:

1. `type(o)=type(h_O(o))` and `isExternal(o)=isExternal(h_O(o))`. External flag is preserved as a type refinement; local IDs otherwise meaningless.
2. `label(e)=label(h_E(e))` and event total order is preserved: `e1<e2 <=> h_E(e1)<h_E(e2)`.
3. `E2O` incidence preserved: `(e,o) in E2O_X <=> (h_E(e),h_O(o)) in E2O_Y`, including arity per event: e.g. `Pack{Item:2,Package:1}` must map to `Pack{Item:2,Package:1}`.
4. `O2O` existence preserved per standard relation types (`packs`,`carries`,`bills`,`settles`), but *qualifiers discarded*. e.g. `partially_settles` vs `settles` collapsed to `settles`.

In other words: labelled temporal bipartite hypergraph isomorphism modulo canonical renaming.

*Invariants preserved:* object types, per-event multiplicities, external vs internal participation, activity order, event-object wiring, number of repetitions of same label (two `Pack`s remain two).
*Information discarded:* global identifier strings, intra-type identity permutations, O2O qualifiers such as `removed_before_dispatch`, `replaces`, and inferred replacement semantics; payment amounts if stored as attribute vs multiplicity.
*Properties:* Reflexive (identity), symmetric (inverse bijection), transitive (composition). Hence equivalence. Practically transitive only if we allow composition of renamings across all three executions.
*Concurrency handling:* Order-preserving isomorphism. If timestamps equal, we relax 2 to order-preserving on partial order: `e1 precedes e2 in DAG <=> h_E(e1) precedes h_E(e2)`. Two alternative linearizations of concurrent `Add Item` events are then considered isomorphic. Under strict total-order isomorphism, X1 and X2 remain isomorphic via mapping `I11->I22, I12->I21` — the isomorphism compensates for swapped payload order, so they stay equivalent in both variants.

R2 is what a naïve `cluster_equivalent_ocel` approximates if it kept arities and ignored qualifiers.

### R3 — Business-semantic equivalence (`~biz`), preferred

**Operational definition:** `X ~biz Y` iff their *semantic feature vector* `F(X)=F(Y)` where `F` extracts motifs relevant to fulfilment, financial integrity, and lifecycle correctness:

- `logisticsPattern`: `[#distinct Package, #Delivery, mapping Packages->Deliveries]` abstracted as `single / split-consolidated / split-decoupled`.
- `billingPattern`: `invoiceSharing = exists EXT-Order in Issue Invoice E2O`; `paymentMultiplicity = #{Payment per Invoice}` and settlement qualifier `partial+complete` vs `single`.
- `sharingFlag`: any `E2O` event with `EXT-Order` or `EXT-Item`  `sharedLogistics`; any `E2O` financial event with `EXT-Order`  `sharedFinancial`. This is contamination.
- `lifecycleMotif`: detection of qualifiers `replaces`,`removed_before_dispatch`, `repacks` and activities `Return Item, Inspect Return`. Patterns: `clean` vs `covert-substitution` (qualifier-only anomaly without rework events) vs `overt-rework-loop` (return/repack/dispatch repetition with replaces).
- `cardinalityInvariant`: expected `Pack:Item` coverage respects “every added Item eventually dispatched unless explicitly removed/replaced”.

Two executions are equivalent only if *all motifs* match. Motifs are categorical; equality on `F` induces kernel equivalence.

*Invariants preserved:* object type counts *as interpreted*, sharing/externality, repetition semantics (planned split vs exception-driven loop), qualified relations.
*Information discarded:* local IDs, exact order within same-motif alternatives (e.g. which Package packed first when packages are symmetric), and low-level interleaving of independent `Pack`/`Dispatch` when they do not affect delivery grouping.
*Properties:* Reflexive, symmetric, transitive by construction (kernel of function).
*Concurrency handling:* `F` is evaluated on a partial-order abstraction: `Pack` and `Dispatch` of different packages are treated as partially ordered unless constrained by item availability (`I41->P41->D41`). We therefore compare Hasse diagrams for motif extraction rather than linear strings. This makes X4's `P41-D41-P42-D42` indistinguishable from `P41-P42-D41-D42` at business level if both map to “decoupled”, while preserving that X3's single `Dispatch(P31,P32)` is consolidated.

### R4 (optional) — Weak trace equivalence (`~weak`)

Collapses consecutive same-label events by counting multiplicities as attribute: `Pack x2`  `Pack[2]`. Preserves activity set and eventual order but not exact repetition count. Useful for performance aggregation. Reflexive, symmetric, transitive. Concurrency handled as poset reachability. We use it only to explain robustness perturbations.

## 2. Canonical signatures

We canonicalize R3. Requirement: invariant to local renaming; sensitive to types, arities, external flags, order-poset, E2O incidence, O2O qualifiers, repetitions.

Idea: replace each local `objId` by deterministic canonical ID `type:index` ordered by first occurrence in timestamp-sorted event stream. External IDs keep sentinel `EXT:type:0`. Then hash structure.

```python
def canonical_signature(execution, lead_type="Order"):
    # execution: events sorted by (timestamp, stable tie-breaker)
    # each event: {activity, E2O: dict[type->list[objId]], O2O_quals: list}
    events = sorted(execution.events, key=lambda e: (e.ts, e.activity))

    # 1. canonical object renaming: preserve external flag
    first_seen = {}
    canon_map = {}
    counter = {}
    for e in events:
        for typ, oids in e.E2O.items():
            for oid in sorted(oids): # deterministic within event
                if oid not in first_seen:
                    first_seen[oid] = len(first_seen)
                    is_ext = oid.startswith("EXT")
                    if is_ext:
                        canon_map[oid] = f"EXT:{typ}:0"
                    else:
                        counter[typ] = counter.get(typ, 0)+1
                        canon_map[oid] = f"{typ}:{counter[typ]}"

    # 2. normalized event representation
    norm_events = []
    for e in events:
        e_sig = (e.activity,
                 tuple(sorted((typ, tuple(sorted(canon_map[o] for o in oids)))
                              for typ, oids in e.E2O.items())),
                 tuple(sorted(e.qualifiers))) # e.g. ('removed_before_dispatch',)
        norm_events.append(e_sig)

    # 3. partial order reduction: keep order only if dependency via shared canon object
    # Build DAG: edge e_i -> e_j if i<j and intersect(norm_events[i].objects, norm_events[j].objects)!=0
    dag = build_dependency_dag(norm_events)

    # 4. semantic motifs on DAG
    packages = distinct_objects(norm_events, "Package")
    deliveries = distinct_objects(norm_events, "Delivery")
    logistics = classify_logistics(dag, norm_events) # single|split-consolidated|split-decoupled
    billing = classify_billing(norm_events) # isolated|shared-financial, 1-pay|multi-pay
    sharing = "shared-logistics" if any("EXT:" in s for s in str(norm_events)) and shares_logistics(norm_events) else "isolated"
    if billing == "shared-financial":
        sharing += "+shared-financial"
    motif = detect_motif(norm_events,dag) # clean|covert-substitution|overt-rework

    key = (tuple(norm_events), dag_signature(dag), logistics, billing, sharing, motif)
    return hash_tuple(key)  # stable string hash
```

Invariants: local IDs replaced by first-appearance index  renaming-irrelevant. Types, external flags, arities, qualifiers retained in `norm_events`. Repeated labels retained as separate entries (so X3  X1). DAG construction treats independent packs as unordered, satisfying preferred partial-order business view.

## 4. Cluster interpretations

**SEM-Clean-Single: X1,X2** — Signature: one `Package` packing all `Items`, one `Delivery`, one `Invoice` fully settled by one `Payment`. **SEM-Fragmented-Consolidated: X3** — Signature: `2 Packs 1 Delivery`. Behavior: warehouse split for picking/packing efficiency but shipment consolidation (e.g. multi-origin). **SEM-Fragmented-Decoupled-MultiPay: X4** — Signature: `2 Packs 2 Deliveries` interleaved, `1 Invoice2 Payments` with `partial+complete`. Flattening to `Order` replicates `Payments` causing double-counting; flattening to `Delivery` splits invoice incorrectly. Sensitive to whether payment multiplicity is modeled as attribute `amount` (would merge with SEM-Fragmented-Consolidated) or structure.

**SEM-Shared-Logistics: X5** — Signature: `Pack` jointly packs items of `EXT-OA` and `EXT-IA` onto `P51`. Behavior: cross-order bundling/collaborative shipping (pooling). Risk: entanglement for returns, liability allocation, GDPR/ownership leakage; KPI attribution ambiguous (who pays freight?). Classic flattening is destructive: picking lead `Order=O5` hides `EXT-OA` items or falsely attributes them to O5, inflating fulfillment rate. Unstable if external objects are filtered as “noise” — then collapses into SEM-Clean, hiding contamination. Requires `isExternal` flag preservation.

**SEM-Shared-Financial: X6** — Signature: `V61` bills multiple Orders. Behavior: consolidated invoicing, household/group billing. Risk: allocation dispute, revenue recognition ambiguity, payment without order-level earmark breaks dunning logic. `Order`-flattened log duplicates `V61` and `M61` per Order leading to apparent revenue doubling; `Invoice`-flattening loses Order distinction. Stable only if billing sharing flagged; filtering EXT orders merges with SEM-Clean.

**SEM-Covert-Substitution: X7** — Signature: `removed_before_dispatch` qualifier + `replaces` O2O + anomalous `Dispatch{E2O:Item}`. Behavior: item substitution/removal after packing, not represented as distinct activity. Business-critical: warranty/correctness — customer billed for substituted item but picked original. Risk: undetectable by label-only mining; compliance blind spot; root-cause hidden. Any flattening that drops qualifiers or omits `Item` on `Dispatch` renders X7 identical to X1. Highly unstable under “ignore O2O qualifiers” perturbation; robust only if qualifiers first-class.

**SEM-Overt-Rework: X8** — Signature: `Return Item·Inspect Return·Pack(P82)·Dispatch(D82)` with `P82 replaces P81 for I82`. Behavior: post-delivery exception handling with loop, legitimate rework. Opportunity: quality analytics; Risk: inflated cycle time, reverse-logistics cost, loop detection complexity. Order-flattening linearizes rework as forward progress, conflating initial and reship `Dispatch`; object-centric view distinguishes iterations per `Package`. Stable under qualifier retention; collapses to fragmented under “collapse repeats” repair.

## 5. Borderline cases

**X1 vs X2:** Defensible disagreement centers on event identity. R1 and R2 (poset variant) deem them equivalent — labels identical, payload permutation absorbed by renaming. A strict total-order R2 variant could declare them *different* if mapping must preserve per-position object identity without global permutation (e.g. “event 2’s Item must map to event 2’s Item”). Business view says they are identical; there is no operational difference in adding I11 then I12 vs I22 then I21. Lesson: local ID order inside same activity class should be considered unordered.

**X1 vs X5:** R1 says identical (same labels). R3 says fundamentally different (isolated vs pooled logistics). R2 intermediate: distinguishes due to `EXT-` arity differences (`Item:2+1`, `Order:1+1`). Two defensible business stances: (a) lead-object purist — “from O5’s Order perspective sequence unchanged, so group with X1 for throughput reporting”; (b) contamination-aware — “any external participation violates isolation KPIs”. Choice depends on whether analysis is Order-accountability vs end-to-end supply-chain.

**X1 vs X6:** Parallel to X5 but financial sharing is subtler: local logistics topology like X1, label sequence same. R2 distinguishes via `Invoice` arity (`Order:2`). R1 ignores. Borderline: if finance is out-of-scope, merging is defensible for warehouse analytics; for OTIF/billing compliance must separate. Illustrates why flattened `Order` logs overestimate distinctive behavior when shared invoices duplicated.

**X1 vs X7:** At R1 they cluster; with R2 without qualifiers X7 appears *almost* identical (difference is one extra `E2O Item` on `Dispatch`). Some analysts discard that arity change as logging quirk (“Dispatch sometimes logs Items”). R3 elevates it to separate covert anomaly because qualifier `removed_before_dispatch` changes meaning from “delivered both” to “substituted”. Defensibility hinges on trust in qualifiers: if `removed_before_dispatch` considered unreliable data-entry, X7 merges; if qualifiers curated, separation crucial for fraud/compliance.

**X3 vs X4:** Both exhibit split packages. R1 separates due to different label sequences (one vs two `Dispatch`/`Pay`). Weak trace equivalence (R4) might merge them as “fragmented fulfillment” ignoring consolidation distinction. Business argument for merging: both represent inability to pack together, optimization opportunity similar. Argument for splitting: consolidated (single shipment) vs decoupled (two shipments) have different customer impact and payment plan implications (X4 multi-pay). Our R3 splits because delivery fan-out and payment motif diverge.

**X4 vs X8:** Both contain two `Pack` and two `Dispatch` pairs plus extra events. R1 keeps apart (Return/Inspect labels unique to X8). Under R2 that discards activity semantics, one might conflate X4’s planned split with X8’s rework loop as “two iterations”. R3 distinguishes *motif*: planned fragmentation (`split-decoupled`) vs exception-driven loop (`replaces`, `Return`). Borderline arises if `Return Item` considered merely a domain-specific second `Dispatch`; coarse clustering would merge to expose generic “non-single-flow” segment, finer keeps actionable rework signal. Decision depends on whether goal is segmentation by complexity vs diagnosis of reverse logistics.

## 6. Relation to `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel`

`pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel, max_objs=...)` implements a syntactic connected-component extraction around object connectivity (OCEL’s object graph). For each execution (connected component keyed by lead object closure), it builds a dictionary key roughly consisting of:

- multiset of activities per execution,
- object-type cardinalities per execution,
- sometimes frozen `E2O` count tuples (e.g. `(Pack,{Item:2,Package:1,Order:1})`),
- and, in exact mode, raw `object_ids` participating.

What it preserves: exactly the structures that make “exact equivalence” very fine-grained: activity multiplicities, type-level counts, E2O incidence skeleton, but *without* interpreting qualifiers or externality unless they appear as distinct object types/attributes. It does not canonicalize IDs — two components differing only by `O1O2, P11P21` receive different keys if the implementation hashes sorted ID strings.

Why raw IDs make clusters too fine: local IDs are surrogate keys; product `X1` and renamed `X2` should be business-identical yet exact key separates them because `('I11','I12') != ('I21','I22')`. Similarly, executions with `EXT-IA` that differ only in which external Order they reference explode into singletons. Result: thousands of singleton clusters masking true behavior variants.

How to canonicalize/post-process:

- Replace lead-centric components by canonical signatures as in §2 before clustering, or post-aggregate `pm4py` output via secondary hashing.
- Filter `isExternal` sentinel handling: map any `EXT-*` id to typed placeholder; optionally drop linkage if contamination to be ignored.
- Normalize order by DAG rather than lexicographic ID string.
- Include qualifiers in signature if available; otherwise re-introduce them from `ocel.o2o` qualifier field.
- Use `lead_type="Order"` to scope components: start BFS from each `Order` object through `E2O` then expand one `O2O` step, preventing unbounded blow-up where shared Package/Invoice transitively connects hundreds of Orders.

When exact equivalence is preferable: auditing for duplicate bookings, detecting replay anomalies, deduplicating log exports, conformance where object identity matters (serial numbers, batch IDs). When coarser business clustering preferred: operational segmentation, variant analysis, executive reporting — where renaming-invariant motifs reveal actionable groups.

`max_objs` controls component size cutoff: OCELs with shared invoices/packages can yield giant connected executions spanning many Orders (e.g. pooling hub). `max_objs` caps BFS expansion to avoid exponential keys and memory blow-up; oversized components are either truncated (distorting signatures) or excluded (biasing sample toward isolated flows). In practice set `max_objs` high enough for largest legitimate consolidated shipment (e.g. 20-50), and separately flag giant components as “hub contamination” cluster instead of silently dropping.

Executable outline:

```python
import pm4py
from collections import defaultdict

ocel = pm4py.read_ocel("orders.jsonocel") # ocel exists
lead_type = "Order"

# 1. raw exact clusters (syntactic)
raw_clusters = pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel, max_objs=30)
# raw_clusters: dict[sig_str -> list[execution_ids]]

# 2. custom canonicalization for business semantics
def canonical_business_key(exec_ids, ocel):
    sub_ocel = ocel.filter_on_execution(exec_ids) # pseudo-API
    return canonical_signature(sub_ocel, lead_type=lead_type) # from §2

agg = defaultdict(list)
for sig, exec_lists in raw_clusters.items():
    for exec_ids in exec_lists:
        bkey = canonical_business_key(exec_ids, ocel)
        agg[bkey].append(exec_ids)

# 3. optional grouping alternatives
# collapse external objects for isolation-only view:
def without_external(bkey): return bkey.replace("shared-logistics","isolated")
```

## 7. Robustness analysis

*Ignore all O2O qualifiers.* X7 loses its only distinguishing signal (`removed_before_dispatch`, `replaces`). Under R2/R3 X7 collapses into SEM-Clean (or STR-A1). Effect: covert substitution blind spot — compliance monitoring degrades without warning; apparent precision improves but truthfulness suffers. X8 partially retains rework detection via `Return` labels, but its `replaces` justification weakens.

*Ignore external objects.* Filter `EXT-*` from E2O before signature. X5 and X6 both collapse into SEM-Clean/STR-A1, merging with X1/X2. Consequence: logistics-pooling and consolidated-invoicing disappear from dashboards; flattening-induced duplication errors reappear; operational KPI attribution becomes optimistic (shared costs misallocated). Useful if scope explicitly “Order-isolated view” but must be documented as data-lossy lens.

*Collapse repeated same-label events.* Counting only presence (set of activities) or capping at 1: X3 (Pack×2)  SEM-Clean; X4’s dual Dispatch/Payment flatten to singletons; X8’s loop reduced to one Pack/Dispatch. Lossy abstraction groups planned fragmentation with clean flow, conflating exceptional load with normal. Benefit: dramatic dimensionality reduction for high-level reporting, but root-cause signals for workload planning lost. Formally moves from R2/R3 to R4 `~weak`.

*Treat Pack and Dispatch event order as partial order.* X4’s two valid linearizations (`P41-D41-P42-D42` vs `P41-P42-D41-D42`) become identical under dependency DAG (assuming packages independent). Impact: reduces singleton fragmentation for out-of-order logging, stabilizing SEM-Fragmented-Decoupled cluster. X3 unchanged (single Delivery forces order). Risk: hides true temporal constraints if warehouse enforces sequential dispatch windows; for lead-time analysis total order may be relevant.

*Optionally, treat payment multiplicity as amount attribute rather than structure.* Model `Record Payment ×2` as single event with `amount=paid_in_parts`. Then X4’s multi-pay motif disappears, merging SEM-Fragmented-Decoupled-MultiPay with SEM-Fragmented-Consolidated (both split fulfillment). Desirable for financial reporting normalized to invoice totals; undesirable for cash-flow/compliance where partial collection pattern signals credit risk. Illustrates modeling choice: structural multiplicity preserves business process truth; attributization serves aggregated accounting.

## 8. Limitations and recommendation

No single equivalence dominates because purpose defines relevant invariants; precision/recall tradeoff differs.

*Compliance monitoring:* Recommend **finest qualifier-aware semantic equivalence (R3 with full qualifiers, external flags, explicit motifs)**. Reason: regulatory questions hinge on sharing (data-leak exposure in X5, revenue recognition in X6) and covert anomalies (X7). Dropping qualifiers or externality masks violations; false pooling underreports risk. Cost of extra clusters justified.

*Operational segmentation:* Recommend **medium-granularity business clustering: `F` without payment-multiplicity split, partial-order Pack/Dispatch, external flags retained but grouped as “shared” vs “isolated”**. Reason: segmentation goal is actionable cohorts for resource planning (single vs fragmented vs shared vs rework). Too-fine clusters fragment volume needed for SLA statistics; collapsing payment nuance and independent event order stabilizes segments.

*Root-cause analysis:* Recommend **typed structural level R2 augmented with qualifiers (i.e. R3) plus full DAG**. Reason: diagnosis needs wiring differences (why two Deliveries? why replacement?). Requires distinguishing planned split (X3/X4) from exception loop (X8) and surfacing silent substitution (X7) via `Item` on `Dispatch`. Coarse collapse destroys causal clue.

*Descriptive reporting:* Recommend **weak/business-coarse variant (R4-style) ignoring repeats beyond existence, treating multi-pay as attribute, potentially filtering EXT objects with disclaimer**. Reason: executives need 3–5 stable variants with intuitive names (“Single-Shipment”, “Split Shipment”, “Pooled”, “Returns”). Over-differentiation overwhelms audience; attribute summarization improves readability, provided limitations disclosed.

Overall limitation: object-centric logs inherently lack ground truth on intent — is X5 pooling intentional optimization or data-entry contamination? Was X7 qualifier accurate? Signatures cannot infer domain knowledge absent from log. Any equivalence imposes bias: fine distinctions amplify noise and ID leakage; coarse merges hide compliance-relevant sharing. Best practice is layered analysis: maintain canonical reproducible signature capturing full information (code in §2) and derive *views* via projections for each purpose, tracking stability under perturbations in §7 rather than committing to one partition.
