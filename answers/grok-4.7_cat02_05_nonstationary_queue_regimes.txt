# 1. Data audit and estimands

The table covers 28 consecutive days, 2026-04-06 through 2026-05-03, for one queue. Opening hours are 07:00–19:00. Twelve hourly bins in the representative vectors match a **12.0-hour (720-minute)** open day. Counts in `arrivals` and `completed` are opening-hour flows. No service is assumed overnight (**assumption A7**, taken from the problem statement).

| Quantity | Unit and definition used here | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival intensity \(\lambda^a\) | Cases per open hour = daily arrivals / 12 | Not a homogeneous Poisson rate |
| Completion intensity \(\lambda^c\) | Cases per open hour = daily completions / 12 | Not same-day demand served, and not capacity, except where a separate argument says the day was supply-constrained |
| Net drift | Cases/day, arrivals − completions | Not a measured change in on-hand stock |
| Handling time | Daily median and p90, minutes, active handling, waiting excluded | Not mean handling, not sojourn, not flow time |
| \(L\) | `mean_wip`, cases, mean of 15-minute snapshots | Not proved to be the close-of-day inventory |
| Capacity | `staff_fte`, mean concurrent FTE that day | Not hourly deployment, not productive minutes after shrinkage |
| Mix | `complex_pct`, percent of that day’s arrivals | Not proved to be work content |
| Outage | `outage_min` | Clock alignment is approximate except where the hourly vector shows zeros |
| Morning concentration | `peak_08_10_pct` | Not recoverable as 08:00–09:59 from the hourly vectors (see below) |
| Offered-load index \(I_{\mathrm{med}}\) | \(\sum a_d m_d / \sum (\mathrm{FTE}_d \times 720)\), with \(m_d\) the daily median | A **lower bound** on offered load only under A4, not a measured utilization |

**Assumption A1.** Median and p90 describe active handling of cases **completed** that day.  
**Assumption A2.** The open window is exactly 720 minutes.  
**Assumption A3.** Snapshots behind `mean_wip` fall during opening hours. If they were spread over 24 hours, every \(L\) would be diluted by about 12 frozen overnight hours and every Little’s Law reading below would change.  
**Assumption A4.** \(E[S] \ge\) the daily median (supported by p90 ≫ median), and FTE × open minutes is available handling time. Shrinkage, coaching, and breaks would push true load up. On the outage day, effective minutes are reduced by \(180 \times 6\) only if handling fully stopped.  
**Assumption A5 (sensitivity only).** A lognormal with the observed median and p90 maps \((m, p_{90})\) to a mean via \(\sigma = \ln(p_{90}/m)/1.28155\) and \(E[S] = m \exp(\sigma^2/2)\). This is not identified. It is used only to show the direction of the median-to-mean gap.  
**Assumption A6.** Daily mean FTE is spread flat across hours when a peak-hour load is discussed. If managers already stack FTE into the peak, that calculation overstates peak load.  
**Assumption A8.** Representative hourly vectors are real partitions of those dates: their sums equal the daily arrivals and completions. They are still purposive “candidates,” not a sample of days.

**Observation — conservation gap.** Over 28 days, arrivals sum to **2519** and completions to **2409**, so cumulative net flow is **+110 cases** relative to the start of 2026-04-06. End-of-window Sunday `mean_wip` is **19**, against **18** on the first Monday and **12–14** on early Sundays. Within the high-mix weekdays the gap widens inside the week, not only across weekends: daily nets run about **+10 to +16** while day-to-day WIP changes are mostly **−2 to +5**. Early baseline days move roughly together (nets near zero, WIP near 18–23). From the outage onward, \(\sum(A-C)\) and `mean_wip` are not two views of one conserved stock.

**Hypothesis H1.** Unobserved exits (abandonment, transfer, cancellation) grew once waits rose.  
**Hypothesis H2.** `mean_wip` covers a subset of cases (stage filter, “active today,” or a measure with a weekend cycle) rather than all carry-over stock. Weekend WIP drops harder than net flow, and Monday WIP jumps harder than net flow, which fits a measurement cycle as well as a real parking of cases outside the snapshot.  
H1 and H2 are both consistent with the aggregates. They are not separated here. Either one blocks treating \(L\) and \(A-C\) as the same inventory system after 2026-04-15. **H3** (arrival or completion scope mismatch) is also open. No abandon count is identified.

**Threats to naive inference**

1. **Several mechanisms shift at different dates.** Weekday versus weekend staffing, a 180-minute outage, a two-day recovery surge, a discontinuous mix and handling-time jump on 2026-04-20, and a staffing change that began at an unknown time on 2026-04-29. A single \((\lambda, E[S], c)\) is not a physical constant of this month.
2. **Flow and WIP disagree** by on the order of tens to about 110 cases, depending on the unobserved opening stock. Steady-state queueing identities that need one conserved system are not identified after the outage.
3. **Handling time is a median and a p90, not a mean,** and it is measured on completions. On right-skewed days \(E[S] > m\). On the outage day the median is estimated from 58 completions against 105 arrivals. On congested days, finished cases can be a selected subset of the work that arrived.
4. **Intraday structure dominates waiting, and two peak measures disagree.** Daily totals inside a regime are smooth. Hourly vectors are not. For 2026-04-07, bins 08:00–09:59 contain \(5+12=17\) arrivals (**16.8%**), while `peak_08_10_pct` is **31**. The 09:00–10:59 pair is \(12+20=32\) (**31.7%**). The same one-hour shift roughly matches 2026-04-15 (33% vs reported 32) and 2026-04-25 (22% vs 22). For 2026-04-21, **no** two-hour morning window is near the reported **47%** (08:00–09:59 is 9.6%; 09:00–10:59 is 36.5%). The column and the vectors are not one hourly census.
5. **Carry-over and unknown \(B_0\).** Completions need not have arrived that day. Overnight waits sit outside an open-hours snapshot if A3 holds, so open-hours \(L/\lambda\) is not calendar sojourn.
6. **The 28 rows are serially dependent and the design is confounded.** Backlog couples days. Each weekday occurs only four times, and the mix shift and the intervention occupy the last two weeks, so day-of-week, trend, mix, and staffing are not a balanced factorial. Lag-1 autocorrelation of daily arrivals is about **0.39**, and of WIP about **0.60** (section 5). Effective information is much closer to a handful of regimes than to 28 iid draws.
7. **`staff_fte` is a daily mean.** Hourly deployment is unknown (A6). Whether the outage is already inside the day’s FTE average is unknown; the hourly completion zeros are the direct evidence that handling stopped.
8. **`complex_pct` may be a coding change, a mix change, or both.** The flow imbalance does not depend on that label: at essentially the same 6.0 / 5.5 FTE pattern, weekday completions stayed near 100 while weekday arrivals moved from about 100 to about 114.

Pooled totals used below: mean arrivals \(2519/28 = 90.0\) per day, mean completions \(2409/28 = 86.0\), mean WIP \(836/28 = 29.9\), mean FTE \(144/28 = 5.14\), total staff-minutes \(144 \times 720 = 103{,}680\). Arrival-weighted complex share is **33.8%**. The unweighted mean of daily medians is \(867/28 = 31.0\) minutes. That 31-minute figure is a mixture location, not a typical day (section 4).

Weekends stay separate because FTE, volume, and diurnal shape differ by a factor of about two, not because Saturday is a different “process type” in a BPMN sense.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

**Alternative: one mix break, weekday/weekend as the only standing split, outage treated as an excised shock, staffing treated as a covariate inside the post-mix era.**

| Piece | Dates | What the headline would say |
|---|---|---|
| Excised shock | 2026-04-15 through 2026-04-17 | Outage plus two-day drain. Same treatment as the primary for these three days |
| Pre-mix weekday | 2026-04-06–04-10 and 04-13–04-14 | Identical to the primary baseline weekday: about 100 arrivals, 100 completions, median 25, \(I_{\mathrm{med}}\approx 0.59\) |
| Pre-mix weekend | 2026-04-11–04-12, optionally 04-18–04-19 | Same small, balanced weekend |
| Post-mix weekday, pooled | 2026-04-20–04-24, 04-27–04-28, **and** 04-29–05-01 (n=10) | Arrivals **114.3/day**, completions **106.7/day**, drift **−7.6/day** (still short). Complex share mid-40s. One “new normal” |
| Post-mix weekend, pooled | 2026-04-25–04-26 and 05-02–05-03 | A blend of \(\omega=1.15\) at 2.5 FTE and \(\omega=1.00\) at 3.0 FTE |

**Operational story this tells.** On 2026-04-20 the case mix (or the coding of mix, or both) changed, handling times jumped, and the center entered a heavier era. Weekends remain a volume/staffing seasonality. The outage is bad luck sitting on top, not a third kind of demand. Extra FTE after 04-29 is a knob inside the new era, reported as a covariate (6.0 versus 7.0), not as a new stochastic regime.

**Where the two agree.** The 04-20 break is the main level shift in mix, median, p90, peak column, and weekday drift. The outage is not “high demand.” Recovery completions should stay out of any steady rate. Weekends should not be averaged with weekdays before \(\lambda\) or \(c\) is stated.

**Strength of the alternative.** Fewer cells, so the post-mix era has 10 weekdays instead of 7+3. It matches a management narrative of “before the mix change” and “after.” It is the right place to **estimate** the staffing coefficient, because 6.0-FTE and 7.0-FTE days share a mix band. Used that way, it converges with the primary segmentation.

**Weakness, and why the primary cut is still the one used for claims about performance.** The post-mix weekday headline averages two intervals with **opposite** net-flow signs:

- 2026-04-20–04-28 weekdays (n=7): drift **+13.4/day** (losing ground every day).
- 2026-04-29–05-01 (n=3): drift **−6.0/day** (slowly clearing).
- Weighted blend: \((7 \times 13.4 - 3 \times 6.0)/10 = 7.6\) cases/day still short.

**7.6 is not a softened version of either interval.** A plan sized to “the new normal loses about 8 cases a day” overstates the loss **after** the extra FTE and understates the loss **before** it. The same blend pulls completions up to 106.7, which looks like a small gain on the baseline 99.6, even though the entire pre-intervention high-mix block completed **100.6**, i.e. no gain at all, while the three intervention days completed **121**.

A third cut — clustering days by high versus low WIP — is a poorer alternative. It would put 2026-04-15 (capacity removed) in the same class as 2026-04-22 (mix-driven overload) and would treat 2026-04-16’s WIP of 52 as the same state as a high-mix day, despite completions of 129 versus about 100 and a falling rather than rising stock. Symptom clustering erases the mechanism the staffing decision needs.

**Practical choice.** Use the primary boundaries when the question is “what regime are we in, and is throughput capacity or demand?” Use the alternative’s post-mix pool only as the sampling frame inside which FTE is the covariate. Do not publish the blended 106.7 completions or the blended −7.6 drift as operating characteristics.

# 4. Pooled versus segmented statistics

Pooled 28-day summaries: arrivals **90.0/day** (7.5/open hour), completions **86.0/day** (7.2/h), mean of daily medians **31.0 min**, mean of daily p90s from the daily figures sits in the 60s–70s once the later days are included, mean WIP **29.9**, mean FTE **5.14**, arrival-weighted complex share **33.8%**, \(I_{\mathrm{med}} = 78149/103680 = 0.75\), flow ratio \(A/C = 2519/2409 = 1.05\). Ranges, not standard errors: weekday arrivals already run from **92 to 121**, so a ±SE around 90 would describe a mixture center, not a day anyone staffed.

Weekends are 8/28 of rows and only \(359/2519 = 14\%\) of arrivals, but they pull the **row-wise** mean arrival from 108 down to 90. A model fit to daily rows with equal day weights over-represents weekends relative to case volume. Arrival-weighted and day-weighted answers differ.

The daily-arrival index of dispersion (sample variance / mean) is about **10.0** on all 28 days and about **0.66** on the 20 weekdays pooled, but only about **0.26** inside baseline weekdays and **0.29** inside high-mix weekdays. Almost all of the “overdispersion” in the pooled daily series is the weekend/weekday level gap plus the regime shift. Inside a regime the daily totals are **quieter than Poisson**. A negative-binomial fit on the 28 days would invent extra-Poisson demand that the roster never sees inside a weekday of one regime.

**2. Case-mix and handling-time mixture**

Arrival-weighted complex share is about **21%** on baseline weekdays and **44–45%** from 2026-04-20 onward. The pooled **33.8%** sits in the gap. No day has complex share in the low 30s: the pre-jump maximum in this table is **27** (2026-04-16) and the post-jump minimum is **39** (2026-05-03).

Daily medians form three clusters, not a cloud around 31:

- Baseline weekdays: **24–26** (n=7).
- High-mix weekdays before the intervention: **34–36** (n=7).
- The **29–33** band is exactly the transient days: outage (31), recovery (33, 29), intervention (33, 32, 33).

The pooled mean of medians, **31**, is the transient band. It is not the center of either steady cluster. A single service distribution with mean near 31 minutes describes a day type that occurred mainly while the system was recovering from an outage or starting a new roster.

Work content moved more than headcount. Baseline weekday median-minutes of arrivals are 17,530 against 29,880 staff-minutes (\(I_{\mathrm{med}}=0.59\)). High-mix congested weekdays: 27,951 against the **same** 29,880 staff-minutes (\(I_{\mathrm{med}}=0.94\)). Volume +14% and median handling +40% compound: \(1.14 \times 1.40 \approx 1.60\) times baseline median-minutes, and \(1.60 \times 0.59 \approx 0.94\).

It should not be read as “typical flow time rose to 4 hours.” Section 6 states the only windows where the ratio is even an approximate sojourn.

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

**Daily sequence.** Arrivals follow a weekly rectangle (five days near 100–120, two days near 40) plus two level changes: a step up in weekday arrivals around 2026-04-20 (about 100 → about 114) and a completion step around 2026-04-29 (about 101 → about 121) that arrivals do not share. Completions have additional spikes at the outage (58) and the recovery (129, 112). WIP is piecewise: teens–low-20s, a two-day spike to the 50s, a partial retreat, a climb from the high 30s to about 50, then a decline through the 30s after the intervention, with a weekend sawtooth throughout (Monday WIP jumps, Saturday WIP drops, by more than that day’s net flow).

**Change-point evidence (heuristic, not a formal test).** Largest one-day jump in `complex_pct` is **+18** from 2026-04-19 (23) to 2026-04-20 (41). Neighboring jumps are a few points. `outage_min>0` flags only 2026-04-15. Weekday FTE first sits about +1 above the pre-outage same-weekday level on **2026-04-29** (the 04-16/04-17 uplift is a separate, earlier recovery action and should not be given the same label). These three dates are the segmentation skeleton. With four weeks and the intervention at the end, the post-intervention sample is three weekdays and one weekend. That is enough to see the sign of drift flip, not enough to estimate a new steady state or a decay rate.

**Serial dependence.** Lag-1 Pearson autocorrelation of the 28 daily arrivals is about **0.39**. That is largely the weekday block structure and the level shift, not a queue feedback. Inside the seven baseline weekdays the lag-1 correlation is about **−0.3** (small n): high days are followed by slightly lower days, which matches underdispersion. Lag-1 autocorrelation of WIP is about **0.60** despite weekend crashes that **lower** it. WIP behaves like a stock: yesterday’s unfinished work is today’s opening state, minus whatever H1/H2 remove. An iid standard error on 28 days would be overconfident. A stationary AR(1) effective-sample approximation \(n(1-r)/(1+r)\) is about 7 for WIP and is only a warning, because the series is not a stationary AR(1). It has a weekend cycle and two breaks.

**Daily dispersion versus Poisson.** Sample index of dispersion of arrivals:

| Window | n | Sample var / mean | Reading |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| All days | 28 | ≈ **10.0** | Mixture of weekends, weekdays, and level shifts. Not a demand property |
| All weekdays | 20 | ≈ **0.66** | Still mixed across regimes |
| Baseline weekdays | 7 | ≈ **0.26** | Quieter than Poisson. Dispersion statistic \(\sum(x-\bar x)^2/\bar x \approx 1.6\) on 6 df, near the lower 5% tail of \(\chi^2_6\). Suggestive, not precise |
| High-mix weekdays pre-intervention | 7 | ≈ **0.29** | Same pattern (\(\approx 1.75\) on 6 df) |

**Bound:** a Poisson day-total, including a non-homogeneous Poisson process with any fixed diurnal curve, requires variance at least about equal to the mean. Within-regime daily totals sit below that. Upstream smoothing, appointments, or a finite daily demand pool are plausible. n=7 per cell, so this is a caution against a Poisson daily model, not a fitted inhibition parameter.

**Hourly vectors — unevenness is not a Fano factor.** For one day, the variance of 12 hourly bins divided by the mean bin mixes three things: the diurnal curve, regime bursts, and stochastic noise. A fully deterministic hump still produces a ratio well above 1. A true Fano factor needs replicates of the **same** hour. These four days are not replicates. The statistic below is an **intraday unevenness index** (sample variance across the 12 bins, divided by the mean bin).

| Day | Role | Arrival unevenness | Peak bin / mean | Completion unevenness | Shape note |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2026-04-07 | Ordinary Tuesday, arrivals sum to 101, completions to 99 | ≈ **3.2** | 20 / 8.4 = **2.4** (bin 10:00–11:00) | ≈ **1.7** | Unimodal. Lag-1 of hourly arrivals ≈ **0.6** (smooth hump). Completion peak is 15 in the **next** bin (11:00–12:00) |
| 2026-04-15 | Outage Wednesday | ≈ **4.1** | 22 / 8.8 = **2.5** | ≈ **2.3**, with three structural zeros | Zeros are the outage, not a variance parameter. Post-restart rate is lower than the morning of the same day |
| 2026-04-21 | Bursty high-mix Tuesday, sums to 115 and 101 | ≈ **10.1** | 34 / 9.6 = **3.5** | ≈ **0.98** | Isolated spikes: 34, then 8; later 24, then 5; later 14. Lag-1 of hourly arrivals ≈ **0** (slightly negative). Completions stay in a narrow band 8–13 after mid-morning |

Total-variation distance between the 2026-04-07 and 2026-04-21 **arrival share** vectors is about **0.30**. About 30% of the day’s mass would have to move hours to turn the ordinary hump into the burst day. The burst day is not a scaled copy of the ordinary profile. It has three separated spikes (mid-morning, early afternoon, later afternoon), and its lag-1 hourly correlation collapses because the spikes last one bin.

**What the completion shape says about capacity.** On 2026-04-07, completions still follow the hump, lagged by about an hour, with unevenness 1.7 against the arrival unevenness 3.2: partial smoothing, residual slack. On 2026-04-21, arrival unevenness is about 10 and completion unevenness is about 1, with completions flat near 8–13 per hour while single arrival hours hit 34 and 24. That is a queue feeding a saturated server pool. Daily completion intensity on such a day estimates **capacity**, not demand. Using \(\lambda^c\) as if it were \(\lambda^a\) would hide the shortfall (101 completions versus 115 arrivals) and hide the hours that created the wait.

**Peak-hour load is a different regime inside the “average” day (assumptions A4–A6).** Baseline implied mean handling near 28 minutes and 6 FTE is about \(6 \times 60/28 \approx 13\) cases/hour of capacity. The ordinary peak bin has **20** arrivals, so that hour’s offered load is about **1.5** even though the **day’s** \(I_{\mathrm{med}}\) is 0.59. Later hours at 4–8 arrivals refill the slack. High-mix implied capacity is on the order of **8–10** cases/hour at 6 FTE if handling is around 40–45 minutes; a bin of **34** is then several times that hour’s capacity, and unlike the baseline day the **daily** account does not get back to balance. A stationary model fed with daily \(\lambda\) and daily \(\rho \approx 0.6\)–0.75 will understate morning waits on ordinary days and will miss unbounded build-up on high-mix days.

**Why four representative days cannot carry a stochastic model.** They were chosen as illustrations (ordinary, outage, burst, weekend), one or two weekdays of each story, with no repeated hour-of-week. They cannot identify hour-of-day variance, the distribution of spike sizes, whether spikes are the complex variant, service time by hour, or queue length by hour. The `peak_08_10_pct` column disagrees with an 08:00–09:59 reading of the same dates, sharply so on the burst day. No arrival process (Poisson, NHPP, Cox, or batch) is identified. The supports that **are** identified are qualitative and useful: within-regime daily totals are smooth; weekday hours are uneven; after the mix shift the unevenness includes isolated multi-hour bursts; completions smooth those bursts when the day is saturated; weekends are flatter; outage hours are structural zeros.

**Stationarity verdict.** Stationary open-hours behavior is plausible only as an approximation inside baseline weekdays, and only after weekday/weekend separation. The outage day, the recovery pair, the high-mix build-up, and the first intervention days are transients or growing stocks. Pooling them produces a process with two service modes, two arrival levels, a three-hour absorbing hole, and a drift that changes sign — then a single \(M/M/c\) or a single daily regression reports the average of those modes.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Little’s Law used here is the open-hours accounting ratio \(W = L/\lambda\), with \(L =\) mean of daily `mean_wip` and \(\lambda\) in cases per open hour. It equals mean sojourn time in the snapshot system only if, over that window, arrivals (or departures) are the throughput of **the same** system \(L\) measures, boundary stocks are negligible or explicitly included, and the time average is long enough that start-of-window and end-of-window transients do not dominate. It is **not** produced by substituting the 25- or 35-minute median for flow time. Median handling is \(E[S]\)’s lower bound, not \(W\).

Overnight hours are outside \(W\) under A3. A case that waits from 19:00 to 07:00 accumulates 12 hours of calendar sojourn that this ratio never sees. Finite-window bias is small when \(A \approx C\) and the net over the window is tiny relative to throughput, and large when the stock trends.

# 7. Operational implications

**Staff to the high-mix load, not to the pooled load.** From 2026-04-20 through 2026-04-28 the weekday roster that had been enough (about 6.0 FTE, Friday 5.5) met about **101** completions against about **114** arrivals, every single weekday, overload factor about **1.13**, \(I_{\mathrm{med}}=0.94\). The intervention’s extra FTE (7.0, Friday 6.5, weekend 3.0) is about +15–20% capacity and lines up with the sign change in drift: weekdays about **−6 cases/day**, the following weekend about **0**. Under A5 the post-intervention load is still near the mid-0.9s. The extra people stop the bleeding. They do not restore the baseline’s spare 30–40% of median-minutes.

**Do not date the clear-down from these aggregates.** Two clocks disagree. Measured WIP falls from about 50 to the low 30s once the extra FTE is in, which at a surplus of about 6 cases per weekday is a short clear-down **if** `mean_wip` is the customer-visible queue. The cumulative \(A-C\) residual is about **+110** by 2026-05-03, which at the same surplus is on the order of **three to four more weeks** of weekdays **if** that residual is real on-hand work and the surplus holds. H1 (losses) would make the true remaining commitment smaller and would make customer loss the harm instead. Until opening and closing on-hand are logged, a single recovery date is not an estimate.

**Recovery staffing and intervention staffing are different plays.** 2026-04-16 reached 129 completions with 7.0 FTE because an outage had pre-loaded the queue and the mix share was still 27%. Using 129 as the “demonstrated daily capacity” of a 7-FTE post-mix day overstates what the heavy mix will sustain and marks ordinary demand-constrained days (baseline completions near 100 at 6 FTE, with slack) as under-performance. Plan an outage surge as a **two-day drain** sized to the hole that was dug: on the observed outage day, about **37** arrivals landed during a 3-hour stop and the full day finished **+47**. A 180-minute midday stop at this arrival rate is a multi-day backlog event, not an afternoon inconvenience. The post-14:00 completion rate on that vector (5.6/h) was already slower than the same day’s morning (7.5/h), so the restart itself needs a staffed ramp, not only a return to the normal rota.

**Peak smoothing is first-order in the high-mix regime and easy to undervalue from daily averages.** Baseline daily \(I_{\mathrm{med}}\approx 0.59\) coexists with a peak hour near or above **1.5** under A5–A6, cleared later the same day. After the mix shift, daily load is already at the ceiling and 2026-04-21 shows arrival bins of **34** and **24** against an hourly capacity on the order of 8–10 cases. Smoothing or capping those bins changes waits sharply when \(\rho\) is near 1, because there is no afternoon spare capacity to absorb them. The same smoothing looks optional under pooled \(\rho \approx 0.75\).

The burst profile is not “08:00–10:00.” On 2026-04-21 the large bins are separated: one mid-morning spike, one early-afternoon spike, one later spike, each lasting a single hour in the vector, with lag-1 hourly correlation near zero. `peak_08_10_pct` near 45–50 would put the extra person in a two-hour morning window that **does not contain** the afternoon spikes and, given the column/vector clash, may not even be the morning window the column names. **Recommendation:** do not rewrite the rota from `peak_08_10_pct`. Log hourly arrivals by variant for two more weeks, then place the extra FTE on the bins that actually spike.

**Policy that looks reasonable on pooled averages and is risky on the segments.** Revert weekday staffing to 6.0 FTE (and weekends to 2.5) once `mean_wip` prints in the 30s, on the grounds that pooled \(I_{\mathrm{med}}=0.75\), pooled completions per FTE stayed about 17, and the last week’s WIP was already falling. Pooled 0.75 averages baseline slack (\(I_{\mathrm{med}}=0.59\)) with high-mix near-saturation (\(0.94\)) and with an outage. Completions per FTE stayed flat **because** handling got longer, not because load was unchanged (about 420 versus about 670 median-minutes per FTE). WIP falls **because** the extra FTE is present. Removing it, while complex share stays near 44% and median handling stays in the low-to-mid 30s, returns weekday \(\omega\) from about 0.95 to about 1.13. The backlog starts growing again at roughly 10–15 cases per weekday. A second, related error is to adopt 129 completions as the new normal target; that day is the recovery transient.

**Data to collect before the next capacity decision**

- Opening and closing on-hand for this queue, plus abandonments, transfers, and cancellations, so \(A - C - \Delta \mathrm{on\text{-}hand}\) is an identity rather than a 110-case residual.
- Clock time of the 2026-04-29 change, and hourly FTE, not only the daily mean.
- Case-level handling time by variant, so `complex_pct` can be confirmed as work content. A coding change that does not add minutes would not have held completions flat while arrivals rose; case-level data can still show whether the p90 move is a second mode (complex cases) or a stretch of every case.
- Sojourn versus active handling. The baseline occupancy ratio near 2.4 open hours is a hypothesis about wait, not a measurement. An event log with case id, activity, start, and complete would settle it.
- Hourly arrivals and completions as a series, not four candidate days, so hour-of-week variance and spike frequency exist as estimates. Keep outage hours as a known mask rather than as low-demand draws.
- A stated rule for `peak_08_10_pct`. As published, it does not match 08:00–09:59 on the vectors that were supplied for the same dates.

**PM4Py, once a real log exists.** Daily aggregates cannot be passed to a process-mining library as if they were cases. With case ids and timestamps, arrival intensity should be estimated by calendar hour and variant, sojourn separated from active handling, and outage intervals masked before any rate is averaged. Until that log exists, the defensible operating picture is the segmented one: balanced baseline, a stoppage with a two-day drain, then a heavier mix that the old FTE did not cover, then an extra FTE that is large enough to flip daily drift and too new, and too confounded with an unreconciled stock, to treat as a solved steady state.

def unevenness(x):
    """Variance across bins / mean bin. NOT a process Fano factor.
    A deterministic diurnal curve produces a value above 1 with zero noise.
    """
    x = np.asarray(x, dtype=float)
    return np.var(x, ddof=1) / np.mean(x)

def lag1(x):
    s = pd.Series(np.asarray(x, dtype=float))
    return s.autocorr(lag=1)

def summarize(g):
    a = g["arrivals"].sum()
    c = g["completed"].sum()
    staff = g["staff_min"].sum()
    med_work = g["median_work_min"].sum()
    return pd.Series(
        {
            "n": len(g),
            "arrivals_per_day": a / len(g),
            "completions_per_day": c / len(g),
            "drift_per_day": (a - c) / len(g),
            "omega_A_over_C": a / c,
            "arrivals_per_open_hour": a / len(g) / 12,
            "completions_per_open_hour": c / len(g) / 12,
            "mean_wip": g["mean_wip"].mean(),
            "wip_min": g["mean_wip"].min(),
            "wip_max": g["mean_wip"].max(),
            "mean_fte": g["staff_fte"].mean(),
            "median_service_mean_of_daily": g["median_service_min"].mean(),
            "median_service_min": g["median_service_min"].min(),
            "median_service_max": g["median_service_min"].max(),
            "p90_mean_of_daily": g["p90_service_min"].mean(),
            "complex_arrival_wtd": np.average(g["complex_pct"], weights=g["arrivals"]),
            "I_med": med_work / staff,
            "completions_per_fte": c / g["staff_fte"].sum(),
            "peak_mean": g["peak_08_10_pct"].mean(),
        }
    )
