## 1. Decision rule

Allocate scarce swarms by their incremental portfolio benefit, subject to process eligibility, evidence quality, and service obligations. A high observational breach probability is a reason to investigate; it is not sufficient reason to use this intervention.

**Eligibility and evidence.** Before dispatch, verify the latest event timestamps, the intervention ledger, and whether a diagnostic bottleneck remains. The case must be before `Diagnosis Complete`, and a 45-minute swarm must still have a plausible opportunity to change the deadline outcome. Check setup time and downstream work as well as time to deadline. A previous alert is not a previous swarm. A previous swarm normally excludes another swarm unless materially new evidence supports a distinct, useful diagnostic action. I-03 is ineligible for this intervention regardless of its risk or contractual value.

A first model alert opens a review item. Automated dispatch requires the case to remain allocation-worthy at two consecutive valid refreshes; a specialist may instead validate a concrete diagnostic need and authorize dispatch without waiting for a second refresh. Do not wait for persistence when a verified urgent obligation requires action. A second prediction based on the same inputs is evidence of persistence, not independent confirmation of accuracy.

A material change means actionable new diagnostic evidence, a verified change in process state or queue conditions, a corrected faulty input, or crossing a predefined decision boundary such as the last feasible swarm start. A recalibrated risk movement of at least 0.05 can trigger review, but must be traced to a real input change before it justifies another notification or treatment. New useful evidence can qualify even if predicted risk falls. Mere 30-minute refreshes do not qualify.

**Calibration.** Use the following as working sensitivity bands, not statistical confidence intervals:

- SME: add 0.04–0.09, giving I-05 approximately 0.61–0.66, I-07 0.76–0.81, and I-10 0.50–0.55.
- Strategic Enterprise: subtract 0.05–0.12, giving I-02 approximately 0.54–0.61, I-03 0.70–0.77, I-04 0.36–0.43, and I-08 0.31–0.38.
- Public Sector: retain the central predictions provisionally, with sampling uncertainty. I-06 additionally requires a data-quality review; its 0.32 jump is not trustworthy evidence while the queue feature is imputed.
- Non-Native-Language Support: retain 0.69 as a provisional model output for I-09, explicitly mark calibration and effect uncertainty, and obtain an appropriately supported diagnostic review. There is insufficient evidence to invent a numerical correction or automatically downgrade this group.

Clip probabilities to [0,1] and apply the same calibration version to consecutive prefixes when assessing changes. These predictions are only proxies for risk without a new swarm, conditional on prior treatment and queue exposure. They do not identify either an untreated counterfactual or a causal swarm effect. Do not mechanically rescale the supplied effect ranges when correcting predictions. Those ranges are planning inputs, not established causal bounds; zero benefit remains possible. Any proposed risk reduction must also be compatible with baseline risk.

**Prioritization.** Let V be breach-avoidance value, B false-alarm burden, L the supplied lower effect, and M the midpoint effect. For a transparent conservative ranking, calculate:

D_low = V × L − €450 − B

D_mid = V × M − €450 − B

S = 0.5 × D_low + 0.5 × D_mid.

For today's discretionary allocation, require D_low > 0 and positive S after the estimated marginal queue harm. These are risk-preference choices, not confidence guarantees. Charging all of B to every swarm is deliberately conservative. If B occurs only when the case would otherwise avoid breach, an alternative expected charge is approximately (1 − p_without_new_swarm) × B, under that specific definition. A successfully prevented breach must not be classified as a false alarm simply because the treated case closed on time.

For selected set A, estimate queue harm as H(A) = sum over untreated cases j of V_j × the incremental breach probability caused by A. Recompute the exposed set and marginal harm whenever selection or scheduling changes. Include urgent non-monetary obligations as constraints before ranking; no such overriding obligation is established for a listed case merely by its segment name.

To make a concrete allocation, I use an explicit provisional planning assumption: their average breach-avoidance value is €15,000 and central spillover is 0.02 per case per slot. This gives a €1,800 marginal spillover debit per slot. These are assumptions for the decision below, not facts supplied about the queue. Verify them before dispatch, using the actual exposed cases; if they cannot be checked, obtain a manual queue assessment rather than silently treating spillover as zero.

Normally book at most three slots and reserve the fourth until 15:00. Reassess the reserve if a time-critical obligation arrives or the credible loss from waiting exceeds its option value. Arrival probability and regulatory consequences are unspecified, so retaining one slot implements management's preference rather than a calculated optimum. A positive portfolio total does not justify adding a case with negative marginal benefit.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

Maintain one case-level alert episode with states: review needed, eligible and queued, booked, treated, suppressed, or no longer stage-eligible. Store the evidence fingerprint, calibration/model versions, treatment history, decision reason, next review time, and last feasible swarm start. A notification count never increases priority by itself.

**Cool-down and suppression.** After an acknowledged alert, suppress equivalent notifications for 120 minutes. Continue recomputing eligibility and allocation at each valid 30-minute refresh and when capacity changes. Update the existing work item rather than generating another page. Cool-down expiry prompts a review if needed, not an automatic new alert or swarm. Suppress model-driven swarm suggestions permanently for the episode once `Diagnosis Complete` is verified.

**Escalation.** Break notification cool-down for actionable new evidence, a verified serious input correction, a safety or contractual escalation, or the approach of the last feasible start. For cases with at most two hours to deadline, review at least every 15 minutes while a useful action remains possible; an operational timer must work even if the model feed fails. If a swarm is declined, identify the responsible owner and the next feasible service action. Alternative actions also consume resources and must be recorded.

**Re-entry.** An untreated case may re-enter selection when evidence, queue harm, available capacity, or its action window materially changes; it need not generate another alarm. A treated case requires genuinely new diagnostic evidence and specialist approval for an exception. Estimate the incremental effect of a second swarm conditional on the first, rather than reusing the first-swarm range. An unchanged high score, or the absence of an immediate score decrease after treatment, does not justify repeating treatment.

**I-05 versus I-07.** I-05's refresh-driven third alert should be merged and suppressed. First verify the swarm ledger: if it has never received a swarm, it remains eligible for its first one and keeps its original waiting age; if already treated, the unchanged evidence rules out another. Its high SME-adjusted risk and large plausible effect remain visible on the watchlist.

I-07's new customer log breaks suppression and receives immediate diagnostic review even though p_now declined from 0.75 to 0.72. Determine whether the log changes the useful action or effect estimate and whether there is time to act. It may reopen consideration for a first swarm, or a reviewed exception to the repeat ban. The new event creates a review opportunity, not an automatic entitlement to one of the four slots. Without improved expected benefit or an overriding obligation, it remains unselected under today's queue assumptions.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Value-based starvation.** The current booking favors two Enterprise cases; I-05 and I-07 can have larger probability reductions than I-02 while receiving lower monetary scores. Report both expected breaches prevented and contractual value, so monetary success cannot conceal worse service for lower-value customers. Guarantee all cases a named owner, a last-action-window review, and access to mandatory safety or contractual escalation independently of value. After two allocation rounds of capacity-based deferral, require a senior review of alternatives and accumulated delay; duplicate notifications neither reset nor increase that waiting age.

Introduce a rolling equity constraint for repeated use: ordinarily at least one in four discretionary, non-regulatory swarm opportunities should go to an appropriate lower-value or empirically underserved case when its estimated marginal portfolio benefit is nonnegative. Assess opportunity against eligibility, useful effect, and remaining time, not population share alone. Log exceptions rather than manufacture a harmful swarm to meet a quota. If the constraint is repeatedly infeasible because interruption costs exclude these cases, change staffing or provide protected diagnostic time and evaluate its effects. The current portfolio's negative conservative margins for the listed lower-value cases justify an exception under the planning assumptions, but require checking those assumptions rather than treating exclusion as permanent.

**Non-Native-Language Support.** Small samples must not become a permanent exclusion mechanism. I-09 receives supported review and a booking despite uncertainty. Use qualified language assistance, record communication-related barriers, and audit missed and successful intervention opportunities. Gather additional outcome and effect evidence through supervised, ethically acceptable allocation among comparable candidates. Report wide intervals; do not confuse sparse evidence with low benefit. Conversely, selection today is not evidence that the effect estimate is reliable.

**Differential calibration.** Monitor calibration and decision outcomes separately for SME, Enterprise, Public Sector, language-support context, and meaningful intersections. Apply validated segment corrections with partial pooling where samples are small. Audit false negatives and late interventions for SME and unnecessary swarms after the Enterprise redesign. Compare access after accounting for stage, urgency, and plausible treatment benefit; equal raw alert rates are not the objective.

**Selective labels and intervention feedback.** Successful treatment changes the outcome that becomes a training label. Naively retraining on treated non-breaches can make formerly high-risk groups look safe and reduce their future access. Untreated cases harmed by queue exposure can look intrinsically difficult, causing the policy to deprioritize them again. Record the pre-decision prefix, proposed and actual action, selection probability where applicable, timing, and spillover exposure. Keep untreated-risk estimation and treatment-effect estimation conceptually separate, and never label a treated non-breach as a proven false alert.

**Unequal spillover.** Identify the actual six exposed cases and all other substantially affected work, including cases outside this table. Attribute added waiting, breaches, and service deterioration by segment, language context, and value band. Include these groups in the welfare calculation even when their cases never alerted. If lower-value or language-support queues repeatedly absorb interruptions, alter specialist coverage, stagger swarms, or create protected work periods. Their harms are part of the intervention policy's performance, not background noise.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational monitoring, continuously and at each dispatch.** Track stage transitions, event-feed latency, missing or imputed inputs, deadline slack, first-alert-to-review time, review-to-start time, swarm completion, repeat-treatment exceptions, and post-swarm diagnostic progress. For I-06, inspect the raw queue feed and reproduce the prediction with verified inputs before lifting the hold. If the missing field reflects a wider queue-observability outage, require manual congestion assessment for other dispatches too. Stop a proposed swarm if the effective stage has passed or there is no plausible remaining diagnostic action.

**Predictive monitoring, using mature outcomes.** Assess reliability curves, calibration intercept and slope, Brier score, and risk calibration around actual allocation decisions, stratified by segment, stage, time to deadline, missingness, and policy version. Keep prediction horizons and deadline definitions consistent. Account for unresolved cases and label delay; do not treat still-open cases as non-breaches. Audit whether Enterprise redesign corrections and SME offsets remain valid. A provisional trigger is absolute calibration error above 0.05 across two adequately supported completed weekly windows, or a material change that reverses allocations. Investigate, recalibrate in shadow, and validate before promotion. For sparse groups, use pooled estimates and uncertainty intervals rather than waiting indefinitely for a large standalone sample.

**Causal-effect estimation.** Log offers, actual swarms, specialist participation, exact timing, prior treatment, reasons for selection or rejection, and every eligible candidate's pre-decision features. Compare like decision times to avoid giving treated cases an artificial survival advantage. Where equipoise exists and no overriding obligation applies, randomize offers or tie-breaking among eligible candidates and record assignment probabilities. An interpretable primary estimand is the effect of offering the policy; treatment-received estimates need additional assumptions about compliance.

Individual randomization alone is insufficient for total impact because specialists create shared-queue interference. Use a feasible queue- or shift-level randomized rollout, or randomized capacity-intensity schedule, with carryover measured across periods. Estimate both the direct effect and the change in outcomes for untreated exposed cases. Conceptually, outcomes depend on both own treatment and queue exposure: Y(treatment, exposure). Ensure the design can distinguish those dimensions; a queue-level comparison alone may identify total policy effect without separately identifying both components.

If randomization is unavailable, use prospectively logged, time-aligned observational comparisons with overlap checks and suitable adjustment, such as doubly robust estimation. Report residual confounding, especially unlogged specialist judgment; do not claim that matching or propensity scores remove it. Avoid extrapolating effects to cases that the policy never allows into comparison groups. Any exploration remains within the daily capacity and a monitored harm budget.

**Capacity and spillover monitoring.** Record slots booked, used, cancelled and reserved; reserve releases and regulatory arrivals; specialist time diverted; queue length and age; and the untreated slack distribution. Estimate spillover by congestion and exposure rather than applying 0.01–0.03 universally. Track both aggregate and tail delays, including whether the normal 8–20-minute range is exceeded. A sustained move above 20 aggregate delay minutes per swarm triggers an immediate congestion review; adverse 15–30-minute conditions are a stress signal, not a conversion formula for risk. Before each remaining swarm, cancel or defer it if estimated marginal harm exceeds its score unless an explicit overriding obligation applies. I-02's approximate 0.0203 spillover boundary is today's concrete example, not a universal threshold.

**Fairness monitoring and review.** Report, by group and relevant intersections, eligibility, offers, treatment, capacity-based denials, waiting time, late reviews, repeat swarms, attributable untreated harm, and breach outcomes. Include uncertainty and decision reasons. Review the one-in-four opportunity constraint over a rolling 20 discretionary opportunities; flag fewer than five qualifying offers when suitable candidates existed. Also investigate repeated breaches of the last-action-window review requirement or persistent differences in waiting and spillover after accounting for urgency and actionable need. Inspect individual cases as well as aggregate rates.

**Policy-induced drift and rule changes.** Version the model, calibration, effect estimates, spillover estimates, and allocation rule. Preserve pre-action predictions and collect outcomes for all eligible cases, including untreated and exposed cases. Compare stable or randomized policy periods to distinguish process changes from changes created by selection. Calibrate a no-new-swarm risk model using data and methods appropriate to that counterfactual; raw post-policy labels, even among untreated but exposed cases, are not a clean validation set.

Revisit the allocation rule weekly at first, and immediately after a material data outage, workflow redesign, unexpected safety event, or strongly negative portfolio result. Change the score weighting, evidence gates, reserve policy, or capacity-dependent cutoff when validated calibration, credible effect estimates, queue externalities, or equity obligations change the relative value of actions. Evaluate proposed changes against total portfolio breaches, monetary and non-monetary harm, and fairness—not model discrimination alone. Use prospective shadow evaluation followed by a monitored rollout; retain a rollback rule for increased untreated harm. The threshold to change is an intervention-utility and capacity rule, not a single p_now cutoff.
