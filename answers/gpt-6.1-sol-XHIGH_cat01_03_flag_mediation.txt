## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

The flag is an output of a scoring and intake rule, not underlying risk. Eligibility below uses the flag computed under the original rule, even when its display or handling changes. Writing `flag=0` into a database could change display, routing, priority, audit records, or all four; it does not define a unique intervention.

Operationalize the policies before estimation:

- **P0:** current display, priority triage, automatic review triggers plus human referrals, escalation rules, staffing, and queue disciplines.
- **P1:** hide only the flag indicator from human screens and notifications. Keep the score and other human information visible, and leave automated priority and routing rules unchanged. Staff may infer the flag from the score. Disabling automated flag consumers would be a different policy.
- **P2:** keep display and priority triage; disable flag-triggered mandatory review, while retaining legally required review and human referrals based on the same information. Specify which triggers are removable before implementation.
- **P3:** keep P0 routing and add one equally qualified reviewer per pilot system, protected for flagged reviews during prespecified staffed hours. Do not transfer a reviewer from existing capacity. Existing servers retain their dispatch rules; the extra server takes the oldest eligible flagged review, subject to documented urgency overrides. Keep review procedures fixed.
- **P4:** keep manual-review policy and modify a specified escalation component. The main capacity variant adds one qualified senior server during prespecified hours without changing criteria. A criteria variant replaces a documented escalation rule with another rule, retaining mandatory referrals; its effects need separate evaluation.

For a policy (p), let (Y_i(p)) be arrival-to-close time when the **whole shared-queue system** operates under (p), including its effects through other cases. Define policy differences as intervention minus comparator; negative time differences indicate improvement. Use all arrivals in a fixed, sufficiently long enrollment period, follow them to closure, specify starting backlog and capacity calendars, and report incomplete follow-up separately. Do not select only cases that close during the intervention window. Standardize contrasts to a common intake population.

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |

Use **policy/component effects, controlled direct effects relative to named mediators, and sequential interventional analogues**, rather than assert a unique decomposition of the flag association. The occurrence intervention changes whether a stage happens; the waiting intervention changes capacity for stages that remain required. These are different actions.

Natural effects would involve objects such as (Y(1,M(0))): outcomes under one flag-handling regime with a mediator from another. Review findings, resource assignment, and queue state can be changed by handling and then confound later mediators and outcomes. Cross-world independence is therefore especially implausible. Interventional analogues avoid that particular cross-world construction but still require consistency, sequential exchangeability, legal-history support, and an explicit interference model. Merely naming independently switchable policy components does not identify separable direct/indirect effects; the necessary component-exclusion assumptions are unverified. These contrasts need not add to a single “flag effect.”

Priority can displace another case at triage. Extra reviews and escalations change shared queues, so outcomes depend on other cases' assignments and on treatment saturation. One-case potential outcomes without an exposure environment are inadequate. Triage findings and resource assignment can be affected by flag handling and then predict both review and completion; review findings similarly predict escalation and decisions. They are exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounders. Conditioning on them in an ordinary regression can remove part of the effect, while omitting them can confound later mediator effects. Earlier waits and workload may also change later referrals, assignments, and effort, creating treatment–confounder feedback. `arrival_load` is only a coarse proxy for initial congestion, not the queue state when downstream stages become eligible. Time-indexed queues make these feedback paths temporally ordered rather than a same-time causal cycle.

## 3. Stage decomposition

All entries are **mean elapsed local-clock hours**, rounded to two decimals. Review and escalation cells average only cases with that stage; they remain `NA` when the stage is absent for every case in a subgroup. Consequently, conditional column means do not sum to the total in mixed-route groups. For an individual case, sum only intervals on its observed route. Residual-to-close starts at the last observed stage completion: escalation, otherwise review, otherwise triage.

The intervals before optional stages are **queue-wait proxies**: without referral/queue-entry timestamps, they can include deferred referrals, off-hours, batching, or other work. Stage start-to-done intervals measure elapsed stage duration, not necessarily uninterrupted labor. Assume comparable clocks and no clock-offset changes during this example; do not infer the actual timezone from the timestamps.

Review/escalation support, in table order: **4/2, 10/6, 0/0, 6/1, 8/7, 6/4, 4/2, 6/6, 4/0**. The single medium-complexity escalation cannot support a stable estimate.

Flagged cases finish triage after 0.84 hours on average versus 3.43 hours, yet close after 16.51 versus 8.17 hours. Review occurs in 10/10 flagged and 4/10 unflagged cases; escalation occurs in 6/10 and 2/10. Conditional review gaps are 6.50 versus 1.88 hours, while review service durations are similar, 2.70 versus 2.75 hours. Weighting stage means by occurrence rates gives an accounting identity: the flagged group gains 2.59 hours before triage completion but accumulates 7.35 additional review-stage hours and 3.65 additional escalation-stage hours; residual time differs by −0.07 hours, yielding the 8.34-hour total gap. These are descriptive route contributions, not mediation effects. High-load flagged cases have longer review gaps, consistent with congestion, but their case mix differs. Escalation groups are selected by findings and risk. The score ranges do not overlap across flag groups, and resources differ; neither the group comparison nor adjustment for this tiny dataset identifies a causal flag effect.

Conditioning on “reviewed” or “escalated” cases does not by itself isolate an indirect effect. Those strata can be selected by flag handling, hidden risk, and human findings. Resource assignment likewise cannot automatically be treated as an ordinary baseline control.

Directions below are hypotheses relative to P0 under comparable arrivals and staffing skill. They are not numerical causal predictions from this log.

Pilot **P3** for baseline-flagged cases assigned manual review under unchanged P0 rules, including both arrival-load categories. Add one equally qualified reviewer during prespecified hours; preserve existing review and senior staffing.

Randomize the entire shared-queue system in blocks whose length exceeds the empirically measured backlog-adjustment period. Prespecify wash-in, enroll arrivals during stable exposure, follow every enrolled case to closure, and record starting and inherited backlog. Estimate assignment-based effects with block-level uncertainty and explicit carryover analysis. If carryover cannot be controlled, stop this design and require independent parallel queue systems. Determine duration and independent-block count from prospective volume, variability, and quality-audit requirements, rather than these twenty cases.

Primary outcome: mean arrival-to-close time for baseline-flagged arrivals. Secondary outcomes: system-wide and unflagged means/p90s; actual review wait; senior wait/backlog; referral rates; elapsed services; cost; blinded decision errors and mandatory-step compliance.

Guardrail: no staffing transfers; pause if unflagged or senior p90 waiting deteriorates by more than 15% against the prespecified contemporaneous/control baseline for two monitoring periods, or if workload limits are breached.

Minimum data: score/rule version, intake complexity and independent risk assessment, assignment/display logs, eligibility/referral/queue-entry timestamps, stage starts/completions, all competing arrivals, staffing/skills/concurrency calendars, reviewer and senior IDs, audited correctness and follow-up.

Stop for a serious compliance breach, an audited error increase exceeding the prespecified noninferiority margin, persistent queue harm, missing implementation data, or insufficient added capacity/assignment fidelity.

## 7. Reproducible PM4Py-oriented Python

Requires Pandas and PM4Py. The event table uses completion timestamps with observed stage starts; Register and Close are recorded point events. It contains no events for skipped review or escalation. Local times are parsed as naive wall times because the site timezone is unknown. Before combining sites or interpreting staffing calendars, localize using verified site zones and resolve ambiguous/nonexistent times.

The code calls both public DFG discovery functions. It passes a start key to performance discovery if the installed public signature supports it. Raw performance-DFG edge statistics must not automatically be labeled queue waits: conventions can depend on the PM4Py version. Explicit completion-to-next-start gaps and completion-to-completion deltas are printed alongside the DFGs; stage intervals remain the authoritative decomposition.

]

    # NaT propagates to NaN; absent stages remain structurally missing.
    return (later - earlier).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk:** Independent intake adjudication shows that unmeasured risk requires the long downstream investigations even without flag-driven referrals, and most apparent waiting is necessary evidence collection. Favor risk-specific handling over the interpretation of avoidable flag-induced queue burden.
2. **Queue interference:** Protected capacity draws scarce skills from other work, or earlier review completions overload seniors and substantially worsen unflagged/system-wide tails. Replace protected P3 with pooled additive capacity or an escalation-capacity pilot.
3. **Decision quality:** Blinded audits or follow-up show that accelerated review under P3 increases material errors, missed mandatory scrutiny, or harm beyond the agreed margin. Stop P3 even if cycle time improves.
4. **Timestamp semantics:** Corrected clocks, verified queue-entry times, or active-work records reveal that the long gaps are clock artifacts, deliberate scheduling, or unrecorded service rather than reducible queues. Recompute the decomposition and target the actual source of delay.
5. **Positivity/lack of overlap:** The relevant route alternatives are impermissible for high-risk cases, or pilot support exists only for lower-risk daytime arrivals. Withdraw general claims about P2 or system-wide P3 benefits; restrict recommendations to supported populations and periods.
6. **Reviewer behavior:** Added capacity causes more discretionary referrals/escalations, slower effort, or changed scrutiny, enough to eliminate gains or damage quality. Reject the stable-routing/service premise and redesign allocation or procedures before continuing.

## 9. Final synthesis

Directly observed, flagged cases finish triage sooner: 0.84 versus 3.43 hours from arrival. Nevertheless, their mean cycle time is 16.51 versus 8.17 hours. Every flagged case receives review, compared with four unflagged cases; six versus two escalate. Review gaps are much longer for flagged cases, while conditional review service durations are similar. These measurements describe elapsed intervals and selected routes, not causal effects.

A plausible mechanism is priority moving flagged cases quickly into more frequent review and escalation, where shared capacity is scarce. Queueing and additional required stages can outweigh the initial gain. Higher underlying risk, complexity, staffing schedules, resource specialization, and delayed evidence collection provide competing explanations. Flag display could also alter discretionary judgment independently of automated routing.

The causal contribution of each pathway remains unidentified. Scores do not overlap across flag groups, risk is imperfectly measured, downstream findings and queue states are missing, and one case's handling affects others. Approve/Reject supplies no independent quality standard. Neither a natural mediation decomposition nor the effect of indiscriminately setting the flag to zero follows from these data.

The most useful next evidence is a limited P3 pilot: retain scrutiny and priority, add equivalent review capacity, and randomize the shared system over adequately controlled periods. Measure actual queue entries, inherited backlog, senior congestion, unflagged outcomes, and blinded decision quality. A reduction in review waiting and cycle time without quality loss or displaced congestion would justify expansion; otherwise, those measurements would identify whether senior capacity, routing, or another mechanism deserves intervention.
