Temporal precedence: quarantine 10:20 precedes release 13:50; resource feasibility: both C2 and C5 are physically in L7; causal speculation: J5’s progress is contingent on L7 clearance, while J2 proceeds via a supervisor override recorded elsewhere.

**Motif B — Batch synchronization with shared rack:** J1 – B1 – OVEN-1 – RACK-7 – J3. J1 and J2 are ready hours before J3 (11:55); the cure event at 12:05 relates to all three jobs, both batches share RACK-7, and B2 is delayed by RACK-7 cleaning despite OVEN-1 being idle.

**Motif C — Fixture contention across jobs:** J3 – FIX-A – CNC-2 – J4. J3 acquires FIX-A at 09:00 and releases at 11:40. J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50, but CNC-2 is idle 10:05–10:47, and J4’s `Machine Process` only starts at 11:45. The absence of an explicit wait event makes the coupling invisible in job-flattened logs.

**Motif D — Calibration-coupled testing:** J5 – TEST-1 – CAL-3 – J6. J5 tests 16:10–16:35; CAL-3 is then recalibrated 16:35–17:20; J6, ready at 16:05, starts at 17:25. TEST-1 is idle 16:35–17:25, so the delay is not machine-capacity but tool-state coupling.

**Motif E — Shipment consolidation across batches:** J1 – S1 – J4 – B1/B2. J1 is packed at 13:30; J4 at 19:10; S1 dispatches at 19:25. The consolidation couples jobs that never share a machine, lot, or batch.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

Every figure below states a counterfactual and is presented as association, not causal proof.

### 2.1 Batch B1 synchronization waiting (J1, J2)

- **J1:** Ready 08:10; `Batch Cure` 12:05. Wait = **3 h 55 min** (12:05  08:10).
- **J2:** Ready 08:25; `Batch Cure` 12:05. Wait = **3 h 40 min** (12:05  08:25).
- **Counterfactual:** If B1 permitted split batches or if J1/J2 formed a separate cure batch upon readiness, waiting could approach the setup/transfer time (~10 min, comparable to J3’s 10 min wait). The 3+ hour waits are therefore synchronization-induced, not processing-induced.

### 2.2 Fixture-related delay for J4 (FIX-A)

- **Observed timeline:** J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50. CNC-2 is idle 10:05–10:47. J3 holds FIX-A 09:00–11:40. J4’s `Machine Process` starts at 11:45.
- **Total job-level wait (arrival to start):** 11:45  09:50 = **1 h 55 min**.
- **Machine-idle portion attributable to fixture absence:** From 10:05 (machine available) to 11:40 (fixture released) = **1 h 35 min**.
- **Counterfactual:** If FIX-A were available to J4 at 09:50 (e.g., duplicate fixture or J3 not using it), J4 could have started near 09:50 or 10:05, making the 1 h 35 min gap avoidable. Note: J4 is also in B2 (starts 14:50), but since J4 processes at 11:45, B2 is not the binding constraint here.

### 2.3 Shipment consolidation delay for J1 (S1)

- **J1 packed:** 13:30. **J4 packed:** 19:10. **S1 dispatch:** 19:25.
- **J1 wait in packed state:** 19:25  13:30 = **5 h 55 min**.
- **Counterfactual reference:** S2 and S3 dispatch 25 min and 35 min after their last packed job, respectively. If S1 followed the same “dispatch shortly after readiness” policy, J1 could have left near 13:30 + ~0.3 h = ~13:50–14:00. The incremental delay is therefore roughly **5 h 25 min to 5 h 55 min** attributable to waiting for J4.

### 2.4 Calibration-tool delay for J6 (CAL-3)

- **J6 ready for final test:** 16:05. **J5 tests:** 16:10–16:35 (using CAL-3). **CAL-3 recalibrated:** 16:35–17:20. **J6 tests:** 17:25–17:50.
- **Total J6 wait (readiness to start):** 17:25  16:05 = **1 h 20 min**.
- **Decomposition:** Even without calibration, J6 might queue behind J5 until ~16:35 (+ transition  16:40), giving a baseline wait of ~35 min. The recalibration block alone is **45 min** (16:35–17:20), plus a ~5 min transition to J6 at 17:25.
- **Counterfactual:** If CAL-3 were continuously available or calibrated in parallel, J6 could plausibly start near 16:40, reducing wait by **~45–50 min**.

### 2.5 Component-lot release coupling (J5 via L7)

- **L7 quarantined:** 10:20 (includes C5 for J5 and C2 for J2). **L7 released:** 13:50.
- **J5 ready for B2:** 14:40; B2 starts 14:50. J2, despite being in the same quarantined lot, entered B1 earlier due to a supervisor override.
- **Counterfactual:** If L7 release were decoupled from individual job readiness (e.g., J5’s component released separately or override applied), J5 could have proceeded toward B2 earlier than 13:50. The observed gap 10:2013:50 (**3 h 30 min**) is the lot-level block, though only a portion is directly added to J5’s critical path because J5 also waits for B2 synchronization and RACK-7.

## 4. What flattening misses

### Job-flattened DFG
- **Makes visible:** Linear stage progression per job (Release  Component  Machine Process  Inspect  Pack/Rework  Dispatch); rework frequency (2/6); median stage durations.
- **Hides / duplicates:** Batch partner readiness (J1 waiting for J3 in B1); fixture waits (J4 for FIX-A); lot quarantine (J2/J5 via L7); calibration queue (J6 behind CAL-3); shipment partner waits (J1 for J4 in S1). If a job has multiple component-related events, flattening may duplicate them or drop them based on extraction rules.
- **Misleading conclusion:** “No individual job has a total duration more than 1.8 standard deviations above the mean” implies uniform performance. In reality, J1 waits 3 h 55 min for B1 and 5 h 55 min for S1—delays that are distributed across other objects and therefore invisible in the job-only view.

### Machine-flattened summary
- **Makes visible:** Utilization percentages (CNC-2 54%, OVEN-1 44%, TEST-1 58%) and event sequences (SetupProcessRelease, etc.).
- **Hides:** The reason for long gaps. OVEN-1’s 73-min gap (12:55–14:50) coincides with RACK-7 cleaning/quarantine, not OVEN-1 failure. CNC-2’s 42-min gap (10:05–10:47) coincides with FIX-A being held by J3. TEST-1’s 50-min gap (16:35–17:25) coincides with CAL-3 recalibration.
- **Misleading conclusion:** Low utilization suggests ample capacity and no bottleneck. The object-centric view reveals that jobs are queued for non-machine resources, so adding machine capacity would not reduce waiting.

### Batch-flattened viewpoint (inferred)
- **Makes visible:** B1 and B2 cure events; batch-level durations.
- **Hides:** Internal readiness disparity (J1 at 08:10 vs. J3 at 11:55). Without linking to individual jobs, the synchronization cost is invisible.
- **Misleading conclusion:** A uniform batch duration might suggest consistent throughput, masking that early jobs bear disproportionate waiting costs.

### Tool-flattened viewpoint (not shown in summaries, but derivable)
- **Makes visible:** FIX-A acquire/release (09:00, 11:40); RACK-7 use in B1/B2; CAL-3 calibrate/test cycles.
- **Hides:** Which jobs are affected (J3/J4 for FIX-A; J5/J6 for CAL-3) and whether the tool is idle or in maintenance. There is no explicit “Wait for Tool” event, so job-flattening never sees the dependency.
- **Misleading conclusion:** Tool events appear sparse (only 15 Job-Tool interactions), suggesting low impact, yet they drive some of the longest waits.

### ComponentLot-flattened viewpoint
- **Makes visible:** `Quarantine Lot` (10:20) and `Release Lot` (13:50) on L7 relating to C2, C5.
- **Hides:** Which jobs depend on those components unless an integration pipeline links lot events back to jobs. The standard extraction excluded them entirely.
- **Misleading conclusion:** A manager might see a 3.5-hour quality hold with no affected jobs in the job-flattened log, concluding it was irrelevant.

### Shipment-flattened DFG
- **Makes visible:** CreateLoadDispatch sequence; median 0.8 h and 0.3 h.
- **Hides:** The consolidation partner disparity. J1 waits 5 h 55 min, yet the shipment-flattened median only captures the short loaddispatch interval.
- **Misleading conclusion:** Shipment appears fast and predictable (median <1 h), while object-level consolidation creates extreme outliers hidden by averaging.

### Why low machine utilization coexists with long job waiting
The jobs are not waiting for machine time; they are waiting for **coupling conditions**—fixture release (FIX-A), lot clearance (L7), batch partner arrival (B1/B2), rack availability (RACK-7), calibration state (CAL-3), and shipment partner readiness (S1). Because these conditions involve objects (Tool, ComponentLot, Batch, Shipment) that are not represented in machine-event logs, the machines sit idle while jobs queue elsewhere. Traditional flattenings therefore misattribute waiting to “process variance” rather than cross-object synchronization.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

### Narrative 1: J4’s processing path through fixture and machine (J4, C4, CNC-2, FIX-A, J3, C3)

- **Temporal precedence:** J3 acquires FIX-A 09:00; J4 reaches CNC-2 09:50; CNC-2 idle from 10:05; J3 releases FIX-A 11:40; J4 `Machine Process` starts 11:45.
- **Resource feasibility:** CNC-2 can physically run, but the process event for J4 also relates to FIX-A, implying fixture dependency. Only one FIX-A is recorded.
- **Policy constraint:** There is no `Wait for Tool` event; the system does not schedule J4 on CNC-2 without the fixture being present.
- **Causal speculation:** J4’s 1 h 55 min delay from arrival to start is plausibly driven by FIX-A contention with J3, not by CNC-2 load. The 1 h 35 min window during which CNC-2 is idle but J4 is present is strong associative evidence.

### Narrative 2: J5’s path through lot, batch, oven, and rack (J5, C5, L7, C2, J2, B2, OVEN-1, RACK-7)

- **Temporal precedence:** L7 quarantined 10:20; released 13:50. J5 ready for B2 14:40. B2 starts 14:50. OVEN-1 idle 12:55–14:50; RACK-7 cleaning/quarantine until 14:35.
- **Resource feasibility:** J5 requires C5, which is in L7. B2 requires OVEN-1 and RACK-7. J2 (C2, also in L7) enters B1 earlier via supervisor override recorded separately.
- **Policy constraint:** The supervisor override for J2 suggests lot-blocking is policy-enforced but can be waived; J5 receives no override.
- **Causal speculation:** J5’s delay has at least two coupled sources: the lot block (10:20–13:50) and the batch/rack synchronization (ready 14:40 to start 14:50, but RACK-7 unavailable until 14:35). The lot event is invisible in the job-flattened log, so a manager might wrongly blame B2 scheduling alone.

### Narrative 3: J1’s path through batch and shipment (J1, C1, B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J2, J3, S1, J4)

- **Temporal precedence:** J1 ready 08:10; B1 cure 12:05; J1 packed 13:30; J4 packed 19:10; S1 dispatch 19:25.
- **Resource feasibility:** B1 requires OVEN-1 and RACK-7; S1 requires both J1 and J4 to be packed.
- **Policy constraint:** Batch B1 groups J1–J3 regardless of readiness spread. Shipment S1 consolidates J1 and J4, with no partial-dispatch option shown.
- **Causal speculation:** J1’s total flow is elongated first by synchronization with J3 (batch) and then by synchronization with J4 (shipment). The 5 h 55 min shipment wait is entirely decoupled from J1’s own processing quality or speed.

### Narrative 4: J6’s testing path via calibration (J6, C6, TEST-1, CAL-3, J5)

- **Temporal precedence:** J6 ready 16:05; J5 tests 16:10–16:35 using CAL-3; CAL-3 recalibrated 16:35–17:20; J6 tests 17:25.
- **Resource feasibility:** TEST-1 performs tests, but every `Final Test` requires CAL-3. Only one CAL-3 is observed.
- **Policy constraint:** No parallel calibration event exists; recalibration follows J5’s test.
- **Causal speculation:** J6 waits 1 h 20 min, of which ~35 min is queue behind J5 and ~45 min is the calibration block. Without the CAL-3 coupling, TEST-1 could process J6 immediately after J5, reducing wait by roughly half.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

### Intervention 1: Reduce batch synchronization (dynamic or split batching)
- **Target:** Batch synchronization (B1, B2).
- **Expected benefit:** Eliminate 3 h 55 min (J1) and 3 h 40 min (J2) waits; reduce cascade risk.
- **Jobs/objects helped:** J1, J2, and any future early-ready job in a multi-component batch.
- **Jobs/objects harmed:** OVEN-1 energy efficiency may drop with smaller loads; RACK-7 cleaning frequency may rise; cure-profile consistency could vary.
- **New cost/risk:** Quality variance if thermal profile depends on full load; increased setup frequency.
- **Perverse metric:** “Average batch size” incentivized upward, recreating synchronization.
- **Object-centric evaluation:** Compare `Batch-Job` interaction wait times and `Batch-Machine` idle-to-start gaps before/after; measure per-job synchronization cost (batch start  job readiness).

### Intervention 2: Address tool contention (duplicate fixtures / parallel calibration)
- **Target:** FIX-A and CAL-3.
- **Expected benefit:** Remove 1 h 35 min+ J4 wait and ~45 min J6 calibration delay.
- **Jobs/objects helped:** J4, J6, all CNC-2 and TEST-1 users.
- **Jobs/objects harmed:** Capital and maintenance costs; space requirements.
- **New cost/risk:** Calibration drift if duplicate CAL-3 units are not synchronized; fixture misalignment.
- **Perverse metric:** “Tool utilization rate” pushed high by over-scheduling, recreating queues.
- **Object-centric evaluation:** Track `Job-Tool` interaction durations and machine-idle-while-job-present gaps; use `extract_ocel_features` to compare interaction intensity.

### Intervention 3: Address shipment policy (early/partial dispatch)
- **Target:** Shipment consolidation (S1, S2, S3).
- **Expected benefit:** J1 dispatches near 13:50 instead of 19:25, cutting ~5 h 35 min wait.
- **Jobs/objects helped:** J1, customers requiring early delivery.
- **Jobs/objects harmed:** J4 may ship separately with higher freight cost; logistics tracking complexity increases.
- **New cost/risk:** More shipments = higher transport cost; potential loss of volume discounts.
- **Perverse metric:** “Shipment consolidation rate” incentivized, delaying dispatch to “fill” containers.
- **Object-centric evaluation:** Measure `Shipment-Job` partner-wait distribution (time from first packed job to dispatch); compare S1, S2, S3 object-level metrics.

### Intervention 4: Improve logging and integration (not operational)
- **Target:** Data-integration artifacts; missing `Wait for Tool`, inconsistent `ComponentLot` linkage.
- **Expected benefit:** Reveal true coupling for future decisions; correct misattribution of delays.
- **Jobs/objects helped:** All analysis and planning; no direct operational job effect.
- **Jobs/objects harmed:** Data storage and pipeline processing overhead.
- **New cost/risk:** False confidence if new events are noisy or inconsistently coded.
- **Perverse metric:** “Event completeness percentage” drives addition of useless events.
- **Object-centric evaluation:** Compare `Job-ComponentLot` interaction counts before/after pipeline integration; verify `discover_ocdfg` now shows lot-related edges and `discover_objects_graph` includes L7-J5 links.

### Intervention 5: RACK-7 scheduling and cleaning optimization
- **Target:** RACK-7 availability blocking B2.
- **Expected benefit:** B2 could start closer to J4/J5 readiness; reduce OVEN-1 idle time.
- **Jobs/objects helped:** J4, J5, B2 jobs.
- **Jobs/objects harmed:** If cleaning is rushed, cure contamination risk rises.
- **New cost/risk:** Reduced hygiene; potential quality failure.
- **Perverse metric:** “RACK-7 turnover time” minimized at expense of cleaning quality.
- **Object-centric evaluation:** Measure `Batch-Tool` interaction durations and `Batch-Machine` gap times (OVEN-1 idle periods); compare B1 and B2 synchronization costs.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

### Six observations that would weaken the top-ranked explanations

1. **Batch timing correction:** If B1’s `Batch Cure` event was actually recorded at 08:15 but mis-timestamped to 12:05, then J1/J2 waits disappear, undermining the batch-synchronization hypothesis.
2. **FIX-A maintenance record:** If FIX-A was under scheduled maintenance 09:00–11:40 (not used by J3), then J4’s wait is not fixture contention but planned downtime, reducing the shared-tool ranking.
3. **RACK-7 independence proof:** If RACK-7 was assigned exclusively to B1 during 12:05–14:35 and B2 used a different rack (not shown), then B2’s delay is not RACK-7 contention.
4. **J5’s independent material shortage:** If J5 was missing C6 (not shown in lot records) rather than blocked by L7, the lot-coupling explanation for J5 collapses.
5. **S1 customer directive:** If S1’s 19:25 dispatch was a customer-mandated “hold until J4 completes,” then shipment consolidation is a policy choice, not a systemic bottleneck.
6. **CAL-3 emergency failure:** If the 16:35–17:20 recalibration was an unscheduled repair (not routine), J6’s delay is an anomaly, reducing the expected recurrence of calibration coupling.

### Six additional fields or event types to discriminate hypotheses

1. **`Wait for Tool` / `Wait for Fixture` event:** Explicit timestamp and target object (e.g., J4 waits for FIX-A from 10:05) would distinguish tool contention from machine delay.
2. **`Batch Ready` per job:** Individual readiness events within B1/B2 would quantify synchronization cost precisely (J1 ready vs. batch start).
3. **`Shipment Policy` / `Shipment Request`:** Distinguishes customer-driven consolidation from system-imposed delay.
4. **`Calibration Schedule` event:** Planned vs. unplanned recalibration for CAL-3; would separate routine policy from congestion.
5. **`Component Availability` event:** Direct `ComponentLot`  `Job` linkage with timestamp, resolving the extraction inconsistency for L7.
6. **`Override` event reason code:** Explains J2’s B1 entry despite L7 quarantine; distinguishes policy exceptions from standard flow.

With these fields, an object-centric process model could move from associative inference (temporal precedence + resource exclusivity) to explicit causal chains linking jobs, tools, lots, batches, and shipments.
