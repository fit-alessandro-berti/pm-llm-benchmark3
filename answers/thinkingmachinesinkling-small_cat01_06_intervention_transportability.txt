## 9. Recommendation

`conditional go`

The evidence at Site A is too confounded to justify copying the intervention unmodified at Site B. The 9-hour median reduction at A is best interpreted as a bundle of effects—parallelization, API latency reduction, two added document reviewers, a marketing-driven shift to simpler digital claims, and new daily huddles—observed without randomization or a negative-control process. Because Site B differs structurally on prerequisites (`originals_required` for 62% of cases), legal loops (40% high-value/public requiring `Legal Review` and potential `Request Supplement`), strict role specialization, legacy case-locking, heavier case complexity, and smaller assessment/legal pools (3 and 2 reviewers), direct transport of the control-flow change is unsafe.

What should be transferred is the principle that independent verification tasks should not wait sequentially when prerequisites are satisfied, coupled with real-time event logging and queue discipline (huddles, WIP caps). What must not be copied is the blanket instruction to create `Identity Check` and `Document Check` simultaneously for every case immediately after `Register`. At Site B, this violates statutory order for the majority of cases and would generate false concurrency through workarounds.

The subgroup that should enter first is the purest eligible stratum: digital claims that do not require original documents and are not anticipated to trigger legal review (approximately 23–30% of volume, depending on overlap with legal rules). For these cases, identity and document checks use independent external data, prerequisites are met at registration, and the only structural barrier is the IT case lock. The pilot should start here, with unlocked concurrent task tracking, separate reviewers, and stage-specific WIP limits. Expected benefits are a 4–8 hour median reduction for this subgroup, with stable quality, if assessment and legal bottlenecks are monitored. Risks include bottleneck shift to assessment (3 staff) and false concurrency if the system is not upgraded.

Expansion should be triggered only if the pilot demonstrates (a) true concurrency >30% in the eligible stratum (measured by concurrent open tasks), (b) median cycle-time reduction 4 hours, (c) no increase in rework or supplement rate >2 percentage points, and (d) assessment/legal queue times stable. Modification should occur if the effect appears only when queue huddles are added (indicating co-intervention dependence) or if originals-required cases show no benefit (confirming prerequisite-bound transport). Abandonment is warranted if IT-lock workarounds dominate, if p90 worsens due to bottleneck shift, or if legal/rework congestion exceeds stop rules. Ultimately, Site B needs a segmented, prerequisite-respecting design—not a mirrored process tree—validated by a phased, stratified pilot that treats mechanism and implementation as separate failure modes. Only then can partial transport be confirmed.

## 10. Assumption register

1. `Assumption: Parallel identity/document tasks can achieve true concurrency at Site B if the case-lock is removed.` — Needed because without concurrent ownership, “parallel” is only timestamp overlap. — Test via IT audit and concurrent open-task measurements in pilot. — If false, intervention fails at mechanism level; must upgrade IT first or abandon true concurrency.

2. `Assumption: Site A’s observed median reduction is partly caused by parallelization rather than solely by co-interventions (API, staff, mix, huddles).` — Needed to avoid overestimating transferable effect. — Test via controlled design with negative-control process and subgroup analysis; estimate effect with/without co-interventions at A if retrospective data permits. — If false, transferable effect is near zero; expansion conditions must be stricter.

3. `Assumption: Original-document receipt delay (median 18 h) is independent of verification parallelization and dominates wait for 62% of cases.` — Needed to define eligible subgroups correctly. — Test by measuring `Originals Receipt -> Document Check` duration and correlation with identity-check timing at B. — If false (e.g., originals delay is endogenous to case type that also benefits from parallel identity), eligibility rules change.

4. `Assumption: Legal review and supplement loops are structural at B and not present at A, making loop time a bound on cycle-time improvement.` — Needed to explain why tail may worsen. — Test by comparing supplement rate and legal queue time pre/post in pilot; model loop frequency. — If false (loops are rare or faster than estimated), bottleneck-shift risk is lower.

5. `Assumption: Role specialization prevents same-reviewer concurrency but does not prevent cross-reviewer coordination if handoffs are explicit.` — Needed for resource design. — Test via pilot assignment to separate reviewers and measure coordination time/errors. — If false (coordination overhead exceeds wait savings), parallelization is net negative.

6. `Assumption: Case complexity and incoming mix at B are stable enough to allow 12-week pilot inference.` — Needed for statistical validity. — Test by monitoring weekly mix; adjust for seasonality in analysis. — If false, external trends confound pilot outcomes; extend or abort.

7. `Assumption: Real-time timestamp logging can distinguish true concurrency from back-entry artifacts.` — Needed for outcome measurement. — Test by auditing event entry times and comparing with system open-task logs. — If false, overlap % is unreliable; use other metrics (waiting time, service time).

8. `Assumption: Assessment and legal pools are fixed (3 and 2) and cannot be expanded in pilot phase.` — Needed for bottleneck-shift modeling. — Test via management confirmation and capacity tracking. — If false (resources can be scaled), bottleneck-shift scenario weakens and optimistic transfer becomes more feasible.
