## 1. System boundary and assumptions

**Environmental scope used:**  
I use a **fulfillment gate-to-gate** boundary: from **Receive order** through **Confirm delivery / Close**, including in-house production, packaging, rework, transport to the customer, and observed damage/return loops.

**Inside the boundary:**
- In-house production energy: `energy_kwh` on `CNC_A`, `CNC_B`, `heat_treat`, and `inspection_cell` paths.
- Production and packaging material waste: `material_scrap_kg`, `package_material_kg`.
- Transport emissions: `transport_kgco2e` by `route_mode`: `parcel_ground`, `parcel_air`, `dedicated_van`, `consolidated_truck`.
- Rework loops: `Inspection -> Rework production -> CNC production`, `Package -> Rework packaging -> Package reinforced/standard`.
- Batch obsolescence as waste: batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days when forecasts are wrong.
- Resource consumption before cancellation: `Cancel due to delay` cases still represent lost service value and partially consumed operational effort.

**Outside the boundary:**
- Upstream raw-material extraction and supplier manufacturing before the distributor’s fulfillment boundary.
- Product use phase and end-of-life recycling/disposal, unless observable as damage-related returns or rework in the log.
- Carrier upstream fuel production or lifecycle emissions not already reflected in `transport_kgco2e`.
- Facility-wide emissions not captured by the event-log attributes.

**Uncertainty and data limitations:**
- The log is flattened by order, but line-item detail is important for partial shipments, backorders, and batch obsolescence.
- Returns are only partially observable through damage-related returns and rework loops.
- Empty-return transport for dedicated vans is mentioned but not fully quantified in the provided summary.
- Route reliability must be estimated from `promised_lead_time_hours`, actual transport/cycle time, and disruption history.
- The discovered process model has low precision because emergency and partial-shipment paths combine in many ways during disruptions. Therefore, recommendations should be monitored through conformance checking, path-level analysis, and exception governance rather than relying only on the discovered model.

The objective is **multi-criteria**, not single-carbon minimization: reduce energy, transport emissions, material waste, rework, and obsolescence while keeping service, quality, and resilience within acceptable operational limits.

## 3. Intervention options

### S1. Green shipping recommender using promised lead time, route reliability, and customer segment

- **Target path or segment:** All `Package standard/reinforced -> Ship ground/air/consolidated truck/dedicated van` decisions. Highest relevance: `Stock -> standard pack -> air` (5% of orders, high transport emissions), Standard-stock Service-contract SP1 violations, and Critical-downtime dedicated-van paths.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** At shipment decision, recommend `parcel_ground` or `consolidated_truck` when the probability of meeting the promised lead time remains above 85%. Use `parcel_air` or `dedicated_van` only when slack is insufficient, the case is true Critical-downtime, or a disruption makes the low-carbon route unreliable.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Service should be protected if the 85% probability threshold and segment-specific slack are enforced. Quality is largely neutral. Resilience improves if the recommender explicitly checks alternative routes, but it worsens if low-carbon routes are forced during carrier disruption.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `route_mode`, `promised_lead_time_hours`, actual transport time, late delivery, `customer_type`, `case_type`, `expedite_flag`, SP1 conformance, carrier disruption events, and alternative-route availability.

### S2. Dynamic batching for CNC and heat treatment with maximum waiting-time caps by segment

- **Target path or segment:** `Plan production -> Batch wait -> CNC production` and `CNC production -> Heat treatment -> Inspection`, especially Made-to-order paths.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Use larger batches where energy per item improves, especially `CNC_A` batch sizes 12–20 and heat-treatment batches above 10. But cap batch wait by segment and due-date slack to avoid excessive waiting, late delivery, and obsolescence.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Energy per item decreases, but cycle time and late-delivery risk increase if batch wait is too long. Obsolescence risk rises if demand forecasts are wrong. Resilience improves if batch sizes remain flexible and segment-specific caps prevent capacity lock-in.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `batch_id`, `batch_size`, batch wait, heat-treatment batch wait, `energy_kwh`, late delivery, promised-lead-time slack, batch items not shipped within 14 days, machine queue length, and demand-forecast confidence where available.

### S3. Shift Made-to-order production from CNC_B to CNC_A when queue and due-date slack allow it

- **Target path or segment:** Made-to-order cases currently using `CNC_B`, especially the high-rework path `MTO -> batch wait -> CNC_B -> inspection -> rework -> ground` with 32% late delivery and 1.8 kg average scrap.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** `CNC_A` has lower rework than `CNC_B` (9% vs 18% for Made-to-order). Shifting eligible cases to `CNC_A` reduces rework production, scrap, repeated energy use, and packaging/customer-return loops.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Quality improves because first-pass yield is better. Cycle time may increase if `CNC_A` queue is long. Late-delivery risk increases if cases are shifted without sufficient slack. Resilience depends on maintaining enough `CNC_B` capacity for genuinely time-critical or low-risk cases.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `machine_group`, `rework_flag`, `material_scrap_kg`, cycle time, late delivery, `CNC_A`/`CNC_B` queue times, due-date slack, part geometry or part family, and rework loops.

### S4. Replace air for Standard-stock Service-contract orders with priority pick + ground unless slack is insufficient

- **Target path or segment:** `Stock -> standard pack -> air`, especially Standard-stock Service-contract orders where SP1 is violated in 22% of cases.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Parcel air emits approximately 4–6x more `transport_kgco2e` per order than ground. Priority picking plus `parcel_ground` can preserve service for many Standard-stock Service-contract orders without air.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Transport emissions fall materially for affected orders. Cycle time may increase relative to air, but priority picking can reduce internal handling time. Late-delivery risk is acceptable only when ground on-time probability exceeds the SP1 threshold. Quality is neutral. Resilience requires available ground capacity and an alternative route.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `route_mode`, `customer_type`, `promised_lead_time_hours`, actual transport time, late delivery, air share, SP1 violations, ground carrier reliability, and priority-pick handling time.

### S5. Consolidate truck departures twice daily for non-urgent stock orders

- **Target path or segment:** `Package standard -> Ship ground` for non-urgent Standard-stock orders, especially Regular customers and Service-contract orders with sufficient slack.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** `consolidated_truck` reduces transport `kgCO2e` by about 35–55% versus individual ground parcels, but requires 12–24h consolidation wait. Twice-daily departures create a repeatable low-carbon default for orders that can absorb the wait.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Transport emissions fall for eligible orders. Cycle time increases by the consolidation wait, so late-delivery risk rises if “non-urgent” is misclassified. Quality is neutral. Resilience risk increases if consolidation creates over-dependence on fixed departure times or one carrier.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `route_mode`, consolidation wait, `transport_kgco2e`, promised-lead-time slack, late delivery, carrier capacity, disruption events, and share of orders shifted from individual ground to consolidated truck.

### S6. Improve packaging-route compatibility checks before shipment

- **Target path or segment:** `Package standard -> Rework packaging` and `Package reinforced -> Rework packaging`, plus Q1 violations for Made-to-order parts over 8 kg.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Prevent packaging rework by validating package type against `route_mode`, part geometry, weight, and damage risk before shipment. Enforce reinforced packaging for high-risk MTO heavy parts unless inspection certifies low damage risk.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Reduces packaging rework loops, delays, damage-related returns, and package material waste from incorrect packaging. Quality improves. Cycle time should improve because rework loops are avoided. Resilience improves because fewer cases enter unstable rework loops.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `Rework packaging` frequency, `package_material_kg`, `route_mode`, part weight/geometry, Q1 conformance, damage-related returns, and rework packaging path durations.

### S7. Add inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations

- **Target path or segment:** `Inspection -> Rework production`, especially cases involving `CNC_B`, heat-treated parts, or part families with high historical rework.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Prevents rushed inspections from releasing defective parts into packaging and transport. Reduces rework production, scrap, repeated energy use, and downstream customer returns.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Quality and first-pass yield improve. Cycle time may increase if inspection queues grow. Late-delivery risk increases if the gate is applied without capacity planning. Resilience improves if the gate stabilizes production and reduces unpredictable rework loops.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `rework_flag`, `machine_group`, part family, inspection queue time, `material_scrap_kg`, first-pass yield, rework production loops, and damage-related returns.

### S8. Replace some dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes

- **Target path or segment:** `Critical -> emergency production -> reinforced pack -> dedicated van`, especially cases where dedicated vans often return empty.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Shared emergency lanes can provide reserved capacity for true downtime cases while reducing empty-return transport and dedicated-van emissions.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Transport emissions fall if shared lanes meet the required lead time. Service risk rises if shared lanes are less reliable than dedicated vans. Quality is neutral. Resilience depends on carrier reliability, reserved capacity, and an alternative route for Service-contract customers.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `route_mode`, `case_type`, actual transport time, late delivery, empty-return rate where observable, carrier reliability, SP2 justification, and alternative-route availability.

### S9. Split Critical-downtime into true downtime and precautionary urgent categories

- **Target path or segment:** Critical-downtime Service-contract cases, especially the 8% path using emergency production and dedicated van with very high transport emissions.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** True downtime cases keep high-priority transport. Precautionary urgent cases can use priority ground, consolidated truck, or air only when slack requires it. This prevents all Critical-downtime cases from defaulting to the highest-emission route.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Service for true downtime is protected. Precautionary cases may experience longer cycle time but lower emissions. Misclassification is the main service risk. Quality is neutral. Resilience improves because transport capacity is reserved for cases that truly need it.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `case_type`, downtime justification, `promised_lead_time_hours`, `route_mode`, late delivery, customer contract terms, SP2 justification, and actual downtime impact where available.

### S10. Introduce carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular made-to-order customers

- **Target path or segment:** Made-to-order Regular cases, especially those using immediate production, air transport, or expedited handling.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Offer customers a choice between a lower-carbon promise date using ground/consolidated transport and dynamic batching, and an expedited option using air or faster production. This reduces unnecessary `expedite_flag` cases and air transport.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Service is maintained if customers accept the negotiated promise date. Late-delivery risk may fall because promises become more realistic. Quality is neutral. Resilience improves because the process is less dependent on rushed production and air transport.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `promised_lead_time_hours`, `expedite_flag`, `route_mode`, customer acceptance, late delivery, batch wait, `transport_kgco2e`, and cancellation due to delay.

### S11. Reduce partial shipments by changing availability-check rules

- **Target path or segment:** `Pick from stock -> Partial shipment -> Backorder wait`, including the high-late path `Stock -> partial shipment -> ground + backorder`.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Avoid unnecessary second shipments and backorder loops by deciding at availability check whether to ship full, ship partial only if customer-accepted, or wait for the missing line item. Fewer partial shipments reduce repeated transport, packaging, and customer-contact effort.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Transport and customer-contact burden fall. Cycle time may increase if the process waits for full order availability. Late-delivery risk rises if waiting for missing items is too long. Quality is neutral. Resilience depends on inventory availability and clear customer communication.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `Partial shipment`, `Backorder wait`, line-item availability, late delivery, cancellation due to delay, `transport_kgco2e`, customer acceptance of partial shipment, and order completeness.

### S12. Create a sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides

- **Target path or segment:** All overrides of SP1, SP2, and Q1, especially air shipments, dedicated vans, and non-reinforced packaging for high-risk MTO parts.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Requires a recorded justification, approver, alternative considered, and expected service benefit before a higher-impact route or packaging choice is used. This reduces unjustified air, dedicated-van, and packaging exceptions.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Service and quality are protected because exceptions must be justified rather than silently made. Transport emissions fall where exceptions were unnecessary. Resilience improves because alternative-route reasoning becomes explicit and auditable.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** Exception event, policy ID, justification text or code, approver, `route_mode`, `case_type`, `customer_type`, SP1/SP2/Q1 conformance, and outcome metrics such as late delivery and returns.

| **Obsolescence rebound** | Large production batches and forecast-driven batch production | Energy per item falls, but unsent batch items create storage, waste, and eventual scrap. The prompt notes 6% of batch-produced items may not ship within 14 days when forecasts are wrong. | Limit batch size; require demand-confidence threshold; monitor batch items not shipped within 14 days; avoid batching for uncertain or one-off demand. |
| **Customer behavior rebound** | Carbon-aware promises, partial-shipment rules, air replacement | Customers may request expedite, choose air, split orders, cancel delayed full orders, or lose trust if promises change. | Offer transparent promise-date options; require customer acceptance for partial shipment; monitor `expedite_flag`, cancellations, partial shipments, and complaint/return signals. |
| **Resilience rebound** | Consolidated trucks, shared emergency lanes, dedicated-van reduction | Lower-emission transport can reduce route redundancy and increase dependence on fixed departures or specific carriers. | Enforce R1: at least one alternative route for Service-contract customers; maintain carrier diversification; monitor disruption impact. |
| **Data/model rebound** | Low-precision discovered model, emergency and partial-shipment variants | Misclassification of path or segment can cause the wrong “green” decision, shifting risk to service or quality. | Use conformance checking, exception log, path-level monitoring, and periodic process-model validation rather than relying only on the discovered model. |

## 6. Balanced recommendation

Recommend a **coherent, segment-specific redesign package**, not isolated green rules.

### 1. Shipping and promise governance

Implement **S1 + S4 + S5 + S8 + S9 + S12** as one decision layer:

- Default to `parcel_ground` or `consolidated_truck` when the probability of meeting the promised lead time is above 85%.
- For **Standard-stock Service-contract** orders, replace air with **priority pick + ground** unless promised-lead-time slack is insufficient or ground reliability is poor.
- Use **twice-daily consolidated truck departures** for non-urgent stock orders that can absorb 12–24h consolidation wait.
- For **Critical-downtime**, split cases into:
  - **True downtime:** may use `dedicated_van` or shared emergency lane with explicit justification.
  - **Precautionary urgent:** use priority ground, consolidated truck, or air only when slack requires it.
- Use **shared emergency courier lanes** where they can meet the required service level, reducing dedicated-van empty returns.
- Require a **sustainability exception log** for every SP1, SP2, or Q1 override.

This avoids the lowest-emission trap: air and dedicated vans are not banned. They are reserved for cases where service or downtime protection genuinely requires them.

### 2. Production and batching governance

Implement **S2 + S3 + S7**:

- Use **dynamic batching** for `CNC_A` and heat treatment, but with **segment-specific maximum waiting-time caps**.
- Prefer batch sizes where energy benefit is evidenced, such as `CNC_A` batch 12–20 and heat-treatment batches above 10, but only when due-date slack and demand confidence support the wait.
- Shift eligible Made-to-order cases from `CNC_B` to `CNC_A` when `CNC_A` queue and due-date slack allow it, because `CNC_A` has lower rework.
- Keep `CNC_B` available for tight due dates or low-risk parts, but apply a **quality gate** for high-rework machine/part combinations.
- Monitor heat-treatment batch wait carefully because waiting for heat-treatment batches is associated with 1.7x higher late-delivery risk.

This balances energy reduction against waiting, late delivery, and obsolescence.

### 3. Packaging and quality governance

Implement **S6 + S7**:

- Add a pre-shipment **packaging-route compatibility check** to prevent `Rework packaging` loops.
- Enforce Q1: Made-to-order parts over 8 kg use reinforced packaging unless inspection certifies low damage risk.
- Use reinforced packaging where it reduces damage-related returns, but avoid over-packaging by tracking `package_material_kg` and return rates.
- Use inspection quality gates to stop rushed inspections from creating later rework, scrap, and customer returns.

This reduces hidden material waste and improves quality without simply increasing packaging material.

### 4. Customer and order-completeness governance

Implement **S10 + S11**:

- For **Regular Made-to-order** customers, introduce carbon-aware promise-date negotiation: a lower-carbon option with ground/consolidated transport and dynamic batching, versus an expedited option with higher emissions.
- Reduce partial shipments by changing availability-check rules:
  - Ship full order when waiting is acceptable.
  - Ship partial only if the customer accepts it and the missing line item is not critical to downtime.
  - Avoid backorder loops that create second shipments and high late-delivery risk.

This addresses customer behavior and avoids shifting service risk into cancellations or dissatisfaction.

### Why this package is balanced

- **Environmental impact:** reduces unnecessary air and dedicated-van transport, consolidates eligible ground shipments, improves batching energy efficiency, reduces rework, packaging rework, and obsolescence.
- **Service:** uses promised-lead-time slack and route reliability before shifting to lower-carbon modes; protects true Critical-downtime cases; keeps air available when service requires it.
- **Quality:** improves first-pass yield through CNC_A routing and inspection gates; reduces damage returns through packaging compatibility and Q1 compliance.
- **Resilience:** maintains alternative routes for Service-contract customers, avoids over-dependence on one carrier or fixed consolidation departure, keeps production capacity flexible, and creates an auditable exception process.

Acceptable deterioration should be defined operationally, for example:
- No material increase in Service-contract late delivery or p90 cycle time.
- Critical-downtime late delivery remains near its current low level.
- Made-to-order rework and damage returns do not increase.
- R1 alternative-route availability is maintained during carrier disruption.
- Batch obsolescence does not increase as a result of larger batches.

## 7. Validation and governance plan

### 1. Data enrichment and baseline

Before full rollout, enrich the event log with:
- Line-item identifiers to support partial shipment, backorder, and batch obsolescence analysis.
- Route decision event: recommended route, selected route, reason, and approver.
- Batch release event: batch size, forecast confidence, due-date slack, and maximum allowed wait.
- Inspection certification event: pass/fail, low-damage-risk certification, and quality gate result.
- Packaging compatibility check event: package type, route mode, part weight/geometry, and Q1 result.
- Exception log event: policy overridden, justification, approver, alternative considered, and expected service benefit.
- Carrier disruption and empty-return indicators where available.
- Damage-related return or customer-return indicator where available.

Baseline KPIs should be calculated by:
- Segment: `case_type` × `customer_type`.
- Path: stock/ground, stock/air, partial/backorder, MTO CNC_A, MTO CNC_B, heat treatment, rework, Critical dedicated van, Critical stock/ground exception.
- Policy: SP1, SP2, Q1, R1.

Because the discovered model has low precision, use **conformance checking and path-level variant analysis** rather than relying only on the discovered process model.

### 2. Shadow mode and phased rollout

**Phase 1: Shadow mode**
- Run the green shipping recommender and batching rules in shadow mode.
- Compare recommended route/batch decision with actual decision.
- Estimate counterfactual transport emissions, cycle time, and late-delivery risk.

**Phase 2: Low-risk pilots**
- Pilot S4 for Standard-stock Service-contract air replacement where ground on-time probability is high.
- Pilot S5 twice-daily consolidation for non-urgent Standard-stock Regular orders.
- Pilot S6 packaging-route compatibility for MTO heavy parts.

**Phase 3: Production and critical pilots**
- Pilot S2 dynamic batching with segment caps.
- Pilot S3 CNC_B-to-CNC_A routing where slack allows.
- Pilot S8 shared emergency lanes for true Critical-downtime cases.
- Pilot S9 Critical-downtime classification.

**Phase 4: Full rollout with governance**
- Expand only where service, quality, and resilience KPIs remain within agreed bands.

### 3. Conformance monitoring

Monitor continuously:

| Policy | Indicator | Target direction |
|---|---|---|
| **SP1** | Share of cases using air/dedicated van when ground/consolidated on-time probability >85% | Reduce from observed 22% in Standard-stock Service-contract cases toward single digits |
| **SP2** | Share of dedicated-van cases with explicit Critical-downtime justification | Reduce missing justification from observed 31% |
| **Q1** | Share of MTO parts over 8 kg shipped without reinforced packaging or low-damage-risk certification | Reduce and correlate with return/rework loops |
| **R1** | Share of Service-contract cases with at least one alternative route available during carrier disruption | Maintain high compliance |
| **Exception log** | Share of policy overrides with complete justification and approval | Target near-complete completeness |

### 4. Segment-level service monitoring

Monitor by `case_type` and `customer_type`:

- **Standard-stock Regular:** late delivery, p90 cycle time, partial shipment rate, cancellation due to delay.
- **Standard-stock Service-contract:** late delivery, p90 cycle time, air share, SP1 violations, ground reliability.
- **Made-to-order Regular:** cycle time, late delivery, `expedite_flag`, batch wait, promise-date acceptance, cancellation.
- **Made-to-order Service-contract:** cycle time, late delivery, rework, batch wait, heat-treatment wait, SP1 violations.
- **Critical-downtime Service-contract:** median/p90 cycle time, late delivery, dedicated-van share, true vs precautionary classification, SP2 justification.

Alert if:
- Service-contract late delivery increases materially.
- Critical-downtime p90 cycle time worsens.
- Made-to-order rework or damage returns increase.
- Batch obsolescence increases after batching changes.
- Partial-shipment backorder loops increase after availability-rule changes.

### 5. Environmental and quality KPIs

Track:
- `energy_kwh/order` and energy per produced item by `machine_group` and `batch_size`.
- `transport_kgco2e/order` by `route_mode` and segment.
- Share of `parcel_air`, `dedicated_van`, `consolidated_truck`, and `parcel_ground`.
- `material_scrap_kg/order`.
- `package_material_kg/order`.
- Rework packaging count.
- Rework production loop count.
- First-pass yield.
- Damage-related returns.
- Batch items not shipped within 14 days.
- Empty-return exposure for dedicated vans where measurable.

### 6. Process-mining validation after implementation

After implementation, compare before/after event-log evidence:

- **Shipping path changes:**
  - `Stock -> standard pack -> air` should decrease for eligible Standard-stock Service-contract cases.
  - `Stock -> standard pack -> consolidated truck` should increase for non-urgent eligible cases.
  - `Critical -> emergency production -> dedicated van` should decrease for precautionary cases and remain justified for true downtime cases.

- **Production path changes:**
  - `Plan production -> Batch wait -> CNC production` should show batch sizes in the intended range.
  - Batch wait should remain within segment caps.
  - `CNC_B -> Inspection -> Rework production` should decrease for shifted cases.
  - `Inspection -> Rework production` should decrease overall.

- **Packaging path changes:**
  - `Package standard/reinforced -> Rework packaging` should decrease.
  - Q1 violations should decrease.
  - Damage-related returns should decrease for MTO heavy parts.

- **Order-completeness changes:**
  - `Pick from stock -> Partial shipment -> Backorder wait` should decrease.
  - Cancellation due to delay should not increase.
  - Customer acceptance of partial shipment should be explicit where partial shipment occurs.

- **Resilience signals:**
  - Alternative-route availability for Service-contract customers should remain high.
  - Carrier disruption impact should be visible in route-mode substitution and late delivery.
  - Exception log should show whether overrides are justified and whether they correlate with service protection.

### 7. Exception governance

The sustainability exception log should capture:
- Case ID and segment.
- Policy overridden: SP1, SP2, Q1, or R1.
- Reason code: tight slack, carrier disruption, true downtime, customer contract requirement, quality risk, capacity constraint.
- Alternative route or packaging considered.
- Approver and approval timestamp.
- Expected service benefit.
- Actual outcome: late delivery, return, rework, customer complaint.

Governance cadence:
- **Weekly:** review exception rate, SP1/SP2/Q1 violations, late delivery, rework, and carrier disruption.
- **Monthly:** root-cause review of high-impact exceptions and new process variants.
- **Quarterly:** update route reliability estimates, batching caps, machine routing rules, and packaging compatibility rules.

The redesign should be considered successful when environmental KPIs improve **and** service, quality, and resilience KPIs remain within agreed operational bands, with exceptions explicitly justified rather than silently occurring.
