## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

**Scenario S1 (smooth).**
- **Chemistry.** Friday's remaining capacity is 2.7 analyst-hours. Four samples are ahead of B-417 (FIFO) or one urgent sample is ahead (priority). Friday service cannot finish before the 18:00 close, so B-417 pauses. Monday 08:00 resumes the work. Under priority, B-417 finishes ~Mon 10:00. Under FIFO, it finishes ~Mon 14:30–15:00. The combined central estimate is Mon 10:00–15:00.
- **Microbiology.** Ready Sun 12:00–20:00. No authorization means the readout starts Mon 08:00 and finishes Mon 08:30–09:30. The weekend pause adds roughly 12–20 h of wait but is 2–5 h shorter than the chemistry branch on this path. With authorization (p0.25–0.45), micro finishes Sunday evening, which saves nothing on the smooth path.
- **Join (maximum, not sum).** Join = max(chemistry, microbiology)  Mon 10:00–15:00. Chemistry is the binding branch in most sampled cases. Microbiology binds only if chemistry is fast (near the minimum service time under priority).
- **QA.** The backlog at Monday 08:00 is about 4–5 batches (5 current, plus ~1.6 arrivals on Friday, ~1.4 on Saturday, minus ~1.8 and ~1.6 served). Monday 08:00–12:00 with two reviewers clears ~3 batches, leaving ~2–3 ahead of B-417. The wait is roughly 2–5 h, so QA starts around Mon 14:00–17:00. Service has a 1.5 h median and 2.5 h mean, so it finishes Mon 16:00–20:00. About 20–25% of smooth cases run past 20:00 and pause until Tue 08:00.
- **Release.** +0.25–0.75 h  Mon 16:30–21:00.

**Scenario S2 (chemistry rerun).**
- The first chemistry result is borderline, usually around Mon 12:00–14:40. The rerun takes priority and lasts ~1.8 h. If it starts before ~16:00, it finishes the same day. If it starts later, it pauses to Tue 08:00.
- The chemistry join moves to Mon 14:00–17:00 (same day) or Tue 08:00–10:00 (paused).
- QA then waits for both branches. A late join lands QA service after 18:00, so the release falls on Tuesday morning. The earliest time is Mon ~15:00 (~72 h).

**Scenario S3 (QA documentation query).**
- QA reviews on Monday afternoon, finds missing information, and raises a query. The working-hours clock runs from Tuesday 08:00. The query adds 4–12 QA working hours, which is roughly Tuesday to Wednesday morning. A re-review takes ~1.5–2 h, which adds a QA wait.
- Release lands Tue 12:00–Wed 10:00 (92–116 h), with a tail to Wed afternoon or Thu if the investigation overlaps.

**Scenario S4 (microbiology contamination).**
- Indication is read Mon ~09:00 (65.5 h). Confirmation incubation runs continuously for 20–30 h, so it is ready Tue 05:00–15:00. The readout needs a microbiologist from Tue 08:00 and takes 0.5–1.5 h, so the confirmed result is Tue 08:30–16:30.
- This is a true sum, not a maximum: indication  incubation  readout. The weekend pause sits in the readout step, not the incubation step.
- If the defect is confirmed critical and no investigation is opened, the outcome is rejection after QA review, around Tue evening or Wed (100–120 h).
- If an investigation is opened (30–55%), the documentation-only branch adds 8–20 working hours (Wed–Fri, 140–165 h). The laboratory branch adds 3–7 calendar days (Thu–Mon, 145–240 h).

**Scenario S5 (laboratory investigation).**
- Opened Monday or Tuesday. Calendar days are continuous, so the clock runs through the weekend. Terminal: Thu 24 Sep to Mon 28 Sep, with a tail to Tue 29.

**Scenario S6 (temperature excursion confirmed).**
- The excursion is a common latent state. Given confirmation, the rerun probability rises to ~0.25–0.35 and contamination to ~0.10–0.20. The investigation probability rises to 0.30–0.55.
- This is a joint shift, not an additive one. The expected number of extra delays is larger than an independence calculation would suggest. Its timing depends on whether confirmation happens before or after QA release. If it arrives after release, a recall is a separate event, outside the terminal time scope.

**Where maximum replaces sum.** Join = max(chemistry clock, micro clock). The remaining chemistry time is not added to the micro readout. Only one of these branches is on the critical path in a given sample.

**Where pauses matter.** Friday 18:00 (chemistry closes), Saturday–Sunday (no routine microbiology, no Sunday QA), Monday–Tuesday QA closure at 20:00, and the 8–20 working-hour investigation clock.

**Most plausible terminal events.**
1. **Electronic Release on Mon 21 Sep, ~16:30–21:00** (smooth path, ~45–55%, most likely single outcome).
2. **Electronic Release on Tue 22 Sep, morning or early afternoon** (rerun, QA spillover, or late chemistry).
3. **Electronic Release on Tue 22–Wed 23** after a documentation query.
4. **Batch Rejected** is less probable overall but occurs in the contamination branch, around Tue evening to Thu.

**Note on the expected terminal time.** Do not report a mean. The distribution is multimodal: a Monday cluster, a Tuesday–Wednesday cluster, and a thin lab-investigation tail.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

1. **Documentation-query and investigation path (path uncertainty).** Probability 0.15–0.30 for a query and 0.30–0.55 for investigation under abnormal states. Each step adds one to three working days. This is the main driver of the P80–P90 spread.
2. **Temperature-excursion dependence (dependence uncertainty).** A single unknown state raises the rerun, contamination, and investigation probabilities together. It is the main reason the compound tail is fatter than the product of marginals.
3. **QA backlog at the join (queue uncertainty).** The Monday-noon backlog is 1–5 batches, and each batch adds ~1.25 h with two reviewers. This sets whether a smooth release lands Monday evening or Tuesday morning.
4. **Chemistry queue discipline and rerun timing (queue plus service uncertainty).** FIFO versus priority shifts the join by ~4–5 h. Rerun timing relative to the 18:00 close determines whether a rerun costs hours or a whole day.
5. **Weekend microbiology calendar and authorization (calendar uncertainty).** The Monday 08:00 restart sets the readout time. Authorization (p0.25–0.45) changes the micro clock but has little effect on the smooth path because chemistry binds later. It matters most for contamination confirmation, whose readout lands on Tuesday regardless of authorization.

Observation uncertainty (current residual service times and the actual queue order) is a secondary driver for the first 1–2 days.
