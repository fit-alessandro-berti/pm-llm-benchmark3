- **Assumption A1 (Scan delay bound):** Arrival timestamps $t_{\text{arr}}$ reflect scan times, which may lag physical arrival by $[0, 2]$ minutes: $t_{\text{phys}} \in [t_{\text{arr}} - 2, t_{\text{arr}}]$. A job with $t_{\text{arr}} \le t_{\text{dec}} + 2$ was potentially physically present at decision time $t_{\text{dec}}$.
- **Assumption A2 (Work conservation unless constrained):** Resources start eligible waiting jobs immediately unless blocked by calendar unavailability, shift limits, or deliberate idle-waiting for an upcoming appointment/meeting.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

### Queue QA
- **Supporting primary diagnosis (FIFO with Appointment preemption):**
  - **A01, A02, A04, A05, A06, A07, A08**: Once A03 is removed at 08:20, the remaining seven jobs (`A02` $\rightarrow$ `A04` $\rightarrow$ `A05` $\rightarrow$ `A06` $\rightarrow$ `A07` $\rightarrow$ `A08`) are executed in **strictly increasing order of arrival time** (`08:04`, `08:13`, `08:21`, `08:29`, `08:35`, `08:40`).
  - Crucially, at **08:32**, **A02** (`arr` 08:04, P3, 20m) is selected ahead of **A04** (`arr` 08:13, P1, 7m). At **09:05**, **09:20**, and **09:32**, **A05** (P3), **A06** (P2), and **A07** (P3) are all selected ahead of **A08** (`arr` 08:40, **P1**, 25m). This refutes Priority and SPT as primary disciplines in QA.
  - **A03** (`arr` 08:08, Appt 08:20) jumping ahead of A02 at **08:20** supports an Appointment override when the resource frees up exactly at the appointment time.
- **Contradicting / Requiring explanation:**
  - **A06** (`arr` 08:29, Appt 09:15, P2): At **09:05**, A1 finishes A04. If appointments are strictly honored via lookahead, A1 should either idle for 10m, pick A06 early (5m early), or pick **A07** (`est_min`=9m, finishing at 09:14 $\le$ 09:15). Instead, A1 picks **A05** (`est_min`=15m) by strict FIFO, causing A06's appointment to start 5 minutes late at 09:20. *Explanation:* QA uses a **myopic (non-lookahead) appointment rule**: an appointment jumps the FIFO queue only if it is already due (or within a tight tolerance, e.g., $\le 5$ mins away) at the moment the resource becomes free. At 09:05, 09:15 was still 10 minutes away, so FIFO prevailed; at 09:20, A06 was overdue AND next in FIFO anyway.
  - **2-minute gaps (08:00–08:02 and 08:30–08:32):** Explained by setup/dispatch lag or arrival scan timing.

### Queue QB
- **Supporting primary diagnosis (Preemptive Priority with resumption/dispatcher damping):**
  - **B02** (`arr` 09:14, P1) preempts **B01** (P3) at **09:16** (2m dispatch/checkpoint lag).
  - **B05** (`arr` 09:46, P1) is selected at **10:00** ahead of paused **B01** (P3) and waiting **B04** (P3, 8m).
  - **B01** resumes at **10:14** after B05 finishes, ahead of **B04** (P3) and **B06** (`arr` 10:05, P2), showing that an already-started job retains priority over later arrivals unless preempted.
- **Contradicting / Requiring explanation:**
  - **B03** (`arr` 09:20, P2) vs **B01** (P3) and **B05** (`arr` 09:46, P1): Why did B01 resume at **09:31** over B03 (P2), only to be preempted by B03 at **09:48** ("second stop: dispatcher reassignment")? And why did B03 start at **09:48** when **B05** (P1, `arr` 09:46) had already arrived 2 minutes earlier?
    - *Explanation 1 (Preemption policy + Scan/Dispatch delay):* Automatic preemption only triggers for **P1** (hence B03 P2 could not preempt B01 at 09:31). At 09:48, a human dispatcher manually intervened to reassign B1 to B03 because B03 had been waiting 28 minutes. Why didn't the dispatcher pick B05 (P1)? Either B05's physical arrival/triage into the dispatch screen lagged its 09:46 scan, or the dispatcher had already locked in B03's reassignment just before 09:46.
  - **10:28 decision (B04 vs B06 and B07):** At 10:28, **B04** (`arr` 09:35, P3, 8m) is chosen over **B06** (`arr` 10:05, **P2**, 20m) and **B07** (`arr` 10:08, P3, 6m, **Appt 10:30**). This directly contradicts pure Priority (which predicts B06) and Appointment scheduling (which predicts B07). It requires an undocumented **aging / max-wait threshold** (B04 had waited 53 minutes) or a batch/cutoff rule where jobs from the 09:xx hour must clear before 10:xx arrivals.

### Queue QC
- **Supporting primary diagnosis (SPT with SLA/Deadline emergency override):**
  - **C02** (8m) $\rightarrow$ **C03** (14m) $\rightarrow$ **C04** (22m) at **10:10, 10:18, 10:32**, and **C06** (6m) $\rightarrow$ **C01** (36m) at **11:34, 11:40**: Among the initial backlog `{C01(36m), C02(8m), C03(14m), C04(22m)}`, execution follows exact SPT order, leaving the oldest job **C01** (`arr` 10:00) for last.
- **Contradicting / Requiring explanation:**
  - **10:32 decision (C04 over C06 and C05):** At 10:32, **C06** (`arr` 10:25, `est_min`=6m) was already waiting, yet **C04** (`est_min`=22m) was chosen! *Explanation:* **Gated / Frozen Batch SPT**. The initial backlog present when C1 became available at 10:10 (`C01–C04`) was sorted by SPT into a fixed schedule (`C02, C03, C04, C01`), or C06 was held in an intake buffer until 10:54.
  - **10:18 & 10:32 vs 10:54 (C05 P1, `est_min`=40m, DL 10:50):** Why was C05 ignored at 10:18 (when its 10:50 deadline was already unreachable given `est_min`=40m: $10:18 + 40\text{m} = 10:58 > 10:50$) and at 10:32, but selected at **10:54** (4 minutes *after* its deadline expired)? *Explanation:* The queue operates under SPT by default, with an **SLA-breach escalation trigger** that promotes a job to the head of the queue *only after* its deadline is actually breached ($t \ge \text{deadline}$, i.e., $10:54 > 10:50$), or after a fixed waiting-time threshold.
  - **11:40 decision (C01 over C07):** At 11:40, **C01** (P3, 36m, DL 13:00) is chosen over **C07** (`arr` 10:40, P2, 18m, DL 12:00). Selecting C01 (36m) guarantees that C07 will miss its 12:00 deadline (C01 finishes at 12:16)! This contradicts pure SPT (18m < 36m), Priority (P2 > P3), and EDD (12:00 < 13:00), strongly supporting the **Gated Batch** hypothesis (C01 was the final unexecuted member of the 10:10 backlog wave, or had hit a 100-minute anti-starvation cap).

### Queue QD
- **Supporting primary diagnosis (Skill-Based Routing + Calendar-aware non-preemptive scheduling):**
  - Every job is assigned to a resource matching all required skills (`D01, D04, D08, D09` $\rightarrow$ D-M; `D02, D03, D05` $\rightarrow$ D-F; `D06, D07, D10` $\rightarrow$ D-X).
  - Multi-skill jobs **D05** (`firmware+electrical`) and **D07** (`mechanical+electrical`) are routed exclusively to the single resource possessing the composite skill set (D-F and D-X, respectively).
  - **D08** (P1, 8m) is chosen over **D09** (P3, 20m) by D-M at **11:35**.
  - **D05** (`est_min`=30m) is held by D-F at **11:36** because 30m exceeds the 24m remaining before D-F's `12:00-12:20` meeting (showing non-preemptive calendar lookahead).
- **Contradicting / Requiring explanation:**
  - **D-F idling 11:36–12:00 instead of taking D10 (`arr` 11:25, P1, `elec`, 12m):** At 11:36, D-F is free for 24 minutes before its 12:00 meeting. D10 requires `electrical` (which D-F has), is **Priority 1**, and takes only `est_min`=12m (would finish at 11:48 $\ll$ 12:00). Yet the note on D10 says *"D-F still unavailable until 12:20"*. *Explanation:* Either (a) D-F's unavailability actually began at **11:36** (e.g., pre-meeting prep or travel, with only 12:00–12:20 logged as the formal meeting), or (b) **static queue partitioning at arrival**: D10 was hard-assigned to D-X upon arrival at 11:25 (or D-F's queue was locked for D05 next, blocking D10 from jumping over D05 on D-F).
  - **D-X idling 11:20–11:30:** D-X becomes available at 11:20 and waits 10m for D06's 11:30 appointment, even though **D08** (`arr` 11:16, P1, `mech`, 8m) was waiting and would have finished at 11:28 (2m before D06's appointment). *Explanation:* D-X was dedicated/pre-assigned to D06 upon shift start, or a safety buffer (e.g., $\ge 10\text{m}$) prevents starting another job before an appointment.
  - **D-X at 11:48 choosing D07 (P3, 28m) over D10 (P1, 12m):** Both D07 and D10 were waiting and eligible for D-X at 11:48. Choosing D07 violates Priority (P3 vs P1) and SPT (28m vs 12m). *Explanation:* Either **FIFO within D-X's sub-queue** (`D07` arrived 11:14 vs `D10` at 11:25) or **Specialist Skill Priority** (D07 requires `mech+elec` which *only* D-X can do, whereas D10 only requires `elec`).

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

### Test 1: Queue QC at 10:18 and 10:54 — *SPT + Post-Breach Escalation* vs. *Gated-Batch SPT + Priority Override*
- **The Ambiguous Episode:** In QC, **C06** (`arr` 10:25, `est_min`=6m, P3) is ignored at **10:32** in favor of **C04** (`arr` 10:07, `est_min`=22m, P3), and **C05** (`arr` 10:11, `est_min`=40m, P1, DL 10:50) is ignored at **10:18** and **10:32** before finally being selected at **10:54**.
- **Competing Explanations:**
  1. **Hypothesis $H_{\text{breach}}$ (Dynamic SPT + Hard Deadline-Breach Override):** The queue dynamically evaluates all waiting jobs by SPT, *except* that any job whose deadline has actively passed ($t_{\text{dec}} \ge \text{deadline}$) receives immediate preemptive top priority. (Explains C05 at 10:54, though C04 over C06 at 10:32 requires assuming C06 wasn't cleared for dispatch until >10:32).
  2. **Hypothesis $H_{\text{wave}}$ (Wave/Batch SPT + Aging Threshold):** Jobs arriving before C1 starts work at 10:10 (`C01–C04`) form Wave 1; jobs arriving after 10:10 (`C05–C07`) form Wave 2, except that P1 jobs have a maximum wait SLA of 40 minutes (so C05, arriving at 10:11, becomes mandatory at $10:11 + 40\text{m} = 10:51 \approx 10:54$).
- **Counterfactual Next-Job Prediction:**
  - Suppose a new job **C08** arrives at **10:45** with **P1**, `est_min` = **15m**, and **Deadline = 12:00**. At **10:54**, what is selected?
  - Under **$H_{\text{breach}}$**: **C05** is still chosen at 10:54 because its deadline (10:50) is actively breached, whereas C08's deadline (12:00) is not breached. At **11:34**, C06 (6m) is chosen before C08 (15m).
  - Under **$H_{\text{wave}}$ (with P1 max-wait cap)**: At 10:54, C05 has waited 43m (>40m cap) so C05 is chosen; but if C05's deadline had been **11:30** instead of 10:50, $H_{\text{breach}}$ predicts **C06 (6m)** at 10:54, whereas $H_{\text{wave}}$ still predicts **C05** at 10:54.
- **Discriminating Observable:** The **system SLA state / escalation timestamp (`escalation_flag_timestamp`)** or **dispatch queue snapshot (`eligible_pool_id`)** at 10:32 and 10:54—specifically whether C05's priority weight changed at 10:50 (deadline breach) vs. 10:51 (40m wait timer), and whether C06 was in the active dispatch pool at 10:32.

### Test 2: Queue QD at 11:35 (D-M) and 11:48 (D-X) — *Resource-Local FIFO with Dedicated Assignment at Arrival* vs. *Global Pool with Skill-Rarity / Priority Routing*
- **The Ambiguous Episode:** At **11:35**, D-M picks **D08** (`arr` 11:16, P1, 8m) over **D09** (`arr` 11:20, P3, 20m). Here **FIFO, Priority, and SPT all predict D08**. Meanwhile, at **11:48**, D-X picks **D07** (`arr` 11:14, P3, 28m, `mech+elec`) over **D10** (`arr` 11:25, P1, 12m, `elec`), while D-F sat idle from 11:36 to 12:00 without taking D10.
- **Competing Explanations:**
  1. **Hypothesis $H_{\text{local-FIFO}}$ (Push-Routing at Arrival + Local FIFO + Appointment Lock):** Upon arrival, the router immediately pushes each job into a specific resource's local FIFO queue based on skills and estimated workload. D05 was pushed to D-F (blocking D-F from 11:36 to 12:00 because D-F only looks at the head of its own FIFO queue, which is 30m D05!). D06, D07, and D10 were pushed to D-X's local queue; D-X executed D06 (appt), then D07 (`arr` 11:14), then D10 (`arr` 11:25) in strict **FIFO** order! And D-M executed D01 (`11:00`), D04 (`11:05`), D08 (`11:16`), D09 (`11:20`) in strict **FIFO** order!

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked from most plausible (1) to least plausible (5):

1. **Push-Routing at Arrival with Head-of-Line (HOL) Blocking in QD (High Plausibility)**
   - *Mechanism:* Instead of a shared pull pool, jobs in QD are assigned to a specific technician's personal queue at arrival. Once assigned, each technician works their personal queue in **strict FIFO** (with appointment overrides).

2. **Unlogged Triage / Intake Buffering Delay Beyond the 2-Minute Scan Lag (High Plausibility)**
   - *Mechanism:* The prompt notes arrival scans can be delayed up to 2 minutes, but after scan, a job may require physical staging or dispatcher triage before becoming *eligible* for dispatch.

3. **Unrecorded Pre-Meeting / Post-Job Wrap-Up Unavailable Time (Moderate-High Plausibility)**
   - *Mechanism:* Resource calendar exceptions only log formal events (e.g., D-F's `12:00-12:20` meeting, A1's `08:52-08:58` calibration), omitting prep time or cleanup.
   - *How it alters the diagnosis:* Explains the 2-minute gap on A1 between A03 (`completion` 08:30) and A02 (`start` 08:32), and explains why D10's note states *"D-F still unavailable until 12:20"* when D-F finished D03 at 11:36. If D-F was actually unavailable 11:36–12:20, QD no longer violates work conservation on D-F.
4. **Manual Dispatcher Intervention / Starvation Prevention (Moderate Plausibility)**
   - *Mechanism:* Human dispatchers periodically override automated rules when a job's waiting time crosses a pain threshold (e.g., ~45–60 minutes) or when a customer complains.
   - *How it alters the diagnosis:* Directly documented in QB at 09:48 (*"second stop: dispatcher reassignment"* for B03) and explains why B04 (waiting 53m) was picked at 10:28 over P2 B06 and Appt B07, as well as why C01 (waiting 100m) was picked at 11:40 over P2/urgent C07.
5. **Strategic or Noisy `est_min` Manipulation (Low-Moderate Plausibility)**
   - *Mechanism:* Because every single job in QA, QB, QC, and QD has a realized total service duration **identically equal** to `est_min` to the exact minute (e.g., A01 `est_min`=18, `08:02-08:20`=18m; B01 `est_min`=45, `14+17+14`=45m), either `est_min` in this export was retroactively overwritten by realized duration, or work is paced/capped strictly to the estimate.
   - *How it alters the diagnosis:* If `est_min` in the table was overwritten post-hoc by actual duration, then a job that appeared short at arrival (or vice versa) might have been selected under SPT even where the table shows an SPT violation (e.g., C04 vs C06, or B04 vs B07).
