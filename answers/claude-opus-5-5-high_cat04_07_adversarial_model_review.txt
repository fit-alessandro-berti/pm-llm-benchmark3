# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

## Use cases distinguished

| Use case | What a wrong model costs | Acceptable fitness/precision trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Exploration / discovery** (variant overviews, frequency and performance overlays, hypothesis generation) | Misleading insights, but no automated decisions | High fitness on real behaviour matters most, so moderate imprecision is tolerable **if it is labelled**. Structural errors that change the story (for example, "checks are optional") are still harmful because analysts draw conclusions from them. |
| **Conformance enforcement / compliance monitoring** (automated alerts, audit sampling, control evidence) | False negatives (forbidden cases pass silently) create regulatory and financial risk. False positives (legitimate cases flagged) create alert fatigue and eventually lead people to ignore alerts. | Precision **against policy** is the priority: every hard-policy-forbidden trace must be rejected. Fitness on *legitimate* behaviour still matters, because a model that rejects valid cases stops being used. Precision computed only against the observed log is not a substitute. |
| **Simulation / what-if analysis** | Wrong capacity, cycle-time, or risk estimates | Needs *representative* behaviour: correct routing probabilities, rework loops, and attribute-dependent branches (external vs. internal). Over-permissive models simulate impossible paths, such as skipping checks. Over-fitted models cannot simulate new mixes. |
| **Stakeholder communication / training** | Misunderstood process rules | Comprehensibility matters, but a picture that is easy to read and **wrong** about mandatory steps is worse than a harder one that is right. |
| **Change management / maintenance** | Inconsistent updates, lost ownership | Modularity and a one-to-one mapping from policy clauses to model elements matter more than any conformance number. |

The use case determines which error type is expensive. For exploration, Model S's permissiveness is a known bias. For enforcement, it is disqualifying. For enforcement, Model C's rejection of unseen valid behaviour (F2, F4) is also disqualifying unless the organisation explicitly adopts a whitelist regime (F8). Even then, C would have to contain all *approved* behaviour, and it does not: it rejects B2, which the policy explicitly calls valid.

## Assumptions

1. The model strings use PM4Py process-tree syntax: `->` sequence, `X` exclusive choice, `+` concurrency (interleaving), `*` loop, and `tau` a silent step. "Accepts a trace" means an optimal alignment of cost 0, i.e. trace-level alignment fitness 1.0. A high but imperfect fitness is **not** acceptance. Section 5 shows why threshold-based acceptance is dangerous here.
2. Events are atomic (complete lifecycle only). Timestamps are assumed to give a correct total order. Ties or bulk-logged events would undermine the order-based claims (P2, P4, P7).
3. The log contains `Correct Dossier`, because Model C has a branch for it. Model S does not contain that activity at all.
4. The sourcing attribute (external/internal) and the affected-check attribute exist in some source system but not in the discovery log. They can in principle be joined back in.
5. The reported numbers are treated as unverified claims. No seeds, preprocessing, cost functions, or holdout case IDs were supplied, so the results are not reproducible and the "same split" claim cannot be verified.
6. To check structural claims, I converted the two expressions to Petri nets with PM4Py 2.7 (`pm4py.convert_to_petri_net`) and aligned **only the synthetic probe traces** against them, using default costs. These are properties of the models and probes, not new results on the business log. Where cited, they are labelled as such.
7. Policy hierarchy: P1–P7 plus probes marked "valid" or "forbidden" are **hard** requirements. "Plausible", "policy-dependent", and unmentioned orderings are **open** and must go to the process owner, not be decided silently by a model.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

## Strongest case for Model S

- It gets the **structural skeleton** right: Register first, Release last, exactly one of Manager/Auto Approval. Those are P1 and P5.
- It lets the two checks occur in either order (P2), treats Supplier Review as optional (the only defensible choice while the sourcing attribute is missing, P3), and treats Expedite Review as optional.
- It generalises to new *combinations* of known activities: holdout fitness 0.981 vs. 0.992 on training. It accepts B2, B5 and B7, which Model C partly rejects. That directly answers F2 and F4.
- It is cheap to align, has no duplicate labels, and is easy to update (one leaf per activity).
- Its imprecision is **honest**: nobody reading S would believe it enforces anything, and F7 explicitly scopes it to exploration. For frequency or performance overlays, a permissive model that fits nearly every case loses little information.
- Accidentally, it does reject B8 (the unexplained repeated Credit Check), because it has no repetition at all.

## Strongest case against Model S

- It turns all four review activities into `X(activity, tau)` inside one concurrency block. That makes the **mandatory** checks optional and puts Expedite Review **in parallel** with the checks it must follow. That breaks P2 and P4, and it accepts the forbidden probes B3 and B4 with perfect fitness.
- It lacks `Correct Dossier` entirely. It cannot represent P6 at all, and it rejects every legitimate correction case (B6, multi-round corrections). It rejects B8 for the wrong reason: it also rejects every legitimate recheck. So S is simultaneously **too permissive** on checks and **too strict** on rework.
- Its high fitness is largely bought with silent steps. A missing Credit Check costs a silent move (cost 1 in PM4Py's default scheme) instead of a model move (cost 10,000), so fitness counts it as perfect.
- As an exploration model it still misleads. Variant analysis projected onto S cannot show rework, and any conformance overlay tells analysts that "skipping both checks" is normal behaviour.

## Concrete undesirable or policy-ambiguous behaviours admitted by S

Each trace below is accepted by S with alignment fitness 1.0 (checked on the probe traces with PM4Py default costs).

| # | Trace | Why it matters | Cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| S-1 | `Register Dossier, Manager Approval, Release Order` (B3) | Approval without either mandatory check. Violates P2. | Mandatory checks made optional through `tau`. This is *noise tolerance applied to the wrong activities*, i.e. a semantic flaw. |
| S-2 | `Register Dossier, Technical Review, Auto Approval, Release Order` | Credit Check skipped before an **automatic** approval, the highest-risk combination. | Same as S-1. |
| S-3 | `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` (B4) | Expedite before both checks. Violates P4. | **Accidental semantic flaw**: Expedite Review was placed in the same concurrency block as the checks. No amount of noise tolerance requires that. |

**Intentional noise tolerance vs. accidental flaws.** Optional Supplier Review (P3), optional Expedite Review (P4), free order of the two checks (P2), and exclusive approval (P5) are intentional and policy-consistent. Optional Credit Check and Technical Review are *noise tolerance* (robustness to missing events). That is defensible for exploration only, and even there it should be done by alignment costs, not by changing the model's language. Placing Expedite Review concurrent with the checks, and omitting Correct Dossier, are *accidental semantic flaws*: no stated data-quality problem justifies them. They look like artefacts of an infrequent-behaviour filter plus a concurrency fallback in the discovery algorithm.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

## Strongest case for Model C

- Every path it accepts was actually observed. When C says a case is conformant, that verdict is backed by real, repeated behaviour.
- It correctly rejects the three forbidden or suspicious probes B3, B4 and B8. For an enforcement use, false negatives are the expensive error, and C commits none on the probe set.
- Its logic matches a legitimate control philosophy (F8): in regulated environments, "deny unless approved" whitelisting is common, and a variant catalogue can be reviewed line by line by an auditor.
- It conservatively rejects B6, which the policy says is valid only if the correction affected Technical Review alone, and the log cannot tell us that. Rejecting until evidence arrives is defensible.
- Precision of 0.941 means it adds little behaviour beyond what was observed, so it is useful as documentation of *what actually happens*.

## Strongest case against Model C

**Branch enumeration.** C is an exclusive choice over eight complete sequences. It does not represent *rules*: "checks in any order", "Expedite optional after checks", and "Supplier optional" appear only as whichever combinations happened to be frequent. Its language is exactly 8 traces. Consequences:

- **B2 is rejected**, even though the policy calls it valid (Technical Review before Credit Check followed by Auto Approval). This is a direct falsification of "audit-ready": C is not even a faithful whitelist of *approved* behaviour.
- Expedite Review only appears after Supplier Review and only with Credit Check first. P4 explicitly allows Expedite before *or* after Supplier Review, and P2 allows either check order.
- Auto Approval only appears in one branch, without Supplier Review, Expedite Review or reversed check order. So B5 (plausible valid) is rejected.
- The number of branches grows combinatorially. Check order (2) × Supplier optional (2) × Expedite optional with order relative to Supplier (3) × approval type (2) gives 24 correction-free variants before any rework. C covers 7 of them.

**Duplicate labels.** 48 visible transitions for 9 activities. Register Dossier and Release Order appear 8 times each, Credit Check and Technical Review 9 times each. The duplicates carry **no semantic distinction**; they are copies created by enumeration. This is F3's point: there is no single element that "owns" the Credit Check rule, so impact analysis requires inspecting every branch.

**Unseen combinations.** Any valid combination not in the 8 branches is rejected. With F4's holdout cases (Supplier + Auto Approval + alternate order), this explains the 0.973 → 0.744 fitness drop. Under a threshold regime the results become perverse. On the probe traces, the **forbidden** B4 gets fitness 0.909 against C (one log move against the first branch), while the **plausible valid** B5 gets only 0.750, the same as the forbidden B3. So C's fitness scores do not even *rank* policy compliance correctly.

**Correction handling.** Exactly one correction round is allowed. It always repeats both checks, and always Credit Check first. P6 and F6 say the rechecks depend on what the correction affected (only Credit Check, only Technical Review, or both). A second correction, or a correction affecting one check, is rejected. B6 gets fitness 0.917 against C, higher than valid B2 at 0.800, because it is "one model move away" from the correction branch. C does not encode P6; it memorised one rework variant.

**Holdout degradation.** A 23-point drop is the classic overfitting signature. F8 argues that unseen behaviour should be treated as non-compliant *until approved*. That is a legitimate governance stance, but it requires (a) a model containing all behaviour that has already been approved (C fails on B2), and (b) a fast, cheap process for adding newly approved behaviour. C's structure makes that process expensive and error-prone: adding "Supplier Review allowed with Auto Approval" requires new branches for every check order and Expedite position.

**Maintainability.** A policy change such as "add a sanctions screening step before approval" requires editing all 8 branches consistently. Nothing in the model guarantees consistency. Ownership per activity is impossible because each activity is scattered across branches.

**Is the precision an artefact of memorisation?** Largely, yes. Align-ETC precision measures whether the model allows continuations not seen after observed prefixes. A variant list trivially has few such continuations, so precision close to 1 is *by construction*, not evidence of policy alignment. The fact that it is 0.941 rather than about 1.0 suggests some branches were not observed in the training prefixes. That points to hand-added branches or variants selected on the full log (leakage), which would also inflate C's training fitness. Either way, precision computed on the training log of a model built by listing training variants is circular.

(a) S's `+` of four `X(a, tau)` blocks means each activity occurs at most once, in any interleaving. There is no loop, so the B6 and B8 rejections are certain under standard semantics.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

Ranked by likely impact on the board's decision (1 = highest).

1. **Positive-log-only precision (F5).** Precision was computed only against observed traces, so nobody measured whether either model rejects forbidden behaviour. That is exactly the property that decides enforcement. The metric table gives no hint that S accepts B3 and B4. This single gap could lead the board to deploy a model that approves unchecked orders. *Fix:* a curated negative suite with recall reported per policy clause.

2. **Fitness rewarded by optionalizing mandatory behaviour (with silent-transition effects).** S makes the mandatory checks skippable through `tau`. Under PM4Py's default costs, a silent move costs 1 and a visible model move costs 10,000, and the fitness normalisation effectively ignores silent costs. So a trace missing Credit Check scores 1.0 against S. The 0.992 and 0.981 fitness values partly *measure the flaw*. The same silent steps make token replay depend on invisible-transition heuristics and make precision states harder to interpret. *Fix:* report fitness separately on cases that satisfy hard policy, and on a negative suite.

3. **Train/holdout leakage and split sensitivity.** There are no seeds, no case IDs, and no statement of whether the split was random, temporal, or variant-stratified. C's precision below 1.0 hints that its branches were not all selected on training data. The 0.23 fitness drop for C could come from drift (temporal split), rare-variant allocation (random split with a heavy-tailed variant distribution), or different preprocessing of the two halves. With heavy-tailed variants, a different seed can move holdout fitness by several points. *Fix:* published seeds, multiple repeats, and both temporal and variant-stratified splits.

4. **Missing-event bias (F4).** Missing Supplier Review events make external cases look internal. Every model with an optional Supplier Review accepts them, so the error is invisible. Missing check events are hidden by S (optional checks) but reported by C, so the two models disagree about whether *data quality* problems count as *compliance* problems. Holdout fitness mixes up "unseen legitimate behaviour" with "incomplete recording". *Fix:* perturbation tests, plus a separate data-quality category for alerts.

5. **Generalization rewarded by excessive permissiveness.** Token-based generalization and holdout fitness both reward models that accept more. A flower model would top both. S's 0.907 and 0.981 are partly rewards for accepting forbidden behaviour. *Fix:* report generalization only together with negative-suite rejection.

6. **Duplicated-label effects.** Duplicates split firing counts, which lowers C's token-based generalization mechanically. They enlarge the alignment search space, raising runtime. They make token replay ambiguous (which copy fires?). They inflate size-based complexity. The undocumented "33" does not match the 39 extra copies I count from the expression. *Fix:* report duplication with a definition, and separate semantic duplicates (recheck vs. initial check) from enumeration duplicates.

7. **Alignment-cost sensitivity.** Unknown cost settings. With uniform costs, omitting a mandatory check costs the same as omitting Expedite Review, and B4 (forbidden) costs *less* against C than B5 (plausible valid). Business-severity costs (much higher for a missing mandatory check or Expedite before the checks) would reorder the ranking of deviations and change aggregate fitness. *Fix:* publish costs and run a sensitivity grid.

8. **Stakeholder-score methodology.** An informal 1–5 score with no protocol measures perceived simplicity, probably influenced by the presenter and the rendering. S's 4.4 coexists with the compliance officer's correct conclusion that S is unacceptable, which suggests raters did not grasp what the silent branches permit. *Fix:* a comprehension test with objective questions (Section 9).

9. **Simplicity measure irreproducibility.** Not required by the brief, but material: recomputing arc-degree simplicity from the stated expressions reverses the reported ranking (S about 0.64, C about 0.89). The reported simplicity is therefore either another metric or another artefact. A board should not use a number that cannot be recomputed.

10. **Runtime confounding by model size.** 0.44 s vs 0.018 s reflects C's 50 transitions (vs. 13), plus more non-fitting traces needing larger searches, plus unknown hardware. It says nothing about model quality, and even C's runtime is affordable for batch monitoring. *Fix:* report runtime on the same hardware for fitting and non-fitting traces separately, as a feasibility constraint rather than a quality score.

# 7. Third Alternative

## Strategy

`model_third` is a **balanced POWL backbone with explicit policy layers**, i.e. a procedural model with explicit uncertainty and post-hoc policy checks:

1. **Layer A: procedural backbone (POWL).** It encodes the hard, control-flow-observable policy: P1, P2, P4, P5, P7, and the structural part of P6 (a correction is followed by rechecking a non-empty subset of the checks). It is small, block-structured (so it converts to a process tree, a Petri net and BPMN), and each policy clause maps to one element.
2. **Layer B: declarative constraints (DECLARE).** These are redundant, clause-level rules used for *explanation*: each violation is attributed to a policy clause, and the rules can also be applied to logs where alignments are too expensive.
3. **Layer C: attribute and uncertainty checks (Python).** These cover what control flow cannot decide: P3 (Supplier Review for external orders) and P6 (affected checks). They return one of four statuses: `VIOLATION`, `SUSPICIOUS`, `UNVERIFIABLE` (data missing), or `UNRATIFIED` (behaviour not covered by policy). Missing data is **surfaced**, not silently accepted.

Once the sourcing attribute is joined, Layer A can be specialised into a small attribute-conditioned family (`model_third_external`, `model_third_internal`). The code is given below.

## 1. Compact formal description

Let Checks, Sup and Exp be sub-models:

- **Checks** = (Credit Check ∥ Technical Review) · ( Correct Dossier · ( Credit Check | Technical Review | (Credit Check ∥ Technical Review) ) )*
- **Sup** = Supplier Review | τ
- **Exp** = Expedite Review | τ
- **Appr** = Manager Approval | Auto Approval

`model_third` is the strict partial order over { Register Dossier, Checks, Sup, Exp, Appr, Release Order } with:

- Register Dossier ≺ every other node, and Release Order ≻ every other node;
- Checks ≺ Exp ≺ Appr;
- Sup ≺ Appr;
- Sup is **unordered** with Checks and Exp.

The equivalent process tree (same Petri-net language, verified by conversion) is:

```text
->('Register Dossier',
   +( ->( +('Credit Check','Technical Review'),
          *(tau, ->('Correct Dossier', X('Credit Check','Technical Review', +('Credit Check','Technical Review')))),
          X('Expedite Review', tau) ),
      X('Supplier Review', tau) ),
   X('Manager Approval','Auto Approval'),
   'Release Order')
```

Size: 13 visible leaves for 9 labels. The 4 duplicates are Credit Check ×3 and Technical Review ×3, and they carry a business meaning: initial check, "recheck only this one", and "recheck both". Model C has 48 visible leaves. `pm4py.check_soundness` reports the converted Petri net as sound.

## 2. PM4Py code (self-contained)

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import Transition, SilentTransition, OperatorPOWL, StrictPartialOrder
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

RD, CC, TR = "Register Dossier", "Credit Check", "Technical Review"
SR, ER, CD = "Supplier Review", "Expedite Review", "Correct Dossier"
MA, AA, RO = "Manager Approval", "Auto Approval", "Release Order"


def build_model_third(sr_after_checks=False, supplier_mode="optional"):
    """supplier_mode: 'optional' (sourcing unknown), 'mandatory' (external), 'absent' (internal)."""
    rd = Transition(label=RD)

    # P2: both mandatory checks, either order.
    initial_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[Transition(label=CC), Transition(label=TR)])

    # P6: each correction is followed by a non-empty recheck set.
    recheck_both = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[Transition(label=CC), Transition(label=TR)])
    recheck = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                           children=[Transition(label=CC), Transition(label=TR), recheck_both])
    cd = Transition(label=CD)
    correction_round = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[cd, recheck])
    correction_round.order.add_edge(cd, recheck)
    corrections = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[SilentTransition(), correction_round])

    check_phase = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[initial_checks, corrections])
    check_phase.order.add_edge(initial_checks, corrections)

    # P3: Supplier Review optional only while sourcing is unknown.
    if supplier_mode == "optional":
        supplier = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[Transition(label=SR), SilentTransition()])
    elif supplier_mode == "mandatory":
        supplier = Transition(label=SR)
    else:
        supplier = SilentTransition()

    # P4: optional Expedite Review after the (final) checks, before approval.
    expedite = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[Transition(label=ER), SilentTransition()])
    # P5: exactly one approval.
    approval = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[Transition(label=MA), Transition(label=AA)])
    ro = Transition(label=RO)

    model = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[rd, check_phase, supplier, expedite, approval, ro])
    edges = [
        (rd, check_phase), (rd, supplier), (rd, expedite), (rd, approval), (rd, ro),
        (check_phase, expedite), (check_phase, approval), (check_phase, ro),
        (supplier, approval), (supplier, ro),
        (expedite, approval), (expedite, ro),
        (approval, ro),
    ]
    if sr_after_checks:
        edges.append((check_phase, supplier))
    for source, target in edges:
        model.order.add_edge(source, target)
    return model


model_third = build_model_third()
model_third_external = build_model_third(supplier_mode="mandatory")
model_third_internal = build_model_third(supplier_mode="absent")

if __name__ == "__main__":
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_third)
    print("sound:", pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm)[0])
    tree = pm4py.convert_to_process_tree(model_third)
    print(tree)
```

The partial order is given transitively closed, which POWL requires. `sr_after_checks=True` gives the stricter variant in which Supplier Review must follow the initial check phase. That variant is recommended if the process owner rules that supplier effort should not be spent before the dossier passes its checks.

DECLARE templates cannot express "a repeated check requires a correction since its previous occurrence" (P7), "exactly one of two activities" (P5, full), or the attribute conditions (P3, P6). Layer C covers them:

```python
CHECKS, APPROVALS = (CC, TR), (MA, AA)


def policy_checks(trace, case_attrs=None, event_attrs=None):
    """trace: list of activity labels; event_attrs: per-event dicts aligned with trace.
    Returns a list of (status, message); status in VIOLATION, SUSPICIOUS, UNVERIFIABLE, UNRATIFIED."""
    case_attrs = case_attrs or {}
    event_attrs = event_attrs or [{} for _ in trace]
    findings = []

    # P7: a repeated mandatory check needs a Correct Dossier since its previous occurrence.
    last_idx = {}
    for i, a in enumerate(trace):
        if a in CHECKS:
            if a in last_idx and CD not in trace[last_idx[a] + 1:i]:
                findings.append(("SUSPICIOUS", f"P7: {a} repeated at position {i} without an intervening Correct Dossier"))
            last_idx[a] = i

    # P6: rechecks after each correction must match the affected checks.
    for i, a in enumerate(trace):
        if a != CD:
            continue
        stop = next((j for j in range(i + 1, len(trace)) if trace[j] == CD or trace[j] in APPROVALS), len(trace))
        rechecked = {x for x in trace[i + 1:stop] if x in CHECKS}
        if not rechecked:
            findings.append(("VIOLATION", f"P6: Correct Dossier at position {i} not followed by any recheck"))
        affected = event_attrs[i].get("affected_checks")
        if affected is None:
            findings.append(("UNVERIFIABLE", f"P6: affected checks unknown for Correct Dossier at position {i}; rechecked {sorted(rechecked)}"))
        else:
            affected = set(affected)
            if affected - rechecked:
                findings.append(("VIOLATION", f"P6: affected checks {sorted(affected - rechecked)} not repeated after position {i}"))
            if rechecked - affected:
                findings.append(("SUSPICIOUS", f"P6/P7: unaffected checks {sorted(rechecked - affected)} repeated after position {i}"))

    # P3: Supplier Review depends on sourcing.
    sourcing = case_attrs.get("sourcing")
    has_sr = SR in trace
    if sourcing == "external" and not has_sr:
        findings.append(("VIOLATION", "P3: external order without Supplier Review"))
    elif sourcing == "internal" and has_sr:
        findings.append(("UNRATIFIED", "P3: Supplier Review on an internal order"))
    elif sourcing is None and not has_sr:
        findings.append(("UNVERIFIABLE", "P3: sourcing unknown; absence of Supplier Review cannot be assessed"))

    # Open policy question: Supplier Review before the initial checks.
    if has_sr:
        sr_pos = trace.index(SR)
        if not all(c in trace[:sr_pos] for c in CHECKS):
            findings.append(("UNRATIFIED", "Supplier Review before both initial mandatory checks; ordering not covered by policy"))
    return findings


def verdict(procedural_fitness, findings):
    statuses = {s for s, _ in findings}
    if procedural_fitness < 1.0 or "VIOLATION" in statuses:
        return "NON-CONFORMANT"
    if "SUSPICIOUS" in statuses:
        return "SUSPICIOUS"
    if "UNVERIFIABLE" in statuses or "UNRATIFIED" in statuses:
        return "CONFORMANT-PENDING"
    return "CONFORMANT"
```

## 4. Explicit semantics

| Requirement | Encoding in `model_third` |
|---|---|
| **Mandatory checks (P2)** | Credit Check and Technical Review are plain (non-skippable) transitions in a concurrent block, so they occur in either order. There is no `tau` alternative. Their absence costs a visible model move in alignments and is reported as a deviation. The approval node is reachable only after the entire check phase. |
| **Optional Supplier Review (P3)** | `X(Supplier Review, tau)`, ordered after Register and before the approval, unordered with the checks and Expedite. The optionality is **explicitly justified by the missing sourcing attribute**, and every case without Supplier Review is labelled `UNVERIFIABLE` for P3 until sourcing is known. With sourcing available, `model_third_external` makes it mandatory and `model_third_internal` removes it. |
| **Expedite Review ordering (P4)** | `X(Expedite Review, tau)` after the full check phase (initial checks and any rechecks), before the approval, and unordered with Supplier Review, so it can occur before or after it. |
| **Exactly one approval (P5)** | `X(Manager Approval, Auto Approval)` executed once, strictly between everything else and Release Order. Two approvals, or none, is a deviation. |
| **Correction and recheck (P6, P7)** | `*(tau, ->(Correct Dossier, X(CC, TR, +(CC, TR))))`: zero or more correction rounds, each followed by a non-empty recheck set (only Credit Check, only Technical Review, or both in either order), as the process owner described in F6. A repeated check without a preceding correction (B8) cannot be replayed (P7) and is flagged `SUSPICIOUS` by Layer C. Whether the recheck set equals the affected set is checked in Layer C when the attribute exists. Otherwise it is `UNVERIFIABLE`. |
| **First/last (P1)** | Register Dossier precedes every node and Release Order follows every node, each occurring exactly once. |

Deliberate choices that the process owner must ratify:

- (i) Corrections are allowed only after the initial check phase and before Expedite and approval. A correction triggered mid-check, or after an Expedite Review, is a deviation.
- (ii) A correction with no recheck is a violation, following F6's "only CC, only TR, or both".
- (iii) Supplier Review is allowed before the checks (flagged `UNRATIFIED`), because the policy does not order it.
- (iv) Auto Approval is compatible with Supplier and Expedite Review (B5 "plausible valid").

## 5. Limitations caused by the missing attributes

- **Sourcing missing.** An external order with a missing (or unrecorded, F4) Supplier Review is procedurally conformant. Layer C can only mark it `UNVERIFIABLE`. Missing-event noise and internal orders are indistinguishable. The P3 false-negative rate cannot be estimated until sourcing is joined from the ERP/order master. Until then, compliance reporting must show the number of P3-unverifiable cases rather than claim P3 conformance.
- **Affected checks missing.** B6-type traces are accepted procedurally, but P6 is only structurally approximated: "some recheck happened". Under-rechecking (correction affected both checks, only one was repeated) and over-rechecking cannot be detected. The share of `CONFORMANT-PENDING` cases is itself a key metric.
- **Semantic duplicates.** The three Credit Check copies are indistinguishable in the log. Alignments choose among them by cost, which is harmless for fitness but means "recheck" vs. "initial check" is inferred from position, not recorded. Recording an explicit `recheck` flag would remove this ambiguity.
- **Stricter variants depend on policy rulings** (Supplier ordering, corrections after Expedite). They are one-line changes (`sr_after_checks`, moving `corrections`), not rewrites. That is the maintainability advantage over Model C.

# 9. Validation Experiments

## Program

1. **Reproducibility package.** Publish the raw log hash, preprocessing code (activity normalisation, lifecycle filter, handling of same-timestamp events, case filtering), PM4Py version, seeds, holdout case IDs, cost functions, and every metric script. Every metric in the board pack must be recomputable by a third party. The current reported values fail this, and two of them (simplicity, duplicate count) could not be reproduced from the model expressions.
2. **Case splits.** (a) Ten seeded random case-level 80/20 splits (seeds 0–9), reporting mean and range. (b) A **temporal** split (train on the first 80% of cases by start time, test on the last 20%). (c) A **variant-stratified** split so that rare variants appear in both halves in proportion. Models must be (re)discovered on training data only. Hand-written models such as the third alternative are frozen *before* the holdout is opened.
3. **Attribute-stratified holdout.** Join sourcing (and, where available, affected checks and urgency). Report every metric separately for external/internal, expedited/non-expedited, corrected/uncorrected, and Manager/Auto Approval. The third alternative's attribute-conditioned variants are evaluated on their own strata.
4. **Curated negative suite.** Written from policy (not mined), with the clause each trace violates. It includes: missing Credit Check, missing Technical Review, missing both; Expedite before or between checks; two approvals; no approval; events after Release; Register not first; a repeated check without correction; a correction without recheck; approval before the check phase; plus systematic mutations of valid holdout traces. Report **rejection recall per clause**. Also build a curated **positive suite** (B1, B2, B5, B7, the 24 correction-free combinations, and multi-round corrections) and report acceptance per behaviour class.
5. **Perturbation tests.** Drop Supplier Review with probability p ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.2}. Drop each check with the same p. Duplicate random events (logging duplicates) and swap events that share timestamps. Measure how many verdicts flip and whether flips are classified as data-quality issues or compliance issues.
6. **Unseen-combination tests.** Leave-one-combination-out: remove all training cases with a given feature combination (for example Supplier + Auto Approval, or Technical Review first + Expedite, or two correction rounds), fit or keep the model, and test acceptance on the removed cases. Report the share accepted, and separately check that the negative suite is still rejected.
7. **Alignment-cost sensitivity.** Grid over (a) uniform costs, (b) business-severity costs (model moves on mandatory checks and approvals high, optional activities low), and (c) silent-step costs 0 vs 1. Report changes in fitness, deviation ranking, and accept/reject verdicts. Rankings that flip under plausible costs must be reported as unstable.
8. **Soundness and reachability.** `pm4py.check_soundness` for every net. Language checks via bounded extensive playout: every positive-suite trace must be in the language, no negative-suite trace may be. Check that every transition lies on some path from the initial to the final marking (no dead transitions).
9. **Metrics reported separately**, never averaged: training and holdout fitness (trace-average **and** log-level, stating which), precision on training and holdout, negative-suite recall, positive-suite acceptance, token-based generalization, simplicity (arc-degree plus size and duplicate count with definitions), and runtime on fixed hardware. Each with a bootstrap 95% confidence interval over cases.
10. **Stakeholder comprehension protocol.** At least 8 participants per role (compliance, operations, IT, process owner). Each model is shown in the same notation (BPMN generated from each model, same layout conventions). Model identity and author team are blinded, and presentation order is randomised (within-subject, counterbalanced). Participants answer 10 pre-registered objective questions ("Can an order be released without Credit Check?", "Can Expedite Review occur before Supplier Review?", "What happens after a correction affecting only Technical Review?"). Measures: accuracy (primary), time, confidence, and a perceived-clarity rating (secondary). Pre-register the analysis, report per role, and publish the materials.
11. **Decision rule** (applied before any aggregate comparison). A model is **rejected for compliance use regardless of other metrics** if any of the following hold:
    - it accepts any hard-forbidden trace (negative-suite recall < 100% on hard clauses, including B3, B4, B8-type traces);
    - it rejects any hard-valid trace (B1, B2, B7, and the owner-approved combination set);
    - its Petri net is unsound or has dead transitions;
    - its temporal-holdout fitness on policy-valid cases has a confidence interval whose lower bound is below an agreed floor (for example 0.95);
    - verdicts flip for more than an agreed share (for example 2%) of cases under the plausible-cost grid;
    - its metrics cannot be reproduced from the published package.

    A model is rejected for **exploration** use if it cannot represent activities present in more than an agreed share of cases (S lacks `Correct Dossier`), or if its structure asserts behaviour that contradicts hard policy without a visible caveat.

## PM4Py code skeletons

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.algo.conformance.alignments.petri_net import algorithm as alignments
from pm4py.algo.simulation.playout.petri_net import algorithm as playout

CASE, ACT, TS = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
SEEDS = list(range(10))


# ---------- preprocessing (published, versioned) ----------
def preprocess(df):
    df = df.copy()
    if "lifecycle:transition" in df.columns:
        df = df[df["lifecycle:transition"].str.lower() == "complete"]
    df[ACT] = df[ACT].str.strip()
    # exact duplicates (same case, activity, timestamp) are logging artefacts; keep one and report the count
    n_before = len(df)
    df = df.drop_duplicates(subset=[CASE, ACT, TS])
    print("removed exact duplicates:", n_before - len(df))
    return df.sort_values([CASE, TS], kind="mergesort")


# ---------- reproducible splits ----------
def random_case_split(df, seed, train_frac=0.8):
    cases = np.array(sorted(df[CASE].unique()))
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    rng.shuffle(cases)
    cut = int(train_frac * len(cases))
    train_ids = set(cases[:cut])
    return df[df[CASE].isin(train_ids)], df[~df[CASE].isin(train_ids)]


def temporal_split(df, train_frac=0.8):
    starts = df.groupby(CASE)[TS].min().sort_values(kind="mergesort")
    cut = int(train_frac * len(starts))
    train_ids = set(starts.index[:cut])
    return df[df[CASE].isin(train_ids)], df[~df[CASE].isin(train_ids)]


def variant_stratified_split(df, seed, train_frac=0.8):
    variants = df.groupby(CASE)[ACT].agg(tuple)
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    train_ids, singletons = [], []
    for _, ids in variants.groupby(variants).groups.items():
        ids = np.array(sorted(ids))
        if len(ids) == 1:
            singletons.extend(ids)
            continue
        rng.shuffle(ids)
        train_ids.extend(ids[: max(1, int(round(train_frac * len(ids))))])
    singletons = np.array(sorted(singletons))
    rng.shuffle(singletons)
    train_ids.extend(singletons[: int(round(train_frac * len(singletons)))])
    train_ids = set(train_ids)
    return df[df[CASE].isin(train_ids)], df[~df[CASE].isin(train_ids)]


def stratify(df, attribute):
    return {value: part for value, part in df.groupby(attribute)}


# ---------- synthetic trace suites ----------
def traces_to_df(traces):
    rows = []
    for cid, trace in traces.items():
        for i, activity in enumerate(trace):
            rows.append({CASE: str(cid), ACT: activity,
                         TS: pd.Timestamp("2026-01-01") + pd.Timedelta(minutes=i)})
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(pd.DataFrame(rows), case_id=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS)


def mutate(trace, rng):
    """Policy-violating mutations of a valid trace; returns (clause, mutated_trace) pairs."""
    out = []
    for check in ("Credit Check", "Technical Review"):
        if check in trace:
            out.append(("P2-missing-" + check, [a for a in trace if a != check]))
    if "Expedite Review" in trace:
        t = [a for a in trace if a != "Expedite Review"]
        out.append(("P4-expedite-first", t[:1] + ["Expedite Review"] + t[1:]))
    approval = next(a for a in trace if a in ("Manager Approval", "Auto Approval"))
    other = "Auto Approval" if approval == "Manager Approval" else "Manager Approval"
    out.append(("P5-double-approval", trace[:-1] + [other] + trace[-1:]))
    out.append(("P5-no-approval", [a for a in trace if a != approval]))
    check = rng.choice(["Credit Check", "Technical Review"])
    idx = max(i for i, a in enumerate(trace) if a == check)
    out.append(("P7-repeat-without-correction", trace[: idx + 1] + [check] + trace[idx + 1:]))
    out.append(("P1-event-after-release", trace + ["Technical Review"]))
    return out


def perturb(df, activity, p, seed):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    mask = (df[ACT] == activity) & (rng.random(len(df)) < p)
    return df[~mask]


def duplicate_events(df, p, seed):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    dup = df[rng.random(len(df)) < p]
    return pd.concat([df, dup]).sort_values([CASE, TS], kind="mergesort")


def leave_combination_out(df, predicate):
    """predicate(trace_tuple) -> bool selects the held-out combination."""
    variants = df.groupby(CASE)[ACT].agg(tuple)
    held = set(variants[variants.map(predicate)].index)
    return df[~df[CASE].isin(held)], df[df[CASE].isin(held)]


# ---------- model evaluation ----------
def to_net(model):
    if isinstance(model, str):
        model = pm4py.parse_process_tree(model)
    return pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)


def acceptance(df, net, im, fm):
    diag = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(df, net, im, fm)
    ids = df[CASE].drop_duplicates().tolist()   # diagnostics follow case order of the log
    return {cid: d["fitness"] == 1.0 for cid, d in zip(ids, diag)}


def evaluate(model, train_df, test_df, positive_df, negative_df):
    net, im, fm = to_net(model)
    report = {"sound": pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm)[0]}
    for name, part in (("train", train_df), ("test", test_df)):
        fit = pm4py.fitness_alignments(part, net, im, fm)
        report[f"{name}_avg_trace_fitness"] = fit["average_trace_fitness"]
        report[f"{name}_log_fitness"] = fit["log_fitness"]
        report[f"{name}_precision"] = pm4py.precision_alignments(part, net, im, fm)
    report["generalization_tbr_train"] = pm4py.generalization_tbr(train_df, net, im, fm)
    report["simplicity_arc_degree"] = pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm, variant="arc_degree")
    labels = [t.label for t in net.transitions if t.label is not None]
    report["visible_transitions"] = len(labels)
    report["duplicate_copies"] = len(labels) - len(set(labels))  # definition: extra copies per label
    pos = acceptance(positive_df, net, im, fm)
    neg = acceptance(negative_df, net, im, fm)
    report["positive_acceptance"] = np.mean(list(pos.values()))
    report["negative_rejection"] = 1 - np.mean(list(neg.values()))
    report["accepted_negatives"] = [c for c, ok in neg.items() if ok]
    report["rejected_positives"] = [c for c, ok in pos.items() if not ok]
    return report


def bootstrap_ci(df, metric_fn, n=200, seed=0):
    cases = df[CASE].unique()
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    values = []
    for _ in range(n):
        sample = rng.choice(cases, size=len(cases), replace=True)
        parts = [df[df[CASE] == c].assign(**{CASE: f"{c}#{k}"}) for k, c in enumerate(sample)]
        values.append(metric_fn(pd.concat(parts)))
    return np.percentile(values, [2.5, 97.5])


# ---------- alignment-cost sensitivity ----------
def cost_grid_fitness(df, net, im, fm, mandatory=("Credit Check", "Technical Review", "Manager Approval",
                                                  "Auto Approval", "Register Dossier", "Release Order")):
    P = alignments.Variants.VERSION_STATE_EQUATION_A_STAR.value.Parameters
    log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df)
    results = {}
    for name, (c_mand, c_opt, c_tau) in {"uniform": (1, 1, 0), "severity": (10, 1, 0),
                                         "tau_costly": (1, 1, 1)}.items():
        model_cost = {t: (c_tau if t.label is None else c_mand if t.label in mandatory else c_opt)
                      for t in net.transitions}
        sync_cost = {t: 0 for t in net.transitions if t.label is not None}
        costs = []
        for trace in log:
            trace_cost = [c_mand if e[ACT] in mandatory else c_opt for e in trace]
            aligned = alignments.apply_trace(trace, net, im, fm, parameters={
                P.PARAM_MODEL_COST_FUNCTION: model_cost, P.PARAM_SYNC_COST_FUNCTION: sync_cost,
                P.PARAM_TRACE_COST_FUNCTION: trace_cost})
            costs.append(aligned["cost"])
        results[name] = costs
    return pd.DataFrame(results)   # compare verdict flips (cost == 0 or tau-only) and deviation rankings


# ---------- bounded language check ----------
def bounded_language(net, im, fm, max_len=12):
    EP = playout.Variants.EXTENSIVE.value.Parameters
    lang = playout.apply(net, im, fm, variant=playout.Variants.EXTENSIVE,

# ---------- decision rule ----------
def compliance_gate(report, hard_negative_ids, hard_positive_ids, holdout_ci_low, flip_rate,
                    fitness_floor=0.95, max_flip=0.02, reproducible=True):
    reasons = []
    if not report["sound"]:
        reasons.append("unsound net")
    if set(report["accepted_negatives"]) & set(hard_negative_ids):
        reasons.append("accepts hard-forbidden traces")
    if set(report["rejected_positives"]) & set(hard_positive_ids):
        reasons.append("rejects hard-valid traces")
    if holdout_ci_low < fitness_floor:
        reasons.append("holdout fitness on policy-valid cases below floor")
    if flip_rate > max_flip:
        reasons.append("verdicts unstable under cost grid")
    if not reproducible:
        reasons.append("metrics not reproducible")
    return ("REJECT", reasons) if reasons else ("ELIGIBLE", [])
```

Usage outline: `df = preprocess(pm4py.read_xes("dossiers.xes"))`; for each seed and split type, compute `evaluate(model, train, test, traces_to_df(positive_suite), traces_to_df(negative_suite))` for Model S, Model C and `model_third` (`pm4py.convert_to_petri_net` accepts the POWL object directly). Then apply `compliance_gate`, and report all metrics disaggregated with confidence intervals. No metric values are claimed here; the business log has to be run through this program.

# 10. Governance Recommendation

- **Model S: exploration only, with conditions, not compliance.** It may be used for high-level exploratory overlays (frequency, waiting time) only if (a) it is labelled "descriptive, not normative: permits skipping mandatory checks", and (b) `Correct Dossier` is added to it. Without that activity, S cannot show rework at all, which is one of the phenomena exploration is supposed to reveal. S must **not** be used for compliance, alerting, audit sampling, or simulation. As a simulation model it would generate orders released without checks. F7's defence holds only for this narrow scope.
- **Model C: retire as a process model, keep as an artefact.** It should be retired for compliance: it rejects the explicitly valid B2, cannot represent P6, and its structure makes approved changes costly (F2, F3). It may be archived as a **variant catalogue** (documentation of frequent observed paths, useful as a regression test set for the third alternative), not as a normative or exploratory model.
- **Compliance use: neither original. Adopt `model_third` after it passes the gate below.** Until then, compliance monitoring should use the Layer B DECLARE rules plus Layer C checks as an interim rule-based control. They are transparent and clause-attributed, and they already catch B3, B4, B8 and the missing-check mutations.

**Conditions before deploying `model_third` for compliance:**

1. The reproducibility package (Section 9.1) is published, and S and C are re-evaluated with it so the comparison is fair.
2. The process owner rules on the open questions: Supplier ordering relative to the checks; corrections after Supplier or Expedite, or during the initial checks; corrections with no recheck; Auto Approval with Expedite. The model is updated with the one-line switches.
3. The compliance gate passes: 100% rejection of the hard negative suite, 100% acceptance of hard-valid probes and owner-approved combinations, a sound net, a temporal-holdout fitness floor on policy-valid cases, and stable verdicts across the cost grid.
4. The sourcing attribute is joined, and the attribute-conditioned variants are evaluated per stratum. Until then, P3 is reported as "unverifiable" with counts, never as "conformant".
5. Capturing an `affected_checks` attribute on `Correct Dossier` events is commissioned in the source system. Until then, P6 findings are `CONFORMANT-PENDING` and sampled for manual review. If this pending share exceeds an agreed threshold, the board is informed.
6. A shadow-mode run of 4–8 weeks alongside manual audits, measuring the rate of false alerts and missed findings, including data-quality alerts separated from compliance alerts.
7. The documented comprehension study shows that each role answers the key questions about the third alternative at least as accurately as for S.
8. Ownership and change control are set up: each policy clause is mapped to its model element and rule, the model is versioned, and a regression suite (B1–B8, X1–X9, Model C's variants) is re-run on every change.

# 11. Dissenting View

**Rebuttal.** This review condemns two models for unreproducible metrics and then recommends a third model with *no metrics at all*. `model_third` was written from policy text and my own reading of it, not from evidence. Every "deliberate choice" (Supplier Review unordered with the checks, corrections only before Expedite, mandatory non-empty rechecks, Auto Approval compatible with Expedite) is a normative guess that may encode my assumptions as control logic. Worse, the `CONFORMANT-PENDING` category could become a permanent loophole: while the two attributes are missing, a large share of cases would carry neither an acceptance nor a violation, and nobody acts on "pending". Model C's advocate has a serious point. In a regulated setting, a whitelist where every allowed path was *observed and reviewed* is a stronger control than a generalising model that accepts combinations (B5, X6) no one has approved. B5 is only "plausible". C's false positives (B2) are cheap to fix by adding a branch under change control, whereas a generalising model's false negatives are invisible. The hybrid also has three layers, which is harder to explain than either original. On comprehension, the one criterion the board actually measured, it may lose to S. A defensible alternative is to keep C as the enforcement whitelist, add B2 and approved combinations quickly, and use S for exploration.

**What would change my decision.** I would favour a governed whitelist (an extended C) if the regulator or internal audit requires explicit path-level approval. I would also switch if the shadow run shows the third alternative accepting cases that manual audit rejects, or if the `CONFORMANT-PENDING` share stays high because the attributes cannot be captured. I would favour S-like simplicity for communication if the documented comprehension study shows the third alternative is systematically misread. Finally, if a reproducible temporal split showed C's holdout fitness close to its training fitness, the overfitting argument against C would weaken considerably.
