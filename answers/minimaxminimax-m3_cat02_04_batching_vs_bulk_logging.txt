# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

**Resource-identity assumption.**
- *Trusted*: G1-G4 and G6-G8 produce single-resource series with consistent timing. G5 is the only cluster where trust changes the conclusion; if `analyst_pool` were genuinely one person, the 4 overlapping cases would force us to classify it as *concurrent multitasking*, not *shared-account artifact*. With trust, G5 looks like a single very busy analyst; without trust, the same pattern is one of three possible workers.
- *Not trusted*: G5 becomes a *resource-unknown* cluster. Utilization is uncomputable. Service-time variance is interpretable only as the union of up to three workers, and the apparent 5-min start cadence becomes consistent with three analysts picking up tickets in turn.

**Timestamp-precision assumption.**
- *Exact*: G7's 15-min precision is treated as ground truth, giving every case exactly 15-min service and making G1, G6, G7 indistinguishable on a "same start + same complete" basis.
- *Interval-censored*: G7 becomes `[13:45,14:00)  [14:15,14:30)` for every case; G6's `17:00  17:00` is interval-censored to zero width and therefore *undefined* service, which is the correct representation. Under interval censoring, G1 is unchanged (1-s precision is fine), G6 must be censored for service-time analysis, and G7 must be censored entirely.

**Three cases where the same temporal pattern supports multiple physical explanations.**

1. **Identical start + identical complete (G1, G6, G7)**. The pattern is the same: same start, same complete, multiple cases. For G1 the explanation is *physical batch on a 4-item fixture*. For G6 it is *bulk end-of-shift entry*. For G7 it is *timestamp coarsening at a 15-min precision floor*. The temporal pattern alone is *not* diagnostic; only the source metadata (PLC vs manual_bulk_form vs legacy_MES with 15-min precision) and the queue-arrival profile disambiguate.
2. **Identical start, divergent complete (G2) and identical complete, divergent start (G3)**. Both patterns can arise from (a) one physical batch with logging that records only one end, (b) start/end batching with a common setup or common sign-off, or (c) a system that only logs the boundary event with sub-second resolution. The "begin group" hint vs the "submit folder" hint decides between (a) and (b).
3. **Back-to-back adjacency (G4, G8)**. The pattern is *clean handoff* with negligible gap. This can be (a) genuinely serial single-item service, (b) one operator working through a queue with negligible transport time, or (c) a batch where the operator records only the boundary between consecutive items. Q01 ending at 15:45:00 and Q02 starting at 15:45:00 (zero gap) is the canonical signature of all three explanations; only the recorded lag (3 s for Q01Q02) and the absence of overlap rule out a true simultaneous batch.

**Other re-analyses worth flagging.**
- *G8 with clocks synchronized*: the 3-min offset between safety-end (15:35) and first start (15:38) disappears or grows, changing the diagnosis from "queue accumulation after capacity loss" to either "rapid recovery" or "deeper backlog" respectively.
- *G2 with finer tablet precision*: the 10:00 common start might split into a 30-s window of distinct starts, changing the diagnosis from "start batching" to "rapid sequential setup".
- *G5 with three distinct badges*: utilization shifts from one resource to three, bottleneck detection becomes per-analyst, and the apparent concurrency becomes genuine parallel work.

# 6. Data-collection redesign

Prioritized (highest impact first):

1. **Separate execution from recording timestamps.** Every lifecycle field must have a `lifecycle_state` (`queued`, `started`, `paused`, `resumed`, `completed`) and both `execution_time` (worker/system clock at the moment of state change) and `recording_time` (DB insertion). Without this, G6 and G7 cannot be untangled.
2. **Capture per-item sub-events within a batch.** When a batch is processed (RBT-7 fixture, T-B inspection set, T-C review folder), emit one event per item with its own `start`/`complete`, plus a `batch_id` linking the items. This is the only way to recover the per-item service distribution inside G1/G2/G3.
3. **Resource identity at badge level, not login level.** Replace shared logins (`analyst_pool`) with per-analyst badge events; if a shared account is unavoidable, retain the workstation ID and login duration so post-hoc splitting is possible. Directly fixes G5.
4. **Pause/resume lifecycle events.** Especially for G8 and any long activity that may be interrupted (safety checks, machine downtime). The current schema cannot represent an interrupted activity at all.
5. **Group/batch identifiers at the source.** A first-class `batch_id` plus `batch_role` (setup, member, completion) preserves the operational meaning that timestamps lose. Required for G1, G2, G3 disambiguation.
6. **Workstation capacity and constraint metadata.** For each resource, store `capacity` (items per cycle for G1), `mode` (single-user, multi-user), and `clock_source` (which clock generated the timestamp). This is what makes the physical interpretation testable.
7. **Timestamp precision metadata, kept honest.** Document both `precision` (claimed granularity) and *measured* skew/drift between source clocks. The G7 legacy MES rule must be documented; the G8 safety/repair clock offset must be measured and published.
8. **Synchronized clocks with NTP/PTP and recorded offset.** A `clock_offset_ms` field on each event allows analysis to correct for skew rather than guessing.
9. **Standardize queue-arrival semantics.** `queue_arrival` should be the upstream system's emission time, not the local recording time, and the upstream source identity should be retained for traceability (it is partially retained via `source` but not fully disambiguated from local sources).
10. **Capture operator intent at boundaries.** Fields like `setup_started_at`, `setup_completed_at`, `first_item_started_at`, `last_item_completed_at` separate setup from service, which is the dominant confounder in G2 and G3.

# 7. Final adjudication

**High-confidence interpretations (safe to use in queue/conformance analysis):**
- **G1** as a genuine 4-item simultaneous batch with shared 3-min service, *provided* PLC cycle events are inspected to confirm the fixture held four items. Confidence 85%.
- **G7** as a timestamp-coarsening artifact; carry interval-censored timestamps and *do not* compute service time from the rounded values. Confidence 90%.
- **G6** as end-of-shift bulk entry; **exclude** from service-time and utilization analysis; *include* only queue-arrival statistics with `recorded_at  queue_arrival` as the lower bound on actual waiting. Confidence 95%.

**Exclude or interval-censor before queue analysis:**
- **G6** must be excluded from service-time distributions (would yield 0-min service) and from utilization (would yield impossible 700% busy at 17:00). Keep only for queue-arrival analysis with explicit artifact flag.
- **G5** must be excluded from any per-resource utilization; keep for activity-level statistics with a resource-unknown flag. Interval-censor service-time variance statements.
- **G7** should be interval-censored; do not compute point estimates of service or synchronization metrics.

**Require operational validation:**
- **G2**: confirm whether the "begin group" tap is a true common setup or whether each inspection is independently initiated. Without UI instrumentation this remains ambiguous.
- **G3**: confirm whether the 11:30 complete is a true joint sign-off or a single form-submit timestamp overwriting per-item completion. Without field-level audit log the common-complete is a *necessary but not sufficient* signature.
- **G4**: confirm whether the scanner station serves one operator sequentially or whether multiple totes can be packed concurrently. The 4-s lead-in for P02 suggests at least one clock-skew or anticipation artifact.
- **G8**: synchronize the safety-check and repair-queue clocks; capture a "station released" event; only then can the 30-38 min waiting time be unambiguously attributed to the safety check rather than to clock drift.
