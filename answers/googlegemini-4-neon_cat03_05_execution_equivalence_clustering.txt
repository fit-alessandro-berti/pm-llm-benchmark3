## 1. Equivalence design space

An object-centric execution centered on a lead `Order` can be modeled as a typed relational structure $X = (E, \prec, \lambda, O, \tau, \text{ext}, R_{\text{E2O}}, R_{\text{O2O}})$, where $E$ is a finite set of events ordered by $\prec$, $\lambda: E \to \Sigma$ assigns activity labels, $O$ is a set of objects with types $\tau: O \to T$, $\text{ext}: O \to \{\text{LOC}, \text{EXT}\}$ distinguishes local from external (`EXT`-prefixed) objects, $R_{\text{E2O}} \subseteq E \times O$ captures event-to-object incidence, and $R_{\text{O2O}} \subseteq O \times Q \times O$ captures qualified object-to-object relations. Different choices of what to preserve yield distinct equivalence relations across X1–X8.

### 1.1 Activity-sequence equivalence ($E_{\text{act}}$)
- **Definition**: $X \sim_{\text{act}} Y$ iff their timestamp-ordered activity-label sequences $(\lambda(e_1), \dots, \lambda(e_n))$ are identical.
- **Invariants preserved**: Event count, activity vocabulary, and global linear order of activity labels.
- **Information discarded**: All objects, object types, multiplicities, E2O incidences, O2O topology and qualifiers, and external-object boundaries. This is a control-flow baseline, not an object-centric equivalence.
- **Algebraic properties**: Reflexive, symmetric, and transitive (tuple equality).
- **Concurrency handling**: Strict total ordering breaks ties arbitrarily when timestamps coincide. To handle concurrency deterministically, simultaneous events should be grouped into timestamped multisets of labels (step sequences) or compared via an event-label partial order.

### 1.2 Typed structural equivalence under local identifier renaming ($E_{\text{iso}}$)
- **Definition**: $X \sim_{\text{iso}} Y$ iff there exist bijections $\phi_E: E_X \to E_Y$ and $\phi_O: O_X \to O_Y$ preserving event order ($\prec$), activity labels ($\lambda$), object types ($\tau$), externality flags ($\text{ext}$), E2O incidence ($R_{\text{E2O}}$), and qualified O2O edges ($R_{\text{O2O}}$). Local object identifiers are treated purely as anonymous nodes.
- **Invariants preserved**: Exact object counts per type, exact event-to-object binding topology, local-versus-external boundary status, exact event sequence, and every O2O qualifier.
- **Information discarded**: Raw local identifier strings (e.g., `O1` vs. `O2`, `I11` vs. `I22`) and intra-event object listing order.
- **Algebraic properties**: Reflexive (identity bijection), symmetric (inverse bijection), and transitive (bijection composition).
- **Concurrency handling**: Replace the total order $\prec$ with a strict partial order (e.g., strict timestamp inequality $t(e_1) < t(e_2)$, or causal per-object precedence). Isomorphism then preserves both strict ordering and concurrency, retaining reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity.

### 1.3 Business-semantic equivalence ($E_{\text{sem}}$, preferred)
- **Definition**: $X \sim_{\text{sem}} Y$ iff their canonical commercial-and-exception signatures $\sigma_{\text{sem}}(X) = \sigma_{\text{sem}}(Y)$ match across four dimensions: (1) run-length collapsed activity phases paired with their typed local/external E2O profiles; (2) bucketed commercial multiplicities (`0`, `1`, `2+`) for `Delivery`, `Invoice`, and `Payment`; (3) functional domains coupled to `EXT` objects (`ORDER_ENTRY`, `LOGISTICS`, `FINANCE`); and (4) non-nominal typed O2O qualifiers (excluding baseline `contains`, `packs`, `carries`, `bills`, `settles`).
- **Invariants preserved**: Commercial fulfillment and settlement cardinality (single vs. split delivery; single vs. multi-payment), lifecycle motifs (standard flow vs. split loop vs. return-and-repack rework), cross-order boundary coupling by domain, E2O type-level participation per phase, and semantic O2O exceptions.
- **Information discarded**: Local identifiers, exact counts of `Item` and internal `Package` objects when consecutive packing steps converge into one isolated delivery, and exact repetition counts of homogeneous consecutive `Add Item` or `Pack Package` events.
- **Algebraic properties**: Reflexive, symmetric, and transitive, as equality under a deterministic canonical map $\sigma_{\text{sem}}$.
- **Concurrency handling**: Events with equal timestamps are combined into sorted concurrent steps prior to run-length collapsing, or compared via the per-delivery partial-order motif so independent warehouse actions do not induce spurious splits.

### 1.4 Lead-order-scoped local equivalence ($E_{\text{loc}}$, cautionary contrast)
- **Definition**: Project out all `EXT` objects and drop all O2O qualifiers, then test typed E2O isomorphism on the remaining local subgraph.
- **Invariants preserved**: Local activity order, local object counts, and local E2O binding.
- **Information discarded**: Cross-order coupling (`EXT` objects) and O2O semantic qualifiers.
- **Algebraic properties**: Reflexive, symmetric, and transitive (isomorphism after deterministic projection).
- **Concurrency handling**: Identical to $E_{\text{iso}}$ via partial-order preservation. $E_{\text{loc}}$ illustrates how naive lead-order extraction silently merges cross-order sharing (X5, X6) into the baseline (X1).

## 2. Canonical signatures

The canonical signature for our preferred relation $E_{\text{sem}}$ is fully invariant to local identifier renaming because local IDs are used solely to count distinct commercial objects and are never emitted into the signature. Externality is retained through the `EXT` flag, E2O incidence is retained at the `(object_type, locality)` level per collapsed phase, and O2O exceptions are retained as typed qualifier triples.

```python
from collections import defaultdict
from typing import Dict, Iterable, List, Set, Tuple

# Type aliases:
# Event = (timestamp, activity, [(obj_type, obj_id), ...])
# O2O   = (src_type, src_id, qualifier, tgt_type, tgt_id)

COMMERCIAL_TYPES = ("Delivery", "Invoice", "Payment")
NOMINAL_O2O = {"contains", "packs", "carries", "bills", "settles"}

ACTIVITY_DOMAIN = {
    "Create Order": "ORDER_ENTRY",
    "Add Item": "ORDER_ENTRY",
    "Pack Package": "LOGISTICS",
    "Dispatch": "LOGISTICS",
    "Return Item": "LOGISTICS",
    "Inspect Return": "LOGISTICS",
    "Issue Invoice": "FINANCE",
    "Record Payment": "FINANCE",
}

O2O_TYPE_DOMAIN = {
    "Package": "LOGISTICS",
    "Delivery": "LOGISTICS",
    "Invoice": "FINANCE",
    "Payment": "FINANCE",
}


def is_external(obj_id: str) -> bool:
    return obj_id.startswith("EXT")


def bucket_count(n: int) -> str:
    if n == 0:
        return "0"
    if n == 1:
        return "1"
    return "2+"


def canonical_semantic_signature(
    events: List[Tuple[int, str, List[Tuple[str, str]]]],
    o2o_edges: Iterable[Tuple[str, str, str, str, str]],
) -> Tuple:
    # 1. Group events by timestamp to handle same-time concurrency deterministically
    by_ts: Dict[int, List[Tuple[str, Tuple[Tuple[str, str], ...]]]] = defaultdict(list)
    objs_by_type: Dict[str, Set[str]] = defaultdict(set)
    ext_domains: Set[str] = set()

    for ts, activity, e2o in events:
        type_profile = set()
        has_ext = False
        for otype, oid in e2o:
            objs_by_type[otype].add(oid)
            locality = "EXT" if is_external(oid) else "LOC"
            type_profile.add((otype, locality))
            if locality == "EXT":
                has_ext = True
        if has_ext and activity in ACTIVITY_DOMAIN:
            ext_domains.add(ACTIVITY_DOMAIN[activity])
        by_ts[ts].append((activity, tuple(sorted(type_profile))))

    # Canonical step sequence (sorted within same timestamp) + run-length collapse
    collapsed_phases: List[Tuple] = []
    for ts in sorted(by_ts):
        step = tuple(sorted(by_ts[ts]))
        if not collapsed_phases or collapsed_phases[-1] != step:
            collapsed_phases.append(step)

    # 2. Commercial object multiplicity buckets (Delivery, Invoice, Payment)
    comm_mult = tuple(
        (otype, bucket_count(len(objs_by_type[otype])))
        for otype in COMMERCIAL_TYPES
    )

    # 3. Non-nominal O2O qualifiers (typed, ID-free) + external O2O domain detection
    o2o_exceptions: Set[Tuple[str, str, str]] = set()
    for src_t, src_id, qual, tgt_t, tgt_id in o2o_edges:
        objs_by_type[src_t].add(src_id)
        objs_by_type[tgt_t].add(tgt_id)
        if is_external(src_id) or is_external(tgt_id):
            for t in (src_t, tgt_t):
                if t in O2O_TYPE_DOMAIN:
                    ext_domains.add(O2O_TYPE_DOMAIN[t])
        if qual not in NOMINAL_O2O:
            o2o_exceptions.add((src_t, qual, tgt_t))

    # Recompute commercial multiplicities in case an object appears only in O2O
    comm_mult = tuple(
        (otype, bucket_count(len(objs_by_type[otype])))
        for otype in COMMERCIAL_TYPES
    )

    return (
        tuple(collapsed_phases),
        comm_mult,
        tuple(sorted(ext_domains)),
        tuple(sorted(o2o_exceptions)),
    )
```

For total-order executions like X1–X8 (where each event has a distinct timestamp), each `step` is a singleton `(activity, type_profile)`. Consecutive identical phases—such as the two `Add Item` events in all executions and the two consecutive `Pack Package` events in X3—collapse cleanly, whereas alternating `Pack -> Dispatch -> Pack -> Dispatch` in X4 and the return loop in X8 are preserved.

## 3. Cluster assignments

| Execution | Activity-sequence cluster ($E_{\text{act}}$) | Typed-structural cluster ($E_{\text{iso}}$) | Preferred semantic cluster ($E_{\text{sem}}$) | Defining signature (compact $\sigma_{\text{sem}}$) | Main reason |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **X1** | `A1` | `T1` | `S1` | `phases=STD; D:1,V:1,M:1; ext=NONE; exc=NONE` | Isolated single-delivery, single-payment baseline. |
| **X2** | `A1` | `T1` | `S1` | `phases=STD; D:1,V:1,M:1; ext=NONE; exc=NONE` | Exact local-ID isomorphism of X1 (`I22I11`, `I21I12`). |
| **X3** | `A2` | `T2` | `S1` | `phases=STD; D:1,V:1,M:1; ext=NONE; exc=NONE` | Consecutive local `Pack` events converge to one isolated `Delivery` and full settlement; cartonization split is internal. |
| **X4** | `A3` | `T3` | `S2` | `phases=SPLIT_LOOP; D:2+,V:1,M:2+; ext=NONE; exc={partially_settles, completes_settlement}` | Split deliveries plus installment settlement with partial/complete qualifiers. |
| **X5** | `A1` | `T4` | `S3` | `phases=STD+EXT_LOG; D:1,V:1,M:1; ext={LOGISTICS}; exc=NONE` | Logistics boundary crossed via shared package/delivery with `EXT-OA`/`EXT-IA`. |
| **X6** | `A1` | `T5` | `S4` | `phases=STD+EXT_FIN; D:1,V:1,M:1; ext={FINANCE}; exc=NONE` | Financial boundary crossed via consolidated invoice and unallocated payment with `EXT-OB`. |

| **X8** | `A4` | `T7` | `S6` | `phases=RETURN_REPACK; D:2+,V:1,M:1; ext=NONE; exc={repacks, replaces}` | Post-dispatch reverse logistics (`Return Item`, `Inspect Return`) followed by repack and redelivery. |

Under the cautionary $E_{\text{loc}}$ relation (omitted from the table columns), `{X1, X2, X5, X6}` collapse together while X3, X4, X7, and X8 stay separate. Notice that $E_{\text{sem}}$ produces six clusters rather than $E_{\text{iso}}$'s seven: it intentionally coarsens internal box-splitting (X3) into `S1` while preserving every commercially, operationally, or boundary-significant distinction.

## 4. Cluster interpretations

### `S1` — Clean Isolated Single-Delivery Fulfillment (`{X1, X2, X3}`)
- **Signature**: `phases=STD | D:1, V:1, M:1 | ext=NONE | exc=NONE`.
- **Business behavior**: All ordered items are packed locally (into one or more cartons), shipped together in a single isolated delivery, billed on one dedicated invoice, and settled in a single payment without returns or substitutions.
- **Risks and opportunities**: Serves as the golden "happy path" benchmark for straight-through processing. Within `S1`, X3 signals a secondary warehouse optimization opportunity—carton consolidation—without indicating customer-facing fulfillment failure.
- **Poor traditional flattening**: Flattening on `Package` duplicates order creation, invoicing, and payment events for X3 (inflating apparent revenue/invoice counts), while flattening on `Order` erases how items map to cartons.
- **Stability under data repair**: High. Standardizing timestamps, deduplicating benign packing logs, or normalizing local IDs keeps X1–X3 together; only explicitly splitting `Package` cardinality into commercial reporting would separate X3.

### `S2` — Split-Delivery & Installment Settlement (`{X4}`)
- **Signature**: `phases=SPLIT_LOOP | D:2+, V:1, M:2+ | ext=NONE | exc={partially_settles, completes_settlement}`.
- **Business behavior**: Items are fulfilled in staggered shipments (`D41`, `D42`)—typical of backorders or multi-warehouse sourcing—and settled via multiple partial payments against one invoice.
- **Risks and opportunities**: Higher freight cost, split-delivery customer friction, and elevated days sales outstanding (DSO) / credit-collection risk. Opportunity for automated backorder communication and freight-threshold policies.
- **Poor traditional flattening**: Flattening on `Delivery` duplicates `Issue Invoice` and both `Record Payment` events across two cases, corrupting payment-to-delivery attribution; flattening on `Payment` duplicates both shipments.
- **Stability under data repair**: Moderate to high. Reordering concurrent `Pack`/`Dispatch` events or dropping O2O payment qualifiers preserves `S2` via `D:2+` and `M:2+`, though aggregating payments into a single attribute would strip its financial-risk dimension.

### `S3` — Logistics-Shared Fulfillment (`{X5}`)
- **Signature**: `phases=STD+EXT_LOG | D:1, V:1, M:1 | ext={LOGISTICS} | exc=NONE`.
- **Business behavior**: Items from `O5` are physically co-packed with `EXT-IA` (belonging to `EXT-OA`) into one shared package and delivery, while invoicing remains strictly isolated per order.
- **Risks and opportunities**: Reduces freight cost via multi-order consolidation, but creates cross-order delay propagation (one missing item in `EXT-OA` delays `O5`), damage/loss liability ambiguity, and customs or packing-slip compliance risks.
- **Poor traditional flattening**: Flattening on `Order` silently drops `EXT-OA` and `EXT-IA`, making X5 indistinguishable from X1; flattening on `Package` merges two independent orders' billing lifecycles into one tangled case.
- **Stability under data repair**: Fragile to scope truncation. Aggressive lead-order filtering or low `max_objs` extraction caps erase `EXT` objects and collapse `S3` into `S1`.

### `S4` — Financially-Shared Billing (`{X6}`)
- **Signature**: `phases=STD+EXT_FIN | D:1, V:1, M:1 | ext={FINANCE} | exc=NONE`.
- **Business behavior**: Logistics is fully isolated, but `V61` bills both `O6` and `EXT-OB`, and `M61` settles `V61` without order-level payment allocation.
- **Risks and opportunities**: Streamlines B2B periodic statement billing, but introduces cash-application opacity, revenue-recognition and dispute-isolation risk (a dispute on `EXT-OB` can freeze payment for `O6`), and audit-trail gaps.
- **Poor traditional flattening**: Flattening on `Order` hides `EXT-OB` and misrepresents full shared settlement as order-specific cash; flattening on `Invoice` conflates independent logistics lead times of two orders.
- **Stability under data repair**: Fragile to scope truncation, and sensitive to synthetic allocation repair (if an ETL job heuristically splits `M61` across orders without tagging the shared invoice, `S4` loses its audit signal).

### `S5` — Pre-Dispatch Item Substitution (`{X7}`)
- **Signature**: `phases=STD+DISP_ITEM | D:1, V:1, M:1 | ext=NONE | exc={replaces, removed_before_dispatch}`.
- **Business behavior**: Two items are initially packed, but `I71` is removed prior to dispatch and replaced by `I72`, which is explicitly bound to `Dispatch` while the order still ships once and bills normally.
- **Risks and opportunities**: Classic "silent exception"—hidden inventory shrinkage, pick-pack rework, potential customer-specification mismatch, or billing discrepancy if `V71` still charges for `I71`.
- **Poor traditional flattening**: Flattening on `Order` reproduces the exact happy-path activity sequence of X1, completely masking the substitution; flattening on `Item` splits `I71` and `I72` into separate traces, breaking their `replaces` relationship.
- **Stability under data repair**: Low to moderate. If ETL drops O2O qualifiers *and* standardizes `Dispatch` E2O schemas by removing `Item` links, `S5` vanishes into `S1`.

### `S6` — Post-Dispatch Return & Repack Rework (`{X8}`)
- **Signature**: `phases=RETURN_REPACK | D:2+, V:1, M:1 | ext=NONE | exc={repacks, replaces}`.
- **Business behavior**: After initial consolidated dispatch (`D81`), item `I82` is returned, inspected, repacked into `P82` (replacing `P81` for `I82`), and redelivered (`D82`), followed by a single invoice and payment.
- **Risks and opportunities**: Direct reverse-logistics cost, extended order-to-cash cycle time, and quality/transit-damage signals. Opportunity for root-cause analysis on packaging failure or carrier handling.
- **Poor traditional flattening**: Flattening on `Order` loses which specific item (`I82`, not `I81`) caused the return and which package was replaced; flattening on `Delivery` severs the causal link between `D81` and replacement shipment `D82`.
- **Stability under data repair**: High. Even if O2O qualifiers are dropped, `Return Item` and `Inspect Return` keep `S6` cleanly separated from all forward-only clusters.

## 5. Borderline cases

### X1 versus X2 (Identifier renaming and symmetric event order)
Both executions represent identical single-package orders; X2 merely renames identifiers and logs `Add Item` for `I22` before `I21`. Under $E_{\text{act}}$, $E_{\text{iso}}$, and $E_{\text{sem}}$, X1 and X2 **agree** (same cluster) because local IDs carry no semantics and swapping `I22  I11`, `I21  I12` yields an exact isomorphism. They would only **disagree** under a flawed key that retains raw IDs or assumes numeric suffixes encode mandatory processing order (e.g., SKU priority encoded in IDs rather than attributes).

### X1 versus X5 (Isolated logistics versus cross-order package sharing)
Under $E_{\text{act}}$ and $E_{\text{loc}}$ (projecting onto local objects only), X1 and X5 **merge** because O5's own activity trace and local object graph are identical to X1's. Under $E_{\text{iso}}$ and $E_{\text{sem}}$, they **split** (`S1` vs. `S3`) because `EXT-OA` and `EXT-IA` couple O5's physical fulfillment to another order. Agreement is defensible when analyzing order-level billing throughput alone; separation is essential for warehouse bottlenecks, SLA contagion, and loss liability.

### X1 versus X6 (Isolated billing versus cross-order invoice sharing)
As with X5, $E_{\text{act}}$ and $E_{\text{loc}}$ **merge** X1 and X6 because local logistics and event labels match X1. Under $E_{\text{iso}}$ and $E_{\text{sem}}$, they **split** (`S1` vs. `S4`) because `V61` co-bills `EXT-OB` and `M61` lacks order-level allocation. A purely physical fulfillment analyst might reasonably merge X1 and X6 (identical warehouse and delivery behavior), whereas finance, audit, and cash-application teams must keep them distinct.

### X1 versus X7 (Happy path versus hidden pre-dispatch substitution)
Under $E_{\text{act}}$—and even under a qualifier-blind object-count equivalence—X1 and X7 **merge**, sharing identical activity sequences and local object counts `(1,2,1,1,1,1)`. Under $E_{\text{iso}}$ and $E_{\text{sem}}$, they **split** (`S1` vs. `S5`) due to `I72 replaces I71`, `removed_before_dispatch`, and `Item:I72` in `Dispatch`. Merging is defensible only if pre-dispatch internal substitutions that still meet on-time single-delivery SLAs are deemed customer-invisible; separating is mandatory for inventory accuracy, quality control, and billing compliance.

### X3 versus X4 (Multi-package consolidated delivery versus split delivery and partial payment)
Under a warehouse-multiplicity equivalence focusing solely on `Package` count (`P = 2`), X3 and X4 **merge** as "multi-package orders." Under $E_{\text{sem}}$ (and $E_{\text{iso}}$), they **split** (`S1` vs. `S2`): X3's two packages ride on one delivery and settle in one payment, whereas X4 splits both physical deliveries (`D41`, `D42`) and financial settlement (`M41`, `M42`). Warehouse capacity planners may group X3 and X4 together because both double packing effort; commercial and credit analysts should keep X3 with `S1` and isolate X4.

### X4 versus X8 (Split forward fulfillment versus post-dispatch return and redelivery)
Both executions generate two packages and two deliveries (`Pack -> Dispatch` twice). If payment multiplicity is treated as an attribute rather than structure, X4 and X8 even share identical local object counts `(1,2,2,2,1,1)`, causing count-based clusterings to **merge** them as "two-shipment orders." Under $E_{\text{act}}$, $E_{\text{iso}}$, and $E_{\text{sem}}$, they **split** sharply (`S2` vs. `S6`): X4 ships two distinct items in planned/staggered waves, whereas X8 executes reverse logistics (`Return Item`, `Inspect Return`) to redeliver the *same* item `I82` in a replacement package.

## 6. Relation to `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel`

In PM4Py, `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel, object_type="Order", max_objs=...)` decomposes an OCEL into object-centric executions anchored on the specified lead `object_type` and groups executions that share an exact structural key. That key encodes the execution's ordered event structure, object types, and event-to-object relational topology (and, depending on OCEL 2.0 extraction settings, object-level lifecycles and interaction graph), yielding a partition conceptually close to $E_{\text{iso}}$.

Exact keys become overly fine in practice for three reasons:
1. **Identifier and position sensitivity**: If raw object identifiers leak into labels, or if canonical indices depend on arbitrary tie-breaking of concurrent events, isomorphic executions (or X1 vs. X2 under naive keying) fragment into singletons.
2. **Exact cardinality explosion**: Orders with 2, 3, 4, or 20 items (or 1 vs. 2 cartons as in X1 vs. X3) receive distinct keys even when their business behavior is identical.
3. **Interleaving jitter**: Independent parallel activities (e.g., `Pack` of item 2 before or after `Dispatch` of item 1 in X4) generate separate total-order keys.

To obtain actionable business clusters, PM4Py's returned exact clusters should be **post-processed and re-aggregated**: each cluster representative (or sub-OCEL) is mapped through an ID-invariant canonicalizer like `canonical_semantic_signature`, tagging non-lead `Order` objects and their exclusive children as `EXT`, collapsing homogeneous phases, bucketing commercial cardinalities, and merging fine exact clusters that share the same semantic signature.

**When exact equivalence is preferable**: Exact equivalence ($E_{\text{iso}}$ or raw `cluster_equivalent_ocel`) is superior for forensic auditing, regression testing of ERP integrations, conformance replay, and detecting subtle schema drift (such as `Item` unexpectedly appearing in `Dispatch` in X7).

**Impact of `max_objs` and large connected executions**: Shared logistics hubs (X5) and consolidated invoices (X6) create transitive bridges across orders. Without a `max_objs` cap, connected-component extraction can snowball into giant components containing thousands of orders, exhausting memory and collapsing distinct executions into one unclusterable blob. Conversely, setting `max_objs` too small silently drops or truncates hub executions—erasing X5 and X6 from analysis unless truncated executions are explicitly quarantined and their boundary-crossing objects tagged as `EXT`.

```python
import pm4py
from collections import defaultdict

# Assumes `ocel` is an existing PM4Py OCEL 2.0 object and
# `canonical_semantic_signature` from Section 2 is imported.

LEAD_TYPE = "Order"
MAX_OBJS = 50  # Guard against giant shared-invoice / bulk-truck components

# 1. Exact structural clustering in PM4Py
exact_clusters = pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(
    ocel, object_type=LEAD_TYPE, max_objs=MAX_OBJS
)

def sub_ocel_to_semantic_signature(sub_ocel, lead_order_id):
    """Canonicalize one lead-Order sub-OCEL, tagging foreign Orders/Items as EXT."""
    rel = sub_ocel.relations  # columns: ocel:eid, ocel:activity, ocel:timestamp, ocel:oid, ocel:type
    o2o_df = getattr(sub_ocel, "o2o", None)

    # Identify external orders (any Order other than the focal lead order)
    all_orders = set(rel.loc[rel["ocel:type"] == LEAD_TYPE, "ocel:oid"])
    ext_orders = all_orders - {lead_order_id}
    ext_objs = set(ext_orders)

    # Propagate EXT tag to objects exclusively owned by external orders via O2O 'contains'
    o2o_edges = []
    if o2o_df is not None and len(o2o_df) > 0:
        type_map = dict(zip(rel["ocel:oid"], rel["ocel:type"]))
        for row in o2o_df.itertuples(index=False):
            src, tgt, qual = row[0], row[1], row[2]
            if src in ext_orders and qual == "contains":
                ext_objs.add(tgt)
            o2o_edges.append((
                type_map.get(src, "Unknown"),
                f"EXT-{src}" if src in ext_objs else str(src),
                str(qual),
                type_map.get(tgt, "Unknown"),
                f"EXT-{tgt}" if tgt in ext_objs else str(tgt),
            ))

    # Build event tuples sorted by timestamp
    events = []
    for (eid, ts, act), grp in rel.groupby(
        ["ocel:eid", "ocel:timestamp", "ocel:activity"], sort=True
    ):
        e2o = [
            (r["ocel:type"], f"EXT-{r['ocel:oid']}" if r["ocel:oid"] in ext_objs else str(r["ocel:oid"]))
            for _, r in grp.iterrows()
        ]
        events.append((ts, act, e2o))

    return canonical_semantic_signature(events, o2o_edges)

# 2. Post-process & aggregate exact PM4Py clusters into business-semantic clusters
semantic_clusters = defaultdict(list)

for exact_key, cluster_members in exact_clusters.items():
    members = cluster_members if isinstance(cluster_members, list) else [cluster_members]
    for sub_ocel in members:
        order_ids = sorted(
            sub_ocel.relations.loc[sub_ocel.relations["ocel:type"] == LEAD_TYPE, "ocel:oid"].unique()
        )
        # Anchor on each local lead order (or flag multi-order hubs)
        lead_id = order_ids[0]
        sem_sig = sub_ocel_to_semantic_signature(sub_ocel, lead_order_id=lead_id)
        semantic_clusters[sem_sig].append((exact_key, lead_id, sub_ocel))

# 3. Inspect coverage to catch executions dropped by MAX_OBJS
all_ocel_orders = set(ocel.objects.loc[ocel.objects["ocel:type"] == LEAD_TYPE, "ocel:oid"])
clustered_orders = {lead_id for items in semantic_clusters.values() for _, lead_id, _ in items}
oversized_orders = all_ocel_orders - clustered_orders  # Quarantine for hub-level inspection
```

## 7. Robustness analysis

Testing $E_{\text{iso}}$ and $E_{\text{sem}}$ against five perturbations reveals which cluster boundaries are structurally robust and which depend critically on data fidelity.

1. **Ignore all O2O qualifiers**:
   - Under $E_{\text{iso}}$ and our full $E_{\text{sem}}$ (which keeps typed E2O profiles in `phase_trace`), cluster membership remains unchanged: X4 is still isolated by `SPLIT_LOOP` and `D:2+, M:2+`; X7 is still isolated by `Item` in `Dispatch`; X8 is still isolated by `Return Item` and `Inspect Return`.
   - However, semantic interpretability degrades: X4's two payments become indistinguishable from duplicate payments, and X7's `Item`-in-`Dispatch` link loses its `replaces`/`removed_before_dispatch` explanation. Crucially, under a coarser semantic signature that omits E2O type profiles, **X7 silently collapses into `S1` (`{X1, X2, X3, X7}`)**.

2. **Ignore external objects**:
   - Erasing `EXT-OA`, `EXT-IA`, and `EXT-OB` causes **X5 and X6 to collapse directly into the baseline** under both $E_{\text{iso}}$ (`{X1, X2, X5, X6}`) and $E_{\text{sem}}$ (`{X1, X2, X3, X5, X6}`). Because their local subgraphs are completely standard, cross-order coupling is the most fragile dimension under naive data extraction.

3. **Collapse repeated same-label events**:
   - Under *consecutive run-length collapsing*, `A2` (X3) merges into `A1` under $E_{\text{act}}$, mirroring how $E_{\text{sem}}$ already absorbs X3 into `S1`, while X4 and X8 stay distinct.
   - Under *global activity deduplication* (retaining only the set or first occurrence of activity labels), **both X3 and X4 collapse into `A1`** under $E_{\text{act}}$, leaving only X8 separate. Under $E_{\text{sem}}$, X4 survives via `D:2+, M:2+` and payment qualifiers, though its interleaved fulfillment cadence disappears.

4. **Treat `Pack Package` and `Dispatch` order as a partial order**:
   - Replacing total order with per-package causal precedence (`Pack(P_i) < Dispatch(P_i)`) leaves X1–X8 in the exact same clusters: X3's two packages still converge on one `Dispatch(D31)`, X4's two `Pack -> Dispatch` chains stay two parallel deliveries, and X8's return loop remains causally serialized through `D81` and `I82`.
   - More importantly, partial-order modeling improves *robustness* on unseen data: a split-delivery order logged as `Pack(P1), Pack(P2), Dispatch(D1), Dispatch(D2)` stays in `S2` alongside X4 rather than falsely collapsing its consecutive `Pack` and `Dispatch` runs under total-order run-length encoding.

5. **Treat payment multiplicity as an amount attribute rather than structure**:
   - Collapsing `M41` and `M42` into a single `Payment` object with an installment-count attribute removes `M:2+` and `partially_settles`/`completes_settlement` from X4. X4 still remains distinct from X3 (`D:2+` vs. `D:1`) and X8 (no return events), but loses its credit-risk distinction from single-payment split deliveries. Under a naive object-count equivalence, X4 and X8 would then share identical counts `(1,2,2,2,1,1)` and falsely merge.

## 8. Limitations and recommendation

No single equivalence relation can simultaneously optimize precision, interpretability, and scalability across all process-mining use cases because every abstraction trades sensitivity for generalization. Coarsening internal cartonization (`S1`) improves executive clarity but hides packing overhead; retaining exact object counts and interleavings ($E_{\text{iso}}$) detects every anomaly but explodes into thousands of tiny variants on real enterprise logs.

Recommended equivalence levels by purpose:

- **Compliance monitoring  $E_{\text{iso}}$ (or $E_{\text{sem}}$ augmented with strict E2O/O2O rules)**: Compliance requires zero false negatives on hidden exceptions and boundary violations. Exact preservation of `EXT` coupling (X5, X6), O2O qualifiers (X7's substitution, X4's settlement rules), and unexpected E2O bindings ensures auditability and prevents silent collapses.
- **Operational segmentation  $E_{\text{sem}}$ (preferred commercial-and-exception equivalence)**: Operations teams need stable, actionable cohorts—clean fulfillment (`S1`), split shipments (`S2`), shared logistics (`S3`), shared billing (`S4`), substitutions (`S5`), and returns (`S6`)—without fragmenting cohorts every time an order contains 3 items instead of 2.
- **Root-cause analysis  Hybrid two-stage workflow ($E_{\text{sem}}$ to isolate, then $E_{\text{iso}}$ within cluster)**: Use $E_{\text{sem}}$ to isolate problem cohorts (such as `S2`, `S5`, or `S6`), then drill down with $E_{\text{iso}}$ (preserving exact item-to-package bindings, timestamps, and O2O qualifiers) to pinpoint *which* item or carton triggered rework, substitution, or delay.
- **Descriptive reporting  Coarse $E_{\text{sem}}$ (or $E_{\text{act}}$ only when annotated with boundary/exception flags)**: Management dashboards require compact, stable KPIs (e.g., percentage straight-through vs. split vs. return vs. shared-boundary). Pure $E_{\text{act}}$ should never be reported alone for object-centric logs, as it misclassifies X5, X6, and X7 as baseline happy paths.

Ultimately, equivalence design in object-centric process mining is a modeling decision, not a one-time preprocessing step: the right partition is defined by which business differences an organization can afford to ignore and which it cannot afford to miss.
