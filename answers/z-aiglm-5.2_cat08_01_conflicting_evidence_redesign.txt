1. Current-process diagnosis
The current process suffers from a severe contradiction between front-end efficiency and downstream quality/compliance. The most frequent path—Auto eligibility screen to Fast approval (2,550 cases) to Dispatch replacement (2,300 cases)—is highly attractive on initial speed (mean 1.0h to Fast approval, 3.0h to Dispatch) and pre-rework cost (31% cheaper). However, this path generates significant downstream failure. Process-mining artifacts reveal the following problems:
- **High rework and long tail on the fast path:** The edge `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` occurs 260 times with a mean of 96 hours and a p90 of 180 hours. Complaints occur in 14.1% of Fast approval cases, compared to only 3.3% in the Repair path. This hidden cost reduces the fast path's cost advantage to just 9% after accounting for complaints and service credits.
- **Compliance violations for high-value devices:** Alignment diagnostics show frequent "Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices." This violates Normative rule R1 (claims above EUR 400 require Manual/Fraud review) and Normative rule R2 (no dispatch before evidence completeness).
- **Segment-based outcome variance:** Premium high-value devices experience a 22% complaint/reopen rate on the fast path, versus 5% on the reviewed/repair path. The fast path is only truly effective for Premium and Standard low-risk accessories, which have low complaint rates (6% and 8%) and tolerate fast dispatches.
- **Inefficient evidence loops:** The "Request missing evidence" activity occurs at least twice in 26% of cases that enter it. The path `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` takes a mean of 34 hours, creating a massive bottleneck for unclear defect claims (which have a 140-hour median cycle time and high abandonment).
- **Low model precision:** Only 68% of traces fit the normative Petri net, with precision being low because low-frequency paths inappropriately mix auto approval, risk review, and repair in violation of policy.

2. Redesign A: Segment-Aware Guardrails and Pre-Dispatch Validation
**Redesigned process flow:** 
Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen -> [Segment Check]
- *High-value devices:* Enforce Evidence completion -> Fraud/risk review -> Pre-dispatch quality gate -> Dispatch replacement.
- *Low-risk accessories:* Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement (no pre-dispatch gate).

**Targets:** 
Premium and Standard high-value device segments, specifically the paths violating R1 and R2.

**Mechanism of improvement:** 
By mandating evidence completion and risk review before dispatch for high-value items, the redesign eliminates the 22% and 18% fast-path complaint rates for these segments. The lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate (adding 6-18 hours) intercepts the wrong replacement part selection (which causes the 96-hour mean complaint tail and EUR 410 expected error cost). This aligns the process with R1 and R2, turning the compliance exceptions into standard practice.

**Sacrifices / Unresolved:** 
Deliberately sacrifices cycle time for high-value devices, pushing them from 20-28 hours to the 96-120 hour reviewed/repair path territory. It heavily increases workload on the Fraud/risk review team (already a bottleneck on Mondays), leaving capacity constraints unresolved unless staffed.

3. Redesign B: Digitized Evidence Resolution Loop
**Redesigned process flow:** 
Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen -> Request missing evidence -> Evidence Portal -> Automated completeness validation -> Auto eligibility screen -> Decision.

**Targets:** 
Unclear defect / missing evidence segment (22% of cases) and the `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` loop (890 cases, 34h mean).

**Mechanism of improvement:** 
Decouples evidence collection from the manual reviewer queue. The evidence portal (reducing repeated requests by 30-45%) allows customers to upload proof, which an automated check validates before returning the case to the Auto screen. This breaks the 26% re-loop rate and bypasses the 34-hour manual review wait, drastically reducing the 140-hour median cycle time for this segment.

**Sacrifices / Unresolved:** 
Sacrifices customers with low digital literacy, who may abandon the process when forced into a portal, potentially increasing the 320 `Request missing evidence -> Claim abandoned` count. It does not resolve the replacement dispatch errors for high-value devices.

  1. *Failure:* Parts price confirmation is not actually automated, causing the auto-generated quote to be inaccurate and delaying `Customer approval`.
