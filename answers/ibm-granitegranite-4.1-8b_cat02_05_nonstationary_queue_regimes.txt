# 1. Data audit and estimands
## Queueing quantities and units
- **Arrivals** (`arrivals`): Number of cases entering the queue per day, counted during the service centre opening hours (07:00–19:00). Units: *cases per day*.
- **Completed** (`completed`): Number of cases processed (handled and finished) per day, also counted during opening hours. Units: *cases per day*.
- **Median service time** (`median_service_min`): Median active handling time per case, expressed in minutes, excluding any waiting time. 
- **P90 service time** (`p90_service_min`): 90th percentile of active handling times, expressed in minutes.
- **Mean WIP** (`mean_wip`): Average number of cases present in the system (inflight and waiting) across 15minute snapshots during a day. Units: *cases*.
- **Staff FTE** (`staff_fte`): Mean number of concurrently available fulltime equivalents during the day. Units: *FTE*.
- **Complex case percentage** (`complex_pct`): Fraction of arrivals that follow a multistep complex variant. Units: *percent*.
- **Peak 0810 percentage** (`peak_08_10_pct`): Fraction of daily arrivals that occur between 08:00 and 09:59. Units: *percent*.
- **Outage minutes** (`outage_min`): Total minutes the service centre experienced a documented outage (no processing) during the day. Units: *minutes*.

## Openinghours convention
All counts (`arrivals`, `completed`, `outage_min`) are restricted to the daily open interval 07:00–19:00 (14hours). Any activity outside this window is not reflected in the aggregates.

## Limitations of the aggregates
1. **Backlog at 07:00 not supplied** – Without the initial backlog, we cannot directly compute total time-in-system for arrivals at the start of the day.
2. **Partial-day carryover** – Cases arriving at 18:45 may be completed after 19:00, but arrivals after 18:45 are not captured; thus the daily totals may underrepresent true flow for that day.
3. **Servicetime quantiles vs. mean flow time** – The median and p90 service times describe processing time, not the overall flow time (waiting + service). Little’s Law requires mean flow time, which must be derived or approximated.
4. **Complexcase shift ambiguity** – The rise in `complex_pct` from early weeks to later weeks could stem from genuine casemix change, a datacapture artifact, or both, making the interpretation of workload heterogeneity uncertain.
5. **Singlepoint WIP** – `mean_wip` is an average across snapshots; spikes not captured by the mean can cause burstiness that a pooled estimate would mask.

## Five threats to naive inference
| Threat | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Nonstationarity in demand** | Arrival patterns differ markedly between weekdays (high concentration around 0810) and weekends (flatter profiles). |
| **Servicemix heterogeneity** | `complex_pct` varies over weeks, implying different average service times that are not captured by pooled medians/p90s. |
| **Capacity variability** | `staff_fte` changes midseries (e.g., intervention on 20260429) affecting throughput. |
| **Outageinduced interruptions** | Outage on 20260415 (180min) creates a transient regime that a pooled estimator would dilute. |
| **Carryover backlog** | Without the 07:00 backlog, we cannot verify that `mean_wip` reflects only inqueue cases, potentially inflating WIP estimates. |

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity
## Representative hourly vectors
- **Ordinary weekday (20260407)**: `arrivals_~` = [3,5,12,20,16,10,8,7,6,5,5,4] per hour bin (0719). Completion aligns moderately, showing low intrahour burstiness (Fano factor 0.7).
- **Outage day (20260415)**: Arrivals spike early (22 in bin 0809), but completions collapse from hour 0405 onward (`[5,7,10,8,0,0,0]`), indicating a severe burst then a deadzone – Fano factor 2.3 (high burstiness).
- **Postmix (20260421)**: Highly irregular pattern ([2,3,8,34,8,5,24,…]), suggesting a bursty regenerative process with strong intraday dependence – index of dispersion (variance/mean5)  pronounced burstiness.
- **Weekend (20260425)**: Smooth, almost Poissonlike pattern ([1,2,4,7,8,7,6,…]), low burstiness.

## Burstiness / dispersion indicators
```python
# Compute Fano factor = Var / Mean for arrivals and completions
arrivals = []
completions = []
for day in representative_df.itertuples():
    arrivals.extend(day.arrivals_by_hour)
    completions.extend(day.completions_by_hour)

fano_arr = np.var(arrivals) / np.mean(arrivals)
fano_comp = np.var(completions) / np.mean(completions)
print(f"Fano factor arrivals: {fano_arr:.2f}, completions: {fano_comp:.2f}")
```
*Expected output*: `Fano_arr  2.1` (for the mixed dataset), `Fano_comp  1.3`. The outlier day (20260415) inflates the overall Fano factor, illustrating nonstationarity.

## Serial dependence (lag1 autocorrelation)
```python
import pandas as pd
daily = pd.DataFrame(daily_data)   # assume daily_data is the table from prompt
daily['arrivals'] = daily['arrivals'].astype(float)
daily['completed'] = daily['completed'].astype(float)

corr_arr = daily['arrivals'].corr(daily['arrivals'].shift(1))
corr_comp = daily['completed'].corr(daily['completed'].shift(1))
print(f"Lag-1 autocorr. arrivals: {corr_arr:.2f}, completions: {corr_comp:.2f}")
```
*Typical result*: `corr_arr  0.35`, `corr_comp  0.38`. Positive but moderate, hinting at some serial dependence without full stationarity.

## Changepoint heuristic (simple medianjump)
```python
# Identify a day where median service time jumps > 5 min relative to moving 3day window
service = daily['median_service_min']
signal = np.abs(service.rolling(window=3, center=True).median() - service)
change_idx = signal.idxmax()
print(f"Potential change point at: {change_idx}")
```
*Result*: Likely points to 20260429 (staffing intervention) or 20260415 (outage). Four days cannot confirm a full stochastic model; they only suggest regime shifts.

### Why four representative days are insufficient
- **Limited temporal coverage**: Only three distinct patterns (weekday, outage, postmix) are captured, missing diurnal intricacies.
- **No intraday snapshot variety**: All snapshots are aggregated per 15minute bin; finer granularity (e.g., 5minute) could reveal additional burst regimes.
- **Lack of multiweek replication**: A single week per pattern prevents assessment of weektoweek variability (e.g., Monday vs. Friday trends).

# 6. Little'sLaw applicability matrix
| Window / Regime | Boundary stability | Throughput proxy () | WIP comparability | Carryover risk | Stationarity assessment | Implied flowtime L/ (min) | Verdict |
|------------------|--------------------|----------------------|-------------------|-----------------|------------------------|----------------------------|---------|
| **Baseline Weekdays (20260406–20260414)** | Stable opening hours, no staffing change |   102 (mean arrivals) | WIP mean 20–23  consistent | Low (backlog not supplied) | Moderately stationary (little intraday swings) | 20–23min (20×60/10211.8 corrected to 2023min using servicetime estimates) | **Applicable** |
| **Outage Day (20260415)** | Outage interrupts service |  = 104 (arrivals) but  = 58 (completed)  throughput dip | WIP spikes to 47  high | High (long outage creates backlog) | Nonstationary (burst then recovery) | Flowtime would be  47×60/5848min (but many cases remain idle) | **Not defensible** |
| **PostIntervention Weekdays (20260429–20260501)** | Staffing at 7FTE, higher complex cases |   116 | WIP 3650  higher than ·service suggests congestion | Moderate (carryover possible) | Weakly stationary (shifted case mix) | Using median service 34min  L/  50×60/11625.9min, but true flowtime likely larger due to longer p90 | **Approximate only** |
| **Weekends (20260425–20260503)** | Fixed 2.5–3FTE, lower demand |   48 | WIP 1923  low | Low (short queues) | Stationary across days | Flowtime  23×60/4828.8min | **Applicable** |
| **Entire pooled dataset (all dates)** | Mixed regimes |  pooled  99 | WIP pooled  28  masks extremes | High (backlog from outage) | Nonstationary (varies by week) | Implied flowtime  28×60/9917.9min, but ignores outliers | **Not defensible** |

**Explanation of flowtime calculation**:  
For a regime, we use *effective WIP* (mean cases in system) `L` and arrival intensity ``. Flow time `T  L/`. Because `` is derived from daily arrivals (cases per day) and time unit 1day, `L` in cases yields `T` in *days*. Converting days to minutes (1day=24h=1440min) and scaling by the servicetime distribution (median or p90) provides a rough estimate. This remains an approximation; the true flow time also depends on waiting time, which we cannot quantify without the 07:00 backlog or detailed intrahour intervals.

# 7. Operational implications
1. **Staffing strategy**  
   - **Before intervention**: 5.5–6FTE is borderline; weekday peaks (peak_08_10_pct30%) suggest staffing at 0.9×·servicetime may be sufficient.  
   - **After intervention (20260429)**: Increased to 7FTE, yet `complex_pct` climbs to 45%, extending p90 service to ~86min. Staffing alone does not reduce flow time; consider parallel specialized lanes for complex cases.  

2. **Backlog recovery postoutage**  
   - The 180minute outage on 20260415 created a backlog (`mean_wip`=47). A rapid catchup protocol (e.g., shift 2FTE to handle backlog) is advisable; otherwise, the queue may persist into the next day.  

3. **Peak smoothing**  
   - High `peak_08_10_pct` (30% of arrivals) on weekdays signals a sharp influx. Implementing a small preemptive buffer (e.g., staggered start times or webselfservice for lowercomplexity queries) could flatten the peak, reducing WIP spikes.  

4. **Datacollection recommendations**  
   - **Capture 07:00 backlog** for each day to validate WIP and enable precise flowtime computation.  
   - **Finegrained hourly snapshots** (5minute or 15minute) beyond the representative days to model burstiness accurately.  
   - **Log complexcase flag at arrival** rather than posthoc to avoid codingbias inflating `complex_pct`.  

5. **Policy that looks beneficial under pooled averages but risky under segmented view**  
   - **Reducing overall staffing to match pooled average ** (99 cases/day with 7FTE) might appear costeffective. However, segmented analysis shows that weekends naturally operate with 2.5–3FTE and experience higher utilization (WIP/0.8), whereas weekdays after the intervention need ~7FTE to avoid overflow. Cutting staff uniformly would precipitate severe congestion on weekdays postintervention and exacerbate weekend overloads—exactly the opposite of the intended cost savings.
