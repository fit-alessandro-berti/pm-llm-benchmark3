## 1. Decision rule  

**Eligibility** – A case may receive a “Senior Diagnostic Swarm” only while its stage is **Triage** or **Diagnosis Started**. Cases already at **Diagnosis Complete** (e.g., I03) are automatically excluded because the intervention has almost no effect after that point.  

**Alert persistence & cooldown** – Alerts are generated every 30min. Upon an alert a **2hour cooldown** is triggered. During the cooldown, further alerts for the same case are suppressed **unless a material new event** is logged (e.g., a new diagnostic log, change in case parameters, SLA milestone shift). If a new event occurs, the alert is reissued and the case reenters the eligibility queue.  

**Repetition threshold** –  
- **Three or more alerts** within a 6hour window **without** a new event  case enters **suppression**; no swarm allocated until a new event occurs.  
- **Exactly two alerts** without a new event  the case may still receive a swarm **only if** the adjusted absolute riskreduction midpoint exceeds0.20 **and** the case is not already under a swarm; otherwise it proceeds to cooldown.  

**Calibration adjustment (applied to the predicted probability before computing benefit)**  
| Segment | Adjustment | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| SME | +0.065 (midpoint of underprediction 0.04–0.09) | Model understates breach risk |
| Strategic Enterprise | –0.085 (midpoint of overprediction 0.05–0.12) | Workflow redesign caused overstatement |
| Public Sector | none (moderate sample, acceptable calibration) | Keep asis, note uncertainty |
| NonNativeLanguage Support | keep asis but flag high uncertainty | Small validation sample |

**Prioritization score** – For each eligible case we compute an **Expected Net Benefit (ENB)**:  

\[
\text{ENB}= (\text{midpoint of absolute riskreduction})\times(\text{value of avoiding breach}) - €450\;(\text{swarm cost}) - (\text{falsealarm burden}) - (\text{expected spillover harm})
\]

Expected spillover harm = (number of untreated diagnosisstage cases at the moment of intervention)×0.02 (midpoint of 0.01–0.03)×(average value of those untreated cases). The untreatedcase count is updated dynamically as cases are intervened upon.  

**Allocation rule** – Select cases in descending order of ENB, subject to the alertpersistence rules and the constraint that **no more than four swarms are deployed per day**, with **one slot reserved** for unexpected regulatory incidents (management preference). If a selected case is a diagnosisstage case, it is removed from the untreatedcase pool, reducing spillover costs for any subsequent selections.  

**Minimum evidence change** – For repeated alerts, a material new event must be logged; otherwise the case waits for the next cooldown cycle.

- **Direct economic benefit** =  (riskreduction×value). For optimistic: 0.28×€60k+0.15×€100k+0.22×€25k=€37,300; lowerhalf midpoints give €25,150.  
- **Spillover harm** = (slots×untreatedcase count×midpoint0.01–0.03)×average untreated value (I06€15k + I09€20k €17.5k). Example: 3 slots×3 untreated×0.01×€17.5k=€1,575 €1.4k–€2.8k range.  
- **Net impact** = Direct benefit–€1,350–Spillover harm (using scenariospecific bounds).

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy  

| Concept | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Cooldown** | 2hour window after an alert is issued. Further alerts for the same case are suppressed unless a material new event is logged. |
| **Suppression** | If a case accumulates **3 alerts** within a 6hour window **without** a new event, it is placed in suppression; no swarm may be allocated until a new event occurs. |
| **Escalation** | A case with exactly **2 alerts** without a new event may still receive a swarm only when the adjusted absolute riskreduction midpoint>0.20 and no prior swarm has been performed; otherwise it proceeds to cooldown. |
| **Reentry** | After cooldown expires, the case reenters the eligibility queue. If a new event was logged during cooldown, the alert is reissued and the case is treated as a fresh alert. |

**Comparison of I05 and I07**  

- **I05** has generated a **third alert** with **no new event** since the first alert. According to the repetition threshold, this triggers **suppression**; the case must wait for a material new event before any swarm consideration.  
- **I07** has also generated a **third alert**, but a **new customer diagnostic log** has been uploaded, constituting a **material new event**. The alert is therefore reevaluated; the case exits cooldown and is reeligible for intervention, despite the slightly lower current risk estimate (p_now=0.72 vs. previous 0.75).  

Thus, the presence of a new event differentiates a *suppressed* case (I05) from a *reeligible* case (I07).

## 5. Fairness and feedbackloop risks  

| Risk | Discussion | Concrete safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| **Valuebased prioritization starving lowervalue cases** | Cases with high contractual value (e.g., I08€100k) naturally rank higher, potentially leaving lowvalue but still missioncritical cases without swarm attention for extended periods. | **Rotate a minimumquota per value quartile**: e.g., reserve at least one slot per cycle for a case in the bottomvalue quartile, regardless of ENB ranking. |
| **Uncertainty for NonNativeLanguage Support** | Small validation sample leads to vague calibration and interventioneffect estimates; decisions may be biased or miscalibrated. | **Segmentspecific calibration monitoring**: update SME/NNL adjustment factors monthly using the most recent breach outcomes; if uncertainty (e.g., wide confidence intervals) exceeds a preset threshold, default to “wait” rather than intervene. |
| **Differential calibration across segments** | SME underpredicts, Strategic Enterprise overpredicts; using raw probabilities would systematically misallocate slots. | **Apply the segment adjustments listed in Section1** as a mandatory step before any ENB calculation; document the adjustment factor in the case file for auditability. |
| **Selective intervention changing future training labels** | Cases that receive a swarm and subsequently avoid breach may be labelled “successful intervention” in the training set, biasing the model toward overestimating effectiveness. | **Maintain a “shadow” control set**: for every intervened case, keep a comparable nonintervened case with similar baseline characteristics; use the paired outcomes to estimate the *causal* effect (ATE) and adjust the predictive model only after confirming a stable positive effect. |
| **Spillover harms falling systematically on untreated groups** | Untreated diagnosisstage cases (often lowervalue or SME) may bear increased breach probability each time a slot is used, creating a hidden inequity. | **Spillover impact tracking**: after each swarm, record breach outcomes of all untreated diagnosisstage cases. If the aggregate increase exceeds €2k per slot on average, **temporarily pause** further slot allocations until a mitigation plan (e.g., additional diagnostic resources) is enacted. |

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan  

| Monitoring dimension | What to track | Frequency | Action triggers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Operational** | Swarm utilisation (slots used / available), queue delay added per slot (minutes), number of cases in each stage | Every shift (every 8h) | If slot utilisation>80% for three consecutive shifts, review capacity allocation rule; if average queue delay>20min per slot, consider pausing further interventions. |
| **Predictive** | Distribution of p_now across segments; calibration plots (observed breach vs. predicted probability) | Every 30min (model refresh) + daily summary | If segmentspecific Brier score increases>0.02 relative to baseline, reestimate calibration adjustments (add/subtract the SME/SE/NNL factors). |
| **Causaleffect** | Actual breach outcome for intervened vs. matched nonintervened cases; estimate average treatment effect (ATE) per segment | After each swarm (realtime) and monthly aggregate | If 95% CI of ATE no longer includes the midpoint used in ENB (e.g., 0.19 for Strategic Enterprise), update the riskreduction range for that segment. |
| **Capacity** | Remaining swarm slots, waitinglist length, aggregate delay to nonintervened diagnosisstage cases | Realtime dashboard | If remaining slots=0 before 15:00 and no regulatory incident, log “capacity exhausted”; if spillover harm estimate (from causal monitoring) exceeds €2k per slot, trigger a policy review (possible temporary reduction in slot pool). |
| **Fairness** | Intervention counts per segment, per value quartile, per languagesupport group; spillover impact on untreated groups | Weekly report | If any segment receives <5% of slots for two consecutive weeks *or* spillover impact on a protected group exceeds the preset ceiling, initiate a fairness audit and adjust the minimumquota rotation rule. |

**Policychange decision process**  
1. **Data aggregation** – compile the above metrics over a rolling 30day window.  
2. **Threshold check** – if any metric crosses its trigger (see column “Action triggers”), a **policyreview flag** is raised.  
3. **Rootcause analysis** – a shortlife working group (operations, datascience, segment leads) determines whether the drift is due to model decay, capacity bottleneck, or external factors.  
4. **Adjustment** – possible actions: (a) recalibrate segment adjustment factors; (b) modify the ENB formula (e.g., weight spillover differently); (c) adjust the minimumquota rotation; (d) temporarily suspend swarm allocations for a defined period.  
5. **Communication** – publish a brief “policystatus” note to all caseowners and senior management, documenting the change and its rationale.  

This monitoringandrecalibration loop ensures the earlywarning policy remains **decisiontheoretically coherent**, **operationally realistic**, and **explicitly aware of uncertainty** while guarding against hidden fairness and spillover harms.
