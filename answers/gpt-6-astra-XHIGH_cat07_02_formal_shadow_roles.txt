# 1. Proposed role structure

Use a layered structure: a primary operational role, secondary capabilities, and time-limited delegations. These are proposed interpretations of observed work, not a reconstruction of reporting lines or an authorization register. A resource may contribute to several roles without being counted as several people in a staffing plan.

# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

A core activity is characteristic of a role's continuing responsibility, supported by work patterns, case context, and organizational remit. Peripheral work may support that responsibility, represent cover or training, or have a narrower meaning than its label suggests. Neither frequent nor occasional execution proves decision authority.

- **Automated intake:** A1/A2 are Nora's complete observed profile and operational core. Her A2 is explicitly described as a precheck; distinguish its output from a human entitlement decision. There is no evidence of a peripheral resolution capability. Examine the automated-to-human handoff rather than equating validation counts.
- **Front-office resolution:** Repeated A1/A2/A4/A12 and routine sequence fragments establish the core. Technical and commercial touches generally support customer resolution, subject to complexity and delegation. Amira's broader exposure suggests a more experienced generalist contribution than Ben's profile alone shows, but does not prove superior performance. Giorgio's recurring A7/A8 work warrants a secondary capability beyond incidental assistance.
- **Simple-request support:** The temps share the front-office backbone but have demonstrated an overwhelmingly low-complexity scope. Diagnosis and negotiation remain peripheral. Their contracts and short tenure provide context, not automatic competence ratings. The 17 reopens justify examining documentation and supervision; without a denominator and comparison group, they neither establish a reopen rate nor show that every temp closure was defective.
- **Technical delivery:** A3/A5/A10 are core for both analysts. Chen's higher complexity and security-adjacent work support an advanced-case specialization within that family. His late shift and routing may partly explain exposure, so difficulty should not be attributed solely to ability. A6 remains peripheral unless task details establish a continuing security review responsibility. Configuration certification does not establish security sign-off authority.
- **Security assurance:** A6 is core despite its rarity across the whole process. Erik's certification and high/critical exposure substantiate specialization; technical and KB work can support security resolution. During the incident spike, fewer A6 events could reflect effective screening, constrained access, or both. Event counts cannot distinguish those explanations or establish unused capacity.
- **Commercial work:** A7/A8 are core for Fatima and a substantial secondary capability for Giorgio. Of Giorgio's 72 A7 events, 31 occurred during the specified absence, leaving 41 outside it; 70 of his 88 negotiations also occurred outside it. This supports investigating an ongoing role but does not exclude other substitution episodes or ordinary customer liaison. Median pricing-exception duration rose from 38 to 74 minutes during cover, approximately 1.95 times the earlier median. That is an operational signal, not a controlled estimate of competence: case mix, queues, and support may differ. Fatima's 16 and Giorgio's 20 A9 events require separate authority checks.
- **Supervisory oversight:** A9 is Hana's core decision activity, reinforced by escalation and reopen involvement. Commercial and operational touches are peripheral. Mateo's workshop approvals were preliminary: 13 of 21, approximately 62%, were rechecked. Rechecking does not prove error; the other eight cannot be assumed final or exempt from review. Delegation limits and final decision records must settle the authority question.
- **Quality and knowledge work:** A11/A14 are core for Ivan and Julia. Configuration and implementation might mean test setup, correction, or delivery assistance. Their similar case complexity strengthens the common-role interpretation, while their different shifts affect coverage. Independence of checking must be assessed per case and person rather than inferred from a Quality title.
- **Incident coordination:** Mateo's core is connecting functions, particularly during escalations and reopen recovery. This is expressed through transitions and follow-through rather than a single dominant activity. His A9 and A6 participation has documented preliminary/screening meanings and does not establish authority equivalent to Hana or Erik. Commercial and technical touches plausibly represent liaison or triage; sustained substantive specialist execution could justify another capability after review.

Assess these distinctions within complexity, customer segment, product line, escalation level, shift, and time period. Report both event counts and distinct cases touched: repeated work on one case should not masquerade as broad experience. Cases handled by several resources represent shared exposure, so resource case counts must not be summed to estimate the number of process cases.

He connects diagnosis to safe implementation on difficult cases. Value depends on the risks assessed and decisions supported, not merely A6 volume. Concentration creates absence and queue risks, while screening can protect his time or conceal unmet demand. Identify qualified backup and inspect critical referrals that never reached him. **Quality analysts: delivery-to-learning bridge.** Ivan and Julia connect implementation, quality checks, and KB updates, potentially spreading lessons from defects. Their technical touches can accelerate correction. Verify appropriate independence of checks and whether KB updates improve subsequent work; neither independence nor knowledge quality follows from event frequency.

**Automation and temporary workforce: precheck-to-resolution boundary.** Nora feeds agents, including temps who close simple cases. This supports scale, but the reopened cases expose a possible weakness in completing and accepting entitlement evidence. Establish who owns final validation and which findings require escalation. # 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

This illustrative workflow requires the underlying event log; aggregate counts cannot reconstruct traces or networks. It uses canonical PM4Py columns. The result adapters assume role objects expose `activities` and `originator_importance`, and network objects expose `connections`; adapt these adapters if the installed version returns different structures.

```python
from pathlib import Path
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

C = "case:concept:name"
A = "concept:name"
T = "time:timestamp"
R = "org:resource"
S = "temporary_substitution"

# Do not expand aggregate counts into invented traces.
raw = pd.read_csv(Path("event_log.csv"), dtype={C: "string", A: "string", R: "string"})
required = [C, A, T, R, "case_complexity", S,
            "customer_segment", "product_line", "escalation_level"]
missing = set(required) - set(raw.columns)
if missing:
    raise ValueError(f"Missing columns: {sorted(missing)}")
if raw[[C, A, T, R]].isna().any().any():
    raise ValueError("Resolve missing case, activity, timestamp, or resource values")
raw[T] = pd.to_datetime(raw[T], utc=True, errors="raise")
raw["source_order"] = range(len(raw))

# Avoid bool("False") == True; preserve unknown substitution status.
flag_map = {"true": True, "1": True, "1.0": True, "yes": True,
            "false": False, "0": False, "0.0": False, "no": False}
labels = raw[S].astype("string").str.strip().str.lower()
if (labels.notna() & ~labels.isin(flag_map)).any():
    raise ValueError("Unrecognized substitution labels; consult the data dictionary")
raw[S] = labels.map(flag_map).astype("boolean")

log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    raw, case_id=C, activity_key=A, timestamp_key=T
)
log = log.sort_values([C, T, "source_order"], kind="stable").reset_index(drop=True)
log["substitution_state"] = (
    log[S].map({True: "substitution", False: "routine"}).fillna("unknown")
)

# Source order is reproducible, not proof of causal ordering at equal times.
# Resolve ties using authoritative sequence/lifecycle data for network analysis.
timestamp_ties = log.loc[log.duplicated([C, T], keep=False), [C, A, T, R]]
observed_activities = sorted(log[A].unique())  # Reconcile "Coordinate" and A2 semantics.

roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log)
activity_similarity = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log)
handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log)
working_together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log)


def edge_frame(network):
    return pd.DataFrame(
        [(u, v, weight) for (u, v), weight in network.connections.items()],
        columns=["source", "target", "weight"],
    )


similarity_edges = edge_frame(activity_similarity)
handover_edges = edge_frame(handover)
working_edges = edge_frame(working_together)

role_rows = []
for i, role in enumerate(roles):
    for resource, importance in role.originator_importance.items():
        role_rows.append({
            "candidate_role": f"activity_role_{i:02d}",
            R: resource,
            "candidate_activities": tuple(role.activities),
            "algorithm_importance": importance,
        })
membership = pd.DataFrame(role_rows, columns=[
    "candidate_role", R, "candidate_activities", "algorithm_importance"
])

# Separate event volume from distinct-case exposure.
event_profile = (
    log.groupby([R, "substitution_state"], dropna=False)
       .agg(events=(A, "size"), cases_touched=(C, "nunique"))
       .reset_index()
)

# Here complexity, segment, and product are fixed case attributes. If they
# evolve, define arrival-time/event-time context rather than taking an
# arbitrary or final value and attributing it to earlier decisions.
case_columns = ["case_complexity", "customer_segment", "product_line"]
for column in case_columns:
    if log.groupby(C)[column].nunique(dropna=False).gt(1).any():
        raise ValueError(f"Define time-aware case context for {column}")
case_context = log[[C] + case_columns].drop_duplicates(C)


def combine_states(states):
    if states.eq("substitution").any():
        return "substitution"
    if states.eq("unknown").any():
        return "unknown"
    return "routine"


# One row per resource-case. Substitution means at least one flagged event
# by that resource on the case, not that all its work on the case was cover.
resource_case = (
    log.groupby([R, C], dropna=False)["substitution_state"]
       .agg(combine_states).reset_index()
       .merge(case_context, on=C, how="left", validate="many_to_one")
)
resource_summary = (
    resource_case.groupby(R)
    .agg(cases_touched=(C, "nunique"),
         substitution_cases=("substitution_state", lambda x: x.eq("substitution").sum()),
         unknown_status_cases=("substitution_state", lambda x: x.eq("unknown").sum()))
    .reset_index()
)

# Join discovered membership with complexity and substitution evidence.
# Preserve overlapping membership, then deduplicate cases within each role.
role_case_members = membership.merge(resource_case, on=R, how="left")
role_case = (
    role_case_members.groupby(["candidate_role", C], dropna=False)
    .agg(case_complexity=("case_complexity", "first"),
         substitution_state=("substitution_state", combine_states))
    .reset_index()
)
role_exposure = (
    role_case.groupby(["candidate_role", "case_complexity", "substitution_state"],
                      dropna=False)
             .size().rename("distinct_cases").reset_index()
)

# Numeric scores permit medians. For category labels, report category shares
# using role_exposure; do not invent thresholds or ordinal distances.
score = pd.to_numeric(case_context["case_complexity"], errors="coerce")
is_numeric_score = (
    case_context["case_complexity"].notna().any()
    and score.notna().eq(case_context["case_complexity"].notna()).all()
)
if is_numeric_score:
    resource_case["complexity_score"] = pd.to_numeric(resource_case["case_complexity"])
    medians = (resource_case.groupby(R)["complexity_score"].median()
               .rename("median_case_complexity").reset_index())
    resource_summary = resource_summary.merge(medians, on=R, validate="one_to_one")
    role_case["complexity_score"] = pd.to_numeric(role_case["case_complexity"])
    role_complexity_medians = role_case.groupby("candidate_role")["complexity_score"].median()

role_resource_evidence = membership.merge(resource_summary, on=R, how="left")
role_event_evidence = membership.merge(event_profile, on=R, how="left")
context_activity = (
    log.groupby([R, A, "case_complexity", "customer_segment", "product_line",
                 "escalation_level", "substitution_state"], dropna=False)
       .size().rename("events").reset_index()
)

# Event filtering is suitable for routine activity-profile comparisons.
routine_events = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(log, S, [False], level="event")
routine_roles = []
routine_similarity = None
if not routine_events.empty:
    routine_roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(routine_events)
    if routine_events[R].nunique() > 1:
        routine_similarity = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(routine_events)

# For networks, retain complete cases with known routine status. Removing
# middle events could invent handovers that were never adjacent in the log.
routine_case_mask = log.groupby(C)["substitution_state"].agg(lambda x: x.eq("routine").all())
routine_case_ids = routine_case_mask.index[routine_case_mask]
routine_traces = log[log[C].isin(routine_case_ids)].copy()
routine_handover = None
routine_working = None
if not routine_traces.empty and routine_traces[R].nunique() > 1:
    routine_handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(routine_traces)
    routine_working = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(routine_traces)

focus = {"R07_Giorgio", "R13_Mateo", "R05_Erik"}
focus_handovers = handover_edges.loc[
    handover_edges["source"].isin(focus) | handover_edges["target"].isin(focus)
].sort_values("weight", ascending=False)

# Review alongside a separately verified authority/delegation register.
# Never derive approval rights from candidate membership or similarity.
print(role_resource_evidence)
print(role_exposure)
print(focus_handovers)
```

Interpret and extend the workflow as follows:

- **Inspect case distributions alongside similarities.** Compare Chen/Dina, Ivan/Julia, and Kai/Lina using distinct-case complexity, escalations, product, and segment. Activity resemblance is not interchangeable competence. PM4Py's similarity implementation need not reproduce the supplied cosine values exactly; document its metric and compute cosine separately if reproducing those values is required.
- **Interpret networks cautiously.** Examine front-office-to-technical-to-quality transitions, commercial-to-approval transitions, and Mateo's links to specialists and reopened cases. Working-together means shared case participation, not necessarily simultaneous collaboration. Handover means recorded sequence adjacency, not necessarily deliberate delegation or hierarchy. Check lifecycle semantics, timestamp ties, edge weighting, bot traffic, and long-case effects before interpreting centrality.
- **Stratify without inventing paths.** Select complete cases for fixed complexity, product, and segment cohorts. For time or escalation windows, retain true adjacent pairs from complete traces and label their context. Do not delete intermediate events and call the newly adjacent resources a real handover. Different shifts and complex cases change collaboration opportunities; inspect actual paths and compare suitable peers before interpreting Mateo's centrality.
- **Test temporal stability.** Map the stated weeks to dates and compare before/during/after the substitution and incident episodes with matched case mixes. Candidate IDs are arbitrary across discovery runs; align by activities and resource overlap. Test sensible discovery thresholds and resample whole cases to assess stability. Preserve rare security and critical-case evidence even when it produces a singleton. Routine-only cases are a selected baseline and may exclude the hardest work.
- **Separate behavior from permission.** Join formal title, team, certification, tenure, contract, and shift as descriptive attributes. Maintain a separate authority register with scope, decision limits, effective dates, supervision requirements, and final signatory. Algorithm importance is neither a competence score nor permission. Participation in a critical case does not prove independent resolution of it.
- **Use suitable timing and outcome data.** One event timestamp, without explicit start/completion data, cannot generally separate service effort from queueing. Use `pm4py.get_service_time` or `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` only with suitable interval/lifecycle data and checked semantics. Otherwise report defined elapsed intervals rather than labor effort. Investigate reopen causes, approval reversals, and referral waits at case level; aggregate changes do not establish causation.

# 6. Operational interpretation

Management should maintain a role-and-capability map showing primary ownership, demonstrated secondary work, approved delegation limits, and coverage arrangements. The proposed structure preserves routine specialization while making commercial cover, coordination, and specialist dependencies visible. Review it with practitioners and accountable managers before changing responsibilities.

**Staffing:** Plan capacity from demand, case difficulty, actual effort, arrival patterns, and shift coverage. Put Giorgio's front-office and commercial work in one capacity budget, including displaced work during Fatima's absence. Protect coordination and security capacity and assess whether late-shift technical/quality staff can obtain timely decisions from day-shift approvers and specialists. Other people's A6 events warrant a scope discussion, not an automatic reduction in Erik's coverage.

**Training:** Use the technical family for mentoring and gradual exposure to harder cases, with Chen as a possible mentor subject to capacity and performance review. Support Giorgio with exception playbooks and supervised commercial development where needed. Improve the temps' entitlement-note prompts, training, and closure criteria, then compare outcomes on similar simple cases. Investigate whether workflow design or the automated handoff made complete notes difficult, rather than attributing all 17 reopens to individual competence.

**Risk control:** Record preliminary assessment, recommendation, and final approval separately. Assign ownership of pending rechecks and document delegation limits. Monitor critical referrals both before and after specialist acceptance, define urgency criteria, and provide direct specialist access when urgent review is warranted. Examine the two delayed critical cases individually. Check who implements and quality-checks each solution, and provide independent review where required by the process.

**Resilience:** Recognize shadow work as useful capacity. Convert recurring successful cover into supported arrangements with training, decision limits, handover material, and tested absence coverage. Develop qualified backup for Erik and explicit substitutes for Mateo's routing work. A backup needs appropriate information, system access, and authority, as well as a similar activity history.

Track case-mix-adjusted reopen rates, entitlement documentation completeness, first-pass quality, exception turnaround, critical referral waiting time, approval recheck/backlog measures, and absence coverage. Distinguish corrections from routine rechecks, and evaluate screening quality alongside the reduction in referrals. Report denominators and uncertainty and account for workload and routing changes when evaluating improvements.

Three cautions are essential:

1. **Do not consolidate staff simply because profiles resemble one another.** Chen and Dina have different risk exposure; experienced agents and temps have different demonstrated scope; shifts constrain substitutability. Combining apparent duplicates can remove advanced capability, availability, or necessary independent review.
2. **Do not cut rare roles because event counts are low.** Security counts omit the severity of prevented harm, effort, filtering, and standby needs. Low observed work may reflect an access bottleneck or unserved demand.
3. **Do not treat temporary cover as proven permanent capacity.** Giorgio's longer cover-period reviews and Mateo's preliminary approvals/rechecks show why capacity estimates must include support, follow-up, and displaced duties. Ten weeks of observational evidence cannot establish sustainable staffing levels or unrestricted competence.
