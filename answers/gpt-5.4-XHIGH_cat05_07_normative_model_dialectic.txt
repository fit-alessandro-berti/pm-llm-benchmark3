## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

**Supported by the record**
- CS-9 is the highest-formal baseline in the record and has been effective since 2025-01-01. For supplier onboarding it requires `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` before activation, and `Manual_Procurement_Approval` before activation. It also requires requester and manual approver to be different humans.
- M0 captures only part of that baseline. It models the standard control-flow skeleton, but omits the requester/approver separation rule, any conflict-of-interest rule, any `Request_More_Info` loop, any auto-approval route, any emergency/provisional route, any reactivation path, and any explicit scope carve-out for rejected leads.
- PD-3 existed and was used from 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31. It contemplated `Auto_Approve` for low-risk DE suppliers already in an approved corporate group, with sanctions and automated master-data validation before activation and `Quality_Review` within two business days after provisional activation.
- FD-7 existed and was effective only from 2026-07-10 00:00 through 2026-07-12 23:59. It expressly allowed provisional activation before quality review and manual procurement approval, but still required sanctions before activation, capped spend at EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval, and required daily monitoring plus quality review within two business days.
- Notification capture degraded after the 2026-07-01 CRM migration: missingness rose from 2% to 29%, and some linkages rely on mutable text fields rather than stable immutable joins.
- `Request_More_Info` behavior is real and common: 47 cases show it, and none activated before requested evidence arrived.
- `Auto_Approve` was not a rare anomaly. In June and July, 312 cases used it; 289 appear PD-3-eligible, 14 are clearly ineligible, and 9 lack the attributes needed to prove eligibility. Six more `Auto_Approve` cases occurred in the first week of August after PD-3 expired on 2026-07-31.
- Internal Audit identified SUP-703 and SUP-708 as potentially severe.

**Disputed or authority-dependent propositions**
- Whether PD-3 was a valid exception overlay that could lawfully displace CS-9 for eligible cases, or merely evidence that management tolerated departures from CS-9 without formally amending it.
- Whether rejected leads like SUP-709 and reactivations like SUP-710 belong inside the same scope as M0, or instead belong to separate intake/reactivation processes.
- Whether some missing `Notify_Requester` events reflect true nonperformance or only incomplete collection and weak cross-system linkage.

**Unproved propositions on this record**
- That the factual continuation of flood conditions after 2026-07-12 created an unwritten extension of FD-7.
- That permanent low-risk auto-approval had been approved, or was effective, by 2026-08-02.
- That every auto-approved case missing eligibility attributes was in fact eligible.

**Burden of proof**
- A `NONCOMPLIANT` finding should require all three elements: an applicable controlled rule or valid overlay, reliable case-linked evidence of the event/order/identity facts, and no proven exception that covers the case.
- A `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` finding should require a stronger showing, and the burden should rest on operations: explicit authority, exact effective dates, scope or eligibility facts, and immutable case-linked evidence that every overlay condition was met.
- Where the main uncertainty is event capture, case linkage, or label taxonomy, the correct default is `DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE` or `MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT`, not a substantive noncompliance finding.
- Severe allegations such as sanctions-after-activation or approval bypass should rest on immutable timestamps, stable IDs, or corroborated records, not on inference from a single imperfect log.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The strongest prosecution case begins with authority. CS-9 is controlled, effective, and never superseded in the record by a formally ratified permanent amendment. M0 may be incomplete, but it faithfully expresses CS-9's core gating logic: sanctions and quality before activation, and manual approval before activation. Desirable business outcomes, operator convenience, or repetition in practice do not amend that baseline.

PD-3 is weak as authority to displace CS-9. It appears in steering-committee minutes and follow-up email, was never incorporated into CS-9 or M0, and the Compliance observer later stated that she had not approved weakening CS-9. On that reading, `Auto_Approve` is at most a tolerated monitored exception, not a new rule of general application. That matters because the burden then stays on operations to prove a valid, case-linked exception rather than on reviewers to infer one from volume of use.

Expiry also matters. FD-7 expired automatically at 23:59 on 2026-07-12. PD-3 expired on 2026-07-31. SUP-705 and SUP-714 therefore are not merely stale-model false positives; they are exception behavior continued after the written window closed. The six post-pilot `Auto_Approve` cases in the first week of August show normalization of an exception into ordinary practice without ratification. That is governance failure, not just model drift.

Some cases are materially serious regardless of any model debate. SUP-703 used `Auto_Approve` for a high-risk new foreign supplier that was plainly ineligible, then activated before `Quality_Review` and without `Manual_Procurement_Approval`, with EUR 180,000 of early spend. SUP-708 activated a high-risk supplier 40 minutes before sanctions clearance and before manual approval. Those are not artifacts of missing loops or label mismatches. They are direct failures of core gating controls, and Internal Audit was justified in treating them as potentially severe.

The aggregate evidence reinforces that this was not just isolated operator confusion. Among the 312 June-July `Auto_Approve` cases, 14 are clearly ineligible and 9 cannot even prove eligibility. A sound exception regime should not produce that amount of known misuse and undocumented use. Even if the majority were business-sensible, the control design did not stop unauthorized deployment of the route.

The prosecution also gains force from risks that M0 does not detect. SUP-712 looks control-flow conformant but violates CS-9's same-person rule once aliases are resolved. SUP-715 fits M0 yet shows an undisclosed conflict-of-interest issue under the broader policy framework. So the real compliance picture may be worse than M0 suggests, not better.

A fair prosecution must still concede genuine defects. SUP-707 is better explained by a missing loop in M0 than by misconduct. SUP-709 and SUP-710 suffer from scope ambiguity. SUP-711 and SUP-713 are classic data or taxonomy defects. Notification evidence is genuinely degraded after the CRM move. But those concessions narrow the accusation; they do not erase the real noncompliances in SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714, and SUP-715. Even SUP-702 and SUP-704, while much better defended, still depend on contested overlay authority rather than straightforward CS-9 compliance.

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest defense is not that every deviation was acceptable. It is that M0 is too stale, too narrow, and too data-naive to act as the sole normative benchmark in mid-2026.

First, M0 omits known live overlays. PD-3 and FD-7 were real operational instructions that staff followed openly rather than covertly. FD-7 in particular reads as a bona fide emergency directive, and SUP-704 fits it closely: it occurred on 2026-07-11 within the directive window, sanctions preceded activation, initial spend stayed below EUR 20,000, daily monitoring exists, and quality review plus approval happened the next business day. Labeling that case `NONCOMPLIANT` merely because M0 was not updated would punish the wrong artifact.

Second, the June-July `Auto_Approve` volume shows that PD-3 was not a one-off rogue bypass but an operating mode. Of 312 such cases, 289 appear eligible on the business facts. That does not prove impeccable governance, but it strongly suggests that a large fraction of M0 deviations are really evidence that the normative model failed to encode a live monitored exception regime. SUP-702 is the cleanest example: low-risk, DE, approved corporate group, within the 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31 window, pilot eligibility fields complete, and notification provable through a stable `notification_id`.

Third, M0 is structurally incomplete. It cannot represent `Request_More_Info` loops that both operations and compliance regard as desirable. SUP-707 therefore looks deviant only because the model has no place for safe evidence-gathering iterations. The aggregate pattern helps the defense: 47 such cases occurred and none activated before requested evidence arrived. That pattern looks more like prudent control execution than circumvention.

Fourth, several deviations are plainly scope or taxonomy defects rather than business misconduct. SUP-709 turns on whether rejected pre-screen leads are even in the onboarding process governed by M0; the scope text is ambiguous. SUP-710 is a reactivation with a previously valid quality certification; forcing it into the new-supplier skeleton may be a category mistake. SUP-713 is a renamed activity with equivalent audit content and a one-week lag in formal mapping. SUP-711 is a duplicated register event with the same source transaction ID, which is a data-cleaning problem, not a second onboarding.

Fifth, notification evidence is no longer fully observable in the onboarding log. After the 2026-07-01 CRM migration, missingness jumped to 29%, and some links rely on mutable text fields. A missing onboarding-log event therefore stopped being a reliable proxy for no notification. SUP-706 should not be treated as proven noncompliance on the present record. More broadly, once notification moved into another system, a static POWL over the onboarding log became an inadequate end-to-end test.

Finally, the defense can correctly distinguish trace deviation from business misconduct. A stale label map or missing mirror event is not equivalent to activating a high-risk supplier before sanctions. The review method should classify those phenomena differently.

A fair defense must also concede hard limits. Informal practice does not permanently amend a controlled standard. PD-3's authority is weaker than a formal policy revision, and it expired on 2026-07-31. FD-7 expired on 2026-07-12 with no written extension. That leaves SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, and SUP-714 exposed even on the defense's best reading, and it does not excuse SUP-712 or SUP-715 because those are non-control-flow governance failures rather than model-shape defects.

**System-level judgment on operational compliance**

The operation is materially noncompliant, but not because every M0 deviation is a true breach. The strongest system-level evidence of real operational noncompliance is the existence of a bounded but important class of unjustified control failures: SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714, and SUP-715. Those cases involve either ineligible use of an exception route, use of a route after expiry, sanctions or approval bypass, separation-of-duties failure, or undisclosed conflict risk. The aggregate `Auto_Approve` data strengthens that judgment because 14 cases are clearly ineligible and 9 are undocumented, which shows that exception governance was materially porous.

**System-level judgment on model validity**

M0 is not valid as the sole conformance benchmark for this environment. It overstates noncompliance in justified or benign cases because it omits live overlays, loops, scope distinctions, taxonomy mapping, and cross-system evidence realities. The right conclusion is therefore dual: CS-9 remains the controlling baseline, but M0 should be retired as a standalone pass-fail instrument and replaced by a layered architecture.

**Where I reject each side**

I reject the prosecution to the extent it treats all M0 deviations as straightforward noncompliance. That overreaches on SUP-702, SUP-704, SUP-707, SUP-709, SUP-710, SUP-711, and SUP-713, and it ignores the proven data and scope defects.

I reject the defense to the extent it treats repeated practice, business desirability, or management expectation as self-ratifying authority. That fails for SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714, and SUP-715, and it fails systemically because the 14 clearly ineligible plus 9 undocumented `Auto_Approve` cases show real control erosion, not merely stale modeling.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The correct target is a layered conformance architecture rather than a single static POWL.

- `POWL control-flow core`: model the route skeleton only. Start with `Register_Supplier`, then branch by route into a standard manual-approval path, a low-risk auto-approval path, an emergency/provisional path, or a rejection path. Keep `Notify_Requester` before `Close_Case` for all completed routes. Represent `Request_More_Info` as a repeatable pre-review mechanism on the standard route. Do not try to encode every eligibility or authority nuance in pure POWL.
- `DECLARE` or log-skeleton constraints: use declarative rules for route-independent obligations. Examples: `Sanctions_Check` must precede any `Activate_Supplier`; `Activate_Supplier` must never occur after `Reject_Supplier`; `Notify_Requester` must precede `Close_Case`; `Manual_Procurement_Approval` must precede activation unless an active overlay explicitly permits otherwise; `Quality_Review` must precede activation unless an active overlay allows post-activation completion within a deadline.
- `pandas` or data-aware rules: enforce what POWL cannot. This includes PD-3 eligibility (`risk=LOW`, `country=DE`, `approved_group=True`, date within 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31), FD-7 effective window and critical-supplier scope, EUR 20,000 spend cap until retrospective approval, next-business-day or two-business-day deadlines, notification linkage through stable `notification_id`, reactivation certification validity, and whether a reject case is `intake_reject` rather than accepted onboarding.
- Resource and conflict controls: keep requester/approver separation, alias resolution, delegation validity, reviewer-approver independence, and conflict disclosure in data checks plus organizational controls, not only in the flow model. SUP-712 and SUP-715 show why control-flow conformance alone is insufficient.
- Policy-overlay governance: every non-base route should have an overlay record with `overlay_id`, `authority_doc_id`, `rule_version_id`, exact effective timestamps, scope predicate, mandatory controls, approver identity, publication timestamp, and auto-expiry behavior. Minutes or email may document history, but they should not silently supersede a controlled standard without a controlled exception ID or waiver registry.
- Scope rules: define explicit `case_type` values such as `new_onboarding`, `reactivation`, `intake_reject`, and `withdrawal`. Rejected leads may end after sanctions pre-screen if they are truly `intake_reject`. Reactivations may reuse prior quality only if the previous certification is still valid and no material-change rule is triggered. Withdrawals should be a separate terminal path rather than being misread as rejects or incomplete onboardings.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

The following POWL is an executable control-flow approximation. It intentionally keeps amount, date, eligibility, identity, and authority logic out of the pure model and pushes those checks into overlays and data rules. The `Request_More_Info` mechanism is approximated as a silent-seeded loop allowing zero or more information-request cycles before the final `Quality_Review`.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")

# Standard manual route: sanctions runs in parallel with the quality track.
std_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
request_more_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
quality_review = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
tau = SilentTransition()

# Approximation: zero or more Request_More_Info events before the final Quality_Review.
# POWL alone cannot bind each request to a separate evidence-arrival record.
request_loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[tau, request_more_info])
quality_track = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[request_loop, quality_review])
quality_track.order.add_edge(request_loop, quality_review)

standard_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[std_sanctions, quality_track])
manual_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
standard_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[standard_checks, manual_approval, standard_activate])
standard_route.order.add_edge(standard_checks, manual_approval)
standard_route.order.add_edge(manual_approval, standard_activate)

# Low-risk auto-approval route.
auto_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
master_data_validation = Transition(label="Master_Data_Validation")
auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
auto_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
auto_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")

auto_pre = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[auto_sanctions, master_data_validation])
auto_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[auto_pre, auto_approve, auto_activate, auto_quality])
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_pre, auto_approve)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_approve, auto_activate)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_activate, auto_quality)

# Emergency/provisional route.
em_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
em_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
em_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
em_manual = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")

emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[em_sanctions, em_activate, em_quality, em_manual])
emergency_route.order.add_edge(em_sanctions, em_activate)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(em_activate, em_quality)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(em_activate, em_manual)

# Abbreviated rejection route for intake/pre-screen failures.
rej_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
reject_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[rej_sanctions, reject])
reject_route.order.add_edge(rej_sanctions, reject)

route_choice = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[standard_route, auto_route, emergency_route, reject_route]
)

notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, route_choice, notify, close])
M1.order.add_edge(register, route_choice)
M1.order.add_edge(route_choice, notify)
M1.order.add_edge(notify, close)
```

This POWL is deliberately not asked to encode reactivation and withdrawal inside the same skeleton. That is safer. Those should be explicit `case_type` overlays with dedicated routing logic and data conditions, rather than hidden as ad hoc omissions of `Register_Supplier` or `Quality_Review`.

**Event and data-collection proposal**

- `event_log` should contain at least: `case_id`, `case_type`, `activity`, `raw_activity_label`, `canonical_activity_label`, `event_time_utc`, `recorded_at_utc`, `source_system`, `source_event_id`, `source_record_hash`, `rule_version_id`, `overlay_id`, `event_status`, `notification_id`, `actor_person_id`, `actor_alias_id`, `actor_role`, and `delegation_id`. Both `event_time_utc` and `recorded_at_utc` should be immutable and retained separately.
- `case_snapshot` should freeze decision-relevant attributes at the time of routing: `supplier_id`, `supplier_group_id`, `country_code`, `risk_rating`, `approved_group_flag`, `critical_relief_flag`, `initial_spend_eur`, `spend_cap_eur`, `pilot_eligibility_snapshot_json`, `reactivation_flag`, `prior_quality_cert_id`, `prior_quality_valid_to_utc`, and `material_change_flag`. The point is to judge a case by the facts known when the route was chosen, not by later edited master data.
- `overlay_registry` should contain: `overlay_id`, `rule_version_id`, `authority_doc_id`, `authority_type`, `approved_by_person_id`, `published_at_utc`, `effective_from_utc`, `effective_to_utc`, `revoked_at_utc`, `recorded_at_utc`, `scope_predicate_json`, `mandatory_controls_json`, and `system_kill_switch_id`. Expiry should be machine-enforced, not dependent on staff memory.
- `identity_alias_graph` should contain: `person_id`, `alias_id`, `alias_source`, `confidence_score`, `valid_from_utc`, and `valid_to_utc`, so that same-person requester/approver checks do not depend on manual detective work after the fact.
- `delegation_and_coi` should contain: `delegation_id`, `delegator_person_id`, `delegate_person_id`, `delegation_scope`, `delegation_effective_from_utc`, `delegation_effective_to_utc`, `coi_disclosure_id`, `relationship_flag`, `reporting_line_flag`, `disclosed_at_utc`, `mitigation_decision_id`, and `mitigation_approved_by_person_id`.
- `notification_evidence` should be a first-class table rather than a mutable text note: `notification_id`, `case_id`, `channel`, `sent_at_utc`, `recorded_at_utc`, `source_message_id`, `delivery_status`, and `immutable_payload_hash`. The onboarding log should reference this table by stable `notification_id`; free-text joins should be retired.
- `label_mapping` should contain: `raw_activity_label`, `canonical_activity_label`, `mapping_version_id`, `approved_at_utc`, `approved_by_person_id`, and `equivalence_rationale`. That prevents cases like SUP-713 from turning a naming lag into a fake process deviation.
- A companion compliance-check dataset should materialize route verdict inputs per case: `applicable_rule_version_id`, `applicable_overlay_id`, `eligibility_pass_flag`, `effective_window_pass_flag`, `sod_pass_flag`, `coi_disclosure_pass_flag`, `notification_evidence_pass_flag`, `post_activation_quality_deadline_utc`, and `retrospective_approval_deadline_utc`.

With that architecture, POWL handles the visible route skeleton, declarative rules handle route-independent precedence, data checks handle time/eligibility/identity/amount logic, and organizational controls govern who may authorize overlays and how those overlays expire. That is the most defensible way to preserve CS-9's core intent while stopping M0 from misclassifying justified exceptions and benign data defects.
