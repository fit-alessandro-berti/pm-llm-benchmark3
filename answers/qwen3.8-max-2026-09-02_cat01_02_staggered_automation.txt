def sparkline(series):
    s = series.astype(float).values
    if np.all(np.isnan(s)): return ''
    s = s[~np.isnan(s)]
    if s.size == 0: return ''
    mn, mx = s.min(), s.max()
    if np.isclose(mx, mn): return '' * s.size
    chars = ''
    idx = np.round((s - mn) / (mx - mn) * (len(chars)-1)).astype(int)
    return ''.join(chars[i] for i in idx)

def did_table(treated, controls, pre_start, pre_end, post_start, post_end, label):
    t_pre = window_mean(treated, pre_start, pre_end)
    t_post = window_mean(treated, post_start, post_end)

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary: comparative interrupted time series with matched never-treated controls | Branch-specific adoption month; separate level and slope change; relative-time categories; estimate Alpha/Beta and Gamma separately | Delta and Epsilon never-treated; sensitivity using Gamma pre-July as not-yet control for Alpha/Beta | `arrivals`, `fte`, `high_complexity_pct`, routing-campaign indicator if available, fraud-desk period indicator, month effects, branch baseline trends | Branch-specific ITT-like availability effects on median, p90, rework, escalation, CSAT | Conditional parallel trends: after adjusting for observed shocks, adopter and control trends would have continued similarly; stable measurement; no unadjusted spillovers; no anticipation | Pre-trend plots and placebo adoption dates; covariate balance; leave-one-branch-out; residual autocorrelation checks; sensitivity bounds | Controls experience different May complexity/routing/fraud-desk shocks, so relative change is not the counterfactual. |
| Alternative 1: synthetic control / augmented synthetic control | Post-adoption period compared with weighted pre-adoption trajectory of donor branches | Weighted combination of Delta/Epsilon, with pre-July Gamma usable only as donor for Alpha/Beta if not contaminated | Pre-period outcomes, arrivals, FTE, high complexity, escalation, rework, routing indicators | Branch-specific average post-adoption ITT effect | A convex combination of donors reproduces the treated branch’s counterfactual; no interference; stable measurement; sufficient pre-period | Pre-period RMSPE; in-time and in-space placebo tests; leave-one-donor-out; check donor weights | Only two never-treated controls and a common May shock make synthetic counterfactuals fragile or poorly fitted. |
| Alternative 2: heterogeneity-robust staggered DiD/event study | Cohort-specific relative time: April cohort and July cohort; group-time ATTs; avoid naïve TWFE | Primarily never-treated Delta/Epsilon; not-yet-treated Gamma only with anticipation sensitivity | Branch and calendar-time fixed effects; time-varying covariates; possibly propensity weighting for observed shocks | Dynamic ATT by event time, heterogeneous by branch/cohort | Conditional parallel trends; no anticipation or correctly modeled anticipation; no interference; estimator handles heterogeneous timing | Event-study lead coefficients; placebo adoption dates; compare Callaway-Sant’Anna / Sun-Abraham / de Chaisemartin-D’Haultfoeuille estimates; cluster wild bootstrap caution | Very small number of branches/clusters and dynamic heterogeneous effects make estimates unstable; spillovers bias ATTs. |

The primary comparative interrupted time-series strategy is preferable because the data contain only five branches, three treated units, two controls, and eight months, making large-sample staggered DiD estimators unstable. CITS uses each branch as its own baseline while explicitly comparing level and slope changes to never-treated branches, and it can condition on observed time-varying shocks (arrivals, FTE, high-complexity share). It also allows branch-specific event windows, which is important because Alpha/Beta and Gamma adopted at different times and under different case-mix conditions. The approach does not pretend that a single common effect exists; it reports branch-specific relative changes and a cautious pooled range. Its main limitation is that parallel trends are not guaranteed, especially because controls also experienced May complexity shocks and July recovery. Therefore, the design should be used with pre-trend checks, placebo windows, and sensitivity bounds rather than as proof. Synthetic controls and heterogeneity-robust event studies are useful alternatives but are fragile with only two controls and short panels.

A single leadership statement such as “automation reduces cycle time by X%” is not defensible from these data. The defensible quantitative statements are branch-specific, window-specific, relative to a counterfactual, and uncertain.

### 5.3 Treatment-intensity analysis

Among treated branch-months, compare months with low vs high actual fast-path use:

| intensity group | months | mean `automated_fast_path_pct` | mean high complexity % | mean arrivals | mean median h | mean p90 h | mean rework % | mean escalation % | mean CSAT |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| low: <35% | 5 | 28.6 | 27.8 | 1,131 | 46.5 | 99.8 | 12.2 | 10.8 | 3.94 |
| high: 35% | 6 | 45.3 | 32.0 | 1,215 | 47.9 | 107.0 | 13.6 | 11.2 | 3.91 |

Raw high-intensity months do not show better outcomes; they are later, have higher complexity, and include Alpha’s high-pressure period. A fixed-effect intensity regression can be estimated descriptively, but the dose is not exogenous: easier cases may be selected into the automated fast path, while harder cases may be escalated. Therefore, the intensity analysis cannot be interpreted as a causal dose-response without case-level triage scores and queue data.

### 5.4 Pre-trends and regression to the mean

Pre-trends are short but important. Alpha and Beta had higher baseline cycle times than Gamma/Delta and were selected because of historically weak performance. That creates regression-to-the-mean risk: some later improvement could occur even without automation. However, Jan–Mar pre-period medians were relatively stable, not sharply elevated immediately before rollout. Gamma’s apparent improvement depends heavily on the pre-window: compared with Jan–Mar, Gamma’s post-adoption median is almost unchanged and p90 is worse; compared with the troubled Apr–Jun window, Gamma looks strongly improved. That pattern is consistent with recovery from a May/June complexity and staffing shock, not necessarily automation. Controls also worsened in May–June and partially recovered in July–August, so any adopter recovery must be compared with the same calendar recovery.

### 5.5 Bounded conclusions

For at least two outcomes:

| outcome | arithmetic anchor | plausible lower scenario | plausible upper scenario | bounded conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `median_cycle_h` | Descriptive DID: about 4h relative to controls | No true automation benefit; controls happened to worsen/recover differently: 0h relative effect | Automation helped more than the DID suggests: 6h relative effect | Median likely did not worsen because of automation and may have improved relative to no automation, but a precise percent reduction is not identified. |
| `p90_cycle_h` | Absolute p90 rose ~+8 to +10h for Alpha/Beta; relative DID about 6 to 7h | Automation did not reduce tail; common shocks produced the relative gap: 0h relative effect, absolute tail still worse | Automation attenuated tail deterioration by ~10h relative to counterfactual | Tail cycle times worsened in all branches. Automation may have attenuated tail worsening, but this is not proven and could be confounded. |

## 6. Threats, tests, and decision consequences

| threat | why it matters here | direction of likely bias | test or additional evidence | consequence if threat is severe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Selection into adoption | Alpha/Beta were chosen for poor historical cycle times; Gamma after readiness pilot | Likely overstates benefit if weak branches regress or receive extra attention | Historical baselines, pre-trends, synthetic controls, placebo branches | False positive conclusion that automation improves performance. |
| Nonparallel trends | Controls worsened in May and recovered in July; adopters did too | Ambiguous; could overstate or understate automation effect | Pre-trend checks, placebo adoption dates, branch-specific slopes | DID/CITS estimates do not approximate causal effects. |

## 8. Rollout recommendation

`expand with an embedded evaluation`

Do not roll out to both Delta and Epsilon as a blanket operational change. Start with Delta first, because it has the most stable staffing, lower baseline complexity, and no major adverse staffing shock in the observed period; keep Epsilon as a concurrent control for at least one quarter. Use a stepped-wedge or randomized start-date design: Delta receives automation in month 1 of the evaluation, Epsilon receives it in month 4 if guardrails are met. If business pressure requires both branches to begin quickly, randomize the exact start date, pilot scope, or triage threshold configuration, and ensure at least one branch remains unexposed for comparison for 8–12 weeks.

Collect case-level event logs, not branch-month aggregates only. Required fields: case ID, branch, registration timestamp, triage mode and decision, fraud-desk referral and wait timestamps, specialist queue entry/exit, rework loops, resolution/close timestamps, complexity class, routing-campaign flag, customer identifier hash for CSAT linkage, and survey response indicator. Record monthly FTE, arrivals, and system uptime. Monitor spillovers by measuring fraud-desk waiting time, specialist queue length, and cross-branch routing volumes for both treated and control branches.

Guardrails: pause or reduce automated fast-path eligibility if p90 cycle time rises more than 10% above synthetic-control forecast for two consecutive months, rework rises more than 2 percentage points, escalation creates specialist backlog exceeding baseline, or CSAT response-adjusted mean falls more than 0.15 with stable response composition. Track response rates and compare survey respondents to nonrespondents on case attributes.

Decision rule: expand to Epsilon and broader rollout only if, after at least 3–4 post-adoption months, the embedded evaluation shows a credible median cycle-time improvement of at least 5% or 2 hours relative to the counterfactual, no more than 5% p90 worsening, rework not materially higher, escalation changes understood, and CSAT stable or improved. If tail performance worsens, redesign triage thresholds and specialist capacity before scaling.

    s = series.astype(float).values
    s = s[~np.isnan(s)]
    if s.size == 0:
        return ""
    mn, mx = s.min(), s.max()
    if np.isclose(mx, mn):
        return "" * s.size
    chars = ""
    idx = np.round((s - mn) / (mx - mn) * (len(chars) - 1)).astype(int)
    return "".join(chars[i] for i in idx)

for k in [-3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2]:
    df[f"rel_{k}"] = (df["rel_time"] == k).astype(int)
df["rel_pre_3plus"] = (df["rel_time"] <= -3).astype(int)
df["rel_post_3plus"] = (df["rel_time"] >= 3).astype(int)

rel_bins = [-np.inf, -2.5, -1.5, -0.5, 0.5, 1.5, 2.5, np.inf]
rel_labels = ["<=-3", "-2", "-1", "0", "1", "2", ">=3"]
df["rel_cat"] = pd.cut(df["rel_time"], bins=rel_bins, labels=rel_labels)

print("\nRelative-time means for treated branch-months only")
print(
    df.dropna(subset=["rel_time"])
      .groupby("rel_cat", observed=True)[outcomes]
      .mean()
      .round(2)
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Transparent adopter-specific pre/post contrasts
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
windows = {
    "Alpha": {"pre": ("2026-01", "2026-03"), "post": ("2026-04", "2026-08")},
    "Beta": {"pre": ("2026-01", "2026-03"), "post": ("2026-04", "2026-08")},
    "Gamma": {"pre": ("2026-04", "2026-06"), "post": ("2026-07", "2026-08")},
}
metric_cols = outcomes + ["automated_fast_path_pct", "arrivals", "fte", "high_complexity_pct"]

rows = []
for branch, w in windows.items():
    pre_start, pre_end = w["pre"]
    post_start, post_end = w["post"]

    pre = df[
        (df["branch"] == branch)
        & (df["month"] >= pd.Period(pre_start, "M"))
        & (df["month"] <= pd.Period(pre_end, "M"))
    ]
    post = df[
        (df["branch"] == branch)
        & (df["month"] >= pd.Period(post_start, "M"))
        & (df["month"] <= pd.Period(post_end, "M"))
    ]

    row = {
        "branch": branch,
        "pre_window": f"{pre_start} to {pre_end}",
        "post_window": f"{post_start} to {post_end}",
    }
    for col in metric_cols:
        row[f"{col}_pre"] = pre[col].mean()
        row[f"{col}_post"] = post[col].mean()
        row[f"delta_{col}"] = post[col].mean() - pre[col].mean()
    rows.append(row)

prepost = pd.DataFrame(rows)
print_cols = ["branch", "pre_window", "post_window"] + [f"delta_{c}" for c in metric_cols]
print("\nAdopter-specific pre/post contrasts")
print(prepost[print_cols].round(2).to_string(index=False))

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Comparisons against never-treated branches
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
def window_mean(branch_list, start, end):
    mask = (
        df["branch"].isin(branch_list)
        & (df["month"] >= pd.Period(start, "M"))
        & (df["month"] <= pd.Period(end, "M"))
    )
    return df.loc[mask, outcomes].mean()

def did_table(treated_branches, control_branches, pre_start, pre_end, post_start, post_end, label):
    t_pre = window_mean(treated_branches, pre_start, pre_end)
    t_post = window_mean(treated_branches, post_start, post_end)
    c_pre = window_mean(control_branches, pre_start, pre_end)
    c_post = window_mean(control_branches, post_start, post_end)

    t_change = t_post - t_pre
    c_change = c_post - c_pre
    did = t_change - c_change

    out = pd.DataFrame({
        "treated_change": t_change,
        "control_change": c_change,
        "descriptive_did": did,
    })
    print(f"\n{label}")
    print(out.round(2))

did_table(
    ["Alpha", "Beta"], ["Delta", "Epsilon"],
    "2026-01", "2026-03", "2026-04", "2026-08",
    "Alpha/Beta vs never-treated, Jan-Mar to Apr-Aug"
)

did_table(

    def fit_and_print(formula, label, keep_prefixes):
        # ASSUMPTION: This model conditions on observed branch/month/covariates.
        # ASSUMPTION: It does NOT prove causality because rollout was endogenous,
        #             spillovers exist, and treatment intensity is selected.
        res = smf.ols(formula, data=data).fit(
            cov_type="cluster",
            cov_kwds={"groups": data["branch_code"]}
        )
        keep = [
            ix for ix in res.params.index
            if any(ix.startswith(p) for p in keep_prefixes)
        ]
        out = pd.DataFrame({
            "coef": res.params[keep],
            "cluster_se": res.bse[keep],
            "t": res.tvalues[keep],
            "p": res.pvalues[keep],
        })
        print(f"\n{label}")
        print(out.round(3))
        return res

    # Descriptive panel association: binary availability flag.
    # CAUSAL ASSUMPTION WOULD BE: conditional on covariates and fixed effects,
    # automation assignment is as-if random. That assumption is NOT credible here.
    for y in ["median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h"]:
        formula = (

            f"+ C(branch) + C(month_num)"

        fit_and_print(

            ["automation", "high_complexity_pct", "arrivals_100", "fte"]

    # Treatment-intensity association: actual fast-path percentage.
    # CAUSAL ASSUMPTION WOULD BE: fast-path percentage is exogenous conditional
    # on covariates. That assumption is NOT credible because cases are selected
    # into fast path by triage rules and unobserved complexity.
    for y in ["median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h"]:
        formula = (

## 10. Final claim ladder

- `Observed`: Automation availability coincided with a large increase in automated fast-path use, higher escalation percentages, flat/slightly worse medians in Alpha/Beta, and absolute p90 increases in all early adopters.
- `Suggestive`: Compared with never-treated branches, adopters showed less median deterioration and less p90 deterioration, and Gamma improved relative to its immediate troubled pre-adoption window.
- `Conditionally causal`: If parallel trends held after adjusting for routing, staffing, complexity, fraud-desk spillovers, and survey selection, automation availability likely reduced median cycle-time deterioration by a few hours, but the tail effect remains uncertain.
- `Not supported`: A single universal claim that “automation reduces cycle time by X%” or an immediate full rollout based only on the current branch-month evidence.
