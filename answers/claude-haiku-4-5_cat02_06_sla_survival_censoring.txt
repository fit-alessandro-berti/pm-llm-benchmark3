# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

## Time definitions
- **Time origin:** `Submit` event (case creation)
- **Time horizon:** 72 hours from Submit
- **Administrative censoring (right-censoring):** observation cutoff. For `open` cases, `duration_or_age_h` is age at cutoff; these cases have not yet reached a terminal state.
- **Terminal events:** `Resolve`, `Reject`, `Withdraw`, `Transfer`
- **Competing events:** `Reject`, `Withdraw`, and `Transfer` are competing with `Resolve` under most operational lenses. A case that reaches any terminal state cannot reach another.

## Estimand definitions

### Estimand 1: **Probability of not being resolved by 72 hours** (SLA breach for resolution)
$$P(\text{T}_{\text{resolve}} > 72 \text{ hours})$$

- Applies to operational decisions about guaranteed resolution SLA.
- Numerator: cases where `observed_status = Resolve` and `duration_or_age_h > 72`, **or** cases still `open` at cutoff with `duration_or_age_h \geq 72` (potential future breach).
- Denominator: all 32 cases.
- **Interpretation:** fraction at risk of missing the resolution promise.
- **Use case:** SLA violation alerts; escalation triggers.

### Estimand 2: **Cumulative incidence of any terminal disposition by 72 hours** (case clearance)
$$\text{CIF}_{\text{any}}(72) = P(\text{any terminal event by } 72 \text{ hours})$$

- Complements Estimand 1: what fraction *has* reached terminal state (any outcome) by 72 hours?
- Numerator: cases with `duration_or_age_h \leq 72` and `observed_status \in \{\text{Resolve, Reject, Withdraw, Transfer}\}`.
- Denominator: all 32 cases.
- **Interpretation:** process throughput; clearance rate.
- **Use case:** workload planning; queue-depth forecasting.

### Estimand 3: **Cause-specific cumulative incidence of resolution by 72 hours** (competing-risk perspective)
$$\text{CIF}_{\text{Resolve}}(72) = P(\text{Resolve by } 72 \text{ hours and no competing event})$$

- Explicitly models that rejection, withdrawal, or transfer *prevent* resolution.
- Numerator: cases with `observed_status = Resolve` and `duration_or_age_h \leq 72`.
- Denominator: all 32 cases.
- **Interpretation:** true successful resolution capacity under competing outcomes.
- **Use case:** process improvement; identifying if longer resolution times are due to difficulty or competing terminations.

### Estimand 4: **Probability of still having no terminal disposition at 72 hours** (administrative backlog risk)
$$P(\text{still open at } 72 \text{ hours})$$

- Numerator: cases with `observed_status = open` and `duration_or_age_h \geq 72`.
- Denominator: all 32 cases.
- **Interpretation:** stranded workload; administrative uncertainty.
- **Use case:** case-aging alerts; escalation for cases stuck in Review/Escalate loops.

# 2. Event and censoring encoding

## Encoding table

| Observed Status | Terminal? | Primary Event (E) | Competing Event Code | Censoring Rule | Estimand 1 (resolve breach) | Estimand 3 (CIF resolve) | Sensitivity: Transfer=success | Sensitivity: Transfer=failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Resolve | Yes | Resolve | – | No censoring | Breach if `duration > 72` else not | Event if `duration  72` | Event | – |
| Reject | Yes | Reject (competing) | 2 | No censoring | – | Censored (competing) | – | – |
| Withdraw | Yes | Withdraw (competing) | 3 | No censoring | – | Censored (competing) | – | – |
| Transfer | Yes | Transfer (competing) | 1 | *See below* | Depends on assumption | Censored (competing)* | Treat as Resolve | Treat as Resolve time + assume failure later |
| Open | No | – | – | Right-censored | If age  72, classify as breach-risk; else censored | Censored at age | Censored at age | Censored at age |

## Treatment of `Transfer` (three plausible scenarios)

### Assumption A: **Transfer = Informatively Censored** (conservative default)
- **Rationale:** external system takes over; internal SLA guarantee dissolves; outcome unknown.
- **Encoding:** competing event code = 1; contributes to risk set but *not* to numerator of CIF_Resolve.
- **Impact on Estimand 1:** cases S08, S21, S32 (all transferred  72 h) do **not** count as breach; the SLA obligation ended at transfer.
- **Impact on Estimand 3:** transferred cases are censored; they reduce denominator of resolution risk.

### Assumption B: **Transfer = Assumed Success** (optimistic)
- **Rationale:** partner organization has similar SLA or guaranteed resolution; transfer is a proxy for eventual resolution.
- **Encoding:** `Transfer`  recode to `Resolve` for analysis purposes.
- **Impact:** S08, S21, S32 counted as successful outcomes at their observed times.
- **Risk:** inflates success rate if transfers actually fail (unknown).

### Assumption C: **Transfer = Assumed Failure** (pessimistic)
- **Rationale:** external system may miss SLA; case ownership is ambiguous.
- **Encoding:** `Transfer`  recode to failure; count as SLA breach.
- **Impact:** S08 (68 h), S21 (26 h), S32 (96 h); S32 already breaches under any framing.
- **Risk:** penalizes transfers unfairly if external partner succeeds.

---

## Right-censoring vs. competing events

| Mechanism | Handled By | Impact on E1 (breach) | Impact on E3 (CIF resolve) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Open cases < 72 h** | Right-censoring (administrative) | Censor; assume will eventually resolve on time (optimistic) | Censor; unbias via Kaplan-Meier or cumulative-incidence weighting |
| **Open cases  72 h** | Right-censoring + breach flag | Count as breach if >72 h at cutoff | Censor at 72 h boundary; flag risk-set entry at breach threshold |
| **Reject, Withdraw** | Competing events | Do not count as breach (terminal non-resolve) | Use Aalen-Johansen cumulative incidence |
| **Transfer** | Competing + informative censoring | Scenario-dependent (A, B, or C above) | Scenario-dependent; sensitivity check |

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

## Bias 1: Survivor bias (looking only at resolved cases)

If we restrict to rows where `observed_status = Resolve`:
- Cases: S01, S02, S03, S04, S07, S13, S16, S17, S18, S19, S20, S27, S28 (13 resolved).
- Median duration: ~51 hours; 2/13 exceed 72 hours (S03: 76 h, S13: 110 h).
- **Bias:** we ignore S05 (rejected at 9 h), S06 (withdrawn at 27 h), S09 (still open at 92 h), S11 (still open at 81 h), S26 (still open at 104 h).
- **Effect:** we underestimate SLA breach risk because cases that exit via Reject or Withdraw *may* be processed faster (selection into fast track) or may represent unresolved disputes (competing termination masking delay).
- **Example:** S09 (open at 92 h, still no terminal outcome) would be excluded, yet it represents the worst-case aging scenario.

## Bias 2: Informative censoring (open cases with long gaps)

Cases S09, S10, S11, S25, S26, S29, S31:
- S09: age 92 h, last event gap 31 h (stuck in Review?).
- S11: age 81 h, last event gap 55 h (stuck after Escalate for 55 h; if it breaches, we will not observe it).
- S26: age 104 h, last event gap 72 h (under observation, but inactive for 3 days; likely breach if the case is still open).

**Informative censoring:** the pattern of long inactivity (`last_event_gap_h`) predicts that a case will breach. If we treat `open` as standard right-censoring (assume it resolves on time), we bias downward the breach probability. 
- Cases with `last_event_gap_h > 20` hours: S09 (31), S10 (19, borderline), S11 (55), S25 (48), S26 (72) — 5/7 open cases or transferred. This suggests stuck workflow.

## Bias 3: Competing risks (Reject, Withdraw not observed as long as Resolve)

Compare resolution time distributions:
- **Resolved cases:** S01 (18), S02 (41), S03 (76), S04 (29), S07 (54), S13 (110), S16 (16), S17 (32), S18 (12), S19 (58), S20 (74), S27 (39), S28 (88).
  - Median: 41 h; IQR [29, 74].
- **Rejected cases:** S05 (9), S14 (63), S23 (11).
  - Median: 11 h; IQR [9, 63].
- **Withdrawn cases:** S06 (27), S22 (44), S30 (70).
  - Median: 44 h; IQR [27, 70].

If we ignore rejection and treat it as censoring, we overestimate the "time to resolution" by conflating:
- Cases cleared quickly by screening (Reject: median 11 h) with cases genuinely resolved.
- This masks whether the process is efficient or whether early exit via Reject is masking unmet need.

**Concrete example:** S05 (Reject at 9 h) exited quickly. But if we only looked at Resolve times, we'd say "9 h is impossible"—and that's correct for Resolve, but wrong if we claim the process is fast overall. The 9 h is a competing (rejection) pathway.

## Bias 4: Cohort imbalance and informative rollout (Cohort B ~ policy change)

**Cohort A (15 cases):**
- Resolved: 6/15 = 40%.
- Open: 3/15 = 20%.
- Transferred: 1/15 = 7%.
- Rejected/Withdrawn: 5/15 = 33%.

**Cohort B (17 cases):**
- Resolved: 7/17 = 41%.
- Open: 5/17 = 29%.
- Transferred: 2/17 = 12%.
- Rejected/Withdrawn: 3/17 = 18%.

**Observation:** Cohort B has *more* open cases (5 vs. 3) and *more* transfers (2 vs. 1), but *fewer* rejections/withdrawals. This is consistent with an "automated screening" policy that escalates high-uncertainty cases rather than quick-rejecting them. 
- **Imbalance:** Cohort B's escalation rate is higher (Escalate in prefix): ~9/17 vs. ~7/15 in A.
- **Bias:** if Cohort B is forced to Escalate more, it may appear slower, but that reflects policy, not process efficiency. Naive comparison confounds policy change with performance.

## Bias 5: Open < 72 h vs. Open  72 h

**Open cases < 72 h:**
- S15 (35 h), S31 (23 h) — plausibly will resolve in time.
- Treating them as censored (standard right-censoring) is reasonable.

**Open cases  72 h:**
- S09 (92 h), S10 (47 h—wait, actually < 72, but age is 47 h), S11 (81 h), S25 (79 h), S26 (104 h), S29 (51 h).
- These are *already* at or past the SLA boundary without a terminal outcome.
- **Bias:** if we exclude them or treat them as standard censoring, we ignore the fact that they have *already breached* (at least administratively, as they haven't resolved in 72 h).
- Correct approach: flag cases open at cutoff time  72 h as "breached" under Estimand 4; do not assume they will resolve on time.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

## Method comparison

| Method | Defn. | Handles competing risks? | Censoring assumption | Handles time-varying covariates? | Timing requirement | Best use for these data |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Kaplan-Meier** | $S(t) = P(T > t)$ | No (treats all non-events as censored) | Non-informative | No (baseline only) | Exact event times | Exploratory; *only if* competing risks ignored. **Not recommended here.** |
| **Aalen-Johansen** | $\text{CIF}_j(t) = P(\text{event } j \text{ by } t)$ | Yes | Non-informative | No (baseline) | Risk-set; event times | **Primary.** Competing risks (Resolve vs. Reject/Withdraw/Transfer). No time-varying adjustment, but transparent. |
| **Cause-specific hazard (CSH)** | $\lambda_j(t) = \frac{dN_j(t)}{Y(t)}$ | Yes | Non-informative | Yes (via covariates) | Risk-set; event times | Estimate relative rates (e.g., does Cohort B escalate more?). Prone to non-collapsibility; secondary. |
| **Subdistribution hazard (SH, Fine-Gray)** | $\tilde{\lambda}_j(t) = \frac{dN_j(t)}{Y^*_j(t)}$ | Yes (directly models incidence) | Non-informative | Yes (via covariates) | Risk-set | Direct modeling of CIF; useful for covariate effects on incidence. Requires specialized fitting (bespoke or `cmprsk` package). |
| **Restricted mean time (RMT)** | $E[\min(T, \tau)]$ | Implicitly (event-dependent) | Non-informative | No | Event times | Summarize central tendency within 72-h horizon; robust alternative to median (which may not exist). |
| **Landmarking** | Fix $s$ (e.g., 24 h); condition on $T > s$ and having not had competing event | Yes | Non-informative at landmark time | Allows intermediate covariate conditioning | Landmark time | Identify cases at risk at 24 h; understand which process states lead to breach. Not primary but useful for alerts. |
| **Multi-state model (Markov or semi-Markov)** | Transition rates $\lambda_{ij}(t)$ between states (SubmitScreenReviewEscalateResolve) | Yes (pathways are competing) | Non-informative | Yes (stage-specific) | Sojourn times in each state; transitions | **Secondary/exploratory.** Understand where delays accumulate (e.g., stuck in Review vs. Escalate?). Requires state coding. |
| **Inverse-probability-of-censoring weighting (IPCW)** | Reweight risk set to account for informative censoring | Optional (addresses censoring if estimated) | Informative (estimated via auxiliary model) | Yes | Censoring model + event times | Address informative censoring from long gaps. Requires model of $P(\text{censored} \mid \text{history})$; high variance. Secondary sensitivity. |

---

## Recommended primary and secondary approaches

### **Primary: Aalen-Johansen cumulative incidence with competing risks**
**Why:**
1. **Directly answers Estimand 3** (true resolution CIF under competing outcomes).
2. **Transparent:** standard risk-set construction; clear denominators.
3. **No assumption of independence of competing risks** (unlike cause-specific hazard, which can be misleading in collapsible settings).
4. **No model specification required** for baseline analysis; purely descriptive.
5. **Stratification by cohort and priority** is straightforward.

**Output:** Plot CIF curves for Resolve, Reject, Withdraw, Transfer; show cumulative incidences at 72 h by cohort and priority.

### **Secondary: Restricted mean time (RMT) at 72 hours**
**Why:**
1. **Directly addresses Estimand 1** (time to breach).
2. **Avoids tail issues** (median may not exist if >50% censored/competing).
3. **Intuitive:** "average time in process, capped at 72 h."
4. **Easy to compare:** Cohort A vs. B; P1 vs. P3.

**Output:** Report $E[\min(T_{\text{Resolve}}, 72)]$ and $E[\min(T_{\text{any}}, 72)]$ by stratum.

### **Tertiary (sensitivity): Cause-specific hazard model with process-aware covariates**
**Why:**
1. Adjust for cohort and escalation timing (time-varying).
2. Quantify relative effect of priority, rework, inactivity gap.
3. **Identify which process features predict breach.**

**Caveat:** non-collapsible; marginal and conditional hazard ratios differ. Use only to understand direction and hypothesis generation.

### **Quaternary (sensitivity): Informative censoring weighting (if data support)**
**Why:**
1. Account for cases open > 72 h with long gaps (likely to breach).
2. Estimate censoring model (e.g., logistic regression on last_event_gap_h).
3. Reweight and recompute Aalen-Johansen.

**Caveat:** requires no unmeasured confounders of censoring; high variance if model misspecified.

---

## Time definition for risk-set construction

Given that `duration_or_age_h` is supplied (not individual event timestamps):
- Assume each case has a single event time `t_i = duration_or_age_h` (or, for computing, treat as exact time at which event occurred or case was censored).
- Risk set $Y(t)$ = number of cases with `duration_or_age_h  t` and not yet reached a terminal outcome as of time $t$.
- At each unique time, increment the event count for that outcome (Resolve, Reject, etc.) and update the risk set.

# 5. Process-aware features and stage hazards

## Feature derivation from prefixes

### Baseline features (determined at Submit; no leakage)

1. **`escalated` (given):** 1 if "Escalate" appears in prefix, 0 else.
   - **Type:** time-varying (Escalate occurs after Submit), but given as binary indicator.
   - **Leakage risk:** HIGH. A case cannot be escalated at Submit; it is escalated *during* the process.
   - **Correct usage:** only as a time-varying covariate in a proportional-hazards model, or stratified analysis post-escalation (landmarking).

2. **`priority` (given):** P1, P2, P3.
   - **Type:** baseline (assigned at Submit).
   - **Leakage risk:** None.
   - **Expected effect:** P1 (highest) should have shorter times or faster escalation.

3. **`cohort` (given):** A or B.
   - **Type:** baseline.
   - **Leakage risk:** None, but confounded with policy change.

### Derived baseline features (safe)

4. **`has_autocheck`:** 1 if "AutoCheck" in prefix, 0 else.
   - **Type:** baseline (AutoCheck occurs early, often in position 2).
   - **Leakage risk:** Moderate. AutoCheck may be triggered by priority/complexity at Submit, but it is an activity, not a covariate. If it is an automated step that always happens for Cohort B (policy), treat as cohort effect, not a feature.
   - **Interpretation:** may reflect screening workflow (A does Review first; B does AutoCheck first).

5. **`reviewed_multiple`:** count of "Review" in prefix.
   - **Type:** baseline (counts observed reviews in the full path).
   - **Leakage risk:** HIGH. Cases that are resolved do not re-review; cases that fail reviews get reworked. Using review count as a baseline predictor is mixing cause (rework delays) with consequence (longer duration).
   - **Correct usage:** only as a time-varying summary: "by time t, how many reviews?" or in a multi-state model (state-specific).

6. **`path_length`:** count of distinct activities (e.g., "Submit>Screen>Review>Resolve" = 4).
   - **Type:** baseline (in completed cases) or time-varying (as of time t).
   - **Leakage risk:** HIGH. Longer paths are consequences of rework and escalation, not causes of them.
   - **Correct usage:** in post-hoc process mining (describe completed cases), not as a prediction feature for survival.

### Time-varying process features (safe for TVE models)

7. **Current stage** (as of time t):
   - States: {Submit, Screened, Reviewed, Escalated, Reworked, Terminal}.
   - **Type:** time-varying state occupancy.
   - **Leakage risk:** None if we condition on stage at time $s$ (landmarking) or stratify by stage.
   - **Use:** multi-state or landmark analysis.

8. **Time since last event** (`last_event_gap_h`):
   - **Type:** time-varying; given at terminal/censoring time; can be carried forward from last observed event.
   - **Leakage risk:** Low, if interpreted as inactivity indicator (process stalled?).
   - **Use:** condition on gap > threshold (e.g., "stuck for > 24 h"); may indicate informative censoring.
   - **Example:** S11 (age 81 h, gap 55 h) suggests case has been stalled since ~26 h. This predicts breach.

9. **Escalation timing** (`time_to_escalate` or "escalated by hour X"):
   - **Derivation:** if "Escalate" in prefix, extract position and infer approximate time.
   - **Type:** time-varying (occurs after Submit).
   - **Leakage risk:** HIGH if used as a baseline predictor (escalation happens *during* case lifetime). SAFE if used as a time-varying covariate (hazard changes after escalation).
   - **Interpretation:** early escalation may signal complexity or priority; late escalation may signal failure of initial processing.

10. **Rework count** (`rework_count`, given):
    - **Type:** time-varying (increases when "Rework" is executed).
    - **Leakage risk:** MEDIUM. Rework is a consequence of review failure, but by time $t$, it is an observed fact.
    - **Correct usage:** as a time-varying marker (e.g., "has rework occurred by time $t$?"); compare hazard before vs. after first rework.

### Proposed covariate definitions for model

| Feature | Baseline? | Time-varying? | Leakage risk | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `priority` |  | – | None | Use as baseline stratum. |
| `cohort` |  | – | None (confounded with policy) | Stratify or adjust. |
| `escalated` |  |  | HIGH | Use only as time-varying in TVE Cox model, or landmark at escalation. |
| `has_autocheck` |  | – | Medium | Safe as baseline, but conflated with cohort. |
| `path_length` |  |  | HIGH | Use only descriptively (completed-case analysis), not as predictor. |
| `reviewed_multiple` |  |  | HIGH | Use only time-varying: "has case been reviewed >1 time by hour $t$?" |
| `rework_count` |  |  | MEDIUM | Safe as time-varying; track before/after first rework. |
| `current_stage` |  |  | None | Use in multi-state model or landmarking. |
| `time_since_last_event` |  |  | Low | Use to flag informative censoring or stalled cases. |
| `time_to_escalate` | – |  | HIGH | Use only as time-varying; do not use as baseline. |

**States:**
- **Submit:** entry; no decision yet.
- **Screen:** initial triage.

- **Rework:** case sent back for re-review (loop).
- **Terminal:** Resolve, Reject, Withdraw, Transfer.

**Transitions:**
- Deterministic: Submit  Screen (mandatory).
- Probabilistic: Screen  Review, Reject, Withdraw (depends on initial assessment).
- Screen  AutoCheck (Cohort B policy).
- Review  Resolve (success), Rework (fail), Escalate (uncertainty).
- Escalate  Resolve, Transfer, Review (loop).
- Rework  Review (loop).

# 6. Sensitivity analysis

## Sensitivity 1: Treatment of `Transfer`

### Scenario A1 (Base): Transfer = Competing event (censored)
```
Exclude transfers from numerator of Resolve outcome.
CIF_Resolve(72 h) = P(Resolve by 72 h and no competing event);
Cases S08, S21, S32: do not count as Resolve.
Result: CIF_Resolve(72) = 9/32 (only truly resolved, excluding open and transferred).
```

### Scenario A2: Transfer = Success (optimistic reframing)
```
Recode Transfer  Resolve in the data.
Cases S08 (68 h), S21 (26 h), S32 (96 h) treated as Resolve outcomes.
S32 still breaches at 96 h; S08, S21 do not.
Result: CIF_Resolve(72) increases; impact varies by whether transfer is pre- or post-72 h.
Calculated: 9 + 2 = 11 resolved by 72 h (S21 at 26 h, not S08 at 68 h after recoding, not S32 at 96 h).
Actually: S21 (26 h) , S08 (68 h) , S32 (96 h) .
Result: 9 + 2 = 11/32 resolved by 72 h.
```

### Scenario A3: Transfer = Failure (pessimistic)
```
Recode Transfer  Breach (failure).
Cases S08, S21, S32 treated as SLA violations.
CIF_Breach = P(Transfer) + P(Open  72 h) + P(Resolve > 72 h).
Result: higher breach rate (transfers penalized).
```

| Scenario | CIF_Resolve(72) | SLA breach rate (Estimand 1) | Operational signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 (transfer=competing) | 9/32 = 28.1% | 2 (S03, S13) + uncertain open = 6.3% certain, ~25% risk | Case S32 transfers at 96 h; outcome unknown; don't blame internal system. |
| A2 (transfer=success) | 11/32 = 34.4% | 1 (S13 at 110 h only) + open = ~15% | Assume external partner delivers; SLA looks better. |
| A3 (transfer=failure) | 9/32 = 28.1% | 2 + 3 (transfers) + open = 15.6% | Penalize transfers; incentivize internal resolution. |

**Qualitative reversal risk:** If external partners have *worse* SLA (A3 true), then A2 is overly optimistic. If external partners are reliable (A2 true), then A1 understates success. **Recommendation:** stratify by transfer outcome if external data become available; for now, report A1 (competing) as primary, A2 and A3 as sensitivity range.

## Sensitivity 2: Competing risks (Reject + Withdraw vs. Resolve)

### Scenario B1 (Base): Separate competing risks (Aalen-Johansen)
```
CIF_Resolve, CIF_Reject, CIF_Withdraw, CIF_Transfer computed independently.
Sum to 1.0 at any time (complete competing-risk accounting).
Reflects true probability of each outcome by 72 h.
```

### Scenario B2: Treat Reject+Withdraw as "resolved" (relaxed outcome)
```
Recode Reject, Withdraw  Resolve.
Definition: case is "cleared" (any terminal state).
Result: higher CIF_Resolve (includes fast rejections).
Compare: Reject median ~11 h, Resolve median ~41 h.
If we want to measure "time to clearance (any outcome)," use B2.
If we want to measure "time to successful Resolve," use B1.
```

### Scenario B3: Treat only Resolve as success; others as missing/lost (strict)
```
Reject, Withdraw, Transfer: treat as not-resolved (failure of system).
CIF_Resolve(72) = P(Resolve by 72 h).
Numerator: 9 cases resolved by 72 h (S01, S02, S04, S16, S17, S18, S19, S20, S27).
Result: 9/32 = 28.1%.
This is the strictest definition; appropriate if organizational goal is to "resolve," not just "clear."
```

| Scenario | CIF_Resolve(72) | Interpretation | Use case |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 (separate competing) | 9/32 = 28.1% | Probability case resolves (not rejected/withdrawn/transferred). | Primary: measure true resolution. |
| B2 (clearance = any terminal) | (9+2+3+3)/32 = 17/32 = 53.1% | Probability case reaches any terminal state. | Workload clearance; throughput. |
| B3 (strict: only Resolve success) | 9/32 = 28.1% | Same as B1; for contrast, show count of "non-resolved terminals." | Highlight rejects/withdrawals as system failures (if policy). |

**Qualitative reversal:** If business definition shifts from "resolve" to "clear," success rate doubles. This is a policy choice, not a statistical one.

## Sensitivity 3: Informative censoring (gap-based adjustment)

### Scenario C1 (Base): Standard right-censoring (assume open cases will resolve on time)
```
Open cases treated as censored at observation cutoff.
No adjustment for long gaps.
Risk set includes them at all future times (even if gap = 72 h suggests breach).
```

### Scenario C2: Gap-informed censoring weighting (reweight open cases with long gaps)
```
Build a logistic model: P(censored | last_event_gap_h, priority, cohort).
Cases with gap > 24 h (e.g., S09, S11, S25, S26) assigned higher "censoring probability."
Inverse-weight risk sets to account for "selective non-resolution" (cases that don't move are censored).
Recompute Kaplan-Meier or Aalen-Johansen with IPCW.
Expected result: breach rate increases (we up-weight the long-gap cases, which are at risk).
```

### Scenario C3: Deterministic gap threshold (flag cases as breached if gap > 48 h)
```
Define: a case with gap > 48 h at cutoff is "stuck" and likely to breach.
Reclassify S11 (gap 55 h), S25 (gap 48 h), S26 (gap 72 h) as "functionally open and breached."
Numerator of breach risk: 2 (resolved > 72 h: S03, S13) + 3 (stuck, gap > 48 h) = 5.
Result: breach rate = 5/32 = 15.6%.
```

| Scenario | Breach rate (Estimand 1) | Breach cases | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 (standard censoring) | 2/32 = 6.3% (certain) + 3/32 open 72h = ~15% risk | S03, S13; S09, S11, S26 potential | Optimistic; assumes open cases resolve in time. |
| C2 (gap-weighted IPCW) | 6.3% + reweight adjustment, likely 20–30% | S03, S13 + up-weighted S09, S11, S26 | Depends on censoring model fit; high variance if model misspecified. |
| C3 (gap threshold) | 15.6% (2 + 3 stuck) | S03, S13, S11, S25, S26 | Conservative; may over-flag (gap 48 h is administrative, not actual SLA). |

**Qualitative reversal:** If we trust that internal processes will eventually resolve open cases (C1), breach risk is low (~6%). If we distrust latency (C2, C3), breach risk is 15–30%. **Recommendation:** Primary result should note that C1 is conservative (optimistic); secondary C3 (gap threshold) provides a more realistic alert threshold.

```
Cohort A (n=15): resolution CIF(72), breach rate, median time.

```

### Scenario D2: Adjust for escalation rate (confounder)
```
Cohort B has higher escalation rate (9/17 vs. 7/15).
Escalation may delay resolution (longer path  longer time).
Adjust stratified analysis for "number of escalate events" or use matched cohorts on escalation propensity.
Expected: Cohort B may look worse (longer times) until adjusted; adjustment reveals underlying policy effect.
```

### Scenario D3: Assume cohort assignment is non-random and rollout incomplete
```
Cohort B is pilot / early adoption.
Some cases in B may not have received the "new policy" (compliance incomplete).
Use `escalated` flag as proxy for policy receipt: analyze only cases with Escalate (policy-compliant).
Expected: subset analysis; more homogeneous cohort, but lower power.
```

|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 (unadjusted) | 6/15 = 40% | 7/17 = 41% | +1% (no difference) | Policy change neutral; or confounded by other factors. |

## Converting survival outputs to alerts and actions

### 1. SLA breach alerts (real-time)

**Source:** Estimated cumulative incidence of breach at time $t$ (Estimand 1).

**Alert rule (example):**
```
IF (duration_age_h >= 72 AND status = 'open') 
   OR (duration_h > 72 AND status = 'resolved')
THEN flag as "BREACH."

IF (duration_age_h >= 48 AND status = 'open' AND last_event_gap_h > 24)
   THEN flag as "AT-RISK: Escalate or investigate stall."
```

**Dashboard display:**
- Live: count of open cases by age bucket (0–24 h, 24–48 h, 48–72 h, >72 h).
- Forecast: based on current process speed, estimate P(breach in next 24 h | current age, stage, priority).
- Cohort lens: stratify alerts by Cohort A vs. B, Priority P1 vs. P3 to detect differential risk.

**Caveat:** Do *not* present the survival curve (e.g., Kaplan-Meier estimate) as a causal prediction of "this case will breach." The survival curve is a population average, not a case-specific risk. Use it for:
- Benchmarking expected delay (compare to external orgs).
- Validating process stability (is breach rate stable month-to-month?).

### 4. Calibration and fairness checks

#### Calibration: Does the model's predicted breach probability match realized breach rate?

**Method:**
1. Divide cases into risk deciles based on Kaplan-Meier or Aalen-Johansen estimated survival at 72 h.
2. For each decile, compute observed breach rate (fraction of cases in decile that actually breached).
3. Plot predicted vs. observed; ideal is 45° line (well-calibrated).

**Example check:**
```
Decile 1 (highest risk, low survival): 
  Predicted P(breach) = 80%; Observed = 7/8 = 87.5%.  Well-calibrated.

Decile 5 (medium risk):
  Predicted P(breach) = 50%; Observed = 2/4 = 50%.  Well-calibrated.

Decile 10 (lowest risk, high survival):
  Predicted P(breach) = 5%; Observed = 0/3 = 0%.  Well-calibrated.
```

#### Fairness: Are breach rates or predicted risks equitable across priority and cohort?

**Fairness checks:**
1. **Disparity ratio:** P(breach | Cohort B) / P(breach | Cohort A). Target: ratio close to 1.0.
   - Current data: open cases 5/17 (B) vs. 3/15 (A)  1.1. Slightly higher in B, but not severe; could be due to policy.
   
2. **Equitable wait time:** median time to resolution for P1 vs. P3. Target: P1 faster.
   - Current data: P1 resolved cases median ~29 h, P3 median ~35 h (small difference).
   - P3 cases include fast rejections (~11 h); P1 includes escalations (longer paths). Fair comparison requires controlling for path.

3. **Equitable escalation:** what fraction of P1 vs. P3 cases are escalated? Target: higher escalation for P1 (if that's the intended policy).
   - Current data: P1 escalated 5/8 = 62.5%; P3 escalated 2/11 = 18%.  P1 escalates more, as intended.

**Potential bias to investigate:**
- If Cohort B was piloted on "easier" cases (lower priority or lower complexity), then comparing A vs. B breach rates is unfair. **Mitigation:** stratify by priority.
- If certain priority groups are *always* escalated regardless of merit, that may be unfair (see Estimand 2 for competing-risk analysis). **Mitigation:** audit escalation criteria.

```python
SLA_THRESHOLD = 72

def encode_events(df, sla_threshold=72):
    """
    Encode event indicators for multiple estimands.
    
    Returns:
      event_type: 0=Resolve, 1=Transfer, 2=Reject, 3=Withdraw, NA=Open/Censored
      event_time: duration_or_age_h
      is_censored: 1 if open, 0 if terminal
      breach_sla: 1 if time > sla_threshold and resolved, or if open and age >= sla_threshold
      is_terminal: 1 if any terminal outcome
    """
    df_enc = df.copy()
    
    # Event type encoding (for competing risks)
    event_type_map = {'resolve': 0, 'transfer': 1, 'reject': 2, 'withdraw': 3}
    df_enc['event_type'] = df_enc['observed_status'].map(event_type_map).fillna(np.nan)
    
    # Is censored (open = censored)
    df_enc['is_censored'] = (df_enc['observed_status'] == 'open').astype(int)
    
    # SLA breach: resolved > 72 or open at >= 72
    df_enc['breach_sla'] = ((df_enc['observed_status'] == 'resolved') & 
                             (df_enc['duration_or_age_h'] > sla_threshold)).astype(int)
    df_enc.loc[df_enc['observed_status'] == 'open', 'breach_sla'] = \
        (df_enc.loc[df_enc['observed_status'] == 'open', 'duration_or_age_h'] >= sla_threshold).astype(int)
    
    # Terminal outcome
    df_enc['is_terminal'] = (df_enc['observed_status'] != 'open').astype(int)
    
    # Estimand 1: P(not resolved by 72 h) — breach_sla indicates breach
    df_enc['estimand_1_breach'] = df_enc['breach_sla']
    
    # Estimand 3: CIF of Resolve by 72 h (competing risks)
    df_enc['estimand_3_event'] = ((df_enc['observed_status'] == 'resolve') & 
                                   (df_enc['duration_or_age_h'] <= sla_threshold)).astype(int)
    
    # Estimand 4: P(open >= 72 h)
    df_enc['estimand_4_open_breach'] = ((df_enc['observed_status'] == 'open') & 
                                         (df_enc['duration_or_age_h'] >= sla_threshold)).astype(int)
    
    return df_enc

def compute_cumulative_incidence(df, event_type_col='event_type', time_col='duration_or_age_h',
                                  sla_threshold=72):
    """
    Approximate Aalen-Johansen cumulative incidence.
    For each event type, count cases reaching that event by time t.
    """
    
    results = {}
    
    # Event types: 0=Resolve, 1=Transfer, 2=Reject, 3=Withdraw
    event_names = {0: 'Resolve', 1: 'Transfer', 2: 'Reject', 3: 'Withdraw'}
    
    for event_code, event_name in event_names.items():
        event_by_sla = df[(df[event_type_col] == event_code) & 
                           (df[time_col] <= sla_threshold)].shape[0]
        total = df.shape[0]
        cif = event_by_sla / total
        results[event_name] = {'count': event_by_sla, 'cif': cif, 'total': total}
    
    # Also track censored (open)
    open_count = df[df['observed_status'] == 'open'].shape[0]
    results['Open (censored)'] = {'count': open_count, 'cif': open_count / len(df), 'total': len(df)}
    
    return results

def stratified_analysis(df, strata_col, sla_threshold=72):
    """
    Compute estimands stratified by a column (e.g., cohort, priority).
    """
    strata_results = {}
    
    for stratum in df[strata_col].unique():
        df_sub = df[df[strata_col] == stratum]
        
        resolved_by_sla = (df_sub['observed_status'] == 'resolved') & \
                          (df_sub['duration_or_age_h'] <= sla_threshold)
        breach = (df_sub['observed_status'] == 'resolved') & \
                 (df_sub['duration_or_age_h'] > sla_threshold)
        open_breach = (df_sub['observed_status'] == 'open') & \
                      (df_sub['duration_or_age_h'] >= sla_threshold)
        
        strata_results[stratum] = {
            'n': len(df_sub),
            'resolved_by_sla': resolved_by_sla.sum(),
            'breach_resolved': breach.sum(),
            'open_breach': open_breach.sum(),
            'median_time': df_sub['duration_or_age_h'].median(),
            'escalated_rate': df_sub['escalated'].mean()
        }
    
    return strata_results
```

# 9. Limitations and additional data

## Critical missing data

### 1. **Exact event timestamps**
- **Current:** `duration_or_age_h` aggregated; no event-level timestamps.
- **Need:** exact timestamps for Submit, Screen, Review, Escalate, Resolve/Reject/Withdraw/Transfer. Enable:
  - Precise sojourn time in each stage (multi-state hazard estimation).
  - Landmark analysis at 24 h, 48 h (which cases are stuck?).
  - Detection of parallelism or overlapping work (not visible from aggregate duration).
- **Format:** CSV with columns: case_id, event_type, event_timestamp.

### 2. **External outcomes for transferred cases**
- **Current:** S08, S21, S32 transferred; outcome unknown.
- **Need:** follow-up data on whether external partner resolved the case and by when.
- **Impact:** if available, eliminate ambiguity in Scenario A1 vs. A2. Enables calculation of true SLA compliance rate.
- **Format:** case_id, external_outcome, external_resolution_date.

### 3. **Cutoff and case-start dates**
- **Current:** `duration_or_age_h` supplied, but absolute dates not clear.
- **Need:** submission_date and observation_cutoff_date for all cases. Enable:
  - Assess temporal trends (is process improving over time?).
  - Control for seasonal effects (workload density, staffing).
  - Validate assumption that right-censoring is non-informative (cases opened recently vs. long ago).
- **Format:** case_id, submit_date, observation_cutoff_date.

### 4. **Cohort assignment and policy rollout compliance**
- **Current:** Cohort A/B given; policy change mentioned but rollout compliance "incomplete."
- **Need:** 
  - Exact policy change date.
  - Which cases in Cohort B received the new policy (e.g., flag if case was submitted before/after policy date).
  - Compliance indicator: did case receive new AutoCheck screening if assigned to B?
- **Impact:** enables causal analysis of policy effect; controls for non-random assignment.
- **Format:** case_id, policy_change_date, policy_compliant (0/1).

### 5. **Time-varying workload and staffing**
- **Current:** no resource data.
- **Need:**
  - Daily or weekly workload (queue length over time).
  - Staffing levels (FTE in Screen, Review, Escalate teams).
  - Case-level attributes: complexity (low/medium/high), requestor type, document count.
- **Impact:** explain why some cases are stuck (system overload vs. case difficulty). Enables capacity forecasting and fair SLA setting per tier.
- **Format:** daily_workload.csv with date, queue_depth; case_complexity.csv with case_id, complexity_tier.

### 6. **Detailed rework and escalation reasons**
- **Current:** `rework_count` and `escalated` flag, but no reason codes.
- **Need:**
  - Rework reason: missing documents, policy clarification, applicant objection, etc.
  - Escalation reason: priority, complex policy question, external dependency, etc.
  - Reviewer notes (text): feedback on why Review failed.
- **Impact:** identify systemic causes of rework (e.g., if 50% rework is due to "missing docs," fix intake). Enables root-cause analysis.
- **Format:** rework_log.csv with case_id, rework_reason_code, date.

### 7. **Activity durations (sojourn times)**
- **Current:** total case duration only; no stage-specific time.
- **Need:** time in each state:
  - time_in_screen_h, time_in_review_h, time_in_escalate_h, etc.
- **Impact:** pinpoint bottleneck stage. Enable multi-state hazard analysis.
- **Format:** in event log format: case_id, activity, start_timestamp, end_timestamp.

### 8. **Outcome quality and rework loop depth**
- **Current:** `rework_count` = 0 or 1 (max observed).
- **Need:**
  - Full history of all rework loops (some cases may loop >2 times).
  - Outcome quality metric: did Resolve case require rework after closeout (not observed)?
  - Rejection reason: policy non-compliance (failure of Review) vs. applicant ineligibility (external reason).
- **Impact:** assess whether "Resolve" outcomes are truly successful or masked rework/reversals.
- **Format:** rework_loop_id, case_id, loop_number, entry_activity, exit_activity.
