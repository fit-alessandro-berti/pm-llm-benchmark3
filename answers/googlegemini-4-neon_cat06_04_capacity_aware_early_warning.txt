## 1. Decision rule

A deployable early-warning policy must separate **prognostic risk** ($p$, the probability of breach without intervention) from **actionable causal lift** ($\Delta p$, the reduction in breach probability from a Senior Diagnostic Swarm), while pricing both direct disruption and queue spillovers. The policy operates as a five-stage gate-and-allocate rule executed at each 30-minute refresh and on material case events.

### Stage 1: Hard Eligibility Gate
A case $i$ is eligible for a Senior Diagnostic Swarm only if all four conditions hold:
1. **Process stage:** Current stage $\in \{\text{Triage}, \text{Diagnosis Started}\}$. Cases at or beyond `Diagnosis Complete` are **automatically excluded** from swarms and routed to resolution-stage management escalation instead.
2. **Remaining SLA window:** Time to SLA deadline $\ge 1.5\text{ h}$ (a 45-minute swarm leaves $\le 45\text{ min}$ for remaining work below this floor; cases between 1.5 h and 2.0 h require explicit feasibility sign-off or a lightweight non-swarm intervention).
3. **Prior swarm constraint:** No prior swarm on case $i$, unless a materially new diagnostic event invalidates the earlier diagnosis.
4. **Data integrity:** No missing/imputed high-leverage features (such as live queue state). Cases with corrupted/imputed features enter a **15–30 minute Verification Hold** for a rapid manual or telemetry check before consuming a specialist slot.

### Stage 2: Alert Persistence & Minimum Evidence Change
To prevent alert fatigue from 30-minute clock refreshes:
- **First alert:** Passes directly to scoring if eligible.
- **Repeated alert (no prior swarm):** Passes re-evaluation **only if** at least one re-entry condition is met: (a) a materially new event/artifact arrives (e.g., customer log, stage transition, failed test), (b) calibrated breach risk increases by $\Delta p_{\text{cal}} \ge +0.05$ on complete data, or (c) the case hits a **dwell-time backstop** (stalled in Triage/Diagnosis past segment-specific time limits). Otherwise, the alert is suppressed.

### Stage 3: Calibration Adjustment & Causal Robustification
Raw observational scores $p_{\text{now}}$ are converted into **calibrated probability intervals** $[p_{\text{cal}}^{\text{lo}}, p_{\text{cal}}^{\text{hi}}]$ with midpoint $\hat{p}_{\text{cal}}$ using recent segment backtests:
- **SME:** Shift upward by $+[0.04, 0.09]$ (midpoint $+0.065$), capped at $0.99$.
- **Strategic Enterprise:** Shift downward by $-[0.05, 0.12]$ (midpoint $-0.085$), floored at $0.01$.
- **Public Sector:** Keep centered with moderate uncertainty band $\pm 0.04$.
- **Non-Native-Language Support (NNLS):** Keep centered with wide epistemic uncertainty band $\pm 0.15$.

Supplied effect ranges $[\Delta p^{\text{lo}}, \Delta p^{\text{hi}}]$ are treated as **uncertain prior intervals**, not known causal effects:
- Enforce the physical ceiling $\Delta p_{\text{eff}} \le \hat{p}_{\text{cal}}$ (a swarm cannot prevent more breaches than would occur).
- For segments known to overpredict (Strategic Enterprise), apply a conservative 10–15% haircut to the point-estimate $\Delta p$ when testing robustness, because observational risk inflation typically inflates estimated absolute lift.
- For sparse segments (NNLS), do **not** penalize epistemic uncertainty by defaulting to the lower bound; evaluate using both midpoint and an exploration/equity adjustment (see Section 5).

### Stage 4: Portfolio-Adjusted Prioritization Score
Eligible, verified cases are ranked by **Portfolio-Adjusted Net Expected Value (PANEV)** rather than $p_{\text{now}}$:

$$\text{PANEV}_i = \underbrace{\widehat{\Delta p}_i \cdot V_i}_{\text{Direct avoided breach value}} - \underbrace{C_{\text{swarm}}}_{€450} - \underbrace{(1 - \hat{p}_{i,\text{cal}}) \cdot \text{FA}_i}_{\text{Expected false-alarm burden}} - \underbrace{\text{Spill}_k}_{\text{Marginal queue externality}} - \underbrace{\text{VoW}_i}_{\text{Option value of waiting}} + \underbrace{E_i}_{\text{Equity / VoI bonus}}$$

- **False-alarm burden:** Modeled in expectation as $(1 - \hat{p}_{i,\text{cal}})\cdot \text{FA}_i$ (with a robustness check deducting 100% of $\text{FA}_i$ if disruption occurs regardless of counterfactual outcome).
- **Marginal spillover shadow price ($\text{Spill}_k$):** Under base congestion, each slot adds $0.01\text{–}0.03$ breach risk to 6 untreated diagnosis cases ($0.06\text{–}0.18$ extra breaches/slot). At an average untreated diagnosis-case value of $€12{,}000\text{–}€20{,}000$, $\text{Spill}_k \approx €1{,}200\text{–}€3{,}600$ per slot, rising sharply as concurrent swarms increase.
- **Option value of waiting ($\text{VoW}_i$):** High when a case is still in `Triage` with ample SLA runway ($\ge 6\text{ h}$) **and** has moderate $\hat{p}_{\text{cal}}$ with large $\text{FA}_i$ (waiting resolves uncertainty and avoids unnecessary disruption). Near zero when the case is in `Diagnosis Started` with $\le 3.5\text{ h}$ left, because the intervention window is about to close permanently.
- **Robustness filter:** To claim a slot immediately, a case should have (1) positive midpoint $\text{PANEV}_i$ above $\text{Spill}_k$, and (2) non-catastrophic downside under lower-bound $\Delta p$ (avoiding negative direct EV before spillovers).

### Stage 5: Capacity Reservation, Sequencing, and Congestion Circuit-Breaker
- **Reserve policy:** Hold **1 of 4 slots** in reserve until 15:00 for the anticipated regulatory incident (whose non-monetary compliance penalties outweigh marginal commercial EV), unless a live case faces an imminent, irreversible catastrophic loss exceeding the reserve option value.
- **Staggered dispatch & circuit-breaker:** Dispatch selected swarms sequentially/staggered rather than simultaneously so specialist interruption does not trigger nonlinear congestion. Pause additional non-regulatory swarms if live diagnosis-queue wait times exceed the base assumption (>20 min aggregate delay/slot).

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

Continuous 30-minute scoring creates high alert volume where time-decay alone nudges probabilities upward without new operational facts. The temporal policy governs four state transitions:

### 1. Suppression & Deduplication Rules
- **No-new-event suppression:** If a case was already scored and deferred (Wait), subsequent 30-minute refreshes are **silently logged, not surfaced to operators**, unless a re-entry trigger fires.
- **Post-stage suppression:** Once `Diagnosis Complete` is recorded, all diagnostic-swarm alerts are permanently muted and converted (if $\hat{p}_{\text{cal}} \ge 0.65$) into a separate resolution-expediting stream.
- **Imputation suppression:** Alerts driven by newly missing/imputed core features are routed to a **Data-Ops / Triage verification queue** rather than the Swarm dispatcher until live data is restored or manually confirmed (as applied to **I-06**).

### 2. Cool-Down Windows
- **Deferred (unswarmed) cases:** Minimum **60-minute cool-down** before re-ranking on drift alone, overridden immediately by any material event.
- **Swarmed cases:** **Hard block** on a second swarm for the remainder of the incident lifecycle, liftable only via senior-lead override upon a documented structural change (e.g., wrong root cause invalidated by a new system failure or major scope expansion).

### 3. Re-Entry Logic (Minimum Evidence Change)
A suppressed or waiting case re-enters active swarm consideration if **any** of the following occurs:
1. **New information artifact:** New customer diagnostic log, crash dump, escalation note, or diagnostic test failure.
2. **Process-state transition:** Transition from `Triage` to `Diagnosis Started` (changing urgency and lowering option value of waiting—key trigger for **I-08**, **I-01**, and **I-10**).
3. **Material calibrated risk jump:** $\Delta \hat{p}_{\text{cal}} \ge +0.05$ since last evaluated decision on complete (non-imputed) features.
4. **Telemetry repair:** Missing feature restored and verified risk remains above threshold (**I-06**).

### 4. Escalation & Dwell-Time Backstops
- **Anti-starvation dwell-time backstop:** If a deferred case remains in `Triage` for $> 2.5\text{ h}$ without advancing—or reaches $\le 3.0\text{ h}$ to SLA while still eligible—it automatically resurfaces once for human review (either swarm or lightweight fast-track), preventing silent abandonment.
- **Congestion-aware deferred re-queue:** If a case was deferred purely due to capacity/congestion rather than low EV, it retains priority when a slot opens or at the 15:00 reserve-release checkpoint.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

An unguarded expected-value policy creates five predictable ethical, statistical, and operational pathologies. Below are the specific risks and concrete safeguards required for deployment.

### 1. Value-Based Prioritization Starving Lower-Value Cases (e.g., SME)
- **Risk:** Because $\text{PANEV}$ scales linearly with contractual breach value $V_i$, high-value Strategic Enterprise cases (€25k–€100k) systematically outbid SME cases (€5k–€11k) even when SME cases have *higher* risk reduction ($\Delta p$ of 0.25–0.40 for I-05 vs. 0.08–0.22 for I-02). Under a pure linear-EV rule, SMEs rarely receive swarms.
- **Concrete safeguards:**
  - **Tiered intervention menu:** Create a dedicated **lightweight triage assist** (10–15 min, 1 mid-level specialist, negligible queue spillover) available specifically for high-$\Delta p$, lower-value cases like I-07 and stalled SME cases.
  - **Concave value weighting or protected capacity floor:** Cap effective value ratios in scoring (e.g., $V_{\text{eff}} = \min(V_i, €60\text{k})$ or concave utility $V_i^{0.8}$) **or** reserve at least **1 swarm slot per day** (or 15–20% of weekly swarm capacity) for a protected pool covering SME and Public Sector cases whose $\widehat{\Delta p}_i \ge 0.25$.
  - **Contractual breach-rate ceiling:** Impose a hard governance cap on segment SLA breach rates (e.g., no segment may exceed a rolling 14-day breach rate $> 1.5\times$ its contractual target).

### 2. Epistemic Uncertainty Penalizing Non-Native-Language Support (NNLS)
- **Risk:** Small validation sample size for NNLS (I-09) yields wide intervals for both calibration and $\Delta p$. Risk-averse decision rules ("use lower confidence bound") systematically exclude sparse minority segments, perpetuating both under-service and permanent data scarcity.
- **Concrete safeguards:**
  - **Upper-Confidence-Bound (UCB) / Exploration bonus ($E_i$):** For under-sampled segments ($N_{\text{val}} < 200$), evaluate eligibility using midpoint-plus-exploration bonus rather than worst-case lower bounds, deliberately acquiring outcome observations until calibration uncertainty narrows to within $\pm 0.05$.
  - **Domain-specific diagnostic support:** Pair NNLS swarms with bilingual/translation-ready triage checklists and record language-barrier delay as an explicit model feature so communication friction is not misattributed to low technical resolvability.

### 3. Differential Calibration Across Segments
- **Risk:** Unadjusted scores over-allocate swarms to Strategic Enterprise (overpredicted by 0.05–0.12 after workflow redesign) and under-allocate to SME (underpredicted by 0.04–0.09), worsening both economic waste (excess Enterprise false alarms) and SME breach rates.
- **Concrete safeguards:**
  - **Mandatory segment-stratified post-hoc calibration:** Fit separate isotonic or Platt recalibration layers per segment (with hierarchical Bayesian shrinkage for sparse segments like NNLS), and block deployment of uncalibrated raw probabilities into the decision rule.
  - **Workflow-change recalibration gate:** Any operational workflow redesign (such as the Strategic Enterprise redesign) automatically flags that segment's calibration layer as *stale*, triggering an interim empirical bias offset within 7 days and full refitting within 30 days.

### 4. Selective Intervention Corrupting Future Training Labels (Performative Feedback Loop)
- **Risk:** When high-risk cases are swarmed and consequently do *not* breach, naive retraining treats them as negative labels ($Y=0$). The next model iteration learns that those high-risk patterns are "safe," suppressing future breach predictions and silently dismantling the early-warning system. Conversely, untreated segments accumulate true breaches, exaggerating apparent segment disparities.
- **Concrete safeguards:**
  - **Treatment-aware logging & model separation:** Log treatment assignment $A_i \in \{0,1\}$, swarm timestamp, and policy propensity score $e(x_i) = P(A_i=1 \mid x_i)$ for every case. Maintain two separate models: a **baseline untreated-risk model** $P(Y=1 \mid X, A=0)$ and a **treatment-effect model** $\tau(X) = P(Y=1 \mid X, A=0) - P(Y=1 \mid X, A=1)$.
  - **Counterfactual retraining:** Retrain the prognostic risk model exclusively on untreated cases adjusted via **Inverse Probability of Treatment Weighting (IPTW)** or **Doubly Robust (DR)** estimators—never by pooling intervened and non-intervened outcomes as if interventions had not occurred.
  - **Borderline randomized holdout:** Randomly withhold swarms (with full clinical/ethical guardrails excluding extreme emergencies) on 10–15% of near-threshold marginal cases to guarantee overlap ($0 < e(x) < 1$) for unbiased retraining.

### 5. Spillover Harms Falling Systematically on Untreated Groups
- **Risk:** As shown in the Base scenario (0.58 direct avoided breaches vs. 0.18–0.54 spillover breaches), swarming high-value Enterprise cases shifts diagnosis-queue delays onto non-intervened cases—disproportionately SME and Public Sector tickets—creating a hidden cross-segment risk transfer.
- **Concrete safeguards:**
  - **Displacement-compensated queue aging:** Whenever a swarm pulls senior specialists off the ordinary diagnosis queue, grant an automatic **priority-aging boost** in the standard queue to the untreated diagnosis cases whose wait time increased, especially those closest to SLA breach.
  - **Segment-stratified spillover cap:** Track queue wait-time inflation by segment; trigger the congestion circuit-breaker (halting discretionary swarms) if median diagnosis wait time for untreated SME or Public Sector cases increases by $> 25\%$ above baseline during swarm hours.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

A live intervention policy must monitor not only model accuracy, but causal lift, queue externalities, capacity economics, and segment equity.

### 1. Five-Pillar Monitoring Framework

| Pillar | Key Metrics | Cadence | Red-Flag Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Operational** | Swarm duration (target $\le 45\text{ min}$); specialist interruption hours; **aggregate delay added to diagnosis queue** (vs. 8–20 min assumption); alert suppression rate; false-alarm customer complaint rate; missing/imputed feature rate (like I-06). | Real-time / Daily | Queue delay $> 20\text{ min/slot}$; missingness on any core feature $> 2\%$; FA complaints exceed budgeted burden by $> 25\%$. |
| **Predictive** | Segment-stratified **calibration slope & intercept**, Expected Calibration Error (ECE), Brier score, and feature Population Stability Index (PSI)—evaluated on **untreated/IPTW-adjusted cases**. | Weekly (Monthly for sparse NNLS) | Segment calibration bias $|\bar{p} - \bar{y}| > 0.04$ (rolling 2–4 weeks) or PSI $> 0.20$ after a workflow change. |
| **Causal-Effect** | Estimated Conditional Average Treatment Effect ($\widehat{\text{CATE}}$) by segment and stage (`Triage` vs. `Diagnosis Started`); empirical spillover slope ($\Delta p_{\text{untreated}}$ per concurrent swarm under low/high congestion). | Monthly / Quarterly | Observed $\widehat{\text{CATE}}$ falls below the lower bound of assumed $\Delta p$ ranges, or spillover exceeds $0.03$/case/slot. |
| **Capacity** | Daily slot utilization; **reserve-slot hit rate** (how often the pre-15:00 regulatory incident arrives and uses Slot 4); regret from waiting (e.g., how often Wait cases like I-08 hit `Diagnosis Complete` before re-evaluation). | Weekly | Reserve slot unused $> 85\%$ of days with high forgone EV, or $> 15\%$ of Watchlist cases expire unswarmed and breach. |
| **Fairness** | Swarm allocation share vs. breach-risk share by segment; **spillover-adjusted breach rate** per segment; NNLS validation sample accumulation and confidence-interval width; SME lightweight-triage resolution rate. | Biweekly / Monthly | Any segment's rolling breach rate exceeds $1.5\times$ SLA target, or untreated SME/Public queue wait rises $> 25\%$ during swarm bursts. |

### 2. Plan for Estimating True Causal Intervention Effects & Spillovers
Because observational $p_{\text{now}}$ cannot reveal causal lift $\Delta p$, implement a three-tier identification strategy:
1. **Borderline Tie-Breaking Randomization (Gold Standard):** When multiple Tier-2/marginal cases have $\text{PANEV}_i$ within $\pm €1{,}000$ of the cutoff (or compete for the final slot), use logged probabilistic tie-breaking to assign the slot. Over 2–3 months, this yields an unbiased local causal estimate of $\Delta p$ without denying clearly high-value emergencies.
2. **Capacity-Cutoff Regression Discontinuity (RD) & Doubly Robust CATE:** Exploit natural daily variation in available slots (e.g., 3 vs. 4 slots, or whether the 15:00 regulatory incident arrived) as an instrumental/RD shift in treatment probability. Combine with **Causal Forests / Doubly Robust estimators** using logged propensities to update segment- and stage-specific $\Delta p$ ranges.
3. **Congestion-Indexed Spillover Modeling:** Measure untreated diagnosis-stage breach rates as a function of concurrent swarm count and queue depth (exploiting quasi-random timing of swarm dispatches). Replace the static `0.01–0.03 × 6 cases` rule with a dynamic congestion-dependent spillover curve $\text{Spill}(k, \text{queue\_depth})$.

### 3. Detecting Policy-Induced Drift & Decision Triggers for Policy Change
To distinguish genuine operational improvement from **policy-induced label drift**:
- **Drift diagnostic:** Compare predicted vs. observed breach rates separately on (a) the randomized borderline holdout arm, (b) unswarmed cases weighted by IPTW, and (c) swarmed cases. If raw global calibration looks "too high" only on high-score bins while the untreated holdout remains well-calibrated, the shift is **treatment confounding**, not model degradation—do *not* naively recalibrate downward.
- **Concrete governance triggers to revise thresholds or allocation rules:**
  1. **Recalibrate segment offsets** whenever untreated/IPTW calibration bias exceeds $\pm 0.04$ for two consecutive weeks (immediately required now for Strategic Enterprise and SME).
  2. **Update $\Delta p$ priors & stage gates** quarterly once $\ge 50$ treated/comparable-control pairs accumulate per segment, or immediately if `Triage` swarms underperform `Diagnosis Started` swarms by $> 50\%$ (which would justify shifting more cases to Wait until `Diagnosis Started`).
  3. **Adjust reserve-slot policy** if 60-day tracking shows the regulatory arrival probability is $< 15\%$ (release reserve earlier, e.g., at 13:30) or $> 50\%$ with high penalties (make the 1-slot reserve mandatory).
  4. **Tighten spillover shadow price / congestion circuit-breaker** if observed queue delay exceeds 20 min/slot in $> 20\%$ of swarm windows, or if adverse-scenario net-negative days occur more than once per month.
  5. **Recalibrate fairness weights** if quarterly audits show spillover-adjusted breach disparities widening across SME or NNLS segments, increasing protected capacity or concave value compression until parity bounds are restored.
