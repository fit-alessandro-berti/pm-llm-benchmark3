## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

I treat the flag as part of a policy bundle, not as a magical manipulable cause. I therefore prefer **total policy effects**, **controlled or separable component effects**, and **interventional mediation analogues** over natural direct/indirect effects, because natural effects would require cross-world claims such as “the same case has no flag but keeps the manual-review path it would have had under the flag,” which is fragile with shared queues and exposure-induced routing. The supplied process and intervention definitions are the basis for the contrasts below. 

| estimand ID | intervention contrast                                                                              | unit and population                                                | outcome                                                   | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue                            | well-defined?                                               | main identification obstacle                                                                          |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| E1          | P1 suppress flag display vs P0 current system                                                      | All post-introduction arrivals eligible to have the flag displayed | Total cycle time, arrival to close                        | Total policy effect of removing the display-and-handling cue                | Yes as a randomized display policy; no as “set risk to low” | Flag is derived from risk score; underlying risk, complexity, and human information remain            |
| E2          | Priority lane on vs priority lane off, while keeping current downstream routing rules              | Flag-eligible cases at arrival                                     | Arrival to triage completion                              | Controlled or separable triage-component effect                             | Yes as a workflow intervention                              | Triage resource assignment and calendar congestion differ between flagged and unflagged cases         |
| E3          | P2 retain priority triage but remove automatic mandatory manual review vs P0                       | Flagged cases after triage                                         | Total cycle time and triage-done-to-close time            | Interventional indirect analogue through manual-review occurrence           | Yes as a policy; not a natural indirect effect              | Manual review may still occur by human judgment; risk affects both routing and outcome                |
| E4          | P3 add protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases vs P0                                    | Flagged cases that enter manual review                             | Manual queue wait and total cycle time                    | Interventional indirect analogue through manual-review waiting time         | Yes if the added capacity is real and measured              | Shared queues create interference; one case’s capacity protection changes other cases’ waits          |
| E5          | P4 change escalation criteria or senior capacity vs P0                                             | Reviewed cases that are escalation-eligible                        | Escalation occurrence, escalation wait, cycle time        | Interventional indirect analogue through escalation                         | Yes if criteria/capacity are auditable                      | Escalation is highly confounded by risk, complexity, and decision difficulty                          |
| E6          | Flag display effect while holding manual-review and escalation occurrence fixed to a chosen regime | Flagged or near-threshold cases                                    | Cycle time after triage                                   | Controlled direct effect of display/priority not through routing occurrence | Partly; operationally artificial                            | Holding mediators fixed can be incoherent when queues and staff behavior respond to policy            |
| E7          | P2 or P4 vs P0                                                                                     | Risk-eligible cases with later quality labels                      | Decision outcome, reversal, appeal, missed-risk indicator | Total policy effect on decision quality/compliance                          | Yes only if quality is measured beyond Approve/Reject       | Observed outcome is a decision, not ground truth; rejected/approved cases have different verification |
| E8          | P3 or P2 vs P0, measured on unflagged cases                                                        | Unflagged cases sharing review and escalation queues               | Unflagged queue waits and total cycle time                | Interference or spillover effect                                            | Yes under cluster/time-block policy definition              | SUTVA is false at case level; capacity reallocation can help or harm other cases                      |
| E9          | Naive “set `flag=0` in the data” while leaving actual operations unchanged                         | Recorded cases                                                     | Any process or quality outcome                            | Ill-defined causal contrast                                                 | No                                                          | The recorded flag is not the same as the organization’s response to seeing it                         |

Interference arises because manual-review and escalation queues are shared: routing one flagged case into review can lengthen another case’s waiting time. Exposure-induced confounding can occur after triage because the flag affects manual review, and manual review reveals information that then affects escalation, service duration, and outcome. Treatment-confounder feedback is plausible if early priority triage changes the information available, the reviewer assigned, or the queue state observed by staff, and those post-flag variables then influence later routing. `arrival_load` is only a coarse arrival snapshot, so unmeasured queue state can confound downstream waiting even after adjusting for observed load.

## 3. Stage decomposition

Values are **median hours**. For optional stages, absent stages are **not** entered as zero. A stage-specific median uses only cases with both the relevant start and completion timestamps. `NA` means no case in that subgroup had that stage. `residual-to-close` is close minus the latest observed prior completion: triage done, manual done, or escalation done. The data use local timestamps and dashes for non-occurring stages.

The observed pattern is consistent with acceleration at triage followed by downstream delay. Flagged cases reach triage much faster than unflagged cases, and their triage service median is also shorter. But every flagged case enters manual review, compared with only four of ten unflagged cases, and six of ten flagged cases are escalated, compared with two of ten unflagged cases. The dominant clock time for flagged cases is therefore not triage; it is waiting after triage, especially triage-done-to-manual-start and, for escalated cases, manual-done-to-escalation-start. However, this is not automatically causal. Flagged cases have higher risk scores, no low-complexity cases, and many high-complexity cases. Reviewers and triage resources also differ by flag status. The same pattern could reflect the policy bundle, true risk/complexity, queue congestion, resource assignment, or all of them.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question                                                     | proposed design or analysis                                                                                                                                         | adjustment or stratification set                                                                               | assumptions                                                                                            | diagnostic or falsification test                                                                                                     | result that would change your conclusion                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Is the flag bundle causing longer cycles near the threshold? | Local-threshold or within-risk-score design around the operational flag cutoff; compare just-above and just-below cases                                             | Risk score bandwidth, complexity, arrival_load, calendar time, resource availability                           | Cases cannot precisely manipulate score; unobserved risk is smooth at threshold                        | Check balance in complexity, arrival times, and pre-triage covariates; look for discontinuities in triage wait and review occurrence | If downstream waits do not jump at the threshold while review occurrence does, the flag-bundle delay story weakens     |
| Is downstream delay a queue-state effect?                    | Queue-state-aware survival/regression model for each interval: triage wait, manual queue wait, escalation queue wait                                                | Exact queue length/WIP at each stage start, staff on duty, calendar time, arrival_load, complexity, risk_score | Queue state is measured before the interval and not post-outcome                                       | If flagged coefficient shrinks after queue-state adjustment, queueing explains much of the contrast                                  | If flag remains large after exact queue and resource controls, direct handling or hidden risk becomes more plausible   |
| What does P2 identify?                                       | Randomized encouragement: after priority triage, eligible flagged cases are assigned to default manual review vs default no mandatory review, with override allowed | Stratify by risk-score band, complexity, arrival_load, triage_resource, time block                             | Assignment affects cycle time mainly through manual-review occurrence, except for documented overrides | First stage: review occurrence falls in P2 arm; check override rates and balance                                                     | If review occurrence barely changes, P2 is not a real intervention in practice                                         |
| What does P3 identify?                                       | Time-block or team-level capacity pilot adding protected manual-review slots for flagged cases                                                                      | Time block, staffing, incoming load, complexity/risk mix                                                       | Added capacity is additive, not stolen from unflagged queues                                           | Compare flagged and unflagged queue waits in treatment vs control blocks                                                             | If flagged waits fall but unflagged waits rise materially, the policy is redistributive rather than capacity-improving |
| Can mediation be decomposed without natural effects?         | Interventional g-computation or sequential simulation: set mediator distributions for review occurrence, review wait, escalation to policy-specific regimes         | Baseline risk_score, complexity, arrival_load, calendar time, resource, measured queue state                   | Sequential exchangeability within measured histories; positivity for mediator regimes                  | Compare simulated vs observed stage distributions under P0; perform mediator-specific posterior predictive checks                    | If the model cannot reproduce observed stage timings, mediation estimates are not credible                             |
| Are unobserved risk factors driving everything?              | Sensitivity analysis adding hypothetical `U_underlying_risk` that affects flag, review, escalation, and cycle time                                                  | Observed risk_score, complexity, outcome, manual/escalation routing                                            | Plausible range for hidden-risk effect can be bounded from audit or later quality data                 | Vary strength of hidden-risk confounding until the P2/P3 effect disappears                                                           | If modest hidden risk explains the whole cycle-time contrast, avoid claiming a flag-handling mechanism                 |
| Is there a negative-control signal?                          | Use a negative-control exposure or outcome, such as a synthetic non-operational flag, or a pre-routing timestamp interval that the displayed flag cannot affect     | Calendar time, arrival_load, complexity, resource schedules                                                    | Negative control shares confounding but cannot be causally affected by flag display                    | A real flag should not predict negative-control outcomes after adjustment                                                            | If it does, residual temporal/resource confounding is likely                                                           |
| How large are interference effects?                          | Interference-aware design using time-block randomization and queue simulation, estimating direct effects on treated cases and spillovers to untreated cases         | Block-level arrivals, staff, WIP, service capacity, risk mix                                                   | Within-block policies affect shared queues; cross-block interference limited                           | Check whether unflagged manual/escalation waits shift when flagged policy changes                                                    | If spillovers dominate direct benefits, case-level causal estimates are misleading                                     |

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy                                                               | expected triage effect                                                                     | expected downstream queue effect                                                         | expected cycle-time effect                                                                        | quality/compliance risk                                                             | capacity spillover                                                                   | evidence strength                                                                     | recommendation                                                      |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| P0: keep current system                                              | Fast triage for flagged cases                                                              | High manual-review and escalation burden for flagged cases                               | Flagged cases remain slow overall despite early acceleration                                      | Conservative if review/escalation are valuable; inefficient if over-routing         | Shared queues may delay both flagged and unflagged reviewed cases                    | Descriptive evidence only; 20 cases                                                   | Keep only as baseline/control condition                             |
| P1: suppress flag display, keep risk score and human information     | Likely removes or weakens priority triage, so flagged-risk cases may wait longer initially | May reduce flag-triggered automatic routing, but humans may still route high-score cases | Ambiguous: slower triage may offset less mandatory review                                         | Risk of inconsistent recognition of high-risk cases                                 | Could relieve review queues if display was driving over-review                       | Weak; not isolated from human use of risk score                                       | Not preferred alone; useful only as a display experiment            |
| P2: retain priority triage, remove automatic mandatory manual review | Preserves early acceleration                                                               | Should reduce review volume for borderline flagged cases if overrides are not universal  | Likely shorter for medium/borderline flagged cases; limited effect on truly high-risk escalations | Main risk is missed review or undocumented discretionary routing                    | Likely frees manual and possibly senior capacity                                     | Moderate descriptive support for occurrence pathway, but not causal                   | Preferred limited pilot with strict quality guardrails              |
| P3: retain routing, add protected manual-review capacity             | Preserves early acceleration                                                               | Should reduce manual queue wait for flagged reviewed cases                               | Shorter if manual queue is the main bottleneck; less effect if escalation dominates               | Lower quality risk than P2 because review remains                                   | Positive only if capacity is additive; harmful if unflagged capacity is cannibalized | Moderate descriptive support for waiting pathway                                      | Good operational fallback if extra capacity is available            |
| P4: retain manual review, change escalation criteria or capacity     | No major triage change                                                                     | Targets senior-team bottleneck and escalation occurrence/wait                            | High potential for escalated high-complexity cases                                                | Criteria relaxation can create serious missed-risk risk; capacity addition is safer | May free or overload senior capacity depending on design                             | Descriptive support from escalated vs non-escalated contrast, but strongly confounded | Pilot only with audit-quality labels and senior-capacity monitoring |

Selected limited pilot: **P2 as a randomized encouragement, not a blanket removal of review**.

* **Eligibility:** newly flagged, medium-complexity cases with risk scores near the lower/middle flagged range, no hard regulatory review trigger, and no triage-discovered red flag requiring review. Exclude high-complexity cases and very high risk scores.
* **Randomization or assignment rule:** after priority triage, randomize eligible cases 1:1 to P0 default manual review or P2 default no mandatory review. Human override is allowed but must record a reason.
* **Queue-capacity guardrail:** monitor manual and senior WIP by time block; pause if unflagged waits rise materially or if senior backlog exceeds staffed capacity.
* **Primary and secondary outcomes:** primary: triage-done-to-close and total cycle time without quality degradation. Secondary: manual occurrence, manual queue wait, escalation, override rate, appeals, rework, audit failures, and unflagged queue waits.
* **Minimum process data needed:** all stage start/done timestamps, exact queue length at each stage start, resource, risk score, complexity, override reason, decision outcome, and later quality labels.
* **Stopping conditions:** excess adverse decisions, missed escalations, compliance breach, high override rate, severe spillover, or timestamp/data-quality failure.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk:** If unobserved underlying risk strongly explains both being flagged and requiring long manual/escalation work, then the preferred interpretation would shift from policy-induced downstream delay toward justified risk handling.

2. **Queue interference:** If reducing automatic review for eligible flagged cases does not reduce shared manual or senior waits, or harms unflagged cases through queue reallocation, then P2 would be less attractive than capacity-focused P3/P4.

3. **Decision quality:** If manual review or escalation substantially reduces bad approvals, bad rejections, fraud loss, appeals, or audit failures, then shorter cycle time would not justify removing mandatory review.

4. **Timestamp semantics:** If timestamps mainly reflect batch data entry, local clock artifacts, or off-shift recording rather than actual start/completion, then the stage-wait decomposition would no longer support the queue mechanism.

5. **Positivity or lack of overlap:** If flagged high/medium cases have no comparable unflagged or near-threshold cases in risk, complexity, arrival time, or reviewer availability, then local causal estimates could not support a broader policy change.

6. **Reviewer behavior change:** If reviewers respond to P2 by performing informal undocumented checks, overusing overrides, or escalating more defensively, then the intended manual-occurrence intervention would not actually be delivered.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed:** In the supplied 20 cases, flagged cases have faster arrival-to-triage-start and slightly shorter triage service, but longer total cycles. They are much more often manually reviewed and escalated; for reviewed/escalated cases, downstream waits dominate the clock time. Direct observation also includes resource separation: flagged triage is handled by T1/T2, while unflagged triage is handled by T3/T4.

**Plausible mechanism:** The flag is not itself a delay cause; it activates a policy bundle. Priority triage accelerates early handling, then mandatory review and senior escalation move cases into scarce shared queues. High complexity and unobserved risk probably amplify both routing and service times.

**Unidentified:** Without randomized or quasi-random flag handling, we cannot separate flag display from risk, complexity, reviewer choice, hidden case facts, or queue interference. Natural direct/indirect effects are especially fragile because manual review and escalation can change later confounding and other cases’ queues.

**Most useful next evidence:** A guarded P2 randomized-encouragement pilot on medium-complexity borderline flagged cases. It retains priority triage but tests whether automatic review occurrence, rather than risk alone, drives downstream cycle time while tracking quality, overrides, and queue spillovers.
