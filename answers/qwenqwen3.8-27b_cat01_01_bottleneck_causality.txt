## 1. Executive causal assessment

The crude Orion–Nova contrast (21.96 vs 15.24 h) is not credible as evidence that Orion causes longer completion. Within every observed complexity stratum Orion is faster: high 31.57 vs 35.00, medium 17.17 vs 19.38, low 7.50 vs 9.64. The same reversal appears for queue wait (12.80 vs 8.79 crude; within complexity Orion lower: 19.47 vs 22.75 high, 9.25 vs 11.13 medium, 3.45 vs 5.06 low) and review service (7.04 vs 5.04 crude; within complexity roughly equal or lower). A total Orion effect cannot be separated from direct capacity/skill effects and mediated queue/rework effects. This pattern is classic confounding by case mix: Orion receives more high-complexity, compliance-required, night, high-load cases. I therefore provisionally classify the observed longer Orion times as mainly confounded, confidence 70/100. Strongest evidence against a harmful Orion effect is the within-complexity reversal and equal/lower rework and similar 30-day appeals. Strongest evidence for residual confounding is no overlap for Orion day medium/high, only completed cases, and unmeasured case risk/reviewer skill. The proposed 50% reassignment may succeed only if moved cases are low/medium day cases where Nova has capacity; it may fail or backfire if it transfers high-complexity, compliance, night, or high-load cases to a group with less experience/capacity, increasing queue wait, rework, or abandonment.

def df_to_xes(sub):
    parts = ['<?xml version="1.0" encoding="UTF-8"?>', "<log>"]
    for _, r in sub.iterrows():
        parts.append(f'<trace id="{escape(str(r["case_id"]))}">')
        for act in str(r["trace"]).split(">"):
            act = act.strip()
            if act:
                parts.append(f'<event><string key="concept:name" value="{escape(act)}"/></event>')
        parts.append("</trace>")
    parts.append("</log>")
    return "\n".join(parts)

def normalize_edge_value(v):
    if isinstance(v, dict):
        return v.get("weight", v.get("count", 1))
    return v

def get_dfg_edges(dfg):
    if hasattr(dfg, "edges"):
        raw = dfg.edges
    elif isinstance(dfg, dict):
        raw = dfg.get("edges", dfg)
    else:
        raw = dfg

    if isinstance(raw, list):
        raw = {tuple(x): 1 for x in raw}

    return {tuple(k): normalize_edge_value(v) for k, v in raw.items()}

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. Unobserved case risk and complexity granularity. Most valuable addition: a detailed case-risk score or structured complexity features captured at routing, such as claim amount, injury severity, policy type, or prior history.
2. Differential completion, abandonment, or still-open cases. Most valuable addition: the full case population with status and timestamps for open, abandoned, and closed cases, including reason for non-completion.
3. Queue state and staffing capacity by group and shift. Most valuable addition: real-time queue length, staffing/certification counts, and arrival timestamps by group/shift to reconstruct wait mechanisms.
4. Randomized or quasi-random routing variation. Most valuable design change: a small randomized routing pilot with pre-specified eligible strata and group assignment, enabling a causal estimate rather than observational matching.
5. Activity-level timing decomposition. Most valuable addition: event-level timestamps for Register, Classify, Compliance Check, Review, Request Info, and Close to separate queueing, compliance service, and rework service.
