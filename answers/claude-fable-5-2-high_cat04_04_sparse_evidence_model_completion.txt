# 1. Evidence Ledger

Abbreviations used throughout: RO = Register Order, CC = Credit Check, ER = Engineering Review, SC = Supplier Confirmation, XR = Expedite Review, AC = Approve Configuration, RP = Release Production, AR = Archive Case. "a ≺ b" means "the completion event of a is logged before the completion event of b".

Headline counts derived from the 30 traces (7 variants):

- CC logged before ER in 19 cases, ER before CC in 11 cases.
- SC present in 23 cases, absent in 7 (4 with CC first, 3 with ER first).
- When SC is present, it follows CC in 23/23 cases, follows ER in 21/23 cases, and precedes ER in 2/23 cases. SC never precedes CC.
- XR present in 2 cases: always after both checks and before AC, once after SC and once before SC, never without SC.
- RO first and the suffix AC, RP, AR last in 30/30 cases; no activity is ever repeated.

Why two DFG directions do not prove concurrency. The pair (CC, ER) appears as both (CC, ER): 17 and (ER, CC): 11. At least five different processes generate exactly this footprint: (a) truly parallel work; (b) an exclusive choice between two strictly sequential routings, for example driven by customer type; (c) overlapping work in which only the completion order varies; (d) a strict sequence combined with coarse timestamps and ingestion-order ties (S7); (e) a mixture of sub-populations each having a fixed order. For two atomic activities the languages of `+(CC, ER)` and `X(->(CC, ER), ->(ER, CC))` are identical, so no completion-only log, however large, can separate (a) from (b); only start timestamps, resource data, or a case attribute that predicts the order can. The DFG also discards case context: it cannot show that SC ≺ ER occurs only when CC came first, or that both XR traces also contain SC. The asymmetric pairs (ER⇄SC 13/2, SC⇄XR 1/1) are weaker still, because the minority direction rests on one or two cases. I therefore treat "both directions observed" as "no evidence for a fixed order", which is weaker than "concurrent".

Three small calculations calibrate how much the absences mean:

- Missing SC: if 5–10% of true SC events are lost, 23 observed events imply about 24–26 true ones, so roughly 1–3 of the 7 SC-less cases are artifacts and 4–6 are genuine skips. If SC were mandatory for all 30 cases, at least 7 losses would have probability about 0.03 at a 10% loss rate and below 0.001 at 5%. Missingness alone is an unlikely explanation, but any individual SC-less case has roughly a 15–40% chance of being an artifact.
- Unobserved ER SC CC: SC overtook the second check in 2 of 15 CC-first cases with SC. If the same rate held for the 8 ER-first cases with SC, seeing zero such traces has probability about 0.32. The absence barely discriminates.
- XR never seen without SC: under independence both XR cases would contain SC with probability about (23/30)² ≈ 0.59. Also uninformative.

# 2. Shared Assumptions

1. Event-completion semantics. Every event is a completion. An edge a ≺ b claims only "a completes before b completes"; it does not claim that a finished before b started. D5 ("may begin after") is therefore checked only through its necessary consequence: SC completes after at least one check has completed.
2. Unordered does not mean parallel. Where a model leaves two activities unordered, it asserts that both completion orders are acceptable. It is silent on whether the work overlapped (D7).
3. Tie-breaking (S7) is not modeled. Orders seen in only one or two cases (SC ≺ ER, XR ≺ SC) are accepted as real but flagged as fragile.
4. Missing SC events are represented as a control-flow alternative. Every model that allows an SC skip uses the same path for a stocked component (S4), an SC event lost in the integration (S5), and an external component processed without confirmation (a violation of S3). This is a disclosed abstraction: a fitting SC-less trace is "compatible with the model", not "verified compliant".
5. The external/stocked attribute is absent. No data guard can be attached to the SC choice, and S3 cannot be verified by any of the three models.
6. Optionality is represented as `X(activity, tau)` in the POWL models and as separate enumerated branches in the conservative tree. The SC and XR choices are independent in the balanced and permissive models; the conservative model keeps the observed co-occurrence.
7. Scope. All 30 cases are complete, approved, and archived. Rejections, cancellations, rework loops, and repeated activities have no evidence in the log or in D1–D7, so all three models exclude them. This may be an export-filter artifact.
8. Terminology. "Underfitting risk" is used broadly for ways the model may fail to capture the real process: under-generalization (rejecting legitimate behavior because the sample is small) and under-constraint (admitting behavior that a real rule forbids).
9. The three candidates are nested hypotheses, L(conservative) ⊂ L(balanced) ⊂ L(permissive), with 7, 12, and 14 traces respectively. The nesting is deliberate: each step up adds one identifiable group of contested traces, so disagreement between models localizes exactly which assumption matters.

# 3. Conservative Candidate

**Inductive-bias statement.** The log is treated as complete: a behavior is admitted only if it was observed as a whole trace. No pairwise order relation is recombined and no optional activity is moved to a context in which it was not seen. The implicit claims are strong and probably accidental: SC precedes ER only when CC came first, XR occurs only together with SC, and the XR/SC order is tied to the CC/ER order. The model is a closed-world, zero-generalization hypothesis whose value is as a novelty detector, not as a description of the process.

**Compact formal expression.**

```text
->( RO,
    X( ->(CC, ER, SC),
       ->(ER, CC, SC),
       ->(CC, ER),
       ->(ER, CC),
       ->(CC, SC, ER),
       ->(CC, ER, SC, XR),
       ->(ER, CC, XR, SC) ),
    AC, RP, AR )
```

The language is exactly the 7 observed variants.

**PM4Py code (ProcessTree).**

```python
import pm4py

observed_middle_parts = [
    ["Credit Check", "Engineering Review", "Supplier Confirmation"],                     # 12 cases
    ["Engineering Review", "Credit Check", "Supplier Confirmation"],                     #  7 cases
    ["Credit Check", "Engineering Review"],                                              #  4 cases
    ["Engineering Review", "Credit Check"],                                              #  3 cases
    ["Credit Check", "Supplier Confirmation", "Engineering Review"],                     #  2 cases
    ["Credit Check", "Engineering Review", "Supplier Confirmation", "Expedite Review"],  #  1 case
    ["Engineering Review", "Credit Check", "Expedite Review", "Supplier Confirmation"],  #  1 case
]
branches = ", ".join(
    "->( " + ", ".join("'%s'" % activity for activity in part) + " )"
    for part in observed_middle_parts
)
model_conservative = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "->( 'Register Order', X( " + branches + " ), "
    "'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case' )"
)
```

Duplicate labels and scalability. The shared prefix and suffix are factored out, but the choice block still contains CC ×7, ER ×7, SC ×5, and XR ×2. The resulting Petri net has 25 visible transitions for 8 activities. Three consequences follow:

- The branch must be chosen before the trace reveals which branch is right (four branches start with CC), so replay needs look-ahead; alignments handle this, token-based replay does not do so reliably.
- The model grows linearly with the number of variants, and the number of variants grows factorially with real concurrency, so every new legitimate variant requires a model edit.
- The same language can be written as a prefix tree with fewer duplicates, `X( ->(CC, X( ->(ER, X(tau, ->(SC, X(tau, XR)))), ->(SC, ER) )), ->(ER, CC, X(tau, SC, ->(XR, SC))) )`, which is smaller but harder to audit against the variant list.

**Behavior deliberately excluded.**

- XR without SC (RO CC ER XR AC RP AR and RO ER CC XR AC RP AR).
- The mirror images of the two XR traces (RO CC ER XR SC … and RO ER CC SC XR …).
- SC before ER combined with XR (RO CC SC ER XR …).
- SC before CC in any form (RO ER SC CC …).
- Everything outside D1–D6: repeats, a missing check, SC or XR after AC, XR before both checks.

**Known underfitting risks.**

- Under-generalization: with 2 singleton variants in 30 cases, the Good–Turing estimate of the probability that the next case shows an unseen variant is about 7%, with a wide interval. Expect false alarms on legitimate cases, concentrated in expedited orders.
- It encodes coincidences as rules (XR requires SC; XR/SC order tied to check order), although D6 and S6 say otherwise.
- It inherits the missing-SC ambiguity through the two SC-less branches.
- It offers no explanation of why the variants exist, so it cannot support redesign or what-if analysis.

# 4. Balanced Candidate

**Inductive-bias statement.** A trace is admitted if (i) every activity pair in it appears in an order that was observed at least once somewhere in the log (eventually-follows closure), (ii) no rule of medium or higher confidence forbids it, and (iii) the two optional activities are treated as independent choices. The model recombines observed pairwise orders but does not invent an order that has never been seen merely because a low-confidence rule allows it. The result is one partial order with 12 traces. Its five traces without XR are exactly the five observed non-expedite variants, so all of its generalization is concentrated in expedited cases, where only 2 of 7 possible orderings have been seen.

**Compact formal expression.**

```text
->( RO, CORE, AC, RP, AR )

CORE = PO( nodes = { CC, ER, X(SC, tau), X(XR, tau) },
           order = { CC ≺ X(SC, tau),
                     CC ≺ X(XR, tau),
                     ER ≺ X(XR, tau) } )
```

CC and ER are unordered, ER and SC are unordered, SC and XR are unordered.

**PM4Py code (POWL).**

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

ro = Transition(label="Register Order")
cc = Transition(label="Credit Check")
er = Transition(label="Engineering Review")
ac = Transition(label="Approve Configuration")
rp = Transition(label="Release Production")
ar = Transition(label="Archive Case")

# Optional activities. The silent branch of sc_opt stands for "not required"
# AND for "event not logged"; the model cannot tell these apart.
sc_opt = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.XOR, [Transition(label="Supplier Confirmation"), SilentTransition()]
)
xr_opt = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.XOR, [Transition(label="Expedite Review"), SilentTransition()]
)

core = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[cc, er, sc_opt, xr_opt])
core.order.add_edge(cc, sc_opt)   # soft edge: 23/23 observed, D5 would also allow ER alone
core.order.add_edge(cc, xr_opt)   # D6, S6, 2/2 observed
core.order.add_edge(er, xr_opt)   # D6, S6, 2/2 observed
# no edge cc-er, er-sc_opt, sc_opt-xr_opt: both completion orders are accepted

model_balanced = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[ro, core, ac, rp, ar])
model_balanced.order.add_edge(ro, core)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(core, ac)   # D3, D4, D6: everything in core precedes approval
model_balanced.order.add_edge(ac, rp)     # D2
model_balanced.order.add_edge(rp, ar)     # D1
```

**How sequence, concurrency, and partial order were decided.** One rule is applied to every pair:

1. Add an edge a ≺ b if a rule of at least medium confidence demands it and the log has no counterexample.
2. Leave the pair unordered if both orders were observed and no rule of at least medium confidence forbids either.
3. If only one order was observed and the reverse is supported only by low or low-to-medium evidence, keep the edge but mark it as soft, to be tested by the permissive model.
4. A stakeholder statement that the log contradicts as a description is not encoded.

| Pair | Evidence | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| CC, ER | 19 versus 11; D7 unknown; S1 contradicted by 11 cases; S2 consistent | Unordered. This is an interleaving of completions, not a claim of real parallelism |
| CC, SC | CC ≺ SC in 23/23; reverse supported only by D5 (low–medium) | Soft edge CC ≺ SC |
| ER, SC | 21 versus 2; D5 supports SC not waiting for both | Unordered |
| CC, XR and ER, XR | 2/2 each; D6 and S6 | Edges CC ≺ XR and ER ≺ XR |
| SC, XR | 1 versus 1; S6 explicitly allows both | Unordered |
| Anything in the core, AC | D3, D4, D6; 30/30 | Sequence core → AC |
| SC with XR co-occurrence | Both XR cases contain SC, but that has probability about 0.59 under independence; no rule links them | Independent choices |

A partial order is needed rather than a process tree because the resulting relation (CC ≺ SC, CC ≺ XR, ER ≺ XR, with ER and SC unordered) is the N-shaped pattern that is not series-parallel. A block-structured tree can only approximate it by duplicating labels or by over-generalizing; for example `+(CC, ER, X(SC, tau), X(XR, tau))` would admit SC and XR before any check.

**Behavior deliberately excluded.**

- SC completing before CC (RO ER SC CC …), although D5 would allow it.
- SC before both checks; XR before either check; SC or XR after AC.
- A missing CC or ER; repeats of any activity; a second XR.
- A fixed CC → ER sequence as a requirement (S1 is not enforced; ER-first cases are accepted).

**Known underfitting risks.**

- The soft edge CC ≺ SC may be a sampling artifact (probability about 0.32 of seeing no counterexample even if the symmetric behavior exists). If so, the model will raise false alarms on ER SC CC traces.
- Five of its 12 traces are unobserved and all involve XR, a behavior seen twice. If expedite is in reality tied to supplier lead times, the two XR-without-SC traces are over-generalization.
- Independence of the SC and XR choices and of both from the check order is assumed, not tested.
- The SC skip accepts external-component cases without confirmation; S3 is not checkable.
- If S1 is a real control, the model silently accepts 11 violations (Section 10).
- No rework, rejection, or cancellation paths.

# 5. Permissive Candidate

**Inductive-bias statement.** The domain rules, not the log, define the language: every trace that uses the six mandatory activities exactly once, uses SC and XR at most once, and satisfies D1–D6 read literally is admitted, whether or not anything like it was observed. The log serves only as a consistency check. Compared with the balanced model, the only additional freedom is the full disjunctive reading of D5: SC may complete once either check has completed. The result is the largest language consistent with the rules under the "no repetition, no skipped mandatory activity" scope, 14 traces.

**Compact formal expression.**

```text
->( RO, X( CORE_cc, CORE_er ), AC, RP, AR )

CORE_g = PO( nodes = { CC, ER, X(SC, tau), X(XR, tau) },
             order = { g ≺ X(SC, tau),
                       CC ≺ X(XR, tau),
                       ER ≺ X(XR, tau) } )        for g in { CC, ER }
```

Equivalently: all orderings in which SC, if present, is not before both checks, and XR, if present, is after both checks.

**PM4Py code (POWL).**

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def core_with_supplier_gate(gate):
    """Checks plus optional SC and XR; `gate` is the check that SC must follow."""
    cc = Transition(label="Credit Check")
    er = Transition(label="Engineering Review")
    sc_opt = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR, [Transition(label="Supplier Confirmation"), SilentTransition()]
    )
    xr_opt = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR, [Transition(label="Expedite Review"), SilentTransition()]
    )
    core = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[cc, er, sc_opt, xr_opt])
    core.order.add_edge(cc if gate == "CC" else er, sc_opt)  # D5: one check is enough
    core.order.add_edge(cc, xr_opt)                          # D6, S6
    core.order.add_edge(er, xr_opt)                          # D6, S6
    return core


# "SC after CC or after ER" is a disjunctive precondition. A single strict
# partial order cannot express it, so it is modeled as a choice between two
# partial orders with duplicated labels. The two branches overlap on every
# trace in which SC follows both checks or is skipped.
either_gate = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.XOR, [core_with_supplier_gate("CC"), core_with_supplier_gate("ER")]
)

ro = Transition(label="Register Order")
ac = Transition(label="Approve Configuration")
rp = Transition(label="Release Production")
ar = Transition(label="Archive Case")

model_permissive = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[ro, either_gate, ac, rp, ar])
model_permissive.order.add_edge(ro, either_gate)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(either_gate, ac)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(ac, rp)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(rp, ar)
```

**Boundary between reasonable generalization and forbidden behavior.**

- Reasonable: any reordering or recombination that a stated rule explicitly allows, even at low-to-medium confidence and without observation. This covers SC after ER alone (D5), XR without SC (D6 and S6 do not mention SC as a precondition), and every XR/SC order (S6).
- Forbidden: behavior that would require violating a rule of medium or higher confidence, or a structural feature for which there is neither a rule nor an observation.

**Behavior deliberately excluded.**

- SC before both checks (even the literal D5 requires one check).
- XR before CC or before ER (D6, S6); XR twice (D6).
- AC without both checks (D3); SC or XR after AC (D4, D6).
- RP without or before AC (D2); a start other than RO or an end other than AR (D1).
- Repeats, rework loops, rejection and cancellation paths (no evidence of any kind).

**Known underfitting risks.**

- Under-constraint: if credit clearance is a real gate for supplier commitments, the model accepts a control breach (RO ER SC CC …) as normal. That trace rests on D5 alone.
- Seven of its 14 traces are unobserved, so half of the language is extrapolation.
- It has the same SC-skip ambiguity as the other models.
- It is still narrow in other dimensions: it rejects any rework or cancellation, so "permissive" refers to ordering and optionality only.
- Four labels are duplicated and the two branches overlap, so the branch taken in a replay carries no meaning.

# 6. Unobserved Behavior Audit

All three models are acyclic, so their languages are finite. They were enumerated exhaustively by bounded playout (Section 8), giving 7, 12, and 14 traces. The conservative model admits no unobserved trace; this is exact, not merely true within a sample, because the enumeration bound (12 events) exceeds the longest possible trace (8 events).

| Model | Unobserved trace | Why admitted | Plausible, risky, or clearly undesirable | Evidence used |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Balanced | RO CC ER XR AC RP AR | SC and XR are independent choices | Plausible; risky if the missing SC is a lost event | D6, S6, V3, V6 |
| Balanced | RO ER CC XR AC RP AR | Same, with ER first | Plausible; same caveat | D6, S6, V4, V7 |
| Balanced | RO CC ER XR SC AC RP AR | XR and SC unordered | Plausible | S6, V7 (XR ≺ SC), V1 |
| Balanced | RO ER CC SC XR AC RP AR | XR and SC unordered | Plausible | S6, V6 (SC ≺ XR), V2 |
| Balanced | RO CC SC ER XR AC RP AR | ER and SC unordered; XR after both checks | Plausible to risky: every pair was observed, the combination was not | V5, D5, D6 |
| Permissive | The five balanced rows above | Same reasons | Same ratings | Same evidence |
| Permissive | RO ER SC CC AC RP AR | SC gated by ER alone | Risky: supported by D5 only; a control breach if credit must clear before supplier commitment | D5 |

# 7. Comparative Model Matrix

| Criterion | Conservative | Balanced | Permissive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expected fitness on the 30 observed cases | Perfect by construction | Perfect | Perfect |
| Expected fitness on future cases | Lowest; roughly 85–97% of cases if the process is stable | High; fails only if SC before CC, rework, or cancellations occur | Highest of the three; fails only on behavior outside D1–D6 |
| Expected precision risk | None relative to the log (7 of 7 traces observed) | Low: 5 of 12 traces unobserved, all in the rare XR sub-space | Low to moderate: 7 of 14 traces unobserved, one of them outside the XR sub-space |
| Generalization potential | None | Moderate, evidence-bounded | Highest within the rules; still no loops or cancellations |
| Structural simplicity | Worst in content (7 branches, 21 duplicated leaves) although each branch is a trivial sequence | Best: one partial order, each label once, 3 internal order edges | Intermediate: two copies of the balanced core, 4 labels duplicated |
| Explainability | Trivial to audit against the log; explains nothing about the process | High: each edge maps to a rule or a count | Medium: easy to state in words, but the XOR of overlapping partial orders is awkward to read |
| Robustness to missing SC events | Partial: tolerated only in the two observed SC-less contexts; a lost SC in an expedite case would be rejected | Good: SC skippable in any context | Good: same as balanced |
| Handling of unseen interleavings | Rejects all | Accepts those built from observed pairwise orders | Accepts all that the rules allow |
| Suitability for compliance versus exploration | Novelty detection and a strict audit baseline; too brittle for process description | Day-to-day conformance triage and communication with stakeholders | Exploration, sensitivity analysis, and an upper bound on "acceptable" |
| Main way it can be wrong | Coincidences encoded as rules | The CC ≺ SC edge is an artifact, or XR really depends on SC | D5 is wrong and credit is a real gate for SC |

# 8. Validation Code

The script is standalone: it rebuilds the three candidates, reconstructs the log, and runs all checks.

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

ABBR = {
    "RO": "Register Order", "CC": "Credit Check", "ER": "Engineering Review",
    "SC": "Supplier Confirmation", "XR": "Expedite Review",
    "AC": "Approve Configuration", "RP": "Release Production", "AR": "Archive Case",
}
SHORT = {name: short for short, name in ABBR.items()}


def expand(short_trace):
    """'RO CC ER' -> 'Register Order,Credit Check,Engineering Review'"""
    return ",".join(ABBR[token] for token in short_trace.split())


def as_tuple(short_trace):
    return tuple(expand(short_trace).split(","))

# Candidate models
# --------------------------------------------------------------------------
def build_conservative():
    middles = ["CC ER SC", "ER CC SC", "CC ER", "ER CC", "CC SC ER",
               "CC ER SC XR", "ER CC XR SC"]
    branches = ", ".join(
        "->( " + ", ".join("'%s'" % ABBR[t] for t in m.split()) + " )" for m in middles
    )
    return pm4py.parse_process_tree(
        "->( 'Register Order', X( " + branches + " ), "
        "'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case' )"
    )


def core_with_supplier_gate(gate):
    cc = Transition(label="Credit Check")
    er = Transition(label="Engineering Review")
    sc_opt = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR, [Transition(label="Supplier Confirmation"), SilentTransition()]
    )
    xr_opt = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR, [Transition(label="Expedite Review"), SilentTransition()]
    )
    core = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[cc, er, sc_opt, xr_opt])
    core.order.add_edge(cc if gate == "CC" else er, sc_opt)
    core.order.add_edge(cc, xr_opt)
    core.order.add_edge(er, xr_opt)
    return core


def wrap(core):
    ro = Transition(label="Register Order")
    ac = Transition(label="Approve Configuration")
    rp = Transition(label="Release Production")
    ar = Transition(label="Archive Case")
    root = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[ro, core, ac, rp, ar])
    root.order.add_edge(ro, core)
    root.order.add_edge(core, ac)
    root.order.add_edge(ac, rp)
    root.order.add_edge(rp, ar)
    return root


model_conservative = build_conservative()
model_balanced = wrap(core_with_supplier_gate("CC"))
model_permissive = wrap(
    OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR, [core_with_supplier_gate("CC"), core_with_supplier_gate("ER")]
    )
)
MODELS = {

def exhaustive_language(net, im, fm, max_len=12):
    """Bounded exhaustive playout. The candidates are acyclic, so a bound above
    the longest trace (8 events) makes the enumeration complete. For a model
    with loops the result would only be a bounded under-approximation."""
    variant = simulator.Variants.EXTENSIVE
    simulated = simulator.apply(
        net, im, fm, variant=variant,
        parameters={variant.value.Parameters.MAX_TRACE_LENGTH: max_len},
    )
    return {tuple(event["concept:name"] for event in trace) for trace in simulated}


def first(value):
    """check_soundness returns (bool, diagnostics) in recent PM4Py versions."""
    return value[0] if isinstance(value, (tuple, list)) else value


languages = {}
for position, (name, model) in enumerate(MODELS.items()):
    print("=" * 78)
    print(name.upper(), "|", type(model).__name__)

    # 2. accepting Petri net
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    print("places=%d transitions=%d arcs=%d"
          % (len(net.places), len(net.transitions), len(net.arcs)))

    # 3. soundness (Woflan)
    sound = first(pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True))
    print("sound:", sound)

    # 4. quality metrics on the 30 observed cases
    print("alignment fitness:", pm4py.fitness_alignments(log, net, im, fm))
    print("alignment precision: %.4f" % pm4py.precision_alignments(log, net, im, fm))
    print("generalization (TBR): %.4f" % pm4py.generalization_tbr(log, net, im, fm))
    print("simplicity (arc degree): %.4f" % pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm))

    # 5a. bounded exhaustive playout (complete here only because of acyclicity)
    language = exhaustive_language(net, im, fm)
    languages[name] = language
    unseen = sorted(language - observed)
    print("language size: %d | observed variants covered: %d of 7 | unobserved: %d"
          % (len(language), len(language & observed), len(unseen)))
    for trace in unseen:
        print("   unobserved:", " ".join(SHORT[a] for a in trace))

    # 5b. sampled playout: a plausibility check, NOT a proof of language inclusion
    sample = pm4py.play_out(net, im, fm)
    sample_variants = {tuple(e["concept:name"] for e in trace) for trace in sample}
    print("sampled playout: %d distinct variants; all inside enumerated language: %s"
          % (len(sample_variants), sample_variants <= language))

    # 6. positive / negative suite, checked in two independent ways
    suite_ok = True
    for short_trace, expected in SUITE:
        in_language = as_tuple(short_trace) in language
        single = pm4py.parse_event_log_string([expand(short_trace)])
        alignment = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(single, net, im, fm)[0]
        zero_cost = alignment["fitness"] == 1.0   # no log move, no visible model move
        if not (in_language == zero_cost == bool(expected[position])):
            suite_ok = False
            print("   SUITE MISMATCH:", short_trace, "| expected", bool(expected[position]),
                  "| language", in_language, "| alignment", zero_cost)
    print("positive/negative suite passed:", suite_ok)

# Nesting of the hypotheses; exact because all three languages are finite
print("=" * 78)
print("L(conservative) == observed variants:", languages["conservative"] == observed)
print("L(conservative) < L(balanced):", languages["conservative"] < languages["balanced"])
print("L(balanced) < L(permissive):", languages["balanced"] < languages["permissive"])
```

Results when I ran this script with PM4Py 2.7.23.4 (reported as measurements, with their limits):

- All three nets are sound workflow nets. All 30 cases fit all three models (100% fitting traces).
- Language sizes are 7, 12, and 14; the conservative language equals the observed variants; the strict nesting holds; the suite of 27 traces passes for all three models under both checks.
- Alignment-based precision was 1.00 (conservative), about 0.92 (balanced), and about 0.89 (permissive).
- TBR-based generalization was about 0.28–0.32 (conservative) and about 0.75 (balanced). For the permissive model it varied between about 0.45 and 0.67 across repeated runs, because token-based replay is not deterministic with duplicate labels and silent transitions. I would not use that number to rank the models.
- Arc-degree simplicity was about 0.82, 0.79, and 0.73. It ranks the conservative net as simplest because a bundle of sequences has low node degree, which ignores its 21 duplicated leaves. Net sizes were 20 places/25 transitions, 12/11, and 22/22.
- The reported `log_fitness` of the two POWL models is marginally below 1 (0.99997–0.99998) even though every trace fits, because silent moves carry a small alignment cost.

Limitations:

- The exhaustive enumeration is a proof of language content only because the candidates have no loops. For a model with loops, bounded playout gives a lower bound on the language and PM4Py offers no exact language comparison.
- `pm4py.play_out` is sampled. The script uses it only to check that no sampled trace falls outside the enumerated language; it does not prove inclusion or coverage.
- Precision and generalization are computed relative to 30 cases. With two expedite cases, both numbers are dominated by sampling noise in exactly the region where the models differ.
- No metric can evaluate the SC-skip ambiguity, since the model cannot see the missing attribute.

# 9. Recommendation and Data-Collection Plan

**Immediate use: `model_balanced`.** It replays all 30 cases, uses each label once, and every edge is traceable to a count or a rule. Its only untested extrapolation lies in expedited cases. It should be used for monitoring and triage, not for automated blocking, because an SC-less trace cannot be verified as compliant.

**Sensitivity analysis: `model_permissive`.** The two models differ only in traces where SC completes before CC. Running both shows directly whether any conclusion depends on the soft edge CC ≺ SC. The conservative model is useful as a third tier for novelty detection:

- fits conservative: seen before;
- fits balanced only: expected recombination, record it;
- fits permissive only: SC completed before CC, review with finance and procurement;
- fits none: deviation.

**Data to collect.**

1. Lifecycle timestamps (start and complete, ideally also assignment) for CC, ER, SC, and XR, with the performing resource.
2. Raw timestamp precision and an explicit tie flag with the ingestion index, to quantify how many CC/ER, SC/ER, and SC/XR orders were decided by tie-breaking.
3. The external/stocked attribute at component level, including the number of external components per order.
4. A reconciliation of SC events against the supplier system of record, with a flag for events recovered after the integration fault.
5. Expedite request data: request timestamp, requester, and order priority.
6. Case outcome and the export filter: rejected, cancelled, and still-open orders, to learn whether the "always approved" skeleton is an artifact.
7. Candidate decision attributes for the check order: customer type, order value, product family.
8. More cases. If SC before CC occurs at the rate suggested by the mirrored CC-first behavior (about 3–4% of cases), roughly 85–100 further cases give a 95% chance of seeing it at least once. XR without SC under independence (about 1.5% of cases) needs roughly 200.

**How the ambiguities get resolved.**

| Question | Decisive observation | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| CC and ER: sequence, exclusive orderings, or concurrency | Start of one check before completion of the other in a substantial share of cases | Overlap confirms S2 and refutes S1 as practice |
| | No overlap, and the order is predicted by a case attribute | Data-driven XOR between two sequences; replace the unordered pair by a guarded choice |
| | No overlap, ER-first cases all share tied or coarse timestamps | S1 may hold; the ER-first order is an artifact (Section 10) |
| SC gate: CC only or either check | A trace RO ER SC CC … with a real time gap, or an SC start before CC completion | Adopt the permissive model |
| | Several hundred SC cases with no such trace, plus confirmation from finance | Harden the soft edge CC ≺ SC |
| SC optional or mandatory | Stocked case without SC | Legitimate skip (S4) |
| | External case without SC in the log but present in the supplier system | Missing event (S5); repair the log, do not change the model |
| | External case without SC in either system | Real violation of S3; add a guard so the skip is allowed only for stocked orders |
| | Stocked case with SC | SC is not driven only by the attribute |
| XR independent of SC | XR in a stocked, SC-less case | Keeps the balanced design |
| | No such case in a large sample, or procurement states XR concerns supplier lead time | Nest XR under the SC branch |
| XR and SC order | Further traces of RO CC ER XR SC … and RO ER CC SC XR … | Confirms that the conservative model's coupling was coincidence |
| Scope | Any repeated activity, a second XR, or an archived case without AC | Falsifies all three candidates; loops or exit paths are needed |

After the data arrive, the candidates should be re-scored on the new cases only (out-of-sample), since all three fit the current 30 perfectly by construction.

# 10. Uncertainty Statement

Confidence in the main modeling decisions, as rough subjective probabilities:

| Decision | Confidence | Main reason for doubt |
|---|---|---|
| Skeleton RO first, then the checks, then AC → RP → AR, for completed cases | About 95% | None in the data |
| That skeleton covers all real cases (no rejection, cancellation, or rework) | About 50–60% | Only completed cases appear; a custom-order process without rejections is unusual |
| CC and ER both mandatory before AC | About 90% | D3 is not rated "high"; 30/30 agree |
| Both CC/ER completion orders are legitimate | About 85% | Coarse timestamps and ties could create the ER-first order |
| CC and ER truly overlap in time | About 55–65% | Not testable without start timestamps (D7) |
| SC is genuinely optional | About 85–90% | S3 and S4 are compatible; pure missingness has probability of about 0.03 or less |
| Any single SC-less case was a legitimate skip | About 60–85% | 1–3 of 7 expected to be lost events |
| SC need not wait for ER | About 75% | Two cases plus D5; tie artifacts possible |
| SC must wait for CC (soft edge, balanced over permissive) | About 55% | 23/23, but the absence of a counterexample has probability about 0.32 even if the edge is false; D5 says otherwise |
| XR only after both checks and before AC | About 85–90% | D6, S6, 2/2 |
| XR and SC unordered | About 75–80% | One trace each way, plus S6 |
| XR may occur without SC | About 60–65% | Never observed; no rule either way |
| XR at most once; no repeats of other activities | About 80% | D6 for XR; only absence of evidence for the rest |

The choice between balanced and permissive is close. I prefer the balanced model for operational use because wrongly flagging a rare legitimate trace is cheaper than silently accepting a supplier commitment before credit clearance, not because the evidence clearly favors it.

Alternative interpretations that would materially change the recommendation:

1. S1 is a binding financial control, and the ER-first order is partly a timestamp artifact or reflects real violations. Then the appropriate normative model is `->(RO, CC, PO({ER, X(SC, tau), X(XR, tau)}, {ER ≺ X(XR, tau)}), AC, RP, AR)`, which fits only 19 of the 30 cases. The 11 ER-first cases would become findings rather than normal behavior, and none of the three candidates should be used for compliance. Lifecycle timestamps and tie flags decide this.
2. All orders in this export contain external components. Then the 7 SC-less cases are 1–3 lost events plus 4–6 violations of S3; SC should be mandatory in a compliance model, and the skip branch in all three candidates hides the most important deviation in the log.
3. The two SC-before-ER cases are tie artifacts. Then SC follows both checks, D5 is unsupported, and the simple process tree `->(RO, +(CC, ER), +(X(SC, tau), X(XR, tau)), AC, RP, AR)` would be preferable to any partial order. It has 10 traces and rejects the 2 cases of V5.
