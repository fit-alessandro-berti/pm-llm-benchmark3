## 3. Assumptions

### Arrivals
- Demand is non-stationary and seasonal: monthly multipliers (1.7 peak, 1.0 trough) and weekday ratios (Monday 2.2× Wednesday) are constant over the 18-month horizon but vary deterministically.
- Architect clustering is modeled empirically: the top 20 architects generate 31% of volume in clusters; the twin draws cluster sizes from the observed distribution rather than assuming independence.
- Case-type proportions are stationary; no structural shift in mix is assumed unless a stress scenario introduces it.

### Service times
- Human-service times are drawn from fitted log-normal or gamma distributions (chosen by AIC on available timestamp differences), with medians/p90 values anchored to the empirical table.
- System-service times (`portal`, `system`, `Receive missing documents` registration) are deterministic or exponential with very short mean.
- Resource calendars truncate service availability; no overtime is modeled except for "urgent" legal exceptions.

### Routing
- Branch conditions (`requires_fire`, `requires_environment`, `requires_public_notice`) are inferred from case attributes and variant frequencies. Where thresholds are missing (e.g., area for fire review), a probabilistic rule calibrated to observed activation rates is used.
- The process tree is interpreted as an executable control-flow graph; `X` (exclusive choice) and `+` (parallel) nodes are resolved by attribute conditions and resource availability, not by hidden random draws alone.

### Resources / calendars
- Calendars are reconstructed from HR data and applied exactly; holiday and council-week effects are deterministic calendar blocks.
- Part-time allocations (fire reviewers, environmental reservations) reduce effective capacity proportionally.
- Inspector travel is district-dependent; D3/D5 travel times are drawn from longer empirical distributions.
- Resource absence (illness, training) is not explicitly in the log; stress scenarios inject absence manually.

### Rework
- Rework loops are probabilistic, not deterministic. The probability depends on case complexity and, for `Inspection rework`, on district and inspector skill.
- Rework service times are drawn from the same family as primary activities but with a scale factor (>1) to represent correction effort.
- `Completeness rework` (missing docs) is applicant-driven: service time is near-zero (system/portal), but waiting for `Receive missing documents` is drawn from a heavy-tailed (Pareto/log-normal) applicant-response distribution.

### Applicant / third-party response
- `Receive missing documents` delay and `Public-notice` objection extensions are applicant/third-party driven, not municipal-resource driven.
- These are modeled as exogenous delays with heavy tails (p90 >> median) and are not truncated by resource calendars.
- The missing-objection problem is handled by treating `Public-notice` duration as a mixture: 14-day fixed base + heavy-tailed extension drawn from the observed long-notice distribution, with district-level correlation.

### Cross-case dependencies
- Inspection batching improves travel efficiency but introduces synchronization delay. The model assumes rational batching behavior: inspectors and schedulers prefer batches if they reduce total travel time, up to the `max_batch_wait` constraint.
- Architect batches affect arrival intensity and may increase internal queue contention (e.g., same planner handles related applications). No direct resource-preemption is modeled, but queue priorities can be adjusted.

### Non-stationary demand
- The arrival generator produces seasonally varying intensity but does not adapt to backlogs (no endogenous demand suppression). This is appropriate for a municipality-facing open application system.

### Missing data
- Start-timestamp absence means service/waiting splits are partly inferred; the twin treats all observed "completion-to-completion" intervals as composed of queue wait + service + possible hidden delays. The simulation calibrates service parameters from the subset of activities with complete timestamps and uses queue dynamics to reproduce total interval distributions.
- Removed emergency decisions mean the twin may slightly underestimate very fast paths; a sensitivity analysis adds a "fast-track" scenario.
- The flattened log underrepresents dependencies; the twin adds explicit dependency structures (batch pools, architect clusters, district objection correlation) to compensate.

## 5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty treatment

**Warm-up approach.** The system starts empty or with a lightly loaded state (e.g., 200 cases distributed across activities). Warm-up continues until:
- Queue lengths stabilize: coefficient of variation of mean queue length over rolling 30-day windows < 0.1.
- Resource utilization reaches steady-state mean.
- Case-type mix in active cases approximates the arrival mix.
Typically 3–4 simulated months (approx. 3,000–5,000 submissions) are discarded.

**Run length.** Each replication runs 24 months (to cover two seasonal cycles) or 18 months aligned with the log plus 6 months projection. For policy comparison, a 24-month horizon captures peak-trough interactions.

**Replications and seeds.**
- Baseline: 50 replications with independent random seeds (`numpy.random.SeedSequence` or `random.seed`).
- Individual interventions (`D1`–`D9`): 30 replications.
- Combined/stress (`D10`, `S1`–`S3`): 30 replications.
- Seeds are stratified: arrival process, service-time draws, routing choices, and dependency shocks use separate streams to allow variance decomposition.

**Confidence and credible intervals.**
- For median and quantiles (`p50`, `p90`, `p99`): percentile bootstrap (1,000 resamples) within each experiment; report 95% CI.
- For mean backlog or utilization: standard t-interval.
- Because durations are heavy-tailed (p90/p99 far from median), reporting only mean is avoided. All decision metrics include median and `p90`; tail robustness uses `p99` where sample allows.

**Sensitivity analysis.**
- One-at-a-time (OAT) tornado: vary arrival rate multiplier (±15%), service-time scale (±10%), batching wait threshold (±2 days), and objection proxy rate (±20%). Rank impacts on `p90` duration.
- Global sensitivity: Morris screening on routing probabilities and resource capacities for a subset of key interventions.
- Heavy-tail handling: service distributions are fitted with log-normal or generalized Pareto for tails. In simulation, if drawn values exceed physical feasibility (e.g., negative or >30 days for a task), they are truncated or resampled with a warning flag tracked in logs.

**Event-log replay validation.**
- Run the baseline twin over the 18-month log period.
- Compare simulated trace variants to empirical variants (`pm4py.statistics.variants.get_variants`) using:
  - Variant frequency distance (L1 or chi-square).
  - Fitness/conformance if a Petri net is derived from the tree (`pm4py.conformance`).
- Target: >85% of case variants reproduced; rare variants (7%) can diverge by >20% if explained by missing emergency removals.

**Distributional validation.**
- By case-type and variant group: compare simulated and empirical distributions of total case duration using Kolmogorov–Smirnov (with correction for discrete time) or Wasserstein distance.
- By activity: compare simulated waiting-time and service-time distributions to empirical summaries (median/p90 table). Where start times are missing, validate that simulated total interval (wait + service) matches the observed "completion-to-completion" interval.

**Resource-utilization validation.**
- Compare simulated resource busy-time ratios to reconstructed calendar occupancy from HR (not event logs). For clerks/planners/inspectors, target simulated utilization within ±10% of expected effective FTE utilization (e.g., environmental 40% reserved implies max ~60% usable).

**Bottleneck validation.**
- Compare simulated queue dynamics: identify activities with longest median waiting (`Planning review` 6d, `Environmental review` 8d, `Site inspection` 8d). The twin should reproduce the same bottleneck ranking.
- Validate seasonality: March/May and September/November backlogs should be 1.5–2× January backlogs.

**Out-of-sample validation.**
- Split the 18-month log: months 1–15 for calibration; months 16–18 for validation.
- Calibrate arrival rates, service distributions, and routing probabilities on the training set.
- Predict duration distributions and variant frequencies for months 16–18; check coverage of observed values within 95% prediction intervals.

**Cross-validation of dependencies.**
- Hold out batches of architect-related submissions and district-level long-notice cases. Test whether the dependency structures reproduce clustering and correlation patterns.

## 7. Decision criteria

**Primary metrics (by case type and aggregate).**

| Metric | Definition | Decision threshold / range |
|--------|-----------|---------------------------|
| **Median duration (`Dur_50`)** | 50th percentile of total case time (`Issue/Reject` timestamp – `Submit` timestamp) | Improve by 10% vs. baseline for target case types; do not increase >5% for non-target types. |
| **Tail duration (`Dur_90`)** | 90th percentile | Improve by 15% for target types; critical for SLA compliance. Must not worsen `Dur_99` by >20%. |
| **SLA compliance** | % of cases completed within case-type target (e.g., Simple < 25d, Complex < 180d) | Baseline estimated; intervention selected if it raises compliance by 5 percentage points for at least one case type without reducing another by >3pp. |
| **Fairness (district)** | Coefficient of variation of `Dur_50` across districts; Gini coefficient of waiting time | Reduce CV by 10% or maintain within ±0.05 of baseline. Prevent `D3` from systematically delaying D5 cases. |
| **Fairness (architect)** | Difference in median duration between large-architect batches and single submissions | `D7` should reduce this gap; if gap widens, reject or adjust slot design. |
| **Resource utilization (`U`)** | Effective busy time / available calendar time by role | Target 65–85% for human roles; <95% to avoid burnout/instability. `D4` should lift inspector `U` into healthy range without exceeding 90%. |
| **Rework rate (`R`)** | % of cases entering any loop (`Completeness`, `Inspection`, `Planning`, `Legal`, `Appeal`) | Reduce by 2pp if possible; `D1` targets completeness loops specifically. |
| **Backlog (`B`)** | Cases pending > target age (e.g., >30d for Simple, >120d for Complex) | Reduce by 15% vs. baseline under historical demand; must remain stable under `S1`. |
| **Cost / capacity** | Additional FTE or system investment (temporary staff, pre-checker IT) | Quantified in FTE-months or EUR. Only select if duration/SLA gains justify cost under budget constraint. |

**Robustness criterion.** An intervention is recommended only if:
- It improves `Dur_50` and `Dur_90` in baseline (`B`).
- Under `S1` (high demand + holiday) and `S2` (burst + absence), `Dur_90` does not exceed baseline `Dur_90` by >15%.
- Resource utilization does not enter unstable (>95%) or severely underused (<30%) zones.

**Combined policy (`D10`) evaluation.** Synergy is required, not additive:
- The improvement of `D10` on `Dur_90` should be  60% of the sum of individual improvements (`D1`+`D2`+`D3`+`D4`); otherwise, interactions are negative (e.g., planner bottleneck shift) and the combination should be decomposed.

**Selection rule.** Rank interventions by a weighted score:
`Score = 0.3·Dur_90 + 0.2·SLA + 0.2·Fairness + 0.15·Backlog + 0.15·(1 - CostPenalty)`
Choose the highest-scoring intervention that satisfies robustness thresholds. If `D10` scores highest but fails robustness in `S3`, fall back to the best robust subset.

**Important caveat.** The twin has not been fully calibrated against ground truth; no claim of exact optimality is made. Decisions are presented as "expected improvement ranges" with confidence intervals from replication analysis.

def compute_summary_statistics(df, log):
    """
    Computes arrival rate averages, variant shares, and attempts service/waiting
    separation ONLY where both start and end are available.
    """
    # Case arrival average by month / weekday (non-stationary analysis)
    df["month"] = df["time:timestamp"].dt.month
    df["weekday"] = df["time:timestamp"].dt.weekday  # 0=Monday
    arrival_stats = df.groupby(["month", "weekday", "case_type"]).size().unstack(fill_value=0)

    # Variants (already computed; shown for consistency)
    variants_df = pm4py.statistics.variants.get_variants(log)

    # Service / waiting estimation where possible
    # IMPORTANT: Many human activities lack start timestamps. We do NOT invent
    # start times. We compute observed completion intervals and flag gaps.
    # For demonstration, assume a synthetic 'start_timestamp' exists only for
    # cases where it is genuinely present; otherwise service is NA.
    if "start_timestamp" in df.columns:
        df["start_timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["start_timestamp"], errors="coerce")
        df["service_time_min"] = (
            df["time:timestamp"] - df["start_timestamp"]
        ).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
        # Waiting before activity: time since previous activity end in same case
        df_sorted = df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"])
        df_sorted["prev_end"] = df_sorted.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].shift(1)
        df_sorted["waiting_before_min"] = (
            df_sorted["start_timestamp"] - df_sorted["prev_end"]
        ).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
        # Note: negative or NA values indicate missing/corrupt timestamps; keep as NaN
    else:
        df["service_time_min"] = np.nan
        df["waiting_before_min"] = np.nan
        print("WARNING: start_timestamp missing. Service/waiting split is partially inferred.")

    return {
        "arrival_stats": arrival_stats,
        "variants": variants_df,
        "service_wait_df": df,
    }
