## 1. Drift diagnosis table

## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

- **January:** Fully mature baseline, 18% breach rate and AUROC 0.81, with low unknown and missing-feature shares.
- **February:** Similar mature performance and path mix; breach rate is 19% and AUROC 0.82.
- **March:** Stable mature reference continues: breach rate 18%, AUROC 0.81. January–March are a useful baseline, subject to seasonality and quality checks.
- **April:** Migration coincides with invalid or missing prefixes, duplicates and unknown activities. AUROC falls to 0.67, mean risk exceeds observed breach by nine percentage points, and 18% of variants are unmapped. This is primarily a schema and pipeline incident; repaired replay is needed before assigning residual changes to drift.
- **May:** The patch and partial backfill coincide with improved metrics and quality, but ECE and Brier remain worse than baseline. Unrecomputed cached features and inconsistent deduplication leave a residual pipeline hypothesis.
- **June:** The remote-routing policy plausibly explains V2's rise to 52%, while remote-fix time remains stable. Observed breach is 0.15 versus mean risk 0.20. A larger share of lower-risk cases can change aggregate calibration without changing the conditional relationship; test within comparable strata before calling it concept drift.
- **July:** Supplier misses coincide with part wait rising to 65 h and V3 breach to 0.46. This is an early process-performance warning and possible concept drift. Even at 98% maturity, check whether long cases are selectively missing.
- **August:** Part wait reaches 130 h, V3 represents 35% of paths, and its observed matured breach rate is 0.64. Overall breach is 0.28 versus mean risk 0.19, with AUROC 0.68. Both greater V3 share and worse outcomes within V3 plausibly contribute; 93% maturity and changing case mix prevent exact attribution.
- **September:** Part wait reaches 148 h and Partner Z enters with a higher V3 share and different customer characteristics. Process delays, case mix and possibly changed conditional risk overlap. The observed Z gap is not a clean partner effect: Z labels mature more slowly, and overall maturity is 82%.
- **October:** Only 55% of labels are available and the backlog particularly affects longer paths. The observed 0.16 breach rate, 0.23 mean risk, AUROC 0.64 and ECE 0.130 describe a selected labeled subset, not the full cohort. Apparent reversal from underprediction to overprediction could be selection, a real shift, or both. The 155 h completed-edge median and 41% V3 share remain operational signals, subject to event and completion censoring.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

1. **Maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting.** Preserve each prediction and feature snapshot as it existed at diagnosis. Measure label availability by days since prediction and promised date, month, source, path and resolution duration. Recompute metrics at identical cohort ages and after a prespecified near-complete maturity threshold, with counts and intervals. If backlog selection explains October's apparent improvement, metrics should change as long-path labels arrive and matched-vintage gaps may narrow; persistent deterioration in mature comparable cohorts supports real performance change. Report worst-case breach-rate bounds: observed breaches divided by all cases through that value plus the unlabeled fraction. Where defensible, estimate inverse-probability-of-labeling weights and test sensitivity to nonrandom missingness; bounds for AUROC and calibration need separate analysis. Check whether an open case past its promise is already known to breach under the actual outcome definition.
2. **Normalize, deduplicate and replay.** Rebuild histories from raw source records with versioned mapping, event IDs and ordering; investigate conflicting duplicates instead of deleting all repeated activities. Recompute prefixes, queue and cached event-count features and replay the frozen model. If migration explains April, coverage and repaired metrics should recover substantially; if not, inspect residual feature, population and label changes. Compare original live scores separately from repaired counterfactual scores. Count missing or multiple diagnosis events and missing predictions.
3. **Stratified calibration and discrimination.** On mature point-in-time predictions report prevalence, mean score, calibration intercept and slope, reliability curves, Brier and AUROC by variant, source and month, with sample sizes and uncertainty. Stable within-stratum metrics alongside changed aggregate performance support a mix explanation for June; deterioration within comparable strata supports additional model or conditional-risk change. Eventual variant is a post-prediction diagnostic, potentially affected by later routing, so it is not automatically a usable live feature or causal adjustment.
4. **Variants and DFGs.** Compare normalized variant and edge counts and shares, unmapped and incomplete traces, and performance-DFG medians, upper quantiles and counts, including aged open part orders. Contrast event-calendar with case-start views, stratified by source and maturity. A routing change should change path frequencies even if edge durations remain stable; a supplier disruption should show sustained part delays and aged open orders, including within non-Z cases. If changes disappear after cleaning and consistent observation windows, measurement or composition is more plausible.
5. **Conditional outcome relationship.** On mature cases with valid prefixes, compare pre- and post-disruption outcomes within common prediction-time feature, risk and source strata and, descriptively, within the same eventual path. Fit month-by-feature interactions or standardize to a fixed feature distribution, with uncertainty. Persistent differences support concept drift; differences that disappear after case-mix adjustment support covariate or population shift. Eventual-path conditioning can select different cases across periods and does not prove causation; check routing and pre-diagnosis characteristics. Unobserved time-varying supplier conditions can also change the apparent relationship.
6. **Partner transport checks.** At comparable maturity, assess overlap in prediction-time variables, geography and device age. Reweight non-Z cases toward Z's distribution and standardize both sources to a common population; compare breach rates and model metrics before and after weighting, with effective sample sizes and intervals. A gap that closes supports composition as a contributor; a persistent gap motivates investigation of service conditions, unmeasured factors or model behavior. Weak overlap, missing features and differential label selection limit transport and prevent a causal or fairness conclusion. Use eventual V3 only as descriptive decomposition.
7. **Missingness and lineage.** Trace source event IDs through mapping versions, ordering, prefix construction, deduplication, queue joins, cache versions and model input snapshots. Compare missingness and prediction coverage by date and source. A migration or patch discontinuity that disappears on rebuild supports pipeline failure; a persistent shift in correctly sourced inputs supports genuine covariate or population change. Verify outcome definitions and timestamps against source records, including cases without predictions.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Immediate containment:** Treat April predictions and recent immature-cohort metrics as unreliable. Fix prediction coverage and route invalid or missing prefixes to a documented manual or rules-based fallback. Escalate the parts backlog and review affected cases using information available to staff. Preserve original scores, inputs and pipeline versions. Do not use later part receipt or eventual variant as a feature at diagnosis.

**Repair first:** Normalize activities, resolve duplicates, fully rebuild affected cached features, and validate queue joins, ordering and diagnosis timing. Replay and compare against live scores. January–March are candidate training/reference data after normal quality checks. April is unsafe until history and derived features are repaired and verified; exclude unrecoverable records. May is eligible only after record-level validation of the patch, features and deduplication. June is a useful mature post-policy cohort if validated. July and August can be used only once labels are sufficiently mature for the intended cutoff and selection is assessed; 98% and 93% are not automatically representative. September's 82% maturity, especially slower Z labels, makes it unsafe for naive training or calibration until maturity or a validated censoring approach. October at 55% with selective backlog is unsafe for naive training or evaluation. Preserve chronological holdouts and enforce historical feature and label availability.

**Short-term recalibration:** After repair, use a recent, representative, sufficiently mature calibration window disjoint from training and evaluation. If discrimination remains useful and error is mostly systematic, fit an intercept or slope adjustment and validate on a later mature cohort, including segment calibration and business thresholds. June overprediction alone does not justify an adjustment for August's different regime. August–September ranking has deteriorated, so a global intercept cannot be assumed sufficient. If no representative mature labels exist, retain operational containment and wait for evidence.

**Full retraining:** Consider after sufficient repaired, mature post-supplier and Z data demonstrate persistent loss of discrimination or conditional performance, and assess whether supplier recovery will change the regime again. Add reliable information available at diagnosis, such as supplier status, region or device age, where appropriate; evaluate missingness and disparate impact. Compare against the repaired frozen model and recalibration using forward-in-time, fixed-vintage validation and a later holdout, business costs and uncertainty. A segment-specific or variant-specific model is not yet justified: Z labels are limited and slow, and eventual paths may be unknown at diagnosis. Consider a source or known-at-diagnosis routing model, or pooled interactions and partial pooling, only if mature sample sizes and out-of-time validation show material benefit. Define rollout and rollback criteria.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

Ingest immutable, versioned event, feature, prediction, outcome and deployment snapshots keyed by case and prediction time. Retain case-start cohorts for outcomes and model comparisons, and event-calendar views for operational workload. Attach data cutoff, cohort age, open-case count, source, mapping version and model version to every report.

- **Predictive and calibration:** Track AUROC, Brier, calibration intercept and slope, reliability curves, score and outcome distributions, decision-threshold workload and prediction coverage, with counts and intervals overall and by source and, diagnostically, eventual path. Compare matched vintages and mature cohorts; distinguish live scores from repaired replays.
- **Label maturity:** Track pending counts, arrival curves and lag distributions by cohort, source, path and length, plus backlog and known overdue cases. Publish preliminary metrics with maturity and sensitivity bounds, and refresh as outcomes arrive.
- **Data quality:** Alert on unknown activities, missing or duplicate diagnosis events, prefix timing, prediction coverage, queue missingness, duplicate conflicts, event ordering, cache freshness and lineage/version mismatches.
- **Process and performance DFG:** Monitor path and edge counts and shares, unmapped and incomplete traces, edge duration quantiles and counts, aged open part orders and completion rates. Compare calendar and case-start views and stratify by source.
- **Population:** Track source, region and device-age distributions, feature coverage, scores and overlap, with group representation and maturity. Examine group calibration and service outcomes on comparable cohorts.
- **Interventions and deployments:** Annotate migration, patches, policy changes, supplier incidents, partner onboarding, model and calibration releases, and API recovery. Track routing decisions and service interventions, which can themselves affect observed outcomes. Record owners, thresholds and rollback decisions.

Set thresholds using baseline variability, minimum counts, effect sizes and repeated-window evidence, with operationally urgent exceptions. A data-quality alarm triggers pipeline investigation and suppresses automatic model-change decisions until repaired. A label-delay alarm triggers backlog investigation and provisional reporting, not automatic retraining. Process or population alarms prompt stratified investigation; a sustained predictive alarm on sufficiently mature, quality-checked cohorts triggers the recalibration/retraining evaluation. Escalate severe supplier delays operationally without waiting for labels.

The sketch assumes `case_id`, `activity`, `timestamp` and a stable source-specific `event_id`; it observes events before a fixed cutoff. An event-calendar slice contains only that month's events, so edges can bridge removed events and variants are partial. A case-start cohort contains all observed events through the cutoff for cases first seen in that month, including later events, and can also be incomplete. Confirm true case creation and completeness against the source. The placeholder resource permits temporal extraction when no resource is available; resource-based temporal statistics are then meaningless and must be excluded from interpretation.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

raw = pd.read_csv("field_service_events.csv")
raw["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(raw["timestamp"], utc=True, errors="raise")
cutoff = pd.Timestamp("2026-11-01", tz="UTC")  # exclusive
raw = raw.loc[raw["timestamp"] < cutoff].copy()
raw["activity"] = raw["activity"].replace(
    {"Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"}
)

# Use (source, event_id) instead when IDs are only unique within a source.
fields = ["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"]
if raw["event_id"].isna().any():
    raise ValueError("Missing event IDs require a source-specific deduplication rule")
conflicts = raw.groupby("event_id")[fields].nunique(dropna=False).gt(1).any(axis=1)
if conflicts.any():
    raise ValueError(f"Conflicting event IDs: {int(conflicts.sum())}")
raw = raw.drop_duplicates(subset=["event_id"]).sort_values(
    ["case_id", "timestamp", "event_id"], kind="stable"
)
if "org:resource" not in raw:
    raw["org:resource"] = "RESOURCE_UNAVAILABLE"

log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    raw, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="timestamp"
)
case, time = "case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"
first_seen = log.groupby(case)[time].min()


def summarize(frame):
    if frame.empty:
        return {"events": 0, "cases": 0}
    dfg, starts, ends = pm4py.discover_dfg(frame)
    perf, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
        frame, perf_aggregation_key="median"
    )
    temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(frame, grouper_freq="ME")
    return {
        "events": len(frame), "cases": frame[case].nunique(),
        "variants": pm4py.get_variants(frame), "dfg": dfg,
        "start_activities": starts, "end_activities": ends,
        "median_edge_seconds": perf, "temporal_features": temporal,
    }


summaries = {}
for start in pd.date_range("2026-01-01", "2026-10-01", freq="MS", tz="UTC"):
    end = start + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1)
    # PM4Py's event filter includes both bounds; enforce a half-open month.
    calendar = pm4py.filter_time_range(log, start, end, mode="events")
    calendar = calendar.loc[calendar[time] < end].copy()
    ids = first_seen.index[(first_seen >= start) & (first_seen < end)]
    cohort = log.loc[log[case].isin(ids)].copy()
    summaries[start.strftime("%Y-%m")] = {
        "cutoff_exclusive": str(cutoff),
        "event_calendar": summarize(calendar),
        "case_start": summarize(cohort),
    }
# `summaries` holds counts, graphs, variants and temporal tables; no model is fit.
```

Timestamp ties require a source-defined sequence for a reliable DFG; event ID is only a deterministic tie-breaker here. Monitor unknown labels and incomplete cases separately. Temporal-feature frequency aliases and output schemas can vary by pandas/PM4Py version; validate them and month boundaries with a small known log before deployment.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

- **Train on immature outcomes:** Treating unresolved cases as non-breaches or training only on available October labels can bias risk and ranking. Repair the backlog, use mature fixed-vintage cohorts, and quantify selection before training.
- **Calibrate around a pipeline bug:** An intercept adjustment can hide wrong prefixes or cached features while individual predictions remain unreliable. Restore lineage and recompute first; assess repaired replay separately from historical live performance.
- **Ignore supplier-driven change:** Assuming every August error is a data issue could leave delayed parts cases underprioritized. Escalate supplier delays and monitor aged open orders now, while testing mature conditional outcomes and updating the model when justified.
- **Attribute Partner Z's gap to unfair model behavior without evidence:** Different path mix, rural conditions and slower label arrival affect the observed gap. Compare mature cohorts and performance within supported strata, assess overlap, and conduct a separate fairness and service-access review; avoid both premature blame and dismissal of a real disparity.
- **Retrain on a temporary routing mix or backlog:** A model fitted to a transient composition or outage may degrade when conditions recover. Validate forward across regimes, monitor the policy and supplier situation, and retain rollback capability.
- **Use future path information at diagnosis:** Training on eventual V3 or later part receipt leaks future information and exaggerates performance. Use these for retrospective diagnostics unless actually known at prediction time; enforce timestamped feature availability in training and replay.
