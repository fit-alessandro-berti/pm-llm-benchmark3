## 1. Executive causal assessment
Verdict: not identifiable from the supplied evidence, with a lean toward mainly confounded association. Confidence: 55/100 that a genuine harmful total effect of Orion assignment of the size implied by the crude gap is not established; confidence in any signed causal effect is low.

Strongest evidence for a causal Orion harm: none in the supplied cuts. Crudely Orion averages 21.96h versus Nova 15.24h, a +6.72h gap, but Orion has 42.9% High and 42.9% Medium complexity versus Nova 12.5% and 25.0%, 92.9% Night versus 25.0%, and 64.3% compliance-required versus 25.0%. Within every complexity stratum the sign reverses: High Orion 31.57h vs Nova 35.00h (n=6 vs 2), Medium 17.17h vs 19.38h, Low 7.50h vs 9.64h. Mean waits are also lower for Orion within strata, and review service is broadly similar within complexity.

Strongest evidence against identifiable harm is therefore not proof of benefit: strata are tiny (Low Orion n=2, High Nova n=2), and unobserved case risk, reviewer skill, queue state and selection into completed cases remain. Stratification removes observed complexity mix, not routing on unobservables.

Reassigning 50% blindly could succeed if Nova has spare capacity and the routing rule truly sends Orion unnecessarily hard cases. It could fail because the gap is case-mix, not Orion production, and could backfire by loading Nova, lengthening its queue, mis-matching certified reviewers to difficult/compliance work, and increasing rework or appeals without improving total cycle time.

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests
| rank | hypothesis | process mechanism | evidence supporting it | evidence contradicting it | discriminating analysis or falsification test | predicted observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mainly confounded by complexity and routing | Routing sends harder, higher-risk cases to Orion; complexity increases compliance, wait, service and cycle, creating a crude Orion penalty without Orion causing delay | Crude +6.72h reverses to about -2.5h after complexity standardization; Orion has far more High/Medium, Night and compliance cases | Within-stratum samples are tiny and unobserved risk may still favor Orion; standardization is descriptive, not identification | Exact matching on complexity, shift, arrival_load and pre-assignment compliance_required, with overlap/positivity diagnostics and sensitivity bounds for unobserved risk | If mainly confounded, matched Orion-Nova differences should be near zero in common-support cells and sensitive to modest unobserved risk; a large stable matched gap would weaken this hypothesis |
| 2 | Beneficial Orion effect masked by case mix | Certified Orion reviewers handle difficult cases with slightly lower service per complexity and lower queueing, but are assigned cases that would be even slower under Nova | Within each complexity stratum Orion cycle, wait and service are lower; rework is equal or lower within Medium/Low | Benefits are based on 2-6 cases per cell; Orion Low n=2 and Nova High n=2 cannot support a general benefit claim | Within-route and within-reviewer comparison using per-episode service (first review only, second review separately), variant-adjusted for compliance and Request Info | If beneficial, Orion should show lower first-review service and lower Request Info probability within the same complexity/compliance variant; equal or higher per-episode service would weaken this hypothesis |

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 0: Diagnostic freeze | Pause blanket 50% reassignment; reconstruct event timestamps, queue state, reviewer assignment and completion status for all started cases | Remove measurement and selection ambiguity before changing routing | Process audit at case level | No routing change; protect complex cases; publish overlap cells | Conformance to variants, DFG by group, wait/service/residual decomposition, backlog at Classify, open-case censoring | Identifies common support and whether completed-only sampling biases the contrast | Do not proceed to pilot if event reconstruction shows systematic missing timestamps for one group |

| Phase 2: Queue/compliance capacity intervention | Add temporary night compliance capacity and load-based triage without changing group labels; expedite Compliance Check queue and cross-train Nova reviewers | Queue_wait and compliance bottleneck, not resource-group label itself | Shift-week or compliance queue, with stepped-wedge timing if feasible | Maintain certification requirements; do not divert High cases from Orion; monitor quality and rework | Compliance waiting time, queue length at Classify, wait_before_review split, DFG compliance frequency, cycle by group and shift | Separates congestion effects from group effects; if waits fall for both groups, capacity was the mechanism | Roll back if compliance capacity gains do not reduce wait or if quality/rework worsens |
| Phase 3: Conditional scaled routing | Adopt complexity-based routing only if pilot shows benefit: Orion for High/compliance, Nova for eligible Low/Medium with spare capacity; otherwise retain current routing and invest in capacity | Match certified capacity to case difficulty and real queue state | Routing policy for newly classified cases | Ongoing positivity monitoring, monthly overlap and fairness/quality dashboards, exception logging for routing overrides | Variant mix, stratified cycle by complexity/shift, reviewer-level service, rework, appeals, queue spillover | Tests transportability beyond pilot cells and prevents extrapolation to unsupported High/Nova or Low/Orion combinations | Revert to prior routing if scaled policy creates unsupported cells, longer waits for non-pilot groups, or quality deterioration |

**Final recommendation:** Do not implement 50% reassignment now. Freeze the blanket move, publish the stratified reversal to management, and treat Orion's crude mean as a case-mix signal, not a performance verdict. Run Phase 0 immediately to reconstruct queues, verify completion selection, and define overlap eligibility. Then run a 6-8 week pilot randomizing only cases in demonstrated overlap cells (mainly Medium and Low, with compliance status and shift stratified), capping Nova's queue and protecting High/compliance cases for certified Orion review unless pilot evidence justifies inclusion. Simultaneously add temporary night compliance/queue capacity and test whether wait falls for both groups. Pre-register cycle, wait, service per review, rework, appeal and spillover queue metrics, with stopping rules for queue growth, rework or appeals. Scale only if randomized pilot shows a benefit in common-support cases without harming quality; otherwise keep complexity-based routing and invest in capacity, where the process evidence points.

```python
    lines = ["case_id,activity,timestamp"]

        log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(build_log_string(sub))
        dfg = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
    except Exception as e:
        print(f"WARNING: PM4Py parsing/DFG failed for {g} ({e}); using manual directly-follows counts.")
        dfg = manual_dfg(sub)
    dfgs[g] = dfg
    print(f"\nDFG for {g}:")
    for (a, b), c in sorted(dfg.items()):
        print(f"  {a} -> {b}: {c}")

print("\nDirectly-follows comparison (Orion vs Nova counts):")
all_edges = sorted(set(dfgs.get("Orion", {})) | set(dfgs.get("Nova", {})))
for e in all_edges:
    o = dfgs.get("Orion", {}).get(e, 0)
    n = dfgs.get("Nova", {}).get(e, 0)
    print(f"  {e[0]} -> {e[1]}: Orion={o}, Nova={n}")
    if o == 0 or n == 0:
        print(f"  WARNING: edge {e} has poor support in one group (Orion={o}, Nova={n}).")

if (df.groupby(["complexity", "group"]).size() < 3).any():
    print("WARNING: at least one complexity x group cell has fewer than 3 cases; stratified contrasts are fragile.")
print("Done. These diagnostics describe association and process structure only; they do not identify causal effects.")
```

## 8. Residual uncertainty
1. Unobserved case risk may drive routing beyond recorded complexity, so within-stratum Orion-Nova differences may still reflect case selection rather than group effects. Most valuable addition: a pre-assignment routing-risk score or recorded routing reason captured at Classify for every case.
2. Reviewer skill and certification heterogeneity within Orion and Nova are unobserved, so group averages may mask individual reviewer effects or skill-based assignment. Most valuable addition: pseudonymized reviewer_id with certification level recorded at the start of each review.
3. The completed-only sample may exclude abandoned or still-open cases differentially by group, complexity or cycle length, biasing all contrasts. Most valuable addition: case_status at extract date for all started cases, including open and abandoned with censoring time.
4. Queueing, compliance waiting, per-episode service and Request Info delays are aggregated, so mediated mechanisms cannot be separated from total cycle time and spillover cannot be reconstructed. Most valuable addition: event-level start and complete timestamps for every activity in the trace.
5. Overlap is sparse: Low Orion and High Nova have only two cases each, and Day Orion has one case, so many policy-relevant contrasts lack positivity support. Most valuable design change: a stratified randomized routing pilot restricted to demonstrated overlap cells.
