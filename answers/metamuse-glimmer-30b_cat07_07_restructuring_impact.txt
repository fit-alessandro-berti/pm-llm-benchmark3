* **Local knowledge loss:** Early informal advice prevented referrals. After restructuring referrals increase, rework shifts downstream. Observable as increased `local caseworker  Technical Shared Service` frequency and increased rework at Implementation.

* **Exception handling collapse:** Cross-team cover drops, resilience to absence falls. Absence-driven variants disappear, but `reassignment_count` spikes during peak absence periods.

# 5. Pilot design

* **Pilot scope:** Phase 1 pilot on high-complexity cases only, Team East as test group, Team North as control. Keep Intake and simple Eligibility local. Central Technical Shared Service activated for `case_complexity` = high and `required_specialty` in {legal,risk,security}.
* **Comparison group:** Team North unchanged embedded model for high-complexity cases. Team South as secondary control for medium complexity.
* **Pre/post observation window:** 8 weeks pre-pilot baseline, 8 weeks pilot, 4 weeks washout. Event log filtered by pilot period.
* **Segmentation:** By `team`, `case_complexity`, `required_specialty`, `customer_segment`, `location`. Also segment by `local_expertise_flag`.
* **Guardrail metrics:** Median wait after handover to Technical Shared Service >6h for 2 consecutive weeks; rework rate increase >+2pp vs baseline; 90th percentile cycle time increase >+15%; `reassignment_count` mean >1.5; specialist utilization >95% sustained.
* **Staff feedback collection:** Weekly pulse on role clarity, perceived accountability, informal advice availability. Link feedback to `org:resource` and correlate with waiting time and rework.
* **Rollback or adjustment triggers:** Any guardrail breached for 2 weeks  pause routing for high-complexity cases, increase shared service headcount or revert to embedded tech for pilot team. If East shows >10% improvement in 90th percentile and rework stable, expand to South. If North/South show degradation, modify routing thresholds.

# 6. Recommendation

Pilot first, do not proceed to full rollout. The restructuring can improve specialist utilization and standardize decisions, but it creates a central queue that is invisible in average cycle time and risks tail latency, rework, learning loss and fairness divergence between East and North/South.

Proceed conditionally:
* Run the phased pilot above with East high-complexity cases vs North control.
* Monitor leading indicators weekly: waiting time after handover to Technical Shared Service, queue variance, reassignment count, variant explosion.
* Require lagging confirmation: stable or improved 90th percentile cycle time, rework rate not increasing >1pp, SLA miss rate stable, fairness gap across teams <10%.

Management should not infer success from early average cycle time improvements. Average cycle time can improve due to automation and faster simple cases while high-complexity tail, rework and learning degrade. Decisions must be based on segmented performance DFG, handover network changes, and role clarity metrics.

If pilot shows central queue forming without guardrail breaches and East benefits without North/South degradation, proceed to modified rollout with capacity buffer for Technical Shared Service and retention of local technical coaching loops.
