## 1. What is observed

Automation availability is a binary branch-month indicator that turns on in April 2026 for Alpha and Beta (selected for high volume and historically poor cycle times) and in July for Gamma (after a short readiness pilot). Delta and Epsilon remain never-treated through August. Actual treatment intensity, captured by automated_fast_path_pct, starts near the background 4–6 % level and rises only gradually after availability (reaching ~48–50 % by August for the early adopters and ~32 % for Gamma).  

Branch selection was endogenous and non-random: managers targeted historically weak, high-volume sites. Contemporaneous operational changes include the March introduction of a centralized fraud desk serving all branches, an April-onward routing campaign that directed roughly 10 % more arrivals to Alpha/Beta (and 5 % more to Gamma after July), concurrent FTE movements (Alpha –2 in May, Beta +1 in April, Gamma –1 in June, Delta +1 in June), and a May product change that raised high_complexity_pct by approximately 5 percentage points across every branch.  

Outcome measurement is limited to branch-month aggregates. Median and p90 cycle times, rework and escalation percentages, and mean CSAT (1–5) are available, but case-level paths, specialist or fraud-desk waiting times, within-month variance, and CSAT response counts (response rates varied 20–45 %) are not. Managers could also alter local work practices during rollout. These features produce a staggered, intensity-ramping, heavily confounded observational panel of five branches over eight months.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|----------|---------------------------------|------------------|----------------------|-------------------|-------------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Primary: staggered event-study / group-time ATT (never-treated controls) | Relative-time indicators to first availability; calendar-month FE | Never-treated (Delta, Epsilon); later-treated as controls for early periods | high_complexity_pct, fte, log(arrivals) or arrivals/fte, branch FE | E1, E3, E4, E5, E6, E7 | Conditional parallel trends; no anticipation; limited interference; no other time-varying unobservables | Pre-trend coefficients; covariate balance; placebo adoption dates | Residual confounding + shared-desk interference + tiny clusters (N=5) |
| Alternative 1: synthetic control (per early adopter) | Binary post-availability | Weighted combination of never-treated (and pre-Gamma) matched on pre-period outcomes and covariates | Pre-period median/p90, complexity, FTE, arrivals | E1 / E3 for Alpha or Beta | Donor pool can reproduce pre-trends; no interference; stable weights | Pre-period fit (RMSPE); placebo-in-space | Only two pure donors; post-period shocks common; interference |
| Alternative 2: continuous intensity panel model | automated_fast_path_pct (or lagged) | Within-branch or within-month variation | Branch FE + month FE (or trends) + complexity + FTE + load | E2 (dose-response) | Conditional exogeneity of intensity given FE and covariates; no feedback | Residual plots; lead intensity tests | Endogenous intensity (managers/cases select); dynamic confounding |

The primary staggered event-study is preferable because the design supplies a clean never-treated group, staggered timing that permits relative-time diagnostics, and the ability to adjust for the major observed time-varying confounders (complexity shock, staffing, volume). It avoids the classic two-way fixed-effects contamination problem by focusing on never-treated comparisons and relative-time indicators. Power is extremely limited, so the exercise remains largely descriptive, yet it makes the parallel-trends assumption transparent and targets the policy-relevant availability contrast while still allowing intensity explorations as secondary analyses.

## 5. Quantitative evidence

Transparent windows: Alpha/Beta pre-period = Jan–Mar 2026 (automation = 0); overall post = Apr–Aug; late post = Jul–Aug. Gamma pre = Jan–Jun; post = Jul–Aug. Never-treated = Delta + Epsilon average.

**Branch-specific pre/post changes (adopters)**  
- Alpha: median –0.4 h overall / –3.3 h late; p90 +9.7 h; rework +0.5 pp; escalation +2.9 pp; CSAT +0.02; intensity +34 pp; arrivals +242; FTE –1.6; complexity +5.8.  
- Beta: median +1.9 h overall / –0.9 h late; p90 +9.8 h; rework –0.6 pp; escalation +2.5 pp; CSAT –0.02; intensity +34 pp; arrivals +207; FTE +1; complexity +2.  
- Gamma: median –3.4 h; p90 –0.9 h; rework –0.5 pp; escalation +2.0 pp; CSAT +0.09; intensity +24.5 pp.

**Comparison to never-treated**  
Never-treated (Jan–Mar → Jul–Aug): median +3.6 h, p90 +14.7 h, rework +2.5 pp, escalation +2.0 pp, CSAT –0.06.  
Rough DiD (adopter change minus never-treated change): median ≈ –4.5 to –5.5 h, p90 ≈ –5 to –7 h, rework ≈ –1.5 to –2 pp, escalation ≈ +0.7 to +1 pp, CSAT ≈ +0.08 to +0.10. Gamma versus never-treated yields similar relative median improvement (≈ –3.5 to –4 h).

**Treatment-intensity analysis**  
Overall correlation of automated_fast_path_pct with median +0.41, p90 +0.55, escalation +0.66 (selection-driven). Within post-adoption months only: median +0.20, p90 +0.41, rework +0.57, escalation +0.48. After simple branch and complexity adjustment the intensity coefficient on median is near zero or modestly negative, but standard errors are large.

**Pre-trends and regression to the mean**  
Jan–Mar levels are roughly stable across branches; the large common deterioration occurs in May–June (complexity shock) and partial recovery in July–August occurs for both treated and never-treated units. Adopters began with higher baseline cycle times (selection). Gamma (mid-tier baseline) also shows relative improvement, reducing pure mean-reversion explanations, yet residual regression-to-the-mean cannot be ruled out for Alpha/Beta.

**Sensitivity / bounded conclusions**  
- Excluding the May–June shock months attenuates relative median gains.  
- Assuming full mean-reversion for the two weakest branches moves the median DiD toward zero.  
- Bounding residual confounding from unobserved specialist waits or practice changes leaves the median relative improvement in the –2 to –6 h range and the p90 effect straddling zero. CSAT bounds remain near zero once non-response is acknowledged. All statements remain descriptive associations under explicit parallel-trends and limited-interference assumptions.

## 6. Threats, tests, and decision consequences

| threat | why it matters here | direction of likely bias | test or additional evidence | consequence if threat is severe |
|--------|---------------------|--------------------------|-----------------------------|---------------------------------|
| Selection into adoption | Weak, high-volume branches chosen first | Over-state benefits if mean-reversion; under-state if hard-to-improve | Compare Gamma; pre-trend plots; synthetic control | Spurious relative gains; over-optimistic targeting of weak sites |
| Non-parallel trends | Common May shock + differential recovery; different baselines | Ambiguous (often toward over-statement of benefits) | Event-study pre-trend coefficients; placebo dates | Invalid DiD / event-study |
| Time-varying confounding | Fraud desk, routing, staffing, product change all concurrent | Depends on correlation with adoption timing | Adjust for observables; residual diagnostics | Residual bias in any adjusted estimate |
| Treatment-intensity endogeneity | Managers / case mix determine actual fast-path use | Intensity models biased (harder residual cases) | Instrument with time-since-availability; case-level selection data | Misleading dose-response |
| Spillovers via fraud desk | Central desk; auto may send more borderline cases | Under-state branch benefits or create system-wide congestion | Desk wait times by source branch; never-treated escalation patterns | SUTVA violation; net system effect differs from branch effect |
| Staffing changes | Concurrent FTE movements | Confound capacity effects | Interact FTE × post; examine load | Mis-attribute staffing to automation |
| Case-mix shifts | May product change raises complexity everywhere | Dilutes or interacts with automation | Adjust / stratify by high_complexity_pct | Biased if automation differentially effective on complex cases |
| Anticipation | Managers knew rollout schedule | Contaminates pre-period | Relative-time –1 / –2 coefficients | Invalid identification |
| Survey non-response | 20–45 % response, no counts | CSAT biased up or down | Collect response N; link to operational metrics | Unreliable CSAT claims |
| Measurement aggregation | Branch-month medians / percentages only | Hides path heterogeneity and individual effects | Case-level event logs required | Misleading process conclusions |
| Small number of clusters | 5 branches, 3 treated, short panels | Unreliable SEs; fragile inference | Cluster-robust SEs with explicit warning; randomization inference | Over-confident policy claims |
| Dynamic / heterogeneous treatment effects | Learning, intensity ramp, early vs late adopters | Simple averages mask trajectories | Event-study relative time; group-time ATT | Wrong average for policy decisions |
| Managerial practice changes | Local work practices altered during rollout | Confounds pure technology effect | Fidelity metrics; qualitative data | Attribute management change to automation |
| Routing-campaign interference | Extra volume deliberately sent to adopters | Load increase worsens outcomes | Adjust arrivals; counterfactual volume | Net effect includes volume policy, not pure automation |

## 8. Rollout recommendation

expand with an embedded evaluation

Begin with Delta (lower historical complexity and cycle times, comparable size to Gamma) while retaining Epsilon as a pure never-treated control for at least four additional months; then phase Epsilon. Assign availability by a transparent staggered calendar (or, if operationally feasible, randomize the month of go-live or the initial intensity target within a narrow window). Maintain a minimum three-month clean pre-period (already largely available) and a four-month post-period before any scale-up decision.

Collect case-level event logs that distinguish the four process variants, specialist-review and fraud-desk waiting times by originating branch, daily queue-length snapshots, productive FTE by skill, and CSAT response counts (not only means). Monitor weekly branch-level median, p90, escalation, and CSAT; set guardrails that pause further intensity ramp if any branch’s p90 rises more than 10 h above its own pre-period or if CSAT falls more than 0.2 points for two consecutive months.

Spillover monitoring requires fraud-desk and specialist wait times stratified by source branch and a system-wide escalation series. After four post-months, apply the same event-study / relative DiD framework used here. Scale to remaining branches only if (a) relative median cycle improvement is at least –2 h, (b) relative p90 change is no worse than +3 h, (c) CSAT is stable, and (d) no material increase in shared-desk congestion is detected. This design simultaneously captures operational learning and generates higher-quality causal evidence than the current endogenous staggered rollout.

## 10. Final claim ladder

- Observed: Automation availability was accompanied by a gradual rise in automated_fast_path_pct (to ~50 % for early adopters); all branches experienced a common March escalation increase and a May complexity-driven cycle-time shock; adopters were deliberately selected for historically weak performance; relative to never-treated branches, adopters showed less median and p90 deterioration in the later post-period.
- Suggestive: After accounting for the common complexity and escalation shocks, the pattern of relative median-cycle improvement is consistent with a beneficial contribution from rising automation intensity, while escalation rose modestly and tail performance remained mixed.
- Conditionally causal: Under the assumptions of conditional parallel trends, limited interference, and no residual confounding from staffing or local practice changes, branch availability reduced median cycle time by approximately 4–5 hours relative to never-treated branches.
- Not supported: Any unconditional statement of the form “automation reduces cycle time by X %,” any claim of clear tail-time improvement, or any assertion of material CSAT gains.
