# 1. Current-process diagnosis
- **The dominant path is fast but defective:** `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval (2550)` + `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement (2300)` + `Dispatch -> Quality/compliance close (2320)` is by far the most frequent trace. Performance DFG shows why it is attractive: `Intake -> Auto screen mean 0.8h / median 0.5h`, `Auto -> Fast approval mean 1.0h / median 0.4h`, `Fast -> Dispatch mean 3.0h / median 1.8h`, `Dispatch -> Close mean 5.0h / median 3.2h`. This yields median 8h for Premium low-risk accessory, 13h for Standard low-risk, 20-28h for high-value on fast path.
- **Contradiction - speed/cost vs rework/compliance:** The same fast path drives systemic rework. `Customer complaint/reopen occurs in 9.2% of all cases`, but `14.1% of Fast approval cases vs only 3.3% of Repair quote -> Customer approval -> Repair device cases`. Absolute flows: `Fast approval -> Complaint 140`, `Dispatch -> Complaint 260`, then `Complaint -> Manual eligibility review 310` and `-> Escalation board 120`. Performance shows rework is long-tail: `Fast -> Complaint mean 72h / median 50h`, `Dispatch -> Complaint mean 96h / median 70h`, `Complaint -> Manual mean 40h / median 31h`, `Complaint -> Escalation mean 54h / median 42h`. Cost story collapses after rework: `Fast approval 31% cheaper before rework, but only 9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments, service credits`. Wrong-replacement cost is EUR 95 low-risk vs EUR 410 high-value, so the saving is segment-dependent.
- **Compliance gap hidden by high fitness:** Token-based replay `log fitness 0.91 but only 68% fitting traces` means ~32% of cases deviate, with frequent move-on-log deviations: `Fast approval before Evidence completeness`, `Dispatch without risk review for high-value devices`, `Complaint/reopen after close`. This directly violates R1 (claim >EUR 400 must have Manual or Fraud/risk review before Fast approval), R2 (no dispatch before evidence completeness except Premium low-risk accessory), and R3 (reopened claim must pass Manual review before close/denial). `Precision is low` because low-frequency combinations of auto-approval + risk review + repair proliferate. The discovered model over-generalizes policy.
- **Evidence loop is the cycle-time engine:** `Request missing evidence` is central: inflow `Auto -> Request 650 + Manual -> Request 780 =1430`, outflow `Request -> Manual 890 + Abandoned 320 + Fraud 220`. `26% of cases that enter it have >=2 occurrences`. Performance: `Manual -> Request mean 14.0h / median 10.0h`, `Request -> Manual mean 34.0h / median 26.0h / p90 88.0h`. This explains `Unclear defect / missing evidence: 22% share, median 140h, highest abandonment` (`Request -> Abandoned 320`, `Customer approval -> Abandoned 70`, total `Abandoned -> END 390`). Average cycle time is misleading; median vs p90 divergence and this loop dominate.
- **Quality/CX failure is segment-specific, not uniform:**
  - Premium low-risk accessory (18%): fast 6% complaint vs manual 3%, but `service-credit exposure high if >24h`. Median fast 8h vs manual 46h. Manual path breaches SLA.
  - Standard low-risk (25%): fast 8% vs manual 4%, tolerates 72h if visibility good. Median fast 13h vs manual 52h.
  - Premium high-value (14%): fast 22% complaint vs 5% reviewed/repair, median 20h vs 96h, plus high compliance risk if R1/R2 violated. Temporal deviation `Manual -> Repair quote often too slow for Premium` and `Dispatch -> Complaint long-tail linked to incorrect part selection` hit here hardest.
  - Standard high-value (21%): fast 18% vs 6%, median 28h vs 120h, cost of wrong replacement high.
  - Slow reviewed path is *not* always better: for low-risk accessories the absolute quality gain (3-4pp) does not justify +38-39h and service credits; for high-value the 13-17pp gain + EUR 410 error avoidance + compliance does.
- **Resources amplify the trade-off:** Auto screen EUR 0.20 unlimited but `rules outdated and not segment-aware` - explains 2550 auto approvals including high-value. Manual review: 7 reviewers x 36h/week at EUR 34/h, `queue grows sharply >82% utilization`. Fraud/risk: 2 specialists at EUR 52/h, `bottleneck Mondays/after campaigns` - yet only `Fraud -> Fast 240 + Repair 310 + Denied 210` flows through it, suggesting bypass. # 2. Redesign A: Upstream Precision - Segment-Aware Triage + Evidence-First Intake
- **Redesigned process flow:** At Intake, split by segment x risk x value before eligibility: (1) Premium/Standard low-risk accessory with clear evidence -> Guarded Auto Lane: updated segment-aware auto rules -> Fast approval -> Dispatch -> Close; (2) High-value or >EUR 400 or fraud signals -> Mandatory Manual and/or Fraud/risk review, no direct Auto->Fast; (3) Unclear defect / missing evidence -> Evidence-First Lane: structured evidence portal + completeness check must succeed before any Manual/Fast decision; `Request missing evidence -> Manual` loop is replaced by portal self-service with assisted fallback, max one structured loop before human outreach. R2 enforced in system: block Dispatch without evidence-complete flag except Premium low-risk accessory.
- **Targets segments, paths, activities:** Targets `Intake -> Auto (3600)` overuse and `Auto -> Fast (2550)` leakage to high-value; targets `Request missing evidence (1430 inflow, 26% repeat)` and `Manual <-> Request` 14h+34h loop; primarily serves low-risk accessories (43% of volume) and Unclear defect segment (22%).
- **Mechanism of improvement:** Prevents defects upstream rather than catching downstream. Updating auto rules (2-week deploy) stops R1/R2 violations at source; evidence portal cuts 30-45% of repeat requests, attacking median 140h unclear-defect time and 320 abandonments via first-time-right. Keeps fast median 8-13h for low-risk where quality delta is small (6% vs 3%, 8% vs 4%), preserving SLA and avoiding manual queue. Reduces `Fast before evidence` and `Dispatch without risk review` move-on-log deviations.
- **Deliberately sacrifices / leaves unresolved:** Does not fix wrong part selection (`Dispatch -> Complaint 260` long-tail remains) or repair delays; does not add pre-dispatch assurance. Accepts 15-35% increase in Manual inflow from tighter auto rules, pressuring 7 reviewers and 2 fraud specialists. Accepts portal may increase abandonment for low digital-literacy customers. Leaves `Complaint -> Manual 310` recovery burden in main flow.

# 3. Redesign B: Downstream Assurance - Repair-Default for High-Value + Pre-Dispatch Gate
- **Redesigned process flow:** Keep Intake/Auto largely as-is, but change decision and fulfillment: (1) All high-value devices (35% of cases: 14% Premium +21% Standard) default to `Repair quote -> Customer approval -> Repair device -> Quality check -> Close`, Fast approval->Dispatch disabled for them unless Fraud/risk review + evidence-complete both pass; (2) Add lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate after Fast approval / before Dispatch for remaining fast-lane cases: automated part-compatibility + value check, sampling full inspection for high-value exceptions; (3) Enforce R3 in workflow: any `Customer complaint/reopen` cannot go to `Close/Denied` without `Manual review`, and `Fast approval -> Close (350)` direct close is removed - must go via Dispatch or Quality check.
- **Targets segments, paths, activities:** Targets `Fast approval -> Dispatch 2300` and `Dispatch -> Complaint 260` + `Fast -> Complaint 140`; targets Premium/Standard high-value where fast complaint 18-22% vs reviewed 5-6% and wrong-replacement cost EUR 410; targets `Quality check (345 closes, 45 reworks)` underuse and `Rework repair 12.5% high-value vs 4.0% low-risk`.
- **Mechanism of improvement:** Trades 6-18h gate + longer repair path (Customer approval mean 30h, Repair->Quality 9h mean) for large rework reduction: shifting high-value from 18-22% complaint toward 5-6% and catching part errors before shipment avoids EUR 410 reship + 96h median complaint tail + Escalation load. Uses underused inspectors instead of overloaded reviewers. Improves conformance on R1/R2/R3 and precision by removing non-allowed auto->dispatch for high-value. Preserves speed for low-risk fast lane except small gate delay.
- **Deliberately sacrifices / leaves unresolved:** Deliberately slows Premium high-value (20h fast median -> 96h+ reviewed median), risking expectation breach and `Manual -> Repair quote too slow for Premium` finding. Increases pressure on 3 repair technicians (part-price wait) and 2 inspectors, and on `Customer approval` abandonment (currently 70). Does not solve upstream evidence loop (890 Request->Manual, 26% repeat) - Unclear defect 140h median persists. Adds cost upfront (technician/inspector hours) betting on downstream saving; net saving only 9% currently, so exact ROI cannot be known.

# 4. Redesign C: Recovery Decoupling - Separate Rework Lane + Visibility-Driven Waiting
- **Redesigned process flow:** Leave main happy path intact, but extract all exception handling: (1) Create dedicated Recovery Lane owned outside Manual reviewers: all `Customer complaint/reopen (460 total: 140+260+80+30 minus overlap)`, `Rework repair (45)`, and second `Request missing evidence` enter separate queue with case history packet and differentiated SLA; (2) Replace generic `Complaint -> Manual (310)` return with triaged return: minor accessory errors -> fast reship in recovery lane, high-value/compliance -> Escalation-tier; expand Escalation from single 45-min batch to two-tier: async senior reviewer for Standard + daily board only for Premium high-value/compliance; (3) Add proactive status tracker + estimated completion for Standard low-risk (tolerates 72h if visibility good) and human-assisted evidence help for low-literacy to prevent `Abandoned 390`; `Quality/compliance close -> END 3015` gets reopen-window control to stop `Complaint after close`.
- **Targets segments, paths, activities:** Targets rework/complaint subsystem and abandonment, not initial decision; serves Standard low-risk (25% visibility-sensitive), Unclear defect abandoners, and Premium complaint experience; relieves `Manual eligibility review (1400 entry + 890 +310 re-entry)` overload and `Escalation board` scalability limit.
- **Mechanism of improvement:** Prevents contamination: today rework (40h/54h mean) clogs the same 7 reviewers needed for first-time decisions, creating queue >82% spiral and slowing `Manual -> Repair quote mean 22h`. Decoupling protects throughput while improving CX via transparency and faster recovery (dedicated ownership, no Monday fraud bottleneck). Reduces abandonment without forcing digital-only portal. Keeps fast-lane speed unchanged, betting that fast + good recovery beats slow + prevention for low-risk segments.
- **Deliberately sacrifices / leaves unresolved:** Does not prevent initial defects - R1/R2 violations, wrong part selection, and 14-22% fast-lane complaints continue to occur; merely handles them better. Adds organizational complexity and handoff risk; requires new roles/routing. Leaves `Fraud/risk review` bypass and `Auto rules outdated` untouched. Risks moral hazard where frontline relies on recovery lane.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks
- **Redesign A failure modes:** (1) Tighter auto rules increase Manual inflow 15-35% beyond 82% utilization -> queue explosion: `Manual -> Request 14h mean / Request -> Manual 34h mean` worsen, Premium SLA breaches and service credits spike, negating evidence gains. (2) Evidence portal reduces repeats 30-45% on average but increases `Request -> Abandoned (320)` for low digital-literacy / unclear-defect customers, converting delay into loss and biasing complaint metrics downward artificially.
  - **Bottleneck migration:** From `Dispatch -> Complaint` downstream to `Manual eligibility review (7 reviewers)` and `Fraud/risk review (2 specialists, Monday peak)` upstream.
- **Redesign B failure modes:** (1) Repair-default + 6-18h gate breaches Premium high-value expectations: `Manual -> Repair quote already too slow for Premium`, `Customer approval mean 30h p90 80h` + part-price wait cause cancellations/escalations (`Escalation 120 inflow` overflows 45-min slot) and shift to `Claim abandoned / Denied`. (2) Sampling gate miscalibrated: too light misses EUR 410 part errors (no improvement), too heavy adds 18h to all low-risk fast cases destroying 8-13h advantage for only 3-4pp quality gain.
  - **Bottleneck migration:** From Manual/Fraud to `Repair quote (3 technicians)` + `Quality check (2 inspectors)` + `Customer approval wait`; Fraud idle while repair queue grows.
- **Redesign C failure modes:** (1) Recovery lane becomes hidden rework factory: without prevention, inflow `Complaint 460 + Rework 45 + repeat evidence` saturates new team, creating second queue with same 40-54h means, while frontline loosens discipline (low precision worsens). (2) Visibility without speed backfires: Standard customers told to wait 72h with tracker still complain if tracker is inaccurate, and reopen-window control on `Close -> Complaint after close` is perceived as denial of recourse, increasing Escalation and regulatory risk.
  - **Bottleneck migration:** From `Manual review` main flow to Recovery team + tiered Escalation; main-flow metrics improve cosmetically while total end-to-end (including recovery) stagnates.

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence
- **First pilot:** Start with A-light (segment-aware auto-rule update + R2 system block) on one low-risk accessory subsegment + one high-value control group, for 2-4 weeks. Rationale: 2-week deploy, reversible, directly tests core contradiction (does blocking Auto->Fast for >EUR400/high-value reduce 18-22% complaints without exploding manual queue?). Do NOT pilot full portal or full repair-default yet - isolate routing effect.
- **Metrics to monitor:** Segmented median + p90 cycle time (not average), `Request missing evidence` repeat rate and `Request -> Abandoned` rate, `Fast -> Complaint` and `Dispatch -> Complaint` rates by segment, R1/R2/R3 alignment fitness/% fitting, manual/fraud utilization and queue hours, wrong-part cost exposure, Premium >24h service-credit triggers, Standard visibility satisfaction/abandonment.
- **Stop/go thresholds (qualitative/ranges):** STOP/rollback if manual queue wait pushes Premium low-risk median much beyond 24h SLA or manual utilization stays >82% with growing p90, or abandonment rises materially in piloted group, or high-value complaint does not move down from 18-22% band. GO to next stage if fitting traces rise from 68% upward with stable/ median for low-risk, repeat-evidence flat/down, and complaint delta directionally down for high-value without SLA collapse. No exact ROI gate - require directional Pareto improvement on at least two dimensions without severe regression on third.
- **Sequencing logic:** (1) A-light routing -> (2) Add evidence portal with assisted fallback for Unclear defect only (tests 30-45% repeat reduction vs literacy abandonment) -> (3) Add B-gate sampled only for high-value dispatch (tests 6-18h cost vs EUR410 catch rate, using underused inspectors) -> (4) Add C-decoupling for complaints/rework if Steps 1-3 leave residual 310 Complaint->Manual load or escalation saturation. Order preserves speed for low-risk first, then assurance for high-value, then recovery - each step's bottleneck (reviewers -> technicians/inspectors -> recovery team) is observed before adding next load.
- **How to learn whether improvements are real rather than shifted downstream:** Track end-to-end including rework: `Intake to final END` plus `Complaint -> Manual -> Escalation -> Dispatch/Repair` tail, not happy-path `Intake -> Close` only; compare pre/post cost *after* reshipments/credits (31% vs 9% lesson); monitor displaced queues (manual, fraud Monday, repair, escalation slot) jointly; use segment-stratified comparison (Premium/Standard x low/high x unclear) with control group, check `Fast approval -> Close 350 bypass` and `Complaint after close` counts to catch close-gaming; require both conformance (% fitting, R-violations) and outcome (complaint, abandonment) to move together - if cycle time improves but fitting worsens or complaints shift to post-close, treat as shift not gain.
