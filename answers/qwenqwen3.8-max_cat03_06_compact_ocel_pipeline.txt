## 1. Modeling assumptions

- **Object types and capitalization**  
  I use singular, capitalized object types:
  - `Order`
  - `Item`
  - `Package`
  - `Delivery`
  - `Invoice`
  - `Payment`

  Object IDs keep the narrative prefixes: `O100`, `O200`, `I1`–`I3`, `P1`, `P2`, `D1`, `D2`, `V1`, `M1`, `M2`.

- **E2O qualifier vocabulary**  
  The event-to-object qualifiers describe the **role of the object in the event**, not static object-object relationships. The vocabulary used in the pipeline is:

  - `order_created`
  - `order_item_added`
  - `item_added`
  - `order_reserved`
  - `item_reserved`
  - `order_packed`
  - `item_packed`
  - `package_packed`
  - `package_dispatched`
  - `delivery_dispatched`
  - `delivery_confirmed`
  - `package_delivered`
  - `invoice_issued`
  - `order_billed`
  - `payment_recorded`
  - `invoice_partially_settled`
  - `invoice_settlement_completed`
  - `order_closed`

  These qualifiers are intentionally verbose so that a reviewer can distinguish operational participation from static relationships.

- **O2O direction conventions**  
  The `o2o` table is directed as **subject  object**, where the subject is the containing, carrying, billing, or settling object:

  - `Order --contains--> Item`
  - `Package --packs--> Item`
  - `Delivery --carries--> Package`
  - `Invoice --bills--> Order`
  - `Payment --partially_settles--> Invoice`
  - `Payment --completes_settlement--> Invoice`

  I do not store inverse rows. The inverse meaning can be derived analytically if needed, but the source-style O2O fact is stored once.

- **Directness of logistics and financial relations**  
  I use a middle-ground policy:

  - **Order/item creation and item addition** are directly linked to both `Order` and `Item`.
  - **Reserve Stock** is directly linked to the reserved items and to the orders operationally affected by the reservation.
  - **Pack Package** is linked to the package, the packed items, and the orders whose items are packed. This is defensible because packing is operationally performed for sales-order items.
  - **Dispatch Package** and **Confirm Delivery** are linked to `Package` and `Delivery`, but not directly to `Order` or `Item`. This avoids falsely suggesting that an entire order is delivered when `P1` combines items from two orders and `O100` is split across two packages.
  - **Issue Invoice** is linked to `Invoice` and both billed orders.
  - **Record Payment** is linked only to the `Payment` object and `Invoice V1`. Payments are not linked directly to orders or items because allocation is ambiguous without an explicit settlement rule.

- **Preventing analytical propagation from being confused with source facts**  
  The `relations` table contains only event-to-object links that are treated as operationally observed at event time. Static relationships remain in `o2o`. I do **not** materialize propagated links such as:

  - payment  invoice  order,
  - delivery  package  item,
  - package  item  order,

  unless I am willing to label them explicitly as derived. If an analyst wants propagated relations, they should create a separate analytical relation set with qualifiers such as `derived_order_delivered` or `derived_order_paid`, rather than mutating the source-style OCEL relations.

- **Version-dependent PM4Py assumption**  
  The pipeline assumes that PM4Py exposes an OCEL constructor compatible with:

  ```python
  ocel = OCEL(events=events, objects=objects, relations=relations, o2o=o2o)
  ```

  In some PM4Py versions, the OCEL 2.0 class may be named or imported differently, for example as `OCEL2`. If so, only the import/constructor line should be adjusted. The DataFrame semantics should remain unchanged.

## 4. Expected flattened behavior

### Flattening on `Order`

**Approximate trace shape**

- `O100` trace:
  1. `Create Order`
  2. `Add Item`
  3. `Add Item`
  4. `Reserve Stock`
  5. `Pack Package` for `P1`
  6. `Pack Package` for `P2`
  7. `Issue Invoice`
  8. `Close Order`

- `O200` trace:
  1. `Create Order`
  2. `Add Item`
  3. `Reserve Stock`
  4. `Pack Package` for `P1`
  5. `Issue Invoice`
  6. `Close Order`

**Copied shared events**

The following events are shared by both orders and will be copied into both flattened order traces:

- `E06 Reserve Stock`
- `E07 Pack Package P1`
- `E11 Issue Invoice V1`

**Omitted lifecycle aspects**

Because I intentionally did not link dispatch, delivery confirmation, or payments directly to orders, the flattened `Order` log omits:

- `Dispatch Package`
- `Confirm Delivery`
- `Record Payment`

Thus the order-centric view shows order administration, packing, invoicing, and closure, but not cash settlement or final delivery.

**Likely DFG edges**

Typical directly-follows edges include:

- `Create Order`  `Add Item`
- `Add Item`  `Add Item` for `O100`
- `Add Item`  `Reserve Stock`
- `Reserve Stock`  `Pack Package`
- `Pack Package`  `Pack Package` for `O100`
- `Pack Package`  `Issue Invoice`
- `Issue Invoice`  `Close Order`

**Misleading interpretations**

- Shared events appear as if each order independently performed them.
- The cross-order consolidation in `P1` is hidden.
- The absence of payment and delivery events may make it look like orders close immediately after invoicing.
- `O100` having two `Pack Package` events may be misread as rework unless the package/object context is known.

### Flattening on `Package`

**Approximate trace shape**

- `P1` trace:
  1. `Pack Package`
  2. `Dispatch Package`
  3. `Confirm Delivery`

- `P2` trace:
  1. `Pack Package`
  2. `Dispatch Package`
  3. `Confirm Delivery`

**Copied shared events**

## 6. Modeling alternatives

### Alternative 1: Source-minimal E2O relations

Under this policy, E2O relations would only include objects explicitly recorded by the source event. For example:

- `Pack Package P1` would relate only to `P1`, not to `O100` or `O200`.
- `Issue Invoice V1` might relate only to `V1`.
- `Confirm Delivery D1` might relate only to `D1`.

**Effect on discovery**

- Flattened order logs would become much sparser.
- Order-centric DFGs might miss packing, invoicing, or delivery-related steps.
- Object-centric graphs would show fewer cross-object synchronizations.
- Relation inflation would be minimized, but end-to-end traceability would weaken.

This policy is conservative and faithful to raw event capture, but it may understate operational connectivity.

---

### Alternative 2: Full operational involvement

Under this policy, every event that can be operationally associated with an object would be linked to that object. For example:

- `Dispatch Package P1` would be linked to `O100` and `O200`.
- `Confirm Delivery D1` would be linked to `I1`, `I3`, `O100`, and `O200`.
- Payments might be linked to orders through invoice allocation.

**Effect on discovery**

- Order-centric flattened logs would become more end-to-end.
- Order DFGs could include logistics and payment steps.
- Shared events would be copied into more cases, increasing frequency counts.
- The risk of relation inflation and misleading order-level conclusions would increase.

This policy improves apparent completeness but can hide the fact that some links are derived rather than directly observed.

---

### Alternative 3: O2O-propagated analytical relations with explicit derived qualifiers

Under this policy, the source-style OCEL would remain minimal, but an analyst would compute propagated relations separately. For example:

- `Payment M1` could be analytically related to `O100` and `O200` via `V1`.
- `Confirm Delivery D1` could be analytically related to `O100` and `O200` via `D1  P1  I1/I3  O100/O200`.

These propagated relations should use qualifiers such as:

- `derived_order_paid`
- `derived_order_delivered`
- `derived_item_delivered`

**Effect on discovery**

- Flattened order logs could show full order-to-cash and order-to-delivery lifecycles.
- Discovery would depend heavily on propagation rules.
- Payment allocation would require an explicit rule, such as proportional allocation or settlement priority.
- If derived relations are not clearly labeled, analysts may mistake them for source observations.

This is often the best analytical approach, but it requires clear separation between observed and derived facts.

---

### Alternative 4: Split shared events into per-order events

Another alternative would be to split multi-object events into separate events:

- One `Reserve Stock` event for `O100` and one for `O200`.
- One `Pack Package` sub-event for the `O100` part of `P1` and one for the `O200` part.
- One invoice line or invoice event per order.

**Effect on discovery**

- Traditional flattening becomes simpler.
- Cross-order synchronization disappears.
- The process looks more case-independent.
- The business reality of joint operations is lost.

This alternative can make classical process mining easier, but it weakens the object-centric story.

---

## 7. Failure modes

### 1. Relation inflation

If every possible O2O-derived link is added to `relations`, activities will appear connected to many more objects than were actually observed in the event. This can:

- inflate activity frequencies per object type,
- create overly dense OC-DFGs,
- produce misleading Petri-net synchronization,
- make shared events appear more important than they are.

In this dataset, linking every delivery and payment to all orders and items would cause substantial relation inflation.

---

### 2. Missing direct E2O links

If important direct links are omitted, object-centric views become incomplete. For example:

- If `Issue Invoice V1` is not linked to `O100` and `O200`, the invoice-order convergence disappears.
- If `Pack Package P1` is not linked to both orders, the cross-order packing behavior is hidden.
- If `Reserve Stock` is not linked to both orders, the joint reservation is lost.

This failure mode creates sparse but misleadingly simple process views.

---

### 3. Treating O2O propagation as observation

O2O relationships are static or master-data relationships. If they are propagated into E2O relations without labeling, analysts may believe the event system directly recorded those relations.

For example:

- `M1` settles `V1`.
- `V1` bills `O100` and `O200`.

It does not follow that the payment system directly observed allocation of `M1` to `O100` and `O200`. Treating that inference as observed can lead to incorrect compliance or performance conclusions.

---

### 4. Duplicate shared events after flattening

Flattening copies shared events into each related object case. In the order-flattened view:

- `E06 Reserve Stock` appears once for `O100` and once for `O200`.
- `E07 Pack Package P1` appears once for `O100` and once for `O200`.
- `E11 Issue Invoice V1` appears once for `O100` and once for `O200`.

This is expected, but frequency-based DFGs may overstate how many times the business performed the activity independently.

---

### 5. Ambiguous payment allocation

`M1` and `M2` are recorded against `V1`, not against `O100` or `O200`. Without an allocation rule, we cannot say how much of each payment should be attributed to each order.

If an analyst forces payment-order links, the result depends on assumptions such as:

- proportional allocation,
- oldest-order-first allocation,
- item-price-based allocation,
- invoice-line allocation.

The dataset itself does not determine the allocation.

---

### 6. Overly short Payment traces

Flattening on `Payment` produces one-event traces:

- `M1`: `Record Payment`
- `M2`: `Record Payment`

Such traces have no meaningful directly-follows structure. They hide the fact that the payments are part of a settlement process for `V1`. Payment behavior should be analyzed together with `Invoice` or through object-centric relations.

---

### 7. Constructor/API version mismatch

PM4Py’s OCEL support can vary by version. Possible mismatches include:

- `OCEL` versus `OCEL2`,
- different import paths,
- different return types for discovery functions,
- different visualization function names.

The safest approach is to isolate the constructor and discovery calls. The DataFrame semantics should remain stable even if the PM4Py API wrapper changes.

---

### 8. False total order for same-time events

This particular dataset has no timestamp ties. However, in general, flattening and discovery may impose a total order by sorting on timestamp, event ID, or object ID. If two events have identical timestamps, the inferred order may be artificial.

Even here, if derived events were added with rounded timestamps, one could accidentally create ties and infer false precedence. Timestamp precision and stable sorting matter.
