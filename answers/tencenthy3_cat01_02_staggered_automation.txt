Treatment-intensity analysis.** Within adopters, comparing first post-adoption month to Jul–Aug: Beta autofp +23pp (2750) and median 2.3h (~ 0.10 h/pp); Gamma autofp +7pp (2532) and median 2.4h (~ 0.34 h/pp); Alpha autofp +23pp (2548) with median 5.0h from Apr to Aug but a mid-period spike during complexity/FTE shock. *Arithmetic:* higher fast-path share co-moves with lower median in Beta/Gamma. *Interpretation:* confounded by learning, routing, and case mix.

**4. Pre-trends and regression to mean.** Jan–Mar shows all branches drifting upward (e.g., Gamma median 36.439.6, Delta 34.436.2) and escalation rising in March with fraud-desk launch. Adopters were selected for high baselines, so some relative improvement could reflect regression to mean; however never-treated branches worsened more by Jul–Aug, arguing against pure mean reversion. Pre-trend checks in an event-study are essential.

**5. Sensitivity / bounded conclusions.** For `median_cycle_h`, if one (risky) attributes the difference between adopter  and control  to automation, Alpha vs Epsilon implies ~ 5.5h ( 11% of pre), but a defensible bounded range is **6% to +3%** per branch with low confidence. For `p90_cycle_h`, adopters’ increases resemble controls, so automation tail effect is bounded **5% (offset) to +10% (harm from spillovers)**. CSAT bounded **0.1 to +0.1**, indistinguishable from noise.

## 6. Threats, tests, and decision consequences

|---|---|---|---|---|

| nonparallel trends | branches follow different trajectories | confounds diff-in-diff | pre-adoption event-study coefficients | invalid inference |

## 8. Rollout recommendation

Recommendation: `expand with an embedded evaluation`

We advise against a blanket expansion or a pure pause. The current evidence is insufficient for a definitive causal claim but suggests possible median-cycle benefits in high-baseline branches and clear tail/escalation risks. Therefore the organization should proceed to Delta and Epsilon but only under a phased, evaluation-embedded design that also improves identifiability.

Next branch subset: Activate automation in Delta first (e.g., 2026-09), while holding Epsilon as a never-treated control for at least three monthly post-adoption measurements. This creates a new staggered step, expanding the number of adoption cohorts from three to four and giving a fresh control. Within Delta, enforce a randomized case-level eligibility rule (or at minimum a monitored target) so that `automated_fast_path_pct` varies across weeks, providing clean treatment-intensity variation beyond the binary flag.

Minimum measurements: Use the already observed May–Aug 2026 period as Delta’s extended pre-baseline (4 months). Require at least 3 post-adoption months for Delta before any decision about Epsilon, and thereafter a further 3 months after Epsilon activation.

Process-mining and queue data to collect: case-level event logs with variant tags (fast path, escalated, rework loops), specialist-queue lengths, and—critically—fraud-desk waiting times, which are currently missing. Daily FTE and arrivals by risk class, plus CSAT response counts (not just means) to correct survey selection.

Spillover monitoring: track fraud-desk load and cross-branch routing shares weekly; if borderline-case volume to the desk rises >15%, alert.

Guardrails: if p90_cycle_h or escalation_pct exceeds its pre-trend projection by >10% for two consecutive months, or CSAT (adjusted for response rate) drops >0.2, pause further scale-up and audit specialist capacity.

Decision rule for scale-up: expand to Epsilon only if Delta shows an adjusted median-cycle reduction of at least 3% with a cluster-robust CI excluding zero, and no tail degradation beyond +5% relative to trend. This balances operational benefit with causal learning.

## 10. Final claim ladder

- Observed: After adoption, `automated_fast_path_pct` rose from ~4% to 25–50% in Alpha, Beta, and Gamma, while escalation rates increased by 3–4 pp and p90 cycle times rose across all branches including never-treated controls.
- Suggestive: Relative to never-treated Delta and Epsilon, Alpha’s typical (median) cycle time did not rise as much and may have modestly improved, implying a possible small ITT benefit on median latency under changing case mix.
- Conditionally causal: If one assumes conditional parallel trends and no unmeasured spillovers, a dose-response association suggests each +10 pp of automated fast-path use corresponds to roughly 0.1 to 0.3 h in median cycle in Beta/Gamma, but this remains confounded by contemporaneous complexity and staffing changes.
- Not supported: A blanket statement that “automation reduces cycle time by X%” across branches, especially for tail (p90) performance or customer satisfaction, is not supported by the supplied data.
