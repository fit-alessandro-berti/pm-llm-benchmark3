## 1. Executive causal assessment
Verdict: `partly causal through mechanisms`

Confidence: `68/100`

The crude comparison suggests Orion is slower: mean cycle time is 21.96h for Orion versus 15.24h for Nova, a +6.72h gap. But that is not a credible causal estimate of an Orion total effect. The strongest evidence against a blanket harmful Orion effect is the aggregate-versus-stratified reversal: within every observed complexity stratum Orion is faster, not slower (Low 7.50h vs 9.64h, Medium 17.17h vs 19.38h, High 31.57h vs 35.00h). Using the pooled completed-case complexity distribution as common weights, the complexity-standardized Orion-minus-Nova contrast is about -2.51h. That strongly suggests confounding by case mix and operating regime.

The strongest evidence that resource group still matters causally is that it is not a mere label. In this process, assignment to Orion comes bundled with night coverage, difficult-case routing, more compliance-required work, and a different reviewer pool. Those are real mechanisms. As a total effect, the crude Orion association is misleading; as a direct effect net of queueing/compliance/rework, evidence of Orion harm is weak; any plausible Orion effect is more likely mediated through queue_wait, compliance handling, night operations, and reviewer allocation. The crude Orion disadvantage is also more queue-like than pure service (+4.01h wait versus +2.00h review service).

The proposed 50% reassignment may help only if it relieves a genuine queue bottleneck and Nova receives matching night/compliance capacity. It may fail or backfire if it simply moves hard night work into a less prepared queue, while only completed cases are observed and overlap between groups is poor.

Supported by the prompt: `routing_rule -> resource_group`, `complexity -> compliance_required`, Orion's concentration in difficult/night work, and `queue_wait`/`review_service`/`rework -> cycle_time`. ## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests
| rank | hypothesis | process mechanism | evidence supporting it | evidence contradicting it | discriminating analysis or falsification test | predicted observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Orion is beneficial or neutral after conditioning on case mix | Harder cases are deliberately routed to Orion because it has more certified reviewers; those reviewers shorten wait/service/rework relative to Nova for comparable cases | Within every observed complexity stratum Orion is faster; pooled complexity-standardized contrast is about `-2.51h`; conditional rework is not worse | Overlap is poor on `shift` and `arrival_load`; unobserved case risk may still differ within each complexity bin | Exact or propensity-score matching in overlap cells on `complexity`, `shift`, `arrival_load`, `compliance_required`, and trace; inspect overlap/positivity and run sensitivity analysis for hidden case risk | If true, Orion remains non-inferior or faster in matched support, and the sign does not flip under moderate hidden-bias sensitivity |
| 2 | Shift/load regime, not the Orion label, drives most delay | Night work and high load inflate queueing; Orion merely carries that operating regime | Orion is `92.9%` night and almost always high load; overall night mean is `21.74h` vs day `13.98h`; Nova night is also slower than Nova day | Even with that worse regime, Orion is still faster within complexity; some within-group day/night differences are inconsistent because cells are tiny | Reconstruct queue state at assignment and run a small randomized or quasi-random routing pilot within night/high-load strata, or an interrupted experiment that swaps coverage blocks while holding capacity explicit | If true, delay follows queue state and night/high-load exposure; moving night load to Nova without added capacity reproduces the slowdown there |

## 6. Intervention and learning plan
| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 0: Instrument first | Add reviewer ID/certification, queue snapshot at routing, compliance start/end timestamps, and open-case status; freeze routing thresholds during measurement | Separate confounding, mediation, and selection | All incoming cases plus daily backlog snapshot | No routing change yet; QA threshold on missingness | Queue length at `Classify`, `Classify -> Compliance`, `Compliance -> Review`, review durations, variants, open-case aging | Makes later comparisons interpretable and reveals overlap/positivity limits | If any key field is missing on more than 5% of cases, extend this phase and do not reassign work yet |
| Phase 1: Capacity relief without relabeling | Add temporary night/compliance capacity or an overflow reviewer pool for Orion's high-load queue | Queueing and compliance backlog | Shift block or queue block | Protect Nova daytime SLA; cap overflow volume; preserve specialist access for truly hard cases | Wait decomposition, queue length trajectory, compliance-edge frequency, cycle time by shift/complexity | Tests whether the bottleneck is capacity rather than the Orion label | Roll back if Nova queue age rises more than 20% or rework/appeal rates worsen by more than 3 percentage points |
| Phase 2: Stratified routing pilot | Randomize or quasi-randomize 10-20% of overlap-eligible cases between Orion and Nova within `complexity x shift x arrival_load x compliance_required` strata | Total causal effect of actual assignment in supported overlap | Case | Exclude rare specialist cases; hard WIP caps; daily monitoring of waits and quality | `cycle_time`, `queue_wait`, `review_service`, `rework`, variant, open-case hazard, appeal | Strongest identification available without full-scale disruption | Stop any stratum if mean wait worsens by more than 2h for two consecutive reviews or appeal rate rises by more than 3 percentage points |
| Phase 3: Scale by mechanism | If pilot works, change dynamic routing rules based on queue state, compliance capacity, and certification, not a flat 50% split | Sustainable load balancing | Routing rule by shift/complexity bucket | Keep a holdout cohort; stagger rollout; weekly review of queue spillovers | Standardized cycle time, DFG/variant stability, queue distribution, open-case aging, quality | Tests transportability beyond the pilot and avoids overgeneralizing from one region of support | Roll back any bucket whose standardized cycle time or open-case aging degrades more than 10% versus pilot baseline |

Do not approve the blanket 50% Orion-to-Nova reassignment. The data do not support the story that Orion is intrinsically slow; after even coarse complexity adjustment, Orion looks at least as good and probably better. What looks slow is the operating bundle around Orion: night-heavy work, compliance exposure, and queue accumulation. The safer immediate move is to instrument the queue and compliance steps, then relieve that bottleneck directly with temporary night/compliance capacity. In parallel, run a small stratified routing pilot only where both groups can credibly handle comparable work. That yields a defensible causal estimate in the overlap region and avoids destabilizing specialist handling of the hardest cases. Expand reassignment only if the pilot improves cycle time without worse rework, appeals, or open-case aging. If the pilot fails, keep the work where expertise sits and fix capacity, not labels.

## 8. Residual uncertainty
1. Coarse `complexity` may hide important residual severity differences. Most valuable addition: a pre-routing case-risk score or claim-severity field recorded before assignment.
2. The observed wait measure does not reveal actual queue state at routing. Most valuable addition: queue length or WIP by group at the moment each case is classified/routed.
3. The reviewer-pool mechanism is latent. Most valuable addition: reviewer ID with certification level for each review event.
4. Compliance is only observed as a yes/no requirement, not as a timed subprocess. Most valuable addition: compliance start and end timestamps.
5. The sample is conditioned on completion. Most valuable design change: retain open and abandoned cases in the extract with censoring date/status for survival-style analysis.
