**Shared mechanisms.** Both sites have two verification sub-tasks whose wall-clock time is the sum of their durations if sequential and approximately the maximum if concurrent. Both have an Assessment that waits for both. Both have queue wait before Assessment, and both show complexity raising cycle time, rework and appeal.

**Site-specific mechanisms.** Originals gating blocks Document Check start for 62% of B's cases. Legal review and supplement loops exist only at B. B's single-owner lock and separate role pools mean concurrency requires coordination. Back-entry affects measurement only at B.

**Effect modifiers.** `originals_required` moderates the mechanism itself because it removes the edge. `role_specialization` and `IT_case_lock` determine whether concurrency is executed. `legal_review_required` moderates tail and rework. `case_complexity` moderates magnitude. Identity and document capacity moderate whether saved service time becomes saved waiting. `digital_documents` is a selection variable that changes the causal pathway, not just its size.

**Why a similar control-flow diagram does not guarantee transportability.** The same parallel operator can produce different outcomes. First, statutory edges can make the same diagram illegal at one site. Second, resource and lock constraints determine whether modeled concurrency is executed at all. Third, parallelization saves time only when verification is on the critical path, and upstream originals delays can dominate. Fourth, measured overlap can be produced by recording practice. Fifth, co-interventions (API, staffing, huddles) act on the same outcome nodes, so the before/after difference at A mixes several effects. Transport requires the same mechanism to operate under comparable effect modifiers, not an identical graph.

## 5. Quantitative transport scenarios

**Baseline.** Site B: median 44 h, p90 110 h.

**Reasoning.**
- Eligible share for identity–document parallelization. The DFG shows 1,160 Identity  Document handoffs out of 3,400 cases, which is 34%. These are the non-original cases where a serial handoff occurs without any rule requiring it. For the 62% of originals-required cases, Document Check cannot start before receipt. Identity (median 3.4 h) finishes well inside the originals delay (median 18 h), so the critical path gain is about zero.
- Per-case saving. For an eligible case, the ideal saving is about min(3.4, 8.1)  3.4 h. Allowing for queueing and imperfect execution, use 1.5–3.4 h.
- Population effect. About 34% × 1.5–3.4 h  0.5–1.2 h on average, so the median shifts by roughly 0.5–1.5 h. This is not the 8–10 h implied by copying A.
- Originals-request lever. The DFG shows Identity  Originals Receipt in 1,020 cases (30%). If originals requests start at registration rather than after identity, the originals wait could fall by up to 3.4 h for these cases. Whether the 18 h delay is measured from request or from registration is unknown.

## 6. Candidate Site B intervention

**6.1 Eligibility rules.**
- Phase 1: cases where originals are not legally required, the case is not public-sector or high-value (no legal review), and complexity is below the top tail threshold.
- Phase 2 (only if gates pass): add legal-path cases with a rule that document release waits for legal triage or uses reversible draft outputs.
- Originals-required cases stay on the current sequence, with a separate test of earlier originals requests (see 6.2).

**6.2 Concurrency rules.**
- Identity Check and Document Check may run concurrently for eligible cases.
- For originals-required cases, Identity Check may run concurrently with Originals Receipt. Document Check may not start before Originals Receipt. Document Check may not start before Originals Receipt is recorded, even if the document image is available.
- Originals Receipt request should be issued at registration (not after identity completion) in the originals-start arm of the pilot.
- Assessment starts only when Identity Check and Document Check are both complete. This is non-negotiable.

**6.3 Statutory prerequisites that remain mandatory.**
- Originals must be received before Document Check where legally required.
- Assessment requires completed identity and document checks.
- Legal Review applies to public-sector and high-value cases.
- Supplement requests trigger repeat document checking and reassessment.
- Final decision precedes Notify.

**6.4 Queue and WIP controls.**
- Per-reviewer WIP cap of six open verification tasks (retained from A) with a separate cap for document tasks.
- Release rule: do not release document tasks if the document queue exceeds a threshold (set during Phase 0 from baseline queue distributions).
- Daily queue huddle in both arms, with a standard agenda that includes WIP and queue age.

**6.5 Resource responsibilities.**
- Identity reviewers own identity tasks. Document reviewers own document tasks.
- Dual-qualified reviewers may take both tasks only within a pilot pod and only under WIP caps.
- Assessors own the Assessment task and may see completion states of both checks.
- Legal reviewers are unchanged in phase 1.

**6.6 Legal-review and supplement loops.**
- Phase 1: legal-path cases follow the current sequence.
- Phase 2: document outputs from parallel work are marked as provisional until legal triage. A supplement request invalidates provisional outputs, logs the reason, and returns the case to Document Check. Reassessment is required.

**6.7 Logging requirements.**
- Each sub-task (identity, originals, document, assessment, legal, supplement) logs system-generated start and complete events with reviewer ID and pool.
- No back-entry for primary outcomes. Any back-entry is flagged and time-stamped.
- Originals request and receipt events are logged separately.
- Lock overrides are logged.
- Supplement reason codes and affected tasks are logged.
- Queue entry and exit times per pool are logged.

**6.8 Partial-order relation set (POWL-style).**

```text
Nodes:
  R = Register
  I = Identity Check
  O? = XOR(Originals Receipt, skip)           # data-dependent in practice
  D = Document Check
  S = Assessment
  L? = XOR(LOOP(Legal Review, [Request Supplement -> Document Check -> Assessment]), skip)
  X = XOR(Approve, Reject)
  N = Notify

Partial order (edges mean "must finish before start"):
  R -> I
  R -> O?
  O? -> D                 # statutory: no Document Check before originals when required
  I -> S                  # Assessment waits for identity
  D -> S                  # Assessment waits for document
  S -> L?
  L? -> X
  X -> N

Unordered (permitted concurrency):
  I  || O?                # identity runs while originals are awaited
  I  || D                 # only in the non-original case (guard G_nonorig)
  O? || (any other node not on the chain above)

Guards (not expressible as pure POWL):
  G_nonorig: originals_required = false  -> permits I || D without O? -> D
  G_legal:   public_or_high_value = true -> L? executed
```

**How the pilot separates mechanism failure from implementation failure (220 words).** A mechanism failure means that when the intended concurrency is actually executed, the expected saving does not occur. Implementation failure means concurrency is not executed as intended, or is executed in a way that causes other harm.

The pilot separates these by measuring two things. Fidelity is measured from system-generated events: the share of eligible cases where identity and document work genuinely overlap, lock overrides, and back-entry. Mechanism is measured within fidelity-compliant cases: the critical-path reduction and cycle-time change, compared with control cases in the same pod-block. If the overlap ratio is low, the failure is implementation (locks, roles, training). If overlap is high but critical-path time does not fall, the failure is mechanism. Here, the likely explanation is that the saved service time is hidden by upstream waits (originals, queue) or moved into another queue. If critical-path time falls but rework or tail rises, the failure is a side effect of the mechanism, most likely through loops.

Stratification distinguishes these further. A null effect in originals cases with a clean overlap ratio indicates the statutory gate, not the implementation, is the constraint.

## 8. PM4Py model-construction code

```python
# Self-contained sketch. Constructor names follow PM4Py POWL and process-tree APIs
# as commonly documented; verify against the installed version (2.7+ assumed).
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    Transition, SilentTransition, StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Operator
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Site A process trees (before and after)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
A_BEFORE = ("-> ( 'Register', 'Identity Check', 'Document Check', "
            "'Assessment', X ( 'Approve', 'Reject' ), 'Notify' )")
A_AFTER = ("-> ( 'Register', + ( 'Identity Check', 'Document Check' ), "
           "'Assessment', X ( 'Approve', 'Reject' ), 'Notify' )")

tree_a_before = pm4py.parse_process_tree(A_BEFORE)
tree_a_after = pm4py.parse_process_tree(A_AFTER)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Convert process trees to accepting Petri nets
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
net_a_before, im_a_before, fm_a_before = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_a_before)
net_a_after, im_a_after, fm_a_after = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_a_after)

print("Site A before is workflow net:", pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net_a_before))
print("Site A after is workflow net:", pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net_a_after))

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Site B candidate as POWL (strict partial order + optional legal loop)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
register = Transition(label="Register")
identity = Transition(label="Identity Check")
originals = Transition(label="Originals Receipt")
doc = Transition(label="Document Check")
assessment = Transition(label="Assessment")
notify = Transition(label="Notify")
approve = Transition(label="Approve")
reject = Transition(label="Reject")

# Originals receipt is conditional on a legal flag. In reality this is a data guard;
# POWL can only express a choice here.
originals_opt = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                             children=[originals, SilentTransition()])

# Legal review loop: Legal Review, then optionally (supplement -> document -> assessment -> Legal Review)*.
# Same activity labels are used for repeats, so the log vocabulary matches.
legal = Transition(label="Legal Review")
supplement = Transition(label="Request Supplement")
doc_redo = Transition(label="Document Check")
assess_redo = Transition(label="Assessment")
rework = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[supplement, doc_redo, assess_redo])
rework.order.add_edge(supplement, doc_redo)
rework.order.add_edge(doc_redo, assess_redo)
legal_loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[legal, rework])
legal_opt = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                         children=[legal_loop, SilentTransition()])

decision = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[approve, reject])

site_b = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, identity, originals_opt, doc,
                                   assessment, legal_opt, decision, notify])

# Explicit order edges (absence of an edge = permitted concurrency)
site_b.order.add_edge(register, identity)        # identity after registration
site_b.order.add_edge(register, originals_opt)   # originals request after registration
site_b.order.add_edge(originals_opt, doc)        # statutory: no document check before originals
site_b.order.add_edge(identity, assessment)      # Assessment waits for identity ...
site_b.order.add_edge(doc, assessment)           # ... and for document
site_b.order.add_edge(assessment, legal_opt)
site_b.order.add_edge(legal_opt, decision)
site_b.order.add_edge(decision, notify)
# Note: no edge identity -> doc, so identity and document are concurrent in the model
# (the non-original guard is a data condition, see limitations below).

# Sanity check of mandatory edges where the API allows it
def _has_edge(spo, a, b):
    try:
```

## 9. Recommendation

**Choice: conditional go.** Do not copy Site A's intervention as proposed.

**What should be transferred.** The mechanism: Identity Check and Document Check may run concurrently where no statutory prerequisite blocks document work. Assessment must still wait for both. Also transferable: the queue-discipline practices (WIP caps and daily huddles), the requirement for event-level sub-task timestamps, and the stratified before/after analysis design.

**What should not be copied.** The "for every case" parallel release, which would start Document Check before originals are received for 62% of cases and violate the statutory order. A's 9-hour median reduction as an expected effect: under independence, concurrency alone can save at most about 3.5 h at the median, and most of A's gain likely came from waiting, mix, capacity and measurement. A's overlap rate (71%) as evidence of concurrency. Phase-1 scope should not include legal-path cases.

**Which subgroup enters first.** Non-original cases that are not public-sector or high-value, below the top complexity tail. In the baseline, about 34% of B's cases show an Identity  Document handoff, which is where serialization is observed and no rule requires it.

**Expected benefits and risks.** Expected median reduction is about 0.5–1.5 h (1–3%) and a small p90 improvement in this segment (scenario S1). Larger gains would require reducing the originals wait (scenario S5), which is a separate and possibly more valuable test. Risks: lock workarounds that inflate measured overlap while true concurrency stays low (S2); bottleneck shift to the document pool (S3); and, in phase 2, legal supplement loops that worsen the tail while average cycle time improves (S4).

**Triggers for expansion, modification or abandonment.**
- Expand to phase 2 (legal-path cases with provisional outputs) if: critical-path reduction is at least 0.5 h in the eligible segment; rework and supplement-return rates are not worse than control by the stop thresholds; p90 is not worse; overlap ratio from system events is at least 60% in executed cases; and no statutory-order violations occur.
- Modify if: overlap is high but critical-path time does not fall (the saved time is hidden by queue, so target the queue); or the document pool saturates (adjust release rule or add dual-qualified capacity).
- Abandon the parallel release if: any statutory-order violation occurs; back-entry or lock override exceeds 5% and cannot be controlled; or rework rises by more than 2 pp with no compensating gain.

Separately, the originals-request-at-registration lever should be evaluated on its own, since it targets the dominant B-specific delay.

## 10. Assumption register

1. **Originals requirement is observable at registration.** Why needed: to decide eligibility for Document Check start. How to test: audit a sample of cases for legal-flag accuracy against the statute and document records. Consequence if false: eligibility errors produce illegal early document work or lost gains.

2. **The DFG Identity  Document handoffs (1,160) reflect lock-driven serialization, not a rule-based or documentary dependency.** Why needed: to justify the 34% eligible share. How to test: interview teams and inspect whether document tasks could have started; compare handoff timing with lock events. Consequence if false: the eligible share is smaller and the expected gain drops toward zero.

3. **Originals delay (median 18 h) is measured from the originals request, and the request can be issued at registration.** Why needed: to estimate the originals-start lever (S5). How to test: review timestamps for request, dispatch and receipt. Consequence if false: the originals-start lever has no effect and the 18 h delay is intrinsic.

4. **Identity and document service times at B are independent of each other and of queue state.** Why needed: to bound the per-case saving at min(service times). How to test: correlation of service times at case level; queue-state covariates. Consequence if false: saving is either larger (positive correlation through shared case features) or smaller (congestion) than assumed.

5. **Dual-qualified reviewers, or coordinated release across separate pools, can execute concurrency without idle capacity elsewhere.** Why needed: to justify the operational design. How to test: time-and-motion or utilization data by pool; pilot pod pilot with utilization tracking. Consequence if false: concurrency shifts the bottleneck, producing S3 outcomes.

6. **Site A's before/after effect is not identified; the residual effect after removing mix, capacity, API and measurement changes is unknown.** Why needed: to justify not using A's magnitude. How to test: stratify A's data by complexity and digital status; obtain a negative-control process; re-estimate with pre-trend. Consequence if false: A's mechanism is larger than assumed and S1 understates the benefit, but the no-go on magnitude copying still holds.

7. **Supplement returns (345 Request Supplement  Document Check) are caused by legal review and not by parallel release.** Why needed: to assess the risk of phase 2. How to test: reason codes for supplements; comparison of supplement rates for parallel-released and control cases in the pilot. Consequence if false: parallel release may increase supplement rates through rushed checks, which would enlarge the tail risk in S4.

8. **The DFG frequencies are internally consistent and represent the full six-month population.** Why needed: to use 1,160, 2,108, 360 and 345 as population counts. Note: Legal Review outflows (680 + 300 + 360 = 1,340) do not exactly equal Assessment  Legal Review (1,360), and Request Supplement  Document Check (345) does not equal 360. How to test: reconcile event counts against the case table; check for incomplete cases and unlogged events. Consequence if false: shares and scenario ranges shift by a few percentage points, though the qualitative conclusions hold.
