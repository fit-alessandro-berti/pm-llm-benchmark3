## 1. Materiality Framework

Severity should reflect the business control objective, not the alignment edit distance alone. A practical five-level framework is:

| Severity | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 1 – Negligible | Harmless technical noise, expected retries, UI telemetry, or a clearly fitting hidden routing step. No meaningful control, customer, financial, or reporting impact. |
| 2 – Low | Documentation, terminology, or model-scope issue with little evidence of an unsafe payment or customer harm. Requires repair or monitoring, but not urgent case escalation. |
| 3 – Moderate | A potentially meaningful control-evidence gap, notification-evidence weakness, or process/model mismatch that could impair auditability or operational assurance. |
| 4 – High | Material policy non-compliance, unreliable evidence for a high-value payment, or a control sequence issue with meaningful exposure but some mitigating evidence or reversibility. |
| 5 – Critical | Payment executed before required approval, or another severe control failure involving high value, high fraud risk, recent bank change, irreversibility, or likely regulatory/customer harm. |

Severity assessment should consider:

- **Control objective:** Was coverage validated, loss assessed, fraud screened, approval obtained, payment authorized, and claimant notified as required?
- **Amount and exposure:** A EUR 480,000 payment with incomplete fraud evidence is intrinsically more material than a EUR 1,200 payment.
- **Risk indicators:** HIGH fraud risk and recent beneficiary-bank changes materially increase payment-control risk.
- **Irreversibility and timing:** A payment already accepted by the bank before approval is more serious than a late or missing log entry for a completed assessment.
- **Customer and regulatory harm:** Late or unprovable notification, improper payment, weak fraud controls, and poor audit evidence can create conduct, complaints, AML/fraud, and supervisory risk.
- **Recurrence:** A single bad record may be local; repeated patterns suggest a design, integration, or operating-control failure.
- **Evidence reliability:** A signed external report is stronger than an absent core-system event; a mutable CRM text link is weaker than immutable correlated evidence.
- **Model scope:** Denied or withdrawn claims may be legitimate paths outside a model designed only for payable claims. They should not automatically be treated as operational non-compliance.
- **Data-aware policy conditions:** The model allows Auto Approve structurally, but P1/P2 impose amount, risk, and bank-change conditions. A synchronous path can still be a policy failure.

**Confidence is not severity.** Severity asks: “If this happened as indicated, how serious is it?” Confidence asks: “How reliable is the conclusion given the evidence?” For example:

- CL-604 is high-confidence, critical severity because bank acceptance and approval timestamps establish payment-before-approval.
- CL-606 has potentially moderate severity but lower confidence because the assessment may have occurred externally and simply not been logged in the core system.
- CL-608 has moderate-to-high evidence concern, but not necessarily a notification failure, because a linked portal message exists but its linkage is mutable.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Alignment cost is a technical measure of how many log/model edits are needed under a configured cost function. Here, every visible log or model move costs one. That is useful for identifying structural mismatch, but it is not a measure of fraud exposure, compliance risk, customer harm, financial materiality, or certainty.

Several supplied cases demonstrate this directly:

1. **CL-602: raw cost 1, critical business concern.**  
   The only alignment deviation is `M(Fraud Screen)`. Technically that is one missing model event. Business-wise, it concerns a EUR 480,000 HIGH-risk payment after a beneficiary-bank change one day earlier. P2 requires Fraud Screen and Manual Approve before payment. A uniform cost of one cannot represent the difference between a missing fraud screen on this case and a missing event on a low-value claim.

2. **CL-604: raw cost 3, critical and potentially irreversible failure.**  
   The alignment requires an unmatched early payment plus inserted fraud screen and later payment. The business issue is that the bank accepted payment at 10:03 and manual approval was recorded at 10:20. P3 explicitly states that payment before applicable approval is severe. The important fact is not that three edits were needed; it is that funds were released before the required authorization and may be difficult to recover.

3. **CL-609: raw cost 0, high policy failure.**  
   The trace is fully synchronous because the process tree permits Auto Approve structurally. However, P1 only allows Auto Approve when amount is at most EUR 5,000, fraud risk is LOW, and no recent bank change occurred. EUR 18,000 and MEDIUM risk violate those conditions. Control-flow alignment does not test data predicates unless they are explicitly modeled or evaluated separately.

4. **CL-610: raw cost 0, critical fraud-control concern.**  
   The claim is EUR 4,500 and LOW risk, but the beneficiary bank changed one hour before payment. P1 prohibits Auto Approve after a recent bank change, and P2 requires Fraud Screen and Manual Approve before payment. The structural model path fits perfectly, while the data-aware policy result is severe. This is a clear example of a **zero-cost policy failure**.

5. **CL-601: raw cost 3, negligible business concern.**  
   The three log-only moves are Open Workbench, Autosave, and Close Workbench. P5 says UI telemetry and autosave events are not business activities unless they reveal a meaningful issue. The technical cost is therefore inflated by activity granularity: the log records interface behavior that the business model intentionally does not represent.

6. **CL-603: raw cost 1, likely harmless retry noise.**  
   The second Notify Claimant event has the same message ID and is associated with a retrying connector. The alignment sees an extra visible event. But the business may have sent one notification and retried its transport. The event model should distinguish business notification from transport attempt, rather than treating every technical retry as a customer-contact deviation.

7. **CL-605: raw cost 0 because a silent move is fitting.**  
   The `T(tau,c=0)` transition is hidden routing behavior introduced in the Petri-net representation. It is not evidence of an omitted activity or a control failure. Silent transitions show why the mechanics of model execution must not be equated with observable business work.

8. **CL-606: raw cost 1, but external evidence may support compliance.**  
   The core log lacks Assess Loss, producing `M(Assess Loss)`. Yet a licensed external adjuster signed an assessment report before fraud screening and approval. The missing log event is an evidence-integration issue unless investigation shows the report is invalid, unrelated, or untimely. Alignment cannot itself determine whether off-system evidence is sufficient.

9. **CL-611: raw cost 2, likely terminology rather than process failure.**  
   `Assess Damage` appears in the log while `Assess Loss` appears in the model. If they represent the same controlled task, the two edits arise from label mismatch, not from a missing assessment plus an extra unrelated activity. Activity semantics and governance of mappings matter.

10. **CL-607 and CL-612: raw cost 4, largely model-scope mismatch.**  
    Denial and withdrawal cause the payable model to insert assessment, approval, payment, and notification steps. But a denied lead should not be paid, and a withdrawn claim should not proceed to approval/payment. High cost here may indicate that the model does not represent legitimate non-payable outcomes, not that operations skipped multiple mandatory steps.

Therefore, technical cost is informative when it identifies deviations worth examining, but it must be interpreted through:

- the **cost function** chosen;
- **policy data conditions** outside the control-flow model;
- **event granularity** and technical telemetry;
- **cross-system evidence** and correlation quality;
- **label semantics**;
- **model scope**;
- **timing and irreversibility**;
- and the distinction between a missing event and a missing underlying activity.

# 2. Construct a small event log from case traces

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Construct a risk-prioritization evidence table
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# This is deliberately not a replacement for expert judgment.
# It preserves separate columns for raw alignment cost and business severity.
# The "review_priority_band" is an operational triage aid, not a risk formula.

def review_priority_band(row):
    if row["business_severity"] >= 5:
        return "Urgent investigation / immediate control action"
    if row["business_severity"] == 4:
        return "Priority remediation and case review"
    if row["business_severity"] == 3:
        return "Targeted evidence validation and remediation"
    if row["business_severity"] == 2:
        return "Governance / model / data repair"
    return "Monitor or suppress from business-risk queue"
