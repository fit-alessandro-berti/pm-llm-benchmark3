The dynamic policy aims to balance six competing socio-technical objectives:
1. **Short-term throughput:** Minimize waiting times and clear urgent items to prevent SLA breaches.
2. **Skill development:** Facilitate targeted upskilling of trainees by routing them to appropriate complexity cases under supervision.
3. **Workload fairness & Overtime Risk:** Distribute new work evenly to prevent burnout, actively protecting resources flagged for high overtime risk.
4. **Continuity of ownership:** Minimize handovers to avoid context switching delays and customer frustration, provided skill levels safely permit it.
5. **SLA risk management:** Prioritize urgent cases for immediate assignment to specialists.
6. **Resilience & bottleneck avoidance:** Prevent specialist silos from creating massive backlogs by utilizing competent generalists or supervised trainees for appropriate tasks.

### Hard Constraints (Safety & Compliance)
1. **Skill eligibility:** Resources cannot be assigned to a skill marked `0` (not allowed).
2. **Batch limits:** No resource receives more than 4 new assignments in a single batch.
3. **Supervision for high-priority:** No high-priority (urgent/high) work can go to a trainee (skill 1) unless supervised by a specialist (skill 3). High-priority tasks strictly require a skill level of 2 or 3.
4. **Security rules:** Security Reviews require skill 3, or skill 1–2 explicitly supervised by a skill 3 specialist (R7_Gita or R8_Hugo).
5. **Approval rules:** Final Approvals require skill 2 or 3. Skill 1 may assist but cannot finalize.
6. **Overtime protection:** Avoid assigning to high-overtime-risk resources unless no safer alternative exists and the SLA risk is urgent.

### Soft Constraints (Preference Trade-offs)
1. **Continuity vs. Complexity:** Continuity is strongly preferred to save handover costs. *Exception:* High-complexity cases assigned to skill 1–2 (where error risk exceeds handover delay) should be routed to a specialist.
2. **Training allocation:** At least 20% of normal/low-priority tasks should be routed to trainees to support their documented training priorities, provided SLA risks remain low.
3. **Batching:** Quality Checks can be batched if priority is normal/low and SLA time remaining is >24h.

# 2. Assignment policy

The assignment policy follows a deterministic, multi-stage scoring algorithm. For each case $C$ in the queue:

**Step 1: Eligibility and Hard Constraint Filtering**
* Filter out resources with `skill = 0` for the `required_skill`.
* Filter out resources where `overtime_risk = "high"` (unless urgent and no alternatives).
* Filter out trainees (`skill = 1`) for high-priority cases unless a specialist is co-assigned for supervision.

**Step 2: Scoring Mechanism (Sum of normalized scores 0–100)**
* **SLA Urgency Score (0–30):** Urgent (30), High (20), Normal (10), Low (5).
* **Skill Match Score (0–25):** Specialist/skill 3 (25), Competent/skill 2 (15), Trainee/skill 1 (5).
* **Continuity/Handover Cost Score (0–20):** Current owner (20). If handover cost is high, subtract 15 if changed. If complexity > 0.7 and owner is skill 1, override continuity to favor skill 3 (to prevent 3.3h rework delays).
* **Workload/Overtime Fairness Score (0–15):** $15 \times \frac{\text{Hours Remaining Today}}{8}$. If `overtime_risk = "high"`, divide score by 4.
* **Training & Development Score (0–10):** Award 10 points if the task is Normal/Low priority, matches the resource's `training_priority`, and the 20% training threshold has not yet been met in the current batch.

**Step 3: Tie-breaking and Exception Handling**
1. Ties are broken by lowest `current_open_cases`.
2. If a trainee scores highest, automatically schedule a specialist (highest available score for that skill) as a supervisor.
3. If a High-Complexity case has an owner with a skill < 2, it is removed from the owner and routed to the highest-scoring specialist to mitigate the historical 2.7h handover wait vs. 3.3h rework penalty.

# 6. Process-mining audit indicators

To ensure the deployed policy is functioning as intended, the following indicators should be tracked using the event logs:

1. **Handover Frequency:** (Avg `reassignment_count` per case). *Target: < 1.5.* (Should decrease compared to First-Available).
2. **Skill Mismatch Rate:** % of activities performed by a resource with `skill < 2` without a logged supervisory co-occurrence. *Target: 0% for urgent tasks.*
3. **Workload Distribution Variance:** Standard deviation of `current_open_cases` across resources at the time of assignment. *Target: < 3.0.*
4. **Overtime Risk Exposure:** Number of new assignments granted to resources flagged `overtime_risk = "high"`. *Target: < 10% of total assignments.*
5. **SLA Miss Rate:** % of cases where `time:timestamp` at completion exceeds `sla_deadline`. *Target: < 2%.*
6. **Training Assignment Share:** % of normal/low cases routed to resources matching their `training_priority`. *Target: > 20%.*
7. **Specialist Bottleneck Indicator:** Average `workload_snapshot` of skill-3 resources compared to skill-1/2 resources. *Target: < 1.5x difference.*
8. **Rework/Appeal Rate:** % of cases requiring a "Rework Correction" activity immediately following an assignment by the new policy. *Target: < 5%.*
9. **Deviations from Policy:** Instances where an auditor or manager overrides the automated assignment. (Tracked via the `assignment_reason` column).
