## 3. Causal-abductive explanation

```text
UI_release -> policy_change -> ETL_change -> reviewer_behavior -> true_process -> recorded_labels -> recorded_order -> normative_model -> alignment_moves -> measured_fitness -> business_outcomes
```

- **UI release (May 31)** introduced a new label (`Risk Screen`) for a process step that previously used `Classify`. This label change is logged inconsistently, causing mismatches between the recorded logs and the normative model. The UI change itself does not alter the underlying process flow but introduces semantic ambiguity.
  
- **ETL change (June 15)** altered how timestamps are recorded for `Decision` and `Notify` events. The ETL pipeline now uses warehouse-ingestion timestamps instead of source-system sequence numbers. This change affects the timing alignment between the real process and the model, leading to increased misalignments and reduced fitness scores.

- **Reviewer behavior (July 10–August 12)** shows a pattern of noncompliance, particularly with the addition of a `Supervisor Sign-off` requirement for high-value claims. While the normative model does not include this step, the manual audit indicates that some reviewers occasionally bypass compliance checks. This behavior introduces additional deviations that are not captured by the model.

- **Technical-event and extraction defects** (June-July) create further inconsistencies. The introduction of `Queue Routed` events after risk screening, coupled with the ETL change, results in traces where the decision appears before the notify action. These discrepancies contribute to the observed alignment cost and misaligned traces.

- **Obsolete model** (normative process tree) fails to capture the new labeling and added compliance checks. As a result, the model cannot accurately align with the actual traces, leading to a significant drop in conformance. The model's inability to accommodate the new `Risk Screen` step and `Supervisor Sign-off` requirement exacerbates the misalignment.

- **Hidden variables and incomplete data** (e.g., missing high-value case attributes, incomplete incident detection) further obscure the true extent of the conformance drop. Without reliable audit samples to verify whether certain deviations are genuine bypasses or missing events, it is challenging to pinpoint the exact cause of the issue.

These mechanisms jointly create the observed pattern of conformance collapse by introducing semantic mismatches, timing inconsistencies, and noncompliant behavior that are not accounted for in the existing model. The model's control-flow only design fails to capture the nuanced changes introduced by UI updates, ETL adjustments, and procedural modifications, leading to a significant decrease in alignment and conformance metrics. Despite the presence of potential employee noncompliance, the root cause lies primarily in the model's obsolescence and the technical and semantic defects introduced by recent changes.

## 7. Provisional conclusion

The conformance collapse observed in the data is most plausibly attributed to an **obsolete normative model** that fails to account for recent process changes introduced by the UI release, ETL modifications, and additional compliance requirements. The introduction of `Risk Screen` and `Queue Routed` events, along with the requirement for `Supervisor Sign-off` on high-value claims, were not reflected in the original model. These changes led to significant label mismatches and timestamp inconsistencies, resulting in a high alignment cost and a marked decrease in conformance.

While employee noncompliance and technical defects play roles, they are secondary to the structural mismatch between the model and actual operations. The evidence suggests that the model's inability to accommodate these new elements has led to increased misalignments and reduced alignment fitness. The compliance reports indicating no material change in appeal rates further complicate the picture, as they suggest that underlying compliance issues may persist despite the observed conformance drop.

Immediate actions should focus on updating the process model to include the new `Risk Screen` and `Supervisor Sign-off` steps. Additionally, efforts should be made to correct the technical event logging issues and ensure that all case attributes, such as the high-value status, are properly captured and used in analysis. A manual audit of compliance steps and a review of reviewer behavior may also be necessary to identify any noncompliant practices that could be contributing to the observed outcomes.

## 8. Model-governance recommendation

1. **Version control of process models**: Establish a versioning system for all process models to track changes and ensure that any updates are documented and reviewed.

2. **Event-semantic contracts**: Define clear contracts for event semantics to prevent semantic drifts, such as ensuring that `Risk Screen` is consistently mapped to `Classify` across all contexts.

3. **Technical-event policy**: Implement a policy for handling technical events like `Queue Routed` to ensure they are either standardized or excluded from conformance checks based on their relevance to the process.

4. **Timestamp provenance**: Maintain rigorous provenance of timestamps to ensure accurate timing analysis, especially when using ETL pipelines that may alter event timing.

5. **Data-aware constraints**: Incorporate data constraints directly into the process model to prevent deviations that could lead to conformance issues, such as ensuring that high-value claims require a `Supervisor Sign-off`.

6. **Post-release validation**: Conduct comprehensive validation after any significant changes to the process or system to ensure that the updated model accurately reflects the real-world process and maintains high conformance levels.
