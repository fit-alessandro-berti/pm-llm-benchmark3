# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

For case \(c\) and branch \(i \in \{A,B,C\}\), define:

- \( \text{arrival}_c \): entry to the overall process.
- \( \text{fork\_release}_c \): earliest time all branch work is permitted to begin.
- \( S_{c,i} \): branch \(i\) start.
- \( E_{c,i} \): branch \(i\) completion.
- \( F^{\text{start}}_c \): final activity start.
- \( F^{\text{end}}_c \): final activity completion.

The decomposition components are:

\[
\text{upstream\_elapsed}_c = \text{fork\_release}_c - \text{arrival}_c
\]

\[
\text{queue}_{c,i} = S_{c,i} - \text{fork\_release}_c
\]

\[
\text{service}_{c,i} = E_{c,i} - S_{c,i}
\]

Under the stated AND-join rule, \( \text{join\_ready} \) is not directly logged but is:

\[
\text{join\_ready}_c = \max_i E_{c,i}
\]

Branch-specific synchronization waiting after completion is:

\[
\text{sync\_wait}_{c,i} = \text{join\_ready}_c - E_{c,i}
\]

The elapsed fork-join stage time is:

\[
\text{fork\_join\_elapsed}_c = \text{join\_ready}_c - \text{fork\_release}_c
\]

Downstream queueing before the final activity is:

\[
\text{downstream\_queue}_c = F^{\text{start}}_c - \text{join\_ready}_c
\]

Final service is:

\[
\text{final\_service}_c = F^{\text{end}}_c - F^{\text{start}}_c
\]

Total case flow time is:

\[
\text{total\_flow\_time}_c = F^{\text{end}}_c - \text{arrival}_c
\]

The no-double-counting identity is:

\[
\text{total\_flow\_time}_c =
\text{upstream\_elapsed}_c +
\text{fork\_join\_elapsed}_c +
\text{downstream\_queue}_c +
\text{final\_service}_c
\]

For each branch \(i\),

\[
\text{queue}_{c,i} + \text{service}_{c,i} + \text{sync\_wait}_{c,i}
=
(S_{c,i} - \text{fork\_release}_c)
+ (E_{c,i} - S_{c,i})
+ (\text{join\_ready}_c - E_{c,i})
=
\text{join\_ready}_c - \text{fork\_release}_c
=
\text{fork\_join\_elapsed}_c
\]

Therefore, summing all branch queue + service + synchronization components gives:

\[
\sum_i \left(\text{queue}_{c,i} + \text{service}_{c,i} + \text{sync\_wait}_{c,i}\right)
=
3 \cdot \text{fork\_join\_elapsed}_c
\]

So summing all branch synchronization waits would triple-count the elapsed fork-join interval. For example, in F02 the synchronization waits are A=10, B=0, C=35, summing to 45 minutes, but the actual fork-join elapsed time is only 60 minutes. The sum of all branch queue+service+sync components is 180 minutes, exactly \(3 \times 60\). The synchronization waits are overlapping slack, not additive elapsed delay.

Reasoned influence ranking:

1. **B is the strongest branch controller of total completion time.**  
   B is the critical branch in 8 of 12 cases. Its mean queue-plus-service time is 56.67 minutes, higher than A’s 50.00 despite A having the largest mean service. B also has the largest tail: p90 queue-plus-service is 74.0 minutes and max is 75 minutes. When B is critical, the mean fork-join elapsed time is 65.0 minutes, the highest among branches. A counterfactual 50% reduction in B queue delay saves 92.5 minutes of join_ready time across the 12 cases.

2. **A is second, but its large service time is misleading.**  
   A has the largest mean service time, 50 minutes, but zero observed queue and is critical in only 2 of 12 cases. A 20% A-service reduction saves only 15 minutes of join_ready time across all cases, because A is not critical in most cases and in F01 the next branch B is only 5 minutes behind.

3. **C is the weakest branch controller.**  
   C has the lowest mean queue-plus-service time, 30.58 minutes, and is critical in only 2 of 12 cases. Its large mean synchronization wait, 30.42 minutes, is mostly a symptom of finishing early, not evidence of control. A supplementary 20% C-service reduction would save only about 9.4 minutes of join_ready time: F04 saves 4.4 minutes and F08 saves 5.0 minutes.

Ties and near-ties:

- There are no exact observed ties for the critical branch.
- Important near-ties: F01 has A at 09:00 and B at 08:55, only 5 minutes apart; F11 has A at 11:30 and B at 11:20, 10 minutes apart; F10 has B at 11:20 and A at 11:15, 5 minutes apart; F08 has C at 10:50 and A at 10:45, 5 minutes apart.
- These gaps cap the benefit of accelerating the nominally critical branch.

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

The following are estimates, not observations. I assume ceteris paribus: only the targeted branch or downstream queue changes; all other starts, services, and queues remain fixed.

I distinguish:

- **join_ready saving**: reduction in the max branch completion time.
- **realized total-flow saving under a fixed downstream lag**: if `final_start` moves earlier by the same amount as `join_ready`, then total flow saving equals join_ready saving.
- **realized total-flow saving under a fixed final_start policy**: if `final_start` is fixed by release policy, branch acceleration may produce zero total-flow saving even though `join_ready` improves.
- **capacity-constrained bound**: if the final station is the only constraint, realized saving is between 0 and the join_ready saving.

## CF1: Reduce A service time by 20%

A service is 50 minutes in every case, so a 20% reduction is 10 minutes.

Direct join_ready benefits:

- F01: A_end moves from 09:00 to 08:50, but B_end is 08:55, so join_ready improves only from 09:00 to 08:55: **5 minutes**.
- F11: A_end moves from 11:30 to 11:20, tying B_end at 11:20, so join_ready improves from 11:30 to 11:20: **10 minutes**.

Zero-benefit cases:

- F02–F10 and F12: A is not the critical branch, so join_ready does not change.

Secondary effects:

- A resources would be freed 10 minutes earlier in every case, which could reduce future A queueing if A capacity is constrained.
- In F01, B becomes the new critical branch; in F11, A and B tie.
- If `final_start` is fixed, the realized total-flow saving is zero despite the join_ready improvement.

## CF2: Reduce B queue delay by 50%

B queue is halved, so B_end moves earlier by half of B_queue.

Direct join_ready benefits:

- F02: 10 minutes
- F03: 10 minutes
- F05: 10 minutes
- F06: 15 minutes
- F07: 15 minutes
- F09: 12.5 minutes
- F10: 5 minutes
- F12: 15 minutes

Zero-benefit cases:

- F01, F04, F08, F11: A or C remains critical.

New critical-branch pattern after CF2, using deterministic tie-breaking A before B before C:

- A becomes critical or tied in F01, F02, F05, F06, F10, F11: 6 cases.
- B remains critical in F03, F07, F09, F12: 4 cases.
- C remains critical in F04, F08: 2 cases.

Secondary effects:

- B resources are freed earlier, which may reduce future B queueing.
- A becomes the new branch bottleneck in many cases, so further B-only improvements will have diminishing returns.
- The fork-join tail improves: old max fork_join_elapsed is 75 minutes; after CF2 it is 60 minutes.
- If `final_start` is fixed, the realized total-flow saving is zero despite the join_ready improvement.

## CF3: Reduce downstream queueing by 50%

This is a proxy for adding final-station capacity or improving final release policy.

Direct total-flow benefits:

- Every case has positive downstream queue, so every case benefits.
- Aggregate saving: 88.0 minutes; mean saving: 7.33 minutes.
- Full elimination of downstream queue would be an upper bound of 176.0 minutes aggregate, 14.67 minutes mean.

Important caveat:

- The observed final services do not overlap on the same final resource. At `join_ready`, both F1 and F2 are busy only in F09 and F10; in the other 10 cases at least one final resource is idle at `join_ready`.
- Therefore downstream queue is not primarily explained by final-station capacity congestion. It may be a release policy, unobserved resource unavailability, or another fixed lag.
- Adding a third final resource is unlikely to capture most of the 88-minute saving unless the release/scheduling rule also changes.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

## Recommendation 1: Attack the B queue

**Redesign:** Add B capacity, cross-train staff to B, or split B work into parallel stations so that B queue delay is reduced by at least 50%.

**Expected benefit:**  
Under CF2, aggregate join_ready saving is 92.5 minutes across 12 cases, mean 7.71 minutes. The fork-join tail improves from a maximum of 75 minutes to 60 minutes. This is the largest branch-specific lever identified.

**Throughput:**  
Earlier `join_ready` means cases can enter the final stage sooner, increasing throughput if arrivals are steady.

**Flow-time tail:**  
The largest B-queue cases improve substantially: F06, F07, and F12 each save 15 minutes; F02, F03, and F05 save 10 minutes.

**Resource utilization:**  
B resources are freed earlier. If B queue is caused by insufficient B capacity, utilization of the new B capacity should be monitored; if B queue is caused by batching or release policy, added capacity may be underused.

**Possible new bottleneck:**  
A becomes critical or tied in 6 of 12 cases after CF2. A’s constant 50-minute service time becomes the next branch constraint.

**Synchronization effects:**  
In cases where A or C remains critical, reducing B queue only increases B’s synchronization slack; it does not improve `join_ready`.

**Pilot/measure first:**  
Measure B queue start times, B resource capacity, B service distribution, and whether B queue is caused by capacity, batching, or skill constraints. Pilot the change on a subset of cases and measure B queue, `join_ready`, `final_start`, and total flow time.

## Recommendation 2: Change the final release/downstream queue policy

**Redesign:** Start the final activity as soon as `join_ready` is reached and a final resource is actually available, rather than allowing a fixed downstream lag. Target at least a 50% reduction in downstream queue.

**Expected benefit:**  
Under CF3, aggregate total-flow saving is 88.0 minutes, mean 7.33 minutes. Full elimination is an upper bound of 176.0 minutes, but that is not justified by the capacity evidence.

**Throughput:**  
Earlier final completions free F1 and F2 sooner, improving final-station throughput.

**Flow-time tail:**  
The largest downstream queues are 20 minutes in F10 and F11; a 50% reduction saves 10 minutes in each. F07, the longest total-flow case at 130 minutes, saves 7.5 minutes.

**Resource utilization:**  
Final resources are currently not saturated: each has six non-overlapping 20-minute services. Over the full 07:55–12:40 window, each final resource is busy 120 of 285 minutes, about 42%. Earlier starts would raise utilization but should remain feasible.

**Possible new bottleneck:**  
If downstream queue is removed, branch fork-join delay becomes more visible. B remains the main branch bottleneck, so Recommendation 1 is still needed.

**Synchronization effects:**  
This change does not alter branch synchronization waits; it only reduces time after `join_ready`.

**Pilot/measure first:**  
Measure actual F1/F2 availability, the release rule, and the reason for `final_start - join_ready`. Do not simply add a third final resource without evidence, because the observed delays are not primarily capacity-driven.

# 4. Counterfactuals

# CF1: reduce A service time by 20%.
# A service is 50 minutes in every case, so this is a 10-minute reduction.
new_A_end = df["A_end"] - pd.to_timedelta(0.20 * df["A_service"], unit="m")

new_crit_cf2 = pd.concat([df["A_end"], new_B_end, df["C_end"]], axis=1).idxmax(axis=1).str[0]
print("\nCF2 new critical-branch counts:", new_crit_cf2.value_counts().to_dict())

# CF3: reduce downstream queue by 50%.
df["cf3_total_saving"] = 0.50 * df["downstream_queue"]

# Realized total-flow savings under a fixed downstream lag equal join_ready savings.
# If final_start is fixed by policy, realized savings are zero; the data cannot
# distinguish these policies.
df["cf1_total_saving_fixed_lag"] = df["cf1_join_saving"]
df["cf2_total_saving_fixed_lag"] = df["cf2_join_saving"]

df["cf2_total_saving_fixed_start"] = 0.0

cf_cols = ["case", "cf1_join_saving", "cf2_join_saving", "cf3_total_saving"]

# 7. Limits of inference

- **Causality is not identified.** The timestamps show when branches started and ended, but not why B queued, why C queued, or why final starts were delayed. B queue could be capacity, batching, skill, or release policy.
- **Branch resource capacity is unobserved.** There are no branch resource IDs or capacity logs. A’s total service time is 600 minutes across 12 cases, which implies multiple A resources or overlapping work, but the exact capacity cannot be inferred.
- **Work preemption is not identifiable.** Service time is computed as end minus start. If a branch paused and resumed, that idle time would be incorrectly counted as service.
- **Final-station waiting may not be a true queue.** `final_start - join_ready` is downstream queueing only under the model assumption. The observed final services do not overlap on the same resource, and at most `join_ready` times both F1 and F2 are busy. Therefore the downstream delay may be a release policy, unobserved resource unavailability, or another fixed lag rather than ordinary queueing.
- **Synchronization waiting is inferred, not logged.** `join_ready` is computed as the latest branch completion under the stated AND-join rule. If the real join rule differs, or if there is hidden rework, the synchronization waits change.
- **Counterfactuals are ceteris paribus estimates.** They assume no dynamic arrival changes, no resource reallocation, and no behavioral response. In steady state, reducing one bottleneck may shift congestion elsewhere.
- **The sample is small.** Twelve cases give useful process-mining insight, but percentages and means are unstable for statistical inference.
- **Minute-level timestamps limit precision.** Counterfactual values such as 12.5 minutes are model outputs, not observed event times.
