## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

**Factual mechanism:** The 58.5 h cycle is dominated by three serial queues, not by service content: 13 h before D1's 8 h complex check, ~9.5 h before A1, and ~16 h before C1. The sanctions ambiguity was visible at Triage but only became actionable after A1 re-discovered it, so S1 advice (Tue 19:20–21:20) and the compliance evidence assembly landed *after* the last assessment, and C1 then read a complex corporate file from scratch (4.5 h).

**Minimal intervention:** Add two non-gating edges at Triage — an S1 preliminary packet and a provisional C1 slot — plus optional C1 pre-read that overlaps the D1 queue. Nothing is removed: Identity, D1 complex check, A1 assessment, final C1 review, M1 decision and notify all remain, in the same order, on the same resources.

**Temporal propagation:** The intervention cannot move the critical path's front segment: Identity (I1 queue) and Document (D1 queue, complex 5–9 h) are untouched, so earliest assessment readiness stays ~Tue 05:40 and A1's queue still sets a ~Tue 15:00 start. The gain therefore comes from two places only: (i) the final review's *duration* shrinks by the 1–3 h pre-read saving because the sanctions file is already indexed, and (ii) the reserved slot converts an unbounded ~16 h wait into a bounded wait behind the two urgent Monday cases. Assessment may also finish 0–30 min earlier because the ambiguity is pre-framed rather than discovered. Because the decision is signed in M1's next working window, a compliance finish late Tuesday converts into a Wednesday-morning decision (8.5 h), whereas a finish by ~22:30 Tuesday can convert into a same-night decision (19 h). That threshold effect, not the pre-read itself, produces most of the best-case gain.

**Process-feasibility check:** Rule 5/7 satisfied: final C1 review follows the final A1 assessment; no RMI occurs in the base branch. Rule 9/10 satisfied: A1  C1, and C1's own Monday pre-read is explicitly preliminary and does not discharge the final obligation. Rule 11: S1 advisory Mon 09:00–10:30 overlaps I1/D1 work in *other pools*, which is permitted; C1 pre-read Mon 14:00–15:30 must not overlap another C1 interval, so it consumes 1.5 h of scarce compliance capacity. Rule C9: the reservation is a claim, and honoring it ahead of urgent cases requires a logged priority decision.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best ( 14 to 19 h): packet complete, pre-read saves 2–3 h, slot honored Tue ~19:30, review 3 h, M1 signs Tuesday night. Base ( 6 to 10 h): pre-read saves 1–1.5 h, slot honored after the urgent cases, review finishes overnight, M1 signs Wed 09:30. Worst (0 to +4 h): packet incomplete because documents had not been checked, C1 re-reads anyway, the reservation is bumped, and 1.5 h of C1 time was burned for nothing. A separate branch: the preliminary screen formally requests info on Monday; that adds a Document+Assessment cycle, but the external response (8–36 h) partly overlaps the existing 13 h D1 queue, so the net ranges from roughly 2 h to +20 h.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Logs showing C1 pre-read frequently occurs before documents are verified and is discarded; evidence that the two urgent Monday cases were delayed by the reservation; S1 utilization near saturation; or data showing final review duration is insensitive to pre-read for complex corporate files (saving < 1 h). Any of these turns a modest gain into a net system loss.

### K-219

**Factual mechanism:** Originals arrived Mon 17:30, but routing to D1 imposed a ~16 h wait for a 4 h check. The case was then ready Tue 13:30, waited ~6.5 h for A2, and waited overnight for M1's Wed 08:30 window. Cycle 48 h, of which roughly 22 h is queue/calendar idle.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best (20 to 24 h): D2 free at 17:40, 5 h check, A2 evening slot, M1 signs Tue 08:30. Base (12 to 16 h): 7 h check finishing ~Tue 02:00, A2 mid-afternoon, M1 Tuesday evening. ### K-411

**100-case weekly rollout.** At single-case scale each intervention looks like a local scheduling improvement; at 100 cases the system's two hard constraints — one certified compliance reviewer (C1) and one generalist document resource (D2) — dominate. ## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is **K-411**. It deletes an edge the normative model never required, changes no verification content, no routing, and no decision authority, and its legality is unconditional because no originals are needed and the two checks sit in different pools. Its weakness is honest and measurable: the gain is capped by the assessment queue, so it may deliver 3 h rather than 18 h — but it cannot make outcomes worse if logging is genuine. **K-337 is the most dangerous despite being the fastest.** Removing 48–112 h looks decisive, yet it changes the admissible decision set rather than the schedule: compliance may reopen the loop anyway, and rejections convert avoided rework into appeals, resubmissions, loss exposure and unmeasured post-decision monitoring. A signed, notified decision is also the least reversible artifact in the whole process.

Transportability is weakest exactly where the single-case counterfactual quietly assumes someone else's capacity is free: K-104's reserved C1 slot and K-219's "D2 is available now." Both borrow slack from a pool of one; at 100 cases the borrowing becomes displacement, and K-219's substitution simultaneously undermines K-411 by loading D2. K-411's gain is also non-transportable in a different way: it is a readiness gain, and Little's law lets a saturated A2 absorb it entirely.

The most valuable additional data are interval-level resource logs (not just activity timestamps) to test exclusivity and true overlap; queue-position and priority-discipline records at A2 and C1, so waiting can be modeled rather than guessed; D2 first-pass yield on handwritten originals, the single parameter that flips K-219's sign; reservation ledgers showing honored versus bumped C1 slots and urgent-case waiting; materiality and third-confirmation outcomes for K-337-type discrepancies; and external-responder response-time distributions by weekday, to separate genuine savings from weekend luck.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **structural** — The factual `Identity Check  Document Check` edge in K-411 is a legacy-system artifact, not a control requirement; removing it leaves verification content and the normative partial order intact.
2. **temporal** — Queue snapshots are soft forecasts; waiting times are drawn around them (e.g., D2 "5 h"  start 17:40–21:00), and M1 signing plus assessor shift windows create calendar cut-offs that make savings discontinuous rather than proportional.
3. **resource** — Shift coverage exists for the evening/overnight intervals used (D1 Mon 21:40–Tue 05:40 observed; D2 and A2 evening work assumed by analogy), and C1 remains the only certified reviewer for the whole window, so no compliance work can be offloaded except advisory work to S1.
4. **behavioral** — Triage staff actually detect the sanctions ambiguity early enough to build a useful packet, assessors do not re-discover it from scratch, and suppliers respond within the stated 8–36 h envelope with the observed weekday/weekend pattern.
5. **quality** — D2's first-pass yield on handwritten originals is roughly 65–80% (p(rework)  0.2–0.35), and C1's pre-read genuinely removes 1–3 h of final review rather than duplicating it.
6. **quality** — In K-337 the residual supplier-total inconsistency is bounded and conditionable in most cases, so a reasoned conditional approval or an explicit rejection is defensible; the discrepancy is never silently dropped.
7. **interference** — For the single-case counterfactuals, other cases' routing, priorities and arrivals are held approximately fixed; this is explicitly false at 100-case scale, where C1 reservations, D2 substitution and earlier assessment arrivals displace non-target cases.
8. **interference** — Rework suppressed in K-337 does not vanish: some fraction returns as appeals or resubmissions (new arrivals) and as post-decision covenant monitoring, and compliance retains independent authority to reopen an information request, so avoided loops can reappear at the bottleneck.
