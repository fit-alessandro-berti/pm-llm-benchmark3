## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

Framework. I use **interventional (stochastic) direct/indirect analogues** and **separable effects of policy components**, not natural direct/indirect effects. Natural effects ask what cycle time would be if a case were flagged but its mediators took the values they would have had if unflagged. That contrast is not well defined here: the flag is a **policy bundle** (display + priority lane + near-mandatory review + escalation propensity), mediators are **shared-queue states**, and one case’s review wait is a function of other cases’ flags. “Set `flag=0`” is not the same intervention as “hide the badge,” “stop auto-routing to review,” or “add review capacity.” Separable/interventional effects target those components one at a time and stay in a single hypothetical world.

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1-TE-P1 | P1 vs P0: suppress flag **display and auto-rules**, leave `risk_score`, complexity, and case text visible | incoming cases over a stable arrival window; estimand is a **system-level** mean because of interference | arrival-to-close cycle time | interventional **total** effect of the display/routing bundle | Partial: display-off is specified; staff can still reconstruct the cutoff from the score | No overlap on `risk_score` (0.28–0.61 vs 0.64–0.93); interference; possible reconstruction of the flag |
| E2-TRIAGE | P0 priority lane on vs off (component of P1/P2) | same; also report the flagged subpopulation | arrival→triage_done (wait + service) | interventional **total** effect on triage completion | Yes as a resource-assignment rule (T1/T2 vs T3/T4) | `arrival_load` and off-hours; lane is completely aliased with resources T1/T2 |
| E3-IIE-MROCC | P2 vs P0: keep priority triage; **remove automatic mandatory review** (review becomes discretionary) | newly flagged cases; secondary spillover on unflagged | cycle time; I(manual review) | **interventional/separable indirect analogue via review occurrence** (not a natural IE) | Yes as a routing-table change; not as “set MR to its no-flag natural value” | Exposure-induced confounding: residual risk and complexity still select who is reviewed; humans may compensate |
| E4-IIE-MRWAIT | P3 vs P0: keep routing; add **protected MR capacity** for flagged cases | all contemporaneous cases (flagged and unflagged) | manual_queue_wait among reviewed; cycle time | interventional effect **mediated by waiting, holding occurrence policy fixed** | Yes as a capacity intervention; distinct from E3 | Queue discipline, off-shift calendar, and cross-case interference unidentified from n=20 |
| E5-IIE-ESC | P4 vs P0: keep MR; tighten escalation criteria and/or add senior slots | reviewed cases; system-level waits for the senior team | I(escalation), escalation wait, cycle time, reject rate | interventional analogue **via escalation occurrence and senior queue** | Partial: “change criteria” needs an explicit rule (e.g., High complexity only) | Escalation is post-review and confounded by `U_underlying_risk` and reviewer judgment |
| E6-CYC-ASSOC | Observational flag=1 vs flag=0 under P0 | the 20 observed cases | cycle time | **associational only**; not a causal total effect of “setting the flag” | No, as a causal estimand | Complete positivity failure on score; complexity/risk confounding; collider stratification if one conditions on review |
| E7-QUALITY | P1 or P2 vs P0 | flagged cases, especially High complexity | reject/approve; escalation-conditional reject; (if labeled) error vs a later gold standard | interventional **total** effect on the decision | Poorly identified without an outcome gold standard | Reject is almost collinear with High+escalation; no observed true-risk label |
| E8-CDE-P2 | P2 vs a world with priority **and** forced review: contrast of review mandate holding lane fixed | flagged cases | cycle time and quality | **controlled/separable direct** effect of the mandate, not NDE | Yes as a policy contrast | Reviewers may change thoroughness if they know review is optional (behavior change) |
| E9-SPILL | Any of P1–P4 vs P0 | **unflagged** cases sharing MR/senior queues | their MR/escalation waits and cycle times | interference / spillover effect (not a single-world NIE) | Yes as a cluster/system contrast | Requires measuring queue state; 20 cases, one day, cannot separate calendar from load |

E6 is reported because the raw flag contrast is what the puzzle starts from; it is **not** an identified effect of a well-defined intervention.

Interference, feedback, confounding. Shared MR and senior queues mean one case’s flag changes other cases’ waits (interference; SUTVA fails). The flag is assigned from a score that is itself caused by unobserved risk, so comparing flag=1 vs 0 confounds policy with case mix. After the flag is shown, reviewers see the badge **and** the evolving queue, so mediator–outcome relations are contaminated by **exposure-induced confounding** and **treatment–confounder feedback** (flag → queue state → wait → later escalation). Natural (cross-world) effects would require a case to keep its own no-flag mediators while the rest of the system still behaved as under flags—an undefined experiment. Overnight clock gaps further mix idle calendar time with true congestion.

## 3. Stage decomposition

Units are **median hours**. A dash in the source data means the stage did not occur: that interval is **not imputed as 0** and is shown as `NA` when nobody in the subgroup has the stage. When only a subset has the stage, the cell is the median **among cases that visited it** (support noted in the narrative). Residual-to-close is last observed stage-complete → close. Total cycle is arrival → close for every case.

Flagged cases start triage in ~12 min and finish service ~0.4 h faster; unflagged wait ~2.5 h for a slower T3/T4 lane. Every flagged case then waits a long time for review (median 6.8 h vs 1.8 h among the four unflagged reviews), and six flagged cases wait another ~7 h for seniors. Cycle time is therefore longer **because extra stages and extra queueing dominate the triage gain**, especially for High-complexity flagged cases (~24 h). High arrival load stretches flagged review waits (7.0 vs 4.5 h), consistent with shared congestion rather than a purely compositional story. These contrasts are **not causal**: flag is a deterministic cutoff of an imperfect score, complexity jointly drives flag and work content, overnight clock time inflates waits, and flagged volume occupies M2 and the senior team used by unflagged High cases (R09, R20). Occurrence of review/escalation is a different pathway from waiting time given occurrence; collapsing them into “flag delays cases” mixes policy, mix, and interference.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Is the triage speedup caused by the **badge/lane**, or by the score itself? | Local-threshold / regression-discontinuity around the empirical cutoff (~0.62–0.64), **plus** a P1 encouragement: randomly hide the badge in a narrow window | Condition on score in a window, complexity, arrival_load, hour-of-day; do **not** condition on review | Continuity of U_risk at the cutoff; limited reconstruction of the flag from the raw score | Density test on score; continuity of complexity and load; placebo outcomes that should not jump (e.g., residual-to-close) | Smooth triage wait across 0.64, or a jump that remains after hiding the badge ⇒ lane is not the mechanism |
| How much delay is **MR occurrence** vs **MR waiting**? | Mediation-oriented **interventional** decomposition under a P2-like stochastic intervention: set review probability to the unflagged-complexity-specific rate, separately from a P3-like wait intervention | Stratify by complexity and load; treat I(MR) and wait as distinct mediators; avoid natural-effect cross-worlds | Sequential randomization given measured history; no unmeasured risk that jointly sets review and wait beyond queue state | If P2 (fewer reviews) cuts cycle almost as much as P3 (same reviews, shorter waits), occurrence dominates | Discretionary review rate stays ~100% for flagged High cases, or waits stay long after volume falls ⇒ waiting/calendar, not the mandate |
| Do shared queues create **interference**? | Queue-state-aware analysis: at each MR/escalation start, regress wait on counts of flagged cases already in that pool, clock hour, and remaining work | Condition on time-varying queue length, resource id, off-shift indicator; cluster by day | Conditional interference only through measured occupancy (partial interference) | Permutation of flags holding arrival times: does predicted M2 occupancy still explain R09/R20 waits? | Queue occupancy adds no explanatory power once hour-of-day is in the model ⇒ calendar, not interference |
| What would a **policy experiment** identify? | Cluster-randomized or stepped-wedge assignment of P1/P2/P3 at team-day level (because of queues) | Design-based: no case-level adjustment required for ITT; still stratify reporting by complexity | Stable arrivals; no anticipation; SUTVA at the **cluster** level | Balance of arrivals and scores across arms; contamination if staff share a queue across arms | ITT on cycle time near 0 while process maps still show the old mandate ⇒ noncompliance, not a null mechanism |
| Is residual risk confounding the flag contrast? | Sensitivity / omitted-risk analysis: bound E6 by how much U_omitted_risk would need to differ across the cutoff; use complexity and later reject as imperfect proxies | Report Horowitz–Manski / E-value style bounds; do not claim conditional ignorability | Proxy is not itself affected by the flag (reject **is** affected—use only as a caution) | Placebo contrast just below the cutoff (0.55–0.61 vs 0.44–0.54) should be small if score is the main confounder | Large cycle gaps **inside** flag=0 by score, or bounds that include 0 for P0 contrasts ⇒ mix, not policy |
| Negative controls | Negative-control **outcome**: residual-to-close after the last real stage (should be similar if close is clerical). Negative-control **exposure**: Low-complexity cases, who should be unaffected by a High-risk mandate except via spillover | Low-complexity stratum; residual interval | Residual is not a hidden extra review | Residuals are already ~1 h in every row | Residual much longer for flagged cases ⇒ unrecorded post-processing, not the stated mechanism |
| Decision quality vs speed | Audit sample or delayed downstream outcome (rework, complaint, known fraud label) compared across P0 vs P2/P4 | Stratify by complexity; do not condition on escalation when the policy changes escalation | Label independent of recorded path | Escalation–reject collinearity: if tightening P4 cuts escalation but not audited error, escalation was bureaucratic | Audited error rises under P2 by more than a pre-specified margin ⇒ keep mandatory review for High cases |
| Can we learn anything from n=20 without an experiment? | Descriptive process mining only: stage tables, resource swimlanes, DFGs **split by flag**, with explicit support warnings | None for causal claims | None sufficient for NDE/NIE | Small-cell and positivity checks (already failed for score overlap) | Treat any “identified natural IE” from these 20 rows as a specification error |

**Limited pilot (choose P2, Medium-complexity flagged cases only).**

Eligibility: new cases with the production flag on and intake complexity = Medium (in these data, 5 cases, only one escalation). High-complexity flagged cases stay on P0 (mandatory review) as a safety carve-out. Low-complexity cases stay unflagged as a negative-control stream.

Assignment: case-level Bernoulli (p=0.5) to **mandatory review** (P0-like) vs **discretionary review** (P2) after the same priority triage lane. Randomize at case level only because Medium cases rarely hit the scarce senior team; analyze with day-level cluster robust SEs and stop if senior occupancy exceeds a guardrail.

Queue-capacity guardrail: if M2+M1+M3 backlog exceeds a pre-set count or if any High-complexity case (flagged or not) waits >4 h to start review, freeze enrollment and revert new Medium cases to mandatory review.

Primary outcome: arrival-to-close cycle time. Secondary: I(review), review wait among reviewed, I(escalation), audited disagreement vs a senior second read on a 100% sample of P2 non-reviewed cases plus a 50% sample of reviewed cases, reject rate, and spillover waits for contemporaneous High cases.

Minimum process data: the same start/complete stamps as here, resource ids, live queue length at each start, score, complexity, hour, and the audit label. Do not invent events for skipped stages.

Stop if: audited miss-rate on P2 non-reviewed cases exceeds a pre-specified bound; spillover wait for High cases rises above the guardrail; or enrollment yields a pre-specified information fraction with a near-null cycle-time effect **and** an unchanged review rate (noncompliance).

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk.** If a later gold-standard or downstream-harm label shows that flagged cases carry substantially more omitted risk than `risk_score` and complexity capture, the extra review/escalation is quality-producing delay. P2 would then be the wrong first pilot; keep mandatory review at least for High complexity.

2. **Queue interference.** If occupancy-conditioned waits vanish once hour-of-day is controlled, the long “queues” are mostly off-shift calendar gaps. Capacity protection (P3) would waste reserve staff; changing shift coverage would dominate routing changes.

3. **Decision quality.** If a senior audit finds that P2 non-reviewed Medium cases have a higher miss/false-Approve rate than the pre-specified bound, cycle-time gains are not worth the mandate removal. Revert to P0 or restrict P2 further.

4. **Timestamp semantics.** If start/complete stamps are batch-written, timezone-shifted, or “done” means “next morning’s paperwork,” the apparent 6–8 h review and escalation waits are recording artefacts. The priority-lane contrast might remain real, but the downstream-delay mechanism would not.

5. **Positivity / overlap.** If in a larger sample the flag is **not** a hard cutoff—comparable scores appear with and without the badge—and those comparable cases have similar cycle times, the P0 flag gap is mostly mix. Policy effects E1/E3 would shrink and P2 would be deprioritized.

6. **Reviewer behavior change.** If, once the badge is hidden (P1) or the mandate is lifted (P2), T1/T2 stop fast-tracking or reviewers re-impose de facto mandatory review / different reject standards, the separable-effect mapping from policy table to pathway table breaks. Measured ITT would no longer correspond to the intended mechanism.

## 9. Final synthesis

What is directly observed is a one-day, 20-case log in which the flag is a near-deterministic cutoff of `risk_score`. Flagged cases use a different triage pool (T1/T2), start triage in minutes rather than hours, finish a shorter triage service, then **all** enter manual review and often a scarce senior step. Their median cycle time is about 16 h against about 6 h when unflagged. Unflagged Low-complexity cases never enter review. Rejects occur only after High-complexity escalation. Clock time, not working time, is what the stamps record.

A plausible mechanism is a **policy bundle**, not a magical effect of a bit: the badge buys a fast lane and then spends that gain on a near-mandatory extra path through congested, partly overnight, shared queues. Occurrence of review and escalation is a different mediator from waiting given occurrence. Complexity and omitted risk almost certainly lengthen service even without a badge, so the raw flag contrast overstates a causal policy effect.

What remains unidentified is every natural direct/indirect effect of “setting the flag to zero,” the split between calendar idle and true congestion, the quality value of escalation, and spillovers onto unflagged High cases. Cross-world mediator values are not defined under interference.

The most useful next intervention is not hiding the flag and not adding exclusive capacity first. It is a guarded **P2 pilot on Medium-complexity flagged cases**: keep priority triage, randomize the review mandate, audit every skipped review, and watch High-complexity spillover. That experiment targets the pathway that actually consumes the clock, while leaving the highest-risk path on the current safety bundle.
