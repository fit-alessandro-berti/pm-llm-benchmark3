# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

def top_network_edges(network, label, n=20):
    """
    Robust printer for PM4Py network outputs.
    Handles dict-of-dicts, edge-tuple dictionaries, or DataFrame matrices.
    """
    edges = []

    if isinstance(network, dict):
        for source, target_value in network.items():
            if isinstance(target_value, dict):
                for target, weight in target_value.items():
                    edges.append((source, target, weight))
            elif isinstance(source, tuple) and len(source) == 2:
                edges.append((source[0], source[1], target_value))

    elif isinstance(network, pd.DataFrame):
        stacked = network.stack()
        edges = [
            (index[0], index[1], weight)
            for index, weight in stacked.items()
        ]

    edges.sort(key=lambda x: x[2], reverse=True)

    print(f"\nTop {label} edges:")
    for source, target, weight in edges[:n]:
        print(f"{source} -> {target}: {weight:.3f}")

# 6. Operational interpretation

Management should use the discovered structure as a risk-aware operating model, not as a simple headcount clustering.

For staffing, the bot and temporary agents should continue to absorb high-volume, low-complexity intake and standard closure work. However, temporary agents should be explicitly scoped to low-complexity cases and should not be treated as equivalent to permanent front-office generalists. Permanent front-office agents should own entitlement validation exceptions and complex standard responses. Technical work should be tiered: Dina can handle routine-to-medium technical resolution, while Chen should be reserved for high-complexity, security-adjacent, or critical technical cases. Erik should be protected as a critical security resource, not optimized away because his event volume is low. Fatima should remain the formal commercial owner, with Giorgio as a trained, threshold-limited backup. Hana should focus on final approvals and high-risk escalations, while Mateo should continue coordination but with clear delegation limits.

For training, the organization should invest in entitlement validation discipline for temporary agents, because incomplete A2 notes caused reopens. Giorgio should receive formal commercial pricing training and explicit delegation rules if the organization wants to keep using him as a commercial shadow. Chen could be developed as a security deputy for Erik, reducing single-point risk. Mateo needs training in escalation triage, security risk thresholds, and the boundary between preliminary screening and final approval. Quality analysts should be trained to preserve independence and to convert findings into KB updates.

For risk control, the organization should create a formal authority matrix. A9 events should be distinguished into preliminary screening and final approval. Pricing exceptions handled by Giorgio should have thresholds and sign-off rules. Security filtering by Mateo should include automatic fast-track rules for critical indicators to avoid delays like the two critical cases waiting more than two days. Temporary-agent closures should be sampled, especially A2 validation notes. Quality checks should be targeted to cases closed by temporary agents, reopens, and high-complexity technical implementations.

For resilience, shadow and bridging roles should be made visible and supported rather than ignored. Giorgio’s commercial shadow role is useful but should not be informal and invisible. Mateo’s brokerage role should be documented with escalation playbooks. Erik’s rare critical role needs backup coverage. Nora’s automation boundary needs exception monitoring. Quality analysts should be treated as organizational memory keepers, not spare technical capacity.

Two important cautions:

First, do not reduce staffing or change responsibilities based only on mined activity frequency. Erik has low volume but high criticality; cutting or overloading him would increase severe risk. Similarly, Hana’s approval role cannot be replaced by Mateo just because both appear in escalation activity.

Second, do not treat temporary substitution as proof of permanent competence. Giorgio kept pricing work moving during Fatima’s absence, but duration increased, indicating partial rather than full substitution. Mateo performed preliminary approvals, but Hana rechecked many of them, indicating that Mateo should not automatically inherit supervisory authority. Temporary agents closed high volumes of simple cases, but reopen evidence shows that volume alone is not sufficient evidence for expanding their authority.
