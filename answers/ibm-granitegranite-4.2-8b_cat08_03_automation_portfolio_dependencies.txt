**Why this order?**  
* Phase1 introduces the lightweight KYC hit classifier and workbench, directly targeting the longest median wait (KYC 4h) and the highimpact exception (entity mismatch). Human escalation ensures falsenegative risk is managed; the workbench surfaces control gaps early, satisfying compliance without deep integration.  
* Phase2 adds the two deepintegration controlstrengthening modules (contract generation + loan activation bot). Because they require corebanking hooks (status read/write, foureyes check), they occupy the two deepintegration slots allowed in the first six months. Contract generation also cuts contractremediation loops, directly addressing control violations. The bot enforces the foureyes rule automatically, removing reliance on manual checks during monthend rush.  
* Phase3 evaluates realworld performance against concrete gate criteria (cycletime reduction, waitingtime caps, rework drop, utilization balance, CSAT). This datadriven gate prevents premature scaling and ensures that automation does not overload credit analysts or compliance specialists. It also provides evidence to senior management that “frequency alone” is not the driver; the biggest gains come from fixing synchronization delays (KYC, entity mismatch, contract remediation).

**Resourcedemand impact (first year)**  
- Backoffice FTEs: initial load shift from repetitive registration clerking (Phase1) to handling workbench tickets and KYC escalations; overall utilization stays below peak (>65%) because automation reduces repetitive manual steps.  
- Credit analysts: receive cleaner normalized statements (via workbench flagging) and fewer contractrelated revisions; rework rate drops ~15%, so analyst effective workload improves.  
- Compliance specialists (3FTE): see fewer routine entitymismatch cases (50% reduction) and fewer contract remediation tickets; they focus on complex escalations and periodic model validation.  
- Legal ops (4FTE): contract generation reduces prep time; monthend peak load eases by ~20%.  
- External valuers: triage (not in first year) would be added later; current load unchanged.

7. Final recommendation  

**Recommend the Compliancefirst portfolio (A12Control remediation workbench, A3KYC hit preclassifier with human escalation, A9Contract generation with rule checks, and A11Loan activation bot) as the firstyear automation wave.**  

**Why this choice aligns with the evidence and tradeoff argument:**

1. **Processmining shows that lowfrequency but highwaitingtime activities dominate endtoend performance** – KYC check (4h median wait), Resolve entity mismatch (31h), and external valuation (58h) are the biggest contributors to total cycle time. Automating the highestfrequency activity (Register application, A1) alone would merely shave a few minutes off the start of the flow but would not relieve the downstream bottlenecks; without addressing those waits, overall loantofund time could still be long because cases would sit in KYC or contractremediation queues.

2. **Exception handling and control risk are material** – entitymismatch (38% rework), contract remediation loops (8.5% overall, 14% for renewals), and the foureyes principle for highexposure SME cases create risk exposure that can translate into regulatory or reputational impact. The Compliancefirst portfolio explicitly includes a controlstrengthening module (A9 contract rule checks) and an enforcement bot (A11) that checks foureyes separation, plus a workbench (A12) that surfaces policy ambiguities early. This satisfies the compliance insistence that “at least one module in the first wave strengthen controls, not only reduce labour.”

3. **Human escalation capacity is respected** – A3 includes mandatory human review for lowconfidence KYC hits and for complex entity mismatches; A12 routes unresolved mismatches to compliance specialists, ensuring that falsenegative risk is managed. The bot (A11) never activates a loan without verifying that two distinct owners have completed their respective steps, thereby protecting the foureyes rule even during monthend rush.

4. **Resourcedemand change is realistic** – Lightintegration modules (A3 and A12) free backoffice clerks from repetitive registration and remediation tasks, while the two deepintegration modules (A9 and A11) are scheduled for the first six months, respecting the IT limit of two deep corebanking integrations. Credit analyst rework drops thanks to cleaner statements (workbench flags) and fewer contract revisions, so analyst workload does not increase.

5. **Gatecriteriadriven rollout avoids overpromise** – By measuring cycletime reduction, waitingtime caps, rework drop, utilization balance, and customer CSAT in Phase3, the bank can objectively decide whether to add a bottleneck module (e.g., A6 or A7) in year2. This keeps the firstyear focus on controls and synchronization, delivering measurable improvement while avoiding the common pitfall of automating the highestfrequency step first and then discovering downstream congestion.

**In short:** senior management’s desire to automate the highestfrequency activity is understandable for quick visibility, but processmining evidence indicates that the dominant performance driver is synchronization (long waits) and control exposure. The Compliancefirst portfolio directly tackles those issues, satisfies the compliance requirement, respects humanescalation needs, and fits within budget, integration limits, and resource realities. Therefore, it provides the most defensible, riskaware prioritization for the first year.
