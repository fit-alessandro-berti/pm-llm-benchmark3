## 1. Materiality Framework

I would separate **technical conformance cost** from **business materiality**. The supplied `raw_cost` is useful as a diagnostic signal, but it is only an edit-distance score under a uniform cost function where every visible log/model move costs 1. It does not know that paying EUR 480,000 without auditable fraud-screen evidence is more serious than logging `Autosave`.

Severity scale used below:

| Severity | Meaning                                                                                                                                                             |
| -------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|        1 | Benign or immaterial: fitting behavior, harmless telemetry, duplicate retry noise, or no meaningful control/customer risk.                                          |
|        2 | Data/model/evidence quality issue with low immediate business harm; the business action was probably valid, but the log/model needs repair.                         |
|        3 | Material control weakness, evidence weakness, or data-aware policy violation with moderate exposure or uncertain operational impact.                                |
|        4 | Serious policy/control failure, especially around approval routing or beneficiary changes, but with lower exposure or some mitigating facts.                        |
|        5 | Critical control failure: high financial exposure, high fraud/compliance risk, payment before required approval, or missing mandatory pre-payment control evidence. |

The assessment considers:

| Dimension                | Why it matters here                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Control objective        | Main objectives are valid claim intake, coverage validation, loss assessment, correct approval path, controlled payment, and timely claimant notification.    |
| Amount/exposure          | A EUR 480,000 or EUR 250,000 case has materially different financial exposure from a EUR 800 case even if both have `raw_cost=1` or `raw_cost=0`.             |
| Reversibility            | A payment accepted by the bank is harder to reverse than a logging defect or duplicate notification.                                                          |
| Timing                   | `Pay Claim` before the applicable approval is materially worse than the same events in the correct order.                                                     |
| Customer/regulatory harm | Missing or weak notification evidence may create customer-service or regulatory-record risk even if payment controls were correct.                            |
| Recurrence               | A single duplicate retry is less concerning if recognized as connector behavior; four zero-cost auto-approval policy violations are a systemic control issue. |
| Evidence reliability     | External evidence can reduce severity, but mutable links or unstable identifiers reduce confidence.                                                           |
| Model scope              | Denials and withdrawals may be valid business outcomes outside a payable-claims model; high raw cost may reflect model scope, not operational misconduct.     |

**Confidence is not the same as severity.** Severity asks, “How bad would this be, given the business context?” Confidence asks, “How sure are we about that interpretation?” For example, CL-602 is high severity because fraud-screen evidence is mandatory for a high-risk EUR 480,000 claim with a recent bank change. But confidence about whether a fraud check was literally never performed is lower than confidence that auditable evidence is missing. A missing event is not automatically proof that an activity did not occur.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Alignment cost is a measure of **edit distance under a chosen cost function**. Here every visible log or model move costs 1. That design makes technical comparison convenient, but it does not encode financial exposure, policy conditions, evidence quality, irreversibility, or model scope.

CL-601 and CL-604 both have `raw_cost=3`, yet they are not remotely equivalent. CL-601 contains harmless workbench and autosave telemetry around a valid EUR 1,200 auto-approved claim. CL-604 contains a EUR 90,000 payment accepted by the bank before required approval, plus missing fraud-screen evidence. Same raw cost; completely different control risk.

CL-602 has only `raw_cost=1`, but the missing move is `Fraud Screen` on a EUR 480,000 HIGH-risk claim with a bank change one day earlier. Under P2, that missing evidence is a mandatory pre-payment control. A one-edit deviation can be critical.

CL-609 and CL-610 show the opposite problem: `raw_cost=0` does not mean compliant. The process tree permits `Auto Approve`, so the traces fit perfectly. But P1 and P2 use data conditions not encoded in the tree. CL-609 violates the auto-approval eligibility rule because amount and risk do not qualify. CL-610 is more serious because a recent beneficiary-bank change requires Fraud Screen and Manual Approve.

CL-607 and CL-612 have `raw_cost=4`, but the high cost mainly reflects scope mismatch. A denied claim and a withdrawn claim should not be forced through a payable-claim model unless the organization has decided that the model covers all intake outcomes. Their high cost says “the model cannot represent this lifecycle,” not “a payment control failed.”

CL-605 shows why hidden moves need semantic interpretation. `T(tau,c=0)` is a silent model-routing transition introduced by the Petri-net representation. It is fitting and does not mean a business activity was hidden or missing.

CL-611 shows activity granularity and label governance. `Assess Damage` versus `Assess Loss` creates an L/M pair and `raw_cost=2`, but the business interpretation may be a synonym/mapping issue rather than a missing assessment. Conversely, if the labels represented different tasks, the same cost would be more serious. The cost alone cannot decide.

CL-603 and CL-608 show that notification deviations depend on evidence context. A duplicate notification with the same message ID and retry logs is harmless connector noise. A missing core notification with a CRM/portal message may satisfy P4, but only if the linkage is retained and reliable. Mutable links reduce confidence even when the nominal alignment cost is only 1.

CL-606 shows why missing evidence is not the same as non-execution. The core log lacks `Assess Loss`, but an external licensed-adjuster report exists before fraud screening and approval. The operational step may have occurred; the defect is likely event correlation and evidence capture.

The largest business distinction is irreversibility. A payment before approval can create immediate financial and regulatory exposure. A duplicate retry event, label mismatch, or scope mismatch can usually be repaired in logging/modeling. Uniform edit cost cannot capture that difference.

def contains_activity(trace, activity):
    return activity in trace

def first_position(trace, activity):
    return trace.index(activity) if activity in trace else None

def p2_triggered(row):
    amount = row["amount_eur"]
    amount_trigger = pd.notna(amount) and amount > 50000
    risk_trigger = row["risk_level"] == "HIGH"
    bank_trigger = row["recent_bank_change"] is True
    return bool(amount_trigger or risk_trigger or bank_trigger)

def p1_auto_allowed(row):
    amount = row["amount_eur"]
    known_no_bank_change = row["recent_bank_change"] is False
    return bool(
        pd.notna(amount)
        and amount <= 5000
        and row["risk_level"] == "LOW"
        and known_no_bank_change
    )

def pay_before_manual(row):
    pay_pos = first_position(row["trace"], "Pay Claim")
    manual_pos = first_position(row["trace"], "Manual Approve")
    return bool(pay_pos is not None and manual_pos is not None and pay_pos < manual_pos)
