## 1. What is observed

The panel records branch-month exposure. Case-level paths were not stored. Availability (`automation` = 1) begins in April 2026 at Alpha and Beta and in July 2026 at Gamma. Delta and Epsilon stay at 0 through August. Actual use is a different series. `automated_fast_path_pct` is already 4% at Alpha, Gamma, Delta, and Epsilon and 6% at Beta in every pre-availability month, then rises after go-live: Alpha 25, 32, 39, 46, 48; Beta 27, 34, 41, 48, 50; Gamma 25 then 32. By August about half of Alpha and Beta cases, and about a third of Gamma cases, finish on the automated fast path.

Alpha and Beta were selected for high volume and poor historical cycle times. Gamma entered after a readiness pilot. No branch was randomized. Managers could change local practice during the rollout.

In March a centralized fraud desk began serving all five branches. From April a routing campaign directed about 10% more incoming cases to Alpha and Beta; Gamma received about 5% more after July. Productive FTE moved in opposite directions: Alpha −2 in May, Beta +1 in April, Gamma −1 in June, Delta +1 in June. A May product change lifted `high_complexity_pct` in several branches, by roughly 5 to 8 points.

`median_cycle_h` and `p90_cycle_h` are separate branch-month summaries. `rework_pct` and `escalation_pct` have no accompanying case counts. `csat_1to5` is a survey mean; response rates varied from about 20% to 45%, and branch-month response counts are unavailable. Fraud-desk waiting times were not recorded.

Availability is a branch-month regime, not a case-level treatment. Fast-path share is a second treatment inside that regime, chosen case by case. A pooled two-way fixed-effects coefficient is a poor summary here. Alpha and Beta switch on in April, Gamma in July, under different conditions. When effects vary with time since go-live, the pooled weight on a cohort-time cell can be negative, so one coefficient need not equal either cohort’s effect. The May complexity shift and the April routing campaign sit inside that blend.

The primary strategy estimates the April cohort against Delta and Epsilon at each event time, and estimates Gamma against those never-treated branches in July and August. Gamma is a control only while it is still untreated, April through June. That follows the cohort-time logic of Callaway and Sant’Anna and of Sun and Abraham: availability is the contrast, timing stays visible, and the two rollouts are not forced into one number.

Synthetic control is a better check than a primary design. Alpha and Beta start together, so neither is a valid post-period donor for the other, and the untreated pool after July is two branches. An intensity regression asks the dose question, but automated-fast-path share moves with learning and case mix, so it does not recover the effect of granting availability.

Alpha’s median gap versus Delta was already closing before April, the early adopters were selected for long cycles, and routing, FTE, and complexity moved on the start dates. The cohort event study is preferable because those breaks stay visible by cohort and event time, instead of disappearing into one five-cluster coefficient.

## 5. Quantitative evidence

Windows, unweighted means of branch-months. April cohort pre = Jan–Mar, post = Apr–Aug. Gamma’s preferred window is May–Jun vs Jul–Aug, so complexity (27%), the June FTE loss, and arrival volume are already in the baseline. Hours are means of monthly medians or of monthly p90s, not pooled case quantiles.

| Alpha | Jan–Mar vs Apr–Aug | 48.9 → 48.5 (−0.4) | 98.5 → 108.1 (+9.7) | +0.5 | +2.9 | +0.02 | +242 | +34 |

Same calendar window for the never-treated branches, Jan–Mar vs Apr–Aug: Delta median +6.0 h and p90 +17.5 h; Epsilon median +4.4 h and p90 +14.6 h; rework +1.8 and +2.1 pp; escalation +1.7 and +1.9 pp; CSAT −0.09 and −0.08. Gamma’s longer raw window (Jan–Jun vs Jul–Aug) shrinks its own median change to about −3.4 h and its raw p90 change to about −0.9 h, because the early months were already fast.

**2. Comparison with never-treated and not-yet-treated branches (arithmetic)**

April-cohort event study against the Delta/Epsilon average. Pre-period median gap = +10.0 h. Gap minus that pre-gap, by event month: April −2.6 h, May −5.4, June −4.8, July −4.0, August −5.8. Mean of those five gaps: **−4.5 h**. The same construction for p90 (pre-gap +18.5 h): −4.7, −7.4, −6.4, −4.9, −8.1; mean **−6.3 h**. Escalation pre-gap is +0.25 pp in each of January, February, and March, then the post gap is +1.15 pp in every month from April through August, so the relative step is **+0.9 pp** and flat. Rework pre-gap is +0.4 pp; the relative step is about −2.2 pp in April and about −1.9 pp afterward.

Gamma, May–Jun to Jul–Aug, minus the same calendar change: median −5.1 h vs Delta and −3.3 h vs Epsilon; p90 −9.4 h vs Delta and −5.9 h vs Epsilon; rework about −2.0 to −2.2 pp; escalation about +0.9 to +1.0 pp. Using Jan–Jun as Gamma’s baseline leaves a median contrast of roughly −3 to −5 h and a p90 contrast of roughly −5 to −9 h. The relative p90 gap is carried by the controls getting worse, not only by Gamma’s level.

First post month only, before the May product change and before Alpha’s FTE loss, versus the March-to-April change at Gamma, Delta, and Epsilon: Alpha median −2.6 h and p90 −4.8 h; Beta median −0.6 h and p90 −0.9 h; rework −2.0 and −2.4 pp; escalation +1.0 and +0.8 pp. Arrivals in that same month rose 14% at Alpha and Beta and about 4% at the three not-yet-treated branches, which matches the stated extra routing of about 10%.

**3. Intensity rather than the binary flag (arithmetic)**

Mean fast-path share at Alpha and Beta by event month, paired with the median gap above: 26% with −2.6 h, 33% with −5.4 h, 40% with −4.8 h, 47% with −4.0 h, 49% with −5.8 h. The relative median gap shifts when availability turns on and does not scale with the later climb from about 25% to about 50%. Gamma’s relative median gap is about −4.1 h at 25% use and −4.3 h at 32% use. Rework tells the same step story: a drop in the go-live month, then no further decline as the fast-path share doubles. At Alpha, rework rose from 11.2% to 14.5% while the fast-path share rose from 25% to 48%.

Decomposition that keeps the common shocks visible. Go-live month rework, controls unchanged that month: −2.0, −2.4, −2.0 pp. May rework, treated and untreated together: about +2.2 to +2.8 pp. March escalation, all five branches: exactly +2.0 pp. Adoption-month escalation, controls unchanged that month: +0.8 to +1.0 pp.

**4. Pre-trends and regression to the mean**

Escalation and rework gaps versus Delta/Epsilon are flat across January–March. Those two series have the cleanest pre-period in the panel, and both then break in the adoption month at all three adopters. Median pre-trends do not. Alpha minus Delta runs 16.0, 14.1, 13.2 hours before any availability. Alpha and Beta were chosen because cycles were already long, so continued closing of that gap is what regression to the mean predicts. Absolute levels argue against a simple reversion story for April–June: Alpha’s median stays near 49–54 hours and Beta’s rises, rather than falling toward the rest of the pack. August is more ambiguous, because every branch’s median falls and Alpha’s 43.3 hours is its best month. Gamma was not selected for poor cycles. Its May–June medians were a local high after the complexity shock, and Delta and Epsilon rebound from that same shock; Gamma’s extra drop is the part regression to the mean does not automatically explain, and two months cannot pin it down.

**5. Bounded reading for median and p90**

Arithmetic and causal reading stay separate.

For the median, the descriptive relative gaps sit around −2.6 to −5.8 hours (April cohort, by month) and around −3 to −5 hours (Gamma versus never-treated). Absolute April-cohort changes are −0.4 hours at Alpha and +1.9 hours at Beta. Beta’s April median rose in a month when complexity eased and FTE rose by one. The Alpha–Delta gap was already narrowing. A scenario interval for a causal availability effect on the branch-month median, at something like constant load, runs from **0 to about −6 hours** over one to five months. Zero remains plausible. Dividing the −4.5 hour mean gap by the April cohort’s 47.4 hour pre-period median produces −9%. That quotient is how a spurious “reduces cycle time by 9%” sentence gets built. It drops the absolute Alpha and Beta changes, drops the tail, and treats a confounded gap as an effect.

For p90, absolute April-cohort changes are about **+10 hours**, with Alpha peaking at 119.9 in June. Descriptive relative gaps average about −6 hours and range from about −5 to −8. Gamma’s raw short-window change is −15.6 hours; its raw long-window change is about −0.9 hours. The p90/median ratio rises from May in every branch, including Delta and Epsilon. A scenario interval for a causal availability effect on the branch-month p90 is **−8 to +10 hours**. The sign is open. Relative protection and absolute tail lengthening are both in the arithmetic.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

`expand with an embedded evaluation`

Treat one remaining branch and leave the other untreated for four months as the contemporaneous control. Randomize whether Delta or Epsilon goes first. Alpha and Beta were chosen for high volume and long cycle times; another manager-chosen wave would repeat that selection. If leadership refuses randomization, pre-commit Epsilon first and Delta second. Epsilon holds the operational burden: complexity near 38%, rework near 16%, and a June p90 above 100 hours. Delta stays the holdout.

Keep routing shares, productive FTE, and the fraud-desk referral rule frozen. The 2026 calendar contains that bundle alongside availability, which is why it cannot support a percentage. Freeze the triage threshold for the whole window so the treatment version stays put.

Use the eight observed months as the pre-period, and add four weekly snapshots immediately before go-live under the freeze so anticipation is visible. Follow the treated branch for at least four months, preferably six, before any decision on the holdout.

Collect a case-level event log: path variant, timestamps for registration, triage, specialist review, fraud-desk entry and exit, rework, and close; risk class; human overrides; daily productive FTE; arrivals by source. Report median and p90 inside each variant, and split queue time from touch time. Store CSAT on the case with a response indicator, using a fixed monthly sample. Log fraud-desk queue length and wait every week for all five branches.

Extra borderline referrals can slow Alpha and Beta even though their automation flag is unchanged. Pause new referrals from the expanding branch if network fraud-desk median wait exceeds its pre-window level by 20% for two weeks.

Weekly guardrails against the holdout: suspend automated fast-path closure of high-complexity cases if treated p90 exceeds that branch’s own June–August p90 by more than 15% for two weeks; suspend if fixed-sample CSAT falls by more than 0.15 versus the holdout; suspend if rework exceeds the pre-window mean.

At month four, open the holdout only if its comparison shows a lower median, rework no higher, p90 no higher, a CSAT gap no worse than −0.10, and an intact fraud-desk bound. If the median is better and the p90 is worse, leave the holdout untreated and revise the escalation threshold before adding any branch. If four months are too noisy to see a few hours of median separation or a tail breach, extend the window. A two-branch contrast is too small for an “X%” announcement.

    "Alpha": "2026-04",

def load_and_validate(csv_text: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    df = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(csv_text))
    expected = {
        "branch",
        "month",
        "automation",
        "arrivals",
        "fte",
        "high_complexity_pct",
        "automated_fast_path_pct",
        "median_cycle_h",
        "p90_cycle_h",
        "rework_pct",
        "escalation_pct",
        "csat_1to5",
    }
    missing = expected.difference(df.columns)
    if missing:
        raise ValueError(f"missing columns: {missing}")
    if len(df) != 40:
        raise ValueError(f"expected 40 branch-months, found {len(df)}")
    if df.duplicated(["branch", "month"]).any():
        raise ValueError("duplicate branch-month keys")
    if df[list(expected)].isna().any().any():
        raise ValueError("unexpected missing values")
    if not set(df["automation"]).issubset({0, 1}):
        raise ValueError("automation must be 0/1")
    if (df["median_cycle_h"] > df["p90_cycle_h"]).any():
        raise ValueError("median exceeds p90")
    if (df["fte"] <= 0).any() or (df["arrivals"] <= 0).any():
        raise ValueError("non-positive arrivals or FTE")
    for col in ("high_complexity_pct", "automated_fast_path_pct", "rework_pct", "escalation_pct"):
        if ((df[col] < 0) | (df[col] > 100)).any():
            raise ValueError(f"{col} outside 0-100")
    if ((df["csat_1to5"] < 1) | (df["csat_1to5"] > 5)).any():
        raise ValueError("CSAT outside 1-5")
    for branch, start in ADOPT.items():
        sub = df.loc[df["branch"] == branch, ["month", "automation"]]
        if start is None:
            if (sub["automation"] != 0).any():
                raise ValueError(f"{branch} should remain untreated")
        else:
            pre = sub.loc[sub["month"] < start, "automation"]
            post = sub.loc[sub["month"] >= start, "automation"]
            if (pre != 0).any() or (post != 1).any():
                raise ValueError(f"automation flag disagrees with adopt month for {branch}")
    df = df.sort_values(["branch", "month"]).reset_index(drop=True)
    print(f"validated rows={len(df)} branches={df['branch'].nunique()} months={df['month'].nunique()}")
    return df


def add_design_columns(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    out = df.copy()
    months = sorted(out["month"].unique())
    month_index = {m: i for i, m in enumerate(months)}
    out["adopt_month"] = out["branch"].map(ADOPT)
    out["rel_time"] = [
        np.nan
        if adopt is None
        else month_index[month] - month_index[adopt]
        for month, adopt in zip(out["month"], out["adopt_month"])
    ]
    # Relative-time indicators. Never-treated rows stay 0 on every dummy.
    # For the April cohort, rel_time is a pure calendar shift, so a saturated
    # event study plus month FE is collinear until Gamma's later start.
    for k in range(-6, 5):
        out[f"rel_{k}"] = (out["rel_time"] == k).astype(int)
    out["arrivals_per_fte"] = out["arrivals"] / out["fte"]
    pre_floor = (
        out.loc[out["automation"] == 0]
        .groupby("branch")["automated_fast_path_pct"]
        .mean()
    )
    out["afp_intensity"] = out["automated_fast_path_pct"] - out["branch"].map(pre_floor)
    out["cohort"] = out["adopt_month"].fillna("never")
    return out


def print_trends(df: pd.DataFrame) -> None:

    for outcome in OUTCOMES:

        for branch, g in df.groupby("branch"):

        plt.title(outcome)
        plt.xlabel("month")
        plt.ylabel(outcome)
        plt.legend()
        plt.xticks(rotation=45)
        plt.tight_layout()
        plt.savefig(f"/tmp/trend_{outcome}.png")
        plt.close()


def window_means(df: pd.DataFrame, branches, start, end, cols) -> pd.Series:
    mask = df["branch"].isin(branches) & (df["month"] >= start) & (df["month"] <= end)
    return df.loc[mask, cols].mean()


def print_pre_post(df: pd.DataFrame) -> None:
    cols = OUTCOMES + ["arrivals", "automated_fast_path_pct", "arrivals_per_fte", "high_complexity_pct"]
    windows = {
        "Alpha": ("2026-01", "2026-03", "2026-04", "2026-08"),
        "Beta": ("2026-01", "2026-03", "2026-04", "2026-08"),
        "Gamma_short": ("2026-05", "2026-06", "2026-07", "2026-08"),
        "Gamma_long": ("2026-01", "2026-06", "2026-07", "2026-08"),
    }
    branch_of = {
        "Alpha": ["Alpha"],
        "Beta": ["Beta"],
        "Gamma_short": ["Gamma"],
        "Gamma_long": ["Gamma"],
    }
    print("\nPRE/POST contrasts (unweighted means of branch-months; arithmetic only)")
    for name, (pre_a, pre_b, post_a, post_b) in windows.items():
        pre = window_means(df, branch_of[name], pre_a, pre_b, cols)
        post = window_means(df, branch_of[name], post_a, post_b, cols)
        delta = post - pre
        print(f"\n{name} pre {pre_a}:{pre_b} post {post_a}:{post_b}")
        for col in cols:
            print(f"  {col}: pre={pre[col]:.3f} post={post[col]:.3f} delta={delta[col]:.3f}")
        controls = ["Delta", "Epsilon"]
        cpre = window_means(df, controls, pre_a, pre_b, OUTCOMES)
        cpost = window_means(df, controls, post_a, post_b, OUTCOMES)
        print("  vs Delta+Epsilon mean change (descriptive gap, not an effect):")
        for col in OUTCOMES:
            did = (post[col] - pre[col]) - (cpost[col] - cpre[col])
            print(f"    {col}: control_delta={cpost[col] - cpre[col]:.3f} gap={did:.3f}")


def print_event_study(df: pd.DataFrame) -> None:
    """April cohort vs never-treated. Descriptive gaps, not ATT estimates."""
    print("\nAPRIL COHORT event-time gaps vs Delta/Epsilon (arithmetic)")
    treated = df[df["branch"].isin(["Alpha", "Beta"])]
    controls = df[df["branch"].isin(["Delta", "Epsilon"])]
    pre_t = treated[treated["rel_time"] < 0]
    pre_c = controls[controls["month"].isin(sorted(pre_t["month"].unique()))]
    for outcome in OUTCOMES:
        base = pre_t[outcome].mean() - pre_c[outcome].mean()
        print(f"\n{outcome}: pre_gap={base:.3f}")
        for t in range(0, 5):
            tm = treated.loc[treated["rel_time"] == t, outcome].mean()
            months = treated.loc[treated["rel_time"] == t, "month"].unique()
            cm = controls.loc[controls["month"].isin(months), outcome].mean()
            print(f"  t={t}: treated={tm:.3f} control={cm:.3f} gap={tm - cm:.3f} vs_pre={tm - cm - base:.3f}")


def fit_models(df: pd.DataFrame) -> None:
    # Descriptive TWFE. Under staggered adoption and heterogeneous effects this
    # coefficient can put negative weight on some cohort-time cells. It is not
    # the Callaway-Sant'Anna ATT, and it does not prove causality.
    # Intensity model: afp_intensity is endogenous (learning, case mix, practice).
    groups = df["branch"].to_numpy()
    focus_binary = ["automation"]
    focus_intensity = ["afp_intensity", "high_complexity_pct", "arrivals_per_fte"]
    for outcome in OUTCOMES:
        m_bin = smf.ols(
            f"{outcome} ~ automation + C(branch) + C(month)",
            data=df,
        ).fit(cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": groups})
        m_int = smf.ols(
            f"{outcome} ~ afp_intensity + high_complexity_pct + arrivals_per_fte"
            " + C(branch) + C(month)",
            data=df,
        ).fit(cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": groups})
        print(f"\n== DESCRIPTIVE_TWFE_AVAILABILITY_NOT_CAUSAL :: {outcome} ==")
        print(f"n={int(m_bin.nobs)} r2={m_bin.rsquared:.3f}")
        for name in focus_binary:
            print(
                f"  {name}: coef={m_bin.params[name]:.3f} "
                f"cluster_se={m_bin.bse[name]:.3f} p={m_bin.pvalues[name]:.3f}"
            )
        print(f"== DESCRIPTIVE_INTENSITY_NOT_CAUSAL :: {outcome} ==")
        print(f"n={int(m_int.nobs)} r2={m_int.rsquared:.3f}")
        for name in focus_intensity:
            print(
                f"  {name}: coef={m_int.params[name]:.3f} "
                f"cluster_se={m_int.bse[name]:.3f} p={m_int.pvalues[name]:.3f}"
            )
    print(
        "\nWARNING: cluster-robust standard errors with G=5 branches are not "
        "reliable. Do not treat these p-values as decision criteria."
    )
    print(
        "WARNING: median_cycle_h and p90_cycle_h are aggregated branch-month "
        "summaries. Averaging medians does not recover a pooled median, a "
        "difference of medians is not a median treatment effect, and a "
        "difference of p90s is not a case-level quantile effect."
    )


def main() -> None:
    df = add_design_columns(load_and_validate(CSV))
    print("\nRelative-time value counts (NaN = never treated):")
    print(df["rel_time"].value_counts(dropna=False).sort_index().to_string())
    print("\nRelative-time indicator sums:")
    rel_cols = [c for c in df.columns if c.startswith("rel_")]
    print(df[rel_cols].sum().to_string())
    print_trends(df)
    print_pre_post(df)
    print_event_study(df)
    fit_models(df)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

## 10. Final claim ladder

- Observed: In every go-live month, rework fell by about two points while it was unchanged at branches that stayed manual, and escalation rose by about one point on top of a two-point March increase shared by all five branches. Fast-path use then climbed gradually, to about half of cases at Alpha and Beta and about a third at Gamma.
- Suggestive: Relative to Delta and Epsilon, the April cohort’s median and p90 gaps were narrower by roughly 3–6 and 5–8 hours after April, and Gamma’s July–August median and p90 fell by more than the same calendar change at the never-treated branches, with complexity and FTE already shifted before Gamma’s start.
- Conditionally causal: If parallel trends held after complexity and productive load, and if fraud-desk spillovers and regression to the mean were both negligible, availability would line up with a few hours on the median and about 1–2 points less rework, alongside about one point more escalation. Those assumptions are strained by selection, the narrowing Alpha–Delta pre-gap, routing, and staffing, so the figures stay off the decision slide.
- Not supported: “Automation reduces cycle time by X%,” an absolute shortening of the tail at Alpha or Beta, a customer-satisfaction gain, or immediate expansion to both Delta and Epsilon on the strength of this panel.
