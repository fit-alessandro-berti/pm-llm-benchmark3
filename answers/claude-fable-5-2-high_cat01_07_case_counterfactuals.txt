## 1. Feasibility model

Conventions used throughout: Mon = 2026-04-06; K-337 runs into the following week (Mon 04-13 to Wed 04-15). Factual cycle times (Register start to Notify complete) are K-104 58.5 h, K-219 48.0 h, K-337 214.2 h, K-411 29.5 h. All counterfactual times are approximate scenario values, not predictions.

Partial-order relations (`<` = must complete before the other starts; `||` = no order imposed):

```text
# Common skeleton (all cases)
Register < Triage
Triage < Identity Check
Triage < Document Check[1]
Identity Check < Assessment[1]
Document Check[k] < Assessment[k]                      (k = 1..n)
Assessment[n] < Decision < Notify                      (n = final assessment)

# Concurrency (no order imposed)
Identity Check || Document Check[1]
Identity Check || Originals Receipt
Escalation Advice || {Identity Check, Document Check, Assessment}
Preliminary Compliance Screen || {Identity Check, Document Check, Assessment}

# Originals-required variant (K-219, K-337)
Triage < Originals Receipt < Document Check[1]

# Rework variant (K-337 factual; K-219 risk branch)
Assessment[k] < Request More Info[k] < Document Check[k+1] < Assessment[k+1]
Compliance Review[j] < Request More Info[k] < Document Check[k+1] < Assessment[k+1] < Compliance Review[j+1]

# High-risk / high-value variant (K-104, K-337)
Assessment[n] < Compliance Review[final] < Decision
Triage < Escalation Advice                                         (optional)
Triage < Preliminary Compliance Screen < Compliance Review[final]  (optional)
```

Conditional and declarative constraints:

```text
C1  Register is the first event; Triage directly follows it.
C2  IF originals_required THEN Originals Receipt precedes every Document Check;
    Identity Check is not blocked by Originals Receipt.
C3  IF NOT originals_required THEN Identity Check and Document Check may overlap in time.
C4  Assessment[k] requires the completed Identity Check AND the latest Document Check[k].
C5  IF high_risk OR high_value THEN exactly one final Compliance Review lies between
    the latest Assessment and Decision.
C6  Request More Info may be issued only by Assessment or Compliance Review.
C7  After Request More Info: a new Document Check and a new Assessment are mandatory;
    IF high_risk OR high_value THEN a new or explicitly reaffirmed Compliance Review too.
C8  A Preliminary Compliance Screen never satisfies C5 (not-substitutable).
C9  Four-eyes: resource(final Assessment) != resource(final Compliance Review).
C10 Decision precedes Notify; Decision is signed by M1.
C11 No resource has two overlapping service intervals; S1 advisory work may overlap
    with checks performed by OTHER resources.
C12 Queue forecasts are random variables (treated here as roughly +/-50%), not start times.
C13 Calendar (inferred from the log, not given): M1 is observed signing only between
    08:30 and 18:40 on weekdays; external parties do not respond over the weekend.
C14 K-337 intervention only: Request More Info[2] is forbidden; Decision must be
    {approve with conditions, reject} and must document the unresolved discrepancy.
```

Which interventions change what:

- **Timing only:** K-411 (both verification tasks created at triage). No activity, resource, or decision rule changes; only the release time of the Document Check moves. K-104 is mostly timing (the compliance request and escalation are released at triage instead of after Assessment), but it also adds two optional activities (early Escalation Advice, Preliminary Compliance Screen), so it is "timing plus optional routing".
- **Routing / resource allocation:** K-219 (D2 instead of D1). The control flow is unchanged in the good branch, but the resource change makes a rework branch (Request More Info, Document Check 2, Assessment 2) more likely, so routing changes stochastically.
- **Allowed decision policy:** K-337. Removing the second loop is not a time edit; it removes an allowed action (C14) and forces a decision on incomplete evidence. The outcome to compare is a vector (time, decision, exposure, defensibility), not a duration.

Data caveat: the factual log shows D1 on K-337 Document Check 1 (Tue 08:30-14:30) and on K-219 Document Check (Tue 09:30-13:30) at the same time, which violates C11 if both are pure service intervals. I treat this as interleaving or imprecise timestamping, which means logged intervals are elapsed time rather than touch time and all service-time inferences for D1 are soft.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

Factual mechanism: The 58.5 h decomposes into waits the intervention cannot touch and waits it can. Untouchable: the D1 queue (Triage end Mon 08:50 to D1 start Mon 21:40, close to the 13 h forecast), the 8 h complex Document Check, and the A1 queue (9.5 h, close to the 9 h forecast). Touchable: the ambiguity was known at Mon 08:50, yet C1 was first asked at Tue 21:20, about 36.5 h later, after a serial S1 step (Tue 19:20-21:20). C1 then had 15 h 50 min of queue and a 4.5 h review "from scratch".

Minimal intervention: Change only what happens at Triage: T1 sends the packet to S1 and places a provisional hold on C1. T1, I1, D1, A1, C1, and M1 keep their roles; the Identity Check, Document Check, and Assessment keep their factual timestamps. I do not assume D1 or A1 become faster, and I treat the hold as releasable, not as a guarantee.

Temporal propagation: S1 can advise on Monday morning because advisory work may overlap with checks. A 1-2 h preliminary screen entered into C1's queue at Mon 08:50 with a 20 h forecast would be reached around Tuesday morning to midday, which is after the Document Check finished (Tue 05:40) and before Assessment ended (Tue 19:10), so the screen could even use verified documents. Nothing before Tue 19:10 moves, because Assessment still needs D1's output and a free A1. After Tue 19:10 two things change: the serial S1 step is no longer on the critical path, and the compliance wait has already been running since Monday. The final review is shorter only by the 1-3 h of reading already done. If C1 reviews on Tuesday evening, the case still waits for M1 until Wed 08:30, so part of the saved time is lost at the signing boundary.

Process-feasibility check: Identity and Document Checks precede Assessment (rule 2). The final Compliance Review follows the only Assessment and precedes Decision (rule 5). The preliminary screen is logged as a separate activity and does not replace the final review (rule 10). A1 assesses and C1 reviews, so four-eyes holds; S1 only advises. C1's screen and final review are separate intervals that must not overlap other C1 work (rule 11). The final review should explicitly reference the Assessment result rather than reaffirm the preliminary view.

Best/base/worst case: Best: the hold is honoured Tue about 19:30-22:00, M1 signs Wed 08:30, Notify about Wed 09:10, saving about 9.5 h. Base: the review happens first thing Wednesday (about 08:00-11:00), Decision between about 11:15 and M1's early-afternoon signing, saving about 4-7 h. Worst: the packet lacks the ownership evidence that only the Document Check reveals, the slot is released because Assessment ran late against the Monday forecast, and the review starts near the factual Wed 13:10, saving 0-1 h; a low-probability tail adds 8-36 h if early advice prompts a precautionary information request that the factual path never needed.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: C1's calendar showing that the Wed 13:10 start was fixed by urgent cases the hold could not legitimately displace; evidence that the ambiguity could only be characterised from verified corporate documents; historical error of Assessment-completion forecasts larger than a slot width (frequent no-shows); or audit findings that preliminary views anchor final compliance opinions.

### K-219

Factual mechanism: The triage choice of D1 bought accuracy at the price of a 16 h wait after originals arrived (Mon 17:30 to Tue 09:30). The 4 h check produced no rework. A2 then finished at Tue 22:30, after M1's observed signing hours, so a further 10 h was lost overnight. Two waits therefore dominate: the D1 queue and the missed Tuesday signature.

Minimal intervention: Only the Document Check resource changes, from D1 to D2, with release at Originals Receipt (Mon 17:30). Identity Check (I2), the assessor (A2), and M1 stay the same. D2's speed and accuracy are D2's own, not D1's.

Temporal propagation: D2's queue was forecast at 5 h on Mon 16:00, so a start around Mon 20:30-Tue 00:30 is plausible if D2 works evenings, as the document pool evidently does (D1 worked overnight on K-104). Handwritten material pushes D2 towards 5-9 h, giving completion around Tue 02:00-09:30, which is 4-11 h earlier than the factual Tue 13:30. That earlier readiness enters an assessment queue of roughly 6 h (with one assessor in training), so Assessment plausibly ends Tue 11:30-18:30. The decisive effect is a threshold: if Assessment ends before M1's last Tuesday signing, the case gains a whole night; if not, Decision is Wed 08:30 exactly as in the factual case. In the rework branch, D2 flags an unreadable item, Assessment 1 issues Request More Info, the customer takes 8-36 h, and a second Document Check and Assessment each queue again.

Process-feasibility check: Originals Receipt precedes the Document Check (rule 4); Identity Check precedes Assessment and may run before the originals arrive. No compliance review is needed. Any Request More Info is issued by Assessment (rule 6) and is followed by a new Document Check and Assessment (rule 7). D2's interval does not collide with its K-411 work (Tue 15:00-19:30). Removing K-219 from D1 also removes the Tuesday D1 overlap with K-337.

Best/base/worst case: Best: D2 starts Mon 20:30, finishes about Tue 02:00, Assessment by late morning, Notify early Tuesday afternoon, about −19 h. Base: no rework, Notify Tue about 14:30-18:30, about −14 to −18 h. Worst: clarification needed, Notify between Wed afternoon and Fri morning, about +6 to +48 h; or D2 misreads silently and the error surfaces after the decision. With an assumed 20-40% rework probability the expected change is roughly −7 h to +1 h; break-even lies near a 35-45% rework probability.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: D2's historical clarification or error rate on handwritten originals above roughly 35-45%; a D2 work list on Monday evening much longer than forecast, or no D2 evening shift; A2 workload on Tuesday that pushes Assessment past M1's last signing; or evidence that the Tuesday D1 overlap means D1 could in fact have started K-219 earlier at no cost.

### K-337

Factual mechanism: The first loop cost about 2 days (Wed 11:30 to Fri 10:30 including D1 and A1 queueing). The second loop cost about 4 days: 70.5 h of external waiting dominated by the weekend, then 4 h of D1 queue, 3.5 h check, an overnight wait for A1, a 5 h wait for C1, and an overnight wait for M1. Only one inconsistency remained after Document Check 2, and the eventual confirmation was accepted.

Minimal intervention: Everything up to and including Assessment 2 (Fri 08:00-10:30, A1) stays factual. The only change is that A1 may not issue Request More Info 2; A1 must close the assessment with the discrepancy recorded and a recommended disposition. C1 and M1 keep their roles. This is a change in decision policy, so the decision itself becomes an outcome of the counterfactual.

Temporal propagation: Assessment 2 becomes the final assessment, so the case enters C1's queue at Fri 10:30 instead of the following Tuesday. With a Thursday forecast of 5 h, a start around Fri 12:30-16:30 is plausible; a review of incomplete evidence is likely at the slow end (4-6 h), since C1 must judge materiality rather than confirm. If the review ends before M1's last Friday signing, Decision is Friday evening; otherwise it rolls over the weekend to Mon 08:30. Document Check 3 and Assessment 3 disappear, freeing about 3.5 h of D1 and 2.5 h of A1. If the case is approved with a condition precedent, the supplier confirmation is still needed, but it is verified as post-decision condition fulfilment rather than through the full check-assess-review chain.

Process-feasibility check: Rule 7 is satisfied for loop 1 by Document Check 2 and Assessment 2. Rule 5 holds: the Compliance Review follows the latest Assessment and precedes Decision. Four-eyes holds (A1 versus C1). C1 may not circumvent the ban by issuing its own request; it can give a conditional or adverse opinion. The discrepancy must appear in the Assessment, the review, and the decision rationale, so it is not silently ignored.

Best/base/worst case: Best: review Fri about 12:30-16:30, Decision before close, Notify Fri about 17:40, about −111 h, approve with conditions. Base: review ends Friday evening, Decision Mon 04-13 08:30, about −48 h, approve with conditions precedent; effective completion depends on the supplier's Monday reply, so the real gain is nearer 1-1.5 days. Worst: C1 or M1 will not accept the exposure and the case is rejected; hindsight suggests this would be a false rejection, and an appeal or resubmission could end later than the factual Wed 04-15.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Materiality of the remaining inconsistency (a large amount would make rejection or deadlock the likely result); a rule that high-risk cases cannot be conditionally approved; appeal and resubmission rates after forced decisions; evidence that the supplier would have answered on Friday if chased; or M1 and C1 Friday availability data.

### K-411

Factual mechanism: The legacy system and case lock serialised two checks that rule 3 allows in parallel. The Document Check task did not exist until Tue 13:20, started 15:00, and finished 19:30; the case then waited 14 h for A2, and a further 2 h for M1. The serialisation delayed readiness, but the largest single wait was the assessment queue.

Minimal intervention: Create the Document Check task at Triage completion (Tue 09:35) and let D2 take ownership while I2 holds the identity task. Same resources, same service times, same assessor. Real-time timestamps change what is recorded, not what is done.

Temporal propagation: The Document Check now queues from 09:35 rather than 13:20. With a 4 h forecast, D2 would start around 13:30, only about 1.5 h earlier than the factual 15:00, because the factual wait after 13:20 happened to be short. The gain equals the work of cases that arrived at D2 between 09:35 and 13:20 and are now overtaken; if none arrived, D2 still starts at 15:00. Identity Check is unchanged, so readiness for Assessment is the later of the two branches: about Tue 18:00 (range 16:30-19:30). At A2, the case sits behind K-219 (ready since Tue 13:30, served 20:00-22:30) and overtakes only cases that arrived between its new and old ready times. Unless A2 continues after 22:30, Assessment starts Wednesday morning, perhaps up to 1.5 h earlier. M1 signed this case 2 h after Assessment; the four observed signing times (08:30, 08:30, 14:00, 18:00) are compatible with batch signing, in which case a slightly earlier Assessment changes nothing.

Process-feasibility check: Rule 3 permits the concurrency (no originals). Both checks complete before Assessment (rule 2). No compliance review is needed. I2 and D2 are different resources, so rule 11 holds; the workaround must still prevent two simultaneous writers on the same record. Service intervals may not actually overlap (11:30-13:20 versus about 13:30-18:00); the concurrency is mostly in queueing.

Best/base/worst case: Best: D2 starts about 12:00, finishes about 16:30, A2 assesses on Tuesday night, M1 signs Wed 08:30, Notify about 09:00, about −5.5 h. Base: Assessment starts Wed 08:00-09:30, Decision at or shortly before Wed 14:00, about −0 to −2 h. Worst: the workaround causes a lost update or duplicated entry and one check is partly redone, about +2 to +5 h, or the log shows concurrency that did not occur.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: D2's work list for Tue 09:35-15:00 (no intervening arrivals means no gain; idle time before 11:30 means a larger one); A2's shift pattern after 22:30; M1's signing times across many cases; incident logs from the lock workaround; and the identity-failure rate, which determines how many parallel Document Checks would be wasted.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104: early S1 packet and provisional C1 hold | +1-2 h C1 preliminary work; about 2 h S1 work moved earlier; −1-3 h on the final review if the packet was complete; slot-holding overhead | Post-assessment wait for C1 on target cases | C1 queue for cases without a hold; S1 inbox of speculative packets | Urgent compliance cases (two added Mon 16:00); other high-risk cases awaiting C1, such as K-337 types | Ambiguous: positive for flagged cases at low volume, neutral to negative at scale | Single server near full utilisation; no-show slots destroy capacity; priority is zero-sum |
| K-219: D2 instead of D1 for handwritten originals | About −4 h D1; +5-9 h D2; plus rework probability × (extra Document Check + extra Assessment) | D1 queue | D2 queue; assessment queue (earlier arrivals plus rework); external clarification wait | D2's standard digital cases (K-411 types) delayed; D1's complex cases (K-104, K-337 types) helped | Positive only while D2's queue advantage exceeds its service-time and rework penalty | Queue advantage vanishes with volume; rework multiplies downstream work; signing-boundary step |
| K-337: no second Request More Info | −1 D1 check (about 3.5 h) and −1 A1 assessment (about 2.5 h); C1 review earlier and harder; new post-decision monitoring and appeals work | D1 and A1 rework queues; external waiting | C1 (earlier, bunched before weekends); M1 (contested decisions); appeals and condition monitoring | Cases behind in D1 and A1 queues helped; other C1 cases delayed; later cases compete with resubmissions | Logged cycle time strongly positive; risk-adjusted effect ambiguous to negative | Weekend cliff; changed decision mix; deferred demand returns with a lag and partly outside the log |
| K-411: both verification tasks at Triage | Same total work; Document Check released earlier; more simultaneous work in progress; wasted Document Checks when identity fails | Hidden wait for task creation | Document queue earlier in the day; assessment queue; M1 signing batches | Cases overtaken at D2 and A2 | Small positive; near zero when assessment is the binding constraint | Gain is capped by the slower branch, then the assessment queue, then the signing window |

**Compliance reservations.** C1 is a single server whose forecast queue was already 16-20 h early in the week. One provisional slot is a cheap priority claim; 100 are not. If each high-risk case adds a 1-2 h preliminary screen, that is 100-200 h of new C1 demand, repaid by only 1-3 h per final review and only when the packet was complete. Slots booked from Monday forecasts will often miss the actual Assessment completion, so C1 idles or the hold is released; when every case holds a reservation, none has priority, and genuinely urgent cases are displaced. The net effect is plausibly negative unless screens are limited to triage-flagged ambiguities and holds stay soft.

**D1/D2 substitution.** D2's short queue is a marginal advantage. Sending 100 handwritten cases (4-8 h each) to D2 erases it within the first handful; D2's generalist customers then wait behind them while D1's queue shrinks. The bottleneck migrates from D1 to D2.

**Rework.** At an assumed 20-40% clarification rate, D2 routing adds 20-40 extra Document Checks and Assessments plus external waits. Banning second loops removes roughly one D1 check and one A1 assessment per affected case, but pulls harder, evidence-incomplete reviews forward to C1 and creates appeals, resubmissions, and condition monitoring that reappear later, partly outside the event log.

**Assessment.** Parallel verification and early D2 starts advance readiness, not assessor capacity. A1 and A2 queues (4-12 h) absorb earlier arrivals; with one assessor in training the gain mostly converts into a longer assessment wait, and M1's apparent signing windows quantise whatever remains.

**Weekends and externals.** Savings are step functions of shift and weekend boundaries: K-337 gains about 48 h or about 111 h depending on a Friday cut-off. A blanket no-second-loop rule mainly helps cases whose request would straddle a weekend; D2-triggered clarifications launched on Thursday or Friday do the opposite.

**Timestamps.** If workaround users enter times in batches, logs show overlap that never happened or hide real lock conflicts. The factual D1 overlap on Tuesday shows the log already mixes elapsed and touch time. Rollout evaluation must use system-generated start and complete events, or measured concurrency is a logging artefact.

## 6. Pilot designs

Intervention 1 is creating both verification tasks at Triage (K-411 type). Intervention 2 is the early escalation packet with a soft provisional C1 hold (K-104 type).

| pilot element | intervention 1 | intervention 2 |
|---|---|---|
| eligibility | Cases with no originals required, digital standard documents, not high-risk or high-value, routed through the legacy system that creates only the identity task | High-risk or high-value cases in which Triage records a specific compliance ambiguity (for example sanctions or ownership) and enough material exists to fill a minimum packet checklist |
| assignment rule | Randomise at Triage by case-ID parity or by alternating days; if contamination through shared queues is a concern, randomise by day or week (switchback) rather than by case | Randomise flagged cases individually at Triage, with a hard cap on simultaneous holds (for example two) so that C1 load is bounded; holds are released automatically if Assessment is not complete at slot time |
| sample/support logic | Volume is high, so a few hundred cases over 3-4 weeks covers Monday peaks and normal days; compare only within strata of entry-time D2 and A2 queue length so treated and control cases share the same congestion support | Volume is low and C1 is one person, so run 6-8 weeks; stratify by C1 queue at Triage and by packet completeness; do not extrapolate beyond the share of C1 time actually tested |
| primary outcome | Register-to-Notify cycle time, plus time from Triage to "both checks complete" to separate earlier readiness from earlier completion | Time from Assessment completion to Decision, and total cycle time; final Compliance Review service time |
| process-path outcome | Share of cases with truly overlapping check intervals; order of check completion; frequency of Document Checks performed on cases that then fail identity | Share of cases in which the preliminary screen preceded Assessment; hold kept, released, or missed; any added Request More Info; confirmation that every case still has a final review after the final Assessment |
| quality outcome | Check error and rework rates; data conflicts or lost updates attributed to the workaround | Agreement between preliminary and final compliance opinion; later audit findings; changes in decision mix (approve, conditions, reject); four-eyes compliance |
| queue-spillover outcome | Waiting time of non-pilot cases at D2 and A2; assessment queue length by hour; M1 batch sizes | Waiting time of non-pilot and urgent compliance cases; C1 idle minutes inside held slots; S1 advisory load |
| logging validation | System-generated start and complete events; comparison of event time with entry time to detect batch entry; automatic check for overlapping intervals per resource | Separate activity labels for preliminary screen and final review; timestamps for hold creation, release, and use; automatic check that final review follows the latest Assessment and uses a different person from the assessor |
| stopping rule | Stop if any data-integrity incident affects a decision, if conflict or redo rate exceeds a pre-set threshold (for example 2%), or if non-pilot median wait at A2 rises by more than a pre-set margin without a cycle-time gain | Stop if urgent compliance cases breach their deadline because of a hold, if more than a pre-set share of holds (for example 30%) go unused, or if any case is decided without a proper final review |

## 7. Final synthesis

Most robust: creating both verification tasks at Triage (K-411). It uses a concurrency the rules already allow, changes no decision or reviewer, and its downside is bounded. The likely gain for this case is small (0-6 h) because the document queue, the assessment queue, and M1's signing rhythm absorb most of the earlier readiness, but it rarely makes a case slower and is easily reversed. Early escalation with a soft C1 hold (K-104) is second: a plausible 4-10 h gain and possibly better compliance preparation, provided the hold never becomes a hard reservation on the only certified reviewer.

Most dangerous: prohibiting the second information loop (K-337). It offers the largest logged saving (about 2 to 4.6 days), but only by changing what is decided. Hindsight shows the discrepancy was benign, so a conditional approval would have been right and a rejection wrong; a policy cannot know that in advance. Risk moves to appeals, loss exposure, and compliance defensibility, largely outside the event log, and is hard to undo.

Least transportable: the D2 reassignment (K-219) and the C1 reservation (K-104). Each exploits spare capacity or priority that exists only while few cases use it. K-219's gain needs D2's short queue and a Tuesday-afternoon signature; at 100 cases D2 becomes the bottleneck and rework compounds. K-337 also transports poorly because its saving is tied to one weekend.

Most valuable data: resource calendars and M1 signing times; queue lengths at every enqueue and start event rather than five snapshots; arrival streams of non-target cases at D2, A2, and C1; system-generated start, suspend, and complete timestamps (the D1 overlap suggests current ones are unreliable); D2 versus D1 clarification and error rates on handwritten documents; supplier response times by weekday; and post-decision outcomes such as appeals, breached conditions, and losses.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. `structural` — The twelve normative rules are complete and binding; early Escalation Advice and a Preliminary Compliance Screen are optional additions that never substitute for the final Compliance Review, and a document checker's clarification need is formally issued through Assessment.
2. `temporal` — The working calendar is inferred from the log: document and assessment staff are seen working evenings or nights, M1 is seen signing only 08:30-18:40 on weekdays (possibly in batches), and neither M1 nor external parties act at weekends.
3. `temporal` — Queue snapshots are forecasts with wide error (about ±50%); observed service times are reused where the resource and task are unchanged, and the guidance ranges are used otherwise.
4. `resource` — Queues are roughly first-come-first-served without pre-emption, a provisional C1 slot is a soft hold released when the case is not ready, and the Tuesday D1 overlap is a logging or interleaving artefact rather than true double service.
5. `behavioral` — Staff keep the same thoroughness under the interventions: A1 reaches the same assessment, C1 does not anchor on a preliminary view, and M1 is willing to sign a documented conditional approval when further requests are barred.
6. `quality` — D2's clarification probability on difficult handwriting is unobserved and taken as 20-40% for illustration; the benign nature of K-337's remaining discrepancy is known only with hindsight and would not be known at decision time.
7. `interference` — Single-case counterfactuals hold all other cases' paths and queue positions approximately fixed; this is knowingly false at 100 cases, where treated cases displace untreated ones and each other.
8. `behavioral` — External parties (customers, suppliers, senders of originals) respond with the same timing regardless of internal changes, so Originals Receipt and supplier reply times are not moved by any intervention.
