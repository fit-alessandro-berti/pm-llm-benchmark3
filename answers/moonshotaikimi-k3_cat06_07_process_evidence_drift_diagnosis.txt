## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

- **January — stable reference month.**  
  **Observed:** labels are mature, observed and predicted rates are 0.18/0.19, AUROC is 0.81, ECE is 0.025, unknown activities are 0.2%, and the parts edge is 28 h.  
  **Interpretation:** this is a valid predictive and process baseline, although the parts edge is the lowest observed value.

- **February — stable.**  
  **Observed:** predictive metrics, variant mix, label maturity, and breach rates remain close to January. The parts edge rises slightly to 31 h.  
  **Interpretation:** no material drift diagnosis is supported.

- **March — stable predictions, weak process early warning.**  
  **Observed:** model metrics remain near baseline, but the parts edge reaches 35 h. Mature V3 breach remains 0.30.  
  **Interpretation:** there may be an emerging process trend, but it has not yet produced a clear outcome or model-performance break.

- **April — primarily a schema and feature-pipeline incident.**  
  **Observed:** observed breach is still 0.19 and labels are fully mature, while mean predicted risk jumps to 0.28, AUROC falls to 0.67, ECE rises to 0.110, unknown activities reach 17%, queue-feature missingness reaches 14%, and 18% of variants are unmapped. The renamed diagnosis event caused prefixes to be built incorrectly or not at all. Duplicate handling was incomplete.  
  **Interpretation:** corrupted prefixes, duplicate events, and missing features explain both poorer ranking and overprediction. The apparent variant shift is mostly artificial: after excluding the 18% unmapped cases, the mapped V1/V2/V3 proportions remain approximately 45/35/20. The slight increases in mature variant-specific breach rates leave a residual question, but they are not the dominant explanation.  
  **Falsifier:** if replaying raw events with normalized labels, authoritative deduplication, and fully recomputed prefix features does not move April close to baseline, investigate a secondary real April effect.

- **May — partial technical recovery plus a mild process warning.**  
  **Observed:** unknown activities fall to 2%, missing queue features to 3%, AUROC recovers to 0.78, and mean predicted risk falls to 0.21, but calibration remains worse than the first quarter. The parts edge rises to 38 h and mature V3 breach is 0.33. The variant-weighted expectation is approximately \(0.43(0.16)+0.34(0.11)+0.22(0.33)=0.179\), matching the observed 0.18.  
  **Interpretation:** the patch repaired most new records, but April’s cached features were not fully recomputed and early-May records may straddle the patch. A mild genuine parts trend is also possible. May should not be treated as completely clean without lineage validation.

- **June — intentional routing/process-mix change, not yet strong concept drift.**  
  **Observed:** V2 rises to 52%, V1 falls to 30%, labels remain mature, and data quality is near normal. Observed breach falls to 0.15 while mean predicted risk remains 0.20 and ECE rises to 0.060. The remote edge remains fast.  
  **Interpretation:** the aggregate reduction is largely explained by shifting cases into lower-risk remote resolution. Within-route rates are mostly stable: V1 remains 0.16 and V2 improves slightly to 0.09. V3 rises to 0.35 and the parts edge reaches 42 h, so there is an early warning, but not enough to declare a major concept break.  
  **Nuance:** if planned route is unavailable to the model, a policy-induced change in route assignment can appear as a change in \(P(Y\mid X)\). Operationally, however, the evidence supports process/mix drift rather than deteriorated outcome mechanics within the same route.  
  **Falsifier:** matched cases following the same route before and after 1 June show a material outcome change not explained by case features.

- **July — supplier-mediated process drift and probable concept drift begin.**  
  **Observed:** the supplier issue starts on 10 July. The parts edge jumps to 65 h, mature V3 breach rises to 0.46, and V3 share rises modestly to 22%. Labels are 98% mature. Aggregate observed breach remains 0.19 because half of cases are remote.  
  **Interpretation:** the high remote share masks substantial V3 deterioration. This is a genuine process-performance break and, relative to the original diagnosis-time feature set, probable concept drift. Global calibration remains deceptively close because V2 offsets V3.  
  **Falsifier:** after matching V3 cases on diagnosis-time attributes and supplier/part characteristics, July outcomes are statistically consistent with May–June.

- **August — severe parts-process deterioration, case-mix shift, and some censoring.**  
  **Observed:** V3 reaches 35%, the parts edge reaches 130 h, mature V3 breach reaches 0.64, observed aggregate breach is 0.28 versus mean prediction 0.19, and labels are only 93% mature.  
  **Interpretation:** this is not merely a label artifact. The mature V3 rate and performance edge establish real deterioration. The variant-weighted expectation is approximately \(0.25(0.18)+0.40(0.11)+0.35(0.64)=0.313\), already above the currently observed 0.28; informative censoring may make the eventual aggregate higher. Both process drift and supplier-mediated concept drift are strongly implicated, alongside increased V3 mix.

- **September — overlapping supplier, population, mix, and maturity effects.**  
  **Observed:** Partner Z enters at 24%, V3 reaches 42%, the parts edge reaches 148 h, observed breach is 0.31 versus mean prediction 0.21, and only 82% of labels are mature. Partner Z’s current observed breach is 0.45 versus 0.27 elsewhere, but its labels mature more slowly.  
  **Interpretation:** the initial supplier deterioration cannot be caused by Partner Z because it began in July and was severe in August. Partner Z nevertheless amplifies the V3 mix and introduces unmodeled rural and device-age differences. The variant-weighted expectation is approximately \(0.22(0.19)+0.36(0.12)+0.42(0.61)=0.341\), above the currently observed 0.31. Process drift, probable concept drift, population shift, and censoring overlap.  
  **Falsifier for a Partner-specific explanation:** mature source-by-variant results and transport reweighting show that source adds little once path, device, geography, and supplier conditions are included.

- **October — heavily confounded by informative label immaturity.**  
  **Observed:** label maturity falls to 55%, median label delay reaches 52 d, observed breach falls to 0.16, and AUROC/Brier/ECE appear poor. Yet V3 remains 41%, Partner Z rises to 27%, and the parts edge remains 155 h. Feature-quality indicators are near normal.  
  **Interpretation:** the observed breach reduction is not credible evidence of operational recovery. Long-resolution cases are disproportionately missing labels, so current breach incidence is likely understated and all predictive metrics are noncomparable with mature months. The apparent overprediction—0.23 predicted versus 0.16 observed—could reverse as labels arrive. The underlying supplier and population issues probably persist, but October cannot yet quantify them. If the 155 h edge uses only completed edges, even that duration may be conservative because long open orders are censored.  
  **Falsifier:** complete labels and independent timestamp-derived outcomes confirm a stable 0.16 breach rate and label arrival is unrelated to path length, source, or expected duration.

April, June, August–September, and October therefore have distinct signatures: **April** has stable outcomes but broken features; **June** has a mature-label outcome reduction following a routing intervention; **August–September** have within-path and process-performance deterioration compounded by population shift; and **October** has an outcome-censoring break despite continuing adverse process evidence.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

1. **Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting**
   - **Procedure:** For each case-start cohort \(m\) and age \(d\), plot label availability \(A_m(d)\), cumulative observed breach incidence \(B_m(d)\), and predictive metrics. Stratify by variant, source, path length, and promised-date horizon. Recreate historical reports at exactly the same cohort age as the October cutoff, then recompute all months at full maturity. Use inverse-probability-of-censoring weights when label availability depends on path or source. Where contractually valid, reconstruct breach directly from promised and resolution timestamps rather than waiting for the API.
   - **Under label delay/censoring:** October’s breach rate rises materially; August–October metrics improve at fixed maturity; historical and current cohorts become more comparable at the same vintage.
   - **Under genuine concept/process drift:** the July–September V3 gap remains at full maturity and at fixed vintage.
   - **Under April pipeline failure:** April does not improve with label maturity because its labels were already mature; only feature replay repairs it.

2. **Recomputation after activity-label normalization and duplicate cleaning**
   - **Procedure:** Canonicalize both diagnosis labels, deduplicate using immutable event IDs and an audited cross-source fingerprint, rebuild every prefix from event order, invalidate all cached derived features, and rescore April and May with the same frozen model. Compare feature values, prediction coverage, score distributions, and metrics before and after replay.
   - **Under pipeline failure:** unknown/unmapped rates and queue missingness fall; April’s mean risk moves from 0.28 toward 0.19; AUROC, Brier, and ECE move toward January–March; the cached event-count feature changes materially.
   - **Under genuine drift:** cleaned April predictions and metrics remain degraded even though process and label evidence are stable.
   - **Under label censoring:** feature replay changes August–October little because the failure is in outcome availability rather than inputs.

3. **Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source, and month**
   - **Procedure:** For each month × variant × source × maturity stratum, report AUROC and PR-AUC with confidence intervals, observed/expected cases, calibration intercept and slope, Brier score, ECE with sensitivity to binning, and reliability curves. Use planned route for deployment-facing cuts and realized route for retrospective diagnosis.
   - **Under population/covariate shift:** aggregate metrics deteriorate, but within-source and within-path calibration is comparatively stable; reweighting restores much of the aggregate performance.
   - **Under concept drift:** V3 underprediction and poor calibration persist after July for both Partner Z and non-Partner-Z cases.
   - **Under censoring:** apparent calibration and AUROC move materially as maturity increases.
   - **Under pipeline failure:** the defect is concentrated in April/pre-replay data rather than continuing smoothly across later months.

4. **Process-variant, DFG, and performance-DFG comparison**
   - **Procedure:** Produce monthly event-calendar and case-start DFGs; compare variant frequencies, conformance to V1–V3, transition frequencies, and median/p90 edge durations. Track open-edge age and use survival methods for `Order Part -> Part Received`, because completed-edge medians are right-censored.
   - **Under process drift:** V2 frequency changes at 1 June; the parts edge changes at 10 July and worsens in August; remote timing remains stable.
   - **Under pipeline failure:** April shows fragmented traces, `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY`, duplicate diagnosis events, and an artificial unmapped variant, while canonical replay restores approximately the baseline topology.
   - **Under concept drift without topology change:** the path graph may remain similar while the performance distribution of a specific edge changes.

5. **Outcome relationship conditional on the same path**
   - **Procedure:** Using mature, cleaned cases, compare outcomes within the same variant and similar diagnosis prefix. Match or adjust for customer, device, geography, part type, promised horizon, diagnosis-time risk, and planned route. Fit a time-interaction model such as breach versus period × path + diagnosis-time features. Add supplier, supplier batch, and quoted lead time available at diagnosis. Use actual post-diagnosis waiting time only as mechanistic evidence, not as a deployment feature.
   - **Under concept drift:** same-path, similarly risky cases have higher breach odds after the supplier onset, and the effect tracks supplier delay.
   - **Under covariate/population shift:** the period coefficient shrinks materially after adding Partner Z, rural distance, device age, part mix, and planned route.
   - **Under pipeline or label explanations:** the effect disappears after feature replay or full label maturity, respectively.
   - **Selection caveat:** conditioning on realized route can introduce selection bias; repeat the analysis using route eligibility or planned route.

6. **Population reweighting and transport-style checks**
   - **Procedure:** Reweight September–October cases to the January–June source and covariate composition using inverse-propensity or entropy balancing. Train or score a pre-September model on non-Partner-Z cases, transport it to Partner Z, and compare mature stratified performance. Check positivity and effective sample size.
   - **Under transportable population shift:** reweighting removes much of the aggregate gap, and Partner Z performance is consistent after covariate adjustment.
   - **Under concept/process drift:** non-Partner-Z V3 cases still deteriorate after July, especially in association with supplier delay.
   - **Under label censoring:** transport conclusions change as Partner Z labels mature.
   - **Under lack of overlap:** weights become extreme, indicating that reweighting is not credible and that mature Partner Z data or a segment model is required.

7. **Feature missingness, prediction coverage, and lineage audits**
   - **Procedure:** Version the raw schema, canonical mapping, deduplication rule, feature-cache generation, model, and outcome API. Audit event counts, duplicate rates, unknown labels, nulls, point-in-time joins, prediction coverage, and feature hashes by case, source, and day. Specifically compare the cached “number of events before diagnosis” with a rebuilt value.
   - **Under pipeline failure:** anomalies align with 1 April and 6 May, affect downstream feature lineage, and disappear after replay.
   - **Under genuine drift:** feature availability and lineage remain stable while substantive feature distributions or outcome relationships change around the routing or supplier dates.
   - **Under label delay:** feature lineage is clean while outcome-arrival lineage shows the October backlog.

8. **Intervention and deployment analyses**
   - **Procedure:** Use interrupted time series or event studies around 1 April, 6 May, 1 June, 10 July, 1 September, and the October backlog. Compare affected routes with unaffected routes or suppliers and annotate model, threshold, mapping, and policy versions.
   - **Under routing-mix drift:** the aggregate break occurs at 1 June, while within-route rates remain mostly stable.
   - **Under supplier-driven concept drift:** same-route outcomes deteriorate after 10 July and track affected suppliers or part categories.
   - **Under pipeline failure:** the break occurs exactly at migration and disappears after canonical replay.
   - **Under label backlog:** process timestamps continue to deteriorate in October while observed outcomes artificially improve.

All comparisons should use confidence intervals, minimum cell sizes, and prespecified maturity windows. Point estimates for Partner Z and rare variants should not be overinterpreted.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

The proportionate response is **pipeline repair and containment now, segment-aware interim calibration after validation, feature enrichment, and full retraining only after mature post-drift labels are available**.

| Stage | Decision | Required gate |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate containment | Freeze automated retraining and threshold changes. Quarantine April’s original features and predictions. If the diagnosis event cannot be canonicalized in production, abstain rather than score a wrong prefix. Mark August–October evaluations provisional. Keep the last validated model only behind input-contract checks. Because mature July–August evidence shows real V3 underprediction, add a temporary operational review or conservative uplift for cases known at diagnosis to require parts or have adverse supplier ETAs. | Critical schema, duplicate, missing-feature, or prediction-coverage alerts block scoring/training. Label immaturity blocks model-change decisions but does not block supplier mitigation. |
| Short-term recalibration | Recalibrate only after feature replay and using mature labels. Prefer calibration by planned route/source, with shrinkage for small cells. If within-stratum discrimination is stable and only the calibration intercept/slope moved, recalibration is sufficient. Do not globally recalibrate to June’s remote-heavy mix or October’s immature labels. | Data-quality pass, adequate maturity, and stable within-stratum ranking. July–August can support an interim V3 calibrator; September should wait for greater maturity. |
| Feature repair and enrichment | Rebuild all prefixes and cached features from canonical events. Add only variables available immediately after diagnosis: source, rural travel distance, device age, part requirement, part type, supplier, quoted or forecast lead time, routing eligibility/planned route, and policy regime. Do not use realized final variant or actual part-wait duration unless known at prediction time. | Successful raw-to-feature reconciliation, point-in-time correctness checks, and stable schema contracts. |
| Full retraining | Retrain if mature, cleaned data show persistent within-path deterioration, feature repair does not recover performance, and stratified discrimination remains weak. Use time-ordered validation and regime indicators rather than a random split. A rolling or time-weighted model is preferable to treating January–October as homogeneous. | August–October labels mature, April/May replay reconciled, and a champion model improves out-of-time mature validation without unacceptable segment regressions. |

| Months | Current training status | Reason and permitted use |
|---|---|---|
| January–March | **Safe reference data** | Mature labels, stable pipeline, stable process. Useful as a baseline, but not sufficient to learn the later supplier or Partner Z regimes. |
| April | **Unsafe as currently stored** | Labels are mature, but prefixes and derived features are corrupted. Use only after canonical replay, deduplication, cache invalidation, and reconciliation. |
| May | **Conditionally usable** | Mostly recovered, but early-May records and April backfill require lineage validation. Prefer rebuilt data and regime tagging. |
| June | **Safe but regime-tagged** | Mature labels and acceptable quality, but the routing policy changed. Include a policy indicator or analyze as a separate regime. |

A **pooled hierarchical model** with route, source, supplier, and relevant interactions is preferable initially. A separate V3 model or calibrator is justified if planned parts routing is known at prediction time, the V3 interaction remains large, and mature sample sizes are adequate. A separate Partner Z model is premature until enough mature Partner Z cases exist and transport tests fail. Realized V1/V2/V3 must not be used as a future-leaking input; if route is unknown at diagnosis, produce scenario predictions or model the routing decision separately.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

Use an immutable raw-event store followed by a canonical event log, point-in-time feature snapshots, prediction snapshots, and versioned label vintages. Every metric should carry event-schema version, feature-cache version, model version, reporting cutoff, and label-maturity status.

| Monitor | Minimum signals and cuts | Dependency and action |
|---|---|---|
| Data-quality monitor | Schema version, unrecognized activity names, duplicate-event rate, `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY`, missing queue features, timestamp ordering, late events, event-volume reconciliation, prediction coverage, feature-cache lineage, and raw-to-feature hashes. | Highest-priority gate. A critical failure marks inputs invalid, suppresses drift conclusions and training, and routes to data engineering. |
| Label-maturity monitor | Maturity at fixed ages and fixed calendar vintages; median/p90 label delay; cumulative breach incidence; missingness by variant, source, path length, and promised horizon; reconciliation of API labels to timestamp-derived outcomes. | Below the maturity threshold, predictive and calibration metrics are provisional and retraining is blocked. The alarm goes to outcome operations, not automatically to model retraining. |
| Predictive monitor | Mean and quantiles of risk, score PSI, AUROC and PR-AUC, score coverage, and prediction volume, cut by month, source, planned/realized variant, and maturity. | Computed only after data-quality and label gates. Score drift alone triggers investigation, not retraining. |
| Calibration monitor | Observed/expected ratio, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, ECE with bin sensitivity, and reliability curves at fixed maturity. | Stable discrimination plus shifted calibration supports recalibration; unstable ranking or a conditional-relationship shift supports retraining analysis. |
| Process-variant monitor | V1/V2/V3 and rare/unmapped shares, conformance rates, route-transition frequencies, new variants, and differences between event-calendar and case-start cohorts. | A mix alert first triggers stratified and reweighted evaluation. If within-variant performance is stable, classify as mix/process drift rather than concept drift. |
| Performance-DFG monitor | Edge frequencies, completed-edge median/p90 durations, open-edge age, bottleneck survival curves, `Order Part -> Part Received`, diagnosis-to-`Remote Fix`, queue times, workload, and supplier/part cuts. | Can trigger immediate operational action regardless of label maturity. It becomes a model-retraining signal only when mature within-path outcomes also deteriorate. |
| Population monitor | Source/Partner Z share, geography, rural distance, device age, customer and part mix, promised horizon, missingness, multivariate feature drift, and transport weights/effective sample size. | Requires source-stratified and reweighted evaluation before changing thresholds or making fairness claims. |
| Intervention/deployment monitor | Migration and patch versions, routing-policy dates, supplier status, Partner Z onboarding, outcome API status, model and calibrator versions, thresholds, overrides, and action exposure. | Annotates every metric break and defines event-study windows. Prevents a known deployment from being mislabeled as spontaneous model decay. |

Alert dependency should be:

1. **Data invalid:** repair or roll back the pipeline; suppress training and drift attribution.
2. **Data valid but labels immature:** publish provisional metrics and label-arrival diagnostics; do not recalibrate or retrain.
3. **Variant or population alert:** compute stratified, fixed-maturity, and reweighted metrics before declaring model decay.
4. **Within-stratum calibration alert with stable discrimination:** test a segment-aware calibrator.
5. **Within-stratum outcome deterioration plus a performance-DFG break:** declare a candidate process-mediated concept drift; use champion/challenger validation and then retrain if persistent.
6. **Process-performance alert with immature labels:** escalate supplier or capacity operations now, while keeping model training gated.
7. **Known intervention:** analyze the break relative to the intervention date before invoking an unexplained-drift response.

The following sketch produces auditable monthly evidence. It assumes one row per event and case-level label fields repeated on events.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

DIAG = "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
ACTIVITY_MAP = {
    DIAG: DIAG,
    "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": DIAG,  # normalize before any feature extraction
}
PART_EDGE = ("Order Part", "Part Received")
REMOTE_EDGE = (DIAG, "Remote Fix")


def _variant_counts(log):
    out = {}
    for variant, traces in pm4py.get_variants(log).items():
        try:
            n = len(traces)
        except TypeError:
            n = int(traces)
        name = " > ".join(map(str, variant)) if isinstance(variant, (tuple, list)) else str(variant)
        out[name] = n
    return dict(sorted(out.items(), key=lambda kv: (-kv[1], kv[0])))


def _edge_summary(log, prefix):
    keys = [
        f"{prefix}order_part_edge_n",
        f"{prefix}order_part_median_h",
        f"{prefix}remote_edge_n",
        f"{prefix}remote_median_h",
    ]
    if len(log) == 0:
        return dict.fromkeys(keys)

    dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
    perf, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)

    def median_hours(edge):
        stats = perf.get(edge, {})
        value = stats.get("median") if isinstance(stats, dict) else None
        return None if value is None else round(float(value) / 3600.0, 2)

    return {
        f"{prefix}order_part_edge_n": int(dfg.get(PART_EDGE, 0)),
        f"{prefix}order_part_median_h": median_hours(PART_EDGE),
        f"{prefix}remote_edge_n": int(dfg.get(REMOTE_EDGE, 0)),
        f"{prefix}remote_median_h": median_hours(REMOTE_EDGE),
    }


def monthly_process_evidence(csv_path="field_service_events.csv"):
    raw = pd.read_csv(csv_path)
    required = ["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"]
    missing_cols = set(required) - set(raw.columns)
    if missing_cols:
        raise ValueError(f"Missing required columns: {missing_cols}")

    for col in ["timestamp", "ingested_at", "label_available_at", "promised_at"]:
        if col in raw.columns:
            raw[col] = pd.to_datetime(raw[col], errors="coerce", utc=True).dt.tz_convert(None)

    audit = {"rows_read": len(raw)}
    invalid = raw[required].isna().any(axis=1)
    audit["invalid_required_rows"] = int(invalid.sum())
    raw = raw.loc[~invalid].copy()

    # Canonicalize the renamed diagnosis activity before deduplication/features.
    raw["activity"] = raw["activity"].replace(ACTIVITY_MAP)

    # Deterministic duplicate strategy: latest ingestion wins for a repeated event_id;
    # then remove exact case/activity/timestamp fingerprints. Removed rows are returned
    # for review because identical timestamps can occasionally be legitimate.
    sort_col = "ingested_at" if "ingested_at" in raw.columns else "timestamp"
    raw = raw.sort_values(sort_col, kind="mergesort", na_position="first")
    duplicate_mask = pd.Series(False, index=raw.index)
    if "event_id" in raw.columns:
        duplicate_mask |= raw["event_id"].notna() & raw.duplicated("event_id", keep="last")
    duplicate_mask |= raw.duplicated(["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"], keep="last")

    duplicate_audit = raw.loc[duplicate_mask].copy()
    clean = raw.loc[~duplicate_mask].copy()
    audit.update({
        "duplicate_rows_removed": int(duplicate_mask.sum()),
        "duplicate_rule": "latest event_id, then exact case/activity/timestamp fingerprint",
        "activity_normalization": ACTIVITY_MAP,
    })

    clean["case_start"] = clean.groupby("case_id")["timestamp"].transform("min")
    clean["case_start_month"] = clean["case_start"].dt.to_period("M").astype(str)

    formatted = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        clean,
        case_id="case_id",
        activity_key="activity",
        timestamp_key="timestamp",
    )
    case_col, act_col, ts_col = (
        "case:concept:name",
        "concept:name",
        "time:timestamp",
    )
    formatted = formatted.sort_values([case_col, ts_col])
    event_log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(formatted)

    periods = pd.period_range(
        clean["case_start"].min().to_period("M"),
        clean["timestamp"].max().to_period("M"),
        freq="M",
    )

    event_rows, case_rows = [], []
    variants_by_month, feature_audit = {}, {}

    for period in periods:
        month = str(period)

        # Event-calendar cohort: work occurring in the month. Traces are clipped at
        # month boundaries, so this measures operational load and in-month behavior.
        month_log = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            event_log,
            str(period.start_time),
            str(period.end_time),
            mode="events",
        )
        event_df = formatted.loc[
            formatted[ts_col].between(period.start_time, period.end_time)
        ]
        event_row = {
            "month": month,
            "events": int(len(event_df)),
            "cases_touching_month": int(event_df[case_col].nunique()),
            "unknown_activity_share": round(float(event_df[act_col].eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean()), 4),
        }
        event_row.update(_edge_summary(month_log, "event_window_"))
        event_rows.append(event_row)

        # Case-start cohort: cases first seen in the month, retaining all events
        # available at the extract cutoff. This is the prediction/evaluation cohort;
        # recent cohorts can still have incomplete traces and labels.
        case_ids = clean.loc[clean["case_start_month"].eq(month), "case_id"].unique()
        if len(case_ids) == 0:
            continue
        case_df = formatted.loc[formatted[case_col].isin(case_ids)].copy()
        case_log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(case_df)
        variants_by_month[month] = _variant_counts(case_log)

        temporal_features = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(case_df)
        top_missing = (
            temporal_features.isna()
            .mean()
            .sort_values(ascending=False)
            .head(10)
            .round(4)
            .to_dict()
        )
        feature_audit[month] = {
            "temporal_feature_shape": temporal_features.shape,
            "top_missing": top_missing,
        }

        case_row = {
            "month": month,
            "cases_started": int(len(case_ids)),
            "events_for_cohort": int(len(case_df)),
            "unknown_activity_share": round(float(case_df[act_col].eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean()), 4),
            "distinct_variants": len(variants_by_month[month]),
            "temporal_feature_shape": str(temporal_features.shape),
            "temporal_top_missing": top_missing,
        }
        case_row.update(_edge_summary(case_log, "case_cohort_"))

        first_by_case = case_df.sort_values(ts_col).groupby(case_col).first()
        if "queue_feature" in first_by_case.columns:
            case_row["missing_queue_feature"] = round(
                float(first_by_case["queue_feature"].isna().mean()), 4
            )
        if "source" in first_by_case.columns:
            case_row["source_mix"] = first_by_case["source"].value_counts(normalize=True).round(4).to_dict()

        if "breach_label" in case_df.columns:
            labels = case_df.groupby(case_col).agg(breach=("breach_label", "max"))
            if "label_available_at" in case_df.columns:
                labels["label_at"] = case_df.groupby(case_col)["label_available_at"].max()
                mature = labels["label_at"].notna()

                diag_time = (
                    case_df.loc[case_df[act_col].eq(DIAG)]
                    .groupby(case_col)[ts_col]
                    .min()
                )
                if "promised_at" in case_df.columns:
                    anchor = case_df.groupby(case_col)["promised_at"].max()
                else:
                    anchor = diag_time
                labels["label_delay_days"] = (
                    labels["label_at"] - anchor
                ).dt.total_seconds() / 86400.0
            else:
                labels["label_at"] = pd.NaT
                mature = labels["breach"].notna()

            observed = labels.loc[mature, "breach"].mean()
            case_row["label_maturity"] = round(float(mature.mean()), 4)
            case_row["observed_breach_rate"] = (
                None if pd.isna(observed) else round(float(observed), 4)
            )
            if "label_delay_days" in labels:
                delay = labels["label_delay_days"].median()
                case_row["median_label_delay_days"] = (
                    None if pd.isna(delay) else round(float(delay), 2)
                )

        case_rows.append(case_row)

    return {
        "audit": audit,
        "duplicate_audit": duplicate_audit,
        "event_calendar": pd.DataFrame(event_rows),
        "case_start_cohorts": pd.DataFrame(case_rows),
        "variant_counts_by_case_start_month": variants_by_month,
        "feature_audit": feature_audit,
    }
```

PM4Py is used here for event normalization, process discovery, variants, and temporal-feature extraction—not as a fictitious drift detector. Drift decisions should be made by the gated monitoring logic above.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

| Failure mode | Consequence | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Retraining on October’s immature labels | The model learns that long, unresolved, high-risk cases are non-breaches, worsening future underprediction. AUROC, Brier, and ECE are also evaluated on a biased subset. | Enforce a maturity gate; use fixed-vintage backtesting, cumulative-incidence estimates, and inverse-probability-of-censoring weights; reconcile API labels to timestamps. |
| Calibrating around April’s pipeline bug | The calibrator learns to compensate for wrong prefixes, duplicate events, and missing features. Those corrections become harmful once the pipeline is repaired. | Quarantine April, replay raw events, invalidate caches, and calibrate only after lineage reconciliation. Use input contracts and abstention for unmapped diagnosis events. |
| Ignoring genuine supplier-driven process/concept drift | Treating August–September as only mix or label delay leaves V3 cases materially underpredicted and delays expediting, capacity, or supplier intervention. | Monitor V3 observed/expected risk and parts-edge survival; add supplier and quoted-lead-time features; use a temporary parts-case guardrail while labels mature. |
| Attributing Partner Z’s higher rate to unfair model behavior without stratified evidence | The organization may change thresholds or source-specific treatment when the gap is actually caused by V3 mix, rural distance, device age, supplier conditions, or slower labels. Conversely, dismissing the concern without analysis could conceal real inequity. | Use mature source-by-variant calibration and error rates, uncertainty intervals, reweighting/transport checks, and fairness review. Collect legitimate operational features and audit proxies before deploying source-specific treatment. |
| Treating June’s aggregate improvement as concept drift | A global downward recalibration fitted to a remote-heavy month would underpredict standard and parts-dependent cases. | Annotate the routing intervention, compare within-route outcomes, report mix-adjusted performance, and model planned route or policy regime. |
| Using realized variant or actual part wait as a predictor | Offline performance appears strong, but the model is undeployable immediately after diagnosis because those facts are not yet known. | Enforce point-in-time feature validation. Use planned route, part requirement, supplier, and forecast lead time; reserve realized path and actual wait for retrospective diagnosis. |
| Applying one global recalibration across heterogeneous regimes | Average calibration may improve while V2 is overcorrected and V3 remains underpredicted. | Use hierarchical or segment-aware calibration with shrinkage, minimum cell sizes, and out-of-time validation by route and source. |
| Letting a label-delay gate block all operational response | Waiting for perfect labels avoids bad retraining but allows the supplier problem to continue causing breaches. | Decouple model-action gates from process-action gates: label delay suppresses retraining, while performance-DFG and open-order alerts still trigger supplier escalation. |
