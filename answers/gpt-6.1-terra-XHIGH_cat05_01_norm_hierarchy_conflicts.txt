## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts and authority.** GPIP-4 is board-approved, applies to all purchases, and reserves its written exceptions to the Chief Risk Officer (CRO). The CRO neither issued a documented exception nor ratified ECD-2. ECD-2 was signed by the COO and Incident Commander, and the CFO sent an ordinary email. The record does not establish that an incident directive signed by those officials is an A7 exception. Past treatment of signed directives as temporary overlays supports taking ECD-2 seriously, but does not establish authority to displace GPIP-4, particularly its new-supplier clearance requirement.

**Working interpretation.** Apply GPIP-4 as the presumptive floor; a specific, timely ECD-2 overrides the local PO-before-commitment and supplier-register requirements for a purchase within its scope. B7, C1, C2 and C4 support that limited result, although B7's “ordinary” wording is ambiguous. ECD-2 adds joint authorization and recording duties. It does not, without an established A7 exception, remove GPIP-4's pre-commitment approvals or segregation requirement. D is relevant to interpretation and operational instructions, but the CFO's authority to interpret Finance controls does not by itself establish authority to dispense with the board's express Finance Controller and independent-budget-owner approvals. D's reference to essential integrity controls reinforces a cautious reading. D2 adds a Legal clearance step for covered purchases over EUR 150,000; it does not substitute for the other controls.

For A1, I interpret a joint Incident Command authorization as a final business approval when it is the only decision authorizing the purchase. In that situation, the requester or committer cannot be one of the final joint approvers. This is an interpretation of “final business approver,” not a stated definition; a later, genuine independent final business approval could change the application of A1, although it would not automatically cure missing pre-commitment controls. Two Incident Command members are not automatically an independent budget owner and a Finance Controller. They could satisfy multiple roles if their authority, independence, and timely approval are established.

**Scope and time.** Apply each source at the legally effective time of the relevant act. ECD-2's end time is treated as inclusive through 18:00 CEST; a commitment after that time needs a valid extension or another applicable exception. Affected-line parts needed to contain the event are presumptively within C's scope; a backup part with three days' stock is contested. C does not automatically govern all purchases made during its window. B applies to Aachen maintenance and production-support purchases; a cafeteria repair with no production effect is presumptively outside it, although the site's actual scope definition could change that conclusion. B4 requires an imminent safety harm or expected production stoppage within six hours; urgency alone is insufficient. Its PO timing exception is separate from its conditional Shift Lead substitution. B2 specifies that Plant and Finance approvals are required but does not expressly put them before commitment; A2 independently requires its specified approvals before commitment where the EUR 50,000 threshold applies. B6's 24-hour form requirement applies to use of the local emergency procedure.

**Evidence and burden.** A contemporaneous authorization can be proved later; a late approval is different from a late entry recording an earlier approval. ON-44 is an evidence-weight note, not an exception, and its stated ERP recovery uncertainty concerns the July 14 outage; a separate clock defect must be established where relevant. Prefer corroborated recordings, acknowledgments, signed records and reliable access logs to a conflicting database timestamp. The proponent of an exception must establish its authority, scope and conditions, and the organization must be able to substantiate required approvals. Missing entries alone do not prove that an act did not occur. Classifications below distinguish an observed breach from an unresolved ability to prove compliance. Where the facts support a narrow inference rather than proof, the confidence and missing-evidence fields make that limitation explicit. Severity reflects control impact, not a numerical score.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

- **P-501 — INDETERMINATE instead of JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** Require affirmative proof that the final Plant/business approver was independent of both ENG-1 and SHIFT-1, rather than provisionally inferring it from the recorded Plant approval and lack of a SHIFT-1 delegation. This is credible because an event label alone does not identify its actor. If the final approver was either prohibited person, the result becomes NONCOMPLIANT instead. The B4 PO exception remains separately justified.
- **P-503 — INDETERMINATE instead of NONCOMPLIANT.** Treat C3 as preliminary operational authorization rather than the final business approval, and require proof of a separate final approver before applying A1. This is credible because the sources do not define “final business approver.” The case would still need evidence of timely A2 and A3 controls. Alternatively, if PROC-3 is established as a final joint approver, C's broad scope alone cannot change the A1 result under the baseline hierarchy.
- **P-504 — NONCOMPLIANT instead of INDETERMINATE.** Interpret the SOP's maintenance scope as covering all site maintenance, including the cafeteria, and find no qualifying B4 emergency. The known post-commitment PO and unapproved supplier then conflict with B1 and B3. This is plausible from the words “maintenance ... purchases,” but weaker if the controlled SOP or site practice limits maintenance to production support; food spoilage alone establishes neither a production stoppage nor imminent safety harm.
- **P-505 — INDETERMINATE or JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION instead of NONCOMPLIANT.** Establish a timely Controller approval from independent evidence, or establish that a qualified Incident Command authorizer actually gave that approval before commitment; also establish the independent budget-owner approval. The acknowledgment and reliable clock correction could then place commitment before 18:00 and support C's PO exception. A mere change of the ERP timestamp cannot cure the separate missing or late Finance approval. A broader claim that D1 legally replaces A2 requires a stronger premise about CFO authority than the record establishes.
- **P-506 — INDETERMINATE instead of NONCOMPLIANT.** Show a contemporaneous, credible prediction of a production stoppage within six hours or imminent safety harm, making B4 applicable independently of C. That would justify the later PO, but would not prove the separate A2 budget-owner approval. An effective extension of C would also need evidence of an authorized issuer, scope and time; the buyer's unsupported statement alone is weak.
- **P-507 or P-508 — JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION instead of INDETERMINATE.** Prove that the timely Incident Command authorizations were also approvals by an independent budget owner and a Finance Controller, that B2's Plant and Finance requirements were met, and that required records were timely. The named authorizers could hold multiple roles, so this is possible; membership alone does not establish it. Conversely, authoritative evidence that no timely Controller or budget-owner approval occurred would make either case NONCOMPLIANT under A2.
- **P-507 or P-508 — NONCOMPLIANT on a narrower directive reading.** Conclude that B7 does not empower the COO/Incident Commander to supersede the controlled supplier-register requirement, or that the purchase lies outside C's scope. The unapproved supplier then breaches B3. This reading respects the ambiguity of “ordinary” but must explain why the explicit C2/C4 language and prior incident-overlay practice do not govern this local requirement.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

A **policy breach** is an established failure of an applicable obligation, such as P-502's clearance after a corroborated commitment, or P-503's segregation conflict under the stated final-approval interpretation. P-505's recorded Finance approval is late, subject to evidence of an earlier qualifying approval. A **logging defect** is a late or inaccurate system entry for an act that was timely performed and timely recorded as required: P-507's ERP entry is a candidate, provided the signed PDF and outage evidence are authenticated. **Late evidence of a timely act** can establish the act's occurrence, but does not necessarily establish that C3's separate four-hour recording obligation was met. An **inability to prove compliance** is the current status of the A2 role and approval evidence in P-507 and P-508, and of several final-approver or scope questions elsewhere; it is not by itself proof that an approval was absent.

Preserve original calls, supplier acknowledgments, signed PDFs, metadata, access logs, clock calibration records, approval/delegation rosters, supplier-register snapshots, and incident forecasts. Reconstruct actual commitment and approval times, retaining both original and corrected timestamps with an audit trail. Verify each purported approval's content, actor, authority and independence, including whether an Incident Command decision actually covered the relevant budget or Finance control. Determine whether any emergency form was required and recorded within 24 hours. Do not backdate or represent a later approval as an earlier one.

For P-501, verify the final approver and segregation before closing the provisional exception. For P-502, escalate the late new-supplier clearance and confirm Finance and budget approvals, assess supplier and goods risk, and document the control breach and mitigation. For P-503, escalate the apparent segregation conflict and determine whether timely Compliance and A2 approvals existed; review whether the backup purchase was in scope. For P-504, establish the supplier's new status, SOP scope and final approver. For P-505, reconcile the call, acknowledgment and clock evidence, and locate any earlier qualifying Controller and budget approvals. For P-506, seek the contemporaneous emergency forecast and any authorized extension; document the late PO unless the B4 exception is established. For P-507, authenticate the signed PDF and outage trail and establish the A2 and B2 approvals. For P-508, verify the four-hour authorization record and the budget, Finance and Plant approval roles.

Where controls were missed, obtain any still-useful risk review, financial approval, supplier assessment or legal advice now, label it as retrospective mitigation, and assess whether receipt or further use should be held pending that review. Track unresolved evidence and exceptions to closure with owners and deadlines. Monitor pre-commitment approval rates by threshold and supplier status, late PO and emergency-form rates, scope disputes, exception duration, repeated use of the same authorizers, and aging of unresolved evidence. Independently sample incident records against calls and supplier acknowledgments rather than relying only on ERP event order.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

“GPIP-4 sets the minimum controls for all purchases. Controlled local procedures may add requirements. An incident directive signed by the COO and Incident Commander may temporarily change only the local PO sequence and supplier-register requirements if it identifies the incident, covered goods or services, locations, effective start and expiry, and reasons. It does not waive GPIP-4. Only a written, signed Chief Risk Officer exception may modify specified GPIP controls, and it must identify its scope, controls, compensating safeguards, start and expiry; any renewal requires the same authority before expiry. No verbal extension is effective.

For purchases of EUR 50,000 or more, an independent budget owner and Finance Controller must approve before commitment. For purchases above EUR 100,000 or from a new supplier, Compliance must clear before commitment. A requester or committer must not participate as a final business approver. Incident Command authorizers may also satisfy a required role only when their distinct authority and independence are documented and their approval expressly covers that role. Emergency status alone waives tendering only. Any permitted use of an unapproved supplier must include a documented proportionate supplier-risk review and does not itself waive new-supplier Compliance clearance.

For an in-scope directive purchase, two distinct Incident Command members must authorize before commitment; a verbal authorization must be recorded within four hours, identifying the members, decision and actual time. Preserve contemporaneous evidence and distinguish the time of the act, record creation and system entry. Later evidence may prove a timely act but cannot convert a late act into a timely one. After expiry, normal controls resume unless a valid renewal is already effective. Record and review all emergency exceptions promptly, set a maximum duration and renewal limit, and require independent periodic review of repeated exceptions and their causes to prevent emergency arrangements becoming routine.”
