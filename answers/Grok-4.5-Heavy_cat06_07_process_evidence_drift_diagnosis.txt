## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

January–March form a stable baseline: full label maturity, consistent variant mix (~45 % V1 / 35 % V2 / 20 % V3), parts edge ~30 h, AUROC ~0.81, ECE ~0.02–0.025, and matured breach rates by variant essentially constant. No drift signals of concern.

April is not a process or concept failure. The workflow-system migration renamed the diagnosis activity; the feature pipeline continued to recognise only the old label, producing 17 % UNKNOWN activity, 14 % missing queue features, 18 % unmapped variants and corrupted prediction prefixes. Mean predicted risk inflated while the matured observed breach rate remained unchanged at ~0.19. The May mapping patch recovered most records but left residual feature-lineage gaps (cached event counts not fully recomputed). Treating April metrics as evidence of concept drift would be incorrect; they are pipeline artefacts.

June is a distinct, genuine process-mix change caused by the new remote-routing policy. V2 share rose to 52 %, overall observed breach fell to 0.15 exactly as expected from V2’s stable low matured rate (~0.09–0.11). Discrimination held (AUROC 0.79); the modest rise in ECE reflects a mild covariate shift from the altered mix, not a change in the outcome relationship inside any variant. Calling June “concept drift” would misdiagnose a policy-driven compositional effect.

July marks the onset of real process degradation: the parts-supplier edge lengthened to 65 h and V3 matured breach began to rise (0.46). August–September cannot be treated as a single homogeneous model failure. The dominant driver is severe process drift (parts edge 130 h → 148 h) that induces genuine concept drift on the V3 segment: the same diagnosis-time path features now map to a substantially higher breach probability because the critical path exceeds the SLA. Simultaneously V3 share itself rises. September adds a distinct population shift—Partner Z onboarding (24 % of volume, 58 % routed to V3, higher rural distance and device age, slower label maturity). The observed overall breach of 0.31 therefore mixes three overlapping but separable phenomena. A single “retrain everything” narrative would miss that supplier recovery could reverse much of the August concept shift while leaving the Partner-Z population issues intact.

October is heavily confounded by label delay and censoring. Maturity is only 55 %, median delay 52 d, and the outcome-API backlog preferentially leaves long-path (V3 / Partner-Z) cases unlabelled. The reported observed breach of 0.16 is therefore not comparable to mature months; short-path cases dominate the labelled set and artificially depress the rate. Underlying process performance remains degraded (parts edge 155 h). Metrics computed on the available labels cannot be used for drift diagnosis until maturity curves stabilise.

The four focal periods therefore require four different explanations and four different remedial priorities.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

1. **Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting**  
   Plot cumulative observed breach versus days since case start (or since diagnosis), stratified by month, variant and source. Perform fixed-vintage evaluation: score only those cases whose labels would already have been mature under the historical reporting lag.  
   - Under pure label censoring (October hypothesis): recent curves are still rising at the cutoff; late-arriving labels push the breach rate toward or above the mean predicted risk; metrics improve when restricted to the mature subset.  
   - Under true process/concept improvement: curves flatten early at a low rate and the mature subset remains low.

2. **Recomputation after activity-label normalisation and duplicate cleaning**  
   Map both “Initial Diagnosis Completed” and “Diagnostic Assessment Completed” to a single canonical label; drop exact (case, activity, timestamp) duplicates; recompute every prefix feature, including the previously cached event-count feature; re-score April–May.  
   - Under pipeline failure: AUROC recovers toward 0.80, predicted mean returns to ~0.19, ECE collapses, UNKNOWN share → 0.  
   - Under concurrent real concept drift already present in April: metrics remain degraded after the clean recompute.

3. **Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source and month**  
   Reliability diagrams, AUROC, Brier and ECE computed inside each (month × V1/V2/V3 × Partner-Z/non-Z) cell, using only mature labels.  
   - Under pure mix / covariate shift: within-stratum metrics stay stable; overall degradation is explained by changing weights.  
   - Under concept drift: the V3 stratum shows clear under-prediction from July onward; a residual Partner-Z gap remains after variant control.

4. **Comparison of process variants and DFG / performance-DFG edges**  
   Monthly variant frequencies plus median and p90 times on the critical edges (Order Part → Part Received, diagnosis → Remote Fix, diagnosis → Dispatch).  
   - Under process drift: edge times jump independently of any model score (already observed for parts); variant shares move with policy and onboarding dates.  
   - Under pure model or label artefacts: process edges remain stable while predictive metrics degrade.

5. **Tests of whether the outcome relationship conditional on the same path changed**  
   Restrict to cases that follow identical (or very similar) activity sequences / variants and comparable elapsed time to diagnosis; compare breach rates or logistic coefficients pre- versus post-supplier crisis (and pre- versus post-Partner Z).  
   - Under concept drift: statistically significant rise in conditional breach probability for V3 paths after July.  
   - Under pure covariate or population mix: conditional rates remain stable; only the marginal distribution changes.

6. **Population re-weighting or transport-style checks for Partner Z**  
   Re-weight non-Z cases to match Z’s observed variant and common-feature distribution (or use inverse-propensity / matching); compare predicted versus actual breach. Alternatively train on non-Z and evaluate on matched Z.  
   - Under pure population/mix shift: calibration recovers after re-weighting.  
   - Under missing features or true residual concept difference (rural distance, device age): a gap remains → new features or a segment model are required.

7. **Feature missingness and lineage audits**  
   Trace every feature’s computation graph for April cases: which prefixes used the old versus new activity name, which used pre-patch caches, which contained duplicates. Flag corrupted prefixes and correlate the flag with absolute prediction error.  
   - Under pipeline failure: high fraction of April prefixes are corrupted and the corruption flag strongly predicts large errors.  
   - Under real drift: error remains elevated even on the clean subset.

These tests are designed so that each pair of competing hypotheses produces distinguishable observable outcomes.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Immediate containment (current)**  
- Quarantine all April (and residual May) prefixes until full label normalisation, duplicate removal and feature recompute are verified.  
- Publish every metric with its label-maturity percentage and a “provisional” flag whenever maturity < 95 %.  
- Suppress any automatic retraining or global recalibration trigger while data-quality or maturity alarms are active.  
- Raise an operational (non-ML) alert on the Order Part → Part Received edge so that supplier management can act independently of the model.

**Short-term recalibration**  
- Once maturity ≥ 95 % and data-quality gates are green, fit stratified isotonic or Platt calibrators on recent mature data, at minimum separating V3 from V1/V2.  
- June data are usable after confirming within-variant stability.  
- Do not calibrate on October or on any cohort whose maturity is still rising; that would embed the censoring bias.  
- Segment-specific calibrators are already justified by the different base rates and the recent V3 concept shift.

**Feature repair**  
- Permanently fix the activity-label and duplicate-handling logic in the production pipeline.  
- Ingest the previously missing Partner-Z covariates (travel distance, device age, rural flag) as soon as they are available.  
- If a real-time or short-horizon supplier-delay signal can be obtained at diagnosis time, add it as an explicit feature.

**Full retraining**  
- Unsafe for training targets until resolved: April (pipeline corruption), October (and any month still < 90 % mature).  
- Safe once cleaned and mature: January–March baseline plus post-May months that have reached full maturity.  
- Prefer a rolling or time-aware training window that includes post-supplier-change mature data, or a hierarchical / mixture model with explicit variant interactions.  
- Variant-specific or Partner-Z segment models are justified: V2 remains short and stable, V3 has undergone a clear conditional-outcome shift, and Partner Z brings both a new mix and previously unseen covariates. A single global model will continue to under-predict the drifted V3 segment.  
- Retrain only after maturity curves have stabilised and either the process edge has recovered or the new features capture the changed regime. Global recalibration alone is insufficient for the August–September under-prediction on V3.

Decision rule: if the parts edge recovers and stratified metrics return to baseline, light stratified recalibration may suffice. If the new population and missing features persist, expanded-feature retraining (or a segment model) is required. Never retrain solely on the distorted observed labels of low-maturity months.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

Hierarchical monitors with explicit alert dependencies:

1. **Data-quality & pipeline monitors** (highest priority)  
   Unknown-activity share, missing-feature rate, activity-label consistency (old vs new diagnosis name), duplicate-event rate, feature-lineage completeness.  
   Alarm → quarantine affected prefixes, trigger recompute/back-fill, and suppress all predictive and recalibration alerts until resolved.

2. **Label-maturity & censoring monitors**  
   Percentage of labels available by case-start vintage, by variant and by source; median label delay; outcome-API backlog size; maturity curves (observed breach vs time since cutoff).  
   Alarm (maturity < 90 % or delay spike) → mark all metrics “provisional” and block any automatic retraining or recalibration.

3. **Process-variant & performance-DFG monitors**  
   Monthly variant distribution (V1/V2/V3/unmapped); median and p90 times on key edges (especially Order Part → Part Received and diagnosis → Remote Fix).  
   Alarm on edge-time drift → route to operations for process investigation before any model action is considered.

4. **Population monitors**  
   Partner/source mix, proxies for rural share or travel distance, device-age distribution.  
   Alarm → force stratified evaluation and transport checks before global model decisions.

5. **Predictive & calibration monitors** (enabled only when data-quality and maturity gates are green)  
   Overall and stratified (variant × source × month) AUROC, Brier, ECE, mean predicted vs observed; reliability diagrams per segment.  
   Alarm only on clean, mature data.

6. **Intervention / deployment monitors**  
   When model scores influence dispatch priority or other actions, track intervention rates and realised outcome lift; feed residual concept-change signals back into the monitoring stack.

Alert dependency graph: data-quality or pipeline alarm freezes predictive monitors and blocks retrain; label-maturity alarm marks metrics provisional and blocks retrain/recal; process-DFG alarm forces root-cause process work first; population alarm forces stratification before any global action. This prevents a pure label-delay signal from triggering unnecessary retraining.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

def monthly_process_evidence(csv_path: str, report_months: list[tuple[str, str]]):
    """Return auditable monthly process summaries (no model fitting)."""
    df = pd.read_csv(csv_path)

    # Normalise renamed diagnosis labels before any feature or DFG work
    df["concept:name"] = df["concept:name"].replace({
        "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
    })

    # Duplicate-handling: keep first occurrence per (case, activity, timestamp)
    df = df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"])
    df = df.drop_duplicates(
        subset=["case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"],
        keep="first"
    )

    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
    )

    summaries = {}
    for start, end in report_months:
        # Event-calendar cohort (events that fall inside the window)
        log_cal = pm4py.filter_time_range(df, start, end, mode="events")
        # Case-start cohort (cases whose start timestamp falls inside the window)
        log_start = pm4py.filter_time_range(df, start, end, mode="traces_contained")

        dfg_cal, start_act_cal, end_act_cal = pm4py.discover_dfg(log_cal)
        perf_dfg_cal, start_perf, end_perf = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log_cal)
        variants_cal = pm4py.get_variants(log_cal)

        # Example temporal-feature extraction for lineage audit (optional)
        # temp_feats = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(...)

        # Performance DFG values are typically (mean, ...) tuples; extract median/mean safely
        parts_edge = perf_dfg_cal.get(("Order Part", "Part Received"))
        remote_edge = perf_dfg_cal.get(("Initial Diagnosis Completed", "Remote Fix"))

        summaries[start] = {
            "n_cases_calendar": len(log_cal["case:concept:name"].unique()) if len(log_cal) else 0,
            "n_cases_start_cohort": len(log_start["case:concept:name"].unique()) if len(log_start) else 0,
            "variant_counts": {str(k): int(v) for k, v in variants_cal.items()},
            "key_edge_mean_s": {
                "OrderPart_to_PartReceived": parts_edge[0] if parts_edge else None,
                "Diagnosis_to_RemoteFix": remote_edge[0] if remote_edge else None,
            },
            "note": "Calendar cohort counts events in window; start cohort counts cases that began in window (different interpretation for open cases)."
        }
    return summaries
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

1. **Retraining on immature or censored labels (especially October or late September)**  
   The model would learn the artificially low breach rate produced by still-open long-path cases. Once those labels mature, performance collapses and the model systematically under-predicts the true (still elevated) risk.  
   Mitigation: enforce a hard maturity threshold (≥ 95 % or fixed-vintage only) before any training or recalibration set is accepted; keep a provisional-metric flag; back-test every candidate model with delayed labels.

2. **Calibrating or “fixing” around a pipeline bug (April residual)**  
   A global recalibrator would absorb the April prediction inflation and UNKNOWN-activity artefacts into a new mapping. Subsequent clean data would then be mis-calibrated in the opposite direction, or engineering effort would be wasted on features that were simply never computed.  
   Mitigation: quarantine April–May data until full lineage recompute and duplicate removal are verified; never recalibrate on known-corrupted prefixes; run the feature-completeness audit first.

3. **Ignoring genuine supplier-driven process + concept drift (July–September V3)**  
   Treating the rise in breach solely as population or calibration drift and only re-weighting or recalibrating leaves the model under-predicting risk for parts-dependent cases. Dispatch priority remains wrong, SLAs continue to be missed, and customer impact persists while the external delay is unaddressed.  
   Mitigation: always pair predictive monitors with performance-DFG edge alerts; when the parts edge spikes, force V3-stratified evaluation and consider temporary V3-specific thresholds or manual overrides until the supplier recovers or the model is retrained on recent mature V3 labels (ideally with an explicit delay feature).

4. **Attributing Partner Z’s population shift to “unfair model behaviour” without stratified evidence**  
   Higher raw breach rates for Partner Z could be misread as bias, prompting forced equalisation or down-weighting that harms rural customers who actually need higher-risk flags, or diverting effort into fairness interventions instead of adding the missing rural-distance and device-age features.  
   Mitigation: require stratified (variant × source) metrics and transport/re-weighting tests before any fairness claim; if a residual gap remains after matching on common features and variant, add the missing covariates or train a Partner-Z / rural-specific head; document that higher V3 routing and slower label maturity already explain a large fraction of the raw gap.

5. **Treating the June V2 mix change as concept drift and retraining prematurely**  
   An unnecessary full retrain on a simple policy-driven volume shift that the existing model already discriminates well introduces version churn and instability for no gain.  
   Mitigation: require within-variant stability checks; if AUROC and calibration remain intact inside V2 and the overall change is explained by mix weights, prefer light recalibration or no action.

The monitoring architecture’s data-quality and maturity gates prevent most of these failure modes from automatically escalating into harmful model updates.
