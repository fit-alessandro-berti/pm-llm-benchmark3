## 1. Feasibility model

```
Register < Triage
Triage < IdentityCheck
Triage < DocumentCheck                      (if originals NOT required: independent of IdentityCheck)
OriginalsReceipt < DocumentCheck             (if originals required)
IdentityCheck < Assessment
DocumentCheck < Assessment
[HighRisk OR HighValue] => Assessment(latest) < ComplianceReview(final) < Decision
[NOT HighRisk AND NOT HighValue] => Assessment(latest) < Decision
Decision < Notify
RequestMoreInfo <- {Assessment, ComplianceReview}
RequestMoreInfo < DocumentCheck(new) < Assessment(new)
[HighRisk OR HighValue] => Assessment(new) < ComplianceReview(new-or-reaffirmed) < Decision
PreliminaryComplianceScreen  {IdentityCheck, DocumentCheck}   (may overlap, does not satisfy final CR)
EscalationAdvice  {IdentityCheck, DocumentCheck, Assessment}  (advisory, non-blocking)
Person(FinalAssessment)  Person(FinalComplianceReview)
```

```text
CONDITIONAL / DECLARATIVE CONSTRAINTS
- R1: A resource cannot occupy two service intervals concurrently (hard, all cases).
- R2: Queue-length figures are forecasts sampled at a point in time; actual wait may deviate ±30-50%.
- R3: C1 is the sole certified compliance reviewer this window  any reservation of C1 time is a zero-sum allocation against other queued cases.
- R4: Preliminary compliance work (screen) can reduce final CR duration (1-3h) but cannot be substituted for it (Rule 10).
- R5: D1 is faster on complex/corporate docs; D2 is slower but generalist — substituting resources changes expected duration and rework probability, not control-flow legality.
- R6: External Request-More-Info intervals are exogenous (8-36h, worse over weekends) and not compressible by internal policy.
- R7: Removing a scheduled RequestMoreInfo loop (K-337) is a *decision-policy* change: it does not just save time, it forces a decision under residual uncertainty, altering compliance defensibility and appeal risk (Rule 6 permits, does not mandate, RFI; the intervention forbids a second one).
- R8: True concurrency (K-411) is only legal because Rule 3 conditions apply (no originals required); if originals were required, Identity and Document Check could not be parallelized this way.
```

**Which interventions change what:**
- **K-104** — adds a new *routing* branch (preliminary screen task) plus a *timing* effect (C1 slot reservation); final decision policy is unchanged (Rule 10 respected).
- **K-219** — pure *routing* substitution (D1D2); timing and quality both shift as a side effect, decision policy unchanged.
- **K-337** — a genuine *decision-policy* change: an admissible RFI loop is prohibited, forcing decision on incomplete evidence.
- **K-411** — a *timing/concurrency* correction: removes an artificial serialization (system/case-lock artifact), does not change control-flow legality (Rule 3 already permitted concurrency) or decision policy.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104
**Factual mechanism:** The sanctions ambiguity is noticed at Triage but not escalated; it resurfaces at Assessment (Tue 15:10-19:10), triggering ad hoc Escalation Advice (Tue 19:20-21:20) and only then a full-from-scratch Compliance Review (Wed 13:10-17:40) after a long C1 queue wait.

**Minimal intervention:** At Triage, route a preliminary packet to S1 and reserve a provisional C1 slot. Preliminary compliance screening runs concurrently with Identity/Document checks; the *final* Compliance Review still must occur after the *final* Assessment (Rule 10 is respected, not bypassed).

**Temporal propagation:** Because C1 receives partial evidence early, some reading/context-building happens off the critical path (during the ~24-36h that Identity/Document checks are running). When Assessment 1 finishes, C1 does not start "cold" — the final Compliance Review shrinks by roughly the 1-3h "preliminary savings" band. However, reserving a C1 slot early is a real allocation against C1's already-congested queue (18-20h at snapshot times, with two urgent compliance cases just added Monday 16:00) — so while K-104 finishes sooner, the reservation itself may delay unrelated urgent cases that would otherwise have been served first.

**Process-feasibility check:** Legal under Rules 5, 6, 10: preliminary screen  final review; final review still follows final Assessment; four-eyes still enforceable if A1 (Assessment)  C1 (Compliance) — satisfied since A1 and C1 are different people.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best — packet is largely correct, final CR compresses to ~2h, decision issued midday Wednesday instead of evening. Base — packet is partially useful, final CR ~3-4h, decision Wed afternoon. Worst — packet is materially incomplete/misleading, C1 must partly redo work, final CR takes as long as factual (or longer due to reconciling conflicting drafts), while other urgent cases were still delayed by the reservation — a net negative-sum outcome.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If sanctions-ambiguity details typically change substantially between Triage and Assessment (i.e., the preliminary packet is usually stale/wrong), the "savings" assumption fails and the reserved slot becomes pure overhead — reversing the conclusion that this intervention is net-beneficial even locally.

### K-219
**Factual mechanism:** Handwritten "standard" documents are deliberately routed to D1 (specialist judgment) despite a long D1 queue; originals arrive Mon 17:30 but Document Check does not start until Tue 09:30 because D1 is occupied.

**Minimal intervention:** As soon as originals arrive, assign Document Check to D2 rather than waiting for D1.

**Temporal propagation:** D2's queue (5h at Mon 16:00 snapshot) clears around 21:00 Monday, so D2 can start ~21:00-Mon night versus D1's 09:30 Tue start — roughly 8-12h earlier readiness. Document Check duration itself is longer on D2 (4-8h vs D1's 3-6h) and handwriting raises clarification risk. If clean, the check finishes by early Tuesday morning, well before the factual 13:30 Tuesday completion, giving Assessment (A2) a chance to start earlier — but only if A2 is actually free; A2's own queue (6h at Tue 08:00) may absorb some of that gained slack, so the propagated benefit to Assessment start-time is partial, not one-for-one.

**Process-feasibility check:** Legal — originals-required sequencing (Rule 4) preserved (Originals Receipt still precedes Document Check); Identity Check unaffected/independent.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best — D2 reads handwriting correctly, no clarification needed, Assessment and Decision advance by ~12-24h. Base — minor uncertainty causes short internal review but no formal RFI; net gain ~4-10h. Worst — D2 requests clarification, triggering a new Document Check + Assessment cycle (Rule 7), erasing the gain and potentially finishing later than the factual Wed 08:30 decision.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Empirical clarification-rate data showing D2 requests info on >30-40% of handwritten "standard" documents would flip this from a net-positive routing change to a net-negative one, since rework cost (8-36h external turnaround) dominates any startup-time savings.

### K-337
**Factual mechanism:** Two sequential Request-More-Info loops (external, slow/inconsistent supplier) extend the case by roughly 4-5 calendar days, including a weekend delay, before evidence is finally accepted and Compliance Review/Decision proceed.

**Minimal intervention:** After Assessment 2, forbid a second RFI loop; require Compliance Review and Decision using currently available evidence (approve-with-conditions or reject — not silent acceptance).

**Temporal propagation:** Removing RFI2 eliminates both the external wait (Fri 10:30-Mon 09:00) and Document Check 3/Assessment 3 (Mon-Tue, ~5h of internal work). Compliance Review now attaches directly after Assessment 2 (Fri ~10:30), subject to C1's queue at that time (backlog was "clearing" through the week per snapshots) — plausibly starting Friday afternoon and completing same day or, if C1 is unavailable, rolling into the next business day. This removes ~2-4 business days versus the factual timeline.

**Process-feasibility check:** Rules 5, 6, 8 still satisfied (Compliance Review after latest Assessment, Decision after Compliance Review, Notify after Decision); four-eyes preserved if C1  A1 for the final pair. Rule 7's *option* for RFI is not violated by *not exercising* it, but the intervention explicitly narrows discretion — this is a policy constraint, not a control-flow violation.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best — the single remaining inconsistency is minor; C1/M1 approve with added conditions (e.g., additional monitoring), losing little defensibility, saving ~3 days. Base — approval with materially tighter conditions, higher documented residual risk, saving ~2-3 days but raising future audit/appeal exposure. Worst — case is rejected or approved incorrectly, generating appeal, reputational, or loss-exposure costs that exceed the value of 2-3 days saved.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If historical data show that the "one remaining inconsistency" pattern (as in Assessment 2's note) is resolved favorably in the second RFI round more than ~70% of the time with no new issues, then suppressing RFI2 systematically trades a small, well-characterized risk for a real compliance/quality loss — reversing the case for adopting this intervention broadly (though it may still be justified in truly time-critical single cases).

### K-411
**Factual mechanism:** A legacy system limitation created only the Identity Check task at Triage; the Document Check task was generated only after Identity Check completed, artificially serializing two logically independent checks (no originals required, so Rule 3 concurrency should apply).

**Minimal intervention:** Create both verification tasks at Triage and use a controlled workaround so I2 and D2 can genuinely run in parallel, with real-time (not batch) timestamps.

**Temporal propagation:** D2's queue (4h at Tue 08:00 snapshot) would allow a start around 12:00 Tuesday rather than 15:00 — roughly 3h earlier start, finishing perhaps 15:00-20:00 Tuesday instead of 19:30. Identity Check is essentially unaffected (I2 availability similar). The case becomes "ready" for Assessment earlier, but Assessment (A2) has its own queue (5-6h per snapshots) that is largely independent of this case's readiness — so the net downstream gain is smaller than the upstream gain (partial pass-through, not full).

**Process-feasibility check:** Fully legal — Rule 3 explicitly allows concurrency when no originals are required; the intervention corrects an implementation artifact rather than violating any rule. Logging integrity (real-time timestamps) is essential to avoid *appearing* concurrent without actually being concurrent.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best — D2 truly starts ~3h earlier and A2 has a lull, so Decision advances ~4-6h. Base — D2 starts earlier but A2 queue absorbs the slack; net Decision timing shifts by 1-3h. Worst — the workaround is imperfectly logged (a resource records overlapping intervals inaccurately), creating an audit/process-integrity concern with no material time benefit, or the earlier-ready case lands in a newly congested Assessment queue caused by other parallelized cases, offsetting the gain.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If Assessment queue congestion is driven primarily by inflow timing rather than total volume, then earlier readiness across many cases (rollout scenario) could compress the Assessment queue into a worse peak, reversing the presumed benefit at scale even though it is essentially costless for a single case.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 (prelim CR + reserved C1 slot) | +partial C1 hours earlier, -final C1 hours later (net roughly neutral to slightly positive per case) | K-104's own final CR queue-wait | C1 queue for *other* urgent cases (reservation displaces FIFO order) | Two urgent compliance cases noted Mon 16:00 | Ambiguous  mildly negative if scaled, since C1 is a hard single-resource bottleneck | C1 is the only certified reviewer — any reservation is zero-sum, not additive capacity |
| K-219 (D2 substitution) | +D2 hours, -D1 hours (case-level) | D1 queue (frees up faster-per-hour capacity for complex corporate docs) | D2 queue (slower per document, plus rework risk) | Other standard cases queued behind D2 | Ambiguous  positive if clarification rate low, negative if high | Rework probability is a threshold effect: below some handwriting-difficulty level, benefits dominate; above it, costs dominate sharply |
| K-337 (suppress RFI2) | -external wait, -1 Document Check, -1 Assessment cycle | Assessment/Document queues (fewer repeat visits) | Compliance/appeal "queue" (post-decision disputes) — not visible in these snapshots | Future cases needing appeal/rework handling | Negative on quality/compliance dimension even if positive on throughput | Removing a loop changes decision *distribution*, not just latency — a discrete regime change, not a linear speed-up |
| K-411 (true concurrency) | Minimal net change; converts sequential D2 wait into earlier start | Document queue effective wait for this case | Assessment queue (earlier arrivals bunch up) | Other cases queued at A2 around the new earlier arrival time | Positive locally, mildly negative in aggregate if Assessment is the true bottleneck | Amdahl-type effect: speeding up a non-bottleneck stage (Document Check) shows in end-to-end time only up to the slack available at the bottleneck (Assessment) |

**100-case weekly rollout (300 words):**

Applying these four interventions at scale exposes very different interference profiles. K-104's reservation of C1 time is the most dangerous to scale: C1 is a single-resource monopoly bottleneck (Rules table, only certified reviewer). Reserving provisional slots for 100 cases would not create capacity — it would reorder a fixed queue, systematically privileging cases with early (possibly wrong) escalation flags over already-waiting urgent cases, plausibly lengthening the tail of the C1 queue and increasing missed SLA cases, even if average preliminary-savings partially offset per-case final-review time. K-219's D1D2 substitution rollout depends entirely on the empirical clarification/rework rate on handwritten documents: at scale, moving many complex/handwritten cases to D2 shifts congestion from D1 to D2, and if even 20-30% require a clarification round, the induced rework loops (Document Check + Assessment repeats) could push both D2 and Assessment queues into sustained backlog, especially compounding with weekend/external delays if clarification requests are routed externally. K-337's suppression of RFI2, if applied broadly, converts what is currently absorbed as "time cost" into a "quality/compliance cost" distributed across the whole caseload — some fraction of the ~100 cases will be decided on incomplete evidence that would otherwise have resolved favorably, raising aggregate appeal and loss-exposure risk in a way not visible in cycle-time metrics; it also disproportionately affects cases whose external suppliers are slow (correlated with the very cases now denied a second RFI). K-411's concurrency fix is the most benign at scale for compliance purposes, but its main systemic risk is twofold: (1) many cases becoming Assessment-ready earlier could produce synchronized arrival bursts at A1/A2, shifting (not eliminating) the bottleneck to Assessment and partially erasing the individual-case gain (Amdahl-style diminishing returns); (2) poorly logged workarounds, replicated 100 times, could generate systematic timestamp artifacts that undermine audit trails and downstream process-mining validity — an interference effect on *measurement*, not just throughput.

|---|---|---|

| assignment rule | Randomize at Triage: legacy sequential task creation vs. dual concurrent task creation (documented workaround) | Randomize at Originals Receipt: assign D1 (control) vs. D2 (treatment) when both have some queue capacity |
| sample/support logic | Stratify by day-of-week and Assessment-queue load at Triage time (per snapshot regime) to detect bottleneck-migration effects | Stratify by document complexity indicator (handwriting/legibility flag) and by D1/D2 queue length at originals-arrival time |
| primary outcome | End-to-end cycle time (RegisterNotify) | End-to-end cycle time (RegisterNotify) |
| process-path outcome | Actual concurrency achieved (interval overlap in timestamps) vs. nominal concurrency | Frequency and stage of any Request More Info / rework loop triggered post-D2 |
| quality outcome | Decision consistency vs. control (no expected change; monitor for anomalies) | Decision accuracy/appeal rate; document-accuracy audit sample comparing D1 vs D2 outcomes |
| queue-spillover outcome | Downstream Assessment queue wait-time distribution, especially peak bunching | D1 queue relief measured against D2 queue growth; downstream Assessment queue impact |

## 7. Final synthesis

Among the four interventions, **K-411 (removing artificial serialization via true concurrency)** is the most robust: it is legal under the existing rules in every plausible scenario where originals are not required, its downside is bounded (logging integrity, minor queue re-timing), and it is fully reversible and easy to pilot with clean instrumentation. Its main limitation is that gains are capped by whatever slack exists at the Assessment bottleneck — a real but modest and well-understood nonlinearity.

**K-337 (suppressing the second Request-More-Info loop)** is the most dangerous despite offering the largest apparent time savings. It is not a timing intervention at all — it is a decision-policy change that forces conclusions on incomplete evidence. Its risk (appeal exposure, compliance defensibility, wrongful approvals/rejections) is slow-moving, hard to observe in short pilots, and not reversible once decisions are issued. Speed gains here are precisely the kind of "efficiency" that trades hidden tail risk for average-case throughput.

The **case-level counterfactual is least transportable to a system-wide policy for K-104**: locally, reserving C1 time and running a preliminary screen looks purely beneficial, but at scale it is a zero-sum reallocation of the single scarce compliance resource. What helps one flagged case necessarily displaces another queued case — a fact invisible when analyzing K-104 in isolation but decisive at the 100-case level.

The most valuable additional data would be: (1) actual resource-occupancy logs (not just queue-length snapshots) to distinguish true idle capacity from forecast noise; (2) historical RFI-resolution outcomes (how often unresolved discrepancies self-resolve favorably) to calibrate K-337's real risk; and (3) document-type-specific rework rates by resource (D1 vs D2) to calibrate K-219's threshold behavior.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. Queue-length snapshots approximate true resource availability within ±30-50%. — *temporal*
2. C1 is a strict single-unit bottleneck with no substitute capacity this window. — *resource*
3. Preliminary compliance screening genuinely transfers partial knowledge to the final reviewer without needing full rework. — *behavioral*
4. D2's clarification-request probability on handwritten documents is materially higher than D1's. — *quality*
5. Removing a Request-More-Info loop changes the decision distribution (not just timing) because some fraction of unresolved discrepancies would have resolved favorably. — *structural*
6. Rule 3 concurrency (no originals required) is sufficient legal basis for true parallel Identity/Document execution without additional system constraints beyond the noted case-lock artifact. — *structural*
7. Earlier stage completion does not guarantee earlier downstream stage start, because downstream resources (Assessment, Compliance) have independent queues. — *interference*
8. Workarounds for concurrency can be logged with real-time timestamps without introducing false-concurrency artifacts, given adequate controls. — *resource*
