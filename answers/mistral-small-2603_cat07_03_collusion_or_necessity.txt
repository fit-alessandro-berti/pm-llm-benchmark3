# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

**Internal Audit Observation: Control Exceptions in Procurement-to-Payment Process**

Our review of the procurement-to-payment event log identified several control exceptions and data-quality issues that warrant targeted follow-up, particularly in high-risk subgroups. The most concerning pattern involves the Buyer_Carla–Approver_Gita pairing, where 42 cases violated the four-eyes principle, 84 cases showed unusually fast approvals (median 8 minutes), and 36 cases exhibited repeated subcontracting loops. While these may reflect legitimate emergency handling for medical supplies, the concentration of exceptions, shared-account usage for "Approver_Gita," and batch-entered retrospective justifications raise questions about control effectiveness.

A second high-risk area involves VendorMaster_Lena and AP_Kim, where 9 four-eyes violations and 16 fast approvals (median 17 minutes) were concentrated in vendor family V-77x. Manual source-system entries (83%) and tight correction loops suggest potential breakdowns in segregation of duties during a banking platform migration. Data-quality issues—including mapping errors in legacy ERP records and shared service accounts—complicate root-cause analysis.

We recommend prioritizing 170 cases for detailed review: all Buyer_Carla–Approver_Gita emergency cases, VendorMaster_Lena–AP_Kim transactions, and end-of-quarter Temp_Nina–Buyer_Carla entries. Key documents to inspect include emergency override justifications, vendor change requests, access logs for shared accounts, and source-system mapping tables. The audit team should also evaluate whether emergency protocols are appropriately documented and whether role designs inadvertently enable control circumvention. Data-quality limitations, such as incomplete vendor migration tickets and ERP mapping errors, should be addressed to restore confidence in the event log’s integrity.
