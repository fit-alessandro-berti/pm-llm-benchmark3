# 2. Integrated explanation

**The two curves have different shapes because they are driven by different mixtures of mechanisms.** Service time falls monotonically (18.8  10.8 min); errors are U-shaped (3.4%  2.5%  7.9%). A single mechanism cannot generate both.

**Why service time falls — three forces pushing the same way (a consilient artifact).**
1. *Cold-start / setup*: the first events of every shift carry backlog context loading, system warm-up, handover reading. This recurs daily even for seniors, so it is **task-set activation, not durable learning**.
2. *Easier case-mix*: complexity falls 0.72  0.43 and high-priority share 41%  19%. Using the WIP table as a rough slope ( 9.7 min per complexity unit at low WIP), the drop from bin 1–5 to bin 16–30 (complexity 0.18) plausibly accounts for **~1.5–2 of the 5.2-minute fall** — i.e. roughly a third of the "learning curve" is composition, not skill.
3. *Pressure and batching*: from bin 31–50 to 76+, complexity falls only 0.07 ( 0.5 min of the 2.1-minute fall), while queue goes 31  88. Most of that late speed-up is **WIP-driven truncation plus batch amortisation** (Junior_H/I's 9–18-event Document Review runs), not learning. Bot_Risk's zero-duration events may further deflate the mean if bot share rises late.

**Why errors fall then rise.** Early errors reflect *hard cases plus cold start* (48–56% complex in Early/Night first hours). The middle of the shift is the genuine steady state: moderate queue (31–33), warm operators, moderate difficulty. Late errors rise **even though cases are easiest**, which rules out case-mix as the explanation for the late rise, and the WIP table shows why: at *fixed* complexity band, errors roughly triple from low to high WIP (2.15.8; 4.210.9).

**The critical discriminating fact: speed falls and errors rise *only under pressure*.** Under low WIP, late-shift error rates are flat or better (Seniors 2.2 vs 2.1; Mid 2.9 vs 2.8; Juniors 4.4 vs 5.1). If fatigue were the dominant cause, quality would degrade late regardless of queue. It does not. What the data support is a **conditional model**: elapsed time and circadian phase lower the *reserve* of attention; queue pressure *spends* it. Fatigue is a modifier of the pressure effect, not an independent main effect — hence the strong experience interaction (high-WIP errors: Seniors 4.9%, Mid 6.8%, Juniors 12.4%, Night 8.2%). Juniors have the least spare capacity, so pressure converts into shortcuts fastest; seniors absorb it.

**Circadian effects show up as a *different* late-shift phenotype, not the same one.** Day's last two hours (15:00–17:00, queue 75) are *fast and wrong* (10.9 min, 7.5%). Night's last two hours (04:00–06:00, circadian nadir, queue 55) are *slow and moderately wrong* (15.2 min, 5.1%). Same elapsed time, opposite speed signature: pressure dominates in Day, alertness dominates at night. Elapsed-time-only models cannot reproduce this.

**Two measurement mechanisms inflate the apparent late-shift collapse — and one inflates the apparent late-shift speed.**
- *Detection-time bias*: Quality Check is deferred to shift end, so errors *committed* mid-shift are *recorded* in the last 45 minutes. Unless errors are attributed to the originating decision event, the late-shift error rate is partly an ascertainment artifact. Rework-within-7-days is also right-censored for recent cases.
- *Cost shifting*: 10.8-minute events with 13.7% rework may not be cheap at all. Work is conserved and exported downstream — event service time falls while **case-level total effort (touch + rework) may be flat or higher**.
- *Batching*: short measured durations inside a batch reflect one amortised setup and possibly overlapping work, not less effort per case.

**Where the damage actually concentrates (volume matters).** Using the low-WIP band as reference, excess errors are approximately: high-WIP/low-complexity 6200 × (5.82.1)%  **230**; high-WIP/high-complexity 1800 × (10.94.2)%  **120**; medium-WIP cells  125. So ~74% of excess errors sit in high-WIP cells, and **about two-thirds of them are on *low*-complexity cases**. The high-complexity/high-WIP cell is the worst *per-case* risk (5.2× baseline) but not the largest *absolute* burden. Any intervention that protects only complex cases misses most of the harm.

**The feedback loop that makes this structural, not behavioural.** Day arrivals outpace finish rate late (queue 3875)  Late inherits 70  shortcuts and deferred QC  rework and un-checked cases return to the queue  Early inherits 61 with 48% complex carryover  Early's first hour is hard and slow  the cycle repeats. Queue pressure at the end of a shift is largely **produced by arrival profiles, staffing and handover policy**, i.e. by management, and errors are the system's output, not a worker's disposition.

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

**Worker-level conclusions from this log are unsafe without adjustment.**

- **Selection bias is the assignment rule, not the worker.** Seniors deliberately take complex cases early after weekend/night backlog; juniors receive complex early cases *and* the late high-WIP load. Raw comparisons therefore measure *what people were given*, not *how well they worked*. Junior_H/I's short durations are a batching signature; Night_L/M's slow times reflect urgent exceptions and the circadian nadir. Any ranking must be standardised to a common complexity/priority/WIP/activity/shift mix, with shrinkage and interval estimates — Night staff have too few events for stable individual rates.
- **Measurement error is systematic, not random.** `start_timestamp`-derived durations are wall-clock, not effort; overlapping intervals, batch amortisation and Bot_Risk's zero-duration events all bias "service time" downward exactly where pressure is highest. `error_flag` records *detection*, and Quality Check deferral moves detection to the shift boundary — so late-shift workers are penalised for a *scheduling policy*. Rework-within-7-days is right-censored for recent cases and for cases handed to another shift. `case_complexity_score` may be noisy or scored after outcomes (leakage). Verify provenance before using any of it evaluatively.
- **Queue pressure is created by management.** Day's arrival rate exceeds finish rate late; Late inherits 70; Early inherits 61 with 48% complex carryover. High-WIP exposure is an *assignment* by the staffing and handover system. Attributing its error consequences to individuals is a category error — and a predictable way to generate concealment rather than improvement.
- **Automation quality is a shared responsibility.** Bot_Risk misclassifies more under queue pressure, but the *correction* is logged against a human. Humans must not be debited for upstream automation defects, nor rewarded for skipping review. Track bot-origin error propagation and hidden human rework minutes explicitly.
- **Punitive use of error rates predictably backfires (Goodhart).** Expect under-reporting, deferral of Quality Checks, cherry-picking easy cases, parking cases below the WIP threshold, and inflated closure counts with higher escaped defects. Use adjusted metrics for **staffing, routing, coaching and system redesign**, with a just-culture distinction between system-induced error, at-risk behaviour and (rare) recklessness.
- **Governance.** Involve worker representatives; publish indicator definitions and their known biases; give individuals access to their own adjusted data and a right of reply; minimise identifiable behavioural data, aggregate for management views, and set an explicit policy that these indicators are **not** used for individual discipline or pay. Do not treat "faster" as "better" without a paired quality and escaped-defect measure.

**Cadence & governance:** indicators 3, 4, 11, 12 near-real-time (operational alerts); 1, 2, 5, 6, 8, 9, 13, 14 daily; 7, 10, 15 weekly with reviewer sign-off. Every alert must name an **action owner** and an expected response. Every published rate carries its standardisation basis, denominator, follow-up completeness and CI. Explicit anti-gaming monitors: cases parked just below WIP 70, QC deferrals, closure without QC, reassignment spikes before shift end. No indicator on this dashboard is authorised for individual punitive use.
