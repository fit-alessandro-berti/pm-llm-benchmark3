## 2. Rule Interpretation

**RR1 — Drafter/approver separation.** After alias normalization and reliable service-account attribution, the set of humans who performed `Draft_Change_Request` and the set who performed `Approve_Change` on the same case must be disjoint. The rule applies at every risk level and on emergency cases. A valid delegation does not relax it. Same user ID is sufficient proof of a breach. Two user IDs are a breach once they resolve to one human.

**RR2 — Implementer/verifier separation.** The human set for `Implement_Change` and the human set for `Verify_Implementation` on the same case must be disjoint, using the same normalization. The rules do not require the verifier to hold the Independent Verifier title, and they do not forbid drafter = implementer or approver = verifier. An unattributed `svc_qms` event cannot be treated as a different human. A raw comparison of `u_dm` with `svc_qms` can pass four-eyes while the control objective is unmet.

**RR3 — High-risk approval authority.** If `risk = HIGH` and the RR4 emergency exception is not fully made out, `Approve_Change` must be performed by the person who is QM at that timestamp, or by a delegate whose delegation is valid at that timestamp. Quality Engineer, Independent Verifier, Process Engineer, Shift Supervisor, Maintenance Lead, and Site Director are not QM grants. Validity requires all of: effective interval covers the timestamp; scope covers the case scope and the activity; where the instrument requires absence, the principal is formally absent or unavailable; and the actor is the named delegate, not a further recipient, where subdelegation is prohibited.

**RR4 — Emergency substitution.** A HIGH-risk case with a credible emergency flag and a credible safety rationale may be approved by the person who is IC at that time, or by a valid IC delegate, and then implemented. The exception is justified only when a QM or a valid QM delegate also performs `Retrospective_QM_Review` within two business days. Both legs are required. A later review does not repair an approver who lacked IC authority, and a valid IC approval does not survive a missed or unauthorized review.

Business-day count: the site calendar is Monday–Friday, and this period has no holiday. Saturday 2026-08-15 and Sunday 2026-08-16 are not business days. The clock starts at `Approve_Change`. The deadline is the end of the second following business day, UTC: approval on Tuesday 2026-08-11 gives a deadline of Thursday 2026-08-13 23:59:59 UTC. Friday 2026-08-14 is the third business day. The same CHG-505 result holds if the window is 48 elapsed hours, or two site working days of ordinary shift length. `numpy.busday_count(date(2026,8,11), date(2026,8,14)) == 2` only because the end date is exclusive; that count measures intervening days and does not put a Friday review inside the window.

The case log contains an emergency flag and a cooling-system scope. It does not contain a safety-rationale statement or an incident identifier. Linkage of CHG-505 and CHG-510 to CI-44 is inferred from scope, the emergency flag, and the delegation window. That inference is plausible and is stated as an inference.

**RR5 — Identity evidence.** `svc_qms` is not a human. Conformance requires reliable session evidence tying the click to an authorized human. Badge entry to a room, a personally assigned but shared terminal, and unavailable CCTV are not reliable. Badge plus FIDO at the verifying station is reliable for CHG-509. Aliases of one human are merged before RR1 and RR2.

**RR6 — Suspicion versus proof.** Repeated pairs, reciprocal approvals, shared workstations, fast handovers, and a dense working-together network are indicators for investigation. They are not a finding of collusion and they do not flip an otherwise valid delegated approval into a violation.

**Principal acting during leave.** Leave makes Asha formally unavailable for delegations that require absence. It leaves her QM authority in place. An approval by Asha during leave meets RR3 if the human at the keyboard is Asha. A delegate approval elsewhere on the same leave day still has its absence condition satisfied when the principal’s authenticated window has ended and the leave record continues. The 25-minute VPN on 13 August corroborates one overlapping case; it does not cancel the rest of the leave.

**Later database row.** A QMS insert after the event cannot by itself prove that a delegation already existed. It can be a migration of an older instrument. D-QM-01 is proved for 11 August by the signed PDF of 2026-08-09 18:00, despite the 2026-08-12 09:15 insert. D-QM-02 has a controlled row created before its interval. D-IC-04 remains invalid because the parent forbids subdelegation and the only record is email.

Technical resource conformance means the identifier, interval, and system row line up. Control-objective conformance means the right human, with authority that actually covered that change, performed the act. Those can diverge in either direction.

## 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

The production cell is small. Clara drafts, Diego implements, and Eva verifies most cases. That working-together structure matches the job design. Repeated collaboration inside it is not collusion evidence.

**Clara drafted and Ben approved 18 of 23 HIGH-risk production changes in the prior 60 days.** Asha approved 3, two of them emergencies. No pair-rotation rule exists, and they share a production cell. Benign explanation: a stable drafter and a designated quality reviewer, with the August cluster partly explained by D-QM-01. Adverse explanation: Ben routinely approved HIGH changes without a QM delegation, and the pair’s familiarity became rubber-stamping. The delegation on file lasts five days, so it cannot explain a 60-day concentration. Discriminators: the timestamps of all 18 approvals joined to written delegations; rejection and challenge rates; whether any approval falls outside both Asha’s presence and a signed delegate interval.

**Ben drafted four LOW-risk training changes that Clara approved.** No financial benefit is known. Benign explanation: ordinary cross-coverage of low-risk documents in a two-person cell. Adverse explanation: reciprocal approval so each can move the other’s documents without an independent reviewer. Discriminators: whether Clara ever returned Ben’s drafts, the time-to-approve distribution, and whether any of those four used split aliases the way CHG-508 does. CHG-508 itself is direct self-approval, not one of those four reciprocal approvals.

**Shared workstation on CHG-503.** Benign explanation: the QMS kiosk in the maintenance area was used by a qualified person other than Diego, and the badge only proves that Diego entered the room. Adverse explanation: Diego verified his own implementation through `svc_qms`, which hides RR2 from a resource-ID check. Discriminators: interactive logon identity, a second factor at 12:30, keyboard or screen capture, and anyone else’s badge at that terminal.

**Asha’s VPN during leave, overlapping CHG-504.** Benign explanation: she connected for the one urgent approval she describes, then left. The log contains only one `u_as` event, and it sits inside 09:15–09:40. Adverse explanation: someone else used her QM credentials for that short window. Discriminators: which authenticator asserted, whether the QMS session rode that VPN, and where she physically was.

**Ben’s account during sick leave.** CHG-506 is the denied office-IP approval at 13:00 on 13 August. The retrospective event at 16:00 on 14 August is also inside the sick-leave interval; Ben’s denial, as stated, names the CHG-506 approval. Benign explanation: he worked from the office despite the certificate and his denial is mistaken or incomplete. Adverse explanation: another person at an office workstation used his session or password to complete HIGH-risk quality decisions while Asha was away. Discriminators: building badge, MFA device location, screen recording, and whether the 14 August review came from the same workstation.

**Eva’s changing place in the network.** Through 11 August she verifies as Independent Verifier, which is the designed control, including the two emergency cases. From 12 August she verifies as Quality Engineer, including changes tied to Ben, a peer Quality Engineer. On CHG-507 she approves instead, and Grace verifies. On CHG-509 she approves and, on the strong session evidence, also verifies, while Clara drafts and implements. Benign explanation: after the role change the site kept using the same experienced person to verify, and CHG-507 was a mistaken activity choice. Adverse explanation: approval and verification collapsed onto one person, and the Independent Verifier title was used to bless a HIGH approval she could not give. Discriminators: the work instruction for who may verify after 12 August, and the CHG-507 screen audit showing whether Eva selected the wrong activity.

**D-IC-04 and the Clara–Farid–Diego emergency cluster.** Benign explanation: during CI-44, Farid believed an email could extend his temporary IC authority to the process engineer who was already drafting. Adverse explanation: the subdelegation was used so Clara could approve a change she had just drafted, defeating both RR1 and the IC boundary, with Diego implementing minutes later. Discriminators: the email wording, whether Diego or Farid knew Clara would be the approver, and whether CHG-510’s content was already agreed before the draft. The authority defect is established either way. Intent is not.

**Late QMS insert for D-QM-01.** Benign explanation: a signed instrument from 9 August was loaded in a migration on 12 August. Adverse explanation: the row was created after Ben had already approved, to paper over CHG-501. The earlier signed PDF supports existence before use. Discriminators: PDF signing certificate, migration change ticket, and whether CHG-501’s approval transaction could see the delegation.

Handover concentration after normalization is dominated by Clara → Ben or Clara → Asha or Clara → Farid, then Diego, then Eva. Self-handovers Clara → Clara (CHG-502, CHG-510) and Ben → Ben (CHG-508) are segregation breaches. The dense clique of Clara, Ben, Diego, and Eva is the cell’s ordinary working-together network. Under RR6 it is a monitoring signal, not a collusion finding.

## 6. Control Improvements

Record every delegation in one controlled workflow before its effective start. Store signer, signature time, effective interval, principal, delegate, activity list, scope, risk ceiling, incident identifier, absence requirement, and a hard “subdelegation allowed” flag. Reject email grants and any grant whose parent has subdelegation turned off.

Keep the legal effective interval separate from the database insert time. Migration must load the original signed timestamps. The transaction should refuse a delegate when the insert is later than the event unless a completed migration exception, tied to the signed instrument, is already on the case.

Check principal availability at decision time against the leave and on-call register. Where a delegation requires absence, block the delegate while the principal has an open authenticated session, and record a break-glass reason when the principal acts during leave. Keep the principal’s own authority available.

Resolve aliases to a canonical human at write time, and run four-eyes on that canonical value. Do not allow two active user IDs for one person to complete a controlled pair.

Remove `svc_qms` from approval, verification, and retrospective review, or require step-up FIDO bound to the badge at that workstation for each of those events. Store workstation, source network, authenticator, and session identifier on the event.

Start the two-business-day emergency clock in the system from approval, using the site calendar, and block closure until a scoped QM or valid QM delegate completes the review. Keep RR1 in force on emergency cases so the drafter cannot be the emergency approver.

Rotate the HIGH-risk drafter/approver pair, and report concentration and reciprocal approval as indicators with a threshold. Keep that queue separate from the violation queue so a high pair count is reviewed as a pattern and is not auto-classified as collusion.

Retain the signed PDF, migration ticket, absence status, badge event, FIDO assertion, source IP, and workstation identifier with the case for the retention life of the change record. For shared rooms used in HIGH-risk verification, retain a person-level session record that can stand in for missing CCTV.
