## 1. Analytical assumptions

- **Case notion** is an analytical partition, not a claim about the process’s “true” instances. By default, flattening on type \(T\) creates one case per \(T\)-object and includes only events with a **direct E2O relation** to that object. Thus, Payment produces four cases, while Order produces four much broader cases.
- **Convergence** means that several business entities meet in one execution or object: for example, O2 and O3 converge in e13/P3, and O1–O3 converge in e16/D2. Depending on the selected type, this either copies an event into several cases or collapses several entities into one case. **Divergence** means that one entity fans out into several units or executions, such as O1 splitting into P1/P2 and D1/D2. Flattening can serialize these branches and make them resemble rework.
- Copied shared events are **both evidence and duplication**. They are evidence that the same execution affects several objects, but duplicated rows are not independent executions. Object-involvement frequencies and execution frequencies must therefore be reported separately.
- The baseline performs **no O2O propagation**. The `packs`, `carries`, `bills`, and settlement relations inform interpretation but do not add events to cases. A sensitivity variant may use explicit, directed paths with source-event and path provenance; generic undirected transitive closure is not permitted because it could leak O4-specific information into O3 through their shared P4 context.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

1. **Order flattening—O1’s split can resemble rework.** O1 contains e11/e12, e15/e16, and e23/e24 for its two packages and deliveries. Its trace therefore generates Pack Package, Dispatch, and Confirm Delivery self-loops. These edges are **informative** about divergence into P1/P2 and D1/D2, but **harmful** if interpreted as repeated attempts at the same task. First- versus last-confirmation performance also answers different questions.

2. **Cross-order package P3—Order and Item flattening.** The single execution e13 is copied into O2/O3 and into I21/I31. Summing either flattened log charges one packing execution twice; subsequent e16/e24 copies amplify the shared-history effect. This is harmful for operational workload counts but informative about synchronization. Package flattening avoids copying e13, yet its single P3 case hides the fact that two orders supplied the package.

3. **Cross-order package P4—shared branch is not a shared whole lifecycle.** e14 is copied into O3/O4 and I32/I41, while e17/e27 repeat that sharing downstream. Nevertheless, O3 also has the P3/D2 branch, whereas O4 does not. In Package flattening, P4 has the same three-activity shape as P1–P3. Treating the shared Pack–Dispatch–Confirm pattern as evidence that O3 and O4 are equivalent orders would be harmful; treating it as evidence of a joint physical branch is informative.

4. **Consolidated delivery D2—simultaneous inflation and concealment.** Order flattening copies e16/e24 into O1, O2, and O3; Package flattening copies them into P2/P3. Thus one dispatch and one confirmation become several involvements. Conversely, Delivery flattening gives D2 the same two-event shape as D1/D3 and hides its exceptional load. The 11:05–16:00 interval is valid for D2, but repeatedly treating it as independent order or package performance would bias aggregate statistics.

5. **Consolidated invoice V2—Order and Invoice flattening.** e19, e22, and e25 are each copied into O2 and O3. A global Order-log sum therefore counts V2’s issue twice, and each order trace appears to contain the complete shared settlement history. Invoice flattening correctly retains one V2 case but collapses the two orders. Neither view supports allocating V2 or its payments between O2/O3. The consolidation itself is informative; per-order invoice counts or settlement claims are not.

6. **Partial payments—apparent loops and premature settlement.** V2’s Invoice trace is Issue Invoice  Record Payment  Record Payment, producing a self-loop. M2 only `partially_settles` V2; completion comes from M3/e25, a distinction absent from the activity label. In Order flattening, O2/O3 both receive both payments. O3’s e22–e24–e25–e27 interleaving, together with O4’s e26-before-e27 pattern, can produce Confirm Delivery  Record Payment and Record Payment  Confirm Delivery edges. This looks like a payment/confirmation cycle, not two instalments plus distinct delivery branches. The Invoice view is informative for V2 settlement; per-order causal interpretation is unsafe.

7. **Near-singleton Payment lifecycles.** M1–M4 become singleton cases containing only e21, e22, e25, or e26. There are no directly-follows edges, and the DFG cannot reconstruct that M2 and M3 both concern V2. Calling this a settled-payment process model would be harmful; using it to verify that exactly four payment records exist is useful.

8. **Item and Package simplicity can be deficiency, not standardization.** Every Item trace has the same four activities, and every Package trace has the same three. Item I21/I31 end at e24 even though V2’s final payment e25 and O2/O3 closure remain outside their lifecycle. The simple DFG is therefore informative about physical handling but cannot support claims about financial or end-to-end completion.

9. **Equivalent-execution clustering needs source event identity.** Copies of e16 share a source event ID and must not become independent executions. Conversely, e29 and e30 are distinct Close Order events despite sharing activity and timestamp. Clustering only on activity/timestamp could merge O2/O3 closure incorrectly, while clustering every repeated activity separately could miss genuine sharing. This is context-dependent and must be validated against event IDs and related objects.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

Create all six flattened logs as inexpensive diagnostics, but publish only question-specific interpretations:

- **Order:** direct order involvement, Create-to-Close spans, and order-attributed variants—not global execution effort or V2 allocation.
- **Item:** physical handling timestamps and item coverage.
- **Package/Delivery:** package handling and delivery-level dispatch-to-confirmation performance, with D2’s load reported separately.
- **Invoice:** V1–V3 issuance/settlement, especially V2’s two instalments.
- **Payment:** payment-record reconciliation, not lifecycle discovery.

Cross-object questions require an **OC-DFG** for event-level, multi-type structure; an **ET–OT graph** to expose activity participation and lifecycle deficiency; an **OTG plus object-interaction summary** to locate sharing around e13, e16, and V2; and explicit O2O queries for `packs`, `carries`, `bills`, and settlement. Equivalent-execution clustering is useful only with a declared equivalence rule and source-event provenance.

Contradictory frequencies should first be reconciled by units. For example, Dispatch occurs six times in the Order log, four times in the Package log, and three times in the Delivery log. These are respectively order, package, and delivery involvements of the same e15–e17 executions. If object-centric and flattened performance results disagree, map every contributing row back to its source event and state whether the measure concerns executions, objects, or case-involvements. Irreducible allocations, especially V2 between O2/O3, should remain explicitly unknown.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Decision: B. Use Order as the primary lead object, with explicitly secondary Item, Package, Delivery, Invoice, and Payment lifecycles.**

- **Decision criteria:** direct activity coverage, fit to the business question, shared-event multiplicity, branch preservation, and whether performance has a defensible denominator.
- **Strongest supporting evidence:** Order alone directly covers all 31 events and all activity types, including creation and closure. It is therefore the only practical primary view for order-attributed Create-to-Close analysis.
- **Strongest counterargument:** that coverage may simply reflect generous E2O annotation. Long Order traces do not establish ownership: P3/P4, D2, and V2 are genuinely shared, and Order flattening creates 11 extra copies. A Delivery or Invoice model is more accurate for its own execution unit.
- **Residual risk:** stakeholders may still treat the Order DFG as the official end-to-end process, count shared work repeatedly, or infer per-order settlement that the data cannot support.
- **Conditions for changing the decision:** use Package/Delivery as primary for logistics questions and Invoice for cash-collection questions. Reject any primary lead for a purely system-wide interaction analysis. Order could become a stronger standalone choice only if authoritative allocation and execution-weighting rules were supplied and validated.

print(ocel)

# Basic frequency inspection: these describe the source OCEL, not a chosen case notion.

print(object_type_frequency)

# Attach object types to actual E2O relations for a direct-relation audit.
e2o_with_types = ocel.relations.merge(
    ocel.objects[["ocel:oid", "ocel:type"]],
    on="ocel:oid",
    how="left"
)
direct_e2o_type_frequency = e2o_with_types["ocel:type"].value_counts()

    "Order", "Item", "Package", "Delivery", "Invoice", "Payment"
]

flattened_dfgs = {}

flattening_audit = {}

    # Multiplicity k means that one source event will enter k flattened cases.
    per_event_multiplicity = (
        e2o_with_types
        .loc[e2o_with_types["ocel:type"] == object_type]
        .groupby("ocel:eid")
        .size()
    )
    direct_copy_multiplicities[object_type] = per_event_multiplicity

    flattening_audit[object_type] = {
        "covered_source_events": int(len(per_event_multiplicity)),
        "expected_case_event_rows": int(per_event_multiplicity.sum()),
        "expected_extra_copies": int((per_event_multiplicity - 1).sum()),
        "events_with_multiple_selected_objects": int(
            (per_event_multiplicity > 1).sum()
        )
    }

    flat_log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(
        ocel, object_type=object_type
    )
    flattened_logs[object_type] = flat_log

    observed_rows = sum(len(trace) for trace in flat_log)
    if observed_rows != flattening_audit[object_type]["expected_case_event_rows"]:
        raise ValueError(f"Unexpected flattening multiplicity for {object_type}")

    # These models describe the flattened projection, not independent executions.
    flattened_dfgs[object_type] = pm4py.discover_dfg(flat_log)
    flattened_log_skeletons[object_type] = (
        pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(flat_log)
    )

print("Direct-E2O flattening audit:")
print(flattening_audit)

# Object-centric models retain the source event structure, but still require

ocdfg_model = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
object_type_graph = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
event_type_object_type_graph = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

1. **Copied-event sensitivity.** Compare ordinary flattening with fractional involvement weights \(1/k\), where \(k\) is the number of selected-type objects directly related to the event. Thus e16/e24 receive weight \(1/3\) in Order flattening, while e13 receives \(1/2\). Also temporarily remove multi-order events to identify which DFG edges depend on them, without treating the resulting broken traces as a better log. Report execution-weighted and involvement-weighted frequencies separately.

2. **Direct versus propagated relations.** Compare the baseline counts—42 Order, 24 Item, 12 Package, 6 Delivery, 7 Invoice, and 4 Payment rows—with bounded propagation variants. For example, propagate order-linked financial events to contained items, or package packing events to a delivery through `carries`, always retaining path provenance. Reject undirected closure and test whether new edges are semantically stable rather than merely longer.

3. **Lead-object sensitivity.** Repeat coverage, multiplicity, variant, and performance comparisons with Package, Delivery, and Invoice as primary. Recalculate rather than compare trace lengths: Delivery’s Dispatch-to-Confirm interval, Invoice’s Issue-to-last-Payment interval, and Order’s Create-to-Close interval measure different accomplishments.

4. **OC-DFG frequency-metric sensitivity.** For Dispatch, event frequency is 3 (e15–e17). Unique participating objects are 4 Orders, 6 Items, 4 Packages, and 3 Deliveries; summed E2O involvements are respectively 6, 6, 4, and 3. A total-object metric therefore need not equal either an event or unique-object metric. Similar checks should be applied to e16, which is one event with nine related objects.

5. **Identifier-based validation.** Reconcile every discovered frequency and performance outlier to source event IDs. Specific checks include one e13 execution despite four order/item involvements, one D2 despite its multi-package load, two V2 instalments rather than two invoices, and distinct e29/e30 closures. Finally, verify that lifecycle simplicity is not being caused solely by missing direct E2O coverage.
