## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

We use **separable / interventional** effects, not natural direct/indirect effects. Reason: flag triggers a bundle (priority lane, mandatory review rule, escalation propensity, reviewer behavior), queues create interference (violates SUTVA / no-interference), and `manual_queue_wait` is an exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounder for escalation. Cross-world independence for natural effects is implausible and untestable. Controlled direct effects (CDE) and randomized interventional analogues (G-computation / inverse-weighting of mediator distribution) are well-defined as policy contrasts.

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1-total | P0 (current bundle) vs P1 (suppress display, keep risk info) for all arriving cases | case, Mar-02 cohort + future arrivals | total cycle: `close-arrival` | total policy effect | Yes, if P1 protocol is specified | interference via shared queues; hidden risk `U_underlying_risk` |
| E2-triage | P0 vs P1 | case, all arrivals | `triage_done` or `triage_start-arrival` + `triage_service` | total effect on early stage / CDE precursor | Yes | triage prioritization spillover to unflagged; arrival_load coarse |
| E3-occur | P0 vs P2 (retain priority triage, remove mandatory manual review, keep human discretion) | flagged-eligible cases | `manual_review` occurrence | interventional indirect via occurrence (eliminated / stochastic) | Yes as separable dispatch component | positivity: flagged almost always reviewed under P0; reviewer behavior change |
| E4-wait | P0 vs P3 (same routing + protected review capacity) with `manual_review=1` | reviewed cases | `manual_start - triage_done` | interventional indirect via waiting, holding occurrence/service fixed | Yes, capacity intervention | queue interference; time-varying congestion not captured by `arrival_load` |
| E5-esc | P0 vs P4 (same review, changed escalation threshold/capacity) | reviewed cases | `escalation` occurrence + `escalation_queue_wait` + cycle | interventional indirect via escalation | Yes if threshold rule written | exposure-induced confounding: review wait affects escalation; scarce senior team |
| E6-cycle-decomp | P2 vs P0; P3 vs P0; P4 vs P0 | case, flagged stratum | total cycle | separable / path-specific policy effects on cycle time | Yes, three distinct contrasts | conflating "set flag=0" with "change response to flag"; complexity-risk confounding |
| E7-quality | P0 vs P1/P2/P4 | case, flagged + high `complexity` | `decision_outcome` (Approve/Reject correctness, compliance, rework) | total + CDE holding speed fixed | Marginally; needs gold-standard quality | `outcome` is decision not quality; `risk_score` imperfect; selective review changes information |

P0-P4 are distinct: P1 removes information signal but leaves underlying risk; P2 changes routing; P3 changes capacity; P4 changes senior filter. They do not share a common "direct effect of flag."

```
t5 REVIEW SERVICE: {complexity, review_resource, U_reviewer_severity} -> manual_service (= manual_done - manual_start)
t6 ESCALATION ROUTING: {manual_service output, flag, U_human_judgment} -> escalation [0/1]
t7 ESCALATION QUEUE+SERVICE: -> escalation_queue_wait (= escalation_start - manual_done) -> senior_service (= escalation_done - escalation_start) [scarce senior team]
t8 CLOSE: residual (= close - max(triage_done,manual_done,escalation_done)) -> cycle_time (= close-arrival) + decision_outcome
FEEDBACK: t4/t7 queues shared -> interference across cases arriving close in time
```

Interference, exposure-induced confounding, feedback: shared triage/review/senior queues mean one case's flagged status shifts `triage_wait`, `manual_queue_wait`, `escalation_queue_wait` for others — SUTVA fails. `manual_queue_wait` is caused by flag-induced load and confounds `escalation -> cycle_time` and `escalation -> outcome`, creating treatment-confounder feedback: flag  wait  escalation  wait  cycle. Conditioning on wait or escalation induces collider / over-control bias. `complexity` confounds flag-mediator and mediator-outcome; unmeasured `U_underlying_risk` omitted from `risk_score` confounds all downstream paths and violates sequential ignorability needed for natural effects.

Flagged cases start triage ~2.2h earlier and have ~0.35h shorter triage service, consistent with priority lane and T1/T2 assignment, yet total cycle is ~8.3h longer on average. The excess concentrates in `triage-done-to-manual-start` (+4.6h) and added escalation load (60% vs 20% escalated, each adding ~9h queue+service). Within high complexity, flagged still longer but gap shrinks, showing case-mix confounding. Contrasts are not causal: flag correlates with `complexity`/`U_underlying_risk`, queues induce interference, and conditioning on post-flag mediators (review/escalation) selects riskier cases.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Is longer cycle due to flag near threshold? | Within-risk-score / RDD local comparison: compare cases just above vs below flag cutoff (e.g., 0.61-0.67: R20 0.61 flag0, R15 0.64 flag1, R08 0.67 flag1) on triage wait, review rate, cycle | narrow `risk_score` band + `complexity`, `arrival_load`, arrival hour | continuity of U_risk at cutoff; no manipulation of score; same queue regime | McCrary density test; placebo cutoff; balance of `complexity` at cutoff | No jump in review/esc/cycle at cutoff  occurrence mediation weak; large jump  supports mandatory-review channel |
| Does queue state explain wait gap? | Queue-state-aware: model `manual_queue_wait` as function of # flagged cases already waiting at `triage_done` + staffing, stratified by flag | `arrival_load`, time-of-day, outstanding review queue length, `review_resource` | queue length pre-determined; no unmeasured staffing shock | Flag coefficient should shrink after conditioning on queue length; test flag×queue interaction | Flag effect persists with full queue control  suggests service/assignment not just congestion |
| What is policy effect of removing mandate? | Randomized encouragement / pilot P2: randomly suppress mandatory review for eligible flagged (physician override allowed), ITT + CACE | intention-to-treat; stratify by `complexity`, `risk_score` | exclusion (encouragement only via routing), monotonicity | Check override rate; compare compliers vs always-takers; pre-period balance | Large ITT cycle drop with no quality drop  supports P2; no drop  wait/escalation not driven by mandate |
| Occurrence vs waiting mediation? | Mediation-oriented interventional: decompose via g-computation with two mediators (occurrence, then wait conditional on occurrence); CDE of flag holding review=0/1 | `complexity`, `risk_score`, `arrival_load`; sensitivity for U | sequential ignorability for interventional version; no cross-world needed | Compare stochastic intervention (set review distribution of flag0) vs CDE; bootstrap CI | Most effect via occurrence indicator, little via wait-level  prioritize P2; opposite  prioritize P3 |
| Spurious flag effect? | Negative control: exposure = future flag rule applied to pre-period; outcome = pre-triage wait before flag existed; alternative: effect of flag on `triage_start-arrival` for cases arriving when priority lane closed | same case-mix adjusters | no anticipation; lane closure exogenous | Any "effect" in pre-period indicates confounding / calendar effect | Positive control fails  downgrade causal claim for all downstream contrasts |
| How fragile to hidden risk? | Sensitivity for unobserved risk: Rosenbaum / VanderWeele bias + E-value; simulate U that raises both flag odds and cycle by X | `risk_score`+`complexity` baseline | calibrated U prevalence from chart review | Report tipping point: how strong U must be to explain 8h gap | Small tipping strength  conclusion reverses to case-mix; large required  mechanism more credible |
| Spillover to unflagged? | Interference-aware: cluster-randomize review slots / senior hours by day/shift; estimate direct + spillover (Hudgens-Halloran) using queue-length exposure mapping | cluster = shift; exposure = peer flagged share | partial interference within shift; no cross-shift carryover | Unflagged cycle rises with peer flagged share; total system time per shift | No spillover gradient  per-case analysis suffices; steep gradient  must evaluate system-level P3/P4 |

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 keep current | fast flagged triage (-2h), slow unflagged triage | review wait ~6.5h flagged; esc 60%; senior bottleneck | flagged ~16.5h mean, 16.5h median; system heavy | possibly higher scrutiny, unknown accuracy | unflagged delayed in triage; senior queue blocks all | weak causal, descriptive only | do not lock in; use as control arm |

| P2 retain priority, remove mandate | retains -2h triage gain | review occurrence  sharply; review wait  2-4h; esc  | flagged -4 to -8h (largest cycle saving); unflagged triage + review improve | moderate: missed escalation if discretion fails; needs audit | positive spillover to all queues | moderate; supported by 100% vs 40% gap + threshold cases | **preferred pilot** with quality guardrails |
| P3 protected review capacity | retains triage gain | flagged wait  2-5h, unflagged wait may  if capacity split | flagged -2 to -5h; system total flat or  if capacity idle | low quality risk; preserves scrutiny | negative spillover to unflagged unless new hires | weak-moderate; needs queue data | consider if P2 unsafe or mandates fixed |
| P4 change esc criteria/capacity | minimal triage change | esc rate , esc wait ; review wait unchanged | flagged+esc subgroup -5 to -9h; overall -1 to -3h | moderate-high: higher false Approve if threshold too high | relieves scarcest resource; large leverage | moderate; esc adds ~9h for 6/10 flagged | pilot jointly with P2 as second arm |

Pilot (P2 with P4 safety, vs P0): **Eligibility:** flagged, `risk_score` 0.60-0.85, non-High complexity or High with attending override, excluding legally mandated reviews. **Randomization:** individual 1:1 to mandatory vs discretionary review within arrival shift, stratified by complexity/load, with opt-in senior override logged. **Guardrail:** cap discretionary arm at 40% of review slots; real-time queue monitor — pause if unflagged review wait >4h or senior backlog >6h. **Outcomes:** primary total cycle + `manual_queue_wait`; secondary escalation rate, 30-day correctness/rework/appeal, compliance checklist. **Minimum data:** arrival/triage/manual/escalation start+done, resources, risk_score, complexity, queue length at each handoff, override reason, outcome audit. **Stopping:** stop for harm if Reject overturn rate or high-risk miss +5pp vs control; stop for efficacy if cycle -4h with non-inferior quality; interim at n=60; max n=150.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. Hidden risk dominates: if omitted `U_underlying_risk` explains >70% of review/escalation and duration gap within narrow `risk_score`/complexity bands, the flag-to-delay mechanism reverses to pure case-mix selection.
2. Queue interference reverses sign: if flagged priority substantially shortens unflagged triage via load-smoothing or extra staffing (not captured by coarse `arrival_load`), system total under P0 is lower and P2/P3 harm unflagged.
3. Decision quality: if audit shows flagged manual+senior review catches 10pp+ more false Approves or compliance violations, longer cycle is justified and fastest-cycle policy (P2) is no longer preferred.
4. Timestamp semantics: if `manual_start`/`escalation_start` record assignment rather than service start, or dashes conflate skipping with missing logs, or clocks are non-synchronous, waiting vs service decomposition reverses.
5. Positivity / lack of overlap: if no unflagged High-risk or flagged Low-risk exist and reviewers never override mandate, CDE/interventional contrasts require extrapolation and estimated P2 benefit collapses.
6. Behavior change by reviewers: if removing mandate causes risk-averse over-referral (reviewers escalate more to avoid blame) or P1 causes defensive triage, downstream load rises and P2/P1 increase rather than decrease cycle time.

## 9. Final synthesis

What is directly observed: flagged cases (n=10) start triage ~2.2h earlier with shorter triage service yet average ~8.3h longer total cycle than unflagged (16.5h vs 8.2h, medians 16.5h vs 5.8h). All flagged receive manual review versus 40% of unflagged; 60% versus 20% are escalated. Conditional review wait is 6.5h versus 1.9h; service times are similar; escalation adds ~9h queue+service. Plausible mechanism: priority lane accelerates triage but mandatory routing plus shared review/senior queues and M2 concentration create occurrence and waiting mediation that outweighs early gain, amplified by High complexity enrichment. What remains unidentified: whether delay is causal flag-handling versus hidden risk, how much is occurrence versus waiting, and spillover magnitude, given interference, exposure-induced confounding, imperfect risk_score, and n=20. Most useful next evidence: a stratified pilot of P2 (retain priority, discretionary review with override logging and queue guardrails) versus P0, measuring cycle, queue lengths, escalation, and audited decision quality — separating the manipulable routing bundle from merely hiding the flag.
