## **Recommendation**
**Do not scale Redesign R nationally yet.** Instead, **run a controlled regional experiment (Alternative D) with targeted refinements (Alternative E)** to isolate the root causes of worsening tail metrics, rework, and compliance risks. This approach balances the clear benefits for simple low-risk cases with the need to mitigate deterioration in complex segments (apartments, old buildings, businesses).

**Immediate actions:**
- **Pause** the survey-skipping rule and post-billing activation checks.
- **Scale** coordinator ownership *only* for simple low-risk houses (where benefits are proven).
- **Launch** a 12-week regional experiment in two high-risk segments (apartments, old buildings) with:
  - Randomized testing of survey-skipping (M1, M6).
  - Structured reason codes for failures (M3).
  - Full downstream cost tracking (M4).
  - Conformance monitoring for billing-before-activation (M7).

**Success criteria for broader rollout:**
- p90 cycle time  35 days (vs. current 38.5).
- First-time-right  80% (vs. 76%).
- Complaint rate  8% (vs. 9.8%).
- Coordinator utilization  85%.

## **Explanation of Metric Conflicts**

1. **High fitness, low precision:**
   - *Fitness* (0.94) indicates the model captures observed pilot cases well, but *precision* (0.51) shows it allows too many unrealistic paths (e.g., billing before activation, skipped surveys). This reflects the redesign’s simplified coordinator path enabling edge cases.

2. **Improved median, worse tail:**
   - Simple cases (34% of pilot) benefited from merged validation/scheduling and fewer handovers, but complex cases (apartments, old buildings) suffered from skipped surveys, permit gaps, and manual activations, creating long delays.

3. **Fewer handovers, lower resilience:**
   - Coordinator ownership reduced handovers but concentrated knowledge in a single role. When coordinators were absent, cases stalled because context wasn’t shared (e.g., failed visits  second visits delayed).

4. **Lower visible cost, higher rework:**
   - Reduced handovers cut coordination costs, but hidden rework (second visits, complaints, manual activations) increased downstream costs (EUR 198  EUR 207 estimated).

5. **Segment-specific effects:**
   - Simple houses: Streamlined process worked.
   - Apartments/old buildings: Survey skipping and permit gaps caused rework.
   - Business customers: Manual activations led to billing disputes.

## **Final Recommendation**

**Immediate Actions:**
1. **Pause** survey skipping and post-billing activation checks.
2. **Scale** coordinator ownership *only* for simple low-risk houses (where median duration and first-time-right improved).
3. **Launch** a 12-week regional experiment in two high-risk segments (apartments, old buildings) with:
   - Randomized testing of survey skipping (M1, M6).
   - Structured failure reason codes (M3).
   - Full downstream cost tracking (M4).
   - Conformance monitoring for billing-before-activation (M7).

**Success Thresholds for Broader Rollout:**
- p90 cycle time  35 days.
- First-time-right  80%.
- Complaint rate  8%.
- Coordinator utilization  85%.

**If experiment succeeds:**
- Roll out refined redesign (Alternative E) to medium-risk segments.
- **Only scale nationally (Alternative A) if all thresholds are met.**

**If experiment fails:**
- Roll back survey skipping and post-billing activation (Alternative B).
- Revert to baseline for high-risk segments.

This approach minimizes risk while maximizing learnings, ensuring that any broader rollout is evidence-based and segment-appropriate.
