## 2. Rule Interpretation

- **RR1 — Drafter/approver separation:** For each case, `human(Draft_Change_Request) != human(Approve_Change)`. Aliases are normalized first.
- **RR2 — Implementer/verifier separation:** For each case, `human(Implement_Change) != human(Verify_Implementation)`. A service account cannot pass this rule unless reliably mapped to an authorized human.
- **RR3 — High-risk approval authority:** For `risk=HIGH` and `activity=Approve_Change`, the approver must be the standing QM principal or a valid QM delegate. A delegation is valid only when all are true: effective interval covers the event timestamp, scope covers the case, required principal absence/unavailability is established, and no prohibited subdelegation occurred.
- **RR4 — Emergency substitution:** For HIGH-risk emergency changes, IC or valid IC delegate approval can substitute for QM approval at the approval step, but a QM or valid QM delegate must perform `Retrospective_QM_Review` within two business days, and the emergency/safety rationale must be credible. The emergency flag alone is insufficient without incident linkage or safety justification.
- **RR5 — Identity evidence:** Service accounts are not human identities. Aliases must be normalized. Shared workstations and generic accounts require reliable session-to-human mapping.
- **RR6 — Suspicion versus proof:** Repeated pairs, reciprocal approvals, shared workstations, and concentrated handovers are investigative indicators only. They do not prove collusion absent supporting evidence.

Business-day convention for RR4: use UTC timestamps and the site calendar, count Monday–Friday excluding recorded holidays. The emergency event day is day 0; the deadline is the end of the second following business day. Here 2026-08-11 is Tuesday, with no holiday, so the RR4 deadline for CHG-505 is end of Thursday 2026-08-13. A review on Friday 2026-08-14 is late. Counting the event day inclusively would also make 2026-08-14 late, so the conclusion is robust.

Principal action while on leave: leave is an availability fact, not automatic revocation of standing authority. If Asha personally approves while on leave, RR3 can still be satisfied. But leave plus unexplained account use requires identity scrutiny. Conversely, a delegation that requires principal absence is weakened if the principal is demonstrably active for the relevant period.

Later database entries: a QMS database record created after an event cannot, by itself, prove the delegation existed at the event time. It can corroborate an earlier signed instrument if the signed PDF, signature metadata, and migration/backfill history are reliable. Thus D-QM-01 is acceptable for earlier events because the signed PDF predates the interval, but the 2026-08-12 database creation remains a data-quality caveat.

def evaluate_delegation(d, event):
    reasons = []
    when = event["ts_utc"]
    human = event["human_for_checks"]

    if human != d["delegate"]:
        reasons.append("actor is not delegate")
    if event["concept:name"] not in d["activities"]:
        reasons.append("activity not in delegation scope")
    if event["risk"] not in d["risks"]:
        reasons.append("risk not in delegation scope")
    if d["scopes"] and event["scope"] not in d["scopes"]:
        reasons.append("change scope not covered")
    if d["emergency_only"] and not event["emergency"]:
        reasons.append("emergency-only delegation used for non-emergency")
    if d["allowed_case_ids"] and event["case:concept:name"] not in d["allowed_case_ids"]:
        reasons.append("case not in delegated incident scope")
    if not (d["start"] <= when <= d["end"]):
        reasons.append("event outside effective interval")
    if d["requires_principal_absent"] and not in_intervals(d["principal"], absence_intervals, when):
        reasons.append("principal absence not evidenced")
    if d["prohibited_subdelegation"]:
        reasons.append("prohibited subdelegation")
    if (d["signed_evidence_time"] is not None and d["signed_evidence_time"] > when):
        reasons.append("signed evidence postdates event")

    caveats = [d["evidence_note"]]
    if d["record_time"] is not None and d["record_time"] > when:
        caveats.append("DB record postdates event; signed evidence controls only if authentic")

    return len(reasons) == 0, reasons, " | ".join(caveats)

def business_day_deadline(event_ts, n=2):

def network_edges(net, top=15):
    rows = []
    try:
        for u, v, data in net.edges(data=True):

## 6. Control Improvements

- **Delegation creation:** Require pre-approved, machine-readable delegations in the QMS before effective time. Explicitly record authority type, activity, risk, scope, case/incident, emergency status, principal, delegate, expiry, no-subdelegation clause, and delegate acceptance.
- **Effective-time recording:** Synchronize signed documents and QMS records. If migration/backfill creates a late record, preserve original signature metadata, hashes, and audit trail. Do not treat a late database entry alone as proof of earlier authority.
- **Principal availability:** Integrate HR leave, on-call, and urgent-override status into the approval engine. If the principal is formally unavailable, permit delegate action; if the principal personally acts, log an override and suspend relevant delegate use for that case.
- **Alias management:** Enforce a canonical human ID in the QMS. Block approvals where `Draft_Change_Request` and `Approve_Change` map to the same human, regardless of user ID string.
- **Individual authentication:** Eliminate generic service accounts where possible. Where unavoidable, bind each service session to a named human through MFA/FIDO, workstation, badge, timestamp, and session token. Require re-authentication for high-risk actions.
- **Emergency review:** Require structured safety rationale, incident linkage, and automatic two-business-day RR4 clocks. Escalate overdue reviews and require the retrospective reviewer to be QM-qualified for the emergency scope, not merely a general QM delegate.
- **Pair rotation:** Although no formal pair-rotation policy exists, introduce secondary approvers or periodic rotation for HIGH-risk production changes. Monitor concentration as a control-health signal, not as proof of misconduct.
- **Network monitoring:** Baseline handover and working-together networks over longer periods. Combine network indicators with timestamps, after-hours activity, exception rates, identity evidence, and substantive review findings.
- **Evidential retention:** Retain signed delegations, QMS audit trails, FIDO/MFA logs, VPN sessions, badge logs, CCTV where lawful, workstation sessions, HR leave records, emergency rationales, and reviewer qualifications under synchronized time and chain-of-custody controls.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

The apparent nonconformance is a mixture, not a single collusion story. Roughly, **five to six cases contain genuine authority or SoD control failures** if the D-QM-02 absence gap is enforced: CHG-502, CHG-505 retrospective review, CHG-507, CHG-508, CHG-509, and CHG-510. **Two cases are primarily identity/data problems**: CHG-503 and CHG-506. **CHG-504 is technically authorized but identity-sensitive** because Asha’s leave conflicts with her VPN session. **CHG-501 is a valid substitution** under D-QM-01, with a database-lag caveat.

Organizational-pattern risk is real but unproven: the Clara–Ben concentration and reciprocal LOW-risk pattern warrant review, but repetition alone does not establish collusion. The highest uncertainty lies in credential sharing, D-QM-02’s principal-absence condition, D-QM-01 database timing, CHG-503 service attribution, and whether CHG-505 is formally within CI-44.

Three highest-priority investigations:

1. **CHG-510 and emergency authority chain:** invalid attempted subdelegation, same-human draft/approval, and out-of-scope retrospective review.
2. **Identity and account-use cluster:** CHG-503 `svc_qms`, CHG-506 Ben-while-sick, and CHG-504 Asha VPN/leave overlap.
3. **Delegation and pattern governance:** D-QM-01 timing/authenticity, D-QM-02 absence condition, and the Clara–Ben/reciprocal-approval pattern.
