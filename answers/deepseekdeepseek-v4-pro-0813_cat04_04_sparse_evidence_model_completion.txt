| V4: 3× `RO,ER,CC,AC,RP,AR` | Skip of SC with ER-first order. | Same missingness ambiguity. | Medium count | Supports optional SC across both check orders. |
| V5: 2× `RO,CC,SC,ER,AC,RP,AR` | SC may occur after CC but before ER. | Does not prove SC only needs one check; may be a delayed ER. | Low count | Relevant for D5; balanced model should not require both checks before SC if fitting this. |
| V6: 1× `RO,CC,ER,SC,EX,AC,RP,AR` | EX after both checks and after SC, before AC. | Does not prove EX is always after SC. | Very low count | Supports D6 and permits EX after SC. |
| V7: 1× `RO,ER,CC,EX,SC,AC,RP,AR` | EX after both checks but before SC, before AC. | Does not prove EX may always precede SC. | Very low count | Supports D6 and permits EX before SC. |
| D1 | Intended start is Register Order; intended end is Archive Case. | Does not prove all started/ended cases are in log. | High | Hard constraint in all models. |
| D2 | Release Production requires Approve Configuration. | Does not prove no other approval or release prerequisites. | High | Hard constraint in all models. |
| D3 | CC and ER should both be completed before Approve Configuration. | Does not prove they are jointly sufficient. | Medium-to-high | Hard constraint in all models. |
| D4 | Supplier Confirmation, when required, must occur before Approve Configuration. | Does not prove SC is required. | Medium | Hard ordering when SC occurs. |
| D5 | SC may begin after either CC or ER; it need not wait for both. | Does not prove SC can never start before both. | Low-to-medium | Used in balanced; relaxed in permissive. |
| D6 | Expedite Review is optional, at most once, requires completion of both CC and ER, and precedes Approve Configuration. | Does not prove EX cannot occur before SC or after SC in all contexts. | Medium | Used in balanced and permissive; optional EX after both checks. |
| D7 | Completion timestamps do not reveal whether CC and ER were concurrent, sequential, or overlapping. | Prevents any strong concurrency conclusion from DFG. | Unknown/high metaknowledge | Avoid claiming true concurrency from traces alone. |
| S1 | Finance manager: Credit Check must finish before Engineering Review starts. | Does not match V2/V4/V7 unless those traces reflect data/process violations. | Stakeholder assertion, contradicted by log | Cannot be treated as a hard constraint across all models. |
| S2 | Engineering lead: checks normally run in parallel; whichever finishes first appears first. | Does not prove actual overlapping execution from completion log. | Medium plausible | Supports CC/ER interleaving or concurrency. |
| S3 | Procurement lead: SC mandatory for every external component. | Does not prove all cases are external. | Stakeholder assertion | SC should be mandatory only when external flag is known; flag absent. |
| S4 | Production planner: stocked components legitimately skip SC. | Does not provide the external/stocked attribute. | Medium | Justifies optional SC abstraction. |
| S5 | Data engineer: external/stocked attribute not exported; 5–10% of SC events may be missing. | Does not identify which missing SC events are true skips vs integration losses. | Medium | Missingness must be modeled as optionality/silent branch, with disclosed uncertainty. |
| S6 | Compliance officer: EX permitted only after both principal checks; may be before or after SC, but before approval. | Does not prove EX is mandatory in any case. | Medium | Used as D6; supports both EX/SC orders. |
| S7 | Only completion timestamps; same timestamp ordered by ingestion index. | Does not prove true precedence; same-timestamp order is an artifact. | High logging limitation | Strengthens the case for partial-order/concurrency uncertainty. |

A DFG containing both `Credit Check -> Engineering Review` and `Engineering Review -> Credit Check` only shows that both completion orders occur in different traces. It does **not** show that the two activities were simultaneously enabled, overlapped in time, or causally independent. With only completion timestamps and ingestion-order tie-breaking, parallel, sequential, and overlapping execution can all produce the same two directions. Therefore, the DFG alone must not be used to infer concurrency.

# 2. Shared Assumptions

1. **Event-completion semantics**: Each event in the trace is a completed activity. Traces are completion orders, not start orders or resource assignments.
2. **Atomicity and multiplicity**: Activities are atomic and occur at most once per case. Duplicate labels within a trace are treated as invalid unless otherwise stated.
3. **Start and end**: `Register Order` must start and `Archive Case` must end every case.
4. **Mandatory approval-production arc**: `Approve Configuration` always precedes `Release Production`, and `Release Production` always precedes `Archive Case`.
5. **Missing Supplier Confirmation**: The log cannot separate true skipped SC from missing SC events caused by the integration issue. Models therefore represent absent SC as an optional/silent branch, not as proof of true optionality.
6. **External/stocked ambiguity**: Since the external/stocked attribute is absent, no model can enforce S3’s “SC mandatory for external components” directly. Optionality is represented via `XOR(tau, Supplier Confirmation)`.
7. **Optionality**: Optional activities are represented by an explicit silent transition / tau branch, not by removing the activity from the process.
8. **Uncertain concurrency**: Completion-order data are insufficient to prove true concurrency. Candidate models may use interleavings or partial orders, but this is an abstraction, not a causal claim.
9. **Domain-rule confidence ordering**: High-confidence rules D1–D2 are enforced in all models; medium-confidence D3/D4/D6 are enforced unless a model explicitly opts to relax them; low-medium D5 is enforced in balanced but relaxed in permissive.

# 3. Conservative Candidate

## Inductive-bias statement

The conservative model treats the seven observed variants as the only supported behavior. It avoids adding unobserved interleavings, optionality beyond observed skips, or concurrency claims. It enforces high-confidence start/end and approval-release ordering, but does not generalize beyond the observed complete sequences.

## Compact formal expression

Let `V = {v1, ..., v7}` be the seven observed variant sequences:

```text
M_cons = XOR(
  ->(RO, CC, ER, SC, AC, RP, AR),
  ->(RO, ER, CC, SC, AC, RP, AR),
  ->(RO, CC, ER, AC, RP, AR),
  ->(RO, ER, CC, AC, RP, AR),
  ->(RO, CC, SC, ER, AC, RP, AR),
  ->(RO, CC, ER, SC, EX, AC, RP, AR),
  ->(RO, ER, CC, EX, SC, AC, RP, AR)
)
```

## Behavior deliberately excluded

- Any unobserved interleaving, such as `RO, CC, ER, EX, SC, AC, RP, AR`.
- Any unobserved combination of optional SC/EX, such as SC-before-both-checks or EX in a no-SC variant.
- Any new variant not present in the 30-case log.

## Known underfitting risks

- The model is an almost pure replay model and will reject valid future variants.
- It cannot distinguish true SC optionality from missing SC; if missingness is real, the no-SC variants may be incomplete records.
- It ignores D5/D6 generalization potential because it only accepts observed EX/SC placements.
- XOR enumeration scales poorly with variant count and is impractical for larger logs.

## Duplicate labels and scalability

Each branch contains distinct activity labels within that branch; duplicate labels occur only across XOR branches, which is safe because only one branch is chosen. However, the approach does not scale to logs with many variants.

# 4. Balanced Candidate

## Inductive-bias statement

The balanced model admits all observed variants and encodes the most plausible domain generalization: both principal checks must complete before approval; Expedite Review is optional and requires both checks; Supplier Confirmation is optional and may start after at least one principal check. It uses partial-order branches to represent the two possible first-completion orders for CC and ER, while avoiding a single unbounded parallel block that would allow unsupported behavior.

## Compact formal expression

Let:

```text
opt(SC) = XOR(tau, Supplier Confirmation)
opt(EX) = XOR(tau, Expedite Review)

SPO_CC_first = PartialOrder(
    children = {CC, ER, opt(SC), opt(EX)},
    edges = {CC -> ER, CC -> opt(SC), ER -> opt(EX)}
)

SPO_ER_first = PartialOrder(
    children = {ER, CC, opt(SC), opt(EX)},
    edges = {ER -> CC, ER -> opt(SC), CC -> opt(EX)}
)

M_bal = ->( Register Order,
            XOR(SPO_CC_first, SPO_ER_first),
            Approve Configuration,
            Release Production,
            Archive Case )
```

## PM4Py code

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    Operator,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
    StrictPartialOrder,
)

ro = Transition("Register Order")
ac = Transition("Approve Configuration")
rp = Transition("Release Production")
ar = Transition("Archive Case")

# CC-first partial-order branch
cc1 = Transition("Credit Check")
er1 = Transition("Engineering Review")
sc1 = Transition("Supplier Confirmation")
ex1 = Transition("Expedite Review")
opt_sc1 = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [SilentTransition(), sc1])
opt_ex1 = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [SilentTransition(), ex1])

cc_first = StrictPartialOrder([cc1, er1, opt_sc1, opt_ex1])
cc_first.order.add_edge(cc1, er1)
cc_first.order.add_edge(cc1, opt_sc1)
cc_first.order.add_edge(er1, opt_ex1)

# ER-first partial-order branch
er2 = Transition("Engineering Review")
cc2 = Transition("Credit Check")
sc2 = Transition("Supplier Confirmation")
ex2 = Transition("Expedite Review")
opt_sc2 = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [SilentTransition(), sc2])
opt_ex2 = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [SilentTransition(), ex2])

er_first = StrictPartialOrder([er2, cc2, opt_sc2, opt_ex2])
er_first.order.add_edge(er2, cc2)
er_first.order.add_edge(er2, opt_sc2)
er_first.order.add_edge(cc2, opt_ex2)

check_sc_ex_branch = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [cc_first, er_first])
model_balanced = OperatorPOWL(Operator.SEQUENCE, [ro, check_sc_ex_branch, ac, rp, ar])
```

## How sequence, concurrency, and partial order are decided

- **Credit Check and Engineering Review**: The log shows both `CC -> ER` and `ER -> CC`. S2 claims they normally run in parallel, but D7 says completion timestamps cannot prove that. Therefore, I do **not** use a single parallel `+` block as the primary representation, because it would erase the “first completed check” information. Instead, I use `XOR(SPO_CC_first, SPO_ER_first)`: two partial-order branches, one with CC initially enabled and one with ER initially enabled. This preserves both observed interleavings while not claiming true simultaneous start.
- **Supplier Confirmation**: It is optional because of S4 and S5. In each partial-order branch, `opt(SC)` has an incoming edge from the first principal check, so SC cannot occur before both checks. It is unordered with respect to the second check, so it can occur before or after that second check.
- **Expedite Review**: It is optional and has an incoming edge from the second principal check, so it requires both CC and ER. In the partial-order branch, after the second check, `opt(EX)` and `opt(SC)` are unordered if SC has not already occurred, so EX may occur before or after SC, matching D6 and observed variants V6/V7.

## Behavior deliberately excluded

- SC before both CC and ER.
- EX before both CC and ER.
- Approve Configuration before both CC and ER.
- Release Production before Approve Configuration.
- Any missing start or end activity.
- Multiple occurrences of SC, EX, CC, or ER.

## Known underfitting risks

- The model still represents CC/ER as two first-completion branches rather than true simultaneous concurrency; if true overlap is common, it may be behaviorally adequate but semantically incomplete.
- It assumes D5’s “SC after at least one check” is correct. If SC can start before both checks in some cases, the model will reject those cases.
- It treats missing SC as optionality; if SC was truly mandatory for external cases and missing only because of export issues, the model underfits the real process.

# 5. Permissive Candidate

## Inductive-bias statement

The permissive model keeps high-confidence and medium rules that are strongly supported, but relaxes the low-medium D5 rule. It allows Supplier Confirmation to occur anywhere after Register Order and before Approve Configuration, including before both principal checks. It still requires both CC and ER before Approve Configuration and still requires Expedite Review to occur only after both CC and ER and before approval.

## Compact formal expression

```text
opt(SC) = XOR(tau, Supplier Confirmation)
opt(EX) = XOR(tau, Expedite Review)

M_per = ->(
  Register Order,
  XOR(
    ->( +(CC, ER, opt(SC)), opt(EX) ),
    ->( +(CC, ER), opt(EX), opt(SC) )
  ),
  Approve Configuration,
  Release Production,
  Archive Case
)
```

The first branch allows CC, ER, and optional SC to occur in any order, followed by optional EX. The second branch requires both checks to complete, then allows optional EX before optional SC.

## PM4Py code

## Boundary between reasonable generalization and forbidden behavior

Reasonable generalization includes:

- Any interleaving of CC and ER after Register Order.
- SC optional and, if present, before AC.
- EX optional and, if present, after both CC and ER and before AC.
- SC before or after EX when both checks are complete.
- SC before both checks as a low-confidence relaxation of D5.

Still forbidden:

- Missing `Register Order`, `Approve Configuration`, `Release Production`, or `Archive Case`.
- `Release Production` before `Approve Configuration`.
- `Approve Configuration` before both `Credit Check` and `Engineering Review`.
- `Expedite Review` before both `Credit Check` and `Engineering Review`.
- Multiple occurrences of `Credit Check`, `Engineering Review`, `Supplier Confirmation`, or `Expedite Review`.

## Behavior deliberately excluded

- EX before both CC and ER.
- AC before both CC and ER.
- RP before AC.
- Missing start or end activities.
- Duplicate activities.

## Known underfitting risks

- The model allows SC before both checks, which contradicts D5 and may be too permissive for compliance.
- It still requires EX after both checks; if rare cases allow EX after only one check, the model would reject them.
- The ProcessTree representation uses two branches rather than a fully connected partial order; behaviorally it is broad, but structurally it may still be less expressive than a general Petri net.

        for trace_str in positive_suite:
            trace_log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string([trace_str])
            fit = pm4py.fitness_alignments(trace_log, net, im, fm)
            print("  POS", trace_str, "->", fit)
        for trace_str in negative_suite:
            trace_log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string([trace_str])
            fit = pm4py.fitness_alignments(trace_log, net, im, fm)
            print("  NEG", trace_str, "->", fit)
    except Exception as e:
        print("  Error:", e)

print("The models may generate an infinite or very large set of traces; API-level comparisons are bounded/sampled.")

# 9. Recommendation and Data-Collection Plan

## Recommendation

Use the **balanced model** for immediate use. It fits all observed cases, respects the medium-confidence compliance rules, handles missing SC conservatively via optionality, and generalizes only the most plausible unobserved interleavings.

Use the **conservative model** for sensitivity analysis and as a lower-bound replay model. If stakeholders need an audit artifact of exactly what was observed, conservative is safer than permissive.

The permissive model is better for exploratory process mining and failure-case brainstorming, not for compliance sign-off.

## Data-collection plan

1. **Collect lifecycle timestamps**:
   - For each activity: `start_timestamp`, `complete_timestamp`, `schedule_timestamp`.
   - This directly addresses D7 by distinguishing sequence, overlap, and true concurrency.

2. **Collect same-timestamp tie-break metadata**:
   - Preserve actual timestamps with timezone and sub-second precision.
   - Store ingestion order separately from event time to avoid S7 artifacts.

3. **Collect case attributes**:
   - `external_component` or `stocked_component` flag.
   - `supplier_confirmation_required` flag.
   - `expedite_review_required` flag.

4. **Reconcile supplier events**:
   - Export supplier confirmation events from the procurement system.
   - Cross-check cases where SC is absent in process log but present in procurement data.

5. **Collect resource/system information**:
   - Which system executed each event?
   - Which resource or team completed it?
   - Useful for detecting integration-driven missingness.

6. **Most informative future traces**:
   - Traces with **start and complete timestamps overlapping for CC and ER**.
   - Traces with **SC before both CC and ER**.
   - Traces with **EX before SC and CC/ER in both orders**.
   - Traces with **external component flag = true and no SC event**.
   - Traces with **stocked component flag = true and no SC event**.
   - Traces with **same timestamp for CC and ER but different ingestion indexes**.

These would help distinguish XOR-vs-sequence-vs-concurrency, true optionality vs missingness, and D5 validity.

# 10. Uncertainty Statement

| Decision | Calibrated confidence | Main alternative |
|---|---|---|
| RO start and AR end are mandatory | Very high, ~95–98% | A small number of cases may be aborted outside the log. |
| RP requires AC | Very high, ~95–98% | No material alternative. |
| CC and ER both required before AC | Medium-high, ~75–85% | Some cases may allow approval with only one check due to risk rules. |
| CC and ER are truly concurrent | Low-to-medium, ~40–60% | The completion-order interleavings may reflect sequential work with different priorities. |
| SC is optional | Medium, ~50–70% | SC may be mandatory for external components and missing only because of S5. |
| SC may occur after only one check | Low-to-medium, ~40–60% | SC may actually require both checks, and V5 could be a timestamp/order artifact. |
| EX is optional and after both checks | Medium, ~60–75% | EX may be required for some risk profiles or may occur after only one check in rare cases. |
| Balanced model is the best current candidate | Medium, ~60–70% | If S1 is materially correct despite the observed ER-first traces, the balanced and permissive models overgeneralize. |

A plausible alternative interpretation is that **S1 is correct** and the ER-first variants are due to logging/ingestion order errors or noncompliant cases. If that were true, the balanced model’s allowance of ER before CC would be wrong, and a stricter model with `Register Order -> Credit Check -> Engineering Review` would be preferred. Another material alternative is that **Supplier Confirmation is mandatory for all external-component orders**, and the absence of SC in V3/V4 is primarily integration missingness. Under that interpretation, the optional-SC abstraction in all three models would understate the real process, and the model should condition SC on an external/stocked attribute.

The main uncertainties are therefore not about the approval-release spine, but about the true ordering/overlap of Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, and Expedite Review, and about missingness versus true optionality.
