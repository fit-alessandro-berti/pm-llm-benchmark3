# Process Redesign for Reduced Environmental Impact

## 1. System boundary and assumptions

**Environmental scope (inside boundary)**  
- Direct process energy: machine-group electricity (`energy_kwh`) on CNC_A/B, heat treatment, inspection, and emergency production.  
- Transport emissions: `transport_kgco2e` for parcel_ground, parcel_air, dedicated_van, consolidated_truck from package to confirm-delivery.  
- Material waste: `material_scrap_kg` from production/rework and `package_material_kg` from standard vs reinforced pack and packaging rework.  
- Indirect process waste driven by rework loops, partial shipments (second leg), batch obsolescence (items not shipped within 14 days), and damage-related returns that re-enter the process.

**Outside boundary**  
- Upstream raw-material extraction and primary metal production beyond scrap recorded in the log.  
- Customer-site use-phase and end-of-life of spare parts.  
- Capital equipment embodied carbon and facility HVAC.  
- Carrier vehicle manufacturing and network-level modal shifts not visible in order-level `route_mode`.

**Assumptions and uncertainty**  
- Event-log attributes are complete enough to attribute energy, scrap, and transport at path level; shared machine overhead is allocated per batch_id.  
- Promised lead time and historical route reliability are usable proxies for the 85% on-time probability in SP1.  
- Obsolescence is observed only as “not shipped within 14 days”; true write-off value is uncertain.  
- Empty backhaul of dedicated vans is inferred from path notes, not directly metered.  
- Precision of the discovered model is low under disruption (emergency + partial combinations); effect estimates are directional ranges, not precise LCA figures.  
- Resilience is assessed via alternative-route availability (R1) and Critical-downtime service, not full multi-echelon inventory simulation.

## 2. Sustainability-related process indicators

| Indicator | Definition (from log attributes / paths) | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Energy intensity | Avg `energy_kwh` per order and per item by `machine_group`, `batch_size`, case_type | Track CNC/heat efficiency vs waiting |
| Transport intensity | Avg `transport_kgco2e` per order by `route_mode`, segment, expedite_flag | Detect unjustified air/van use |
| Modal share | % orders on ground / consolidated_truck / air / dedicated_van by customer_type and slack to promise | SP1/SP2 conformance |
| Material scrap rate | Avg `material_scrap_kg` per order; scrap per CNC path; rework_flag rate | Production quality & machine choice |
| Packaging material | Avg `package_material_kg`; reinforced vs standard; rework packaging frequency | Q1 and compatibility |
| Rework loop rate | % cases with rework_production or rework packaging; inspectionrework share | Hidden energy + scrap |
| Batch efficiency | Energy per item vs `batch_size`; Batch wait hours; % batch items unshipped >14 days | Energy–obsolescence trade-off |
| Service level | Late delivery % vs promised_lead_time_hours; median/p90 cycle time by segment | Guardrail |
| Partial-shipment rate | Partial shipment + backorder paths; second-ship transport | Extra legs and contact load |
| Resilience proxy | % Service-contract orders with 1 alternative route retained; Critical true-downtime on-time | R1 and downtime protection |
| Policy conformance | SP1 violation rate; SP2 justification missing; Q1 violation vs return loops | Governance |
| Exception density | Sustainability exception log volume and approved override rate | Controlled flexibility |

## 3. Intervention options

**S1 — Green shipping recommender**  
- Target: All segments at PackageShip; highest leverage on Standard-stock Service-contract (SP1 violated 22%) and MTO with slack.  
- Mechanism: Select ground or consolidated_truck when predicted P(on-time)  85% given promised_lead_time_hours and route reliability; reserve air for insufficient slack.  
- Service/quality/resilience: Protects service if reliability model is calibrated; may slightly raise p90 if reliability overestimated; keeps alternate modes available (R1).  
- Evidence to monitor: route_mode mix vs slack, transport_kgco2e, late % by segment, SP1 conformance, expedite_flag after recommend.

**S4 — Priority pick + ground instead of default air (Standard-stock Service-contract)**  
- Target: Stock  standard pack  air path (~portion of 5% air + Service-contract air bias).  
- Mechanism: Cut 4–6× transport emissions when promise still allows ground; priority pick reduces pick wait.  
- Service/quality/resilience: Median cycle may rise a few hours but within promise if slack-checked; resilience improves by preserving air capacity for true urgency.  
- Evidence: Pre/post air share for Standard-stock Service-contract, cycle time vs promise, late %, transport_kgco2e.

**S5 — Twice-daily consolidated truck for non-urgent stock**  
- Target: Stock  standard pack  ground eligible orders (large Regular stock share).  
- Mechanism: 35–55% lower transport_kgco2e vs individual parcels; pools volume.  
- Service/quality/resilience: Adds 12–24h consolidation wait—apply only when slack  consolidation window + transit; do not use for Critical or tight Service-contract promises.  
- Evidence: consolidated_truck share, wait time in DFG, late %, transport_kgco2e, cancellation due to delay.

**S2 — Dynamic batching with segment-specific wait caps**  
- Target: MTO Plan production  Batch wait  CNC / heat treatment (13% efficient batch path vs rush paths).  
- Mechanism: Prefer batch_size 12–20 on CNC_A and heat >10 where energy per item drops sharply (e.g., CNC_A 2.81.3 kWh/item), but cap Batch wait (e.g., Regular MTO higher cap, Service-contract tighter, Critical bypass).  
- Service/quality/resilience: Reduces energy and can stabilize quality vs tiny batches; excess wait raises late risk (heat wait 1.7× late) and obsolescence (6% unshipped >14d). Caps prevent unacceptable delay.  
- Evidence: batch_size distribution, energy_kWh/item, Batch wait, late %, obsolescence proxy, scrap.

**S3 — Prefer CNC_A over CNC_B when queue and due-date slack allow**  
- Target: MTO CNC paths; especially MTO  CNC_B  rework  ground (5%, 32% late, 32 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap).  
- Mechanism: CNC_B faster but 18% vs 9% rework; shifting to CNC_A cuts rework-driven energy, scrap, and loops when slack exists.  
- Service/quality/resilience: May increase cycle time if CNC_A queued; quality and late risk improve when rework avoided; keep CNC_B for tight due dates.  
- Evidence: machine_group mix, rework_flag, energy_kWh, scrap_kg, cycle time, late % by machine.

**S7 — Inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations**  
- Target: Inspection  Rework production (470) and rushed inspection misses.  
- Mechanism: Stronger gate / sampling on CNC_B and high-risk geometries reduces false-pass defects that later cause rework packaging or returns; cuts hidden scrap and repeat CNC energy.  
- Service/quality/resilience: Adds inspection time; reduces downstream rework cycle and returns; improves quality stability.  
- Evidence: inspectionrework rate, rework_flag, material_scrap_kg, return-related loops, cycle time at inspection_cell.

**S6 — Packaging–route compatibility check**  
- Target: Package standard/reinforced  Rework packaging (260+360) and Q1-related returns.  
- Mechanism: Block incompatible pack/route/geometry before ship; enforce reinforced for MTO >8 kg unless certified (Q1). Cuts packaging rework, double handling, and damage returns (reinforced already cuts damage returns ~40%).  
- Service/quality/resilience: Slight pack-time increase; fewer delays from rework packaging; better quality; material may rise if more reinforced used correctly (trade vs return waste).  
- Evidence: rework packaging frequency, package_material_kg, Q1 conformance, damage returns, late from pack loops.

**S9 + S8 — Split Critical-downtime; shared emergency courier lanes**  
- Target: Critical  emergency production  reinforced  dedicated van (8%, 52 kgCO2e); SP2 missing justification 31%.  
- Mechanism: S9 separates true downtime vs precautionary urgent; only true downtime gets dedicated_van. S8 replaces some vans with shared emergency courier lanes to cut empty returns while protecting service.  
- Service/quality/resilience: True downtime service maintained; precautionary moves to air or priority ground; resilience retained via shared lane capacity rather than exclusive vans; unjustified van use drops.  
- Evidence: case_type subtype, SP2 justification rate, dedicated_van share, transport_kgco2e, Critical late %, alternative route availability.

**S11 — Tighten availability-check to reduce partial shipments**  
- Target: Pick  Partial shipment (1010)  backorder (220); 7% path with 24% late, extra transport.  
- Mechanism: Clearer stock rules / allocate full order or scheduled split with customer promise update; fewer second shipments and cancellations (60 from backorder).  
- Service/quality/resilience: May increase first-ship wait or honest longer promise; reduces late surprise and duplicate transport/pack energy; fewer cancel-due-to-delay.  
- Evidence: partial shipment rate, second-leg transport, late %, cancel due to delay, customer segment OTIF.

**S10 — Carbon-aware promise negotiation (Regular MTO)**  
- Target: Made-to-order Regular (22%, 18% late, high energy).  
- Mechanism: Offer slightly longer promise in exchange for batch-friendly slot + ground; aligns promise with low-energy batch path (16 kWh, 5.8 kgCO2e) vs rush+air (24 kWh, 23 kgCO2e).  
- Service/quality/resilience: Service measured against negotiated promise improves; not for Service-contract/Critical; resilience unchanged if capacity reserved.  
- Evidence: promised_lead_time_hours changes, batch_size, route_mode, late vs promise, energy and transport for Regular MTO.

*(S12 supports governance across all; covered in section 7.)*

## 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching energy vs obsolescence and late delivery**  
   Larger CNC/heat batches cut kWh/item but raise Batch wait (up to ~38h), heat-related 1.7× late risk, and ~6% items unshipped >14 days—displacing impact into scrap, write-off, and expedited replacement orders (air/van).

2. **Ground/consolidate vs service failure expedites**  
   Aggressive SP1 ground or S5 consolidation can increase late deliveries, triggering express courier, emergency production, or cancellations—rebounding to higher transport_kgco2e and energy than the original air shipment.

3. **Reinforced packaging material vs damage returns**  
   Enforcing Q1 increases `package_material_kg` (reinforced doubles material). Under-enforcement increases damage returns, rework packaging, and reship emissions. Net benefit only if return reduction dominates.

4. **CNC_A shift overload**  
   Moving volume from CNC_B to CNC_A reduces rework rate but can queue CNC_A, pushing due-date risk, emergency production paths, and selective return to CNC_B under time pressure—displacing quality gains.

5. **Critical van reduction without capacity design**  
   Cutting dedicated vans (S8/S9) without sized shared emergency lanes or clear true-downtime criteria risks downtime events, premium ad-hoc transport, and reputation-driven over-expediting of precautionary orders.

6. **Partial-shipment suppression  hidden inventory or longer promises**  
   Stricter availability rules may increase safety stock (obsolescence/energy in storage handling) or longer quoted leads; customers may split orders themselves, recreating multiple shipments outside controlled batching.

7. **Inspection gate queues**  
   Heavier inspection reduces missed defects but can recreate inspection_cell queues that previously caused rushed inspections—or push work to bypass paths during peaks, displacing rework downstream.

8. **Customer behavior / promise negotiation**  
   Carbon-aware longer promises (S10) for Regular MTO may drive customers to reclassify as Service-contract or inflate urgency, increasing air and van demand (segment leakage).

9. **Empty backhaul and shared lane fill**  
   Shared emergency lanes only win if fill rates improve; poor scheduling can retain high emissions per part while adding coordination delay.

## 6. Balanced recommendation

Implement a **gated green-operations package** that couples mode choice, machine/batch discipline, and quality gates—not a pure “lowest emission” redesign.

**Core package**  
1. **Shipping control (S1 + S4 + S5 + SP1/SP2 enforcement)**  
   - Deploy green shipping recommender at PackageShip using promised_lead_time_hours, route reliability, and segment.  
   - Default Standard-stock Service-contract to priority pick + ground when slack supports 85% on-time; air only on slack failure.  
   - Offer twice-daily consolidated truck only for Regular non-urgent stock with explicit slack  consolidation window.  
   - Retain air and dedicated capacity for true Critical and tight Service-contract promises (do not flatten all premium transport).

2. **Critical segmentation (S9 + S8 + SP2)**  
   - Split Critical-downtime into true downtime vs precautionary urgent.  
   - Dedicated van only with justification event (SP2); otherwise shared emergency courier lane or air.  
   - Preserves resilience (R1) while cutting empty-van emissions on the 8% ultra-high transport path.

3. **Production energy and scrap (S2 + S3 + S7)**  
   - Dynamic batching toward CNC_A batch 12–20 and heat >10 with **hard wait caps by segment** (bypass/caps tight for Service-contract; batch-friendly for Regular MTO via S10 promises).  
   - Prefer CNC_A when due-date slack and queue allow; reserve CNC_B for tight dates.  
   - Inspection quality gate on high-rework combinations to break inspectionreworkCNC loops (high energy/scrap path).

4. **Packaging and fulfillment integrity (S6 + Q1 + S11)**  
   - Mandatory pack–route–geometry check; reinforced for MTO >8 kg unless certified.  
   - Reduce partial shipments via availability-check rules and honest promise updates to eliminate high-late double-ship path.

5. **Commercial alignment (S10 selective)**  
   - Carbon-aware promise negotiation only for Regular MTO; never trade away Service-contract or true Critical protection.

**Why this balances**  
- Largest unjustified transport hotspots (Std SC air, non-justified vans, avoidable parcel vs consolidate) are cut with slack gates, so service is not sacrificed where the log shows promise headroom.  
- Energy and scrap gains come from fewer rework loops and better batching, not from forcing all MTO into long waits.  
- Quality improves via CNC_A preference, inspection gates, and packaging compatibility—addressing the 1.8 kg scrap / 32% late rework path.  
- Resilience is explicit: alternate routes retained, Critical true-downtime still fast, air/van capacity reserved rather than abolished.  
- Obsolescence and rebound are controlled with batch wait caps, segment rules, and exception logging rather than maximizing batch size or minimizing modal carbon alone.

## 7. Validation and governance plan

**Post-implementation event-log validation**  
- Re-mine DFG and path performance tables monthly for the same variants (stock-ground, stock-air, MTO batch-CNC_A-heat-ground, MTO CNC_B-rework, Critical-van, partial-shipment).  
- Compare segment dashboards to baseline: energy_kWh/order, transport_kgco2e, material_scrap_kg, package_material_kg, rework_flag rate, median/p90 cycle time, late delivery %, cancel-due-to-delay, partial-shipment rate, obsolescence proxy (% batch items not shipped 14 days).  
- A/B or stepped rollout: enable S4/S5/S1 by lane or warehouse; enable S3/S2 on selected part families; hold Critical S9/S8 behind stricter pilot KPIs (true-downtime OTIF  baseline).

**Conformance monitoring**  
- **SP1:** % orders with ground/consolidated when predicted on-time 85%; violation rate on Standard-stock Service-contract (baseline 22%) target sharp reduction; every air/van ship stores recommender score + override reason.  
- **SP2:** dedicated_van without justification event (baseline 31%)  near-zero; justification linked to true-downtime flag.  
- **Q1:** MTO >8 kg without reinforced or certification; correlate violations with return/rework loops.  
- **R1:** Service-contract orders retaining 1 alternative route during disruption drills or carrier outages.  
- Precision/drift: track fitness of discovery model under disruption; alert when emergency+partial combinations spike.

**Segment-level service and sustainability guards**  
- Hard guardrails: Critical true-downtime late % and Service-contract late % must not worsen beyond agreed tolerance (e.g., +1 pp) or auto-rollback mode restrictions.  
- Regular MTO: optimize energy/transport subject to late % vs **negotiated** promise.  
- Stock Regular: maximize consolidate/ground share subject to cancel and late bounds.

**Exception governance (S12)**  
- Sustainability exception log mandatory for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides: user, segment, slack, reason code, expiry.  
- Weekly review of top override reasons; feed into recommender thresholds and batch caps.  
- Forbidden silent overrides; random audit of Critical van and air on Standard-stock SC.  
- Continuous alignment: if rework or obsolescence rises after batching changes, tighten caps before adding capacity expedites.

**Decision rule for scale-up**  
Scale an intervention only if environmental indicators improve **and** late-delivery, rework/return, and resilience proxies stay within agreed bands for each segment—accepting that the lowest-emission path on paper is rejected when it violates Critical service, R1, or drives rework/obsolescence rebound.
