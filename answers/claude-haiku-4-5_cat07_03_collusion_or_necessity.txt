# 2. Benign alternative explanations

## Pattern A: Four-eyes violation – Create PO + Approve PO (Buyer_Carla + Approver_Gita)

**Benign explanation 1: Emergency handling with explicit exception process**
- Emergency medical supplies have hard deadlines (patient care, regulatory compliance). Carla may be trained to fast-track, and Gita may be a delegated approver for urgent cases (role notes: "delegated approver for urgent cases").
- 88% emergency_flag=True and consistent 8-minute turnaround suggests this is a known operational pattern, not concealment.
- **Supporting evidence:** (a) Formal approval authority documented in access-control system for Gita; (b) business justification in case notes (e.g., "life safety delay risk"); (c) no late retrospective justifications for emergency cases; (d) pattern visible for 12+ months with no escalation.
- **Refuting evidence:** (a) retrospective justifications missing or delayed (18% late); (b) Gita approval authority not formally delegated in role database; (c) no case notes; (d) pattern emerged recently (last 2–3 weeks).

**Benign explanation 2: Data-quality artifact – shared account or bot mapping**
- If "Approver_Gita" is a shared account and Carla's PO actually created by Bot_PO (misattributed due to legacy_erp mapping error), then there is no human-to-human four-eyes violation.
- Carla is a buyer; Bot_PO creates many POs. If Bot_PO creation is logged as Gita due to mapping error, the violation is a false positive.
- **Supporting evidence:** (a) legacy_erp mapping error documented in data-quality flags; (b) cross-check of detailed audit trail (entered_by field) vs. org:resource field shows discrepancies; (c) Bot_PO handles 1730 distinct cases and may interact with multiple approvers.
- **Refuting evidence:** (a) detailed audit trail shows Carla (not bot) as entered_by for most PO creates; (b) mapping error is random, not correlated with Carla/Gita pair.

## Pattern B: Four-eyes violation – Change Vendor Bank Details + Payment Approval (VendorMaster_Lena + AP_Kim)

**Benign explanation 1: Legitimate bulk vendor master maintenance, miscoded as individual cases**
- Vendor family V-77x recently migrated banking platforms. Bank-detail changes may have been a single maintenance ticket executed by Lena, but the event log records each procurement case separately rather than linking them to the maintenance ticket.
- All 9 cases involve the same vendor family, 83% manual_upload source, and data-quality flags note "missing linkage to maintenance ticket."
- **Supporting evidence:** (a) Lena's case notes or work orders reference vendor platform migration; (b) all 9 bank-change dates cluster within a 1–2 week period (maintenance window); (c) maintenance ticket exists in separate system and shows Lena as executor; (d) no payment approvals by Kim occurred *before* the maintenance window; (e) amount_band is high (68%) but consistent with normal routine for V-77x.
- **Refuting evidence:** (a) bank changes spread over several months, not a maintenance window; (b) no maintenance ticket found; (c) some cases involve different vendor accounts (not all V-77x).

**Benign explanation 2: Operational specialization for high-risk vendor family**
- AP_Kim is noted as handling "higher-risk suppliers." Vendor family V-77x may be flagged as higher-risk due to complexity, international transfers, or prior issues. Lena and Kim may be the designated pair for this family.
- 72% vendor concentration is extreme, but if it reflects business design (dedicated team for one high-risk group), it is not collusion—it is role specialization.
- **Supporting evidence:** (a) formal work-assignment system documents Kim as primary approver for V-77x; (b) V-77x has compliance notes or risk flags; (c) Lena/Kim pair handles V-77x for 6+ months with no escalation; (d) payment-approval details show legitimate compliance checks.
- **Refuting evidence:** (a) no documented risk flag for V-77x; (b) other approvers (Iris, Jonas) also handle V-77x cases without the fast-approval pattern; (c) vendor family designation is ad-hoc.

## Pattern C: Four-eyes violation – Emergency Override + Retrospective Justification by same person (Manager_Hugo, Approver_Gita)

**Benign explanation 1: Data migration from spreadsheet, not real-time same-person approval**
- 12 of 17 cases have identical timestamps (to the minute) and were batch-entered after quarter-close-upload from a spreadsheet.
- The actual approvals may have occurred at different times (contemporaneously with the override), but the retrospective justifications were entered together in a post-quarter reconciliation batch.
- Real timing is in the original spreadsheet or Hugo/Gita's email; event log timestamps are artifacts of the migration process.
- **Supporting evidence:** (a) source_system="quarter_close_upload" for batch; (b) spreadsheet file exists and shows original timestamps; (c) Hugo/Gita emails or case notes show real approval sequence; (d) the 5 non-batch cases (non-identical timestamps) comply with the three-day rule.
- **Refuting evidence:** (a) original spreadsheet lost or timestamps invalid; (b) non-batch cases also violate the rule; (c) case notes are post-hoc.

**Benign explanation 2: Legitimate emergency exception with aggressive but compliant timing**
- The three-day rule allows retrospective justification by a *different* resource. If Hugo overrides on Day 1 and Gita justifies on Day 2 (within the window), it complies—but if both logged as same org:resource due to shared account or role overlap, it appears as violation.
- Alternatively, Hugo may be empowered to both override and justify for his own exceptions; the rule may be interpreted differently in his role (e.g., manager self-review for internal repairs is acceptable).
- **Supporting evidence:** (a) Hugo's role documentation permits self-justification for internal exceptions; (b) actual entered_by field shows different users (Hugo and Gita or assistant) even if org:resource is the same; (c) business notes justify timing as acceptable.
- **Refuting evidence:** (a) rule is unambiguous: different *human* resource required; (b) entered_by field shows Hugo for both override and justification; (c) rule waivers are not documented.

## Pattern D: Repeated subcontracting loops (Approver_Gita  Buyer_Carla, etc.)

**Benign explanation 1: Normal workflow clarification and rework**
- Approver Gita rejects or requests changes on PO (Carla  Gita). Carla revises and resubmits (Gita  Carla). This is standard approval workflow, not suspicious.
- Emergency cases are likely to require rework due to incomplete information or fast-track entry.
- 36 cycles over 126 cases (29%) is high but plausible for a high-volume emergency channel with time pressure.
- **Supporting evidence:** (a) case notes show "revision requested," "provide additional quote," etc.; (b) approval rejection codes and comments are documented; (c) loop frequency declines over time (process maturation); (d) final approval is by a different human than the request originator.
- **Refuting evidence:** (a) no notes in case; (b) loops occur without timestamp gaps (instantaneous back-and-forth, suggesting rubber-stamping); (c) loop frequency increasing over time.

**Benign explanation 2: Managerial review and escalation (Hugo  Dan)**
- Dan may routinely escalate weekend repairs to Hugo, who reviews and delegates back for execution. This is a normal handover pattern in hierarchical organizations.
- 29 cycles over 104 Dan/Hugo cases (28%) reflects expected management overhead for urgent operational decisions.
- **Supporting evidence:** (a) org structure diagram shows Hugo as Dan's manager; (b) case notes indicate "manager approval required" for weekend; (c) turnaround times are consistent with management availability; (d) final execution is by Dan (the specialist).
- **Refuting evidence:** (a) Hugo and Dan are peers, not manager/report; (b) case notes lack escalation justification; (c) loops occur at odd times suggesting artificial workflow manipulation.

## Pattern E: Vendor concentration and manual-source concentration (VendorMaster_Lena + AP_Kim)

**Benign explanation 1: High-risk vendor requiring specialized handling**
- Vendor family V-77x is a high-value, high-complexity supplier (international, regulatory compliance, frequent bank changes). Lena and Kim are the designated specialists.
- Manual handling may reflect custom audit requirements or integration with external banking systems that are not automated.
- 83% manual_upload is high, but appropriate if V-77x requires certified documentation or custom compliance checks.
- **Supporting evidence:** (a) V-77x is multi-country or multi-legal-entity; (b) audit or compliance requirements documented; (c) Kim's role includes manual approval for V-77x; (d) automation feasibility study exists and concluded manual process is required.
- **Refuting evidence:** (a) other high-value vendors are handled automatically; (b) no compliance requirement for V-77x; (c) Lena/Kim manual process predates vendor risk assessment.

**Benign explanation 2: Transition to new vendor or temporary operational disruption**
- V-77x may have recently onboarded or undergone system integration. Until automation is complete, Lena and Kim are handling a temporary backlog.
- 44 cases over one quarter is modest volume; may dissipate once integration is complete.
- **Supporting evidence:** (a) V-77x onboarded in last 6 months; (b) automation project in progress; (c) timeline shows Lena/Kim concentration is recent (not 12+ months); (d) business case for automation exists.
- **Refuting evidence:** (a) V-77x is a long-standing vendor; (b) no automation project; (c) Lena/Kim concentration is static over many quarters.

## 4.1 PM4Py filtering and discovery: Four-eyes violations

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.algo.filtering.log import filter_four_eyes_principle

# Load and format
log_df = pd.read_csv('event_log.csv')
log_df['time:timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(log_df['time:timestamp'])
log_df = log_df.sort_values(['case:concept:name', 'time:timestamp'])

# Standardize and format for pm4py
log_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    log_df,
    case_id='case:concept:name',
    activity_key='concept:name',
    timestamp_key='time:timestamp',
    resource_key='org:resource'
)

# Apply four-eyes principle for Create PO + Approve PO
log_four_eyes = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
    log_df,
    activity_a='Create Purchase Order',
    activity_b='Approve Purchase Order',
    resource_key='org:resource'
)

# Separate violations (same resource) from compliant (different resource)
violations = log_four_eyes[log_four_eyes['resource_a'] == log_four_eyes['resource_b']]
compliant = log_four_eyes[log_four_eyes['resource_a'] != log_four_eyes['resource_b']]

# Analyze by resource pair
violation_summary = violations.groupby(['resource_a', 'resource_b']).agg({
    'case:concept:name': 'count',
    'emergency_flag': 'sum',
    'amount_band': lambda x: (x.isin(['high'])).sum()
}).rename(columns={'case:concept:name': 'violation_count'})
print("Four-eyes violations (Create PO + Approve PO):")
print(violation_summary)

# Apply for Change Vendor Bank Details + Payment Approval
log_bank_payment = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
    log_df,
    activity_a='Change Vendor Bank Details',
    activity_b='Payment Approval',
    resource_key='org:resource'
)
bank_violations = log_bank_payment[log_bank_payment['resource_a'] == log_bank_payment['resource_b']]
print("\nFour-eyes violations (Bank Details + Payment):")
print(bank_violations[['case:concept:name', 'resource_a', 'timestamp_a', 'timestamp_b', 'vendor_id']].head(20))
```

## 4.3 Subcontracting network (bidirectional loops)

from pm4py.algo.discovery.subcontracting import algorithm as subcontracting

# Discover subcontracting network (A assigns work to B who then assigns back to A)

## 4.4 Comparative timing analysis: Emergency vs. routine

```python
# Filter emergency and non-emergency subsets
emergency = log_df[log_df['emergency_flag'] == True]
routine = log_df[log_df['emergency_flag'] == False]

# Path-performance analysis: Create PO  Approve PO
def analyze_path_timing(log_subset, activity_a, activity_b, label):
    """Compute median time between activity_a and activity_b for each resource pair."""
    paired = []
    for case_id in log_subset['case:concept:name'].unique():
        case_log = log_subset[log_subset['case:concept:name'] == case_id].sort_values('time:timestamp')
        activities = case_log[['concept:name', 'org:resource', 'time:timestamp']].values
        for i, (act_i, res_i, ts_i) in enumerate(activities):
            if act_i == activity_a:
                for j in range(i+1, len(activities)):
                    act_j, res_j, ts_j = activities[j]
                    if act_j == activity_b:
                        duration = (ts_j - ts_i).total_seconds() / 3600  # hours
                        paired.append({'resource_a': res_i, 'resource_b': res_j, 'duration_hours': duration})
                        break
    if paired:
        paired_df = pd.DataFrame(paired)
        print(f"\n{label} – Create PO  Approve PO timing:")
        grouped = paired_df.groupby(['resource_a', 'resource_b'])['duration_hours'].agg(['median', 'count', 'min', 'max'])
        print(grouped.sort_values('median'))
    return paired_df if paired else None

emergency_timing = analyze_path_timing(emergency, 'Create Purchase Order', 'Approve Purchase Order', 'Emergency cases')
routine_timing = analyze_path_timing(routine, 'Create Purchase Order', 'Approve Purchase Order', 'Routine cases')

# Global comparison
all_timing = pd.concat([emergency_timing, routine_timing], ignore_index=True, keys=['emergency', 'routine'])
print("\nGlobal median (Create PO  Approve PO):", all_timing['duration_hours'].median(), "hours")
```

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

## Internal Audit Report: Procurement-to-Payment Control Assessment

**Executive Summary**

This audit reviewed 2,348 procurement cases from the last quarter, focusing on four-eyes principle compliance, approval timing, and resource specialization patterns. We identified four resource pairs and one activity cluster with control exceptions or anomalies that warrant targeted follow-up, but found no direct evidence of fraud or collusion. The findings reflect a combination of likely benign explanations (emergency handling, role specialization, data-quality artifacts) alongside some genuine control gaps that require remediation.

**Key Findings**

1. **Four-eyes violations: 42 PO-approval cases (1.8% of all cases) and 9 vendor-bank-change cases (0.4%)**. The violations are concentrated in three subgroups: (i) Buyer Carla + Approver Gita, with 88% emergency flagging; (ii) Buyer Dan + Manager Hugo, with 79% emergency flagging; and (iii) VendorMaster Lena + AP Kim, all involving vendor family V-77x. The high proportion of emergency cases suggests these may be authorized exceptions, but formal documentation of authority and emergency protocols should be reviewed.

2. **Approval timing anomalies**. Carla/Gita and Dan/Hugo cases show median approval times of 8–10 minutes (40–50× the global baseline of 2.1 hours), consistent with expedited emergency handling. Lena/Kim bank-change-to-payment approvals average 17 minutes (28× the 7.8-hour baseline). While emergency cases justify faster processing, the speed limits substantive review and should be mitigated by compensating controls (e.g., supervisory sampling, post-approval reconciliation).

3. **Vendor and process concentration in Lena/Kim subgroup**. Seventy-two percent of Lena/Kim cases (n=44) involve vendor family V-77x, 83% are manual uploads, and four-eyes violations occur in 9 cases. The data-quality flag notes a missing linkage to a vendor platform-migration maintenance ticket. If the bank-change cases are part of a legitimate bulk-maintenance operation, they should be linked and documented; if not, they warrant further scrutiny.

4. **Data-quality artifacts masking true approval timing**. Twelve of 17 same-person emergency-override-and-justification violations have identical timestamps and were batch-entered from a spreadsheet during quarter-close migration. Real approval timing is unknown; source documents (original spreadsheet or emails) should be examined to confirm whether the three-day rule was actually violated.

5. **Shared-account and system-mapping ambiguity**. Approver_Gita's account is shared (handled 260 events but only 248 distinct cases); 23 emergency approvals show entered_by values for assistants. Bot_PO creation events are sometimes misattributed to human approvers due to a legacy_erp mapping-table error. These issues create false positives in four-eyes analysis and obscure true accountability.

**Risk Assessment**

- **High concern**: VendorMaster Lena + AP Kim subgroup (vendor concentration, manual processing, fast approval, four-eyes violation, missing documentation). Requires investigation to confirm legitimacy of V-77x handling.
- **Medium concern**: Buyer Carla + Approver Gita (88% emergency cases, fast approval, four-eyes violations, 18% late retrospective justifications). Requires review of emergency-authority documentation and case justifications.
- **Medium concern**: Emergency-override retrospective-justification batch entries (12 of 17 same-person approvals due to data migration). Real timing must be reconciled with source documents.
- **Lower concern**: Buyer Dan + Manager Hugo (likely legitimate managerial escalation for weekend repairs, but should confirm Hugo's formal approval authority for Dan's cases).

**Recommendations**

1. Retrieve and review formal role documentation, delegation memos, and emergency-approval authorities for Gita, Hugo, Lena, and Kim. Confirm that same-person approvals in flagged cases are authorized exceptions.
2. For Lena/Kim subgroup: locate the V-77x platform-migration maintenance ticket and reconcile the 9 bank-change cases with the maintenance scope and timeline. If no ticket exists, conduct detailed case review for legitimate business justification.
3. For batch-entry retrospective justifications: retrieve the original spreadsheet or email chain to confirm actual approval dates and recompute compliance with the three-day rule.
4. Implement compensating controls for expedited emergency cases (Carla/Gita, Dan/Hugo): require supervisory sampling of a random 10% of cases within 5 business days, and quarterly retrospective review by a manager independent of the approver.
5. Correct the legacy_erp Bot_PO mapping-table error to ensure accurate attribution of create-PO events. Recompute four-eyes violations to quantify the false-positive count.
6. Formalize approval-authority matrix and emergency-protocol policy, and digitize in the ERP system to support automated compliance monitoring in future quarters.

**Limitations**

This assessment is constrained by incomplete audit-trail data (missing entered_by details for some events, loss of original source documents post-migration, missing linkages to external systems such as vendor master and incident tracking). The event log does not capture email approvals, verbal authorizations, or access logs. Conclusions are based on event timing and resource pairs, which are indirect proxies for control effectiveness. Follow-up investigation should include document review, interviews, and cross-system validation.
