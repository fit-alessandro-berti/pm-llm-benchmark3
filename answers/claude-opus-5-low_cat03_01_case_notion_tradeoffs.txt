## 1. Analytical assumptions

**Case notion.** A case notion here is a *projection function* that maps each event to zero, one, or many cases by looking at the event's direct E2O relation set. Flattening on object type T creates one case per object of type T, and assigns event e to case o iff (o, e) is in the E2O relation. Nothing else. This means a case notion is not a "view of the truth" but a lossy encoding: it decides which co-occurrences become *sequence* (visible as DFG edges) and which become *nothing at all*.

**Convergence.** One event is related to several objects of the chosen type, so the event is replicated across several cases. In this log convergence is not hypothetical: e13 relates to O2 and O3; e16 relates to O1, O2 and O3; e19/e22/e25 each relate to O2 and O3; e14/e17/e27 each relate to O3 and O4. Under Order flattening the 31 events expand to 41 case-events (O1: 12, O2: 9, O3: 13, O4: 7), i.e. ten replicated event instances.

**Divergence.** One case contains several events of the same activity that belong to *different* sub-threads, and flattening serialises them into a spurious ordering. O1 is the canonical example: e11 (P1) and e12 (P2) are two independent packing threads, but the Order-flattened trace shows `Pack Package → Pack Package`, then `Dispatch → Dispatch` (e15, e16), then `Confirm Delivery → Confirm Delivery` (e23, e24). O3 diverges too, across P3 (e13) and P4 (e14).

**Are copied shared events evidence or duplication?** Both, and the distinction is *metric-dependent*, not ontological. For control-flow *reachability* questions ("can an order be dispatched before it is invoiced?") the copy is legitimate evidence: e16 genuinely happened to O1, O2 and O3. For *counting* questions ("how many dispatches did we perform?") the copy is pure duplication: Order flattening reports 6 Dispatch case-events for 3 real dispatch operations. For *performance* questions the copy is worse than duplication — it is contamination, because the same duration is attributed to three orders whose contribution to that duration is unequal. I therefore treat copies as evidence for existence/ordering claims and as duplication for any frequency, cost, or resource-load claim, and I require every reported number to be labelled with its counting basis (events / unique objects / total objects).

**Permitted transformations.** (a) Direct E2O only, as given — this is the default and the baseline for every number I quote. (b) An *explicitly labelled* propagated variant, e.g. propagating Invoice- and Payment-related events to Items via `Order contains Item` (so I11 would inherit e18, e21, e28). I permit building it as a **contrast log**, never as the primary log, because propagation manufactures convergence: e19 would be copied to I21, I31, I32 and e22/e25 likewise, turning the currently clean Item log into a convergence-heavy one. (c) Deriving an Order↔Package↔Delivery bridge from O2O purely for *annotation* (marking P3 as "cross-order") rather than for event assignment. (d) Down-weighting shared events by 1/|related objects of the lead type| for load metrics.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

**D1 — Cross-order package P3 inflates packing volume (Order flattening).** e13 relates to O2 and O3, so the Order log contains two `Pack Package` case-events at 10:12 for one physical packing operation. Any capacity model built on the Order log will believe the packing station handled 6 packing operations (e11, e12, e13×2, e14×2) instead of 4. **Harmful** for resource/cost analytics; **informative** if the question is "how many orders were touched by packing", which is a different question that must be named as such.

**D2 — Cross-order package P4 creates a false `Pack Package → Pack Package` rework variant for O3 (Order flattening).** O3's trace contains e13 (10:12, P3) and e14 (10:20, P4). A variant reader sees repeated packing 8 minutes apart and will hypothesise repacking or a packing error. The ground truth is a *split shipment*: I31 went into P3 with O2's item, I32 into P4 with O4's item. **Harmful**, and specifically dangerous because it mimics a real quality-failure signature. The same trap hits O1 (e11/e12, 10 minutes apart) where the cause is again a split, not rework.

**D3 — Consolidated delivery D2 contaminates three orders' transport performance (Order and Item flattening).** e16 (11:05) and e24 (16:00) are copied into O1, O2 and O3, and into I12, I21 and I31. All three orders therefore report an identical 4h55 dispatch-to-delivery leg. But the 4h55 is a property of D2's route (which had to serve P2 and P3), not of any single order. O2, whose only shipment rode on a truck routed for O1's second package, is charged for a delay it did not cause. **Harmful for attribution**, **informative for diagnosis**: the fact that identical durations recur across unrelated orders is itself the fingerprint of consolidation, and can be used as a detector.

**D4 — Consolidated invoice V2 double-counts billing events (Order flattening).** e19 is copied to O2 and O3, so the Order log shows 4 `Issue Invoice` events for 3 invoices. Summing invoiced amounts per order-case would double-count V2 unless amounts are pre-allocated. **Harmful** for any financial aggregate; the Invoice flattening (3 cases, 3 issue events) is the correct basis for invoice counts.

**D5 — Partial payments look like payment failure (Order flattening).** O2 and O3 each show `Record Payment` twice — e22 (12:40, M2) and e25 (next day 09:00, M3) — separated by 20h20. Read on the Order log this is a "payment repeated after ~20h" variant, the classic dunning/failed-payment signature. The O2O qualifiers say otherwise: M2 `partially_settles` V2, M3 `completes_settlement` V2. **Harmful without qualifiers, informative with them.** Note also that in the *Payment* flattening the instalment relationship vanishes completely (M2 and M3 are separate length-1 cases), so the one thing the Order log distorts is the one thing the Payment log deletes; only the Invoice log states it correctly.

**D6 — Payment flattening is a degenerate near-singleton (Payment flattening).** M1, M2, M3, M4 each relate to exactly one event (e21, e22, e25, e26). Every case has length 1, every throughput time is 0, the DFG has no edges, and log-skeleton constraints collapse to trivia. Any tool that renders this will render something that *looks* like a valid model. **Harmful as a case notion; the correct use of Payment is as an attribute/dimension on Invoice cases.**

**D7 — Delivery flattening is a two-event lifecycle that hides its own explanatory variable (Delivery flattening).** D1/D2/D3 give clean legs of 3h00, 4h55 and 22h45. The tempting conclusion "D3 is our worst route" ignores that D3's confirmation (e27) crosses a calendar boundary into 2026-02-04 10:00 — plausibly a next-business-day effect, and that D3 carries only P4. With n=3 and no explanatory attributes in the case, this flattening supports *measurement* but not *inference*. **Context-dependent**: fine for an SLA dashboard, unsafe for root-cause claims.

**D8 — Item flattening truncates the lifecycle at Confirm Delivery (Item flattening).** All six items have the identical variant Add Item → Pack Package → Dispatch → Confirm Delivery. It is tempting to declare Item the "clean" case notion. But no item is related to e01–e04, e18–e20, e21/e22/e25/e26, or e28–e31: 15 of the 31 events, and the entire order-to-cash half of the process, are simply absent. Perfect conformance here is an artefact of amputation, not of process discipline. **Harmful if mistaken for completeness; highly informative for fulfilment throughput.**

**D9 — Start-time bias across all non-Order flattenings.** Only Order cases begin at Create Order (e01 09:00 … e04 09:06). Item cases begin at Add Item (e05 09:15 for I11), Package cases at Pack (e11 10:00), Delivery cases at Dispatch. Comparing "cycle time" across flattenings therefore compares different clocks; the Item log understates end-to-end time by the order-entry lag (15–25 min here) and by the entire post-delivery billing tail. **Harmful whenever numbers from two flattenings are placed in one chart.**

**D10 — P2 and P3 become artificially identical (Package flattening).** Because both are carried by D2, they share e16 and e24 verbatim. Their traces differ only in the Pack timestamp (10:10 vs 10:12), so clustering or variant analysis will merge them and report a "dominant consolidated-package pattern". This is real, but its apparent statistical weight (2 of 4 packages) is a consequence of one truck, not of two independent observations. **Context-dependent**; must be corrected by counting distinct deliveries, not packages, when assessing transport variability.

**D11 — Simultaneous Close Order events create arbitrary ordering (Order flattening, cross-case).** e29 and e30 both occur at 2026-02-04 11:05. Any global ordering — and hence any inter-case or handover analysis — is decided by tie-breaking, not by the process. **Harmful for sequence claims, negligible for per-case analysis.**

**D12 — Propagation would destroy the Item log's cleanliness.** If Invoice/Payment events were propagated to Items through `Order contains Item`, e19 would be copied to I21, I31, I32 and e22/e25 likewise; I31 and I32 (both from O3) would each acquire the full billing tail. The Item log would gain length but acquire exactly the convergence and divergence it currently lacks — and O3's items would show two Record Payments. **Harmful if done silently; informative only as an explicitly labelled contrast.**

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

**Flattened logs I would still create: four of the six.**

- **Item log** — safe for: picking-to-packing latency (e05→e11 = 45 min for I11), packing-to-dispatch handover, and per-unit fulfilment variant conformance. It is the only flattening with zero convergence and zero divergence, so its DFG edges are literally true. Never use it for lead time or for anything financial.
- **Package log** — safe for: dock/handover performance per package and packing-station sequencing. Must be read with the caveat that P2/P3 share e16/e24 (D10) and that P3/P4 are cross-order (annotate from O2O, do not propagate).
- **Delivery log** — safe for: carrier leg SLA (the three durations above) and nothing else. Report it as a metric table, not a process model; a 1-edge DFG is not a discovery result.
- **Invoice log** — safe for: days-sales-outstanding, instalment counts, and issue-to-settlement latency (V1: 12:00→12:30; V2: 12:05→next-day 09:00; V3: 12:10→next-day 09:30). It is the only view where the M2/M3 instalment sequence is a real edge.
- **Order log** — create it, but as a *diagnostic* artefact with divergence explicitly flagged, not as the reporting baseline. It is the only flattening covering all 8 activities, and it is the only one that can answer "is invoicing ever issued before dispatch?" (it is not: e18 at 12:00 follows e15 at 11:00).
- **Payment log** — do **not** create it as a case log (D6). Payments enter analysis as attributes of Invoice cases.

**Questions that require object-centric structures.**

- *"How much of an order's cycle time is actually caused by other orders sharing its package or truck?"* — needs the **OC-DFG** with per-object-type edges, so the D2 leg is one edge annotated with 3 orders rather than three edges.
- *"Which object types actually interact, and how often?"* — **object interaction summary** and the **OTG**: it will show Order–Order interaction (via e13, e14, e16, e17, e19, e22, e24, e25, e27) which no flattening can express, and which is the formal statement of "we consolidate".
- *"Which activities touch which object types?"* — **ET–OT graph**: it shows immediately that Payment participates in exactly one activity (Record Payment) and Delivery in exactly two (Dispatch, Confirm Delivery), which is the structural reason those flattenings are near-singletons (D6, D7). This is the cheapest way to *predict* lifecycle truncation before flattening.
- *"Are O2 and O3 structurally the same execution?"* — **equivalent-execution / connected-component clustering**, which will show that O2, O3 and O4 are not separable: O2–O3 are linked by P3 and V2, O3–O4 by P4. O1 is linked to O2/O3 only by D2.
- *"How many real dispatch operations occurred?"* — OC-DFG with *event*-based frequency (3), contrasted against *unique-object* frequency (5 order-participations… precisely: O1, O2, O3 on e16; O1 on e15; O3, O4 on e17).

**Reconciling contradictions.** Rule of precedence: (1) counts of *operations* come from the object type that the operation is intrinsically about (dispatch counts from Delivery/Package, invoice counts from Invoice, payment counts from Payment-as-attribute) — never from Order; (2) counts of *affected commercial entities* come from Order; (3) whenever a flattened finding and an OC finding disagree, the OC view is the arbiter of *structure* and the flattened view is retained only as the explanation of *why the KPI looked that way*. Concretely: if the Order log reports "6 dispatches" and the OC-DFG reports "3", the reconciliation statement is "3 dispatch operations affecting 6 order-participations, because D2 consolidated three orders (e16)". Every contradiction should terminate in a sentence of that form, naming the shared event.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Position B: a primary lead object (Order) plus explicitly secondary lifecycles (Package/Delivery for fulfilment, Invoice for order-to-cash).**

**Decision criteria.** (i) *Activity coverage*: does the type reach all 8 activities? (ii) *Semantic ownership*: is the type the entity whose completion the business actually cares about? (iii) *Distortion that is diagnosable vs. distortion that is silent*: I prefer a type whose distortions are visible and correctable over one whose losses are invisible. (iv) *Reconstructibility*: can the missing structure be recovered from O2O and companion logs?

**Strongest supporting evidence.** Order is the only type related to Create Order (e01–e04) and Close Order (e28–e31), and it is related to every one of the 31 events at least once — no other type reaches even half of them (Item: 16 events, Package: 10, Invoice: 5, Delivery: 6, Payment: 4). Its distortions are *enumerable and named*: exactly ten replicated event instances (e13, e14, e16×2, e17, e19, e22, e24×2, e25, e27), each traceable to a specific consolidation (P3, P4, D2, V2). Meanwhile Item's and Package's cleanliness is bought by deleting the entire financial half of the process — a silent loss, and the worse failure mode. Note explicitly that I am **not** choosing Order because its traces are longest; O3's 13 events include 3 duplicates, and length is precisely the symptom of convergence, not a virtue.

**Strongest counterargument.** Order fails criterion (iii) on divergence, not convergence. Convergence is correctable by counting rules; divergence corrupts the *model shape* itself. Under Order flattening, three of the four orders (O1, O3, and arguably O2 via its double payment) exhibit spurious self-loops, so the discovered model for a 4-case log is already wrong in 3 cases. A purist would say: use only Item and Invoice, both of which are divergence-free, and join them at the reporting layer rather than at the log layer. That argument is strong and I concede it would be correct if the analysis question were purely control-flow discovery.

**Residual risk.** (a) Analysts will read the Order DFG's `Pack→Pack` and `Record Payment→Record Payment` edges as rework and open a non-existent quality investigation (D2, D5). (b) Per-order performance will be blamed on the order rather than on the consolidation partner (D3). (c) The secondary logs use different start clocks, so any cross-log KPI chart is misleading unless clocks are stated (D9). (d) With 4 orders, 3 deliveries and 3 invoices, *no* conclusion here is statistically supported; everything is structural illustration.

**Conditions under which I would change.** → To **A (Order alone)** if E2O were enriched so that packing/dispatch events carried only their own order's items (i.e. consolidation modelled as separate events per order), removing both convergence and divergence. → To **C (reject a lead object)** if the consolidation rate rose — e.g. if a typical delivery carried 20 packages from 15 orders, the ten duplications here would become the dominant mass of the log and the Order flattening would stop being a log and become a join table. → To **Item as primary** if the analysis mandate narrowed to warehouse throughput and the financial tail were out of scope. → To **Invoice as primary** if the mandate were receivables, where V2's instalment structure is the object of study and fulfilment is context.

## 6. PM4Py analysis outline

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd

# ---------- 0. Structural reconnaissance BEFORE choosing any case notion ----------
print(ocel)  # events / objects / E2O relation counts

events_df = ocel.get_extended_table()
activity_freq = ocel.events[ocel.event_activity].value_counts()
objtype_freq = ocel.objects[ocel.object_type_column].value_counts()
print("Activity frequencies (event-based, no duplication):\n", activity_freq)
print("Objects per type:\n", objtype_freq)

# How many objects of each type does each event touch? -> predicts convergence per lead type.
rel = ocel.relations
conv_profile = (rel.groupby([ocel.event_id_column, ocel.event_activity,
                             ocel.object_type_column])[ocel.object_id_column]
                   .nunique().reset_index(name="n_objects"))
convergence_hotspots = conv_profile[conv_profile["n_objects"] > 1] \
    .sort_values("n_objects", ascending=False)
print("Events that will be duplicated when flattening, by object type:\n",
      convergence_hotspots)   # expect e16 (Order=3), e13/e14/e17/e19/e22/e24/e25/e27 (Order=2), e16/e24 (Package=2)

# Divergence predictor: does one object see the same activity more than once?
div_profile = (rel.merge(ocel.events[[ocel.event_id_column, ocel.event_activity]],
                         on=ocel.event_id_column, how="left", suffixes=("", "_y"))
                  .groupby([ocel.object_type_column, ocel.object_id_column,
                            ocel.event_activity])[ocel.event_id_column]
                  .nunique().reset_index(name="n_events"))
divergence_hotspots = div_profile[div_profile["n_events"] > 1]
print("Object/activity pairs that will diverge:\n", divergence_hotspots)
# expect (Order,O1,Pack Package)=2, (Order,O1,Dispatch)=2, (Order,O1,Confirm Delivery)=2,
#        (Order,O3,Pack Package)=2, (Order,O3,Record Payment)=2, (Invoice,V2,Record Payment)=2

# ---------- 1. Flatten on all six candidate types ----------
OBJECT_TYPES = ["Order", "Item", "Package", "Delivery", "Invoice", "Payment"]
flat_logs, flat_dfgs, flat_skeletons, flat_stats = {}, {}, {}, {}

for ot in OBJECT_TYPES:
    log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, ot)
    flat_logs[ot] = log
    dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
    flat_dfgs[ot] = {"dfg": dfg, "start": start_acts, "end": end_acts}
    flat_skeletons[ot] = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(
        log, noise_threshold=0.0)   # 0.0 first: with 3-6 cases, noise filtering would delete real behaviour
    flat_stats[ot] = {
        "n_cases": log["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
        "n_case_events": len(log),
        "n_distinct_source_events": log["concept:name"].count()  # replace with source-event id if retained
    }

print(pd.DataFrame(flat_stats).T)
# Expected: Order 4 cases / 41 case-events (31 source events -> +10 duplicates)
#           Item 6/24, Package 4/12, Delivery 3/6, Invoice 3/7, Payment 4/4 (all length-1)

# Explicit duplication audit: Order-flattened event count vs. true event count
order_dup = len(flat_logs["Order"]) - len(ocel.events)
print(f"Order flattening replicates {order_dup} event instances "
      f"({order_dup / len(ocel.events):.0%} inflation)")

# Degeneracy guard: refuse to interpret a DFG built on near-singleton lifecycles
for ot in OBJECT_TYPES:
    trace_lens = flat_logs[ot].groupby("case:concept:name").size()
    if trace_lens.max() <= 2:
        print(f"[DEGENERATE] {ot}: max trace length {trace_lens.max()} -> "
              f"DFG carries no sequencing information; use as metric table only.")
# expects Delivery (len 2) and Payment (len 1) to trigger

# ---------- 2. Targeted performance, per flattening, with stated clocks ----------
perf_dfgs = {}
for ot in ["Order", "Item", "Package", "Delivery", "Invoice"]:
    perf_dfgs[ot] = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(flat_logs[ot])
# NOTE: Order-level Dispatch->Confirm Delivery durations are NOT order-specific;
# e16/e24 give the identical 4h55 to O1, O2 and O3. Cross-check against perf_dfgs["Delivery"].

# ---------- 3. Object-centric views (no flattening, no duplication) ----------
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)          # expect an Order-Order edge: the formal trace of consolidation
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)        # expect Record Payment ~ {Order, Invoice, Payment} only
interactions = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)

print("OTG edges:", otg)
print("ET-OT edges:", etot)
print(interactions.head(50))

# Which orders are entangled with which, and through what?
order_pairs = interactions[
    (interactions["ocel:type_1"] == "Order") & (interactions["ocel:type_2"] == "Order")
]
print("Entangled order pairs (expect O2-O3 via P3/V2, O3-O4 via P4, O1-O2-O3 via D2):\n", order_pairs)

# ---------- 4. Three counting bases for the SAME OC-DFG edge ----------
def ocdfg_edge_counts(ocdfg_obj, ot, act_from, act_to):
    edges = ocdfg_obj["edges"]
    return {
        "events":        len(edges.get("event_couples", {}).get(ot, {}).get((act_from, act_to), [])),
        "unique_objects": len(set(edges.get("unique_objects", {}).get(ot, {}).get((act_from, act_to), []))),
        "total_objects": len(edges.get("total_objects", {}).get(ot, {}).get((act_from, act_to), [])),
    }

dispatch_counts = ocdfg_edge_counts(ocdfg, "Order", "Pack Package", "Dispatch")
print("Pack->Dispatch under three counting bases (Order):", dispatch_counts)
# The three numbers WILL differ; reporting only one of them is the core reporting error here.

# ---------- 5. Clustering: justified because the decision is B, not A ----------
# Position B keeps Order as lead but denies that orders are independent cases.
# Clustering tests exactly that denial: if O2/O3/O4 fall into one cluster, "per-order"
# reporting is arithmetically unsound and the secondary lifecycles are mandatory.
clusters = pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel, "Order")
print("Order clusters (equivalent executions):", clusters)

# ---------- 6. Contrast log: explicitly propagated relations (NOT the baseline) ----------
o2o = ocel.o2o  # Order-contains-Item etc.
contains = o2o[o2o["ocel:qualifier"] == "contains"][["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2"]] \

propagated = (rel[rel[ocel.object_type_column] == "Order"]
              .merge(contains, left_on=ocel.object_id_column, right_on="order"))
print("Item-log size, direct E2O:", len(flat_logs["Item"]),
      "| with Order->Item propagation:", len(propagated))
# Propagation lengthens Item traces but imports Order's convergence AND divergence
# (I31/I32 would each inherit e19, e22, e25). Report both, never silently substitute.
```

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

**S1 — Remove or down-weight copied shared events (Order flattening).** Build three Order-log variants: (a) as-is (41 case-events); (b) *shared-events-removed*, dropping e13, e14, e16, e17, e19, e22, e24, e25, e27 from every case except a designated "owner" order (e.g. assign e16 to O1 as the majority contributor by package count); (c) *fractionally weighted*, where e16 contributes 1/3 of an event to each of O1, O2, O3 and e13 contributes 1/2 to O2 and O3. Compare (i) the frequency of the `Pack Package → Pack Package` and `Record Payment → Record Payment` edges, (ii) total Dispatch count (expect 6 → 3 → 3.0), and (iii) per-order cycle time. **Acceptance test:** any conclusion that survives all three variants is robust; any conclusion that only holds under (a) is an artefact of duplication and must be reported with an explicit "order-participation" label rather than an operational count.

**S2 — Direct E2O versus explicitly propagated relations.** Compare the Item log built from direct relations (6 cases × 4 events, one variant) with the propagated variant that pushes Order-level events (e01–e04, e18–e20, e21/e22/e25/e26, e28–e31) down through `contains`. Measure: number of variants (expect 1 → several, because O3's items pick up the double payment while O1's items pick up a single one), convergence count (0 → nonzero, since e19 lands on I21, I31 and I32), and mean trace length (4 → ~9). **Acceptance test:** propagation is reported only as a "what-if the billing tail belonged to items" scenario. If a stakeholder question can only be answered by the propagated log, that is evidence the question is really an Order- or Invoice-level question misfiled as an Item question.

**S3 — Change the lead object and re-ask the same three questions.** Take three fixed questions — Q1 "how long from packing to proof of delivery?", Q2 "how many settlement steps per receivable?", Q3 "what is end-to-end lead time?" — and answer each under Order, Item, Package, Delivery, Invoice. Expected disagreements: Q1 gives 4h00 (P1: e11 10:00 → e23 14:00) under Package but is unanswerable cleanly under Order for O1 because O1 has two packing and two delivery events (which pairs with which?); Q2 gives 2 under Invoice for V2 but reads as "two payments per order" for both O2 and O3 under Order; Q3 is only answerable under Order at all. **Acceptance test:** a question is assigned to the flattening where the answer is *unique and non-duplicated*; a question that gives different answers under two flattenings without one of them being structurally invalid is escalated to the OC-DFG.

**S4 — Three counting bases on the OC-DFG.** For every reported edge, compute event-based, unique-object-based and total-object-based frequency (see `ocdfg_edge_counts` above). For the Order-type `Dispatch → Confirm Delivery` edge these will diverge sharply because e16/e24 involve three orders. Publish the ratio *total-objects / events* per edge as a **consolidation index**; edges with an index near 1 (e.g. anything involving O1's P1/D1 chain) are safe to read per-order, edges with an index of 2–3 (everything touching P3, P4, D2, V2) are not. **Acceptance test:** no KPI is published without its counting basis named in the metric label.

**S5 — Temporal-tie and calendar sensitivity.** Re-run the Order-log ordering with the e29/e30 tie (both 2026-02-04 11:05) broken both ways and confirm no reported finding flips. Separately, recompute D3's 22h45 leg (e17 11:15 → e27 next-day 10:00) on a business-hours clock; if D3 ceases to be the outlier, "worst route" claims are calendar artefacts, not performance.

**S6 — Cluster stability.** Run `cluster_equivalent_ocel` on Order and check whether O2, O3 and O4 collapse into one cluster (they are chained by P3 and P4) while O1 attaches only via D2. If the four orders do not decompose into four independent components — and here they do not — then "per-order" reporting is a modelling convention rather than a fact about the process, and the Position-B requirement to publish Package/Delivery/Invoice lifecycles alongside the Order view is mandatory rather than optional.
