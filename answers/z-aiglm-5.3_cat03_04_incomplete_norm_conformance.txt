## 1. Conformance semantics

I classify every observation into exactly one primary bucket, with secondary buckets where evidence is genuinely mixed.

- **Forbidden behavior**: the observation instantiates a relation the norm explicitly marks forbidden (N3, T6, E10). Under the stated closed-world rule, forbidden relations are nonconforming *per instance* — a weight of 1 is as decisive as a weight of 100. However, the classification still requires that the relation be *real*: a join artifact or ETL merge that merely produces a forbidden-looking edge belongs in the data-quality bucket even though the norm status is "forbidden."
- **Missing mandatory behavior**: a mandatory relation (N1, N4, T1, T2, E1–E8) is absent *within its stated scope*. Two gates apply before declaring nonconformance: (a) scope applicability (N4 covers only externally shipped packages; E8 excepts typed unapplied cash), and (b) abstraction adequacy (a directly-follows summary can hide an "eventually" rule that was actually satisfied; an attribute can carry information the object model omits).
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior**: an observed relation absent from the norm (e.g., `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, `object_codeath`, `Reopen Order -> Close Order`). Because the norm is open-world outside forbidden relations and mandatory columns, silence is not prohibition. These are inputs to norm evolution, not findings of nonconformance.
- **Typical-pattern absence**: a typical relation (N2, N5, T3) missing for some instances. Typical status licenses legitimate exceptions; without specified tolerance bands, absence rates cannot be declared deviant — only noteworthy.
- **Likely data-quality error**: the observation is better explained by the data pipeline than by the process (the Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice join on a reused identifier is the exemplar).
- **Abstraction mismatch**: the summary representation cannot express the rule's true semantics — the norm itself admits N1 is an "eventually" rule approximated by directly-follows.
- **Insufficient evidence**: trace-level or attribute-level data is required before any bucket can be confirmed (the two CreateValidate gaps, the 14 successor-less invoices).

**Open vs. closed world**: closed-world reasoning applies *only* to forbidden relations and to mandatory columns within scope — there, absence of the expected relation or presence of the forbidden one is decisive. Everywhere else the norm is open-world: an unmodeled observed relation is conforming-by-default, and an absent typical relation is not a violation. Closed-world misuse — treating the normative edge set as exhaustive — is the single largest interpretive risk in this snapshot.

PM4Py diagnostics report structural graph fit; they carry no normative status metadata and are never a complete business or legal interpretation.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

**Can establish**: aggregate directly-follows frequencies per object type, and therefore which activity pairs are common, rare, or absent in the summary.

**Cannot establish**: trace-level sequences, interleaved events on the same object, cross-object synchronization, event attributes (e.g., the carrier reference), or whether an absent successor reflects censoring rather than behavior.

N3's two instances are individually decisive because forbidden status is per-instance.

**Cross-abstraction tension**: `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` is a sequencing prohibition, so the DF abstraction is the *right shape* for N3 — the observation is strong evidence. By contrast, `Create Order -> Validate Order` is the *wrong shape* for N1's business rule, making the same abstraction weak evidence there.

**The "eventually vs. directly-follows" danger**: a directly-follows relation asserts that validation is the *next* event for that order. The business rule asserts only that validation *eventually* occurs. This is precisely the stated explanation for the two N1 gaps. Treating the DF gap as automatic nonconformance would generate false positives; conversely, dismissing it entirely would mask genuine skips. The only resolution is trace-level replay of the two orders. The same caveat applies to N4's two packages: interleaved events on a package (e.g., a status update) would break PackDispatch DF without any packing omission.

### OTG

**Can establish**: existence, relation kind (interaction/cobirth/codeath), and weights of object-to-object edges.

**Cannot establish**: which events produced an edge, temporal ordering, or whether a linked object is the *correct* object.

**Cross-abstraction tension — the central example**: the (Package, object_interaction, Delivery) edge exists with weight 200, so T2 is *conforming at the OTG level*, yet E6 fails for 2 of 200 dispatch events at the ET-OT level. The same pattern holds for T1 (edge present, 1450) versus E2 (2 Add Item events without Order). Graph-level conformance can coexist with event-level suspicion; neither subsumes the other.

**T6 cobirth**: a weight of 1 is fully decisive *if real*. The plausible single-event hypothesis — one combined create-and-pay event that co-births an Order and a Payment, records a Payment (the 93rd), and carries no Invoice (the E8 gap) — would elegantly explain Ranks 2 and 6 together. It remains a hypothesis until the event is inspected; an ETL merge artifact remains an equally live explanation.

**Codeath (7)**: the norm says nothing; open-world silence means these are observations, not violations. They are evidence that the norm's object-lifecycle coverage is incomplete, not that behavior is deviant.

### ET-OT

**Can establish**: per-event-type linkage completeness — whether every event of a type carries the required object type (E1–E8), and whether forbidden linkages occur (E10).

**Cannot establish**: linkage *correctness* (a Record Payment linked to the wrong invoice is invisible), attribute-based substitutes for objects (E6's carrier reference), or exception typing that the log never captures (E8's "unapplied cash" label).

**Counts**: E7 (93/93) is fully satisfied; E8's 92 of 93 isolates exactly one event. E10's weight of 1 is nominally decisive, but the stated join-on-reused-identifier cause makes this primarily a data-integrity incident — severity is high precisely because financial lineage is contaminated, even though the *process* is likely innocent. Frequency and severity are fully decoupled under forbidden semantics.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1 semantics (DF vs. eventually).** *Risk*: two orders generate false nonconformance, breeding alert fatigue and masking real skips. *Clarification*: "Every standard Order must reach Validate Order before any terminal state; the DF relation is a monitoring proxy only."  **mandatory** (eventually-follows), with the proxy explicitly labeled.
2. **N3 tie-breaking and scope.** *Risk*: same-timestamp or batch-boundary events are unjudgeable; dispatch of a package linked to a cancelled order through another order is undefined. *Clarification*: strict per-order total ordering; forbidden applies to any package whose dispatch references the cancelled order.  **forbidden**, with a defined evidence standard (trace-level, not aggregate).
3. **E8 exception typing.** *Risk*: the "unapplied cash" exception exists in the norm but no typing discipline enforces it, so legitimate behavior is formally nonconforming (the 93rd payment). *Clarification*: require a mandatory event attribute `payment_class  {applied, unapplied}`, or redefine the exception structurally (no Invoice link  unapplied).  **mandatory** labeling.
4. **E6/T2 Delivery object vs. carrier attribute.** *Risk*: two dispatch events are nonconforming under one reading and fine under another; conformance becomes arbitrary. *Clarification*: state whether a carrier-reference attribute satisfies the requirement or whether Delivery-object creation is required.  **mandatory** object linkage recommended; attribute fallback **typical**.
5. **T6 definition of "born" and DQ carve-out.** *Risk*: an ETL merge artifact is escalated as a forbidden violation. *Clarification*: define object birth events precisely and require a data-provenance check before any cobirth finding is escalated.  **forbidden**, with a verification protocol.
6. **Explicitly unspecified entries.** *Risk*: auditors infer rules for ReopenClose (N6 already correct), codeath, and MCR relations, converting benign rarity into findings. *Clarification*: add explicit "unspecified" rows for codeath and MCR-related edges.  **explicitly unspecified**.
7. **Tolerance bands and N4 scope markers.** *Risk*: without statistical bands, "92 of 110" and "3 MCR events" cannot be judged; without a shipment-class attribute, "externally shipped" is retrofitted from outcomes. *Clarification*: declare that mandatory/forbidden conformance is per-instance and typical relations get monitoring thresholds *outside* the norm; require a shipment-class attribute on Pack/Dispatch events.  bands **explicitly unspecified**; attribute **mandatory**.
