# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

The apparent contradiction is largely explainable by what each network measures. Mira_Coordinator can generate many sequential handovers and return-loops while still having low sustained co-presence with other resources on the same cases. Centrality is therefore evidence of a distinctive routing position, not by itself evidence of formal authority, competence, overload, or wrongdoing.

## Handover-of-work network

PM4Py's handover-of-work network counts directed, consecutive activity successions between different resources within cases. It asks: *whose event is immediately followed by whose event?*

For Mira_Coordinator, the evidence is strong and consistent:

- Highest handover out-degree centrality (0.82), high in-degree (0.51), and by far the highest betweenness (0.77).
- Inbound handovers from Bot_Rules (920), Temp_1 (190), Temp_2 (170), and the specialists returning work (Omar_Medical 160, Priya_Legal 140).
- Outbound handovers distributed across all four adjusters (280–350 each), Omar_Medical (210), and Priya_Legal (180).
- Elapsed times on her edges are minutes, not days, consistent with short routing acts rather than long substantive work.

**What this implies:** Mira is the main sequential junction between automated triage, temporary staff, specialists, and adjusters. Work repeatedly passes *through* her rather than merely *to* her. Bot_Rules is upstream of her (betweenness 0.41), and Vendor_Pay is a downstream sink (high in-degree, zero out-degree), so she sits mid-process between automated front-end and human/vendor back-end.

**What it does not imply:** Handover does not imply hierarchy, approval authority, or that she does the substantive work in the neighbouring events. A one-line "Assign Specialist" event creates the same edge as a substantive review. It also does not show *why* work was routed, whether routing was correct, or whether she initiated the handover versus simply being the resource recorded on an intermediate system step. Because she touches 905 distinct cases with 960 events — roughly one event per case — her handover degree is built from many single, brief touches spread widely, which mechanically inflates degree relative to resources who work repeatedly within fewer cases.

## Working-together network

The working-together network is undirected and based on resources co-occurring in the same cases, regardless of adjacency or order. It asks: *who shares casework?*

Here Mira looks peripheral: weighted degree 0.16, versus 0.43–0.49 for the TeamLead–adjuster cores. Her pairwise co-case rates are only 4–8%, while Alice–Carla (27%), Bruno–Diego (26%), and similar within-team pairs reach 19–27%.

**What this implies:** Mira does not form stable, repeated case-sharing partnerships. The claims operation has two durable production cores (North: Alice/Carla/Elise; South: Bruno/Diego/Farid), and Mira is not embedded in either. Her involvement is episodic and case-broad rather than team-deep.

**What it does not imply:** It does not imply she is unimportant or disconnected. Co-occurrence percentages are normalised per case, so a resource who touches 905 different cases briefly — and then, as the evidence states, "disappears from the same case for several events" — will show low pairwise rates with everyone simply because her attention is diluted across many cases. Low working-together scores are exactly what a broadcast-style router would produce. Nor does co-occurrence prove real collaboration for the high scorers: Alice and Carla may never communicate despite sharing cases.

**Reconciliation:** Handover measures *flow through a point*; working-together measures *sustained joint presence*. A triage broker maximises the first and minimises the second. The two networks are describing the same behaviour at different grains, not contradicting each other.

## Subcontracting network

The subcontracting network (here n=2) detects A  B  A patterns: resource A appears again within two events after B. It asks: *who sends work out and takes it back?*

Mira dominates here too (betweenness 0.69), with the two largest patterns: Mira  Omar_Medical  Mira (126) and Mira  Priya_Legal  Mira (98). The activity fragments — Request Missing Information  Medical Review  Assign Specialist/Reopen — suggest she dispatches specialist questions and re-enters the case when answers return.

**What this implies:** Her role is not fire-and-forget routing. She closes the loop: she frames or triggers specialist involvement and is the return address for specialist output. This is consistent with the handover edges Omar  Mira and Priya  Mira at short elapsed times (11–12 minutes), i.e., she receives and acts on answers quickly.

**What it does not imply:** Despite the name, this is not evidence of contractual subcontracting, delegation of accountability, or that she supervises Omar or Priya. An A–B–A pattern can arise from case ownership conventions ("return to coordinator queue"), system design, or genuine task delegation. Note also that Alice  Carla  Alice (86) and Bruno  Diego  Bruno (82) show the same pattern inside teams, presumably the approval loop, so the pattern itself is a normal feature of this process, not unique to Mira. Her distinctiveness is that her loops cross team and specialism boundaries rather than staying inside one team.

## Performance/time statistics

Median service time per event separates *working time* from *case-level effect*:

- Mira's median service time is short (4.2 min) — the shortest of any human — yet her average case cycle-time contribution is flagged "high variance." Brief touches, widely varying downstream consequences.
- Omar_Medical (37 min) and Priya_Legal (51 min) have genuinely long service times and high/very high cycle contributions; their exception shares (0.62, 0.71) confirm they receive pre-selected hard cases, so their slowness is partly case-mix, not necessarily inefficiency.
- Vendor_Verify (2.1 h) and Vendor_Pay (1.3 h) contribute mainly *waiting* time, not service time — an important distinction PM4Py's `get_service_time` and case waiting-time decomposition can make precise. Their low exception shares (0.18, 0.09) suggest they are volume utilities, not exception handlers.
- The bots are near-instant (4–7 sec) but Bot_Rules still has betweenness 0.41: automation shapes who Mira sees, since exception routing from Bot_Rules is her single largest inbound source.

**What this implies:** Mira's own hands-on time is trivial; any performance risk she creates operates through queueing and routing delays *between* her events and other people's, which is a waiting-time phenomenon, not a service-time one. The absence experiment is the strongest performance evidence available: six days without her left ordinary claims unchanged but pushed the 90th-percentile cycle time for exception cases from 8.4 to 13.9 days. That is consistent with an exception-queue function that nobody else absorbed.

**What it does not imply:** It does not prove she personally was the scarce resource — cases may have accumulated because no one else had system permissions, queue visibility, or willingness to pick them up. Correlation with one absence period is one observation, potentially confounded (e.g., case mix that week).

## Role-discovery or activity-profile similarity

`discover_organizational_roles` and `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` group resources by *which activities they perform and how often*.

On the stated profiles, Mira's activity set (Request Missing Information, Reopen Claim, Escalate Complaint, Assign Specialist) barely overlaps anyone else's. She would likely form a singleton or near-singleton role, whereas Alice/Bruno cluster together (Triage, Approve Settlement), Carla/Diego together, Elise/Farid together, and the bots/vendors form machine/external clusters.

**What this implies:** Her activity footprint is structurally unique in the log — nobody else is recorded doing this combination. That supports the view that a distinct function exists, whether or not it exists on the org chart.

**What it does not imply:** Activity-profile uniqueness does not establish that the function is formally designed, skilled, or even performed as logged. One data-quality flag deserves attention: "Assign Specialist" appears in Mira's profile and in subcontracting fragments but is **not** among the 14 canonical activities listed for the process. That could be a logging variant, an informal activity label, or evidence her work is recorded differently from everyone else's — which would artificially make her profile look unique and disjoint. Role discovery on uncleaned labels would inherit that artifact.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

## Hypothesis A — Formal coordinator / exception desk

- **Mechanism:** The role is designed: Bot_Rules routes rule failures and exceptions to a named exception desk; Mira staffs it, requests missing information, assigns specialists, and reopens/escalates as intended.
- **Supporting evidence:** Largest inbound edge is Bot_Rules  Mira with a note "exception desk routing"; exception share 0.48 on her cases; she handles 64% of reopened claims and 71% of complaints despite touching only 22% of claims; the process owner can describe the function ("queue cleaner").
- **Contradicting/limiting evidence:** A designed desk would normally have documented backup, yet a 6-day absence stalled exceptions. It is also odd for a formal desk to be described by the process owner as "not a real team lead," and her activity label "Assign Specialist" sits outside the canonical activity list, suggesting the role is at most semi-formalised.
- **Observable predictions:** Her routing behaviour should be stable over time and rule-like: exception types predict destinations; her case mix and volumes should be roughly constant across the 12 weeks; routing decisions should correlate with recorded case attributes (case_type, priority, amount_band) more than with her personal workload.

## Hypothesis B — Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency

- **Mechanism:** Regardless of design intent, exception flow has converged on one individual; queues form behind her judgement and availability, creating fragility and delay risk.
- **Supporting evidence:** Betweenness 0.77 (handover) and 0.69 (subcontracting) — the network has few alternative paths around her; the absence-period deterioration (p90 exception cycle time 8.4  13.9 days) is a natural experiment pointing at dependency; specialists' work returns to her rather than flowing directly to adjusters.
- **Contradicting/limiting evidence:** Her own service time is short and her outbound elapsed times are minutes, so she is not slow at the events she performs; ordinary claims were unaffected by her absence, so she is not a bottleneck for the process as a whole, only for the exception subpopulation. One absence is a single, uncontrolled observation. Betweenness is also inflated by log structure: if the system forces a coordinator event into the trace, centrality reflects system design, not personal indispensability.
- **Observable predictions:** Waiting times before her events should grow with her concurrent caseload; exception-case cycle time should correlate with her daily event volume and absence/weekend patterns; queue ageing for exception_flag=True cases should show step changes around her leave, and similar (smaller) effects for any partial cover.

## Hypothesis C — Organisational boundary spanner / triage broker

- **Mechanism:** Mira's value is relational knowledge across boundaries — North/South teams, adjusters, medical/legal specialists, temps, vendors — knowing "where hard cases should go," and translating between Bot_Rules' crude exception output and specialist input requirements.
- **Supporting evidence:** Adjusters' testimony ("the person who knows where hard cases should go"); she is the only resource connected to every group (bots, both team leads, all adjusters, both specialists, both temps); Omar–Priya co-occur in only 4% of cases, so complex multi-specialist cases need someone to sequence them — plausibly her; low working-together scores fit a broker who connects otherwise separate groups rather than joining them.
- **Contradicting/limiting evidence:** Brokerage value is hard to distinguish from mere queue position in log data — a passive mailbox in the same position would produce nearly identical networks. There is no direct evidence in the log of knowledge quality, e.g., that her specialist assignments are more accurate than alternatives.
- **Observable predictions:** Her routing choices should be hard to predict from case attributes alone (tacit judgement), yet associated with better downstream outcomes — fewer re-routings, fewer reopens after assignment, shorter specialist turnaround — compared with cases routed by Bot_Rules directly to team leads. If a simple rules model reproduces her decisions with equal outcomes, the tacit-knowledge claim weakens.

## Hypothesis D — Data/system artifact: recorded router rather than actual router

- **Mechanism:** Mira's account (or a shared coordinator queue account) is written into traces by the workflow system whenever an exception step fires; centrality reflects logging conventions, not individual behaviour.
- **Supporting evidence:** ~1.06 events per case (960 events / 905 cases) — a single stamp-like touch; the non-canonical "Assign Specialist" activity; her disappearance from cases for several events after acting; implausibly uniform short service times for routing acts.
- **Contradicting/limiting evidence:** The absence effect is hard to explain by pure logging artifact — if the stamp were automatic, her absence should not stall exceptions (unless her account was also the queue others couldn't access). Colleagues describe a real person with real knowledge.
- **Observable predictions:** Timestamps clustering at batch-like intervals, identical service durations, activity only ever recorded with `lifecycle:transition` complete and never start, and events appearing during recorded leave would support artifact; conversely, start/complete pairs with variable durations and case-note-like attribute variation would refute it. Checking `lifecycle:transition` values per resource is the first test.

## Hypothesis E — Quality gatekeeper / informal control point (including automation monitor)

- **Mechanism:** Mira functions as an unofficial control: she sanity-checks Bot_Rules fraud-screen exceptions, verifies Temp and Vendor_Verify outputs (Temp_1  Mira 190; Temp_1–Vendor_Verify subcontracting 68), and decides whether specialist spend (Omar, Priya) is justified — a combined quality gate and automation supervisor.
- **Supporting evidence:** Bot_Rules  Mira  Bot_Rules subcontracting loop (62; "Fraud Screen  manual exception  Fraud Screen update") suggests she feeds corrections back into the rules engine; her cases reopen/complain at high rates, meaning she works where quality failures surface; she sits between temporary staff and the rest of the process.
- **Contradicting/limiting evidence:** Gatekeeping implies authority to stop or reverse work, for which the log offers no direct evidence (no rejection/rework-to-sender patterns quantified). She may simply forward rather than review. No evidence of any control failure or inappropriate decision exists in the data provided.
- **Observable predictions:** Cases passing through her should show lower downstream error proxies (reopen-after-close, complaint-after-payment) than matched exception cases that bypass her; her fraud-screen updates should change subsequent Bot_Rules behaviour on similar cases; browsing her inbound mix should show disproportionate Temp/Vendor-originated work relative to their volumes.

These hypotheses are not mutually exclusive: a formally intended exception desk (A) can drift into key-person dependency (B) precisely because boundary-spanning knowledge (C, E) accumulates in the post-holder, and all of them can be exaggerated by logging conventions (D).

# 3. Risk assessment

**Bottleneck risk (moderate-to-high, scoped to exceptions).** The evidence supports a bottleneck claim only for the exception subpopulation: ordinary claims were unaffected by her absence, while exception p90 cycle time rose ~65%. Structural betweenness of 0.77 means few logged paths bypass her. However, her event-level speed is fast, so any bottleneck is one of capacity/availability and queue attention, not slow execution.

**Coordination risk (moderate).** Handoffs per exception case are numerous (Bot_Rules  Mira  specialist  Mira  adjuster), and each handoff at 8–51 minutes of elapsed time plus inter-event waiting adds latency and information-loss risk. The low working-together embedding means adjusters have no durable working relationship with her to fall back on informally; coordination depends on the formal queue working.

**Key-person risk (high, best-supported risk).** A single absence measurably degraded exception handling, no other resource performs her activity profile, and specialist return-loops terminate at her. Impact is concentrated in exactly the cases (reopened, complaints, medical/legal) where delay is most reputationally and financially costly. This is a dependency finding, not a criticism of the individual.

**Compliance/control risk (uncertain; flagged, not established).** The compliance concern is structurally legitimate: one person both detects problems (fraud-screen exceptions, missing information) and selects the specialist/adjuster who resolves them, which concentrates discretion over specialist spend and case outcomes. In a regulated process this warrants auditability — recorded routing rationale, segregation where feasible, sampling of decisions. It must be stated clearly: **there is no evidence in this data of wrongdoing, favouritism, or control failure.** Her adjuster assignments are in fact fairly evenly spread (350/330/290/280), which if anything counts against favouritism toward one adjuster; specialist routing rates should be tested against case attributes before any concern escalates.

**Workload fairness risk (two-sided).** Mira's raw event count (960) is not extreme versus adjusters (~700–1110), but her work is interruption-driven across 905 cases, a cognitive-load profile that event counts understate. Conversely, Omar and Priya carry long service times on the hardest cases (exception shares 0.62/0.71), and the vendor steps dominate elapsed time. Fairness assessment based on event counts alone would mislead in both directions; service-time and case-mix adjustment are needed. Temp_1/Temp_2 rarely co-work (3%) and feed exceptions upward — their training/supervision load may be landing on Mira invisibly.

**Data-quality risk (material).** The non-canonical "Assign Specialist" activity, possible queue-account logging, unknown completeness of `lifecycle:transition` (which determines whether service times are trustworthy), and vendor timestamps that may reflect batch file exchanges rather than real work, all threaten inference. Network metrics computed on uncleaned labels — especially her singleton role profile — should be treated as provisional until the logging semantics of her events are verified.

# 5. Recommendation

**Change immediately (low-regret, no structural redesign):**
- Provide named, trained backup cover for the exception queue so absence no longer stalls exception cases; give the backup queue visibility and system permissions equal to Mira's.
- Require a short structured routing-reason code (attribute value) on her assignment/reopen events, making existing discretion auditable without slowing the work materially.
- Resolve the data questions: confirm what "Assign Specialist" is, whether her timestamps are start/complete, and whether a person or a queue account generates her events.
- Do **not** remove or bypass Mira: on current evidence that would remove the only demonstrated exception-handling capacity. Any removal must be preceded by a replacement mechanism — documented routing rules, a staffed rota, or automation changes — plus a shadow period comparing outcomes.

**Monitor (define metrics and review monthly):**
- Exception-queue ageing and waiting-time-before-Mira; exception p90 cycle time; her betweenness on rolling exception-only handover networks.
- Outcome quality by routing path: reopen-after-assignment, complaint-after-payment, specialist turnaround, for her-routed vs lead-routed exception cases.
- Assignment distribution across adjusters/specialists conditioned on case_type, priority, amount_band, region, channel — as a fairness dashboard, with case-mix adjustment.
- Vendor waiting-time components (Vendor_Verify, Vendor_Pay), which may be a larger absolute delay source than anything internal.

**Do not change until more evidence is available:**
- Do not formalise or abolish the role, redistribute specialist selection, or automate her routing until Hypotheses A–E are discriminated by the analyses above — particularly the attribute-predictability of her decisions and their outcome advantage.
- Do not infer performance judgements about Mira, Omar, or Priya from raw times without case-mix adjustment; their slowness may be selection, not inefficiency.
- Do not act on the compliance concern beyond audit logging until routing-decision audits show actual anomalies; current evidence supports transparency measures, not restriction or sanction.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira_Coordinator appears to be the routing point for difficult claims, not a routine claim handler. She touches only about one claim in five, but most reopened claims and complaints, and she frequently passes work to medical, legal and adjusting staff and receives their replies. This explains why she looks central when work is handed over, yet peripheral when measuring who repeatedly works on the same claims together: her contribution is brief triage and reassignment, not sustained casework.

That creates value and risk. During her six-day absence, ordinary claims were unaffected but difficult claims waited much longer, suggesting a single-person dependency for exceptions. There is no evidence of wrongdoing, but concentrated discretion over specialist selection needs transparent rules and audit.

Do not remove the role abruptly. Clarify its authority, provide trained backup, log routing reasons, monitor exception queues and outcomes, and test whether some routing can be safely standardised or automated.
