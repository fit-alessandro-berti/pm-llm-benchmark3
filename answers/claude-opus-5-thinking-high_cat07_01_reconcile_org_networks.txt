# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

Before going network by network, one arithmetic observation should frame everything else, because it changes how much weight each figure can carry.

Mira_Coordinator has **960 events over 905 distinct cases**, i.e. ~1.06 events per case. On the large majority of her cases she performs **exactly one event**, and there are only ~55 "extra" events available for cases where she appears twice or more.

That single fact is in tension with two other tables:

- Her outgoing handover edges sum to 210 + 180 + 350 + 330 + 290 + 280 = **1,640**, and her listed incoming edges sum to 920 + 160 + 140 + 190 + 170 = **1,580**. A resource with 960 events cannot be the source of more than 960 direct successions (fewer, since some events are case-final). The handover table therefore cannot be a count of *immediate* successor pairs with single-occurrence counting.
- The subcontracting table attributes **126 + 98 + 62 = 286** A→B→A patterns to her. Every such pattern requires at least two Mira events in the same case, so 286 patterns need ≥ 572 of her events distributed over ≥ 286 multi-event cases — but only ~55 multi-event cases are arithmetically possible.

The most likely benign explanation is a **metric-parameterization difference**: PM4Py's handover-of-work and subcontracting discovery can be computed with non-immediate succession and exponential decay (the `beta` parameter) and with "multiple occurrences" counting, over all successors within a window rather than only the direct follower. Under those settings, one Mira event on a case legitimately generates several fractional or repeated edges. Other possibilities are that the networks were computed over a longer window than the 12-week activity table, or that `lifecycle:transition` was not filtered, so start/complete pairs were double-counted in the SNA but deduplicated in the event counts.

This matters for the interpretation task: **the appearance of extreme centrality is partly a measurement choice, and the appearance of peripherality is partly a normalization choice.** Both need to be corrected before we treat the contradiction as a fact about the organization.

## Handover-of-work network

*What it measures.* Directly-follows (or window-follows) relations between the resources of consecutive events **within the same case**. It is a flow measure over transitions.

*What it implies about Mira.* Her out-degree centrality (0.82) and betweenness (0.77) are the highest of any resource, and her out-edge set is unusually broad: two team leads' worth of adjusters (Carla, Diego, Elise, Farid), both specialists (Omar, Priya), plus return flows from the temporaries. She is the only resource that connects Team North, Team South, the specialist pool, the temporaries and the rules engine in a single node. Structurally that is a **brokerage position over a structural hole**: paths between otherwise weakly connected clusters run through her.

*What it does not imply.*
- Handover is **succession, not delegation**. PM4Py infers an edge from temporal adjacency inside a case; it has no knowledge of authority, instruction or task ownership. A resource who inserts a short bookkeeping event between two other people's events acquires handover edges automatically, without exercising any control.
- Betweenness on a graph with only 15 nodes is fragile. With a hub-and-spoke exception topology, *whoever* sits on the exception path will have betweenness near the maximum; the value describes the topology as much as the person.
- **Bot_Rules also has high betweenness (0.41) and is on the critical path of essentially all 4,100 claims**, whereas Mira is on 22%. In absolute flow terms the automated router, not Mira, is the process's main structural chokepoint. Vendor_Pay (in-degree 0.26, out-degree 0.00) is a pure sink handling 2,840 cases. Any conclusion about "the central resource" that ignores the bots and vendors is measuring humans only because humans are interesting.
- High centrality is neither good nor bad by itself. The same number is produced by a well-designed exception desk and by an undocumented single point of failure.

## Working-together network

*What it measures.* Undirected co-appearance of two resources **in the same case**, here expressed as a percentage of *all* cases.

*What it implies about Mira.* Taken at face value: weighted degree 0.16, and pair rates of 4–8%, far below the 19–27% of the intra-team pairs. She looks peripheral.

*What it does not imply — and this is the core of the reconciliation.* The denominator is all claims, and **Mira touches only 22% of claims**. Her co-case rate with anyone is therefore capped at 0.22. Conditioning on her own caseload:

| Pair | Reported co-case rate (all cases) | Implied rate within Mira's cases |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mira — Carla | 8% | ≈ 36% |
| Mira — Diego | 7% | ≈ 32% |
| Mira — Elise | 6% | ≈ 27% |
| Mira — Farid | 6% | ≈ 27% |
| Mira — Alice | 5% | ≈ 23% |
| Mira — Bruno | 4% | ≈ 18% |

Rescaled, she is *not* peripheral at all: within her own case population she co-works with each senior adjuster about as often as Alice co-works with Carla, and she does so with **all six** front-line staff plus both specialists rather than with one team. The "surprisingly peripheral" note in the data is largely an artifact of comparing an unconditional rate for a low-volume, high-breadth resource against unconditional rates for high-volume, low-breadth pairs.

Three further reasons working-together understates her:
1. **Breadth splits weight.** Her participation is spread across ~10 partners; the team pairs concentrate theirs on one. Broad-and-thin is the standard signature of a broker, not of a peripheral actor.
2. **Co-occurrence is unweighted by effort or time.** Her events are 4.2 min median against 18–24 min for adjusters and 37–51 min for specialists. Any effort-weighted variant will shrink her further, even if her routing decision determines the next several hours of work.
3. **Co-occurrence is symmetric and order-blind.** It cannot express "she decided who the next worker would be", which is precisely the function at issue.

So the contradiction is only apparent: handover measures *transitions* (she has many, across clusters), working-together measures *shared case membership as a share of the whole log* (she has few cases, each touched briefly). **A one-touch router is mathematically guaranteed to be central in the first and thin in the second.** No hidden mechanism is needed to explain it.

## Subcontracting network

*What it measures.* With n = 2: A acts, B acts, and A acts again within the window — read as "A handed work out and took it back", i.e. retained case ownership across a delegation.

*What it implies about Mira.* Her subcontracting betweenness (0.69) dwarfs everyone else's, and the two dominant fragments (Request Missing Information → Medical Review → Assign Specialist/Reopen; Escalate Complaint → Legal Review → Reopen/Assign Specialist) are exactly the shape of *delegate to specialist, then resume disposition*. She is also the **B** side of `Bot_Rules → Mira → Bot_Rules` (Fraud Screen → manual exception → Fraud Screen update), i.e. the human fallback inside an automated decision.

*What it does not imply.*
- The A→B→A pattern is **highly susceptible to false positives from bookkeeping**. A coordinator whose job is to open and close a routing ticket around someone else's work produces the identical signature with zero supervisory authority. "Ownership" is an interpretation the metric cannot verify.
- It is **very sensitive to n**. At n = 1 the pattern may vanish; at n = 5 nearly every coordinator looks like a subcontractor. A single n = 2 table is not a finding.
- The arithmetic inconsistency above bears directly here: the reported frequencies are not reconcilable with 1.06 events per case, so either the counting variant is non-standard or the "A again" events are being generated by something other than genuine second visits.
- Note what is **absent**: she performs no `Approve Settlement` and no `Triage Claim`. Her discretion is *allocative* (who works this next), not *adjudicative* (what the claim is worth). That distinction should discipline the compliance discussion.
- `Assign Specialist` appears in her activity profile but is **not in the 14-activity catalogue** given for the process. That is an unresolved schema discrepancy, not a behavioural finding (see §3, data quality).

## Performance / time statistics

*What it implies.* Her median service time (4.2 min) is small, but her cycle-time contribution is flagged "high variance", and the natural experiment is the strongest evidence in the whole dossier: during a **6-day absence**, ordinary low-value claims were unaffected while exception cases queued and their 90th-percentile cycle time rose **8.4 → 13.9 days (+65%)**. The selectivity of that effect — tail of one segment, no effect on the mean of another — is much harder to explain by coincidence than a global slowdown would be, and it is consistent with her performing a *routing* function that only exception cases consume.

*What it does not imply.*
- Six days, one occurrence, no control group. Confounders are wide open: concurrent volume or mix shifts, holidays, other absences, vendor SLA variation, or simply that queued work is *deferred* rather than *lost*. Without checking whether throughput recovered afterwards, we cannot separate "capacity loss" from "temporary queueing".
- Service time is not cycle time. **The dominant waiting components in this log are the vendors** — Vendor_Verify at 2.1 h median over 1,500 cases and Vendor_Pay at 1.3 h over 2,840 cases, plus the 1.2–1.4 h lead-to-payment gaps. In expected minutes per claim across the whole population, vendor waiting outweighs anything Mira does. Her effect is concentrated, not large in aggregate.
- Her exception share (0.48) is high but far below Omar (0.62) and Priya (0.71), which is what you would expect if she *selects* the cases they receive. Correlation with difficulty here is by construction and says nothing about her performance.
- The elapsed times on her out-edges (16–19 min to adjusters, 42–51 min to specialists) mix her own latency with the receiver's queueing; they cannot be read as her delay.

## Role discovery / activity-profile similarity

*What it would show.* Her profile — Request Missing Information, Reopen Claim, Escalate Complaint, Assign Specialist — is a **union of exception-handling activities that no one else holds together**. Activity-based similarity would give her partial overlap with Temp_1/Elise/Farid on Request Missing Information and with Alice on Reopen Claim, but no overlap on the core value-adding activities (Assess Damage, Negotiate, Approve, Validate, Issue Payment).

Consequences for role mining (Song & van der Aalst-style clustering as in `discover_organizational_roles`):
- She will most likely form a **singleton role**, or be split across an "information-chasing" role and an "escalation/reopen" role. Either outcome argues that she is a **distinct organizational function**, not a member of Team North or Team South.
- She will **not** cluster with the team leads, because she lacks Triage and Approve Settlement. This is direct evidence against the "informal team lead" reading and against the process owner's "just a queue cleaner" framing in equal measure: queue cleaners do not hold the escalation and specialist-assignment activities.
- Caveat: activity-based similarity is blind to volume, timing, case difficulty and authority. Two resources with identical activity vectors can be a trainee and a manager. Role discovery is a hypothesis generator here, not an adjudicator.
- Asymmetry worth noting: Alice performs Reopen Claim and Bruno does not. The two "identical" team leads are not identical, which weakens any model that treats North and South as symmetric.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

## H1. Formal coordinator / exception desk (the role as designed)

**Mechanism.** The process has a designed bifurcation: Bot_Rules routes ordinary claims straight to a team lead and abnormal ones to a dedicated exception desk. Mira receives the abnormal stream, performs one classifying action (chase information, escalate, reopen, assign a specialist), and returns the case to the normal flow. One touch, then out.

**Supporting evidence.** 1.06 events per case; the Bot_Rules → Mira edge (920) is her single largest inflow and is annotated as exception routing; 64% of reopened and 71% of complaint cases; exception share 0.48, roughly double the team leads'; the notes explicitly describe her events as routing/assignment/clarification; ordinary claims were unaffected by her absence; her activity profile is exactly the exception vocabulary.

**Contradicting / limiting evidence.** A purely formal desk should not be the *only* node bridging both teams and both specialists — a documented desk would normally have a backup, and the absence effect shows there was none. Her subcontracting betweenness (0.69) exceeds what a pass-through desk needs. And the process owner does not describe her role the way the log does, which suggests the function is not formally specified anywhere.

**Predictions.** A `case_type`/`exception_flag`-conditioned log will show near-deterministic Bot_Rules → Mira routing for exception cases; her events will cluster tightly at position 3–4 in the exception variants; her service-time distribution will be unimodal and short; a normative routing model will show high conformance for her decisions.

## H2. Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency

**Mechanism.** Because she is the only person who knows the routing logic, exception cases cannot advance without her. She is not slow — she is *singular*. The constraint is availability, not throughput.

**Supporting evidence.** The absence quasi-experiment (exception P90 8.4 → 13.9 days, ordinary unchanged); no second resource shares her activity profile; "high variance" cycle-time contribution; she is the sole predecessor of both specialists in the handover table; adjusters describe her as "the person who knows where hard cases should go", i.e. tacit, uncodified knowledge.

**Contradicting / limiting evidence.** Only 22% of claims depend on her, so the aggregate exposure is bounded. Her own service time is trivial, so she is not a *capacity* bottleneck in the queueing sense — the specialists (37 and 51 min, 316 and 251 cases) and the vendors (2.1 h, 1.3 h) hold far more work-in-process. One 6-day window with no control period is weak causal evidence, and the queue may have been recoverable rather than lost. Some of the apparent structural indispensability is the parameterization artifact discussed in §1.

**Predictions.** Repeat absences (vacation, sickness, weekends, shift ends) will each show the same tail inflation for `exception_flag=True` and none for ordinary claims; exception-case waiting time will correlate with her concurrent workload; the queue will show a catch-up burst on her return rather than permanent loss; a specialist-side arrival analysis will show arrivals stop when she is away.

## H3. Organizational boundary spanner / triage broker

**Mechanism.** Team North, Team South, the specialist pool, the temporaries and the automation each have their own internal cohesion but no direct channel to one another. She spans those structural holes, translating "this case is abnormal" into "this case belongs with Omar / with Carla / back in the queue".

**Supporting evidence.** Highest handover out-degree and betweenness with the broadest and most cluster-diverse neighbourhood; low pairwise co-work with high aggregate breadth (the canonical broker signature — high betweenness, low clustering, thin edges); return edges from Omar and Priya (160, 140) showing round-trip mediation; escalation inflow from both temporaries; the Omar—Priya co-case rate of only 4% implies the specialists do not coordinate with each other, so someone must.

**Contradicting / limiting evidence.** Brokerage is a *positional* claim that a static SNA snapshot cannot separate from being a mandated relay. Bot_Rules already spans everything upstream, so part of the bridging is automated, not hers. If her routing were fully rule-determined, the "brokerage" would be an artifact of where the workflow put her rather than a discretionary function.

**Predictions.** Burt's constraint computed on the working-together graph will be lowest for her; node-removal simulation will fragment the resource graph into North/South/specialist components only when she is deleted; her routing will show entropy above what case attributes alone predict (real discretion) — or below, if she is a relay; role-mining will place her in a singleton cluster.

## H4. Specialist scheduler / demand shaper for the true capacity constraint

**Mechanism.** The scarce resources are Omar (330 events) and Priya (260 events) at 37 and 51 min per event. Mira is effectively their intake filter, deciding both *whether* and *when* a case reaches them. Her value is protecting scarce specialist capacity; her risk is that specialist utilization is set by one person's judgement.

**Supporting evidence.** She is the sole listed predecessor of both specialists (210, 180); the specialist return edges close the loop through her; the specialists' extreme exception shares (0.62, 0.71) show they receive a pre-filtered stream; the operations manager's prediction of "specialist overload" if she were removed is exactly this mechanism; the 42–51 min mean elapsed times on her specialist edges look like queue waiting, not her latency.

**Contradicting / limiting evidence.** The Mira—Omar/Priya co-case rates are not reported, so specialist-side dependence is inferred from the handover table alone; specialists may also receive work through paths not shown in a top-edges list. Only ~4% of cases reach a specialist at all, so the scope is narrow.

**Predictions.** Specialist queue length and idle time will track her activity hour-by-hour; during her absence specialist arrivals will drop and then spike; case attributes will predict specialist choice only partially, with a resource-specific residual; medical-vs-legal assignment for ambiguous cases will show a personal prior.

## H5. Human-in-the-loop monitor for automation

**Mechanism.** She is the designated fallback when Bot_Rules cannot decide — low-confidence fraud scores, unmatched routing rules, data-quality failures — and returns the case to the automated flow after correction.

**Supporting evidence.** The `Bot_Rules → Mira → Bot_Rules` subcontracting fragment (62) with the explicit "Fraud Screen → manual exception → Fraud Screen update" annotation; Bot_Rules' exception share (0.31) is markedly above Bot_Ingest's (0.19), consistent with the rules engine being where exceptions are *created*; Bot_Rules → Mira (920) is far larger than Bot_Rules → Alice (610) or → Bruno (590), so the largest single destination of the router is the human exception desk; Temp_1 → Vendor_Verify → Request Missing Information shows a parallel vendor-failure loop.

**Contradicting / limiting evidence.** This explains at most a few hundred events, not her whole profile; Reopen and Escalate Complaint have nothing to do with automation monitoring. Whether she is *correcting* the bot or merely *recording* its output is not observable in the log.

**Predictions.** Her events will concentrate on cases where Bot_Rules produced a low-confidence or null decision; automation-related touches will co-move with rules-engine releases and show concept drift after deployments; her Fraud-Screen-adjacent events will be systematically shorter than her escalation events.

## H6. Quality gatekeeper / informal appeals channel

**Mechanism.** Reopen Claim and Escalate Complaint are the process's failure-detection activities, and she owns them. She is where the process discovers it got something wrong.

**Supporting evidence.** 64% of reopens, 71% of complaints; she holds Reopen and Escalate while no one else holds both; the compliance reviewer's observation that she both detects and dispatches is a factual description of this profile; the highest exception share among non-specialists.

**Contradicting / limiting evidence.** Detection and *recording* of detection are indistinguishable in an event log — the complaint may be raised by a customer and merely logged by her, which would make her a scribe rather than a gatekeeper. Alice also performs Reopen Claim (so the function is at least partly shared), and she has no approval authority, which a genuine quality gate would normally require.

**Predictions.** Reopen causes, once a reason code exists, will be attributable upstream rather than to her; complaint arrival timestamps will precede her events by a customer-contact interval; reopen rates by original handler will differ systematically, and those differences will be visible in her routing choices.

## H7. Measurement / data artifact

**Mechanism.** Part of the "contradiction" is produced by the analysis pipeline, not by the organization: non-immediate-succession and multiple-occurrence SNA settings inflate her handover and subcontracting counts; an unconditional denominator deflates her working-together weight; short bookkeeping events manufacture both handover and A→B→A patterns.

**Supporting evidence.** The two numeric inconsistencies in §1 (1,640 out-edges and 286 subcontracting patterns from 960 events over 905 cases); `Assign Specialist` missing from the 14-activity catalogue; `lifecycle:transition` present but with no stated filter; the notes themselves attributing her low co-work to "short routing events".

**Contradicting / limiting evidence.** Artifacts cannot explain the absence experiment, the reopen/complaint concentration, or the qualitative testimony. This hypothesis therefore *rescales* the others rather than replacing them.

**Predictions.** Recomputing with `beta=0`, single-occurrence, immediate-successor settings and `lifecycle:transition == complete` will cut her raw edge counts by roughly half to two-thirds while leaving her rank order intact; conditional normalization will move her working-together degree from peripheral to mid-pack; the `Assign Specialist` label will resolve to either an undocumented activity or a second system's event stream.

**Overall stance.** H1 + H3 + H4 are largely *the same behaviour described at three levels* (job description, network position, capacity effect) and together account for most of the evidence. H2 is best read as a *property* of that behaviour under current staffing rather than a rival explanation. H5 and H6 are real but partial sub-roles. H7 sets the error bars on all of them.

# 3. Risk assessment

**Bottleneck risk — moderate, narrow, and probably availability-driven rather than capacity-driven.** Her own service time is negligible, so she is not saturating. But she is a *serialization point* for the exception stream: 22% of claims cannot advance until she acts once. The absence data (P90 +65% for exception cases, no change for ordinary ones) is the expected signature. Calibration: one 6-day window, no control, no evidence about whether the backlog was recovered. Also, the largest waiting-time contributors in this process are Vendor_Verify and Vendor_Pay, and the largest structural exposure is Bot_Rules, which touches ~all 4,100 claims. Fixing Mira while ignoring the vendors would optimize the smaller term.

**Coordination risk — the substantive risk, and it is about knowledge, not behaviour.** The routing logic appears to live in one person's head ("she knows where hard cases should go"). Nothing in the log records *why* a case went to Omar rather than Priya or back to an adjuster. That makes the coordination function unauditable, untrainable and unimprovable — and it also means management genuinely cannot tell whether her decisions are good. The disagreement between the process owner ("queue cleaner") and the adjusters ("knows where hard cases go") is itself a coordination-risk indicator: the organization does not share a model of what this role does.

**Key-person risk — real, bounded, and currently unmitigated.** No other resource shares her activity profile; the team leads lack Reopen/Escalate/Assign Specialist and are busy with Triage and Approve. There is no observable deputy. Combined with tacit routing knowledge, the exposure is a *knowledge* dependency more than a *capacity* one, which is worse in one respect (harder to backfill) and better in another (cheaper to fix, via documentation and cross-training, than by hiring).

**Compliance / control risk — a legitimate design concern, with no evidence of wrongdoing.** The compliance reviewer's point is structurally valid: the same actor detects the anomaly and selects the next handler, so there is no independent check on allocation. Three calibrations are essential. (1) Her discretion is **allocative, not adjudicative** — she performs no Approve Settlement, no Triage, no Negotiate, no Issue Payment. Monetary outcomes are still gated by team leads and Vendor_Pay, so the classic segregation-of-duties failure (self-approval) is *not* present in this log. (2) Allocative discretion nonetheless matters, because who reviews a claim can influence its outcome and its cycle time — this is a control *gap*, not a control *breach*. (3) There is **no evidence whatsoever of improper influence, favouritism or manipulation**, and none should be implied. The correct characterization is: an undocumented discretionary control point in a regulated process, which should be made observable before anyone judges it.

**Workload fairness risk — visible but ambiguous in both directions.** Her 48% exception share means she carries a disproportionately difficult mix, and short median service times understate cognitive load and interrupt-driven work; escalation desks are classic burnout roles. Conversely, 960 short events could be read as light duty, which is presumably how "queue cleaner" arose. Neither reading is supportable from event counts alone — service time is a poor proxy for effort, and there is no data on concurrency, interruptions or after-hours work. Note also the asymmetries worth checking for equity: Elise/Farid (juniors) absorb Request Missing Information alongside Assess Damage, the temporaries carry low-exception routine work at 0.13–0.14, and Alice performs Reopen while Bruno does not.

**Data-quality risk — high, and it currently caps every other conclusion.** Concretely: (a) `Assign Specialist` is not in the documented 14-activity catalogue; (b) her handover edge counts (~1,640 out) and subcontracting counts (286 patterns) are numerically incompatible with 960 events over 905 cases, so the SNA parameters are non-default or the windows differ; (c) `lifecycle:transition` exists but no filtering is documented, risking start/complete double-counting; (d) no routing-reason attribute, so intent is unrecorded; (e) the absence analysis has no matched control period; (f) `Bot_Rules` handles both Triage and Fraud Screen with 4,750 events over 4,090 cases, and Vendor_Verify shows 1,550 events over 1,500 cases against Temp_1/Temp_2's 990 Validate Coverage events, so coverage validation is split across three channels in a way that needs verifying before any resource comparison is trusted. **Recommendation: do not make irreversible organizational decisions on these numbers until (a)–(c) are resolved.**

# 4. Further process-mining analyses

Assume `log = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id='case:concept:name', activity_key='concept:name', timestamp_key='time:timestamp')`, with `org:resource` as the resource key, and that `lifecycle:transition` is filtered to a single transition first.

**A. Handover-of-work: parameter sensitivity and conditional networks.**
Recompute `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` under a grid: `beta=0` vs `beta>0`, direct successors only vs all successors, single vs multiple occurrence. Report her out-degree, in-degree and betweenness for each cell. This directly tests H7 and tells us how much of her 0.82/0.77 is a parameter choice. Then split the log with `pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values` on `exception_flag` and build separate handover networks: the prediction is that she barely exists in the `False` network and dominates the `True` one. Compare edge asymmetry (Mira→X vs X→Mira) to distinguish dispatch from round-trip mediation, and always report the bot and vendor nodes in the same graph so the comparison is not human-only.

**B. Working-together: fix the normalization, then measure brokerage properly.**
Recompute `pm4py.discover_working_together_network` and convert raw weights to (i) conditional rates P(partner | resource's own cases) and (ii) Jaccard / cosine similarity over case sets, instead of share-of-all-cases. Export to `networkx` and compute, per resource, weighted degree, local clustering coefficient, Burt's constraint and effective size. Prediction: her raw weight stays low, her conditional weight rises to team-lead levels, her clustering is the lowest and her effective size the highest in the graph — the quantitative statement of "broker, not periphery". Add node-removal simulation (delete each resource, recompute the number of connected components and average path length) to size structural indispensability for her *and* for Bot_Rules.

**C. Subcontracting: sensitivity to n and to bookkeeping events.**
Run `pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network` for n ∈ {1, 2, 3, 5} and check whether the Mira→Omar/Priya patterns survive at n = 1 and how fast they inflate. Then re-run on a log with pure-coordination activities (Assign Specialist, Reopen Claim) removed: if the A→B→A signature collapses, the pattern is bookkeeping (limits H1/H4's "ownership" reading); if it survives via substantive activities, genuine delegate-and-resume is supported. Also reconcile the reported frequencies against `groupby('org:resource')` event and case counts as a hard consistency check, and inspect the `Bot_Rules → Mira → Bot_Rules` fragment separately to test H5.

**D. Activity-based similarity and organizational role discovery.**
Run `pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` and `pm4py.discover_organizational_roles`. Confirm whether she forms a singleton role, joins the team leads, or joins the information-chasers (Temp_1, Elise, Farid). Then repeat on the exception-only sublog and on time-sliced sublogs (weeks 1–4, 5–8, 9–12) to detect role drift. Extend the similarity vector beyond activity frequency to include successor distribution and case-attribute mix (`case_type`, `priority`, `amount_band`), then cluster: if she remains distinct under every representation, "distinct organizational function" is robust rather than an artifact of one feature set.

**E. Filtering: exception_flag, activities, variants, time ranges.**
Use `pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values` on `exception_flag=True/False` and on the specialist activities; use `pm4py.filter_variants` to extract the top exception variants and check her position and multiplicity within them; use time-range filtering to cut the log into the 6-day absence window plus matched control windows (same weekdays, adjacent weeks, similar volume and case mix). For the absence question, run a proper interrupted-time-series or difference-in-differences design: exception cases as treated, ordinary cases as control, with bootstrap confidence intervals on the P90 rather than a point comparison, and an explicit check for post-absence catch-up throughput. Separately, build a routing-audit dataset (one row per exception case: attributes in, next resource out) and fit an interpretable model; measure how much of her choice is explained by `case_type`, `priority`, `amount_band`, `region`, `channel`. High explained variance means her routing is effectively rule-based and codifiable; a large residual quantifies real discretion — and residual dependence on `region` or `channel` after controlling for `case_type` is the specific fairness signal a regulator would ask about. Report it as a signal to investigate, not as a finding.

**F. Service and waiting time decomposition.**
Use `pm4py.get_service_time` and `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` to split cycle time into service and waiting per case, then attribute waiting to the *predecessor-successor pair* rather than to a resource. Specific comparisons: waiting before her events (queue in front of the exception desk) vs waiting after them (specialist/adjuster queue); the same for Vendor_Verify (2.1 h) and Vendor_Pay (1.3 h), which should be quantified first because they are the largest terms; and specialist queue length over time against her hourly activity. Complement with survival analysis of time-to-specialist-review and a simple queueing or discrete-event simulation of the exception path under scenarios: Mira present, Mira absent, Mira plus a trained deputy, and routing rules codified into Bot_Rules. That simulation is the only defensible way to price any structural change before making it. Finally, run concept-drift checks over the 12 weeks on both routing shares and cycle times, so a trend is not mistaken for a person effect.

# 5. Recommendation

## Change immediately (low-risk, reversible, mostly instrumentation)

1. **Fix the measurement before the organization.** Resolve `Assign Specialist` against the activity catalogue; document and standardize SNA parameters (successor scope, `beta`, occurrence counting, `lifecycle:transition` filter); republish the handover and subcontracting figures with the arithmetic reconciled against event/case counts. Half of the current "contradiction" is a reporting problem and it is cheap to remove.
2. **Report working-together with a conditional denominator.** Any dashboard that describes a 22%-coverage resource as "peripheral" using an all-case denominator will keep producing wrong management conclusions.
3. **Capture the routing decision as data.** Add a mandatory reason code and target-queue field to Assign Specialist / Escalate / Reopen. This is the single highest-value change: it converts tacit knowledge into an auditable, trainable, improvable asset, and it addresses the compliance reviewer's concern without changing anyone's authority.
4. **Name and train a documented deputy** for the exception desk (a senior adjuster or a team lead is the natural candidate) and put a real backup on the roster for absences. This removes the key-person exposure at essentially no structural cost.
5. **Make the exception queue visible** with its own SLA and dashboard (queue depth, age, P50/P90 by `exception_flag`, specialist queue length). The absence incident was detected retrospectively; it should have been visible live.
6. **Formalize the role in writing.** The "queue cleaner" vs "knows where hard cases go" split is a management failure, not a data finding. Publish the role's scope and boundaries — this costs nothing and removes a real source of friction and mis-measurement.
7. **Start the vendor conversation in parallel.** Vendor_Verify at 2.1 h over 1,500 cases and Vendor_Pay at 1.3 h over 2,840 cases are, in expected minutes per claim, the largest delay terms in the process. Do not let the interesting human question crowd out the bigger arithmetic.

## Monitor (instrument now, decide later)

- Exception-queue depth and P90 cycle time for `exception_flag=True`, continuously and with her presence/absence as a covariate; repeat the absence analysis on every subsequent gap with matched controls.
- Specialist utilization and queue length for Omar and Priya, plus their arrival mix, to test H4 and to detect whether she is buffering or starving them.
- Routing-model residual: what fraction of her assignments is explained by case attributes. A shrinking residual means the rules are ready to be codified; a persistent residual localizes where genuine expertise lives.
- Reopen and complaint rates by *original* handler once reason codes exist, to see whether reopens are upstream quality problems rather than exception-desk volume.
- Bot_Rules' exception rate (0.31 vs Bot_Ingest's 0.19) and its trend after each rules release — if automation quality improves, her Bot_Rules inflow should fall, which is the cleanest path to reducing the dependency.
- Fairness indicators: her caseload difficulty mix, junior adjusters' Request Missing Information share, and the temporaries' scope creep.
- Whether backlogs recover after her absences (deferred work) or persist (lost capacity). These two cases call for opposite interventions.

## Do not change until more evidence is available

- **Do not remove, reassign or reduce the exception-desk function.** The evidence for its value (selective absence effect, 64%/71% concentration, unique activity profile) is stronger than the evidence for any problem it causes. If a future decision does remove or split it, the coordination function must be explicitly replaced first, by all four of: (i) codified routing rules embedded in Bot_Rules with a low-confidence fallback, (ii) a pull-based specialist intake queue with published prioritization so cases can advance without a named router, (iii) a trained, rostered deputy pool, and (iv) escalation SLAs with monitoring that would surface a routing failure within hours. Absent all four, removal converts a 22%-of-claims dependency into a general escalation and specialist-overload problem — exactly the operations manager's prediction, and the absence data is consistent with it.
- **Do not split detection from assignment yet**, despite the compliance concern being legitimate in principle. She holds no approval, triage or payment authority, so the immediate segregation-of-duties exposure is limited to allocation. Adding a second approver to every routing step would lengthen the path for the process's hardest cases. Instrument first (reason codes plus the routing audit); if the audit shows outcome-relevant discretion, then introduce a targeted control — e.g. dual assignment only for high `amount_band` or complaint cases — rather than a blanket gate.
- **Do not restructure teams, redistribute her work to Alice and Bruno, or change her grade** on the current numbers. The team leads are already the approval bottleneck for settlements, and the SNA figures underpinning any such case are not yet reconciled.
- **Do not draw performance conclusions from her 4.2-minute median service time in either direction.** It supports neither "underworked" nor "overloaded".
- **Do not treat her high centrality as a problem to be eliminated.** In a process with a designed exception path, someone must be central there; the goal is to make that centrality documented, backed up and observable, not to flatten it.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira_Coordinator is best understood as the organization's exception router — not a team lead, and not a hidden gatekeeper of decisions. Almost every hard case passes through her once: she receives it from the rules engine, decides where it should go, and hands it on. That single short touch explains the apparent contradiction in the numbers. Handover and subcontracting analyses count transitions, so a resource who sits between teams looks extremely central. The working-together analysis counts shared cases as a share of all claims, and she touches only 22 percent of them, so every partnership looks thin. Measured against her own caseload, she is in fact tightly connected to both teams. The evidence points to real value and real fragility: exception cases queued up during her six-day absence, while ordinary claims were unaffected. We therefore recommend documenting her routing rules, training a deputy, and improving the data before changing anything structural about the role.
