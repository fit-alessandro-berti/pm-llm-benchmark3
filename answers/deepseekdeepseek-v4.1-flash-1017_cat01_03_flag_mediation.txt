## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

P0 = current system. P1 = suppress display of the flag but keep the risk score and automatic routing unchanged. P2 = keep priority triage but remove automatic mandatory manual review. P3 = keep current routing and add protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases. P4 = keep manual review but change escalation criteria or senior-team capacity.

I use **interventional/controlled analogues**, not natural direct/indirect effects. Natural effects are poorly defined here because mediators are post-exposure, share queues, affect future queue states, and have mediator-outcome confounding by hidden risk/complexity. Where useful, I state separable or stochastic interventional versions of “occurrence” vs “waiting/service” mechanisms.

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | P0 vs P1 | All newly arrived cases eligible under current policy, per case | Total cycle time, arrival to close | Total policy effect of masking flag display; automatic routing unchanged | Partly; yes if display fully blinded, no if risk score reveals flag | Display-only may not change automatic routing; staff may infer flag from score or queue lane |
| E2 | P0 vs P2 | All eligible cases, per case | Total cycle time | Total policy effect of removing automatic mandatory review while retaining priority triage | Yes under protocolized removal | Optional human review, hidden risk, changed queue composition, resource reallocation |
| E3 | P0 vs P1 | Flagged cases under P0 eligibility | Arrival-to-triage-done time; triage service time | Controlled/total component effect on triage completion | Partly; automatic priority lane unchanged | If display occurs after triage, effect should be null; score visibility may break masking |
| E4 | P0 vs P2 | Flagged cases under P0 eligibility | Total cycle time | Interventional/separable indirect effect through manual-review occurrence | Only as a separable/interventional analogue | Occurrence changes queue composition and post-review selection; not a natural indirect effect |
| E5 | P0 vs P3 | Flagged reviewed cases | Total cycle time | Interventional/controlled indirect effect through manual queue wait | Partly; capacity changes wait, service, and shared queue | Shared queues and interference; protected capacity may reallocate work and change service mix |
| E6 | P0 vs P4 | Flagged reviewed cases eligible for escalation | Total cycle time | Interventional/separable indirect effect through escalation occurrence and/or escalation queue | Yes if P4 is a clear protocol; partial otherwise | Escalation uses unmeasured risk/outcome; scarce senior team creates interference |
| E7 | P0 vs P3 | All eligible cases, per case | Total cycle time | Total policy effect of protected review capacity | Yes under implemented capacity policy | Spillovers to unflagged cases and queue feedback; positivity under high load |

Interference arises because review and senior queues are shared: one case’s review/escalation changes another case’s waiting time. Exposure-induced confounding arises because flag triggers routing, which changes later queue state and resource assignment for the same and other cases. Treatment-confounder feedback arises because earlier triage speed changes congestion, manual review changes escalation decisions, and escalation changes senior queue state. Hidden risk and complexity can confound mediator-outcome relationships. Standard natural direct/indirect effects are therefore not identified; queue-aware interventional analogues are preferable.

Selected pilot: **P3, protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases**.  
**Eligibility:** newly arrived flagged cases in participating teams/shifts; include unflagged cases for spillover monitoring; urgent safety overrides allowed.  
**Assignment:** stepped-wedge or cluster randomization by team/shift/queue, not by individual case, to reduce contamination.  
**Queue-capacity guardrail:** reserve a prespecified maximum share of manual-review capacity, e.g. 20–25%; automatically suspend protection if unflagged waits exceed a threshold or high-risk overdue reviews accumulate.  
**Primary outcomes:** flagged total cycle time and flagged triage-done-to-manual-start wait.  
**Secondary outcomes:** manual-review occurrence, escalation rate, triage wait, unflagged cycle/wait, resource utilization, decision quality (reversals, appeals, adverse events).  
**Minimum data:** all timestamps, flag, risk_score, complexity, arrival_load, stage queue occupancy, resource/team IDs, and adjudicated decision outcomes.  
**Stopping conditions:** decision-quality harm signal, unacceptable unflagged wait spillover, inability to maintain protected capacity, or futility after a prespecified sample.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk:** If unmeasured severity strongly predicts both flag assignment and long downstream processing, the apparent flag-bundle delay may be hidden-risk confounding and the mechanism interpretation would reverse.
2. **Queue interference:** If protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases materially lengthens unflagged waits and feeds back into overall congestion, P3 would no longer be preferred on system performance.
3. **Decision quality:** If removing automatic mandatory review shortens cycle time without increasing harmful approvals, rejections, or adverse outcomes, P2 would become more attractive and the recommendation would reverse.
4. **Timestamp semantics:** If local clock time, overnight gaps, manual timestamp entry, or start/done definitions distort manual and escalation waits, the stage decomposition and any waiting-mediated conclusion could reverse.
5. **Positivity or lack of overlap:** If there are effectively no unflagged high-risk cases, no flagged normal-load cases, or no comparable reviewed cases across policies, the policy contrasts are extrapolations and the preferred pilot may be infeasible.
6. **Behavior change by reviewers:** If masking the flag or protecting capacity changes reviewer thresholds, escalation behavior, or resource scheduling in ways not captured by the intervention, the observed mechanism and policy ranking could reverse.

## 9. Final synthesis

Directly observed: flagged cases are triaged much faster—mean arrival-to-triage-start 14.4 vs 148.8 minutes and triage service 36.0 vs 57.0 minutes—yet their total cycle is much longer, 990.6 vs 490.2 minutes. All flagged cases had manual review, and 6/10 escalated; reviewed flagged cases waited 390.0 minutes from triage completion to manual start, versus 112.5 minutes for reviewed unflagged cases. Their longer process is therefore not explained by slower triage or manual service; it is concentrated in manual queue waiting, escalation, and case complexity. Plausible mechanism: the flag bundle gives triage priority but routes almost all flagged cases into shared manual and senior queues, where waiting and interference dominate. However, flag is a policy marker derived from an imperfect score and correlates with complexity and hidden risk. What remains unidentified: occurrence-versus-waiting mediation, escalation selection, queue spillovers, and decision quality; natural direct/indirect effects are not warranted. The most useful next evidence comes from a queue-level pilot of P3: protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases, with stepped-wedge or team randomization, spillover monitoring, and decision-quality outcomes. P3 is safer than removing mandatory review (P2), and it tests whether the wait bottleneck is causal while preserving review for high-risk flags.
