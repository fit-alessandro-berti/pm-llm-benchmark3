## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts used as anchors**

- `GPIP-4` is the highest-formality source here: board-approved, enterprise-wide, effective before all case events.
- `DE-AAC-PROC-17 Rev. 6` is a controlled local SOP that can add stricter site controls but cannot silently weaken `GPIP-4`.
- `ECD-2` is a written, time-limited incident directive effective from `2026-07-14 09:00 CEST` through `2026-07-18 18:00 CEST`, and is expressly limited to "critical sterilization-line lots required to contain the current contamination event."
- The CFO email of `2026-07-15 07:20 CEST` is lower-formality than A-C, but it is still relevant because the CFO has explicit authority to interpret Finance controls.
- The outage note changes evidential weight only. It does not create a substantive exception.

**Interpretive assumptions**

- I do not impose one universal ranking. I use an issue-specific hierarchy: enterprise integrity controls, local workflow rules, incident-specific workflow overrides, and evidential rules are assessed separately.
- `GPIP-4 A1` and `A3` are preserved unless an authorized exception clearly says otherwise. `ECD-2` and the CFO email are too silent and too weak, respectively, to impliedly waive segregation of duties or Compliance clearance.
- `ECD-2` can, however, override local workflow timing and supplier-register restrictions within its own scope and time window because `B7` allows a corporate incident directive to supersede ordinary local workflow steps, and `C1/C2/C4` expressly speak to timing and supplier-onboarding.
- Narrow reconciliation of `A2` with `C3` and `D1/D2`: for covered `ECD-2` purchases, I treat joint authorization by two distinct Incident Command members as satisfying the emergency business-approval function, and I treat the CFO email as satisfying the finance-control function, but only within the `ECD-2` window and only where neither authorizer is the requester or committer. This is an interpretation, not an express textual fact.
- I do not extend that reconciliation to cases before `2026-07-15 07:20`, after `2026-07-18 18:00`, outside `ECD-2` scope, or where the same person requests/commits and also serves as the effective final approver.
- I read "critical sterilization-line lots" narrowly: parts for the affected line, or items needed to restore that line in the immediate response interval, are in scope; backup-line contingency stock with roughly three days remaining is outside the main reading.
- For `B6`, I treat `C3`'s required Incident Command record as a functional substitute for the local emergency form in `ECD-2` cases, unless the facts show no incident record at all.

**Decision method**

1. Identify the legally meaningful act time, usually `Commit_to_Supplier`, and apply rules that existed at that moment.
2. Test whether the case is inside the relevant source scope: site, purchase type, incident window, and substantive emergency scope.
3. Apply explicit overrides only to the issues they actually address. Silence does not impliedly waive preserved controls.
4. Treat retrospective evidence as proof of a timely act only if stronger contemporaneous evidence exists; late approvals do not automatically cure a missing pre-commitment control.
5. Put the burden of proof on the organization. Where authority or evidence is incomplete, the organization must show why an exception path was valid.

`overrides_for(po_timing, case, t) if in_window(ECD2, t) and in_scope(case, critical_sterilization_lot).`

`overrides_for(supplier_register, case, t) if in_window(ECD2, t) and in_scope(case, critical_sterilization_lot).`

`require(legal_before_commit(case)) if d2_applies(case) and amount(case) > 150000.`

`forbidden(final_business_approver(x, case)) if requester(x, case) or committer(x, case).`

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

1. `P-505` could change from `COMPLIANT` to `NONCOMPLIANT` if one rejects my narrow reconciliation of `A2` and reads the CFO email as unable to substitute for the enterprise pre-commit approval structure. That alternative is credible because `A2` is board-level text and `D1` is an uncontrolled email. It is weaker because the CFO does have explicit authority over Finance controls, and the contemporaneous supplier acknowledgment strongly supports a within-window commitment.

2. `P-507` could change from `COMPLIANT` to `NONCOMPLIANT` if "must be recorded within four hours" in `C3` is read to require an ERP entry, not any signed incident record. That reading is textually plausible. I reject it because the signed PDF was created within four hours, and the case facts specifically mention a document-system outage, which makes a substance-over-system reading more defensible.

3. `P-508` could change from `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` to `COMPLIANT` if `D2` is read as unambiguously incorporating `D1` and simply adding a Legal checkpoint above `EUR 150,000`. That is credible because `D2` says "as well," which naturally reads as cumulative. I still stop at `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` because the controlling source is only an email and the amount is high enough that a stricter authority reading is reasonable.

4. `P-503` could change from `NONCOMPLIANT` to `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` only if two premises change at once: first, backup-line contingency pumps with three days of stock must count as "critical sterilization-line lots"; second, `PROC-3` must be allowed to serve as requester, committer, and effective emergency approver. The first premise is arguable because Operations used the label "critical contingency." The second premise is weak because it directly conflicts with the purpose and text of `A1`.

5. `P-506` could change from `NONCOMPLIANT` to `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` if a written extension of `ECD-2`, issued before `2026-07-18 18:00`, or a contemporaneous dual-IC authorization record is produced. That alternative is credible because contamination recovery clearly continued after the formal window. It is weak on the present record because the directive had an express expiry and the only claimed extension is oral.

6. `P-502` could change from `NONCOMPLIANT` to `INDETERMINATE` or even `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` if one treats `ECD-2` itself as a de facto enterprise exception to `A3` for same-day containment purchases from new suppliers. That is not frivolous given the severity of the incident and the organization's prior practice of treating incident directives as temporary overlays. It remains weak because prior practice is expressly not shown for new-supplier Compliance clearance, and `A4/A7` strongly point the other way.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

**How the cases split**

| Category | Cases | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Policy breach | `P-501`, `P-502`, `P-503`, `P-504`, `P-506` | The substantive pre-commit control was missing, the case was out of scope/time, or preserved GPIP controls were not met |
| Logging defect | `P-507` | Core approvals were timely, but system posting lagged; the defect is in recording mechanics, not in the control itself |
| Late evidence of a timely act | `P-505`, `P-507` | Stronger contemporaneous evidence supports a timely approval/commit despite later ERP entries |
| Inability to prove full compliance cleanly | `P-505`, `P-508` | The case is defensible, but the record is not as formal as the baseline policy would ideally require |

**Immediate case-level actions**

- `P-501`: record as a control breach; document why no pre-commit Plant/Finance sign-off occurred; retrain on the fact that local emergency does not waive Finance or Plant controls.
- `P-502`: perform post-incident Compliance review of the new supplier; determine whether goods can remain in use; mark the late Compliance as non-curative.
- `P-503`: open an integrity review focused on role concentration by `PROC-3`; require management sign-off on whether the purchase should have been scoped into the incident at all.
- `P-504`: treat as misuse of the emergency path; require normal vendor review and local management action because the purchase was outside the contamination-response scope.
- `P-505`: preserve call logs, the supplier acknowledgment with full headers, and the clock-drift evidence; annotate ERP so later reviewers do not misread the `18:06` entry as dispositive.
- `P-506`: require a written management memorandum acknowledging that the directive had expired; no ratification should be framed as curing the missing pre-commit path.
- `P-507`: preserve the signed PDF, document-system outage notice, and ERP lag explanation; classify the late ERP entry as a recording defect, not a breach.
- `P-508`: require an executive post-incident review because of the amount and the reliance on `D2`; preserve all role/approval evidence in one packet.

**Monitoring actions**

- Implement an automatic block or escalation when a buyer tries to commit after a directive's expiry timestamp without a written extension.
- Add a mandatory scope code on every incident purchase: affected line, fallback line, hours to failure, and whether the item is needed to contain the incident or merely to improve resilience.
- Add a real-time segregation check that flags any requester or committer also entered as emergency approver.
- Require emergency authorizations to be captured in a single incident register with timestamp source, approver identity, and whether the evidence is oral call, signed PDF, or system entry.
- Run a 30-day post-incident audit of all emergency buys from unapproved or new suppliers and all buys over `EUR 100,000`.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

Proposed replacement clause:

> **Incident Procurement Hierarchy and Exception Rule**
>
> 1. `GPIP-4 A1` segregation of duties and `A3` Compliance clearance for new suppliers or purchases above `EUR 100,000` remain mandatory unless waived by a written exception signed by the Chief Risk Officer or formally designated deputy.
> 2. A site or corporate incident directive may suspend only the following local controls, and only within its written scope and time window: PO-before-commit timing, local supplier-register restrictions, and named documentation mechanics.
> 3. Every incident directive must define covered categories by asset, line, and urgency threshold. "Critical" must mean either required to avoid shutdown within a stated number of hours or required to restore the affected line.
> 4. During a valid incident directive, dual authorization by two pre-designated Incident Command members may substitute for ordinary business approval only if neither authorizer is the requester or committer.
> 5. Finance concurrence during an incident must be given either by a Controller, the CFO, or a pre-named finance delegate. Amounts above `EUR 150,000` additionally require Legal clearance before commitment.
> 6. Oral incident authorizations are valid only if recorded within four hours in an approved evidence channel. If systems are unavailable, signed PDFs, recorded calls, or timestamped emails are acceptable, but they must be uploaded to the central register within 24 hours.
> 7. No incident directive may be orally extended. Extensions must be written, issued before expiry, and must state the new expiry time and any changed approval matrix.
> 8. Later approvals do not cure missing pre-commit controls; they may only mitigate consequences after the breach is recorded.
> 9. Safeguard against emergency normalization: if the same site uses the incident path for the same supplier or item class more than twice in 30 days, Procurement Risk must review whether a standing sourcing or supplier-approval remedy is required.

This replacement text resolves the present uncertainty by making authority, scope, thresholds, segregation, new-supplier treatment, acceptable retrospective evidence, and expiry mechanics explicit, while also preventing the emergency path from becoming a de facto normal workflow.
