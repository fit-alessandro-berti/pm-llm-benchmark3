# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

The procurement-to-payment log identifies potential control exceptions and patterns requiring targeted review, but does not establish collusion or other misconduct. Frequent collaboration between Aline and Elena, and between Boris and Felix, is consistent with assigned teams and high workload. Rapid approvals and return loops involving Carla–Gita and Dan–Hugo are plausibly associated with emergency medical procurement and weekend repairs. Their emergency rationale and compliance with retrospective review requirements should nevertheless be verified.

The most concerning subgroup is the VendorMaster_Lena–AP_Kim bank-change/payment cohort. It combines nine reported same-human bank-change/payment approval cases, short approval intervals, high-value exposure, vendor-family concentration, and substantial manual-source usage. These counts overlap potentially and should not be added. Legitimate V-77x banking-platform maintenance and specialized payment routing remain credible explanations. The present log lacks maintenance-ticket linkage, so independent bank verification and authorization cannot yet be established.

We recommend prioritizing original bank-change records, beneficiary details, maintenance tickets, authenticated approval identities, and payment-release timestamps. Emergency cases should be tested for a justification by a different human within three working days, using the applicable working calendar. Same-day emergency approval does not itself demonstrate authorization to waive separation of duties.

Data-quality limitations materially affect interpretation. Shared accounts, bot-to-human mappings, and quarter-close uploads may misstate actors, event order, and elapsed time. Reconciliation should separate confirmed control failures from unresolved records and recording artifacts. Confirmed exceptions warrant remediation; attribution of intent requires additional evidence beyond network frequency, short intervals, or process deviations.
