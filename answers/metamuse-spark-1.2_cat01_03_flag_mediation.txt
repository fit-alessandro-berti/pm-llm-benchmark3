## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

The flag is not a standalone cause but a signal that triggers a *bundle* of organizational actions. "Set flag=0" without changing the rule that creates the flag or the downstream routing is ill-defined, and "set flag=0" vs "suppress display of flag but keep risk information" vs "change routing given flag" are different hypothetical manipulations. With shared queues interference and mediator-outcome confounding induced by the flag itself, cross-world counterfactuals are not identified.

We use **separable effects / interventional (in)direct effects** framework rather than natural direct/indirect effects. Natural effects require cross-world independence and no exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounding, both violated here. Controlled direct effects (CDE) are definable by fixing a mediator, but interventional effects that fix the *distribution* of the mediator under a policy are more policy-relevant when positivity fails and when we want P2-P4 contrasts.

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1-TOTAL | P0 (current bundle: flag visible + priority triage + near-automatic manual review + current escalation) vs P1 (suppress flag display, keep underlying `risk_score`, `complexity`, human judgment and capacity unchanged) | single case arriving at a given time, population of all arriving cases in observation window | `cycle_time` = `close`-`arrival` | total effect (separable effect of the flag component) | marginally well-defined if we define intervention as withholding display, not rewriting history of risk; not well-defined as do(flag=0) holding risk_score fixed | imperfect `risk_score` omits `U_underlying_risk`; confounding by `complexity`; interference via shared queues violates SUTVA; flag is deterministic function of threshold so positivity fails near cutoff without local variation |
| E2-TRIAGE | P0 vs P1 | same as E1 | `triage_done`-`arrival` (or `triage_service` and `triage_wait` separately) | total effect on early stage; also CDE of flag on triage holding `manual_review`=0 | well-defined | measurement of waiting vs service requires `start_timestamp`; `arrival_load` coarse; resource assignment `triage_resource` is post-treatment collider |
| E3-OCCURRENCE | P0 vs P2 (retain priority lane but remove mandatory manual review: flagged cases reviewed only on same human criteria as unflagged) | flagged-eligible population (risk_score > threshold or complexity=High) | `cycle_time` | interventional indirect effect via `manual_review` occurrence (and sequential indirect via escalation conditional on review) | well-defined as policy contrast; not as NDE/NIE | exposure-induced confounding: `flag` -> `manual_queue_wait` and `arrival_load` -> `manual_review` -> `cycle_time`; strong positivity violation: P(flag=1, manual_review=0) 0 under P0 |
| E4-WAITING | P0 vs P3 (keep routing, add protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases vs shared queue) | flagged cases and unflagged cases queued for manual review | `triage_done`-to-`manual_start` (`manual_queue_wait`) and `cycle_time` | indirect effect mediated by `manual_queue_wait` holding `manual_review`=1, separable effect of queue mechanism | well-defined as capacity intervention | interference: one case's flag affects another's wait; `manual_queue_wait` not manipulable without changing senior capacity; feedback: future queue state depends on past flag prevalence |
| E5-ESCALATION | P0 vs P4 (change escalation threshold/capacity: e.g., higher review score required for escalation or added `senior_service` capacity) | cases that enter manual review | `escalation` indicator, `escalation_queue_wait`+`senior_service`, `cycle_time` | interventional indirect via `escalation` occurrence and via `escalation_queue_wait` | well-defined as policy | sequential mediation: `manual_service` outcome affects escalation decision; `U_underlying_risk` confounds manual->escalation->cycle_time; scarcity makes escalation queue interference large |
| E6-CYCLE-POLICY | P2 vs P0 and P3 vs P0 and P4 vs P0 | whole system (all arrivals) including spillover to unflagged | `cycle_time` distributional (median, 90th pct) | total policy effect decomposed into direct vs queue-mediated spillover (interventional analogue) | well-defined | interference and queuing dynamics mean total effect  average individual effect; need system-level estimand |
| E7-QUALITY | P0 vs P1 and P0 vs P2 | cases with decision | `decision_outcome` (Approve/Reject), and discordance vs later audit if available | total effect and CDE on outcome (CDE fixing `manual_review` to isolate triage speed vs review quality tradeoff) | only partially well-defined: outcome is not time but decision quality needs ground truth `U_true_risk` | unobserved `U_true_risk`/`U_underlying_risk` confounds flag->review->outcome; selective review creates collider bias; decision quality  observed outcome without external label |

> Framework choice: **Separable/interventional effects** for E3-E6 because they map to implementable levers (priority, mandatory review, capacity, escalation threshold) and avoid cross-world counterfactuals `Y(1,M(0))`. We report CDE only where fixing mediator corresponds to a feasible operation (e.g., triage stage before review). We avoid claiming identified NDE/NIE.

Interference, exposure-induced confounding, and feedback in 180 words: Shared `manual_review` and `senior_service` queues create **interference**: flag prevalence for other cases at time t raises `manual_queue_wait` and `escalation_queue_wait` for index case, violating SUTVA. **Exposure-induced confounding**: flag increases `manual_review` and also changes queue state (`arrival_load` interaction, priority allocation); queue state then confounds the `manual_review`->`cycle_time` and `escalation`->`cycle_time` relationships because waiting and service times share capacity. **Treatment-confounder feedback**: fixing manual review to isolate a direct effect is impossible without also altering downstream eligibility for escalation (`manual_service` determines escalation), so `manual_service` is both mediator and confounder for escalation. `U_underlying_risk` correlates with `risk_score`, `complexity`, `flag`, and service needs, leaving residual confounding even after adjusting for score. Timestamps and resource assignment are colliders if conditioned on.

## 3. Stage decomposition

Stages: `arrival->triage_start` = triage_wait; `triage_start->triage_done` = triage_service; `triage_done->manual_start` = manual_queue_wait (NA if no review); `manual_start->manual_done` = manual_service; `manual_done->escalation_start` = escalation_queue_wait; `escalation_start->escalation_done` = senior_service; residual `last_done->close` (1h if immediate close). Absent stages are coded NA and excluded from means for that interval; averaging zeros would underestimate conditional waiting and conflate occurrence with duration. Means shown (medians similar but n small).

*Handling of absent stages: `NA` means structural zero - not averaged as 0. Subgroup means for queue intervals are conditional on reaching that stage; `n reviewed / n escalated` reported to avoid mixing occurrence and duration.*

Explanation (132 words): The contrast flag=1 vs 0 is consistent with **acceleration at triage** (2.2h shorter `triage_wait`, 0.35h shorter `triage_service`) and **delay downstream**: conditional manual queue 4.6h longer for flagged, plus 60% of flagged vs 20% of unflagged are escalated, and flagged escalated carry 7h additional queue+2h service. Within flagged, even non-escalated median total (10.8h) exceeds unflagged no-manual (5h) and approaches flagged normal-load dynamics. However these are **not automatically causal**. Flag assignment covaries with `complexity` and `risk_score` (High complexity mean total 19.5h regardless of flag), `arrival_load` does not explain the gap (flagged Normal load still slower than unflagged High load), shared queues induce interference, and mandatory review positivity violation means `flag=1, manual=0` is almost unobserved. Observed differences conflate selection, routing policy, and congestion.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Is total effect on cycle time due to underlying risk vs flag policy? | Within-risk-score local threshold: compare cases just above vs below flag threshold (RDD) using `risk_score` centered at cutoff (0.6-0.67) | `risk_score` narrow window ±0.05, stratify by `complexity`, `arrival_load`, time-of-day | Continuity of `U_underlying_risk` at cutoff; no manipulation of score; flag is deterministic threshold | McCrary density test for sorting; placebo threshold; covariate balance (complexity, arrival_load) at cutoff | Sharp jump in `manual_review` but no jump in `cycle_time` or `escalation` would suggest flag bundle, not risk, drives delay; jump in `U_omitted risk` proxies would indicate confounding |
| Does manual queue waiting mediate delay beyond occurrence? | Queue-state-aware survival analysis: model `manual_queue_wait` as function of concurrent queue length (# pending manual cases) at `triage_done`, interact with flag | Adjust for `complexity`, `risk_score`, `arrival_load`, clock time; include queue length as time-varying covariate; decompose wait vs service | Queue length captures contention, not post-treatment collider; sequential ignorability given queue state | Test association of flag with wait after controlling for queue length; falsify by showing flagged wait = unflagged wait at same queue length | If flag still predicts longer wait conditional on same queue length, suggests reviewer assignment or priority within queue, not just volume |
| What is policy-relevant effect of removing mandatory review? | Randomized encouragement / pilot: randomize days or hours to P2 (priority triage retained, mandatory review off) vs P0, cluster randomization by shift | Intent-to-treat analysis stratified by `risk_score`, `complexity`; adjust for `arrival_load` as precision | Exclusion restriction: randomization affects cycle only via review occurrence/queue; no staff behavior spillover across clusters | First-stage: review rate difference; negative control: triage_wait should be equal (priority same); check contamination | If P2 reduces median `cycle_time` by >3h without increasing adverse outcome (missed reject), support that occurrence mediation is dominant |
| Can we estimate interventional indirect via review/escalation? | Sequential g-computation / interventional mediation with time-varying mediators: fit models for reviewqueueserviceescalation, then simulate stochastic interventions setting mediator distributions to P2/P4 | Baseline: `risk_score`, `complexity`, `arrival_load`, arrival time; time-varying: queue length, `triage_wait` | Sequential ignorability conditional on observed history; positivity (need reviewed & non-reviewed at each risk stratum); no interference (approx by conditioning on queue) | Sensitivity to ordering (swap queue vs service); cross-check with inverse-probability weighting of review; stability across bootstrap | Large change between parametric assumptions or IPW vs g-computation indicates model dependence; non-robust indirect estimate weakens claim |
| Is there residual confounding by hidden risk? | Negative-control outcome/exposure: use `arrival_load` high vs normal within flag=0 as exposure, and use triage_service (pre-review) as negative-control outcome for review effect | Adjust for `complexity`, `risk_score`; expect no flag effect before review | If confounding, it affects both mediator and outcome; negative control should be null if no bias | Test flag->triage_service vs flag->manual_queue: triage_service effect should be small after adjusting for priority; also test `complexity` as negative-control exposure for flag threshold | If flagged vs unflagged just below threshold show identical pre-review outcomes but divergent post-review waits, less confounding; if negative control shows effect, indicates hidden risk bias |
| How sensitive are conclusions to unobserved risk? | Sensitivity analysis for unobserved `U_underlying_risk`: Rosenbaum bounds / E-value / tipping-point simulation adding U that increases both flag odds (OR 2-5) and cycle time by 2-5h | Vary prevalence of U by complexity; include as latent class | Specification of U effect sizes; monotonicity | Report how strong U must be to explain away crude 8h gap; calibrate using observed `complexity` effect (High vs Low 15h) | If small U (OR 1.5, +2h) could erase effect, conclusion fragile; if requires extreme U (OR 5, +10h) implausible, effect more robust |
| Does interference bias individual-level estimates? | Interference-aware design: partial interference clusters (shifts/hours) with varying flag prevalence; estimate direct vs spillover via two-stage randomization or by instrumenting individual's wait with others' flag load at that hour | Adjust for own `flag`, `complexity`, cluster-level flag proportion, `arrival_load` | Partial interference within shift, no cross-shift carryover; consistency | Estimate spillover: unflagged manual_wait regressed on contemporaneous flagged count; test if flagged wait increases with own cohort size; placebo: queue wait vs future flagged arrivals (should be null) | Significant spillover coefficient (+1h per 2 extra flagged in queue) would mean total policy effect underestimated by individual comparisons and P3 capacity guardrail is essential |

Pilot recommendation (250 words): **Pilot P2 (priority triage retained, mandatory manual review removed) with embedded P4 escalation guardrail.**

Eligibility: arrivals with `risk_score` in [0.55,0.85] (where flag near threshold and positivity exists) and either complexity Medium/High; exclude extreme `risk_score>0.90` where review remains mandatory for safety.

Randomization: shift-block cluster randomization (e.g., 8-hour blocks 08:00-16:00, 16:00-00:00) 1:1 P0 vs P2 to limit within-shift interference; blocks stratified by expected `arrival_load` (High/Normal). Within P2, manual review by pre-specified criteria: `risk_score0.70` or complexity=High or clinician override with reason code.

Queue-capacity guardrail: real-time monitor pending manual queue >6 cases or wait >4h triggers overflow pool (cross-trained M reviewers) and pauses new mandatory triggers; senior escalation queue >4 cases triggers P4 rule (second reviewer confirmation required).

Outcomes: primary `cycle_time` median + p90 and `manual_queue_wait`; secondary `decision_outcome` appropriateness (blinded audit of 20% sample for missed Reject), escalation rate, triage_wait.

Data needed: per-stage `start_timestamp`+`end_timestamp`, `case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, `org:resource`, queue length snapshots hourly, override reasons.

Stopping: early stop if audited miss rate >5% higher in P2 or senior “Reject after not escalated” >10%; or if manual_wait not improved after 60 cases; else evaluate after 100 cases/40 reviewed.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk dominates:** If `risk_score` omits strong predictors of both flag and true service need (e.g., `U_underlying_risk` OR >3 for flag and +6h on manual/escalation need), then crude flagged-longer cycle is selection, P2 reduction disappears and quality deteriorates.
2. **Queue interference is the sole driver:** If manual/escalation waits are entirely driven by contemporaneous flagged volume and time-of-day staffing, adding capacity (P3) alone would have achieved all of P2's gain; removing mandatory review would then not improve cycle beyond capacity fix and would be unnecessary.
3. **Decision quality reverses speed benefit:** If audit shows `decision_outcome` errors rise sharply when manual review or escalation skipped (e.g., flagged Reject rate 20% vs <2% when skipped, or senior-detected fraud missed), the shorter cycle is a false economy and P0/P3 dominates despite longer time.
4. **Timestamp semantics are inverted:** If `manual_start` records *assignment* not *start of work* (or `triage_done->manual_start` includes triage documentation lag unrelated to queue), then the decomposed "queue wait" is actually service/documentation time; the mediation via queuing vanishes and the delay is diligence, not congestion.
5. **Positivity / lack of overlap failure:** If near-threshold comparison reveals no unflagged cases with High complexity and high risk_score that would be reviewed voluntarily (i.e., P(flag=1|riskthreshold)=1), then P2's intended review rate is unidentifiable; estimated savings extrapolate outside support and real P2 would still review ~95% of flagged.
6. **Behavior change by reviewers (risk compensation):** If suppressing mandatory review causes reviewers to compensate by escalating more aggressively or spending longer `manual_service` on remaining reviews (to avoid misses), then per-case service and escalation_queue_wait increase, offsetting occurrence savings and potentially lengthening `cycle_time` beyond P0.

## 9. Final synthesis

**What is directly observed:** 20 cases; flagged (n=10) average triage_wait 0.24h vs 2.48h unflagged and triage_service 0.60h vs 0.95h, yet total cycle 16.5h vs 8.2h. Flagged are 10/10 manual review vs 4/10 unflagged, 60% vs 20% escalation; conditional manual_queue_wait 6.5h vs 1.9h and escalation_queue_wait ~7h. High complexity cases average 19.5h irrespective of flag; flagged high-load and flagged with escalation are longest (17.6h-20.3h).

**Plausible mechanism:** Flag bundle accelerates triage via priority lane but induces downstream delay primarily through *occurrence mediation* (near-automatic manual review) that is amplified by *queue-wait mediation* in a shared, scarce senior queue, plus *escalation mediation* (6/10 flagged escalated). Complexity and imperfect risk_score confound flag assignment and service need, and interference means flagged volume spills over to raise waits for all queued cases.

**What remains unidentified:** Whether longer waits reflect appropriate diligence for `U_underlying_risk` vs policy-induced over-referral; the isolated causal effect of flag display vs routing (cross-world NDE/NIE non-identified, positivity violated, exposure-induced confounding); system interference magnitude; and decision quality tradeoffs without ground-truth labels.

**Most useful next evidence:** Interventional evidence from a cluster-randomized pilot of **P2** (keep priority triage, remove mandatory review, keep escalation criteria) with queue-capacity guardrails. P2 directly tests the occurrence mechanism while preserving triage gain and allowing estimation of interventional indirect effects on cycle_time and wait times, conditional on `risk_score`/`complexity` stratification and audit of decision quality, without assuming natural effects. If P2 shows no quality loss, it disentangles flag handling from downstream capacity; if quality suffers, P3/P4 become justified.
