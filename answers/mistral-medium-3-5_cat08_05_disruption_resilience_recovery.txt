# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance
- **True Resilience**: Adaptations that preserved critical functions *without compromising non-negotiable controls*.
  - **A (fast lane)**: Prioritized hospitals while using **B (manual authorization)** for high-risk products.
  - **B (manual authorization)**: Maintained compliance for controlled substances.
  - **F (temporary release control)**: Added human checks for high-risk orders.
  - **I (defer noncritical)**: Protected capacity for life-saving supplies.
- **Risky Shortcuts**: Adaptations that preserved throughput but *created compliance gaps*.
  - **C (skip authorization)**: Unacceptable for any product; no fallback controls.
  - **G (dispatch without scan)**: Critical traceability failure, especially for controlled/cold-chain.
  - **D (stale snapshots)**: Led to inventory errors and reconciliation burden.
- **Ambiguous**:
  - **E (manual pick lists)**: Resilient if paired with strict manual logging; risky if scans are missing.
  - **H (phone proof)**: Acceptable for ambient products but insufficient for controlled/cold-chain.
  - **J (backlog prioritization)**: Resilient if rules are explicit and auditable; risky if ad-hoc.

**Key Distinction**: Resilience = *preserving mission-critical outcomes with acceptable risk*. Concealed noncompliance = *short-term gains masking long-term audit/legal exposure* (e.g., missing controlled-substance dual-control).

# 4. Recovery process model
**Redesigned Process for Future Disruptions**
*Trigger Conditions*:
- WMS downtime > 2 hours **OR** inventory sync failure > 1 hour **OR** authorization system unavailable.

*Process Variants by Risk Tier*:
1. **Tier 1 (Hospital-critical + Controlled/Cold-chain)**:
   - **Triage**: Immediate fast-lane (A) but *mandatory* manual authorization (B) + temperature/security checks (F).
   - **Pick/Pack**: Manual pick lists (E) *with* dual-signature logging; temporary release control (F) for 100% of controlled substances.
   - **Dispatch**: Manual scan logs (retire G); courier handoff requires signed paperwork.
   - **Proof**: Photo + signed form (retire H for this tier).

2. **Tier 2 (Pharmacy-standard Ambient)**:
   - **Triage**: Defer non-urgent (I) if backlog > 4h.
   - **Authorization**: Pre-approved customer list (retire C); manual check (B) if not on list.
   - **Pick/Pack**: Manual pick lists (E) with timestamped logs.
   - **Dispatch**: Manual scan logs; phone proof (H) acceptable.

3. **Tier 3 (Wholesale-bulk)**:
   - **Triage**: Defer by default (I); process only if capacity allows.
   - **Controls**: Minimal (inventory snapshot D *only* if <24h old).

*Manual Evidence Capture*:
- **All tiers**: Timestamped logs for every manual step (picks, packs, checks).
- **Tier 1**: Additional temperature logs (cold-chain) and dual-control signatures (controlled substances).

*Backlog Prioritization (J)*:
- **Order**: Tier 1 > Tier 2 > Tier 3.
- **Within tiers**: FIFO *unless* product risk (e.g., recall-sensitive) overrides.

*Reconciliation & Audit-Remediation*:
1. **During Disruption**:
   - Dedicated team logs all manual deviations (e.g., skipped scans) in a central spreadsheet.
2. **Post-Recovery**:
   - **K (reconcile reservations)**: Automate where possible (e.g., compare manual logs to WMS).
   - **L (audit remediation)**: Prioritize Tier 1 gaps first; use temporary controls (F) to validate fixes.

# 7. Management trade-off statement

Management **must accept** that during a disruption, *some* throughput degradation is inevitable to preserve non-negotiable controls (e.g., controlled-substance dual-control, cold-chain temperature docs). The **Hospital-critical fast lane (A)** and **manual authorization (B)** should be retained as they balance resilience with compliance. However, management **must not accept** adaptations that create irreversible compliance gaps, such as **skipping authorization (C)** or **dispatching without scans (G)**, as these expose the company to regulatory penalties and loss of customer trust. The evidence that would change this recommendation is a demonstrated ability to *automate* fallback controls (e.g., real-time offline authorization checks) or prove that the risk of noncompliance is lower than the risk of supply chain failure (e.g., during a national health emergency). Until then, the priority should be on **pre-approved lists, manual scan logs, and extended temporary controls** to minimize hidden risks while maintaining operational continuity.
