Consequence: **you cannot use the discovered model as a routing design**; it would institutionalize policy-violating combinations. Any redesign must be specified against the normative net + segment attributes, not against the discovered graph.
- **Rework statistics (the contradiction made numeric).** `Request missing evidence` occurs 2× in **26%** of cases that enter it. `Customer complaint/reopen` is 9.2% overall but **14.1% for Fast approval vs 3.3% for `Repair quote  Customer approval  Repair device`**. And the economic pivot: Fast approval is **31% cheaper before rework but only 9% cheaper after** complaints, reshipments and service credits.
- **Temporal profile deviations.** `Manual eligibility review  Repair quote` breaches Premium service expectations; `Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen` (mean 96 h / p90 180 h) is explicitly attributed to **incorrect replacement part selection** — a decision-quality defect, not a queue defect.
- **Segment outcomes.** Fast path is fine for accessories (complaint 6–8%) and *demonstrably bad* for high-value devices (**22% Premium / 18% Standard** complaint vs 5–6% on the reviewed/repair path), while the reviewed path's 96–120 h threatens **service credits for Premium low-risk accessories if >24 h**. The "unclear defect / missing evidence" cohort (22% of cases) sits at **140 h median** with the highest abandonment.
- **Resource reality.** Manual eligibility review = 7 reviewers × 36 h = 252 productive h/week at EUR 34/h with **queue blow-up above 82% utilization**; `Manual eligibility review` is entered 2,600 times per 5,000 cases (1,400 initial + 890 evidence returns + 310 complaint returns = 0.52 touches/case). Fraud/risk (2 specialists, EUR 52/h) has Monday/campaign spikes. Repair quote delay is **waiting for part-price confirmation**, not technician time. Escalation board is one 45-minute daily slot.

**1.2 The contradiction, stated explicitly**

The most frequent path is attractive *as a mass-production flow*: ~10 h of edge time, EUR 0.20 auto-screen, no EUR 34–52/h human touches, and a 31% cost advantage before rework. But the evidence says this advantage is an **accounting artifact of closing the cost window at the moment of dispatch**. Once complaints (14.1% vs 3.3%), reshipments and credits are charged back, the advantage collapses to 9% — and the complaint edge `Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen` has a 96 h mean / 180 h p90 long tail attributed to wrong part selection, with expected error cost **EUR 410 per high-value device** (EUR 95 for accessories). Meanwhile the slower path buys real quality (3.3% complaints) and compliance (review before approval) but at 96–120 h and with a 30 h quote-approval wait that violates Premium expectations. **Both statements are true in different cost horizons and different segments** — so "go faster" and "go slower" are both wrong as global policies. The correct unit of decision is the **segment × defect-type × value cell**, and the correct metric is **rework-adjusted end-to-end cost and stable-close time**, not mean cycle time.

**1.3 Two artifact reconciliation caveats (data quality, not decoration)**

- Terminal edges account for only **3,625 of 5,000 cases** (`Close  END` 3,015 + `Denied  END` 220 + `Abandoned  END` 390): **~1,375 cases (~28%) have no listed terminal edge** — open/in-flight cases or truncation. Any cycle-time or complaint rate computed on closed cases is therefore exposed to **censoring/survivorship bias**, and slow, unresolved cases (the very cohort we care about) are the ones most likely missing.
- Complaint entries total 510 edges vs the reported 9.2%  460 cases  **~1.1 complaint occurrences per complaining case**: some customers reopen more than once, and only 430 complaint occurrences have a listed successor (` Manual eligibility review` 310, ` Escalation board` 120). Reopen loops are thus **under-modelled**, which is exactly where cost hides.

# 2. Redesign A: Risk-tiered guardrail routing (segment-aware triage with hard approval gates)

**Redesigned flow**
`Intake claim  Structured triage (segment + defect type + claim value + evidence-sufficiency score)`, then three lanes enforced by system logic rather than by reviewer habit:

- **Green lane** (low-risk accessories, value < EUR 400, evidence complete): `Auto eligibility screen  Fast approval  Dispatch replacement  Close`, unchanged in speed. Only change: a **non-blocking parts-number self-verification** (photo/part-code confirm) for Premium, and a **lightweight pre-dispatch gate** for Standard (which tolerates 72 h with visibility).
- **Amber lane** (mid-risk devices, or evidence not yet complete): `Auto screen  Fraud/risk review` **or** `Manual eligibility review  Technical decision (Repair quote)  Fulfillment  Close`. Fast approval is technically possible here but requires a review signature first.
- **Red lane** (high-value devices, any claim > EUR 400, Premium high-value regardless of value): **mandatory `Fraud/risk review` or `Manual eligibility review` *before* `Fast approval` (R1 hard-coded as a token precondition)**, **mandatory evidence-completeness confirmation before `Dispatch replacement` (R2, no override)**, and a **blocking pre-dispatch parts-verification gate**.

**Targets:** the `Fast approval  Dispatch replacement` mass (2,300 cases) split by segment; the high-value cells where complaint is 22%/18% on the fast path; the named alignment deviations (approval before evidence completeness; dispatch without risk review). Activities restructured: auto-screen rules, `Fast approval`, `Dispatch replacement` (+ one new gate activity).

**Mechanism of improvement:** the log already proves the fast path is *locally* good (accessories: 6–8% complaint, ~8–13 h) and *globally* destructive for high-value devices (22% complaint, R1/R2 violations). Guardrail routing **converts implicit normative obligations (R1, R2) into routing preconditions**, so conformance becomes a property of the design rather than a reviewer's discipline, while preserving the speed of the path that the data exonerates. The pre-dispatch gate is justified by a bounded expected-value argument: a wrong replacement costs **EUR 410** for a high-value device vs **EUR 95** for an accessory, against 6–18 h of added latency and ~EUR 38/h of inspection — clearly favourable for high-value, clearly not for Premium accessories where exceeding 24 h triggers service credits. Hence the gate is applied **selectively**, which is the non-generic part.

**Deliberately sacrifices / leaves unresolved:** it does **not** touch the `Request missing evidence  Manual eligibility review` loop (26% repeat, 34 h per iteration) nor the 140 h unclear-defect cohort; it **accepts +50–100 h on the red lane** and accepts that updated auto-screen rules may push **15–35% more cases into manual review** (mitigated by segment-awareness but not eliminated); it accepts 6–18 h of added latency for high-value dispatch and a new dependency on 2 inspectors and 2 fraud specialists.

# 3. Redesign B: Evidence-complete start (one evidence window, assisted completion, decision clock)

**Redesigned flow**
`Intake claim  Structured evidence-sufficiency assessment (auto pre-validation of uploads + defect-type checklist)`  **one consolidated evidence window** (single request listing everything needed, upload pre-validated on submission, with an assisted channel for low-digital-literacy customers)  then either `Auto eligibility screen  Fast approval  Fulfillment  Close`, or `Manual/Fraud review  Technical decision`, **and** a hard **decision clock**: if evidence is still incomplete at day 5, the case moves to `Decision without full evidence` (goodwill/partial settlement, or `Claim denied` with a stated rationale and one appeal) instead of looping back to `Manual eligibility review`. `Claim abandoned` (390 terminal cases) is treated as a **defect to be rescued** — an outbound contact attempt before abandonment is recorded.

**Targets:** the **unclear defect / missing evidence cohort (22% of cases, 140 h median, highest abandonment)**; the `Request missing evidence` activity (1,430 in-edges) and specifically the loop `Request missing evidence  Manual eligibility review` (890 edges, mean 34 h) versus `Manual eligibility review  Request missing evidence` (780 edges, mean 14 h) — a self-feeding loop worth ~26% repeat incidence.

**Mechanism of improvement:** this redesign does not accelerate the loop, it **deletes it**. Two structural facts support it: (i) the loop's asymmetry (14 h out, 34 h back) means each repeat costs ~48 h of edge time plus a fresh reviewer touch at EUR 34/h — 890 such returns are the single largest avoidable cost/latency pool in the log; (ii) the 30–45% repeat-reduction range of the evidence portal maps directly onto the 26% repeat statistic. The decision clock converts an unbounded open loop into a **bounded decision**, trading a small rise in denials/goodwill settlements for a large fall in 140 h cases and abandonment. The assisted channel is the counterweight to the portal's known abandonment risk for low digital literacy — that is why it is in the design, not in a footnote.

**Deliberately sacrifices / leaves unresolved:** intake becomes slightly slower and more structured even for easy cases; a stricter evidence standard may **deny or abandon claims that today are approved loosely** (a real CX and regulatory-appeal exposure); the EUR 410 wrong-part-selection problem is untouched; R1/R2 compliance for high-value devices is only partially improved (evidence confirmation helps R2, says nothing about R1); it requires portal build/maintenance and an assisted-support resource that is not in the current resource list.

# 4. Redesign C: Right-first-time fulfillment (repair-first decisioning, parts verification, planned recovery lane)

**Redesigned flow**
After `Technical decision`, a **fulfilment decision engine** replaces the reflex `Fast approval  Dispatch replacement`:

- **Low-risk accessories**: replacement stays (fast, cheap, complaint 6–8% acceptable) with non-blocking parts self-verification.
- **Mid- and high-value devices with viable repair**: **repair-first**, enabled by a **standing parts price catalogue / auto price lookup** that removes the "waiting for part-price confirmation" delay behind `Repair quote  Customer approval` (30 h) and `Manual eligibility review  Repair quote` (22 h), plus **pre-approved repair bands** so `Customer approval` becomes a one-click confirm rather than a 30 h negotiation. Repair is chosen over replacement because the log says repair delivers **3.3% complaints vs 14.1%**, and replacement of a high-value device carries a **EUR 410** expected error cost; the accepted risk is the 12.5% `Rework repair` rate, which is a *repair-internal* loop (45/42 edges, 14 h each) and far cheaper than a wrong replacement + 96 h complaint tail.
- **Blocking pre-dispatch parts-verification gate** for high-value devices only.
- **Planned recovery lane** for `Customer complaint/reopen` (9.2% of cases, ~510 occurrences over ~460 cases): instead of the 40 h edge `Customer complaint/reopen  Manual eligibility review`, the reopen is scheduled into a **same-day reviewer confirm (which still satisfies R3 — manual eligibility review before final close or denial)** combined with the escalation board's goodwill authority delivered as an **asynchronous panel** rather than one 45-minute daily slot. Standard segments get **proactive status visibility** (they explicitly tolerate 72 h if visibility is good), which suppresses complaint generation rather than servicing it.

**Targets:** `Fast approval  Dispatch replacement` for devices; `Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen` (96 h mean / 180 h p90, wrong-part long tail); `Customer complaint/reopen  Manual eligibility review` (40 h) and ` Escalation board` (54 h); the `Repair quote` waiting time.

**Mechanism of improvement:** it attacks the **decision-quality defects** and the **recovery path**, where the cost asymmetry is largest (31%  9% is created almost entirely after dispatch). Front-loading a parts-verification gate costs 6–18 h but removes a EUR 410-class error and the 96 h complaint tail; repair-first converts an expensive, error-prone decision into a slower but stable one (5% vs 22% complaint); the recovery lane stops paying **40 h + 54 h** of latency to a customer who has already had a failure, and it stops the repeat-reopen behaviour implied by 510 occurrences / 460 cases.

**Deliberately sacrifices / leaves unresolved:** front-path cycle time rises for devices (the reviewed/repair path is 96–120 h today) — this is *accepted*, because the data shows speed on this cell buys complaints, not satisfaction; repair volume rises against **3 technicians** and the 12.5% rework-repair rate becomes more visible; a fast recovery lane creates a **moral-hazard/gaming risk** (complaining becomes the fastest route to service); the intake/evidence loop and the stale auto-screen rules are untouched; the price catalogue requires a commercial change outside the process boundary.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A — Risk-tiered guardrail routing**
1. **Inflow shock.** Updated auto-screen rules are explicitly reported as capable of raising manual-review inflow by 15–35%; at 0.52 touches/case today and blow-up above 82% utilization, this can push `Intake  Manual eligibility review` (mean 7.5 h) and `Manual eligibility review  Repair quote` (22 h) into multi-day queues — *including for Premium accessories*, triggering the >24 h service-credit exposure. The redesign then destroys the one thing it promised to keep.
2. **Mis-tiering.** If defect-type/value attributes are noisy at intake, a high-value device can be tiered green (R1/R2 violations continue, now with a false "compliant" label) or an accessory tiered red (pure latency, zero benefit). The gate then provides *compliance theatre*.
3. **Fraud/risk starvation on spikes.** Monday and post-campaign loads starve the amber/red lanes; the log already shows `Intake  Fraud/risk` at 11–18 h means.
- **Bottleneck migration:** from `Dispatch replacement` and the complaint tail  **`Manual eligibility review` / `Fraud/risk review` queues and the 2 pre-dispatch inspectors**, with complaint/reopen volume *partially* converting into review waiting time rather than disappearing.

**Redesign B — Evidence-complete start**
1. **Abandonment regression.** `Request missing evidence  Claim abandoned` already yields 320 cases; a portal-first design with an evidence gate can raise abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers, converting visible dissatisfaction (complaints) into invisible loss (abandoned claims) and worsening the "Unclear defect" cohort's optics.
2. **Decision-clock backlash.** Resolving at day 5 without full evidence produces more denials/goodwill settlements and more `Escalation board` referrals — and the escalation board is a **single 45-minute daily slot** that cannot absorb the surge.
3. **Over-strict evidence validation** rejects claims that today clear `Fast approval`, inflating `Manual eligibility review  Request missing evidence` instead of removing it.
- **Bottleneck migration:** from the 34 h evidence-return loop  **assisted-channel / portal-support staff and the escalation board**, with residual load moving into appeals rather than into the log.

**Redesign C — Right-first-time fulfilment + planned recovery**
1. **Repair capacity collapse.** Repair-first pushes volume onto 3 technicians while the known delay (`Repair quote` waiting for part-price confirmation, 30 h to customer approval) is only fixed if the commercial price catalogue lands; otherwise `Repair quote  Customer approval` gets *worse*, and 12.5% `Rework repair` on high-value devices becomes a visible repeat-repair annoyance.
2. **Complaint-channel gaming.** A same-day recovery lane makes `Customer complaint/reopen` the fastest route to a replacement/credit, raising the 9.2% complaint rate and shifting cost into service credits; combined with the observed repeat reopens (510 occurrences / 460 cases), a second-order loop can appear: fail  complain  recover  fail again  complain again.
3. **Gate latency erodes the SLA.** At the upper end (18 h) the pre-dispatch gate pushes Premium high-value cases past their window and increases credit exposure faster than it removes EUR 410 errors.
- **Bottleneck migration:** from dispatch accuracy  **repair technicians and inspectors**, and from the 40 h reopen loop  **reviewer capacity for same-day R3 confirms**, which is the same pool Redesign A is already straining.

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**Stage 0 — Instrument the truth before changing it (2 weeks, no process change)**
Because the artifacts conflict and ~28% of cases have no terminal edge, first fix measurement:
- **Cohort frame by segment × defect type × value** (never averages alone), with a *censoring flag* for cases still open.
- **Primary metrics:** *time to **stable** close* (close never reopened within 30 days), *rework-adjusted cost per case* (labour at EUR 0.20/34/52/45/38 + expected error cost EUR 95/410 + reship + service credits), *complaint occurrences per case within 30 days of close* (not just complaint rate), *abandonment rate*, *manual-review touches per case*, *reviewer utilization* (flag >82%) and fraud-specialist queue depth.
- **Compliance metrics from alignments:** R1/R2/R3 violation rate per trace, and model precision (to detect new spaghetti paths).
- **Counter-metrics to catch shifting:** denial rate, goodwill/credit spend, escalation-board referrals, `Rework repair` rate, reopen count per case.
- **Go criterion to proceed:** the 31%9% cost gap and the 14.1% vs 3.3% complaint gap reproduce in the instrumented baseline. *If not, the redesign priorities change.*

**Stage 1 — First pilot: Redesign A's guardrails, narrow slice = `Standard low-risk accessories` (25% of cases), 4 weeks**
Sequencing logic: this is the **most tolerant segment** (explicitly accepts 72 h with visibility), the cheapest place to learn the 15–35% inflow question, and the rules update is deployable in two weeks. Run the new auto-screen rules **in shadow mode for one week** (compare routing decisions against current without acting), then enable live. In the same pilot, enable the **non-blocking parts self-verification for Premium low-risk accessories** (zero added wait — protects the 24 h credit window) but *not* the blocking gate.
- **Monitor:** manual-review inflow delta vs the 15–35% band, reviewer utilization (<82%), complaint rate (from 8% fast path toward the 4% manual-path reference), R2 violations (target zero), abandonment, median cycle time (<24 h Premium / <72 h Standard).
- **Stop/go:** *Stop or roll back* if review inflow rises >20% for two consecutive weeks or utilization 82%, if abandonment rises >2–3 pp, or if complaints do not fall at all. *Go* if complaints fall 2 pp with inflow delta 15% and no R2 violations.

**Stage 2 — Redesign B on the `Unclear defect / missing evidence` cohort (22%), 6 weeks, randomized split**
Sequencing logic: the evidence loop (26% repeats, 34 h return edge, 140 h cohort) is the largest *structural* rework pool and is independent of the routing question — and running it second lets Stage 1's measurement settle. Randomize within cohort: **(i) portal + single evidence window**, **(ii) the same + assisted completion**, **(iii) status quo**. This is the only way to detect the known digital-literacy abandonment effect rather than assume it away. Add the day-5 decision clock only in arm (ii) for the first 3 weeks.
- **Monitor:** repeat evidence requests (26%  <18%), cohort median time to *stable* decision (140 h  70 h), abandonment (must not rise >2 pp in any arm), denial/goodwill rate, complaint occurrences per case, portal abandonment split by digital-literacy proxy (assisted vs self-serve).
- **Stop/go:** *Stop* the portal-only arm if abandonment exceeds the assisted arm by >5 pp or overall abandonment rises >3 pp. *Go* to scaling if repeats fall 8 pp **and** stable-close time falls 30% **and** complaint occurrences per case do not rise.

**Stage 3 — Redesign C, split pilot on high-value devices (10 weeks)**
Sequencing logic: highest expected value per case (EUR 410 error cost, 22%/18% complaint rates) but the longest implementation lead (price catalogue, gate resourcing), so it starts only after Stages 1–2 have freed reviewer capacity. Run **50% holdout**: blocking pre-dispatch parts-verification gate + repair-first decisioning in treatment; then, second half of the window, open the **planned recovery lane** with same-day R3 reviewer confirm.
- **Monitor:** wrong-replacement incidents (EUR 410-class), complaint/reopen for high-value fast path (18–22%  10%), `Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen` tail (96/180 h), `Rework repair` (12.5% baseline — acceptable if complaints fall), repair-quote lead time (30 h edge), technician and inspector utilization, R1/R2/R3 violation rate, reopen occurrences per complaining case (repeat-reopen check).
- **Stop/go:** *Stop the gate* if added latency exceeds ~18 h and credit exposure rises before any error reduction is visible. *Stop or cap the recovery lane* if overall complaint rate rises (>11–12%) — that signals gaming. *Go* if rework-adjusted cost per high-value case falls and stable-close time is flat or better.

**Stage 4 — Full Redesign A for high-value/amber/red lanes, then portfolio choice (weeks 14–20)**
Only now deploy the R1/R2 hard gates for high-value and >EUR 400 claims, using Stages 1–3 to know whether the freed reviewer capacity can absorb the 15–35% inflow band. Conclude with a **segment-by-segment policy table** (fast lane stays for accessories, guardrails for devices, evidence clock for unclear defect) rather than a single global flow.

**How to know improvements are real rather than displaced downstream**
1. **Close the cost window properly.** Report cost per case with a **60-day post-close tail** (credits, reship, `Rework repair`, repeat reopens). If the 31%9% advantage keeps collapsing in the new designs, the saving was shifted, not created.
2. **Use stable close, not first close.** A case that closes in 10 h and reopens at 96 h (`Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen`) must count as *worse* than a 100 h case that never reopens (`Repair quote  …  Quality check  Close`). Stable-close time and reopen-per-case are the anti-Goodhart pair.
3. **Re-run process mining after each stage**: re-derive the frequency and performance DFG and re-align to the same normative Petri net. Check specifically for **displaced deviations** — e.g. "Fast approval before evidence completeness" morphing into "Dispatch before gate", or complaints moving *after* close (where the 80 unmatched complaint occurrences suggest they partly hide today). Rising non-conformance with falling cycle time = displacement.
4. **Watch the constraint migrate, by name.** Track reviewer utilization (>82%), fraud-specialist Monday backlog, inspector occupancy, technician queue at `Repair quote`, and escalation-board referrals. A redesign that relieves `Dispatch replacement` but pushes utilization past 82% has only moved the queue.
5. **Compare cohorts, not averages, and never read the fast path's mean alone.** Every claim of improvement must be stated as: *for which segment, which defect type, which value band, and at what rework-adjusted cost* — and every stage carries an explicit "who got worse" statement. Where the evidence cannot settle a trade-off (e.g. the censored 28% of unclosed cases), that uncertainty is carried into the decision rather than resolved by assumption.
