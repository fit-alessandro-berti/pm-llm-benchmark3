## 1. Temporal Data Semantics

Each release is assessed against its own event-time state. For each case, sort by `(time:timestamp, event_index)` ascending and carry forward the last nonblank value of each changing attribute. A blank means no update; an attribute remains UNKNOWN until evidence supplies a value. Apply explicit updates on an event when constructing that event's snapshot. Reviews are assumed to denote completed reviews, and the supplied log is treated as complete for the listed control activities unless its evidence identifies a gap.

Write `e ≺ f` when event `e` occurs before event `f`: its timestamp is earlier, or the timestamps are equal and its `event_index` is smaller. Write `e ≼ f` for before or equal. All required prior activities must strictly precede release under this ordering. Source order therefore matters even when wall-clock timestamps match. In TX-311, the Enhanced_Review sees US; the subsequent jurisdiction update sees DE; release sees DE.

Final attributes are not historical snapshots. In particular, TX-305's EUR 150,000 becomes known at settlement on April 7, not at release on April 6. “May equal release amount” establishes neither identity nor an earlier effective time. Similarly, DE at the end of TX-311 cannot be copied onto its earlier review.

I use the following materiality interpretation:

- For amount-dependent review validity, compare amounts with the amount covered by the relevant review. An increase strictly above 20% invalidates that review. Exactly 20%, a smaller increase, and a decrease alone do not. Successive small increases can cumulatively breach the reviewed baseline; a later decrease does not automatically reinstate an already invalidated review.
- The latest Standard_Review or Enhanced_Review supplies the baseline for the immediate R4 check. When relying on a particular Enhanced_Review for R1, also check that review's own baseline; a later Standard_Review cannot substitute for enhanced review or repair its invalidation.
- Changes to jurisdiction, risk level, or customer type are conservatively treated as material because they can change the review's policy or scope. The policy does not exhaustively define non-amount materiality; this interpretation should be versioned. Creation establishes the initial baseline. An exemption reference, database insertion, or annotation is not automatically a material transaction-data change.
- An unknown reviewed amount, or a change from an unknown amount, does not establish a greater-than-20% increase. It can prevent us from establishing amount coverage or freshness. That is uncertainty, not a proven R4 violation.

A sanctions-list addition is a change to shared reference data. The `jurisdiction` on that event identifies the addition's target; it should not be indiscriminately treated as a transaction-jurisdiction update. This distinction makes no numerical difference for TX-304, whose jurisdiction is already XZ.

Four times must be kept separate:

| Time | Meaning and consequence |
|---|---|
| Event time | When the business event occurred, such as release or completion of a review. This controls trace ordering. |
| Effective time | When a fact or authorization applies. An attribute update takes effect at its event timestamp unless credible evidence establishes otherwise. An exemption's `effective_from` is a separate applicability condition. |
| Record time | When a system persisted or received the record. An 11:00 insertion does not by itself prove an 11:00 approval or invalidate an earlier approval. |
| Evidence time | When supporting evidence was created, signed, or independently timestamped, and separately when the assessor obtained or verified it. A displayed approval time is a claim; a verified signature timestamp is stronger evidence. Certificate creation time is not a signature timestamp. |

Later evidence may establish an earlier fact without changing the business event's actual time. EX-77's verified 09:05 signature supports approval before the 09:40 release despite its 11:00 database insertion. EX-88's displayed 08:50 approval conflicts with a certificate created at 10:30; that prevents accepting the displayed field as conclusive digital proof, but does not alone disprove the claimed 08:45 oral approval. The latter needs separate assessment. The time when this audit received the evidence is not supplied and should not be invented.

The expiry convention above is a half-open validity interval, `[effective_from, expires_at)`. None of these releases occurs exactly at expiry. No listed exemption waives R2, R3, R4's invalidation semantics, or evidence requirements.

For concise formal logic, let `S_x(c,e)` be the last supported update to attribute `x` effective at or before `e`, or `U` if none exists. Let `Rel(f)`, `ER(r)`, `SR(r)`, `CA(p)`, `SS(s)` and `Add(j,u)` denote release, completed enhanced review, completed standard review, compliance approval, completed screening and jurisdiction-list addition. Control events in a formula belong to the release's case; list additions are shared reference events. `latest` always means the maximum under `≺`, never the last row in unsorted input. Conditions use truth values `T`, `F`, `U`.

**R1.** Define `G1(f) = (S_jurisdiction(f) ≠ DE) ∧ (S_amount(f) ≥ 100000)`. Let `M(f)` be the latest definite material data change through release, with an initial baseline if there was no later change. Define `FreshER(r,f)` as `ER(r) ∧ r ≺ f ∧ M(f) ≺ r ∧ AmountValid(r,f)`, with `U` when unresolved intervening materiality could defeat freshness. Then `G1(f) → [∃r FreshER(r,f) ∨ ∃x ValidExemption(x,R1,f)]`.

**R2.** Define `G2(f) = (S_risk(f) = HIGH) ∨ (S_customer_type(f) = PEP)`. Then `G2(f) → ∃r,p [ER(r) ∧ CA(p) ∧ r ≺ p ≺ f ∧ Relevant(r,f) ∧ Approves(p,r)]`. A Standard_Review is not an ER. Approval of only a standard packet does not satisfy `Approves(p,r)` for an enhanced review. “Relevant” means the packet addresses the applicable risk/customer state. A later risk/type change without packet evidence makes relevance uncertain; the text does not create a blanket requirement to repeat R2 approval for every amount update.

**R3.** For each `Add(S_jurisdiction(f),u)` with `u ≺ f`, let `s0` be the latest matching screen strictly before `u`, if one exists. If it exists, require `∃s1 [SS(s1) ∧ u ≺ s1 ≺ f]`. Equivalently, an addition after the most recent pre-release screen leaves an outstanding rescreening obligation. Multiple additions must all be covered; a screen after the latest addition covers the earlier ones if its scope/list version is appropriate. With no screening baseline, this rule alone does not establish a general initial-screen obligation.

**R4.** For a candidate review `r` with known positive reviewed amount `B = S_amount(r)`, `InvalidAmount(r,f)` is true when some explicit amount update `u` satisfies `r ≺ u ≼ f` and `S_amount(u) > 1.20 B`. Thus `AmountValid(r,f) = ¬InvalidAmount(r,f)` when the necessary amount history is known; otherwise it is `U`. Invalidation persists for that review. A new review creates a new baseline. Apply the immediate invalidation check to the latest SR/ER and check any ER actually relied on under R1. R4 invalidation alone is not a separate release violation when no active amount-dependent obligation relies on that review.

**R5.** `ValidExemption(x,k,f) = HeadComplianceAuthority(x) ∧ Waives(x,k) ∧ EffectiveFrom(x) ≤ time(f) < Expiry(x) ∧ ScopeMatches(x,S(f)) ∧ ProvenApprovalExisted(x,f)`. `ProvenApprovalExisted` means credible evidence establishes authorization no later than the trigger, with any decisive equal-time order resolved. `RecordTime(x) > time(f)` does not imply invalidity. `DisplayedApprovalTime(x) < time(f)` does not imply timely approval. An unknown conjunct makes the result `U` unless another conjunct is already false.

**R6.** Strong three-valued logic applies: `F ∧ U = F`, `T ∧ U = U`, `T ∨ U = T`, and `F ∨ U = U`. An inactive guard requires no control. A true guard with false fulfillment is a deviation; a decisive unknown guard or fulfillment is unresolved. Fulfillment proven for either possible guard value can resolve an unknown trigger. At case level, any independently proven violation yields NONCOMPLIANT while other uncertainties remain reported. Otherwise, an unresolved decisive obligation yields INDETERMINATE; an indispensable proven waiver yields JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION; otherwise all applicable obligations satisfied yields COMPLIANT.

**R7.** `S_x(c,e) = value(arg max≼ {u: u belongs to c, explicitly updates x, and is supported as effective by e})`; the empty set yields `U`. In this log, ordinary attribute effective order equals `(timestamp,event_index)`. A justified retrospective effective-time correction must retain its record/evidence provenance rather than overwrite history without an audit trail.

## 4. Ambiguities and Alternative Readings

- **TX-302 — ordinary compliance versus exception labeling.** COMPLIANT records that its 09:20 ER independently meets R1. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION is also a defensible reporting convention if every valid invoked waiver receives that label, even where redundant. That changes labeling, not whether the release met policy. If the ER were shown incomplete or outside scope, the proven EX-77 would become necessary and JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION would be appropriate. If the signature were invalidated but the ER remained valid, the overall case would still be COMPLIANT. Requiring exemption insertion before release would reject EX-77, but would add a condition absent from R5 and still would not defeat the independent ER.
- **TX-305 — unresolved threshold and review coverage.** A trustworthy historical release payload showing amount <EUR 100,000 would exclude R1 and support COMPLIANT under the supplied rules. A release amount ≥EUR 100,000 together with reliable review-snapshot and change-history evidence establishing a fresh ER would also support COMPLIANT. Evidence of a >20% post-review increase with no later ER would establish NONCOMPLIANT. Proving only that settlement equaled release at EUR 150,000 would establish the trigger, but would not necessarily establish the review's amount coverage. A more permissive interpretation could treat the ER event itself as sufficient for every possible amount if complete immutable change history establishes no intervening material change; I do not infer that coverage from a document explicitly lacking an amount. R4 uncertainty is not evidence that an increase actually happened.
- **TX-310 — evidential standard for approval.** Independent contemporaneous Head_Compliance authorization, an authenticated approval service record, or corroborated oral-approval evidence meeting the organization's authorization standard could establish approval before 10:00 and lead to JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. Proof that authorization first occurred after release, or a binding requirement for a qualified signature that could not have existed before this certificate's 10:30 creation, would lead to NONCOMPLIANT. A strict “waiver must be proved to be accepted” assessment can already report NONCOMPLIANT on the current record; my retrospective factual assessment retains INDETERMINATE because earlier oral approval is contested rather than disproved. Operationally, such a waiver should remain unaccepted pending resolution. The requester witness's interest reduces evidential weight but is not proof of false testimony.
- **TX-311 — release guard and equal-time ordering.** The supplied order establishes US at ER and DE at release, so no new ER is required by R1. A policy that permanently retains an obligation once the transaction ever becomes foreign/high-value could demand a review after the later material change, but that is a different, persistent-trigger rule. Credible evidence that the DE update was actually effective after release would restore the US release state; the existing ER could still satisfy R1 because it followed the earlier amount increase. To establish a violation, one would additionally need an invalidating change after that ER or evidence that the ER did not complete. If event_index were unavailable, the review's jurisdiction could be uncertain; both equal-time events still precede the logged release, so that ambiguity alone would not make release jurisdiction unknown.
- **TX-306 — meaning of materiality and approval scope.** Under R4, 4.55% does not invalidate amount coverage. A separately specified approval cap of EUR 220,000, a rule making every amount change material, or evidence of a larger intermediate increase would change the analysis and could require a new ER/approval. No such cap or extra rule is supplied. R2's risk/type basis remains unchanged.
- **TX-308 — decreases and the threshold boundary.** The decrease does not invalidate the ER; the remaining EUR 100,000 is still within R1 because the threshold is inclusive. A prior unlogged increase above EUR 168,000 after the EUR 140,000 ER would invalidate that review under the persistent-invalidation interpretation even if the amount later fell to EUR 100,000. A policy allowing explicit reinstatement after decreases would need that reinstatement's evidence and rule version. Neither situation appears here.

These alternatives distinguish uncertainty about facts from changes to the policy interpretation. A broader “absence from the log does not imply absence in reality” standard would also weaken proven omission findings; missing control-event capture would have to be identified and investigated, rather than selectively assumed only for favorable outcomes.

    raw["update_" + col] = raw[col]  # retain provenance before forward fill

def known(v):
    return not pd.isna(v)

def key(e):
    return (e[T], int(e[I]))

def tri_and(*values):
    if any(v is False for v in values):
        return False
    return None if any(v is None for v in values) else True

def tri_or(*values):
    if any(v is True for v in values):
        return True
    return None if any(v is None for v in values) else False

def eq(v, target):
    return bool(v == target) if known(v) else None

def different(a, b):
    return known(a) != known(b) or (known(a) and known(b) and bool(a != b))

def after(event, marker):
    return marker is None or key(marker) < key(event)

def amount_valid(review, history):
    """Review-relative baseline; any threshold breach persists until a new review."""
    if review is None:
        return False  # no review, rather than an amount classification
    baseline = review["amount"]
    if not known(baseline):
        return None  # amount coverage cannot be established
    for event in history:
        if key(review) < key(event) and known(event["update_amount"]):
            # Once a known reviewed amount is exceeded by >20%, a later decrease
            # does not resurrect that review. Values are exact integers here.
            if float(event["amount"]) * 5 > float(baseline) * 6:
                return False
    return True

def obligation_status(trigger, satisfied):
    if trigger is False:
        return "NOT_TRIGGERED"
    if satisfied is True:
        return "SATISFIED" if trigger is True else "SATISFIED_IF_TRIGGERED"
    if trigger is True and satisfied is False:
        return "VIOLATION"
    return "REVIEW_REQUIRED"

def stamp(out, prefix, event):
    out[prefix + "_ts"] = event[T] if event is not None else pd.NaT
    out[prefix + "_index"] = int(event[I]) if event is not None else pd.NA

TRACK = ["Standard_Review", "Enhanced_Review", "Compliance_Approve",
         "Sanctions_Screen", "Sanctions_List_Update"]
rows = []
all_list_updates = list_updates.sort_values([T, I], kind="stable").to_dict("records")
for case, group in df.groupby(C, sort=False):
    latest = {activity: None for activity in TRACK}
    latest_review = None
    material = uncertain_material = risk_type_change = None
    history, reviews, approvals = [], [], []
    previous = None
    for event in group.to_dict("records"):
        # Initial data establish the baseline. Later risk/type/jurisdiction
        # changes are conservatively material; small amount changes are not.
        if previous is None:
            material = event
        else:
            for col in ["risk_level", "jurisdiction", "customer_type"]:
                if known(event["update_" + col]) and different(previous[col], event[col]):
                    material = event
                    if col in ["risk_level", "customer_type"]:
                        risk_type_change = event
            if known(event["update_amount"]) and different(previous["amount"], event["amount"]):
                baseline = (latest_review["amount"] if latest_review is not None
                            else previous["amount"])
                if not known(baseline) or not known(previous["amount"]):
                    uncertain_material = event
                elif (float(event["amount"]) > float(previous["amount"]) and
                      float(event["amount"]) * 5 > float(baseline) * 6):
                    material = event
        history.append(event)
        activity = event[A]
        if activity in TRACK:
            latest[activity] = event
        if activity in ["Standard_Review", "Enhanced_Review"]:
            latest_review = event
        if activity == "Enhanced_Review":
            reviews.append(event)
        if activity == "Compliance_Approve":
            approvals.append(event)
        previous = event
        if activity != "Release_Funds":
            continue

        amount, jur = event["amount"], event["jurisdiction"]
        r1_trigger = tri_and(None if not known(jur) else bool(jur != "DE"),
                             None if not known(amount) else bool(amount >= 100000))
        r2_trigger = tri_or(eq(event["risk_level"], "HIGH"), eq(event["customer_type"], "PEP"))
        r1_candidates = []
        r2_candidates = []
        for review in reviews:
            if not key(review) < key(event):
                continue
            r1_candidates.append(tri_and(
                after(review, material), amount_valid(review, history),
                None if uncertain_material is not None and not after(review, uncertain_material)
                else True))
            paired = any(key(review) < key(approval) < key(event)
                         and "standard review packet" not in str(approval["note"]).lower()
                         for approval in approvals)
            # A changed risk/type after review needs packet evidence for relevance;
            # timestamps alone cannot prove that this review covered the new state.
            relevant = True if after(review, risk_type_change) else None
            r2_candidates.append(tri_and(paired, relevant))
        r1_ordinary = tri_or(*r1_candidates)  # False for an empty candidate set
        r2_satisfied = tri_or(*r2_candidates)

        # Sanctions additions are global reference-data events. In this fixture,
        # every Sanctions_List_Update denotes an addition; production needs an action field.
        prior_adds = [u for u in all_list_updates if key(u) < key(event)]
        screen = latest["Sanctions_Screen"]
        if known(jur):
            relevant_adds = [u for u in prior_adds if u["target_jurisdiction"] == jur]
            last_add = relevant_adds[-1] if relevant_adds else None
            if last_add is None:
                r3_status = "NOT_TRIGGERED"
            elif screen is None:
                r3_status = "REVIEW_REQUIRED"  # no baseline-screen rule was supplied
            elif key(last_add) < key(screen) < key(event):
                r3_status = "SATISFIED"
            else:
                r3_status = "VIOLATION"
        else:
            last_add = None
            r3_status = "REVIEW_REQUIRED" if prior_adds else "NOT_TRIGGERED"

        av = amount_valid(latest_review, history) if latest_review is not None else True
        r4_invalidated = None if av is None else not av
        out = {C: case, "release_ts": event[T], "release_index": event[I],
               **{col: event[col] for col in ATTRS},

               "r2_trigger": r2_trigger, "r2_satisfied": r2_satisfied,

        for activity_name, last in latest.items():

        stamp(out, "last_relevant_list_add", last_add)
        stamp(out, "last_material_change", material)
        stamp(out, "last_possible_material_change", uncertain_material)

        for col in ATTRS[:-1]:
            out["unknown_" + col] = not known(event[col])
            updates = [e for e in history if known(e["update_" + col])]

        out["unknown_any_core_attribute"] = any(out["unknown_" + col] for col in ATTRS[:-1])

def valid_r1_exemption(row):
    if not known(row["exemption_id"]):

    if not known(row["approved_by"]):
        return None
    scope = tri_and(eq(row["jurisdiction"], row["scope_jurisdiction"]),

                   bool(row["effective_from"] <= row["release_ts"] < row["expires_at"]),

    return None if pd.isna(v) else bool(v)

## 6. Why Control-Flow-Only Conformance Is Insufficient

1. **Final-state leakage changes historical applicability.** Assigning the final EUR 150,000 to all TX-305 events invents knowledge at review and release. Assigning final DE to TX-311's ER changes the state that event actually reviewed. Trace-level attributes cannot recover these snapshots.
2. **Conditional activation cannot be represented by one unconditional precedence rule.** R1 depends jointly on jurisdiction and an inclusive amount threshold; R2 depends on HIGH risk or PEP status. Requiring ER before every release produces false positives for cases with inactive guards, while making ER optional misses active obligations. TX-307 requires enhanced review despite DE and EUR 70,000.
3. **A review can become stale after it occurs.** TX-309 contains ER before release and would pass a simple precedence check, but a later 22.88% increase invalidates it. Detecting this requires the reviewed amount and intervening updates. Conversely, rejecting all updates after ER would wrongly reject TX-306 and TX-308.
4. **Late recording is different from late authorization.** A sequence containing Record_Exemption after Release_Funds cannot distinguish EX-77's strong earlier approval from EX-88's disputed earlier approval. Backdated displayed fields cannot solve that problem either. It requires authority, scope, effective time and evidence quality.
5. **Missing values require three-valued reasoning.** An activity sequence cannot establish TX-305's release threshold or amount coverage. Defaulting UNKNOWN to LOW or zero suppresses obligations; defaulting it to a high value asserts unsupported violations. Missingness need not be decisive when another fact settles the condition.
6. **Equal timestamps do not imply simultaneous or interchangeable events.** Ignoring event_index can place TX-311's DE update before its ER. Sorting by timestamp alone, or retaining an arbitrary dataframe order, changes reviewed-state reconstruction even when the final activity multiset is identical.
7. **Correct labels and ordering do not prove the correct packet was approved.** TX-307's approval concerns a standard packet. Even a trace containing both ER and Compliance_Approve could approve a different review version. Evidence must link the approval to a relevant completed ER.
8. **External reference-data changes cross case boundaries.** R3 depends on which jurisdiction was added, when the list version became effective, and which version was screened. A per-case activity projection can omit a global update or fail to identify its target and produce a false pass.
9. **The assessment concerns each release, not eventual completion.** A review, approval or rescreen after release does not retroactively meet a prior-event constraint. Multiple releases can have different states and outcomes; checking only the last release or whether activities occur somewhere in the trace hides earlier deviations.

Control-flow conformance remains useful for unconditional sequence and completion requirements. Faithful implementation of these rules needs event-time data guards, historical snapshots, reference-data versions and explicit exception evidence, or a formalism that incorporates those features. A high token-replay or alignment fitness score alone is not a compliance conclusion.

## 7. Recommended Logging and Control Improvements

- **Capture an immutable release decision and payload.** Store amount/currency, risk, jurisdiction, customer type, exemption ID/version, applicable obligations, supporting review/approval/screen IDs, and the allow/hold decision atomically with Release_Funds. Hash or sign the payload. Link settlement to that exact release identifier and record adjustments separately.
- **Record separate effective, event, record and evidence times.** Keep `occurred_at`, `effective_from`, `effective_to`, `recorded_at`, `approved_at_claimed`, trusted `signed_at`, timestamp-authority verification results, and certificate validity/creation metadata. Retain original claims and subsequent corrections with provenance; do not overwrite them into a single timestamp. A claimed backdate must remain distinguishable from verified earlier authorization.
- **Version the rules and their interpretations.** Log rule ID/version, evaluation timestamp, outcome and reason, materiality threshold/baseline, inclusive amount boundary, expiry convention and waiver-acceptance standard. Explicitly decide cumulative amount increases, reinstatement after decreases, non-amount materiality, R2 packet relevance and whether any obligations persist after their release guard becomes false.
- **Bind reviews and approvals to data versions.** Record review type, start/completion lifecycle, reviewed amount/risk/jurisdiction/customer type, immutable snapshot hash, reviewer identity and scope. Link Compliance_Approve to a specific completed Enhanced_Review and packet version, including any approval amount cap and authority evidence. Give invalidation and replacement reviews explicit causal links.
- **Make exemptions structured and auditable.** Capture approving identity and Head_Compliance role at approval time, rules waived, jurisdiction and customer-type scopes, amount bounds/currency, effective interval, revocation/expiry status, approval channel, signatures and evidence provenance. Record outage-delayed insertions separately from authorization. Require independent corroboration where an interested witness is the sole source of earlier approval.
- **Log complete change provenance and explicit unknowns.** Store old/new values, source system, change reason, initiator, verification status and effective time. Distinguish no update, known value, explicitly cleared value and unavailable/unknown; blanks alone cannot represent all four. Capture intermediate amount changes so final decreases cannot hide prior invalidation.
- **Version sanctions data and screening results.** Record added/removed jurisdiction, effective timestamp, list publication/version/hash and distribution receipt time. A screen should record the exact transaction jurisdiction, list version, result and completed-at time. Correlate shared list updates with every affected open transaction; R3 specifically requires a fresh screen, while any release-prohibition rule should be separately encoded.
- **Guarantee stable ordering and atomic decision evaluation.** Preserve event_index with unique event IDs, monotonic source sequence numbers and causal links. Define ordering across sources, handle duplicated/replayed events, and retain reliable UTC clock provenance. Evaluate the release against a stable data/list version and prevent a concurrent material change from slipping between validation and release.
- **Handle unresolved decisions explicitly.** At a future release gate, route decisive UNKNOWN values or disputed waivers to a hold/manual-resolution workflow with reason codes and evidence requests. Re-evaluate on material changes, retain the original decision, and append later audit reassessments. Completeness monitoring should detect missing activity events, data-feed outages and delayed reference updates.
