## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation
The raw/mapped fitness gap (e.g., 0.55 vs. 0.90 in March) reveals that the system migration introduced **labeling drift** without fundamentally altering the underlying process. Mapping `Eligibility Screen` to `Validate Eligibility` recovers conformance, but this does not prove business logic was unchanged—only that the *observable* control-flow remained consistent. Duplicated `Open Case` events and missing resources primarily affect **logging drift** (e.g., provenance gaps) and **resource/organizational drift** (e.g., team-level analyses), but they do not explain post-May rework or cycle-time spikes. The DFG and performance patterns after May (e.g., `AssessRequest Info` rising from 12% to 29%) reflect **real behavioral drift** (supplier-driven rework) and **performance drift** (queueing from staffing cuts), not just logging artifacts. The priority rule further redistributed delays, complicating attribution.

def jaccard_dfg_distance(dfg1, dfg2):
    edges1 = set(dfg1.index)
    edges2 = set(dfg2.index)
    intersection = edges1.intersection(edges2)
    union = edges1.union(edges2)
    return 1 - len(intersection) / len(union)

## 9. Provisional attribution
The **strongest conclusions** are:
1. **March fitness collapse (95% confidence)**: Pure logging drift from system migration. The raw/mapped fitness gap and label changes are unambiguous.
2. **May rework spike (85% confidence)**: Supplier Z disruption. The rework rate doubles *only* for Supplier Z cases, and the timing aligns with the API disruption.
3. **October complexity spike (90% confidence)**: Seasonal population drift. The high-complexity share jumps align with historical renewal seasons.

**Plausible but confounded**:
- **Staffing-driven queueing (80% confidence)**: The June cycle-time spike coincides with staffing cuts, but supplier backlog may mediate this.
- **Priority rule effects (75% confidence)**: The July rise in `Senior Assessment` usage suggests behavioral drift, but seasonal complexity overlaps.

**Mediation/interaction**:
- Supplier Z cases were already more complex, so the May rework spike may reflect **interaction** between supplier disruption and case mix. The priority rule likely **mediates** seasonal complexity by accelerating high-complexity cases.

**Revisions needed if**:
- Event-level data shows queueing is uniform across priority classes (would weaken priority rule hypothesis).
- Supplier Z cases show no rework spike in May (would refute supplier disruption).
- Historical seasonality differs from 2026 (would weaken seasonal attribution).

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. **IF** raw fitness drops >30% but mapped fitness remains stable, **THEN** investigate logging drift (e.g., label schema changes), **BECAUSE** this pattern suggests semantic drift without real process change.
2. **IF** rework rate increases >30% for a specific supplier, **THEN** escalate to supplier quality team, **BECAUSE** this indicates supplier-driven behavioral drift.
3. **IF** median cycle time increases >20% but service time is stable, **THEN** check queue waiting times by priority class, **BECAUSE** this suggests performance drift from staffing or priority rules.
4. **IF** high-complexity share increases >10pp compared to prior year, **THEN** compare to historical seasonality, **BECAUSE** this may reflect population drift.
5. **IF** `Senior Assessment` usage rises >50% in a month, **THEN** validate priority rule compliance, **BECAUSE** this suggests control-flow drift from policy changes.
6. **IF** missing resource % exceeds 5% for migrated activities, **THEN** audit logging pipelines, **BECAUSE** this indicates logging drift.
7. **IF** mapped fitness drops >5% without label/schema changes, **THEN** investigate behavioral drift (e.g., variants, alignments), **BECAUSE** this suggests real process deviation.
