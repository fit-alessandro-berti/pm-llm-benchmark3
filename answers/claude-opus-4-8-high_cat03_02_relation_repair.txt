## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

I treat the domain semantics as defeasible constraints and layer them with the identifier, timing, amount, customer, and route signals in the log. The classification below tells me how much weight a violation or a match should carry.

**Hard invariants** (must never be violated by a repair):
- Referential integrity: every relation must point to an object ID and event ID that actually exist in the tables. I will not invent objects or events.
- Directionality of O2O qualifiers (rule 8): `DEL-X carries PK-7` is stored one way only; I never silently add the reverse.
- Type compatibility of qualifiers: `contains` runs Order→Item, `packs` Package→Item, `carries` Delivery→Package, `bills` Invoice→Order, `settles` Payment→Invoice. A repair that crosses these types is malformed.

**Strong defaults** (expected unless there is specific counter-evidence; rules 1, 2, 3, 4, 7):
- `Create Order` → exactly one Order; `Add Item` → one Order + one Item; `Pack Item` → one Item + one Package; `Dispatch Package` → one Package + one Delivery; `Record Payment` → one Payment + normally one Invoice.
- Lifecycle ordering: an Item is created (Add Item) before it is packed; a Package is packed before it is dispatched; an Invoice is issued before it is paid. Time-ordering that respects this raises confidence; a repair that inverts it is penalized.
- Existing O2O structure mirrors E2O structure. Where an O2O edge already exists (`PK-8 packs IT-A2`), a corresponding E2O relation on the packing event is a strong default, and vice versa.

**Weak heuristics** (suggestive, never decisive; rules 6, 9):
- Identifier prefix similarity: `N-420/N-421/N-42*`, `B-771/B-772`, `PK7-1/PK8-1/PK8-2`. These co-locate candidates but frequently collide across distinct entities.
- Amount arithmetic: `PAY-1=100`, `PAY-2=130`, `INV-44=230`, `ORD-A=130`, `ORD-B=100`. Sums are consistent with several partitions; per rule 6 and the constraints, arithmetic is evidence, not proof.
- Attribute echoes: `order_hint`, `route_hint`, `route`, `portal_customer`, `customer`. Hints are provenance breadcrumbs, not authoritative foreign keys.

**Evidence against a repair:**
- A proposed edge that would force a cardinality far outside its default (e.g., a Payment settling two Invoices) without independent support.
- A proposed edge justified *only* by an inference I myself introduced (circular confirmation).
- A repair that would make a distinguishing attribute contradictory (e.g., binding a package to a delivery whose route differs).

**Combination method.** I score each candidate by starting from its cardinality default, then adjusting up for *independent* corroborating signals (a matching route *and* a matching prefix *and* lifecycle-consistent timing count as three weak signals that can jointly justify a provisional edge) and adjusting down for missing corroboration or default violations. Crucially, I keep a provenance ledger: if an O2O edge is inferred from an E2O edge, that O2O edge cannot later be cited as independent support for the same E2O edge. Signals that come from genuinely different channels (route attribute vs. carrier prefix vs. scanner ref vs. amount) are treated as independent; signals that are the same fact re-expressed are treated as one.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A — conservative / minimum-change.** Add only edges that are backed by *two independent channels* and respect default cardinality.
- *ev08 and PK-8:* Treat ev08 as a scanner-outage item-only row (rule 3). Add the E2O `ev08 → PK-8` and the mirrored O2O `PK-8 packs IT-B1` as **provisional** (P2/P3), flagged for human reconciliation, because prefix `PK8-2` is the only real signal and it is weak. If forced to minimize change, one may even leave it unresolved; I keep it provisional because the outage pattern is explicitly sanctioned.
- *ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:* Bind ev10 → DEL-Y (P5/P6), provisional. Rationale: default is one package → one delivery, DEL-Y is otherwise an orphan, and the `N-421` prefix matches. The trailing `?` is treated as noise, not as evidence of bulk dispatch.
- *ev11 and deliveries:* Leave delivery attribution **unresolved**. Per rule 5 and constraint, `N-42*` cannot be uniquely assigned; ORD-A stays the only confirmed relation. No DEL edge is added.
- *INV-44 and ORD-B:* **Not added.** ev12 lists only ORD-A; the 230=130+100 arithmetic is evidence, not proof, and the constraint forbids treating it as proof. INV-44 remains a single-order invoice in Bundle A.
- *PAY-2 and INV-44:* Provisional settle (P11/P12) *only if* INV-44 is single-order; but if INV-44 bills only ORD-A (130), then PAY-2=130 actually matches ORD-A's value and PAY-1=100 matches ORD-B — which suggests PAY-1 may be misapplied. To avoid over-committing, Bundle A leaves PAY-2 as **unapplied cash (unresolved)** and does not touch the already-observed PAY-1→INV-44 edge.
- *Lifecycle coherence:* Orders A/B keep clean create→add-item lifecycles. PK-8 gets a plausible pack+dispatch chain. DEL-Y is rescued from orphanhood. INV/PAY stays deliberately partial rather than fabricated. This bundle preserves uncertainty and avoids inventing a joint-invoice narrative.

**Bundle B — "joint invoice / consolidated NORTH shipment" interpretation.** Commit to the reading that C17 placed two orders fulfilled and billed together.
- *ev08 and PK-8:* Same as A — IT-B1 packed into PK-8 (now higher confidence, because a consolidated shipment makes cross-order packing natural).
- *ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:* Interpret ev10 as part of a **bulk NORTH dispatch**. Two coherent sub-readings: (B1) ev09→DEL-X carries PK-7, ev10→DEL-Y carries PK-8 (two deliveries); or (B2) both packages ride DEL-X as a bulk dispatch and DEL-Y is a spurious/duplicate record. Bundle B prefers B1 but flags DEL-Y-as-duplicate as a live alternative.
- *ev11 and deliveries:* Under B1, `N-42*` acknowledges **both** DEL-X and DEL-Y together (rule 5 explicitly allows several deliveries acknowledged jointly). Added as provisional multi-delivery E2O rather than a unique assignment — satisfying the constraint that ev11 not be assigned uniquely without justification.
- *INV-44 and ORD-B:* **Added.** The joint-invoice reading makes 230 = ORD-A + ORD-B coherent, and rule 6 permits one invoice over multiple orders. Both E2O `ev12→ORD-B` and O2O `INV-44 bills ORD-B` are added (provisional, ~0.55 within this bundle).
- *PAY-2 and INV-44:* **Added** as a partial payment: PAY-1(100)+PAY-2(130)=230 fully settles the joint invoice. Both settle edges present.
- *Lifecycle coherence:* This bundle yields the most "complete" lifecycles — every object closes out — which is exactly why it is dangerous. It is internally coherent but rests on chained arithmetic and prefix matches; each added edge lowers the independence of the next.

**Bundle C — "unapplied-cash / lost-carrier-id" minimalist.** Deliberately under-repairs. ev08 item-only left unresolved; ev10 left unresolved (no delivery); ev11 customer/order-only (no delivery); INV-44 single-order; PAY-2 unapplied cash. Useful as the null hypothesis: it shows how little the data forces.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

**Order.** ORD-A and ORD-B both have coherent create→add-item lifecycles from ev01–ev05, supported by both E2O (event listings) and O2O (`contains`). No repair needed; no orphan. Under Bundle A, ORD-B never reaches an invoice/payment, which is a *legitimate* incomplete lifecycle, not an error.

**Item.** IT-A1, IT-A2 have create+pack lifecycles double-supported (E2O ev06/ev07 and O2O `packs`). IT-B1 has create (ev05, `ORD-B contains IT-B1`) but its packing is only provisional. IT-B1 is a near-orphan for packing; Bundle A links it to PK-8 provisionally. I explicitly note the *circular-reasoning risk*: the O2O `PK-8 packs IT-B1` I add is derived from the ev08 E2O guess, so it must not be counted as independent confirmation of that guess.

**Package.** PK-7 has pack (ev06) + dispatch (ev09→DEL-X), double-supported by E2O and O2O `carries`. PK-8 has pack (ev07→IT-A2, double-supported) plus provisional dispatch (ev10→DEL-Y). Suspicious one-to-many risk: if both P2/P3 and the existing `PK-8 packs IT-A2` hold, PK-8 packs items from *two different orders* (IT-A2 from ORD-A, IT-B1 from ORD-B) — plausible for consolidation but worth flagging.

**Delivery.** DEL-X: dispatch (ev09) + `carries PK-7`, double-supported. DEL-Y: with no repair it is an **orphan object** (no event, no O2O). Bundle A rescues it via ev10→DEL-Y (provisional); Bundle C leaves it orphaned. ev11's delivery attribution is deliberately unresolved, so neither delivery gets a false "confirmed" status.

**Invoice.** INV-44 has issue (ev12→ORD-A) + `bills ORD-A` + settle from PAY-1, all double-supported. Its relation to ORD-B is unresolved (Bundle A) or provisional (Bundle B). No orphan.

**Payment.** PAY-1→INV-44 double-supported (E2O ev13 + O2O `settles`). PAY-2 (ev14) is an **orphan event/object pair** under Bundle A (unapplied cash) — an intentionally preserved incompleteness rather than a fabricated settle.

**Independence summary.** Only PK-7/DEL-X, PAY-1/INV-44, IT-A1/IT-A2 packing edges enjoy genuinely independent E2O+O2O support. All *repaired* edges have mirrored O2O that are derivative, so I do not double-count them.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**OC-DFG.** Unrepaired: the `Pack Item → Dispatch Package` flow is broken for the PK-8/IT-B1 branch, and `Dispatch Package` has a dangling variant (ev10 with no delivery). Bundle A restores a `Pack Item(ev08) → Dispatch Package(ev10)` path along the Package/Delivery object types and adds a DEL-Y-typed dispatch node. Bundle B additionally introduces `Issue Invoice → Record Payment` edges spanning ORD-B, and a joint `Confirm Delivery` node touching two deliveries — creating a denser, more "complete-looking" DFG that may be spurious.

**OTG (object-type graph).** Unrepaired OTG lacks a Package–Delivery link through PK-8 and any Delivery–Order acknowledgment beyond ORD-A. Bundle A adds Package↔Delivery (via DEL-Y) and Package↔Item (PK-8↔IT-B1). Bundle B further adds Invoice↔Order (INV-44↔ORD-B) and strengthens Payment↔Invoice multiplicity, potentially turning a 1:1 Payment–Invoice edge into 2:1.

**ET–OT graph.** `Dispatch Package` gains a Delivery participation it lacked at ev10; `Confirm Delivery` gains Delivery participation under Bundle B (none under A). `Record Payment` gains a second Invoice-touching instance under B; under A it stays single.

**Flattenings.**
- *Order:* Bundle B duplicates the `Issue Invoice`/`Record Payment` activities onto ORD-B's trace, lengthening it; Bundle A leaves ORD-B ending at packing/dispatch. This directly changes variant counts.
- *Package:* Both bundles give PK-8 a dispatch event; the PK-8 trace becomes `Pack Item → Dispatch Package`.
- *Delivery:* DEL-Y goes from empty (dropped in flattening) to a single-event or two-event trace.
- *Invoice:* Under B, INV-44's trace spans two orders' worth of context; under A it is single-order.
- *Payment:* PAY-2 appears as an isolated `Record Payment` (A, unapplied) or as a settle attached to INV-44 (B).

**Activity frequencies.** By *events*, counts are fixed regardless of repair (14 events). By *unique objects touched*, Bundle B inflates `Issue Invoice`/`Record Payment`/`Confirm Delivery` object coverage. By *total object participations*, repairs add participations (ev08+PK-8, ev10+DEL-Y, and more under B). Reporting event-frequency vs. object-frequency will diverge most under Bundle B.

**Equivalent-execution clustering around Order.** Unrepaired, ORD-A and ORD-B differ (A reaches invoicing, B does not), so they land in different clusters. Bundle A preserves this asymmetry. Bundle B makes them more similar (both billed/paid via the joint invoice), potentially collapsing them into one equivalence class — a material analytical change resting on weak evidence.

Concrete fragments that may appear/disappear: `Dispatch Package(DEL-Y)` node (appears under A/B), `Confirm Delivery→Delivery` edge (appears only under B, and even then non-uniquely), `Issue Invoice→ORD-B` edge and `Record Payment(PAY-2)→INV-44` edge (appear only under B), PAY-2 isolated node (appears under A/C).

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# --- 0. copy originals before repair ---
events_0, objects_0 = events.copy(), objects.copy()
relations_0, o2o_0 = relations.copy(), o2o.copy()

# --- 1. selected additions (Bundle A) ---
e2o_add = pd.DataFrame([
    {"ocel:eid": "ev08", "ocel:oid": "PK-8", "ocel:qualifier": "packs"},
    {"ocel:eid": "ev10", "ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:qualifier": "carries"},
])
o2o_add = pd.DataFrame([
    {"ocel:oid": "PK-8",  "ocel:oid_2": "IT-B1", "ocel:qualifier": "packs"},
    {"ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:oid_2": "PK-8",  "ocel:qualifier": "carries"},
])

# --- 2. referential integrity ---
valid_eids = set(events["ocel:eid"])
valid_oids = set(objects["ocel:oid"])
assert set(e2o_add["ocel:eid"]) <= valid_eids, "unknown event id in E2O additions"
assert set(e2o_add["ocel:oid"]) <= valid_oids, "unknown object id in E2O additions"
assert (set(o2o_add["ocel:oid"]) | set(o2o_add["ocel:oid_2"])) <= valid_oids, "unknown object in O2O"

# --- 3. append + duplicate check ---
relations_r = pd.concat([relations, e2o_add], ignore_index=True)
o2o_r = pd.concat([o2o, o2o_add], ignore_index=True)
assert not relations_r.duplicated(subset=["ocel:eid","ocel:oid","ocel:qualifier"]).any(), "dup E2O"
assert not o2o_r.duplicated(subset=["ocel:oid","ocel:oid_2","ocel:qualifier"]).any(), "dup O2O"

# --- 4. construct/update OCEL (VERSION-DEPENDENT constructor line below) ---
# NOTE: the OCEL constructor signature and expected column names vary across
# pm4py releases; adjust column mapping / arguments to your installed version.
ocel = pm4py.objects.ocel.obj.OCEL(
    events=events, objects=objects, relations=relations_r
)          # <-- VERSION-DEPENDENT: OCEL(...) args differ between pm4py versions
try:
    ocel.o2o = o2o_r  # attaching O2O is also version-dependent
except Exception as e:
    print("attach o2o manually for this pm4py version:", e)

# --- 5. discovery ---
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
otg   = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot  = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

# --- 6. flatten on Order and Delivery ---
order_log    = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Order")
delivery_log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Delivery")

# We deliberately do NOT call pm4py.ocel_o2o_enrichment and treat its output
# as business proof: enrichment propagates structure we assumed, so it would
# confirm our own guesses rather than validate them.
print(order_log["concept:name"].value_counts())
print(delivery_log["concept:name"].value_counts())
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Arithmetic-driven joint invoice.** Adding `INV-44 bills ORD-B` because 130+100=230 fabricates an `Issue Invoice→ORD-B` process path and collapses ORD-A/ORD-B into one equivalence class. The constraint explicitly forbids treating the sum as proof; discounts/taxes could equally explain 230 for ORD-A alone.

2. **PAY-2 forced as a settle.** Labeling PAY-2 as settling INV-44 destroys the legitimate "unapplied cash" pattern (rule 7). Worse, since PAY-2=130 equals ORD-A's declared value, an aggressive matcher might instead reassign PAY-1, corrupting an already-observed, double-supported edge.

3. **Unique delivery assignment for ev11.** Resolving `N-42*` to DEL-X (or DEL-Y) invents a confirmed-delivery lifecycle for a specific delivery on the strength of a wildcard prefix, falsely closing a lifecycle and changing which orders count as "delivered."

4. **Treating mirrored O2O as independent confirmation.** Adding `PK-8 packs IT-B1` from the ev08 guess and then citing it as evidence for the ev08 guess is circular; it doubles apparent support and can push a 0.55 provisional edge to a false "accept."

5. **Balancing packages symmetrically.** Fabricating a second item into PK-7 (or splitting IT-B1 across packages) to make weights/contents look "even" invents `Pack Item` participations with no scan, route, or prefix support — pure structure-completion.

6. **Reading ev10's trailing `?` as a bulk-dispatch signal.** Over-interpreting a data-quality artifact as semantics could merge PK-7 and PK-8 onto DEL-X, deleting the DEL-Y branch and materially altering the OTG and Delivery flattening.

7. **Completing every lifecycle on principle.** Rescuing DEL-Y, PAY-2, and IT-B1 packing all at once produces a tidy, fully-closed graph that looks trustworthy precisely because it is uniform — masking that each edge is independently weak and jointly unverified.
