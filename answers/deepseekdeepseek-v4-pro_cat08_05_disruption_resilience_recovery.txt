3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance
- **Resilience** is the capacity to sustain critical functions while actively managing risk under degraded conditions. It keeps essential safety and regulatory controls alive (even if they are slower or less automated), makes compromises transparent, and plans for eventual reconciliation. In this case, Manual authorization check (B) and Temporary release control (F) are resilient: they substituted manual vigilance for missing electronic checks without losing the control objective. Defer noncritical order (I) is also resilient, provided the deferral is bounded and communicated — it absorbed capacity shock by postponing lowerpriority work.
- **Concealed noncompliance** occurs when shortcuts bypass mandatory controls with no contemporaneous record, creating a hidden liability that may never be fully corrected. Skip system authorization check (C) and Dispatch without scan (G) are clear examples: they erased authorization and chainofcustody evidence, trading immediate throughput for laterfound regulatory exposure. The auditremediation queue (L) in T4 illustrates the consequence — operations “looked” recovered, but compliance was being retrofitted, not maintained.
- **Ambiguous adaptations** lie in between, depending on risk appetite and compensating measures. Hospitalcritical fast lane (A) could be resilient if the missed authorization is later validated and the gap is short, but without that it becomes concealed noncompliance. Phone proof of delivery (H) is weak but better than nothing; if paired with a mandatory followup it becomes part of a resilient fallback, otherwise it conceals weak delivery evidence. The distinction hinges on whether the deviation is (a) intentional and governed, (b) captured in a log that enables recovery of the mandatory evidence, and (c) closed within a predefined risk window.

4. Recovery process model
The redesigned process activates on a declared “WMS Outage” state and operates in three lanes until normal systems are restored.

**Trigger conditions**
- WMS or inventory/scanning system unavailable for >30 minutes, or declared by Incident Commander.

**Triage and lane assignment (performed at order receipt)**
1. **Criticalcare lane** – Hospitalcritical orders, all product types (immediate)
2. **Standardcare lane** – Pharmacystandard orders with controlledsubstance, coldchain, or recallsensitive items; also any order that cannot be deferred beyond 24h.
3. **Deferred lane** – Wholesalebulk (noncoldchain) and routine Pharmacystandard orders; placed in a managed backlog with a maximum hold time of 48h, then moved to standard lane.

**Minimum controls by product/customer risk**

| Coldchain | – Continuous temperature logger with reading recorded on packing list at start and at courier handoff; if logger fails, manual thermometer reading every 30 min.<br>– Photographic evidence of temperature display and packaging. |
| Controlledsubstance | – Dualcontrol signature on pick and pack (two authorised staff).<br>– Visual ID and signature of recipient at dispatch or on delivery (courier signed manifest + phone photo). |

1. Criticalcare lane orders that were partially manual  complete system entry, verify authorisation, close.

7. Management tradeoff statement
Management must accept that during a major cyber incident, perfect realtime compliance is unachievable and some noncritical throughput will be sacrificed to protect hospital patients and the integrity of controlledsubstance/coldchain products. It is acceptable to use manual, paperbased documentation as a temporary bridge, provided it captures the essential evidence contemporaneously and is not treated as a permanent substitute. What must never be accepted is the systematic bypass of authorization or chainofcustody records — particularly for controlled, coldchain, or recallsensitive items — without an immediate, documented compensating control, because this conceals regulatory breaches and creates patientsafety risk. If new evidence showed that an automatic offline app could replicate scanning and authorization checks with minimal friction even during an outage, the recommendation would shift toward retiring paperonly flows and mandating that digital fallback. Likewise, if cost analysis proved that maintaining the recommended kit and drills outweighs the risk of rare outages, a leaner, riskaccepted model might be chosen, but only with explicit boardlevel acceptance of the increased compliance exposure.
