## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

All conclusions below are based solely on the supplied benchmark record: M0, CS-9, PD-3, FD-7, the data-collection change, stakeholder practice, observed cases, and aggregate evidence. 

**Stipulated facts supported by the record**

M0 is a control-flow model for supplier onboarding. It requires `Register_Supplier`, then concurrent `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review`, then an XOR decision: either the approved branch with `Manual_Procurement_Approval` before `Activate_Supplier`, or `Reject_Supplier`. `Notify_Requester` follows the decision, and `Close_Case` follows notification.

CS-9 is the formally approved compliance standard effective from 2025-01-01 onward. It requires sanctions and quality review before activation, manual procurement approval before activation, and a requester/manual-approver separation-of-duties rule. M0 does not encode the resource rule. CS-9 also mentions abbreviated pre-screening for rejected leads, but does not settle whether such leads are governed by M0.

PD-3 was a real steering-committee pilot from 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31, but it was not incorporated into CS-9 or M0. It allowed `Auto_Approve` for LOW-risk DE suppliers already in an approved corporate group, with sanctions and automated master-data validation before activation and quality review up to two business days after provisional activation. Its authority is ambiguous because the compliance observer allowed a monitored exception but later denied approving a weakening of CS-9.

FD-7 was a written emergency directive effective only from 2026-07-10 00:00 through 2026-07-12 23:59. It allowed provisional activation before quality review and manual approval for critical relief suppliers, but sanctions still had to occur before activation, spend was capped at EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval, daily transaction monitoring was required, and quality review had to occur within two business days.

From 2026-07-01, notifications moved partly to CRM. The onboarding log became incomplete for `Notify_Requester`: about 70% of notifications are mirrored, and notification missingness rose from 2% to 29% after migration.

`Request_More_Info` cycles are common, apparently desirable, and absent from M0. Forty-seven cases contain such cycles, and none activated before the requested evidence arrived.

Aggregate evidence shows material exception use: 312 June/July `Auto_Approve` cases, of which 289 appear PD-3-eligible, 14 are clearly ineligible, and 9 have missing eligibility attributes. Six post-pilot cases used `Auto_Approve` in the first week of August.

**Disputed propositions**

The first major disputed proposition is whether PD-3 had authority to override CS-9 or only created monitored, temporary exceptions. The record supports the existence of PD-3, but not a settled governance hierarchy between PD-3 and CS-9.

The second disputed proposition is whether factual continuation of flood recovery after 2026-07-12 extended FD-7. The record supports that operations considered recovery ongoing, but it does not support a written extension.

The third disputed proposition is scope. It is unclear whether rejected pre-screen leads, reactivations, and perhaps other intake objects are full M0 cases. SUP-709 and SUP-710 cannot be judged purely by replaying M0 without first deciding the case notion.

The fourth disputed proposition is evidentiary reliability. A stable CRM `notification_id` is strong notification evidence; a mutable text field entered two days later is weaker. Therefore SUP-702 and SUP-706 should not be treated alike.

The fifth disputed proposition is label equivalence. `QA_Clearance` appears substantively equivalent to `Quality_Review`, but the mapping was approved one week after SUP-713.

The sixth disputed proposition is the materiality of conflict relationships not explicitly prohibited by CS-9. SUP-715 does not breach the requester/manual-approver rule as stated, but it does implicate conflict-of-interest disclosure.

**Unproved propositions**

It is unproved that operations had permanent authority to continue low-risk auto-approval after 2026-07-31.

It is unproved that the 9 auto-approved cases with missing eligibility attributes were eligible.

It is unproved that a mutable CRM text link for SUP-706 is sufficient audit evidence of notification.

It is unproved that prior certification alone satisfies CS-9 for reactivation in SUP-710.

It is unproved that “no transactions before sanctions clearance” cures a sanctions-before-activation violation in SUP-708.

**Burden of proof**

For declaring a case materially noncompliant, I would require: an applicable rule, a reliable trace or corroborated event record, no valid exception in force, and a material breach of either required order, eligibility, authority, resource separation, spend cap, or evidence obligations. For severe findings, the evidence should be stronger than a raw conformance deviation alone.

For accepting an exception, the burden should be on the operation, not the auditor. A valid exception needs contemporaneous authority, effective dates, scope, eligibility snapshot, compensating controls, and immutable evidence. Informal practice, anticipated approval, or post-hoc belief should not be enough.

For data-quality findings, absence in the onboarding log should not automatically prove absence in reality after the CRM migration. But weak external evidence should not be silently promoted to full compliance either.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The strongest prosecution case is that the operation allowed temporary exceptions, emergency procedures, and informal practices to dilute a formally approved compliance standard without adequate governance.

CS-9 is the controlled standard. M0 implements its core control-flow logic for the standard route: sanctions and quality review before activation, manual approval before activation, notification, and closure. PD-3 was not incorporated into CS-9 or M0. FD-7 was narrow, written, and expired automatically. Therefore the prosecution would argue that deviations from CS-9 remain deviations unless the operation can prove a valid exception.

The clearest severe case is SUP-703. It used `Auto_Approve` for a HIGH-risk new foreign supplier on 2026-06-20, despite PD-3 being limited to LOW-risk DE suppliers in an approved corporate group. The first purchase was EUR 180,000. This is not a harmless trace variant. It combines ineligible pilot use, missing manual approval before activation, late quality review, and large financial exposure.

SUP-708 is also severe. A HIGH-risk supplier was activated 40 minutes before `Sanctions_Check`, with no pilot or emergency. CS-9 and FD-7 both preserve sanctions-before-activation as a hard constraint. The fact that no transactions occurred before sanctions clearance mitigates realized harm but does not erase the activation control failure. The prosecution would say the control is designed to prevent exposure at the activation boundary, not merely at first transaction.

SUP-705 and SUP-714 show normalization of expired exceptions. SUP-705 followed an emergency-style route on 2026-07-13, after FD-7 expired. SUP-714 used `Auto_Approve` on 2026-08-02, after PD-3 expired. Operations believed the flood emergency factually continued and that permanent auto-approval was imminent, but neither belief is a written extension or amendment. A controlled compliance system cannot be amended by operational expectation.

Aggregate evidence strengthens this prosecution theory. In June and July, 14 auto-approved cases were clearly ineligible and 9 lacked eligibility attributes. Six more post-pilot cases used auto-approval in early August. Those figures show more than isolated misunderstanding; they suggest that exception logic was becoming an alternative operating model without formal adoption.

The prosecution should also emphasize deviations hidden by M0. SUP-712 fits the control-flow trace but violates CS-9’s requester/manual-approver separation rule once aliases are resolved to the same human. M0’s silence on resources does not make the case compliant. SUP-715 fits M0, but an undisclosed spouse/same-reporting-line relationship between quality reviewer and manual approver raises conflict-of-interest concerns. Even if not expressly prohibited, missing disclosure is a governance breach.

The prosecution must acknowledge genuine model and data defects. SUP-707’s `Request_More_Info` behavior appears desirable. SUP-711 looks like a migration duplicate. SUP-713 is likely a label-mapping problem. SUP-706 is weakened by the CRM migration. But those defects do not rescue SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-705, SUP-714, or SUP-712. A defective model can overstate some deviations while the operation is still materially noncompliant in high-risk areas.

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest defense is that M0 is no longer a faithful normative architecture for the observed process. A raw M0 deviation is not automatically a business violation.

M0 is incomplete. It encodes only control-flow for one standard onboarding path. It omits `Request_More_Info`, even though stakeholders agree the activity is desirable and aggregate evidence says none of the 47 cases with such cycles activated before requested evidence arrived. SUP-707 therefore looks like a model defect, not misconduct.

M0 is also incomplete with respect to policy overlays. PD-3 was a real steering-committee pilot for low-risk DE group suppliers, and FD-7 was a real emergency directive. SUP-702 fits the PD-3 fact pattern: LOW-risk DE supplier, approved corporate group, date inside the pilot window, quality review next business day, complete eligibility fields, and stable CRM notification evidence. SUP-704 fits FD-7: critical pump supplier, activation on 2026-07-11, spend below EUR 20,000, daily monitoring, and quality plus manual approval next business day. Treating those cases as ordinary M0 violations would ignore better contextual evidence.

M0 is incorrectly scoped or at least scope-ambiguous for rejected leads and reactivations. SUP-709 failed sanctions pre-screen and was rejected without quality review. CS-9 says activation requires sanctions and quality, but rejected leads may use abbreviated pre-screening, and the scope document is ambiguous. SUP-710 is a reactivation of a supplier closed six months earlier with prior quality certification still valid; CS-9 does not define reactivation. A conformance checker should not invent scope rules after the fact.

M0 and the event collection are also misaligned. After 2026-07-01, notifications moved to CRM, and missingness in the onboarding log rose sharply. SUP-702 has stable CRM notification evidence despite no onboarding notification event. SUP-706 has weaker CRM evidence, so it is indeterminate, not automatically noncompliant. SUP-713 uses `QA_Clearance`, which audit documents show was equivalent to `Quality_Review`; the case exposes a data dictionary lag. SUP-711 has two `Register_Supplier` rows with the same source transaction ID, which is a migration duplicate rather than a true duplicate business registration.

The defense must still concede genuine risk. SUP-703 is not a defensible pilot case. SUP-708 violates sanctions-before-activation for a high-risk supplier. SUP-705 and SUP-714 may be operationally understandable, but expired or anticipated exceptions are not authority. SUP-712 shows that a model can fit while the case violates a resource rule. Therefore the defense can defeat “all M0 deviations are misconduct,” but it cannot defeat “the exception governance system is materially weak.”

At the **system level**, I would not judge the whole operation as uniformly noncompliant. Several M0 deviations are justified exceptions, model defects, or data-quality problems. SUP-702, SUP-704, SUP-707, SUP-711, and SUP-713 should not be counted as ordinary misconduct.

However, the operation is **materially noncompliant in exception governance and high-risk control enforcement**. SUP-703 and SUP-708 are severe. SUP-705 and SUP-714 show continuation beyond formal authority. SUP-712 shows that a trace can fit M0 while violating a mandatory resource rule. The aggregate evidence—14 clearly ineligible auto-approvals, 9 with missing eligibility attributes, and 6 post-pilot auto-approvals—supports a system-control weakness, not merely isolated model obsolescence.

I reject the prosecution’s argument where it treats every M0 deviation as a compliance breach. That would misclassify desirable information requests, emergency cases within FD-7, CRM migration effects, duplicate events, rejected-lead scope issues, and label-renaming cases.

I reject the defense’s argument where it treats practice, expectation, or operational usefulness as equivalent to authority. Desirable behavior can justify model revision, but it does not retroactively amend CS-9. Temporary exceptions must expire unless extended. Ineligible high-risk auto-approval and activation before sanctions cannot be normalized by saying the model is obsolete.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The revised architecture should be layered rather than forcing all obligations into one POWL model.

**Revised control-flow model**

The control-flow model should contain at least these routes:

Standard onboarding route:
`Register_Supplier` → optional/repeatable `Request_More_Info` before decision → concurrent `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` → `Manual_Procurement_Approval` → `Activate_Supplier` → `Notify_Requester` → `Close_Case`.

Low-risk auto-approval route, only when an active policy overlay permits it:
`Register_Supplier` → optional/repeatable `Request_More_Info` → `Sanctions_Check` and `Automated_Master_Data_Validation` before activation → `Auto_Approve` → `Activate_Supplier` → `Quality_Review` within two business days → notification and closure. The model should not make this route globally available without data-aware eligibility checks.

Emergency/provisional route, only under an active emergency directive:
`Register_Supplier` → optional/repeatable `Request_More_Info` if feasible → `Sanctions_Check` → provisional `Activate_Supplier` → daily monitoring, retrospective `Quality_Review`, and retrospective `Manual_Procurement_Approval` → `Notify_Requester` → `Close_Case`. Spend caps and two-business-day deadlines must be enforced outside POWL.

Rejected-lead route:
For pre-screen failures, permit `Sanctions_Check` → `Reject_Supplier` → `Notify_Requester` → `Close_Case`, but only if the scope rule classifies the object as a pre-screen lead rather than a fully accepted onboarding case. If the lead has entered full onboarding, the standard quality requirements should be explicit.

Reactivation route:
Define a separate case type. A reactivation may reuse prior `Quality_Review` only if certification validity, supplier identity continuity, and maximum age are documented. Otherwise, it follows standard onboarding. The event log must link reactivation to the prior case and certification artifact.

Withdrawal route:
If a supplier/requester withdraws before activation, close through `Withdraw_Supplier` or equivalent, notify the requester, and record that no activation occurred. Do not require full activation controls unless policy says the withdrawal happened after full onboarding acceptance.

**Data-aware eligibility, amount, and time rules**

These rules should be implemented as policy overlays and data checks, not as pure POWL:

| Rule family               | Required check                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Auto-approval eligibility | Risk band must be LOW, country must be DE, approved corporate group must be true, policy version must be active at activation time, and eligibility attributes must be complete and snapshotted before route selection. |
| Pilot dates               | PD-3 route valid only from 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31 unless an immutable extension exists.                                                                                                                          |
| Emergency dates           | FD-7 route valid only from 2026-07-10 00:00 through 2026-07-12 23:59 unless a written extension exists.                                                                                                                 |
| Emergency spend cap       | Cumulative spend before retrospective approval must be ≤ EUR 20,000.                                                                                                                                                    |
| Emergency monitoring      | Daily monitoring events must exist for every calendar/business day with provisional exposure.                                                                                                                           |
| SLA                       | Pilot and emergency quality review must occur within two business days after provisional activation.                                                                                                                    |
| Notification              | `Notify_Requester` must be evidenced before `Close_Case`; CRM evidence must use stable IDs, not mutable text links.                                                                                                     |
| Label mapping             | `QA_Clearance` may count as `Quality_Review` only under an approved mapping with effective date and content equivalence.                                                                                                |

**Resource and conflict-of-interest rules**

These should be explicit constraints:

The requester and manual approver must be different canonical humans after alias resolution.

Delegation must be recorded with delegator, delegate, authority, effective period, and scope.

Quality reviewer and manual approver relationships—spouse, direct reporting line, same small team, financial interest—should at least require disclosure. Depending on policy risk appetite, they may require independent second review.

Actor identity must be resolved through a canonical person ID, not username strings.

**Policy-overlay governance**

Every pilot, emergency directive, waiver, and permanent amendment should have:

policy/rule version ID; issuing authority; approval evidence; compliance sign-off status; effective start and end timestamps; scope; eligibility conditions; compensating controls; expiry behavior; extension mechanism; and retirement status.

A monitored exception should not silently become a permanent route. If operations wants permanent low-risk auto-approval, it needs a formal CS-9 amendment, revised model, data checks, and audit acceptance criteria.

**Where each part belongs**

POWL should represent allowed control-flow variants: standard manual route, low-risk auto route, emergency route, rejection route, notification-before-closure, concurrency of sanctions and quality in the standard route, and an approximation of repeatable information requests.

DECLARE/log-skeleton-style constraints should express cross-cutting obligations: if activated then sanctions must precede activation; if standard route then manual approval precedes activation; if closed then notification precedes close; if `Request_More_Info` exists then requested evidence must arrive before activation; `Reject_Supplier` and `Activate_Supplier` should not both occur in the same lifecycle unless a reopen/reactivation rule applies.

Pandas/data checks should enforce risk/country/group eligibility, effective dates, spend caps, business-day SLAs, CRM linkage, label mappings, duplicate source transaction IDs, missing attributes, and route-selection snapshots.

Organizational controls should govern authority, delegation, conflict-of-interest disclosure, compliance sign-off, emergency declarations, pilot closure, permanent policy amendments, and audit review.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

The following POWL is a defensible approximation. It deliberately duplicates transition labels across branches because each branch needs its own local ordering. The `Request_More_Info` mechanism is approximated as zero-or-more requests before the branch decision/approval point; exact request/evidence-response pairing should be checked in the event data. Amount, risk, date, identity, and authority rules are not encoded in POWL.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def po(nodes, edges=()):
    """Convenience constructor for a StrictPartialOrder."""
    model = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=nodes)
    for source, target in edges:
        model.order.add_edge(source, target)
    return model


def zero_or_more_request_more_info():
    """
    Approximation of zero-or-more Request_More_Info events.

    In process-tree/POWL loop semantics, the first child is the 'do' part.
    Using a silent transition as the do part permits zero visible requests;
    the redo child can repeat Request_More_Info. Pairing each request with
    Evidence_Received / Evidence_Validated should be enforced in data checks.
    """
    return OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.LOOP,
        children=[
            SilentTransition(),
            Transition(label="Request_More_Info"),
        ],
    )


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Standard manual-approval route:
# Register -> optional/repeatable information requests interleaved before
# decision -> concurrent Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review -> Manual Approval
# -> Activate -> Notify -> Close
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

std_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
std_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
std_info_loop = zero_or_more_request_more_info()

# No order among sanctions, quality, and information requests inside this
# partial order. All must complete before manual approval.
std_pre_approval = po(nodes=[std_sanctions, std_quality, std_info_loop])

std_manual = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
std_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
std_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
std_close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

standard_manual_route = po(
    nodes=[std_pre_approval, std_manual, std_activate, std_notify, std_close],
    edges=[
        (std_pre_approval, std_manual),
        (std_manual, std_activate),
        (std_activate, std_notify),
        (std_notify, std_close),
    ],
)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Low-risk auto-approval route:
# Available only when a policy overlay says the supplier is eligible.
# POWL captures the shape; data checks enforce LOW risk, DE, approved group,
# active effective window, and two-business-day Quality_Review SLA.
# Quality_Review and Notify_Requester are both after activation and before
# Close_Case, but their relative order is left open.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

auto_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
auto_master_data = Transition(label="Automated_Master_Data_Validation")
auto_info_loop = zero_or_more_request_more_info()

auto_pre_activation = po(nodes=[auto_sanctions, auto_master_data, auto_info_loop])

auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
auto_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
auto_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
auto_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
auto_close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

auto_post_activation = po(nodes=[auto_quality, auto_notify])

low_risk_auto_route = po(
    nodes=[
        auto_pre_activation,
        auto_approve,
        auto_activate,
        auto_post_activation,
        auto_close,
    ],
    edges=[
        (auto_pre_activation, auto_approve),
        (auto_approve, auto_activate),
        (auto_activate, auto_post_activation),
        (auto_post_activation, auto_close),
    ],
)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Emergency/provisional route:
# Available only under an active emergency directive.
# Sanctions_Check remains before activation. Quality review, manual approval,

rej_sanctions_only = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
```

**Event and data-collection proposal**

The event log should contain immutable `event_id`, stable `case_id`, `supplier_legal_entity_id`, `source_system`, `source_transaction_id`, canonical activity name, raw activity label, lifecycle transition, event timestamp, record timestamp, and correction timestamp. Duplicate events with the same source transaction ID should be flagged rather than treated as business repeats.

Each case should store a route-selection snapshot: selected route, rule/version ID, risk band, country, approved corporate group flag, critical-relief flag, activation timestamp, provisional flag, eligibility attributes, and the user/system that selected the route. Missing eligibility should block auto-approval or create a review exception.

CRM notifications should be linked by immutable `notification_id` and stable case foreign key. Mutable text fields may be retained as notes but should not be primary audit keys. The log should record sent timestamp, delivery status, recipient, channel, and source system.

Resource data should use canonical human IDs with alias tables. The log should capture requester, manual approver, quality reviewer, auto-approval service account, delegation ID, manager-chain snapshot, relationship/COI disclosure ID, and any waiver or independent-review evidence.

Emergency and pilot authority should be represented as data, not tribal knowledge: authority document ID, approver, compliance sign-off status, effective start, effective end, scope, extension ID, expiry status, and compensating controls.

Monitoring and financial exposure should be joined to the process case: first purchase timestamp, cumulative spend before retrospective approval, daily monitoring timestamps, quality due date, quality completion date, manual approval completion date, and SLA breach flags. This is necessary to distinguish SUP-704-style justified emergency use from SUP-705-style expired emergency practice.
