## 1. Conformance semantics

A **forbidden behavior** is an observed relation explicitly prohibited by the norm. It is a graph-level nonconformance when the relation and its scope are correctly represented. It may still be caused by bad identifiers, joins, timestamps, or object associations rather than real-world behavior.

A **missing mandatory behavior** exists when a mandatory relation is absent for an in-scope case or event. Mandatory rules are evaluated case-by-case, not by an unstated tolerance rate. However, scope, event multiplicity, and abstraction must be checked before concluding that the business process failed.

An **unexpected but unregulated behavior** is a relation not listed in the norm but also not forbidden. Under the open-world portions of the norm, it is not a violation merely because it is novel or rare.

A **typical-pattern absence** is a departure from a desirable/common pattern. It is a signal for review, not a nonconformance, unless another rule or policy makes the behavior mandatory.

A **likely data-quality error** is a graph relation plausibly caused by extraction, joins, reused identifiers, missing event-object links, lifecycle construction, or timestamp errors. It can still be a formal graph deviation; the classification of the underlying business behavior remains provisional.

An **abstraction mismatch** occurs when the business rule is about eventual reachability or ordering, but the observed model is directly-follows. An intermediate event can remove a directly-follows edge without violating an eventual business rule.

**Insufficient evidence** applies where aggregate graph weights cannot establish per-event scope, object identity, timing, multiplicity, or whether a purported exception was properly typed.

The norm is closed-world only for explicitly forbidden relations and mandatory relations within their stated scope. Thus N3, T6, and E10 are prohibited if genuinely observed; N1, N4, E1–E8, T1, and T2 require investigation of apparent gaps. Relations absent from the norm—such as `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, package–delivery codeath, and customer review links—remain open-world and are not violations by omission.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

An OC-DFG can establish that, in an object projection, one activity directly follows another and can quantify the number of affected object traces. It supports the strong graph-level concern for the two N3 `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` observations, because cancellation precedes dispatch in the same Order projection.

It cannot by itself establish complete business causality, real-world shipment release, or eventual reachability when an intermediate event exists. N1 explicitly says Orders should **eventually** enter validation, while the summary uses a directly-follows approximation. Therefore, 98/100 direct edges do not prove that the remaining two Orders failed validation. They may have a review, hold, or other intermediate activity. Conversely, if the full traces show no later validation for in-scope standard Orders, N1 becomes a mandatory failure.

The 198/200 N4 result is a real mandatory deviation in the current OC-DFG representation, because all 200 packages are stated to be externally shipped. Still, raw package traces must determine whether packing is absent entirely or merely not directly adjacent to dispatch.

N2 and N5 are typical, not mandatory. The 83 `Validate -> Close` relations and 92 invoice-payment relations are supportive but do not create a required rate. The four write-offs are an expressly legitimate N5 exception. The 14 invoices with no observed successor require receivables review, but not a conformance finding without a payment deadline or closure rule.

### OTG

An OTG establishes that object types interact, co-birth, or co-death, usually as aggregate structural relations. It identifies the T6 Order–Payment cobirth as prohibited if the lifecycle construction is correct. It also shows expected Order–Item and Package–Delivery interaction structures.

It cannot reliably prove that each individual `Add Item` event had an Order interaction or that each specific dispatch event involved its Delivery. That requires event-level object memberships. The apparent contradiction between OTG package–delivery interaction weight 200 and ET–OT Delivery coverage of 198 illustrates this limitation: a Package–Delivery relationship may exist in the OTG even when two dispatch events lack the required Delivery association.

The seven codeath edges are not suspicious merely because they are unmodeled. The norm is open-world for codeath, and the stated shared confirmation event is a plausible explanation.

### ET–OT

ET–OT establishes whether an event type is associated with required object types. It is the most direct model family for E1–E8 and E10. The missing Invoice on one `Record Payment` is an E8 gap because no event is explicitly typed as unapplied cash. Finance’s statement that unapplied cash is possible does not retroactively satisfy the exception.

Likewise, two dispatches lacking Delivery associations are E6 representation gaps despite carrier references. A carrier reference can help repair the data, but it is not currently the mandatory Delivery object relation.

Weights require caution. The 1,450 versus 1,448 Add Item counts show two fewer Order associations, but aggregate counts alone do not prove exactly two distinct nonconforming events if events can have multiple Items or Orders. Event-level records are needed.

A relation can be conforming at one abstraction but suspicious at another: Package–Delivery OTG interaction may exist while E6 associations are incomplete; an Order may eventually validate while N1’s direct OC-DFG edge is absent. PM4Py diagnostics therefore identify structural differences, not final legal, operational, or business judgments.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

**Mandatory**
- N1: two non-direct `Create -> Validate` cases are violations only if in-scope standard Orders never validate eventually.
- N4: two Pack-to-Dispatch gaps are OC-DFG mandatory gaps; business failure depends on whether packing occurred elsewhere in trace.
- E2: two fewer Add Item–Order associations are potential gaps; event-level multiplicity determines the number of affected events.
- E6: two Dispatch–Delivery gaps are mandatory representation failures unless valid Delivery objects/links are recovered.
- E8: one Payment-without-Invoice is a gap unless it is explicitly retyped as unapplied cash.
- E1, E3–E5, E7, T1, and T2 have positive aggregate support.

**Forbidden**
- N3: two cancelled Orders followed by dispatch.
- T6: one Order–Payment cobirth.
- E10: one Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice association.
- For all three, incorrect source linkage can change the conclusion from real behavior to data-quality error, but not erase the observed graph deviation.

**Typical**
- N2: 83 `Validate -> Close` paths support the typical pattern; other paths are not violations.
- N5: 92 paid invoices support the pattern; four write-offs are legitimate exceptions.
- Fourteen invoices without successor are review candidates, not violations, absent timing or payment obligations.

**Unregulated**
- `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72).
- `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review` (3).
- `Reopen Order -> Close Order` (1).
- Order–Customer interaction (100), Order–RiskCase interaction (3), and the review–Customer association (3).
- Package–Delivery codeath (7).
- These become governed only if a separate policy adds a mandatory, forbidden, or typical rule.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

- **N1 temporal meaning:** Risk: direct-follow monitoring can falsely report validation failures. Clarify that every standard Order must eventually validate within a defined period, with permitted intermediate events. **Mandatory.**
- **N4 sequencing meaning:** Risk: legitimate packing followed by an intermediate scan is classified as nonconforming. Clarify whether packing must directly precede dispatch or merely occur before dispatch. **Mandatory.**
- **Cancellation effective point:** Risk: warehouse and Order systems may disagree on when dispatch becomes prohibited. Define cancellation status, effective timestamp, and allowed reversal/return scenarios. **Forbidden** after effective cancellation.
- **Delivery-object equivalence:** Risk: carrier references substitute inconsistently for Delivery objects. Specify whether a carrier reference must materialize a Delivery object and ET–OT link. **Mandatory.**
- **Unapplied-cash typing:** Risk: missing Invoice links are silently excused. Require each payment either to link an Invoice or carry a controlled unapplied-cash type. **Mandatory.**
- **Cobirth definition:** Risk: batch imports or reused IDs produce false T6 breaches. Define “same business event,” object-creation source, and treatment of technical batch events. **Forbidden** for genuine business events.
- **Identifier namespace controls:** Risk: sensor and financial objects can be joined by reused identifiers. Require namespace-qualified joins and prohibit sensor-to-financial relations. **Forbidden.**
- **Typical-pattern governance:** Risk: N2 and N5 cannot distinguish acceptable exceptions from operational debt. Define monitoring windows, aging thresholds, and escalation criteria while retaining non-mandatory status. **Typical.**
- **Unregulated review/customer/codeath/reopen relations:** Risk: analysts may create inconsistent local interpretations. Explicitly retain these as outside this norm unless another policy owns them. **Explicitly unspecified.**

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

**Immediate containment**
- Prioritize N3: hold the two cancelled-order shipments, prevent warehouse release after effective cancellation, and notify fulfilment/control owners.
- Prioritize T6: quarantine the Order–Payment cobirth event from financial-control reporting and stop any creation workflow that can produce it.
- Also quarantine the E10 sensor–Invoice join from downstream financial analytics.

**Data repair**
- Correct the reused-ID join causing E10.
- Reconstruct the two missing Dispatch–Delivery links from carrier references and Delivery master data where valid.
- Reconcile the unlinked payment; either link its Invoice or explicitly type it as unapplied cash.
- Reconcile Add Item event-object memberships and document ET–OT cardinality rules.

**Process investigation**
- Audit the two N3 traces across Order management, warehouse, carrier, and cancellation systems.
- Inspect the two N4 package traces for missed pack scans versus dispatch-before-pack behavior.
- Inspect whether the two N1 cases eventually validated and whether they are standard Orders.

**Norm revision**
- Resolve the N1/N4 direct-follow versus eventual-ordering ambiguity.
- Define Delivery-object treatment, unapplied-cash typing, cancellation effective time, and cobirth semantics.
- Leave review, customer, codeath, and reopening behavior explicitly unspecified unless ownership changes.

**Monitoring**
- Set zero-tolerance alerts for N3, T6, and E10 after data-quality controls are fixed.
- Monitor mandatory ET–OT completeness at event level, not only aggregate weights.
- Track invoice aging and eventual validation/closure timing separately from direct-follow rates.
- Reconcile OTG and ET–OT derivations regularly to detect projection inconsistencies.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

Robust conclusions are the observed graph-level forbidden relations N3, T6, and E10, plus the ET–OT representation gaps for Delivery and Invoice associations. Their real-world interpretation depends on source integrity, especially for E10 and potentially T6. N1 and N4 depend on whether directly-follows is intended to encode eventual business ordering and on scope classification. Typical and unspecified relations cannot be judged noncompliant from rarity or absence alone. Graph summaries alone cannot determine individual event identity, timestamp order, exception typing, object multiplicity, causal responsibility, or whether missing links reflect process behavior versus extraction defects.
