## 1. Interaction-graph description

**Node types.** Job (J1–J6) is the case-like object. Component (C1–C6) is 1:1 with jobs. ComponentLot (L7, L8) is a hub over components. Machine (CNC-2, OVEN-1, TEST-1) and Tool (FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3) are reusable resources. Batch (B1, B2) and Shipment (S1–S3) are pure synchronization objects: they exist only to join jobs.

**Edge types.**
- Declared O2O (direct observation): `requires` (Job→Component), `contains` (Lot→Component), `batches` (Batch→Job), `consolidates` (Shipment→Job).
- E2O co-participation (direct observation): e.g. `Batch Cure` 12:05 {B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J1, J2, J3, C1, C2, C3}; `Machine Process` 11:45 {J4, C4, CNC-2, FIX-A}.
- Pipeline-dependent E2O: `Quarantine Lot` 10:20 and `Release Lot` 13:50 relate to {L7, C2, C5, J2, J5} in one extraction and to no Job in the one that produced the Job flattening.
- Inferred: tool *holds* (FIX-A by J3, 09:00–11:40, from acquire/release); *blocks* (J3 ⇢ J4 via FIX-A); *waits-for* (J1 ⇢ J4 via S1; J1, J2 ⇢ J3 via B1); transitive Job–Lot (Job→Component→Lot); L8 coupling of J1 and J3 (structural only, no event evidence).

**Key structural fact.** Lots, batches and shipments partition the jobs differently: lots {J2, J5}, {J1, J3}; batches {J1, J2, J3}, {J4, J5}; shipments {J1, J4}, {J2, J3}, {J5, J6}. The union is one connected component over all six jobs. J6, in no batch and no lot, is still reachable through S3 and CAL-3.

**Higher-order motifs.**
1. *Batch join star* (Job–Batch–Machine–Tool): {J1, J2, J3}–B1–OVEN-1–RACK-7. Start time is the maximum of member readiness and resource availability.
2. *Tool relay on an idle machine* (Job–Tool–Machine–Job): J3–FIX-A–J4 at CNC-2.
3. *Rack bridge* (Batch–Tool–Batch–Machine): B1–RACK-7–B2 on OVEN-1; the tool state, not the oven, separates the batches.
4. *Lot fork with asymmetric policy* (Lot–Component–Job–Batch): L7→C2→J2→B1 (override) versus L7→C5→J5→B2 (held).
5. *Cross-partition cycle* (Job–Batch–Tool–Shipment): J1–B1–J3–FIX-A–J4–S1–J1. J1 is exposed twice to the same upstream chain.
6. *Test pair* (Job–Machine–Tool–Shipment): {J5, J6}–TEST-1–CAL-3, rejoined in S3.

**Edge list** (`=` observed, `~` inferred):

```
J1 =requires C1   J2 =requires C2   J3 =requires C3   J4 =requires C4   J5 =requires C5   J6 =requires C6
L7 =contains {C2,C5}        L8 =contains {C1,C3}
B1 =batches {J1,J2,J3}      B2 =batches {J4,J5}
S1 =consolidates {J1,J4}    S2 =consolidates {J2,J3}    S3 =consolidates {J5,J6}
B1 =event {OVEN-1,RACK-7}   B2 =stated {OVEN-1,RACK-7}
J4 =event {CNC-2,FIX-A}     J3 ~holds FIX-A [09:00,11:40]
J5,J6 =event {TEST-1,CAL-3}
L7 =event(pipeline-dependent) {J2,J5}
J3 ~blocks J4 (FIX-A)       J3 ~gates {J1,J2} (B1)      J5 ~gates J4 (B2)
RACK-7 ~gates B2 (until 14:35)   CAL-3 ~blocks J6 [16:35,17:20]   J4 ~gates J1 (S1)
L8 ~couples {J1,J3} (no event)
```

## 4. What flattening misses

| Viewpoint | Makes visible | Hides or duplicates | Misleading conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Job | Per-job order, rework loop | Whom a job waits for. `Batch Cure` (2 events) appears 5 times, `Dispatch` (3 events) 6 times. Lot events are absent | "`Pack -> Dispatch` median 1.1 h and no outlier job, so flow is stable", despite J1's 355 min and the z-score ceiling |
| Machine | Utilization, setup–process–release sequence | Jobs waiting on tools, partners or lots. Non-productive events mask idleness | "CNC-2 at 54% with a 48-min maximum gap has spare capacity, so nothing queues there", while J4 waited 115 min |
| Batch | Load→Cure→Unload, two cases | Member readiness spread (235 versus 10 min); RACK-7 state between batches | "Batching is efficient: both cycles take about 50 min" |
| Tool | FIX-A held 2 h 40 min; RACK-7 cleaning; CAL-3 recalibration | Who waited and whether the machine was idle. Unrelated jobs are interleaved into one "tool case" | A DFG edge such as `Final Test -> Recalibrate -> Final Test` reads as routing; high FIX-A occupancy reads as productivity |
| ComponentLot | Quarantine→Release, 3 h 30 min for L7 | Divergent outcomes (J2 overridden, J5 held); L8 has no events; components of unrelated jobs merge into one case | "The quarantine delayed both jobs by 3.5 h" (J2 was not delayed) or, from the Job side, "there was no quarantine" |
| Shipment | Create→Load→Dispatch, 0.8 h and 0.3 h | Waiting of already packed jobs before `Load` | "Shipping takes 1.1 h; no shipment bottleneck" |

**Why low utilization coexists with long waiting.** A step can start only when a conjunction holds: the job, the machine, the tool, the lot release and, for joins, all partners. Utilization measures one conjunct averaged over a shift. When another conjunct is missing, the machine is starved; its idleness is the symptom of the wait, not evidence against it. CNC-2 was idle 10:05–10:47 with J4 present; OVEN-1 was idle 12:55–14:50 with J4 ready from 13:10; TEST-1 was idle 16:35–17:25 with J6 ready. Waiting is also concentrated on particular job-interval pairs, whereas utilization and medians are averages.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**Narrative A: J3 – FIX-A – CNC-2 – J4 – B2 – S1 – J1 (Job, Tool, Machine, Batch, Shipment).**
- *Temporal precedence:* J3 acquires FIX-A 09:00; J4 reaches CNC-2 09:50; release 11:40; J4 starts 11:45; J3 batch-ready 11:55; B1 12:05; J4 batch-ready 13:10; J1 packed 13:30; B2 14:50; J4 packed 19:10; S1 dispatched 19:25.
- *Resource feasibility:* CNC-2 could have taken J4 from 10:05 to 10:47; FIX-A could not.
- *Policy constraints:* J4's process requires FIX-A; batch membership is fixed; S1 leaves complete.
- *Causal speculation:* that the hold was longer than necessary (it spans a CNC-2 idle window, suggesting an idle-held or off-machine fixture, perhaps J3 rework); that it delayed B1 through J3; that it delayed S1. Calc 2b argues against the last: B2 was pinned near 14:40 regardless. J4's 4 h 20 min between B2 and packing is unexplained; a TEST-1/CAL-3 queue behind J5 and J6 (16:10–17:50) or rework are candidates only.

**Narrative B: L7 – C2/C5 – J2/J5 – B1/B2 – RACK-7 – OVEN-1 (Lot, Component, Job, Batch, Tool, Machine).**
- *Temporal precedence:* J2 batch-ready 08:25; quarantine 10:20; B1 cures C2 at 12:05 during the quarantine; RACK-7 in "cleaning/quarantine" until 14:35; release 13:50; J5 ready 14:40; B2 14:50.
- *Resource feasibility:* OVEN-1 free from 12:55; RACK-7 from 14:35; J5 from 14:40.
- *Policy constraints:* the lot hold was applied to J5 and overridden for J2 in a separate system; batch-complete rule.
- *Causal speculation:* that RACK-7's quarantine was triggered by curing quarantined C2 (suggestive wording and timing, no linking event). If true, one lot event is a common cause of both B2 constraints. That J5 would have been ready earlier without the hold is likewise unproven.

**Narrative C: B2 – J5/J6 – TEST-1 – CAL-3 – S3 (Batch, Job, Machine, Tool, Shipment).**
- *Temporal precedence:* J5 ready 16:00, J6 16:05; J5 tested 16:10–16:35; recalibration 16:35–17:20; J6 tested 17:25–17:50; S3 dispatched 35 min after its last pack.
- *Resource feasibility:* TEST-1 was free from 16:35; CAL-3 was not.
- *Policy constraints:* every `Final Test` needs CAL-3; the recalibration trigger is unknown; S3 waits for both jobs.
- *Causal speculation:* J5 probably waited for J6 in S3 by roughly the 75-min gap between test ends (pack times not supplied); the near-collision at 16:00–16:05 may itself descend from L7 through B2's timing.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

**I1. Ready-set, time-boxed batching on OVEN-1 (reduces synchronization).**
- Target: batch synchronization. Benefit: up to 220 min each for J1 and J2.
- Helped: J1, J2, J4. Harmed: stragglers J3 and J5 (solo or later cures); RACK-7 (more cycles, more cleaning).
- Cost/risk: energy, part-load cures; J1's gain converts into S1 waiting unless I3 accompanies it.
- Perverse metric: oven fill rate rewards waiting; "batch wait" alone rewards single-job cures.
- Evaluation: per `Batch Cure` event, each member's start-minus-readiness, joined through Job→Shipment to dispatch time; compare matched batch sizes across a staggered rollout.

**I2. Tool decoupling: second fixture or release-when-idle rule for FIX-A; CAL-3 recalibration scheduled into empty-queue windows; second rack.**
- Target: tool contention. Benefit: 42–110 min locally for J4, 45–50 for J6.
- Helped: J4, J6, B2. Harmed: J3 (re-setup if the fixture is pulled); J5 if recalibration moves ahead of it.
- Cost/risk: capital, calibration compliance; gains absorbed by B2 (calc 2b).
- Perverse metric: tool utilization rewards hoarding.
- Evaluation: overlap of tool-hold intervals with "job present and machine idle" intervals, plus end-to-end Job→Shipment time rather than local waiting.

**I3. Shipment cutoff with partial dispatch when a partner's predicted readiness exceeds a threshold.**
- Target: consolidation. Benefit: up to 340 min for J1.
- Helped: early finishers (J1; probably J5). Harmed: J4 and J6 (ship alone), customers wanting complete deliveries.
- Cost/risk: freight cost, split deliveries.
- Perverse metric: `Load -> Dispatch` time or truck fill, both already "good".
- Evaluation: per-job Pack→Dispatch from Job–Shipment relations, reporting tails, against cost per shipment.

**I4. Component-level quarantine with a single, propagated override record.**
- Target: lot coupling. Benefit: up to 210 min for J5 if C5 is unaffected.
- Helped: J5, B2, J4. Harmed: J2-like jobs if overrides tighten (B1 would have slipped 105 min); quality.
- Cost/risk: traceability effort, escapes.
- Perverse metric: quarantine duration (rushed releases) or override count (under-reporting).
- Evaluation: for each `Quarantine Lot`, follow Lot→Component→Job→Batch and compare held and overridden siblings on downstream delay and rework.

**I5. Logging only: `Wait for Tool` and queue events, tool-state events, readiness provenance, lot events linked to jobs with a qualifier, overrides imported.**
- Target: data artifacts. Benefit: none operationally; makes the ranking testable.
- Helped: analysts, planners. Harmed: operators (scan burden); KPI continuity (numbers will look worse).
- Cost/risk: transitive linking duplicates lot events across jobs.
- Perverse metric: logging completeness encourages rubber-stamp scans.
- Evaluation: run both pipelines in parallel; reconcile E2O counts per type pair; verify that J2 and J5 gain exactly the two lot events.

**I6. Align partitions: plan batch-mates as shipment-mates where customers allow.**
- Target: the J1–B1–J3–FIX-A–J4–S1 cycle. Benefit: a job waits for one partner set, not two.
- Helped: J1. Harmed: customer-driven consolidation; planners.
- Cost/risk: less freight optimization.
- Perverse metric: alignment percentage pursued regardless of due dates.
- Evaluation: count distinct jobs each job transitively waits for in the interaction graph, before and after.

## 7. PM4Py analysis outline

```python
import pandas as pd
import networkx as nx
import pm4py

EID, ACT, TS, OID, OT = "ocel:eid", "ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp", "ocel:oid", "ocel:type"
rel = ocel.relations.copy()
otype = ocel.objects.set_index(OID)[OT].to_dict()

# 1. Object-level interactions (symmetric: each pair appears in both directions)
inter = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)
pair_counts = (inter[inter["ocel:type"] != inter["ocel:type_2"]]
               .groupby(["ocel:type", "ocel:type_2"])
               .agg(interactions=(EID, "size"), distinct_events=(EID, "nunique"))
               .reset_index())

# 2. Job-job coupling through shared hub objects (event-based)
g_inter = pm4py.discover_objects_graph(ocel, graph_type="object_interaction")
neigh = {}
for a, b in g_inter:
    neigh.setdefault(a, set()).add(b)
    neigh.setdefault(b, set()).add(a)
coupling = []
for hub, ns in neigh.items():
    if otype.get(hub) in {"Batch", "Shipment", "Tool", "Machine", "ComponentLot"}:
        jobs = sorted(o for o in ns if otype.get(o) == "Job")
        coupling += [(j1, j2, otype[hub], hub) for i, j1 in enumerate(jobs) for j2 in jobs[i + 1:]]
coupling = pd.DataFrame(coupling, columns=["job_a", "job_b", "via_type", "via_object"])

# 2b. Job-lot links from O2O, independent of how lot events were extracted
o2o = ocel.o2o
req = o2o[o2o["ocel:qualifier"] == "requires"].rename(columns={OID: "job", "ocel:oid_2": "component"})
con = o2o[o2o["ocel:qualifier"] == "contains"].rename(columns={OID: "lot", "ocel:oid_2": "component"})
job_lot = req.merge(con, on="component")[["job", "component", "lot"]]

# 3. Type-level graphs; detect activities that touch lots but never jobs
otg_types, otg_edges = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)                      # {(relation, ot1, ot2): weight}
etot_acts, etot_types, etot_edges, etot_w = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)  # {(activity, ot): weight}
lot_acts = {a for (a, t) in etot_w if t == "ComponentLot"}
job_acts = {a for (a, t) in etot_w if t == "Job"}
unlinked_lot_activities = lot_acts - job_acts      # expected: Quarantine Lot, Release Lot

# 4. OC-DFG: compare three frequency metrics per typed edge
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
rows = []
for ot, edges in ocdfg["edges"]["event_couples"].items():
    for edge, couples in edges.items():
        rows.append({"object_type": ot, "edge": edge,
                     "event_couples": len(couples),
                     "unique_objects": len(ocdfg["edges"]["unique_objects"][ot][edge]),
                     "total_objects": len(ocdfg["edges"]["total_objects"][ot][edge])})
freq = pd.DataFrame(rows)
freq["objects_per_event_couple"] = freq["total_objects"] / freq["event_couples"]   # >1 flags joins

# 5. Flattened baselines and their duplication factor
flat_perf, duplication = {}, {}
for ot in ["Job", "Machine", "Batch", "Tool", "ComponentLot", "Shipment"]:
    flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, ot)
    dfg, sa, ea = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(flat)
    flat_perf[ot] = pd.DataFrame([{"edge": e, **stats} for e, stats in dfg.items()])
    duplication[ot] = len(flat) / flat[EID].nunique()

# 6. Waiting from each job's own previous event to the shared start event (not from machine gaps)
job_rel = rel[rel[OT] == "Job"].sort_values(TS).copy()
job_rel["prev_ts"] = job_rel.groupby(OID)[TS].shift(1)
job_rel["wait_min"] = (job_rel[TS] - job_rel["prev_ts"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60
shared = (rel[rel[OT].isin(["Machine", "Tool", "Batch", "Shipment"])]
          .groupby(EID)[OID].agg(lambda s: tuple(sorted(s))).rename("shared_objects"))
starts = job_rel[job_rel[ACT].isin(["Machine Process", "Batch Cure", "Final Test", "Dispatch"])].join(shared, on=EID)
sync = (starts.groupby([EID, ACT])
        .agg(jobs=(OID, "nunique"), max_wait=("wait_min", "max"),
             min_wait=("wait_min", "min"), job_minutes=("wait_min", "sum")))
sync["sync_spread"] = sync["max_wait"] - sync["min_wait"]     # B1: 235 - 10

# 7. Tool-hold intervals (activity names are assumptions) and overlap with job waiting
START_ACTS, END_ACTS = ["Acquire Tool", "Start Cleaning", "Start Recalibration"], ["Release Tool", "End Cleaning", "End Recalibration"]
tool_ev = rel[rel[OT] == "Tool"].merge(job_rel[[EID, OID]].rename(columns={OID: "holder"}), on=EID, how="left")
h_start = tool_ev[tool_ev[ACT].isin(START_ACTS)].rename(columns={TS: "start", OID: "tool"}).sort_values("start")
h_end = tool_ev[tool_ev[ACT].isin(END_ACTS)].rename(columns={TS: "end", OID: "tool"}).sort_values("end")
holds = pd.merge_asof(h_start, h_end[["tool", "end"]], left_on="start", right_on="end", by="tool", direction="forward")

def blocked_minutes(row):
    if not isinstance(row["shared_objects"], tuple) or pd.isna(row["prev_ts"]):
        return 0.0
    h = holds[holds["tool"].isin(row["shared_objects"]) & (holds["holder"] != row[OID])]
    lo, hi = h["start"].clip(lower=row["prev_ts"]), h["end"].clip(upper=row[TS])
    return (hi - lo).dt.total_seconds().clip(lower=0).sum() / 60

starts["tool_blocked_min"] = starts.apply(blocked_minutes, axis=1)
starts["unexplained_wait_min"] = starts["wait_min"] - starts["tool_blocked_min"]

# 8. Job features against object-centric waiting
feat = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(ocel, "Job", enable_object_lifecycle_paths=True,
                                   enable_object_work_in_progress=True, include_obj_id=True)
job_wait = starts.groupby(OID)[["wait_min", "tool_blocked_min"]].sum()

# 9. Dependency paths between jobs, e.g. J3 -> ... -> J1
G = pm4py.convert_ocel_to_networkx(ocel, variant="ocel_to_nx")
path_j3_j1 = nx.shortest_path(G.to_undirected(), "J3", "J1")

print(pair_counts, freq.sort_values("objects_per_event_couple", ascending=False),
      sync.sort_values("job_minutes", ascending=False), duplication, unlinked_lot_activities, sep="\n\n")
```

## 8. Falsification and additional data

**Observations that would weaken batch synchronization as the top explanation:**
1. "Ready for batch" for J1 and J2 is a status flag set at staging, with physical readiness near 11:55.
2. OVEN-1 or RACK-7 was unavailable between 08:25 and 11:55 (preheat, an unlogged earlier load, maintenance), making it a resource wait.
3. A recipe, thermal-load or order rule requires J1, J2 and J3 to cure together, so the wait is J3's upstream lateness, not a batching choice.
4. Batch waiting is slack: J1 would have waited for S1 until 19:25 anyway, so removing it changes no dispatch time. The supplied data already partly support this.
5. Over more batches, readiness spread is usually small, and B1 is an exception produced by J3's FIX-A hold.
6. Unbatched jobs such as J6 show comparable total waiting.
7. After conditioning on tool-hold and quarantine intervals, batch membership no longer predicts delay.
8. B2-type starts track RACK-7 availability rather than last-member readiness once more instances are observed.

**Additional fields or event types that would discriminate among hypotheses:**
1. `Wait for Tool` and queue-enter/queue-exit events carrying job, machine and tool identifiers.
2. Tool-state events: FIX-A mount/unmount and in-use versus idle-held; RACK-7 cleaning reason code; CAL-3 recalibration trigger (count, interval or drift).
3. Provenance of readiness timestamps (sensor, scan or manual; which system).
4. Batch-formation events (`Create Batch`, `Assign Job`) with rule, planner, oven capacity and recipe compatibility.
5. Lot events linked to components and jobs with qualifiers, plus the supervisor-override records with timestamp, scope and approver.
