# 1. Competing mechanism table

| Mechanism                  | Expected signature                                                                 | Evidence supporting it                                                                 | Evidence against or limiting it                                                        | How to test it                                                                 |
|----------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| learning or warm-up       | Service time decreases and errors stabilize or fall with sequence position early in shift, holding WIP and complexity fixed | Early bins (1–5 to 16–30) show falling service time and error rate while complexity and queue decline modestly | Speed-up continues past position 50 where complexity is already low; later error rise contradicts pure learning | Within-resource fixed-effects regression of service time on sequence position, stratified by WIP and complexity |
| fatigue                   | Service time rises and errors increase late in shift or under long cumulative exposure | Error rate and rework climb sharply after position 50 and in last two hours of Day/Late shifts | Service time keeps falling (not rising) in high-WIP bins; Night shift shows different pattern | Model service time and error as function of minutes-since-start and cumulative events, with quadratic terms |
| circadian/time-of-day effects | Performance varies systematically by clock hour independent of shift position or WIP | Night shift shows higher baseline service time and exception handling; Early shift clears backlog faster | Within-shift position effects persist across Early/Day/Late shifts at similar clock times | Add hour-of-day fixed effects or sinusoidal terms to models already controlling for shift and WIP |
| queue pressure            | Service time falls while error rate rises when WIP or queue_length_at_start is high | High-WIP rows show shortest service times, highest errors, and elevated rework; position 51–75+ bins coincide with rising queue | Early bins have high queue yet lower error than late high-WIP bins; complexity also declines | Stratify by WIP bands within resource and sequence-position deciles; use instrumental-variable approach with upstream arrival rate |
| case-mix/selection        | Complexity and priority decline with sequence position; seniors preferentially take complex cases early | Complexity score falls monotonically; high-complexity share highest in first hour and Night/Early shifts | Error rise under high WIP persists after controlling for complexity; Juniors show larger degradation | Include case_complexity_score and priority as covariates; examine senior vs. junior case assignment by position |
| batching/workarounds      | Short consecutive service times for same activity family, often late in shift, with low variance | Document Review batching by Juniors late in Day shift; Quality Check bursts in final 45 min | Batching does not explain error increase under high WIP for non-batched activities | Apply pm4py.discover_batches and examine inter-event gaps within activity_family by resource and shift position |
| automation interaction    | Error spikes after Bot_Risk output when queue > 70; human corrections increase | Bot_Risk corrections rise sharply above queue 70; Seniors correct more early | Not all activities involve Bot_Risk; error rise also occurs on non-Bot activities under pressure | Filter events where prior activity = Risk Classification and bot_flag = true; compare correction rates by queue band |

# 2. Integrated explanation

Service time declines across the work period primarily because case-mix shifts toward lower-complexity items, queue pressure induces faster processing, and some learning occurs in the first 20–30 events. Error rates, however, rise only when high WIP coincides with later sequence positions, indicating that queue pressure overrides any remaining learning gains and interacts with residual complexity and automation hand-offs. Early-shift complexity carry-over inflates initial service times, while late-shift speed reflects both selection of simpler remaining cases and behavioral response to backlog rather than pure fatigue. Circadian effects appear mainly on Night shifts with higher exception handling, but within-shift position effects dominate during Day and Late shifts. Batching and Quality Check deferral create localized error bursts that are management-induced rather than individual. The net pattern is therefore a composite: learning and selection reduce time early, queue pressure accelerates work at the cost of quality later, and automation amplifies errors under overload.

# 4. Intervention options

1. Strictly cap shift length: modest benefit on late-shift error reduction; risk of throughput loss and increased handovers; monitor end-of-shift error rate and case completion within shift.
2. Move complex cases away from first hour: reduces early overload; risk of delaying urgent cases; monitor first-hour complexity share and service-time variance.
3. Add queue-pressure alert when WIP > 70: directly targets strongest error driver; low risk if alerts are non-punitive; monitor error rate above vs. below threshold.
4. Force random assignment: reduces senior selection bias; risk of mismatching expertise; monitor complexity distribution by resource group and error rates.
5. Introduce mandatory micro-breaks in final two hours: may blunt fatigue and batching; risk of queue growth; monitor last-two-hour error rate and batch size.
6. Defer low-priority quality checks: reduces late bursts; risk of downstream rework increase; monitor 7-day rework_flag rate.
7. Add extra senior reviewer during Day peaks: addresses inherited backlog; cost is main risk; monitor queue at Day-shift start and high-priority completion time.
8. Penalize workers with higher late-shift error rates: no expected benefit, high risk of under-reporting and gaming; monitor changes in error_flag recording completeness.

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

Worker-level error rates are confounded by assignment of complex carry-over cases, inherited queue length, and Bot_Risk quality, making individual comparisons unfair without full adjustment. Measurement error arises because start_timestamp and service time may be logged differently under batching or system delays. Queue pressure is created upstream by staffing and arrival patterns, not solely by worker speed. Automation interaction means some errors are inherited rather than generated. Punitive use of raw late-shift error rates will distort recording behavior and discourage handling of difficult cases.
