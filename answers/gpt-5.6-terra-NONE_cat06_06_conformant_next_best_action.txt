## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

The decision should not be made by comparing only expected deadline loss for P-884. The relevant harm structure is asymmetric:

- **Timely execution benefit:** approximately €20,000–€35,000 in avoided operational loss.
- **False rejection or prolonged-hold harm:** approximately €15,000–€80,000, depending on duration, customer impact, and whether the payment is ultimately legitimate.
- **Unlawful execution harm:** plausibly €100,000–€500,000 or more, plus non-monetary consequences that are not reliably reducible to euros: regulatory intervention, sanctions exposure, mandatory remediation, audit findings, reputational damage, management accountability, and weakened control credibility.

That asymmetry creates a dominance result for A and B: even if they improve the chance of meeting the deadline, they bypass mandatory safeguards exactly where risk is elevated—score 72 and an unresolved sanctions match. Their limited commercial upside cannot justify prohibited execution or a four-eyes breach.

For the conformant options:

- **D with S4** has the best overall balance when S4 may arrive near the early end of the 35–65 minute range. It adds information, preserves independent review, avoids imposing a known delay on P-901, and retains a plausible—though uncertain—path to timely execution.
- **C with S2** is preferred when the marginal probability of S4 arriving late is high, or when P-884’s urgency and risk profile justify reallocating scarce second-officer capacity. It is operationally faster in expectation than waiting for S4, but transfers a meaningful 0.12–0.28 breach-probability increase to another high-value case.
- **E** is safety-oriented but commercially costly. It is justified if current evidence is materially inadequate for any responsible decision, not merely because more information is always desirable.
- **F** controls unlawful-execution risk only if the documented rejection standard is actually met. It should not be treated as a convenient way to avoid second review. Given the 0.55–0.75 predicted ultimate approval rate after conformant review, immediate rejection has substantial false-negative risk.
- **G** is not an operational shortcut. It has option value only if a real incident basis exists and the required authorization and identifier are both completed. Waiting for it as the sole path is dominated by continuing normal review concurrently.

The approval prediction of 0.55–0.75 supports prioritizing a valid review path rather than presuming rejection. But it is observational: expedited screening, staff interruption, escalation, and different reviewer behavior may change outcomes. It should inform queueing and contingency planning, not substitute for control requirements.

### Why A and B cannot be recommended on numerical grounds

A favorable forecast—such as a high probability of meeting the deadline or a high predicted ultimate approval probability—does not alter the process state. The current facts trigger mandatory second review twice over:

1. current risk score is 72, above the 70 threshold; and  
2. the fuzzy sanctions match remains unresolved.

Action A skips that review. Action B simulates it using the same person, R17, who completed the first review, violating the explicit four-eyes principle. A model’s expected-value estimate cannot waive a mandatory legal, regulatory, or internal-control requirement.

### What would make F or G valid

**For F to become valid:**

- the institution’s documented policy must permit rejection at this point;
- the evidence must meet the defined rejection threshold, not merely indicate unresolved uncertainty;
- the person making/recording the decision must have authority;
- the rationale, evidence, and audit trail must be documented;
- if policy requires second review before rejection under the present trigger conditions, that review must occur first.

**For G to become valid:**

- a Compliance Director must actually authorize the override;
- a genuine incident must exist;
- a valid incident identifier must be created, recorded, and justified;
- the authorization must specify the exception’s scope and remain consistent with applicable sanctions obligations;
- the exception must not be used to conceal or bypass a SAR-related hold, and any SAR must not be disclosed to the customer.

## 5. Resource spillovers

### S2 versus S4

**Use S2 (Action C)** when the expected cost of waiting for S4 exceeds the expected spillover harm to P-901. S2 provides the earliest available independent review but imposes a known 45-minute interruption on another high-value case, increasing P-901’s breach probability by 0.12–0.28.

**Wait for S4 (rank-1 plan)** when S4 is likely available near 35 minutes and can be pre-briefed, so that review begins immediately on arrival. This avoids transferring risk to P-901 and pairs naturally with alternate screening, which may finish before S4 becomes available.

The best implementation is not passive waiting: prepare the evidence packet, decision log, unresolved-match analysis, customer-evidence history, and screening results so S4 can make an independent assessment immediately.

### Alternate-screening API quota

There are only three expedited calls remaining. One call is justified here because:

- P-884 is high value;
- it involves a sanctioned-border region;
- the match is unresolved;
- re-screening may change the quality of the second-review decision.

But the call should be targeted and documented. It should not be treated as a substitute for S2/S4 review, nor repeatedly used if the vendor output is unlikely to be incrementally informative. Preserving two calls protects the portfolio against later higher-severity cases.

### Waiting for an exception

Seeking G as the sole plan is risky because the Director response time of 20–60 minutes could consume most of the 90-minute window, and a valid incident still must be created and justified. It should therefore be a parallel, conditional inquiry only where a genuine incident is plausible—not a reason to delay normal review.

4. F may move upward only if a documented rejection threshold becomes clearly satisfied; it does not become preferable merely because review capacity is scarce.
