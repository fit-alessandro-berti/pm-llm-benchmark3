### Why Bidirectional DFG Edges Do Not Prove Concurrency

Observing both $(A, B)$ and $(B, A)$ in a Directly-Follows Graph—such as `(Credit Check, Engineering Review): 17` and `(Engineering Review, Credit Check): 11`, or `(Supplier Confirmation, Expedite Review): 1` and `(Expedite Review, Supplier Confirmation): 1`—is **consistent with** concurrency, but does **not prove** it. At least five distinct generative mechanisms produce identical bidirectional DFG pairs:

1. **True parallelism / overlapping execution (S2):** $A$ and $B$ execute concurrently; whichever finishes first emits its completion event first.
2. **Case-level exclusive choice (XOR) of sequences:** One subpopulation always executes $A$ strictly before $B$ (e.g., standard credit-first orders), while another subpopulation executes $B$ strictly before $A$ (e.g., engineering-led custom designs), with zero temporal overlap in any single case.
3. **Flexible sequential execution:** Workers perform $A$ and $B$ strictly one after the other without a fixed rule, choosing the order ad hoc per case.
4. **Short loops ($A \to B \to A$):** In a DFG alone, rework loops generate both $(A, B)$ and $(B, A)$. Here, inspecting the full traces rules out loops within the 30 observed cases (every activity occurs at most once), demonstrating that the DFG loses cardinality information.
5. **Completion-timestamp and ingestion-index artifacts (D7, S7):** Even if $A$ always starts before $B$ (partial compliance with S1), a slower $A$ can finish after a faster $B$, inverting completion order. Worse, if both tasks complete within the same coarse timestamp granularity (e.g., batch sync or same-second logging), ingestion order arbitrarily determines whether the log records $(A, B)$ or $(B, A)$.

# 2. Shared Assumptions

All three candidate models share the following baseline assumptions and explicitly disclosed abstractions:

1. **Completion-event semantics (D7, S7):** Every transition in every model represents the *recorded completion* of an activity, not its start or duration. Wherever a model places activities in parallel or leaves them unordered in a partial order, that represents *admissible interleaving of completion events*, not a claim that the activities executed simultaneously.
2. **Data-blind abstraction of the missing `external/stocked` attribute (S3, S4, S5):** Because the component-source attribute was not exported, no control-flow-only ProcessTree or POWL model can enforce S3 (`SC` mandatory when `external`) while allowing S4 (`SC` skipped when `stocked`). All three models therefore abstract this data-conditioned decision into an **unguarded control-flow alternative** (either separate variant branches or `XOR(SC, tau)`). This is a deliberate abstraction, not a claim that skipping `SC` is always valid.
3. **Conflation of legitimate skips and missing telemetry (S4, S5):** Approximately 5–10% of `SC` events (roughly 1.5–3 traces out of 30) may be missing due to an integration defect, yet 7/30 traces (23.3%) lack `SC`. Because individual cases cannot be labeled as "stocked" vs. "dropped telemetry," all three models admit `SC`-free executions at the control-flow level. Consequently, none of the three models can flag an external-component order with a missing `SC` event without enriched data.
4. **Representation of optionality:** Optional activities (`Supplier Confirmation`, `Expedite Review`) are represented structurally—via explicit branch enumeration in `model_conservative`, and via `XOR(activity, tau)` (`SilentTransition` / `tau`) in `model_balanced` and `model_permissive`.
5. **High-confidence backbone and acyclicity (D1, D2, D3, D6):** All three models enforce `Register Order` as the unique start, `Approve Configuration  Release Production  Archive Case` as the invariant suffix, mandatory single execution of both `Credit Check` and `Engineering Review` prior to `Approve Configuration`, and acyclicity (no activity executes more than once, matching D6 and 0 observed repetitions across all 30 cases).

# 3. Conservative Candidate

### Inductive-Bias Statement

`model_conservative` adopts a **minimal-generalization, high-precision inductive bias** rooted in the hypothesis that, given $n=30$ traces and unresolved stakeholder contradictions (S1 vs. S2; S3/S4 vs. S5), the safest descriptive baseline is to admit *only* the seven observed completion sequences while factoring out only the invariant start (`Register Order`) and suffix (`Approve Configuration  Release Production  Archive Case`). It refuses to hypothesize unobserved interleavings (such as `EX` without `SC` or `SC` after `ER` alone), prioritizing zero control-flow hallucination over recall of plausible future variants. Because it uses an explicit XOR enumeration of the seven observed middle sequences in a `ProcessTree`, it introduces **duplicate labels** (`Credit Check` and `Engineering Review` each appear 7 times; `Supplier Confirmation` 5 times; `Expedite Review` 2 times). This structure does **not scale**: enumerating interleavings grows factorially ($O(n!)$) with concurrent activities and exponentially ($O(2^k)$) with optional activities, inflating Petri net size, impairing human maintainability, and increasing alignment search overhead.

### Compact Formal Expression

Using ProcessTree notation (`->` sequence, `X` exclusive choice):

```text
->(
  'Register Order',
  X(
    ->('Credit Check', 'Engineering Review', 'Supplier Confirmation'),
    ->('Engineering Review', 'Credit Check', 'Supplier Confirmation'),
    ->('Credit Check', 'Engineering Review'),
    ->('Engineering Review', 'Credit Check'),
    ->('Credit Check', 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Engineering Review'),
    ->('Credit Check', 'Engineering Review', 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Expedite Review'),
    ->('Engineering Review', 'Credit Check', 'Expedite Review', 'Supplier Confirmation')
  ),
  'Approve Configuration',
  'Release Production',
  'Archive Case'
)
```

### Behavior Deliberately Excluded

- **Unobserved `EX`/`SC` combinations:** `RO, CC, ER, EX, SC, AC, RP, Arch` and `RO, ER, CC, SC, EX, AC, RP, Arch` (even though symmetric to V6 and V7).
- **`EX` without `SC` (stocked expedited orders):** `RO, CC, ER, EX, AC, RP, Arch` and `RO, ER, CC, EX, AC, RP, Arch`.
- **Early `SC` combined with `EX`:** `RO, CC, SC, ER, EX, AC, RP, Arch`.
- **`SC` triggered by `ER` before `CC` (D5):** `RO, ER, SC, CC, AC, RP, Arch` and `RO, ER, SC, CC, EX, AC, RP, Arch`.
- **`SC` before both principal checks:** `RO, SC, CC, ER, AC, RP, Arch` and `RO, SC, ER, CC, AC, RP, Arch`.
- **All high/medium-confidence rule violations:** skipping `CC` or `ER` (D3), `EX` before either check (D6, S6), `SC` or `EX` after `AC` (D4, D6), altered start/suffix (D1, D2), and rework loops.

### Known Underfitting Risks

Although `model_conservative` strongly **overfits** at the control-flow level (admitting zero unobserved traces), it still carries meaningful **normative, data-level, and noise-related underfitting risks**:

1. **Data-condition underfitting (S3 vs. S4/S5):** Because branches V3 and V4 unconditionally allow skipping `SC`, external-component orders that violate S3 or suffer from the 5–10% integration loss (S5) are accepted as 100% conforming.
2. **Normative underfitting relative to S1:** Retaining V2, V4, and V7 legitimizes `ER` completing before `CC`; if Finance's rule (S1) is actually mandatory and those 11 traces are compliance breaches or S7 ingestion-order artifacts, the model underfits the true policy.
3. **Singleton-variant elevation:** Treating 1-count traces (V6 and V7) as permanent first-class branches risks encoding one-off exceptions or logging quirks as standard behavior.
4. **No temporal/causal discrimination (D7, S7):** Admitting both `CC  ER` and `ER  CC` branches cannot distinguish compliant sequential work from batch-timestamp tie-breaking artifacts.

# 4. Balanced Candidate

### Inductive-Bias Statement

`model_balanced` adopts a **parsimonious partial-order inductive bias** that generalizes only along dimensions backed by *both* domain evidence and empirical regularity while avoiding duplicate labels. It models completion-order independence where the log and stakeholders support it (`CC` vs. `ER`; `SC` vs. `EX`), encodes every medium- and high-confidence precedence constraint (`D1–D4`, `D6`, `S6`), and conservatively anchors `Supplier Confirmation` to `Credit Check` (`CC  SC_opt`) because `Credit Check` precedes `Supplier Confirmation` in 23/23 observed `SC` traces while V5 proves `SC` need not wait for `Engineering Review`. Crucially, the resulting partial order contains a non-series-parallel **N-structure** (`SC_opt  CC  EX_opt  ER`), which cannot be expressed by a duplicate-free `ProcessTree`, making **POWL** the natural formalism.

### Decision Rationale: Sequence, Concurrency, and Partial Order

- **`Credit Check` vs. `Engineering Review`  Unordered (concurrent completion in partial order):** A strict sequence `CC  ER` (S1) fails on 11/30 traces (36.7%). An explicit XOR of two sequences forces duplicate labels whenever `SC` interleaves (as in V5). Leaving `CC` and `ER` unordered in a partial order captures completion-order independence (consistent with S2 and agnostic under D7) while guaranteeing both complete before `Approve Configuration` (D3).
- **`Supplier Confirmation`  Optional (`XOR(SC, tau)`) with partial-order constraint `CC  SC_opt  AC`, concurrent with `ER` and `EX_opt`:**
  - *Optionality:* 7/30 traces lack `SC`; even discounting 5–10% (~1.5–3 traces) for S5 missingness leaves plausible S4 stocked-part skips.
  - *Precedence:* V5 (`CC, SC, ER`, 2×) rules out forcing `(CC  ER)  SC`. However, `CC` precedes `SC` in **all 23** observed traces containing `SC`, whereas `ER, SC, CC` was never observed and D5 has only low-to-medium confidence. Requiring `CC  SC_opt` while leaving `SC_opt` unordered with `ER` and `EX_opt` is the minimal partial-order generalization that covers all 7 observed variants without leaping to unobserved `ER`-only triggers or pre-check `SC`.
- **`Expedite Review`  Optional (`XOR(EX, tau)`) with partial-order constraints `CC  EX_opt`, `ER  EX_opt`, `EX_opt  AC`, concurrent with `SC_opt`:** Directly encodes D6 and S6 (both principal checks must finish before `EX`; `EX` occurs at most once before `AC`; `EX` may precede or follow `SC`, corroborated by V6 and V7).
- **Prefix and Suffix  Strict sequence:** `RO` precedes `{CC, ER}`, and `AC  RP  Arch` follows `{SC_opt, EX_opt}` (D1, D2).

### Compact Formal Expression

POWL `StrictPartialOrder` $P = (N, \prec)$ with:

- Nodes $N = \{\text{RO}, \text{CC}, \text{ER}, \text{SC}_{\text{opt}}, \text{EX}_{\text{opt}}, \text{AC}, \text{RP}, \text{Arch}\}$, where $\text{SC}_{\text{opt}} = \text{XOR}(\text{SC}, \tau)$ and $\text{EX}_{\text{opt}} = \text{XOR}(\text{EX}, \tau)$.
- Direct (Hasse) precedence edges:
  $$\text{RO} \prec \text{CC},\quad \text{RO} \prec \text{ER},\quad \text{CC} \prec \text{SC}_{\text{opt}},\quad \text{CC} \prec \text{EX}_{\text{opt}},\quad \text{ER} \prec \text{EX}_{\text{opt}},\quad \text{SC}_{\text{opt}} \prec \text{AC},\quad \text{EX}_{\text{opt}} \prec \text{AC},\quad \text{AC} \prec \text{RP},\quad \text{RP} \prec \text{Arch}$$
  (closed under transitivity).

This exact finite language has cardinality **12** (all 7 observed variants + 5 unobserved, domain-consistent linearizations).

### PM4Py Code (`model_balanced`)

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

ro = Transition(label="Register Order")
cc = Transition(label="Credit Check")
er = Transition(label="Engineering Review")
sc_opt = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.XOR,
    [Transition(label="Supplier Confirmation"), SilentTransition()],
)
ex_opt = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.XOR,
    [Transition(label="Expedite Review"), SilentTransition()],
)
ac = Transition(label="Approve Configuration")
rp = Transition(label="Release Production")
arch = Transition(label="Archive Case")

model_balanced = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[ro, cc, er, sc_opt, ex_opt, ac, rp, arch]
)

# Direct (Hasse) precedence relations
model_balanced.order.add_edge(ro, cc)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(ro, er)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(cc, sc_opt)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(cc, ex_opt)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(er, ex_opt)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(sc_opt, ac)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(ex_opt, ac)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(ac, rp)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(rp, arch)

# Ensure transitive closure across PM4Py versions
if hasattr(model_balanced.order, "add_transitive_edges"):
    model_balanced.order.add_transitive_edges()
```

### Behavior Deliberately Excluded

- **`SC` completing before `CC`:** Both `RO, ER, SC, CC, AC, RP, Arch` (symmetric D5 case) and `RO, SC, CC, ER, AC, RP, Arch` / `RO, SC, ER, CC, AC, RP, Arch` (`SC` before both checks).
- **`EX` before both principal checks complete:** e.g., `RO, CC, EX, ER, AC, RP, Arch` or `RO, ER, EX, CC, AC, RP, Arch` (violates D6, S6).
- **Skipping `CC` or `ER`:** Violates D3.
- **`SC` or `EX` after `Approve Configuration`:** Violates D4, D6, S6.
- **Altered start/suffix or activity repetition:** Violates D1, D2, D6.

### Known Underfitting Risks

1. **Unobserved `EX` without `SC` (`CC, ER, EX` and `ER, CC, EX`):** If expediting is operationally meaningful only when external supplier lead times exist (i.e., `EX` implies `SC`), `model_balanced` underfits by allowing expedited stocked orders.
2. **Data-condition and missing-telemetry blindness (S3, S5):** Like all three models, `SC_opt` accepts `SC`-less external orders and missing `SC` events without raising a deviation.
3. **Treating `CC` and `ER` as order-independent despite S1:** If Finance's rule (S1) is genuinely binding, allowing `ER` before `CC` underfits normative policy.
4. **Unobserved `CC, SC, ER, EX`:** Combines two low-frequency phenomena (V5's early `SC` and V6/V7's `EX`); plausible under D5+D6, but empirically unseen.

# 5. Permissive Candidate

### Inductive-Bias Statement

`model_permissive` adopts a **high-recall, exploratory inductive bias** designed to accommodate plausible but unseen interleavings in a small ($n=30$) sample while preserving every medium- and high-confidence domain invariant (`D1–D4`, `D6`, `S6`). Specifically, because D5 has the lowest confidence among the substantive precedence rules and because small-sample censoring could easily hide `ER, SC, CC` (or early procurement confirmation launched immediately after order registration), `model_permissive` drops the `CC  SC_opt` edge from `model_balanced`. Removing that single edge eliminates the non-series-parallel N-structure, collapsing the partial order into a clean, duplicate-free `ProcessTree` in which optional `Supplier Confirmation` runs fully concurrently with the `(CC || ER)  EX_opt` block prior to `Approve Configuration`.

### Boundary Between Reasonable Generalization and Forbidden Behavior

- **Reasonable generalization (admitted, 18 total traces = 7 observed + 11 unobserved):**
  - All 12 traces of `model_balanced`.
  - The 2 unobserved traces where `SC` completes after `ER` and before `CC` (`ER, SC, CC` and `ER, SC, CC, EX`), directly supported by symmetric D5.
  - The 4 unobserved traces where `SC` completes before *both* `CC` and `ER` (`SC, CC, ER`, `SC, ER, CC`, `SC, CC, ER, EX`, `SC, ER, CC, EX`), representing the plausible hypothesis that procurement can pre-confirm known external components immediately upon `Register Order` if D5 is weaker than stated.
- **Behavior that remains strictly forbidden:**
  - Any violation of D1 (start $\neq$ `RO`, end $\neq$ `Arch`).
  - Any violation of D2 (`RP` before `AC`, missing `AC`/`RP`/`Arch`, or post-`AC` insertion of checks/SC/EX).
  - Any violation of D3 (omitting `CC` or `ER`, or executing either after `AC`).
  - Any violation of D4 (`SC` after `AC`).
  - Any violation of D6/S6 (`EX` before both `CC` and `ER` finish, `EX` after `AC`, or executing `EX` more than once).
  - Any rework or repetition loops (0 observed instances; unsupported by any stakeholder or domain rule).

Using ProcessTree notation (`->` sequence, `+` parallel, `X` exclusive choice, `tau` silent skip):

  'Release Production',
  'Archive Case'

   - *Earliest violated constraint:* Position 3 (`Expedite Review`). Violates `->(+(CC, ER), X(EX, tau))` (D6, S6: `EX` cannot fire until both `CC` and `ER` have completed).
2. **`RO, CC, SC, AC, RP, Arch`**
   - *Earliest violated constraint:* Position 4 (`Approve Configuration`). Violates D3: the parallel block `+(CC, ER)` has not completed `Engineering Review`, so `AC` is not enabled.
3. **`RO, CC, ER, AC, SC, RP, Arch`**
   - *Earliest violated constraint:* Position 5 (`Supplier Confirmation`). Violates D4: `SC` is placed strictly before `Approve Configuration` and cannot execute after approval.
4. **`RO, CC, ER, RP, AC, Arch`**
   - *Earliest violated constraint:* Position 4 (`Release Production`). Violates D2: `Approve Configuration` must precede `Release Production`.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

# 8. Validation Code

The script below is self-contained. It reconstructs the 30-case event log, verifies the DFG, constructs all three candidates, converts each to an accepting Petri net, runs soundness checks, computes alignment-based fitness/precision, TBR generalization, and simplicity, runs bounded/sampled playout, and evaluates a positive/negative trace test suite using exact alignment-based trace membership.

**Methodological limitations explicitly noted:**
- Standard PM4Py playout (`pm4py.play_out`) performs **bounded/sampled simulation**; it **cannot exhaustively compare infinite languages** (such as models with loops) and **cannot prove language inclusion or exhaustiveness** even for finite languages because rare paths may be missed in any finite random sample.
- Alignment-based membership checking (`pm4py.fitness_alignments` on single traces) provides an **exact membership decision for each individually supplied finite trace**, which is ideal for testing curated positive/negative suites, but it checks only the enumerated test cases rather than the full language.

model_balanced.order.add_edge(ex_opt, ac)

        grouped = event_log_or_df.groupby("case:concept:name")["concept:name"].apply(tuple)
        return set(grouped.tolist())
    return {tuple(event["concept:name"] for event in trace) for trace in event_log_or_df}

    """Exact alignment-based membership test for a single finite trace."""
    single_log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string([",".join(trace_activities)])
    fit = pm4py.fitness_alignments(single_log, net, im, fm)
    return abs(fit["log_fitness"] - 1.0) < 1e-9


observed_trace_set = {tuple(acts) for _, acts in observed_variants}

# 9. Recommendation and Data-Collection Plan

### Immediate-Use and Sensitivity-Analysis Recommendations

- **Recommended for immediate use: `model_balanced`.** It achieves exact fit on all 30 observed cases, avoids duplicate labels, enforces every medium- and high-confidence constraint (`D1–D4`, `D6`, `S6`), and generalizes cleanly across the five highest-plausibility unseen combinations without opening the risky pre-check `SC` loophole. Any deployment must explicitly document that `SC` optionality is currently unguarded (S3/S5) and that `ER` before `CC` is treated as descriptively valid pending resolution of S1/S7.
- **Recommended for sensitivity analysis: `model_permissive`.** Running conformance checks in parallel against `model_permissive` quantifies how many future deviations flagged by `model_balanced` stem specifically from the uncertain `CC  SC` assumption (i.e., `ER, SC, CC` or early-procurement `SC` traces) versus genuine high-confidence rule violations (`D1–D4`, `D6`). (Where a strict audit freeze is needed, `model_conservative` can serve as a secondary novelty detector to alert analysts whenever *any* previously unseen variant appears.)

### Concrete Data-Collection Plan

| Ambiguity to Resolve | Attributes / Lifecycle Data to Collect | How It Resolves the Ambiguity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **True concurrency vs. sequence vs. tie-breaking (D7, S1, S2, S7)** | Both `start` and `complete` lifecycle transitions (`lifecycle:transition`) with millisecond UTC timestamps and synchronized source clocks, plus `ingestion_timestamp` separate from business event time. | Overlapping intervals (`CC_start < ER_complete` and `ER_start < CC_complete`) confirm S2 concurrency; non-overlapping `CC_complete < ER_start` vs. `ER_complete < CC_start` distinguishes S1 from XOR-sequence; separating business time from ingestion time eliminates S7 artifacts. |

### Maximally Informative Future Traces

Collecting 100–200 enriched cases will be far more informative if analysts specifically watch for the following discriminating trace signatures:

1. **Interval-overlapping trace (`CC_start < ER_start < ER_complete < CC_complete`):** Directly refutes strict sequence (S1) and XOR-sequence, confirming true concurrency (S2).
2. **Cleanly non-overlapping `ER_complete < CC_start` (with verified millisecond timestamps):** Refutes S7 tie-breaking as the sole cause of `ER  CC` and forces an explicit governance decision between S1 (compliance failure) and flexible/XOR sequencing.
3. **`RO, ER, SC, CC, AC, RP, Arch` (with `SC_start > ER_complete` and `SC_complete < CC_complete`):** Directly falsifies `model_balanced`'s `CC  SC` assumption and confirms symmetric D5 (an OR-precondition on `CC` or `ER`).
4. **`RO, SC, CC, ER, AC, RP, Arch` (with `SC_start < min(CC_complete, ER_complete)`):** Falsifies strict D5 and confirms `model_permissive`'s fully concurrent `SC` hypothesis.
5. **`RO, CC, ER, EX, AC, RP, Arch` with `component_source = stocked` and verified clean integration:** Confirms that `EX` does not depend on `SC` (validating `balanced`/`permissive`'s independence between `EX_opt` and `SC_opt`).
6. **`RO, CC, ER, AC, RP, Arch` with `component_source = external`:** If matched to a dead-letter queue error, quantifies S5 telemetry loss; if no integration error occurred, proves an active S3 compliance breach.

# 10. Uncertainty Statement

### Calibrated Confidence Assessment by Modeling Decision

| Modeling Decision | Calibrated Confidence | Key Epistemic Drivers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Start = `RO`, End = `Arch`, invariant `AC  RP  Arch` suffix (D1, D2)** | **High (90–95%)** | Unanimous across 30/30 traces and corroborated by high-confidence domain rules D1 and D2. |
| **Both `CC` and `ER` mandatory before `AC` (D3)** | **Medium–High (80–90%)** | Unanimous across 30/30 traces and D3, though rare emergency bypasses could remain unseen at $n=30$. |
| **`EX` optional, at most once, strictly after both `CC` and `ER`, unordered with `SC` (D6, S6)** | **Medium–High (75–85%)** | Strongly corroborated by D6, Compliance (S6), and both observed `EX` traces (V6, V7), though empirical count is very small ($n_{\text{EX}}=2$). |
| **Optionality of `SC` at the control-flow level (D4, S3, S4, S5)** | **High for descriptive presence of skips (85–95%); Low for data-level validity (30–45%)** | 23.3% absence exceeds the 5–10% integration loss, strongly supporting true stocked skips (S4); however, without the `external/stocked` attribute we have low confidence in classifying any individual `SC`-less trace. |
| **`CC` and `ER` modeled as order-independent / concurrent rather than strict sequence (D7, S1, S2, S7)** | **Medium (55–70%)** | Strong empirical split (19:11) and Engineering testimony (S2) favor order-independence, but Finance (S1) and missing start timestamps / tie-breaking (D7, S7) prevent ruling out non-compliance or logging artifacts. |
| **`CC  SC` precedence in `model_balanced` vs. symmetric D5 or fully concurrent `SC` (D5, V5)** | **Low–Medium (40–55%)** | `CC` precedes `SC` in 23/23 `SC` traces, yet early `SC` occurs in only 2/15 `CC`-first `SC` traces (13.3%). At that same rate across the 8 `ER`-first `SC` traces, observing zero `ER, SC, CC` cases has probability $(1 - 2/15)^8 \approx 32\%$, so small-sample censoring is very plausible. |
| **Acyclicity / no rework loops** | **Medium (65–80%)** | Zero repetitions in 30 cases and explicit at-most-once rule for `EX` (D6), but low-frequency rework (e.g., 2–5% of orders requiring re-engineering) could easily be absent from a 30-case sample. |

### Plausible Alternative Interpretations That Would Materially Change the Recommendation

1. **Normative S1 Enforcement + S7 Ingestion-Order Artifact (or Widespread Non-Compliance):**
   Suppose Finance's rule (S1: `CC` must finish before `ER` starts) is a binding financial-risk control, and the 11 traces with `ER` before `CC` arise either because both checks batch-complete at end-of-day with identical timestamps broken by ingestion index (S7) or because Engineering routinely bypasses credit holds. Under this interpretation, deploying `model_balanced` for operational monitoring would **falsely classify ~37% of cases as compliant**. The recommendation would materially change: instead of `model_balanced`, the organization should deploy a **normative sequential conformance model (`CC  ER`)** for compliance auditing while fixing timestamp granularity, explicitly accepting lower descriptive fitness on the historical log to expose true policy breaches.

2. **Symmetric D5 Censored by Small Sample Size ($n=30$):**
   As shown above, if `SC` can start as soon as *either* `CC` or `ER` completes (symmetric D5) with a ~13% early-confirmation rate, there is roughly a 1-in-3 chance of observing zero `ER, SC, CC` cases in a 30-trace log. If `ER, SC, CC` actually occurs in 3–5% of future orders, `model_balanced` will generate unnecessary false-positive conformance alerts in production. Confirming symmetric D5 would materially shift the immediate-use recommendation away from `model_balanced` toward either a **D5-symmetric OR-precondition model** (expressing "at least one of `CC` or `ER` before `SC`" via a guarded Petri net or two-branch POWL XOR) or **`model_permissive`** paired with data-level compliance guards.
