# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

An activity is core here when it is a repeated, characteristic part of the resource's normal assignment, supported by case mix and team context. A peripheral event can be legitimate support, a handoff, a substitute action, or a record of review; its count alone does not grant decision rights. Case counts may overlap across resources, so they should not be summed into process totals.

- **Automated intake and precheck:** A1 and A2 are its entire observed workload. Its low-complexity case mix and the stated precheck function limit what can be inferred from 610 A2 events; final entitlement authority should be verified elsewhere.
- **Permanent front-office service:** A2/A4/A12 recur at high volume and are central to service delivery. The smaller A3/A5/A8/A13 counts represent assistance and handoffs. Amira's higher-complexity mix and Giorgio's exception work warrant skill distinctions inside the home team.
- **Temporary front-office capacity:** A1/A2/A4/A12 are operationally core during seasonal deployment, but the scope of A2 appears limited to simple cases. Their lack of certification and the later validation-note rework call for supervision and documentation checks; substitution or high volume is not evidence of permanent exception authority.
- **Technical diagnosis and implementation:** A3/A5/A10 are core for both analysts. A6, QA, reopening, and knowledge updates are secondary interactions. Chen's critical-case and security-adjacent exposure may reflect advanced expertise or case routing; equal activity mixes do not settle that question.
- **Security review specialist:** A6 is the defining control despite only 96 events. Erik's high/critical exposure makes the role capacity sensitive. Technical and quality events around a review are peripheral; Mateo's temporary referral filtering did not replace Erik's specialist judgment on critical cases.
- **Commercial exception specialist:** A7/A8 are Fatima's core work. Her A9 events may be recommendations, attendance, or delegated action; the log alone cannot distinguish them. Giorgio's absence coverage demonstrates a useful backup but the 38-to-74-minute median duration increase shows that backup capacity was slower in that episode.
- **Senior service and commercial bridge:** Routine front-office tasks remain Giorgio's home workload. A7/A8 are a substantial second function, especially during Fatima's absence, rather than random noise. Pricing certification supports capability, but the applicable limits of delegated authority must be checked separately.
- **Supervisor and escalation decision gate:** A9 is Hana's characteristic activity and formal title supports decision authority. A8/A13 and occasional checks are context or review. Mateo's preliminary A9 entries, including 13 rechecks by Hana, should not be counted as equivalent supervisor decisions.
- **Quality and knowledge gate:** A11/A14 are core for Ivan and Julia; their smaller A10/A12 counts may be follow-through after findings. Their close similarity and similar complexity support one operational role, subject to checking whether one has distinct sign-off rights.
- **Cross-functional incident coordination:** Mateo's core function is evidenced by cross-team sequences, reopens, high-complexity cases, and broad handoffs, rather than one dominant activity count. A9 preliminary actions and specialist touches are boundary work. The week-5 substitution and week-7/8 referral filtering are temporary episodes, not proof of permanent approval or security-review authority.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

- **R07_Giorgio, commercial shadow and backup:** A formal senior service agent with pricing certification repeatedly performs A7/A8, and covered Fatima's absence. This supplies continuity and context for complex customer conversations. It also risks a slower exception queue and unclear limits on pricing or approval decisions. Document the conditions under which he can review, negotiate, and escalate; measure outcomes separately from Fatima's.
- **R13_Mateo, escalation broker:** His wide activity span, high/critical case mix, reopening work, and placement between specialists and the supervisor fit a routing and coordination role. Filtering low-risk security referrals reduced false-escalation complaints, but two critical cases waited over two days. His preliminary A9 entries are especially important to label as preliminary until an authority record shows otherwise. This bridge can reduce noise and handoff delay while also becoming a bottleneck or obscuring accountability.
- **R05_Erik, rare critical specialist:** Erik has a clear formal Security Specialist title, so the shadow aspect is his operational importance being nearly invisible to volume-based discovery, not an allegation of informal or improper practice. A small number of his reviews can control high-risk cases. Referral filtering, queue aging, and specialist backup matter more than total event share.
- **R14_Nora, automation boundary:** Automated A1/A2 work shifts routine intake away from humans, creating a boundary between precheck and accountable human validation. Treating bot A2 events as equivalent to human decisions could understate validation capacity and control needs.
- **R11_Kai and R12_Lina, temporary capacity bridge:** They absorb simple demand and free experienced agents for harder cases. Their closure and note-quality episode shows why a supervised scope, clear rework ownership, and targeted training matter.

These bridges can be intentional and valuable. Whether they are formally delegated, time-limited, or merely advisory must be checked against policy, decision records, and outcomes rather than inferred from activity labels.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

The following illustrative workflow assumes case_complexity is a numeric score; map labels to an ordered score first if the raw file uses categories. PM4Py return shapes can vary by release, so the role-output adapter should be checked against the installed version. The adapter shown assumes activity/resource pairs. Keep formal title, certification, and approval rights in a separate roster rather than deriving them from mined events.

~~~python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

df = pd.read_csv("events.csv")
df["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"], utc=True)
df["case_complexity"] = pd.to_numeric(df["case_complexity"], errors="coerce")
df["substituted"] = (
    df["temporary_substitution"].astype(str).str.lower()
      .isin(["true", "1", "yes"])
)

log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)

roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log)
similarity = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log)
handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log)
working_together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log)

# Inspect strong similarity pairs and incident edges in both networks,
# especially those involving R07, R13, R05, R08, and R14.
print(similarity)
print(handover)
print(working_together)

# Adapt this normalization if the installed PM4Py release returns
# a different structure. Keep overlapping memberships.
role_members = pd.DataFrame(
    [
        (role_id, resource, tuple(sorted(activities)))
        for role_id, (activities, resources) in enumerate(roles)
        for resource in resources
    ],
    columns=["mined_role", "org:resource", "mined_activities"],
)

# One resource-case record prevents multiple events on one case from
# inflating that resource's complexity distribution.
case_resource = (
    log.groupby(["case:concept:name", "org:resource"], as_index=False)
       .agg(
           complexity=("case_complexity", "first"),
           substituted=("substituted", "max"),
       )
)
resource_context = (
    case_resource.groupby("org:resource", as_index=False)
        .agg(
            cases=("case:concept:name", "nunique"),
            median_complexity=("complexity", "median"),
            substituted_case_share=("substituted", "mean"),
        )
)
activity_counts = pd.crosstab(log["org:resource"], log["concept:name"])
activity_counts = activity_counts.add_prefix("events_")

role_context = (
    role_members.merge(resource_context, on="org:resource", how="left")
                .merge(activity_counts, left_on="org:resource",
                       right_index=True, how="left")
)
print(role_context)

# Re-run on ordinary operation to see whether a mined role depends on
# recorded substitution episodes. Compare rather than silently pool them.
ordinary_roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(
    log.loc[~log["substituted"]].copy()
)

# Review role_context alongside a separately verified roster containing
# home team, title, certifications, and specific decision rights.
# A similarity edge or A9/A7 count is evidence of observed work, not
# evidence of approval authority or permanent competence.
~~~

Two staffing cautions are essential. Reassess the structure after normalizing for case mix, time period, handoffs, service time, quality outcomes, and the process owner's formal delegation rules.
