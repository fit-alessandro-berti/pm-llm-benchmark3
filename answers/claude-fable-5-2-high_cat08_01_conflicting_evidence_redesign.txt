## 1. Current-process diagnosis

**The contradiction, stated plainly.** The dominant path (Intake -> Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement -> Close) looks excellent on speed and unit cost, but it is cheap partly because it skips the controls the policy requires, and fast partly because the clock stops before its failures become visible. The evidence does not say "fast is bad" or "reviewed is good". It says the answer flips by segment and risk.

**Evidence that the fast path is attractive**

- *Frequency DFG:* 3,600 of 5,000 claims (72%) go to the auto screen, 2,550 of those go straight to Fast approval, and 2,300 go on to Dispatch replacement. This is the process for roughly half of all claims.
- *Performance DFG:* the edge means along this path sum to about 10 hours (0.8 + 1.0 + 3.0 + 5.0). The reviewed alternatives carry single edges that are longer than that whole path: Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote has mean 22 h and p90 60 h; Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review has mean 34 h and p90 88 h.
- *Cost:* the auto screen costs EUR 0.20 per case against EUR 34-52 per labor hour for review, and fast-approval cases are 31% cheaper before rework.

**Evidence that the advantage is partly an illusion**

- *Rework statistics:* complaint/reopen occurs in 14.1% of Fast approval cases against 9.2% overall. In the DFG, 400 of the 510 arcs into Customer complaint/reopen come from Fast approval (140) or Dispatch replacement (260). The cost advantage falls from 31% to 9% once complaints, reshipments and service credits are counted.
- *The 9% is an average that probably hides a sign flip.* Fast-path complaint rates are 18-22% for high-value devices against 6-8% for accessories, and a wrong replacement costs EUR 410 against EUR 95. The fast path is therefore plausibly still much cheaper for accessories and plausibly more expensive for high-value devices. The prompt does not give segment-level cost, so this is a hypothesis to test, not a result.
- *The cycle-time measure is censored.* Dispatch replacement -> Close takes a mean of 5 h, but Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen has median 70 h and p90 180 h. The case is "closed" long before the failure appears, which is why alignments report "Customer complaint/reopen after close" as a frequent move-on-log deviation. A failed fast case then waits a further mean 40 h for Manual eligibility review or 54 h for the Escalation board. That puts it past 110 h before recovery starts, which is no better than the 96-120 h medians of the reviewed path.
- *Conformance:* token-replay fitness is 0.91 but only 68% of traces fit. About 1,600 traces deviate, each by only a few moves. This is the signature of a small number of systematic skips, not chaotic behaviour. The alignment diagnostics name them: Fast approval before evidence completeness (R2), dispatch without risk review for high-value devices (R1), and reopen after close. The cause is structural: the auto-screen rules are "outdated and not segment-aware", so the violations are built into the routing rather than being operator error.
- *R3 exposure:* 120 of the 430 observed exits from Customer complaint/reopen go to the Escalation board, which exits directly to Dispatch (65), Repair (45) or Claim denied (10) with no Manual eligibility review arc.

**Where the slow path is not better**

- *Premium low-risk accessories (18%):* the manual path takes a median of 46 h against 8 h and breaches the 24-hour service-credit threshold, to move complaints from 6% to 3%. At EUR 95 per wrong replacement that is worth roughly EUR 3 per case, and R2 explicitly exempts this segment. Here the fast path is the correct, compliant design.
- *Standard low-risk accessories (25%):* the fast path takes 13 h and customers tolerate 72 h with good status visibility. There are about 59 hours of unused slack for a light control, so a full manual path (52 h) is unnecessary.
- *The repair path's 3.3% reopen rate is survivor-conditioned.* It counts only cases that completed Repair quote -> Customer approval -> Repair device. The DFG shows 80 complaints directly after Repair quote (about 14% of 570 quotes) and 70 abandonments after Customer approval. Rework repair also hits 12.5% of repaired high-value devices.
- *Selection effect:* cases reach the reviewed path for a reason, so the 5-6% versus 18-22% gap is not a clean causal effect of review. The temporal profile links the long-tail complaints to *incorrect replacement part selection*, which an eligibility review does not necessarily fix.

**Other structural problems**

- *Evidence loop:* Request missing evidence occurs 1,430 times. Of those, 890 return to Manual eligibility review (mean 34 h), 320 end in abandonment (82% of all 390 abandonments), and 26% of cases that enter it repeat it. The "unclear defect / missing evidence" segment (22%) has a 140 h median.
- *Failure demand on reviewers:* Manual eligibility review receives about 2,600 entries, of which 1,200 (46%) are re-entries: 890 from the evidence loop and 310 from reopens. The seven reviewers provide 252 h per week, and queues grow sharply above 82% utilization (about 207 h). Any redesign that adds review work without removing this failure demand will stall.
- *Fraud/risk review:* 980 entries go to two specialists and 210 end in denial. Waits are 11-18 h in and 16-21 h out, with peaks on Mondays and after campaigns.
- *Premium temporal deviation:* Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote is too slow for Premium cases, driven by part-price confirmation.

**Caveats on the evidence itself**

- The frequency DFG is not flow-conserving. Only 3,625 cases reach END, and about 1,200 more arcs enter Manual eligibility review than leave it. Either edges were filtered or many cases are still open. This must be reconciled before baselining.
- Mid-risk devices appear in the context but not in the segment outcomes.
- The 350 Fast approval -> Close cases have no fulfilment event.
- Low precision of the discovered model means rare mixed paths should not be over-read.

## 2. Redesign A: Risk-tiered routing with policy guards (make the fast path conditional)

**Redesigned flow.** Replace the outdated auto screen with a segment-aware one that classifies each claim by value, device class, customer tier, defect clarity and evidence status, then routes it to one of three lanes.

- *Express lane:* Premium low-risk accessories. Auto screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch -> Close, unchanged. It uses the R2 exception, targets under 24 h, and is controlled by a post-hoc sample audit instead of a gate.
- *Verified-fast lane:* Standard low-risk accessories, and mid-risk devices with a clear defect at or below EUR 400. An automated evidence-completeness confirmation becomes a hard precondition of Fast approval. If evidence is incomplete, one consolidated request is sent. The customer gets a 72-hour promise with proactive status messages.
- *Reviewed lane:* any claim above EUR 400 or any high-value device. The system cannot issue Fast approval without a review token. The default is a short-form Manual eligibility review by the seven-reviewer pool; Fraud/risk review is used only when a risk score triggers it, since R1 accepts either. Premium cases go first in the queue. The outcome is Fast approval -> Dispatch, or Repair quote.

**Targets.** The 2,550-case Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval arc; the R1 and R2 move-on-log deviations; the Premium and Standard high-value segments (35% of cases; 18-22% fast-path complaints).

**Mechanism of improvement.**

- The most frequent deviations become impossible paths rather than monitored ones, which should raise the 68% fitting-trace share directly.
- Review effort moves to where an avoided error is worth the most. If the 12-17 point complaint gap on high-value devices were fully causal and each complaint cost a wrong replacement, it would be worth roughly EUR 49-70 per case, which pays for more than an hour of reviewer time at EUR 34. The 3-4 point gap on accessories is worth about EUR 3-4, or 5-7 minutes of review. These are upper-bound illustrations, not ROI.
- Defaulting R1 to manual review protects the two fraud specialists.

**Deliberately sacrificed or unresolved.**

- High-value claims lose the 20-28 h fast median and land somewhere between the fast and reviewed medians.
- Manual-review inflow rises by the 15-35% the prompt warns about.
- It does not fix the evidence loop or the unclear-defect segment.
- It may not fix wrong part selection, because that is a fulfilment error rather than an eligibility error.

## 3. Redesign B: Evidence-first intake and error-proofed dispatch (remove failure demand at both ends)

**Redesigned flow.** Routing stays as it is; the change is to what information exists at each step.

1. Guided intake through the evidence portal, with an assisted phone or agent channel in parallel. Checklists are specific to the defect type, and a serial-number lookup pre-fills model, warranty status and claim value.
2. An explicit "evidence complete" milestone is logged *before* eligibility assessment.
3. Auto or manual eligibility, then the technical decision, as today.
4. The replacement SKU is derived from the serial number and bill of materials, not chosen freely.
5. A lightweight pre-dispatch gate, staffed by the underused quality inspectors, applies only to high-value replacements and SKU-mismatch flags.
6. Dispatch, then a provisional close that becomes final only after delivery confirmation or a complaint window.

**Targets.** The Request missing evidence loop (1,430 events, 26% repeats, 320 abandonments); the unclear-defect segment (22%, 140 h median); the Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen long tail (260 arcs, linked to wrong part selection); the reopen-after-close deviation.

**Mechanism of improvement.**

- Evidence completeness is satisfied before approval by construction, which covers R2 for every segment without adding a reviewer step.
- The portal's 30-45% reduction in repeated requests cuts the 890 re-entries into Manual eligibility review, releasing scarce reviewer hours.
- The gate attacks the diagnosed cause of late complaints using idle capacity. Scoping it is an economic decision. At an illustrative 10-20 minutes of inspector time (EUR 6-13), it breaks even if it catches about 1.5-3% of high-value dispatches (EUR 410 each). For accessories it would need to catch about 7-13% (EUR 95 each), which is roughly the entire fast-path complaint rate, so accessories stay ungated.

**Deliberately sacrificed or unresolved.**

- Intake takes more customer effort, with a real abandonment risk for customers with low digital literacy.
- Gated dispatches take 6-18 h longer.
- R1 is not addressed: high-value claims can still be auto-approved without review.
- The fraud bottleneck and the slow repair path are untouched.
- Premium accessories gain little.

## 4. Redesign C: Compress the reviewed path and the recovery loop (make the good path fast)

**Redesigned flow.** A and B make the fast path safer. C makes the reviewed/repair path and complaint recovery quicker, through organisation, parallelism and decision rights.

- *High-value cell:* high-value claims go to a small cross-skilled cell with a single case owner. Some reviewers are trained for first-level risk checks, so the two specialists see only triggered cases and the Monday and post-campaign peaks are absorbed.
- *Parallel work:* eligibility review and repair-quote preparation run concurrently instead of in sequence.
- *Pre-priced quotes:* quotes come from a pre-priced parts catalogue or fixed-price bands, which removes the part-price confirmation wait.
- *Pre-authorisation:* the customer authorises at intake any repair that is free or within a price band, so the Customer approval step occurs only for out-of-band quotes.
- *Quality check stays:* Repair -> Quality check is kept and made stricter for high-value devices.
- *Reopen fast lane:* every reopen goes to a named senior reviewer with delegated authority (replacement, repair, goodwill up to a limit), who performs the R3 Manual eligibility review within a day. The Escalation board handles only above-limit cases and denials.

**Targets.** Premium and Standard high-value reviewed paths (96 h and 120 h medians); the temporal-profile deviation on Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote (mean 22 h, p90 60 h); Repair quote -> Customer approval (mean 30 h, p90 80 h); the 80 quote-stage complaints and 70 post-approval abandonments; the reopen waits of 40-54 h; the daily 45-minute board slot.

**Mechanism of improvement.**

- These edges are dominated by waiting for prices, customers and meeting slots, not by work. Removing handoffs and approvals shortens the path without removing controls.
- R3 becomes compliant by design, because the delegated decision-maker *is* the manual review.
- The board's satisfaction effect is kept while its scalability limit is removed.

**Deliberately sacrificed or unresolved.**

- It does not reduce fast-path defects at source or fix R1/R2. It makes failure cheaper to recover and the alternative more attractive.
- It does nothing for accessories or the evidence loop.
- Parallel quoting wastes technician effort on claims that are later denied.
- Delegated authority risks goodwill leakage.
- It needs organisational change and catalogue maintenance.

## 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A**

- *Failure: reviewer overload.* The added inflow pushes reviewer utilization past 82%. Queues then grow non-linearly and Premium high-value claims become slower than today's reviewed path.
- *Failure: weak causal effect.* The reviewed path's 5-6% complaint rate may reflect case mix, or repair versus replacement, and not review itself. Forced reviews would then add cost without cutting wrong-part complaints.
- *Failure: threshold gaming and hollow reviews.* Claim values may bunch just under EUR 400, or the short-form review may degrade into a rubber stamp. Both would produce paper conformance without real control.
- *Bottleneck migration:* from downstream complaint handling to the Manual eligibility review queue; secondarily to Request missing evidence, because the completeness guard exposes more incomplete claims; and to the fraud specialists on Mondays if risk triggers are set too wide.

**Redesign B**

- *Failure: invisible abandonment.* Abandonment moves from after Request missing evidence (visible, 320 cases) to before claim creation (invisible in the event log). The log would then look better while outcomes get worse, especially for low-digital-literacy customers.
- *Failure: "complete" is not "sufficient".* Checklists get satisfied with poor photos, and the loop survives under another name.
- *Failure: gate checks against bad master data.* If the serial-to-SKU mapping is itself wrong, the gate checks picks against an incorrect reference, adding 6-18 h with a low catch rate.
- *Bottleneck migration:* to the two quality inspectors, who are underused *except* after repair campaigns and would then collide with repair QC peaks; and to the assisted-intake channel.

**Redesign C**

- *Failure: mis-set price bands.* Dissatisfaction moves from the quote stage to post-repair billing disputes.
- *Failure: inconsistent or generous delegated decisions.* Cost leaks through goodwill, and cross-trained first-level risk checks may dilute fraud detection (210 denials today).
- *Failure: speed pressure on repair.* Rework could rise above the current 12.5% for high-value devices.
- *Bottleneck migration:* from part-price confirmation and the Escalation board to the repair bench (three technicians, parts availability), then to Quality check, and to the single senior reviewer who holds delegated authority.

## 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**Stage 0 (about two weeks, no process change): repair the measurement.**

- Reconcile the DFG flow imbalance, including open cases and the 1,200 unexplained absorptions at Manual eligibility review.
- Measure review and gate touch times; utilization cannot be managed without them.
- Baseline every segment, including mid-risk devices.
- Redefine the outcome as *time and cost to true resolution*, with a complaint observation window of at least 14 days, since the p90 complaint latency is 180 h.

**Stage 1 (first pilot): two narrow pilots on separate populations and separate resource pools.**

- *1a, R1 guard from Redesign A, Premium high-value only.* This segment has the highest complaint rate (22%) and the highest compliance exposure. Roll it out stepped by product line or region, and use Standard high-value as a concurrent comparison. A compliance rule should not be randomly withheld, so a stepped rollout replaces randomization here.
- *1b, pre-dispatch gate from Redesign B, Standard high-value replacements.* Randomize by case, gate versus no gate. This is legitimate because the gate is not a normative rule. It uses inspectors, not reviewers, so it does not interfere with 1a.

**Metrics to monitor**

- *Leading (days):* reviewer utilization and queue age p90; share of claims above EUR 400 with a review token before approval; evidence confirmed before dispatch; gate catch rate and added hours; fraud queue on Mondays.
- *Lagging (by intake cohort, after the window matures):* complaint/reopen within 14 days per segment; wrong-part rate; service credits; cost per resolved claim including rework; p90 time to true resolution; share of Premium accessories within 24 h; abandonment and denial rates; share of fitting traces and counts per deviation type.

**Stop/go thresholds (ranges, not point targets)**

| Pilot | Go | Stop or redesign |
|---|---|---|
| 1a: R1 guard | Premium high-value complaint rate closes at least about half the gap from 22% toward 5%; median first resolution stays well below the 96 h reviewed median (roughly 48 h or less); utilization stays below about 80% | Utilization above 82% for two consecutive weeks; Intake -> Manual eligibility review p90 rises materially above the 18 h baseline; less than about a quarter of the gap closes (which would suggest case mix, not review) |
| 1b: pre-dispatch gate | Catch rate clearly above the 1.5-3% illustrative break-even; added time in the lower half of the 6-18 h range | Catch rate near zero; or the complaint long tail is unchanged despite catches |
| Portal (Stage 2) | Repeated requests fall by at least about 20% relative (against the claimed 30-45%) | Total abandonment, including pre-submission drop-off, rises by more than a few points, or rises markedly in assisted or low-digital cohorts |

**Sequencing logic: release capacity before consuming it; fix the compliance exposure first where volume is small.**

- *Stage 2:* the evidence portal with an assisted channel on the unclear-defect segment, to free reviewer hours from the 890 re-entries.
- *Stage 3:* extend A to Standard high-value and add the automated evidence guard for Standard accessories, only if Stage 2 has demonstrably lowered reviewer load.
- *Stage 4:* Redesign C. The delegated reopen lane can start early because it is cheap and fixes R3. The pre-priced catalogue and high-value cell come last, because their value grows with the volume that A routes onto the reviewed path, and because the catalogue has the longest lead time (start building it during Stage 1).
- *Throughout:* Premium low-risk accessories stay on the Express lane as a stable reference group.

**Telling real improvement from displacement**

- Judge cohorts by intake week on true-resolution metrics, treating immature cohorts as right-censored. Never judge on first-close time.
- Use concurrent controls and stratify by segment, value and defect type, to separate intervention effects from mix shifts.
- Keep a fixed guardrail panel covering every exit and neighbouring queue: abandonment (including pre-intake), denials, escalations, fraud queue, quality-check queue, open-case aging, service credits, and complaints arriving through channels outside the log. An improvement counts only if no guardrail worsens by a comparable amount.
- Re-run token replay and alignments per cohort. Fitting traces should rise above 68% *because the named deviations fall*, without new deviation types or workarounds appearing, such as claim values bunching under EUR 400.
- Check flow conservation: cases must reach END, not accumulate as work in progress.
