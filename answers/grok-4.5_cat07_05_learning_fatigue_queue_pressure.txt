# 2. Integrated explanation

Service time and error rates do not move for one reason. The shift is a stack of mechanisms that dominate in different windows.

**Opening (positions 1–15):** Shifts inherit backlog. Early and Late start with long queues (61, 70); Night and Early first-hour high-complexity shares are 56% and 48%. Seniors preferentially absorb hard carryover. Mean complexity and priority are highest, so mean service time is high (18.8 then 15.2 min). Some genuine warm-up exists—especially for juniors (first-20 service 19.6 min)—but a large share of the early “slowness” is case-mix and selection, not pure learning failure. Errors are moderate because staffing is fresher and complexity is handled more by seniors.

**Mid-shift (16–50):** Complexity and priority fall (complexity ~0.50–0.54, high-priority ~25–28%). Queues ease toward ~31–33. Service time declines to ~12.9–13.6 min. This looks like learning/warm-up and stabilization, and within-resource speed-up after the first ~20 events supports a real proficiency gain. But easier residual mix and lower pressure co-explain the drop. Errors bottom out (~2.5–2.7%). This is the closest window to a “clean” operating regime.

**Late shift (51+):** Queues rebuild (62 then 88), especially Day (end queue 75). Service time falls further (11.4  10.8 min) while errors and 7-day rework jump (4.6%7.9%; 8.2%13.7%). That pattern is the opposite of simple fatigue-as-slowdown. It matches a **speed-quality tradeoff under queue pressure**, amplified by:
- lower average complexity (0.460.43) and lower priority share, which *enable* faster clicks through easier work;
- batching and workarounds (junior Document Review runs; QC deferred into last 45 minutes);
- automation strain (more Bot_Risk corrections when queue>70);
- experience gradients (juniors’ later high-WIP error 12.4% vs seniors 4.9%).

Fatigue and circadian load likely **reduce control** (raising error propensity) without lengthening service time when throughput pressure and managerial pace norms push speed. Night looks different: fewer events, more urgent exceptions, longer late service (15.2)—circadian and case-mix, not the same Day-peak pressure signature.

**Why service time falls while errors rise only in some conditions:**  
Speed-up without error rise occurs when mix eases and WIP stays moderate (positions ~16–50; low/medium WIP). Speed-up *with* error rise occurs when WIP>70, especially on remaining high-complexity work (error 10.9%) and for juniors/mids. Low-complexity high-WIP still shows elevated errors (5.8% vs 2.1% baseline), so pressure is not only “hard cases left.” Selection (seniors early on hard cases), learning (within-shift proficiency), fatigue/circadian (late control loss), queue pressure (dominant late Day/Late), batching (artificial short service), and bot interaction (correction load under stress) jointly produce the table. Reducing the story to “workers get tired” or “workers learn” alone is inconsistent with falling late service times, early complexity skew, and the WIP×complexity interaction.

# 3. Proposed analyses

## Design principles
- Prefer **within-resource** and **within-shift** contrasts to avoid confounding skill and assignment.
- Always **adjust for complexity, priority, activity_family**.
- Separate **minutes_since_shift_start / sequence_position** from **clock hour** and **shift_id**.
- Treat WIP/queue as both covariate and stratum; test thresholds near 70.
- Detect batches before interpreting short service times as skill.

## PM4Py-oriented workflow (pseudocode)

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

# --- Load & format ---
df = pd.read_csv("claims_event_log.csv")
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp"
)
log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df)

# Service / waiting / arrival-finish features
df = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df)
df = pm4py.insert_case_arrival_finish_rate(df)
df = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(df)

# Event-level service time in minutes
df["service_min"] = (
    pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"]) - pd.to_datetime(df["start_timestamp"])
).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0

# --- 1. Within-resource comparisons ---
# Fixed-effect style: demean by org:resource
df["svc_demean"] = df.groupby("org:resource")["service_min"].transform(
    lambda s: s - s.mean()
)
df["err_demean"] = df.groupby("org:resource")["error_flag"].transform(
    lambda s: s - s.mean()
)

# First 20 vs later within resource × shift
df["early_pos"] = df["sequence_position_within_shift"] <= 20

# --- 2. Within-shift position effects ---
df["pos_bin"] = pd.cut(
    df["sequence_position_within_shift"],
    bins=[0, 5, 15, 30, 50, 75, 10_000],
    labels=["1-5", "6-15", "16-30", "31-50", "51-75", "76+"]
)
# Humans only
humans = df[df["org:resource"] != "Bot_Risk"]
pos_summary = humans.groupby("pos_bin").agg(
    mean_svc=("service_min", "mean"),
    err_rate=("error_flag", "mean"),
    rework=("rework_flag", "mean"),
    mean_cx=("case_complexity_score", "mean"),
    mean_q=("queue_length_at_start", "mean"),
    n=("service_min", "size")
)

# --- 3. WIP / queue stratification ---
df["wip_band"] = pd.cut(
    df["wip_at_start"], bins=[-1, 30, 70, 10_000],
    labels=["low", "med", "high"]
)
df["cx_band"] = pd.qcut(df["case_complexity_score"], q=2, labels=["low_cx", "high_cx"])
wip_cx = df.groupby(["wip_band", "cx_band"]).agg(
    mean_svc=("service_min", "mean"),
    err=("error_flag", "mean"),
    n=("service_min", "size")
)

# --- 4. Complexity-adjusted residual models (conceptual) ---
# OLS: service_min ~ C(org:resource) + case_complexity_score + priority
#                  + wip_at_start + sequence_position_within_shift
#                  + minutes_since_shift_start + C(shift_id) + C(activity_family)
#                  + resource_experience_months
# Same for error_flag (logit). Inspect residual position & WIP coefficients.

# --- 5. Shift and time-of-day controls ---
df["hour"] = pd.to_datetime(df["start_timestamp"]).dt.hour
# Compare same pos_bin across shift_id; clock hour vs minutes_since_shift_start
tod = df.groupby(["shift_id", "hour"]).agg(
    mean_svc=("service_min", "mean"),
    err=("error_flag", "mean"),
    mean_cx=("case_complexity_score", "mean"),
    mean_wip=("wip_at_start", "mean")
)

# Optional PM4Py filters for slices
# log_day_peak = pm4py.filter_time_range(log, "....T15:00:00", "....T17:00:00", mode="events")

# --- 6. Batching detection ---
batches = pm4py.discover_batches(log)  # inspect concurrent/sequential batches
# Dotted chart / distribution for visual batch structure
# pm4py.save_vis_dotted_chart(log, "dotted.png", attributes=["org:resource"])
# pm4py.save_vis_events_distribution_graph(log, "dist.png")

# Run-length hint for Document Review juniors
dr = df[(df["concept:name"] == "Document Review") &
        (df["org:resource"].isin(["Junior_H", "Junior_I"]))].sort_values(
    ["org:resource", "start_timestamp"]
)
# flag sequential runs with short service_min and stable queue_length_at_start

# --- 7. Resource-experience interactions ---
def exp_group(r, months):
    if str(r).startswith("Senior"): return "Senior"
    if str(r).startswith("Mid"): return "Mid"
    if str(r).startswith("Junior"): return "Junior"
    if str(r).startswith("Night"): return "Night"
    return "Bot"

df["exp_grp"] = [exp_group(r, m) for r, m in
                 zip(df["org:resource"], df["resource_experience_months"])]
interact = df[df["exp_grp"] != "Bot"].groupby(
    ["exp_grp", "early_pos", "wip_band"]

# --- 8. Rework / error downstream ---
rework_by_act = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(log)
# Service time profiles
# st = pm4py.get_service_time(log, aggregate_method="mean")

# Paths involving Bot_Risk corrections / Rework Correction
perf_dfg = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)
# Filter high-queue bot events then human Risk/Rework paths
high_q = df[df["queue_length_at_start"] > 70]
bot_corr = high_q[high_q["concept:name"].isin(
```

## Statistical tests to prioritize
1. **Mixed models:** `service_min` and `error_flag` ~ position + wip + complexity + priority + hour + (1|resource) + (1|shift_id).
2. **Spline / threshold** on `wip_at_start` and `queue_length_at_start` (knot near 70).
3. **Interaction:** `experience_group × wip_band × late_position`.
4. **Matched sets:** same resource, same activity_family, similar complexity; contrast low vs high queue.
5. **Mediation-style decomposition:** how much of positionservice_time runs through complexity vs residual warm-up.
6. **Downstream:** error_flag / rework_flag within 7 days by originating position bin and WIP; activity-level rework via `get_rework_cases_per_activity`.
7. **Assignment model:** P(senior | high complexity, first hour, post-weekend) to quantify selection.
8. **Batch-adjusted service times:** exclude or flag discover_batches runs when estimating learning curves.

**Priority package (mechanism-aligned):** (3) WIP alert + capacity, (7) senior peak cover, careful (2) only for *non-urgent* complex admin work, (5) breaks **paired with** staffing so queue does not spike, improve Bot_Risk under load, and redesign QC timing without dropping checks. # 5. Ethical and operational cautions

**Selection bias:** Seniors systematically take harder early/post-backlog cases; juniors may see different activity families and late batched Document Review. Raw speed or error rankings confound skill with assignment. “Slow” early work often means responsible absorption of carryover complexity.

**Measurement error:** `error_flag` and `rework_flag` may reflect bot misclassification, delayed QC discovery, customer-driven rework, or upstream triage—not only the last human touch. Instant Bot_Risk paths make human “correction” look like human failure under queue>70. Service time collapses under batching do not equal efficiency.

**Queue pressure created by management:** Staffing levels, shift overlaps, intake throttling, and incentive systems generate WIP>70. Attributing the 10.9% high-WIP high-complexity error rate to individual carelessness privatizes a system load problem. Day-shift end queue 75 is an operational design outcome.

**Automation quality:** Penalizing humans for correcting Bot_Risk under load blames the safety net. Track bot disagreement/correction rates as system KPIs; improve the model or confidence routing instead of silent human absorption.

**Punitive use of raw late-shift error rates:** Late errors co-move with queue, mix, batching, deferred QC, and experience. Punishment without adjustment drives gaming: avoiding complex cases, postponing event starts, skipping documentation, or suppressing error reporting. That worsens learning data and patient/claimant outcomes. Do not recommend punishment on raw late-shift errors.

**Fairness across shift types:** Night staff face different urgency and volume; Early clears others’ backlog. Cross-shift league tables are misleading without case-mix and role adjustment.

**Worker-level conclusions:** Prefer team-, shift-, and condition-level (WIP×complexity) interventions. Publish risk-adjusted metrics if individual feedback is needed, with transparent controls and right of contextual review. Fatigue is not the only explanation; early cases are more complex; late speed often signals pressure, not mastery.

# 6. Monitoring dashboard specification

Process-mining dashboard (shift + resource + system views), minimum indicators:

1. **Service time:** mean/median/P90 event service_min by activity_family, pos_bin, shift_id, exp_grp (humans only; Bot separate). Trend with complexity-adjusted residuals.

2. **Waiting time:** case and event waiting from `insert_case_service_waiting_time`; waiting for high-priority and high-complexity cases separately; time from arrival to first triage.

3. **WIP / queue:** live and historical `wip_at_start`, `queue_length_at_start`; % time WIP>70; queue at shift start vs end by shift_id; arrival vs finish rates (`insert_case_arrival_finish_rate`).

4. **Rework:** `rework_flag` rate within 7 days; `get_rework_cases_per_activity`; rework loops involving Rework Correction and post–Bot_Risk paths; rework stratified by originating WIP band.

5. **Error rate:** `error_flag` overall and residual after complexity, WIP, activity, shift; error in last two hours vs mid-shift; never sole individual ranking input.

6. **Complexity mix:** mean/P90 `case_complexity_score` and high-priority share by sequence bin, hour, resource; first-hour high-cx share (backlog stress index).

7. **Batching indicators:** output of `discover_batches`; max consecutive same-activity runs per resource; share of Document Review events in runs 9; QC events concentrated in last 45 minutes (burst index); short-service-time cluster rate.

8. **Handover / queue ownership changes:** resource switches per case; ownership changes near shift boundaries; orphaned queue growth at handoff; Early clearance of Night backlog volume.

9. **Worker-load fairness:** distribution of high-cx and high-priority assignments by exp_grp; Gini of complexity load; overtime/sequence depth (76+ share) by resource; junior exposure to WIP>70.

10. **Automation health (recommended 9th/10th):** Bot_Risk volume; human correction rate vs queue_length_at_start; downstream rework after bot paths.

11. **Speed-quality operating point:** scatter or curves of mean service_min vs error_rate by WIP band and cx_band (system “risk zone” tile when high WIP  high cx).

12. **Flow visuals:** dotted chart by `org:resource` and shift; events distribution by hour; performance DFG for triage…close/rework bottlenecks.

**Alert rules (examples):** WIP>70 for >N minutes; last-2h error residual above control limit; QC burst ratio; Bot correction rate spike; unfair high-cx load on juniors; shift-end queue above target without staffing response.

**Interpretation rule on every tile:** pair throughput/service-time gains with error, rework, waiting, and mix—so “faster late shift” cannot be read as success when WIP-driven quality loss or deferred QC is the mechanism.
