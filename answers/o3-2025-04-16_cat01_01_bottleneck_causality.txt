## 1. Executive causal assessment  
Verdict: mainly confounded  
Confidence: 70  

Evidence for:  
• Within every observed complexity stratum Orion’s mean cycle time is 2–3 h shorter than Nova’s (Simpson-type reversal).  
• Orion’s shorter times are visible in both components: queue_wait ( 2 h less) and review_service ( 0.4 h less), despite heavier night-shift coverage and higher compliance share—conditions usually associated with delay.  
• No quality penalty is visible: Orion has 1/14 appeals versus Nova’s 2/16.  

Evidence against:  
• All night-shift, high-load, high-complexity, compliance-required cases are almost exclusively routed to Orion; overlap is sparse, so residual confounding by unobserved_case_risk or reviewer_skill may still inflate Orion’s totals.  
• The sample contains only completed cases; if Nova abandons more long-running files, a survivorship bias could exaggerate its apparent speed.  
• Small cell sizes (<3) limit precision, especially in low-complexity Orion cases.  

Why the 50 % reassignment may succeed, fail, or backfire:  
Because Orion is not intrinsically slower, simple load-balancing could even lengthen average cycle time if Nova inherits Orion’s harder night-load mix without matching its certification depth. Conversely, limited redistribution confined to low-risk segments might improve equity in reviewer utilisation with little performance loss. The plan may also erode Orion’s specialised night expertise, raising rework or appeal rates that appear only after the 30-day window. A cautiously randomised pilot is therefore safer and more informative than a wholesale 50 % shift.

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 – Diagnostic pilot (2 wks) | Randomly route 25 % of upcoming medium-complexity, night cases to Nova and 25 % of day, low-complexity cases to Orion | Isolate resource_group effect from routing confounding | Case | Daily balance check; exclude urgent fraud cases | Cycle_time, queue_wait, review_service, appeal_rate | Provides unconfounded estimate; tests positivity | Abort if cycle_time of experimental arms > 20 % baseline for 3 consecutive days |
| 2 – Queue management tweak | Add floating triage clerk 18:00-23:00 irrespective of group | Reduce queue_wait mediator | Shift | Track queue length; maintain reviewer capacity | Queue_wait distributions before/after | Tests whether wait, not service, drives delay | Rollback if wait reduction < 10 % or cycle_time  |
| 3 – Compliance streamlining | Automate document check for policy-mandated compliance | Shorten compliance-required path | Case segment | Parallel-run automation with manual validation | Time between Classify and Review, compliance error rate | Identifies mediation share of compliance | Halt if automation error > 2 % |

Final recommendation ( 150 words)  
Do not reallocate 50 % of Orion work wholesale. The apparent Orion slowness vanishes—and may even reverse—once complexity and routing conditions are recognised. Instead, run the randomised pilot described above to obtain overlap where the current data are silent. Simultaneously attack the clearly dominant driver—queue_wait—by adding night triage capacity and by streamlining mandatory compliance checks. These actions are likely to deliver faster overall throughput irrespective of which badge (Orion or Nova) the reviewers wear, while generating data that will let us quantify direct skill effects versus process bottlenecks. Roll back any component whose monitored performance deteriorates beyond pre-defined thresholds.

case_id,trace,complexity,group,shift,arrival_load,compliance_required,wait_before_review_h,review_service_h,rework_count,cycle_time_h,outcome_30d

# 1. crude and complexity-stratified means
crude = df.groupby('group')['cycle_time_h'].mean()
print("Crude mean cycle time:\n", crude, "\n")

strat = df.groupby(['complexity','group'])['cycle_time_h'].mean().unstack()
print("Stratified by complexity:\n", strat, "\n")

# 2. complexity-standardised contrast using pooled weights
weights = df['complexity'].value_counts(normalize=True)
std_means = {}
for g in ['Orion','Nova']:
    std_means[g] = sum(df[df['group']==g].groupby('complexity')['cycle_time_h'].mean()*weights)
print("Complexity-standardised means:", std_means, "\n")

# 3. overlap counts
overlap = df.groupby(['complexity','shift','group']).size()
print("Overlap counts:\n", overlap, "\n")
if any(overlap<3):
    warnings.warn("Some cells have <3 cases; interpretation may be unreliable.")

# 4. Directly-follows graphs per group

    log = pm4py.read_csv(StringIO('\n'.join(['case_id,activity']+[

        for act in r.trace.split('>')]))
    )

## 8. Residual uncertainty  
1. Unobserved reviewer identity and skill – add `reviewer_id` to tie performance to individuals.  
2. Actual queue length at arrival to Review – log real-time `queue_position` to separate capacity from routing.  
3. Timestamp of compliance start/end – capture `Compliance_Start`/`Compliance_End` to measure its precise contribution.  
4. Fate of open or abandoned cases – track `case_status` so survival bias can be tested.  
5. Load-sensitive routing parameters – log the engine’s decision rationale (`routing_score`) to model routing_rule explicitly.
