## 1. What is observed

Availability. `automation=1` switches on for Alpha and Beta in April and for Gamma in July; Delta and Epsilon stay at 0 through August. Twelve of forty branch-months are "treated" by the flag.

Actual use. `automated_fast_path_pct` starts at 25-27% in the adoption month and climbs about 7 points per month (Alpha 25→48, Beta 27→50, Gamma 25→32). Non-adopters sit at a 4-6% floor. The flag therefore overstates exposure early and understates it later; intensity is still rising in August, so no branch has reached a steady state.

Selection. Alpha and Beta were chosen for high volume and poor historical cycle times; their Q1 medians (46.9-50.4 h) and p90s (92-101 h) are the worst in the network. Gamma passed a readiness pilot. Delta and Epsilon were not chosen and have the lowest or mid-range baselines. Nothing about rollout was random.

Contemporaneous changes. March: a fraud desk begins for all branches and `escalation_pct` rises about +2 pp everywhere. April onward: routing pushes about +10% arrivals to Alpha/Beta (+5% to Gamma after July); arrivals per FTE in Alpha move from ~31 to ~42 by June. May: a product change lifts `high_complexity_pct` by 5 points in every branch; Alpha loses 2 FTE, Gamma loses 1 in June. Arrivals fall network-wide in July-August.

Measurement. Outcomes are branch-month medians, p90s and percentages; CSAT is a survey mean with unknown response counts and 20-45% response rates; fraud-desk waiting time is unrecorded; `rework_pct` and `escalation_pct` repeat identical values for consecutive months, indicating coarse or periodically refreshed measurement.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PRIMARY: cohort-stacked staggered event study (Callaway-Sant'Anna / Sun-Abraham logic), cohorts April {Alpha, Beta} and July {Gamma}, clean controls only | Relative-time indicators k = -3..+4 per cohort, reference k = -1; never use already-treated units as controls | Never-treated Delta+Epsilon for both cohorts; not-yet-treated Gamma added for the April cohort in April-June | None in main spec; sensitivity spec adds arrivals/FTE and `high_complexity_pct` (with the caveat that load is partly caused by the routing campaign) | E1, E4, E5, E7; E3 from k = 3,4 | Parallel counterfactual trends between adopters and clean controls; no anticipation; no interference through the fraud desk; aggregated medians respond like case-level outcomes | Lead coefficients (k = -3, -2) near zero; Gamma placebo in April-June (Gamma's untreated change should match Delta/Epsilon); cohort-specific estimates agree; leave-one-branch-out; permutation over the 10 possible 3-of-5 adopter sets | Adopters were selected on poor cycle time and received extra load and FTE losses: counterfactual trend differs from controls in unknown direction; with 5 units inference is essentially descriptive |
| ALT 1: comparative interrupted time series per adopter, with control series | Level shift + slope change at adoption month in each adopter's series, differenced against the pooled control series | Delta+Epsilon (and pre-July Gamma) as a common shock series | Month effects taken from controls; optional load and complexity as time-varying regressors | E1, E7 (shape), E4 | Common shocks (fraud desk, May product change, July demand drop) affect adopters and controls alike; pre-period is long enough to fit a trend (it is not: 3 points for Alpha/Beta) | Fit on Gamma Jan-Jun with a fake break in April; check residual autocorrelation; compare level-shift to slope-change fit | Three pre-months cannot separate a level shift from a pre-existing trend or from regression to the mean |
| ALT 2: (augmented) synthetic control per adopter | Binary post indicator per adopter | Donor pool Delta, Epsilon, and Gamma's untreated months for the April cohort | Weights fit on Jan-Mar levels of the five outcomes plus load and complexity | E1, E4 for each adopter separately (case-study effects) | Adopter's counterfactual is a convex combination of donors; donors untreated and unaffected by spillover | Pre-period RMSPE; in-space placebos (treat Delta as if treated); in-time placebo for Gamma | Alpha and Beta lie outside the donor convex hull (worst baselines), so weights extrapolate; 2-3 donors cannot form a credible synthetic unit |
| ALT 3: continuous-treatment (dose-response) panel with branch and month fixed effects | `automated_fast_path_pct` as intensity; also intensity interacted with post | Within-branch variation over months, netting calendar shocks from all branches | Arrivals/FTE, `high_complexity_pct` in a second spec | E2 | Intensity changes only through learning/uptake, not through changes in which cases are eligible; linear dose-response; no reverse causality (managers may push automation harder when queues grow) | Non-monotone within-branch relation (Alpha: intensity rises every month while median first rises, then falls); coefficient stability when load is added; compare step (k = 0) vs slope | Intensity is endogenous to load and staffing; a 25% share and a 50% share select different cases so the "dose" is not one treatment |

Why the primary strategy is preferable. The data have two adoption cohorts, two never-treated branches and one branch that is a clean not-yet-treated control for three months. A cohort-stacked event study uses exactly that structure: each cohort is compared only to units that are untreated in the same calendar months, so calendar shocks that hit everyone (fraud desk in March, product change in May, demand decline in July-August) are differenced out without using already-treated Alpha/Beta as controls for Gamma, which is the forbidden comparison in naive two-way fixed effects. With heterogeneous and still-growing effects, TWFE would mix the April cohort's months 3-4 with Gamma's month 0 and could assign negative weights; the stacked design keeps cohort-specific, event-time-specific contrasts visible so heterogeneity (E8) and dynamics (E7) become outputs rather than nuisances. It also yields natural diagnostics: Gamma's April-June movement is a placebo for the April cohort, and lead coefficients test pre-trends. Synthetic control fails on support (adopters are the worst-baseline units), comparative ITS fails on pre-period length, and the intensity model is a useful complement but cannot be primary because intensity is endogenous. The primary design's weakness is candid: with five clusters, its standard errors are scale indicators, and its credibility rests on the placebo and leave-one-out checks, not on p-values.

Facts: every untreated series (Gamma, Delta, Epsilon) rose by 5.7-6.8 h at the median and 16-18 h at the p90 in April-June; Alpha and Beta rose by 1.5-2.5 h and 10-11.5 h. The three untreated series move together, which is the strongest support the data offer for a common-shock counterfactual. Interpretation: relative to that counterfactual, the April cohort's median is 3.4-5.6 h lower (about 7-12% of baseline) and its p90 is about 6 h lower, but the p90 still rose in absolute terms. The descriptive two-way fixed-effects coefficient on the availability flag is -4.2 h (median), -6.5 h (p90), -2.0 pp (rework), +0.9 pp (escalation), +0.09 (CSAT); leave-one-branch-out ranges are -3.6 to -4.6 h (median) and -5.6 to -7.3 h (p90). These are consistent, but they share the same parallel-trends assumption, so agreement is not independent confirmation.

| Within-branch correlation of share with outcome, post months only | Alpha -0.39, Beta -0.34 | Alpha +0.20, Beta +0.31 | – | – | – |

Facts: the intensity coefficient halves when load and complexity are added; within Alpha and Beta the fast-path share rises monotonically while the median first worsens (May-June) then improves, and the p90 is positively correlated with share. Gamma achieves its largest single-month improvement at only 25% share in its adoption month, and Alpha/Beta show their step in escalation and rework at 25-27% share. Interpretation: the evidence looks more like an adoption-month step (routing rule changes for escalation and rework) plus a slow, load-confounded drift for cycle times than like a clean dose-response. A linear dose-response is not supported; intensity is not a valid instrument for the effect of use.

5.4 Pre-trends and regression to the mean

Pre-period slopes over Jan-Mar (h/month, median / p90): Alpha -0.5 / -1.0; Beta +0.5 / +0.9; Gamma +1.6 / +3.0; Delta +0.9 / +1.7; Epsilon -0.6 / -1.2. Alpha's pre-trend is slightly improving and Gamma's slightly worsening before its July adoption; neither is large relative to the DiD magnitudes, but three points cannot reject a trend of ±1 h/month, which over five months is ±5 h, the same size as the estimated effect. Event-study leads (k = -3, -2 relative to k = -1) are +0.5 and +1.1 h for the median, small and not in the direction that would fabricate a benefit. Regression to the mean: Alpha and Beta were selected on poor historical cycle times. If Q1 were an unusually bad quarter for them, their later improvement would be partly mechanical. Their Q1 months are internally stable (Alpha 46.9-50.4; Beta 45.4-46.4), which argues against an acute selection-on-noise problem, but "historical" selection may reach back before January and we cannot see it. Gamma's alt-window result (-8.5 h) is the most RTM-exposed number: its May-June pre-window is a peak shared by all branches, and controls fell 3.4-5.2 h without treatment; that is why the Jan-Jun window (-3.9 h DiD) is preferred.

5.5 Sensitivity and bounded conclusions

Median cycle time. Point contrasts: -3.4 to -5.6 h (April cohort DiD), -3.9 h (Gamma), -4.2 h (TWFE), -2.8 to -5.9 h (intensity at 45 pp). If adopters' counterfactual rise were only half the controls' (+2.6 h instead of +5.2 h, e.g. because higher-volume branches are less sensitive to a product change), the April-cohort effect shrinks to -0.8 to -3.0 h. If the extra +10% routed volume and Alpha's FTE loss would otherwise have added 2-4 h, the effect of automation alone could be -6 to -9 h. Range carried forward: roughly -1 to -7 h (-2% to -14%), sign most likely negative.

P90 cycle time. Raw within-adopter change +9.7 h (worse); DiD -6.2 to -7.1 h (better than controls); load-adjusted intensity model implies -4.5 h; ratio p90/median widened more in adopters than in controls. Under the same half-counterfactual scenario the DiD turns to about +2 h; under a full-load-attribution scenario it is about -10 h. Range: -10 to +3 h; the sign is not settled and the relative-to-median tail clearly widened.

Escalation. +0.7 to +1.1 pp across every window and specification; the step occurs in the adoption month of all three adopters and in no control month. Range +0.5 to +1.5 pp.

Rework. -1.4 to -2.5 pp relative to controls, but a purely mechanical composition effect (fast-path cases cannot loop) of the observed size is expected if 25-50% of cases bypass the loop-prone stages; the true change in loops per escalated case is unbounded by these data.

CSAT. +0.07 to +0.11 relative to controls; a response-rate shift within the stated 20-45% band could produce differences of this size, so the bounded conclusion is "not distinguishable from zero".

The requested single statement "automation reduces cycle time by X%" is not defensible: it conflates median and tail (which moved in opposite absolute directions), availability and use (intensity still rising), automation and its routing bundle, and rests on three selected adopters and five clusters.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

`expand with an embedded evaluation`

Which branches next. Both remaining branches, but sequentially and not as a plain rollout. Epsilon (38% high-complexity, worst non-adopter p90) resembles Alpha; Delta (23% complexity, best baseline) tests whether benefits exist where headroom is small. Decide the order by a documented coin flip, giving a two-step randomized stepped wedge with a two-month gap.

Assignment mechanism. Inside each newly enabled branch, randomize at case level: eligible cases are routed 50/50 to Automated versus Manual Triage for the first three months, with the seed logged in the event record. This identifies the direct technology effect (E10) free of branch-level confounding, while the stepped branch order identifies the total branch effect including queue interactions (E1). Hold routing campaigns, FTE and escalation thresholds fixed in Delta and Epsilon; log any manager-initiated practice change with a date.

Minimum measurements. Pre-period: reconstruct case-level event logs for the eight observed months plus 2025 for all five branches; these aggregates are insufficient. Post-period: at least three months at 40% or more fast-path share per branch, so mature states are compared rather than learning phases.

Process-mining and queue-state data. Timestamps for Register, Manual/Automated Triage start and end, Specialist Review queue-entry and start, rework loops (count and type), fraud-desk handoff and return, Resolve and Close; risk class and eligibility flag per case; daily specialist and fraud-desk queue lengths and productive FTE; per-path cycle-time and rework distributions so p50 and p90 are computed per stream, not blended.

Spillover monitoring. Weekly fraud-desk waiting time by source branch and escalated-case cycle time in the still-untreated branch; a rise there after the first step is interference and must enter the estimand.

Guardrails. Pause automated routing in a branch if, for two consecutive months, p90 exceeds the control counterfactual by more than 10%, escalated-stream rework rises more than 2 pp, complaints or reopens rise, or CSAT among escalated respondents falls 0.15 with stable response rates.

Decision rule for scale-up. Go to full use when the case-level randomized contrast shows a median reduction of at least 3 h (or 5%), p90 not worse than +5%, no rise in loops per escalated case, and the branch-level stepped estimate has the same sign. If the median improves but the tail or escalated rework worsens, redesign escalation thresholds and specialist capacity before scaling.

## 10. Final claim ladder

- `Observed:` In the adoption month, all three adopters show a step of about +1 pp in `escalation_pct` and about -2 pp in recorded `rework_pct`, while `automated_fast_path_pct` rises from 4-6% to 25-27% and then climbs roughly 7 points per month; no such steps appear in Delta or Epsilon.
- `Suggestive:` Relative to the never-treated and not-yet-treated branches, which deteriorated by about 5 h at the median and 16 h at the p90 in April-June, adopters' medians were 3.4-5.6 h lower and their p90s about 6 h lower than the parallel-trends counterfactual, despite 19-29% more arrivals per FTE; this is consistent with a modest cycle-time benefit of the automation-plus-routing bundle but rests on three pre-months, three selected adopters, and an untested fraud-desk spillover.
- `Conditionally causal:` If parallel trends hold and the fraud desk did not slow untreated branches, availability of automation in a weak-performing branch reduced median cycle time by roughly 1-7 h and raised escalation by 0.5-1.5 pp over the first five months; the effect on p90 is not identified in sign (range -10 to +3 h), and the recorded rework decline is largely a composition effect of fast-path cases that cannot loop.
- `Not supported:` A single statement that "automation reduces cycle time by X%" for the network, a transportable effect for Delta or Epsilon, any customer-satisfaction effect (survey composition unknown), any dose-response return to deeper fast-path use, or any claim that the slowest 10% of cases were helped.
