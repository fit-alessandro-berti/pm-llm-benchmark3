# 1. Data audit and estimands

### Queueing quantities, units, and opening-hours convention
The service center operates on a **12-hour active schedule (07:00–19:00, or 720 opening minutes/day)** across 28 consecutive days (`2026-04-06` through `2026-05-03`, 336 opening hours total). Because cases can carry over overnight while no service occurs between 19:00 and 07:00, every time-based metric must distinguish **active opening hours** ($T_{\text{open}} = 12\text{ h/day}$) from **24-hour wall-clock time** ($T_{\text{wall}} = 24\text{ h/day}$).

| Quantity | Theoretical Estimand & Units | Supplied Proxy | Audit Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arrival intensity $\lambda(t)$ | Instantaneous/mean entry rate $[\text{cases / open-hr}]$ | `arrivals` ($A_d$, $[\text{cases/day}]$) and 4 hourly vectors | Daily counts hide intraday bursts; 19:00–07:00 digital arrivals (if any wait until 07:00) are not separated. |
| Effective throughput $X(t)$ | Departure rate of served cases $[\text{cases / open-hr}]$ | `completed` ($C_d$, $[\text{cases/day}]$) and 4 hourly vectors | Mixes same-day and carry-over cohorts; omits unlogged exits (abandonments, cancellations, transfers). |
| Active service time $S$ | Mean $\mathbb{E}[S]$ and variance $\text{Var}(S)$ $[\text{active min/case}]$ | `median_service_min` ($s_{50}$) and `p90_service_min` ($s_{90}$) | Excludes waiting; $\mathbb{E}[S]$ and $\text{Var}(S)$ are unreported; unclear if recorded at arrival cohort or completion cohort. |
| Flow (sojourn) time $W$ | Mean time in system $\mathbb{E}[W_q + S_{\text{in-scope}}]$ $[\text{open-hr}]$ | *None directly supplied* (must be bounded/inferred where valid) | Never equal to $s_{50}$; overnight carry-over adds 12 wall-clock hours of zero-service storage per night. |
| System WIP $L(t)$ | Time-average cases in system $[\text{cases}]$ | `mean_wip` ($L_d$, 15-min snapshots) | Snapshot window (12h vs 24h) and state scope (whether paused/deferred cases are included) are unstated. |
| Server capacity $c(t)$ | Concurrent active servers $[\text{FTE}]$ or $[\text{staff-min/day}]$ | `staff_fte` ($c_d$; gross $720 c_d\text{ min/day}$) | Averages out shift overlaps, breaks, and exact transition hours (e.g., `2026-04-29`). |
| Offered utilization $\rho$ | Dimensionless traffic intensity $\lambda \mathbb{E}[S] / c$ | Bounded via $(A_d, s_{50}, s_{90}, c_d, \text{outage\_min})$ | Requires distributional bounds for $\mathbb{E}[S]$ and assumes single-tasking concurrency per FTE. |

### Explicit modeling assumptions
* **Assumption A1 (Snapshot window):** `mean_wip` averages the 48 (or 49 boundary-inclusive) 15-minute snapshots taken during opening hours (07:00–19:00). *Why needed:* Aligns $L_d$ with opening-hour rates $\lambda_d = A_d / 12$. (If 24-hour snapshots were used instead, constant overnight carry-over $B_{\text{night}}$ would weight $L_d$ as $0.5 L_{\text{open}} + 0.5 B_{\text{night}}$.)
* **Assumption A2 (Service-time bounds):** Active service time $S > 0$ is right-skewed with $\mathbb{E}[S] \ge s_{50}$. To avoid point-estimate hubris, we pair the **exact distribution-free lower bound** $\mathbb{E}[S] \ge 0.4 s_{50} + 0.1 s_{90}$ (from $S \ge 0$ below $p_{50}$, $S \ge s_{50}$ on $[p_{50}, p_{90})$, $S \ge s_{90}$ above $p_{90}$) and the **skewness floor** $\mathbb{E}[S] \ge s_{50}$ with a **lognormal point proxy** $\widehat{\mathbb{E}[S]}_{\text{LN}} = s_{50} \exp\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}[\ln(s_{90}/s_{50})/1.2816]^2\right)$, checked against the **single-tasking completion ceiling** $S_{\max,\text{cap}} = (720 - \text{outage\_min}) \cdot c_d / C_d$. *Why needed:* Queueing load $\rho$ depends on $\mathbb{E}[S]$, not $s_{50}$.
* **Assumption A3 (Single-channel active concurrency):** One `staff_fte` provides at most 60 active handling minutes per opening hour without parallel multitasking. *Why needed:* Converts `staff_fte` into a capacity ceiling in staff-minutes.

### Seven empirical threats to naive inference (Data Audit)
1. **Flow-conservation contradiction (unlogged exits or filtered WIP scope):** **Observation:** Across all 28 days, $\sum A_d = 2{,}519$ and $\sum C_d = 2{,}409$, yielding a cumulative net excess $\sum(A_d - C_d) = +110$ cases, peaking at **$+128$ cases** on `2026-04-28`. Under lossless closed-queue conservation ($B_t = B_0 + \sum(A - C)$ with $B_0 \ge 0$), WIP on Sunday `2026-04-26` would have to exceed $96\text{–}100$ cases all day; yet observed `mean_wip` is **20** (dropping from **43** on Friday `2026-04-24` to **23** on Saturday, then rebounding to **49** on Monday `2026-04-27`). Similarly, `mean_wip` on `2026-05-03` is **19** despite cumulative $A - C = +110$. **Hypotheses:** (**H1a**) Significant unlogged exits occur (customer abandonment/reneging, overnight expiration, or transfer out of queue during high congestion), and/or (**H1b**) `mean_wip` snapshots exclude cases parked in a weekday-only specialist/complex hold state over weekends. Naive cumulative backlog summation is therefore invalid.
2. **Internal discrepancy between `peak_08_10_pct` and hourly vectors:** **Observation:** While the 4 representative `arrivals_by_hour` and `completions_by_hour` vectors sum *exactly* to daily `arrivals` and `completed` ($101/99$, $105/58$, $115/101$, $51/43$), the share of arrivals in bins `08-09` and `09-10` (indices 1–2, representing 08:00–09:59) never matches `peak_08_10_pct`:
   * `2026-04-07`: $(5+12)/101 = \mathbf{16.8\%}$ vs reported **`31%`** (note bins `09-11` give $32/101 = 31.7\%$).
   * `2026-04-15`: $(6+13)/105 = \mathbf{18.1\%}$ vs reported **`32%`** (bins `09-11` give $35/105 = 33.3\%$).
   * `2026-04-21`: $(3+8)/115 = \mathbf{9.6\%}$ vs reported **`47%`** (actual spikes occur in `10-11` [34], `13-14` [24], and `16-17` [14]; even shifted bins `09-11` only reach $36.5\%$).
   * `2026-04-25`: $(2+4)/51 = \mathbf{11.8\%}$ vs reported **`22%`** (bins `09-11` give $11/51 = 21.6\%$).
   **Hypothesis (H2):** Either `peak_08_10_pct` suffers from an ETL timezone/window-indexing bug, or it measures upstream portal submission time whereas `arrivals_by_hour` logs downstream queue release (consistent with batch releases at 10:00/13:00/16:00 on `2026-04-21`). Consequently, `peak_08_10_pct` cannot be taken at face value as 08:00–09:59 queue arrivals.
3. **Quantile-only service times and completion-selection bias:** On overload days (e.g., `2026-04-22`: $C=103$, $c=6.0 \implies 4{,}320$ gross staff-min), the lognormal mean proxy $\widehat{\mathbb{E}[S]}_{\text{LN}} = 45.4\text{ min}$ exceeds the single-tasking completion ceiling $4{,}320/103 = 41.9\text{ min}$. Either the upper tail above $p_{90}$ is lighter than lognormal (e.g., a bounded two-type mixture), active handling spans multiple days, or faster/simpler cases preferentially complete while complex cases stall in WIP (**survivor/completion bias**). Conversely, during outage recovery (`2026-04-16`), $s_{50}$ rises to $33\text{ min}$ and $s_{90}$ to $72\text{ min}$ *before* `complex_pct` jumps, reflecting rework/restart overhead on stale backlog.
4. **Endogenous staffing confounds capacity inference:** `staff_fte` increases specifically in response to crises (`7.0` and `6.5` on `2026-04-16`–`17` post-outage; `7.0`, `6.5`, and `3.0` starting `2026-04-29`). Treating `staff_fte` as exogenous creates reverse-causality bias.
5. **Unlogged sub-daily intervention boundary:** The staffing intervention began *during* `2026-04-29` at an unknown hour, making `2026-04-29` a censored transition day rather than a pure post-intervention observation.
6. **Construct ambiguity of `complex_pct`:** The jump from $18\text{–}27\%$ (`04-06`–`04-19`) to $39\text{–}47\%$ (`04-20`–`05-03`) may combine a true case-mix shock with a taxonomy/coding change. However, concurrent jumps in $s_{50}$ ($+9\text{ min}$), $s_{90}$ ($+34\text{ min}$), `mean_wip` ($+20\text{ cases}$), and intraday batching confirm a genuine operational regime change rather than a pure relabeling artifact.
7. **Missing 07:00 initial backlog and serial dependence:** Daily rows are coupled by overnight carry-over, 7-day calendar cycles, and multi-day congestion waves, violating independence assumptions in standard regressions or confidence intervals.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

### Operational story: Congestion-State & Pooling-Scale Segmentation
Whereas the Primary Segmentation is anchored in *external calendar epochs and historical triggers*, a materially different queueing interpretation classifies operating days by **internal server congestion (`WIP / staff_fte`), server-pool scale (`staff_fte`), and net queue drift (`arrivals - completed`)**, supplemented by an intraday peak/off-peak lens:

1. **Regime A1 — Multi-Server Subcritical Equilibrium (`staff_fte` $\in [5.5, 6.0]$, `WIP/FTE` $< 4.0$, $|A-C| \le 3$):** `2026-04-06`–`04-10`, `04-13`–`04-14` ($n=7$). Servers have ~32–35% daily slack to absorb midday arrival waves within the same shift.
2. **Regime A2 — Small-Pool De-Multiplexed Skeleton Shifts (`staff_fte` $\in [2.5, 3.0]$):** *All 8 weekend days* (`04-11`–`12`, `04-18`–`19`, `04-25`–`26`, `05-02`–`03`). **Operational story:** In a $G/G/c$ queue, reducing $c$ from $6.0$ to $2.5$ destroys statistical multiplexing: a single 80-minute complex case ties up **40% of total center capacity**! Hence *every* weekend—before and after the mix shift, and even after the 3.0-FTE intervention—operates at high queue-per-server pressure (`WIP/FTE` $= 4.8\text{–}9.2$, median $6.5$) and elevated service medians ($28\text{–}36\text{ min}$).
3. **Regime A3 — Supply-Shocked / Saturated Queue Growth ($A - C \ge +10$, `WIP/FTE` $\ge 6.0$):** `2026-04-15` (outage day, $+47$) grouped with `2026-04-20`–`24` and `04-27`–`28` ($n=8$ total). **Operational story:** Regardless of whether capacity deficit stems from a 180-minute server halt (`04-15`) or uncompensated service-time inflation + arrival bursts (`04-20`+), offered work exceeds effective daily capacity ($\rho_{\text{eff}} > 1$), driving rapid queue accumulation and suspected reneging.
4. **Regime A4 — Surge-Staffed Backlog Liquidation (`staff_fte` $\ge 6.5$, $C - A \ge +5$, falling WIP):** Groups `2026-04-16`–`17` *and* `2026-04-29`–`05-01` into a single functional recovery regime ($n=5$). **Operational story:** Whenever management deploys 6.5–7.0 FTE into a deep backlog (`WIP` $\ge 32$), servers operate in continuous *saturation-drain mode* (zero starved-server idle time), achieving 112–129 completions/day across *both* Week 2 and Week 4!
