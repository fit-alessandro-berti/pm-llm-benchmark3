## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 | +S1 (early), +C1 (reserved) | Compliance | Assessment (if C1 preempted) | Other compliance cases | Negative (delays others) | C1 is a bottleneck; reservation crowds out urgent cases |
| K-219 | +D2, -D1 | Document | Assessment (if D2 rework) | Cases waiting for D1 | Mixed (local gain, global risk) | D2 rework probability for handwriting |
| K-337 | -External rework | Assessment, Compliance | None | None | Positive | Decision quality risk (appeals) |
| K-411 | +D2 (parallel) | Identity, Document | Assessment | Cases in Assessment queue | Negative (congestion) | Assessment queue saturation |

**100-case weekly rollout analysis:**
Reserving C1 capacity (K-104) for 100 high-risk cases would severely strain the single compliance resource, delaying other urgent cases by ~20–40h each. Substituting D2 for D1 (K-219) in 100 standard cases could reduce Document Check time by 2–4h per case but risks 10–20% rework (adding 8–14h), potentially increasing total workload. Suppressing rework loops (K-337) in 100 high-risk cases could save ~2–3 days per case but may increase appeal rates by 5–10%, with compliance defensibility concerns. Parallel verification (K-411) for 100 digital cases could save 2–4h each but flood the Assessment queue (A1/A2 capacity: ~12 cases/day), causing spillover delays. Weekend effects (K-337) and external responses (K-219/K-337) introduce variability. Timestamp workarounds (K-411) may create audit issues if not logged properly. The most scalable intervention is K-337 (if decision policy change is acceptable), while K-104 and K-411 risk bottleneck migration.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is **K-337** (suppress second rework loop), as it directly addresses the largest time sink (external delays) while explicitly acknowledging the decision policy trade-off. Its effects are predictable, and the risk (appeals) can be monitored. The most dangerous intervention is **K-104** (early compliance reservation), as its local benefits for one case create systemic delays for others due to C1’s scarcity. The least transportable counterfactual is **K-411** (parallel verification): while it works for individual digital cases, scaling it to 100 cases would flood the Assessment queue, turning a local optimization into a system-wide bottleneck. Additional valuable data include **real-time queue lengths for C1 and A1/A2**, **D2’s rework probability for handwritten documents**, and **external response time distributions** for supplier interactions. These would refine estimates of interference and nonlinearities in the system.
