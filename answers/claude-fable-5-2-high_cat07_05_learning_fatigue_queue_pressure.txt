# 2. Integrated explanation

The aggregate curve "service time falls all shift, errors rise at the end" is a blend of at least two different regimes plus several composition effects. No single mechanism reproduces all the tables.

**Regime 1: positions 1–50 (roughly the first 4–5 hours).** Service time falls from 18.8 to 12.9 min and errors fall or stay flat (3.4% → 2.5–2.7%), rework likewise (6.1% → 4.8–5.0%). Speed and quality improve together. The dominant cause is case mix: shifts open with carried-over backlog that is more complex (0.72) and more urgent (41% high priority), and seniors deliberately pick those cases first. Given that high complexity adds about 8–10 minutes and doubles error rates at any WIP level, the fall in complexity to 0.50 plausibly accounts for most of the early speed-up. A genuine warm-up component is likely on top (re-orientation, reading handover notes, opening systems), and the junior error pattern (5.1% early, 4.4% later under low WIP) is consistent with juniors struggling with hard early cases. But the near-identical proportional speed-up across experience groups (26–30%) says this is mostly a shared, exogenous effect, not within-shift skill learning. The label "apparent learning" in the 16–30 bin should stay "apparent" until complexity is adjusted.

**Regime 2: positions 51+ (roughly the last 2–3 hours).** Service time keeps falling (12.9 → 10.8 min), but complexity barely moves (0.50 → 0.43) while the queue doubles and then nearly triples (31 → 62 → 88). Errors rise 2.7% → 7.9% and rework 5.0% → 13.7% on the easiest, lowest-priority cases of the day. Case mix predicts the opposite, and learning would not raise errors. This is the signature of a speed-accuracy trade-off under queue pressure: people shorten checks, skim documents, accept Bot_Risk output unverified and batch work. The WIP table confirms the trade-off inside each complexity band (about 25% faster, about 2.7× more errors). As a rough consistency check, the high-WIP cells of that table weighted by volume give about 7% errors, close to the 7.9% seen in the 76+ bin, leaving only a small residual for other late-shift mechanisms.

**Why errors rise only in some conditions.** Late in the shift under low WIP, error rates are essentially the same as in the first 20 events for every resource group. Errors rise only where WIP is high, and the rise is graded by experience: seniors 2.2% → 4.9%, mid-level 2.9% → 6.8%, night staff 3.7% → 8.2%, juniors 4.4% → 12.4%. The shift table says the same thing from another angle: the Early shift is as long as the others but ends with a shrinking queue (61 → 42) and only 4.2% errors, while the Day shift ends with a growing queue (38 → 75) and 7.5%. So time on task alone is a poor predictor; time on task combined with high queue is a strong one. Faster work is therefore not the same phenomenon in the two regimes: early it reflects easier cases and warm-up at no quality cost, late it reflects pressure and is paid for in errors and rework.

**What fatigue and circadian effects still contribute.** Fatigue is not ruled out. The "later events" columns pool everything after event 20 rather than isolating the final hours, early raw error rates are inflated by complexity, and tired workers may be precisely the ones who cope worst with pressure. A fatigue × pressure interaction would look like the observed pattern. But the data do not support fatigue as the main or sole cause. Circadian effects are plausible for the Night shift (slowest late service time, 15.2 min) and for the afternoon dip, but night work is confounded with different people, fewer and more urgent cases, and the day peak is confounded with demand. These effects are unidentified, not absent.

**Composition and measurement effects that distort the curve.**
- *Survivorship in the position bins.* Only high-volume workers on busy shifts reach position 76+. Night staff and slow, complex-case handlers are under-represented, so the last bin compares different people and shifts, not the same people later.
- *Activity mix.* Late positions contain more Quality Check, Close Case, Rework Correction and batched Document Review events, which are inherently short. Part of the late "speed-up" is a change in what is being done.
- *Detection timing.* Because Quality Check is pushed to the end of the shift, errors made earlier are discovered in the last 45 minutes. Depending on how `error_flag` is stamped (where the error was made versus where it was found), the late-shift error peak is partly a detection artefact.
- *Automation.* Some late errors originate with Bot_Risk and are only attributed to the human who later touched or corrected the case.
- *Service-time validity.* Taken at face value, the bin means imply about 700 minutes of cumulative service time by event 50, which is reached at roughly 300 minutes into the shift. Recorded durations therefore overlap: they include interleaving, holding cases open or waiting (for example during Customer Callback). A drop in recorded service time under pressure may partly reflect a switch from interleaved to serial or batch handling, not less effort per case.
- *Feedback loop.* Errors generate Rework Correction work, which adds to WIP in the same or the next shift. Late and Early shifts inherit that backlog as "complex carryover", which restarts the cycle.

In summary: early in the shift, case-mix and selection dominate, with some warm-up. Late in the shift, queue pressure dominates, amplified by inexperience, batching, delayed quality checks and unverified automation, with fatigue and circadian effects as possible but so far unidentified modifiers.

# 3. Proposed analyses

**Design principles**

- **Within-resource comparisons.** Use resource fixed effects (or random intercepts) so each worker is compared with themselves across positions, WIP levels and shifts. This removes stable skill differences and most of the survivorship problem in the 76+ bin.
- **Within-shift position effects.** Model position flexibly (bins or splines), separately from `minutes_since_shift_start`, and check for saturation (warm-up) versus late divergence (fatigue). Re-estimate on resource-shifts that have at least 76 events to hold composition constant.
- **WIP/queue stratification.** Estimate every effect within low, medium and high WIP, and specifically on the off-diagonal cells: early and high WIP (Late shift start, post-weekend backlog) and late and low WIP (end of Early shift). These cells separate pressure from time on task.
- **Complexity adjustment.** Use the continuous `case_complexity_score`, priority and activity as covariates, and report directly standardised position curves at a fixed case mix. Check the reliability of the score itself.
- **Shift and time-of-day controls.** Add clock-hour, weekday and shift-type effects. Use the 09:00–14:00 and 14:00–17:00 overlap windows, where workers at hour 1–3 and hour 6–8 of their shift face the same queue at the same clock time.
- **Batching detection.** Flag batched events and estimate their effect on service time and downstream rework; rerun all models without them.
- **Resource-experience interactions.** Test WIP × experience group and complexity × experience group (and `resource_experience_months` as a continuous moderator) to see who needs protection under pressure.
- **Rework/error downstream analysis.** Trace each rework or error back to the originating event and its conditions (WIP, position, batch, bot involvement), and measure detection lag and rework cost, so the throughput gain under pressure can be netted against rework minutes.

**PM4Py-oriented code**

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
import statsmodels.formula.api as smf

#-- 1. Load and format ------------------------------------------------------
raw = pd.read_csv("claims_events.csv")
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    raw,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
)
df["shift_start_time"] = pd.to_datetime(df["shift_start_time"], utc=True)

#-- 2. Event-level features -------------------------------------------------
df["svc_min"] = (df["time:timestamp"] - df["start_timestamp"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60
df["clock_hour"] = df["start_timestamp"].dt.hour
df["weekday"] = df["start_timestamp"].dt.dayofweek
df["shift_type"] = df["shift_start_time"].dt.hour.map(
    {6: "Early", 9: "Day", 14: "Late", 22: "Night"})
df["shift_key"] = df["org:resource"] + "|" + df["shift_id"].astype(str)
df["pos_bin"] = pd.cut(df["sequence_position_within_shift"],
                       [0, 5, 15, 30, 50, 75, np.inf],
                       labels=["1-5", "6-15", "16-30", "31-50", "51-75", "76+"])
df["wip_band"] = pd.cut(df["wip_at_start"], [-1, 30, 70, np.inf],
                        labels=["low", "medium", "high"]).astype(str)
df["cx_band"] = np.where(df["case_complexity_score"] >= 0.6, "high", "low")
df["exp_group"] = df["org:resource"].str.split("_").str[0]
df["is_bot"] = df["org:resource"].eq("Bot_Risk")

df = df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "start_timestamp"])
prev_end = df.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].shift()
df["wait_min"] = (df["start_timestamp"] - prev_end).dt.total_seconds() / 60
prev_res = df.groupby("case:concept:name")["org:resource"].shift()
df["handover"] = prev_res.notna() & (prev_res != df["org:resource"])

#-- 3. Case-level service/waiting time and load -----------------------------
df = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df, start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")
df = pm4py.insert_case_arrival_finish_rate(df, start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")
hourly = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
    df, grouper_freq="h", start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")
svc_by_activity = pm4py.get_service_time(
    df, aggregation_measure="median", start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")

human = df[~df["is_bot"]].copy()

#-- 4. Descriptive checks ---------------------------------------------------
pm4py.save_vis_events_distribution_graph(df, "events_by_hour.png", distr_type="hours")
pm4py.save_vis_events_distribution_graph(df, "events_by_weekday.png", distr_type="days_week")
pm4py.save_vis_dotted_chart(
    human, "dotted_resource.png",
    attributes=["time:timestamp", "org:resource", "concept:name"])

keys = ["pos_bin", "wip_band", "cx_band"]
strat = human.groupby(keys, observed=True).agg(
    n=("svc_min", "size"), svc=("svc_min", "median"),
    err=("error_flag", "mean"), rework=("rework_flag", "mean"))

std_mix = human.groupby(["cx_band", "priority", "concept:name"]).size()
std_mix = std_mix / std_mix.sum()
cell = human.groupby(["pos_bin", "cx_band", "priority", "concept:name"], observed=True)[
    ["svc_min", "error_flag"]].mean()
standardised = cell.mul(std_mix, axis=0).groupby("pos_bin", observed=True).sum()

who_reaches = human.groupby("pos_bin", observed=True).agg(
    resources=("org:resource", "nunique"),
    share_day=("shift_type", lambda s: (s == "Day").mean()),
    share_junior=("exp_group", lambda s: (s == "Junior").mean()))

per_shift = human.groupby("shift_key").agg(
    recorded=("svc_min", "sum"),
    first=("start_timestamp", "min"), last=("time:timestamp", "max"))
per_shift["overlap_ratio"] = per_shift["recorded"] / (
    (per_shift["last"] - per_shift["first"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60)

#-- 5. Batching -------------------------------------------------------------
batches = pm4py.discover_batches(
    human, merge_distance=15 * 60, min_batch_size=5,
    start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")
for (activity, resource), n_batches, batch_types in batches[:20]:
    print(activity, resource, n_batches, {k: len(v) for k, v in batch_types.items()})

human = human.sort_values(["shift_key", "start_timestamp"])
new_run = human["concept:name"] != human.groupby("shift_key")["concept:name"].shift()
human["run_id"] = new_run.groupby(human["shift_key"]).cumsum()
human["run_len"] = human.groupby(["shift_key", "run_id"])["concept:name"].transform("size")
p10 = human.groupby("concept:name")["svc_min"].transform(lambda s: s.quantile(0.10))
human["batched"] = (human["run_len"] >= 9) & (human["svc_min"] <= p10)
human["eos_qc_burst"] = (
    human["concept:name"].isin(["Quality Check", "Rework Correction"])
    & (human["minutes_since_shift_start"] >= 435))

#-- 6. Models: within-resource, position, WIP, complexity, clock ------------
m = human[human["svc_min"] > 0].copy()
m["log_svc"] = np.log(m["svc_min"])
m["wip10"] = m["wip_at_start"] / 10
m["pos10"] = m["sequence_position_within_shift"] / 10
m["early"] = (m["sequence_position_within_shift"] <= 20).astype(int)
groups = m["shift_key"].astype("category").cat.codes

svc_model = smf.ols(
    "log_svc ~ early + pos10 + wip10 + case_complexity_score + C(priority)"
    " + C(Q('concept:name')) + C(clock_hour) + C(weekday) + batched"
    " + C(Q('org:resource'))",
    data=m).fit(cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": groups})

err_model = smf.logit(
    "error_flag ~ early + pos10 + C(wip_band, Treatment('low')) * C(exp_group)"
    " + C(wip_band, Treatment('low')) * case_complexity_score"
    " + pos10:C(wip_band, Treatment('low'))"
    " + C(priority) + C(Q('concept:name')) + C(clock_hour) + C(shift_type) + batched",
    data=m).fit(cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": groups})

overlap = m[m["clock_hour"].between(14, 16) & m["shift_type"].isin(["Day", "Late"])]
overlap_model = smf.logit(
    "error_flag ~ C(shift_type) + wip10 + case_complexity_score"
    " + C(Q('concept:name')) + C(clock_hour)",
    data=overlap).fit()

#-- 7. Downstream rework and automation -------------------------------------
rework_per_activity = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(df)
qc_bursts = pm4py.filter_paths_performance(
    df, ("Quality Check", "Rework Correction"), 0, 45 * 60)
slow_cases = pm4py.filter_case_performance(df, 7 * 86400, 365 * 86400)

high_wip_cases = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df, "wip_band", ["high"], level="case", retain=True)
low_wip_cases = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df, "wip_band", ["high"], level="case", retain=False)
for name, sub in [("high_wip", high_wip_cases), ("low_wip", low_wip_cases)]:
    perf_dfg, sa, ea = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(sub)
    pm4py.save_vis_performance_dfg(perf_dfg, sa, ea, f"perf_dfg_{name}.png")

bot_cases = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df, "org:resource", ["Bot_Risk"], level="case", retain=True)
bot_ev = bot_cases[bot_cases["is_bot"]][
    ["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", "queue_length_at_start",
     "case_complexity_score"]].rename(columns={"time:timestamp": "bot_ts"})
fix = bot_cases[(~bot_cases["is_bot"])
                & bot_cases["concept:name"].isin(["Risk Classification", "Rework Correction"])]
fix = fix.groupby("case:concept:name")["start_timestamp"].min().rename("fix_ts")
bot_ev = bot_ev.join(fix, on="case:concept:name")
bot_ev["corrected"] = bot_ev["fix_ts"] > bot_ev["bot_ts"]
bot_ev["fix_lag_h"] = (bot_ev["fix_ts"] - bot_ev["bot_ts"]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
bot_by_queue = bot_ev.groupby(bot_ev["queue_length_at_start"] > 70).agg(
    corrected=("corrected", "mean"), lag=("fix_lag_h", "median"),
    complexity=("case_complexity_score", "mean"))

#-- 8. Before/after windows and case-mix clusters ---------------------------
before = pm4py.filter_time_range(df, "2026-01-01 00:00:00", "2026-03-31 23:59:59", mode="events")
after = pm4py.filter_time_range(df, "2026-04-01 00:00:00", "2026-06-30 23:59:59", mode="events")
for i, cluster in enumerate(pm4py.cluster_log(df)):
    print(i, cluster["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
          cluster["case_complexity_score"].mean(), cluster["error_flag"].mean())
```

**How to read the results**

| Result | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| `early` coefficient stays large after complexity, activity and WIP controls, and is larger for juniors | Genuine warm-up |
| `early` shrinks towards zero after adjustment | The early slowness was case mix and selection |
| `pos10` positive for errors within the low-WIP band and at fixed clock hour | Time-on-task fatigue |
| `pos10:wip_band[high]` positive | Fatigue amplifies pressure (interaction, not a main effect) |
| WIP terms remain strong within resource, complexity and position | Queue pressure |
| In the 14:00–17:00 overlap, Day workers (hour 6–8) do no worse than Late workers (hour 1–3) at equal WIP | Late-shift errors are driven by queue, not by time on task or clock |
| Clock-hour effects persist across shift types | Circadian component |
| `batched` lowers service time and raises 7-day rework | Batching is a risky workaround, not just efficient organisation |
| Bot correction rate at queue > 70 stays higher after complexity matching, with longer fix lag | Verification is being skipped or delayed under pressure |
| `overlap_ratio` well above 1 | Recorded service time includes interleaving; treat raw durations with caution |

Further checks: sensitivity to re-attributing errors to the originating event instead of the detecting event; a net-throughput calculation (minutes saved per event under high WIP versus added rework minutes per event); and lagged-WIP or arrival-surge instruments, because rework raises WIP and makes it partly an outcome.

Seniors are the most pressure-resistant group (4.9% under high WIP) and can absorb complex cases and bot corrections. Also lowers the backlog passed to the Late shift. | Cost. Removing a senior from early complex work elsewhere. The reviewer can become a bottleneck or just add inspection without relieving flow. | Day-shift end queue; minutes with WIP > 70; junior and mid-level error rate under high WIP; Bot_Risk correction lag; senior load; Late-shift starting queue. | Promising; test for a defined period with before/after comparison adjusted for arrivals and case mix. |
| 8 | Penalise workers with higher late-shift error rates | None that survives adjustment; at best a weak deterrent. | Raw late errors reflect queue, assignment, detection timing, batching norms and bot output, not individual care. Penalties encourage cherry-picking easy cases, hiding or not logging errors, avoiding quality checks and slowing down, which raises queues. Juniors and high-volume workers are hit hardest. | If any individual indicator is used, it must be adjusted (complexity, WIP, activity, position, bot origin) with confidence intervals; watch for drops in logged errors without drops in 7-day rework, and for shifts in case selection. | Reject. Use adjusted, system-level indicators for coaching, staffing and training instead. |

Recommended combination: 3 and 7 as the core, a modified 6 (earlier and continuous quality checks, never fewer), a pilot of 5, a small randomised slice from 4, targeted routing instead of 2, and no action on 1 and 8 without new evidence.

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

- **Selection bias.** Workers do not receive comparable cases. Seniors take complex backlog early; night staff handle urgent exceptions; juniors get batched Document Review. Raw service times and error rates therefore rank assignment patterns, not people. Only workers with many events reach the late position bins, so late-shift figures over-represent high-volume workers and busy shifts.
- **Measurement error.** `error_flag` depends on when and whether a Quality Check happens. End-of-shift bursts make errors appear late and may attribute them to the detecting or last-touching resource. Rework within 7 days is consistently about 1.8 times the error rate, so the flag misses part of the quality problem. Recorded service time includes interleaving and waiting, and batch logging can compress durations. The complexity score may be imprecise or set after the fact. Small samples per worker and condition produce wide uncertainty.
- **Queue pressure created by management.** WIP at the end of the Day shift is a consequence of staffing, shift overlap, intake scheduling, targets and inherited backlog. Individuals do not choose it. Holding workers accountable for errors made in conditions the organisation created shifts responsibility from system design to the people with least control.
- **Automation quality.** Bot_Risk errors and unverified bot output enter human statistics as corrections or downstream errors. More corrections under high queue may indicate a weaker bot on peak-time cases, not careless staff. Bot accuracy needs its own audit by case type and load, and bot-originated errors should be reported separately.
- **Punitive use of error rates.** Punishment based on raw late-shift error rates is unjustified by this evidence and likely harmful: it suppresses error reporting, discourages taking hard cases, weakens quality checks and damages trust in the data, which makes every later analysis worse. Group differences (for example juniors at 12.4% under high WIP) call for support, routing and staffing changes.
- **Further cautions.** Aggregate patterns do not license conclusions about any individual (ecological fallacy). Resource-level analysis should be minimised, aggregated or pseudonymised where possible, shared with staff and their representatives, and comply with applicable data-protection and co-determination rules. Any single indicator that becomes a target will be gamed, so speed, quality and waiting indicators must be read together. All findings here are observational and based on coarse tables; they should be treated as hypotheses until the adjusted analyses and pilots confirm them.

# 6. Monitoring dashboard specification

**Global filters:** date range, shift type, activity and `activity_family`, resource group (individual view restricted and adjusted), complexity band, priority, WIP band, bot involvement. Every indicator is shown both raw and case-mix adjusted where applicable.

| # | Indicator | Definition from the event log | Breakdown / visual | Signal to watch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Service time | Median and 90th percentile of `time:timestamp - start_timestamp` per activity; complexity-adjusted version (observed versus expected). | By position bin, WIP band, shift, experience group; line chart. | Sharp drop under high WIP without a change in mix. |
| 2 | Waiting time | Start of event minus completion of the previous event in the case; case-level waiting from `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time`. | By priority and complexity; performance DFG. | Rising wait for high-priority or complex cases. |
| 3 | WIP and queue | `wip_at_start`, `queue_length_at_start`; minutes per shift above 50 and above 70; arrival and finish rates. | Intraday time series per shift; heatmap hour × weekday. | Time above 70; queue growth within the Day shift. |
| 4 | Error rate | Share of events with `error_flag`, attributed to the originating event; adjusted for complexity, activity and WIP. | Position × WIP heatmap; by experience group. | Errors above baseline at equal case mix; the low-WIP late-shift cell as the fatigue indicator. |
| 5 | Rework | `rework_flag` within 7 days; share of cases with Rework Correction; rework minutes per 100 events; rework per activity. | By originating activity, WIP band at origin, batch status. | Rework rising faster than logged errors (under-detection). |
| 6 | Complexity and priority mix | Mean `case_complexity_score`, share high-complexity, share high-priority. | By position bin, hour, shift, resource group; stacked bars. | Mix shifts that explain speed changes; complex cases started at high WIP. |

| 8 | Quality Check timing and coverage | Lag from activity completion to Quality Check; share of Quality Checks in the last 45 minutes of a shift; share of cases closed with a Quality Check; unchecked backlog age. | By shift and priority; histogram of Quality Check clock times. | End-of-shift bursts; any fall in coverage. |
| 9 | Handover and queue ownership changes | Resource changes between consecutive events of a case; cases crossing a shift boundary; carryover count and its complexity at shift start. | Handover network between resource groups; by shift transition. | Rising carryover and complex backlog inherited by Early and Late shifts. |
| 10 | Worker-load fairness | Distribution of events, complexity-weighted load, high-WIP exposure minutes and high-priority share per resource; dispersion (for example Gini or max/median) within experience group. | Box plots per group; no raw ranking of named individuals. | Juniors or specific people carrying disproportionate high-WIP or complex load. |

**Operating rules:** show confidence intervals and minimum sample sizes; annotate intervention start dates; alert on system conditions (WIP above threshold, Quality Check backlog age, batch bursts), not on individual raw error rates; review the dashboard with staff and recalibrate expected-value models when routing, staffing or Bot_Risk versions change.
