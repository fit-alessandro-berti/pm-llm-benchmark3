## 1. Decision rule

Use a **gated, robust portfolio rule**, recalculated before every dispatch. Neither `p_now` nor `p_now × case value` is sufficient.

**Eligibility and evidence gates**

1. **Stage and feasibility:** The case must still be before `Diagnosis Complete`, with enough time to complete the 45-minute swarm and act on its findings. Recheck the stage immediately before starting. A high-risk case past that stage is ineligible.
2. **Actionability:** A diagnostician must identify a plausible swarm mechanism—such as missing diagnostic expertise, an unresolved technical hypothesis, or information that could redirect the case. “The model is worried” is not a mechanism.
3. **Data quality:** An alert based principally on imputation is held pending rapid validation or independent evidence. Missing data must not, however, permanently exclude the case.
4. **Prior intervention:** No repeated swarm in the same diagnosis episode unless a material event creates a new intervention rationale and a duty manager approves the exception.
5. **Alert evidence:**  
   - A first intervention review normally requires a material event or a verified risk increase of at least 0.10; smaller changes go to the watchlist unless deadline-critical.  
   - A persistent second alert, with no prior swarm, can proceed to review after a verified increase of at least 0.05 plus human confirmation.  
   - These changes trigger **review**, not automatic intervention. Imputation and routine 30-minute refreshes do not count as evidence.

**Calibration treatment**

Use calibration intervals for triage and urgency, while retaining uncertainty:

- **SME:** use an interval approximately `p_now + 0.04` to `p_now + 0.09`.
- **Strategic Enterprise:** use approximately `p_now  0.12` to `p_now  0.05`, pending post-redesign validation.
- **Public Sector:** retain `p_now` without a point correction, but widen review uncertainty because of the moderate sample.
- **Non-Native-Language Support:** do not invent a directional correction. Use human, language-aware review and explicitly stress both calibration and intervention effect.
- A score jump whose provenance is imputation receives no calibration-based endorsement.

These corrections do **not** convert the observational prediction into a causal intervention effect, nor should they mechanically scale the supplied risk-reduction ranges.

**Value and portfolio test**

For case \(i\), calculate:

\[
B_i^{base}=\delta_i^{mid}V_i-450-F_i,\qquad
B_i^{low}=\delta_i^{low}V_i-450-F_i ,
\]

A routine candidate must have \(B_i^{low}>0\), pass all gates, and remain worthwhile after queue review. Sparse-effect segments and post-redesign Strategic cases also receive a zero-benefit stress test.

For \(k\) used slots, approximate untreated-queue harm by:

\[
\text{added breaches}\approx 6ks,\qquad
H(k,s)\approx ks\sum_{j\in U}V_j+H_{\text{delay}},

where \(s\) is the per-slot, per-case spillover and \(U\) is the set of six exposed untreated cases. Among feasible portfolios, prefer high base total benefit, subject to:

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

- **One case, one alert episode:** Group routine refreshes into an episode keyed to the case, diagnosis attempt, model version, and material evidence. Repeated refreshes must not repeatedly consume review capacity or reset prioritization.
- **Cool-down after a no-swarm disposition:** Suppress unchanged duplicates for two routine refreshes—approximately 60 minutes—while retaining a named case owner. Material events, verified data corrections, and pre-specified stage/deadline triggers bypass the cool-down.
- **Persistent-alert escalation:** Persistence can escalate a case to human review, but not directly to a swarm. After suppression, re-entry requires:
  1. a material event, such as a substantive new log, diagnostic finding, verified staffing/queue change, or dependency change;
  2. a verified risk increase of at least 0.10 since disposition plus independent human confirmation; or
  3. a pre-specified urgency or stage trigger established at the original disposition.
- **After a swarm:** Suppress further swarm recommendations for the remainder of that diagnosis episode. A repeat requires a material new event, a new causal rationale, continued pre-`Diagnosis Complete` eligibility, positive robust portfolio value, and duty-manager approval.
- **No silent abandonment:** Suppressed or waiting cases still receive deadline ownership, data remediation, and appropriate non-swarm mitigation.
