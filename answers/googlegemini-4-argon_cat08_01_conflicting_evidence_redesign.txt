# 1. Current-process diagnosis

### The Core Contradiction: Illusory Efficiency vs. Downstream Failure
The process-mining evidence reveals a classic local-optimization trap: the dominant path (`Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen [3,600] -> Fast approval [2,550] -> Dispatch replacement [2,300]`) appears ultra-fast and cheap on the surface, with median step transitions under 2 hours (`0.5h`, `0.4h`, `1.8h`) and a EUR 0.20 screening cost. However, because the auto-screen rules are outdated and segment-blind, this fast path acts as a defect amplifier for complex claims. 

According to the **Rework statistics**, `Fast approval` cases are 31% cheaper before rework, but that cost advantage collapses to just 9% once downstream `Customer complaint/reopen` (14.1% rate on the fast path vs. 3.3% on the repair path), reshipments, and service credits are included. For `Premium high-value` (22% reopen rate) and `Standard high-value` (18% reopen rate) segments, pushing claims through `Fast approval` frequently results in shipping the wrong replacement part—incurring a EUR 410 expected error cost per case and violating compliance rules **R1** and **R2**. Conversely, the slower reviewed/repair path delivers high quality (3.3%–6% reopen rate) but imposes a 96-to-120-hour median cycle time on high-value devices, triggering severe service-credit penalties when applied indiscriminately to `Premium low-risk accessories` (which require <24h turnaround but suffer a 46h median cycle time on the manual path).

### Specific Process-Mining Artifact Evidence
1. **Frequency DFG:** Shows massive volume on the unreviewed fast track (`Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval`: 2,550 of 5,000 cases) alongside heavy, non-value-adding rework loops (`Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review`: 890 cases; `Customer complaint/reopen -> Manual eligibility review`: 310 cases) and high friction-driven leakage (`Claim abandoned`: 390 total cases across `Request missing evidence` [320] and `Customer approval` [70]).
2. **Performance DFG:** Identifies severe queuing and interaction bottlenecks. The loop `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` takes a mean of 34.0h (p90: 88.0h). Technical handoffs are equally sluggish: `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote` takes a mean of 22.0h (p90: 60.0h), and `Repair quote -> Customer approval` adds a mean of 30.0h (p90: 80.0h). Furthermore, `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` shows a long-tail latency of mean 96.0h (p90: 180.0h), reflecting the physical lag of a customer receiving the wrong replacement part and initiating a complaint.
3. **Conformance and Alignment Diagnostics:** Token-based replay shows 32% of traces are non-fitting (68% fitting, 0.91 fitness). Alignment diagnostics pinpoint exact policy breaches: `Fast approval` occurring before evidence completeness (violating **R2**), `Dispatch replacement` without risk review for >EUR 400 devices (violating **R1**), and `Customer complaint/reopen` bypassing `Manual eligibility review` by routing straight to `Escalation board -> Dispatch replacement/Repair device/Claim denied` (120 cases total, violating **R3**).
4. **Rework & Segment Outcomes:** 26% of cases entering `Request missing evidence` loop through it at least twice, driving the `Unclear defect / missing evidence` segment (22% of volume) to a 140-hour median cycle time. Meanwhile, `Rework repair` hits 12.5% of repaired high-value devices (`Quality check -> Rework repair`: 45 of 360 checked cases).
5. **Resource & Cost Constraints:** `Manual eligibility review` (7 FTEs, EUR 34/h) sits near its 82% utilization tipping point; any naive compliance fix that dumps auto-screened cases into manual review will cause exponential queue growth. Conversely, `Quality check` (2 inspectors, EUR 38/h) is structurally underutilized except after repair campaigns.

# 2. Redesign A: Segment-Gated Triage & Pre-Dispatch Inspector Buffer

### Redesigned Process Flow
1. **Rule-Updated Auto-Triage:** Reconfigure `Auto eligibility screen` to segment by product value, customer tier, and policy rules **R1/R2**.
2. **Low-Risk Fast Track:** `Premium low-risk accessory` (18% share, exempted by **R2**) and `Standard low-risk accessory` (25% share, once basic digital evidence is verified) route directly to `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement`.
3. **High-Value Compliance Routing:** All claims >EUR 400 (`Premium` and `Standard high-value devices`, 35% share) are blocked from immediate `Fast approval`. Standard high-value claims route to `Manual eligibility review` or `Fraud/risk review`, while Premium high-value claims get priority queuing in `Fraud/risk review` to prevent SLA breaches.
4. **Cross-Trained Pre-Dispatch Quality Gate:** Reallocate idle capacity from the underutilized `Quality check` inspectors (2 FTEs, EUR 38/h) to insert a lightweight **Pre-Dispatch Part Verification** step between `Fast approval` (or post-review approval) and `Dispatch replacement` specifically for high-value replacements.
5. **R3 Enforcement:** Hard-code workflow routing so `Escalation board` outputs must pass through a sign-off step in `Manual eligibility review` before executing `Dispatch replacement`, `Repair device`, or `Claim denied`.

### Target Segments, Paths, and Activities
* **Segments:** `Premium/Standard high-value devices` (35% share) and `Premium/Standard low-risk accessories` (43% share).
* **Paths/Activities:** `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement`, `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` (260 cases), and `Escalation board` bypasses (120 cases).

### Mechanism of Improvement
By blocking high-value devices from unvetted `Fast approval`, the organization eliminates **R1/R2** violations and stops the 18%–22% reopen wave caused by EUR 410 wrong-replacement errors. Using existing `Quality check` inspectors to verify replacement part selection before dispatch catches part-matching errors before physical shipment (avoiding the 96h mean reopen lag) without hiring new headcount. Keeping accessories on the fast path preserves the 8h median cycle time for `Premium low-risk accessories` (avoiding service-credit penalties) and the 13h median for `Standard low-risk accessories`.

### Deliberate Sacrifices and Unresolved Issues
* **Sacrifices:** Cycle time for high-value devices previously sneaking through the 20h–28h fast path will increase by 6–18 hours (for the pre-dispatch gate) plus review queue time.
* **Unresolved:** Does not fix the `Unclear defect / missing evidence` loop (22% of cases, 140h median cycle time), the `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote` delay (22h mean), or `Rework repair` (12.5% on high-value devices). Furthermore, increasing manual/risk review inflow by 15%–35% risks pushing `Manual eligibility review` past its 82% utilization cliff.

# 3. Redesign B: Upfront Guided Intake Portal & Manual Capacity Liberation

### Redesigned Process Flow
1. **Mandatory Guided Intake Portal (Pre-Screen):** Deploy the new evidence portal *at* `Intake claim` before eligibility routing. The portal enforces structured defect categorization and mandatory photo/serial-number uploads up front, with an immediate automated callback/chat fallback for customers who stall (mitigating low digital-literacy abandonment).
2. **Elimination of Post-Screen Ping-Pong:** By guaranteeing evidence completeness at intake (**R2** satisfied upfront), eliminate the `Auto eligibility screen -> Request missing evidence` (650 cases) and `Manual eligibility review -> Request missing evidence` (780 cases) loops, along with the 890-case return loop (`Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review`, mean 34.0h).
3. **Reinvested Manual Reviewer Capacity:** Removing the 890-case rework loop frees massive capacity in `Manual eligibility review` (7 FTEs). Reallocate this freed capacity to absorb high-value device claims redirected from `Auto eligibility screen` (satisfying **R1**) and to process `Customer complaint/reopen` cases rapidly (satisfying **R3**).

### Target Segments, Paths, and Activities
* **Segments:** `Unclear defect / missing evidence` (22% share, 140h median cycle time) and `High-value devices` requiring manual review.
* **Paths/Activities:** `Auto/Manual eligibility -> Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` (1,430 inflows, 890 returns, 320 abandonments) and `Intake claim -> Manual eligibility review` (mean 7.5h, p90 18.0h).

### Mechanism of Improvement
`Manual eligibility review` is currently choked by handling the same claims two or three times (26% of missing-evidence cases loop $\ge 2$ times). Capturing complete evidence at intake cuts repeated evidence requests by 30%–45% and eliminates the 34.0h mean (88.0h p90) wait between evidence request and manual re-review. This structurally lowers reviewer utilization well below the 82% nonlinear queuing threshold, allowing `Manual eligibility review` to absorb the 15%–35% surge from updated auto-screen rules without blowing up queues. Better upfront defect data also improves initial replacement part selection.

### Deliberate Sacrifices and Unresolved Issues
* **Sacrifices:** Adds upfront friction at `Intake claim` for all customers, and despite fallback support, risks increasing early-stage `Claim abandoned` for customers with low digital literacy. High implementation complexity and longer lead time than a simple rule change.
* **Unresolved:** Leaves the downstream repair chain untouched—`Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote` (22.0h mean), `Repair quote -> Customer approval` (30.0h mean, 70 abandonments), and `Rework repair` (45 cases) remain slow and error-prone.

# 4. Redesign C: Pre-Priced Repair Fast-Track & Economic Replacement Shift

### Redesigned Process Flow
1. **Pre-Approved Part-Price Matrix (Eliminating Quote Delays):** Build a standardized part-price catalog so `Auto eligibility screen`, `Manual eligibility review`, and `Fraud/risk review` can generate instant repair orders without waiting on `Repair quote` technicians (3 FTEs, EUR 45/h) to confirm part prices (currently causing a 22.0h mean / 60.0h p90 delay).
2. **Upfront Customer Repair Pre-Consent:** For `Standard high-value devices` (21% share) and mid-risk claims, capture conditional repair approval at `Intake claim` ("Repair if under coverage/threshold"), bypassing the `Repair quote -> Customer approval` wait (mean 30.0h, p90 80.0h, 70 abandonments) and routing straight to `Repair device`.
3. **Shift High-Value Claims from Unvetted Replacement to Fast-Track Repair:** Re-route high-value claims that currently take the high-error replacement path (18%–22% reopen rate, EUR 410 wrong-part risk) onto the accelerated `Repair device -> Quality check` path (which has only a 3.3%–6% customer reopen rate).
4. **Technician Reallocation to High-Value Repair QA:** Freed from manual price-quoting clerical work, the 3 `Repair quote` technicians (EUR 45/h) are reassigned to assist with complex high-value device diagnostics and repair execution, directly attacking the 12.5% internal `Rework repair` rate (`Quality check -> Rework repair`: 45 cases).

### Target Segments, Paths, and Activities
* **Segments:** `Premium high-value device` (14% share, 96h reviewed/repair median) and `Standard high-value device` (21% share, 120h reviewed/repair median).
* **Paths/Activities:** `Manual/Fraud review -> Repair quote -> Customer approval -> Repair device -> Quality check -> Rework repair`.

### Mechanism of Improvement
The prompt's data shows that the repair path produces vastly superior customer outcomes (3.3% reopen vs. 14.1% on fast replacement) and full compliance, but is crippled by administrative delays: 22.0h mean waiting for a price quote and 30.0h mean waiting for customer approval (52 hours of pure dead time out of a 96h–120h cycle). Automating part-price lookup and pre-consenting customers removes ~36–50 hours from the repair path. This makes the high-quality repair path fast enough for `Premium high-value` SLAs while sparing the company the EUR 410 wrong-replacement penalty and utilizing existing technician skills on actual technical quality rather than pricing admin.

### Deliberate Sacrifices and Unresolved Issues
* **Sacrifices:** Higher direct processing labor per claim compared to automated replacement dispatch, and physical repair cycle time (`Repair device -> Quality check`: 9.0h mean) still cannot match instant replacement speed for customers who prefer a brand-new swap.
* **Unresolved:** Does not resolve the front-end `Request missing evidence` rework loop (22% unclear defect segment still takes ~140h) and does not directly expand `Fraud/risk review` capacity (2 FTEs) during Monday/campaign spikes.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

### Redesign A: Segment-Gated Triage & Pre-Dispatch Inspector Buffer
* **Failure Mode 1 (Queueing Tipping-Point Collapse):** Updating auto-screen rules increases manual-review inflow by 15%–35%. Because `Manual eligibility review` (7 FTEs) becomes nonlinear above 82% utilization, pushing 500–1,200 additional cases into manual/risk review without first removing the 890-case evidence rework loop could cause `Intake claim -> Manual eligibility review` (currently 7.5h mean / 18.0h p90) to spiral past 72+ hours, triggering massive service-credit penalties on `Premium high-value` claims.
* **Failure Mode 2 (Superficial Pre-Dispatch Inspection):** If the 2 `Quality check` inspectors only check whether the dispatched SKU matches the approved SKU on the ticket—when the underlying defect was misdiagnosed at intake due to missing evidence—the customer still receives an inappropriate replacement (adding 6–18h of gate delay without preventing the EUR 410 error).
* **Displaced Bottleneck:** Migrates from downstream `Customer complaint/reopen` and `Escalation board` directly upstream to **`Fraud/risk review` (2 FTEs, especially on Mondays/post-campaign)** and **`Manual eligibility review` (7 FTEs)**. Additionally, if a repair campaign coincides with a replacement spike, the **2 `Quality check` inspectors** will become a shared contention point between `Repair device -> Quality check` and the new pre-dispatch gate.

### Redesign B: Upfront Guided Intake Portal & Manual Capacity Liberation
* **Failure Mode 1 (Digital Literacy Abandonment Surge):** Making evidence completion strict at intake could cause customers with low digital literacy or complex intermittent defects to abandon the claim before submission, inflating `Claim abandoned` well beyond the current 390 cases and hiding dissatisfaction as "drop-offs" rather than complaints.
* **Failure Mode 2 (Garbage-In Compliance Workarounds):** Customers frustrated by mandatory portal fields may upload irrelevant photos or select arbitrary defect drop-downs just to bypass the gate. This satisfies **R2** on paper (token replay looks compliant), but dumps noisy data into `Auto eligibility screen` or `Manual eligibility review`, recreating the 14.1% reopen rate or triggering post-intake `Request missing evidence` anyway.
* **Displaced Bottleneck:** By unclogging the front-end eligibility screen and freeing manual reviewers, claims will flow much faster into **`Fraud/risk review` (2 specialists)** and **`Repair quote` (3 technicians)**. The part-price confirmation wait (`Manual/Fraud review -> Repair quote`: 21–22h mean, 50–60h p90) will immediately become the primary process pacemaker.

### Redesign C: Pre-Priced Repair Fast-Track & Economic Replacement Shift
* **Failure Mode 1 (Outdated Part-Price Matrix & Margin Erosion):** If the automated part-price catalog is static while supplier component prices fluctuate, the system will auto-approve repairs that exceed economic repair thresholds (where replacement or denial would have been cheaper), or cause physical stalls at `Repair device` because the quoted part is actually out of stock.
* **Failure Mode 2 (Customer Backlash Against Repair vs. Replacement):** `Premium high-value` customers accustomed to receiving a fast replacement unit (20h median on fast path) may balk at receiving a repaired unit (even if cycle time drops from 96h to ~45h), driving complaints back into `Customer complaint/reopen -> Escalation board` (mean 54.0h).
* **Displaced Bottleneck:** Eliminating the 52h combined buffer of `Repair quote` and `Customer approval` dumps a sudden wave of approved devices directly onto the physical **`Repair device` bench** and **`Quality check` (2 inspectors)**. What was previously an administrative pricing bottleneck becomes a physical shop-floor and spare-parts inventory bottleneck.

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

### Sequencing Logic: Why Order Matters
Do **not** start by deploying the full auto-screen rule update across all segments (Redesign A alone), because adding 15%–35% more volume to `Manual eligibility review` before fixing the 890-case evidence-rework loop will push the 7 reviewers past their 82% utilization cliff. Conversely, waiting months to build the full IT portal (Redesign B) leaves high-value compliance breaches (**R1/R2**) and EUR 410 wrong-replacement errors bleeding cash today. 

Therefore, the optimal sequence is:
1. **Stage 1 (Weeks 1–4): Targeted Containment Pilot (Scoped Redesign A)** — Stop the highest-cost bleeding (High-Value Fast Approval errors & **R3** violations) using existing idle `Quality check` capacity, while buffering `Manual eligibility review` from overload.
2. **Stage 2 (Weeks 5–10): Capacity Liberation Pilot (Redesign B)** — Roll out the Guided Evidence Portal to drain the 890-case `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` loop and drop reviewer utilization below 70%.
3. **Stage 3 (Weeks 9–14): Repair Value-Stream Pilot (Redesign C)** — Once front-end flow is stable and compliant, automate part-price lookup to compress the 96h–120h repair path for high-value devices.

---

### Stage 1 (First Pilot): High-Value Pre-Dispatch Gate & Selective Rule Update (Scoped Redesign A)
* **Pilot Design (2-Week Deployment):**
  1. Update `Auto eligibility screen` rules **only** for `Premium/Standard high-value devices` (>EUR 400) and `Premium low-risk accessories`:
     * Lock in instant `Fast approval` for `Premium low-risk accessories` (<24h SLA, **R2** exempt).
     * Route high-value claims with complete initial evidence to a dedicated fast-review lane plus a **Pre-Dispatch Part Verification gate** staffed by the 2 underutilized `Quality check` inspectors (EUR 38/h), rather than dumping them all into the general `Manual eligibility review` queue.
  2. Enforce hard routing from `Escalation board` through `Manual eligibility review` sign-off before closure/dispatch (**R3** fix; only 120 cases per 5,000, adding negligible load).
* **Metrics to Monitor:**
  * *Primary Quality/Cost:* High-value `Fast approval / Dispatch -> Customer complaint/reopen` rate (baseline: 18%–22%) and wrong-replacement error frequency (EUR 410/case).
  * *Primary Compliance:* Alignment deviations on **R1**, **R2**, and **R3** (baseline: 32% non-fitting traces).
  * *Capacity/Queue Guardrail:* `Manual eligibility review` utilization rate (critical threshold: 82%) and p90 wait time on `Intake claim -> Manual eligibility review` (baseline: 18.0h) and `Auto screen -> Fraud/risk review` (baseline: 28.0h).
  * *Customer Guardrail:* Median and p90 cycle time for `Premium low-risk accessories` (must stay <24h) and `Premium high-value devices`.
* **Stop/Go Thresholds:**
  * **GO to Stage 2 if:** High-value reopen rate drops by $\ge 35\%$ (e.g., from 18–22% toward <12%), **R1/R3** violations on piloted segments drop near zero, pre-dispatch gate adds $\le 12$ hours median delay, and `Manual eligibility review` utilization stays $\le 80\%$.
  * **PAUSE / ADJUST if:** `Manual eligibility review` utilization exceeds 82% or p90 queue time exceeds 24h (trigger temporary overtime or narrow the rule inflow until Stage 2 frees capacity), OR if `Fraud/risk review` Monday p90 exceeds 36h.

---

### Stage 2: Guided Evidence Portal A/B Test (Redesign B)
* **Pilot Design:** Route 50% of incoming `Standard` and `Unclear defect` claims through the new Guided Evidence Portal at intake (with an immediate "Request Call/Chat Assist" button if a user stalls for >3 minutes). Keep 50% on the legacy intake flow as a concurrent control group.
* **Metrics to Monitor & Thresholds:**
  * *Rework Loop:* Inflow to `Request missing evidence` (baseline: 28.6% of all cases) and multi-loop rate ($\ge 2$ requests, baseline: 26%). **Go threshold:** $\ge 30\%$ reduction in repeated evidence requests.
  * *Abandonment Guardrail:* Total `Claim abandoned` rate (intake drop-off + post-request abandonment; baseline: 320/5,000 = 6.4% at evidence stage). **Stop/Redesign UI threshold:** Total abandonment increases by >1.5 percentage points over control.
  * *Capacity Liberation:* `Manual eligibility review` utilization drops by $\ge 10$ percentage points, allowing full deployment of **R1/R2** auto-screen rules across all remaining segments.

---

### Stage 3: Pre-Priced Repair Fast-Track Pilot (Redesign C)
* **Pilot Design:** Integrate top 80% most common high-value repair parts into an automated price table and enable upfront repair pre-consent for `Standard high-value` claims. Reassign 1–2 `Repair quote` technicians to high-value `Repair device` QA.
* **Metrics to Monitor & Thresholds:**
  * *Speed:* Combined cycle time from `Manual/Fraud review` to `Repair device` (baseline mean: 22h + 30h + 20h = 72h). **Go threshold:** Reduction of $\ge 30$ hours on median cycle time.
  * *Shop-Floor Quality:* High-value `Quality check -> Rework repair` rate (baseline: 12.5%). **Go threshold:** Drops below 7%.

---

### How to Verify Improvements Are Real Rather Than Shifted Downstream
To prevent local-metric gaming or hidden problem displacement, the sponsor must enforce four longitudinal tracking mechanisms across all stages:
1. **30-Day Cohort Total-Cost-of-Claim Accounting:** Because `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` has a mean latency of 96.0 hours (p90: 180.0 hours / 7.5 days), fast-path savings look artificially good in the first week. Track each weekly intake cohort for a full 21–30 days to verify that net cost *after* reshipments, EUR 410/EUR 95 part errors, and service credits actually declines beyond the current 9% margin.
2. **Full-Funnel Abandonment Tracking (Pre-Intake + Post-Intake):** If Redesign B cuts `Request missing evidence -> Claim abandoned` (currently 320 cases) by simply causing customers to quit on the intake webpage before a case ID is generated, the DFG will falsely show a drop in abandonment. Log session-start-to-submission drop-offs alongside in-process `Claim abandoned` (390 cases) plus post-claim CSAT/NPS.
3. **End-to-End Trace Fitness & Conservation of Volume:** Monitor token-based replay fitness (baseline: 68% fitting traces) alongside downstream queue lengths (`Repair device`, `Quality check`, `Escalation board`). If blocking `Fast approval` simply shifts 500 cases into `Repair quote -> Claim abandoned` (currently 70 cases) or causes `Quality check -> Rework repair` (currently 45 cases) to spike, the bottleneck was merely displaced, not solved.
4. **First-Time-Right Resolution Rate by Segment:** Track the percentage of claims in each of the 5 segments that reach `Quality/compliance close -> END` within their segment SLA *without* ever touching `Request missing evidence`, `Rework repair`, `Customer complaint/reopen`, or `Escalation board`.
