# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1. **Tail latency.** Medians can improve while p90/p95 explode if newly standardized simple cases speed up and complex cases slow down simultaneously (the central scenario predicts almost exactly this). Average cycle time has no sensitivity to dispersion. *Detector:* stratified p90/p95 with bootstrap CIs (G1); bimodality in the duration histogram.
2. **Composition shift / Simpson's paradox.** Aggregate movement mixes population change with true performance change. Shifting mix toward simple cases (or pre-cutover WIP drainage) can make the average improve while every within-stratum metric worsens. *Detector:* always report within-stratum deltas beside aggregate; freeze a complexity-mix-adjusted comparison.
3. **High-complexity case degradation.** These cases now depend on the longest, most contended queues (TSS + legal/risk + shared approval serially). Even a better "correct routing" rate can increase their end-to-end latency and rework probability. Their smaller volume dilutes them in averages. *Detector:* dedicated complexity-stratum panel (G1–G3); wait stacking along the complex-case path.
4. **Local knowledge loss and soft-signal erosion.** The informal consult that prevented referrals never appears in the log as an activity — its loss shows up *later* as referral creep, clarification loops, and rework. By the time rework accumulates, damage compounding through skill decay is underway. *Detector:* E2/E5/E6 leading triad; `local_expertise_flag` trajectories.
5. **Fairness divergence cancelled by aggregation.** East improving while North/South degrade nets to "stable"; a disadvantaged customer segment (language/regional accommodation) is small enough to vanish in the blend. Stable average here is compatible with an inequitable system. *Detector:* stratum-gap metrics (G3/G8); CSAT by segment; complaint joins.
6. **Learning pipeline decay.** If caseworkers stop co-working hard cases, juniors never build judgment. Effects are cohort-based and appear one to three quarters later — after the rollout is declared successful. *Detector:* G6; activity-breadth per resource; `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` homogenization; escalation-vs-tenure curves.
7. **Accountability diffusion.** Dual approval layers and pull-advice make ownership ambiguous precisely on contested decisions. Cases still complete (often with an extra loop), so averages look fine while decision rights rot. *Detector:* E8/E9; `discover_organizational_roles` showing a faceless blended approver; cases with multiple resources on decision activities.
8. **Exception-handling brittleness and hidden bottlenecks.** Standardization deletes undocumented recovery paths. Locally, teams improvised around absence/outages; the shared queue concentrates exactly the traffic that improvisation used to deflect, creating a correlated single point of failure. Rule-rigidity even *raises* variant conformity (looks like compliance) while making the system fragile. *Detector:* resilience-in-absence test (G7); `discover_subcontracting_network` dependency on the shared unit; variant-thinning plus workaround-variant bursts.

# 5. Pilot design

**Objective/hypotheses.** H1: pooling relieves East without degrading quality (benefit case). H2: shared queues do not become hidden bottlenecks (central risk). H3: harm to North/South local-expertise-dependent flows stays within guardrails (donor-team risk).

**Pilot scope (staged, reversible).**
- *Wave 0 (weeks 1–2):* Stand up TSS in **shadow mode** — observe all teams' potential referrals without changing routing; validate intake-triage quality and arrive/completion balance against live load.
- *Wave 1 (weeks 3–12):* East routes all eligible cases to TSS (largest expected benefit; best feasibility test). Simultaneously, **North high-complexity slice only** routes centrally as a deliberate harm-detection arm; North/South keep the informal-advice channel formally protected (protected "office hours" consult, logged under a designated activity so it becomes measurable).
- *Deferred:* removal of the local supervisor position and legal/risk detection-rule expansion. Legal/risk capacity must be **added before** broader detection goes live (near-saturated queue: precondition, not optimization). When the shared approval queue ships, mandate a single named accountable approver field per decision.

**Comparison group.** Stepped-wedge: not-yet-cutover teams and untouched strata (e.g., simple/mid-complexity cases that stay local in North/South) form concurrent controls; East/North-Slice pre-periods serve as their own baselines (difference-in-differences). No team that continues full local operation should be disturbed during Wave 1.

**Observation windows.** 12-week frozen pre-baseline (captures 1 full monthly cycle); weeks 1–2 post-cutover observed for queue buildup but excluded from KPI evaluation; weeks 3–12 evaluated; gates at day 30 (operational), day 60 (quality/fairness), day 90 (full readout, explicitly excluding long-horizon learning claims).

**Mandatory segmentation.** Every reported metric is cut by `team` × `case_complexity` × `required_specialty` × `customer_segment` × `priority`. Suppress cells n<20 rather than averaging them in.

**Guardrail metrics and triggers.**

| Guardrail | Threshold | Response |
|---|---|---|
| TSS tier utilization (incl. triage/QC absorption) | >85% median week | Add capacity / re-tier before continuing |
| Legal/Risk queue wait | median >12h or p90 >24h | Pause detection-rule expansion; escalate staffing |
| Referral induction (E2) | mid-complexity referrals +>25% | Restore protected informal channel; inspect triage rules |
| Rework per stratum (G2) | >+1 pt vs own baseline | Freeze wave; root-cause post-touch rework |
| SLA miss per stratum (G3) | >+1 pt | Freeze wave |
| Wait fairness tripwire | any segment/location p90 doubles vs its own baseline | Immediate rollback of affected routing |
| Role clarity (E8/E9) | reassignment +>15% or approval revisits >3% | Rebuild approval accountability rule |
| CSAT (G4) | any segment <0.1 | Open adjustment review |

**Staff feedback collection.** Weekly anonymous pulse by team/role (5 fixed Likert items + free text), coded into the `handover_reason` taxonomy so qualitative themes can be joined with log trends; biweekly frontline interviews during Waves 0–1; an explicit in-tool flag (recorded as an event attribute) for "workaround/bypass used" so informal adaptation enters the log rather than staying invisible.

**Rollback/adjustment logic.** (i) Any red guardrail 2 consecutive weeks  targeted adjustment (capacity, priority rules, triage thresholds). (ii) Same red 2 further weeks  pause wave; revert affected routing (rules are configuration; reversible). (iii) Severe event (fairness tripwire, queue meltdown, SLO breach on a customer segment)  immediate rollback; in-flight cases migrated back with enriched case notes. (iv) Expansion to Wave 2 requires all guardrails green for 4 consecutive weeks plus reviewed staff-feedback synthesis.

**Modify, then pilot first — a gated, staged rollout led by East, with the structural modifications below. Do not approve full rollout at this point.**

What management **must not** infer from early averages:

- Busy specialists + "higher utilization" is not productivity; it may be silent absorption of triage and QC work.
- Quality that looks stable in month 1 reflects detection lag and censoring; rework and learning effects mature over quarters.
- Absence of complaints is not fairness; small disadvantaged segments rarely surface in aggregates.

def boot_ci_delta(x, y, iters=800, seed=7):                      # 90% CI for median delta
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed); x, y = np.asarray(x, float), np.asarray(y, float)
    d = [np.median(rng.choice(x, len(x))) - np.median(rng.choice(y, len(y))) for _ in range(iters)]
    return tuple(np.percentile(d, [5, 95]))

rows = []

    if min(len(sb), len(sa)) < 20: continue
    lo, hi = boot_ci_delta(sa["duration_d"], sb["duration_d"])
