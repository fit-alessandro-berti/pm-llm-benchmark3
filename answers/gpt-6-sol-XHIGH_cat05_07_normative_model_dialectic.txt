## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

M0 requires one registration, both checks before either decision branch, manual approval before activation on the approved branch, then notification and closure. Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review have no required order relative to each other. M0 says nothing about the humans performing work, risk, amounts, dates, or the authority for an exception. CS-9 is the controlled standard from 2025-01-01: both checks precede activation, manual approval precedes every activation, and requester and manual approver are different humans. Its abbreviated pre-screen language does not settle whether rejected leads belong in M0.

PD-3 records a dated steering-committee pilot, eligibility criteria, and altered control timing; it does not establish that the committee had authority to waive CS-9. The observer's “monitored exception” comment supports awareness and conditional operational permission, but her later statement defeats an inference that she personally approved a CS-9 amendment. FD-7 expressly describes a narrow, time-limited emergency route and controls. Its expiration is established; continued flood recovery is not an extension. Whether its issuer had the requisite exception authority should be checked, although the directive is substantially stronger evidence of authorization than an operator's belief.

The traces establish observed events, not necessarily all performed events. A stable CRM notification ID can corroborate an absent onboarding event; a mutable link entered later cannot alone do so. The July CRM migration plausibly explains aggregate missingness, but it cannot automatically explain SUP-702's June omission. SUP-711's repeated source transaction ID establishes a likely ingestion duplicate. SUP-712's aliases resolve to one human, so its perfect M0 trace does not establish compliance. SUP-715 has an undisclosed relationship that calls for a conflict-of-interest finding, but the materials do not establish a categorical ban on that relationship.

I use reliable, case-linked evidence and the rule effective at the activity time to decide noncompliance. An absent event in a known incomplete log is insufficient proof that the activity never occurred; a positive, reliably timestamped event in the wrong order is stronger evidence. Serious findings warrant corroboration of timestamps, identities, and case boundaries. The party claiming an exception must produce a contemporaneous authorized instrument, prove that this case met its scope and dates, and evidence each compensating control. Unproved exception authority is recorded separately from the operational facts; it does not convert an otherwise proved CS-9 departure into conformity. Equally, a formal trace mismatch alone does not establish misconduct.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

CS-9 is the controlling, approved rule. Neither meeting minutes, operational normalization, nor a desirable outcome silently amends its manual-approval and pre-activation quality requirements. PD-3 conflicts with both, and the Compliance observer disclaimed approval to weaken CS-9. Even among the 312 Auto_Approve cases in June and July, 14 are clearly outside PD-3's stated criteria and nine cannot be shown eligible. The other 289 only appear eligible: that does not prove a valid waiver or the required automated master-data validation. Six more auto approvals in the first week of August show that a temporary practice persisted after PD-3 expired. SUP-714 is a direct example; expected future approval has no present authority.

SUP-703 is not a borderline pilot case. It was high risk, new, and foreign, yet used Auto_Approve and activated before quality review; its first purchase was EUR 180,000. SUP-708 activated before sanctions clearance and manual approval without any applicable exception. The absence of transactions in the 40-minute interval reduces realized loss but does not cure the prohibited activation or the exposure it created. Internal Audit's severe-risk concern about these two cases is well grounded. SUP-705 repeated a provisional sequence on July 13, after FD-7 expired. The fact that flood recovery continued cannot keep a dated delegation alive. Its EUR 12,000 initial spend does not authorize the sequence.

M0 also understates noncompliance. SUP-712 matches its control flow but violates CS-9's different-human rule once aliases are resolved. SUP-715 matches M0 while lacking the disclosure required by the conflict-of-interest policy; the evidence supports a disclosure failure, not an invented ban on spouses approving. M0's high deviation count therefore cannot be dismissed as mere false positives. Approvals, sanctions, quality, and resource independence protect different interests; later clean reviews or low spend do not retroactively satisfy “before activation.”

The prosecution must concede genuine defects: repeated information requests in SUP-707 are useful and absent from M0; SUP-711 is a duplicate log row; SUP-713 is a substantively equivalent renamed check; notification capture degraded after July 1; rejected leads and reactivations have unsettled case boundaries. M0 should be enforced as evidence of the currently approved route where it applies, while each flagged trace is investigated against CS-9 and reliable source evidence rather than treated as an automatic violation.

## 3. Defense Brief

M0 is an incomplete representation of even the controlled standard. It omits the requester–approver identity rule, so SUP-712 looks conforming when it is not. It cannot express the documented pilot or emergency paths, amount limits, risk eligibility, deadlines, or daily monitoring. Its requirement to finish Quality_Review before any decision overstates the stated CS-9 requirement for rejected leads: CS-9 permits an abbreviated pre-screen and specifies the two checks before activation, not necessarily before rejection. If rejected leads are intake cases, SUP-709 exposes a model defect; if M0 begins only after pre-screen acceptance, it is outside M0. The scope document does not decide between those readings. A reactivation with a still-valid prior certification, SUP-710, similarly cannot be assessed faithfully as a fresh registration without a formal reactivation rule and linkage to the earlier certification.

M0's checks are concurrent. Quality_Review before Sanctions_Check is permitted; in SUP-708 the breach is activation before sanctions and approval, not the order of the two checks. SUP-707's two information requests obtained missing certificates before approval, and 47 cases show the same general practice without early activation. A repeatable evidence-gathering step is a sensible model amendment. SUP-711 should be evaluated after deduplication by source transaction ID, and SUP-713's audit documents support a semantic mapping from QA_Clearance to Quality_Review despite the mapping's later formal approval. A label or duplicate-row mismatch is not evidence of an unsafe business act.

The notification question requires cross-system evidence. Missingness jumped from 2% to 29% after the July CRM migration, so raw-log notification violations have weak positive predictive value. SUP-706's mutable, late-entered link leaves its notification genuinely uncertain; it does not prove absence. SUP-702 has a stable CRM notification ID, making its notification much more credible, although the June date means the July migration is not an explanation by itself. The CRM time also precedes the next-business-day quality review, which a revised pilot flow should allow while still requiring both before final closure.

PD-3 was a real steering decision with a narrow window and criteria, not an operator's invented shortcut. SUP-702 satisfies the stated risk, geography, group, timing, and review deadline, subject to verifying automated master-data validation and competent waiver authority. FD-7 describes precisely the temporary provisional path used in SUP-704: sanctions first, critical supplier during the window, EUR 18,000 initial spend, daily monitoring, and next-business-day quality and approval. These facts justify treating the sequence differently from SUP-705 and SUP-708. A high raw deviation rate can thus reflect M0's obsolescence, ambiguous scope, and fragmented logging.

The defense must concede limits. Informal practice cannot make SUP-703 pilot eligible, extend PD-3 into August, extend FD-7 beyond July 12, or satisfy CS-9's different-human rule. It cannot infer a valid waiver from the observer's words alone. Better modeling explains some deviations; it does not erase the high-risk events or the need to preserve a controlled baseline.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

Use a controlled baseline plus versioned, narrowly scoped overlays, then evaluate business events with source-quality and resource evidence. Keep the current CS-9 controls operative unless an authorized exception expressly supersedes them. A proposed POWL revision must go through approval; encoding a route is not itself permission to use it.

* Control flow in POWL: register; choose standard approval, eligible pilot auto approval, emergency provisional activation, early pre-screen rejection, or rejection after full review; notify before closure. Keep standard sanctions and quality concurrent, both complete before manual approval and activation. Permit zero or more Request_More_Info activities before a final decision; require evidence received before any approval that depends on it. In the pilot route require sanctions and automated master-data validation before Auto_Approve and activation; permit quality before or after activation, with notification after activation and possibly before quality, while closure waits for the review. In the emergency route require sanctions before provisional activation, then retrospective quality and manual approval before closure. These routes are proposed structure, subject to external guards and formal authorization.

* Temporal and data rules outside POWL: match event-time risk, legal entity, DE jurisdiction, approved corporate-group membership, and validation snapshots to PD-3; match critical-relief purpose and effective local time to FD-7; calculate two-business-day deadlines using a specified holiday calendar; sum all spend before retrospective approval, rather than checking only the first purchase; verify daily monitoring. Express order invariants such as “sanctions precedes every activation,” “notification precedes closure,” and “each activation has a valid route-specific approval” as DECLARE or log-skeleton-style checks, with overlay-specific exceptions. Use pandas or equivalent keyed data checks for dates, risk, amounts, and cross-system joins. A POWL XOR branch is not an eligibility guard.

* Resource controls: resolve aliases to stable human IDs before testing requester versus manual approver; record delegation and authority to approve; require disclosure of covered conflicts and a documented recusal or independent decision where policy calls for one. Check these with identity joins and organizational review. Do not infer from CS-9 a prohibition on relationships that it does not state.

* Overlay governance: maintain immutable versions with issuer, delegated waiver power, explicit provisions superseded, scope, effective window and timezone, guard predicates, compensating controls, approval time, and sunset. FD-7 takes priority only within its authorized scope and interval; PD-3 has no effect after July 31. Unapproved or proposed rules are recorded but fail closed. An extension must be signed and effective before the activity it covers; retrospective documentation may explain conduct but should not rewrite its authority at event time. Review the pilot evidence and either amend CS-9 through its owner or end the route.

* Scope rules: define intake and accepted-onboarding boundaries so an early sanctions pre-screen rejection is either an explicit abbreviated branch in the same case or a separately coded intake case, never selected opportunistically after observing the trace. Define reactivation as a distinct case type, whether prior certification can be carried forward, its maximum age and scope, and what fresh sanctions, approval, and registration evidence is required. Define withdrawal as a distinct terminal outcome with actor, timestamp, reason, notice, and closure rules; do not count it as rejection or activation. Link prior cases and certifications with stable IDs. Until these rules are approved, classify ambiguity as a scope finding and do not create an undocumented bypass.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

The code constructs a candidate POWL, using a silent do-step and a Request_More_Info redo-step to allow zero or more requests. The loop represents the request activity, not the full question–response exchange; evidence receipt and its deadline need separate data checks. Branch guards, business-day timing, cumulative spend, identity, and authority are deliberately external. A quality review before activation is allowed on the auto route because it has no order edge to activation; it must still finish before closure. The emergency branch specifically models provisional activation before retrospective checks. A case that completed both checks and manual approval before activation can use the standard branch. Early rejection is shown as a proposed in-scope intake variant, not a resolution of today's scope dispute. Reactivation is not silently exempted in this model: it needs a separately approved case-type definition before adding its route.

~~~python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    OperatorPOWL, SilentTransition, StrictPartialOrder, Transition
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def event(name):
    return Transition(label=name)


def partial(nodes, edges=()):
    model = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    for before, after in edges:
        model.order.add_edge(before, after)
    return model


def sequence(*nodes):
    return partial(nodes, zip(nodes, nodes[1:]))


def more_info():
    # LOOP(do, redo): tau (Request_More_Info tau)*, hence zero or more.
    return OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.LOOP,
        children=[SilentTransition(), event("Request_More_Info")],
    )


def standard_checks():
    # No edges among these nodes: sanctions and quality are concurrent.
    return partial([
        event("Sanctions_Check"),
        event("Quality_Review"),
        more_info(),
    ])


standard_approval = sequence(
    standard_checks(),
    event("Manual_Procurement_Approval"),
    event("Activate_Supplier"),
    event("Notify_Requester"),
)

standard_rejection = sequence(
    standard_checks(),
    event("Reject_Supplier"),
    event("Notify_Requester"),
)

s = event("Sanctions_Check")
v = event("Automated_Master_Data_Validation")
r = more_info()
a = event("Auto_Approve")
act = event("Activate_Supplier")
q = event("Quality_Review")
n = event("Notify_Requester")
pilot_auto_approval = partial(
    [s, v, r, a, act, q, n],
    [(s, a), (v, a), (r, a), (a, act), (act, n)],
)
# q is unordered with act and n, permitting pre- or post-activation
# quality and notification before or after quality. Root closure waits for q.

s = event("Sanctions_Check")
r = more_info()
act = event("Activate_Supplier")
q = event("Quality_Review")
m = event("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
n = event("Notify_Requester")
emergency_provisional = partial(
    [s, r, act, q, m, n],
    [(s, act), (r, act), (act, q), (act, m), (act, n)],
)

s = event("Sanctions_Check")
r = more_info()
rej = event("Reject_Supplier")
n = event("Notify_Requester")
early_prescreen_rejection = partial(
    [s, r, rej, n],
    [(s, rej), (r, rej), (rej, n)],
)

route = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[
        standard_approval,
        standard_rejection,
        pilot_auto_approval,
        emergency_provisional,
        early_prescreen_rejection,
    ],
)
M1_candidate = sequence(
    event("Register_Supplier"),
    route,
    event("Close_Case"),
)
~~~

Normalize each policy overlay as a versioned row, with foreign keys to its authority record and to enumerated control requirements. The table below shows the decision fields; store the detailed predicates and evidence requirements as child rows, not free text alone. “Candidate” means it is not an active waiver merely because M1 contains its path.

Build an append-only event record with stable event ID, source system and transaction ID, canonical activity ID and observed label, case ID and case type, supplier/legal-entity ID, lifecycle state, occurrence time with UTC offset, immutable ingestion/record time, and correction/superseded-event links. Preserve both effective time and recorded/approved time for mappings and policy versions, so a later label mapping cannot masquerade as contemporaneous approval. Capture rule/version and authority IDs selected at each decision, eligibility snapshots (risk, jurisdiction, corporate-group status, purpose), automated-validation result and evidence ID, quality certificate and prior-case link, provisional status, transaction amounts and approval release time, and daily-monitoring records. Record canonical human IDs plus all actor aliases, requester and approver roles, delegation chain and validity, and conflict disclosure and disposition.

CRM must emit an immutable notification ID, case and supplier keys, recipient, delivery state, source timestamp, and onboarding correlation ID; reconcile it by stable IDs rather than editable text. Deduplicate SUP-711-like rows by source transaction ID while retaining raw records and the correction audit trail. Conformance reporting should show separate results for structural POWL fit, overlay eligibility and timing, resource/conflict controls, scope, and evidence quality, with unresolved cases excluded from a single misleading compliance percentage.
