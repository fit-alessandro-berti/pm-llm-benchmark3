## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

- **January–March (Baseline)**: Stable period with expected behavior. High AUROC, consistent process variant distribution, and mature labels.
- **April (Data-Pipeline Failure)**: The sudden drop in predictive performance was purely synthetic. The workflow-system migration renamed the diagnostic event, breaking feature extraction (evidenced by 14% missing queue feature and 18% unmapped variants). This is an engineering outage, not concept or process drift.
- **May (Partial Recovery)**: The label patch fixed event mapping, but the failure to backfill cached temporal features left residual data corruption.
- **June (Process Drift without Concept Drift)**: A business policy routed more cases to V2 (Remote resolution). This shifted the covariates (Covariate Drift) and process variants (Process Drift), but the conditional breach rates per variant (V1/V2/V3) remained highly stable. AUROC recovered.
- **July–August (Process Drift leading to Concept Drift)**: The supplier parts delay severely impacted the `Order Part -> Part Received` edge. By August, the V3 matured breach rate nearly doubled to 0.64. The fundamental relationship between V3 features and the outcome changed, constituting genuine Concept Drift.
- **September (Population Shift)**: Partner Z was onboarded, injecting a distinct population. They heavily favor V3 and introduce unmeasured covariates (rural distance, device age). This amplifies the observed breach rate due to both the supplier issue (Concept Drift) and the new underlying customer base (Population Shift).
- **October (Label Delay/Censoring)**: The outcome API backlog crippled label maturity (down to 55%). The reported 0.16 breach rate is an illusion caused by right-censoring, as breaches naturally take longer to mature. October is not a true recovery; it's a measurement blind spot.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

1. **Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting**
   - *Test*: Plot the proportion of resolved cases as a function of days since case start. Evaluate October breach rates only on the subset of cases that resolved quickly.
   - *Expected result*: Under the censoring hypothesis, October's true breach rate will project much higher once adjusted for delayed cases. If October genuinely improved, the mature subset would show low breaches.
2. **Recomputation after activity-label normalization and duplicate cleaning**
   - *Test*: Re-run the April feature pipeline using the correct mapping (`Diagnostic Assessment Completed` -> `Initial Diagnosis Completed`) and duplicate removal.
   - *Expected result*: Under the pipeline-failure hypothesis, April's AUROC and Brier scores should instantly recover to March levels.
3. **Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source, and month**
   - *Test*: Plot calibration curves specifically for V3 cases in August vs March, and Partner Z vs Standard cases in September.
   - *Expected result*: Under concept drift, V3 will be severely under-predicted in August. Under population shift, Partner Z will show different calibration curves than standard cases if unobserved covariates dictate risk.
4. **Comparison of process variants and DFG/performance-DFG edges**
   - *Test*: Extract the median time for `Order Part -> Part Received` vs `Diagnosis -> Remote Fix`.
   - *Expected result*: Under supplier-driven process drift, only the V3-specific edges will exponentially degrade (seen in Jul-Oct), while V2 edges remain flat.
5. **Tests of whether the outcome relationship conditional on the same path changed**
   - *Test*: Compare `P(Breach | V2)` and `P(Breach | V3)` across months.
   - *Expected result*: Validates that June was merely process drift (`P(Breach|V2)` unchanged) while August was concept drift (`P(Breach|V3)` spiked).
6. **Population reweighting or transport-style checks for Partner Z**
   - *Test*: Reweight standard September cases to match the observable V1/V2/V3 distribution of Partner Z, then compare breach rates.
   - *Expected result*: If reweighted standard cases still breach less than Partner Z cases, it confirms unobserved covariates (rural distance, device age) are impacting performance.
7. **Feature missingness and lineage audits**
   - *Test*: Check the distribution of the "number of events before diagnosis" feature in April/May versus March.
   - *Expected result*: Confirms that the May patch failed to backfill cached historical states.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

- **Immediate Containment**: Apply the label mapping fix and backfill all cached features for April and May. Block October data from all metrics and model updates due to extreme censoring.
- **Short-term Recalibration**: Recalibrate the model specifically for V3 cases (or apply a heuristic risk buffer) to account for the ongoing supplier delays that began in August.
- **Feature Repair**: Ingest "rural travel distance" and "device age" into the feature store to address the Partner Z population shift.
- **Full Retraining**:
  - *Safe months*: January–March, June, July.
  - *Unsafe months*: April–May (pipeline corruption), October (immature labels).
  - *Decision*: A full retrain is not recommended until Partner Z features are engineered and labels mature. However, a segment-specific model or variant-specific ensemble (separate models for V1, V2, V3) is highly justified because V2 (remote) and V3 (parts) exhibit entirely distinct feature importances, delay causes, and population distributions.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

The architecture separates data quality, process changes, and label maturity from true predictive drift via directed alert dependencies.

**Monitors:**
1. *Label-Maturity Monitor*: Alerts if % of cases with final labels < 95%. **Dependency:** Halts all automatic retraining and suppresses false "breach rate drop" performance alerts (protecting against October-style censoring).
2. *Data-Quality Monitor*: Tracks feature missingness (e.g., queue features) and unmapped variants. **Dependency:** Halts inference and retraining (protecting against April-style failures).
3. *Process-Variant & Performance-DFG Monitor*: Tracks V1/V2/V3 distributions and critical edge median times (`Order Part -> Part Received`).
4. *Population Monitor*: Tracks divergence of input features to catch source shifts like Partner Z.
5. *Predictive & Calibration Monitor*: Tracks stratified Brier score and AUROC per variant.

**Monthly process-evidence extraction code:**

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.log.util import dataframe_utils

def extract_monthly_process_evidence(csv_path):
    # Read and format dataframe
    df = pd.read_csv(csv_path)
    df = dataframe_utils.convert_timestamp_columns_in_df(df)
    
    # Normalize the renamed diagnosis labels before feature extraction
    df['activity'] = df['activity'].replace({'Diagnostic Assessment Completed': 'Initial Diagnosis Completed'})
    
    # Duplicate-handling strategy: sort by case and time, drop consecutive identical activities
    df = df.sort_values(by=['case_id', 'timestamp'])
    df = df.loc[df['activity'].shift() != df['activity']]
    
    # Format for PM4Py
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id='case_id', activity_key='activity', timestamp_key='timestamp')
    
    # Cohorts: Case-start (for outcome alignment) and Event-calendar (for pipeline incident detection)
    case_starts = df[df['activity'] == 'Case Created'].groupby('case_id')['timestamp'].min().to_dict()
    df['case_start_month'] = df['case_id'].map(case_starts).dt.to_period('M')
    df['event_month'] = df['timestamp'].dt.to_period('M')
    
    summaries = {}
    # Iterate over case-start cohorts for performance and DFG analysis
    for month in sorted(df['case_start_month'].dropna().unique()):
        month_df = df[df['case_start_month'] == month]
        if month_df.empty:
            continue
            
        # Discover variants to check for process drift
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(month_df)
        
        # Discover performance DFG to monitor supplier delays
        perf_dfg, perf_start, perf_end = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(month_df)
        
        # Extract temporal features to get case durations
        temporal_features = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(month_df)
        
        # Safely extract target edge performance (default to 0 if edge didn't occur)
        part_edge_time = perf_dfg.get(('Order Part', 'Part Received'), 0)
        remote_edge_time = perf_dfg.get(('Initial Diagnosis Completed', 'Remote Fix'), 0)
        
        summaries[str(month)] = {
            'cohort': 'case_start',
            'num_cases': len(month_df['case_id'].unique()),
            'unique_variants': len(variants),
            'median_order_to_part_received_sec': part_edge_time,
            'median_diagnosis_to_remote_sec': remote_edge_time,
            'avg_case_duration_sec': temporal_features['case_duration'].mean() if not temporal_features.empty else 0
        }
        
    return summaries
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

1. **Retraining on immature labels (October)**
   - *Failure mode*: Treating October's low observed breach rate (0.16) as ground truth will cause the model to learn that long, delayed cases do not breach, artificially lowering its risk predictions and missing real SLA violations.
   - *Mitigation*: Implement strict label-maturity cutoffs. Never evaluate or retrain on a cohort until >95% of its cases have reached a terminal state.
2. **Calibrating around a pipeline bug (April)**
   - *Failure mode*: Retraining on April data would force the model to map missing queue features and `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` to breach probabilities, baking a transient IT bug into the model's logic.
   - *Mitigation*: Separate data-quality alerts from performance alerts. Do not retrain if pipeline health checks (e.g., feature missingness > 5%) fail; backfill features instead.
3. **Ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept drift (August–September)**
   - *Failure mode*: Assuming the August degradation is just "data noise", resulting in systemic under-prediction of V3 breach risk during the supplier crisis.
   - *Mitigation*: Use stratified calibration monitors to detect segment-specific deterioration, and apply short-term heuristic overrides or segment recalibration for V3.
4. **Attributing Partner Z's population shift to unfair model behavior without stratified evidence**
   - *Failure mode*: Observing higher breach rates for Partner Z and concluding the model is inherently biased against them, leading to naive threshold adjustments.
   - *Mitigation*: Perform causal transport/reweighting tests. Recognize that Partner Z inherently uses the riskier V3 process more often and operates in rural areas. Mitigate by engineering the missing features (rural distance, device age) rather than arbitrarily bumping scores.
