## 1. Analytical assumptions

To analyze this synthetic order-fulfilment process without falling into single-perspective dogmatism, we must explicitly define our analytical terminology:

*   **Case notion:** The operational definition of an analytical unit (a "case") around which event logs are projected, transforming a multi-entity execution graph into a collection of sequential traces.
*   **Convergence:** The structural phenomenon where multiple distinct objects, workflows, or process paths merge into a shared operation, event, or physical container (e.g., multiple orders sharing package P3 or delivery D2).
*   **Divergence:** The structural phenomenon where a single operational entity branches out into multiple distinct downstream paths, objects, or lifecycle steps (e.g., order O1 splitting across packages P1 and P2).
*   **Copied shared events:** In traditional OCEL flattening, when an event is linked to multiple objects of the target type (e.g., `Dispatch` e16 linked to O1, O2, and O3), flattening replicates that event into the trace of *each* involved object. We treat this simultaneously as **evidence** of multi-entity collaboration and as **duplication** that inflates event frequencies and alters process discovery metrics.
*   **Analytical transformations and relation propagation:** By default, we adhere strictly to the direct E2O relation set provided in the log. However, we permit explicit O2O relation propagation (e.g., mapping events connected to Items I21 directly to Order O2 via the `contains` relation) only as a comparative diagnostic tool to evaluate how hidden relational structures distort flattened views.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

1.  **Cross-order package convergence (Order Flattening):**
    *   *Affected flattening:* Order.
    *   *Specific events/objects:* Events e13 (`Pack Package` for P3 involving O2, O3, I21, I31) and e16 (`Dispatch` for D2 involving O1, O2, O3).
    *   *Distortion:* In the Order DFG, O2 and O3 become tightly coupled. The DFG will show artificial concurrency or sequential ordering loops between O2 and O3 because e13 and e16 are duplicated into both traces. This creates a false appearance that O2's packaging directly depends on O3's items, inflating apparent process complexity.
    *   *Nature of effect:* **Harmful** for assessing individual order performance; informative only if looking for warehouse batching patterns.

2.  **Cross-order package convergence (Item Flattening):**
    *   *Affected flattening:* Item.
    *   *Specific events/objects:* Package P4 containing items I32 (from O3) and I41 (from O4) during event e14 (`Pack Package`) and e17 (`Dispatch`).
    *   *Distortion:* Items I32 and I41 share identical timestamps for packing and dispatch, forcing their individual item traces into lockstep synchronization. An analyst might incorrectly infer that item I32 cannot be packed until item I41 is ready, creating a phantom dependency.
    *   *Nature of effect:* **Harmful** and misleading regarding true item-level workflow independence.

3.  **Consolidated delivery entangling multiple orders (Delivery Flattening vs Order Flattening):**
    *   *Affected flattening:* Order.
    *   *Specific events/objects:* Delivery D2 carrying P2 (for O1) and P3 (for O2, O3) during e16 (`Dispatch`) and e24 (`Confirm Delivery`).
    *   *Distortion:* When flattening on Order, O1, O2, and O3 all inherit events e16 and e24. This injects shared synchronization barriers into the middle and end of O1, O2, and O3 lifecycles. Performance metrics for O1 will show an artificial delay in delivery confirmation caused by waiting for O2/O3 items consolidated in D2.
    *   *Nature of effect:* **Harmful** performance bias, distorting true lead times for individual customer orders.

4.  **Consolidated invoicing merging orders (Order Flattening):**
    *   *Affected flattening:* Order.
    *   *Specific events/objects:* Invoice V2 billing both O2 and O3 in e19 (`Issue Invoice`) and e22 (`Record Payment`).
    *   *Distortion:* O2 and O3 share identical invoicing and initial payment timestamps. In an Order DFG, this creates a false structural bond where O2 and O3 appear to proceed through financial settlement as a joint case, masking independent order values and payment statuses.
    *   *Nature of effect:* **Context-dependent**; helpful if evaluating account-level billing, harmful if measuring individual order revenue realization.

5.  **Partial payments creating multi-step settlement loops (Invoice Flattening):**
    *   *Affected flattening:* Invoice.
    *   *Specific events/objects:* Invoice V2 receiving partial payment M2 (e22) followed by completion payment M3 (e25).
    *   *Distortion:* In the Invoice view, V2 exhibits a unique variant: `Issue Invoice` $\rightarrow$ `Record Payment` (partial) $\rightarrow$ `Record Payment` (complete). V1 and V3 show a single-step payment variant. When aggregated, this introduces an apparent rework or iteration loop into the generic invoice lifecycle that does not apply to all invoices.
    *   *Nature of effect:* **Informative** for financial risk analysis, but inflates apparent payment process variability.

6.  **Near-singleton lifecycle truncation (Delivery Flattening):**
    *   *Affected flattening:* Delivery.
    *   *Specific events/objects:* Delivery D1, D2, D3 participating only in `Dispatch` (e15–e17) and `Confirm Delivery` (e23, e24, e27).
    *   *Distortion:* The resulting process model for Delivery consists of a trivial two-activity trace (`Dispatch` $\rightarrow$ `Confirm Delivery`). All upstream context (Order creation, item addition, packing) and downstream context (invoicing, payment) is completely wiped out, leading to extreme lifecycle truncation.
    *   *Nature of effect:* **Harmful** if mistaken for an end-to-end process; informative only for carrier transit duration.

7.  **Near-singleton lifecycle truncation (Payment Flattening):**
    *   *Affected flattening:* Payment.
    *   *Specific events/objects:* Payments M1, M2, M3, M4 participating only in `Record Payment` (e21, e22, e25, e26).
    *   *Distortion:* The trace length for any Payment case is exactly 1. Process discovery algorithms fail entirely to produce a DFG, treating payments as disconnected atomic events rather than a process.
    *   *Nature of effect:* **Harmful** and analytically dead-end for traditional sequence mining.

8.  **Order splitting across packages (Item Flattening):**
    *   *Affected flattening:* Item.
    *   *Specific events/objects:* Order O1 containing items I11 (packed in P1, dispatched via D1) and I12 (packed in P2, dispatched via D2).
    *   *Distortion:* When viewed from the Item perspective, I11 and I12 diverge immediately after `Add Item` (e05, e06). I11 is processed via P1/D1 (e11, e15, e23), while I12 is processed via P2/D2 (e12, e16, e24). An analyst examining Item traces sees two completely disparate execution paths for items belonging to the same order, obscuring the order's unified intent.
    *   *Nature of effect:* **Context-dependent**—essential for warehouse item-path analysis, but obscures customer-facing order status.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

*   **Which flattened logs should still be created?**
    *   **Order-flattened log:** Essential for customer-centric SLA tracking, lead-time analysis from order creation to order closing, and top-level revenue auditing.
    *   **Package-flattened log:** Useful for warehouse operations, box-utilization studies, and carrier batching efficiency.
*   **Which questions can each flattened log answer safely?**
    *   *Order log:* "What is the total lead time from order creation (e01) to order close (e28)?" and "What percentage of orders experience delayed invoicing?"
    *   *Package log:* "How many packages contain items from multiple orders (e.g., P3, P4)?" and "What is the transit time from packing to delivery confirmation for a given package?"
*   **Which questions require advanced OC approaches?**
    *   *Cross-entity dependency questions:* "Which specific orders were delayed because delivery D2 was held up by a missing item in package P3?" requires an **OC-DFG** or **Object Transition Graph (OTG)**.
    *   *Resource and container congestion:* "How do shared packages (P3, P4) and consolidated invoices (V2) create bottlenecks across multiple customer orders?" requires **Object Interaction Summaries** and **ET–OT graphs**.
*   **How to reconcile contradictory findings:**
    *   When Order flattening suggests a long lead time (inflated by shared delivery D2 waiting on O3), but Package flattening shows P1 finished rapidly, we reconcile by inspecting the **OCEL Event-To-Object (ET–OT) graph**. We separate shared resource bottlenecks from core workflow steps, ensuring that multi-object entanglement is recognized as an operational interaction rather than a delay inherent to the single order.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Position: B. Use a primary lead object plus explicitly secondary lifecycles.**

*   **Decision criteria:** A process analysis must prioritize the primary business entity that initiates and terminates the commercial relationship (the customer **Order**), while formally retaining secondary object types (**Package**, **Invoice**, **Delivery**) to contextualize operational and financial entanglements without corrupting the primary workflow spine.
*   **Strongest supporting evidence:** Orders O1–O4 initiate the process via `Create Order` (e01–e04) and terminate it via `Close Order` (e28–e31). They are the only object type participating across the entire breadth of the lifecycle—from commercial intake, through physical fulfillment, to financial settlement.
*   **Strongest counterargument:** Forcing Order as the sole lead object introduces severe distortions: shared events (e.g., dispatch D2 in e16) artificially couple independent orders (O1, O2, O3) into synchronized pseudo-cases, masking the true operational independence of warehouse packing and carrier transport.
*   **Residual risk:** Analysts may still misinterpret cross-order synchronization artifacts in the primary Order DFG as mandatory business rules (e.g., believing O2 *must* ship with O3), leading to flawed capacity planning.
*   **Conditions under which you would change your decision:** If the primary business objective shifted entirely from customer order fulfillment to warehouse throughput and box-packing optimization, the lead object would need to transition to **Package**.

## 6. PM4Py analysis outline

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd

# Assume 'ocel' is a pre-loaded OCEL 2.0 object in the workspace
print("--- OCEL Summary ---")
print(ocel)

print("\n--- Activity and Object-Type Frequencies ---")

print("Object Type Counts:", object_type_counts)

print("\n--- Flattening on Order ---")

# 2. Flattening on Package (Secondary Logistics View)
print("\n--- Flattening on Package ---")
flattened_package_log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type="Package")
package_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(flattened_package_log)
print("Package DFG discovered with", len(package_dfg), "edges.")

# 3. Discovering Log Skeleton for Orders
print("\n--- Log Skeleton (Order) ---")
skeleton = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(flattened_order_log)
print("Log skeleton constraints extracted.")

# 4. Object-Centric Process Discovery
print("\n--- Object-Centric Process Discovery (OC-DFG) ---")
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
print("OC-DFG discovered successfully.")

# 5. Object Transition Graph (OTG) & ET-OT Graph
print("\n--- Object Transition Graph & ET-OT Graph ---")
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
```

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

1.  **Down-weighting or removing copied shared events:**
    *   *Method:* Re-run Order flattening after down-weighting or filtering out events linked to more than two orders (such as multi-order dispatches e16 and multi-order invoices e19).
    *   *Goal:* Measure how much apparent process complexity in the Order DFG is driven by shared logistics and billing resources versus true order-specific steps.
2.  **Comparing direct E2O relations with propagated O2O relations:**
    *   *Method:* Construct an augmented event log where Item-level events (or Package-level events) are explicitly propagated up to Orders via the O2O `contains` and `packs` relations before flattening.
    *   *Goal:* Evaluate whether explicit relation propagation reveals hidden bottlenecks that direct E2O flattening misses.
3.  **Sensitivity to lead object selection:**
    *   *Method:* Compare process metrics (mean case duration, activity frequencies) derived from Order flattening versus Package flattening.
    *   *Goal:* Quantify the performance bias introduced when shifting the analytical lens from commercial fulfillment to physical logistics.
4.  **Comparing frequency metrics across structural views:**
    *   *Method:* Contrast event-frequency metrics, unique-object frequency metrics, and total-object frequency metrics within the OC-DFG.
    *   *Goal:* Ensure that high-volume orders or heavily shared packages (like P3) do not distort aggregate workload and throughput calculations.
