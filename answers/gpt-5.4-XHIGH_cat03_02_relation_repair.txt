## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model
I use the following evidence model.

- Hard invariant: any added relation must reference an existing event or object already present in the dataset; I do not invent new events or objects.
- Hard invariant: O2O qualifiers are directional, so I only add edges in the stored direction such as `Delivery carries Package` or `Invoice bills Order`.
- Hard invariant: a repair should not imply an impossible local temporal inversion, for example a payment attached to an invoice that would have to occur before the invoice exists.
- Strong default: `Create Order` should relate to exactly one Order, `Add Item` to one Order and one Item, `Pack Item` to one Item and one Package, `Dispatch Package` to one Package and one Delivery, `Issue Invoice` to one Invoice and one or more Orders, and `Record Payment` to one Payment and usually one Invoice.
- Strong default: explicit exception clauses matter. The prompt allows item-only `Pack Item` rows from scanner outage, multi-package dispatch, customer-level delivery confirmation, and unapplied cash. Those exceptions prevent me from forcing a single completion when evidence is weak.
- Weak heuristic: identifier similarity is informative. `PK8-2` points toward `PK-8`; `N-421?` points toward `DEL-Y` with carrier ref `N-421`; `N-42*` is compatible with both `N-420` and `N-421`.
- Weak heuristic: time, customer, route, amount, and the existing O2O motifs are supportive context. Same-day ordering, `customer=C17`, route `NORTH`, and the financial split `100 + 130 = 230` all raise plausibility, but none of them proves a relation alone.
- Evidence against a repair: arithmetic equality is not proof because fees, discounts, taxes, or prior balances may exist; wildcard or truncated identifiers weaken uniqueness; and a relation added only to “complete” every object lifecycle should be penalized.
- Evidence against a repair: I do not count a mirrored O2O edge as independent confirmation if it is only the object-level restatement of an E2O repair I already assumed.

I combine evidence in layers. Hard invariants first eliminate impossible or schema-breaking proposals. Strong defaults then set the expected cardinality, but explicit exceptions can keep a candidate unresolved instead of rejected. Weak heuristics accumulate confidence when they are independent: identifier match is strongest for the packaging and dispatch ambiguities, while amount and customer are strongest for invoice and payment. Existing O2O structure matters because the log already uses `Order contains Item`, `Package packs Item`, `Delivery carries Package`, `Invoice bills Order`, and `Payment settles Invoice`; that makes analogous missing relations plausible. When two repairs explain the data similarly well, I prefer the one that adds fewer assumptions, and if neither is clearly better I leave the relation unresolved.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles
Bundle A: conservative/minimum-change repair.

- `ev08` and `PK-8`: add `ev08 -> PK-8` and `PK-8 packs IT-B1`.
- `ev10` and `DEL-X/DEL-Y`: add `ev10 -> DEL-Y` and `DEL-Y carries PK-8`; do not also attach `DEL-X`.
- `ev11` and the delivery objects: leave delivery relations unresolved and keep only the observed `ORD-A` relation. The wildcard `N-42*` and the portal semantics are too weak for a unique delivery repair.
- `INV-44` and `ORD-B`: add `ev12 -> ORD-B` and `INV-44 bills ORD-B`, but keep both provisional.
- `PAY-2` and `INV-44`: add `ev14 -> INV-44` and `PAY-2 settles INV-44`, also provisional.

This bundle is coherent without pretending the portal confirmation is fully understood. `PK-8` becomes a plausible multi-item package containing `IT-A2` and `IT-B1`; `DEL-Y` becomes the likely second delivery because its carrier reference fits `ev10`; `INV-44` becomes a likely combined invoice for both orders; and `PAY-1` plus `PAY-2` become a likely two-part settlement of that invoice. `ORD-B` still lacks explicit delivery confirmation, and `DEL-Y` lacks explicit `Confirm Delivery`; I treat that incompleteness as acceptable rather than a defect to be repaired away.

Bundle B: plausible alternative interpretation.

- Handle `ev08` and `PK-8` exactly as in Bundle A.
- Handle `ev10` and `DEL-X/DEL-Y` exactly as in Bundle A.
- For `ev11`, add `DEL-X`, `DEL-Y`, and `ORD-B`. The event is interpreted as one customer-portal batch acknowledgment for customer `C17`, with `N-42*` being a lossy reference covering both `N-420` and `N-421`.
- Handle `INV-44`/`ORD-B` and `PAY-2`/`INV-44` exactly as in Bundle A.

This alternative yields a tidier end-to-end story: both orders are created, itemized, dispatched, batch-confirmed, invoiced together, and paid in two parts. It is plausible because both deliveries are `NORTH`, both orders belong to the same customer, and the portal field is customer-centric. It is weaker than Bundle A because a single `Confirm Delivery` event would then carry four business objects, and the extra `ORD-B` attachment is driven by customer-level symmetry rather than object-specific evidence.

I do not elevate the `ev10 -> DEL-X` bulk-dispatch interpretation to a main bundle. It is possible, but it explains less cleanly because it leaves the well-matching `DEL-Y` object looking like an unused or canceled delivery record.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit
- Order: under Bundle A, `ORD-A` has a plausible lifecycle `Create Order -> Add Item(s) -> Confirm Delivery -> Issue Invoice`; `ORD-B` has `Create Order -> Add Item -> Issue Invoice`. `ORD-B` is not orphaned, but it remains only partially completed because no delivery confirmation is forced onto it. The only suspicious one-to-many pattern is one invoice billing two orders, which is domain-consistent but still provisional.
- Item: all three items are related to exactly one observed `Add Item` event and one `Pack Item` event after repair. No item is orphaned. No item becomes many-to-many across packages or orders.
- Package: `PK-7` stays simple. `PK-8` becomes a two-item package with two pack scans followed by one dispatch, which is a plausible package lifecycle. The only mildly unusual feature is that one package would contain items from two different orders; that can happen in consolidated shipping, so I treat it as unusual but legitimate.
- Delivery: `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y` each receive one dispatch in Bundle A. `DEL-Y` lacks explicit confirmation, but that is a partial lifecycle, not an orphan. If I forced `ev11` onto a delivery object, downstream service-completion metrics would change materially, so I leave that ambiguity open.
- Invoice: `INV-44` becomes a one-invoice-to-two-orders object. That cardinality is plausible. It is supported by same-customer and amount evidence, but there is no direct invoice-line evidence for `ORD-B`, so the support is broad rather than sharp.
- Payment: `PAY-1` and `PAY-2` each keep exactly one `Record Payment` event. Linking both to `INV-44` yields a plausible many-payments-to-one-invoice settlement pattern. Unapplied cash remains a live alternative only for `PAY-2`.

Support is not equally independent across all repaired pairs. `ev08 -> PK-8` and `PK-8 packs IT-B1` are mostly two representations of the same scanner clue. Likewise, `ev12 -> ORD-B` and `INV-44 bills ORD-B`, and `ev14 -> INV-44` and `PAY-2 settles INV-44`, come from the same financial evidence family. By contrast, `ev10 -> DEL-Y` has somewhat more independent support because the event’s `external_ref` and the object’s `carrier_ref` are separate fields. I therefore do not treat the parallel O2O additions as extra proof; they are coherent modeling choices once the E2O hypothesis is accepted.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects
- OC-DFG: the unrepaired log understates the package and invoice continuations. `PK-8` only shows one `Pack Item` before `Dispatch Package`, `INV-44` only shows one `Record Payment`, and `ORD-B` stops at `Add Item`. Bundle A adds the fragment `Pack Item -> Pack Item -> Dispatch Package` for `PK-8`, adds `Add Item -> Issue Invoice` for `ORD-B`, and adds the invoice-level chain `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment -> Record Payment`. Bundle B adds another major fragment: `Confirm Delivery` becomes a successor on both deliveries and on `ORD-B`, giving `ORD-B` the path `Add Item -> Confirm Delivery -> Issue Invoice` instead of `Add Item -> Issue Invoice`.
- OTG: the unrepaired OTG misses four likely edges: `PK-8 packs IT-B1`, `DEL-Y carries PK-8`, `INV-44 bills ORD-B`, and `PAY-2 settles INV-44`. Bundle A inserts all four. Bundle B has the same OTG as Bundle A because its extra assumptions are E2O-only around `ev11`.
- ET–OT graph: in the unrepaired log, `Confirm Delivery` touches only object type `Order`. Bundle A leaves that unchanged. Bundle B adds `Confirm Delivery -> Delivery`, which is a substantial semantic shift because a delivery-completion relation becomes explicit. `Dispatch Package -> Delivery` also goes from one delivery-related event in the unrepaired log to two in Bundles A and B.
- Flattening on `Order`: unrepaired, `ORD-B` looks like a short case with `Create Order, Add Item`. Bundle A adds `Issue Invoice` to `ORD-B`, bringing it closer to `ORD-A` but still not matching it. Bundle B also adds `Confirm Delivery` to `ORD-B`, making the two order cases nearly equivalent except that `ORD-A` has two `Add Item` events and `ORD-B` only one.
- Flattening on `Package`: unrepaired, `PK-8` has one `Pack Item` and one `Dispatch Package`. Bundles A and B make `PK-8` a repeated-pack case with two `Pack Item` events before dispatch, which can create a visible self-loop or repeated activity fragment in the package-level view.
- Flattening on `Delivery`: unrepaired, only `DEL-X` has a case at all. Bundle A creates a second delivery case for `DEL-Y` with `Dispatch Package` only. Bundle B makes both deliveries look completed by appending `Confirm Delivery` to each.
- Flattening on `Invoice` and `Payment`: `Invoice` changes materially in Bundles A and B because `INV-44` becomes shared by two orders and two payment events, generating `Record Payment -> Record Payment` on the invoice case. `Payment` flattenings stay single-event cases, but the object-event relation count changes because `PAY-2` becomes invoice-linked.
- Activity frequencies: event counts do not change across bundles because no new events are invented. What changes is relation volume. Bundle A adds four E2O relations and four O2O relations; Bundle B adds three more E2O relations on top of that for `ev11`. Unique objects touched by `Dispatch Package`, `Issue Invoice`, `Record Payment`, and possibly `Confirm Delivery` therefore increase even though activity counts stay fixed.
- Equivalent-execution clustering around `Order`: unrepaired, `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` likely fall into different variants because `ORD-B` has no invoicing or confirmation. Bundle A pulls `ORD-B` closer by giving it invoicing, but it still remains a shorter variant. Bundle B may collapse them into one near-cluster, which is analytically convenient but risky because that regularity comes from the most uncertain repair in the dataset.

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline
```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Preserve originals
events_orig = events.copy(deep=True)
objects_orig = objects.copy(deep=True)
relations_orig = relations.copy(deep=True)
o2o_orig = o2o.copy(deep=True)

# Bundle A selected additions
selected_e2o = pd.DataFrame(
    [
        {"ocel:eid": "ev08", "ocel:oid": "PK-8",   "ocel:qualifier": None},
        {"ocel:eid": "ev10", "ocel:oid": "DEL-Y",  "ocel:qualifier": None},
        {"ocel:eid": "ev12", "ocel:oid": "ORD-B",  "ocel:qualifier": None},
        {"ocel:eid": "ev14", "ocel:oid": "INV-44", "ocel:qualifier": None},
    ]
)

selected_o2o = pd.DataFrame(
    [
        {"ocel:oid": "PK-8",   "ocel:oid_2": "IT-B1", "ocel:qualifier": "packs"},
        {"ocel:oid": "DEL-Y",  "ocel:oid_2": "PK-8",  "ocel:qualifier": "carries"},
        {"ocel:oid": "INV-44", "ocel:oid_2": "ORD-B", "ocel:qualifier": "bills"},
        {"ocel:oid": "PAY-2",  "ocel:oid_2": "INV-44", "ocel:qualifier": "settles"},
    ]
)

relations_rep = pd.concat([relations_orig, selected_e2o], ignore_index=True)
o2o_rep = pd.concat([o2o_orig, selected_o2o], ignore_index=True)

# Referential integrity
valid_event_ids = set(events_orig["ocel:eid"])
valid_object_ids = set(objects_orig["ocel:oid"])

missing_rel_eids = set(relations_rep["ocel:eid"]) - valid_event_ids
missing_rel_oids = set(relations_rep["ocel:oid"]) - valid_object_ids
missing_o2o_src = set(o2o_rep["ocel:oid"]) - valid_object_ids
missing_o2o_tgt = set(o2o_rep["ocel:oid_2"]) - valid_object_ids

if missing_rel_eids or missing_rel_oids or missing_o2o_src or missing_o2o_tgt:
    raise ValueError(
        {
            "missing_rel_eids": missing_rel_eids,
            "missing_rel_oids": missing_rel_oids,
            "missing_o2o_src": missing_o2o_src,
            "missing_o2o_tgt": missing_o2o_tgt,
        }
    )

# Duplicate checks
if relations_rep.duplicated(subset=["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"]).any():
    dup_rows = relations_rep[
        relations_rep.duplicated(subset=["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"], keep=False)
    ]
    raise ValueError(f"Duplicate E2O rows detected:\n{dup_rows}")

if o2o_rep.duplicated(subset=["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"]).any():
    dup_rows = o2o_rep[
        o2o_rep.duplicated(subset=["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"], keep=False)
    ]
    raise ValueError(f"Duplicate O2O rows detected:\n{dup_rows}")

# Construct/update OCEL object.
# Version-dependent: some PM4Py versions accept this exact constructor, others require extra kwargs.
from pm4py.objects.ocel.obj import OCEL
try:
    ocel = OCEL(events=events_orig, objects=objects_orig, relations=relations_rep, o2o=o2o_rep)
except TypeError:
    ocel = OCEL(
        events=events_orig,
        objects=objects_orig,
        relations=relations_rep,
        o2o=o2o_rep,
        object_changes=pd.DataFrame(),
    )

# Discovery
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

# Flattening
flat_order = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Order")
flat_delivery = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Delivery")

# Optional additional flattenings for inspection
flat_package = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Package")
flat_invoice = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Invoice")

# Important methodological note:
# Do not call pm4py.ocel_o2o_enrichment and then treat its output as proof
# of the business semantics. Enrichment may suggest structural links, but it
# does not resolve whether this dataset truly intended the repair.
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings
- Treating `230 = 130 + 100` as proof, rather than evidence, would manufacture a certain multi-order invoice even though fees, credits, or missing orders are possible.
- Treating `PAY-2` as certainly settling `INV-44` would erase the live alternative of unapplied cash or a missing second invoice, distorting receivables analysis.
- Forcing `ev11` onto `DEL-X` alone would create a false one-delivery confirmation path and hide the equally plausible customer-level or multi-delivery reading.
- Forcing `ev11` onto both deliveries and both orders without qualification would make the process look cleaner and more synchronized than the evidence supports, especially in order clustering and service-level views.
- Adding both `DEL-X carries PK-8` and `DEL-Y carries PK-8` would invent a split-carrier handoff that the dataset never suggests.
- Adding reverse O2O edges such as `PK-8 carries DEL-Y` or `ORD-B billed_by INV-44` would violate the stated directionality and corrupt the OTG semantics.
- Inferring `Order -> Package` or `Order -> Delivery` edges just because paths can be traversed through items and packages would densify the graph with unsupported shortcuts.
- Counting `PK-8 packs IT-B1`, `INV-44 bills ORD-B`, or `PAY-2 settles INV-44` as independent confirmation after first assuming the paired E2O repairs would double-weight a single clue family and overstate certainty.
