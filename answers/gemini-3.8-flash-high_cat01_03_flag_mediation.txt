### Mediation Framework Justification
Standard Baron-Kenny or Pearl Natural Direct Effects (NDE) and Natural Indirect Effects (NIE) are theoretically invalid for this queueing process for three reasons:
1. **Exposure-Induced Mediator-Outcome Confounding:** The upstream intervention (priority triage lane $A_{\text{lane}}$) dictates the exact calendar timestamp at which a case enters the manual review queue. The prevailing queue backlog at that calendar time acts as a time-varying confounder of the relationship between downstream review/escalation and total cycle time that is itself caused by the exposure. This violates Pearl's cross-world independence assumption ($Y(a, m) \perp M(a^*) \mid C$).
2. **Untestable Cross-World Counterfactuals:** Standard mediation requires counterfactual quantities such as $Y_i(a, M_i(a^*))$—the outcome if a case were flagged but its manual review routing were set to what it would have been if unflagged. In an operational queueing environment, these nested counterfactuals cannot be physically realized.
3. **Severe Interference (SUTVA Violation):** In shared-server queueing networks, individual potential outcomes $Y_i(a)$ are undefined because unit $i$'s waiting time depends on the treatment status of all preceding and concurrent cases in the system.

Consequently, we adopt the **Separable Effects Framework** (Robins, Richardson, Didelez 2020) and **Interventional Indirect/Direct Effects** (VanderWeele, Vansteelandt, Robins 2014):
- We decompose the compound flag policy bundle into physically separable actions: priority lane assignment ($A_{\text{lane}} \in \{0, 1\}$), review referral mandate ($A_{\text{mandate}} \in \{0, 1\}$), and cognitive visual flag display ($A_{\text{label}} \in \{0, 1\}$).
- For indirect pathways through queueing and routing, we formulate **interventional mediation estimands** that replace deterministic cross-world counterfactuals with population draws from conditional mediator distributions ($G_{M \mid C}$), and **controlled direct effects (CDE)** that set downstream routing interventions directly.

### Dynamic Process and Queueing Feedback
Interference arises because manual reviewers (M1–M3) and senior escalation handlers are finite, shared resources serving both flagged and unflagged cases. Fast-tracking flagged cases through triage injects them in rapid succession into the shared manual review queue. This surge increases the queue length and waiting time for subsequent arrivals, violating SUTVA (one case's treatment changes other cases' potential waiting times).

Exposure-induced confounding occurs because the triage acceleration alters the exact clock time at which a case enters the manual review queue. The prevailing queue congestion at that calendar time acts as an intermediate confounder between the downstream review/escalation steps and total cycle time, which is itself causally downstream of the initial flag handling.

Treatment-confounder feedback emerges dynamically: as the manual queue backs up from mandatory flagged referrals, human reviewers alter their downstream behavior (e.g., spending less time per case, changing escalation thresholds, or prioritizing easier unflagged cases), feeding back into further queue delay and differential escalation probabilities across risk strata.

### Explanation of Contrasts and Non-Causality
The observed data show dramatic acceleration at triage: flagged cases start triage in 14.4 minutes versus 148.8 minutes for unflagged cases, and finish triage in 36.0 minutes versus 57.0 minutes. Conversely, massive delays appear downstream: flagged cases wait an average of 390.0 minutes (6.50 hours) in queue for manual review compared to 112.5 minutes for unflagged reviewed cases. Furthermore, 60% of flagged cases escalate into a 420.0-minute senior queue wait, driving their total cycle time to 990.6 minutes (16.51 hours) versus 490.2 minutes (8.17 hours) for unflagged cases.

These contrasts are not automatically causal. First, 100% of flagged cases are subjected to mandatory manual review compared to only 40% of unflagged cases; unflagged cases predominantly bypass review and close immediately after triage. Second, flagged cases carry much higher complexity (5 High, 5 Medium, 0 Low) than unflagged cases (3 High, 3 Medium, 4 Low). Higher underlying complexity directly increases service durations and escalation propensity independently of the flag. Third, cross-case queue congestion creates shared-server spillover.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Does the flag causally increase downstream queue waiting and total cycle time? | Sharp Regression Discontinuity Design (RDD) or local matching around the risk score threshold ($\approx 0.62 - 0.63$) | Local window of `risk_score` ($[0.60, 0.65]$), controlling for `complexity` and `arrival_load` | Continuity of potential outcomes at threshold; no manipulative sorting of risk scores around 0.63 | McCrary density test on `risk_score`; balance test of intake `complexity` across the cutoff | If cases with `risk_score` = 0.64 (flagged) show identical manual review queue times to cases with 0.61 (unflagged), the flag does not cause downstream queue delays |
| How much of the manual queue wait is caused by fast triage vs. system congestion? | Queue-state-aware conditioning using virtual waiting time and queue length at triage completion | Queue backlog (number of waiting cases and active server states for M1–M3) at exact timestamp `triage_done` | No unobserved queue prioritization; service durations are conditionally independent given queue state | Placebo check: regress manual queue wait on flag after conditioning on exact queue length at `triage_done` | If the flag coefficient drops to zero after conditioning on queue depth at triage exit, downstream delay is purely an arrival-schedule congestion artifact |
| What is the causal impact of mandatory review vs. discretionary review? | Randomized Encouragement Design / Stepped-Wedge Policy Rollout of Policy P2 | Stratified by intake `complexity`, shift arrival load, and triage resource lane | Instrument monotonicity (encouragement only increases review); exclusion restriction | Test for balance of baseline covariates across encouragement arms | If removing mandatory review reduces average cycle time by >5 hours without increasing false approvals, the bottleneck is policy-induced |

### Limited Pilot Specification: Policy P2
- **Eligibility:** Newly arriving cases with `risk_score` $\ge 0.63$ (or flag=1) evaluated as **Medium complexity** (estimated $N \approx 50$/week). High-complexity flagged cases remain temporarily mandated to manual review for safety; Low-complexity cases remain unflagged.
- **Randomization / Assignment Rule:** Eligible Medium-complexity flagged cases are randomized 1:1 at intake to **Arm A (Intervention P2)**: routed to priority triage (T1/T2), with triage operators granted full discretion to approve/close directly or refer to manual review; versus **Arm B (Control P0)**: routed to priority triage (T1/T2) with mandatory referral to manual review.
- **Queue-Capacity Guardrail:** If the average waiting time in the shared manual review queue exceeds 4.0 hours over any rolling 4-hour window, triage discretion in Arm A dynamically defaults to direct closure for all cases with `risk_score` $< 0.75$ unless explicit red flags are documented.
- **Primary and Secondary Outcomes:** Primary outcome is total case cycle time ($T_{\text{close}} - T_{\text{arrival}}$). Secondary outcomes include manual review referral rate, escalation rate, manual queue waiting time, and decision error rate (measured via post-hoc blind re-audits).
- **Minimum Process Data Needed:** Case ID, arrival timestamp, triage start/done, assigned triage resource, discretionary referral indicator, manual start/done, review resource, escalation start/done, close timestamp, final outcome, and blind audit error classification.
- **Stopping Conditions:** The pilot will be terminated immediately if the blinded re-audit detects a false-approval rate in Arm A that exceeds Arm B by $> 2.0$ percentage points ($p < 0.05$), or if system-wide queue waiting exceeds 8.0 hours across two consecutive shifts.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden Risk Overriding Process Inefficiency:** If unmeasured latent fraud risk ($U_{\text{underlying\_risk}}$) is concentrated almost exclusively among flagged cases such that bypassing manual review would result in catastrophic financial losses exceeding the holding cost of queue delays, then the policy recommendation to remove mandatory review (P2) would reverse in favor of retaining mandatory review and expanding senior capacity.
2. **Queue Interference Inversion:** If detailed multi-server queue modeling reveals that the apparent manual queue delay of flagged cases (390 minutes) is not caused by the influx of flagged cases, but rather by heavy unflagged cases intermittently blocking reviewers M1–M3 due to head-of-line blocking or non-preemptive priority, then segregating or deprioritizing flagged cases would not alleviate system delays and the operational diagnosis would reverse.
3. **Decision Quality Asymmetry / Defensive Shirking:** If an audit shows that manual reviewers (M1–M3) and triage operators (T1/T2) rely on the flag as a cognitive safeguard and that under discretionary routing (P2) they rubber-stamp borderline high-risk cases without adequate scrutiny (producing an unacceptable surge in false approvals), the recommendation to eliminate mandatory routing would reverse in favor of automated algorithmic gates.
4. **Timestamp Semantics and Batch Logging Distortion:** If empirical investigation proves that `triage_done` does not record the real-time completion of triage but is instead batched into the database at fixed hourly export times, or if `manual_start` represents the time a reviewer opened a case file rather than active investigation (e.g., cases sit "opened" in an active hopper while the reviewer is at lunch or attending meetings), the inferred 6.5-hour manual queue waiting time would be a logging artifact, reversing the queue-bottleneck conclusion.
5. **Positivity and Structural Lack of Overlap:** If intake complexity and risk score are determined by rules that render flag assignment deterministic across specific case categories (e.g., zero overlap between flagged and unflagged cases within High-complexity sub-populations), then any attempt to identify separable or controlled direct effects without the flag is structurally non-positive, invalidating policy extrapolations for High-complexity cases.
6. **Reviewer Behavioral Compensation / Moral Hazard:** If human reviewers, upon seeing the flag suppressed (P1) or made discretionary (P2), actively compensate by increasing their own triage duration on all unflagged cases (e.g., spending 60+ minutes performing exhaustive manual background checks out of fear of missing high-risk items), the total system cycle time across all cases would drastically worsen, reversing the recommendation to loosen triage mandates.

## 9. Final synthesis

Directly observed: Cases carrying the high-risk flag experience markedly accelerated triage (mean wait 14.4 minutes vs. 148.8 minutes; triage service 36.0 minutes vs. 57.0 minutes), yet their total cycle time is roughly double that of unflagged cases (990.6 minutes vs. 490.2 minutes). All flagged cases are routed to manual review, and 60% are escalated to senior review, whereas 60% of unflagged cases bypass review entirely.

Plausible mechanism: The flag operates as a compound organizational policy bundle. Priority triage swiftly pushes flagged cases into the manual review queue. Because manual review is mandatory for flagged cases and reviewer capacity is strictly shared, this routing creates an artificial bottleneck. Downstream queue waiting times surge to 6.5 hours at manual review and 7.0 hours at escalation, completely erasing the 2.2-hour triage gain.

What remains unidentified: Observational data cannot separate whether downstream delays stem from the mandatory review policy, reviewer bias induced by seeing the flag, or unmeasured case complexity and true latent risk. Crucially, SUTVA violations from shared queue interference prevent naive estimation of natural direct and indirect effects.

Most useful next intervention: A randomized encouragement or factorial pilot of Policy P2 (priority triage with discretionary rather than mandatory manual review) for borderline risk cases provides the cleanest identification. It decouples triage acceleration from downstream review mandates while establishing whether discretionary routing preserves decision quality without clogging downstream queues.
