## 1. Decision rule

The rule has five layers. A case must pass the gates (A, B) before it is scored (C, D); scoring then determines the slot order (E). Everything below is deployable as a checklist that a duty manager can run at every 30-minute refresh.

A. Eligibility gates (hard)
- Stage gate: current stage is Triage or Diagnosis Started. Once `Diagnosis Complete` has occurred the case is ineligible for a swarm regardless of p_now (ARR is 0.00–0.03, which cannot cover €450 plus spillover). Ineligible high-risk cases are routed to a separate "non-swarm escalation" path (resolution lead attention, pre-staged customer validation, proactive SLA communication).
- Feasibility gate: time to deadline T must be at least 1.0 h (swarm duration 45 min plus a minimum of resolution/validation time). For 1.0 h ≤ T < 2.0 h the effect range is haircut by 50 percent, because faster diagnosis cannot be converted into a non-breach if there is no time left for resolution work and customer validation.
- Repeat gate: no prior swarm on this case, unless a materially new event has occurred and the duty manager approves (Section 4).
- Data-quality gate: if a live feature feeding the score is imputed or stale, the alert is "unverified". Unverified alerts cannot consume a slot until the feature is checked manually (target ≤ 30 min); until then the case is treated as lying anywhere between p_prev and p_now.

B. Calibration robustification
The prediction is observational and known to be miscalibrated by segment. Convert p_now into an interval p_adj:
- SME: p_adj = p_now + [0.04, 0.09] (mid +0.065).
- Strategic Enterprise: p_adj = p_now − [0.05, 0.12] (mid −0.085).
- Public Sector: p_adj = p_now ± 0.03 (no shift; moderate sample).
- Non-Native-Language Support: no shift, but widen to p_now ± 0.10, and widen the supplied ARR range to [0.7 × lower, 1.1 × upper]. Uncertainty is treated as width, not as a downward shift, so that the group with the least data is not automatically deprioritized (Section 5).
- Effect adjustment: ARR_adj = ARR × min(1, p_adj_mid / p_now). When the model overpredicts, the absolute effect is scaled down in proportion (a smaller baseline risk leaves less to remove). When it underpredicts, the effect is not scaled up (conservative). The supplied ranges are treated as plausible bands, never as known causal effects; decisions must survive the lower bound.

C. Case score
Net direct expected value, computed at lower bound and midpoint:
NDE = ARR_adj × V − €450 − (1 − p_adj) × FA_burden
The false-alarm burden is weighted by the probability the case would not have breached anyway, since that is when the disruption is a pure cost. Non-monetary obligations (regulatory duties, public-sector statutory service levels, equal-service obligations for language-support cases) are not converted into euros; they act as tier overrides (a "duty tier" that can claim a slot ahead of the score order, and the reserve slot policy).

D. Externality hurdle
Each slot imposes a marginal spillover cost on the untreated diagnosis-stage pool:
MSC ≈ 6 × s × V̄_untreated, s = 0.01–0.03 under the base model
With today's pool V̄ ≈ €15k–€25k, MSC ≈ €900–€5,400 per slot, base point ≈ 6 × 0.02 × €20k ≈ €2,400. MSC is recomputed live from the actual untreated pool and current queue depth (congestion-dependent); it rises when queue wait exceeds its normal band.

E. Tiering and prioritization
- Tier A (intervene now): NDE_low > 0 and NDE_mid > MSC, and either T ≤ 4 h or waiting would forfeit eligibility. Order slots by time-criticality first (shortest T, Diagnosis Started before Triage), then by NDE_mid − MSC.
- Tier B (wait with explicit trigger): eligible and NDE_mid > 0 but either NDE_low ≤ 0, or NDE_mid ≤ MSC, or verification pending, or T is long enough that information is worth more than the slot now. Each Tier B case carries a written trigger (p_adj threshold, stage transition, T threshold, or data verification) that promotes it to Tier A automatically.
- Tier C (do not intervene; non-slot actions): eligible but NDE_low ≤ 0 and NDE_mid ≤ MSC, or a suppressed repeat alert.
- Tier D (ineligible): stage or feasibility gate failed.
- Alert persistence and minimum evidence change: an alert stays open until a decision is recorded. A refresh only re-opens a decided case if there is a material change: cumulative Δp_adj ≥ 0.10 since the last decision, a stage transition, a discrete new event (customer artifact, reassignment, dependency failure), T crossing 3 h or 1.5 h, or a change in the data-quality flag. Otherwise the refresh is logged and suppressed, not paged.
- Capacity policy: at most four slots today; one slot is held in reserve until 15:00 for the possible regulatory incident (duty tier), then released to the highest-ranked Tier B case whose trigger has fired or whose score exceeds the hurdle. Swarms are sequential (both specialists deliver one swarm), so the four slots are four 45-minute windows starting 12:00, 12:45, 13:30, and the reserve window.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

Alerts are decisions, not pages. Each case carries an alert state: NEW → DECIDED (intervene / wait-with-trigger / no) → SUPPRESSED (repeat without material change) → RE-ENTRY (material change) → CLOSED (Diagnosis Complete reached, case closed, or swarm delivered).

- Material change (required for re-entry after any decision): cumulative Δp_adj ≥ 0.10 since the last decision, a stage transition, a discrete new event (customer artifact uploaded, reassignment, dependency failure, customer escalation), T crossing 3 h or 1.5 h, or a data-quality flag changing. A drift of a few points across refreshes does not qualify; to filter refresh noise, a probability change must persist across two consecutive refreshes unless it accompanies a discrete event.
- Cool-down: after a "no" decision, 60 minutes (two refreshes) during which repeats are suppressed. After a "wait-with-trigger" decision, the case is not re-paged; it promotes itself only when its trigger fires. After a delivered swarm, the case is closed to further swarms for the day; a second swarm requires a materially new event and duty-manager sign-off, and is logged as an exception.
- Suppression is visible, not silent: the dashboard lists suppressed alerts with the suppression reason and the last decision, so a suppressed case can still be picked up by a human, and suppression rates by segment are auditable (Section 5).
- Escalation: a case whose trigger fires moves up one tier automatically; a Tier B case that generates a second qualifying (material) alert is treated as Tier A if slots are available; a Tier A case denied a slot for capacity reasons is first in line when a slot frees or the reserve releases.
- Re-entry after CLOSED: only via a new incident record.

I-05 versus I-07:
- I-05 is on its third alert, p moved 0.55 → 0.57 (Δ 0.02), no new event, no stage change, T still 5 h. This is a refresh repeat carrying no new information. It is SUPPRESSED: no re-evaluation, no page, no re-ranking. Its standing is unchanged from the first decision (Tier C/B, below hurdle). It re-enters only if Diagnosis Started is reached with T ≤ 3 h, p_adj ≥ 0.75, or a discrete event occurs. Repeated unchanged alerts must not accumulate into priority, otherwise the policy rewards cases simply for sitting in the queue longer.
- I-07 is also on its third alert, and its probability actually fell (0.75 → 0.72), but a new customer diagnostic log was uploaded. That is a discrete material event, and the model may not have incorporated it yet. I-07 therefore RE-ENTERS evaluation. The evaluation result is still "no swarm slot" (1.5 h feasibility haircut, €5k value, NDE ≈ €0–€0.3k against a €2.4k hurdle), but re-entry triggers a concrete non-slot action now: the assigned engineer reads the log immediately, the resolution lead is placed on standby, and if the log identifies the fault, the case is fast-tracked without a swarm. The difference between the two cases is not the outcome "swarm or not" but whether the alert earns a fresh decision at all.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

Value-based prioritization starving lower-value cases. Ranking by ARR × V means SME cases (values €5k–€11k) almost never clear a hurdle set by spillover on a pool that contains €60k–€100k accounts. Safeguards: (i) a capacity quota, at least 25 percent of weekly slots go to cases below the median value, so that the SME queue is served when its effect-per-slot is high (I-05's ARR 0.25–0.40 is the highest in the portfolio); (ii) a concave value transform or cap (e.g. V capped at €40k) in the priority score, so that a single very large account does not dominate; with a €40k cap, I-08's midpoint turns negative and I-04 still ranks first, which shows how much the current ranking depends on uncapped values; (iii) non-slot interventions (senior review, checklist, interpreter) are offered to every Tier B/C case so that "no slot" never means "no action".

Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support. A naive robust rule that acts on lower bounds penalizes the group with the least data, which is a structural disadvantage rather than a risk assessment. Safeguards: uncertainty is represented as interval width, not as a downward shift; the midpoint is used for ranking with the lower bound only as a sign check; NNLS cases are prioritized for the effect-estimation design in Section 6 (an exploration bonus), because every swarm on an NNLS case is also a measurement; interpreter or written-language support is added as a low-cost complementary action; and NNLS false-alarm burden is reviewed separately, because customer disruption may be experienced differently across language groups.

Differential calibration across segments. Uncorrected, the model under-alerts SMEs (underprediction 0.04–0.09) and over-alerts Strategic Enterprise (overprediction 0.05–0.12), which shifts both attention and false-alarm burden by segment. Safeguards: segment-specific shifts applied now (Section 1B), segment-wise recalibration (isotonic or Platt per segment and stage) within the month, and a monthly check that alert rates per segment track realized breach rates per segment within ±0.03.

Selective intervention changing future training labels. Cases that are swarmed and then do not breach are recorded as non-breaches. Retraining on those labels teaches the model that the prefixes it alerted on were low risk, so it alerts less on exactly those prefixes, which reduces interventions, which reduces the effect, which is a self-cancelling loop. Safeguards: every swarm is logged as a treatment flag with timestamp and the decision features at that moment; training either excludes treated cases from the outcome model or includes treatment as a covariate; calibration is assessed on untreated cases and on inverse-probability-weighted treated cases; a small randomized hold-out (Section 6) provides labels that are independent of the policy; and the policy version in force is stored with every case so that drift can be attributed.

Spillover falling systematically on untreated groups. If slots go to high-value cases, the six untreated diagnosis-stage cases that absorb 8–20 minutes of delay per slot are disproportionately SME and Public Sector, so the fairness cost is invisible in the treated population. Safeguards: the untreated pool's breach and delay rates are monitored by segment during swarm hours versus matched non-swarm hours; a cap of two swarms in any four-hour window when queue depth exceeds its normal band; junior backfill on the ordinary diagnosis queue whenever a swarm starts; spillover harm is booked to the segments that bore it in the monthly fairness report, not averaged away.

Additional safeguards: the reserve slot for regulatory incidents is a duty-tier rule, not a value rule, and its usage is reported; all overrides of the score order (duty tier, manager exceptions) are logged with reasons and reviewed weekly.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

Operational monitoring (daily): slots used and unused; swarm start and duration versus the 45-minute assumption; time from alert to decision and from decision to swarm; queue depth and wait time on the diagnosis queue in the 90 minutes after each swarm start versus matched control periods, to test the 8–20 minute delay assumption; reserve-slot outcome (used for a regulatory incident, released, or wasted); frequency of manager overrides and second-swarm exceptions.

Predictive monitoring (weekly and monthly): reliability diagrams, expected calibration error and Brier score per segment and per stage, computed on untreated cases and separately on treated cases; distribution of p_now by stage to detect drift; imputation rate of each live feature with an alarm when more than 5 percent of scored cases have a key feature imputed (the I-06 problem); segment shift recomputed monthly, replaced by segment-wise recalibration when at least 100 labeled untreated cases per segment are available; recalibration triggered when a segment's bias exceeds 0.03 for two consecutive months.

Causal-effect monitoring (continuous, reviewed quarterly): the supplied ARR ranges are priors to be replaced by estimates. Design: (a) randomize the swarm among borderline cases whose score lies within ±20 percent of the hurdle (about 20 percent of borderline cases withheld), which is ethically defensible because those are the cases where the expected net value is close to zero; (b) regression-discontinuity analysis around the score cutoff for non-randomized cases; (c) doubly-robust estimation using the logged decision features for all cases; (d) after every swarm, the specialist records whether the alert reason was confirmed, which estimates the "wrong reason" rate that drives the adverse scenario; (e) spillover estimated by comparing untreated diagnosis-stage cases' delays and breaches in swarm hours against matched non-swarm hours, stratified by queue depth. Targets: per-segment ARR confidence interval within ±0.05, spillover per slot within ±0.01, NNLS estimates reported separately even while wide.

Capacity monitoring (monthly): realized marginal net value of the first through fourth slot; if the third or fourth slot's estimated marginal net value is at or below zero over a month, reduce daily slots or raise the hurdle; if Tier A cases are denied for capacity on more than 20 percent of days, request additional senior capacity or a lighter intervention variant; reserve utilization below 10 percent of days makes the reserve a soft preference released at 14:00, above 50 percent makes it a hard rule and a capacity request.

Fairness monitoring (monthly, published internally): share of slots by segment versus share of eligible Tier A/B alerts by segment (review if the ratio is outside 0.8–1.25); breach rate among untreated cases by segment in swarm hours versus other hours; false-alarm burden realized by segment; suppression rate by segment; NNLS effect and calibration estimates with sample sizes; quota compliance for lower-value cases.

Policy-induced drift detection: track model performance separately on cases scored under each policy version; watch for declining predicted risk on prefixes that were historically swarmed while their untreated counterparts' breach rates hold steady (the label-contamination signature); compare the randomized hold-out's realized breach rate with the model's prediction for the same cases; retrain with treatment indicators and re-validate on untreated data before deployment.

Changing the threshold or allocation rule: changes are pre-registered and made at quarterly review or when a trigger fires, namely estimated ARR for a segment falling outside its supplied range, estimated spillover per untreated case exceeding 0.03, calibration bias above 0.05 in any segment, a fairness ratio outside its band for two consecutive months, or the fourth slot's marginal value clearly positive or negative. Any new rule runs alongside the old one for four weeks (alternating days or randomized shifts) before full adoption, with the same six-section review, so that the allocation rule itself is evaluated with the same discipline as the model.
