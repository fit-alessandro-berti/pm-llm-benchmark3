## 2. Rule Interpretation

Let `h(e)` be the human resolved for event `e` (alias-normalized, or session-attributed for a service account, or undefined), and `t(e)` its timestamp.

- **RR1:** for every case, `h(Draft)` and `h(Approve)` must both be defined and different. It applies at every risk level and has no emergency exception.
- **RR2:** for every case, `h(Implement)` and `h(Verify)` must both be defined and different. It does not prohibit approver = verifier or drafter = implementer; I report those as control weaknesses, not violations.
- **RR3:** for a HIGH-risk case on the normal route, `h(Approve)` must hold the QM role at `t`, or hold a valid QM delegation at `t`. A delegation is valid when all of these hold:
  - `t` lies inside the effective interval;
  - the case scope is inside the delegation scope;
  - the principal is formally absent at `t`, if the delegation requires it;
  - the chain contains no prohibited subdelegation;
  - the delegate personally acted;
  - the delegation was granted before `t`.
- **RR4:** for a HIGH-risk case with a credible emergency flag and safety rationale, approval by the IC or a valid IC delegate is sufficient to implement. This holds only if a QM or valid QM delegate performs the retrospective review in time. If either condition fails, the case falls back to RR3. RR4 does not waive RR1 or RR2.
- **RR5:** a service-account event counts toward conformance only when reliable session evidence maps it to an authorized human. Otherwise the step is undemonstrated, which is different from violated.
- **RR6:** pair frequencies, reciprocity, shared terminals and concentrated handovers raise the priority of an investigation. They never change a classification to misconduct without independent evidence.

**Business days for RR4.** Business days are Monday to Friday, with no holidays in the period. The clock starts at `Implement_Change`, the moment a change without QM approval takes effect. The trigger day is day 0, and the deadline is the end of the second following business day. As a cross-check I also apply a stricter 48-business-hour reading.

| Case | Implemented | Deadline | Review | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CHG-505 | Tue 08-11 13:10 | Thu 08-13 | Fri 08-14 16:00 | Late by one business day under both readings |
| CHG-510 | Tue 08-11 14:30 | Thu 08-13 | Wed 08-12 10:00 | On time under both readings |

Site local time could shift day boundaries, but not these two results.

**Principal acting while on leave.** Leave is an HR status, not a revocation of role. Asha's own approval is therefore authorized if she was the human who acted, and the question becomes identity, not authority. For the delegation, I read "absent" as the formal leave record. A 25-minute remote session does not end D-QM-01, but a delegate approval made while the principal was demonstrably active would be flagged; none occurred. On a stricter reading, in which any availability suspends the delegation for the day, CHG-506 would fail on that ground as well.

**Later database entry.** A record created on 08-12 09:15 proves only that the system knew of the delegation from that moment. It cannot prove that the delegation existed on 08-10 or 08-11, because a migration can backfill or be used to backdate. Earlier existence rests on the signed PDF of 08-09 18:00. Its weight depends on the signature type, a trusted timestamp, the custody chain and the legacy-system audit trail. I therefore separate two questions: whether the delegation was granted before use (probable), and whether the system could verify it at the event (it could not, for CHG-501).

**Delegate on sick leave.** The delegation stays formally in force, but it is personal. An action by the delegate's account during certified absence is an identity red flag. If another person acted, the approval is void, and consensual credential use amounts to a prohibited subdelegation.

**Outside the six rules.** RR1–RR6 do not say who may approve LOW-risk changes or which role may verify. I flag those points as observations only.

## 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

None of the indicators below establishes collusion. Each one sets a priority for collecting evidence.

| Indicator | Benign explanation | Adverse explanation | Discriminating evidence |
|---|---|---|---|

Separately, no QM delegation to Ben before 08-10 is in evidence, so the 18 approvals raise an authority question first. | Delegation register for the 60 days; expected pair frequency given rosters; approval lead times and rejection rates for Ben versus Asha; later deviations on those changes |

| Alias b.koch and the late delegation record both appear around the 08-12 migration | Migration artifacts: a duplicate account and a reset creation time | The migration window was used to create a second identity or backfill a delegation | Migration log, account provisioning history, legacy audit trail |

**Network interpretation.** Clara drafts 9 of 10 cases, Diego implements 8, and Eva verifies 7 (8 with the attributed service event). The handover network is therefore dominated by edges that the process design itself produces: Diego to Eva is the heaviest (6 of 33 handovers), then Clara to Eva (4), Clara to Ben (3) and Ben to Diego (3). These weights measure role assignment, not affinity. The working-together network is nearly complete among five people and does not discriminate at all.

The informative edges are the self-loops: Clara to Clara (CHG-502, CHG-510) and Ben to Ben (CHG-508). The Ben self-loop appears only after alias normalization. A pair frequency means something only against a null model built from rosters and availability, and ten cases cannot provide one.

**Structural point.** From 08-13 12:00 to 08-14 23:59 the QM was on leave and her only delegate was on sick leave. No valid QM approver existed for production changes in that window. Both contested u_bk events fall inside it. A coverage gap explains pressure to borrow credentials without excusing it, and it is a less sinister hypothesis than collusion that fits the same facts.

def four_eyes_violations(log, a1, a2, resource_key):
    """Cases in which a1 and a2 were performed by the same value of resource_key."""
    try:
        bad = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, a1, a2, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS,
                                               case_id_key=CASE, resource_key=resource_key,
                                               keep_violations=True)
    except TypeError:  # older pm4py: no keep_violations -> result holds the conforming cases
        ok = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, a1, a2, resource_key=resource_key)
        bad = log[~log[CASE].isin(ok[CASE])]
    return set(bad[CASE].unique())


def four_eyes_pandas(log, a1, a2, resource_key):
    """Independent cross-check of the pm4py result."""
    x = log[log[ACT] == a1][[CASE, resource_key]].merge(log[log[ACT] == a2][[CASE, resource_key]],
                                                        on=CASE, suffixes=("_1", "_2"))
    return set(x.loc[x[resource_key + "_1"] == x[resource_key + "_2"], CASE])

def covers(tbl, human, ts):
    return bool(((tbl["human"] == human) & (tbl["start"] <= ts) & (ts < tbl["end"])).any())


def required_roles(row):
    if row[ACT] == "Retrospective_QM_Review":
        return {"QM"}
    if row["risk"] != "HIGH":
        return set()                                  # RR3 does not apply to LOW risk
    return {"QM", "IC"} if row["emergency"] else {"QM"}   # RR4 route needs retrospective review


def evaluate_authority(row):
    human, ts, need = row["res_evidenced"], row[TS], required_roles(row)
    out = {CASE: row[CASE], "activity": row[ACT], "ts": ts, "raw_id": row[RES], "human": human,
           "required": "/".join(sorted(need)) or "none (RR3 n/a)", "basis": "", "verdict": "", "caveats": ""}
    caveats = []
    if covers(ABSENCE, human, ts):
        caveats.append("ACTOR_FORMALLY_ABSENT_AT_EVENT"
                       + ("+PERSONAL_SESSION_ON_RECORD" if covers(SESSIONS, human, ts) else "+NO_SESSION_CORROBORATION"))
    if not need:
        out.update(basis="no role requirement", verdict="NOT_APPLICABLE", caveats=";".join(caveats))
        return out
    standing = ROLES[(ROLES.human == human) & ROLES.role.isin(need) & (ROLES.start <= ts) & (ts < ROLES.end)]
    if len(standing):
        out.update(basis="standing " + standing.role.iloc[0], verdict="AUTHORITY_OK", caveats=";".join(caveats))
        return out
    best = None
    for _, d in DELEG[(DELEG.delegate == human) & DELEG.role.isin(need)].iterrows():
        fails, open_cond, notes = [], [], []      # hard failures / unproven conditions / caveats
        if not (d.start <= ts < d.end):
            fails.append("OUTSIDE_INTERVAL")
        if row["scope"] not in d.scopes:
            fails.append("SCOPE_MISMATCH")
        if not d.chain_valid:
            fails.append("PROHIBITED_SUBDELEGATION")
        if d.needs_absence and not covers(ABSENCE, d.principal, ts):
            open_cond.append("PRINCIPAL_ABSENCE_NOT_ON_RECORD" + ("(weekend)" if ts.dayofweek >= 5 else ""))
        if d.needs_absence and covers(SESSIONS, d.principal, ts):
            notes.append("PRINCIPAL_ACTIVE_SESSION_AT_EVENT")
        if row[ACT] not in d.activities:
            notes.append("ACTIVITY_NOT_NAMED_IN_DELEGATION")
        if d.role == "IC":
            notes.append("INCIDENT_LINK_AND_SAFETY_RATIONALE_MANUAL")
        if pd.isna(d.system_ts) or d.system_ts > ts:
            notes.append("NOT_IN_CONTROLLED_SYSTEM_AT_EVENT"
                         + ("(signed doc predates event)" if pd.notna(d.signed_ts) and d.signed_ts <= ts
                            else "(no prior signed doc)"))
        cand = (len(fails), len(open_cond), d.id, fails, open_cond, notes)
        best = cand if best is None or cand[:2] < best[:2] else best
    if best is None or "OUTSIDE_INTERVAL" in best[3]:
        out.update(basis="no standing role; no delegation in force", verdict="NO_AUTHORITY",
                   caveats=";".join(caveats))
        return out
    _, _, did, fails, open_cond, notes = best
    verdict = ("DELEGATION_INVALID:" + "+".join(fails) if fails
               else "DELEGATION_CONDITIONAL:" + "+".join(open_cond) if open_cond else "DELEGATION_OK")
    out.update(basis=did, verdict=verdict, caveats=";".join(caveats + notes))
    return out

**How service-account attribution is represented.** The `SVC_ATTR` table holds one row per service event, with a candidate human and a manually assigned grade. Three resource columns follow from it:

- `org:resource` keeps the raw ID.
- `res_evidenced` merges aliases and substitutes a human only for a strong attribution. Any other service event becomes a per-event placeholder that can never equal a human.
- `res_worstcase` also applies weak candidates.

A violation that appears only in the worst-case view is printed as `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION_IF_WEAK_ATTRIBUTION_TRUE`.

**Result when run (pm4py 2.7.x).**

## 6. Control Improvements

- **Delegation creation:** create delegations only in the QMS, as structured records with delegate, role, scope, activities covered, conditions and a subdelegation flag, signed electronically by the principal. E-mail delegations should be rejected by the workflow. The system should block a subdelegation where the parent prohibits it.
- **Effective-time recording:** store the signing time, the registration time and the effective interval as separate fields with trusted timestamps. Preserve original creation times through migrations. The workflow should refuse an approval that relies on a delegation not yet registered.
- **Principal availability:** link delegations to the HR absence system, so that a conditional delegation activates only on a recorded absence. Define what happens when a principal logs in during leave. Require a second-line delegate, so that leave and sickness cannot leave the site without a QM approver, as happened on 08-13 and 08-14.
- **Alias management:** give each human one identity key, with all accounts mapped to it in the identity system. Segregation checks should run on that key. Retire legacy accounts at migration and reconcile accounts to humans periodically.
- **Individual authentication:** remove the service account from GMP-relevant steps. Require personal re-authentication with a second factor at each approval and verification signature, bound to the transaction. Apply short lock times on shared terminals, and alert on any login by a user recorded as absent.
- **Emergency review:** make the retrospective review a system task with a computed deadline and escalation to the QM. Record the incident ID and safety rationale as mandatory fields. State explicitly that self-approval is never permitted in an emergency, and name who may review emergency changes outside the production-equipment scope.
- **Pair rotation:** set a cap on the share of one drafter's HIGH-risk changes that one approver may sign in a period, or sample such changes for independent QM review. Reciprocal approvals should require a third reviewer.
- **Network monitoring:** produce monthly handover and working-together metrics on normalized identities, compared against roster-based expectations. Report self-loops, reciprocal pairs and actions during recorded absence as indicators for review, never as automatic findings.
- **Evidential retention:** retain session, VPN, MFA, badge and workstation logs, and CCTV for controlled areas, for at least the change-record retention period, with synchronized clocks and hash-protected storage.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

The ten cases show a mix of problems, and more of the apparent nonconformance is real than a data-quality explanation alone would suggest.

- **Genuine authority or segregation failures (about half the cases).** CHG-507 and CHG-510 are clear unauthorized HIGH-risk approvals. CHG-502 and CHG-508 are self-approvals on LOW-risk changes. CHG-505 had a valid emergency approval, but its QM review was late and made under a delegation that does not cover it.
- **Identity and data quality (about a third as the primary issue).** CHG-503 has no attributable verifier. CHG-506 rests on a contested account. CHG-501 depends on a delegation registered late, and CHG-504 and CHG-509 carry residual identity or condition doubts. Whether CHG-503 and CHG-506 conceal real violations cannot be decided on current evidence.
- **Organizational-pattern risk.** The Clara–Ben concentration and the reciprocal approvals are unproven as misconduct, and staffing explains them plausibly. They still matter, because a small group can complete whole changes among themselves and the system does not notice.

Only CHG-501 and CHG-504 are substantively sound, and CHG-509 is sound if Asha was absent. Confidence is high for the rule breaches that do not depend on identity, and medium to low wherever a conclusion turns on who was at the keyboard.

The three highest-priority investigations are:

1. **Use of u_bk during Ben's certified sick leave** (the CHG-506 approval and the CHG-505 review), together with the QM coverage gap on 08-13 and 08-14. This may be compromise or sharing of delegated QM credentials on HIGH-risk changes.
2. **The emergency chain around CI-44:** Farid's prohibited subdelegation, Clara's self-approval in CHG-510, and the invalid or late retrospective reviews. Both cooling changes need a genuine QM review now.
3. **Authority for HIGH-risk approvals by non-QMs before 08-10:** Eva's approval in CHG-507 and Ben's 18 approvals in the prior 60 days, for which no delegation is in evidence.

Independently of these, CHG-503 should be re-verified by an independent verifier without waiting for attribution to be resolved.
