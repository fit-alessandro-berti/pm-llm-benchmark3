## 1. Conformance semantics

The norm is partial, so "differs from the norm" and "violates the norm" are different claims. I use seven classes in this priority order.

**Forbidden behavior** — an observation instantiates a relation marked `forbidden` (N3, T6, E10). The norm is closed-world here, so one occurrence is a violation of record and no "the norm didn't mention it" defence exists. Only whether the *recorded* relation reflects reality stays arguable.

**Missing mandatory behavior** — a `mandatory` relation is absent for members inside its **stated scope** (N1, N4, T1, T2, E1–E8). Two questions precede a verdict: does the scope cover the deficient cases, and is the operationalization (directly-follows) the right test of the intent ("eventually")?

**Unexpected but unregulated behavior** — behavior with no normative counterpart, neither mandated nor forbidden. Under the open-world assumption this is not a deviation: `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, `Manual Compliance Review -> Customer`, `Package object_codeath Delivery`, N6, T4, T5, E9. Reading silence as prohibition would turn an admittedly incomplete model into a false compliance baseline.

**Typical-pattern absence** — a `typical` relation occurs less often than ideal (N2, N5, T3). This is a *rate* observation, not a per-case verdict; the norm states no minimum frequency, so nothing exists against which 83/100 or 92/110 can be called deficient.

**Likely data-quality error** — the relation as recorded is probably not the relation as it occurred: identifier reuse, faulty joins, missing labels, ingestion loss. Markers are semantic implausibility, disagreement between two views of one fact, and weight 1 on an otherwise absent edge type. This does not erase a finding; it redirects remediation from the process to the pipeline.

**Abstraction mismatch** — behavior may satisfy the norm's *intent* while failing its *encoding*, as in N1, where "should eventually enter validation" is tested as a directly-follows edge.

**Insufficient evidence** — neither conformance nor violation is establishable, because the outcome may lie outside the snapshot (the 14 invoices without successors) or the distinguishing attribute is absent.

**Open- versus closed-world effects.** Closed-world reasoning is licensed in exactly two places: forbidden relations, and the *absence* of mandatory relations inside scope. Elsewhere the model is open and an unmodelled edge carries no verdict. Crucially, the open-world reading does not rescue mandatory gaps: I may not dismiss N1 or N4 with "maybe it happened and wasn't modeled", but must name the mechanism — abstraction, scope, or ingestion — that would explain the gap and the evidence confirming it.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

It establishes, per object type, the ordering of event types along that type's lifecycle and how many objects show a given adjacency. This is the only family here supporting a *sequencing* claim, which is why N3 (2 Orders) is the one finding I call a violation on behavioral grounds alone: the edge asserts that for the same Order a dispatch followed a cancellation. It cannot establish causality, authorisation, whether the objects in a cross-type story are the right ones, or whether an absent edge means absent behavior; it cannot separate "did not happen" from "was not recorded", and it hides non-adjacent occurrences.

Every count carries an implicit denominator that must be audited. N1's 98 is out of 100 *newly created standard* Orders; N4's 198 is out of 200 *externally shipped* Packages. If either denominator is wrong — if two of the 200 are returns — the gap dissolves with no process change. Conversely N5's 92 sits against 110 invoices and the residual decomposes into 4 write-offs plus 14 without successors, exactly the shape N5's scope predicts.

**The directly-follows danger.** N1 states an *eventually* rule but is tested as a directly-follows edge, and the context says the 2 exceptions may have an unrecorded intervening review. Such a test yields false positives whenever any event intervenes, and false *negatives* too: an order could show `Create -> Validate` and have that validation later invalidated, and the edge would still be green. The same hazard hits N2, which admits intervening events in its own scope text, and N4, where a repack or hold breaks the edge without breaking the control. Any finding resting on a mandatory OC-DFG edge must be re-tested by reachability before escalation.

### OTG

It establishes which object *types* interact, co-birth or co-die, with aggregate weights — the right level for structural and segregation statements. Hence T6 matters: cobirth of Order and Payment is a claim about the system's object-creation architecture, and one occurrence shows the architecture permits what the norm forbids. It says nothing about individual objects, ordering, or how a type-level edge distributes across instances; weight 1 on a forbidden edge and weight 1 on a permitted edge are structurally identical and semantically opposite.

The type level is systematically more forgiving than the event level, which gives the clearest illustration of the required point. T2 `(Package, object_interaction, Delivery)` has weight 200 and is fully satisfied, yet ET–OT shows `Dispatch Package | Delivery` at 198. The relation is therefore **conforming at the OTG abstraction and suspicious at the ET–OT abstraction**: the type pair interacts, but two specific dispatch events do not participate. T1 (1450, satisfied) against E2 (1448) repeats the pattern, and a reviewer stopping at OTG would issue a clean bill of health on both. The 7 codeath edges show the inverse trap: they look like novel behavior but follow from two lifecycles terminating in one confirmation event, and the norm addresses codeath nowhere.

### ET–OT

It establishes per-event-type coverage of object types, the tightest test of whether each event carries the references it must. E1 (100), E3 (1450), E4 (200), E5 (200), E7 (93) and E9 (3) are satisfied; the gaps are E6 (198 of 200), E2 (1448 of 1450) and E8 (92 of 93). It cannot establish ordering, per-object completeness, or *why* a reference is missing: a missing relation is ambiguous between "the object did not exist", "it existed unlinked", and "the link was lost". Context resolves two of these probabilistically — the carrier attribute on the 2 dispatches points to an unmaterialised Delivery, a linkage problem rather than behavior, while the unlabelled payment points to a missing *label* rather than a missing object.

E10 needs most care. The edge is forbidden, so closed-world reasoning applies and it must be reported; but the stated cause means the behavior almost certainly never occurred. The right output is a data-integrity finding against the pipeline plus a formal record that a forbidden edge appeared, not a business finding that operations linked telemetry to finance. Dismissing it as "just a join bug" would be wrong: identifier reuse that can attach a sensor to an invoice can attach anything to anything.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

**Mandatory — satisfied:** N1 for 98/100 Orders; N4 for 198/200 Packages; T1 and T2 at type level; E1 (100), E3 (1450), E4 (200), E5 (200), E7 (93).

**Mandatory — gaps needing scope or abstraction resolution:** N1, 2 Orders (*conforming* if eventually-follows succeeds, a real control gap if validation is absent from the full trace); N4, 2 Packages (*out of scope* if not externally shipped); E6, 2 events (*data quality* once a Delivery is recovered from the carrier reference); E2, 2 events (*data quality* if an unresolved Order key exists, *out of scope* if the target is a pre-Order basket); E8, 1 event (*legitimately excepted* the moment it is explicitly typed as unapplied cash, which E8's own scope permits).

**Forbidden — occurred as recorded:** N3, 2 Orders (reclassifies only if the cancellation timestamp or Order identity is wrong); T6, weight 1 (data quality if the extractor collapses two births into one event); E10, weight 1 (behaviorally void if identifier reuse is confirmed, but still a reportable integrity defect).

**Typical — rate observations, not violations:** N2 (83); N5 (92 of 110, with 4 write-offs named legitimate and 14 unresolved); T3 (92). None is deficient, since no minimum frequency is stated. The 14 open invoices shift to *insufficient evidence* when the snapshot window binds, and to a receivables finding only if past due.

**Unregulated — no verdict available:** `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72); `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review` (3) with T5 and E9; `Manual Compliance Review–Customer` and `Order–Customer` (T4, another policy); `Reopen Order -> Close Order` (N6, 1); `Package object_codeath Delivery` (7). Each enters a regulated bucket only by explicit norm revision, never by inference from silence.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

**(1) "Eventually" encoded as directly-follows (N1, N2, N4).** Risk: 2 Orders and 2 Packages look like control failures purely because an event intervened, while genuine bypasses producing the right adjacency pass unnoticed. Clarify by restating each rule as reachability over the object's own event sequence. Status: **mandatory** as a reachability rule.

**(2) No scope predicate for "externally shipped" (N4) or "standard Order" (N1).** Risk: the denominators 200 and 100 are unauditable, so every gap is contestable and returns may be counted wrongly. Clarify by naming the attribute and value that place an object in scope. Status: **mandatory** for the definition, out-of-scope objects **explicitly unspecified**.

**(3) No tolerance bands or minimum frequencies.** Risk: 83/100 (N2) and 92/110 (N5) invite arbitrary escalation or dismissal as reviewers substitute personal thresholds. Clarify with per-relation reporting thresholds plus an exception-reason breakdown of the residual. Status: **typical**, thresholds binding for reporting only.

**(4) Cobirth is forbidden for Order–Payment (T6) but codeath is unaddressed.** Risk: the 7 Package–Delivery codeath edges cannot be evaluated, and an improper Invoice–Payment codeath would also escape. Clarify by stating a policy for every lifecycle-relationship kind the extractor emits. Status: Package–Delivery codeath **explicitly unspecified**, financial-object codeath **forbidden**.

**(5) E8's unapplied-cash exemption has no operational definition.** Risk: the exemption is unusable, so the one payment without an invoice is recorded as a violation despite a legitimate explanation. Clarify by requiring a payment-application status attribute and allowing the exemption only when set. Status: **mandatory** for the label, enabling **typical** treatment of the exception.

**(6) Cancellation is only partly governed.** Risk: N3 forbids dispatch after cancellation, but nothing says what must happen on cancellation or how issued invoices are handled, so the 2 breaches have no defined remediation path. Clarify by adding cancellation-propagation relations and an authoritative cancellation timestamp. Status: **mandatory**.

**(7) Reopen is unspecified without a boundary (N6).** Risk: reopening becomes a legitimate route around validation and cancellation controls. Clarify by requiring re-validation on reopen, or by stating that reopened Orders re-enter N1's scope. Status: **mandatory**.

**(8) No identifier-integrity provisions.** Risk: E10 and T6 are indistinguishable from real behavior at graph level, and identifier reuse can manufacture arbitrary forbidden edges. Clarify by requiring namespaced identifiers, lineage for derived relations, and a quarantine class distinct from "violation". Status: **mandatory** for identifier uniqueness.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

**Immediate containment.** The two priority findings are N3 (2 Orders) and T6 (weight 1), because both are forbidden relations touching value movement rather than reporting artefacts. For N3: retrieve the two Order and Package identifiers, check carrier tracking, recall anything in transit, and hold the cancellation-to-warehouse interface until the control gap is understood. For T6: locate the composite event and suspend that emitter's object-birth path. E10 is contained, not escalated — stop the offending join and quarantine the edge without deleting the record that it appeared.

**Data repair.** Backfill Delivery objects for the 2 dispatches from their carrier references (E6). Resolve or explain the 2 `Add Item` events lacking an Order reference (E2). Apply or explicitly classify the single unapplied payment and add the attribute E8's exemption requires. Remove the `Sensor Heartbeat | Invoice` edge at source by namespacing the reused key, then re-scan for other edges the same join produced. Reconcile the OTG-versus-ET–OT discrepancies (1450/1448, 200/198).

**Process investigation.** Re-test N1 and N4 with an eventually-follows query before treating either gap as a control failure. Test whether the 2 packages missing `Pack -> Dispatch`, the 2 dispatches missing a Delivery, and the 2 dispatches after cancellation are overlapping sets — if so this is one operational defect with one interface fix, not three findings. Age the 14 invoices without successors against their terms to separate open credit from stalled receivables. Confirm the 4 write-offs carry approvals under finance policy, not under this norm.

**Norm revision.** Implement the section 6 clarifications in order: reachability semantics, scope predicates, the E8 label, cancellation propagation, then codeath policy, tolerance bands, the reopen boundary and identifier integrity. Record explicitly that `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, `Manual Compliance Review–Customer`, `Package object_codeath Delivery` and `Reopen Order -> Close Order` are *unspecified* rather than tolerated violations, so the next reviewer does not re-litigate them.

**Monitoring.** Alert on any occurrence of a forbidden relation (N3, T6, E10) with zero tolerance. Trend the mandatory-coverage ratios (98/100, 198/200, 1448/1450, 198/200, 92/93) as rates, so drift from two exceptions to twenty is visible. Track typical relations (N2 83, N5 92/110, T3 92) with exception-reason decomposition and no automatic escalation. Explicitly exclude the rare unregulated behaviors — 3 compliance reviews, 1 reopen, 7 codeath edges — from alerting: rarity is not risk, and alerting on them trains reviewers to ignore the channel.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

**Robust.** Three forbidden relations are present as recorded — N3 (2 Orders), T6 (weight 1), E10 (weight 1) — and all must be reported regardless of cause, because the norm is closed-world for forbidden items. Five mandatory relations show arithmetically certain shortfalls: N1 98/100, N4 198/200, E2 1448 of 1450, E6 198 of 200, E8 92 of 93. T1 and T2 hold at type level, and E1, E3, E4, E5, E7 are fully covered. `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72), `Manual Compliance Review–Customer` (3), `Package object_codeath Delivery` (7) and `Reopen Order -> Close Order` (1) are not violations.

**Dependent on the norm's incompleteness.** Whether N2 at 83 and N5 at 92/110 are acceptable is undecidable without tolerance bands. Whether the 2 packages missing `Pack -> Dispatch` fall inside N4 is undecidable without a shipment-type definition. Whether the N1 gap is a violation at all depends on replacing directly-follows with the "eventually" semantics the norm itself states. The significance of codeath, reopen, risk reviews and customer links depends on policies outside this document.

**Dependent on suspected data quality.** The benign behavioral reading of E10 rests entirely on the identifier-reuse hypothesis; if that is wrong it becomes a serious finding. E6's benign reading rests on the carrier attribute corresponding to a real Delivery. E8's exculpatory reading rests on a finance claim the data does not corroborate, since no unapplied-cash label exists. T6's severity depends on whether a real shared birth occurred or the extractor collapsed two births.

**Undecidable from graph summaries alone.** Authorisation, intent, monetary impact and identity correctness are invisible here. No aggregate says whether the 2 post-cancellation dispatches were one systemic defect or two unrelated errors, whether the 14 unpaid invoices are within terms, whether the 2 orders lacking a validation edge were in fact validated, or whether the 200 packages and 100 orders describe consistent populations. Every finding above is a hypothesis about event-level facts, and becomes a conclusion only once the identifiers named in the evidence column are inspected directly.
