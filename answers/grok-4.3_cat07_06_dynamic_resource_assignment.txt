# 1. Objectives and constraints
**Safety/compliance constraints (hard rules, never violated):**  
- Skill 0 assignments are forbidden.  
- Security Review requires skill 3 or skill 1–2 only under explicit supervision by R7_Gita or R8_Hugo.  
- Final Approval requires skill 2–3; skill 1 may prepare only.  
- High-priority cases require skill 2 unless supervised.  
- No resource receives >4 new assignments in one batch.  
- High-overtime-risk resources receive urgent work only when no safer alternative exists.  
- High-complexity cases with high handover cost retain current owner unless owner skill <2 and supervision is impossible.

**Preference trade-offs (soft objectives, balanced rather than optimized):**  
- Short-term throughput versus long-term skill development.  
- Workload fairness and overtime-risk distribution versus SLA risk.  
- Continuity of ownership versus opportunities for supervised training.  
- Resilience to absence or bottlenecks versus immediate specialist utilization.  
- Batching of Quality Check only when SLA slack permits.

# 2. Assignment policy
```
For each case in urgency order (urgent > high > normal > low):
  1. Eligibility filter: retain only resources with skill 1 for required activity,
     satisfying all hard constraints (supervision, overtime risk, max 4 assignments).
  2. Compute composite score (weights chosen to avoid single-objective dominance):
     - SLA_urgency = 0.30 * (hours_remaining / max_SLA) inverted
     - Skill_match = 0.25 * skill_level (penalize skill 1–2 on high complexity)
     - Continuity = 0.20 * (1 if current_owner else 0) – 0.15 * handover_cost_band
     - Fairness = 0.15 * (1 – (open_cases / max_open)) – 0.10 * overtime_risk_flag
     - Training = 0.10 * (1 if resource.training_priority matches activity
                           and priority  normal and SLA slack 12h)
     - Resilience = 0.05 * (1 – specialist_bottleneck_indicator)
  3. Select highest composite score; if tie, prefer current owner, then lowest open cases,
     then documented training priority.
  4. Exception handling: if no eligible resource satisfies SLA risk, escalate to team lead
     for manual supervision or overtime approval; record deviation.
```

# 4. Comparison with baseline policies
First-available routing yields lowest immediate waiting time but highest reassignment counts and skill-mismatch rework. Specialist-only routing minimizes short-term errors on Security/Approval yet creates queues and violates training/fairness constraints. The proposed policy trades a modest increase in average service time for lower long-term rework, better continuity on high-complexity cases, and measurable training assignments, while respecting overtime-risk and supervision rules. It improves workload distribution and absence resilience compared with specialist-only, at the cost of slightly higher SLA exposure on non-urgent work than first-available.

# 5. Explanation to workers
Assignments try to keep cases with the person who already knows them when the case is complex or has already changed hands several times. When a case is urgent and the current person lacks the right skill level, we bring in a specialist and, if needed, ask for quick supervision rather than just passing the work on. We also reserve a few lower-priority cases each round for people who are building a particular skill, provided the deadline is not at risk. No one is given more than four new cases at once, and we avoid piling urgent work on colleagues already flagged as high overtime risk.
