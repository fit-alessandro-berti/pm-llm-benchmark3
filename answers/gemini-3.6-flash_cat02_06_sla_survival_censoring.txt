# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

## Time Origin, Horizon, and Event Definitions
* **Time Origin ($t = 0$):** The timestamp of the `Submit` activity when a case enters the case-management system.
* **SLA Horizon ($T_{\text{SLA}} = 72\text{ hours}$):** The maximum allowable elapsed time from `Submit` to operational resolution.
* **Target Event:** Operational resolution, represented by the terminal activity `Resolve`.
* **Competing Events:** Terminal internal outcomes that permanently prevent internal resolution (`Withdraw`, `Reject`) and external handoffs (`Transfer`).
* **Censoring:** Administrative right-censoring at the freeze observation cutoff for cases with `observed_status == 'open'`.

---

## Definition of Estimands

### Estimand 1: Operational Non-Resolution Risk by 72 Hours ($R_{\text{NonRes}}(72)$)
Defined as $1 - P(\text{Resolved by } t = 72\text{ h})$. This measures the probability that a case fails to reach internal `Resolve` within the SLA window. Under this estimand, terminal non-resolutions (`Withdraw`, `Reject`, `Transfer`) occurring before 72 hours count as failure to achieve operational resolution.
$$\text{Decision Context:} \text{ SLA policy reporting, client-facing commitments, and top-level operational performance metrics.}$$

### Estimand 2: Cumulative Incidence of Late Resolution ($CIF_{\text{Resolve}}(t > 72)$)
The probability of experiencing the specific event `Resolve` after 72 hours, computed using a competing-risks framework (Aalen-Johansen cumulative incidence function):
$$CIF_{\text{Resolve}}(t) = \int_{0}^{t} S(u^-) h_{\text{Resolve}}(u) \, du$$
where $S(u)$ is overall survival from any exit event and $h_{\text{Resolve}}(u)$ is the cause-specific hazard for resolution.
$$\text{Decision Context:} \text{ Backlog management, estimating re-work costs, and routing long-running active cases.}$$

### Estimand 3: Active State Survival at 72 Hours ($S_{\text{Active}}(72)$)
Defined as $P(T_{\text{Terminal}} > 72\text{ h})$, the probability that a case remains open in-progress (has not reached `Resolve`, `Withdraw`, `Reject`, or `Transfer`) by 72 hours.
$$\text{Decision Context:} \text{ Staffing allocation, active queue sizing, and operational bottleneck tracking.}$$

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

## 1. Survivor Bias (Exclusion of Open Cases)
Dropping open cases removes cases that have already exceeded the 72-hour SLA limit without resolving.
* **Case Evidence:** Cases **S09** (open at 92h), **S11** (open at 81h), **S25** (open at 79h), and **S26** (open at 104h) are all active cases whose age exceeds 72 hours.
* **Bias Direction:** Deleting S09, S11, S25, and S26 removes confirmed SLA breaches from the denominator, underestimating the true SLA breach rate.

## 2. Informative Censoring & Inactivity Gaps
Standard Kaplan-Meier assumes censoring is independent of future hazard. However, `last_event_gap_h` indicates varying risk profiles among open cases.
* **Case Evidence:** **S26** (Cohort B, open at 104h, `last_event_gap_h` = 72h) has seen no activity for 72 hours, indicating structural stagnation. Conversely, **S15** (Cohort A, open at 35h, `last_event_gap_h` = 2h) is actively moving.
* **Bias Direction:** Treating S26 as ordinary non-informative censoring assumes its future resolution probability equals that of an active case, overestimating overall survival/resolution curves.

## 3. Competing Risks Misclassification
Treating terminal exits (`Withdraw`, `Reject`) as uninformative right-censoring causes standard Kaplan-Meier estimators ($1 - S_{\text{KM}}(t)$) to overestimate the cumulative incidence of resolution.
* **Case Evidence:** **S05** (rejected at 9h), **S12** (withdrawn at 15h), **S23** (rejected at 11h), and **S06** (withdrawn at 27h) permanently exited the process.
* **Bias Direction:** Standard KM assumes these withdrawn/rejected cases would eventually resolve at the same rate as active cases if followed longer, artificially inflating the estimated resolution rate.

## 4. Cohort Imbalance & Non-Random Rollout
Cohort B introduced upfront automated screening (`Submit>AutoCheck>...`), but rollout compliance was incomplete.
* **Case Evidence:** In Cohort B, cases like **S16**, **S18**, and **S19** received upfront `AutoCheck`, but **S17**, **S20**, **S23**, **S26**, and **S30** bypassed upfront `AutoCheck` and followed Cohort A's legacy path (`Submit>Screen...`).
* **Bias Direction:** A naive comparison between Cohort A and Cohort B that ignores compliance conflates policy effects with case selection bias.

## 5. Active Open Cases Below vs. Above 72 Hours
* **Cases Below 72h:** **S15** (35h), **S24** (67h), **S29** (51h), **S31** (23h). These cases are currently within SLA but remain at risk of breaching before resolution.
* **Cases Above 72h:** **S09** (92h), **S11** (81h), **S25** (79h), **S26** (104h). These cases have already breached SLA. Treating them as "at risk" of future breach or censoring them without acknowledging their current breach status corrupts risk estimation.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

## Methodological Comparison

```
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
| Method                            | Treatment of Competing Risks      | Handling of Time-Varying Prefix   | Primary Operational Use Case      |
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
| Kaplan-Meier (1 - KM)             | Treats as ordinary censoring;     | Baseline features only;           | Unbiased baseline survival ONLY   |
|                                   | OVERESTIMATES incidence           | susceptible to immortal bias      | if no competing events exist      |
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
| Aalen-Johansen CIF                | Mathematically exact non-         | Baseline / Stratified             | Unbiased estimation of marginal   |
|                                   | parametric subdistribution        | cohort features                   | SLA resolution probabilities      |
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
| Cause-Specific Hazards (Cox CSH)  | Censoring at competing event      | Time-dependent covariates         | Etiologic evaluation of process   |
|                                   | times; estimates rate ratio       | or landmarking                    | event impact on direct resolution |
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
| Subdistribution Hazards           | Directly models subdistribution   | Baseline features or fixed        | Direct probability scoring for    |
| (Fine-Gray)                       | hazard for CIF                    | landmark windows                  | SLA breach risk prediction        |
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
| Restricted Mean Time (RMST)       | Integrates survival curve up to   | Baseline cohorts / Priority       | Summary metric of expected active |
|                                   | T = 72h                           | tiers                             | processing time before SLA limit  |
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
| Landmarking Analysis              | Evaluates risk sets at fixed      | Eliminates leakage/immortal bias  | Real-time operational risk alerts |
|                                   | landmarks (e.g., t=24h, 48h)      | using history up to landmark      | at 24h/48h operational shifts     |
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
| Multi-State Model (MSM)           | Full transition matrix between    | Explicit modeling of stage-to-    | Process bottleneck identification |
|                                   | intermediate process states       | stage hazards and state durations | and multi-stage hazard simulation |
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
```

---

## Recommended Primary and Secondary Approaches

### Primary Recommendation: Aalen-Johansen CIF with Fine-Gray / Cause-Specific Hazards
* **Justification:** Competing outcomes (`Withdraw`, `Reject`, `Transfer`) represent significant terminal volume. Aalen-Johansen prevents the overestimation inherent in standard Kaplan-Meier. Cause-Specific Cox models quantify risk ratios across priority tiers and cohorts.

### Secondary Recommendation: Landmarking Multi-State Model ($t_{\text{LM}} \in \{24\text{h}, 48\text{h}\}$)
* **Justification:** Avoids immortal-time bias and data leakage when evaluating intermediate events (`Escalate`, `Rework`). By freezing covariates at landmark time $t_{\text{LM}}$, operational teams can generate conditional breach probability alerts for active cases without using future process logs.

# 6. Sensitivity analysis

To evaluate the stability of process conclusions, three sensitivity scenarios were designed to test variations in transfer modeling, competing risks, and informative censoring.

```
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
| Analysis Parameter                | Scenario 1: Baseline              | Scenario 2: High-Risk / Stalled   | Scenario 3: Optimistic Handoff    |
|                                   | (Administrative Censoring)        | (Imputed Breach)                  | (External Success)                |
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
| Treatment of `transferred`        | Right-censored at transfer time T | Non-resolution failure            | External resolution assumed met   |
|                                   |                                   | (SLA breach at 72h)               | within SLA                        |
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
| Treatment of `withdrawn`/`reject` | Competing risks (Aalen-Johansen)  | Competing risks                   | Merged into administrative exit   |
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
| Informative Censoring Rule        | Standard right-censoring for      | Stalled open cases                | Standard censoring                |
|                                   | open cases                        | (gap > 48h) imputed as breach     |                                   |
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
```

## Potential Qualitative Reversals

1. **Cohort B Policy Superiority Reversal:** Under Scenario 1, Cohort B exhibits lower raw hazard due to automated screening. However, under Scenario 2, Cohort B cases with high transfer rates (S21, S32) and long open gaps (S25, S26) flip Cohort B from superior to inferior relative to Cohort A in 72-hour SLA compliance.
2. **Priority P1 Hazard Reversal:** P1 cases experience frequent escalation (e.g., S04, S08, S11, S18, S21, S25, S29, S32). If `Escalate` is analyzed naively without landmarking, P1 cases appear to have higher risk of breach purely because escalation is triggered on complex, long-running cases.

# 7. Operational interpretation

## Operational Governance Matrix

```
+-----------------------+----------------------------------+-----------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------+
| Risk Trigger Window   | Process & Survival Condition     | Operational Action                      | Governance & SLA Policy                 |
+-----------------------+----------------------------------+-----------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------+
| Landmark t = 24h      | Case in `Review` stage;          | Re-assign to Senior Analyst;            | Early-intervention alert; prevent       |
|                       | `last_event_gap_h` > 12h         | bypass secondary screening              | stage stagnation before 48h             |
+-----------------------+----------------------------------+-----------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------+
| Landmark t = 48h      | Case `Escalated` or `Rework` = 1;| Expedite priority review queue;         | SLA Breach Warning; require active      |
|                       | Conditional breach prob > 45%    | prohibit external transfer              | supervisor sign-off                     |
+-----------------------+----------------------------------+-----------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------+
| Elapsed Age > 72h     | Case `open` without resolution   | Route to SLA Breach Remediation team    | Formal SLA breach recorded; log         |
|                       |                                  |                                         | root-cause bottleneck                   |
+-----------------------+----------------------------------+-----------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------+
```

## Non-Causal Risk Interpretation
* **Escalation Danger:** An elevated hazard ratio for escalated cases ($\text{HR} > 1$) does **not** imply that escalation causes delays. Escalation is a marker of underlying case complexity. Mandating a reduction in escalations without reducing case complexity will increase undetected breaches.

## Calibration and Fairness Checks
1. **Priority Parity:** Priority P1 cases must be evaluated against priority-specific baseline hazards. Scoring P1 cases on P3 baseline models forces unnecessary emergency re-allocations.
2. **Cohort Parity:** Policy changes in Cohort B must be calibrated separately for compliant (`Submit>AutoCheck`) vs. non-compliant (`Submit>Screen`) execution paths to prevent unfair performance metrics for operators handling non-standard cases.

# 2. SENSITIVITY SCENARIO ENCODINGS
# ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
def encode_scenarios(data):
    df_enc = data.copy()
    
    # Scenario 1: Baseline (Transfers Censored at T, Rejections/Withdrawals Competing)
    # Event codes: 0=Censored, 1=Resolve, 2=Reject/Withdraw, 3=Transfer
    df_enc['status_scen1'] = df_enc['observed_status'].map({
        'resolved': 1, 'rejected': 2, 'withdrawn': 2, 'transferred': 0, 'open': 0
    })
    
    # Scenario 2: Pessimistic Transfer & Stalled Imputation
    # Transfers count as failure (Code 2). Open cases with gap > 48h imputed as failure at age T.
    def sc2_map(row):
        if row['observed_status'] == 'resolved':
            return 1
        elif row['observed_status'] in ['rejected', 'withdrawn', 'transferred']:
            return 2
        elif row['observed_status'] == 'open':
            if row['last_event_gap_h'] > 48:
                return 2 # Imputed breach/stalled
            return 0
        return 0
    df_enc['status_scen2'] = df_enc.apply(sc2_map, axis=1)

    # Scenario 3: Optimistic Transfer (Transfers assumed Resolved Externally within SLA)
    def sc3_map(row):
        if row['observed_status'] in ['resolved', 'transferred']:
            return 1
        elif row['observed_status'] in ['rejected', 'withdrawn']:
            return 2
        return 0
    df_enc['status_scen3'] = df_enc.apply(sc3_map, axis=1)

    # Baseline Feature: Initial activity check
    df_enc['starts_with_autocheck'] = df_enc['prefix'].str.startswith('Submit>AutoCheck').astype(int)

    return df_enc

df_encoded = encode_scenarios(df)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. NON-PARAMETRIC CUMULATIVE INCIDENCE / KM ESTIMATOR (PURE PYTHON FALLBACK)
# ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
def compute_km_cif(df_sub, time_col='duration_or_age_h', status_col='status_scen1'):
    """
    Computes Kaplan-Meier survival & Nelson-Aalen / Competing Risk Cumulative Incidence
    without dropping open cases.
    """
    times = sorted(df_sub[time_col].unique())
    n_at_risk = len(df_sub)
    
    records = []
    surv = 1.0
    cif_resolve = 0.0
    
    for t in times:
        risk_set = df_sub[df_sub[time_col] >= t]
        n_k = len(risk_set)
        
        # Events at time t
        events_t = df_sub[df_sub[time_col] == t]
        d_resolve = (events_t[status_col] == 1).sum()
        d_competing = (events_t[status_col] == 2).sum()
        c_censored = (events_t[status_col] == 0).sum()
        
        # KM estimate
        d_total = d_resolve + d_competing
        if n_k > 0:
            hazard_total = d_total / n_k
            hazard_resolve = d_resolve / n_k
            
            # Update Aalen-Johansen CIF for resolution
            cif_resolve += surv * hazard_resolve
            surv = surv * (1.0 - hazard_total)
            
        records.append({
            'time': t,
            'n_at_risk': n_k,
            'd_resolve': d_resolve,
            'd_competing': d_competing,
            'censored': c_censored,
            'survival_S_t': surv,
            'cif_resolve': cif_resolve,
            'non_resolution_risk': 1.0 - cif_resolve
        })
        
    return pd.DataFrame(records)
