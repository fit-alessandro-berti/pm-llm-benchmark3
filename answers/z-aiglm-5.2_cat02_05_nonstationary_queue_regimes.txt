# 1. Data audit and estimands

## Definitions and units

- **Service window**: 07:00–19:00, 12 opening hours per day. No service overnight; cases may carry over.
- **Arrivals** `_day`: count of new cases arriving during opening hours on a given day. Units: cases/day.
- **Completions** `_day`: count of cases completed during opening hours. Units: cases/day. Note: completions need not correspond to same-day arrivals.
- **Service time**: active handling time excluding wait. We have **median** and **p90**, not the mean. Mean service time is required for an M/G/c utilization calculation but is **not identifiable** from median/p90 alone.
- **mean_wip**: mean number of cases in system (waiting + in service) from 15-minute snapshots. Units: cases.
- **staff_fte**: mean concurrently available FTE. Approximates server count `c`.
- **complex_pct**: percentage of arrivals following a multi-step complex variant.
- **peak_08_10_pct**: share of daily arrivals in 08:00–09:59 (2 of 12 hours).
- **outage_min**: minutes of outage on that day.

## Estimands we would like but do not have

1. Per-case arrival timestamps and completion timestamps (needed for true flow-time distribution).
2. Backlog at 07:00 each day (opening inventory).
3. Mean service time (we only have median/p90).
4. Service-time distribution shape (needed for `E[S²]` in Pollaczek–Khinchine or Kingman approximations).
5. Arrival process autocorrelation structure across days.

## Threats to naive inference

1. **Non-stationarity within the month**: `complex_pct` jumps from ~22% to ~44% around 2026-04-20, coinciding with `p90_service_min` rising from ~49 to ~80+. A pooled mean service time would blend two regimes.
2. **Outage confound**: 2026-04-15 has a 180-min outage with near-zero completions 11:00–14:00, followed by a recovery surge (129 completed on 04-16). Treating these as normal high/low throughput is misleading.
3. **Weekday/weekend composition**: Weekends have ~40–50 arrivals vs ~100–120 weekdays and ~2.5–3.0 FTE vs ~6.0–7.0. Pooling produces an artificial "average" day that matches no real operating condition.
4. **Completion  throughput**: Because of carry-over, `completed` on day `t` partly reflects arrivals from `t-1` or earlier. Using daily completions as a throughput proxy for Little's Law requires careful window choice.
5. **Median is not mean**: Substituting `median_service_min` for mean flow time (or mean service time) in a queueing formula is a category error. The p90 values suggest right-skewed service distributions, so mean > median.
6. **Case-mix/coding ambiguity**: The `complex_pct` rise could be a real mix shift, a reclassification, or both. We cannot distinguish these from daily aggregates.
7. **Serial dependence**: Daily WIP and arrivals trend upward; daily points are not independent. Standard errors treating days as i.i.d. will be too small.
8. **Burstiness within day**: 2026-04-21 has 34 arrivals in the 09:00–10:00 bin and 24 in the 12:00–13:00 bin against a baseline hourly mean near 10. A Poisson assumption on hourly bins is inappropriate on such days.
9. **Backlog unobserved**: High WIP after the outage (47, 52) may be recovery backlog rather than fresh demand pressure.
10. **Staffing intervention timing**: The intervention began 2026-04-29 but exact time is unknown, so we cannot cleanly attribute within-day effects.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

### Alternative: capacity-pressure-driven segmentation

Instead of segmenting by calendar/mix events, segment by **implied capacity pressure**, approximated by `mean_wip / staff_fte` (cases per FTE in system):

| Regime | Dates | Pressure class | Story |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | 04-06 to 04-14 weekdays | Low pressure (WIP/FTE ~3–4) | Near balance, mild WIP |
| A2 | Weekends | Low absolute WIP but high WIP/FTE (~5–6) because FTE is tiny | Different bottleneck: server scarcity, not demand |
| A3 | 04-15 to 04-17 | Transient backlog spike from outage | Outage + recovery; not a steady regime |
| A4 | 04-20 to 04-28 | High pressure (WIP/FTE ~7–8) | Mix shift + demand surge overwhelm capacity |
| A5 | 04-29 onward | Decompression with added FTE | Intervention brings WIP/FTE back toward ~5 |

### Comparison with primary segmentation

**Strengths of the alternative:**
- Directly targets the operational quantity of interest (congestion), not calendar labels.
- Makes weekends and post-mix-change weekdays comparable on the same pressure axis.
- Avoids assuming the mix change is "real"; it simply observes high WIP/FTE.

**Weaknesses:**
- `mean_wip / staff_fte` is a snapshot average, not a utilization measure. It ignores that some WIP is waiting while servers may be idle at off-peak hours. It can overstate pressure on weekends where low arrivals mean low actual server occupancy.
- It conflates demand-driven and capacity-driven pressure (outage vs mix shift), which the primary segmentation keeps separate.
- WIP/FTE is sensitive to the unobserved opening backlog; if 07:00 carry-over is large, the ratio is inflated for reasons unrelated to that day's load.

**Verdict:** The primary segmentation is preferable for causal narrative and staffing recommendations; the alternative is useful as a robustness check and for detecting that weekends are not simply "quiet."

### Change-point evidence

- **Outage (04-15)**: hard change point, externally confirmed.
- **Mix change (04-20)**: `complex_pct` jumps from 22 (04-17) to 41 (04-20); `p90_service_min` from 60 to 78. A step is clearly visible. Whether this is demand or coding is unresolved.

### Why four representative days cannot establish a stochastic model

- Four days are not a sample of the arrival process; they are **selected exemplars**. Selection bias inflates apparent regime contrast.
- No replication within regime × hour-of-week, so we cannot estimate the full covariance of the 12-vector arrival distribution.
- The outage day's completion vector is censored, not random.
- Weekend has only one exemplar and only one weekday-off-peak exemplar.
- We cannot estimate day-of-week effects, week effects, and hour effects separately from four rows.

A defensible stochastic model would require at least several weeks of hourly (or finer) event-level data per regime, plus the unobserved opening backlog.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Little's Law: `L =  W`, where `L` is long-run average number in system, `` is long-run average throughput (arrival rate in steady state with finite WIP), `W` is long-run average flow time. It applies to **stable, stationary** systems. We compute implied `W = L / ` only where defensible, using **arrivals** as the throughput proxy when WIP is stable (arrivals  completions) and the system is not in a transient backlog state. Units: `L` in cases, `` in cases/day, `W` in days. Convert to hours by ×24 (calendar) or ×12 (opening-hours throughput basis — but Little's Law uses wall-clock time, so ×24 is correct for flow time including overnight waits; ×12 would be an "active-time" pseudo-flow-time that is not the standard Little's Law quantity).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 weekdays (04-06–04-14, excl. outage) | Good: arrivalscompletions, WIP stable ~20 | Arrivals  99/day | mean_wip ~20 comparable across days | Low–moderate (some carryover, but near balance) | Approx stationary within regime | W  20 / 99  0.20 day  4.8 h (calendar) | Applicable (approx) |
| R2 weekends (04-11,12,18,19) | Good within weekend; not linked to weekday WIP | Arrivals  42/day | WIP ~14 weekend-only | Moderate: weekend backlog may persist to Monday | Approx stationary within weekend | W  14 / 42  0.33 day  8 h (calendar) | Approximate only |
| R3 outage day (04-15) | Not stable; WIP jumps, completions collapse | Neither arrivals nor completions valid as throughput | Not comparable | Severe | Not stationary | Not defensible | Not defensible |

**Important caveats:**
- The implied flow times are **calendar** flow times including overnight waits, because `mean_wip` is a 15-minute snapshot average over the whole day and cases wait overnight. They are **not** the active flow time, and **not** comparable to `median_service_min` (which excludes waiting).
- Comparing implied `W  4.8 h` (R1) with `median_service_min = 25` is a category error: one is total system time (wait + service, calendar), the other is active service only.
- For R5, WIP is **growing**, so the system is not in steady state. The implied `W` is an artifact of dividing an increasing `L` by an input rate; it reflects backlog growth, not a stable flow time.

# 7. Operational implications

### Staffing
- **Weekend is server-scarce, not demand-light.** `complex_pct` on weekends is comparable to weekdays (post-mix ~40–44), and median service is 29–36 min, but FTE is only 2.5–3.0. A pooled-average staffing rule would under-allocate weekend FTE because pooled average demand looks low. Under the segmented view, weekend WIP/FTE is actually high. Recommend reviewing whether 2.5 FTE is sufficient given that service times on weekends are not shorter.
- **Post-mix-change weekdays need more than a linear extrapolation of pre-mix FTE.** The mix shift raised p90 service from ~47 to ~82 min. If this is a real mix shift (not coding), effective per-case work roughly doubled in the tail. Adding 0.5–1.0 FTE (as in R6) reduced WIP from ~44 to ~36 and p90 from ~82 to ~74, suggesting partial relief but not full recovery.
- **Outage recovery requires surge capacity, not steady-state capacity.** R4's completion rate (129) is not a sustainable target.

### Backlog recovery
- After the 04-15 outage, backlog was not fully cleared before the 04-20 mix change hit. The system then carried elevated WIP (~44) for over a week. Recommendation: track opening backlog at 07:00 explicitly so recovery targets are observable.
- The 04-29 intervention appears to have drawn WIP down, but with only 3–4 days of data we cannot confirm a return to R1-equilibrium WIP. Continue monitoring.

### Peak smoothing
- `peak_08_10_pct` rises from ~28–30% (R1) to ~42–50% (R5). With 2 of 12 hours, a uniform share would be ~17%; the post-mix regime concentrates ~45% of arrivals in 17% of the hours. The representative 04-21 vector shows two intraday bursts (09–10 and 12–13).
- Recommendation: appointment scheduling or arrival smoothing for complex variants, since complex cases both arrive in bursts and consume long service time, compounding congestion (Kingman's formula: waiting scales with `c_a² + c_s²`).

### Data collection
- Capture per-case arrival, start, and completion timestamps; opening backlog at 07:00; mean service time, not only median/p90; staff schedules with actual break times; a flag for the staffing intervention start time.
- Maintain a coding-version field to disambiguate real mix shifts from reclassification.

### Policy that looks beneficial under pooled averages but risky under segmented view

**"Reduce FTE on low-arrival days to match pooled average demand."** Under pooled averages, weekend arrivals look low enough that cutting weekend FTE appears safe. Under the segmented view, weekends already have high WIP/FTE and long service times; cutting FTE further would push WIP/FTE even higher and risk weekend-to-Monday backlog spillover. The pooled recommendation is **risky**.

A second example: **"Use the 04-16 completion rate as the throughput target for staffing."** Pooled, this looks like a high-capacity day proving 7.0 FTE can clear ~129/day. Segmented, it is recovery from an outage, not sustainable. Setting it as a target would overstaff against a phantom throughput need and misattribute backlog-clearing speed to steady capacity.

# crude lag-1 autocorrelation (raw, includes trend)
lag1_raw = np.corrcoef(arr[:-1], arr[1:])[0,1]
# after removing weekday/weekend mean (very rough)
wk_mean = daily.groupby("day")["arrivals"].transform("mean")
demeaned = daily["arrivals"] - wk_mean
lag1_demeaned = np.corrcoef(demeaned.values[:-1], demeaned.values[1:])[0,1]
print(f"\nLag-1 autocorr (raw): {lag1_raw:.3f}")
print(f"Lag-1 autocorr (day-of-week demeaned): {lag1_demeaned:.3f}")

# ---- 6. Simple change-point heuristic (not ground truth) ----
# Use max absolute difference in rolling 7-day means of complex_pct.
daily["complex_pct"] = daily["complex_pct"].astype(float)
# Candidate change: compare mean of days before vs after each candidate date
def cp_stat(series):
    vals = series.values
    best = None
    for i in range(3, len(vals)-3):
        left = vals[:i]; right = vals[i:]
        stat = abs(left.mean()-right.mean())
        if best is None or stat > best[0]:
            best = (stat, i)
    return best
stat, idx = cp_stat(daily["complex_pct"])
print(f"\nCrude change-point (complex_pct): date={daily['date'].iloc[idx].date()}, "
      f"mean jump={stat:.1f} pts (left~{daily['complex_pct'].iloc[:idx].mean():.1f}, "
      f"right~{daily['complex_pct'].iloc[idx:].mean():.1f})")

# ---- 7. Little's Law implied flow time for chosen windows ----
def little_implied(df, label, throughput_col="arrivals"):
    L = df["mean_wip"].median()
    lam = df[throughput_col].median()
    W_days = L/lam if lam>0 else np.nan
    W_hours_cal = W_days*24
    return {"label":label,"L_med":L,"lambda_med":lam,
            "W_days":W_days,"W_hours_calendar":W_hours_cal}

print("\n=== Little's Law implied flow times (calendar) ===")
little_rows = []
little_rows.append(little_implied(daily[masks["R1_pre_mix_wd"]],"R1 (arrivals)"))
little_rows.append(little_implied(daily[masks["R2_weekend"]],"R2 weekend (arrivals)"))
little_rows.append(little_implied(daily[masks["R5_postmix_wd"]],"R5 (arrivals)"))
little_rows.append(little_implied(daily[masks["R5_postmix_wd"]],"R5 (completions)","completed"))
little_rows.append(little_implied(daily[masks["R6_intervention_wd"]],"R6 (arrivals)"))
