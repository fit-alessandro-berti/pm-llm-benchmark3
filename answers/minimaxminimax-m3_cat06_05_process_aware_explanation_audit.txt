## 2. Process-model consistency checks

A minimum validation suite has six layers. Each is necessary; none is sufficient on its own. A high frequency in a discovered Directly-Follows Graph (DFG) is **not** a normative rule — it only describes observed behaviour and inherits all timestamp and ingestion artefacts.

1. **Prefix-time availability (point-in-time feature audit).**
   For every feature used at `t0`, replay the case log up to and including `Coverage Check Completed` and verify the feature value can be materialized from that prefix alone. This catches `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`, `final_handler_team`, `last_resource_role`, and the sequence patterns whose both endpoints lie after `t0`. Limit: cannot detect lineage bugs where a *registered-at-registration* field is silently edited later — for those, an edit-audit log on the source table is required.

2. **Activity-order feasibility (declarative + DFG conformance).**
   Encode the normative process as a declarative model (e.g., Declare constraints) with at minimum:
   - `Coverage Check Completed` must precede any `Damage Assessment Completed`.
   - `Damage Assessment Completed` must precede the first `Settlement Decision Recorded`.
   - `Appeal` requires a preceding `Partial`/`Deny` `Settlement Decision Recorded`.
   - `Claim Closed` must follow a final `Settlement Decision Recorded` (or `Withdrawn`) and must not be followed by any other activity.
   For each case, compute conformance. DFG frequencies are reported alongside, but conformance decisions rely on the declarative model. Limit: declarative models can over-constrain (rare-but-legal paths rejected) or under-constrain (unwanted paths allowed); both should be reviewed against the process owners.

3. **Same-timestamp ambiguity handling.**
   Group events with identical timestamps per case. For each group: (a) flag the absence of a reliable sequence index; (b) attempt a deterministic ordering using the event-class hierarchy (e.g., Documents Requested < Documents Received < Documents Validated), activity semantic precedence, and resource handoff; (c) where order cannot be determined, mark the case as ambiguous and exclude from conformance-sensitive explanations. Case Z's `Claim Closed`/`Damage Assessment Completed` collision is the canonical example.

4. **Complete-trace feature detector.**
   Maintain a registry of features whose definition traverses the full trace (e.g., `total_*`, `*_anywhere`, `num_*_total`, `final_*`, `*_complete`, variant IDs over the full trace, sequence patterns whose terminal node is past `t0`). Auto-block these from prefix-time pipelines. Limit: a feature can be both complete-trace and salvageable (e.g., `final_handler_team`  `handler_team_at_t0`); the registry should be paired with a *repairability* annotation.

5. **Enabled-vs-occurred distinction.**
   Some features describe activities that are *enabled* at `t0` but have not necessarily *occurred* (Fraud Investigation is a process option after Coverage Check; Documents Requested is the entry to the evidence cycle). The model must not use observed occurrence as a `t0` feature. A "fraud risk score" from prior history is legitimate; a "fraud investigation opened (binary)" is leakage. Similarly, "expected number of document cycles for a case of this complexity" is a defensible prior; "actual document cycles" is not.

6. **Repeated Settlement Decision legitimacy.**
   Count `Settlement Decision Recorded` events per case. A count of two is legitimate **only** if a `Partial`/`Deny` is followed by an `Appeal` and then a second `Settlement Decision Recorded`. A count of two without an intervening Appeal is a process violation and must be investigated before being used in training or explanation. Counts above two should be rare and reviewed individually.

These six layers should run on every training and evaluation cut. A feature passing all six is admissible for prediction; it still needs the additional proxy/fairness review in §3 and §5 before being reported with causal language.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

The repaired signals fall into five classes. The standard each class is held to differs on three axes: predictive validity, causal interpretation, and operational actionability.

- **Defensible predictive signal (legitimate prefix-time attribute).**
  Examples after repair: `initial_damage_complexity`, `claimant_channel`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, `coverage_check_duration`, `initial_reserve` (if confirmed registration-time), and any *prefix* variant id truncated at `t0`.
  *May claim:* "associated with higher/lower breach risk in the observed data." *May not claim:* that changing the feature changes the outcome. These are inputs to the model, not levers unless an explicit causal/operational pathway exists (e.g., reassigning channel is operationally meaningful, but only if the channel–breach relation is robust to confounding).

- **Process-state or congestion signal.**
  Examples after repair: `queue_length_at_t0`, `coverage_check_duration`, time since last handover. These describe the operational state at the moment of prediction.
  *May claim:* "the case enters a busy queue," which is operationally meaningful. *May not claim:* the queue *causes* a breach in a counterfactual sense — cases routed to busy queues may also be complex, and the partial correlation is the relevant number. Actionable only insofar as queue management is a real lever (reassign, escalate).

- **Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review.**
  Examples: `claimant_region`, possibly `claimant_channel` and `initial_reserve` to the extent they correlate with socioeconomic factors.
  *May claim:* the feature has predictive value. *May not claim:* the feature is a delay *driver* or that interventions targeted at the feature (e.g., differential treatment by region) are appropriate. A fairness audit must quantify breach-rate and feature-importance disparities across protected groups mediated by the proxy, and check whether the model would behave differently if the proxy were replaced by a structural mediator (e.g., repair-network density in the region).

- **Drift / calendar proxy.**
  Examples: `calendar_week` (week 52 = holiday period, weeks surrounding the policy change). The feature is measured at `t0`, but its importance reflects regime conditions.
  *May claim:* performance may degrade when those regime conditions change. *May not claim:* week-of-year causes delay. Actionability: monitor performance by `policy_regime_id`; consider de-weighting or stratifying.

- **Unusable until lineage is repaired.**
  Examples: `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (SQL off-by-one), `same_day_document_count_snapshot` (post-`t0` uploads included), `final_handler_team` (no historical timestamp), `risk_score_current` (mutable, no timestamp), `last_resource_role` (complete-case definition), and all full-trace aggregates.
  *May not claim:* anything until a point-in-time computation is built and back-tested. Their current SHAP values must not be reported as `t0` evidence.

The important meta-point: a feature can be highly predictive (good AUC contribution) yet still fail the causal or actionable test. Predictive validity, causal interpretation, and operational actionability are three separate bars; conflating them is the dominant failure mode in the team's narrative.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

Prioritized plan, ordered by leverage and cost. Items 1–4 are prerequisites for trustworthy SHAP reporting; 5–8 govern ongoing trust.

1. **Feature-store redesign with point-in-time semantics.**
   Treat every feature as having a `valid_from` / `valid_to` window and a definition that includes the SQL used to compute it. The lineage notes in this case (off-by-one on `prior_claim_count`, post-`t0` documents in `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, mutable `risk_score_current`, no historical timestamp on `final_handler_team`) are exactly what a feature store prevents. Enforce that any feature used at `t0` is materialized from events with timestamp  `t0` and from attribute snapshots whose `valid_from  t0`.

2. **Historical feature timestamps and immutable audit trail.**
   For mutable warehouse fields (handler team, risk score, complexity edits), require an append-only change log with effective timestamps. This is the only way to recover the value that *was* true at `t0` and to detect late edits.

3. **Prefix-only recomputation pipeline.**
   Build a "prefix replay" that, for each case, materializes the full feature vector at `t0` from the prefix log alone. Re-train the model on these vectors. SHAP explanations then reflect what the model could have known at prediction time. Compare the prefix-only model's calibration and ranking against the leaky model to quantify leakage-driven inflation.

4. **Process-conformance test suite in CI.**
   Run the six-layer suite from §2 on every training and evaluation cut. Block promotion of any feature that fails the prefix-time availability check, and block any sequence pattern whose endpoints are not both  `t0` or not both process-legitimate. Maintain a per-process-version conformance report so that normative edits trigger re-validation.

5. **Calibration after feature removal.**
   After dropping the leaky features, recalibrate (Platt or isotonic) on a hold-out set that is itself prefix-clean. Report Brier score, AUC, and calibration plots stratified by `initial_damage_complexity`, `claimant_region`, and `policy_regime_id`. A large drop in AUC after leakage removal is itself diagnostic: it confirms that the original model was leaning on future information.

6. **Explanation stability checks.**
   For each case, perturb prefix features within plausible ranges (queue length ±2, complexity left as registered, channel unchanged) and report the variance of the predicted probability and the top-3 SHAP contributors. Unstable explanations on minor perturbations indicate either fragile features or attribution artefacts from multicollinearity between proxies and direct signals.

7. **Proxy / fairness analysis.**
   For each retained proxy (`claimant_region`, and arguably `claimant_channel`, `initial_reserve`), fit a mediated model that includes the structural mediator (repair-network density, language coverage, claim-severity band) and report the change in the proxy's SHAP importance. Quantify breach-rate and feature-importance disparities across protected groups. Set a minimum standard: any proxy whose importance does not materially drop when its mediators are included should be reviewed for removal or explicit fairness mitigation.

8. **Monitoring for schema and process drift.**
   Track, on a rolling window: (a) prefix-time availability pass rate of the feature set; (b) frequency of new sequence patterns; (c) the share of cases that violate any declarative constraint, including the `Claim Closed`/`Damage Assessment Completed` ordering anomaly; (d) `policy_regime_id` distribution and per-regime calibration; (e) disparity metrics on `claimant_region` and `claimant_channel`. Alerts should fire when any metric moves beyond a tolerance band tied to the last fully validated release.

The cumulative effect of items 1–4 is that SHAP values reported alongside predictions become auditable claims about what the model knew at `t0` rather than post-hoc rationalizations; items 5–8 keep that property intact as data, policy, and process evolve. The normative process and its versioning remain the ground truth against which every feature and every pattern is judged.
