1. System boundary and assumptions
• Gate-to-gate scope: from “Receive order” until “Confirm delivery / Close”.  
– Inside: machine electricity (energy_kwh), process scrap (material_scrap_kg), packaging material (package_material_kg), internal rework loops, on-site batching waits, outbound transport (transport_kgCO2e, route_mode).  
– Outside: supplier upstream impacts, customer use & end-of-life, employee commuting, empty-pack return.  
• Functional unit: one customer order as logged (may contain several line items).  
• Allocation: batch energy and scrap are divided over the items actually shipped; unused stock after 14 days is counted as obsolescence scrap.  
• Data uncertainty: -10 % / +20 % on energy meters, ±15 % on transport kgCO2e (routing assumption), rework under-reporting 5 %.  
• Service-level boundary: promised_lead_time_hours and late-delivery flag set contractual limits that may not be exceeded for Service-contract or Critical-downtime segments.

3. Intervention options

I1. Green-shipping recommender (S1 + S4 combined)  
• Target: Standard-stock Service-contract orders currently using parcel_air.  
• Mechanism: if promised_lead_time_hours – expected ground transport time > 24 h and SP1 85 % confidence, default to parcel_ground; keep parcel_air flag for exceptions.  
• Service/quality/resilience: negligible cycle-time increase (<24 h slack); keeps alternative air in reserve for disruptions (R1).  
• Evidence: promised lead-time vs actual arrival, SP1 overrides logged.

I2. Twice-daily consolidation departures (S5)  
• Target: Stock orders eligible for consolidated_truck.  
• Mechanism: fixed 11 a.m. / 8 p.m. truck runs reduce waiting from 12-24 h to max 9 h while preserving load factor.  
• Effect: –35-55 % transport kgCO2e, small cycle-time improvement, more predictable resilience.  
• Evidence: timestamp between “Package” and “Ship consolidated”; load factor logs.

I3. Dynamic batching with waiting-time caps (S2)  
• Target: Made-to-order jobs on CNC_A and heat_treat.  
• Mechanism: form batches until either (a) size 8 or (b) oldest job waited 12 h (Service-contract) / 18 h (Regular).  
• Effect: –25-35 % machine kWh vs immediate runs; late-delivery drop vs legacy large batches; limit obsolescence.  
• Evidence: batch_id, batch_size, Batch wait, energy_kwh, due-date slack.

I4. Queue-aware routing from CNC_B to CNC_A (S3)  
• Target: MTO Regular & Service-contract where CNC_A queue 6 h.  
• Mechanism: prefer CNC_A (lower rework, lower kWh) unless due-date risk >10 %.  
• Effect: –15 % energy, –9 % rework loops, slight cycle-time increase for a minority.  
• Evidence: machine_group, rework_flag, inspection outcomes.

I5. Packaging-route compatibility check (S6)  
• Target: all orders before “Ship”.  
• Mechanism: rules engine couples part geometry, packaging type, and route_mode; flags mismatch before dispatch.  
• Effect: –60 % packaging rework, –8 % returns; neutral energy; small cycle-time gain.  
• Evidence: rework_flag, route_mode, package_material_kg.

I6. Extra inspection gate for high-risk combos (S7)  
• Target: CNC_B heavy parts >8 kg.  
• Mechanism: enforce second optical check; only proceed to packaging if pass.  
• Effect: –40 % downstream rework material waste; +1-2 h cycle-time; improves resilience (fewer field failures).  
• Evidence: Inspection_cell timestamps, rework loops.

I7. Shared emergency courier lanes (S8 + S9)  
• Target: Critical-downtime Service-contract orders.  
• Mechanism: pool urgent loads from multiple distributors into scheduled regional micro-hubs (milk-run) instead of single dedicated vans.  
• Effect: –25-40 % transport kgCO2e per urgent order; keeps 15 h median lead-time; resilience improved (redundant capacity).  
• Evidence: route_mode change to “shared_courier”, kgCO2e, cycle time.

I8. Partial-shipment suppression with availability logic (S11)  
• Target: Standard-stock Regular orders prone to backorder + second shipment.  
• Mechanism: only allow partial shipment when >70 % of lines available AND customer flagged “urgent”; otherwise wait up to 24 h for replenishment.  
• Effect: –1.6 kgCO2e/order (fewer second transports), –0.04 kg scrap (less repack); slight  late-delivery from 7 %9 % acceptable to Regular customers.  
• Evidence: partial-shipment path frequency, customer_type, lateness.

I9. Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation (S10)  
• Target: Regular Made-to-order quotes issued by sales portal.  
• Mechanism: show two options: “standard green” (longer lead time, ground) vs “fast/high-carbon” surcharge.  
• Effect: demand displacement of ~15 % MTO orders to longer lead; energy unchanged, transport –20 %; resilience benefit (capacity buffer).  
• Evidence: promised_lead_time_hours, chosen option, subsequent route_mode.

I10. Sustainability exception log & approval (S12)  
• Cross-cutting governance: require explicit approval event for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides.  
• Mechanism: visibility deters unjustified exceptions.  
• Effect: expected 50 % drop in violations; negligible service impact; resilience protected via formal override path.  
• Evidence: presence of “override_approved” event, automatic KPI.

5. Rebound and displacement risks
1. Batching rebound: larger batches cut machine kWh but can overrun promised lead-time and generate obsolete stock (scrap ).  
2. Transport displacement: switching from air to ground may push more orders into “express ground” carrier with lower reliability, risking emergency re-ship by air.  
3. Packaging trade-off: reinforced packaging halves damage returns but doubles material; need risk-based rule to avoid excess.  
4. Rework rebound: stricter inspection lowers field failures but adds indoor energy/time; must cap at value-adding checks.  
5. Obsolescence displacement: delayed partial shipments reduction may shift inventory holding upstream, increasing warehouse energy not in current boundary.  
6. Customer behaviour: carbon-aware promise option might tempt sales teams to always offer “fast/high-carbon” if commission biased toward revenue; governance needed.  

6. Balanced recommendation
Adopt an integrated “Eco-Service Flow” redesign containing:

A. Transport layer  
• Deploy I1 (green-shipping recommender) and I2 (scheduled consolidation) to cut routine transport CO2e while meeting SP1.  
• Keep air and dedicated van capacity as safeguarded overrides logged via I10; introduce I7 shared emergency courier for Critical-true-downtime only.

B. Production & batching layer  
• Implement I3 dynamic batching with waiting caps and I4 queue-aware CNC routing. This keeps energy low without late deliveries or high rework.  
• Add I6 extra inspection gate for known high-risk machine/part combos.

C. Order-fulfilment layer  
• Enforce I5 packaging-route compatibility and Q1 rule conformance.  
• Apply I8 partial-shipment suppression with 24 h hold window; communicates expected delay transparently.  
• Offer I9 carbon-aware promise options to Regular MTO customers, smoothing workload peaks.

D. Governance & visibility  
• Activate I10 sustainability exception log; weekly dashboards showing E1-T2-M2-S2 indicators, SP1/SP2/Q1 violations, and alternative-route success (R1).  

This package jointly delivers an estimated 20-25 % reduction in average transport CO2e, 10-15 % reduction in machine energy, and 15-20 % cut in rework/material waste, while keeping late-delivery for Service-contract 5 % and preserving a rapid Critical-downtime path with improved shared resilience.

7. Validation and governance plan
Event-log based closed-loop monitoring (quarterly):

1. Conformance checks  
• SP1/SP2/Q1 violation ratio 5 %; any override must carry “override_approved” event.  
• Process-mining model recalibrated monthly to ensure new paths (shared_courier, carbon_aware) represented.

2. KPI tracking by segment  
• S1, S2 (cycle time, late delivery) and R1 (alternative route success) for each customer_type & case_type.  
• Energy (E1), Transport (T1), Material (M2) trends with control charts; trigger investigation at 2  drift.

3. Intervention dashboards  
• Dynamic batching performance: B1 median wait <12 h (Service-contract) / <18 h (Regular), batch energy table.  
• Green-shipping recommender hit rate and override reasons.  
• Shared courier load factor >70 %, lead-time p90 <24 h.

4. Post-deployment audits  
• Quarterly matched-pair comparison of orders pre/post redesign for late deliveries and customer complaints.  
• Annual LCA boundary review – decide whether to extend to inbound supply and warehouse utilities.

5. Governance structure  
• Sustainability steering committee with Operations, Sales, Quality; meets monthly.  
• Exception log reviewed weekly; chronic override patterns trigger root-cause kaizen.  
• Continuous improvement loop feeds new rules into recommender and batching algorithms.

By grounding every change in event-log evidence and tying governance to explicit policies, the distributor can cut environmental impact substantially without surrendering the service, quality, and resilience its customers require.
