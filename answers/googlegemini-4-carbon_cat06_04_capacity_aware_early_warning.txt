## 1. Decision rule

A deployable intervention policy cannot rely on observational breach probability ($p_{\text{now}}$) alone, because high risk does not imply high *treatability*, and intervening imposes externalities on the shared diagnosis queue. We deploy a four-gate **Hurwicz-Adjusted Net Portfolio Value (NPV)** policy:

### Gate 1: Process & Data Eligibility (Hard Filters)
1. **Stage Eligibility:** Case must be strictly prior to `Diagnosis Complete` (`Triage` or `Diagnosis Started`). Post-diagnosis cases receive zero swarm allocation (redirected to resolution managers if needed).
2. **Feasibility Horizon:** Time to SLA deadline must exceed the swarm duration plus minimal resolution handoff ($\ge 1.25\text{ hours}$).
3. **Data Integrity:** Key dynamic features (e.g., live queue congestion) must be observed, not imputed. If a sharp risk jump ($\Delta p > 0.15$) coincides with feature imputation, place the case on a **15-minute Verification Hold** rather than consuming immediate capacity.
4. **History & Evidence Gate:** A case is eligible on its first alert, OR on a subsequent alert *if and only if* either:
   - No prior swarm occurred AND a **materially new event** occurred (e.g., customer log upload, stage transition, scope change), OR
   - It was previously deferred strictly due to capacity scarcity (not suppressed as stale).
   Pure clock-refresh alerts without new event payloads are suppressed.

### Gate 2: Segment Calibration Adjustment ($p_{\text{cal}}$)
Before evaluating risk and false-alarm probabilities, adjust raw $p_{\text{now}}$ for known backtest drift:
- **SME:** $p_{\text{cal}} = \min(0.99,\; p_{\text{now}} + 0.065)$ (correcting $+0.04$ to $+0.09$ underprediction).
- **Strategic Enterprise:** $p_{\text{cal}} = \max(0.01,\; p_{\text{now}} - 0.085)$ (correcting $-0.05$ to $-0.12$ post-redesign overprediction).
- **Public Sector:** $p_{\text{cal}} = p_{\text{now}}$ (acceptable calibration).
- **Non-Native-Language Support (NNLS):** $p_{\text{cal}} = p_{\text{now}}$, tagged with a high-epistemic-uncertainty penalty in treatment scoring (below).

### Gate 3: Ambiguity-Averse Direct Value Scoring
Because supplied absolute risk reductions $[\Delta_{\min}, \Delta_{\max}]$ are uncertain observational/expert bounds rather than identified causal effects—and because $p_{\text{cal}}$ caps the maximum possible breach reduction—we compute a conservative **effective risk reduction** $\Delta_{\text{eff}}$:
1. Bound the upper effect by calibrated risk: $\Delta'_{\max} = \min(\Delta_{\max},\; p_{\text{cal}})$.
2. Apply a Hurwicz pessimism weight $w_{\text{low}}$ to hedge against model misspecification ($w_{\text{low}} = 0.60$ for standard segments; $w_{\text{low}} = 0.70$ for sparse segments like NNLS):
   $$\Delta_{\text{eff}} = w_{\text{low}} \Delta_{\min} + (1 - w_{\text{low}}) \Delta'_{\max}$$

Next, compute **Robust Expected Direct Benefit ($\text{EDB}$)**:
$$\text{EDB}_i = \Delta_{\text{eff}, i} \cdot V_i - C_{\text{swarm}} - (1 - p_{\text{cal}, i}) \cdot B_{\text{FA}, i}$$
where $V_i$ is breach-avoidance value, $C_{\text{swarm}} = €450$, and $(1 - p_{\text{cal}, i}) B_{\text{FA}, i}$ weights false-alarm disruption by the calibrated probability that the case would *not* have breached organically (using full $B_{\text{FA}, i}$ in stress testing).

### Gate 4: Spillover Hurdle & Reserve Shadow Pricing
Each swarm imposes congestion externalities on ~6 untreated `Diagnosis`-stage cases ($\approx 0.01\text{–}0.03$ added breach risk each). Assuming an average untreated queue case value of $€15,000\text{–}€20,000$, the expected **marginal spillover harm ($E_{\text{spill}}$)** under base congestion is:
$$E_{\text{spill}} \approx 6 \times 0.02 \times €16,700 \approx €2,000 \text{ per slot}$$
(rising to $€3,000+$ as more slots are consumed simultaneously and specialist interruptions compound).

- **Minimum Hurdle:** Intervene only if $\text{EDB}_i > E_{\text{spill}}$ (at least $+€2,000$).
- **Urgency & Option-Value Deferral:** If Time to SLA $\ge 7.0\text{ h}$ and stage is `Triage`, ordinary triage may resolve ambiguity without specialist interruption. Apply a deferral discount unless $\text{EDB}_i$ exceeds both $E_{\text{spill}}$ and the option value of waiting ($€3,500$).
- **Regulatory Reserve Rule:** Hold Slot 4 in reserve until 15:00 with a shadow price of **$€4,500$** (reflecting regulatory penalty/reputational exposure). Release Slot 4 before 15:00 only if a live case has Net Portfolio Value ($\text{EDB}_i - E_{\text{spill}}$) $> €4,500$ AND faces irreversible SLA decay within $<2\text{ hours}$.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

Because the model scores prefixes every 30 minutes, naive threshold alerting conflates **persistent state** with **new information**. Our temporal state machine governs every case across four transition rules:

1. **Suppression & Cool-Down (Anti-Chatter Rule):**
   - When an alert fires and is *not* selected for immediate intervention (due to sub-hurdle score), the case enters a **90-minute (3-cycle) Cool-Down**.
   - Subsequent 30-minute refreshes are **suppressed** unless the case clears the **Material Evidence Gate**: (a) an event-log state transition (e.g., `Triage` $\to$ `Diagnosis Started`), (b) an external artifact arrival (customer log, config dump, dependency failure notice), or (c) an organic jump $\Delta p_{\text{cal}} \ge +0.12$ driven by *non-imputed* case features (excluding pure elapsed-time clock decay).
2. **Capacity-Wait vs. Stale-Suppression Distinction:**
   - Cases placed in **Wait** for strategic/data reasons (**I-06** data hold, **I-08** option-value deferral, **I-01** urgency wait) remain in an active `Deferred Pool` evaluated at scheduled checkpoints (12:15 for I-06; 14:00/15:00 for I-01/I-08), rather than being silenced indefinitely.
3. **Re-Entry After Prior Non-Intervention vs. Prior Swarm:**
   - *No prior swarm:* Re-enters active ranking immediately upon clearing the Material Evidence Gate (e.g., **I-02** is on its second alert with no prior swarm; if its first alert was in `Triage` and it has now entered `Diagnosis Started` with $+0.08$ higher risk, stage transition + escalating diagnostic stall justifies active eligibility).
   - *Prior swarm already delivered:* Hard-locked against a second swarm **unless** an exogenous scope-changing event occurs (e.g., hidden multi-system outage discovered) AND a specialist lead signs off that a second 45-minute swarm has positive marginal diagnostic yield before `Diagnosis Complete`.
4. **Deadline-Decay Escalation & Cutoff:**
   - As Time to SLA drops below $3.5\text{ h}$ while still in `Triage` or `Diagnosis Started`, deferral discounts vanish (act now or lose treatability).
   - Once Time to SLA drops below **$1.25\text{ h}$** (or stage hits `Diagnosis Complete`), swarm eligibility terminates automatically; the alert routes to a **Non-Swarm Fast-Track** (managerial customer comms / workaround deployment) that does not interrupt diagnostic specialists.

### Explicit Comparison: I-05 vs. I-07
- **I-05 (`p_prev = 0.55` $\to$ `p_now = 0.57`, 3rd alert, no new event):** Classic clock-driven chatter. The $+0.02$ tick reflects 30 minutes less slack on the 5.0 h SLA clock, not new diagnostic complexity. Re-evaluating I-05 every 30 minutes invites operator fatigue or accidental slot consumption. Under our policy, I-05 is **suppressed** until it transitions to `Diagnosis Started` or logs a new diagnostic obstacle.
- **I-07 (`p_prev = 0.75` $\to$ `p_now = 0.72`, 3rd alert, new customer log uploaded):** Illustrates why **probability deltas ($\Delta p$) cannot substitute for event semantics**. Although `p_now` *decreased* by $0.03$ (likely because historical cases where customers upload logs slightly outperform non-responsive customers), the uploaded log is a **materially new diagnostic substrate** that unlocks previously blocked root-cause analysis. Thus, I-07 **breaks cool-down and re-enters active evaluation**. However, *eligibility does not guarantee swarm allocation*: with $€5,000$ contract value and only 1.5 h remaining, spending a 2-specialist swarm on I-07 fails the portfolio spillover test ($\text{EDB} \approx €780 < €2,000$ spillover). Instead, I-07’s valid alert triggers an immediate non-swarm action (flagging the assigned triage engineer to parse the new log immediately).

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

Optimizing expected monetary value under capacity constraints creates five structural pathologies that will degrade both equity and model validity over time unless bounded by hard operational guardrails:

1. **Starvation of Lower-Value Segments (SMEs):**
   - *Risk:* Because Strategic Enterprise values ($€25\text{k}\text{–}€100\text{k}$) are $5\text{–}20\times$ SME values ($€5\text{k}\text{–}€11\text{k}$), pure $\text{EDB}$ ranking permanently starves SMEs (as seen today: I-05, I-07, and I-10 receive zero swarms, while SMEs simultaneously absorb queue delays). Chronic SME breaches trigger churn and reputational damage not captured in single-ticket SLA penalties.
   - *Concrete Safeguard:* Implement a **Weekly Segment Floor & Non-Disruptive Tier**: (a) Cap Strategic Enterprise consumption at $\le 75\%$ of weekly swarm slots, reserving a protected **SME/Public Sector Equity Quota** (at least 15–20% of weekly slots) ranked by *relative breach-risk reduction* ($\Delta_{\text{eff}}$) rather than euro value; (b) pair denied high-risk SME cases (like I-07) with a lightweight **15-minute Single-Lead Consult** sourced outside the shared diagnosis specialist pool.
2. **Epistemic Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support (NNLS):**
   - *Risk:* NNLS cases (like **I-09**) have small validation samples, making both $p_{\text{now}}$ and treatment bounds wide. Pure ambiguity aversion ($w_{\text{low}} = 0.70$) systematically penalizes minority/NNLS queues simply for having less historical data; conversely, naive intervention fails if language friction—not technical complexity—is the bottleneck.
   - *Concrete Safeguard:* Pair NNLS swarms with bilingual specialist matching or real-time translation tooling (ensuring the intervention addresses the actual mechanism), and replace pure pessimism penalties with an **Upper-Confidence-Bound (UCB) Exploration Bonus** capped at 1 slot per week for sparse segments to actively shrink epistemic variance.
3. **Differential Calibration Across Segments:**
   - *Risk:* Unadjusted probabilities systematically transfer capacity from SMEs (underpredicted by $0.04\text{–}0.09$) to Strategic Enterprise (overpredicted by $0.05\text{–}0.12$).
   - *Concrete Safeguard:* Enforce **Segment-Stratified Isotonic/Platt Recalibration** prior to policy scoring (Gate 2) and require segment-level Expected Calibration Error ($\text{ECE} < 0.03$) before deploying raw probabilities.
4. **Performative Prediction & Selective Label Censorship:**
   - *Risk:* If we consistently swarm high-risk Strategic cases (I-04, I-02) and prevent their breaches, future observational retraining on realized outcomes ($Y \in \{0,1\}$) will learn that Strategic cases with these prefixes *do not breach*—causing future models to severely underpredict risk on treated profiles ("victim of its own success") while reinforcing intervention bias.
   - *Concrete Safeguard:* Log the complete intervention tuple `(prefix_features, propensity_score, action_taken, slot_congestion)` and **never retrain the baseline risk model naively on treated cases**. Estimate $P(Y^0 = 1 \mid X)$ (untreated counterfactual risk) using **Doubly Robust / Inverse Propensity Weighting (IPW)** on untreated cases and randomized/quasi-experimental holdouts.
5. **Regressive Spillover Concentration on Untreated Groups:**
   - *Risk:* Swarms pull senior specialists off the shared `Diagnosis` queue. Because Strategic cases get rescued via swarms while SME and Public Sector cases sit untreated in the shared queue, **SMEs pay the time tax (8–30 min/slot delay) to subsidize Strategic SLA compliance**, compounding breach disparities.
   - *Concrete Safeguard:* Enforce a **Queue Congestion Circuit Breaker**: track cumulative daily spillover delay attached to each untreated active ticket. Any untreated case that accumulates $\ge 25\text{ minutes}$ of swarm-induced specialist delay receives automatic priority-queue promotion ahead of newly arriving work, and total daily swarms are throttled from 4 to 2 whenever queue utilization exceeds 85%.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

To keep the policy honest over time, we monitor five coupled domains on weekly and monthly cadences, backed by an explicit causal identification strategy and governance triggers.

### 1. Five-Pillar Monitoring Framework
- **Operational & Data-Health Monitoring (Real-Time / Daily):**
  - Feature missingness/imputation rate (alerting immediately on failures like **I-06**’s queue field), alert firing rate per 30-min cycle, cool-down suppression ratio, false-alarm dispute rate reported by account teams, and reserve-slot utilization before vs. after 15:00.
- **Predictive Calibration Monitoring (Bi-Weekly, Untreated Cohort Only):**
  - Segment-stratified Brier score, reliability diagrams, and $\text{ECE}$ across SME, Strategic, Public Sector, and NNLS—evaluated strictly on *untreated* cases (weighted by inverse propensity of non-treatment) across `Triage` and `Diagnosis Started` prefixes.
- **Causal-Effect Monitoring (Monthly):**
  - Realized diagnosis duration (`Diagnosis Started` $\to$ `Diagnosis Complete` time saved) as an immediate proximal mediator (high signal-to-noise), alongside ultimate SLA breach delta ($\hat{\tau}_{\text{CATE}}$) stratified by segment and stage.
- **Capacity & Spillover Monitoring (Daily / Weekly):**
  - Queue wait-time inflation during and 2 hours after each swarm (testing whether actual aggregate delay matches the $8\text{–}20\text{ min}$ base assumption or hits the $15\text{–}30\text{ min}$ adverse regime) and realized breach rates of the untreated `Diagnosis` cohort on 0-slot, 1–2-slot, and 3–4-slot days.
- **Fairness & Starvation Monitoring (Weekly):**
  - Segment share of swarm allocations vs. segment share of SLA breaches; cumulative spillover delay minutes borne by SME/Public/NNLS tickets vs. Strategic tickets.

### 2. Causal Identification Plan (Estimating Treatment & Spillover Effects Without Unethical Holdouts)
We cannot randomly deny swarms to obvious catastrophic cases (like I-04), so we combine three ethical identification designs:
1. **Borderline Tie-Breaker Randomization (Micro-Experimentation):** For cases falling in the "marginal net value zone" ($\text{EDB} - E_{\text{spill}} \in [€0, €2,500]$, such as I-01, I-02, I-06), randomize swarm allocation with known probabilities $p \in [0.3, 0.7]$ when capacity permits. This yields clean, unconfounded local causal estimates and valid IPW weights.
2. **Regression Discontinuity / Instrumental Variables via Exogenous Scarcity:** Exploit quasi-random variation across shifts—comparing identical-score cases arriving when specialists are unavailable (or post-15:00 reserve release vs. pre-15:00 lock) as an instrument for swarm receipt.
3. **Cluster-Time Exposure Variation for Spillovers:** Estimate queue spillover ($\gamma$) by regressing untreated `Diagnosis`-stage case delays and breaches on the number of concurrent swarms in that 2-hour window, controlling for baseline arrival volume and staffing levels.

### 3. Detecting Policy-Induced Drift & Recalibration Triggers
Change the probability adjustments, hurdle rates, or allocation rule when any of the following concrete thresholds trip:
- **Calibration Drift Trigger:** If segment-specific $|\text{Mean Predicted } p_{\text{cal}} - \text{IPW-Adjusted Observed Breach Rate}| > 0.04$ over a rolling 100-case window (or 30 cases using Bayesian shrinkage for NNLS), update Gate 2 segment offsets or refit the counterfactual baseline model.
- **Spillover Escalation Trigger (Dynamic Hurdle Update):** If measured aggregate queue delay exceeds **22 minutes per slot** (or untreated diagnosis breach rate rises by $>0.025$ per slot) over two consecutive weeks, automatically raise $E_{\text{spill}}$ from $€2,000$ to $€3,500$ and cap concurrent swarms at 1 per 2-hour block.
- **Effect Decay / Stage Misalignment Trigger:** If proximal `Diagnosis` time saved by swarms drops below **15 minutes** in a segment (indicating ineffective swarms or misdiagnosed alerts), increase that segment’s pessimism weight $w_{\text{low}}$ to $0.80$ and audit swarm playbooks.
- **Reserve Rule Adjustment:** Track empirical pre-15:00 regulatory arrivals over 60 business days. If arrival frequency $\times$ expected breach value saved falls below $€2,500$/day, lower the Slot 4 shadow price from $€4,500$ to $€2,500$ so high-value deferred cases (like **I-08** or validated **I-06**) are not needlessly starved.
