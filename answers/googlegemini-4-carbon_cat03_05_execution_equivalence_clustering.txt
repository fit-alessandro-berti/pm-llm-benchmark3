## 1. Equivalence design space

Clustering object-centric executions requires choosing which structural, temporal, and domain distinctions constitute signal versus noise. Letting $\mathcal{X}$ denote the universe of Order-centered executions, where each $X \in \mathcal{X}$ comprises timestamped events $E$, typed objects $O$ partitioned into focal lead (`LEAD`), execution-local (`LOCAL`), and external (`EXT`) scopes, event-to-object incidence $R_{E2O} \subseteq E \times O$, and qualified object-to-object relations $R_{O2O} \subseteq O \times \Sigma_{Q} \times O$, we define four progressively domain-aware equivalence relations.

### 1.1 Activity-sequence equivalence ($\equiv_{\text{seq}}$)
* **Operational definition:** Project $X$ onto the sequence of activity labels of events incident to the lead `Order` object: $\pi_{\text{seq}}(X) = \langle \text{act}(e_1), \dots, \text{act}(e_n) \rangle$ ordered by timestamp. $X_i \equiv_{\text{seq}} X_j \iff \pi_{\text{seq}}(X_i) = \pi_{\text{seq}}(X_j)$.
* **Invariants preserved:** Event count involving the lead order, activity label multiset, and sequential control-flow order along the `Order` lifecycle.
* **Information discarded:** All non-lead object types, object cardinalities, E2O bindings, `EXT` boundary crossings, and all O2O topologies and qualifiers.
* **Equivalence properties:** Reflexive, symmetric, and transitive, provided simultaneous events are broken deterministically; equality of projected sequences is a true equivalence relation.
* **Concurrency / same-time events:** Because $\equiv_{\text{seq}}$ assumes a total order, simultaneous events must be represented as lexicographically sorted multisets at each timestamp step (a step sequence); true concurrency of independent sub-objects is otherwise flattened into arbitrary interleavings.

### 1.2 Typed structural equivalence under identifier renaming ($\equiv_{\text{struct}}$)
* **Formal definition:** $X_i \equiv_{\text{struct}} X_j$ iff there exist bijections $\phi_E: E_i \to E_j$ and $\phi_O: O_i \to O_j$ such that: (1) $\text{act}(e) = \text{act}(\phi_E(e))$ and $e_a <_t e_b \iff \phi_E(e_a) <_t \phi_E(e_b)$; (2) $\text{type}(o) = \text{type}(\phi_O(o))$ and $\text{scope}(o) = \text{scope}(\phi_O(o))$ for $\text{scope} \in \{\text{LEAD}, \text{LOCAL}, \text{EXT}\}$; (3) $(e, o) \in R_{E2O, i} \iff (\phi_E(e), \phi_O(o)) \in R_{E2O, j}$; and (4) $(o_1, q, o_2) \in R_{O2O, i} \iff (\phi_O(o_1), q, \phi_O(o_2)) \in R_{O2O, j}$.
* **Invariants preserved:** Exact object multiplicities per `(type, scope)`, exact event counts, complete E2O hypergraph topology, qualified O2O directed multigraph, and strict temporal order.
* **Information discarded:** Raw local object identifiers (e.g., `I11` vs. `I22`) and absolute wall-clock timestamps (preserving only ordinal spacing).
* **Equivalence properties:** Reflexive (identity maps), symmetric (inverse bijections), and transitive (composition of bijections).
* **Concurrency / same-time events:** Handled cleanly by replacing strict total order $<_t$ with a weak order $\preceq_t$ (events at equal timestamps form unordered antichains invariant under $\phi_E$). However, asynchronous interleavings of causally independent branches (e.g., packing two separate deliveries) remain non-isomorphic.

### 1.3 Boundary-aware business-semantic equivalence ($\equiv_{\text{sem}}$, Preferred)
* **Operational definition:** Two executions are equivalent iff they share the same canonical semantic signature $\sigma_{\text{sem}}(X)$ (formalized in Section 2), which: (1) tiers customer- and finance-facing commitment object cardinalities (`Delivery`, `Invoice`, `Payment`) into $\{0, 1, \text{MANY}\}$ while abstracting internal handling cardinalities (`Item`, `Package`) to existence ($\ge 1$); (2) folds homogeneous internal steps (`Add Item`, intra-delivery `Pack Package`, and per-invoice `Record Payment` repetitions) into stage milestones; (3) replaces raw global event order with a causal partial order induced by shared boundary objects (treating independent delivery waves as concurrent unless linked by rework objects); and (4) preserves deduplicated sets of scoped E2O incidence pairs `(ObjectType, Scope)` per milestone and scoped O2O qualifier triples `(SrcType:Scope, qualifier, DstType:Scope)`.
* **Invariants preserved:** Customer shipment fragmentation (`1` vs. `MANY` deliveries), billing/settlement fragmentation (`1` vs. `MANY` invoices/payments), domain-specific externality (`EXT` in logistics vs. finance), anomalous E2O bindings (e.g., item-level dispatch modifications), O2O semantic qualifiers, and sequential rework loops.
* **Information discarded:** Local IDs, exact line-item counts (`2` vs. `10` items), internal warehouse carton splits consolidated into one delivery (`X3`), and benign interleavings of independent shipping waves.
* **Equivalence properties:** Reflexive, symmetric, and transitive, as $\equiv_{\text{sem}}$ is the kernel of a deterministic canonical map $\sigma_{\text{sem}}$. (Unlike edit-distance clustering, no non-transitive threshold is used.)
* **Concurrency / same-time events:** Native partial-order treatment. Events sharing no causal path through non-lead sub-objects (`Delivery`, `Invoice`, returned `Item`) commute, so both simultaneous timestamps and arbitrary interleavings map to the same canonical DAG.

### 1.4 Coarse governance-outcome equivalence ($\equiv_{\text{gov}}$)
* **Operational definition:** Abstracts $\sigma_{\text{sem}}(X)$ into a 3-tuple `(IsolationStatus, CommitmentPattern, ExceptionClass)` where `IsolationStatus` $\in \{\text{ISOLATED}, \text{EXT\_SHARED}\}$ (merging logistics and financial sharing), `CommitmentPattern` $\in \{\text{SINGLE\_PASS}, \text{PHASED}\}$ (`MANY` deliveries or payments without rework), and `ExceptionClass` $\in \{\text{CLEAN}, \text{MODIFIED\_OR\_REWORKED}\}$ (any substitution qualifier or return/repack milestone).
* **Invariants preserved:** High-level SLA conformance: whether an order was isolated, single-pass, and exception-free.
* **Information discarded:** Which department shared external objects (`X5` vs. `X6`) and whether rework occurred pre-dispatch (`X7`) or post-dispatch (`X8`).
* **Equivalence properties & concurrency:** Reflexive, symmetric, transitive (deterministic coarsening of $\sigma_{\text{sem}}$); completely order-insensitive outside the presence of exception motifs.

## 2. Canonical signatures

Our preferred relation $\equiv_{\text{sem}}$ computes a deterministic, hashable signature $\sigma_{\text{sem}}(X)$ invariant to local renaming. First, every object is assigned a scope (`LEAD` for the focal order, `EXT` for `EXT-*` identifiers or non-focal orders and their items, and `LOCAL` otherwise). Next, boundary multiplicities for `Delivery`, `Invoice`, and `Payment` are bucketed into `0`, `1`, or `MANY`. Events are folded into causal stages: line additions collapse into `ADD_ITEMS`; each `Delivery` forms a `FULFILL_WAVE` aggregating its upstream `Pack Package` events and `Dispatch` event; consecutive `Issue Invoice` and `Record Payment` events per invoice form `BILL_SETTLE` stages; and explicit rework activities (`Return Item`, `Inspect Return`) remain distinct milestones. Causal edges connect stages only when an earlier stage produces or dispatches a non-lead sub-object (`Package`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`, or returned `Item`) consumed by a later stage, plus global ordering from `ORDER_INIT` (`Create Order` $\to$ `ADD_ITEMS`) to fulfillment and billing. Thus, parallel split deliveries (`X4`) form unordered multiset waves, whereas return-and-repack (`X8`) forms a directed chain (`Wave1 -> Return -> Inspect -> Wave2`).

```python
from collections import Counter, defaultdict

def bucket(n: int) -> str:
    return "0" if n == 0 else ("1" if n == 1 else "MANY")

def canonical_semantic_signature(execution: dict) -> dict:
    """
    execution keys:
      'lead_order': str
      'objects': dict[oid, otype]
      'events': list of {'id': eid, 'act': str, 'ts': int, 'oids': set[str]} (timestamp-sorted)
      'o2o': list of (src_oid, qualifier, dst_oid)
    """
    lead_id = execution["lead_order"]
    objs = execution["objects"]
    events = execution["events"]

    # 1. Scope assignment (invariant to local ID renaming)
    scope = {}
    for oid, otype in objs.items():
        if oid == lead_id:
            scope[oid] = "LEAD"
        elif oid.startswith("EXT-") or (otype == "Order" and oid != lead_id):
            scope[oid] = "EXT"
        else:
            scope[oid] = "LOCAL"

    # 2. Boundary commitment multiplicities & external flags
    local_counts = Counter(
        otype for oid, otype in objs.items() if scope[oid] != "EXT"
    )
    boundary_card = {
        t: bucket(local_counts[t]) for t in ("Delivery", "Invoice", "Payment")
    }
    ext_types = tuple(sorted({objs[oid] for oid, sc in scope.items() if sc == "EXT"}))

    def e2o_sig(ev_list):
        pairs = {(objs[oid], scope[oid]) for ev in ev_list for oid in ev["oids"]}
        return tuple(sorted(pairs))

    # 3. Stage folding & delivery-wave grouping
    # Map each package to its downstream delivery via O2O 'carries' or shared Dispatch E2O
    pkg_to_deliv = {}
    for src, qual, dst in execution["o2o"]:
        if objs.get(src) == "Delivery" and objs.get(dst) == "Package":
            pkg_to_deliv[dst] = src
    for ev in events:
        if ev["act"] == "Dispatch":
            delivs = [o for o in ev["oids"] if objs[o] == "Delivery"]
            pkgs = [o for o in ev["oids"] if objs[o] == "Package"]
            for p in pkgs:
                if delivs:
                    pkg_to_deliv[p] = delivs[0]

    init_evs = [e for e in events if e["act"] == "Create Order"]
    add_evs = [e for e in events if e["act"] == "Add Item"]
    inv_evs = [e for e in events if e["act"] == "Issue Invoice"]
    pay_evs = [e for e in events if e["act"] == "Record Payment"]

    # Build ordered/concurrent fulfillment & rework graph
    # Group Pack + Dispatch per Delivery; keep Return / Inspect as explicit nodes
    wave_events = defaultdict(lambda: {"pack": [], "dispatch": [], "first_ts": float("inf"), "last_ts": -1})
    rework_nodes = []

    for idx, ev in enumerate(events):
        act = ev["act"]
        if act == "Pack Package":
            pkgs = [o for o in ev["oids"] if objs[o] == "Package"]
            d_id = pkg_to_deliv.get(pkgs[0], f"UNSHIPPED_{idx}") if pkgs else f"UNSHIPPED_{idx}"
            wave_events[d_id]["pack"].append(ev)
            wave_events[d_id]["first_ts"] = min(wave_events[d_id]["first_ts"], idx)
        elif act == "Dispatch":
            delivs = [o for o in ev["oids"] if objs[o] == "Delivery"]
            d_id = delivs[0] if delivs else f"DELIV_{idx}"
            wave_events[d_id]["dispatch"].append(ev)
            wave_events[d_id]["last_ts"] = max(wave_events[d_id]["last_ts"], idx)
        elif act in {"Return Item", "Inspect Return"}:
            rework_nodes.append((idx, act, e2o_sig([ev]), ev["oids"]))

    # Canonicalize fulfillment waves; order waves strictly only if separated by rework
    if not rework_nodes:
        # Independent waves commute (partial-order multiset collapsed by multiplicity bucket)
        wave_sigs = Counter(
            ("WAVE", e2o_sig(w["pack"]), e2o_sig(w["dispatch"]))
            for w in wave_events.values()
        )
        fulfill_motif = ("CONCURRENT_WAVES", tuple(sorted((sig, bucket(c)) for sig, c in wave_sigs.items())))
    else:
        # Preserve causal chain across rework events and waves
        timeline = []
        for w in wave_events.values():
            timeline.append((w["last_ts"], ("WAVE", e2o_sig(w["pack"]), e2o_sig(w["dispatch"]))))
        for idx, act, sig, _ in rework_nodes:
            timeline.append((idx, ("REWORK", act, sig)))
        timeline.sort(key=lambda x: x[0])
        fulfill_motif = ("SEQUENTIAL_REWORK_CHAIN", tuple(item[1] for item in timeline))

    # 4. Deduplicated scoped O2O qualifier signature
    o2o_sig = tuple(sorted({
        (f"{objs[src]}:{scope[src]}", qual, f"{objs[dst]}:{scope[dst]}")
        for src, qual, dst in execution["o2o"]
    }))

    return {
        "boundary_card": boundary_card,
        "ext_types": ext_types,
        "init_add_e2o": (e2o_sig(init_evs), e2o_sig(add_evs)),
        "fulfill_motif": fulfill_motif,
        "finance_e2o": (e2o_sig(inv_evs), e2o_sig(pay_evs)),
        "o2o_qualifiers": o2o_sig,
    }
```

## 4. Cluster interpretations

### `SEM-1` — Clean Isolated Single-Shipment Fulfillment (`{X1, X2, X3}`)
* **Concise signature:** `D:1, V:1, M:1 | ISOLATED | CONCURRENT_WAVES(1xStd) | O2O:Base`
* **Business behavior:** The order is fulfilled completely within its own boundary, delivered to the customer in one shipment, billed on one dedicated invoice, and settled in a single payment without item exceptions. Warehouse staff may pack items into one carton (`X1, X2`) or multiple cartons consolidated onto the same truck (`X3`).
* **Operational risks or opportunities:** Represents the "happy path" benchmark for cycle-time and margin analysis. Within `SEM-1`, monitoring the ratio of multi-carton orders (`X3`) offers a secondary warehouse optimization opportunity to reduce packaging waste and handling labor.
* **Poorly representing flattening:** Flattening on `Order` distorts `X3` by displaying repeated `Pack Package` self-loops that resemble packing rework; flattening on `Package` duplicates `Create Order`, `Dispatch`, `Issue Invoice`, and `Record Payment` across `P31` and `P32` (classic convergence/divergence distortion).
* **Stability under data repair:** Highly stable. Even if missing intermediate `Pack Package` timestamps are imputed or local IDs re-indexed, `SEM-1` membership holds unless `Delivery` records are mistakenly split.

### `SEM-2` — Phased Fulfillment and Installment Settlement (`{X4}`)
* **Concise signature:** `D:MANY, V:1, M:MANY | ISOLATED | CONCURRENT_WAVES(MANYxStd) | O2O:{partially_settles, completes_settlement}`
* **Business behavior:** Items ship across multiple independent packages and deliveries (e.g., backorder or multi-warehouse fulfillment), billed on a single invoice settled via partial payments.
* **Operational risks or opportunities:** Elevated shipping cost per order, longer cash-conversion cycles, and customer dissatisfaction from split shipments. Customers frequently withhold full payment until the final delivery arrives—a causal link worth investigating.
* **Poorly representing flattening:** Flattening on `Delivery` misses the financial lifecycle (`V41, M41, M42`) or duplicates it across `D41` and `D42`; flattening on `Order` creates alternating `Pack -> Dispatch` loops and `Record Payment` self-loops without revealing whether one package was reshipped or distinct items shipped separately.
* **Stability under data repair:** Moderately stable, but sensitive to financial reconciliation repairs: if an ETL script aggregates split bank transfers (`M41, M42`) into one net clearing record, `SEM-2` shifts to a pure split-delivery signature.

### `SEM-3` — Cross-Order Logistics Consolidation (`{X5}`)
* **Concise signature:** `D:1, V:1, M:1 | EXT(Order,Item) | 1xWave(Pack+Disp:EXT) | O2O:+packs(Item:EXT)`
* **Business behavior:** Multiple orders (likely for the same ship-to location) are combined in the warehouse into a shared package (`P51`) and delivery (`D51`), while order-level invoicing remains strictly separate (`V51`).
* **Operational risks or opportunities:** Opportunity for substantial freight and carton savings; operational risk of picking/packing mix-ups across orders, plus SLA coupling where a stockout on `EXT-OA` delays `O5` at the packing station.
* **Poorly representing flattening:** Order-flattened logs completely erase `EXT-OA` and `EXT-IA`, making `X5` indistinguishable from `X1` (`SEQ-1`). Conversely, flattening on `Package` merges the events of two separate orders into a chimeric trace with multiple `Create Order` events.
* **Stability under data repair:** Vulnerable to boundary-scoping repairs. Sub-log extraction pipelines that drop foreign-order references during privacy masking or partition pruning cause `X5` to collapse into `SEM-1`.

### `SEM-4` — Collective Cross-Order Invoicing (`{X6}`)
* **Concise signature:** `D:1, V:1, M:1 | EXT(Order) | 1xWave(Std); Fin:EXT | O2O:+bills(Order:EXT)`
* **Business behavior:** Logistics execution is isolated (`P61, D61`), but billing is consolidated across orders onto a collective invoice (`V61`) settled by an unallocated lump-sum payment (`M61`).
* **Operational risks or opportunities:** Reduces invoicing overhead for B2B accounts, but introduces severe accounts-receivable risk: a dispute on `EXT-OB` can stall payment `M61` for `O6`, and unallocated cash complicates order-level profitability and dunning.
* **Poorly representing flattening:** Order-centric flattening hides shared billing entirely (matching `X1`), whereas `Invoice`-centric flattening interleaves independent warehouse dispatches from `O6` and `EXT-OB` as if they belonged to one fulfillment flow.
* **Stability under data repair:** Sensitive to ERP allocation repairs. If an automated ERP routine synthesizes virtual order-level payment splits during data extraction, the unallocated shared structure may be obscured unless `EXT-OB` remains linked to `V61`.

### `SEM-5` — Pre-Dispatch Item Substitution / Short Modification (`{X7}`)
* **Concise signature:** `D:1, V:1, M:1 | ISOLATED | 1xWave(Disp:+Item) | O2O:+{replaces, removed_before_dispatch}`
* **Business behavior:** Both `I71` and `I72` are added and initially packed into `P71`, but before `D71` departs, `I72` replaces `I71` (`removed_before_dispatch`), with `I72` explicitly recorded on `Dispatch`.
* **Operational risks or opportunities:** High compliance and revenue-leakage risk. Because no explicit rework activity occurs, invoice `V71` risks billing for `I71` instead of (or in addition to) `I72`, and inventory counts for `I71` may drift if removed items are not returned to stock.
* **Poorly representing flattening:** Order-centric, Package-centric, and Delivery-centric flattenings all yield the exact seven-event baseline sequence of `X1`, rendering the substitution invisible to traditional control-flow discovery.
* **Stability under data repair:** Fragile if data-cleaning pipelines strip "non-standard" E2O bindings (removing `Item:I72` from `Dispatch` to conform to a rigid schema) AND drop O2O qualifiers, which would misclassify `X7` into `SEM-1`.

### `SEM-6` — Post-Dispatch Return and Redelivery Rework (`{X8}`)
* **Concise signature:** `D:MANY, V:1, M:1 | ISOLATED | Chain(Wave->Return->Inspect->Wave) | O2O:+{repacks, replaces(Pkg)}`
* **Business behavior:** After initial delivery `D81`, item `I82` is returned, inspected, repacked into replacement package `P82`, and reshipped via `D82` before billing and settlement.
* **Operational risks or opportunities:** Direct margin erosion from reverse logistics, inspection labor, double freight, and delayed invoicing (since `V81` waits until `D82` completes). Signals quality or picking defects.
* **Poorly representing flattening:** Flattening on `Item` splits `I81` (clean) from `I82` (returned) while duplicating order/invoice events; flattening on `Package` disconnects `P81` and `P82`, losing the causal fact that `P82` replaces `P81` for the same order's defective line.
* **Stability under data repair:** Very stable. Because rework manifests redundantly across activity labels (`Return Item`, `Inspect Return`), boundary cardinalities (`D:MANY`), and O2O qualifiers, missing O2O metadata alone cannot erase this cluster.

## 5. Borderline cases

* **X1 versus X2:** Raw identifier equivalence (e.g., uncanonicalized `pm4py` keys or lexicographic ID indexing) separates `X1` and `X2` because `X2` adds `I22` before `I21`, reversing lexicographic order relative to timestamp appearance. Conversely, $\equiv_{\text{struct}}$ and $\equiv_{\text{sem}}$ agree they are identical under the local bijection $I11 \mapsto I22, I12 \mapsto I21$ because `Pack Package` links the unordered item set $\{I21, I22\}$.
* **X1 versus X5:** Order-projected control-flow equivalence ($\equiv_{\text{seq}}$) and strictly isolated local projections merge `X1` and `X5` because `O5` experiences the exact baseline seven-activity sequence and single local `Package`/`Delivery`/`Invoice`/`Payment`. By contrast, $\equiv_{\text{struct}}$ and $\equiv_{\text{sem}}$ separate them because `P51` and `D51` cross execution boundaries (`EXT-OA`, `EXT-IA`), creating operational coupling absent in `X1`.
* **X1 versus X6:** A warehouse-scoped object-centric equivalence (restricting E2O/O2O graphs to `{Order, Item, Package, Delivery}`) merges `X1` and `X6` because their physical fulfillment topologies are isomorphic. Enterprise-wide $\equiv_{\text{sem}}$ and $\equiv_{\text{struct}}$ separate them because `V61` and `M61` bind `EXT-OB`, introducing cross-order credit and cash-allocation coupling.
* **X1 versus X7:** Control-flow equivalence ($\equiv_{\text{seq}}$) and object-multiplicity summaries (`1 Order, 2 Items, 1 Package, 1 Delivery, 1 Invoice, 1 Payment`) merge `X1` and `X7`. However, $\equiv_{\text{struct}}$ and $\equiv_{\text{sem}}$ separate them on two independent grounds: `Dispatch` carries an explicit E2O link to `Item:I72`, and qualified O2O edges encode `replaces` and `removed_before_dispatch`.
* **X3 versus X4:** A warehouse-packing equivalence (clustering by `|Package| > 1` or multi-pack handling) groups `X3` and `X4` together as split-pack orders. Our preferred $\equiv_{\text{sem}}$ instead groups `X3` with `X1/X2` and isolates `X4`, arguing that `X3` consolidates cartons prior to `Dispatch` into a single customer delivery (`D31`) and single full payment (`M31`), whereas `X4` fragments both customer delivery (`D41, D42`) and financial settlement (`M41, M42`).
* **X4 versus X8:** A coarse cardinality profile over `(Package:MANY, Delivery:MANY, Invoice:1)` merges `X4` and `X8` if `Payment` count is treated as an attribute and rework labels are abstracted as generic fulfillment loops. Both $\equiv_{\text{struct}}$ and $\equiv_{\text{sem}}$ strictly separate them: `X4` executes parallel/phased first-time shipments for distinct items (`I41` vs. `I42`) with installment payments, whereas `X8` executes a sequential reverse-logistics loop (`Return Item -> Inspect Return`) reshipping the *same* item `I82`.

## 6. Relation to `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel`

In PM4Py, `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel, object_type)` first extracts one sub-OCEL execution per object of `object_type` (via connected-component / interaction graph traversal) and groups sub-OCELs using a structural hash key. Specifically:

1. **Information preserved by the PM4Py key:** Depending on the PM4Py release, the internal key encodes exact event activity sequences (and relative ordering), object type counts, E2O relation tuples, and O2O relation tuples within each extracted sub-OCEL.
2. **Why raw object identifiers make exact clusters too fine:** If raw object strings appear directly in relation tuples—or if local objects are canonically indexed by sorting raw string IDs rather than computing graph-isomorphism orbits—renaming breaks equivalence. For instance, sorting `{I21, I22}` in `X2` assigns index `0` to `I21` (added second), whereas sorting `{I11, I12}` in `X1` assigns index `0` to `I11` (added first), splitting `X1` and `X2` into separate exact clusters. Exact keys also split `2`-item from `3`-item baseline orders and `X3` from `X1`.
3. **Canonicalizing and post-processing returned clusters:** Because exact PM4Py clusters form a strict refinement of $\equiv_{\text{sem}}$ (once focal lead orders are tracked), we post-process returned sub-OCELs by identifying the focal `Order` (e.g., the `Order` participating in `Create Order` within the component, tagging co-occurring orders as `EXT`), computing `canonical_semantic_signature` per sub-OCEL, and re-aggregating into coarser semantic buckets.
4. **When exact equivalence is preferable:** Exact structural clustering (post local-ID canonicalization) is preferable for regression testing process-mining pipelines, auditing strict SOP bots, or detecting synthetic log duplication, where even one extra `Add Item` or carton split matters.
5. **Impact of `max_objs` and large connected executions:** Shared objects (`P51` in `X5`, `V61` in `X6`, or a master batch/truck in production logs) bridge multiple orders into giant connected components. Without scoping, traversal pulls `EXT-OA`'s entire lifecycle into `O5`'s sub-OCEL; conversely, bounding component extraction via `max_objs` prevents memory blowup but introduces boundary-truncation artifacts—two identical orders linked to differently sized collective invoices may be truncated at different depths, fracturing exact equivalence unless external orders are pruned to stub `EXT` boundary nodes first.

```python
from collections import defaultdict
import inspect
import pm4py

def cluster_orders_semantically(ocel, max_objs: int = 100):
    # Handle optional max_objs support across PM4Py versions
    sig_params = inspect.signature(pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel).parameters
    kwargs = {"max_objs": max_objs} if "max_objs" in sig_params else {}
    exact_clusters = pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel, "Order", **kwargs)

    semantic_clusters = defaultdict(list)

    for exact_key, sub_ocels in exact_clusters.items():
        for sub_ocel in sub_ocels:
            ev_df = sub_ocel.events.sort_values(sub_ocel.event_timestamp)
            obj_df = sub_ocel.objects
            rel_df = sub_ocel.relations.sort_values(sub_ocel.event_timestamp)
            o2o_df = sub_ocel.o2o

            objects = dict(zip(obj_df[sub_ocel.object_id_column], obj_df[sub_ocel.object_type_column]))
            order_ids = [oid for oid, otype in objects.items() if otype == "Order"]

            # Identify focal lead Order (created locally and not prefixed EXT-)
            create_rels = rel_df[rel_df[sub_ocel.event_activity] == "Create Order"]
            created_orders = [
                oid for oid in create_rels[sub_ocel.object_id_column].unique()
                if objects.get(oid) == "Order" and not str(oid).startswith("EXT-")
            ]
            lead_order = created_orders[0] if created_orders else order_ids[0]

            # Build normalized execution dict for custom canonicalization
            oids_by_eid = rel_df.groupby(sub_ocel.event_id_column)[sub_ocel.object_id_column].apply(set).to_dict()
            events = [
                {
                    "id": row[sub_ocel.event_id_column],
                    "act": row[sub_ocel.event_activity],
                    "ts": idx,
                    "oids": oids_by_eid.get(row[sub_ocel.event_id_column], set()),
                }
                for idx, (_, row) in enumerate(ev_df.iterrows())
            ]
            o2o = list(zip(
                o2o_df[sub_ocel.object_id_column],
                o2o_df[sub_ocel.qualifier],
                o2o_df[sub_ocel.object_id_column + "_2"],
            )) if not o2o_df.empty else []

            exec_dict = {
                "lead_order": lead_order,
                "objects": objects,
                "events": events,
                "o2o": o2o,
            }
            sem_sig = canonical_semantic_signature(exec_dict)
            hashable_sig = repr(sem_sig)
            semantic_clusters[hashable_sig].append((lead_order, sub_ocel))

    return semantic_clusters
```

## 7. Robustness analysis

Evaluating how `SEQ`, `STR`, and `SEM` respond to five structural perturbations exposes which invariants anchor each cluster:

1. **Ignore all O2O qualifiers (retain unqualified O2O edges and E2O):** Surprisingly, **no `SEM` or `STR` clusters merge on `X1–X8`**, though signatures weaken. `X4` retains `Delivery:MANY` and `Payment:MANY`; `X8` retains `Return Item` and `Inspect Return` events; and `X7` *barely* stays separate (`SEM-5`) solely because `Dispatch` retains its anomalous E2O binding to `Item:I72` (plus an unqualified `Item -> Item` edge). If `Item:I72` had not been logged on `Dispatch`, dropping qualifiers would silently collapse `X7` into `SEM-1`!
2. **Ignore external objects (strip all `EXT-*` nodes and incident E2O/O2O edges):** **`X5` (`SEM-3`) and `X6` (`SEM-4`) both collapse into `SEM-1` (`{X1, X2, X3, X5, X6}`)**, reducing `SEM` from 6 to 4 clusters (and `STR` from 7 to 5 clusters, merging `X5, X6` into `STR-1`). All cross-order consolidation visibility is lost.
3. **Collapse repeated same-label events (consecutive run-length collapse):** In `SEQ`, `X3` (`Pack, Pack -> Pack`) merges into `SEQ-1` (`{X1, X2, X3, X5, X6, X7}`), reducing `SEQ` from 4 to 3 clusters (`X4` remains separate because `Pack -> Dispatch -> Pack -> Dispatch` alternates). If paired with quotienting symmetric co-packed local objects, `STR-2` (`X3`) merges into `STR-1`, converging toward `SEM-1` (which is already invariant to intra-delivery `Pack` repetition).
4. **Treat `Pack` and `Dispatch` event order as a partial order rather than a total order:** On `X1–X8`, cluster memberships remain unchanged (`X3` has one `Dispatch` synchronizing two packages, whereas `X4` has two `Dispatch` events). However, in `STR`, relaxing total order merges `X4`'s interleaved `Pack(P41) -> Dispatch(D41) -> Pack(P42) -> Dispatch(D42)` with batched split-delivery variants `Pack(P41) -> Pack(P42) -> Dispatch(D41) -> Dispatch(D42)`—an invariance `SEM-2` already guarantees via `CONCURRENT_WAVES`.
5. **Treat payment multiplicity as an amount attribute rather than structure:** Collapsing `M41, M42` and their partial-settlement qualifiers into a settled invoice attribute strips the financial dimension from `X4`, converting `SEM-2` into a pure phased-shipping cluster. `X4` still remains distinct from `SEM-1` (`Delivery:MANY` vs. `1`) and `SEM-6` (`X8`, no rework chain); only if delivery multiplicity were *simultaneously* ignored would `X4` collapse into `SEM-1`.

## 8. Limitations and recommendation

No single equivalence relation can simultaneously optimize fidelity, interpretability, and statistical sample size across all analytical tasks:

* **Compliance monitoring $\rightarrow$ Typed structural equivalence ($\equiv_{\text{struct}}$, relaxed to causal partial orders on ties):** Audits (e.g., SOX billing controls, silent item substitutions like `X7`, or unauthorized cross-order billing like `X6`) require near-zero false negatives. Over-fragmenting benign carton counts (`X3`) is an acceptable price to guarantee that structural anomalies and unauthorized E2O bindings are never smoothed away.
* **Operational segmentation $\rightarrow$ Boundary-aware business-semantic equivalence ($\equiv_{\text{sem}}$, Preferred):** Process owners optimizing SLA throughput, freight consolidation (`X5`), installment collection (`X4`), and reverse logistics (`X8`) need clusters aligned with actionable operational regimes—abstracting noise like item counts and internal cartonization (`X1–X3`) while keeping logistics sharing (`X5`) distinct from financial sharing (`X6`).
* **Root-cause analysis $\rightarrow$ Typed structural equivalence ($\equiv_{\text{struct}}$) enriched with timing/attribute predicates:** Diagnosing *why* `I82` was returned in `X8` or *why* `X4` shipped in two waves requires fine-grained sub-variants—distinguishing `2` vs. `5` cartons (`X3`) or interleaved vs. batched packing (`X4`) often pinpoints warehouse shift bottlenecks or inventory allocation bugs that `SEM` intentionally abstracts.
* **Descriptive reporting $\rightarrow$ Coarse governance-outcome equivalence ($\equiv_{\text{gov}}$):** Executive dashboards malfunction when displaying hundreds of topological variants. Grouping executions into four macro-buckets—*Clean Single-Pass* (`{X1,X2,X3}`), *Phased Multi-Commitment* (`{X4}`), *Cross-Order Consolidated* (`{X5,X6}`), and *Exception/Rework* (`{X7,X8}`)—delivers stable, legible KPI trends.

Ultimately, equivalence relations form a quotient lattice: finer partitions ($\equiv_{\text{struct}}$) preserve diagnostic precision at the cost of combinatorial variant explosion, whereas coarser partitions ($\equiv_{\text{seq}}$, $\equiv_{\text{gov}}$) stabilize KPI aggregation at the cost of masking cross-object entanglement (`X5, X6`) and silent structural exceptions (`X7`). Maintaining a canonical fine-to-coarse pipeline—computing exact components first and deterministically folding into $\sigma_{\text{sem}}$ and $\equiv_{\text{gov}}$—resolves this tension without sacrificing reproducibility.
