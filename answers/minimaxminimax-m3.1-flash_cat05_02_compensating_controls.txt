Why "same expected loss" is not the only standard: FEP-1 is a structural/architectural control (segregation of duty) providing diversification of error, error-detection independence, and non-negotiable structural constraint; its value includes (a) certainty of operation (not probabilistic), (b) attribution/blame and deterrence, (c) reduced single-point-of-compromise, (d) coverage of threats no compensating control can fully see (e.g., collusion-resistant authenticity of receipt). Compensating controls are probabilistic, may be overridden, may share the same failure modes (control-plane compromise), and their residual risk is concentrated in a small number of high-severity scenarios (irreversible, high-amount). def case_times(df):
```python
    g = df.groupby("case_id")
    out = {}
    for case, sub in g:
        d = {}
        for act in sub["activity"].unique():
            ts = sub.loc[sub["activity"] == act, "timestamp"]
            d[act] = (ts.min(), ts.max())
        out[case] = d
    return out
```

## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP1 is a *structural* control: it removes the possibility that one human actor both initiates and authorises a transfer. That single mechanism underwrites several distinct threat scenarios, and the compensating set (AV2, MG4, RA7, ED1) covers them very unevenly.

| # | Objective / threat scenario | How FEP1 addresses it | AV2 | MG4 | RA7 | ED1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| O1 | **Single-actor fraudulent or erroneous instruction** (one person creates *and* authorises) | Directly: forces a second, distinct human at the authorisation point | **Fails** — automated checks are not actor separation; a single actor can pass them and still be the authoriser | **Partially prevents** if performed *before* release/settlement by an independent manager; **detective** if post-settlement | **Detective only**; becomes corrective only if it completes inside the recall window | **Permits** a single operator only under seven conditions; the permission substitutes a ratifier for a releaser and keeps O1 only conditionally covered |
| O2 | **Compromise or sharing of one identity / credential** (stolen session, shared account, delegated override token) | Partially: a second human with their own credential raises the number of compromises needed | **Fails** (documented: cannot establish who controlled a compromised account) | **Mitigates** only if the manager is independent and authenticates independently of the preparer's channel; **fails** if the manager's credential is the one that was compromised (AP202 pattern) | **Detective**; may reveal credential misuse days later, after recall expiry | **Fails** as an O2 control: a single operator keeps one compromised identity sufficient to complete the payment |

| O8 | **Collusion / coordinated deviation between the two actors** | **Fails** — FEP1 assumes the two actors are not co-conspirators | Partial: duplicate and limit checks constrain but do not detect collusion | **Only** if the manager is outside the pair's relationship network (m09 setting u23's rating = unresolved conflict) | **Primary detector**, at a delay | Increases collusion surface by re-introducing a single actor; requires the ED1 ratifier to be genuinely independent |
| O9 | **Irreversibility / conversion of a defect into permanent loss** (settlement, 24-hour recall window) | Only indirectly — a second actor who declines before release prevents settlement | Reduces exposure but does not act on settlement timing | **The decisive control** if it lands before settlement; post-settlement it is detective only | Detective; becomes corrective only if the audit completes while the transfer is still recallable | Condition 6 (pre-settlement approval) and condition 7 (audit within one business day) are the only recoverability levers |

| O11 | **Control-environment availability and integrity** (validation outage, unsigned manual fallback, emergency token created after the fact, clock/timestamp integrity) | n/a — FEP1 is silent on the health of the surrounding control plane | **Fails when unavailable** (AP208) and no substitute existed | n/a | Detects the outage's consequences only in the payment, not the outage | Condition 1 (token *before* Prepare) and condition 5 (fully operational) are integrity conditions; if routinely satisfied post hoc, ED1 stops being a control (AP206) |
| O12 | **Detection latency and assurance quality of the audit** | n/a | n/a | n/a | **Is** the detection-latency control (RA7) | Condition 7 sets the ED1 latency ceiling (1 business day) |

**Reading of the decomposition.** AV2 is a *content* control (is this payment correct, duplicate, sanctioned, within limit?) and is blind to O1, O2, O7 and O8. MG4 is the only control that can act on O1/O2/O3/O7/O8/O9 *at the authorisation point*, and therefore carries almost the entire compensating weight; its value collapses to zero if it is late, if the manager is conflicted, or if it is a counter-signature. RA7 is the only *recovery* control, and only inside a ~24-hour window measured from settlement. ED1 is not a control but a *permission with seven integrity conditions* — it re-opens O1 in exchange for constraining O3, O6, O9 and O12.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

### 2.1 The eight gates

A compensating package is scored gate-by-gate for the specific threat mechanism of the case, not for the case in general. Each gate is *pass / partial / fail / unknown*.

| Gate | Question | Pass | Partial | Fail / Unknown |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **G1 Timing vs. irreversibility** | Does the control act before the point of no return (settlement), and ideally before release? | Manager action before release | Manager action after release but before settlement (hybrid) | Manager/audit action after settlement, or audit after recall expiry |
| **G2 Independence** | Is the compensating human independent of the preparer in fact — separate identity, separate credential, no reporting relationship, no shared workstation/session, no shared override credential? | Independent resource, independent channel, L3+ | Independent resource but shared workstation/channel, or independence unverified | Conflicted (m09 sets u23's rating), credential delegated (AP202), or independence unverified without compensating evidence |
| **G3 Coverage** | Does the package cover the *dominant* threat mechanism of this payment (beneficiary change, duplicate, receipt fabrication, amount breach)? | Dominant mechanism covered preventively | Dominant mechanism covered only detectively, or covered only by a check that does not exist for this threat (receipt) | Dominant mechanism not covered by any functioning control (AP208 beneficiary change with validation down) |
| **G4 Overrideability / exception integrity** | Are overrides blocked, bound to a named independent approver, and unable to be exercised by the preparer or by the beneficiary of the approval? | No override used, or override by an independent authorised approver | Override used by a neutral party with documentation | Self-override, delegated-credential override, or override followed by same-user release (AP202) |
| **G5 Recoverability** | If the control fires late, is the transfer still recallable, and did anyone try? | Defect surfaced <24h before recall expiry, with recall capability confirmed | Recall window open but unexercised or untested | Recall window closed and no recovery (AP202, AP208) |
| **G6 Evidence quality** | Is there evidence of *substantive* review (invoice, bank details, receipt) rather than presence/click? | Documented substantive review | Approval recorded, scope of review undocumented | Rubber stamp, unsigned fallback checklist, or review of an incomplete record |
| **G7 Collusion resistance** | Can the package detect behaviour by a *pair* rather than by a single actor? | Independent actor outside the pair, with review competence and records | Audit sampling of the pair | Both controls inside the same dyad; only detective coverage (AP204/209) |
| **G8 Systemic integrity** | Is the exception channel controlled, or is it normalising a permanent waiver? | Waivers rare, tokenised, reviewed, expiring | Waivers documented but untrended | Convenience-based waivers or post-hoc token creation becoming routine (AP201, AP206) |

### 2.2 Grades

- **Fully equivalent** to FEP1 for the case's threat profile: G1–G8 pass, and the package contains at least one control that acts **pre-settlement on the dominant threat** with a demonstrably independent human. (AP203.)
- **Partially equivalent**: G1–G3 effectively pass for the dominant threat but 1 gate is partial/fail on a *secondary* threat, and residual exposure is bounded and low relative to amount. (AP201, AP206, AP207.)
- **Merely mitigating**: the only operative control is detective and/or fires outside the recoverability window, or independence/coverage is unverified rather than established; residual exposure is material but bounded by other controls. (AP202 in part, AP209.)
- **Ineffective**: the control was absent, or was defeated by the same actor, or the detection completed after irreversibility with no recovery and the dominant threat was uncovered. (AP202, AP208.)
- **Distinct from all of the above: prevented before completion** — the preventive layer stopped the payment, so the objective was never exposed to test. (AP205.)

### 2.3 Why "same expected loss" is not the only available standard

1. **Structural vs. probabilistic.** FEP1 is deterministic: the prohibited state is *unreachable by construction*, regardless of counterparty behaviour, system load, or an individual's state of mind. Every compensating control is probabilistic and state-dependent (service availability, manager availability, review quality, settlement cut-offs). Equal *expected* loss assumes distributions that are not verifiable from the data we have (which payments had a defect, what a given manager would notice), so the equivalence claim is unfalsifiable and therefore not auditable.
2. **Tail concentration and correlation.** Expected loss is linear in probability; FEP1's protection is not. The population's realised loss is dominated by a few irreversible events (AP202 EUR 480k unrecovered; AP208 EUR 900k with no pre-settlement check at all). A package that leaves one EUR 900k case unprotected has not made the *distribution* equivalent, only the mean.
3. **Shared failure modes.** Compensating controls frequently inherit the failure mode of the actor they compensate for: AP202 shows a delegated override credential, so the actor's own compromise (or the actor's own intent) reached through the "second line" of control. A structural control's value is precisely that the second actor is *not* downstream of the first actor's credentials or channel.
4. **Control-objective vs. loss-outcome framing.** Auditing and supervisory standards evaluate whether the control operated *at the point where the objective attaches* (authorisation). A detective control cannot discharge an authorisation-stage objective, however small the residual amount; substituting "no loss materialised" for "the control operated" is the classic error the brief warns against.
5. **Non-monetary objectives.** FEP1 also delivers attribution, deterrence, evidential trail for dispute, and *equal treatment of cases*. Waivers granted on convenience (AP201) or post hoc (AP206) erode the credibility of the whole control set and shift the residual risk from "one payment" to "the exception channel", which is where AP202-type losses breed.
6. **Expected-loss estimates are assumption-driven.** Saying "EUR 1,800 at risk versus a two-person overhead" makes the residual-loss comparison degenerate for small payments while leaving the same two-person rule for EUR 900k, i.e. it makes the control strength inversely proportional to exposure — the opposite of what risk-based control design requires. Severity-conditional tolerance (rather than pure expected loss) is the right frame: tolerate procedural deviation where the amount and threat profile bound the exposure, and do not tolerate it where severity is high or the dominant threat is uncovered.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

**Chosen semantics.** A control action is classified by *its effect on the loss chain at the point of irreversibility*, not by the calendar moment the human acts and not by the control's name. Three consequences follow:

- A control is **preventive** only if it can *stop* the transfer before the money becomes irreversible (or, for MG4 pre-release, before the erroneous instruction is created at all).
- A control is **detective** if it identifies a defect in an authorisation that has already been granted, without being able to unwind it.
- A control is **corrective** if it can still stop, recall or recover after the defect is known — i.e. it is only corrective while the recall window is open.
- Therefore a **manager action after Release_Payment but before settlement is a hybrid**: *detective at the authorisation stage* (it did not authorise or block the release instruction) and *preventive at the settlement stage* (it prevented the irreversible outflow), while *preserving the corrective option* (recall). If settlement has already occurred, the same action collapses to **detective only**; if it occurs before release, it is **purely preventive**. This is why AP207 (ratify 14:40, settle 16:00) is materially better than AP202/AP208 (ratify post-settlement), even though both are "manager actions after release".
- By the same logic, **RA7 is detective by design** and becomes *partly corrective only when the audit completes while the transfer is still recallable*. No audit in this population triggered a recall, so RA7 operated purely detectively in every case — and in AP202/AP208 it operated after the window had closed, making it near-worthless for those payments.

A package with a substantive, independent, pre-release MG4 (AP203, AP206) or a pre-settlement substantive MG4 (AP207) is materially stronger than any amount of post-hoc auditing.

## 5. Counterfactual and Residual-Risk Analysis

Qualitative likelihood bands are used (negligible / low / moderate / high / very high) to avoid false precision; where a magnitude is implied it is expressed as a fraction of the payment amount and labelled as an exposure range rather than a probability.

**AP202 — beneficiary redirection to a related party (EUR 480,000).**
Most plausible loss mechanism: the vendor bank account was changed two hours before the payment; the preparer/releaser suppressed both AV2 warnings and released using an override credential nominally belonging to m09; the money left to a related party's account and was never recovered.
Control that breaks the chain: only (i) a *hard* override gate — a bank-change warning that cannot be released by the preparer or by the holder of a delegated credential — or (ii) an independent, out-of-band ownership confirmation before release. Neither existed.
What remains exposed: the entire amount, irreversibly; plus the credential-delegation model, which will reproduce this pattern for any u23-type user.
Under a truly independent second releaser: the *override* would still have to be granted, and if it were granted by someone with genuine authority and no conflict, the payment would likely not have proceeded — so the marginal effect is to move the loss from "very high" to "low-to-moderate". But note the honest limit: an independent releaser is a weak substitute for an override gate. If the second actor is as dependent as m09, nothing changes. The residual after adding a second pair of eyes is still driven by the control-plane defect, not the execution-plane defect.

**AP204 — fabricated service receipt behind a EUR 220,000 consulting payment.**
Most plausible loss mechanism: one of the two actors fabricated or accepted a false service receipt; the second actor either did not verify the receipt or was not in a position to challenge it; the audit found it ~4 days later.
Control that breaks the chain: RA7, eventually — but only as a detective control, and only after settlement. Nothing prevented it.
What remains exposed: EUR 220,000 (recovery not evidenced) and, more importantly, the other 37 payments approved by the same pair in 30 days, none of which has been shown to be clean.
Under a truly independent second releaser — and here the case is unambiguous — the payment is caught **before settlement**, converting a realised EUR 220k loss into a cancelled payment, *provided* the second actor is competent to validate a consulting service receipt and records the review. The structural caveat is decisive: if the dyad is a designed two-person cell, then "adding an independent releaser" means removing the second signature from the cell and giving the case to someone outside it. A re-paired pair from the same team would not help.

**AP208 — unverified bank detail on a EUR 900,000 payment (no realised loss).**
Most plausible loss mechanism: a bank account changed three days earlier was used for a large first-to-this-vendor payment, with no ownership verification because AV2 was down and the fallback checklist was unsigned; the payment settled one hour after release.
Control that breaks the chain: none available at the authorisation point; the ratification (next day, after settlement) and audit (day five) are both post-hoc.
What remains exposed: the full EUR 900,000, unrecoverable, if the change had been fraudulent. This is the population's largest latent exposure and it is *silent* — the control produced no signal at all.
Under a truly independent second releaser: **partial at best.** If the second actor performs an out-of-band call-back to the vendor's known contact to confirm the account, the mechanism is broken. If the second actor simply reviews the same documents in the same system, the mechanism survives intact — because the missing capability is bank-ownership verification, not a second pair of eyes. The correct remedy is availability + independent ownership verification, in that order.

**AP206 — emergency claim created after the fact (EUR 95,000).**
Most plausible loss mechanism: none materialised on this payment — the amount was small, the vendor established, AV2 clean, and an independent manager authorised before release. The mechanism to worry about is at the *portfolio* level: if tokens are created after preparation starts, ED1 condition 1 becomes decorative, and the next AP202-type loss can be re-labelled "emergency".
Control that breaks the chain: the token issuance log itself (server-side timestamp + clock-sync evidence) and, downstream, condition 6's independent pre-settlement approval.
What remains exposed: the *integrity of the exception channel* — the mechanism by which the group voluntarily disables its own second-pair-of-eyes rule.
Under a truly independent second releaser: the marginal effect on this payment is close to nil (m11 already provided an independent pre-release authorisation). The marginal effect on the *portfolio* is significant: eliminating the single-operator path entirely removes the channel through which AP202-type losses become "permitted". This is a case where the second pair of eyes is less valuable than the removal of the waiver.

**AP207 — erroneous release instruction caught only by ratification timing (EUR 35,000).**
Most plausible loss mechanism: a wrong amount or wrong vendor detail passes the automated checks (they cover duplicates, sanctions, bank changes, limits, PO — not commercial judgement) and becomes an irreversible instruction.
Control that breaks the chain: m15's ratification at 14:40, which stopped the *settlement* of a payment already released, and preserved recall.
What remains exposed: a 35-minute interval in which the erroneous instruction existed and was live; if the 16:00 settlement had been any earlier, or if the ratifier had been unavailable, the error would have been irreversible with only post-hoc audit left.
Under a truly independent second releaser: the error would have been blocked at 14:05 with no dependence on settlement cut-offs and no dependence on a single ratifier's availability. Here the compensating package's effectiveness is a function of two external parameters (cut-off rules and manager availability) that the group does not control — a fragile basis for a high-volume control design.

**AP203 — genuine emergency under a valid ED1 permission (EUR 75,000).**
Most plausible loss mechanism: a critical machine repair paid on an emergency basis, where the real risk is *circumventing* the two-person rule under deadline pressure and then paying a wrong party.
Control that breaks the chain: m02's pre-release review (invoice, bank account, service report) — the substantive check occurred at 10:04, before release and two hours before settlement.
What remains exposed: AV2's blindness to receipt authenticity, and the risk that ED1's conditions are treated as a formality across the emergency population.
Under a truly independent second releaser: marginal. The objective was already met; the useful change would be to extend ED1's audit discipline (condition 7) to the whole emergency population and to sample receipts of emergency payments for goods-receipt evidence — because ED1's rate of *failed* conditions (AP206, AP208) is itself the metric worth watching.

**AP201 — convenience-driven same-user handling (EUR 1,800).**
Most plausible loss mechanism: a genuinely erroneous or slightly dishonest low-value payment (wrong invoice, minor kickback) that the automated checks cannot see.
Control that breaks the chain: nothing preventive — this is the archetypal case where only post-hoc audit stands between the group and the loss, and the audit was 25 hours after release.
What remains exposed: EUR 1,800 of payment-level exposure (negligible in isolation) plus the *waiver-normalisation* exposure, which is cumulative: if convenience waivers become the default path for sub-threshold payments, the population of payments with no independent human at authorisation grows, and the aggregate exposure is the sum of many small unverified outflows — which is a different risk profile from one large fraud, and one that is very poorly detected by a 100%-audit regime scoped only to emergency same-user payments.
Under a truly independent second releaser: the payment-level exposure goes to approximately zero, at a cost that is disproportionate for EUR 1,800. This is precisely where a *tolerance with controls* (small-amount, low-risk-vendor, clean-AV-2, pre-settlement manager ratification, complete audit trail) is defensible — provided the waiver is explicitly scoped, quantified, trended and periodically re-tested, and provided the same tolerance is never extended to bank changes, new vendors or overrides.

**AP205 — duplicate invoice blocked before release (EUR 12,000).**
Most plausible loss mechanism: a double payment to an established vendor (either an upstream re-submission error or a deliberate attempt at duplicate disbursement).
Control that breaks the chain: AV2's duplicate-invoice check, at 12:02, before any release.
What remains exposed: nothing at payment level; the residual is that the *root cause* was never examined and a duplicate can be re-presented dressed as a legitimate second invoice.
Under a truly independent second releaser: essentially no marginal effect — the automated check is doing the work, and a second human adds little against duplicates. The valuable follow-up is upstream: identify why a duplicate was raised and whether the re-presentation was attempted again.

**AP209 — clean payment by the AP204 pair (EUR 42,000).**
Most plausible loss mechanism: none in this payment; the concern is the pair's correlated behaviour, where one actor's error or intent passes because the second actor is habituated rather than independent.
Control that breaks the chain: an independent reviewer *outside* the dyad reviewing a sample of the pair's payments, plus receipt verification on a sample basis.
What remains exposed: an unquantified but non-trivial share of the 38 payments handled by the pair (37 in 30 days plus this one) if the dyad is not genuinely two-person.
Under a truly independent second releaser outside the pair: this is the change with the highest expected benefit in the whole population, because it addresses the *correlation* rather than the individual payment. The residual after the change is ordinary residual risk, but it is bounded and measurable, unlike the present state where the group's assurance rests on an unverified organisational-design assumption.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

### 6.1 Evidence to collect

**Per-payment, retained for the full audit and recall window**
1. Emergency-token issuance records: server-side creation timestamp, requesting human, authorising human, linked incident ticket, and clock-sync attestation for every host in the chain (directly resolves AP206's 13:02 ambiguity).
2. Override records: warning code, the *human* who exercised the override, the credential used, the credential's owner, and the identity attestation attached to it (resolves AP202's m09 credential).
3. MG4 workpapers: the artefacts reviewed (invoice, bank detail confirmation, receipt), reviewer identity and level, timestamp of review and of any settlement hold, and free-text evidence of substantive review (distinguishes review from counter-signature).
4. Bank-detail change tickets: requester, approver, verification method (out-of-band call-back to a pre-existing contact), and the elapsed time between change and payment.
5. Vendor master-data and onboarding files, including related-party screening for new and recently-changed payees.
6. Settlement timestamps, cut-off rules, and the operational definition of "irrevocable" used by the bank for recall purposes; recall requests and bank responses.
7. Audit workpapers with evidence of the receipt and bank-detail checks actually performed, not merely the reviewer's name.

**Environmental**
8. Authentication/session logs: workstation identifier, IP, MFA assertion, session identity per release — the decisive evidence for AP204/AP209's shared workstation.
9. Organisation design documentation for any dedicated two-person payment cell (explicitly missing) and the pairing rota (fixed vs. rota-driven).
10. Incident and change records for the validation service: outage windows, root cause, and, for each release executed during an outage, the substitute control applied and whether it was signed.
11. Population data: all payments by the dyads and by the same-workstation users over 12 months, so the 37-payment figure can be tested for abnormality (e.g. against the peer distribution of high-value consulting payments) rather than treated as an allegation.

### 6.2 Monitoring rules

| Metric | Definition | Suggested trigger | Alert or proof? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Same-user rate** | Completed payments where Prepare resource = Release resource ÷ completed payments; reported monthly, segmented by amount band, entity, vendor tenure, and ED1 eligibility. Compute **separately** for attempted-but-blocked releases, which are evidence of control operation and must not enter the denominator as violations. | Any occurrence > EUR 100k; monthly rate > 2% in any segment; any month-on-month rise; any same-user case lacking a matched, valid ED1 permission record | **Alert.** A same-user payment is a process exception, not evidence of misconduct. It is "proof" only of a breach of FEP1, and even that requires the ED1 qualification to be checked first |
| **ED1 integrity rate** | Share of same-user payments with (a) token created before Prepare, (b) amount < 100k, (c) vendor  180 days, (d) no bank change in 7 days, (e) AV2 fully operational with no override, (f) independent L3+ approval before settlement, (g) audit within one business day. | Any single condition failing; any token created after Prepare; any two or more failures on one payment (a systematic failure pattern) | **Alert**, and here it is closer to proof: a token timestamp that post-dates preparation is a hard documentary fact, not an inference |
| **Override rate** | Override events ÷ validation events, split by warning type (bank change, operator limit, sanctions, PO mismatch). Sub-metrics: override by the preparer; override by a credential's non-owner; override by a manager in the preparer's reporting line; override without a documented rationale. | **Zero tolerance** for preparer-self-override and for delegated-credential use; any bank-change override; any override without an independent, non-conflicted approver; override rate above baseline by segment | **Alert** for the rate; **near-proof** for preparer-self-override or use of another person's credential, because both are documentary facts about authority, independent of intent |
| **Late manager approval** | Manager action relative to release and settlement: pre-release / post-release-pre-settlement / post-settlement, plus minutes elapsed from release. | Any same-user payment with no manager action **before settlement**; any post-settlement ratification; any ratification by a conflicted manager | **Alert.** Late approval is a control-timing failure, not proof of misconduct. Post-settlement ratification on a same-user payment should be treated as an incident, because it means the payment was irreversible before authorisation existed |
| **Audit-before-recall-window** | Audit completion timestamp versus release and versus settlement, expressed as hours and as "audit completed before recall expiry" (corrective-capable) vs. "audit completed" (detective only). | Target 100% for ED1 payments; any audit > 24h after release; any gap where the audit completed after recall expiry | **Alert**, and the *corrective-capable rate* is the more informative metric: it measures whether RA7 could ever have saved money, not merely whether it happened |
| **Repeated resource pairs** | Unordered (preparer, releaser) pairs with  5 payments in a rolling 30 days; separately, pairs that also share a workstation or session. Compare each pair's high-value/receipt-heavy mix against the peer baseline. | Any pair above the threshold; any pair with  1 confirmed defect (then re-audit 100% of the pair's payments); any pair whose defect rate exceeds the peer median | **Alert.** Repeated pairing is a *structural* signal about the control design (a two-person cell) as well as a possible collusion signal. It is **not** proof; AP204 shows a genuine defect and AP209 shows a clean payment in the same pair |
| **Shared workstations / session reuse** | Distinct users per workstation in payment roles; releases where the workstation's session identity differs from the releasing identity; authentication events per payment. | Any workstation shared by  2 payment actors; any session-identity mismatch; any single-authenticator workstation in the payment path | **Alert.** Session-identity mismatch is close to proof of the *mechanism* (one human acting as two), though not of intent |
| **Bank-change proximity** | Hours between bank-detail change and payment; share of payments to vendors whose account changed within 7 days / 1 day / 24h; first payment to a vendor; account change requested by a person with a relationship to the payee. | Any payment within 24h of a bank change (escalate to mandatory out-of-band confirmation before release); any first payment to a vendor together with a recent account change; any account change where verification was not out-of-band | **Alert** on proximity. If the change requestor is a related party of the payee, the pattern becomes a strong investigative indicator — but proof still requires corroboration (device, banking, HR records) |
| **Control outages and fallbacks** | Validation availability %; count of releases executed during an outage; count of unsigned manual fallbacks; releases where the outage was not matched by an incident ticket; tokens issued during an outage; clock drift between components. | **Zero** releases during an outage without a documented, signed substitute; zero unsigned fallbacks; any outage exceeding a defined threshold triggers a compensating-control attestation; clock drift > 1 minute flags every ordering assertion | **Alert.** An unsigned fallback (AP208) is proof that a control did not operate; it says nothing about intent |
| **Audit quality sampling** | Per audit: was the receipt verified, was the bank detail verified, was the review recorded before or after settlement, was a recall considered. | Sample-based quarterly on ED1 payments and on any pair under review; report the substantive-review rate, not just the audit count | **Alert / quality metric.** A low substantive-review rate would explain why "audit performed" is not equivalent to "control effective" |

### 6.3 Alerting discipline

- **An alert is not proof of misconduct.** Every metric above detects a *state of the control environment* — an exception, a timing failure, a correlation. None of them establishes intent, collusion, or fraud, and none of them should be recorded, reported to a regulator, or relied on in an employment process as if it did.
- The one item that approaches documentary proof of a *control* breach is the AP202 override pattern: a human who is not the credential's owner exercised a control block, and the preparer who was being blocked was the same person who released. That is proof of the authority failure; it is **not** proof that m09 or a relative directed it.
- Escalation ladder: metric breach  documented explanation with evidence  control-design change  only then, where a pattern persists after design change, referral for forensic investigation.
- Confirmed misconduct requires corroboration across independent sources: credential forensics, device/session forensics, out-of-band vendor confirmation, HR and related-party screening, and — for the dyad cases — an examination of whether the "second" actor ever had the information, competence and independence to disagree.

### 6.4 Process recommendations (control design, not just monitoring)

1. **Make bank-change and sanctions warnings non-overridable by the preparer**, with a named independent approver whose credential is not delegable, and a mandatory out-of-band vendor confirmation for any payment within 7 days of an account change (closes AP202 and AP208 directly).
2. **Bind every credential to one human**, with MFA and no shared accounts or delegated tokens for override functions (closes the AP202 credential path).
3. **Require the emergency token to be issued by a control function, not the requester**, with a server-side timestamp and a linked incident ticket; treat "token later than Prepare_Payment" as an automatic, logged exception that triggers case-level review (closes AP206).
4. **Prohibit releases while AV2 is unavailable** unless a signed fallback is produced by a named second approver with a stated, testable check — never an unsigned checklist (closes AP208).
5. **Define and evidence a settlement cut-off and a recall procedure**, and require that any post-release ratification be completed before a defined cut-off, with the case auto-escalating if the cut-off passes unratified (protects AP207's timing advantage from becoming luck).
6. **Sample-test the substantive quality of MG4 and RA7 reviews** (receipt verified? bank detail verified? before or after settlement?), because "an approval occurred" and "an audit occurred" are the two most common false assurances in this population.
7. **Resolve the two-person cell question in writing.** If AP204/AP209 are a designed cell, the group must either (a) assign an independent challenger outside the cell for a risk-based sample, or (b) accept and quantify the correlated-error exposure explicitly as a documented risk acceptance with a review date — not leave it as an undocumented assumption.

```python
rel = log[log["activity"] == "Release_Payment"].set_index("case_id")[["resource", "timestamp"]]
rel.columns = ["rel_resource", "rel_ts"]
pair_df = prep.join(rel, how="outer").reset_index()
pair_df["released"] = pair_df["rel_ts"].notna()
pair_df["nominal_four_eyes_violation"] = (
    pair_df["released"] & (pair_df["prep_resource"] == pair_df["rel_resource"])
)

pm4py_four_eyes_cases: set[str] = set()
pm4py_semantics = "PM4Py filter not executed (pandas-only fallback)"
if pm4py is not None:
    try:
        result = None
        for candidate in (log, None):
            try:
                if candidate is None:
                    from pm4py.convert import convert_to_event_log  # type: ignore
                    candidate = convert_to_event_log(log)
                result = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
                    candidate, "Prepare_Payment", "Release_Payment")
                break
            except Exception:
                result = None
        if result is not None:
            if isinstance(result, pd.DataFrame):
                pm4py_four_eyes_cases = set(result["case_id"].dropna().unique())
            else:  # PM4Py EventLog
                cases_seen = set()
                for trace in result:
                    attrs = getattr(trace, "attributes", {}) or {}
                    cid = attrs.get("case_id", getattr(trace, "case_id", None))
                    if cid is not None:
                        cases_seen.add(str(cid))
                pm4py_four_eyes_cases = cases_seen
    except Exception as exc:
        pm4py_semantics = f"PM4Py filter raised: {exc}"

my_violations = set(pair_df.loc[pair_df["nominal_four_eyes_violation"], "case_id"])
completed = set(pair_df.loc[pair_df["released"], "case_id"])
if pm4py_four_eyes_cases:
    if pm4py_four_eyes_cases == my_violations:
        pm4py_semantics = "PM4Py returned the VIOLATING cases (consistent with pandas computation)"
    elif completed - my_violations and pm4py_four_eyes_cases == (completed - my_violations):
        pm4py_semantics = ("PM4Py returned the COMPLIANT cases in this build; "
                           "violating set derived as the complement")
    else:
        pm4py_semantics = "PM4Py output not directly comparable; pandas computation is authoritative"

print(f"[i] nominal four-eyes violations (pandas): {sorted(my_violations)}")
print(f"[i] {pm4py_semantics}")
if pm4py_four_eyes_cases:
    print(f"[i] PM4Py four-eyes cases: {sorted(pm4py_four_eyes_cases)}")

# --------------------------------------------------------------------------- 5. attempted-but-blocked releases (PM4Py cannot do this)
blocked = log[log["activity"].isin(["Attempt_Release", "Automated_Validate_Block",
                                    "Cancel_Payment", "Validation_Service_Unavailable"])]
blocked_cases = sorted(
    set(blocked["case_id"]) - set(pair_df.loc[pair_df["released"], "case_id"])
)
print(f"[i] attempted / blocked (no completed release): {blocked_cases}")

# --------------------------------------------------------------------------- 6. per-case evidence flags
rows = []
for case in cases:
    sub = log[log["case_id"] == case]

    def first_ts(*acts):
        s = sub[sub["activity"].isin(acts)]
        return s["timestamp"].min() if len(s) else pd.NaT

    def last_ts(*acts):
        s = sub[sub["activity"].isin(acts)]
        return s["timestamp"].max() if len(s) else pd.NaT

    prep_ts, rel_ts = first_ts("Prepare_Payment"), first_ts("Release_Payment")
    att_ts, mgr_ts = first_ts("Attempt_Release"), first_ts(*MANAGER_ACTS)
    audit_ts, settle_ts = first_ts("Audit_Review"), first_ts("Bank_Settlement")
    prep_res = sub.loc[sub["activity"] == "Prepare_Payment", "resource"]
    rel_res = sub.loc[sub["activity"] == "Release_Payment", "resource"]
    released = pd.notna(rel_ts)
    same_user = bool(released and prep_res.iloc[0] == rel_res.iloc[0]) if released else False

    if pd.isna(mgr_ts):
        mgr_timing = "none"
    elif pd.notna(rel_ts) and mgr_ts <= rel_ts:
        mgr_timing = "pre_release"
    elif pd.notna(settle_ts) and rel_ts < mgr_ts <= settle_ts:
        mgr_timing = "post_release_pre_settlement"
    elif pd.notna(settle_ts) and mgr_ts > settle_ts:
        mgr_timing = "post_settlement"
    else:
        mgr_timing = "post_release_unknown_settlement"

    audit_h = (audit_ts - rel_ts).total_seconds() / 3600 if (released and pd.notna(audit_ts)) else None
    recall_deadline = (settle_ts if pd.notna(settle_ts) else rel_ts) + timedelta(hours=24)
    audit_within_1bd = bool(pd.notna(audit_h) and audit_h <= 24)          # operational proxy
    audit_before_recall = bool(pd.notna(audit_ts) and audit_ts <= recall_deadline)

    av_activities = set(sub.loc[sub["activity"].str.contains("Validate|Validation"), "activity"])
    av_ok = bool(av_activities) and not (
        {"Automated_Validate_Warning", "Validation_Service_Unavailable"} & av_activities)

    rows.append({
        "case": case,
        "prep_resource": prep_res.iloc[0] if len(prep_res) else None,
        "rel_resource": rel_res.iloc[0] if len(rel_res) else None,
        "released": released,
        "attempted_release": pd.notna(att_ts),
        "same_user_release": same_user,
        "nominal_four_eyes_violation": same_user,
        "prep_ts": prep_ts, "rel_ts": rel_ts, "mgr_ts": mgr_ts,
        "audit_ts": audit_ts, "settlement_ts": settle_ts,
        "manager_timing": mgr_timing,
        "audit_hours_after_release": round(audit_h, 2) if audit_h is not None else None,
        "audit_within_1_business_day": audit_within_1bd,
        "audit_before_recall_expiry": audit_before_recall,
        "av_checks_ran": av_ok,
        "av_block_event": "Automated_Validate_Block" in av_activities,
        "av_outage_event": "Validation_Service_Unavailable" in av_activities,
    })

ev = pd.DataFrame(rows)
out = ev.merge(facts, on="case", how="left")

# --------------------------------------------------------------------------- 7. derived flags
def ed1_conditions(r):
    """Return dict of the seven ED-1 conditions; None = unknown."""
    token_lead = r["token_lead_minutes"]
    return {
        "c1_token_before_prepare": None if pd.isna(token_lead) else bool(token_lead > 0),
        "c2_amount_under_100k": bool(r["amount_eur"] < 100000),
        "c3_vendor_active_180d": bool(r["vendor_active_days"] >= 180),
        "c4_no_bank_change_7d": (True if pd.isna(r["bank_change_hours_ago"])
                                 else bool(r["bank_change_hours_ago"] > 7 * 24)),
        "c5_av_full_pass_no_override": bool(r["av_result"] == "full_pass"
                                            and not r["av_override_by_preparer"]
                                            and r["av_checks_ran"]),
        "c6_independent_mgr_pre_settlement": bool(
            r["manager_timing"] in ("pre_release", "post_release_pre_settlement")
            and r["manager_independent"] is True),
        "c7_audit_within_1bd": bool(r["audit_within_1_business_day"]),
    }

flags = []
for _, r in out.iterrows():
    c = ed1_conditions(r)
    known = [v for v in c.values() if v is not None]
    c_fail = [k for k, v in c.items() if v is False]
    c_unknown = [k for k, v in c.items() if v is None]
    flags.append({
        **{f"ed1_{k}": v for k, v in c.items()},
        "ed1_all_conditions_met": bool(known and not c_fail and not c_unknown),
        "ed1_failed_conditions": ";".join(c_fail) or "-",
        "ed1_unknown_conditions": ";".join(c_unknown) or "-",
        "bank_change_within_7d": bool(pd.notna(r["bank_change_hours_ago"])
                                      and r["bank_change_hours_ago"] <= 7 * 24),
        "bank_change_within_24h": bool(pd.notna(r["bank_change_hours_ago"])
                                       and r["bank_change_hours_ago"] <= 24),
        "self_override": bool(r["av_override_by_preparer"] and r["same_user_release"]),
        "repeat_pair_per_facts": bool(r["pair_payments_30d"] and r["pair_payments_30d"] > 1),
        "shared_workstation": bool(r["shared_workstation"]),
        "materially_at_risk": bool(pd.notna(r["amount_eur"]) and r["amount_eur"] >= 100000),
    })
out = pd.concat([out.reset_index(drop=True), pd.DataFrame(flags)], axis=1)

for _, r in out[out["released"]].iterrows():

    lambda r: len(pairs.get(tuple(sorted([str(r["prep_resource"]), str(r["rel_resource"])])), []))
    if r["released"] else 0, axis=1)
out["repeated_pair_log"] = out["pair_occurrence_count_log"] > 1
```

**Behavioural notes carried into the interpretation (also stated in the script header):**

1. **PM4Py semantics are version-sensitive.** The script does not assume which side of the comparison `filter_four_eyes_principle` returns; it reconciles the result against an independent pandas computation and prints which reading applied. If a future build changes the signature or the EventLog/DataFrame preference, the fallback path keeps the analysis running.
2. **PM4Py is policy-blind.** It has no concept of ED1 permissions, overrides, warnings, settlement irreversibility, or recall windows. It will therefore flag AP203 and AP207 as "violations" purely because one resource label appears twice — which is exactly why the script labels its output *nominal* and why the final classification must come from the gates in Section 2, not from the PM4Py call.
3. **Attempted releases need a separate rule.** AP205 has `Attempt_Release`, not `Release_Payment`; treating it as a completed same-user payment would inflate the same-user rate with a case where the preventive control *worked*. The script isolates it explicitly.
4. **The join to case facts is where judgement enters.** Columns such as `manager_independent`, `av_override_by_preparer`, `pair_payments_30d` and `token_lead_minutes` are analyst inputs from the case table, not derivable from the log; where the log and the facts conflict (AP206's token timestamp), the script emits *both* a failed condition and an `ed1_unknown_conditions` marker rather than silently picking one.
