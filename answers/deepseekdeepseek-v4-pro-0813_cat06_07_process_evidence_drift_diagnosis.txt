## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

January–March are a stable baseline. Observed breach is 0.18–0.19, predicted risk 0.18–0.19, AUROC 0.81–0.82, ECE 0.022–0.025, and maturity is 100%. Process variant mix is stable: roughly 45% V1, 35% V2, 20% V3. Parts edge is 28–35 h. This is the pre-drift reference period.

April is primarily a schema/pipeline incident, not real drift. The workflow migration renamed the diagnosis activity, so prediction prefixes were built at the wrong event or not built at all. Unknown activity share jumped to 17%, missing queue feature to 14%, and variant unmapping rose to 18%. The model overpredicted risk: mean predicted 0.28 vs observed 0.19. AUROC dropped to 0.67 and ECE rose to 0.110. Crucially, matured variant breach rates and the parts edge remained similar to March, suggesting the underlying process had not changed as much as the feature pipeline had broken.

May is partial recovery. The mapping patch and backfill reduced unknown activity to 2% and missing queue to 3%. AUROC recovered to 0.78 and ECE to 0.050. Residual damage remains because not all derived features were recomputed.

June is a genuine process/covariate shift, not concept drift. The remote-routing policy increased V2 from ~35% to 52%. Since V2 has a low breach rate (~0.09–0.11), observed breach fell to 0.15. The model predicted 0.20, indicating overprediction from the changed mix. AUROC remained acceptable at 0.79. Conditional outcome rates by variant were stable: V1 0.16, V2 0.09, V3 0.35. The main failure was failure to adapt to the new variant mix.

July is early supplier-driven process drift. The parts edge increased from 42 h in June to 65 h in July. V3 matured breach rose from 0.35 to 0.46. The overall observed breach remained 0.19 because high V2 share offset V3 deterioration. AUROC fell to 0.76.

August is severe supplier-driven process and concept drift. The parts edge reached 130 h; V3 share rose to 35%; V3 matured breach reached 0.64. Observed breach rose to 0.28 while predicted risk stayed 0.19. AUROC fell to 0.68 and ECE to 0.120. Label maturity was 93%, but censoring is not the main explanation: a variant-weighted estimate gives roughly 0.31 overall breach, close to observed 0.28. This is a real conditional outcome change for parts-dependent cases.

September is overlapping process/concept drift and population shift. Parts delay remained severe at 148 h. V3 breach was 0.61. Partner Z onboarded with 24% share, was more V3-heavy, and brought rural/device-age features not in the original model. Label maturity fell to 82%, and Partner Z labels matured more slowly. The observed breach of 0.31 and AUROC of 0.66 reflect both real drift and some label/cohort confounding. This should not be treated as a single population-shift story or a single label-delay story.

October is heavily confounded by label delay and censoring. Maturity is only 55%, median label delay is 52 d, and the outcome API backlog disproportionately affects longer resolution paths. Observed breach of 0.16 is not comparable to mature months. Variant mix and parts duration suggest true breach remains high: V3 share is 41%, Partner Z 27%, parts edge 155 h. The predicted risk of 0.23 is probably still too low. October’s AUROC 0.64 and Brier 0.205 are biased by incomplete outcomes.

Thus April is data-pipeline failure, June is routing/process mix shift, August–September is real supplier/process/concept drift plus population shift, and October is primarily label immaturity.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

### 3.1 Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting

**Test:** For each case-start cohort, compute observed breach at 30, 60, 90, and 120 days and at cutoff. Plot maturity curves and fixed-vintage metrics.

- Under **label delay/censoring**, October’s observed breach will rise toward the high V3/Partner Z expectation as delayed cases mature; current 0.16 is biased downward.
- Under **true improvement**, October’s stabilized breach should remain near 0.16–0.18.
- Under **concept drift**, August–September curves stabilize high or moderate, while October eventually joins them.

### 3.2 Recomputation after activity-label normalization and duplicate cleaning

**Test:** Rebuild April and May features after mapping `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` to `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, removing duplicate events, and backfilling `number of events before diagnosis`.

- Under **data-pipeline failure**, April AUROC should recover to roughly 0.80–0.82, ECE to <0.05, and predicted risk to ~0.19.
- Under **real drift**, April would remain degraded after cleaning because underlying relationships changed.

### 3.3 Stratified calibration and discrimination by variant, source, and month

**Test:** Compute observed vs predicted risk, AUROC, Brier, and ECE by V1/V2/V3 and by Partner Z/non-Partner-Z for mature cases only.

- Under **process-mix shift only**, June variant-specific calibration/discrimination remains stable; aggregate miscalibration is explained by changed weights.
- Under **concept drift**, Aug–Sep V3 calibration/discrimination degrade while V1/V2 remain stable.
- Under **population shift**, Partner Z shows worse calibration/discrimination than non-Partner-Z, and adding rural/device features improves it.

### 3.4 Variant and DFG/performance-DFG comparison

**Test:** Monthly DFG and performance DFG extraction, especially `Order Part -> Part Received`, `Initial Diagnosis -> Remote Fix`, and variant shares.

- Under **process drift**, key edge durations change and explain much of the breach movement; performance-DFG edges are leading indicators.
- Under **concept drift beyond process**, adding observed lead time does not fully explain V3 breach changes; there is residual conditional outcome change.
- Under **pipeline failure**, April’s DFG shows `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY`, unmapped variants, and a broken diagnosis node.

### 3.5 Conditional outcome relationship on the same path

**Test:** Fit a simple saturated/mature-cohort model: `breach ~ month + variant + part_lead_time + source`, with interactions. Test whether V3-month effects remain after controlling for observed part lead time.

- Under **process-only drift**, V3-month coefficients shrink when part lead time is included; risk can be recovered from process features.
- Under **concept drift**, V3-month coefficients remain significant after controlling for observed lead time, suggesting missing supplier/quality features.

### 3.6 Population reweighting or transport-style checks for Partner Z

**Test:** Reweight Sep–Oct to historical source/variant mix and compare metrics; estimate non-Partner-Z vs Partner Z separately.

- Under **population shift only**, reweighting restores calibration; non-Partner-Z behavior is stable.
- Under **concept/process drift as well**, reweighting does not fix V3/supplier-driven degradation.
- Under **censoring confusion**, apparent Partner Z vs non-Partner-Z gap changes as label maturity is held constant.

### 3.7 Feature missingness and lineage audit

**Test:** Audit monthly feature missingness, duplicate counts, unknown activity share, schema versions, and lineage.

- Under **pipeline failure**, anomalies concentrate in April/May and align with metric degradation.
- Under **real drift**, feature quality in Aug–Sep remains high while discrimination/calibration drops.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

### Stage 0: Immediate containment

- Freeze the current model.
- Do **not** retrain on April, September, or October.
- Apply a temporary risk overlay for parts-dependent cases, especially V3 with parts wait or supplier delay. Use recent mature V3 breach estimates from Jul–Aug to set a conservative uplift.
- Increase human review or threshold caution for Partner Z/rural cases until features are available.
- Remove October observed breach from KPI dashboards or label it “immature cohort.”

### Stage 1: Data cleaning and short-term recalibration

- Recompute April/May features after label normalization, duplicate removal, and queue-feature backfill.
- Recalibrate only on clean mature data: Jan–Mar as baseline; May–Jul after cleaning; August only with a fixed-vintage maturity check.
- Use stratified calibration by variant/source. A simple aggregate recalibration is insufficient because V3’s risk has shifted more than V1/V2.
- For June, recalibration or a mix-aware intercept update is reasonable because conditional outcomes were stable.
- For Aug–Sep, recalibration alone is not enough: V3 conditional risk changed, so discrimination may also be damaged. Use segment-level recalibration as a bridge, not a final fix.

### Stage 2: Feature repair

- Normalize renamed activities and remove duplicates.
- Backfill missing derived features.
- Add features:
  - process variant/path;
  - parts lead time or supplier delay flag;
  - rural travel distance and device age;
  - Partner Z/source indicator;
  - outcome maturity time and data-quality flags;
  - schema/pipeline version.
- Rebuild prediction prefixes only after the correct diagnosis activity is identified.

### Stage 3: Full retraining

- Wait for Sep–Oct labels to mature sufficiently, or use a censoring-aware/survival-style outcome model.
- Train on clean mature cohorts. Safe months:
  - Jan–Mar: safe, but pre-drift;
  - Apr: unsafe unless fully recomputed;
  - May: safe after backfill/cleaning;
  - Jun–Jul: safe for mature cohorts;
  - Aug: usable only with fixed-vintage/censoring checks and mature subset;
  - Sep–Oct: unsafe until maturity is high and outcome backlog is cleared.
- A segment-specific or variant-specific model is justified: V1/V2 are stable; V3 has changed substantially. Options:
  - separate V3 model; or
  - one model with variant interactions and parts/supplier features.
- A separate Partner Z model is not automatically justified; include source/rural/device features and validate transportability.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

Implement a layered monitoring system:

- **Predictive monitor:** AUROC, Brier, ECE on mature cohorts only. Alert on degradation after maturity and data-quality gates.
- **Calibration monitor:** Reliability curves and ECE stratified by variant, source, and month.
- **Label-maturity monitor:** Percentage of labels matured at cutoff, median label delay, outcome API backlog, censoring rate.
- **Data-quality monitor:** Unknown activity share, missing queue feature, duplicate rate, schema-change events, pipeline version.
- **Process-variant monitor:** V1/V2/V3/unmapped shares; alert on large mix changes.
- **Performance-DFG monitor:** Monthly median duration for `Order Part -> Part Received`, diagnosis-to-`Remote Fix`, and other key edges.
- **Population monitor:** Partner Z share, rural travel distance, device age distributions.
- **Intervention/deployment monitor:** Model version, feature pipeline version, activity-label mapping version, patch dates.

**Alert dependencies:**

- If label maturity is low or outcome API backlog is high, suppress predictive/calibration retraining alerts because low observed breach may be censoring, not true improvement.
- If data quality is poor, route to pipeline repair rather than model retraining.
- If performance-DFG or process-variant alarms fire, trigger process investigation; do not immediately retrain.
- If population shift alarms fire, require stratified evaluation before aggregate conclusions.
- Deployment/intervention events create a shadow evaluation for a model change.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Read raw events
raw = pd.read_csv("field_service_events.csv")
raw["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(raw["timestamp"], errors="coerce")

# Schema normalization BEFORE feature extraction
ACTIVITY_ALIASES = {
    "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed",
    "DIAG_ASSESS_COMPLETED": "Initial Diagnosis Completed",
}
raw["concept:name"] = raw["activity"].replace(ACTIVITY_ALIASES)
raw["case:concept:name"] = raw["case_id"].astype(str)

# Duplicate-handling strategy:
# conservative event-level dedupe after normalization; audit dropped events.
raw["is_duplicate"] = raw.duplicated(
    subset=["case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"],
    keep="first",
)
df = raw[~raw["is_duplicate"]].copy()
df["is_unknown_activity"] = df["concept:name"].isin(
    ["UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY", "unknown"]
)

# PM4Py formatted log
log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)

# Case-start cohort key; different from event-calendar month.
df["case_start"] = df.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].transform("min")

monthly_summaries = []
for month_start in pd.date_range("2026-01-01", "2026-11-01", freq="MS"):
    month_end = month_start + pd.DateOffset(months=1)

    # Event-calendar view: events that occurred in this calendar month.
    # Useful for DFG/performance/process-volume summaries.
    month_log = pm4py.filter_time_range(
        log, str(month_start), str(month_end), mode="events"
    )

    dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(month_log)
    perf_dfg, perf_start, perf_end = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(month_log)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(month_log)

    # Case-start cohort view: cases whose first event occurred in month.
    # Useful for outcome/label maturity summaries, not equivalent to event calendar.
    cohort_ids = df.loc[
        (df["case_start"] >= month_start) & (df["case_start"] < month_end),
        "case:concept:name",
    ].unique()
    cohort = df[df["case:concept:name"].isin(cohort_ids)]

    order_part_perf = perf_dfg.get(("Order Part", "Part Received"))

    monthly_summaries.append({
        "month": month_start.strftime("%Y-%m"),
        "event_calendar_cases": int(
            df.loc[
                (df["time:timestamp"] >= month_start)
                & (df["time:timestamp"] < month_end),
                "case:concept:name",
            ].nunique()
        ),
        "case_start_cohort_cases": int(cohort["case:concept:name"].nunique()),
        "variant_counts": {str(v): len(c) for v, c in variants.items()},
        "top_dfg_edges": dict(list(dfg.items())[:10]),
        "order_part_to_part_received_perf_seconds": order_part_perf,
        "unknown_activity_share": float(cohort["is_unknown_activity"].mean()),
        "duplicate_events_in_month": int(
            raw.loc[
                (raw["time:timestamp"] >= month_start)
                & (raw["time:timestamp"] < month_end),
                "is_duplicate",
            ].sum()
        ),
        # Monthly process evidence; interpretation is event-calendar,
        # while outcome metrics should use case-start cohorts.
        "temporal_features": pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
            month_log
        ).to_dict(),
    })

# Auditable monthly summaries; no model fitting.
monthly_summaries
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

### 6.1 Retraining on immature labels

**Failure mode:** Retraining on October’s observed breach of 0.16 would teach the model that breach risk is low just as delayed high-risk V3/Partner Z cases mature and breach. The model would under-predict future risk.

**Mitigation:** Use fixed-vintage label-maturity curves; require high maturity or censoring-aware labels; exclude or down-weight immature cohorts.

### 6.2 Calibrating around a pipeline bug

**Failure mode:** Recalibrating on April’s damaged predictions would encode `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY`, missing queue, duplicated events, and incorrect diagnosis prefixes into the model. When the pipeline is fixed, the model would be mis-calibrated again.

**Mitigation:** First normalize labels, deduplicate, backfill features, and recompute predictions. Compare cleaned April with Jan–Mar before using it.

### 6.3 Ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept drift

**Failure mode:** If August–September is treated mostly as label delay or population shift, the organization may miss the V3 conditional breach increase caused by parts supplier failures. The model would continue to under-predict parts-dependent cases.

**Mitigation:** Monitor performance-DFG edges and variant-specific breach rates. Retrain a V3 segment or use strong V3 interactions with parts lead time and supplier features.

### 6.4 Attributing Partner Z population shift to model unfairness without stratified evidence

**Failure mode:** Partner Z has higher observed breach and slower label maturation. Treating this as model bias or unfairness could lead to misleading “fairness” adjustments; treating it only as bias could mask real rural/device risk differences. Treating it as purely outcome change could ignore missing features.

**Mitigation:** Stratify by source, variant, and maturity. Add rural travel distance and device age to the model. Use reweighting/transport checks to separate distribution shift from model error.

### 6.5 Treating June routing mix as concept drift and fully retraining

**Failure mode:** June’s variant mix shifted sharply because of remote-routing policy. V2’s conditional breach remained stable. A full retraining on June data could overfit the temporary mix and degrade performance if the mix shifts again.

**Mitigation:** Test conditional outcome stability by variant. If V1/V2/V3 conditional risks are stable, use recalibration or mix-aware intercept adjustment rather than full retraining.
