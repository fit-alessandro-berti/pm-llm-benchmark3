## 1. Materiality Framework
Severity is defined by a structured, business-focused lens that separates *control objective adherence* from *financial/reversibility metrics*. It evaluates **only** whether policy overlays (P1–P4) were followed in sequence and evidence sufficiency for critical decisions; it does not incorporate raw alignment cost, UI telemetry, or technical silent moves (T). Severity 1–5 scale:

- **Severity 1 (Low)**: Harmless technical noise (e.g., UI telemetry UI events like `Open Workbench`, retry notifications with same message ID). No policy violation; evidence reliable; no customer/regulatory impact; easily reversible (e.g., notification timing met via portal). Example: CL-601, CL-603 retry.
- **Severity 2 (Moderate)**: Minor event-label mismatch or missing core log for external evidence (e.g., `Assess Damage` vs `Assess Loss` when external adjuster report exists; CRM notify outside core log). Policy sequence followed (e.g., Fraud Screen before Pay Claim), but core log lacks standard event; evidence reliability medium (e.g., verbal check without documentation); low customer harm (e.g., slight delay in core log record); not irreversible. Example: CL-611, CL-608.
- **Severity 3 (Moderate–High)**: Policy condition violated at decision point (e.g., Auto Approve used when bank change occurred 7 days or amount >5,000 EUR for LOW risk; MEDIUM risk auto-approve when P1 restricts auto-approve to 5k/LOW), but Pay Claim not issued *before* required approval (i.e., P3 violation avoided in alignment). Exposure moderate (financial loss possible if fraud exists); timing risk (e.g., bank accepts payment before screen), but no actual payment issued before screen in alignment; reversible via recall if caught early; evidence reliability medium (e.g., bank timestamp clear, but verbal check undocumented). Example: CL-610, CL-609 (if auto-approve used for MEDIUM >5k).
- **Severity 4 (High)**: Operational deviation where Pay Claim occurs *before* applicable Fraud Screen + Manual Approve (violating P3), or model suggests Pay Claim for denied/withdrawn claim (even if no payment made), or policy condition ignored despite clear contextual signal (e.g., HIGH risk + bank change). Customer harm: claimant delay >24h notification risk; regulatory exposure (fraud control failure); partial irreversibility (payment already issued; cannot undo if claim paid); evidence reliability low or ambiguous (e.g., verbal check "relied on" with no evidence). Example: CL-604 (payment logged at 10:03, screen at 10:20), CL-607 (model suggests Pay Claim for denied claim), CL-612 (model suggests Pay Claim after withdrawal).
- **Severity 5 (Severe)**: Explicit P3 violation: Pay Claim step executed (log shows `Pay Claim` event or model aligns Pay Claim *before* Fraud Screen + Manual Approve in sequence), with high amount (50k EUR) or HIGH risk, and no compensating evidence (e.g., no fraud screen before payment). Irreversible (payment processed); high customer harm (financial loss, trust damage); regulatory breach risk; evidence unreliable or missing (e.g., no fraud evidence for verbal check); policy condition directly ignored. Example: CL-604 alignment shows `L(Pay Claim,c=1)` before `M(Fraud Screen,c=1)`  actual payment (bank accepted at 10:03) occurred before screen (manual at 10:20), meeting P3 severe failure. Note: Severity is assessed *from business context + policy sequence*, not raw cost. Confidence differs from severity: Severity reflects *likelihood of policy violation given evidence*; Confidence reflects *epistemic certainty about evidence interpretation* (e.g., high confidence for timestamped bank acceptance; medium confidence for "verbal check relied on" with no written evidence; low confidence for scope disputes like denied leads). Confidence is scored 1–5 (1 = low, 5 = high) per case based on evidence clarity (e.g., concrete timestamps = high; ambiguous verbal reliance = medium; scope disagreement = low). Raw alignment cost (sum of L/M costs) is a technical metric for model edit distance only; it does not equate to severity because: (a) silent moves (T, cost 0) may be correct internal routing (e.g., CL-605), (b) UI telemetry (e.g., `Open Workbench`) is not a business activity per P5 and incurs cost 1 in raw but causes no policy harm, (c) missing external evidence (e.g., adjuster report) costs 1 for M(Assess Loss) but may be accurate, (d) duplicate notifications (same message ID + retry) add L cost but meet P4 timing via portal, (e) policy violations (e.g., auto-approve with bank change) often show raw cost 0 (synchronous alignment) because no L/M for policy check step, and (f) irreversibility (payment) is not captured in cost function. Thus, raw cost is misleading for business risk prioritization.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Raw alignment cost (sum of `L`/`M` costs under cost function = 1 per L/M move) measures only the technical edit distance between log events and model trace (ignoring `T` moves, which cost 0). It does **not** reflect business risk because:

1. **Silent moves (`T`, cost 0) can be correct and harmless**: CL-605 shows `T(tau)` for internal routing before Auto Approve. The cost 0 correctly indicates no edit needed; however, if a silent move hid a policy violation (e.g., routing bypassed fraud check), cost would still be 0, while risk would be severe. Thus, cost 0  risk-free (e.g., CL-605 has severity 1, but a hypothetical silent bypass would have severity 5; cost cannot distinguish).

2. **UI telemetry (e.g., `Open Workbench`, `Autosave`, retry notifications) incurs cost 1 but causes no policy harm**: CL-601 (`L(Open Workbench,c=1)` etc.) and CL-603 (`L(Notify Claimant,c=1)` for retry) have raw cost >0 due to `L` moves, but P5 explicitly states UI telemetry "is not a business activity unless it reveals a meaningful control issue". Hence, cost 1 for UI events is misleading for risk (they cause no customer/regulatory harm), while severity remains 1.

3. **Policy condition violations often show raw cost 0**: CL-610 (bank change 1h before, Auto Approve synchronous, raw 0) and CL-609 (MEDIUM risk Auto Approve synchronous, raw 0) have no `L`/`M` for the policy condition check (e.g., no `L` for "bank-change flag" or `M` for "skip Auto Approve"). Thus, raw cost 0 falsely suggests compliance, while P1 violation exists (risk severity 3). Similarly, CL-607 (denied claim + M(Auto Approve), raw 4) has high cost due to multiple `M` moves, but the violation (model suggesting Pay for denied claim) is not reflected in cost magnitude relative to severity.

4. **Missing external evidence (e.g., adjuster report) costs 1 for `M(Assess Loss)` but may be accurate**: CL-606 has `M(Assess Loss,c=1)` because core log lacks `Assess Loss` (uses `Assess Damage`), raw cost 1. However, the external adjuster report exists and likely reflects the assessment; thus, the `M` move indicates a labeling mismatch, not a factual error. Severity is 2 (moderate), not tied to cost 1; cost cannot assess evidence reliability.

5. **Irreversibility (e.g., payment) is ignored by cost function**: CL-604 shows payment accepted by bank at 10:03 while Fraud Screen at 10:20; raw cost 3 reflects edit distance, but payment is irreversible (financial loss if fraud exists). Cost does not incorporate timing impact or customer harm; severity 5 reflects irreversibility, while cost 3 is moderate.

6. **Duplicate notifications (same message ID + retry) inflate raw cost but meet policy**: CL-603 has `L(Notify Claimant,c=1)` due to retry, raw cost 1, but P4 is satisfied (notify within 24h via portal). Severity is 1; cost 1 suggests deviation, but no business risk. Thus, raw cost overstates "deviation" for harmless technical behavior.

7. **Policy severity depends on sequence + evidence, not step count**: P3 ("Pay Claim before applicable approval is severe") cares about *order* (Pay before Screen), not whether UI events are logged. CL-604 alignment shows `L(Pay)` before `M(Fraud Screen)`, triggering severity 5, but raw cost 3 does not encode "severe" semantics; cost is just edit size. Similarly, CL-610 (sync Auto Approve with bank change) has raw cost 0 but severity 3 because condition violated; cost cannot infer condition check.

**Concrete examples using supplied cases**:
- CL-604: Raw cost 3 (L+M for Pay/Screen), but severity 5 (P3 violation; payment already issued). Cost 3 does not indicate severity; a cheaper alignment (e.g., synchronous Pay+Screen) would have raw cost 0 but still violate P3 if Pay occurs first.
- CL-610: Raw cost 0 (synchronous Auto Approve), but severity 3 (P1 violation; bank changed 1h before). Cost 0 falsely implies compliance; no L/M for policy condition check.
- CL-603: Raw cost 1 (L for retry Notify), but severity 1 (P4 met). Cost 1 suggests deviation; but retry is harmless per P5.
- CL-607: Raw cost 4 (multiple M moves), but confidence low (scope dispute); severity 4 due to model suggesting Pay for denied claim. Cost 4 might seem "high", but confidence low and policy ambiguous; risk depends on actual gate enforcement, not cost.
- CL-605: Raw cost 0 (T move), severity 1; cost 0 correctly reflects no edit, but a silent bypass would also cost 0 while having severity 5. Cost cannot distinguish these.

Thus, raw alignment cost is a useful *technical diagnostic* (e.g., identifying frequent L/M patterns like retry or label mismatches) but **cannot be used to assess business risk severity, confidence, or prioritize remediation** because it ignores policy semantics (P1–P4), evidence reliability, irreversibility, and harmless technical noise (P5). Severity must be assessed separately using business context, policy conditions, and evidence clarity (as done in Section 2), while raw cost informs only pattern frequency (e.g., "many cases have L(Notify)" for retry pattern).
