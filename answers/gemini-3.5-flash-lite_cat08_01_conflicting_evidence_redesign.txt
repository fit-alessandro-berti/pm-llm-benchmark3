# 1. Current-process diagnosis

### Main performance, quality, compliance, and customer-experience problems
The warranty process suffers from severe systemic friction across multiple operational dimensions:
* **Performance:** The process is heavily bottlenecked by manual decision-making and inefficient triage. The *Manual eligibility review* queue is severely constrained by human resource limits (7 reviewers, EUR 34/hr), causing cases to languish with a mean time of 7.5 to 22 hours per transition, and up to 140 hours for cases caught in *Request missing evidence* loops. 
* **Quality & Rework:** The fast path relies on an outdated, blunt *Auto eligibility screen* that pushes high-value items straight to *Fast approval* without adequate checks. This creates a high downstream failure rate: *Customer complaint/reopen* occurs in 14.1% of Fast approval cases. Furthermore, *Rework repair* affects 12.5% of repaired high-value devices, indicating poor initial diagnostic accuracy and incorrect replacement part selection.
* **Compliance & Risk:** The process violates core internal policies. Alignment diagnostics reveal frequent "move-on-log" deviations including *Fast approval* before evidence completeness, *Customer complaint/reopen* after close, and *Dispatch replacement* without risk review for high-value devices. This directly violates Normative rules **R1** (claims >EUR 400 requiring manual or risk review) and **R2** (no replacement dispatch before evidence completeness, except for Premium low-risk accessories).
* **Customer Experience:** Outcomes are heavily polarized. Premium high-value device customers suffer a staggering 22% complaint/reopen rate on the fast path, while Standard low-risk accessory customers experience unnecessary delays (median 13–52 hours) due to a one-size-fits-all process design.

### The Fast-Path Contradiction
Process-mining evidence presents a classic illusion of efficiency: the most frequent path (`START -> Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close -> END`) appears fast and cheap on paper. It costs 31% less *before* rework and takes a median of 1.8 to 3.2 hours per step. 

However, viewing this path in isolation is misleading. Downstream analysis shows that this speed is an artifact of shifting costs downstream. Because the auto-screen rules are outdated and segment-agnostic, high-value and high-risk claims bypass necessary scrutiny. Consequently, the initial savings are erased by expensive downstream errors: 
* Wrong replacement parts for high-value devices cost an expected EUR 410 per incident.
* Complaint handling and reshipments reduce the initial 31% cost advantage to a mere 9% net savings.
* Customer trust is eroded, notably driving complaint rates up to 22% for Premium high-value segments. 

The fast path is not genuinely efficient; it is a high-velocity error-propagation engine.

### Reference to Specific Process-Mining Artifacts
1. **Aggregated Frequency DFG:** Highlights that 2,550 out of 5,000 cases flow through `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval`, establishing it as the dominant operational artery.
2. **Performance DFG (p90 edges):** Exposes massive tail-latencies, such as `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` having a p90 of 88 hours, and `Customer complaint/reopen -> Manual eligibility review` reaching 100 hours p90.
3. **Token-Based Replay Summary:** Demonstrates poor normative alignment with a log fitness of 0.91 and only 68% fitting traces, proving that more than one-third of cases violate process compliance rules.
4. **Alignment Diagnostics:** Specifically isolates critical procedural violations: `Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices` and `Fast approval before Evidence completeness`.
5. **Customer Segment Outcomes Data:** Proves that Premium high-value device customers endure a 22% complaint/reopen rate on the fast path versus only 5% on the reviewed/repair path, mathematically validating that speed without risk-segmentation destroys value for critical segments.

# 2. Redesign A: Segment-Driven Intelligent Triage & Automated Gatekeeping

### Redesigned Process Flow
`START -> Intake claim -> Segment-Aware Dynamic Auto-Screen (New) -> [Conditional Split]`
* **Path 1 (Low-Risk, Verified Data):** Instant auto-approval -> Automated inventory matching -> *Dispatch replacement* -> *Quality/compliance close*.
* **Path 2 (High-Value / High-Risk / Missing Evidence):** Automated evidence completeness check -> *Fraud/risk review* or *Manual eligibility review* (routed dynamically based on EUR >400 threshold and risk score) -> *Repair quote* or *Fast approval* -> *Fulfillment*.

### Target Segments, Paths, and Activities
* **Segments:** Targets all segments, but explicitly bifurcates Standard/Premium low-risk accessories from High-Value devices (EUR >400).
* **Paths & Activities:** Replaces the blunt *Auto eligibility screen* with a dynamic rules engine. It injects a mandatory, automated pre-dispatch quality gate for high-value devices to cross-reference defect types with replacement part selections.

### Mechanism of Improvement
By embedding segment and financial thresholds (*R1* and *R2*) directly into the intake logic, the process stops treating a EUR 45 accessory the same way it treats a EUR 800 device. Low-risk, low-cost accessories flow through a friction-free digital path, protecting low-cost speed. Simultaneously, high-value claims are automatically captured by risk and manual review layers *before* financial commitment. This eliminates illegal fast-approvals, slashes wrong-replacement errors (expected cost EUR 410), and cuts the 14.1% Fast Approval complaint rate.

### Deliberate Sacrifices and Unresolved Trade-offs
* **Sacrifice:** Increases cycle time and resource pressure for high-value device segments. Manual review queues will see an initial volume surge.
* **Unresolved:** Relies on customer data accuracy at intake; if initial defect descriptions are false or incomplete, edge-cases will still leak into incorrect routing paths.

# 3. Redesign B: Digital Self-Service Evidence Portal with Asynchronous Decisioning

### Redesigned Process Flow
`START -> Intake claim -> Guided Digital Evidence Portal (New) -> Automated Completeness Verification -> [Async Decision engine] -> Targeted Human Exception Handling -> Fulfillment -> END`

### Target Segments, Paths, and Activities
* **Segments:** Targets Standard and Premium low-risk accessories, as well as clear-defect high-value devices where customers are digitally adept. 
* **Paths & Activities:** Completely overhauls the *Request missing evidence* loop. Instead of serial back-and-forth emails/calls (which currently repeat in 26% of cases and drag p90 times to 88+ hours), the portal forces structured photo/diagnostic upload at intake.

### Mechanism of Improvement
The primary bottleneck in the current process is the iterative ping-pong of missing evidence (`Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review -> Request missing evidence`). By deploying a guided digital portal that leverages the finding that a new evidence portal reduces repeat requests by 30–45%, the process eliminates dead time. Asynchronous auto-decisioning evaluates completeness instantly. This decouples data gathering from human labor, freeing up the 7 manual reviewers and 2 fraud specialists to focus purely on complex or ambiguous exceptions rather than chasing missing serial numbers or photos.

### Deliberate Sacrifices and Unresolved Trade-offs
* **Sacrifice:** Explicitly harms customers with low digital literacy. The prompt notes that a portal may increase claim abandonment for this demographic. 
* **Unresolved:** Does not solve internal technical diagnosis errors for complex electronic internal failures; it only optimizes the *intake and evidence* phase of the workflow.

# 4. Redesign C: Hybrid Concierge Routing for Premium Tiers & Automated Repair-Diagnostic Pairing

### Redesigned Process Flow
`START -> Intake claim -> Segment & Value Recognition -> [Bifurcated Track]`
* **Track 1 (Standard Tier):** Streamlined digital self-service with automated rules.
* **Track 2 (Premium Tier - Low Risk & High Value):** Instant routing to a dedicated "Concierge Desk" -> Integrated Technical-Diagnostic Pre-Check -> Precision Part-Price Confirmation -> Expedited Repair or Replacement.

### Target Segments, Paths, and Activities
* **Segments:** Specifically targets Premium customers (both low-risk accessories and high-value devices, representing 32% of total volume combined).
* **Paths & Activities:** Bypasses standard generic queues for Premium segments, routing them through a specialized concierge track. It targets the repair quote delay loop by pre-authorizing part prices within predefined thresholds to eliminate waiting times for part-price confirmation.

### Mechanism of Improvement
Premium customers have strict service-level expectations and high service-credit exposure if cycle times exceed 24 hours. The current process forces Premium high-value claims through the same slow, bureaucratic queues as standard claims, resulting in a 22% complaint rate. By creating a dedicated, high-touch, fast-turnaround track with pre-authorized part pricing, Redesign C slashes cycle times for high-margin customers, protects service-level agreements, and drives down the long-tail rework-repair rates (currently 12.5% for high-value devices) through specialized technician assignment.

### Deliberate Sacrifices and Unresolved Trade-offs
* **Sacrifice:** Increases operational cost per case for the Premium tier and concentrates labor pressure on specialized high-cost internal resources (technicians/specialists).
* **Unresolved:** Standard tier customers receive no cycle-time benefit and may experience perceived service disparity if tier differentiation is too visible.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

### Redesign A (Segment-Driven Intelligent Triage & Automated Gatekeeping)
* **Failure Mode 1:** The automated pre-dispatch quality gate becomes overly conservative, blocking legitimate fast-approvals and artificially inflating manual review volume beyond the 82% utilization tipping point.
* **Failure Mode 2:** Outdated inventory/defect mapping databases lead to false-negative part mismatches, frustrating customers with unearned blocks.
* **Displaced Bottleneck:** Migrates from the *Auto eligibility screen* downstream to the *Fraud/risk review* and *Manual eligibility review* queues, creating a severe queue backlog on Mondays and post-campaign periods.

### Redesign B (Digital Self-Service Evidence Portal with Asynchronous Decisioning)
* **Failure Mode 1:** High customer abandonment rates among non-digital-native demographics who struggle with photo uploads and structured form-fills.
* **Failure Mode 2:** Customers upload low-resolution or irrelevant images, causing automated completeness verification to fail repeatedly and driving customers offline into expensive phone channels.
* **Displaced Bottleneck:** Migrates from intake/evidence-gathering downstream to customer support phone lines and manual exception handling for abandoned digital claims.

### Redesign C (Hybrid Concierge Routing for Premium Tiers & Automated Repair-Diagnostic Pairing)
* **Failure Mode 2:** Premium tier criteria gaming, where Standard customers find loopholes to enter the high-cost Concierge track, inflating operational labor costs.
* **Failure Mode 2:** Pre-authorized part-pricing rules lead to financial leakage when technicians abuse pricing thresholds for complex repairs.
* **Displaced Bottleneck:** Migrates internal resource pressure directly onto the specialized repair technicians and the single daily 45-minute *Escalation board* slot, making it a critical chokepoint.

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

### Staged Experiment Plan

#### Phase 1: First Pilot (The "Rules & Guardrails" Sprint - Redesign A Foundation)
* **Scope:** Update the *Auto eligibility screen* rules within 2 weeks, specifically hard-coding normative rules **R1** (mandatory review for claims >EUR 400) and **R2** (evidence completeness check before replacement dispatch). Test exclusively on Standard High-Value devices.
* **Metrics to Monitor:** Complaint/reopen rate on high-value fast paths (baseline 18%), manual reviewer queue utilization, and compliance alignment fitness score.
* **Stop/Go Thresholds:** 
  * *Stop:* If manual reviewer utilization exceeds 82% and queue wait times surpass 48 hours.
  * *Go:* If high-value complaint rates drop below 10% without exceeding manual review capacity limits.
* **Sequencing Logic:** Establishes compliance baselines and stops financial bleeding on high-value items before investing in heavy portal technology.

#### Phase 2: Second Pilot (Digital Evidence Portal - Redesign B)
* **Scope:** Roll out the self-service evidence portal to a 30% randomized cohort of Standard Low-Risk Accessory claimants.
* **Metrics to Monitor:** Evidence request loop frequency (baseline: 26% repeat twice), customer abandonment rate, and median cycle time for low-risk accessories.
* **Stop/Go Thresholds:**
  * *Stop:* If claim abandonment increases by >5% compared to the control group.
  * *Go:* If repeat evidence requests drop by >30% and median cycle time for low-risk accessories falls below 10 hours.
* **Sequencing Logic:** Validates digital adoption and iron-outs UX bugs before broad enterprise deployment.

#### Phase 3: Third Pilot (Concierge Track - Redesign C)
* **Scope:** Introduce the dedicated Concierge Track and pre-authorized part pricing for 50% of incoming Premium high-value claims.
* **Metrics to Monitor:** Premium customer satisfaction, service-credit exposure incidents (target: zero exceedances past 24 hours), and repair rework rates.
* **Stop/Go Thresholds:**
  * *Stop:* If specialist labor cost per case exceeds the financial savings gained from avoiding service credits.
  * *Go:* If Premium customer complaint rates drop below 3% and service-credit payouts fall by >50%.
* **Sequencing Logic:** Leverages learnings from Phases 1 and 2 to ensure baseline compliance and efficient intake before dedicating expensive human concierge resources.

### Learning Whether Improvements Are Real vs. Shifted Downstream
To prevent illusory optimization (where fixing an intake metric simply creates a downstream crisis), the experiment monitoring framework must track **Lagged Conformance and Quality Indicators** for 30 days post-pilot closure:
1. **The Reopener Lag Check:** Track whether cases closed during a pilot reappear as `Customer complaint/reopen` or hit the *Escalation board* 14 to 30 days later. If front-end cycle time drops by 50% but downstream complaints rise by a corresponding margin, the speed is superficial.
2. **True-Net Cost Accounting:** Compare pre-rework costs against post-rework/reshipment costs per cohort. A real improvement must demonstrate a net-cost reduction *after* factoring in replacement errors (EUR 95 / EUR 410) and service credits.
