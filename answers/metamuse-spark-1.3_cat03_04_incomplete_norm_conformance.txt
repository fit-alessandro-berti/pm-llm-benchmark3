## 1. Conformance semantics

**Closed vs open world:** The norm is **closed-world only** for (a) relations explicitly marked `forbidden` — any true occurrence is noncompliant, and (b) `mandatory` columns **within stated scope** — absence where scope applies is a deviation. It is otherwise **open-world**: absence from the normative edge set does **not** imply prohibition, and presence does not imply violation. `typical` and `unspecified` are open-world.

Classification used here:

- **Forbidden behavior:** observed instance of a relation explicitly marked `forbidden` (N3, T6, E10). Requires object-identity and event-semantics verification; if verified, it is a violation regardless of frequency. Rarity does not mitigate.
- **Missing mandatory behavior:** absence of a `mandatory` relation where its scope holds (N1, N4, T1-T2, E1-E8). Must discuss **scope** — e.g., N4 applies only to externally shipped packages — and **abstraction** before concluding violation.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior:** observed relation with non-trivial weight that has no normative status (`unspecified` or wholly unmodeled, e.g., T4, T5, E9, N6). Benign by default; suspicious only if other evidence shows harm, workaround, or scope creep.
- **Typical-pattern absence:** absence of a `typical` relation (N2, N5, T3). Not a violation alone. Requires business reason: legitimate exceptions exist by definition (credit notes, write-offs, unapplied cash, intermediate steps). Only *unexplained* remainder is suspicious.
- **Likely data-quality error:** pattern best explained by join, extraction, mapping, or identifier-reuse error rather than real business action. E.g., Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice on reused numeric ID. Classified separately from process violation; needs lineage check.
- **Abstraction mismatch:** diagnostic artifact from summarization, not business reality. Main cases: `eventually` collapsed to `directly-follows`, lifecycle start/end collapsed to `cobirth/codeath`, attribute-encoded relation omitted from object link (carrier reference vs Delivery object).
- **Insufficient evidence:** graph summaries lack case/event IDs, timestamps, attribute values, and scope flags. Counts alone cannot decide violation vs exception vs error. Explicit evidence request required.

PM4Py diagnostics report graph difference; they do not encode `mandatory/typical/forbidden/unspecified`, scope conditions, tolerances, or data-quality knowledge and therefore are not a legal/business verdict.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG
**Can establish:** Per-object-type sequential adjacency summaries and missing/extra adjacency counts. E.g., it flags 2× `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`, 2/100 missing `Create -> Validate`, 2/200 missing `Pack -> Dispatch`, 18/110 `Issue Invoice` not followed by `Record Payment` (4 Write Off +14 no successor), and unmodeled adjacencies like `Validate -> Issue Invoice` (72) and `Reopen -> Close` (1).

**Cannot establish:** Order, causality, or authorization without timestamps and case IDs. It cannot distinguish `eventually follows` from `directly follows`, cannot see unlogged intermediate events, and cannot enforce scope (e.g., N4 only for external shipment). Low counts (1-2) are highly sensitive to one mis-linked event.

**Counts/scopes:** Denominators matter. 98/100 vs 198/200 vs 92/110 have different business meanings because N1/N4 are mandatory-scoped while N5 is typical with stated exceptions. No tolerance bands are given, so statistical significance cannot be inferred. 83 `Validate -> Close` as directly-follows does not mean 17 violated N2; N2 explicitly permits intermediates.

**Cross-abstraction:** A relation can be conforming as business process but deviant as DFG, and vice versa. The critical danger is interpreting an **eventually-expected** rule as a **directly-follows** rule: N1 says every standard Order should *eventually* enter validation. The summary approximates this with directly-follows. If an unrecorded review, invoice, or data gap sits between Create and Validate, DFG shows a miss while the business rule is satisfied. The additional context explicitly warns the 2 missing cases may be this artifact. Same for N2: expecting `Validate -> Close` directly would falsely incriminate normal invoice/review/fulfilment chains. Conversely, `Cancel -> Dispatch` is forbidden as eventually (no dispatch *after* cancel in same Order), so DFG adjacency understates risk — non-adjacent violations would also count; event-level temporal check is needed.

### OTG
**Can establish:** Which object-type pairs interact, co-occur at birth/death. It confirms strong backbone: Order-Item 1450 (T1), Package-Delivery 200 (T2), Invoice-Payment 92 (T3). It surfaces 1× `Order cobirth Payment` (T6 forbidden), 100× Order-Customer (T4 unspecified), 3× Order-RiskCase (T5 unspecified), 7× Package codeath Delivery (unmodeled).

**Cannot establish:** Whether interaction was correct business interaction (Add Item vs spurious link), whether cobirth/codeath semantics match system birth definition, or whether missing interaction is attribute-encoded. OTG has no direction, no event context, no cardinality rules.

**Counts/scopes:** Weights are interaction counts, not case counts. 1450 Item interactions across ~100 Orders (~14.5/Order) is plausible; 2 missing Order links out of 1450 is 0.14% — likely extractor drop, not process redesign. 1 cobirth in thousands of births deserves investigation but not systemic conclusion. 7 codeaths out of 200 deliveries (3.5%) with shared confirmation event is a lifecycle-modeling choice, not noncompliance — norm is silent on codeath, so open-world applies.

**Cross-abstraction:** `Package interaction Delivery` 200 looks fully conforming at OTG, yet ET–OT shows 2 Dispatch events lack Delivery linkage and OC-DFG may still be fine. Conversely, Order cobirth Payment is forbidden at OTG even if ET–OT per-type links look individually mandatory-compliant. Codeath is conforming/benign at OTG while meaningless at DFG.

### ET–OT
**Can establish:** Whether each event type carries required object links. It validates E1 (100/100), E4-E5 (200/200), E7 (93/93) and flags: Add Item–Order 1448/1450 (E2 gap 2), Dispatch–Delivery 198/200 (E6 gap 2), Record Payment–Invoice 92/93 (E8 gap 1), Sensor–Invoice 1 (E10 forbidden), Manual Review–RiskCase/Customer 3/3 (E9 unspecified, Customer unmodeled).

**Cannot establish:** Whether missing link is truly missing business fact vs alternative representation (carrier attribute vs Delivery object; unapplied cash not typed), or spurious link vs real (sensor join error). It also cannot enforce E8 exception because snapshot has no `unapplied cash` typing — the exception mechanism exists in text but not in data.

**Counts/scopes:** E8 scope is conditional: mandatory *except* explicitly typed unapplied cash. With zero events so typed, the 1 unlinked payment is technically deviant but Finance asserts unapplied cash is possible — a labeling/scope gap, not proof of violation. E6 gap is mitigated by carrier attribute noted in context; the norm does not state whether attribute satisfies the Delivery-object requirement.

**Cross-abstraction:** ET–OT gap can be benign at OTG/business level: 2 dispatches without Delivery objects still have carrier reference and OTG still shows 200 Package-Delivery interactions (suggesting interaction exists elsewhere or via different event). Similarly, Record Payment without Invoice violates E8 at ET–OT but may satisfy T3 typicality (unapplied cash allowed). Sensor–Invoice is a clear E10 ET–OT forbidden link, but at OTG there is no Sensor type tracked — showing need to check multiple families.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

- **Mandatory (violation only if scope holds and abstraction verified):** N1 Create->Validate (2 missing – likely DFG artifact, need trace); N4 Pack->Dispatch (2 missing – need external-shipment flag); T1 Order-Item / E2-E3 Add Item links (2 Order-links missing – probable extraction drop); T2 Package-Delivery / E6 Dispatch-Delivery (2 missing – carrier attribute may satisfy business intent, need norm clarification); E1,E4,E5,E7 baselines conforming; E8 Record-Invoice (1 missing – reclassifies as allowed if event is typed/labeled unapplied cash).
- **Forbidden (any verified true occurrence = violation):** N3 Cancel->Dispatch (2 – robust violation candidate); T6 Order cobirth Payment (1 – violation if birth semantics confirmed, else extractor error); E10 Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice (1 – forbidden if real, but high likelihood DQ error; classification flips from violation to DQ fix upon ID-join proof).

- **Unregulated / explicitly unspecified (benign unless collateral evidence):** N6 Reopen->Close (1); T4 Order-Customer (100) and Manual Review–Customer (3) – *must not* be marked violation for absence from norm, governed elsewhere; T5 Order-RiskCase (3) + E9 Manual Review–RiskCase (3); Package codeath Delivery (7) – joint confirmation pattern; Validate->Issue Invoice (72) – normal fulfilment adjacency though unmodeled. Condition flipping to concern: bulk growth, bypass evidence (e.g., RiskCase used to skip validation), or reuse for financial linkage.

# norm_* bare graphs DO NOT carry status; statuses kept separately (see norm_status below).

def classify(deviation, status):
    # Open-world: unknown edges -> 'unregulated'; typical absence -> 'typical-absence'
    # Forbidden/mandatory evaluated only within scope; DQ/abstraction flags added by analyst rules
    if status == "forbidden":
        return "forbidden behavior (verify identity, then violation)"
    if status == "mandatory":
        return "missing mandatory (verify scope + abstraction)"
    if status == "typical":
        return "typical-pattern absence/presence (needs business reason)"
    return "unexpected but unregulated (benign by default)"

# 4. Example: enrich, don't replace, PM4Py output
semantic_findings = []
for d in diag_ocdfg.get("deviations", []):
    key = (d["otype"], d["src"], d["tgt"])

# Repeat for diag_otg, diag_etot; keep diag_* raw for audit.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **No frequencies/tolerances.** Risk: every 1-2 gap becomes dispute; analysts over-escalate rarity. Clarification: define review thresholds (e.g., investigate forbidden >0, mandatory miss >1% or >n cases) as **explicitly unspecified** guidance, not compliance rule, to separate monitoring from violation.
2. **N1 eventually vs directly-follows conflation.** Risk: false N1 violations when intermediates exist. Clarification: restate N1 as **mandatory** *eventually* with allowed intermediate vocabulary and max time bound; DFG conformance must use eventually-follows, not DFG adjacency.
3. **N2 intermediate vocabulary incompletely modeled.** Risk: Validate->Issue (72) misread as deviation; true bypasses hidden. Clarification: enumerate expected intermediates (Issue Invoice, fulfilment, review) as **typical** paths; keep N2 **typical**.
4. **N4 scope flag undefined.** Risk: cannot decide 2 missing Pack->Dispatch. Clarification: define `shipment_type=external` attribute as scope trigger; make N4 **mandatory** when flag=external, **explicitly unspecified** otherwise.
5. **E8/T3 unapplied-cash typing missing.** Risk: 1 unlinked payment perpetually deviant despite Finance legitimacy. Clarification: require `payment_type=unapplied` event attribute/type; E8 **mandatory** except when typed, T3 **typical** with same exception.
6. **Delivery-object vs carrier-attribute equivalence.** Risk: 2 dispatches judged noncompliant despite carrier reference. Clarification: state whether carrier attribute satisfies E6/T2 or Delivery object is **mandatory**; interim allow attribute as **typical** alternative with migration deadline.
7. **Cobirth/codeath lifecycle semantics.** Risk: 1 T6 cobirth and 7 codeaths uninterpretable; extractor defines compliance. Clarification: define birth/death derivation rules in norm appendix; keep T6 **forbidden** but scope to validated birth definition; declare Package-Delivery codeath **explicitly unspecified** (accepted) to prevent future false positives.
8. **Reopen/revalidate lifecycle (N6).** Risk: reopened Orders can close without re-validation. Clarification: add rule: Reopen must eventually hit Validate/Review before Close as **mandatory** or declare reuse of N1 as **typical**; currently **unspecified** leaves loop-hole.
