## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

I avoid classical natural direct/indirect effects as the primary framework because the flag is not a single manipulable cause; it activates a policy bundle and changes shared queue states. Natural effects would require cross-world contrasts such as “the case is flagged but the queue behaves as if it were unflagged.” Instead, I use controlled direct effects where a policy component can be fixed, interventional/stochastic effects for queue-wait mediators, and separable effects where the bundle can be split into distinct operational changes.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | P1 vs P0: suppress flag display but leave risk score and human information unchanged | case; all incoming cases eligible for flagging after flag introduction | total cycle time, `close - arrival` | total policy effect of flag suppression | Yes, if display suppression can be operationalized and randomized | clinicians may infer risk from score; shared-queue interference |
| E2 | set `priority_lane=1` vs `priority_lane=0`, holding downstream review/escalation policy fixed | case; cases arriving during normal triage operations | time from arrival to `triage_done` | controlled direct effect of priority triage on triage completion | Partially; priority lane must be a real manipulable resource | triage queue interference and resource assignment confounding |
| E3 | P0 vs P2: remove automatic mandatory manual review for flagged cases | case; flagged cases, especially medium/high complexity | total cycle time | interventional indirect effect through `manual_review` occurrence | Partially; requires a credible regime for review assignment | exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounding by queue state and case mix |

Interference arises because flagged cases enter shared review and escalation queues; one case’s flag changes waits for others, so case-level effects are not separable from flag prevalence. Exposure-induced confounding occurs because the flag changes priority triage and mandatory review, which alter queue state and subsequent routing; `manual_queue_wait` is a mediator but also affects later escalation and closing. Treatment-confounder feedback can occur if reviewers infer risk from priority status or triage speed and adjust review/escalation thresholds, and if delayed cases accumulate, changing congestion for later arrivals. Queue-capacity guardrail: protected capacity is capped at 20% of total review hours; if unflagged manual-review waiting exceeds its baseline 90th percentile by more than 25% for two consecutive days, the guardrail triggers reversion. def summarize_stage(subset: pd.DataFrame, name: str) -> None:
    n = len(subset)
    if n == 0:
        warnings.warn(f"Subgroup {name} is empty.")
        return

    if n < 5:
        warnings.warn(f"Subgroup {name} has only {n} cases; estimates are unstable.")

    stage_columns = {
        "arrival_to_triage_start": "arrival-to-triage-start",
        "triage_service": "triage service",
        "triage_done_to_manual_start": "triage-done-to-manual-start",
        "manual_service": "manual service",
        "manual_done_to_escalation_start": "manual-done-to-escalation-start",
        "escalation_service": "escalation service",
        "residual_to_close": "residual-to-close",
        "total_cycle": "total cycle",
    }

    row = {"subgroup": name, "n": n}
    for column, label in stage_columns.items():
        values = subset[column].dropna()
        if len(values) == 0:
            row[label] = "NA"
        else:
            if len(values) < 3:
                warnings.warn(f"Stage support for {label} in {name} is below 3.")
            row[label] = f"{values.median():.2f}h [{len(values)}]"

    print(row)

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. Hidden risk: if unobserved underlying risk is much higher among flagged cases and manual review/escalation prevents serious adverse decisions, then preserving or even expanding review may be preferable to reducing cycle time.

2. Queue interference: if protected flagged review capacity substantially increases unflagged waiting or creates system-wide congestion, the preferred policy would shift from P3 to separate capacity expansion or demand reduction.

3. Decision quality: if removing or shortening manual review increases false approvals, false rejections, rework, or appeals, then cycle-time gains would not justify P2-style review removal.

4. Timestamp semantics: if `manual_start` or `escalation_start` records queue entry, batch upload, or local-clock artifacts rather than actual work start, the waiting/service decomposition would be invalid.

5. Positivity or lack of overlap: if there are almost no unflagged cases with risk scores/complexity comparable to flagged escalated cases, threshold or matching estimates would be unsupported and policy inference would reverse to “unidentifiable.”

6. Behavior change by reviewers: if reviewers compensate for flag suppression, protected capacity, or review removal by escalating more, working slower, or re-reviewing cases, the expected cycle-time reduction could disappear or reverse.

## 9. Final synthesis

Directly observed: flagged cases receive much faster triage start and slightly shorter triage service, but they enter manual review almost universally, wait longer before review, often escalate, and have longer total cycle times. The stage table shows the delay is mainly triage-done-to-manual-start, manual-to-escalation-start, and escalation service, not triage. Plausible mechanism: the flag creates a policy bundle—priority triage plus mandatory review—that shifts cases into shared review and senior queues. Because flagged cases are also more complex and have higher recorded risk, the bundle increases downstream waiting and processing, producing longer total completion despite faster triage. Unidentified: the counterfactual cycle time and decision quality under alternative policies are not identified from these 20 cases. We cannot separate the effect of mandatory review occurrence from queue congestion, escalation selection, and unobserved underlying risk; interference means one case’s flag affects others’ waits. The most useful next evidence would come from the P3 pilot: randomized protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases, with queue guardrails and quality outcomes. It preserves review while testing whether manual-review waiting is the dominant mediator. If protected capacity reduces flagged cycle time without harming unflagged waits or decision quality, the delay is queue-capacity driven. If it merely shifts congestion or fails to reduce total time, the policy should move toward risk-stratified review removal or escalation-capacity changes.
