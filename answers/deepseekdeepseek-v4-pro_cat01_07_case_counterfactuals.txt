- **Factual mechanism:** D1 was chosen for handwriting expertise, but the long D1 queue forced the document check to start only on Tuesday morning, delaying the whole case.
- **Minimal intervention:** Reroute to D2 immediately when originals arrive.
- **Temporal propagation:** Originals arrive Mon17:30. D2’s queue at 16:00 is 5h; with roundtheclock work, D2 could start by 22:30. If D2 works overnight and takes 7h, the check finishes Tue05:30. Entering the Assessment queue at that time (A2 had 6h queue at Tue08:00, but earlier readiness may see a lower premorning queue), Assessment could start around 10:00–12:00 and finish by 14:00–16:00. Decision could then be reached Tuesday evening or early Wednesday, similar or slightly earlier than the factual Wednesday 08:50. If D2 requests clarification, a new documentassessment loop adds at least 1–2 days.
- **Processfeasibility check:** No compliance review is needed; originals were received before the document check. The sequence Register  Triage  Identity  Originals  Documents  Assessment  Decision  Notify is legal. No foureyes constraint applies.
- **Best/base/worst case:**  
  *Best*: D2 finishes in 4h, Assessment Tue afternoon, decision Tue evening  16h cycletime reduction.  
  *Base*: D2 takes 7h, Assessment Tue evening, decision Wed morning  negligible change (0–4h reduction).  
  *Worst*: D2 requests clarification; rework adds 24–48h and may require D1 anyway.
- **Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Data showing that D2’s error rate on handwritten documents is >40% and rework loops are exceptionally long would make the intervention unattractive.

### K-337
- **Factual mechanism:** Two successive rework loops consumed seven days, including a weekend.
- **Minimal intervention:** Suppress the second Request More Info and proceed to Compliance Review and Decision with the evidence available after Assessment2.
- **Temporal propagation:** Assessment2 finishes Fri10:30. Instead of waiting for an external response over the weekend, the case moves directly to C1. The C1 queue is forecast to be small by Friday (Thu 08:00 backlog 5h, clearing), so Compliance Review could start Friday afternoon and finish by 19:00. Decision by M1 could occur Friday evening or Monday morning, removing the weekend and the third document/assessment loop. Cycle time shrinks by roughly 3–5 days.
- **Processfeasibility check:** The intervention alters decision policy: the process no longer requires a full evidence set. The Compliance Review must still occur after the latest Assessment (Assessment2) and before Decision, which is satisfied. Foureyes holds (A1 and C1 differ). However, the normative rule that “Assessment may issue Request More Info” is bypassed by policy, not by process impossibility. This is a conscious policy override.
- **Best/base/worst case:**  
  *Best*: C1 finishes Fri, M1 approves with conditions, no later challenge  4day saving, acceptable residual risk.  
  *Base*: C1 raises concerns, requests extra internal analysis (not external request), adds 24h, decision Mon  3day saving.  
  *Worst*: Decision is rejected, appeal follows, total organisational cost exceeds the benefit of early closure.
- **Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If historical data show that cases closed with unresolved supplier inconsistencies have a >30% appeal rate and large financial reversals, the speed gain is not worth the quality loss.

### K-411
- **Factual mechanism:** The legacy system created only one verification task at a time, imposing a serial lock.
- **Minimal intervention:** Create both tasks at Triage and ensure true concurrency; use realtime timestamps.
- **Temporal propagation:** Triage finishes Tue09:35. I2 queue 2h  Identity starts 11:35. D2 queue 4h  Document starts 13:35. They run concurrently. Identity finishes 13:35, Document finishes 17:35–21:35 depending on duration (5–8h). The case is ready for Assessment by Tuesday night. A2’s queue is around 6h at 08:00, growing during the day; the case enters the queue and Assessment likely still starts Wednesday morning, perhaps at 09:00 instead of 09:30. The very modest shift (1–2h) arises because Assessment is the dominant bottleneck.
- **Processfeasibility check:** No compliance constraints. Identity and Document can be concurrent. The workaround must not violate queue ownership or timestamp integrity. The foureyes rule does not apply. All mandatory activities are present.
- **Best/base/worst case:**  
  *Best*: Document finishes early enough that Assessment starts at 08:00 Wed; decision at 13:00  1.5h saved.  
  *Base*: trivial gain (<1h).  
  *Worst*: Timestamp logging error flags an audit exception, causing rework or penalty.
- **Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If the legacy lock cannot be bypassed without destabilising the case management system, the intervention is infeasible.

**100case weekly rollout analysis**  

If each intervention is applied to 100 similar cases in a single week, interference becomes central.  

- **K-104 reservation**: Reserving a C1 slot for 100 cases would quickly saturate C1’s capacity. C1 can handle roughly 2–3 full reviews per day; 100 cases would require guaranteed windows that simply do not exist. The attempt would create a strict priority inversion: other highrisk cases would see their already long compliance queue (up to 20h) explode to several days, potentially breaching regulatory timelines. The benefit per case shrinks as slots are pushed later, and the systemic damage grows nonlinearly when compliance deadlines are missed. Reservation works for a handful of cases but collapses under scale.  

- **K-219 D2 substitution**: Moving 100 handwritten standard cases from D1 to D2 would rebalance queues, lowering D1’s backlog (likely by 30–40h over the week) and raising D2’s. Because D2 is slower and errorprone, the total work hours added (extra processing and rework) would increase the average documentcheck queue length, possibly by 15–25%. The Assessment queue would absorb the rework spikes, creating intermittent bottlenecks. The net effect is a shift in congestion rather than a clear systemwide speedup.  

- **K-337 rework suppression**: Avoiding a second external loop for 100 cases would dramatically reduce both documentcheck and assessment workloads, equivalent to removing hundreds of hours from D1 and A1. This could shorten the assessment queue by 20–30%, benefiting all cases. However, the risk of downstream appeals, compliance reinspections, or regulatory sanctions could later overwhelm the compliance and legal teams, creating a delayed bottleneck that is harder to manage. The systemwide effect is positive in the immediate week but uncertain beyond.  

- **K-411 concurrency workaround**: Applying true concurrency to 100 lowrisk cases would increase the simultaneous load on I2 and D2 by a small amount, but the real impact is on the Assessment queue. If all 100 cases become ready for assessment 1–3h earlier, the Assessment queue would receive a compressed arrival batch, potentially raising the maximum waiting time for all assessmentseeking cases by several hours during the middle of the week. The bottleneck migrates downstream to Assessment. The net systemwide cycletime change may be nil because the gain in the verification stage is absorbed by the fixed assessment capacity. Timestamp integrity at scale would require rigorous automation; manual workarounds could produce audit failures.

## 6. Pilot designs

The two highestranked interventions are **K-411** and **K-104**.

| pilot element | intervention 1: K-411 (parallel verification) | intervention 2: K-104 (early escalation + C1 reservation) |

## 7. Final synthesis

The intervention that appears **most robust** is K-411 (parallelisation of verification). It requires only a modest system workaround, offers a small but reliable speed improvement, and carries little risk of severe adverse effects. Its main value lies in proving that true concurrency is achievable without compromising audit integrity, and its learnings can be transferred to a broad class of standard cases.  

The **most dangerous** intervention, despite its appealing speed, is K-337 (suppressing the second rework loop). While it can cut cycle time by several days, it fundamentally changes the decision policy and exposes the organisation to unacceptable compliance risk, potential appeals, and loss of defensibility. A single unfavourable appeal could erase the gains of many faster cases.  

A **local counterfactual that is least transportable to systemwide policy** is K-104. Reserving a provisional C1 slot works only because C1 has some slack in the singlecase scenario; scaling this to dozens of highrisk cases would quickly saturate the sole compliance reviewer and create a priority inversion that delays other urgent cases past their deadlines. The intervention’s architecture relies on a unique resource that cannot be replicated, making it fragile under load.  

The most **valuable additional data** would be: (1) realtime queue lengths and resource utilisation logs (not just snapshot forecasts), to understand dynamic congestion; (2) actual servicetime distributions per resource and case type, especially D2 errorandrework probabilities; (3) external supplier response time distributions for requests for more information; and (4) complianceoutcome records linking evidentiary completeness to appeal rates and financial loss. These data would allow more reliable counterfactual modelling and safer pilot design.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. *Preliminary S1 compliance work reduces later C1 review duration by 1–3 hours.* — **temporal**  
2. *D2’s error rate on difficult handwriting is 25%, and rework loops add 24–48h.* — **quality**  
3. *Removing a rework loop does not cause the Compliance Review to extend its own duration.* — **behavioral**  
4. *A reserved C1 slot can be honoured within ±2hours; other urgent cases are not preempted by external mandates.* — **interference**  
5. *The system workaround for concurrent verification produces correct realtime timestamps without database inconsistencies.* — **structural**  
6. *Assessment queue waiting times are proportional to the amount of work ahead and are not subject to priority inversion from other case types.* — **resource**  
7. *Suppressing a Request More Info does not cause the decision maker (M1) to intentionally delay to compensate for lower confidence.* — **behavioral**  
8. *The snapshot queue forecasts are unbiased estimates of remaining work hours; no systematic under or overestimation.* — **temporal**
