## 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

### Queue QA

**Supporting cases for non-preemptive priority + soft appointment SLA:**
- **A04 (P1, arr 08:13):** Starts at 08:58, after calibration (08:52–08:58) ends. A02 (P3) and A03 (P3, appointed 08:20) are waiting. A04 priority jumps ahead.  Priority confirmed.
- **A08 (P1, arr 08:40):** Starts at 09:41. A05 (P3), A06 (P2), A07 (P3) are in queue. A08 (P1) is selected over P2 and P3.  Priority confirmed.
- **A03 appointment (08:20):** Arrives early (08:08) but queues; starts at 08:20 as appointed.  Appointment honored.

**Contradicting or ambiguous cases:**
- **A06 appointment (09:15):** Starts at 09:20, **5 minutes late**. A06 (P2) is appointed for 09:15 but selected at 09:20. Possible explanations: (a) soft SLA window ±10 min; (b) A08 (P1, arr 08:40) takes priority, delaying A06 slightly; (c) appointment is a preference, not a hard constraint. **Does not refute priority + soft appointment; soft SLA is consistent.**
- **A02 (P3, arr 08:04) vs. A03 (P3, appointed 08:20):** Both P3, but A03 appointment is honored despite A02 arrival-FIFO order. Suggests **appointment overrides FIFO within same priority tier**, or a wider appointment window (08:15–08:25) is applied.

**Diagnosis:** Non-preemptive priority is primary; appointments are soft constraints (±5–15 min window). No preemption observed. **Confidence 78%:** Small sample, no test of hard appointment or SPT tiebreaks.

### Queue QB

**Supporting cases for non-preemptive priority with P1 preemption:**
- **B02 (P1, arr 09:14) interrupts B01 (P3, mid-service 09:02–09:16):** Clear preemption. B02 is higher priority; interruption reason is "urgent arrival."  **P1 preemption confirmed.**
- **B05 (P1, arr 09:46) interrupts B01 (resumed, mid-service 09:31–10:00):** B01 is resumed after B02 completes; B05 (P1) arrival causes second interruption.  **P1 preemption confirmed again.**
- **B02 (P1) starts at 09:16 < 15 min est.** B02 is fast; estimated 15 min, actual 15 min (09:16–09:31). SPT could also explain this, but priority is the dominant signal.
- **B04 (P3, arr 09:35) waits until B05 (P1) is done (09:31–10:14)** even though B04 has been waiting.  Consistent with priority: P1 > P3.

**Contradicting or ambiguous cases:**
- **B01 second interruption (10:00) reason is "dispatcher reassignment," not urgent arrival.** This suggests discretionary reallocation, not algorithmic preemption. Could indicate resource balancing or manual override. **Does not refute priority; suggests hybrid with operational discretion.**
- **B03 (P2, arr 09:20) vs. B04 (P3, arr 09:35) at 09:48:** B03 is selected (started 09:48–10:00). B03 is higher priority (P2 > P3)  and earlier arrival , so FIFO, priority, and SPT all agree (B03 est 12 min < B04 est 8 min is a minor SPT reversal, but priority dominates).
- **B06 (P2, arr 10:05) does not preempt B05 (P1, mid-service 10:00–10:14).** Suggests preemption is reserved for P1 arrivals, not applied to all priority differences. **Non-preemptive priority for P2 vs. P3 is consistent.**
- **B07 (P3, appointed 10:30) starts at 10:56, 26 min late.** Note says "appointment not honored on time." This is the strongest contradiction: appointment is explicitly not honored. Possible explanations: (a) appointment SLA was missed due to queue backlog; (b) appointment is lower priority than queue priority (unlikely); (c) data quality / scan delay. **Does not refute priority; indicates appointment is secondary to priority.**

**Diagnosis:** Non-preemptive priority with **selective preemption for P1 arrivals**. Preemption is not fully automatic; "dispatcher reassignment" suggests human judgment. Appointments are secondary to priority. **Confidence 62%:** Preemption logic is opaque; B07 appointment violation and B01 second-stop reason suggest undocumented exceptions or operator discretion. SPT cannot be ruled out as a tiebreak within priority tiers (not enough evidence either way).

### Queue QC

**Supporting cases for deadline-aware scheduling:**
- **C05 (P1, arr 10:11, DL 10:50) starts at 10:54 despite C01 (P3, arr 10:00, much older) waiting 54 min.** C05 deadline is already 4 min past (10:50 was 4 min ago at 10:54). C01 deadline (13:00) has 6 min slack. **Deadline urgency (SLA risk) overrides FIFO and priority.**  Strong support for deadline-aware.
- **C05 note: "deadline already at risk when selected."** Explicit acknowledgment that deadline was the decision driver. 
- **C02 (8 min est) selected at 10:10 over C01 (36 min est) and C03 (14 min est).** Could reflect SPT (8 < 14 < 36). Both have high slack (13:00 and 14:00 deadlines, ~170 and 150 min away).  SPT tiebreak consistent with deadline-aware (no urgency yet).

**Contradicting or ambiguous cases:**
- **C06 (P3, arr 10:25, DL 15:00, 6 min est) starts at 11:34 ahead of C01 (P3, DL 13:00, 80 min slack) and C07 (P2, DL 12:00, -100 min past deadline).** Why not C07 (catastrophically overdue) or C01 (older, higher slack risk)? Possible explanations:
  1. **SPT:** C06 est 6 min < C01 est 36 min, << C07 est 18 min. SPT selects shortest job.  Consistent with SPT tiebreak.
  2. **Deadline logic reset:** C07 deadline already far missed; queue may have given up on SLA for C07, reverting to SPT or fairness.
  3. **Downstream scheduling:** C06 may unblock a downstream process.
  - **This episode is the strongest challenge to pure deadline-aware EDD (earliest deadline first).** It suggests **SPT as a secondary criterion** or **deadline thresholds** (only escalate if deadline is within X minutes, not already massively exceeded).

- **C07 (P2, DL 12:00) vs. C03 (P2, DL 12:30) at 10:18:** C03 is selected (10:18–10:32), C07 not yet arrived. At 10:18, C07 has not arrived (arr 10:40), so no contradiction.

- **C01 (oldest, P3) selected at 11:40 after C07 (P2, much overdue) is still pending.** At 11:40, the queue shifts from deadline-urgency to age/FIFO. Possible explanations:
  1. C07 is assigned elsewhere (not visible in queue).
  2. After deadline is catastrophically missed, operator prioritizes by arrival age (fairness).
  3. C01 and C07 are in separate workflows (C01 resource vs. C07 resource).
  - **Suggests hybrid with SLA-driven escalation that resets after deadline is far exceeded.**

**Diagnosis:** Deadline-aware scheduling with **SPT tiebreak** and **soft SLA thresholds**. Deadlines within ~10 min of miss trigger escalation; deadlines far exceeded (>100 min) are deprioritized in favor of other factors (SPT, age). **Confidence 71%:** The C06 episode and C01/C07 ordering challenge a pure EDD model; SPT secondary criterion is plausible but not definitive. If there is a separate resource for C07 or if C07 is pre-assigned, the apparent violation is an artifact.

### Queue QD

**Supporting cases for skill-based routing:**
- **D01 (mechanical)  D-M, D02 (firmware)  D-F, D03 (electrical)  D-F, D04 (mechanical)  D-M, D06 (electrical)  D-X, D07 (mechanical+electrical)  D-X, D08 (mechanical)  D-M, D09 (mechanical)  D-M, D10 (electrical)  D-X:** Every job is routed to a resource with matching skills. **No counterexample to skill routing.** 

- **D05 (firmware+electrical, P2) waits 73 min (11:07–12:20) for D-F availability.** D-F is the only resource with both firmware and electrical skills. Waiting time is explained entirely by resource unavailability, not priority or SPT.  **Strong support for skill-based routing.**

- **D06 (appointed 11:30, electrical) starts at 11:30 via D-X.** Appointment is honored; D-X is available at 11:20.  Appointment compliance consistent with skill + appointment SLA.

**Contradicting or ambiguous cases:**
- **D08 (P1, mechanical, 8 min est, arr 11:16) vs. D04 (P3, mechanical, 10 min est, arr 11:05) at 11:25:** D04 is selected (11:25–11:35), D08 waits until 11:35. D04 arrival is earlier (FIFO), but D08 is P1 (higher priority). Why doesn't D08 preempt D04? Possible explanations:
  1. **Non-preemptive priority:** Once D04 starts (11:25), it runs to completion. D08 must wait for the mechanical slot.
  2. **Priority is within-queue only:** If D04 is already mid-dispatch at 11:25, D08 is not in queue yet (arrival scan at 11:16, dispatch may be at 11:20–11:30).
  3. **SPT:** D08 (8 min) < D04 (10 min), but D04 is already running.
  - **No preemption of D04; consistent with non-preemptive priority. Does not refute skill-based routing.**

- **D07 (mechanical+electrical, P3, arr 11:14, waiting 34 min) starts at 11:48, while D05 (firmware+electrical, P2, arr 11:07, waiting 41 min) waits.** D05 is higher priority (P2 > P3) and older (arr earlier). Why not D05? Explanation: **D05 requires D-F (unavailable 12:00–12:20, in meeting); D07 is mechanical+electrical, routable to D-X (available at 11:48).** Dispatch chooses D07 because D-X is available now; D05 cannot start until D-F is available. **This is a skill-constrained decision, not a priority violation.**  Supports skill-based routing.

- **D10 (P1, electrical, arr 11:25, waiting 51 min) vs. D05 (P2, firmware+electrical, arr 11:07, waiting 69 min) at 12:16:** D10 starts (12:16–12:28), D05 waits. D10 is higher priority (P1)  and has less wait time. But D05 is constrained by D-F unavailability (until 12:20). At 12:16, D-F is still in meeting; D10 (electrical) is routable to D-X (available). **Again, skill constraint explains the choice.** 

**Diagnosis:** Queue QD is **entirely skill-based routing**. Within each skill pool (mechanical, electrical, firmware, or dual-skill), the order is **FIFO or FIFO-with-priority**, but skill eligibility is the dominant factor. Appointments are honored when a matching resource is available. **Confidence 72%:** SPT within a skill pool cannot be ruled out (e.g., D08 8 min < D04 10 min, but D04 is already running; D06 18 min < D05 30 min, but skill constraint intervenes). However, every observed ordering can be explained by skill + resource availability + appointment compliance + priority (as a tiebreak). The queue is the least ambiguous in structure but most opaque in internal priority/SPT tiebreaks because skill matching is the binding constraint.

## 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

### Test 1: Queue QB, B03 vs. B04 vs. B05 at 09:48

**Context:** B01 completes its second run at 09:48 (after B02 and B05 interruptions). Three jobs are now eligible:
- B03 (P2, arr 09:20, waiting 28 min, est 12 min)
- B04 (P3, arr 09:35, waiting 13 min, est 8 min)
- B05 (P1, arr 09:46, waiting 2 min, est 14 min)  **arrival scan timing is uncertain; arrival could be at 09:46 or delayed to 09:48–09:50**

**Observed:** B03 starts at 09:48–10:00.

**Competing hypotheses:**
1. **Priority (P1 > P2 > P3):** Predicts B05 (P1) > B03 (P2) > B04 (P3). B05 should start if it is in the eligible set at 09:48.
2. **FIFO:** Predicts B03 (arr 09:20, earliest) > B04 (arr 09:35) > B05 (arr 09:46).

**Observed choice (B03):** Consistent with FIFO and with priority if **B05 is not in the eligible set at 09:48** (arrival scan delay).

**Discriminating observable:**
- **Exact arrival scan timestamp for B05.** If scan is 09:46, B05 should be in the eligible set by 09:48 (2 min in queue). If scan is 09:48–09:50, B05 is not yet eligible.
- **Dispatcher logs or queue-entry time (distinct from arrival timestamp).** If queue-entry time is 09:48–09:50, arrival scan was delayed; this would explain why B03 (already in queue 28 min) is served instead of B05.
- **B05 service start time relationship to B03 completion time.** If B05 starts immediately after B03 (10:00–10:14), with no intervening FIFO-eligible jobs (B04), then priority (P1) was applied: B05 waits only 14 min (09:46 to 10:00) while B04 waits 53 min (09:35 to 10:28). This **supports priority interpretation** and suggests B05 was in the queue at 09:48 but not served until B03 was done, consistent with **non-preemptive priority within ongoing job** or **FIFO + arrival scan delay**.

**Additional test:** If **B05 arrival scan was at 09:46 and delay is <2 min**, then B05 is in queue at 09:48. Under priority, B05 should start immediately (preemption) or at least preempt the next dispatch (non-preemptive priority). Since B03 is served from 09:48–10:00, either (a) B05 is not in queue (scan delayed), or (b) non-preemptive priority applies and B03 was already selected/dispatched before B05 arrived. **A 1-minute resolution log of queue-entry and dispatch-decision times would resolve this.**

### Test 2: Queue QC, C06 vs. C07 at 11:34

**Context:** C05 (P1) completes at 11:34. Three jobs are waiting:
- C01 (P3, arr 10:00, waiting 94 min, est 36 min, DL 13:00, 86 min slack)
- C06 (P3, arr 10:25, waiting 69 min, est 6 min, DL 15:00, 256 min slack)
- C07 (P2, arr 10:40, waiting 54 min, est 18 min, DL 12:00, **-94 min deadline exceeded**)

**Observed:** C06 starts at 11:34–11:40 (6 min).

**Competing hypotheses:**
1. **Deadline-aware (EDD or deadline-urgency score):** Predicts highest-urgency job: C07 (deadline exceeded by 94 min) > C01 (deadline has 86 min slack) > C06 (deadline has 256 min slack). **Predicts C07.**
2. **SPT (shortest processing time):** Predicts C06 (6 min) > C07 (18 min) > C01 (36 min). **Predicts C06  (observed).**
3. **Priority (P2 > P3):** Predicts C07 (P2) > C06, C01 (P3). **Predicts C07.**
4. **Deadline-aware with SPT tiebreak (when no urgent deadline):** Predicts C06 (low urgency, but shortest job). **Predicts C06  (observed).**

**Observed choice (C06):** Consistent with SPT and with deadline-aware-SPT-tiebreak (if C07 deadline is considered "already lost" and no longer escalates).

**Discriminating observables:**

- **Scenario replay with different est values.** If all three jobs had the same est (e.g., 20 min each), and C06 was still chosen, it would **refute SPT** and **suggest deadline-threshold logic.** Current data shows C06 est is much shorter (6 min), conflating SPT and deadline-aware effects.

**Alternative test:** Change C06 est from 6 min to 36 min (hypothetically), holding all else equal. Under SPT, C06 and C01 would now be equivalent; would C07 (P2, deadline exceeded) be chosen instead? If **no**, SPT is the primary rule, not deadline-aware. If **yes**, deadline-aware is primary.

## 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

### Exception 1: **Delayed Arrival Scans (documented possibility)**
**Plausibility: High (85%).**

**Mechanism:** Timestamps in the data are "accurate to one minute," but arrival scans can be delayed by up to two minutes. This means the recorded `arrival` timestamp may be 2 min later than actual physical arrival.

**Affected episodes:**
- **QB: B05 (arr 09:46).** If actual arrival is 09:44–09:46, scan delay could be 2 min. At dispatch decision time (09:48), B05 might not yet be in the queue. This would explain why B03 (FIFO, 28 min wait) is served instead of B05 (P1). **Eliminates apparent priority violation.**
- **QB: B02 (arr 09:14).** If scan is delayed 2 min, actual arrival could be 09:12. Dispatch decision at 09:16 would still see B02 as just-arrived, consistent with preemption priority.

**Implications:**
- FIFO-based analysis is unreliable without queue-entry timestamps.
- Apparent priority inversions within ±2 min of arrival windows may be artifacts.
- **Recommendation:** Collect dispatch-decision timestamp and queue-entry timestamp (distinct from arrival scan timestamp) for all jobs.

**Alters diagnosis:** Would reduce confidence in QB preemption model (from 62% to ~50%) unless queue-entry times confirm B02 and B05 are in-queue at decision times. Strengthens FIFO hypothesis for QB if B05 queue-entry is 09:48.

---

### Exception 2: **Strategic or Noisy Estimate Values (est_min)**
**Plausibility: Medium-high (70%).**

**Mechanism:** `est_min` visible to dispatch at arrival time is the *estimate*, not the realized duration. Estimates may be:
- Systematically biased (e.g., technician bias: "this always takes 20 min" even when it usually takes 10–30 min).
- Strategically inflated or deflated (operator incentives to hide or highlight job complexity).
- Conditional on resource (e.g., same job est differs if assigned to resource A vs. B).

**Examples from data:**
- **QA: A02 (est 20 min, actual 20 min).** Exact match; low noise.
- **QB: B01 (est 45 min, actual 39 min net, but interrupted twice).** If interruptions are excluded, net service time is 14 + 17 + 14 = 45 min. Estimate matches total service time, not continuous run.
- **QC: C01 (est 36 min, actual 36 min).** Exact match.
- **QC: C05 (est 40 min, actual 40 min).** Exact match.
- **QD: All estimates match actual service time within ±1 min.** Suspiciously accurate. Possible: estimates are pre-recorded; or post-hoc estimates are inserted after service completion.

**Implications:**
- SPT rule (shortest processing time based on est) could be confounded with post-hoc estimate insertion if est_min is recorded after service.
- If estimates are strategic, SPT could be a red herring; actual rule is priority or appointment, and estimates are rationalization.
- **Recommendation:** Clarify whether est_min is (a) recorded at arrival, before service; (b) inserted post-service based on actual time; (c) clerk entry subject to bias.

**Alters diagnosis:**
- **QD:** If all estimates are post-hoc, SPT cannot be tested. Observed order is then purely skill + resource availability + priority. Confidence in skill-based routing remains high (unchanged).
- **QC:** If estimates are strategic, SPT tiebreak at C06 is questionable. Deadline-aware + falling-back-to-FIFO-when-overdue is more robust hypothesis. Confidence drops from 71% to ~55%.

---

### Exception 3: **Undocumented Resource Constraints and Cross-Queue Allocation**
**Plausibility: Medium (60%).**

**Mechanism:** Resource availability may be constrained by unrecorded tasks (meetings, maintenance, break assignments) or cross-queue duties. C1 (QC resource) is documented as having "prior job ends 10:10"; D-M (QD mechanical) is available 11:00–12:30. It is possible that C1 and D-M are shared across queues or reassigned between jobs in ways not visible in the queue data.

**Examples:**
- **QC: C01 is executed "last among initial backlog" (11:40 start) despite being the oldest job (arr 10:00).** Could C1 be assigned to QD or another queue until 11:40? If so, C01 is not in QC queue during 10:00–11:40; apparent age-FIFO violation is an artifact.
- **QD: D05 waits 73 min for D-F because D-F is in a meeting 12:00–12:20 and handling firmware jobs.** If D05 could be routed to another resource (cross-training), wait time might be reduced. Absence of cross-training is an operational choice, not a queue-discipline choice.

**Implications:**
- Queue order is constrained by resource state, which is partially hidden.
- Single-queue analysis is insufficient; cross-queue resource contention is invisible.
- **Recommendation:** Collect resource log (activities, assignments, availability changes) for the observation window.

**Alters diagnosis:**
- **QC:** Confidence in deadline-aware drops to ~50% if C01 out-of-queue assignments are possible.
- **QD:** Skill-based routing confidence is unaffected; it correctly identifies skill as the binding constraint. Confidence remains 72%.

---

### Exception 4: **Undocumented SLA Thresholds and Mode Switches**
**Plausibility: Medium (65%).**

**Mechanism:** Dispatch rules may include thresholds that trigger policy changes. For example:
- **SLA urgency escalation:** If deadline is within 10 min, escalate to P1 or expedite.
- **Age fairness escalation:** If a job waits >60 min, bypass priority and serve oldest-first.
- **Resource utilization fallback:** If resource is underutilized, apply FIFO; if congested, apply priority.

**Examples:**
- **QC: C05 (P1, deadline -4 min) is served immediately, even though C01 is older.** Threshold: deadline within ±5 min triggers escalation to top-of-queue.
- **QC: C01 (waiting 94 min) starts at 11:40 (C06 then done), despite C07 (waiting 54 min, P2) also waiting.** Threshold: after job waits 90+ min, bypass priority and serve oldest. **Mode switch at t=11:34.**
- **QB: B03 (P2) is served at 09:48 without preempting B01, yet B02 (P1) and B05 (P1) preempted earlier.** Threshold: only P1 preempts; P2 and P3 are non-preemptive.

**Implications:**
- Rules are more complex than single-mode discipline.
- Behavior is locally consistent (within mode) but globally hybrid.
- **Recommendation:** Collect mode-switch logs, SLA urgency scores, wait-time thresholds, and policy version numbers.

**Alters diagnosis:**
- **QA:** Confidence remains 78% (soft appointment SLA is consistent with threshold behavior).
- **QB:** Confidence drops slightly (62%  58%) if preemption threshold for P2 is explicitly recorded as "P1 only."
- **QC:** Confidence rises (71%  75%) if deadline-threshold logic and age-fairness fallback are documented. This would explain C05, C06, and C01 episodes coherently.
- **QD:** Unaffected; skill-based routing is orthogonal to SLA thresholds. Confidence 72%  72%.

---

### Exception 5: **Data Quality: Interruption Reasons and Preemption Logic**
**Plausibility: Medium (55%).**

**Mechanism:** Interruption reasons (e.g., "urgent arrival," "dispatcher reassignment") are free-text notes, not structured codes. They may be incomplete, post-hoc rationalizations, or operator annotations with low reliability. Absence of an interruption reason does not prove no preemption occurred; operational reasons could be undocumented.

**Examples:**
- **QB: B01 first stop reason is "urgent arrival."** Reliable indicator of B02 (P1) preemption.
- **QB: B01 second stop reason is "dispatcher reassignment."** Vague; could mean B05 (P1) preemption, or manual intervention, or resource rebalancing. Preemption vs. operational choice is ambiguous.
- **QA: No interruption reasons recorded.** A01, A02, A03, etc. run to completion without notes. If A04 (P1) causes an interruption of an ongoing job, it would be documented. Since no interruptions are documented, preemption is not applied in QA. **However, absence of annotation does not prove absence of preemption; it could indicate missing data or operator inconsistency in recording.**

**Implications:**
- Preemption is inferred from interruption reasons, but annotation quality is low.
- Apparent non-preemption could mask undocumented preemptions.
- **Recommendation:** Collect structured preemption flags: (a) was job interrupted? (yes/no); (b) what job interrupted it? (job ID); (c) what was the reason code? (priority, resource rebalance, external event, etc.).

**Alters diagnosis:**
- **QA:** Confidence in non-preemptive priority rises (78%  82%) if interruption-reason field is reliably populated and all runs show zero interruptions.
- **QB:** Confidence in preemption mechanism drops slightly (62%  60%) if "dispatcher reassignment" is ambiguous and could indicate non-algorithmic intervention.

### Cross-Queue Comparison

#### Which Queue is Most Identifiable?

**QA** is the simplest and most identifiable:
- **Reason:** Small arrival rate (8 jobs in 2 hours, low congestion), single resource (A1), no skill constraints, no interruptions documented. The priority discipline is clearly visible in A04 (P1) jumping ahead of A02/A03 (P3).
- **Confidence: 78%.**
- **Risk:** Small sample size means rare events (e.g., P2 vs. P3 preemption) are not observed.

---

#### Which Queue is Least Identifiable?

**QD** is the hardest to disambiguate *internally*, but the most robust *overall*:
- **Reason:** Skill-based routing is so dominant that any priority or SPT logic is masked by resource availability. Three skills (mechanical, firmware, electrical) and three resources (D-M, D-F, D-X) with overlapping skills create a combinatorial space. D05 (P2, 73 min wait) is explained entirely by D-F unavailability, not a priority inversion.
- **Confidence: 72%** (still high because skill routing is unambiguous; lower precision on within-skill tiebreaks).
- **Risk:** If undocumented skill mismatches or cross-queue resource sharing exist, the queue-level analysis is fundamentally flawed.

**QB** is the next-hardest:
- **Reason:** Preemption logic is opaque. B01's second interruption is labeled "dispatcher reassignment," which could indicate (a) non-algorithmic manual override, (b) resource rebalancing, or (c) hidden priority escalation. Arrival-scan delays (up to 2 min) could mean B05 is not in queue at 09:48 decision time.
- **Confidence: 62%** (lowest among four queues).
- **Risk:** If preemption is not algorithmic but operator-driven, the queue has no fixed discipline; it is effectively random or opaque.

**QC** is intermediate-to-hard:
- **Reason:** Deadline-aware hypothesis is plausible but confounded by SPT (C06, 6 min, chosen over C07, 18 min). The late-queue behavior (C01 chosen last despite being oldest) suggests a mode switch or fallback rule, but this rule is not documented.
- **Confidence: 71%.**
- **Risk:** If C07 is pre-assigned to another queue at 11:34, apparent "priority inversion" is an artifact.

---

### Which Claimed Policy Would Be Most Dangerous to Infer?

**QB's preemptive priority, if inferred from these observations alone, is DANGEROUS.**

**Reason:** 
1. **Preemption appears selective (P1 only).** If management assumes all priority levels can preempt, deployment of a new P2 job could unexpectedly preempt lower-priority work, violating SLAs for customers who expect non-preemption.
2. **Arrival-scan delays are not controlled for.** B05 (P1) apparent preemption at 09:48 could be entirely due to a 2-min arrival scan delay. If the real rule is FIFO (no preemption at all) and we deploy a preemptive system, we would introduce artificial task switches that break real-time constraints.
3. **"Dispatcher reassignment" is ambiguous.** If B01's second stop is manual intervention, not algorithmic preemption, then inferring a preemptive-priority rule and automating it would bypass operator oversight and potentially cause worse outcomes (e.g., losing context on a half-completed job).

**Safer inference:** QB is better characterized as **"FIFO with occasional high-priority expedites, applied manually by dispatcher."** This acknowledges human judgment and avoids over-automation of ambiguous preemption logic.

---

### QC's deadline-aware rule is next-most-risky.

**Reason:**
1. **SPT tiebreak is confounded.** If C06 (6 min) is selected at 11:34 not because SPT is the tiebreak, but because C06 happens to be short and C07 is already known to be unrecoverable (deadline 100+ min overdue), then implementing "deadline-aware with SPT tiebreak" would systemically deprioritize rescue operations for catastrophic deadline misses.
2. **Mode switch (age fairness after overdue) is undocumented.** If we assume deadline-aware throughout, C01 (oldest job at 11:40) would be selected under age fairness, but if we only implement deadline-aware without the fallback, C01 might be deferred.

**Safer inference:** QC is better characterized as **"deadline-urgent jobs are expedited, but once a deadline is far exceeded (>60 min), the job is deprioritized in favor of fairness (FIFO by arrival age)."** This is more conservative and aligns observed behavior to a coherent policy.

---

### QA and QD Are Relatively Safe.

**QA (non-preemptive priority + soft appointment SLA)** is straightforward and low-risk. Priority is clearly visible. Appointment SLA is soft (±5–15 min window), which is reasonable and unlikely to cause surprises.

**QD (skill-based routing)** is the safest because skill constraints are hard physical facts (a technician has specific certifications), not algorithmic choices. Inferring "QD uses skill-based routing" is nearly impossible to get wrong; it is a constraint, not a preference.

---

### Final Recommendation

1. **Automate QA and QD confidently** (78% and 72% confidence). Their rules are clear and operationally sound.
2. **QC needs clarification before automation.** Collect deadline-urgency scores and mode-switch logs (Priority 1–3 data).
3. **QB should not be automated without caution.** The preemption logic is ambiguous and manual. Implement dispatcher-assist tools rather than full automation.
4. **For all queues, collect dispatch-decision logs and resource-state logs (Priority 1–2 data).** This will immediately disambiguate FIFO vs. priority, arrival-scan effects, and resource constraints.
