## 1. Materiality Framework

**Purpose.** Alignment output tells us *where the observed trace and the normative model disagree and how many edits reconcile them*. Business severity asks a different question: *if this narrative is true, how much does it hurt, can it be undone, and does it break a control that exists for a reason?* The framework below scores the second question and keeps the technical cost as a separate, descriptive attribute.

**Severity dimensions (scored 1–5, combined by judgment, not by a weighted sum):**

| Dimension | What it asks | How it moves the score |
|---|---|---|
| Control objective | Which control failed and what harm does that control exist to prevent? Fraud-prevention and payment-authorization controls (P1–P3) dominate; communication controls (P4) are secondary; telemetry hygiene (P5) is not a control at all. | A breached fraud/authorization control starts at 4 regardless of amount; a breached communication control starts at 2; no breached control caps at 1–2. |
| Amount / exposure | Money already out of the door or at risk, plus indirect exposure (regulatory fine, legal). | Scales within the band set by the control objective. EUR 480k with no fraud screen is worse than EUR 90k with no fraud screen, but both are in the top band. |
| Reversibility | Is the money recoverable? Payment to a newly changed beneficiary account is the least recoverable outcome in this dataset. | Irreversible outcomes lift the score; missing evidence for a payment that is still recoverable or was never made lowers it. |
| Timing | Did an event happen in the wrong order (payment before approval, P3), or did a fraud indicator fire immediately before payment (bank change one hour before payment)? | Sequence violations of authorization gates are treated as severe even when the "right" events all exist. |
| Customer / regulatory harm | Was the claimant informed within 24h (P4)? Was a legitimate claimant denied or delayed? Is there a reporting obligation? | Moderate lift; can become high if a regulator requires demonstrable notification evidence for large claims. |
| Recurrence | Is this a one-off or part of a pattern (74 retry duplicates, 9 adjuster gaps, 4 zero-cost auto-approve violations)? | Recurrence raises priority of the *pattern* even when each instance is low; for control failures it also indicates a systemic design flaw rather than human error. |
| Evidence reliability | Is the evidence a system-generated immutable event, an external signed document with a stable identifier, a mutable free-text link, or a verbal statement? | Affects *confidence*, and indirectly severity: an unverifiable claim that a control was performed is treated as "control not evidenced", which is a failure of the control's audit objective even if the check actually happened. |
| Model scope | Was the case ever meant to be measured against this model (P6: denied leads, withdrawals)? | Out-of-scope cases get low severity for the *deviation* but generate a governance action (fix scope or fix model). |

**Deviation taxonomy used throughout:**
1. *Process-model mismatch* – the model does not describe a legitimate real-world path (denial, withdrawal, silent routing).
2. *Event-label mismatch* – the activity happened but under a different name (`Assess Damage`).
3. *Missing evidence* – the activity probably happened, but the core log has no event (external adjuster report, CRM notification, verbal fraud check). Sub-graded by how good the external evidence is.
4. *Actual operational deviation* – the sequence or occurrence of business activities genuinely violated the process (payment before approval).
5. *Data-aware policy failure* – control flow fits the model perfectly but the branch taken was not permitted for this case's data (amount, risk, bank change).
6. *Harmless technical noise* – telemetry, retries, duplicates, transport artefacts.

**Severity vs confidence.** *Severity* = the impact if the business narrative is true. *Confidence* = how sure we are that the narrative is the correct reading of the evidence. They are independent axes:
- CL-604 is high severity and high confidence: the bank's acceptance timestamp is external, immutable evidence that payment preceded approval.
- CL-602 is high severity with *split* confidence: we are highly confident the fraud-screen control is *not evidenced*; we have low confidence about whether an informal check actually happened. Because the control's purpose includes auditability, low confidence in the check's occurrence does *not* reduce severity — it increases the urgency of investigation.
- CL-606 is low severity with medium-high confidence: a signed adjuster report makes it likely the assessment occurred; the residual doubt is about identifier stability.
- CL-608 is low-moderate severity with medium confidence: the CRM message exists, but a mutable text link means the correlation could be wrong or altered.
A low-confidence, high-severity item is an *investigation priority*; a high-confidence, low-severity item is a *hygiene priority*; neither is determined by raw cost.

**Zero-cost policy failures explained.** CL-605, CL-609 and CL-610 all have cost 0 and are indistinguishable to the alignment. CL-605 is genuinely compliant. CL-609 and CL-610 took a branch the process tree allows but the business policy forbids for their data. The alignment is computed on control flow only; the guards in P1/P2 (amount, risk, bank-change recency) are not encoded in the tree, so a forbidden branch is "fitting". The pattern evidence says four such cases exist in the month; they are the most dangerous items in the dataset precisely because standard conformance reporting will never surface them.

**Silent moves.** The `T(tau,c=0)` in CL-605 is a Petri-net routing transition created by converting the XOR operator. It is fitting, carries no business meaning, and must not be counted, reported, or investigated.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

Missing events are never treated as proof that an activity did not occur; each test below is designed to discriminate between "did not happen", "happened but not logged", and "happened but logged elsewhere".

**1. CL-602 — Fraud screen missing on a EUR 480k HIGH-risk claim with a fresh bank change.**
- H1: No fraud check occurred; the approver bypassed the control under pressure.
- H2: A fraud analyst performed a check informally (phone/email) and never recorded it.
- H3: The fraud tool logged the check, but its events are not fed into the core log (integration gap).
- H4: The bank change and payment are part of a deliberate diversion involving social engineering or an insider.
- Discriminating evidence: fraud-tool database for a case record or query on this claim; fraud-team phone/email logs around the approval time; who changed the bank account, via which channel, and from which IP/device; whether the claimant, contacted through a pre-existing independent channel, confirms the new account; approver's access logs and workload that day; whether Manual Approve is technically possible without a Fraud Screen record (test in UAT). If H3, the pattern will show many `M(Fraud Screen)` on manually approved cases; if H1/H2, only this approver or this period.

**2. CL-604 — Payment accepted by the bank 17 minutes before approval was recorded.**
- H1: The payment system has no dependency on approval status; anyone with payment rights can disburse.
- H2: An "urgent/manual payment" override exists and was used deliberately.
- H3: Approval was given verbally at ~10:00 and recorded at 10:20 (recording lag; still a P3 evidence breach, but a different remediation).
- H4: Clock skew between systems.
- Discriminating evidence: payment-system audit trail (initiator, channel, override flags); approval record metadata (created-at vs approved-at, whether the approver's session was active at 10:00); a month-wide query for pay-timestamp < approval-timestamp (if H1, expect a steady stream; if H2, a few flagged cases; if H3, small lags concentrated on certain approvers); NTP/clock logs of the core system against the bank's acknowledgement — H4 is unlikely because the bank timestamp is external, but a check costs little.

**3. CL-606 and pattern B — `M(Assess Loss)` with an external adjuster report.**
- H1: The assessment was fully performed by the licensed adjuster; only the event is missing (logging gap).
- H2: The report exists but belongs to a different claim or was attached later (correlation error) — relevant for the two cases without stable identifiers.
- H3: The report was signed but the loss figure used for payment differs from it (assessment overridden without trace).
- Discriminating evidence: report's claim identifier and its immutability (hash, DMS version history); comparison of the report's loss figure with the paid amount; report timestamp vs Fraud Screen timestamp (it must precede); adjuster licence registry check; for the two unlinked cases, whether any other claim references the same document.

**4. CL-608 and pattern H — Notification absent in the core log, present in CRM via a mutable link.**
- H1: The claimant was notified via the secure portal at +3h; the CRM link is correct.
- H2: The CRM entry refers to a different claim or was edited to point to this one.
- H3: No notification was actually delivered (portal message drafted, not sent, or bounced).
- Discriminating evidence: CRM field audit history (was the link ever edited?); the message body (claim number, amount, recipient) against the claim; portal delivery/read receipts; claimant login after +3h; whether other large claims show the same pattern (systemic channel, supporting H1).

**5. CL-609 / CL-610 and pattern E — Zero-cost auto-approvals that violate P1/P2.**
- H1: The rule engine's thresholds/conditions are misconfigured (e.g., 5k threshold not applied, bank-change lookback not implemented).
- H2: The rule engine is correct but consumed stale or missing data at decision time (risk score computed later; bank change replicated to the decision store with a delay > 1 hour).
- H3: A manual override was labelled `Auto Approve`.
- H4 (CL-610 specifically): Account takeover or insider changed the bank account and the claim was pushed through the automated path deliberately.
- Discriminating evidence: rule-engine configuration history and its decision log for these claims (inputs seen, rule fired); timestamps of risk-score computation and bank-change replication vs approval; user ID attached to the `Auto Approve` event (system account vs human); the bank-change audit trail (who, channel, device, IP); independent claimant confirmation; whether the four flagged cases share a rule version, a time window, a user, or a beneficiary account.

**6. CL-603 and pattern A — Duplicate `Notify Claimant`.**
- H1: Connector retried and re-logged a single message (duplicate event, one delivery).
- H2: Two real notifications were sent (customer received the message twice).
- H3: The duplicate is a genuinely different message that reused a message ID (ID generation bug).
- Discriminating evidence: transport log retry flags and delivery acknowledgements (one or two deliveries?); message payload comparison; the pattern being confined to one connector (supports H1); customer complaints about duplicate messages.

**7. CL-611 and pattern C — `Assess Damage` vs `Assess Loss`.**
- H1: Pure vocabulary difference; the same UI step writes `Assess Damage` to the log.
- H2: `Assess Damage` is a distinct, narrower step (e.g., property damage only) and the full loss assessment was not done.
- H3: The mapping table was changed after the fact to make the case conform (governance risk).
- Discriminating evidence: UI-to-event mapping in the core system's configuration; screen definitions; whether any case has both `Assess Damage` and `Assess Loss`; mapping-table change log (who, when, why) and whether conformance results were re-run afterward.

**8. CL-607 / CL-612 and pattern D — Denials and withdrawals.**
- H1: Legitimate terminations outside the model's scope.
- H2: A denial or withdrawal is being used to close a claim that should have been paid (customer harm) or to suppress a claim under investigation.
- Discriminating evidence: denial reason codes against coverage terms; complaint and appeal records; whether the same user denies/withdraws disproportionately; whether withdrawals cluster after assessment (as in CL-612) which might indicate claimants being discouraged; whether claimants received acknowledgement (P4-in-spirit).

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Alignment cost is the minimum total edit weight required to reconcile an observed trace with a model run. Every element of that definition — *minimum*, *edit weight*, *observed trace*, *model* — is a modelling choice, and each choice decouples the number from business risk.

**Cost-function design.** The supplied cost function assigns 1 to every visible move regardless of which activity is involved. So `L(Autosave)` costs the same as `L(Pay Claim)`, and `M(Fraud Screen)` on a EUR 480k claim (CL-602) costs the same as a duplicated notification (CL-603). CL-601 accumulates cost 3 from three screen-session events and ties with CL-604, in which EUR 90,000 was paid before anyone approved it. A cost function *could* be weighted, but weights are still attached to activity labels, not to the amount, the risk class, or the recency of a bank change — so even a good weighting cannot separate CL-605 from CL-610.

**Optimality and alternative alignments.** The alignment is *an* optimal solution, not *the* explanation. For CL-604 the same uniform cost function admits an alignment of cost 2 (treat the observed `Manual Approve` as a log move and insert a model `Auto Approve` before the observed `Pay Claim`), which is cheaper than the supplied cost-3 alignment and describes a completely different story: an over-approved auto claim instead of a payment released before approval. The bank timestamp tells us which story is true; the optimizer does not know. Treating alignments as authoritative diagnostic inputs, as instructed, is a practical convention; it is not a claim that the cheapest edit is the truest narrative.

**Scope.** CL-607 (denied) and CL-612 (withdrawn) carry the highest costs in the table (4 each) because the model has no legitimate way to end without payment, so it "demands" `Assess Loss`, `Auto Approve` and `Pay Claim`. Three of the four cost units in each case are model moves for activities that would have been *wrong* to perform. High cost here measures the model's incompleteness (P6), not the case's misbehaviour. The month-level evidence confirms that denials and withdrawals dominate the cost ≥ 4 population; an analyst sorting by cost would spend most of their time on the least risky cases.

**Hidden moves.** CL-605 contains a `T(tau,c=0)` produced by converting the XOR in the process tree into Petri-net routing. It is a modelling artefact with no business referent; it is fitting, and reporting it would be a false positive. Zero cost is correct for CL-605 — but zero cost is also the result for CL-609 and CL-610, which shows that "fitting" is a statement about control flow, not about compliance.

**Data conditions.** P1 and P2 are guards on branches: the Auto Approve branch is permitted only if amount ≤ 5k, risk is LOW and no bank change occurred within seven days. The process tree encodes the branch but not the guard. CL-609 (EUR 18k, MEDIUM) and CL-610 (bank change one hour before payment) therefore align perfectly with cost 0 while breaching the very controls the model was built to enforce. Four such cases exist in the month. These are the highest-risk items an ordinary conformance report will never show; only a data-aware check (evaluate the guard per case) or a data-Petri-net can surface them.

**Activity granularity and labels.** CL-611 pays cost 2 because the same real-world assessment was written as `Assess Damage` in the system and `Assess Loss` in the procedure; a naming difference is double-counted as one extra and one missing activity. In CL-606, the assessment was performed at a different granularity of the organisation — by an external adjuster whose work product is a signed document, not an event — and the alignment reports `M(Assess Loss)` exactly as it would if no assessment had happened. The alignment sees the log; the business happened partly outside it.

**Duplicated telemetry.** CL-603's second `Notify Claimant` shares a message ID with the first and is accompanied by a transport retry; 74 cases in the month show the same signature on one connector. It is a logging artefact of a non-idempotent connector. Its cost of 1 is the same as CL-602's missing fraud screen; a monthly KPI that sums costs would attribute 74 units of "non-conformance" to a connector and 1 to a possible six-figure fraud.

**Missing-but-external evidence.** A model move means "no matching event in the log", not "the activity did not occur". CL-606 (signed report), CL-608 (CRM message at +3h) and CL-602 (verbal claim) all have `M(...)` at cost 1, but their evidential situations range from strong-documentary to weak-mutable to none. Severity and confidence must be assessed against the external evidence and its reliability; the alignment cannot express that gradient.

**Irreversibility.** The cost function has no notion of time or reversibility. CL-604 is scored as "one misplaced event" and CL-610 as "nothing wrong", but in both the money has left the company — in CL-610 to an account changed an hour earlier, which is the least recoverable outcome imaginable. Conversely, CL-612's cost 4 relates to a payment that was never made and therefore carries zero financial exposure. Risk lives in the direction of the deviation (money out vs money not out), in the counterparty, and in the clock — none of which enter an edit distance.

**Net effect on this dataset.** Ordering by raw cost puts CL-607 and CL-612 first and CL-609/CL-610 last; ordering by business severity puts CL-602, CL-604 and CL-610 first and CL-607/CL-612 near the bottom. The rank correlation between the two is negative. Alignment cost is indispensable for *locating* and *describing* deviations; translating them into risk requires the policy overlay, the case data, the external evidence and the reversibility of the outcome — which is what Sections 1–5 do.

## 7. PM4Py Reproduction and Aggregation Code

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# 1. Normative model
TREE = ("-> ( 'Register Claim', 'Validate Coverage', 'Assess Loss', "
        "X ( -> ( 'Fraud Screen', 'Manual Approve' ), 'Auto Approve' ), 'Pay Claim', 'Notify Claimant' )")
tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(TREE)

# 2. Accepting Petri net (the conversion introduces silent/tau routing transitions, cf. CL-605)
net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)

# 3. Event log = the observed (log-side) events of each supplied alignment: S(...) and L(...) moves only.
#    Built as a formatted dataframe so that case IDs are controlled.
traces = {
    "CL-601": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Open Workbench", "Assess Loss", "Autosave",
               "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant", "Close Workbench"],
    "CL-602": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Manual Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-603": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim",
               "Notify Claimant", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-604": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Pay Claim", "Manual Approve", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-605": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-606": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Fraud Screen", "Manual Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-607": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Deny Claim", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-608": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Fraud Screen", "Manual Approve", "Pay Claim"],
    "CL-609": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-610": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-611": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Damage", "Fraud Screen", "Manual Approve",
               "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-612": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Withdraw Claim"],
}
rows = []
for case, acts in traces.items():
    for i, a in enumerate(acts):
        rows.append({"case_id": case, "activity": a,
                     "timestamp": pd.Timestamp("2024-01-01 08:00:00") + pd.Timedelta(minutes=i)})
log_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(pd.DataFrame(rows), case_id="case_id",
                                activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="timestamp")
# Equivalent alternative: pm4py.parse_event_log_string([",".join(a) for a in traces.values()], sep=",")

# 4. Alignments
aligned = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(log_df, net, im, fm)

# 5. NOTE: PM4Py may return a different but equally (or more) optimal alignment than the supplied one.
#    Observed when running this script: for CL-604 PM4Py returns M(Auto Approve) + L(Manual Approve) (2 visible
#    moves) instead of the supplied L(Pay Claim) + M(Fraud Screen) + M(Pay Claim) (3 visible moves); for CL-603 it
#    may place the duplicate before the synchronous Notify; tau moves may or may not be displayed. PM4Py's tuples are
#    (log_label, model_label) with '>>' for a skip and None for tau, and its internal 'cost' field uses PM4Py's own
#    weights (10000 per visible move plus tau penalties), NOT the supplied uniform cost of 1. We therefore recount a
#    uniform-1 raw cost from the move tuples and keep the SUPPLIED alignments as the authoritative diagnostic input.
def classify(step):
    l, m = step
    if l == ">>" and m is None:
        return "T(tau)"
    if l == ">>":
        return f"M({m})"
    if m == ">>":
        return f"L({l})"
    return f"S({l})"

case_ids = list(traces.keys())
recomputed = []
for cid, res in zip(case_ids, aligned):
    moves = [classify(s) for s in res["alignment"]]
    raw = sum(1 for mv in moves if mv.startswith(("L(", "M(")))
    recomputed.append({"case": cid, "pm4py_alignment": " -> ".join(moves),
                       "pm4py_raw_cost_uniform1": raw, "pm4py_internal_cost": res["cost"],
                       "pm4py_fitness": round(res["fitness"], 3)})
recomputed = pd.DataFrame(recomputed)
print(recomputed.to_string())

# 6. Supplied (authoritative) move summaries + business context, one row per case
supplied = pd.DataFrame([
    # case, amount, risk, bank_change_7d, approval_path, pay_before_approval, fraud_screen_evidence, notify_evidence,
    # log_moves, model_moves, tau_moves, raw_cost (supplied), model_scope
    ("CL-601", 1200, "LOW", False, "Auto", False, "n/a", "core",
     ["Open Workbench", "Autosave", "Close Workbench"], [], 0, 3, "in"),
    ("CL-602", 480000, "HIGH", True, "Manual", False, "none (verbal only)", "core", [], ["Fraud Screen"], 0, 1, "in"),
    ("CL-603", 3200, "LOW", False, "Auto", False, "n/a", "core (duplicated)", ["Notify Claimant"], [], 0, 1, "in"),
    ("CL-604", 90000, "MEDIUM", False, "Manual", True, "none", "core", ["Pay Claim"], ["Fraud Screen", "Pay Claim"], 0, 3, "in"),
    ("CL-605", 800, "LOW", False, "Auto", False, "n/a", "core", [], [], 1, 0, "in"),
    ("CL-606", 75000, "HIGH", False, "Manual", False, "core", "core", [], ["Assess Loss"], 0, 1, "in"),
    ("CL-607", None, None, False, "Denied", False, "n/a", "core", ["Deny Claim"],
     ["Assess Loss", "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim"], 0, 4, "disputed"),
    ("CL-608", 250000, "HIGH", False, "Manual", False, "core", "CRM (mutable link)", [], ["Notify Claimant"], 0, 1, "in"),
    ("CL-609", 18000, "MEDIUM", False, "Auto", False, "n/a", "core", [], [], 0, 0, "in"),
    ("CL-610", 4500, "LOW", True, "Auto", False, "n/a", "core", [], [], 0, 0, "in"),
    ("CL-611", 62000, "HIGH", False, "Manual", False, "core", "core", ["Assess Damage"], ["Assess Loss"], 0, 2, "in"),
    ("CL-612", 2000, "LOW", False, "Withdrawn", False, "n/a", "none", ["Withdraw Claim"],
     ["Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"], 0, 4, "disputed"),
], columns=["case", "amount_eur", "risk", "bank_change_7d", "approval_path", "pay_before_approval",
            "fraud_screen_evidence", "notify_evidence", "log_moves", "model_moves", "tau_moves",
            "raw_cost", "model_scope"])

# Policy overlay (P1-P4) evaluated on case data -- this is what control-flow alignment cannot see
def policy_flags(r):
    flags = []
    payable = r.approval_path in ("Auto", "Manual")
    if r.approval_path == "Auto":
        if r.amount_eur > 5000: flags.append("P1:amount>5k")
        if r.risk != "LOW": flags.append("P1:risk!=LOW")
        if r.bank_change_7d: flags.append("P1:bank-change<7d")
    p2_trigger = payable and ((r.amount_eur or 0) > 50000 or r.risk == "HIGH" or r.bank_change_7d)
    if p2_trigger and (r.approval_path != "Manual" or r.fraud_screen_evidence.startswith("none")):
        flags.append("P2:fraud-screen/manual-approve missing")
    if r.pay_before_approval: flags.append("P3:pay-before-approval")
    if payable and r.notify_evidence == "none": flags.append("P4:notify missing")
    if payable and "mutable" in r.notify_evidence: flags.append("P4:notify evidence weak")
    return flags

supplied["policy_flags"] = supplied.apply(policy_flags, axis=1)
supplied["n_policy_flags"] = supplied.policy_flags.str.len()
supplied["n_log_moves"] = supplied.log_moves.str.len()
supplied["n_model_moves"] = supplied.model_moves.str.len()
supplied["zero_cost_policy_failure"] = (supplied.raw_cost == 0) & (supplied.n_policy_flags > 0)

# Analyst judgment from Section 2 -- entered explicitly, NOT derived by formula, and kept in separate columns
severity = {"CL-601": 1, "CL-602": 5, "CL-603": 1, "CL-604": 5, "CL-605": 1, "CL-606": 2,
            "CL-607": 1, "CL-608": 2, "CL-609": 4, "CL-610": 5, "CL-611": 1, "CL-612": 1}
confidence = {"CL-601": "High", "CL-602": "High (unevidenced) / Low (check occurred)", "CL-603": "High",
              "CL-604": "High", "CL-605": "High", "CL-606": "Medium-High", "CL-607": "High",
              "CL-608": "Medium", "CL-609": "High", "CL-610": "High", "CL-611": "High", "CL-612": "High"}
dev_type = {"CL-601": "harmless technical noise", "CL-602": "missing evidence -> probable operational deviation",
            "CL-603": "harmless technical noise (retry duplicate)", "CL-604": "actual operational deviation",
            "CL-605": "fitting (silent move)", "CL-606": "missing evidence (external)",
            "CL-607": "process-model mismatch (scope)", "CL-608": "missing evidence (weak correlation)",
            "CL-609": "data-aware policy failure", "CL-610": "data-aware policy failure",
            "CL-611": "event-label mismatch", "CL-612": "process-model mismatch (scope)"}
supplied["business_severity_1_5"] = supplied.case.map(severity)
supplied["confidence"] = supplied.case.map(confidence)
supplied["deviation_type"] = supplied.case.map(dev_type)

# 7. Risk-prioritization evidence table: technical cost and business severity side by side, sorted by judgment
evidence = supplied[["case", "amount_eur", "risk", "bank_change_7d", "approval_path", "raw_cost",
                     "n_log_moves", "n_model_moves", "tau_moves", "policy_flags",
                     "zero_cost_policy_failure", "deviation_type", "business_severity_1_5", "confidence"]]
evidence = evidence.sort_values(["business_severity_1_5", "amount_eur"], ascending=[False, False])
pd.set_option("display.width", 250); pd.set_option("display.max_colwidth", 60)
print(evidence.to_string(index=False))

# Aggregations supporting Sections 3 and 6
print("\nSpearman rank correlation raw_cost vs business severity:",
      evidence[["raw_cost", "business_severity_1_5"]].corr(method="spearman").iloc[0, 1].round(2))
print("\nMove-pattern frequency (supplied alignments):")
print(pd.concat([supplied.explode("log_moves").log_moves.dropna().map(lambda a: f"L({a})"),
                 supplied.explode("model_moves").model_moves.dropna().map(lambda a: f"M({a})")])
      .value_counts().to_string())
print("\nBy deviation type:")
print(supplied.groupby("deviation_type").agg(cases=("case", "count"), mean_raw_cost=("raw_cost", "mean"),
                                             max_severity=("business_severity_1_5", "max"),
                                             total_amount=("amount_eur", "sum")).to_string())
print("\nBy risk level:")
print(supplied.groupby("risk", dropna=False).agg(cases=("case", "count"), mean_raw_cost=("raw_cost", "mean"),
                                                 mean_severity=("business_severity_1_5", "mean"),
                                                 policy_flags=("n_policy_flags", "sum")).to_string())
print("\nZero-cost policy failures (invisible to control-flow conformance):",
      supplied.loc[supplied.zero_cost_policy_failure, "case"].tolist())
```

Running this reproduces the supplied alignments for all cases except where PM4Py selects an alternative optimum (CL-604 at two visible moves; CL-603 with the duplicate placed before the synchronous notification), which is itself evidence for Section 6: the alignment is one cheapest explanation among several, and the Spearman correlation between supplied raw cost and analyst severity across the twelve cases is negative (about −0.4).
