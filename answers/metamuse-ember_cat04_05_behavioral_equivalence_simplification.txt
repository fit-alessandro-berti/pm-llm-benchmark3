# 1. Equivalence Notions

Let $\Sigma_{vis}=\{\text{Open Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Approve Revision, Rework Case, Close Case}\}$. Abbreviations below: $O$ = Open Case, $An$ = Analyze Revision, $Ch$ = Check Revision, $Ap$ = Approve Revision, $Rw$ = Rework Case, $Cl$ = Close Case. Only complete traces are compared: they start in the initial marking and end in the final marking.

Parametric forms, for $n\ge 1$ loop iterations:

- Model A: $O,\; S_1, Rw, S_2, Rw,\dots,S_n,\; Cl$, where each segment $S_i$ is an ordering of $An_i,Ch_i$ followed by optionally $Ap_i$. Hence $\#Ch=\#An=n$, $\#Rw=n-1$, $0\le\#Ap\le n$, and $Ap_i$ is after both $An_i$ and $Ch_i$.
- Model B: $O,\; \text{merge}(Ch,\; T),\; Cl$, where $T$ is $An_1,\dots,An_n$ with optionally $Ap_i$ after $An_i$, separated by $Rw$. Hence $\#Ch=1$, $\#An=n$, $\#Rw=n-1$, $0\le\#Ap\le n$, and the single $Ch$ may be interleaved anywhere in $T$.

## visible trace equivalence after hiding `tau`

Define projection $\pi$ deleting every silent event: the `tau` leaf of $X(Ap,tau)$, and silent Petri-net transitions with label `None`. $L_{vis}(M)=\{\pi(w)\mid w\in L(M)\}$ over $\Sigma_{vis}$. Models are visible-trace equivalent iff $L_{vis}(A)=L_{vis}(B)$ as sets of label sequences. Duplicate labels are distinguished **only by label and occurrence position/count**, not by activity-instance identity, resource, or case attributes. Two `Check Revision` events are therefore indistinguishable except that there are two of them and where they occur.

## business-relevant equivalence under P1

Retain order, counts and placement of all visible labels, including $Ap$. Split the part between $O$ and $Cl$ by $Rw$ into segments $S_1\ldots S_n$. The P1 compliance predicate is: each segment contains exactly one $An$ and exactly one $Ch$ before the next $Rw$ or $Cl$. P1 equivalence is taken as visible-trace equivalence, hence in particular identical segment patterns. Even the coarser segment-count abstraction already separates A and B, because B permits a segment with zero $Ch$.

## bounded visible-language equivalence under P3

$L_{vis}^{\le 12,\le3}(M)=\{t\in L_{vis}(M)\mid |t|\le12,\ \#An(t)\le3\}$. Bounded equivalence is equality of these two bounded sets. A difference inside the bound is a proof of unbounded non-equivalence. Equality inside the bound is only evidence, not a proof for traces needing more than 12 events or more than 3 $An$.

## abstracted dashboard equivalence under P2

Define $\alpha_{P2}(t)=(\#An(t),\ \mathbf{1}[\#Ch(t)>0],\ \mathbf{1}[\#Rw(t)>0])$, requiring $O$ first and $Cl$ last, with $Ap$ and `tau` deleted, $Ch$ multiplicity collapsed to Boolean, and $An/Ch$ interleaving ignored. P2 equivalence is $\alpha_{P2}(L_{vis}(A))=\alpha_{P2}(L_{vis}(B))$.

# 2. Structural Comparison

| Feature | Model A | Model B | Semantic consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loop scope | Loop body is $\rightarrow(+ (An,Ch), X(Ap,tau))$; the loop repeats the whole analyze-check-approve unit. Second loop child is only $Rw$. | Loop body is $\rightarrow(An, X(Ap,tau))$; the loop repeats only analyze-approve. $Ch$ is outside the loop. | A couples repetition of $Ch$ to repetition of $An$. B decouples them. |
| Minimum and maximum Check Revision occurrences | Minimum 1, maximum unbounded, exactly $\#An$. | Minimum = maximum = 1. | For $n\ge2$, A traces have $\ge2$ checks and cannot be in B; B traces with $n\ge2$ have one check for several analyses and cannot be in A. |
| Possible placement of Check Revision | Inside its own iteration, in parallel with that iteration's $An$: before or after $An$, but before that iteration's $Ap$, $Rw$ or $Cl$. It cannot cross an $Ap$, $Rw$, or iteration boundary. | Concurrent with the entire loop: before first $An$, between $An$ and $Ap$, after $Ap$, before/after $Rw$, or after the last $Ap$, as long as it is between $O$ and $Cl$. | B allows $Ap\rightarrow Ch$ and $Ch$ in a segment containing no $An$ of its own. A forbids both. |
| Optional approval occurrences | $X(Ap,tau)$ once per iteration: $0\le\#Ap\le\#An$, and each present $Ap$ follows both $An$ and $Ch$ of its iteration. | $X(Ap,tau)$ once per loop iteration: $0\le\#Ap\le\#An$, each present $Ap$ follows its $An$, but may precede or follow the sole $Ch$. | Approval count alone does not distinguish the models; approval placement relative to $Ch$ does. |
| Concurrency | $+(An,Ch)$ inside the loop body, i.e. concurrency is per-iteration only. | $+(Ch,\text{loop})$ at the top level between $O$ and $Cl$, i.e. concurrency spans the whole loop. | Interleaving scope is the decisive structural difference. |
| Join before Close Case | The outer sequence forces the loop to finish, hence the last iteration's $Ch$, before $Cl$. | The outer sequence forces both parallel branches, $Ch$ and the loop, to finish before $Cl$. | Both guarantee $Ch$ before $Cl$ in every complete trace, but only A guarantees it per analysis segment and before that segment's $Rw$. |

# 3. Verdicts

| View | Verdict | Confidence | Decisive reason | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 operational compliance | not equivalent | high | A enforces one $Ch$ per $An$ segment before $Rw/Cl$; B enforces only one $Ch$ total, so a second $An$ segment can have no check. | A allows $Ch$ before $An$ within a segment. If compliance intended "check after the revision exists", A also needs tightening, but that does not rescue B. |
| P2 executive dashboard | equivalent only under stated abstraction | high | $\alpha_{P2}$ maps both models to the same set: $\#An=n\ge1$, has-check = true, has-rework $=(n>1)$. | Holds only while check multiplicity, $Ap$, and $An/Ch$ interleaving stay hidden. It is not operational equivalence. |
| P3 bounded testing | not equivalent | high | Counterexamples below have length $\le8$ and $\#An\le2$, inside the bound, so bounded sets differ. | A bounded difference proves unbounded non-equivalence; a bounded match would not have proved unbounded equivalence. |
| Unbounded visible trace equivalence | not equivalent | high | Invariants $\#Ch=\#An$ in A versus $\#Ch=1$ in B, and in A $Ap$ after $Ch$ versus B allowing $Ch$ after $Ap$. | Intersection is non-empty, e.g. $\langle O,An,Ch,Cl\rangle$ is in both. Non-equivalence is not disjointness. |

# 4. Counterexample Traces

Trace notation: $\langle O, An, Ch, Ap, Rw, Cl\rangle$ with abbreviations from Section 1.

| Trace ID | Trace | Model A | Model B | First decisive semantic difference | Relevant view |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | $\langle O, An, Ch, Rw, An, Ch, Cl\rangle$ | yes | no | Second $Ch$ exceeds B's single $Ch$; B has $\#Ch=1$. | P1, P3 |
| A2 | $\langle O, Ch, An, Ap, Rw, Ch, An, Ap, Cl\rangle$ | yes | no | Two segments each have their own $Ch$ then $Ap$; B allows only one $Ch$ total. | P1, P3 |
| B1 | $\langle O, An, Ap, Ch, Cl\rangle$ | no | yes | $Ch$ is after $Ap$ in the only iteration; A requires $Ap$ after $+(An,Ch)$. | P1, P3 |
| B2 | $\langle O, An, Ch, Rw, An, Cl\rangle$ | no | yes | Second $An$ segment has no $Ch$ before $Cl$; B's one $Ch$ was used in segment 1. | P1, P3 |
| B3 | $\langle O, Ch, An, Ap, Rw, An, Cl\rangle$ | no | yes | The sole $Ch$ precedes segment 1 and segment 2 has no check; also $\#Ch=1\neq\#An=2$ for A. | P1, P3 |
| C1 | $\langle O, An, Ch, Cl\rangle$ | yes | yes | Common trace; shows the languages intersect. | P1, P2, P3 |

Optional `Approve Revision` and hidden `tau`: `tau` is the silent branch of $X(Ap,tau)$. Hiding it contributes $\varepsilon$, makes $Ap$ optional, and creates no visible event and no count. $Ap$ itself is visible in P1/P3, so B1 is a genuine P1/P3 witness. If $Ap$ were additionally hidden, B1 would collapse to $\langle O,An,Ch,Cl\rangle$, which is in both models, so B1 alone would disappear. The non-equivalence survives without any $Ap$-witness, however: A1, A2, B2 and B3 contain no $Ap$ or do not depend on $Ap$ placement, and they differ purely by check multiplicity per segment. Absence of $Ap$ cannot be read as an observable "auto-approval event"; in the visible language it is only absence.

# 5. Bounded Comparison Code

The code below is exhaustive BFS over $(\text{marking},\text{visible trace})$ pairs under the P3 bound. It is not a random playout. `pm4py.play_out(tree)` follows the variant's choice/playout policy and can miss rare traces; it is mentioned only to be excluded from the verdict.

```python
import pm4py
from collections import Counter, deque

MODEL_A = """->(
  'Open Case',
  *(
    ->(
      +('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision'),
      X('Approve Revision', tau)
    ),
    'Rework Case'
  ),
  'Close Case'
)"""

MODEL_B = """->(
  'Open Case',
  +(
    'Check Revision',
    *(
      ->(
        'Analyze Revision',
        X('Approve Revision', tau)
      ),
      'Rework Case'
    )
  ),
  'Close Case'
)"""

MAX_VISIBLE = 12
MAX_ANALYZE = 3
ANALYZE_LABEL = "Analyze Revision"
MAX_EXPANSIONS = 500000

def clean_marking(marking):
    return Counter({place: count for place, count in marking.items() if count > 0})

def fire(net, marking, transition):
    next_marking = Counter(marking)
    for arc in net.arcs:
        weight = getattr(arc, "weight", 1)
        if arc.target == transition:
            next_marking[arc.source] -= weight
        elif arc.source == transition:
            next_marking[arc.target] += weight
    return clean_marking(next_marking)

def marking_key(marking):
    return frozenset(clean_marking(marking).items())

def bounded_complete_traces(net, initial_marking, final_marking):
    """Exhaustive BFS under P3. Visited is keyed by (marking, trace), not by
    marking alone: two different traces reaching the same marking must both be
    explored. Silent transitions (label None, or the string 'tau') are fired
    without extending the trace; the (marking, trace) visited set prevents
    silent cycles from looping forever. A trace is recorded only when the
    current marking equals the final marking exactly."""
    results = set()
    visited = set()
    queue = deque()
    queue.append((clean_marking(initial_marking), ()))
    expansions = 0
    while queue:
        marking, trace = queue.popleft()
        key = (marking_key(marking), trace)
        if key in visited:
            continue
        visited.add(key)
        expansions += 1
        if expansions > MAX_EXPANSIONS:
            raise RuntimeError("Expansion cap reached; result would be incomplete")
        if clean_marking(marking) == clean_marking(final_marking):
            if trace:
                results.add(trace)
            continue
        for transition in pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, marking):
            label = getattr(transition, "label", None)
            if label == "tau":
                label = None
            next_marking = fire(net, marking, transition)
            if label is None:
                queue.append((next_marking, trace))
            elif len(trace) + 1 <= MAX_VISIBLE:
                if label == ANALYZE_LABEL and trace.count(ANALYZE_LABEL) >= MAX_ANALYZE:
                    continue
                queue.append((next_marking, trace + (label,)))
    print(f"Expansions: {expansions}; bounded complete traces: {len(results)}")
    return results

def main():
    tree_a = pm4py.parse_process_tree(MODEL_A)
    tree_b = pm4py.parse_process_tree(MODEL_B)
    net_a, im_a, fm_a = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_a)
    net_b, im_b, fm_b = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_b)
    print("Sound A:", pm4py.check_soundness(net_a, im_a, fm_a, print_diagnostics=True))
    print("Sound B:", pm4py.check_soundness(net_b, im_b, fm_b, print_diagnostics=True))
    traces_a = bounded_complete_traces(net_a, im_a, fm_a)
    traces_b = bounded_complete_traces(net_b, im_b, fm_b)
    print("Only in A:")
    for trace in sorted(traces_a - traces_b):
        print(trace)
    print("Only in B:")
    for trace in sorted(traces_b - traces_a):
        print(trace)
    print("Bounded visible languages equal:", traces_a == traces_b)
    """Heuristic playout, deliberately not used for the verdict:
    pm4py.play_out(tree_a) / pm4py.play_out(tree_b) sample according to a
    variant. Absence of a trace in samples never proves its absence."""

if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

Expected outcome, following from Section 4: the bounded sets differ; A1/A2 appear only in A, B1/B2/B3 only in B. The BFS is exhaustive up to the stated bound because every $(\text{marking},\text{trace})$ pair with visible length $\le12$ and $\#An\le3$ is enqueued unless it is an exact repeat, which cannot lead to a new visible trace.

# 6. Stochastic and Footprint Comparison

Footprints are a pairwise abstraction: directly-follows, eventually-follows and parallelism relations between activities. They can add evidence because a relation present in one model and absent in the other points to a behavioural difference. For example, $Ap\rightarrow Ch$ as a directly-follows relation is possible in B but not in A, and $Ch$ parallel with the loop is the B structure. But equal footprints do **not** imply equal languages: footprints lose counts, iteration structure and global consistency. Both models have $An$ parallel with $Ch$ in some sense, yet A repeats $Ch$ per iteration and B does not; that multiplicity is invisible to a pure pairwise relation summary.

Stochastic language and Earth Mover's Distance are weaker still for the original deterministic trees, because the originals define **no transition probabilities**. The following is therefore only a schema for a hypothetical sensitivity analysis in which an analyst explicitly declares probabilities, e.g. $P(Ap\text{ vs }tau)=0.5$ and a loop-exit probability. Those numbers are not part of Models A or B and must not be presented as discovered facts.

```python

from Section 5. Footprint inspection (API details vary by PM4Py version):

fp_b = pm4py.discover_footprints(tree_b)
print("Footprints A:", fp_a)
print("Footprints B:", fp_b)

"""Stochastic part, only if stochastic nets with declared probabilities are
```

A low `compute_emd` value would mean the two **approximated, truncated, probability-weighted** trace distributions are close under the chosen alphabet distance and sampling scheme. It would not prove support equality: distinguishing traces may have tiny declared probability, may fall outside the sampled/truncated support, and EMD depends entirely on the invented probabilities. Conversely, a large EMD can be manufactured by changing those invented probabilities. Footprints and EMD are therefore supporting diagnostics, never substitutes for the language comparison in Sections 4 and 5.

# 7. Complexity Comparison

Counting convention: total nodes = internal operator nodes + leaves; depth = number of edges on the longest root-to-leaf path; duplicate-label count counts extra syntactic leaf occurrences of a visible label beyond the first. Precise pseudocode, directly runnable in structure on a parsed PM4Py tree:

```python
from collections import Counter

def tree_stats(node):
    children = list(getattr(node, "children", []) or [])
    if not children:
        label = getattr(node, "label", None)
        silent = label is None or label == "tau"
        labels = Counter() if silent else Counter([label])
        return {"total": 1, "internal": 0, "leaves": 1,
                "visible_leaves": 0 if silent else 1,
                "silent_leaves": 1 if silent else 0,
                "depth_edges": 0, "labels": labels}
    child_stats = [tree_stats(child) for child in children]
    labels = Counter()
    for stats in child_stats:
        labels.update(stats["labels"])
    return {"total": 1 + sum(s["total"] for s in child_stats),
            "internal": 1 + sum(s["internal"] for s in child_stats),
            "leaves": sum(s["leaves"] for s in child_stats),
            "visible_leaves": sum(s["visible_leaves"] for s in child_stats),
            "silent_leaves": sum(s["silent_leaves"] for s in child_stats),
            "depth_edges": 1 + max(s["depth_edges"] for s in child_stats),
            "labels": labels}

def duplicate_summary(labels):
    extra = sum(count - 1 for count in labels.values() if count > 1)
    distinct = sum(1 for count in labels.values() if count > 1)
    return extra, distinct

print(tree_stats(tree_a), duplicate_summary(tree_stats(tree_a)["labels"]))
print(tree_stats(tree_b), duplicate_summary(tree_stats(tree_b)["labels"]))
print("Simplicity A:", pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net_a, im_a, fm_a))
print("Simplicity B:", pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net_b, im_b, fm_b))
```

Derived exact structural values (simplicity is reported by the code rather than guessed, since it depends on the converter's generated places/transitions):

| Measure | Model A | Model B |
|---|---|---|
| Process-tree node count | 12 (5 internal + 7 leaves) | 12 (5 internal + 7 leaves) |
| Depth | 4 edges (5 nodes on longest root-to-leaf path) | 5 edges (6 nodes) |
| Visible leaves | 6 | 6 |
| Silent leaves | 1 (`tau`) | 1 (`tau`) |
| Duplicate-label count, syntactic | 0 extra occurrences; each visible label occurs once as a leaf | 0 extra occurrences; each visible label occurs once as a leaf |
| Semantic repetition | $An,Ch,Ap$ all repeat via the loop | $An,Ap$ repeat via the loop; $Ch$ does not |
| Converted Petri-net simplicity | value printed by `pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net_a, im_a, fm_a)` | value printed by `pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net_b, im_b, fm_b)` |

Equal syntactic node counts do not imply equal behaviour: the same five operators are nested differently, which changes loop scope and concurrency scope.

# 8. Simplified Model

Target semantics: a clearly defined compromise, **P1-core + P2**. P1-core is the segment property of Section 1 (exactly one $An$ and one $Ch$ per segment before $Rw/Cl$), tightened by the explicit business decision that the check must follow the analysis it checks ($An$ before $Ch$ in a segment), with $Ap$ optional after $Ch$. This S model also preserves the P2 dashboard projection exactly, but it does not preserve the full P3 visible language of A or B. A single model cannot preserve P1, P2 and P3 simultaneously here, because P3 retains precisely the distinctions P1-core/P2 remove.

## Compact expression

```text
->(
  'Open Case',
  *(
    ->(
      'Analyze Revision',
      'Check Revision',
      X('Approve Revision', tau)
    ),
    'Rework Case'
  ),
  'Close Case'
)
```

This removes the $+(An,Ch)$ concurrency and replaces it with the canonical sequential order $An\rightarrow Ch\rightarrow$ optionally $Ap$ inside the loop body.

## PM4Py code

```python
SIMPLIFIED = """->(
  'Open Case',
  *(
    ->(
      'Analyze Revision',
      'Check Revision',
      X('Approve Revision', tau)
    ),
    'Rework Case'
  ),
  'Close Case'
)"""

simplified_model = pm4py.parse_process_tree(SIMPLIFIED)
simplified_net, simplified_im, simplified_fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(simplified_model)
print(pm4py.check_soundness(simplified_net, simplified_im, simplified_fm, print_diagnostics=True))
print("Simplicity simplified:", pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(simplified_net, simplified_im, simplified_fm))
print(tree_stats(simplified_model), duplicate_summary(tree_stats(simplified_model)["labels"]))
```

Structural comparison, so "simpler" is substantiated: S has 11 total nodes (4 internal + 7 leaves) versus 12 (5+7) for both A and B; depth 4 edges, equal to A and less than B's 5; visible leaves 6 and silent leaves 1, same as both; syntactic duplicate labels 0, same as both; one fewer internal operator (no parallel node) and a sequential rather than concurrent check, so its converted net has fewer synchronization constructs. It is simpler than both by node/operator count and simpler than B by depth as well; it is not claimed to be shallower than A.

## Preservation table

| Property | Status | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| $O$ first, $Cl$ last | exact | Outer sequence unchanged in all three models. |
| At least one $An$ and at least one $Ch$ | exact | Loop body executes at least once; each iteration has one $An$ and one $Ch$. |
| Exactly one $Ch$ per $An$ segment before $Rw/Cl$ | exact (P1-core) | As in A; restored relative to B, where later segments could have none. |
| $\#Rw=\#An-1$; $Rw$ separates attempts | exact | Loop structure unchanged. |
| $Ap$ optional per segment, after $An$ and $Ch$ | exact relative to A | Same $X(Ap,tau)$ per iteration, sequenced after $Ch$ as in A. |
| $An$ before $Ch$ within a segment | strengthened | New canonical order; A allowed $Ch$ before $An$. |
| $Ch$ before $An$ traces, e.g. $\langle O,Ch,An,Cl\rangle$ | lost | Deliberately removed as "check before the revision exists". |
| Dashboard tuple language under P2 | exact | $\alpha_{P2}$ image is again $\#An=n\ge1$, has-check true, has-rework $=(n>1)$, same as A and B. |
| Full visible language under P3 | lost / not preserved | S is a proper subset of A ($n=1$ part also in B); it equals neither A nor B. |
| B-only traces: one check for many analyses, check after approval | lost | Deliberately, because they violate the per-segment check requirement. |
| Permissiveness about $An/Ch$ order | weakened to one canonical order | Concurrency is abstracted away inside a segment. |

# 9. Final Recommendation

- **P1 operational compliance:** Model A is the suitable original, because it is the only original that gives every analysis segment its own check before $Rw/Cl$. Model B is unsuitable for compliance purposes: it certifies nothing about later analysis attempts. The simplified S model is preferable to A as the operational model **if** the business accepts the stated tightening that a check follows its analysis; S keeps the P1-core segment property, makes the intended order auditable, and removes the misleading $Ch$-before-$An$ executions of A. If existing $Ch$-before-$An$ executions are real and must remain admissible, keep A and do not replace it with S.
- **P2 executive dashboard:** A, B and S are all equivalent under the stated dashboard abstraction, so any of them yields the same dashboard tuples. Use S (or an even smaller dashboard-only model) for reporting if desired, but never use dashboard equivalence as evidence of compliance. The dashboard must be labelled as hiding check multiplicity, approvals and interleaving.
- **P3 bounded testing:** Neither original can stand in for the other, and S is not a P3-equivalent replacement for either. Keep A and B as separate test oracles for their respective languages, and use S only as the oracle for the new, explicitly adopted P1-core specification.

Warning on bounded comparison: the BFS in Section 5 settles non-equivalence because distinguishing traces occur inside the bound. Had the bounded languages matched, that would have been only evidence: a difference requiring more than 12 visible events or more than 3 $An$ occurrences, or a difference visible only through probabilities, footprints or timing that the bounded visible language does not capture, would have been missed. Bounded agreement must therefore always be reported with its bound, and never upgraded to a proof of unbounded equivalence without a separate structural argument.
