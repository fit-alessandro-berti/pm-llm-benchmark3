# 2. Benign alternative explanations

**Create PO + Approve PO by Carla/Dan/Gita (four-eyes on spend).**
1. *Emergency rush channel.* 88%/79% emergency_flag and specialization in medical supplies / facilities repairs suggest a sanctioned expedite path where the same person legitimately handles both steps under an emergency policy. *Supports:* a documented emergency SOP permitting same-actor PO/approval up to a threshold; matching emergency_flag; on-call rosters. *Refutes:* emergency_flag set retroactively, amounts above the emergency threshold, or cases with no genuine urgency (routine amount_band, business-hours timestamps).
2. *Small-team / after-hours coverage.* Specialized buyers with few available approvers may be forced into dual roles on weekends. *Supports:* timestamps outside core hours, absence of alternative approvers on shift. *Refutes:* violations concentrated during staffed hours when a second approver was available.

**Change Vendor Bank Details + Payment Approval (Lena/AP_Kim, V-77x).**
1. *Legitimate mass bank-platform migration.* The log explicitly notes V-77x changed banking platforms and several changes were mass-maintenance. *Supports:* a maintenance ticket referencing the platform change, identical new bank details matching official vendor communication, treasury sign-off. *Refutes:* missing/forged ticket, new IBANs not matching official vendor records, per-case (not mass) changes, changes immediately preceding one-off payments.
2. *Role design in vendor master.* Vendor-master staff may legitimately both maintain and touch payment steps if the "four-eyes" rule here is not actually defined. *Supports:* a control matrix showing no segregation requirement. *Refutes:* an explicit policy requiring different actors for bank change vs payment approval.

**Emergency Override + Retrospective Justification by same person (Hugo/Gita).**
1. *Data-migration artifact.* 12/17 share identical to-the-minute timestamps from a quarter_close spreadsheet upload, strongly indicating a batch-entry attribution error rather than real same-person justification. *Supports:* source_system="quarter_close_upload", identical timestamps, entered_by differing from org:resource. *Refutes:* distinct real-time timestamps and matching entered_by/approved_by fields.
2. *Delegated-assistant entry under a shared account.* Justifications entered by assistants but stamped to "Approver_Gita." *Supports:* entered_by values naming assistants. *Refutes:* entered_by equals the approver and the justification content shows no independent review.

**Fast Carla→Gita / Dan→Hugo approvals.**
1. *Genuine urgency.* Medical/repair emergencies legitimately compress cycle time. *Supports:* emergency_flag, priority attribute, criticality of purchase_category. *Refutes:* routine categories, non-urgent amounts, business-hours cases with no urgency marker.
2. *Pre-cleared / framework orders.* Rush items may be pre-negotiated, so approval is a formality. *Supports:* existing contract_type=framework, prior vendor qualification. *Refutes:* new vendors or one-off spot buys approved in minutes.

**Subcontracting loops (Gita↔Carla, Hugo↔Dan, Lena↔Kim).**
1. *Iterative clarification/correction.* Approver returns the case for a fix, then re-approves. *Supports:* comment/annotation fields, changed attributes between loop passes. *Refutes:* no change between passes (pure re-stamp), or the loop consistently ending in higher payout.
2. *Automated feedback pattern.* For Bot_Match↔Kim, the loop is machine-generated exception routing. *Supports:* bot resource IDs, deterministic timing. *Refutes:* human overrides embedded in the "automated" loop.

# 3. Case prioritization

**High-priority clusters (investigate first).**
- **Cluster H1 — VendorMaster_Lena + AP_Kim, vendor family V-77x (bank-change → payment).** Reason: the highest-consequence control (bank-detail change vs payment authorization), high-amount (68%), high vendor concentration (72%), 83–100% manual_upload, 17-minute approvals, and a tight Lena→Kim→Lena correction loop all coincide on the *same* small population. This is the single most concerning subgroup. *Inspect first:* the vendor-master change records for each V-77x case (old vs new IBAN/bank), the platform-migration maintenance ticket and its linkage (or absence) to each case, source_system="manual_upload" upload logs, approver identity and independence, payment amounts and beneficiary account, and whether any payment went to a newly changed account within minutes.
- **Cluster H2 — Shared-account and bot/human mapping integrity.** Reason: the "Approver_Gita" shared account (23 emergency approvals) and the Bot_PO/human collapse in legacy_erp corrupt the very fields used to detect the other patterns. *Inspect first:* the source_system="legacy_erp" mapping table, entered_by vs approved_by vs org:resource for the 23 shared-account approvals, and identity/access-management logs to re-attribute actions to real individuals. Resolve this before finalizing any four-eyes conclusion.

**Medium-priority clusters.**
- **Cluster M1 — Buyer_Carla + Approver_Gita emergency PO/approval (126 cases, incl. 42 four-eyes and 84 fast).** Reason: real segregation-of-duties exceptions, but strongly explained by an emergency medical rush channel; needs confirmation that the emergency path is authorized and that flags/amounts are genuine. *Inspect first:* the emergency SOP and thresholds, emergency_flag provenance and timing, amount_band vs emergency limit, retrospective-justification completeness and independence.
- **Cluster M2 — Buyer_Dan + Manager_Hugo weekend repairs (104 cases).** Reason: same as M1 but facilities/weekend; verify staffing/coverage justification and managerial-loop legitimacy. *Inspect first:* on-call rosters, weekend approver availability, the Hugo→Dan→Hugo loop content.
- **Cluster M3 — Emergency Override → Retrospective Justification exceptions (17).** Reason: compensating-control failure, but largely a suspected migration artifact. *Inspect first:* the 12 identical-timestamp records, quarter_close_upload provenance, and the 5 non-batch cases (treat those 5 as the real exceptions to test).

**Low-priority / monitoring-only clusters.**
- **Aline→Elena and Boris→Felix routine PO approvals (510/495).** High co-working but low emergency, low amount, low vendor concentration; do **not** treat frequency as suspicious. Monitor via periodic sampling only.

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

**Evidence that would INCREASE concern.**
- **Beneficiary account mismatch:** for V-77x, a new IBAN/bank account in `Change Vendor Bank Details` that does *not* match official vendor correspondence, or that routes to an account not on the vendor master of record.
- **Missing/forged maintenance ticket:** absence of the platform-migration ticket, or a ticket that cannot be linked to the specific procurement cases, especially where a payment followed a bank change within minutes.
- **Genuine same-actor payment diversion:** after cleaning `org:resource` via `entered_by`/`approved_by`, the bank-change→payment four-eyes violations persist with truly the same individual, during staffed hours, with no emergency justification.
- **Retroactive flag manipulation:** `emergency_flag` timestamps (or audit-trail edits) showing the flag was set *after* the fast approval, or emergencies on routine `purchase_category`/high `amount_band` above the emergency threshold.
- **Empty subcontracting loops:** Lena→Kim→Lena (or Gita/Hugo) loops where no attribute changes between passes but the payout increases — indicating re-stamping rather than correction.
- **Access-log contradictions:** IAM/login logs showing the "approver" was not logged in at the approval timestamp, or a single person operating multiple shared accounts.
- **Concentration to a single ultimate beneficiary:** V-77x sub-vendors resolving to the same bank account or owner.

**Evidence that would REDUCE concern.**
- **Verified mass-maintenance:** a treasury/vendor-master ticket for the V-77x platform migration, with new bank details matching official vendor letters, applied as a batch to many vendors (not one-off before a payment).
- **Documented emergency SOP:** an approved policy permitting same-actor PO creation/approval under emergency thresholds, with the Carla/Gita and Dan/Hugo cases falling within it, corroborated by after-hours timestamps and on-call rosters showing no alternate approver.
- **Confirmed data artifacts:** the 12 identical-timestamp retrospective justifications provably originating from `quarter_close_upload`, and `entered_by` showing assistants distinct from the approver — reclassifying those four-eyes "violations" as attribution errors.
- **Corrected mapping table:** the `legacy_erp` bot/human mapping fixed so Bot_PO and human approvers separate cleanly, collapsing the apparent same-resource create/approve overlaps.
- **Independent verification evidence:** call-back or dual-confirmation records for bank-detail changes prior to the first payment, even where system timing looks fast.
- **Post-hoc payment integrity:** reconciliation showing all V-77x payments reached the correct, expected vendor accounts with no clawbacks or complaints.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

During the last quarter we reviewed the procurement-to-payment event log for segregation-of-duties exceptions, timing anomalies, and concentration patterns. The analysis identified several **control exceptions**, but the evidence does **not** establish, and should not be read as alleging, fraud or collusion by any individual. Most exceptions have credible operational or data-quality explanations that must be tested before any conclusion is drawn.

The majority of four-eyes exceptions on Create/Approve Purchase Order (42 cases, 1.8%) fall within emergency medical-supply and weekend facilities-repair channels and are strongly associated with emergency handling and small-team coverage rather than misconduct. Similarly, most same-person retrospective-justification exceptions (12 of 17) appear to be a data-migration artifact from a quarter-close spreadsheet upload, and a shared "Approver_Gita" account plus a legacy-ERP bot/human mapping error are known to distort resource attribution. These data limitations mean some detected "violations" may be misattributed, and, conversely, some genuine exceptions may be masked; resource-level findings should be treated as provisional until identities are reconstructed from `entered_by`/`approved_by` and the mapping table is corrected.

The **most concerning subgroup** is the VendorMaster_Lena / AP_Kim activity on vendor family V-77x, where vendor-bank-detail changes and payment approvals cluster with high-value amounts, high vendor concentration, near-total manual uploads, very short approval intervals, and a tight correction loop. This combination warrants **targeted follow-up**: verify the platform-migration ticket and its case linkage, confirm that changed bank details match official vendor records, and reconcile the associated payments to the correct beneficiaries. We recommend prioritizing this subgroup, resolving the identified data-quality issues, and confirming the emergency and vendor-master control designs before drawing further conclusions.
