## 1. Decision rule

The policy is a portfolio decision, not a ranking by `p_now`. It selects swarms to maximize expected net value: avoided breach value minus swarm cost, false-alarm burden, queue externalities, and the option value of holding a reserve slot, subject to fairness and operational constraints.

**Eligibility gates**
1. **Process stage:** Only cases before `Diagnosis Complete` are eligible. `Diagnosis Complete` cases are excluded unless diagnosis is formally reopened.
2. **Data quality:** If a critical feature is missing or imputed, the case may be reviewed but is not auto-selected until the data issue is verified.
3. **Alert validity:** First alerts are reviewable. Repeated alerts require either a materially new event or a calibrated risk increase of at least about **0.05** since the last actionable alert.
4. **Intervention plausibility:** The case must have a non-trivial lower-bound absolute risk-reduction estimate. High breach probability alone is insufficient.

**Calibration / robustification**
- **SME:** add approximately **+0.04 to +0.09** to `p_now` for alerting urgency, because the model underpredicts. Do not infer that the causal swarm effect is larger.
- **Strategic Enterprise:** subtract approximately **0.05 to 0.12** from `p_now` for alerting urgency, because the model overpredicts after the workflow redesign. Require stronger corroboration before using upper-bound effect estimates.
- **Public Sector:** no systematic shift, but sample size is moderate; treat estimates as normal-to-cautious.
- **Non-Native-Language Support:** keep the point estimate but widen the uncertainty interval and require language-capable staffing; use lower-bound effect estimates for robust decisions.

**Prioritization logic**
Use an interval version of:

\[
\text{Direct net benefit} \approx \text{Absolute risk reduction} \times \text{Value of avoiding breach} - €450 - \text{False-alarm burden}
\]

Then subtract or stress-test against **spillover harm** and **uncertainty penalties**.

A practical tiering rule is:
- **Tier A:** Stage-eligible, valid alert, lower-bound direct net benefit clearly positive, urgent or high-value, manageable uncertainty.
- **Tier B:** Positive expected benefit but weaker lower bound, data uncertainty, high false-alarm burden, or marginal net value after spillovers.
- **Tier C:** Ineligible, suppressed repeated alert, or expected benefit too uncertain/small relative to spillover and reserve option value.

**Spillover and reserve**
Each used slot imposes queue externalities. Under the base model, approximate spillover cost per slot as:

\[
6 \text{ exposed cases} \times \Delta p_{\text{spillover}} \times \text{exposed-case value proxy}
\]

I use **€15,000** as a conservative proxy for an exposed diagnosis-stage case unless better queue data exist. One slot is reserved because the possible regulatory incident has unlisted but potentially high operational and contractual importance. The reserve is released only if the marginal candidate’s robust net benefit clearly exceeds the reserve’s option value.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**Cool-down**
- If a case generates repeated alerts without a materially new event, suppress further swarm notifications for **four 30-minute model refreshes**, i.e. about **2 hours**.
- If a swarm has already been used on a case, repeat swarm is forbidden unless there is a materially new event or diagnosis is formally reopened.

**Suppression**
- Suppress repeated alerts when all of the following hold:
  - No new customer data, stage change, or operational event.
  - Calibrated risk increase is less than about **0.05**.
  - Time to deadline is not so short that immediate action is required.
- Suppressed alerts are still logged for audit and fairness monitoring.

**Escalation**
- Override suppression if any of the following occurs:
  - A materially new event appears, such as a new diagnostic log, customer escalation, missing data restored, or stage delay.
  - Calibrated risk rises by **0.10 or more**.
  - Time to SLA deadline falls below the practical intervention lead time, roughly one hour plus swarm duration.
  - Data quality is restored and reveals a genuine deterioration.

**Re-entry**
- A suppressed case re-enters the candidate pool when:
  - A materially new event occurs; or
  - The cooldown expires and the case still has elevated calibrated risk and positive robust net benefit; and
  - The case remains before `Diagnosis Complete`.

**I-05 versus I-07**
- **I-05** is a third alert with **no new event** and only a small raw increase from 0.55 to 0.57. Even though SME underprediction means true risk may be higher, the alert lacks new evidence and the case has low value. It should be suppressed under cooldown to avoid alert fatigue and unnecessary customer disruption.
- **I-07** is also a third alert, but it has a **new customer diagnostic log**. That is a materially new event. Therefore, unlike I-05, it is not suppressed. ## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Value-based prioritization can starve lower-value cases**
- Risk: A pure value-weighted rule would repeatedly favor Strategic Enterprise and high-value cases, while SME or lower-value cases receive no intervention.
- Safeguards:
  - Maintain a **fairness cap**: no more than a defined share, e.g. 60%, of weekly swarms should go to the top value quartile unless there is a documented exceptional reason.
  - Require **mandatory review** for low-value cases with urgent deadlines and materially new evidence.
  - Provide a **non-slot lightweight review** path for cases like I-07, so they are not ignored merely because a full swarm is not economical.
  - Audit selection outcomes by value tier and segment.

**Non-Native-Language Support uncertainty**
- Risk: Sparse validation data may cause under- or over-intervention, and the swarm may be less effective if language barriers slow diagnosis.
- Safeguards:
  - Require a language-capable specialist or translator for NLL swarms.
  - Use lower-bound effect estimates for NLL cases rather than excluding them automatically.
  - Collect outcome data separately for NLL cases and update effect estimates with a segment-specific model.
  - Include NLL cases in randomized or quasi-randomized intervention evaluation when capacity allows.

**Differential calibration across segments**
- Risk: SME underprediction and Strategic Enterprise overprediction can bias intervention toward or against segments.
- Safeguards:
  - Apply segment-specific calibration adjustments before prioritization.
  - Monitor calibration separately for SME, Strategic Enterprise, Public Sector, and NLL.
  - If a segment’s calibration error exceeds a pre-agreed threshold, require human review for that segment rather than automatic selection.
  - Avoid using a global threshold that inherits the worst calibration error.

**Selective intervention changes future training labels**
- Risk: If swarms prevent breaches in high-risk treated cases, future labels may show those cases as non-breaches. A model trained naively may learn that such cases are low risk, creating a feedback loop.
- Safeguards:
  - Log every intervention as a treatment variable.
  - Store the counterfactual question: “What would have happened without the swarm?”
  - Use causal estimation methods rather than naive supervised learning on treated outcomes.
  - Where possible, randomize among near-tied eligible cases to create unbiased treatment-effect evidence.
  - Use inverse-probability weighting or counterfactual labeling when retraining.

**Spillover harms may fall systematically on untreated groups**
- Risk: Swarms for high-value cases may delay diagnosis for untreated cases, and those spillover harms may concentrate in lower-value, SME, Public Sector, or NLL cases.
- Safeguards:
  - Monitor queue delay and breach-risk increases for untreated cases by segment, value tier, and language-support status.
  - If spillover harm exceeds a threshold, reduce active slots or raise the net-benefit threshold.
  - Give affected untreated cases priority in ordinary diagnosis queue after a swarm-heavy period.
  - Include spillover cost directly in the allocation score, not only in post-hoc review.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational monitoring**
Track:
- Slots used, reserved, and released.
- Time from alert to swarm start.
- Swarm duration and whether it finished before `Diagnosis Complete`.
- Specialist interruption minutes.
- Ordinary diagnosis queue length and delay.
- Regulatory incident arrivals and whether reserve was available.

Trigger: If average queue delay from swarms exceeds 20 minutes per slot, or reserve is unavailable for a high-priority regulatory incident, reduce active swarm count or tighten eligibility.

**Predictive monitoring**
Track:
- Calibration by segment: observed breach rate versus predicted probability.
- Calibration slope and intercept for SME, Strategic Enterprise, Public Sector, and NLL.
- Alert volume, repeated-alert rate, and suppression rate.
- Data-quality issues, especially imputed features.
- Stability of `p_prev` to `p_now` changes.

Trigger: If segment calibration error exceeds about **0.05** for two consecutive weeks, update segment adjustment and require human review for that segment.

**Causal-effect monitoring**
Track:
- Breach outcome after swarm versus matched untreated cases.
- Time to `Diagnosis Complete`.
- Heterogeneous effects by stage, segment, time-to-deadline, and value tier.
- Whether repeated alerts with new events produce larger effects than unchanged alerts.

Estimation plan:
- Use randomized tie-breaking when multiple eligible cases compete for a marginal slot.
- Use reserve-slot availability as an instrumental variable when randomization is not feasible.
- Use stepped-wedge or matched difference-in-differences analysis for policy changes.
- Update absolute risk-reduction intervals with Bayesian hierarchical estimates by segment and stage.

Trigger: If the posterior probability that net benefit is positive falls below about 70% for a case tier or segment, pause automatic selection for that tier or segment.

**Capacity and spillover monitoring**
Track:
- Delay imposed on non-intervened diagnosis-stage cases.
- Estimated spillover probability increase per slot.
- Congestion level at the time of each swarm.
- Number of untreated cases affected by each slot.

Trigger: If estimated spillover exceeds **0.025** per slot or congestion delays exceed the assumed 8–20 minutes, reduce active slots, increase reserve, or require higher direct net benefit before intervention.

**Fairness monitoring**
Track:
- Intervention rates by segment, value tier, language-support status, and alert history.
- Breach outcomes for treated and untreated groups.
- Distribution of spillover harm across segments and value tiers.
- Number of low-value urgent cases receiving review or alternative support.

Trigger: If intervention rates or avoided-breach benefits differ materially across protected or operationally important groups without a documented net-benefit reason, adjust the allocation rule, add fairness caps, or introduce targeted review.

**Policy recalibration and threshold change**
The rule should be reviewed weekly by an operations-model governance group. Change the threshold or allocation rule only when there is evidence of persistent misalignment, such as:
- Sustained calibration error by segment.
- Causal effect estimates materially lower or higher than current ranges.
- Spillover harm consistently exceeding assumptions.
- Reserve slot consistently unused while high-net-benefit cases are missed.
- Fairness metrics showing systematic disadvantage.
- Repeated alerts causing avoidable burden without new information.

The policy should be updated as a versioned decision rule, with each change accompanied by an impact review covering expected net benefit, fairness, spillover, and reserve adequacy.
