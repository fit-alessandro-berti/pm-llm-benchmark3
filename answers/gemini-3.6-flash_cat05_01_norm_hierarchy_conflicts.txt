## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

### Authority, Norm Hierarchy, and Exceptions
1. **Fact**: Normative Source A (GPIP-4) is a Board-approved global policy effective 2026-01-01. Normative Source B (DE-AAC-PROC-17, Rev 6) is a controlled local SOP. Normative Source C (ECD-2) is a joint executive incident directive. Normative Source D (CFO Email) is administrative guidance.
2. **Interpretation on Conflict Hierarchy**:
   - **Governance & Integrity Controls (GPIP-4 A1, A3)**: GPIP-4 represents the highest authority. Under A4 and A7, emergency status waives *competitive tendering only*; all other controls (such as Segregation of Duties under A1 and Compliance Clearance for new suppliers or >EUR 100k under A3) remain binding unless a written exception is issued specifically by the Chief Risk Officer (CRO). Incident directives (ECD-2) signed by the COO/Incident Commander cannot implicitly override GPIP-4 integrity controls.
   - **Financial Controls & Approval Thresholds (GPIP-4 A2, Local SOP B2, CFO Email D1–D3)**: The CFO has explicit authority to interpret and operationalize Finance controls (D3). Therefore, CFO Email D1 validly delegates local site Finance approval to joint Incident Command authorization up to EUR 150,000 during an active ECD-2 window, provided essential integrity controls are preserved (D4).
   - **Procedural Timing and Vendor Registration (Local SOP B1, B3, ECD-2 C1, C2, C4)**: ECD-2 validly supersedes ordinary local workflow steps (SOP B1 PO-first rule, SOP B3 approved-supplier register) under the authority of SOP B7 and C4 for purchases falling within ECD-2's explicit scope and effective window.
3. **Assumption**: Historical corporate practice treated signed incident directives as policy overlays for operational workflows, but *never* as waivers for new-supplier Compliance clearance. Thus, ECD-2 cannot be interpreted as an implicit waiver of GPIP-4 A3.

### Scope Boundaries and Emergency Emergency Rationale
1. **Interpretation of ECD-2 Scope (C6)**: ECD-2 applies to "critical sterilization-line lots required to contain the current contamination event." Internal Audit's interpretation (components needed within 24 hours for the affected line) is adopted as the primary benchmark because emergency derogations must be narrowly construed. Components for backup lines with multi-day stock safety margins do not qualify under emergency continuity scope.
2. **Assumption on Temporal Scope**: ECD-2 is strictly bound by its stated temporal window (2026-07-14 09:00 CEST to 2026-07-18 18:00 CEST). Verbal extensions by operational personnel have no legal or normative effect.

### Evidential Discipline and Temporal Anchor
1. **Fact**: Under Evidence Note ON-44, ERP timestamps post-outage may lag real-world actions by up to 12 minutes, or suffer clock skew.
2. **Interpretation on Evidence Priority**: Substantive legal commitment occurs when a contract is formed with the supplier (e.g., recorded phone agreement, supplier email acknowledgment), not when a database entry is committed. Contemporaneous external/physical evidence (phone recordings, signed PDFs, supplier timestamped receipts) supersedes ERP database timestamps.
3. **Assessment Timepoint**: Compliance is evaluated at the exact time the commitment to the supplier was executed (`Commit_to_Supplier`). Per GPIP-4 A6, subsequent approvals mitigate financial or operational risk but do not retroactively cure a pre-commitment procedural breach.

9.  supported_by(evidence) :- valid_timestamp(E) if system_outage_active(E) then contemporaneous_recording(E) or signed_pdf(E) or supplier_acknowledgment(E) else erp_log(E).

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

### Case P-502
* **Alternative Classification**: **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**
* **Required Premise Shift**: ECD-2 C4 ("takes precedence over ordinary purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding requirements") must be interpreted as a broad corporate waiver that includes global GPIP-4 A3 Compliance Clearance for new suppliers, treating "supplier-onboarding" holistically.
* **Critique of Alternative**: **Weak**. GPIP-4 A4 explicitly dictates that emergency status waives *competitive tendering only*, reserving all other control waivers to the CRO under A7. Historical practice confirms corporate incident directives have never bypassed new-supplier Compliance clearance.

### Case P-505
* **Alternative Classification**: **NONCOMPLIANT**
* **Required Premise Shift**: Strict adherence to the database-of-record premise, holding that internal ERP timestamps are absolute and binding regardless of physical evidence, contract law, or documented system outage skew.
* **Critique of Alternative**: **Weak**. Defeated by Evidence Note ON-44 and fundamental contract law. Legal commitment occurred when the supplier acknowledged the order at 17:59:31 CEST, within the active ECD-2 window. System clock skews cannot create a retroactive policy breach.

### Case P-507
* **Alternative Classification**: **NONCOMPLIANT**
* **Required Premise Shift**: Strict non-delegation premise regarding GPIP-4 A2, asserting that the CFO's informal email (Normative Source D) cannot legally override the requirement for a separate Finance Controller approval for purchases exceeding EUR 50,000.
* **Critique of Alternative**: **Credible but Ultimately Inferior**. While Normative Source D was an un-cataloged email, D3 establishes that the CFO holds explicit authority to interpret Finance controls. Exercising that authority during a declared emergency to streamline approvals under EUR 150k via Incident Command constitutes a valid administrative determination.

### Case P-501
* **Alternative Classification**: **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**
* **Required Premise Shift**: Application of an implicit "substantive emergency necessity" doctrine, holding that an imminent line stoppage (4 hours) automatically grants emergency authority to the on-duty Shift Lead, overriding formal delegation requirements.
* **Critique of Alternative**: **Weak**. SOP B4 explicitly conditions Shift Lead substitution on active delegation when the Plant Manager is reachable. Because the Plant Manager was reachable and not delegated, and B5 strictly forbids waiving pre-commitment Finance approval, the procedural breach remains uncured.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

```text
NORTHBRIDGE STERILE SYSTEMS — CORPORATE POLICY AMENDMENT
AMENDED CLAUSE: GPIP-4 SECTION 8 / SOP OVERLAY DIRECTIVES
```

### 8.1 Norm Hierarchy and Incident Continuity Directives
In the event of a declared site emergency, Emergency Continuity Directives (ECDs) issued jointly by the Chief Operating Officer (COO) and Incident Commander supersede local Standard Operating Procedures regarding operational order timing (`Create_PO` relative to `Commit_to_Supplier`) and local approved-vendor registration rules. **Under no circumstances shall an ECD, CFO email, or local SOP alter, waive, or weaken Global Policy GPIP-4 core integrity controls, specifically Section A1 (Segregation of Duties) or Section A3 (Compliance Clearance for New Suppliers / High-Value Purchases), without a written, time-bound Exception Certificate issued directly by the Chief Risk Officer (CRO) pursuant to Clause A7.**

### 8.2 Scope Definitions and Financial Delegation Thresholds
1. **Emergency Scope**: Emergency procurement authority is strictly restricted to goods and services required to mitigate imminent safety hazards or production stoppages expected within 24 hours on primary affected lines. Secondary, backup, or contingency inventory buffer purchases are expressly excluded.
2. **Emergency Financial Approvals**: During an active, written ECD window, joint authorization by two distinct Incident Command members satisfies internal Finance approval requirements for operational purchases up to **EUR 150,000**. Purchases exceeding **EUR 150,000** additionally require pre-commitment written Legal Clearance. Compliance Clearance under A3 remains strictly mandatory prior to supplier commitment regardless of transaction value.

### 8.3 Retrospective Recordkeeping, Expiry, and Safeguards Against Normalization
1. **Contemporaneous Recording**: Verbal emergency authorizations must be logged in writing within **four (4) hours**. External proof of commitment time (telecom logs, timestamped supplier acknowledgments) governs in disputes involving system outages or database latency.
2. **Hard Expiry**: Directives expire automatically at the exact minute specified in the instrument. Extensions must be issued in writing prior to expiry; verbal extensions are legally void.
3. **Anti-Normalization Safeguard**: Within **72 hours** of the expiration of any ECD directive, Internal Audit and the CRO Risk Committee shall receive an automated audit trail of all emergency commitments. Any procurement executed without compliant pre-commitment approvals or proper delegation shall trigger an immediate administrative hold and mandatory executive disclosure.
