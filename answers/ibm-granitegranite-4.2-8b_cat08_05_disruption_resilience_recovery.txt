### 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance  
*(Explain difference using this case; identify true resilience, risky shortcuts, and ambiguous cases depending on risk appetite. Ground in DFGs, metrics, and segment risks.)*  

- **True resilience** = *Adaptation that preserves core regulatory/operational controls (e.g., authorization validation, scan/temp documentation at the right workflow point) while temporarily adjusting workflow speed or method to meet urgent needs. It is observable in real time (no hidden gap), does not increase hidden audit workload after operational recovery, and aligns with mission (e.g., protecting hospitals). Evidence is visible in process flow (DFG nodes like Manual authorization check) and metric stability (e.g., hospital fill rate ~91% during T2 when using A + B; traceability exceptions fell from 9.5%5.4% in T3 when B/D were done promptly). Examples: **B (manual auth check)**, **J (backlog prioritization with explicit rule)**, **E (manual pick list with pick-log entry)**. These kept the *required step* (check, prioritize, log) but used manual execution; no step was skipped or deferred past dispatch.  

- **Concealed noncompliance (risky shortcut)** = *Adaptation that bypasses or delays a mandatory control step (e.g., "skip system authorization check," "dispatch without scan," "phone POD without temp verification for cold/controlled") to preserve throughput in the short term. The bypass is not visibly tracked in real time during T2 (e.g., no "skip" flag on the pick list), so the gap is hidden until audit remediation (L) or customer complaint. Metrics during T2 directly show the consequence: traceability exceptions spiked (9.5% vs 0.8%), cold-chain doc missing 4.8% (vs 0.4%), controlled missing 2.9% (vs 0.2%), and bulk p90 cycle time doubled (180h vs 72h). After operational recovery (T3–T4), the gap is discovered only during audit remediation (L), creating a hidden workload tail ("audit remediation queue persisted after operational recovery"). This is **not resilience** because resilience requires maintaining control integrity *during* the disruption; bypassing the control means the system did not reliably protect compliance while operating. Examples: **C (skip system auth)**, **G (dispatch without scan)**, **H (phone POD for controlled/cold without temp log)**, **D (using stale snapshot without expiry)**, **L (post-invoice audit remediation)**. Note: D *could* be resilient if snapshot refreshed 4h (modified), but using it without expiry was a shortcut causing staleness  risk. G is the highest risk due to scan loss for cold/controlled; C is high risk for controlled-substance authorization.  

- **Ambiguous (depends on risk appetite)** = *Adaptation where the intent aligns with mission but the control detail could be clarified without major delay. Example: **I (defer noncritical)**. Defering bulk to protect capacity is reasonable; however, *without notifying pharmacies*, it caused pharmacy dissatisfaction (7.8% cancellation). With a clear rule ("notify pharmacy within 2h of defer") and capacity threshold (>80%), it becomes resilient (J-aligned). Without notification, it risks concealed customer impact (fairness gap). Similarly, **F (temp doc sometimes missed)** is ambiguous if temp log is added at pick (modify); without it, it’s concealed risk. Risk appetite: If the organization prioritizes absolute regulatory compliance (e.g., for cold chains), *all* shortcuts bypassing scan/temp at pick (C, G, H for controlled) are non-resilient and must be retired. If the organization prioritizes speed for hospital supply with modest compliance tolerance (e.g., allow <1% traceability exceptions during incident), then *only* C and G must be retired (no skip auth; no dispatch without scan); H for controlled could be modified (paper receipt), and D must have expiry. **Resilience definition used here is operational: "Did the adaptation reliably uphold the mandatory control (authorization check, scan/temp documentation at pick) in real time during the disruption? If yes  resilience; if no (bypass/delay), even if throughput was preserved  concealed noncompliance. Hidden audit tail after recovery confirms concealed nature.** This matches constraints (no treating throughput preservation as resilience if controls disappear; no equating after-the-fact audit with real-time compliance).

>      - *Cold-chain* (all): **Mandatory Paper Temp Log at Pick** (see Evidence Capture). *No dispatch without temp log signed by picker + supervisor.*

>   1. **Pick List**: Paper list with item ID, location, "picked" timestamp, picker name. *Signed by picker at pick completion.*  
>   2. **Temp Log (Cold-chain)**: Paper log with temperature reading (°C/°F), location, time, picker initials, supervisor initials at pick. *Logged immediately; no reconstruction later.*  
>   3. **Dual-Control Log (Controlled)**: Paper log with order ID, product, "pharmacist checked" (Y/N), pharmacist name, initials, time. *Signed by pharmacist at pick; supervisor initials if applicable.*

If snapshot >4h old (e.g., used at 10 AM for a 2 PM pick), staff must do **on-floor verification** (check bin physically + log "verified" on pick list) before picking. *No reliance on stale snapshot.*

### 5. Resilience indicators  
*(Define leading (predict early) and lagging (measure outcome) indicators. Include throughput, service protection, traceability, control completeness, backlog age, rework, and fairness across customer groups. Ground in metrics and model.)*  

> **Leading indicators** (monitor during incident; predictive of hidden risk/resilience):

> 2. **Snapshot Validity Compliance**: % of picks using a snapshot 4 hours old (i.e., snapshot printed at shift start used for picks within that shift). *Target: 90%.* *Why leading:* >4h snapshot use predicts inventory staleness  traceability mismatches (D risk); easy to track at pick. *Data source:* Snapshot print time stamp on pick list vs pick time.  
> 3. **Temp/Dual-Log Presence Rate**: % of Cold-chain and Controlled-substance orders with Paper Temp Log or Paper Dual-Control Log signed at pick (within <5 min of pick). *Target: 98%.* *Why leading:* Missing log predicts cold-chain doc missing spike (4.8% T2) and controlled missing (2.9% T2); detects G/F risk early. *Data source:* Log signature field on pick list; supervisor checklist at pick.  
> 4. **Backlog Notification Rate**: % of Bulk orders deferred (capacity >80%) where pharmacy customer received notification within 2 hours of defer decision. *Target: 100%.* *Why leading:* Low rate predicts pharmacy dissatisfaction/cancellations (7.8% T2); simple to verify via email/call log. *Data source:* Notification log timestamp vs defer decision time.  
> 5. **Reconciliation Mini-Check Pass Rate**: % of picks where Reconciliation Mini-Check ("all yes" for snapshot validity, logs signed) passes without flag at pick time. *Target: 95%.* *Why leading:* Low pass rate predicts mismatches needing later audit (K/L tail); done at pick, so predicts hidden workload. *Data source:* Mini-check form "Pass/Flag" field.  

> **Lagging indicators** (measure at stabilization/end of incident; confirm resilience/outcome):  
> 1. **Hospital-critical Fill Rate**: % of Hospital-critical orders dispatched/delivered within baseline median cycle time (8h) or +2h buffer during incident. *Target: 90% by Day 3.* *Why lagging:* Directly reflects service protection mission; compares to observed 91% T2 (good) vs baseline ~95%. *Data source: Order status vs dispatch time.*  
> 2. **Traceability Exception Rate**: % of orders with missing scan, temp doc, or auth record *at dispatch time* (i.e., after Reconciliation Mini-Check; no post-invoice count). *Target: 1.0% by Day 6.* *Why lagging:* Matches baseline 0.8% and T3/T4 recovery; directly measures concealed risk elimination. *Data source: Reconciliation Scan flag count / total orders.* (Excludes post-invoice fixes; only counts flags at dispatch.)  
> 3. **Control Completeness Score**: Weighted average of (Temp Log signed % for Cold + Dual-Control Log signed % for Controlled) at pick. *Target: 99% by Day 3.* *Why lagging:* Measures core control integrity; baseline 0.2%/0.4% missing, T2 saw 2.9%/4.8% missing  target avoids spike. *Data source: Log signature verification.*  
> 4. **Backlog Age (Days)**: Maximum age (days) of any order in Backlog Queue (Bulk Hold) from receipt to dispatch start. *Target: 2 days by Day 4.* *Why lagging:* Reflects prioritization fairness; baseline bulk median 36h (~1.5 days); T2 bulk p90 180h (~7.5 days) due to deferral without notice. *Data source: Order receipt date vs dispatch start date.*

> *Indicator rationale per constraints:*  
> - Leading indicators are **real-time, workflow-embedded** (log timestamps, checklist fields), not retrospective (no reliance on audit after invoice). They predict hidden risk (e.g., Auth-Check Completion Rate <95%  likely skip attempt).
