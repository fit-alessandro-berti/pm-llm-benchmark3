## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

I treat a repair as a hypothesis about missing relations, not as a completion requirement. “Confidence” below is a comparative judgment about an individual candidate given this log; it is not a calibrated posterior probability. Existing relations remain in place in both bundles.

- **Hard invariant:** Every added E2O endpoint must name an existing event and object; every O2O endpoint must name two existing objects. Do not duplicate an existing relation. O2O direction and qualifier matter: `DEL-Y carries PK-8` is distinct from a reversed edge. These are structural requirements for the proposed data.
- **Strong default:** Event activities have the stated usual cardinalities and lifecycle order: an item is added before packing, a package is packed before dispatch, and a delivery is confirmed after dispatch. One `Pack Item` row usually has one Item and one Package; one `Dispatch Package` row usually has one Package and one Delivery; one payment event normally has one Payment and one Invoice. These defaults can be broken by the specified outages, bulk dispatches, and unapplied cash.
- **Strong default:** A specific identifier match is substantial evidence. `PK8-2` points toward PK-8, and `N-421?` points toward DEL-Y's `N-421`. The question mark lowers confidence in the latter. Existing O2O links are useful background structure, but an O2O edge inferred from the same event cannot then independently validate that event.
- **Weak heuristic:** Shared customer C17, NORTH route, 10:00 dispatch time, `N-42*` prefix, invoice total 230 versus order values 130 + 100, and payment amounts 100 + 130 all increase plausibility. None uniquely determines an edge. A value may include fees or discounts, and a payment may remain unapplied.
- **Evidence against a repair:** A proposed edge that gives an event an unexplained extra object, points against a more specific identifier, puts confirmation before dispatch, or turns one package into two deliveries without a transfer record deserves a penalty. DEL-X's `N-420` is evidence against assigning ev10's `N-421?` to DEL-X. The existence of DEL-Y is not itself evidence that every event must be attached to it.

I combine evidence by checking endpoints and direction first, then lifecycle and normal cardinality, then the independent identifier, customer, route, time, and amount clues. I compare complete bundles because shared edges change interpretation: a multi-delivery confirmation and a two-order invoice can be coherent together without either being compelled. I do not add confidence scores as though correlated clues were independent; `N-421?` and `N-421`, for example, express one identifier match.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A — conservative/minimum-change repair (selected).** Add P01–P04 only. Ev08 is a second pack operation into PK-8, so PK-8 can contain IT-A2 from ORD-A and IT-B1 from ORD-B. This mixed-order package is unusual but permitted by the observed scanner reference; it is not a reason to force the orders onto one invoice or delivery acknowledgment. Ev10 dispatches PK-8 with DEL-Y; DEL-X remains linked to PK-7 and ev09. Ev11 stays related only to ORD-A: the vague `N-42*` and portal customer do not distinguish DEL-X from DEL-Y or prove that both were acknowledged. INV-44 remains observed as billing ORD-A only, even though its total exceeds ORD-A's declared value. PAY-2 remains a recorded Payment without an Invoice relation. The result has coherent partial lifecycles: each package has a pack and dispatch, while neither delivery has a recorded confirmation; ORD-B has no observed invoice; PAY-2 may be unapplied. Those gaps are acceptable.

**Bundle B — consolidated acknowledgment and finance, with a scanner outage.** Leave ev08 item-only and do not add `PK-8 packs IT-B1`; the `PK8-2` reference is interpreted as a stale or unconfirmed scanner value. Keep P03–P04, because `N-421?` still favors DEL-Y over DEL-X; do not attach ev10 to DEL-X. Add P05 and P06 together, interpreting ev11's `N-42*` as one customer portal acknowledgment of both NORTH deliveries. Its existing ORD-A relation is retained. Add P07–P10: the invoice bills both ORD-A and ORD-B, and PAY-2 is applied to INV-44. This forms a coherent consolidated financial lifecycle: two orders on one invoice, followed by two recorded payments. It also gives each delivery a dispatch and confirmation. IT-B1's Pack Item event still lacks a Package link, however; the stated scanner outage makes that a defensible partial lifecycle. Bundle B does not silently infer `ev11 → ORD-B` merely because the invoice covers ORD-B, and it does not assert that both delivery confirmations or the amount equalities are proved.

A further plausible variant of B would attach ev11 to only DEL-X, since ORD-A is already on ev11 and DEL-X carries one ORD-A item. That variant yields an unconfirmed DEL-Y and relies on a choice between two matching carrier prefixes. It is weaker than the group interpretation if the portal acknowledged all deliveries together, but the log cannot eliminate it.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

**Order.** In A, ORD-A has creation, two Add Item events, the order-only confirmation, and invoice issue. ORD-B has creation and one Add Item event, with no invoice or explicit delivery acknowledgment. Neither is an orphan. The apparent ORD-A confirmation is not proof of which delivery was confirmed. B gives ORD-B an invoice event but still no order-level confirmation; one invoice billing two orders is allowed by the domain. Adding ORD-B to ev11 solely to complete its lifecycle would manufacture evidence.

**Item.** IT-A1 and IT-A2 have Add Item and Pack Item events with observed Order and Package O2O links. IT-B1 has Add Item and Pack Item events; A associates its ev08 pack with PK-8 and adds the directional `PK-8 packs IT-B1` link. The resulting package spans two orders. B leaves the item-only ev08 as an unresolved scanner outage, so IT-B1 lacks a known Package even though its Pack Item event exists. None of these Item events is orphaned.

**Package.** PK-7 has ev06 then ev09; PK-8 has ev07, ev08, then ev10 in A. Two Pack Item events for PK-8 are normal if it contains two items, and the two orders do not make the package invalid. B gives PK-8 only ev07 then ev10 and treats ev08's package as unknown. Both packages have a dispatch event. The added PK-8–IT-B1 O2O edge in A is derived from P01 plus ev08's observed IT-B1 relation; it must not be counted as a second, independent scan.

**Delivery.** DEL-X has observed ev09 and `carries PK-7`. DEL-Y gains ev10 and `carries PK-8` in A. Neither has an E2O confirmation in A; this is missing information, not an orphan delivery. B relates ev11 to both deliveries, creating a one-event-to-two-deliveries acknowledgment that the domain explicitly permits. The new DEL-Y carry edge is inferred from P03 and the observed PK-8 on ev10, not independently verified. Existing DEL-X/PK-7 relations, by contrast, are separately present as E2O and O2O records, though their common source system is unknown.

**Invoice.** INV-44 has ev12 and the observed `bills ORD-A` relation. A leaves its 230 total unexplained by the stated 130 order value; fees, taxes, discounts, or incomplete relations could account for it. B adds ORD-B to both ev12 and the bill relation, which is a permitted one-invoice-to-many-orders structure. P08 cannot serve as independent support for P07 because it was inferred from the same consolidated-bill hypothesis.

**Payment.** PAY-1 has ev13 and the observed `settles INV-44` edge. PAY-2 has ev14, so it is not an orphan object or orphan event, but A leaves the cash unapplied. B adds ev14–INV-44 and `PAY-2 settles INV-44`; two payments on one invoice are plausible. The added O2O payment edge follows the E2O hypothesis and cannot independently confirm it. No amount equality establishes settlement or rules out credits and fees.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**OC-DFG.** The unrepaired Package projection has PK-7 `Pack Item → Dispatch Package` and PK-8 `Pack Item → Dispatch Package`. A inserts ev08 into PK-8, yielding `Pack Item → Pack Item → Dispatch Package`; B retains the original PK-8 sequence. The Delivery projection has only DEL-X's `Dispatch Package` in the unrepaired log, adds a single dispatch trace for DEL-Y in A, and adds `Dispatch Package → Confirm Delivery` for both deliveries in B. ORD-A's observed `Create Order → Add Item → Add Item → Confirm Delivery → Issue Invoice` sequence is unchanged. B adds `Issue Invoice` to ORD-B after its Add Item event. The Invoice projection gains a second `Record Payment` step in B; A retains one. Edge counts and performance statistics can change even where the activity vocabulary does not.

**OTG and ET–OT graph.** The observed O2O data already contains the type pairs Order–Item, Package–Item, Delivery–Package, Invoice–Order, and Payment–Invoice. Thus A and B need not create any new *type pair* in an OTG based on O2O relations, but A strengthens Package–Item and Delivery–Package instance support, while B strengthens Invoice–Order and Payment–Invoice. An OTG implementation based on event co-occurrence may compute different weights; its edge definition should be checked before interpreting a changed weight. In the event-type to object-type graph, A increases support for `Pack Item → Package` and `Dispatch Package → Delivery`, which already exist. B introduces `Confirm Delivery → Delivery` and increases support for `Issue Invoice → Order` and `Record Payment → Invoice`. B's one ev11 contributes two Delivery incidences, not two new confirmation events.

**Flattenings.** Flattening on Order from direct E2O relations leaves A's two order traces as observed; B adds ev12 to ORD-B. It does not automatically copy package, delivery, invoice-payment, or O2O events into an Order trace. Package flattening exposes A's extra ev08 on PK-8; B's PK-8 trace stays `ev07, ev10`. Delivery flattening has an empty DEL-Y trace before repair, `ev10` in A, and `ev10, ev11` in B; DEL-X gains ev11 only in B. The shared ev11 appears once in each B delivery trace. Invoice flattening appends ev14 after ev13 only in B. Payment flattening still has one Record Payment event per Payment in all three logs, because PAY-2 already relates to ev14. Direct E2O flattening does not turn the O2O settlement edge into another Payment event.

**Frequencies and clustering.** There are always 14 distinct events: Create Order 2, Add Item 3, Pack Item 3, Dispatch Package 2, Confirm Delivery 1, Issue Invoice 1, and Record Payment 2. Direct E2O incidences rise from 22 unrepaired to 24 in A and 27 in B; distinct objects touched by an event rise from 11 to 12 when DEL-Y is attached. Counts of unique objects per activity can stay fixed while incidences rise: A makes Pack Item touch PK-8 twice, and B makes Record Payment touch INV-44 twice. A makes PK-8 a bridge between ORD-A and ORD-B, which can pull their neighborhoods together in equivalent-execution clustering around Order if the method follows O2O paths. B removes that bridge but adds a shared invoice; both can cause more similar or merged order contexts. Direct Order flattening, however, still distinguishes ORD-A's two Add Item events and confirmation from ORD-B's one Add Item event. Report whether a clustering method follows E2O only or also inferred O2O paths before explaining a changed cluster.

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

The code uses Bundle A and assumes standard OCEL column names. Its constructor line is explicitly version-dependent. It deliberately omits confidence and justification from native relation rows, while keeping those values in the reasoning artifact above. The code checks structure, not business truth.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Preserve the input tables for comparison with every repair bundle.
events_orig = events.copy(deep=True)
objects_orig = objects.copy(deep=True)
relations_orig = relations.copy(deep=True)
o2o_orig = o2o.copy(deep=True)

assert events_orig["ocel:eid"].is_unique
assert objects_orig["ocel:oid"].is_unique
assert {"ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"} <= set(relations_orig)
assert {"ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"} <= set(o2o_orig)

event_by_id = events_orig.set_index("ocel:eid")
object_by_id = objects_orig.set_index("ocel:oid")

def e2o_row(eid, oid, qualifier):
    assert eid in event_by_id.index and oid in object_by_id.index
    values = {
        "ocel:eid": eid,
        "ocel:oid": oid,
        "ocel:qualifier": qualifier,
        "ocel:activity": event_by_id.at[eid, "ocel:activity"],
        "ocel:timestamp": event_by_id.at[eid, "ocel:timestamp"],
        "ocel:type": object_by_id.at[oid, "ocel:type"],
    }
    return {col: values.get(col, pd.NA) for col in relations_orig.columns}

e2o_new = [
    e2o_row("ev08", "PK-8", "package"),
    e2o_row("ev10", "DEL-Y", "delivery"),
]
o2o_new = [
    {"ocel:oid": "PK-8", "ocel:oid_2": "IT-B1", "ocel:qualifier": "packs"},
    {"ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:oid_2": "PK-8", "ocel:qualifier": "carries"},
]

events_repaired = events_orig.copy(deep=True)
objects_repaired = objects_orig.copy(deep=True)
relations_repaired = pd.concat(
    [relations_orig, pd.DataFrame(e2o_new, columns=relations_orig.columns)],
    ignore_index=True,
)
o2o_repaired = pd.concat(
    [o2o_orig, pd.DataFrame(o2o_new, columns=o2o_orig.columns)],
    ignore_index=True,
)

assert set(relations_repaired["ocel:eid"]) <= set(events_repaired["ocel:eid"])
assert set(relations_repaired["ocel:oid"]) <= set(objects_repaired["ocel:oid"])
assert set(o2o_repaired["ocel:oid"]) <= set(objects_repaired["ocel:oid"])
assert set(o2o_repaired["ocel:oid_2"]) <= set(objects_repaired["ocel:oid"])
assert not relations_repaired[
    ["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"]
].duplicated().any()
assert not o2o_repaired[
    ["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"]
].duplicated().any()
assert not (o2o_repaired["ocel:oid"] == o2o_repaired["ocel:oid_2"]).any()

from pm4py.objects.ocel.obj import OCEL
# Version-dependent constructor/import: adapt argument names to installed PM4Py.
ocel_repaired = OCEL(
    events=events_repaired,
    objects=objects_repaired,
    relations=relations_repaired,
    o2o=o2o_repaired,
)

ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel_repaired)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel_repaired)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel_repaired)
order_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel_repaired, "Order")
delivery_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel_repaired, "Delivery")
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. Assigning ev11 uniquely to DEL-X because ORD-A is observed on the event would erase the equally compatible DEL-Y or group-acknowledgment interpretations.
2. Assigning ev11 to both deliveries without marking it provisional would duplicate one confirmation across two Delivery flattenings and inflate delivery-confirmation coverage.
3. Treating `230 = 130 + 100` as proof that INV-44 bills ORD-B would create a shared invoice and may falsely merge the Order neighborhoods; fees or adjustments are possible.
4. Treating PAY-2's 130 as proof of settlement would turn potentially unapplied cash into a paid invoice and add a false `Record Payment → Record Payment` Invoice fragment.
5. Linking ev10 to DEL-X merely because it shares the 10:00 timestamp and NORTH route with ev09 would give DEL-X two dispatches while disregarding the closer `N-421?`/DEL-Y match.
6. Adding PK-7 to ev10 as a bulk dispatch would duplicate its observed ev09 dispatch and invent a multi-package event unsupported by ev10's recorded PK-8.
7. Assuming PK-8 cannot mix orders and moving IT-B1 to PK-7 would contradict `PK8-2` and fabricate a PK-7 pack operation for IT-B1.
8. Inferring `ev11 → ORD-B` through A's shared package or B's shared invoice would convert a graph path into a direct event observation. That can falsely add a confirmation to ORD-B's Order trace.
9. Counting P02 as independent support for P01, P04 for P03, P08 for P07, or P10 for P09 would double-count one hypothesis and overstate confidence.
