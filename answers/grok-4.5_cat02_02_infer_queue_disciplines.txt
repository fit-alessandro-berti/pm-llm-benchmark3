# 2. Reconstructed dispatch decisions

Assumption A1: a job is “plausibly waiting” if its recorded arrival is at least one minute before the decision instant (allowing up to two minutes of scan delay only when it changes the eligible set).  
Assumption A2: preemption/resume decisions are decision opportunities.  
Assumption A3: appointment eligibility is treated as “due at the stated time” with a short tolerance (about 0–5 minutes), not as a wide window, unless noted.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

## QA
- **Supports appointment+FIFO:** A03 at 08:20 over A02 and A04; A06 at 09:20 when due; A02 before A04; A05 before A08; A07 before A08; no preemption across the morning; calibration gap respected before A04.
- **Challenges:** A06 starts at 09:20 vs appointment 09:15 (explainable: A05 occupied A1 until 09:20—non-preemptive appointment, not a policy violation). A04 and A08 are high priority yet delayed (contradicts pure priority, not appt+FIFO).
- **Undocumented exception needed?** No strong one if primary is appointment+FIFO. Pure priority or pure SPT would need multiple ad-hoc excuses.

## QB
- **Supports preemptive priority / urgent pri-1:** B02 preempts B01; B05 runs before B04 and before remaining B01; interruption reason text (“urgent arrival”).
- **Supports resume-interrupted hybrid:** B01 resumed at 09:31 (over B03) and at 10:14 (over B06 and short B07).
- **Challenges:** 09:48 selection of B03 while B05 (pri 1) exists—unless scan delay hides B05 or dispatcher override is accepted as labeled. 10:28 B04 before B06 (pri 2)—clear priority violation; fits FIFO after the interrupted job is cleared. B07 appointment missed by ~26 minutes—appointment is not an active discipline here.
- **Exceptions required:** at least the labeled dispatcher reassignment; possibly pri-1-only preemption (B05 did not preempt B01 in the 09:46–09:48 window); resume-stack not in the candidate list as a named pure mechanism.

## QC
- **Supports SEPT:** C02 first among four waiting jobs; C03 next; C06 before C01; note that oldest initial job finished last among that backlog.
- **Supports conditional SLA override:** C05 (pri 1, deadline 10:50) eventually jumped the residual backlog after its deadline was already at risk.
- **Challenges:** C04 at 10:32 over C06 (est 6) and over deadline-critical C05—violates live SEPT, priority, and EDF simultaneously. C01 at 11:40 over C07 (higher priority, shorter est, earlier deadline)—only FIFO/aging explains it.
- **Exceptions required:** either (i) SEPT frozen on the opening backlog sequence with later insertions handled differently, or (ii) policy shift / anti-starvation when C01 waited too long, or (iii) noisy/strategic `est_min` (not evidenced beyond the contradiction itself).

## QD
- **Supports skill-based routing:** no resource ever runs a job outside its skills; D05 only on D-F; D-M sequence is purely mechanical; D07 (mech+electrical) on D-X; parallel start of D01 and D02 on different resources.
- **Supports appointment + per-resource FIFO:** D06 held until 11:30 on D-X; D04 before D08; D07 before D10; D03 before D05 on D-F.
- **Challenges priority/SPT as primary:** D08 (pri 1, est 8) waits behind D04; D10 (pri 1, est 12) waits behind D07; D-F idle while D10 could run.
- **Exceptions / structural ambiguity:** D-F not taking D10 may be (a) arrival-time binding assignment to D-X, (b) deliberate reservation for D05 after the meeting, or (c) an unrecorded preference—only (a)/(b) keep skill-based+FIFO intact without calling it a violation.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

## Episode A — QB at 10:28 (B01 finally completes)
**Competing explanations**
1. **Hybrid preemptive-priority with resume, then FIFO among residual jobs**  next = B04.  
2. **Standard preemptive/non-preemptive priority (no FIFO fallback)**  next = B06 (pri 2).

**Predictions already diverge on the observed choice** (B04 was taken, favoring 1).  
**Additional observable that would discriminate going forward:** at the next free instant, record the controller’s ranked eligibility list and whether `priority` is consulted only when `urgency_flag=true` / `priority==1`. If a new pri-2 arrives while a pri-3 is mid-service and is **not** preempted, and later loses to an older pri-3, that confirms pri-1-only urgency + FIFO residual rather than full priority scheduling.

## Episode B — QC at 10:32 (after C03)
**Competing explanations**
1. **Live SEPT recomputed each free moment**  next = C06 (est 6).  
2. **Frozen SEPT order on the opening backlog (C02,C03,C04,C01) with new arrivals deferred until that sequence’s next member**  next = C04.  
3. **Priority or EDF**  next = C05.

Observed = C04, which matches (2) only.  
**Additional observable:** `dispatch_decision_time` plus `est_min` (and remaining estimate) **of every waiting job at that instant**, and a policy-version or “backlog snapshot id.” If the system still stores an ordered initial-backlog plan and ignores newer shorter jobs until that plan advances, (2) is confirmed; if C06 was absent from the eligibility set due to scan lag or skill filter, the contradiction dissolves without freezing.

## Episode C — QD at 11:36 (D-F free; cross-check with QB/QC ambiguity style)
**Competing explanations**
1. **Open skill pool (any capable free resource may take any eligible job)**  D-F should start D10.  
2. **Arrival-time assignment / sticky routing to one capable resource** (D10 bound to D-X) **or reservation for D05**  D-F stays idle.

Observed idle period favors (2).  
**Additional observable:** `assigned_resource` at queue-entry (not only at service start), or an eligibility-set snapshot showing whether D10 was offered to D-F. A single field `routing_mode  {pool, sticky}` would separate skill-based pooling from skill-based dedicated queues.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked most plausible first:

1. **Labeled manual override (dispatcher reassignment on QB at 09:48)**  
   Plausibility: high—explicitly in the data.  
   Effect: removes the B03-over-B05 event from the automatic-policy likelihood; QB’s automatic policy can stay closer to pri-1 preemption + resume + FIFO.

2. **Sticky skill assignment / non-pooled routing on QD (and D-F reservation around the meeting)**  
   Plausibility: high—explains D-F idling with D10 waiting and D05’s exclusive path without abandoning skill-based routing.  
   Effect: strengthens QD as skill-based + per-resource FIFO + appointments; weakens any claim of global SPT/priority across QD.

3. **Arrival-scan delay (up to 2 minutes) and one-minute timestamp granularity**  
   Plausibility: medium-high (stated measurement property).  
   Effect: can soften QB 09:48 (B05 arrived 09:46 may be barely visible) and slight gaps (08:30 vs 08:32 on QA). Cannot explain multi-step priority inversions (QA 09:05 A05 vs A08; QD D04 vs D08; QC C01 vs C07).

4. **Non-preemptive appointment / non-preemptive service with resume cost (QA, QB, QD)**  
   Plausibility: high as a structural constraint, not a one-off exception.  
   Effect: late A06 and non-preemption of low-priority work are expected; favors hybrid designs over pure preemptive priority on QA/QD. On QB it rationalizes resume-B01 behavior if context-switch cost is charged.

5. **SLA-triggered policy switch (especially QC)**  
   Plausibility: medium—task allows it; C05 note says deadline already at risk when selected.  
   Effect: QC may be SEPT in normal mode and priority/EDF when a pri-1 deadline is blown; still does not justify C01C07 unless aging is part of the switch.

6. **Noisy or strategic `est_min` (SEPT mis-identification)**  
   Plausibility: low-medium—no independent realized-vs-estimate audit table, though realized lengths sometimes differ (e.g., B01 segments).  
   Effect: if est_min is unreliable, QC’s SEPT support weakens and “undocumented hybrid” rises; QA/QD diagnoses (appointment, FIFO, skills) barely change because they did not rely on est ordering.

7. **Data-quality: missing interruption reasons / unrecorded calibration-like breaks**  
   Plausibility: medium for small gaps; low as a global alternative.  
   Effect: could invent hidden preemptions, but would be speculative; should not overturn skill constraints or appointment hits that are positively evidenced.

8. **Pure priority or pure SPT as the sole rule on QA or QD**  
   Plausibility: low given repeated counterexamples.  
   Effect: only tenable with many stacked exceptions; inferior to the hybrids above.

# 6. Additional event data needed

Priority order tailored to remaining ambiguities:

1. **Dispatch-decision timestamp** (distinct from service start) and **agent/system id** that made the choice.  
2. **Eligibility set at decision time** (job ids considered), including jobs filtered out for skill, appointment hold, or sticky assignment.  
3. **Resource state vector** at decision: available/busy/reserved/in-meeting/calibrating, and **remaining time to next known unavailability**.  
4. **Queue-entry / visibility time** (scan time) separate from true arrival; flag if delay &gt; 0.  
5. **Appointment tolerance window** (early/late allowances) and whether the job was in “appointment held” state.  
6. **Preemption flag, preempting job id, resume policy, and preemption reason code** (urgent pri-1, dispatcher, SLA, failure)—especially for QB.  
7. **Routing mode and assigned_resource at entry** for multi-skilled queues (pool vs sticky); required skill expression evaluated true/false per resource.  
8. **Current estimated remaining processing time** at each decision (not only arrival `est_min`), plus realized segment lengths already consumed.  
9. **Deadline/SLA state** (slack, breached yes/no, policy mode normal vs endangered).  
10. **Priority aging / wait-time weight** if any anti-starvation exists (QC C01).  
11. **Policy version or rule-set id** active at the decision (to detect mid-day SLA switches).  
12. **Manual override flag** and operator id (to separate QB-like reassignments from automatic discipline).

Collection plan: instrument QD and QB first (routing eligibility + preemption/resume), then QC (estimates, slack, aging), then QA (appointment windows)—because QA is already the most coherent with current fields.

# 7. Final synthesis

- **Most identifiable: QD.** Skill constraints repeatedly bind, appointments hold a resource idle, and within-resource order matches FIFO while systematically violating global priority and SPT. The residual ambiguity is pool vs sticky assignment, not the primary discipline family.  
- **Next most identifiable: QA.** Appointment-when-due plus FIFO non-preemptive service explains the forks; pure priority and pure SPT are directly refuted by A02A04 and A05A08.  
- **Least identifiable: QC.** Opening backlog looks like SEPT, C05 looks like a late SLA/priority override, yet C04C06 and C01C07 reintroduce frozen-order, aging, or undocumented hybrid explanations with similar fit.  
- **QB is intermediate but fragile:** one clear pri-1 preemption anchors “preemptive priority,” but resume-stack behavior, a labeled dispatcher override, a missed appointment, and a late FIFO segment mean a **claimed clean preemptive-priority policy would be the most dangerous inference** from these observations alone—management could “confirm” priority while the live rule is actually urgent-preempt + resume + FIFO with manual edits.

Cross-queue pattern: **documentation-missing disciplines are hybrids**. Appointments and skills locally dominate when present; priority is decisive mainly as **preemptive urgency (QB)** or **late SLA rescue (QC)** rather than as a global sort key; SEPT is plausible only on QC and even there is not stable across all decision opportunities.
