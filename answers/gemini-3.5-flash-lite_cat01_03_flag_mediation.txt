## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

| estimid ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| EST-01 | Set flag=1 vs flag=0 (P0 vs P1) | All cases at registration | Total cycle time | Total policy effect | Yes (if policy is clear) | Unobserved underlying risk confounding flag status |
| EST-02 | Priority lane active vs standard queue | Cases with risk_score $\ge$ threshold | Triage completion duration | Controlled direct effect on triage | Yes | Correlation between risk score, arrival load, and triage resource speed |
| EST-03 | Mandatory review triggered vs bypassed | Flagged cases (`flag=1`) | Manual review occurrence indicator | Natural indirect effect component | No (underlying risk confounds reviewer discretion) | Treatment-confounder feedback between queue state and manual review |
| EST-04 | Shared manual queue vs dedicated queue | Flagged cases in high-load windows | Manual queue waiting time | Interventional indirect effect via queue | Yes | Interference across cases sharing the same manual review resource |
| EST-05 | Escalation to senior team vs direct close | Cases reaching manual review | Escalation occurrence indicator | Controlled direct effect | Yes | Complexity and human judgment endogeneity |
| EST-06 | P0 vs P1 (Full policy counterfactual) | All cases | Total cycle time | Total interventional effect | Conditional on policy definition | Unobserved confounders (`U_risk`, reviewer fatigue) |
| EST-07 | P0 vs P2 (Remove mandatory review) | Flagged cases | Final decision outcome (Approve/Reject) | Total effect on decision quality | Yes | Missing counterfactual decisions for unreviewed cases |

*Framework note:* We use **Interventional Analogues** and **Controlled Direct Effects (CDE)** rather than pure Natural Direct/Indirect Effects (NDEs/NIEs). Pure path-specific effects fail here because the downstream mediators (manual review queues, escalation decisions) are subject to intermediate confounders (queue wait times, resource contention, and reviewer discretion) that are themselves affected by prior treatments (priority triage timing and arrival load).

**Interference, exposure-induced confounding, and treatment-confounder feedback:**
Interference occurs because manual review and escalation queues are shared (`review_resource` M1, M2, M3 and senior escalation team). A surge of flagged cases arriving concurrently increases `manual_queue_wait` and `escalation_queue_wait` for *subsequent* or *concurrent* cases, violating the Stable Unit Treatment Value Assumption (SUTVA). Treatment-confounder feedback appears when system congestion (`arrival_load` and dynamic queue lengths) affects both the likelihood of routing a case to manual review/escalation and the subsequent service times due to reviewer fatigue or triage prioritization.

*Handling absent stages:* Stages that do not occur (e.g., manual service for bypassed cases, escalation for unescalated cases) are denoted as `NA` and are **excluded** from the calculation of conditional averages for that specific stage. They are not assigned a value of zero, which would artificially deflate true waiting or service metrics.

### Mechanistic interpretation of observed contrasts
The data reveals a striking paradox: **flagged cases receive faster initial triage** (arrival-to-triage start is 19.0m vs 138.0m for unflagged; triage service is 9.0m vs 54.0m), yet they **suffer dramatically longer total cycle times** (1083.0m vs 279.0m). 

These contrasts are **not automatically causal**. The acceleration at triage is a direct artifact of the priority lane policy for `flag=1`. However, the downstream delays (manual review service of 230m vs 180m, and escalation adding ~300m) are heavily confounded by case mix (`complexity=High` cases are disproportionately flagged) and queue congestion. The flag acts as a routing funnel that concentrates complex, heavy-work cases into scarce manual and senior queues, creating bottlenecks that swamp the initial time savings.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Local threshold design (Regression Discontinuity) | Compare cases just above and just below the risk score flag threshold | `risk_score`, `complexity`, `arrival_load` | Continuity of potential outcomes around the flag cutoff | Check density of risk scores around threshold for manipulation (McCrary test) | Discontinuity in baseline case complexity at threshold invalidates design |
| Queue-state-aware analysis | Stratify completion time by queue length (active cases) at arrival | Queue backlog count, `arrival_load`, `complexity` | Markovian queue assumptions; no unobserved arrival clustering | Test independence of arrival time and prior queue state | Queue state strongly predicts arrival time (batch arrivals) |
| Policy change / Natural experiment | Compare metrics before and after temporary suspension of mandatory review | `complexity`, time-of-day fixed effects | Parallel trends in unobserved case risk across periods | Placebo test on pre-intervention periods | Pre-trends show significant divergence between flag groups |
| Mediation-oriented analysis | Marginal Structural Models (MSMs) with inverse probability weighting | Time-varying confounders (`arrival_load`, queue size) | Sequential ignorability; no unmeasured mediator-outcome confounders | Estimate effect on negative control outcome (e.g., triage service time for unflagged) | Significant effect found on negative control (indicates residual confounding) |
| Negative-control outcome | Analyze triage service time as outcome for flag assignment | `risk_score`, `complexity` | Triage service time cannot be causally affected by manual review | Check if flag predicts triage service time after adjusting for priority lane | Flag remains predictive of triage service even outside priority lane |
| Sensitivity analysis for unobserved risk | E-value / Rosenbaum bounds for hidden confounding | Unobserved risk `U_risk` | Bounded confounding strength | Test robustness to varying strengths of unobserved risk | A small unmeasured confounder completely eliminates the estimated total effect |
| Interference-aware design | Cluster standard errors by arrival hour / shift cohorts | Shift identifiers, concurrent queue load | Interference is bounded within temporal/shift clusters | Compare spatial/temporal spillover decay across shift boundaries | Spillovers extend far beyond shift boundaries, violating cluster independence |

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **P0 (Current system)** | Fast for flagged (priority lane) | Severe bottleneck at manual & escalation queues | Long total cycle time (1083m for flag=1) | Low (high scrutiny on risk) | High across shared review resources | High (observed baseline) | Reject (status quo creates severe bottlenecks) |
| **P1 (Suppress display)** | Neutral / standard queue for all | Eliminates flag-driven downstream congestion | Reduced total cycle time for high-risk cases | High (misses high-risk signals) | Low | Medium (counterfactual extrapolation) | Reject (sacrifices risk detection for speed) |
| **P2 (Remove mandatory review)** | Fast for flagged | Relieves manual queue pressure | Significantly shorter cycle time | Moderate-High (relies on imperfect `risk_score`) | Moderate | Medium (requires pilot) | **Select for Pilot** (balances speed & capacity) |
| **P3 (Protected capacity)** | Fast for flagged | Isolated queues prevent cross-case waiting | Moderate cycle time | Low | Low (isolated resources) | Low (requires staffing expansion) | Consider long-term if budget permits |
| **P4 (Modify escalation)** | Fast for flagged | Reduces senior queue backlog | Moderate-Short cycle time | Moderate (risk of false negatives at senior tier) | Moderate | Low | Consider secondary optimization |

### Limited Pilot Specification (Policy P2: Retain priority triage, remove automatic mandatory manual review)
* **Eligibility:** All incoming cases with `flag = 1` and `complexity` in {Low, Medium}. High-complexity flagged cases remain under P0 mandatory review.
* **Randomization or Assignment Rule:** Alternating case assignment (odd/even case IDs) or randomized digital coin flip at intake: 50% of eligible flagged cases bypass mandatory manual review and go directly to close (unless human triage officer overrides based on qualitative notes); 50% follow P0.
* **Queue-Capacity Guardrail:** If manual review queue waiting time exceeds 180 minutes, automatically pause the bypass rule for 2 hours to protect quality thresholds.
* **Primary and Secondary Outcomes:** 
  * *Primary:* Total case cycle time (Registration to Close).
  * *Secondary:* Final decision outcome agreement rate and downstream defect/appeal rate.
* **Minimum Process Data Needed:** Timestamps for registration, triage start/done, manual review start/done (if applicable), close, along with resource IDs and case complexity tags.
* **Stopping Conditions:** Immediate termination of the pilot if the proportion of rejected/defective cases in the bypass group increases by more than 15% relative to the control group over a rolling window of 50 cases.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk:** If unobserved case risk (`U_risk`) is negatively correlated with the recorded `risk_score` (e.g., sophisticated fraud evades the risk model), suppressing the flag or bypassing manual review would increase catastrophic default rates, reversing the recommendation for Policy P2.
2. **Queue interference:** If manual review and escalation queues operate under extremely low utilization where waiting times are near zero, shared-queue congestion externalities disappear, making downstream delays negligible and invalidating the bottleneck hypothesis.
3. **Decision quality:** If manual review and escalation tiers provide zero marginal detection accuracy (i.e., rubber-stamping approvals), then longer cycle times represent pure administrative waste with no compensatory benefit in compliance or risk mitigation.
4. **Timestamp semantics:** If timestamps record administrative batch-update times rather than actual work start/completion instants, measured service times are artifactual, and acceleration/delay conclusions are reversed.
5. **Positvity / lack of overlap:** If high-complexity cases receive the flag with probability 1 (violating positivity), controlled direct effects cannot be non-parametrically identified across the full complexity spectrum.
6. **Behavior change by reviewers:** If human reviewers experience severe moral hazard or strategic fatigue when presented with a high-risk flag—such as intentionally stalling flagged cases to meet internal workload quotas—then the observed downstream delay is driven by human behavioral adaptation rather than structural queue capacity limits.

## 9. Final synthesis

What is **directly observed** in the event data is a stark structural divergence: cases carrying the high-risk flag (`flag=1`) experience dramatically faster initial triage (averaging 19 minutes to triage start versus 138 minutes for unflagged cases) yet suffer vastly longer total completion times (averaging 1083 minutes versus 279 minutes). 

The most **plausible mechanism** accounting for this paradox is a combination of policy routing and queue saturation: the flag successfully funnels high-risk cases into a priority triage lane, but simultaneously subjects them to mandatory, resource-constrained manual review queues and senior escalation paths shared across the organization. Because flagged cases disproportionately concentrate high-complexity work, they saturate downstream shared resources, creating severe waiting-time backlogs that erase initial triage gains.

What **remains unidentified** is the separable causal effect of the flag net of case complexity and unobserved risk (`U_risk`). Because complexity drives both flag assignment and downstream service duration, and because shared queues induce interference across cases, standard conditional independence assumptions fail. 

The **most useful next evidence** will be generated by implementing **Policy P2 via a randomized pilot** (randomly assigning 50% of eligible flagged cases to bypass mandatory manual review). This pilot directly targets the downstream bottleneck identified in our process analysis, unconfounds review occurrence from underlying risk, and provides immediate empirical data on whether streamlining downstream verification preserves decision quality while safely compressing cycle times.
