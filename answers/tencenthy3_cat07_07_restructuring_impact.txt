4. **Fairness across teams and segments**: East may improve while North/South degrade. Average hides the redistribution; `customer_segment` needing regional accommodation may suffer more handovers.
5. **Learning stagnation**: Local staff routed away from hard cases stop building skill. `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` drops within local teams, signaling future fragility not visible in today’s averages.
6. **Accountability diffusion**: When a shared queue approves, `handover_reason` becomes generic. `discover_subcontracting_network` flattens, making root-cause of errors hard to trace despite stable averages.
7. **Exception handling proliferation**: Stable averages can coexist with new error variants (`get_variants`) where cases bounce between local and shared pools, eroding resilience.

# 5. Pilot design

- **Pilot scope**: Route all `case_complexity=high` and `required_specialty` in {legal, risk, security} from **Team East** and **Team North** to the new Technical Shared Service and shared approval queue. Keep simple eligibility local.
- **Comparison group**: **Team South** continues current embedded model; low-complexity cases in pilot teams act as internal control.
- **Pre/post observation window**: 6 weeks baseline (pre) + 12 weeks pilot (post), with weekly checkpoints.
- **Segmentation**: Analyze by `team` (East vs North vs South), `case_complexity`, `required_specialty`, and `customer_segment` to isolate who wins/loses.
- **Guardrail metrics**:
  - Median shared-queue wait < 5h (Tech), < 4h (Approval)
  - `rework_flag` rate increase < 2pp vs baseline for North
  - East 90th percentile cycle time reduction > 10%
  - SLA miss rate per `customer_segment` within ±1pp of baseline
- **Staff feedback collection**: Monthly structured interviews; annotate `handover_reason` with free-text on clarity; track `local_expertise_flag` usage.
- **Rollback or adjustment triggers**: If median Tech wait > 8h for 2 consecutive weeks, or North `rework_flag` > 12%, or `reassignment_count` rises > 20%, pause shared routing for North and retain local supervisor.

# 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation: Pilot first / modified rollout, not full immediate implementation.**  
The restructuring should proceed as a segmented pilot (e.g., high-complexity and East/North) with strict guardrails. Management should *not* infer success from stable or improved early average cycle time because:
- Early shared-queue volumes may be low (honeymoon effect) before backlog builds.
- Averages mask tail latency for complex cases and fairness shifts between East and North/South.
- Local learning loss and accountability diffusion appear in role/network metrics, not in mean durations.
- `customer_segment` accommodation issues surface in rework and satisfaction, not in median throughput.

Conditional on leading indicators (queue waits, variant stability, resource similarity) staying within guardrails, the model can extend to South. If lagging indicators show fairness decay or rework rise, revert to hybrid local-embedded model.
