## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

**Stipulated (documentary or deterministic):**
- CS-9 is controlled, approved, and in force from 2025-01-01; it requires both checks before activation, manual approval before activation, and requester/approver segregation. It is silent on rejected leads' scope and on reactivation.
- PD-3 was in force 2026-06-01–2026-07-31, is recorded only in minutes/email, and was never incorporated into CS-9 or M0. The Compliance observer disclaimed having approved a weakening of CS-9.
- FD-7 was in force 2026-07-10 00:00–2026-07-12 23:59 and expired automatically.
- The CRM cutover occurred 2026-07-01; notification missingness rose 2%29%; CRM links before migration are mutable text fields.
- SUP-711's duplicate registers share one source transaction ID (deterministic dedup). SUP-713's rename mapping was approved one week after execution, with audit-documented content equivalence. SUP-712's two actors resolve to one human via aliases. SUP-715's reviewer/approver are spouses with no disclosure on record.
- Amounts: SUP-703 €180,000; SUP-704 €18,000; SUP-705 €12,000. Aggregates: 312 auto-approvals (289 eligible, 14 ineligible, 9 unknown); 47 RMI cases with no activation before evidence arrived; 6 post-pilot auto-approvals in early August.

**Disputed or authority-dependent:**
- **P1:** Whether PD-3 validly derogated CS-9 during its window (internal authority contest; observer's retraction).
- **P2:** Whether M0 governs rejected leads (explicitly ambiguous scope document).
- **P3:** Whether reactivation is "onboarding" under CS-9/M0 (silence in both).
- **P4:** Whether a CRM notification satisfies the Notify_Requester obligation when the log event is absent (obligation locus: communication vs. logged event).
- **P5:** Correctness of alias resolution (SUP-712) and HR relationship data (SUP-715) — identity assumptions.
- **P6:** Whether a factually persisting flood extends an expired directive (as a matter of governance law: no; the factual persistence itself is asserted, not independently verified).
- **P7:** Whether SUP-710's prior certification meets the *current* standard's requirements (validity asserted; scope/version unstated).
- **P8:** Whether the 9 missing-attribute auto-approvals were eligible (unproved either way).

**Burden-of-proof approach:**
- *Declaring NONCOMPLIANT* requires four conjunctive conditions: (i) a normative rule in force at event time (versioned); (ii) clear scope coverage of that case type; (iii) reliable evidence of the act/timing (immutable timestamps preferred; corroboration where links are mutable); (iv) no valid overlay excuse. Standard: clear-and-convincing. Scope ambiguity alone defeats an adverse finding and converts the case to a MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT with mandatory remediation.
- *Accepting an exception (JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION)* requires written authority in force at execution time, satisfaction and evidencing of the overlay's own conditions, and in-scope application. Informal belief, verbal assurance, or "imminent" amendments never qualify. Retroactive ratification may mitigate severity but cannot reclassify history.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

**Formal authority.** CS-9 is the only controlled instrument. PD-3 is minutes and email; its own Compliance observer testified she did not approve weakening CS-9. An organization that lets steering-committee email override a controlled standard has no standards. Every auto-approval (SUP-702, 703, 714; 312 in aggregate) rests on contested authority; every provisional activation without pre-completed checks rests on a directive that was born dying (FD-7's automatic expiry).

**Expiry discipline is the tell.** SUP-705 (2026-07-13, one day after FD-7 lapsed) and SUP-714 (2026-08-02, two days after PD-3 lapsed) are not isolated: six post-pilot auto-approvals occurred in the first week of August. This is textbook normalization of deviance — the exception became the practice the moment the boundary passed. Operations' defenses ("the emergency factually continued," "permanent approval was imminent") are precisely the arguments that make expiry clauses meaningless. If factual persistence extended FD-7, no emergency directive would ever end.

**The anchor case.** SUP-703 destroys the defense's "paperwork deviation" narrative: a HIGH-risk, ineligible, new foreign supplier auto-approved, activated before quality review, with a first purchase of €180,000 — during a pilot whose spend discipline was never even claimed to apply. Fourteen clearly ineligible auto-approvals plus nine with missing eligibility attributes mean the eligibility gate failed or was never built. One SUP-703 is the catastrophe the controls exist to prevent.

**Desirable outcomes do not amend policy.** Compliance itself concedes Request_More_Info is desirable — and it is still unauthorized. Forty-seven cases ran an activity that does not exist in the normative model. The remedy for desirable deviation is fast-tracked amendment, not tolerance; otherwise the "desirable" label becomes a self-service amendment channel. Likewise, operations' comfort with auto-approval amends nothing.

**Hidden risk behind apparent conformance.** M0's blindness cuts both ways: SUP-712 (self-approval via aliases) and SUP-715 (undisclosed spousal conflict) are *conformant traces* violating CS-9's resource rule and the CoI disclosure duty. A conformance program that scores these green is worse than none — it launders the risk. And SUP-708 shows the sequencing control bit through: activation 40 minutes before sanctions clearance on a HIGH-risk supplier, flagged by Audit. That no transactions preceded clearance is luck, not control.

**Data integrity collapse undermines the detective layer.** Notification missingness at 29% post-migration means the organization cannot demonstrate closure communications for roughly a third of cases. SUP-706's "evidence" is a mutable text field typed two days later. You cannot run an exceptions-based compliance program on logs this fragile — which is an argument for fixing enforcement, not for leniency.

**Concession (required):** M0 is genuinely defective — it omits CS-9's own resource rule, cannot express the RMI loop, and is silent on reactivation and rejected leads. The prosecution therefore enforces **CS-9's substance**, not M0's letter, wherever M0 is demonstrably incomplete. That concession narrows but does not eliminate the violation set: 703, 705, 708, 712, 714, 715 stand on written rules in force at the time.

## 3. Defense Brief

**M0 cannot be the sole yardstick because it contradicts and under-represents its own source.** CS-9's requester/approver segregation is unmodeled; the RMI loop that occurred in 47 cases (benignly — none activated before evidence arrived) is inexpressible; reactivation and rejected leads are unscoped. A model that cannot state what CS-9 requires, and that brands authorized behavior as deviation, produces a deviation rate that measures *model debt*, not misconduct. Operations' core claim is partially correct and the sample proves it: of 15 cases, at least 5 (702, 704, 707, 709, 710) and arguably 711 and 713 involve no business wrongdoing at all.

**Overlays were real governance acts, properly evidenced.** PD-3 was a steering-committee decision with eligibility fields, a defined window, and a monitoring regime; SUP-702 satisfies every stated condition on complete eligibility data, sanctions-first sequencing, next-day quality, and a CRM notification with a *stable* notification_id. SUP-704 satisfies every FD-7 condition: sanctions before activation, €18,000 under cap, daily monitoring, next-day quality and approval. Punishing these conflates a governance-layer transition with misconduct. The observer's retraction is an intra-authority dispute, not a demonstration that the committee lacked power.

**Concurrency semantics and ordering artifacts.** M0 already declares sanctions and quality concurrent; much of the observed "disorder" (quality after activation) is exactly what PD-3 and FD-7 authorize. Reading M0's letter against overlay-authorized traces manufactures violations.

**The data layer is provably unreliable, and absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.** The CRM migration moved the notification of record out of the onboarding log (N4); a 27-point jump in missingness coincident with cutover is a pipeline defect signature, not a behavioral one (SUP-706). Migration duplicated registrations with identical transaction IDs (SUP-711) — deterministic, repairable, and inflating deviation counts. Vocabulary drifted (QA_Clearance, SUP-713) with audit-certified content equivalence; the defect is a late mapping approval, a metadata-governance lapse of days, not a skipped control.

**Scope is genuinely ambiguous where it matters most for fairness.** CS-9 itself contemplates an abbreviated pre-screen for rejected leads (SUP-709) yet never says those leads are M0-cases; the model owner and process owner disagree, and the scope document is ambiguous. Adverse findings cannot rest on ambiguity. Reactivation (SUP-710) appears in no instrument at all; the actors nonetheless ran a sanctions check and relied on a valid certification — a reasonable good-faith construction of a silent statute.

**Trace deviation  business misconduct.** SUP-708 produced zero transactions before sanctions clearance — the exposure was potential, caught, and closed. SUP-705 spent €12,000 with all substantive safeguards observed. SUP-714's supplier was substantively eligible under the exact criteria whose permanence everyone expected. Risk-proportionate response demands differentiation, not uniform condemnation.

**Concessions (required):** Informal practice cannot self-authorize: SUP-705 and SUP-714 lack any written basis, and "imminent" is not effective. Desirability (SUP-707) requires amendment, not tolerance. No data defect explains SUP-703's ineligibility-plus-€180k, SUP-708's inverted sequencing, or SUP-712's aliasing. Unmodeled resource rules bind anyway — CS-9 wrote them. And the defense cannot explain away fourteen ineligible auto-approvals as model debt; that is an enforcement failure inside the pilot itself.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

**Layer L0 — Governance and scope registry (organizational, not modelable):**
Case-type taxonomy: NEW_ONBOARDING, REACTIVATION, REJECTED_LEAD_INTAKE, WITHDRAWAL. Each carries an explicit governing model reference. Rejected leads are declared intake cases governed by a lightweight pre-screen model (sanctions  reject/escalate). Reactivation is declared in-scope with its own route (sanctions + certification-validity check + manual approval; no re-registration). Withdrawals exit before decision with mandatory notification. Policy version registry with effective/retirement timestamps; overlay registry (pilots, emergencies) each requiring: named authority, written instrument, scope predicate, hard expiry, mandatory ratification review, and an auto-generated incident on any post-expiry use.

**Layer L1 — Control flow (POWL):** Routing, choice, concurrency, the RMI loop, rejection path, and notification-before-closure (Section 7). POWL deliberately excludes all attribute conditions.

**Layer L2 — Data-aware constraints (DECLARE / log-skeleton style):**
- `response(Sanctions_Check[clean], Activate_Supplier)` — activation only after a *cleared* sanctions result, not merely the event.
- `precedence(Sanctions_Check, Activate_Supplier)` on all activating routes.
- `succession(Notify_Requester, Close_Case)` — no closure without notification or a recorded waiver.
- `responded_existence(Quality_Review, Activate_Supplier)` with route-conditional window (before activation on standard; 2 business days after on auto/emergency).
- `absence(Register_Supplier, 2)` per case pre-dedup (migration guard).
- Attribute guards expressed as constraint payloads: `rule_version_id`, `overlay_id`, `eligibility_snapshot_hash`.

**Layer L3 — Data checks (pandas / SQL over the event store):**
Eligibility recomputation from snapshots (risk tier, jurisdiction, group status) joined to the overlay registry's window; amount-cap checks against route-specific ceilings; business-day arithmetic for quality-deferral deadlines; actor alias resolution via the identity hub before any SoD test; CoI join against the HR relationship graph plus disclosure records; notification-linkage integrity (FK `notification_id`, immutability check); duplicate detection on `source_transaction_id`; label-synonym resolution only via versioned, pre-approved mapping tables.

**Layer L4 — Organizational controls:** SoD matrix enforced at assignment time (workflow blocks self-approval before it happens); CoI disclosure workflow triggered by HR-graph signals; delegation registry with evidence artifacts; emergency-declaration protocol with pre-set expiry and retrospective-approval SLA; ontology-change control requiring mapping approval *before* first production use; CRM write-back SLA (24h) with reconciliation reporting.

**Allocation rationale:** POWL expresses *what sequences are legal*; DECLARE expresses *what relations must hold between events and attributes*; pandas expresses *computations over data POWL/DECLARE cannot see* (amounts, calendars, graphs, joins); organizational controls express *what must be true about humans and authorities before events occur*.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

**Stated limitations before the code:** (1) POWL cannot enforce amounts, risk tiers, dates, identities, or overlay windows — these live in the overlay table and L3 checks. (2) POWL's LOOP operator requires a "do" part; a zero-or-more RMI cycle is approximated with a silent do-part, and the loop sits at one position (pre-routing). RMI interleaved inside routes or after a decision is not expressible without duplicating loop blocks; I choose the single-loop approximation and expose it. (3) The emergency and auto routes intentionally permit quality/approval *after* activation, matching the overlays; the accompanying rules bound those deferrals. Encoding the bounds in POWL is impossible and pretending otherwise would hide the gap.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# ---------- Activities (fresh instances per block to keep sub-orders independent) ----------
register      = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
notify        = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close         = Transition(label="Close_Case")
reject        = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
rmi           = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")

# --- Standard route pieces ---
sanc_std      = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
qual_std      = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
man_std       = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
act_std       = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
checks_std    = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanc_std, qual_std])          # concurrent, per CS-9/M0
std_route     = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[checks_std, man_std, act_std])
std_route.order.add_edge(checks_std, act_std)   # both checks done before activation
std_route.order.add_edge(man_std, act_std)      # manual approval before activation
                                                # checks-block || manual approval (mutually concurrent)

# --- Low-risk auto-approval route (overlay-governed) ---
sanc_auto     = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
auto          = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
act_auto      = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
qual_auto     = Transition(label="Quality_Review")                      # deferred, <=2 business days (overlay)
auto_route    = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanc_auto, auto, act_auto, qual_auto])
auto_route.order.add_edge(sanc_auto, auto)      # validation before decision
auto_route.order.add_edge(sanc_auto, act_auto)  # sanctions strictly before activation
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto, act_auto)
auto_route.order.add_edge(act_auto, qual_auto)  # deferred quality AFTER activation (overlay-bounded)

# --- Emergency/provisional route (FD-style overlay-governed) ---
sanc_em       = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
act_em        = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
qual_em       = Transition(label="Quality_Review")                      # retrospective, <=2 business days
man_em        = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")         # retrospective approval
emerg_route   = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanc_em, act_em, qual_em, man_em])
emerg_route.order.add_edge(sanc_em, act_em)     # FD-7 hard rule: sanctions before activation
emerg_route.order.add_edge(act_em, qual_em)     # provisional activation precedes quality
emerg_route.order.add_edge(act_em, man_em)      # provisional activation precedes manual approval
                                                # quality || retrospective approval

# --- Rejection path (abbreviated pre-screen; scope: intake cases) ---
sanc_rej      = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
rej_path      = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanc_rej, reject])
rej_path.order.add_edge(sanc_rej, reject)

# --- Repeatable Request_More_Info: zero-or-more, approximated with a silent do-part ---
# LIMITATION: single insertion point (between registration and routing); POWL LOOP needs a
# do-child, so 'zero-or-more' is modeled as LOOP(tau, RMI). Frequency limits/SLAs -> overlay.
rmi_loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP,
                        children=[SilentTransition(), Transition(label="Request_More_Info")])

# --- Route selection ---
routes = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                      children=[std_route, auto_route, emerg_route, rej_path])

# --- Root: register -> (RMI)* -> route -> notify -> close ---
M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, rmi_loop, routes, notify, close])
M1.order.add_edge(register, rmi_loop)
M1.order.add_edge(rmi_loop, routes)
M1.order.add_edge(routes, notify)     # notification before closure on ALL exits, incl. rejection
M1.order.add_edge(notify, close)
```

**Event / data-collection proposal (fields per event, append-only store):**
`case_id`; `case_type` (L0 taxonomy); `activity`; `activity_synonym_of` (resolved via ONT version); `rule_version_id` (normative version in force); `overlay_ids[]` (authority references, e.g., PD-3:v1, FD-7:v1, with validity evaluated at `effective_time`); `eligibility_snapshot` (JSON + SHA-256: risk_tier, jurisdiction, group_status, inputs); `amount_eur`, `currency`; `actor_id`, `actor_alias_resolution_id`, `role`; `delegation_id` (delegation evidence FK); `notification_id` (UUID minted at send time, immutable, CRM webhooks write back 24h); `source_transaction_id` (dedup key); `effective_time` (business time, immutable) and `record_time` (system ingestion, immutable); corrections only via compensating events, never updates. Nightly reconciliation: missing CRM mirrors, post-expiry overlay usage (auto-incident), duplicate keys, unmapped labels, SoD/CoI joins. This makes every rule in the overlay table computable and every judgment in Section 5 reproducible from the log rather than from testimony.
