# 1. Assumptions and notation

**A1 — Time axis.** All displayed times are interpreted as minutes after 08:00 on 2026-05-12. The observation window is \(H=[08{:}00,14{:}00)\), length 360 minutes.

**A2 — Rounding uncertainty.** A reported timestamp \(t\) represents an actual timestamp approximately in \([t-2.5,t+2.5]\) minutes. Most tables below use reported times as central estimates and treat roughly ±5 minutes as the uncertainty in a duration formed from two independently rounded endpoints. Durations of 0 or 5 minutes should therefore not be over-interpreted.

**A3 — Simultaneous-event convention.** At an identical reported time:
1. service completions are processed first;
2. arrivals to the station queue are processed second;
3. service starts are processed third.

Thus, a case that ends S1 at 10:00 and starts S2 at 10:00 would have zero central S2 waiting time. Queue and WIP are right-continuous after this ordered event processing.

**A4 — Queue definitions.**
- S1 queue: cases arrived externally but not yet started S1.
- S2 queue: cases with completed S1 but not yet started S2.
- Station WIP: queue plus active service at that station.
- Total system WIP: cases externally arrived but not yet fully completed S2; this includes waiting and active cases at both stations.

**A5 — Capacity.** S1 has nominal capacity two single-task resources, I1 and I2. S2 has nominal capacity two single-task resources, C1 and C2. C2 has zero available capacity from 10:55 through 11:10.

**A6 — Resource consistency.** A reported resource assignment is retained. Where intervals appear to overlap after rounding, this is treated as a data-quality/rounding issue rather than proof of actual double-booking. Missing-start imputation respects nominal reported-time resource sequencing where possible.

**A7 — Meaning of MISSING.** For `MISSING` starts, the activity occurred, resource and completion are known, but start time is unknown. The start is bounded by:
- queue-entry time;
- activity completion time;
- the previous known completion on the same resource;
- the next known start on the same resource, when available;
- nonnegative service duration.

These constraints can be loose because prior/next timestamps are themselves rounded.

**A8 — Central missing-start imputation.** A central value is the midpoint of the feasible interval after applying A7. It is a reporting device, not a claim that the start occurred at the midpoint.

**A9 — OPEN activities.** An `OPEN` activity contributes elapsed service from its recorded start to 14:00. Its final service time is right-censored and is at least the elapsed value. It has no observed completion and does not count as completed throughput.

**A10 — Blank starts/ends.** Blank S1 timestamps mean the case was waiting for S1 at 14:00. Blank S2 timestamps following completed S1 mean waiting for S2 at 14:00. They are not missing completed activities.

**A11 — Waiting time convention.**
For case \(i\):
- \(W_{1i}=S1start_i-arrival_i\),
- \(V_{1i}=S1end_i-S1start_i\),
- \(T_{1i}=S1end_i-arrival_i=W_{1i}+V_{1i}\),
- \(W_{2i}=S2start_i-S1end_i\),
- \(V_{2i}=S2end_i-S2start_i\),
- \(T_{2i}=S2end_i-S1end_i=W_{2i}+V_{2i}\).

For missing or censored values, these are ranges or lower bounds.

**A12 — “Completed case” total sojourn.** For a fully completed case,
\[
T_i=S2end_i-arrival_i.
\]
Cases with completed S2 but missing S1/S2 start can still have exact total observed case sojourn because arrival and final S2 completion are known. Open and waiting cases have lower bounds to 14:00 rather than final total sojourns.

**A13 — Utilization.** Resource busy time is active service time, not station sojourn. For fully observed intervals, it is calculated from service intervals. For missing/open work, lower/central/upper estimates are shown. Station utilization is busy resource-minutes divided by nominal available resource-minutes; C2’s planned outage is removed from its denominator.

**A14 — Little’s Law.** Little’s Law is used only as a descriptive consistency check on a relatively stable, internally bounded period, not as proof of stationarity. The start-up transient, outage window, and right-censored tail are excluded from the preferred check.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

Let \(a_i\) be external arrival, \(b_i\) S1 start, \(c_i\) S1 completion, \(d_i\) S2 start, and \(e_i\) S2 completion.

For observed cases:

\[
W_{1i}=b_i-a_i,\qquad V_{1i}=c_i-b_i,\qquad T_{1i}=c_i-a_i.
\]

\[
W_{2i}=d_i-c_i,\qquad V_{2i}=e_i-d_i,\qquad T_{2i}=e_i-c_i.
\]

The end-to-end completed-case sojourn is:

\[
T_i=e_i-a_i.
\]

For an open activity at time \(H=14{:}00\):

\[
V^{elapsed}_{ki}=H-start_{ki}, \qquad V^{final}_{ki}\ge V^{elapsed}_{ki}.
\]

For a waiting case:

\[
W_{ki}^{elapsed}=H-entry_{ki}, \qquad W^{final}_{ki}\ge W^{elapsed}_{ki}.
\]

For each missing start \(s\), construct a feasible range:

\[
s \in [L,U],
\]

where:

\[
L=\max(\text{queue entry},\text{prior resource completion}),
\]

\[
U=\min(\text{activity completion},\text{next resource start}),
\]

subject to rounding caveats. Then:

\[
V \in [end-U,\ end-L],
\]

and, for S2:

\[
W_2 \in [L-entry,\ U-entry].
\]

The central estimate uses \(s_c=(L+U)/2\), unless that midpoint would conflict with an explicitly imposed operational constraint.

### Queue length and WIP

For station \(k\), with queue-entry time \(q_{ki}\), service start \(s_{ki}\), and service end \(f_{ki}\):

\[
Q_k(t)=\sum_i \mathbf{1}[q_{ki}\le t < s_{ki}],
\]

\[
B_k(t)=\sum_i \mathbf{1}[s_{ki}\le t < f_{ki}],
\]

\[
WIP_k(t)=Q_k(t)+B_k(t).
\]

For open activity, \(f_{ki}\) is set to 14:00 solely for observed-window occupancy calculations. For a case still waiting, no service interval is created.

Total system WIP is:

\[
WIP_{sys}(t)=\sum_i \mathbf{1}[a_i\le t < e_i],
\]

with \(e_i=14{:}00\) for all incomplete cases when calculating observed-window occupancy.

### Rates

For interval \([u,v)\):

\[
\lambda_k = \frac{\#\{\text{station arrivals in }[u,v)\}}{v-u},
\qquad
X_k=\frac{\#\{\text{station completions in }[u,v)\}}{v-u}.
\]

S1 arrivals are external arrivals. S2 arrivals are S1 completions. Throughput counts completed services, not starts and not open work.

### Lower, central, and upper scenarios

- **Optimistic:** missing start as late as feasible when that minimizes resource service and maximizes pre-service waiting; for capacity pressure this is generally favorable at the resource level but unfavorable for queue waiting.
- **Central:** midpoint of feasible start interval.
- **Pessimistic:** missing start as early as feasible, maximizing active service and minimizing pre-service waiting.

Because waiting and service trade off for a fixed completion, “optimistic” must be interpreted relative to the metric of interest. Therefore scenario tables report both waiting and busy-time implications rather than claiming one scenario is globally optimistic.

### Limited Little’s-Law check

A cautious S2 check can be made over approximately 11:15–13:30, after C2 returns and before the severe end-of-window censoring dominates. During this 135-minute interval, S2 has several starts/completions but remains nonstationary because backlog continues to evolve.

Using observed starts/completions, S2 completion throughput is roughly 4–5 completed services over 135 minutes:

\[
X_{S2}\approx 1.8\text{ to }2.2\ \text{cases/hour}.
\]

Central S2 waiting for cases beginning S2 in the corresponding period is roughly 40–55 minutes, and service is roughly 40–45 minutes, so station sojourn is roughly 80–100 minutes. A rough Little estimate would imply:

\[
L_{S2}\approx X_{S2}\times T_{S2}
\approx (1.8\text{ to }2.2)\times(1.3\text{ to }1.7)
\approx 2.3\text{ to }3.7 \text{ cases}.
\]

Observed S2 WIP in that period is often around 3–5 cases and rises toward six by 14:00. The direction is consistent, but the match is not expected to be exact because arrivals, capacity, queue size, and censoring are not stationary. It is therefore a sanity check, not a validated steady-state estimate.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

### Evidence

1. **S1 queue delay increases substantially over time.**  
   Early observed S1 waits are 5–20 minutes. By P10–P20 they are generally 70–105 minutes, and P22 has already waited at least 110 minutes by 14:00. This is direct evidence that S1 capacity was materially tight during most of the window.

2. **S2 also accumulates backlog, especially after late morning.**  
   Early S2 waits are generally 5–25 minutes. Later observed waits are often 45–65 minutes; P15 waits 75–110 minutes before starting S2, and four cases are still waiting for S2 at 14:00 while two are in service.

3. **S2 has more end-of-window unfinished work.**  
   At 14:00 S2 contains six cases, versus two cases at S1. This strongly indicates that S2 is the immediate downstream bottleneck at the cutoff.

4. **Observed service durations are longer at S2 than S1.**  
   S1 completed service is usually 20–30 minutes. S2 completed service is usually 25–50 minutes, commonly 40–45 minutes. With two resources at each station, the longer S2 service time implies lower nominal capacity unless S2 has compensating operational advantages not observed here.

5. **C2 outage removes one-half of S2 nominal capacity for 15 minutes.**  
   From 10:55–11:10, only C1 is available. The outage is short, but it occurs when S2 already has accumulating work and can propagate delay downstream.

### Interpretation by period

**08:00–roughly 10:30: S1 is visibly constrained, with S2 beginning to build pressure.**  
S1 queues grow as external arrivals are relatively dense. Both inspectors are generally busy or plausibly busy. S2 is also processing long tasks, but its queue is initially smaller than S1’s. The evidence supports S1 as a major early contributor to total delay.

**Roughly 10:30–11:15: combined pressure, intensified by the C2 outage.**  
By this period S1 has released enough work for S2 to experience sustained queueing. The C2 maintenance interval temporarily halves S2’s available capacity. The outage alone is too short to explain all later S2 delay, but it is consistent with worsening backlog and with P08’s wait crossing the outage.

**11:15–14:00: S2 is the stronger downstream bottleneck, while S1 remains capacity constrained.**  
C2 returns at 11:10, yet S2 queueing persists and grows. By 14:00 S2 has four waiting and two active cases. S1 has only one waiting and one active case. Thus the final state is more consistent with S2 as the immediate bottleneck, even though S1 had long waits and likely constrained upstream release earlier.

### Overall conclusion

The evidence supports a **time-varying combination**, not a single immutable bottleneck:

- **S1 is an important early bottleneck or co-bottleneck**, shown by steadily increasing S1 waits and high two-resource occupancy.
- **S2 becomes the dominant downstream bottleneck by late morning/afternoon**, shown by longer service times, persistent S2 waits after C2 returns, six S2 cases in WIP at cutoff, and only 14 S2 completions versus 20 S1 completions.
- The **C2 outage plausibly amplified**, rather than solely caused, the S2 backlog.

A stronger causal statement would require actual unrounded timestamps, validated resource calendars, and confirmation whether undocumented parallel assistance occurred.

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

Three scenarios are compared for the four missing starts. Open services remain right-censored in all scenarios.

| scenario | missing starts P05 S2 / P07 S1 / P11 S2 / P15 S1 | effect on waiting estimates | effect on service/busy-time estimates | interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Late-start / queue-heavy | Set each missing start to its latest feasible value | Maximizes waiting before service: P05 S2 50, P07 S1 75, P11 S2 80, P15 S1 125 min; P15 S2 wait 110 | Minimizes missing-activity service: each missing completed activity can approach 0 at reported precision; P15 S1 can approach 0 | Favors the explanation “queues caused delay,” but is operationally implausible if zero-length service is taken literally; rounding means near-zero may still represent a short task. |
| Central midpoint | P05 S2 09:50; P07 S1 09:22:30; P11 S2 11:45; P15 S1 11:57:30 | Intermediate waits: 25, 37.5, 40, 107.5 min respectively | Intermediate services: 25, 37.5, 40, 17.5 min; open elapsed work retained | A neutral descriptive reconstruction, useful for plotting but not a statistically justified estimate. |
| Early-start / service-heavy | Set each missing start to earliest feasible value | Minimizes missing-case waiting: P05 S2 0, P07 S1 0, P11 S2 0, P15 S1 90 min; P15 S2 wait 75 | Maximizes missing-activity service: P05 50, P07 75, P11 80, P15 S1 35 min | Favors the explanation “longer active service consumed capacity.” It may create apparent resource overlaps at displayed resolution, which could be due to rounding or incomplete logging. |

### Conclusions stable across scenarios

1. **S1 waiting becomes large.**  
   Even without using P07 or P15’s exact starts, observed S1 waits increase strongly from the first cases to the late cases. P22’s elapsed S1 wait is at least 110 minutes.

2. **S2 is materially congested at the cutoff.**  
   This is independent of all missing-start choices: at 14:00 there are exactly four S2 waiting cases and two open S2 services.

3. **S2 output lags S1 output.**  
   By 14:00, S1 has completed 20 services, while S2 has completed 14. The six-case difference exactly corresponds to the six cases at S2 or beyond S1 completion and not yet S2-complete.

4. **The final bottleneck is downstream at S2.**  
   S2 WIP of six versus S1 WIP of two is not dependent on imputation.

5. **C2 outage is a plausible aggravating factor.**  
   It unambiguously removes one resource for 15 minutes. The precise number of delayed cases attributable to it is not identifiable from this log alone.

### Conclusions sensitive to scenarios

1. **Mean active service time and utilization.**  
   Missing starts can shift service minutes substantially: P07 can contribute 0–75 S1 minutes; P11 can contribute 0–80 S2 minutes. Thus a narrow utilization point estimate would be unjustified.

2. **Exact queue trajectory around P05, P07, P11, and P15.**  
   Their missing starts alter whether they are counted as waiting or busy at intermediate times. End-of-window WIP is unaffected, but time-average queue and busy occupancy are not.

3. **Relative magnitude of S1 versus S2 utilization.**  
   Central estimates suggest both are highly utilized, with S2 probably near or above S1 once its outage-adjusted denominator is used. But the ranking can change under extreme missing-start assignments.

4. **Whether certain apparent resource conflicts are real.**  
   For example, an early imputation for a missing start may overlap another displayed activity on the same resource. Given 5-minute rounding, missing timestamps, and the explicit warning about apparent conflicts, such overlap should be treated as an audit flag rather than conclusive evidence of impossible execution.

### Additional censoring implications

- P15 and P17 final S2 service times are unknown beyond lower bounds of 30 and 5 minutes at 14:00.
- P21 final S1 service time is unknown beyond 30 minutes.
- P16, P18, P19, P20, and P22 have waiting times that will exceed their observed elapsed waits.
- Therefore, naive averages over completed cases understate final average waiting, station sojourn, and total case sojourn for the population present during the window.
- The completed-case mean total sojourn of about 146 minutes is a **completion-conditioned measure**; it excludes the long-sojourn cases unfinished at 14:00 and is therefore likely downward-biased for all arrivals.

def parse_status_aware(x):

# 2. Missing-start feasible intervals based on supplied constraints.
# Conservative reported-time bounds are explicitly embedded.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
missing_bounds = {
    ("P05", "S2"): (pd.Timestamp("2026-05-12 09:25"), pd.Timestamp("2026-05-12 10:15")),
    ("P07", "S1"): (pd.Timestamp("2026-05-12 08:45"), pd.Timestamp("2026-05-12 10:00")),
    ("P11", "S2"): (pd.Timestamp("2026-05-12 11:05"), pd.Timestamp("2026-05-12 12:25")),
    ("P15", "S1"): (pd.Timestamp("2026-05-12 11:40"), pd.Timestamp("2026-05-12 12:15")),
}

    "late_start_queue_heavy": 1.0,  # latest feasible start
    "central_midpoint": 0.5,
    "early_start_service_heavy": 0.0,  # earliest feasible start
}

def choose_missing_start(case, station, scenario):
    lo, hi = missing_bounds[(case, station)]
    f = scenario_fraction[scenario]
    return lo + (hi - lo) * f

def ts_or_cutoff(value, raw_status):
    """For observed-window occupancy only: OPEN ends at cutoff."""
    if raw_status == "OPEN":
        return cutoff
    return value

def make_station_intervals(df, station, scenario):
    """
    Build station records with:
      entry: queue entry
      start: observed or scenario-imputed start
      end_occ: observed completion or cutoff for OPEN
      completed: observed station completion before cutoff
    """
    out = []
    for _, r in df.iterrows():
        case = r["case"]

        if station == "S1":
            entry = r["arrival_s1_ts"]
            start = r["s1_start_ts"]
            end = r["s1_end_ts"]
            start_status = r["s1_start_status"]
            end_status = r["s1_end_status"]
            resource = r["s1_resource"]
        else:
            entry = r["s1_end_ts"]  # S2 queue entry only after completed S1
            start = r["s2_start_ts"]
            end = r["s2_end_ts"]
            start_status = r["s2_start_status"]
            end_status = r["s2_end_status"]
            resource = r["s2_resource"]

        if pd.isna(entry):
            continue

        if start_status == "MISSING":
            start = choose_missing_start(case, station, scenario)

        # A blank start means waiting, not a completed missing activity.
        started = pd.notna(start)

        # OPEN is an active interval to cutoff for occupancy only.
        end_occ = ts_or_cutoff(end, end_status) if started else pd.NaT
        completed = (end_status == "OBSERVED") and pd.notna(end)

        out.append({
            "case": case,
            "station": station,
            "entry": entry,
            "start": start,
            "end_observed": end,
            "end_occ": end_occ,
            "resource": resource,
            "started": started,
            "completed": completed,
            "start_status": start_status,
            "end_status": end_status,
        })

    return pd.DataFrame(out)

def durations_for_station(intervals):
    x = intervals.copy()
    x["wait_min"] = np.where(
        x["started"],
        (x["start"] - x["entry"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60,
        (cutoff - x["entry"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60
    )
    x["wait_is_elapsed_lower_bound"] = ~x["started"]

    x["service_min_observed_window"] = np.where(
        x["started"],
        (x["end_occ"] - x["start"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60,
        np.nan
    )
    x["service_is_elapsed_lower_bound"] = x["end_status"].eq("OPEN")

    x["station_sojourn_min_observed_window"] = np.where(
        x["started"],
        (x["end_occ"] - x["entry"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60,
        (cutoff - x["entry"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60
    )
    return x

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Queue/WIP event sweep with completion -> arrival -> start ordering.
# Here each case's station entry is a queue arrival. Events at same time
# are processed by delta order: busy end first, queue entry second, start third.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
def queue_wip_trace(intervals):
    events = []
    for _, r in intervals.iterrows():
        # queue entry happens for every station-arriving case
        events.append((r["entry"], 1, 0, r["case"], "queue_entry"))
        if r["started"]:
            # service start removes from queue and adds busy
            events.append((r["start"], -1, 1, r["case"], "service_start"))
            # completion/open cutoff removes busy; open uses cutoff occupancy boundary
            if pd.notna(r["end_occ"]):
                events.append((r["end_occ"], 0, -1, r["case"], "service_end_or_cutoff"))

    # event priority: busy completion first, entry second, start third
    priority = {"service_end_or_cutoff": 0, "queue_entry": 1, "service_start": 2}
    events = sorted(events, key=lambda z: (z[0], priority[z[4]]))

    q = busy = 0
    records = [(window_start, q, busy, q + busy, "initial")]
    for t, dq, db, case, label in events:
        if t < window_start or t > cutoff:
            continue
        q += dq
        busy += db
        records.append((t, q, busy, q + busy, f"{label}:{case}"))

    trace = pd.DataFrame(records, columns=["time", "queue", "busy", "wip", "event"])
    trace = trace.sort_values(["time"]).reset_index(drop=True)

    # Time-weighted means over [window_start, cutoff).
    trace["next_time"] = trace["time"].shift(-1).fillna(cutoff)
    trace["duration_min"] = (trace["next_time"] - trace["time"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60
    valid = trace["duration_min"].clip(lower=0)
    mean_q = (trace["queue"] * valid).sum() / 360
    mean_busy = (trace["busy"] * valid).sum() / 360
    mean_wip = (trace["wip"] * valid).sum() / 360

    summary = {
        "mean_queue": mean_q,
        "mean_busy": mean_busy,
        "mean_wip": mean_wip,
        "peak_queue": trace["queue"].max(),
        "peak_busy": trace["busy"].max(),
        "peak_wip": trace["wip"].max(),
        "queue_at_14": trace.loc[trace["time"] <= cutoff, "queue"].iloc[-1],
        "busy_at_14": trace.loc[trace["time"] <= cutoff, "busy"].iloc[-1],
    }
    return trace, summary

def resource_busy_minutes(intervals):
    """
    Sum interval busy minutes by resource. This can double count only if
    data imply overlapping intervals on the same resource; such conflicts
    should be audited rather than silently erased.
    """
    started = intervals[intervals["started"]].copy()
    started["busy_min"] = (
        (started["end_occ"] - started["start"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60
    )
    return started.groupby("resource", dropna=False)["busy_min"].sum()

        busy_by_resource = resource_busy_minutes(intervals)

        utilization = busy_total / available

        scenario_rows.append({

            "busy_at_14": qsummary["busy_at_14"],
