# 1. Assumptions and notation

All durations below are in minutes. A displayed time is treated as a rounded observation, not as an exact physical event time.

- **A1 (window and event convention).** The analysis window is the closed observation interval from 08:00 to 14:00, but rates are reported on half-open subintervals `[08:00,10:00)`, `[10:00,12:00)`, and `[12:00,14:00]`. For a state table, all events with the same displayed time are applied together and the reported state is the state immediately after those events. Within a physical handoff, completion/queue entry is conceptually before the next start. This avoids counting a zero-duration rounded handoff as extra WIP.
- **A2 (rounding).** Each rounded operational or arrival timestamp represents an actual time within ±2.5 minutes. Thus, a duration with two rounded endpoints and displayed duration `d` has a nominal interval `[max(0,d-5), d+5]`. A duration to the fixed cutoff 14:00 has a ±2.5-minute endpoint uncertainty when its other endpoint is rounded. These are endpoint-tolerance intervals; they are not independent guarantees because resource-order constraints can correlate the endpoint errors.
- **A3 (capacity).** S1 has two single-task resources, I1 and I2; S2 has two, C1 and C2. I use the supplied no-overlap rule when imposing constraints. The C2 unavailability is treated as real over 10:55–11:10, subject to the timestamp rounding of nearby activity events.
- **A4 (queue entry).** S1 queue entry is `arrival_s1`. S2 queue entry is the actual completion of S1. No bypass is allowed. I do not need to assume FIFO to calculate counts or inventory; the central reconstruction uses the apparent resource order and the natural arrival order only to select a readable imputation.
- **A5 (missing starts).** `MISSING` is retained as a distinct status. The central values are scenario imputations, not observations. They respect the preceding and following work on the same resource, the case's known completion, nonnegative service, and the S1-to-S2 precedence constraint. The hard ranges in the case table deliberately allow very short service because the prompt supplies no positive minimum service time.
- **A6 (central imputation).** For a central event ledger I use P07 S1 start 09:30, P15 S1 start 11:45, P05 S2 start 09:45, P07 S2 start 10:20, and P11 S2 start 12:00. These produce central missing-start service estimates of 30, 30, 30, 40, and 25 minutes respectively and are close to the observed service-time pattern, but they are not asserted to be true.
- **A7 (open and blank activities).** For an OPEN activity, 14:00 is an observation cutoff, not a completion. Its elapsed service is calculated to the cutoff and its final service is at least that elapsed amount, with no finite upper bound from this data. A blank S2 after completed S1 means S2 waiting, and a blank S1 means S1 waiting; neither is converted into an unobserved start.
- **A8 (rates).** “S1 arrivals” means external arrivals. “S2 arrivals” means completed S1 activities entering the S2 queue. S1 departures are completed S1 activities and S2 departures are completed S2 activities. A departure rate is therefore not silently treated as a final throughput rate for censored cases.
- **A9 (utilization).** Busy time is the union of resource-active intervals inside 08:00–14:00. For an open activity, only elapsed busy time through 14:00 is counted. The central busy times use A6 and displayed durations; scenario ranges are plausible imputation bands and should be read with the rounding sensitivity discussed later.
- **A10 (station names).** For case (i), let (a_i) be external arrival, (s_{1i},e_{1i}) S1 start/end, and (s_{2i},e_{2i}) S2 start/end. “S1 wait” is before S1, and “S2 wait” is between S1 completion and S2 start.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

For a fully observed case, I use


- (W_{1i}=s_{1i}-a_i), (S_{1i}=e_{1i}-s_{1i}), and (R_{1i}=e_{1i}-a_i=W_{1i}+S_{1i});
- (W_{2i}=s_{2i}-e_{1i}), (S_{2i}=e_{2i}-s_{2i}), and (R_{2i}=e_{2i}-e_{1i}=W_{2i}+S_{2i});
- total case sojourn (T_i=e_{2i}-a_i=R_{1i}+R_{2i}).

For an OPEN activity with start (s), elapsed service is (C-s), where (C=14{:}00), and final service is at least (C-s). For a case still waiting at (C), the displayed elapsed wait is a lower-bound observation of its eventual wait. For example, P16 has an S2 wait of about 85 minutes at the cutoff, but its eventual S2 wait is longer if it starts after 14:00.

For a time (t), the central event-sweep quantities are


- (Q_1(t)=#{i:a_ile t<s_{1i}}),
- (B_1(t)=#{i:s_{1i}le t<e_{1i}}),
- (Q_2(t)=#{i:e_{1i}le t<s_{2i}}), and
- (B_2(t)=#{i:s_{2i}le t<e_{2i}}).

For open cases, the right endpoint in the last expression is replaced by the cutoff only for calculating observed exposure; the case remains active at 14:00. Total WIP is (L(t)=Q_1(t)+B_1(t)+Q_2(t)+B_2(t)), equivalently external arrivals to date minus completed S2 departures to date. Mean WIP is the area under the relevant step function divided by 360 minutes. Peak WIP is the largest post-event count in the central rounded event ledger.

For a subinterval (I), arrival and departure rates are count divided by the duration of (I). Resource utilization is (U_r=b_r/360), where (b_r) is busy minutes for resource (r); station utilization is the sum of resource busy minutes divided by (2\times360).

Lower, central, and upper values are used as follows:


- fully observed endpoint differences use the displayed difference as central and the A2 interval as the rounding range;
- missing starts use the intersection of precedence, same-resource non-overlap, completion, and successor constraints for a hard range, with A6 as the central value;
- OPEN values report observed elapsed service and a one-sided final-service statement;
- the sensitivity scenarios in section 6 vary the missing starts over resource-feasible, short-service and long-service choices. They do not pretend to identify a unique hidden event time.

Resource-level rounding-only envelopes are wider than the central values. For example, the ten fully timestamped I2 S1 services sum to 290 displayed minutes but independent endpoint tolerance gives a rough 240–340-minute envelope, or 0.67–0.94 utilization, before enforcing all no-overlap correlations. Similarly, treating only the eight known completed I1 services, two missing starts, and P21's elapsed activity as hard rather than empirical bounds gives a very wide range; this is why the station table labels the narrower bands as plausible scenario ranges rather than confidence intervals.

A finite-window inventory check is safer than a steady-state Little's-Law claim here. The S1 station exposure sums to 2,085 case-minutes: 1,845 from P01–P20, 130 observed for P21, and 110 observed for P22. Since 22 cases entered S1 during the window, (22/6\times(2{,}085/22/60)=5.79), matching the integrated mean S1 WIP. For S2, the 20 S2 entrants have 1,340 observed station case-minutes, so (20/6\times(1{,}340/20/60)=3.72), matching integrated S2 WIP. This is an exact finite-window exposure identity with right-censoring, not evidence of stationarity. No subinterval is sufficiently steady to justify using completed departures and completed-case mean sojourn as an ordinary (L=\lambda W) estimate: S1's queue is filling early, S2's queue is filling late, and arrivals/departures are visibly changing by subperiod.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

The evidence supports a time-varying bottleneck rather than a single station dominating throughout.


- **08:00–10:00: upstream pressure at S1.** External arrivals average 6.5/h while S1 completions average 3.0/h. With central S1 service around 28 minutes and only two resources, the S1 queue rises to roughly 4–6 cases. S2 has only a small early queue because the pipeline is still filling. The low early S2 departure rate is partly a startup effect, not by itself proof that S2 is the morning bottleneck.
- **10:00–12:00: both stations are constrained.** S1 still has six or seven queued cases and S2's queue becomes persistent, reaching three or more. S1's mean service is shorter, but incoming work is heavy; S2's longer central service, around 38 minutes, makes its effective two-resource capacity tighter. The observed C2 outage removes 15 minutes of nominal availability from 10:55 to 11:10. P08 had already completed S1 at 10:20 but did not start C2 until 11:15, so at least part of its 55-minute S2 wait is consistent with the outage. The exact counterfactual delay cannot be identified because the neighboring events are rounded and a five-minute handoff gap may also be present.
- **12:00–14:00: downstream S2 is the active terminal bottleneck.** External arrivals fall to 0.5/h and S1 completes seven cases, draining its queue from about six to one. S2 receives seven S1 completions but completes six, while its queue reaches five before ending at four. Four cases are still waiting for S2 at 14:00, in addition to two active S2 cases. This late accumulation is direct evidence of downstream constraint after the upstream arrival burst has ended.

Across the whole window, central station busy time is slightly higher at S1 (595 of 720 resource-minutes, 0.83) than at S2 (570 of 720, 0.79), but that comparison mixes startup, a C2 outage, open activities, and a changing arrival rate. It does not overturn the queue evidence: S1 is the early accumulation point, while S2 becomes the limiting completion station by noon. A defensible summary is therefore “S1 bottleneck early, joint pressure in the middle, S2 bottleneck late,” with S1 having the slightly higher whole-window utilization estimate only under the central imputation.

These are sensitivity scenarios, not claims about operator behavior.

Known rounded endpoint durations can expand these display-grid values by approximately five minutes per completed activity if endpoint errors are considered independently. That expansion is deliberately not folded into a falsely precise single utilization number; physical non-overlap and the fixed six-hour window impose additional correlations.

The stable conclusions are:


- the 14 completed S2 cases and 20 completed S1 cases are observed facts, and missing internal starts do not change their outer-endpoint total sojourns;
- P21 and P15/P17 remain right-censored, and P16/P18/P19/P20 remain S2 queue cases at the cutoff;
- final WIP is 8 and the final composition is two S1 cases plus six S2 cases, regardless of the missing-start imputation;
- S1's early queue buildup and S2's late queue buildup survive all three scenarios;
- C2's outage is a credible contributor to P08's S2 delay, but its exact counterfactual effect is not identifiable.

The unstable or weakly identified conclusions are the exact start times and waits for P05, P07, P11, and P15; the precise clock time of the maximum queue; final service durations for all OPEN activities; any completion rate after 14:00; and small differences between whole-window S1 and S2 utilization. The hard ranges also show why a missing start must not be silently replaced by its central value.

def to_ts(clock):
    return pd.Timestamp(f"{DAY} {clock}")

def scenario_frame(name):
    d = raw.copy()
    for station in ["s1", "s2"]:
        d[station + "_start_sc"] = d[station + "_start_ts"]
        d[station + "_end_obs"] = d[station + "_end_ts"]
        open_mask = d[station + "_end_kind"].eq("OPEN")
        d.loc[open_mask, station + "_end_obs"] = CUTOFF
    for (case, field), clock in imputations[name].items():
        d.loc[d["case"].eq(case), field + "_sc"] = to_ts(clock)
    d["s1_wait_min"] = (d["s1_start_sc"] - d["arrival_s1_ts"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60
    d["s1_service_elapsed_min"] = (d["s1_end_obs"] - d["s1_start_sc"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60
    d["s2_wait_min"] = (d["s2_start_sc"] - d["s1_end_ts"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60
    d["s2_service_elapsed_min"] = (d["s2_end_obs"] - d["s2_start_sc"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60
    return d

def event_sweep(d):
    events = []
    def add(t, q1=0, b1=0, q2=0, b2=0):
        if pd.notna(t) and WINDOW_START <= t <= CUTOFF:
            events.append([t, q1, b1, q2, b2])

    for _, r in d.iterrows():
        add(r["arrival_s1_ts"], q1=1)
        if pd.notna(r["s1_start_sc"]):
            add(r["s1_start_sc"], q1=-1, b1=1)
        if r["s1_end_kind"] == "TIMESTAMP":
            add(r["s1_end_ts"], b1=-1, q2=1)
        if pd.notna(r["s2_start_sc"]):
            add(r["s2_start_sc"], q2=-1, b2=1)
        if r["s2_end_kind"] == "TIMESTAMP":
            add(r["s2_end_ts"], b2=-1)

    ev = pd.DataFrame(events, columns=["time", "dq1", "db1", "dq2", "db2"])
    grouped = ev.groupby("time", as_index=False)[["dq1", "db1", "dq2", "db2"]].sum()
    snapshots = pd.date_range(WINDOW_START, CUTOFF, freq="30min")
    times = sorted(set(grouped["time"]) | set(snapshots))
    deltas = grouped.set_index("time")
    state = [0, 0, 0, 0]
    area = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    previous = WINDOW_START
    out = []
    for t in times:
        dt = (t - previous).total_seconds() / 60
        if dt > 0:
            area = [x + y * dt for x, y in zip(area, state)]
        if t in deltas.index:
            change = deltas.loc[t].tolist()
            state = [x + int(y) for x, y in zip(state, change)]
        out.append([t, *state, sum(state)])
        previous = t
    result = pd.DataFrame(out, columns=["time", "Q1", "B1", "Q2", "B2", "WIP"])
    means = {k: area[i] / WINDOW_MINUTES for i, k in enumerate(["Q1", "B1", "Q2", "B2"])}
    means["S1_WIP"] = means["Q1"] + means["B1"]
    means["S2_WIP"] = means["Q2"] + means["B2"]
    means["total_WIP"] = sum(area) / WINDOW_MINUTES
    peaks = {k: int(result[k].max()) for k in ["Q1", "B1", "Q2", "B2", "WIP"]}
    return result, means, peaks, area

def union_minutes(frame, start_col, end_col):
    intervals = sorted((a, b) for a, b in zip(frame[start_col], frame[end_col])
                       if pd.notna(a) and pd.notna(b) and b > a)
    total = 0.0
    current_start = current_end = None
    for a, b in intervals:
        if current_start is None:
            current_start, current_end = a, b
        elif a <= current_end:
            current_end = max(current_end, b)
        else:
            total += (current_end - current_start).total_seconds() / 60
            current_start, current_end = a, b
    if current_start is not None:
        total += (current_end - current_start).total_seconds() / 60
    return total

def station_summary(d, sweep, means, peaks):
    result = []
    for station in ["S1", "S2"]:
        if station == "S1":
            start = d["s1_start_sc"]
            end = d["s1_end_obs"]
            entered = d["arrival_s1_ts"].notna()
            completed = d["s1_end_kind"].eq("TIMESTAMP")
            wait = d["s1_wait_min"]
            service = d["s1_service_elapsed_min"]
            qmean, qpeak, bmean, bpeak = means["Q1"], peaks["Q1"], means["B1"], peaks["B1"]
        else:
            start = d["s2_start_sc"]
            end = d["s2_end_obs"]
            entered = d["s1_end_kind"].eq("TIMESTAMP")
            completed = d["s2_end_kind"].eq("TIMESTAMP")
            wait = d["s2_wait_min"]
            service = d["s2_service_elapsed_min"]
            qmean, qpeak, bmean, bpeak = means["Q2"], peaks["Q2"], means["B2"], peaks["B2"]
        started = start.notna()
        result.append({
            "station": station,
            "arrivals": int(entered.sum()),
            "completed": int(completed.sum()),
            "started_wait_mean": round(wait[started].mean(), 2),
            "completed_service_mean": round(service[completed].mean(), 2),
            "queue_mean_peak": f"{qmean:.2f}/{qpeak}",
            "active_mean_peak": f"{bmean:.2f}/{bpeak}",
        })
    return pd.DataFrame(result)
