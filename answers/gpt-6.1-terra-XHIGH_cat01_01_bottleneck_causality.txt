## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Verdict: not identifiable from the supplied evidence. Confidence: 85/100** in that identification judgment, not in any particular direction of effect. Among these 30 completed cases, Orion's mean cycle time is 21.96 hours versus Nova's 15.24, a 6.71-hour crude disadvantage. Orion receives more high-complexity and night cases. Within each recorded complexity category, the direction reverses: Orion's mean is 2.14 to 3.43 hours lower. This is strong evidence that the crude group comparison mixes performance with case allocation; it is not proof that Orion is faster, because cases can differ within each category and assignment is not random.

The strongest evidence against concluding that allocation alone explains the gap is that there is little comparable support: just two Orion low-complexity and two Nova high-complexity cases, and only one Nova medium- and one Nova high-complexity case at night. Shift, load, reviewer certification, detailed case risk, and queue conditions are not jointly controlled. Waiting includes time around compliance, while review service includes repeated reviews; neither component isolates a group effect. Only completed cases are observed, and the two appeals are too few to assess quality.

The total assignment effect includes any resulting changes in waiting, review and rework. A direct effect would hold specified mediators fixed; a mediated effect concerns a specified pathway through them. Neither is identified here, and direct or mediated effects require additional assumptions and well-defined interventions.

Moving half of future Orion cases could help if it relieves an overloaded queue and Nova has qualified, available capacity. It could fail if the apparent difference reflects case mix or night coverage, or backfire if Nova lacks capacity or certification, creating congestion or worse decisions. A small eligible-case routing experiment and a separate capacity intervention are safer ways to learn, with cycle time and quality monitored together.

The prompt supports assignment after classification, preferential routing of difficult cases, night coverage, required compliance being performed, and the definitions of waiting, service and cycle time. The causal arrows from group, shift, load, skill, and risk to process outcomes are plausible assumptions, not established effects. Unobserved case risk may affect both completion and later quality; an appeal cannot cause earlier completion. `cycle_time -> case_completed` represents latent completion time relative to an observation cutoff. Queue load and routing evolve together: assignments increase future queues, which can affect later routing. A static DAG suppresses this feedback.

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests

| rank | hypothesis | process mechanism | evidence supporting it | evidence contradicting it | discriminating analysis or falsification test | predicted observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Case mix and routing explain much of the crude gap | Difficult cases are preferentially routed to Orion and need more work and compliance. | High complexity 42.9% versus 12.5%; all three complexity contrasts reverse. | Coarse complexity may omit risk; reversal alone does not identify a causal effect. | Collect pre-routing severity and eligibility; check overlap, then match or standardize on complexity, shift, load and risk within supported strata. Perform sensitivity analysis for unmeasured risk. | A large crude gap shrinking under balance supports allocation as an explanation; a stable gap in a well-supported randomized comparison weakens it. |
| 2 | Orion has a beneficial effect masked by allocation | Certified reviewers resolve similar cases faster or avoid extra work. | Orion's observed mean is lower in every complexity stratum, including wait. | Tiny cells, stronger night presence and unknown severity; waits are not reviewer service. | Randomize eligible cases within complexity, shift and load blocks; compare intention-to-treat time and quality, and inspect reviewer-level variation. | Consistently shorter completion times for randomized Orion cases without worse quality favors benefit; a null or opposite effect weakens it. |

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Baseline and eligibility | Capture all incoming cases, routing decisions, queue state, reviewer certification and event timestamps; define pre-assignment eligibility and outcome follow-up. | Case mix, selection and bottleneck localization | All arriving cases; no change to routing | Maintain mandatory compliance and qualified reviewers; record incomplete cases and adverse outcomes. | Arrival, classification, routing, compliance start/end, review start/end, information requests, closure and appeal; queue lengths and variants. | Establishes overlap, baseline congestion and feasibility; does not identify a group effect by itself. | Pause progression if eligibility, completeness or timestamp quality cannot be verified. |
| 2. Small routing pilot | Randomize a small prespecified fraction of cases that both groups can legally and operationally handle; block by complexity, shift and available capacity. Compare assigned groups by intention to treat. | Group assignment and its operational bundle | Individual eligible case; randomization within blocks, with time blocks or clusters considered if spillovers are material | Capacity caps, certification and policy compliance; monitor both groups and untreated cases, including backlog and quality. Predefine sample size or duration and analysis. | Assigned and actual group, queue and compliance timings, repeat review, cycle-time distribution, open cases, appeals and decision audit. | Provides a causal estimate for eligible cases under pilot load; track deviations and interference before extrapolating to 50%. | Suspend allocation if a prespecified capacity or backlog threshold is exceeded, compliance fails, or audited quality deteriorates; set thresholds before launch. |
| 3. Capacity intervention | Add qualified night review or compliance capacity in selected time blocks, or streamline compliance handoffs without removing required checks. | Queueing and compliance waiting | Randomized or staggered eligible time blocks, accounting for spillovers | Preserve certification and checks; ensure capacity is real and track costs and quality. | Queue age, arrivals, staffing, compliance wait and duration, review service, rework and backlog. | Tests whether capacity reduces waiting independently of a simple group relabeling; block-level assignment supports system effects. | Roll back if backlog shifts elsewhere, compliance or quality deteriorates, or prespecified operational limits are breached. |
| 4. Evaluate and scale cautiously | Review pilot and capacity results jointly; scale only a measured, supportable policy and continue monitoring. | Sustainable routing and capacity | Subsequent eligible cases or operating blocks | Include long-running and abandoned cases; preserve capacity for nonpilot work. | Case completion curves, tail latency, queue utilization, variants, appeal and audited decision quality. | Estimates transportability and system effects as load changes; supports or rejects expansion. | Halt expansion or revert if prespecified time, backlog or quality limits are breached. |

Do not implement the proposed 50% transfer on the basis of the aggregate mean. First establish which cases Nova can handle with the required certifications and coverage, and measure the whole flow from arrival through compliance, review and closure, including cases still open. Run a small randomized routing pilot only within that eligible population, while independently testing added queue or compliance capacity in selected operating blocks. The primary routing estimate should compare cases by their randomized assignment, including cases whose actual routing deviates; report completion over time and the full cycle-time distribution, not only the mean among completed cases. Monitor workload and outcomes for cases outside the pilot because moving work can change both queues.

Predefine operational and quality limits, follow-up, sample size or pilot duration, and a decision rule with the operations and compliance teams. Compare appeals and audited decision quality as well as time, recognizing that appeals are delayed and rare. Scale only if results and capacity support it; the pilot's estimate applies to its eligible cases and workload, so a larger transfer needs renewed monitoring for congestion and spillovers.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Within-category case risk:** The single most valuable additional field is a detailed, pre-routing severity score capturing the risk used by the routing engine.
2. **Causal effect of assignment:** The most valuable design change is randomized routing among cases eligible for both groups, stratified by shift and capacity.
3. **Location and cause of waiting:** The most valuable additional data is a timestamped event history including queue entry and exit, compliance start and end, and review start and end.
4. **Bias from completed-case selection:** The most valuable design change is an inception cohort containing every arriving case, including open and abandoned cases, with a common observation cutoff.
5. **Decision quality after reassignment:** The most valuable additional field is an independent, blinded audit of decision correctness using the same follow-up protocol for both groups.
