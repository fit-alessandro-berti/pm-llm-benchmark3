# 1. Assumptions and notation

**A1 (Event-order convention):**
Events are ordered by timestamp, with ties resolved by:
1. Resource availability (e.g., a case cannot start before its resource is free).
2. Queue entry time (earlier arrivals take precedence).
3. For simultaneous rounded events (e.g., two cases completing S1 at 08:25 ±2.5 min), we assume a **FIFO queue** with no preemption.

**A2 (Rounding treatment):**
All timestamps are ±2.5 minutes. For overlapping intervals, we use the **earliest possible start** and **latest possible end** to bound estimates. E.g., a reported S1 start of 08:05 could be [07:57.5, 08:07.5].

**A3 (Capacity assumptions):**
- S1: 2 resources (I1, I2) with **no parallel processing** (A3a).
- S2: 2 resources (C1, C2) with **no parallel processing** (A3b), except during C2’s outage (10:55–11:10), when only C1 is available.

**A4 (Imputation/censoring assumptions):**
- **MISSING S1 start**: Imputed as the earliest possible time after arrival_s1 and before s1_end, constrained by resource availability. For P07, P11, P15:
  - P07: I1 was free at 08:45 (arrival)  start  [08:45, 10:00] (s1_end).
  - P11: I1 was free at 09:25 (arrival)  start  [09:25, 11:05].
  - P15: I1 was free at 10:10 (arrival)  start  [10:10, 12:15].
- **OPEN activities**: Service time is censored at 14:00. For P15 (S2), we assume service time = elapsed time (13:30–14:00 = 30 min) + plausible residual (e.g., 10 min)  range [30, 40] min.
- **WAITING_FOR_S1/S2**: No service time recorded; waiting time = (14:00 – arrival_s1) for S1, or (14:00 – s1_end) for S2.

**A5 (Little’s Law applicability):**
Applied only to intervals where:
- Throughput is stable (e.g., no major disruptions).
- WIP is stationary (e.g., no large batches).
- We use **interval-averaged** WIP and throughput.

---

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

**Definitions:**
- **Queue waiting time (W):** Time from arrival to service start.
- **Service time (S):** Time from start to end (observed or imputed).
- **Sojourn time (T):** W + S.
- **WIP (Work-in-Progress):** Number of cases in queue + active service.
- **Throughput ():** Cases completed per interval.
- **Utilization ():** (Total service time) / (interval duration × capacity).

**Formulas:**
1. **Waiting time (W):**
   - For S1: `W = s1_start – arrival_s1` (or imputed if s1_start is MISSING).
   - For S2: `W = s2_start – s1_end` (or imputed if s2_start is MISSING).
2. **Service time (S):**
   - Observed: `s_end – s_start`.
   - Imputed (MISSING start): `s_end – s_start_min` (lower bound) or `s_end – s_start_max` (upper bound).
3. **Sojourn time (T):**
   - `T = W + S`.
4. **WIP:**
   - At time *t*: Cases with `s_start < t < s_end` (active) + cases waiting (no `s_start` recorded).
5. **Little’s Law:**
   - `WIP =  × T` (applied to intervals with stable  and T).

**Uncertainty handling:**
- For MISSING starts, we compute **lower/central/upper bounds** using resource constraints.
- For OPEN cases, we use **elapsed time + plausible residual** (e.g., 10 min).

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

**Evidence:**
1. **S1 bottleneck (08:00–10:00):**
   - High WIP (2.1–3.2 cases) and utilization (75–83%) despite 2 resources.
   - P03 and P12 had long waits (20+ min) due to I1/I2’s prior cases.
2. **S2 bottleneck (10:00–11:10):**
   - C2’s outage (10:55–11:10) forced P06/P08 to wait, increasing S2 WIP to 3.5.
   - P08’s S2 wait (65 min) was the longest observed.
3. **Time-varying bottleneck:**
   - **08:00–10:00**: S1 (resource contention).
   - **10:00–11:10**: S2 (C2 outage + backlog).
   - **11:10–14:00**: S2 (OPEN cases reduce throughput).

**Hypotheses:**
- **S1’s imbalance**: I1/I2 may have different speeds (e.g., I1’s 60-min service for P07 vs. I2’s 30 min).
- **S2’s resilience**: After C2’s return, S2 recovers (utilization back to 80–90%).

**Maintenance impact:**
- C2’s outage caused a **15-min delay** for P06/P08 and increased S2 WIP by ~1 case.

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

**Scenarios:**
1. **Optimistic (shortest waits):**
   - Assume all MISSING starts are at arrival time (e.g., P07 starts at 08:45).
   - S1 WIP drops to 1.5 cases (08:00–10:00).
   - S2 utilization rises to 95% (10:00–11:10).
2. **Central (midpoint imputations):**
   - S1 starts imputed as midpoint of [arrival, s1_end].
   - S2 starts imputed as midpoint of [s1_end, s2_end].
   - Results match Table 4.
3. **Pessimistic (longest waits):**
   - Assume all MISSING starts are at s1_end – 1 min (e.g., P07 starts at 09:59).
   - S1 WIP rises to 3.5 cases (08:00–10:00).
   - S2 utilization drops to 60% (10:00–11:10).

**Stable conclusions:**
- S2 is the bottleneck during C2’s outage.
- S1’s utilization is consistently high (>75%).

**Unstable conclusions:**
- Exact WIP/Wait bounds vary by ±1 case (±30 min) under optimistic/pessimistic imputations.

        'service_time': 'first'
