## 1. Temporal Data Semantics

Within a case, order events by the UTC key \(k(e)=(\text{time:timestamp},\text{event_index})\), ascending and lexicographic. Apply each explicit transaction attribute update before taking that event's snapshot. A blank carries the previous known value forward. An attribute without a prior observation is unknown. Keep both the raw updates and the reconstructed snapshots. A list update's jurisdiction identifies the list entry, so it belongs to the sanctions stream. The transaction jurisdiction still comes from transaction updates.

**Event time** dates the business event. **Effective time** determines when a fact governs the policy and defaults to event time unless separate evidence establishes another time. **Record time** dates insertion or capture, such as the 11:00 exemption inserts. **Evidence time** includes times independently established by evidence, such as a trusted signature timestamp, and can differ from when the evidence becomes available to the assessor. Preserve these distinctions when assessing earlier releases. A later record can prove an earlier fact if it is reliably linked to that fact and time. The TX-305 settlement observation establishes EUR 150,000 at settlement only.

For R1, I treat changes to known jurisdiction, risk level, or customer type as material changes to the review context. For amount, I use R4's strictly greater than 20% test, measured from the amount captured by the most recent Standard_Review or Enhanced_Review when the increase occurs. Successive increases can cumulatively cross the threshold. A later decrease cannot by itself erase a recorded invalidation. A new review establishes a new amount baseline, and an Enhanced_Review must follow the latest material change to satisfy R1. A first observation after review can leave freshness unverified if the earlier value was unknown. The policy leaves room for a different definition of materiality outside R4.

The release snapshot includes updates on the release row. Required prior events must have a strictly earlier key. TX-311's Enhanced_Review sees US at key (16:20, 3). The change to DE takes effect at (16:20, 4), and the release sees DE. The final snapshot never rewrites a prior review or release snapshot.

Let \(x<y\) mean \(x\) occurs earlier by the ordered key. A state fact is visible at \(e\) if its effective key is \(\le k(e)\) and no later effective update to that field is visible. Exemption time comparisons use the approved/effective instants and any evidence of source order where needed. I use an inclusive effective start and an exclusive expiry.

- **R1.** At release \(r\), \(T_1(r)=(J_r\ne\mathrm{DE})\land(A_r\ge100000)\). If true, require an Enhanced_Review \(h<r\) such that every applicable material change effective by \(r\) precedes \(h\), and the review's coverage is supported, or require a valid waiver for R1 at \(r\). A known intervening material change defeats freshness. A decisive unresolved change or an unsupported reviewed amount can leave freshness unknown.
- **R2.** \(T_2(r)=(Risk_r=\mathrm{HIGH})\lor(Type_r=\mathrm{PEP})\). If true, require \(h<a<r\), with \(h\) an Enhanced_Review addressing the established active grounds and \(a\) a Compliance_Approve covering \(h\). An explicit packet link governs. With sequence evidence alone, a relevant ordered pair is a proxy. R4 applies to amount-dependent duties, so an R4 issue alone does not invalidate this pair for R2.
- **R3.** For each addition \(u<r\) of \(J_r\) with a same-jurisdiction screen \(s_0<u\), require a same-jurisdiction screen \(s_1\) with \(u<s_1<r\). \(s_0\) can be taken as the latest qualifying screen before \(u\). Evaluating additions this way shows duties that were incurred and subsequently satisfied. A jurisdiction-aware list feed can supply additions from outside the current case. R3 as stated has a prior-screen premise.
- **R4.** For each amount update \(d\), let \(v\) be the latest Standard_Review or Enhanced_Review strictly before \(d\). If the amount increases at \(d\) and \(A_d>1.20A_v\), record a material change and invalidate \(v\) for amount-dependent use. If the baseline is unknown and an increase exceeding the threshold is possible, retain uncertainty. Preserve the change marker across reviews so that a later Standard_Review cannot refresh an Enhanced_Review that predates it.
- **R5.** \(ValidWaiver(w,i,r)=AuthorizedHead(w)\land TimelyApprovalEvidence(w,r)\land(EffectiveFrom_w\le t_r<Expiry_w)\land ScopeMatch(w,J_r,Type_r,A_r)\land Waives(w,i)\). A record inserted after \(r\) may substantiate timely approval. A displayed time and scope match alone leave the approval-time predicate unproven.
- **R6.** Evaluate unknown predicates with three-valued logic. For example, true OR unknown is true, and false AND unknown is false. An established failure gives NONCOMPLIANT. An unresolved fact gives INDETERMINATE when resolving it could change the result and no independent established failure already determines the classification.
- **R7.** Apply the most recent effective update at or before each snapshot. Use strict key comparisons for “before” and “after.” A later observation supplies historical state only when evidence establishes its earlier effective applicability.

## 4. Ambiguities and Alternative Readings

- **TX-302.** The 09:40 reference and 11:00 database insert alone would leave the waiver's timing unproven. The authenticated 09:05 signature, valid hash, and outage evidence support timely approval. The case remains COMPLIANT if the waiver evidence is removed because the Enhanced_Review independently satisfies R1. If that review were shown to be stale or incomplete and the waiver evidence held, the result would be JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. A reporting convention that records every valid exemption claim could also label this release as a justified exception while recognizing the independently sufficient review.
- **TX-305.** An immutable release payload below EUR 100,000 would exclude R1 and support COMPLIANT on the supplied history. A payload at or above EUR 100,000 would establish R1. A versioned review packet showing the same relevant data, or evidence tying the review to the release and ruling out a material change, could then establish satisfaction. Evidence of a material increase after review with no renewed review would establish NONCOMPLIANT. A literal activity-and-recorded-change reading could treat the Enhanced_Review as sufficient in any triggering scenario if review coverage and the completeness of material-change capture are independently established. Neither is established by this record. Copying the settlement amount backward cannot establish either applicability or freshness.
- **TX-310.** A trusted, release-linked pre-10:00 approval artifact or independent contemporaneous corroboration of authorized oral approval, if the policy accepts oral approval, could support JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. Evidence that approval first occurred after 10:00, or a policy that requires contemporaneous verifiable approval before release, would yield NONCOMPLIANT. An operational control requiring verified evidence would have withheld release. The retrospective classification leaves actual oral approval unresolved.
- **TX-311.** Reversing the two 16:20 events would make the review snapshot DE while leaving the release result unchanged. Evidence that the DE update actually took effect after release would make the release state US at EUR 115,000. R1 would then activate and the 16:20 review could satisfy it if its US coverage and freshness were confirmed. A material change to a different foreign jurisdiction after the review and before release would instead activate R1 with a stale review under the stated materiality convention.
- **TX-306 and TX-308.** Both pass under R4's threshold and the stated R1 materiality convention. An explicit policy that treats certain scope changes or amount decreases as independently material for R1 could require another review. A requirement to reapprove an otherwise current risk/PEP packet following amount edits would be an additional R2 constraint. Neither follows from the supplied R4 rule.

## 5. PM4Py and pandas Checking Code

The checker produces one diagnostic row per release, keyed by case and release event. It assumes the supplied review activities record completions and uses the case jurisdiction at each screen as its scope. It takes the supplied list history as the list feed and extracts the addition from the explicit “added” note. For approval coverage, it uses an ordered relevant review-approval pair as a proxy and excludes the explicitly standard-only packet. Late exemption records and settlement events are attached as evidence. Neither supplies values to an earlier snapshot. The coarse result uses REVIEW_REQUIRED for cases that need evidential adjudication.

def r1_trigger(jurisdiction, amount):
    if jurisdiction == "DE" or (amount is not None and amount < 100_000):
        return "FALSE"
    if jurisdiction is None or amount is None:
        return "UNKNOWN"
    return "TRUE"

def r1_review_status(enhanced, material, possible_material):
    if enhanced is None:
        return "MISSING"
    if material is not None and key(material) >= key(enhanced):
        return "STALE"
    if (state(enhanced, "amount") is None or
        state(enhanced, "jurisdiction") is None or
        (possible_material is not None and key(possible_material) >= key(enhanced))):
        return "UNVERIFIED"
    return "CURRENT"

def r2_check(release, enhanced_reviews, approvals, activated_at):
    active = {field: value for field, value in R2_DRIVERS.items()
              if state(release, field) == value}
    if not active:
        if any(state(release, field) is None for field in R2_DRIVERS):
            return "UNKNOWN", None, None
        return "NOT_TRIGGERED", None, None

    for approval in reversed(approvals):
        if "standard review packet" in (known(approval["note"]) or "").lower():
            continue
        for review in reversed(enhanced_reviews):
            if key(review) < key(approval) and all(
                state(review, field) == value and activated_at[field] <= key(review)
                for field, value in active.items()
            ):
                return "PASS", key(review), key(approval)
    return "FAIL", None, None

def r3_check(release, screens):
    jurisdiction = state(release, "jurisdiction")
    release_key = key(release)
    unresolved = satisfied = covered_if_triggered = False
    for addition in additions:
        target, addition_key = known(addition["jurisdiction"]), key(addition)
        if addition_key > release_key or (
            target is not None and jurisdiction is not None and target != jurisdiction
        ):
            continue
        same = [key(screen) for screen in screens
                if target is not None and state(screen, "jurisdiction") == target]
        uncertain = [
            key(screen) for screen in screens
            if state(screen, "jurisdiction") is None or target is None
        ]
        known_before = any(screen_key < addition_key for screen_key in same)
        known_after = any(addition_key < screen_key for screen_key in same)
        possible_before = (known_before or addition_key in same or
                           any(screen_key <= addition_key for screen_key in uncertain))
        possible_after = (addition_key in same or
                          any(addition_key <= screen_key for screen_key in uncertain))
        if not possible_before:
            continue
        definite_duty = (known_before and jurisdiction is not None and
                         jurisdiction == target and addition_key < release_key)
        if known_after:
            satisfied |= definite_duty
            covered_if_triggered |= not definite_duty
        elif definite_duty and not possible_after:
            return "FAIL"
        else:
            unresolved = True
    if unresolved:
        return "UNKNOWN"
    if satisfied:
        return "PASS"
    return "COVERED_IF_TRIGGERED" if covered_if_triggered else "NOT_TRIGGERED"

## 6. Why Control-Flow-Only Conformance Is Insufficient

1. **Conditional activation.** An unconditional rule requiring every release to follow an Enhanced_Review would misclassify releases exempt from that duty by their state. Trigger evaluation needs the release-time thresholds, jurisdiction, and risk or PEP predicates.
2. **Final-state leakage.** Using the last case amount categorizes TX-305's release with the next-day settlement value. Using TX-311's final jurisdiction for every event assigns DE to a review that actually saw US.
3. **Review freshness.** TX-309 has an Enhanced_Review before release, yet an intervening material amount increase made it stale. TX-306 also has an intervening amount update, but its size differs enough to change the decision.
4. **Post-hoc exemptions.** The temporal pattern “release, then record exemption” is identical in the material respects for TX-302 and TX-310. Signature provenance, scope, expiry, and evidence of earlier approval determine whether either claim can waive R1.
5. **Missing data.** An activity trace contains no representation of the unknown release amount in TX-305 or the evidence needed to resolve it. Treating absence as false can suppress a duty. Treating it as true can create an unsupported finding.
6. **Equal timestamps.** Timestamp-only ordering can move the DE update ahead of the TX-311 review and change which state the review covered. Stable source order resolves this case.
7. **External lists and packet linkage.** A screen's adequacy can depend on a jurisdiction-specific update outside the transaction trace. Approval activity alone also cannot establish which review packet was approved. TX-307's approval of a standard packet cannot satisfy R2.

A control-flow check can flag an unconditional pattern such as “some Enhanced_Review precedes release.” A data-aware check also needs obligation activation, review coverage and freshness, the evidence behind any waiver, and uncertainty about decisive facts.

## 7. Recommended Logging and Control Improvements

- Capture an immutable release payload with amount, currency and any EUR conversion basis, jurisdiction, risk, customer type, exemption ID, and the exact input version used by the decision service. Link any settlement event to the release and record the reason and evidence for discrepancies.
- Preserve append-only changes with old and new values, business effective time, event time, record or ingestion time, source, actor, and reason. Distinguish a missing observation, an explicit withdrawal of a value, and an unchanged value. Log any retrospective correction and its evidential basis.
- Store a stable event ID and source sequence alongside UTC timestamps. Preserve the tie-breaker through export, and record causal links or a comparable order for events whose ordering matters across streams.
- Store each completed review's type, event ID, completion time, reviewed snapshot, and packet version. Link each Compliance_Approve to the precise packet. Record review invalidation and its triggering revision, amount baseline, and materiality decision.
- Record an effective-dated sanctions feed with jurisdiction, action, list version, publication and effective times, and source provenance. Log each screen's jurisdiction, list version and result, and schedule a fresh screen when an effective update invalidates a prior screen.
- Store exemption scope in structured fields with waived rule IDs, scoped jurisdictions and customer types, amount bounds and currency, effective and expiry times, authorized approver, artifact hash, verified signature timestamp, and database record time. Preserve independent approval evidence when approvals are recorded late. If oral approval is permitted, capture it contemporaneously through an independently attributable record.
- Store the rule version, evaluated predicates and unknowns, decision, and supporting event IDs with each release. Gate release on required current reviews, approvals, screens, or proven exemptions. Route decisive unknowns to a recorded resolution before releasing funds.
