## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

I use additive repairs: observed events, objects, and relations are preserved, and absence of a relation is treated as unknown rather than as a negative fact.

- **Hard invariant:** Relation endpoints must exist; O2O direction and qualifier must be preserved; no duplicate relations, invented objects, or invented events. `DEL-Y carries PK-8`, for example, is directionally valid, whereas reversing it would not be.
- **Hard invariant for these bundles:** Lifecycle repairs may not contradict timestamps. Thus packing must follow item creation, dispatch must follow packing, and the proposed invoice/payment relations must respect their observed event times.
- **Strong default:** Apply the activity cardinalities in the domain rules. In particular, item-only `Pack Item`, package-only `Dispatch Package`, and payment-only `Record Payment` rows are candidates for missing relations, but can also be legitimate exceptions.
- **Strong default:** Existing O2O structure constrains interpretation. For example, ORD-A already contains items in both PK-7 and PK-8, so an ORD-A delivery confirmation could plausibly concern either or both delivery routes.
- **Weak heuristic:** Identifier similarity generates candidates. `PK8-2` strongly suggests PK-8, especially after `PK8-1`; `N-421?` strongly suggests DEL-Y. Conversely, `N-42*` matches both DEL-X and DEL-Y and cannot discriminate between them.
- **Weak heuristic:** Timestamps support local reconciliation and lifecycle feasibility, but the identical dispatch timestamps do not prove bulk dispatch. Customer, route, and amount evidence is weaker still: every relevant route is NORTH, C17 is shared by both orders, and no item weights support weight-based reasoning.
- **Evidence against a repair:** A repair is weakened when it depends only on arithmetic, orphan removal, lifecycle completion, or an O2O edge inferred from the very E2O edge it is supposed to corroborate. Adding DEL-X to ev10 is also weakened by its existing N-420 dispatch and the better DEL-Y identifier match.

Identifier evidence proposes candidates; lifecycle, cardinality, and existing structure test their coherence. Amounts such as \(130+100=230\) and \(100+130=230\) are corroboration only, not proof. Confidence values below are judgmental edge-support scores, not probabilities that must sum to one.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A: conservative/minimum-change repair.** A accepts P1/P3 and includes P2/P4 as provisional structural reconciliation. Thus ev08 concerns IT-B1 and PK-8, making PK-8 a plausible two-item package; ev10 dispatches PK-8 through DEL-Y, while DEL-X remains associated only with PK-7. Ev11 remains related only to ORD-A: this records no delivery identifier and does **not** assert that no delivery occurred. INV-44’s observed billing scope remains unchanged, with ORD-B’s billing status unresolved. PAY-2 remains payment-only, compatible with unapplied cash or an unidentified invoice. These choices yield feasible packing, dispatch, invoicing, and payment times without completing every lifecycle.

**Bundle B: consolidated-C17 interpretation.** B starts with A and jointly promotes P7/P8, P10/P11, and P12/P13. Ev11 is interpreted as acknowledging both deliveries together, not as evidence for either one uniquely. INV-44 bills both orders, and two payments settle that invoice. B does not add P9 merely to complete ORD-B. The result is coherent: ORD-A is split across two packages, ORD-B’s item shares the second package under A, both deliveries are acknowledged together, and the invoice has permitted one-to-many order and payment structure. It is nevertheless a stacked hypothesis; its amount consistency does not independently prove its financial edges. Other mixed bundles remain possible.

**Bundle C: weaker identifier-error interpretation.** C retains the ev08 repair but replaces P3/P4 with ev10DEL-X and `DEL-X carries PK-8`. DEL-Y remains a possibly preregistered but unused object, and ev11 remains order-only. This yields a coherent one-delivery/two-package lifecycle, but is weaker because it must explain away the good DEL-Y identifier match. It illustrates why removing DEL-Y’s orphan status is not itself a repair objective.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

- **Order:** ORD-A has creation, item additions, an order-level confirmation, and invoicing. ORD-B has creation and item addition, with packing/dispatch reachable indirectly under A. Its missing confirmation or billing relation is not repaired merely for completeness. Existing `contains` cardinality is ordinary: two items for ORD-A and one for ORD-B.
- **Item:** Every item is added before packing. Under A, each item occupies one package; there is no item–package many-to-many relation. IT-B1’s formerly unusual item-only packing row becomes normal, but that was not mandatory.
- **Package:** PK-7 has packing followed by dispatch. PK-8 has two packing events followed by dispatch. `PK-8 packs {IT-A2, IT-B1}` is a plausible one-to-many relation, not an automatic cardinality violation.
- **Delivery:** DEL-X and DEL-Y each have one dispatch and one carried package in A. Neither has a recorded delivery-confirmation event. B adds one shared confirmation event to both, which is permitted but creates substantial synchronization semantics. C would instead give DEL-X two packages and leave DEL-Y unused.
- **Invoice:** In A, INV-44 has issue and one observed payment; ORD-B’s possible inclusion remains open. In B, one invoice bills two orders and receives two payments—both plausible one-to-many structures, not proof of consolidated billing.
- **Payment:** Both payments have their own `Record Payment` event. PAY-1 is independently linked to INV-44; PAY-2 remains invoice-less in A. B gives each payment one invoice, not one payment many invoices.

No event is objectless: even unrepaired ev08, ev10, ev11, and ev14 retain an item, package, order, or payment respectively. DEL-Y is the clearest orphan object before repair and becomes connected in A.

Independent E2O-plus-O2O corroboration already exists for the observed order–item containments, PK-7/IT-A1, PK-8/IT-A2, DEL-X/PK-7, INV-44/ORD-A, and PAY-1/INV-44. By contrast, P2 cannot independently confirm P1, nor P4 independently confirm P3; B’s inferred financial O2O edges have the same limitation. Treating those derived edges as additional evidence would be circular.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

- **OC-DFG:** In the unrepaired log, PK-8 has one packing participation and DEL-Y has no delivery lifecycle. A adds a Package-perspective `Pack Item  Pack Item` self-loop on PK-8, followed by `Dispatch Package`; DEL-Y contributes a second single-dispatch instance, possibly changing frequencies more than type-level topology. B adds `Dispatch Package  Confirm Delivery` for both delivery objects. On Invoice, B adds a `Record Payment  Record Payment` self-loop. On Order, B gives ORD-B the new suffix `Add Item  Issue Invoice`.
- **OTG:** The new qualifiers already occur elsewhere (`packs`, `carries`, `bills`, and `settles`), so a type/qualifier-level OTG may remain structurally unchanged while instance connectivity and multiplicities change substantially. If an implementation also derives object interactions from event co-participation, B’s ev11 introduces Order–Delivery and Delivery–Delivery interaction around one event.
- **ET–OT graph:** Unrepaired `Pack Item` has one item-only exception, `Dispatch Package` has one package-only exception, and `Record Payment` has one payment-only exception. A removes the first two exceptions; B removes the payment-only exception. B also changes `Confirm Delivery` from Order-only to one Order plus two Deliveries, and `Issue Invoice` from one Order to two Orders at ev12.
- **Flattenings:** Without O2O propagation, Order flattening is unchanged by A. B changes ORD-B from `Create Order  Add Item` to include `Issue Invoice`, duplicating shared ev12 across two flattened order cases. Package flattening adds ev08 to PK-8 in A. Delivery flattening introduces DEL-Y’s ev10 in A; B appends shared ev11 to both delivery traces. Invoice flattening gains ev14 only in B. Payment flattening remains two single-event payment traces because PAY-2 already had ev14.
- **Frequencies:** Raw event/activity counts never change. A increases Pack Item object-event participations from 5 to 6 and Dispatch Package participations from 3 to 4. B additionally increases Confirm Delivery from 1 to 3, Issue Invoice from 2 to 3, and Record Payment from 3 to 4. Distinct-object counts can remain unchanged—for example, PK-8 and INV-44 were already present at those activities—so event counts, unique objects, and total object participations must not be conflated.
- **Equivalent-execution clustering around Order:** A leaves strict direct-E2O order traces unchanged, so it need not change strict trace clustering. In object-graph clustering, however, P2 connects ORD-A and ORD-B through shared PK-8, potentially merging previously separate components. B strengthens this coupling through shared invoice/payment structure. Their activity sequences still differ, so neither repair automatically makes the orders equivalent executions.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Adding every ev11 candidate** would turn one portal event into a two-order/two-delivery synchronization, create false `Dispatch Package  Confirm Delivery` lifecycle edges, and duplicate ev11 across flattened cases.
2. **Using invoice arithmetic as decisive evidence** could merge ORD-A and ORD-B financially, hiding separate billing, fees, or an uninvoiced order and changing order-based execution clusters.
3. **Using payment arithmetic as decisive evidence** could make INV-44 appear fully settled, introduce a spurious invoice-level payment self-loop, and conceal genuinely unapplied cash.
4. **Attaching IT-B1 to both packages “for safety”** would create unsupported item–package many-to-many behavior and could distort package weights, routes, and delivery ownership.
5. **Adding DEL-X alongside DEL-Y to ev10** could make PK-8 appear dispatched by two deliveries, while interpreting the identical timestamps as bulk dispatch could falsely connect both packages to both deliveries.
6. **Adding ORD-B to ev11 merely to complete its lifecycle** would manufacture a delivery confirmation from shared customer and route attributes that do not discriminate between orders.
7. **Propagating inferred O2O edges back into E2O and then counting them as validation** would be circular. It could also duplicate shared events across flattenings and invent directly-follows behavior that no observed event relation supports.
