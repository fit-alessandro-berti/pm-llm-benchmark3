## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional verdict:** `not identifiable from the supplied evidence`  
**Confidence:** 82 / 100

The available data decisively refute a naive causal reading of the aggregate gap, yet do not identify the true causal effect of assigning a case to Orion versus Nova. Crudely, Orion cases average 21.96h compared with Nova’s 15.24h (+6.72h). However, this aggregate penalty reverses in every observed complexity stratum: Orion is faster by 2.14h in Low (7.50h vs 9.64h), 2.21h in Medium (17.17h vs 19.38h), and 3.43h in High (31.57h vs 35.00h), yielding a complexity-standardized advantage of -2.51h for Orion.

Two forces prevent causal identification. First, extreme non-overlap prevents separating resource group from shift and arrival load: 92.9% (13/14) of Orion cases occur at night under high load, with zero Orion Day cases in Medium or High complexity, and only four Nova Night cases total. Second, 82%–96% of Orion’s within-stratum advantage occurs in `wait_before_review_h` rather than `review_service_h`, meaning the apparent advantage could reflect night-shift queue dynamics, unobserved case-risk differences within coarse complexity bands, or selection into the completed-case sample (`case_completed = 1`) rather than superior reviewer performance.

Reassigning 50% of future Orion work to Nova could therefore **fail or backfire**: if Orion’s faster service and lower/equal within-stratum rework reflect certified reviewers handling complex cases efficiently, shifting half of Orion’s difficult cases onto Nova—especially without matching night staffing or compliance capacity—would likely lengthen queue waits, increase rework, and worsen total cycle time.

| **Phase 1: Instrumentation & Baseline Audit** (Weeks 1–2) | No routing change; capture full event timestamps (`Start`/`Complete` per activity), WIP counts at `Classify`, open/abandoned cases, and reviewer IDs | Measurement bias, survivorship censoring, and queue-state confounding | All incoming cases (observational cohort) | Zero operational disruption; read-only logging | Activity-level sojourn vs service times (`Classify->Compliance`, `Compliance->Review`, `Review->Request Info`); survival curves including open cases; DFG conformance | Separates queueing from compliance and service, and tests whether `case_completed` censoring distorts Orion vs Nova comparisons | Halt pilot progression if logging completeness < 98% or if open-case audit shows severe hidden Orion backlog |
| **Phase 2: Controlled Quasi-Random / Stratified Pilot** (Weeks 3–6) | Route 15% of eligible **Low and Medium** cases during overlapping staffing windows via 1:1 randomized assignment (or deterministic hash on case ID) between Orion and Nova | Direct causal effect of `resource_group` on `review_service`, `rework`, and `queue_wait` with true positivity | Individual case at `Classify` exit (stratified by `complexity` and `shift`) | Exclude High-complexity cases initially; cap Nova’s High/Med WIP backlog; ensure certified-reviewer backup on Nova | Within-stratum ATE on total `cycle_time`, initial `queue_wait`, initial vs repeat `review_service`, `rework` rate, and 30-day appeals | Achieves true overlap (`positivity`) and eliminates `unobserved_case_risk` routing confounding for Low/Medium strata | Roll back immediately if pilot Nova Med/Low cycle time exceeds baseline p90 by >20%, rework rises >5 percentage points, or SLA breaches double |
| **Phase 3: Queueing & Compliance Capacity Redesign** (Weeks 7–12) | Decouple and parallelize `Compliance Check` right after `Register` (predictive compliance pre-triage) and align night/day reviewer shift buffers to arrival WIP rather than moving 50% of Orion cases | Pre-review bottleneck (`wait_before_review_h` = ~58% of cycle time) and compliance handoff queueing | Shift-level compliance/review queues and pre-review workflow | Gradual rollout by complexity tier; monitor compliance false-negative rate | Reduction in `wait_before_review_h` and compliance-to-review handoff idle time; DFG variant shift (`Classify>Compliance` vs parallel) | Directly tests the largest time component (`queue_wait`) without destroying Orion’s certification advantage on difficult cases | Revert parallel compliance if compliance defect/appeal rate rises above historical control limits |

**Final recommendation:**  
Reject the proposal to blindly reassign 50% of Orion’s work to Nova. The crude +6.72h gap is driven by Orion handling 3.4× more High-complexity cases and 2.6× more compliance cases; within every complexity stratum, Orion is 2.1h–3.4h faster and has equal or lower rework. Moving half of Orion’s complex, night-heavy caseload onto Nova—where Day capacity, compliance experience, and certification density are lower—risks creating severe queues and higher rework, worsening overall cycle times.

Instead, execute the three-phase learning plan above. First, close the measurement blind spots by logging open cases, exact activity timestamps, and queue WIP so `wait_before_review_h` can be split into pure queueing versus compliance delay. Second, run a safeguarded 15% randomized routing pilot on Low and Medium cases in overlapping shift windows to obtain unbiased causal estimates of Orion versus Nova performance. Third, target the true operational lever—`wait_before_review_h`, which accounts for nearly 58% of total cycle time—by decoupling compliance bottlenecks and balancing shift-level capacity rather than arbitrarily relabeling resource groups.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Unrecorded open and abandoned cases (`case_completed = 1` conditioning):** We cannot tell whether Orion’s faster within-stratum cycle time is partly an artifact of uncompleted complex cases still sitting in Orion’s queue. *Most valuable addition:* Full initiation-cohort records including start timestamps and censoring status (`open`, `abandoned`, `completed`) for every registered case in the period.
2. **Confounding of `wait_before_review_h` by queue WIP and compliance waits:** Because `wait_before_review_h` bundles post-`Classify` queueing with waiting around `Compliance Check`, we cannot separate reviewer queue speed from compliance bottlenecking or arrival-time backlog. *Most valuable addition:* Activity-level `start` and `complete` timestamps for every event (`Register`, `Classify`, `Compliance Check`, `Review`, `Request Info`, `Close`).
3. **Near-complete non-overlap between `resource_group` and `shift`:** Having zero Orion Day cases in Medium/High and only one Nova Night case each in Medium/High makes shift and group statistically inseparable without strong extrapolation. *Most valuable addition:* A small randomized or quasi-random routing pilot assigning overlapping Day/Night cases to both groups.
4. **Unobserved case risk within coarse 3-level `complexity`:** The routing engine likely uses finer risk signals than `Low`/`Medium`/`High`, leaving residual confounding by `unobserved_case_risk` inside every stratum. *Most valuable addition:* Pre-routing propensity/risk score or the exact routing-engine feature inputs (claim amount, policy tenure, claim type, automated risk flags) logged at `Classify`.
5. **Reviewer-level skill vs group-level assignment:** Even if Orion performs better within strata, we do not know whether that stems from individual reviewer certification, team-level queue discipline, or shift-specific support. *Most valuable addition:* Anonymized `reviewer_id` and certification-tier attributes linked to each `Review` event.
