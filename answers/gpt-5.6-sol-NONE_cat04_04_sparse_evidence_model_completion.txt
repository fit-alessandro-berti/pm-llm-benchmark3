Abbreviations used below are `R` Register Order, `C` Credit Check, `E` Engineering Review, `S` Supplier Confirmation, `X` Expedite Review, `A` Approve Configuration, `P` Release Production, and `Z` Archive Case.

# 2. Shared Assumptions

1. Each trace represents one completed case and each listed event is interpreted as an **activity completion**, not an activity start.
2. Logged order is not automatically causal order. In particular, opposite C/E orders and timestamp-tie serialization are compatible with parallelism, overlap, or alternative sequential execution.
3. R is first, Z is last, and the suffix `A  P  Z` is mandatory.
4. C and E occur exactly once in these candidate models. X occurs zero or one time. No loops are inferred from the sample.
5. Whenever S is represented, it occurs exactly once before A. An absent S is represented by a silent/control-flow alternative.
6. The absent-S alternative is an abstraction over two observationally indistinguishable mechanisms: a legitimate stocked-component skip and an unlogged S event. It is **not** a claim that S is intrinsically optional for all orders.
7. The external/stocked attribute is unavailable, so none of the models can attach the S branch to its proper data condition.
8. Process-tree `+` denotes parallel execution with all interleavings of completion events; `X` denotes exclusive choice; `->` denotes sequence; `tau` is silent. POWL order edges denote strict precedence, not necessarily immediate succession.
9. The candidates describe visible control-flow languages. They do not model resources, durations, data guards, missing-event probabilities, or start/complete lifecycle pairs.

# 3. Conservative Candidate

The conservative candidate has an empirical-language bias: it admits only the seven distinct observed variants, regardless of frequency. This maximizes sample precision and avoids converting uncertain stakeholder statements into new behavior. It consequently overfits the finite log, duplicates labels across XOR branches, and treats rare traces as equally structurally valid once admitted.

**Compact expression**

\[
M_C=\operatorname{XOR}\left(
RCE SAPZ,\;
REC SAPZ,\;
RCEAPZ,\;
RECAPZ,\;
RCSEAPZ,\;
RCESXAPZ,\;
RECXSAPZ
\right)
\]

Spaces above only emphasize sections; each term is a complete sequence.

```python
import pm4py

# XOR enumeration of the seven distinct observed variants.
# Repeated labels in different branches denote separate tree leaves with the
# same visible activity label.
model_conservative = pm4py.parse_process_tree(

    "->('Register Order','Credit Check','Engineering Review',"
       "'Supplier Confirmation','Approve Configuration',"
       "'Release Production','Archive Case'),"
    "->('Register Order','Engineering Review','Credit Check',"
       "'Supplier Confirmation','Approve Configuration',"
       "'Release Production','Archive Case'),"
    "->('Register Order','Credit Check','Engineering Review',"
       "'Approve Configuration','Release Production','Archive Case'),"
    "->('Register Order','Engineering Review','Credit Check',"
       "'Approve Configuration','Release Production','Archive Case'),"
    "->('Register Order','Credit Check','Supplier Confirmation',"
       "'Engineering Review','Approve Configuration',"
       "'Release Production','Archive Case'),"
    "->('Register Order','Credit Check','Engineering Review',"
       "'Supplier Confirmation','Expedite Review',"
       "'Approve Configuration','Release Production','Archive Case'),"
    "->('Register Order','Engineering Review','Credit Check',"
       "'Expedite Review','Supplier Confirmation',"
       "'Approve Configuration','Release Production','Archive Case')"

```

Duplicate visible labels are acceptable for language enumeration but can make the converted Petri net larger and alignments less interpretable: multiple transitions with the same label represent branch-specific copies. The structure grows roughly with the total length of enumerated variants rather than with reusable business constraints, so it scales poorly as new variants arrive.

**Behavior deliberately excluded**

- Every unseen C/E/S/X interleaving, even if compatible with D3–D6.
- X in a case with no logged S.
- `R,E,S,C,A,P,Z`, although analogous to observed `R,C,S,E,A,P,Z`.
- `R,C,E,X,S,A,P,Z`, although S6 permits X before S.
- Repetition, loops, rework, early archive, and release before approval.
- Any trace with missing C, E, A, P, R, or Z.

**Known underfitting risks**

- It cannot generalize to routine but as-yet-unseen interleavings.
- It encodes accidental completion and ingestion orders as whole-trace constraints.
- It does not generalize S6 symmetrically.
- It cannot represent X in legitimate stocked-component cases.
- It provides no reusable causal explanation; a new valid variant requires another branch.
- It may reject future conforming work merely because 30 cases gave sparse coverage.

# 4. Balanced Candidate

The balanced candidate favors a compact partial-order explanation while restricting weakly supported combinations. It treats C and E as unordered completions because both orders are common and completion-only data cannot select sequence versus concurrency. S is optional observationally and can complete after either check, but not before both checks have begun; at completion level this is approximated by requiring at least one of C/E to precede S. X follows both checks. The model uses a controlled XOR over five POWL scenarios so that it captures observed and stakeholder-supported orderings without granting every unconstrained interleaving.

**Compact expression**

Let `PO(V, <)` denote all linearizations of a strict partial order:

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_B = R \cdot \operatorname{XOR}(&
PO(\{C,E\},\varnothing),\\
&PO(\{C,E,S\},\{C<S\}),\\
&PO(\{C,E,S\},\{E<S\}),\\
&PO(\{C,E,X\},\{C<X,E<X\}),\\
&PO(\{C,E,S,X\},\{C<X,E<X\})
) \cdot A \cdot P \cdot Z
\end{aligned}
\]

The two S branches enforce “at least one principal completion before S.” Their overlap deliberately creates duplicate derivations for traces where both checks precede S, but not duplicate visible events within one execution. In the `S+X` branch, X must follow both checks while S is unordered relative to the checks and X. The absence of a check-to-S edge there is necessary to admit observed `C,S,E,X`; requiring X after both checks still prevents approval before the checks.

**Exact ordering decisions**

- **C versus E:** no precedence edge. This is a partial-order/concurrency hypothesis, supported by V1–V5, S2, D7, and S7, but not proven.
- **S versus C/E:** without X, use an XOR of `C<S` and `E<S`, approximating D5 at completion level. This admits S after at least one completed check and allows the other check on either side.
- **X versus C/E:** add both `C<X` and `E<X`, directly encoding D6/S6.
- **S versus X:** add no edge, because V6/V7 and S6 support either order.
- **All prefix activities versus A:** the scenario block completes before A, ensuring D2–D4 and D6.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

def po(nodes, edges=()):
    block = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=nodes)
    for source, target in edges:
        block.order.add_edge(source, target)
    return block

# No logged Supplier Confirmation, no Expedite Review
c0, e0 = Transition("Credit Check"), Transition("Engineering Review")
no_s_no_x = po([c0, e0])

# Supplier Confirmation after at least one principal check:
# branch 1 requires C < S; branch 2 requires E < S.
c1, e1, s1 = (
    Transition("Credit Check"),
    Transition("Engineering Review"),
    Transition("Supplier Confirmation"),
)
s_after_c = po([c1, e1, s1], [(c1, s1)])

c2, e2, s2 = (
    Transition("Credit Check"),
    Transition("Engineering Review"),
    Transition("Supplier Confirmation"),
)
s_after_e = po([c2, e2, s2], [(e2, s2)])

# Expedite without a logged Supplier Confirmation
c3, e3, x3 = (
    Transition("Credit Check"),
    Transition("Engineering Review"),
    Transition("Expedite Review"),
)
x_no_s = po([c3, e3, x3], [(c3, x3), (e3, x3)])

# Supplier and expedite both present: X follows both checks;
# S and X have no fixed relative order.
c4, e4, s4, x4 = (
    Transition("Credit Check"),
    Transition("Engineering Review"),
    Transition("Supplier Confirmation"),
    Transition("Expedite Review"),
)
s_and_x = po(
    [c4, e4, s4, x4],
    [(c4, x4), (e4, x4)],
)

scenario = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[no_s_no_x, s_after_c, s_after_e, x_no_s, s_and_x],
)

r = Transition("Register Order")
a = Transition("Approve Configuration")
p = Transition("Release Production")
z = Transition("Archive Case")

model_balanced = po(
    [r, scenario, a, p, z],
    [(r, scenario), (scenario, a), (a, p), (p, z)],
)
```

**Behavior deliberately excluded**

- S as the first completion after R when X is absent.
- X before either C or E has completed.
- Approval before C or E, or before a present S/X.
- More than one S or X.
- Release before approval and archive before release.
- Any route omitting a principal check.

**Known underfitting risks**

- If S can legitimately complete before both checks, non-expedite traces of that form are rejected.
- If Finance’s strict `C finish < E start` rule is normative and enforced, the model is too broad.
- If X requires a present S for external orders, the `x_no_s` branch is too broad observationally.
- POWL incomparability permits sequential and overlapping implementations; it cannot assert actual runtime overlap.
- The S/X scenario permits S before both C and E, because S6 gives no relative restriction and D5 concerns start rather than completion. This is a deliberate but uncertain generalization.
- The model conflates missing S with true absence.

# 5. Permissive Candidate

The permissive candidate has a broad rule-consistency bias. It requires the invariant backbone—R first, C/E before approval, present S/X before approval, X after both checks, then `APZ`—while leaving S freely ordered against C, E, and X. This admits plausible unseen serializations and missing-event patterns without allowing omissions of the two principal checks, premature expedite, premature approval, repetition, or suffix violations.

**Compact expression**

\[
M_P = R \cdot
\operatorname{XOR}\left(
PO(\{C,E\},\varnothing),
PO(\{C,E,S\},\varnothing),
PO(\{C,E,X\},\{C<X,E<X\}),
PO(\{C,E,S,X\},\{C<X,E<X\})
\right)
\cdot A\cdot P\cdot Z
\]

**Boundary of reasonable generalization**

Reasonable generalization means varying the completion order of C, E, and present S, varying S/X order, and allowing observational absence of S or optional absence of X. It does **not** mean discarding high-confidence invariants: R and Z remain endpoints; C and E remain mandatory before A; X remains after both; S and X, if visible, remain before A; X is at most once; and `A<P<Z` is fixed.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

def po(nodes, edges=()):
    block = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=nodes)
    for source, target in edges:
        block.order.add_edge(source, target)
    return block

c0, e0 = Transition("Credit Check"), Transition("Engineering Review")
checks_only = po([c0, e0])

c1, e1, s1 = (
    Transition("Credit Check"),
    Transition("Engineering Review"),
    Transition("Supplier Confirmation"),
)
checks_supplier = po([c1, e1, s1])  # all completion orders

c2, e2, x2 = (
    Transition("Credit Check"),
    Transition("Engineering Review"),
    Transition("Expedite Review"),
)
checks_expedite = po([c2, e2, x2], [(c2, x2), (e2, x2)])

c3, e3, s3, x3 = (
    Transition("Credit Check"),
    Transition("Engineering Review"),
    Transition("Supplier Confirmation"),
    Transition("Expedite Review"),
)
checks_supplier_expedite = po(
    [c3, e3, s3, x3],
    [(c3, x3), (e3, x3)],
)

scenario = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[
        checks_only,
        checks_supplier,
        checks_expedite,
        checks_supplier_expedite,
    ],
)

r = Transition("Register Order")
a = Transition("Approve Configuration")
p = Transition("Release Production")
z = Transition("Archive Case")

model_permissive = po(
    [r, scenario, a, p, z],
    [(r, scenario), (scenario, a), (a, p), (p, z)],
)
```

**Behavior deliberately excluded**

- X before completion of C or E.
- Approval before either principal check.
- Approval before a visible S or X.
- Missing C or E.
- Multiple S or X occurrences.
- S after approval.
- Release before approval, or archive before release.
- Events before R or after Z.

**Known underfitting risks**

- It still excludes rework, cancellation, rejection, and exceptional archive paths.
- It cannot express the hidden external/stocked data guard.
- It excludes an X that starts after both checks but whose completion is logged after A; D6 suggests this is appropriate, but lifecycle semantics could complicate it.
- It may underfit genuine duplicate events caused by retries.

The primary risk is instead **overfitting the broad rules rather than the sample**: by interpreting incomparability liberally, it admits S completing before both checks, which D5 does not directly support.

# 6. Unobserved Behavior Audit

The conservative language is finite and, ignoring silent transitions and branch identity, equals the seven observed visible variants. It therefore admits no unobserved complete visible trace of any length.

# 8. Validation Code

The code below reconstructs the 30 cases, builds all candidates, converts them to accepting Petri nets, checks workflow-net soundness, computes available metrics, performs sampled playout, and evaluates a manual trace suite.     r, a, p, z = (
        Transition("Register Order"),
        Transition("Approve Configuration"),
        Transition("Release Production"),
        Transition("Archive Case"),
    )
    return po(
        [r, scenario, a, p, z],
        [(r, scenario), (scenario, a), (a, p), (p, z)],
    )

def make_permissive():

        Transition("Engineering Review"),

        Transition("Credit Check"),
        Transition("Engineering Review"),
        Transition("Expedite Review"),

    checks_expedite = po([c2, e2, x2], [(c2, x2), (e2, x2)])

        Transition("Credit Check"),
        Transition("Engineering Review"),
        Transition("Supplier Confirmation"),
        Transition("Expedite Review"),
    )
    checks_supplier_expedite = po(
        [c3, e3, s3, x3],
        [(c3, x3), (e3, x3)],

        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[
            checks_only,
            checks_supplier,
            checks_expedite,

    r, a, p, z = (
        Transition("Register Order"),
        Transition("Approve Configuration"),
        Transition("Release Production"),
        Transition("Archive Case"),
    )
    return po(
        [r, scenario, a, p, z],
        [(r, scenario), (scenario, a), (a, p), (p, z)],

    return pm4py.parse_event_log_string([",".join(labels)])

def numeric_fitness(result):
    # Common PM4Py return forms include a float or a dictionary.
    if isinstance(result, (int, float)):
        return float(result)
    if isinstance(result, dict):
        for key in (
            "average_trace_fitness",
            "averageFitness",
            "log_fitness",
            "perc_fit_traces",
        ):
            if key in result:
                value = float(result[key])
                if key == "perc_fit_traces":
                    value /= 100.0
                return value
    raise ValueError(f"Unrecognized fitness result: {result!r}")

    net, im, fm = accepting_nets[name]
    print(f"\n=== {name}: manual suite ===")
    failures = []

    for expected, traces in (("accept", cases["positive"]),
                             ("reject", cases["negative"])):
        for labels in traces:
            result = pm4py.fitness_alignments(
                single_trace_log(labels), net, im, fm
            )
            score = numeric_fitness(result)
            accepted = score >= 1.0 - 1e-9
            passed = accepted if expected == "accept" else not accepted
            print(
                "PASS" if passed else "FAIL",
                expected,
                f"fitness={score:.6f}",
                labels,
            )
            if not passed:
                failures.append((expected, labels, score))

All three proposed models are finite-language models because no loop operator is used, but generic PM4Py sampled playout still does not guarantee enumeration of every linearization. Alignment-based checks over a manually defined suite establish only those cases, not arbitrary language inclusion or equivalence. Exact model-to-model language comparison would require exhaustive reachability/language construction or formal automata methods and can become infeasible—or undecidable under richer net classes.

# 9. Recommendation and Data-Collection Plan

Use **`model_balanced` for immediate operational analysis**. It preserves the stable backbone and compliance constraints, fits all observations, allows the strongest evidence-backed unseen interleavings, and makes its uncertain choices explicit. It should not yet be used as proof that C and E execute simultaneously.

Use **`model_permissive` for sensitivity analysis and exploratory conformance**. Comparing deviations under balanced and permissive boundaries will identify cases whose classification depends primarily on weak assumptions about S ordering or missingness. Retain the conservative candidate as a historical baseline rather than the main operational model.

A concrete data-collection plan is:

1. **Export lifecycle transitions**
   - Record `start`, `complete`, `suspend`, and `resume` timestamps for C, E, S, and X.
   - Preserve timestamp precision and clock source.
   - Retain the original source sequence and ingestion timestamp separately.
   - This distinguishes actual overlap from alternate sequential orders and ingestion tie-breaking.

2. **Export case and component data**
   - `component_source_type`: external, stocked, mixed, unknown.
   - `supplier_confirmation_required`: boolean plus rule/version.
   - `supplier_confirmation_id` and source-system message ID.
   - `expedite_required`, trigger reason, approver, and policy version.
   - These attributes separate data-conditioned optionality from missing events.

3. **Measure missingness directly**
   - Reconcile order cases against the supplier system using immutable event IDs.
   - Record extraction status, connector retries, and late-arriving events.
   - Estimate S missingness by component type and time period rather than using one global percentage.
   - Never infer “stocked” solely from absence of S.

4. **Resolve C/E policy conflict**
   - Ask Finance whether S1 is a normative rule, a local practice, or a description of intended scheduling.
   - Audit C start, C complete, E start, and E complete for violations.
   - Determine whether E-first completions are allowed overlap or policy nonconformance.

5. **Collect maximally informative future cases**
   - A case with `C complete < E start` supports strict sequence.
   - A case with `E start < C complete` directly refutes S1 and supports overlap.
   - A case with `C start < E start < C complete < E complete`, or its reverse, confirms overlap.
   - External cases with and without a source-system S event distinguish mandatory-S and missingness hypotheses.
   - Stocked cases with an explicit “S not required” decision validate true optionality.
   - Cases where S starts after only C versus after only E test D5.
   - X cases with S both before and after X test S6.
   - A legitimate X case with explicitly stocked components tests whether X and S optionality are independent.
   - Repeated S or X events would test the current at-most-once assumptions.

6. **Evaluate prospectively**
   - Freeze all three models before collecting the next batch.
   - Report both control-flow conformance and data-condition conformance.
   - Compare predictive acceptance of new adjudicated traces, not merely retrospective fitness.
   - Review traces admitted only by the permissive model with domain owners.

# 10. Uncertainty Statement

| Modeling decision | Confidence | Basis and residual uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| R first and Z last | High | D1 and all 30 cases agree; exported-window effects remain possible |
| `A < P < Z` | Very high | D2 and unanimous observed suffix |
| C and E both precede A | Medium-to-high | D3 and all observations agree, but D3 is not labeled fully high confidence |
| C and E each occur exactly once | Medium | True in all observed cases; no explicit domain rule excludes rework |
| No fixed C/E completion order | High | Both orders are frequent and completion-only logging is known |
| Actual C/E concurrency or overlap | Medium-low | S2 is plausible, but D7/S7 prevent confirmation without lifecycle timestamps |
| S may be observationally absent | High | Seven cases lack S |
| S is truly optional for some cases | Medium | S4 is plausible, but the component-type attribute is absent |
| Any particular absent S is a legitimate skip | Low | S5 supplies a competing missing-event explanation |
| Present S must precede A | Medium-to-high | D4 and every observed S case agree |
| S completion can occur after either one of C/E | Medium | V5 proves one direction; D5 and variable completion duration support broader symmetry |
| S completion before both C/E is reasonable | Low-to-medium | Possible under completion semantics, but not directly observed and only indirectly supported by D5 |
| X is optional and at most once | Medium-to-high | D6/S6 and observations agree; only two X cases exist |
| X follows both principal completions and precedes A | Medium-to-high | D6/S6 agree; both X cases conform |
| S and X have no fixed relative order | Medium-to-high | Both orders occur once and S6 explicitly allows both |
| Balanced model is the best immediate compromise | Medium | Depends on accepting partial order as uncertainty representation rather than operational concurrency |

A materially different interpretation is that **S1 is a binding compliance rule and the seven E-first traces plus the E-first portions of other traces are policy violations or logging artifacts**. Under that interpretation, the recommended operational model should enforce `C complete < E start`, perhaps using a sequence-based ProcessTree, while nonconforming historical cases should be retained as deviations rather than fitted behavior. Conversely, if lifecycle data confirms routine overlap and shows S can complete before both checks, the permissive model—or a data-aware POWL model—would become the stronger recommendation.
