# 2. Benign alternative explanations

**A. CarlaGita fast approvals + GitaCarlaGita loop (36)**
1. *Designated emergency channel.* Gita is the documented delegated approver for urgent cases; medical supplies are time-critical. **Supports:** 88% emergency flag, known rush channel, low vendor concentration, low manual share, near-constant 11-min median (systemic SLA, not opportunistic). **Refutes:** emergency flags without incident tickets; fast approvals on non-emergency cases within the same pair; amounts clustered just below higher approval thresholds.
2. *Informal specialization / queue routing.* Carla is the medical-supplies specialist; Gita is the only urgent-case approver, so co-occurrence is structural. **Supports:** Gita's low total volume concentrated in urgent approvals; loop endpoints separated by clarification-type events. **Refutes:** pairing persists when other approvers are available; approval timestamp precedes quote receipt (sequence anomaly).

**B. DanHugo weekend repairs + HugoDanHugo loop (29)**
1. *On-call managerial override* for facilities failures outside business hours. **Supports:** weekend timestamps, 79% emergency share, Hugo's "exceptions" role. **Refutes:** overrides on business days with normal approvers available.
2. *Genuine escalation/clarification loop* — iterative repair scoping. **Supports:** intermediate events are information exchanges, not re-approvals. **Refutes:** loop used to re-approve after amount-splitting.

**C. LenaKim bank change  fast payment (V-77x) + 11-case loop**
1. *Banking-platform migration mass maintenance*, recorded per-case; payments released once the maintenance window closed. **Supports:** regional spread, clustered change dates, uniform vendor family, `manual_upload` tooling, 83% manual share. **Refutes:** changes on scattered dates targeting individual vendors; no maintenance tickets; failed/absent call-back verification; new account used for a first-time payment.
2. *Role-based pairing* — Kim handles higher-risk suppliers, Lena is the bank-change specialist. **Supports:** workload notes; maker-checker within vendor master. **Refutes:** the 9 same-resource cases — role separation would not produce them.
3. *Data-quality artifact* — missing ticket-to-case linkage makes co-occurrence look tighter than it is. **Supports/refutes:** linkage restored showing valid ticket IDs (supports) or no tickets (refutes).

**D. Same-person retrospective justifications (17)**
1. *Quarter-end backfill by support staff* under a manager's account (Temp_Nina support period). **Supports:** 12/17 identical timestamps, `quarter_close_upload`, `entered_by`  `org:resource`. **Refutes:** `entered_by` equals the override person; timestamps spread over time.
2. *Genuine late self-justification* — a control lapse, not concealment. **Supports:** distinct real-time timestamps, case-specific text. **Refutes:** copy-pasted identical text; justification timestamp preceding the override.

**E. Vendor Check + Bank Details by same person (31)**
1. *Vendor-master team design* with team-level maker-checker. **Supports:** SOP, maker-checker fields, workload spread across Lena *and* Marc. **Refutes:** one individual dominating; no second-person approval in vendor master.
2. *Small-team cross-coverage* during absences/peaks. **Supports:** roster records. **Refutes:** pattern invariant to roster.

### 4.1 Four-eyes filtering and artifact correction

```python
import pandas as pd, pm4py

df = pm4py.format_dataframe(pd.read_csv("p2p_log.csv", parse_dates=["time:timestamp"]),
                            case_id="case:concept:name",
                            activity_key="concept:name",
                            timestamp_key="time:timestamp")
RES = "org:resource"

# Raw violations (UPPER BOUND — includes artifacts)
viol_po   = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(df, "Create Purchase Order", "Approve Purchase Order", attribute_key=RES)
viol_bank = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(df, "Change Vendor Bank Details", "Payment Approval", attribute_key=RES)
viol_rj   = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(df, "Emergency Override", "Retrospective Justification", attribute_key=RES)

# Artifact-corrected recomputation using entered_by / approved_by
po = df[df["concept:name"].isin(["Create Purchase Order", "Approve Purchase Order"])]
agg = (po.groupby("case:concept:name")
         .agg(creators=("entered_by", lambda s: set(s.dropna())),
              approvers=("approved_by", lambda s: set(s.dropna())),
              systems=("source_system", lambda s: set(s.dropna()))))
agg["true_violation"] = [bool(c & a) and not (s & {"legacy_erp"})
                         for c, a, s in zip(agg.creators, agg.approvers, agg.systems)]
```

### 4.4 Timing: path performance and service/waiting time

```python
fast = pm4py.filter_paths_performance(
    df, ("Change Vendor Bank Details", "Payment Approval"),
    parameters={"positive": True})   # keep only cases containing the path

def path_hours(d, a1, a2):
    x = d[d["concept:name"]==a1][["case:concept:name","time:timestamp",RES]]
    y = d[d["concept:name"]==a2][["case:concept:name","time:timestamp",RES]]
    m = x.merge(y, on="case:concept:name", suffixes=("_a","_b"))
    m = m[m["time:timestamp_b"] >= m["time:timestamp_a"]]
```

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

**Would increase concern:**
- `entered_by == approved_by` on the 9 bank-change/payment cases in a **live** system (distinct timestamps, not migrated).
- No maintenance ticket exists for the specific V-77x vendors/dates; changes target individual vendors on scattered dates rather than mass lists.
- Failed or absent call-back verification; new bank account used for a **first-time payment** within days of the change.
- Payment amounts clustered just below approval thresholds (`amount_band` boundaries); split POs to the same vendor/day in the fast clusters.
- Access logs showing the same device/IP/credentials for VendorMaster_Lena and AP_Kim on `manual_upload`; shared "Approver_Gita" credentials used without documented delegation.
- `emergency_flag=True` with no matching incident ticket, or flags entered by the requesting buyer.
- Justification texts copy-pasted across cases, or timestamped *before* the override (sequence inversion).
- V-77x beneficiary accounts recently opened, with shared addresses/phones across vendors, or sanctions/adverse-media hits.
- Fast-channel behavior persisting after emergency policy was tightened.

**Would reduce concern:**
- Maintenance tickets with maker-checker records covering the V-77x changes as one migration project; ticket-to-case linkage restored.
- `entered_by  org:resource` for the shared-account events, with a delegation-of-authority matrix naming Gita (urgent) and Hugo (on-call).
- The legacy_erp mapping table demonstrating bot/human conflation, plus Bot_PO logs showing automated PO creation.
- Call-back verification and bank confirmations on file for every changed account.
- Incident tickets and goods receipts matching the emergency-flag population.
- Distinct, case-specific justifications entered in real time; timestamps consistent with business hours and rosters.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

Testing of the procurement-to-payment event log identified control exceptions, most notably four-eyes breaches on purchase-order approval (42 cases, 1.8%) and on vendor bank-detail changes followed by payment approval (9 cases, 0.4%). The evidence reviewed does not, by itself, establish misconduct, and several patterns have plausible operational explanations. The fast approval channels linking emergency-focused buyers with delegated approvers are consistent with documented emergency handling and informal specialization, although retrospective justifications were late or missing in 18–21% of those cases — a control weakness irrespective of intent. A material share of the apparent same-person justifications, and some four-eyes breaches, coincide with known data-quality defects: batch spreadsheet migration at quarter close, a shared Approver_Gita account, and a legacy ERP mapping error that conflates bot and human resources. These defects must be remediated before any individual-level attribution is made. The most concerning subgroup is the V-77x vendor cluster, where bank-detail changes and payment approvals were performed by the same resource in nine cases, with 72% vendor concentration, 68% high-value invoices, an 83% manual source-system share, and a median of 17 minutes between bank change and payment. The concurrent banking-platform migration offers a plausible benign explanation, but the missing linkage between maintenance tickets and procurement cases prevents confirmation. We recommend a targeted review of the V-77x cases — maintenance tickets, call-back verification records, payment runs, and access logs — together with re-performance of four-eyes testing on cleansed data, stricter evidence requirements for emergency flags, and enforcement of justification deadlines. These findings should be reported as control exceptions requiring follow-up, not as conclusions of fraud or collusion.
