## 2. Rule Interpretation

Let an event be (e=(c,a,t,u,\ldots)), where (c) is the case, (a) the activity, (t) the UTC timestamp, and (u) the raw account. Let (H(e)) be the event-level human identity after alias normalization and session attribution. (H(e)) may remain unknown.

A delegation (d) is valid for event (e) and role (r) only when all of the following hold:

[
\begin{aligned}
ValidDelegation(d,e,r) ={}&
Delegate(d)=H(e) \
&\land Role(d)=r \
&\land Start(d)\le t(e)\le End(d) \
&\land Activity(e)\in Activities(d) \
&\land Scope(e)\subseteq Scope(d) \
&\land RequiredAbsence(d,t(e)) \
&\land PreEventAuthenticInstrument(d) \
&\land ValidDelegationChain(d).
\end{aligned}
]

A technically valid delegation still does not establish that the identified person actually performed the event; identity attribution is a separate condition.

**RR1 — Drafter/approver separation.** For each case, collect the normalized humans performing `Draft_Change_Request` and `Approve_Change`.

* **FAIL** when the sets intersect on a known human.
* **PASS** when both sets are resolved and disjoint.
* **UNRESOLVED** when an unidentified actor could be the drafter or approver.

Thus CHG-508 fails even though `b.koch != u_bk` as strings.

**RR2 — Implementer/verifier separation.** Apply the same set-intersection test to `Implement_Change` and `Verify_Implementation`. A service-account string different from the implementer’s account is not enough to pass. CHG-503 is therefore unresolved, not a technical pass, because Diego may or may not have been the person behind `svc_qms`.

**RR3 — High-risk approval authority.** For a non-emergency HIGH-risk approval, the resolved actor must have either:

1. an active standing QM role at the event timestamp; or
2. a valid QM delegation covering that activity, scope, interval, principal condition, and delegation chain.

Role titles are evaluated at the event timestamp. Eva’s August 2026 Quality Engineer role does not make her QM, and her earlier Independent Verifier role does not make her QM either.

**RR4 — Emergency substitution.** RR4 is interpreted as an exception to RR3, not as an exclusion of normal QM approval. A HIGH-risk emergency approval may therefore be performed by:

* a standing QM or valid QM delegate; or
* the standing IC or a valid IC delegate.

The justified-exception test additionally requires:

1. approval before implementation;
2. credible emergency and safety-rationale evidence, not merely `emergency=true`;
3. a retrospective review by a standing QM or valid QM delegate; and
4. completion within two business days.

**Business-day convention.** I exclude the approval date, count the next Monday–Friday days, use UTC because all supplied timestamps and delegations are UTC, and exclude site holidays. The site has no holiday in the period. The deadline is the end of the second business day.

For an approval on Tuesday, August 11:

* Wednesday, August 12 = business day 1;
* Thursday, August 13 = business day 2;
* deadline = August 13 at 23:59:59 UTC.

Therefore, CHG-505’s Friday, August 14 review is late. CHG-510’s Wednesday, August 12 review is timely. A stricter working-hours interpretation would also leave CHG-505 late, so that conclusion is robust.

**RR5 — Identity evidence.** A service account has no role. It supports conformance only after transaction-specific evidence attributes it to a human. Direct user IDs are also rebuttable: account use during leave, denial by the named user, anomalous location, or missing individual authentication can make the actor unresolved.

**RR6 — Suspicion versus proof.** Pair concentration, reciprocal approval, shared terminals, unusual handovers, and account use during absence are investigative indicators. They do not independently prove collusion, credential sharing, or intentional misconduct.

**Principal activity while on leave.** Formal leave does not suspend a person’s standing authority. If Asha genuinely performed CHG-504, her approval is authorized. Her isolated urgent intervention also does not automatically cancel D-QM-01 unless the instrument says that any temporary availability suspends the delegation. It is nevertheless relevant evidence when assessing whether “absence” was intended to mean formal leave, physical absence, or complete operational unavailability.

For D-QM-02, the result is different: Asha’s formal leave ended on August 14. The fact that August 15 was a Saturday does not prove that she was absent or formally unavailable. CHG-509 therefore needs a separate availability record.

**Later database entry.** A database record created after an event cannot, by itself, prove that authority already existed. It may be a retrospective entry. D-QM-01 is nevertheless supportable because a signed PDF timestamped August 9 predates the August 11 uses. The later August 12 QMS entry is best treated as migration metadata, subject to verification of the PDF’s signature, integrity, and archival chain. 

**Technical rule versus underlying objective.** The supplied RR1 and RR2 prohibit only drafter=approver and implementer=verifier. They do not prohibit approver=verifier. Consequently, Eva’s approval and strongly attributed verification in CHG-509 are not a stated RR1/RR2 breach, but they weaken broader independence and should be reviewed against any policy not supplied here. Similarly, the rules do not specify which roles may perform `Verify_Implementation`; role qualification for Grace and post-August-11 Eva is therefore an open control-design question rather than a proven violation.

def active_roles(
    human: object,
    timestamp: pd.Timestamp,
) -> list[str]:
    if not human:
        return []

    mask = (
        (roles["human"] == human)
        & (roles["start"] <= timestamp)
        & (roles["end"] >= timestamp)
    )
    return sorted(
        roles.loc[mask, "role"].tolist()
    )


def absence_status(
    human: str,
    timestamp: pd.Timestamp,
) -> str:
    mask = (
        (absences["human"] == human)
        & (absences["start"] <= timestamp)
        & (absences["end"] >= timestamp)
    )
    return (
        "CONFIRMED"
        if mask.any()
        else "NOT_ESTABLISHED"
    )


def scope_status(
    event: pd.Series,
    delegation: pd.Series,
) -> str:
    if event["scope"] != delegation["scope"]:
        return "OUT_OF_SCOPE"

    required_incident = delegation["required_incident"]
    if pd.notna(required_incident):
        # The supplied event CSV has no incident_id.
        if (
            "incident_id" not in event.index
            or pd.isna(event.get("incident_id"))
        ):
            return "INCIDENT_LINK_NOT_ESTABLISHED"

        if event["incident_id"] != required_incident:
            return "WRONG_INCIDENT"

    return "COVERED"


def role_authority(
    event: pd.Series,
    required_role: str,
) -> tuple[str, str, object]:
    """
    Evaluate structural authority at the event timestamp.

    When actual identity is unresolved, evaluate the account owner's
    structural entitlement but retain a separate unresolved-identity status.
    """
    human = (
        event["actor_human"]
        if pd.notna(event["actor_human"])
        else event["claimed_human"]
    )

    standing = active_roles(
        human,
        event[TS],
    )
    if required_role in standing:
        return (
            "VALID_STANDING_ROLE",
            f"standing={required_role}",
            None,
        )

    candidates = delegations[
        (delegations["delegate"] == human)
        & (
            delegations["delegated_role"]
            == required_role
        )
        & (delegations["start"] <= event[TS])
        & (delegations["end"] >= event[TS])
    ]

    if candidates.empty:
        return (
            "INVALID",
            "no in-interval delegation",
            None,
        )

    indeterminate_result = None
    failures: list[str] = []

    for _, delegation in candidates.iterrows():
        activity_ok = (
            event[ACT]
            in delegation["activities"]
        )
        scope_result = scope_status(
            event,
            delegation,
        )
        principal_absence = (
            absence_status(
                delegation["principal"],
                event[TS],
            )
            if delegation["absence_required"]
            else "NOT_REQUIRED"
        )

        details = (
            f"activity={'OK' if activity_ok else 'NO'}; "
            f"scope={scope_result}; "
            f"absence={principal_absence}; "
            f"pre_event="
            f"{bool(delegation['pre_event_instrument'])}; "
            f"chain={bool(delegation['valid_chain'])}"
        )

        hard_failure = (
            not activity_ok
            or scope_result
            in {"OUT_OF_SCOPE", "WRONG_INCIDENT"}
            or not delegation["pre_event_instrument"]
            or not delegation["valid_chain"]
        )

        unknown_condition = (
            scope_result
            == "INCIDENT_LINK_NOT_ESTABLISHED"
            or (
                delegation["absence_required"]
                and principal_absence
                != "CONFIRMED"
            )
        )

        if (
            not hard_failure
            and not unknown_condition
        ):
            return (
                "VALID_DELEGATION",
                details,
                delegation["id"],
            )

        if (
            not hard_failure
            and unknown_condition
        ):
            indeterminate_result = (
                "INDETERMINATE_DELEGATION",
                details,
                delegation["id"],
            )
        else:
            failures.append(
                f"{delegation['id']}: {details}"
            )

    return (
        indeterminate_result
        or (
            "INVALID",
            " | ".join(failures),
            None,
        )
    )

def approval_authority(
    event: pd.Series,
) -> tuple[str, str]:
    if event["risk"] != "HIGH":
        return (
            "RR3_NOT_APPLICABLE_LOW_RISK",
            "Low-risk approval authority not specified",
        )

    checks = [
        (
            "QM",
            *role_authority(event, "QM"),
        )
    ]

    # RR4 adds IC authority as a permissible emergency route.
    if bool(event["emergency"]):
        checks.append(
            (
                "IC",
                *role_authority(event, "IC"),
            )
        )

    valid = next(
        (
            result
            for result in checks
            if result[1].startswith("VALID")
        ),
        None,
    )
    unknown = next(
        (
            result
            for result in checks
            if result[1]
            == "INDETERMINATE_DELEGATION"
        ),
        None,
    )

    chosen = valid or unknown or checks[-1]

    if valid:
        status = "STRUCTURALLY_VALID"
    elif unknown:
        status = "INDETERMINATE"
    else:
        status = "INVALID"

    if "UNRESOLVED" in event["identity_status"]:
        status += "_IDENTITY_UNRESOLVED"
    elif "DISPUTED" in event["identity_status"]:
        status += "_IDENTITY_DISPUTED"

    (
        route,
        structural_status,
        detail,
        delegation_id,
    ) = chosen

    basis = (
        f"route={route}; "
        f"{structural_status}; "
        f"delegation={delegation_id or '-'}; "
        f"{detail}; "
        f"identity={event['identity_status']}"
    )
    return status, basis

SITE_HOLIDAYS: set = set()


def rr4_deadline(
    approval_timestamp: pd.Timestamp,
) -> pd.Timestamp:
    """
    Exclude the approval date, count Monday-Friday business days,
    and set the deadline to the end of business day 2.
    """
    current_date = approval_timestamp.date()
    counted = 0

    while counted < 2:
        current_date += timedelta(days=1)

        is_weekday = current_date.weekday() < 5
        is_holiday = current_date in SITE_HOLIDAYS

        if is_weekday and not is_holiday:
            counted += 1

    return pd.Timestamp(
        datetime.combine(
            current_date,
            time.max,
        ),
        tz="UTC",
    )

def network_frame(
    sna,
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    result = pd.DataFrame(
        [
            {
                "source": source,
                "target": target,
                "weight": weight,
            }
            for (
                source,
                target,
            ), weight in sna.connections.items()
        ]
    )

    if result.empty:
        return result

    return result.sort_values(
        ["weight", "source", "target"],
        ascending=[False, True, True],
    )

## 7. Executive Conclusion

The apparent nonconformance is not explained by one mechanism.

**At least five of the ten cases contain a clear, material authority or segregation failure under the supplied record:**

* CHG-502: drafter approved her own case.
* CHG-505: retrospective review was late and lacked applicable QM authority as recorded.
* CHG-507: HIGH-risk approval by a non-QM without delegation.
* CHG-508: same-human draft and approval hidden by aliases.
* CHG-510: self-approval, invalid subdelegation, and unauthorized retrospective review.

**Two further cases are dominated by identity failure rather than presently provable authorization failure:** CHG-503 and CHG-506. Either could become a serious SoD or authority violation once the human actor is resolved. CHG-506 is particularly concerning because the named user denies the action and the account was used from an office workstation after sick leave began.

**One case is a well-supported valid substitution:** CHG-501. **One is likely an authorized principal action with residual credential-sharing uncertainty:** CHG-504. **One remains conditional:** CHG-509, because the delegation’s scope and interval fit but Asha’s required absence on August 15 is not established.

Organizational-pattern risk is elevated by the Clara–Ben concentration and reciprocal approvals, but the supplied evidence does not prove collusion. The systemic weakness is more concrete: the organization does not consistently bind sensitive transactions to a verified human, a precise event-time authority instrument, and an auditable activation condition.

The three highest-priority investigations are:

1. **Reconstruct the CHG-506 approval session** to identify the person who used `u_bk`, preserve evidence, and assess credential compromise or coordinated misuse.
2. **Identify the CHG-503 verifier** using terminal, authentication, badge, endpoint, and application-session evidence; determine whether Diego verified his own implementation.
3. **Audit the emergency-control trail for CHG-505 and CHG-510**, including CI-44 linkage, safety rationales, D-IC-04 communications, review completion evidence, and the absence of an authorized retrospective QM reviewer.

The current evidence supports substantial genuine control failure, substantial identity/data-quality exposure, and an elevated—but not proven—organizational collusion risk.
