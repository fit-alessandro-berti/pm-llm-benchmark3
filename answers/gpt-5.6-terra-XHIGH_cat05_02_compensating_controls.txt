## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 has a strict **nominal rule**—different human resources must prepare and release a completed payment—but its underlying objective is broader: prevent a single person, a compromised identity, or an insufficiently independent pair from causing an erroneous or fraudulent transfer.

| Objective / threat scenario | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. One person initiates and authorizes a payment without meaningful challenge** | Preventive for covered data anomalies if it blocks; a clean pass does not independently authorize the payment. | Preventive if an independent, substantive approval occurs before release or before a controllable settlement point. | Detective after release; partly corrective only if action occurs while recall remains viable. | Restricts when same-user handling may be used, but does not remove the objective. |
| **2. Bank-account takeover, recent bank-detail change, or payment to an account not controlled by the legitimate vendor** | Strong coverage for bank-account ownership and changes in the prior seven days; warning overrides materially weaken it. Does not establish who controlled a compromised user account. | Can independently review bank details and challenge a change, if review is real and timely. | Can identify anomalies retrospectively, but usually too late to prevent settlement. | Requires no bank change in the preceding seven days, thereby excluding a high-risk category from emergency single-user processing. |
| **3. Duplicate invoice, sanctions, operator-limit, or basic PO-match failure** | Directly preventive when configured to block. A warning is only preventive if override authority is independent and substantive. | Can review exceptions, but is weaker than a hard block for high-confidence rule failures. | Can detect missed or overridden issues later. | Requires a full AV-2 pass without override; therefore a warning, outage, or override prevents ED-1 eligibility. |
| **4. Fictitious goods/services, fabricated receipt, poor commercial reasonableness, or inadequate receipt evidence** | Does not cover goods receipt, commercial reasonableness, or collusive fabrication. A clean pass is not evidence that work was performed. | Potentially preventive if the manager reviews invoice, bank details, receipt/service evidence, and business purpose. Rubber-stamping does not compensate. | May detect fabricated evidence, but normally after funds are exposed. | Does not independently address this risk; ED-1 relies on the manager review and audit components. |
| **5. Collusion, sham independence, shared credentials, or a two-person cell acting as one control mind** | Does not detect collusion and cannot prove who controlled a credential. | Helps only if the manager is genuinely independent and not conflicted or captured. | Can detect patterns and anomalies, but may not prove collusion. | Does not solve collusion; it limits emergency eligibility but still depends on real independence. |
| **6. Emergency exception misuse or retrospective creation of emergency authority** | AV-2 is still required under ED-1 and must have passed cleanly. | Independent approval before settlement is required. | A timely 100% audit is required for emergency same-user payments. | Preventive governance control only if every condition is met, including token activation **before** Prepare_Payment. A late token is not a harmless sequencing detail unless logging evidence resolves it. |
| **7. Irreversible settlement and recovery of an already released transfer** | Preventive only before release; no correction once the transfer is beyond the platform’s control. | Before settlement, may stop or hold a pending transfer if the manager has actual authority. After settlement, generally detective/escalatory. | Detective by design; becomes partly corrective only if the audit identifies a defect in time to cancel, stop, recall, or recover funds. | Requires timely audit, but does not itself guarantee recall or recovery. |

A clean AV-2 pass is therefore meaningful but bounded evidence. It supports conclusions about duplicate, sanctions, bank ownership, recent bank changes, operator-limit, and basic PO-match checks—not goods receipt, commercial reasonableness, collusion, or account compromise.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A compensating package should be assessed against the causal path to loss, not against the number of recorded activities.

Let the material sequence be:

- **T0:** Prepare_Payment  
- **T1:** AV-2 validation  
- **T2:** Release_Payment  
- **T3:** Bank settlement / irreversibility  
- **T4:** Audit review and possible recall or recovery  

A package can be evaluated as follows.

| Test dimension | Fully equivalent for the control objective | Partially equivalent | Merely mitigating | Ineffective |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Timing** | An independent, substantive challenge occurs before release, or before settlement where the payment is demonstrably held and can still be stopped. | Review occurs after release but before settlement, but hold authority or settlement status is not fully evidenced. | Review/audit occurs after settlement but within a possible recall period. | Review occurs after settlement and after practical recall capacity. |
| **Independence** | Reviewer is L3+, independent of the operator, free of unresolved conflict, and uses their own credentials. | Different person exists, but independence or authority is uncertain. | Supervisor or related party reviews retrospectively, with limited challenge. | Same person, shared account, credential borrowing, or conflicted/rubber-stamped approval. |
| **Coverage** | Combined controls address the relevant risk: bank details, supporting receipt, service/goods evidence, business reason, and exceptions as applicable. | AV-2 covers structured risks but manager evidence is incomplete. | Only AV-2 clean pass exists, leaving receipt/commercial risks unaddressed. | Key risk is outside control coverage and no substantive compensating review occurred. |
| **Overrideability** | Warnings can be overridden only by an independently authenticated, substantive approver with documented rationale. | Override exists but evidence of independent review is incomplete. | Override is possible and weakly documented. | Preparer uses another person’s override credential, or warning is self-overridden without independent challenge. |
| **Recoverability** | Detection and action occur before settlement or during a demonstrated recall window. | Detection is near the recall boundary or settlement timing is uncertain. | Audit is timely enough to identify loss but recovery is uncertain. | Audit is too late for ordinary recall and no recovery path is shown. |
| **Evidence quality** | Immutable timestamps, workflow state, approval artifacts, reviewer identity, supporting documents, and audit completion are retained. | Some key facts are inferred from labels such as “Ratify” or “Audit_Review.” | Outcome evidence substitutes for control evidence. | Missing or contradictory identity, timing, approval, or emergency evidence. |
| **Collusion resistance** | At least two genuinely independent control minds must act, with separate authentication and meaningful review. | Different users act, but repeated pairing or shared workstation evidence raises concern. | Different credentials exist but no evidence supports actual independence. | Same actor controls both roles, or distinct identities are plausibly a façade. |

A **fully equivalent package** does not make the nominal FEP-1 condition true. Rather, it can meet the underlying payment-integrity objective despite a nominal same-user exception. Under ED-1, this requires all seven conditions, including the pre-Prepare emergency token, clean AV-2 pass, independent pre-settlement manager approval, and timely RA-7 audit.

A **partially equivalent package** reduces risk but leaves a meaningful gap: for example, manager review after release, incomplete review evidence, lack of proof of a settlement hold, or AV-2 coverage without receipt validation.

A **merely mitigating package** is useful but insufficient to replace segregation: for example, an audit after settlement, a clean AV-2 pass with no independent challenge, or a manager ratification after irrevocability.

An **ineffective package** exists where critical controls are bypassed, unavailable, non-independent, too late, or unsupported by evidence.

An **indeterminate result** is appropriate where the evidence cannot establish a material condition, such as whether an emergency token was actually active before preparation, whether a post-release manager had stop authority, or whether distinct users were genuinely independent.

“Same expected loss” is not the only standard because:

- FEP-1 is a policy and governance requirement, not merely a pricing model.
- Intentional fraud creates tail risk and adaptive adversaries, for which historical expected-loss estimates are unstable.
- A preventive control before settlement and a late detective control can have similar average-loss estimates while having very different irreversibility, deterrence, auditability, and legal consequences.
- Common-mode failure matters: repeated pairs, shared workstations, shared credentials, and collusion can make apparently independent controls fail together.
- The organization may rationally impose a stricter control standard for accountability, regulatory defensibility, and confidence in payment authorization.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control | Preventive effect | Detective effect | Corrective effect | Important limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FEP-1** | Prevents a single identified human from both preparing and releasing a completed payment. | Limited: logs can reveal same-user breaches. | None by itself. | Does not prove substantive review, receipt validity, bank ownership, or absence of collusion. |
| **AV-2** | Strongly preventive when it blocks a covered condition before release. A warning is only as strong as its override control. | Validation results identify covered anomalies. | None directly. | Does not prove goods/services receipt, commercial reasonableness, absence of collusion, or identity behind a compromised account. |
| **MG-4** | Strong when independent, substantive, and before release or an enforceable pre-settlement stop point. | Can identify defects during review. | Can cancel or hold a pending payment only if it has authority before settlement. | “Manager_Ratify” is not proof of substantive review, independence, or stop authority. |
| **RA-7** | None for the already released payment. | Primary purpose is retrospective detection. | Partly corrective if the defect is found and acted on before irrevocability or within a viable recall/recovery window. | Audit after the recall window may document a loss without reducing it. |
| **ED-1** | Prevents unauthorized same-user exception use when system-enforced and all conditions are satisfied. | Requires evidence from AV-2, MG-4, and RA-7. | No direct corrective mechanism. | It is a permission with strict preconditions; it does not validate receipts, commercial reasonableness, or collusion. |

A manager action **after Release_Payment but before Bank_Settlement** is best treated as a **hybrid** control:

- It is **detective** of an instruction that has already been released into the payment process.
- It is **preventive of loss** only if the manager can actually stop, reject, or hold the transfer before settlement.
- It is not equivalent to a pre-release approval if release itself creates an irreversible or externally actionable instruction.
- If “ratification” is merely a later record entry without hold authority, it is detective at best and may be practically ineffective.

This is why AP-207 can be plausible under ED-1, while AP-201 remains only partially mitigated: both have post-release manager activity, but the former has an emergency context, substantive review evidence, timely audit, and a more complete ED-1 package.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

Evidence should be collected in a way that distinguishes a control’s existence from its effectiveness.

**Identity, independence, and access evidence**

- Preserve SSO, MFA, privileged-access, session, IP, and device logs for preparers, releasers, managers, and override users.
- Prohibit shared service accounts from being treated as independent human actors.
- Retain organizational reporting lines, L3 status, declared conflicts, and temporary delegation records for MG-4 approvers.
- Obtain the documented purpose and operating design of any dedicated two-person payment cell before interpreting repeated pairs as benign.
- Preserve workstation-sharing evidence, but treat shared workstation use as an investigation signal rather than proof of shared credentials or collusion.

**Payment-state and timing evidence**

- Retain immutable timestamps for preparation, validation, warning, override, release, settlement submission, bank acceptance, settlement, cancellation, recall request, and recall outcome.
- Establish whether Release_Payment is reversible, whether a manager can impose a hold after release, and when the bank’s recall clock begins.
- Record whether a manager approval is an actual workflow gate or merely a ratification record.
- Separate audit start time, audit completion time, finding time, escalation time, and recall-action time.

**Substantive review evidence**

- Store the invoice, PO, receipt/service report, goods-received evidence, vendor bank verification, commercial rationale, and manager review attestation.
- Require an override rationale linked to the exact AV-2 warning, with independently authenticated approver identity.
- Treat AV-2 pass evidence as limited to the checks AV-2 actually ran.
- For recent bank changes and new vendors, require stronger evidence such as out-of-band bank verification, vendor-master approval history, and change provenance.

**Emergency and outage evidence**

- Maintain tamper-evident emergency-token issuance, activation, expiry, approver, and clock-synchronization logs.
- Require any emergency token to be active before Prepare_Payment; a later token should automatically create an exception record.
- Log AV-2 availability, outage duration, failed dependencies, fallback-control use, manual checklist completion, signer identity, and compensating approval.
- Do not allow an unsigned manual checklist to be treated as a substitute for AV-2 coverage.

Suggested monitoring rules:

| Monitor | Rule / denominator | Investigation purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Same-user rate** | Completed payments where preparer equals releaser ÷ all completed payments; split into ED-1 evidenced, ED-1 incomplete, and non-emergency cases. | Detect use of the exception path, convenience bypasses, and concentration by operator. |
| **Override rate** | AV-2 warnings overridden ÷ AV-2 warnings; segment by user, manager, warning type, amount band, vendor age, and bank-change proximity. | Detect weak warning governance and overrides by the preparer or by borrowed credentials. |
| **Late manager approval** | Manager action after release; separately report manager action after settlement. | Identify reviews that were not genuinely preventive and test whether a settlement hold existed. |
| **Audit-before-recall-window rate** | Completed audits with actionable findings before the bank-defined recall deadline ÷ payments requiring RA-7; report emergency same-user cases separately. | Measure whether RA-7 can be corrective rather than merely documentary. Use audit completion, not audit start, where possible. |
| **Repeated resource pairs** | Pair concentration, streaks, and percentage of a user’s releases performed with one counterpart. | Identify common-mode dependence and possible payment-cell design issues. |
| **Shared workstations** | Payments where preparer/releaser/manager sessions overlap on the same device, especially for repeated pairs. | Identify potential credential-sharing or weak physical separation. |
| **Bank-change proximity** | Payments to accounts changed within 7 days, especially with new vendors, warning overrides, high-value transfers, or emergency tokens. | Detect bank-detail takeover exposure and ED-1 ineligibility. |
| **Control outages** | Releases during AV-2 outage or degraded mode; split by manual-checklist status, amount, bank-change proximity, and post-settlement approval. | Detect releases occurring without the normal preventive control. |
| **Manager-review quality** | Approvals lacking linked invoice, bank verification, receipt/service evidence, rationale, or reviewer access evidence. | Distinguish substantive approval from rubber-stamping. |
| **Recovery effectiveness** | Defects found, recalls initiated, recalls accepted, funds recovered, and elapsed times. | Test whether RA-7 actually has corrective value. |

An alert is **not proof of misconduct**. For example:

- A repeated u12/u13 pair may be a documented payment cell.
- A shared workstation may reflect a controlled operational environment.
- A late emergency token may be a logging or synchronization issue.
- Use of a supervisor’s credential may indicate misconduct, poor access design, or a disputed delegation.

Each should trigger evidence preservation and investigation, not an automatic conclusion of fraud or collusion.

    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)
log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(formatted_df)

# Required PM4Py call. In current PM4Py versions this filter is ordinarily
# used to return cases violating the four-eyes condition for the two activities.
pm4py_four_eyes_result = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
    log, "Prepare_Payment", "Release_Payment"
)

def case_ids_from_pm4py_result(result):
    """Accept either a DataFrame or an EventLog-like PM4Py return value."""
    if isinstance(result, pd.DataFrame):
        return set(result["case:concept:name"].astype(str))

    case_ids = set()
    for item in result:
        attrs = getattr(item, "attributes", {})
        case_id = attrs.get("concept:name", attrs.get("case:concept:name"))

        # EventLog traces normally carry the case identifier in trace attributes.
        if case_id is None:
            try:
                if len(item) > 0:
                    case_id = item[0].get("case:concept:name")
            except (TypeError, KeyError, AttributeError):
                pass

        if case_id is not None:
            case_ids.add(str(case_id))
    return case_ids

pm4py_four_eyes_case_ids = case_ids_from_pm4py_result(pm4py_four_eyes_result)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Small facts table constructed from the supplied case/control facts.
# Empty manager-L3 values deliberately represent missing evidence, not False.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
facts_csv = """case:concept:name,amount_eur,vendor_at_least_180_days,bank_change_days,emergency_token_pre_prepare,av_result,manager_independent,manager_substantive_review,manager_l3_evidenced,settlement_time_fact,shared_workstation,manual_checklist_signed

AP-204,220000,True,,False,PASS,,,,,True,
AP-205,12000,True,,False,BLOCK,,,,,False,
AP-206,95000,True,,False,PASS,True,,,,False,
AP-207,35000,True,,True,PASS,True,True,,,False,
AP-208,900000,True,3,True,UNAVAILABLE,False,False,,,False,False
AP-209,42000,True,,False,PASS,,,,,True,
"""

facts = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(facts_csv))
bool_columns = [
    "vendor_at_least_180_days",
    "emergency_token_pre_prepare",
    "manager_independent",
    "manager_substantive_review",
    "manager_l3_evidenced",
    "shared_workstation",
    "manual_checklist_signed",

for col in bool_columns:
    facts[col] = (
        facts[col]
        .astype("string")
        .str.strip()
        .str.lower()
        .map({"true": True, "false": False})
        .astype("boolean")
    )

facts["settlement_time_fact"] = pd.to_datetime(
    facts["settlement_time_fact"], utc=True, errors="coerce"

# Build per-case timing and resource evidence from the event log.

cases = pd.DataFrame(
    {"case:concept:name": sorted(events["case:concept:name"].astype(str).unique())}

time_specs = {
    "prepare_ts": ["Prepare_Payment"],
    "release_ts": ["Release_Payment"],
    "manager_ts": ["Manager_Approve", "Manager_Ratify"],
    "audit_ts": ["Audit_Review"],
    "settlement_observed_ts": ["Bank_Settlement"],
}
for output_col, activities in time_specs.items():
    temp = (
        events.loc[events["concept:name"].isin(activities)]
        .groupby("case:concept:name", as_index=False)["time:timestamp"]
        .min()
        .rename(columns={"time:timestamp": output_col})
    )
    cases = cases.merge(temp, on="case:concept:name", how="left")

for activity, output_col in [
    ("Prepare_Payment", "prepare_resource"),
    ("Release_Payment", "release_resource"),
]:
    temp = (
        events.loc[events["concept:name"].eq(activity)]
        .sort_values("time:timestamp")
        .groupby("case:concept:name", as_index=False)["org:resource"]
        .first()
        .rename(columns={"org:resource": output_col})
    )
    cases = cases.merge(temp, on="case:concept:name", how="left")

activity_sets = events.groupby("case:concept:name")["concept:name"].agg(set)
blocked_attempt_cases = {
    str(case_id)
    for case_id, activities in activity_sets.items()
    if (
        "Automated_Validate_Block" in activities
        and "Attempt_Release" in activities
        and "Release_Payment" not in activities
    )
}

cases["completed_payment"] = cases["release_ts"].notna()
cases["same_user_nominal"] = (
    cases["completed_payment"]
    & cases["prepare_resource"].eq(cases["release_resource"])
)
cases["blocked_release_attempt"] = cases["case:concept:name"].astype(str).isin(
    blocked_attempt_cases
)

def pair_key(row):
    if not row["completed_payment"]:
        return pd.NA
    return "|".join(sorted([str(row["prepare_resource"]), str(row["release_resource"])]))

cases["resource_pair"] = cases.apply(pair_key, axis=1)
pair_counts = cases["resource_pair"].value_counts(dropna=True)

    cases["resource_pair"].map(pair_counts).fillna(0).astype(int).gt(1)

    out["settlement_time_fact"]

def strictly_before(left, right):
    result = pd.Series(pd.NA, index=left.index, dtype="boolean")
    known = left.notna() & right.notna()
    result.loc[known] = (left.loc[known] < right.loc[known]).to_numpy()
    return result

def within_24_hours(start, end):
    """A timing proxy only; one business day may differ from 24 elapsed hours."""
    result = pd.Series(pd.NA, index=start.index, dtype="boolean")
    known = start.notna() & end.notna()
    delta = end.loc[known] - start.loc[known]
    result.loc[known] = (
        (delta >= pd.Timedelta(0)) & (delta <= pd.Timedelta(hours=24))
    ).to_numpy()
    return result

out["manager_before_release"] = strictly_before(

# Static ED-1 facts before manager and audit assessment.
out["ed_static_conditions"] = (

# This tests timing/independence/review evidence available in the supplied facts.
# It does not infer L3 status from the word "manager".
out["manager_timing_independence_review_evidenced"] = (
    out["manager_before_settlement"].fillna(False)
    & out["manager_independent"].eq(True).fillna(False)
    & out["manager_substantive_review"].eq(True).fillna(False)
)

# "Audit_Review" is treated as an audit timestamp proxy here; it may represent
# start rather than completion in a real log and thus cannot itself prove RA-7.
out["ed_all_observable_excluding_L3"] = (
    out["ed_static_conditions"].fillna(False)
    & out["manager_timing_independence_review_evidenced"].fillna(False)
    & out["audit_within_24h_of_release_proxy"].fillna(False)
)
out["ed_all_conditions_evidenced"] = (
    out["ed_all_observable_excluding_L3"]
    & out["manager_l3_evidenced"].eq(True).fillna(False)
)

# PM4Py-filtered cases, restricted to cases with an actual Release_Payment.
completed_case_ids = set(
    out.loc[out["completed_payment"], "case:concept:name"].astype(str)
)
pm4py_violating_completed_cases = pm4py_four_eyes_case_ids & completed_case_ids

# Independent check is intentionally retained as a cross-check of PM4Py output,
# not as a replacement for it.
manual_violating_completed_cases = set(
    out.loc[
        out["completed_payment"] & out["same_user_nominal"],
        "case:concept:name",
    ].astype(str)
)
out["returned_by_pm4py_four_eyes_filter"] = out["case:concept:name"].astype(str).isin(
    pm4py_violating_completed_cases
)

print("PRELIMINARY CONTROL EVIDENCE ONLY — NOT A FINAL COMPLIANCE VERDICT")
print("=" * 88)

PM4Py behavior can vary by version in three relevant ways:

1. `filter_four_eyes_principle` may return an EventLog or a pandas DataFrame; the code handles both.
2. The filter is generally used to identify cases violating the two-activity four-eyes condition, but the code prints an explicit cross-check rather than silently assuming version-specific semantics.
3. For logs with multiple Prepare_Payment or Release_Payment events per case, PM4Py may evaluate resource-set overlap rather than the first observed pair. The supplied data has at most one actual release per completed case, but a production implementation should define and test multi-event semantics explicitly.

The code cannot determine substantive review quality, true human identity behind credentials, manager conflict, L3 status, receipt authenticity, collusion, recall feasibility, or the final compliance verdict.
