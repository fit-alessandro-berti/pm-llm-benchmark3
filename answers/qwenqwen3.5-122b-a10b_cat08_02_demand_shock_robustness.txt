# 1. Stress scenarios

To design a resilient process, we must model disruptions that differ in nature (volume, mix, resource availability, logistics) rather than assuming a uniform scaling of demand. Based on the historical logs and variant behaviors, the following four scenarios define the boundary conditions for the redesign.

**S1: Winter Infection Wave (Demand Mix Shift)**
*   **Description:** Total daily volume increases by ~43% (matching Peak period summary), but critically, the case mix shifts toward high-effort categories. Urgent cases rise from 18% to 29%, and Complex-panel cases rise from 13% to 20%.
*   **Key Disruption:** Resource contention for Medical Validation and Specialist Review. The data shows Specialist Review queue times jump from 5h (Normal) to 36h (Peak), and Medical Validation queue from 2h to 22h.
*   **Implication:** Standard batching rules (I2) fail because Urgent cases get stuck behind Routine batches; strict priority (I1) starves Complex cases which require Medical validation.

**S2: Assay-Machine Degradation (Capacity Shock)**
*   **Description:** One of the two assay machines (likely M2, noted as having a higher failure rate) goes offline or operates at 50% speed.
*   **Key Disruption:** Assay machine utilization jumps from 98% (Peak) to effectively 100%+ on remaining hardware. Repeat-after-failure cases cluster after night shifts; machine instability increases repeat rates (currently 1140 normal -> 1260 peak).
*   **Implication:** Throughput drops significantly. If Routine cases are routed to the failing machine (as per current logic), they block capacity needed for Urgent runs.

**S3: Specialist/Medical Validator Absence (Human Resource Shock)**
*   **Description:** The single specialist reviewer is unavailable (scheduled every second Friday) coinciding with a surge, or a Medical Validator calls in sick during a peak week.
*   **Key Disruption:** Medical Validation utilization is already 93% at peak. Removing one resource pushes effective utilization >100%. Technical Validation queue (currently 16h median at peak) will explode because validated results cannot move to release.
*   **Implication:** Urgent cases miss the 18-hour penalty threshold. Complex-panel cases (which require Medical validation) face extreme delays.

**S4: Courier Logistics Compression (Input Flow Shock)**
*   **Description:** Courier routes consolidate or are delayed, causing sample arrivals to compress into fewer windows (e.g., 10:00 and 17:00 spikes intensify).
*   **Key Disruption:** Pre-analytical check utilization hits 96% (Peak). With compressed arrivals, queue times spike from 1.8h to 9.5h. Quality drops under pressure, increasing Recollection loops (7.9% recollection rate in Peak vs 4.1% Normal).
*   **Implication:** Intake clerks spend more time explaining errors (reducing registration capacity), and poor samples clog the Assay machines later, wasting capacity.

# 2. Bottleneck-migration analysis

Relieving a bottleneck often shifts the constraint downstream. The following analysis traces the flow using the DFG frequencies and queue metrics provided.

**S1 (Winter Wave) Migration Path:**
1.  **Initial Constraint:** Pre-analytical Check (96% util). High volume + mix shift overwhelms inspection.
2.  **Migration:** If Pre-analytical is cleared faster (e.g., via automation), the **Assay Machines** (98% util) become the immediate constraint.
3.  **Secondary Migration:** Once samples pass Assay, **Technical Validation** (101% util) becomes the primary blocker. Results sit here for 16h median.
4.  **Final Constraint:** **Medical Validation/Specialist Review**. Since Complex-panel cases increased to 20%, and they require Medical Validation, this step absorbs the most delay (36h queue). Relieving Technical Validation simply floods Medical Validation faster.

**S2 (Machine Outage) Migration Path:**
1.  **Initial Constraint:** Available Assay Capacity (M1 only).
2.  **Migration:** **Test Preparation** queue grows (currently 11h median peak). Technicians finish prep but have no machine to run.
3.  **Risk:** If M2 is used for Routine cases (high volume), its failure forces all Routine to M1. If M1 is dedicated to Urgent (I3), Routine waits indefinitely at Test Prep.
4.  **Downstream Impact:** **Repeat Assay** loops increase. If M1 is strained, error rates rise, sending more cases to "Repeat assay" (1260 peak frequency), consuming double capacity.

**S3 (Validator Absence) Migration Path:**
1.  **Initial Constraint:** Medical Validation / Specialist Review.
2.  **Migration:** **Technical Validation** queue expands rapidly. Since 4,050 cases (Peak) go Tech->Release directly, but 3,420 go Tech->Medical, the latter pool backs up.
3.  **Upstream Backfire:** As Medical Validation stalls, **Technical Validators** stop releasing results. Assay machines eventually fill up (buffer limits reached), forcing **Assay Run** to pause.
4.  **Result:** The bottleneck migrates backward to the Assay Machines, effectively halting new testing despite available machine time.

**S4 (Courier Compression) Migration Path:**
1.  **Initial Constraint:** Courier Intake / Registration (88% util).
2.  **Migration:** **Pre-analytical Check** (96% util). Samples arrive in waves; clerks process fast, then wait. During peaks, error rates rise (Recollection requests: 780 peak vs 760 normal, despite lower total volume).
3.  **Feedback Loop:** Recollection requests return to "Receive sample," consuming fresh capacity. This creates a "death spiral" where valid cases wait longer because invalid cases occupy the same queue space.
4.  **Downstream:** Poorly checked samples reach **Assay Run**, causing higher repeat rates (Repeat assay frequency rises), further straining the machines.

# 3. Robust redesign package

This package focuses on stabilizing flow and reducing waste rather than simply adding headcount. These interventions are selected to mitigate the commonalities across S1, S2, and S4 (Quality/Input stability).

**R1. Early Pre-analytical Defect Triage (Based on I6)**
*   **Action:** Implement a rapid visual/automated check within 2 hours of receipt to reject untestable samples immediately.
*   **Robustness:** Works in S1 (Volume), S2 (Capacity protection), and S4 (Quality drop). It prevents bad samples from consuming Assay Machine capacity (the hardest resource to scale).
*   **Dependency:** Requires training for phlebotomists/couriers on rejection criteria.
*   **Downstream Risk:** Minimal. By removing bad samples, it reduces load on Assay and Technical Validation. However, if rejection criteria are too strict, it increases Recollection requests (feedback loop), so calibration is key.
*   **Failure Mode:** If the "triage" is too slow, it becomes a new bottleneck at Receive Sample.

**R2. Dynamic Courier Slot Management (Based on I7)**
*   **Action:** Negotiate staggered pickup windows with couriers to flatten the 10:00/17:00 spikes. Offer incentives for off-peak delivery.
*   **Robustness:** Critical for S4 and S1. Smoothing input prevents the Pre-analytical queue from spiking to 9.5h median.
*   **Dependency:** Courier cooperation and IT integration for slot booking.
*   **Downstream Risk:** Low. Smoother input allows Pre-analytical staff to maintain consistent quality, reducing Recollection loops (which overload Registration).
*   **Failure Mode:** If demand spikes exceed physical intake capacity regardless of timing, this only delays the inevitable congestion.

**R3. Strategic Outsourcing of Complex-Panels (Based on I10)**
*   **Action:** Contract a partner lab for Complex-panel cases when internal Specialist Review queue exceeds 12 hours. Accept 12h transport delay.
*   **Robustness:** Highly effective for S1 (Mix shift) and S3 (Staff absence). Complex panels drive the 36h queue at Specialist Review. Offloading them protects Urgent/Routine throughput.
*   **Dependency:** Reliable partner SLA and data integration.
*   **Downstream Risk:** Traceability complexity increases. If the partner fails, the lab loses visibility.
*   **Failure Mode:** If the transport delay (12h) plus partner processing time exceeds the Urgent 18h target, this cannot be used for Urgent-Complex cases. Must filter by urgency.

**R4. Cross-Training for Accession Correction (Modified I4)**
*   **Action:** Cross-train *one* Technical Validator to handle Accession Corrections during peaks only.
*   **Robustness:** Addresses the Accession Correction bottleneck (91% util peak) which blocks Test Preparation.
*   **Dependency:** Availability of a Tech Val who can spare time.
*   **Downstream Risk:** **High.** Taking a Tech Val away from Technical Validation (101% util peak) risks creating a downstream backlog. This must only activate when Accession queue > 4h AND Tech Val queue < 4h.
*   **Failure Mode:** If activated incorrectly, it starves the Assay output, causing machines to idle while samples wait for validation.

# 6. Residual vulnerabilities

Even with the redesign, specific fragilities remain due to the inherent constraints of the system.

1.  **Medical Validation Single Point of Failure:**
    *   **Vulnerability:** Medical validators are required for Urgent and Complex cases. There is no automation for this step. If the last available validator leaves during a peak (S3), the entire release process halts.
    *   **Cause:** Regulatory requirement for human sign-off.
    *   **Mitigation Limit:** Cross-training helps slightly, but only if trained staff are present.

2.  **The "Specialist Review" Scheduled Gap:**
    *   **Vulnerability:** The data notes "One specialist unavailable every second Friday." If a Winter Wave (S1) coincides with this scheduled absence, the Specialist Review queue (normally 36h peak) could exceed 48h, breaching service levels for Complex cases.
    *   **Cause:** Fixed staffing schedule vs. variable demand.
    *   **Mitigation Limit:** Outsourcing (R3) covers this, but only for non-Urgent cases.

3.  **Redesign Vulnerability: Outsourcing Traceability:**
    *   **Vulnerability:** R3 (Outsourcing) relies on a partner lab. If the partner lab experiences their own outage or transport delay, our internal system shows "In Transit" but the result is lost. We lose control over the 12h transport window.
    *   **Cause:** External dependency introduced to solve internal capacity.
    *   **Mitigation Limit:** Requires manual tracking overrides, increasing administrative burden.

4.  **Technology Debt in Automation (I8/I6):**
    *   **Vulnerability:** R1 (Early Triage) and potential automation (I8) rely on digital metadata. If the LIS (Lab Information System) crashes or data formats change, the automated gates fail, reverting to manual bottlenecks instantly.
    *   **Cause:** Integration fragility.
    *   **Mitigation Limit:** Manual override processes are slower and prone to the original errors.
