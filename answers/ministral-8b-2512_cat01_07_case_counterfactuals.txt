### 100-Case Weekly Rollout Analysis
Applying these interventions to 100 cases would create significant interference:
1. **C1 Reservation (K-104)**: Reserving C1 for high-risk cases would exacerbate compliance backlogs, delaying other urgent cases. The system’s C1 capacity is already strained (e.g., Mon 08:00 queue: 20 h), and reserving slots for 100 cases would require doubling capacity, which is impractical.
2. **D2 Substitution (K-219)**: Assigning D2 to 100 cases would overload D2’s workload, increasing rework risk. D2’s slower speed (8-14 h for complex cases) would push assessment queues beyond capacity, especially if rework occurs.
3. **Rework Suppression (K-337)**: Suppressing rework loops could reduce document queue pressure but increase compliance risk. If 10% of cases are appealed, the system may face higher loss exposure.
4. **Parallelization (K-411)**: Parallelizing 100 cases would overwhelm the assessment queue, as A1 and A2 are already bottlenecked (e.g., Tue 08:00 queue: 9 h). This would require additional assessors or longer cycle times.

**Key Interference Effects**:
- **Weekend and External Responses**: Supplier delays (K-337) would still occur, but suppressing rework loops could reduce weekend bottlenecks.
- **Timestamp Behavior**: Poorly logged concurrency (K-411) could create false efficiency claims, leading to overestimation of time savings.
- **Bottleneck Migration**: Assessment queues would become the primary bottleneck, requiring additional resources or process redesign.
