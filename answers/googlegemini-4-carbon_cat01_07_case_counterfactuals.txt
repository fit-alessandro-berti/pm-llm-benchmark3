## 1. Feasibility model

```text

Advisory & Preliminary Prep (optional, non-replacing):
  Triage  Escalation Advice
  Triage  Preliminary Compliance Screen
  (Both may run concurrently with Identity Check, Document Check_k, or Assessment_k)

Compliance Gate (when high_risk = true OR high_value = true):
  Assessment_final  Compliance Review_final  Decision

Iterative Rework Sub-order (for each loop k  1 when Request More Info_k occurs):
  {Assessment_k OR Compliance Review_k}  Request More Info_k
  Request More Info_k  Document Check_{k+1}  Assessment_{k+1}
  (If high_risk = true OR high_value = true: Assessment_{k+1}  Compliance Review_{k+1/reaffirmed}  Decision)
```

```text
CONDITIONAL AND DECLARATIVE CONSTRAINTS

C1 (Concurrency Eligibility):
  IF originals_required = false THEN Identity Check || Document Check_1 is valid;
  IF originals_required = true THEN complete(Originals Receipt)  start(Document Check_1).

C2 (Mandatory Final Governance):
  IF (high_risk = true OR high_value = true) THEN EXISTS Compliance Review_final
  SUCH THAT complete(Assessment_final)  start(Compliance Review_final)
  AND complete(Compliance Review_final)  start(Decision).

C3 (Non-Substitution of Preliminary Work):
  Preliminary Compliance Screen  Compliance Review_final; C2 remains binding regardless of early screening.

C4 (Four-Eyes Separation of Duties):
  resource(Assessment_final)  resource(Compliance Review_final).

C5 (Rework Reset Invariant):
  FOR ALL k: occurrence(Request More Info_k) IMPLIES mandatory fresh execution of
  Document Check_{k+1} AND Assessment_{k+1} prior to Decision (plus Compliance Review per C2).

C6 (Unary Resource Capacity):
  FOR ALL resources r  {T1,T2,I1,I2,D1,D2,A1,A2,C1,S1,M1}, active service intervals of r are pairwise disjoint.

C7 (Policy-Bounded Discrepancy Handling — K-337 Intervention Scope):
  IF unresolved_discrepancy = true AND RMI_cap_reached = true
```

| K-219 (`D1` $\rightarrow$ `D2` substitution) | Shifts $4\text{ h}$ off `D1`; adds $5.5\text{–}8\text{ h}$ on `D2` (+ expected rework load on `D2`/`D1` and `A2`). | `D1` complex-document queue (helping cases like K-104/K-337). | `D2` queue and `Assessment` (`A2`) Tuesday afternoon queue. | Standard digital cases on `D2` (like K-411) delayed; complex corporate cases on `D1` accelerated. | **Positive at low volume; Negative past rework/utilization threshold.** | Kingman congestion curve on `D2` amplified by positive feedback from handwriting rework loops returning to `D2` and `A2`. |
| K-337 (Cap second `RMI` loop) | Eliminates `D1` ($3.5\text{ h}$) and `A1` ($2.5\text{ h}$) third-pass work; adds $0.5\text{–}1.5\text{ h}$ friction to `C1` and `M1`. | `D1` and `A1` rework queues; active WIP awaiting external suppliers. | Immediate Friday `C1`/`M1` queues; downstream appeals, covenant-monitoring, or re-registration queues. | Other cases waiting on `D1`/`A1` benefit; contemporary `C1` cases face longer complex reviews. | **Short-run throughput Positive; Net Risk-Adjusted Value Negative.** | Weekend cutoff cliff saves ~48 h nominal time, but rejected clients re-entering as "new" cases re-inject greater total load. |
| K-411 (Unlock concurrent `I`/`D` tasks) | Zero net work-hour change on `I2`/`D2` (plus minor logging overhead); shifts `D2` demand $2\text{–}4\text{ h}$ earlier. | Artificial inter-stage idle gap between `Identity` and `Document` checks. | Instantaneous `D2` queue depth and downstream `Assessment` (`A1`/`A2`) buffer. | Cases already in `D2` or `A2` queues that get interleaved or overtaken. | **Weakly Positive (bounded by `Assessment` bottleneck).** | Bottleneck migration: accelerating non-bottleneck parallel branches yields diminishing returns once `Assessment` saturates. |

Scaling any of these interventions to 100 cases per week fundamentally breaks single-case *ceteris paribus* assumptions through queue displacement and bottleneck migration. 

Under **K-104**, `C1` is a single-point-of-failure resource starting Monday with a 20-hour backlog. Pre-booking 100 provisional slots while upstream `D1` and `A1` times remain stochastic creates severe schedule fragmentation: either `C1` sits idle waiting for delayed assessments, or reservations cascade-bump urgent unreserved arrivals. Moreover, incomplete early packets reduce the $1\text{–}3\text{ h}$ final review savings, increasing total `C1` workload and driving utilization past unity.

Under **K-219**, shifting 100 borderline/handwritten files from `D1` to `D2` relieves `D1` for complex corporate files, which is initially beneficial. However, because `D2` requires $1.5\text{–}4\text{ h}$ more service time per case than `D1`, 100 transfers erase `D2`’s slack, delaying digital cases like K-411. Worse, if even 20% of handwritten cases trigger `Request More Info`, those 20 cases consume another $80\text{–}160\text{ h}$ of `D2` and `A2` rework capacity, tipping both pools into nonlinear congestion.

Under **K-411**, releasing 100 `Document Check` tasks at Triage creates a morning surge on `D2` and pushes synchronized completions into `Assessment` hours earlier. During weeks when an assessor is in training, `Assessment` is the binding bottleneck; earlier verification merely converts invisible IT-lock waiting into visible `Assessment` queue time without raising system throughput. Furthermore, if staff bypass real-time timestamping under heavy parallel load and batch-close tasks at shift end, event logs will show *false concurrency* or inflated service durations, corrupting capacity forecasts.

Finally, under **K-337**, suppressing second rework loops eliminates `D1`/`A1` third passes and avoids Friday-to-Monday external supplier dead zones, sharply cutting reported cycle times. Yet at 100 cases/week, pushing unresolved discrepancies onto scarce `C1` slows compliance throughput, while elevated rejections and conditional approvals generate external interference: appeals, covenant breaches, and re-submitted applications that re-enter `Register` the following Monday as hidden failure demand.

## 6. Pilot designs

*Note: `intervention 1` = Rank 1 (**K-411** parallel verification workaround); `intervention 2` = Rank 2 (**K-219** selective `D1` $\rightarrow$ `D2` document routing).*

| pilot element | intervention 1 | intervention 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **eligibility** | Low-risk, low-value cases with `originals required = no` and standard digital documents arriving at Triage. | Low-risk, low-value cases with `originals required = yes`, standard document structure (both printed and moderately handwritten), where `D1` queue forecast exceeds `D2` by $\ge 6\text{ h}$. |
| **assignment rule** | Case-level stratified randomization (50% concurrent workaround / 50% legacy sequential) stratified by morning vs. afternoon Triage completion and day of week. | Blocked randomization (50% route to `D2` at `Originals Receipt` / 50% hold for `D1`), stratified by Triage legibility tag (`clean-printed` vs. `handwritten`). |
| **sample/support logic** | Run for 2 weeks (~60–80 eligible cases; 30–40 per arm), throttled to $\le 8$ concurrent workaround cases/day so `D2`/`A2` queues stay near baseline support. | Run for 3 weeks (~50–70 eligible cases; 25–35 per arm), capping transfers at $\le 4$ cases/day to measure `D2` quality/speed without saturating `D2` capacity. |
| **primary outcome** | End-to-end elapsed time from `Triage` completion to `Decision`, decomposed into verification completion time vs. `Assessment` wait time. | End-to-end elapsed time from `Originals Receipt` to `Decision` (evaluating both median gain and 90th-percentile tail delay). |
| **process-path outcome** | Realized concurrency ratio (% of cases where `start(D2) < complete(I2)`) and queue-entry rank shifts at `Assessment`. | Incidence rate of `Request More Info` rework loops (`Doc Check 2` + `Assessment 2`) and proportion beating `M1`’s same-day sign-off cutoff. |
| **quality outcome** | Rate of version-conflict/lock errors, lost checklist annotations, or `Assessment` kickbacks due to out-of-sync verification notes. | Blinded post-decision QA audit on a 30% sample checking transcription accuracy on handwritten fields and missed material discrepancies. |
| **queue-spillover outcome** | Change in `D2` wait time for non-pilot cases and Tuesday/Wednesday queue growth at `A1`/`A2` during training windows. | Change in `D1` wait time for complex corporate cases (positive spillover) vs. `D2` wait-time inflation for standard digital cases (negative spillover). |

## 7. Final synthesis

Unlocking concurrent verification in **K-411** is the most robust intervention. Because normative rules already permit parallel identity and document checks when originals are not required, removing the legacy task lock introduces zero compliance or decision-quality degradation. Even when downstream `Assessment` congestion absorbs most of the earlier readiness, worst-case performance roughly matches the status quo, and the change is instantaneously reversible.

Conversely, suppressing the second rework loop in **K-337** is the most dangerous intervention despite delivering the largest nominal speed gain ($48\text{–}64\text{ h}$). Deleting a verification loop does not accelerate work; it alters the decision policy under uncertainty. Forcing high-risk, high-value approvals with conditions or rejections over unverified supplier discrepancies exposes the institution to silent tail risks—regulatory sanctions, credit losses, client appeals, and re-submitted shadow workload—that remain invisible to short-term cycle-time metrics.

Local single-case counterfactuals are least transportable to system-wide policy in **K-104** (and secondarily high-volume **K-219**). Reserving a provisional slot on single-server bottleneck `C1` looks nearly costless for one isolated case, but across 100 weekly cases it is a zero-sum priority game that fragments `C1`’s schedule, wastes preparation on incomplete early packets, and starves urgent unreserved compliance arrivals.

To move from plausible bounds to sharp operational policies, four event and queue datasets are highest value: (1) distinct `task_ready` / `queue_enter` timestamps separated from `service_start` (currently conflated with shift schedules and IT task-creation delays); (2) resource shift calendars and active-work pauses (to separate overnight off-shift hours from active service); (3) granular document complexity and legibility tags linked to resource-specific `Request More Info` rates; and (4) post-decision longitudinal tracking of conditional-approval breaches, appeals, and re-applications.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **`temporal` — Shift-schedule continuity across pools:** We assumed evening/overnight work observed on `D1` (21:40–05:40) and `A2` (20:00–22:30) reflects regular shift capacity available to `D2` in K-219, whereas `M1` and `A1` operate strictly on daytime windows (~08:00–19:00), creating overnight sign-off cliffs.
2. **`resource` — Hard single-point bottleneck at Compliance (`C1`):** We assumed `C1` cannot delegate final certification or use overtime elasticity during the observation window, making any net addition or fragmentation of `C1` time directly displace other high-risk cases.
3. **`quality` — Transferability of preliminary compliance work (K-104):** We assumed `S1` framing and preliminary `C1` screening remain 60–80% reusable after `D1` and `A1` finish, rather than being invalidated by late-discovered corporate ownership facts.
4. **`behavioral` — Compliance with real-time logging (K-411):** We assumed operators using a manual lock workaround will log true `start` and `complete` times rather than opening both tasks early and batch-closing them, which would distort observed service durations.
5. **`quality` — Monotonic legibility-rework trade-off on `D2` (K-219):** We assumed `D2` responds to illegible handwriting conservatively by triggering `Request More Info` rather than guessing values and passing latent errors silently through `A2`.
6. **`structural` — Governance invariance under loop truncation (K-337):** We assumed banning `Request More Info 2` applies equally to `Compliance Review` (`C1`), forcing `C1` and `M1` to either reject or craft binding conditions rather than returning the file informally to `A1`.
7. **`interference` — Approximate FIFO discipline within non-reserved queues:** For single-case counterfactuals (K-219, K-411), we assumed earlier arrival into `A2`’s queue improves or preserves start order without triggering adaptive reprioritization by assessors.
8. **`interference` — Endogenous failure-demand feedback at scale (K-337):** In the 100-case rollout, we assumed a non-trivial fraction of forced rejections and restrictive conditional approvals re-enter the system via appeals or fresh registrations rather than disappearing cleanly.
