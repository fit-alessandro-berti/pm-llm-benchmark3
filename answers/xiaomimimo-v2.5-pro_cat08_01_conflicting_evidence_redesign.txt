# Current-process diagnosis

The warranty process is caught in a classic speed-quality trade-off, but process-mining evidence shows this trade-off is not evenly distributed across segments, creating opportunities for targeted redesign.

**Main performance, quality, compliance, and customer-experience problems:**
1. **Fast path creates downstream costs**: The most frequent path (`Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement`) is cheap (31% cheaper before rework) but leads to high complaint/reopen rates (14.1% for Fast approval vs. 3.3% for the repair path). This inflates post-rework costs, narrowing the advantage to just 9%.
2. **Compliance violations are systemic**: Alignment diagnostics show frequent "move-on-log" deviations: `Fast approval before Evidence completeness` and `Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices`. This directly violates normative rules R1 (claims >EUR 400 need manual/fraud review) and R2 (replacement requires evidence completeness). The 68% fitting traces indicate significant policy breaches.
3. **Segment-specific pain points**: Premium high-value devices have a 22% complaint rate on the fast path vs. 5% on the reviewed/repair path. However, the reviewed/repair path is slow (median 96-120 hours), risking Premium service expectations. For unclear defect/missing evidence cases (22% of volume), the median cycle time is 140 hours with high abandonment, creating poor customer experience.
4. **Rework loops are expensive**: `Request missing evidence` occurs at least twice in 26% of cases that enter it, and the edge `Customer complaint/reopen -> Manual eligibility review` has a mean of 40 hours, creating long-tail delays. Rework repair occurs in 12.5% of repaired high-value devices, wasting resources.
5. **Resource bottlenecks misaligned with risk**: Manual eligibility reviewers operate near capacity (queue grows >82% utilization), yet the auto-screen rules are outdated and not segment-aware, forcing many low-risk cases into manual review. Meanwhile, the `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` edge has a mean of 96 hours, indicating that catching errors post-dispatch is extremely costly.

**The contradiction in the evidence**: The fast path appears optimal for average cycle time and cost, but this is a mirage. It simply shifts costs downstream into complaints, service credits, rework, and compliance exceptions. The slower paths are more expensive upfront but prevent these downstream failures, especially for high-value and Premium segments.

# Redesign A: Segment-Aware Routing with Pre-Dispatch Quality Gate

**Redesigned process flow**:
1. **Updated auto-screen rules** deployed in two weeks: Segment-aware rules based on claim amount, customer tier, defect type, and evidence completeness. Premium low-risk accessories (<EUR 400, clear evidence) can still use fast approval. Standard low-risk accessories (<EUR 200) use fast approval. All other cases (high-value, Premium, unclear defect) require at least Manual eligibility review.
2. **Pre-dispatch quality gate** for all replacement dispatches: A lightweight verification step (6-18 hours) to confirm correct part selection, using automated matching against device model and defect type, with human review only for ambiguous cases.
3. **Evidence portal with fallback**: Deploy new portal to reduce repeated evidence requests by 30-45%, but include phone support option for low-digital-literacy customers to limit abandonment.
4. **Streamlined fraud/risk review**: Use tiered review—low-risk cases get automated checks only; high-risk cases go to specialists.

**Targets**: High-value devices (compliance risk), Premium low-risk accessories (service-credit exposure), and unclear defect cases (repeated evidence requests). The fast path is preserved only for low-risk, low-amount Standard claims.

**Mechanism of improvement**: By segmenting the auto-screen, we reduce inappropriate fast approvals for high-value claims, addressing R1 and R2 violations. The pre-dispatch quality gate catches wrong part selections before they cause complaints (currently linked to the 96-hour `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` pattern). The evidence portal reduces the 26% repeat-request rate.

**Deliberate sacrifices**: Cycle time increases for high-value claims (mandatory manual review adds ~10 hours median). Some Standard low-risk claims may see slightly longer cycle times due to the quality gate. The evidence portal may increase abandonment for some customer segments if not carefully implemented.

# Redesign C: Dynamic Risk-Adaptive Routing with Continuous Learning

**Redesigned process flow**:
1. **Real-time risk scoring engine**: Uses claim attributes (amount, defect type, customer history), evidence quality, and segment to generate a risk score (0-100) and optimal path recommendation.
2. **Dynamic routing rules**:  
   - Score <30: Fast path (low-risk accessories).  
   - Score 30-70: Streamlined review + repair option.  
   - Score >70: Full manual review + fraud/risk review + replacement with quality gate.
3. **Customer-facing decision transparency**: Show customers their risk score (simplified as "Standard" vs. "Premium") and expected path/timeline at intake.
4. **Continuous improvement loop**: Weekly model retraining using outcomes (complaints, rework, compliance) to adjust routing rules.
5. **Escalation buffer**: Reserve 20% of manual review capacity for dynamic routing overflow and urgent cases.

**Targets**: All segments, but especially complex cases with unclear risk. The system learns which paths work best for which combinations.

**Mechanism of improvement**: The risk model optimizes the speed-quality trade-off per case rather than per segment. By showing customers their expected path, we set accurate expectations (reducing complaints). The continuous learning adapts to changing patterns (e.g., new defect types). The escalation buffer prevents bottlenecks.

**Deliberate sacrifices**: Implementation complexity is high. Risk model errors could misroute cases. Customers may perceive the risk score as unfair if not explained well. The system requires ongoing data science resources.
