# 1. Fairness Dimensions

When assessing procedural fairness, several dimensions must be considered beyond mere approval rates:

- **Outcome Parity**: While raw approval rates differ, we must examine whether the adjusted outcome disparity (after controlling for complexity) is significant.
- **Adjusted Outcome Disparity**: Using our adjusted comparison strategy, we can quantify if certain groups experience a statistically significant difference in favorable outcomes.
- **Procedural Burden**: The additional steps and waiting times experienced by different applicant groups are crucial in assessing fairness.
- **Timeliness**: Cycle times and waiting times should be compared across groups to ensure no disproportionate delays.
- **Consistency of Routing**: How well does the routing system direct applicants to appropriate processes, and are there disproportionate handovers or detours?
- **Quality/Accessibility of Support**: The availability and responsiveness of language support and accommodation review services are essential for fairness.
- **Appeal/Re-work Burden**: The likelihood and complexity of appeals or rework for different groups can indicate systemic fairness issues.
- **Resource-Assignment Fairness**: The distribution of caseworkers and specialized resources should be evaluated for biases.

# 2. Adjusted Comparison Strategy

To effectively assess fairness, the following strategy can be employed:

- **Matching/Stratification Variables**: Stratify data by applicant_group, risk_score_band, documentation_completeness, channel, household_size_band, prior_case_count, region, and month to control for known complexity factors.
- **Path/Variant Comparison**: Analyze the different paths (V1 to V6) to identify which routes lead to higher burden or delays for particular groups.
- **Resource-Assignment Comparison**: Compare the distribution of caseworkers and specialized resources across groups to identify imbalances.
- **Waiting-Time Decomposition**: Break down waiting times at each routing point for each group to pinpoint where delays are most pronounced.
- **Sensitivity Analysis**: Test the robustness of findings by varying the assumptions about bias in covariates like risk_score_band and documentation_completeness.
- **Subgroup Checks**: Perform additional checks by region and channel to ensure that findings are not artifacts of specific operational contexts.

Using the variant and waiting-time data:

While some of this may be justified by higher shares of language barriers and accommodation requests, the extended waits in these steps suggest potential inefficiencies.

Some disparities, such as those in Group B's language support steps, may be justified by legitimate routing (rights-preserving actions). However, the disproportionate handovers and waiting times in accommodation review for Group C and the higher appeal rates for Group D suggest areas for concern.

# 4. Plausible Mechanisms

Several mechanisms could explain the observed patterns:

- **Legitimate Routing**: The higher share of language support and accommodation requests for Groups B and C, respectively, may lead to longer wait times due to the specialized nature of these processes.
- **Resource Allocation Imbalance**: The concentration of bilingual caseworkers and accommodation specialists in specific groups may lead to perceived unfairness, despite these roles being necessary for certain applicant needs.
- **Case Complexity**: Higher risk scores and prior-case counts naturally lead to more complex cases, which may require additional review steps and longer processing times.
- **Systemic Bias**: Historical biases in risk scoring and documentation completeness could inadvertently disadvantage certain groups, manifesting as longer processing times and higher appeals.

Legitimate mechanisms include necessary steps for high-risk or complex cases. Concerning mechanisms include disproportionate delays in rights-preserving steps and uneven resource distribution.

# 5. Limitations of Available Attributes

The available attributes have several limitations:

- **Risk Score Band**: While used for routing, it may encode historical biases rather than current risk, potentially leading to unfair treatment.
- **Documentation Completeness**: This attribute is affected by language barriers and may not accurately reflect the true complexity of cases.
- **Applicant Needs**: Unobserved needs, such as emotional support or specific cultural considerations, are not captured.
- **Service Workload**: Resource workload is only observed at daily granularity, missing finer-grained inefficiencies.
- **Appeal Quality**: The assessment of appeal quality or fairness of decisions made during appeals is not captured.
- **Historical Bias**: Risk scores and documentation completeness may reflect past disparities, not current fairness.

# 6. Mitigation Options and Side Effects

Potential mitigation options include:

- **Adding Capacity for Language Support or Accommodation Review**: This would reduce wait times but could increase costs and require training for additional staff.
- **Service-Level Targets for Support Steps**: Setting targets for response times in language support and accommodation review could ensure timely service but might pressure staff.
- **Redesign Routing to Reduce Handovers**: Streamlining the routing process could decrease delays but may require re-evaluating current case-handling policies.
- **Decision-Explanation Improvements**: Providing clearer explanations for decisions can enhance trust and fairness but may require additional resources.
- **Monitoring Adjusted Disparities**: Regular monitoring can help identify emerging issues but requires ongoing resources and commitment.
- **Auditing Risk Score Band and Documentation Completeness**: Ensuring these metrics are fair and unbiased is crucial but may uncover deep-rooted systemic issues.
