## 1. Analytical assumptions

**Case notion.** A case notion is a choice of equivalence class over events, not the recovery of a truth. Flattening on type T assigns each event to as many cases as there are T-objects in its *direct* E2O set, and deletes it if that set is empty — two lossy operators, replication and deletion, both to be quantified before any DFG is read.

**Convergence.** One event relates to several lead objects and is copied into several traces. `e16` (Dispatch, 11:05) names `O1, O2, O3`, so Order flattening turns one physical dispatch into three trace entries, inflating frequencies and destroying case independence.

**Divergence.** One case holds events belonging to *different* objects of a subordinate type, and flattening serialises parallel strands. In `O1` the true strands are `I11: e05→e11→e15→e23` and `I12: e06→e12→e16→e24`; the flattened trace interleaves them by timestamp and yields the edge `e06 (Add Item, I12) → e11 (Pack Package, I11)`, a handover that never happened.

**Copied shared events: evidence or duplication?** Both, but never inside the same metric. The copy of `e16` in `O2` is *evidence* that `O2` was exposed to that dispatch (valid for coverage and blame-propagation); it is *duplication* for anything counting physical operations or cost, since `Dispatch` happened 3 times (`e15, e16, e17`) while the Order log reports 6. Rule: event-identity counts for operational volume, case-event rows for exposure, every number labelled with which it is.

**Permitted transformations.** No propagation by default — I read the E2O table literally. A labelled *propagated variant* is allowed as a sensitivity check only (`V1 bills O1`, `O1 contains I11, I12` ⇒ `e18` reaches `I11, I12`), never merged with the direct variant, since propagating through `V2 bills O2, O3` would attach `M2`/`M3` to `I21, I31, I32` and make every item look as if it were paid in instalments. Also permitted: `1/k` reweighting of copied rows, and treating the tie `e29`/`e30` (both 02-04 11:05) as unordered.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

**(1) Order flattening deletes the key logistics edge.** Invoicing and payment fall between dispatch and confirmation in every order: `O1` = `e15(11:00) e16(11:05) e18(12:00) e21(12:30) e23(14:00) e24(16:00)`; `O2` = `e16 e19 e22 e24`; `O3` = `e17 e19 e22 e24 e25 e27`; `O4` = `e17 e20 e26 e27`. The Order DFG therefore holds **zero `Dispatch → Confirm Delivery` edges**, replaced by `Dispatch → Issue Invoice` and `Record Payment → Confirm Delivery`, so anyone measuring transit time on the Order log silently measures a billing interval. Harmful, and invisible unless you test for the edge's absence.

**(2) A fabricated rework loop.** `O1` and `O4` give `Record Payment → Confirm Delivery` (`e21→e23`, `e26→e27`); `O2` and `O3` give the reverse (`e24→e25`). Aggregated, the Order DFG holds a 2-cycle and a discovered model will show a loop or a flower construct. The reality is two concurrent lifecycles (Invoice, Delivery) with no causal link. Harmful; concurrency mistaken for rework.

**(3) Cross-order package `P3` inflates packing volume.** `e13` names `O2, O3, I21, I31, P3` and is copied into both orders, so packing (4 real events, `e11–e14`) reads 6 and a per-order packing-cost model overstates effort by 50%. Harmful for costing; *informative* for "how many orders were touched by a packing operation", where 6 is right.

**(4) Cross-order package `P4` misattributes root cause to `O3`.** `P4` packs `I32` (`O3`) and `I41` (`O4`), rides `D3`, and is confirmed only at 02-04 10:00 (`e27`), 22h45 after `e17`. This makes `O3` the longest, most complex order variant (13 events, 2 packages, 2 deliveries, 2 payments) and pushes it into day 2, so per-order root-cause analysis indicts `O3` when the real driver is a `D3`/`P4` transit problem shared with `O4`. Complexity inherited from co-membership, not behaviour. Harmful.

**(5) Consolidated delivery `D2` doubles counts and invents redelivery.** `e16` and `e24` each carry `O1, O2, O3`, so "deliveries confirmed" reads 6 against 3. Worse, `O1` shows `Confirm Delivery (e23, 14:00) → Confirm Delivery (e24, 16:00)` and `O3` shows `e24 → e27`, a self-loop reading as a failed first attempt when it is a split shipment (`P1`/`D1` plus `P2`/`D2`). Context-dependent: harmful as a rework signal, useful as a split-shipment detector once you know what it means.

**(6) Consolidated invoice `V2` breaks case independence.** `e19` copied into `O2` and `O3` makes `Issue Invoice` read 4 against 3 and double-counts `V2`'s amount per order. More subtly, `O2` and `O3` have *identical by construction* order-to-cash timings (issue 12:05, instalments 12:40 and 02-04 09:00), so any model treating them as two observations has an effective sample of one. Harmful and statistically silent.

**(7) Partial payments `M2`/`M3` give three incompatible prevalence figures.** Order log: 2 of 4 orders show two `Record Payment` events (50%). Invoice log: 1 of 3 invoices shows `Issue → Payment → Payment` (33%). Payment log: invisible, since `M2` and `M3` are unrelated singletons and the relation exists only as O2O qualifiers. Context-dependent, but the invoice denominator is the only one aligned with the object instalments are a property of.

**(8) Payment flattening is a degenerate near-singleton.** `M1→e21`, `M2→e22`, `M3→e25`, `M4→e26`: four cases of length 1, a DFG of one node with no edges, all durations 0, a vacuous log skeleton. "Payments have zero cycle time and perfect conformance" is an artefact of E2O design, since payments are touched only at creation. Harmful alone; informative as a diagnostic that Payment carries no lifecycle here. Delivery (2 events) and Package (3) sit on the same spectrum.

**(9) Item flattening hides the money and truncates the start.** No item relates to `e01–e04`, `e18–e22`, `e25`, `e26`, `e28–e31`. Lead time starts at `Add Item` (`I11` 09:15) instead of `Create Order` (09:00), and concluding "no billing exceptions exist" is reasoning from a deletion — while `e16`/`e24`, copied ×3, make `Dispatch` and `Confirm Delivery` read 6 against 3. Harmful in both directions at once.

**(10) Batched closing flattens all order-level variation.** `e28–e31` land at 02-04 11:00, 11:05, 11:05, 11:10, giving cycle times of 26h00 (`O1`), 26h01 (`O3`), 26h03 (`O2`), 26h04 (`O4`) — a four-minute spread, while delivery ranges 3h00–22h45 and settlement 30 min–21h20. Harmful for performance work; informative as evidence that `Close Order` is an end-of-day batch job rather than a milestone.

**(11) Consolidation is either duplicated or invisible, never faithful.** In the Package log, `P3` is a well-behaved single case whose trace `e13 → e16 → e24` never reveals that it serves `O2` and `O3`; the Order log reveals it only by duplicating it. Decisive for section 5: this argues for object-centric views, not a better lead object.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

**Flattened logs worth building (four of six).** *Order*, as a coverage spine only — safe for "does every order run `Create Order … Close Order`?" (all four do), "which orders were exposed to which shared operations?" (`O1, O2, O3` all touched by `e16`/`e24`), "are there orders with no invoice event?" (none); unsafe for any frequency, duration or discovered model, per distortions (1), (2), (5), (10). *Package*, for the physical handover: pack-to-dispatch is 60/55/53/55 minutes for `P1–P4`. *Delivery*, for transit and on-time analysis (`D1` 3h00, `D2` 4h55, `D3` 22h45). *Invoice*, for days-sales-outstanding and the instalment variant of `V2`. Not worth building: *Payment* (degenerate, distortion 8); *Item* only as an optional divergence-free lead-time reference, flagged that 15 of 31 events are missing.

**Questions that need object-centric machinery.** How many physical packing and dispatch operations occurred, and at what cost — the OC-DFG with *event*, *unique-object* and *total-object* frequencies side by side, since the flattened answers (6 and 6) are wrong and the event-identity answers (4 and 3) are right. Which orders are coupled through shared resources — the OTG and object interaction summary, showing `O1–O2–O3` via `D2`, `O2–O3` via `P3` and `V2`, `O3–O4` via `P4` and `D3`: one component spanning all four orders, which no flattening can state. Which activities touch which object types — the ET–OT graph, showing `Issue Invoice`/`Record Payment` reaching `Order`, `Invoice`, `Payment` but never `Item` or `Package`, and `Create Order`/`Close Order` reaching `Order` alone, i.e. the deletion structure behind distortion (9). Whether `O3` is deviant or merely a hub — interaction counts plus equivalent-execution clustering. Whether the payment/delivery loop is real — the per-type OC-DFG arms, where Delivery shows a strict `Dispatch → Confirm Delivery` and Invoice a strict `Issue → Record Payment (→ Record Payment)`, with no edge between arms.

**Reconciling contradictions.** On volume, the object-centric event count wins and the flattened count is an upper bound, with the convergence factor reported alongside (Order 1.35, Item 1.50, Package 1.20). On ordering, an edge present in a flattened DFG but absent from every single-type OC-DFG arm is presumed an interleaving artefact until a shared object licenses it — which kills the `Record Payment ↔ Confirm Delivery` cycle. On duration, a measurement belongs to the type owning both endpoints: transit to Delivery, settlement to Invoice, packing to Package, with order-level durations reported only as envelopes. Disputes are settled by returning to the E2O table and naming events, never by comparing pictures.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Position B: a primary lead object (Order) plus explicitly secondary lifecycles (Delivery, Invoice, Package).**

**Decision criteria**, in weight order: activity coverage; observability of case boundaries; convergence rate; divergence freedom; discriminative power of the resulting KPI; alignment with the unit of business accountability.

**Strongest supporting evidence.** On coverage and boundaries Order is the only candidate: 8 of 8 activities, and the only type attached to `e01–e04` and `e28–e31`, hence the only case notion with a defined start and end (Item 4 activities, Package 3, Delivery 2, Invoice 2, Payment 1). But on KPI power it is the *worst*: a four-minute cycle-time spread from batched closing, while Delivery separates 3h00 from 22h45 and Invoice 30 minutes from 21h20. It is also worst on convergence (+35%, `e16`/`e24` ×3) and divergence (`O3` holds two packages, two deliveries, two payments). A type uniquely necessary for scope and uniquely bad for measurement is exactly the profile that argues for primary-plus-secondary. The secondary set is not arbitrary: Delivery and Invoice have zero duplication and zero divergence and own both endpoints of the two real KPIs, and Package adds pack-to-dispatch at +20% duplication.

**Strongest counterargument (for C).** Distortion (11) is unanswerable by any hierarchy: `P3` and `P4` belong to two orders each, `D2` to three, `V2` to two, and every flattening either duplicates or erases this. If the goal is consolidation strategy — "should we keep merging shipments across orders?" — no lead object is defensible and only OC-DFG plus OTG can express the question. I accept this by scoping: B governs the reporting layer, while consolidation questions go to the object-centric layer with no flattened counterpart. Position A is rejected outright, since a sole Order lead would publish the `Confirm Delivery` self-loop, the payment/delivery cycle and a near-constant cycle time as findings.

**Residual risk.** Analysts will read the four logs additively and double-count `e13, e14, e16, e17, e19, e22, e24, e25, e27`. The secondary logs are tiny (3 deliveries, 3 invoices, 4 packages), so variance statistics are anecdotal. Assigning transit to Delivery and settlement to Invoice presumes those objects own their delays; an order-level credit hold would be invisible, since no such activity exists in `e01–e31`. And `O3` will keep looking anomalous even though distortion (4) explains it.

**Conditions for changing.** To **A**: if `Close Order` stopped being batched, if `P3, P4, V2, D2` disappeared so orders became one-package/one-invoice, and if Order's convergence factor fell below roughly 1.05. To **C**: if consolidation grew — deliveries routinely carrying packages from four or more orders, so copied rows outnumbered originals — or if order-level and delivery-level rankings contradicted each other beyond what the section 4 rules can adjudicate. I would also revisit if `Issue Invoice` and `Record Payment` began naming items or packages directly, which would make Item a genuine end-to-end candidate instead of a truncated one.

## 6. PM4Py analysis outline

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd
from collections import Counter

# ---------- 0. Baseline description ----------
print(ocel)

object_types      = pm4py.ocel_get_object_types(ocel)
activity_counts   = pm4py.ocel_get_attribute_values(ocel, ocel.event_activity)
objects_per_type  = pm4py.ocel_objects_ot_count(ocel)
ot_activities     = pm4py.ocel_object_type_activities(ocel)   # ET-OT reachability, tabular
temporal_summary  = pm4py.ocel_temporal_summary(ocel)
objects_summary   = pm4py.ocel_objects_summary(ocel)

n_unique_events = ocel.events[ocel.event_id_column].nunique()          # expected 31
print("unique events:", n_unique_events)
print("activity counts (event identity, NOT case-event rows):", activity_counts)
print("objects per type:", objects_per_type)
print("activities reachable per object type:", ot_activities)

# ---------- 1. Flatten on every candidate, quantify the projection ----------
LEAD_TYPES = ["Order", "Item", "Package", "Delivery", "Invoice", "Payment"]
flat_logs, projection_report = {}, {}

for ot in LEAD_TYPES:
    flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, ot)
    flat_logs[ot] = flat
    covered = set(flat[ocel.event_id_column]) if ocel.event_id_column in flat.columns \
              else set(flat["event_id"])
    n_rows, n_cov = len(flat), len(covered)
    projection_report[ot] = {
        "case_event_rows":       n_rows,                 # Order 42, Item 24, Package 12
        "unique_events_covered": n_cov,                  # Order 31, Item 16, Package 10
        "events_deleted":        n_unique_events - n_cov,# Item 15, Package 21, Payment 27
        "convergence_factor":    n_rows / max(n_cov, 1), # Order 1.35, Item 1.50, Package 1.20
        "n_cases":               flat["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
        "mean_trace_len":        n_rows / max(flat["case:concept:name"].nunique(), 1),
        "n_variants":            len(pm4py.get_variants(flat)),
        "inflated_activity_counts": Counter(flat["concept:name"]),
    }

projection_df = pd.DataFrame(projection_report).T
print(projection_df)   # this table, not a picture, justifies the lead-object choice

# ---------- 2. Per-lead control flow and declarative constraints ----------
dfgs, skeletons = {}, {}
for ot in LEAD_TYPES:
    if projection_report[ot]["mean_trace_len"] < 2:
        dfgs[ot], skeletons[ot] = ({}, {}, {}), None   # Payment: length-1 cases, no edges
        continue
    dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(flat_logs[ot])
    dfgs[ot] = (dfg, start_acts, end_acts)
    skeletons[ot] = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(flat_logs[ot], noise_threshold=0.0)

order_dfg, order_start, order_end = dfgs["Order"]

# Distortion (1): the edge must be ABSENT under Order flattening, present under Delivery
edge_order    = order_dfg.get(("Dispatch", "Confirm Delivery"), 0)
edge_delivery = dfgs["Delivery"][0].get(("Dispatch", "Confirm Delivery"), 0)
assert edge_order == 0, "expected Dispatch->Confirm Delivery to vanish in the Order projection"
print("Dispatch->Confirm Delivery :: Order =", edge_order, "| Delivery =", edge_delivery)

# Distortion (2): bidirectional payment/delivery edges = concurrency artefact
print("apparent loop:",
      order_dfg.get(("Record Payment", "Confirm Delivery"), 0),   # O1 e21->e23, O4 e26->e27
      order_dfg.get(("Confirm Delivery", "Record Payment"), 0))   # O2/O3 e24->e25
# Distortion (5): split-shipment self-loop
print("Confirm Delivery self-loop (Order):",
      order_dfg.get(("Confirm Delivery", "Confirm Delivery"), 0))

# ---------- 3. Object-centric views: the reference for volume and coupling ----------
ocdfg        = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
otg          = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot         = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)
interactions = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)

freq = {"events": {}, "unique_objects": {}, "total_objects": {}}
for measure, key in [("events", "event_couples"),
                     ("unique_objects", "unique_objects"),
                     ("total_objects", "total_objects")]:
    for ot, edges in ocdfg["edges"][key].items():
        for edge, payload in edges.items():
            freq[measure][(ot, edge)] = len(payload)

metric_comparison = pd.DataFrame(freq).fillna(0)
print(metric_comparison)   # columns that diverge mark where flattened counts are unsafe
print(interactions.head(50))  # expect O1-O2-O3 via D2, O2-O3 via P3/V2, O3-O4 via P4/D3

# ---------- 4. Equivalent-execution clustering (justified by decision B) ----------
# B keeps Order primary, so clustering Order executions is meaningful: it tests whether
# O2 and O3 are "the same" case once the shared V2 and P3 are accounted for.
order_clusters = pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel, "Order")
print(order_clusters)

# ---------- 5. Deduplicated / reweighted counts ----------
order_flat = flat_logs["Order"]
copies = order_flat.groupby(ocel.event_id_column)["case:concept:name"].transform("nunique")
order_flat = order_flat.assign(weight=1.0 / copies)
comparison = pd.DataFrame({
    "raw":      pd.Series(Counter(order_flat["concept:name"])),
    "weighted": order_flat.groupby("concept:name")["weight"].sum(),

print(comparison)   # weighted counts must reconstruct the event-identity counts from step 0
```

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

**(1) Remove or down-weight copied shared events.** Recompute every Order-log frequency and edge weight three ways: raw (42 rows), `1/k`-weighted (1/3 for `e16`, `e24`; 1/2 for `e13, e14, e17, e19, e22, e25, e27`), and deduplicated to event identity (31); then rebuild the DFG with all nine multi-order events dropped. A conclusion surviving all three is reportable; one appearing only in the raw view — "packing effort is 6 operations", "`O3` is the most complex order" — is an artefact, and I expect `O3`'s complexity to collapse, since 8 of its 13 events are shared.

**(2) Direct versus explicitly propagated E2O.** Build a second OCEL propagating along O2O (`V2 bills O2, O3` ⇒ `e19, e22, e25` reach `I21, I31, I32`) and re-flatten on Item: traces grow from 4 events to roughly 8–9 and every item under `V2` acquires a spurious two-instalment pattern. Report both, never merge; propagation may serve reachability questions ("was this item on an unpaid invoice?") but never counting or timing.

**(3) Change the lead object.** Answer three fixed questions under all six leads and tabulate disagreements: transit time is unmeasurable under Order (distortion 1) and exact under Delivery; packing operations read 6 (Order), 6 (Item), 4 (Package), 4 (event identity); instalment prevalence reads 50%, 33%, undefined. A finding must hold across at least two leads whose E2O sets contain both endpoints of the measurement, or the lead object is named in the sentence reporting it.

**(4) Compare OC-DFG frequency measures.** For every edge compute *events*, *unique objects* and *total objects*. Where they agree, flattened counts are safe; where they diverge — expected on `Pack Package → Dispatch` and `Dispatch → Confirm Delivery` in the Order and Item arms because of `e16`/`e24` — the object-centric count replaces the flattened one, and no chart is published on such an edge without both numbers shown.

**(5) Hub leave-one-out and de-consolidation counterfactual.** Remove `O3` (with `I31, I32`), which touches 13 of 31 events, both cross-order packages, two deliveries and the consolidated invoice: a conclusion that dies when one object leaves a four-order sample is anecdote, not structure. Separately, split `D2` into `D2a`/`D2b` and `V2` into `V2a`/`V2b`, keeping timestamps, and re-flatten on Order — the `Confirm Delivery` self-loop, the `Issue Invoice` count of 4 and roughly half the convergence factor should vanish while `D3`/`P4`'s 22h45 delay persists. What survives de-consolidation belongs to the process; what disappears belonged to the case notion.
