## 1. Feasibility model

```text
Register < Triage
(Identity Check, Document Check) | concurrent when no originals
Originals Receipt < Document Check (when originals required)
(Identity Check, Document Check) < Assessment
Assessment / Compliance Review -> Request More Info -> Document Check < Assessment
(Assessment, Compliance Review) < Decision (with Compliance Review mandatory for high-risk/high-value)
Decision < Notify
Four-eyes rule: Assessor != Compliance Reviewer
```

```text
Conditional and Declarative Constraints:
1. High-risk/high-value cases: Compliance Review must occur after the latest relevant Assessment and before Decision.
2. If preliminary compliance screen is executed, it saves 1-3 h during the final Compliance Review, but does not replace the mandatory post-Assessment final review.
3. Concurrent execution constraint: Identity Check and Document Check may run in parallel; however, physical Originals Receipt strictly gates Document Check when originals are mandated.
4. Rework constraint: Following Request More Info, both a new Document Check and a new Assessment are compulsory; high-risk/high-value cases require a reaffirmed compliance review.
5. Resource exclusivity constraint: No resource may execute two service intervals simultaneously (e.g., C1 cannot run a preliminary check and a final review concurrently).
```

- **K-104:** Alter routing and preview scheduling (creates preliminary compliance parallelization).
- **K-219:** Alter resource routing (substitutes document resource D2 for D1).
- **K-337:** Alter decision policy (truncates rework loop and forces conditional decision).
- **K-411:** Alter timing and concurrency (unblocks parallel task creation at triage).

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104
- `Factual mechanism:` Case entered via T1 at Mon 08:10. Due to sanctions ambiguity noticed at Triage without escalation, the case waited in queues sequentially: Identity Check completed Mon 12:50, Document Check (D1) finished Tue 05:40, Assessment finished Tue 19:10, followed by Escalation Advice, a cold start Compliance Review by C1, and Decision.
- `Minimal intervention:` At Triage, dispatch a preliminary escalation packet to S1 and secure a provisional time slot with C1.
- `Temporal propagation:` Identity Check and Document Check proceed in parallel with preliminary compliance analysis by S1/C1 (saving 2 hours of review time). Assessment completes at Tue 17:10 (2h earlier). Final Compliance Review starts immediately upon the reserved C1 slot on Wed morning (or Tue evening), bypassing the 18-hour queue wait.
- `Process-feasibility check:` Satisfies four-eyes rule (Assessor A1 $\neq$ Compliance Reviewer C1). Compliance review follows Assessment. Provisional C1 slot respects resource availability.
- `Best/base/worst case:` Best: 36 h total duration, C1 review streamlined by 2.5 h. Base: 44 h total duration, C1 review streamlined by 1.5 h. Worst: 52 h if provisional C1 reservation is invalidated by an emergency override, forcing queue re-entry.
- `Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:` If the preliminary escalation packet lacks crucial data, S1 advisory work must restart from scratch, rendering the provisional slot inefficient and delaying C1.

### K-219
- `Factual mechanism:` Routed to D1 because of handwritten documents. Originals arrived Mon 17:30, but D1 queue kept the task waiting until Tue 09:30.
- `Minimal intervention:` Assign document check to D2 as soon as Originals Receipt is logged (Mon 17:30).
- `Temporal propagation:` D2 starts Document Check earlier (Tue 08:00 when D2 queue clears, instead of Tue 09:30 on D1). Although D2 takes longer per unit (say, 9 h vs D1's 4 h), the avoidance of D1's heavy queue brings completion forward by 12–18 hours. Assessment by A2 shifts from Tue 20:00 to Tue 14:00.
- `Process-feasibility check:` Originals Receipt is complete before D2 starts. Four-eyes and compliance rules are unaffected (low risk/low value).
- `Best/base/worst case:` Best: 24 h duration, D2 completes check without clarification. Base: 30 h duration. Worst: 38 h if D2 struggles with handwriting, triggers a clarification request, and delays downstream assessment.
- `Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:` If handwriting illegibility exceeds D2's capability threshold, causing an immediate rework loop that negates the queue-saving gain.

### K-337
- `Factual mechanism:` Suffered two supplier rework loops (Request More Info 1 and 2), pushing completion to Wed of the following week (166 hours total).
- `Minimal intervention:` Prohibit the second Request More Info loop after Assessment 2; enforce a terminal decision using existing evidence.
- `Temporal propagation:` Eliminates Request More Info 2 (spanning Fri 10:30 to Mon 09:00), Document Check 3, and Assessment 3 entirely. The process moves directly from Assessment 2 (Fri 10:30) to Compliance Review and Decision, saving ~90 hours.
- `Process-feasibility check:` Decision policy is altered: rather than gathering missing data, M1 issues a conditional approval or risk-adjusted rejection based on incomplete supplier figures. Compliance Review still follows Assessment 2 and precedes Decision.
- `Best/base/worst case:` Best: 72 h total duration, clean conditional approval. Base: 84 h duration. Worst: 96 h duration with heightened operational risk due to unresolved supplier discrepancies.
- `Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:` If internal risk policy strictly forbids conditional approval when numerical discrepancies exceed 5%, forcing mandatory escalation rather than truncation.

### K-411
- `Factual mechanism:` Legacy system serial-locked verification tasks. Identity Check ran Tue 11:30–13:20; Document Check only spawned after, running Tue 15:00–19:30.
- `Minimal intervention:` Create both tasks at Triage and deploy a controlled parallel execution workaround.
- `Temporal propagation:` Identity Check and Document Check execute concurrently starting Tue 09:35 (or upon resource availability in I2 and D2 queues). Document Check finishes much earlier (Tue 13:30 instead of 19:30), allowing Assessment by A2 to start on Tue afternoon instead of Wednesday morning.
- `Process-feasibility check:` Both checks complete prior to Assessment. No originals required. Four-eyes rule not violated.
- `Best/base/worst case:` Best: 20 h total duration. Base: 24 h duration. Worst: 28 h duration if simultaneous arrival at Assessment queue causes A2 queuing congestion.
- `Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:` If the legacy system lock cannot be bypassed without generating invalid audit logs that fail compliance inspection.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 (Preview Escalation) | Increases early S1 and C1 load | Compliance queue (net) | Compliance queue (initial reservation) | Urgent compliance cases waiting for C1 | Mixed / Negative on queue variance | Holding C1 slots creates step-function wait times for concurrent high-risk cases |
| K-219 (D2 Substitution) | Shifts load from D1 to D2 | Document Check (D1) | Document Check (D2) | Standard cases queued behind D2 | Positive (better pool balancing) | D2 higher processing variance causes sudden queue spikes when handwriting is complex |
| K-337 (Truncate Rework) | Reduces future D1 and A1 demand | Document & Assessment queues | Decision queue (higher review burden) | None directly; frees capacity | Strongly Positive (frees system capacity) | Eliminating rework loops drops total system load nonlinearly, freeing bottleneck resources |
| K-411 (Parallel Verification) | Shifts demand earlier in the day | Assessment queue (wait time spread) | Assessment queue (immediate arrival peak) | Cases arriving Tuesday afternoon | Neutral / Negative | Simultaneous completion of parallel tasks creates local batching spikes at Assessment |

### 100-Case Weekly Rollout Analysis
Applying these interventions simultaneously across 100 weekly cases reveals severe systemic interactions and nonlinearities. 

First, **compliance capacity reservation** (K-104 policy) scales poorly. C1 is the sole certified compliance reviewer. If multiple cases reserve provisional C1 slots, C1's effective availability drops precipitously, causing severe queuing for unreserved emergency cases and pushing wait times beyond SLA limits. 

Second, **pool substitution** between D1 and D2 (K-219 policy) helps balance load when D1 is overloaded with complex corporate documents. However, offloading handwritten standard docs to D2 increases D2 queue lengths and risks higher rework rates, shifting the bottleneck from D1 to D2. 

Third, **rework suppression** (K-337 policy) acts as a powerful capacity-release valve. By eliminating second-round supplier loops, system-wide load on D1 and A1 drops significantly, absorbing the surge caused by parallel verification workarounds (K-411). 

Fourth, **parallel verification** (K-411) compresses cycle times locally but exacerbates downstream bunching. When Identity and Document checks finish concurrently, they flood the Assessment queue (A1/A2) simultaneously, creating a transient capacity wall. 

Finally, **weekend and external-response effects** interact badly with truncation policies: rushing decisions before supplier responses arrive saves internal time but transfers risk to the decision maker, potentially inflating appeal rates and generating downstream rework that consumes more total resource hours than it saved.

## 6. Pilot designs

| pilot element | intervention 1 (K-337: Truncate Rework) | intervention 2 (K-219: D2 Routing) |
|---|---|---|
| eligibility | All cases triggering a second Request More Info loop | Standard incoming cases with handwritten documents |

| sample/support logic | 100 cases over 4 weeks ($\approx 25$ treatment cases) | 200 cases over 4 weeks ($\approx 100$ per resource) |
| primary outcome | Total process cycle time (hours from Register to Notify) | Document Check completion time and total cycle time |
| process-path outcome | Zero second-round rework loops in treatment arm | Document Check executed by D2 without excessive clarification |
| quality outcome | Decision overturn rate, conditional approval ratio, audit defect rate | Rework rate, subsequent Assessment rejection frequency |

## 7. Synthesis

The most robust intervention is **K-337 (Truncate Rework Loop)**, because eliminating redundant external waiting cycles provides unmatched cycle-time reduction while directly addressing operational paralysis caused by unresponsive suppliers. Conversely, **K-104 (Early Compliance Prep)** is the most dangerous intervention; while it accelerates individual high-risk cases, its reliance on advance slot reservation for a single bottleneck resource (C1) creates severe queue starvation and negative externalities for other urgent cases. 

A critical insight from this analysis is that a local case counterfactual is **least transportable** when it assumes bottleneck resources (like C1) possess elastic capacity. Locally, reserving a slot feels costless; system-wide, it gridlocks the compliance queue. 

To improve future operational modeling, the most valuable additional data to collect are **external supplier response latency distributions** (to calibrate rework risks) and **fine-grained resource concurrency logs** (to track actual vs. nominal multitasking by resources like S1 and C1).

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **Structural:** Mandated control-flow prerequisites (Register before Triage, Document/Identity before Assessment, Assessment before Compliance/Decision) remain strictly immutable across all counterfactuals. (`structural`)
2. **Temporal:** External supplier response times for document collection follow historical probability distributions (8–36 h) unless explicitly truncated by policy. (`temporal`)
3. **Resource:** C1 is strictly a single-resource bottleneck; no additional certified compliance reviewers can be added during the evaluation window. (`resource`)
4. **Behavioral:** Assessors and managers will adhere to forced-decision policies without engaging in informal, unlogged offline information gathering. (`behavioral`)
5. **Quality:** D2's capability on handwritten documents exhibits a predictable error and clarification rate ($\le 25\%$) rather than catastrophic failure. (`quality`)
6. **Interference:** Queue wait times respond dynamically to upstream task release rates, meaning early task completion increases instantaneous load on downstream queues. (`interference`)
7. **Structural:** Legacy system workarounds in K-411 maintain immutable and compliant audit trail timestamps without data corruption. (`structural`)
8. **Behavioral:** S1 advisory work and preliminary C1 screening do not violate the four-eyes independence rule between final Assessment and final Compliance Review. (`behavioral`)
