## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

Treating the January–October trajectory as one homogeneous model decay would conflate four fundamentally different failure mechanisms: an **ingestion/schema outage (April)**, a **benign policy-driven process mix shift (June)**, **compounding external operational/concept and population shifts (July–September)**, and an **informative measurement/censoring collapse (October)**.

### Month-by-month audit narrative

* **January–March (Stable baseline):** System performance is well-calibrated and stable across 3,200–3,410 monthly cases at 100% label maturity (median delay 32–34 d). Observed breach rate (0.18–0.19) matches mean predicted risk (0.18–0.19), AUROC is 0.81–0.82, Brier is 0.130–0.132, and ECE is 0.022–0.025. Variant shares (V1 44–45%, V2 35–36%, V3 20%), within-variant breach rates (V1 ~0.16, V2 ~0.11, V3 ~0.31), and parts delivery times (28–35 h) establish a clean reference regime.
* **April (Data-pipeline failure—not operational or concept drift):** On 1 April, the workflow migration renamed `Initial Diagnosis Completed` to `Diagnostic Assessment Completed`, generated duplicates, and emitted `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` (17.0% vs 0.3% baseline). Unmapped variants spiked to 18% and missing queue features rose to 14%. Because prediction prefixes were built at the wrong timestamp or omitted, mean predicted risk surged to 0.28, AUROC collapsed to 0.67, and ECE hit 0.110. Crucially, **observed evidence refutes operational/concept drift**: overall matured breach rate stayed at 0.19 and `Order Part -> Part Received` held at 34 h. Slight +0.02 ticks in mapped variant breach rates (V1 0.18, V2 0.13, V3 0.34) are a plausible **hypothesis** for minor cutover friction or selection into cleanly logged traces—falsifiable once April logs are normalized and replayed.
* **May (Partial recovery with residual pipeline debt):** Following the 6 May mapping patch, unknown activity fell to 2.0%, missing queue to 3%, AUROC rebounded to 0.78, and within-variant breach rates returned to baseline (0.16 / 0.11 / 0.33). The remaining gap vs Jan–Mar (mean predicted risk 0.21 vs 0.18 observed; ECE 0.050) is consistent with the 5 unpatched days (May 1–5  16% of the month) plus residual cached-feature/deduplication inconsistencies. `Order Part -> Part Received` crept to 38 h—early, non-critical drift.
* **June (Benign process/routing drift—not model breakdown):** On 1 June, the remote-routing policy shifted cases from V1 (43%30%) into V2 (34%52%), with V3 at 18%. Because V2 has a low ~0.09–0.11 breach rate and `Diagnosis -> Remote Fix` stayed fast (3.8–4.6 h), overall breach rate dropped to 0.15—fully explained by the new variant mix ($0.30\times0.16 + 0.52\times0.09 + 0.18\times0.35 = 0.158$). Discrimination stayed strong (AUROC 0.79, Brier 0.139). However, mean predicted risk remained 0.20 (ECE 0.060). **Hypothesis:** Because predictions fire immediately after diagnosis without a routing-policy feature, cases that previously went to V1 (16% breach) now go to V2 (9% breach), creating a localized, intervention-induced shift in $P(Y=1 \mid X)$ that over-predicts risk without harming rank order. This would be falsified if the model already receives the routing decision and still over-predicts within unchanged V1/V2 subpopulations.
* **July (Onset of supplier bottleneck + deceptive error cancellation):** Starting 10 July, the parts supplier missed delivery targets, pushing `Order Part -> Part Received` from 42 h to 65 h and V3 breach rate from 0.35 to 0.46 (V1 0.17 and V2 0.10 remained stable). AUROC slipped to 0.76 and Brier worsened to 0.151. Notably, **ECE paradoxically improved from 0.060 to 0.042**, and mean predicted risk (0.18) nearly matched observed breach rate (0.19). This is a classic **cancellation of opposing errors**: June’s +0.05 over-prediction on rerouted V2 cases was offset by new under-prediction on deteriorating V3 cases. Label maturity dipped slightly to 98%.
* **August (Severe performance-process drift driving V3 concept drift + emerging censoring):** The supplier failure escalated: `Order Part -> Part Received` jumped to 130 h (~4× baseline), V3 matured breach rate surged to 0.64, and V3 share climbed from 22% to 35% (even prior to Partner Z). Because pre-diagnosis features contain no live supplier lead-time signal, the model predicted 0.19 against an observed breach rate of 0.28, crashing AUROC to 0.68 and spiking ECE to 0.120. Moreover, the variant-weighted matured breach rate ($0.25\times0.18 + 0.40\times0.11 + 0.35\times0.64 = 0.313$) already exceeds the 0.28 cutoff rate at 93% maturity—evidence that right-censoring of slower breaching cases had begun.
* **September (Overlapping population shift, unmodeled covariates, persistent concept drift, and growing censoring):** Partner Z onboarded on 1 September (24% of 5,100 cases; 58% V3 vs 36% non-Z), driving overall V3 share to 42% ($0.24\times0.58 + 0.76\times0.36 = 0.413$) while supplier delays worsened to 148 h. Mean predicted risk ticked up to 0.21 (reflecting genuine covariate drift in observed inputs), but lagged far behind the 0.31 observed breach rate (Partner Z 0.45 vs non-Z 0.27; AUROC 0.66; ECE 0.140). Two competing mechanisms overlap: (a) Partner Z’s heavy V3 mix exposes it disproportionately to the supplier crisis, and (b) Partner Z’s unmodeled rural travel distance and older devices induce additional omitted-variable concept drift. Meanwhile, maturity dropped to 82% (slower for Partner Z), and the matured variant-weighted breach rate (~0.34) exceeds the 0.31 cutoff rate—indicating both overall and Partner Z breach rates are **understated** at cutoff.
* **October (Informative label censoring / outcome API backlog masking ongoing operational failure):** Maturity plunged to 55% (median delay 52 d) due to an outcome API backlog hitting longer resolution paths hardest. Consequently, observed breach rate collapsed artificially from 0.31 to 0.16, making the model (mean predicted risk 0.23) appear suddenly *over-predictive* despite AUROC deteriorating further to 0.64. Process and population evidence—`Order Part -> Part Received` worsening to 155 h, V3 at 41%, Partner Z at 27%—proves the underlying August–September deterioration did **not** improve. October’s apparent drop in breach rate is a censoring illusion, falsifiable only if the fully matured October cohort settles near 0.16.

* **Expected result under H1 (Targeted remote-routing shift in Jun + targeted parts-supply bottleneck Jul–Oct):** June shows a clean control-flow frequency shift (`Diagnosis -> Remote Fix` up, `Diagnosis -> Dispatch` down) with stable edge latencies; Jul–Oct show extreme right-tail and median inflation specifically on `Order Part -> Part Received` (65155 h), while other edges stay stable (except potential `Dispatch -> Repair` lengthening for rural Partner Z in Sep–Oct).
* **Expected result under H2 (System-wide field-technician capacity collapse):** Median and p90 sojourn times inflate broadly across `Diagnosis -> Dispatch`, `Dispatch -> Repair Completed`, and `Diagnosis -> Remote Fix`, not just on the parts-delivery edge.

### 5. Conditional outcome stability test given identical process path ($P(Y=1 \mid X, \text{variant}, \text{time})$)
* **Procedure:** Fit a diagnostic logistic/GAM model on matured Jan–Sep data predicting breach $Y$ from the frozen model’s log-odds score $s(X)$, variant dummies, month indicators, and `month × variant` interaction terms; repeat adding `Order Part -> Part Received` duration (as an ex-post mechanism check) and inspect likelihood-ratio tests and partial dependence.
* **Expected result under H1 (Supplier-induced concept drift in V3, stable relationship elsewhere):** `month × V3` coefficients become strongly positive and statistically significant starting July (and huge in Aug–Sep), while `month × V1` and `month × V2` remain near zero. Crucially, once ex-post `Order Part -> Part Received` duration is added, the `month × V3` time-effect collapses toward zero—proving supplier latency mediates the concept drift.
* **Expected result under H2 (Pure covariate drift or undocumented SLA/outcome-definition change):** If pure covariate drift, all `month` and `month × variant` terms are near zero given $s(X)$. If an undocumented SLA tightening occurred globally, main-effect `month` intercepts jump uniformly across V1, V2, and V3 and persist even after controlling for `Order Part -> Part Received`.

### 6. Population reweighting and transportability checks for Partner Z
* **Procedure:** On matured Sep cases (and re-run once Oct matures), estimate propensity weights $w(X) = P(\text{Legacy} \mid X) / P(\text{Partner Z} \mid X)$ using (a) observed model covariates $X$ alone, and (b) $X$ plus variant (V1/V2/V3). Compare Partner Z’s reweighted breach rate, AUROC, and calibration to Legacy’s Sep performance; additionally run a diagnostic model on Partner Z adding rural travel distance and device age to quantify incremental explanatory power ($\Delta$AUROC, $\Delta$Brier).
* **Expected result under H1 (Partner Z gap is purely observable covariate/V3-mix shift):** Reweighting Partner Z to Legacy’s $(X, \text{variant})$ distribution brings Partner Z’s breach rate (~0.45) down to match Legacy’s (~0.27) and equalizes calibration error, with negligible gain from rural distance/device age.
* **Expected result under H2 (Unmodeled-variable population shift / omitted-variable concept drift):** Even after reweighting for observed $X$ and V3 share, Partner Z retains a significantly higher residual breach rate and under-prediction bias, which shrinks substantially once rural travel distance and device age are introduced.

### 7. Feature missingness, distribution, and lineage audits
* **Procedure:** Audit end-to-end feature lineage (raw event $\to$ transformation $\to$ cache/feature store $\to$ inference log) across Jan–Oct. Compute monthly missingness, zero/default-imputation rates, PSI/Wasserstein distance per feature, and parity diffs between batch-backfilled features and live-served inference logs (specifically inspecting the cached "number of events before diagnosis" feature and queue metrics).
* **Expected result under H1 (April/early-May pipeline bug + clean Jun–Oct feature pipeline):** Massive live-vs-recomputed parity mismatches and elevated missingness/duplicate counts concentrate in Apr 1–May 6 (and un-backfilled Apr cache), while Jun–Oct show <1% parity mismatch and genuine, smooth covariate shifts in Sep–Oct aligned with Partner Z’s arrival.
* **Expected result under H2 (Ongoing silent feature-store corruption in Jul–Oct):** Live-vs-recomputed parity mismatches or stale cached features persist in Jul–Oct, explaining part of the late-year AUROC drop independent of supplier delays.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

A single "retrain now" or "recalibrate now" directive would bake either April’s pipeline corruption or October’s informative censoring into production. A **four-stage proportionate remediation plan** is required:

### Stage 1: Immediate containment (Week 1 — No retraining, no global recalibration on Oct)
* **Quarantine corrupted/immature periods:** Exclude **October** (55% maturity), **un-repaired April**, and **immature September** traces from any calibration or training job.
* **Operational risk guardrails:** Because V3 cases currently breach at >60% while the model predicts ~20%, attach an explicit operational override/flag (e.g., high-risk SLA escalation banner whenever diagnosis indicates parts are required while rolling 7-day `Order Part -> Part Received` median exceeds 72 h) rather than mis-calibrating the entire model.
* **Pipeline triage:** Clear the October outcome API backlog and deploy strict schema/label-vocabulary and deduplication gates on the event ingestion pipeline.

### Stage 2: Feature repair and historical data hygiene (Weeks 1–3)
* **Backfill and recompute:** Apply activity-label normalization (`Diagnostic Assessment Completed` $\to$ `Initial Diagnosis Completed`), full-period deduplication (including Apr 1–20), and full recomputation of the cached "number of events before diagnosis" and queue features for April–May.
* **Verify repair via replay:** Confirm via Diagnostic Test #2 that repaired April–May metrics align with baseline before declaring those months clean.

### Stage 3: Short-term recalibration (Weeks 2–4 — Scoped, conditional, and temporary)
* **Why recalibration alone is insufficient long-term:** Platt/isotonic recalibration preserves rank order (AUROC). It can fix June’s mild +0.05 global calibration shift, but it **cannot** recover the AUROC collapse from 0.81 to 0.66–0.68 in Aug–Sep because the model lacks predictive signals for supplier lead time, routing policy, rural distance, and device age.
* **Safe interim recalibration:** Once September reaches 95% maturity (or using matured August + fully matured September cohorts only—never October), fit a **stratified intercept/slope recalibration** (e.g., separate calibration shifts for Legacy vs Partner Z, and—only if known at diagnosis time—parts-expected vs non-parts cases) to immediately repair severely understated risk probabilities while features are engineered.

### Stage 4: Feature-augmented full retraining (Weeks 4–8 — After maturity & feature ingestion)
* **New features required before retraining:**
  1. **Dynamic supplier/process-state features (computed strictly at diagnosis timestamp):** rolling 7-day/14-day median and p90 `Order Part -> Part Received` duration (overall and by part/supplier), open parts-order backlog count. This lets the model adapt dynamically whether the supplier recovers or stays degraded.
  2. **Routing-policy context:** active remote-routing policy flag and diagnosis-time routing intent/eligibility indicator (to account for June’s V2 shift).
  3. **Partner Z / population covariates:** source/partner ID, rural travel distance, device age/model, technician-zone capacity.
* **Training-window safety assessment:**

| Month(s) | Training status | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Jan–Mar** | **Safe (with regime features / time-decay weighting)** | 100% mature, clean pipeline, stable baseline; valuable for learning baseline V1/V2/V3 risk and pre-crisis supplier-lead-time elasticity, though must not dominate post-June routing dynamics without policy features. |
| **Apr** | **Unsafe until repaired** | Corrupted prefixes, duplicates, un-backfilled cached event count; safe *only* after full raw-log replay and parity verification. |
| **May** | **Partially unsafe (May 1–5) until repaired** | Pre-patch ingestion errors and residual cache contamination; safe after full recomputation. |
| **Jun** | **Safe** | 100% mature, clean pipeline, reflects the new V2 remote-routing policy; essential to include so the model learns post-policy routing probabilities. |
| **Jul–Aug** | **Conditionally safe (matured cases only; Aug after 98% maturity)** | Captures supplier-crisis regime; safe to include *provided* live supplier lead-time features are added (otherwise training on Aug permanently hard-codes 130 h part delays even if the supplier recovers). |
| **Sep** | **Unsafe now; safe once maturity 95–98%** | Currently 82% mature with slower Partner Z resolution; training now introduces downward censoring bias especially on Partner Z. |
| **Oct** | **Strictly unsafe** | 55% maturity and severe informative right-censoring from outcome API backlog; must remain quarantined until backlog clears and cohort matures (~60–90 d vintage). |

### Justification for segment-specific vs variant-specific models
* **Variant-specific models (Not directly justified at inference time—use a two-stage or routing-intent design instead):** Because prediction occurs immediately after `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, the realized downstream variant (V1, V2, or V3) is **a future outcome, not an observed input** unless the routing decision is already finalized and logged at diagnosis completion. Routing inference through separate V1/V2/V3 models using *ex-post* variants would cause target leakage and fail in production. Instead, if diagnosis emits a routing/parts-needed flag, include it as an explicit interaction feature—or adopt a **two-stage architecture** that predicts $P(\text{variant} \mid X)$ at diagnosis and combines it with path-conditional breach risk $P(Y=1 \mid X, \text{variant}, \text{supplier\_ETA})$.
* **Segment-specific model for Partner Z (Not yet justified as a standalone model; prefer unified model + features + segment calibration):** Partner Z is observable at inference time, but currently has fewer than two months of immature data (~2,600 started cases total, mostly immature)—far too small and censored to train a separate high-variance model without overfitting. Start with a **unified model** incorporating partner indicator, rural travel distance, device age, and interaction terms (or regularized hierarchical offsets), paired with **Partner Z–specific calibration monitoring**. Revisit a dedicated Partner Z model only after 3–4 fully matured months if stratified residuals persist despite adding rural distance and device age.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

### Layered monitoring components

1. **Data-quality & pipeline integrity monitors (real-time / daily):**
   * Activity-vocabulary diff (alert on any unseen activity label or drop in `Initial Diagnosis Completed` frequency >5%).
   * `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` share (warn >1%, critical >3%), duplicate-event rate, feature missingness (queue feature >2%), and live-vs-batch feature-cache parity check.
2. **Label-maturity & outcome-pipeline monitors (daily / weekly):**
   * Outcome API lag, queue depth, and error rate.
   * Cohort label maturity % at fixed horizons ($t=30, 45, 60$ d), median/p90 label delay, and maturity disparity across variants (V3 vs V2) and sources (Partner Z vs legacy).
3. **Process-variant & control-flow monitors (weekly / monthly):**
   * Monthly variant share distribution (V1/V2/V3/unmapped) and Jensen–Shannon divergence vs reference window.
   * Unmapped/rare variant share (warn >2%).
4. **Performance-DFG monitors (daily rolling / monthly):**
   * Edge-level median and p90 elapsed times from `discover_performance_dfg`, especially `Order Part -> Part Received` (warn >50 h, critical >75 h), `Diagnosis -> Remote Fix` (warn >6 h), and `Dispatch -> Repair Completed`.
   * Tracked on **event-calendar** windows for immediate operational bottleneck detection.
5. **Population & covariate monitors (weekly / monthly):**
   * Source-share shifts (alert on new partner >5% volume), feature-level PSI/Wasserstein distance on clean inputs, and out-of-distribution checks on newly added attributes (rural travel distance, device age).
6. **Predictive discrimination monitors (fixed-vintage cohorts only):**
   * AUROC, PR-AUC, and Brier score computed overall and stratified by `(source, variant)` strictly on cohorts with 95% maturity (plus shadow IPCW estimates for 30–60 d interim visibility).
7. **Calibration monitors (fixed-vintage cohorts only):**
   * ECE, calibration-in-the-large (mean predicted  observed), and calibration slope stratified by `(source, variant)`—specifically preventing opposing-error cancellation like July’s misleading 0.042 aggregate ECE.
8. **Intervention, deployment & business-context monitors (event-driven):**
   * Registry of workflow releases, routing-policy toggles, partner onboardings, and supplier SLA alerts; shadow-mode comparison before promoting any recalibration/retrain.

### Alert dependencies and gating logic

To prevent cascading false alarms (such as October’s label backlog or April’s schema bug triggering destructive retraining), alerts follow a strict **dependency DAG**:

* **Gate 1 (Data-Quality Gate):** If `Data-Quality` is **RED** (schema rename, `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` >3%, missingness spike, or parity failure) $\rightarrow$ **suppress** automated concept-drift/retraining alarms, page Data Engineering, and mark affected periods as `pipeline_tainted`.
* **Gate 2 (Label-Maturity Gate):** If `Label-Maturity` is **RED** (cohort maturity <95%, API backlog active, or V3/Partner Z maturity gap >10 pp) $\rightarrow$ **freeze** automated calibration/retraining triggers and suppress cutoff-based ECE/breach-rate alarms; route to Outcome-API/Platform Engineering and switch dashboards to fixed-vintage + process-proxy reporting.
* **Gate 3 (Process & Population Attribution):** Only when Gates 1 and 2 are **GREEN** (or via independent event-calendar process monitors):
  * `Performance-DFG` **RED** (e.g., `Order Part -> Part Received` >75 h) + `Stratified V3 Calibration` **RED** $\rightarrow$ classify as **operational/concept drift**, trigger SLA escalation guardrail and feature-augmented retrain review.
  * `Process-Variant` **AMBER/RED** + `Intervention Registry` policy change + stable within-variant calibration $\rightarrow$ classify as **planned routing/process shift**, trigger targeted recalibration/policy-feature check rather than emergency retrain.
  * `Population` **RED** (new partner/OOD covariates) $\rightarrow$ trigger stratified transportability audit before any model update.

### Concise Python implementation for monthly process-evidence extraction

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

LABEL_MAP = {
    "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed",
}

def extract_monthly_process_evidence(
    csv_path: str = "field_service_events.csv",
    diagnosis_activity: str = "Initial Diagnosis Completed",
) -> dict[str, pd.DataFrame]:
    """
    Extract auditable monthly process-mining summaries from field-service logs.
    Produces BOTH event-calendar cohorts (live operational latency in month M)
    and case-start cohorts (aligned with prediction cohorts; recent months may be right-censored).
    """
    raw = pd.read_csv(csv_path)
    raw["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(raw["timestamp"], utc=True)

    # 1. Normalize renamed diagnosis labels BEFORE deduplication and feature extraction
    raw["activity_raw"] = raw["activity"]
    raw["activity"] = raw["activity"].replace(LABEL_MAP)

    # 2. Duplicate-handling strategy:
    #    Drop exact duplicates on (case_id, normalized activity, 1-second timestamp bucket)
    #    keeping the earliest ingested record so Apr 1–20 duplicates are handled consistently.
    raw["ts_sec"] = raw["timestamp"].dt.floor("s")
    raw = raw.sort_values(["case_id", "timestamp"])
    dedup_mask = ~raw.duplicated(subset=["case_id", "activity", "ts_sec"], keep="first")
    dup_rate_overall = 1.0 - dedup_mask.mean()
    clean = raw.loc[dedup_mask].drop(columns=["ts_sec"]).copy()

    # Format into PM4Py standard columns (case:concept:name, concept:name, time:timestamp)
    log_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        clean,
        case_id="case_id",
        activity_key="activity",
        timestamp_key="timestamp",
    )

    # Compute case start timestamp for case-start cohort assignment
    case_starts = log_df.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].transform("min")
    log_df["case_start_month"] = case_starts.dt.tz_localize(None).dt.to_period("M")

    months = sorted(log_df["time:timestamp"].dt.tz_localize(None).dt.to_period("M").unique())
    event_calendar_rows, case_start_rows = [], []

    def _edge_median_hours(perf_dfg: dict, edge: tuple[str, str]) -> float | None:
        val = perf_dfg.get(edge)
        if val is None:
            return None
        # Handle PM4Py dict aggregation ({'median': ..., 'mean': ...}) or scalar seconds
        sec = val.get("median", val.get("mean")) if isinstance(val, dict) else val
        return round(float(sec) / 3600.0, 2) if sec is not None else None

    for period in months:
        start_ts = period.to_timestamp(how="start").strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S")
        end_ts = period.to_timestamp(how="end").strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S")

        # --- Cohort A: Event-calendar cohort ---
        # Interpretation: All events occurring inside calendar month M regardless of case start.
        # Best for detecting real-time bottlenecks (e.g., supplier latency in Jul–Oct) without
        # waiting for cases to close, though traces spanning month boundaries are truncated.
        ev_df = pm4py.filter_time_range(log_df, start_ts, end_ts, mode="events")
        if not ev_df.empty:
            ev_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(ev_df)
            ev_perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(ev_df)
            event_calendar_rows.append({
                "month": str(period),
                "events_in_month": len(ev_df),
                "active_cases": ev_df["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
                "unknown_activity_share": round((ev_df["concept:name"] == "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean(), 4),
                "order_to_part_freq": ev_dfg.get(("Order Part", "Part Received"), 0),
                "order_to_part_median_h": _edge_median_hours(ev_perf_dfg, ("Order Part", "Part Received")),
                "diag_to_remote_median_h": _edge_median_hours(ev_perf_dfg, (diagnosis_activity, "Remote Fix")),
            })

        # --- Cohort B: Case-start cohort ---
        # Interpretation: Full traces of cases initiated in month M.
        # Aligns directly with monthly predictive evaluation cohorts and variant mix;
        # however, recent months are right-censored until long-running cases close.
        cs_df = log_df.loc[log_df["case_start_month"] == period].copy()
        if not cs_df.empty:
            variants = pm4py.get_variants(cs_df)
            total_cases = sum(variants.values())
            top_variants = sorted(variants.items(), key=lambda kv: kv[1], reverse=True)[:3]
            cs_perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(cs_df)

            # Extract auditable temporal features summary (no model fitting)
            temp_feats = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(cs_df)
            numeric_temp = temp_feats.select_dtypes(include="number")

            case_start_rows.append({
                "month": str(period),
                "cases_started": total_cases,
                "unique_variants": len(variants),
                "top3_variant_coverage": round(sum(c for _, c in top_variants) / max(total_cases, 1), 4),
                "order_to_part_median_h": _edge_median_hours(cs_perf_dfg, ("Order Part", "Part Received")),
                "diag_to_remote_median_h": _edge_median_hours(cs_perf_dfg, (diagnosis_activity, "Remote Fix")),
                "temporal_feature_cols": numeric_temp.shape[1],
                "temporal_feature_null_rate": round(float(numeric_temp.isna().mean().mean()), 4)
                if not numeric_temp.empty else None,
                "overall_dedup_rate_log": round(dup_rate_overall, 4),
            })

    return {
        "event_calendar_summary": pd.DataFrame(event_calendar_rows),
        "case_start_summary": pd.DataFrame(case_start_rows),
    }
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

1. **Retraining or recalibrating on immature, informatively censored labels (October / September)**
   * *Failure mode:* Taking October’s 0.16 observed breach rate at face value (or folding immature Sep–Oct traces into training) teaches the model that V3 and Partner Z cases rarely breach—precisely when their true breach risk exceeds 0.60 (`Order Part -> Part Received` = 155 h). This severely worsens under-prediction, destroys SLA triage, and misses genuine breaches for the highest-risk customers.
   * *Mitigation:* Enforce a hard **Label-Maturity Gate** (95% maturity or fixed 60–90 d vintage) before any training/recalibration dataset build; track maturity-by-variant and maturity-by-source alongside outcome API queue depth.

2. **Calibrating or retraining around a data-pipeline bug (April / un-backfilled cache)**
   * *Failure mode:* Treating April’s AUROC crash (0.67) and risk over-prediction (0.28 vs 0.19) as concept drift—and recalibrating downward or retraining on un-repaired April data—bakes broken prefix features, duplicates, and `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` encodings into model weights. Once the May 6 mapping patch restores normal features, the "patched" model immediately breaks in the opposite direction (systematic under-prediction on clean inputs).
   * *Mitigation:* Enforce a **Data-Quality Gate** blocking model updates during schema/missingness anomalies; require full raw-log replay, deduplication, and feature-store parity verification before any incident month enters training history.

3. **Treating genuine supplier-driven concept drift as a recalibration problem—or missing it via aggregate error cancellation (July–September)**
   * *Failure mode:* Relying on global ECE (which deceptively improved to 0.042 in July due to opposing V2 over-prediction and V3 under-prediction) delays intervention while V3 breach rates double to 0.64. Applying only global recalibration afterward fails to restore discrimination (AUROC stays ~0.66–0.68) and over-predicts V1/V2 while still under-predicting V3; conversely, retraining on August without adding dynamic supplier lead-time features permanently bakes crisis-era breach rates into the model even if the supplier later recovers.
   * *Mitigation:* Mandate variant-stratified calibration and performance-DFG edge alerts; add live supplier lead-time/backlog features prior to retraining so the model generalizes across both crisis and normal supply states.

4. **Attributing Partner Z’s population shift to unfair or biased model behavior without stratified, maturity-adjusted evidence (September–October)**
   * *Failure mode:* Observing a 0.45 breach rate for Partner Z vs 0.27 for legacy sources and hastily removing partner identifiers, equalizing predicted rates via post-hoc fairness constraints, or abandoning the model misdiagnoses an operational/omitted-variable gap (58% V3 share during a parts crisis, plus unmodeled rural travel distance and older devices, further distorted by slower maturity). Artificially equalizing predictions would strip rural Partner Z customers of needed proactive SLA interventions.
   * *Mitigation:* Run maturity-adjusted stratified audits and propensity reweighting (Diagnostic Test #6) before making fairness or segmentation decisions; collect and incorporate the missing domain covariates (rural travel distance, device age) so high-risk Partner Z cases receive accurate, actionable risk scores.

5. **Overreacting to a benign policy-driven process shift (June)**
   * *Failure mode:* Treating June’s drop in observed breach rate (0.180.15) and +0.05 ECE bump as model failure and triggering an emergency full retrain on only one month of post-policy data causes high variance and discards valuable Jan–Mar baseline signal, even though AUROC remained strong (0.79) and within-variant risk relationships did not break.
   * *Mitigation:* Link monitoring to an **Intervention/Policy Registry**; when control-flow mix shifts due to an intentional policy change while within-variant discrimination holds steady, respond proportionately by logging the policy/routing-intent feature and applying lightweight, monitored recalibration rather than an immediate full retrain.
