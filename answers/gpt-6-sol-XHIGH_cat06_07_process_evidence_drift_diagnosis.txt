The observed breach rate, mean score, AUROC, Brier, and ECE are calculated on available labels, whereas the variant breach rates are described as matured estimates. Their denominators need not match. In particular, a weighted average of the variant rates need not reproduce the reported monthly observed rate. A full-path variant and its elapsed edges are retrospective process evidence, not valid prediction features unless the relevant route or estimate was already known at `Initial Diagnosis Completed`.

## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

- **January–March:** Stable reference period: 100% label maturity, breach rates of 0.18–0.19, AUROC 0.81–0.82, low ECE, nearly unchanged variant shares, and low unknown/missing rates. Parts waiting time rises modestly from 28 to 35 h; that trend alone does not establish deterioration.
- **April:** Treat the sharp AUROC/Brier/ECE change and high mean score (0.28 versus observed 0.19) primarily as a schema and feature-lineage incident. Predictions could be made at the wrong event or omitted altogether, so even the evaluated population may be selected. The 18% unmapped paths are not trustworthy evidence of real routing change. Complete labels rule out label delay as the main cause. A real change could coexist, but it must be tested after replay.
- **May:** The mapping patch and backfill coincide with improved AUROC (0.78) and lower unknown/missing shares, supporting the April diagnosis. Metrics still trail the baseline and mean risk exceeds the observed rate by 0.03. The unrecomputed April cache and uneven deduplication leave a specific repair and audit task; they do not establish lasting concept drift.
- **June:** The remote policy raises V2 to 52% and lowers V3 to 18%. V2 remains quick and its matured breach rate is 0.09. A rough recomposition using earlier variant rates (V1 0.16, V2 0.10, V3 0.31) gives about 0.16 for June's mix, close to the observed 0.15. The changed mix plausibly explains much of the lower base rate and the model's 0.20 mean score; AUROC is still 0.79. This is genuine process/covariate drift, but not compelling evidence of a changed risk relationship within the same path.
- **July:** The supplier issue begins on 10 July; the parts edge reaches 65 h and matured V3 breaches reach 0.46. AUROC declines to 0.76. At 98% label maturity, censoring should still be checked, but the timing and path-specific evidence suggest the start of an operational risk change.
- **August:** Parts waiting time doubles from July to 130 h; V3 share reaches 35%, and its matured breach rate is 0.64. Observed breaches rise to 0.28 while mean predicted risk is only 0.19, with AUROC 0.68 and ECE 0.120. Both a larger high-risk path share and worse outcomes within that path matter. The latter is a plausible concept change relative to diagnosis-time features, subject to maturity and within-V3 mix checks.
- **September:** Supplier delay remains severe (148 h), V3 reaches 42%, and Partner Z adds a distinct rural/older-device population with more V3 cases. AUROC 0.66 and ECE 0.140 are consistent with overlapping supplier, route-mix, and population effects. The observed 0.31 and Z/non-Z gap are provisional because only 82% of labels are mature and Z matures more slowly. Do not assign all deterioration to either Partner Z or the supplier without stratification.
- **October:** The parts edge remains slow (155 h) and Partner Z remains substantial, so operational exposure persists. Yet only 55% of labels are mature; long paths are especially backlogged. The fall in *observed* breaches to 0.16 is therefore not evidence of recovery, and October's apparent calibration and discrimination cannot be compared directly with fully mature months. The low unknown-activity share also makes an April-sized schema failure unlikely on current evidence.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

| Test | Expected under one explanation | Expected under a competing explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Plot label-maturity curves from promised due date to label arrival, split by month, breach status, V3/other, and source. Re-evaluate every cohort at the same 30/60/90-day post-due-date vintage and again when complete, including prediction coverage. | **Selective label delay:** October's rate and metrics move substantially as long paths mature; earlier cohorts artificially cut to October's vintage show similar distortion. | **Real October recovery:** the improvement persists at equal vintage and after completion within comparable routes/sources. |
| Normalize both diagnosis labels, reconcile verified duplicate event IDs, rebuild the decision-point prefix and every derived feature from raw events, and replay April–May scoring. Compare the same case IDs and missing-prediction rates before/after. | **Pipeline failure:** April's unknown/unmapped/missing spikes collapse, score distribution and metrics recover materially, and previously omitted cases reappear. | **Real April process/concept change:** deterioration persists on the same eligible cases after a clean replay, supported by normalized path and edge evidence. |
| Plot reliability curves, calibration intercept/slope, ECE/Brier, AUROC, and case counts by month × variant × source, using equally mature labels; also standardize metrics to a fixed variant mix. | **Mix/covariate shift:** aggregate June or September changes shrink after standardization, while within-cell calibration/ranking is relatively stable. | **Concept drift:** within comparable V3/source cells, especially after July, risk and/or ranking remains worse. Sparse cells require uncertainty intervals. |
| Compare normalized variant shares, transition counts, and performance-DFG edge distributions by event-calendar and case-start cohort, with source breakdown and pre/post policy or supplier dates. | **Real process drift:** V2 rises after 1 June and the raw parts edge slows after 10 July across affected sources even after cleaning. | **Schema artifact:** a path discontinuity disappears with label normalization/deduplication, as expected for much of April. |
| Within retrospectively confirmed V3 cases, match or model on diagnosis-time features, due-date slack, calendar period, source, distance/device age when obtainable, and supplier exposure known at scoring; test month/supplier interactions and out-of-time residuals. | **Concept drift relative to the original inputs:** later V3 cases have higher breach risk at comparable original feature values, with an interaction or residual increase. | **Within-V3 covariate shift:** the rise disappears once case severity, geography, due-date slack, and known supplier signals are included or cases are matched. Future `Part Received` time must not enter the prediction-time feature set. |
| Reweight earlier/non-Z cases to September Z's diagnosis-time feature and intended-route distribution; compare standardized risk, outcomes, and calibration in overlap regions. Inspect slow-label selection separately. | **Population composition:** much of the Z/non-Z gap and aggregate loss is reproduced by the reweighted comparison. | **Source-specific conditional effect or unmeasured exposure:** a material residual remains among comparable, mature cases; poor overlap makes attribution inconclusive rather than proving bias. |
| Audit feature missingness, event-to-feature lineage, raw versus cached prefix counts, event IDs, diagnosis-to-score timing, model/schema versions, and outcome joins by source and date. | **Pipeline failure:** errors cluster around migration/patch dates or source, and corrected features change April scores and coverage. | **Genuine operational drift:** lineage and coverage remain sound while route/edge changes and mature outcome degradation persist. |

Use case-level bootstrap intervals or equivalent uncertainty estimates and report segment sample sizes. Also check that any outcome API repair preserves the same promised-date breach definition; the absence of a documented definition change is evidence against, not proof against, a target change.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

1. **Immediate containment:** Enforce diagnosis-label normalization and scoring-point checks; quarantine or abstain from automated decisions for prefixes with missing/contradictory features, and use a reviewed operational fallback. Restore outcome ingestion and track label coverage. Escalate parts delays operationally, since this can reduce breaches without waiting for a model change. Preserve original scores and versions for replay.
2. **Feature repair:** Recompute all affected April prefixes, queue and event-count features, and scores after verified deduplication; validate May and both source systems. Include cases that received no score in coverage reporting. Only then use April–May in evaluation or training. Build diagnosis-time route intent, supplier ETA/reliability, rural distance, and device age features where they are truly available at prediction time; exclude eventual path and realized parts wait to avoid leakage.
3. **Short-term recalibration:** With clean features and mature, representative labels, an intercept or modest slope adjustment can correct June's changed base rate if ranking within groups remains acceptable. Validate by later month and source before deployment. A new threshold can address operational capacity, but it is not a substitute for calibrated probabilities. August–September's lower AUROC and within-V3 deterioration cannot be repaired by recalibration alone; October cannot supply trustworthy calibration targets yet.
4. **Full retraining:** Once supplier-era and Partner Z cohorts have sufficiently mature outcomes and repaired lineage, compare an updated model against the repaired baseline with forward-time validation, source/variant calibration, coverage, and operational impact. Include the new diagnosis-time features and enough pre/post-change data to handle changing route mix. Promote only if it improves the affected segments without harming others.

For training now, January–March are the clean mature anchor. April is unsafe until full replay; May is mature but only conditionally usable after source/feature audit. June is mature and usable after that audit as a post-routing-policy cohort. July (98%), August (93%), and September (82%) require label completion or a validated censoring correction and sensitivity analysis; do not naively train on their available labels. October (55%) is unsafe for outcome-driven training or calibration. These labels may later become usable at a fixed mature horizon. Separate V3 or Partner Z models are justified only if the risk relationship still differs after clean features, matching and maturity adjustment, and there are enough stable cases for reliable validation. A shared model with route/source interactions or group-aware calibration is the first candidate; source membership alone should not stand in for the missing geography, device, and supplier information.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

Log one immutable record per eligible diagnosis decision: case and source IDs, event and ingestion times, promised date, score time, intended route if known, model/calibrator/schema versions, feature values and missingness, score, and eventual outcome with arrival time. Monitor scoring eligibility and coverage as well as performance so cases lost by a schema error do not silently disappear from AUROC. Maintain both event-calendar process views (what happened this month) and case-start cohorts (the complete, cutoff-limited trajectories of cases that began this month); use diagnosis-time cohorts for score evaluation.

- **Predictive and calibration:** AUROC, Brier, ECE, reliability curves, calibration intercept/slope, observed versus predicted risk, and score/coverage distributions by month, source, intended route, and retrospective variant. Compute outcome metrics only at stated maturity and show intervals and counts.
- **Label maturity:** Due-date-relative completion curves, label delay, unresolved fraction, API backlog, and maturity by source, path length, score band, and eventual outcome where known. Keep provisional and mature dashboards distinct.
- **Data quality:** Activity vocabulary and unknown share, duplicate/conflicting IDs, missing queue and new features, invalid timestamps, diagnosis-prefix alignment, cache-versus-recomputed checks, and raw-to-feature-to-score lineage by source/version.
- **Process and population:** Normalized variant frequencies and transition counts; performance-DFG distributions, especially `Order Part -> Part Received` and diagnosis-to-remote-fix; rural/device-age and source mix; overlap and segment sample size. Record policy, supplier, partner, schema, model, recalibrator, and API deployment dates on the same timeline, with pre/post and rollback checks.

Alert dependencies matter: a schema/coverage alarm triggers data repair and replay before interpreting a predictive alarm. A label-maturity alarm marks outcome metrics provisional and blocks automatic recalibration/retraining; it does **not** suppress independent raw-event alerts for a slow parts edge. A persistent mature-label calibration shift with stable ranking supports recalibration; persistent within-segment discrimination or conditional-risk loss after data repair supports retraining. Every intervention is followed by versioned, fixed-vintage checks.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

def monthly_process_evidence(path="field_service_events.csv", cutoff="2026-11-01"):
    # Expected columns: case_id, activity, timestamp; optional source, event_id.
    df = pd.read_csv(path)
    df["activity"] = df["activity"].replace({
        "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
    })
    df["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["timestamp"], utc=True).dt.tz_localize(None)
    df = df[df["timestamp"] < pd.Timestamp(cutoff)].copy()
    if "event_id" in df:
        key = (["source"] if "source" in df else []) + ["event_id"]
        has_id = df["event_id"].notna()
        conflict = (df.loc[has_id].groupby(key)[["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"]]
                    .nunique().gt(1).any(axis=1))
        if conflict.any():
            raise ValueError("Conflicting event IDs: inspect before deduplication")
        df = pd.concat([df.loc[has_id].drop_duplicates(key),
                        df.loc[~has_id].drop_duplicates()], ignore_index=True)
    else:
        df = df.drop_duplicates()  # Only exact copies; audit ambiguous repeats.
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity",
                                 timestamp_key="timestamp")
    case_col, act_col, time_col = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
    first = log.groupby(case_col)[time_col].min()

    def summarize(part):
        if part.empty:
            return {"events": 0, "cases": 0}
        dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(part)
        perf = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(part)  # Mean edge seconds by default.
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(part)
        temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(part)
        seq = part.sort_values([case_col, time_col], kind="mergesort").copy()
        seq["next_act"] = seq.groupby(case_col)[act_col].shift(-1)
        seq["next_time"] = seq.groupby(case_col)[time_col].shift(-1)
        edge = seq[(seq[act_col] == "Order Part") & (seq["next_act"] == "Part Received")]
        median_h = (edge["next_time"] - edge[time_col]).dt.total_seconds().div(3600).median()
        return {"events": len(part), "cases": part[case_col].nunique(),
                "unknown_share": (part[act_col] == "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean(),
                "dfg_counts": {str(k): int(v) for k, v in dfg.items()},
                "dfg_mean_seconds": {str(k): float(v) for k, v in perf.items()},
                "variant_counts": {str(k): int(v) for k, v in variants.items()},
                "parts_edge_median_hours": None if pd.isna(median_h) else float(median_h),
                "temporal_feature_columns": list(temporal.columns)}

    out = {}
    for month in pd.period_range("2026-01", "2026-10", freq="M"):
        lo, hi = month.start_time, (month + 1).start_time
        calendar = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            log, str(lo), str(hi - pd.Timedelta(microseconds=1)), mode="events")
        ids = first.index[(first >= lo) & (first < hi)]
        cohort = log[log[case_col].isin(ids)]  # Full observed traces through cutoff.
        out[str(month)] = {"event_calendar": summarize(calendar),
                           "case_start_cohort": summarize(cohort)}
    return out
```

The event-calendar view measures activity occurring in the month and can truncate cross-month edges. The case-start view follows the cases that began in the month through the cutoff, so recent cohorts have incomplete paths and edge times; neither view makes eventual V3 or realized parts delay available at diagnosis. Store the cutoff and cleaning version with these summaries, and rerun as more events arrive.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

| Wrong action or attribution | Failure mode | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Retrain on October's available outcomes as if complete | The API selects against long paths, teaching the model a falsely low breach rate and hiding V3 risk. | Hold outcome-driven updates until maturity; use fixed-vintage backtests and source/path-specific maturity curves. |
| Recalibrate around April's inflated scores | A calibration layer masks the broken scoring point and cached features while omitted cases remain invisible. | Repair and replay raw events and features first; require scoring-coverage and lineage gates. |
| Treat June's lower breach rate as proof of a new conditional relationship | Unnecessary retraining can sacrifice discrimination because the V2 policy mainly changed the path mix. | Standardize by variant and inspect within-path, mature-label risk before choosing recalibration or retraining. |
| Ignore the supplier-driven V3 change because aggregate scores can be recalibrated | Ranking and intervention priorities remain wrong for parts cases while breaches increase. | Escalate supplier operations, add diagnosis-time supplier signals, and retrain after matched-case and mature-label confirmation. |
| Attribute Partner Z's apparent gap directly to unfair model behavior or to partner identity | Slower labels, rural distance, older devices, and V3 mix can create an apparent or real gap through different mechanisms; a source-only fix may be ineffective or harmful. | Compare mature like-for-like cases, test transport overlap, add relevant diagnosis-time features, and assess calibrated error and intervention outcomes by source. |
| Dismiss October's slow parts edge because observed breaches fell | Selective label delay can hide continuing operational harm. | Keep process-edge alerts independent of outcome maturity and act on verified supplier delay while outcomes mature. |
