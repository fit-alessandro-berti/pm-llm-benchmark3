## 1. Executive memo

**Decision on Redesign R pilot: segment-gated scale, not national rollout, not rollback**

I recommend management proceed with a hybrid: scale Redesign R only for simple low-risk houses; suspend survey skipping and post-billing activation outside that segment; add conformance, permit, and coordinator-capacity controls; and run targeted measurement before any broader rollout.

R’s core hypothesis—fewer handovers and simpler flow improve speed—was confirmed for a specific segment. Simple low-risk houses improved materially: median duration fell 6.0 days, p90 fell 8.0 days, first-time-right rose 3 points, and complaints fell 1 point. Cases with 3+ handovers fell from 34% to 12%.

But R is not a global improvement. Overall p90 worsened from 31.0 to 38.5 days, p95 worsened from 45.0 to 62.0, first-time-right fell from 82% to 76%, complaints within 30 days rose from 6.1% to 9.8%, manual activation rose from 7% to 15%, and second visits rose from 11% to 18%. Visible cost improved, but estimated downstream-inclusive cost worsened from EUR 198 to EUR 207. The process-mining evidence explains why: pilot model precision fell to 0.51 from 0.79, and alignments show billing before successful activation, repeated installation visits without field survey, missing permit checks for older apartment buildings, and complaint escalation after billing.

National scale would amplify these risks. Rollback would discard the real gains for simple houses and coordinator ownership. We should therefore scale only the part that works, protect the segments that are harmed, and measure before broader rollout.

**Immediate actions:**

- Pause national scale-up of full Redesign R.
- Scale coordinator ownership and merged validate/schedule only for simple low-risk houses, with eligibility rules, conformance alerts, and coordinator load limits.
- Suspend survey skipping for medium-risk apartments, older/permit-sensitive buildings, business customers, and rural installs until the predictive rule is validated.
- Restore activation before billing unless a verified technical signal permits otherwise.
- Require permit-check conformance and billing-before-activation alerts.
- Start measurement: M1/M6 for survey-skip validation, M4 for 60-day downstream cost, M7/M3 for conformance and reason codes, and M2/M5 as supporting operational diagnostics.

**Threshold for broader rollout:** Demonstrate in a controlled population that non-simple segments have p90 no worse than baseline, first-time-right and complaints are non-inferior, 60-day downstream cost is no higher, billing-before-activation is eliminated, permit conformance is high, and the coordinator model performs acceptably under absence and high load. If those tests pass, scale R with segment controls nationally. If not, keep the hybrid and redesign the skip rule and activation policy.

## 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

**High fitness but low precision.**  
Fitness measures whether the discovered model can replay observed event sequences. Precision measures whether the model also permits sequences that did not occur. The pilot model has high fitness because the simplified coordinator path is common, but low precision because the event log contains many exception combinations. The model “fits” by becoming permissive: it allows skipped surveys, manual activation, second visits, and billing start in many unobserved orders. High fitness alone is not a sign of a well-controlled process; low precision is a warning that R increased process variation.

**Improved median but worse tail duration.**  
The median improved because many simple cases moved faster. But the tail grew because complex cases failed more often and took longer to repair. The distribution changed shape: faster mass in the middle, longer problematic tails at p90/p95. Aggregate median therefore hides the risk that R is making a minority of cases much worse.

**Fewer handovers but lower resilience.**  
Formal handovers fell, which reduced coordination friction and improved median speed. But handovers also distributed context across teams. Removing them concentrated context in coordinators and activation specialists. When coordinators were overloaded or absent, cases stalled because context was not shared. Thus the same change that reduced visible handovers also reduced the system’s redundancy and resilience.

**Lower visible cost but higher rework or downstream cost.**  
Visible cost before complaint handling improved because R skipped field surveys and reduced handovers in the short term. But those savings were partly shifted downstream: second visits rose, manual activation rose, complaints rose, and billing disputes appeared. Because the cost data exclude downstream complaint handling after week 4, the visible improvement is incomplete and likely understates true cost.

**Improvement for simple houses but deterioration for apartments/old buildings.**  
The simplified flow was appropriate for simple low-risk houses, where address validation and scheduling could be merged and survey skipping was less risky. For apartments and old buildings, the same shortcuts bypassed important permit and risk checks. This segment conflict is not a measurement nuisance; it is the central finding. A single process design is not appropriate across building-risk and customer segments.

## 6. Final recommendation

**Adopt decision E: segment-gated hybrid scale, with immediate controls and focused measurement.**

- **Immediate action:** Deploy Redesign R only for simple low-risk houses. For those cases, allow coordinator ownership and merged address validation/appointment scheduling. For medium-risk apartments, old permit-sensitive buildings, business customers, and rural installs, keep the baseline path with field survey and permit checks.
- **What to pause:** Pause national rollout of full R. Pause survey skipping for all segments except simple low-risk houses. Pause post-billing activation unless a verified technical signal permits it.
- **What to scale:** Scale the coordinator-ownership and simplified scheduling model for eligible simple low-risk houses, with eligibility rules, conformance monitoring, and coordinator utilization limits.
- **What to measure next:** Start M1 and M6 to validate the survey-skip rule. Start M4 and M3 to measure true downstream cost and rework reasons. Start M7 for conformance monitoring on billing-before-activation and permit absence. Use M2 and M5 to diagnose duration components and coordinator resilience.
- **Evidence threshold for broader rollout:** Broader rollout should require: non-simple segment p90 no worse than baseline or within an agreed tolerance; first-time-right and 30-day complaints non-inferior to baseline; 60-day downstream cost no higher; billing-before-activation eliminated or technically justified; permit-check conformance above an agreed threshold; and coordinator model performance acceptable under absence and high load. If these conditions are not met, keep the hybrid and revise the skip rule and activation policy rather than scaling nationally.
