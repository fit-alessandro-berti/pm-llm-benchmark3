# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

**Time origin and scale.** t = 0 at `Submit` (all 32 prefixes begin with `Submit` — observation). Time scale is elapsed hours. For terminal rows, `duration_or_age_h` is the exact time of the terminal event; for `open` rows it is the age at the frozen cutoff, i.e., a right-censoring time. **Assumption A1:** the SLA clock is wall-clock (no business-hours or pause rules supplied). **Assumption A2:** `duration_or_age_h` is exact, not interval-censored. Horizon  = 72 h.

**Events, censoring, and the three things people confuse.**
- *Already-observed breaches:* 16 cases deterministically fail "resolved by 72 h" (4 resolved late: S03, S13, S20, S28; 4 withdrawn; 3 rejected; 4 open past 72: S09, S11, S25, S26; 1 transferred past 72: S32 — still internal at 72 h, so unresolved at 72 h under every reading).
- *Competing events:* `Withdraw`/`Reject` (and `Transfer`, depending on scope) are terminal outcomes that preclude internal resolution. They are **events, not censoring**, for any absolute-risk estimand.
- *Right censoring:* 9 open cases (administrative, at cutoff age) and — under one scenario — transfers.

**Estimands** (each maps to a different decision):

| ID | Definition | Decision it serves |
|---|---|---|
| **E1** | P(T_res > 72): probability the case is **not internally resolved by 72 h** (withdraw/reject are non-resolution; transfer scenario-dependent) | SLA compliance reporting, target setting |
| **E2** | CIF_res()  CIF_res(72): resolves **eventually but late** | Capacity/bottleneck investment vs. demand-side fixes |
| **E3** | P(still internal **and** unresolved at exactly 72 h) — occupancy | Real-time alerting and staffing of the in-flight backlog |
| **E4** | Cause-specific CIFs at 72 h for withdraw / reject / transfer | Root-cause mix (abandonment vs. denial vs. handoff) |
| **E5** | RMST of resolution-free time over [0, 72] | Robust cohort contrast, insensitive to tail instability |

|---|---|---|---|---|
| S03 | **Event**: breach observed at 76 | In risk set at 72; contributes to CIF_res jump at 76, *not* to CIF_res(72) | **TRUE** (unresolved at 72, resolved later) | Resolve-hazard event at 76 |

# 2. Event and censoring encoding

| Status (n) | Time used | E1 role | AJ competing-risk code | Primary censoring rule | Sensitivity alternatives |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| resolved 72 (9) | duration | 0 (success) | event `resolve` | never censored | — |
| resolved >72 (4) | duration | 1 (known breach) | event `resolve` (late) | never censored | report separately from "hard" breaches (remediation differs) |
| withdrawn (4) | duration | 1 (deterministic breach) | event `withdraw` | not censored | (i) censor (KM-style independence — implausible, see §3); (ii) separate "abandonment" estimand; (iii) in-scope reading removes from denominator |
| rejected (3) | duration | 1 (deterministic breach) | event `reject` | not censored | split fast rejections (S05 9 h, S23 11 h — "timely disposition") from slow (S14 63 h) |
| transferred (3) | duration to `Transfer` | see T1–T3 | see T1–T3 | see T1–T3 | external-outcome data would define "resolved by 72 anywhere" |
| open, age <72 (5) | age at cutoff | **unknown** (NaN) | administrative censoring at age | censored at age | optimistic (all resolve 72  lower bound), pessimistic (none  upper bound), gap-based reclassification (stale  likely breach) |
| open, age 72 (4) | age | 1 (known breach) | censored at age | censored at age, flagged deterministic breach at 72 | for E2 tail: censoring is plausibly informative (stale cases) |

**Transfer under three assumptions:**
- **T1 — censor at transfer.** Assumes post-transfer resolution is independent of internal hazard and out of scope. Likely violated: all three transfers are escalated and end `Escalate>Transfer` (severity-driven exit). Inflates occupancy (transferred cases stay "at risk").
- **T2 — competing event `transfer` (primary).** Internal process ends; counts against internal resolution in E1's decomposition. Recommended default.
- **T3 — breach at transfer (internal-ownership SLA).** A case transferred before 72 h can never be internally resolved by 72 h  deterministic breach. Accountability-oriented; raises the E1 lower bound from 16/32 = 0.500 to 18/32 = 0.5625.
- Invariant: S32 (96 h) is a breach under all three.

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

- **Survivor bias.** Resolved-only means: cohort A  54.7 h (n=6), B  45.6 h (n=7) — "B is 9 h faster" [biased observation]. This conditions on resolution and discards 19/32 cases (59%), asymmetrically dropping the worst outcomes: S26 (open, 104 h) and S32 (transferred, 96 h) are excluded while their resolved analogues S13 (110 h) and S28 (88 h) are included.
- **Informative censoring.** All three transfers are escalated and end `Escalate>Transfer` — transfer probability depends on case severity, so T1 censoring is almost certainly informative. Stale open cases (gap: S26 72 h, S11 55 h, S25 48 h, S09 31 h) likely have near-zero resolution hazard (abandoned/lost); administrative censoring at cutoff is informative for them and for the tail of E2.
- **Competing risks.** S30 withdraws at 70 h — one observation-step before the horizon. Censoring it (KM-style) assumes it carried the average subsequent resolution hazard; abandonment at 70 h signals elevated dissatisfaction and *lower* resolution propensity, so KM overstates resolution. Same logic for S22 (wd@44), S14 (rej@63 — 63 h of capacity consumed then denied).
- **Cohort imbalance.** P1 share: A 3/15 (20%) vs. B 5/17 (29.4%); `AutoCheck`-second paths: 2/15 vs. 13/17. Cohort B began after a policy change but rollout was incomplete — AutoCheck appears in A (S01, S15) and 4 B cases lack it (S20, S23, S26, S30) — so cohort is a *period* proxy entangled with priority mix and compliance, not a clean treatment.
- **Open below vs. above 72 h.** S09/S11/S25/S26 (age > 72) are *deterministic* breaches — zero uncertainty. S15 (35 h, gap 2 h) and S31 (23 h, gap 3 h) are actively progressing and may resolve in time; S24 (67 h, gap 4 h) is borderline; S10 (47 h, gap 19 h) and S29 (51 h, escalated) lean breach. Collapsing all nine into one "censored" stratum discards deterministic information; the binary-at-72 estimand exploits it.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

| Method | What it gives here | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Kaplan–Meier (resolution-only) | P(not yet resolved), treating wd/rej/transfer as censored | **Descriptive contrast only** — P(resolved72) = 0.361 vs. AJ 0.304; the ~6 pp gap quantifies competing-risk distortion |
| **Aalen–Johansen CIF** | Cause-specific absolute risks; occupancy at 72 | **PRIMARY** (transfer = T2), reported with deterministic bounds |
| Cause-specific Cox (priority, cohort) | Hazard ratios per cause | Exploratory only; n = 32 with 13 events per cause cannot support stable adjustment — stratify, don't model |
| Fine–Gray subdistribution | Covariate effects on CIF | Defer until ~10× events; with n = 32 the weights make SEs unusable |
| RMST to 72 | ² S_res  58.8 h pooled | **Secondary** for cohort contrast — robust to the unstable tail (last event at 110 h, risk set n = 1) |
| Landmarking (24 h, 48 h) | Conditional breach risk among in-flight cases | **Secondary/operational** — aligns with alert triggers |
| Full multi-state model | Stage-level transition hazards | Design target; **blocked by missing event timestamps**. The feasible coarse 4-state version (in-process  resolve/withdraw/reject/transfer) *is* the AJ model |

**Justification (data-specific, not generic):** the decision is a decomposable absolute-risk question; 9/32 cases exit via competing events and 14/32 are censored before the horizon, so a single hazard curve misleads. AJ + bounds gives decision-aligned numbers with explicit assumptions; landmarking matches the operational moment (triage at 24–48 h); RMST survives the tail instability that makes lifetime CIFs (E2  0.30) extrapolative. Internal consistency check: under T2, E1 = E3 + CIF_wd + CIF_rej + CIF_tr = 0.380 + 0.140 + 0.101 + 0.075  0.696 .

**Headline numbers** [Estimates, pooled AJ-T2]: CIF_res(72) = 0.304; occupancy = 0.380; CIF_wd = 0.140; CIF_rej = 0.101; CIF_tr = 0.075; E1  0.696. **[Bounds]** E1  [0.500, 0.719] (T3 lower: 0.5625); E3  [0.281, 0.438]. At n = 32, sampling uncertainty is roughly ±8–15 pp on CIFs — another reason to lead with bounds.
