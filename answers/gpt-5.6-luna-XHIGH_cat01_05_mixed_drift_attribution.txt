## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The March raw/mapped fitness gap is diagnostic of a semantic and provenance problem: the old model sees `Eligibility Screen` as an unexpected activity and duplicated `Open Case` as extra behavior, whereas the mapping restores fitness from 0.55 to 0.90. It does not show that the business process changed by 0.35 fitness units, nor that every migration effect has been removed. Fitness is model- and representation-dependent, and a mapping can conceal a genuine ordering, routing, or timing change if the new label is behaviorally different despite being semantically equivalent.

Duplicated technical events inflate event counts and can create false directly-follows edges, extra variants, false loops, and misleading durations if timestamps are used naively. They should be de-duplicated for a specified technical-event rule, with the original retained for audit. Missing resources mostly invalidate team/resource attribution and can bias resource-stratified performance; they need not invalidate case-level activity ordering. If missingness is concentrated by migrated activity or team, it can also make observed staffing effects selective rather than merely noisy.

After May, normalized request-info rates rise from 12 to 24–29 per 100 cases, document-to-assessment progression falls, rework variants rise, and cycle-time tails lengthen. Those changes survive the supplied label mapping and align temporally with incomplete supplier bundles and capacity loss. Logging drift may distort their measurement, but it cannot by itself explain the coherent normalized DFG and performance movement.

### Normalized DFG distance

For the seven supplied normalized relation rates, let

\[
D_m=\frac{1}{7}\sum_{e=1}^{7}|r_{m,e}-r_{Jan,e}|,
\]

where rates are occurrences per 100 cases and the result is mean absolute percentage-point distance. January is the reference; February gives the same qualitative baseline. This is a transparent fragment distance, not a distance between complete stochastic process models.

| month | distance from January fragment (mean absolute pp) | main contributors |
|---|---:|---|
| Jan | 0.00 | Reference |
| Mar | 0.43 | Small residual assessment, senior-assessment, and decision changes |
| May | 5.71 | More request-info/rework and lower decision rate |
| Jul | 8.43 | Largest supplied rework and senior-assessment movement |
| Sep | 4.00 | Partial supplier/rework recovery |
| Oct | 9.14 | Senior-assessment rate rises from 4 to 22 per 100 |
| Dec | 5.14 | Partial intervention/seasonal recovery |

The distance assumes comparable case denominators, comparable edge definitions after mapping, and that each listed rate is measured consistently. Omitted edges, alternative paths, concurrency, and duration are treated as unobserved rather than zero. Consequently it can understate drift if a changed edge is omitted, and it cannot establish whether an edge-rate change is causal.

The data support multiple mechanism-specific change points, not one global break. A global change point would conflate representation, behavior, population, and queue dynamics and would be hard to interpret.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Event provenance | migration regime, source/system version, activity label, event type, schema version | Separate semantic changes and technical duplicates from business events | At least 500 events per provenance cell; pool rare labels into an audited `other` category | Raw versus canonical labels, duplicate rates, resource capture | Conditioning on a source can select particular teams or suppliers. |
| Case population | supplier (Z/other), region, complexity band, priority class, arrival month | Standardize case mix while retaining meaningful operational strata | At least 100 cases per month-by-stratum cell; otherwise pool adjacent months or use hierarchical partial pooling | Supplier, season, priority, and complexity effects | Complexity and priority may be affected by document quality or routing. |
| Capacity and ownership | assessor/team, staffing regime, resource completeness, queue | Attribute waiting and service changes to capacity and ownership | At least 50 cases and 200 events per team-regime cell; suppress individual cells below that | Pre/post staffing, team comparisons, missingness sensitivity | Assignment is endogenous to workload and priority. |
| Process phenotype | canonical fast, rework, senior-assessment, and other variant families | Monitor behavior without exploding into tiny exact variants | Keep families with at least 2% of monthly cases and 30 cases; retain rare variants in a residual bucket | Variant shares, DFG distance, conformance | Phenotype may be an outcome of the mechanism being studied. |
| Time regime | month, month-of-year, pre/post intervention, arrival and completion cohort | Distinguish trend, recurrence, backlog carryover, and intervention | At least 3 months per seasonal reference where possible; use rolling windows otherwise | Seasonal versus novel drift, change-point checks | Time is confounded with all changes in the timeline. |
| Outcome cohort | approved/rejected, closed/censored, SLA status, abandonment | Check whether drift changes decisions or merely delay | At least 100 completed outcomes per comparison; report censoring separately | Outcome stability, fairness, and service-level effects | Conditioning on completion can hide long-running cases and selection effects. |

The primary monitoring population should be all registered cases with a valid arrival timestamp, followed longitudinally regardless of whether they close during the window. Report overall measures alongside risk-standardized estimates using supplier, complexity, priority, and season strata; do not make a completed-only population the primary one. This preserves the operational queue signal while preventing case mix from dominating comparisons. Small cells should be pooled or modeled hierarchically, not silently discarded.

Thresholds should be learned per monitor and stratum from historical control limits or bootstrap intervals, with a minimum-support gate. Seasonality should use month-of-year references or seasonal regression and should distinguish arrival from completion month. For the many simultaneous monitors, use a two-stage triage: require persistence or corroboration across related monitors, and control the false-discovery rate within monitor families (for example, Benjamini-Hochberg), while escalating high-severity integrity violations immediately. December should be evaluated against both the renewal-season baseline and a no-intervention counterfactual; it is not a clean universal baseline.

    "month", "raw_model_fitness", "mapped_model_fitness", "fitness_gain",
    "fraction_gap_recovered"]].round(3).to_string(index=False))

# 5. Explainable screening rule, not a formal change-point test. A month is
# flagged when any robust-looking aggregate signal crosses its chosen scale.
joined = monthly.merge(dfg[["month", "dfg_distance_pp"]], on="month", how="left")
joined["candidate_change_point"] = (
    joined["raw_model_fitness"].diff().abs().ge(.15) |
    joined["mapped_model_fitness"].diff().abs().ge(.02) |
    joined["dfg_distance_pp"].fillna(0).ge(3.0) |
    joined["median_cycle_h"].diff().abs().ge(4.0) |
    joined["rework_variant_pct"].diff().abs().ge(4.0)
)
print("Candidate change-point months:\n", joined.loc[
    joined["candidate_change_point"], ["month", "dfg_distance_pp",
    "mapped_model_fitness", "median_cycle_h", "rework_variant_pct"]
].to_string(index=False))
print("Caveat: the DFG distance covers only the seven supplied edges; omitted "

import pm4py

def format_event_log(event_df, case_col="case_id", activity_col="activity",
                     timestamp_col="timestamp"):
    """Return a PM4Py-formatted copy using PM4Py's standard attribute keys."""
    required = {case_col, activity_col, timestamp_col}
    missing = required.difference(event_df.columns)
    if missing:
        raise ValueError(f"missing required columns: {sorted(missing)}")
    copied = event_df.copy(deep=True).rename(columns={
        case_col: "case:concept:name",
        activity_col: "concept:name",
        timestamp_col: "time:timestamp",
    })
    copied["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(copied["time:timestamp"],
                                                utc=True, errors="raise")
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(
        copied, case_id="case:concept:name", activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp")

def slice_time_window(formatted_log, start_datetime, end_datetime):
    start = pd.Timestamp(start_datetime).to_pydatetime()
    end = pd.Timestamp(end_datetime).to_pydatetime()
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(formatted_log, start, end)

def monthly_process_artifacts(formatted_log, start_datetime, end_datetime):
    window = slice_time_window(formatted_log, start_datetime, end_datetime)
    return {
        "dfg": pm4py.discover_dfg(window),
        "variants": pm4py.get_variants(window),
        "temporal_profile": pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(window),
    }

def monthly_dfgs(formatted_log, month_starts):
    """Discover one DFG per [month_start, next_month_start) window."""
    result = {}
    starts = [pd.Timestamp(x, tz="UTC") for x in month_starts]
    for start, end in zip(starts, starts[1:]):
        window = slice_time_window(formatted_log, start, end)
        result[start.strftime("%Y-%m")] = pm4py.discover_dfg(window)
    return result

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest conclusion is that March contains a logging/semantic break. The migration date, the appearance of `Eligibility Screen`, the extra technical `Open Case`, the rise from nine to eleven labels, and the recovery from raw fitness near 0.55 to mapped fitness near 0.90 form a coherent signature. Resource missingness is also strongly attributable to migration-related provenance changes, although its concentration by activity and team means it is informative missingness rather than harmless noise.

The post-May rework change is most plausibly supplier-driven with high, but not complete, confidence. Supplier Z rises from roughly 14.5% to 43–46%, and normalized request-info and return-to-collection rates approximately double or more. The supplier’s non-random complexity means the effect cannot be read as a simple supplier treatment effect. Its effect is likely mediated by staffing: incomplete bundles create additional assessment demand just as four assessors leave, producing queueing and a much larger p90 increase than the modest Assess service-time change supports.

Staffing-driven queueing is therefore a high-confidence contributor to performance deterioration, but its exact share is uncertain. The July priority rule plausibly redistributed waiting from high-complexity or priority cases toward standard cases; the aggregate cannot establish that direction. October-November deterioration is strongly seasonal in population terms: volume and high-complexity share rise sharply, and senior-assessment use follows. Seasonality amplifies, rather than fully explains, the earlier rework and backlog signals.

Persistent behavioral adaptation after Supplier Z’s reported recovery is plausible but lower confidence. Mapped fitness, rework, DFG distance, and missingness do not return to baseline, but residual backlog, renewal season, staffing, and the December interventions provide competing explanations. December improvement cannot be attributed separately to overtime or document-quality work because load also falls.

I would revise this account if matched supplier/non-supplier strata showed no document-quality or rework difference; if team-level queue waiting did not change after June 15; if priority-stratified waits did not redistribute after July 1; or if canonical event-level ordering and durations remained stable after controlling for case mix and backlog. Conversely, replicated seasonal patterns, clean source audits, and event-level interrupted analyses would strengthen the decomposition.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. `IF an unapproved activity label or schema version appears, THEN quarantine it from canonical conformance and open a provenance review, BECAUSE a semantic change can create artificial fitness and DFG drift.`
2. `IF duplicate technical-event rate exceeds its source-specific control limit for two consecutive windows, THEN deduplicate an analysis copy and investigate producer retries, BECAUSE technical duplicates create false loops and variants without proving business rework.`
3. `IF normalized request-info and return-to-collection rates rise within the same supplier-complexity strata, THEN inspect document completeness and supplier operations, BECAUSE a within-stratum loop increase is evidence for supplier-driven behavioral drift.`
4. `IF queue waiting rises while active Assess service time remains near its team-specific baseline, THEN rebalance capacity and inspect WIP by priority, BECAUSE staffing and routing pressure are more plausible than slower task execution.`
5. `IF high-priority waiting falls while standard-case waiting or backlog rises after a priority-policy change, THEN review the policy’s fairness and SLA impact, BECAUSE priority can redistribute delay rather than remove it.`
6. `IF case-mix shares leave the month-of-year envelope but conditional process metrics remain stable, THEN risk-standardize reported drift and investigate intake composition, BECAUSE population drift can mimic behavioral drift in aggregates.`
7. `IF December-like recovery occurs only after both load falls and interventions start, THEN require a seasonally matched or exposed-versus-unexposed evaluation before crediting the intervention, BECAUSE the two causes are mediated and not separately identified.`
