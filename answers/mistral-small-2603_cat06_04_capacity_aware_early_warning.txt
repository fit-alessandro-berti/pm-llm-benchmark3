## 1. Decision rule

**Eligibility and prioritization rule:**
A case is eligible for intervention if:
- It is in **Triage** or **Diagnosis Started** stage (intervention useless after Diagnosis Complete).
- The **alert history** shows either:
  - First alert, **or**
  - Repeated alert **with new evidence** (e.g., new diagnostic log, updated queue data), **and** the risk increase from `p_prev` to `p_now` is  0.05 (to filter noise and repeated alerts without signal).
- **No prior swarm** has been applied to this case (repeated swarms forbidden unless new event occurs).

**Probability adjustment for calibration bias:**
- **SME segment**: Increase `p_now` by 0.065 (midpoint of 0.04–0.09 underprediction).
- **Strategic Enterprise**: Decrease `p_now` by 0.085 (midpoint of 0.05–0.12 overprediction).
- **Public Sector**: Use `p_now` as-is (calibration acceptable).
- **Non-Native-Language Support**: Increase `p_now` by 0.10 (to account for uncertainty and potential underestimation).

**Priority scoring:**
For eligible cases, compute:
```
Priority Score = (Adjusted p_now) × (Value of avoiding breach)  False-alarm burden  (0.02 × Spillover risk proxy)
```
where Spillover risk proxy = number of untreated diagnosis-stage cases × 0.02 (midpoint spillover per untreated case).

Cases are tiered:
- **Tier 1 (High priority)**: Score  €5,000
- **Tier 2 (Medium priority)**: Score  €2,000
- **Tier 3 (Low priority)**: Score < €2,000

Only Tier 1 and Tier 2 cases are considered for intervention. Tier 3 cases are deprioritized unless no higher-tier cases exist.

**Arithmetic:**
- Direct breaches avoided = sum of (risk reduction × intervention effect) for selected cases.

- Net benefit = direct benefit  intervention cost  spillover harm.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**Alert persistence and re-entry logic:**
- **Cool-down period:** 4 hours after a non-intervened alert before a repeated alert can be considered for intervention.
- **Suppression:** If a case triggers repeated alerts **without new evidence**, suppress further alerts for 24 hours (to prevent alert fatigue).
- **Escalation:** If a case triggers a third alert **with new evidence**, escalate to Tier 1 priority regardless of score.
- **Re-entry:** After intervention, a case may re-enter the pool only after:
  - The case is closed, **or**
  - 7 days have passed and no new alerts have occurred.

**Comparison of I-05 and I-07:**
- **I-05**: Third alert, **no new event** since first alert.  Suppress further alerts for 24 hours. No intervention.
- **I-07**: Third alert, **new customer log uploaded**  New evidence. Escalate to Tier 1. Re-entry logic applies post-intervention.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Risks and safeguards:**

| Risk | Safeguard |
|---|---|
| **Value-based prioritization starving lower-value cases** | Set a minimum quota: reserve 1 slot per day for cases with value < €10k, if no higher-value cases meet eligibility criteria. |
| **Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support (I-09)** | Require mandatory second review by a language-support specialist before intervention. Do not intervene unless risk reduction  0.25 (upper half of range). |
| **Differential calibration across segments** | Apply segment-specific bias corrections (as in Section 1). Monitor calibration by segment weekly. |
| **Selective intervention changing future training labels** | Track all intervened cases for 30 days post-intervention. If intervention changes diagnosis path, flag for model retraining. Use synthetic controls to estimate causal effect. |
| **Spillover harms falling on untreated groups** | Monitor untreated diagnosis-stage cases for SLA breaches weekly. If spillover harm exceeds €500 per week, reduce slot allocation or implement queue balancing (e.g., prioritize untreated cases in next allocation). |

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational monitoring:**
- Track daily: number of alerts, interventions, breaches, and spillover breaches.
- Monitor queue delays: aggregate delay per slot used (target:  20 minutes per slot).

**Predictive monitoring:**
- Weekly calibration check by segment (compare predicted vs. actual breach rates).
- Flag segments where calibration error exceeds 0.10.

**Causal-effect monitoring:**
- Use synthetic control or difference-in-differences to estimate intervention effect post-hoc.
- Compare predicted risk reduction (observational) vs. actual breach reduction (causal).

**Capacity monitoring:**
- Track unused slots and regulatory incident arrivals. Adjust reserve policy if incidents exceed 1 per week.

**Fairness monitoring:**
- Monthly fairness audit: compare breach rates and intervention rates by segment. Investigate disparities > 5%.
- Publish fairness metrics internally.

**Recalibration triggers:**
- If calibration error for any segment exceeds 0.10 for two consecutive weeks  adjust bias correction.
- If spillover harm exceeds €1,000 per week  reduce slot allocation by 1.
- If intervention effect estimate (causal) is < 50% of observational prediction for two consecutive months  revise risk-reduction ranges or intervention policy.

**Decision to change threshold:**
- If net benefit (Base scenario) drops below €10k per week  reduce slot allocation or tighten eligibility criteria.
- If false-alarm burden exceeds €2,000 per week  increase false-alarm burden penalty in priority score.
