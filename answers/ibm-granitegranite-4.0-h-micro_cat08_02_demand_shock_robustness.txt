# Stress Scenarios

## 1. Demand Mix Shift
**Scenario Description:** An unexpected surge in Urgent and Complex-panel cases occurs, possibly due to a sudden outbreak of a specific disease. This shifts the case mix significantly from the normal 61%/18%/13%/8% mix to approximately 70%/15%/12%/3%.
**Impact:** Urgent cases may encounter bottlenecks due to increased volume. Complex-panel cases, requiring more resources, may face delays in medical validation and specialist review, leading to extended cycle times.

## 2. Assay-Machine Degradation
**Scenario Description:** Critical assay machines (M1/M2) experience degradation or frequent breakdowns, leading to reduced availability and increased runtime.
**Impact:** Batch wait times at preparation benches increase, particularly for Urgent and Complex-panel cases. Technical validation and repeat assays may become bottlenecked, as machines are offline for maintenance or repairs.

## 3. Specialist Validator Absence
**Scenario Description:** A key medical validator or specialist review specialist is unexpectedly unavailable, either due to illness, training, or leave.
**Impact:** Medical validation times extend significantly, affecting Routine and Complex-panel cases. The Hold for specialist review step becomes a major bottleneck, especially during peak periods.

## 4. Courier Arrival Compression
**Scenario Description:** Courier delays are compressed into a shorter time window, typically between 10:00 and 17:00, rather than being spread evenly throughout the day.
**Impact:** Courier intake utilization spikes, causing delays in receiving samples. This can lead to increased queue lengths at the Pre-analytical check and Test preparation stages, especially during peak demand periods.

# Bottleneck-Migration Analysis

## Scenario 1: Demand Mix Shift
- **Initial Bottleneck:** Pre-analytical check (74% utilization, 9.5h median queue)
- **Migration:** As Urgent and Complex-panel cases increase, the Pre-analytical check queue lengthens, causing delays in Test preparation. This leads to more Request recollection cycles, further straining the Manual accession correction process.
- **Relieving Station:** Increasing the capacity of the Pre-analytical check (e.g., adding clerks or automation) can help. However, this may overload the Manual accession correction specialists, leading to potential errors and further delays.

## Scenario 2: Assay-Machine Degradation
- **Initial Bottleneck:** Assay machines (M1/M2) (98% utilization, 18h median queue)
- **Migration:** Batch wait times at preparation benches increase, particularly for Urgent and Complex-panel cases. Repeat assays become more frequent, further straining assay machines and increasing workload on technical validators.
- **Relieving Station:** Redistributing workload to M2 and implementing preventive maintenance can help. However, if M2 experiences higher failure rates, it may become the new bottleneck, necessitating additional resources or repairs.

## Scenario 3: Specialist Validator Absence
- **Initial Bottleneck:** Medical validation (58% utilization, 22h median queue)
- **Migration:** The absence of a specialist validator creates a significant bottleneck in the Medical validation step, especially for Urgent and Complex-panel cases. This leads to longer cycle times and higher rework rates.
- **Relieving Station:** Cross-training accession correction specialists to handle medical validation can alleviate pressure. However, this shift may increase the risk of errors, impacting overall accuracy and potentially leading to additional rework.

## Scenario 4: Courier Arrival Compression
- **Initial Bottleneck:** Courier intake (60% utilization, 7.5h median queue)
- **Migration:** Courier delays compress into a shorter time window, causing a spike in the number of samples received at the start of the day. This leads to longer queues at the Pre-analytical check and Test preparation stages.
- **Relieving Station:** Adjusting courier pickup slots to spread arrivals more evenly can mitigate this issue. However, if courier delays persist, the Pre-analytical check remains a critical bottleneck, requiring additional staffing or automation.

# Robust Redesign Package

## Interventions
### I1. Urgent-First Dispatch Rule
**Robustness:** Helps prioritize urgent cases, reducing their cycle time. However, it may starve Routine and Complex-panel cases if not carefully managed.
**Assumptions:** Efficient scheduling and buffer management. May fail if not monitored closely, leading to overloading of preparation benches.
### I2. Increase Batch Sizes
**Robustness:** Improves preparation efficiency for Routine cases, reducing batch wait times. However, it may push Urgent cases into longer waits if not controlled.
**Assumptions:** Adequate resources for handling larger batches without compromising quality. May fail if not balanced with staffing and equipment capacity.
### I3. Dedicated Assay Machines
**Robustness:** Reduces cycle times for Critical cases by allocating M1 to Urgent and Complex-panel cases. However, it may increase repeats if M2 is overwhelmed with Routine cases.
**Assumptions:** Effective workload balancing and machine maintenance. May fail if M2 experiences higher failure rates or if Routine cases are not properly prioritized.
### I4. Cross-Training for Accession Correction
**Robustness:** Alleviates pressure on medical validators by handling accession corrections. However, it may create a bottleneck in technical validation if not managed properly.
**Assumptions:** Effective training and supervision of accession correction specialists. May fail if the quality of corrections is compromised, leading to increased rework.
### I5. Evening Medical-Validation Shift
**Robustness:** Provides additional capacity during peak periods, reducing medical validation bottlenecks. However, it may increase costs and require careful scheduling.
**Assumptions:** Availability of qualified medical validators and shift coordination. May fail if validators are not adequately rested, affecting quality and safety.
### I6. Pre-Analytical Defect Triage
**Robustness:** Reduces the number of samples requiring correction, lowering the load on the Pre-analytical check and Test preparation stages. However, it may increase initial screening errors.
**Assumptions:** Accurate triage criteria and sufficient resources for immediate retesting of rejected samples. May fail if triage criteria are too strict, leading to unnecessary rework.
### I7. Courier Pickup Shifts
**Robustness:** Smoothes courier arrival peaks, reducing strain on the Pre-analytical check and Test preparation stages. However, it requires coordination with couriers and may not fully address demand shifts.
**Assumptions:** Reliable courier cooperation and scheduling flexibility. May fail if courier availability is limited or if demand continues to surge.
### I8. Digital Registration Automation
**Robustness:** Reduces manual errors and speeds up registration, improving overall efficiency. However, it requires initial investment and training.
**Assumptions:** Effective integration with existing systems and staff training. May fail if the digital system experiences technical issues or if staff resistance is high.
### I9. Routine Release Batching
**Robustness:** Reduces release work for Routine cases, improving efficiency. However, it may increase waiting times if not properly managed.
**Assumptions:** Coordinated batch preparation and release schedules. May fail if Routine cases are not properly batched, leading to delays.
### I10. Partner Lab Overflow
**Robustness:** Relieves specialist review bottlenecks, reducing cycle times for Complex-panel cases. However, it adds transport delay and complexity in traceability.
**Assumptions:** Reliable partner lab capacity and efficient transfer processes. May fail if transport delays exceed 12h or if communication with the partner lab is compromised.

# Scenario-Specific Policies

## Policy for Demand Mix Shift
**Trigger Condition:** Case mix shift >10% toward Urgent and Complex-panel cases.
**Policy:** Implement I1 (Urgent-First Dispatch Rule) and I6 (Pre-Analytical Defect Triage) to prioritize urgent cases and reduce defects early in the process.

## Policy for Assay-Machine Degradation
**Trigger Condition:** Machine utilization >95% for >2 consecutive days or machine failure rate >5%.
**Policy:** Activate I3 (Dedicated Assay Machines) and I4 (Cross-Training for Accession Correction) to increase efficiency and reduce bottlenecks.

## Policy for Specialist Validator Absence
**Trigger Condition:** No medical validator available for >4 consecutive hours during peak periods.
**Policy:** Implement I4 (Cross-Training for Accession Correction) and I5 (Evening Medical-Validation Shift) to alleviate the bottleneck and maintain service levels.

## Policy for Courier Arrival Compression
**Trigger Condition:** Courier intake utilization >90% for >3 consecutive days.
**Policy:** Implement I7 (Courier Pickup Shifts) to spread arrivals more evenly and reduce strain on the Pre-analytical check and Test preparation stages.

# Residual Vulnerabilities

1. **Assay-Machine Reliability:** Despite interventions like I3 and I4, machine degradation and failure rates may remain a risk, particularly if preventive maintenance is not adequately scheduled.
2. **Specialist Validator Variability:** Even with cross-training (I4) and evening shifts (I5), the variability and unpredictability of specialist validator availability may still pose a challenge.
3. **Courier Dependence:** Relying on I7 to mitigate courier delays assumes consistent courier performance and may not fully address demand shifts caused by external factors.
4. **Digital System Limitations:** I8, while beneficial, may encounter technical issues or resistance, leading to delays in registration and pre-analytical processes.

# Minimum Monitoring Dashboard

## Leading Indicators
- **Urgent Case Cycle Time:** Monitor median and p90 cycle times for urgent cases. Threshold: >18h indicates bottleneck.
- **Pre-analytical Check Queue Length:** Track queue length in hours. Threshold: >9h during peak periods signals a bottleneck.
- **Assay Machine Utilization:** Monitor utilization percentage. Threshold: >95% for >2 consecutive days indicates a risk of degradation.
- **Medical Validator Availability:** Track the number of available validators. Threshold: <50% availability during peak periods indicates a risk.
- **Courier Intake Utilization:** Monitor utilization percentage. Threshold: >90% indicates courier delays.

## Lagging Outcome Metrics
- **Rework Rate:** Monitor overall rework percentage. Threshold: >8% indicates quality issues.
- **Release Result Cycle Time:** Track the overall cycle time for releasing results. Threshold: >72h for Routine cases indicates inefficiency.

These indicators will help in real-time monitoring and prompt corrective actions during peak periods, ensuring the process remains robust and efficient under varying demand and disruption scenarios.
