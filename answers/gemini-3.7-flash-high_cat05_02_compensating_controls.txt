## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

The nominal Four-Eyes Principle (Rule FEP-1) requires that distinct human actors perform `Prepare_Payment` and `Release_Payment`. FEP-1 is an architectural segregation-of-duties mechanism designed to achieve underlying internal control objectives. Decomposing FEP-1 reveals five core threat scenarios and how the compensating controls (AV-2, MG-4, RA-7, and ED-1) interact with each:

### 1. Internal Fraud and Embezzlement (Unauthorized Account Redirection)
* **Threat Mechanism:** An internal operator manipulates payment instructions to disburse funds to an employee-controlled, associate, or shell bank account.
* **AV-2 (Automated Validation):** *Preventive.* Strongly addresses this threat if the fraudulent account was recently added or altered (checks 7-day bank-change history, account ownership, and sanctions lists). *Failure Mode:* Fails if the fraudulent account was aged over seven days or if an operator limit/override warning is bypassed without independent verification.
* **MG-4 (Independent Manager Approval):** *Preventive/Detective.* Highly effective when executed before release or settlement by an independent manager (L3+) who cross-examines invoice details against original contracts. *Failure Mode:* Fails if the manager rubber-stamps without inspecting banking records or has a conflict of interest/shared credentials.
* **RA-7 (Retrospective Audit and Recall):** *Detective/Corrective.* Detects redirection upon ledger and bank statement reconciliation. *Failure Mode:* Purely detective if executed after the 24-hour bank recall window; cannot correct loss once funds are withdrawn from the recipient account.
* **ED-1 (Emergency Permission Package):** *Preventive & Detective Bundle.* Mitigates threat by bounding exposure (amount < EUR 100k, 180-day vendor tenure, no bank change in 7 days) and mandating independent MG-4 approval before settlement plus 1-day RA-7 audit.

### 2. External Social Engineering and Vendor Impersonation (CEO / Supplier Fraud)
* **Threat Mechanism:** External attackers submit falsified payment requests or compromised bank details purporting to be a legitimate vendor.
* **AV-2:** *Preventive.* Flags newly modified bank details (< 7 days) and mismatches in vendor registration files. *Failure Mode:* Blind to sophisticated man-in-the-middle attacks where master vendor data was manipulated upstream outside the 7-day window.
* **MG-4:** *Preventive.* Provides an independent qualitative check, verifying external callback logs or purchase order authorization. *Failure Mode:* Weak if the manager assumes AV-2 pass implies physical verification of supplier identity.
* **RA-7:** *Detective/Corrective.* Uncovers the fraud when the legitimate vendor sends overdue payment notices. *Failure Mode:* Invariably too late for bank recall (typically discovered weeks later).
* **ED-1:** *Preventive Policy Gate.* Strictly excludes new or recently modified bank accounts (< 7 days) and requires established vendor tenure (>= 180 days), shutting down typical impersonation vectors.

### 3. Operational and Data-Entry Errors (Duplicates, Digit Transposition, Wrong Payee)
* **Threat Mechanism:** Operator enters incorrect invoice amounts, transposes banking digits, or processes duplicate invoices.
* **AV-2:** *Preventive.* Highly effective; deterministically catches duplicate invoice numbers, syntax/checksum errors in IBANs, and amount mismatches against purchase orders.
* **MG-4:** *Preventive/Detective.* Moderately effective at spotting gross valuation errors, but humans are less reliable than automated checksums for digit transpositions.
* **RA-7:** *Detective/Corrective.* Identifies duplicate disbursements during bank reconciliation; high probability of recovery from established vendors via credit netting.
* **ED-1:** *Preventive Gate.* Requires full AV-2 pass without override, ensuring duplicate/syntax traps remain active.

### 4. Fictitious Services and Invoicing Without Performance (Goods Not Received)
* **Threat Mechanism:** Payment is prepared and released for services/goods that were never delivered, often involving collusion between vendor and purchasing staff.
* **AV-2:** *Fails Entirely.* Explicitly blind to physical receipt of goods, milestone completion for consulting, or commercial reasonableness.
* **MG-4:** *Preventive.* Primary defense line; manager must verify three-way match (PO, invoice, receiving slip/service delivery sign-off) and assess commercial reasonableness.
* **RA-7:** *Detective.* Samples transactions to verify physical delivery documentation post-payment; triggers vendor dispute or legal recovery.
* **ED-1:** *Partially Mitigates.* Limits exposure by capping amount (< EUR 100k) and requiring pre-settlement manager verification of supporting documentation.

### 5. Policy Circumvention and Authorization Limit Evasion
* **Threat Mechanism:** Operators split large payments or bypass operational spend thresholds using emergency workflows during non-emergencies.
* **AV-2:** *Preventive.* Flags transactions exceeding individual operator limits. *Failure Mode:* Cannot detect split payments across multiple smaller vouchers without aggregate batch rules.
* **MG-4:** *Preventive.* Enforces organizational hierarchy and budget limits before payment execution.
* **RA-7:** *Detective.* Uncovers systemic patterns of threshold evasion and policy abuse.
* **ED-1:** *Preventive Governance.* Enforces strict hard limits (EUR 100k cap, active emergency token) and eliminates discretion.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A compensating control package is an alternative risk-treatment architecture deployed when a primary baseline control (FEP-1) cannot be maintained. The sufficiency of a compensating control package must be judged by its ability to achieve parity with the primary control's underlying risk-reduction objectives across seven critical dimensions.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                 EQUIVALENCE AND SUFFICIENCY SPECTRUM                              |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| FULLY EQUIVALENT     | Independent pre-settlement gating + clean automated validation + timely     |
|                      | audit under valid emergency conditions (e.g., compliant ED-1). Zero delta   |
|                      | in unmitigated loss exposure.                                               |
+----------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| PARTIALLY EQUIVALENT | Minor procedural deviations (e.g., token timing delay, low-value post-      |
|                      | release / pre-settlement ratification) where independent review and bank    |
|                      | recall window remain intact. Residual risk is negligible/tolerable.         |
+----------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| MERELY MITIGATING    | Provides partial screening (e.g., AV-2 pass only) but lacks independent     |
|                      | human oversight or leaves critical dimensions unverified (e.g., receipt).   |
+----------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| INEFFECTIVE          | Complete control failure: credential misuse, conflicted manager, post-      |
|                      | settlement rubber-stamping, unverified bank changes, or active fraud.       |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### The Seven-Dimensional Sufficiency Criteria

1. **Timing (Ex-Ante vs. Ex-Post):**
   * *Ex-ante (Pre-Release):* Strictly preventive; stops fraudulent or erroneous messages before transmission to the banking network.
   * *Intermediary (Post-Release, Pre-Settlement):* Hybrid gatekeeper; transaction is transmitted to the bank queue but can be cancelled/recalled before irrevocable clearing.
   * *Ex-post (Post-Settlement):* Purely detective; relies on uncertainty of legal/commercial recovery.
2. **Independence:**
   * Reviewers must possess structural, organizational, and operational independence (minimum L3 grade, outside direct reporting hierarchy, no shared performance incentives or personal ties with the operator).
3. **Coverage:**
   * Controls must collectively verify mathematical accuracy, duplicate status, bank account provenance (< 7-day changes), vendor legitimacy, authorization thresholds, and substantive proof of performance (goods receipt).
4. **Overrideability and Tamper Resistance:**
   * Automated warnings (AV-2) must not be dismissible by the operator initiating the payment. Managerial override credentials must be cryptographically bound, logged, and non-delegable.
5. **Recoverability and Loss Capping:**
   * If an ex-post control is relied upon, execution must fall strictly within the guaranteed bank recall window ($\le 24$ hours). Total value must be bounded by policy limits (e.g., < EUR 100,000).
6. **Evidence Quality:**
   * Independent approval requires contemporaneous examination of underlying primary artifacts (invoices, receiving reports, bank change callback sheets) rather than passive, unevidenced sign-offs.
7. **Collusion and Shared-Resource Resistance:**
   * Segregation must exist in physical and logical reality, preventing shared workstations, shared generic accounts, or persistent static pairing without rotation.

### Why "Same Expected Loss" is an Insufficient Standard

Evaluating compensating controls purely by mathematical Expected Loss ($\mathbb{E}[L] = P(\text{Defect}) \times \text{Exposure}$) is fundamentally flawed in internal control design for four reasons:

1. **Tail Risk and Catastrophic Asymmetry:** A control system with low expected loss but high tail exposure can lead to catastrophic insolvency (e.g., a single unrecovered EUR 900k fraudulent payment). Internal control frameworks (COSO, SOX 404) require reasonable assurance against material misstatements and tail events, not merely acceptable risk averages.
2. **Moral Hazard and Adversarial Adaptation:** Expected loss calculations assume static failure probabilities. If employees recognize that detective compensating controls replace preventive four-eyes checks, deterrence erodes. Adversaries actively probe and exploit deterministic gaps, exponentially increasing attack frequency.
3. **Statutory and Regulatory Design Requirements:** Financial reporting standards (e.g., Sarbanes-Oxley §404, ISAs) mandate *design effectiveness* of preventive controls. A process that achieves zero loss by pure luck despite defective controls remains a statutory material weakness.
4. **Epistemic Latency and Unobservable Compounding:** Detective controls carry measurement latency. Undetected fraud can compound across multiple accounting periods before discovery, creating systemic balance-sheet vulnerabilities invisible to static single-period loss models.

### Semantic Classification of Manager Action Before Settlement but After Release

The classification of a manager approval occurring *after* `Release_Payment` but *before* `Bank_Settlement` (as seen in AP-201 and AP-207) depends on the system boundary applied:

1. **ERP / Application Layer Perspective (Detective):** At the internal application boundary, `Release_Payment` transitions the transaction state to "Released". The execution has already occurred; therefore, within the software state machine, the manager's action is *detective/ratifying*.
2. **Banking / Treasury Clearing Perspective (Preventive):** At the external treasury boundary, funds do not transfer irrevocably until `Bank_Settlement`. The banking interface operates with a staged release queue where internal authorization holds prevent settlement execution. Prior to the bank cutoff window, a managerial rejection triggers an automated payment cancellation (stop-payment). Thus, relative to irrevocable financial loss, the control is *preventive*.
3. **Chosen Synthesis (Hybrid Gating Control):** We classify pre-settlement manager ratification as a **Hybrid Gating Control**. It operates ex-post to the operator action but ex-ante to financial finality. Its reliability is strictly contingent upon:
   * A guaranteed latency window between release and settlement (e.g., at least 60–90 minutes);
   * An automated, tested stop-payment mechanism that halts clearing upon managerial rejection or timeout.

### 1. Case AP-202 (EUR 480,000 — Fraudulent Account Redirection)
* **Plausible Loss Mechanism:** Operator u23 actively redirected funds to an accomplice's newly added bank account, exploiting supervisor m09's override credentials to bypass automated controls.
* **Control Break Point:** None in actual execution. AV-2 detected the threat (bank change < 7d, limit exceeded), but the preventive block was broken by credential override. Post-settlement ratification by conflicted manager m09 and delayed audit (a03) failed to break the causal chain.
* **Residual Exposure:** Realized total loss of EUR 480,000.
* **Counterfactual under Independent Second Releaser:** An independent second releaser (unconflicted, separate workstation) would have received the AV-2 bank change warning, checked the vendor master file, verified the lack of goods receipt, and refused release, completely eliminating the loss (*High certainty of prevention*).

### 2. Case AP-204 (EUR 220,000 — Fabricated Consulting Invoice)
* **Plausible Loss Mechanism:** Creation of a fictitious high-value consulting invoice with forged service receipts, processed through a collusive or single-operator cell sharing workstation credentials.
* **Control Break Point:** AV-2 passed because the vendor existed and limits were not breached. Nominal FEP-1 failed due to lack of physical/logical independence. Retrospective audit detected the defect 4 days late, after funds had settled.
* **Residual Exposure:** High (EUR 220,000 disbursed; subject to uncertain civil/criminal asset recovery).
* **Counterfactual under Independent Second Releaser:** A truly independent releaser located in a separate department would have performed a substantive review of the deliverable (consulting milestone report), identified the fabricated receipt, and blocked release (*Moderate-to-High certainty of prevention*).

### 3. Case AP-206 (EUR 95,000 — Production Outage Emergency)
* **Plausible Loss Mechanism:** Accelerated single-operator payment during an operational crisis resulting in payment to wrong vendor or erroneous amount.
* **Control Break Point:** Broken ex-ante by independent manager m11 approving the invoice and outage documentation at 13:04, prior to release at 13:08. AV-2 confirmed vendor and bank validity.
* **Residual Exposure:** Very Low (bounded operational risk).
* **Counterfactual under Independent Second Releaser:** A distinct second releaser would have performed the same document inspection; the transaction would have completed successfully with identical risk reduction (*Neutral outcome*).

### 4. Case AP-207 (EUR 35,000 — Safety Repair Payment)
* **Plausible Loss Mechanism:** Single-operator emergency disbursement containing unauthorized charges or inaccurate pricing.
* **Control Break Point:** Broken prior to clearing by independent manager m15 reviewing all supporting safety repair documents and ratifying at 14:40 (80 minutes prior to 16:00 bank settlement).
* **Residual Exposure:** Very Low.
* **Counterfactual under Independent Second Releaser:** Dual authorization would have occurred at 14:05 rather than 14:40. The 35-minute timing shift had zero impact on final loss exposure (*Identical protection*).

### 5. Case AP-208 (EUR 900,000 — High-Value Outage Payment with Recent Bank Change)
* **Plausible Loss Mechanism:** Massive unverified fund transfer to a recently changed bank account during an IT outage, exposing company to catastrophic misdirection or vendor impersonation fraud.
* **Control Break Point:** None during the risk window. AV-2 was down, manual checklists were unsigned, manager ratified 24 hours after settlement, and audit occurred 4 days later. The absence of loss was a counterfactual stroke of luck (the vendor happened to be legitimate).
* **Residual Exposure:** Critical (EUR 900,000 unmitigated risk exposure).
* **Counterfactual under Independent Second Releaser:** An independent senior releaser would have identified the EUR 900k scale, noted the 3-day-old bank change, refused single-user bypass, enforced manual callback verification, and held the payment until IT restoration (*High certainty of risk interception*).

### 2. Distinguishing an Alert from Proof of Misconduct

An automated monitoring alert is a statistical or deterministic indicator of heightened risk, whereas proof of misconduct requires forensic corroboration:

* **Triage Stage (Alert):** Identifies anomalous patterns (e.g., u12/u13 co-approving 37 payments, or an unsigned fallback checklist). This establishes *procedural non-conformance* or *elevated risk profile*, warranting immediate supervisory review and payment hold.
* **Forensic Stage (Proof of Misconduct):** Requires establishing intent, unauthorized benefit, or deliberate policy subversion through corroborating evidence:
  * Network packet analysis and physical badge logs demonstrating one user typed both credentials;
  * Bank beneficiary tracing linking corporate disbursements to employee-related accounts (as established in AP-202);
  * Direct communication logs (emails, chat transcripts) demonstrating active collusion or forged delivery receipts (as in AP-204).

### Explanatory Notes on PM4Py Cross-Version Behavior

1. **`pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` API and Arguments:**
   * In PM4Py v2.2+ and v2.7+, `filter_four_eyes_principle` accepts a `keep_violations: bool` parameter. When `keep_violations=False` (default), it retains traces where `Prepare_Payment` and `Release_Payment` were executed by *distinct* resources. When `keep_violations=True`, it returns traces where the *same* resource executed both activities.
   * In earlier PM4Py v1.x / early v2.x releases, parameter names differed (e.g., `case_id_glue`, `activity_key`, `resource_key` passed via `parameters={...}` dictionary). To ensure portability across PM4Py versions, column names should follow XES standards (`case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, `org:resource`, `time:timestamp`), and fallback manual set differences should be implemented if custom dictionary parameters are required.
2. **Handling of Blocked/Cancelled Traces (e.g., AP-205):**
   * Standard four-eyes filtering algorithms evaluate pairs of completed activities. In cases like AP-205, where `Release_Payment` was never logged (because `Automated_Validate_Block` preceded an `Attempt_Release` and subsequent `Cancel_Payment`), `filter_four_eyes_principle` evaluates neither to conforming nor violating completed pairs. Traces lacking the terminal release activity must be isolated via explicit activity filtering to prevent false-negative misclassification.
3. **Dataframe vs. EventLog Object Handling:**
   * Modern PM4Py (v2.3+) operates natively on `pandas.DataFrame` objects using PyArrow/string dtypes. Older versions required explicit conversion via `pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df)`. Utilizing `pm4py.format_dataframe` ensures compatibility across both tabular and graph-based process mining backends.
