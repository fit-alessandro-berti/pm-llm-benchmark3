## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

For every path, calculate the branch join as **max(chemistry usable-result time, microbiology usable-result time)**. Add QA waiting, review, any subsequent rework and final recording only after that join. The calendar intervals below are examples and envelopes, not a complete simulation.

**Smooth path.**

- Chemistry: the first analyst becomes free Friday 15:54–16:24 and takes the urgent sample under the assumed priority rule. The other becomes free 16:42–17:30. Under risk priority, B-417 can then start, but even the earliest start plus the 1.5 h minimum ends after 18:00. Its remaining work resumes Monday 08:00; an illustrative completion is Monday around 08:15–12:00. Under FIFO, three ordinary samples also precede B-417; using similar service times as a queueing proxy, a plausible result is Monday late morning–afternoon, with later completion possible. Waiting for an analyst and service are distinct; the weekend pause is elapsed time but not laboratory work.
- Microbiology: the 46–54 h incubation finishes Sunday 12:00–20:00. If on-call is authorized, dispatch and readout give a usable result approximately Sunday 13:00–23:00. Otherwise, routine readout begins Monday 08:00 and takes 0.5–1.5 h, giving approximately Monday 08:30–09:30 before any backlog.
- Join: take the later result. On-call can eliminate the microbiology staffing delay but cannot overcome chemistry or QA being closed; without on-call, microbiology often competes with chemistry for the Monday join.
- QA: Friday 15:30–20:00 and Saturday 09:00–13:00 provide 8.5 reviewer-hours before Monday, during which roughly three new arrivals are expected on average. As a rough illustration, at 1.5 h per case the reviewer could handle about five or six typical cases; this does not establish how many of the initial five will still be ahead. Two reviewers are available Monday. A common illustrative wait is 0–6 working hours after the join, then review takes around 1.5 h at its median and about 3.5 h at its 80th percentile. A late Monday review pauses at 20:00 and resumes Tuesday 08:00.
- Rework and terminal step: none on this path. Electronic Release adds 0.25–0.75 elapsed hours after acceptance, including outside QA hours. A very favorable Monday join around 08:15, little QA wait and short review could produce release around 09:00–11:00; a more typical combination gives Monday afternoon through Tuesday morning.

**Chemistry rerun.**

- Chemistry: queueing and primary service follow the same priority or FIFO alternatives. After the primary result, a borderline result triggers a priority rerun requiring 1.0–3.0 laboratory working hours, with mode 1.8 h. If unfinished at 18:00, it pauses until Monday 08:00 or the next laboratory opening. For example, a Monday 11:00 primary result followed by a roughly 1.8 h rerun gives a result near 12:48, before any rerun dispatch delay. A late primary result can push the rerun into Tuesday.
- Microbiology: the same Sunday incubation and on-call versus Monday routine readout apply. With a normal microbiology result, the rerun often makes chemistry the later branch.
- Join and QA: take the later usable result, then apply future QA queueing and review as above. If the join or review reaches Monday 20:00, remaining QA work waits until Tuesday 08:00.
- Rework and terminal step: the rerun also places the case in the stated higher investigation-risk group; a query or investigation can further delay it. If accepted, release takes another 0.25–0.75 h. Monday evening–Tuesday evening is a plausible central envelope; combined delays create the later tail in the table.

**Delayed microbiology or confirmation.**

- Chemistry: use the same service, queue and weekday pause assumptions; a normal result commonly arrives Monday, though FIFO or a rerun can make it later.
- Microbiology: without on-call, a technically ready Sunday culture waits for Monday staffing and is ordinarily recorded Monday 08:30–09:30. If a contamination indication appears, confirmation adds 20–30 continuous hours from the first readout, followed by another 0.5–1.5 h staffed readout. For example, a Monday 09:00 indication becomes technically ready Tuesday 05:00–15:00; its confirmation can be read Tuesday 08:30–16:30 if staffing is available. An early Sunday on-call first read could instead make confirmation ready Monday morning, while a later read can postpone it.
- Join and QA: QA waits for the later of the chemistry result and the usable confirmed microbiology result. After a Tuesday join, allow the uncertain QA queue and review within Tuesday 08:00–20:00, or later openings if needed.
- Rework and terminal step: a contamination indication also raises the investigation probability. A cleared result may lead to release after review; a confirmed critical defect may lead to rejection recording, taking 0.5–1.0 h after confirmation. The timing of the decision and the probability of a critical defect are not specified, so rejection cannot be assigned a reliable probability or exact date.

**QA query or investigation.**

- Chemistry and microbiology: either may be normal or reworked; the QA clock starts at their maximum, not their sum. As a working example, suppose both usable results exist Monday around 10:00–13:00.
- QA: allow a future queue wait, for example 0–6 working hours, then the initial review. A query or investigation can arise during that review; its actual initiation time depends on the queue and review progress.
- Rework: a query adds 4–12 working hours before QA resumes. A documentation-only investigation adds 8–20 working hours; these administrative clocks pause at Monday–Friday 20:00 and resume at 08:00, with Saturday 09:00–13:00 available and no Sunday work. For example, 12 working hours beginning Monday 16:00 finish Tuesday 16:00. An investigation requiring laboratory work instead adds 3–7 calendar days, after which QA still needs to resume when open. A query and investigation may both occur; their order and any overlap are unspecified.
- Terminal step: allow a further review and then 0.25–0.75 h for release if accepted. If a critical defect is confirmed, rejection recording takes 0.5–1.0 h. Administrative rework commonly moves completion into midweek; a laboratory investigation can move it into the following week, especially with additional QA waiting.

## 4. Forecast summary

These ranges are elapsed time from Friday 18 September at 15:30. They are approximate forecast bands, not confidence intervals or quantiles established by a fitted model. The proposed P80-like and P90-like ranges describe where those cumulative-percentile landmarks may fall, rather than saying that each entire scenario ends within the range.

- **Plausible earliest completion:** approximately **65.5–67.5 h**, Monday 21 September **09:00–11:00**, with particularly fast chemistry and QA and no consequential rework. Earlier release would require a different chemistry priority interpretation, overtime, or other favorable departures from the ledger.
- **Central/P50-like:** approximately **71.5–94.5 h**, Monday **15:00** to Tuesday 22 September **14:00**. This includes ordinary Monday readout, possible chemistry spillover and uncertainty in the future QA queue.
- **P80-like:** approximately **92.5–124.5 h**, Tuesday **12:00** to Wednesday 23 September **20:00**. Queries, reruns, confirmation and QA congestion make this landmark highly uncertain.
- **P90-like or severe tail:** approximately **116.5–196.5 h**, Wednesday **12:00** to Saturday 26 September **20:00**. A laboratory investigation or combined rework can carry completion into the following week or beyond; there is no defensible finite upper bound in the supplied data.
- **Most plausible terminal event:** Electronic Release after QA acceptance. Batch Rejected is possible after a confirmed critical defect, but the information given does not support a quantitative rejection forecast.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

1. **QA query and investigation path — path and calendar uncertainty.** A query adds 4–12 working hours; an investigation adds either 8–20 working hours or 3–7 calendar days, before any further QA queue and review. The investigation mix and probabilities are approximate.
2. **Microbiology readiness, staffing and confirmation — calendar and path uncertainty.** Primary incubation ends on Sunday, so on-call authorization determines whether the result is available then or waits for Monday. An indication adds another continuous incubation and staffed readout.
3. **Future QA queue — queue uncertainty.** The five current batches can be processed or supplemented before the join, and overdispersed arrivals and variable service can create a much longer wait than the illustrative envelope.
4. **Chemistry priority, service and rerun — queue, service-time and path uncertainty.** Whether B-417 passes three ordinary samples changes its start time; a rerun and the 18:00 closure can push the usable result into a later opening.
5. **Temperature-excursion status — observation and dependence uncertainty.** The current sensor gap leaves a common cause unresolved. Confirmation raises the risks of both branch anomalies and the investigation probability, making a combined tail more plausible; the size of that effect is unknown.

## 6. Most forecast-changing next event

The most forecast-changing single observation would be **the microbiology readout outcome, including whether a contamination indication is present**, once the primary incubation is ready. A normal usable result removes the confirmation clock and shifts attention to chemistry and the QA queue; an indication introduces another 20–30 h incubation, another staffed readout, and the higher investigation-risk group, shifting the forecast later. If the observation is instead that weekend on-call is denied, expect the initial usable result no earlier than Monday staffing; authorization can make it available Sunday, although chemistry and QA may still determine the join and release time.

A secondary update trigger is **confirmation or clearance of the temperature-excursion flag**. Confirmation should increase the forecast weight on both branch anomalies and investigation, including their joint occurrence; clearance should reduce that shared-cause concern, without making the remaining anomalies impossible.
