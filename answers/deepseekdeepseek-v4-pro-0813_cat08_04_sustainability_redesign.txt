## 1. System boundary and assumptions

The environmental scope is **order-to-delivery process impact**, from `Receive order` through `Close` or `Cancel due to delay`, based on the event log and attribute set.

**Inside boundary:**

- Direct production energy: `energy_kwh` by `machine_group`, including CNC, heat treatment, inspection-related queues and rework loops.
- Transport emissions: `transport_kgco2e` by `route_mode`.
- Packaging material: `package_material_kg`, including packaging rework loops.
- Production scrap and rework: `material_scrap_kg`, `rework_flag`, and rework production loops.
- Batching effects: batch size, batch wait, energy-per-item effects, and obsolescence risk inferred from batch evidence.
- Service outcomes: cycle time, late delivery, partial shipments, cancellations due to delay.
- Policy conformance related to sustainability and quality: SP1, SP2, Q1.

**Outside boundary:**

- Raw material extraction and spare-part manufacturing upstream of the distributor.
- Customer use-phase emissions or downtime impact.
- End-of-life disposal or recycling.
- Full lifecycle of packaging materials.
- Supplier and carrier capital emissions.

**Assumptions and uncertainty:**

- Orders are flattened, so line-item heterogeneity is partially hidden.
- `transport_kgco2e` is treated as direct route-level emissions; empty-return effects for dedicated vans are only partially visible.
- Obsolescence is not directly logged but is inferred from the batching evidence: 6% of batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days when forecasts are wrong.

## 3. Intervention options

### I1. Green shipping recommender using lead-time slack, reliability, and segment

- **Target path/segment:** Standard-stock Service-contract orders currently shipped air; all non-critical stock and MTO route choices.
- **Mechanism:** Use `promised_lead_time_hours`, route reliability, and `customer_type` to select ground or consolidated truck when SP1 can be met with probability above 85%. Air remains available for short slack or disruption.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Strong transport reduction for obvious air-overuse; cycle time may rise slightly for converted air-to-ground orders. Service risk stays low if the 85% slack threshold is enforced. Resilience remains acceptable if alternate routes are retained.
- **Process-mining evidence to monitor:** SP1 violation rate by segment; route-mode distribution by slack bucket; late delivery by chosen route; `transport_kgco2e/order` by route.

### I2. Priority pick + ground for Standard-stock Service-contract orders

- **Target path/segment:** `Stock -> standard pack -> air` path and Standard-stock Service-contract cases.
- **Mechanism:** Replace unjustified air with faster warehouse priority picking plus ground/consolidated. This removes the reason many service-contract stock orders default to air.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Transport emissions fall significantly for this segment. Cycle time rises versus air but can remain within promised lead time; late-delivery risk is small if priority pick works. Quality is neutral.
- **Process-mining evidence to monitor:** Standard-stock Service-contract air share; median/p90 cycle time; late delivery %; SP1 violations; `transport_kgco2e` before/after.

### I3. Consolidated truck departures twice daily for non-urgent stock orders

- **Target path/segment:** Non-critical standard-stock ground parcel paths.
- **Mechanism:** Consolidate individual ground parcels into truck departures twice daily. The evidence shows consolidated trucks reduce transport kgCO2e by 35–55%, but create a 12–24h consolidation wait.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Transport emissions fall substantially. Cycle time increases slightly to moderately for slower stock orders. Late-delivery risk rises if orders are not assigned to departures with enough slack. Resilience risk increases slightly if the consolidated carrier is disrupted, so ground fallback must remain available.
- **Process-mining evidence to monitor:** consolidation wait; missed departure counts; late delivery by departure cutoff; consolidated truck share; `transport_kgco2e/order`.

### I4. Dynamic batching with waiting-time caps by segment

- **Target path/segment:** MTO batch-wait paths, especially `Plan production -> Batch wait -> CNC production` and heat-treatment waits.
- **Mechanism:** Increase average batch size where energy per item improves, especially CNC_A, while imposing maximum waiting-time caps by segment and due-date slack. Avoid large batches for short-slack Service-contract or Critical-downtime orders unless capacity is idle.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Energy per item falls substantially in CNC_A from 2.8 kWh at batch 1–3 to 1.3 kWh at batch 12–20. However, unmanaged batching increases waiting and obsolescence. With caps, late-delivery risk is controlled. Resilience may fall slightly if production becomes too batch-dependent.
- **Process-mining evidence to monitor:** batch size distribution; batch wait by segment; energy per item by batch tier; late-delivery risk; obsolescence % of batch items not shipped within 14 days.

### I5. Shift suitable Made-to-order production from CNC_B to CNC_A

- **Target path/segment:** MTO cases routed to CNC_B, especially the rework-heavy `MTO -> batch wait -> CNC_B -> inspection -> rework -> ground` path.
- **Mechanism:** CNC_A has much lower rework rates: 9% versus 18% on CNC_B. When queue and due-date slack allow, shift production to CNC_A, especially for parts with rework history.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Lower rework, less scrap, lower hidden material and energy waste. Cycle time may increase because CNC_A batching can be slower. Late-delivery risk rises if due-date slack is overestimated. Resilience risk increases slightly if CNC_B is underused and lost as an alternative capacity.
- **Process-mining evidence to monitor:** machine mix by segment; rework rate by `machine_group`; energy per item; queue lengths; late-delivery risk by path.

### I6. Packaging-route compatibility check before shipment

- **Target path/segment:** `Package standard -> Rework packaging` and `Package reinforced -> Rework packaging` loops.
- **Mechanism:** Add a decision rule before shipment to verify that package type is compatible with `route_mode` and part geometry. Enforce Q1 for Made-to-order parts over 8 kg unless inspection certifies low damage risk.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Fewer packaging rework loops, lower `package_material_kg`, fewer delayed shipments, and fewer damage-related returns. Reinforced packaging remains in place where it protects heavy MTO parts.
- **Process-mining evidence to monitor:** packaging rework frequency; Q1 violation rate; package material per order; damage/return-related rework loops; late delivery.

### I7. Inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations

- **Target path/segment:** `Inspection -> Rework production -> CNC` loops, especially after rushed inspection of CNC_B output.
- **Mechanism:** Add or improve inspection quality checks for high-rework part/machine combinations. The goal is to catch defects before packaging and customer return, rather than after rushed inspections miss defects.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Lower rework production, lower scrap, lower energy waste, and fewer customer returns. Risk: inspection queue can become a bottleneck, increasing wait and potentially triggering expedite behaviors.
- **Process-mining evidence to monitor:** inspection queue length; inspection-to-rework loop rate; rework rate by `machine_group`; scrap; late deliveries caused by inspection wait; return-related loops.

### I8. Shared emergency courier lanes for precautionary urgent shipments

- **Target path/segment:** Some dedicated van use, especially cases without SP2 justification.
- **Mechanism:** Replace some single-customer dedicated vans with shared urgent lanes or micro-consolidation for non-critical urgent shipments. This reduces empty-return and underutilized van emissions.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Transport emissions fall. For true Critical-downtime orders, dedicated vans remain available. If shared lanes are slower or less reliable, service risk rises for misclassified urgent orders. Resilience is only acceptable if the dedicated-van option is preserved for true criticals.
- **Process-mining evidence to monitor:** dedicated van share; SP2 justification presence; shipment utilization; response time for urgent segments; `transport_kgco2e`.

### I9. Split Critical-downtime into true downtime versus precautionary urgent categories

- **Target path/segment:** `Critical -> emergency production -> reinforced pack -> dedicated van` path.
- **Mechanism:** Differentiate true downtime orders from precautionary urgent orders. Route true downtime to emergency production and dedicated van; route precautionary urgent to priority stock, fast ground, or shared urgent courier.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Reduces unnecessary emergency production energy and dedicated-van transport emissions. True critical service remains protected. Misclassification creates either service risk or emissions rebound.
- **Process-mining evidence to monitor:** share of Critical-downtime cases classified as true downtime; emergency production frequency; dedicated van rate; late delivery and p90 cycle time for criticals; SP2 conformance.

### I10. Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular Made-to-order customers

- **Target path/segment:** Regular Made-to-order cases, especially those later expedited or shipped air.
- **Mechanism:** Negotiate promise dates to allow slower ground/consolidated delivery and more efficient production batching. This aligns promised lead time with lower-emission paths.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Lower energy and transport emissions by reducing avoidable expediting. Quoted lead times may increase, but late delivery measured against the revised promise can remain stable. Customer conversion/retention risk is possible.
- **Process-mining evidence to monitor:** negotiated lead time; batch size; share of air/expedite for Regular MTO; cancellation rate; late delivery against revised promise.

### I11. Reduce partial shipments by changing availability-check rules

- **Target path/segment:** `Pick from stock -> Partial shipment -> Backorder wait -> second shipment` path.
- **Mechanism:** Improve availability-check logic to allocate stock to complete orders where possible, or to offer customer consent before splitting. This reduces double shipments and extra packaging.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Transport and packaging fall if partial shipments decline. However, forcing complete-order waits may increase cycle time, backorder wait, and cancellation risk. This should be piloted carefully.
- **Process-mining evidence to monitor:** partial shipment rate; backorder wait; second-shipment frequency; cancellation due to delay; late delivery; package and transport per completed order.

## 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching rebound and obsolescence.**  
   Larger batches reduce energy per item but raise `Batch wait` from 4h to 38h in CNC_A. If forecasts are wrong, 6% of batch-produced items are not shipped within 14 days. That creates obsolete inventory, wasted material, extra handling, and possibly replacement production or expedited transport, offsetting energy savings.

2. **Transport modal shift rebound.**  
   Replacing air or dedicated vans with ground/consolidated saves direct emissions, but ground adds 12–24h waiting or 24h transit. If the promise is missed, the order may be escalated to air or dedicated van later. The last-minute expedited shipment can emit more than the originally avoided regular air shipment.

3. **Packaging reduction rebound.**  
   Reducing reinforced packaging to save material can increase damage-related returns for heavy Made-to-order parts. Q1 violations already correlate with returns and rework loops. A damaged part returned by the customer triggers additional production energy, scrap, packaging, and return transport that overwhelms the saved package material.

4. **Rework and inspection bottleneck displacement.**  
   Adding an inspection quality gate reduces rework loops but may increase inspection-cell queue and wait. If that wait causes missed promise dates, orders may be expedited by air or dedicated van. If inspection is rushed to reduce waiting, defects may again pass through and recreate the rework loop.

5. **Obsolescence from longer negotiated lead times.**  
   Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation can enable slower batching and ground transport, but a longer horizon increases the chance of demand changes, cancellations, or order modifications. Process energy and transport already spent on those orders becomes waste, and forecast-driven batches may become obsolete.

6. **Customer behavior and segment creep.**  
   If green rules restrict air/dedicated vans or promise dates, customers or sales staff may reclassify orders as more urgent or Critical-downtime to bypass constraints. This displaces demand into emergency production and dedicated-van paths, which are exactly the highest-emission routes.

7. **Resilience displacement from shared or consolidated lanes.**  
   Replacing dedicated vans with shared lanes or relying on consolidated trucks creates dependency on common schedules or carriers. If a shared lane or consolidated departure fails, Service-contract customers may lose the alternative route required by R1, forcing ad-hoc high-emission recovery.

8. **Partial-shipment reduction rebound.**  
   Reducing partial shipments may increase backorder wait and cancellations. If an order is cancelled after stock has been picked, packed, or partially produced, the energy, packaging, and transport already used become waste.

## 6. Balanced recommendation

A coherent redesign package should focus on **removing unjustified high-emission paths while preserving fast paths for true urgency**.

### Recommended package

1. **Transport and customer-segment controls**
   - Implement **I1 Green shipping recommender** and **I2 priority pick + ground** for Standard-stock Service-contract orders where promised lead time can be met with ground with at least 85% probability.
   - Use **I3 consolidated truck departures** for non-urgent stock orders, but exclude true critical and low-slack orders.
   - Implement **I9 Critical-downtime split** so dedicated vans and emergency production are reserved for true downtime. Use **I8 shared emergency courier lanes** for precautionary urgent shipments.
   - Do **not** ban air or dedicated vans outright. Retain them for true criticals, disruptions, and short-slack service-contract orders.

2. **Production and quality controls**
   - Apply **I4 dynamic batching with waiting-time caps** by segment. Allow large batches only where the energy benefit is high and late-delivery risk is low.
   - Apply **I5 shift from CNC_B to CNC_A** when due-date slack and queue conditions allow, but keep CNC_B as overflow and disruption capacity.
   - Apply **I7 inspection quality gate** to high-rework machine/part combinations, while monitoring inspection queue to avoid creating a new bottleneck.

3. **Packaging and fulfillment controls**
   - Enforce **I6 packaging-route compatibility** and Q1 for heavy Made-to-order parts.
   - Pilot **I11 partial-shipment reduction** only after availability-check logic is improved; do not force complete-order waits for uncertain stock.

4. **Governance**
   - Use **S12 sustainability exception log** for all SP1, SP2, and Q1 overrides. Overrides should include reason, segment, slack, and expected service impact.

### Why this balances environmental, service, quality, and resilience goals

- **Environmental:** It targets the largest tractable emissions sources: avoidable air, empty dedicated vans, packaging rework, and high-rework CNC_B production.
- **Service:** It does not impose a blanket ground-only policy. Air, dedicated vans, and emergency production remain for true critical and short-slack orders.
- **Quality:** It reduces known rework paths through CNC_A shifting, inspection gates, and packaging compatibility.
- **Resilience:** It preserves CNC_B, ground fallback, and dedicated critical lanes. It also adds exception governance rather than creating rigid rules that fail during disruption.

The package is deliberately **not** a lowest-emission option. A lowest-emission design would force ground/consolidated everywhere and larger batches everywhere, but it would likely increase late deliveries, obsolescence, returns, and disruption risk.

## 7. Validation and governance plan

### Pilot and before-after evaluation

- Run changes with a phased rollout by segment, region, or route lane where possible.
- Compare matched orders before and after using `case_type`, `customer_type`, `promised_lead_time_hours`, `route_mode`, and relevant path characteristics.
- Use process discovery on the post-implementation event log to detect new variant combinations and unintended loops.

### Conformance monitoring

- **SP1:** Track violation rate for Standard-stock Service-contract orders. The baseline finding is 22% violation; monitor whether air is still used when ground could meet the promise with >85% probability.
- **SP2:** Track dedicated-van cases with missing justification. The baseline is 31% missing; require justification event or post-hoc override reason.
- **Q1:** Track heavy Made-to-order parts over 8 kg that are shipped without reinforced packaging. Monitor whether violations still correlate with return/rework loops.

### Segment-level service monitoring

- Track late delivery %, median cycle time, and p90 cycle time by segment:
  - `Standard-stock Regular`
  - `Standard-stock Service-contract`
  - `Made-to-order Regular`
  - `Made-to-order Service-contract`
  - `Critical-downtime Service-contract`
- Track partial shipment rate, backorder wait, cancellation due to delay, and p90 for critical orders to ensure the redesign does not degrade urgent service.
- Monitor whether consolidating standard shipments increases missed cutoff events or drives extra expedite flags.

### Environmental and process-path monitoring

- `energy_kwh/order` by segment and `machine_group`.
- Batch-size-adjusted energy per item for CNC_A, CNC_B, and heat treatment.
- `transport_kgco2e/order` by `route_mode` and segment.
- `package_material_kg/order` and packaging rework rate.
- `material_scrap_kg/order` and rework production loops.
- Obsolescence proxy: share of batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days.

### Exception governance

- Use exception log to record all SP1, SP2, and Q1 overrides with reason, segment, slack, and approval.
- Review overrides monthly. If a rule is overridden often, the rule should be revised rather than allowing routine exceptions.
- Set predefined service guardrails: if late delivery or p90 cycle time worsens beyond the current baseline for critical or service-contract segments, roll back the transport mode or batching changes.
- Use conformance checking to verify that actual process paths follow the intended redesigned model, and to catch emergent detours such as ground-to-air escalation or packaging rework loops after consolidation.
