## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

In resolving the missing links of this incomplete OCEL 2.0-style dataset, we must employ abductive reasoning to find the most plausible explanation for the observed events, while acknowledging that our inferences remain defeasible. The evidence model is structured around a hierarchy of rules, ranging from strict systemic constraints to probabilistic business heuristics. 

The consistency rules applied in this analysis are classified as follows:

**Hard Invariants**
*   **Referential Integrity:** Any object identifier (OID) or event identifier (EID) referenced in a proposed E2O or O2O relation must pre-exist in the provided objects or events tables. We cannot hallucinate or invent new objects (e.g., assuming a `PK-9`) or events to force a clean lifecycle.
*   **Temporal Monotonicity within Object Lifecycles:** An object's physical lifecycle cannot move backward in time. A package cannot be dispatched before its items are packed, and an order cannot be invoiced before it is created.

**Strong Defaults**
*   **Explicit Attribute References:** When an event attribute explicitly embeds a known object identifier (or a near-exact match), it is a strong default that the event relates to that object. For example, `ev08` contains `scanner_ref=PK8-2`, strongly pointing to `PK-8`. Similarly, `ev10` contains `external_ref=N-421?`, which strongly points to `DEL-Y` (whose carrier reference is `N-421`).
*   **Arithmetic Summation for Financial Objects:** When the monetary amounts of subordinate objects exactly sum to the total of a parent object within the same customer account, it provides strong evidence of a relationship. The combination of `ORD-A` (130) and `ORD-B` (100) exactly equals the `INV-44` total (230). Likewise, `PAY-1` (100) and `PAY-2` (130) exactly sum to 230. While arithmetic matching is evidence and not definitive proof (due to potential discounts, taxes, or unapplied cash), an exact match in a small dataset is a highly reliable signal.

**Weak Heuristics**
*   **Wildcard Matching:** The `ev11` event (`Confirm Delivery`) has `external_ref=N-42*`. This matches both `DEL-X` (N-420) and `DEL-Y` (N-421). This is a weak heuristic because a portal might use a wildcard or truncated reference when confirming a batch, or it might just be poor data entry for a single delivery.
*   **Customer-Level Grouping:** Objects sharing the same customer (e.g., `C17`) are more likely to be bundled into the same packages, deliveries, or invoices.

**Evidence Against a Repair**
*   **Cross-Level Redundancy (Over-cluttering):** If a process strictly logs events at the Delivery or Order level (e.g., `Confirm Delivery`), forcibly adding E2O relations to every contained Item or Package violates the probable logging schema.
*   **Premature Closure:** Forcing an object to reach the end of its lifecycle (e.g., forcing `ORD-B` to have a `Confirm Delivery` event) when the evidence is ambiguous is strong evidence against a repair. It is better to leave an object as an open case than to fabricate a completion event.

When combining these factors, identifier similarity and arithmetic matching form the primary basis for our high-confidence repairs. Time and lifecycle order act as secondary validation checks to ensure proposed relations do not violate physical reality. Existing O2O structure serves as a template; if `INV-44` bills `ORD-A`, it sets a precedent for how `INV-44` should relate to `ORD-B`.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

To appropriately manage uncertainty, we define two coherent repair bundles. They differ primarily in how aggressively they attempt to close the object lifecycles based on ambiguous portal events.

**Bundle A: Conservative/Minimum-Change Repair**
This bundle strictly accepts only repairs backed by strong, direct evidence (explicit identifiers and exact arithmetic matches) and refuses to extrapolate ambiguous wildcard events.
*   **ev08 and PK-8:** Accepts the E2O link from `ev08` to `PK-8` and the resulting O2O link (`PK-8 packs IT-B1`). The explicit string `PK8-2` in the scanner reference is deemed sufficient evidence, accepting that `PK-8` consolidates items from both `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` for customer `C17`.
*   **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** Accepts the E2O link from `ev10` to `DEL-Y` and the O2O link (`DEL-Y carries PK-8`). The `N-421?` reference is accepted as a match for `DEL-Y`.
*   **ev11 and the delivery objects:** Takes a skeptical stance. The wildcard `N-42*` is too ambiguous. While it matches both deliveries, the portal might only be confirming `ORD-A`. Therefore, `ev11` is left exactly as observed (linked only to `ORD-A`). `DEL-X`, `DEL-Y`, and `ORD-B` do not receive `Confirm Delivery` events in this bundle.
*   **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Accepts the E2O link (`ev12` to `ORD-B`) and O2O link (`INV-44 bills ORD-B`). The exact arithmetic sum (130 + 100 = 230) for the same customer `C17` is treated as a highly reliable signal.
*   **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Accepts the E2O link (`ev14` to `INV-44`) and O2O link (`PAY-2 settles INV-44`) due to the exact amount match completing the invoice balance.

**Bundle B: Plausible Alternative Interpretation (Aggressive Closure)**
This bundle accepts all the strong evidence from Bundle A but takes a more aggressive, holistic interpretation of the ambiguous portal event `ev11`, assuming it represents a bulk status update.
*   **ev08 and PK-8:** Handled identically to Bundle A.
*   **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** Handled identically to Bundle A.
*   **ev11 and the delivery objects:** Interprets the `N-42*` wildcard as an intentional bulk confirmation for all carrier references starting with `N-42`. Consequently, this bundle assigns `ev11` to `DEL-X`, `DEL-Y`, and inherently `ORD-B` (since `ORD-B` items were in `DEL-Y`). This creates a coherent, fully completed lifecycle where both orders and both deliveries are confirmed simultaneously.
*   **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Handled identically to Bundle A.
*   **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Handled identically to Bundle A.

Both bundles yield coherent object lifecycles, but Bundle A prefers to leave `ORD-B` and the deliveries in an unresolved/in-transit state, while Bundle B provides a clean, fully closed system. Bundle A is generally preferable for process mining as it avoids hallucinating happy-path completions.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

The selected repair (Bundle A) significantly improves the lifecycle coherence of the dataset while responsibly maintaining necessary uncertainty.

*   **Order:** `ORD-A` maintains a complete, plausible lifecycle from Creation to Payment. `ORD-B` gains links to the Invoice and Payment events, forming a clear financial lifecycle. However, in Bundle A, `ORD-B` is deliberately left without a `Confirm Delivery` event. This is a plausible, realistic state representing an order that has been billed but whose physical delivery is unconfirmed.
*   **Item:** `IT-A1`, `IT-A2`, and `IT-B1` all progress cleanly from `Add Item` to `Pack Item`. No orphans exist here.
*   **Package:** `PK-7` progresses cleanly. By repairing `ev08`, `PK-8` now progresses properly through `Pack Item` and `Dispatch Package`. There are no longer any disconnected package events.
*   **Delivery:** `DEL-X` is well-formed. By repairing `ev10`, `DEL-Y` is rescued from being an orphan object (previously it had no events natively linked to it) and is now properly tied to `Dispatch Package`. 
*   **Invoice & Payment:** `INV-44`, `PAY-1`, and `PAY-2` all now have complete semantic mappings. `PAY-2` is no longer an orphan event floating without an invoice context.

**Cardinality Analysis:**
The repairs create a notable many-to-many/one-to-many convergence. `PK-8` is now mapped to both `IT-A2` (from `ORD-A`) and `IT-B1` (from `ORD-B`). This cardinality is superficially suspicious because it mixes items from different orders. However, because both orders belong to customer `C17`, this represents a standard logistics optimization: consolidating multiple open orders into a single shipment for the same customer. Similarly, `INV-44` now bills both `ORD-A` and `ORD-B`, which perfectly aligns with the consolidated package. 

**Circular Reasoning Warning:**
It is critical to explicitly identify the circular reasoning trap regarding the financial inferences. We inferred the E2O edge (`ev12` relates to `ORD-B`) based *entirely* on the arithmetic matching of the amounts. We then inferred the O2O edge (`INV-44 bills ORD-B`) based on that exact same arithmetic logic. It is a logical fallacy to then claim that the newly created O2O edge provides "independent structural confirmation" that `ev12` relates to `ORD-B`. Both relations stem from a single, defeasible heuristic (arithmetic equality) and rise or fall together.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

The choice of repair strategy dramatically alters the artifacts generated by downstream PM4Py discovery algorithms.

**OC-DFG (Object-Centric Directly-Follows Graph):**
*   *Unrepaired:* The graph would be highly fragmented. The `Dispatch Package` node for `PK-8` would be disjointed from `DEL-Y`. The `Record Payment` node for `PAY-2` would appear as an isolated island disconnected from the main invoice flow.
*   *Bundle A (Conservative):* The OC-DFG becomes highly cohesive. Strong edges now flow from `Pack Item` to `Dispatch Package` across both packages. `Issue Invoice` acts as a synchronization point, showing incoming edges from both `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` lifecycles. 
*   *Bundle B (Aggressive):* The OC-DFG would show a massive synchronization hub at `Confirm Delivery`, with incoming edges from `DEL-X`, `DEL-Y`, `ORD-A`, and `ORD-B`.

**OTG (Object Type Graph):**
*   *Unrepaired:* The structural graph would show broken hierarchies. `DEL-Y` would not be connected to the graph. `ORD-B` would lack connections to the `Invoice` type.
*   *Bundle A & B:* The OTG strictly maps `Delivery -> Package -> Item -> Order <- Invoice <- Payment`. The addition of the O2O relations perfectly solidifies this domain model.

**ET-OT (Event Type to Object Type Graph):**
*   *Unrepaired:* `ev10` (`Dispatch Package`) would lack a connection to the `Delivery` object type.
*   *Bundle A:* Restores the expected process schema, ensuring `Dispatch Package` always connects to both `Package` and `Delivery` object types.

**Flattenings:**
*   *Flattening on Order:* In the unrepaired log, flattening on `ORD-B` would yield a very short trace (`Create Order` -> `Add Item`). Under Bundle A, flattening on `ORD-B` correctly pulls in the financial lifecycle, extending the trace to `Create Order` -> `Add Item` -> `Issue Invoice` -> `Record Payment`.
*   *Flattening on Delivery:* Flattening on `DEL-Y` would yield an empty trace in the unrepaired log. Under Bundle A, it now yields `Dispatch Package`. Under Bundle B, it would yield `Dispatch Package` -> `Confirm Delivery`.

**Equivalent-Execution Clustering on Order:**
Because Bundle A links `INV-44` and the payments to `ORD-B`, the traces for `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` will cluster much closer together in a behavioral vector space than in the unrepaired log. However, because Bundle A conservatively leaves `Confirm Delivery` off of `ORD-B`, the clustering algorithm will still distinctly separate `ORD-A` (completed delivery) from `ORD-B` (unconfirmed delivery).

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

def validate_ocel_repair(events, objects, relations, o2o):
    """
    Validates the proposed E2O and O2O repairs using PM4Py object-centric capabilities.
    Assumes inputs are pandas DataFrames matching the OCEL 2.0 structure.
    """
    # 1. Copy the original tables before repair to preserve immutability
    events_rep = events.copy()
    objects_rep = objects.copy()
    relations_rep = relations.copy()
    o2o_rep = o2o.copy()

    # 2. Append the selected E2O additions (Bundle A)
    new_e2o = pd.DataFrame([
        {"ocel:eid": "ev08", "ocel:oid": "PK-8", "ocel:qualifier": "None"},
        {"ocel:eid": "ev10", "ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:qualifier": "None"},
        {"ocel:eid": "ev12", "ocel:oid": "ORD-B", "ocel:qualifier": "None"},
        {"ocel:eid": "ev14", "ocel:oid": "INV-44", "ocel:qualifier": "None"}
    ])
    relations_rep = pd.concat([relations_rep, new_e2o], ignore_index=True)

    # 3. Append the selected O2O additions (Bundle A)
    new_o2o = pd.DataFrame([
        {"ocel:oid": "PK-8", "ocel:oid_2": "IT-B1", "ocel:qualifier": "packs"},
        {"ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:oid_2": "PK-8", "ocel:qualifier": "carries"},
        {"ocel:oid": "INV-44", "ocel:oid_2": "ORD-B", "ocel:qualifier": "bills"},
        {"ocel:oid": "PAY-2", "ocel:oid_2": "INV-44", "ocel:qualifier": "settles"}
    ])
    o2o_rep = pd.concat([o2o_rep, new_o2o], ignore_index=True)

    # 4. Perform referential-integrity and duplicate checks
    assert relations_rep["ocel:eid"].isin(events_rep["ocel:eid"]).all(), "E2O contains unknown event OIDs"
    assert relations_rep["ocel:oid"].isin(objects_rep["ocel:oid"]).all(), "E2O contains unknown object OIDs"
    assert o2o_rep["ocel:oid"].isin(objects_rep["ocel:oid"]).all(), "O2O Source contains unknown object OIDs"
    assert o2o_rep["ocel:oid_2"].isin(objects_rep["ocel:oid"]).all(), "O2O Target contains unknown object OIDs"

    relations_rep = relations_rep.drop_duplicates(subset=["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"])
    o2o_rep = o2o_rep.drop_duplicates(subset=["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"])

    # 5. Construct or update an OCEL object
    # Note: This constructor format is version-dependent (PM4Py 2.7.x+ ObjectCentricEventLog)
    ocel = pm4py.objects.ocel.obj.OCEL(events_rep, objects_rep, relations_rep, o2o_rep)

    # 6. Discover graph artifacts
    ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
    otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
    etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

    # 7. Flatten on Order and Delivery 
    # We deliberately avoid using pm4py.ocel_o2o_enrichment() prior to flattening. 
    # Using enrichment algorithms would algorithmically force E2O relations down the O2O graph, 
    # treating our defeasible business heuristics as infallible structural proofs, which leads to over-repair.
    flat_order = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Order")
    flat_delivery = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Delivery")

    return ocdfg, otg, etot, flat_order, flat_delivery
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings

Aggressive, uncalibrated repairs designed solely to make graphs look "clean" or lifecycles "complete" can severely distort the actual business reality. In this dataset, over-repair could create false process behavior in at least five specific ways:

1.  **Fabricating Order Completion:** By forcing `ev11` (`Confirm Delivery`) to link to `ORD-B` based purely on the fact that `ORD-B` items were in the same shipment ecosystem as `ORD-A`, an analyst creates a false positive. In reality, `ORD-B`'s physical delivery might have been damaged, lost, or rejected, and the portal intentionally only confirmed `ORD-A`. Forcing this repair ruins compliance monitoring for delivery SLAs.
2.  **Premature Financial Settlement:** Linking `PAY-2` to `INV-44` because the arithmetic perfectly matches the outstanding balance assumes a clean, happy-path accounting process. In reality, `PAY-2` might have been an unapplied cash deposit, a prepayment for a future order, or a payment assigned to the wrong customer account by the bank. Falsely linking it closes an invoice that the accounting system might still consider delinquent.
3.  **Cross-Contaminating Shipments:** Assuming `PK-8 packs IT-B1` solely because `ev08` packs `IT-B1` and the scanner string was `PK8-2` might be incorrect. If the scanner `PK8-2` was hastily used to pack `IT-B1` into a completely separate, unrecorded package (e.g., `PK-9`), generating the O2O link falsely blends two independent physical shipments into one logical package. This would cause downstream flattening algorithms to incorrectly interleave the dispatch times of unrelated orders.
4.  **Transitive Property Abuse in E2O:** If an analyst decides that because `ev11` confirms `ORD-A`, and `ORD-A` contains `IT-A1`, which is packed in `PK-7`, carried by `DEL-X`, then `ev11` must be explicitly added to the E2O tables for `IT-A1`, `PK-7`, and `DEL-X`. This indiscriminate O2O-to-E2O generation clutters the event log. It creates a false impression that the underlying IT system actively tracks and logs package-level granularity at the time of portal confirmation, which is structurally untrue.
5.  **Hallucinating Dispatch Reality:** Linking `ev10` to `DEL-Y` based on the wildcard `N-421?` assumes the question mark is merely a minor data entry typo. However, in many logistics systems, a question mark or special character explicitly denotes a rejected scan, a failed weight check, or a staging failure. Forcing this link might simulate a successful dispatch for a package that never actually left the warehouse.
