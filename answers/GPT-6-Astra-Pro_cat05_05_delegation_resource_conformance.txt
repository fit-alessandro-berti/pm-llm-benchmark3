## 2. Rule Interpretation

Let \(H(e)\) denote the attributed human performing event \(e\), or “unknown” where attribution is insufficient. Let \(R(h,t)\) denote human \(h\)’s standing roles at time \(t\).

A delegation is usable only when all relevant predicates hold:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V(d,e)={}&
\text{valid grant and evidence}
\land \text{delegate}(d)=H(e)\\
&\land t_e\in I_d
\land \text{scope covers the change}
\land \text{permitted function covers the activity}\\
&\land \text{required principal-absence condition holds}
\land \text{delegation chain is permitted}.
\end{aligned}
$$

I use **PASS, FAIL, and UNKNOWN**. Missing proof of a required condition is not a pass; it is also not automatically proof that the condition was false.

**RR1 — Drafter/approver separation.** For each case \(c\), the human sets for drafting and approval must be disjoint:

$$
H_{\mathrm{Draft}}(c)\cap H_{\mathrm{Approve}}(c)=\varnothing.
$$

A known common human establishes failure. Unresolved identities prevent a positive separation finding unless other evidence resolves the uncertainty. LOW risk and emergency status do not waive RR1. **RR2** applies the same construction to implementation and verification. These checks consider every relevant occurrence, not merely the final approval or verification. 

**RR3 — HIGH-risk authority.** A non-exception HIGH-risk approval requires a standing QM or a valid QM delegate at the approval timestamp. Being a Quality Engineer, Site Director, future delegate, or past delegate is insufficient. RR4 supplies a specific emergency alternative, not a general expansion of approval permissions. 

**RR4 — Emergency substitution.** The emergency route requires credible emergency status and safety rationale, a qualifying IC approval before implementation, and a subsequent QM or valid QM-delegate retrospective review within the deadline. It does not waive RR1 or RR2. A retrospective review cannot retrospectively make prohibited subdelegation or self-approval permissible. 

For the deadline, I adopt this explicit interpretation: use implementation as the trigger, exclude its calendar date, and allow review until the end of the second following Monday–Friday business day. I use UTC dates because the log is UTC and no plant timezone is specified. No holidays are supplied. 

Both emergency implementations occurred on Tuesday, August 11. Their deadline is therefore **the end of Thursday, August 13**:

| Case    | Recorded retrospective review | Timeliness under this interpretation |
| ------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| CHG-505 | Friday, August 14, 16:00      | Late                                 |
| CHG-510 | Wednesday, August 12, 10:00   | Timely                               |

Using approval rather than implementation as the trigger, or preserving the trigger’s time of day when adding two business days, would not change these two findings. Timeliness does **not** establish reviewer authority.  

**Principal action while on leave.** Asha retains her standing QM role. Her personal handling of an urgent case can therefore be authorized. I treat documented leave as supporting D-QM-01 outside a specifically conflicting availability interval; an isolated VPN session does not automatically cancel the whole delegation. A different policy—such as suspension whenever the principal resumes work—would require a documented availability decision and its effective time. The August 13 session cannot retroactively invalidate Ben’s August 11 approval.  

**Effective time versus database-recording time.** A database row created on August 12 cannot, by itself, prove authority on August 11. Here, however, the supplied evidence includes a signed instrument timestamped August 9 with an August 10 effective start. Subject to authenticity and content validation, that supports earlier authority; the migration date is not the delegation’s effective date. Conversely, a later database entry cannot manufacture an earlier delegation where no reliable prior instrument existed. 

**RR5 — Identity evidence.** A service-account string is not a distinct human and cannot establish separation. Attribution must be event-specific and supported by reliable session evidence. Alias normalization is mandatory. **RR6** makes collaboration and network concentration investigative indicators, not determinations of collusion or innocence. 

**Boundary of the assessment.** The supplied rules do not expressly mandate QM approval for LOW-risk changes, prohibit an approver from later verifying, require a particular verifier job title, or impose pair rotation. Thus Eva’s role change and Grace’s title do not alone establish verification violations. Competence, organizational independence, and additional activity permissions remain control-objective questions rather than invented RR1–RR6 requirements.

The workstation, absence, migration, and historical-pair facts support these competing hypotheses; they do not select a unique explanation.  

**Network interpretation.** Handover networks describe resource succession; working-together networks describe participation in the same case. Neither establishes conversation, physical co-presence, agreement, or collusion. PM4Py’s handover function with `beta=0` uses direct succession, while its working-together function links co-case participants. ([GitHub][1])

My calculations from the supplied 43 events, using the attribution decisions above, produce 33 adjacent event transitions. They include six Diego→Eva transitions. Clara and Diego are known participants together in eight cases—an unsurprising concentration given the observed drafting/implementation allocation. Clara and Ben are established co-participants in four cases under this attribution policy; treating Ben’s disputed/sick-leave actions as unquestionably his would increase that count to six. These are sensitivity-dependent descriptions, not misconduct scores.

**The historical 18 approvals also need individual authority checks.** A five-day delegation cannot explain an undated 60-day pattern by itself. Equally, the summary does not prove those approvals fell outside valid delegation intervals: earlier grants and exact event dates are missing.  

For monitoring, I would compare observed pair concentration against assignment opportunities constrained by cell, scope, risk, shift, availability, and actual approval authority. Without those denominators, a statistical significance claim would be unsupported. Rotation or network alerts should trigger review of evidence and work quality, not automatic disciplinary conclusions.

## 5. PM4Py and pandas Checking Code

The script retains unknown human attribution as null and uses **event-specific opaque nodes** for network construction. Those nodes preserve adjacency; they are not treated as proven distinct humans. For four-eyes checks, unresolved cases are withheld from positive conformance conclusions.

`keep_violations=True` explicitly asks PM4Py to retain violations. The independent pandas checks examine all relevant human sets, providing a safeguard for repeated-event cases rather than relying solely on the library filter’s implementation. ([GitHub][2]) PM4Py network weights are printed separately from transparent raw transition and co-case counts; the network object exposes `connections` and `is_directed`. ([GitHub][3])

def active(frame, t):
    return ((frame['start'].isna() | frame['start'].le(t)) &
            (frame['end'].isna() | frame['end'].ge(t)))

def roles_at(human, t):
    return set(roles.loc[roles['human'].eq(human) & active(roles, t), 'role'])

# Three-valued logic: None means not established, never automatic permission.
def and3(values):
    values = list(values)
    if any(v is not None and not bool(v) for v in values):
        return False
    return None if any(v is None for v in values) else True

def or3(values):
    values = list(values)
    if any(v is not None and bool(v) for v in values):
        return True
    return None if any(v is None for v in values) else False

def status(value):
    return 'UNKNOWN' if value is None else ('PASS' if bool(value) else 'FAIL')

def authority_for(e, human):
    required = {'QM', 'IC'} if e[A] == APPROVE and e['emergency'] else {'QM'}
    if pd.isna(human):
        return None
    if roles_at(human, e[T]) & required:
        return True
    results = []
    candidates = delegations[delegations['delegate'].eq(human) & delegations['role'].isin(required)]
    for _, d in candidates.iterrows():
        in_time = bool(d['start'] <= e[T] <= d['end'])
        principal_ok = bool(d['role'] in roles_at(d['principal'], e[T])) if d['chain_ok'] else False
        purpose = True if e[A] == APPROVE else None
        absent = absence_at(d['principal'], e[T]) if d['absence_required'] else True
        prior = d['prior_instrument']
        if pd.notna(d['signed_at']):
            prior = bool(d['signed_at'] <= e[T])
        elif e[T] < d['start']:
            prior = None  # 'Before the interval' does not prove 'before this event'.
        factors = {'interval': in_time, 'scope': scope_ok(d, e), 'purpose': purpose,
                   'absence': absent, 'chain': d['chain_ok'],
                   'principal_power_to_grant': principal_ok, 'prior_instrument': prior}
        result = and3(factors.values())
        checks.append({C: e[C], A: e[A], 'event_id': e['event_id'], 'delegation': d['id'],
                       **{k: status(v) for k, v in factors.items()}, 'result': status(result),
                       'DB_record_later': bool(pd.notna(d['recorded_at']) and d['recorded_at'] > e[T])})
        results.append(result)
    return or3(results)

# Set-based checks include every occurrence, not just the last approval/verification.
def sod_table(log, first, second):
    rows = []
    for case, g in log.groupby(C, sort=True):
        x, y = g[g[A].eq(first)], g[g[A].eq(second)]
        shared = set(x['human'].dropna()) & set(y['human'].dropna())
        if shared:
            flag = 'FAIL'
        elif x.empty or y.empty or x['human'].isna().any() or y['human'].isna().any():
            flag = 'UNKNOWN'
        else:
            flag = 'PASS'
        rows.append({C: case, 'status': flag, 'same_humans': ', '.join(sorted(shared))})
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

## 6. Control Improvements

**Delegation creation and effective-time recording.** Store signed instruments with separate effective-start, effective-end, signing, registration, migration, amendment, and revocation timestamps. Make permitted activities and scope explicit: for example, whether cooling systems belong to production equipment and whether retrospective review is included. Enforce no-subdelegation restrictions in the approval engine, not merely in document text.

**Principal and delegate availability.** Define whether “absent” means formally on leave, operationally unavailable, or not handling the specific case. Record temporary return-to-duty and delegation suspension/resumption as effective-dated decisions. An urgent principal intervention should create an explicit availability record rather than forcing auditors to infer one from VPN activity.

**Aliases, authentication, and segregation.** Maintain effective-dated account-to-human mappings around an immutable employee identity. Apply RR1 and RR2 to humans across aliases, including LOW-risk and emergency cases. Require individual, action-bound authentication for approvals and verification. Where a service executes on someone’s behalf, record the requesting human, signing human, and executing service separately. Unknown attribution must not pass a preventive separation gate.

**Emergency approval and review.** Require an incident identifier, supporting safety rationale, qualifying authority, and an automatically calculated review deadline. Route retrospective review to an explicitly authorized QM or delegate, with escalation before expiry. Retain overdue and unauthorized events as findings even after corrective review; do not backdate a cure. Consider an additional HIGH-risk approver/verifier-independence requirement, but adopt it prospectively rather than claiming it already exists.

**Pair rotation and network monitoring.** Introduce risk-based rotation where enough qualified staff are available, with documented exceptions for specialist work. Evaluate pair concentration against actual assignment opportunities, then examine review substance. Neither repeated pairing nor rotation compliance should substitute for evidence of meaningful challenge.

**Evidential retention.** Preserve original signed grants, validation results, migration lineage, historical role/alias records, availability decisions, business-calendar versions, action-bound authentication, badge/endpoint evidence, and original event timestamps. Keep audit decisions reproducible, including the attribution assumptions and software version used. Protect the evidence from overwrite while applying an appropriate access and retention policy.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

**The evidence indicates a mixed control problem—not a demonstrated collusion scheme and not merely stale master data.**

Under the stated identity assumptions, **five of ten cases have at least one established control failure**: CHG-502 and CHG-508 violate drafting/approval separation; CHG-507 lacks qualifying HIGH-risk approval authority; CHG-510 combines invalid authority with self-approval; and CHG-505 misses its emergency-review deadline. Two cases are primarily identity-assurance problems, one remains indeterminate because a delegation condition is unproven, and two are supported as valid actions, including one legitimate substitution. These counts describe this supplied sample, not plant-wide misconduct prevalence.

The three highest-priority investigations are:

1. **Ben’s disputed account activity, led by CHG-506.** Identify the actual approver and assess possible credential compromise; extend the examination to CHG-505’s sick-leave retrospective action.
2. **The emergency-control chain in CHG-510 and CHG-505.** Establish incident legitimacy, examine the prohibited subdelegation and late review, and obtain properly authorized independent QM assessment of the implemented changes.
3. **CHG-503’s unattributed verification.** Determine whether Diego verified his own implementation or another authorized human performed a genuine independent check.

Clara–Ben concentration warrants targeted review, but **authority defects, overdue emergency oversight, and unreliable personal attribution are currently better-supported priorities than an accusation of collusion**.
