| Legal/rework congestion scenario | Parallel document check is performed for all cases including future legal/supplement cases; supplement rate increases; loops extend; staff fixed | ~40% legal-review cases affected; ~62% originals-affected overlap issues | Median unchanged to +2 h (gains in verification canceled by loop delays) | p90 +15 to +25 h (loops and legal queue) | Rework +4 to +6%; appeals +1 to +2%; document reviewer utilization spikes | 50 |

Reasoning: Site A’s 9-hour median reduction is not a pure treatment effect; after removing capacity (+2 reviewers, ~11% increase), API latency, mix shift (~8 percentage point drop in complexity), and huddles, the structural parallelization effect is likely 3–6 hours at best, and only for cases without binding prerequisites. At Site B, only ~38% of cases avoid the originals prerequisite, and 40% enter legal review with loops. Scaling the likely structural effect (say, 4–6 hours) by the eligible share (~38%) yields an overall median impact of roughly 1.5–2.5 hours, but bottleneck shift and loop effects can erase even that for the tail. The bottleneck-shift scenario captures the risk that parallel upstream work simply feeds an unchanged or saturated downstream system (assessment/legal), improving median for simple cases but extending p90 for complex/loop cases.

## 6. Candidate Site B intervention

**Modified intervention specification (segmented, statutory-compliant)**

- **Eligibility rules for parallel task creation:**
  - After `Register`, `Identity Check` is always created immediately.
  - `Originals Receipt` is created immediately only if originals are legally required.
  - `Document Check` is created immediately if originals are **not** required (digital/non-required path, ~38%). If originals are required, `Document Check` is created in a “pending-receipt” state or deferred until `Originals Receipt` is confirmed complete.
  - Cases requiring `Legal Review` proceed through `Assessment` in parallel with verification, but `Legal Review` remains mandatory after `Assessment` for the 40% public/high-value subgroup.

- **Concurrent execution rules:**
  - `Identity Check` and `Originals Receipt` (when required) may run concurrently.
  - `Document Check` runs concurrently with `Identity Check` only when not blocked by missing originals.
  - `Assessment` waits for **both** `Identity Check` and `Document Check` to reach “complete” status (explicit dependency, not approximate).

- **Statutory prerequisites preserved:**
  - `Document Check` cannot be executed/substantively completed until original documents are received when required.
  - `Legal Review` cannot be skipped for qualifying cases.
  - `Request Supplement` loops back to `Document Check` (if document update needed) and `Assessment` (if re-evaluation needed).

- **Queue and WIP controls:**
  - Per-role open-task caps: Identity  8; Document  10; Assessment  6; Legal  4.
  - Daily queue visibility meeting (optional separate arm) to manage flow, not embedded as hidden co-intervention.

- **Resource responsibilities:**
  - Identity reviewers perform only identity verification.
  - Document reviewers perform only document verification.
  - Assessors perform assessment and determine legal-review need.
  - Legal reviewers handle legal review and supplement requests.
  - No cross-role execution; workload shifts only within role via queue rules.

- **Handling of legal-review and supplement loops:**
  - When `Legal Review` issues `Request Supplement`, the case returns to `Document Check` (if document correction needed) and then `Assessment`. The loop is counted as a separate cycle in metrics.

- **Logging requirements:**
  - Actual task-start and task-end timestamps recorded independently of case-lock status.
  - Case-lock owner logged at each transition.
  - Document-channel flag (originals required / received / digital) logged.
  - Loop-iteration counter incremented on each `Request Supplement`.
  - Workaround events (back-entry, manual lock override) flagged.

**POWL-style relation set (textual)**

```text
Register -> Identity Check
Register -> Originals Receipt [conditional on originals_required]
Originals Receipt -> Document Check [when originals_required]
Register -> Document Check [when not originals_required, approximate]
Identity Check -> Assessment
Document Check -> Assessment
Assessment -> (XOR) Approve
Assessment -> (XOR) Reject
Assessment -> (XOR) Legal Review [conditional on legal_required]

Legal Review -> (XOR) Approve
Legal Review -> (XOR) Reject
Legal Review -> (XOR) Request Supplement

Request Supplement -> Document Check [loop]
Request Supplement -> Assessment [loop]

(Approve | Reject) -> Notify
```

**Compact POWL node/edge specification (code representation)**
- Root `StrictPartialOrder` nodes: `Register`, `Identity Check`, `Originals Receipt`, `Document Check`, `Assessment`, `Legal Review`, `Request Supplement`, `Approve`, `Reject`, `Notify`.
- Order edges: `(Register, Identity Check)`, `(Register, Originals Receipt)`, `(Originals Receipt, Document Check)`, `(Identity Check, Assessment)`, `(Document Check, Assessment)`, `(Assessment, Legal Review)`, `(Legal Review, Request Supplement)`, `(Request Supplement, Document Check)`, `(Request Supplement, Assessment)`, `(Legal Review, Approve)`, `(Legal Review, Reject)`, `(Assessment, Approve)`, `(Assessment, Reject)`, `(Approve, Notify)`, `(Reject, Notify)`.
- `OperatorPOWL` (`XOR`) embedded for post-Assessment split and for legal-review exit/supplement split.
- `OperatorPOWL` (`LOOP`) embedded for supplement  document/assessment cycle.

**How the pilot separates mechanism failure from implementation failure (220 words)**

Mechanism failure means parallel verification does not reduce cycle time even when correctly implemented—because prerequisites (originals) block document work, assessment is saturated, or legal loops consume any upstream gain. Implementation failure means staff do not create tasks concurrently, the legacy lock suppresses true ownership, or timestamps are entered late, producing an artificial overlap measure without real flow change.

The pilot separates these by measuring both true overlap (independent server/audit timestamps, not case-lock status) and waiting-time decomposition. If true overlap is high (>40% in Stratum 1) but waiting before assessment does not fall, the mechanism is failing: the bottleneck has shifted downstream (assessment/legal) or prerequisites still dominate. If true overlap is low (<15%) but cycle time improves, implementation is failing: staff are not actually working in parallel, and any gain comes from co-interventions (huddles, recording changes). The stratified design adds further separation: in Stratum 1 (digital, no legal), prerequisites and loops are absent, so any failure is likely implementation (IT lock, staff behavior) or assessment saturation. In Stratum 2/4 (originals required), low overlap combined with no improvement confirms mechanism failure—the statutory prerequisite prevents concurrency. By holding staffing, marketing, and IT constant, and by using concurrent randomized controls within the same pools, the pilot isolates the structural parallelization effect from confounding changes that obscured Site A’s results.

## 8. PM4Py model-construction code

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    Transition, SilentTransition, OperatorPOWL, StrictPartialOrder, POWL
)

# ---------- 1. Site A: before and after trees ----------
# Before: strict sequential verification
tree_before = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "-> ( 'Register', 'Identity Check', 'Document Check', "
    "'Assessment', X ( 'Approve', 'Reject' ), 'Notify' )"
)
net_before, im_before, fm_before = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_before)

# After: parallel identity and document verification
tree_after = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "-> ( 'Register', + ( 'Identity Check', 'Document Check' ), "
    "'Assessment', X ( 'Approve', 'Reject' ), 'Notify' )"
)
net_after, im_after, fm_after = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_after)

# ---------- 2. Site B candidate using POWL ----------
# Nodes for the backbone and loop
reg    = Transition("Register")
id_c   = Transition("Identity Check")
orig   = Transition("Originals Receipt")
doc    = Transition("Document Check")
ass    = Transition("Assessment")
legal  = Transition("Legal Review")
supp   = Transition("Request Supplement")
app    = Transition("Approve")
rej    = Transition("Reject")
noti   = Transition("Notify")

# Silent nodes for XOR splits/joins in the loop and after assessment
s_ass  = SilentTransition("s_after_assessment")
s_exit = SilentTransition("s_legal_exit")
s_back = SilentTransition("s_loop_back")

# Core verification backbone: explicit partial order.
# Note: Document Check depends on Originals Receipt (conservative/statutory approx).
# In reality this is data-dependent; the model encodes the required path.
spo_core = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[reg, id_c, orig, doc, ass],
    order={
        (reg, id_c),
        (reg, orig),
        (orig, doc),
        (id_c, ass),
        (doc, ass),
    }
)

# Post-assessment legal/rework loop modeled as an OperatorPOWL (LOOP + XOR)
# The loop contains: Legal Review, then XOR(exit to approve/reject, or supplement).
# Supplement loops back (represented by a silent transition in the loop body).
loop_legal = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=OperatorPOWL.LOOP,
    children=[
        legal,
        OperatorPOWL(
            operator=OperatorPOWL.XOR,
            children=[
                s_exit,   # path out of loop toward final decision
                supp      # supplement request
            ],
            parent=None
        ),
        s_back    # silent transition representing loop back to doc/assessment
    ],
    parent=None
)

# Root POWL: combine backbone and loop, then final notification paths.
# Assessment feeds the loop; loop exit feeds Approve/Reject; both feed Notify.
root_spo = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[reg, id_c, doc, ass, loop_legal, app, rej, noti, orig],
    order={
        (reg, id_c),
        (reg, orig),
        (orig, doc),
        (id_c, ass),
        (doc, ass),
        (ass, loop_legal),
        (loop_legal, app),
        (loop_legal, rej),
        (app, noti),
        (rej, noti),
    }
)

# Build root POWL model
model_b = POWL(nodes=[root_spo])

# Convert POWL to Petri net
net_b, im_b, fm_b = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_b)

# ---------- 3. Workflow-net / soundness checks ----------
is_wf = pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net_b, im_b, fm_b)
sound_result = pm4py.check_soundness(net_b, im_b, fm_b) if is_wf else (False, "Not a workflow net")
print("Workflow net:", is_wf)
print("Soundness:", sound_result)

# ---------- 4. Optional playout ----------
# Use basic playout to generate sample traces from the model
try:
    sample_traces = pm4py.play_out(
        net_b, initial_marking=im_b, final_marking=fm_b,
        variant=pm4py.playout.Variants.BASIC_PLAYOUT, params={}
    )
    print("Sample trace count:", len(sample_traces))
    for t in sample_traces[:3]:
        print("Trace:", [ev["concept:name"] for ev in t])
except Exception as e:
    print("Playout error (expected if variant not available):", e)

# ---------- 5. What this model CANNOT encode ----------
# - Data-dependent prerequisites: Document Check should wait ONLY when
#   originals are legally required; the model uses a structural dependency
#   (Originals Receipt -> Document Check) that applies universally.
# - Resource capacity and specialization: the net has no places/tokens
#   representing reviewer pools (4/7/3/2) or per-role WIP limits.
# - Case-lock behavior: simultaneous ownership and late timestamp entry
#   are not representable in control-flow alone.
# - Duration distributions: service times (median identity 3.4h, doc 8.1h)
#   and external delay (originals receipt median 18h) are absent.
# - Quality/rework dynamics: the loop structure captures iteration but not
#   the probability of supplement (360/1360  26%) or its quality impact.
# - Case mix and complexity: high-complexity vs simple cases are not
#   distinguished in the net.
# - Concurrent control/interference: randomization and stratum effects
#   are outside the process model.
```

## 9. Recommendation

**Choice: `conditional go`**

What should be transferred: The principle that verification tasks with independent prerequisites and available capacity can be executed concurrently to reduce sequential queue wait. The measurement framework—tracking true overlap (independent of case-lock status), waiting before assessment, and loop iterations—is essential. The queue-management practice (visible WIP, per-role limits) can be adopted, but only if separated from the parallelization test.

What should not be copied: Site A’s blanket simultaneous creation for every case, its cross-trained flexible staffing model, its reliance on a system that supports multi-owner timestamps, and its specific capacity and technology changes (API upgrade, +2 reviewers, marketing mix shift). The observed 9-hour median reduction and 871% overlap increase are confounded and should not be treated as an expected effect size at B. The daily queue huddles should not be bundled into the initial pilot.

Which subgroup should enter first: Stratum 1—cases with no legally required originals and no mandatory legal review (approximately 20–25% of volume, within the 38% digital subset). These cases structurally resemble Site A: identity and document checks both depend on independent external/digital data, there is no statutory receipt delay, and there is no downstream legal loop to invalidate document work. Only after demonstrating a true overlap increase, a waiting-time reduction, and stable rework/appeals in Stratum 1 should the pilot expand to Stratum 2 (originals required, but no legal review), where parallel identity work can begin immediately while document work waits on receipt. Strata with legal review (Stratum 3/4) should be excluded from initial parallelization of document checks, or included only with explicit loop tracking and conservative WIP controls, because premature document review risks supplement-driven rework.

Expected benefits: For Stratum 1, a 4–8 hour reduction in median cycle time (10–20%) is plausible if true overlap reaches 40–60% and assessment capacity absorbs the flow. The primary gain should appear in “waiting before Assessment,” not in service times, since task durations are unchanged. For the overall site, scaling only to eligible cases yields a modest overall median improvement (likely 2–5 hours) unless the pilot demonstrates that assessment and legal pools can be expanded or restructured.

Expected risks: (1) Bottleneck shift—parallel verification feeds assessment faster, but with only 3 assessors and 2 legal reviewers, wait may move downstream, leaving median flat and p90 worse. (2) Implementation illusion—the legacy case lock prevents true overlap, producing timestamp artifacts that mimic Site A’s 71% overlap without real flow improvement. (3) Quality erosion—parallel document work in legal-review cases may increase supplement requests, raising rework from 17% toward 20% and appeals above 6%. (4) Role saturation—document reviewers (7 FTE) may become overloaded if parallel creation is not paired with WIP limits.

Evidence that should trigger expansion: In Stratum 1, concurrent control comparison shows median cycle time reduction 4 hours, true overlap 30% (independent audit), waiting before assessment reduced proportionally, rework stable (<18%), appeals stable (<5.5%), and p90 does not worsen. Expansion to Stratum 2 should require that originals receipt delay is not extended by task-creation confusion, and that document reviewer utilization remains below 85%. Evidence that should trigger modification: If true overlap is low (<15%) but cycle improves, the mechanism is not parallelization—likely huddles or recording changes—so the intervention should be redesigned to target the actual driver. Evidence that should trigger abandonment: If rework or appeals rise >2 percentage points in any stratum, if p90 increases >15%, or if IT workarounds generate data-quality failures that prevent measurement.

This conditional path respects Site B’s statutory ordering, separate specialist roles, legacy IT constraints, and heavier case mix, while testing whether Site A’s structural mechanism—removing binding sequential verification waits—actually applies where prerequisites and loops are present.

## 10. Assumption register

1. **Assumption:** The primary causal mechanism at Site A was concurrent verification reducing sequential queue wait, not capacity or technology changes. — **Why needed:** To justify testing parallelization rather than hiring or upgrading APIs. — **How to test:** Hold staffing and IT constant in B pilot; compare effect size to A’s likely structural component (~3–6 h). — **Consequence if false:** The pilot tests the wrong lever; any improvement will be misattributed, and copying the process change will not replicate results.

2. **Assumption:** Site B’s 62% originals-requirement rate is a binding structural constraint that prevents true document-check parallelization for that subgroup. — **Why needed:** Determines eligibility rules and expected effect size. — **How to test:** Measure overlap rate and waiting time separately for originals-required vs digital-only cases in pilot. — **Consequence if false:** If originals can be verified digitally or received instantly, parallelization applies more broadly, increasing potential benefit but also risk.

3. **Assumption:** The legacy IT case-lock can be worked around or partially lifted for pilot measurement without full system replacement. — **Why needed:** True overlap measurement requires multi-owner capability or independent audit. — **How to test:** Implement independent timestamp logging; compare system-status overlap to audit overlap. — **Consequence if false:** Overlap will be underestimated or misrecorded, producing false negatives or false positives, and the pilot cannot distinguish mechanism from implementation failure.

4. **Assumption:** Separate specialist roles at B (no cross-training) mean parallel task creation does not automatically balance load across identity and document pools. — **Why needed:** Predicts bottleneck shift rather than smooth load absorption. — **How to test:** Monitor per-pool queue lengths and utilization during pilot. — **Consequence if false:** If staff informally cross-train, the bottleneck may not materialize, but role accountability and quality risks change.

5. **Assumption:** Assessment (3 FTE) and legal review (2 FTE) pools have limited elasticity and can become saturated if upstream verification accelerates. — **Why needed:** Explains why median might improve but p90 worsens. — **How to test:** Measure assessment and legal queue lengths and wait times by stratum. — **Consequence if false:** If downstream capacity is actually elastic, the bottleneck-shift scenario is invalid, and larger gains are possible.

6. **Assumption:** The supplement loop (Request Supplement  Document/Assessment) is frequent enough (360/1360 legal cases  26%) to make premature document work risky in legal cases. — **Why needed:** Justifies excluding legal-review cases from initial document-check parallelization. — **How to test:** Track loop entry rate by treatment arm and by whether document check was performed pre-assessment. — **Consequence if false:** If loops are rare or unrelated to upfront document quality, excluding legal cases is overly conservative and delays potential benefits.

7. **Assumption:** Site A’s overlap metric (8%  71%) was confounded by improved timestamp recording and system use, so true overlap was lower. — **Why needed:** Prevents using 71% as a realistic target for B. — **How to test:** At B, compare system-recorded overlap to independent audit overlap during pilot. — **Consequence if false:** If A’s overlap was truly 71%, B must achieve comparable system changes to replicate the mechanism; failure to reach 71% would be interpreted as failure, even if true overlap and cycle time improve.

8. **Assumption:** Case mix and complexity at B are stable during the pilot, and marketing or external shifts do not confound results. — **Why needed:** Ensures that any observed change is attributable to the pilot intervention. — **How to test:** Monitor incoming case characteristics (complexity flag, originals-required rate, legal-required rate) weekly; stop or adjust if shifts exceed ±5 percentage points. — **Consequence if false:** If mix shifts (e.g., more simple digital claims arrive), the pilot will overestimate transportability, leading to false expansion and subsequent failure at full scale.
