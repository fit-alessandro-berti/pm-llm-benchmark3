# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1. **Tail Latency in High-Complexity Cases**
   - *Risk*: While average cycle time may improve, the 90th percentile for high-complexity cases could increase by 30-50% due to queue starvation in the shared service model.
   - *Hidden by*: Average cycle time calculations that don't segment by complexity.
   - *Detection*: Monitor `pm4py.get_case_duration(event_log, case_complexity="high").quantile(0.9)` and compare with pre-change baseline.

2. **Local Knowledge Loss and Unnecessary Referrals**
   - *Risk*: Local caseworkers may lose the ability to make early judgments about specialist need, leading to 20-40% more unnecessary referrals that inflate specialist queues.
   - *Hidden by*: Average cycle time may appear stable if referrals are processed quickly, but system capacity is wasted.
   - *Detection*: Track `pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(event_log, "handover_reason", ["unnecessary referral"]).count()` and compare referral rates by team.

3. **Fairness Across Customer Segments**
   - *Risk*: Small customer segments or those requiring regional accommodations may experience 25-50% longer wait times due to standardized routing that doesn't account for their specific needs.
   - *Hidden by*: Aggregate metrics that don't segment by customer segment or location.
   - *Detection*: Monitor `pm4py.get_waiting_time_by_segment(event_log, "customer_segment")` and compare with pre-change baselines.

4. **Learning and Capability Decline**
   - *Risk*: Local staff may stop developing technical skills if all complex cases are routed away, leading to long-term capability decline that won't be visible for 6-12 months.
   - *Hidden by*: Short-term metrics that don't measure skill development or case complexity handled locally.
   - *Detection*: Track `pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(event_log, ["N_Case1", "Tech_SS1"])` over time to measure skill divergence.

5. **Accountability Gaps and Rework Loops**
   - *Risk*: Shared approval queues may create accountability gaps where no one feels responsible for case outcomes, leading to 15-30% more rework loops that don't affect average cycle time.
   - *Hidden by*: Rework may be processed quickly in the shared service, keeping average cycle time stable.
   - *Detection*: Monitor `pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(event_log, "shared approval queue")` and track escalation rates.

6. **Exception Handling and Resilience Decline**
   - *Risk*: Centralization may reduce the organization's ability to handle exceptions, with 40-60% longer resolution times during system shocks or specialist absences.
   - *Hidden by*: Average metrics during normal operations don't reflect performance during exceptions.
   - *Detection*: Simulate absence scenarios with `pm4py.simulate_absence_impact(event_log, ["Tech_SS1"])` and compare with pre-change resilience.

7. **Queue Starvation and Priority Inversion**
   - *Risk*: The shared service model may create priority inversion where medium-priority cases get processed while high-priority cases wait, leading to SLA breaches that don't affect average cycle time.
   - *Hidden by*: Average metrics that don't segment by priority.
   - *Detection*: Monitor `pm4py.get_waiting_time_by_priority(event_log)` and track SLA breaches by priority.

8. **Specialist Overload and Hidden Bottlenecks**
   - *Risk*: Specialists may become overloaded with additional tasks (triage, quality checks) that aren't visible in utilization metrics, leading to 30-50% longer processing times for their core work.
   - *Hidden by*: Utilization metrics that don't account for task switching or additional responsibilities.
   - *Detection*: Track `pm4py.get_service_time(event_log, "Technical Shared Service")` for core vs. non-core activities and monitor task switching frequency.

# 5. Pilot design

**Pilot Scope:**
- Teams: North and East (representing different current performance profiles)
- Timeframe: 12 weeks (8 weeks observation, 4 weeks adjustment)
- Case types: All cases with complexity "medium" or "high" and required_specialty in {"technical", "legal", "risk"}
- Volume: ~60% of eligible cases (randomly selected) to test system capacity

**Comparison Group:**
- Teams: South (continues with current model)
- Case types: Same as pilot group
- Volume: All eligible cases during pilot period

**Pre/Post Observation:**
- Pre-pilot: 8 weeks of baseline data collection
- Pilot: 12 weeks of implementation
- Post-pilot: 4 weeks of follow-up (with pilot changes maintained)

**Segmentation:**
- By team: North vs. East vs. South (comparison)
- By complexity: Low vs. Medium vs. High
- By specialty: Technical vs. Legal vs. Risk
- By customer segment: Large vs. Medium vs. Small vs. Regional

**Guardrail Metrics (Stop/Adjust Triggers):**
1. Specialist utilization >92% for >2 weeks
2. Approval queue wait time >5h for >1 week
3. Rework rate in Technical Shared Service >15% for >2 weeks
4. High-complexity case 90th percentile cycle time >25d
5. Small customer segment wait time >20% above baseline
6. Local caseworker specialist referrals >30% above baseline
7. Staff satisfaction score <3.5/5 on role clarity questions

**Staff Feedback Collection:**
- Weekly pulse surveys (5 questions on role clarity, workload, fairness)
- Bi-weekly focus groups with caseworkers and specialists
- Monthly deep-dive interviews with team leads
- Real-time feedback channel for urgent issues

**Rollback Triggers:**
1. Any guardrail metric breached for >3 weeks
2. Staff satisfaction score <3.0/5 for >2 weeks
3. Customer complaints increase by >40%
4. SLA breaches increase by >25% for high-priority cases
5. Specialist turnover during pilot period

**Adjustment Triggers:**
1. Any guardrail metric breached for >1 week
2. Staff satisfaction score 3.0-3.5/5 for >2 weeks
3. Customer complaints increase by 20-40%
4. SLA breaches increase by 15-25%
5. Specialist utilization 85-92% for >3 weeks

**Pilot Phases:**
1. **Preparation (4 weeks):**
   - Finalize routing rules
   - Train staff on new processes
   - Implement monitoring dashboards
   - Establish feedback channels

2. **Soft Launch (2 weeks):**
   - Route 30% of eligible cases to shared services
   - Monitor guardrail metrics daily
   - Daily standups with pilot teams

3. **Full Pilot (8 weeks):**
   - Route 60% of eligible cases to shared services
   - Monitor guardrail metrics weekly
   - Weekly leadership reviews

4. **Adjustment (2 weeks):**
   - Analyze results
   - Make targeted adjustments
   - Prepare for either rollback or expansion

5. **Evaluation (2 weeks):**
   - Compare pilot vs. comparison group
   - Analyze segment-specific impacts
   - Prepare final recommendation

# 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation: Proceed with pilot first, with strict monitoring and adjustment protocols**

**Conditions for Full Rollout:**
1. Pilot demonstrates:
   - No guardrail breaches during pilot period
   - Specialist utilization <85% during normal operations
   - High-complexity case cycle time improves or remains stable
   - Staff satisfaction score 4.0/5 on role clarity
   - No disproportionate impact on small customer segments

2. Management commits to:
   - Maintaining local expertise through rotation programs
   - Implementing fairness monitoring for all customer segments
   - Establishing clear accountability frameworks for shared queues
   - Continuing to monitor tail latency and not just averages

**What Management Should Not Infer from Early Averages:**
1. **Stable average cycle time  success**: Early averages may mask tail latency problems, fairness issues, or hidden bottlenecks that only emerge over time.

2. **High specialist utilization  efficiency**: Utilization above 85% may indicate hidden overload rather than efficient use of resources, especially if specialists are absorbing additional tasks.

3. **Reduced handover frequency  better process**: Fewer handovers may indicate lost local expertise rather than process improvement if accompanied by increased rework or unnecessary specialist referrals.

4. **Improved North/South performance  universal benefit**: East may benefit differently, and small customer segments may be negatively impacted even if large segments improve.

5. **Short-term staff satisfaction  long-term acceptance**: Initial enthusiasm for standardized processes may give way to frustration with reduced autonomy and accountability gaps.

6. **Stable rework rates  quality maintained**: Rework may be processed quickly in shared services, masking quality issues that only become apparent in customer satisfaction metrics.

**Modifications to Consider Before Full Rollout:**
1. **Hybrid model**: Maintain part-time embedded specialists in North/South to preserve local expertise while still achieving utilization benefits.

2. **Priority routing**: Implement priority-based queue discipline to prevent starvation of high-complexity cases.

3. **Fairness safeguards**: Establish segment-specific SLAs and monitoring for small customer segments.

4. **Accountability framework**: Create clear ownership rules for shared queues to prevent accountability gaps.

5. **Learning preservation**: Implement rotation programs to maintain local expertise and prevent skill atrophy.

6. **Resilience planning**: Develop contingency plans for specialist absences that don't rely on cross-team handovers.

# Queue length monitoring
def monitor_queue_lengths(log, resource, time_window="1D"):
    """Monitor queue lengths over time for a specific resource"""
    df = pm4py.convert_to_dataframe(log)
    df = dataframe_utils.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df)
    queue_df = df[df["org:resource"] == resource].copy()
    queue_df["queue_length"] = queue_df.groupby(pd.Grouper(key="time:timestamp", freq=time_window))["case:concept:name"].transform("nunique")
    return queue_df[["time:timestamp", "queue_length"]].drop_duplicates()

# Specialist utilization monitoring
def monitor_specialist_utilization(log, specialists, time_window="1D"):
    """Monitor utilization of specialists over time"""
    df = pm4py.convert_to_dataframe(log)
    specialist_df = df[df["org:resource"].isin(specialists)]
    utilization = specialist_df.groupby([pd.Grouper(key="time:timestamp", freq=time_window), "org:resource"]).size()
    utilization = utilization.unstack().fillna(0)
    utilization["total_utilization"] = utilization.mean(axis=1)
    return utilization[["total_utilization"]]

# Rework analysis by activity
def analyze_rework_by_activity(log):
    """Analyze rework patterns by activity"""
    rework = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(log)
    rework = rework.sort_values("rework_rate", ascending=False)
    return rework

# Temporal feature extraction
def extract_temporal_features(log):
    """Extract temporal features for predictive monitoring"""
    df = pm4py.convert_to_dataframe(log)
    temporal_df = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(df)
    return temporal_df
