# 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

The main dependency chain is intake and classification → KYC and document collection → normalized financials and credit analysis → valuation when required → committee pack and decision when applicable → contract → signing → activation. KYC and document review often run concurrently even though the event log records a sequence; the actual join matters more than the apparent order. Not every case follows the committee or collateral path, so routing and controls must be applied by product, exposure and policy rather than inferred from one aggregate path.

- **Upstream data quality:** A1 must preserve identifiers and flag uncertain duplicates. A2 must route product and exposure correctly, because those attributes determine document, collateral and control requirements. A5 needs a maintainable requirements catalogue and a route for valid alternatives. A6 needs complete, legible documents and source-linked figures. A8 requires reconciled KYC status, statements, credit notes and any required valuation. A9 needs the authoritative decision and current contract rules. A11 depends on all required final statuses, not just a generated contract.
- **Control dependencies:** A12 can coordinate evidence and remediation, but a trusted enforcement point must block signing without KYC clearance and activation without all required controls. High-exposure SME assignments must enforce different people for credit analysis and committee packaging. A9 must check terms against the applicable committee decision and flag Credit-line renewal discrepancies. Any case without an applicable committee decision follows its authorized approval path; do not invent a decision. Compliance and legal owners approve rules, exceptions and changes. A10 and A11 remain gated until these controls work reliably.
- **Human capacity:** A3 and A4 route uncertain hits and mismatches to compliance specialists; neither closes them autonomously. The two senior specialists are concentrated dependency points in a three-person pool, so provide trained backup, coverage, prioritization and an exception queue. A5 and A6 need RM assistance and analyst review respectively. Reserve legal and operations capacity for remediation, especially at month end.
- **External valuation:** A7 can identify collateral requirements early and route or track requests in parallel with other work where policy and required information permit. The external valuer still performs the valuation; partner turnaround, quality and capacity remain dependencies. Committee packaging must wait for an acceptable valuation when required. This matters especially for Equipment finance, 71% of which requires valuation, versus 18% of Working-capital cases.
- **Integration:** In the recommended plan, reserve the first six months' two deep core-banking integrations for A12 and A6. A5 uses the existing portal and A4 uses a secure case workbench with controlled, permissioned extracts and explicit manual handoffs; neither assumes deep core integration. Verify that these interfaces can deliver sufficiently current data. If they cannot, narrow the pilots or change the integration allocation before rollout; do not treat a stale extract as a control gate. Later integrations remain subject to IT capacity and security approval.

| Bottleneck and synchronization | A5, A12, A4, A6 | Document completeness, KYC mismatch delays, analyst capacity and controlled readiness at joins | Defers the obvious registration win, automated valuation routing and downstream straight-through processing. Benefits depend on specialist and analyst capacity and on reliable control status. |
| Compliance first | A12, A4, A3, A9 | KYC investigation, remediation ownership and contract controls | Less direct attention to document loops, analyst normalization and external valuation; A3 may increase specialist workload. Each portfolio includes a control-strengthening module: A9's contract checks in the quick-win portfolio and A12 in the other two. In the quick-win option, pre-signing KYC and four-eyes controls must be established through existing independently verified controls or a prerequisite control project before enabling affected automation. If those cannot be demonstrated, hold the affected modules and use manual processing; the four-module limit is not a reason to waive a control.

# 4. Expected process-level effects

These are directional hypotheses, not additive savings. The median waits are activity-level observations, not an end-to-end critical-path calculation. Concurrent KYC and document work, differing case populations, conditional branches and overlap mean that activity frequencies, waits and directly-follows counts cannot simply be summed. Validate with case-level timestamps, actual parallel starts and joins, segment-specific variants and queue measurements. The directly-follows graph describes recorded adjacency, not proof of causation or complete business routing.

**Quick win — A1, A5, A9, A10.** Registration appears in position 2 for 98% of cases, and Receive → Register occurs 11,880 times, so A1 can save substantial back-office handling and reduce its 1.5-hour median pre-registration wait. That early gain may be absorbed by classification, KYC or analyst queues, especially as back-office utilization already falls below 65% after 15:00. A5 can reduce the 3,300 Document review → Request missing documents transitions and the 2,800 recorded returns to review, with fewer repeat requests and RM handovers. Customer response remains outside the bank's direct control. A9 can reduce contract rework, including the 650 Contract preparation → Remediate control issue and 510 recorded return transitions, if decision data is correct. A10 may reduce the 44-hour median wait before signing and the 21% reminder loop, subject to customer adoption and identity checks. Analyst, legal and activation demand can rise or bunch; month-end legal capacity needs planning. Contract checks and a verified pre-signing gate should reduce violations, but faster flow without those gates could amplify them. The 6.5% provisional-signing finding must be addressed before automating signing.

**Bottleneck and synchronization — A5, A12, A4, A6.** A5 addresses missing-document loops and RM handovers as above. A4 targets 1,750 mismatch activities with a 31-hour median prior wait and 38% exception rate; it is on the critical path in 82% of cases where it appears. The KYC → mismatch → KYC return occurs 1,750 and 1,160 times respectively, while 380 recorded mismatch → contract transitions deserve investigation for status and control interpretation. Faster resolution should reduce waiting for affected cases, but case-level benefit depends on whether the concurrent financial branch is also ready. A6 targets 5,200 normalization activities, 75 minutes mean service and 20 hours median waiting, and the 5,000 Normalize → Credit analysis transitions. It can free analyst time for judgment and exceptions, helping absorb cleaner inflow. A12 makes blockers and responsibility visible before contracts and signing, reducing remediation handovers and potentially the 8.5% overall contract-remediation loop; verify the 14% Credit-line renewal rate separately. Specialist investigation and initial remediation may increase as hidden issues surface. External valuation's 58-hour median wait and weekly committee cadence still limit eligible cases; A12 supports control enforcement but does not itself shorten those external waits. With validated gates and assignments, provisional signing and four-eyes breaches should fall, while compliance retains final responsibility.

**Compliance first — A12, A4, A3, A9.** A4 can shorten mismatch waits; A3 may reduce routine KYC handling but may also generate more escalations to the three specialists and concentrate work further on the two senior specialists. A12 clarifies handovers and makes incomplete control evidence visible; A9 can reduce contract preparation and the remediation loop when rules and decision data are reliable. Together they can reduce signing-before-clearance, decision/contract mismatches and the 5.8% High-exposure four-eyes violation rate, provided gates and assignments are enforced. Financial document review's 18-hour median wait and 24% exception rate, missing-document loops, normalization and valuation waits remain largely unaddressed, so cycle-time improvement may be modest where those branches determine the join. The committee's weekly cadence and legal month-end workload can continue to create queues.

# 5. Risks and compensating controls

- **False negatives and false positives:** Validate A3 against adjudicated hits, especially consequential misses, and A4 against verified entity matches. Use conservative thresholds, independent review of uncertain or high-risk cases, sampling of apparently clear cases, and explicit escalation service levels. Measure both missed risks and avoidable escalations; never silently clear a hit solely on a model suggestion.
- **Extraction, routing and rule errors:** Show A6 source evidence and confidence alongside extracted values; require analyst verification for low-confidence, poor-quality or unusual documents. Maintain product/exposure and document rules with accountable owners; send ambiguity to people. Reconcile A9 terms against the authoritative decision and maintain versioned, legally approved rules, including renewal cases.
- **Auditability and drift:** Record input and source references, model and rule versions, suggestion, confidence where applicable, human reviewer, override and reason, final decision, timestamps and control evidence in an access-controlled, tamper-evident audit trail. Monitor accuracy and override rates by product, exposure, document quality and time; periodically revalidate models and rules, investigate drift and suspend or roll back if performance degrades.
- **Exception handling and shifted demand:** Define ownership, priority, coverage, escalation and fallback for unresolved matches, missing documents, low-quality PDFs, inconsistent artifacts and failed integrations. Track queue age and staffing, not just automated throughput. Provide alternative customer channels and RM support; throttle intake or rollout if specialist, analyst or legal queues grow.
- **Four-eyes and legal sequencing:** Check person identity and roles across credit analysis and committee packaging for High-exposure SME cases, including substitutes and month-end work; prevent the same person completing both and audit attempted overrides. Enforce KYC clearance before signing, with a fail-closed response to missing or stale status. Block activation until all required controls and signing evidence are complete. Investigate provisional-signing cases and use authorized remediation, not an automated retrospective clearance.
- **External and operational failure:** Monitor valuer turnaround and quality with partner escalation and backup capacity. Reconcile portal and workbench data with authoritative systems; use manual verification if an interface fails. Control access, protect customer data and exercise rollback and business-continuity procedures.

# 6. Recommended phased rollout

The recommended first-year modules are A5, A12, A4 and A6. The sequence below is conditional on readiness; the phases can overlap where dependencies and capacity allow.

1. **Months 0–2: establish the baseline and launch A5.** Map case-level parallel work, conditional variants and true join times; validate data quality and control status with compliance, legal, operations and RMs. Segment by Micro, SME, High-exposure SME and product. Agree staffing, a requirements catalogue, customer alternatives and ownership of exceptions. Pilot A5 on a limited set of products through the existing portal; train RMs to give consistent instructions. Start A12 design and policy reconciliation. Gate expansion of A5 on accurate checklists, manageable exception queues, customer usability and no material rise in repeated requests or abandonment.
2. **Months 2–4: deploy A12 as the first deep core integration.** Connect or reliably verify authoritative control and decision status, establish remediation owners, and implement tested enforcement at the actual signing and activation points. Configure and test the High-exposure four-eyes check, including month-end coverage. Pilot with a manual fallback and investigate the existing provisional-signing and contract-remediation populations. Gate wider release on end-to-end evidence that missing or stale KYC status blocks signing, incomplete controls block activation, role conflicts are caught, and audit records and escalation ownership are complete. If enforcement cannot be proven, retain manual controls and hold dependent signing or activation automation.
3. **Months 3–6: pilot A4 using a secure non-deep interface.** Route a bounded mismatch population to specialists with source evidence and human adjudication. Train backup reviewers for the two senior specialists and protect time for difficult cases. Gate expansion on validated match quality, no unacceptable false clearance, timely escalation and stable specialist queue age. Verify data freshness; use manual verification or narrow the pilot when status cannot be trusted.
4. **Months 4–6: pilot A6 as the second deep core integration.** Start with supported statement types and analyst review, then expand only after source-linked accuracy is validated across document quality and segments. Reserve analyst capacity for exceptions and judgment; release additional upstream volume gradually. Gate expansion on acceptable extraction accuracy and override rates, no deterioration in credit-analysis queue age, and a working fallback for poor PDFs.
5. **Months 7–12: scale the four modules only where gates remain satisfied.** Review partner valuation SLAs and consider earlier manual valuation requests where policy permits; schedule committee and legal capacity around ready-case arrival and month-end peaks. Evaluate A2 or A7 for a future investment, and defer A8–A11 automation until their data and control prerequisites are demonstrated. Any later deep integration is subject to IT capacity; no additional first-year module is assumed in the four-module budget.

Monitor weekly during pilots and monthly after stabilization: end-to-end elapsed time and tail percentiles by product and exposure; time from each branch's completion to committee readiness; queue age and throughput for compliance specialists, analysts, legal and operations; mismatch and document-loop rates; customer response, completion and abandonment; extraction accuracy and overrides; valuation turnaround and late rate; committee-ready-to-decision time; and contract remediation by product. Track attempted and actual KYC-before-signing and four-eyes breaches, incomplete-control activation attempts, evidence completeness, audit and override coverage. Compare with the pre-pilot baseline and a suitable contemporaneous comparison where feasible. Gate each expansion on sustained control effectiveness, acceptable exception quality and no unmanageable downstream queue growth; pause, throttle or revert to the manual process if these conditions fail. Control targets and tolerances must be approved by their accountable owners before launch.

# 7. Final recommendation

Choose the **bottleneck and synchronization portfolio: A5, A12, A4 and A6**. It attacks both sides of a frequent join: incomplete financial documents and slow KYC mismatches, while protecting analyst capacity and establishing control ownership. It also gives the bank a concrete response to signing, four-eyes and contract-remediation findings. The portfolio does not solve external valuation or weekly committee timing; manage those operationally and reassess A7 after measuring the remaining constraint.

Do not put A1 first solely because registration is frequent. Its very high feasibility and labor benefit make it a credible later investment, but registration's 1.5-hour median wait is small relative to mismatch, document, normalization and downstream waits, and its back-office pool has spare capacity later in the day. Accelerating intake before compliance and analysts can absorb it may simply move the queue. A5 offers a visible, feasible early win while reducing repeated work; A12, A4 and A6 address consequential controls and capacity. If measurement later shows registration is constraining flow and downstream queues are stable, A1 becomes a strong next candidate.
