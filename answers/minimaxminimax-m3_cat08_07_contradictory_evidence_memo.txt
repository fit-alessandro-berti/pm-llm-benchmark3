# 3. Explanation of Metric Conflicts

**High fitness, low precision.**
Fitness measures how much of the observed behavior the model reproduces. Under Redesign R, more cases fit the simplified coordinator path, so fitness rises. Precision penalizes the model's allowance of behavior not actually observed. Because Redesign R permits skipped surveys, manual activations, and billing-before-activation as legal moves, the model must admit many behaviors it does not need to — precision falls. The combination indicates a *more uniform* but *less disciplined* process, not necessarily a better one.

**Improved median, worse tail.**
Median captures the typical simple house, which Redesign R serves well. The tail is dominated by apartments and old buildings, where the skip rule creates rework, missed permits, and second visits, and where post-billing activation produces late complaints. The two metrics describe different populations responding to different parts of the change. A single number that aggregates them hides the segment structure — exactly what the segment table exposes.

**Fewer handovers, lower resilience.**
Handovers of work dropped (cases with 3+ handovers: 34%  12%), and the social-network analysis shows simpler handover topology. But the *working-together* network is more centralized: when a coordinator is unavailable, the case waits because context is siloed. Reduced handovers reduce coordination overhead in the happy path but reduce redundancy in the unhappy path. The two effects are simultaneous because they are different dimensions of the same organizational change.

**Lower visible cost, higher downstream cost.**
Mean pre-complaint cost fell (EUR 184  161) because fewer field surveys and simpler scheduling reduce direct effort. But the second-visit team is now running at 92%, manual activation has more than doubled, and complaint escalations after billing have grown 3%  8%. The EUR 207 estimate is incomplete (no data after week 4). Visible cost captures what the redesign optimized; downstream cost captures what it broke. The redesign saved euros on the activity it eliminated and spent euros on the activities it triggered.

**Simple houses vs. apartments/old buildings.**
The skip rule's predictive model was tuned (or assumed to be tuned) on the simple-house distribution. When the same rule is applied to medium-risk apartments and permit-sensitive old buildings, the false-negative rate for "needs survey / needs permit" rises. Each segment is therefore an unbiased test of a different hypothesis: for simple houses, the redesign works; for complex cases, the risk classifier is the bottleneck, not the process steps.
