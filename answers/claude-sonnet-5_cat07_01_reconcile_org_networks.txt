# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

**Handover-of-work network.** This network is built from direct succession of activities performed by *different* resources within the same case. Mira's very high out/in-degree and betweenness (0.82 / 0.51 / 0.77) mean that a large share of process paths pass *through* her as a routing point, especially between the automated triage layer and specialists/adjusters. It does **not** imply she personally resolves cases, adds value, or has formal authority — only that work items structurally flow via her. It also does not distinguish "value-adding coordination" from "queueing delay."

**Working-together network.** This captures sustained joint case involvement (co-occurrence across many events on the same case), which tends to be high for stable dyads that share deep, multi-step work (e.g., team-lead/adjuster pairs). Mira's low co-occurrence does **not** mean she is uninvolved; it means her touches are short, single-event interventions (routing, reopening, escalation) rather than embedded participation. The metric is biased toward "thick" collaboration and structurally under-represents brokers whose value is brevity and reach, not depth.

**Subcontracting network (n=2).** This detects delegate-and-return patterns (ABA). Mira's high subcontracting betweenness with Omar/Priya shows she hands off a bounded sub-task to a specialist and the case later re-enters her orbit (reopen/assign). This is consistent with an orchestration or gatekeeping function, but the pattern alone cannot distinguish deliberate delegation from coincidental re-routing caused by queue backlogs.

**Performance/time statistics.** Her short median service time (4.2 min) confirms her activities are administrative/decisional rather than execution-heavy. Elevated exception-flag share (0.48) shows her caseload is skewed toward harder cases, but correlation is not proof of causation — she may attract exceptions because she is *assigned* to them, not because her presence *creates* them. High variance in cycle-time contribution suggests inconsistent effect: sometimes she accelerates flow, sometimes cases wait on her.

**Role-discovery / activity-profile similarity.** Her activity mix (Request Missing Information, Reopen Claim, Escalate Complaint, Assign Specialist) does not overlap cleanly with adjusters, team leads, or specialists — a similarity/clustering analysis would likely place her in her own cluster or a small "exception-handling" cluster with Bot_Rules downstream neighbors. This supports the idea of a distinct organizational function, but activity-label clustering says nothing about authority, discretion, or whether the role is formally sanctioned.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

## Formal coordinator / exception desk
- **Mechanism:** Mira is the designated intake point for anything that cannot follow the standard Bot_Rules  Team Lead  Adjuster path; she screens, requests missing information, and reassigns.
- **Supporting evidence:** Her core activities align exactly with an exception-desk function; she touches 64% of reopens and 71% of complaints; Bot_Rules routes directly to her for exception cases.
- **Contradicting/limiting evidence:** The process owner explicitly denies she has formal team-lead status; she is peripheral in working-together with both team leads, which would be unusual if she were an officially integrated coordinator.
- **Predictions:** Stable routing rules over time, consistent presence in SOP/case-management documentation, and stable (not growing) centrality across periods if the role is formally staffed and backed up.

## Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency
- **Mechanism:** Because she is a structural cut-vertex for exception-flagged flow, her limited capacity throttles a specific subset of cases even though aggregate volume looks fine.
- **Supporting evidence:** During her 6-day absence, median cycle time was unaffected but the 90th percentile for exception cases rose from 8.4 to 13.9 days; her handover betweenness (0.77) is far above anyone else's.
- **Contradicting/limiting evidence:** Median case flow was untouched, showing she is not a bottleneck for the majority of volume; team leads retain their own triage/approval channels independent of her.
- **Predictions:** Repeat absences should reproduce the same tail-latency spike specifically in exception/complaint subpopulations; introducing a trained backup should flatten the effect in future logs.

## Organizational boundary spanner / triage broker
- **Mechanism:** She functions as a bridge between silos (Team North, Team South, specialists, vendors, bots), making brief high-leverage decisions rather than doing sustained casework — a classic broker profile (high betweenness, low clustering).
- **Supporting evidence:** Subcontracting fragments show delegate-then-reclaim patterns to Omar/Priya; qualitative comments from adjusters ("knows where hard cases should go") describe exactly a brokerage function; her low working-together score is explainable by brief, cross-cutting ties rather than absence of engagement.
- **Contradicting/limiting evidence:** Overlaps substantially with the "formal coordinator" hypothesis and is hard to separate from it using network shape alone; broker status could be an emergent, informal accretion rather than a designed role.
- **Predictions:** If she is a genuine structural broker, removing her should shift betweenness to another resource (e.g., team leads) rather than eliminate the position's need — the "bridge" role should persist even if the person changes.

## Data artifact / shared-queue account (additional)
- **Mechanism:** Some or all "Mira_Coordinator" events could represent activity performed under a shared exception-queue account (multiple staff, or automated logging tagged to one login) rather than a single individual's discretionary judgment.
- **Supporting evidence:** Extremely short, uniform service times (4.2 min median) across nearly 1,000 distinct cases is consistent with templated/queue-tool actions; the additional "Assign Specialist" activity does not appear in the canonical 14-activity list, suggesting a possible logging/taxonomy inconsistency.
- **Contradicting evidence:** Her handover partners vary meaningfully by case type (medical vs. legal), which suggests real case-specific judgment rather than a static script.
- **Predictions:** Inspecting timestamp regularity, lifecycle-transition patterns, and whether "Mira_Coordinator" maps to one or multiple underlying user IDs would confirm or refute this.

## Quality gatekeeper (additional)
- **Mechanism:** She pre-screens exception-flagged cases before they reach specialists, filtering out cases that don't need escalation and reducing specialist overload.
- **Supporting evidence:** Her caseload's exception share (0.48) is far above baseline, and the Escalate Complaint  Legal Review  Reopen fragment suggests screening before formal specialist involvement.
- **Contradicting evidence:** Her recorded activities (request info, assign) suggest facilitation rather than an approval/veto step; no direct evidence she blocks referrals.
- **Predictions:** If true, specialist (Omar/Priya) utilization should rise disproportionately during her absence, independent of raw case volume.

# 3. Risk assessment

- **Bottleneck risk:** Concentrated and demonstrated, but scoped narrowly to exception/complaint/reopen subpopulations — not a general-process bottleneck (median cycle time was stable during her absence).
- **Coordination risk:** Moderate. Standard team-lead/adjuster workflows appear self-sufficient (strong working-together ties independent of Mira), so coordination risk is largely confined to non-standard cases lacking an obvious fallback router.
- **Key-person risk:** High for the exception-handling function specifically. No resource currently shares her network position; no evidence of a trained backup or redundancy.
- **Compliance/control risk:** The concern that she both detects issues and assigns the next handler is a legitimate structural observation (concentration of discretion), but there is no evidence in the data of misuse — this is a control-design gap, not a demonstrated integrity failure.
- **Workload fairness risk:** Her exception load and Omar's/Priya's slow, high-exception caseloads suggest concentration of hard cases on a few individuals; conversely, temps handle low-exception, low-complexity work — this may be by design (skill-based routing) or may indicate uneven burden; cannot be distinguished from current data alone.
- **Data-quality risk:** The unlisted "Assign Specialist" activity and the very short, uniform durations raise a legitimate question about whether all "Mira_Coordinator" events represent one person's discretionary decisions or a mix of logging conventions/shared tooling.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira_Coordinator looks like two different people depending on which network you view: in the workflow (handover) network she sits at the center of exception routing; in the collaboration (working-together) network she looks marginal. Both are true — her value comes from short, high-leverage routing and escalation decisions, not from sustained casework, which naturally makes her central in one measure and peripheral in another.
