## 1. Interaction-graph description

The plant is an object-centric interaction graph, not a set of six independent job traces. Nodes are typed instances: six Jobs (`J1`–`J6`), six Components (`C1`–`C6`), two ComponentLots (`L7`, `L8`), three Machines (`CNC-2`, `OVEN-1`, `TEST-1`), three Tools (`FIX-A`, `RACK-7`, `CAL-3`), two Batches (`B1`, `B2`), and three Shipments (`S1`–`S3`).

Directed edges carry qualifiers from the O2O table and from event-to-object relations:

- `Job —requires→ Component`
- `ComponentLot —contains→ Component`
- `Batch —batches→ Job` (and, via the 12:05 `Batch Cure` event, also touches Machine, Tool, and Components)
- `Shipment —consolidates→ Job`
- Job/Machine use-and-release (counted as 12 Job–Machine interactions)
- Job/Tool acquire–hold–release (15 Job–Tool interactions; some holds inferred)
- Tool–Machine co-occurrence on the same event (`RACK-7` with `OVEN-1`, `FIX-A` with `CNC-2`, `CAL-3` with `TEST-1`)

**Higher-order motifs (more than two object types)**

1. **Lot–component–job fork.** `L7 —contains→ {C2, C5}` and `{J2, J5} —requires→ {C2, C5}`. A single `Quarantine Lot` / `Release Lot` pair (10:20–13:50) therefore sits on two otherwise unrelated jobs. `L8` analogously ties `J1`/`C1` to `J3`/`C3`.
2. **Batch–oven–rack barrier.** `B1 —batches→ {J1, J2, J3}` and the 12:05 `Batch Cure` relates `B1`, `OVEN-1`, `RACK-7`, those three jobs, and `C1`–`C3`. The same rack then gates `B2` with `{J4, J5}`. Jobs, a batch, a machine, a tool, and components form one synchronization clique.
3. **Fixture–machine–batch–shipment cascade.** `J3` holds `FIX-A` on `CNC-2` until 11:40; `J4` cannot start `Machine Process` until 11:45 on the same fixture and machine. `J3` is also the last arrival into `B1` (ready 11:55). Late `J4` is later paired with already-packed `J1` in `S1`. Five object types (`Job`, `Tool`, `Machine`, `Batch`, `Shipment`) close a path from `J3`’s fixture hold to `J1`’s dispatch wait.
4. **Tester–calibrator–job queue.** Every `Final Test` needs both `TEST-1` and `CAL-3`. `J5` occupies the pair 16:10–16:35; `CAL-3` then recalibrates 16:35–17:20 while `TEST-1` is idle; `J6` starts only at 17:25.

**Observed versus inferred edges**

Directly observed: the listed O2O qualifiers; the 12:05 multi-object `Batch Cure`; `Quarantine Lot` / `Release Lot` on `L7` with `C2`, `C5`, `J2`, `J5`; acquire/release of `FIX-A`; pack and dispatch timestamps; `Final Test` and recalibration intervals.

Inferred, not logged as waiting: “waiting attributable to batch synchronization if readiness is trusted”; `J4` blocked by `FIX-A` (there is no `Wait for Tool`); `J6` blocked by `CAL-3` rather than by `TEST-1` capacity; shipment-partner waiting after pack; the causal chain from `J3`’s fixture hold to `S1`’s late dispatch.

**Compact edge list**

```
J1-requires-C1; J2-requires-C2; J3-requires-C3; J4-requires-C4; J5-requires-C5; J6-requires-C6
L7-contains-C2; L7-contains-C5; L8-contains-C1; L8-contains-C3
B1-batches-{J1,J2,J3}; B2-batches-{J4,J5}
S1-consolidates-{J1,J4}; S2-consolidates-{J2,J3}; S3-consolidates-{J5,J6}
B1 --BatchCure@12:05--> {OVEN-1, RACK-7, C1, C2, C3}
B2 --BatchCure@14:50--> {OVEN-1, RACK-7}
J3 --holds 09:00-11:40--> FIX-A --needed_by--> J4@CNC-2
L7 --Quarantine 10:20 / Release 13:50--> {C2,C5,J2,J5}
{J5,J6} --FinalTest requires--> {TEST-1, CAL-3}
```

The graph is sparse in Job–Job edges. Coupling is almost entirely *indirect*, through Batch, Tool, ComponentLot, and Shipment nodes. That is why job-flattened DFGs look “broadly normal” while waiting remains unpredictable.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

All figures below are time differences under stated counterfactuals. Temporal order and resource occupancy make the waits *feasible*; they do not prove a unique cause.

**C1. B1 synchronization wait for `J1`.**  
Ready 08:10, `B1` starts 12:05. Difference: **3 h 55 min**.  
Counterfactual: if a batch (or a single-job oven cycle) could start when `J1` was ready, or when the first two of `{J1, J2, J3}` were ready, that interval would not be charged to `J1`. The 10 min from `J3`’s readiness (11:55) to start is ordinary setup; the remaining 3 h 45 min is the pacemaker gap created by waiting for `J3`.

**C2. B1 synchronization wait for `J2`.**  
Ready 08:25, start 12:05. Difference: **3 h 40 min**.  
Same counterfactual as C1. `J2` had already entered `B1` despite `L7` still being under quarantine (supervisor override). If readiness is trusted, almost all of this interval is batch sync, not lot hold.

**C3. Fixture-associated wait for `J4`.**  
`J4` reaches `CNC-2` at 09:50. `CNC-2` is idle 10:05–10:47. `FIX-A` is held by `J3` until 11:40. `J4`’s `Machine Process` starts 11:45 (5 min after fixture release) and relates to `J4`, `C4`, `CNC-2`, `FIX-A`.  
Idle-start counterfactual (`CNC-2` free at 10:05 and `FIX-A` available): start 10:05 versus 11:45 → **1 h 40 min**.  
Arrival counterfactual (process could start at 09:50): **1 h 55 min**.  
The 10:05–10:47 machine-idle window shows the machine was not the binding constraint for at least 42 min of that wait. No `Wait for Tool` event exists; the attribution to `FIX-A` is inferred from hold overlap plus the 5 min offset from release to start.

**C4. `S1` consolidation wait for `J1`.**  
`J1` packed 13:30; `J4` packed 19:10; `S1` dispatches 19:25. Pack-to-dispatch for `J1`: **5 h 55 min**.  
Counterfactual: if `S1` followed the same last-job slack as `S2`/`S3` (25–35 min after the *early* job), dispatch would be about 13:55–14:05. Excess versus a 30 min handling allowance: **5 h 25 min**. Versus `S2`’s 25 min slack: **5 h 30 min**. The shipment-flattened DFG (`Create Shipment → Load → Dispatch`, median Load→Dispatch 0.3 h) does not contain this number.

**C5. `CAL-3`-associated wait for `J6`.**  
`J5` ready 16:00, tests 16:10–16:35. `CAL-3` recalibrated 16:35–17:20 (45 min). `J6` ready 16:05, tests 17:25–17:50. `TEST-1` idle 16:35–17:25.  
Total ready-to-start for `J6`: **1 h 20 min**. Recalibration/idle gap after `J5` finishes: **50 min**.  
Counterfactual: if `Final Test` did not require a post-`J5` recalibration of `CAL-3`, `J6` could start at 16:35 (or immediately after a short changeover). The extra 50 min is then associated with the calibrator lifecycle, not with `TEST-1` capacity (the tester is idle). Sequential use of `TEST-1` by `J5` still explains ~30 min (16:05–16:35) even without recalibration.

**C6. `B2` / `RACK-7` decomposition (additional).**  
`J4` ready 13:10, `J5` ready 14:40, `B2` starts 14:50. `J4` wait: **1 h 40 min**. `OVEN-1` idle 12:55–14:50 = **1 h 55 min**. `RACK-7` remains in cleaning/quarantine until 14:35, so **1 h 40 min** of oven idle precedes rack release; then 5 min until `J5` is ready and 10 min until start.  
Counterfactual A: rack available at 12:55 and `J5` ready with `J4` → oven could load near 13:10, removing most of `J4`’s 1 h 40 min.  
Counterfactual B: rack still dirty until 14:35 but `J5` ready earlier → start could move to ~14:35, saving `J4` only **15 min**. Both resource and peer-job constraints bind; the split is not identified from timestamps alone.

**C7. Lot clock versus job clocks.**  
`L7` quarantine 10:20–13:50 = **3 h 30 min**. `J5` is not ready for `B2` until 14:40 (**50 min** after release). `J2` is already inside `B1` at 12:05, 1 h 45 min *before* lot release. Treating the lot interval as a uniform job delay would overstate `J2` and understate that `J5` also needs post-release staging.

**Compound (not separately causal).** `J1` accumulates C1 (3 h 55 min) plus C4 (5 h 55 min) = **9 h 50 min** of synchronization-like waiting on two different object types, while no job-level duration exceeds 1.8 SD of the six-job mean. That is possible only if other jobs are similarly stretched by different objects (`J4` fixture + `B2`, `J5` lot + batch, `J6` calibrator), so the job-flattened distribution stays compact.

**Why not rank machines first.** The brief is that traditional flattenings look normal and machine utilization is moderate. Ranking capacity first would ignore the idle-while-waiting pattern that is the entire point of the object-centric evidence.

**Alternative ranking (policy-first).** If the KPI is *finished-job waiting after value-add is done*, `S1` (rank 2) outranks `B1` because 5 h 55 min post-pack exceeds `J1`’s 3 h 55 min pre-cure wait. If the KPI is *in-process coupling that creates cascading lateness*, `B1` plus `FIX-A` stay ahead because they delay `J4`, which then loads `S1`. Both rankings are consistent with the same graph.

## 4. What flattening misses

**Job viewpoint.** Visible: a tidy DFG (`Release → Stage → Machine → Inspect` with a small rework loop, then `Pack → Dispatch`); six complete traces; no job beyond 1.8 SD of the mean. Hidden: `Quarantine Lot` / `Release Lot`; fixture waits; who is pacemaker in `B1`/`B2`; packed-job wait inside `S1`. Duplicated: inspect/rework looks like a job-quality problem (2 of 6) even if some rework is lot-driven. Misleading conclusion: “flow is stable and balanced.” The 1.1 h median `Stage→Machine` and 1.1 h `Pack→Dispatch` are mixtures of near-zero waits (`J3` into `B1`, `S2`/`S3`) and multi-hour sync waits (`J1`/`J2` into `B1`, `J1` in `S1`).

**Machine viewpoint.** Visible: utilization and native sequences (CNC setup–process–release; oven load–cure–unload; test calibrate–test–release); modest “longest gaps” (48 / 73 / 39 min). Hidden: *why* a gap exists (`FIX-A` held by `J3`, `RACK-7` in quarantine, `CAL-3` recalibrating). Duplicated: each batch cure is one oven event but three job completions, so oven productivity and job throughput disagree. Misleading conclusion: “no severe machine bottleneck” (true as a capacity statement, false as an explanation of waiting). Also, recorded machine gaps understate object-centric idle (`OVEN-1` 12:55–14:50 is 115 min, not 73; `TEST-1` 16:35–17:25 is 50 min, not 39), so even the slack is under-reported.

**Batch viewpoint.** Visible: synchronization barrier; `B1` start 12:05 vs. staggered readiness; `B2` start 14:50. Hidden: that `B1`’s pacemaker `J3` was late because of `FIX-A` on `CNC-2`; that `B2` waits on both `RACK-7` until 14:35 and `J5` until 14:40 after an `L7` release at 13:50. Duplicated: the single 12:05 event is one batch activity and three job activities. Misleading conclusion: “batching is the bottleneck” — incomplete without the upstream fixture and lot story.

**Tool viewpoint.** Visible: `FIX-A` 09:00–11:40; `RACK-7` used by both batches and dirty until 14:35; `CAL-3` on every final test plus 16:35–17:20 recalibration. Hidden: jobs that wait without an acquire event (`J4` has no `Wait for Tool`); shipments downstream of a fixture-delayed job. Duplicated: tool holds can be double-counted with machine processing. Misleading conclusion: tools are accessories. They are the objects that convert idle machines into waiting jobs.

**ComponentLot viewpoint.** Visible: `L7` quarantine/release spanning `C2` and `C5`. Hidden or distorted: `J2`’s override path; `L8` has no timed quality events in the excerpt, so lots look unevenly instrumented. Duplicated: if an integrator *does* attach lot events to jobs, `J2`/`J5` traces gain activities that `J1`/`J3`/`J4`/`J6` lack, breaking DFG comparability. Misleading conclusion: either “lots do not affect jobs” (current extraction) or “both lot members were equally blocked” (naive re-linking). Neither is true.

**Shipment viewpoint.** Visible: three short `Create → Load → Dispatch` traces; medians 0.8 h and 0.3 h. Hidden: `J1` packed 13:30 waiting for `J4` packed 19:10. The log “does not represent how long an already packed job waits for its shipment partners.” Misleading conclusion: shipping is fast. For `J1`, shipping is the longest single wait in the file.

**Why low machine utilization coexists with long job waiting.** A job waits on the *conjunction* of objects it needs (machine ∧ tool ∧ batch peers ∧ lot state ∧ shipment partners). The machine can be idle because another conjunct is false: `CNC-2` idle while `FIX-A` is on `J3`; `OVEN-1` idle while `RACK-7` is in cleaning/quarantine and `J5` is not ready; `TEST-1` idle while `CAL-3` recalibrates. Utilization measures only the machine conjunct. Waiting time measures the first time all conjuncts are true. Flattening to machines reports the idle; flattening to jobs reports a delay with no pointer to the missing conjunct.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**Narrative A — `J3` / `FIX-A` / `CNC-2` / `B1` / `J1` / `S1`.**  
Temporal precedence: `J3` acquires `FIX-A` 09:00, releases 11:40; `J4` is at `CNC-2` from 09:50; `J4` process 11:45; `J3` ready for batch 11:55; `B1` cures 12:05; `J1` packed 13:30; `J4` packed 19:10; `S1` dispatch 19:25.  
Resource feasibility: `FIX-A` cannot be on `J3` and `J4` at once; `B1` as logged is one cure for three jobs; `S1` consolidates `J1` and `J4`.  
Policy constraints: batch-full start; shipment consolidation; no logged `Wait for Tool`.  
Speculation (not proved): `J3`’s fixture cycle *causes* `J3` to be `B1`’s pacemaker and *causes* `J4`’s CNC delay, which *causes* `S1` to hold `J1`. A longer CNC program on `J3`, a missing component `C3`, or an unlogged quality step could equally explain 09:00–11:40. The cascade is a consistent reading of the graph, not a unique identification.

**Narrative B — `L7` / `C5` / `J5` / `B2` / `RACK-7` / `OVEN-1` / `J4`.**  
Precedence: `Quarantine Lot` 10:20 on `L7,C2,C5,J2,J5`; `Release Lot` 13:50; `J4` ready 13:10 (already waiting); rack clean 14:35; `J5` ready 14:40; `B2` 14:50; oven idle since 12:55.  
Feasibility: `J5` needs `C5` from `L7`; `B2` needs both jobs and `RACK-7` on `OVEN-1`.  
Policy: lot release before `J5` proceeds; rack cleaning/quarantine; batching `J4` with `J5`.  
Speculation: that the lot hold is why `J5` is the `B2` pacemaker, and therefore why `J4` waits 1 h 40 min. Competing binders are the rack until 14:35 (only 15 min before start) and whatever `J5` does in the 50 min after release. `J2`’s override shows lot policy is not applied uniformly, so using `L7` as a hard causal lock on every member is unsafe.

**Narrative C — `J5` / `CAL-3` / `TEST-1` / `J6` / `S3`.**  
Precedence: both ready for test ~16:00–16:05; `J5` 16:10–16:35; recalibration 16:35–17:20; `J6` 17:25–17:50; `S3` ships 35 min after its last pack.  
Feasibility: every `Final Test` requires `CAL-3` as well as `TEST-1`; the tester is idle during recalibration, so tester capacity is not the scarce conjunct in that window.  
Policy: post-test recalibration; `S3` consolidates `J5` and `J6` (here the late job is `J6`, so consolidation mainly hurts `J5` if `J5` packs first — pack times for these two are not given, only the 35 min last-job slack).  
Speculation: recalibration is caused by `J5`’s test rather than a calendar slot; `J6` could have tested first and shifted the wait onto `J5`. Order is not identified.

Across narratives, **precedence is dense, feasibility is clear, policy is partly explicit, causation is under-identified.** The same timestamps support several binders at once (`B2`: lot + rack + peer job). That is why the brief forbids a single bottleneck.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

**I1. Reduce batch synchronization (smaller batches / start-when-ready / extra rack).**  
Target: `B1`/`B2` full-set waits.  
Benefit: remove most of C1–C2 (up to ~3 h 45 min for `J1`/`J2`) and part of `J4`’s 1 h 40 min if `B2` can start at 13:10.  
Helped: `J1`,`J2`,`J4` and any early-ready future jobs.  
Harmed: oven changeover count rises; `J3`/`J5` may lose “free” ride-along if partial racks are deprioritized.  
New cost/risk: extra `RACK-*` inventory and qualification; possible cure-quality variation on partial loads.  
Perverse metric: “oven utilization” or “jobs per cure” — staff will re-batch to keep the metric, restoring sync waits.  
OCEL evaluation: compare ready-to-`Batch Cure` per job, stratified by batch size; keep `Batch` as an object and do not flatten waits into job cycle time only.

**I2. Duplicate or pre-stage `FIX-A` (and record tool waits).**  
Target: fixture contention.  
Benefit: `J4` can use `CNC-2` in the 10:05–10:47 idle window; C3’s 1 h 40 min shrinks. If `J4` then packs earlier, `S1` may release `J1` earlier (spillover benefit).  
Helped: `J4`,`S1`,`J1`.  
Harmed: `J3` if a second fixture is lower precision; other jobs if the spare is taken from another cell.  
Risk: capital and calibration of a second fixture.  
Perverse metric: “CNC utilization” — may encourage starting `J4` without a qualified fixture.  
OCEL evaluation: add `Wait for Tool` events related to `{Job, Tool, Machine}`; difference-in-differences on `acquire→process` start for jobs requiring `FIX-A`.

**I3. Shipment policy: dispatch on first packed job, or re-pair by ready-time rather than static `{J1,J4}`.**  
Target: consolidation wait.  
Benefit: C4’s 5 h 55 min for `J1` falls toward the 25–35 min slack of `S2`/`S3`.  
Helped: early-ready members of mixed shipments (`J1` in this sample).  
Harmed: fill rates, freight cost, `J4` if it loses a dedicated truck; customer receiving if split deliveries are forbidden.  
Risk: more shipments (`S1` splits into two).  
Perverse metric: “shipments per day” or “truck fill” — operations will re-consolidate and restore `J1`-like waits.  
OCEL evaluation: for each `Shipment`, compute `dispatch − min(pack)` and `dispatch − max(pack)` on related jobs; a policy win shrinks the first without inflating the second.

**I4. Logging and extraction (no operational change).**  
Target: data-integration artifacts (rank 5) that hide ranks 1–4.  
Benefit: lot events linked consistently to jobs *and* lots; explicit wait activities; shipment DFG that includes post-pack dwell; machine gaps reconciled with tool calendars (oven 115 min vs. 73 min; TEST-1 50 min vs. 39 min).  
Helped: analysts, and indirectly every job whose wait is currently unattributed.  
Harmed: none operationally; event stores grow; naive DFGs may look *worse* after waits become visible (political risk).  
Risk: double-relating lot events to jobs without marking them as lot-level (the brief’s alternative pipeline).  
Perverse metric: “event completeness” counted as raw event volume — invites dummy scans.  
OCEL evaluation: require every wait hypothesis to cite a related object set; compare Job flattening with and without lot events; treat disagreement as a data-quality object, not as throughput.

**I5. Calibrator policy: overlapping CAL standard, off-tester recalibration, or sequence tests to bunch recalibration.**  
Target: `CAL-3` lifecycle on `J6`.  
Benefit: recover ~50 min (C5) and `TEST-1`’s 16:35–17:25 idle.  
Helped: `J6`; possibly `S3` if `J6` is last to pack.  
Harmed: `J5` if order flips; measurement integrity if recalibration is skipped.  
Risk: metrology noncompliance.  
Perverse metric: “tester utilization” — encourages testing without a valid `CAL-3`.  
OCEL evaluation: relate `Final Test` and `Recalibrate` to `{Job, TEST-1, CAL-3}`; attribute idle only when the tester is free *and* the calibrator is occupied.

**I6. Lot-release decoupling: split `L7`-style lots by destination job, or stage clean components before quarantine of the sibling.**  
Target: component-lot coupling.  
Benefit: `J5` need not inherit `C2`’s fate; `B2` may start nearer `J4`’s 13:10 (subject to `RACK-7`).  
Helped: `J5`,`J4`,`B2`.  
Harmed: `J2` if splitting reduces sample size for lot QA; quality traceability.  
Risk: more lot IDs, weaker statistical sampling.  
Perverse metric: “lots on hold” count — encourages silent overrides like the one that put `J2` into `B1` at 12:05 while `L7` was still quarantined.  
OCEL evaluation: keep `ComponentLot` as a first-class object; measure fraction of lot members that continue under override; do not treat `J2` and `J5` as exchangeable.

Spillovers are the design point: freeing `FIX-A` can pull in `S1`; splitting `L7` can pull in `B2` and then `S3`; shrinking batches can raise oven setups and contend more for `RACK-7`. Local machine KPIs will fight every sync-reducing change.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

**Observations that would weaken the top-ranked (batch-synchronization) explanation**

1. `J1`/`J2` “ready for batch” at 08:10/08:25 includes unreleased `L8` holds or missing components, so they were not actually oven-eligible.
2. A documented rule forbids starting `B1` before a scheduled 12:00 oven slot; the 3 h 55 min is schedule slack, not peer-job sync.
3. `RACK-7` (or another rack) was unavailable for the whole morning; then the binder is the tool, not the batch membership of `J3`.
4. Independent `Batch Cure` events exist per job but were collapsed in the extract; there was no real three-job barrier.
5. `J3` was ready much earlier than 11:55 and the 11:55 stamp is a system write-back at load time.
6. Partial-load cures are routine in this plant; `B1` waited for a quality sample, not for `J3`.
7. After de-batching in a trial week, early-job waits remain near 4 h (points to lots, fixtures, or shipments instead).
8. `J1`/`J2` work-in-process clocks pause for breaks/shifts that explain most of 08:10–12:05 without reference to `J3`.

**Additional fields / event types that would discriminate hypotheses**

1. Explicit `Wait for Tool`, `Wait for Batch Peer`, `Wait for Lot Release`, `Wait for Shipment Partner` with multi-object relations.
2. Eligible-to-start flags at each station (counterfactual readiness, not just last-scan time).
3. Tool state calendar (`FIX-A` dirty/issued/free; `RACK-7` cleaning reason and owner; `CAL-3` due-date vs. event-triggered recalibration).
4. Batch policy attributes: minimum fill, max wait, allowed partial load, oven recipe compatibility.
5. Shipment constraints: customer merge rules, truck appointment, Incoterms, split-shipment permission for `S1`.
6. Lot-quality payload: reason for `Quarantine Lot` at 10:20, sample IDs, and whether override on `J2` was authorized exception or extract error.
7. Machine vs. tool event identity: whether `Calibrate`/`Recalibrate` is related to `TEST-1`, `CAL-3`, or both (explains 39 min vs. 50 min gaps).
8. Resource request queues with timestamps (`J4` requested `FIX-A` at …), so contention is observed rather than inferred from 11:40→11:45.
9. Shift, break, and maintenance calendars to test non-object explanations of 08:10–12:05 and 12:55–14:50.
10. Downstream customer timestamps so `S1` wait is scored as true delivery delay versus inventory repositioning.

Together these would separate **peer synchronization**, **tool occupancy**, **lot policy**, **shipment policy**, **machine capacity**, and **extract artifacts**—the six families that currently co-explain the same six jobs.
