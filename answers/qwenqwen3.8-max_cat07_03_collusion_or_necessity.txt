def retrospective_is_compliant(case_events):
    overrides = case_events[case_events["concept:name"] == "Emergency Override"]
    justifications = case_events[case_events["concept:name"] == "Retrospective Justification"]

Because the emergency rule has resource and time constraints, supplement DECLARE/log-skeleton with a custom rule:

```python
def emergency_rule_check(case_events):
    """
    Rule:
    Emergency Override must be followed within 3 working days by
    Retrospective Justification from a different human resource.
    """
    overrides = case_events[case_events["concept:name"] == "Emergency Override"]
    justifications = case_events[case_events["concept:name"] == "Retrospective Justification"]

    violations = []

    for _, override in overrides.iterrows():
        override_time = override["time:timestamp"]
        override_resource = override["clean_resource"]

        valid = justifications[
            (justifications["time:timestamp"] > override_time) &
            (justifications["clean_resource"] != override_resource)
        ]

        within_3_working_days = any(
            np.busday_count(
                override_time.date(),
                row["time:timestamp"].date()
            ) <= 3
            for _, row in valid.iterrows()
        )

        if not within_3_working_days:
            violations.append(override["case:concept:name"])

    return violations
```

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

Based on the current evidence, the procurement-to-payment pattern is best understood as a mixture of emergency handling, informal specialization, and data-quality limitations, with one cluster requiring elevated review. The most concerning subgroup is the VendorMaster_Lena/AP_Kim activity around vendor family V-77x, where bank-detail changes and payment approvals occur close together, often through manual_upload, with high vendor concentration and a small number of four-eyes exceptions. This combination creates a control-risk indicator, but it is not by itself evidence of wrongdoing, particularly because a banking-platform migration and mass-maintenance activity may explain some records.

The emergency medical and facilities clusters involving Buyer_Carla/Approver_Gita and Buyer_Dan/Manager_Hugo show unusually fast approvals and repeated loops, but the high emergency_flag rate, low vendor concentration, and operational context are broadly consistent with urgent handling and informal specialization. Some retrospective justifications were batch-entered or recorded by the same person, which weakens control evidence and may reflect quarter-close catch-up rather than intentional bypass. Shared-account use and bot-to-human mapping errors further reduce confidence in resource attribution.

The audit should distinguish control exceptions from misconduct. Recommended follow-up: targeted testing of V-77x bank-change/payment cases; verification of independent bank validation, approval limits, goods receipt, and underlying vendor documentation; review of emergency overrides for independent retrospective approval; and remediation of shared accounts, batch-upload timestamps, and source-system mapping. Until those procedures are complete, findings should be described as control exceptions and data-quality issues requiring investigation, not as established fraud or collusion.
