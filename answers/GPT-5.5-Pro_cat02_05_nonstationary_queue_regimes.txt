# 1. Data audit and estimands

I treat the service-center day as the 12-hour opening interval from 07:00 to 19:00. All per-hour rates below are therefore **per opening hour**, not per 24 calendar hours.

**Queueing quantities and units**

| Quantity                |                                                        Definition used here |                         Unit | Important limitation                                                                                         |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------: | ---------------------------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Arrival intensity       |                                                             `arrivals / 12` |       cases per opening hour | Daily arrivals hide within-day bursts.                                                                       |
| Completion intensity    |                                                            `completed / 12` |       cases per opening hour | Completions need not correspond to same-day arrivals.                                                        |
| Per-FTE completion rate |                                              `completed / (12 * staff_fte)` | completed cases per FTE-hour | Uses mean daily FTE; ignores intraday staffing, skill mix, breaks, and outage effects.                       |
| Active service behavior |                                     `median_service_min`, `p90_service_min` |   minutes of active handling | These are **not** waiting time or total flow time. The mean service time is not supplied.                    |
| WIP                     |                                         `mean_wip` from 15-minute snapshots |              cases in system | Start-of-day and end-of-day backlog are not supplied.                                                        |
| Net pressure            |                                                      `arrivals - completed` |                cases per day | Useful as a pressure signal, but not a verified backlog balance without boundary WIP and hidden-exit checks. |
| Flow time               | time from arrival to completion, including waiting and overnight carry-over |                hours or days | Not observed directly. It cannot be replaced by median service time.                                         |

**Explicit assumptions**

A1. When estimating daily rates, I divide counts by 12 opening hours. For the outage day I report open-hour rates, and separately note the apparent non-outage service-window interpretation.

A2. I use `arrivals - completed` as a **backlog pressure indicator**. Equating it exactly to backlog change would require confirming that arrivals, completions, and WIP refer to the same closed population with no uncounted exits, transfers, cancellations, or measurement differences.

A3. Per-FTE rates assume the daily mean `staff_fte` is reasonably comparable across days. This is weaker on the outage day and on 2026-04-29, when the temporary staffing intervention began at an unlogged time during the day.

**Threats to naive inference**

1. **Non-stationarity:** the four weeks include an outage, recovery days, a possible case-mix or coding change, a staffing intervention, weekdays, and weekends. A single stationary queueing model would average incompatible states.

2. **Carry-over backlog:** cases can carry overnight, and the 07:00 backlog is not supplied. A high completion day may reflect clearing older work, not same-day service capacity.

3. **Service time is not flow time:** the table gives median and p90 active handling time only. Little’s Law requires mean number in system, throughput, and mean time in system, not median active service.

4. **Daily aggregation hides intraday burstiness:** the 2026-04-21 representative vector has severe arrival spikes despite an ordinary-looking daily total.

5. **Capacity is averaged:** `staff_fte` is a daily mean. It does not show whether staff were aligned with the arrival peak, whether staff had the right skills, or whether they were productive during the outage.

6. **Complex-case share may be endogenous or partly artificial:** the jump from roughly low-20% complex cases to roughly low-to-high-40% could be a real demand shift, a coding change, or both.

7. **Outage/recovery contamination:** 2026-04-15 and 2026-04-16/17 should not be treated as normal low- and high-throughput observations.

8. **Serial dependence:** WIP, complexity, service times, and peak load show persistence. Daily rows are not independent draws.

9. **Possible peak-window inconsistency:** under the stated hourly-bin convention, the representative vectors’ 08:00–09:59 shares do not match the daily `peak_08_10_pct` values. For example, 2026-04-07 has 5 + 12 = 17 arrivals in the 08–10 bins, or about 17%, while the daily table reports 31%. I therefore treat the daily peak column and the representative vectors as separate aggregate evidence rather than forcing exact reconciliation.

10. **Flow-balance audit issue:** over all 28 days, arrivals exceed completions by 110 cases. Under a closed-flow interpretation that would imply substantial backlog growth, yet late-period mean WIP falls. This does not make the data unusable, but it means exact backlog claims need start/end WIP and definition checks.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

A serious alternative is a **state-pressure segmentation** rather than a calendar segmentation.

| Alternative regime                      | Dates                                               | Operational story                                                                                  | Evidence                                                                                               |
| --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Balanced low-pressure state             | Mostly 2026-04-06 to 2026-04-14, plus 2026-04-18/19 | The system is roughly matching arrivals and completions under low-complexity demand.               | Complex share mostly 18–24%; net counts near zero; WIP mostly low-to-moderate.                         |
| Disruption and immediate recovery state | 2026-04-15 to 2026-04-17                            | A discrete outage creates a backlog shock, followed by catch-up work.                              | 2026-04-15 has 180 outage minutes and only 58 completions; 2026-04-16/17 have 129 and 112 completions. |
| Backlog-accumulation state              | 2026-04-20 to 2026-04-28                            | Demand, mix, and burstiness exceed the effective capacity of the pre-intervention staffing model.  | Complex share around 40–47%; p90 service 78–88; net +106 over these nine days.                         |
| Recovery under added capacity           | 2026-04-29 to 2026-05-03                            | Extra staffing improves outflow and reduces WIP, but the period is too short to call steady state. | Staff increases on weekdays and weekend; net −18 over weekdays and 0 over the weekend; WIP declines.   |

This alternative represents a **backlog-dynamics story**: balanced operation, shock, accumulation, and recovery. Its strength is that it aligns directly with queue pressure and Little’s-Law concerns. Its weakness is that it mixes weekdays and weekends, so it is less useful for staffing calendars. It also conditions on observed outcomes such as WIP and `arrivals - completed`, which can be endogenous.

Another plausible alternative is a **case-mix-first segmentation**: pre-2026-04-20 versus post-2026-04-20, with outage and staffing intervention treated as flags. This would be appropriate if the central question were whether the complex-variant increase changed service behavior. Its strength is parsimony; its weakness is that it could hide weekend capacity constraints and the 2026-04-29 staffing intervention.

The primary segmentation is better for operational staffing. The state-pressure alternative is better for explaining backlog dynamics. The case-mix alternative is better for auditing whether the complex-case jump was real or coding-driven.

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

The representative hourly vectors show that daily counts are insufficient for queueing behavior.

| 2026-04-25         | Weekend candidate           |                1.18 |       0.53 |                   0.72 |          0.45 | Lower volume, but still time-of-day structure.                                               |

These Fano factors are diagnostic indices over 12 hourly bins, not proof of a Poisson or non-Poisson process. They combine true random variation with deterministic time-of-day shape. Still, the contrast is important: 2026-04-21 has one hour with 34 arrivals, about 3.55 times that day’s hourly mean. A daily model with 115 arrivals/day would miss this surge.

The daily sequence also rejects a simple stationary interpretation. Using the supplied 28 rows, lag-1 autocorrelation is high for active service measures and complexity: approximately 0.87 for median service, 0.89 for p90 service, and 0.93 for complex share. Arrival lag-7 autocorrelation is approximately 0.99, reflecting strong day-of-week structure. WIP lag-1 autocorrelation is around 0.61, consistent with carry-over or persistent operating state.

There is change-point evidence around 2026-04-20: complex share jumps from the low/mid-20s to the low/mid-40s, p90 service jumps into the 78–88 minute range, and peak share increases. A transparent mean-shift heuristic on complex share, median service, p90 service, and peak share would select 2026-04-20 as the strongest candidate cut, but that does not prove the cause. The change could be a real case-mix shift, a coding change, burstier intake, or a combination.

The four representative days cannot establish a full stochastic model. They are selected examples, not a random sample; they lack hourly WIP, hourly staffing, skill mix, case-level service times, and arrival-to-completion linkages. They are sufficient to warn against a pooled daily queueing model, but insufficient to fit a reliable nonhomogeneous arrival process or service distribution.

# 7. Operational implications

The service center should be managed as multiple operating regimes, not as one average queue.

For **staffing**, the high-complexity weekday regime needs a different plan from the baseline weekday regime. Baseline weekdays were roughly balanced around 100 arrivals and completions per day with about 6 FTE. High-complexity pre-intervention weekdays had about 114 arrivals/day but only about 101 completions/day with similar staffing, while median service rose to 35 minutes and p90 service to 83 minutes. The temporary staffing increase appears directionally useful because post-intervention weekdays completed about 121/day and reduced WIP, but the period is too short and partly mixed on 2026-04-29, so it should be monitored rather than declared a stable new capacity.

For **backlog recovery**, outage recovery and post-intervention recovery should be reported separately from ordinary throughput. A day with 129 completions after an outage should not reset the expected service rate upward. Recovery should be tracked with explicit 07:00 and 19:00 backlog, aged WIP, and the split between new arrivals and old backlog.

For **peak smoothing**, daily totals are not enough. The 2026-04-21 hourly vector shows a bursty pattern with large spikes. Possible policies include appointment slotting, pre-triage before the main morning peak, controlled release of non-urgent work, targeted staffing during known peaks, and separating quick simple cases from complex multi-step cases. Any such policy should be audited for fairness and service-quality effects, because smoothing by deferring complex cases could reduce apparent WIP while worsening outcomes for complex applicants.

For **weekend coverage**, absolute WIP is lower, but WIP per FTE is not necessarily low. Reducing weekend staffing based only on lower arrivals could be risky, especially after the complex-case shift.

For **data collection**, the most valuable additions are case-level arrival timestamps, service-start timestamps, completion timestamps, hourly staffing by skill, start/end-of-day backlog, exact outage start/end, exact intervention start time on 2026-04-29, complexity coding rules, and raw event logs with variants. The complex-case jump should be audited as a possible coding change before attributing the service-time increase solely to real demand.

A policy that looks beneficial under pooled averages but risky under segmentation would be: **staff to the pooled average of 90 arrivals/day and remove temporary staff because pooled completions per FTE-hour are stable.** Segmented evidence shows that high-complexity pre-intervention weekdays were accumulating about 13–14 extra cases per day despite similar per-FTE completion rates. That pooled policy would likely recreate backlog growth during the high-complexity weekday regime.

def fano_cv(values):
    x = np.asarray(values, dtype=float)
    mean = x.mean()
    if mean == 0:
        return np.nan, np.nan, mean
    return x.var(ddof=0) / mean, x.std(ddof=0) / mean, mean

# Transparent segmentation heuristic:
# Exclude the explicit outage day and scan for a single calendar cut that maximizes
# standardized differences in selected mix/service/burst features. This is a diagnostic,
# not ground truth, and it does not replace the known outage and intervention annotations.
signal_cols = ["complex_pct", "median_service_min", "p90_service_min", "peak_08_10_pct"]
work = df.loc[df["outage_min"].eq(0)].sort_values("date").reset_index(drop=True)
scores = []
min_size = 5
for cut in range(min_size, len(work) - min_size):
    left = work.iloc[:cut]
    right = work.iloc[cut:]
    score = 0.0
    details = {}
    for col in signal_cols:
        sd = work[col].std(ddof=0)
        standardized_delta = (right[col].mean() - left[col].mean()) / sd if sd else 0.0
        details[col] = standardized_delta
        score += abs(standardized_delta)
    scores.append({
        "candidate_start_date": work.loc[cut, "date"].date().isoformat(),
        "score_sum_abs_std_deltas": score,
        **{f"std_delta_{k}": v for k, v in details.items()}
    })
scores = pd.DataFrame(scores).sort_values("score_sum_abs_std_deltas", ascending=False)
print("\nSIMPLE CHANGE-POINT HEURISTIC: TOP CANDIDATE CUTS")
print(scores.head(5).round(3).to_string(index=False))
