# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

For case \(c\), let \(a_c\) be arrival, \(r_c\) fork release, \(s_{ci},e_{ci}\) the start and end of branch \(i\in\{A,B,C\}\), and \(f_c,g_c\) the start and end of F. Durations below are in minutes. Under the stated AND-join rule, all three end times are prerequisites for F.

| Quantity | Definition | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Upstream elapsed \(U_c\) | \(r_c-a_c\) | All elapsed time before branch release, including any upstream waiting |
| Branch queue \(Q_{ci}\) | \(s_{ci}-r_c\) | Time from permission to begin until recorded start |
| Branch service \(S_{ci}\) | \(e_{ci}-s_{ci}\) | Start-to-end elapsed time, an estimate of active work if uninterrupted |
| Branch completion offset \(X_{ci}\) | \(Q_{ci}+S_{ci}=e_{ci}-r_c\) | How late this branch finishes relative to release |
| Join readiness \(J_c\) | \(\max_i e_{ci}\) | Inferred time at which all branches are complete |
| Synchronization wait \(W_{ci}\) | \(J_c-e_{ci}\) | Time the finished branch result waits for its siblings |
| Fork–join elapsed \(L_c\) | \(J_c-r_c=\max_i X_{ci}\) | Wall-clock duration of the parallel stage |
| Downstream queue \(D_c\) | \(f_c-J_c\) | Ready-to-start gap, interpretable as queueing if F is eligible for dispatch at \(J_c\) |
| Final service \(V_c\) | \(g_c-f_c\) | Recorded duration at F |
| Total flow \(T_c\) | \(g_c-a_c=U_c+L_c+D_c+V_c\) | Arrival-to-final-completion elapsed time |

The critical set is \(\{i:e_{ci}=J_c\}\), retaining every branch in a tie. Following **any one** branch from release to join gives \(Q_{ci}+S_{ci}+W_{ci}=L_c\). Summing these three path lengths gives \(3L_c\) because they cover the same clock interval. Even the individual synchronization waits overlap. In F02, C waits from 08:50 to 09:25 and A waits from 09:15 to 09:25. Their sum of 45 branch-minutes counts the last ten minutes twice, and all 35 minutes of their union already lie inside the 60-minute stage. These waits describe the age of completed results and do not establish that the branch resources sat idle.

# 3. Aggregate critical-path analysis

**B most strongly controls join readiness in this sample.** It finishes last in eight cases and determines both 75-minute stages. A has the longest service time, but it starts immediately in all twelve cases. B's 19.17-minute mean start delay raises its mean completion offset above A's.

Each pair below gives the mean and empirical P90 in minutes. P90 uses nearest rank, the 11th ordered observation out of 12. The **margin** \(M_{ci}=\max(0,X_{ci}-\max_{j\ne i} X_{cj})\) measures how much sooner the join could occur if branch \(i\) were made fast enough to reach the next-latest completion, holding siblings fixed. It is zero for a noncritical branch or a tied leader.

| Branch | Service mean / P90 | Queue mean / P90 | Completion offset mean / P90 | Last to finish | Mean sync | Margin mean / max | Influence |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| A | 50.00 / 50 | 0.00 / 0 | 50.00 / 50 | 2/12 (16.7%) | 11.00 | 1.25 / 10 | Second by mean margin; sets a 50-minute floor in every case |
| B | 37.50 / 45 | 19.17 / 30 | 56.67 / 75 | 8/12 (66.7%) | 4.33 | 10.00 / 25 | First by frequency, mean margin and tail impact |
| C | 20.17 / 22 | 10.42 / 25 | 30.58 / 55 | 2/12 (16.7%) | 30.42 | 1.00 / 7 | Ties A on frequency; controls F04 and F08 |

There are no observed ties. The closest leads are five minutes in F01, F08 and F10. A is second to finish in all ten cases led by B or C, so it limits what accelerating either can achieve. Over the twelve cases, B accounts for 120 minutes of lead over its nearest sibling, versus 15 for A and 12 for C. These are separate, one-branch-at-a-time opportunities. In F07 and F12, B combines a 30-minute queue with 45 minutes of service and finishes 25 minutes after A. These are also the two longest case flows, 130 and 125 minutes. C's long mean synchronization wait mostly reflects early completion. Its two exceptions have C start delays of 35 and 25 minutes.

Mean total flow decomposes as \(19.58+61.00+14.67+20.00=115.25\) minutes. Observed P90 is 75 minutes for the parallel stage and 125 for total flow. The branch ranking concerns elapsed time and its sensitivity on these cases. Inferring a throughput bottleneck would require resource and demand data.

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

For each branch change, hold releases, sibling intervals and F durations fixed to isolate its direct effect. Reducing branch \(i\)'s completion offset by \(h_{ci}\ge0\) advances the join by \(\min(h_{ci},M_{ci})\). Thus a ten-minute improvement can yield anything from zero to ten minutes at the join. The first two savings columns assume each case keeps its observed ready-to-F delay, so a minute gained at the join advances final completion by one minute. This is a sensitivity calculation. Simultaneous feasibility at F is tested separately with a dispatch model. Fractional minutes come from the percentage interventions.

| Case | A service −20%, join gain | B queue −50%, join gain | Two-server dispatch, flow gain vs log | Third server, extra flow gain vs two |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| F01 | 5 | 0 | 8 | 0 |
| F02 | 0 | 10 | 15 | 0 |
| F03 | 0 | 10 | 15 | 0 |
| F04 | 0 | 0 | 18 | 0 |
| F05 | 0 | 10 | 10 | 0 |
| F06 | 0 | 15 | 15 | 0 |
| F07 | 0 | 15 | 15 | 0 |
| F08 | 0 | 0 | 15 | 0 |
| F09 | 0 | 12.5 | 15 | 0 |
| F10 | 0 | 5 | 20 | 0 |
| F11 | 10 | 0 | 15 | 5 |
| F12 | 0 | 15 | 10 | 0 |

The dispatch scenarios assume F1 and F2 are initially free, interchangeable and continuously staffed, with only these cases present. Dispatch the earliest ready case as soon as a server is free, breaking readiness ties by case ID. The third-server scenario adds an identical server under that same rule. This separates the effect of changing dispatch from the marginal effect of buying capacity.

| Scenario and reference | Total flow minutes saved | Mean saved per case | Cases with zero gain | Resulting mean flow | Resulting P90 / max flow |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Observed | — | — | — | 115.25 | 125 / 130 |
| A service −20%, observed wait durations | 15 | 1.25 | 10 | 114.00 | 125 / 130 |
| B queue −50%, observed wait durations | 92.5 | 7.71 | 4 | 107.54 | 115 / 115 |
| Two-server dispatch vs observed | 171 | 14.25 | 0 | 101.00 | 115 / 115 |
| Third server vs two-server dispatch | 5 | 0.42 | 11 | 100.58 | 115 / 115 |

1. **A service from 50 to 40 minutes.** All twelve A intervals shrink by ten minutes, saving 120 service-minutes. Only F01 and F11 gain at the join. In F01, B finishes at offset 45 and limits the gain to five. In F11, A and B now tie at 40, so the gain is ten. In the other ten cases B or C still finishes last, and A's synchronization wait grows by ten. Reducing A occupancy could free resources for other work, although these cases show no A start delay. The stage P90 stays at 75.
2. **B start delay halved, service held fixed.** The assumed intervention removes 115 branch-queue minutes and yields 92.5 minutes at the joins. F01 and F11 are still controlled by A, F04 and F08 by C. F02 and F06 reach A/B ties. In F05, reducing the B delay by 12.5 minutes advances the join by ten before A takes over. F07 and F12 each gain 15 minutes, bringing stage P90 down to 60. Achieving these earlier starts simultaneously may require changing machine availability or sequencing. Moving one case forward on a shared machine could move another back.
3. **Dispatch F when ready, then test extra capacity.** The observed downstream gaps sum to 176 minutes. With observed joins, pooled two-server dispatch leaves only five waiting minutes, all in F11. F09 and F10 occupy the two modeled servers when F11 becomes ready at 11:30. The third server removes those five minutes. All other cases already start at readiness under two-server dispatch, so they gain zero from a third. Branch criticality and synchronization are unchanged by either F-only change. The 171-minute dispatch gain depends on the assumed availability. For example, neither server has a recorded case at 09:00 when F01 becomes ready, yet F01 waits eight minutes. Hidden work, staffing or a release rule could explain that gap.

**How much of an earlier join reaches the customer depends on F.** If observed F start times are fixed appointments, both branch changes yield zero final-completion gain in every case. Each earlier join simply lengthens the pre-F wait. Replaying the branch changes against the *same two-server dispatch baseline* gives a less extreme example. A still gains five minutes on F01, but F11's ten-minute join gain is entirely absorbed, leaving five total flow minutes saved. With B accelerated, the two-server model saves 97.5 total minutes against that baseline. This includes five minutes on F11, whose own join is unchanged but whose server frees earlier. For the joint B and dispatch redesign, this gives 171 + 97.5 = 268.5 flow minutes saved against the log, with P90 total flow of 100 minutes.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

1. **Pilot earlier machine access for B.** Record machine eligibility, occupancy, maintenance, setup and queue position at each fork. Trial earlier reservation or dispatch for cases at risk of exceeding the 50-minute A completion time. Compare similar shifts on B queue P90, stage and total-flow P90, completions per staffed hour, machine utilization and delays displaced onto other jobs. The modeled queue cut shortens both longest stages by 15 minutes while preserving B's 450 recorded service-minutes. Earlier starts can reduce work in process and the time finished A and C results wait. Sustainable throughput improves only if access or sequencing recovers usable machine time or prevents starvation downstream. Reservations can also leave a machine idle. Watch for A becoming critical, occasional late C reviews, and earlier joins bunching at F.
2. **Pilot ready-triggered dispatch across F1 and F2 before expanding the station.** Timestamp actual eligibility, assignment and start, and record duties and staffing on both resources. When either qualified resource is available, dispatch the oldest eligible case. Compare ready-to-start delays, P90 total flow, completed cases per staffed hour and busy fractions on both resources. The replay lowers total-flow P90 from 125 to 115 while a third server gives no additional P90 improvement. The log contains 240 F service-minutes over the 220 minutes from first readiness to last completion. That is 54.5% of two continuously staffed servers' time **on these cases**. Hidden work or skills restrictions could make pooling less effective. With 20-minute service the two servers' nominal combined capacity is six releases per hour. Faster dispatch moves completions earlier and may use idle staffed time, but it leaves the service requirement and pre-join synchronization intact. Check peak contention and upstream supply, particularly B, before forecasting a sustained throughput gain.

# 6. Reproducible Python

```python
from io import StringIO
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

raw = """case arrival fork_release A_start A_end B_start B_end C_start C_end final_start final_end final_resource
F01 07:55 08:10 08:10 09:00 08:15 08:55 08:20 08:38 09:08 09:28 F1
F02 08:05 08:25 08:25 09:15 08:45 09:25 08:30 08:50 09:40 10:00 F2
F03 08:20 08:40 08:40 09:30 09:00 09:45 08:45 09:03 10:00 10:20 F1
F04 08:35 08:55 08:55 09:45 09:05 09:35 09:30 09:52 10:10 10:30 F2
F05 08:50 09:10 09:10 10:00 09:35 10:10 09:15 09:35 10:20 10:40 F1
F06 09:05 09:25 09:25 10:15 09:55 10:30 09:40 10:00 10:45 11:05 F2
F07 09:20 09:40 09:40 10:30 10:10 10:55 09:45 10:03 11:10 11:30 F1
F08 09:35 09:55 09:55 10:45 10:00 10:30 10:20 10:50 11:05 11:25 F2
F09 09:50 10:10 10:10 11:00 10:35 11:15 10:15 10:35 11:30 11:50 F1
F10 10:05 10:25 10:25 11:15 10:45 11:20 10:30 10:50 11:40 12:00 F2
F11 10:20 10:40 10:40 11:30 10:50 11:20 10:45 11:03 11:50 12:10 F1
F12 10:35 10:55 10:55 11:45 11:25 12:10 11:00 11:18 12:20 12:40 F2"""
d = pd.read_csv(StringIO(raw), sep=r"\s+").set_index("case").sort_index()
for col in d.columns.drop("final_resource"):
    d[col] = pd.to_datetime("2026-05-19 " + d[col], format="%Y-%m-%d %H:%M")

branches = ["A", "B", "C"]
minute = pd.Timedelta(minutes=1)
m = pd.DataFrame(index=d.index)
m["upstream_elapsed"] = (d.fork_release - d.arrival) / minute
# AND-join eligibility uses all three end times. A directly-follows edge
# in a timestamp-sorted trace does not encode these parallel dependencies.
m["join_ready"] = d[[i + "_end" for i in branches]].max(axis=1)
q, s, w = (pd.DataFrame(index=d.index) for _ in range(3))
for i in branches:
    q[i] = (d[i + "_start"] - d.fork_release) / minute
    s[i] = (d[i + "_end"] - d[i + "_start"]) / minute
    # Custom synchronization wait, distinct from branch queue and service.
    w[i] = (m.join_ready - d[i + "_end"]) / minute
    m[i + "_queue"], m[i + "_service"], m[i + "_sync"] = q[i], s[i], w[i]
x = q + s

def critical(spans):
    # Preserve every maximizer in deterministic A/B/C order.
    return spans.apply(lambda row: "+".join(i for i in branches
                                            if row[i] == row.max()), axis=1)

m["critical_branch"] = critical(x)
m["fork_join"] = (m.join_ready - d.fork_release) / minute
m["downstream_queue"] = (d.final_start - m.join_ready) / minute
m["final_service"] = (d.final_end - d.final_start) / minute
m["total_flow_time"] = (d.final_end - d.arrival) / minute
assert (x.add(w).eq(m.fork_join, axis=0)).all().all()
assert (m.upstream_elapsed + m.fork_join + m.downstream_queue
        + m.final_service).eq(m.total_flow_time).all()
assert m.select_dtypes("number").ge(0).all().all()

def p90(values):
    ordered = values.sort_values()
    return ordered.iloc[(9 * len(ordered) + 9) // 10 - 1]  # nearest rank

margin = pd.DataFrame({i: (x[i] - x.drop(columns=i).max(axis=1)).clip(lower=0)
                       for i in branches})
summary = pd.DataFrame({
    "S_mean": s.mean(), "S_p90": s.apply(p90), "Q_mean": q.mean(),
    "Q_p90": q.apply(p90), "X_mean": x.mean(), "X_p90": x.apply(p90),
    "critical_n": x.eq(m.fork_join, axis=0).sum(), "sync_mean": w.mean(),
    "margin_mean": margin.mean(), "margin_max": margin.max()
})
print(m.assign(join_ready=m.join_ready.dt.strftime("%H:%M")).to_string())
print(summary.round(2).to_string())

xa, xb = x.copy(), x.copy()
xa["A"] = q.A + 0.8 * s.A
xb["B"] = 0.5 * q.B + s.B
def ready_from(spans):
    return d.fork_release + pd.to_timedelta(spans.max(axis=1), unit="min")

ja, jb = ready_from(xa), ready_from(xb)
gain_a, gain_b = (m.join_ready - ja) / minute, (m.join_ready - jb) / minute

def pooled_end(ready, server_count):
    # All servers initially free, identical eligibility, no external work.
    # FCFS by readiness, then case ID. Keep each case's measured F duration.
    available = [ready.min()] * server_count
    end = {}
    for c, t in ready.sort_index().sort_values(kind="stable").items():
        k = min(range(server_count), key=lambda idx: (available[idx], idx))
        available[k] = max(t, available[k]) + m.at[c, "final_service"] * minute
        end[c] = available[k]
    return pd.Series(end).reindex(d.index)

e2 = pooled_end(m.join_ready, 2)
e3 = pooled_end(m.join_ready, 3)
savings = pd.DataFrame({
    "A_same_wait": gain_a, "B_same_wait": gain_b,
    "pool2_vs_log": (d.final_end - e2) / minute,
    "pool3_vs_pool2": (e2 - e3) / minute,
    "A_within_pool2": (e2 - pooled_end(ja, 2)) / minute,
    "B_within_pool2": (e2 - pooled_end(jb, 2)) / minute
})
print(savings.assign(A_last=critical(xa), B_last=critical(xb)).to_string())
base = m.total_flow_time
pool_flow = (e2 - d.arrival) / minute
result = {}
for label, gain in savings.items():
    ref = pool_flow if label in {"pool3_vs_pool2", "A_within_pool2",
                                "B_within_pool2"} else base
    after = ref - gain
    result[label] = [gain.sum(), gain.mean(), gain.eq(0).sum(),
                     after.mean(), p90(after), after.max()]
print(pd.DataFrame.from_dict(result, orient="index", columns=[
    "sum_saved", "mean_saved", "zero_n", "mean_flow", "p90_flow", "max_flow"
]).round(3).to_string())
print("Observed mean/p90/max", base.mean(), p90(base), base.max())
print("P90 fork-join, observed/A/B:", *map(p90, [m.fork_join,
                                                 xa.max(axis=1), xb.max(axis=1)]))

# Each activity instance is one interval. No invented branch resource IDs.
activities = []
for activity in [*branches, "F"]:
    prefix = "final" if activity == "F" else activity
    part = pd.DataFrame({
        "case:concept:name": d.index, "concept:name": activity,
        "start_timestamp": d[prefix + "_start"].to_numpy(),
        "time:timestamp": d[prefix + "_end"].to_numpy()
    })
    if activity == "F":
        part["org:resource"] = d.final_resource.to_numpy()
    activities.append(part)
intervals = pd.concat(activities, ignore_index=True)
log = pm4py.format_dataframe(intervals, case_id="case:concept:name",
                             activity_key="concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
                             start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")
assert len(log) == 4 * len(d)
service_sec = pm4py.get_service_time(log, aggregation_measure="mean",
                                     start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")
print("PM4Py mean elapsed service (minutes)")
print(pd.Series(service_sec).sort_index().div(60).round(2).to_string())
```

# 7. Limits of inference

- The timestamps identify elapsed intervals. Interpreting a branch start delay as demand queueing assumes that work was actionable at release. Reservations, missing inputs, priorities or shift schedules could produce the same interval. Preemption, pauses and rework inside a start–end pair are invisible, so active effort and resource occupancy require separate evidence.
- Branch resource identities, parallel capacity, eligibility and other workloads are missing. Overlapping intervals show concurrent open activities, but machine counts and utilization cannot be recovered from them. Finished branch results can wait while their workers or machines serve other cases.
- Join readiness is inferred from the stated three-input rule. An unlogged approval after the last branch, batching, a fixed release appointment or resource-specific routing would change the interpretation of the pre-F gap. F1/F2 labels identify who processed each case, without establishing who was free, eligible or staffed at readiness.
- These twelve cases on one day support descriptive margins and conditional replays. They do not establish why delays occurred, how a queue cut could be achieved, how displaced jobs would fare or the steady-state throughput effect. The empirical P90 is the second-largest observation. Minute recording limits precision near ties, and the modeled half-minutes do not represent additional measurement precision.
