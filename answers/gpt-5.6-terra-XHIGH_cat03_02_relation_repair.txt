## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

**Hard invariants**

- **Referential integrity:** every added E2O relation must reference an existing event and object; every O2O relation must reference two existing objects.
- **O2O directionality:** qualifiers are directional. For example, a proposed package-delivery edge must be `DEL-Y carries PK-8`, not the reverse.
- **No duplicate relation rows:** do not add an E2O or O2O row already present with the same endpoints and qualifier.
- **No invented entities or events:** repairs may only add relations among supplied identifiers.

**Strong defaults**

- `Pack Item` normally has one Item and one Package. ev08 already has `IT-B1`; `scanner_ref=PK8-2` strongly supports adding `PK-8`.
- `Dispatch Package` normally has one Package and one Delivery. ev10 already has `PK-8`; `external_ref=N-421?` strongly, though not perfectly, suggests `DEL-Y` with `carrier_ref=N-421`.
- O2O package containment/carrying relations should usually agree with direct packing and dispatch E2O evidence when both represent the same operational fact.
- A package may contain several items, including items from different orders, unless a domain rule says otherwise. No such exclusivity rule is supplied.
- An invoice can bill multiple orders, and one invoice can receive multiple payments; those are allowed but should not be inferred merely to complete lifecycles.

**Weak heuristics**

- Identifier similarity: `PK8-2` resembles `PK-8`; `N-421?` resembles `N-421`; `N-42*` is compatible with both `N-420` and `N-421`.
- Time: dispatches at 10:00 precede the delivery confirmation at 14:00; invoice and payment follow fulfillment.
- Route and customer: both deliveries are NORTH and both orders belong to C17.
- Amounts: `130 + 100 = 230`, and `100 + 130 = 230`, but these are only supportive because fees, taxes, discounts, credits, partial payments, and unrelated cash are possible.

**Evidence against a repair**

- `N-421?` is materially more consistent with `DEL-Y` than with `DEL-X`; assigning ev10 to `DEL-X` would require treating the identifier as erroneous.
- ev11 has only `ORD-A` observed and uses a wildcard-like `N-42*`; it does not uniquely distinguish `DEL-X`, `DEL-Y`, or a joint confirmation.
- `INV-44` already bills `ORD-A`, but its amount exceeding ORD-A's declared value does not prove that ORD-B is billed.
- `PAY-2` has no invoice reference; although its amount complements PAY-1, unapplied cash is explicitly permitted.

I combine evidence qualitatively rather than as a mechanical score. A direct scanner/carrier identifier plus activity cardinality is stronger than a shared customer or matching amount. Existing O2O structure is useful, but an O2O edge inferred from an assumed E2O edge is not independent confirmation of that E2O edge.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

### Bundle A: conservative/minimum-change repair

Bundle A accepts only the relations with direct operational identifiers and cardinality support:

- **ev08 and PK-8:** add `ev08  PK-8` and `PK-8 packs IT-B1`.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** add `ev10  DEL-Y` and `DEL-Y carries PK-8`; reject DEL-X as the target of ev10.
- **ev11 and delivery objects:** retain only the observed relation to ORD-A. Leave both delivery candidates unresolved rather than assigning the confirmation uniquely or jointly.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** leave ORD-B unlinked to INV-44.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** leave PAY-2 unapplied.

This yields coherent physical handling: IT-A1 is packed in PK-7 and dispatched through DEL-X; IT-A2 and IT-B1 are packed in PK-8 and dispatched through DEL-Y. PK-8 therefore contains items belonging to two orders, which is unusual but not prohibited. ORD-B remains commercially incomplete: it is created, receives an item, and that item is packed and dispatched indirectly through its package, but it has no direct invoice or confirmation relation. That incompleteness is preferable to falsely asserting consolidation.

### Bundle B: consolidated-account and aggregate-confirmation interpretation

Bundle B includes Bundle A’s physical repair and additionally promotes the financial and confirmation candidates jointly:

- **ev08 and PK-8:** same as Bundle A.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** same as Bundle A; DEL-Y remains the better match for `N-421?`.
- **ev11 and delivery objects:** add ev11 relations to both DEL-X and DEL-Y, and add ORD-B. Interpret `N-42*` and `portal_customer=C17` as a customer-portal acknowledgement covering both NORTH deliveries.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** add `ev12  ORD-B` and `INV-44 bills ORD-B`.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** add `ev14  INV-44` and `PAY-2 settles INV-44`.

Bundle B gives a coherent consolidated lifecycle: ORD-A and ORD-B are delivered under a shared customer acknowledgement, INV-44 bills both orders, and PAY-1 plus PAY-2 settle the invoice. The amount pattern supports this bundle, but does not prove it. The main risk is that an apparently tidy financial lifecycle is an artifact of over-interpreting compatible totals and customer identity.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

- **Order:** ORD-A has create, item addition, a customer/order-level confirmation, and invoicing. ORD-B has create and item addition, with its item physically packed and dispatched through PK-8, but no direct invoice or confirmation in Bundle A. That is incomplete, not impossible.
- **Item:** all three items have Add Item and Pack Item events. IT-B1 becomes properly paired with PK-8 after A1.
- **Package:** PK-7 has one packed item and one dispatch. PK-8 has two packed items and one dispatch. The one-package/two-item relation is normal; the cross-order composition is suspicious but allowed.
- **Delivery:** DEL-X is dispatched with PK-7; DEL-Y is dispatched with PK-8. Neither has a direct confirmation relation in Bundle A. They are not orphan objects, but their confirmation lifecycle is unresolved.
- **Invoice:** INV-44 is issued and bills ORD-A. Its total is suspicious relative to ORD-A alone, but no hard inconsistency exists because value attributes are not necessarily invoice net totals.
- **Payment:** PAY-1 is recorded and settles INV-44. PAY-2 is recorded but commercially unallocated; this is an allowed unapplied-cash state.
- **Events:** every event is related to at least one object. ev08 and ev10 become more cardinality-consistent after repair.

The selected repairs have both E2O and O2O counterparts, but they are not fully independent support. The O2O additions are derived from the same scanner/carrier evidence that motivates the E2O additions. It would be circular to treat those new O2O rows as extra proof. Similarly, using the newly inferred PK-8/IT-B1 relation to “prove” that ORD-B must be invoiced would be circular lifecycle completion.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

In the **unrepaired log**, ev08 is an Item-only Pack Item event and ev10 is a Package-only Dispatch Package event. DEL-Y has no event relation, and PK-8’s trace omits the packing of IT-B1. This weakens Package–Item and Package–Delivery interaction counts.

Under **Bundle A**:

- **OC-DFG:** PK-8 gains a sequence containing ev07, ev08, and ev10. This can add or strengthen a `Pack Item  Pack Item` edge for Package cases and a `Pack Item  Dispatch Package` edge. DEL-Y gains a one-event Dispatch Package trace.
- **OTG:** Item–Package and Package–Delivery type associations gain additional support. These type pairs already exist through ev06/ev07 and ev09, so the primary effect is higher multiplicity rather than a wholly new type edge.
- **ET–OT graph:** ev08 gains Package participation; ev10 gains Delivery participation. Consequently, `Pack Item–Package` and `Dispatch Package–Delivery` incidence becomes more complete.
- **Order flattening:** direct E2O flattening on Order changes little in Bundle A because package and delivery links are not automatically propagated through O2O relations. ORD-B still lacks direct invoice and confirmation events.
- **Package flattening:** PK-8 obtains `Pack Item, Pack Item, Dispatch Package`; PK-7 remains `Pack Item, Dispatch Package`.
- **Delivery flattening:** DEL-X has Dispatch Package; DEL-Y gains Dispatch Package.
- **Invoice and Payment flattenings:** unchanged from the observed log: INV-44 has Issue Invoice then PAY-1’s Record Payment; PAY-2 remains a separate one-event payment case.
- **Frequencies:** event frequencies remain fixed: three Pack Item events, two Dispatch Package events, two Record Payment events. Unique participating Delivery objects for Dispatch Package increase from one to two. Total global objects do not change; only relation incidence counts change.
- **Equivalent-execution clustering around Order:** ORD-A and ORD-B remain relatively distinct because they do not share direct invoice or confirmation events. If a clustering method traverses O2O paths, PK-8 can still create a weak cross-order connection, but that is method-dependent.

Under **Bundle B**, additional behavior appears:

- `Confirm Delivery  Issue Invoice` can occur for both ORD-A and ORD-B.
- Invoice flattening gains a second Record Payment, potentially producing `Record Payment  Record Payment` on INV-44.
- Delivery flattenings gain Confirm Delivery after Dispatch Package for both DEL-X and DEL-Y.
- The OTG and ET–OT graph gain Order–Delivery participation for ev11 and Payment–Invoice participation for ev14.
- Shared ev11 and ev12 events can cause ORD-A and ORD-B to cluster into a shared or equivalent execution, even though their physical fulfillment is partly distinct. This may be correct for a consolidated account process, or a false merger if Bundle B is wrong.

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.ocel.obj import OCEL

# Preserve originals for comparison and reproducibility.
events_original = events.copy(deep=True)
objects_original = objects.copy(deep=True)
relations_original = relations.copy(deep=True)
o2o_original = o2o.copy(deep=True)

events_r = events.copy(deep=True)
objects_r = objects.copy(deep=True)
relations_r = relations.copy(deep=True)
o2o_r = o2o.copy(deep=True)

# Ensure expected OCEL relation columns exist.
for col in ["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"]:
    if col not in relations_r.columns:
        relations_r[col] = pd.NA

for col in ["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"]:
    if col not in o2o_r.columns:
        o2o_r[col] = pd.NA

# Bundle A additions only. null qualifier means no new event-role semantics are asserted.
e2o_additions = pd.DataFrame([
    {"ocel:eid": "ev08", "ocel:oid": "PK-8",  "ocel:qualifier": pd.NA},
    {"ocel:eid": "ev10", "ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:qualifier": pd.NA},
])

o2o_additions = pd.DataFrame([
    {"ocel:oid": "PK-8",  "ocel:oid_2": "IT-B1", "ocel:qualifier": "packs"},
    {"ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:oid_2": "PK-8",  "ocel:qualifier": "carries"},
])

relations_r = pd.concat([relations_r, e2o_additions], ignore_index=True, sort=False)
o2o_r = pd.concat([o2o_r, o2o_additions], ignore_index=True, sort=False)

# Referential-integrity checks.
event_ids = set(events_r["ocel:eid"].dropna())
object_ids = set(objects_r["ocel:oid"].dropna())

bad_eids = set(relations_r["ocel:eid"].dropna()) - event_ids
bad_e2o_oids = set(relations_r["ocel:oid"].dropna()) - object_ids
bad_o2o_sources = set(o2o_r["ocel:oid"].dropna()) - object_ids
bad_o2o_targets = set(o2o_r["ocel:oid_2"].dropna()) - object_ids

assert not bad_eids, f"Unknown event IDs in E2O: {bad_eids}"
assert not bad_e2o_oids, f"Unknown object IDs in E2O: {bad_e2o_oids}"
assert not bad_o2o_sources, f"Unknown O2O sources: {bad_o2o_sources}"
assert not bad_o2o_targets, f"Unknown O2O targets: {bad_o2o_targets}"

# Duplicate checks, treating null qualifiers consistently.
def duplicate_mask(df, cols):
    tmp = df[cols].astype("object").copy()
    tmp = tmp.where(tmp.notna(), "__NULL_QUALIFIER__")
    return tmp.duplicated(keep=False)

e2o_dupes = duplicate_mask(relations_r, ["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"])
o2o_dupes = duplicate_mask(o2o_r, ["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"])

assert not e2o_dupes.any(), "Duplicate E2O rows found after repair"
assert not o2o_dupes.any(), "Duplicate directed O2O rows found after repair"

# VERSION-DEPENDENT: constructor signature differs across PM4Py releases.
# Some versions may require positional arguments or an object_changes argument.
ocel_repaired = OCEL(
    events=events_r,
    objects=objects_r,
    relations=relations_r,
    o2o=o2o_r
)

# Discovery calls. Exact result structures can vary by PM4Py version.
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel_repaired)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel_repaired)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel_repaired)

# Flattenings use direct E2O case membership; do not assume O2O is transitively expanded.
flat_order = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel_repaired, "Order")
flat_delivery = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel_repaired, "Delivery")

# Intentionally do not call pm4py.ocel_o2o_enrichment here.
# Such enrichment can be useful for exploration, but it does not prove the
# business semantics of inferred package, delivery, invoice, or payment links.
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. Adding `ev10  DEL-X` merely because both dispatches occur at 10:00 could falsely create a bulk-dispatch pattern and leave DEL-Y artificially orphaned.
2. Assigning ev11 uniquely to DEL-X or DEL-Y treats `N-42*` as more precise than it is and can fabricate delivery-confirmation paths.
3. Adding both deliveries to ev11 without qualification can create false synchronized delivery behavior and make DEL-X and DEL-Y appear operationally inseparable.
4. Inferring `INV-44 bills ORD-B` solely because `130 + 100 = 230` converts a plausible accounting pattern into a false hard relationship.
5. Allocating PAY-2 to INV-44 solely because payment amounts sum to the invoice total can hide unapplied cash, prepayments, refunds, or another invoice.
6. Adding `ORD-A contains IT-B1` because IT-B1 shares PK-8 with ORD-A’s item would corrupt explicit order ownership and flattening-based Order traces.
7. Treating newly added O2O rows as independent evidence for the E2O rows that caused them is circular and can inflate confidence in later lifecycle inferences.
8. Forcing every Delivery to have Confirm Delivery and every Payment to settle an Invoice would manufacture complete happy-path lifecycles unsupported by the observed log.
