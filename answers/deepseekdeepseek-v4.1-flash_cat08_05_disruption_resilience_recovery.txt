# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

In this case, **resilience** is an adaptation that preserves mission-critical throughput while keeping minimum controls appropriate to product and customer risk. True resilience includes manual authorization by trained compliance staff, temporary pharmacist release for controlled substances, hospital-critical fast lane with explicit waiver rules, deferral of truly noncritical bulk orders, documented backlog prioritization, and reconciliation of manual reservations. These adaptations accepted degradation but did not erase accountability.

**Risky shortcuts** are deviations that preserve throughput by dropping a control that is essential for safety, traceability, or legality. Examples include skipping system authorization for controlled, cold-chain, or recall-sensitive products; dispatching without scan; using phone PoD as the only delivery evidence for controlled products; relying on a stale morning inventory snapshot for cold-chain or controlled stock; and missing quality/release or temperature records. In the T2 DFG, these are visible as move-on-log deviations: skip system authorization check, dispatch without scan, phone proof of delivery, and spreadsheet invoice hold. Move-on-model deviations also include missing quality/release, missing scan, missing PoD, and missing temperature-control records. These are not merely process variants; they create hidden compliance and traceability risk.

**Ambiguous adaptations** depend on risk appetite and execution. Hospital-critical fast lane is resilient if it only bypasses nonessential electronic steps while retaining authorization and high-risk controls. It becomes concealed noncompliance if it ships controlled or cold-chain product without a waiver trail. Deferring noncritical orders is resilient if explicit and time-limited; it becomes unfair or commercially damaging if informal and prolonged. Manual pick lists are resilient if paired with reconciliation; they become concealed noncompliance if treated as complete without later scan reconstruction. Audit remediation is necessary, but after-the-fact remediation is not equivalent to real-time compliance. The T4 audit-remediation queue persisting after cycle-time recovery proves that operational metrics can look recovered while compliance debt remains hidden.

- Order intake available but inventory synchronization lag >30 minutes, or scan failure >5% of stations.
- Dispatch/courier scanning unavailable for >30 minutes.
- Cold-chain or controlled-substance evidence capture unavailable.
- Escalation levels: Level 1 degraded; Level 2 manual workaround; Level 3 full continuity with regulator/quality notification.

**Triage rules**
- Risk score = customer criticality + product risk + age/contractual urgency.
- Customer: Hospital-critical = 3, Pharmacy-standard = 2, Wholesale-bulk = 1.
- Product: Controlled/Recall-sensitive = 3, Cold-chain = 2, Ambient = 1.
- Hospital-critical and high-risk products enter fast lane, but never bypass minimum controls.
- Defer only Wholesale-bulk ambient and non-urgent Pharmacy-standard ambient. Set maximum deferral age and customer communication SLA.
- Controlled, cold-chain, and recall-sensitive orders are not deferred unless an explicit clinical/regulatory exception is approved.

**Minimum controls by product/customer risk**
- Hospital-critical ambient: manual authorization, timestamped inventory check, unique incident order ID, dispatch manifest, phone/ePoD acceptable.
- Hospital-critical cold-chain: manual authorization, fresh inventory check, temperature log at pack and handoff, secure dispatch manifest, ePoD.
- Controlled-substance: manual authorization, dual-control check, fresh inventory, pharmacist release, secure handoff, dual signatures, no phone-only PoD.
- Recall-sensitive: offline recall check, lot/expiry capture, manual release, dispatch manifest, PoD with lot reference.
- Pharmacy-standard ambient: manual authorization, serialized pick list, sample release, manifest, phone PoD acceptable for low risk.
- Wholesale-bulk ambient: defer if needed; if shipped, manual authorization, batch pick, manifest, post-hoc scan within defined window.

**Manual evidence capture**
- Preprinted or locally generated barcoded incident order IDs.
- Required fields: customer, product, lot, expiry, quantity, authorization source, temperature, courier, timestamps, signatures.
- Use controlled spreadsheet templates with validation, not free-form sheets.
- Phone logs must include call ID, time, staff name, and customer contact.
- Courier manifest must list every order and be reconciled before invoice close for high-risk products.

**Backlog prioritization**
- Order backlog by score: Hospital-critical > controlled/cold/recall > Pharmacy-standard > Wholesale-bulk.
- Within tier, oldest first, then contractual/penalty risk.
- Publish daily fairness report: deferral rate, cancellation rate, and fill rate by customer group.
- Cap pharmacy-standard deferral age to avoid silent service degradation.

**Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow**
- During incident: daily reconciliation of inventory snapshot vs reservations, scan vs dispatch, PoD vs invoice, and temperature/dual-control records.
- After system return: risk-based audit queue.
- High-risk missing evidence: resolve within 24 hours or escalate to quality/regulatory.
- Medium-risk: resolve within 72 hours.
- Low-risk: resolve within 7 days.
- Close only with evidence. If evidence is missing, record an incident waiver and owner.
- Do not treat audit remediation as real-time compliance; move high-risk remediation into the incident response flow.

# 5. Resilience indicators

| category | leading indicators | lagging indicators |
|---|---|---|
| Throughput | Manual picks/hour vs plan; dispatch manifest completion rate; courier pickup delay | Cases/day; median and p90 cycle time by customer segment |
| Service protection | Hospital-critical queue age; deferral rate by segment; emergency order volume | Fill rate by segment; cancellation rate; customer complaint rate |
| Traceability | Scan-gap rate; incident order ID coverage; courier manifest completeness | Traceability exceptions; PoD completeness; reconciliation variance |
| Control completeness | Offline authorization checklist completion; dual-control signature rate; cold-chain temperature log capture | Missing cold-chain temperature documentation; controlled dual-control missing; recall-check misses |
| Backlog age | Oldest backlog age; backlog by risk tier; deferral age | p90 backlog clearance time; audit-remediation queue age |
| Rework | Manual data correction rate; reconciliation queue size; invoice holds | Rework/correction case rate; invoice-close delay |
| Fairness | Deferral/cancellation rate by customer group; communication SLA compliance | Fill-rate gap Hospital vs Pharmacy vs Wholesale; complaint/exception rate by segment |

High-risk missing controls should have zero tolerance. If controlled dual-control missing >0%, cold-chain temperature missing >0.4%, or dispatch scan gap >1% for high-risk products, the system should be considered outside resilient operation.

# 6. Recommendations for future disruptions

| priority | recommendation | implementation effort | expected benefit | residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Adopt tiered minimum controls and break-glass rules by customer/product risk | Low | Prevents skip authorization and dispatch-without-scan for high-risk products | Misclassification of product/customer risk |
| 2 | Deploy offline evidence kit with barcoded incident IDs and controlled templates | Medium | Reduces traceability reconstruction and audit-remediation tail | Manual transcription errors |
| 3 | Hard-gate cold-chain, controlled, and recall-sensitive orders | Medium-high | Protects highest compliance and patient-safety risks | Capacity bottleneck during incidents |
| 4 | Require dispatch manifest and scan-at-handoff fallback | Medium | Closes largest traceability gap seen in T2 | Courier noncompliance and manual reconciliation |
| 5 | Run daily reconciliation war room during incident and recovery | Medium | Reduces hidden backlog and inventory truth decay | Resource cost and staff fatigue |
| 6 | Formalize backlog prioritization and fairness caps | Low | Reduces unfair Pharmacy/Wholesale service loss | Customer dissatisfaction remains if caps too strict |
| 7 | Build leading-indicator dashboard for incident triggers | Medium | Enables earlier escalation and fewer improvised shortcuts | Data quality during system outage |
| 8 | Pre-incident tabletop exercises and cross-training | Low-medium | Faster, more consistent triage under pressure | Staff turnover and drift |

# 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should accept controlled degradation for low-risk ambient and bulk orders, explicit deferral of noncritical work, manual authorization for critical orders, and temporary overtime for reconciliation—provided every deviation is logged, time-bound, and reviewed. Management should not accept skipped authorization for controlled, cold-chain, or recall-sensitive products; dispatch without scan for high-risk goods; phone-only PoD for controlled substances; stale inventory as a basis for cold-chain or controlled allocation; or treating after-the-fact audit remediation as equivalent to real-time compliance. The recommendation would change if offline authorization lists become real-time, scanning and inventory synchronization are restored with high confidence, cold-chain and dual-control evidence can be captured electronically, and backlog/audit queues return below defined thresholds. Until then, throughput preservation without critical controls should be treated as concealed noncompliance, not resilience.
