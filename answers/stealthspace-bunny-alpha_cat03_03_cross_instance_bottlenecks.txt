## 1. Interaction-graph description

The objects form a **typed constraint network**, not six independent job chains. Jobs meet through shared batches, tools, component lots, and shipments; those objects carry synchronization, availability, or consolidation policies.

Important node types are Job, Component, ComponentLot, Machine, Tool, Batch, and Shipment. Important edges include:

- Job **requires** Component.
- Component **contained in** ComponentLot.
- Job **batches** Batch; Batch processed on Machine and using Tool.
- Job or Batch **acquires/releases/uses** Tool.
- ComponentLot **quarantines/releases** its components.
- Job **consolidated by** Shipment.

A compact selected adjacency list is:

- `J1–requiresC1–contained_inL8`; `J2C2L7`; `J3C3L8`; `J4C4`; `J5C5L7`; `J6C6`, with no lot supplied for C4 or C6.
- `B1–batches{J1,J2,J3}–processed_on/uses{OVEN-1,RACK-7}`.
- `B2–batches{J4,J5}–processed_on/uses{OVEN-1,RACK-7}`.
- `J3–acquires/releasesFIX-A`; `J4–MachineProcess(11:45){C4,CNC-2,FIX-A}`.
- `L7–contains{C2,C5}`; `L7–quarantines(10:20)/releases(13:50)C2,C5`, with the event also naming J2 and J5.
- `{J5,J6}–requires_at(Final Test)CAL-3`.
- `S1–consolidates{J1,J4}`; `S2{J2,J3}`; `S3{J5,J6}`.

**Observation versus inference:** The supplied O2O relations and stated event participation are direct records. “Contained in,” downstream effects of lot release, exclusive tool requirements, feasible alternative start times, and delay attribution are analytical inferences. Co-occurrence at an event does not by itself establish causality or a mandatory processing sequence.

Higher-order motifs include:

1. **Job–Component–ComponentLot:** L7 couples J2 through C2 and J5 through C5 despite their different downstream routes.
2. **Job–Batch–Machine–Tool–Component:** B1’s 12:05 event joins OVEN-1, RACK-7, three jobs, and three components.
3. **Job–Tool–Machine–Component:** J3’s FIX-A hold overlaps J4’s arrival at CNC-2; J4’s actual process later names FIX-A and C4.
4. **Job–Tool–Machine:** J5 and J6 share CAL-3-dependent testing on TEST-1.
5. **Job–Shipment–Job:** S1 joins J1’s early packing with J4’s much later packing.

Thus J3 can affect J4 through a tool, J2 and J5 through a lot, J5 and J6 through calibration, and J1 and J4 through dispatch consolidation.

## 4. What flattening misses

No Batch- or Tool-flattened summaries were supplied; their expected visibility below is conditional on retaining the stated object links.

- **Job:** Direct event sequences and median durations are visible. They hide lot events, tool holds, batch barriers, and packed-to-dispatched waiting. A misleading conclusion is that normal-looking, below-1.8-standard-deviation jobs have no material queue exposure.
- **Machine:** Utilization and event gaps are visible. Batch membership, tool state, lot release, and shipment policies are hidden. The 12 Job–Machine interactions count use/release, not waiting. Thus 54–58% utilization does not prove that machines caused—or could relieve—the queues.
- **Batch:** B1/B2’s oven and rack events should be visible. Independent member readiness, component constraints, and downstream shipment coupling remain fragmented. Two Batch–Machine interactions must not be interpreted as low impact; B1 alone can hold J1 and J2 for hours.
- **Tool:** FIX-A holds, CAL-3 use/recalibration, and rack cleaning should be visible. Implicit requirements and unlogged waits remain invisible unless connected to process events; some inferred holds must not be presented as 15 confirmed waits.
- **ComponentLot:** L7’s C2/C5 membership and quarantine/release are visible, while affected jobs disappear unless containment is traversed. One event projected onto five objects can be counted repeatedly. The opposite Job–ComponentLot/ComponentLot–Job counting conventions can duplicate associations. Neither J2’s actual lot delay nor J5’s can be recovered from the 210-minute lot state alone.
- **Shipment:** S1’s partners and the nominal `Create ShipmentLoadDispatch` path are visible. Already-packed waiting is absent. S2’s 25-minute and S3’s 35-minute final-pack lags cannot be compared directly with S1’s 355 minutes for J1, nor can the nominal 0.8 + 0.3 hours be assigned to J1.

Low utilization coexists with waiting because a machine can be available without its job, fixture, rack, calibrated tool, released material, batch partner, or shipment partner being ready. Utilization measures occupied machine time, not usable flow capacity.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

### Narrative 1: J1 through material, batch, oven, rack, and shipment

- **Temporal precedence:** J1 requires C1 in L8, is batch-ready at 08:10, and shares B1’s 12:05 cure. It packs at 13:30 and dispatches with S1 at 19:25.
- **Resource feasibility:** The cure event ties J1/C1 to B1, OVEN-1, and RACK-7. No earlier feasible oven/rack slot is documented.
- **Policy constraints:** B1 membership may require common processing; S1 may impose a partner-ready departure rule.
- **Causal speculation:** Batch synchronization plausibly contributes to J1’s early wait, and S1 consolidation to its post-pack wait. The timestamps do not prove that a different batch would improve dispatch, or that B1 caused the 13:30 packing time.

### Narrative 2: J3’s fixture, J4’s machining, and S1

- **Temporal precedence:** J3 holds FIX-A from 09:00–11:40. J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50; CNC-2 idles 10:05–10:47; J4 processes at 11:45. J4 packs at 19:10 for the 19:25 S1 dispatch.
- **Resource feasibility:** If FIX-A is exclusive, J3 and J4 cannot use it concurrently. The CNC idle interval alone therefore does not establish available machining capacity.
- **Policy constraints:** Fixture handoffs apparently lack an explicit wait activity; S1 couples the resulting J4 schedule to already-packed J1.
- **Causal speculation:** The fixture plausibly affects the five minutes after release and possibly downstream timing. Neither the entire 115-minute wait nor J4’s responsibility for S1’s departure is established.

### Narrative 3: L7 through B2 and the shared calibration gate

- **Temporal precedence:** L7 is quarantined at 10:20 and released at 13:50. J2 enters B1 earlier by override. J5 is batch-ready at 14:40; B2 starts at 14:50. J5/J6 become test-ready at 16:00/16:05; CAL-3 is recalibrated 16:35–17:20; J6 tests 17:25–17:50.
- **Resource feasibility:** J5’s C5 belongs to L7; B2 uses RACK-7; final tests require CAL-3. TEST-1’s idle interval during calibration is consistent with a nonmachine constraint.
- **Policy constraints:** Lot disposition, batch formation, calibration validity, and S3’s 35-minute post-last-pack rule affect shared lifecycles.
- **Causal speculation:** These links can propagate delays, but L7 release cannot explain B1’s 12:05 start, and S3’s missing packing timestamps prevent a measured partner-wait calculation.

## 7. PM4Py analysis outline

This uses recent PM4Py object-discovery APIs and the canonical `source, target, count` interaction-summary schema.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Assumed available: ocel = pm4py.read_ocel("events.ocel.json")
OBJ_TYPES = [
    "Job", "Component", "ComponentLot", "Machine",
    "Tool", "Batch", "Shipment"
]

def pair_counts(summary):
    d = summary.reset_index()
    d.columns = [
        str(c[-1] if isinstance(c, tuple) else c)
        for c in d.columns
    ]
    return (
        d.groupby(["source", "target"], as_index=False, sort=True)["count"]
         .sum()
    )

# Compare event-object co-occurrence frequencies, not bottleneck strength.
job_interactions = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel, "Job")
batch_interactions = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel, "Batch")
job_counts = pair_counts(job_interactions)
batch_counts = pair_counts(batch_interactions)

# Structural object and event-type graphs.
objects_graph = pm4py.discover_objects_graph(
    ocel, graph_type="interacts"
)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)
full_ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel, ocel_obj_types=OBJ_TYPES)

# Distinct Job-Batch associations provide a second frequency perspective.
job_batch_graph = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(
    ocel, ocel_obj_types=["Job", "Batch"]
)
distinct_job_batch_edges = list(job_batch_graph.edges("Job", "Batch"))

comparison = pd.DataFrame({
    "Job_event_cooccurrences": [int(job_counts["count"].sum())],
    "Batch_event_cooccurrences": [int(batch_counts["count"].sum())],
    "Distinct_Job_Batch_edges": [len(distinct_job_batch_edges)]
}).T.rename(columns={0: "frequency"})

print(comparison)
print(objects_graph, otg, etot, full_ocdfg)

# Job and Shipment flattenings retain different event subsets.
job_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Job")
shipment_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Shipment")
print("Job-flattened rows:", len(job_flat))
print("Shipment-flattened rows:", len(shipment_flat))

# Performance DFGs summarize connected event deltas; they do not establish why.
job_performance = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(ocel, variant="dfg")
shipment_performance = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
    ocel, variant="dfg", ocel_obj_types=["Shipment"]
)

# Object-level lifecycle features; use explicit wait events for attribution.
job_features = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(ocel, "Job")
```

## 8. Falsification and additional data

Observations that would weaken the top-ranked synchronization explanation:

1. Trusted, scope-specific readiness timestamps place J1 or J2’s true batch eligibility near 11:55 rather than 08:10/08:25.
2. A documented process specification requires common cure, setup, or inspection that makes an earlier independent start physically infeasible.
3. Across many batches, starts follow fixed schedules irrespective of member readiness, contradicting a readiness-triggered barrier.
4. Controlled split-batch trials achieve earlier feasible starts without downstream lateness, quality harm, or disproportionate setup costs.
5. Trace correction shows that the 12:05 event does not represent the relevant processing start for J1/J2.
6. Repeated periods show no association between early-ready members and material readiness-to-start delay.

Additional discriminating fields and event types:

- **Stable Event, Object, and Batch identifiers**, object types, qualifiers, source system, and ingestion time.
- **Explicit `ReadyFor…` events** identifying scope: material, machine-ready, tool-ready, batch-ready, or test-ready.
- **`WaitForBatch`, `WaitForTool`, `WaitForLotRelease`, and `WaitForShipmentPartner`**, with reason and linked blocker.
- **Resource state transitions:** rack cleaning/quarantine, calibration due/valid-until, maintenance, reservation, setup, and remaining duration.
- **Batch membership history**, arrival-window rules, maximum wait, cure/recipe requirements, and membership-change reason.
- **Override actor, reason, scope, approval, and expiry**, including why J2 could bypass the lot event.
- **Tool requirement/exclusivity rules**, alternative fixture/tool identifiers, and reservation ownership.
- **Actual pack and planned/actual departure events**, partner-ready time, carrier cutoff, partial-shipment permission, and consolidation policy.
- **Disposition and provenance:** requested/started/completed/cancelled, normal/exceptional, and whether a relationship is logged, inferred, or supplied by another pipeline.
