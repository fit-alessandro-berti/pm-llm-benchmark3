# 1. Stress scenarios

1. **Winter Infection Wave (Demand Mix Shift):** Overall volume surges with a disproportionate shift toward Urgent and Complex-panel cases (as seen in the peak period data where Urgent hits 29% and Complex hits 20%).
2. **Assay-Machine Degradation (Equipment Outage):** M2, which already has a higher failure rate, experiences a spike in errors or downtime, severely reducing total assay capacity and generating a surge of Repeat-after-failure cases.
3. **Specialist Absence (Medical/Specialist Review Shortage):** A medical validator or the specialist (who is already absent every second Friday) is unavailable during a high-volume period, choking the release of Urgent and Complex-panel cases.
4. **Courier Arrival Compression (Intake Spike):** Weather or traffic delays cause couriers to miss the 10:00 slot and arrive simultaneously at 17:00, compressing intake and registration into a massive, instantaneous backlog.

# 2. Bottleneck-migration analysis

*   **Winter Infection Wave:** The surge in Urgent and Complex-panel cases initially bottlenecks at the Assay machines (98% peak utilization) because Urgent cases bypass batching, fragmenting machine schedules. If assay capacity is relieved (e.g., by batching or routing), the bottleneck immediately migrates to Technical validation (101% peak utilization) and Medical validation (93% peak utilization), as Complex cases require these downstream steps.
*   **Assay-Machine Degradation:** If M2 degrades, Repeat-after-failure cases surge. This loops back to Assay run, creating a vicious cycle. If managers attempt to relieve this by routing all cases to M1, M1 becomes a severe bottleneck. If relieved by outsourcing (I10), the bottleneck migrates to transport logistics and eventually to Medical validation due to traceability complexity.
*   **Specialist Absence:** Medical validation and Specialist review become the primary bottlenecks, causing the Hold for specialist review queue to skyrocket (already 36h at peak). If relieved by adding a temporary evening shift (I5), the bottleneck shifts upstream to Technical validation, which feeds the medical validators and is already over capacity.
*   **Courier Arrival Compression:** Intake and Registration (85% peak utilization) are instantly overwhelmed. If registration is automated (I8) to clear the front-end backlog, the massive wave of samples slams into Pre-analytical check (96% peak utilization) and Accession correction (91% peak utilization, only 2 specialists). Relieving Accession correction then pushes the wave to Preparation benches and Assay machines.

# 3. Robust redesign package

These interventions provide structural resilience across multiple scenarios:

*   **Early pre-analytical defect triage (I6):** Rejecting poor samples within 2 hours prevents defective samples from consuming capacity at Pre-analytical check, Accession correction, and Assay. Crucially, it reduces recollection loops, which currently drain registration clerks' time. *Assumption:* Triage can be performed rapidly without high false-positive rejection rates. *Failure risk:* May overload the triage staff during courier arrival spikes.
*   **Automate registration for digital orders (I8) + Shift courier pickup slots (I7):** I7 smooths the physical arrival of samples, while I8 instantly clears the digital backlog. This combination is highly robust against courier compression. *Assumption:* Clinics provide high-quality digital metadata. *Failure risk:* Pushes a smoothed but still large wave of physical samples into Pre-analytical check, which could become overloaded if physical handling isn't also streamlined.
*   **Cross-train two technical validators for accession correction (I4):** Accession correction has only 2 specialists and a 14h peak queue; unblocking it frees many cases. *Assumption:* Technical validators have the bandwidth to switch tasks. *Failure risk:* Because Technical validation is at 101% peak utilization, activating this cross-training during a wave of completed assays will severely overload Technical validation.

# 4. Scenario-specific policies

*   **Policy A: Dynamic Batching & Machine Routing**
    *   *Trigger:* Assay machine queue exceeds 10h OR Urgent case mix exceeds 25%.
    *   *Action:* Implement I2 (Increase Routine batch sizes to 24) and I3 (Dedicate M1 to Urgent/Complex; route Routine to M2).
    *   *Deactivation:* Assay machine queue drops below 5h.
    *   *Reasoning:* Protects Urgent/Complex cases on the reliable M1. Forces efficiency on Routine cases via M2 and larger batches, accepting that Routine cases will be delayed to meet Urgent 18h SLAs.
*   **Policy B: Validation Capacity Surge & Overflow**
    *   *Trigger:* Medical validation queue exceeds 12h OR Specialist review queue exceeds 24h (e.g., during Specialist absence).
    *   *Action:* Implement I5 (Temporary evening medical-validation shift) and I10 (Outsource Complex-panel overflow).
    *   *Reasoning:* Adds capacity where cross-training is impossible. Outsourcing acts as a pressure valve for Complex panels when internal validation is choked.
*   **Policy C: M2 Quality Throttle**
    *   *Trigger:* M2 repeat-after-failure rate exceeds 12% (indicating machine degradation).
    *   *Action:* Halt Routine processing on M2; implement I9 (Hold Routine releases) to reduce release work, and route only critical volume to M1.

# 6. Residual vulnerabilities

*   **Technical Validation Overload:** Technical validators are already at 101% utilization during peaks. If we successfully accelerate Urgent and Complex cases through the Assay machines (via I3/M1 dedication) and unblock front-end queues (via I8/I4), a massive wave of completed assays will slam into Technical validation simultaneously. The redesign lacks a strong mechanism to increase Technical validation capacity, making it the most fragile point in the new system.
*   **M2 Quality Spiral:** By routing Routine cases to M2 (I3) and increasing their batch sizes to 24 (I2), we create a vulnerability. If M2's already-high failure rate spikes, a massive batch of 24 Routine cases will fail at once, causing a huge surge in Repeat-after-failure cases that will clog the system and consume excessive reagents and time.
*   **Outsourcing Traceability:** Outsourcing Complex panels (I10) introduces a 12h transport delay and external dependency. If the partner lab experiences delays or loses traceability, the internal team has no control, and it may still require internal medical validators to resolve the discrepancies, defeating the purpose of the outsource.

# 7. Minimum monitoring dashboard

**Leading Indicators (Actionable warnings):**
*   **Pre-analytical Defect Rate (Hourly):** Threshold > 5%. Warns of incoming recollection loops and Accession correction backlog before queues form.
*   **Assay Machine M2 Rework Rate (Shiftly):** Threshold > 10%. Warns of M2 degradation; triggers Policy C before repeat cases overwhelm the system.
*   **Urgent Bypass Rate at Prep (Hourly):** Threshold > 60%. Indicates severe schedule fragmentation at Assay machines, warning of impending capacity drop.
*   **Accession Correction Queue Length (Hourly):** Threshold > 5h. Leading indicator of downstream starvation and upstream clerk drain.

**Lagging Indicators (Outcome metrics):**
*   **p90 Cycle Time by Case Type (Daily):** Ensures Routine cases aren't completely starved by Urgent prioritization.
*   **Percentage of cases with >12h queue (Daily):** Tracks overall system congestion and flow health.
