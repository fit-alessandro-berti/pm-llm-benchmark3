# Part B: reusable event-level PM4Py functions
import pm4py
from datetime import datetime

def prepare_event_log(df, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="timestamp"):
    df = df.copy()
    df[timestamp_key] = pd.to_datetime(df[timestamp_key])
    event_log = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id=case_id, activity_key=activity_key, timestamp_key=timestamp_key)
    return event_log

def slice_window(event_log, start, end, timestamp_key="timestamp"):
    start = pd.Timestamp(start)
    end = pd.Timestamp(end)
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(event_log, start_date=start, end_date=end, parameter_timestamp_key=timestamp_key)

def monthly_dfgs(event_log, start="2026-01-01", end="2027-01-01", timestamp_key="timestamp"):
    dfgs = {}
    for month in pd.period_range(start, end, freq="M"):
        start_date = month.to_timestamp()

## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

Raw fitness collapses in March while mapped fitness stays near baseline, implying the normative model was exposed to renamed activities, duplicated technical events, and missing resource attributes rather than a wholesale redesign. The gap implies label/provenance drift: raw events no longer match the old model's activity vocabulary and clean case structure. It does not imply the business process was unchanged; mapping can make a log conforming by renaming `Eligibility Screen` to `Validate Eligibility` and deleting duplicate `Open Case`, even if the screen's semantics, eligibility criteria, or downstream behavior changed. Duplicated technical events primarily distort conformance, DFGs, and variant counts by creating artificial self-loops or extra transitions; missing resources primarily distort resource/team analytics, workload, and attribution, while having less direct effect on activity ordering. Post-May DFG and performance patterns cannot be dismissed as logging drift alone because mapped fitness remains below baseline, rework transitions (`Assess -> Request Info`, `Request Info -> Collect Documents`) double, `CollectDocs -> Assess` falls, cycle times and p90 rise, and these changes track supplier-Z share, staffing cuts, and seasonal complexity rather than the March label change. The stable mapped fitness after March shows logging drift is separable, but not the sole explanation for later operational drift.

5. One global change point versus multiple mechanism-specific change points.

The data do not support a single global change point. This pattern is more consistent with multiple mechanism-specific change points interacting over time than with one universal process break.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Global monthly | month, quarter | Track overall trend and seasonality | All cases | Baseline vs month, seasonal comparison | Seasonality can mask or mimic drift. |
| Supplier segment | `supplier_Z` flag or supplier ID if available | Isolate supplier-driven rework and document quality | At least 30 cases/month or at least 5% of monthly cases | Supplier Z vs non-Z DFG, rework, cycle time | Supplier Z is nonrandomly complex and regional. |
| Complexity/priority segment | `high_complexity`, priority class if available | Separate case-mix drift from behavioral drift and priority delay redistribution | At least 30 cases/month per segment | Senior assessment, waiting, service time by complexity/priority | Priority may be missing; complexity may be endogenous. |
| Resource/team segment | team or assessor group, resource completeness flag | Monitor staffing, workload, and team-specific behavior | At least 30 cases/month and at least 80% resource completeness | Waiting/service by team, resource missingness effects | Missing resource IDs and work redistribution can bias team comparisons. |
| Outcome/document-quality segment | approve/reject, document bundle complete if available | Connect rework to document quality and outcomes | At least 30 cases/month per segment | Rwork vs completeness, outcome drift | Outcome is endogenous to process decisions. |
| Logging stratum | raw label era, duplicate `Open Case` flag, missing-resource flag | Separate provenance artifacts from business-process drift | At least 30 cases/month per stratum | Raw vs mapped DFG, missingness impact | Conditioning on post-migration artifacts can bias performance comparisons. |

Primary monitoring population: use mapped, deduplicated cases with valid `case_id` and timestamp as the primary business-process monitoring population. Retain cases with missing resource identifiers but flag them; do not exclude them from process-flow monitors. Exclude only cases with fatal missing case identity or timestamp. This preserves the operational population, avoids mixing raw logging artifacts with business drift, and still allows separate logging-quality monitors.

## 7. Monitoring architecture

| monitor | statistic/artifact | reference window | alert logic | diagnostic follow-up | likely false-positive source | owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Label/schema monitor | unmapped label rate, distinct activity labels, label entropy | trailing 3 months + same month prior year if available | new unmapped label or entropy outside metric-specific quantile bands | inspect event samples, update label mapping, check migration changes | new legitimate activity introduced | data engineering |
| Duplicate technical event monitor | duplicate `Open Case` rate, self-loop rate | seasonal baseline | duplicate rate above upper control limit | audit deduplication rules and technical event logs | legitimate case re-open or technical retry | data engineering |
| Event ordering monitor | DFG L1 distance, key transition rates | seasonal baseline | DFG distance or key edge outside metric-specific bands | DFG diff, alignment to normative model, variant review | volume spikes or seasonal mix | process mining |
| Variant distribution monitor | variant entropy, top-variant share, fast/rework variant rates | seasonal baseline | KL divergence or entropy shift beyond band | compare variants to baseline, inspect new variants | minor new variant with low frequency | process mining |
| Rework monitor | `Assess -> Request Info`, `Request Info -> Collect Documents`, rework variant percentage | trailing 3 months adjusted for supplier and complexity mix | EWMA/CUSUM above seasonal-adjusted band | segment by supplier/complexity, inspect document completeness | document-quality campaign or policy change | operations |
| Temporal profile monitor | median and p90 service time per activity | seasonal baseline, mix-adjusted | activity service time shifts beyond metric-specific band | decompose cycle time by activity, inspect complexity mix | complexity mix change | operations |
| Queue waiting monitor | waiting before `Assess`, waiting before `Collect Documents`, p90 waiting | seasonal baseline, workload-adjusted | waiting increases beyond expected volume/mix effect | analyze queue workload, staffing, priority class | volume spike or renewal season | staffing |
| Service time monitor | `Assess` service time, `Senior Assessment` service time | seasonal baseline | service time shifts after controlling for mix | inspect assessor skill, case complexity, senior mix | senior-assessment mix change | operations |
| Case mix monitor | high-complexity percentage, supplier-Z percentage, volume, priority mix | seasonal baseline | case-mix shift beyond expected seasonal range | population segmentation, supplier/complexity review | renewal season or new customer segment | data/operations |
| Resource completeness monitor | missing resource percentage, valid resource identifier rate | trailing 3 months | missingness above metric-specific upper limit | repair provenance, inspect migration mapping, team assignment | new system fields or identifier format change | data engineering |
| Conformance monitor | mapped model fitness, alignment cost, constraint violation rates | seasonal baseline | mapped fitness declines beyond band or violations concentrate in specific constraints | alignment analysis, normative model review, exception mining | normative model outdated | process mining |
| Outcome monitor | approve/reject rates, senior-assessment decision rates, cycle-time outcomes | seasonal baseline | outcome shift beyond metric-specific band | quality review, policy review, case mix adjustment | case mix or policy change | business owner |

No universal threshold should be used. Each monitor should use its own reference distribution, preferably seasonal-adjusted. For recurring seasonality, use same-month prior-year baselines when available, otherwise use STL decomposition or a seasonal-naive baseline with known renewal-season adjustment. For multiple testing, treat monitors as hierarchical: primary monitors are DFG distance, rework, queue waiting, case mix, mapped fitness, and resource completeness. Secondary monitors should trigger diagnostic follow-up only when a primary monitor also changes or when two related secondary monitors change together. Use Benjamini-Hochberg or a pre-specified corroboration rule rather than alerting on every isolated metric.

import pm4py


def prepare_event_log(
    df,
    case_id="case_id",
    activity_key="activity",
    timestamp_key="timestamp",
):
    """
    Convert a pandas event dataframe to a PM4Py EventLog.
    The input dataframe is copied before formatting.
    """
    df = df.copy()
    df[timestamp_key] = pd.to_datetime(df[timestamp_key])
    event_log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id=case_id,
        activity_key=activity_key,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
    )
    return event_log


def slice_window(event_log, start, end, timestamp_key="timestamp"):
    """
    Slice an EventLog to a time window.
    """
    start = pd.Timestamp(start)
    end = pd.Timestamp(end)
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(
        event_log,
        start_date=start,
        end_date=end,
        parameter_timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
    )


def monthly_dfgs(
    event_log,
    start="2026-01-01",
    end="2027-01-01",
    timestamp_key="timestamp",
):
    """
    Compute monthly DFGs.
    """
    dfgs = {}
    for month in pd.period_range(start, end, freq="M"):
        start_date = month.to_timestamp()
        end_date = (month + 1).to_timestamp()
        sub_log = slice_window(event_log, start_date, end_date, timestamp_key=timestamp_key)
        if len(sub_log) > 0:
            dfgs[str(month)] = pm4py.discover_dfg(

def discover_structural_models(
    event_log,
    start="2026-01-01",
    end="2027-01-01",
    timestamp_key="timestamp",
):
    """
    Optionally discover log skeletons and DECLARE models by month.
    """
    models = {}
    for month in pd.period_range(start, end, freq="M"):

        end_date = (month + 1).to_timestamp()

def remove_duplicate_open_case(
    df,
    case_id="case_id",
    activity_key="activity",
    timestamp_key="timestamp",
    target="Open Case",
):
    """
    Remove duplicated technical Open Case events per case.
    This is a normalization helper; it does not modify the input dataframe.
    """
    df = df.sort_values([case_id, timestamp_key]).copy()
    duplicate_mask = (
        (df[activity_key] == target)
        & df.duplicated(subset=[case_id, activity_key], keep="first")
    )
    return df[~duplicate_mask]


def compare_raw_and_normalized(
    raw_df,
    label_mapping,
    case_id="case_id",
    activity_key="activity",
    timestamp_key="timestamp",
    remove_duplicates=True,
):
    """
    Compare raw and normalized logs without overwriting the original dataframe.

    label_mapping example:
    {
        "Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"
    }
    """
    raw_df = raw_df.copy()
    normalized_df = raw_df.copy()

    normalized_df[activity_key] = normalized_df[activity_key].replace(label_mapping)

    if remove_duplicates:
        normalized_df = remove_duplicate_open_case(
            normalized_df,
            case_id=case_id,
            activity_key=activity_key,
            timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
        )

    raw_log = prepare_event_log(
        raw_df,
        case_id=case_id,
        activity_key=activity_key,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
    )

    normalized_log = prepare_event_log(
        normalized_df,
        case_id=case_id,
        activity_key=activity_key,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
    )

    return {
        "raw_dfg": pm4py.discover_dfg(
            raw_log,
            parameters={"dfg:minimum": 1},
        ),
        "normalized_dfg": pm4py.discover_dfg(
            normalized_log,
            parameters={"dfg:minimum": 1},
        ),
        "raw_variants": pm4py.get_variants(raw_log),
        "normalized_variants": pm4py.get_variants(normalized_log),
        "raw_temporal_profile": pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(
            raw_log,
            parameters={"tp:variant": "median"},
        ),
        "normalized_temporal_profile": pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(
            normalized_log,
            parameters={"tp:variant": "median"},
        ),
    }

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest conclusion is that the March raw-fitness collapse is primarily a logging/semantic migration effect. Raw fitness falls from about 0.92 to 0.55 while mapped fitness remains near 0.90, distinct labels rise, duplicated `Open Case` events appear, and resource missingness increases. This is a high-confidence, mechanism-specific change point; it should not be read as a March operational failure. The next strongest attribution is supplier-driven rework after May 5. Supplier-Z share jumps from about 15% to 43%, rework variants rise from 12% to 24%, `Assess -> Request Info` doubles, and both improve partially after September 10. Confidence is high that supplier disruption increased rework, but not that it explains all delay because Supplier Z cases were already more complex. Staffing cuts after June 15 and the July 1 priority rule plausibly generated queueing drift. Service time for `Assess` changes only modestly while reconstructed waiting rises, p90 cycle time expands more than median, and mapped fitness stays low. Confidence is moderate-to-high for queueing, but priority may have redistributed delay rather than created all of it. Seasonal renewal from October 1 is a strong population/case-mix effect: volume, high-complexity share, senior-assessment transitions, and cycle times rise, then partially fall in December. Confidence is high for seasonality, but December also includes overtime and document-quality interventions, so improvement is mixed. Persistent behavioral adaptation is plausible but not proven: rework remains above baseline after supplier recovery and through December. Measurement missingness is a real provenance issue for resource/team analytics, but it does not explain the DFG or cycle-time drift. The main interaction is that supplier cases are nonrandomly complex, staffing cuts affect queue waiting, and the priority rule changes waiting by case class; therefore aggregate cycle time is a mediated outcome, not a direct measure of any single cause. I would revise the account if supplier-segmented DFGs showed no post-May rework increase among comparable complexity cases, if waiting before `Assess` did not rise after June, if priority-class waiting showed no redistribution, or if a formal change-point analysis placed the main DFG shift at a different date.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF raw_model_fitness falls sharply while mapped_model_fitness remains stable and distinct_activity_labels or duplicate_open_case_pct rise, THEN freeze operational conclusions and audit label mapping, deduplication, and event provenance, BECAUSE raw conformance can collapse from semantic or technical logging migration without a real business-process change.

2. IF mapped DFG shows increased Assess->RequestInfo and RequestInfo->CollectDocs after supplier_Z_pct rises, THEN segment by supplier, document completeness, and complexity and inspect incomplete document bundles, BECAUSE supplier disruption can generate rework loops that propagate into cycle time and conformance loss.

3. IF waiting before Assess rises while Assess service time remains stable and p90 cycle time expands after staffing cuts, THEN analyze queue workload, assessor capacity, and team distribution, BECAUSE staffing reductions create queueing drift rather than uniformly slower service.

4. IF high-complexity waiting falls while standard-case waiting rises after the priority rule, THEN monitor delay separately by priority class and review SLA fairness, BECAUSE priority rules can redistribute delay across case populations without changing total workload.

5. IF high_complexity_pct and volume rise in October-November with senior-assessment transitions increasing, THEN apply a seasonal baseline and compare with prior renewal seasons, BECAUSE annual renewal is a recurring population and case-mix drift source that can mimic new behavioral drift.

6. IF rework remains elevated after supplier recovery and lower seasonal load, THEN investigate persistent behavioral adaptation, training, and document-quality practices, BECAUSE process behavior may have changed independently of the original supplier disruption.

7. IF missing_resource_pct or unmapped labels rise in migrated activities, THEN quarantine resource/team analytics and repair provenance before attributing performance to teams, BECAUSE missing identifiers can create apparent team or activity drift that is not operational.
