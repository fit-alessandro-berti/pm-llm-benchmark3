## 1. Interaction-graph description

In an object-centric event log (OCEL), the six jobs do not execute as isolated linear traces; they form a dense, multi-layered bipartite and hypergraph structure connecting seven object types: **Job**, **Component**, **ComponentLot**, **Machine**, **Tool**, **Batch**, and **Shipment**.

### Node and edge taxonomy

- **Important node types:**
  - *Primary flow units:* `Job` (`J1`–`J6`), `Component` (`C1`–`C6`).
  - *Policy/grouping containers:* `ComponentLot` (`L7`, `L8`), `Batch` (`B1`, `B2`), `Shipment` (`S1`–`S3`).
  - *Physical capacity units:* `Machine` (`CNC-2`, `OVEN-1`, `TEST-1`), `Tool` (`FIX-A`, `RACK-7`, `CAL-3`).
- **Important edge types and qualifiers:**
  - *Object-to-Object (O2O) structural/policy edges:* `requires` (`Job -> Component`), `contains` (`ComponentLot -> Component`), `batches` (`Batch -> Job`), `consolidates` (`Shipment -> Job`).
  - *Event-to-Object (E2O) operational co-occurrence edges:* `uses/releases` (`Job <-> Machine`, `Job <-> Tool`), `cures_on` (`Batch <-> Machine`), `cures_with` (`Batch <-> Tool`), `quarantines/releases` (`ComponentLot <-> Job/Component`), and `overrides` (cross-system exception linking `J2` to `B1` during `L7` quarantine).

### Direct observations vs. inferred edges

- **Directly observed edges:** Static O2O declarations (`J1..J6 requires C1..C6`, `L7/L8 contains C*`, `B1/B2 batches J*`, `S1..S3 consolidates J*`) and explicit E2O event bindings—such as the 12:05 `Batch Cure` event linking `{B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J1, J2, J3, C1, C2, C3}`, the 11:45 `Machine Process` linking `{J4, C4, CNC-2, FIX-A}`, the 10:20/13:50 `Quarantine/Release Lot` events linking `{L7, C2, C5, J2, J5}`, and `Final Test` events linking `{J5/J6, TEST-1, CAL-3}`.
- **Inferred edges:** Blocking, contention, and bypass relations must be inferred because the log omits explicit waiting activities (`Wait for Tool`) and cross-system links. Examples include `J3 --[blocks via FIX-A]--> J4` (inferred from `J3`'s 09:00–11:40 hold overlapping `J4`'s 09:50 arrival at `CNC-2`), `B1 --[blocks via RACK-7 cleaning]--> B2`, `J4 --[delays via S1]--> J1`, `J5 --[triggers CAL-3 recalibration blocking]--> J6`, `J2 --[override]--> B1`, and inferred `Job–Tool` hold durations derived by pairing acquire/release timestamps.

### Higher-order motifs (>2 object types)

1. **Batch-Cure Synchronization Hyperedge (`Batch + Machine + Tool + Job + Component`):** At 12:05, a single `Batch Cure` event binds 9 objects across 5 types (`B1`, `OVEN-1`, `RACK-7`, `J1–J3`, `C1–C3`). Any upstream delay to `J3` holds `J1` and `J2` at the barrier and defers `RACK-7` cleaning.
2. **Dual-Resource Machining Gate (`Job + Component + Machine + Tool`):** `J4`'s 11:45 `Machine Process` requires simultaneous availability of `C4`, `CNC-2`, and `FIX-A`. Because `J3` holds `FIX-A` across machine boundaries until 11:40, `CNC-2` sits idle (10:05–10:47) without `J4` being able to start.
3. **Cross-Layer Lot-Batch-Shipment Coupling (`ComponentLot + Component + Job + Batch + Shipment`):** `L7` couples `C2` (`J2`) and `C5` (`J5`). Through `B1` (`J1, J2, J3`), `B2` (`J4, J5`), and `S1–S3` (`{J1,J4}`, `{J2,J3}`, `{J5,J6}`), a lot quarantine on `L7` and a tool delay on `J3` propagate laterally across all six jobs.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

Every calculation below isolates a plausible delay component under an explicit counterfactual; none constitutes standalone causal proof because unobserved constraints (e.g., operator availability, rework triggers, or independent CNC-2 schedules) could co-vary.

1. **Synchronization-induced batch wait for `J1` and `J2` in `B1`:**
   - *Observed:* `J1` ready 08:10; `J2` ready 08:25; `J3` ready 11:55; `B1` starts 12:05.
   - *Calculation:* Total wait for `J1` = `12:05 - 08:10` = **3 h 55 min (235 min)**. Total wait for `J2` = `12:05 - 08:25` = **3 h 40 min (220 min)**. Relative to `J2`'s readiness (08:25), `J1` waited 15 min for `J2` and **3 h 30 min (210 min)** for `J3` (08:25–11:55) plus 10 min final batch staging (11:55–12:05).
   - *Counterfactual assumption:* `OVEN-1` and `RACK-7` were available at 08:10/08:25, single-job or two-job curing (`J1`+`J2`) was technically and policy-permitted without `J3`, and `J2` was not already restricted prior to `L7` quarantine at 10:20.

2. **Delay plausibly associated with `FIX-A` contention for `J4`:**
   - *Observed:* `J3` holds `FIX-A` 09:00–11:40; `J4` arrives at `CNC-2` at 09:50; `CNC-2` is idle 10:05–10:47 (42 min); `J4` `Machine Process` starts at 11:45.
   - *Calculation:* Total `J4` wait at `CNC-2` = `11:45 - 09:50` = **1 h 55 min (115 min)**. Because `CNC-2` was busy 09:50–10:05 (15 min) and 10:47–11:45 (58 min) on other work, the **conservative minimum** delay attributable strictly to `FIX-A` while `CNC-2` sat idle is **42 min** (10:05–10:47), plus 5 min post-release transition (`11:45 - 11:40`). If `CNC-2`'s 10:47–11:45 work was opportunistically pulled forward *only because* `J4` was blocked by `FIX-A`, the upper bound associated with `FIX-A` after `CNC-2` first cleared at 10:05 is **100 min** (`11:45 - 10:05`).
   - *Counterfactual assumption:* `J4` required `FIX-A` immediately upon reaching `CNC-2`, and either `CNC-2`'s 10:47–11:45 run was lower-priority filler (giving 100 min) or non-preemptible independent work (giving 42–47 min).

3. **Delay imposed on `J1` by `S1` shipment consolidation:**
   - *Observed:* `J1` packed at 13:30; `J4` packed at 19:10; `S1` (`J1, J4`) dispatches at 19:25.
   - *Calculation:* Total post-pack residence time for `J1` = `19:25 - 13:30` = **5 h 55 min (355 min)**. Since `S1` dispatches **15 min** after `J4` is packed (19:10–19:25), the consolidation wait strictly waiting for partner `J4` is `19:10 - 13:30` = **5 h 40 min (340 min)**. (Using `S2`'s 25 min post-pack dispatch latency as a baseline for normal shipment loading, `J1` still suffered **330 min** of excess consolidation wait.)
   - *Counterfactual assumption:* Dedicated or split shipment dispatch for `J1` at ~13:45–13:55 was permitted by customer policy and carrier/dock capacity existed at 13:30.

4. **Delay plausibly associated with `CAL-3` for `J6`:**
   - *Observed:* `J5` ready 16:00; `J6` ready 16:05; `J5` tests 16:10–16:35; `CAL-3` recalibrated 16:35–17:20; `TEST-1` idle 16:35–17:25; `J6` tests 17:25–17:50.
   - *Calculation:* Total `J6` wait = `17:25 - 16:05` = **1 h 20 min (80 min)**. This decomposes into **30 min** overlapping `J5`'s test setup/execution (16:05–16:35) and **50 min** during which `TEST-1` is idle waiting on `CAL-3` recalibration (16:35–17:20, 45 min) plus restart/staging (17:20–17:25, 5 min).
   - *Counterfactual assumption:* A second calibrated `CAL-3` tool (or offline recalibration) would allow `J6` to start on `TEST-1` at 16:35 (saving **50 min**), whereas decoupling `J5` from `J6` entirely via a second test/tool pair would save up to **80 min**.

5. **Cross-batch `RACK-7` cleaning vs. `L7` lot delay on `B2` (`J4` and `J5`):**
   - *Observed:* `OVEN-1` idle 12:55–14:50 (115 min); `J4` ready 13:10; `L7` quarantined 10:20–13:50 (210 min); `RACK-7` cleaning/quarantine ends 14:35; `J5` ready 14:40; `B2` starts 14:50.
   - *Calculation:* `J4` waited `14:50 - 13:10` = **1 h 40 min (100 min)** for `B2`. Of that, **85 min** (`14:35 - 13:10`) occurred while `OVEN-1` was idle and `RACK-7` was unavailable. Once `RACK-7` cleared at 14:35, `J5` arrived only **5 min** later (14:40), leaving **15 min** (`14:50 - 14:35`) plausibly linked to `J5`'s post-`L7` arrival and `B2` staging.
   - *Counterfactual assumption:* Had a spare `RACK` existed at 13:10 and `B2` did not wait for `J5`, `J4` could have started 100 min earlier—shifting `J4`'s packing earlier by ~100 min and saving `J1` an equivalent ~100 min in `S1`.

## 4. What flattening misses

Flattening an OCEL onto a single object type projects a multi-dimensional network onto 1D timelines, distorting both control-flow and performance diagnostics:

- **Job flattening:**
  - *Makes visible:* Per-job activity sequence (`Release -> Stage -> Machine -> Inspect -> Rework -> Pack -> Dispatch`), rework loops (2 of 6 jobs), and single-job total durations.
  - *Hides or duplicates:* Hides `L7` lot events (not linked to `Job` at source), `Tool` acquire/release/recalibration states, `J2`'s cross-system override, and batch/shipment partner arrivals.
  - *Misleading conclusion:* Because no job exceeds 1.8 SD above the mean and edge medians sit at 0.2–1.1 h, managers conclude flow is balanced—missing that `J1` spent ~9.8 h passively synchronized (`B1` 235 min + `S1` 355 min) while `J3` spent time actively holding `FIX-A` and `J4` suffered compounded tool/batch waits.
- **Machine flattening:**
  - *Makes visible:* Machine utilization (44%–58%), standard machine cycles, and inter-event gaps (39–73 min).
  - *Hides or duplicates:* Hides jobs queuing off-machine (at `FIX-A`, `B1`/`B2`, `S1`), hides *why* a machine sat idle (e.g., `OVEN-1` idle 12:55–14:50 while `RACK-7` cleaned, or `TEST-1` idle 16:35–17:25 during `CAL-3` recalibration), and fails to explain `OVEN-1`'s 73 min recorded gap vs. 115 min curing idle window.
  - *Misleading conclusion:* "Machines are under-utilized (<60%), so there is no capacity constraint and no queuing problem."
- **Batch flattening:**
  - *Makes visible:* `Batch` lifecycle (`B1`, `B2`), cure durations, and `Batch–Machine`/`Batch–Tool` counts (2 each).
  - *Hides or duplicates:* Hides individual `Job` pre-batch waiting (`J1` 235 min vs `J3` 10 min), duplicates shared machine/tool events if attributed per batch without cross-batch `RACK-7` cleaning links, and hides downstream `Shipment` splitting (`B1` splits across `S1` and `S2`).
  - *Misleading conclusion:* "`B1` and `B2` executed promptly once batched (`B1` 10 min after `J3`, `B2` 10 min after `J5`), so batching adds negligible delay."
- **Tool flattening:**
  - *Makes visible:* High frequency of `Job–Tool` interactions (15) and tool states like `CAL-3` recalibration or `RACK-7` cleaning.
  - *Hides or duplicates:* Hides unlogged waiting (`Wait for Tool` absent), duplicates events when multiple jobs co-occur on `RACK-7` in `B1`, and loses whether a tool hold occurred while a machine was busy or idle.
  - *Misleading conclusion:* "`FIX-A` was only used twice (`J3`, `J4`), so tool utilization is low and non-critical"—ignoring that `J3`'s 160 min hold straddled `J4`'s arrival.
- **ComponentLot flattening:**
  - *Makes visible:* Quality-control lifecycle (`Quarantine Lot` 10:20 -> `Release Lot` 13:50 on `L7`).
  - *Hides or duplicates:* Hides `J2`'s 12:05 override (outside the lot/job log), hides asymmetric downstream impact (`J2` enters `B1`; `J5` delays `B2`), and omits jobs without lot holds (`J4, J6`).
  - *Misleading conclusion:* "Lot quarantine affected `J2` and `J5` identically for 3.5 hours," or conversely (in `Job` view) "Lot quarantine had zero impact."
- **Shipment flattening:**
  - *Makes visible:* Three clean `Create Shipment -> Load -> Dispatch` traces (0.8 h + 0.3 h median).
  - *Hides or duplicates:* Hides the **5 h 40 min** gap between `J1` packing (13:30) and `J4` packing (19:10), and hides that `S1` couples a `B1` job (`J1`) with a `B2` job (`J4`).
  - *Misleading conclusion:* "Outbound logistics is fast and predictable (1.1 h total), so shipping does not delay customer delivery."

### Why low machine utilization coexists with long job waiting

Low machine utilization (44%–58%) and long job waits coexist because **waiting occurs off-machine at secondary gates and synchronization barriers**. When `FIX-A`, `RACK-7`, or `CAL-3` is occupied or under maintenance/cleaning, jobs cannot enter `CNC-2`, `OVEN-1`, or `TEST-1`, forcing both the job *and* the primary machine to wait simultaneously. Meanwhile, batching (`B1`, `B2`) and shipment consolidation (`S1`) force fast jobs (`J1`, `J2`) to sit idle in buffers while machines remain free. Finally, because different jobs wait at different points (`J1` in `B1`/`S1`, `J4` at `FIX-A`/`B2`, `J6` at `CAL-3`), per-job variance stays below 1.8 SD while total system unpredictability stays high.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

### Narrative 1: The `J3 -> FIX-A -> B1 -> J1/J2` double-coupling and `S1` cascade
*(Spans 6 object types: `Job, Component, Tool, Machine, Batch, Shipment`)*

- **Temporal precedence:** `J3` (requiring `C3`) acquires `FIX-A` at 09:00 and releases it at 11:40. `J4` (`C4`) reaches `CNC-2` at 09:50; `CNC-2` sits idle 10:05–10:47; `J4` starts `Machine Process` at 11:45. `J1` (`C1`) and `J2` (`C2`) are batch-ready at 08:10 and 08:25; `J3` becomes ready at 11:55; `B1` cures at 12:05. `J1` packs at 13:30, `J4` packs at 19:10, and `S1` (`J1, J4`) dispatches at 19:25.
- **Resource feasibility:** `FIX-A` cannot be simultaneously mounted for `J3` and `J4`, physically preventing `J4` from using `CNC-2`'s 10:05–10:47 idle slot. `OVEN-1` + `RACK-7` require all `B1` jobs present before the 12:05 `Batch Cure`.
- **Policy constraints:** Batch grouping (`B1` batches `J1, J2, J3`) holds `J1` and `J2` for `J3`. Consolidation (`S1` consolidates `J1, J4`) then holds packed `J1` for `J4` across batches. Thus `J1` is penalized *twice* by `J3`'s `FIX-A` hold: directly via `B1`, and indirectly via `J3 -> FIX-A -> J4 -> B2 -> S1`.
- **Causal speculation:** We cannot prove from timestamps alone whether `J3`'s `FIX-A` hold was unusually long (e.g., due to one of the two logged `Inspect -> Rework` loops) or whether `CNC-2`'s 10:47–11:45 occupancy was caused by another job filling `J4`'s blocked slot versus higher-priority scheduled work.

### Narrative 2: The `L7` lot-quarantine split and `B2 -> TEST-1 -> CAL-3` spillover
*(Spans 7 object types: `ComponentLot, Component, Job, Batch, Tool, Machine, Shipment`)*

- **Temporal precedence:** `L7` contains `C2` (`J2`) and `C5` (`J5`). `J2` reaches batch readiness at 08:25. `Quarantine Lot` hits `L7` at 10:20; `B1` cures with `J2` at 12:05; `Release Lot` occurs at 13:50. `J5` reaches batch readiness at 14:40 (`B2` starts 14:50). Later, `J5` reaches `TEST-1` at 16:00 (5 min before `J6`), tests with `CAL-3` 16:10–16:35, and `CAL-3` recalibrates 16:35–17:20 before `J6` tests 17:25–17:50. `S3` (`J5, J6`) dispatches 35 min after `J6` packs.
- **Resource feasibility:** `TEST-1` cannot run `Final Test` without a calibrated `CAL-3` (leaving `TEST-1` idle 16:35–17:25). `J5` and `J6` cannot test concurrently.
- **Policy constraints:** Quality policy froze `C2` and `C5` at 10:20, yet a separate-system supervisor override bypassed quarantine for `J2` (allowing `B1` at 12:05) while leaving `J5` blocked until 13:50. `S3` consolidation then binds `J5` and `J6` together at dispatch.
- **Causal speculation:** Without knowing `J5`'s pre-10:20 progress, we cannot prove `L7` alone delayed `J5` until 14:40 (e.g., `J5` may have required rework or earlier `CNC-2` time 10:47–11:45). Nor can we prove `J5`'s test *triggered* `CAL-3` recalibration rather than coinciding with a fixed 16:35 calibration schedule.

### Narrative 3: Cross-batch `RACK-7` coupling and phantom `OVEN-1` slack
*(Spans 5 object types: `Batch, Tool, Machine, Job, Component`)*

- **Temporal precedence:** `B1` (`J1–J3`, `C1–C3`) starts `Batch Cure` on `OVEN-1` with `RACK-7` at 12:05. `OVEN-1` becomes idle at 12:55 until 14:50. `J4` (`C4`) is ready for `B2` at 13:10; `RACK-7` remains in cleaning/quarantine until 14:35; `J5` (`C5`) is ready at 14:40; `B2` starts at 14:50.
- **Resource feasibility:** Even though `OVEN-1` is physically empty from 12:55 to 14:50, `RACK-7` is a single shared carrier undergoing cleaning/quarantine until 14:35, rendering `B2` physically infeasible at 13:10.
- **Policy constraints:** Mandatory rack cleaning/quarantine between batches and `B2` pairing (`J4, J5`) serialize `B1` and `B2` through `RACK-7` rather than `OVEN-1`.
- **Causal speculation:** It is unconfirmed whether `RACK-7` cleaning took 100 min (12:55–14:35) due to `J2`'s overridden lot material in `B1` ("quarantine"), normal thermal cool-down, or unlogged `Unload Batch` delays (which could also explain the 73 min recorded `OVEN-1` gap).

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

1. **Max-wait batch release timeout on `OVEN-1` (Synchronization reduction)**
   - *Target mechanism:* Batch synchronization barriers (`B1, B2`).
   - *Expected benefit:* Caps wait time for early-ready jobs (e.g., releasing `J1+J2` after a 60 min cap instead of waiting 235 min for `J3`).
   - *Helped:* `J1, J2, J4` (earlier cure and earlier `S1`/`S2` readiness).
   - *Harmed:* Straggler jobs (`J3`, `J5`) forced into solo trailing batches, plus `OVEN-1`/`RACK-7` effective capacity (more batch cycles).
   - *New cost/risk:* Higher energy/oven cycles; potential `RACK-7` cleaning collisions (`B1` trailing batch delaying `B2` even further).
   - *Perverse incentive metric:* Measuring *only* "Job batch-wait time" incentivizes tiny 1-job batches that exhaust `OVEN-1` and `RACK-7` availability.
   - *Object-centric evaluation:* Compare `Job` total flow time alongside `Batch` fill rate, `OVEN-1` cycle count, and `RACK-7` cross-batch blocking intervals via OCDFG performance edges.

2. **Secondary tooling pool / offline calibration swap (`FIX-A`, `RACK-7`, `CAL-3`) (Tooling)**
   - *Target mechanism:* Shared-tool contention and state locks.
   - *Expected benefit:* Eliminates machine starvation during `CNC-2` (42–100 min), `OVEN-1` (85 min), and `TEST-1` (50 min) idle windows.
   - *Helped:* `J4` (at `CNC-2` and `B2`), `J6` (at `TEST-1`), `J1` (indirectly via `J4` in `S1`), and `B2` decoupling from `B1`.
   - *Harmed:* Quality/maintenance teams (more fixtures to calibrate/clean); potentially `TEST-1` if unblocked arrivals create a genuine `Machine` queue.
   - *New cost/risk:* Capex for duplicate tools; calibration drift across multiple `FIX-A` or `CAL-3` units increasing `Inspect -> Rework` rates.
   - *Perverse incentive metric:* Tracking "Tool utilization %" encourages hoarding/batching jobs per tool or skipping `CAL-3` recalibration / `RACK-7` cleaning.
   - *Object-centric evaluation:* Track `Machine` idle-while-`Job`-waiting co-occurrence graphs (`Job–Machine–Tool` hyperedges) and rework rates conditioned on tool ID (`CAL-3a` vs `CAL-3b`).

3. **Time-boxed split-shipment policy (`S1–S3`) (Shipment policy)**
   - *Target mechanism:* Shipment consolidation delays (`S1`).
   - *Expected benefit:* Releases finished jobs (`J1` packed 13:30) after a max consolidation window (e.g., 60–90 min) instead of waiting 355 min for cross-batch partners (`J4`).
   - *Helped:* Early-packed jobs (`J1`, and potentially `J2/J3` or `J5/J6` under asymmetric delays).
   - *Harmed:* Freight cost per job; receiving customers who require single-invoice consolidated deliveries.
   - *New cost/risk:* Higher transport cost, dock congestion, and split-delivery administration.
   - *Perverse incentive metric:* Optimizing "Packed-to-Dispatch lead time" alone encourages partial shipping of everything, blowing up freight budgets.
   - *Object-centric evaluation:* Measure `Shipment` consolidation wait (`max(Pack) - min(Pack)` per `Shipment`) jointly with freight cost per `Job` and `O2O` batch-to-shipment alignment (`B1` jobs grouped into `S1`).

4. **Object-centric telemetry & cross-system logging upgrade (Logging-only)**
   - *Target mechanism:* Data-integration and observability artifacts.
   - *Expected benefit:* Makes hidden coupling auditable without altering shop-floor physics: introduces explicit `Wait for Tool` / `Wait for Partner` milestones, links `ComponentLot` events and supervisor overrides to `Job` IDs, and anchors `Shipment` cycle time at first-job `Pack`.
   - *Helped:* Planners, quality engineers, and `J2/J5` auditability.
   - *Harmed:* Operators/supervisors facing higher logging overhead or scrutiny over informal overrides (like `J2`).
   - *New cost/risk:* ERP/MES integration effort; alert fatigue if unactionable waiting events flood logs.
   - *Perverse incentive metric:* Measuring "Override count" punitively drives supervisors to create shadow workarounds off-system.
   - *Object-centric evaluation:* Audit E2O completeness (% of `ComponentLot` and `Tool` events linked to `Job`), reconcile `Machine` idle gaps against active `Tool`/`Batch` holds, and verify zero unattributed delays >15 min.

5. **Lot-aware batch assignment & quarantine isolation (Lot/Policy coupling)**
   - *Target mechanism:* `ComponentLot` coupling (`L7`) and cross-batch contamination (`RACK-7` quarantine).
   - *Expected benefit:* Prevents quarantined or at-risk lot components (`C2`, `C5`) from entering shared `B1` runs (avoiding `RACK-7` extended quarantine) or stalling clean partners (`J4` in `B2`).
   - *Helped:* `J4` (unblocked `B2`), `J1/J3` (shielded from lot risk in `B1`), and quality compliance.
   - *Harmed:* `J2` (loses early `B1` override shortcut, waiting until 13:50 `Release Lot`).
   - *New cost/risk:* Stranded lot capacity if `L7` waits separately; potential friction between production throughput targets and QA holds.
   - *Perverse incentive metric:* Measuring "Batch on-time start" encourages unauthorized overrides like `J2`'s to fill `B1`.
   - *Object-centric evaluation:* Track `ComponentLot -> Batch -> Tool` contagion paths (`L7` quarantine overlap with `RACK-7` cleaning duration) and `Job` compliance rate.

## 7. PM4Py analysis outline

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd

# Assumes `ocel` is an already-loaded pm4py.OCEL object
# 1. Object interaction summary & object-type / event-type graphs
interactions_df = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)          # Object-Type Graph (coupled object types)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)        # Event-Type to Object-Type bindings
obj_graph = pm4py.discover_objects_graph(ocel, graph_type="object_interaction")

# Convert to NetworkX to inspect multi-type motifs (e.g., B1 <-> RACK-7 <-> B2, L7 <-> J2/J5)
nx_graph = pm4py.convert_ocel_to_networkx(ocel)

# 2. Discover Object-Centric DFG and compare two object-centric frequency metrics:
#    - Event-couple count (number of event transitions)
#    - Unique-object count (number of distinct objects traversing an edge)
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)

freq_comparison = []
for ot, edges in ocdfg["edges"]["event_couples"].items():
    for (act_src, act_tgt), eid_set in edges.items():
        # Metric 1: raw event-transition frequency (eid couples)
        event_freq = len(eid_set)
        # Metric 2: unique interacting objects for that object type along the edge
        unique_obj_freq = len(ocdfg["edges"]["unique_objects"][ot].get((act_src, act_tgt), set()))
        # Metric 3: total object occurrences (captures batching/multi-object fan-out)
        total_obj_freq = len(ocdfg["edges"]["total_objects"][ot].get((act_src, act_tgt), set()))
        freq_comparison.append({
            "object_type": ot,
            "edge": f"{act_src} -> {act_tgt}",
            "event_couple_freq": event_freq,
            "unique_object_freq": unique_obj_freq,
            "total_object_freq": total_obj_freq,
            "fanout_ratio": total_obj_freq / max(event_freq, 1),
        })
freq_df = pd.DataFrame(freq_comparison)

# 3. Compare flattened DFGs across Job, Machine, Batch, Tool, Shipment, ComponentLot
#    Note: We DO NOT infer waiting solely from Machine event gaps; instead we retain
#    multi-object event relations below to separate tool/batch/lot/shipment holds.
perf_summaries = {}
for ot in ["Job", "Machine", "Batch", "Tool", "ComponentLot", "Shipment"]:
    flat_df = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, ot)
    perf_dfg, starts, ends = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(flat_df)
    perf_summaries[ot] = {"perf_dfg": perf_dfg, "starts": starts, "ends": ends}

# 4. Extract object-centric features per Job (e.g., interacting object counts,
#    lifecycle duration, and multi-type degree) instead of machine-gap heuristics
job_features, feature_names = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(ocel, "Job")
job_feat_df = pd.DataFrame(job_features, columns=feature_names)

# 5. Cross-object waiting decomposition (linking Job readiness to Batch/Tool/Shipment events
#    rather than attributing machine idle gaps directly to machine capacity)
events = ocel.events
relations = ocel.relations
job_rel = relations[relations["ocel:type"] == "Job"].merge(
    events[["ocel:eid", "ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp"]], on="ocel:eid"
)
co_objects = relations.merge(
    job_rel[["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid"]].rename(columns={"ocel:oid": "job_id"}),
    on="ocel:eid",
)
# Inspect jobs co-occurring with Tools, Batches, ComponentLots, and Shipments
multi_type_exposure = co_objects.groupby(["job_id", "ocel:type"])["ocel:oid"].nunique().unstack(fill_value=0)
print(freq_df.sort_values("fanout_ratio", ascending=False).head(15))
print(multi_type_exposure)
```

## 8. Falsification and additional data

### Six observations that would weaken the top-ranked explanations (Batch synchronization & Shared-tool contention)

1. **Unrecorded downstream/upstream rework on `J1, J2, J4`:** Evidence showing `J1` and `J2` actually required off-log rework or cooling between 08:10/08:25 and 11:55—meaning their "ready for batch" timestamps were inaccurate and `B1` did not truly force a 3.5–3.9 h wait.
2. **Duplicate unreported tooling:** Discovery that spare `FIX-A` or `RACK-7` units existed and `J4`/`B2` waited on missing operators, material kitting, or safety sign-offs rather than tool occupancy.
3. **Mandatory non-preemptible `CNC-2` work (10:47–11:45):** Evidence that `CNC-2`'s 10:47–11:45 run was a higher-priority job (e.g., `J5` or `J6`) that would have bumped `J4` even if `FIX-A` had been free at 09:50.
4. **Customer-mandated `S1` delivery synchronization:** Evidence that `S1`'s customer contract forbids split shipments *and* refuses deliveries before 19:00, making `J1`'s 13:30–19:10 wait a scheduled delivery window rather than a consolidation penalty.
5. **Deterministic periodic `CAL-3` recalibration:** Proof that `CAL-3`'s 16:35–17:20 calibration was a fixed daily shift break rather than test-triggered, and `TEST-1` also required operator shift handover during that window.
6. **Pre-existing `J4` / `J5` rework correlation:** If the two `Inspect -> Rework` loops belonged to `J3` and `J4` (or `J5`), shifting the dominant bottleneck from cross-job coupling to single-job quality yield loss.

### Six additional fields or event types to discriminate among hypotheses

1. **Explicit `Wait Started` / `Blocked By` events with `blocking_oid`:** Distinguishes `Tool` waits (`FIX-A`), `Machine` queues (`CNC-2` 10:47–11:45), `Operator` absence, and `Batch` partner waits.
2. **`Tool State` lifecycle events (`Calibrate`, `Clean`, `Quarantine`, `Mount/Unmount`):** Separates mandatory post-`B1` contamination cleaning on `RACK-7` from normal cool-down, and distinguishes `J5`-triggered vs. scheduled `CAL-3` recalibration.
3. **Operator/Technician object type (`Worker`) linked to all E2O events:** Tests whether `OVEN-1` (12:55–14:50) and `TEST-1` (16:35–17:25) idle windows were actually operator-constrained rather than tool-constrained.
4. **Direct `Job` links on `ComponentLot` (`Quarantine`/`Release`) and `Override` events (plus `override_reason` and `approver`):** Quantifies whether `J2`'s `B1` entry caused `RACK-7`'s quarantine and whether `J5` was genuinely ready before 13:50.
5. **Granular `Batch` & `Shipment` boundary timestamps (`Ready for Batch`, `Batch Staging Complete`, `Job Packed`, `Shipment Cut-off`, `Carrier Arrival`):** Separates internal consolidation wait from external carrier arrival delays (`S1–S3`), and reconciles `OVEN-1`'s 73 min inter-event gap against its 115 min idle window.
6. **Job priority, rework attribution (`Rework` linked to `Component` and `Tool`), and due-date fields:** Distinguishes policy-driven scheduling (`CNC-2` 10:47–11:45) and quality defects from pure physical contention.
