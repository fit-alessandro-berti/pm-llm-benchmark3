## 1. Feasibility model

```
Partial-order relations:
- Register < Triage
- Triage < (Identity Check  Document Check)
- Identity Check < Assessment
- Document Check < Assessment
- Assessment < (Compliance Review  Decision) [if high-risk/high-value]
- Compliance Review < Decision
- Decision < Notify
- Request More Info  new Document Check  new Assessment  (new Compliance Review if high-risk/high-value)  Decision
- Four-eyes rule: final Assessment  final Compliance Review
```

```
Constraints:
1. Identity Check and Document Check may run concurrently if no originals required.
2. Originals Receipt must precede Document Check when originals required.
3. High-risk/high-value cases require Compliance Review after latest Assessment and before Decision.
4. Request More Info triggers rework requiring new Document Check and Assessment (and Compliance Review if applicable).
5. Resources cannot perform two service intervals simultaneously.
6. C1 is the only certified compliance reviewer in the window.
7. D1 is faster on complex corporate documents; D2 is slower but more available.
8. Assessment queues may grow due to training or workload shifts.
```

Interventions alter:
- **Timing only**: K-219, K-411 (reordering without changing routing or policy)
- **Routing**: K-104 (adds parallel preliminary escalation path)
- **Decision policy**: K-337 (prohibits second rework loop, changing allowed outcomes)

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

**Factual mechanism:**
The case suffered from a sanctions ambiguity that was only escalated after Assessment, leading to a late Escalation Advice and a full re-read of evidence during Compliance Review. The long Document Check queue (D1) and Assessment queue (A1) delayed the discovery of the issue.

**Minimal intervention:**
At Triage, a preliminary escalation packet is sent to S1, and a provisional slot is reserved for C1. Preliminary compliance work (e.g., initial sanctions screening) can proceed in parallel with Identity and Document checks. Final Compliance Review still follows Assessment but benefits from preliminary work.

**Temporal propagation:**
- Preliminary work by S1 and C1 starts immediately after Triage, overlapping with Identity and Document checks.
- Compliance Review duration reduces by 1–3 h due to preliminary evidence review.
- C1’s provisional slot reservation may delay other urgent compliance cases (e.g., two urgent cases added Monday 16:00).
- Identity and Document queues are unaffected, but Assessment queue may see slightly earlier arrival due to faster downstream flow.

**Process-feasibility check:**
- Four-eyes rule preserved: final Assessment (A1) and final Compliance Review (C1) are performed by different resources.
- Compliance Review still occurs after Assessment, as required.
- No violation of partial-order constraints.

**Best/base/worst case:**
- **Best**: Packet is complete and accurate; preliminary work reduces Compliance Review by 3 h; no delay to other cases. Cycle time: -24 h.
- **Base**: Packet is partially complete; preliminary work saves 1 h; C1 reservation delays one other case by 2 h. Cycle time: -12 h.
- **Worst**: Packet is incomplete, causing rework; C1 reservation blocks a critical case for 6 h. Cycle time: -6 h (net neutral due to delays elsewhere).

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:**
- If preliminary packet is so incomplete that it delays Identity or Document checks (e.g., requiring additional information upfront), the intervention could increase cycle time.
- If C1’s provisional slot reservation causes a high-priority case to miss a deadline, the intervention may be deemed harmful.

### K-219

**Factual mechanism:**
The case required Document Check by D1 due to handwritten documents, but D1’s queue was long (11 h at Mon 16:00). The handwriting was difficult but legible, and no rework was needed.

**Minimal intervention:**
Assign Document Check to D2 immediately after Originals Receipt, instead of waiting for D1. D2 is slower but more available.

**Temporal propagation:**
- Document Check starts 10–12 h earlier (Tue 09:30  Mon 21:30 if D2 starts immediately after Originals Receipt at Mon 17:30).
- D2’s slower processing (4–8 h for standard handwritten docs) may increase Assessment queue congestion.
- Higher chance of Request More Info due to D2’s lower familiarity with handwriting (15–30% risk).
- If rework occurs, cycle time increases by 8–36 h (external response time).

**Process-feasibility check:**
- No change to routing or compliance constraints.
- Four-eyes rule preserved.

**Best/base/worst case:**
- **Best**: D2 processes without issues; no rework. Cycle time: -10 h.
- **Base**: D2 requests clarification; rework loop adds 24 h. Cycle time: +14 h.
- **Worst**: Rework loop triggers two additional loops; cycle time: +48 h.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:**
- If D2’s processing reveals a critical error that D1 would have caught, the decision quality may suffer, justifying the longer D1 queue.

### K-337

**Factual mechanism:**
The case required two Request More Info loops due to inconsistent supplier data, each causing 8–36 h delays. The supplier response was slow and inconsistent.

**Minimal intervention:**
After Assessment 2, prohibit the second Request More Info loop. Require a decision using available evidence, with conditions or rejection as options.

**Temporal propagation:**
- Eliminates the second Request More Info (Thu 10:30  Mon 09:00) and subsequent Document Check 3 and Assessment 3.
- Compliance Review still occurs, but may flag unresolved discrepancies.
- Decision occurs 2–3 days earlier, but with higher risk of suboptimal outcome.

**Process-feasibility check:**
- Changes decision policy: allows approval with conditions or rejection, even if evidence is incomplete.
- Compliance Review may still require full evidence, creating a conflict if the decision is made without it.

**Best/base/worst case:**
- **Best**: Supplier discrepancy is minor; decision is approve with conditions; no appeal. Cycle time: -48 h.
- **Base**: Decision is reject; appeal risk increases. Cycle time: -48 h.
- **Worst**: Compliance Review flags unresolved discrepancy; decision is overturned on appeal; compliance defensibility weakened. Cycle time: -24 h (net neutral).

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:**
- If the unresolved discrepancy is material to compliance (e.g., sanctions-related), the intervention may violate regulatory requirements, making it infeasible.

### K-411

**Factual mechanism:**
A case lock prevented Document Check from starting until Identity Check completed, despite no originals requirement. This added 4 h to cycle time.

**Minimal intervention:**
Create both verification tasks at Triage and allow concurrent work by I2 and D2, with real-time logging to avoid false concurrency.

**Temporal propagation:**
- Document Check starts 2–4 h earlier (Tue 15:00  Tue 11:00 if D2 starts immediately after Triage).
- Assessment queue grows due to earlier arrival of cases.
- Logging errors may create false concurrency, violating compliance or four-eyes rules.

**Process-feasibility check:**
- No change to routing or compliance constraints.
- Four-eyes rule preserved.

**Best/base/worst case:**
- **Best**: Real-time logging is perfect; no false concurrency. Cycle time: -12 h.
- **Base**: Minor logging errors; no compliance impact. Cycle time: -8 h.
- **Worst**: Logging errors cause false concurrency; compliance review flags the case. Cycle time: +4 h (net negative).

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:**
- If real-time logging is unreliable, the intervention may introduce compliance risks that outweigh time savings.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|--------------|-------------------------------|------------------------|-----------------------|---------------------------|----------------------------|------------------|
| K-104 | +1 h C1 (preliminary work) | Compliance queue (slightly) | Compliance queue (if reservation blocks urgent case) | Other high-risk cases | Neutral to negative | Reservation of scarce C1 capacity |
| K-219 | +4 to +8 h D2; +15–30% rework risk | Document queue (D1) | Assessment queue (if rework occurs) | Cases routed to D2; cases requiring D1 | Negative if rework occurs | Substitution between D1 and D2; rework loop risk |
| K-337 | -24 to -48 h per case | Assessment queue; Document queue | Compliance queue (if decision is overturned) | Cases with slow external responses | Positive if decision quality holds | Decision policy change; appeal risk |
| K-411 | +0 h (same work) | Identity/Document queues | Assessment queue | Cases arriving at Assessment after Tue 09:00 | Neutral to negative | Logging reliability; queue displacement |

### 100-case weekly rollout analysis:
Applying the same policy to 100 similar cases in one week would amplify interference effects.

- **K-104**: Reserving C1 slots for 100 cases would create a severe bottleneck. C1’s capacity is already tight (16–20 h queue at Mon 08:00). Even if each case only reserves 1–2 h, the total reservation could exceed 100–200 h, delaying other urgent cases by days. The intervention’s benefit (earlier compliance input) would be offset by systemic delays. A pilot should limit reservations to a small fraction of C1’s capacity.

- **K-219**: Routing 100 cases to D2 would reduce D1’s queue but increase D2’s load. D2’s slower processing (4–8 h vs. D1’s 3–6 h) could shift the bottleneck to Document Check. If 20% of cases require rework (due to D2’s lower familiarity), the Assessment queue could grow by 20 cases, delaying other cases by 40–100 h. The intervention is only beneficial if D2’s error rate is low and D1’s queue is consistently long.

- **K-337**: Prohibiting rework loops for 100 cases would reduce cycle time by 2,400–4,800 h (10–20 days of work). However, if 10% of cases have material unresolved discrepancies, the decision quality could degrade, leading to appeals or compliance violations. The systemic risk is high: a 10% appeal rate could overwhelm the Decision team and create a new bottleneck. A pilot should monitor decision reversals and compliance flags closely.

- **K-411**: Enabling concurrency for 100 cases would reduce cycle time by 800–1,600 h (3–7 days). However, if real-time logging fails for even 5% of cases, the compliance team could spend 40–80 h investigating false concurrency, creating a new bottleneck. The intervention’s success depends entirely on logging reliability, which is untested.

**Key nonlinearities:**
- Reservation of scarce compliance capacity (K-104) has a threshold effect: a small increase in reservations can cause a large systemic delay.
- Rework loop risk (K-219, K-337) is multiplicative: a small increase in error rate can cause a large increase in downstream workload.
- Logging reliability (K-411) is a binary risk: if it fails, the intervention becomes harmful.

## 6. Pilot designs

| pilot element | intervention 1 (K-337) | intervention 2 (K-411) |
|---------------|------------------------|------------------------|
| **Eligibility** | High-risk/high-value cases with external supplier data; exclude cases with material compliance flags | Cases with no originals required and digital documents; exclude cases with complex handwriting |
| **Assignment rule** | Randomly assign 20% of eligible cases to intervention; control group continues with rework loops | Randomly assign 50% of eligible cases to intervention; control group continues with case lock |
| **Sample/support logic** | 50 cases per arm; monitor decision quality, appeal rates, compliance flags | 100 cases per arm; monitor logging errors, false concurrency, Assessment queue growth |
| **Primary outcome** | Cycle time reduction; decision reversals; compliance flags | Cycle time reduction; logging error rate; false concurrency incidents |
| **Process-path outcome** | Proportion of cases requiring rework loops; proportion of cases approved with conditions | Proportion of cases with concurrent Identity/Document checks; Assessment queue delay |
| **Quality outcome** | Decision correctness; appeal rate; compliance defensibility | Decision correctness; compliance review flags |
| **Queue-spillover outcome** | Compliance queue displacement; Assessment queue changes | Assessment queue growth; Identity/Document queue changes |
| **Logging validation** | Manual review of 10% of cases for compliance flags | Automated logging audit for concurrency violations |
| **Stopping rule** | Stop if appeal rate > 15% or compliance flags > 10% | Stop if logging error rate > 5% or false concurrency > 2% |

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is **K-337 (suppress second rework loop)**, despite its potential to change decision policy. It offers the highest expected benefit in cycle time reduction while maintaining reversibility and providing clear metrics for decision quality. The principal risk—suboptimal decisions leading to appeals—is measurable and can be mitigated with a pilot that monitors appeal rates and compliance flags.

The most dangerous intervention is **K-219 (different document resource)**, despite its potential speed. Routing cases to D2 shifts workload from D1 to D2 and introduces a high risk of rework loops, which can overwhelm the Assessment queue and create systemic delays. The intervention’s benefit is highly sensitive to D2’s error rate, which is untested.

A local case counterfactual is least transportable to a system-wide policy in **K-104 (earlier escalation/compliance preparation)**. The intervention’s benefit depends on C1’s capacity and the completeness of preliminary packets. System-wide application would create a severe bottleneck, making the intervention harmful at scale despite its local benefits.

Additional event or queue data most valuable:
- **Real-time logging accuracy** (for K-411),
- **D2’s error rate on handwritten documents** (for K-219),
- **C1’s capacity utilization and queue displacement** (for K-104),
- **Appeal rates and compliance flags** (for K-337).

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **Structural**: The partial-order relations and constraints accurately reflect the process rules.
2. **Temporal**: Queue forecasts are uncertain but directionally correct; service-time ranges are rough estimates.
3. **Resource**: C1 is the only certified compliance reviewer; D1/D2 have fixed speed differences.
4. **Behavioral**: External supplier responses are slow and inconsistent; preliminary packets may be incomplete.
5. **Quality**: D2’s lower familiarity with handwriting increases rework risk; preliminary compliance work reduces review time.
6. **Interference**: Reservation of C1 capacity displaces other cases; rework loops amplify downstream workload.
7. **Compliance**: Four-eyes rule and compliance review requirements are non-negotiable unless explicitly changed.
8. **Logging**: Real-time logging is reliable unless stated otherwise.
