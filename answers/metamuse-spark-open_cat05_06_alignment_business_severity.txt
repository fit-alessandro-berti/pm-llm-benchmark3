## 1. Materiality Framework

**Severity is business risk, not edit distance.** Technical `raw_cost` sums `L/M` moves under a uniform cost function (1 per visible move). Business severity weights the control objective that failed, financial exposure, reversibility, timing relative to irreversibility, customer/regulatory harm, recurrence, and evidence reliability.

We assess every case on eight dimensions:

1. **Control objective**: Is the violated control preventive (blocks payment) vs detective? Payment controls and fraud screening are preventive and critical.
2. **Amount / exposure**: EUR amount and fraud-risk level (LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH), beneficiary-bank change (classic fraud red flag).
3. **Reversibility & timing**: `Pay Claim` is irreversible once accepted by the bank. Pay-before-approval cannot be repaired by a later `M(Pay)` in the alignment; the alignment repair is a model fiction.
4. **Customer / regulatory harm**: Failure to notify, wrongful denial, or wrongful payment to fraudster.
5. **Recurrence**: Isolated vs pattern indicative of systematic logging, model, or control defect.
6. **Evidence reliability**: Core log only vs corroborated by CRM/portal/external licensed adjuster with stable linkage. A missing event is not proof the activity did not occur; we weigh alternate evidence and link integrity.
7. **Model scope**: The tree was designed for *payable* claims per P6. Deviations that reflect claims correctly leaving the payable scope (denied, withdrawn) are model-mismatch, not operational failure.
8. **Data-aware policy overlay**: P1-P3 impose amount/risk/bank-change conditions not encoded in the tree. A fully synchronous alignment can still be a material policy failure.

**Severity scale (1–5):**

* **1 – No material risk:** Fitting trace, harmless telemetry/retry, scope-excluded outcome with no payment, or silent `tau` routing. No exposure.
* **2 – Low:** Minor evidence-linkage weakness, alternate evidence likely exists, low amount, no irreversibility, easily verifiable.
* **3 – Moderate:** Data-aware rule breach with medium amount/risk, or notification evidence fragile but within SLA.
* **4 – High:** Material policy breach with significant exposure, fraud indicator (bank change + HIGH risk), or systematic approval-rule bypass.
* **5 – Critical:** Severe preventive control failure with large/irreversible payment: missing mandatory fraud screen on HIGH-risk/high-value or payment released *before* required approval (P3), especially with recent bank change.

**Confidence vs Severity:** Confidence is epistemic certainty in our interpretation given evidence quality; Severity is potential business impact *if* our interpretation is correct. High severity can coexist with low confidence (e.g., mutable CRM link) and vice versa. We never treat `M(A)` as proof A did not happen – it proves A has no *matched log event* in the core log under the current labeling/correlation. We seek corroborating evidence and linkage tests before concluding a control was omitted.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

We do not infer `M(A)` = A never happened. Each pattern needs discriminating evidence.

**CL-602 – Missing Fraud Screen on 480k HIGH + bank change:**
* H1 – *Step skipped under time pressure/bypass:* Fraud screen intentionally skipped, verbal claim is post-hoc rationalization. 
* H2 – *Performed offline but not logged:* Analyst called fraud desk, no ticket created.
* H3 – *Logging failure:* Fraud tool executed but event not ingested.
* **Tests:** Pull fraud system audit trail for case ID/claimant ID/date range; interview approver + fraud desk with calendar/phone logs; check bank-change alert queue and whether fraud screen is mandatory gate; examine approver's historical bypass rate.

**CL-604 – Pay 10:03 before Approval 10:20:**
* H1 – *Control bypass/race:* Payment service does not wait for approval; approval later is rubber-stamp.
* H2 – *Clock/log delay:* Approval occurred earlier but logged 17 min late due to batch.
* H3 – *Model repair illusion:* No real early payment, just mis-correlated duplicate Pay events.
* **Tests:** Compare bank’s acceptance log vs core `Pay Claim` event ID; check approval UI clickstream timestamp vs DB commit; verify if second `M(Pay)` in alignment corresponds to real second disbursement or zero; review payment API authorization check – does it query approval state? Check other high-value cases for same temporal inversion.

**CL-606 – External adjuster Assess Loss:**
* H1 – *Correct substance, integration gap* (preferred): Licensed adjuster report at 10:05 is valid Assess Loss, Fraud/Manual after is compliant.
* H2 – *Assess Loss truly missing, external doc is unrelated/expired:* Report is for different damage or prior year.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Uniform `raw_cost` is a syntactic edit distance under a cost function that assigns 1 to every visible `L` or `M` and 0 to `T` and `S`. It has no concept of amount, risk, irreversibility, timing, evidence reliability, model scope, or policy data conditions. Five+ cases prove the divergence:

**1. Zero cost can hide critical policy failure (data conditions).** CL-609 (EUR 18k MEDIUM auto-approved) and CL-610 (EUR 4.5k with bank change 1h auto-approved) are *fully synchronous* `raw_cost 0`. The tree permits `Auto Approve` as a control-flow choice, but P1/P2 forbid it on these data values. A regulator or fraud scenario would rate CL-610 **Critical (5)** – paying a fresh bank account without fraud screening – yet cost says “perfect”. This is **cost-function design blindness** to data-aware rules: uniform costs weight only *which* activity happened, not *under what business data*.

**2. Low cost can be maximal risk (missing mandatory preventive control).** CL-602 `raw_cost 1` (`M Fraud Screen`) vs CL-603 `raw_cost 1` duplicate notification. Identical cost, opposite risk: CL-602 is EUR 480k HIGH-risk + recent bank change paid without documented fraud screen – systemic fraud exposure and audit breach – while CL-603 is a harmless transport retry with same message ID. Cost collapses a **preventive control omission on high exposure** to the same penalty as technical noise, ignoring control objective, amount, and recurrence.

**3. Same cost conflates irreversibility with noise (timing & reversibility).** CL-604 `raw_cost 3` and CL-601 `raw_cost 3` are equal. CL-601’s 3 is three UI telemetry log moves (no money moved). CL-604’s 3 is `L(Pay)` *before* required Fraud/Manual + `M(Fraud)` + `M(Pay)` repair – i.e., an **irreversible payment released before approval**, proven by bank timestamp 10:03 vs approval 10:20. P3 states this is severe even if alignment repairs cheaply: the second `M(Pay)` is a *model fiction* to re-sync, not a second real payment. The cost function is **order-insensitive** and **reversibility-blind**; it treats a premature real disbursement identically to autosave clicks.

**4. High cost can be zero risk (scope incompleteness).** CL-607 `raw_cost 4` and CL-612 `raw_cost 4` are the month’s highest, yet both reflect **model scope mismatch, not operational failure**: a correctly denied non-payable lead (no payment intended) and a correctly withdrawn claim (customer-initiated termination). Per P6, the tree was designed for payable claims; penalizing non-progression through `M(Assess)/M(Auto)/M(Pay)` measures *model incompleteness*, not compliance. Ranking by raw cost would prioritize investigating correct denials over EUR 480k fraud-screen omissions – a **scope vs deviation confusion**.

**5. Cost overstates risk when evidence lives outside core (missing-but-external evidence).** CL-606 `raw_cost 1` (`M Assess Loss`) suggests assessment omitted, yet a licensed external adjuster report at 10:05 exists and fraud/approval followed – substantive control satisfied. Similarly CL-608 `M Notify` `raw_cost 1` counts a missing core notification even though P4 explicitly allows CRM/portal notification if linked, and a CRM message at +3h exists (though linkage mutable). Technical cost cannot distinguish **core log absence** from **control absence**; severity must weigh alternate evidence reliability.

**6. Hidden moves and granularity expose additional blind spots.** CL-605 includes `T(tau,c=0)` – a silent routing step from tree-to-net conversion that is *fitting* with zero cost, correctly adding no risk. Conversely CL-611 `raw_cost 2` (`L Assess Damage` + `M Assess Loss`) is a pure **label synonym** after a mapping table update; two moves penalize vocabulary drift, not a missed assessment (EUR 62k HIGH-risk controls otherwise done). Cost is **granularity- and labeling-sensitive** while business risk is vocabulary-agnostic once alias is ratified.

In sum, alignment cost is useful for **diagnosing log-model edit distance** but must be kept in a separate column from business severity. Real risk requires overlaying P1-P5 data conditions, timestamp ordering vs bank acceptance, irreversibility per P3, evidence linkage integrity, and scope charter – none of which the uniform 1-per-move function encodes.

    if "T tau" in row["supplied_moves"]:
        flags.append("Silent tau (fitting)")
    if "M Assess Loss" in row["supplied_moves"] and row["case_id"] in ["CL-606"]:
        flags.append("Missing Assess with external evidence")
    if "M Notify" in row["supplied_moves"]:
        flags.append("Missing Notify (alternate channel?)")
    if "L Deny" in row["supplied_moves"] or "L Withdraw" in row["supplied_moves"]:
        flags.append("Out-of-scope (denied/withdrawn)")
    if "Assess Damage" in row["supplied_moves"]:
        flags.append("Label mismatch")
    if row["raw_cost"]==0 and "VIOLATION" in row["policy_overlay"]:
        flags.append("Zero-cost data-aware violation")
    if "P3 severe" in row["policy_overlay"] or ("M Fraud" in row["supplied_moves"] and row["amount_eur"]>50000):
        flags.append("Severe control failure (fraud/missing/pay-before)")
    return "; ".join(flags) if flags else "Fitting/compliant"

df["deviation_pattern"] = df.apply(flag_pattern, axis=1)

# Aggregation table
agg = df.groupby("deviation_pattern").agg(
    cases=("case_id","count"),
    case_list=("case_id", lambda x: ", ".join(x)),
    avg_raw_cost=("raw_cost","mean"),
    total_exposure_eur=("amount_eur","sum"),
    high_risk_count=("risk", lambda x: (x=="HIGH").sum()),
    max_severity=("business_severity_1_5","max")
).reset_index().sort_values("max_severity", ascending=False)

print("\n=== Risk-Prioritization Evidence Table (separate columns) ===")
print("Technical raw_cost  business severity. Use both columns for triage.\n")
evidence_table = df[["case_id","raw_cost","amount_eur","risk","policy_overlay","business_severity_1_5","confidence","deviation_pattern"]]
print(evidence_table.to_string(index=False))

print("\n=== Pattern Aggregation ===")
print(agg.to_string(index=False))
