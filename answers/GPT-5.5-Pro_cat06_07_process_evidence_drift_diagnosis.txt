## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

**January–March:** This is the stable reference period. Labels are fully mature, observed breach rate and mean predicted risk are aligned around 0.18–0.19, AUROC is about 0.81–0.82, Brier and ECE are stable, unknown activity share is near zero, and the variant mix is stable. V3 is already the riskiest path, with breach rates around 0.30–0.32, but the model appears to handle the baseline mixture reasonably well.

**April:** April should be treated primarily as a schema and feature-pipeline incident, not as ordinary model drift. The renamed diagnosis activity caused prediction prefixes to be built at the wrong event or not built at all. Unknown activity share jumps to 17%, missing queue feature to 14%, and unmapped/other variants to 18%. The model overpredicts aggregate breach risk: mean predicted risk is 0.28 while observed breach rate is 0.19. Because labels are 100% mature and part-receipt performance is not materially worse than March, label delay and supplier concept drift are weak explanations for April.

**May:** May is a partial recovery month. The mapping patch brought unknown activity share down to 2% and missing queue feature to 3%, and AUROC recovered from 0.67 to 0.78. However, ECE remains above baseline at 0.050, and the audit says the cached “number of events before diagnosis” feature was not fully backfilled for April. May should be used cautiously: it is not a clean baseline month, but it is also not the same failure mode as April.

**June:** June is a real process-mix change, not necessarily concept drift. V2 Remote jumps to 52% after the new remote-routing policy, and V2 is the lowest-breach variant. The aggregate observed breach rate falls to 0.15, while mean predicted risk remains 0.20. That looks like aggregate overprediction caused by a new, lower-risk routing mix or inadequate calibration for the larger V2 segment. The edge from diagnosis to `Remote Fix` remains stable, and V2 breach rate is 0.09, close to its prior level. This is not the same as April because data quality is mostly normal; it is not the same as August because parts performance has not yet collapsed.

**July:** July is the transition month. Labels are 98% mature, so metrics are nearly comparable to prior months. The supplier issue begins on 10 July, and the `Order Part -> Part Received` median rises to 65 h from 42 h in June. V3 breach rate rises to 0.46. Aggregate metrics worsen moderately, but the high V2 share still masks some of the V3 deterioration. July is best viewed as the first sign of parts-specific performance and possible concept drift.

**August:** August is a genuine operational deterioration month. Parts delay becomes severe: the `Order Part -> Part Received` median reaches 130 h. V3 share rises to 35%, and V3 matured breach rate reaches 0.64. The model underpredicts: observed breach rate is 0.28 versus mean predicted risk of 0.19, with AUROC down to 0.68 and ECE up to 0.120. Data quality is normal, so April-style pipeline failure is not a sufficient explanation. Label maturity is 93%, so the final breach rate may still move, but the deterioration is already visible.

**September:** September layers population shift on top of the supplier/parts problem. Partner Z enters with 24% share, more rural customers, older devices, and a much higher V3 routing share. Overall V3 share reaches 42%, and part receipt remains very slow at 148 h. The observed breach rate is 0.31 and mean predicted risk is only 0.21. However, labels are only 82% mature and Partner Z labels mature more slowly, so the exact size of the Partner Z gap is unstable. September should be diagnosed as overlapping process drift, concept drift, covariate drift, population shift, and label-delay risk.

**October:** October must not be treated as a normal performance month. Label maturity is only 55%, median label delay rises to 52 d, and the backlog disproportionately affects longer paths. The observed breach rate drops to 0.16 even though V3 share remains 41%, Partner Z share is 27%, and part receipt median is 155 h. That combination is internally inconsistent unless the October labels are heavily censored. October can still be used for data-quality, process, population, and feature-distribution monitoring, but not for ordinary supervised performance evaluation or retraining.

The four key months therefore have different signatures: April is mainly a pipeline/schema break; June is a routing and process-mix change; August–September are real parts-driven operational deterioration with likely concept drift and new population composition; October is dominated by label maturity and censoring.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

Immediate containment should separate model-performance alarms from data-quality and label-maturity alarms. April and October should be excluded from headline model-health conclusions until repaired or matured. Operationally, high-risk V3 cases in August–October should receive a conservative overlay because the parts edge and V3 breach rates show real deterioration. That overlay should be explicitly marked as a temporary business rule or segment recalibration, not as proof that the whole model is obsolete.

Short-term recalibration is justified for stable, repaired, sufficiently mature cohorts. A reasonable first step is variant-specific calibration for V1, V2, and V3, with special caution for V3 after the supplier disruption. June’s overprediction can likely be improved with recalibration or mix-aware thresholds, because the main change is the larger low-risk V2 share. August–September should not be pooled naïvely with Jan–Jun; they represent a different parts-supplier regime. October should not be used for recalibration until labels mature or a censoring-aware method is used.

Feature repair must happen before any serious retraining. The raw event log should be normalized, duplicate-cleaned, and used to recompute all prefix features, including the cached “number of events before diagnosis.” The repaired feature store should carry mapping version, source system, feature-computation timestamp, and duplicate-removal status. New candidate features should include supplier reliability, part category, expected part lead time, rural travel distance, device age, partner/source, travel region, and route-policy indicators.

Full retraining is justified only after the feature repair and maturity checks. The original model was trained before the remote-routing policy, the supplier disruption, and Partner Z onboarding; therefore a retrain will probably be needed. But retraining on unrepaired April data or immature October labels would encode noise. The model should be retrained with temporal validation that tests pre-migration, post-routing, supplier-disruption, and Partner-Z periods separately.

Training-month safety:

| Month   | Training status                                     | Reason                                                                                                      |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Jan–Mar | Safe baseline                                       | Mature labels, stable process, low data-quality issues.                                                     |
| Apr     | Unsafe until repaired                               | Diagnosis rename, unknown activities, wrong prefixes, duplicate inconsistency, incomplete derived features. |
| May     | Conditionally usable                                | Mature labels and mostly repaired mapping, but lineage checks are needed.                                   |
| Jun     | Usable after stratification                         | Mature labels; real routing change should be represented, not discarded.                                    |
| Jul     | Mostly usable after final maturity check            | 98% mature; supplier issue begins, useful as transition period.                                             |
| Aug     | Use only after maturity and supplier-feature design | Real V3 concept/process drift; 93% maturity, but segment labels should be completed.                        |
| Sep     | Unsafe for ordinary training until more mature      | Partner Z shift and 82% maturity; use for monitoring and transport checks first.                            |
| Oct     | Unsafe for supervised training or calibration       | 55% maturity and outcome API backlog skewed toward long paths.                                              |

A segment-specific or variant-specific model is justified at least for calibration, and possibly for the core model. V2 behaves differently from V3: remote-fix elapsed time is stable and breach rates are low, while V3 is exposed to supplier lead-time shocks. A hierarchical approach is preferable to completely separate models if sample sizes or governance are concerns: one shared model with variant/source interactions, plus segment-specific calibrators and thresholds. Partner Z should not automatically receive a separate model until stratified evidence shows residual error after controlling for V3, rurality, device age, and label vintage.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

The monitoring architecture should be layered so that alerts are interpreted in the right order.

First, a **data-quality and lineage layer** should monitor schema versions, activity-label mappings, unknown activity share, duplicate rate, prefix validity, missing feature rates, source-system changes, and feature-cache freshness. This layer must gate model-performance interpretation: if diagnosis-prefix construction is broken, AUROC and ECE are not actionable model-drift signals.

Second, a **label-maturity layer** should track label completeness by case-start month, variant, source, risk decile, and path length. Metrics should be tagged as mature, provisional, or censored. A label-delay alarm should trigger outcome-pipeline remediation and fixed-vintage reporting, not automatic retraining.

Third, a **predictive and calibration layer** should monitor AUROC, PR-AUC if class imbalance matters, Brier, ECE, reliability curves, risk-decile lift, and threshold action rates. These should be reported overall and by V1/V2/V3, Partner Z/non-Z, region, device age band, and source system.

Fourth, a **process and performance layer** should monitor variant shares, discovered DFGs, performance DFGs, and key edge times. The critical edges here are `Order Part -> Part Received`, diagnosis to `Remote Fix`, diagnosis to dispatch, and repair to close. This layer distinguishes routing changes from model deterioration.

Fifth, a **population layer** should monitor source/partner mix, rurality, travel distance, device age, part categories, and other features added after Partner Z. It should include transport/reweighting checks so that aggregate degradation is not mistaken for unfair source-specific error.

Sixth, an **intervention and deployment layer** should monitor model version, feature-pipeline version, mapping patch dates, routing policy changes, supplier incidents, Partner onboarding dates, and any manual overrides or operational interventions. Deployment events should be joined to metric changes before retraining decisions are made.

Alert dependencies should be explicit:

`data-quality failure -> repair/recompute first -> suppress retraining decision`

`label-maturity failure -> mark metrics provisional -> run fixed-vintage/censoring analysis -> suppress ordinary recalibration`

`process/performance drift with mature labels -> run stratified calibration and concept-drift tests`

`population shift with stable within-segment calibration -> consider reweighting, thresholds, or segment calibration`

`within-segment outcome relationship change with mature labels -> retraining candidate`

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CASE = "case:concept:name"
ACT = "concept:name"
TS = "time:timestamp"
CANON_DIAG = "Initial Diagnosis Completed"

DIAG_RENAME = {
    "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": CANON_DIAG,
    CANON_DIAG: CANON_DIAG,
}

EDGE_PART = ("Order Part", "Part Received")
EDGE_REMOTE = (CANON_DIAG, "Remote Fix")


def read_normalize_clean(path="field_service_events.csv"):
    df = pd.read_csv(path, parse_dates=[TS])
    required = {CASE, ACT, TS}
    missing = required - set(df.columns)
    if missing:
        raise ValueError(f"Missing required columns: {sorted(missing)}")

    # Keep raw activity for audit, but normalize before prefixes, variants, and features.
    df["raw_activity"] = df[ACT]
    df[ACT] = df[ACT].replace(DIAG_RENAME)

    # Uniform duplicate strategy across sources and dates, not only after 20 April.
    # Prefer event_id if present; otherwise use a conservative exact-event signature.
    dup_keys = ["event_id"] if "event_id" in df.columns else [CASE, ACT, TS]
    for c in ["org:resource", "source_system", "lifecycle:transition"]:
        if c in df.columns and c not in dup_keys:
            dup_keys.append(c)

    df["duplicate_after_normalization"] = df.duplicated(dup_keys, keep="first")
    dup_audit = (
        df.assign(month=df[TS].dt.to_period("M").astype(str))
          .groupby("month")["duplicate_after_normalization"]
          .agg(events_flagged="sum", event_rows="size")
          .reset_index()
    )

    df = df.loc[~df["duplicate_after_normalization"]].copy()
    df = df.sort_values([CASE, TS, ACT])

    # Case-start cohort: aligns with prediction and eventual labels.
    starts = (
        df.loc[df[ACT].eq("Case Created")]
          .groupby(CASE)[TS]
          .min()
          .rename("case_start_time")
    )
    fallback_starts = df.groupby(CASE)[TS].min().rename("fallback_case_start_time")
    df = df.merge(starts, on=CASE, how="left").merge(fallback_starts, on=CASE, how="left")
    df["case_start_time"] = df["case_start_time"].fillna(df["fallback_case_start_time"])
    df["case_start_month"] = df["case_start_time"].dt.to_period("M").astype(str)

    # PM4Py formatting; no model fitting here.
    log_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id=CASE,
        activity_key=ACT,
        timestamp_key=TS,
    )
    return log_df, dup_audit


def consecutive_edge_median_hours(df, edge):
    if df.empty:
        return pd.NA
    ordered = df.sort_values([CASE, TS]).copy()
    ordered["next_activity"] = ordered.groupby(CASE)[ACT].shift(-1)
    ordered["next_time"] = ordered.groupby(CASE)[TS].shift(-1)
    mask = (ordered[ACT].eq(edge[0])) & (ordered["next_activity"].eq(edge[1]))
    if not mask.any():
        return pd.NA
    hours = (ordered.loc[mask, "next_time"] - ordered.loc[mask, TS]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    return float(hours.median())


def classify_path(path):
    acts = set(path)
    if "Remote Fix" in acts:
        return "V2 Remote"
    if "Order Part" in acts or "Part Received" in acts:
        return "V3 Parts-dependent"
    if "Dispatch Technician" in acts and "Repair Completed" in acts:
        return "V1 Standard"
    return "Unmapped/other"


def summarize_log_slice(df, month, cohort_type):
    if df.empty:
        return {"month": str(month), "cohort_type": cohort_type, "n_cases": 0, "n_events": 0}

    # Required PM4Py process evidence.
    dfg, start_activities, end_activities = pm4py.discover_dfg(df)
    perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(df)
    pm4py_variants = pm4py.get_variants(df)

    # Temporal features for auditing seasonality/calendar effects; not a drift detector.
    temporal_df = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(df.copy())
    temporal_cols_added = len(set(temporal_df.columns) - set(df.columns))

    paths = df.sort_values([CASE, TS]).groupby(CASE)[ACT].agg(tuple)
    variant_counts = paths.map(classify_path).value_counts(normalize=True)

    queue_cols = [c for c in df.columns if "queue" in c.lower()]
    missing_queue_share = (
        float(df[queue_cols].isna().any(axis=1).mean()) if queue_cols else pd.NA
    )

    return {
        "month": str(month),
        "cohort_type": cohort_type,
        "n_cases": int(df[CASE].nunique()),
        "n_events": int(len(df)),
        "unknown_activity_share": float(df[ACT].eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean()),
        "missing_queue_feature_share": missing_queue_share,
        "v1_share": float(variant_counts.get("V1 Standard", 0.0)),
        "v2_share": float(variant_counts.get("V2 Remote", 0.0)),
        "v3_share": float(variant_counts.get("V3 Parts-dependent", 0.0)),
        "unmapped_other_share": float(variant_counts.get("Unmapped/other", 0.0)),
        "median_h_order_part_to_part_received": consecutive_edge_median_hours(df, EDGE_PART),
        "median_h_diagnosis_to_remote_fix": consecutive_edge_median_hours(df, EDGE_REMOTE),
        "n_dfg_edges": len(dfg),
        "perf_dfg_part_edge_value": perf_dfg.get(EDGE_PART, pd.NA),
        "n_pm4py_variants": len(pm4py_variants),
        "temporal_feature_columns_added": temporal_cols_added,
    }


def monthly_process_audit(path="field_service_events.csv"):
    log_df, dup_audit = read_normalize_clean(path)
    months = pd.period_range(
        log_df[TS].min().to_period("M"),
        log_df[TS].max().to_period("M"),
        freq="M",
    )

    rows = []
    for month in months:
        start = month.to_timestamp()
        end = (month + 1).to_timestamp()

        # Event-calendar cohort: events occurring in the month.
        # Useful for operational workload and edge performance, but can fragment cases.
        event_calendar = pm4py.filter_time_range(log_df, start, end, mode="events")
        rows.append(summarize_log_slice(event_calendar, month, "event_calendar"))

        # Case-start cohort: all events for cases created in the month.
        # Useful for prediction cohorts and label maturity, but requires waiting for outcomes.
        case_ids = log_df.loc[
            (log_df["case_start_time"] >= start) & (log_df["case_start_time"] < end),
            CASE,
        ].unique()
        case_start = log_df.loc[log_df[CASE].isin(case_ids)].copy()
        rows.append(summarize_log_slice(case_start, month, "case_start"))

    summaries = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    return summaries, dup_audit


if __name__ == "__main__":
    monthly_summaries, duplicate_audit = monthly_process_audit("field_service_events.csv")
    print(monthly_summaries.to_string(index=False))
    print("\nDuplicate audit:")
    print(duplicate_audit.to_string(index=False))
```
