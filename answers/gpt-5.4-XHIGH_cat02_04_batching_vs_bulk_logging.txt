# 3. Consequences for performance analysis

G6-like data can simultaneously inflate waiting and collapse service toward zero because real work that happened before the bulk close is counted as pre-start waiting, while the logged service interval is compressed to the bulk-submit instant.

| cluster | waiting time bias | service time bias | utilization and throughput bias | bottleneck and conformance bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | Early cases may look as if they waited a few minutes, but that may simply be fixture-fill time before one shared cycle starts | Usually reasonable if the 3-minute interval is the real machine cycle; misleading if copied from a batch header to each case | Resource utilization is overstated if the four case intervals are summed as if one worker did four separate jobs; throughput is fine only if modeled as a 4-up cell | Conformance may falsely infer forbidden parallel case handling unless the model allows shared-capacity batch execution |
| G2 | Raw waiting up to `10:00` may be real queueing before the group begins, but any within-group serial delay is hidden | Later cases are biased upward because time after the common `10:00` start includes waiting behind earlier cases, not just their own inspection effort | One resource appears to process four overlapping inspections, inflating utilization; throughput counts are fine, but the service decomposition is wrong | Bottleneck detection shifts from queueing/setup to inspection time; conformance may falsely show simultaneous case starts |
| G3 | If starts are real, waiting is less distorted than service; if starts were also entered late, waiting is uncertain | Service is biased upward, especially for early-finished cases whose completion is logged at the shared `11:30` submit time | Utilization is overstated by overlapping intervals on one resource; throughput falsely spikes at `11:30` | Bottleneck detection may overstate the review step and create artificial end-synchronization patterns in conformance checking |
| G4 | Waiting likely reflects a real FIFO queue and is the cleanest in the dataset | Service looks credible: constant 4-minute intervals with immediate recording | Utilization and throughput look plausible for a busy single server | The main risk is semantic, not temporal: PM4Py may call it a batch although it behaves like ordinary serial flow, which can distort conformance rules about batching |
| G5 | Waiting may be interpretable only at pool level; at individual level it is not attributable to a person | Service per named resource is uninterpretable because one login can represent up to three analysts | If `analyst_pool` is treated as one worker, utilization can exceed physical plausibility; throughput is acceptable only as pool throughput | Bottleneck detection can falsely blame a single analyst; conformance on performer constraints is unreliable because the performer identity is collapsed |
| G6 | Waiting is grossly overstated because the logged `17:00` start is almost certainly the closure-entry time, not the work-start time | Service is near zero by construction although the tickets clearly consumed effort earlier | Utilization is understated if computed from logged durations, while throughput shows an artificial burst at `17:00` | Bottleneck detection can wrongly identify extreme upstream waiting and miss the actual closure effort; conformance may show impossible instant completion behavior |
| G7 | Raw waiting is unusable because later cases have negative waits; exact queue analysis is invalid without interval censoring | Uniform 15-minute service is likely a rounding artifact, not exact work duration | Utilization is distorted by artificial simultaneity; throughput bunches at quarter-hour boundaries | Bottlenecks and conformance can be driven by clock granularity rather than process behavior; this cluster should be treated as interval data |
| G8 | Waiting is upward-biased if the delay is really downtime or a safety hold rather than ordinary queueing; it may still be valid for end-to-end customer delay | Service looks credible: 7-minute sequential tasks with second-level capture | Utilization during the recovery window is high and probably real; throughput appears as a restart burst after blockage removal | Bottleneck detection may incorrectly blame triage capacity when the real cause is exogenous downtime; conformance can mistake recovery work for deliberate batching |

# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

If resource IDs are trusted as person-level identities, `G5` looks like impossible or at least very heavy single-worker overlap and would invite a multitasking explanation. If the IDs are treated as pooled/shared accounts, `G5` becomes compatible with ordinary parallel work by multiple analysts, but individual utilization and per-person queueing become non-identifiable. `G1`, `G4`, and `G8` are less sensitive to this assumption because their primary signal comes from machine/cell capacity or strict serial adjacency rather than from ambiguous human overlap.

If timestamps are treated as exact, `G1`, `G4`, and `G8` support strong temporal claims. If timestamps are interval-censored by stated precision, `G7` changes the most: its apparent simultaneity collapses into a wide uncertainty box and the negative waits become evidence of rounding rather than bizarre execution. `G2` and `G3` weaken slightly under 1-minute censoring, but their operational context still makes start-group and end-submit interpretations stronger than literal parallelism. `G6` remains a bulk-logging case even after censoring because 1-minute precision alone does not explain seven zero-duration closures recorded together.

The same temporal pattern can support multiple physical explanations in at least these cases:

1. `G1`: identical start and end can mean a true four-up fixture batch, a machine-cycle interval copied from a batch header to each item, or a per-item log generated only when the batch cycle closes.
2. `G2`: identical starts can mean a real common setup, a UI-generated `begin group` artifact, or minute-rounded starts that were only near-synchronous.
3. `G3`: identical ends can mean a true synchronized finish, a folder-level bulk submit, or minute-rounded completions after staggered actual finish times.
4. `G8`: a post-gap sequential chain can mean downtime recovery, a safety hold, a meeting/lunch interruption, or a hidden upstream release gate; the unsynchronized clocks prevent confident causal attribution.

# 6. Data-collection redesign

1. Capture separate case-level execution timestamps for `work_start` and `work_complete`, and keep `recorded_at` as a distinct ingestion timestamp rather than letting it stand in for execution.
2. Synchronize source clocks across upstream queue, workstation, scanner, safety, and database systems; store clock-offset health so cross-system lag can be audited later.
3. Replace shared logins with badge-level or session-level actor identity, while retaining a pool/team identifier for pooled-capacity analysis.
4. Add explicit group or batch identifiers plus `begin_group`, `end_group`, and `submit_group` events so UI-driven worklists and true operational batches are distinguishable.
5. Record workstation capacity metadata such as fixture slots, parallel lanes, and pool size, because concurrency plausibility depends on physical capacity.
6. Log pause, resume, blocked, downtime, and safety-hold events on the same clock as execution events so backlog after capacity loss can be separated from ordinary queueing.
7. Version and expose timestamp precision metadata per field and source, including the exact rounding rule when values are rounded or censored.
8. Preserve source insertion order and edit history, and keep both event-occurrence time and database-write time, because sequence and lag are crucial for distinguishing live capture from delayed submission.

# 7. Final adjudication

High-confidence interpretations are `G1` as a genuine capacity-backed simultaneous batch, `G3` as end-of-folder/bulk submission, `G6` as bulk closure logging, and `G7` as timestamp coarsening rather than literal simultaneity. `G2` is also fairly strong as a start-group artifact because the UI semantics directly support it. `G4` is the cleanest ordinary execution pattern and is best treated as serial FIFO work, whether or not PM4Py labels it `Sequential batching`.

For queue analysis, `G6` should be excluded from raw waiting and service calculations; `G7` should be interval-censored and not used for exact waiting-time estimation; `G3` should not use the raw completion as a precise service end; `G2` should not use the shared `10:00` start as four exact service starts; `G5` should not be used for person-level utilization unless badge identity is recovered. `G1` can remain in analysis, but only with a capacity-aware resource model so utilization is not quadruple-counted.

Operational validation is still required for `G5` and `G8` before drawing strong performance conclusions. `G5` needs identity disambiguation to separate pooled concurrency from logging collapse. `G8` needs clock alignment with the safety system to confirm whether the long waits are true queueing, downtime exposure, or both. Without those checks, bottleneck claims for those clusters remain conditional rather than adjudicated.
