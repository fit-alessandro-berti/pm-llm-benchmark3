# 2. Integrated explanation

**The curve is a composite.** The aggregate pattern (service time falls from 18.8 to 10.8 min while errors form a U-shape) comes from several processes acting at once, and the positional bins mix them.

1. **The early shift is dominated by carryover complexity.** Bins 1–5 have complexity 0.72, 41% high-priority, and inherited backlog. Complex cases take roughly 75% longer at low WIP (22.4 vs 12.7 min) and carry about twice the error rate. Much of the apparent learning curve between bins 1 and 30 is therefore the complexity mix easing, not people getting faster. Real warm-up probably exists, since first-20-event times are longer for all groups. Its size is unknown until complexity is held constant. Early errors (3.4%) are lower than the WIP × complexity table would predict for that mix (5.6% for medium WIP and high complexity). The likely reasons are that senior staff were assigned the hardest work, that workers are fresh and careful, and that queue pressure has not yet built up.

2. **Late-shift speed is mostly a response to queue pressure, not learning.** Within complexity bands, going from low to high WIP cuts service time by about a quarter (12.7 to 9.4 min for low complexity, 22.4 to 16.8 for high). Errors rise multiplicatively, about 2.6–2.8×. The pattern is consistent across complexity bands, which suggests a speed-accuracy trade-off: workers shorten reviews, skip verification, batch, and accept bot output or correct it hastily. In the late bins, complexity keeps falling (0.46 to 0.43) while WIP jumps (62 to 88), so the two effects push in opposite directions for errors and in the same direction for speed. Speed therefore keeps improving while quality collapses.

3. **Errors rise only in some conditions because the trade-off needs pressure to bite.** Under low WIP, later-shift error rates match first-20 rates for every group (Seniors 2.1% vs 2.2%, Mid 2.8% vs 2.9%, Night 3.6% vs 3.7%). Time on task alone is therefore not producing errors. Under high WIP, errors double or more (Seniors 4.9%, Mid 6.8%, Juniors 12.4%), and the increase is steeper with less experience. Experience buffers against pressure: seniors speed up with little quality loss until WIP is very high. Juniors suffer twice, from complexity early and from pressure late.

4. **Fatigue is plausible but not identified.** The 76+ bin has a 7.9% error rate, higher than the 5.8% high-WIP low-complexity stratum. The gap of about 2 points could reflect fatigue amplifying pressure (an interaction, not an independent effect), junior-heavy composition, QC-delay bursts, batching, or higher correction of bot output. Fatigue would be expected to show as slower or more variable work, but speed rises. It could be acting through compensatory speed-ups, but that cannot be separated from pressure without the tests in section 3. Because time-on-task and queue both increase with position, the data cannot separate them without controlled variation.

5. **Circadian effects matter mainly for Night.** Night has the slowest late service (15.2 min) and moderate errors (5.1%), with few events and urgent exceptions. A 56% complex share in the first hour makes it hard to separate pre-dawn performance dips from case-mix. For Day and Late, the dominant explanation is demand, not clock time: Day queue grows from 38 to 75, Late inherits 70.

6. **Selection effects distort the bins.** Senior staff take complex cases early. Late in the shift, easier cases may be cherry-picked, while high-priority cases are processed first (priority share falls 41% to 19%). Only fast workers reach positions 76+, so that bin over-represents fast workers. Workers' speed is thus partly the cause of reaching a late position, not an effect of it.

7. **Measurement and workflow artifacts add further distortion.** Document Review batching by two juniors produces very short recorded service times in late Day. Quality Check being deferred to the end of the shift means errors are detected late, in bursts, and may be assigned to the wrong time window. Rework within 7 days is right-censored at the end of the log. Bot_Risk's instant classification shifts work to human correction, especially when queue > 70.

8. **Feedback loops.** Pressure causes rushing, which causes errors, which cause rework, which adds to WIP, which increases pressure. Rework correction is itself an activity that adds load, so the system can drift into a high-WIP regime that persists across shifts (Late inherits 70 from Day).

**Summary:** Service time falls because complexity falls, warm-up occurs, and, most importantly, pressure induces speed-ups. Errors stay flat while WIP is low or moderate and rise once pressure exceeds the capacity of experience and checks to absorb it. Fatigue and circadian effects are secondary hypotheses that interact with pressure and need explicit identification.

# Kitagawa-style decomposition between early and late positions
early = human[human.sequence_position_within_shift <= 5]
late  = human[human.sequence_position_within_shift >= 76]
human["cx_band"] = pd.qcut(human["case_complexity_score"], 5, labels=False)
def strata(d): return d.groupby(["cx_band","high_wip"]).agg(n=("service_min","size"), t=("service_min","mean"))
se, sl = strata(early), strata(late)
w_e = se["n"]/se["n"].sum(); w_l = sl["n"]/sl["n"].sum()
common = se.index.intersection(sl.index)
composition = ((w_l[common]-w_e[common])*(se["t"][common]+sl["t"][common])/2).sum()
within = (((sl["t"][common]-se["t"][common]))*(w_e[common]+w_l[common])/2).sum()

| 5 | **Mandatory micro-breaks in the final two hours** | Low cost; may reduce any genuine fatigue effect and create recovery. | If WIP is high, breaks reduce capacity and increase queue; if fatigue is not the driver, there is no benefit. Can be seen as a burden. | Stepped-wedge or randomized by team: compare error after breaks vs matched periods, service time variability, WIP during the break, gaps in activity. Judge by error per unit WIP, not speed. |
| 6 | **Defer low-priority quality checks to the next shift** | Smooths the end-of-shift QC burst and frees capacity at peaks. | Delayed detection lets errors propagate and adds next-shift load. The QC backlog may become a permanent deferral. Low priority may still have compliance exposure. Not acceptable if it amounts to reducing QC. | Only with bounds: maximum deferral, risk-based sampling, never skip. Track QC delay, QC coverage, error detection lag, downstream rework within 7 days, QC backlog size. Better: spread QC throughout the shift. |
| 7 | **Add an extra senior reviewer during day-shift demand peaks** | Targets the shift with the largest queue growth (38 to 75) and highest late error (7.5%). Seniors maintain quality under pressure better. Reduces WIP, so reduces the driver. | Cost; senior time drawn from complex cases; if demand exceeds capacity structurally it may only delay the problem. May create dependence. | WIP and queue at late-Day; error/rework in last two hours; senior utilization; time above WIP 70; junior support interactions. Compare peak days with and without a reviewer, or use staggered rollout. |
| 8 | **Penalize workers with higher late-shift error rates** | None supported by the evidence. | Raw late-shift error rates reflect WIP, complexity, assignment, batching, bot interaction and detection lag. Penalties would incentivize case-avoidance, hiding errors, and cherry-picking, worsening selection bias and trust. Unfair to juniors and night staff, who face more pressure. | Do not implement. Use risk-adjusted, system-level indicators and support (coaching, staffing). Monitor error-reporting behavior, case refusals, and rework declared vs detected if any person-level feedback is ever used, only as adjusted, supportive analysis. |

**Priority order suggested by the evidence:** (3) alert with action, (7) peak capacity, QC smoothing instead of deferral (variant of 6), and a controlled trial of (5); use (4) only as a limited experiment; do not do (8).

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

- **Selection bias.** Workers do not get comparable cases. Seniors take complex cases early, juniors may have cherry-picked easy ones, priority cases go first, and only fast workers reach late positions. Raw per-worker error rates and speeds therefore confound skill with assignment. Adjust for complexity, priority, activity, WIP, and shift before interpreting anything, and report uncertainty. Small counts per worker make rankings unstable.
- **Measurement error.** Service time depends on `start_timestamp` validity. Batches with compressed or shared timestamps produce artificially short times. Errors are detected through QC, and QC is delayed, so error timing and attribution are noisy. The `rework_flag` is censored at the log end and may include rework caused by the customer or by upstream steps. Complexity scores may be assigned after the work or be subjective. `wip_at_start` vs `queue_length_at_start` may be defined differently. Validate each measure before building metrics on it.
- **Queue pressure created by management.** Staffing, scheduling, assignment policy, SLA targets, and QC timing shape WIP. Holding workers accountable for errors produced by pressure that they do not control is unjust. Fix the system-level drivers first (capacity at peaks, handover backlog, QC scheduling).
- **Automation quality.** Bot_Risk errors are attributed to humans when they fail to catch or when they correct. A higher human correction rate under high queue can signal bot weakness, not human error. Bot quality should be monitored as an independent system component, with human override data treated as feedback on the bot. Avoid automation bias, where workers who rush approve bot output.
- **Punitive use of error rates.** Do not base discipline or pay on raw late-shift rates. It invites gaming (hiding errors, avoiding complex or late cases, delaying logging), reduces reporting and learning, and harms night staff and juniors disproportionately. If person-level analysis is used, apply it for support (training, workload), with adjusted estimates, confidence intervals, worker input and human review.
- **Privacy and consent.** Fine-grained timing data are sensitive. Limit access, aggregate to team or shift level for routine reporting, and be transparent about use. Ensure works council or labor-law compliance where applicable.
- **Fairness of load.** Night and junior staff face different case mixes and pressure. Monitor the distribution of complex, urgent and high-WIP work across workers.
- **Causal humility.** The data are observational. Mechanisms such as fatigue and circadian rhythm are not identified by the current tables. Use natural experiments and staged pilots before making large policy changes.

# 6. Monitoring dashboard specification

Segmentation for every indicator: shift type, clock hour, position-in-shift or hour-in-shift, activity family, priority, complexity band, experience group (not individual for routine views), and WIP band.

| # | Indicator | Definition / event-log computation | Purpose and alert logic |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Service time** | Median and P90 of `time:timestamp  start_timestamp`, by activity × complexity band × WIP band (`pm4py.get_service_time`). Show complexity-adjusted ratio (observed/expected from baseline model). | Separates speed-up from case-mix. Alert on very fast adjusted times combined with rising errors. |
| 2 | **Waiting time** | Gap between the previous event's end (or case availability) and the next start, by activity and priority (from `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time`, `discover_performance_dfg`). | Shows queueing delay, SLA risk for high-priority cases, and backlog aging. |
| 3 | **WIP and queue** | `wip_at_start`, `queue_length_at_start`, time spent above 70, and arrival vs finish rate (`pm4py.insert_case_arrival_finish_rate`). | Primary pressure indicator; triggers the queue alert; tracks the WIP > 70 threshold and hysteresis. |
| 4 | **Error rate (risk-adjusted)** | `error_flag` rate by stratum, plus observed/expected using a model with complexity, WIP, activity and experience. Show funnel plots with confidence intervals. | Avoids raw comparisons. Alert when the adjusted rate exceeds limits, not the raw rate. |
| 5 | **Rework and detection lag** | Share of events with rework within 7 days (with censoring handling), rework volume as a share of load (`pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity`), time from work to QC and to error detection. | Captures delayed quality costs and feedback loops. |
| 6 | **Complexity and priority mix** | Mean and distribution of `case_complexity_score`, high-complexity share, high-priority share, by hour-in-shift and shift. | Reveals inherited backlog, cherry-picking, and composition drift. |
| 7 | **Batching indicators** | Share of events in runs of  9 same-activity consecutive events with a short gap; median service time within runs; inter-event gap distribution; dotted chart (`pm4py.view_dotted_chart`); `pm4py.discover_batches` output; QC burst in the last 45 minutes. | Flags workarounds and timestamp artifacts; compares quality of batched vs unbatched work. |
| 8 | **Handover and queue ownership changes** | Number of resource changes per case, shift-boundary handovers, cases carried across shifts, age of carryover, reassignments (resource change in the case path), time lost on handover edges in the performance DFG. | Identifies backlog transfer and ownership gaps. |
| 9 | **Worker-load fairness** | Distribution (Gini or max/median ratio) across workers of complex-case share, high-WIP exposure, urgent case share, events per hour, and overtime, adjusted for role. Shown at team level, with individual detail only for supervisory review. | Checks that pressure and complexity are distributed justly and supports workload balancing; prevents misuse of errors. |
| 10 | **Bot–human interaction** | Bot_Risk correction rate by queue band and complexity; downstream error after corrected vs accepted classification; override latency. | Separates bot quality from human performance; checks automation bias. |
| 11 | **QC coverage and timeliness** | Share of eligible events with QC, QC delay, QC backlog, errors caught per QC hour, share of QC conducted in the last 45 minutes. | Guards against reducing quality checks; tracks deferral policy. |
| 12 | **Time-on-task and recovery** | Error and service time by minutes since shift start and by time since last gap/break, at matched WIP. | Monitors possible fatigue and break effects separately from pressure. |

**Operational rules**

- Show control charts (by hour-in-shift and WIP band) and compare to baseline models, not raw averages.
- Show counts and confidence intervals, and suppress individual-level views with small counts.
- Pair every speed indicator with a quality indicator (service time with error/rework), so speed-ups are never read alone.
- Use intervention markers (alert launch, extra senior, break pilot) and stepped comparison windows to evaluate changes.
- Review model assumptions and metric definitions quarterly, including timestamp validation and complexity score calibration.
