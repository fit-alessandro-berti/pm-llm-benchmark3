### Diagnostic Discriminators
1. **The Causality Test ($\text{start} \ge \text{queue\_arrival}$):** In physical execution, an activity cannot start before the case arrives at the queue. A violation of this rule (as seen in G7) is a definitive indicator of timestamp coarsening, clock desynchronization, or retrospective back-dating.
2. **The Recording Lag Signature ($\Delta_{\text{rec}} = \text{recorded\_at} - \text{complete}$):** Machine-driven physical batches (G1) exhibit low, deterministic recording lags (e.g., 2–3 seconds). Manual bulk entry (G6, G7) exhibits sequential transaction delays (e.g., 14:16, 14:17, 14:18) as the user enters or commits records sequentially into the database.
3. **The Duration Spectrum:** True multitasking or common setup yields distinct durations that grow monotonically with completion order (G2: 8, 14, 21, 27 min). Administrative bulk entry (G6) produces degenerate zero-duration intervals ($\text{start} = \text{complete}$).

# 3. Consequences for performance analysis

Interpreting event logs without correcting for logging artifacts introduces severe structural biases into queue waiting times, service times, resource utilization, and bottleneck identification.

### Cluster-by-Cluster Bias Analysis

#### G1: Apply label (RBT-7)
- **Waiting Time:** Upstream arrivals span 08:54 to 08:58, while all cases start at 09:00. Naive analysis calculates waiting times from 2 to 6 minutes (mean = 4.25 min). This combines true queue delay with "batch formation wait" (cases waiting for the fixture to fill).
- **Service Time:** Logged service time is 3 minutes for all 4 cases. If evaluated naively without batch semantics, analysts report 3 minutes of work per case.
- **Resource Utilization:** Naively summing individual durations yields $4 \times 3 = 12$ minutes of work performed in a 3-minute window. For a single-capacity server ($c=1$), this produces an impossible utilization rate of $400\%$. In reality, RBT-7 is a multi-capacity fixture ($c=4$) operating at $100\%$ utilization during those 3 minutes.
- **Bottleneck Detection:** If evaluated with $c=1$, RBT-7 is falsely identified as an overloaded bottleneck. Correctly modeled as $c=4$, it is recognized as a high-throughput batch resource with batch-arrival synchronization requirements.

#### G2: Surface inspection (T-B)
- **Waiting Time:** Logged waiting times ($10:00 - \text{arrival}$) range from 6 to 18 minutes (mean = 11.75 min). However, because S02, S03, and S04 were inspected sequentially, their actual physical starts were 10:08, 10:14, and 10:21. True waiting times were 22 min for S02 ($10:08 - 09:46$), 25 min for S03 ($10:14 - 09:49$), and 27 min for S04 ($10:21 - 09:54$). Naive analysis severely underestimates queue waiting time by up to 21 minutes.
- **Service Time:** Logged service times expand artificially: S01 = 8m, S02 = 14m, S03 = 21m, S04 = 27m (mean = 17.5 min). In physical reality, each item required 6 to 8 minutes of inspection (mean = 6.75 min). Service duration is inflated by up to $300\%$.
- **Resource Utilization:** Summing logged durations yields 70 minutes of work within a 27-minute span (apparent utilization = $259\%$). True physical utilization was $100\%$ (27 minutes of continuous single-piece work).
- **Bottleneck Detection:** The station appears to exhibit extreme service time variability (standard deviation $\approx 8.2$ min), leading process engineers to investigate non-existent quality defects or worker fatigue on item S04, when the variance is entirely an artifact of the UI "begin group" button.

#### G3: Release review (T-C)
- **Waiting Time:** Start times (11:02, 11:10, 11:17, 11:24) accurately reflect when each document review commenced. Waiting times ($11:02 - 10:40 = 22$m, etc.) are accurately captured.
- **Service Time:** Because all items complete at 11:30 upon folder submission, logged durations are E01 = 28m, E02 = 20m, E03 = 13m, E04 = 6m (mean = 16.75 min). True individual review times were ~7–8 minutes each. Early items are artificially inflated by up to $300\%$.
- **Resource Utilization:** Naive duration aggregation yields 67 minutes of work inside a 28-minute window ($239\%$ utilization). True utilization was $100\%$ across the 28 minutes.
- **Bottleneck Detection:** Downstream processes experience an artificial "batch wave." Downstream activities appear starved between 11:02 and 11:30, followed by a sudden burst arrival of 4 cases at 11:31, causing artificial downstream queue spikes.

#### G4: Pack item (T-D)
- **Waiting Time:** Accurate. Items wait in the upstream buffer (P01 waits 7m, P05 waits 19m) and are processed in strict FIFO order.
- **Service Time:** Accurate. Each item takes exactly 4 minutes.
- **Resource Utilization:** Accurate. Station is $100\%$ utilized from 12:05 to 12:25 ($5 \times 4 = 20$ minutes of work in a 20-minute window).
- **Bottleneck Detection:** Accurately characterized as a stable single-server FIFO queue ($M/D/1$). Queue wait is correctly attributed to arrival bunching between 11:58 and 12:02.

#### G5: Root-cause analysis (analyst_pool)
- **Waiting Time:** Appears as 15m, 15m, 16m, 15m (mean = 15.25 min).
- **Service Time:** Durations are 25m, 15m, 20m, 10m (total work = 70 person-minutes).
- **Resource Utilization:** If `analyst_pool` is treated as a single person ($c=1$), apparent utilization is $70 / 30 = 233\%$. Under true operational pooling ($c=3$), utilization is $70 / (3 \times 30) = 77.8\%$.
- **Bottleneck Detection:** Assuming $c=1$ falsely flags root-cause analysis as a catastrophic system bottleneck operating far beyond stability limits ($\rho > 1$). Recognizing $c=3$ reveals a stable, moderately loaded pool, though peak concurrency (4 active cases from 13:12 to 13:20) indicates either transient multitasking or unlogged additional labor.

#### G6: Close ticket (T-F) — The G6 Bulk Logging Paradox
- **The Paradox of Inflated Waiting and Near-Zero Service:**
  In G6, seven tickets arriving between 14:05 and 16:25 are all stamped with $\text{start} = 17:00$ and $\text{complete} = 17:00$.
  1. *Inflated Waiting Time:* Naive queue wait ($17:00 - \text{arrival}$) produces massive delays: C01 waited 175 minutes, C02 waited 148 minutes, C03 waited 122 minutes, with a mean wait of $103.4$ minutes.
  2. *Near-Zero Service Time:* Logged duration is $17:00 - 17:00 = 0.0$ minutes (or at most 60 seconds if measured to `recorded_at` at 17:01).
- **Consequences:**
  - *Queueing Theory Breakdown:* In classical queueing models ($M/M/1$), queue length and waiting time scale with server utilization:
    $$W_q = \frac{\lambda}{\mu(\mu - \lambda)}$$
    Here, logged service rate $\mu \to \infty$ (service time $\to 0$), which mathematically implies $W_q \to 0$ and utilization $\rho = \lambda / \mu \to 0$. Yet the empirical log reports massive waiting times ($W_q > 100$ min) alongside zero utilization!
  - *Erroneous Managerial Conclusions:* Automated process mining dashboards report that resource T-F has $0\%$ utilization (idle all day) while tickets suffer severe SLA breaches waiting for T-F. In reality, tickets were resolved earlier in the shift or required trivial administrative sign-off, and T-F merely batched their administrative closure at shift conclusion.

#### G7: Manual measure (T-G)
- **Waiting Time:** Severely corrupted. Because `legacy_MES` quantizes start times to 14:00, cases M02, M03, M04, and M05 (which arrived at 14:01, 14:04, 14:07, and 14:11) produce negative waiting times: $-1, -4, -7, -11$ minutes. Naive analytics pipelines will crash, convert negative values to null, or clamp them to zero, biasing queue analysis downward.
- **Service Time:** Every case is assigned an identical 15-minute duration (14:00 to 14:15), completely masking the true physical cycle time of ~3 minutes per part.
- **Resource Utilization:** Summing logged durations yields $5 \times 15 = 75$ minutes of work in a 15-minute window ($500\%$ utilization).
- **Bottleneck Detection:** Falsely implies a massive multi-part batch oven or fixture rather than an individual manual operator with a coarse digital clock.

#### G8: Repair triage (T-H)
- **Waiting Time:** Logged waiting times range from 38 to 65 minutes. However, this delay was not caused by service congestion or slow processing; it was caused by an unrecorded 35-minute safety inspection (15:00–15:35).
- **Service Time:** Accurately captured at 7 minutes per item.
- **Resource Utilization:** Station was $0\%$ utilized for triage from 15:00 to 15:38, then $100\%$ utilized from 15:38 to 16:13.
- **Bottleneck Detection:** Without contextual linkage to the safety check, process mining algorithms misidentify Repair Triage as an unpredictable bottleneck subject to massive queue surges, failing to recognize that the surge was triggered by upstream capacity downtime.

1. **Failure of PM4Py to Differentiate Physical Realities:**

2. **Threshold Censoring Sensitivity:**
   - Setting `min_batch_size=5` silently eliminates clusters G1, G2, G3, and G5 ($N=4$) from batch detection entirely, falsely categorizing them as non-batched processes. An analyst relying strictly on PM4Py defaults would overlook critical batching and logging patterns simply due to cluster sample size.
3. **Merge Distance Invariance:**
   - Because the events in G1–G8 touch, overlap, or co-occur instantaneously, varying `merge_distance` between $0$s and $900$s produces identical cluster groupings. The heuristic cannot distinguish between work that was executed simultaneously versus work that was merely entered into a database within the same 15-minute window.

# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

### Sensitivity to Resource ID Trust
Process mining algorithms traditionally assume a bijective (1:1) mapping between a resource identifier and an individual physical human worker or machine. Violating this assumption fundamentally alters analytical conclusions:
1. **The Shared-Account Effect (G5):**
   - *If Resource ID is Trusted:* The analyst assumes a single person (`analyst_pool`) conducted four complex root-cause investigations simultaneously between 13:00 and 13:30, logging 70 person-minutes in 30 minutes ($233\%$ utilization). This leads to conclusions of severe worker multitasking, cognitive switching waste, and chronic overload.
   - *If Treated as Shared Account:* Recognizing that up to 3 analysts share the login redistributes the 70 minutes across a 3-person pool ($77.8\%$ utilization). Concurrency is recognized as parallel execution by distinct individuals, not multitasking stress. However, the 4-case peak concurrency (13:12–13:20) proves that either unlogged staff were present or sessions were left idle in background VDI tabs.
2. **Workstation/Cell Capacity (G1):**
   - *If Resource ID is Modeled as Unit Capacity ($c=1$):* RBT-7 appears to generate 12 minutes of work in 3 minutes ($400\%$ utilization), triggering false bottleneck alerts.
   - *If Modeled as Multi-Slot Fixture ($c=4$):* Concurrency is recognized as intended physical machine capacity.
3. **Desk/Tablet Sharing (G2, G3, G7):**
   - If individual logins (T-B, T-C, T-G) are shared by multiple operators on the same shift without badge re-authentication, what appears to be "start batching" (G2) could actually be two operators inspecting parts concurrently at the same station.

### Exact Timestamps vs. Interval Censoring
When timestamps are stored with coarse precision, treating them as exact mathematical points generates severe analytical pathologies:
1. **Resolution Breakdown in G7 (15-min precision):**
   - *Treated as Exact Points:* Cases M01–M05 start at exactly 14:00:00 and end at 14:15:00. For M02–M05 (arrivals 14:01–14:11), this creates an impossible negative waiting time (causality violation).
   - *Treated as Interval-Censored ($[t - \Delta, t + \Delta]$):* The recorded start 14:00 represents an interval $[13:52:30, 14:07:30]$ under round-to-nearest, or $[14:00:00, 14:15:00]$ under round-up. When modeled as intervals, the causality violation vanishes: true starts occurred after queue arrivals within the 15-minute measurement window. The apparent "simultaneous batch" dissolves into an unidentifiable sequence of independent measurements.
2. **Resolution in G2 and G3 (1-min precision):**
   - 1-minute precision rounds event completions to whole minutes, masking whether completions in G3 (11:30) were truly simultaneous down to the second or merely completed within the same 60-second window.

### Three Cases Where Identical Temporal Patterns Support Divergent Physical Realities

#### Case A: Identical Start and Completion ($\text{start}_i = \text{start}_j \land \text{complete}_i = \text{complete}_j$)
This identical mathematical pattern appears in G1, G6, and G7, yet represents three mutually exclusive physical realities:
1. *Genuine Machine Fixture Processing (G1):* Physical parts are clamped together into an automated cell and undergo simultaneous physical transformation.
2. *Administrative End-of-Shift Bulk Closure (G6):* No physical transformation occurs at that time; an operator performs a single batch click to clear a queue of previously completed work.
3. *Coarse Clock Quantization (G7):* Independent, sequential manual activities performed over a 15-minute window are forced into identical timestamp buckets by legacy software rounding rules.

#### Case B: Identical Start with Staggered Completions ($\text{start}_i = \text{start}_j \land \text{complete}_i < \text{complete}_j$)
This pattern (observed in G2) supports three competing physical explanations:
1. *UI Group-Start Artifact with Sequential Execution:* The operator clicks "Start Group" once, then performs single-piece sequential manual inspection (6–8 min per piece).
2. *Common Thermal/Chemical Setup with Sequential Unloading:* Items are placed together into a shared heating oven or chemical bath at 10:00, then removed and inspected individually at 10:08, 10:14, 10:21, and 10:27.
3. *Parallel Cognitive Multitasking:* A single inspector lays out all 4 parts across a bench, scanning back and forth across them simultaneously, signing off simpler items first.

#### Case C: Back-to-Back Adjacency ($\text{complete}_i = \text{start}_{i+1}$)
This pattern (observed in G4 and G8) supports three divergent operational explanations:
1. *Deliberate Sequential Batching (G4):* An operator intentionally allows work to accumulate into a batch before processing items back-to-back without leaving the workstation.
2. *Post-Downtime Queue Drainage (G8):* The operator is not following a batching policy; an unrecorded machine breakdown or safety check halted operations, creating a queue backlog that the operator subsequently services via ordinary single-piece FIFO flow.
3. *Scanner Auto-Advance UI Artifact:* The barcode scanner software automatically initiates the next queued work order the exact second the previous barcode is scanned, regardless of whether the operator is physically ready to work on the next item.

### Detailed Specification of Redesign Areas

1. **Execution Timestamps:** Decouple user interface start triggers from physical execution. Replace single-button group starts with individual step triggers. If an operator must initiate work on a physical batch, the system must log both the `batch_setup_start` and the individual `item_execution_start`.
2. **Recording Timestamps:** Mandate that every event record store both the physical client-side event timestamp (`execution_timestamp`) and the server receipt timestamp (`recorded_at`). A divergence between the two exceeding 5 seconds automatically flags the event for retrospective entry or offline caching.
3. **Source Clocks:** Implement Network Time Protocol (NTP) or Precision Time Protocol (PTP, IEEE 1588) across all operational technology (OT) and information technology (IT) networks. In G8, clock skew between the safety system and scanner prevented direct correlation of downtime; unified UTC synchronization eliminates this gap.
4. **Resource / Badge Identity:** Completely eliminate generic logins such as `analyst_pool`. Every transaction must log the unique operator badge ID (`operator_id="USR-4091"`). In multi-operator cells, log both the station ID (`station="RBT-7"`) and the human supervisor ID.
5. **Group / Batch Identifiers:** Extend the event schema with native batch attributes:
   - `batch_id`: Unique GUID grouping cases processed together.
   - `batch_type`: Enumeration distinguishing `PHYSICAL_MACHINE_BATCH`, `ADMINISTRATIVE_BULK_ENTRY`, `UI_GROUP_START`, or `FIFO_SEQUENTIAL_RUN`.
   - `batch_size`: Total count of cases in the batch.
   - `batch_position`: Ordinal sequence within the batch (e.g., 1 of 4).
6. **Workstation Capacity:** Embed workstation capacity parameters directly into the process mining metadata dictionary. Process mining tools must evaluate server utilization against workstation capacity $c$ rather than assuming single-server $c=1$.
7. **Pause / Resume Events:** Incorporate explicit pause, suspension, and downtime event types. In G8, rather than leaving a 38-minute unexplained queue gap, the system should log `ResourceUnavailable(resource="T-H", reason="Safety_Check", start=15:00, complete=15:35)`.
8. **Timestamp Precision Metadata:** Event logs must include source precision attributes (e.g., `precision_seconds=900` for legacy MES, `precision_seconds=1` for barcode scanners). Analytics engines can then automatically apply interval-censored survival analysis rather than treating quantized points as exact times.

### Summary of Operational Guidance for Process Mining Practitioners
- **Never Equate Identical Timestamps with Physical Simultaneity:** As proven by G1 vs. G6 vs. G7, identical timestamps can indicate a multi-part machine, a bulk-closure click, or a coarse clock. Contextual metadata, recording lags, and device architectures must guide interpretation.
- **De-link PM4Py Pattern Detection from Execution Semantics:** PM4Py batch discovery is a valuable temporal pattern detector, but its output categories (`Simultaneous`, `Batching on Start`, `Batching on End`, `Sequential batching`, `Concurrent batching`) reflect interval geometry, not operational causality.
- **Pre-process Logging Artifacts Before Queue Mining:** Feeding raw logging artifacts into automated queueing or simulation engines produces catastrophic distortions—such as infinite service rates in G6, negative waiting times in G7, and 400% utilization in G1 and G2. Applying the correction protocols defined above is an essential prerequisite for valid process intelligence.
