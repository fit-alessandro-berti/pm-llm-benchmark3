# 1. Assumptions, Objectives, and Decision Variables

## Assumptions
1. **Service Time Distributions**: Service times are modeled as uniform distributions. For ordinary and complex-tail cases:
   - Ordinary case times are uniformly sampled from the `ordinary case minutes` column.
   - Complex-tail cases are uniformly sampled from the `complex-tail case minutes` column, weighted by the `complex-tail probability`.
2. **Resource Multiplier Impact**: Resource-specific multipliers are applied to sampled service times.
3. **Parallelism for ME/ES**: Parallel execution supposes two resources do the tasks concurrently, splitting active time. Sequential execution assumes one resource performs both tasks, adding service times linearly without handover.
4. **SLAs Counting Delays**: SLAs include Intake, Diagnostic, and Validation time, including setup delays, coordination delays, and preemption penalties.
5. **Queue Management Rules**:
   - Cases have priority queues: P1 > P2 > P3.
   - Cases preempted once cannot be further displaced (max 1 preemption per case).
6. **Uncertainty Scenarios**: Demand multipliers, absence (U3), and other scenarios affect only arrivals or resource schedules, leaving service times and prioritization unchanged.
7. **Work Allocation Policies**: Only qualified resources can take on specific skills. For cross-skill overflow or constrained resources, this range may be expanded explicitly by policies.
8. **Fairness**: Utilization fairness is measured as the ratio of the most-loaded resource's utilization to the least-loaded among active resources (excluding planned downtimes or breaks).

## Objectives
Key objectives are:
1. **SLA Compliance Under Tail Risk**: Minimize violations of P1 (90% completion <120 minutes), P2 (90% <8 hours), and P3 (90% <24 hours) across base and shock scenarios. Maximize resilience to uncertainty (U1-U5).
2. **Utilization Fairness as Robustness**: Achieve high utilization ratios without excessively overloading individual workers. Planned utilization over 90% is unacceptable.
3. **Minimize Penalties**: Reduce total handover/setup/coordination penalties.
4. **Practical Implementability**: Policies must be simple enough for transparent routing, ensuring operational ease and preventing major errors.

## Decision Variables
1. Degree of resource pooling (strict specialization, full pooling, hybrid).
2. SLA reservation strategies (e.g., exclusive P1 capacity, overflow conditions).
3. Parallel vs. sequential execution for ME/ES work.
4. Dispatch rules (e.g., shortest expected remaining processing time, priority-driven allocation).
