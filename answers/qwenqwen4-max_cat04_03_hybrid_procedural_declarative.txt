```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import PetriNet, Marking
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.utils import petri_utils


def build_onboarding_net(strict_legal: bool = True):
    net = PetriNet("controlled_vendor_onboarding")
    initial = Marking()
    P = {}

    def place(name, tokens=0):
        pl = PetriNet.Place(name)
        net.places.add(pl)
        P[name] = pl
        if tokens:
            initial[pl] = tokens
        return pl

    def trans(name, ins, outs, label=None):
        tr = PetriNet.Transition(name, label)
        net.transitions.add(tr)
        for i in ins:
            petri_utils.add_arc_from_to(P[i], tr, net)
        for o in outs:
            petri_utils.add_arc_from_to(tr, P[o], net)
        return tr

    for name in ["start", "received", "cmd_todo", "rss_todo", "vba_todo",
                 "cmd_done", "rss_done", "vba_done", "screen_ok",
                 "classifiable", "clar_pending", "classified",

        place(name)
    initial[P["start"]] = 1
    initial[P["legal_clear"]] = 1
```

## 1.2 The R5/R6 conflict, stated plainly

* **R5 (Legal):** `Sanctions Escalation`  `Obtain Legal Opinion` **before** `Activate Vendor`, *under all circumstances*.
* **R6 (Operations):** activation **may precede** the opinion if jointly authorized *outside the log*, with the opinion within 24 h.

These are not two rules about different situations; they are two rules about the **same** situation with opposite modalities ("never" vs. "sometimes allowed"). No activity-only formalism can reconcile them, because the discriminating condition (joint authorization) **does not exist in the event log**. Any single verdict for T6 is therefore a *policy decision*, not a conformance fact.

### Resolution options

**(A) R5-strict / fail-closed (CHOSEN as the implemented default).**
The net contains a *legal-clearance* milestone: `Sanctions Escalation` consumes clearance and creates a legal hold; only `Obtain Legal Opinion` restores clearance; `Activate Vendor` requires clearance. R6 is treated as an **out-of-band governance waiver**, not as a conformance path: an escalated activation before the opinion is always reported `fail`, and may only be *waived* by a recorded human decision that is stored next to the verdict.
*Compliance risk:* legitimate emergency business is blocked or delayed; predictable behavioural side effects are shadow processes, unrecorded escalations, or back-dated opinion timestamps — i.e. the policy protects the log's appearance more than the underlying risk. Mitigation: monitor "escalation-free high-risk cases" as a gaming indicator.

**(B) R6-exception-aware / evidence-gated.**
Ship a *second, separately versioned* model variant in which `Activate Vendor` does not consume clearance, plus a mandatory external check: an authorization record with 2 distinct authorizers (neither being the activating resource), timestamped **before** activation, and an opinion within 24 h after activation. Missing evidence  `fail` (never `pass`).
*Compliance risk:* the hard control now depends on an attribute/registry that today does not exist; if the check is implemented fail-open, every unauthorized emergency activation looks conformant. Also creates severity inversion: an opinion at 25 h violates a *hard* 24 h deadline while the temporal profile (12 h ± 2·6 h = 24 h) would only *warn*.

**(C) Dual-regime reporting / quarantine.**
Run both variants, never merge them, emit two verdicts plus a `policy_regime` tag, and route the case to a human arbiter. Highest transparency, highest operational cost; useful during policy migration.

**Implemented choice:** (A) as `procedural_model` and as the authoritative DECLARE scoping, with (B) available as an explicitly named variant (`build_onboarding_net(strict_legal=False)` + `emergency_check`). T6 is reported under **both** in §8. The compliance risk of (A) is accepted knowingly: we prefer a visible false positive (blocked emergency) over an invisible false negative (unauthorized activation of a sanctioned vendor).

| R5 | Petri net legal-hold guard (**authoritative**, strict) + scoped DECLARE | `escalate: screen_ok+legal_clear  screen_ok+legal_hold`; `opinion: legal_hold  legal_clear`; `activate` requires `legal_clear`; scoped `precedence(OLO,AV)` on escalated cases | Guarded (ternary) precedence: "if SE then OLO before AV" | **Plain binary DECLARE cannot express the guard.** Unconditional `precedence(OLO,AV)` would fail T1/T2/T4/T5. Scoping = explicit log filter, not a DECLARE template |
| R6 | Resource/data layer + external authorization registry (**authoritative**) | `emergency_check()` in §7; hard 24 h deadline | Trigger condition is *outside* the log | Activity-only conservative approximation = R5-strict. No `emergency` attribute exists today  fail-closed |
| R7 | DECLARE `precedence(RSS,SE)` (**authoritative**) + net + skeleton | `("Run Sanctions Screening","Sanctions Escalation")`; net `screen_ok` place; skeleton `always_before` same orientation | Pure ordering prerequisite | Triple redundancy (cheap, keep) |
| R8 | DECLARE `noncoexistence` (**authoritative**) + skeleton | `(MA,EDD)`, `(MA,CA)` | Symmetric mutual exclusion | Overlaps R2; net routes enforce it too |
| R9 | Resource-aware layer (**authoritative, exclusive**) | `filter_four_eyes_principle` + explicit dataframe SoD | Resources are not in the activity alphabet | No activity-only approximation exists; missing `org:resource`  fail-closed |
| R10 | Log Skeleton `activ_freq` (**authoritative**) + data-layer counts | `AV:{0,1}`, `RV:{0,1}`, `RFP:{0,1}` | Cardinality domains are exactly what `activ_freq` expresses | Net enforces it structurally; DECLARE `absence(n)` semantics are version-dependent, avoided |
| R11 | Data/ordering layer with occurrence indices (**authoritative**) + scoped DECLARE | §7 `r11_check`; scoped `response(CBD,Suspend Vendor)` on cases with post-activation CBD | Needs "after activation", "another verification", "before any later payment" — occurrence-relative, data/ordering hybrid | Excluded from the net (A14). `precedence(VBA,RFP)` is **vacuously satisfied** by the pre-activation verification — a trap |
| R12 | Temporal profile (**authoritative, advisory**) | 3 pairs, seconds, `zeta=2.0` | Statistical expectation, not a control-flow rule | Cannot express hard deadlines (R6's 24 h); silent when a pair is not instantiated (T5, T8) |

# 3. Procedural Backbone

## 3.1 Compact formal description

Backbone projection (process-tree notation, documentation only):

```
SEQ( Receive Onboarding Request,
     AND( Capture Master Data, Run Sanctions Screening, Verify Bank Account ),
     LOOP(  , SEQ(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification) ,  ),
     Classify Risk,
     XOR( SEQ(Manager Approval, Activate Vendor, Release First Payment, Close Onboarding),
          SEQ(Enhanced Due Diligence, Compliance Approval, Activate Vendor,
              Release First Payment, Close Onboarding),
          SEQ(Reject Vendor, Close Onboarding) ) )
```

The **implemented** model is an accepting Petri net, because the R5 legal-clearance guard is a *milestone/state* condition, not a block-structured construct. Net skeleton (places `P`, transitions labelled unless ``):

```
start  receive  received  fork  {cmd_todo, rss_todo, vba_todo}
capture/screen/verify  {cmd_done, rss_done+screen_ok, vba_done}
join(cmd_done,rss_done,vba_done)  classifiable
req_clar: classifiable  clar_pending ; rec_clar: clar_pending  classifiable
classify: classifiable  classified
low/high/reject: classified  low_ready | high_ready | reject_ready
manager: low_ready  approved ; edd: high_ready  edd_done ; compliance: edd_done  approved
reject: reject_ready  rejected
escalate: {screen_ok, legal_clear}  {screen_ok, legal_hold}      # R7 + hold
opinion:  legal_hold  legal_clear                                # R4/R5
activate: {approved, legal_clear*}  active                       # *strict variant only
pay:      active  paid
close_paid:   {paid, screen_ok}  closed
close_reject: {rejected, screen_ok, legal_clear*}  closed
M0 = {start:1, legal_clear:1}   Mf = {closed:1}
```

`screen_ok` is a self-loop token for `escalate` and is consumed at closing, so no token is left over at `Mf`; `legal_clear` is consumed by `activate` (accepted path) or by `close_reject` (rejected path). A rejected case with an unanswered escalation cannot reach `Mf` — R4 is enforced even on the rejection route.

## 3.2 PM4Py code

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import PetriNet, Marking
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.utils import petri_utils
from pm4py.objects.log.obj import EventLog, Trace

LABELS = [
    "Receive Onboarding Request", "Capture Master Data", "Run Sanctions Screening",
    "Verify Bank Account", "Classify Risk", "Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification",
    "Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion", "Enhanced Due Diligence",
    "Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval", "Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor",
    "Change Bank Details", "Suspend Vendor", "Release First Payment", "Close Onboarding",
]

# Activities deliberately NOT in the procedural net (post-activation, R11 -> data layer)
OUT_OF_NET = {"Change Bank Details", "Suspend Vendor"}


def build_onboarding_net(strict_legal: bool = True):
    """strict_legal=True  -> R5-strict resolution (chosen default)
       strict_legal=False -> R6-exception variant (needs external authorization check)"""
    net = PetriNet("controlled_vendor_onboarding")
    initial, P = Marking(), {}

    def place(name):
        pl = PetriNet.Place(name); net.places.add(pl); P[name] = pl; return pl

    def trans(name, ins, outs, label=None):
        tr = PetriNet.Transition(name, label); net.transitions.add(tr)
        for i in ins:
            petri_utils.add_arc_from_to(P[i], tr, net)
        for o in outs:
            petri_utils.add_arc_from_to(tr, P[o], net)
        return tr

    for n in ["start", "received", "cmd_todo", "rss_todo", "vba_todo", "cmd_done",
              "rss_done", "vba_done", "screen_ok", "classifiable", "clar_pending",
              "classified", "low_ready", "high_ready", "edd_done", "reject_ready",
              "approved", "active", "paid", "rejected", "legal_clear", "legal_hold",
              "closed"]:
        place(n)
    initial[P["start"]] = 1
    initial[P["legal_clear"]] = 1          # legal clearance milestone

    trans("receive", ["start"], ["received"], "Receive Onboarding Request")
    trans("fork", ["received"], ["cmd_todo", "rss_todo", "vba_todo"])
    trans("capture", ["cmd_todo"], ["cmd_done"], "Capture Master Data")
    trans("screen", ["rss_todo"], ["rss_done", "screen_ok"], "Run Sanctions Screening")
    trans("verify", ["vba_todo"], ["vba_done"], "Verify Bank Account")
    trans("join", ["cmd_done", "rss_done", "vba_done"], ["classifiable"])

    trans("req_clar", ["classifiable"], ["clar_pending"], "Request Clarification")
    trans("rec_clar", ["clar_pending"], ["classifiable"], "Receive Clarification")
    trans("classify", ["classifiable"], ["classified"], "Classify Risk")

    trans("choose_low", ["classified"], ["low_ready"])
    trans("choose_high", ["classified"], ["high_ready"])
    trans("choose_reject", ["classified"], ["reject_ready"])
    trans("manager", ["low_ready"], ["approved"], "Manager Approval")
    trans("edd", ["high_ready"], ["edd_done"], "Enhanced Due Diligence")
    trans("compliance", ["edd_done"], ["approved"], "Compliance Approval")
    trans("reject", ["reject_ready"], ["rejected"], "Reject Vendor")

    trans("escalate", ["screen_ok", "legal_clear"], ["screen_ok", "legal_hold"],
          "Sanctions Escalation")
    trans("opinion", ["legal_hold"], ["legal_clear"], "Obtain Legal Opinion")

    trans("activate", ["approved"] + (["legal_clear"] if strict_legal else []),
          ["active"], "Activate Vendor")
    trans("pay", ["active"], ["paid"], "Release First Payment")
    trans("close_paid", ["paid", "screen_ok"] + ([] if strict_legal else ["legal_clear"]),
          ["closed"], "Close Onboarding")
    trans("close_reject", ["rejected", "screen_ok"] + (["legal_clear"] if strict_legal else []),
          ["closed"], "Close Onboarding")

    final = Marking({P["closed"]: 1})
    return net, initial, final


# CANONICAL PROCEDURAL MODEL (R5-strict)
procedural_model = build_onboarding_net(strict_legal=True)
# Explicitly separate, versioned policy variant -- never merged with the above
procedural_model_emergency = build_onboarding_net(strict_legal=False)

NET_ALPHABET = {t.label for t in procedural_model[0].transitions if t.label}


def project_log(log, alphabet=NET_ALPHABET):
    """The procedural layer is evaluated on the projection of the case onto NET_ALPHABET.
       Repeated 'Verify Bank Account' events after activation are also projected out
       (R11 is owned by the data layer)."""
    out = EventLog()
    for tr in log:
        t = Trace(dict(tr.attributes))
        seen_verify = False
        for ev in tr:
            a = ev["concept:name"]
            if a not in alphabet or a in OUT_OF_NET:
                continue
            if a == "Verify Bank Account":
                if seen_verify:      # post-activation re-verification -> data layer
                    continue
                seen_verify = True
            t.append(ev)
        if len(t):
            out.append(t)
    return out


def procedural_verdicts(log, model=procedural_model):
    net, im, fm = model
    diag = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(project_log(log), net, im, fm)
    return [d["is_fit"] for d in diag]
```

## 3.3 Explanation of the modelling choices

* **Concurrency.** `fork`/`join` give true AND-concurrency for the three checks; any interleaving is accepted (T2's `RSS,VBA,CMD` order is as valid as T1's). `Classify Risk` is the join synchronisation — this is *not* expressible as a precedence pair alone, which is why DECLARE only carries the three weaker `always_before`-style prerequisites.
* **Choices.** Three silent transitions consume the single `classified` token: low-risk, high-risk, rejection. This structurally guarantees R1, R8 and the "no approval in a rejected case" half of R2 without any declarative rule.
* **Clarification loop.** `classifiable  clar_pending` enforces strict request/response alternation, permits arbitrarily many cycles, and makes any final outcome unreachable while a request is pending — exactly R3, including the part DECLARE cannot express.
* **Terminal outcomes.** Two transitions labelled `Close Onboarding` (paid vs. rejected route) with final marking `{closed:1}`; leftover-token accounting is explicit so that "reached the end" is meaningful.
* **Legal guard.** The `legal_clear`/`legal_hold` pair is a milestone. It is the reason for choosing a net over a process tree: a tree cannot express "activation is blocked while a hold is active" without duplicating the whole subtree per escalation state.
* **Deliberately excluded.** `Change Bank Details`, `Suspend Vendor`, repeated `Verify Bank Account`, and any second payment. R11 interleaves with an at-most-once payment and requires occurrence-relative ordering; encoding it would need counters/guards and would make the net non-block-structured and unreadable. **Disclosed approximation:** the procedural layer is *projection-based*, hence blind to R11 and to post-activation escalation. Also disclosed: only one pending escalation (A5) and only the pre-classification clarification window (A4).

```python
    """Flatten to PM4Py's flat key convention: (template, act) / (template, act1, act2)."""
```

## 4.1 Rule semantics and direction

| Rule | Direction | Reading | Why this template (and not a neighbour) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `init(ROR)` | unary | first event is `Receive Onboarding Request` | stronger than `existence`; matches B1 |
| `exactly_one(ROR/CR/CO)` | unary | exactly one occurrence | `existence` would allow duplicates; R10-style caps for these three |
| `noncoexistence(RV,AV)` | symmetric | never both in one case | **not** `nonsuccession`: `nonsuccession(RV,AV)` only forbids RV *before* AV and would let T8 (AV then RV) pass |
| `noncoexistence(MA,CA)`, `(MA,EDD)`, `(RV,MA)`, `(RV,CA)` | symmetric | route/decision exclusivity | R2/R8 are cardinality-of-co-occurrence statements, not ordering |
| `altresponse(RC,RCl)` | RC  RCl | each request is answered before the next request | `response` would allow `RC,RC,RCl`; `chainresponse` would forbid any interleaving event (illegal under concurrency) |
| `response(SE,OLO)` | SE  OLO | every escalation is eventually followed by an opinion | `responded_existence` only demands *some* opinion, even one **before** the escalation; kept only as a weaker drift probe |
| `response(MA,AV)`, `response(CA,AV)` | approval  activation | an approval must lead to activation | `precedence(MA,AV)` unconditionally would force a manager approval in high-risk cases |
| `response(RV,CO)`, `response(RFP,CO)` | outcome  close | terminal closure | ordering-free obligation |
| `precedence(RSS,SE)` | RSS is prerequisite of SE | SE only after screening | R7 is a prerequisite, not an obligation: screening does **not** force escalation |
| `precedence(EDD,CA)`, `precedence(CR,MA/CA/RV)` | source precedes target | route prerequisites | no obligation implied |
| `succession(AV,RFP)` | both directions | payment happens iff after activation | = `response`  `precedence`; avoids two duplicated entries |
| scoped `precedence(OLO,AV)` | OLO prerequisite of AV, **escalated cases only** | R5-strict | binary DECLARE has no guard argument; the guard is the sub-log filter |
| scoped `precedence(RCl,AV/RV/CO)` | answered before outcome | R3 (partial) | still cannot count pending requests  §7 scan is authoritative |
| scoped `response(CBD,Suspend)` | change  suspension | R11 (partial) | unconditional version would also suspend pre-activation changes, which R11 does not require |

**Not used, on purpose:** `chainresponse`/`chainprecedence`/`chainsuccession`/`nonchainsuccession` (adjacency is destroyed by the concurrent block and by interleaved legal work — T7 legitimately puts `Obtain Legal Opinion` between EDD and Compliance Approval); `absence(n)` (version-dependent cardinality semantics — R10 goes to `activ_freq`); `coexistence` (would force unwanted activities).

# 5. Log-Skeleton Model

Orientation per A12: `always_before = (prerequisite, dependent)`, `always_after = (trigger, obligation)`.

```python
log_skeleton = {

        ("Receive Onboarding Request", "Classify Risk"),

        ("Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor"),                  # R1
        ("Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval"),           # R2/R8
        ("Manager Approval", "Enhanced Due Diligence"),        # R8
        ("Reject Vendor", "Manager Approval"),                 # R2
        ("Reject Vendor", "Compliance Approval"),              # R2
    },
    "activ_freq": {
        "Receive Onboarding Request": {1},
        "Capture Master Data": {1},
        "Run Sanctions Screening": {1},
        "Classify Risk": {1},
        "Manager Approval": {0, 1},
        "Compliance Approval": {0, 1},
        "Enhanced Due Diligence": {0, 1},
        "Reject Vendor": {0, 1},
        "Activate Vendor": {0, 1},                             # R10
        "Release First Payment": {0, 1},                       # R10
        "Close Onboarding": {1},
    },
    "directly_follows": set(),
}
```

**Defence of the populated sets, overlaps and disagreements**

* `equivalence(RC,RCl)` is the only *counting* constraint the skeleton can add beyond DECLARE: it catches T5 (1 vs 0) even if `altresponse` were mis-scoped. `equivalence(AV,RFP)` is redundant with `succession` + `activ_freq`, but cheap and catches T8's missing payment.
* **Deliberate omission:** `("Obtain Legal Opinion","Activate Vendor")` is *not* in `always_before`. Log-skeleton constraints are unconditional; adding it would fail T1/T2/T4/T5 (no opinion at all). This is a **coverage disagreement**, not a truth disagreement: R5 is owned by the net guard and the scoped DECLARE rule. Consequently T6 passes this layer while violating the authoritative policy — an intentional, documented blind spot.
* **Deliberate omission:** `(RC,RCl)` in `always_before`/`always_after` pairs beyond the one response entry: with several cycles, "every occurrence of a before every occurrence of b" is false for legitimate logs. Alternation belongs to `altresponse`/the net.
* **Deliberate omission from `activ_freq`:** `Verify Bank Account` (R11 permits unbounded re-verification), `Request/Receive Clarification`, `Sanctions Escalation`, `Obtain Legal Opinion`, `Change Bank Details`, `Suspend Vendor`. `activ_freq` takes *finite* count sets; writing `{0,1,2,3}` would silently invent a cap. Unbounded activities are left unconstrained here.
* **`directly_follows` left empty:** the key's semantics (allowed-set vs. observed-set) varies across PM4Py versions, and under true concurrency the set of legitimate adjacent pairs is large and unstable (`Receive Onboarding Request` may be followed by any of three checks). An allowed-set reading would over-reject; a mandatory-set reading is not standard. Adjacency where it matters is already in the net.
* Overlap with DECLARE: `always_after(SE,OLO)`  `response(SE,OLO)`; `never_together`  `noncoexistence`; `activ_freq` for ROR/CR/CO  `exactly_one`. These are *intentional* cross-checks that detect tool/orientation bugs, not independent evidence.

# 6. Temporal Profile

```python
HOUR = 3600.0

temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (48 * HOUR, 24 * HOUR),   # (172800, 86400)
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"):   (12 * HOUR,  6 * HOUR),   # ( 43200, 21600)
    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"):       (72 * HOUR, 36 * HOUR),   # (259200, 129600)
}
zeta = 2.0

# pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile(log, temporal_profile, zeta=zeta)
```

Two-sided acceptance bands: clarification `[0, 96] h`, opinion `[0, 24] h`, payment `[0, 144] h` (lower bounds clipped at 0).

**Why warnings, not violations.** R12 is explicitly described by Compliance as *normative enough to flag but not to reject*. Statistically, a 2 band on a skewed, heavy-tailed duration distribution produces false positives by construction (waiting for a vendor, weekends, jurisdiction holidays). Promoting these to control-flow violations would (i) reject legally sound cases, (ii) create pressure to manipulate timestamps, and (iii) blur the audit distinction between *"the process was executed in the wrong order / by the wrong person"* (hard) and *"the process was slow"* (advisory). Hard time obligations — R6's 24 h opinion deadline — are **not** delegated to this layer; they are implemented as a hard check in §7. Note the deliberate severity separation: an opinion at 25 h is only a temporal *warning* here but a *hard failure* under the R6 exception path.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

Required attributes: `case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, `time:timestamp`, `org:resource`.
Optional/needed-but-absent today: `risk_level` (output of `Classify Risk`), `sanctions_hit`, `lifecycle:transition`, and the case-level emergency registry fields `emergency:authorized`, `emergency:authorizers`, `emergency:authorization_time`.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

H24 = pd.Timedelta(hours=24)


def events_df(log):
    df = pm4py.convert_to_dataframe(log)
    for col in ("org:resource",):
        if col not in df.columns:
            df[col] = None                       # A13: missing -> hard rules fail closed
    df["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"], utc=True, errors="coerce")
    return df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"], kind="mergesort")


def _sel(df, cid, act):
    return df[(df["case:concept:name"] == cid) & (df["concept:name"] == act)]


def _res(df, cid, act):
    return set(_sel(df, cid, act)["org:resource"].dropna())


def run_resource_data_checks(log, policy="R5-strict", authorization_registry=None):
    """Returns findings: (case, rule, severity, message). severity in {fail, warning, not_evaluated}"""
    df = events_df(log)
    findings = []
    for cid, g in df.groupby("case:concept:name"):
        acts = list(g["concept:name"])

        # ---- attribute gate -------------------------------------------------
        if g["time:timestamp"].isna().any():
            findings.append((cid, "ATTR/time", "fail", "unparseable timestamp: ordering unverifiable"))
        sod_relevant = {"Verify Bank Account", "Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval",
                        "Capture Master Data", "Activate Vendor"} & set(acts)
        if sod_relevant and g[g["concept:name"].isin(sod_relevant)]["org:resource"].isna().any():
            findings.append((cid, "ATTR/resource", "fail", "R9 unverifiable: org:resource missing"))

        # ---- R9a: Verify Bank Account != applicable approval ---------------
        verifiers = _res(df, cid, "Verify Bank Account")
        approvers = _res(df, cid, "Manager Approval") | _res(df, cid, "Compliance Approval")
        if verifiers and approvers:
            clash = verifiers & approvers
            if clash:
                findings.append((cid, "R9a", "fail", f"four-eyes breach: {sorted(clash)}"))
        # ---- R9b: Capture Master Data != Activate Vendor -------------------
        capturers, activators = _res(df, cid, "Capture Master Data"), _res(df, cid, "Activate Vendor")
        if capturers and activators and (capturers & activators):
            findings.append((cid, "R9b", "fail", f"four-eyes breach: {sorted(capturers & activators)}"))

        # ---- R2: exactly one approval in an accepted case ------------------
        n_ma, n_ca = acts.count("Manager Approval"), acts.count("Compliance Approval")
        if "Activate Vendor" in acts and n_ma + n_ca != 1:
            findings.append((cid, "R2", "fail", f"accepted case with {n_ma+n_ma*0+n_ca} approvals"))
        if "Reject Vendor" in acts and (n_ma or n_ca):
            findings.append((cid, "R2", "fail", "approval present in rejected case"))

        # ---- R10: hard cardinality caps ------------------------------------
        for a in ("Activate Vendor", "Reject Vendor", "Release First Payment"):
            if acts.count(a) > 1:
                findings.append((cid, "R10", "fail", f"{a} occurs {acts.count(a)} times"))

        # ---- R3 (counting part DECLARE cannot do): no pending request at outcome
        pending = 0
        for a in acts:
            if a == "Request Clarification":
                pending += 1
            elif a == "Receive Clarification":
                pending = max(0, pending - 1)
            elif a in ("Activate Vendor", "Reject Vendor", "Close Onboarding") and pending:
                findings.append((cid, "R3", "fail", f"{a} with {pending} unanswered clarification(s)"))
                break

        # ---- R5 / R6: escalation vs. activation ----------------------------
        se = _sel(df, cid, "Sanctions Escalation")
        av = _sel(df, cid, "Activate Vendor")
        olo = _sel(df, cid, "Obtain Legal Opinion")
        if len(se) and len(av):
            t_se, t_av = se["time:timestamp"].min(), av["time:timestamp"].min()
            t_olo = olo["time:timestamp"].min() if len(olo) else None
            early_activation = (t_olo is None) or (t_av < t_olo)
            if early_activation:
                if policy == "R5-strict":
                    findings.append((cid, "R5", "fail",
                                     "activation before legal opinion after escalation (R6 not accepted)"))
                else:  # R6-exception-aware, evidence-gated, fail-closed
                    rec = (authorization_registry or {}).get(cid)
                    if not rec:
                        findings.append((cid, "R6/evidence", "fail",
                                         "no emergency authorization record: fail-closed"))
                    else:
                        auths = set(rec.get("authorizers", []))
                        t_auth = rec.get("authorization_time")
                        if not rec.get("authorized") or len(auths) < 2 \
                           or (activators & auths) or t_auth is None or t_auth > t_av:
                            findings.append((cid, "R6/authorization", "fail",
                                             "invalid joint authorization (count/identity/time)"))
                        elif t_olo is None or (t_olo - t_av) > H24:
                            findings.append((cid, "R6/deadline", "fail",
                                             "legal opinion not within 24h of emergency activation"))
                        else:
                            findings.append((cid, "R6", "warning",
                                             "authorized emergency activation: exception, not conformance"))

        # ---- R11: post-activation bank change ------------------------------
        if len(av):
            t_av = av["time:timestamp"].min()
            for _, ch in _sel(df, cid, "Change Bank Details").iterrows():
                t_ch = ch["time:timestamp"]
                if t_ch <= t_av:
                    continue                                    # pre-activation change: R11 silent
                susp = _sel(df, cid, "Suspend Vendor")
                if not (susp["time:timestamp"] > t_ch).any():
                    findings.append((cid, "R11a", "fail", "no Suspend Vendor after post-activation change"))
                later_pay = _sel(df, cid, "Release First Payment")
                later_pay = later_pay[later_pay["time:timestamp"] > t_ch]
                if len(later_pay):
                    t_pay = later_pay["time:timestamp"].min()
                    rev = _sel(df, cid, "Verify Bank Account")
                    if not ((rev["time:timestamp"] > t_ch) & (rev["time:timestamp"] < t_pay)).any():
                        findings.append((cid, "R11b", "fail",
                                         "payment after bank change without re-verification"))
                    if len(susp) and not ((susp["time:timestamp"] < t_pay).any()):
                        findings.append((cid, "R11c", "warning", "payment while suspension unresolved"))

        # ---- data-aware routing plausibility (blind spot today) ------------
        if "risk_level" not in g.columns:
            findings.append((cid, "DATA/risk_level", "not_evaluated",
                             "cannot check that the route matches the classified risk"))
    return findings
```

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

**Duplicated enforcement.** R1 is enforced three times (net choice place, DECLARE `noncoexistence`, skeleton `never_together`); R7 three times; R4 four times if `responded_existence` is counted; R2/R8 overlap each other almost entirely. This is *deliberate* but must be labelled: duplicated findings are **one violation reported by three detectors**, not three violations. Without de-duplication by requirement ID, severity scoring and dashboards triple-count and mislead. The weaker duplicates (`responded_existence` vs `response`) are retained only as *drift probes*: if the strong rule holds and the weak one is what a miner reports, the log has changed shape.

**Constraints that become inconsistent under loops / post-activation change.**
* R10 (`Release First Payment`  1) versus R11 ("before any *later* Release First Payment"): if payment already precedes the bank change, R11's re-verification clause is unreachable/vacuous. The two rules are only jointly satisfiable when the change happens between activation and the single payment. This must be stated to Compliance, not silently "resolved".
* `Suspend Vendor` has no counterpart `Unsuspend`/`Reactivate` activity in the alphabet, so "suspension resolved" is unobservable; §7 can only emit a warning (R11c).
* Unbounded repetition breaks finite `activ_freq` sets (`Verify Bank Account`, clarification, escalation) — hence their omission.
* `always_before` under loops: "every a before every b" is false for legitimate multi-cycle clarification; only alternation templates and the net loop are sound there.
* The net's single `legal_hold` token forbids two concurrent escalations, and (strict variant) forbids escalation after activation. Both are conservative approximations that could reject legitimate cases; they are disclosed, not hidden.

**Vacuity.** Most DECLARE rules and all temporal pairs are *vacuously* satisfied in short traces: T3 satisfies every noncoexistence, response and precedence rule simply because the triggers are absent. T5's unanswered clarification produces **zero** temporal deviations; T8's missing payment likewise. `precedence(VBA, RFP)` is satisfied by the *pre-activation* verification and therefore proves nothing about R11's re-verification. And the combination `altresponse(RC,RCl)` + `equivalence(RC,RCl)` + scoped `precedence(RCl,AV)` still admits `RC,RCl,RC,AV,RCl` — which is exactly why the pending-request scan in §7 (and the net) is authoritative for R3. Vacuous passes must be reported as `not evaluated`/`vacuous`, never as evidence of compliance.

**Missing case attributes.** No `emergency` flag or joint authorization (R6 is *unfalsifiable* from the log); no `risk_level` or `sanctions_hit`, so we cannot check that T1 really was low-risk or that T6's high-risk route was warranted — routing plausibility is a data condition, not a control-flow condition; no `org:role`/org model, so R9 is identity-based only (a manager approving her own verification under a second user ID escapes); no `lifecycle:transition`, so "completion" of the three checks is assumed.

**Timestamp-only limitations.** Hour granularity and complete-only events make waiting time vs. processing time indistinguishable; concurrent events can be serialized arbitrarily (the AND-block tolerates this, adjacency-based rules would not); equal timestamps make "before" undefined (needs a stable tie-break); clock skew across systems can fabricate or hide an R5 violation; the Gaussian temporal profile has a negative lower bound, so T7's 2-hour activationpayment is not flagged; and the profile is silent whenever a pair is absent.

**Support/confidence as normative truth.** Setting `support = 1.0, confidence = 1.0` is a *policy assertion*. If these dictionaries were instead mined from history, the emergency activations of R6 would appear as a small confidence deficit on R5, and a threshold-based miner would happily drop the very rule Legal insists on. Rare-but-catastrophic violations (sanctions activation) are precisely the ones with low support. Conversely, high support for a bad habit (Cora verifying and approving) would normalize an SoD breach. Therefore: mined models are *evidence*, the versioned policy dictionaries are *norms*, and any divergence between them is a governance ticket — never an automatic relaxation.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

**Canonical procedural representation.** The accepting Petri net `procedural_model = build_onboarding_net(strict_legal=True)`, stored with a semantic version, its alphabet, its projection function, and its final marking. A process tree is kept only as human-readable documentation of the backbone; the net is canonical because the legal-clearance milestone is not block-structured. `procedural_model_emergency` is a *separate artifact* with its own version and its own approval chain — the two are never merged, averaged, or auto-selected at runtime.

**Authoritative policy layer.** Hard controls: the net (routing, R3 alternation, R5 guard) **plus** the scoped DECLARE sets **plus** the resource/data layer (R9, R6, R11, counting parts of R2/R3/R10). Advisory: temporal profile (R12). The log skeleton is a *regression and drift detector*, not an authority: it is cheap, unconditional, and intentionally incomplete (R5). Every finding carries `(requirement_id, layer, authoritative: bool, severity)`.

**Execution order.**
0. **Attribute/quality gate** — case id, labels, timestamps, `org:resource`; unparseable  quarantine, do not silently continue.
1. **Resource/data hard checks** (§7), including the emergency-registry join and R11 occurrence logic. Run first because they are the checks no other layer can approximate, and because they determine whether an exception path is even admissible.
2. **Procedural conformance** on the projected log (alignments; report the first deviating event, not just a fitness score).
3. **DECLARE**: base model on the full log, then each scoped model on its filtered sub-log; record the scope with each verdict.
4. **Log skeleton** on the full log (orientation pinned by unit tests).
5. **Temporal profile** with `zeta = 2.0`, advisory only.
6. **Arbitration**: de-duplicate by requirement ID, apply the fail-closed rule for missing evidence, and attach the active `policy_regime` (`R5-strict` | `R6-exception`).

**Surfacing contradictory findings.** Never collapse layers into one boolean. Show a per-requirement verdict strip, e.g. T6: `R5: fail (net, strict) | R5-scoped DECLARE: fail | skeleton: pass (rule not expressible) | temporal: pass | R6: fail (evidence missing)`, with the earliest decisive event (`Activate Vendor@13`) highlighted and an explicit banner: *"Legal R5 and Operations R6 conflict; the discriminating authorization is not in the event log. Verdict shown under regime R5-strict; the R6-exception verdict is available only with an external authorization record."* T4 gets the complementary banner: *"All activity-only layers pass; the violation is resource-based."* T7 gets: *"Ordering conformant; temporal expectation exceeded (34 h vs 12 h ± 12 h) — review, do not reject."* Hard failures quarantine the case; warnings open a compliance review task; a waived R5 violation stays `fail` in the record with a linked, signed waiver — the verdict is never rewritten to `pass`. Finally, T1–T8 are shipped as a regression suite that pins template semantics, skeleton orientation, projection behaviour, and the fail-closed default across PM4Py upgrades.
