## 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

The key distinction is whether an adaptation preserves essential service **while preserving or compensating for required controls**, or whether it preserves throughput **by removing controls and shifting the problem into later reconciliation**.

In this case, the DFGs and metrics support a plausible interpretation: teams did not simply fail. They improvised a mission-critical supply chain under outage. The resilient core was hospital-critical triage, manual authorization for some orders, temporary release control for controlled items, deferral of noncritical bulk, and later backlog prioritization and reconciliation. The concealed noncompliance core was the removal of system-enforced authorization, quality/release, scan, proof-of-delivery, and temperature evidence, followed by a long audit-remediation tail.

### True resilience, with conditions

These adaptations can be considered resilient when they are bounded, visible, risk-proportionate, and recoverable:

- **A. Hospital-critical fast lane**, when combined with minimum authorization and risk-based release. The hospital-critical median cycle time of 10h and fill rate of 91% show service protection. But the fast lane is resilient only if it does not bypass required controls for controlled, cold-chain, or recall-sensitive items.
- **B. Manual authorization check**, when performed by trained compliance staff with captured evidence. It preserved a control path for 2,500 orders during a period when system authorization was unavailable.
- **D. Manual inventory snapshot check**, when used early in T2 and refreshed before inventory became stale. It enabled 3,920 manual pick lists and was a reasonable early workaround.
- **E. Manual pick list**, when accompanied by mandatory evidence capture. It maintained physical flow but becomes noncompliant if scans and traceability are merely reconstructed later without adequate controls.
- **F. Temporary release control**, when applied at 100% for controlled-substance, cold-chain, and recall-sensitive items. It likely reduced controlled-substance errors, but the observed cold-chain documentation gap shows it must be strengthened.
- **I. Defer noncritical order**, when transparent and time-boxed. It protected hospital-critical capacity but must be governed to avoid unfair harm to pharmacy and wholesale customers.
- **J. Backlog prioritization**, when explicit and fairness-aware. It helped hospital-critical service return to baseline, but informal rules can create hidden inequity.
- **K. Reconcile manual reservation**, as a recovery control. It is necessary to restore inventory and order integrity, provided it is tracked and completed within SLAs.

### Risky shortcuts

These adaptations preserve throughput but remove or weaken required controls:

- **C. Skip system authorization check** is a risky shortcut when it relies on assumed standing authorization. It may be acceptable only as a documented low-risk exception, not as a normal workaround.
- **G. Dispatch without scan** is the most serious traceability shortcut. It accelerated dispatch but created the largest custody and traceability gap, especially dangerous for controlled-substance items.
- **H. Phone proof of delivery** is risky for controlled and high-risk products because it is weaker than signed or electronic POD and can delay evidence entry.
- **D. Manual inventory snapshot check** becomes risky as the snapshot ages. It is not inherently bad, but stale inventory data creates overpick, wrong lot, and reconciliation risk.
- **E. Manual pick list** becomes risky if evidence capture is weak and scans are reconstructed after the fact.
- **F. Temporary release control** becomes risky if sampling misses cold-chain temperature documentation or if release is not consistently documented.

### Ambiguous depending on risk appetite

Some adaptations are neither clearly resilient nor clearly noncompliant; their acceptability depends on product risk, customer criticality, duration, and evidence quality:

- **A. Hospital-critical fast lane before full electronic authorization** is acceptable only if a minimum offline authorization control exists and the product is not high-risk.
- **C. Skip system authorization check for known ambient customers** may be acceptable for low-risk ambient items if the customer is pre-approved, the exception is documented, and retroactive validation occurs quickly.
- **G. Dispatch without scan for low-risk ambient items** may be acceptable only if an equivalent courier manifest exists and post-scan occurs within a short SLA. It should not be accepted for controlled, cold-chain, or recall-sensitive items.
- **H. Phone proof of delivery for ambient items** may be acceptable with structured fields, recipient identity, order ID, timestamp, and rapid entry.
- **I. Defer noncritical order** is operationally rational but commercially and fairness-sensitive. It is acceptable only with customer communication, recommit dates, and capacity reserves.
- **J. Backlog prioritization** is resilient if explicit and fair; it is risky if informal and biased toward one customer group.
- **L. Audit remediation** is useful but not equivalent to real-time compliance. It is acceptable as a recovery mechanism, but not as a substitute for control design.

The important judgment is this: **hospital-critical throughput preservation was not sufficient resilience by itself**. During T2, hospital-critical fill rate remained 91%, but traceability exceptions reached 9.5%, cold-chain temperature documentation missing reached 4.8%, controlled-substance dual-control missing reached 2.9%, and dispatch without scan was a major observed deviation. That combination shows operational resilience coexisting with concealed noncompliance risk.

## 4. Recovery process model

The redesigned recovery process should be called an **Emergency Order-to-Delivery Recovery Model**. It should be pre-approved, drilled, and activated before teams improvise under pressure.

**Notation:** Arrows denote flow. Square brackets denote decision gates. “Hold” means pause the order and notify the customer or internal owner. “Exception” means record the gap and route it to a risk-based remediation queue.

### Trigger conditions

Activate recovery mode when any of the following occur:

- WMS is unavailable for more than 30 minutes.
- Order intake, inventory synchronization, scanning, electronic proof of delivery, or temperature monitoring is unavailable for more than 1 hour.
- A cyber incident is confirmed and system data integrity is uncertain.
- Hospital-critical, controlled-substance, or cold-chain order flow is at risk.
- Backup systems cannot validate inventory, authorization, or traceability in real time.

Escalate to a higher recovery level when:

- Outage exceeds 4 hours.
- High-risk product exceptions exceed zero.
- Inventory snapshot age exceeds the approved maximum.
- Backlog age exceeds segment-specific thresholds.
- Reconciliation indicates material inventory or financial discrepancy.

Exit recovery mode only when:

- WMS and dependent systems are restored and validated.
- Manual data has been imported or reconciled.
- High-risk exception queues are closed or formally accepted with risk owner approval.
- Audit remediation SLAs are met or escalated.
- Finance confirms invoice close is supported by adequate evidence.

### Triage rules

Triage every incoming order by customer criticality and product risk.

| Tier | Customer/product combination | Recovery rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1** | Hospital-critical + controlled-substance, cold-chain, or recall-sensitive | Expedite, but require 100% minimum controls. If any required control is unavailable, hold and notify. Do not ship without authorization, temperature/security evidence, dual-control, and dispatch scan or equivalent manifest. |
| **Tier 2** | Hospital-critical + ambient | Fast lane allowed. Require minimum authorization, manual or system release check, and dispatch scan or equivalent manifest. |
| **Tier 3** | Pharmacy-standard + controlled-substance, cold-chain, or recall-sensitive | Priority after Tier 1/2. Require 100% high-risk controls. No deferral for product-risk reasons; defer only for documented capacity constraints with customer notice. |
| **Tier 4** | Pharmacy-standard + ambient | Standard manual flow. May be deferred if capacity is constrained, but only with deferral notice, recommit date, and fairness quota. |
| **Tier 5** | Wholesale-bulk + non-urgent | Defer or backlog by default during severe capacity constraints. Communicate expected processing date and offer alternatives where possible. |

Product overrides:

- **Controlled-substance:** always require authorization, dual-control, lot/serial capture, security seal, dispatch scan or equivalent manifest, and signed or equivalent POD.
- **Cold-chain:** always require temperature documentation, cold-chain equipment confirmation, release check, dispatch scan or equivalent manifest, and POD.
- **Recall-sensitive:** always require lot/expiry verification, recall status check, and enhanced traceability.
- **Ambient:** may use risk-based sampling and documented exceptions, but authorization and dispatch evidence must still be captured.

### Minimum controls by product/customer risk

**Authorization**

- Use real-time system authorization when available.
- If unavailable, use an offline customer/product authorization list with expiration dates.
- For high-risk items, require phone compliance signoff and dual approval.
- For low-risk ambient items, standing authorization may be used only as a documented exception with retroactive validation within 24 hours.
- If authorization cannot be evidenced, hold the order.

**Inventory**

- Use live system inventory when reliable.
- If unavailable, use timestamped inventory snapshots.
- Refresh snapshots at least every 2 hours or after material inventory movement.
- If snapshot age exceeds the approved maximum, hold high-risk items.
- For controlled, cold-chain, and recall-sensitive items, verify lot, expiry, location, and temperature zone before pick.

**Pick and pack**

- Use manual pick lists only with mandatory fields.
- Capture order ID, customer, product, lot/expiry, quantity, picker, packer, location, time, and risk-specific checks.
- For high-risk items, require dual verification or a second-person check.
- If scanning is unavailable, use a courier or pack manifest as the minimum traceability record.

**Quality/release**

- Do not omit quality/release check.
- Use temporary release control as a compensating control.
- Apply 100% manual release for controlled-substance, cold-chain, and recall-sensitive items.
- Apply risk-based sampling for ambient items.
- Release must record pharmacist identity, time, temperature check where applicable, and security check where applicable.

**Dispatch**

- Require scan or equivalent courier manifest.
- The manifest must include order ID, courier, driver, seal number, temperature logger ID where applicable, dispatch time, and destination.
- For controlled, cold-chain, and recall-sensitive items, do not dispatch without scan or equivalent manifest.
- For ambient items, dispatch without scan may be allowed only as a documented exception with post-scan within a short SLA.

**Proof of delivery**

- Use electronic POD when available.
- If unavailable, use structured phone POD with mandatory fields: recipient name, recipient role, order ID, delivery time, delivery location, and any exception.
- For controlled-substance and high-risk items, require signed POD or equivalent tamper-evident confirmation.
- Enter POD evidence within 2 hours for high-risk items and within the same business day for low-risk items.

**Invoice close**

- Do not close high-risk cases with open authorization, temperature, dual-control, scan, or POD exceptions.
- Low-risk cases may close with a documented exception only if the remediation SLA is met and the exception is visible on a dashboard.
- Finance should treat unresolved high-risk exceptions as open compliance liabilities, not ordinary receivables.

### Manual evidence capture

Use a standard manual evidence template for every order processed outside the normal system flow.

Mandatory fields:

- Case/order ID
- Customer name and customer class
- Product name, lot, expiry, quantity
- Customer authorization method and evidence
- Inventory source: system, snapshot timestamp, manual count
- Picker and pick time
- Packer and pack time
- Release check: pharmacist, time, result
- Temperature documentation for cold-chain
- Dual-control and security seal for controlled-substance
- Recall-sensitive lot/expiry verification
- Courier name, driver, seal, dispatch time
- Dispatch scan or manifest reference
- POD method, recipient, time, and reference
- Invoice status and exception flags

Evidence rules:

- Time-stamp every manual record.
- Version manual spreadsheets or paper forms.
- Require dual entry or dual verification for controlled-substance, cold-chain, and recall-sensitive items.
- Reconcile manual evidence to system records daily during recovery and within 24 hours after system restoration.

### Backlog prioritization

Use an explicit backlog scoring model rather than informal judgment.

Suggested scoring:

- Clinical criticality: 40 points
- Product risk: 30 points
- Customer SLA or contractual priority: 20 points
- Order age: 10 points

Additional rules:

- Hospital-critical patient-specific orders receive highest clinical priority.
- Controlled-substance, cold-chain, and recall-sensitive items receive product-risk priority.
- Pharmacy-standard and Wholesale-bulk orders must receive a protected capacity reserve, for example 10–20% of available processing capacity, to prevent unfair starvation.
- Deferrals must include a customer notification, expected recommit date, and escalation path.
- Cancellation requires approval and customer notice.
- Backlog age must be tracked by segment and product risk.

Aging thresholds:

- Hospital-critical backlog: p90 should not exceed baseline p90 plus 20% for more than one recovery cycle.
- Pharmacy-standard backlog: p90 should not exceed 2x baseline p90.
- Wholesale-bulk backlog: p90 should not exceed 3x baseline p90 or the agreed SLA, whichever is stricter.

### Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow

When systems return:

1. Freeze manual data and identify all orders processed in recovery mode.
2. Import or manually enter manual evidence into the system.
3. Reconcile inventory reservations, picks, packs, dispatches, PODs, and invoices.
4. Create exception queues by risk level.
5. Assign SLAs and owners.
6. Close exceptions only with evidence.
7. Perform root-cause analysis for repeated gaps.
8. Notify customers or regulators where required.
9. Complete post-incident review within 5 business days.

Exception queue SLAs:

| Exception type | Risk level | SLA |
|---|---|---|
| Missing controlled-substance authorization or dual-control | High | 24–48 hours |
| Missing cold-chain temperature documentation | High | 24–48 hours |
| Missing recall-sensitive lot/expiry or recall check | High | 24–48 hours |
| Missing dispatch scan or equivalent manifest for high-risk item | High | 24–48 hours |
| Missing POD for controlled or high-risk item | High | 24–48 hours |
| Missing ambient scan or POD | Medium | 72 hours |
| Missing authorization for low-risk ambient item | Medium | 72 hours |
| Documentation or formatting gaps | Low | 5 business days |

Audit remediation must be separate from operational close. A case may be operationally delivered but still compliance-open. Management dashboards must show both states.

### Process tree

```text
START
-> Incident trigger [WMS down > 30 min or high-risk impact]
-> Declare recovery mode [incident commander, compliance, operations, finance]
-> For each order:
   Receive order
   -> Emergency triage [customer criticality x product risk]
      -> Tier 1/2: expedite with minimum controls
      -> Tier 3/4: standard manual flow
      -> Tier 5: defer/backlog with customer notice
   -> Authorization control [system / offline whitelist / phone compliance / hold]
   -> Inventory control [system / timestamped snapshot / hold if stale or high-risk]
   -> Pick/pack with manual evidence [mandatory fields; dual check for high-risk]
   -> Release control [100% high-risk / sampled ambient]
   -> Dispatch control [scan or equivalent manifest / hold high-risk if missing]
   -> POD capture [electronic / structured phone / signed for high-risk]
   -> Invoice hold [close only if evidence complete or approved low-risk exception]
-> System recovery
   -> Reconcile manual reservations, inventory, orders, dispatch, POD, invoices
   -> Audit remediation queue [risk-based SLA]
   -> Post-incident review
-> END
```

| Manual evidence capture completion at dispatch | 100% for high-risk; at least 95% for ambient | Prevents traceability gaps from becoming audit backlog. |
| Exception queue size and aging | High-risk exceptions under SLA; no high-risk exception older than 48 hours | Makes hidden workload visible. |
| Backlog age p90 by segment | Hospital-critical within 1.2x baseline p90; Pharmacy-standard within 1.5x; Wholesale-bulk within 2x or agreed SLA | Detects service degradation before cancellations rise. |
| Deferral notification timeliness | 100% of deferrals notified within 2 hours | Protects customer trust and fairness. |
| Capacity allocation by segment | Predefined reserve for Pharmacy-standard and Wholesale-bulk | Prevents hospital-critical protection from becoming unfair starvation. |
| Staff readiness | Recovery roles trained and drilled within last 6 months | Reduces human error during manual workarounds. |

## 6. Recommendations for future disruptions

| Priority | Recommendation | Implementation effort | Expected benefit | Residual risk |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| **P0** | Create a pre-approved emergency operating model with trigger conditions, roles, exit criteria, and a risk matrix by customer and product. | Low–Medium | Reduces improvisation, speeds activation, and makes deviations intentional rather than accidental. | If not drilled, teams may still improvise under pressure. |
| **P0** | Implement hard stops for high-risk controls: no dispatch for controlled-substance, cold-chain, or recall-sensitive items without authorization, temperature/security evidence, dual-control, and dispatch scan or equivalent manifest. | Medium | Prevents the most serious safety and regulatory exposures. | May reduce throughput if backup evidence capture is not available. |
| **P0** | Standardize manual evidence capture with mandatory fields, time stamps, dual verification for high-risk items, and daily reconciliation. | Low–Medium | Reduces traceability reconstruction and audit backlog. | Data-entry errors and manual workload remain. |
| **P1** | Build an offline authorization whitelist with expiration dates, phone compliance signoff, and dual approval for high-risk orders. | Medium | Preserves authorization control when the system is down. | Lists can become stale if not maintained. |
| **P1** | Introduce backlog and deferral governance with explicit scoring, customer notification, recommit dates, cancellation approval, and fairness quotas. | Low–Medium | Reduces unfair customer harm and backlog aging. | Customer dissatisfaction may still occur during severe capacity constraints. |
| **P1** | Implement inventory snapshot refresh rules, variance thresholds, and automated reconciliation after system recovery. | Medium | Reduces stale inventory risk and reconciliation errors. | Integration complexity and data-quality issues may persist. |
| **P1** | Deploy backup scanning, electronic POD, courier manifests, and temperature logger download capability. | High | Restores traceability and proof-of-delivery evidence during outages. | Cost, maintenance, and integration complexity. |
| **P2** | Conduct regular recovery drills and after-action reviews using realistic outage scenarios. | Low–Medium | Improves team coordination and reduces human error. | Skill decay if drills are infrequent. |
| **P2** | Add financial close controls so invoice close is blocked or flagged when high-risk compliance exceptions remain open. | Low–Medium | Prevents operational close from masking noncompliance. | May delay revenue recognition if exceptions are not resolved quickly. |
| **P2** | Create a regulatory/customer notification playbook for compliance breaches, recall-sensitive items, controlled-substance gaps, and cold-chain failures. | Low–Medium | Reduces legal, regulatory, and customer-trust risk. | Notification decisions can be complex and time-sensitive. |

## 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should accept bounded, visible deviations that protect hospital-critical supply while creating compensating evidence: hospital-critical fast-lane triage, offline authorization for pre-approved low-risk ambient orders, deferral of non-urgent bulk orders with customer notice, and after-the-fact reconciliation for low-risk documentation gaps. Management should not accept uncontrolled authorization skips, dispatch without scan or equivalent manifest for controlled, cold-chain, or recall-sensitive items, missing temperature or dual-control evidence, informal backlog rules that sacrifice pharmacy and wholesale service, or closing cases while high-risk exceptions remain open. The recommendation would change if evidence showed patient-safety impact, hospital-critical fill below the pre-approved threshold, any unexplained high-risk control gap, traceability exceptions or audit-remediation aging beyond SLA, material inventory misstatement from stale snapshots, or fairness metrics showing unacceptable harm to pharmacy or wholesale customers. In that case, the appropriate response is immediate hold, customer or regulatory notification where required, and restoration of full controls before further throughput is prioritized.
