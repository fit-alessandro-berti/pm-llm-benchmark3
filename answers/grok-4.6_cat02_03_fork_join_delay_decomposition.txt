# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

All times are treated as complete-event timestamps. For case \(c\) and branch \(i\in\{A,B,C\}\):

- \(t^{\mathrm{arr}}(c)\): process arrival
- \(t^{\mathrm{fork}}(c)\): fork release (earliest legal branch start)
- \(t^{\mathrm{start}}(c,i),\ t^{\mathrm{end}}(c,i)\): branch \(i\) service interval
- \(t^{F}_{\mathrm{start}}(c),\ t^{F}_{\mathrm{end}}(c)\): final-station interval

**Case-level elapsed components (no overlap in calendar time):**

\[
\begin{align}
U(c) &= t^{\mathrm{fork}}(c)-t^{\mathrm{arr}}(c)
&&\text{upstream elapsed before fork release}\\
Q(c,i) &= t^{\mathrm{start}}(c,i)-t^{\mathrm{fork}}(c)
&&\text{branch-specific queue after the fork}\\
S(c,i) &= t^{\mathrm{end}}(c,i)-t^{\mathrm{start}}(c,i)
&&\text{branch active service}\\
J(c) &= \max_{i} t^{\mathrm{end}}(c,i)
&&\text{join-ready (latest branch completion)}\\
W_{\mathrm{sync}}(c,i) &= J(c)-t^{\mathrm{end}}(c,i)
&&\text{branch slack after completion}\\
T_{\mathrm{FJ}}(c) &= J(c)-t^{\mathrm{fork}}(c)
&&\text{elapsed fork–join stage}\\
D(c) &= t^{F}_{\mathrm{start}}(c)-J(c)
&&\text{downstream wait before }F\\
S_F(c) &= t^{F}_{\mathrm{end}}(c)-t^{F}_{\mathrm{start}}(c)
&&\text{final service}\\
T(c) &= t^{F}_{\mathrm{end}}(c)-t^{\mathrm{arr}}(c)
&&\text{total case flow time}
\end{align}
\]

**Critical branch.** \(i^*(c)=\arg\max_i t^{\mathrm{end}}(c,i)\). If several branches share the same \(J(c)\), the critical *set* is retained and a single label is assigned by alphabetical order \(A\prec B\prec C\) (deterministic; unused on the base log).

**Identities that prevent double counting.**

\[
T_{\mathrm{FJ}}(c)=\max_i\bigl(Q(c,i)+S(c,i)\bigr)=Q(c,i^*)+S(c,i^*).
\]

For *every* branch, \(Q(c,i)+S(c,i)+W_{\mathrm{sync}}(c,i)=T_{\mathrm{FJ}}(c)\). Therefore

\[
T(c)=U(c)+T_{\mathrm{FJ}}(c)+D(c)+S_F(c)=U(c)+Q(c,i^*)+S(c,i^*)+D(c)+S_F(c).
\]

Branch queues and services are parallel, not sequential. They are reported as diagnostics; only the maximizing path is an addend of elapsed time.

**Why summing synchronization waits double-counts.** \(W_{\mathrm{sync}}(c,i)\) is slack *inside* the same calendar interval \([\min_i t^{\mathrm{end}}(c,i),\,J(c)]\). Those waits run concurrently. The elapsed synchronization span is \(\max_i W_{\mathrm{sync}}(c,i)\), not the sum. Summing gives

\[
\sum_i W_{\mathrm{sync}}(c,i)=3\,T_{\mathrm{FJ}}(c)-\sum_i\bigl(Q(c,i)+S(c,i)\bigr),
\]

which is not a duration the case experiences. Adding \(\sum_i W_{\mathrm{sync}}\) (or \(\sum_i(Q+S)\)) to \(U+D+S_F\) inflates \(T(c)\). Example F02: slacks are \(10+0+35=45\), but the join only stays open for \(35\) minutes after the first branch finishes; \(A\) and \(C\) wait at the same time. The elapsed decomposition is \(20+60+15+20=115\), not \(20+(0+20+5)+(50+40+20)+45+15+20=235\).

# 3. Aggregate critical-path analysis

**Assumption A1.** Branch work does not start before \(t^{\mathrm{fork}}\) (supported: every start is \(\ge\) fork; \(A\) always starts *at* fork). **Assumption A2.** Service intervals are uninterrupted (cannot test preemption from complete-event data). **Hypothesis H1 (used only for influence of \(F\), not for the table above).** Odd cases are dedicated to F1 and even cases to F2: the assignment is exclusive in the log, F08 starts before F07, and several \(D(c)\) values equal the wait for the previous same-resource completion.

| branch | mean / med / min–max service | mean / med / min–max queue | mean / max sojourn \(Q+S\) | times last (\(n=12\)) | mean / med sync wait | share of mean \(T_{\mathrm{FJ}}\) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A Assay | 50.0 / 50 / 50–50 | 0.0 / 0 / 0–0 | 50.0 / 50 | 2 (F01, F11) | 11.0 / 10 | \(100/12=8.33\) min (\(13.7\%\)) |
| B Stress | 37.5 / 35 / 30–45 | 19.17 / 20 / 5–30 | 56.67 / 75 | 8 | 4.33 / 0 | \(520/12=43.33\) min (\(71.0\%\)) |
| C Docs | 20.17 / 20 / 18–30 | 10.42 / 5 / 5–35 | 30.58 / 57 | 2 (F04, F08) | 30.42 / 32.5 | \(112/12=9.33\) min (\(15.3\%\)) |

Mean \(T_{\mathrm{FJ}}=61\). Attribution is \(\frac{1}{12}\sum_c(Q+S)_{i^*(c)}\) and sums to \(61\). Mean flow time is \(115.25\) minutes (\(U\) mean \(19.58\), \(D\) mean \(14.67\), \(S_F=20\)).

When last, mean sojourn is \(A=50\), \(B=65\), \(C=56\). Both tail fork–joins (\(75\) on F07 and F12) are \(B\). \(C\)’s queue is right-skewed: eight cases wait only \(5\) minutes, but F04 (\(35\)) and F08 (\(25\)) are enough to put \(C\) on the critical path. \(A\) is a flat \(50\)-minute wall with no queue.

**Influence ranking (not a service-time ranking).**

1. **B, clearly first.** Highest last-completion frequency (\(8/12\)), highest mean and tail sojourn, near-zero slack, and \(71\%\) of mean fork–join elapsed. Mean *service* is shorter than \(A\); influence comes from queue plus service.
2. **A and C are a near-tie for second, for different reasons.** \(C\) contributes slightly more to mean \(J(c)\) (\(9.33\) vs \(8.33\)) because its two critical sojourns exceed \(50\). \(A\) is never the tail but is the deterministic floor: any serious cut to \(B\) collides with \(A\) (Section 4). Ranking \(A\) above \(C\) because “\(50>20\)” would be the error the prompt warns against.

Downstream congestion is a separate, large addend (mean \(D=14.67\), comparable to a typical \(B\) queue). It does not choose the critical *branch*, but it can hide branch improvements on the F1 afternoon chain (F09–F11) and on several even-numbered F2 cases.

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

**Assumption A3.** Other branches’ start/end times do not move when one lever is changed (no cross-branch resource sharing, no behavioral response). **Assumption A4 (pass-through).** \(D(c)\) and \(S_F(c)\) stay at observed values, so \(\Delta T(c)=\Delta J(c)\). This is an *upper bound* if \(F\) can start earlier one-for-one. **Assumption A5 (resource floor).** F1 serves odd cases in order and F2 even cases; \(t^{F}_{\mathrm{start}}{}^{'}=\max\bigl(J'+D_{\mathrm{orig}},\ t^{F}_{\mathrm{end,prev}}{}^{'}\bigr)\). Residual wait after both join-ready and the previous same-resource completion is treated as policy/setup and is kept unless the intervention targets \(F\). Half-minute figures appear because \(50\%\) of a \(5\)- or \(25\)-minute \(B\) queue is \(2.5\); the source clock is one-minute.

### CF1 — cut \(A\) service by \(20\%\) (\(50\to 40\) on every case)

| case | \(\Delta J\) | new critical | pass-through \(\Delta T\) | notes |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| F01 | 5 | B | 5 | Only \(5\) of the \(10\)-minute cut reaches \(J\); \(B\) ends at 08:55 |
| F11 | 10 | A+B (tie \(\to\) A) | 10 | Pass-through; **fully masked** under A5 because F09 still occupies F1 until 11:50 |
| other 10 | 0 | unchanged | 0 | \(A\) was not last; \(B\) or \(C\) still sets \(J\) |

- **Direct benefit (A4):** \(15\) minutes across \(12\) cases, mean \(1.25\). Two cases only.
- **Zero-benefit cases:** ten cases, because another branch remains last.
- **Secondary effects.** \(A\) already has zero queue, so faster assays do not unlock \(A\)-queueing; they create \(A\) idle. F01’s earlier F1 finish (about five minutes) does not knock on to F03 (F03 still starts at 10:00). Under A5, F11’s ten-minute join gain is absorbed by F1 congestion — a concrete case of the final station masking a branch improvement. Investing in a \(20\%\) assay cut is a weak flow-time lever on this log.

### CF2 — cut every \(B\) queue by \(50\%\)

New \(B\) end \(=t^{\mathrm{end}}_B-0.5\,Q_B\). New \(J=\max(t^{\mathrm{end}}_A,\,t^{\mathrm{end}}_B{}^{'},\,t^{\mathrm{end}}_C)\).

| case | \(0.5Q_B\) | \(\Delta J\) | new critical | zero / partial / full |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| F01 | 2.5 | 0 | A | zero — \(A\) still last |
| F02 | 10 | 10 | A+B | full vs \(B\) queue; hits the \(A\) floor |
| F03 | 10 | 10 | B | full |
| F04 | 5 | 0 | C | zero — \(C\) still last |
| F05 | 12.5 | 10 | A | partial — \(A\) becomes last after \(10\) of \(12.5\) |
| F06 | 15 | 15 | A+B | full vs \(B\) queue; hits the \(A\) floor |
| F07 | 15 | 15 | B | full (tail case) |
| F08 | 2.5 | 0 | C | zero — \(C\) still last |
| F09 | 12.5 | 12.5 | B | full |
| F10 | 10 | 5 | A | partial — \(A\) becomes last |
| F11 | 5 | 0 | A | zero — \(A\) still last |
| F12 | 15 | 15 | B | full (tail case) |

- **Direct benefit (A4):** \(92.5\) minutes total, mean \(7.71\) (\(\approx 6.7\%\) of mean \(T\)).
- **Zero-benefit cases:** F01, F04, F08, F11 (\(4/12\)). Speeding a non-critical branch does nothing to that case’s join.
- **Partial-benefit cases:** F05, F10 (and F02/F06, which land exactly on \(A\)’s \(50\)-minute sojourn). Further \(B\) acceleration beyond those points has zero extra case-level effect.
- **Resource floor (A5).** Holding previous \(F\) completions fixed, F05 and F09 are masked by an occupied F1 (\(70\) minutes total instead of \(92.5\)). If earlier cases on the same resource also move, knock-on unmasks those two and the pass-through total is recovered. Secondary effect: earlier \(B\) completions move \(J\) toward \(A\)’s flat \(50\)-minute ceiling; \(C\)’s already-large sync wait grows where \(B\) was last; F04/F08 remain \(C\)-limited. Downstream arrivals cluster earlier and can either ease a dedicated F chain (knock-on) or, in a busier regime than this sample, worsen \(F\) contention.

### CF3 — reduce downstream queueing (capacity / dispatch)

Two nested interventions.

**CF3-full.** \(D(c)\to 0\), \(F\) starts at \(J(c)\). Per-case savings equal observed \(D\): \(8,15,15,18,10,15,15,15,15,20,20,10\). Total \(176\) minutes, mean \(14.67\), **every case benefits**, no branch remains “in the way.” Mean \(T\) falls by \(12.7\%\). Branches and \(J(c)\) are unchanged.

**CF3-pool.** Hypothesis H1 plus a shared two-server FIFO by join-ready, start as soon as a server is free, service still \(20\). On *this* sample the join-ready gaps never put three cases in service at once, so both servers stay sufficient and CF3-pool reproduces CF3-full. A third server is unnecessary here if assignment and the residual idle-while-ready delay are removed.

**CF3-policy-only.** Keep dedicated F1/F2, set start to \(\max(J(c),\) previous same-resource end\()\). Savings total \(171\); only F11 keeps a \(5\)-minute dedicated-resource wait (F09 would finish at 11:35 vs \(J=11:30\)). Almost all observed \(D\) is residual delay while a named resource is already free, not two-server saturation.

- **Direct benefit.** Largest mean-flow lever of the three required counterfactuals, but it does not shorten the fork–join stage or the \(75\)-minute \(B\) tails.
- **Zero-benefit cases.** None for CF3-full. Branch-criticality is irrelevant because the lever sits after the join.
- **Secondary effects.** \(F\) utilization rises because idle-while-ready minutes disappear; quality/setup risk if that residual was a review buffer rather than a queue. Branch improvements that A5 would have masked (F11 in CF1) become visible. If volume grows, pooling without a third server can create a true \(F\) queue that this day does not show.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

### Redesign 1 — attack \(B\) queueing (capacity, sequencing, or admission), not \(B\) service first

Pilot the causes of the \(5\)–\(30\) minute \(B\) waits, especially the \(30\)-minute waits on F06, F07, and F12, before buying a faster test protocol. Count how many stress tests run concurrently (capacity is not identifiable from these timestamps). A half-cut in \(B\) queue is the only *branch* lever that moves both the mean join and the flow-time tail (F07 \(130\to 115\), F12 \(125\to 110\) under A4).

- **Throughput.** Twelve cases in one morning; \(B\) is not shown to be the system’s arrival bottleneck. The gain is flow time, not more completions, unless released capacity is used for volume.
- **Tail.** Best targeted tail intervention among the three counterfactuals.
- **Utilization.** Unknown \(B\) staffing. If queues are utilization-driven, a \(50\%\) queue cut may need a large capacity increment and will lower utilization of existing testers.
- **New bottleneck.** \(A\)’s constant \(50\)-minute service becomes the fork–join floor (already binds or ties in CF2 on F02, F05, F06, F10). F04 and F08 stay \(C\)-critical until documentation queue spikes are handled.
- **Synchronization.** \(C\) will wait even longer after finishing; that is slack, not extra elapsed time.
- **Measure first.** Concurrent \(B\) in service, reason codes for \(B\) start lag after fork, and whether F04/F08 \(C\) delays share a staff roster with \(B\).

### Redesign 2 — reform Release Decision dispatch (pool F1/F2, start when join-ready), do not add F3 first

The log does not show two saturated final servers. It shows dedicated assignment plus a repeated \(\approx 15\)-minute residual after both join-ready and, often, resource free. Removing that residual (CF3) saves more mean flow time than CF1 or CF2, with a two-server pool already enough on this day.

- **Throughput.** Busy time at \(F\) stays \(20\times 12=240\) minutes. Throughput is unchanged unless residual idle was blocking later arrivals that are not in this extract.
- **Tail.** Helps F07, F10, and F11 by \(15\)–\(20\) minutes but leaves the \(75\)-minute fork–joins intact. Pair with Redesign 1 if the tail is the KPI.
- **Utilization.** Apparent F1/F2 idleness is partly policy. Pooling raises responsiveness without a third badge.
- **New bottleneck.** Earlier \(F\) starts do not queue two pooled servers here; they could if arrivals grow or if residual delay was actually unlogged setup that still consumes a server.
- **Synchronization.** Unchanged. Unmasking: F11-style \(A\) gains would finally reach the customer.
- **Measure first.** For every case, why \(t^{F}_{\mathrm{start}}>J(c)\) when at least one of F1/F2 is idle — setup, batch release, dedicated assignment, or hidden work. That measurement decides whether the residual is a queue, a policy, or service that was mis-labeled as wait.

**Risks common to both.** \(n=12\), one day, no A/B/C resource identifiers, one-minute clocks, and no outcome/quality fields. A one-week pilot with resource-level timestamps should precede any capacity purchase. Do not spend on faster \(A\) service or a third \(F\) server on the strength of these twelve rows.

# 6. Reproducible Python

```python
"""Fork-join delay decomposition for the 2026-05-19 assay / stress / docs log."""

from datetime import datetime

import pandas as pd
import pm4py

DAY = "2026-05-19"
FMT = "%Y-%m-%d %H:%M"

# 1. Construct the supplied table in memory
raw = [
    # case, arrival, fork, A_s, A_e, B_s, B_e, C_s, C_e, F_s, F_e, F_res
    ("F01", "07:55", "08:10", "08:10", "09:00", "08:15", "08:55", "08:20", "08:38", "09:08", "09:28", "F1"),
    ("F02", "08:05", "08:25", "08:25", "09:15", "08:45", "09:25", "08:30", "08:50", "09:40", "10:00", "F2"),
    ("F03", "08:20", "08:40", "08:40", "09:30", "09:00", "09:45", "08:45", "09:03", "10:00", "10:20", "F1"),
    ("F04", "08:35", "08:55", "08:55", "09:45", "09:05", "09:35", "09:30", "09:52", "10:10", "10:30", "F2"),
    ("F05", "08:50", "09:10", "09:10", "10:00", "09:35", "10:10", "09:15", "09:35", "10:20", "10:40", "F1"),
    ("F06", "09:05", "09:25", "09:25", "10:15", "09:55", "10:30", "09:40", "10:00", "10:45", "11:05", "F2"),
    ("F07", "09:20", "09:40", "09:40", "10:30", "10:10", "10:55", "09:45", "10:03", "11:10", "11:30", "F1"),
    ("F08", "09:35", "09:55", "09:55", "10:45", "10:00", "10:30", "10:20", "10:50", "11:05", "11:25", "F2"),
    ("F09", "09:50", "10:10", "10:10", "11:00", "10:35", "11:15", "10:15", "10:35", "11:30", "11:50", "F1"),
    ("F10", "10:05", "10:25", "10:25", "11:15", "10:45", "11:20", "10:30", "10:50", "11:40", "12:00", "F2"),
    ("F11", "10:20", "10:40", "10:40", "11:30", "10:50", "11:20", "10:45", "11:03", "11:50", "12:10", "F1"),
    ("F12", "10:35", "10:55", "10:55", "11:45", "11:25", "12:10", "11:00", "11:18", "12:20", "12:40", "F2"),
]
COLS = ["case", "arrival", "fork_release", "A_start", "A_end", "B_start", "B_end",
        "C_start", "C_end", "final_start", "final_end", "final_resource"]
df = pd.DataFrame(raw, columns=COLS)
time_cols = [c for c in COLS if c not in ("case", "final_resource")]
for c in time_cols:
    df[c] = pd.to_datetime(DAY + " " + df[c], format=FMT)

# 2. Decomposition (minutes). Sync waits are diagnostics, not addends of T.
def minutes(td):
    return td.total_seconds() / 60.0

BRANCHES = ("A", "B", "C")

df["upstream_elapsed"] = minutes(df["fork_release"] - df["arrival"])
for i in BRANCHES:
    df[f"{i}_queue"] = minutes(df[f"{i}_start"] - df["fork_release"])
    df[f"{i}_service"] = minutes(df[f"{i}_end"] - df[f"{i}_start"])
    df[f"{i}_sojourn"] = df[f"{i}_queue"] + df[f"{i}_service"]

# 3. join_ready = max branch end; ties: record the full set, label by A < B < C
end_mat = df[[f"{i}_end" for i in BRANCHES]]
df["join_ready"] = end_mat.max(axis=1)
for i in BRANCHES:
    df[f"{i}_sync"] = minutes(df["join_ready"] - df[f"{i}_end"])

def critical_set(row):
    j = row["join_ready"]
    return [i for i in BRANCHES if row[f"{i}_end"] == j]

df["critical_set"] = df.apply(lambda r: "+".join(critical_set(r)), axis=1)
df["critical_branch"] = df["critical_set"].str.split("+").str[0]  # A ≺ B ≺ C
df["fork_join"] = minutes(df["join_ready"] - df["fork_release"])
df["downstream_queue"] = minutes(df["final_start"] - df["join_ready"])
df["final_service"] = minutes(df["final_end"] - df["final_start"])
df["total_flow_time"] = minutes(df["final_end"] - df["arrival"])

# Identity check (no double counting): T = U + max(Q+S) + D + S_F
recon = df["upstream_elapsed"] + df["fork_join"] + df["downstream_queue"] + df["final_service"]
assert (recon - df["total_flow_time"]).abs().max() < 1e-9

print("=== Case-level decomposition (minutes) ===")
show = ["case", "upstream_elapsed", "A_queue", "A_service", "B_queue", "B_service",
        "C_queue", "C_service", "join_ready", "critical_branch", "critical_set",
        "fork_join", "downstream_queue", "final_service", "total_flow_time"]
out = df[show].copy()
out["join_ready"] = out["join_ready"].dt.strftime("%H:%M")
print(out.to_string(index=False))
```

# 7. Limits of inference

These timestamps identify intervals and the max-based join. They do not identify mechanisms.

- **Causality.** That \(B\) is usually last does not prove that shortening \(B\) *causes* shorter flow outside Assumption A3. Shared staff, batching, or a hidden rule that delays \(B\) until \(A\) is nearly done would make the counterfactuals fail. Nothing in the log rules those in or out.
- **Branch resource capacity.** \(A\) starts every \(15\) minutes and runs \(50\) minutes, so several assays overlap; at least four \(A\) servers are possible, or one server with unlogged parallelism. \(B\) and \(C\) also overlap (F07/F08 on \(B\); F04/F05 on \(C\)). Server counts, idle time, and whether queues are capacity queues versus batching or priority are not identifiable. Redesign 1 therefore starts with a resource census, not a purchase order.
- **Preemption and multi-tasking.** Complete-event data give one start and one end. A \(45\)-minute \(B\) interval could be \(45\) minutes of work or \(30\) minutes of work with a \(15\)-minute interruption. \(Q\) and \(S\) inherit that ambiguity. The constant \(A=50\) and \(F=20\) look like protocols; \(B\) and \(C\) look more variable, which is still not proof of preemption.
- **Whether \(D(c)\) is a queue.** By definition \(D=t^{F}_{\mathrm{start}}-J\ge 0\) here, so the *arithmetic* of a downstream wait is identified. The *substance* is not. Candidates that fit the same stamps: (i) a true F1/F2 queue under dedicated assignment; (ii) a release policy or cadence (many residuals are \(15\) minutes while a named resource is already free); (iii) unlogged setup charged to wait; (iv) a hold for an unobserved approval. F08 starting before F07 already rejects a single FIFO combined queue. Adding a third final resource is not justified until idle-while-ready is explained.
- **Upstream.** \(U\) is almost always \(20\) minutes. That could be a fixed preparation step or a paced release every \(15\) minutes after a \(20\)-minute offset. The extract starts after that step; its internals are out of scope.
- **Generalization.** One day, twelve cases, no quality or rework events, clocks to one minute. Ties never occur in the base data; the alphabetical rule is a convention, not an empirical finding. Influence rankings and the \(92.5\) / \(176\) minute savings are descriptive of this sample under the stated assumptions, not population estimates.
