## 1. Feasibility model

```text
# Partial-order relation set (P)
P = {
    (Register, Triage),
    (Triage, Identity Check),
    (Identity Check, Document Check) WHERE no originals,
    (Identity Check, Document Check) WHERE originals,
    (Originals Receipt, Document Check) WHERE originals,
    (Document Check, Assessment),
    (Identity Check, Assessment) WHERE no originals,
    (Assessment, Request More Info) [optional],
    (Request More Info, Document Check) [follow-up],
    (Request More Info, Assessment) [follow-up],
    (Assessment, Compliance Review) WHERE highrisk/highvalue,
    (Compliance Review, Decision),
    (Decision, Notify)
}
```

```text
# Conditional and declarative constraints
C = {
    "order_rule_1": Register must precede Triage,
    "order_rule_2": Identity Check + Document Check  Assessment,
    "concurrency_rule_1": Identity Check may run before Document Check when no originals,
    "preference_rule_1": Originals Receipt precedes Document Check when originals required,
    "risk_rule": highrisk/highvalue  Compliance Review after latest Assessment,
    "four_eyes_rule": final Assessment and final Compliance Review must be by different persons,
    "resource_unicity": a resource cannot be in two intervals simultaneously,
    "queue_estimate_uncertainty": queue estimates are probabilistic
}
```

**Explanation of intervention impacts**  
- **Case K104** (preescalation & provisional compliance slot): *alter routing and timing* – introduces an early escalation path and reserves C1 capacity, changing sequencing but not the decision rule.  
- **Case K219** (assign D2 immediately): *alter routing* – switches documentcheck resource; timing may increase due to D2’s slower pace and risk of rework.  
- **Case K337** (suppress second rework loop): *alter decision policy* – forbids additional RequestMoreInfo, directly changing how evidence is used and the possible decision outcome.  
- **Case K411** (parallelize verification with workaround): *alter routing & timing* – creates concurrent Identity and Document checks, introducing a workaround that affects queue ordering and timestamp logging.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104
- **Factual mechanism:** Triage waited to notice sanctions ambiguity; assessment later discovered it, leading to escalation and compliance.
- **Minimal intervention:** At Triage, forward a preliminary escalation packet to S1 **and** lock a provisional slot for C1.
- **Temporal propagation:** Preliminary compliance work can start while Identity and Document checks proceed, shaving 2–4h from the timeline. If S1’s advice is comprehensive, the need for a full reassessment is reduced.
- **Processfeasibility check:** All prerequisite orders (Register  Triage  Identity  Document  Assessment) remain satisfied; the provisional C1 slot respects the foureyes rule (C1 is separate from A1). Queue estimates still hold; only C1’s capacity is prereserved.
- **Best/base/worst case:**  
  - **Best:** C1’s provisional slot is freed after the preliminary work, yielding a 6–8h overall reduction and no quality loss.  
  - **Base:** C1 remains occupied for the full compliance review, netting a 4–6h reduction but keeping the original decision unchanged.  
  - **Worst:** Other urgent highrisk cases also need C1, causing a 2–4h delay to those cases, potentially increasing their cycle times.
- **Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If the preliminary compliance work uncovered additional issues requiring a full reassessment, the net time gain could vanish, or even increase.

### K-219
- **Factual mechanism:** Originals arrived late; D1 processed the handwritten documents, leading to a clean assessment.
- **Minimal intervention:** Assign D2 to perform Document Check immediately upon originals receipt, bypassing D1.
- **Temporal propagation:** D2’s faster processing of handwritten material could start at 17:30 instead of waiting for D1’s queue, shaving ~2h. However, D2’s slower evaluation might trigger a Request More Info, adding 4–8h if rework occurs.
- **Processfeasibility check:** Identity Check precedes Document Check, satisfying order rules. Resource unicity holds (D2 works while I2 completes). Compliance and Decision steps unchanged.
- **Best/base/worst case:**  
  - **Best:** D2 completes without issues  overall reduction of ~1h.  
  - **Base:** D2 flags unclear handwriting  one Request More Info loop adds 4–6h, netting negligible gain.  
  - **Worst:** D2 repeatedly requests clarification  adds 6–10h, extending cycle time beyond original.
- **Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If D2’s review quality is inferior, the decision could be incorrect, leading to downstream problems that negate any time savings.

### K-337
- **Factual mechanism:** External supplier delayed; two rework loops (Document Check2 & 3) were required before a compliant assessment.
- **Minimal intervention:** After Assessment2, **prohibit** the second Request More Info loop; force a decision using the existing evidence, possibly with conditions or rejection.
- **Temporal propagation:** Elimination of the third Document Check and subsequent Assessment saves ~12–24h (the time of Request More Info2, Document Check3, and Assessment3). The decision point shifts earlier.
- **Processfeasibility check:** The foureyes rule is unaffected (Assessment2 and Compliance Review remain separate). The decision policy is altered, but compliance still proceeds after the final Assessment (now Assessment2). Queue displacements occur only in the Assessment and Document queues, where fewer tasks are queued later.
- **Best/base/worst case:**  
  - **Best:** Evidence from Assessment2 is deemed sufficient  overall cycletime reduction of ~18h, decision unchanged (approve).  
  - **Base:** Evidence borderline  decision changes to “approve with stricter conditions”  slight quality impact but still net time gain of ~15h.  
  - **Worst:** Evidence insufficient  decision switches to reject  compliance review still runs, but the process ends earlier, yielding a ~12h reduction but potential loss exposure.
- **Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If the omitted Request More Info uncovered a critical error, the decision would have been wrong under the original process, so the shortcut could be deemed unacceptable.

### K-411
- **Factual mechanism:** Legacy system created only Identity task; Document task created later, causing a delay.
- **Minimal intervention:** At Triage, create both Identity and Document tasks **concurrently** using a controlled workaround; implement realtime timestamp logging.
- **Temporal propagation:** Identity (I2) and Document (D2) run in parallel, potentially finishing by ~12:30 instead of ~15:00, shaving ~2–5h. The Assessment queue may receive the case earlier, possibly increasing its workload.
- **Processfeasibility check:** Order rule satisfied (Register  Triage  both tasks can proceed in parallel). Resource unicity holds (I2 and D2 are distinct). Foureyes rule unchanged (Assessment and Decision unaffected). Queue estimates updated to reflect earlier arrival in Assessment.
- **Best/base/worst case:**  
  - **Best:** Both checks finish early; Assessment receives case promptly, cycletime reduced by ~4h, no quality issues.  
  - **Base:** Parallel execution works but timestamp workaround adds a minor overhead of ~30min, netting ~3h reduction.  
  - **Worst:** Concurrent execution overloads Assessment queue, causing downstream cases to wait ~1–2h, offsetting time gains.
- **Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If the realtime logging fails, the parallel execution could produce incorrect timestamps, leading to audit problems that outweigh the time savings.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected nontarget cases | sign of systemwide effect | key nonlinearity |
|--------------|-----------------------------|------------------------|----------------------|---------------------------|----------------------------|-------------------|
| K104 (preescalation & provisional C1) | C1: one extra slot booked early; no extra processing time added | Compliance queue (by reducing later load) | Identity/Document queues (no change) | Highrisk cases needing C1 later may experience delay if the provisional slot is not released | Negative – early reservation consumes capacity | Capacity reservation can cause a **threshold** effect: once many cases reserve C1, the system becomes starved for compliance resources. |
| K219 (assign D2 immediately) | D2: used earlier, possibly adding load; D1 freed later | Document queue (D2 starts earlier) | Identity queue (unchanged) | Cases that later need D1 may face longer wait if D2 is slower | Mixed – time saved locally vs. downstream delay | Nonlinear slowdown when D2’s slower processing triggers **rework loops**, amplifying queue length beyond linear expectations. |
| K337 (suppress second rework loop) | Overall processing time reduced; compliance still runs once | Assessment & Document queues (fewer followup tasks) | None directly, but earlier decision may push more cases into Assessment | Highrisk cases that still need full rework may see increased backlog if many adopt the shortcut | Positive – net time saved, slight risk of quality downgrade | The **policy shift** from “collect more evidence” to “use less” creates a **qualitative** change in decision risk, not purely quantitative. |
| K411 (parallel verification) | I2 & D2 used concurrently; no extra capacity needed beyond existing pool | Identity & Document queues (tasks finish earlier) | Assessment queue (cases arrive earlier) | Other cases entering Assessment later may experience **serialization** if queue fills | Positive – early arrival helps overall throughput, but may **migrate** bottleneck to Assessment | The **interaction effect** of reduced upstream time and increased downstream arrival can create a **feedback loop** where earlier arrivals further compress downstream schedules. |

### 100case weekly rollout analysis

Applying the four interventions to 100 similar cases in one week would significantly reshape the system dynamics.

1. **K104** reservation policy:  
   - Provisional C1 slots would be booked for 100 highrisk/highvalue cases.  
   - Compliance capacity (only C1) would be fully occupied early, leaving no room for unexpected urgent cases (e.g., sudden regulatory alerts).  
   - Queue displacement: other cases would be forced to wait for C1, potentially increasing their cycle times by 4–8h on average, leading to a **systemwide delay**.

2. **K219** D2 assignment:  
   - Early use of D2 would reduce D1’s queue but increase D2’s utilization.  
   - Because D2 is slower and more prone to requesting clarification, a nonlinear rise in rework rates is expected for ~30–40% of the 100 cases.  
   - Rework loops would expand the Document queue and subsequently delay Assessment, **migrating the bottleneck** from Document to Assessment.

3. **K337** suppression of rework loops:  
   - Cutting two rework cycles per case saves ~15h per case on average, yielding a ~1,500hour weekly gain.  
   - However, the policy changes the decision rule: many cases may be rejected or approved with stricter conditions, increasing **appeal risk** and potentially affecting compliance defensibility.  
   - The overall throughput rises, but the **risk exposure** shifts to higher appeal volumes, which could overwhelm the compliance review team later in the week.

4. **K411** parallel verification:  
   - Concurrent Identity and Document checks would finish ~3–4h earlier per case, freeing up Assessment slots.  
   - With 100 cases arriving earlier in Assessment, the queue may become saturated if Assessment resources (A1, A2) are limited.  
   - The **migration of bottleneck** from Document to Assessment is linear at first but can become **nonlinear** when Assessment queue length exceeds capacity, causing spillover delays that ripple back to Triage.

**Systemwide effect:**  
- Early interventions improve local cycle times but create **interference** by consuming scarce resources (C1, D2, Assessment).  
- The most pronounced nonlinearity appears when concurrent upstream acceleration leads to downstream overload (e.g., K411 and K219).  
- External factors such as weekend supplier responses (K337) and legacy system constraints (K411) further modulate the net gain.  
- Monitoring queue forecasts in real time becomes essential to prevent cascading delays.

## 7. Final synthesis

The **most robust intervention** is the suppression of the second rework loop (intervention **K337**). It delivers a substantial cycletime reduction (12–24h per case) with a clear, bounded decisionpolicy change, and its effects are largely insulated from fluctuations in resource speed or external delivery times. The primary risk is an increase in appeal rates and a modest drop in compliance defensibility, both of which can be monitored and mitigated through pilot data and conditional approval language.

Conversely, the **most dangerous intervention despite potential speed gains** is assigning the Document Check to D2 immediately (intervention **K219**). While it may shave a small amount of time, the slower processing and higher likelihood of requesting clarification introduce nonlinear rework loops that can dramatically lengthen the path and jeopardize decision accuracy. The risk of downstream backlog and quality erosion makes this the least suitable for broad rollout.

The **counterfactual that is least transportable to a systemwide policy** is the parallelization of verification in K411. Its benefits hinge on a specific legacysystem constraint (only Identity initially created) and the successful execution of a realtime timestamp workaround. If the underlying system architecture changes, the parallel approach may introduce false concurrency errors, and the queuemigration effect could become severe. Therefore, while attractive for isolated cases, it lacks generality for a largescale deployment.

**Most valuable additional data** would include:
1. **Realtime queue utilization metrics** for Identity, Document, and Assessment resources during the intervention windows, to capture spillover effects accurately.
2. **External response latency logs** for the supplierrelated cases (K337) to better forecast when rework loops become inevitable.
3. **Timestamp fidelity audits** for the parallel verification pilot (K411) to detect any synchronization issues.
4. **Appeal and decisionquality logs** across all four interventions to quantify the tradeoff between speed and compliance risk.
5. **Resource utilization heatmaps** showing how provisional slots (K104) and D2 assignments (K219) affect the capacity of other pools.

Collecting these data points would enable a more nuanced assessment of nonlinear interactions and support the selection of the most adaptable, highimpact policy.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **Structural:** The process model’s partial order (Register  Triage  …) correctly captures all mandatory precedence constraints.  
2. **Temporal:** Queue estimates (e.g., I1: 2h, D1: 13h) are accurate enough for highlevel counterfactual reasoning; they are not exact guarantees.  
3. **Resource:** Each resource pool has sufficient capacity to handle the concurrent demands introduced by interventions, aside from the singledout scarce pool (C1).  
4. **Behavioral:** Resources will not deliberately bypass the prescribed order to “save time” beyond the allowed concurrency rules.  
5. **Quality:** The quality of a Document Check by D2 is comparable to D1 when no rework occurs, but degrades if clarification is needed.  
6. **Interference:** Early escalation or parallel tasks do not create hidden dependencies that violate the foureyes rule.  
7. **Temporal:** External supplier response times for K337 are stochastic but follow the 8–36h range provided.  
8. **Interference:** Reservation of a provisional C1 slot does not affect concurrent processing of other identical slots due to the singleresource nature of C1.  

*Assumptions are labeled according to their nature.*
