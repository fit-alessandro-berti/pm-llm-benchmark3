The base values and “other features” in the local explanations are not independently credible until the whole feature vector—not just the displayed contributors—passes the same lineage audit. Because critical leaked variables were used by the model, deleting them only from the explanation does not make the existing prediction valid.

## 2. Process-model consistency checks

A concrete validation suite should combine feature lineage checks with normative process conformance:

1. **Freeze and reconstruct each prediction prefix**
   - Define `t0` as immediately after the first `Coverage Check Completed`.
   - Build the prefix using event time and a reliable sequence index.
   - For every feature, require both:
     - `effective_time <= t0`, and
     - `available/ingested_time <= scoring_time`.
   - Use bitemporal storage where late-arriving records are possible. Event time alone is insufficient if the system did not yet know the value.

2. **Automated feature-lineage assertions**
   - Label every feature as static, prefix aggregate, mutable snapshot, external snapshot, or complete-trace aggregate.
   - Fail any `t0` feature whose SQL scans events after `t0`, uses closure/final fields, uses extraction-date dimensions, or joins a snapshot newer than `t0`.
   - Detect names and transformations such as `final_*`, `total_*`, `last_*`, full variants, future decision values, and unrestricted window functions. These are warning heuristics, followed by actual dependency inspection.
   - Recompute representative feature vectors from raw data truncated physically at `t0`. Values must match production online values.

3. **Normative activity-order checks**
   - Encode the simplified process as a Petri net/BPMN model or declarative constraints:
     - registration precedes coverage completion;
     - after coverage, either fast-track assessment or evidence cycles may occur;
     - each received/validated document follows the relevant request;
     - damage assessment precedes an initial settlement decision;
     - appeal is enabled only by Partial or Deny;
     - a post-appeal settlement decision is permitted;
     - closure follows a final decision or withdrawal.
   - Use token replay or alignments to identify missing, extra, or reordered events, while preserving parallelism for fraud investigation.
   - Treat deviations as data-quality or exceptional-process findings, not automatically as impossible reality.

4. **Same-timestamp policy**
   - Order tied events only with a reliable source sequence index or another documented ordering key.
   - Without one, represent ties as a partial order. Do not create directional patterns such as `Closed -> Damage Assessment` from arbitrary row order.
   - Report whether a claimed edge is certain, one of several possible linearizations, or incompatible with all valid linearizations.
   - Where normative semantics resolve a tie, use that only for conformance assessment—not to claim that the source observed the inferred order.

5. **Enabled, impossible, observed, and future-possible status**
   - At every prefix, replay the trace to establish enabled activities.
   - At the displayed `t0`:
     - evidence request or fast-track assessment can be future-possible;
     - a fraud investigation may be future-possible if triggering conditions can subsequently arise;
     - a settlement decision is downstream, not observed;
     - an appeal is not currently enabled because no Partial or Deny decision has occurred;
     - closure is not enabled.
   - Explanations must distinguish:
     - **observed by `t0`**: usable;
     - **currently enabled**: process possibility, not an observed fact;
     - **future-possible**: cannot be encoded as if it happened;
     - **impossible under the normative model**: likely data/model inconsistency.
   - A predicted probability of a future path is potentially admissible only if computed solely from prefix data and clearly labeled as a prediction—not as `appeal_seen_anywhere`.

6. **Repeated decision semantics**
   - Index settlement decisions by occurrence.
   - Permit a second `Settlement Decision Recorded` only after an eligible first outcome and an intervening appeal.
   - Validate that Partial or Deny precedes the appeal and that the reconsideration decision follows it.
   - Do not flag `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` as inherently impossible; flag its use at `t0` as future leakage.
   - Investigate additional decisions without an appeal or correction mechanism.

7. **DFG, footprint, and alignment roles**
   - A DFG is useful for discovering observed adjacencies and rare edges, but a frequent edge is not a normative rule. Frequency can reflect logging granularity, parallel-event linearization, or a dominant cohort.
   - A footprint can summarize precedence, succession, and apparent concurrency, but it is also vulnerable to timestamp and sampling artifacts.
   - Alignments assess fit against the normative model and localize deviations, but results depend on model completeness and move costs.
   - Prefix conformance is essential here: full-trace conformance cannot justify exposing future events at `t0`.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

### Defensible predictive signal

Potential members, after confirming lineage, include:

- `initial_damage_complexity`
- `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`
- `claimant_channel`
- `initial_reserve` only if historically available at `t0`
- repaired `prior_claim_count_before_t0`
- a versioned `risk_score_as_of_t0`
- properly timestamped employee experience as of `t0`

**May be claimed:** The value was known at prediction time and is associated with model-estimated breach risk in the validated population. For prefix age, it is also reasonable to state that more of the fixed target window has already elapsed.

**May not be claimed:** That changing the feature would change settlement time, that it identifies the root cause, or that intervention on it is beneficial. Even a plausible domain mechanism is not causal evidence.

### Process-state or congestion signal

Potential members include:

- `queue_length_at_t0`
- `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`
- an actual team/resource assignment as of `t0`
- prefix-only counts and states
- observed activities through `t0`, or later-stage transitions only when already observed

**May be claimed:** These describe the claim’s current process state, accumulated age, or contemporaneous workload. They can support triage after calibration and prospective validation.

**May not be claimed:** That queue reduction necessarily yields the SHAP-implied reduction, or that a future activity occurred because it is enabled. Operational actionability requires an intervention study or credible causal design and capacity constraints must be considered.

### Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review

Potential members include:

- `claimant_region`
- `claimant_channel`
- repaired prior-claim history
- possibly initial reserve or complexity if coding differs systematically by group

**May be claimed:** The feature adds predictive information out of sample, if demonstrated. Correlation alone does not make a feature invalid.

**May not be claimed:** That region, channel, or claimant history is itself a delay cause or that adverse treatment based on it is fair. Review should include legal permissibility, subgroup calibration and error rates, indirect discrimination, interaction effects, missingness, stability, and comparison with less sensitive operational alternatives. Removing a named feature also does not remove its proxies.

### Drift/calendar proxy

Potential members include:

- `calendar_week`
- explicit holiday indicators
- policy regime, staffing regime, or seasonal workload variables

**May be claimed:** A time period was associated with risk under the historical regime.

**May not be claimed:** That week number intrinsically caused delay or that the relationship will persist. Random train/test splitting is inadequate; use forward temporal validation and test performance across policy boundaries. Explicit holiday and policy variables are usually more interpretable than raw week.

### Unusable until lineage is repaired

This class includes:

- `final_handler_team`
- current `initial_reserve` if no historical version exists
- `prior_claim_count_before_t0` under the current SQL
- `same_day_document_count_snapshot`
- `risk_score_current`
- `last_resource_role`
- extraction-date employee experience

Repair means reconstructing the exact value effective **and known** at `t0`, then retraining and recalibrating. It is not acceptable to substitute the current value and merely relabel it “as of `t0`.”

Some features are not merely repairable versions of the same concept: `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_decision`, full-trace variants, and total future document requests must be excluded from this early model. A separate prefix-derived concept may replace them, but it is a different feature.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **Immediately quarantine the current model and explanations**
   - Stop operational use of probabilities or SHAP explanations that rely on closure duration, future appeals, final decisions, final teams, full variants, complete-trace roles, or future event patterns.
   - Audit all model inputs, including features absent from the top-12 ranking. Low-importance leakage can still distort fitting and calibration.

2. **Build a point-in-time-correct feature store**
   - Store `case_id`, feature value, effective time, ingestion/availability time, source version, transformation version, and prediction cutoff.
   - Use immutable feature snapshots for each scoring event.
   - Implement strict as-of joins that cannot select data after `t0`; record snapshot age and matching tolerance.
   - Maintain effective-dated team assignments, resource roles, reserve history, risk-score versions, and employee attributes.

3. **Recompute features from physically truncated prefixes**
   - Generate training rows by truncating each trace at the first coverage-completion `t0`.
   - Ensure aggregates scan only that prefix and earlier cross-case data.
   - Replace full-trace variants with prefix variants or normative process-state markings.
   - Correct prior-claim SQL to use `claim_created_at < t0`, with explicit identity, lookback, current-claim exclusion, and late-arrival rules.
   - Replace nightly document snapshots with event-level or prior-snapshot counts.

4. **Introduce automated conformance and temporal tests**
   - Run normative alignment/declarative checks, repeated-decision validation, appeal-enablement checks, and closure-order checks.
   - Treat same-timestamp events without sequence indices as partial orders.
   - Unit-test each explanation feature against “observed,” “enabled,” “future-possible,” and “impossible” statuses at the prediction prefix.
   - Reject sequence features that depend on arbitrary tie-breaking or events outside the prefix.

5. **Retrain and recalibrate from scratch**
   - Merely suppressing leaked contributors in the user-facing explanation is insufficient.
   - Use forward-chaining or rolling temporal validation, with separate assessment around the policy change and holiday period.
   - Report discrimination, Brier score/log loss, calibration curves, threshold performance, and subgroup calibration.
   - Compare against simple prefix-only baselines to quantify how much prior performance came from leakage.

6. **Validate explanation faithfulness and stability**
   - Generate explanations only from the exact online feature vector and deployed model version.
   - Test local explanation stability across bootstrap models, nearby queue-snapshot tolerances, reasonable feature repairs, and correlated-feature groupings.
   - Present uncertainty when correlated variables can exchange attribution.
   - Add automated language rules preventing “causes,” “determines,” or “driver” unless supported by causal evidence; prefer “associated with” or “contributed to the model estimate.”

7. **Separate prediction from intervention**
   - Mark each retained feature as predictive-only, potentially actionable, or non-actionable.
   - For queue management, routing, or staffing interventions, use randomized pilots, stepped-wedge designs, or credible quasi-experimental analysis.
   - Do not interpret SHAP differences as expected treatment effects.

8. **Perform proxy, fairness, and governance analysis**
   - Review region, channel, prior history, reserve, complexity, and their interactions for subgroup calibration, false-positive rates, coverage, missingness, and disparate impact.
   - Evaluate whether operational variables such as repair-network availability explain the useful signal with less fairness risk.
   - Test models with and without proxy features. Retain a correlated feature only where predictive benefit, legal basis, governance controls, and harm analysis support it.
   - Audit downstream triage policies separately: a calibrated prediction can still produce unfair action.

9. **Monitor schema and process drift**
   - Alert on changes to activity labels, duplicate coverage events, missing sequence indices, timestamp precision, snapshot schedules, decision coding, and appeal semantics.
   - Monitor feature distributions, missingness, unseen prefix variants, conformance rates, calibration, and subgroup metrics.
   - Version the normative process model and explicitly annotate policy-regime changes. A newly frequent DFG edge should trigger investigation, not automatic acceptance as a valid rule.

10. **Establish release gates**
    - Require point-in-time replay success, zero prohibited future dependencies, conformance-test passage, temporal holdout performance, calibration acceptance, fairness review, and explanation-language review before deployment.
    - Preserve reproducible evidence linking every displayed explanation value to the source records that were actually available at `t0`.
