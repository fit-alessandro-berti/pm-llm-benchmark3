# Warranty Process Redesign: Reconciling Speed, Quality, Cost, and Compliance

## 1. Current-process diagnosis

### The Core Contradiction: The "Efficiency Illusion" of the Dominant Fast Path
The warranty process is trapped in a classic local-optimization fallacy. Management observes that the single most frequent end-to-end path in the directly-follows graph (DFG)—`Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close`—appears extraordinarily fast and economical. Auto-screening capacity is virtually unlimited at €0.20 per case; the transition from intake to fast approval takes a median duration of under an hour (0.5h to auto screen + 0.4h to fast approval); and dispatch takes a median of only 1.8 hours. Before accounting for downstream rework, cases utilizing fast approval appear **31% cheaper** than reviewed cases. Consequently, 2,550 of the 5,000 cases (51%) are pushed directly through fast approval, with 2,300 dispatched immediately as replacements.

However, process-mining evidence reveals that this path achieves speed and apparent low cost by shifting risk, compliance infractions, and operational failures downstream. Far from an efficient resolution engine, the dominant path acts as a defect incubator:
1. **Downstream Quality Collapse:** Fast approval cases produce a customer complaint/reopen rate of **14.1%**, more than four times the complaint rate of the reviewed/repair path (**3.3%**). Out of 510 total customer complaints, 400 originate directly from `Fast approval` (140) and `Dispatch replacement` (260).
2. **The Net Cost Reversal:** When downstream customer complaints, reshipments, wrong replacement costs (€410 per high-value device, €95 per accessory), and service credits are factored in, the initial 31% cost advantage collapses to a mere **9%**. For high-value devices, the fast replacement path is net-negative.
3. **Severe Regulatory Non-Compliance:** The fast path achieves its speed by systematically violating core governance controls, creating substantial audit and financial liabilities.

### Synthesis of Specific Process-Mining Artifacts

1. **Conformance Replay and Alignment Diagnostics (Fitness & Rules R1/R2):**
   Token-based replay against the normative Petri net reveals a log fitness of **0.91**, but trace fitness is only **68%**—meaning 32% of all claims violate organizational policy. Move-on-log alignment diagnostics demonstrate two major structural deviations:
   - *Violation of Rule R1:* Claims above €400 bypass mandatory `Manual eligibility review` or `Fraud/risk review` and flow straight into `Fast approval`.
   - *Violation of Rule R2:* `Dispatch replacement` occurs before evidence completeness is verified for high-value devices (where exceptions are strictly forbidden by policy).
   Outdated, non-segment-aware auto-screening rules treat high-value devices identically to low-risk accessories, causing low precision and widespread compliance breaches.

2. **DFG Flow Asymmetry and Fulfillment Bias:**
   Out of 5,000 intake claims, 3,600 (72%) flow to `Auto eligibility screen`, while 1,400 (28%) go to `Manual eligibility review`. Of the auto-screened claims, 71% (2,550 cases) are routed to `Fast approval`. The process exhibits an overwhelming bias toward replacement: **2,300 cases** result in `Dispatch replacement`, whereas only **390 cases** ever reach `Repair device`. This occurs despite repair having a dramatically lower complaint rate (3.3%) than replacement.

3. **Performance DFG Latency Tails and Failure Loops:**
   The performance DFG exposes extreme downstream friction that offsets upfront speed:
   - While `Auto screen -> Fast approval` takes median 0.4h (mean 1.0h, p90 4.0h), the downstream failure edges exhibit massive latency tails: `Fast approval -> Customer complaint/reopen` takes median 50.0h (mean 72.0h, p90 150.0h); `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` takes median 70.0h (mean 96.0h, p90 180.0h).
   - Once a complaint occurs, routing to `Manual eligibility review` takes median 31.0h (mean 40.0h, p90 100.0h), and routing to the `Escalation board` takes median 42.0h (mean 54.0h, p90 130.0h).
   - The evidence-chasing loop is severely congested: `Auto screen -> Request missing evidence` takes median 3.1h, but `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` takes median 26.0h (mean 34.0h, p90 88.0h).

4. **Rework Loops and Component-Mismatch Costs:**
   Rework statistics show operational deterioration across two major loops:
   - *Evidence Loop:* `Request missing evidence` occurs at least twice in **26%** of cases entering it, driving 320 abandoned claims.
   - *Replacement Errors:* Temporal profile deviations confirm that the long-tail dispatch-to-complaint delay (p90 180h) is directly linked to incorrect replacement part selection. Because a wrong replacement costs **€410** in expected losses for high-value devices and **€95** for accessories, unverified fast dispatches directly generate massive capital waste.
   - *Repair Rework:* Even when devices are repaired, `Rework repair` occurs in **12.5%** of high-value devices (vs. 4.0% for low-risk devices), pointing to inadequate technical quality gates before initial return.

5. **Customer Segment Outcome Polarization:**
   Cross-analyzing segment data reveals that the current process treats diverse customer needs and asset risks with a blunt, inappropriate mechanism:
   - *Premium High-Value Devices (14% share):* Suffer a catastrophic **22% complaint/reopen rate** on the fast path (vs. only 5% on the reviewed/repair path). Delivering a 20-hour median cycle time is meaningless when nearly a quarter of these high-value customers experience failures, wrong parts, or compliance breaches.
   - *Standard High-Value Devices (21% share):* Suffer an **18% complaint rate** on the fast path (vs. 6% on reviewed paths), compounded by the €410 wrong replacement cost.
   - *Premium Low-Risk Accessories (18% share):* Achieve excellent outcomes on the fast path (median 8h cycle time, 6% complaints). However, when routed to the manual path, median cycle time balloons to **46 hours**. Because service-credit exposure is triggered when cycle time exceeds 24 hours, the slow manual path causes severe financial penalties on cheap accessories where labor cost (€34/h) already exceeds the item's margin.
   - *Unclear Defect / Missing Evidence Segment (22% share):* Suffers an agonizing median cycle time of **140 hours**, driving the majority of the 390 total claim abandonments.

6. **Resource Utilization and Capacity Cliffs:**
   - *Manual Eligibility Review:* 7 reviewers (€34/h, 252 productive hours/week) face a steep queuing cliff: delay times escalate non-linearly whenever utilization exceeds **82%**. Pushing high-value claims into manual review without offloading accessories will immediately cause queue collapse.
   - *Fraud/Risk Review:* 2 specialists (€52/h) represent an acute bottleneck on Mondays and post-marketing campaigns (`Manual review -> Fraud review` p90 is 44.0h).
   - *Repair Quoting:* 3 technicians (€45/h) experience high delays (`Manual review -> Repair quote` p90 is 60.0h), driven almost entirely by waiting for part-price confirmation rather than technical estimation.
   - *Quality Check:* 2 inspectors (€38/h) are underutilized despite high-value repair rework sitting at 12.5%.
   - *Escalation Board:* Capped at one daily 45-minute slot; while effective at customer recovery, it handles only 120 cases and cannot scale.

## 2. Redesign A: Value-and-Risk Bifurcated Architecture (Express No-Touch vs. Regulated Device Gateway)

### Redesigned Process Flow
Redesign A structurally decouples the warranty process into two mutually exclusive operating lanes at intake, governed strictly by asset risk (the €400 threshold) and customer segment SLAs:

```
[Intake Claim]
       │
       ▼
[Segment & Value Routing Engine]
       │
       ├─────────────────────────────────────────┐
       ▼ (Asset ≤ €400: Accessories - 43%)       ▼ (Asset > €400: Devices - 35-57%)
[Express No-Touch Lane]                   [Regulated Device Gateway]
       │                                         │
[Automated Serial/SKU Validation]         [Mandatory Manual Review / Triage]
       │                                         │
[Instant Auto-Approval]                   [Evidence Verification Gate (R2)]
       │                                         │
[Direct Replacement Dispatch]             [Technical Decision: Repair vs. Replace]
       │                                         │
[Auto Compliance Close]                   [Pre-Dispatch Part Verification]
                                                 │
                                          [Fulfillment & Quality Close]
```

- **Track 1: Express No-Touch Lane (Low-Risk Accessories, 43% of total volume):**
  - All accessory claims (Premium 18%, Standard 25%) are routed through updated auto-screen logic.
  - Bypasses manual review and evidence completion requests entirely. Automated SKU matching approves the claim instantly.
  - For Premium accessories, dispatch occurs in <8–12 hours, guaranteeing resolution well within the 24-hour service-credit exposure window.
  - *Policy Codification:* Normative Rule R2 is formally amended in corporate policy to exempt all accessories under €400, extending the waiver currently restricted to Premium accessories.
- **Track 2: Regulated Device Gateway (Mid-Risk & High-Value Devices, € > 400):**
  - Fast approval is hard-coded as **inaccessible** for any device over €400, eliminating the R1 violation at the root.
  - Claims undergo mandatory structured technical triage by the 7 Manual Reviewers, who are now shielded from accessory volume.
  - Replacement dispatch is physically and procedurally blocked until evidence completeness is signed off (strictly enforcing R2).
  - High-value replacements must pass an automated SKU-to-serial compatibility check before warehouse release.

### Target Segments, Paths, and Activities
- **Segments:** Premium & Standard Low-Risk Accessories (43%); Premium & Standard High-Value Devices (35%).
- **Paths:** Eliminates the unconstrained `Auto screen -> Fast approval` pathway for high-value devices; eliminates the slow `Intake -> Manual eligibility review` pathway for accessories.
- **Activities:** Retools `Auto eligibility screen` into a strict segment-based router; expands `Manual eligibility review` into dedicated device technical triage.

### Mechanism of Improvement
1. **Workload Shielding & Queue Preservation:** Removing ~2,150 accessory cases from manual review queues reduces demand on the 7 manual reviewers by over 40%. This keeps manual review utilization safely below the critical 82% threshold, allowing device claims to be reviewed promptly without queue explosions.
2. **Elimination of Service-Credit Penalties:** Guarantees that Premium accessory claims never touch the 46-hour manual path, eliminating service-credit breaches (>24h).
3. **Eradication of Rule R1/R2 Breaches & Defect Reduction:** Prohibiting auto-approval on high-value devices restores trace fitness above 95% and eliminates the move-on-log alignment violations. High-value complaint rates drop from 18–22% down to the reviewed baseline of 5–6%, saving €410 per prevented mis-dispatch.

### Deliberate Sacrifices and Unresolved Issues
- **Sacrifices Ultra-Fast Device Turnaround:** High-value devices will no longer experience 20–28 hour turnaround times; their median cycle time will intentionally rise to 48–72 hours to ensure quality and compliance.
- **Accepts Baseline Accessory Leakage:** Intentionally accepts a 6–8% baseline replacement error and scrap rate on accessories as a rational trade-off against reviewer labor costs (€34/h).
- **Unresolved Evidence Loops:** Does not fundamentally resolve the intake friction for the 22% "Unclear defect" cases; customers with poor documentation must still undergo manual ping-pong.

## 3. Redesign B: Shift-Left Interactive Evidence Engine with Pre-Dispatch Verification Gate

### Redesigned Process Flow
Redesign B keeps a unified routing backbone but eliminates downstream failure modes by transforming the front-end information intake and adding an automated physical quality barrier before dispatch:

```
[Intake Claim: Interactive Evidence Portal]
  - Synchronous Computer-Vision Photo Validation
  - Real-Time Serial & Receipt OCR
  - Assisted Omni-Channel Fallback for Low Digital Literacy
       │
       ▼ (Evidence Confidence Score Generated)
[Enhanced Auto-Screen Engine]
       │
       ├─────────────────────────────────────────┐
       ▼ (ECS ≥ 95% & Defect Unambiguous)        ▼ (ECS < 95% or High-Risk Anomaly)
[Rapid Path]                              [Assisted Triage Queue]
  - Accessories: Instant Fast Approval      - Asynchronous Specialist Review
  - Devices: 10-Min Asynchronous Sign-Off    - Structured One-Touch Outreach
       │                                         │
       └────────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                            │
                            ▼
           [Pre-Dispatch Technical Verification Gate]
             - Barcode SKU-to-Serial Compatibility Check
             - 6-12 Hour Physical Staging & Quality Hold
                            │
                            ▼
              [Dispatch Replacement / Close]
```

- **Component 1: Shift-Left Interactive Evidence Portal:**
  - Replaces static web forms with a dynamic intake portal. Customers upload defect photos guided by on-screen stencils and submit proof of purchase verified via real-time OCR.
  - The system validates completeness in-session ("shift-left"). If a photo is blurry or the serial number is obscured, the customer is prompted immediately during intake, eliminating the asynchronous 34-hour `Request missing evidence -> Manual review` loop.
  - *Assisted Fallback:* Low digital-literacy users can opt into a conversational SMS/messaging flow or an automated scheduling callback to prevent claim abandonment.
- **Component 2: Evidence-Scored Multi-Tier Screening Engine:**
  - Auto-screen (€0.20/case) calculates an automated Evidence Confidence Score (ECS).
  - Claims with high ECS (>95%) and unambiguous defects follow an accelerated path: accessories auto-approve; devices over €400 receive a 10-minute asynchronous desk review by Manual Reviewers, strictly fulfilling Rule R1 while maintaining rapid velocity.
- **Component 3: Pre-Dispatch Technical Verification Gate:**
  - Before warehouse fulfillment, an automated WMS barrier cross-references the replacement part SKU against the registered device model and serial number.
  - Warehouse operators perform a mandatory barcode verification scan within a dedicated 6–12 hour staging window, preventing incorrect part dispatch.

### Target Segments, Paths, and Activities
- **Segments:** The "Unclear defect / missing evidence" segment (22% share, 140h cycle time); high-value devices vulnerable to wrong replacement selection (€410 error cost).
- **Paths:** Replaces `Auto screen -> Request missing evidence` and `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review`; guards `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement`.
- **Activities:** Redesigns `Intake claim` into an active validation portal; introduces `Pre-dispatch verification gate`.

### Mechanism of Improvement
1. **Compression of the Evidence Loop:** Resolving missing information synchronously during intake reduces the 26% repeat-request rate by 35–45%. The median 140-hour cycle time for unclear defects collapses to 36–48 hours.
2. **Interception of Fulfillment Errors:** The pre-dispatch gate catches wrong-part mismatches before physical shipping, directly eliminating the root cause of the 260 complaints emerging from `Dispatch replacement` and saving €410 on devices and €95 on accessories.
3. **Reconciling Speed with Compliance:** High-value devices can still achieve relatively fast fulfillment (24–36h) because reviewers receive complete, pre-validated dossiers, enabling a compliant 5-minute sign-off that satisfies R1 and R2 without multi-day queue delays.

### Deliberate Sacrifices and Unresolved Issues
- **Deliberate Fulfillment Delay:** Adds a mandatory 6–12 hour latency buffer to all replacement dispatches, preventing sub-hour fulfillment.
- **High Technical & Capital Complexity:** Demands substantial upfront software engineering (computer vision, portal UI, ERP/WMS integration).
- **Customer Usability Friction:** Overly rigid front-end validation risks alienating less tech-savvy customers, potentially worsening the 320 abandoned claims if assisted fallback channels fail.
- **Leaves Repair Quoting Delays Intact:** Does not address the 22-hour delay in obtaining repair part prices.

## 4. Redesign C: Circular Repair-First Architecture with Cross-Functional Swarming Cells

### Redesigned Process Flow
Redesign C fundamentally shifts the fulfillment philosophy from a disposable, replacement-first model to a circular, diagnostic-and-repair-first model, while dismantling serialized departmental queues in favor of collaborative swarming:

```
[Intake Claim]
       │
       ▼
[Standardized Pre-Priced Diagnostic & Triage]
  - Fixed-Tier Repair Menus (Zero Part-Price Quoting Delay)
  - Pre-Authorized Warranty Terms at Intake
       │
       ▼
[Cross-Functional Warranty Swarm Pods]
  (3 Manual Reviewers + 1 Fraud Specialist + 1 Repair Tech)
  - Single-Touch Joint Assessment for Complex Claims
  - Dynamic Real-Time Escalation (Replaces 45-Min Board)
       │
       ├─────────────────────────────────────────┐
       ▼ (Accessories: Low-Risk)                 ▼ (Devices: Mid & High-Value)
[Bulk Replacement Dispatch]               [Direct Bench Repair & Refurbishment]
                                                 │
                                          [Mandatory Quality Inspector Check]
                                            (Underutilized QC Staff Re-Engaged)
                                                 │
                                          [Return to Customer & Compliance Close]
```

- **Component 1: Standardized Pre-Priced Repair Catalogs:**
  - Replaces custom quoting delays (`Manual review -> Repair quote` mean 22h, p90 60h; `Repair quote -> Customer approval` mean 30h, p90 80h).
  - Establishes a pre-approved, flat-rate repair catalog for standard defects (e.g., Tier 1 screen/casing, Tier 2 motherboard/power, Tier 3 sub-assembly).
  - Customers pre-authorize warranty repair terms at intake, allowing technicians to start physical repair immediately upon device receipt without waiting for price confirmations or approval rounds.
- **Component 2: Cross-Functional Warranty Swarm Pods:**
  - Dissolves the siloed, sequential handoffs between Manual Reviewers (7 staff, €34/h), Fraud Specialists (2 staff, €52/h), and Repair Techs (3 staff, €45/h).
  - Reorganizes staff into two agile Swarm Pods. Non-auto-screened cases are reviewed jointly in a single touch, smoothing out Monday and post-campaign fraud review spikes.
- **Component 3: Repair-First Fulfillment for Devices (> €400):**
  - Switches the primary resolution for devices from replacement to repair, leveraging the proven **3.3% complaint rate** of repair (vs. 18–22% on fast replacement).
  - To eliminate the 12.5% high-value repair rework rate, the 2 underutilized Quality Check inspectors (€38/h) are integrated into a mandatory post-repair testing gate (`Repair device -> Quality check -> Quality close`).
- **Component 4: Dynamic Pod Escalation (Retiring the 45-Minute Board):**
  - Retires the unscalable daily 45-minute Escalation Board (120 cases).
  - Reopened claims (310 cases) and customer disputes are swarm-resolved by the pod within 2–4 hours, strictly satisfying Rule R3 with full manual auditability.

### Target Segments, Paths, and Activities
- **Segments:** High-Value Devices (Premium 14%, Standard 21%); reopens and escalations across all segments.
- **Paths:** Eliminates the sequential chain `Manual review -> Fraud review -> Repair quote -> Customer approval`; redirects `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement` toward repair.
- **Activities:** Eliminates part-price quoting waits; expands `Quality check` to 100% of repaired devices; replaces `Escalation board` with dynamic pod triage.

### Mechanism of Improvement
1. **Defect Eradication via Repair Quality:** Transitioning devices from fast replacement to certified repair capitalizes on the 3.3% complaint rate, eliminating the €410 wrong-replacement expense and dramatically improving customer retention.
2. **Elimination of Quoting Latency:** Standardized repair catalogs eliminate the 52 hours of combined delay spent waiting for part-price confirmations and customer approvals.
3. **Queue Balancing via Swarming:** Cross-functional swarming dissolves individual queue barriers, preventing manual review utilization from hitting the 82% explosion threshold. Fraud specialists lend real-time consults rather than managing a monolithic queue.
4. **Activation of Underutilized QC Capacity:** Re-engaging the 2 underutilized Quality Check inspectors directly suppresses the 12.5% high-value repair rework rate down to under 4%.

### Deliberate Sacrifices and Unresolved Issues
- **Sacrifices Instant Gratification:** Physical repair inherently takes 48–72 hours of shop time; it cannot match the immediate fulfillment of a brand-new replacement dispatch.
- **Customer Expectation Friction:** Certain Premium customers who expect a brand-new unit may push back against receiving a repaired/refurbished device, requiring strict terms-of-service communication.
- **Higher Labor Touch-Cost:** Involving technicians and quality inspectors in hands-on repair increases direct labor hours compared to automated no-touch replacement.

## 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

### Redesign A: Value-and-Risk Bifurcated Architecture

#### Failure Modes
1. **Intake Misclassification and Fraudulent SKU Gaming:**
   Because the accessory track operates as a "no-touch express lane" without human verification, dishonest claimants or retail partners may intentionally misclassify mid-tier or high-value devices as low-risk accessories (or bundle them under accessory SKUs) to exploit auto-approval. This would trigger inventory write-offs and financial leakage, eroding the net cost benefits.
2. **Capacity Overwhelm in the Regulated Device Lane:**
   Disallowing Fast Approval for all claims over €400 redirects approximately 1,750 cases into mandatory manual review. If manual triage takes longer than modeled, the 7 manual reviewers (252 total weekly hours) will rapidly breach their **82% utilization threshold**. Queues will explode non-linearly, driving device cycle times from 20–28 hours to weeks, creating massive customer backlash.

#### Displaced Bottleneck
The primary bottleneck migrates from downstream customer complaints and escalation boards back to the **Manual Eligibility Review and Fraud/Risk specialist queues**. The 2 fraud specialists (already vulnerable on Mondays and post-marketing campaigns) will face an acute backlog of high-value device referrals, creating an administrative bottleneck at intake.

---

### Redesign B: Shift-Left Evidence Engine & Pre-Dispatch Gate

#### Failure Modes
1. **Customer Intake Friction and Digital Abandonment Surge:**
   The interactive portal requires customers to upload sharp photos, serial numbers, and receipts synchronously. Customers with low digital literacy, poor smartphone cameras, or older demographics may find the interface frustrating and abandon claims prematurely. Abandonment could surge well beyond the current 320 cases, generating brand damage and off-portal escalations.
2. **Pre-Dispatch Staging Gridlock and Carrier Cut-Off Failures:**
   Inserting a 6–12 hour physical and algorithmic verification gate before shipping requires a dedicated warehouse staging area. If WMS barcode scanners suffer read errors, or if warehouse staffing falls behind during volume surges, packages will miss daily courier cut-off times. Staging bays will overflow, turning a digital delay into physical warehouse gridlock.

#### Displaced Bottleneck
The bottleneck migrates from the front-end administrative missing-evidence loop into the **Physical Warehouse Dispatch Staging Area and Logistics Quality Assurance Station**. Fulfillment latency shifts from the digital front-end to physical dock operations.

---

### Redesign C: Circular Repair-First Swarming Architecture

#### Failure Modes
1. **Customer Backlash Against Repaired Goods ("Replacement Entitlement"):**
   Consumer electronics buyers—especially Premium customers who paid high prices for flagship devices (14% share)—frequently demand a brand-new replacement when a failure occurs. Presenting them with a repair timeline (48–72h) or a refurbished unit may provoke fierce resistance, driving customers to bypass the pod and flood executive escalation channels, negating the 3.3% quality advantage.
2. **Spare-Parts Stockouts and Technician Bench Starvation:**
   A repair-first model depends entirely on immediate component availability. If flat-rate repair menus are established but physical spare parts (e.g., replacement motherboards, displays) are backlogged due to global supply chain disruptions, devices will sit disassembled on benches for weeks. Cycle times will blow out past 200 hours.

#### Displaced Bottleneck
The bottleneck migrates from eligibility screening to the **Physical Repair Bench and Technician Labor Capacity**. The 3 repair technicians (€45/h) become the single throughput constraint of the entire organization. Repair bays will overflow with disassembled devices while intake reviewers sit idle in their swarm pods.

---

## 7. Recommended experiment sequence

### Staged Rollout Strategy
The organization must avoid an all-at-once transformation. The recommended sequence pilots the lowest-complexity, highest-relief interventions first to liberate operational capacity before introducing more complex structural changes:

```
[Phase 1: Week 1-4]
  Pilot Redesign A Express Lane on Accessories (43% Volume)
  + Enforce R1/R2 on Premium High-Value Devices Only
       │
       ▼ (Evaluate Gate Metrics & Validate Freed Reviewer Capacity)
[Phase 2: Week 5-10]
  Deploy Redesign B Shift-Left Evidence Portal on "Unclear Defect" Cohort (22%)
  + Introduce WMS Pre-Dispatch Verification Gate
       │
       ▼ (Evaluate Gate Metrics & Fulfillment Error Interception)
[Phase 3: Week 11-16]
  Roll out Redesign C Flat-Rate Repair Catalogs & Swarm Pods for Devices
```

### 1. First Pilot: Focused Dual-Segment Cohort
- **Scope:** A 4-week pilot restricted to two distinct customer segments in a single product line or geographic region:
  1. *Premium Low-Risk Accessories (18% of volume):* Route 100% through the Redesign A Express Lane (instant auto-approval, zero manual review, guaranteed <12h dispatch).
  2. *Premium High-Value Devices (14% of volume):* Enforce Rule R1 (no fast approval; mandatory triage) paired with a prototype pre-dispatch SKU verification gate and dedicated manual sign-off.
- **Strategic Rationale:** Premium accessories represent an immediate win (eliminating the 46h manual delay that triggers service credit penalties), while Premium high-value devices tackle the segment with the highest complaint rate (22%), highest cost of error (€410), and highest regulatory liability. Offloading accessories instantly frees manual review capacity to absorb high-value triage.

### 2. Metrics to Monitor
- **Cycle Time Distribution:**
  - Premium accessory median cycle time (target: <12h; 0% exceeding the 24h service-credit threshold).
  - High-value device median cycle time (target: 48–60h, eliminating the 96h manual tail).
- **Quality & Downstream Rework:**
  - Complaint/reopen rate on Premium high-value devices (target: reduction from 22% to <8%).
  - Complaint rate on Premium accessories (target: maintain ≤6%).
  - Mismatched replacement dispatches (target: 0 reported wrong-part shipments).
- **Compliance & Conformance:**
  - Token replay trace fitness on the pilot cohort (target: >95%).
  - Move-on-log alignment violations of Rules R1 and R2 (target: zero occurrences).
- **Resource Health:**
  - Manual Review queue utilization rate (target: 65%–78%; must not exceed 82%).
  - Customer intake abandonment rate (target: maintain ≤6.4% baseline).
- **Financial Bottom Line:**
  - Net Cost per Claim: `[Direct Labor + Replacement Asset Cost + Shipping + Rework/Reshipment Losses (€410/€95) + Service Credits Payouts]`.

### 3. Stop / Go Thresholds
- **GO Thresholds (Proceed to Phase 2):**
  - Premium high-value complaint/reopen rate falls from 22% to **below 9%**.
  - Zero wrong-part replacement escalations occur (saving €410 per prevented error).
  - Premium accessory cycle time median remains **<12 hours** with zero service credit payouts.
  - Manual review queue utilization stabilizes **below 80%**.
  - Net cost per claim across the pilot cohort decreases by **12% to 18%**.
- **ITERATE / PAUSE Thresholds (Hold and recalibrate):**
  - Manual review utilization hovers between **80% and 84%**, indicating device triage takes longer than expected. *Action:* Refine screening rules or add light automated pre-scoring.
  - Accessory complaint rate rises slightly (between 7% and 9%). *Action:* Tighten SKU eligibility criteria.
- **STOP / ROLLBACK Thresholds (Abort pilot immediately):**
  - Manual review queue utilization exceeds **85%**, causing wait times to spike beyond 48 hours.
  - Customer abandonment on device claims increases by **>15%** over baseline.
  - Fraud/leakage on express accessories exceeds the labor cost savings of manual review.

### 4. Sequencing Logic
1. **Unclog the Core First (Weeks 1–4):** Deploying the accessory express lane removes 43% of case volume from human reviewer touchpoints. This generates the necessary capacity buffer for the 7 manual reviewers to absorb high-value device triage without exceeding the 82% utilization cliff.
2. **Fix the Intake Defect Leak (Weeks 5–10):** With manual review stabilized, deploy Redesign B's interactive evidence portal to tackle the 22% "Unclear defect" segment, compressing the 140-hour cycle time and eliminating repeated evidence loops. Simultaneously activate the pre-dispatch warehouse verification gate to stop wrong-part shipments.
3. **Transition to Circular Repair (Weeks 11–16):** Once fulfillment errors and intake queues are controlled, negotiate flat-rate repair catalogs and restructure technicians into Redesign C Swarm Pods. This captures the 3.3% complaint advantage on repairable devices while utilizing the 2 underused Quality Check inspectors.

### 5. Learning Whether Improvements Are Real vs. Shifted Downstream
To ensure cycle-time and cost gains are authentic rather than illusory downstream deferrals, the executive sponsor must mandate four auditing mechanisms:
- **Mandatory 60-Day Cohort Observation Window:**
  The performance DFG proves that `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` takes a mean of 96 hours and a p90 of 180 hours (7.5 days). Evaluating pilot results after only 7 or 14 days will miss the entire long-tail complaint distribution. All pilot cohorts must be tracked for a minimum of 60 days post-close before confirming quality gains.
- **Cross-Channel Escalation Tracking:**
  Monitor total customer service contacts, call center inquiry volumes, and executive escalations outside the formal warranty portal. If warranty portal complaints drop by 100 cases but general call center complaint volume increases by 100 cases, friction was merely displaced to another customer touchpoint.
- **Closed-Loop Physical Inventory Reconciliations:**
  Audit warehouse return bins, scrapped inventory, and returned "wrong replacement" SKUs. A genuine operational improvement will produce a measurable, physical reduction in returned €410 high-value replacement devices at the warehouse dock. Physical inventory reconciliation provides ground-truth validation that cannot be falsified by process-logging artifacts.
- **Comprehensive Lifecycle Cost Accounting:**
  Track end-to-end net claim cost rather than isolated activity touch-costs. An extra €15 in upfront manual triage labor that prevents a €410 wrong replacement and a €50 service credit represents an immediate €445 net operational gain to the enterprise.
