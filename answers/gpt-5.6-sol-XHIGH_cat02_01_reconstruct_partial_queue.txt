# 1. Assumptions and notation

**A1 — Event ordering and interval convention.** Activities and station residence are treated as half-open intervals, \([start,end)\). At the same reported timestamp I process:

1. service completions;
2. service starts, which remove cases from a queue;
3. new station arrivals, which add cases to a queue.

Thus a resource may finish one case and start another at the same reported time without overlap. WIP at 14:00 means the left limit \(14{:}00^{-}\), before censoring the observation.

**A2 — Rounded-clock estimates.** Calculations first use the reported timestamps as central estimates. A difference between two independently rounded timestamps has an approximate envelope of \(x\pm5\) minutes, truncated at zero where necessary. If one endpoint is the exact 14:00 cutoff, the envelope is \(x\pm2.5\) minutes. These are deterministic endpoint envelopes, not confidence intervals.

**A3 — Cutoff.** The observation cutoff is treated as exactly 14:00. OPEN service is clipped at 14:00 for observed busy time and elapsed service, but its final duration has no finite upper bound from these data. A blank start after station entry represents an elapsed, right-censored queue wait.

**A4 — Capacity and outage.** Each recorded resource executes at most one continuous, non-preemptive activity at a time. S1 has 720 scheduled resource-minutes over six hours. S2 has \(720-15=705\) available resource-minutes because C2 is unavailable on \([10{:}55,11{:}10)\). The outage interval is treated as exact as stated.

**A5 — Population coverage.** The table is assumed to contain every case present in or arriving to the two-station process during the window, with no unlisted carry-in. This is needed for absolute WIP. If carry-in was omitted, reported WIP and arrival counts are lower bounds.

**A6 — Queue discipline.** No FIFO assumption is made. Queue lengths are station-level counts, while recorded resource assignments are retained for service feasibility.

**A7 — Missing-start constraints.** A missing start must be no earlier than station entry and the preceding job’s completion on that resource, and no later than its own completion. This gives the following rounded-clock feasible ranges:

| case/activity | preceding work on resource | feasible missing start | resulting wait | resulting service |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| P07 S1/I1 | P05 ends 09:25 | 09:25–10:00 | 40–75 | 0–35 |
| P15 S1/I1 | P13 ends 11:40 | 11:40–12:15 | 90–125 | 0–35 |
| P05 S2/C1 | P03 ends 09:45 | 09:45–10:15 | 20–50 | 0–30 |
| P11 S2/C2 | P08 ends 12:00 | 12:00–12:25 | 55–80 | 0–25 |

The corresponding approximate rounding envelopes are respectively: P07 wait 35–80/service 0–40; P15 wait 85–130/service 0–40; P05 wait 15–55/service 0–35; P11 wait 50–85/service 0–30.

**A8 — Imputation scenarios.** “Early,” “central,” and “late” scenarios use the lower endpoint, midpoint, and upper endpoint of each feasible missing-start range. The central values are illustrative midpoints, not recovered observations. Early starts minimize queue waiting but maximize active service; late starts do the reverse.

**A9 — Physical conflicts and logging anomalies.** The constrained central schedules contain no positive-duration resource overlaps. The reported C1 transition from P07 to P09 at 11:00 is permitted by A1. Independent rounding errors at a shared boundary cannot all vary freely because physical resource order must still hold.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

For case \(i\) at station \(s\), let:

- \(a_{is}\): station arrival; \(a_{i1}\) is external arrival and \(a_{i2}=e_{i1}\);
- \(b_{is}\): activity start;
- \(e_{is}\): activity completion;
- \(T=14{:}00\).

For a completed or started activity:

\[
W_{is}=b_{is}-a_{is},\qquad
S_{is}=e_{is}-b_{is},\qquad
R_{is}=e_{is}-a_{is}=W_{is}+S_{is}.
\]

Here \(W\) is queue waiting, \(S\) is active service, and \(R\) is station sojourn. For OPEN work, observed elapsed service is \(T-b_{is}\), while final \(S_{is}\ge T-b_{is}\). For an unstarted case waiting at the cutoff, observed queue exposure is \(T-a_{is}\), while final waiting is at least that exposure.

For a completed end-to-end case:

\[
R_i^{\text{case}}=e_{i2}-a_{i1}=R_{i1}+R_{i2}.
\]

Queue, active-service, and station-WIP counts are:

\[
Q_s(t)=\sum_i 1[a_{is}\le t<b_{is}],
\]

with \(b_{is}=T\) for an unstarted case;

\[
B_s(t)=\sum_i 1[b_{is}\le t<\min(e_{is},T)],
\]

and

\[
L_s(t)=Q_s(t)+B_s(t)
      =\sum_i1[a_{is}\le t<\min(e_{is},T)].
\]

Process-wide WIP is \(L_1(t)+L_2(t)\), since no case bypasses either station and the two station-residence intervals are consecutive.

Time-weighted means use area under the count curve:

\[
\bar Q_s=\frac{1}{360}\int_0^{360}Q_s(t)\,dt,\qquad
\bar L_s=\frac{1}{360}\int_0^{360}L_s(t)\,dt.
\]

Busy time is the union of each resource’s clipped service intervals. Station utilization is:

\[
U_s=\frac{\text{observed resource busy-minutes}}
          {\text{scheduled available resource-minutes}}.
\]

It measures utilization within 08:00–14:00 only; unfinished service after 14:00 is not included.

For a subperiod \([u,v)\), arrival and departure rates are:

\[
\lambda_s(u,v)=\frac{\#\{a_{is}\in[u,v)\}}{v-u},\qquad
\mu_s(u,v)=\frac{\#\{e_{is}\in[u,v)\}}{v-u}.
\]

Reported subperiod counts follow the rounded-time convention. An event exactly at 10:00 or 12:00 can move to an adjacent bin under timestamp uncertainty.

Lower/central/upper estimates are obtained as follows:

- missing-start uncertainty: early/midpoint/late feasible starts;
- right censoring: elapsed values plus one-sided final-duration bounds;
- rounding: central rounded-clock values plus endpoint envelopes;
- WIP total: independent of missing-start imputation because moving a missing start only reallocates time between queue and service.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

**Observed evidence**

- S1 receives 13 cases but completes only six services before 10:00. Its WIP rises from one at 08:00 to seven at 10:00, and waits progress from 5–20 minutes for the earliest cases to around 80–105 minutes later.
- S2 also builds, but more slowly initially because S1 gates its arrivals. Its central completed-cohort wait is about 34 minutes versus about 65 minutes at S1.
- Nominal completed service is longer at S2: approximately 34–38 minutes versus 25–29 minutes at S1. With two resources, these correspond to rough unconstrained capacities of about 3.1–3.5 cases/hour at S2 and 4.2–4.8 cases/hour at S1.
- From 12:00 to 14:00, external arrivals almost stop. S1 completes seven cases and falls from seven WIP to two, while S2 receives seven, completes six, and ends with six WIP.
- At 14:00, S1 has one open and one waiting case; S2 has two open and four waiting cases.

**Interpretation**

The evidence is most consistent with a **time-varying combination**:

1. **08:00 to roughly 10:00 — S1 is the visible entrance bottleneck.** The external burst greatly exceeds S1 departures, causing the larger queue and rapidly increasing S1 waits. S2 is partially starved by S1 early on, although it still begins accumulating work because its services are longer.

2. **Roughly 10:00 to 12:00 — both stations are congested.** S1 retains high WIP while S2’s queue grows. It would be misleading to name a single fixed bottleneck in this interval.

3. **12:00 to 14:00 — S2 becomes the operational bottleneck.** S1 drains after external arrivals subside, but its releases continue feeding S2 at about 3.5/hour. S2 departures are only about 3/hour, and its unresolved WIP rises relative to S1.

**Maintenance interval**

During 10:55–11:10, C2 is unavailable while the S2 queue is material:

- P10 enters S2 at 10:55;
- P07 completes at 11:00;
- P11 enters at 11:05;
- C2 does not start P08 until 11:15.

The outage removes only 15 minutes, about 2% of full-window S2 capacity, but it occurs at a high-leverage time when queued work exists and C1 is occupied. It therefore plausibly increases P08’s wait and propagates delay to later C2 work. A precise counterfactual delay cannot be identified: without maintenance, C2 might have started P08 at 10:50, but that depends on non-preemptive dispatch, setup, and assignment decisions that are not observed.

Thus the outage reinforces S2’s mid-morning congestion but does not explain the entire S2 backlog. Longer S2 service demand and sustained S1 releases are also material.

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

The three missing-start scenarios give:

| scenario | missing-start rule | S1 completed mean wait | S1 completed mean service | S1 mean queue / utilization | S2 completed mean wait | S2 completed mean service | S2 mean queue / utilization |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Early-start / queue-optimistic | earliest resource-feasible starts | 63.5 | 28.8 | 4.1 / 84% | 31.8 | 38.2 | 2.1 / 81% |
| Central | feasible midpoints | 65.3 | 27.0 | 4.2 / 79% | 33.8 | 36.3 | 2.2 / 77% |
| Late-start / queue-pessimistic | start at recorded completion | 67.0 | 25.3 | 4.3 / 74% | 35.7 | 34.3 | 2.3 / 73% |

The late-start extreme allows zero active duration. It is a mathematical bound, not a claim that zero-minute service is operationally likely.

**Conclusions stable across the scenarios**

- Missing starts do not change station sojourn, total WIP, arrivals, departures, or completed end-to-end total sojourn.
- S1 has the larger queue and waits during the external-arrival burst.
- S1 drains after noon while S2 retains and accumulates unresolved work.
- S2’s nominal service-time mean remains longer than S1’s under all three missing-start scenarios.
- The time-varying bottleneck conclusion is therefore robust.

**Conclusions that are not stable**

- Exact active-service means and resource utilization depend inversely on the missing-start choice.
- Full-window utilization alone cannot reliably rank the stations: independently combining low S1 busy time with high S2 busy time can reverse their utilization ordering.
- Instantaneous rounded-clock peaks can change by one case when nearby arrivals and completions reverse actual order.

**Right censoring**

- P21 contributes 30 observed S1 service minutes, but its final service duration and departure time are unknown.
- P15 and P17 contribute 30 and 5 observed S2 service minutes; their final service durations have no finite upper bounds.
- P22’s S1 wait and P16/P18/P19/P20’s S2 waits are lower-bounded by their cutoff exposures.
- Completed-only service and sojourn summaries can therefore be downward biased if the open cases are unusually long. Busy-time utilization within the window is still identifiable because only elapsed work before 14:00 is required.

**Rounding**

Rounding affects short waits, service durations, bin assignment, and event ordering. It has less effect on broad conclusions such as the roughly 100-minute late S1 waits, the six unresolved S2 cases, and the post-noon transfer of congestion. Rounding envelopes should not be interpreted as independent probabilistic intervals, especially for adjacent jobs on the same resource.

**Logging alternatives**

Delayed logging or undocumented assistance could invalidate the continuous single-resource constraints used for missing-start bounds. That is a plausible data-quality hypothesis, not an observed fact. If it occurred, the missing-start ranges would need to be widened and resource utilization should be treated as based on recorded ownership rather than physical execution.

    f["resource"] = data[p + "_resource"]

    for i, case in enumerate(f["case"]):
        key = (station, case)
        if key in scenario_starts[scenario]:
            f.loc[i, "start"] = scenario_starts[scenario][key]

    f["entered"] = f["entry"].notna() & (f["entry"] < CUTOFF)
    f["completed"] = f["end"].notna()

    f["queue_end"] = f["start"].where(f["start"].notna(), CUTOFF)
    f["station_end"] = f["end"].where(f["end"].notna(), CUTOFF)

    f["wait_exposure_min"] = np.where(
        f["entered"], minutes(f["queue_end"] - f["entry"]), np.nan
    )
    f["service_min"] = np.where(
        f["start"].notna(), minutes(f["station_end"] - f["start"]), np.nan
    )
    f["wip_exposure_min"] = np.where(
        f["entered"], minutes(f["station_end"] - f["entry"]), np.nan
    )
    return f

def interval_peak(left, right):
    events = []
    for a, b in zip(left, right):
        if pd.notna(a) and pd.notna(b) and b > a:
            events.append((b, 0, -1))  # endings first
            events.append((a, 1, +1))  # beginnings second
    level = peak = 0
    for _, _, change in sorted(events):
        level += change
        peak = max(peak, level)
    return peak

def summarize_station(station, scenario):
    f = station_frame(station, scenario)
    comp = f[f["completed"]]
    busy = f["service_min"].sum(skipna=True)
    capacity = 720 if station == "S1" else 705
    return {
        "scenario": scenario,
        "station": station,
        "arrivals": int(f["entered"].sum()),
        "completed": int(f["completed"].sum()),
        "completed_mean_wait": comp["wait_exposure_min"].mean(),
        "completed_mean_service": comp["service_min"].mean(),
        "mean_queue": f["wait_exposure_min"].sum() / 360,
        "mean_wip": f["wip_exposure_min"].sum() / 360,
        "queue_peak": interval_peak(f["entry"], f["queue_end"]),
        "wip_peak": interval_peak(f["entry"], f["station_end"]),
        "busy_min": busy,
        "util_pct": 100 * busy / capacity,
    }

scenario_summary = pd.DataFrame([
    summarize_station(st, sc)
    for sc in ["early", "central", "late"]
    for st in ["S1", "S2"]
])
print("\nPANDAS STATION UNCERTAINTY SCENARIOS")
print(scenario_summary.round(2).to_string(index=False))

# Resource busy time by scenario. The constrained schedules do not overlap,
# so summing interval durations equals the resource-interval union here.
resource_rows = []
for sc in ["early", "central", "late"]:

            })
resource_result = pd.DataFrame(resource_rows)
print("\nRESOURCE BUSY TIME BY SCENARIO")
