## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

January–March are the control regime. Case volume, variant mix (≈45 / 35 / 20), unknown-activity, queue missingness, AUROC ≈ 0.81, Brier ≈ 0.13, ECE ≈ 0.023, and mean predicted risk all sit on top of the observed breach rate (0.18–0.19). V1/V2/V3 matured rates are flat. Nothing in this window needs a drift story.

**April is a schema/pipeline incident, not model decay.** The 1 April source rename is a documented break at the exact prediction anchor. The metric pattern is the signature of wrong prefixes, not of a harder process: scores jump (0.19→0.28) while the *observed* breach does not (0.19); discrimination and calibration collapse together; unknown-activity, missing queue features, and unmapped variants spike in lockstep (17 / 14 / 18%). Duplicate events until 20 April add a second, narrower contamination. Because the outcome definition did not change and April labels are 100% mature, this month is unsafe for *X*, not for *Y*. Treating April as concept drift would “fix” a mapping bug by moving the decision surface.

**May is a partial repair, not a new regime.** The 6 May patch remapped the activity and backfilled most April *events*, but a cached pre-diagnosis event count was not fully recomputed. Residual unknown-activity (2%), missingness (3%), ECE 0.050, and AUROC 0.78 are what an incomplete lineage replay looks like. Variant mix is almost back to Q1. Do not fold May into either “April is still broken” or “the model has recovered and later months are comparable without caveat.”

**June is a real routing/process-mix change and need not be concept drift.** The 1 June remote-routing policy is visible in the variant table (V2 34%→52%, V1 43%→30%) and in the aggregate breach rate (0.18→0.15). Within-variant matured rates do not move in a way that requires a new *P(Y\|X)*: V2 is 0.09, V1 0.16, V3 0.35. Diagnosis→`Remote Fix` latency is unchanged. The model’s mean prediction stays ~0.20, so ECE rises to 0.060 — a **mix-unaware score** facing a more remote-heavy population, not a broken remote path. AUROC 0.79 is still near baseline. Retraining here would be optional; a mix-aware recalibration or a routing-intent feature is the proportionate response.

**July is the first month in which a second, independent mechanism starts.** The supplier begins missing deliveries on 10 July. The parts edge moves 42 h → 65 h and V3’s matured breach jumps 0.35→0.46, while V1/V2 stay put. Headline observed breach only returns to 0.19 because V2 is still half the book. AUROC slips to 0.76. Label maturity is still 98%, so this is not censoring. July is early **performance-process drift on V3** that, because the score is issued at diagnosis, already presents to the model as **segment-specific concept drift**.

**August–September are an overlap, not one failure.** Three mechanisms stack, and they must be kept separate.

- *Process / performance:* parts wait 65 h → 130 h → 148 h; V3 mix 22% → 35% → 42%. This is in the DFG, not in the confusion matrix.
- *Concept (V3):* matured V3 breach 0.46 → 0.64 → 0.61. Same named path, new outcome rate, prediction point still *before* the wait. V1/V2 matured rates remain historically normal, which is why a single global “the process changed” slogan is false.
- *Population (September only):* Partner Z arrives (24%), is more rural, older-device, and 58% V3. Volume steps 4,180→5,100. Z’s currently observed 0.45 breach is **not** a clean residual: Z labels mature more slowly, so some of that gap is censoring and some is V3 selection.
- *Calibration vs ranking:* ECE 0.120–0.140 and AUROC 0.68→0.66. Recalibration can treat a mean-score bias; it cannot restore ranking once a high-risk segment both grows and changes its label mechanism.
- *Mild label delay in August (93%) and material delay in September (82%)* already color headline rates, but August’s *matured* V3 rate is high enough that delay is not the main August story.

**October is heavily confounded by label maturity and must not be compared to mature months.** Maturity 55%, median delay 52 d, outcome-API backlog biased against long (V3) paths: the observed breach of **0.16 is a selected, early-resolving subset**, not a process recovery. Predicted risk 0.23, AUROC 0.64, ECE 0.130, Brier 0.205 are computed on that selected *Y*. Partner Z is 27% and V3 is 41%; parts wait is still 155 h. A naïve reading (“breach fell, model is over-predicting, retrain/calibrate”) inverts the selection bias. October can support **data-quality and maturity monitors**; it cannot yet support a performance, concept-drift, or fairness conclusion.

Homogeneous-failure is the error to avoid: April is *X*-lineage, June is *policy mix*, July–September are *supplier + V3 concept + (from September) Partner Z*, October is *right-censored evaluation*. Several diagnoses are simultaneously true, and their start dates differ.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

**T1 — Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting.** For each case-start month *m*, plot % of outcomes known and observed breach rate as a function of waiting time *w* (0–90 d). Separately, freeze a model and evaluate each vintage only once it hits ≥95% maturity (and again at 100%). *Expected if October is mostly censoring:* October’s curve starts low (early closes, mostly V1/V2) and rises toward August/September mature V3-heavy rates as long paths label; AUROC/ECE on the mature October vintage move materially from the cutoff snapshot. *Expected if October is true recovery / reverse concept drift:* the maturity curve is flat at ~0.16 and the mature vintage still shows low breach and poor AUROC. *Expected if September’s Z gap is maturity, not risk:* Z and non-Z curves converge as *w* grows.

**T2 — Recompute after activity-label normalization and duplicate cleaning.** Map `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` → `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, drop exact (case, activity, timestamp) duplicates (and near-duplicates within a small clock-skew window), rebuild prefixes at the **normalized** diagnosis event, recompute cached “events before diagnosis,” then rescore April–May. *Expected if April is pipeline failure:* unknown-activity and unmapped collapse, mean predicted risk returns toward 0.19, AUROC/ECE return near Q1. *Expected if April also had real process/concept drift:* quality flags clear but AUROC stays depressed or within-variant breach rates move. *Expected if May residuals are only the unrecomputed cache:* May ECE falls from 0.050 after a full feature replay and not after remap-only.

**T3 — Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source, and month.** Reliability diagrams and AUROC/Brier/ECE on {V1, V2, V3} × {Z, non-Z} × month, **restricted to mature labels**. *Expected under V3-only concept drift:* V1/V2 slices stay near Q1; V3 ECE and AUROC break from July. *Expected under global model failure or pipeline rot:* all slices break together (this is the April pattern). *Expected under Partner Z population shift without residual concept drift:* Z vs non-Z differ in mix and maybe intercept, but V3-Z and V3-non-Z reliability curves overlay. *Expected under label delay:* immature October slices look “good” on V2 and empty/biased on V3.

**T4 — Variant shares and DFG / performance-DFG edges.** Monthly `get_variants`, `discover_dfg`, and `discover_performance_dfg` on **case-start** and **event-calendar** cohorts (they answer different questions; see §5). Track `Order Part → Part Received` and diagnosis → `Remote Fix`, plus unmapped-variant mass. *Expected under June routing drift:* V2 share jumps; remote-fix edge time unchanged. *Expected under supplier performance drift:* parts-edge median steps in July and again in August; V3 count rises as cases wait. *Expected under April pipeline failure:* a spike in unmapped/`UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` edges that disappears after T2, with no corresponding parts-edge move (April parts wait is 34 h, in family with March). *Expected under “one process collapse”:* V1 and remote edges degrade too — they do not.

**T5 — Conditional outcome given the same path (*P(Y\| variant, diagnosis-time X)*).** Fit a simple diagnosis-time risk model (or use the production score) on Jan–Mar, then test month×variant intercepts and interactions on mature labels; alternatively, compare breach rates in propensity-matched diagnosis-time neighborhoods. *Expected under concept drift:* V3 intercept/interaction becomes significant in July–September after controlling for the original *X*. *Expected under pure covariate/mix drift (June):* month intercepts vanish inside variant. *Expected under missing Partner Z features:* a Z residual remains after variant control, and shrinks once rural distance / device age are added. *Expected under pipeline-corrupted X:* April residuals disappear after T2.

**T6 — Population reweighting / transport for Partner Z.** (a) Reweight September–October non-Z cases to Z’s diagnosis-time *X* and variant mix; (b) reweight Z to the August non-Z mix; (c) train on non-Z, evaluate on mature Z (and the reverse). *Expected under pure mix/transportable shift:* reweighted non-Z reproduces Z’s mature breach; the old model is miscalibrated on Z only through features it was never given. *Expected under a new Z-specific concept:* residual remains after reweighting and after adding rural/device features. *Expected under censoring:* transport results flip when restricted to equally mature labels.

**T7 — Feature missingness and lineage audits.** For every derived feature used at the diagnosis prefix (queue length, events-before-diagnosis, activity one-hots), plot missingness, value hash, and source-system version by event date vs case-start date. Confirm whether April backfill rewrote events only or features too, and whether post-20 April dedup is applied retroactively. *Expected under documented pipeline failure:* a cliff on 1 Apr, a partial recovery on 6 May, a second small cliff for records whose cached features were not invalidated. *Expected under real operational missingness:* missingness concentrates in a source or variant and survives remapping. *Expected if later months are still pipeline:* unknown-activity and missing-queue would remain elevated after June — they are not.

Each test is a **discriminator**, not a single yes/no: run T2 before interpreting April in T3–T5; run T1 before interpreting September–October in T3, T5, or T6.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Immediate containment (days).** (1) Mark April scores and any unrepaired May derived features as **invalid for training, calibration, and official monitoring**. (2) Put October — and September Partner Z — under a **maturity hold**: publish only provisional metrics, do not fire retrain jobs from their ECE/AUROC. (3) Normalize diagnosis aliases and replay the feature store, including the cached pre-diagnosis count; apply dedup to the full new-source history, not only after 20 April. (4) Operationally, treat V3 / parts-wait as an **ops page** (supplier), not a model page. Containment is lineage + evaluation hygiene, not a new sklearn fit.

**Short-term recalibration (1–2 weeks, only where ranking still holds).** June is the only clean recalibration candidate: AUROC 0.79, mix shift, stable within-variant rates — a variant-weighted isotonic or a simple intercept shift on the remote-routed segment is enough. Do **not** calibrate on April (you would bake in wrong prefixes). Do **not** calibrate on October (you would bake in early-label selection; the model would learn to under-predict V3). Do **not** globally calibrate August–September: AUROC 0.66–0.68 means the **ranking** is wrong on the growing V3 segment; stretching the score will hide ECE and leave the worst cases misordered.

**Feature repair (parallel, 2–4 weeks).** Repair is higher leverage than another global fit: alias-normalized prefixes; full derived-feature invalidation; a **supplier/lead-time snapshot** available at diagnosis (even a lagging 7-day parts-edge median); routing-policy / intended-variant flag after 1 June; Partner Z source id plus the missing rural-distance and device-age fields. After repair, rerun T2 and only then decide whether April can be rescued as training data.

**Full retraining (after repair + mature vintages).** Retrain when: (a) the feature store has been replayed, (b) July–September (and eventually October) vintages reach ≥95% label maturity, (c) T5 still shows a V3 intercept shift that a supplier feature does not absorb. Use **Jan–Mar, repaired May, Jun, and mature Jul–Aug** as the core training window. **Unsafe until repaired:** April (and May if the cache is still stale). **Unsafe until mature:** October; treat September as partial (down-weight or maturity-filter, especially Z). A single global retrain on “all 2026 labels currently on disk” will mix a pipeline month, a policy-mix month, a supplier-break month, a new population, and a censored month into one confused surface.

**Segment- or variant-specific models.** Justified, but hierarchical rather than a zoo. V1 and V2 have stable matured rates and a stable remote edge — they can share a backbone. V3 after July is a different outcome mechanism at the same prediction point; a V3 head (or a parts-risk booster) is proportionate. Partner Z does **not** yet justify its own model: first add the omitted covariates and re-run T6. If a residual Z gap remains on mature labels after those features, then a source-specific calibration layer is enough to start; a separate Z model is only warranted if T3/T5 show different *slopes*, not just a different intercept.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

Build eight monitors with **hard dependencies**, so a downstream “model quality” alarm cannot autonomously open a retrain ticket.

1. **Data-quality (gate 0).** Unknown-activity share, diagnosis-alias mismatch, duplicate rate, missing queue / cached-feature rate, unmapped-variant share, feature-store vs event-store lineage lag. *Breach ⇒ freeze scoring validity flags and block retrain/recalibrate.* This is the April detector.
2. **Label-maturity (gate 1).** % mature by case-start vintage, median label delay, maturity by variant and by source, Kaplan–Meier-style labeling hazard. *If maturity <95% or delay jumps (October pattern), mark predictive/calibration monitors PROVISIONAL and block retrain.* A label-delay alarm **never** pages the retrain job; it pages data engineering / the outcome API.
3. **Process-variant (gate 2a).** Monthly V1/V2/V3/unmapped shares on the **case-start** cohort; χ² or simple threshold vs a 3-month baseline; annotation channel for known interventions (1 Jun policy, 1 Sep Partner Z). Mix alarms explain ECE movement without implying concept drift (June).
4. **Performance-DFG (gate 2b).** Median/P90 on `Order Part → Part Received` and diagnosis → `Remote Fix`, plus queueing time after diagnosis. This is the supplier detector. It pages ops/supply, and only **informs** the model monitor.
5. **Population.** Partner Z share, rural/device-age moments, volume. New-segment alarm opens a **stratification requirement**, not a fairness verdict.
6. **Predictive (discrimination).** AUROC/PR-AUC on **mature, quality-gated** slices only, by variant and source.
7. **Calibration.** ECE, Brier, mean predicted vs mature observed, again sliced. Allowed to recommend **segment recalibration** only if gate 0–1 are green and AUROC is intact (June-like).
8. **Intervention / deployment.** A change log (rename, mapping patch, routing policy, supplier incident, partner onboarding, outcome-API backlog, model version). Every predictive alert must join this log before action. Retrain requires: DQ green, maturity green, and either (performance-DFG + T5 concept evidence) or a documented population feature gap — not ECE alone.

**Alert dependency (short).** `DQ_fail OR maturity_fail ⇒ suppress model_retrain, suppress global_recalibrate, keep ops pages alive`. `variant_mix_only + AUROC_ok ⇒ recalibrate_or_explain`. `parts_edge_fail + V3_concept_test_fail + mature_labels ⇒ retrain_V3_head_or_add_supplier_feature`. `population_new_segment ⇒ require stratified report; do not emit “biased model” without T6`.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

DIAGNOSIS_CANON = "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
DIAGNOSIS_ALIASES = {
    "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": DIAGNOSIS_CANON,
    "Initial Diagnosis Completed": DIAGNOSIS_CANON,
}
PARTS_EDGE = ("Order Part", "Part Received")
REMOTE_EDGE = (DIAGNOSIS_CANON, "Remote Fix")

def _edge_median(perf_dfg, edge):
    stats = perf_dfg.get(edge)
    if stats is None:
        return None
    if isinstance(stats, dict):
        return stats.get("median") or stats.get("mean")
    return float(stats)

def load_and_clean(path: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    raw = pd.read_csv(path)
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        raw,
        case_id="case_id",
        activity_key="activity",
        timestamp_key="timestamp",
    )
    df["concept:name"] = df["concept:name"].replace(DIAGNOSIS_ALIASES)
    df = df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", "concept:name"])
    # Exact duplicates first; then same-activity collisions inside 1s (migration echoes).
    df = df.drop_duplicates(
        ["case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"], keep="first"
    )
    dt = df.groupby(["case:concept:name", "concept:name"])["time:timestamp"].diff()
    df = df[dt.isna() | (dt > pd.Timedelta(seconds=1))]
    starts = df.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].transform("min")
    df["case:start_timestamp"] = starts
    return df.reset_index(drop=True)

def monthly_process_evidence(path: str, year: int = 2026, end_month: int = 10):
    """Auditable monthly summaries. Event-calendar = what ran that month.
    Case-start = the predictive-monitoring vintage (do not mix the two)."""
    df = load_and_clean(path)
    rows = []
    for month in range(1, end_month + 1):
        start = pd.Timestamp(year, month, 1)
        end = start + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1)
        # Calendar cohort: operations that occurred in-month (edge waits, unknown labels).
        ev = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            df, start.to_pydatetime(), end.to_pydatetime(), mode="events"
        )
        # Start cohort: cases whose lifecycle we would have scored as a monthly vintage.
        started = df[(df["case:start_timestamp"] >= start) & (df["case:start_timestamp"] < end)]
        dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(ev) if len(ev) else ({}, {}, {})
        pdfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(ev) if len(ev) else ({}, {}, {})
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(started) if len(started) else {}
        temporal = (
            pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(started)
            if len(started) else pd.DataFrame()
        )
        n_started = started["case:concept:name"].nunique()
        n_ev = len(ev)
        rows.append({
            "month": start.strftime("%Y-%m"),
            "n_events_calendar": n_ev,
            "n_cases_started": n_started,
            "unknown_activity_share_calendar": (
                float((ev["concept:name"] == "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean()) if n_ev else None
            ),
            "diagnosis_alias_normalized_share": (
                float((ev["concept:name"] == DIAGNOSIS_CANON).mean()) if n_ev else None
            ),
            "unmapped_or_rare_variant_share": (
                1.0 - sum(v for v in variants.values()) / max(n_started, 1)
                if isinstance(variants, dict) else None
            ),
            "top_variants_started": (
                {str(k): int(v) for k, v in sorted(variants.items(), key=lambda kv: -kv[1])[:8]}
                if isinstance(variants, dict) else {}
            ),
            "parts_wait_median_s_calendar": _edge_median(pdfg, PARTS_EDGE),
            "remote_fix_median_s_calendar": _edge_median(pdfg, REMOTE_EDGE),
            "dfg_edge_count_calendar": len(dfg),
            "temporal_feature_cols": list(temporal.columns)[:12],
            "cohort_note": (
                "calendar edges measure in-month work; start cohort measures the scored vintage"
            ),
        })
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

if __name__ == "__main__":
    summary = monthly_process_evidence("field_service_events.csv")
    print(summary.to_string(index=False))
```

Event-calendar numbers answer “what did the shop floor do in August?” (the 130 h parts wait). Case-start numbers answer “what will the August scoring vintage look like once labeled?” Mixing them is how October’s 0.16 observed breach gets mistaken for a recovery. The snippet intentionally does not fit a predictor and does not call a non-existent PM4Py drift detector.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

**Retraining on immature October (and raw September Z) labels.** The outcome API backlog censors long V3 paths, so the observed 0.16 breach is a fast-case sample. A retrain would down-weight parts risk, look better on the current cutoff, and fail when October matures toward August-like V3 rates. *Mitigation:* train and certify only on vintages past a pre-declared maturity threshold; keep a frozen champion until the October vintage is mature; T1 is a release gate, not a dashboard decoration.

**Calibrating around the April pipeline bug.** Isotonic regression on April would learn “our scores are 9 pp too high” because prefixes were built on the wrong/missing diagnosis event, not because base rates moved. After the May remap those calibrators would be systematically low. *Mitigation:* DQ gate 0 blocks calibration; T2 must restore April (or April is dropped) before any score mapping is estimated; never share an April-fitted calibrator with later months.

**Ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept drift (recalibrate or “wait it out”).** V1/V2 are fine; V3 matured breach doubled. A global ECE fix, or a story that “this is only mix,” leaves the diagnosis-time ranker blind to the only segment whose *P(Y\|X)* changed. Capacity and customer promises on parts-dependent jobs keep breaking. *Mitigation:* T4 + T5 run every month; a V3/supplier feature or V3 head is in the short-term path; ops is paged from the performance-DFG even when model retrain is correctly suppressed.

**Attributing Partner Z to unfair model behavior without stratified evidence.** Z is more rural, more V3, and slower to label. A pooled ECE or a raw 0.45 vs 0.27 comparison will read as disparate impact or “the model hates Z,” and may trigger the wrong rebuild (or the wrong fairness intervention) while the actual gaps are omitted covariates, V3 selection, and censoring. *Mitigation:* no fairness or source-bias claim without mature, variant-stratified T3 and a T6 reweight/transport check; add rural distance and device age before interpreting residuals; if a residual remains, prefer a source calibration layer over an accusation.

**Two further failure modes.** (5) *Treating June as concept drift and spending a full retrain* on a policy mix change with stable within-variant rates — mitigation: intervention log + variant monitor; recalibrate or add a routing feature only. (6) *Declaring the entire April–October window a single model failure* and either ripping out the system or averaging all months into one training soup — mitigation: the staged windowing in §4 and the gate graph in §5. The costly error is not picking the wrong single label; it is applying one remedy to four different breaks.
