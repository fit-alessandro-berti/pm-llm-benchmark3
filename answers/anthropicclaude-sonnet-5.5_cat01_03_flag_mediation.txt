## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

**Regimes**
- **P0**: current bundle. The flag drives the priority lane and near-mandatory manual review, and humans still use the score and their own judgment.
- **P_ref**: hypothetical "flag-blind" regime. All flag-triggered actions are off and humans rely on score and judgment, as before the flag existed. This is what "set flag = 0" would mean if it were an intervention. It is not P1.
- **P1**: display of the flag is suppressed, but the score, human information and automated routing are unchanged. It manipulates only the information channel. Humans can still recompute the threshold from the score, so compliance is partial.
- **P2**: keep the lane, drop mandatory review (review becomes discretionary).
- **P3**: keep routing, add protected review capacity for flagged cases.
- **P4**: keep review, change escalation criteria (P4a) or senior capacity (P4b).

**Framework.** I do not claim natural direct or indirect effects (NDE/NIE) of "the flag."
1. The flag is a deterministic function of the score in this sample (flag = 1 iff score  about 0.62). It is not manipulable independently of the score or of the actions it triggers.
2. NDE/NIE need cross-world independence and no treatment-induced mediator-outcome confounders. Queue state, reviewer workload and senior availability violate this.
3. Shared queues violate SUTVA.

Instead I use:
- **Total effects** of policy regimes, defined at system level.
- **Separable effects** of bundle components: display, lane, review rule, review capacity, escalation rule.
- **Controlled direct effects** with the mediator set by rule.
- **Interventional (distributional) indirect effects**, where the mediator is drawn from its regime-specific distribution given (score, complexity, queue state).

Manual wait exists only if review occurs. P3 leaves routing unchanged, so the waiting estimand is not truncated. For P2, waiting is defined only in the always-reviewed stratum.

**Interference and feedback (about 170 words).** Shared triage, review and senior queues mean case i's treatment changes case j's waits, so SUTVA fails. The priority lane pushes unflagged cases back (unflagged triage wait is about 149 min versus about 14 for flagged). Mandatory review of flagged cases lengthens the review queue for later flagged and unflagged cases. Each escalation occupies scarce senior capacity.

Exposure-induced confounding: queue length at triage_done, reviewer workload and senior availability are partly caused by earlier flagged cases' treatment. They in turn affect later cases' wait, escalation and outcome. They are mediator-outcome confounders affected by treatment, which blocks natural effects.

Treatment-confounder feedback: reviewers adapt thresholds to queue state (skipping discretionary reviews when the queue is long, escalating less when seniors are busy), and staffing may respond to flagged volume. U_underlying_risk drives the imperfect score, hence the flag, and also drives discretionary review, escalation and outcome, so flag-review associations remain confounded even within complexity.

Negative-control exposure: placebo cutoffs (e.g. 0.5, 0.75) and T1 vs T2 within the flagged lane | Same as primary | Controls share confounding but no causal path | A discontinuity at placebo cutoffs or a residual difference by flag would show bias | Nonzero effects on controls (confounding or timestamp artifacts such as close batching) |
| Sensitivity to unobserved risk | Tipping-point analysis. Parameterize U_underlying_risk's association with P(review or escalation) and cycle time. E-value or Rosenbaum  for matched pairs. Manski bounds. Calibrate with auditor-adjudicated severity in a random sample | Complexity, score, load, audit proxy | U acts through the parameterized channels | How large an association would nullify the finding | A modest  (e.g. about 1.2) or small E-value nullifies the flagged excess (downgrade the policy story) |
| Interference | Switchback or cluster randomization by shift block or site. Exposure mapping: share of flagged cases among the previous k queue entries. Overall vs spillover effects | Block, weekday, load, staffing roster | Partial interference within a block. Washout removes carryover | Unflagged wait vs flagged-ahead dose-response. Carryover test (first cases after a switch). Placebo on a non-shared resource | Unflagged waits rise by about as much as flagged waits fall (zero-sum). Significant carryover |

## 6. Policy comparison

Numbers below are directional, anchored on the descriptive table and not causally estimated.

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 current | Flagged reach triage completion in about 50 min vs about 206 for unflagged | Flagged review wait about 390 min; escalation wait about 420 min | Flagged about 990 min vs about 490 unflagged (descriptive, confounded) | Baseline | Unflagged triage waits are the cost of the lane | Descriptive only | Baseline for comparison |
| P1 display suppressed | None if routing is automatic; possible loss of lane if lane depends on the display | Sign unclear; reviewers may change discretionary reviews | Near zero to modest | Information hidden, audit and compliance concerns; score is still visible | Small | Very weak | Do not adopt. Use only as a small randomized information test |
| P2 no mandatory review | Unchanged | Lower review arrivals (a fraction of flagged cases skip review); less queue load | Large reduction for de-reviewed cases (hundreds of min) | High: hidden risk missed, and rejects occur only after review and escalation | Frees review and senior capacity | Weak | Not first. Consider later in a low-risk stratum with audit |
| P3 protected capacity | Unchanged | Flagged review wait falls (390 toward about 100–250 if slots suffice); unflagged wait may rise 0–60 min unless work-conserving | Flagged 150 to 300 min; system-level effect depends on spillover | Low (routing and criteria unchanged) | Capacity is reallocated or added; senior queue may see earlier arrivals | Moderate for mechanism, weak for magnitude | Pilot first |

**Pilot: P3, switchback design (250 words)**
- **Eligibility:** flagged cases in participating review pools on weekday shifts. Exclude safety-mandated immediate escalations. Unflagged cases stay in the system as monitored spillover units.
- **Assignment:** randomize 12-hour shift blocks 1:1 to protected vs standard, stratified by weekday and expected load. Analyze cases by arrival block and exclude the first 3 hours after each switch as washout. The protected arm reserves about one-third of review slots for flagged cases and is work-conserving (unused slots revert after 30 min). Review stays mandatory and escalation rules are unchanged, so waiting is manipulated with occurrence held fixed.
- **Guardrails:** unflagged reviewed median wait  baseline +30 min. Senior queue length  a pre-set limit. Automatic reversion on breach.
- **Primary outcome:** block-level mean and P90 arrival-to-close cycle time across all cases.
- **Secondary outcomes:** flagged and unflagged manual wait, escalation rate and wait, triage waits, review service time, reviewer overrides, 30-day audited reversal rate.
- **Minimum data:** queue-entry, start and done timestamps per stage. Resource IDs and rosters. Queue-length snapshots. risk_score, flag, complexity. Override reasons. Audit outcomes.
- **Stopping:**
  - reversal rate up  3 percentage points;
  - two consecutive weeks of guardrail breach;
  - escalation rate shifts > 10 points (reviewer behavior change);
  - senior backlog above the limit;
  - futility if interim CI excludes a 60-min improvement.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk.** My preferred mechanism is "the lane relocates waiting downstream, with occurrence and waiting amplified by a capacity-limited review pool." It would reverse if, with adjudicated or audit-coded severity in the model (or at the RD threshold), the flagged excess in review occurrence, wait and escalation largely disappears. The gap would then be case mix, and P3 would deliver little.
2. **Queue interference.** If the switchback shows that protected capacity raises unflagged waits and system-level mean or P90 cycle time by about as much as flagged waits fall, or that the review pool is not the bottleneck (shift scheduling or the senior queue is), I would drop P3 for P2 or P4b.
3. **Decision quality.** If audits show mandatory review or escalation changes decisions or prevents reversals materially (especially in the moderate-score band), P2 and P4a are harmful and I would keep P3 or P4b. If review changes nothing in a stratum, I would move to P2 there.
4. **Timestamp semantics.** If manual_start is a scheduled slot or late logging rather than first touch, if queue entry is not triage_done, if wait includes unstaffed hours, or if the M2 overlaps reflect data errors, the waiting mediator is mismeasured. The relocation story and the P3 target would need re-estimation with business-time calendars.
5. **Positivity or lack of overlap.** The flag is deterministic in score and Low complexity is never flagged. If the pilot cannot create overlap (all high-complexity cases require review by rule, or no cases near the threshold), P2, P4a and RD results are extrapolations, and the recommendation restricts to the strata with support.
6. **Reviewer behavior change.** If protected capacity or discretionary review changes reviewer thresholds (more escalations when capacity exists, rubber-stamping flagged cases, ignoring the default), the escalation rate and decision mix shift. The waiting gain may then be offset, and I would pivot to P4 and reviewer-training interventions.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed.** In 20 cases (10 flagged), flagged cases start triage about 14 min after arrival versus about 149 for unflagged, yet wait about 390 min for review versus about 113 among the four reviewed unflagged. They are always reviewed (10/10 versus 4/10), escalated more often (6/10 versus 2/10), and close in about 991 versus 490 min. Escalation wait (about 420 versus 390) and the post-stage residual (about 67 versus 71) barely differ. The flag equals score  about 0.62, Low complexity is never flagged, and the flag is collinear with triage staff.

**Plausible mechanism.** The flag is a bundle. The lane speeds triage but does not carry into the review queue. Mandatory review adds occurrence and waiting in a shared, capacity-limited pool (M2 is the busiest), and escalation adds a further senior wait. Waiting is relocated and amplified downstream, and case mix inflates the gap. Unflagged cases probably pay for the lane.

**Unidentified.** Natural direct and indirect effects of "the flag" are not identified: there is no separately manipulable flag, cross-world assumptions are untestable, queue state is an exposure-induced confounder, cases interfere, hidden risk is unmeasured, and decision quality has no ground truth. There is no overlap at low complexity, and n = 20.

**Most useful next evidence.** A switchback pilot of P3 (protected, work-conserving review capacity with mandatory review unchanged). It manipulates waiting with occurrence held fixed and measures spillover on unflagged and senior queues, with audited quality outcomes. It also sets up later stratified P2 and P4 tests.
