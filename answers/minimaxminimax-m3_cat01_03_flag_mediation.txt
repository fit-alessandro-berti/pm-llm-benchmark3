## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

I use a mix of **controlled direct effects (CDEs)**, **interventional indirect effects (IIEs, Vansteelandt/VanderWeele 2014)**, and **separable effects (Ren/Jansson/Rubio)**. The flag is part of a *policy bundle* (priority lane + mandatory review + escalation tendency), so a single "set flag = 0" is not a well-defined manipulation; we instead decompose the bundle. Natural direct/indirect effects are not identified because `manual_review` and `escalation` are post-flag decisions affected by reviewer judgment that is itself exposure-induced (exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounding). Where I need occurrence mediation I use **IIE(p)** — randomizing the mediator at fixed probability `p` rather than deterministically setting it. Where I need waiting-time mediation I use a **separable occurrence/wait decomposition** so that the queue component can be analyzed separately from the occurrence component.

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | P0 vs hypothetical "no-flag-policy bundle" world | case | cycle_time | Total policy effect on cycle time | Yes, conceptually; not point-identified | Hidden risk `U_underlying_risk` shared with flag |
| E2 | P0 vs P2 (drop mandatory manual review) | case | triage_wait | CDE on triage waiting (manual occurrence fixed to 0) | Yes | Priority lane still active under P2; spillover remains |
| E3 | P0 vs randomized "manual review at fixed p=0.3" for all cases | case | cycle_time | IIE(p=0.3) of manual review occurrence | Yes | Need independence of encouragement instrument |
| E4 | P0 vs hypothetical removal of manual-review queue interference (capacity-protected) | case | manual_queue_wait | Separable-effect analogue for queue | Yes, with cluster randomization | Interference across cases in shared queue |
| E5 | P0 vs P4 (new escalation criterion) | case | escalation occurrence and cycle_time | CDE on escalation | Yes | Reviewer-judgment shift post-policy |
| E6 | P0 vs counterfactual "flag retained but priority lane off" | case | triage_wait | Direct priority-lane effect | Marginally | Reviewers may still prioritize informally |
| E7 | P0 vs P1 (display suppression) | case | cycle_time and decision_outcome | Total effect of *display* vs *labeling* | Yes | Hawthorne/behavior change by reviewers |
| E8 | P0 vs P0 restricted to flagged, stratified by complexity | flagged case | cycle_time | Within-flag heterogeneity | Yes | Positivity: no Low-complexity flagged cases |

```
  risk_score computed -> flag (deterministic I[risk_score>tau])

t=triage_start..triage_done:
  priority_lane = flag
  triage_wait ~ f(priority_lane, arrival_load, triage_resource_pool)
  triage_service ~ f(complexity, U_case_difficulty)
  decision_point: send to manual_review? ~ g(flag, complexity, U_reviewer_judgment)

t=manual_start..manual_done (conditional):
  manual_queue_wait ~ f(shared_queue_size, manual_review_occupancy)
  manual_service ~ f(complexity, reviewer_seniority)
  decision_point: escalate? ~ h(manual_review, complexity, U_reviewer_judgment)

t=escalation_start..escalation_done (conditional):
  escalation_queue_wait ~ f(senior_team_capacity, arrival_load)
  senior_service ~ f(complexity)

t=close:
  cycle_time = sum of waiting + service + residual
  decision_outcome ~ f(manual_review, escalation, reviewer_seniority)
```

**Interference and feedback (180 words).** Three sources of identification trouble are present. (i) **Queue interference**: manual-review and senior-team queues are shared, so a flagged case's *presence* lengthens the wait of *other* cases (including unflagged ones), and conversely removing mandatory review reduces load for all — standard SUTVA fails. (ii) **Exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounding**: `U_reviewer_judgment` is influenced by *seeing* the flag (labeling effect), so `manual_review  decision_outcome` is confounded by exposure-correlated judgment; natural indirect effects through `manual_review` are therefore not identified without an instrument. (iii) **Treatment-confounder feedback**: priority triage at `flag=1` also means that the *timing* of triage for flagged cases is earlier, so the queue state experienced at later stages differs systematically by flag; arrival_load is therefore both a confounder and a consequence of the policy. These imply that any "direct effect of flag" must be interpreted as a *policy-bundle* contrast, not a single-node manipulation.

**Interpretation (180 words).** Three contrasts are consistent with "triage accelerates but downstream dominates." (1) Flagged triage wait is ~10× shorter (14.4 vs 148.8 min) while their total cycle is ~2× *longer* (990.6 vs 490.2). (2) When flagged cases enter manual review, their queue wait (390 min) is ~3.5× the unflagged conditional queue (112.5 min), and the gap is largest under High arrival_load. (3) Flagged-high-complexity cases, all of whom are routed to escalation, have ~1344 min total cycle, ~4× unflagged-low. These are not automatically causal because (a) flag correlates with complexity (no Low-complexity flagged cases — positivity failure), (b) High arrival_load coincides with the morning flagged batch, and (c) manual-review *occurrence* is itself a consequence of the flag. The "acceleration" is real at triage; the "delay" is real downstream; the decomposition is consistent with both queue-mediated and occurrence-mediated mediation, which we must distinguish.

| F1: flag  priority_lane  triage_wait (negative) | Priority lane shortens triage queueing | Flagged triage_wait 10× shorter than unflagged | Reviewers do not informally re-prioritize; triage resource pools not pre-empted by other steps | Unflagged cases simply arrive earlier in the queue by chance | P2/P6: keep priority, drop review mandate; P3 with protected triage slots | If priority remains, flagged continue to be fast at triage |

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Is the flag  triage_wait causal? | Regression-discontinuity around `risk_score` threshold with bandwidth selection; compare triage_wait just above and below  | Local linear regression, bandwidth via MSE; adjust for `arrival_load`, calendar time | No manipulation of `risk_score` around ; smooth running variable | Density test (McCrary) on `risk_score`; placebo cutoffs | If wait jumps discontinuously at  only due to bundle (priority lane), effect is real; if no discontinuity despite label change, the display itself does nothing |
| Does manual_review queue wait cause the bulk of cycle delay? | Queue-state-aware analysis: model `manual_queue_wait` as a function of rolling queue length at triage_done; include queue length as time-varying confounder | Stratify by `manual_review` occurrence; compare queue-conditional wait between flags | Queue length is measured or proxied well | Plot residual queue wait vs flag after conditioning on queue length; placebo using unflagged cases only | If flagged-vs-unflagged gap shrinks to ~0 once queue length is controlled, interference is the mechanism |
| Is mandatory review driving delay vs reviewer behavior? | Encouragement design: randomize suppression of flag display (P1 vs P0) within flagged cases | Cluster-randomize by triage shift to mitigate interference | Encouragement does not change underlying routing | Compare (a) display-only and (b) no-display flagged cases on manual_review occurrence and cycle_time | If P1 (suppressed) reduces manual_review occurrence, the *display* itself influences routing (P3/P4 not sufficient) |
| Can we estimate the mediated effect through manual_review? | IIE(p=0.3) on manual_review occurrence; separable occurrence/wait decomposition | Use inverse-odds weighting with `U_reviewer_judgment` instrumented by display suppression | Encouragement instrument is valid | Compare IIE(p) estimates with CDEs at p=0 and p=1; check monotonicity | If IIE estimate exceeds CDE(p=0) by orders, occurrence mediation is large |
| Is there an unobserved-risk confounder? | E-value sensitivity on the flagcycle_time association; sensitivity parameter for `U_underlying_risk` correlation with flag | Assume plausible `U_underlying_risk`–outcome correlation; report E-value | Strong-ignorability after adjusting | If E-value is high (>3), the unobserved-risk explanation is implausible | If E-value low, hidden risk could plausibly explain the gap |
| Does shared-queue interference matter? | Cluster-randomized rollout of P3 (protected manual-review capacity) at triage-resource level; partial interference model | Randomize at shift or resource level; estimate SUTVA-violation magnitude | Partial interference within clusters | Compare within-cluster variance pre/post pilot; spillover test on adjacent shifts | If variance collapses with protected capacity, interference is dominant |
| Is the cycle-time increase actually bad? | Negative-control outcome: a randomly assigned "case id hash" label that should not affect cycle_time | Compare flagged with synthetic label only | Random label has no real effect | If synthetic label predicts cycle_time, residual confounding or drift | If real, the flag bundle includes a confounded component |

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 (current) | Fast for flagged (~14 min wait) | Slow manual queue (~390 min) for flagged; some spillover to unflagged | ~990 min total for flagged | Quality may be appropriate; throughput poor | High — flagged queue is a system bottleneck | Strong on observation, weak on mechanism | Baseline |
| P1 (suppress display) | Likely no change in routing policy, but reviewers may informally de-prioritize flagged | Could worsen if priority lane still active; could improve if reviewers re-route | Uncertain; quality risk highest | Reviewer behavior changes; possible missed high-risk cases | Depends on reviewer discretion | Indirect evidence only | Pilot with monitoring |
| P2 (drop mandatory review) | Triage stays priority | Manual queue shrinks (fewer entrants); remaining flagged cases go faster | Cycle time for flagged likely falls substantially | May miss high-risk cases; compliance risk | Reduced spillover | Medium; mechanism partly understood | Promising but requires guardrail |
| P3 (protected manual-review capacity) | Triage unchanged | Manual queue wait falls most for flagged; smaller spillover effect | Cycle time falls for flagged; small gain for unflagged | Quality preserved | Capacity is the lever | Strongest single-policy evidence | **Pilot candidate** |
| P4 (change escalation criteria/capacity) | Triage unchanged | Senior team wait falls for those still escalated | Cycle time falls for escalated subgroup | Risk of over- or under-escalation | Modest | Medium; depends on criterion design | Pair with P3 |

**Pilot (250 words).** **Policy:** P3 (protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases) with a within-flag encouragement arm to also evaluate P1-like display effects. **Eligibility:** all flagged cases during a 4-week pilot window at two matched sites (one intervention, one control), excluding nights/weekends in week 1 to stabilize operations. **Assignment:** stratified cluster randomization at the *triage-resource shift* level (so interference is contained within a cluster); within each intervention shift, half of flagged cases are randomly assigned to *flag-display suppressed* (P1-like) to disentangle display from routing. **Capacity guardrail:** a minimum of 2 protected manual-review slots per intervention shift, with a hard cap that the protected queue may not exceed 4 hours; if it does, the guardrail triggers and cases spill to the standard queue with a logged exception. **Primary outcome:** cycle time for flagged cases. **Secondary outcomes:** manual_queue_wait, escalation_queue_wait, decision_outcome (Approve/Reject), inter-shift spillover (cycle time for unflagged cases at the intervention site), and a reviewer-behavior audit (rate of discretionary review). **Minimum process data:** all stage start/end timestamps, queue-length snapshots at triage_done and manual_start, reviewer ID and seniority, post-close quality audit on a 10% sample. **Stopping conditions:** (i) intervention-site flagged-cycle-time drops < 20% over baseline by week 2  continue; (ii) spillover cycle-time for unflagged at intervention site worsens by >15%  pause and revisit capacity; (iii) reviewer audit shows display suppression changes routing in >25% of cases  declare P1 effect estimable and extend arm.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk dominates.** If `U_underlying_risk` is substantially higher in flagged cases than `risk_score` captures, the longer cycle is appropriate risk-control; then P2 (drop mandatory review) and P3 (only capacity) would be net-wrong, and P4 with explicit risk re-scoring becomes preferred.
2. **Queue interference is the principal driver, but capacity is misallocated.** If P3 with protected slots does *not* reduce flagged manual_queue_wait by 30% over 4 weeks, then the bottleneck is upstream of capacity (e.g., reviewer availability rather than queueing), and the recommendation should pivot to P4 (escalation criteria + reviewer staffing) rather than P3.
3. **Decision quality is materially worse under shorter cycles.** If a post-close quality audit shows that faster processed flagged cases have higher error/rework rates, then prioritizing cycle-time reduction is wrong; the recommendation reverses toward keeping mandatory review and *adding* reviewer capacity (a hybrid of P3 and P4 with quality guardrails).
4. **Timestamps reflect batch processing rather than continuous flow.** If `triage_done` records a batch checkpoint (e.g., hourly), then observed triage service times are an artifact of the recording scheme, not real processing; the apparent "fast triage" is an artifact, and the whole decomposition changes.
5. **Positivity failure for low-complexity flagged cases.** If, with more data, the absence of Low-complexity flagged cases reflects a true *deterministic* rule (e.g., flag never issued for Low) rather than sampling, then within-flag heterogeneity at low complexity is unidentified and any policy that relies on equal routing across complexity is invalid; this would force a complexity-conditional policy instead.
6. **Reviewer behavior changes when the flag is displayed.** If P1 (suppress display) yields different manual-review rates than P0, the *display* itself drives routing independent of any policy intervention on capacity, so the bundle cannot be cleanly decomposed into priority-lane + review-mandate + escalation components; the preferred intervention becomes a labeling policy (P1-like) rather than a capacity policy (P3).

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed.** Flagged cases spend ~14 min waiting for triage versus ~149 min for unflagged; their triage service is ~36 vs ~57 min; their total cycle time is ~991 vs ~490 min. All flagged cases go through manual review; 6/10 escalate; unflagged rates are 4/10 and 2/10. Stage decomposition is consistent with downstream queueing dominating any triage-stage gain.

**Plausible mechanism.** The flag's policy bundle contains three components: a priority lane (which accelerates triage), a mandatory manual-review trigger (which feeds cases into a shared, capacity-constrained queue), and a tendency to escalate (which feeds a scarce senior team). Shared-queue interference plus the mandatory review rule are the most plausible explanations for the cycle-time penalty; complexity/risk confounding explains part of the association but not the entire queue-gap.

**Unidentified.** Whether the cycle-time penalty is *caused* by the bundle or by reviewers' reaction to seeing the flag (P1 effect) is not identified. Whether the penalty is value-added (better risk decisions) is not identified from this data. The natural indirect effect through `manual_review` is not identified because reviewer judgment is exposure-induced.

**Most useful next evidence.** A P3 pilot with a within-shift P1-like encouragement arm and queue-length instrumentation, as specified in Section 6. It simultaneously estimates (i) capacity as a lever, (ii) display as a lever, and (iii) interference magnitude — three things the current data cannot separately identify.
