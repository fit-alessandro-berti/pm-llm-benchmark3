# Cyber-Incident Adaptation Review: Resilience, Concealed Noncompliance, and Recovery Redesign

## 1. Disruption timeline

**Reading convention.** DFGs are supplied for T0 (baseline), T2 (manual workaround) and T4 (stabilization) only. No DFG exists for T3, so statements about T3 structure are abductive inferences from its aggregate metrics plus the fact that it sits between a fully manual and a mostly systemic control regime. Where I infer rather than observe, I say so.

### T0 — Day -30 to Day -1: controlled steady state

Operational state: the normative order-to-delivery chain runs end to end. 42,000 cases over 21 business days (~2,000/day), model fitness 0.96 with 91% fitting traces. Dominant variant is the happy path `Receive → Validate → Reserve → Pick → Pack → Quality/release → Dispatch → POD → Invoice close`, carrying 36,800 cases to close (87.6% of intake). The exception repertoire is small, *named*, and *inside* the model: Compliance hold (700, of which 520 return to validation), Backorder wait (900 → 600 pick / 300 cancel), Repack/recheck (450 → 400 back to release). Risk posture: **low and visible**. Traceability exceptions 0.8%, cold-chain documentation gaps 0.4%, controlled-substance (CS) dual-control gaps 0.2%, rework 3.5%, cancellation 2.1%. Critically, exceptions are *states in the process*, not evidence gaps discovered later.

### T1 — Day 0 morning: loss of the system of record

WMS unavailability removes, in one step, three different things that are easy to conflate: (a) the *execution* aid (pick directions, routing), (b) the *control* mechanism (authorization enforcement, scan-based verification, dual-control prompts), and (c) the *evidence* mechanism (immutable event capture). The organization successfully substituted (a), partially substituted (b), and largely failed to substitute (c). That single asymmetry explains almost every downstream finding in this case.

### T2 — Day 0 afternoon to Day 2: manual workaround, throughput bought with evidence

Operational state: 5,600 cases arrive (~2,240/day, i.e. *above* baseline daily intake — the demand side did not degrade, and probably rose as customers hedged). Fitness collapses to 0.71 with only 44% fitting traces. Dominant variant is a wholly new chain: `Receive → Emergency triage → {Manual auth | Skip auth | Fast lane} → Manual inventory snapshot check → Manual pick list → Manual pack → (Temporary release control) → Dispatch without scan → Phone POD → Spreadsheet invoice hold`. Every step after "Receive" is a substitute activity; the normative labels essentially disappear.

Service outcome is deliberately and successfully skewed. Hospital-critical median cycle time rises only 8h → 10h (1.25x) and p90 15h → 21h (1.40x), with fill rate held at 91%. Pharmacy-standard median goes 23h → 54h (2.35x), p90 49h → 118h (2.41x), fill rate collapsing to 61%. Wholesale-bulk median 36h → 96h (2.67x), p90 72h → 180h (2.50x). The mission-priority intent is unambiguous in the numbers.

Completion, however, is far worse than the cycle-time medians suggest, because medians are computed on cases that *finished*. Of 5,600 intake, only 2,100 reach any form of financial close (37.5%), and that close is `Spreadsheet invoice hold → END` — an *off-system* terminal state, not `Invoice close`. Meanwhile 1,080 cases are deferred (500 at intake, 580 after snapshot check), 960 enter the Backlog queue, only 420 return to picking, 320 are cancelled, and ~220 are still queued at phase end. Cancellation reaches 7.8% (3.7x baseline) and rework 12.5% (3.6x).

Control coverage is the buried headline. Of 3,600 manually packed cases, only 1,650 (45.8%) pass through Temporary release control; 1,800 (50.0%) go straight from Manual pack to Dispatch without scan with **no release check of any kind** — this is exactly the "missing Quality/release check" move-on-model deviation. Of 3,250 cases dispatched without scan, only 2,600 (80%) obtain even a Phone POD, leaving ~650 cases (11.6% of intake) with *no delivery evidence at all*. And 1,600 of 5,100 triaged cases (31.4%) skipped system authorization entirely, with a further 1,000 fast-lane cases (19.6%) that "sometimes" preceded authorization — so at most 49% of triaged cases received a documented authorization check.

Risk posture: **high, and largely invisible in the operational dashboards**. Traceability exceptions 9.5% (11.9x baseline, ~532 cases), cold-chain documentation missing 4.8% (12x, ~269 cases), CS dual-control missing 2.9% (14.5x, ~162 cases). Note the internal contradiction: pattern F claims Temporary release control covered *all* controlled-substance orders, yet ~162 CS cases lack dual control. Either coverage was not what the narrative claims, or the control was performed without producing evidence. Both readings require action, and the second is the more dangerous because it will pass a self-assessment.

### T3 — Day 3 to Day 6: partial recovery, the most deceptive phase

Operational state: intake system returns, inventory sync and scanning do not. 11,400 cases in 4 days (~2,850/day, 143% of baseline daily rate) — the organization is now absorbing new demand *plus* burning backlog. Fitness improves to 0.82 / 59% fitting. Cycle times improve markedly (Hospital 9h/18h; Pharmacy 38h/90h; Bulk 84h/160h) and exception rates roughly halve (traceability 5.4%, cold-chain 2.3%, CS 1.4%).

Abductively, T3 is a **hybrid mode**: electronic intake and validation restored (so "Skip system authorization check" should have largely died), but picking, dispatch scanning and reservation integrity still manual or unreliable, so evidence-side deviations persist. The absolute numbers show why halving a rate is not recovery: 5.4% of 11,400 is ~616 traceability exceptions, *more cases than the whole of T2's 532*. Rate improvement plus volume ramp produced a net increase in defect creation. This is the phase where "we are recovering" became true operationally and false compliance-wise.

T3 also carries a specific, under-recognized hazard: partially restored systems invite *silent* reliance on wrong data. In T2 everyone knew the system was down; in T3 an unreliable inventory record looks authoritative. Pattern D's staleness problem mutates into a synchronization-error problem, which is harder to detect and is the plausible generator of the 9.0% rework rate.

### T4 — Day 7 to Day 21: operational recovery without compliance recovery

Operational state: 35,000 cases (~2,333/day), fitness 0.90 / 76% fitting. The DFG has re-acquired the normative backbone but permanently grown a *remediation shadow*: `Receive → Backlog prioritization` (1,500; 1,050 to validation, 450 cancelled), `Inventory reservation → Reconcile manual reservation` (1,400; 900 to pick, 300 cancelled), `Dispatch → Proof-of-delivery remediation` (1,200, only 800 closing), `Invoice close → Audit remediation` (2,300, only 2,100 ending — ~200 still open at observation end). Compliance holds nearly double against baseline (1,300 vs 700), consistent with retrospective authorization scrutiny of T2/T3 cases.

Recovery is genuinely uneven across three dimensions:

- **By segment.** Hospital-critical is fully recovered (median 8h = baseline, p90 16h vs 15h, ratio 1.07). Pharmacy-standard is not (median 28h vs 23h; p90 74h vs 49h, ratio 1.51). Wholesale-bulk is far from it (median 58h vs 36h; p90 120h vs 72h, ratio 1.67). The protection hierarchy created in T2 persisted three weeks after the trigger event.
- **By metric family.** Cycle time is ~recovered for the priority segment while compliance rates are not: traceability 2.2% is still 2.75x baseline, cold-chain 0.9% is 2.25x, CS dual-control 0.5% is 2.5x. In absolute terms T4 produced ~770 traceability exceptions, ~315 cold-chain gaps and ~175 CS gaps — the *largest absolute compliance-defect volume of any phase*.
- **By workload visibility.** ~8.3% of closed invoices required audit remediation, and ~400 of 1,200 POD remediations plus ~200 audit remediations remained open. The "audit-remediation queue persisted after operational recovery" line is the whole lesson: the incident ended for operations and continued for quality, finance and regulatory affairs.

Cumulative incident footprint (T2+T3+T4, 52,000 cases): roughly 1,918 traceability exceptions (~1,500 above baseline expectation), ~846 cold-chain documentation gaps (~640 excess), ~497 CS dual-control gaps (~370 excess). Those ~370 excess CS cases and ~640 excess cold-chain cases are the population that a GDP inspector, a DEA/CSA-equivalent authority, or a product-liability claim would examine.

**One risk nobody in the pattern list owns.** Recall-sensitive product appears in the process context but in *no* adaptation pattern. Printed snapshots do not carry quarantine, hold, or recall flags; manual pick lists with reconstructed scans do not reliably preserve lot/batch-to-customer linkage. Therefore between T1 and roughly Day 7 the firm probably could not have executed a recall with documented effectiveness, and it may have shipped stock that the WMS had flagged as quarantined. This capability loss produces **zero** signal in any KPI reported here.

## 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

### The distinction

Deviation from the baseline model is not the discriminator — in T2 fitness was 0.71 and *should* have been, because the baseline model presumes a working WMS. Judged against the baseline, every adaptation looks like a violation; judged against nothing, every throughput save looks like heroism. The useful test is against the **control objectives** the baseline model happened to implement, plus the **evidence** that those objectives were met.

An adaptation is **resilience** when all four hold:

1. **Objective preservation** — the control's purpose (eligibility, product integrity, chain of custody, delivery proof) is still achieved, by a different mechanism.
2. **Contemporaneous evidence** — the achievement is recorded *as it happens*, in a form a third party can verify.
3. **Bounded and declared** — scope, duration and authority are explicit; someone owns it and someone can stop it.
4. **Truthful signalling** — the adaptation makes its own residual risk visible; downstream metrics are not made to look better than reality.

An adaptation is **concealed noncompliance** when throughput or lead time is preserved by *deleting* a control or its evidence, especially when the deletion is invisible in the operational KPIs and only surfaces later as remediation work. The word "concealed" is doing real work here: the concealment need not be intentional. In this case it was structural. Cycle time and fill rate were measured continuously; control completeness was measured after the fact. A system that reports speed in real time and compliance in arrears will *always* look resilient while it is degrading.

Two corollaries specific to this incident:

- **Throughput preserved with controls intact is resilience; throughput preserved by control deletion is a loan.** T2 "delivered" 3,250 dispatches and repaid it with ~532 traceability exceptions, ~162 CS gaps, ~269 cold-chain gaps, 12.5% rework, 1,200 POD remediations and a 2,300-case audit queue that outlived the incident. The interest rate was high and the repayment schedule was set by regulators and customers, not by the firm.
- **After-the-fact remediation is bookkeeping, not compliance.** Reconstructing a scan record establishes what was probably true; it does not establish that a pharmacist assessed the release, that a customer was licensed for a controlled substance at the moment of shipment, or that a cold-chain leg stayed in range. Some facts are not recoverable, and pretending otherwise is precisely how a resilience story becomes an integrity finding.

### Classification for this case

**True resilience (objective preserved, evidence mostly preserved, intent legitimate)**

- **B. Manual authorization check.** The clean case: same objective, degraded mechanism, human accountability, 49% of triaged volume. Its problem is capacity, not legitimacy.
- **F. Temporary release control**, *as applied to controlled substances*. Deliberate risk-based rationing of a scarce specialist control, and the CS gap (2.9%) really did stay below the general traceability gap (9.5%). Its cold-chain omission is a separate failure, treated below.
- **J. Backlog prioritization**, *if* the priority rule can be evidenced. The recovery-ordering pattern across segments is coherent and mission-consistent; only its formality is in doubt.
- **K. Reconcile manual reservation** and **L. Audit remediation** are **necessary but insufficient** resilience — genuine repair capability that the firm deserves credit for, disqualified from full marks by their timing. Repair that runs only after the dashboard turns green is by construction late.

**Risky shortcuts / concealed noncompliance (control or evidence deleted, invisible in operational metrics)**

- **C. Skip system authorization check.** Substituting an assumption for a control, unrecorded, at 31.4% of triaged volume, with unverifiable scope. No risk appetite justifies this for regulated product, and the ambient-only claim cannot be evidenced.
- **G. Dispatch without scan.** The largest evidence deletion, and the one that stacks: 1,800 cases carry both no release check and no dispatch record. It severed chain of custody for exactly the product classes where chain of custody *is* the compliance obligation.
- **D. Manual inventory snapshot check, beyond the first ~12 hours.** Reasonable at hour 2, indefensible at hour 60, and hazardous throughout because the substitute silently discarded quarantine/hold/recall status. This is the pattern most likely to have caused an actual product-safety event rather than a documentation one.
- **The unlettered pattern: Spreadsheet invoice hold → END.** 2,100 cases terminating in an off-system financial state deserves naming. It is a financial-control and revenue-recognition exposure (billing on unverified delivery, receivables outside the system of record), and it also *hides* the compliance gap inside a finance artifact where quality assurance will not look.

**Ambiguous — resolvable only by stated risk appetite and by evidence the firm does not currently have**

- **A. Hospital-critical fast lane.** The prioritization is almost certainly right; the authorization bypass almost certainly was not necessary to achieve it. Ambiguous today because we cannot tell how much of the 1.40x-vs-2.50x protection came from queue position (legitimate) versus from control removal (not). Resolve by drill: if a lane with mandatory offline authorization still holds hospital p90 near 20h, the bypass was never needed.
- **E. Manual pick list.** Keeping physical flow with paper is exactly what resilience looks like; "scans reconstructed later" is exactly what concealment looks like. Same pattern, two faces. Ambiguity collapses once lot/batch capture is contemporaneous.
- **H. Phone POD.** Defensible for ambient pharmacy volume, indefensible for controlled and cold-chain product, and its 80% capture rate hides a 650-case zero-evidence subpopulation. Ambiguity is really product-class dependence.
- **I. Defer noncritical order.** Legitimate triage in principle, but "noncritical" was asserted rather than assessed, deferral had no expiry, and a third of the backlog queue silently became cancellations. Whether this is stewardship or unilateral rationing depends entirely on whether customers were told and whether the rule was pre-agreed.
- **F. Temporary release control, as applied to cold-chain.** Listed here as well as above deliberately: the same adaptation was resilience for one hazard class and concealed noncompliance for another. Adaptations should be judged per product class, not per pattern name.

The pattern across all three groups is consistent: **the firm was good at substituting execution, adequate at substituting judgement, and poor at substituting evidence.** Every true-resilience item preserved a human decision. Every concealed-noncompliance item deleted a record. The redesign below is therefore weighted heavily toward offline evidence capture, because that is the specific capability that failed.

## 4. Recovery process model

### 4.1 Notation

I use a process-tree / POWL-style notation with these operators, then expand the leaf activities as BPMN-style bullets.

- `→(a, b, c)` — **sequence**: a then b then c.
- `×(a, b)` — **exclusive choice**: exactly one branch, taken on the stated guard.
- `∧(a, b)` — **concurrency / partial order**: both occur, order unconstrained.
- `↺(a, b)` — **loop**: do `a`; optionally do `b` and repeat `a`.
- `τ` — **silent step** (legitimate skip).
- `[G]` — **guard** on a branch; `⟨X⟩` — **role** accountable for the step.
- `⟂` — a partial-order edge: the target may start before the source finishes, but may not *complete* before it.

Two design rules are embedded structurally rather than left to discipline:

- **No control may be silently skipped.** Every bypass is modelled as an explicit choice into a *named* compensating activity that emits evidence. There is no `τ` branch past a mandatory control for regulated product.
- **Evidence-completeness gates money and closure, not dispatch.** Regulated cases may ship under degraded mode, but they may not *close* until evidence exists. This keeps throughput while forcing the debt to be visible and bounded.

### 4.2 Top-level model

```
DegradedModeRecovery =
  →( M0_Detect_and_Declare,
     M1_Mode_Switch,
     ↺( ∧( M2_Intake_and_Triage,
            M3_Degraded_Fulfilment,
            M4_Continuous_Reconciliation,
            M5_Incident_Control_Tower ),
        M6_Mode_Reassessment ),
     M7_Exit_Gate,
     M8_Restoration_and_Closure )
```

`M4` runs *concurrently* with fulfilment — the single most important structural change from what happened, where reconciliation (K) and remediation (L) were sequential successors of recovery. `M6` is the loop body: the mode is re-derived at fixed intervals, so degraded mode cannot persist by inertia (which is how snapshot D aged to 60 hours).

### 4.3 M0/M1 — Explicit trigger conditions and mode declaration

Declaration is a decision with a named owner, not an emergent state. Three trigger tiers:

- **Tier 1 (Degraded-Assisted)** — declared when *any* of: WMS or scanning unavailable >30 min; inventory-accuracy confidence below threshold; order-intake unavailable >30 min; loss of temperature-monitoring telemetry; a security containment action that isolates warehouse systems.
- **Tier 2 (Degraded-Manual)** — declared when *any* of: WMS unavailable with no ETA <4h; both scanning and inventory sync unavailable; evidence-debt inflow projected to exceed the remediation capacity ceiling; a confirmed cyber incident with unknown blast radius (which is a data-*integrity* trigger, not just an availability trigger — restored-but-untrusted data is a Tier 2 condition, and this is exactly what made T3 dangerous).
- **Tier 3 (Protective Stop)** — declared when controls cannot be met for a product class even manually: no pharmacist coverage, no offline authorization extract, no lot/batch capture capability, or suspected data manipulation. Tier 3 stops *that product class only* — it is a scalpel, not a shutdown.

`M1_Mode_Switch` steps, all time-boxed to 60 minutes from declaration:

- Notify: Incident Commander, Responsible Person / QP, Qualified Pharmacist, Security, Finance, Customer Service, and — per pre-agreed protocol — the regulator and key hospital customers. Regulatory notification is a *step in the process*, not an afterthought.
- Activate pre-staged artifacts (see 4.6): offline authorization extract, stock extract with status flags, pre-numbered batch records, offline handhelds, paper POD books, courier manifest forms.
- Open the **Evidence Debt Register** and start the clock. Nothing else may dispatch until this register exists.
- Publish the allocation policy version and the priority score definition to Customer Service (this is what makes J and I defensible).
- Physically segregate all quarantined, on-hold, recalled and short-dated stock into a locked zone, because paper picking cannot see status flags. This one physical action substitutes for the system control that D silently lost.

### 4.4 M2 — Intake and triage rules

```
M2_Intake_and_Triage =
  →( Receive_order,
      Classify_case( customer_tier × product_risk × clinical_urgency ),
      Compute_priority_score ⟨Control Tower⟩,
      ×( [Lane_1_Life_Critical]      Route_expedite_full_controls,
         [Lane_2_Standard]           Route_normal,
         [Lane_3_Deferrable]         →( Allocate_partial, Notify_customer, Park_with_expiry ),
         [Lane_4_Blocked]            →( Protective_hold, Notify_customer, Offer_alternative ) ) )
```

`Compute_priority_score = w1·clinical_urgency + w2·customer_tier + w3·product_risk + w4·age_in_backlog + w5·contractual_commitment`, with weights fixed *before* the incident, versioned, and written onto every case. Explicit rules:

- **Lane 1** is reserved for clinically urgent lines regardless of customer tier — a community pharmacy's oncology or emergency line outranks a hospital's routine ambient restock. This corrects the segment-proxy error implicit in A and I, where "Hospital-critical" and "clinically critical" were treated as the same thing.
- **Lane 1 accelerates queue position only.** It cannot remove a control. Formally, there is no branch from Lane 1 that bypasses `Authorization_check`.
- **Lane 3 parking carries a mandatory expiry** (e.g. 24h Tier 1 / 48h Tier 2), on which the case is re-scored and either promoted or explicitly cancelled *with customer contact*. This kills the silent 960-in / 420-out backlog queue.
- **Lane 3 prefers partial allocation to full deferral**, since a pharmacy's minimum viable quantity often costs little capacity but prevents a stock-out at the patient.
- **Lane 4** is the Tier 3 scalpel: better a declined order with an alternative than a shipped order that cannot be evidenced.
- Every routing decision writes `case_id, score, lane, decider, timestamp, policy_version`. This is what turns J from "appears resilient" into demonstrably fair.

### 4.5 M3 — Minimum controls by product and customer risk

The core of the redesign: a **control floor** that is invariant to system availability. Mechanisms may degrade; the objective may not.

| Product class | Authorization | Stock-status verification | Release check | Custody / dispatch evidence | Delivery evidence | Degraded-mode dispatch allowed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ambient, standard customer** | Offline signed extract, or documented callback; never assumed | Segregation-zone check + extract ≤12h | Sampled (≥10%), documented | Offline scan **or** single-signature manifest with lot/batch | Phone POD ≤4h with caller/receiver ID, or paper POD | **Yes** |
| **Cold-chain** | Offline extract mandatory, no callback substitute | Extract ≤12h + physical expiry/status check | **100%**, pharmacist or trained delegate | Offline scan **or** two-person manifest; monitor ID and pack-out temperature recorded | Paper POD signed by consignee **plus** monitor readout at receipt | Yes, only with temperature evidence attached at dispatch |
| **Controlled substance** | Offline extract **plus** licence-validity confirmation, dual-signed | Physical count against register, dual-control | **100%**, pharmacist, dual-signed | **Mandatory** two-person signed manifest with seal number; no scan-free, evidence-free handoff | Paper POD, named consignee signature, ID checked | Yes, only with complete custody chain; otherwise Lane 4 |
| **Recall-sensitive** | Offline extract | Extract ≤12h **plus** mandatory recall-hotlist check against the segregation zone | **100%**, with lot/batch recorded on the release record | Lot/batch-to-consignee link captured contemporaneously and legibly | Paper or phone POD **plus** confirmed lot/batch linkage | Yes, only if lot→customer linkage is captured at pick time |
| **Any class, life-critical clinical need** | Never waived; may be performed by on-call RP within 30 min | Segregation-zone check | 100% | Two-person manifest | Any POD within 4h | Yes, with time-boxed control performance |

Three structural devices make the floor enforceable rather than aspirational:

- **`Ship_to_quarantine`** — a legitimate third option between "ship" and "hold". Product physically moves to the customer's quarantine area, embargoed from dispatch to patients until evidence completes. This is how a genuine clinical emergency is served without pretending the control happened.
- **Dual-competency delegation** — pre-trained, pre-qualified deputy checkers (B's capacity constraint is solved before the incident, not during it). If B had had capacity, C would probably never have been invented.
- **Throughput throttling on evidence debt** — if open evidence debt exceeds a declared ceiling, intake into Lanes 2/3 is *deliberately slowed*. Formally: `[evidence_debt > ceiling] → Throttle_intake`. This is the mechanism that prevents the T3 failure mode where improving rates plus rising volume produced record absolute defect counts.

### 4.6 M3b — Manual evidence capture

```
M3_Degraded_Fulfilment =
  →( Authorization_check⟨Compliance/RP⟩,
      Stock_status_verification,
      ∧( Pick_with_batch_record⟨Picker⟩, Capture_lot_batch⟨Picker⟩ ),
      Pack_with_evidence⟨Packer⟩,
      ×( [class ∈ {CS, cold, recall} ∨ sample] Release_check⟨Pharmacist⟩,
         [class = ambient ∧ ¬sample] Documented_sampling_waiver ),
      Custody_handoff( offline_scan × two_person_manifest ),
      ×( Deliver_and_POD, Ship_to_quarantine ),
      Evidence_completeness_check )
  ⟂ Evidence_Debt_Register_entry
```

The `⟂` edge means: the register entry may be opened at any point, but the case cannot *complete* before the register entry is resolved or explicitly accepted as debt with an owner and due date.

Capture standards, chosen specifically to close the T2 gaps:

- **Pre-printed, pre-numbered, dual-purpose batch records** — one document per order carrying order ID, customer, lot/batch, quantity, picker, checker, timestamps, and signature blocks. Sequential numbering makes missing records detectable, which is the property T2 completely lacked (740 cases vanished between pick and pack with no trace).
- **Offline barcode capture with store-and-forward** — handhelds that scan into local storage and sync on restoration. This is the highest-value single investment: it makes G unnecessary while preserving G's speed, and it turns E's "reconstructed scans" into real ones.
- **Two-person signed courier manifest** as the fallback when even offline scanning is down: seal number, lot/batch, case IDs, courier name and signature, both warehouse signatures, time. Photograph each completed page immediately to a controlled store, so evidence survives paper loss.
- **Contemporaneous, not reconstructed.** A record created at the moment of the act is evidence; one created on Day 12 is a reconstruction and must be *labelled* as such in the register. The distinction must survive into the audit trail, because a reconstruction that looks like a scan is worse than a visible gap.
- **Structured phone POD** — pre-printed form with caller, receiver name and role, time of delivery, time of call, condition on arrival; entered within 4h; ambient only.
- **Every deviation is a first-class record**, not an absence. "Skipped because X, authorized by Y, compensating control Z" is auditable; a missing event is not. This alone converts most of T2's concealed noncompliance into declared, defensible degraded operation.

### 4.7 M4 — Concurrent reconciliation (moved from "after" to "during")

```
M4_Continuous_Reconciliation =
  ↺( ∧( Sync_offline_captures,
         Blind_cycle_count( high_risk_SKUs ),
         Reconcile_reservations_vs_physical,
         Age_and_triage_Evidence_Debt_Register,
         Refresh_offline_stock_extract ),
     Wait( ≤12h ) )
```

Running every ≤12 hours during the incident, this directly prevents four observed failures: the 60-hour stale snapshot (D), the 1,400-case reservation divergence discovered only in T4 (K), the 300 late cancellations that hit customers a week after the fact, and the 2,300-case audit queue that outlived operational recovery (L). A reconciliation cell of 3-5 people from hour 1 is far cheaper than a 21-day remediation programme.

### 4.8 M6/M7 — Mode reassessment and exit gate

`M6_Mode_Reassessment` runs at fixed intervals (≤12h) and can move the mode in either direction. Downgrading from manual to assisted mode requires *positive evidence of data integrity*, not merely system availability — the T3 lesson. Specifically: inventory accuracy verified by sampled blind counts, scan capture verified end-to-end on test orders, and authorization data confirmed current.

`M7_Exit_Gate` — the process does not exit degraded mode on cycle time. Exit requires **all** of:

- Operational: cycle-time p90 within 1.2x baseline **per segment**, including Wholesale-bulk. (In this incident bulk was 1.67x on Day 21 and would correctly have kept the incident open.)
- Compliance: traceability exceptions, cold-chain and CS gap rates within 1.25x baseline for two consecutive days.
- Conformance: fitting traces ≥85% and fitness ≥0.93.
- Evidence: Evidence Debt Register open items below a defined floor, **zero** open items for CS, cold-chain and recall-sensitive product, and no item older than its SLA.
- Integrity: inventory accuracy verified; reservation reconciliation backlog cleared.
- Fairness: no customer segment's p90 inflation exceeds the priority segment's by more than a declared factor; no parked order older than its expiry.
- **Recall drill passed under current conditions** — a live lot-trace test proving the traceability chain is genuinely intact. Without this, the recall-execution capability loss stays invisible exactly as it did here.

### 4.9 M8 — Restoration, remediation, and honest closure

```
M8_Restoration_and_Closure =
  →( Backlog_prioritized_drain⟨priority score⟩,
      ↺( Remediate_evidence_item,
         ×( [recoverable] Attach_contemporaneous_evidence,
            [reconstructable] Attach_labelled_reconstruction,
            [unrecoverable] →( Raise_deviation, Risk_assess⟨RP/QP⟩,
                                ×( Accept_with_justification,
                                   Notify_customer,
                                   Market_action_assessment,
                                   Product_recall_or_return ) ) ) ),
      Release_financial_close,
      Regulatory_reporting_decision,
      CAPA_and_model_update )
```

Two non-negotiables here, both aimed at the trap in L:

- **`Release_financial_close` comes after remediation, not before it.** Regulated lines cannot reach `Invoice close` while evidence is pending; they sit in an explicit `Evidence_pending` state visible on the operational dashboard. This removes the `Invoice close → Audit remediation` edge from the process entirely, and with it the illusion of a closed case.
- **The `[unrecoverable]` branch must exist and must be used.** Some T2 cases genuinely cannot be evidenced — an unmonitored cold-chain leg is not recoverable by any amount of paperwork. Those cases require a risk assessment with a named accountable person and a real decision, up to and including customer notification and market action. A process without this branch will always resolve unrecoverable gaps by quiet closure, which is the finding that ends careers.

## 5. Resilience indicators

Indicators are split by *when they inform a decision*. The central failure in this incident was an indicator-timing failure: the operational metrics were leading, the compliance metrics were lagging, and management therefore steered on the half of the picture that looked good.

### 5.1 Leading indicators — observable during the incident, drive intervention

**Throughput and capacity**

- Completed cases per hour by lane, vs degraded-mode capacity plan.
- **Intake-to-completion ratio per phase.** T2 completed 2,100 of 5,600 (37.5%) vs 87.6% at baseline. Median cycle time on completed cases concealed this entirely; this ratio would have exposed it on Day 0.
- WIP by stage, with **orphaned-WIP count**: cases in a stage with no successor event beyond a threshold. T2 had ~740 stranded between pick and pack, ~220 in backlog queue, ~120 unaccounted at deferral. Alarm on any nonzero orphan count for regulated product.

**Service protection**

- Fill rate by segment and by clinical urgency, hourly.
- **Lane-1 promise-kept rate** — share of life-critical lines met within the degraded-mode commitment.
- Stock-out-at-patient escalations from pharmacies (the signal that "noncritical" deferral was misjudged; entirely absent from what was measured here).

**Traceability and control completeness — the indicators that did not exist in T2**

- **Control-coverage rate**: share of dispatched cases with a completed release check. T2 = 45.8%. Target 100% for CS, cold-chain and recall-sensitive; ≥ sampling plan for ambient.
- **Authorization-evidence rate**: share with a documented authorization artifact. T2 ≤ 49%. Target 100%.
- **Custody-evidence rate**: share with scan or signed manifest. T2 ≈ 0% for the 3,250 scan-free dispatches. Target 100%.
- **Delivery-evidence rate**: T2 = 80% of dispatched cases; 650 with nothing. Target 100% within the capture window.
- **Stacked-deviation count**: cases missing ≥2 mandatory artifacts. This is the metric that finds the 1,800 pack-to-scanless-dispatch population that phase-level rates dissolve. Any stacked deviation on a regulated line is a Tier 3 candidate.
- **Evidence-debt inflow rate** (items/hour), **open balance**, and **age distribution (p50/p90/max)** by product class. This is the single best real-time proxy for concealed noncompliance and should sit on the incident dashboard next to throughput, with equal prominence.
- **Debt-to-throughput ratio**: evidence-debt items created per 100 dispatches. Rising ratio means throughput is being financed by control deletion — the precise definition of the failure mode to be prevented.

**Data integrity**

- **Offline extract age at time of use** (hours). Alarm >12h, hard stop >24h. Would have capped D.
- Offline-capture sync lag; unsynced-record count.
- Blind cycle-count variance on high-risk SKUs; reservation-vs-physical divergence.
- **Segregation-zone integrity**: any pick that touched quarantined, recalled or expired stock. Target zero; this is a safety indicator, not a documentation one.

**Backlog and fairness**

- Backlog age p50/p90/max by segment; oldest parked order.
- **Parked-order expiry breaches** — parked past their re-scoring deadline.
- **Deferral-to-cancellation conversion rate**. T2 ran 320/960 = 33% via the backlog queue; anything above ~10% means deferral is functioning as undisclosed cancellation.
- **Priority-score audit rate**: share of routing decisions with a logged score and policy version. Target 100% — this is the evidence that makes J defensible.
- Customer notification rate on deferral/partial allocation. T2: unknown, likely low.

**Control capacity**

- Pharmacist and compliance hours available vs required per 100 regulated lines. Falling below 1.0 predicts the emergence of C-type shortcuts within hours; treat it as an early-warning trigger for calling in deputies or invoking Tier 3.

### 5.2 Lagging indicators — confirm outcomes, drive post-incident action

**Compliance outcome**

- Traceability exception rate, cold-chain documentation gap, CS dual-control gap — by phase, product class **and in absolute case counts**. Rates alone mislead: T4's 2.2% produced ~770 exceptions, more than T2's 9.5% produced (~532). Always report both.
- **Compliance recovery lag** — days from operational recovery (cycle time within tolerance) to compliance recovery (gap rates within 1.25x baseline). Here: cycle time recovered for hospital by ~Day 7, compliance was still 2.25-2.75x baseline at Day 21, so the lag exceeded 14 days and was still open. This is the headline lagging indicator for this class of incident.
- **First-pass evidence-complete rate** — cases closed with complete contemporaneous evidence, no remediation. ~91.7% at T4 (2,300 remediations / 27,800 closes); baseline should be ≥99%.
- **Unrecoverable-gap count** — cases where evidence could never be reconstructed, split by product class. The population that drives deviations, CAPA, notification and market action. Never aggregate this into a percentage; report the count and the case list.
- **Reconstruction share** — remediated evidence that is reconstruction rather than recovered contemporaneous record. High share means the audit trail is a narrative, and an inspector will treat it as one.

**Conformance and process health**

- Fitness and % fitting traces per phase, against a **degraded-mode reference model** as well as the normative one. Judged against the normative model T2 scores 0.71 and looks like chaos; judged against the degraded-mode model it should score ≥0.90, and the gap tells you whether teams followed the *plan* or improvised. Without a degraded-mode model, conformance checking cannot distinguish resilience from disorder — which is why T2's 0.71 is uninformative on its own.
- Rework rate (T2 12.5%, T3 9.0%, T4 5.8% vs 3.5% baseline) and cancellation rate (7.8% / 5.2% / 3.0% vs 2.1%), with root-cause attribution to stale-stock decisions vs deferral vs reconciliation.
- Persistence of remediation edges: `Reconcile manual reservation`, `POD remediation`, `Audit remediation` volumes trending to zero. T4 ended with ~400 POD and ~200 audit items open — the incident was not closed.

**Fairness across customer groups**

- **Segment p90 inflation ratio** (phase p90 ÷ baseline p90): T2 hospital 1.40 / pharmacy 2.41 / bulk 2.50; T3 1.20 / 1.84 / 2.22; T4 1.07 / 1.51 / 1.67.
- **Protection spread** = max ratio ÷ min ratio: 1.79 in T2, 1.85 in T3, 1.56 in T4. Persistently above 1.5 through T4 says the burden allocation was never unwound. Publish an ex-ante acceptable ceiling (e.g. 2.0 during Tier 2, 1.3 by exit) so the trade-off is a decision rather than a residue.
- **Recovery-order fairness**: whether the observed sequence (hospital → pharmacy → bulk) matches the published policy. Here the outcome looks principled; the policy to compare it against did not exist.
- Commitment-kept rate, credit notes, complaints, and customer churn by segment. Pharmacy fill rate of 61% will show up in the churn number one to two quarters later.

**Financial and regulatory**

- Value invoiced on incomplete evidence (the 2,100 spreadsheet-hold closures plus the 2,300 remediated closes).
- Days-sales-outstanding impact from weak POD; disputed invoices attributable to phone POD.
- Regulatory notifications made vs required; inspection findings; deviations and CAPAs raised; **recall-drill success rate under degraded mode**, which doubles as the only real test of whether the traceability chain survived.

## 6. Recommendations for future disruptions

Prioritized by risk reduction per unit of effort. Effort is indicative (S ≤ 1 month, M 1-3 months, L 3-6 months).

**P1 — Do first; these close the gaps that actually caused harm**

1. **Offline authorization capability** (effort M). Daily signed extract of customer × product authorizations, licence validity and quantity limits, held offline and encrypted, plus a same-day revocation hotlist and a 30-minute on-call RP path. *Benefit:* eliminates the largest authorization gap (1,600 skipped cases, 31.4% of triage) and removes the pressure that produced C, while keeping A's speed. *Residual risk:* revocations within the refresh window; mitigated by the hotlist and by callback for CS. Small.
2. **Offline evidence capture kit** (effort M, some capex). Store-and-forward handhelds, pre-numbered batch records, courier manifest books, paper POD forms, photograph-to-controlled-store workflow; stored in the warehouse, tested quarterly. *Benefit:* highest single-point return — makes G unnecessary, converts E from reconstruction to contemporaneous record, and directly attacks the 9.5% traceability and 4.8% cold-chain gaps. *Residual risk:* device failure or non-use under pressure; mitigated by the paper manifest fallback and drills. Low-moderate.
3. **Control floor by product/customer risk, pre-approved** (effort M, mostly governance). The §4.5 matrix, endorsed internally by the RP/QP and pre-discussed with the regulator, defining exactly what may degrade and what may not, plus `Ship_to_quarantine` and Tier 3 protective stop as legitimate options. *Benefit:* converts improvisation into pre-authorized degraded operation; prevents 50% of packs shipping with no release check; makes hard stops usable without heroics. *Residual risk:* the floor is refused in the moment under commercial pressure; mitigated by making Tier 3 an RP authority that commercial cannot override. Low.
4. **Physical segregation of quarantined / recalled / on-hold / short-dated stock, permanently** (effort S). *Benefit:* extremely cheap, and it substitutes for the status flags that paper picking loses — the mechanism most likely to have caused a real product-safety event rather than a paperwork one. *Residual risk:* stock mis-segregated before the incident; mitigated by routine audit. Low.
5. **Evidence Debt Register as a first-class, real-time KPI, with throughput throttling** (effort S-M). Register opened at declaration, aged by product class, displayed beside throughput, with a declared ceiling that throttles Lane 2/3 intake when breached. *Benefit:* makes concealed noncompliance visible while it is being created rather than 21 days later; prevents the T3 pattern where improving rates plus rising volume set an absolute defect record. *Residual risk:* the register itself is manually maintained and can be gamed; mitigated by owner assignment and post-incident audit sampling. Low.

**P2 — Do next; these fix timing, fairness and closure**

6. **Concurrent reconciliation cell from hour 1** (effort S-M, staffing). 3-5 people running the §4.7 loop every ≤12h. *Benefit:* prevents the 1,400-case reservation divergence and the 300 late cancellations; shrinks the post-incident remediation programme dramatically; keeps offline extracts fresh, capping D. *Residual risk:* diverts scarce people from fulfilment during peak stress — an explicit and correct trade-off. Moderate but accepted.
7. **Evidence-gated financial close** (effort M, ERP change). An `Evidence_pending` case state that blocks `Invoice close` for regulated lines until the register item resolves; removes the `Invoice close → Audit remediation` edge and the off-system `Spreadsheet invoice hold → END` terminal state. *Benefit:* structurally prevents mistaking after-the-fact remediation for compliance; protects revenue-recognition integrity. *Residual risk:* cash-flow delay during incidents; quantify and accept, and pre-agree a manual invoicing control with Finance. Moderate.
8. **Published allocation and prioritization policy** (effort M, legal and commercial). Explicit priority score with fixed weights, clinical-urgency override independent of customer tier, partial allocation before deferral, maximum parking age with mandatory re-scoring, contractual allocation annex, proactive customer notification, and per-case score logging. *Benefit:* turns I and J from "possibly unfair if informal" into demonstrably fair; protects against preferential-supply and contractual claims; reduces the 33% deferral-to-cancellation conversion and the 61% pharmacy fill-rate shock. *Residual risk:* customers dispute their tier, or publishing the policy invites gaming; mitigated by clinical-urgency override and periodic review. Low-moderate.
9. **Trained deputy control capacity** (effort M, training). Cross-train and formally qualify deputies for manual authorization checking and, within scope, release checking; maintain a 24/7 on-call roster. *Benefit:* removes the capacity constraint that made B ration itself and made C attractive; enables the 100% coverage the control floor requires. *Residual risk:* deputy competence under pressure; mitigated by scope limits (deputies cannot release CS alone) and drills. Low.

**P3 — Build the muscle and the sensing**

10. **Quarterly degraded-mode drills including a live recall simulation** (effort M, recurring). Full Tier 2 exercise on a live shift, including offline capture, manual release, manifest dispatch, reconciliation loop, and a lot-trace test with a measured effectiveness percentage. *Benefit:* the only way to know the control floor is achievable, and the only way to detect the recall-execution capability loss that no KPI in this incident revealed. Also settles the open question on A: if hospital p90 holds near 20h with full offline authorization, the bypass was never needed. *Residual risk:* drills diverge from real incidents; mitigated by varying scenarios and by injecting the T3 condition (systems back but data untrusted). Low.
11. **Degraded-mode reference model and continuous conformance checking against it** (effort M). Model the §4 process formally, deploy it as a second conformance baseline, and stream conformance checking during incidents. *Benefit:* enables the distinction that was impossible here — did teams follow the degraded plan (resilience) or improvise (unmanaged risk)? Turns process mining from post-hoc forensics into live control. *Residual risk:* model drift and false alarms; mitigated by periodic revalidation. Low.
12. **Resilience dashboard with paired leading indicators** (effort M). Throughput never displayed without its control-completeness and evidence-debt counterparts; segment protection spread visible continuously; exit gate criteria tracked live. *Benefit:* removes the structural bias that let management steer on speed while compliance degraded silently. *Residual risk:* indicator overload; mitigated by a hard limit of ~10 headline metrics. Low.
13. **Retrospective closure of this incident** (effort M-L, one-off, but start now). Risk-based retrospective review of the ~370 excess CS and ~640 excess cold-chain gap cases plus the 650 zero-POD cases; deviation and CAPA records; a documented market-action assessment for cases with unverifiable cold-chain; a regulator and customer notification decision; and closure of the ~600 still-open remediation items. *Benefit:* addresses the actual legal and patient-safety exposure created, and does so on the firm's initiative rather than an inspector's. *Residual risk:* the review surfaces reportable findings — which is the point; earlier self-disclosure is materially better than later discovery. Moderate, and worth it.
14. **Technical resilience of the WMS itself** (effort L, out of process scope but named for completeness). Immutable offline-capable replica or read-only standby for stock, authorization and lot data; segmented recovery so intake, inventory and scanning restore independently; integrity verification before trusting restored data (the T3 lesson). *Benefit:* shortens or eliminates Tier 2 entirely. *Residual risk:* the replica shares the compromise in a cyber event; mitigated by immutability, offline copies and integrity attestation. Moderate.

## 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should accept that in a Tier 2 disruption the business runs slower and less completely, and should stop treating the speed metrics as the scoreboard: accept Pharmacy-standard and Wholesale-bulk p90 inflating by up to roughly 2.5x while life-critical demand is protected, accept deferrals and partial allocations as the price of that protection, accept a standing cost for pre-staged offline capture kit, qualified deputy checkers and a reconciliation cell that mostly sits idle, and accept that evidence-gated invoicing will delay cash during an incident. What management should not accept is what actually happened underneath the good cycle-time numbers: regulated product dispatched with no release check (50% of manual packs), no custody record (3,250 scan-free dispatches) or no delivery evidence at all (650 cases); authorization replaced by assumption for 1,600 orders with unverifiable scope; a stock extract used for 60 hours that could not see quarantine or recall status; cases closed financially and commercially while their compliance evidence was still missing, with ~600 items still open on Day 21; and priority and deferral decisions made by informal rules that cannot now be shown to have been fair. The honest reading of the numbers is that throughput in T2 was partly borrowed from the control layer, that T3 was the most dangerous phase precisely because it looked like recovery, and that compliance was still 2.25-2.75x worse than baseline three weeks after cycle times for the priority segment had returned to normal — so the incident ended for operations and remained open for quality, finance and regulatory affairs. The single decision to make now is to fund the offline authorization extract, the offline evidence-capture kit and the pre-approved control floor, and to put evidence debt on the incident dashboard beside throughput so that the next trade-off is made deliberately rather than discovered later.

Three kinds of evidence would change this recommendation, and management should commission all three. First, a degraded-mode drill: if a hospital fast lane with mandatory offline authorization still holds hospital p90 near 20h, then A's bypass was never necessary and should be permanently prohibited; if the drill shows the lane cannot meet clinical need without bypass, then the honest answer is a pre-authorized, RP-approved, time-boxed bypass with a compensating retrospective review inside 4 hours — narrow, named, and logged — rather than the informal practice observed. Second, a retrospective sample of the 1,600 skipped-authorization cases: if they were verifiably ambient-only with valid standing authorizations and no adverse findings, a tightly scoped ambient exception could be formalized with the offline extract as its basis; if the sample shows scope creep into cold-chain or controlled product, C must be treated as a reportable control failure rather than an efficiency measure. Third, a feasibility and cost test of offline scanning: if store-and-forward capture proves technically infeasible, the two-person signed manifest becomes the primary standard rather than the fallback, and the throughput cost of that should be quantified and accepted rather than quietly avoided. Conversely, if a recall simulation under manual conditions fails to trace lots within the required window — the capability no indicator in this incident measured — the priority order changes immediately: lot-level traceability under degraded mode becomes the first investment, ahead of everything else on this list, because it is the one gap that puts patients rather than paperwork at risk.
