# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

Start the clock at `Submit` and use elapsed hours, including time spent waiting. A `Resolve` at exactly 72 hours is timely. Use all submitted cases as the denominator. Assume the extract includes submissions irrespective of their eventual outcome, the internal dispositions are recorded through cutoff, and a case does not reopen or return after internal exit. These assumptions allow the observed first exit to define the internal endpoint.

Let D be time to first internal disposition and let F_k(t) = P(D ≤ t, first disposition is k), for resolution (R), withdrawal (W), rejection (J), or transfer (X).

| Estimand | Operational question |
|---|---|
| **Internal resolution miss**, Q(72) = 1 − F_R(72) | What fraction of submissions fail to receive an internal resolution by the deadline? This measures the stated success criterion. All competing exits count against timely internal resolution. |
| **Still internally active**, S(72) = P(D > 72) = 1 − Σ_k F_k(72) | What fraction of submissions still need an internal disposition at the deadline? Use this to forecast the queue crossing 72 hours. |
| **Late internal resolution through 96 hours**, L = F_R(96) − F_R(72) | What fraction of all submissions are recovered by internal resolution in the next 24 hours after the deadline? The finite horizon avoids assuming eventual resolution beyond follow-up. |

The same case can contribute differently to the three measures.

| Observed case | Internal resolution miss at 72 | Active at 72 | Internal resolution in (72, 96] |
|---|---|---|---|
| S03, resolves at 76 | Yes | Yes | Yes, R at 76 |
| S09, open at 92 | Yes, already observed | Yes | Known resolution-free through 92, then censored. Outcome at 96 is unknown. |
| S06, withdraws at 27 | Yes | No | No, competing W at 27 |
| S08, transfers at 68 | Yes for the internal SLA | No | No, competing X at 68. For an any-provider resolution SLA its 72-hour outcome is unknown. |

# 2. Event and censoring encoding

Use one record per case with `t = duration_or_age_h`. The indicators below mark **events at t**. A deadline miss is derived from the trajectory. An outstanding case does not acquire a fictitious terminal event at 72.

| Status | Resolution indicator | Any internal disposition indicator | Primary cause code | Follow-up and sensitivity rule |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| resolved | 1 | 1 | 1 (R) | Record resolution at t, including late resolutions. R is also success for an any-provider endpoint. |
| withdrawn | 0 | 1 | 2 (W) | Competing exit at t. A disposition SLA can credit W if the contract allows it. |
| rejected | 0 | 1 | 3 (J) | Competing exit at t. Keep J identifiable if a legitimate rejection fulfills a separate decision SLA. |
| transferred | 0 | 1 | 4 (X) | Internal SLA treats X as a competing exit. An any-provider analysis can censor at t under an explicit follow-up assumption. A handoff SLA can credit X at t. External-resolution bounds leave post-transfer success unknown. |
| open | 0 | 0 | 0 (censored) | Include through age at cutoff, then right-censor. If only the last logged contact establishes active status, shorten follow-up in sensitivity analysis. |

For the **internal** endpoint, a transfer's time and disposition are observed in full. For **any-provider** resolution, a transfer starts a period of missing external observation. Censoring it and extrapolating from retained cases assumes comparable subsequent outcome risks given the observed history and adequate overlap. For **handoff credit**, the event of interest is R or X. That contract measures a documented handoff, without requiring an assumption about when the recipient resolves the case.

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

Only 13 of the 32 cases have an observed `Resolve`. Four of those 13 resolved late (S03, S13, S20, S28), giving 30.8% late **among observed resolvers**. Conditioning on eventual recorded success excludes all seven withdrawals/rejections and all three transfers. Conditioning on completion by cutoff also favors shorter observed paths when follow-up is short. S20 at 74 and S26 open at 104 are both known misses at 72. A resolver-only calculation retains just S20.

The 32 internal SLA labels at cutoff can be reconciled directly:

| Known timely | Known misses | Unclassified at 72 |
|---|---|---|
| 9 internal resolutions by 72 | 18 = 4 late resolutions + 7 withdrawals/rejections + 3 transfers + 4 open at age ≥72 | 5 open below 72 (S10, S15, S24, S29, S31) |

Thus the **observed-data bound** for the extract's internal miss fraction is 18/32 to 23/32 (56.3%–71.9%, rounded). For the active queue it is 9/32 to 14/32. The nine known active at 72 comprise the four late resolvers, the four older open cases and S32, which transferred at 96. These are identification bounds for these cases under the recording assumptions, separate from sampling confidence intervals. S24, open at 67, has five hours left and can still succeed. S25, open at 79, needs overdue-case action.

Censoring an external trajectory at handoff may preferentially remove difficult cases. All three transfers are escalated P1 cases. S21 transfers at 26, leaving 46 hours of unknown external experience before the deadline. A long silent interval such as S26's 72 hours could indicate a real wait or delayed recording. The gap alone cannot distinguish them. At the common freeze, the later cohort can also have a different opportunity to mature. A has 3 P1 cases out of 15 and 2 below-deadline open cases. B has 5 P1 cases out of 17 and 3 below-deadline open cases. Crude differences combine priority mix, calendar conditions, exposure to the policy and follow-up.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

**Primary analysis.** Estimate the four first-disposition cumulative incidence functions with Aalen–Johansen (AJ), reporting Q(72), S(72) and the cause breakdown together. At each elapsed time u, the risk set Y(u) includes every case still internally active and observed just before u. Add S(u−) d_k(u)/Y(u) to F_k for the d_k exits of cause k. Then multiply S by 1 − Σ_k d_k(u)/Y(u). Censored cases contribute to Y through their last observed age. The estimator accommodates dependence among competing causes. Interpretation for the submission population requires independent right censoring, marginally for a pooled curve or conditionally within the analysis strata.

| Pooled AJ estimate at 72 hours | Resolve | Withdraw | Reject | Transfer | Still active |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Probability | 30.4% | 14.0% | 10.1% | 7.5% | 38.0% |

The estimated **internal miss probability is 69.6%**. Only the 38.0% still active contribute to the internal backlog crossing the deadline. The estimated probability of a resolution in (72, 96] is 14.3% of all submissions. Only nine cases are under observation just before 72 (four A, five B), so report risk-set sizes and uncertainty with the curves. For larger-sample inference use pointwise AJ intervals or bootstrap entire cases within cohort, retaining their complete trajectories. With 3–7 cases per cohort-by-priority cell, emphasize counts and bounds and flag horizons with insufficient follow-up.

**Secondary analysis.** Use the restricted mean time to internal disposition through 72, ∫₀⁷² S(u) du. Its estimate, 50.4 hours per submission, quantifies elapsed time occupying the internal process during the first three days. It includes waiting time and cannot measure staff labor hours. Integrating 1 − F_R instead would count hours after a competing disposition as unresolved time. Pair the workload estimate with priority-stratified curves and the finite-sample bounds.

| Method | Role in this analysis |
|---|---|
| Kaplan–Meier | With every internal disposition coded as an event, KM estimates S and agrees with the AJ event-free curve. With only R as an event and W, J, X removed at exit, its complement estimates a net resolution risk constructed from the resolution hazard. Using that complement as actual timely success would ignore the probability of competing exits. |
| Cause-specific hazards | Among cases still active, estimate rates of R, W, J and X separately. Combine all rates to obtain absolute incidence. This would distinguish slow resolution within a stage from rapid exits to other destinations. Removing competing cases at their exits is appropriate for estimating each cause-specific rate. A multivariable hazards fit is poorly supported here. |
| Fine–Gray subdistribution hazards | A baseline cohort or priority model could relate covariates to the resolution CIF. Its modified risk set retains representation of competing exits. The hazard ratio therefore has no direct interpretation as the resolution rate in the live queue. The sparse events give little basis for a proportional subdistribution hazards model. |
| Landmarking | For a case active at a chosen age L < 72, estimate P(no internal R by 72 given active at L and history through L). With dates and a larger log, build and validate snapshots at operational ages such as 24 and 48 hours. The present endpoint times support age-only risk sets, but the final prefixes do not identify each case's stage at those earlier ages. |
| Multi-state model | Estimate transition hazards between observed stages and into each disposition. Use elapsed age and, where supported, time since entry to the current stage. Combine transitions for stage-conditional queue and outcome probabilities. Here the order identifies possible transitions. Estimating stage dwell times and transition hazards requires transition timestamps. |

# 5. Process-aware features and stage hazards

A proposed internal state diagram uses the last recorded activity as a proxy for stage. The arrows are observed directly-follows possibilities across the prefixes.

- `Submit` → `Screen` or `AutoCheck`
- `Screen` → `AutoCheck`, `Review`, `Escalate`, `Resolve`, `Reject` or `Withdraw`
- `AutoCheck` → `Screen`, `Review`, `Escalate` or `Resolve`
- `Review` → `Rework`, `Escalate`, `Resolve`, `Reject` or `Withdraw`
- `Rework` → `Review` or `Resolve`
- `Escalate` → `Review`, `Resolve` or `Transfer`

R, W, J and X are absorbing for internal ownership. For end-to-end tracking, X instead leads to an externally owned state with unobserved subsequent transitions. An open prefix ends observation in its last stage.

| Feature available at a decision time | Construction and interpretation |
|---|---|
| Baseline | Cohort and priority, assuming priority is the value assigned at submission. If priority can change, retain initial priority and update later values only when recorded. |
| Path and current stage | Track the latest observed stage, whether AutoCheck has occurred, and its order relative to Screen. Of 32 paths, 12 begin `Submit>AutoCheck` and 20 begin `Submit>Screen`. This distinction first becomes known at the second event. |
| Review and rework history | Count reviews, explicit reworks and returns to Review **so far**. S03 returns to Review after Escalate with zero explicit reworks. S07 returns after Rework. The two paths motivate distinct history features even when the current stage is the same. |
| Escalation and waiting | With timestamps, switch the escalated indicator from 0 to 1 at escalation and track time since escalation, age in stage and time since last activity. At S11's cutoff the case is aged 81 with 55 hours since the last event. Terminal rows all report gap zero, which cannot reconstruct waits before closure. |

Using final `escalated=yes` from time zero would assign S03's pre-escalation time to the exposed group. Reaching escalation required S03 to remain active until that event. This immortal-time bias coexists with confounding by indication if difficult cases are preferentially escalated. Use start–stop intervals with predictors known immediately before each transition, or freeze each prefix at a landmark. Final variant, final rework count, terminal activity and cutoff gap leak future information into an earlier alert. AutoCheck, escalation and rework can also be downstream of policy. They can inform predictions once observed, while adjusting for them in a total policy-effect analysis could block part of that effect.

# 6. Sensitivity analysis

| Change in analysis | Result at 72 hours and interpretation |
|---|---|
| Transfer as internal competing exit, loss of external follow-up, or credited handoff | The estimated miss is 69.6% for internal R, 68.7% for any-provider R when censoring at transfer, and 62.1% for the composite R-or-X contract. The middle estimate extrapolates retained-case risks after handoff. All transfers being escalated P1 makes that assumption especially fragile. The handoff result uses the observed transfer time only. |
| Bound external outcomes | Assuming an external resolution can first occur at or after transfer and W/J end the case, vary timely success after each pre-deadline transfer from impossible to certain. S08 and S21 can change the answer, while S32 already missed before transfer. Allowing the five younger open cases either outcome as well gives 16/32 to 23/32 possible any-provider misses. No external completion time is needed. |
| Change what counts as satisfactory disposition | If governance credits W and J as timely decisions alongside R, but X remains a competing exit, the estimated miss is 1 − F_R − F_W − F_J = 45.4%. Crediting all four internal dispositions gives 38.0%. Preserve the cause breakdown so that a rising rejection rate is visible even if a decision SLA improves. |
| Vary the reliability of open status | Primary estimates trust follow-up to cutoff. As a stress test, censor each open case at its last recorded event (age minus gap). The pooled AJ internal miss becomes 65.8%. All four open cases observed beyond 72 last had an activity before 72, so their known misses under the primary assumption become unknown under this weaker evidence rule. Earlier censoring is itself potentially informative. With audits and more cases, model observation probabilities using calendar entry, priority, stage and contact history, check weight stability/overlap, and vary residual resolution risk among lost cases. |
| Vary the cohort comparison | Crude internal misses are 70.8% in A and 68.4% in B. Directly standardizing cohort-by-priority CIFs to the pooled priority distribution gives 68.8% and 67.5%. Within P1 the point estimates go the other way (66.7% A, 80.0% B). AutoCheck occurs in 2/15 A prefixes and 13/17 B prefixes, so assignment period and observed route differ. Compare cohorts by assignment period first. With eligibility, calendars and adherence data, also examine actual exposure with time-aware adjustment. |

Even the descriptive cohort ordering depends on the decision. B has a smaller estimated internal resolution miss yet a larger active queue fraction at 72 (41.6% versus 33.9% in A). The internal miss bounds for A (9/15 to 11/15) and B (9/17 to 12/17) permit either cohort to have the higher miss fraction. Selective transfer, unrecorded closures, changing workload or different case severity could reverse an apparent advantage. These data do not identify a policy effect.

# 7. Operational interpretation

For each live case below 72, show hours remaining and, once validated, a landmark estimate of missing resolution by 72 given the prefix available now. For an age-only alert at L, that estimate is 1 − [F_R(72) − F_R(L)] / S(L) when S(L) > 0. Choose an alert threshold against review capacity and the cost of a missed intervention. S24 at age 67 with a four-hour gap could be routed to an available reviewer while five hours remain. S26 at age 104 already belongs on the overdue list. Its long silence calls for owner or status verification. Route known transfers to external follow-up if the contract covers end-to-end service.

For workload, sum predicted probabilities of remaining active at the deadline over the current queue. This forecasts unresolved internal work at the cases' respective deadlines. For SLA governance, publish the resolution CIF, each competing exit, the active fraction, censoring counts and follow-up maturity by submission cohort. An alert score predicts an outcome under observed practice. Estimating how much an escalation would change that outcome requires intervention evidence.

Before deployment, validate on later submissions with mature follow-up, keeping all snapshots of each case in one split. At each landmark compare mean predicted miss with the observed miss estimate in broad risk bands, using competing-risk incidence and censoring adjustment where needed. Report calibration-in-the-large, a censoring-adjusted 72-hour Brier score, alert lead time and false-alert/missed-alert rates at the operating threshold. Check calibration, error rates and access to follow-up actions within each priority and cohort, including transferred P1 cases under the relevant target. Report denominators and uncertainty. This extract is too small to certify subgroup calibration or fairness.

# 8. Python or precise pseudocode

The Python below uses pandas and PM4Py. It computes AJ directly from the risk sets and requires no survival library. The PM4Py log records sequence order only.

```python
from io import StringIO
from collections import Counter
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.log.obj import Event, EventLog, Trace

data = """case cohort priority duration_or_age_h observed_status prefix escalated rework_count last_event_gap_h
S01 A P2 18 resolved Submit>Screen>AutoCheck>Resolve no 0 0
S02 A P3 41 resolved Submit>Screen>Review>Resolve no 0 0
S03 A P2 76 resolved Submit>Screen>Review>Escalate>Review>Resolve yes 0 0
S04 A P1 29 resolved Submit>Screen>Escalate>Resolve yes 0 0
S05 A P3 9 rejected Submit>Screen>Reject no 0 0
S06 A P2 27 withdrawn Submit>Screen>Review>Withdraw no 0 0
S07 A P2 54 resolved Submit>Screen>Review>Rework>Review>Resolve no 1 0
S08 A P1 68 transferred Submit>Screen>Review>Escalate>Transfer yes 0 0
S09 A P3 92 open Submit>Screen>Review no 0 31
S10 A P2 47 open Submit>Screen>Review>Escalate yes 0 19
S11 A P1 81 open Submit>Screen>Escalate yes 0 55
S12 A P3 15 withdrawn Submit>Screen>Withdraw no 0 0
S13 A P2 110 resolved Submit>Screen>Review>Rework>Review>Escalate>Resolve yes 1 0
S14 A P3 63 rejected Submit>Screen>Review>Reject no 0 0
S15 A P2 35 open Submit>Screen>AutoCheck>Review no 0 2
S16 B P2 16 resolved Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Resolve no 0 0
S17 B P3 32 resolved Submit>Screen>AutoCheck>Review>Resolve no 0 0
S18 B P1 12 resolved Submit>AutoCheck>Escalate>Resolve yes 0 0
S19 B P2 58 resolved Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Review>Resolve no 0 0
S20 B P3 74 resolved Submit>Screen>Review>Resolve no 0 0
S21 B P1 26 transferred Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Escalate>Transfer yes 0 0
S22 B P2 44 withdrawn Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Review>Withdraw no 0 0
S23 B P3 11 rejected Submit>Screen>Reject no 0 0
S24 B P2 67 open Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Review no 0 4
S25 B P1 79 open Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Escalate yes 0 48
S26 B P3 104 open Submit>Screen>Review no 0 72
S27 B P2 39 resolved Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Review>Rework>Resolve no 1 0
S28 B P2 88 resolved Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Review>Escalate>Resolve yes 0 0
S29 B P1 51 open Submit>AutoCheck>Escalate>Review yes 0 6
S30 B P3 70 withdrawn Submit>Screen>Review>Withdraw no 0 0
S31 B P2 23 open Submit>AutoCheck>Screen no 0 3
S32 B P1 96 transferred Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Escalate>Transfer yes 0 0"""
df = pd.read_csv(StringIO(data), sep=r"\s+")
H = 72
codes = {"open": 0, "resolved": 1, "withdrawn": 2, "rejected": 3, "transferred": 4}

def encode(cases, scenario):
    out = cases.assign(t=cases.duration_or_age_h, code=cases.observed_status.map(codes))
    if scenario == "followup_lost":
        out.loc[out.code == 4, "code"] = 0
    return out

def aj(cases, scenario, horizon=H):
    x = encode(cases, scenario)
    surv = 1.0
    area = 0.0
    previous_t = 0.0
    cif = {j: 0.0 for j in range(1, 5)}
    for t, rows in x.loc[x.t <= horizon].groupby("t", sort=True):
        area += surv * (t - previous_t)
        previous_t = t
        at_risk = int((x.t >= t).sum())  # just before t, includes ties
        counts = rows.code.value_counts()
        for j in cif:
            cif[j] += surv * counts.get(j, 0) / at_risk
        surv *= 1 - (rows.code != 0).sum() / at_risk
    if surv > 1e-12 and x.t.max() < horizon:
        raise ValueError("Follow-up ends before this horizon")
    area += surv * (horizon - previous_t)
    success = cif[1] + (cif[4] if scenario == "handoff_credit" else 0.0)
    assert abs(surv + sum(cif.values()) - 1) < 1e-9
    return dict(n=len(x), risk_before_h=int((x.t >= horizon).sum()),
                F_resolve=cif[1], F_withdraw=cif[2], F_reject=cif[3],
                F_transfer=cif[4], event_free=surv, miss=1-success, rm_event_free_h=area)

for scenario in ("internal", "followup_lost", "handoff_credit"):
    print(scenario, pd.Series(aj(df, scenario)).round(3).to_dict())
groups = {key: aj(g, "internal") for key, g in df.groupby(["cohort", "priority"])}
strata = pd.DataFrame.from_dict(groups, orient="index")
strata.index.names = ["cohort", "priority"]
print("Internal results at 72 h by cohort and priority\n", strata.round(3))
print("Crude internal results at 72 h by cohort\n",
      pd.DataFrame({k: aj(g, "internal") for k, g in df.groupby("cohort")}).T.round(3))
weights = df.priority.value_counts(normalize=True)
print("Priority-standardized internal miss at 72 h (pooled priority mix)\n",
      strata["miss"].unstack("priority").dot(weights).round(3))
print("P(internal Resolve in (72, 96] h) =",
      round(aj(df, "internal", 96)["F_resolve"] - aj(df, "internal")["F_resolve"], 3))

timely = df.observed_status.eq("resolved") & df.duration_or_age_h.le(H)
young = df.observed_status.eq("open") & df.duration_or_age_h.lt(H)
early_transfer = df.observed_status.eq("transferred") & df.duration_or_age_h.le(H)
for target, unknown in [("internal", young), ("any provider", young | early_transfer)]:
    known_miss = ~(timely | unknown)
    print(target, "possible 72 h miss fraction in extract:",
          (int(known_miss.sum()), int((known_miss | unknown).sum())), "out of", len(df))

last_seen = df.copy()
is_open = last_seen.observed_status.eq("open")
last_seen.loc[is_open, "duration_or_age_h"] -= last_seen.loc[is_open, "last_event_gap_h"]
print("Internal miss if open-case follow-up ends at last event:",
      round(aj(last_seen, "internal")["miss"], 3))

log = EventLog()
for row in df.itertuples(index=False):
    trace = Trace(attributes={"concept:name": row.case})
    for activity in row.prefix.split(">"):
        trace.append(Event({"concept:name": activity}))
    log.append(trace)
variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)  # activity tuples mapped to traces
starts = Counter()
for sequence, traces in variants.items():
    starts[sequence[:2]] += len(traces)
print("Counts by first two activities:", dict(starts))
```

# 9. Limitations and additional data

1. **Establish who was observed and for how long.** Obtain submission dates, the exact freeze time, inclusion rules, and historical status snapshots. Verify incident-case coverage, common clock/time zone, reopenings and backdated closures. These checks determine whether an apparent open case establishes a miss and whether calendar entry explains censoring.
2. **Link external outcomes.** Obtain handoff acceptance, receiving organization, resolution or other disposition, event date and last verification date. This directly addresses the two transfers whose unknown outcomes matter at 72. Record the contractual treatment of transfer, withdrawal and rejection alongside the linked outcomes.
3. **Recover timestamped activity and audit history.** Obtain event occurrence and ingestion times, stage entry/exit, priority changes, and dated escalation/rework decisions. Audit long gaps for true waiting, offline work and delayed logging. This enables stage hazards and leakage-free landmark validation.
4. **Recover assignment and policy delivery.** Obtain rollout dates by team, eligibility and routing rules, actual AutoCheck/escalation exposure times, exceptions and reasons for noncompliance. Collect baseline severity and case complexity. These distinguish calendar assignment, selection into a route and the treatment actually received.
5. **Measure time-varying workload and extend follow-up.** Record arrivals, queue size, staffing, shifts and ownership changes. Accumulate more submissions before and after rollout, with mature follow-up in each priority. This supports capacity estimates, calendar-adjusted comparisons and independent validation of alert performance.
