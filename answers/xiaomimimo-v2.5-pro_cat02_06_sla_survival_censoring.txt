# 4. Statistical analysis plan

**Comparison of Approaches:**
*   *Kaplan-Meier (KM):* Fails here because it cannot handle competing risks. It will overestimate breach risk by treating withdrawals as censored.
*   *Aalen-Johansen / Cumulative Incidence Function (CIF):* Correctly handles multiple terminal states (Resolve, Withdraw, Transfer) simultaneously.
*   *Cause-Specific vs. Subdistribution Hazards:* Cause-specific asks "how fast do they resolve, given they haven't withdrawn yet?" Subdistribution (Fine-Gray) asks "what is the absolute probability of resolution accounting for withdrawals?"
*   *Multi-State Model:* Essential for this process. Models transitions: `Submit` $\to$ `Review` $\to$ `Escalate` $\to$ `Resolve`. Captures the dynamic nature of the process.
*   *Landmarking:* Required to avoid immortal-time bias when analyzing time-varying activities like `Escalate`.

**Recommendation:** 
*   **Primary Approach:** Multi-state model transitioning through `Submit`, `Review`, `Escalate`, and terminal states. This maps directly to the prefix sequences and handles the dynamic states.
*   **Secondary Approach:** Landmark Cumulative Incidence (Aalen-Johansen) at $t=24h$ and $t=48h$ to predict SLA breach probability for active cases, using process states at the landmark as covariates.

# 9. Limitations and additional data

To strengthen inference and resolve the ambiguities identified in this analysis, the following data is critically needed:
1.  **External Transfer Outcomes:** The eventual resolution status and time for S08, S21, and S32. Without this, evaluating their contribution to SLA compliance remains a sensitivity guess.
2.  **Exact Event Timestamps:** The prompt only provides total duration and last-event gaps. Granular timestamps for `Screen`, `Review`, and `Escalate` are required to model transition hazards (e.g., "how fast does a case move from Review to Escalate?") and validate the multi-state model.
3.  **Assignment Mechanism & Rollout Compliance:** We know Cohort B's policy rollout compliance was incomplete. Knowing exactly which cases were *intended* to have `AutoCheck` first but failed to do so would allow for an Intention-to-Treat (ITT) analysis, removing selection bias from the policy evaluation.
4.  **Time-Varying Workload:** Staffing levels or queue depths at the time cases were in `Review` or `Escalate`. High `last_event_gap_h` (like S26's 72 hours) might be driven by system backlog rather than case complexity.
