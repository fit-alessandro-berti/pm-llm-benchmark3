## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Emergency Procurement Authority and Exception Hierarchy (EPX) — replacement for GPIP-4 A4/A7 practice and incident-directive conventions:**

1. **Non-suspendable controls.** Never suspended by emergency status, incident directives, or executive email: (a) **segregation of duties** — requester, committer, and final approver must be three distinct persons, and the committer may never be an authorizer; (b) **Compliance clearance** for any new or unregistered supplier and for amounts above EUR 100,000, which must precede commitment, with a two-hour expedited service level during declared events; (c) **independent budget-owner approval** for amounts  EUR 50,000, which must precede commitment.
2. **Exception authority.** Only the Chief Risk Officer — or a pre-designated deputy reachable 24/7 — may suspend a clause-1 control, in writing, scoped to defined purchases, capped at 72 hours, and reported to the audit committee within five business days. Any other instrument purporting to suspend clause-1 controls is void and must be reported.
3. **Directive authority (operational matters).** An incident directive jointly signed by the COO and Incident Commander may, for purchases within its defined critical scope and window: re-sequence Create_PO after Commit_to_Supplier; authorize suppliers outside the local register; and substitute two-person Incident Command authorization for local operational approvals up to EUR 50,000. The CFO may separately designate the mechanism of Finance-Controller approval (including substitution by two-person Incident Command authorization, plus Legal clearance above EUR 150,000); such designation must be filed in the controlled repository within 24 hours to remain effective and never extends to clause-1 controls.
4. **Critical scope definition.** "Critical event scope" means goods or services required to restore or maintain containment of the declared event on the specifically identified lines, needed within 48 hours of commitment. Contingency stockpiling beyond containment need, purchases for unrelated facilities, and purchases after expiry are excluded. Each in-scope purchase carries a two-member Incident Command scope certificate recorded within four hours.
5. **Retrospective records.** Verbal authorizations are valid only if recorded within four hours; during system outages a signed contemporaneous record satisfies the recording requirement, with ERP synchronization within 24 hours. ERP timestamps are rebuttable by telephone recordings, signed PDFs, access logs, and supplier acknowledgments.
6. **Expiry and anti-normalization.** Directives expire automatically at the stated time; no verbal or unilateral extension is effective. Extensions require a written instrument jointly signed by the COO, the Incident Commander, and the CRO or deputy — maximum 24 hours each, maximum two per event. Upon expiry, ordinary controls resume immediately, without grace period. Safeguards against emergency normalization: (a) every emergency purchase above EUR 50,000 requires CRO and CFO ratification within ten business days or escalation to the audit committee; (b) the audit committee receives a monthly emergency-purchase register; (c) if one requester or site exceeds three emergency purchases or EUR 500,000 aggregate under a single directive, ordinary controls automatically revert for that requester/site pending internal-audit review; (d) emergency procedures may never be used to build stock beyond documented containment need.
