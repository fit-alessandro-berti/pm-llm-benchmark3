## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

The observed tables show that the failure is not one continuous model decay. It is a sequence of overlapping incidents: a schema/pipeline failure in April, a routing-policy mix shift in June, supplier-driven process and possibly concept drift from July through September, a Partner Z population shift in September–October, and severe label censoring in October.

**January–March: stable baseline.**  
Observed breach is 0.18–0.19, mean predicted risk is 0.18–0.19, AUROC is ~0.81, ECE is ~0.02, unknown activity is ~0.2%, missing queue is ~1%, and label maturity is 100%. This is the safest reference period for model behavior. It establishes that the model was well calibrated before the migration and before the routing/supplier/partner changes.

**April: primarily a schema and pipeline incident, not genuine performance deterioration.**  
Observed breach remains 0.19, but mean predicted risk rises to 0.28, AUROC drops to 0.67, Brier rises to 0.184, ECE rises to 0.110, unknown activity reaches 17%, and missing queue reaches 14%. The migration renamed the diagnosis activity, prediction prefixes were built at the wrong event or not built, duplicates appeared, and some events became `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY`. This is observed evidence of pipeline failure. The hypothesis is that the model received corrupted or incomplete inputs, not that the underlying repair process suddenly worsened. This hypothesis would be falsified if, after activity-label normalization, duplicate removal, and full feature recomputation, April metrics still remained materially worse than January–March.

**May: partial pipeline recovery with residual feature debt.**  
After the mapping patch, unknown activity falls to 2%, missing queue to 3%, AUROC recovers to 0.78, and mean predicted risk falls to 0.21. However, ECE is still 0.050 and Brier is 0.146, consistent with the audit finding that some derived features, especially the cached “number of events before diagnosis,” were not fully backfilled. May is therefore best viewed as a recovery month, not a clean training month until feature lineage is verified.

**June: routing-policy and variant-mix shift, not necessarily concept drift.**  
V2 rises from ~35% to 52%, V1 falls to 30%, and observed breach falls to 0.15. Mature variant breach rates are stable: V1 0.16, V2 0.09, V3 0.35. The mix-adjusted expected breach is approximately 0.158, close to the observed 0.15. The model predicts 0.20, so it overpredicts the new lower-risk mix. This is process drift in routing and covariate drift in variant distribution, but there is little evidence of concept drift because the conditional breach rates by variant remain stable. Treating June as model failure would be a mistake; it is mainly a population/process mix change requiring recalibration or mix-aware monitoring.

**July: early supplier-driven process stress.**  
The supplier issue begins on 10 July. `Order Part -> Part Received` median rises to 65h from 42h in June. Mature V3 breach rises to 0.46 from ~0.30–0.35. Overall observed breach is 0.19 and mean predicted risk is 0.18, so average calibration looks acceptable, but AUROC falls to 0.76 and Brier rises to 0.151. This suggests the model is not fully capturing the emerging V3 risk. July is early evidence of process-performance drift and possible concept drift, but label maturity is still high at 98%, so it is more trustworthy than August–October.

**August: overlapping process performance, covariate mix, and likely concept drift.**  
V3 share rises to 35%, part-receipt median rises to 130h, mature V3 breach rises to 0.64, observed breach is 0.28, but mean predicted risk is only 0.19. AUROC falls to 0.68, Brier rises to 0.197, ECE rises to 0.120. The mix-adjusted expected breach using mature variant rates is about 0.31, higher than the observed 0.28, but maturity is 93%, so some under-observation is possible. The main explanation is real deterioration in the parts-dependent process, with covariate drift toward V3 and likely concept drift because V3 conditional breach has changed sharply. Label delay is present but not yet dominant.

**September: supplier drift plus Partner Z population shift plus label immaturity.**  
V3 share reaches 42%, Partner Z reaches 24%, Partner Z cases are 58% V3 versus 36% for non-Z, rural distance and device age are higher, and these features are missing from the model. September observed breach is 0.31, mean predicted risk is 0.21, AUROC is 0.66, ECE is 0.140, and label maturity is only 82%. Partner Z labels mature more slowly, and Partner Z observed breach is 0.45 versus 0.27 for others. September is therefore a confounded month: genuine population shift, ongoing supplier/process drift, possible concept drift, and label censoring all overlap. It should not be used for final model evaluation until labels mature and Partner Z effects are stratified.

**October: dominated by label delay and informative censoring.**  
October has 55% label maturity, median label delay 52 days, observed breach 0.16, mean predicted risk 0.23, AUROC 0.64, Brier 0.205, ECE 0.130. The observed breach rate is not comparable to mature months. The outcome API backlog disproportionately affects longer resolution paths, and longer paths are more likely to breach. Given October’s high V3 share and continued supplier delays, a mature October breach rate would likely be far higher than 0.16. October should be treated as unreliable for model performance, calibration, and retraining decisions until the outcome backlog is resolved and labels mature.

**Why April, June, August–September, and October are not one homogeneous failure:**  
April is a data-schema and pipeline failure with stable observed breach. June is a routing-policy mix change with stable conditional variant risk. August–September contain real process and possibly concept deterioration, plus Partner Z population shift. October is primarily label censoring and outcome backlog. A single “model drift” label would misallocate remediation: April needs pipeline repair, June needs recalibration/mix monitoring, August–September need supplier-aware features and segment analysis, and October needs label-maturity controls.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Immediate containment: do not retrain yet.**  
Freeze any automated retraining that consumes labels available at the reporting cutoff. October, and likely September, are unsafe for supervised learning because outcome labels are immature and informatively censored. Immediately repair the event pipeline: normalize diagnosis labels, remove duplicates, rebuild prediction prefixes, and backfill derived features. Publish two reporting streams: provisional metrics on currently available labels and mature-only metrics on fixed-vintage cohorts. For production decisioning, add manual review or conservative escalation for V3 and Partner Z cases rather than silently changing model thresholds based on October data.

**Short-term recalibration: use mature, repaired cohorts.**  
Once pipeline repair is verified, recalibrate rather than fully retrain. Start with January–March as the clean baseline. Add repaired May only after the cached event-count feature is recomputed. June can be used for calibration because labels are mature and the observed breach drop is explainable by V2 mix, but it should be monitored as a distribution-shift month. July is likely usable after final label confirmation, but it should be treated as the start of the supplier-driven regime. If mature August/September data confirm higher V3 risk, use variant-specific calibration, especially for V3, rather than a single global calibrator.

**Feature repair and feature extension before any retraining.**  
Repair the April/May feature lineage first. Then add features that are now known to matter: Partner Z identifier, rural travel distance, device age, part category, supplier lead-time history, regional supplier performance, queue information, source-system indicator, unknown-activity flag, and duplicate flag. Some of these features may not be available at the exact prediction moment immediately after diagnosis; where future information is unavailable, use historical or contextual proxies, such as supplier-part average lead time over the prior 30 days.

**Full retraining only after mature labels and confirmed conditional change.**  
Full retraining is justified if fixed-vintage evaluation shows that the relationship between features and breach has changed, especially for V3 or Partner Z. The retraining set should include recent mature months that reflect the new process reality, but it should exclude raw October and any unrepaired April/May records. A reasonable staging is: train on January–March for baseline behavior, repaired May–July for transition behavior, and mature August–September once labels are complete. Validate on a held-out mature vintage, not on cutoff labels.

**Months safe or unsafe for training until issues are resolved:**

| Month | Training status | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| January | Safe | Mature labels, clean pipeline, stable performance. |
| February | Safe | Mature labels, clean pipeline, stable performance. |
| March | Safe | Mature labels, clean pipeline, stable performance. |
| April | Unsafe as stored; potentially safe only after full reprocessing | Renamed activity, duplicates, unknown events, missing queue, wrong prediction prefixes. |
| May | Conditionally safe after feature backfill | Patch deployed but cached derived features were not fully recomputed. |
| June | Safe for calibration, with shift monitoring | Mature labels, but routing-policy mix shift changed covariate distribution. |
| July | Conditionally safe after label finalization | High maturity, but supplier change begins; useful for transition learning. |
| August | Unsafe until labels mature and supplier regime is represented | Maturity 93%, V3 breach rises sharply, possible concept drift. |
| September | Unsafe until labels mature and Partner Z censoring is addressed | Maturity 82%, Partner Z labels mature slowly, population shift present. |
| October | Unsafe | Maturity 55%, outcome API backlog, informative censoring, observed breach not comparable. |

**Segment-specific or variant-specific model justification.**  
A variant-specific approach is justified, at least for calibration. V2 is low-risk and stable; V1 is moderate-risk; V3 is high-risk and strongly affected by supplier delays. A single global calibrator is likely to under-serve V3 during supplier stress. Start with variant-specific calibrators and variant-specific monitors. If V3 volume is sufficient and the feature relationship is materially different, a separate V3 model or a hierarchical model with V3-specific parameters is reasonable. Partner Z should first be handled through stratified calibration, feature addition, and fairness checks. A separate Partner Z model is only justified if Partner Z volume is sufficient and the residual gap persists after adding rural/device/supplier features.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

**Predictive monitor**  
Track AUROC, Brier, ECE, calibration slope/intercept, observed/expected breach by decile, and precision/recall at operational thresholds. Compute these only on mature cohorts, for example cases with at least 90 days of label availability or confirmed closure. Provisional metrics may be shown but must not trigger retraining.

**Calibration monitor**  
Maintain reliability diagrams and O/E ratios globally and by segment: V1/V2/V3, Partner Z/non-Z, source system, and high-risk part categories. Alert when segment ECE exceeds 0.05–0.08 for two consecutive mature reporting periods, or when O/E is outside 0.8–1.25.

**Label-maturity monitor**  
Track label maturity at 30, 60, and 90 days; median label delay; outcome API backlog age; and label-arrival hazard by variant and partner. Alert when maturity at 60 days falls below 90%, median label delay rises above 40 days, or the backlog disproportionately affects V3/Partner Z cases.

**Data-quality monitor**  
Track unknown activity share, renamed-label mapping coverage, missing queue feature, missing prediction-time features, duplicate-event rate, prefix correctness, and unmapped variant share. Alert when unknown activity exceeds 1%, missing queue exceeds 3%, duplicate rate exceeds 1%, or unmapped variants exceed 1%.

**Process-variant monitor**  
Track monthly shares of V1, V2, V3, and unmapped/rare variants. Alert on abrupt routing-policy changes, such as V2 increasing by more than 5 percentage points month over month, or V3 increasing sharply without a corresponding model update.

**Performance-DFG monitor**  
Track median and p95 durations for critical edges: `Order Part -> Part Received`, `Initial Diagnosis Completed -> Remote Fix`, `Dispatch Technician -> Repair Completed`, and `Repair Completed -> Case Closed`. Alert when `Order Part -> Part Received` exceeds baseline by 50% or breaches supplier SLA. The stable remote-fix edge should be used as a control: if V2 share changes but remote-fix duration remains stable, the change is routing mix rather than remote-process degradation.

**Population monitor**  
Track Partner Z share, rural distance distribution, device age distribution, regional mix, and PSI between training and serving populations. Alert when Partner Z share changes by more than a few percentage points or when PSI for key covariates exceeds 0.2.

**Intervention/deployment monitor**  
Record model versions, feature-pipeline patches, activity-label migrations, routing-policy changes, supplier incidents, Partner Z onboarding, and outcome API backlog status. Every predictive alert should be annotated with these interventions.

**Alert dependencies to avoid unnecessary retraining:**

1. If label-maturity monitor is active, suppress concept-drift and retraining alerts. Label delay should trigger backfill and censoring-aware reporting, not model replacement.  
2. If data-quality monitor is active, suppress predictive-performance alarms. Pipeline repair takes precedence over calibration or retraining.  
3. If process-variant or population-shift monitors are active but within-stratum calibration is stable, trigger recalibration or segment monitoring, not full retraining.  
4. Retraining alerts should fire only when data quality is acceptable, label maturity is sufficient, and mature cohort metrics degrade for two consecutive periods, preferably with evidence that within-path conditional relationships changed.  
5. Supplier-delay alerts should route to operations and model-feature updates, not automatically to model retraining, because the correct response may be recalibration or adding supplier features.

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

CASE, ACT, TS = "case_id", "activity", "timestamp"

df = pd.read_csv("field_service_events.csv", parse_dates=[TS])
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_key=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS)

# Normalize renamed diagnosis label before feature extraction.
df[ACT] = df[ACT].replace({
    "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed",
    "Diagnostic Assessment Completed ": "Initial Diagnosis Completed",
})
df["is_unknown_activity"] = df[ACT].eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY")

# Duplicate handling: flag exact duplicates, then keep earliest record.
df["duplicate_event"] = df.duplicated(subset=[CASE, ACT, TS], keep=False)
df = (
    df.sort_values([CASE, TS, ACT])
      .drop_duplicates(subset=[CASE, ACT, TS], keep="first")
      .copy()
)

# Cohorts:
# event_month: calendar month in which events occurred; useful for operational DFG evidence.
# start_month: month in which the case started; useful for prediction/label cohorts.
df["event_month"] = df[TS].dt.to_period("M")
case_start = df.groupby(CASE)[TS].min().rename("case_start_time").reset_index()
case_start["start_month"] = case_start["case_start_time"].dt.to_period("M")
df = df.merge(case_start, on=CASE, how="left")

def classify_variant(seq):
    s = set(seq)
    if "Remote Fix" in s:
        return "V2"
    if "Order Part" in s:
        return "V3"
    if "Dispatch Technician" in s:
        return "V1"
    return "Unmapped"

def edge_median(perf_dfg, edge):
    v = perf_dfg.get(edge)
    if v is None:
        return np.nan
    if isinstance(v, dict):
        return v.get("performance", np.nan)
    return v

def month_audit(month_period, cohort):
    start = month_period.start_time
    end = (month_period + 1).start_time

    if cohort == "event":
        # Events occurring in this calendar month.
        mlog = pm4py.filter_time_range(df, start, end, case_key=CASE, timestamp_key=TS)
    else:
        # Cases started in this month; in production, also truncate to prediction time/reporting cutoff.
        cases = case_start.loc[case_start["start_month"] == month_period, CASE]
        mlog = df[df[CASE].isin(cases)]

    if len(mlog) == 0:
        return {}

    variants = pm4py.get_variants(mlog, activity_key=ACT, case_key=CASE)
    variant_counts = {classify_variant(k): len(v) for k, v in variants.items()}
    total_variants = sum(variant_counts.values()) or 1

    dfg = pm4py.discover_dfg(mlog, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_key=CASE)
    perf = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(mlog, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_key=CASE)
    perf_dfg = perf[0] if isinstance(perf, tuple) else perf

    temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
        mlog,
        case_key=CASE,
        timestamp_key=TS,
        activity_key=ACT,
    )

    return {
        "cohort": cohort,
        "month": str(month_period),
        "events": len(mlog),
        "cases": mlog[CASE].nunique(),
        "unknown_activity_share": float(mlog["is_unknown_activity"].mean()),
        "duplicate_share_flagged": float(mlog["duplicate_event"].mean()),
        "missing_queue_share": float(mlog["queue_feature"].isna().mean()) if "queue_feature" in mlog else np.nan,
        "variant_share": {k: v / total_variants for k, v in variant_counts.items()},
        "order_to_part_received_median": edge_median(perf_dfg, ("Order Part", "Part Received")),
        "diagnosis_to_remote_fix_median": edge_median(perf_dfg, ("Initial Diagnosis Completed", "Remote Fix")),
        "dfg_edge_count": len(dfg[0] if isinstance(dfg, tuple) else dfg),
        "temporal_feature_rows": len(temporal),
    }

months = sorted(set(df["event_month"].dropna()))
audit_rows = []
for m in months:
    audit_rows.append(month_audit(m, cohort="event"))
    audit_rows.append(month_audit(m, cohort="case_start"))

monthly_audit = pd.DataFrame(audit_rows)
```
