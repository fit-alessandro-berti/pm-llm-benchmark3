## 1. Feasibility model

```text
Partial order (per case; A  B = A must complete before B starts; A  B = may overlap)

R1  Register  Triage  {Identity Check, Document Check, Preliminary Screen*}
R2  Identity Check  Assessment(final)                         [rule 2]
R3  Document Check(final)  Assessment(final)                  [rule 2]
R4  Originals Receipt  Document Check                         [only if originals required; rule 4]
R5  Identity Check  Document Check                            [only if no originals required; rule 3]
R6  Assessment(last)  Compliance Review(final)  Decision     [if high-risk or high-value; rule 5]
R7  Assessment(last)  Decision                                [otherwise]
R8  Decision  Notify                                          [rule 8]
R9  Request More Info(k)  Document Check(k+1)  Assessment(k+1)  [Compliance(final)]  Decision   [rule 7]
R10 Preliminary Screen  {Identity, Document, Assessment}; Preliminary Screen does NOT satisfy Compliance(final) [rule 10]
R11 Escalation Advice  {Identity, Document}; Escalation Advice  Compliance(final) when relied on
R12 For every resource r: service intervals of r are pairwise disjoint                 [rule 11]
R13 Final Assessor  Final Compliance Reviewer                                         [rule 9]
R14 Queue durations are forecasts; start times are predicted, not fixed               [rule 12]
```

```text
Conditional constraints
C1  IF high_risk OR high_value THEN Compliance(final) after latest Assessment AND before Decision
C2  IF originals_required THEN Document Check start >= Originals Receipt
C3  IF Request More Info issued (by Assessment or Compliance) THEN new Document Check AND new Assessment before Decision;
    IF high_risk OR high_value THEN new or explicitly reaffirmed Compliance(final)
C4  IF no originals_required THEN Identity and Document may run concurrently

Declarative constraints
D1  Register is first; Triage is second
D2  Decision requires all prerequisites complete; no decision on an open discrepancy except under an explicitly adopted
    policy (P-337 in K-337), in which case approve-with-conditions or reject must record the discrepancy
D3  A preliminary screen carries status = preliminary and cannot satisfy the audit requirement for Compliance(final)
D4  Interval timestamps must reflect true service on the named resource; batch-entered or overlapping logs
    invalidate any concurrency claim
D5  Rules 5, 8, 9 and 11 hold under all four interventions; only P-337 alters the request-more-info rule
```

Intervention classification. K-104 alters timing and resource reservation: it adds a preliminary branch and a held slot but leaves the final path intact, so it is primarily a timing and capacity intervention. K-219 alters routing: it changes which document resource performs the check. K-411 alters the structure of concurrency: it changes task creation and parallel routing, and its validity depends on timestamp behaviour. K-337 alters the allowed decision policy: it deletes a mandatory-looking loop and therefore changes which decisions are admissible, not only how long they take.

Calendar note: factual timestamps include off-hours and weekend work. The counterfactuals treat elapsed clock time as the cycle-time measure, with queue waits expressed as forecasts.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

Factual mechanism: The sanctions ambiguity was visible at Triage (Mon 08:20–08:50) but was not escalated, so it surfaced only during Assessment on Tue (15:10–19:10). S1 advised a final compliance review at Tue 21:20. Because no capacity was held, the final C1 review could not start until Wed 13:10, after the C1 queue (16–20 h in this window) cleared. The review then took 4.5 h, mostly reading evidence from scratch. The decision at Wed 18:00 and notify at 18:40 followed. Cycle time was dominated by the wait for C1 rather than by any single check.

Minimal intervention: Only two changes are made. At Triage, a preliminary packet goes to S1, and a provisional C1 slot is held for the post-assessment review. Identity, Document and Assessment resources are unchanged, and the requirement that final compliance follows the final Assessment is preserved.

Temporal propagation: The packet moves the sanctions question forward, so S1 advice can land Monday morning rather than Tuesday night. This does not accelerate Document Check, which still starts when D1 frees (Mon 21:40), because D1 is queue-bound. The flagged documents can be targeted, but the check length stays about 8–12 h. Assessment still starts when A1 is available (about Tue 15:10) but runs about an hour shorter because the ambiguity is partly pre-resolved. The held C1 slot removes the queue wait, so the final review starts about 18:30 Tue and runs 2.5–4.5 h, since preliminary work saves 1–3 h. Compliance therefore ends Tuesday night. The decision is then bound by M1: if M1 signs on Wednesday morning, cycle time falls from about 58.5 h to roughly 50–54 h. If M1 signs only around 18:00 Wednesday, as in the factual log, the gain disappears and the bottleneck migrates to the decision step.

Process-feasibility check: The preliminary screen is labelled preliminary and does not replace the final review (rule 10 and D3). The final review occurs after the latest Assessment and before Decision (rule 5). The four-eyes rule holds because A1 performs the final Assessment and C1 the final review. S1 advice is advisory and may overlap with other checks. Preliminary C1 work and final C1 work are separate intervals, so rule 11 holds. The path remains feasible only if any Request More Info triggered by the preliminary packet is handled under rule 7, with a new Document Check and Assessment.

Best/base/worst case: Best: M1 signs around Wed 08:30 and the held slot is used exactly, giving cycle 47–49 h with approve with conditions. Base: M1 signs at midday Wednesday, giving cycle 50–54 h, with approve with conditions most likely. Worst: the held slot is idle or displaces urgent cases, or the packet is incomplete and triggers a further Request More Info loop, giving 58–65 h and some displacement cost.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Decision-signature logs showing M1 typically signs late afternoon regardless of compliance completion would remove most of the gain. Evidence that held slots displace at least one other urgent review per week would make the policy net negative. Evidence that S1 preliminary advice frequently requests additional information would turn the intervention into a loop generator.

### K-219

Factual mechanism: The triage routed standard but handwritten documents to D1, a complex-document specialist, because of handwriting. Originals arrived Mon 17:30, but D1's queue meant Document Check began Tue 09:30 and ended 13:30. Assessment then started Tue 20:00, which is later than document completion; A2's queue absorbed the difference. The decision followed in the Wednesday morning slot. The cycle was about 48 h, and the document wait was a larger component than the check itself.

Minimal intervention: For this case only, when originals arrive, Document Check is assigned to D2 rather than waiting for D1. Identity, Assessment (A2) and Decision (M1) are unchanged.

Temporal propagation: The D2 queue was about 5 h at Mon 16:00, so D2 can start about 21:00 Monday, not at originals receipt. A 6–9 h handwritten standard check therefore finishes about Tue 03:00–06:00, roughly 8–10 h earlier than the factual 13:30. Assessment is not triggered by document completion alone, however. The Tue 08:00 snapshot implies A2 is busy until about 14:00, and the factual 20:00 start reflects further load. So assessment starts between roughly 14:00 and 20:00 and ends 2–3 h later. Because the M1 morning slot is unchanged, the decision still lands around Wednesday 08:30, and the cycle changes by only 0–2 h. Larger gains require M1 or A2 capacity to open earlier. If D2 requests clarification, the effect changes sharply: an internal clarification returns to Document Check and Assessment (2–4 h); an applicant clarification is a Request More Info requiring an external response (8–36 h) and then a new Document Check and Assessment.

Process-feasibility check: Originals Receipt still precedes Document Check (rule 4). Identity can still proceed earlier. The case is not high-risk or high-value, so no compliance review is required. D2 is permitted for standard documents. No resource overlap is needed because D2 finishes before A2 starts. The path is feasible.

Best/base/worst case: Best: D2 finishes around 03:00, A2 starts 14:00, and M1 signs Tuesday evening, giving 38–42 h. Base: cycle 46–48 h, a gain of 0–2 h, with the same decision. Worst: an applicant clarification adds an external wait and a repeat Document Check and Assessment, giving 60–75 h.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: A low historical clarification rate (below about 10%) with quick internal resolution would make the best case realistic. Evidence that M1 can sign on demand would enlarge the gain. Evidence that D2's handwritten standard checks often exceed 9 h would erase most of the benefit.

### K-337

Factual mechanism: The case went through three document and assessment rounds, each triggered by a gap in evidence. The two external Request More Info waits dominate the 238 h cycle: about 20.5 h for RMI1 and about 70.5 h for RMI2, which spanned a weekend. Final compliance (Tue) and decision (Wed) were short relative to the loop structure. The discrepancy closed only because the supplier confirmed in RMI2.

Minimal intervention: After Assessment 2 (Fri 10:30), RMI2 is not issued. The case proceeds to final compliance (mandatory because the case is high-risk and high-value) and to a decision based on existing evidence. The decision must be approve with conditions or reject, and the unresolved discrepancy must be recorded.

Temporal propagation: Removing RMI2 eliminates the external wait, the third Document Check (about 4.5 h of D1 work) and the third Assessment (about 2.5 h). Final compliance moves to Friday afternoon if C1 has capacity (roughly 12:00–17:00). The decision then depends on M1. If M1 can sign Friday evening, cycle time is about 103–110 h. If the decision waits for the next business morning, Monday around 08:30, cycle time is about 166–170 h. Most of the reduction comes from weekend and external waiting, not from faster internal work. The unresolved discrepancy is carried forward as a condition, and the compliance review works on evidence that is less complete than in the factual case.

Process-feasibility check: Compliance precedes Decision (rules 5 and 8). Four-eyes is preserved because A1 performs Assessment 2 and C1 the final review. Rule 7 is not triggered because no Request More Info is issued. However, the intervention explicitly changes the allowed decision policy (P-337), and that policy must be formally adopted. Under P-337, compliance may not issue a further Request More Info, otherwise the loop is simply moved. The path is feasible only under the changed policy.

Best/base/worst case: Best: C1 and M1 both act Friday; approve with conditions; cycle about 103–110 h. Base: cycle about 165–170 h; approve with conditions (50–60%) or reject (25–35%). Worst: the compliance queue slips into Monday and M1 signs Monday afternoon, giving about 175–185 h, with the decision later challenged on appeal. All cases are still shorter than 238 h, but downstream costs are not in the model.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Evidence that the discrepancy was immaterial and easily conditioned, such as a totals error below a materiality threshold that does not affect exposure, would make the intervention defensible. Evidence that compliance would have issued further requests anyway would show that the loop captured compliance concerns rather than document quality, changing the interpretation of the saving.

### K-411

Factual mechanism: The legacy system created only the identity task at triage. The document task appeared only after identity finished (13:20), and D2 started at 15:00. Document Check ran until 19:30. Assessment waited about 14 h because the A2 queue had grown, starting Wed 09:30. The decision followed at Wed 14:00. The cycle was about 29.5 h, and the assessment wait was the largest component.

Minimal intervention: Both tasks are created at Triage. Identity (I2) and Document Check (D2) run concurrently under a controlled workaround with real-time timestamps. Assessment and Decision are unchanged.

Temporal propagation: Earlier task creation lets D2 start when it becomes free, around 12:00 Tuesday (the 08:00 snapshot shows about 4 h of queue), instead of after identity completes. A standard digital check of 4–5 h then finishes around 16:30–17:30, about 2–3 h earlier than the factual 19:30. Assessment cannot start until both checks are complete and A2 is free. Given A2's saturated queue (6 h at Tue 08:00, and a factual start on Wed 09:30), assessment still starts around Wednesday 08:00–09:30. The decision is then mainly driven by M1's Wednesday slot. The net effect is a reduction of about 1.5–3.5 h. Earlier readiness is real, but earlier final completion is largely absorbed by the queue.

Process-feasibility check: Both tasks are created, but Assessment still requires both checks (rule 2). Concurrency across different resources is allowed when no originals are required (rule 3). Rule 11 requires that I2 and D2 are not assigned to the same person and that their intervals do not overlap for any single resource. The workaround must not allow one person to act as both. No compliance review is needed, so the four-eyes rule is not engaged. The path is feasible only if logs are real-time.

Best/base/worst case: Best: an A2 slot opens Tuesday evening, giving 24–26 h. Base: 26–28 h, with the same decision. Worst: logs are entered in batch, producing overlaps that are reported as exceptions and rework, or earlier-ready cases take A2 slots from other cases, so the net gain is close to zero.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Evidence that A2 has idle slots on Tuesday evenings would turn readiness into completion and enlarge the gain. Evidence that D2's midday queue is usually longer than 4 h would remove the earlier start. Evidence that the legacy system drops or duplicates timestamps would make the intervention's concurrency claims unreliable.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 early escalation and held C1 slot | C1 gains 1.5–2 h of preliminary work per case; final review shortened by 1–3 h; held slot may sit idle | C1 wait for the prepared case; S1 advice moves earlier | C1 queue for other urgent high-risk cases; S1 advisory load | urgent compliance cases (for example, the Mon 16:00 arrivals) | positive only with time-boxed reservations; otherwise negative | one idle held slot on a single-reviewer queue can block a whole block of urgent work |
| K-219 route standard handwritten file to D2 | D2 gains 4–9 h per handwritten case; D1 frees 4–6 h; possible clarification rework | D1 queue for complex corporate files | D2 queue for standard cases; earlier A2 arrivals | other standard D2 cases; complex cases benefit if D1 is freed | small positive if handwritten cases are rare; negative if D2 becomes the default route | D2 cannot substitute for D1 on complex files (8–14 h), so freed D1 capacity helps only if complex work is waiting |
| K-337 no second Request More Info | removes third Document Check (about 4.5 h D1) and third Assessment (about 2.5 h A1); shifts a review to Friday C1 | D1 and A1 queues; weekend backlog | C1 Friday/Monday queue; M1 signature queue; downstream appeal work | other C1 cases on Friday; supplier behaviour (loop incentives) | cycle time positive; decision quality and defensibility negative | removing one loop changes the admissible decision class, not just its duration |
| K-411 parallel I2 and D2 | I2 and D2 demand compressed into the same window; D2 demand arrives earlier; A2 receives work earlier | identity and document waiting for this case | A2 queue (earlier arrivals into a saturated queue); D2 midday queue for others | other standard cases competing for A2 and D2 | small positive if assessment has slack; near neutral otherwise | earlier readiness into a saturated queue yields no completion gain beyond the queue; false concurrency from logs |

**100-case weekly rollout.** Single-case gains do not add linearly. The clearest interference is scarce compliance capacity. C1 is the only certified reviewer, so a held slot for a K-104-type case, whose final assessment may be 20–30 hours away, converts shared capacity into reserved capacity. If ten high-risk cases per week hold slots, idle reserved hours displace other urgent reviews even though each reservation looks cheap locally. Reservations must be time-boxed.

Substitution between D1 and D2 is the second effect. K-219 moves standard handwritten files to D2, freeing D1 for complex corporate work that K-104 also needs. D2 is slow on complex files (8–14 h), so if every handwritten file moves to D2 its queue will exceed D1's within days, displacing the bottleneck rather than removing it.

Rework is the least visible effect. Earlier D2 work and K-411 concurrency bring more checks to Assessment earlier, so any clarification found there reruns document and assessment work. K-337 removes loops but leaves the rework as appeals and conditions that the queue model does not record.

Assessment is the binding constraint in all four cases. Earlier readiness from K-219 and K-411 converts into completion only if assessors can absorb it. With one assessor in training and queues of 6–12 hours, extra early arrivals mostly lengthen queues for other cases, so the weekly net gain is small or negative.

Weekend and external-response effects dominate K-337. Across 100 cases, Friday supplier requests would be shifted into the weekend, producing a Monday surge in Document Check and compliance. This is a bottleneck migration that a single-case analysis cannot show.

Timestamp behaviour versus true concurrency: K-411's real-time logging is essential. Batch entry can make concurrent work look sequential, hiding the gain, or make one resource look double-booked, violating rule 11 and corrupting the queue estimates used to schedule the rollout.

Net sign: K-411 and K-219 are small positive effects with limited spillover. K-104 is positive only with time-boxed reservations. K-337 improves cycle time but is negative for defensibility at scale unless the decision policy is formally approved.

## 5. Robustness comparison

| rank | intervention | robustness across plausible assumptions | expected benefit | principal risk | reversibility | information value of a pilot |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | K-104 time-boxed preliminary screen and held C1 slot | moderate to high; benefit holds if C1 has slack and M1 signs in the morning; falls to zero if M1 is the binding constraint | about 5–12 h cycle reduction in the flagged case; better early sanctions visibility | idle or displacing held slots; incomplete packets creating loops | high: slots can be released and screens withdrawn | high: measures C1 slack, packet accuracy and displacement directly |
| 2 | K-411 parallel I2 and D2 with real-time logging | high; small benefit but low harm across assumptions | about 1.5–3.5 h, mostly absorbed by the assessment queue | false concurrency from poor logs; assessment queue absorbs gain | high: revert to sequential task creation | moderate: validates logging and concurrency rules, though effect size is small |
| 3 | K-219 route handwritten standard file to D2 | moderate; depends on the handwriting clarification rate and whether M1 can sign earlier | 0–2 h base; 8–10 h best case | clarification and rework loops; D2 overload for standard cases | high: routing reverts easily | moderate to high: clarification rate on handwritten files is the key unknown |
| 4 | K-337 no second Request More Info (policy change) | low; benefit depends on whether the discrepancy is immaterial and on the decision policy being accepted | about 70 h base cycle reduction, largest in the set | open discrepancy in a high-risk case; appeal and loss exposure; supplier incentives | low: decisions issued under the policy are costly to reopen | high in principle, but only under governance approval and with decisions that cannot easily be reversed |

Ranking rationale: the ranking weighs cycle time together with feasibility, compliance quality, spillovers and learning value. K-337 has the largest factual saving but the weakest robustness and the most serious compliance consequences. K-104 has the largest robust benefit among the feasible interventions, but its reservation mechanism needs strict time-boxing. K-411 is cheap, low-risk and nearly neutral, but its gains are small. K-219 is useful but depends on an unobserved clarification rate.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is K-411. Its benefit is small, roughly 1.5–3.5 hours, because assessment rather than document checking sets the completion time. But it holds under almost any queue assumption, does not change decision policy, and is easy to reverse. K-104 is the strongest candidate for scale. Its gain, typically 5–12 hours, comes from removing a compliance wait of about 16 hours, but only if the decision signature is not the new bottleneck.

The most dangerous intervention despite its speed is K-337. Removing the second request loop saves roughly 70 hours, mostly by avoiding a weekend wait, but it changes what decisions are admissible. A high-risk, high-value case would be decided with a known discrepancy left open. The time saving is real. The defensibility cost does not appear in the queue model and would be borne later through appeals, losses or audit findings.

The local counterfactual is least transportable for K-104. Its gain depends on C1 having slack in a specific window. The Wednesday backlog-clearing snapshot differs sharply from Monday's load. Holding the only certified reviewer is a zero-sum move that a single-case counterfactual cannot see, because the displaced cases are not in the factual log.

The most valuable additional data are:
1. Event-level queue and reservation logs for C1 and M1, including idle held-slot time and signature availability.
2. Clarification rates by document type and by document resource, especially for handwritten files.
3. Assessor-level arrival and completion streams, to show whether earlier readiness is absorbed by the queue.
4. Supplier response-time distributions by weekday.
5. A labelled record of preliminary versus final compliance outcomes.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. `structural`: The originals-required rule, the compliance-after-final-assessment rule and the four-eyes rule hold in every counterfactual path, and S1 advice is treated as advisory rather than final compliance.
2. `temporal`: Queue snapshots are forecasts; inferred availability times for A2, D2 and C1 may differ by about ±4 hours.
3. `resource`: M1 signs in the Wednesday morning slot in the base case, and earlier signing is possible only if M1's availability changes.
4. `resource`: C1 has enough slack to hold a provisional slot for K-104 without displacing other urgent cases; this is strong and unverified.
5. `behavioral`: D2's clarification rate on handwritten documents is roughly 20–35%, and external Request More Info responses fall within the 8–36 h range.
6. `quality`: Preliminary sanctions packets are accurate enough that they trigger a Request More Info in fewer than 20% of cases.
7. `quality`: The K-337 discrepancy is either immaterial or conditionable, so an approve-with-conditions outcome would be defensible.
8. `interference`: Under the K-411 workaround, timestamps are real-time, batch entry does not create false concurrency, and other cases' queues remain as in the snapshots.
