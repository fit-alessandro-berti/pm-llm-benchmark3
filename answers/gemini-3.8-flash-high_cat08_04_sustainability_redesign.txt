# Process Redesign for Sustainable Industrial Spare-Parts Distribution

## 1. System Boundary and Assumptions

### 1.1 Environmental Scope and Boundaries
To evaluate and optimize environmental impact without compromising operational resilience, the system boundary is structured across Greenhouse Gas (GHG) Protocol Scopes 1, 2, and 3, tailored to an industrial distribution and make-to-order manufacturing process:

*   **Scope 1 (Direct Emissions):** Direct fuel combustion from distributor-owned and dedicated transport assets (specifically `dedicated_van` fleets, including deadhead/empty return mileage).
*   **Scope 2 (Indirect Emissions from Purchased Energy):** Grid electricity consumed on-site across manufacturing, warehousing, and testing cells:
    *   `CNC_A` and `CNC_B` machining energy (spindle load, standby, axis drive).
    *   `heat_treat` furnace energy (soak cycles, thermal ramping, idle holding heat).
    *   Facility baseload, handling, and `inspection_cell` operation.
*   **Scope 3 (Upstream & Downstream Value Chain):**
    *   *Upstream Embodied Footprint:* Material scrap waste (`material_scrap_kg`) from subtractive machining, thermal cracking, and rework loops. Discarded scrap embodies upstream extraction, smelting, billet forming, and transport carbon.
    *   *Consumables:* Packaging materials (`package_material_kg`) differentiating standard single-wall/corrugated packaging versus heavy reinforced timber/composite packaging.
    *   *Downstream Freight Emissions:* Third-party transport carbon (`transport_kgco2e`) encompassing commercial parcel ground, consolidated linehaul trucks, and commercial air freight across domestic and regional routes.
    *   *Reverse Logistics and Obsolescence:* Embodied footprint of finished parts scrapped due to batch overproduction (unsold after 14 days) and customer returns resulting from transit damage or missed quality defects.

### 1.2 Boundary Inclusions and Exclusions
*   **Included:** Order receipt $\rightarrow$ inventory check $\rightarrow$ internal fabrication/heat treatment $\rightarrow$ quality inspection $\rightarrow$ primary/secondary packaging $\rightarrow$ outbound logistics $\rightarrow$ delivery confirmation $\rightarrow$ direct rework/return loops.
*   **Excluded:** End-of-life disposal or operational degradation of parts after customer commissioning (decades-long lifespan outside distributor control); capital asset manufacturing of machine tools and facility construction.

### 1.3 Explicit Uncertainties
*   **Marginal vs. Average Grid Emission Factor:** The exact carbon intensity ($g\text{CO}_2e/\text{kWh}$) of electricity feeding `CNC_A`, `CNC_B`, and `heat_treat` varies by time-of-day and regional grid dispatch; average regional grid factors are assumed.
*   **Carrier Backhaul Accounting:** Whether commercial air freight and consolidated linehaul emission allocations reflect actual belly/cargo load factors or standard weight-distance carbon averages.
*   **Customer Macro-Displacement (System Leakage):** The off-boundary environmental cost incurred if a late spare part prolongs a customer’s unplanned facility downtime (e.g., emergency diesel generator run-hours or industrial plant flaring during an outage).

## 3. Intervention Options

### Option 1: Dynamic Lead-Time Slack-Based Green Logistics Router (Addressing S1 & S4)
*   **Target Path / Segment:** Standard-stock (Regular and Service-contract) and non-urgent Made-to-order orders currently utilizing `parcel_air`.
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Evaluates remaining order slack ($\text{Promised Lead Time} - [\text{Elapsed Time} + \text{P90 Ground Transit Time}]$). Where slack is positive with $\ge 85\%$ probability, the engine defaults shipping to `parcel_ground` or `consolidated_truck`. For Service-contract stock orders where stock-picking occurs within 4 hours, priority warehouse picking is paired with ground freight, directly eliminating the unneeded air freight observed in 22% of Standard-stock Service-contract cases (dropping transport carbon from $18.5\text{ kgCO}_2e$ to $3.6\text{ kgCO}_2e$ per order).
*   **Service / Quality / Resilience Effect:** Maintains OTD above 95% by dynamically checking route reliability; prevents service breaches by preserving air freight exclusively when internal processing delays exhaust slack; retains carrier contracts across both air and ground to uphold Policy R1.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence to Monitor:** Conformance violations of Policy SP1; transition frequency of `Package standard -> Ship air` vs. `Ship ground`; correlation between `promised_lead_time_hours`, dispatch timestamp, and arrival timestamp.

### Option 2: Dynamic SLA-Bounded Batching for CNC and Heat Treatment (Addressing S2)
*   **Target Path / Segment:** `Plan production -> Batch wait -> CNC production -> Heat treatment` for Made-to-order parts.
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Replaces static batch accumulation (which currently waits up to 38 hours for 12–20 parts) with an algorithmically bounded batch release. If a batch size of 8–10 parts is not reached within a maximum wait window (e.g., 12 hours for Service-contract, 24 hours for Regular), the batch is released. This avoids excessive energy per item (holding energy at $\approx 1.5\text{–}1.7\text{ kWh/part}$ vs. $2.8\text{ kWh}$ for micro-batches) while preventing downstream schedule collapses.
*   **Service / Quality / Resilience Effect:** Reduces Made-to-order median cycle times by 20–35 hours; lowers the 1.7x late-delivery risk multiplier associated with heat-treatment batch delays; reduces overproduction driven by static batch targets, directly curbing the 6% obsolescence rate.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence to Monitor:** Waiting time duration in `Batch wait`; `batch_size` distribution; queue lengths at `Heat treatment`; correlation between batch size, `energy_kwh`, and `late_delivery`.

### Option 3: Quality-Risk Machine Allocation and Load Rebalancing (Addressing S3)
*   **Target Path / Segment:** `Plan production -> CNC production` for Made-to-order parts (shifting volume from `CNC_B` to `CNC_A`).
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Currently, `CNC_B` has an 18% rework rate compared to 9% on `CNC_A`, generating excessive scrap ($1.8\text{ kg}$ vs. $0.7\text{ kg}$) and energy ($32\text{ kWh}$ vs. $16\text{ kWh}$). By assigning high-tolerance and complex Made-to-order orders to `CNC_A` by default, total scrap and rework-cycle energy are slashed by approximately 50% for those redirected cases. `CNC_B` is reserved for lower-tolerance, high-speed roughing or emergency production.
*   **Service / Quality / Resilience Effect:** Eliminates high-variance rework loops (`Inspection -> Rework production -> CNC production`), reducing median cycle times for affected orders from 160h to under 110h, and cutting late delivery rates from 32% to below 15%.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence to Monitor:** Resource allocation frequencies per `machine_group`; rework loop counts (`Rework production -> CNC production`); scrap volume by machine (`material_scrap_kg`).

### Option 4: Scheduled Bi-Daily Consolidation Windows for Stock Shipments (Addressing S5)
*   **Target Path / Segment:** `Package standard -> Consolidated truck` for Standard-stock Regular orders.
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Establishes fixed bi-daily consolidation dispatch cut-offs (e.g., 12:00 and 18:00) that aggregate parcel ground volume into full-truckload or consolidated linehauls. Consolidated trucking achieves a 35–55% reduction in transport emissions compared to fragmented single-parcel ground dispatches.
*   **Service / Quality / Resilience Effect:** Adds a predictable 6–12 hour dwell time, which fits comfortably within the 31h median cycle time and 62h p90 ceiling for Standard-stock Regular orders (which have modest 7% baseline late deliveries).
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence to Monitor:** Timestamp delta between `Package standard` and carrier departure; load fill factor; modal share of `Consolidated truck`; late delivery rate in the Regular stock segment.

### Option 5: Digital Packaging-Route Pre-Compatibility Gate (Addressing S6 & Q1)
*   **Target Path / Segment:** `Inspection -> Package reinforced / Package standard -> Rework packaging`.
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Today, 620 orders undergo `Rework packaging` because of packaging-route incompatibilities or part geometry mismatches, generating packaging waste and transit delays. Implementing an automated digital packaging selection step immediately after inspection enforces Policy Q1 (MTO parts $>8\text{ kg}$ automatically routed to reinforced packaging; lighter stock parts routed to standard packaging).
*   **Service / Quality / Resilience Effect:** Completely eliminates the 620 annual packaging rework events; avoids damage-related transit returns (which drop by 40% when reinforced packaging is properly used); prevents unnecessary use of heavy reinforced packaging on non-fragile parts, saving virgin materials.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence to Monitor:** Transition counts to `Rework packaging`; scrap rate of packaging materials (`package_material_kg`); correlation between part weight, packaging type, and customer damage claims.

### Option 6: In-Line Quality Gate Prior to Heat Treatment and Secondary Processing (Addressing S7)
*   **Target Path / Segment:** `CNC production -> Inspection (in-line gate) -> Heat treatment`.
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Currently, parts pass from CNC directly to heat treatment before reaching primary inspection (1,820 orders). When defects occur on `CNC_B`, parts absorb furnace energy ($>10\text{ kWh/order}$) and thermal transformation before the defect is caught at inspection. Moving an automated geometric check *before* heat treatment prevents defective parts from consuming thermal energy and renders rework mechanically feasible (preventing unmachinable scrap post-hardening).
*   **Service / Quality / Resilience Effect:** Reduces scrap by catching machining errors prior to irreversible metallurgical alteration; unburdens `inspection_cell` queues, preventing the rushed inspections that cause field returns.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence to Monitor:** Frequency of `CNC production -> Heat treatment` without an inspection timestamp; scrap volume at `Heat treatment`; rework success rate.

### Option 7: Dedicated Van Triage and Emergency Courier Pooling (Addressing S8 & S9)
*   **Target Path / Segment:** `Package reinforced -> Dedicated van` (Critical-downtime Service-contract orders).
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Currently, 620 orders use dedicated vans, producing an alarming $52.0\text{ kgCO}_2e$ per order, with 31% lacking an explicit Critical-downtime justification (SP2 violation). This intervention implements a two-tier digital validation: (i) verifies whether the customer machine is actively halted ("true downtime") or operating under redundant backup ("precautionary urgent"); (ii) for non-critical cases, re-routes shipments to scheduled express couriers or shared milk-run van networks, reducing empty return legs.
*   **Service / Quality / Resilience Effect:** Protects immediate 14-hour delivery for true catastrophic downtime; eliminates unverified dedicated van usage for precautionary orders without breaching contractual SLA penalties.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence to Monitor:** SP2 compliance rate (% of dedicated van cases containing verified downtime ticket attributes); modal distribution of Critical-downtime orders; empty return miles.

### Option 8: Partial Shipment Suppression via Smart Inventory Reservation (Addressing S11)
*   **Target Path / Segment:** `Availability check -> Pick from stock -> Partial shipment -> Backorder wait`.
*   **Mechanism of Environmental Improvement:** Currently, 1,010 orders experience partial shipments, with 220 entering extended backorder wait. Variant analysis shows that `Stock -> partial shipment -> ground + backorder` doubles transport carbon ($7.2\text{ kgCO}_2e$ vs. $3.6\text{ kgCO}_2e$) due to dual delivery legs, increases cycle time to 79h, and drives late delivery to 24%. By modifying inventory allocation rules to hold dispatch for up to 12 hours if an incoming stock replenishment or production order will complete the bundle, dual shipments are consolidated into a single delivery.
*   **Service / Quality / Resilience Effect:** Reduces secondary packaging and freight trips; cuts late delivery from 24% to baseline levels; improves customer receiving efficiency.
*   **Required Process-Mining Evidence to Monitor:** Frequency of `Pick from stock -> Partial shipment`; count of secondary freight dispatches per order; customer order line completion percentages.

## 5. Rebound and Displacement Risks

A systems-theoretic redesign must anticipate unintended systemic interactions where localized efficiency creates wider lifecycle degradation:

1.  **Batching Rebound vs. Obsolescence and Lead Time:**
    *   *Risk:* Maximizing machine energy efficiency by enforcing large batch sizes ($\ge 15$ parts) on `CNC_A` reduces energy per part to $1.3\text{ kWh}$, but inflates median `Batch wait` from 4h to 38h.
    *   *Displacement:* This queue explosion causes missed delivery windows, triggering customer escalation, which forces orders onto emergency air freight ($18.5\text{–}23.0\text{ kgCO}_2e$). Furthermore, 6% of large-batch items become obsolete when customer demand forecasts shift, converting saved machine energy into 100% scrapped embodied material ($0.7\text{–}1.8\text{ kg scrap/part}$).

2.  **Transport Deceleration vs. Downstream Customer Disruption:**
    *   *Risk:* Enforcing strict ground or consolidated truck routing on orders with tight promised lead times to save transport carbon.
    *   *Displacement:* A minor transit delay eliminates remaining slack, turning on-time deliveries into stockouts. For industrial clients, a missing critical spare part can halt an entire production line or refinery unit. The resulting macro-environmental damage (e.g., flaring hydrocarbons, running auxiliary diesel generators producing thousands of $\text{kgCO}_2e$) eclipses the $15\text{–}30\text{ kgCO}_2e$ saved by avoiding air freight.

3.  **Packaging De-Specification vs. High-Scrap Transit Damage:**
    *   *Risk:* Downgrading heavy Made-to-order parts from reinforced packaging to standard corrugated cartons to minimize packaging material mass (`package_material_kg`).
    *   *Displacement:* Baseline evidence shows reinforced packaging reduces transit damage by 40% for heavy parts ($>8\text{ kg}$). Eliminating reinforced packaging causes mechanical deformation or surface marring in transit, resulting in full customer rejection. The returned part must be completely re-machined from scratch, generating an additional $18.5\text{–}32\text{ kWh}$ of electrical energy and $0.9\text{–}1.8\text{ kg}$ of metal scrap, entirely wiping out the modest packaging material savings.

4.  **Machine Rebalancing Bottlenecks vs. Inspection Queue Compromise:**
    *   *Risk:* Diverting all Made-to-order volume away from defect-prone `CNC_B` to high-yield `CNC_A`.
    *   *Displacement:* Overloading `CNC_A` creates acute queue congestion. Operators under schedule pressure rush the subsequent `inspection_cell` checks. The baseline log reveals that rushed inspections fail to detect sub-surface geometric flaws, allowing defective parts to pass downstream into packaging and shipping, ultimately triggering costly customer returns and emergency rework cycles.

5.  **Split-Shipment Suppression vs. Holding Bottlenecks:**
    *   *Risk:* Mandating 100% complete order fulfillment to avoid dual-leg freight emissions.
    *   *Displacement:* Holding partial shipments until delayed line items are manufactured clogs warehouse staging areas and delays delivery of non-dependent maintenance parts. Customers facing mounting maintenance backlogs cancel orders (`Cancel due to delay`), stranding custom-machined MTO parts as unrecoverable dead inventory.

6.  **Customer Strategic Gaming of Urgency Classifications:**
    *   *Risk:* Applying carbon surcharges or extended lead-time estimates to Regular and non-urgent orders.
    *   *Displacement:* Customer procurement agents learn to systematically flag standard replacement orders as "Critical-downtime" or demand emergency production exceptions. This artificially inflates the volume entering high-emission emergency pathways, defeating triage algorithms and overwhelming dedicated courier logistics.

## 6. Balanced Recommendation: The "Closed-Loop Sustainable Flow" Package

Rather than implementing fragmented green initiatives, the organization must deploy an integrated, four-pillar redesign package that simultaneously balances carbon reduction, lead-time reliability, product quality, and supply-chain resilience.

```
       +-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
       |                 CLOSED-LOOP SUSTAINABLE FLOW ARCHITECTURE                         |
       +-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                                  |
         +----------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+
         |                                        |                                       |
         v                                        v                                       v
+------------------+                    +-------------------+                   +------------------+
|     PILLAR 1     |                    |     PILLAR 2      |                   |     PILLAR 3     |
|   Dynamic Lead   |                    | Precision Machine |                   | Quality Gating & |
|  Time & Carrier  |                    | Routing & Dynamic |                   | Packaging-Route  |
|    Allocation    |                    |     Batching      |                   |  Harmonization   |
+------------------+                    +-------------------+                   +------------------+
         |                                        |                                       |
         +----------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+
                                                  |
                                                  v
                               +-------------------------------------+
                               |              PILLAR 4               |
                               |    Predictive Governance, Conformance|
                               |        & Exception Control          |
                               +-------------------------------------+
```

### Pillar 1: Dynamic Slack-Driven Freight and Fast-Track Warehouse Fulfillment
*   **Operational Integration:** Enforce Policy SP1 via an automated dispatch rule. When orders are received, calculate internal processing time plus ground transit duration.
*   **Fast-Track Stock Picking:** For Standard-stock Service-contract orders, establish an accelerated warehouse pick-and-pack SLA ($\le 3$ hours). This internal acceleration preserves overall order lead time, allowing the company to switch transit from air to ground without extending customer delivery dates. This single action eliminates the high emissions of the 22% non-conforming service-contract air shipments while maintaining late-delivery rates below 5%.
*   **Resilience Safeguard (R1):** The transport management system maintains active commercial agreements with both ground and air carriers. If ground network disruptions or regional weather events deplete slack below an 85% on-time confidence threshold, the workflow automatically elevates the shipment to express air.

### Pillar 2: Defect-Weighted CNC Routing and SLA-Bounded Dynamic Batching
*   **Machining Triage:** Rebalance production by assigning all complex, tight-tolerance MTO parts to `CNC_A` (9% rework baseline), while reserving `CNC_B` (18% rework baseline) for rough-machining, low-complexity geometries, or emergency surge orders.
*   **Dynamic Batching Thresholds:** Replace static batch quotas with dynamic time-decay rules. In `Heat treatment` and `CNC_A`, batches accumulate up to 10 parts, but must automatically release after a maximum wait of 12 hours (Service-contract) or 24 hours (Regular).
*   **Multi-Criteria Payoff:** Reduces average batch wait times from 38 hours to $\le 14$ hours, cutting overall MTO cycle times, mitigating the 1.7x heat-treat late delivery spike, and eliminating the 6% obsolescence scrap rate while capturing 80% of maximum batch electrical efficiency.

### Pillar 3: Upstream Quality Gating and Route-Matched Packaging
*   **In-Line Dimensional Check:** Introduce a rapid in-line inspection checkpoint between `CNC production` and `Heat treatment`. Defective parts are caught prior to thermal processing, eliminating wasted furnace energy and enabling standard mechanical rework instead of complete scrapping.
*   **Rule-Driven Packaging Selection (Q1):** Implement an automated packaging routing gate based on part mass and transport mode: parts $>8\text{ kg}$ traveling via ground or consolidated freight receive reinforced packaging; parts $<8\text{ kg}$ or those moving via protected courier receive optimized standard packaging with biodegradable cushioning. This eliminates the 620 annual packaging rework events and prevents transit damage returns.

### Pillar 4: Critical-Downtime Verification and Pooled Logistics
*   **Strict SP2 Enforcement:** Require a verified machine serial number and operational outage confirmation code before releasing dedicated van dispatches.
*   **Logistics Pooling:** For legitimate Critical-downtime orders within shared metropolitan or industrial corridors, replace single-order dedicated vans with a contracted express courier network that pools emergency deliveries. This preserves the 14-hour emergency delivery timeline while eliminating empty return legs, slashing Critical transport emissions by 30–45%.

## 7. Validation and Governance Plan

To ensure that the process redesign achieves its sustainability goals without introducing invisible service erosion or quality defects, a continuous process-mining validation and governance engine must be embedded directly into operational workflows.

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    Operational Conformance & Governance Loop                                          |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|   Event Log Capture          Conformance Checking          Automated Escalation          Executive Audit              |
|                                                                                                                       |
|  [Event Stream] --------> [Rule Conformance Engine] ----> [Slack Depletion Alert] ---> [Weekly Sustainability Board]   |
|  - timestamps             - Policy SP1 (Air / Slack)      - Manual Override Gate        - Root-cause analysis         |
|  - energy_kwh             - Policy SP2 (Van / Outage)     - Emergency Logistics         - Carrier reallocation        |
|  - material_scrap_kg      - Policy Q1  (Weight / Pack)      Sign-off                    - Continuous model tuning     |
|  - route_mode             - Policy R1  (Carrier Backup)                                                               |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 7.1 Process-Mining Conformance Monitoring
Continuous conformance checks will evaluate incoming event logs against formal business process rules:
*   **Policy SP1 Conformance:** The engine flags any transition `Package standard -> Ship air` or `Package reinforced -> Ship air` where the event attribute `promised_lead_time_hours` minus internal elapsed time exceeds ground transit P90 by more than 12 hours. Any detected violation is flagged as a *Carbon-Non-Compliant Expedite*.
*   **Policy SP2 Conformance:** The transition `Package reinforced -> Dedicated van` must be preceded by an event `Verify downtime ticket`. If this event attribute is absent or unverified, the system holds automated dispatch and alerts logistics management.
*   **Policy Q1 Conformance:** Any case where `weight_kg > 8.0` and the selected path does not transition to `Package reinforced` is blocked at packaging staging to prevent transit damage.
*   **Precision and Drift Monitoring:** Continuously discover variants to verify that emergency and rework paths do not metastasize during seasonal peaks or supply chain disruptions.

### 7.2 Segment-Level SLA and Service Drift Tracking
The process-mining platform will run automated drift-detection algorithms on key performance indicators across customer tiers:
*   **Service-Contract Protection:** Daily tracking of P90 cycle times and late delivery percentages for Service-contract clients (target: $\le 5\%$ late for stock, $\le 10\%$ for MTO, $\le 3\%$ for Critical-downtime). If dynamic batching or consolidation windows create a negative trend ($\ge 2$ consecutive days of increased cycle time), dynamic batch hold caps automatically ratchet down by 25%.
*   **Backorder and Cancellation Surveillance:** Monitor the ratio of `Availability check -> Pick from stock -> Partial shipment` and track transitions to `Cancel due to delay`. If partial-shipment hold buffers cause customer cancellations to rise above 3.5%, the inventory hold parameter relaxes from 12 hours to 6 hours.

### 7.3 Multi-Tier Exception Governance and Override Protocols
Recognizing that real-world operations require operational flexibility, exceptions must be structured, auditable, and transparent:
*   **Two-Tier Authorization Workflow:**
    *   *Tier 1 (Shift Supervisor):* Can authorize ground-to-air mode shifts only when a machine breakdown or quality rework loop has consumed all delivery slack, threatening an immediate customer SLA breach.
    *   *Tier 2 (Logistics Director + Operations Lead):* Required to authorize an unverified `dedicated_van` dispatch. The request must include an account-level justification code.
*   **Sustainability Exception Log:** Every override generates a mandatory audit event in the log containing:
    *   `override_reason` (e.g., "Internal machining rework delay", "Carrier strike", "Customer line down unverified").
    *   `incremental_carbon_kgco2e` (the calculated carbon differential between standard green route and selected override).
    *   `authorizing_user_id`.
*   **Weekly Closed-Loop Governance Review:** A cross-functional board (Operations, Logistics, Quality, and Sustainability) reviews the weekly Pareto analysis of exception logs. If overrides concentrate within specific product lines or machine cells (e.g., repeated air expediting driven by late `CNC_B` rework), corrective engineering action is directed immediately to the upstream root cause rather than treating logistics symptoms.
