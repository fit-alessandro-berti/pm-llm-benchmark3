## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

I use potential-outcome notation for **policy-level interventions**. I do **not** claim natural direct/indirect effects, because they require cross-world independence assumptions such as \(Y(p,m) \perp M(p^*)\), which are implausible here given shared queues, interference, and exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounding. I define **interventional total effects**, **controlled direct effects** where a mediator is set by policy, and **interventional analogues of indirect effects** by contrasting policies that differ only in a downstream routing rule.

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | P0 current flag-triggered bundle vs P1 no flag-triggered automation | Case; all cases in study window | Total cycle time: close  arrival | Total interventional effect | Yes, as a policy contrast | Confounding by underlying risk, complexity, arrival load; shared-queue interference |
| E2 | P0 vs P1 | Case; all cases | Arrival-to-triage-completion time | Total interventional effect on early stage | Yes, as a policy contrast | Priority lane changes queue composition; case mix; interference |
| E3 | P0 vs P2: mandatory manual review vs clinician discretion, holding priority triage fixed | Case; flagged cases | Manual-review occurrence | Total effect on mediator occurrence | Yes, as a policy contrast | Human judgment uses risk score/complexity; unobserved risk |
| E4 | P0 vs P2 | Case; flagged cases | Total cycle time | Interventional analogue of indirect effect through manual-review occurrence | Partly; not a natural indirect effect | Mediator-outcome confounding; manual review assigned to harder cases; interference |
| E5 | P0 vs P3: current shared manual queue vs protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases | Case; cases routed to manual review | Manual queue wait; total cycle time | Interventional effect on waiting-time mediator | Yes, as a policy contrast | Shared-queue interference; capacity spillover to unflagged cases |
| E6 | P0 vs P4: current escalation criteria/capacity vs changed escalation criteria/capacity | Case; reviewed cases | Escalation occurrence, escalation queue wait, total cycle time | Interventional effect through escalation pathway | Yes, as a policy contrast | Escalation decisions are reviewer-driven; senior capacity scarce; interference |
| E7 | P0 vs P1 within strata of risk_score, complexity, arrival_load | Case; high-risk/high-complexity cases | Total cycle time | Conditional total effect | Partly | Small strata; residual unobserved risk; positivity |
| E8 | P0 vs P1 or P0 vs P2 | Case; all cases | Decision outcome: Approve/Reject as quality proxy | Total effect on final outcome | Partly; quality is unobserved | No ground truth; outcome may reflect policy/case mix, not decision quality |
| E9 | Priority lane vs standard triage, holding queue state and case mix fixed | Case; all cases | Triage wait | Controlled direct effect | Partly | Queue state is post-arrival and affected by other cases; interference |
| E10 | P0 vs P3 | Case; flagged cases | Manual-review service time and escalation service time | Interventional effect on service-time mediators | Partly | Service time may reflect complexity and reviewer behavior, not flag |

```text
[Arrival context]
  U_underlying_risk -> risk_score -> flag
  complexity -> flag
  arrival_load, U_queue_capacity -> all queue waits

[Flag policy]
  flag -> priority_lane -> triage_wait
  flag -> manual_review
  flag -> escalation

[Process sequence]
  Arrival -> Triage (triage_wait, triage_service)
    -> Manual Review? (manual_review -> manual_queue_wait, manual_service)
    -> Escalation? (escalation -> escalation_queue_wait, senior_service)
    -> Close

[Outcome]
  triage_wait, triage_service, manual_queue_wait, manual_service,
  escalation_queue_wait, senior_service -> cycle_time
  manual_review, escalation, U_underlying_risk, complexity,
  U_reviewer_behavior -> decision_outcome

[Interference]
  other cases' priority_lane, manual_review, escalation, arrival_load,
  U_queue_capacity, U_senior_capacity -> each queue wait
```

Interference arises because review and escalation queues are shared: a flagged case’s priority triage and mandatory review consume triage, reviewer, and senior capacity, so its treatment changes waiting times for other cases, violating SUTVA. Exposure-induced confounding arises because flag affects manual_review and escalation, which in turn affect downstream queue congestion and later routing; risk_score, complexity, and U_underlying_risk affect both flag and these mediators. Treatment-confounder feedback arises because arrival_load and queue capacity are time-varying, affected by earlier cases’ flag status and routing, and they affect later cases’ flag assignment and outcomes. Reviewer behavior may also change after seeing the flag, creating mediator-outcome confounding affected by exposure. Therefore natural direct/indirect effects are not identified without strong cross-world and no-interference assumptions.

Flagged cases are faster to triage: median arrival-to-triage-start is 12 vs 150 min and median triage service 36 vs 57 min. But their total cycle is much longer: median 987 vs 345 min. The downstream pattern is consistent with a routing/queue mechanism: all flagged cases enter manual review (10/10 vs 4/10), and flagged cases have median manual queue wait 405 vs 105 min among reviewed cases; 6/10 flagged cases are escalated vs 2/10 unflagged, with median escalation queue wait 420 vs 390 min. High-complexity cases have the longest total cycle (median 1167 min) and the highest manual/escalation use. These contrasts are not automatically causal: flagged cases have higher risk scores, more high complexity, and arrive under high load; manual review and escalation are downstream choices; shared queues create interference; and the subgroups are small.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Does flag affect cycle time after accounting for risk score? | Within-risk-score or local-threshold design: compare cases just above vs below the flag threshold, or within narrow risk_score bands | risk_score, complexity, arrival_load, time | Threshold is sharp or ignorability within bands; no unobserved risk near threshold | Balance of complexity/load across threshold; density test for manipulation | If cycle-time discontinuity disappears after adjustment or covariates jump at threshold, flag effect is confounded |
| How much delay is due to shared queue congestion? | Queue-state-aware analysis: model stage waits as function of flag, arrival_load, queue lengths at arrival/triage_done, resource capacity | Time-varying queue lengths, resource availability, arrival_load | Queue state measured adequately; no unobserved capacity | Compare flagged vs unflagged with same queue length; residual autocorrelation | If flag effect on manual/escalation waits vanishes after queue-state adjustment, delay is congestion/interference |
| What is the causal effect of flag-triggered routing? | Policy-change or randomized encouragement design: randomize flagged cases to priority lane + mandatory review vs standard routing, or randomize flag display to reviewers | None or baseline risk/complexity | Randomization, compliance, no interference or measured spillovers | Balance tables; compliance rates; spillover checks | If randomized effect on total cycle is near zero or reversed, observational association is confounded |
| Which pathways mediate? | Mediation-oriented analysis: sequential g-estimation / interventional mediation for P2 and P3 effects on manual_review, manual_queue_wait, escalation, cycle time | Baseline risk/complexity/load; possibly post-treatment queue state | Sequential ignorability; no unmeasured mediator-outcome confounding; no interference | Check mediator distributions under policies; sensitivity for unmeasured confounding | If manual_review occurrence explains most of total effect but queue wait does not, focus on routing; if queue wait explains, focus on capacity |
| Is flag associated with outcomes it should not affect? | Negative-control outcome or exposure: use residual-to-close or future arrival_load as negative controls | Same covariates | Negative control not affected by flag except through confounding | If flag predicts residual-to-close or future load, confounding/selection | If flag predicts negative-control outcome, adjust or doubt causal |
| How strong would unobserved risk need to be? | Sensitivity analysis for unobserved risk: E-value / Rosenbaum sensitivity for U_underlying_risk affecting flag and cycle time; simulate unobserved risk | Observed risk_score, complexity, load | Parameterized unobserved confounding | E-value for total effect; compare with observed risk_score strength | If small unobserved risk could explain effect, conclusion reverses |
| Are there spillovers? | Interference-aware design: cluster-randomize by time blocks or teams; measure queue lengths and outcomes of unflagged cases; use exposure mapping | Cluster/time fixed effects; exposure to flagged cases | Clusters large enough; no cross-cluster interference | Compare unflagged outcomes in high-flag vs low-flag periods; check queue spillover | If unflagged waits increase with flagged share, interference is important and policy must add capacity |
| Are timestamps reliable? | Timestamp semantics / process mining validation: audit event logs, check start/completion ordering, missing stages, clock synchronization | None | Timestamps accurate | Negative durations, missing starts, stage order violations | If timestamps unreliable, all duration contrasts invalid |

## 6. Policy comparison

P0: current system. P1: suppress flag display and disable flag-triggered automation while keeping risk_score visible. P2: retain priority triage but remove automatic mandatory manual review. P3: retain current routing and add protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases. P4: retain manual review but change escalation criteria or capacity.

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 | Fast for flagged | Manual/escalation queues long for flagged | Long for flagged, short for unflagged | Mandatory review may be protective but delays | Flagged priority triage and mandatory review consume shared capacity | Observed association only | Baseline, not ideal |
| P1 | Slower for flagged | Fewer flagged manual reviews, shorter manual queue | May shorten flagged total if manual review avoided, but longer triage | High-risk cases may not get priority/review | Less priority triage may reduce unflagged waits; manual load decreases | Weak | Not recommended alone |
| P2 | Fast for flagged | Fewer flagged manual reviews, shorter manual queue, fewer escalations | Likely shorter for flagged | High-risk cases may skip review | Less manual queue congestion for all | Moderate for occurrence, weak for quality | Pilot with safeguards |
| P3 | Fast for flagged | Shorter manual queue for flagged, minimal spillover if capacity added | Shorter for flagged | More review capacity may improve quality but over-processing | If capacity added, minimal; if reallocated, unflagged waits increase | Moderate for queue wait, weak for causal | Strong candidate pilot |
| P4 | Fast for flagged | Manual queue unchanged, escalation queue shorter if capacity added or criteria changed | Shorter for escalated flagged | Changing criteria may miss escalations or over-escalate | Senior capacity changes affect all escalated cases | Weak-moderate | Pilot with strict escalation criteria |

**Selected pilot: P3.** Eligibility: all cases with flag=1 arriving during the pilot window; exclude cases with missing timestamps or already in senior care. Assignment: randomize eligible flagged cases 1:1 to protected manual-review capacity (P3) versus current shared queue (P0), stratified by complexity (Low/Medium/High) and arrival_load (Normal/High); use time-block randomization if case-level randomization risks interference. Guardrail: protected capacity is additive (e.g., one additional reviewer slot for flagged cases during peak hours); unflagged manual-review capacity cannot be reduced by more than 10%, and unflagged manual queue wait is monitored daily. Primary outcome: total cycle time (close  arrival) for flagged cases. Secondary outcomes: manual_queue_wait, escalation_queue_wait, manual_review occurrence, escalation occurrence, decision outcome/reject rate, unflagged cycle time, and reviewer workload. Minimum process data: arrival, triage start/done, manual start/done, escalation start/done, close, flag, risk_score, complexity, arrival_load, resources, and queue lengths at each stage. Stopping conditions: stop if unflagged manual queue wait increases >15% for two consecutive days; if reject rate or appeals worsen by >5 percentage points; if protected capacity utilization is <30% or >90%; or if flagged total cycle time does not improve by at least 60 minutes within 4 weeks.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk:** If unobserved underlying risk, not captured by `risk_score` or `complexity`, strongly determines both flag assignment and long downstream processing, the observed flag effect could be mostly confounding; the recommendation would reverse.
2. **Queue interference:** If flagged cases’ priority triage and mandatory review substantially increase waits for unflagged cases, or if protected capacity merely reallocates delay, P3 would not improve system cycle time; the recommendation would reverse.
3. **Decision quality:** If mandatory manual review and escalation for flagged cases prevent errors and removing or bypassing them worsens decision quality, then P2 or P1 would be harmful and P3/P0 would be preferred.
4. **Timestamp semantics:** If timestamps are not local clock time, if start/completion are swapped, or if missing stages are recorded as dashes for cases that actually occurred off-system, the stage decomposition is invalid; the recommendation would reverse.
5. **Positivity or lack of overlap:** If there are no comparable unflagged cases with similar risk_score, complexity, and arrival_load, adjustment or threshold designs cannot identify effects; the recommendation would reverse.
6. **Behavior change by reviewers:** If reviewers change behavior after seeing the flag—e.g., rushing triage but deferring manual review, or escalating more or less—the effect of the flag is not stable; P3 may not work as expected and the recommendation would reverse.

## 9. Final synthesis

Directly observed: flagged cases have shorter arrival-to-triage-start (median 12 vs 150 min) and shorter triage service (36 vs 57 min), but longer total cycle (median 987 vs 345 min). All flagged cases enter manual review (10/10 vs 4/10), and 6/10 are escalated vs 2/10; median manual queue wait is 405 min for flagged vs 105 min for unflagged reviewed cases, and median escalation queue wait is 420 vs 390 min. High-complexity cases have the longest cycles (median 1167 min). Plausible mechanism: the flag triggers a policy bundle—priority triage plus mandatory manual review and higher escalation propensity—so early acceleration is offset by downstream queueing and more stages. Shared queues and case mix likely amplify this. Unidentified: the causal effect of the flag itself versus underlying risk/complexity; natural direct/indirect effects; interference; and decision quality. The most useful next evidence is a pilot of P3, adding protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases while retaining priority triage and mandatory review, with randomization or time-block assignment, measuring total cycle time, queue waits, unflagged spillover, and decision quality.
