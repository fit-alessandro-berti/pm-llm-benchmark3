# 1. System boundary and assumptions

I use an **order-to-close lifecycle boundary** for the spare-parts distributor, covering the environmental impacts that are visible in the enriched event log and directly controllable by the process.

## Inside the boundary

The analysis includes:

- **Production energy**: `energy_kwh` associated with CNC_A, CNC_B, heat treatment, inspection, emergency production, and rework loops.
- **Material waste**: `material_scrap_kg`, rework scrap, and obsolescence risk from batch-produced items that are not shipped within 14 days.
- **Packaging material**: `package_material_kg`, reinforced packaging, packaging rework, and damage-related return implications.
- **Transport emissions**: `transport_kgco2e` by `route_mode`: parcel_ground, parcel_air, dedicated_van, consolidated_truck.
- **Process-induced environmental impacts**: waiting, batching, partial shipments, backorders, expediting, rework, returns, and cancellations that cause repeated processing or wasted production.
- **Service, quality, and resilience constraints**: cycle time, late delivery, rework, returns, promised lead time, critical-downtime service, carrier disruption alternatives, and machine/inspection capacity.

## Outside the boundary

The following are excluded or only partially represented because the log does not provide sufficient evidence:

- Upstream raw-material extraction and supplier manufacturing impacts.
- Capital equipment, warehouse building, employee commuting, and IT infrastructure impacts.
- Use-phase impacts of the spare parts at the customer site.
- End-of-life recycling or disposal beyond recorded scrap.
- Full customer downtime cost, although Critical-downtime service is treated as a resilience/service constraint.
- Carrier-internal emissions factors beyond the provided `transport_kgco2e`.

## Key assumptions

1. The enriched attributes are reliable enough for segment-level analysis: `case_type`, `customer_type`, `route_mode`, `machine_group`, `batch_id`, `batch_size`, `energy_kwh`, `material_scrap_kg`, `package_material_kg`, `transport_kgco2e`, `rework_flag`, `expedite_flag`, and `promised_lead_time_hours`.
2. `transport_kgco2e` reflects transport-stage emissions only, not packaging or production-stage lifecycle impacts.
3. `energy_kwh` reflects process energy at machine or order level, not full facility energy.
4. Rework and returns are environmentally relevant because they repeat production, packaging, inspection, and transport.
5. Obsolescence is an environmental issue because produced items that are not shipped within 14 days may become scrap or require disposal.
6. Emergency paths and partial shipments are highly variable; the discovered model has low precision, so governance and exception review are needed alongside statistical monitoring.

## Lifecycle priority implied by the evidence

The case mix suggests two different sustainability problem clusters:

- **Made-to-order cases** are about 34% of orders but dominate production energy and scrap. The segment summary shows MTO energy around 18.5–20.0 kWh/order and scrap around 0.85–0.90 kg/order, versus 0.8–0.9 kWh and 0.05–0.06 kg for Standard-stock cases.
- **Critical-downtime cases** are about 11% of orders but have very high transport emissions, averaging 38.0 kgCO2e/order, with dedicated-van paths around 52.0 kgCO2e/order.

Therefore, the redesign should not simply minimize one metric. It should reduce **MTO energy/scrap/rework** while reducing **unnecessary high-carbon transport**, without harming true critical-downtime service.

| Rework / quality | Packaging rework frequency | `Rework packaging` events | Indicates packaging-route incompatibility. |
| Rework / quality | Damage-related returns | return/rework events, Q1 violations | Reinforced packaging and inspection affect this. |
| Batching | Median and p90 batch wait | `Batch wait`, `batch_id`, timestamps | Batch wait drives late-delivery and obsolescence risk. |
| Batching | Batch size distribution | `batch_size` | Determines energy efficiency and overproduction risk. |
| Service | Median and p90 cycle time by segment | case timestamps, `case_type`, `customer_type` | Detects unacceptable service deterioration. |
| Service | Late delivery rate by segment | promised vs actual delivery | Core service guardrail. |
| Service | Cancellation due to delay | `Cancel due to delay` | Indicates resilience/service failure. |
| Service | Partial shipment and backorder rate | `Partial shipment`, `Backorder wait` | Partial shipments increase transport and customer friction. |
| Resilience | Alternative route availability for Service-contract customers | `route_mode`, carrier disruption events | Required by policy R1. |
| Resilience | Emergency production and expedite frequency | `Emergency production`, `expedite_flag` | High expediting indicates brittle planning. |
| Resilience | Inspection queue length | `inspection_cell` timestamps | Rushed inspection creates rework loops. |

# 3. Intervention options

Below are ten intervention options. They are not isolated green ideas; they are intended to be combined with guardrails.

## I1. Green shipping recommender using lead-time slack and route reliability

- **Target path or segment**:  
  Standard-stock Service-contract and Made-to-order orders currently shipped by air despite sufficient promised lead time. Relevant paths include `Package standard -> Ship air` and `Package reinforced -> Ship air`.

- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**:  
  At the ship decision point, calculate remaining slack:

  `slack = promised_lead_time_hours - predicted handling time - predicted transport time`

  If ground or consolidated truck can meet the promise with probability above 85%, the system recommends the lower-carbon mode. This directly reduces `transport_kgco2e` by avoiding air where it is not service-necessary.

- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**:  
  Service is protected by using probabilistic route reliability rather than a blanket ban on air. Quality is unaffected if packaging rules remain enforced. Resilience is protected by retaining air or an alternative route when carrier disruption risk is high.

- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:  
  Monitor `route_mode`, `promised_lead_time_hours`, actual cycle time, late delivery, `transport_kgco2e`, `expedite_flag`, and SP1 violations. Compare air-to-ground switched orders against similar orders that remained air.

---

## I2. Replace air with priority pick + ground for Standard-stock Service-contract orders

- **Target path or segment**:  
  Standard-stock Service-contract orders, 16% of cases, where average transport is 7.8 kgCO2e/order and SP1 is violated in 22% of cases. The high-emission path is `Stock -> standard pack -> air`, with transport around 18.5 kgCO2e/order.

- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**:  
  Many Service-contract stock orders are shipped by air even when promised lead time may allow ground. By adding priority picking and using ground when slack is sufficient, the process can reduce transport emissions without necessarily breaching SLA.

- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**:  
  Cycle time may increase from the air path median of 17h toward the ground path median of around 34h, but this is acceptable only when promised lead time and route reliability allow it. Quality is unaffected. Resilience requires keeping air as a fallback during carrier disruption or for genuinely tight SLAs.

- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:  
  Monitor pick-to-pack duration, `route_mode`, late delivery, `transport_kgco2e`, customer type, promised lead time, and SP1 conformance. Track whether priority picking creates internal bottlenecks or overtime.

---

## I3. Consolidated truck departures twice daily for non-urgent orders

- **Target path or segment**:  
  Non-urgent Standard-stock and some Made-to-order orders currently shipped as individual parcel_ground. Relevant paths are `Package standard -> Consolidated truck` and `Package reinforced -> Consolidated truck`.

- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**:  
  Consolidated trucks reduce transport kgCO2e by about 35–55% versus individual ground parcels. The trade-off is a 12–24h consolidation wait. The rule should apply only when promised lead time has enough slack.

- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**:  
  Cycle time increases by the consolidation window. Late-delivery risk rises if consolidation is applied to orders with insufficient slack. Resilience risk exists if truck capacity becomes a single point of failure; parcel_ground or air should remain available as exception routes.

- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:  
  Monitor consolidated truck share, consolidation wait time, truck fill rate, `transport_kgco2e`, late delivery, p90 cycle time, and carrier disruption events. Check that consolidation does not push MTO orders into expediting later.

---

## I4. Dynamic batching for CNC and heat treatment with segment-specific waiting caps

- **Target path or segment**:  
  Made-to-order cases entering `Plan production -> Batch wait -> CNC production` and later heat treatment. The DFG shows 3,020 cases entering batch wait.

- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**:  
  CNC_A energy per item decreases from 2.8 kWh at batch size 1–3 to 1.3 kWh at batch size 12–20. Heat treatment energy per item is lowest above batch size 10. Dynamic batching captures this energy benefit while preventing excessive waiting by using segment-specific caps.

- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**:  
  Batch wait increases cycle time. The evidence shows median batch wait rising from 4h to 38h as batch size increases, and heat-treatment batch waiting increases late-delivery risk by 1.7x. Therefore, batching must be constrained by promised lead time, customer segment, and forecast confidence. Critical-downtime orders should not wait for batches. Resilience requires an urgent non-batched lane.

- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:  
  Monitor `batch_size`, batch wait duration, `energy_kwh`, late delivery, `material_scrap_kg`, obsolescence within 14 days, heat-treatment queue, and expedite events after long batch waits.

---

## I5. Shift Made-to-order production from CNC_B to CNC_A when due-date slack allows

- **Target path or segment**:  
  Made-to-order cases routed through CNC_B, especially the path `MTO -> batch wait -> CNC_B -> inspection -> rework -> ground`, which has high energy, scrap, and late delivery: median 160h, 32% late, 32.0 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap.

- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**:  
  CNC_B is faster but has a higher rework rate: 18% versus 9% on CNC_A for Made-to-order cases. Rework repeats CNC production, inspection, packaging, and sometimes transport. Routing to CNC_A when slack exists reduces total energy and scrap by avoiding rework loops.

- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**:  
  CNC_A may have longer queue time, but total cycle time can improve if rework loops are avoided. Quality improves due to lower rework. Resilience risk appears if too much load is shifted to CNC_A and creates a bottleneck; CNC_B should remain available for urgent jobs where speed outweighs rework risk.

- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:  
  Monitor `machine_group`, `rework_flag`, `energy_kwh`, `material_scrap_kg`, queue time, cycle time, late delivery, and due-date slack. Track whether CNC_A utilization creates new p90 delays.

---

## I6. Add inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations

- **Target path or segment**:  
  Cases passing through `Inspection -> Rework production -> CNC production`. The DFG shows 470 inspection-to-rework transitions and 390 rework-to-CNC transitions. Inspection_cell queues are associated with missed defects.

- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**:  
  A risk-based inspection gate reduces hidden material waste and repeated production. High-risk combinations, such as CNC_B plus heavy Made-to-order parts, receive additional inspection or slower release. This reduces scrap, rework energy, return transport, and replacement production.

- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**:  
  Inspection time may increase, creating a potential bottleneck. However, service can improve because fewer defective parts reach packaging or customers. Quality improves. Resilience requires enough inspection capacity so that the gate does not cause rushed inspections elsewhere.

- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:  
  Monitor inspection queue length, inspection duration, `rework_flag`, rework loops, `material_scrap_kg`, damage returns, Q1 packaging certification events, and late delivery for orders held at inspection.

---

## I7. Improve packaging-route compatibility checks before shipment

- **Target path or segment**:  
  Packaging rework paths: `Package standard -> Rework packaging`, `Package reinforced -> Rework packaging`, and subsequent loops. The DFG shows 260 standard-pack rework entries and 360 reinforced-pack rework entries.

- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**:  
  Packaging rework wastes package material, handling energy, and time. A compatibility check before shipment validates package type against `route_mode`, part geometry, weight, and carrier constraints. This reduces repeated packaging, damage returns, and emergency replacements.

- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**:  
  Service improves because fewer orders are delayed by repackaging. Quality improves because packaging is matched to transport risk. Resilience is supported if the check includes alternative packaging rules during carrier disruptions.

- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:  
  Monitor `Rework packaging` frequency, `package_material_kg`, `route_mode`, damage-related returns, Q1 violations, cycle time from packaging to ship, and repeated packaging events.

---

## I8. Split Critical-downtime into true downtime and precautionary urgent categories

- **Target path or segment**:  
  Critical-downtime Service-contract cases, 11% of orders, with average transport 38.0 kgCO2e/order and dedicated-van paths around 52.0 kgCO2e/order.

- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**:  
  Not all Critical-downtime orders represent actual production stoppage. Some may be precautionary. By classifying true downtime versus precautionary urgency, the process can reserve dedicated vans and emergency production for genuine downtime cases. Precautionary urgent cases can use priority ground, consolidated truck, or air only when slack and SLA require it.

- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**:  
  True downtime service is protected and may improve because emergency capacity is less congested. Precautionary cases may experience longer lead time, but this is acceptable if classified transparently. Resilience depends on accurate classification and a clear escalation path.

- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:  
  Monitor downtime reason code, `expedite_flag`, `route_mode`, dedicated-van justification event, `transport_kgco2e`, late delivery, emergency production frequency, and customer escalation rate.

---

## I9. Replace some dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes

- **Target path or segment**:  
  Critical-downtime dedicated-van paths, especially where vans return empty or where SP2 justification is missing. Dedicated-van cases have very high transport emissions, around 52.0 kgCO2e/order.

- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**:  
  Shared emergency courier lanes can combine urgent shipments, reduce empty returns, and lower kgCO2e per order. Dedicated vans remain available when no shared lane can meet the required downtime SLA.

- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**:  
  Service risk exists if shared lanes are not reliable. Therefore, this should apply only to Critical-downtime cases where the shared lane meets the promised recovery time, or to precautionary urgent cases. Resilience requires a fallback dedicated van or air option during disruption.

- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:  
  Monitor dedicated-van share, shared courier share, empty return rate, SP2 justification rate, transport kgCO2e, median and p90 critical cycle time, late delivery, and carrier reliability during disruptions.

---

## I10. Reduce partial shipments by changing availability-check rules

- **Target path or segment**:  
  `Pick from stock -> Partial shipment -> Package standard` and `Partial shipment -> Backorder wait`. The DFG shows 1,010 partial shipment cases and 220 backorder waits. The partial-shipment path has 24% late delivery and higher transport impact.

- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**:  
  Partial shipments create extra packaging, extra transport, extra customer contact, and sometimes second production runs. The availability check should promise complete shipment when the remaining items can be supplied within a reliable window. Splitting should be reserved for Critical-downtime cases or situations where partial shipment clearly improves promised service.

- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**:  
  Fewer partial shipments can reduce late delivery and customer friction. However, holding orders for completeness can increase cycle time and may increase cancellations if the rule is too strict. Critical cases need an exception path.

- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:  
  Monitor partial shipment rate, backorder wait duration, second shipment frequency, `transport_kgco2e`, `package_material_kg`, cancellation due to delay, late delivery, and customer segment.

---

## I11. Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular Made-to-order customers

- **Target path or segment**:  
  Regular Made-to-order cases, 22% of orders, with 18% late delivery and high production energy/scrap.

- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**:  
  At order receipt, the system can offer promise dates that reflect green batching, ground transport, or consolidated truck options. If the customer accepts a slightly longer promise, the order can be batched more efficiently and shipped with lower emissions. Faster promises trigger explicit expediting and carbon visibility.

- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**:  
  Service improves if promises become more realistic. Late delivery and expediting can decrease. However, customer behavior may change: some customers may accept longer dates, while others may request urgent overrides. Resilience depends on not overloading the green batching lane.

- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:  
  Monitor `promised_lead_time_hours`, accepted promise date changes, `expedite_flag`, batch size, batch wait, route mode, late delivery, cancellations, and override reasons.

---

## I12. Sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides

- **Target path or segment**:  
  All segments where air, dedicated van, emergency production, or packaging exceptions are used.

- **Mechanism of environmental improvement**:  
  The exception log records why SP1, SP2, or Q1 was overridden, who approved it, and what the expected service or resilience justification was. This makes high-carbon exceptions visible and manageable rather than hidden.

- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect**:  
  Service and resilience improve because legitimate exceptions are preserved and analyzed. Quality improves because Q1 packaging exceptions are traceable. Environmental performance improves because unjustified exceptions can be reduced over time.

- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor**:  
  Monitor exception count by type, reason code, approver, segment, route mode, machine group, rework, late delivery, and repeat exception patterns.

# 6. Balanced recommendation

I recommend a coordinated redesign package rather than a single intervention. The package should be organized around four pillars.

## Pillar 1: Protect true Critical-downtime service while reducing unjustified emergency transport

Critical-downtime Service-contract cases are only 11% of orders but have the highest transport emissions, averaging 38.0 kgCO2e/order, with dedicated-van paths around 52.0 kgCO2e/order. The objective is not to eliminate emergency transport, but to make it selective.

Recommended actions:

- Implement **I8: Critical-downtime triage** into true downtime versus precautionary urgent.
- Implement **I9: shared emergency courier lanes** for cases where a shared lane can meet the recovery requirement.
- Reserve dedicated vans for justified true downtime cases.
- Enforce **SP2 justification events** for every dedicated-van dispatch.
- Keep a fallback dedicated-van or air option for carrier disruption, consistent with resilience policy R1.

This balances environmental impact and resilience. It reduces unnecessary van emissions while protecting the cases where customer downtime is real.

## Pillar 2: Use lead-time slack to decarbonize Standard-stock and Service-contract transport

Standard-stock Service-contract orders show avoidable air usage: average transport is 7.8 kgCO2e/order, SP1 is violated in 22% of cases, and the stock-to-air path has high transport emissions.

Recommended actions:

- Implement **I1: green shipping recommender** using promised lead time, route reliability, and customer segment.
- Implement **I2: priority pick + ground** for Standard-stock Service-contract orders where slack exists.
- Implement **I3: consolidated truck departures** twice daily for non-urgent stock and MTO orders with sufficient slack.
- Apply **I10: reduce partial shipments** to avoid second transport legs where complete shipment is reliable.

This package reduces transport emissions without forcing the lowest-emission option when service probability is poor. The key guardrail is the SP1 threshold: use ground or consolidated truck only when promised lead time can still be met with probability above 85%.

## Pillar 3: Reduce Made-to-order energy, scrap, and rework without creating obsolescence

Made-to-order cases dominate production energy and scrap. The main environmental opportunity is not simply faster production, but avoiding rework and overproduction.

Recommended actions:

- Implement **I4: dynamic batching with waiting caps**. Use larger batches for CNC_A and heat treatment when due-date slack exists, but do not allow uncontrolled batch wait.
- Implement **I5: route MTO jobs to CNC_A** when queue and slack allow, because CNC_B has a much higher rework rate.
- Implement **I6: inspection quality gate** for high-rework machine/part combinations, especially where rushed inspection creates later rework loops.
- Implement **I7: packaging-route compatibility checks** and enforce Q1 for heavy MTO parts.

This pillar balances energy, scrap, quality, and service. Batching reduces energy per item, but waiting caps and forecast checks prevent obsolescence. CNC_A routing reduces rework, but machine-load monitoring prevents a new bottleneck. Inspection gates reduce hidden waste, but capacity must be protected.

## Pillar 4: Govern promises, exceptions, and customer trade-offs

Many environmental impacts arise from exceptions: air overrides, dedicated vans, emergency production, packaging rework, and partial shipments. These need visibility.

Recommended actions:

- Implement **I11: carbon-aware promise-date negotiation** for Regular Made-to-order customers, allowing batching and ground transport where customers accept realistic dates.
- Implement **I12: sustainability exception log** for SP1, SP2, and Q1 overrides.
- Review exceptions weekly to distinguish legitimate resilience/service exceptions from avoidable habit or poor master data.
- Use exception reasons to update routing rules, batching caps, packaging checks, and critical-downtime triage.

## Why this package is balanced

1. **It does not treat all high-carbon transport as equally unjustified.**  
   Critical-downtime dedicated vans can be justified; Standard-stock air often is not.

2. **It does not pursue batching blindly.**  
   Batching is allowed only with waiting caps, forecast confidence, and segment-specific service rules.

3. **It attacks hidden waste, not just visible transport.**  
   Rework, packaging incompatibility, inspection misses, and obsolescence are included.

4. **It preserves resilience.**  
   Alternative routes, urgent production lanes, inspection capacity, and fallback transport remain part of the design.

5. **It uses process evidence rather than generic sustainability rules.**  
   Each intervention is tied to observed paths, attributes, and policy violations.

## Suggested implementation sequence

1. **Quick wins**:  
   I2 priority pick + ground for Standard-stock Service-contract, I7 packaging compatibility check, I12 exception log.

2. **Operational controls**:  
   I1 green shipping recommender, I3 consolidated truck, I10 partial-shipment reduction, I8 critical triage.

3. **Production redesign**:  
   I4 dynamic batching, I5 CNC_A routing, I6 inspection quality gate.

4. **Customer-facing promise redesign**:  
   I11 carbon-aware promise-date negotiation, supported by exception governance.

# 7. Validation and governance plan

Validation should be continuous, event-log-based, and segment-aware. The goal is to prove that environmental gains are real and not displaced into rework, obsolescence, late delivery, or resilience loss.

## 7.1 Baseline and instrumentation

Before and after implementation, maintain a baseline for each segment and major path:

- Median and p90 cycle time.
- Late delivery rate.
- Cancellation due to delay.
- Rework rate.
- Energy kWh/order and kWh/item.
- Transport kgCO2e/order.
- Scrap kg/order.
- Package material kg/order.
- Partial shipment and backorder rate.
- Batch size and batch wait.
- Obsolescence rate for batch-produced items.
- Dedicated-van justification rate.
- Air usage where ground slack existed.

The event log should be enriched with:

- Lead-time slack at ship decision.
- Route reliability probability.
- Downtime reason for Critical cases.
- SP1/SP2/Q1 override reason and approver.
- Truck load factor.
- Empty-return indicator for dedicated vans.
- Inspection certification for packaging exceptions.
- Return reason code.
- Forecast confidence for batch production.

## 7.2 Conformance monitoring for SP1, SP2, and Q1

### SP1: Ground or consolidated truck when promise can be met with probability above 85%

Monitor:

- Percentage of Standard-stock Service-contract orders shipped by air despite sufficient slack.
- SP1 violation rate by segment and route_mode.
- Late delivery among orders switched from air to ground.
- Exception reasons for SP1 overrides.

Baseline concern: SP1 is violated in 22% of Standard-stock Service-contract cases. After redesign, violations should fall, with any remaining violations explicitly logged.

### SP2: Dedicated vans require explicit Critical-downtime justification

Monitor:

- Percentage of dedicated-van cases with a justification event.
- Percentage of dedicated-van cases classified as true downtime versus precautionary urgent.
- Dedicated-van transport kgCO2e over time.
- Empty-return rate.
- Critical late delivery and customer escalation rate.

Baseline concern: 31% of dedicated-van cases lack justification. The target is near-complete justification with reason codes.

### Q1: Made-to-order parts over 8 kg must use reinforced packaging unless inspection certifies low damage risk

Monitor:

- Q1 violation rate.
- Damage-related returns for MTO heavy parts.
- Packaging rework frequency.
- Package material kg/order.
- Inspection certification events for packaging exceptions.

Baseline evidence: Q1 violations correlate with return/rework loops. The validation dashboard should track whether violations fall and whether damage returns decrease.

## 7.3 Segment-level service monitoring

Service deterioration must be evaluated by segment, not only in aggregate.

| Segment | Key service guardrails |
|---|---|
| Standard-stock Regular | Late delivery should not worsen from baseline 7%; monitor p90 cycle time and cancellations. |
| Standard-stock Service-contract | Late delivery should not worsen from baseline 5%; monitor air-to-ground switches and priority pick performance. |
| Made-to-order Regular | Late delivery should improve or remain controlled relative to baseline 18%; monitor batch wait, CNC routing, and rework loops. |
| Made-to-order Service-contract | Late delivery should improve or remain controlled relative to baseline 13%; monitor expedite overrides and reinforced packaging compliance. |
| Critical-downtime Service-contract | Late delivery should remain near baseline 4%; monitor true-downtime classification, dedicated-van justification, and emergency production response. |

## 7.4 Environmental and lifecycle validation

Use process mining to compare variants before and after changes:

- Energy kWh per MTO order and per batch item.
- Transport kgCO2e per order by route_mode and segment.
- Scrap kg per order, especially for CNC_B and rework loops.
- Package material kg per order, separated by standard and reinforced packaging.
- Rework-related energy and transport.
- Obsolescence rate for batch-produced items.
- Emissions per on-time fulfilled order, to avoid rewarding low-carbon choices that harm service.

Important lifecycle checks:

1. If batching reduces energy, check whether obsolescence or expediting increases.
2. If air usage falls, check whether late delivery, cancellations, or later emergency transport increase.
3. If reinforced packaging is reduced, check whether damage returns and replacement shipments increase.
4. If partial shipments fall, check whether backorder wait and cancellations increase.
5. If dedicated vans fall, check whether Critical-downtime late delivery or escalations increase.

## 7.5 Resilience monitoring

Resilience indicators should include:

- Availability of at least one alternative route for Service-contract customers during carrier disruption.
- Carrier disruption recovery time.
- Dedicated-van fallback availability.
- CNC_A and CNC_B utilization and queue time.
- Inspection queue length.
- Emergency production capacity usage.
- Frequency of SP1/SP2/Q1 exceptions during disruptions.
- Number of orders requiring mode upgrade after initial green routing.

## 7.6 Exception governance

Create a sustainability exception log tied to I12. Each exception should capture:

- Policy violated: SP1, SP2, Q1, or internal batching/cap rule.
- Segment and path.
- Reason code: customer SLA, carrier disruption, critical downtime, quality risk, capacity constraint, or data uncertainty.
- Approver.
- Expected environmental impact.
- Actual outcome: late delivery, rework, return, scrap, transport kgCO2e.

Governance cadence:

- **Daily operational alerts** for missing dedicated-van justification, air usage with sufficient slack, and Q1 violations.
- **Weekly review** of exceptions, rework loops, batch obsolescence, and late-delivery trends.
- **Monthly redesign review** to adjust batching caps, routing probabilities, consolidation windows, packaging rules, and critical-downtime classification logic.

## 7.7 Validation method

Use a phased rollout where possible:

1. Pilot I2 and I7 on Standard-stock Service-contract and high-rework MTO paths.
2. Compare pilot cases with matched non-pilot cases by segment, promised lead time, route, and machine group.
3. Expand to I1, I3, and I10 once service guardrails remain stable.
4. Introduce I4, I5, and I6 with production capacity monitoring.
5. Introduce I8, I9, and I11 with strong exception governance.

The final success test is not simply lower CO2e or lower kWh. It is a multi-criteria improvement:

- Lower transport emissions without higher critical late delivery.
- Lower production energy without higher obsolescence.
- Lower scrap without higher damage returns.
- Less packaging waste without more rework.
- Fewer partial shipments without more cancellations.
- Fewer dedicated vans without harming true downtime recovery.
