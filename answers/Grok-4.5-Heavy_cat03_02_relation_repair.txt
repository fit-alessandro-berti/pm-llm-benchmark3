## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

The repair rests on a graded evidence model that treats the supplied domain semantics as useful but defeasible.

**Hard invariants**  
- No invention of events or objects.  
- O2O qualifiers are directional (DEL carries PK is never stored as the reverse).  
- Referential integrity: every identifier that appears must already exist in the object table.  
- Create Order relates to exactly one Order (already satisfied).  

**Strong defaults**  
- Pack Item relates to one Item and one Package; an item-only row is explained by scanner outage and is later reconciled by the scanner_ref prefix (domain rule 3).  
- Dispatch Package relates to one Package and one Delivery; parallel external_ref / carrier_ref matching is decisive when the activity type matches (domain rule 4).  
- Record Payment relates to exactly one Payment and normally one Invoice (domain rule 7).  
- Existing O2O patterns (PK packs IT, DEL carries PK) are extended only when an independent E2O seed exists.  

**Weak heuristics**  
- Identifier prefix or near-exact match (scanner_ref=PK8-2, external_ref=N-421?, N-42*).  
- Timestamp proximity and global lifecycle order (Create → Add → Pack → Dispatch → Confirm → Issue → Record).  
- Shared customer (C17), shared route (NORTH), complementary declared values / payment amounts, and sequential bank_ref.  
- Arithmetic equality of invoice total to sum of declared_values.  

**Evidence against a repair**  
- Treating arithmetic equality or amount complementarity as proof (explicit domain warning).  
- Forcing a unique delivery object onto Confirm Delivery when the external_ref is truncated (N-42*) and domain rule 5 explicitly permits an order-only portal acknowledgement.  
- Completing every object lifecycle merely for aesthetic closure.  
- Inferring an O2O edge solely from a previously assumed E2O edge and then citing that O2O edge as independent confirmation (circularity).  

Evidence is combined by priority: hard invariants eliminate candidates first; strong defaults that rest on direct identifier evidence receive confidence ≥ 0.75 and status “accept”; weak heuristics receive 0.4–0.7 and status “provisional”; purely speculative links remain “unresolved” or are marked “reject”. Identifier similarity is weighted more heavily than time or amount; amount and customer matches never override an explicit domain caveat. Lifecycle order and cardinality act only as soft consistency checks, never as decisive proof.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A (conservative / minimum-change)**  
Accepts only P01–P04. All financial and Confirm links remain provisional or unresolved.  
- ev08 and PK-8: E2O package link + O2O packs IT-B1. IT-B1 obtains a complete pack step; PK-8 becomes a mixed-order package (IT-A2 + IT-B1).  
- ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y: E2O to DEL-Y + O2O carries PK-8. Parallel to the already-observed DEL-X/PK-7 pair.  
- ev11 and the delivery objects: left unresolved; the event retains only its observed ORD-A link. Domain rule 5 permits order-only portal acknowledgement.  
- INV-44 and ORD-B: left unresolved (arithmetic never treated as proof).  
- PAY-2 and INV-44: left provisional / unresolved; unapplied cash remains a live possibility.  

Resulting lifecycles are intentionally incomplete for ORD-B (no explicit Confirm or Invoice), DEL-Y (no Confirm), and PAY-2 (possible unapplied). This is preferred: the data do not force completeness.

**Bundle B (plausible alternative – combined-customer shipment)**  
Upgrades P05–P07 to accept and adds provisional multi-delivery links for ev11 (P09 + P10).  
- Same pack and dispatch repairs as A.  
- INV-44 bills both ORD-A (observed) and ORD-B; the single invoice event is interpreted as covering the same-customer total.  
- PAY-2 settles the same invoice, producing complementary partial payments (100 + 130 = 230).  
- ev11 is treated as a multi-ack that references both DEL-X and DEL-Y (domain explicitly allows several deliveries acknowledged together).  

The full bundle yields coherent object lifecycles: both orders of customer C17 are packed (one pure, one mixed), dispatched on the same NORTH route, confirmed via the customer portal, invoiced together, and settled by two sequential payments. The price is higher reliance on weak arithmetic and truncated-identifier heuristics; uncertainty is acknowledged by keeping the Confirm links provisional rather than forced.

A stricter pure-min-change variant of A that also omits the provisional financial rows is possible but yields an almost identical downstream model; the version above already satisfies the “preserve uncertainty” requirement.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

**Order**  
ORD-A already possesses a complete observed path (Create–Add–\ldots–Confirm–Invoice). Bundle A adds nothing further; the lifecycle remains coherent. ORD-B obtains Create–Add–Pack (via the new PK-8 link) but still lacks Confirm and Invoice; this is left intentional. No suspicious many-to-many appears.

**Item**  
All three items receive Pack events. IT-A1 and IT-A2 already had O2O packs links; IT-B1 now receives both E2O and O2O evidence independently (scanner_ref is primary, not derived from the O2O). Cardinality stays 1 Item : 1 Package for each packing act; the multi-item nature of PK-8 is a package-level, not item-level, many-to-one.

**Package**  
PK-7 already complete (packs + carries). PK-8 gains packs IT-B1 and carries DEL-Y; both links rest on independent identifier evidence, so no circularity. One-to-many (one package, two items) is expected and harmless.

**Delivery**  
DEL-X already carries PK-7. DEL-Y now carries PK-8. Neither receives a Confirm link under Bundle A; both remain open-ended. No cardinality violation.

**Invoice / Payment**  
INV-44 bills only ORD-A (observed). PAY-1 already settles it. PAY-2 is left unresolved; it may be unapplied cash. No forced one-to-many settlement is introduced. Bundle B would create a 1 Invoice : 2 Orders and 1 Invoice : 2 Payments pattern that is cardinality-legal but rests on weaker evidence.

**Orphans**  
Under Bundle A the only remaining orphans are the unresolved financial and Confirm candidates; they are explicitly declared acceptable. No event or object is left without any possible interpretation.

**Circular-reasoning check**  
The O2O “PK-8 packs IT-B1” is licensed by the scanner_ref on the E2O row, not by a prior assumption of the O2O itself. Likewise the carries link is licensed by the external_ref match. No edge is used to confirm the very assumption that produced it.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**Unrepaired log**  
OC-DFG shows Pack Item → (missing) for IT-B1; Dispatch Package for PK-8 has no Delivery object; Confirm Delivery only on ORD-A; Invoice only on ORD-A; PAY-2 is an isolated Payment. OTG lacks the packs and carries edges for the B-side objects. Flattening on Order yields two short traces; flattening on Delivery yields one incomplete trace; Payment flattening shows an orphan.

**Bundle A**  
- OC-DFG gains Pack Item → Dispatch Package for the IT-B1/PK-8 path and a second parallel Dispatch → (open) Delivery path.  
- OTG gains the two new packs/carries edges; the mixed package appears as a single node connected to two Orders via their Items.  
- ET–OT graph adds Package and Delivery nodes to the Pack and Dispatch activities respectively.  
- Flattening on Order: ORD-B trace lengthens by one Pack step but still ends early.  
- Flattening on Package/Delivery: both packages now have complete Dispatch; DEL-Y appears.  
- Activity frequencies: Pack Item and Dispatch Package object counts rise by one; event counts unchanged.  
- Equivalent-execution clustering around Order remains two distinct clusters (A complete, B partial).

**Bundle B**  
All of the above plus:  
- OC-DFG and OTG acquire Invoice–Order edges for both Orders and Payment–Invoice edges for both Payments; Confirm Delivery acquires edges to both Deliveries.  
- Flattening on Order now shows both Orders sharing the same Invoice and Payment activities (possible artificial synchronisation).  
- Flattening on Delivery shows both deliveries confirmed.  
- Activity frequencies based on unique objects rise for Invoice and Confirm; total-object counts also rise.  
- Clustering may merge the two Orders into a single “combined C17 shipment” cluster, which is an artefact of the stronger assumptions.

Concrete edges that appear only under B: ORD-B → INV-44, PAY-2 → INV-44, DEL-X/DEL-Y ← Confirm. Edges that remain absent under both: any forced ORD-B Confirm or a bulk-dispatch merge.

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.ocel.obj import OCEL

# Assume events, objects, relations, o2o are already loaded as DataFrames
# with standard OCEL 2.0 column names (ocel:eid, ocel:oid, ocel:activity, \ldots)

events_orig = events.copy()
objects_orig = objects.copy()
relations_orig = relations.copy()
o2o_orig = o2o.copy()

# --- Bundle A additions (only high-confidence) ---
e2o_add = pd.DataFrame([
    {"ocel:eid": "ev08", "ocel:oid": "PK-8", "ocel:qualifier": "package"},
    {"ocel:eid": "ev10", "ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:qualifier": "delivery"},

    {"ocel:oid": "PK-8", "ocel:oid_2": "IT-B1", "ocel:qualifier": "packs"},
    {"ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:oid_2": "PK-8", "ocel:qualifier": "carries"},
])

relations_rep = pd.concat([relations, e2o_add], ignore_index=True)
o2o_rep = pd.concat([o2o, o2o_add], ignore_index=True)

# Referential-integrity & duplicate checks
assert set(relations_rep["ocel:eid"]).issubset(set(events["ocel:eid"]))
assert set(relations_rep["ocel:oid"]).issubset(set(objects["ocel:oid"]))
assert set(o2o_rep["ocel:oid"]).issubset(set(objects["ocel:oid"]))
assert set(o2o_rep["ocel:oid_2"]).issubset(set(objects["ocel:oid"]))
assert not relations_rep.duplicated(subset=["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"]).any()
assert not o2o_rep.duplicated(subset=["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"]).any()

# Construct OCEL (version-dependent constructor; adjust if pm4py < 2.7)
# For recent pm4py the signature is typically:
ocel = OCEL(
    events=events,
    objects=objects,
    relations=relations_rep,
    o2o=o2o_rep,          # may be named parameters differently in older releases
)
# Alternative older style (comment/uncomment as needed):
# ocel = pm4py.read_ocel(...)  or manual dict construction

# Discoveries
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
otg   = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot  = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

# Flattenings
flat_order    = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Order")
flat_delivery = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Delivery")

# Explicitly avoid treating o2o enrichment as semantic proof
# (pm4py.ocel_o2o_enrichment would merely materialise already-accepted edges)
print("Validation complete – inspect ocdfg / otg / etot and the two flattenings")
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. Forcing INV-44 to bill ORD-B solely because 130 + 100 = 230 invents a multi-order invoice edge that may never have existed; subsequent Order flattening will spuriously share the Invoice activity.  
2. Assigning both DEL-X and DEL-Y to the single Confirm event on the strength of N-42* creates artificial Confirm → Delivery edges and inflates delivery-success rates.  
3. Completing ORD-B’s lifecycle by adding it to Confirm or Invoice merely for symmetry produces an equivalent-execution cluster that merges two independent orders.  
4. Treating PAY-2 as necessarily settling INV-44 (instead of possible unapplied cash) fabricates a second settlement path and distorts Payment cardinality statistics.  
5. Inventing a fresh Package object for IT-B1 (instead of reusing PK-8) violates the hard “no new objects” constraint and would create an isolated pack–dispatch fragment that never appears in the real log.  
6. Merging the two simultaneous Dispatch events into one bulk event erases the observed distinction between N-420 and N-421 and collapses two Delivery objects into one, destroying parallel-path information in the OC-DFG.
