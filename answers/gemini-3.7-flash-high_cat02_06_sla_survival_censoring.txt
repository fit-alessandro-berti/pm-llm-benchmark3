# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

In high-stakes case management, the colloquial phrase "probability of breaching the SLA" conceals fundamental statistical ambiguities. When cases can terminate through pathways other than operational resolution (such as rejection, withdrawal, or external transfer) or remain unresolved at the observation cutoff, SLA compliance cannot be evaluated by simple proportions or naive duration averages. A mathematically rigorous survival and process-mining framework requires explicit definitions of the time origin, time horizon, event space, censoring mechanisms, and operational estimands.

```
                    TIME HORIZON (tau = 72 hours)
   Origin (t=0)                   |
      Submit                      |
        |------------------------>|------------------------>
        |                         |
        |-- Case S01 (Res, 18h) ->| (On-time Resolution)
        |-- Case S06 (Wit, 27h) ->| (Competing Event: Withdrawn)
        |-- Case S08 (Tra, 68h) ->| (External Handoff: Transferred)
        |-- Case S15 (Open, 35h)->| (Active Open Case: Stochastic Right Censoring)
        |                         |
        |------------------------>|-- Case S03 (Res, 76h) -> (Late Resolution / SLA Breach)
        |------------------------>|-- Case S09 (Open, 92h)-> (Deterministic Breach / Zombie WIP)
        |------------------------>|-- Case S32 (Tra, 96h)-> (Late External Transfer)
```

### Core Time and Event Definitions

* **Time Origin ($T_0$, $t = 0$):** The exact moment the case is registered in the system via the `Submit` activity. All durations and ages are measured continuously in elapsed hours $t \ge 0$ from this origin.
* **SLA Time Horizon ($\tau$):** Fixed at $\tau = 72.0$ hours from $T_0$.
* **Primary Event of Interest ($\epsilon = \text{Resolve}$):** Operational success occurring when a case reaches the terminal absorbing state `Resolve` at time $T_{\text{Resolve}} \le \tau$.
* **Competing Terminal Events ($\epsilon \in \{\text{Withdraw}, \text{Reject}, \text{Transfer}\}$):** Mutually exclusive terminal absorbing states that remove the case from the internal operating queue and permanently prevent internal resolution.
* **Right-Censoring Time ($C_i$):** For actively `open` cases, the observation cutoff time corresponds to the case age $A_i$ at the extract snapshot. Cases with $A_i \le \tau$ are stochastically right-censored with respect to the 72h horizon; cases with $A_i > \tau$ have deterministically breached the 72h window without resolving.

---

### Formal Mathematical Estimands

Let $T_i$ denote the time to the first terminal disposition, and let $\epsilon_i \in \{\text{Resolve}, \text{Reject}, \text{Withdraw}, \text{Transfer}\}$ denote the event type. Let $S_{\text{all-cause}}(t) = P(T_i > t)$ represent the overall event-free survival function (probability that a case remains open in the internal queue at time $t$).

#### Estimand 1: Probability of Failing to Resolve within 72 Hours (Operational SLA Failure)
$$\psi_1 = P(T_{\text{Resolve}} > 72 \text{ or } T_{\text{Resolve}} = \infty) = 1 - F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)$$
where $F_{\text{Resolve}}(t) = P(T \le t, \epsilon = \text{Resolve}) = \int_0^t S_{\text{all-cause}}(u^-) d\Lambda_{\text{Resolve}}(u)$ is the Aalen-Johansen Cumulative Incidence Function (CIF) for resolution, and $\Lambda_{\text{Resolve}}(u)$ is the cause-specific cumulative hazard of resolution.
* **Operational Decision Match:** **Customer Contract & Regulatory Compliance.** This estimand answers: *"Out of 100 incoming customer submissions, what proportion fails to receive a successful internal resolution within the guaranteed 72-hour window?"* Cases that are rejected, withdrawn, transferred, or still open at 72 hours all represent failures to deliver a resolved internal service within SLA.

#### Estimand 2: Cumulative Incidence of Late Resolution (SLA Breach via Delayed Resolution)
$$\psi_2 = P(72 < T \le \infty, \epsilon = \text{Resolve}) = F_{\text{Resolve}}(\infty) - F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)$$
* **Operational Decision Match:** **Late-Stage Remediation, Rework Capacity & Customer Retention.** This estimand isolates the fraction of demand that eventually achieves operational resolution but incurs SLA penalties. It governs decisions regarding expediting late-stage queues versus offering proactive service delay credits.

#### Estimand 3: Probability of Remaining Actively Open at 72 Hours (WIP Queue Congestion)
$$\psi_3 = P(T_{\text{terminal}} > 72) = S_{\text{all-cause}}(72) = P(\min(T_{\text{Resolve}}, T_{\text{Reject}}, T_{\text{Withdraw}}, T_{\text{Transfer}}) > 72)$$
* **Operational Decision Match:** **Floor Workload, Buffer Staffing & Queue Backlog Management.** This estimand measures the probability that a case remains un-dispositioned in the active pipeline at hour 72, consuming operational holding capacity, monitoring overhead, and supervisory triage.

#### Estimand 4: Cause-Specific Net Failure Probability (Hypothetical Intrinsic Capability)
$$\psi_4 = S_{\text{Resolve}}^*(72) = \exp\left(-\int_0^{72} \lambda_{\text{Resolve}}(u) du\right)$$
where $\lambda_{\text{Resolve}}(u) = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{P(u \le T < u+\Delta t, \epsilon = \text{Resolve} \mid T \ge u)}{\Delta t}$.
* **Operational Decision Match:** **Process Engineering & Workflow Speed Optimization.** Under the hypothetical assumption of eliminating competing withdrawals and rejections (treating them as independent censoring), $\psi_4$ models the intrinsic resolution velocity of the workflow mechanism itself.

### Explicit Treatment of `transferred` Cases under Plausible Assumptions

The dataset contains three transferred cases: **S08** (Cohort A, P1, 68h), **S21** (Cohort B, P1, 26h), and **S32** (Cohort B, P1, 96h). All three belong to Priority **P1** and underwent escalation. Their unobserved post-transfer trajectories require distinct structural treatments:

```
                                  +---> Assumption 1: Competing Terminal Event (Internal Loss)
                                  |     Leaves internal remit at t_transfer; never resolves internally.
                                  |
[ Transferred Case (e.g. S08) ] --+---> Assumption 2: Administrative Right Censoring
                                  |     Censored at t_transfer; assumes external resolution hazard = internal.
                                  |
                                  +---> Assumption 3: Informative Worst-Case Imputation (SLA Breach)
                                        Transfer signals unresolvable complexity; imputed as SLA breach.
```

#### Assumption 1: Competing Terminal Outcome / Operational System Loss (Primary Specification)
* **Mechanistic Premise:** From the perspective of the internal case-management organization, a transfer is an absorbing terminal boundary. Internal resources cease work, tracking stops, and the internal team cannot claim operational resolution credit.
* **Statistical Encoding:** $\epsilon_i = \text{Transfer} = 4$ at $T_i = \text{duration\_or\_age\_h}$.
* **Impact on Estimands:** The case enters the risk set for resolution from $t=0$ to $t=T_i$, then exits without resolving. It prevents internal resolution and contributes directly to the operational failure estimand $\psi_1 = 1 - F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)$.

#### Assumption 2: Administrative Non-Informative Right-Censoring at Transfer Time
* **Mechanistic Premise:** Transfer represents a neutral handoff to a partner agency where the case continues to progress toward resolution under an identical hazard profile as remaining internal cases.
* **Statistical Encoding:** $\epsilon_i = 0$ (censored) at $C_i = T_i$.
* **Impact on Estimands:** The case contributes to the risk set up to $T_i$ and is removed from subsequent risk sets. This assumes conditional exchangeability ($T_{\text{Resolve}} \perp \text{Transfer} \mid \mathbf{X}$), which is highly fragile given that 100% of transfers are high-priority P1 escalations.

#### Assumption 3: Informative Censoring / Worst-Case Failure Imputation
* **Mechanistic Premise:** Transfers are systematically used to offload severely stalled, problematic, or intractable P1 cases. The transfer itself reflects an inability to resolve within internal SLA capabilities.
* **Statistical Encoding:** S21 (transferred at 26h) and S08 (transferred at 68h) are imputed as failing the 72h SLA (e.g., resolving after 72h at $t = 120\text{h}$ or treated as non-resolution). S32 (transferred at 96h) is an observed deterministic breach since $96 > 72$.
* **Impact on Estimands:** Provides a conservative upper bound on SLA tail risk and prevents organizational "gaming" through strategic offloading.

#### Assumption 4: Best-Case Immediate External Resolution (Optimistic Sensitivity)
* **Mechanistic Premise:** External partner organizations immediately resolve cases upon receipt.
* **Statistical Encoding:** $\epsilon_i = \text{Resolve} = 1$ at $T_i$. S21 (26h) and S08 (68h) count as on-time resolutions ($T \le 72$), while S32 (96h) counts as a late resolution ($T > 72$).

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

Evaluating SLA compliance solely on completed cases (or completed resolved cases) introduces severe, compounding statistical biases. In this 32-case extract, 23 cases are completed (13 resolved, 3 rejected, 4 withdrawn, 3 transferred) and 9 cases are open.

```
       TOTAL COHORT (N = 32)
         |
         +---> Completed Cases (N = 23) ---> Resolved Only (N = 13) [Breach: 4/13 = 30.8%]
         |                                   (Ignores 9 open cases and 10 non-resolution terminations)
         |
         +---> Open Cases (N = 9)
                 |-- Age <= 72h (N = 5: S10, S15, S24, S29, S31) -> Stochastic Right Censoring
                 +-- Age > 72h  (N = 4: S09, S11, S25, S26)     -> DETERMINISTIC SLA BREACHES!
```

### 1. Survivor Bias and Truncation of Deterministic Breaches

Dropping the 9 open cases discards $28.1\%$ of the dataset. Crucially, open cases are not missing at random:
* Cases **S09** (age 92h), **S11** (age 81h), **S25** (age 79h), and **S26** (age 104h) have **already survived past 72 hours without resolution**.
* Their SLA breach probability is not speculative; it is **$100\%$ deterministic**.
* Excluding S09, S11, S25, and S26 removes four long-duration SLA failures from the denominator and numerator. A completed-case resolved analysis yields a breach rate of $4 / 13 = 30.8\%$ (S03 at 76h, S13 at 110h, S20 at 74h, S28 at 88h). However, when accounting for the 4 already-breached open cases and 1 late transfer (S32 at 96h), there are at least **9 confirmed SLA failures** out of 32 cases ($28.1\%$ absolute lower bound across the entire intake, rising to over $60\%$ under full competing-risk cumulative incidence).

### 2. Informative Censoring and "Ghost" Cases Revealed by Inactivity Gaps

Standard survival estimators assume non-informative censoring: conditional on baseline covariates, the hazard of experiencing an event is independent of the censoring mechanism. The `last_event_gap_h` metric proves this assumption is violated:

```
Case S26 (Age 104h):  [Submit]----[Screen]----[Review]------------------------( 72h Inactivity Gap )------------------------>[Cutoff]
Case S11 (Age 81h):   [Submit]----[Screen]------------[Escalate]----------------( 55h Inactivity Gap )---------------->[Cutoff]
Case S25 (Age 79h):   [Submit]----[AutoCheck]----[Screen]----[Escalate]------------( 48h Inactivity Gap )------------>[Cutoff]
Case S09 (Age 92h):   [Submit]----[Screen]------------[Review]----------------( 31h Inactivity Gap )---------------->[Cutoff]
```

* In open cases above 72h, the time elapsed since the last recorded event is massive: **S26** has been dormant for 72 hours (last event at $t = 32\text{h}$); **S11** dormant for 55 hours (last event at $t = 26\text{h}$); **S25** dormant for 48 hours (last event at $t = 31\text{h}$); and **S09** dormant for 31 hours (last event at $t = 61\text{h}$).
* In contrast, open cases below 72h exhibit active processing: **S15** (gap 2h at age 35h), **S31** (gap 3h at age 23h), **S24** (gap 4h at age 67h), and **S29** (gap 6h at age 51h).
* A long inactivity gap indicates process stall, queue abandonment, or systemic loss ("ghost WIP"). Treating dormant cases as ordinary censored observations severely underestimates future tail risk.

### 3. Competing Risks Fallacy (1 - Kaplan-Meier Bias)

A common pitfall is computing $1 - \text{KM}(t)$ for resolution while treating `withdrawn` (S06, S12, S22, S30) and `rejected` (S05, S14, S23) as censored.
* Kaplan-Meier assumes that censored subjects remain at risk and will eventually experience the event at the same hazard rate as continuing subjects.
* However, a rejected or withdrawn case is **dead to the resolution process**; it can never resolve.
* Treating competing risks as independent censoring falsely redistributes their probability mass to resolution, mathematically guaranteeing that the estimated resolution probability $\hat{F}_{\text{KM}}(t)$ is **upwardly biased** ($\hat{F}_{\text{KM}}(72) = 36.1\%$ vs Aalen-Johansen CIF $F_{\text{Resolve}}(72) = 30.4\%$).

### 4. Cohort Imbalance and Confounding by Rollout Incompletion

Naive comparisons between Cohort A and Cohort B completed cases confound process performance with cohort maturity and priority selection:
* Cohort B is newer and has a higher proportion of open cases (5 open / 17 total = $29.4\%$) compared to Cohort A (4 open / 15 total = $26.7\%$).
* Fast-track cases (such as **S18**, resolved at 12h via `AutoCheck>Escalate`) complete early and populate Cohort B's completed set, whereas complex Cohort B cases (**S25**, **S26**, **S29**) remain open.
* Most critically, **100% of P3 cases in Cohort B (S17, S20, S23, S26, S30) bypassed upfront `AutoCheck`**, starting with `Screen`. Comparing raw cohort averages attributes priority-based operational bypasses to policy failure.

### 5. Contrast: Active Open Cases Below vs Above 72 Hours

| Category | Case IDs | Duration / Age ($A_i$) | Inactivity Gap | Statistical Status | Operational Reality |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Active Open $\le 72\text{h}$** | S10, S15, S24, S29, S31 | 23h – 67h | 2h – 19h (Mean: 6.8h) | **Stochastically Censored.** Genuine uncertainty regarding 72h SLA breach. | Active WIP; expedited intervention can still prevent SLA breach. |
| **Dormant Open $> 72\text{h}$** | S09, S11, S25, S26 | 79h – 104h | 31h – 72h (Mean: 51.5h) | **Deterministic Breaches.** $P(\text{Breach} \mid A_i > 72) = 1.0$. | Abandoned / blocked backlog; breach has occurred; requires root-cause escalation. |

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

To evaluate SLA tail risk rigorously without falling into completed-case or independent-censoring traps, we synthesize seven survival and process-mining methodologies.

```
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                  STATISTICAL ANALYSIS FRAMEWORK                                  |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  1. NONPARAMETRIC MARGINAL ESTIMATION                                                            |
|     * Aalen-Johansen CIF (Primary Estimator for Unconditional SLA Compliance)                     |
|     * All-Cause Kaplan-Meier (Queue Aging & WIP Backlog Retention)                               |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  2. SEMIPARAMETRIC COVARIATE MODELING                                                            |
|     * Cause-Specific Cox Hazards (Direct Process Transition Physics)                             |
|     * Fine-Gray Subdistribution Hazards (Direct Covariate Effect on Marginal CIF)                |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  3. DYNAMIC & PROCESS-AWARE MODELING                                                             |
|     * Landmarking at t_L = 24h, 48h (Dynamic Real-Time In-Flight Prediction)                      |
|     * Multi-State Semi-Markov Model (Stage-to-Stage Transition Dynamics & Bottlenecks)           |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Methodological Comparison

| Method | Target Mathematical Quantity | Treatment of Competing Risks (`Reject`, `Withdraw`, `Transfer`) | Treatment of Open Cases (`Censoring`) | Suitability for Process Tail Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Kaplan-Meier (KM)** | $S(t) = P(T > t)$ | Treats competing events as non-informative censoring. | Censored at cutoff age $A_i$. | **Flawed for Resolution CIF.** Upwardly biased. Valid only for composite all-cause queue survival $S_{\text{all-cause}}(t)$. |
| **Aalen-Johansen (AJ / CIF)** | $F_k(t) = P(T \le t, \epsilon = k)$ | Explicitly models distinct competing terminal states. | Censored at cutoff age $A_i$. | **Gold Standard for Marginal SLA Risk.** Nonparametric, unbiased, sums to $1 - S_{\text{all-cause}}(t)$. |
| **Cause-Specific Hazards (CSH)** | $\lambda_k(t \mid \mathbf{X}) = \lambda_{0k}(t) e^{\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^T \mathbf{X}}$ | Censored at competing event times; models instantaneous event rate. | Censored at cutoff age $A_i$. | **Ideal for Etiological & Queue Physics.** Shows how covariates alter transition rates out of active state. |
| **Fine-Gray Subdistribution (SDH)** | $\bar{\lambda}_k(t \mid \mathbf{X}) = \bar{\lambda}_{0k}(t) e^{\boldsymbol{\gamma}_k^T \mathbf{X}}$ | Retains competing events in risk set forever with zero hazard. | Censored at cutoff age $A_i$. | **Direct Covariate Effects on CIF.** Coefficients map directly to cumulative incidence scale. |
| **Restricted Mean Time (RMST / RMTL)** | $\mu(\tau) = \int_0^\tau S(t) dt$ | Computes restricted mean time lost across competing causes. | Handled via area under CIF / KM curves up to $\tau = 72\text{h}$. | **Excellent for Executive Reporting.** Expresses SLA risk in expected lost hours within horizon $\tau$. |
| **Landmarking Analysis** | $P(T \le 72, \epsilon=1 \mid T > t_L, \mathcal{H}_{t_L})$ | Accounts for all intermediate events observed prior to landmark time $t_L$. | Subsets population alive at $t_L$; censored at cutoff. | **Essential for Dynamic In-Flight Alerts.** Avoids immortal time bias for post-baseline events. |
| **Multi-State Model** | $P_{ij}(s, t) = P(X(t)=j \mid X(s)=i)$ | Multi-state transition matrix between transient and absorbing states. | Handles right censoring at any transient state. | **Comprehensive Process Digital Twin.** Maps full control-flow bottlenecks directly to tail risk. |

---

### Recommended Primary and Secondary Approaches

#### Primary Strategy: Aalen-Johansen CIF + Cause-Specific Cox Landmarking
1. **Marginal Estimation:** Use the nonparametric **Aalen-Johansen estimator** as the primary source of truth for organizational SLA compliance. It computes $F_{\text{Resolve}}(72) = 30.4\%$, establishing that $69.6\%$ of incoming cases fail to achieve on-time resolution under primary competing-risk assumptions.
2. **Dynamic In-Flight Prediction:** Implement **Landmarking at $t_L = 24\text{h}$ and $t_L = 48\text{h}$** combined with Cause-Specific Cox models. At each landmark $t_L$, the active risk set is updated with time-fixed realizations of process history (e.g., rework count accrued by $t_L$, escalation triggered by $t_L$, current stage at $t_L$). This prevents immortal time bias while delivering dynamic failure probabilities for live queue management.

#### Secondary Strategy: Multi-State Semi-Markov Process Model
* Formulate a multi-state transition model with transient states (`Submitted`, `Screening`, `Automated Check`, `In Review`, `Escalated`, `Rework`) and absorbing states (`Resolved`, `Rejected`, `Withdrawn`, `Transferred`).
* Transitions are parameterized by clock-reset semi-Markov sojourn times. This enables process mining simulations: calculating how reducing `Screen` sojourn time or eliminating rework loops compresses the 72h tail risk.

# 5. Process-aware features and stage hazards

Prefix sequences encode rich control-flow and temporal signals. However, naively incorporating downstream process activities into survival models introduces fatal methodological errors: immortal time bias and target leakage.

```
[ t = 0: Submit ] ------------> [ t = 20h: Escalate ] ------------> [ t = 50h: Review ] ------------> [ t = 76h: Resolve ]
       |                               |
       |-- Baseline Feature            +-- Time-Varying Event / Mediator (IMMORTAL TIME if put at t=0!)
           (Priority, Cohort)              Case MUST survive to t=20h to escalate.
```

### Feature Taxonomy and Methodological Risk

| Feature Name | Derivation from Prefix / Extract | Feature Class | Leakage & Immortal Time Risk | Proper Modeling Implementation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`priority` (P1, P2, P3)** | Baseline attribute at submission. | **Baseline Covariate** | **Zero Risk.** Known at $t=0$. | Stratification variable or baseline main effect in Cox / CIF models. |
| **`cohort` (A, B)** | Policy regime indicator. | **Baseline Covariate** | **Zero Risk.** Known at $t=0$. | Stratification variable; evaluate interaction with priority. |
| **`is_autocheck_first`** | `Submit>AutoCheck` vs `Submit>Screen`. | **Baseline / Initial Routing** | **Low Risk.** Occurs at process inception ($t \approx 0$). | Initial branch covariate in stage hazard models. |
| **`current_stage`** | Last observed activity in prefix for open cases. | **Time-Varying State** | **Severe Leakage if Static.** Case must survive to reach stage. | Multi-state transition model or landmark state encoding. |
| **`escalated` (yes/no)** | Occurrence of `Escalate` in prefix. | **Post-Treatment Mediator** | **FATAL IMMORTAL TIME BIAS if at $t=0$.** Cases marked `yes` had to survive to escalation time. | Time-dependent covariate $I(\text{Escalate} \le t)$ or landmarked status at $t_L$. |
| **`rework_count`** | Count of `Rework` events in trace prefix. | **Time-Varying Process Loop** | **High Immortal Time Risk.** Accumulates over time; indicates stalling. | Cumulative counting process $N_{\text{rework}}(t)$ in time-varying Cox model. |
| **`last_event_gap_h`** | Duration from last recorded activity to cutoff. | **Time-Varying Queue Dynamic** | **Informative Censoring Proxy.** Increases monotonically during stalls. | Hazard multiplier for abandonment; trigger for dormant alert. |
| **`gap_to_age_ratio`** | $\text{last\_event\_gap\_h} / \text{duration\_or\_age\_h}$. | **Time-Varying Inactivity Index** | **High Risk if unlagged.** Distinguishes active throughput from dead WIP. | Predictor in landmark model: ratio $> 0.5$ signals zombie case. |

# 6. Sensitivity analysis

Because observational process logs contain ambiguous terminal states, non-random policy rollouts, and informative censoring, a single point estimate is unscientific. We evaluate four structured sensitivity analyses to establish bounding intervals for SLA tail risk.

1. **Priority Hierarchy Reversal (P1 vs P2):**

2. **Cohort B Superiority Reversal (Intention-to-Treat vs As-Treated):**

3. **The Escalation Speed Paradox:**

Treating escalation as a homogeneous effect obscures this operational duality.

# 7. Operational interpretation

Survival curves and process hazard models must be translated into actionable dispatch logic, automated queue governance, and executive controls without confusing statistical prediction with causal effects.

```
DYNAMIC SURVIVAL-BASED QUEUE GOVERNANCE

  [ In-Flight Case Aging ] ---> Calculate P(Resolve <= 72h | History at t)
                                        |
      +---------------------------------+---------------------------------+
      |                                 |                                 |
  Prob >= 80%                     50% <= Prob < 80%                  Prob < 50%  OR  Gap > 24h
  [ GREEN: Normal Queue ]         [ AMBER: Supervisor Watch ]        [ RED: Immediate Intervention ]
  Standard FIFO processing.       Prioritize in specialist queue.    Direct triage; clear blocker;
                                                                     prevent ghost abandonment.
```

### 1. Dynamic Survival Alerts and Workload Dispatch Triggers

Rather than static threshold timers (e.g. alerting at hour 60), the dispatch engine continuously updates the conditional survival probability:
$$P(\text{Resolve} \le 72 \mid T > t, \mathcal{H}_t) = \frac{F_{\text{Resolve}}(72 \mid \mathcal{H}_t) - F_{\text{Resolve}}(t \mid \mathcal{H}_t)}{S_{\text{all-cause}}(t \mid \mathcal{H}_t)}$$
* **Green Status (Low Risk, $P \ge 0.80$):** Standard automated routing.
* **Amber Warning (Moderate Risk, $0.50 \le P < 0.80$):** Triggered when a case enters a second `Review` loop or when inactivity gap $G_t$ exceeds 12 hours. Case is bumped to head-of-line priority.
* **Red Alert (High Risk / Imminent Breach, $P < 0.50$ or $G_t > 24\text{h}$):** Triggered immediately for any case with an inactivity gap exceeding 24 hours (detecting zombie WIP like S09, S11, S25, S26) or any P1 case reaching $t = 40\text{h}$ without escalation.

### 2. Guardrails Against Non-Causal Misinterpretations

Operations leadership must be explicitly cautioned against treating predictive hazard weights as causal levers:
* **"Escalation does not cure cases":** A regression showing that escalated cases have higher breach hazard does *not* mean escalation causes failure. Escalation is an indicator of underlying case difficulty (confounding by indication). Mandating fewer escalations will increase breaches, not decrease them.
* **"AutoCheck speed vs coverage":** AutoCheck resolves simple cases rapidly (S16 at 16h, S18 at 12h), but pushing complex cases through AutoCheck without adequate review capacity creates downstream rework loops (S27, S28).

### 3. Priority and Cohort Fairness Audits

To prevent "priority inversion" (where operational teams meet aggregate SLAs by gaming lower-effort cases while neglecting high-priority demand):
* **Auditing Transfer Laundering:** Monitor P1 transfer rates. If P1 compliance is maintained by transferring $37.5\%$ of P1 cases to external organizations (S08, S21, S32), the SLA metric is artificially inflated.
* **Cohort Rollout Audits:** Mandate automated screening compliance verification. Cohort B's complete omission of AutoCheck for P3 cases must be flagged as a policy violation requiring remediation.

def run_aalen_johansen(data_subset, transfer_mode='competing_risk'):
    """
    Computes nonparametric Aalen-Johansen Cumulative Incidence Functions (CIF)
    and all-cause survival without dropping open cases.
    
    transfer_mode options:
      - 'competing_risk': Transfer is an absorbing competing terminal outcome.
      - 'censored': Transfer is non-informative right-censoring at transfer time.
      - 'resolved_at_transfer': Best-case imputation (resolved at transfer time).
    """
    d = data_subset.copy()
    unique_times = np.sort(d['duration_or_age_h'].unique())
    
    s_prev = 1.0
    cif_res = 0.0
    cif_rej = 0.0
    cif_wit = 0.0
    cif_tra = 0.0
    
    table = []
    
    for t in unique_times:
        sub_t = d[d['duration_or_age_h'] == t]
        at_risk = (d['duration_or_age_h'] >= t).sum()
        
        n_res = (sub_t['observed_status'] == 'resolved').sum()
        n_rej = (sub_t['observed_status'] == 'rejected').sum()
        n_wit = (sub_t['observed_status'] == 'withdrawn').sum()
        n_tra = (sub_t['observed_status'] == 'transferred').sum()
        n_open = (sub_t['observed_status'] == 'open').sum()
        
        if transfer_mode == 'competing_risk':
            d_events = n_res + n_rej + n_wit + n_tra
            inc_res = s_prev * (n_res / at_risk) if at_risk > 0 else 0.0
            inc_rej = s_prev * (n_rej / at_risk) if at_risk > 0 else 0.0
            inc_wit = s_prev * (n_wit / at_risk) if at_risk > 0 else 0.0
            inc_tra = s_prev * (n_tra / at_risk) if at_risk > 0 else 0.0
        elif transfer_mode == 'censored':
            d_events = n_res + n_rej + n_wit
            inc_res = s_prev * (n_res / at_risk) if at_risk > 0 else 0.0
            inc_rej = s_prev * (n_rej / at_risk) if at_risk > 0 else 0.0
            inc_wit = s_prev * (n_wit / at_risk) if at_risk > 0 else 0.0
            inc_tra = 0.0
        elif transfer_mode == 'resolved_at_transfer':
            d_events = (n_res + n_tra) + n_rej + n_wit
            inc_res = s_prev * ((n_res + n_tra) / at_risk) if at_risk > 0 else 0.0
            inc_rej = s_prev * (n_rej / at_risk) if at_risk > 0 else 0.0
            inc_wit = s_prev * (n_wit / at_risk) if at_risk > 0 else 0.0
            inc_tra = 0.0
            
        cif_res += inc_res
        cif_rej += inc_rej
        cif_wit += inc_wit
        cif_tra += inc_tra
        
        s_curr = s_prev * (1.0 - d_events / at_risk) if at_risk > 0 else s_prev
        
        table.append({
            'time': t,
            'at_risk': at_risk,
            'n_res': n_res,
            'n_rej': n_rej,
            'n_wit': n_wit,
            'n_tra': n_tra,
            'n_open': n_open,
            's_open': s_curr,
            'cif_resolved': cif_res,
            'cif_rejected': cif_rej,
            'cif_withdrawn': cif_wit,
            'cif_transferred': cif_tra
        })
        s_prev = s_curr
        
    return pd.DataFrame(table)

def extract_sla_metrics(aj_table, tau=72):

        'SLA_Failure_72 (1 - F_res_72)': 1.0 - cif_72,
        'Late_Resolution_CIF': cif_final - cif_72,
        'Total_Resolution_CIF': cif_final,
        'Active_WIP_72 (S_open_72)': s_open_72
    }

# 9. Limitations and additional data

To transition this statistical framework into a production-grade predictive and causal inference system, the following data gaps must be addressed in prioritized order:

### 1. High-Resolution Event Timestamps (Priority 1)
* **Current Limitation:** The dataset provides only aggregate case duration and `last_event_gap_h`. Intermediate event timestamps are unobserved.
* **Inference Value:** Exact timestamps enable calculating activity-level queue times versus service times, isolating whether delays stem from processing complexity or queue congestion before `Review` and `Escalate`.

### 2. External Outcome Telemetry for Transferred Cases (Priority 1)
* **Current Limitation:** Tracking ceases upon `Transfer` (S08, S21, S32).
* **Inference Value:** All three transferred cases belong to Priority P1. Partner telemetry indicating final resolution time and outcome is essential to eliminate the $25.0\%$ to $53.1\%$ sensitivity spread in P1 SLA compliance.

### 3. Calendar Ingestion Timestamps and Truncation Dates (Priority 2)
* **Current Limitation:** Only relative duration/age is available; calendar submission dates and the extract freeze date are absent.
* **Inference Value:** Calendar dates permit modeling seasonal arrival spikes, weekend staffing effects, and non-parametric truncation adjustments for rolling intake cohorts.

### 4. Time-Varying Queue Depth, Workload, and Staffing Levels (Priority 2)
* **Current Limitation:** Operational congestion at the moment of case transitions is unknown.
* **Inference Value:** Queue depth serves as a time-varying confounder. Disentangling intrinsic case difficulty from operational resource starvation is necessary for fair workload dispatching.

### 5. Assignment Mechanism and Policy Rollout Logs for Cohort B (Priority 3)
* **Current Limitation:** Assignment to Cohort B was non-randomized, and 100% of P3 cases bypassed `AutoCheck`.
* **Inference Value:** Formal compliance logs and routing rules are required to construct instrumental variables or propensity scores for estimating the true causal effect of automated screening.

### 6. Root-Cause Classification for Dormant Inactivity Gaps (Priority 3)
* **Current Limitation:** It is unknown whether long gaps (e.g. S26 gap 72h, S11 gap 55h) reflect client waiting, vendor holds, or orphaned system records.
* **Inference Value:** Reason codes enable distinguishing client-induced delays (excludable from regulatory SLAs) from internal process failures.
