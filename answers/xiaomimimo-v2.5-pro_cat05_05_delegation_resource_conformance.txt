## 2. Rule Interpretation

### RR1 — Drafter / Approver Separation

**Formalization:** For every case *c*, let *D(c)* be the set of humans who performed `Draft_Change_Request` in *c*, and *A(c)* be the set who performed `Approve_Change`. A violation exists if *D(c)  A(c)  * after alias normalization. Risk level is irrelevant — the rule applies to all cases universally.

**Alias normalization requirement:** Because `b.koch` and `u_bk` resolve to the same human (Ben Koch), any case where one alias drafted and the other approved is a violation.

### RR2 — Implementer / Verifier Separation

**Formalization:** For every case *c*, let *I(c)* be the set of humans who performed `Implement_Change` and *V(c)* be the set who performed `Verify_Implementation`. A violation exists if *I(c)  V(c)  * after alias normalization and service-account attribution.

**Service-account treatment:** If `Verify_Implementation` is performed by `svc_qms`, it is excluded from the automated intersection check because the human identity is unknown. A manual attribution step is required; if the attributed human is the implementer, the violation is confirmed.

### RR3 — High-Risk Approval Authority

**Formalization:** For every HIGH-risk case *c* where `Approve_Change` occurs at time *t*, the performing human *h* must satisfy at least one of:
1. *h* has a standing QM role with an interval covering *t*, **or**
2. *h* is the delegate of a valid QM delegation *D* where *t  [D.start, D.end]*, *D.scope  c.scope*, the principal is absent or formally unavailable at *t*, and no prohibited subdelegation occurred.

**Delegation validity conditions:**
- **Temporal:** The event timestamp must fall within the delegation's effective interval (inclusive boundaries).
- **Scope:** The delegation's scope must cover the case's scope. "Production-equipment changes" does not cover "cooling_system" or "training_document" changes unless explicitly stated.
- **Principal absence:** The delegating principal must be absent or formally unavailable. Annual leave, sick leave, and documented unavailability count. Weekend non-attendance is **ambiguous** — see below.
- **No subdelegation:** If the delegation prohibits subdelegation, any purported sub-sub-delegation is void.

### RR4 — Emergency Substitution

**Formalization:** A HIGH-risk case with `emergency = true` may use the emergency pathway if:
1. `Approve_Change` is performed by a human with standing IC role or valid IC delegation at the event timestamp.
2. `Retrospective_QM_Review` is performed by the QM or a valid QM delegate **within two business days** of the emergency implementation.

**Business-day counting:** The clock starts on the first business day after the emergency event (the implementation day is day 0). "Within two business days" means the review must be timestamped no later than the end of the second business day following the event.

| Day | Date | Day-of-week | Business day? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Event day (day 0) | Aug 11 | Tuesday | — |
| Day 1 | Aug 12 | Wednesday |  |
| Day 2 | Aug 13 | Thursday |  |
| Day 3 | Aug 14 | Friday | **Too late** |

Therefore, for any emergency on Aug 11, the retrospective review deadline is **end of Aug 13 (Thursday)**.

### RR5 — Identity Evidence

**Formalization:** `svc_qms` actions are conforming only if session evidence reliably maps the action to an authorized human. "Reliably" means at least two independent factors (e.g., badge + FIDO token, or badge + individual re-authentication at the terminal). Circumstantial co-location (badge in room + shared terminal) is **weak** attribution.

**Alias normalization:** Must occur **before** SoD intersection checks. The system must maintain a canonical mapping and apply it automatically.

### RR6 — Suspicion Versus Proof

Repeated resource pairs, reciprocal approvals, shared workstations, and concentrated handovers are **indicators** — not proof. Collusion requires corroborating evidence such as communications, financial benefit, or deliberate circumvention.

## 6. Control Improvements

### 6.1 Delegation Creation and Recording

| Gap | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| D-QM-01's DB record was created 2 days after the delegation period began | **Require that all delegations be entered into the QMS database before the effective start time.** The signed PDF should be scanned and attached at creation; no delegation should be active until the DB record exists. Use a "pending"  "active" state transition tied to DB entry. |
| D-IC-04 was sent by email and attempted prohibited subdelegation | **Enforce technical controls**: the delegation management system should reject subdelegation attempts when the parent delegation prohibits it. Email-based delegations should not be accepted as valid. |

### 6.2 Effective-Time Recording

| Gap | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Delegation boundaries use wall-clock UTC with no grace period | **Add explicit grace periods or timezone-aware handling.** A delegation ending at 23:59 UTC could miss an event at 23:59:30. Use half-open intervals `[start, end)` and document timezone policy. |

### 6.3 Principal Availability

| Gap | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| No formal definition of "absent" for delegation conditions | **Define "absent" explicitly in SOP**: does it include weekends, public holidays, or only formal leave? Document whether remote availability (VPN) negates absence. |
| Asha acted during leave; Ben's account was used during sick leave | **Implement a "leave lock"**: automatically suspend account interactive logins during approved leave periods, requiring a formal override with audit trail. |

### 6.4 Alias Management

| Gap | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| `b.koch` and `u_bk` not normalized in the QMS, enabling CHG-508 self-approval | **Maintain a canonical alias table in the QMS and enforce normalization on every SoD check.** All user-provisioned IDs must map to exactly one canonical human. Periodic audits should verify no unregistered aliases exist. |

### 6.5 Individual Authentication at Shared Workstations

| Gap | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Shared terminal WS-DM-04 allowed svc_qms action without individual re-authentication (CHG-503) | **Require per-action authentication at shared workstations**: FIDO token, proximity badge + PIN, or similar. Session-level authentication alone is insufficient when the session identity is a service account. |

## 7. Executive Conclusion

### Systemic Judgment

Of the 10 change-control cases reviewed, **five contain genuine resource-rule violations** (CHG-502, CHG-505, CHG-507, CHG-508, CHG-510), **two have unresolved identity issues** that may mask additional violations (CHG-503, CHG-506), and **three are authorized** with minor caveats (CHG-501, CHG-504, CHG-509).

The breakdown of root causes:

| Root-cause category | Cases affected | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **Genuine authority failure** | CHG-505, CHG-507, CHG-510 | Delegation scope and timing were not respected. CHG-510 is the most serious: an invalid subdelegation was used to approve a HIGH-risk emergency, and the retrospective review lacked proper scope. |
| **Identity / data quality** | CHG-503, CHG-506, CHG-508 | CHG-508 was directly caused by failure to normalize aliases. CHG-503 and CHG-506 reflect weak authentication at shared workstations and during leave periods. |
| **Organizational pattern risk** | Clara-Ben concentration (78% of HIGH-risk approvals) | Not proof of collusion, but the absence of pair-rotation, combined with Ben's account activity during sick leave and the reciprocal drafting pattern, warrants a focused investigation. |

The overall control environment shows **systemic weaknesses in delegation scope enforcement, alias management, and shared-workstation authentication**. These are not isolated incidents — they reflect gaps in the QMS configuration and operational discipline.

### Three Highest-Priority Investigations

1. **CHG-510 — Invalid subdelegation used for HIGH-risk emergency approval.** Clara approved a HIGH-risk cooling-system emergency using D-IC-04, which was invalid because D-IC-03 prohibited subdelegation. The retrospective review by Ben also lacked proper scope. This case combines RR1 violation, invalid authority, and scope mismatch. *Investigate:* What communication occurred between Farid and Clara? Did Farid understand the subdelegation prohibition? Was the emergency genuine? What was the actual safety impact?

2. **CHG-506 — Ben's account used during sick leave from an office workstation.** Ben denies performing the approval. If someone else used his credentials, this is a credential-compromise event affecting a HIGH-risk production-equipment approval. *Investigate:* Workstation access logs, badge records, Ben's physical location, and whether the approval decision was substantively appropriate regardless of who made it.

3. **Clara-Ben repeated pairing + reciprocal pattern (across 60 days).** Seventeen of 23 HIGH-risk production changes were drafted by Clara and approved by Ben, with no rotation policy. Ben also drafted LOW-risk changes that Clara approved. Combined with the sick-leave account usage and the alias-based self-approval in CHG-508, this warrants a targeted review of whether Ben's approvals of Clara's drafts show adequate scrutiny. *Investigate:* Approval-time distributions, rejection/query rates, and whether substantive review occurred in the 18 paired cases.
