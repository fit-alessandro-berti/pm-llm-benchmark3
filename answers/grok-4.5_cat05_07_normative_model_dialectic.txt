## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### Stipulated facts (evidence establishes these without needing authority interpretation)
- M0 is the current normative control-flow model aligned to CS-9; CS-9 is controlled and effective from 2025-01-01.
- CS-9 requires Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review before activation, and Manual_Procurement_Approval before Activate_Supplier; requester  manual approver (resource rule not in M0).
- PD-3 authorized Auto_Approve for LOW-risk DE suppliers in an approved corporate group only from 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31; it was never incorporated into CS-9; Compliance observer later denied approving a CS-9 weakening.
- FD-7 authorized provisional activation before Quality_Review and manual approval only 2026-07-10 00:00–2026-07-12 23:59 for critical relief suppliers; Sanctions_Check still required before activation; spend cap EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval; expired automatically.
- From 2026-07-01, Notify_Requester moved to CRM; ~70% mirrored; older links are mutable text fields; notification missingness rose 2%29%.
- Aggregates: 312 Auto_Approve in Jun/Jul (289 PD-3-eligible, 14 ineligible, 9 missing attrs); 47 Request_More_Info cases, none activated before evidence; 6 post-pilot Auto_Approve in first week of August; Internal Audit flagged SUP-703 and SUP-708.
- Label rename Quality_ReviewQA_Clearance was approved one week after SUP-713; content equivalent.
- SUP-711 duplicate Register_Supplier shares one source transaction ID (migration artifact).

### Disputed propositions
- Whether rejected pre-screen leads (SUP-709) are in M0’s scope.
- Whether reactivation (SUP-710) is governed by full M0 or a distinct procedure CS-9 never defined.
- Whether “monitored exception” language in PD-3 constituted valid temporary authority despite later Compliance denial of CS-9 weakening.
- Whether factual continuation of flood conditions (SUP-705) can justify post-expiry FD-7 pattern without written extension.
- Whether Operations’ normalization of auto-approval after PD-3 expiry (SUP-714) has any residual legitimacy.
- Whether CRM mutable-link notifications prove Notify_Requester occurred (SUP-706).
- Whether same-human-via-aliases (SUP-712) and spousal same-line relationship (SUP-715) violate enforceable rules given M0 silence and COI disclosure framing.
- Whether desirable Request_More_Info loops are noncompliance or model incompleteness.

### Burden of proof
- **Declaring NONCOMPLIANT (control-flow or clear CS-9 breach):** Prosecution must show (i) the case is in scope of M0/CS-9, (ii) a required ordering or mandatory activity is absent or inverted without a then-effective written overlay, and (iii) the deviation is not explained by proven logging defect. Standard: preponderance of reliable event and context evidence.
- **Accepting JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION:** Defense must show a written instrument (PD-3, FD-7, or equivalent) whose temporal scope, eligibility predicates, and residual CS-9 constraints were met; informal belief, “imminent” amendment, or factual emergency without instrument does not carry the burden.
- **MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT / DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE:** Favored when scope documents are ambiguous, required attributes cannot be reconstructed, or the only variance is label/log plumbing with independent corroboration of equivalent work.
- **Resource/COI noncompliance:** Requires identity resolution evidence; where policy demands disclosure rather than hard prohibition, burden is to show nondisclosure where disclosure was required, not merely the relationship.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

**Authority hierarchy.** CS-9 and M0 remain the standing normative baseline. PD-3 was an unincorporated, time-boxed steering-committee exception; FD-7 was a 72-hour emergency directive. Neither amended CS-9. When overlays expire, CS-9 snaps back. “Desirable” outcomes, operational convenience, and expected future policy do not retroactively rewrite controlled standards.

**Expiry and normalization of exceptions.** PD-3 ended 2026-07-31. Six August Auto_Approve cases and SUP-714 (2026-08-02) show Operations absorbed the pilot into BAU without permanent amendment. That is classic exception normalization: the monitored pilot became an unmonitored shadow process. FD-7 ended 2026-07-12 23:59; SUP-705 on 2026-07-13 copies the emergency pattern with no written extension. Factual continuity of flooding is not a legal instrument. Allowing post-expiry mimicry destroys the point of automatic sunset clauses.

**High-risk and severe cases.**
- SUP-703: HIGH-risk new foreign supplier, operator selected “pilot” despite ineligibility, Auto_Approve, EUR 180,000 first purchase—far outside PD-3’s LOW-risk DE group predicate and outside any spend discipline. Audit correctly flags severity.
- SUP-708: Activate_Supplier 40 minutes before Sanctions_Check on a HIGH-risk supplier, no pilot/emergency. This is a direct CS-9 inversion of the only control FD-7 still insisted on keeping. Absence of intervening transactions is mitigation of harm, not compliance.
- SUP-712: same human as requester and approver via aliases—CS-9 resource rule breach even when control-flow matches M0.
- Aggregate: 14 clearly ineligible Auto_Approve cases in the pilot window plus 9 with missing eligibility attributes show control failure inside the pilot, not merely after it.

**Deviations M0 cannot see do not excuse them.** M0’s silence on Request_More_Info, auto-approve, emergency provisional paths, and resource rules means fitness of the log to M0 understates true policy breach (SUP-712, SUP-715 COI disclosure) and also understates process reality. That is a reason to repair the model, not to treat CS-9 as optional. Desirable loops (SUP-707) improve evidence quality but still prove M0 is incomplete as a descriptive model; they do not prove the organization may ignore activation prerequisites.

**CRM and missing notify.** Notification is a required step before Close_Case in M0. Systemic missingness after CRM migration (29%) is partly a data pipeline failure, but where notification cannot be immutably evidenced (SUP-706 mutable text link entered two days later), conformance is unproven. Data weakness is not a free pass to close cases without requester notice; it is an internal control deficiency stacked on process risk.

**Rejected leads and reactivation are not automatic absolution.** If process owner includes rejected leads as intake cases, SUP-709’s skip of Quality_Review may be fine as abbreviated pre-screen—but then the organization must document scope. Reactivation SUP-710 skipping Register and Quality_Review without a CS-9 reactivation procedure is ad hoc practice in a control gap, not a defense that “M0 is wrong.”

**Why enforce M0/CS-9 now.** Material noncompliance is demonstrated by ineligible pilot use, post-expiry auto-approve, post-expiry emergency pattern, sanctions-after-activate, segregation-of-duties failure, and weak notification integrity. Pilot/emergency overlays prove the organization knows how to write time-bounded exceptions—and chose not to extend them. Informal practice after sunset is unauthorized. Internal Audit’s severe findings on SUP-703 and SUP-708 are justified. Operations’ claim that high deviation rates are “mostly obsolescence” confuses model debt (real) with authority debt (also real): fixing the model tomorrow does not authorize yesterday’s high-risk activations.

**Prosecution acknowledges genuine model/data defects:** M0 lacks Request_More_Info loops; lacks auto and emergency branches; omits resource/COI rules; concurrent checks are correct but overlays are absent; CRM migration broke notification observability; label aliasing (QA_Clearance) and duplicate register are data/model hygiene issues; scope for reject vs full case and reactivation is ambiguous. Those defects require model and data revision—they do not nullify CS-9’s core sequencing for in-scope activations.

## 3. Defense Brief

**M0 is descriptively obsolete relative to authorized and desirable practice.** During PD-3, eligible low-risk path was Auto_Approve with Quality_Review up to two business days post provisional activation (SUP-702). During FD-7, provisional activation before quality and manual approval was mandatory operational flexibility (SUP-704). M0’s exclusive manual-approval branch and pre-decision quality completion make authorized traces look nonconformant. A model that scores lawful pilot and emergency executions as deviant is unfit as a sole conformance oracle.

**Incomplete control-flow semantics.**
- Concurrency of sanctions and quality is in M0, but quality-after-activate under pilot/emergency is not.
- No loop for Request_More_Info despite 47 cases, zero premature activations, and Compliance agreement that the behavior is desirable (SUP-707). This is stakeholder-endorsed work, not drift.
- No rejection shortcut distinguishing pre-screen fail (SUP-709) from full-cycle reject.
- No reactivation pattern (SUP-710).
- Silent on notification channel migration.

**Incorrect scope and identity of “the process.”** Model owner says M0 begins after pre-screen acceptance; process owner says rejected leads are intake cases. Scope document is ambiguous—SUP-709 cannot be prosecuted as missing Quality_Review without resolving scope. Reactivation lacks CS-9 procedure; applying full onboarding model to SUP-710 invents obligations (fresh Quality_Review, Register) the standard never stated when prior certification remains valid.

**Overlays and better evidence.**
- SUP-702: PD-3 window, eligibility complete, quality next day, stable notification_id—compliant under then-effective overlay; M0 false positive.
- SUP-704: inside FD-7 window, sanctions before activate, spend EUR 18,000 < 20,000, monitoring and retrospective quality/approval next day—justified emergency; M0 false positive.
- SUP-713: QA_Clearance  Quality_Review with post-hoc approved mapping—label inequivalence, not missing control.
- SUP-711: duplicate register same transaction ID—migration duplication, not double onboarding.
- SUP-706: CRM message at +3 hours; absence from onboarding log after known 70% mirror rate is DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE, not proof of skipped notification.

**Trace deviation  business misconduct.** SUP-708 is serious if sanctions-after-activate is true—defense does not deny residual risk—but SUP-705 sits in a grey zone where emergency conditions factually persisted and spend was modest (EUR 12,000); the failure is governance documentation, not reckless spend. SUP-714 reflects change-management lag after a large pilot (312 cases), not necessarily fraud. SUP-715: no explicit prohibition on spousal approvers; COI policy requires disclosure—process gap and ethics issue, not M0 control-flow breach. SUP-712 is a real SoD problem but undetectable by M0; enforcement requires identity graph, not stricter POWL alone.

**Data collection undermines verdicts.** Mutable notification links, missing eligibility attributes (9 cases), alias pairs, and delayed mapping approvals mean several “deviations” are observational. Conformance checking against M0 on a partial log systematically overstates noncompliance (notify missingness 29%) and understates resource violations.

**Defense acknowledges genuine risk and limits on informal practice:** SUP-703 (ineligible high-risk auto-approve, EUR 180k) and SUP-708 (sanctions after activate, no overlay) are materially bad and cannot be waved away as model obsolescence. Post-expiry auto-approve (SUP-714 and six August cases) and post-expiry emergency pattern (SUP-705) exceeded written authority; Operations should have obtained extension or CS-9 amendment. “Believed permanent approval was imminent” is not authority. Pilot eligibility overrides by operators show monitoring failure during PD-3. Informal practice must not permanently displace controlled policy; the cure is layered normative architecture with explicit overlays—not pretending M0 already describes reality.

### System-level judgment

**Operational compliance:** Materially impaired. Authorized overlays explain only a subset of M0 deviations. Ineligible pilot use, two severe Audit cases (SUP-703, SUP-708), SoD alias failure, post-expiry auto-approve normalization, and post-expiry emergency patterning establish real noncompliance—not merely model noise. **Model validity:** M0 is **not fit as the sole normative oracle**. It is obsolete relative to PD-3/FD-7 patterns, incomplete on Request_More_Info, silent on resource/COI rules, wrong or underspecified on rejected-lead and reactivation scope, and coupled to degraded notification data after CRM migration. High apparent deviation rate is a mixture of true violations, justified exceptions, and structural model/data debt. Operations is partly right that M0 over-flags; Internal Audit is right that serious violations are under-controlled.

### Where each side is rejected

**Reject prosecution’s maximalism:** that every M0 deviation is operational misconduct and that CS-9/M0 should be enforced as-is without revision. This ignores lawful SUP-702/704, endorsed SUP-707, scope gaps 709/710, and data issues 706/711/713. Enforcing a knowingly incomplete model produces false findings and resists necessary auto/emergency branches.

**Reject defense’s maximalism:** that deviation rate mostly means obsolescence so enforcement should yield. SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714, SUP-705, and ineligible Auto_Approve volume are authority and control failures. Informal continuation after sunset is not a substitute for amendment. “Imminent permanent approval” and “flood still existed” without instruments fail the burden for JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.

**Net:** Enforce CS-9 core controls (especially sanctions-before-activate, SoD, eligibility gates) immediately; treat PD-3/FD-7 as expired; remediate severe cases; and simultaneously replace M0 with a layered architecture so conformance checking stops confusing authorized flexibility and logging loss with misconduct.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

### Layer A — Revised control-flow (POWL)
- Start: Register_Supplier (optional silent skip only for governed Reactivation entry—see scope).
- Then: concurrent Sanctions_Check and optional pre-decision Quality_Review path depending on route.
- LOOP/XOR approximation: Request_More_Info repeatable before decision.
- XOR routes: (1) Standard manual: Quality_Review complete  Manual_Procurement_Approval  Activate_Supplier; (2) Low-risk auto: Auto_Approve  Activate_Supplier  Quality_Review within bound (data layer); (3) Emergency provisional: Activate_Supplier after Sanctions  Quality_Review and Manual_Procurement_Approval retrospective; (4) Reject_Supplier (quality optional if reject reason = sanctions pre-screen fail).
- Then: Notify_Requester  Close_Case.
- POWL owns partial order, choice, and approximate loop—not amounts, dates, risk scores, or identity.

### Layer B — Data-aware eligibility/amount/time (pandas / DECLARE-style / log skeleton)
- PD-style auto: risk=LOW, countryallowed, corporate_group_approved=true, effective timestamp  pilot/permanent rule version window.
- Emergency: emergency_authority_id active at activate time; supplier_critical_relief=true; sanctions_before_activate; amount_until_retrospective_approval  cap; quality and manual approval within 2 business days; daily monitoring flag.
- Standard: quality and manual approval before activate.
- DECLARE/log-skeleton: Sanctions_Check  Activate_Supplier always; Manual_Procurement_Approval  Activate_Supplier unless route{AUTO_RULE_VER, EMERGENCY_RULE_VER}; Notify_Requester  Close_Case; existence of Quality_Review before close on non-reject paths.

### Layer C — Resource and COI (organizational + data checks)
- requester_id  manual_approver_id after alias resolution.
- Auto_Approve performer  requester where segregation required.
- COI: disclosure record for related-party pairs in same reporting line; block or waive with recorded authority.
- Not expressible faithfully in pure POWL.

### Layer D — Policy-overlay governance
- Overlays are versioned rules with immutable effective_from/effective_to, approval_authority, scope predicate, residual constraints (e.g., sanctions always).
- Pilots and emergencies cannot be silent; activation events must carry rule_version_id and authority_id.
- Expiry is automatic; continuation requires new version. “Monitored exception” requires named monitor and eligibility snapshot frozen at decision time.

### Layer E — Explicit scope rules
- **Rejected leads:** Case type LEAD_REJECT may path Register  Sanctions_Check  Reject_Supplier  Notify  Close without Quality_Review if reject_reason  {SANCTIONS_FAIL, PRESCREEN_FAIL}.
- **Reactivations:** Case type REACTIVATE requires Sanctions_Check; Quality_Review waived only if prior_cert_valid_until  activate_date; Register optional; still needs approval route per risk.
- **Withdrawals:** Allow silent exit to Close after Notify with Withdraw activity (optional future).
- Scope field `case_subtype` mandatory at case open.

### Placement summary
| Concern | Primary home |
|---|---|
| Concurrent checks, XOR routes, loop approx, notifyclose | POWL |
| Always-sanctions-before-activate, existence, ordering skeletons | DECLARE / log skeleton |
| Risk, country, group, amounts, caps, business-day windows, rule versions | pandas/data validation |
| SoD, aliases, COI disclosure | Org policy + identity graph checks |
| Pilot/emergency authority | Overlay registry + event attributes |
| Lead reject / reactivate / withdraw | Scope taxonomy + conditional required sets |

from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# --- Activities ---
register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")  # QA_Clearance mapped upstream to this label
request_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
manual_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")

close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

# Request_More_Info: loop XOR(exit, body); body = request then back
# Most defensible POWL approximation: LOOP with Request_More_Info as do-part
info_loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[skip_info, request_info])
# semantics: execute skip_info, then either exit or request_info and repeat from skip_info

# Pre-decision work after register: sanctions always in model paths below;
# quality concurrent with sanctions on STANDARD path only.

# --- Standard route: sanctions || quality, then manual approval, then activate ---
std_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])
# no edge => concurrent
std_approve_act = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[manual_approval, activate])
std_approve_act.order.add_edge(manual_approval, activate)
standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[std_checks, std_approve_act])
standard_route.order.add_edge(std_checks, std_approve_act)

# --- Low-risk auto route: sanctions then auto_approve then activate then quality ---
# (quality after activate allowed only if data-layer eligibility holds)
auto_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, auto_approve, activate, quality])
auto_route.order.add_edge(sanctions, auto_approve)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_approve, activate)
auto_route.order.add_edge(activate, quality)

# --- Emergency provisional route: sanctions -> activate -> quality || manual_approval ---
# both quality and manual approval required before close; concurrency after activate
emerg_after = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[quality, manual_approval])
# concurrent retrospective controls
emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, activate, emerg_after])
emergency_route.order.add_edge(sanctions, activate)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(activate, emerg_after)

# --- Reject routes ---
# Full reject after checks (sanctions || quality) then reject
reject_after_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])
reject_full = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[reject_after_checks, reject])
reject_full.order.add_edge(reject_after_checks, reject)
# Abbreviated sanctions-only reject (lead pre-screen)
reject_abbrev = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, reject])
reject_abbrev.order.add_edge(sanctions, reject)
reject_choice = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[reject_full, reject_abbrev])

# --- XOR among business outcomes ---
decision = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[standard_route, auto_route, emergency_route, reject_choice]
)

# --- Optional register (reactivation may skip) ---
register_xor = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[register, skip_register])

# --- Top-level partial order ---
# register_xor -> info_loop -> decision -> notify -> close
M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register_xor, info_loop, decision, notify, close])
M1.order.add_edge(register_xor, info_loop)
M1.order.add_edge(info_loop, decision)
M1.order.add_edge(decision, notify)
M1.order.add_edge(notify, close)
