# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

Each PM4Py organizational metric encodes a *different* relation. The apparent contradiction in Mira's profile largely dissolves once the relations are kept apart — but part of it may also be a measurement artifact, so I flag arithmetic inconsistencies rather than assuming the tables are mutually coherent.

### Handover-of-work network

**What it measures:** directed, *directly-follows* adjacency of resources within a case (`discover_handover_of_work_network`). An edge AB means "an event by B immediately followed an event by A in the same case." It is an ordering relation, not a delegation relation, and same-resource successions are normally dropped.

**What it supports for Mira:** out-degree 0.82 (11–12 of 14 possible targets), in-degree 0.51 (7 sources), betweenness 0.77. She is adjacent to nearly every part of the organization — bots, both team leads' adjusters, juniors, both specialists, both temps. Structurally she is the bridge between the automated front end (`Bot_Rules`), the scarce specialist capacity (`Omar_Medical`, `Priya_Legal`) and the rework pool. She appears to control roughly two-thirds of specialist intake (210 of Omar's 316 cases; 180 of Priya's 251), i.e. a near-monopoly, not an absolute one — Omar's in-degree implies ~2 other inbound sources.

**What it does not imply:** (i) authority — adjacency cannot distinguish "instructs" from "logs an event between two other people's events"; (ii) effort — a 4-second bot event and a 51-minute legal review produce identical edges; (iii) causality of delay — the 42/51-minute gaps to specialists may be specialist queueing, not her latency; (iv) indispensability — high betweenness on a graph where she is the *only* logged bridge is partly a property of the process design, not of her.

**Validity caveats:** betweenness on a frequency-weighted graph is meaningless unless weights are inverted (1/frequency) or dropped; the ranking can flip. Bots and vendors must not be silently pooled with humans: `Bot_Ingest``Bot_Rules` (4040) dominates weighted metrics, and `Vendor_Pay` is a sink (out-degree 0.00) that truncates paths.

### Working-together network

**What it measures:** undirected, order-free, frequency-free *co-membership* of two resources in the same case. One brief routing event counts exactly as much as twenty assessment events.

**Why "peripheral" is partly mechanical:** co-case rate is bounded by each partner's marginal case coverage. Mira touches 22% of claims, so *no* pair involving her can exceed 22% under a "% of all cases" reading. Alice–Carla at 27% reflects two resources with long, repeated, within-team involvement; Mira's 1.06 events per case (960/905) spread across ~12 partners cannot generate dense ties. Low weighted degree (0.16) is therefore the expected signature of a **broker with many weak ties**, not evidence of social isolation or of not "really" working with people.

**What it does not imply:** absence of collaboration, absence of influence, or low trust. It also cannot detect *concurrent* work: PM4Py's version ignores interval overlap entirely.

**Consistency flag:** Alice appears in 665/4100  16% of cases, yet Alice–Carla is reported at 27% and Alice–Elise at 24%. Under "% of all cases" these are impossible. The denominator must be something else (e.g. multi-resource cases only, max-normalized weights, or a filtered log). **Peripherality should not be treated as an established fact until the normalization is reproduced.** The right statistics are conditional: P(Carla | Mira) vs P(Carla), Jaccard, or PMI/lift.

# 5. Recommendation

### Change immediately (low-regret, does not remove the function)
1. **Name and document the function.** Whatever the formal title, the *coordination work exists*. Add `Assign Specialist` (and any other shadow activities) to the controlled activity taxonomy, with a mandatory reason code, destination rationale, and timestamp.
2. **Separate decision rights on paper, not by removing them:** she recommends/routes and may reopen; team leads approve settlements; a lead reviews reopens above a threshold. This addresses the compliance concern without stalling complaint handling.
3. **Create real redundancy:** a named, trained backup with complaint-clock authority; extend Bruno's remit to include Reopen/Escalate (Alice already has Reopen); rotate a senior adjuster through the desk one day per week — this builds redundancy *and* is a safe substitutability experiment.
4. **Protect the regulatory clock:** complaint-aging dashboard with alerting and an explicit absence cover rota.
5. **Fix the log before judging anyone:** lifecycle handling, working-together denominator, frozen exception flag, shared-account check, and the approval/payment coverage gap.
6. **Address the status mismatch** in recognition/terms — an undervalued single point of failure is a retention risk.

### Monitor (with thresholds, monthly SNA re-run)
Exception/rework **p90 and queue aging** (not medians); time-to-specialist after assignment; complaint SLA breaches; reopen rate and post-reopen outcome; rework-after-assignment as a *matching-quality* proxy; specialist utilization; share of flagged cases bypassing the desk; routing consistency conditional on case attributes; her cross-team brokerage share and centrality rank stability; post-absence backlog drain; vendor turnaround; the unapproved-payment count.

### Do not change until more evidence exists
- **Do not remove, downgrade, or automate the desk.** If removal is ever contemplated, the replacement must be specified *first*: codified routing rules in `Bot_Rules` with confidence thresholds and an explicit fallback; a named-owner exception queue with rotation; a specialist intake protocol replacing her gatekeeping; documented reopen authority; a complaint-SLA monitor; shadow-mode pilot, then staged rollout with rollback. The ops manager's warning (more formal escalations, specialist overload) is a testable prediction, not a veto.
- Do not split the role into team-based desks — that would sever cross-team brokerage and could increase contention for Omar/Priya; test by node deletion first.
- Do not impose hard SoD blocks on reopening, or infer misconduct from centrality.
- Do not rebalance workload or renegotiate vendor SLAs on unvalidated service-time/centrality numbers.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira sits at the centre of how difficult claims move through the organisation, but she does very little of the hands-on work. The routing network shows her passing work to almost every team, specialist and temporary worker; the teamwork network shows her rarely sharing a case for long. Both are true: she makes short decisions that redirect cases rather than working them side by side with colleagues. When she was away for six days, routine claims were unaffected, but the slowest difficult claims got about 65% slower. That suggests she protects the tail of the process, not everyday throughput. Her logged workload is small, so the dependency is probably knowledge and decision rights, not hours. Nothing here shows misconduct; the open question is undocumented discretion, not intent. We do not recommend removing the role. We do recommend naming and documenting it, adding reason codes to reassignments and reopenings, training a backup, and fixing gaps in the event log before anyone judges performance or compliance from these numbers.
