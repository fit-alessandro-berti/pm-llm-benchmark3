## 1. Interaction-graph description

The appropriate representation is a typed, object-centric interaction graph rather than six isolated Job traces. Nodes are not only Jobs, but also Components, ComponentLots, Machines, Tools, Batches, and Shipments. Edges encode both persistent object-object relationships and event-mediated co-participation.

**Important node types**

- **Jobs:** J1–J6; the primary demand objects.
- **Components:** C1–C6; material requirements of Jobs.
- **ComponentLots:** L7 and L8; shared material-control objects.
- **Machines:** CNC-2, OVEN-1, TEST-1; capacity resources.
- **Tools:** FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3; potentially more constraining than their host machines.
- **Batches:** B1, B2; synchronization and grouping objects.
- **Shipments:** S1–S3; downstream consolidation objects.

**Important edge types / qualifiers**

- `Job --requires--> Component`
- `ComponentLot --contains--> Component`
- `Batch --batches--> Job`
- `Shipment --consolidates--> Job`
- Event-mediated resource links, e.g. `Job --uses--> Machine`, `Job --uses--> Tool`, or `Batch --uses--> OVEN-1/RACK-7`.
- Event-mediated control-state links, e.g. `L7 --quarantine/release affects--> C2/C5/J2/J5`.
- Temporal/resource-state edges inferred from acquire/release intervals, such as J3 holding FIX-A from 09:00–11:40.

**Higher-order motifs**

1. **Batch–tool–machine synchronization motif**  
   `J1/J2/J3 -> B1 -> {OVEN-1, RACK-7}`.  
   J1 and J2 were ready far earlier than the B1 cure start, while J3 became ready shortly beforehand. This is not ordinary OVEN-1 queueing alone: the batch is a policy/control object that synchronizes three Jobs, and RACK-7 is a shared enabling tool.

2. **Tool hold–machine idle–downstream job motif**  
   `J3 -> FIX-A -> CNC-2 <- J4`.  
   J4 reached CNC-2 at 09:50, yet CNC-2 was idle from 10:05 to 10:47 and J4 did not start until 11:45. The likely dependency is not machine occupancy but unavailable fixture capability.

3. **Lot-control–material–job–batch motif**  
   `L7 -> {C2, C5} -> {J2, J5} -> {B1, B2}`.  
   One lot-level quarantine and release governs material for two otherwise separate Jobs. J2 entered B1 under override; J5 waited for release. The same lot event therefore can create asymmetric treatment, rework risk, and apparent inconsistencies in job-level performance.

4. **Job–shipment partner coupling motif**  
   `J1 -> S1 <- J4`, with the shipment dispatched only after J4 is packed.  
   J1’s post-pack delay is caused by a relationship to another Job through S1, not by J1’s own processing.

5. **Tool calibration–test machine idle motif**  
   `J5 -> CAL-3 -> TEST-1 <- J6`.  
   TEST-1 is technically available, but CAL-3 recalibration separates J5 and J6 tests. Thus the effective capacity unit is arguably `(TEST-1, CAL-3)`, not TEST-1 alone.

**Compact edge list**

```text
J1 requires C1; J2 requires C2; J3 requires C3;
J4 requires C4; J5 requires C5; J6 requires C6

L7 contains C2, C5
L8 contains C1, C3

B1 batches J1, J2, J3
B2 batches J4, J5

S1 consolidates J1, J4
S2 consolidates J2, J3
S3 consolidates J5, J6

B1 uses OVEN-1 and RACK-7 at 12:05 [direct event co-participation]
B2 uses OVEN-1 and RACK-7 at 14:50 [direct / state evidence]
J4 uses CNC-2 and FIX-A at 11:45 [direct event co-participation]
J3 holds FIX-A 09:00–11:40 [direct acquire/release evidence]
J5, J6 use TEST-1 and CAL-3 [direct Final Test relation]
L7 quarantine/release affects C2, C5, J2, J5 [direct event relation, but inconsistent integration]
```

Directly observed links include the selected O2O relations and multi-object event relations. Inferred links include J4’s waiting specifically *because* of FIX-A, RACK-7’s causal role in delaying B2, and the proposition that shipment policy—not ordinary dispatch capacity—caused J1’s delay. These are plausible explanations, not established causal facts.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

The calculations below are counterfactual decompositions, not causal proof. They assume recorded readiness timestamps are valid and that the cited constraint, rather than an unrecorded prerequisite, is the relevant marginal constraint.

1. **J1 waiting at B1**

- J1 ready: 08:10.
- B1 starts: 12:05.

\[
12{:}05 - 08{:}10 = 3\text{ h }55\text{ min} = 235\text{ min}
\]

**Counterfactual assumption:** J1 could have entered an earlier or smaller cure batch at 08:10, with no quality, rack, oven, staffing, or policy constraint preventing it. Under that assumption, up to **235 minutes** is synchronization-induced waiting.

2. **J2 waiting at B1**

- J2 ready: 08:25.
- B1 starts: 12:05.

\[
12{:}05 - 08{:}25 = 3\text{ h }40\text{ min} = 220\text{ min}
\]

**Counterfactual assumption:** Like J1, J2 could have been processed without waiting for B1’s final member. Up to **220 minutes** is attributable to batch synchronization. This is complicated by L7 quarantine: J2 entered under supervisor override, so “ready” may represent operational readiness but not full material-release compliance.

3. **Contrast with J3 in the same batch**

- J3 ready: 11:55.
- B1 starts: 12:05.

\[
12{:}05 - 11{:}55 = 10\text{ min}
\]

The B1 synchronization burden is highly uneven: J1 waits 235 minutes, J2 220 minutes, J3 10 minutes.

\[
\text{Mean wait within B1} = (235+220+10)/3 = 155\text{ min}
\]

**Counterfactual assumption:** All three Jobs were equally eligible to proceed individually. The 225-minute spread between J1 and J3 strongly suggests batching redistributes delay across Jobs rather than reflecting a common oven outage.

4. **J4 delay plausibly associated with FIX-A**

- J4 reaches CNC-2: 09:50.
- CNC-2 idle begins: 10:05.
- FIX-A released by J3: 11:40.
- J4 starts Machine Process: 11:45.

A conservative fixture-related delay starts only when the machine is idle:

\[
11{:}45 - 10{:}05 = 1\text{ h }40\text{ min} = 100\text{ min}
\]

The post-release lag is:

\[
11{:}45 - 11{:}40 = 5\text{ min}
\]

This timing is consistent with fixture release plus setup. **Counterfactual assumption:** J4 was otherwise technically and administratively ready at 10:05 and FIX-A was indispensable. Then approximately **100 minutes** of avoidable waiting is plausibly associated with fixture unavailability. The full 115 minutes from J4 reaching CNC-2 at 09:50 to start is an upper bound, because CNC-2 was still occupied until 10:05.

5. **J1 shipment-consolidation delay in S1**

- J1 packed: 13:30.
- S1 dispatches: 19:25.

\[
19{:}25 - 13{:}30 = 5\text{ h }55\text{ min} = 355\text{ min}
\]

J4 packs at 19:10, only 15 minutes before dispatch. S2 dispatches 25 minutes after its last job is packed and S3 after 35 minutes. Using those as a rough comparator:

\[
355 - 25 = 330\text{ min}
\]
or
\[
355 - 35 = 320\text{ min}
\]

**Counterfactual assumption:** If J1 had been in a shipment that dispatched with the normal 25–35 minute post-last-pack delay, then roughly **320–330 minutes** of J1’s post-pack delay is plausibly associated with waiting for S1/J4 consolidation. This does not prove that S1 policy was avoidable; customer, route, or documentation constraints may justify it.

6. **J6 delay plausibly associated with CAL-3**

- J6 ready: 16:05.
- J5 test ends: 16:35.
- CAL-3 recalibration: 16:35–17:20.
- J6 test begins: 17:25.

From readiness to test start:

\[
17{:}25 - 16{:}05 = 80\text{ min}
\]

From the point TEST-1 becomes idle after J5 to J6’s start:

\[
17{:}25 - 16{:}35 = 50\text{ min}
\]

Of this, calibration occupies:

\[
17{:}20 - 16{:}35 = 45\text{ min}
\]

The remaining five minutes plausibly represent setup/transfer:

\[
17{:}25 - 17{:}20 = 5\text{ min}
\]

**Counterfactual assumption:** J6 was otherwise ready, TEST-1 could test J6 immediately after J5, and recalibration was not technically required between those tests. Then **45 minutes** is directly associated with CAL-3 recalibration, while **50 minutes** is the broader effective test-cell delay after J5 ended.

## 4. What flattening misses

**Job viewpoint.** It makes each Job’s local sequence visible: release, stage, process, inspect, pack, dispatch. It hides events linked only to L7, B1, RACK-7, or CAL-3 unless the extraction propagates those relations. It may also duplicate shared events such as one Batch Cure into three Job traces. A misleading conclusion is that Jobs are broadly normal because no individual total duration exceeds 1.8 standard deviations; this misses correlated waiting redistributed across Jobs.

**Machine viewpoint.** It shows utilization, machine event order, and direct machine occupancy. It hides readiness queues, unavailable fixtures, racks, calibrators, lot holds, and shipment waits. A misleading conclusion is “CNC-2 has spare capacity, so J4 could not have been delayed by production.” CNC-2 was physically idle while the required configuration involving FIX-A was unavailable.

**Batch viewpoint.** It makes membership, cure timing, and synchronization barriers visible. It can hide individual Job readiness heterogeneity and may duplicate a Job’s perspective across batch and job contexts. A misleading conclusion would be that B1 completed efficiently at 12:05; that statement ignores J1’s 235-minute and J2’s 220-minute pre-batch waits.

**Tool viewpoint.** It exposes scarce enablers and hold intervals: FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3. It may overstate tool centrality if tool events are sparse or if a tool is merely co-recorded rather than constraining. A misleading DFG conclusion would be that calibration is simply part of TEST-1’s normal sequence, rather than a resource-state change that blocks J6 while TEST-1 is idle.

**ComponentLot viewpoint.** It makes shared quality/release state visible, especially L7 coupling J2 and J5. It can hide downstream job-specific exemptions and create ambiguity because lot events are inconsistently related to Jobs. A misleading conclusion is that L7 uniformly blocked both Jobs; J2 entered B1 via override, while J5 waited.

**Shipment viewpoint.** It makes create/load/dispatch flow visible. It hides the age of already-packed member Jobs because shipment-level elapsed time begins at shipment creation rather than at each Job’s pack completion. A misleading conclusion is that S1 is fast because `Create Shipment -> Dispatch` may be short, despite J1 waiting 355 minutes after packing.

Low machine utilization can coexist with long job waiting because jobs need a feasible **resource bundle** and a policy permit, not merely an idle machine. J4 needs CNC-2 plus FIX-A; J6 needs TEST-1 plus CAL-3; batch jobs need OVEN-1, RACK-7, eligibility, and batch release; J1 needs shipment partner completion. Idle machine time is therefore not equivalent to available end-to-end capacity.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

### Narrative A: J3 fixture hold affects J4 machining

**Objects:** J3, FIX-A, J4, CNC-2, C3/C4.

- **Temporal precedence:** J3 acquires FIX-A at 09:00 and releases it at 11:40. J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50; CNC-2 is idle from 10:05–10:47; J4 begins at 11:45.
- **Resource feasibility:** J4’s Machine Process event directly relates J4, C4, CNC-2, and FIX-A. If FIX-A is mandatory, J4 cannot run with CNC-2 alone.
- **Policy constraints:** Fixture allocation, changeover, cleaning, qualification, and reservation rules are unobserved.
- **Causal speculation:** The five-minute gap after fixture release is consistent with setup, making fixture contention a strong explanation for much of J4’s delay. But an unrecorded operator, program, material, or approval constraint could also explain the delay.

### Narrative B: B1 synchronizes J1 and J2 behind J3

**Objects:** J1, J2, J3, B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, C1/C2/C3.

- **Temporal precedence:** J1 is ready 08:10, J2 08:25, J3 11:55, and B1 starts 12:05.
- **Resource feasibility:** The Batch Cure event relates B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, all three Jobs, and C1–C3. The jobs proceed through a shared cure configuration.
- **Policy constraints:** B1 may require a minimum batch, a common recipe, rack loading rules, or a planned cure window.
- **Causal speculation:** J3’s late readiness appears to define the B1 start more than J1 or J2’s readiness. Yet this need not be irrational: an early partial batch could reduce oven efficiency, violate curing requirements, or create a later rack shortage.

### Narrative C: L7 affects J5, which affects B2 and potentially S3

**Objects:** L7, C5, J5, B2, RACK-7, OVEN-1, S3, J6.

- **Temporal precedence:** L7 is quarantined at 10:20 and released at 13:50. J5 is ready for B2 at 14:40, B2 starts 14:50. J5 ultimately shares S3 with J6.
- **Resource feasibility:** If C5 cannot be used while L7 is quarantined, J5 is materially infeasible before release. B2 additionally requires OVEN-1 and RACK-7.
- **Policy constraints:** Quality-release policy blocks J5, while J2 receives a supervisor override. Shipment S3 can impose another coupling after pack.
- **Causal speculation:** L7 likely contributed to J5’s later progress, but it cannot by itself explain all timing without knowing when J5’s other prerequisites completed and whether B2 was intentionally scheduled for 14:50.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

**Observations that would weaken the top-ranked batch-synchronization explanation**

1. Evidence that J1 and J2 were not actually cure-ready at 08:10 and 08:25; for example, missing inspection, recipe approval, operator release, or material certification.
2. A mandatory technical rule showing B1 could not legally or physically start until J3/C3 was present.
3. OVEN-1 was occupied, unavailable, under maintenance, or lacked an operator before 12:05 despite the simplified record.
4. RACK-7 was unavailable before 12:05, with a valid documented reason unrelated to waiting for J3.
5. Historical data show similar B1 composition but no long early-member waits, implying this instance was exceptional.
6. Partial batches produce unacceptable cure quality, excessive rework, or downstream failures, making the apparent wait economically necessary.
7. Another unrecorded shared dependency—such as quality release, staffing, or recipe changeover—explains the 08:10–12:05 interval better than batching.
8. J1 and J2 were deliberately held for customer sequencing, not batch completion.

**Additional fields or event types needed to discriminate hypotheses**

1. Explicit `Ready for Batch`, `Eligible for Batch`, `Batch Assigned`, `Batch Load Start`, and `Batch Hold Reason` events for every Job.
2. OVEN-1 state logs: occupied, idle, unavailable, maintenance, cleaning, staffing unavailable, recipe changeover, and temperature qualification.
3. RACK-7 lifecycle events: reserve, allocate, load, unload, clean, quarantine, release, and reason codes.
4. Tool lifecycle and reservation data for FIX-A and CAL-3: request time, queue entry, acquire, release, calibration due, calibration validity, and maintenance.
5. Machine-plus-tool feasibility snapshots, so an idle CNC-2 or TEST-1 can be distinguished from an operationally usable workcell.
6. ComponentLot quality data: quarantine reason, affected components, test result, release authority, override approval, and whether the override is temporary or permanent.
7. Job readiness decomposition: material ready, engineering ready, operator ready, machine ready, tool ready, quality ready, and policy eligible.
8. Shipment policy attributes: customer consolidation requirement, route/cutoff time, promised ship date, allowed split shipment, freight cost, and dispatch hold reason.
9. Operator and shift assignment events, since apparent resource waits may actually be labor/authorization waits.
10. Event-relation provenance: whether a Job–Lot or Job–Tool link was directly recorded, propagated by integration, inferred, or manually corrected.
11. Queue discipline and priority information for batches, fixtures, test slots, and shipment allocation.
12. Quality and rework outcomes following partial batches, expedited lot overrides, or deferred calibration, enabling evaluation of whether delay reductions create hidden downstream harm.
