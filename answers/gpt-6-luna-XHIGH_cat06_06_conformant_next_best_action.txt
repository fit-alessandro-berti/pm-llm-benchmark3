## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

The known direct upside is bounded: execution within the deadline is estimated to avoid €20,000–€35,000 in operational loss. That does not mean any fast action will realize that value: the predicted ultimate approval probability of 0.55–0.75 is conditional on a conformant review, and the estimates are observational rather than guaranteed effects of these interventions. The 0.48–0.64 SLA-breach prediction for normal processing also should not be transferred unchanged to a plan that changes staffing, screening, and timing.

P-884’s possible false-rejection or prolonged-hold harm is €15,000–€80,000. That range overlaps and can exceed the deadline benefit, so neither automatic rejection nor an open-ended evidence wait is justified by monetary arithmetic alone. Action F is attractive only if the documented evidence threshold for rejection is actually satisfied.

The potential harm from unlawful execution is €100,000–€500,000 plus non-monetary consequences. It is asymmetric, and the process makes the review gates mandatory. A lower deadline-loss forecast cannot compensate for skipping those gates: A and B remain unacceptable even if they appear to maximize immediate operational value. For a permitted plan, an approval is uncertain and may still be followed by an execution too late to meet the deadline.

The best balance is to preserve the compliant approval opportunity with an independent second review while gathering useful information in parallel. The API call has value only as information that can inform a real review; it cannot clear the case by itself. No precise expected-value calculation is warranted because the review outcome, intervention effects, P-901’s monetary exposure, and non-monetary compliance consequences are not quantified reliably.

- **B:** R17 cannot be both the first analyst and the independent second officer. Self-certification violates the four-eyes rule and cannot be treated as a valid approval path.
- **C:** is conformant if carried out in order: Evidence Validated returns the case to Analyst Review; R17 resumes that review; a different person, S2, performs Second Officer Review; only after the required reviews may the case be approved and then executed. Rejection or hold/report are also possible outcomes.
- **D:** alternate screening is supplementary. It may reduce uncertainty or reveal a false match, but it does not substitute for the second review while a trigger remains. A fresh authoritative risk score below 70 would remove only the score-based trigger; an unresolved fuzzy match still requires the second officer. Even if both triggers were cleared, reassess the route under the applicable process rather than assuming a vendor result authorizes execution.
- **E:** requesting and validating evidence is a permitted repeat of the evidence cycle. The validated evidence returns the case to Analyst Review, and any outstanding second-review trigger remains. A report, if warranted, does not itself authorize execution.
- **F:** is conformant only if documented evidence meets the organization’s actual rejection threshold. That threshold and sufficient supporting evidence are not established in the prompt. If met, record the grounds and reject; do not frame a hold or report as approval to pay.
- **G:** is unavailable now. It requires both Compliance Director authorization and a genuine, recorded incident identifier that is justified. Neither is currently satisfied. Do not fabricate an incident or treat a pending request as an override; verify the authorization’s scope before relying on it.

The process allows Auto Clear only for a case with no alert and a risk score below 40. This case has a current score of 72 and an unresolved fuzzy match, so that route is not available on the stated facts. The requirement to review after Evidence Validated also matters: the observed prefix ends before R17’s post-evidence Analyst Review. SAR information must not be disclosed to the customer.

A and B cannot be made acceptable by a favorable numerical forecast. Their constraints are mandatory process conditions, not costs that a utility calculation can trade away. The predictions are uncertain and observational, while an unlawful execution has strongly asymmetric and non-monetary consequences.
