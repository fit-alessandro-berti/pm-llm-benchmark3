## 2. Prosecution Brief

Operations asks this review to excuse systemic governance breakdown by blaming an imperfect process model (`M0`). That deflection fails once genuine model artifacts are separated from substantive policy breaches. The Prosecution readily concedes that `M0` has representational defects: `SUP-707` (`Request_More_Info` loops that strengthen vetting), `SUP-709` (forcing `Quality_Review` on sanctions-rejected pre-screen leads), `SUP-711` (deterministic migration duplicates sharing a source transaction ID), and `SUP-713` (`QA_Clearance` synonymy) are model or ETL issues, not operational misconduct. Stripping those distractions away exposes an operation with weak preventive guardrails, habitual disregard of authority boundaries, and severe high-risk compliance failures.

### 1. Informal Minutes Cannot Amend Controlled Policy—And Pilot Monitoring Failed
`CS-9` is a formally controlled standard requiring `Quality_Review` and `Manual_Procurement_Approval` prior to every activation. `PD-3` bypassed both gates via steering-committee minutes and follow-up email. Corporate compliance hierarchies exist precisely so business committees cannot dilute mandatory controls outside formal change management. Even if the Compliance observer’s phrase *“monitored exception”* were generously read as temporary forbearance for `SUP-702`, Operations breached the condition embedded in those very words: **monitoring**. Across `312` `Auto_Approve` executions in June–July, `14` were flatly ineligible and `9` lacked the data needed to verify eligibility—a `7.4%` control failure rate in an automated pathway. Worse, `PD-3` was implemented as an unguarded UI selection rather than a system-enforced rule. In `SUP-703`, an operator manually selected “pilot” for a **HIGH-risk new foreign supplier**, bypassing manual procurement approval and pre-activation quality review to execute an immediate **EUR 180,000** purchase.

### 2. Severe Pre-Sanctions Activation Exposure
Both `CS-9`, `PD-3`, and even emergency directive `FD-7` preserve one non-negotiable red line: `Sanctions_Check` **must** precede `Activate_Supplier`. In `SUP-708`, Operations activated a HIGH-risk supplier 40 minutes *before* sanctions screening and before manual procurement approval, with zero pilot or emergency pretext. Defense claims “no transactions occurred” during those 40 minutes. In compliance law, fortuitous absence of loss is not a control; enabling an unscreened high-risk entity in master data creates immediate strict-liability exposure to automated PO release or payment runs.

### 3. Normalization of Deviance and Disregard of Sunset Clauses
Temporary exceptions carry explicit expiration timestamps because emergency and pilot risk tolerances are bounded in time. Operations treated every temporary relaxation as permanent entitlement:
* `FD-7` expired automatically at `2026-07-12 23:59`. On `2026-07-13` (`SUP-705`), Operations unilaterally extended emergency provisional activation without written authority. Factual flood conditions justify *requesting an emergency extension*; they do not empower line operators to self-authorize policy overrides.
* `PD-3` expired on `2026-07-31`. Operations nonetheless executed six `Auto_Approve` cases in the first week of August (`SUP-714`) because staff “believed permanent approval was imminent.” Anticipated policy is non-existent policy. Desirable speed or low perceived risk cannot retroactively legalize unauthorized approvals.

### 4. Severe Governance Violations Masked by `M0`
Far from being overly strict, `M0` is dangerously *under-constraining* on identity and independence:
* **`SUP-712` (Alias Self-Approval)**: The trace aligns perfectly with `M0`, yet the requester and manual approver were the **same human** acting through two user aliases—a textbook fraud vector and direct violation of `CS-9`’s four-eyes mandate.
* **`SUP-715` (Undeclared Familial Conflict)**: Spouses in the same reporting line performed `Quality_Review` and `Manual_Procurement_Approval` without mandatory CoI disclosure, tainting the independence of consecutive control gates.
* **`SUP-706` & `SUP-710` (Auditability & Uncontrolled Reactivation)**: In `SUP-706`, Operations relies on a mutable text link typed into CRM two days post-hoc—failing basic evidentiary non-repudiation. In `SUP-710`, Operations skipped `Quality_Review` during reactivation without any `CS-9` rule defining certificate reuse or reactivation eligibility.

At the system level, Operations exhibits classic normalization of deviance: informal workarounds displaced controlled standards, preventive IT checks were absent, and high-risk suppliers slipped through unvetted.

When a normative model fails to represent information-gathering loops, pre-screen rejections, reactivations, UI label updates, authorized executive overlays, and distributed microservice logging, raw conformance checking manufactures a crisis out of healthy business adaptation and IT telemetry debt.

At the outset, Defense does not defend the indefensible. `SUP-703` (ineligible high-risk auto-approval via unguarded UI selection), `SUP-708` (activation 40 minutes prior to sanctions screening), and `SUP-712` (self-approval across dual aliases) are genuine, serious compliance breaches requiring immediate ERP guardrails and IAM identity consolidation. Likewise, post-expiry executions (`SUP-705`, `SUP-714`) highlight the need for rapid formal extension protocols rather than informal continuation, and `SUP-715` reflects an HR CoI disclosure lapse. Those discrete failures, however, represent a tiny fraction of throughput and must not be inflated by counting `M0`’s structural defects as operational misconduct.

### 1. `M0` Penalizes Diligence and Enforces Irrational Scope
* **Missing Evidence Loops (`SUP-707`)**: Across `47` cases, operators executed `Request_More_Info` cycles to obtain missing quality certificates *before* granting approval, with zero premature activations. Both Compliance and Operations agree this behavior strengthens `CS-9`. Yet `M0` lacks a loop construct, branding diligent verification as nonconformant.
* **Irrational Pre-Screen Concurrency (`SUP-709`)**: Because `M0` places `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` in an unconditional partial order (`checks`) prior to `decision`, it requires engineering QA staff to conduct a full `Quality_Review` on a lead that has *already failed* `Sanctions_Check`! `CS-9` expressly permits abbreviated pre-screens for rejected leads; `M0` mis-scopes intake rejections as full onboardings.
* **Lifecycle Conflation (`SUP-710`)**: `M0` assumes every case is a net-new supplier. `SUP-710` reactivated a supplier closed six months earlier whose prior `Quality_Review` certification remained substantively valid, while performing fresh `Sanctions_Check` and `Manual_Procurement_Approval` before activation. Re-running redundant quality audits wastes capacity without reducing risk.
* **Vocabulary Drift (`SUP-713`)**: Renaming `Quality_Review` to `QA_Clearance` in the UI with identical underlying audit checklists is a schema-mapping lag, not a control failure.

### 2. Authorized Governance Overlays (`PD-3` and `FD-7`) Were Legitimate
Static process models lag behind real-world executive governance:
* **Emergency Directive `FD-7` (`SUP-704`)**: During an active flood threatening plant shutdown, `FD-7` authorized provisional activation after `Sanctions_Check`. `SUP-704` satisfied every safeguard: critical relief scope, EUR 18,000 spend (< EUR 20,000 cap), daily monitoring, and next-business-day `Quality_Review` and `Manual_Procurement_Approval`. Even `SUP-705` (executed hours after midnight expiry while floodwaters remained) strictly honored all substantive `FD-7` risk caps (EUR 12,000 spend, prior sanctions clearance, retrospective review).
* **Pilot Decision `PD-3` (`SUP-702`)**: Steering Committee Decision `PD-3` was reviewed by Compliance, whose observer wrote that the *“pilot may proceed as a monitored exception.”* Operations relied in good faith on that written clearance; Compliance cannot retroactively disavow its own written authorization after execution. In `289` of `312` cases (including `SUP-702`), low-risk German corporate-group suppliers were screened for sanctions, validated, auto-approved, and quality-reviewed within two business days.

### 3. Distributed Logging Defects Are Not Operational Breaches
Following the `2026-07-01` CRM migration (`N4`), `Notify_Requester` missingness surged from `2%` to `29%` because the CRM-to-onboarding mirror dropped ~30% of events. Moreover, `Notify_Requester` is an internal post-decision courtesy event—`CS-9` imposes zero requirement on requester notification prior to activation. `SUP-702` possesses an immutable CRM `notification_id` at `+2 hours`; `SUP-711` is a provable database migration duplicate sharing one source transaction ID; and even `SUP-706` completed every `CS-9` pre-activation gate flawlessly before CRM sync failed. Enforcing `M0` blindly would punish Operations for IT integration defects while completely missing `SUP-712` and `SUP-715`, which `M0` marks 100% conformant.

### 3. System-Level Judgment
* **Operational Compliance**: **Materially Noncompliant in Control Enforcement and Boundary Discipline.** While day-to-day standard and eligible-pilot executions largely functioned as intended (`289/312` pilot cases eligible; `47/47` info-request cases held activation until evidence arrived), the control environment failed its primary purpose—preventing severe exceptions. * **Model & Telemetry Validity**: **`M0` and the Onboarding Log Are Unfit as Standalone Audit Oracles.** `M0` produces both **high false positives**—flagging desirable `Request_More_Info` loops (`SUP-707`), pre-screen rejections (`SUP-709`), valid emergency/pilot overlays (`SUP-702`, `SUP-704`), reactivations (`SUP-710`), and label updates (`SUP-713`)—and **catastrophic false negatives**, certifying alias self-approval (`SUP-712`) and undeclared spousal conflicts (`SUP-715`) as 100% conformant. Simultaneously, the `29%` post-CRM notification drop (`N4`) and un-deduplicated migration extracts (`SUP-711`) corrupt raw replay metrics. Enforcing `M0` as written would halt beneficial diligence while leaving severe identity and CoI risks untouched.

### 4. Where Each Side’s Argument Is Rejected
* **Rejecting the Prosecution**:
  1. *Retroactive Repudiation of `PD-3` (`SUP-702`)*: Compliance cannot have an observer authorize a *“monitored exception”* in writing and retroactively treat eligible, properly monitored executions within the pilot window as policy violations. Governance untidiness in failing to issue a formal `CS-9` addendum rests jointly on Compliance and the Steering Committee, not line operators following `PD-3` accurately.
  2. *Literalist Overreach on Pre-Screens, Loops, and Reactivations (`SUP-707`, `SUP-709`, `SUP-710`)*: Treating missing `Quality_Review` on sanctions-rejected leads or reusing a valid 6-month-old quality certificate alongside fresh sanctions and manual approval as “noncompliance” mistakes model rigidity for risk management.
  3. *Equating ETL Drops with Operational Breach (`SUP-706`, `SUP-711`)*: Missing CRM mirror events (`29%` rate) do not invalidate supplier activations whose pre-activation gates succeeded; at worst, mutable CRM links create *indeterminate* notification auditability (`SUP-706`).
* **Rejecting the Defense**:

  2. *“No Harm, No Foul” on Pre-Sanctions Activation (`SUP-708`)*: Zero transactions during a 40-minute unscreened activation window mitigates financial loss, not compliance liability.
  3. *Siloing CoI from Onboarding Conformance (`SUP-715`)*: Defense cannot validate an approval chain merely because `CS-9` only names requester-vs-approver identity. Corporate CoI disclosure rules govern every fiduciary act; an undisclosed spousal review-and-approval pair in one reporting line invalidates gate independence.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

A sound conformance architecture must separate ordinal control flow from quantitative data guards, relational identity/CoI graphs, and time-bounded policy overlays across four enforcement layers:

### 1. Explicit Scope & Lifecycle Boundaries
* **Pre-Intake Commercial Triage (Out of Scope)**: Informal vendor inquiries dropped *before* master-data intake (`Register_Supplier`) are CRM lead-management records, not onboarding cases.
* **Registered Pre-Screen Rejections (In Scope — Abbreviated Rejection Route)**: Once `Register_Supplier` executes, a failed `Sanctions_Check` (or initial vetting check) may route directly to `Reject_Supplier` $\to$ `Notify_Requester` $\to$ `Close_Case` without requiring `Quality_Review` (`SUP-709`).
* **Supplier Reactivations (In Scope — Reactivation Entry)**: Suppliers inactive $\le 12$ months enter via `Reactivate_Supplier`. Fresh `Sanctions_Check` and `Manual_Procurement_Approval` remain mandatory, but `Verify_Existing_Quality_Cert` may substitute for a full `Quality_Review` **if and only if** the prior certificate is unexpired, covers the identical commodity/risk scope, and prior closure was voluntary dormancy rather than quality/compliance termination (`SUP-710`).
* **Withdrawals (In Scope — Withdrawal Route)**: Applications cancelled by requester or supplier prior to activation/rejection execute `Withdraw_Case` $\to$ `Notify_Requester` $\to$ `Close_Case`, halting open parallel checks cleanly without false “incomplete trace” alerts.

### 2. Allocation Across Formalisms and Controls

| Architectural Layer | Target Formalism / Mechanism | Rules Assigned to This Layer |
|---|---|---|

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

### Explicit POWL Modeling Limitations and Approximations
1. **Controlled Label Reuse Across `XOR` Routes**: Because POWL is strictly hierarchical (`StrictPartialOrder`, `XOR`, `LOOP`) without arbitrary Petri-net cross-branch place sharing, activities whose causal order changes by route (`Sanctions_Check` $\parallel$ `Quality_Review` in Standard vs. `Sanctions_Check` $\prec$ `Activate_Supplier` $\parallel$ `Quality_Review` in Auto-Pilot vs. `Sanctions_Check` $\prec$ `Provisional_Activate_Supplier` $\prec$ `Quality_Review` in Emergency vs. `Sanctions_Check` without `Quality_Review` in Pre-Screen Rejection) must be instantiated as distinct `Transition` objects per branch. This preserves exact trace-language semantics while introducing prefix non-determinism resolved deterministically at the data layer via `policy_rule_id`.
2. **Cross-Branch Intake-to-Vetting Coupling**: Restricting `Verify_Existing_Quality_Cert` exclusively to cases that entered via `Reactivate_Supplier` cannot be enforced across sequential `XOR` blocks without duplicating the entire downstream process tree. `M1` models the structural option cleanly while Layer 3 enforces `(activity == 'Verify_Existing_Quality_Cert') => (entry_type == 'REACTIVATION' AND cert_valid_until >= event_effective_ts)`.
3. **Unbounded Kleene Loop**: `Operator.LOOP(SilentTransition(), Transition("Request_More_Info"))` permits $0..N$ information-request cycles (`SUP-707`). Bounding maximum iterations or enforcing supplier response timeouts is handled by workflow SLA timers rather than unrolling finite POWL branches.
4. **Quantitative, Temporal, and Identity Rules**: Spend caps, two-business-day deadlines, sunset timestamps, alias resolution, and spousal CoI rules cannot be safely encoded in POWL and are enforced via the accompanying normalized tables.

### Executable PM4Py Revised POWL Model (`M1`)

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def make_rmi_loop() -> OperatorPOWL:
    """
    Repeatable 0..N Request_More_Info mechanism.
    In PM4Py POWL, LOOP(do, redo) executes `do` once, then 0..N times `(redo -> do)`.
    Using SilentTransition() as `do` yields exact Kleene-star `Request_More_Info*` semantics.
    """
    return OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.LOOP,
        children=[SilentTransition(), Transition(label="Request_More_Info")],
    )


def build_revised_onboarding_powl() -> StrictPartialOrder:
    # 1. Intake Scope: Net-new registration vs. controlled reactivation (SUP-710)
    entry = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[
            Transition(label="Register_Supplier"),
            Transition(label="Reactivate_Supplier"),
        ],
    )

    # 2A. Standard CS-9 Route: (Sanctions || Quality/ValidCert || RMI*) -> Manual Approval -> Activate
    std_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    std_quality = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[
            Transition(label="Quality_Review"),
            Transition(label="Verify_Existing_Quality_Cert"),  # Data-guarded to Reactivate_Supplier
        ],
    )
    std_rmi = make_rmi_loop()
    std_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[std_sanctions, std_quality, std_rmi])

    std_manual_app = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
    std_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
    standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[std_checks, std_manual_app, std_activate])
    standard_route.order.add_edge(std_checks, std_manual_app)
    standard_route.order.add_edge(std_manual_app, std_activate)

    # 2B. Low-Risk Auto-Approval Route (Authorized Pilot / Tiered Policy Overlay, SUP-702):
    # Sanctions & Master-Data/Eligibility Validation precede Auto_Approve -> Activate_Supplier.
    # Quality_Review (and optional RMI*) may run concurrently or up to +2 business days post-activation,
    # followed by silent confirmation on pass or Deactivate_And_Reject_Supplier on QA failure.
    auto_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    auto_validate = Transition(label="Validate_Eligibility_And_Master_Data")
    auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
    auto_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
    auto_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
    auto_rmi = make_rmi_loop()
    auto_post_qa_gate = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[
            SilentTransition(),
            Transition(label="Deactivate_And_Reject_Supplier"),
        ],
    )
    auto_route = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[
            auto_sanctions,
            auto_validate,
            auto_approve,
            auto_activate,
            auto_quality,
            auto_rmi,
            auto_post_qa_gate,
        ]
    )
    auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_sanctions, auto_approve)
    auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_validate, auto_approve)
    auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_approve, auto_activate)
    auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_activate, auto_post_qa_gate)
    auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_quality, auto_post_qa_gate)
    auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_rmi, auto_post_qa_gate)

    # 2C. Emergency Provisional Route (Directive Overlay, SUP-704):
    # Sanctions_Check MUST precede Provisional_Activate_Supplier.
    # Daily monitoring, RMI*, and Quality_Review occur post-activation before retrospective decision.
    emg_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    emg_prov_activate = Transition(label="Provisional_Activate_Supplier")
    emg_monitoring = Transition(label="Start_Daily_Spend_Monitoring")
    emg_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
    emg_rmi = make_rmi_loop()
    emg_retro_decision = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[
            Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval"),
            Transition(label="Deactivate_And_Reject_Supplier"),
        ],
    )
    emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[
            emg_sanctions,
            emg_prov_activate,
            emg_monitoring,
            emg_quality,
            emg_rmi,
            emg_retro_decision,
        ]
    )
    emergency_route.order.add_edge(emg_sanctions, emg_prov_activate)
    emergency_route.order.add_edge(emg_prov_activate, emg_monitoring)
    emergency_route.order.add_edge(emg_prov_activate, emg_quality)
    emergency_route.order.add_edge(emg_prov_activate, emg_rmi)
    emergency_route.order.add_edge(emg_monitoring, emg_retro_decision)
    emergency_route.order.add_edge(emg_quality, emg_retro_decision)
    emergency_route.order.add_edge(emg_rmi, emg_retro_decision)

    # 2D. Rejection Route (Abbreviated Sanctions Pre-Screen SUP-709 OR Full-Review Rejection)
    rej_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    rej_opt_quality = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[Transition(label="Quality_Review"), SilentTransition()],
    )
    rej_rmi = make_rmi_loop()
    rej_vetting = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[rej_sanctions, rej_opt_quality, rej_rmi])
    rej_action = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
    rejection_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[rej_vetting, rej_action])
    rejection_route.order.add_edge(rej_vetting, rej_action)

    # 2E. Withdrawal Route (Applicant/Requester cancellation before decision)
    wd_opt_sanctions = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[Transition(label="Sanctions_Check"), SilentTransition()],
    )
    wd_opt_quality = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[Transition(label="Quality_Review"), SilentTransition()],
    )
    wd_rmi = make_rmi_loop()
    wd_pre = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[wd_opt_sanctions, wd_opt_quality, wd_rmi])
    wd_action = Transition(label="Withdraw_Case")
    withdrawal_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[wd_pre, wd_action])
    withdrawal_route.order.add_edge(wd_pre, wd_action)

    # 3. Route Selection, Mandatory Notification, and Case Closure
    core_routing = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[
            standard_route,
            auto_route,
            emergency_route,
            rejection_route,
            withdrawal_route,
        ],
    )
    notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
    close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

    M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[entry, core_routing, notify, close])
    M1.order.add_edge(entry, core_routing)
    M1.order.add_edge(core_routing, notify)
    M1.order.add_edge(notify, close)
    return M1


M1 = build_revised_onboarding_powl()

if __name__ == "__main__":
    print("Constructed revised POWL M1 successfully.")
    print(f"Top-level partial order nodes: {len(M1.nodes)}")
```

### Normalized Event & Data-Collection Proposal
To eliminate telemetry ambiguity (`SUP-706`, `SUP-711`), vocabulary drift (`SUP-713`), alias self-approval (`SUP-712`), undeclared CoI (`SUP-715`), and unverified pilot eligibility (`SUP-703`), data collection is normalized into five append-only relational tables:

1. **`onboarding_event_log` (Immutable Append-Only Event Store)**
   * **Keys & Deduplication**: `event_uuid` (PK), `case_id` (FK), `source_system` (`ERP`, `CRM`, `SANCTIONS_API`), `source_tx_id` (`UNIQUE(source_system, source_tx_id)`—blocks `SUP-711` migration duplicates at ingestion).
   * **Canonical & Raw Labels**: `raw_activity_label` (e.g., `"QA_Clearance"`), `canonical_activity_label` (mapped via `activity_ontology_version_id` to `"Quality_Review"`, solving `SUP-713`).
   * **Dual Immutable Timestamps**: `event_effective_ts_utc` (business/system execution timestamp synchronized via NTP, resolving `SUP-708` ordering checks) and `record_ingested_ts_utc` (immutable log-commit timestamp; detects backdated or post-hoc entries like `SUP-706`).
   * **Governance & Actor Binding**: `rule_version_id` (FK to `policy_overlay_registry`), `actor_account_id` (FK), `resolved_person_id` (FK resolved at write time), `delegation_grant_id` (nullable FK).
   * **Cross-Service Evidence Keys**: `notification_id` (nullable immutable UUID FK to `crm_notification_outbox`, replacing mutable text links via transactional outbox/CDC mirroring so 100% of CRM notifications sync reliably).

2. **`case_eligibility_snapshots` (Point-in-Time Decision Inputs)**
   * Captures an immutable hash-signed JSON and columnar snapshot at `Register_Supplier` and immediately prior to route selection (`snapshot_id`, `case_id`, `captured_ts_utc`, `supplier_country`, `risk_tier`, `corp_group_id`, `approved_group_flag`, `prior_quality_cert_id`, `prior_cert_expiry_utc`, `cumulative_po_spend_eur`). Prevents post-hoc attribute tampering and rejects `Auto_Approve` if any required field is `NULL` (`9` June–July cases) or ineligible (`SUP-703`).

3. **`iam_actor_identity_and_delegation` (Canonical Human Resolution)**
   * Maps every `actor_account_id` (ERP login, SSO alias, legacy ID) to a single immutable HR `resolved_person_id` (`employee_tax_or_hr_uid`) with `[valid_from_utc, valid_to_utc]`.
   * Includes `delegation_grants` (`delegation_grant_id`, `delegator_person_id`, `delegate_person_id`, `scope_rule`, `valid_from_utc`, `valid_to_utc`, `approving_manager_person_id`), ensuring acting-authority delegations never permit `requester_person_id == approver_person_id` (`SUP-712`).

4. **`hr_conflict_and_reporting_graph` (Independence & CoI Verification)**
   * Stores directed reporting edges (`subordinate_person_id`, `manager_person_id`, `department_line_id`) and declared/registered personal relationships (`person_a_id`, `person_b_id`, `relationship_type` [`SPOUSE`, `DOMESTIC_PARTNER`, `RELATIVE`, `FINANCIAL_INTEREST`], `coi_disclosure_id`, `compliance_waiver_status`).
   * Runtime and audit checks block or flag any pair of gate actors (`{requester, quality_reviewer, manual_approver}`) sharing a relationship edge or shared direct reporting line without an active `APPROVED` `compliance_waiver_status` (`SUP-715`).

5. **`activity_label_ontology` (Versioned Schema Mapping)**
   * Tracks `ontology_version_id`, `ui_or_service_label`, `canonical_powl_label`, `equivalence_audit_doc_id`, and `effective_from_utc`—requiring CI/CD deployment pipelines to register label synonyms *before* UI releases reach production (`SUP-713`).
