## 1. Materiality Framework

Severity is a business judgment, not a function of alignment cost. I score severity against the following dimensions:

- **Control objective affected**: authorization, anti-fraud, payment release, customer notification, or audit evidence.
- **Amount / exposure**: direct monetary loss or potential undetected fraud.
- **Reversibility**: whether the action can be clawed back or corrected.
- **Timing**: whether the activity happened before a required control, after a deadline, or out of order.
- **Customer / regulatory harm**: risk of customer harm or breach of obligations such as notification and evidence retention.
- **Recurrence**: whether the issue is one-off or systemic.
- **Evidence reliability**: whether the log gap is corroborated by trustworthy external evidence.
- **Model scope**: whether the deviation is inside the process the model is intended to represent.

I use a 1–5 severity scale:

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---:| 1 | No business impact; noise, model artifact, or harmless technical event. |
| 2 | Logging / evidence / model-scope gap with no failed control or improper payment. |
| 3 | Control weakness, such as an unauthorized approval or weak audit evidence, with moderate exposure. |
| 4 | High-risk control bypass or material irreversible action with elevated fraud/regulatory concern. |
| 5 | Critical failure: irreversible payment before required approval, or high-value high-risk claim approved without verifiable control evidence. |

**Confidence is different from severity.** Confidence expresses how strongly the evidence supports the interpretation. A case can be severe but have medium confidence, or low severity with high confidence. Raw alignment cost contributes to neither: it is a syntactic distance between log and model.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

### CL-602: Missing Fraud Screen evidence

- **H1**: The fraud screen was performed verbally or outside the system but was not logged.
- **H2**: The fraud screen was skipped entirely.
- **H3**: The fraud screen was performed in another tool, but the output was not integrated into the case file.

**Discriminating evidence**: search for call recordings, chat logs, manual approver notes, fraud-team queue entries, system access logs, or any fraud-screen artifact. If policy requires a documented Fraud Screen event, even H1 is noncompliant. The existence of a bank change one day before approval makes the absence of evidence particularly important.

### CL-604: Payment before Manual Approve

- **H1**: The payment schedule executed before the manual approval was recorded.
- **H2**: Manual approval happened before payment, but was entered into the system later.
- **H3**: The bank timestamp is in a different timezone or is the settlement time rather than the actual payment instruction time.

**Discriminating evidence**: compare the bank payment instruction timestamp, the scheduler/job start time, the approval UI save audit timestamp, and the database transaction time. If the payment instruction was created before 10:20, H1 or H2 is confirmed.

### CL-606: Missing Assess Loss event

- **H1**: The adjuster performed the assessment externally, but the event was not imported into the core log.
- **H2**: The assessment occurred but was logged under a different label, such as “Assess Damage.”
- **H3**: The adjuster report is not proof of assessment; it may have been created later or attached to the wrong case.

**Discriminating evidence**: search the core log for all assessment labels, check the adjuster report metadata, verify the document-to-case link, and look for downstream activities that depend on the assessment. If the report has a signed timestamp before fraud screen and payment, H1 is most likely.

### CL-608: Missing Notify Claimant event

- **H1**: The notification was sent through the CRM secure portal, but the core system was not updated.
- **H2**: The notification was never sent; the CRM message is a draft, placeholder, or manually populated record.
- **H3**: The notification was sent but the event was lost by the connector.

**Discriminating evidence**: check the CRM message delivery status, the immutable message ID, portal access logs, recipient acknowledgement, and notification-service logs. Inspect the mutable link for edit history.

### CL-609 / CL-610: Auto Approve policy violations

- **H1**: The policy guard is absent from the rule engine or process model.
- **H2**: The guard exists, but the relevant data — amount, risk level, or bank-change history — was not available at decision time.
- **H3**: A manual override forced the Auto Approve branch.

**Discriminating evidence**: capture the rule-engine input parameters at the moment of auto approval, the version history of the rule, the timestamp of the bank-change event relative to the approval, and any override audit trail. For CL-610, the bank-change timestamp is especially important.

### CL-611: `Assess Damage` vs `Assess Loss`

- **H1**: The core system uses a legacy label; the UI and procedure use “Assess Loss”; they are the same activity.
- **H2**: The two labels represent different activities, and the mapping mistakenly equates them.
- **H3**: The mapping table was updated after the case to make the event appear conformant.

**Discriminating evidence**: review the data dictionary, user interface code, signed procedures, mapping table version history, and the author/time of the mapping update. If both labels appear for the same case or in the same period with different owners, H2 becomes more likely.

### CL-607 / CL-612: Out-of-scope denial and withdrawal

- **H1**: The model intentionally covers only payable claims, and denied/withdrawn claims should be excluded.
- **H2**: Denials and withdrawals are part of the same process, and the model is incomplete.
- **H3**: The log includes events from a different process that should have been filtered before conformance.

**Discriminating evidence**: compare process documentation, case-type fields, and log-source filters. If denied and withdrawn cases use the same case IDs and flow through the same intake, H2 is plausible.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Alignment cost is a syntactic edit distance under an arbitrary cost function. It is not a measure of compliance risk, financial exposure, customer harm, or evidence confidence.

First, the cost function is uniform: every visible log/model move costs 1, with no weight for amount, reversibility, risk, or control objective. That is why CL-601 has raw cost 3 from harmless UI telemetry, while CL-602 has raw cost 1 for a €480,000 high-risk payment with missing fraud-screen evidence. The raw costs are inverted relative to business risk.

Second, raw cost depends on model scope. CL-607 and CL-612 have raw cost 4 because the process model does not include denial or withdrawal branches. Yet no payment was intended and no control was bypassed. The high cost is a model-scope artifact. Conversely, CL-609 and CL-610 have raw cost 0 because the process tree permits Auto Approve, even though the policy overlay forbids those exact auto approvals. Zero technical cost can coincide with significant policy violation.

Third, hidden silent moves are fitted at zero cost, but they are not evidence of safety. CL-605 has raw cost 0 and includes a hidden `tau` transition. The zero cost simply means the model can explain the log with a silent routing step; it says nothing about whether P1/P2 data conditions were satisfied.

Fourth, missing events are not proof that activities did not occur. CL-606 has `M(Assess Loss)` in the alignment, but an external licensed adjuster signed an assessment report. CL-608 has `M(Notify Claimant)` in the core log, but the CRM contains evidence of a secure-portal notification. Raw cost treats these as missing activities, while business interpretation must weigh external evidence and evidence reliability.

Fifth, activity granularity and label differences create artificial cost. CL-611 has raw cost 2 because the log says “Assess Damage” and the model says “Assess Loss.” The underlying activity is the same; the cost is purely lexical.

Sixth, duplicated telemetry and transport retries inflate raw cost. CL-601 and CL-603 add costs for UI events and a retry duplicate, neither of which is a meaningful control event.

Seventh, raw cost cannot encode timing and irreversibility. CL-604 has raw cost 3, but the business meaning is that a €90,000 payment left the bank before approval. CL-602 has raw cost 1, but the business meaning is that a €480,000 high-risk payment is supported by no verifiable fraud screen. Irreversibility is not in the cost function.

Finally, confidence is separate. An alignment can show a missing event, but the reason for the missing event — logging gap, external system, or actual omission — requires additional evidence. Alignment cost is a starting point for investigation, not a final risk score.
