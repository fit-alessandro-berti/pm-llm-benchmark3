## 1. Target definition

**Primary Target Formulation:**
The study defines the primary target as the **Conditional Probability of Default given Activation ($P(D_{90}|A)$)**.
*   **Prediction Time:** The timestamp of the first event (`Application Started`) or immediately after the `Identity/KYC Check` event, representing the earliest possible decision point before credit risk materializes.
*   **Prediction Horizon:** 90 days post `Account Activated`.
*   **Unit of Analysis:** Individual Application Case.
*   **Population:** All applications that eventually reach the `Account Activated` state.
*   **Estimand:** The probability that a case, if activated, will default within 90 days of activation, estimated using features observed at the prediction time.
*   **Rationale:** Since `default_90d` is only defined for activated accounts, modeling the default risk for the activated cohort conditional on the early application events avoids the bias of treating non-activated cases as "non-defaults." This aligns with the business goal of pricing risk for accounts that actually enter the loan portfolio.
*   **Alternative Target:** A **Multi-state Competing Risk Target** ($T_{default}$ vs. $T_{rejection}$). This would model time-to-default and time-to-rejection simultaneously. This answers a different question: "What is the risk of default versus the risk of rejection?" It requires survival analysis with competing risks (Fine-Gray model) and is more complex to deploy as a standard early-warning score.

## 2. Cohort construction

**Inclusion Rules:**
*   **Primary Cohort:** Applications with an event `Account Activated` occurring before the data extraction date (15 Feb 2026).
*   **Prediction Cutoff:** 15 Feb 2026.
*   **Label Maturity:** Only cases where `Account Activated` + 90 days + 35 days (delay) < 15 Feb 2026 are considered fully labeled. Cases within the 90-day window are right-censored.

**Exclusion Rules:**
*   Applications started after 1 Jan 2024 (outside window).
*   Cases where `time:timestamp` of prediction point is after `Account Activated` (leakage).

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

**Censoring Types:**
1.  **Ordinary Right Censoring:** Activated accounts where 90 days have not yet elapsed or the outcome feed has not matured at 15 Feb 2026. (3,600 cases).
2.  **Delayed Labels:** Maturity of `default_90d` feed occurs 7–35 days after the 90-day horizon.
3.  **Selection via Activation:** Non-activated cases (Rejected/Withdrawn) are not "non-defaults"; they are unobserved outcomes for the specific question of activation risk.
4.  **Competing/Transfer:** Transferred cases may reappear elsewhere. Outcomes are missing for ~40% due to lack of linkage.
5.  **Permanently Missing:** Partner-originated accounts often lack outcome feeds.

**Primary Analysis Strategy:**
Use **Kaplan-Meier** survival analysis for the primary target (Activated cohort). The model predicts the hazard of default. For the ML classifier, we treat the 3,600 censored cases as right-censored observations in the training set (using libraries like `lifelines` or `scikit-survival`).

**Sensitivity Analysis:**
1.  **Treat Transferred as Default:** Assume all 2,900 transferred cases eventually defaulted to test if the model is overly optimistic about activation risk.
2.  **Treat Non-Activated as Competing Risk:** Use a Fine-Gray model including Rejection/Withdrawal as competing events to estimate sub-distribution hazards.
3.  **Assumption Test:** Check if censoring is independent of the default risk. If activated cases are more likely to be censored because they are "low risk" (e.g., they were paused), the KM estimator will be biased. This is untestable without the full outcome feed.

## 4. Prefix extraction

**Extraction Logic:**
1.  **Stopping Rule:** Extract events up to and including the last event prior to `Account Activated`. Specifically, stop after `Credit Review` or the final loop in that activity.
2.  **Fixed Count vs. Time:** Use a hybrid approach: Maximum 5 events OR 3 days elapsed, whichever comes first. This handles short cases.
3.  **Activity Renaming (15 Oct 2025):** Normalize activity names using a mapping dictionary (e.g., `Credit_Assessment_v2` -> `Credit_Assessment`).
4.  **Repeated Loops:** Count `Credit Review` loops. If a loop > 3, flag as "High Rework" feature.
5.  **Document Uploads:** Use `time:timestamp` of the event to determine if a document upload is *before* the prediction point. Do not count documents uploaded after `Account Activated`.

## 7. Validation design

**Split Strategy:**
*   **Temporal Split:** Split by `time:timestamp` of the first event (`Application Started`).
    *   **Train:** Cases started 1 Jan 2024 – 30 June 2025.
    *   **Test:** Cases started 1 July 2025 – 15 Feb 2026.
*   **Rationale:** The policy change on 1 July 2025 creates a distribution shift. A random split would leak post-policy data into the training set, invalidating the performance on the new regime.
*   **Migration Handling:** Exclude cases started in Oct 2025 (migration period) from both Train and Test to avoid activity name confusion, or apply the normalization mapping consistently.

**Metrics:**
*   **Survival:** C-Index (Concordance Index) for time-to-default.
*   **Binary (at horizon):** AUC-ROC and Calibration (Brier Score) for the 90-day horizon.
*   **Subgroup:** Performance by `case:region` and `case:channel`.

**Why Random Split is Misleading:**
Random splits assume i.i.d. data. The policy change (July 2025) alters the probability of activation and default rates. Training on pre-policy data and testing on post-policy data without temporal separation overestimates model performance.

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

**Capabilities:**
*   Predicts default risk for accounts that *will* be activated.
*   Captures early signals of activation failure (e.g., high rework).
*   Robust to policy shifts via temporal validation.

**Cannot Claim:**
*   **Counterfactual Default:** Cannot estimate the risk of default for rejected applications (selection bias).
*   **Causal Impact:** Cannot determine if changing the model changes the activation rate (deployment feedback loop).
*   **Transfer Risk:** Cannot fully account for transferred accounts due to missing linkage data.

**Deployment Risks:**
1.  **Automation:** If the model is used to *trigger* rejections, the activation rate (and thus the target population) will change.
2.  **Feedback:** If the model flags high-risk cases for manual review, the data generation process (rejection rate) changes, causing distribution drift.
3.  **Data Extract Time:** If the model is deployed on a different data extract schedule, the `label_available_at` logic must be re-validated.
