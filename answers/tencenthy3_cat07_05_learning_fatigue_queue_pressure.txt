# 2. Integrated explanation

The observed performance pattern cannot be reduced to a single causal story. Early in the shift (positions 1–15), service times are longest (18.8–15.2 min) and errors are moderate (3.4–2.8%). This is driven primarily by **case-mix and selection**: the queue carries over complex, high-priority cases from previous shifts, and senior resources deliberately absorb this difficult work while they are fresh. A modest **learning/warm-up** effect also contributes, as routine processing stabilizes by position 16–30, where service time drops to 13.6 min and errors dip to 2.5%.

During the middle of the shift (positions 16–50), the system appears healthiest. Complexity and priority mix ease, WIP is moderate (31–33), and a gentle **learning plateau** keeps service times low (12.9–13.6 min) with the lowest error rates (2.5–2.7%). Here, queue pressure is not yet extreme, and fatigue has not accumulated.

The late-shift deterioration (positions 51–76+) is where mechanisms collide. Service time continues to fall (11.4  10.8 min) while errors and rework climb sharply (4.6%  7.9% errors; 8.2%  13.7% rework). This is **not primarily fatigue** in the classic sense of slowing down; instead, it is a **queue-pressure-induced speed-up**. As WIP explodes (queue length 62  88), workers rush, and juniors/mid-level staff exhibit a marked speed-quality tradeoff (Table 2: high WIP error 10.9% for complex). **Fatigue** then amplifies the error rate under exactly those high-WIP conditions (experience table: juniors 12.4% error later under high WIP). **Automation interaction** worsens this: Bot_Risk’s instant classifications are corrected more often by humans precisely when queues are long, layering rework onto already pressured workers. **Batching** (e.g., Junior_H/I, end-of-shift Quality Check bursts) artificially depresses late service times but masks quality risks that surface as rework. Thus, service time falls due to pressure-driven rushing and batching, while errors rise only when that pressure intersects with fatigue, complex automation handoffs, and residual case exceptions.

| # | Intervention | Expected benefit | Risk | How to monitor impact with event-log indicators |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Strictly cap shift length for all workers | May reduce fatigue-driven late errors. | Ignores that early shift intentionally clears backlog; capping may shift WIP and worsen next shift’s case-mix. | Track `error_rate` by `sequence_position_within_shift` post-cap; monitor shift-end `queue_length_at_start` of next shift. |
| 2 | Move complex cases away from first hour | Reduces early slowness and warm-up strain. | Early shifts have freshest seniors; moving complex cases late piles pressure on already strained period (error spike). | Compare `case_complexity_score` distribution by shift hour; check `service_time` and `error_rate` in later positions. |
| 3 | Add queue-pressure alert when WIP > 70 | Directly interrupts speed-quality tradeoff. | Alert fatigue if no actionable load-balancing follows. | Count events at WIP>70; compare `error_rate` and `service_time` before/after alert implementation. |
| 4 | Force random assignment of cases | Reduces senior selection bias. | Juniors may receive early complex cases, raising errors; loses efficient skill matching. | Monitor `case_complexity_score` by `resource_experience_months`; track `error_rate` for juniors early shift. |
| 5 | Mandatory micro-breaks final two hours | May mitigate fatigue and error spike. | Under high WIP, break enlarges queue, increasing post-break pressure. | Observe late-shift `error_rate` and end-of-shift `wip_at_start`; ensure no WIP ballooning. |
| 6 | Defer low-priority quality checks | Relieves end-of-shift burst, aids throughput. | Must not become removal of checks; deferred checks can let errors escape to customers. | Track `rework_flag` and downstream `error_flag`; confirm QC still completed before `Close Case`. |
| 7 | Extra senior reviewer during day peaks | Seniors keep quality under pressure (4.9% vs juniors 12.4%). | Additional cost; does not fix root demand-supply mismatch. | Day-shift `error_rate` under high WIP; `service_time` for `Senior_*` vs others during 14:00–17:00. |
| 8 | Penalize workers with higher late-shift error rates | (None – rejected per fairness constraints) | Unfair: late errors driven by WIP, automation corrections, and case exceptions, not solely worker effort. | Do not deploy; instead monitor complexity-adjusted, WIP-stratified error rates per resource. |

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

Worker-level conclusions from this log are inherently risky if taken raw. **Selection bias** is severe: seniors voluntarily or by design absorb complex carryover early, so their raw error rates may look artificially stable while they do harder work; juniors look worse partly because they receive pressured, batched, or residual tasks. **Measurement error** is possible because `error_flag` may capture corrections of Bot_Risk’s misclassifications as human errors when the root cause is automation quality, especially at WIP>70. **Queue pressure created by management** (e.g., understaffed Day/Late shifts, demand peaks) is an operational choice, not a worker deficit; punishing workers for the resulting errors blames the victim. **Automation quality** must be audited separately—Bot_Risk’s “instant” classification shifts hidden labor to humans. Finally, the **punitive use of error rates** late in shifts ignores that those rates conflate fatigue, pressure, casemix, and automation handoff; any accountability system must adjust for `wip_at_start`, `case_complexity_score`, and `queue_length_at_start` before judging individual `org:resource` performance.

# 6. Monitoring dashboard specification

A process-mining dashboard should surface at least eight interconnected indicators:

1. **Service time distribution** – median and P90 per `concept:name` and `shift_id` (using `pm4py.get_service_time`).
2. **Waiting time / case service wait** – derived via `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` to expose bottleneck accumulation.
3. **WIP at start / queue length** – real-time trend of `wip_at_start` and `queue_length_at_start` by shift hour.
4. **Rework rate per activity** – from `pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity`, highlighting `Quality Check` and `Rework Correction`.
5. **Complexity-adjusted error rate** – `error_flag` rate stratified by `case_complexity_score` band and `priority`, avoiding raw comparisons.
6. **Complexity mix over shift** – distribution of `case_complexity_score` by `sequence_position_within_shift` to detect case-mix drift.
7. **Batching indicators** – frequency and size of batches from `pm4py.discover_batches`, especially end-of-shift `Quality Check` bursts.
8. **Handover / queue ownership changes** – shift-start vs shift-end `queue_length_at_start` to measure backlog passed between shifts.
9. **Worker-load fairness** – events handled per `org:resource` normalized by `resource_experience_months` and `wip_at_start`, ensuring seniors are not silently overloaded nor juniors unfairly exposed.
