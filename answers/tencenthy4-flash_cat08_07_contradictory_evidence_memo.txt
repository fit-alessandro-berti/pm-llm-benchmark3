## 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

- **High fitness but low precision:** Fitness asks whether observed cases fit the discovered model; precision asks whether the model allows behaviors that did *not* happen. Pilot cases fit the simplified coordinator path well, so fitness rose. But the model also permits many combinations of skipped survey, manual activation, repeat visit, and billing start, so precision collapsed. This is a sign of loose control and variant proliferation, not a clean process.

- **Improved median but worse tail duration:** R speeds up simple cases by merging validation/scheduling and reducing handovers. The same changes harm complex cases: skipped surveys cause wrong assumptions, missing permits create late delays, manual activation adds friction, and overloaded coordinators delay recovery after failed visits. Thus the “typical” case improves while p90/p95 worsen.

- **Fewer handovers but lower resilience:** Formal handovers were reduced, but contextual knowledge became concentrated in coordinators and activation specialists. When a coordinator was absent, cases stalled. The handover network simplified, but the working-together network became more centralized; fewer handovers removed coordination overhead but also removed distributed situational awareness.

- **Lower visible cost but higher rework/downstream cost:** Direct cost fell because simple cases were faster and required fewer transfers. But the visible cost measure excludes late complaint handling, and the downstream estimate is incomplete. More second visits, manual activation, and complaint escalation shift cost into teams and time windows not fully captured in the pilot dataset.

- **Simple houses improve while apartments/old buildings deteriorate:** Low-risk houses benefit from fewer steps and faster scheduling. Medium-risk apartments and old buildings depend on surveys and permit checks; the predictive skip rule and missing permit checks are often wrong there. Apartments also show worsened installationactivation time, and business customers experience billing disputes from manual activation. Aggregate metrics mix these opposite effects, especially because pilot regions had more apartments and older buildings.

## 4. Decision alternatives

| Alternative | Advantages | Risks / evidence concerns | Reasonable if |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Scale R nationally** | Captures median-cycle and visible-cost gains; simple operating model; fewer handovers. | Ignores p90/p95 deterioration, rework, complaints, billing-before-activation, permit misses, low precision, coordinator bottlenecks, and segment harm. | Only after risk-adjusted controls show tail, quality, cost, and conformance metrics at or better than baseline. Current evidence does not support this. |
| **B. Roll back to baseline** | Removes harmful survey skipping and post-billing activation; restores known process controls. | Discards real gains for simple houses; loses coordinator accountability and faster scheduling; may reintroduce appointment delays and handover overhead. | If conformance failures become severe, billing/activation incidents create customer or regulatory risk, or targeted controls cannot stabilize R. |
| **C. Scale coordinator ownership only; suspend survey skipping and post-billing activation** | Preserves likely beneficial accountability and fewer handovers; removes two most suspicious design elements. | Coordinator overload and resilience risks remain; may still miss permit checks or segment-specific needs; no strong measurement plan. | As an interim operating change if risk segmentation is not yet reliable and immediate harm must be stopped. Should include backup/shared-context controls and utilization caps. |
| **D. Run controlled regional experiment with better measurement** | Highest causal learning; can isolate case mix, segment effects, and downstream cost; avoids premature national commitment. | Delays scale-up; experiment design must handle contamination, seasonality, and enough sample size for tail metrics. | If management can tolerate waiting and wants decision-grade evidence before changing design at scale. Best used inside E, not instead of immediate guardrails. |
| **E. Hybrid: deploy R for simple low-risk houses, add controls for risky segments, run measurement before wider scale** | Preserves demonstrated benefits; contains harm; aligns process controls with risk; builds evidence for later decisions. | Requires reliable risk classification, disciplined conformance monitoring, and separate capacity management for activation/second visits. | Best current option given mixed evidence, confounded data, and strong segment-level effect heterogeneity. |

## 6. Final recommendation

**Recommended decision: Alternative E — controlled hybrid deployment.**

- **Immediate action:** Freeze national scaling. Establish a 6–8 week monitored hybrid period with risk gates, daily conformance alerts, and weekly executive review.

- **What to pause:**  
  - Survey skipping for medium-risk apartments, old buildings, permit-sensitive addresses, and any incomplete-risk case.  
  - Activation checks moved after billing start; restore activation-before-billing as the default.  
  - Any rollout beyond simple low-risk houses until measurement gates are passed.

- **What to scale:**  
  - Coordinator ownership and merged validation/scheduling for **simple, low-risk houses only**.  
  - Require shared case notes, backup coordinators, and a coordinator utilization cap around 85% to avoid the 93% bottleneck observed in the pilot.  
  - Mandatory permit checks and pre-billing activation for all other segments.

- **What to measure next:** Prioritize M7 and M4 immediately; then M6 and M1 for survey-skip eligibility; embed M3, M2, M8, and M5 into the hybrid rollout.

- **Evidence required for broader rollout:** Expand only if, after risk adjustment and complete downstream-cost measurement, the hybrid shows: no billing-before-activation; permit-check compliance above 98%; p90/p95 no worse than baseline; first-time-right 82%; second visits 12%; manual activation 8%; complaints 7%; SLA breach 14%; full cost EUR198; and NPS within 2 points of baseline. If these thresholds are not met, keep R limited to simple low-risk houses or roll back remaining risky elements.
