# 2. Benign alternative explanations

- **Pattern: VendorMaster_Lena ↔ AP_Kim around V-77x (bank changes, fast payment approvals, `manual_upload`, loops)**
  - Benign explanation A: planned mass-maintenance after the vendor family’s banking-platform change, logged into cases without the maintenance-ticket linkage.
    - Support: change-program tickets listing V-77x vendors, approved maintenance windows, consistent bank-routing changes across many vendors, uploader is a controlled service function, and payments show normal downstream controls (holds/verification).
    - Refute: bank changes lacking approved tickets, repeated changes to a small subset of vendor IDs, or payments released to newly changed accounts without secondary verification.
  - Benign explanation B: AP_Kim is a designated high-risk-supplier approver and is acting as a compensating control (separate from the person executing the bank change in the source system), while `manual_upload` reflects a temporary workaround.
    - Support: SoD matrix, IAM role assignments, explicit procedure for manual uploads, independent evidence of review (checklists, attachments), and source-system audit shows different user IDs for change vs approval.
    - Refute: the same underlying user ID performs both actions, approvals occur from the same workstation/session, or documentation is consistently missing.
  - Benign explanation C: timestamp/order artifacts from manual imports make actions appear closer together (or reversed) than they were operationally.
    - Support: import logs showing batch times, effective timestamps stored elsewhere, and consistent “identical minute” patterns.
    - Refute: underlying ERP audit trail confirms the actions truly happened minutes apart in the recorded order.

- **Pattern: Fast emergency approvals and some Create-PO/Approve-PO SoD exceptions (Buyer_Carla→Approver_Gita; Buyer_Dan→Manager_Hugo)**
  - Benign explanation A: standing emergency delegation and pre-approved catalogs/framework agreements enable rapid approvals; the event log only captures the final workflow click.
    - Support: emergency policy, purchase_category alignment (medical supplies, facilities repairs), on-call roster, pre-approval references, and consistent retrospective justification by a different person within 3 working days.
    - Refute: fast approvals also occur in non-emergency cases, high-value approvals lack any justification, or the same actor repeatedly appears across override, approval, and justification.
  - Benign explanation B: actor-mapping issues (shared accounts, bot/human confusion) create apparent four-eyes violations.
    - Support: `entered_by` / `approved_by` show different people than `org:resource`; `legacy_erp` cases have higher apparent violations; mapping-table fix reduces violations retrospectively.
    - Refute: `entered_by` and `approved_by` match the same unique identity and source-system audit confirms single-user execution.

- **Pattern: Emergency Override + Retrospective Justification by the same person, sometimes batch-entered after quarter close**
  - Benign explanation A: justifications were created timely in emails/tickets but later migrated to the event log (quarter-close backfill).
    - Support: `source_system="quarter_close_upload"`, identical timestamps, original ticket timestamps within 3 working days, and attachments showing earlier sign-off.
    - Refute: no contemporaneous evidence exists; all justification content appears created only at quarter close.
  - Benign explanation B: assistants entered justifications under a shared account; the approver of record is captured in `entered_by`.
    - Support: `entered_by` contains assistant identities, shared-account policy exists, and access logs show different users.
    - Refute: access logs show the same user/session performed override and justification.

- **Pattern: Subcontracting loops (Gita→Carla→Gita; Hugo→Dan→Hugo; Lena→Kim→Lena)**
  - Benign explanation A: clarification/rework cycles (missing quote, corrected amount/vendor, requested attachments) drive loop patterns.
    - Support: lifecycle transitions indicating rework/rejection, document version changes, or updated attributes between loop steps.
    - Refute: repeated approve-return-approve with no substantive change, consistently within minutes.
  - Benign explanation B: end-of-quarter workload compression increases quick returns and batch updates (especially with Temp_Nina support).
    - Support: temporal clustering near quarter close, higher `quarter_close_upload`, and staffing/shift evidence.
    - Refute: pattern persists evenly throughout the quarter and is isolated to a small vendor family without operational rationale.

- **Pattern: Vendor Check + Change Vendor Bank Details by the same vendor-master resource**
  - Benign explanation A: this reflects role design (vendor master function owns both checks and bank maintenance) and is not a defined four-eyes rule.
    - Support: formal job descriptions, SoD matrix, and separate control elsewhere (e.g., bank-change approval in another module).
    - Refute: policy explicitly requires dual control for bank changes or independent review is absent.
  - Benign explanation B: staffing constraints force task bundling; control relies on post-change monitoring.
    - Support: staffing schedules and evidence of monitoring alerts/reconciliations.
    - Refute: adequate staffing existed but the same individual repeatedly performed both tasks without oversight.

# 4. Network and conformance analyses

The goal is to (a) separate likely operational explanations (emergency specialization, migration work, role design) from (b) true control weaknesses that merit targeted follow-up, while (c) explicitly accounting for known data-quality issues.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pandas.tseries.offsets import BDay

df = pd.read_csv("p2p_event_log.csv", parse_dates=["time:timestamp"])

df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
)

df["actor"] = df["entered_by"].fillna(df["approved_by"]).fillna(df["org:resource"])

log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(
    pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        resource_key="actor"
    )
)

viol_create_approve_po = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
    log,
    "Create Purchase Order",
    "Approve Purchase Order",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="actor"
)

viol_bank_payment = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
    log,
    "Change Vendor Bank Details",
    "Payment Approval",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="actor"
)

handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log)
working_together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log)
subcontracting_2 = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(log, n=2)

case_emergency = df.groupby("case:concept:name")["emergency_flag"].max().astype(bool)
df_em = df[df["case:concept:name"].isin(case_emergency[case_emergency].index)]
df_ne = df[df["case:concept:name"].isin(case_emergency[~case_emergency].index)]

log_em = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(
    pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df_em,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        resource_key="actor"
    )
)
log_ne = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(
    pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df_ne,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        resource_key="actor"
    )
)

fast_po = pm4py.filter_paths_performance(
    log,
    ("Create Purchase Order", "Approve Purchase Order"),
    max_performance=15 * 60
)
fast_bank_to_pay = pm4py.filter_paths_performance(
    log,
    ("Change Vendor Bank Details", "Payment Approval"),
    max_performance=30 * 60
)

df_times = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
    df,
    case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
)

skeleton_model = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(log)
skeleton_conf = pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton(log, skeleton_model)

def retro_justification_ok(g):
    g = g.sort_values("time:timestamp")
    ov = g[g["concept:name"] == "Emergency Override"]
    if ov.empty:
        return True
    t0 = ov["time:timestamp"].iloc[-1]
    r0 = ov["actor"].iloc[-1]
    deadline = t0 + BDay(3)
    retro = g[(g["concept:name"] == "Retrospective Justification") & (g["time:timestamp"] <= deadline)]
    return (not retro.empty) and (retro["actor"] != r0).any()

retro_ok_by_case = df.groupby("case:concept:name").apply(retro_justification_ok)

def log_for_source_system(system_name):
    d = df[df["source_system"] == system_name]
    return pm4py.convert_to_event_log(
        pm4py.format_dataframe(
            d,
            case_id="case:concept:name",
            activity_key="concept:name",
            timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
            resource_key="actor"
        )
    )

log_manual = log_for_source_system("manual_upload")
log_legacy = log_for_source_system("legacy_erp")
log_qclose = log_for_source_system("quarter_close_upload")
```

Recommended comparisons and outputs (computed from the above objects) to support triage:

- Compare violation rates by `source_system`, `vendor_id` (and vendor-family mapping if available), `amount_band`, `region`, and emergency status (emergency vs non-emergency).
- For network outputs, inspect whether edges like VendorMaster_Lena→AP_Kim and loops Lena→Kim→Lena persist after switching from `org:resource` to reconstructed `actor` (using `entered_by`/`approved_by`).
- For timing, build distributions of elapsed time for key segments (Create PO→Approve PO; Bank change→Payment approval; Override→Justification) and stratify by `source_system` and emergency status to distinguish operational “rush channels” from anomalous non-emergency pockets.
- For retrospective justification, use the log-skeleton check to enforce existence/order, and the custom check to enforce both the 3-working-day window and the “different person” requirement; flag cases where only the data-quality explanation (quarter-close upload) could reconcile the timing.

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

Evidence that would **increase concern** (higher likelihood of coordinated control circumvention, not proof of collusion):

- Source-system audit trails show the **same underlying user identity** (not just a shared `org:resource`) performing both a vendor bank change and a payment approval in the same case, especially for V-77x.
- Payments are released to **newly changed bank accounts** shortly after the change, with weak or absent independent verification (no secondary approval, no payee validation, no hold/release control).
- Repeated bank changes for the **same `vendor_id`** across multiple cases, followed by fast approvals by the same small set of actors, without documented migration tickets.
- Manual upload logs show a **single uploader** repeatedly submitting bank-change/payment-related batches, outside approved windows, or with unusual file lineage (e.g., re-uploads, edits near payment time).
- Emergency overrides occur in **non-emergency categories** or with `emergency_flag=False`, or justifications are systematically missing/late without any contemporaneous incident tickets.
- Access logs show shared-account use without attributable `entered_by` detail (e.g., approvals executed from unusual locations/devices) or unusual delegation configuration changes around the event times.

Evidence that would **reduce concern** (supporting emergency/specialization or data-quality explanations):

- Formal documentation of the V-77x bank migration, including ticket IDs, approved maintenance plans, and reconciliations, with verifiable linkage from each bank change to an authorized ticket even if the link is missing in the event log.
- Underlying ERP audit logs confirm segregation (different user IDs, enforced workflows) even when the event log shows overlap due to mapping errors.
- Clear policy evidence that emergency approvals are routed via an on-call/delegated approver (Approver_Gita, Manager_Hugo) with timely retrospective justification by a different person, supported by incident/ticket timestamps.
- Demonstrated mapping-table defects for `legacy_erp` (bot/human confusion) and shared-account practices where `entered_by` reliably identifies the real actor, and re-extracts materially change the apparent violation counts.
- Evidence that “fast” segments are artifacts of batch imports (import timestamp vs effective timestamp), validated by system logs and consistent minute-level patterns.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

Internal audit applied process-mining screening to the procurement-to-payment event log for the last quarter to identify control exceptions and unusual execution patterns. The analysis identified a small number of segregation-of-duties (four-eyes) exceptions and unusually fast approvals. Several fast approval patterns (notably Buyer_Carla→Approver_Gita for emergency medical supplies and Buyer_Dan→Manager_Hugo for weekend repairs) are plausibly consistent with documented emergency handling and informal specialization. These patterns nonetheless warrant verification that compensating controls operated as intended, in particular that retrospective justification occurred within three working days and was completed by a different person than the emergency override actor.

The most concerning subgroup is concentrated around vendor family V-77x, where cases show Change Vendor Bank Details followed shortly by Payment Approval, frequently involving the recurring pair VendorMaster_Lena and AP_Kim and often routed via `source_system="manual_upload"`. This pattern may reflect legitimate mass bank-detail maintenance following a recent banking-platform change, and known data-quality limitations (missing linkage to maintenance tickets, shared accounts, and migration artifacts) may affect attribution and timing in the event log. However, because vendor bank changes combined with payment approvals represent a high-impact diversion risk, we recommend targeted follow-up of this cluster by reconciling event-log actors with `entered_by`/`approved_by`, validating source-system audit trails for bank changes and payment approvals, and confirming the authorization and documentation of the related maintenance tickets and uploads. Findings should be reported as control exceptions pending corroboration rather than as evidence of misconduct.
