## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A compensating-control package should not be judged by counting control events. Instead, evaluate it against **seven dimensions** and classify the package into one of four tiers.

### Seven Evaluation Dimensions
1. **Timing (Gate Position):** Does the control act *before release* (hardest preventive), *after release but before irrevocable bank settlement* (contingent on stop-payment reliability), *after settlement within the 24-hour recall window* (uncertain recovery), or *after the recall window* (purely forensic)?
2. **Independence:** Is the second actor a distinct human at level L3+ with no unresolved reporting/performance-rating conflict, authenticated independently without shared credentials or uncontrolled shared-workstation handoffs?
3. **Coverage (Threat-Space Alignment):** Do the active controls actually address the *specific* threat vectors elevated in the case (e.g., AV-2 covers duplicates/bank changes, not service receipt; MG-4 covers service receipt only if receipts are actually inspected)?
4. **Overrideability & Failure Mode:** Are critical checks hard-blocking and **fail-closed** during outages, or soft-warning/fail-open and bypassable by the initiator (directly or via shared credentials)?
5. **Recoverability:** If a defect slips past release, does an operationally verified stop-before-settlement mechanism or a live bank-recall window exist when detective controls run?
6. **Evidence Quality:** Are controls backed by tamper-resistant timestamps, payload integrity between approval and release, and verifiable proof of substantive review (invoice, bank account, receipt)—or only unverified sign-offs, unsigned checklists, and ambiguous logs?
7. **Collusion & Coercion Resistance:** Does the package resist two-party coordination, repeated tight pairing, shared terminals, or supervisor-subordinate pressure?

### Four-Tier Sufficiency Classification

| Sufficiency Tier | Criteria Across the Seven Dimensions |
|---|---|
| **Fully Equivalent** | Achieves the substantive four-eyes objective before irreversible outflow: (a) hard preventive automated checks passed cleanly without override; (b) an **independent** qualified human performed a **substantive** review of supporting evidence **before release** (or before settlement with verified pre-settlement hold/cancel capability and strict ED-1 eligibility); (c) timely audit preserves recall capacity; (d) high-quality evidence confirms actor separation and no elevated collusion/conflict signal. |
| **Partially Equivalent** | Substantially reduces immediate loss risk via independent pre-settlement human review and clean automated checks, **but** suffers a bounded gap in one or more dimensions—such as a minor precondition/sequencing breach (e.g., late emergency token, approval preceding AV-2), unverified review depth, lack of justified emergency necessity, or untested pre-settlement stop mechanics. Requires remediation/governance action, though immediate financial exposure is low-to-moderate. |
| **Merely Mitigating** | Leaves a material preventive gap open (e.g., audit occurs within recall window on a nominally conforming case whose actor independence is doubtful, or automated checks run cleanly while substantive receipt/independence checks are absent). Reduces *some* threat vectors or provides partial post-facto detectability, but does **not** substitute for independent pre-settlement human authorization. |
| **Ineffective** | Fails to prevent or timely reverse unauthorized/erroneous outflow on material risk vectors: e.g., fail-open during AV-2 outage, self-override with compromised/shared credentials, conflicted or post-settlement ratification, fabricated documentation bypassing nominal four-eyes, or audit occurring after the recall window. (A lucky legitimate payment outcome does **not** upgrade an ineffective package.) |

### Why "Same Expected Loss" Is Not the Only Possible Standard
Equating control sufficiency solely with equal expected loss ($E[L] = p \times \text{Impact}$) is analytically defective for five reasons:
1. **Tail-Risk & Irreversibility Asymmetry:** A low-probability, high-impact unrecoverable wire (e.g., EUR 900,000 in AP-208) is not interchangeable with frequent small recoverable errors of equal mathematical expectation; industrial treasury controls exist specifically to bound catastrophic liquidity and fraud tail risk.
2. **Adversarial & Adaptive Probabilities:** Fraud probabilities are not stationary physical constants. A known weak point—such as an unmonitored convenience bypass, shared override credential, or fail-open outage behavior—attracts opportunistic exploitation, making historical loss frequency a severe underestimate of true risk.
3. **Deterrence & Control-Culture Erosion:** Tolerating unjustified same-user convenience bypasses (e.g., AP-201) because the amount is small erodes normative compliance and normalizes workarounds that later migrate to high-value payments.
4. **Governance, Regulatory, and Audit-Assertion Requirements:** Internal controls over financial reporting, sanctions compliance, and fiduciary stewardship require provable operating effectiveness and authorization accountability—not merely favorable ex-post loss averages.
5. **Non-Repudiation & Evidentiary Integrity:** Even when no money is lost, the organization must be able to *demonstrate* who independently verified and authorized cash disbursement; unverifiable controls fail assurance standards regardless of realized loss.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control / Variant | Primary Classification | Timing Relative to Irrevocable Loss | Threat Scope Addressed | Key Dependency for Effectiveness | Failure Mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FEP-1 (True independent two-person Prepare/Release)** | **Preventive** | Pre-release | Unilateral fraud, clerical error, unauthorized release, some delivery/reasonableness checks (if releaser inspects support) | True actor independence, distinct authentication, substantive review before release | Collusion, credential sharing, shared-workstation spoofing, rubber-stamping (AP-204) |
| **AV-2 (Hard-block mode)** | **Preventive** | Pre-release | Duplicates, sanctions, bank-account ownership, 7-day bank changes, operator limits, basic PO match | Service availability, fail-closed design, accurate master data | System outage with fail-open (AP-208); blind to receipt of goods/services and collusion |
| **AV-2 (Soft-warning mode)** | **Preventive (weak) / Detective trigger** | Pre-release | Same as above, degraded by override path | Independent, non-shareable L3+ override authentication | Self-override via shared/compromised manager credential (AP-202) |
| **MG-4 (Pre-release approval, e.g., AP-203)** | **Preventive** | Pre-release | Commercial reasonableness, receipt verification, bank-detail review, exception authorization | Manager independence (L3+, no conflict), substantive document review, payload lock until release | Rubber-stamping, post-approval tampering, approving before AV-2 runs (AP-206) |
| **MG-4 (Post-release, pre-settlement ratification, e.g., AP-201, AP-207)** | **Hybrid (Detective of unauthorized release; Preventive of settlement)** | Post-release, pre-settlement | Same as pre-release MG-4, *if* review is substantive | **Operationally enforceable pre-settlement hold/cancellation mechanism** and adequate cutoff buffer before settlement | Straight-through processing or late cutoff makes cancellation impossible; manager pressure to rubber-stamp already-released items |
| **MG-4 (Post-settlement ratification, e.g., AP-202, AP-208)** | **Detective (weak)** | Post-settlement (often post-recall) | Accountability documentation only; minimal real-time risk reduction | Timely escalation into bank recall (if <24h) or legal recovery | Confirmation bias ("already paid"), zero prevention, negligible corrective power after 24h |
| **RA-7 (Audit within ~24h recall window, e.g., AP-203, AP-206, AP-207, AP-209)** | **Detective + Partly Corrective** | Post-release / post-settlement (inside recall window) | Broadest substantive scope (receipts, collusion patterns, master-data changes, override propriety) | Fast audit initiation, deep vouching, bank recall acceptance (not guaranteed) | Recall rejected or funds already withdrawn by fraudster; superficial check |
| **RA-7 (Audit after recall window, e.g., AP-202, AP-204, AP-208)** | **Detective (forensic only)** | Post-recall | Pattern discovery, control remediation, disciplinary/legal evidence | Audit independence and forensic depth | Cash loss is usually unrecoverable; cannot substitute for preventive controls |
| **ED-1 (Emergency single-operator protocol)** | **Preventive Governance Gate (meta-control)** | Pre-preparation through post-settlement | Bounds *when* same-user handling is permissible (caps exposure & mandates compensating stack) | System-enforced preconditions (token, amount, vendor tenure, bank change, clean AV-2) | Treated as informal paperwork; bypassed by convenience (AP-201) or misapplied outside limits (AP-208) |

### Semantics of Post-Release, Pre-Settlement Manager Action
A manager ratification executed **after `Release_Payment` but before `Bank_Settlement`** (AP-201, AP-207) is best classified as a **hybrid control**:
- **Detective with respect to the internal release event:** The unauthorized or single-operator release has already occurred inside the ERP, so the manager is detecting whether an already-triggered instruction is legitimate.
- **Preventive with respect to irrevocable economic loss (settlement finality):** Because internal control treats bank settlement as the irreversibility boundary, a manager who catches a defect before settlement can *prevent* funds from irreversibly leaving the company.

However, this hybrid label carries a strict operational condition: **it earns preventive credit only when an enforceable pre-settlement stop/hold mechanism exists** (e.g., batched payment files held in a payment gateway/host-to-host queue that the manager or treasury can reliably freeze before the bank cutoff). Without verified stop-payment mechanics and sufficient cutoff buffer, post-release ratification collapses into a purely detective control—and suffers from behavioral "fait accompli" bias compared with pre-release approval (AP-203).

## 5. Counterfactual and Residual-Risk Analysis

### 1. AP-202 (EUR 480,000 — New Vendor, 2h Bank Change, Self-Override, Unrecovered Fraud)
- **Most plausible loss mechanism:** Insider payment diversion by `u23` (alone using a stolen/shared credential of `m09`, or in collusion with `m09`) directing EUR 480,000 to a related-party bank account created/changed two hours earlier without underlying receipt of goods.
- **Which control should have broken the causal chain:** Three preventive breaks failed in sequence: (1) hard-blocking (non-overridable) AV-2 for the combination of new vendor + 2-hour bank change + operator-limit breach; (2) strong MFA/non-shareable authentication on override credentials so `u23` could not invoke `m09`’s privilege; and (3) mandatory independent pre-release approval by an unconflicted L3+ manager (not `u23`’s direct supervisor).
- **What remains exposed:** Realized loss of EUR 480,000 (Very High / realized), plus systemic exposure if `m09`’s credential sharing or weak override governance persists across other users.
- **Counterfactual with a truly independent second releaser:** A genuinely independent second releaser checking vendor tenure, recent bank-change verification, operator limits, and missing receipt confirmation would very likely have **blocked release** (reducing expected loss risk from Critical/Realized to Low), unless the fraudster simultaneously compromised the second releaser or forged compelling external receipt/bank-verification evidence.

### 2. AP-204 (EUR 220,000 — Distinct Users, Shared Workstation, Fabricated Service Receipt)
- **Most plausible loss mechanism:** Either (a) active collusion between `u12` and `u13` to pay a fictitious consulting invoice, (b) credential sharing / single-person execution by one actor switching accounts on the shared workstation, or (c) chronic rubber-stamping by `u13` across a high-volume 37-payment pairing without verifying service delivery.
- **Which control breaks the causal chain:** AV-2 cannot break this chain because the vendor is established (2 years) with no bank change and AV-2 does not validate service delivery. The chain is broken only by **substantive independent verification of service receipt** (e.g., independent business-owner sign-off / three-way match by the consulting recipient) prior to release, or timely recall-window audit (here missed by 3+ days).
- **What remains exposed:** Very High exposure on EUR 220,000 (past recall window) plus High contagion risk across the other 36 paired payments by `u12`/`u13` over the preceding 30 days.
- **Counterfactual with a truly independent second releaser:** If the second releaser were genuinely independent (separate workstation, unconditioned by repetitive pairing, and required to verify receipt directly with the business owner rather than trusting a PDF uploaded by `u12`), the fabricated receipt would have a **Moderate-to-High likelihood of detection before release** (reducing risk from Critical to Low–Moderate), though sophisticated external-vendor-assisted forgery could still evade a pure treasury releaser without business-owner confirmation.

### 3. AP-208 (EUR 900,000 — AV-2 Outage, Fail-Open Release, 3-Day Bank Change, Lucky Outcome)
- **Most plausible loss mechanism:** Business email compromise (BEC) or insider bank-account hijacking exploiting the 3-day-old bank change while AV-2 is down, enabling unilateral release of EUR 900,000 with zero bank-ownership verification and no pre-settlement or recall-window detection.
- **Which control should have broken the causal chain:** (1) **Fail-closed** payment gateway blocking release during AV-2 outage; (2) system-enforced ED-1 disqualification (amount EUR 900k >> EUR 100k cap; 3-day bank change < 7-day rule); and (3) mandatory independent dual-sign-off on a verified out-of-band bank-ownership callback before release.
- **What remains exposed:** Although this specific transfer happened to be legitimate, **ex-ante residual risk was Critical** (EUR 900,000 completely exposed to irrevocable loss). Ongoing systemic risk remains Critical until fail-open release during AV-2 outages is eliminated.
- **Counterfactual with a truly independent second releaser:** An independent second releaser would have seen the AV-2 outage, the unsigned manual checklist, the EUR 900k magnitude, and the 3-day-old bank change, and—under basic control discipline—halted release pending out-of-band bank verification or AV-2 restoration. This would reduce risk from Critical to **Low–Moderate** (residual risk limited to social-engineering both actors during an outage).

### 4. AP-207 (EUR 35,000 — Valid ED-1, Post-Release / Pre-Settlement Substantive Ratification)
- **Most plausible loss mechanism:** Either (a) `u61` exploits the 35-minute post-release window (14:05–14:40) on an urgent safety repair to overpay or divert funds if the bank settles early or cancellation fails, or (b) `u61` alters payment details relative to the documents later shown to `m15`.
- **Which control breaks the causal chain:** Three layered controls break the chain: (1) AV-2 clean pass prevents bank-change/duplicate/limit/PO manipulation; (2) independent `m15`’s substantive review of all supporting evidence at 14:40 breaks the chain *before* 16:00 settlement (assuming stop-payment works); and (3) `a02`’s audit at 12:00 next day catches any missed defect while ~2–4 hours remain in the 24-hour recall window.
- **What remains exposed:** **Low** residual risk, concentrated in two narrow gaps: failure of the pre-settlement stop/cancellation process if `m15` had objected, or inability of the bank to recall rapidly if `a02` found an issue at hour 22.
- **Counterfactual with a truly independent second releaser:** Moving the independent human check from 14:40 (post-release) to *before* 14:05 (as in AP-203) eliminates the 35-minute released-but-unapproved exposure window and removes dependence on pre-settlement cancellation mechanics, reducing residual risk from **Low to Very Low**—at the modest operational cost of waiting 35 minutes during a safety repair.

### 5. AP-206 (EUR 95,000 — Production Outage, Late Token, Approval Before AV-2)
- **Most plausible loss mechanism:** (a) Premature approval by `m11` (13:04) before AV-2 executed (13:05) creates a blind spot if `m11` rubber-stamped under outage pressure without verifying master-data/limit checks, or (b) `u52` modifies invoice/payment fields between `m11`’s approval (13:04) and release (13:08), or (c) retroactive emergency-token creation (13:02) masks an improper self-release attempt.
- **Which control breaks the causal chain:** AV-2 running cleanly at 13:05 (before release at 13:08) still prevents duplicate/sanctions/bank-change/PO/limit defects; `m11`’s pre-release approval (if substantive) checks commercial validity; and `a05`’s audit at 08:00 next day (~17–19h post-release/settlement) preserves meaningful recall capacity.
- **What remains exposed:** **Low–Moderate** residual risk (elevated because EUR 95k sits just under the EUR 100k ED-1 ceiling, `m11`’s review depth is undocumented, and approval-before-AV sequencing leaves an unverified payload-integrity window).
- **Counterfactual with a truly independent second releaser:** A second independent releaser acting *after* AV-2 (13:05) would directly observe the clean AV-2 output and lock the final release payload, eliminating both the post-approval modification gap and the emergency-token timing ambiguity, lowering residual risk from **Low–Moderate to Very Low**.

### 6. AP-201 (EUR 1,800 — Low-Value Convenience Bypass Without Emergency Token)
- **Most plausible loss mechanism:** Small-value petty fraud (e.g., recurring split-invoice diversion to a friendly office-supply vendor below attention thresholds) or clerical error normalized through repeated convenience bypassing of FEP-1.
- **Which control breaks the causal chain:** AV-2 prevents duplicate/sanctions/bank-change/PO mismatches; independent `m04` ratification at 08:30 intercepts before 10:00 settlement (if stop-payment works and review is substantive); `a02` audit next morning provides late detection (though at 25h post-release, recall capacity is marginal).
- **What remains exposed:** **Low** direct financial exposure on EUR 1,800, but **Moderate** behavioral/governance exposure: unpenalized convenience bypasses invite "threshold-splitting" and erode compliance norms across higher-value payments.
- **Counterfactual with a truly independent second releaser:** Eliminates the unauthorized 24-minute release window (08:06–08:30), removes reliance on pre-settlement cancellation, and preserves normative deterrence—shifting governance risk from **Moderate to Very Low** with minimal operational friction for routine office supplies.

### B. Evidence Collection Enhancements
1. **Immutable Payload Locking & Hashing:** Record a cryptographic hash of payee, IBAN, amount, and invoice ID at `Manager_Approve` and `Automated_Validate` and reject `Release_Payment` if any field mutates afterward (closes the AP-203/AP-206 post-approval tampering window).
2. **Structured Proof of Substantive Review:** Replace bare approval clicks with mandatory structured attestations linked to artifact IDs (invoice, verified bank master record, independent business-owner goods/service receipt confirmation).
3. **Point-in-Time Independence Metadata:** Snapshot HR reporting lines, performance-rating relationships, and L3+ grade at approval time so conflicts (like `m09`/`u23`) are prevented automatically rather than discovered post-facto.
4. **Pre-Settlement Hold Telemetry:** Log bank-batch cutoff times, queue-freeze capabilities, and actual cancellation tests so post-release/pre-settlement controls (AP-207) can be empirically validated.
5. **Formal Registry of Authorized Two-Person Cells:** Maintain signed org-design documentation for any legitimate dedicated payment cells (resolving the AP-204/AP-209 ambiguity) and pair them with periodic rotation and independent business-owner receipt confirmation.

### C. Distinguishing an Alert from Proof of Misconduct
Monitoring alerts are **risk hypotheses and triage signals—never proof of fraud, collusion, or intentional misconduct**. Confusing the two creates unfair disciplinary outcomes and misallocates investigation effort:
- **Plausible benign explanations exist for many alerts:**
  - *Repeated pairs + shared workstation (AP-209):* May reflect an under-documented dedicated two-person treasury cell operating a secure payment terminal.
  - *Late emergency token (AP-206):* May result from system clock skew, asynchronous logging latency, or an operator starting preparation during a verified production outage while the token ticket was still being issued.
  - *Fail-open release during outage (AP-208):* May reflect poor system design and lack of training during an operational emergency rather than malicious intent (indeed, the payment was legitimate).
  - *Same-user blocked attempt (AP-205):* May reflect clerical confusion or missing UI guardrails rather than deliberate probing.
- **Evidentiary progression required:**
  1. **Alert (Automated Signal):** Triggers risk-tiered review (e.g., hold payment before settlement or prioritize recall-window audit).
  2. **Control-Deficiency Finding:** Established when logs and process facts show a control failed to operate as designed (e.g., AP-204’s fabricated receipt passing unchallenged; AP-208’s fail-open EUR 900k release)—independent of actor intent.
  3. **Misconduct / Collusion Determination:** Requires corroborating forensic evidence beyond process logs—such as SSO/endpoint/badge logs proving single-person credential misuse, vendor beneficial-ownership links (as emerging in AP-202), communication records, falsified document metadata, and fair investigative interviews.

### Notes on PM4Py Cross-Version Behavior
Because PM4Py’s high-level LTL filtering helpers have evolved across 2.x releases, the script explicitly guards against four behaviors that cannot be guaranteed identically across all installed versions:
1. **Filter polarity (`POSITIVE` parameter):** In modern PM4Py 2.7+, `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, act1, act2)` returns traces *violating* the four-eyes principle (same resource executing both activities). In some earlier 2.x versions or direct `ltl_checker.four_eyes_principle` invocations, the default polarity returned *conforming* traces (`resource_1 != resource_2`). The script verifies the returned case set against completed cases and automatically aligns polarity with an explicit diagnostic message.
2. **Handling of incomplete cases (e.g., `AP-205`):** When complement logic (`all_cases - conforming_cases`) is used internally, a case lacking `Release_Payment` altogether can appear in the filtered output even though no payment was completed. Intersecting with `completed_cases` prevents `AP-205` from being misclassified as a completed-payment violation and isolates it under `attempted_blocked_cases`.
3. **Return object type:** Newer PM4Py versions return a `pandas.DataFrame` when given a formatted DataFrame, whereas older versions may return an `EventLog` object; the `isinstance(..., pd.DataFrame)` check handles both safely.
4. **Timezone-aware timestamps & default resource column:** `pm4py.format_dataframe` assumes `org:resource` as the default resource key in `filter_four_eyes_principle`, and some older PM4Py builds emit warnings or type mismatches on blank resources or mixed timezone awareness. Normalizing blank resources to `"SYSTEM"` and parsing all timestamps uniformly with `utc=True` prevents silent mismatches.
