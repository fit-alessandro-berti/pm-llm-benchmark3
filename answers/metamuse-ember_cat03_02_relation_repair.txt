## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

**Hard invariants.** Relations must reference existing event and object IDs; object types must fit the activity (e.g. a Package, not an Item, for the package role in Pack Item); O2O direction must follow the qualifier (`DEL-X carries PK-7`, not the reverse); exact duplicate relation tuples are forbidden; and a proposed relation must not invert the observed timestamp order for the same object (e.g. payment before invoice). No new events or objects are invented.

**Strong defaults.** The domain cardinalities are treated as defaults, not axioms: Create Order relates to one Order; Add Item to one Order and one Item; Pack Item normally to one Item and one Package, with a scanner-outage exception; Dispatch Package normally to one Package and one Delivery, with bulk dispatch possible; Record Payment to one Payment and normally one Invoice, with unapplied cash possible; Issue Invoice to one Invoice and one or more Orders.

**Weak heuristics.** Identifier similarity, scanner-reference prefixes, shared route/customer, timestamp proximity, and arithmetic agreement are corroborating signals only. An exact carrier reference is stronger than a truncated `N-42*`; a `?` or `*` materially discounts it. Amount agreement is never proof: fees, taxes, discounts, partial payment, and unapplied cash are all live alternatives.

**Evidence against a repair.** Customer or route mismatch, temporal inversion, creation of an orphan object that had a plausible role, cross-order mixing without consolidation evidence, and reliance on arithmetic alone all count against a candidate.

I combine evidence abductively: a candidate must first pass the hard invariants, then needs either one strong identifier match or convergence of at least two semi-independent signals. Pre-existing O2O edges are independent support; O2O edges inferred from a proposed E2O edge are not counted as independent confirmation of that same edge. Lifecycle completeness is a desideratum, not evidence — I do not reward a repair merely for closing every lifecycle. Confidence around 0.75 denotes a strong provisional candidate, 0.6 a moderate one, and below roughly 0.4 I leave the relation unresolved.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A — separate deliveries, consolidated billing, incomplete ORD-B physical trail.** This is the selected, minimum-change bundle and matches the table above.

- **ev08 / PK-8:** leave ev08 item-only. IT-B1 ends at Pack Item, consistent with the stated scanner-outage possibility. The `PK8-2` token is noted but not treated as decisive because the scanner reference may encode a scanner, not the package.
- **ev10 / DEL-X / DEL-Y:** add ev10–DEL-Y and DEL-Y carries PK-8. DEL-X keeps only PK-7. Both delivery objects acquire dispatched lifecycles and neither is orphaned.
- **ev11 / deliveries:** add nothing. `N-42*` is genuinely ambiguous between N-420 and N-421, and the portal may retain only the customer/order. Adding one delivery fabricates a confirmation; adding both fabricates a joint acknowledgement.
- **INV-44 / ORD-B:** add ev12–ORD-B and INV-44 bills ORD-B provisionally. Customer, timing, and the exact 130+100=230 correspondence converge, but fees or a different billing scope remain possible.
- **PAY-2 / INV-44:** add ev14–INV-44 and PAY-2 settles INV-44 provisionally. Two sequential payments totalling the invoice amount form a coherent partial-settlement story, while unapplied cash remains possible.

Bundle A yields a coherent ORD-A lifecycle and a plausible consolidated invoice, but deliberately leaves ORD-B without a package or delivery trail and leaves both deliveries without confirmation.

**Bundle B — consolidated package and joint delivery acknowledgement.** This alternative accepts more physical consolidation.

- **ev08 / PK-8:** add ev08–PK-8 and PK-8 packs IT-B1. PK-8 then holds IT-A2 (ORD-A) and IT-B1 (ORD-B), explaining two packages for three items and the `PK8-2` second-item token. Both orders share customer C17, route NORTH, and day, so same-customer consolidation is plausible, though not evidenced by an explicit flag.
- **ev10 / DEL-X / DEL-Y:** as in Bundle A, ev10–DEL-Y and DEL-Y carries PK-8. DEL-X carries PK-7 only.
- **ev11 / deliveries:** add both DEL-X and DEL-Y. Within Bundle B this is stronger than a single assignment because ORD-A's items travel on both deliveries, so an order-level confirmation for ORD-A most coherently acknowledges both; `N-42*` is a common prefix and several deliveries may be acknowledged together. Alternatives are weaker *inside this bundle* because a single-delivery reading leaves half of ORD-A unconfirmed. This still is not unique in absolute terms: the portal may have recorded only the order.
- **INV-44 / ORD-B:** add as in Bundle A; physical consolidation does not itself prove billing consolidation, so confidence is unchanged.
- **PAY-2 / INV-44:** add as in Bundle A.

Bundle B closes ORD-A completely and gives ORD-B a package/delivery path through PK-8 and DEL-Y, at the cost of a cross-order package and a many-to-many Order–Package relationship. ORD-B still lacks an ev11 order link, so its confirmation remains incomplete.

**Bundle C — skeptical split-billing / unapplied-cash variant.** Accept only ev10–DEL-Y. Leave ev08, ev11, INV-44–ORD-B, and PAY-2–INV-44 unresolved or rejected: the 230 total could reflect ORD-A plus charges, PAY-1 could be a partial payment, and PAY-2 could be unapplied cash. This bundle is coherent but leaves INV-44 partly settled, PAY-2 without an invoice, and ORD-B without billing — a useful warning that Bundle A's billing additions are abductive, not forced.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

**Order.** In Bundle A, ORD-A has Create  Add ×2  Confirm Delivery  Issue Invoice, a plausible complete order lifecycle. ORD-B has Create  Add  Issue Invoice (provisional), but no package or delivery confirmation. That tail is an acknowledged gap, not a reason to force ev08 or ev11 links. No order exceeds the one-Create-Order default.

**Item.** IT-A1 and IT-A2 each have Add Item  Pack Item, with pre-existing O2O support (PK-7 packs IT-A1, PK-8 packs IT-A2) independent of any new E2O assumption. IT-B1 has Add Item  Pack Item only. This is the clearest orphan tail in Bundle A and is exactly what Bundle B would eliminate by sharing PK-8.

**Package.** PK-7: Pack  Dispatch. PK-8: Pack  Dispatch under Bundle A once DEL-Y carries it. Packages have no creation event in this log, so their lifecycles legitimately start at Pack Item. In Bundle B, PK-8 would have Pack  Pack  Dispatch, a suspicious repeated-activity package lifecycle signalling consolidation.

**Delivery.** DEL-X has Dispatch only; DEL-Y gains Dispatch only in Bundle A. Neither has Confirm Delivery, because ev11 is unresolved. That is preferable to inventing a carrier-specific confirmation from `N-42*`. DEL-Y is not orphaned in Bundle A, whereas a bulk DEL-X reading of ev10 would orphan it.

**Invoice.** INV-44 has Issue Invoice  Record Payment, plus a second Record Payment in Bundle A. One invoice billing two orders is a one-to-many pattern explicitly allowed by the semantics, but it remains a modeling risk because it merges two orders' billing. Two payments settling one invoice is a many-to-one pattern and is consistent with partial settlement.

**Payment.** PAY-1 is fully linked; PAY-2 is linked to its recording event in the observed data and provisionally to INV-44. If the PAY-2 addition is wrong, PAY-2 becomes unapplied cash — possible under the semantics, but then INV-44 is only partly settled.

**Circularity warning.** DEL-Y carries PK-8 is inferred from ev10–DEL-Y plus dispatch semantics; it must not then be cited as independent evidence that ev10–DEL-Y is correct. The same applies to INV-44 bills ORD-B (derived from ev12–ORD-B) and PAY-2 settles INV-44 (derived from ev14–INV-44). Pre-existing edges such as PAY-1 settles INV-44 support the *pattern*, not the identity of the new edge.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**OC-DFG.** Unrepaired, ORD-B's order-level directly-follows graph is only Create Order  Add Item. Bundle A/B add Add Item  Issue Invoice for ORD-B. DEL-Y gains a Dispatch Package node for its object type in Bundle A/B; in Bundle B, DEL-X and DEL-Y also gain Dispatch Package  Confirm Delivery. Bundle B additionally gives PK-8 a Pack Item  Pack Item self-transition before Dispatch Package, making consolidation visible.

**OTG.** Type-level edges mostly already exist, so the OTG changes mainly in object-pair weight. Bundle A raises Delivery–Package from one pair (DEL-X–PK-7) to two, Invoice–Order from one to two, and Payment–Invoice from one to two. Bundle B additionally raises Package–Item from two pairs to three via PK-8–IT-B1. Edge *existence* therefore understates the effect; edge frequency and object-pair counts matter.

**ET–OT graph.** Bundle A creates Dispatch Package–Delivery for ev10, a second Issue Invoice–Order object instance for ev12, and Record Payment–Invoice for ev14. Bundle B further creates Pack Item–Package for ev08 and Confirm Delivery–Delivery for ev11. E2O additions affect ET–OT and OC-DFG; O2O additions alone do not affect flattenings unless an enrichment step is explicitly applied.

**Flattenings.** Order flattening: ORD-B gains Issue Invoice in A/B, but gains neither Pack nor Dispatch because those events are not directly related to ORD-B. Package flattening: PK-8 is Pack  Dispatch in A, Pack  Pack  Dispatch in B. Delivery flattening: DEL-Y goes from empty to Dispatch in A/B; in B, DEL-X and DEL-Y each gain Confirm Delivery. Invoice flattening gains a second Record Payment in A/B. Payment flattening is unchanged by the INV-44 E2O addition, because PAY-2 was already related to ev14.

**Frequencies.** Event/activity counts are invariant: 14 events, with Create Order 2, Add Item 3, Pack Item 3, Dispatch 2, Confirm 1, Issue 1, Record Payment 2. What changes is unique-object and total-object frequency. Unique deliveries involved in Dispatch rise from 1 to 2 in Bundle A. Unique orders in Issue Invoice rise from 1 to 2. Total related objects rise by three in Bundle A (ev10, ev12, ev14), while unique invoices in Record Payment remain one because both payments point to INV-44.

**Equivalent-execution clustering around Order.** Unrepaired, ORD-A and ORD-B have clearly different traces. Bundle A moves ORD-B closer to ORD-A by adding Issue Invoice, but they still differ by Confirm Delivery, so a trace-based clustering should keep them separate. Bundle B is riskier: if clustering uses O2O closure, ORD-A and ORD-B share PK-8 and DEL-Y, and may be clustered as equivalent executions of one consolidated shipment even though they remain two orders. That is a concrete false-merge risk.

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Preserve originals before any repair.
events_0 = events.copy(deep=True)
objects_0 = objects.copy(deep=True)
relations_0 = relations.copy(deep=True)
o2o_0 = o2o.copy(deep=True)

# Bundle A additions. Assumes OCEL 2.0-style column names.
e2o_additions = pd.DataFrame([
    {"ocel:eid": "ev10", "ocel:oid": "DEL-Y",  "ocel:qualifier": "delivery"},
    {"ocel:eid": "ev12", "ocel:oid": "ORD-B",  "ocel:qualifier": "order"},
    {"ocel:eid": "ev14", "ocel:oid": "INV-44", "ocel:qualifier": "invoice"},
])
o2o_additions = pd.DataFrame([
    {"ocel:oid": "DEL-Y",  "ocel:oid_2": "PK-8",   "ocel:qualifier": "carries"},
    {"ocel:oid": "INV-44", "ocel:oid_2": "ORD-B",  "ocel:qualifier": "bills"},
    {"ocel:oid": "PAY-2",  "ocel:oid_2": "INV-44", "ocel:qualifier": "settles"},
])

relations_r = pd.concat([relations, e2o_additions], ignore_index=True)
o2o_r = pd.concat([o2o, o2o_additions], ignore_index=True)
events_r = events.copy(deep=True)
objects_r = objects.copy(deep=True)

# Referential integrity: every referenced event and object must exist.
assert set(relations_r["ocel:eid"]).issubset(set(events_r["ocel:eid"]))
assert set(relations_r["ocel:oid"]).issubset(set(objects_r["ocel:oid"]))
assert set(o2o_r["ocel:oid"]).issubset(set(objects_r["ocel:oid"]))
assert set(o2o_r["ocel:oid_2"]).issubset(set(objects_r["ocel:oid"]))

# Duplicate checks on the full relation tuple.
assert not relations_r.duplicated(
    subset=["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"]).any()
assert not o2o_r.duplicated(
    subset=["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"]).any()

# VERSION-DEPENDENT: the OCEL constructor import and signature vary
# across PM4Py versions; adjust this single line to the installed version.
ocel = pm4py.OCEL(events_r, objects_r, relations_r, o2o_r)

ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

# Deliberately do NOT call pm4py.ocel_o2o_enrichment here: enrichment
# would traverse O2O edges and could present inferred relations as
# if they were observed event-object relations.

order_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "order")
delivery_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "delivery")

print("OCDFG:", ocdfg)
print("OTG:", otg)
print("ET-OT:", etot)
print("Order cases:", len(order_flat))
print("Delivery cases:", len(delivery_flat))
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Cross-order package fabrication.** Adding ev08–PK-8 creates a consolidated package that may never have existed, teaching the model that ORD-A and ORD-B are routinely packed together and merging their lifecycles.
2. **False delivery confirmation.** Assigning ev11 to DEL-X or DEL-Y from `N-42*` invents on-time delivery evidence, shortens apparent lead times, and hides the portal's loss of the carrier identifier.
3. **False ORD-B closure.** Adding ev11–ORD-B would make ORD-B look delivered and paid-path complete, and could pull it into ORD-A's equivalent-execution cluster.
4. **False consolidated billing.** Treating 130+100=230 as proof of INV-44 bills ORD-B merges two orders' revenue and margin, masking fees, discounts, or a separate missing invoice.
5. **False paid-in-full status.** Adding PAY-2 settles INV-44 closes the invoice and suppresses dunning, dispute, and unapplied-cash behaviour that the domain explicitly allows.
6. **Orphaning DEL-Y.** Reading ev10 as bulk DEL-X dispatch despite `N-421?` erases DEL-Y from the dispatch lifecycle and creates a phantom delivery object with no events.
7. **Circular confidence inflation.** If inferred O2O edges are then used as independent support for the E2O edges they were derived from, ET–OT and OTG weights double-count a single guess and present it as corroborated structure.
