# 3. Alternative segmentation

**Alternative A — congestion-state segmentation (primary alternative).** Segment by backlog state rather than calendar: **LOW** (mean_wip  25: Apr 6–14, all of Apr 11–12/18–19, Apr 25–26, May 2–3) vs **HIGH** (mean_wip > 25: Apr 15–17, Apr 20–24, Apr 27–May 1), with an outage overlay on Apr 15. Operational story: the queue is a two-state system ("controllable" vs "overloaded"); memory lives in the backlog, and control (surge staffing) should trigger on state, not on the calendar.

- *Weaknesses:* WIP is endogenous/post-hoc (you only know the state after measuring it); the threshold (25) is arbitrary; it pools different *causes* (outage vs mix shift) and therefore cannot attribute congestion to demand vs capacity — a staffing model built on it would confuse prognosis with diagnosis.

- *Weaknesses:* still pools weekday/weekend within eras ( differs ~2.4×); hides the intervention's effect on the *balance* of the queue (R5w net 13.4/day vs R6w +6/day); era-level service means would mix outage-inflated days with clean ones.

**Comparison.** The primary 8-regime view is best for staffing and Little's-Law work; Alternative A is best for designing control rules; Alternative B is best for narrative and hypothesis generation about the Apr 20 event. They are complementary, not competitors; I carry the primary one forward.

The CUSUM of (arrivals  completed) kinks upward at Apr 15, climbs +76 over Apr 20–28, and bends down from Apr 29. Three independent signals agree on the era structure; they remain heuristics over 20 weekday points with no significance guarantees.

**Why four representative days cannot ground a stochastic model.** They provide 48 bins but no inter-arrival times, no post-intervention weekday vector, no recovery-day vector, and no replication of any regime's intraday pattern; they cannot identify an arrival-process family (NHPP vs Markov-modulated vs scheduled appointments), cannot resolve the T8 discrepancy, and cannot pin the overnight boundary condition. They support *regime-conditional burstiness comparisons only*.

# 7. Operational implications

1. **Staff to workload-minutes, not case counts.** Demand measured in *minutes of work* rose 77% from R1 (2,820 min/day) to R5w (5,005 min/day) while case volume rose only 14% — the mix shift, not headcount demand, broke the queue. Build the staffing model on forecast `complex_pct` × variant-specific service means. If the mix persists, R5w needs <1  roughly 7 weekday FTE (or equivalent service-time reduction); the weekend increase 2.53.0 is justified by R5e's 1.15.
2. **Formalize the outage-recovery playbook.** The Apr 16–17 surge (+1 FTE, 10/h, drained 30 cases in 2 days) worked; codify trigger (e.g., opening backlog or prior-day net flow), target drain rate, and expected recovery horizon. Never use recovery-day throughput (129 completions) as a sustainable-capacity benchmark — it was backlog burn-down at delivered 0.91.
3. **Peak smoothing.** Post-shift, 40–50% of weekday demand arrives in a 2-hour window (location disputed per T8 — resolve before acting). Options: staggered staff start times aligned to the load curve, appointment/slotting for complex cases, opening-hour triage fast lane. Flat capacity against a 2.75× peak guarantees midday WIP build-up even at <1.
4. **Data collection.** Log case-level events (arrival, service start/end, variant), opening and closing backlog counts, exact intervention timestamps, outage intervals, and variant *coding changes*; take WIP snapshots across 24 h. This converts Sections 2–6 from aggregate inference into direct measurement (and enables the PM4Py sketches in Section 8).
5. **A policy that looks good pooled but is risky segmented:** "Pooled /  0.96 and pooled W  4 h look tolerable — end the intervention and return to uniform 6.0-FTE weekday staffing." Segmented, this re-enters R5w conditions (1.17, 13.4 cases/day, WIP +2.2/day) within a week; conversely, uniform 7-FTE staffing year-round would over-provision genuine baseline weeks. The pooled view also cannot see that the *intervention weekend* (FTE 3.0) is running at 0.96 — little headroom left.
