## 2. Process-model consistency checks

Build a small **normative automaton** (or a Declare set) from the stated lifecycle, not from a frequent DFG. Allowed moves after the first `Coverage Check Completed`: fast-track `Damage Assessment Completed`; or `Documents Requested` (then Received → Validated, with another Request possible); `Fraud Investigation Opened` after a trigger, concurrent with evidence or assessment; `Settlement Decision Recorded` only after assessment; `Appeal` only after Partial/Deny; a second `Settlement Decision Recorded` only after appeal; `Claim Closed` only after a final decision or withdrawal, and then **nothing**. A frequent DFG edge is evidence of correlation in the log, not a rule: timestamp ties, missing indices, and parallelism will invent edges such as `Claim Closed → Damage Assessment Completed`.

**Prefix-time availability.** For every feature and every explanation atom, recompute from the event-log prefix `{e | (ts(e), seq(e)) ≤ (t0, seq_t0)}` plus attributes whose `valid_from ≤ t0 < valid_to`. Fail the check if prefix value ≠ value used in the explanation. This catches complete-trace fields (`total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_handler_team`, `final_decision`, `last_resource_role`) even when they are constant on a lucky subset.

**Activity-order feasibility.** Token-based replay (or alignment) against the automaton flags impossible orders. `Claim Closed → Damage Assessment Completed` must fail always. `Appeal → Settlement Decision Recorded` must pass only when an appeal is in the prefix after Partial/Deny. Limitations: alignment fitness can hide optional concurrency (fraud) and silent skips (fast track); a fitting trace can still contain leaked *features*.

**Same-timestamp ambiguity.** Any pair with equal timestamps and a missing sequence index is unlabeled concurrency, not a directly-follows relation. Validation: (i) refuse to instantiate DFG/LTL pattern features on ties; (ii) randomly permute tied events and require pattern SHAP to be unstable before it is shown; (iii) prefer explicit start/complete lifecycle transitions if the source system has them.

**Complete-trace detector.** For each feature, compare `f(prefix)` vs `f(full_trace)`. Any disagreement is leakage. Variant IDs should be prefix variants; “anywhere/total/final/last/current-without-as-of” naming is a review trigger.

**Enabled vs impossible vs future-possible at `t0`.** After coverage check, explanations must label each cited activity:
- *observed in prefix* (only Registered, Coverage Check);
- *enabled or future-possible* (documents, fast-track assessment, fraud if a trigger exists);
- *not yet enabled* (first settlement, appeal, close);
- *impossible* (anything after close; assessment after close; appeal before a Partial/Deny).

An explainer may not treat a future-possible activity as an observed driver. Footprint/Declare response constraints (`Coverage Check` eventually followed by assessment or documents) describe later obligation, not present evidence.

**Repeated settlement decisions.** A second `Settlement Decision Recorded` is legitimate iff an `Appeal` sits between the two decisions and the first decision was Partial or Deny. Checks: `#(Settlement Decision) ≤ 1 + #(Appeal)`; no appeal after Approve/Withdrawn; do not collapse both decisions into one `final_decision` used at `t0`. This is exactly why `Appeal → Settlement Decision Recorded` is semantically different from the same-timestamp closed→assessment pattern: one is a real later continuation, the other is not a process move at all.

**Limitations to state in the suite.** DFG support ≠ permission. Prefix replay does not catch warehouse snapshots refreshed at 23:00 or SQL `extraction_date` predicates. Declare models underspecify real policy (who may open fraud). Pass/fail must therefore combine process constraints with feature-store as-of tests, not process mining alone.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

**Defensible predictive signal.** `initial_damage_complexity` (frozen), `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, and—if lineage is confirmed—`initial_reserve` and a repaired `prior_claim_count_before_t0`. These may be described as information that shifts estimated 10-day breach probability given the prefix. They may not be described as causes, as proof that complexity “makes” handlers slow, or as buttons operations should press without an intervention study.

**Process-state or congestion signal.** `queue_length_at_t0` (within snapshot tolerance) and prefix age as remaining slack on a fixed SLA clock. These describe system state at prediction time. They support capacity and triage hypotheses. They do not, by themselves, justify “reducing queue length will cut this claim’s breach probability by SHAP units.”

**Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review.** `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, and any socioeconomic or language correlates bundled with them. They can remain in a *predictive* model after disparate-impact, calibration-by-segment, and alternative-specification tests (replace region with repair-network coverage, language, and channel). They must not be presented as delay drivers or as reasons to treat a claimant differently. Actionable language, if any, should attach to the operational mediator (network coverage, staffing), not the protected or quasi-protected attribute.

**Drift/calendar proxy.** `calendar_week`, especially week 52 and weeks aligned with the policy change. Predictive validity can be real (holidays, freezes, batching). Causal/operational claims (“this week is badly run”) are not supported. Prefer explicit holiday and policy-regime indicators and monitor coefficient drift when the calendar rolls.

**Unusable until lineage is repaired.** `final_handler_team` (need team at `t0`), `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (need `created_at < t0`), `same_day_document_count_snapshot` (need `upload_ts ≤ t0`), `risk_score_current` (need scored-at timestamp), employee experience as-of extraction date, and any “current/last/final/total/anywhere” attribute. After repair, reclassify: team-at-`t0` and docs-at-`t0` can become process-state signals; a true pre-`t0` prior-claim count can become a defensible prior. They stay unusable as explanations until the repaired values are actually used to retrain and re-explain.

**Not repairable at this prediction time (drop).** `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total` (degenerate at this `t0`), `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`, `last_resource_role`, and all complete-trace sequence patterns above. A later prediction point (e.g., after first `Documents Validated` or after assessment) can revive prefix-gated versions of document-loop counts and `Appeal → Settlement Decision Recorded`.

Standards are distinct: **predictive validity** = available at `t0` and associated with the label under a leak-free fit; **causal interpretation** needs identification, not SHAP; **operational actionability** needs a lever the organization can change and an expected effect; **fairness review** is required whenever a retained predictor is a proxy for region, channel, language, or socioeconomic status even if it is temporally clean.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **Feature-store design (first).** Give every serving feature a `value`, `valid_from`, `valid_to`, and `computed_from_prefix_hash`. Ban names that imply the future (`final_*`, `*_anywhere`, `*_total` on the complete case, `last_*`, `*_current` without as-of). Persist `t0` as the first `Coverage Check Completed` (`timestamp`, `sequence_index`). Offline training must read the same point-in-time API as serving.

2. **Historical feature timestamps.** Backfill as-of snapshots for queue length, handler team, risk score, document counts, and employee experience from event time, not extraction date. Reject joins whose only grain is “latest warehouse row.” Add a CI test: randomly shift `extraction_date` and assert leak-free features do not change.

3. **Prefix-only recomputation.** Retrain after dropping the critical leaked set (`total_case_duration_hours`, appeal/final/variant/total-request/last-role/final-decision, complete-trace patterns). Repair SQL for prior claims and same-day documents, then recompute SHAP on the new model. Recalibrate (reliability diagrams, Brier/ECE by week and region). The old 0.82/0.71/0.64 scores are not to be patched by deleting rows from a leaked attribution list.

4. **Process-conformance tests in the explainer path.** Before an explanation is emitted, run the suite in §2: prefix equality, automaton enablement, tie handling, complete-trace detector, and repeated-settlement legality. Block publication if any cited activity is not observed in the prefix or is impossible. Keep `Appeal → Settlement Decision Recorded` available only for later prefixes where the appeal has already occurred.

5. **Explanation stability.** Bootstrap cases and permute same-timestamp events; require top-k features to be stable. Compare global rankings before vs after leakage removal—rankings that collapse when duration/appeal/variant disappear were never early-process explanations.

6. **Proxy and fairness analysis.** For `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, and reserve, report segment calibration, counterfactual replacement with operational mediators (repair-network coverage, language, staffing), and whether removing the proxy materially changes decisions. Keep them for prediction if they add leak-free lift; never use them in claimant-facing reasons.

7. **Monitoring for schema and process drift.** Watch DFG/Declare drift (new document loops, appeal rates, fraud-open timing), holiday/policy weeks, snapshot-latency of queue joins, and the rate of missing sequence indices. Alert if a feature’s prefix vs full-trace disagreement rate leaves zero, or if explanations start citing activities not enabled at the configured prediction point.

8. **Language policy.** Separate four sentences in every user-facing explanation: (i) what was known at `t0`; (ii) the association with breach probability; (iii) what is explicitly not claimed causally; (iv) what, if anything, is an operational lever. Predictive importance stays in (ii) and never migrates to (iii) or (iv) without a dedicated study.
