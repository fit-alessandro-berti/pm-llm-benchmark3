## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

I treat the dataset as an incomplete OCEL log in which some missing relations are genuinely missing and others are intentionally ambiguous. The repair should not force every object into a “complete” lifecycle.

**Hard invariants**

- **Referential integrity:** every added E2O relation must point to an existing event and object; every added O2O relation must point to two existing objects.
- **No invented entities:** no new events, objects, timestamps, or identifiers are introduced.
- **Directionality of O2O qualifiers:** `DEL-X carries PK-7` is not equivalent to `PK-7 carries DEL-X`.
- **No duplicate technical relations:** the same event–object–qualifier or object–object–qualifier triple should not be added twice.

**Strong defaults**

- `Create Order` normally relates to one Order.
- `Add Item` normally relates to one Order and one Item.
- `Pack Item` normally relates to one Item and one Package, but an item-only row can be a legitimate scanner outage.
- `Dispatch Package` normally relates to one Package and one Delivery, but a bulk dispatch may cover multiple packages.
- `Issue Invoice` normally relates to one Invoice and one or more Orders.
- `Record Payment` normally relates to exactly one Payment and usually one Invoice, but unapplied cash is possible.

**Weak heuristics**

- Identifier similarity: `scanner_ref=PK8-2` suggests `PK-8`; `external_ref=N-421?` suggests `DEL-Y`; `N-42*` suggests either `DEL-X` or `DEL-Y`.
- Timestamps: plausible ordering supports, but does not prove, lifecycle links.
- Customer and route: `C17` and `NORTH` make links plausible but not decisive.
- Amounts: `130 + 100 = 230` is evidence for invoice/payment hypotheses, not proof.
- Existing O2O structure: `DEL-X carries PK-7` independently supports a delivery path for `PK-7`, but absence of `DEL-Y carries PK-8` weakens any inferred delivery-package link.

**Evidence against a repair**

- An event already has a legitimate partial relation, such as `ev08` being an item-only pack row during a scanner outage.
- A relation is inferred only from another inferred relation, such as adding `PK-8 packs IT-B1` because `ev08` was linked to `PK-8`.
- An identifier contains ambiguity, such as `N-421?` or `N-42*`.
- Arithmetic matching is used as the only justification for invoice or payment links.
- Completing a lifecycle would require several speculative edges at once.

**Combination rule**

A single weak cue gives at most provisional status. Two independent cues, such as an event identifier plus an existing O2O relation, can raise confidence. Conflicting cues, such as mixed order hints or ambiguous carrier identifiers, keep the relation unresolved. I do not use an inferred O2O edge as independent confirmation of an inferred E2O edge.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

### Bundle A: conservative/minimum-change repair

Bundle A adds only the two event-object links with the clearest identifier cues and leaves object-to-object structure mostly untouched.

- **ev08 and PK-8:** add `ev08 packs PK-8` as provisional because `scanner_ref=PK8-2` is specific. Do not add `PK-8 packs IT-B1`; the event relation may reflect a scanner outage row, and the package-item O2O edge would be inferred only from the event.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** add `ev10 dispatches DEL-Y` as provisional because `N-421?` resembles `DEL-Y.carrier_ref=N-421`. Do not add `DEL-Y carries PK-8`, and do not link `ev10` to `DEL-X`.
- **ev11 and delivery objects:** leave `ev11` unresolved with respect to `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y`. The wildcard `N-42*` is compatible with both, and the portal may have recorded only the order.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** do not add `ev12 bills ORD-B` or `INV-44 bills ORD-B`. The amount match is suggestive, but invoice arithmetic is not proof.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** do not add `ev14 settles INV-44` or `PAY-2 settles INV-44`. PAY-2 may be unapplied cash, a separate payment, or a payment whose invoice link was not captured.

**Coherence:** Bundle A yields a plausible but intentionally incomplete lifecycle. ORD-A has creation, item addition, delivery confirmation, and invoice events. ORD-B has creation and item addition, plus a provisional pack event involving PK-8, but no confirmed package-item, delivery, invoice, or payment link. DEL-Y has a dispatch event but no carried-package relation. PAY-2 remains a payment object with one event. This is acceptable because the domain explicitly allows unresolved relations.

### Bundle B: consolidated lifecycle alternative

Bundle B interprets the log as a consolidated customer process: two orders, one package path, one delivery confirmation, one invoice, and two payments.

- **ev08 and PK-8:** add `ev08 packs PK-8` and also add `PK-8 packs IT-B1`, treating the item-only row as a scanner outage later reconciled. This creates a mixed-order package containing IT-A2 and IT-B1.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** add `ev10 dispatches DEL-Y` and `DEL-Y carries PK-8`. This completes a delivery path for PK-8.
- **ev11 and delivery objects:** add both `ev11 confirms DEL-X` and `ev11 confirms DEL-Y`, interpreting `N-42*` as a portal acknowledgement of multiple deliveries for customer C17.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** add `ev12 bills ORD-B` and `INV-44 bills ORD-B`, interpreting INV-44 as a consolidated invoice for ORD-A and ORD-B.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** add `ev14 settles INV-44` and `PAY-2 settles INV-44`, interpreting PAY-1 and PAY-2 as split payments against INV-44.

**Coherence:** Bundle B creates more complete lifecycles: ORD-A and ORD-B are both billed, both payments settle the invoice, PK-8 is packed, dispatched, and confirmed, and ORD-B’s item is linked to PK-8. However, the bundle relies heavily on inferred O2O edges and arithmetic cues. It is coherent only if the business process genuinely consolidates orders, packages, deliveries, and payments. If any one assumption is wrong, several downstream edges become false.

### Bundle C: optional single-confirmation variant

If one must assign `ev11` to a delivery, `DEL-X` is the less speculative choice because it is supported by an existing independent O2O chain: `ORD-A contains IT-A1`, `PK-7 packs IT-A1`, and `DEL-X carries PK-7`. `DEL-Y` would require the newly inferred `ev10` link and no observed carries relation. This still does not make `ev11` unique; it only explains why `DEL-X` is weaker to reject than `DEL-Y`.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

### Orders

**ORD-A** has a plausible lifecycle under Bundle A: `ev01` creates it, `ev03` and `ev04` add items, `ev11` confirms delivery, and `ev12` issues an invoice. Existing O2O edges support `contains` items and `bills` relationship. The delivery confirmation is not linked to a delivery object, but that is acceptable because the portal may have recorded only the order.

**ORD-B** has `ev02` and `ev05`, and under Bundle A also participates indirectly in `ev08` only through `IT-B1`, not through an order relation. It has no invoice, payment, or delivery confirmation. This is incomplete but not inconsistent. Adding ORD-B to INV-44 or PAY-2 would require accepting arithmetic and customer cues as stronger than they are.

### Items

**IT-A1** is coherent: added to ORD-A, packed into PK-7, and indirectly connected to DEL-X through O2O.

**IT-A2** is coherent: added to ORD-A, packed into PK-8, and dispatched in `ev10` if `ev10-DEL-Y` is accepted.

**IT-B1** is the weakest item lifecycle. It is added to ORD-B and appears in `ev08`. If `ev08-PK-8` is added, IT-B1 is associated with a package event, but no O2O `PK-8 packs IT-B1` is added. This avoids claiming that IT-B1 was physically packed into PK-8.

### Packages

**PK-7** is coherent: packs IT-A1, dispatched with DEL-X, and has an independent delivery O2O edge.

**PK-8** has `ev07`, `ev08` under Bundle A, and `ev10`. It already has `PK-8 packs IT-A2`. The suspicious part is that `ev08` introduces IT-B1 without a corresponding O2O edge. This is a one-to-many package-item possibility, but it is not confirmed. The package lifecycle is plausible if PK-8 is a reused or partially recorded package, but not if every pack event must imply a package-item O2O edge.

### Deliveries

**DEL-X** is coherent: `ev09` dispatches it and it carries PK-7.

**DEL-Y** under Bundle A has only `ev10`. It is not an orphan object because it is related to an event, but it lacks a carried-package O2O edge. This is acceptable. Under Bundle B, DEL-Y becomes more complete but also more dependent on inferred edges.

### Invoices

**INV-44** has `ev12`, `ev13`, bills ORD-A, and is settled by PAY-1. The invoice total does not match the observed billed order value, but that is allowed because fees, taxes, discounts, or missing order links are possible. Leaving ORD-B unresolved avoids overfitting to arithmetic.

### Payments

**PAY-1** is coherent: recorded in `ev13` and settles INV-44.

**PAY-2** has only `ev14`. Under Bundle A it is a payment object with one event and no invoice link. This may represent unapplied cash or a missing relation. It is not repaired merely to make the payment lifecycle complete.

### Circular reasoning

The main circularity risk is using an inferred E2O relation to justify an inferred O2O relation, then using that O2O relation to justify the E2O relation. For example, adding `PK-8 packs IT-B1` because `ev08` was linked to `PK-8`, and then citing `PK-8 packs IT-B1` as independent support for `ev08`, is circular. The same applies to `DEL-Y carries PK-8` from `ev10-DEL-Y`, and `PAY-2 settles INV-44` from `ev14-INV-44`. Bundle A avoids these circular confirmations by adding only E2O relations and leaving O2O relations unresolved.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **False mixed-order package:** adding `PK-8 packs IT-B1` solely because `ev08` was linked to `PK-8` may create a package that appears to contain items from ORD-A and ORD-B. If the scanner row was only an outage artifact, mining will show a consolidated package that never existed.

2. **False delivery-package lifecycle:** adding `DEL-Y carries PK-8` from `ev10-DEL-Y` can make PK-8 appear dispatched and confirmed. If the question-marked reference was a bulk dispatch or misentry, the delivery trace becomes artificially complete.

3. **Overconfident delivery confirmation:** assigning `ev11` to `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y` because of `N-42*` may create two confirmation edges from one ambiguous portal event. This can make Confirm Delivery appear to close both dispatches, even if the portal only acknowledged ORD-A.

4. **False consolidated invoice:** adding `INV-44 bills ORD-B` because `130 + 100 = 230` can merge ORD-B into the invoice lifecycle. If the invoice was only for ORD-A and the amount difference came from fees, taxes, or a discount, process mining will show a billing pattern that is not real.

5. **False split-payment settlement:** adding `PAY-2 settles INV-44` because PAY-1 and PAY-2 sum to the invoice total can remove the possibility of unapplied cash. This creates a clean payment-to-invoice edge and may make the payment process look more deterministic than it is.

6. **Circular confidence inflation:** using an inferred E2O relation to justify an inferred O2O relation, then using the O2O relation as independent support, makes a speculative repair look stable. This is especially dangerous for `PK-8 packs IT-B1` and `DEL-Y carries PK-8`.

7. **Lifecycle-completion bias:** forcing every object to have a start, middle, and end can suppress exception behavior. PAY-2 without an invoice, ORD-B without a delivery, and DEL-Y without a carried package may be legitimate unresolved records rather than defects.
