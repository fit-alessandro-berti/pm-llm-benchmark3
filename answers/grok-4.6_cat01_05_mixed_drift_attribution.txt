## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The March collapse in `raw_model_fitness` (0.93 to 0.55) with near-stable `mapped_model_fitness` (0.93 to 0.90) is semantic and provenance drift, not a sudden rewrite of the business process. Mapping `Eligibility Screen` onto `Validate Eligibility` and dropping duplicated `Open Case` events restores alignment or token-replay fitness because those two artifacts are exactly the deviations the old normative model cannot parse. A 0.37-point raw drop while cycle time, rework, and mapped DFG rates stay near January is the opposite of a control-flow shock.

Recovered mapped fitness does not prove the business process was unchanged. It only shows that, after neutralizing labels and technical duplicates, remaining traces still fit the intended skeleton almost as well as in January. Residual mapped-fitness loss of about 0.02–0.06 after March is the portion mapping cannot explain; that gap later widens with rework and `Senior Assessment`.

Duplicated `Open Case` events inflate DFG frequencies, create a spurious start activity, and split variants. They bias discovery and raw conformance more than cycle time if the extra event is near-instantaneous. Missing resource identifiers disable organizational mining, handover-of-work, and capacity reconstruction; they do not by themselves move fitness or DFG topology. Because missingness is concentrated in migrated activities, post-March resource KPIs are not missing-at-random.

Post-May DFG and performance patterns cannot be logging drift. After the same mapping, `Assess→Request Info` doubles (12 to 24 per 100 cases), Supplier Z jumps from 18% to 43%, `rework_variant_pct` doubles, and both median and p90 rise while recorded `Assess` service barely moves. Those quantities come from operational tables and survive label normalization. A rename cannot produce a 25-point supplier-mix shift or the 5 May incomplete-bundle disruption.

### 3.5 One global change point versus many

The data do not support one global change point. March is almost pure logging. May is mix plus rework. Mid-June is capacity. July is a policy that aggregates conceal. October is a known seasonal recurrence. Mapped fitness never collapses the way raw fitness does, and cycle time does not move when raw fitness does. A single CPD on a blended KPI would smear these mechanisms together.

Primary monitoring population: **L0 mapped, de-duplicated cases**. That is the only grain that stays comparable across the March label break, keeps enough volume for stable monthly statistics, and still allows L1 slices as diagnostics rather than as the headline KPI. Raw-label L0 should be retained solely for schema and conformance-gap monitors.

Seasonality: do not use one universal threshold. Pair October–December monitors to a same-month reference (prior year once loaded; until then use an explicitly wider in-season band, not the January band). Run off-season monitors against Jan–Feb plus an 8-week roll.

Multiple testing: about a dozen weekly monitors will produce chance breaches. Pre-declare the family; require two consecutive weeks or two corroborating artifacts (for example rework rate and the request-info edge) before a page; keep schema/label alerts on a shorter fuse because they are cheap to dismiss. Review the full family monthly rather than treating every single breach as a new root cause.

def validate_monthly(monthly: pd.DataFrame, dfg: pd.DataFrame) -> None:
    pct_cols = [
        "high_complexity_pct",

    ).all().all()

    assert (monthly["mapped_model_fitness"] + 1e-9 >= monthly["raw_model_fitness"]).all()
    rate_cols = [c for c in dfg.columns if c != "month"]
    assert dfg[rate_cols].apply(lambda s: s.between(0, 150)).all().all()
    print("VALIDATION: monthly summary and DFG rates passed range/uniqueness checks.")


def baseline_relative_changes(monthly: pd.DataFrame, baseline_months: Sequence[str]) -> pd.DataFrame:
    base = monthly[monthly["month"].isin(baseline_months)]
    ref = base.mean(numeric_only=True)
    out = monthly.copy()
    level_cols = ["cases", "median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h"]
    pp_cols = [
        "high_complexity_pct",
        "supplier_z_pct",
        "fast_variant_pct",
        "rework_variant_pct",
        "raw_model_fitness",
        "mapped_model_fitness",
        "missing_resource_pct",
        "duplicate_open_case_pct",
    ]
    for c in level_cols:
        out[f"d_{c}"] = out[c] - ref[c]
        out[f"d_{c}_pct"] = 100.0 * (out[c] / ref[c] - 1.0)
    for c in pp_cols:
        out[f"d_{c}"] = out[c] - ref[c]
    return out


def dfg_l1(a: pd.Series, b: pd.Series, cols: Sequence[str]) -> Tuple[float, float]:
    """City-block distance and MAD on the comparable (non-constant) edges."""
    l1 = float((a[list(cols)] - b[list(cols)]).abs().sum())
    return l1, l1 / len(cols)


def fitness_recovery(monthly: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    x = monthly[["month", "raw_model_fitness", "mapped_model_fitness"]].copy()
    x["gap"] = x["mapped_model_fitness"] - x["raw_model_fitness"]
    x["recovered_share_of_jan_loss"] = x["gap"] / (0.925 - x["raw_model_fitness"]).replace(0, pd.NA)
    return x


def flag_change_points(monthly: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Explainable rule, not a formal CPD.

    Flag a month if any key metric moves more than the practical threshold
    versus the previous month. Thresholds are larger than Jan-Feb noise
    (~1 pp, ~1 h, ~0.01 fitness) but not so large they miss May/October.
    """
    rules = {
        "raw_model_fitness": 0.08,
        "mapped_model_fitness": 0.03,
        "rework_variant_pct": 5.0,
        "supplier_z_pct": 8.0,
        "high_complexity_pct": 8.0,
        "median_cycle_h": 3.0,
        "p90_cycle_h": 8.0,
        "missing_resource_pct": 2.0,
        "distinct_activity_labels": 1.5,
        "duplicate_open_case_pct": 2.0,
    }
    prev = monthly.shift(1)
    flags = []
    for i, row in monthly.iterrows():
        if i == 0:
            flags.append({"month": row["month"], "flags": "baseline", "detail": ""})
            continue
        hits = []
        for col, thr in rules.items():
            delta = row[col] - prev.loc[i, col]
            if abs(delta) >= thr:
                hits.append(f"{col} {delta:+.2f}")
        flags.append(
            {
                "month": row["month"],
                "flags": "; ".join(hits) if hits else "no_break",
                "detail": "mixed/gradual" if len(hits) == 1 else ("multi-artifact" if hits else ""),
            }
        )
    return pd.DataFrame(flags)

def format_event_log(df: pd.DataFrame, case_id: str, activity: str, timestamp: str):
    """Format a dataframe for PM4Py without mutating the caller's frame."""
    import pm4py

    formatted = pm4py.format_dataframe(

def slice_window(log, start: str, end: str, mode: str = "traces_intersecting"):
    """Time-slice with pm4py.filter_time_range.

    mode: 'events' | 'traces_contained' | 'traces_intersecting'
    Use traces_intersecting for monthly operational views; traces_contained
    when cycle-time completeness matters.
    """
    import pm4py

    return pm4py.filter_time_range(log, start, end, mode=mode)


def monthly_dfgs(log, months: Iterable[str]) -> Dict[str, Any]:
    """Discover a DFG per calendar month."""
    import pm4py

    out = {}
    for month in months:
        start = f"{month}-01 00:00:00"
        end_ts = pd.Timestamp(month) + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1)
        end = end_ts.strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S")
        window = slice_window(log, start, end, mode="traces_intersecting")
        dfg, start_act, end_act = pm4py.discover_dfg(window)
        out[month] = {"dfg": dfg, "start": start_act, "end": end_act}
    return out


def monthly_variants(log, months: Iterable[str]) -> Dict[str, Any]:
    import pm4py

    out = {}
    for month in months:
        start = f"{month}-01 00:00:00"
        end = (pd.Timestamp(month) + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1)).strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S")
        window = slice_window(log, start, end, mode="traces_intersecting")
        out[month] = pm4py.get_variants(window)
    return out

def optional_constraint_models(log) -> Dict[str, Any]:
    """Log skeleton and DECLARE; both are optional and can be expensive."""
    import pm4py

    models: Dict[str, Any] = {}
    try:
        models["log_skeleton"] = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(log)
    except Exception as exc:  # pragma: no cover - defensive around optional APIs
        models["log_skeleton_error"] = repr(exc)
    try:
        models["declare"] = pm4py.discover_declare(log)
    except Exception as exc:  # pragma: no cover
        models["declare_error"] = repr(exc)
    return models


def raw_and_normalized_views(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    case_id: str = "case:concept:name",
    activity: str = "concept:name",
    timestamp: str = "time:timestamp",
    label_map: Optional[Mapping[str, str]] = None,
    drop_duplicate_open_case: bool = True,
) -> Dict[str, Any]:
    """Build raw and mapped logs without overwriting the original dataframe.

    Mapping is a copy-on-write replace. Duplicate Open Case events are removed
    only on the normalized view, and only when they are consecutive technical
    extras (same case, identical timestamp or zero-duration repeat).
    """
    import pm4py

    label_map = dict(label_map or LABEL_MAP)
    raw_df = format_event_log(df, case_id, activity, timestamp)
    mapped_df = raw_df.copy()
    act_col = "concept:name"
    mapped_df[act_col] = mapped_df[act_col].replace(label_map)

    if drop_duplicate_open_case:
        mapped_df = mapped_df.sort_values([case_id, timestamp])
        is_open = mapped_df[act_col].eq("Open Case")
        prev_open = is_open.shift(1, fill_value=False)
        same_case = mapped_df[case_id].eq(mapped_df[case_id].shift(1))
        mapped_df = mapped_df.loc[~(is_open & prev_open & same_case)].copy()

    raw_log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(raw_df)
    mapped_log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(mapped_df)
    return {
        "source_df": df,
        "raw_df": raw_df,
        "mapped_df": mapped_df,
        "raw_log": raw_log,
        "mapped_log": mapped_log,
        "label_map": label_map,
    }


def compare_raw_versus_mapped(views: Mapping[str, Any], model=None) -> Dict[str, Any]:
    """Conformance on the same normative model for raw and mapped logs."""
    import pm4py

    raw_log, mapped_log = views["raw_log"], views["mapped_log"]
    if model is None:
        # Discover from mapped log so the reference is semantically aligned.
        model = pm4py.discover_petri_net_inductive(mapped_log)
    net, im, fm = model if isinstance(model, tuple) else (model, None, None)

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest conclusion is that March is logging and semantic drift from the migration, not a business-process rewrite. Raw fitness collapses, two new labels appear, `Open Case` duplicates and resource missingness jump, and after mapping the DFG fragment is almost the January process (L1 = 3) while cycle time does not move. That package is hard to explain any other way.

The next-strongest conclusion is that May begins a real control-flow and mix shift driven by Supplier Z’s incomplete bundles. The supplier share, the request-info edge, rework variants, and both cycle-time quantiles move together in the same month as the documented disruption, and they partially reverse when Z reports recovery. Because Z already served a more complex, non-random slice, part of the May rework is population drift; the 25-point share jump and the within-calendar coincidence still make a supplier quality shock the main May mechanism.

Summer delay is mostly queueing, not slower assessors. Recorded `Assess` service rises only about 0.6 h while median cycle time rises about 13 h by July and p90 about 36–42 h by August. The mid-June attrition, unreplaced capacity, and leftover rework load are jointly sufficient. Attribution between “fewer assessors” and “more rework per assessor” is mediated: dirty bundles raise offered load, so staffing and supplier mechanisms share the wait. August’s still-rising p90 while Z’s share is already falling is the cleanest clue that capacity, not only Z, is binding.

The 1 July priority rule is plausible and important, but not identified in these aggregates. It should redistribute wait from high-complexity to standard cases; the monthly median can rise even if the intended customers get faster. Treat this as a predicted hidden effect until class-conditional waits exist.

October–November are primarily recurring case-mix and volume drift from renewal season, sitting on the thin bench. The `Senior Assessment` surge is a blend of real HC routing and historically inconsistent logging; it should not be read as a new process invented in October. December’s improvement is mixed: seasonal load falls and overtime plus a quality campaign start on 5 December. One monthly row cannot say how much each contributed.

What would revise this account: class-conditional waits that do not fan out after 1 July; a within-Z rework rate that is flat in May; prior-year October that does not show a similar HC and volume jump; or mapped DFG L1 remaining near 3 after May (which would push May back toward logging).

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. `IF raw-model fitness falls by ≥0.08 while mapped-model fitness stays within 0.03 of its baseline and median cycle time moves <3 h, THEN treat the break as a label/schema incident and freeze behavioral root-cause work until a mapping is refreshed, BECAUSE that is the March migration signature: semantic and provenance drift without a change in the business skeleton.`

2. `IF Supplier Z share rises ≥8 pp and the mapped Assess→Request Info rate or rework-variant share rises ≥6 pp inside the Z slice, THEN open a document-bundle quality action with Supplier Z and measure rework on non-Z cases in parallel, BECAUSE incomplete bundles create real extra Collect Documents / Assess loops that also congest the shared assessor queue.`

3. `IF reconstructed wait before Assess rises ≥4 h (median) or ≥10 h (p90) while Assess service time rises <1.5 h, THEN treat the incident as capacity/queueing and check rostered assessor-hours against arrivals, BECAUSE summer deterioration was wait, not slower assessment work.`

4. `IF after a priority-rule change high-complexity wait falls (or rises much less) while standard-case p90 wait rises ≥8 h, THEN review the rule’s SLA impact on the non-priority majority rather than celebrating the HC improvement, BECAUSE the 1 July policy is designed to redistribute delay and monthly totals will hide that transfer.`

5. `IF October–November volume and high-complexity share move together into the pre-declared seasonal band and within-class DFGs stay near their September shape, THEN scale capacity and report mix-adjusted cycle time instead of declaring a new process, BECAUSE renewal season is recurring population drift sitting on whatever bench is left.`

6. `IF mapped fitness, request-info rate, or senior-path rate remain materially above the Jan–Feb baseline after Supplier Z share, seasonal HC share, and known interventions have reverted, THEN investigate persistent behavioral adaptation (new informal checks or habitual senior routing), BECAUSE leftover deviation after mix and logging are stripped is the only clean signature of a new habit.`

7. `IF December-style improvement appears in the same weeks as both falling seasonal load and an overtime/quality campaign, THEN do not retire rework or queue monitors until the same metrics hold in an off-season week without extra overtime, BECAUSE colliding seasonality and interventions make a single recovery month non-identifying, and a true new baseline has not been demonstrated.`
