What it does not imply: That she is unimportant. Working-together networks under-detect brokers, dispatchers, and coordinators who perform many short handoffs. Her low co-occurrence is consistent with someone who touches a case briefly, arranges the next step, and then disappears until the next exception.

This is the main reconciliation: she is central in flow networks but peripheral in co-presence networks because her role is to coordinate movement, not to perform the surrounding work.

**Subcontracting network**  
The subcontracting network shows strong Mira-centered loops:
- Mira  Omar_Medical  Mira: 126 times.
- Mira  Priya_Legal  Mira: 98 times.
- Bot_Rules  Mira  Bot_Rules: 62 times.

This pattern means Mira sends work to a specialist or system and then receives the case back. It is consistent with specialist scheduling, exception routing, and rework management.

What this implies: Mira is acting as an orchestrator. She is not simply passing cases away; she often resumes control after the specialist step.

What it does not imply: That these loops are necessarily efficient or necessary. Some loops may indicate unnecessary rework or poor first-time routing.

**Performance/time statistics**  
Mira_Coordinator’s median service time per event is only 4.2 minutes, much lower than adjusters and specialists. She touches 22% of claims, but 64% of reopened claims and 71% of complaints. Her cases show high exception-flag prevalence: 48% of her cases have exception_flag=True.

During a six-day absence of Mira_Coordinator, ordinary low-value claims did not slow down, but exception_flag=True cases did. The 90th percentile cycle time for exception cases rose from 8.4 to 13.9 days.

This supports an interpretation that Mira is not a long-duration processor, but a critical gate for the exception-heavy tail of work. It also suggests that the delay is concentrated in exceptional cases, not the standard automated flow.

**Role-discovery or activity-profile similarity**  
Using PM4Py’s `pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` or `pm4py.discover_organizational_roles`, Mira would likely be grouped with coordinators, team leads, or possibly routing/administrative roles because her activity profile consists of Request Missing Information, Reopen Claim, Escalate Complaint, and Assign Specialist.

However, role discovery based only on activity labels may understate her discretion. Her exception-flag share and handover centrality distinguish her from ordinary administrative staff. Activity-profile similarity should be combined with case-level filtering, time statistics, and network metrics.

Automated and vendor resources matter here. Bot_Rules has high handover betweenness and creates much of the routing volume into Mira. Vendor_Verify and Vendor_Pay have very long service times, so not all cycle-time degradation around exceptional cases can be attributed to Mira.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

## Hypothesis A: Formal coordinator / exception desk

**Mechanism**  
Mira is the designated human resource for manual exception triage. Bot_Rules routes cases it cannot classify or handle to her. She decides whether the case needs missing information, medical review, legal review, reassignment, escalation, or reopening.

**Evidence supporting it**
- Bot_Rules  Mira_Coordinator is the largest manual routing edge from automation.
- Mira then hands to medical, legal, adjusters, and temps.
- She touches 22% of claims but 64% of reopened claims and 71% of complaints.
- Her median service time is only 4.2 minutes, consistent with routing rather than deep processing.
- Ordinary claims did not degrade during her absence, but exception cases did.

**Evidence contradicting or limiting it**
- Her working-together co-occurrence with team leads is very low, which is unusual for a formal coordinator embedded in daily team operations.
- Some cases routed directly from Alice/Bruno to adjusters bypass Mira, so she is not the only coordinator.
- The role may be partly informal or system-created rather than formally documented.

**Observable predictions in future event logs**
- A future log after role formalization should show clear assignment decisions, less variation in routing outcomes, and another resource covering the same function during absence without major exception-cycle-time degradation.

## Hypothesis B: Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency

**Mechanism**  
The process has become dependent on one person to clear exception queues. When Mira is unavailable, work accumulates because no one else has the knowledge or authority to route difficult cases.

**Evidence supporting it**
- Highest handover out-degree, in-degree, and betweenness.
- High subcontracting betweenness and repeated Mira  specialist  Mira loops.
- During her 6-day absence, the 90th percentile cycle time for exception_flag=True cases rose from 8.4 to 13.9 days.
- Her cases have high cycle-time variance.
- Multiple resources return cases to Mira, suggesting she is the re-entry point for unresolved work.

**Evidence contradicting or limiting it**
- Ordinary low-value claims were unaffected, so she is not a global bottleneck.
- Her median service time is short, so her own processing speed is probably not the limiting factor.
- The bottleneck may be caused by the routing design, not by Mira’s capacity.
- Some delays may occur in specialist or vendor queues before or after her involvement.

**Observable predictions**
- Longer absence or increased exception volume would produce nonlinear queue growth.
- Backlog indicators around Mira’s tasks would predict future cycle-time degradation for exception cases.
- If another trained resource handles the same queue, the effect would mostly disappear.

## Hypothesis C: Organizational boundary spanner / triage broker

**Mechanism**  
Mira bridges different organizational worlds: automation, temporary staff, team leads, adjusters, medical/legal specialists, and exceptional complaint/reopen paths. She converts ambiguous signals into next-step assignments.

**Evidence supporting it**
- High betweenness in handover and subcontracting networks.
- Low working-together co-occurrence is actually consistent with a broker role: she moves between groups without being embedded in one.
- Qualitative evidence: adjusters say she is “the person who knows where hard cases should go.”
- She handles complaints and reopened claims disproportionately.

**Evidence contradicting or limiting it**
- True boundary spanning often involves stronger relationship-based collaboration, but her working-together scores are low.
- She may be a procedural routing node rather than an interpersonal boundary spanner.
- Her influence may come from the system assigning her broker status rather than from social legitimacy.

**Observable predictions**
- If removed or replaced, specialist matching quality would decline and reassignment rates would increase.
- Communication logs, interviews, or surveys outside the event log might show strong informal ties even though working-together co-occurrence is low.
- Network brokerage metrics would remain high even after controlling for case volume.

## Hypothesis D: Queue cleaner / automation exception handler

**Mechanism**  
Bot_Rules creates a manual exception queue. Mira is the human “queue cleaner” who processes those exceptions. Her centrality is partly an artifact of the system routing design.

**Evidence supporting it**
- Process owner says she is “not a real team lead, just a queue cleaner.”
- Very short service times.
- Low working-together co-occurrence.
- High handover centrality with many short routing events.

**Evidence contradicting or limiting it**
- The role is not purely mechanical: selecting between medical, legal, adjuster, or complaint path requires judgment.
- Her absence created significant exception-case delay, so the queue-cleaning function is operationally important.
- She is disproportionately present in reopened and complaint cases, which are not usually simple queue-cleaning work.

**Observable predictions**
- Many of Mira’s decisions may follow repeatable rules and could be partially automated.
- However, if the decisions require tacit knowledge, an automation pilot would show higher reassignment or rework rates.
- Future logs with decision categories would reveal how much variation exists in her routing choices.

## Hypothesis E: Specialist scheduler / quality gatekeeper

**Mechanism**  
Mira controls access to scarce specialist resources. She ensures Omar_Medical and Priya_Legal see only appropriate cases, and she sequences work to specialist availability.

**Evidence supporting it**
- Subcontracting loops with Omar and Priya are frequent.
- Mira sits at the entry and exit of specialist work.
- High exception-flag share suggests she is screening or gating complex work.

**Evidence contradicting or limiting it**
- There is no direct evidence of formal quality criteria or specialist-capacity awareness in the event log.
- Her low working-together score with Omar and Priya suggests she is not jointly reviewing cases; she is only dispatching and receiving them.
- If she were a true quality gatekeeper, we might expect longer service times or more documentation-related events.

**Observable predictions**
- If access to specialists is via Mira, removing her would increase specialist overload or inappropriate referrals.
- Analysis of specialist timestamps might show lower idle/waiting time before specialist cases because Mira manages the queue.

# 3. Risk assessment

## Bottleneck risk

Mira is a bottleneck for exception-heavy work because she is the central routing point. The absence-period evidence supports this: exception cases slowed dramatically. However, this does not mean she is overloaded in all cases. Ordinary low-value claims were unaffected.

The risk is less about Mira’s personal speed and more about the process having a single human funnel for exceptional work. Delays may also be caused by specialists or vendors, but Mira is the visible constraint because work waits to be assigned.

## Coordination risk

Low working-together co-occurrence can indicate fragmented coordination. Mira routes cases but does not work jointly with adjusters or team leads. This can lead to information loss between triage and execution.

However, a broker role naturally has low co-presence. The low co-occurrence is not by itself evidence of poor coordination. The risk is that if Mira’s routing is not well documented, coordination quality depends on her individual memory.

## Key-person risk

This is clearly elevated. During a six-day absence, exception-case 90th percentile cycle time rose from 8.4 to 13.9 days. That is strong operational evidence of dependency. If Mira leaves, the process loses more than labor capacity; it loses routing knowledge.

This risk is worsened if routing rules are tacit and not recorded in the event log. The event log shows what happened, but not why a case went to medical, legal, or a specific adjuster.

## Compliance/control risk

A compliance reviewer’s concern is valid: Mira appears to both detect problems and select the next specialist. This creates possible uncontrolled discretion. The event log alone does not show improper behavior, but it does show a concentration of exception-handling decisions in one resource.

Calibrated wording: there is no direct evidence of wrongdoing. The risk is procedural. If Mira is empowered to route cases to different specialists without recorded criteria, it may be hard to audit fairness, appropriateness, or consistency.

## Workload fairness risk

Mira’s workload is skewed toward exceptions, complaints, and reopens. This is probably more cognitively demanding than the median service time suggests. Temps have low exception-flag shares and low integration. The risk is that difficult case routing is concentrated in one person while routine work is more evenly distributed.

However, fairness cannot be assessed from event counts alone. Service-time distributions, case complexity, and external vendor waits need to be included.

## Data-quality risk

Mira’s centrality may be partly a data artifact. Short “Assign Specialist,” “Reopen,” or “Escalate Complaint” events may be recorded as Mira’s activities even when the actual decision is system-generated or later corrected. If lifecycle transitions are not cleaned, some events may be misattributed or incomplete.

Vendor resources also distort cycle time. Vendor_Verify has a median service time of 2.1 hours and Vendor_Pay 1.3 hours. Long waiting times at vendors can make Mira’s queue look like the problem when the external provider is the real delay.

# 5. Recommendation

## What should be changed immediately

- **Create a documented exception-routing playbook.** Record decision rules for sending cases to medical, legal, adjusters, temps, or back to automation.
- **Train at least one or two backup coordinators.** They should shadow Mira and have access to the same routing rules, so exception queues do not depend on a single person.
- **Implement queue monitoring around Mira’s tasks.** Track incoming handover volume, exceptional backlog, and waiting time from Bot_Rules to Mira’s first action.
- **Record routing decisions explicitly.** Introduce a decision field or note explaining why a case goes to Omar, Priya, or a specific adjuster. This supports auditing and risk management.

## What should be monitored

- Exception-case 90th percentile cycle time, especially during absences or volume spikes.
- Waiting time before and after Mira’s involvement.
- Reopen and complaint rates after Mira’s assignments.
- Specialist utilization and rework rates after referral.
- Handover and subcontracting network metrics over time, to see if another key-person dependency is forming.
- Service/waiting-time decomposition for Vendor_Verify and Vendor_Pay, because part of the cycle-time increase may be external.

## What should not be changed until more evidence is available

- **Do not remove Mira_Coordinator.** The evidence shows she provides a critical exception-coordination function. If removal were considered, the replacement would need to preserve specialist scheduling, complaint routing, and missing-information rework.
- **Do not fully automate her role.** Some routing may be rule-based, but complex complaint and exception decisions may require tacit judgment.
- **Do not interpret high centrality as automatically negative or positive.** Her centrality could be good orchestration, bottleneck risk, or a routing artifact. Further filtered analysis is needed before restructuring.
- **Do not redesign team structures based only on SNA metrics.** The working-together network under-represents lightweight coordinators. Combine SNA with service/waiting times, case outcomes, and interviews.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira_Coordinator appears to be the process’s exception triage point. She touches about 22% of claims but handles 64% of reopened claims and 71% of complaints. Most of her events are short assignments or routing, which is why she looks central in handover and subcontracting charts but not in working-together charts. During her six-day absence, normal low-value claims were unaffected, but exception cases slowed sharply, showing the process depends on her as a coordination function. The evidence supports multiple interpretations: formal coordinator, hidden key-person dependency, boundary spanner, or queue cleaner. We should not remove or automate her role yet. Instead, management should document routing rules, train backup staff, monitor exception queues and handover delays, and audit decision records. This would reduce key-person and compliance risks while preserving the specialist routing function she currently provides.
