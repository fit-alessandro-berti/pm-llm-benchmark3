# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

**Automated Intake (R14_Nora).** Core: A1/A2 only, and only as a precheck. It has *no* peripheral activities and, critically, *no authority* — it cannot diagnose, negotiate, or close. Complexity is trivially low because it only touches the simplest, machine-decidable cases. Substitution is irrelevant (it is a system). The design lesson is that its A1/A2 volume is a throughput artifact, not seniority.

**Front-Office Generalists (Amira, Ben).** Core: A2 Validate, A4 Standard Response, A12 Close. Peripheral: light A3 Diagnose, occasional A8 Negotiate, and for Amira a small A7 tail (18). Complexity is low–medium; Amira leans slightly more medium/high than Ben. Authority is limited to routine resolution; escalation-worthy work is handed off. They are not substitutes for specialists despite touching many activity columns.

**Temp Pool (Kai, Lina).** Core: A1/A2/A4/A12 on very-low-complexity cases only. Peripheral: essentially none. No certifications, <0.5y tenure, and zero high/critical cases. The weeks 8–10 evidence — 17 reopens traced to incomplete Validate notes — shows their *core* activity (A2) is a latent quality risk, so "core" here means "high volume but low reliability," not "competent ownership."

**Technical Analysts (Chen, Dina).** Core: A3 Diagnose, A5 Configure, A10 Implement. Peripheral: A11 Quality (light), A14 KB, and for Chen some A6-adjacent involvement. Chen's complexity (0.71, 42 critical) and security-adjacency make his *effective* role broader than Dina's (0.48). They share a profile but not a complexity tier; authority over technical design is real but excludes approval/security sign-off.

**Quality & Knowledge (Ivan, Julia).** Core: A11 Quality Check and A14 KB Update — A11 is an organizational *control gate*, not just a task. Peripheral: light A5/A10, some A13. Medium complexity. Their authority is a checkpoint authority (accept/reject work), which is qualitatively different from execution volume. Late-shift Ivan vs. day-shift Julia gives coverage rather than differing competence.

**Commercial Specialist (Fatima).** Core: A7 Pricing Exception Review, A8 Customer Negotiation. Peripheral: small A9 (16), incidental A2/A3. Medium–high complexity. She holds pricing decision authority; her low totals vs. front office understate her importance. During her absence pricing duration nearly doubled (38→74 min), confirming she is the true competence center.

**Risk/Security Specialist (Erik).** Core: A6 Security Review — essentially his only real column. Peripheral: tiny A3/A5/A10/A14. High–critical complexity (0.86). Low frequency, high stakes: he is a rare-but-critical control whose value is measured in avoided risk, not throughput. Substitution is dangerous (see §3).

**Supervisor (Hana).** Core: A9 Supervisor Approval. Peripheral: oversight touches on A8/A11/A13. High–critical complexity. Authority is the defining feature: in week 5, 13 of Mateo's 21 preliminary approvals were rechecked by her, proving A9 is a decision right that does not transfer with the activity.

**Incident Coordinator (Mateo).** Core: coordination/routing — he "owns" no activity but appears across A6/A9/A13 and in the reopen/escalation fragments. Peripheral: everything, lightly. High–critical complexity because he touches the hard cases. His authority is *procedural* (routing/filtering) not *decisional*; weeks 7–8 show he can shape Erik's workload by filtering low-risk referrals — beneficial, but also a single point of triage risk.

**Commercial shadow (Giorgio).** Core (formal): Senior Service Agent front-office work (A2/A4/A12). Core (shadow): A7/A8 commercial review that is not his formal title. Peripheral: A9 (20), light technical. He is discussed further in §3.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

**R07_Giorgio — commercial shadow role.** Giorgio's formal title is Senior Service Agent, yet he performs substantial A7 (72) and A8 (88), and formally covered Fatima in weeks 3–4 (31 A7 + 18 A8). His pricing certification makes this legitimate, but it is a *shadow* commercial capability layered on a front-office job. Benefits: resilience (pricing work continues when Fatima is out), faster front-office-to-commercial handoffs, and mentoring capacity. Risks: during his substitution pricing duration nearly doubled (38→74 min), so his throughput/quality at the specialist task is lower; the arrangement is invisible on an org chart, which hides key-person and audit risk. Do **not** treat his substitution episodes as proof of permanent commercial competence (constraint respected).

**R13_Mateo — cross-functional escalation broker (bridging).** Mateo owns no activity but has small counts in nearly every column and sits at the routing point of the reopen/escalation fragments (3 & 5). He handles the most complex mix (0.77 median, 76 critical) yet lacks approval authority: 13 of 21 preliminary approvals in week 5 were overturned by Hana. In weeks 7–8 his filtering of low-risk security referrals reduced false escalations (benefit) but coincided with two critical cases waiting >2 days (risk). He is a bridge, not a decision-maker; his value is coordination, and his danger is being a single triage bottleneck whose filtering can starve the true specialist.

**R05_Erik — rare but critical specialist.** Only ~96 A6 events, so any frequency method underweights him, yet he is the sole real owner of Security Review and works the highest-risk cases (0.86 median, 55 critical). He is a low-volume, high-consequence control. Substitution is especially unsafe: when Mateo pre-filtered his queue, throughput of *complaints* improved but two critical cases breached a 2-day wait — evidence that his function cannot be safely proxied by a router.

**Additional shadow/boundary roles:**

- **Automation boundary role (R14_Nora).** Nora defines the human/system boundary: she front-loads A1/A2 for simple cases, which inflates human "Register/Validate" comparisons and can mask which humans actually validate complex cases. Treating her counts as a role would distort the entire hierarchy.
- **Temporary-workforce role (R11_Kai, R12_Lina).** A seasonal-capacity shadow of the Tier-1 generalists: same activity columns, but no certification and a documented downstream defect (17 reopens from weak Validate notes). They provide surge capacity (benefit) at the cost of latent rework (risk).
- **Quality gatekeeping role (R09_Ivan, R10_Julia).** A11 is a control gate rather than production work; recognizing it as gatekeeping (with authority to reject) rather than "just another activity" is essential to reading the process correctly.

Shadow roles here are not improper — they add resilience and flexibility — but each carries a specific, nameable risk (throughput loss, hidden key-person dependence, triage starvation, latent rework).

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# ---- 1. Load and format ----
df = pd.read_csv("order_to_resolution_log.csv")

log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    # org:resource, case_complexity, temporary_substitution, escalation_level,
    # customer_segment, product_line are preserved as extra columns
)

# ---- 2. Organizational role discovery (activity-based) ----
# Returns groups of (activities, {resource: count}) — an ACTIVITY grouping,
# NOT an authority hierarchy. Treat as a hypothesis, not ground truth.
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log)

role_rows = []
for i, role in enumerate(roles):
    for resource, freq in role.originator_importance.items():
        role_rows.append({
            "role_id": i,
            "activities": tuple(role.activities),
            "org:resource": resource,
            "activity_freq": freq,
        })
roles_df = pd.DataFrame(role_rows)

# ---- 3. Activity-based resource similarity ----
# Similar activity profile != similar authority or risk. Keep as a separate lens.
sim = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log)
sim_df = pd.DataFrame(
    [(a, b, s) for (a, b), s in sim.items()],
    columns=["res_a", "res_b", "similarity"],
)

# ---- 4. Social/handover networks ----
handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log)   # who passes work to whom
together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log)    # who co-works on a case
subc     = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(log)      # A does work "inside" B's case span

# pm4py.view_sna(handover) / pm4py.view_sna(together)  # for visual inspection

def sna_to_df(sna, label):
    rows = [(src, dst, w) for (src, dst), w in sna.connections.items()]
    return pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["source", "target", label])

handover_df = sna_to_df(handover, "handover_w")
together_df = sna_to_df(together, "together_w")

# ---- 5. Enrich resources with complexity + substitution context ----
# Case-level attributes must be reduced to the resource level explicitly,
# otherwise activity frequency silently dominates the interpretation.
per_resource = (
    log.groupby("org:resource")
       .agg(
           n_events=("concept:name", "size"),
           median_complexity=("case_complexity", "median"),
           n_critical=("case_complexity", lambda s: (s >= 0.80).sum()),
           subst_events=("temporary_substitution",
                         lambda s: (s.astype(str) == "True").sum()),
           max_escalation=("escalation_level", "max"),
       )
       .reset_index()
)

# Join the activity-frequency roles with real risk/authority signals.
roles_enriched = roles_df.merge(per_resource, on="org:resource", how="left")

# ---- 6. Guardrails against over-interpretation ----
# (a) Flag resources that look identical by similarity but differ on complexity/risk.
def divergent_pairs(sim_df, per_resource, sim_thresh=0.90, cx_gap=0.15):
    cx = per_resource.set_index("org:resource")["median_complexity"]
    out = []
    for _, r in sim_df[sim_df.similarity >= sim_thresh].iterrows():
        if abs(cx.get(r.res_a, 0) - cx.get(r.res_b, 0)) >= cx_gap:
            out.append((r.res_a, r.res_b, r.similarity,
                        cx.get(r.res_a), cx.get(r.res_b)))
    return pd.DataFrame(out, columns=["res_a", "res_b", "similarity",
                                      "cx_a", "cx_b"])
# e.g. would surface Chen/Dina despite 0.94 similarity.

# (b) Flag "shadow" resources: cross-role activity + substitution + handover breadth.
handover_out_degree = handover_df.groupby("source").size().rename("out_deg")
shadow_flags = (
    per_resource
    .merge(handover_out_degree, left_on="org:resource",
           right_index=True, how="left")
    .assign(
        multi_role=lambda d: (
            roles_df.groupby("org:resource")["role_id"].nunique()
                    .reindex(d["org:resource"]).fillna(0).values > 1),
        substitutes=lambda d: d["subst_events"] > 0,
    )
)
# Giorgio (multi_role + substitutes), Mateo (high out_deg, broad handover)
# and Erik (low freq, high complexity, high escalation) fall out here.

# (c) Explicitly separate the automation boundary before ranking humans.
human_only = per_resource[per_resource["org:resource"] != "R14_Nora"]

# (d) NEVER rank authority by activity_freq alone: authority proxies are
#     A9 ownership (approval), overturn/recheck relationships in handover_df,
#     and escalation_level — inspect these, do not infer from counts.
```

Key modeling notes embedded in the code: role discovery and similarity are treated as *activity* lenses; authority is read from A9 ownership, escalation levels, and handover/recheck edges; case complexity and substitution are joined back explicitly so that a high event count (e.g., R14_Nora, temps) cannot masquerade as seniority; and divergence checks deliberately keep look-alike pairs (Chen/Dina, temps/generalists) from collapsing into one role.

# 6. Operational interpretation

**Staffing.** Protect low-volume, high-consequence roles first. Erik (Security) and Hana (Approval) are single points of critical control despite modest counts — cross-train a *qualified* backup (not a router) and formalize coverage. Use the temp pool (Kai/Lina) strictly for low-complexity surge, and gate their A2 Validate work with a quick review step, since their notes drove 17 reopens.

**Training.** Formalize Giorgio's shadow commercial capability into a documented, certified backup path for Fatima — but pair it with duration/quality targets, because his substitution nearly doubled pricing-review time. Invest in Dina to close the Chen gap on high/critical cases. Give Mateo an explicit, bounded approval mandate (or none), so the week-5 overturn pattern is resolved rather than left ambiguous.

**Risk control.** Recognize A6 (Security) and A11 (Quality) as *control gates*, not throughput tasks, and monitor their bypass. Watch Mateo's filtering: it reduced false escalations but let two critical cases wait >2 days, so add a hard SLA/exception rule for critical security referrals that cannot be filtered away. Track reopen root-causes back to the originating resource (fragment 6) as a standing quality signal.

**Resilience.** Build named backups for each critical role, maintain the automation boundary (Nora) so simple intake stays automated while complex validation stays with certified humans, and keep shadow capabilities *documented* so they add resilience without hidden key-person risk.

**Cautions about acting on mined roles:**
1. *Similar activity profiles do not imply equal authority or risk.* Chen and Dina are 0.94 similar yet differ sharply in critical-case load; Hana and Mateo both "touch escalations" yet only Hana holds sign-off. Rebalancing headcount on similarity alone would move work to under-authorized or under-competent resources.
2. *Substitution is not proof of permanent competence, and volume is not seniority.* Giorgio's coverage came with a measurable slowdown; R14_Nora's huge A1/A2 counts are automation, not leadership; temps' high close counts hide latent rework. Any staffing change should be validated against complexity, escalation, reopen, and duration outcomes — not activity counts from a clustering run — and shadow roles should be formalized deliberately rather than dismantled or blindly institutionalized.
