# System Boundary and Assumptions
The analysis focuses on the spare-part order process for an industrial distributor. The scope includes the entire order lifecycle from receiving the order to final delivery and closure, encompassing all subprocesses such as availability checks, picking, packaging, shipping, and rework. Environmental impact is measured through energy consumption, CO2 emissions, material waste, and rework generation. Service quality is evaluated based on cycle time, late delivery rates, and rework rates. Resilience is assessed by the process's ability to maintain service levels during disruptions. Assumptions include the availability of real-time data for all event attributes and the absence of external factors influencing the process.

# Sustainability-Related Process Indicators
To evaluate the environmental impact, the following indicators are derived from the event log:

- **Energy per Item (kWh):** Total energy consumption divided by the number of items processed.
- **Transport CO2 Emissions (kgCO2e):** Sum of transport emissions divided by the number of items shipped.
- **Material Scrap (kg):** Total material scrap divided by the number of items processed.
- **Rework Rate (%):** Number of reworked items divided by total items processed.
- **Batching Efficiency:** Ratio of batch size to optimal batch size (12-20 items) while monitoring waiting times.
- **Service Level Compliance (%):** Percentage of orders delivered within promised lead time.
- **Quality Defect Rate (%):** Number of defective items divided by total items processed.
- **Resilience to Disruption (%):** Proportion of orders maintained with alternative routes during carrier disruptions.

# Intervention Options
The following interventions aim to reduce environmental impact without compromising service, quality, or resilience:

## S1. Green Shipping Recommender
- **Target Path/Segment:** Standard-stock and Made-to-order Regular and Service-contract orders.
- **Mechanism:** Recommends the most environmentally friendly shipping mode based on promised lead time, route reliability, and customer segment.
- **Effects:** 
  - **Energy:** Potential reduction due to optimized route selection.
  - **Transport:** Lower emissions for compatible routes.
  - **Service:** Maintains or improves service levels by ensuring timely deliveries.
- **Monitoring Evidence:** Shipping mode selection rate, CO2 emissions, and on-time delivery rates.

## S2. Dynamic Batching
- **Target Path/Segment:** CNC_A and Heat Treatment processes for Made-to-order orders.
- **Mechanism:** Adjusts batch sizes dynamically to balance energy efficiency and waiting time, with caps to prevent excessive delays.
- **Effects:**
  - **Energy:** Reduced energy per item at optimal batch sizes.
  - **Service:** Controlled waiting times to prevent delays.
  - **Rework:** Potential increase in rework if batches are too large.
- **Monitoring Evidence:** Batch size distribution, energy per item, and waiting time metrics.

## S3. Prioritize CNC_A for Made-to-order Parts
- **Target Path/Segment:** Made-to-order Regular and Service-contract orders.
- **Mechanism:** Directs production to CNC_A when queue and due-date slack allow, leveraging its lower energy consumption.
- **Effects:**
  - **Energy:** Lower energy per item compared to CNC_B.
  - **Service:** Maintains timely production, especially when slack is sufficient.
  - **Rework:** May increase rework if CNC_B is used due to higher rework rates.
- **Monitoring Evidence:** Energy per item, rework rate, and on-time delivery rates.

## S4. Ground Shipping for Standard-stock Service-contract Orders
- **Target Path/Segment:** Standard-stock Service-contract orders with insufficient lead time slack.
- **Mechanism:** Shifts from air shipping to ground shipping or priority pick to reduce emissions and energy consumption.
- **Effects:**
  - **Energy:** Significant reduction in energy per item.
  - **Transport:** Lower CO2 emissions.
  - **Service:** Potential increase in delivery time if slack is insufficient.
- **Monitoring Evidence:** Shipping mode selection, CO2 emissions, and on-time delivery rates.

## S5. Consolidate Truck Departures
- **Target Path/Segment:** Standard-stock orders.
- **Mechanism:** Consolidates truck departures twice daily for non-urgent stock orders to reduce empty runs.
- **Effects:**
  - **Transport:** Reduced transport kgCO2e.
  - **Service:** Potential delay if consolidation wait exceeds promised lead time.
- **Monitoring Evidence:** Truck departure frequency, transport kgCO2e, and on-time delivery rates.

## S6. Improve Packaging-Route Compatibility
- **Target Path/Segment:** All order types, especially Made-to-order.
- **Mechanism:** Enhances compatibility checks between packaging types and route modes to prevent incompatibility-related rework.
- **Effects:**
  - **Material Scrap:** Reduction in packaging-related rework.
  - **Service:** Maintains order integrity and customer satisfaction.
- **Monitoring Evidence:** Packaging rework frequency, defect rates, and customer satisfaction scores.

## S7. Inspection Quality Gate
- **Target Path/Segment:** Made-to-order orders from high-rework machine/part combinations.
- **Mechanism:** Introduces a quality gate post-inspection to ensure defect detection before packaging.
- **Effects:**
  - **Rework:** Reduction in rework due to early defect identification.
  - **Quality:** Improved product quality and reduced returns.
- **Monitoring Evidence:** Rework rate, defect detection rate, and customer return rates.

## S8. Shared Emergency Courier Lanes
- **Target Path/Segment:** Critical-downtime Service-contract orders.
- **Mechanism:** Introduces shared emergency courier lanes to reduce dedicated van usage and associated emissions.
- **Effects:**
  - **Transport:** Lower emissions per order due to shared usage.
  - **Service:** Maintains critical downtime service levels.
- **Monitoring Evidence:** Courier lane usage, CO2 emissions, and on-time delivery rates for critical orders.

## S9. Distinguish True Downtime from Precautionary Urgency
- **Target Path/Segment:** Critical-downtime orders.
- **Mechanism:** Differentiates between true downtime situations and precautionary urgent orders to optimize resource allocation.
- **Effects:**
  - **Resilience:** Better handling of true downtime situations.
  - **Service:** Avoids over-allocation of resources to precautionary orders.
- **Monitoring Evidence:** Disruption categorization accuracy, service level compliance, and resource utilization rates.

## S10. Carbon-Aware Promise-Date Negotiation
- **Target Path/Segment:** Regular Made-to-order customers.
- **Mechanism:** Allows negotiation of delivery dates based on carbon impact, offering flexibility for environmentally conscious customers.
- **Effects:**
  - **Service:** Customizable delivery options based on customer preferences.
  - **Environmental Impact:** Tailored emissions based on negotiated dates.
- **Monitoring Evidence:** Customer satisfaction with delivery options, CO2 emissions, and order fulfillment rates.

## S11. Reduce Partial Shipments
- **Target Path/Segment:** Standard-stock orders with high backorder rates.
- **Mechanism:** Adjusts availability-check rules to minimize partial shipments, ensuring full shipments when possible.
- **Effects:**
  - **Material Scrap:** Reduction in packaging waste due to full shipments.
  - **Service:** Improved customer satisfaction by delivering complete orders.
- **Monitoring Evidence:** Partial shipment frequency, packaging material usage, and customer satisfaction.

## S12. Sustainability Exception Log
- **Target Path/Segment:** All order types.
- **Mechanism:** Establishes a governance framework for exceptions to sustainability policies (SP1, SP2, Q1).
- **Effects:**
  - **Compliance:** Ensures adherence to sustainability policies while allowing necessary exceptions.
  - **Service:** Maintains service levels during justified exceptions.
- **Monitoring Evidence:** Usage of exception log, compliance rates with sustainability policies, and customer impact assessments.

# Rebound and Displacement Risks
1. **Batching:** Larger batches may increase obsolescence, leading to higher disposal costs.
2. **Transport:** Faster transport modes (air) may reduce delivery times but increase emissions per order.
3. **Packaging:** Reinforced packaging reduces returns but increases material usage.
4. **Rework:** Optimized batching and quality gates can increase rework rates if not properly managed.
5. **Obsolescence:** Dynamic batching and improved routing may lead to higher production volumes, increasing the risk of overproduction and obsolescence.
6. **Customer Behavior:** Customers may shift to more environmentally friendly options if they perceive higher costs or delivery delays.

# Balanced Recommendation
A balanced redesign package should incorporate several interventions to achieve a holistic improvement in environmental impact while maintaining service quality and resilience. The recommended package includes:

1. **S1 (Green Shipping Recommender):** Implement a dynamic shipping recommendation system to optimize shipping modes based on order specifics and lead time. This intervention reduces emissions and energy consumption without compromising service levels.
2. **S2 (Dynamic Batching):** Introduce dynamic batching for CNC_A and heat treatment processes, ensuring batch sizes are optimized to balance energy efficiency and waiting times. This reduces energy consumption and rework while maintaining service levels.
3. **S4 (Ground Shipping for Service-contract Orders):** Shift Standard-stock Service-contract orders to ground shipping when lead times allow, reducing emissions and energy use. This maintains service levels while significantly lowering environmental impact.
4. **S6 (Packaging Compatibility Checks):** Enhance packaging-route compatibility checks to prevent incompatibility-related rework. This reduces material scrap and rework rates, contributing to a cleaner process.
5. **S12 (Sustainability Exception Log Governance):** Establish a governance framework for exceptions to sustainability policies, ensuring compliance while allowing necessary flexibility. This maintains policy adherence and service quality during justified exceptions.

These interventions are chosen because they collectively address key areas of environmental impact (energy, transport, material waste) while preserving service quality and resilience. They avoid the lowest-emission option if it violates service or resilience requirements, ensuring a balanced approach. Monitoring through event-log evidence will ensure adherence to sustainability policies and service metrics, allowing for continuous improvement and adaptation to process dynamics.
