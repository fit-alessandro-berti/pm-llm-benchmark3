# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

### Time-to-Event Fundamentals
* **Time Origin ($t = 0$):** The exact timestamp of the `Submit` event, which initiates every prefix in the dataset.
* **Time Scale and Granularity:** Elapsed clock hours since `Submit`, provided in integer hours via `duration_or_age_h` ($T$). 
* **SLA Horizon ($\tau$):** $\tau = 72$ hours elapsed since `Submit`.
* **Observed Event Time ($T_i$):** Let $T_i^*$ be the true time to a terminal state, and $C_i$ be the administrative observation cutoff time measured from `Submit`. We observe $T_i = \min(T_i^*, C_i)$ and the status indicator at $T_i$.
* **State Space:** At any time $t > 0$, a case is either in an active internal transient state ($S_t = 0$) or has entered an absorbing state:
  * $D = 1$: Operational success (`Resolve`).
  * $D = 2$: Internal non-resolution termination (`Withdraw` or `Reject`).
  * $D = 3$: External handoff (`Transfer`), after which the internal system stops observing the case.
* **Right Censoring ($D = 0$):** Cases with `observed_status == 'open'` are right-censored at their current age $C_i = \text{duration\_or\_age\_h}$. Crucially, if $C_i > 72$, the case is right-censored for its eventual *final duration* $T_i^*$, but its state at the SLA horizon ($t = 72$) is **fully observed** as active ($S_{72} = 0$).

---

### Estimand Definitions and Operational Alignment

Because "probability of breaching the SLA" is ambiguous under competing outcomes and external transfers, we define three distinct estimands:

#### Estimand 1: Probability of Remaining Internally Active Past 72 Hours — $\theta_{\text{active}}(72)$
$$\theta_{\text{active}}(72) = P(T^* > 72)$$
* **Definition:** The probability that a case has not reached *any* terminal internal disposition (`Resolve`, `Withdraw`, `Reject`, or `Transfer`) by $\tau = 72$ hours. Here, the composite event is $E_{\text{any}} = \mathbb{I}(D \in \{1, 2, 3\})$, and only `open` cases are censored.
* **Operational Decision:** **Capacity planning and queue backlog management.** It measures the probability that a submitted case will still be consuming internal case-management resources after 72 hours.

#### Estimand 2: Cumulative Incidence of Timely Operational Resolution (and its Complement) — $\theta_{\text{unres}}(72)$
$$\text{CIF}_{\text{res}}(72) = P(T^* \le 72 \text{ and } D = 1), \quad \theta_{\text{unres}}(72) = 1 - \text{CIF}_{\text{res}}(72) = P(T^* > 72 \text{ or } D \in \{2, 3\})$$
* **Definition:** The probability that a case fails to achieve operational success (`Resolve`) within 72 hours—either because it is still open at 72h, was resolved late ($>72\text{h}$), or terminated early via `Withdraw`, `Reject`, or `Transfer` ($\le 72\text{h}$). Estimated via the Aalen-Johansen estimator treating $D=1$ as the primary event and $D \in \{2, 3\}$ as competing risks.
* **Operational Decision:** **End-to-end customer service quality and SLA success governance.** If a customer or regulator defines SLA compliance strictly as "positively resolved within 72 hours," then early rejections, withdrawals, and external transfers are not timely resolutions.

#### Estimand 3: Late Resolution Burden (Cumulative Incidence of Resolution After 72h) — $\theta_{\text{late\_res}}(72, t_{\max})$
$$\theta_{\text{late\_res}}(72, t_{\max}) = P(72 < T^* \le t_{\max} \text{ and } D = 1) = \text{CIF}_{\text{res}}(t_{\max}) - \text{CIF}_{\text{res}}(72)$$
*(Alternatively formulated conditionally as $P(T^* > 72 \mid D = 1)$ among eventually resolved cases, or via the cause-specific net survival $1 - S_{\text{CS}, 1}(72)$ in a hypothetical world where withdrawals, rejections, and transfers are prevented).*
* **Definition:** The absolute probability mass of cases that eventually succeed (`Resolve`), but do so *after* breaching the 72-hour threshold.
* **Operational Decision:** **Process bottleneck and rework remediation.** Unlike cases that are quickly rejected (`S05` at 9h) or withdrawn (`S12` at 15h), these cases represent viable work that suffered process delays (e.g., `Escalate`, `Rework`, or queue stalls).

### Explicit Treatment of `transferred` Under Three Plausible Assumptions

All three transferred cases in the extract (`S08` at $68\text{h}$, `S21` at $26\text{h}$, `S32` at $96\text{h}$) are **P1 priority** and underwent **`Escalate`** immediately prior to transfer (`...Escalate>Transfer`). Because P1 escalated cases represent the highest urgency and complexity, treating `Transfer` as independent, non-informative random censoring violates survival assumptions. We define three formal encoding scenarios:

1. **Assumption T1: Competing Terminal Event / Internal Handoff Completion ($D_i = 3$)**
   * **Mechanism:** From the internal operations perspective, transferring a case ends internal responsibility; the case exits the internal active risk set permanently at $T_i$, and internal resolution ($D_i = 1$) can never occur.
   * **Impact at 72h:** `S21` ($26\text{h}$) and `S08` ($68\text{h}$) exit the active queue prior to $72\text{h}$ (reducing $\hat{\theta}_{\text{active}}(72)$), but count as non-resolutions under $\hat{\theta}_{\text{unres}}(72)$. `S32` ($96\text{h}$) was still internally active at $72\text{h}$ and thus breached the internal 72h horizon *before* being transferred.
2. **Assumption T2: Non-Informative Right Censoring at Transfer ($\delta_i = 0$ at $T_i$)**
   * **Mechanism:** Assumes the case continues toward resolution externally at the exact same hazard rate as similar cases that remained internal.
   * **Justification & Flaw:** Standard in naive survival models, but highly questionable here because every transfer is a P1 escalated case. However, in a P1- and escalation-stratified model, it provides a baseline "exchangeable continuation" estimate.
3. **Assumption T3: Pessimistic SLA Breach / Delayed External Resolution Bound (Extreme Informative Censoring)**
   * **Mechanism:** External organizational handoffs incur inter-agency friction, and cases are transferred precisely because internal resolution failed after escalation. We assume transferred cases never achieve overall resolution within 72 hours ($T_{\text{global}}^* > 72\text{h}$).
   * **Encoding:** For 72-hour SLA evaluation, `S21` ($26\text{h}$) and `S08` ($68\text{h}$) have their effective resolution times right-censored at $\max(T_i, 72.1\text{h})$ (or coded as guaranteed $>72\text{h}$ breaches), while `S32` ($96\text{h}$) is already $>72\text{h}$. (Conversely, an *Optimistic Bound T3b* treats `Transfer` at $T_i \le 72\text{h}$ as equivalent to timely resolution, $\delta_{\text{res}} = 1$).

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

A common anti-pattern in operational reporting is filtering the dataset to completed resolved cases (`observed_status == 'resolved'`, $N=13$) and computing the empirical fraction with `duration_or_age_h > 72`, or comparing mean durations across cohorts. Using the supplied extract, we demonstrate five distinct structural biases produced by this approach.

### 1. Survivor Bias (Exclusion of Active Open Cases)
* **Empirical Evidence:** Among the $N=13$ resolved cases, exactly 4 breached 72 hours (`S03` at $76\text{h}$, `S13` at $110\text{h}$, `S20` at $74\text{h}$, `S28` at $88\text{h}$), yielding a naive SLA breach rate of $4 / 13 = \mathbf{30.8\%}$ (Cohort A: $2/6 = 33.3\%$; Cohort B: $2/7 = 28.6\%$).
* **The Flaw:** This completely drops all $N=9$ `open` cases. Crucially, 4 of those open cases have **already breached 72 hours** (`S09` at $92\text{h}$, `S11` at $81\text{h}$, `S25` at $79\text{h}$, `S26` at $104\text{h}$). Even if all 4 resolved one second after the cutoff, they would be $>72\text{h}$ resolutions! Including just these 4 already-breached open cases alongside the 13 resolved cases raises the minimum possible breach fraction among non-withdrawn/non-rejected/non-transferred cases to $(4 + 4) / (13 + 4) = \mathbf{47.1\%}$.

### 2. Fundamental Difference Between Open Cases Below vs. Above 72 Hours
* **Open Cases $< 72\text{h}$ (`S10` at $47\text{h}$, `S15` at $35\text{h}$, `S24` at $67\text{h}$, `S29` at $51\text{h}$, `S31` at $23\text{h}$):**
  * **Information Content:** Their 72-hour SLA outcome is **unobserved (right-censored before $\tau=72$)**. They contribute to the risk set up to their current age $C_i$.
  * **Operational Status:** Notice that four of these five (`S15`, `S24`, `S29`, `S31`) have very short inactivity gaps (`last_event_gap_h` $\in \{2, 4, 6, 3\}\text{h}$), indicating active ongoing work. (Only `S10` at $47\text{h}$ shows an early stall with `last_event_gap_h = 19h`). However, `S24` is at $67\text{h}$ in `Review`—it has only 5 hours remaining to complete `Review` and `Resolve` before breaching.
* **Open Cases $> 72\text{h}$ (`S09` at $92\text{h}$, `S11` at $81\text{h}$, `S25` at $79\text{h}$, `S26` at $104\text{h}$):**
  * **Information Content:** For any 72-hour SLA estimand ($\tau = 72$), these cases are **not unknown**—they have *deterministically crossed the 72-hour threshold without a terminal disposition*. Right-censoring only obscures whether they will resolve at $95\text{h}$ or $200\text{h}$, not whether they breached $72\text{h}$.
  * **Operational Status:** Every single open case $>72\text{h}$ has an extreme inactivity gap: `S09` ($31\text{h}$), `S11` ($55\text{h}$), `S25` ($48\text{h}$), and `S26` ($72\text{h}$). For example, `S26` entered `Review` at $104 - 72 = 32\text{h}$ and has sat completely untouched for 72 consecutive hours. Dropping these cases hides a severe "zombie queue" of abandoned or blocked work.

### 3. Informative Censoring from Transfers and Inactivity Gaps
* **Transfer Selection Bias:** Every `transferred` case (`S08` at $68\text{h}$, `S21` at $26\text{h}$, `S32` at $96\text{h}$) is **P1** and **escalated**. Among P1 cases altogether ($N=8$), only 2 resolved (`S04` at $29\text{h}$, `S18` at $12\text{h}$), while 3 were transferred (`S08`, `S21`, `S32`) and 3 remain open (`S11` at $81\text{h}$, `S25` at $79\text{h}$, `S29` at $51\text{h}$).
* **The Distortion:** If a naive analysis looks only at resolved P1 cases (`S04` at $29\text{h}$ and `S18` at $12\text{h}$), P1 appears to have a **0% SLA breach rate** and an average duration of $20.5\text{h}$! In reality, the hardest P1 cases were either transferred out (`S08` just 4 hours before the 72h SLA cliff; `S32` at $96\text{h}$ after breaching) or are sitting stalled in the open queue past 72h (`S11` at $81\text{h}$ with a $55\text{h}$ gap; `S25` at $79\text{h}$ with a $48\text{h}$ gap).

### 4. Competing Risks Distortion (Withdrawals and Rejections)
* **Empirical Evidence:** Rejections occur very early (`S05` at $9\text{h}$, `S23` at $11\text{h}$) or late (`S14` at $63\text{h}$), and withdrawals occur between $15\text{h}$ and $70\text{h}$ (`S12` at $15\text{h}$, `S06` at $27\text{h}$, `S22` at $44\text{h}$, `S30` at $70\text{h}$). All 7 cases exit before $72\text{h}$.
* **The Distortion:** Treating `Withdraw` and `Reject` as standard non-informative right-censoring in a standard Kaplan-Meier model of `Resolve` assumes those 7 cases would have gone on to `Resolve` at the same hazard as remaining active cases. This overestimates the cumulative probability of resolution ($\text{CIF}_{\text{res}}(t)$) because it ignores the fact that withdrawn/rejected cases can *never* resolve (violation of probability conservation across states). Furthermore, `S30` withdrew at $70\text{h}$ after `Review`—likely a customer giving up right before the 72h SLA mark.

### 5. Cohort Imbalance, Non-Random Assignment, and Incomplete Compliance
* **Priority & Case-Mix Imbalance:** Cohort B ($N=17$) has a slightly higher proportion of P1 cases ($5/17 = 29.4\%$: `S18`, `S21`, `S25`, `S29`, `S32`) than Cohort A ($3/15 = 20.0\%$: `S04`, `S08`, `S11`).
* **Incomplete Rollout Compliance in Cohort B:** Cohort B introduced automated screening (`AutoCheck` directly after `Submit`, i.e., `Submit>AutoCheck...`) and a new escalation policy. However, looking at the prefixes in Cohort B:
  * **12 cases** followed the new workflow (`Submit>AutoCheck...`: `S16`, `S18`, `S19`, `S21`, `S22`, `S24`, `S25`, `S27`, `S28`, `S29`, `S31`, `S32`).
  * **5 cases** in Cohort B reverted to the legacy screening order (`Submit>Screen...`: `S17`, `S20`, `S23`, `S26`, `S30`).
  * Crucially, every single P3 case in Cohort B (`S17`, `S20`, `S23`, `S26`, `S30`) bypassed the new `Submit>AutoCheck` entry point! And 2 of Cohort B's worst outcomes (`S20` resolved at $74\text{h}$; `S26` open at $104\text{h}$ with a $72\text{h}$ inactivity gap) occurred in these non-compliant P3 paths. Conversely, in Cohort A, two P2 cases (`S01`, `S15`) already used `AutoCheck` *after* `Screen` (`Submit>Screen>AutoCheck...`). Comparing Cohort A vs. B naively confounds policy intent (ITT) with priority-driven compliance (P3 never got `Submit>AutoCheck`).

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

### Methodological Comparison for This Process Extract

| Method | Target Estimand | Strengths on This Dataset | Weaknesses / Pitfalls on This Dataset |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Kaplan-Meier (KM) on Composite Terminal Event** | $\theta_{\text{active}}(t) = P(T^* > t)$ (Estimand 1) | Valid and unbiased (under independent censoring of `open` cases) for estimating the probability of remaining internally active past $72\text{h}$. Correctly retains `S09`, `S11`, `S25`, `S26` in the risk set past $72\text{h}$. | Cannot distinguish whether a case exited via success (`Resolve`) or failure (`Withdraw`/`Reject`/`Transfer`). If applied to `Resolve` alone by censoring `Withdraw`/`Reject`/`Transfer`, it severely **overestimates** resolution probability (upward bias). |
| **2. Aalen-Johansen (AJ) / Non-Parametric CIF** | $\text{CIF}_k(t) = P(T^* \le t, D = k)$ (Estimands 2 & 3) | **Gold standard for marginal outcome probabilities.** Partitions total probability at any horizon $t$ into mutually exclusive states: $\sum_{k=1}^3 \text{CIF}_k(t) + S_{\text{all}}(t) = 1$. Handles `Resolve`, `Withdraw/Reject`, and `Transfer` simultaneously without upward bias. | Non-parametric stratification across Cohort $\times$ Priority ($2 \times 3 = 6$ strata) yields very small cell sizes ($n \in [3, 7]$ per cell), producing wide variance if fully stratified without pooling. |
| **3. Cause-Specific Hazards (CSH / Cox)** | $\lambda_k^{\text{CS}}(t) = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{P(t \le T^* < t+\Delta t, D=k \mid T^* \ge t)}{\Delta t}$ | Ideal for studying **etiological / process mechanisms**: how baseline covariates (`priority`, `cohort`, initial `AutoCheck`) affect the instantaneous rate of completing work among currently active cases. | Does not directly translate to the marginal probability of breaching 72h ($\text{CIF}_{\text{res}}(72)$) because a covariate (like P1 priority) can increase both the hazard of `Transfer` and the hazard of `Escalate`. |
| **4. Subdistribution Hazards (Fine-Gray)** | $\gamma_k(t)$ modeling $\text{CIF}_k(t)$ directly | Directly links baseline covariates (`priority`, `cohort`) to the cumulative incidence of timely resolution $\text{CIF}_{\text{res}}(72)$ by keeping competing events (`Withdraw`, `Reject`, `Transfer`) in the risk set indefinitely. | **Incompatible with internal time-varying process covariates** (such as `Escalate` or `Rework` occurring mid-case), because keeping already-withdrawn cases in the risk set creates unnatural counterfactuals for internal workflow transitions. |
| **5. Restricted Mean Time (RMST / RMTL)** | $\text{RMST}(\tau) = \int_0^\tau S(t)dt$ or $\int_0^\tau \text{CIF}_k(t)dt$ | Summarizes the entire trajectory up to $\tau = 72\text{h}$ (expected active internal hours per case up to the SLA limit, or expected time lost to each competing outcome) rather than a single cliff-edge point at $72\text{h}$. Stable in small samples ($N=32$). | Masks tail severity beyond $\tau = 72\text{h}$ unless paired with a secondary horizon (e.g., $\tau = 96\text{h}$) to capture late resolutions (`S13` at $110\text{h}$, `S28` at $88\text{h}$) and stalled open cases (`S26` at $104\text{h}$). |
| **6. Landmarking** | $P(T^* > 72 \text{ or } D=k \mid T^* > t_{\text{LM}}, \mathcal{F}_{t_{\text{LM}}})$ | **Solves immortal-time bias** when exact intermediate event timestamps are missing or when generating live operational alerts (e.g., at $t_{\text{LM}} = 24\text{h}$ or $48\text{h}$) conditioned on prefix state and inactivity gap at $t_{\text{LM}}$. | In our static extract, we only know exact timestamps for the *last* recorded event (`T - last_event_gap_h`) and the final/cutoff time $T$, limiting exact state reconstruction at arbitrary $t_{\text{LM}}$ without bounding assumptions. |
| **7. Multi-State Model (MSM)** | Transition intensities $\alpha_{hj}(t)$ and state occupation probabilities $P(S(t) = j)$ | Naturally mirrors the process graph (`Submit` $\to$ `Screen`/`AutoCheck` $\to$ `Review` $\to$ `Escalate`/`Rework` $\to$ Terminal States). Separates *transition probabilities* (branching logic) from *sojourn times* (stage bottlenecks). | Full continuous-time Markov/semi-Markov estimation requires intermediate activity timestamps, which are omitted in the extract except for the final step's inactivity gap (`last_event_gap_h`). |

### Recommended Primary and Secondary Approaches

#### Primary Approach: Non-Parametric Aalen-Johansen (AJ) Competing-Risks Estimator Paired with Restricted Mean Time in State ($\tau = 72\text{h}$ and $\tau = 96\text{h}$)
* **Justification for These Data:**
  1. With $N=32$ cases and 4 distinct status categories (`resolved`, `withdrawn`/`rejected`, `transferred`, `open`), Aalen-Johansen provides an exact, assumption-light accounting of how probability mass flows into timely resolution ($\text{CIF}_{\text{res}}(72)$), early attrition ($\text{CIF}_{\text{attrit}}(72)$), external transfer ($\text{CIF}_{\text{trans}}(72)$), and active SLA breach ($S_{\text{all}}(72) = \theta_{\text{active}}(72)$).
  2. It requires **zero fabrication of missing intermediate event timestamps** because it operates strictly on the observed total duration/age $T_i$ and terminal state $D_i$.
  3. Pairing point estimates at $\tau=72\text{h}$ with $\text{RMST}(72)$ stabilizes comparisons across `Cohort` (A vs. B) and `Priority` (P1, P2, P3) despite small sample sizes.

#### Secondary Approach: Discrete-Stage / Landmark Multi-State Hazard Analysis Utilizing the Observed Last-Event Timestamp ($T_{\text{last}} = T_i - \text{last\_event\_gap\_h}$)
* **Justification for These Data:**
  1. While intermediate timestamps are missing for completed cases (`last_event_gap_h == 0`), for all **9 `open` cases**, the exact timestamp of entry into their current active state (`Review`, `Escalate`, or `Screen`) is deterministically recoverable:
     $$t_{\text{entry\_last}, i} = \text{duration\_or\_age\_h}_i - \text{last\_event\_gap\_h}_i$$
     For example, we know with certainty that `S11` (`open` at $81\text{h}$, gap $55\text{h}$, prefix `Submit>Screen>Escalate`) entered `Escalate` at exactly $81 - 55 = \mathbf{26\text{h}}$, and has been stuck in `Escalate` for $55\text{h}$. Similarly, `S26` (`open` at $104\text{h}$, gap $72\text{h}$, prefix `Submit>Screen>Review`) entered `Review` at $104 - 72 = \mathbf{32\text{h}}$.
  2. This allows a rigorous **stage-conditioned / landmark operational risk model** that evaluates hazard of stalling vs. resolving without committing immortal-time bias.

# 5. Process-aware features and stage hazards

### Feature Engineering, Temporal Classification, and Leakage Analysis

A critical pitfall in process-aware survival analysis is treating mid-process events (`Escalate`, `Rework`, repeated `Review`) as static baseline covariates at $t=0$. To experience `Submit>Screen>Review>Rework>Review>Escalate>Resolve` (`S13`, $110\text{h}$), a case *must survive long enough* without resolving or withdrawing to accumulate 7 process steps. This creates **immortal-time bias** (guaranteeing survival up to the transition time) and **reverse-causality / target leakage** (cases are escalated or reworked *because* they are running late or complex).

# 6. Sensitivity analysis

To evaluate the robustness of our SLA conclusions across Cohort A vs. Cohort B and across priority tiers, we compare **four structured sensitivity scenarios** in Section 8's Python execution:

### Scenario Definitions
1. **Scenario S1 (Baseline Internal Competing-Risks / Aalen-Johansen):**
   * `Resolve` ($D=1$) is operational success; `Withdraw`/`Reject` ($D=2$) and `Transfer` ($D=3$) are competing terminal internal events; `open` ($D=0$) is non-informatively right-censored at current age $C_i$.
2. **Scenario S2 (Transfer Optimistic vs. Pessimistic Bounds):**
   * **S2a (Optimistic External Handoff):** Treats `Transfer` as a valid operational completion equivalent to `Resolve` ($D=1$ at $T_i$).
   * **S2b (Pessimistic External Delay Bound):** Assumes `Transfer` represents a failed internal resolution that will not be resolved externally before 72 hours (`S08` at $68\text{h}$ and `S21` at $26\text{h}$ are held in the un-resolved risk set past $72\text{h}$, censored at $\max(T_i, 75\text{h})$).
3. **Scenario S3 (Informative Censoring / Stagnation Penalty for Long Gaps):**
   * In Scenario S1, open cases $<72\text{h}$ (`S10`, `S15`, `S24`, `S29`, `S31`) are assumed to resolve after cutoff at the same hazard as cases that were actively moving at that age.
   * Under **S3**, any open case $<72\text{h}$ that exhibits severe stage stagnation (`last_event_gap_h >= 15h`, such as `S10` stalled in `Escalate` for $19\text{h}$) or enters `Review` too close to the 72h deadline to plausibly finish (`S24` entering `Review` at $63\text{h}$, when the fastest observed `Review -> Resolve` transition in the entire dataset takes at least $8\text{h}$–$15\text{h}$) is reclassified as an inevitable $>72\text{h}$ breach (censored at $75\text{h}$).
4. **Scenario S4 (Cohort Policy Evaluation: Intent-to-Treat [ITT] vs. Per-Protocol / As-Treated):**
   * **ITT:** Compares Cohort A ($N=15$) vs. Cohort B ($N=17$) as labeled.
   * **As-Treated (`Submit>AutoCheck` Compliance):** Compares compliant Cohort B cases (`Submit>AutoCheck...`, $N=12$) against legacy-screened Cohort B cases (`Submit>Screen...`, $N=5$) and Cohort A ($N=15$), adjusted for `priority` mix.

# 7. Operational interpretation

### Converting Survival Outputs into Real-Time Alerts and Workload Actions

Risk scores derived from observational survival models estimate **conditional event probabilities under the historical policy regime**, *not* the causal effect of intervening on a case. For example, observing that `Escalate` is associated with a high probability of breaching 72h (`S03`, `S11`, `S13`, `S25`, `S28`, `S32`) does **not** mean managers should ban escalation; escalation is a response to underlying case complexity.

Instead, survival outputs should drive a **two-trigger operational decision matrix** at landmark evaluation times $t_{\text{LM}}$:

| Operational Trigger | Target Estimand & Threshold | Concrete Cases Flagged in Extract | Prescriptive Workload Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Horizon-Proximity Alert (Time-to-Cliff)** | Active case with $t_{\text{current}} < 72\text{h}$ where conditional breach probability $P(T^* > 72 \mid T^* > t, \text{state}, \text{priority}) > 0.50$ | **`S24`** (P2, Cohort B, age $67\text{h}$, in `Review`, gap $4\text{h}$) and **`S29`** (P1, Cohort B, age $51\text{h}$, in `Escalate>Review`, gap $6\text{h}$) | **Fast-Track / Swarming:** `S24` has only $5\text{h}$ left before $72\text{h}$ while still in `Review`. Assign immediate senior reviewer sign-off to prevent an imminent breach. |
| **2. Stage-Stagnation Alert (Inactivity Hazard)** | Active case where current stage sojourn time (`last_event_gap_h`) exceeds the 75th percentile of productive stage duration ($\text{gap} \ge 15\text{h}$) | **`S10`** (age $47\text{h}$, gap $19\text{h}$ in `Escalate`), **`S11`** (age $81\text{h}$, gap $55\text{h}$ in `Escalate`), **`S25`** (age $79\text{h}$, gap $48\text{h}$ in `Escalate`), **`S09`** (age $92\text{h}$, gap $31\text{h}$ in `Review`), **`S26`** (age $104\text{h}$, gap $72\text{h}$ in `Review`) | **Queue Unblocking & Escalation Audit:** Do *not* wait until hour 70 to alert on `S10` ($47\text{h}$). It has been sitting untouched in `Escalate` since hour 28. Ping the escalation owner immediately. For `S09`, `S11`, `S25`, `S26` (already $>72\text{h}$), trigger a "Breached Backlog Recovery" sweep. |
| **3. Non-Compliance Routing Alert** | Cohort B case entering `Submit>Screen` instead of `Submit>AutoCheck` | **`S17`, `S20`, `S23`, `S26`, `S30`** (all P3 cases in Cohort B) | **Workflow Enforcement:** Audit why P3 intake routing bypassed `AutoCheck` in Cohort B, leading directly to `S20` ($74\text{h}$) and `S26` ($104\text{h}$ open). |
| **4. Pre-72h Transfer / Withdrawal Governance** | High rate of $D=2$ (`Withdraw`) or $D=3$ (`Transfer`) between $45\text{h}$ and $71\text{h}$ | **`S08`** (transferred at $68\text{h}$) and **`S30`** (withdrawn at $70\text{h}$) | **Anti-Gaming SLA Audit:** Verify that `S08` ($68\text{h}$) was not transferred out 4 hours before the 72h mark simply to avoid recording an internal SLA breach ("clock dumping"). |

### Calibration and Fairness / Equity Checks by Priority and Cohort

1. **Right-Censored Calibration Check (Brier Score / Observed-vs-Expected at $\tau = 72\text{h}$):**
   * Because 4 open cases (`S09`, `S11`, `S25`, `S26`) have ages $> 72\text{h}$, only 5 cases (`S10`, `S15`, `S24`, `S29`, `S31`) are censored prior to $\tau = 72\text{h}$.
   * Check calibration by comparing predicted $\hat{\theta}_{\text{active}}(72)$ and $\hat{\text{CIF}}_{\text{res}}(72)$ within each `Cohort × Priority` cell against IPCW (Inverse Probability of Censoring Weighted) empirical frequencies at $72\text{h}$.
2. **Priority Starvation & Equity Check (P3 vs. P1/P2):**
   * **Observation:** Look at the open cases in P3: `S09` (open at $92\text{h}$, gap $31\text{h}$) and `S26` (open at $104\text{h}$, gap $72\text{h}$), plus late resolutions `S20` ($74\text{h}$) and near-breach withdrawal `S30` ($70\text{h}$). Furthermore, **0% of P3 cases in Cohort B received `Submit>AutoCheck`**, whereas **100% of P1 and P2 cases in Cohort B received `Submit>AutoCheck`**.
   * **Fairness Implications:** The automated screening and escalation policy rollout in Cohort B systematically excluded P3 cases, and P3 cases in `Review` experience the longest untouched starvation gaps ($72\text{h}$ for `S26`). If SLA governance only rewards P1/P2 speed, workers may indefinitely starve P3 cases once they pass 72 hours (since a breached case cannot "breach twice").
   * **Remediation:** Track **post-breach tail hazard** (or RMTL up to $120\text{h}$) alongside the binary 72-hour threshold so that already-breached cases (`S09`, `S11`, `S25`, `S26`) continue accumulating SLA penalty weight until resolved.

        df.groupby("prefix")["case"].apply(list).to_dict()

    else "TERMINATED",
    axis=1,

# ==============================================================================
# 3. ENCODE ESTIMANDS & THREE TRANSFER / SENSITIVITY SCENARIOS
# ==============================================================================
def encode_scenario(data: pd.DataFrame, scenario: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Returns a copy of `data` with survival time `t_obs` and competing event code `event_code`:
      0 = right-censored (open or non-informatively censored)
      1 = operational resolution ('resolved', or 'transferred' under T_opt)
      2 = internal attrition ('withdrawn' or 'rejected')
      3 = external transfer ('transferred' under T1 competing risk)
    Also encodes `event_any` = 1 if any internal termination occurred, 0 if censored.
    """
    d = data.copy()
    d["t_obs"] = d["duration_or_age_h"].astype(float)

    if scenario == "S1_Baseline_Competing":
        # T1: Transfer is competing risk (3); Withdraw/Reject is competing risk (2);
        # Resolve is primary event (1); Open is right-censored at age (0).
        status_map = {
            "resolved": 1,
            "withdrawn": 2,
            "rejected": 2,
            "transferred": 3,
            "open": 0,
        }
        d["event_code"] = d["observed_status"].map(status_map)
        d["event_any"] = (d["event_code"] > 0).astype(int)

    elif scenario == "S2a_Transfer_NonInformative_Censored":
        # T2: Transfer is treated as non-informative right-censoring at transfer time
        # for global resolution; Withdraw/Reject remain competing risks (2).
        status_map = {
            "resolved": 1,
            "withdrawn": 2,
            "rejected": 2,
            "transferred": 0,
            "open": 0,
        }
        d["event_code"] = d["observed_status"].map(status_map)
        # For internal queue active time, transfer still exits internal queue:
        d["event_any"] = (d["observed_status"] != "open").astype(int)

    elif scenario == "S2b_Transfer_Optimistic_Success":
        # T3_opt: Transfer counts as valid timely handoff/completion (code = 1)
        status_map = {
            "resolved": 1,
            "transferred": 1,
            "withdrawn": 2,
            "rejected": 2,
            "open": 0,
        }
        d["event_code"] = d["observed_status"].map(status_map)
        d["event_any"] = (d["event_code"] > 0).astype(int)

    elif scenario == "S3_Pessimistic_Transfer_And_Stall_Bound":
        # T3_pess + Informative Censoring Bound:
        # 1) Transferred cases (S08 at 68h, S21 at 26h, S32 at 96h) are assumed NOT
        #    to resolve externally within 72h -> right-censored at max(t_obs, 75h).
        # 2) Open cases <72h that are severely stalled (last_event_gap_h >= 15h: S10)
        #    or entered Review at >=63h (S24) cannot resolve by 72h -> censored at 75h.
        status_map = {
            "resolved": 1,
            "withdrawn": 2,
            "rejected": 2,
            "transferred": 0,
            "open": 0,
        }
        d["event_code"] = d["observed_status"].map(status_map)
        pessimistic_mask = (d["observed_status"] == "transferred") | (
            (d["observed_status"] == "open")
            & (
                (d["last_event_gap_h"] >= 15)
                | (
                    (d["active_stage"] == "Review")
                    & (d["last_known_event_time_h"] >= 60)
                )
            )
        )
        d.loc[pessimistic_mask & (d["t_obs"] <= 72), "t_obs"] = 75.0
        d["event_any"] = (d["event_code"] > 0).astype(int)

    else:
        raise ValueError(f"Unknown scenario: {scenario}")

    return d


# ==============================================================================
# 4. NON-PARAMETRIC KAPLAN-MEIER & AALEN-JOHANSEN ESTIMATORS (NO DROPPED CASES)
# ==============================================================================
def compute_km_and_aj(
    df_sub: pd.DataFrame, horizon: float = 72.0
) -> dict:
    """
    Computes exact risk-set table, Kaplan-Meier overall active survival S_all(t),
    Aalen-Johansen Cumulative Incidence Functions CIF_k(t) for k in {1, 2, 3},
    and Restricted Mean Survival Time (RMST) up to `horizon`.
    Retains all open (right-censored) cases in the risk set up to their censoring age.
    """
    times = np.sort(df_sub["t_obs"].unique())
    n_total = len(df_sub)

    s_all = 1.0  # KM probability of remaining in initial active state
    cif = {1: 0.0, 2: 0.0, 3: 0.0}
    rmst_active = 0.0
    prev_t = 0.0

    s_at_horizon = 1.0
    cif_at_horizon = {1: 0.0, 2: 0.0, 3: 0.0}

    risk_table = []

    for t in times:
        # Accumulate RMST up to min(t, horizon)
        if prev_t < horizon:
            dt = min(t, horizon) - prev_t
            rmst_active += s_all * dt

        at_risk = (df_sub["t_obs"] >= t).sum()
        at_t = df_sub[df_sub["t_obs"] == t]

        d_k = {k: (at_t["event_code"] == k).sum() for k in (1, 2, 3)}
        d_total = sum(d_k.values())
        censored = (at_t["event_code"] == 0).sum()

        # Aalen-Johansen update: increment CIF_k by S_all(t-) * (d_k / n_t)
        s_prev = s_all
        if at_risk > 0:
            for k in (1, 2, 3):
                cif[k] += s_prev * (d_k[k] / at_risk)
            s_all = s_prev * (1.0 - d_total / at_risk)

        if t <= horizon:
            s_at_horizon = s_all
            cif_at_horizon = cif.copy()

        risk_table.append(
            {
                "t": t,
                "n_risk": at_risk,
                "d_res": d_k[1],
                "d_attrit": d_k[2],
                "d_trans": d_k[3],
                "censored": censored,
                "S_active": s_all,
                "CIF_res": cif[1],
                "CIF_attrit": cif[2],
                "CIF_trans": cif[3],
            }
        )
        prev_t = t

    if prev_t < horizon:
        rmst_active += s_all * (horizon - prev_t)

    # Cause-specific KM for Resolve (treating D=2,3 and D=0 as censored at t_obs)
    s_cs_res = 1.0
    for t in times:
        if t > horizon:
            break
        at_risk = (df_sub["t_obs"] >= t).sum()
        d_res = ((df_sub["t_obs"] == t) & (df_sub["event_code"] == 1)).sum()
        if at_risk > 0:
            s_cs_res *= 1.0 - (d_res / at_risk)

    return {
        "N": n_total,
        "theta_active_72": s_at_horizon,
        "CIF_res_72": cif_at_horizon[1],
        "theta_unres_72": 1.0 - cif_at_horizon[1],
        "CIF_attrit_72": cif_at_horizon[2],
        "CIF_trans_72": cif_at_horizon[3],
        "CIF_res_max": cif[1],
        "theta_late_res": cif[1] - cif_at_horizon[1],
        "CS_unres_72": s_cs_res,
        "RMST_active_72": rmst_active,
        "risk_table": pd.DataFrame(risk_table),
    }

# 9. Limitations and additional data

To move from descriptive/bounded competing-risks estimation to causal policy evaluation and continuous-time multi-state hazard modeling, additional data elements should be prioritized in the following order:

| Priority Rank | Missing Data Element | Specific Analytical Impact & Why It Is Needed |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1 (Highest)** | **Exact Activity Start & Completion Timestamps (Event-Level Log)** | Currently, only total duration $T_i$ and `last_event_gap_h` are known. Having start and end timestamps for every intermediate step (`Screen`, `AutoCheck`, `Review`, `Escalate`, `Rework`) is essential to: (a) separate *active processing time* from *queue waiting time*, (b) fit exact continuous-time multi-state models ($\alpha_{hj}(t)$) and time-dependent Cox models without immortal-time approximations, and (c) verify whether `last_event_gap_h == 0` in completed cases means zero terminal delay or simply that the terminal event was the last logged timestamp. |
| **2** | **External Post-Transfer Disposition & Timestamp (`Transfer` Follow-Up)** | All 3 transfers (`S08`, `S21`, `S32`) occur in escalated P1 cases, causing a **28.1 percentage point swing** in P1 72-hour SLA success between Scenario S1 ($25.0\%$) and Scenario S2b ($53.1\%$). Linking external organization resolution logs narrows these bounds to a point estimate. |
| **3** | **Policy Assignment Mechanism & Rollout Compliance Drivers in Cohort B** | In Cohort B, 100% of P1/P2 cases used `Submit>AutoCheck`, while 0% of P3 cases did. We need routing logs and eligibility rules to know whether P3 cases were intentionally excluded by system configuration, manually overridden by agents, or rejected by the `AutoCheck` service (`S17` ran `AutoCheck` *after* `Screen`). Without this, Cohort B's treatment effect is non-identifiable for P3. |
| **4** | **Calendar Arrival Dates ($t_0$), Observation Cutoff Timestamp, and Queue Workload** | The extract was frozen at one cutoff, so an open case with age $23\text{h}$ (`S31`) arrived $81\text{h}$ *after* an open case with age $104\text{h}$ (`S26`). Calendar timestamps and concurrent active queue counts are required to control for calendar-time congestion, shift/weekend effects, and whether Cohort B was observed closer to the cutoff (shorter maximum potential follow-up). |
| **5** | **Withdrawal & Rejection Reason Codes and Dynamic Priority History** | Distinguishing customer withdrawals caused by service delay (`S30` at $70\text{h}$) from benign customer self-resolution (`S12` at $15\text{h}$) refines Estimand 2. Additionally, audit logs confirming whether `priority` (P1/P2/P3) is strictly fixed at `Submit` or dynamically upgraded upon `Escalate` prevents subtle time-varying confounding. |
