def loop_position(d, a, b, anchor="Release Payment"):
    out = {}
    for cid, g in d.groupby("case:concept:name"):

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

Review of the procurement-to-payment event log for the last quarter identified a number of control exceptions and data-quality limitations that warrant targeted follow-up. These observations concern process and control design and operation; they are not determinations of misconduct, and no conclusion regarding fraud or collusion is drawn.

Four-eyes exceptions were identified in 42 cases in which the same resource created and approved a purchase order (1.8% of cases) and in 9 cases in which the same resource changed vendor bank details and approved payment (0.4%). The former is concentrated in the documented rush channels for emergency medical supplies and weekend facilities repairs, and is broadly consistent with emergency handling and informal specialization supported by existing delegation arrangements. The latter, although low in volume, sits directly on the payment path, is concentrated in a single vendor family (V-77x), and is concentrated in a manual-upload source system that bypasses enforced controls. This subgroup is the most concerning and should be the first target for follow-up.

Exceptionally short approval intervals and repeated handover loops were also observed. Several align with delegated authority for urgent cases and with normal clarification patterns; others coincide with batch-migrated records, a shared approval account, and a bot/human resource-mapping defect, all of which limit the reliability of resource-level conclusions.

We recommend inspection of bank-maintenance change tickets and vendor due-diligence files for the V-77x cases, verification of retrospective justifications against underlying emergency documentation, and correction of shared-account and mapping defects before segregation-of-duties testing is repeated.
