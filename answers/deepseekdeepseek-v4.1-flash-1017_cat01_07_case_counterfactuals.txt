## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104
**Factual mechanism:** The sanctions ambiguity was visible at Triage but not packetized for compliance. Final C1 review therefore started only after Assessment and S1 advice, with much evidence read from scratch, creating a long final-compliance wait and service interval.

**Minimal intervention:** Keep the same Register, Triage, Identity, Document, Assessment, Decision, and Notify resources. Add an early S1 packet after Triage and a provisional C1 reservation, allowing preliminary C1 reading while other checks proceed; final C1 remains after final Assessment.

**Temporal propagation:** Early S1 work may clarify sanctions questions before Assessment, and preliminary C1 reading can reduce final C1 effort by roughly 1-3 h if the packet is complete. The larger gain comes from readiness: the final C1 review can use a reserved slot after Assessment instead of waiting for a new C1 allocation. However, C1 time spent early is unavailable to urgent non-target cases, and an incomplete packet can cause C1 to re-read or re-query evidence, offsetting the saving.

**Process-feasibility check:** Final compliance still follows final Assessment and precedes Decision; A1 and C1 remain different people. Preliminary work does not replace final compliance. C1 cannot perform two service intervals at once, so reservation and preliminary work must be sequenced with C1's other commitments.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best case is about 7-9 h earlier Notify if the packet is complete and the reserved C1 slot actually advances final review. Base case is roughly 4-7 h earlier. Worst case is 1-3 h later or more if C1 introduces re-query/rework and displaces urgent compliance cases; decision may remain approve-with-conditions but with weaker defensibility.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** C1 still performs a full re-read, the reserved slot is not honored, the early packet is materially incomplete, or C1 displacement of urgent cases exceeds the target-case saving. If C1 backlog is already binding, the local gain is likely not system-safe.

### K-219
**Factual mechanism:** Originals arrived Monday 17:30, but D1 could not start until Tuesday 09:30 because of the D1 queue. D1 then completed in about 4 h with no rework. The document wait delayed Assessment and therefore Decision/Notify.

**Minimal intervention:** Route the same document check to D2 as soon as Originals Receipt occurs, rather than waiting for D1. Keep Identity, Assessment, Decision, and Notify unchanged.

**Temporal propagation:** D2 can enter the document queue earlier if D2 has slack, so originals-to-document-complete may move from Tuesday 13:30 to a range around Tuesday 02:30-06:30, or later depending queue. Earlier document completion can make Assessment ready earlier, but A2's queue may absorb the gain. If D2 requests clarification on handwriting, the case re-enters Document Check and must repeat Assessment, which can erase or reverse the benefit.

**Process-feasibility check:** Originals Receipt precedes Document Check; Identity may proceed independently; both Identity and Document Check finish before Assessment. No compliance is required because the case is low-risk/low-value. D2 is a valid document resource, but cannot work two documents simultaneously.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best case is roughly 15-20 h earlier if D2 starts much earlier, no clarification occurs, and A2 can take the case sooner. Base case is roughly 5-15 h earlier. Worst case is 8-36 h later if D2 requires external clarification or if D2 saturation delays the target and other cases; decision quality may also suffer if handwriting is misread.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** D2's remaining queue is no shorter than D1's, D2 clarification probability is high for this handwriting, A2 is already saturated so earlier readiness does not change Assessment start, or D2 saturation causes larger delays to non-target cases.

### K-337
**Factual mechanism:** The case had two rework loops. Assessment 2 requested one more confirmation, which caused a weekend external wait, Document Check 3, Assessment 3, then final compliance and decision. The eventual decision used fully confirmed evidence.

**Minimal intervention:** After Assessment 2, stop the second Request More Info loop. Route the case to final compliance with the unresolved discrepancy explicitly documented, and require M1 to approve with conditions or reject.

**Temporal propagation:** Removing the second loop eliminates the external wait from Friday to Monday, Document Check 3, and Assessment 3. Final compliance can start after Assessment 2 rather than after Assessment 3, but it may take longer because evidence is unresolved. Decision timing then depends on C1 availability and M1 availability. The local cycle-time gain can be large, but it is not merely a subtraction: the decision policy and evidence sufficiency change.

**Process-feasibility check:** High-risk final compliance occurs after the latest relevant Assessment 2 and before Decision; A1 and C1 are different people; no second Request More Info loop occurs. The unresolved discrepancy must appear in the decision rationale, conditions, or rejection reason.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best case saves roughly 3-4.5 calendar days if C1 and M1 are available soon after Assessment 2. Base case saves roughly 1-3 days. Worst case saves little if C1 is backlogged and may worsen quality through appeal, loss exposure, or compliance defensibility if the unresolved discrepancy is material. The decision may be approve-with-conditions or reject rather than the factual approve.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** The unresolved discrepancy is material and cannot be safely conditioned; appeals or loss exposure rise; C1 or M1 is unavailable; final compliance requires the missing confirmation to be defensible; conditions create heavy post-decision monitoring.

### K-411
**Factual mechanism:** Legacy task creation created only the identity task, and a case lock prevented document task ownership until identity completed. Document check therefore started only after Identity, and later batch timestamps obscured actual work timing. Assessment then met a congested A2 queue.

**Minimal intervention:** At Triage, create both verification tasks and use a controlled workaround so I2 and D2 can work concurrently. Require real-time timestamps for task creation, ownership, start, and completion.

Assessment may become ready earlier, but A2 is the downstream bottleneck; earlier readiness may simply move the case into a longer A2 queue. **Process-feasibility check:** No originals are required, so Identity and Document Check can run concurrently. Both must finish before Assessment. **Best/base/worst case:** Best case is about 3-5 h earlier Notify if D2 starts earlier and A2 can absorb the case sooner. Base case is 0-3 h earlier because A2 queue absorbs most of the gain. Worst case is near zero benefit or a small delay if the workaround creates task-ownership errors, assessment bursts, or audit concerns. Decision quality should be unchanged; audit quality improves only if logging is accurate.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** D2 queue is already saturated so earlier task creation does not advance start; A2 wait increases enough to absorb the document gain; workaround timestamps are unreliable; or concurrent verification creates rework/ownership conflicts.

Across 100 similar cases per week, none of these local savings is additive. The K-104 policy is the clearest capacity trap: reserving C1 slots for many high-risk cases would turn the single certified compliance reviewer into a priority-queue bottleneck. Preliminary work helps only when packets are complete; otherwise C1 repeats work and urgent cases wait. K-219 substitution can relieve D1, but sending 100 handwritten standard cases to D2 can saturate D2, increase clarification loops, and leave D1 underused. The right rule is dynamic routing to the shorter expected completion time including rework, not blanket D2 routing. K-337 suppression avoids external waits and removes D1/A1 rework, but at scale it shifts risk from process time to decision quality: many unresolved discrepancies may become conditions or rejections, increasing appeals, loss exposure, and post-decision monitoring load. It also inserts compliance reviews earlier, competing with other C1 work. K-411 is operationally safer, but earlier verification creates a burst at the assessment queue; if A2 is already the bottleneck, most benefit is absorbed as longer assessment waits. Simultaneous identity/document work also raises short-term workload and can create false concurrency if real-time start/stop logging is not enforced. Weekend and external-response effects matter most for K-337 and K-219: avoided or induced rework often spans non-working periods, so calendar-time changes are larger than service-time changes. Queue forecasts should be treated as uncertain; pilot triggers should use observed queue length, C1 backlog, D2 clarification rate, and A2 wait time. With 100 cases, apply interventions selectively by queue state and risk, cap scarce-resource reservations, and monitor non-target queues; otherwise local wins can simply move WIP to the next bottleneck or into latent compliance risk.

| queue-spillover outcome | A2 wait/WIP and non-target assessment delays; short-term I2/D2 load. | D2 queue length, D1 queue length, rework queue, and non-target document delays. |
| logging validation | Compare real-time timestamps with ownership, resource, and task-event logs; sample audit; reject batch entries. | Verify Originals Receipt, D2 start/stop, clarification timestamps, rework, and resource exclusivity. |
| stopping rule | Stop if false concurrency >0, prerequisite breach occurs, A2 queue worsens beyond threshold, or no benefit after prespecified N. | Stop if D2 clarification/rework exceeds threshold, D2 queue spills beyond threshold, or cycle time worsens versus D1. |

## 7. Final synthesis

K-411 appears most robust. It changes only task creation and concurrency in a low-risk digital stream, preserves all hard prerequisites, and is easy to reverse. Its main weakness is that the assessment queue may absorb any verification savings, so it is a modest throughput and audit-quality intervention, not a guaranteed cycle-time fix.

K-337 is most dangerous despite potential speed. It is the only intervention that changes the allowed decision policy. Removing the second Request More Info loop can save days, but unresolved supplier discrepancies become conditions, rejections, appeals, loss exposure, or post-decision monitoring. That risk does not appear in a single-case counterfactual and can be systemic across 100 cases.

The K-337 policy is also least transportable from one case to system-wide policy, because whether an unresolved discrepancy can be conditionally approved depends on materiality, supplier behavior, sanctions/legal exposure, and appeal tolerance. K-104's C1 reservation is also non-transportable under tight capacity, but its mechanism is easier to pilot and reverse; K-337 changes the decision logic itself.

The most valuable additional data are real-time task and resource event logs, C1 arrival/backlog by risk tier, D2 clarification/rework rates, A2 queue wait and WIP, external-response time distributions, and decision outcomes (conditions, rejections, appeals, losses). These would let the organization test whether earlier readiness actually reaches decision, whether scarce C1 time is displaced, and whether avoided rework is replaced by latent quality cost.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. structural — The normative prerequisites, rework semantics, and four-eyes rule remain binding in all counterfactuals.
2. temporal — Resource service intervals can occur outside standard office hours when the factual timestamps already show such work.
3. resource — C1 is the only available final compliance reviewer and cannot perform two service intervals simultaneously.
4. resource — D2 queue and service estimates are forecasts, so D2 routing may or may not start earlier than D1.
5. behavioral — K-104's preliminary packet may be complete enough to reduce final C1 effort, but incompleteness is a material risk.
6. quality — K-337 approve-with-conditions or reject decisions must explicitly document the unresolved discrepancy and not silently ignore it.
7. interference — Single-case counterfactuals hold other system behavior approximately fixed except for the specified policy and its immediate resource use.
8. temporal — K-411 real-time timestamps reflect actual work and cannot be replaced by later batch-created task times.
