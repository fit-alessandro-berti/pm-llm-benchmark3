## 1. Temporal Data Semantics

I reconstruct state per case by sorting on `time:timestamp` ascending and then `event_index` ascending. An attribute cell is blank means “no update at this event”; it is not a null reset. Non-null updates are forward-filled within each case only. I never backfill from later events. The state at `Release_Funds` is the latest known value at the release event, including any update on the release event itself if present and, for same-timestamp events, only updates whose `event_index` is less than or equal to the release event’s index.

For equal timestamps, `event_index` is the tie-breaker: lower index occurs first. For TX-311, `Enhanced_Review` is event_index 3 and `Update_Jurisdiction` is event_index 4, so the jurisdiction change to DE occurs after the Enhanced_Review.

Material amount change under R4 is an `Update_Amount` where the new amount is known, the prior known amount is known, and the increase is greater than 20 percent. A decrease does not invalidate a review by itself. An increase from unknown prior amount is not safely classifiable.

Final case attributes are not copied backward. Settlement data, late exemption records, and post-release evidence are not automatically the release-time state. I distinguish:
- event time: when the logged event occurred;
- effective time: when a value/exemption is asserted to take effect;
- record time: when a database row was inserted;
- evidence time: when a signature, certificate, witness statement, or other proof was created or obtained.

Late records can corroborate earlier approval, but a displayed backdated field alone is not conclusive. Missing decisive amount, risk, jurisdiction, or customer type is not defaulted to low risk; it can force `INDETERMINATE`.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| R4 | Amount increase >20% after latest review | New applicable review for amount-dependent obligations | Material increase after review invalidates that review | Amount before and after change; review time | Decrease does not invalidate; unknown prior amount not safely classifiable | Unknown amount/prior amount => `INDETERMINATE` | Amount before/after, review timestamp |
| R5 | Exemption claimed | Head_Compliance approval, scope match, effective/expiry | Effective_from <= trigger; expires >= trigger; evidence supports approval at trigger | Trigger time | None | Unknown amount/scope => cannot establish validity | Signature, certificate, record time, witness independence |

Concise pseudo-logic:

```text
Before(e, f) := time(e) < time(f) OR (time(e)=time(f) AND event_index(e) < event_index(f)).

StateAt(c, attr, e) := latest non-null update u for attr in case c such that
    time(u) < time(e) OR (time(u)=time(e) AND event_index(u) <= event_index(e)).

MaterialAmountIncrease(m) :=
    m is Update_Amount AND amount_new known AND amount_old known
    AND (amount_new - amount_old)/amount_old > 0.20.

ValidReview(c, ReviewType, e) :=
    exists r of ReviewType in c with Before(r, e)
    AND NOT exists m with MaterialAmountIncrease(m)
        AND Before(r, m) AND Before(m, e).

R1(e):
    if StateAt(jurisdiction) != DE AND StateAt(amount) >= 100000:
        require exists r = Enhanced_Review with ValidReview(c, Enhanced_Review, e)
        OR exists valid exemption x at e matching scope and waiving R1.

R2(e):
    if StateAt(risk)=HIGH OR StateAt(customer_type)=PEP:
        require exists er = Enhanced_Review with Before(er, e)
        AND exists ca = Compliance_Approve with Before(er, ca) AND Before(ca, e).

R3(e):
    let s = latest Sanctions_Screen before e.
    if exists u = Sanctions_List_Update with
        Before(s, u) AND Before(u, e)
        AND u.jurisdiction = StateAt(jurisdiction, e):
        require exists s2 = Sanctions_Screen with Before(u, s2) AND Before(s2, e).

R4:
    if MaterialAmountIncrease(m) and Before(review, m) and Before(m, e):
        review is invalid for amount-dependent obligations at e.

R5(exemption x at trigger e):
    valid iff approved_by=Head_Compliance
    AND effective_from <= e
    AND expires >= e
    AND scope matches StateAt(jurisdiction), StateAt(customer_type), StateAt(amount)
    AND evidence supports approval existed by e.

R6:
    if decisive amount/risk/jurisdiction/customer type is missing and not resolved:
        classify INDETERMINATE.

R7:
    all temporal comparisons use Before(e, f) with event_index tie-break.
```

## 4. Ambiguities and Alternative Readings

TX-305 is the clear `INDETERMINATE` case. If an immutable release payload or contemporaneous review record showed the release amount was < EUR 100,000, R1 would not trigger and the case could become compliant. If it showed amount >= EUR 100,000 and the Enhanced_Review was valid for that amount, R1 would likely be satisfied. If amount >= EUR 100,000 but the review did not cover amount, a stricter reading could still make it noncompliant. The settlement amount of 150,000 at 2026-04-07 08:00 is reliable at settlement time, but there is no immutable release payload, so it does not safely establish the release-time amount.

TX-302 depends on exemption evidence quality. I treat EX-77 as valid because of the qualified signature timestamp at 09:05 and documented outage explaining the 11:00 record insert. However, even if EX-77 were rejected, TX-302 still has an Enhanced_Review at 09:20 before release, so R1 remains satisfied unless one argues the Enhanced_Review was invalid for another reason. If the Enhanced_Review did not actually cover GB/CORPORATE/180k, the result could change.

TX-310 is contradictory. I classify it NONCOMPLIANT because R5 requires evidence that approval existed at the relevant time, and the qualified signing certificate was created at 10:30, after the 10:00 release. The late record and backdated PDF are not conclusive. If an independent, credible Head_Compliance approval record before 10:00 were produced, and the scope matched AE/CORPORATE/<=250k, the case could become `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`. If the only contrary evidence is the requester witness, a weaker evidentiary standard might yield `INDETERMINATE`.

TX-311 is compliant, but it is useful for alternative readings. At release, current jurisdiction is DE because event_index 3 precedes event_index 4. If one evaluated R1 using pre-update US jurisdiction, R1 would trigger, but the Enhanced_Review at 16:20 still occurs after the amount update at 16:10 and before release at 16:30, so R1 would still be satisfied. If the jurisdiction update were instead retroactively effective before the Enhanced_Review, the release state would still be DE, so R1 would not trigger. Thus the classification is stable, but the same-timestamp ordering must be stated.

TX-308 changes if one reads R1’s “after latest material data change” as including any decrease. Under R4, a decrease does not by itself invalidate a review, so I treat the 14:20 Enhanced_Review as valid. If a reviewer treated any amount change as a new material data change requiring fresh review, TX-308 would become noncompliant. Similarly, if the threshold were “more than 100,000” rather than “at least 100,000,” the 100,000 release would not trigger R1.

TX-309 is less ambiguous but depends on the exact 20 percent calculation. The increase 118,000145,000 is approximately 22.88%, which is over 20%, so the 15:25 Enhanced_Review is invalidated for amount-dependent R1. If the rule used absolute amount change or a different base, the result could differ, but the stated R4 makes it noncompliant.

Control-flow-only checks above are intentionally coarse. They can tell whether an activity type appeared before release, but they cannot evaluate whether the review was valid for the current amount, whether an exemption was evidenced by the right approval time, whether a sanctions list update activated R3, or whether a >20 percent amount increase after review invalidated the earlier review.

## 6. Why Control-Flow-Only Conformance Is Insufficient

1. Final-state leakage: copying the final amount, risk, jurisdiction, or customer type backward can wrongly make earlier release events appear compliant or noncompliant.

2. Post-hoc exemptions: an exemption record inserted after release may be valid evidence of earlier approval, but only if signature, certificate, scope, effective time, and provenance support it. Control flow sees only `Record_Exemption` after `Release_Funds` and cannot decide validity.

3. Material changes after review: a review activity can occur before release and still be invalid if amount increased by more than 20 percent after that review. Control flow alone cannot represent amount-dependent invalidation.

4. Conditional activation: rules activate only under data conditions, such as foreign high-value, HIGH risk, PEP status, or sanctions-list update. The same trace shape can be compliant or noncompliant depending on attributes.

5. Missing data: missing amount, risk, jurisdiction, or customer type can make an obligation impossible to establish or exclude. Control-flow conformance often silently treats missing as absent or low risk.

6. Same-timestamp ordering: `Enhanced_Review` and `Update_Jurisdiction` can share a timestamp. `event_index` changes whether the jurisdiction change is before or after the review. Control-flow-only tools may lose or ignore this tie-break.

7. Effective time versus event time: an approval may be event-logged late but effective earlier, or displayed as backdated without reliable evidence. Control-flow order does not capture this distinction.

8. Sanctions-list update semantics: R3 requires a new screen after the list update, not merely the presence of any screen before release. The list update may apply only to a specific jurisdiction and may occur without changing the trace shape.

## 7. Recommended Logging and Control Improvements

- Log immutable release payloads containing the exact amount, jurisdiction, customer type, risk level, exemption reference, and rule version used at release.
- Record separate timestamps for event_time, effective_from, effective_to, record_time, and signature/evidence time for all approvals and exemptions.
- Store exemption scope atomically: jurisdiction, customer type, amount minimum/maximum, waived rules, currency, and approval authority.
- Add change provenance for every attribute update: source system, operator, reason, prior value, new value, and whether the change is material for each rule.
- Use a stable global event ordering key, not just timestamp, and make same-timestamp ordering explicit and auditable.
- Log sanctions-list updates with jurisdiction, list version, effective time, and the screening version used for release.
- Record review coverage: which attributes and amounts the review actually considered, and whether it is Standard_Review or Enhanced_Review.
- Store rule engine decisions with inputs, rule version, activated conditions, missing-value flags, and exception determinations.
- Require independent evidence for oral or late-recorded approvals; capture approver identity, channel, certificate, and witness independence.
- Add data-quality gates that block release or force human review when decisive amount, risk, jurisdiction, customer type, or exemption evidence is missing.
