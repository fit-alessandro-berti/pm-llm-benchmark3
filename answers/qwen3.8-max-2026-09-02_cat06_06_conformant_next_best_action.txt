## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

The key tradeoff is not simply “avoid the 90-minute breach.” The case has three qualitatively different harm classes:

1. **Deadline / operational loss**
   - Executing within the deadline is worth only €20k–€35k.
   - This benefit is conditional: the payment must be lawfully approved and executed in time.
   - The ultimate approval probability after conformant review is only 0.55–0.75, and the prediction is observational. Under intervention, the probability may change.
   - Therefore, the expected deadline benefit of any conformant path is substantially below the headline €20k–€35k.

2. **False rejection or prolonged hold**
   - Estimated customer harm is €15k–€80k.
   - This matters for Actions E and F especially.
   - Action E almost guarantees a missed deadline and may create prolonged-hold harm.
   - Action F avoids prolonged hold but may crystallize false-rejection harm if the rejection threshold is not truly satisfied.

3. **Regulatory / compliance harm from unlawful execution**
   - Estimated €100k–€500k plus non-monetary consequences.
   - This is highly asymmetric and includes reputational, audit, remediation, and potential supervisory consequences.
   - It dominates the €20k–€35k deadline benefit whenever an action creates a nonconformant execution path.

Dominance argument:

- Actions A and B offer at most €20k–€35k of apparent operational benefit.
- They create direct mandatory-control violations and potential regulatory exposure of €100k–€500k plus non-monetary harm.
- Even if the probability of severe regulatory consequence were uncertain, the control violation is immediate and the forecasted benefit is small relative to the downside.
- Therefore, Actions A and B are dominated by conformant second-review strategies and cannot be chosen merely because a predictive model forecasts lower SLA breach probability.

Among conformant or conditionally conformant options:

- **Action C** is preferred because it is the only immediate path to the mandatory independent review needed for a valid approval within the deadline window.
- **Action D** is valuable as an uncertainty-reduction overlay, especially because the current risk score is 12 minutes old and the fuzzy match is unresolved. But it is not a substitute for the second review and can become harmful if it delays the review.
- **Action E** is safe but slow; it is better as a fallback when no timely conformant decision path exists.
- **Action F** is a risk-control exit, but only if the rejection threshold is already documented and satisfied.
- **Action G** is not currently an enabled path; it should not consume the deadline unless the override conditions are realistically attainable.

## 5. Resource spillovers

**S2 versus S4**

- **S2** is immediately available but only by interrupting P-901. This gives P-884 the best chance of a conformant decision before the 90-minute deadline.
- The cost is a 0.12–0.28 increase in P-901 breach probability. P-901 is also high-value and has a 70-minute deadline, so this is not a trivial side effect.
- **S4** avoids harming P-901 but is expected in 35–65 minutes. If S4’s review takes a similar amount of time to S2’s, many S4 paths will miss the P-884 deadline.

The choice depends on the relative expected harm:

- If P-901’s expected breach harm is modest relative to P-884’s chance of on-time conformant execution, S2 remains attractive.
- If P-901 is extremely critical or its breach consequences are likely to exceed P-884’s net benefit, the S4 path becomes more attractive despite the higher probability of missing P-884’s deadline.

**Effect of S4 availability timing**

- If S4 becomes available in **35 minutes**:
  - The S4 path becomes much more credible.
  - If S4 can complete review and decision by roughly t=75–80, Action D with S4 may move above Action C, especially if P-901 spillover is considered unacceptable.
  - The ranking could change to: D/S4 first, C second.
- If S4 becomes available in **65 minutes**:
  - The S4 path is unlikely to preserve the deadline.
  - Action C remains the primary option if the organization wants any realistic conformant chance of meeting the 90-minute deadline.
  - S4 becomes a fallback for compliance rather than a deadline-saving option.

**Alternate-screening API quota**

- Only three expedited API calls remain today.
- Using one call is justified if it materially reduces uncertainty or prevents a false approval/false rejection.
- The best use is parallel with S2 review, where it does not consume decision time.
- A sequential screening-then-review path is less attractive because it may consume the deadline without removing the need for second review.

**Risk of consuming time while waiting for an exception**

- Action G can consume 20–60 minutes or more without producing a valid override.
- If the organization spends the deadline window chasing an override that is not enabled, it may lose both the chance for a timely conformant review and the chance to choose a controlled fallback.
- Therefore, G should not replace C or D unless director authorization and incident creation are already highly likely.
