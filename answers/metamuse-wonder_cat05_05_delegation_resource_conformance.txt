## 2. Rule Interpretation

**RR1 — Drafter/approver separation.** For each case, let *H(Draft)* and *H(Approve)* be the **alias-normalised humans**. Violation iff *H(Draft) = H(Approve)*. Risk level does not exempt a case: the rule as stated applies to every case, though severity is graded by risk. Service-account events are excluded from automatic SoD credit and assessed manually.

**RR2 — Implementer/verifier separation.** Violation iff *H(Implement) = H(Verify)* after normalisation. Where the verifier event is `svc_qms`, RR2 is **indeterminate**, not passed: weak attribution (CHG-503) cannot be used either to clear or to convict; strong attribution (CHG-509) may be used analytically with a manual-confirmation flag.

**RR3 — High-risk approval authority.** For `risk = HIGH` and `emergency = false`, *Approve_Change* is conformant iff the actor, at the event instant, is (a) Asha (standing QM), or (b) a delegate under a delegation where **all** of the following hold simultaneously:
- event timestamp  delegation effective interval (inclusive, UTC);
- case scope  delegation scope (scope matching is literal: "production-equipment" does **not** cover `cooling_system` or `training_document`);
- the principal-absence/unavailability condition, where the delegation imposes one, is evidenced **for that date**;
- the actor is the named delegate personally (identity evidence, not merely account use);
- no prohibited subdelegation is in the chain.
A low database-record timestamp does not invalidate a delegation where a signed instrument predates first use (see "later database entry" below); conversely, a delegate whose *account* was used while the human was demonstrably elsewhere/on sick leave and denies acting cannot be credited.

**RR4 — Emergency substitution.** For `risk = HIGH, emergency = true`:
- Initial approval may be by the IC (Diego, from 2026-07-01) or a valid IC delegate acting within incident scope and interval, **provided** the emergency flag and safety rationale are credible (accepted here at face value; credibility review is a manual step).
- A **Retrospective_QM_Review by the QM or a valid QM delegate** (RR3 delegation tests applied, including scope) must occur **within two business days** of the emergency approval/implementation.
- **Business-day counting**: business days are Mon–Fri excluding site holidays; 2026-08-15/16 were weekend days and there were no holidays. The day of the emergency event is day 0; the next business day is day 1; the deadline is end of day (23:59 UTC) on business day 2. Example: emergency approval Tuesday 2026-08-11  deadline Thursday 2026-08-13 23:59. A review on Friday 2026-08-14 is **late**.
- An invalid or late retrospective review means the emergency exception fails as a whole; the initial IC approval's validity does not cure it.

**RR5 — Identity evidence.** Aliases are collapsed before SoD checks. A service account is not a human; conformance credit requires reliable session-to-human mapping. Unknown operator = indeterminate, not conformant.

**RR6 — Suspicion versus proof.** Pair frequencies, reciprocity, shared workstations, and handover concentration are **indicators only**. They justify investigation, never a misconduct finding by themselves.

**Action by a principal while on leave.** Leave is an availability fact, not a role revocation. Asha's own approval while on leave is an act of the QM and is **authorised** if she personally acted; the delegation to Ben continues in parallel (a principal resuming activity does not automatically revoke a delegation, though plants should define a hand-back). The live question for CHG-504 is identity (credential sharing cannot be excluded), not authority.

**Can a later database entry prove an earlier delegation?** A database *creation* timestamp proves when the record was entered, not when the delegation was granted. Where a signed PDF (2026-08-09 18:00) predates first use, the delegation's existence from its stated effective date is accepted and the 2026-08-12 DB entry is treated as migration lag — a records-quality defect, not retroactive fabrication on this evidence. Absent the signed PDF, a post-hoc DB record alone would be insufficient and the approvals would be indeterminate-to-unauthorised.

## 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

Per RR6, none of the following is proof of collusion. Each indicator has a benign and an adverse explanation; the task is to identify discriminating evidence.

**Indicator 1 — Repeated ClaraBen pair (18 of 23 HIGH-risk production changes in 60 days; Asha approved only 3).**
- *Benign:* Clara is the designated drafter for a production cell and Ben is the standing QM delegate during Asha's leave-heavy period; no pair-rotation policy exists, so concentration is an organisational artefact. Most of these approvals (like CHG-501/503) are individually valid under D-QM-01.
- *Adverse:* A captured approval channel in which a friendly delegate routinely waves through one drafter's HIGH-risk changes, with the principal marginalised.
- *Discriminating evidence:* Approval quality, not frequency — rejection/challenge rates, comment depth, cycle time (e.g., 30-minute draft-to-approval in CHG-503), whether Ben ever rejected or conditioned Clara's changes versus others; Asha's leave pattern across the full 60 days (was she absent for all 18? If she was present and bypassed, the benign explanation collapses); change outcomes/incidents traceable to the pair's approvals.

**Indicator 2 — Reciprocal pattern (Ben drafted four LOW-risk training changes that Clara approved).**
- *Benign:* Small team, mutual cover for routine low-risk paperwork; no financial benefit known; LOW risk throughout.
- *Adverse:* Reciprocal favour-trading ("you approve mine, I approve yours") normalising mutual non-challenge, potentially extensible to higher-risk work.
- *Discriminating evidence:* Whether reciprocity stays confined to LOW risk; whether either ever challenged the other's drafts; whether Clara's approvals of Ben's drafts show the same speed/shallowness signature; note CHG-508 shows Ben self-approving via alias in the same training-document space, which makes this cluster worth a closer look without proving intent.

**Indicator 3 — Shared workstation / service-account session (CHG-503, WS-DM-04).**
- *Benign:* Shared terminals with persistent service-account sessions are a known plant-floor pattern; Diego badged in for an unrelated reason and someone else verified.
- *Adverse:* The implementer verified his own implementation under a generic account, defeating RR2 invisibly.
- *Discriminating evidence:* Application-level audit trails (user actions within the session), badge records for all personnel in the room, shift roster of qualified verifiers present at 12:30 on 2026-08-12, terminal login/logout artefacts.

**Indicator 4 — Absence conflicts and account use (Ben's account active on sick leave, CHG-506; Asha active on leave, CHG-504).**
- *Benign:* (a) Ben briefly worked despite a sick note; (b) Asha genuinely interrupted leave — her VPN window precisely brackets her approval and she claims it, which supports authenticity.
- *Adverse:* Credential sharing or credential compromise in both directions — accounts used as transferable authority tokens. The two cases differ: Asha *claims* her act and the session evidence fits her claim; Ben *denies* his and the office IP contradicts his location. CHG-506 is therefore the serious credential-integrity event; CHG-504 is a lower-grade uncertainty.
- *Discriminating evidence:* MFA/device-bound authentication records, workstation badge-in logs, whether approvals' content required knowledge only the account-holder had, password-reset/helpdesk tickets, and any pattern of `u_bk` use at other anomalous times.

**Indicator 5 — Concentrated handovers (ClaraDiegoEva chain in nearly every case; Diego implements everything).**
- *Benign:* Role design: one Maintenance Lead implements, one verifier verifies. Concentration reflects staffing, not collusion.
- *Adverse:* A closed chain with no independent challenge at any step, where the same three humans shepherd every change.
- *Discriminating evidence:* Segregation is formally satisfied (RR2 passes wherever identities are known); the question is substantive independence — verifier findings rates, rework, post-implementation incidents per implementer/verifier pair compared with plant baselines.

**Net position:** The *proven* problems are authority and identity failures (CHG-506, 507, 510, 505-retrospective, and SoD breaches in 502/508). The *collusion* hypothesis currently rests on frequency alone, which RR6 forbids treating as proof, and is substantially explained by leave cover and cell assignment. Credential misuse (CHG-506) is a more concrete and more serious line of inquiry than pair collusion.

## 6. Control Improvements

1. **Delegation creation & registry as system of record:** delegations must be created in the QMS workflow *before* first use, with the signed instrument attached; late database entry (D-QM-01) should trigger an automatic records-quality deviation even where a signed PDF cures validity. Registry entries must include machine-readable scope, interval, absence conditions, and an explicit subdelegation prohibition flag that the workflow enforces (which would have blocked D-IC-04 automatically).
2. **Effective-time recording:** store grant time, effective-from/to, registration time, and migration provenance as separate fields; approval workflows must evaluate authority against *effective* time at run time, not record-creation time, and block approvals where no covering delegation object exists.
3. **Principal availability:** delegations conditioned on absence must be linked to the HR/roster system so the condition is checked at approval time (CHG-509 would have been blocked or escalated for an absence declaration for 2026-08-15). Define principal hand-back: when a principal resumes activity (CHG-504), the system should prompt explicit revocation or continuation of the delegation.
4. **Alias management:** one human = one ID; retire legacy aliases (`b.koch`), enforce identity resolution in the event log at write time, and run the normalised four-eyes check — not the raw-ID check — in monitoring (CHG-508).
5. **Individual authentication:** prohibit shared service accounts for regulated verification/approval steps, or require individual re-authentication (badge + FIDO) bound to the session before any GxP-relevant action — closing both the CHG-503 unattributable verification and the CHG-506 denied account use. Alert in real time when an account is used while its holder is on certified leave (sick or annual) or from an inconsistent location.
6. **Emergency review control:** the emergency (IC) route must auto-create a retrospective-QM-review task with a two-business-day countdown, scope-checked assignee (QM or in-scope delegate — which would have caught the cooling-system/production-equipment scope mismatch in CHG-505 and CHG-510), and escalation to the Site Director before expiry. An expired emergency exception should block case closure.
7. **Pair rotation & substantive independence:** introduce drafter/approver pair rotation or randomised approver assignment for HIGH-risk changes, with dashboards tracking concentration, approval cycle time, and challenge/rejection rates per pair — monitored as quality indicators, with defined thresholds for review rather than automatic suspicion (consistent with RR6).
8. **Network monitoring:** run the handover/working-together analyses periodically on normalised identities; investigate outliers (e.g., a pair handling >X% of HIGH-risk approvals) by sampling approval *quality*, not by inferring intent from frequency.
9. **Evidential retention:** retain badge, VPN/MFA, workstation, and session logs correlated to case IDs for the record retention period; shared terminals in GxP areas need CCTV or equivalent session attribution coverage; protocol deviations (CHG-503) should have an audit-trail reconstruction performed while artefacts still exist.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

Of the ten cases, the apparent nonconformance decomposes as follows. **Genuine authority failures** are the largest category: CHG-507 (HIGH approval by someone with no QM authority), CHG-510 (void subdelegation, self-approval, and no valid QM retrospective), CHG-505 (emergency approval valid but retrospective review both late and out of delegated scope), and the RR1 self-approvals in CHG-502 and CHG-508 — five cases where the control objective itself, not merely the data, failed. **Identity/data-quality problems** account for CHG-503 (unverifiable verification step on a HIGH-risk case), CHG-506 (QM-delegate approval by an unidentified user of a sick employee's account — the single most serious integrity event), and the identity caveats in CHG-504 and CHG-509, the last of which remains **indeterminate** on the absence condition. **Only CHG-501 is fully clean** (a valid substitution, once the signed delegation PDF is weighed over the migrated database timestamp), and CHG-504 is authorised subject to identity confidence. The recurring Clara–Ben pairing is, on current evidence, adequately explained by cell assignment and Asha's leave cover, and per RR6 it is not evidence of collusion — but the near-total absence of documented challenge in that channel is an organisational-pattern risk worth monitoring. Uncertainty is concentrated in identity attribution (service accounts, shared credentials) rather than in the authority analysis, which is mostly determinable from intervals and scopes.

**Three highest-priority investigations:**
1. **CHG-506 credential compromise** — identify who used Ben Koch's account from an office workstation during certified sick leave to approve a HIGH-risk change; preserve workstation, badge, and MFA logs immediately and assess whether other `u_bk` approvals are affected.
2. **CHG-510 / CHG-505 emergency-route integrity** — reconstruct the CI-44 emergency, determine how a prohibited subdelegation came to be relied upon and why the scope-mismatched, late retrospective reviews were accepted, and review all emergency changes in the period for the same pattern.
3. **CHG-503 verification attribution** — attempt audit-trail reconstruction of the `svc_qms` session before artefacts age out; if the verifier cannot be established, the HIGH-risk change CHG-503 lacks a credible verification record and should be retrospectively assessed for product/process impact.
