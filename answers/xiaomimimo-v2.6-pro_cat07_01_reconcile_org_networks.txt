# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

Before the network-by-network reading, one structural fact frames everything: the funnel has **two routing tiers**. `Bot_Ingest  Bot_Rules` (4,040 edges, 10 s) is a fully automated front door; `Bot_Rules` is the algorithmic triage router (betweenness 0.41); `Mira_Coordinator` is the *second-tier* human exception router. She therefore sits downstream of automation and upstream of the entire human organization. Several of her numbers should be read as properties of that architectural position, not of her as an individual.

### Handover-of-work network
**What it measures.** Directed, event-adjacency-based work passing: resource *i*'s event is immediately followed in the same case by resource *j*'s event. It captures *sequential task dependency and transfer of custody*.

**What it implies for Mira.** Out-degree 0.82 (highest), in-degree 0.51, betweenness 0.77. She is the universal sender and a conduit on most multi-human paths. Her in-edges come from automation (`Bot_Rules` 920), temps (190 + 170) and specialists returning answers (`Omar` 160, `Priya` 140); her out-edges spread across all four adjusters (1,250), both specialists (390) and both team leads implicitly via subcontracting. The two teams are structurally disconnected modules joined by one human node.

**What it does not imply.** It does not measure collaboration intensity, authority, competence or decision quality. Edge counts are inflated by any short logged touch (a 4.2-minute "Assign Specialist" event creates exactly as much edge mass as a 37-minute medical review); they scale with case length and with event granularity (`Bot_Rules` has 4,750 events over 4,090 cases, suggesting duplicate `lifecycle:transition` rows). ### Working-together network
**What it measures.** Undirected **case-set overlap**: in how many cases do both resources appear at all. It measures *breadth of shared case population / co-ownership*, not effort, sequence or intensity.

**What it implies for Mira.** Her ties are broad and shallow (4–8% with everyone), versus 20–27% for the team cores. This is the classic signature of a **broker with distributed, non-redundant ties**, not of a social isolate: she co-appears weakly with *many* groups rather than strongly with one.

**Two calibration caveats are essential here.**
1. **Coverage arithmetic.** The metric is bounded by each partner's case coverage. Mira touches 905/4,100 cases  22%, so 22% is her *ceiling* with anyone; her touches are also split across 9+ partners. - **Future predictions:** Queue length and pre-touch waiting time on exception cases rise superlinearly with her weekly event load; return-time distribution to her is bimodal; granting delegate permissions during absences recovers part of performance (say 30–50%) while a cross-trained successor recovers the remainder more slowly (learning curve); specialist dispatch latency (42/51 min) dominates tail cycle time once her queue is cleared.

### H3. Boundary spanner / triage broker (structural holes)
- **Mechanism:** She connects otherwise decoupled groups — Team North, Team South, medical, legal, temps, vendors, and the automation. Brokers are characterized by high betweenness, low clustering and many weak cross-group ties; they intermediate information rather than co-produce work.
- **Evidence for:** No `Alice  Bruno` and no cross-team handover edges exist except via her and `Bot_Rules` — the two teams are disconnected modules; her out-edges reach *every* group; her working-together ties are broad-but-shallow (lift 1.4–1.7×) which is precisely the broker profile; specialists return answers to her rather than to the requester (she owns the cross-specialty translation); her subcontracting loops cross organizational boundaries (internal  external `Vendor_Verify` via `Temp_1`).
- **Evidence against / limits:** Brokerage may be an artifact of *process design* (she is the only permitted routing node) rather than personal structural capital — the same graph would arise for any occupant of the seat. The working-together metric is coverage-confounded (Section 1), so "peripherality" is partly arithmetic. Brokers can also *hoard* information; the log cannot distinguish enabling brokerage from gatekeeping.
- **Future predictions:** Node-removal simulation on the handover graph raises mean path length and fragments the network into 2–3 components (North, South, specialists); inter-team handover counts collapse without her; if the function is seat-based, a successor reaches similar centrality within 6–10 weeks; if it is personal, the successor's betweenness stays low and inter-team flows route through formal escalation instead.

### H4. Informal team lead / shadow case-disposition authority
- **Mechanism:** Nominal status ("just a queue cleaner") is contradicted by de facto control over reopening, complaint escalation and specialist selection — i.e., case *disposition* without case *approval*. Status inconsistency plus exclusive control of a gatekeeping step produces informal authority and unlogged discretion.
- **Evidence for:** 71% of complaints and 64% of reopens through her; she is the only resource performing `Assign Specialist` — **an activity absent from the official 13-activity taxonomy**, which suggests either an off-model process step or an informal practice that has entered the log; the compliance reviewer's detect-and-dispose concern; the testimonial split (management minimizes her, adjusters credit her) is typical of invisible informal authority.
- **Evidence against / limits:** **No direct evidence of wrongdoing or self-benefit.** Crucially, the financial control chain is separate from her: she cannot approve settlements (`Alice`/`Bruno`) and cannot release payment (`Vendor_Pay`, in-degree 0.26 and out-degree 0.00 — she is not on the payment path). Assignment volumes are balanced (Carla 350 / Diego 330 / Elise 290 / Farid 280), showing no obvious favoritism. Discretion over routing is normal and often desirable in exception handling.
- **Future predictions:** Decision mining on `Assign Specialist` shows *low* predictability from documented attributes and *stable personal affinities* (e.g., a persistent preference for one specialist or one senior adjuster), plus day-of-week/queue-state effects; similar cases receive divergent dispositions in sampled review; unexplained routing variance survives attribute control.

### H5. Automation supervisor / human-in-the-loop fallback for `Bot_Rules`
- **Mechanism:** She is the manual fallback layer of the rules engine: `Bot_Rules` triages and fraud-screens, its exceptions land on her, and she corrects or updates the automated decision (the `Fraud Screen  manual exception  Fraud Screen update` loop).
- **Evidence for:** `Bot_Rules  Mira` is the largest human-received edge (920); subcontracting loop with `Bot_Rules` (62); `Bot_Rules` carries a 0.31 exception share, the highest of the bots; automation's betweenness (0.41) is second only to hers (0.77) — the two form a cybernetic routing pair.
- **Evidence against / limits:** 920 of ~4,750 bot events is ~19% — a minority of bot output; her portfolio includes reopens and complaints outside bot scope; the loop frequency (62) is small relative to her 960 events. Her centrality would survive bot removal from the graph (the teams are still disconnected).
- **Future predictions:** Tightening bot thresholds raises her exception intake within days; bot rule releases shift her case mix measurably; cases where she overrides bot triage show different downstream outcomes (fraud yield, reopen rate) than bot-consistent cases.

### H6. Data artifact / logging proxy (the null-ish hypothesis)
- **Mechanism:** Some of her "events" are system or administrative touches recorded under her user ID (queue assignment, shared desk account, delegation logging), inflating handover edges and betweenness; conversely, real coordination happens off-log (phone/chat), deflating working-together.
- **Evidence for:** 1.06 events per case is an abnormally single-touch profile; the non-taxonomy activity `Assign Specialist`; naming drift elsewhere (`Fraud Screen update`, "Approve?" fragments in subcontracting patterns); the working-together percentages are inconsistent with the distinct-case counts (27% > 16% coverage), indicating a normalization/denominator problem somewhere in the pipeline; bot event counts exceed case counts (4,750 / 4,090), suggesting duplicated `lifecycle:transition` rows.
- **Evidence against / limits:** The absence experiment shows a real operational effect (though explainable by entitlements rather than work); both management and adjusters independently describe a substantive function.
- **Future predictions:** `lifecycle:transition` breakdown shows many `assign`/`schedule` transitions and few `complete` for her events; timestamps cluster at suspiciously round intervals; stripping non-work transitions drops her betweenness substantially but leaves the routing topology intact.

### H7. Cost/quality gatekeeper before expensive specialist use (added)
- **Mechanism:** She filters and redirects before committing costly specialist time: only 210/1,640 out-edges go to `Omar` and 180 to `Priya`; the rest go to adjusters, temps or back to automation.
- **Evidence for:** Specialists are the costliest resources (37/51 min) and see only 316/251 cases with the highest exception purity (0.62/0.71) — consistent with pre-filtering to genuinely hard cases.
- **Evidence against / limits:** Could equally be *under-referral*; low working-together with specialists (and `Omar–Priya` only 4%) hints at weak integration of specialist feedback into case handling.
- **Future predictions:** Referral rates to `Omar`/`Priya` are stable per `case_type` and lower than a rule-based benchmark; cases denied specialist review show higher reopen/complaint rates (measurable) — if so, the gate is over-filtering.

# 3. Risk assessment

All claims below are about **control design and structural exposure**. Nothing in the log evidences misconduct; network centrality is neither good nor bad by itself.

**Bottleneck risk — moderate-to-high for the exception tail, low for routine flow.** The absence diff-in-diff (routine flat, exceptions +65% at P90) localizes the constraint to the exception path. Caveats: the constraint may be *capacity* (one person), *entitlements* (one account), or *knowledge* (one head) — remediation differs; the tail is 22% of volume so systemic throughput is not at risk; and downstream latencies (`MiraOmar` 42 min, `MiraPriya` 51 min, vendor waiting time) are alternative tail drivers.

**Coordination risk — high.** Cross-team coordination is single-threaded: no `AliceBruno` edge exists outside her and `Bot_Rules`. Specialist answers return to her rather than to the requester, making the loop serial instead of parallel. Any absence, overload or misrouting breaks the only bridge between two otherwise modular teams and between internal staff and the two external vendors.

**Key-person risk — high (bus factor  1), and not limited to Mira.** Reopens (64%), complaints (71%) and all cross-team routing pass through one node. But the same report should flag `Omar_Medical`, `Priya_Legal` (each a single-resource function with the longest service times and the hardest case mix) and `Vendor_Pay` (terminal sink on the payment path, 0.93 case-count percentile). A remediation plan that protects only Mira leaves three other single points of failure.

**Compliance / control risk — moderate, design-level.** The compliance reviewer's concern maps to a classic **segregation-of-duties** issue: *problem detection* (exception identification, complaint escalation) and *disposition* (specialist selection, reopening) sit with one role. Important mitigating facts: she does **not** approve settlements and does **not** release payment — the money chain (`Alice`/`Bruno`  `Vendor_Pay`) is separate, so the exposure is *process discretion affecting policyholder outcomes and consistency of treatment*, not financial misappropriation. Regulated claims handling cares about timeliness and consistent, documented decisions; unlogged routing discretion weakens the audit trail even when used entirely in good faith. The right response is decision-logging and rule-based defaults with justified overrides — **not** suspicion of the individual.

**Workload fairness risk — low-to-moderate, ambiguous.** Headline allocation is balanced (350/330/290/280). But the *type* of work differs: her edges to juniors are labelled "rework/missing info" (290/280) versus "reassignment after missing info" to seniors (350/330), and `Temp_1`/`Temp_2` carry the lowest exception shares (0.13–0.14) while `Omar`/`Priya` carry 0.62/0.71. This could be sensible risk-based allocation (seniors get harder work), or systematic dumping of low-value/rework tasks onto juniors and temps. The log cannot distinguish intent; both readings must stay open. Note also that exception share per resource describes **case mix, not individual error rates**.

**Data-quality risk — moderate, and it conditions every other claim.** (i) `Assign Specialist` and "Fraud Screen update" are outside the declared activity taxonomy; (ii) working-together percentages are inconsistent with distinct-case counts (27% > 16% coverage)  normalization/denominator error; (iii) `Bot_Rules` 4,750 events / 4,090 cases suggests duplicated lifecycle rows inflating bot and handover edges; (iv) bot medians of 4–7 s vs. human minutes imply mixed timestamp granularity; (v) vendor "service time" of 1.3–2.1 h almost certainly conflates provider service with queue/waiting time; (vi) `exception_flag` is plausibly a case-level attribute, so per-resource exception shares measure case mix. Any centrality ranking is sensitive to (i)–(iii).

# 5. Recommendation

### Change immediately (low-cost, reversible, evidence-generating — no role restructuring)
1. **Make the routing decision observable.** Promote `Assign Specialist` into the official activity taxonomy with mandatory attributes: destination role, reason code, source of the rule (documented rule / override), and `exception_flag`. Today the single most consequential decision in the exception path is the least documented one. This costs nothing operationally and converts an opaque node into an auditable control.
2. **Write the routing matrix down.** Convert "she knows where hard cases should go" into a one-page decision table (`activity` × `case_type` × `amount_band` × `priority`  destination), with explicit override rules. This preserves her judgment while removing the bus factor, and it is also the cheapest compliance response to the segregation-of-duties concern.
3. **Create a standing delegate with real entitlements.** Cross-train one team lead plus one adjuster (or a second coordinator) on exception routing, and grant **system delegation rights** so that absences do not break the assignment path. This distinguishes and mitigates both the knowledge and the entitlement version of the key-person risk.
4. **Add four-eyes review only where exposure is real:** complaint escalations (`Escalate Complaint`, 71% concentration) and legal escalations (`Mira  Priya`) get a second sign-off or rule-based default destination with logged override justification. Do **not** extend this to routine adjuster reassignment — that would destroy the dispatch speed the absence experiment shows matters.
5. **Fix the data pipeline before trusting the rankings:** reconcile the working-together normalization, de-duplicate `lifecycle:transition` rows for `Bot_*`, and separate vendor service time from waiting time (see §3 data-quality risk).

### Monitor (with named thresholds and owners)
- Exception queue length and **P90 cycle time for `exception_flag=True`** (baseline 8.4 days; alert at > 10 days) — the metric that moved in the absence test.
- **Node concentration:** Mira's handover betweenness and share of reopens/complaints, plus equivalents for `Omar`, `Priya`, `Vendor_Pay` (all are single points of failure).
- **Dispatch latency** `Mira  Omar/Priya` (42/51 min) and specialist utilization — the likely next constraint once routing is fixed.
- **Routing fairness:** destination distribution by adjuster seniority and by `amount_band`/`priority`, and reopen rate after Mira-touch — to detect either favoritism *or* rework-dumping onto juniors/temps, without presuming either.
- **Automation-to-human escalation rate** (`Bot_Rules  Mira`) as a leading indicator of rule drift or tightening thresholds.
- **Loop depth** (`Mira  X  Mira`) and return-time bimodality, to detect whether delegation mode is shifting.

### Do not change until more evidence is available
- **Do not remove or redistribute the coordinator role.** The function — cross-team exception brokerage — is currently performed by exactly one node and the graph shows no substitute path (no `AliceBruno` edge exists). *If* management ever wants to consolidate it, the lost function must be explicitly replaced by: (i) an extended rules engine with a documented exception queue and rotation roster, (ii) a published routing matrix so `Bot_Rules` and team leads can substitute, (iii) a designated second-level reviewer for complaints/legal, and (iv) direct specialist-to-requester return paths so the loop is not serial through one person. Without all four, the operations manager's prediction (formal escalation inflation and specialist overload) is the most likely outcome — `Omar` and `Priya` are already single-resource functions at 37/51 minutes per event.
- **Do not fold her duties into the team leads.** That would erase the only cross-team bridge, tie exception handling to team silos, and make the segregation-of-duties problem *worse* (detection + routing + settlement approval in one role).
- **Do not automate her routing decisions yet.** `Bot_Rules` already generates 31% exceptions; a second algorithmic layer without documented reason codes would hide discretion rather than remove it.
- **Do not conclude non-compliance or misuse of discretion.** The evidence supports "unlogged discretion exists and should be controlled," not "discretion was abused." She is outside the settlement-approval and payment chains. Any fairness or conduct review should be framed as a control review with sampled case audits, not as an individual investigation.
- **Do not renegotiate vendor arrangements on the current service-time figures** until service and waiting time are separated (`Vendor_Verify` 2.1 h, `Vendor_Pay` 1.3 h).

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira_Coordinator is best understood as a cross-team exception router rather than a team lead. Handover and subcontracting networks place her at the centre of exception flow: the rules engine sends her hard cases, she touches each case briefly, and passes it to the right adjuster, specialist or vendor before leaving. That "touch-and-go" pattern explains the apparent contradiction — she is central to who hands work to whom, yet rarely co-owns a case long enough to register strongly in working-together ties, which are dominated by stable two-person teams. Her own work takes about four minutes per event, but a six-day absence raised tail cycle time for exception cases from 8.4 to 13.9 days while routine claims were unaffected, so her value lies in unblocking, not processing. Main risks: single-person dependency (no backup, no cross-team alternative), undocumented routing discretion, and unclear segregation of duties. We recommend documenting routing rules with reason codes, granting a trained delegate real system rights, and adding second review for complaint and legal escalations — not removing the role.
