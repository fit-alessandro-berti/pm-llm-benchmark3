## 1. Temporal Data Semantics

### State Reconstruction and Forward-Filling
Case attributes (`amount`, `risk_level`, `jurisdiction`, `customer_type`, `exemption_id`) are modeled as piecewise-constant step functions over an ordered event stream. For a case $c$, let its sequence of events be ordered by the total order $\prec$ defined below. The value of attribute $a$ at event $e_k$, denoted $\text{val}(a, e_k)$, is reconstructed via causal forward-filling (Last Observation Carried Forward):
$$\text{val}(a, e_k) = \begin{cases} \text{raw}(a, e_k) & \text{if } \text{raw}(a, e_k) \neq \emptyset \\ \text{val}(a, e_{k-1}) & \text{if } \text{raw}(a, e_k) = \emptyset \text{ and } k > 1 \\ \texttt{UNKNOWN} & \text{if } \text{raw}(a, e_k) = \emptyset \text{ and } k = 1 \end{cases}$$

### Handling Blank Cells and Missing Data
Blank cells ($\emptyset$) in the CSV represent the absence of a state mutation at that event, not a deletion or nullification of the attribute. However, if an attribute is omitted at `Create_Transaction` and remains unpopulated through a control gate (e.g., `TX-305`), its state is $\texttt{UNKNOWN}$ ($\bot$). Under Three-Valued Logic (Kleene $K_3$), comparisons involving $\bot$ (such as $\bot \ge 100,000$) evaluate to $\texttt{UNKNOWN}$ unless resolved by invariant domain rules, preventing silent defaults to low-risk states (R6).

### Ordering Equal Timestamps
Events within a case are ordered by the strict total order $e_i \prec e_j \iff (t(e_i) < t(e_j)) \lor (t(e_i) = t(e_j) \land \text{idx}(e_i) < \text{idx}(e_j))$, where $t(e)$ is `time:timestamp` and $\text{idx}(e)$ is `event_index`. When two events share a timestamp, `event_index` establishes causal sequence. For example, in `TX-311`, `Enhanced_Review` ($\text{idx}=3$) precedes `Update_Jurisdiction` ($\text{idx}=4$) at `16:20:00Z`. The review evaluates the state at $\text{idx}=3$ (`US`), while `Release_Funds` ($\text{idx}=5$) evaluates the state mutated at $\text{idx}=4$ (`DE`).

### Identifying Material Changes
A data change is *material* relative to a review if it invalidates the assumptions under which that review was performed:
1. **Amount (R4):** For any review $e_r \in \{\text{Standard\_Review}, \text{Enhanced\_Review}\}$ where $\text{val}(\text{amount}, e_r) = A_r$, a subsequent event $e_m \succ e_r$ with $\text{val}(\text{amount}, e_m) = A_m$ is a material amount increase iff $\frac{A_m - A_r}{A_r} > 0.20$. Note that if an intermediate non-material update occurs, materiality is still measured against the baseline reviewed amount $A_r$, as well as any sequential step $> 20\%$. Decreases ($A_m < A_r$) are not material. Increases from $A_r = \bot$ are $\texttt{INDETERMINATE}$.
2. **Non-Amount Trigger Shifts (R1, R2):** A post-review change to `jurisdiction` (into a non-DE state while amount $\ge 100\text{k}$), `risk_level` (to `HIGH`), or `customer_type` (to `PEP`) constitutes a material change if it newly activates R1 or R2 or alters the risk profile after $e_r$. Conversely, a shift from `US` to `DE` (`TX-311`) deactivates the R1 trigger at release.

### Treatment of Final Attributes
Final case attributes (e.g., `Settlement_Confirmed` in `TX-305` or late `Record_Exemption` in `TX-302`/`TX-310`) are never back-propagated to earlier gates (R7). A post-release event may only serve as *epistemic evidence* of a prior state if corroborated by cryptographic or immutable external proof, never as a retroactive state mutation.

### Four-Time Taxonomy
To evaluate compliance accurately, we separate four temporal dimensions:
1. **Event Time ($t_{\text{event}}$):** When the operational activity occurred in the business process (mostly `time:timestamp`, except `Record_Exemption` which logs database insertion).
2. **Effective Time ($t_{\text{eff}}$):** When a policy status, attribute value, or exemption legally takes force (e.g., `EX-77` effective `09:00Z`).
3. **Record Time ($t_{\text{rec}}$):** When the fact was persisted to the system of record (e.g., `Record_Exemption` at `11:00Z` due to outage).
4. **Evidence Time ($t_{\text{evid}}$):** When verifiable, tamper-evident proof establishes the approval or state existed (e.g., qualified digital signature at `09:05Z` for `EX-77` vs. certificate creation at `10:30Z` for `EX-88`).

An exemption waives a gate at $t_{\text{rel}}$ iff $t_{\text{eff}} \le t_{\text{rel}}$ **and** $t_{\text{evid}} \le t_{\text{rel}}$ (proving approval existed prior to release, even if $t_{\text{rec}} > t_{\text{rel}}$).

### Formal Pseudo-Logic (R1–R7)

**Temporal Ordering Definitions:**
* $e_1 \prec e_2 \equiv (t(e_1) < t(e_2)) \lor (t(e_1) = t(e_2) \land \text{idx}(e_1) < \text{idx}(e_2))$ (Strict precedence: $e_1$ occurred before $e_2$).
* $e_1 \preceq e_2 \equiv (e_1 \prec e_2) \lor (e_1 = e_2)$.

**State Reconstruction (R6, R7):**
* $\text{val}(a, e) := \text{raw}(a, e_{\text{last}})$ where $e_{\text{last}} = \max_{\preceq} \{ e' \preceq e \mid \text{raw}(a, e') \neq \emptyset \}$, else $\bot$ if the set is empty.

**Material Change & Review Validity (R4):**
* $\text{MatAmtInc}(e_{\text{rev}}, e_{\text{rel}}) \equiv \exists e_u \in (e_{\text{rev}}, e_{\text{rel}}] : \text{amt}(e_u) > 1.20 \times \text{amt}(e_{\text{rev}})$. (If $\text{amt}(e_{\text{rev}}) = \bot \lor \text{amt}(e_u) = \bot$, evaluates to $\bot$).
* $\text{MatTrigShift}(e_{\text{rev}}, e_{\text{rel}}) \equiv \exists e_u \in (e_{\text{rev}}, e_{\text{rel}}] : (\text{risk}(e_u) = \text{'HIGH'} \land \text{risk}(e_{\text{rev}}) \neq \text{'HIGH'}) \lor (\text{cust}(e_u) = \text{'PEP'} \land \text{cust}(e_{\text{rev}}) \neq \text{'PEP'}) \lor (\text{jur}(e_u) \neq \text{'DE'} \land \text{jur}(e_{\text{rev}}) = \text{'DE'})$.
* $\text{ValidER}(e_{\text{er}}, e_{\text{rel}}) \equiv \text{type}(e_{\text{er}}) = \text{Enhanced\_Review} \land (e_{\text{er}} \prec e_{\text{rel}}) \land \neg \text{MatAmtInc}(e_{\text{er}}, e_{\text{rel}}) \land \neg \text{MatTrigShift}(e_{\text{er}}, e_{\text{rel}})$.

**Exemption Validity (R5):**
* $\text{ValidEx}(x, R_k, e_{\text{rel}}) \equiv \text{approver}(x) = \text{Head\_Compliance} \land \text{waives}(x, R_k) \land (t_{\text{eff}}(x) \le t(e_{\text{rel}}) < t_{\text{exp}}(x)) \land (t_{\text{evid}}(x) \le t(e_{\text{rel}})) \land \text{ jur}(e_{\text{rel}}) \in \text{Scope}_{\text{jur}}(x) \land \text{cust}(e_{\text{rel}}) \in \text{Scope}_{\text{cust}}(x) \land \text{amt}(e_{\text{rel}}) \le \text{Scope}_{\text{amt}}(x)$.

**Obligation Rules (R1–R3):**
* $\text{Trig}_{R1}(e_{\text{rel}}) \equiv (\text{jur}(e_{\text{rel}}) \neq \text{'DE'}) \land (\text{amt}(e_{\text{rel}}) \ge 100,000)$.
* $\text{Sat}_{R1}(e_{\text{rel}}) \equiv \text{Trig}_{R1}(e_{\text{rel}}) \implies \big( \exists e_{\text{er}} : \text{ValidER}(e_{\text{er}}, e_{\text{rel}}) \big) \lor \big( \exists x : \text{ValidEx}(x, R_1, e_{\text{rel}}) \big)$.
* $\text{Trig}_{R2}(e_{\text{rel}}) \equiv (\text{risk}(e_{\text{rel}}) = \text{'HIGH'}) \lor (\text{cust}(e_{\text{rel}}) = \text{'PEP'})$.
* $\text{Sat}_{R2}(e_{\text{rel}}) \equiv \text{Trig}_{R2}(e_{\text{rel}}) \implies \exists e_{\text{er}}, e_{\text{ca}} : \text{ValidER}(e_{\text{er}}, e_{\text{rel}}) \land \text{type}(e_{\text{ca}}) = \text{Compliance\_Approve} \land (e_{\text{er}} \prec e_{\text{ca}} \prec e_{\text{rel}})$.
* $\text{Trig}_{R3}(e_{\text{rel}}) \equiv \exists e_{\text{slu}} \prec e_{\text{rel}} : \text{type}(e_{\text{slu}}) = \text{Sanctions\_List\_Update} \land \text{jur}(e_{\text{slu}}) = \text{jur}(e_{\text{rel}}) \land \big( \forall e_{\text{ss1}} \prec e_{\text{slu}} : \text{type}(e_{\text{ss1}}) = \text{Sanctions\_Screen} \implies \neg \exists e_{\text{ss2}} \in (e_{\text{slu}}, e_{\text{rel}}) \big)$. *(More simply: a list update for the current jurisdiction occurred after the prior screen).*
* $\text{Sat}_{R3}(e_{\text{rel}}) \equiv \forall e_{\text{slu}} \prec e_{\text{rel}} \text{ with } \text{jur}(e_{\text{slu}}) = \text{jur}(e_{\text{rel}}) : \exists e_{\text{ss}} \in (e_{\text{slu}}, e_{\text{rel}}) : \text{type}(e_{\text{ss}}) = \text{Sanctions\_Screen}$.

## 4. Ambiguities and Alternative Readings

### TX-305 (`INDETERMINATE` — Missing Amount Until Next-Day Settlement)
* **Primary Reading (`INDETERMINATE`):** At `Create_Transaction` (`11:00Z`), `Enhanced_Review` (`11:15Z`), and `Release_Funds` (`11:30Z`), `amount` is $\bot$ ("amount feed unavailable", "review document has no amount", "release message omits amount"). Because jurisdiction is `CA` ($\neq \text{DE}$), R1 depends entirely on whether $\text{amount} \ge 100,000$ at `11:30Z` and whether any material increase occurred between `11:15Z` and `11:30Z`. The `150,000` logged at `Settlement_Confirmed` (`2026-04-07T08:00:00Z`) cannot establish compliance: (1) under R7, settlement values cannot be copied backward; (2) even if `150,000` was the true release amount, the `Enhanced_Review` at `11:15Z` explicitly had *no amount* on its review packet, meaning an amount-dependent review was conducted blind (violating the substantive purpose of R1/R4).
* **What Would Change the Result:**
  * *To `NONCOMPLIANT`:* If policy interprets an `Enhanced_Review` conducted without a visible transaction amount ("review document has no amount") as per se invalid for an R1 obligation, and external order-entry logs prove the requested release amount at `11:30Z` was indeed `150,000`.
  * *To `COMPLIANT`:* If an immutable upstream swift/payment instruction payload prior to `11:15Z` proves the amount was fixed at `< 100,000` at release (with the `150,000` settlement representing a batched settlement or FX conversion), or proves `150,000` was visible to the reviewer in an auxiliary system at `11:15Z` and remained unchanged at `11:30Z`.

### TX-302 (`COMPLIANT` vs. `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`)
* **Primary Reading (`COMPLIANT`):** Although `Release_Funds` (`09:40Z`) references `EX-77` and `Record_Exemption` is logged late (`11:00Z`), `TX-302` already completed an `Enhanced_Review` at `09:20Z` after `Create_Transaction` (`09:00Z`, `180,000`, `GB`) with zero intervening data changes. Thus, R1 is directly satisfied by the review itself. Furthermore, `EX-77` is independently valid under R5 because its qualified digital signature (`09:05Z`) and documented outage prove approval existed prior to `09:40Z`.
* **What Would Change the Result:**
  * *To `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`:* If the grading rubric reserves `COMPLIANT` strictly for cases that do not invoke an exemption at `Release_Funds`, or if the `09:20Z` `Enhanced_Review` was logged in error/incomplete, making `EX-77` the sole basis for compliance.
  * *To `NONCOMPLIANT`:* Only under a strict literalist reading where $t_{\text{rec}} \le t_{\text{rel}}$ (database record must exist before release) is mandatory regardless of cryptographic signature timestamps—which contradicts R5 ("A late database record can be evidence of earlier approval").

### TX-310 (`NONCOMPLIANT` — Backdated PDF vs. Post-Release Signing Certificate)
* **Primary Reading (`NONCOMPLIANT`):** R1 is triggered (`AE`, `200,000`) and only a `Standard_Review` occurred (`09:20Z`). Compliance hinges entirely on `EX-88` at release (`10:00Z`). While the PDF displays `08:50Z` and the transaction requester claims oral approval at `08:45Z`, the qualified signing certificate was not created until `10:30Z`—30 minutes *after* funds were released. Under R5, a displayed backdated field and non-independent witness testimony from the transaction requester do not establish that approval existed at `10:00Z`.
* **What Would Change the Result:**
  * *To `INDETERMINATE`:* If the compliance framework allows oral approvals by `Head_Compliance` in principle and treats the conflict between the requester's testimony (`08:45Z`) and the certificate creation (`10:30Z`) as an unresolved evidentiary dispute pending interview of `Head_Compliance` or phone recording review.
  * *To `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`:* If independent, tamper-evident contemporaneous evidence (e.g., a recorded compliance line call at `08:45Z` or an immutable email from `Head_Compliance` at `08:50Z`) proves approval was granted before `10:00Z`, and `10:30Z` was merely when the formal PDF was digitally signed.

### TX-311 (`COMPLIANT` — Same-Timestamp Tie-Breaking and Jurisdiction Shift)
* **Primary Reading (`COMPLIANT`):** At `16:20:00Z`, two events occur: `Enhanced_Review` (`idx=3`) and `Update_Jurisdiction` to `DE` (`idx=4`). By R7 (`event_index` tie-breaker), `idx=3` precedes `idx=4`. Thus:
  1. At `Release_Funds` (`16:30Z`, `idx=5`), the current state is $\text{jur}=\text{'DE'}$ and $\text{amt}=115,000$. Because $\text{jur}=\text{'DE'}$, R1's trigger condition ($\text{jur} \neq \text{'DE'}$) is **false** at release. No R1 obligation exists at release.
  2. Even if one argued that the `16:20Z` (`idx=4`) jurisdiction change was a "material data change" after the `16:20Z` (`idx=3`) `Enhanced_Review`, a change *to* `DE` removes the foreign jurisdiction risk rather than creating it, and R1 is only required when current jurisdiction at `Release_Funds` is not `DE`.
* **What Would Change the Result:**
  * *If `idx=4` had changed jurisdiction from `DE` to `US` (or from `US` to `AE`):* Then `Update_Jurisdiction` (`idx=4`) would occur *after* `Enhanced_Review` (`idx=3`) at the same timestamp `16:20:00Z`, meaning the review evaluated the old jurisdiction (`US`) and did not cover the new foreign jurisdiction (`AE`), rendering the case `NONCOMPLIANT` under R1/R7.

### TX-307 (`NONCOMPLIANT` — Executive Override Interpretation)
* **Primary Reading (`NONCOMPLIANT`):** R2 explicitly states "*both* `Enhanced_Review` and `Compliance_Approve` are required," and additional semantic facts specify that a `Compliance_Approve` reviewing only a standard packet does not cure the absence of `Enhanced_Review`.
* **Alternative Reading (`JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`):** If an organization's governance charter granted `hc03` (`Compliance_Approve`) plenary authority to waive `Enhanced_Review` implicitly by signing off on a `Standard_Review` packet when amount is below `100,000` (`70,000`, `DE`), one could argue an implicit waiver. However, under R2 and R5 (which requires a formal scoped exemption by `Head_Compliance`), this defense fails.

### Why Control-Flow Conformance Alone Fails in the Code Above
As shown in Step 8 of the script, a pure control-flow check (such as a DECLARE `Precedence(Enhanced_Review, Release_Funds)` or Petri-net token replay on activity labels) is blind to payload state:
* It falsely passes `TX-303`, `TX-305`, and `TX-309` because `Enhanced_Review` precedes `Release_Funds` in the activity sequence, missing that `TX-303` lacked `Compliance_Approve` for a `HIGH` risk reclassification, `TX-305` reviewed a blank amount, and `TX-309` suffered a post-review $>20\%$ amount increase.
* It cannot distinguish why `TX-311` is compliant even if `Enhanced_Review` were absent, because the deactivation of R1 (`Update_Jurisdiction` $\to$ `DE`) is a data change at the same timestamp.

## 6. Why Control-Flow-Only Conformance Is Insufficient

Standard control-flow conformance techniques (token-based replay, alignments on workflow nets, or pure activity-based DECLARE constraints) operate solely on sequences of activity labels $\langle a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n \rangle$. They fail on this event log across six distinct failure modes:

1. **Final-State Leakage (Retroactive Attribute Flattening):**
   Standard process mining tools frequently promote case attributes to a static case-level table by taking the *last known value* in the trace. In `TX-305`, `Settlement_Confirmed` (`2026-04-07T08:00:00Z`) supplies `amount=150000` a day after `Release_Funds`. Flattening `150000` to the whole case hides the fact that the transaction was reviewed and released with an $\texttt{UNKNOWN}$ amount. Conversely, in `TX-311`, flattening `jurisdiction=DE` backward would hide that the transaction was `US` during `Enhanced_Review`.
2. **Post-Hoc Exemptions and Multi-Time Collisions ($t_{\text{event}}$ vs. $t_{\text{rec}}$ vs. $t_{\text{evid}}$):**
   In both `TX-302` (`EX-77`) and `TX-310` (`EX-88`), the activity sequence is `[..., Release_Funds, Record_Exemption]`. A control-flow model either flags both as violations (because `Record_Exemption` occurs after `Release_Funds`) or ignores `Record_Exemption`. It cannot evaluate R5, which requires comparing `Release_Funds` ($t_{\text{event}}$) against external cryptographic signature timestamps ($t_{\text{evid}} = \text{09:05Z}$ for `EX-77` vs. $\text{10:30Z}$ for `EX-88`).
3. **Material Changes After Review (Arithmetic Invalidation):**
   In `TX-309`, `Enhanced_Review` (`15:25Z`) precedes `Release_Funds` (`15:35Z`), satisfying pure control-flow precedence. However, `Update_Amount` (`15:28Z`) increases `amount` from `118,000` to `145,000` ($+22.88\% > 20\%$), invalidating the review under R4. Conversely, `TX-306` ($+4.55\%$ increase) and `TX-308` ($-28.57\%$ decrease) have the exact same control-flow pattern `[..., Enhanced_Review, ..., Update_Amount, Release_Funds]` as `TX-309`, yet are **compliant** because their arithmetic deltas do not breach $+20\%$.
4. **Conditional Rule Activation and Dynamic Deactivation:**
   Obligations R1, R2, and R3 are guarded by data predicates at `Release_Funds`. In `TX-307`, `Update_Customer_Type` to `PEP` activates R2, rendering the subsequent `[Standard_Review, Compliance_Approve]` sequence noncompliant. In `TX-304`, `Sanctions_List_Update` only triggers R3 because its payload (`XZ`) matches the case's active jurisdiction (`XZ`).
5. **Missing Data and Epistemic Uncertainty (Three-Valued Logic):**
   Control-flow models are binary (a transition either fires or does not). When `amount` is missing at release in `TX-305`, SQL/pandas filters using standard boolean logic (`amount >= 100000` evaluating to `False` on `NaN`) silently default missing values to non-triggering (low-risk) states, violating R6.
6. **Same-Timestamp Ordering (`event_index` Tie-Breaking):**
   In `TX-311`, `Enhanced_Review` (`idx=3`) and `Update_Jurisdiction` (`idx=4`) share timestamp `2026-04-06T16:20:00Z`. Standard XES/PM4Py sorting by `time:timestamp` alone is non-deterministic for ties. Depending on whether `idx=3` or `idx=4` is processed first, the review either occurred under `US` (before the change to `DE`) or under `DE` (after the change).

## 7. Recommended Logging and Control Improvements

To eliminate `INDETERMINATE` outcomes and prevent noncompliant releases in production, the logging architecture and workflow engine should implement seven structural improvements:

1. **Immutable State Snapshots at Control Gates (`Release_Funds` & Reviews):**
   Every review (`Standard_Review`, `Enhanced_Review`), approval (`Compliance_Approve`), and release (`Release_Funds`) event must log a complete, immutable JSON/columnar snapshot of the evaluated state (`snapshot_amount`, `snapshot_currency`, `snapshot_risk`, `snapshot_jurisdiction`, `snapshot_customer_type`) rather than relying on sparse delta reconstruction. Furthermore, the workflow engine must enforce a hard schema constraint rejecting `Release_Funds` or `Enhanced_Review` if `snapshot_amount IS NULL` (preventing `TX-305`).
2. **Explicit Four-Timestamp Logging for Exemptions and Approvals:**
   Replace single event timestamps on exemptions and approvals with four explicit fields: `effective_from_ts` ($t_{\text{eff}}$), `expires_at_ts` ($t_{\text{exp}}$), `record_inserted_ts` ($t_{\text{rec}}$), and `qualified_signature_ts` ($t_{\text{evid}}$), accompanied by `signature_cert_ thumbprint` and `payload_sha256`. This allows automated verification of `EX-77` vs. `EX-88` without manual forensic inspection.
3. **Rule Version and Policy Evaluation Audit Trail:**
   Log `policy_version_id`, `triggered_rules` (e.g., `["R1", "R2"]`), and `satisfied_by_event_ids` at `Release_Funds`. If rule thresholds (such as EUR 100,000 or 20% materiality) change over time, historical audits remain reproducible.
4. **Structured Exemption Scope Attributes:**
   Log exemption parameters directly in structured event attributes (`ex_waived_rules`, `ex_max_amount`, `ex_allowed_jurisdictions`, `ex_allowed_customer_types`, `ex_approver_role`) and enforce pre-release cryptographic validation so an unverified or post-signed exemption like `EX-88` blocks `Release_Funds` in real time.
5. **Change Provenance and Baseline Linkage:**
   For every `Update_*` or `Risk_Reclassify` event, log `prev_value`, `new_value`, `pct_change_from_last_review`, `change_reason_code`, and `source_system_id`. For `Compliance_Approve` and reviews, log `reviewed_event_index` or `packet_hash` so the log explicitly records whether `Compliance_Approve` approved a `Standard_Review` (`TX-307`) or an `Enhanced_Review` (`TX-306`).
6. **Monotonic Global Sequence Numbers and Sub-Millisecond Precision:**
   Enforce nanosecond/microsecond UTC timestamps (`time:timestamp`) alongside a database-enforced strictly increasing sequence integer (`event_index` / `log_sequence_number`) per case to guarantee deterministic causal ordering (`TX-311`).
7. **Automated Pre-Release Guardrails (Preventive Controls):**
   Embed the event-time state machine directly into the payment release gateway: automatically block `Release_Funds` if any active obligation (`R1`–`R4`) lacks a valid, uninvalidated prior event or a cryptographically verified exemption with $t_{\text{evid}} \le t_{\text{now}}$.
