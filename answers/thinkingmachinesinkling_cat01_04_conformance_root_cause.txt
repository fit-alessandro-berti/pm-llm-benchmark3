## 3. Causal-abductive explanation

The observed collapse is best explained by a layered artifact cascade rather than a single root cause. The UI release on 31 May changed the screen label from `Classify` to `Risk Screen` without altering the business function; this alone produced massive alignment mismatches because the normative model expects `Classify`. The gateway change on 3 June injected `Queue Routed`—a technical routing signal—into the event stream, creating unexpected moves that the control-flow model cannot accommodate. The ETL change on 15 June replaced source-system sequence numbers with warehouse-ingestion timestamps for `Decision` and `Notify`, producing reversed ordering in 70 traces and further depressing fitness. After transparent normalization—mapping `Risk Screen` back to `Classify`, stripping `Queue Routed`, and reordering by source sequence—fitness rebounds to 89%, demonstrating that roughly two-thirds of the collapse was logging and extraction noise.

However, normalization does not restore the original 93%, and the August–September period introduces a new structural gap. The 1 August policy requiring `Supervisor Sign-off` for claims above EUR 50,000 was not reflected in the normative tree. A candidate model allowing an optional sign-off raises fitness from 66% to 86%, confirming model obsolescence as a second major driver. Meanwhile, `Manual Override` persists even after cleaning, suggesting either a genuine (if minor) behavioral adaptation or a logging-semantics shift from the 12 August patch. Most critically, enhanced-review traces without `Compliance Check` (25 in June–July, 30 in August–September) remain unresolved. They could represent true bypasses (noncompliance) or missing event extraction, especially given the spike in missing-resource percentages.

Unchanged business outcomes—approval rates, appeal rates, and median cycle times—do not prove that no real problem exists, but they do weaken claims of widespread behavioral degradation. If reviewers were systematically skipping compliance checks for high-value claims, one might expect downstream appeals or delays; their absence is consistent with either (a) a small-scale, well-concealed problem or (b) extraction gaps that leave the real process intact. Conversely, stable outcomes do not rule out noncompliance; detection is explicitly warned to be incomplete, and a targeted bypass of high-value claims might not yet appear in aggregate appeal statistics. Thus, the measured conformance drop is primarily an artifact of label, technical-event, timestamp, and model-obsolescence mechanisms operating jointly, while a residual, unverified compliance risk demands targeted audit rather than aggregate alignment metrics.

## 7. Provisional conclusion

The measured conformance collapse—from 93% fitness in April–May to 32% in June–July—is primarily an artifact cascade, not evidence of widespread employee noncompliance or fundamental process redesign. The staged recovery after each normalization layer demonstrates this conclusively. Mapping the UI label `Risk Screen` back to `Classify` recovers a substantial portion of fitness, confirming the 31 May release as a semantic logging disruption rather than a functional change. Removing the injected technical event `Queue Routed` (documented on 3 June) eliminates unexpected moves that the normative tree cannot represent. Reordering `Decision` and `Notify` by source-system sequence number—rather than warehouse-ingestion time, which the ETL switched to on 15 June—corrects 70 reversed pairs, 11 of which were manually verified via source screenshots. After these three repairs, fitness rebounds to 89%, indicating that approximately 57 percentage points of the 61-point collapse are explainable by extraction, labeling, and timestamp defects.

The remaining gap (89% vs. the original 93%) and the August–September pattern require separate assessment. The 1 August policy introducing `Supervisor Sign-off` for claims above EUR 50,000 was not incorporated into the normative model. Extending the tree with an optional sign-off raises August–September fitness from 66% to 86%, establishing model obsolescence as a second, independent driver of the later-period drop. At the same time, `Manual Override` traces persist after cleaning. Reviewers report using this action to correct routing mistakes, and the 12 August logging patch altered its recording semantics. This suggests a minor, durable behavioral or logging adjustment—real, but not catastrophic.

The critical unresolved issue is the missing `Compliance Check` in 25 June–July and 30 August–September enhanced-review traces. These could represent true bypasses of a mandatory control, which would be a serious compliance failure, or they could be extraction gaps, especially given the sharp rise in missing-resource percentages (1.2% to 5.9% to 6.4%). The supervisor memo of 10 July warns against bypassing checks but does not cite a quantified increase; compliance reports no material change in appeal rates (3.8%  3.7%  3.6%) or confirmed incidents, though detection is admitted to be incomplete. Stable approval rates and median cycle times further weaken the claim that reviewers are systematically altering case outcomes. However, stable aggregates do not exonerate localized noncompliance: a targeted bypass of high-value claims might not yet appear in aggregate appeals.

Confidence levels: I assign approximately 90% confidence that the aggregate conformance collapse is artifactual (label, technical event, timestamp, and model-obsolescence mechanisms). I assign approximately 60% confidence that no material operational compliance degradation occurred, and about 40% confidence that some combination of extraction failure and localized noncompliance exists for the missing-check traces. The evidence does not support blaming employees based solely on alignment moves.

Immediate actions should be segregated by stakeholder. IT must restore source-system sequence numbers as the authoritative ordering key for process analysis, update the ETL to filter or tag `Queue Routed` as infrastructure rather than business activity, and fix resource-link extraction to reduce the 6.4% missing-resource rate. Process owners must version the normative model against policy effective dates—specifically adding the `Supervisor Sign-off` requirement for claims exceeding EUR 50,000, preferably as a data-aware guard rather than a control-flow-only option. Compliance must conduct a manual audit of every missing-check enhanced-review case, comparing source-system check records (database updates, supervisor signatures, risk-system logs) against the event log, stratified by claim value. Reviewers should be interviewed specifically about `Manual Override` usage patterns, with reviewer-level process discovery to detect clustering.

What must not be claimed: We must not assert that employees are systematically noncompliant based on alignment diagnostics alone. We must not claim the true process is unchanged; the policy change and `Manual Override` usage represent real adjustments. We must not claim the data is fully repaired without source-level verification of the missing checks. And we must not use the improved fitness from an overpermissive optional-sign-off model as proof that compliance is intact, because a control-flow-only extension cannot enforce the EUR 50,000 condition and may therefore overgeneralize.

## 8. Model-governance recommendation

1. **Versioned normative-model registry tied to policy effective dates.** Every policy change that introduces, removes, or conditions an activity (e.g., the 1 August `Supervisor Sign-off` requirement) must trigger an automatic model-version increment, with the new tree published to the conformance-checking pipeline before the policy takes effect.

2. **Event-semantic contract and UI-change protocol.** Any interface release that renames, relabels, or restructures an activity (e.g., `Classify` to `Risk Screen` on 31 May) must be matched by a signed extraction-layer mapping contract. The process-log schema should remain stable; display labels should never propagate unfiltered into conformance logs.

3. **Technical-event exclusion policy in ETL.** Infrastructure signals such as `Queue Routed` must be tagged at ingestion and excluded from the business-process event stream used for conformance. They may be retained in a separate provenance stream for debugging but must not generate alignment moves.

4. **Timestamp provenance preservation with ordering hierarchy.** The ETL must preserve the source-system sequence number as the authoritative ordering key for process analysis. Warehouse-ingestion timestamps may be stored for latency analytics but must not override source ordering in conformance jobs, preventing the `Decision`/`Notify` reversal observed here.

5. **Data-aware constraint encoding for regulated processes.** Normative models for regulated claims must include case-attribute guards (e.g., `value > 50000` requires `Supervisor Sign-off`), not only control-flow structures. Relying solely on optional activities in a process tree risks overgeneralization and false fitness improvement.

6. **Post-release automated validation gate.** After every UI, ETL, or gateway release, an automated pipeline must compare variant distributions, DFG edges, alignment fitness, and resource-link completeness against the prior 30-day baseline. If fitness drops by more than a defined threshold (e.g., 5%) or new nodes appear, the release must be blocked pending manual audit sign-off.
