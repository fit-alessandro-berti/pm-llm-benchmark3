## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

Operational decision support in high-stakes financial processes cannot rely on simplistic scalar expected-utility functions that equate regulatory breach costs with operational delay losses. The decision framework requires **lexicographic optimization under asymmetric risk constraints**:

```
                    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                    │  1. Hard Conformance Invariants (Rules 1-11) │
                    │     (Zero tolerance for illegal execution)   │
                    └──────────────────────┬───────────────────────┘
                                           │ Passes Filter
                    ┌──────────────────────▼───────────────────────┐
                    │ 2. Multi-Case Portfolio Loss Minimization    │
                    │    (P-884 SLA + P-901 Spillover + Harm)      │
                    └──────────────────────┬───────────────────────┘
                                           │ Maximizes
                    ┌──────────────────────▼───────────────────────┐
                    │ 3. Informational & Resource Efficiency       │
                    │    (Screening Quotas, Resource Utilization)  │
                    └──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Quantitative Payoff Asymmetries
1. **Operational SLA Value**: Executing within 90 minutes yields €20,000–€35,000 in avoided operational loss. This loss is bounded, linear, and purely commercial.
2. **False Rejection / Hold Harm**: Erroneous rejection or prolonged hold imposes €15,000–€80,000 in customer damage. Because baseline approval probability is 0.55–0.75, arbitrary rejection (Action F) destroys substantial expected value (€8,250–€60,000 expected harm).
3. **Regulatory / Compliance Exposure**: Unlawful execution (Action A or B) triggers €100,000–€500,000 in direct statutory fines, compounded by severe non-monetary consequences: regulatory enforcement actions, suspension of correspondent banking access, loss of institutional license, and personal criminal liability for officers.

### Dominance and Counterfactual Arguments
- **Strict Dominance of Hard Controls over Nonconformant Shortcuts**: Even if an unlawful execution had an operational success rate of 99%, the asymmetric tail risk (\(0.46 \times [€100\text{k}–€500\text{k}] = €46\text{k}–€230\text{k}\)) strictly dominates the €20,000–€35,000 operational gain. In legal and compliance frameworks, mandatory controls are non-negotiable safety invariants; they cannot be traded off against commercial convenience.
- **Dominance of Action D over Action C**: Action C achieves P-884 compliance at the direct expense of Case P-901 (increasing P-901's breach probability by 12–28% on a 70-minute deadline). Action D runs alternate screening in parallel during the unavoidable wait for S4 (15–30m vs. 35–65m), preserving P-901 while achieving a 70–85 minute completion window for P-884. Action D optimizes the *joint multi-case portfolio* rather than myopically favoring P-884.
- **Dominance of Conformant Investigation (D/C) over Premature Rejection (F) and Passive Hold (E)**: Action E unnecessarily guarantees a 100% SLA breach when intra-day resources (S4, API calls) exist. Action F prematurely incurs €15,000–€80,000 in customer harm on a case with a 55–75% likelihood of being legitimate.

### Why Predictive Forecasts Cannot Validate Actions A and B
Predictive process monitoring estimates observational outcomes under normal historical process execution. When an actor intervenes to execute without second review (Action A) or self-certify (Action B), the underlying data-generating distribution is fundamentally violated:
1. **Regulatory Invariance**: Legal and normative compliance rules are structural safety constraints, not probabilistic preferences. An algorithm predicting an SLA breach of 0.64 provides zero legal defense against statutory fines for executing a sanctioned payment without four-eyes review.
2. **Endogeneity of Approval Rates**: The historical approval probability (0.55–0.75) reflects cases that *successfully passed* independent second-officer scrutiny. Applying that probability to unverified, unreviewed cases is an egregious counterfactual error (selection bias / survivorship fallacy).

### Evidentiary Thresholds Required for Actions F and G
- **Action F (Rejection)** requires documented objective proof establishing that the beneficiary or transaction matches a designated sanctions list, or that the €480,000 border-region profile violates specific non-derogable institutional risk policies. Without this threshold, immediate rejection constitutes an arbitrary operational breach causing actionable customer harm.
- **Action G (Emergency Override)** requires:
  1. A formal, verified technical/operational incident identifier registered in the enterprise incident management system (e.g., core banking infrastructure outage, catastrophic data corruption). Commercial deadline pressure does *not* constitute a valid incident.
  2. Formal written/cryptographic sign-off from the Compliance Director explicitly waiving standard controls under Rule 10.
