## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

**Framework choice (stated up front).** I do **not** use natural direct/indirect effects. Three features of this process break them: (i) `flag` is a deterministic function of `risk_score` in these data (flagged min 0.64, unflagged max 0.61), so conditional on the score there is *no* exposure variation — cross-world contrasts under a fixed score are not even hypothetically manipulable; (ii) the queue state at manual-review entry and the reviewer identity are **exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounders** (flag  faster triage  earlier queue entry  different congestion; flag  routing  M2 assignment); (iii) shared review/senior pools create **interference**, so one case's mediator value depends on other cases' treatments. I therefore use: **separable (path-specific) effects** for the policy components `A_lane` (priority triage), `A_display` (flag shown), `A_review` (mandatory review routing), `A_esccrit`, `A_capacity`; **interventional direct/indirect effects** (Vansteelandt–Daniel style distribution shifts) for the queue mediators; **controlled direct effects** for capacity/criteria; and **policy-level overall / direct / spillover effects** (Hudgens–Halloran) for anything that changes shared load. "Set flag = 0" is *not* an estimand here; only policy-component interventions are.

**Where the threats live (180 words).** Interference: `manual_review` and `escalation` of *other* cases determine pool occupancy, so `manual_queue_wait` and `escalation_queue_wait` are not individual attributes; P3/P4 change untreated cases' waits too. Exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounding: flag shortens triage, which moves the manual-queue entry clock, which determines congestion and which reviewer is on shift; entry-time queue state and `review_resource` therefore confound waitcycle and escalationcycle *and* are descendants of flag. That alone rules out natural effects and makes "hold the mediator at its untreated value" incoherent without a capacity stipulation. Treatment–confounder feedback: mandating review raises pool load, which raises waits, which delays later cases' escalation entry, which changes senior load — a cross-case loop requiring g-methods or explicit queue simulation rather than one-shot regression adjustment. Additional unmeasured confounding: `U_underlying_risk`/`U_occult_severity` drive score, reviewer decisions, service durations and outcome; `complexity` is an intake-rule proxy that both causes flag and lengthens service.

**Pilot (P2, narrow eligibility; 250 words).**
*Eligibility:* flagged cases with `risk_score` in [0.64, 0.75) **and** `complexity` = Medium, arriving in staffed pilot shifts. Rationale: both observed flagged Rejects were High complexity with score  0.91, so this stratum has no observed adverse decisions — but that is weak evidence, hence audit.
*Assignment:* two-stage. Randomize shifts to saturation 25% vs 75% "mandatory review"; within shift, randomize eligible cases to mandatory review vs discretionary-review-plus-audit. Reviewers may always override to review (recorded as override, kept in intention-to-treat).
*Queue-capacity guardrail:* ring-fence existing M1/M3 hours; halt enrollment if unflagged manual-wait P90 exceeds its trailing 14-shift P90 by >20%, or if M-pool waiting count >6 at any entry, or senior-pool waiting count >3.
*Primary outcome:* total cycle time (close  arrival) in eligible flagged cases. *Secondary:* unflagged manual wait (spillover), review occurrence (first stage), escalation occurrence, senior wait, and **adjudicated** decision error on a blinded 100% audit of non-reviewed eligible cases plus a 20% sample of reviewed ones.
*Minimum data:* per-stage start **and** completion timestamps with documented semantics, queue entry clock, occupancy snapshot at each entry, reviewer/pool ID, override reason, score version, complexity rule version, shift staffing hours, audit labels.
*Stopping conditions:* any audit-confirmed missed high-risk case; two consecutive guardrail breaches; override rate >70% (first stage dead); or a pre-registered futility boundary showing <60 min cycle-time gain after 60 eligible cases.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk.** If adjudicated audit labels (or a credible latent-severity proxy) show that flagged cases carry substantially more unlogged risk, and a bias analysis with U-associations no larger than the observed `complexity` associations nulls the within-High-complexity manual-wait ratio (468/130), then the delay is *appropriate case difficulty*, not flag handling — and P2/P4 become harmful rather than efficient.
2. **Queue interference.** If the saturation experiment shows unflagged manual wait rising steeply with flagged-review saturation, and the queue-state-adjusted flag coefficient collapses to ~0, then the "flag penalty" is pure shared-pool congestion. P3 with *reallocated* capacity would then worsen total system performance, and only additive capacity (E7/E8) or demand reduction survives.
3. **Decision quality.** If the audit finds any materially higher false-approve rate among non-reviewed borderline flagged cases — or shows that the 4 Rejects were only reachable *because* mandatory review fed escalation — then the cycle-time gain from P2 is bought with decision errors, and the recommendation reverses to P3/P4 (capacity, not fewer reviews).
4. **Timestamp semantics.** If `manual_start`/`escalation_start` are assignment or batch-log times rather than work-start times (already suggested by M2 double-booking, uniform 6-minute granularity, and pairwise-overlapping senior slots), then the waiting/service split is wrong: the +345/+174 min "queue" terms could be scheduled batching or in-progress work, and capacity addition would not shorten them.
5. **Positivity / lack of overlap.** Flag is deterministic in `risk_score` (0.61 vs 0.64), no Low-complexity case is flagged, and T1/T2 serve only flagged cases. If a wider sample confirms this structural non-overlap and no threshold-bandwidth support exists, then *no* score-adjusted or natural-effect estimate is admissible, and even the pilot's extrapolation beyond Medium-complexity borderline cases must be withdrawn.
6. **Reviewer behavior change.** If reviewers, seeing the score (P1) or losing the mandate (P2), reconstruct the flag and review anyway (override rate >70%, weak first stage), or if M2 rotates and the escalation-per-review rate equalizes across pools, then the display/routing components are not separable, E6/E9 are uninformative, and the effective lever is reviewer assignment and senior capacity instead.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed.** Flagged cases start triage after 14 min versus 149 min and are served faster (36 vs 57). All 10 flagged cases are manually reviewed versus 4/10 unflagged; conditional manual service is nearly identical (162 vs 165 min). The triage-donemanual-start gap is 390 vs 113 min, escalation occurs 6/10 vs 2/10, senior waiting averages ~412 min against ~116 min of senior service, and mean cycle time is 991 vs 490 min. The exact additive accounting attributes +345 min to manual waiting, +174 to senior waiting, +141 to extra service, against 155 min of triage acceleration. Reject rates are 2/10 in both arms; M2 handles 7 reviews, all escalated.

**Plausible mechanism.** The flag is a *bundle*: a priority-triage component that genuinely accelerates the front end, and a mandatory-review component that injects demand into a shared, apparently M2-concentrated review pool and then into a ~2-slot senior pool. Delay is dominated by *occurrence plus waiting*, not by longer service.

**Unidentified.** Any natural direct/indirect split; the display-only effect (score remains visible); escalation-criteria effects; decision quality (Approve/Reject is not correctness); and spillover magnitude. Positivity fails: flag is deterministic in score, no flagged Low-complexity cases, exclusive triage resources.

**Most useful next evidence.** The narrowly eligible, saturation-randomized P2 pilot with 100% adjudicated audit: it manipulates review *occurrence* (the largest gap term), measures the first stage and unflagged spillover, and prices quality — after which a funded, additive P3/P4 capacity trial becomes interpretable.
