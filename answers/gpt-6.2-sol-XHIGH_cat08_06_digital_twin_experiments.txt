# 1. Model scope and state variables

Build a calendar-driven discrete-event simulation of the municipal permit system. Follow each application from portal submission through first decision, and link appeals to the original case through final disposition. For policies that change what applicants do before submitting, also track application intent, preparation effort, deferral and abandonment where they can be measured or bounded. Start policy comparisons from the same dated snapshot of open applications, resource commitments and scheduled events. Version the routing rules and calendars so the twin can represent a midyear rollout, including which rules apply to cases already open.

- **Case state.** Store case type, district, site or travel zone, architect and project group where known, application value, regulatory flags and their provenance, prior violations, submission date, priority, and applicable policy version. Track each task's status, attempt number, input version, eligibility time, service start and remaining work. Also track applicant holds, notice deadlines, objections, appointments, completed prerequisites, and the decision and appeal history.
- **Workflow.** Run completeness checks until the file is accepted or the case is withdrawn. For a complete file, release Planning and the applicable Fire and Environmental work when their inputs are ready. Allow overlap where the rules and actual practice permit it. In the supplied tree, notice follows the review join and inspection follows notice. Verify those release conditions before allowing earlier scheduling. Drafting waits for every applicable result, including notice and inspection. Model the baseline trigger for legal review explicitly. A Planning, Inspection or Legal finding can return the case to a named earlier task and invalidate results affected by the changed submission. An appeal can reopen the case after the first decision.
- **Resources and queues.** Maintain ready queues by skill, with observed priority rules, eligibility and aging. Use individual or skill-equivalent calendars for clerks, planners, Fire and Environmental reviewers, schedulers, inspectors and legal officers. Include Monday portal support, Simple-only planners, reserved Environmental time, inspector field days, legal council-week restrictions and urgent exceptions, plus holidays and absences. Represent inspection booking separately from the visit. A visit occupies an inspector for service and allocated route travel. Applicant holds, statutory clocks and automated posting use timers, with effectively unlimited capacity for system tasks.
- **Shared state.** Model architect and project-group submission bursts, with shared readiness or response effects if supported. Give related sites or neighborhoods a shared objection exposure so a single controversy can delay several notices. Build inspector tours from compatible ready cases, with appointment windows, capacity, district travel and a maximum hold for the first case. All cases also interact through shared queues and can displace one another when priorities change.

# 2. Parameter-estimation plan

**Log and model preparation.** Use pandas and PM4Py to establish a canonical case, activity and timestamp schema, inspect variants and directly-follows graphs, and align traces against a Petri net converted from the supplied tree. Keep a separate lifecycle and resource table for matching starts and finishes, retaining task occurrence identifiers, source time zones and the distinction between occurrence and posting times. The tree is a starting point for conformance. Its labeled loop redo steps may correspond to unlogged transitions, its legal step is optional, and it omits Planning and Legal rework and appeals. Resolve these differences with process owners before encoding the executable workflow. The reported variant groups are useful calibration strata. Estimate conditional routing from eligible cases and task opportunities rather than treating the variant shares as branch probabilities.

**Arrivals and case attributes.** Estimate submission counts by local date and hour, using the portal's full exposure, with month, weekday, holiday and trend effects. Test whether the peak-to-winter rate ratio of 1.7 and the Monday-to-Wednesday ratio of 2.2 vary by case type or season. Use the 18-month total and mix to anchor the fit. Fit background arrivals plus architect or project clusters, using the top-20 share, weekly counts within architect, inter-submission gaps and same-district clustering to calibrate burst size and timing. Draw case type, district, value, architect and regulatory attributes jointly or conditionally on the arrival group. Verify coverage for incomplete weeks and for applications still open when the log ended.

**Work, waits and external clocks.** With paired lifecycle data, estimate service work by activity, role, case type, attempt and relevant complexity. Use active effort records or observations to separate interruptions from working time. Estimate queue wait from task eligibility to actual start, with eligibility based on all applicable predecessors and data readiness. Decompose inspection wait into booking, applicant/site readiness and appointment delay, and model travel separately. PM4Py's performance DFG describes elapsed time between adjacent logged events, including on parallel paths. Use it as a diagnostic and use observed waiting summaries as calibration targets for the simulated queues. For completion-only tasks, constrain service with the timestamped subset, timesheets, interviews and short time studies. Fit distributions to observed work or use the provided quantiles as provisional anchors. A lognormal fit to a median and p90 is one starting model, with its mean and upper tail checked against workload.

**Capacity reconciliation.** At roughly 400 submissions per week, if every application requires Planning and the pooled Planning quantiles represent active work, the median and p90 alone imply at least about 2.8 hours of work per review on average. Roughly 40% of reviews take at least 3.5 hours and another 10% take at least 14 hours. That implies about 1,120 hours per week before rework, versus 320 scheduled planner hours before deductions. Use the full log and rosters to reconcile volume and review coverage, touch time versus elapsed time, the population represented by each summary, and any external or unlisted staff. Do the same load check for inspection and other specialized pools. If the mismatch persists, report separate evidence-supported capacity or workload scenarios and the resulting backlog growth. Treat a calibrated policy ranking as conditional on resolving it.

**Routing, repeat work and external delays.** Join building area, sensitivity maps and violation history where available. Apply confirmed mandatory rules first. Fire is triggered by the flag or the area threshold, and Environmental review by the flag or district sensitivity. Estimate conditional probabilities for unresolved historical requirements or discretionary routes, with pooling for sparse districts and project types. Estimate permit, rejection, withdrawal and appeal transitions conditional on the available review results and case history. Infer rework from repeated eligible task occurrences and returns to earlier stages, then audit a stratified sample of notes. Estimate the probability, destination, additional work and applicant delay of each return, conditional on stage, case attributes and prior attempts. Fit applicant and consultant response survival curves with right-censoring and, where possible, withdrawal as a competing outcome. Use dated HR rosters and appointment records to estimate actual eligible capacity and relief coverage. Estimate district-to-district travel and achievable bundle sizes using visits or route records. Fit a latent objection and resolution model against notice spells and external objection records where available. Notice lengths alone cannot identify both objection frequency and the effect of an objection, or reliably identify a particular case's objection status.

# 3. Assumptions

- **Arrivals.** The portal accepts submissions outside office hours. A submission can create queued work immediately, with service beginning when an eligible resource can take it. Model pre-submission intent separately where a policy moves work before the portal timestamp.
- **Service times.** Draw positive work requirements conditional on activity, complexity and attempt, with a shared severity effect where supported. Specify which desk work can pause across shifts. Reserve contiguous appointment and travel blocks for field visits. Preserve zero-effort portal receipts and model any posting delay separately from processor capacity.
- **Routing.** Known mandatory flags and authoritative rules take precedence. Complex variance and some New residential applications require notice. Confirm the full notice rules and baseline legal eligibility. Parallel reviews wait at the applicable join. The inspection exemption requires Simple renovation, value strictly below EUR 50,000 and a verified absence of prior violations, subject to other mandatory requirements. Unknown eligibility follows the regular route pending verification.
- **Resources and calendars.** Start from the supplied HR schedules. Confirm the Fire pool's actual Tue–Thu staff-hours, the scheduler roster, drafting ownership, urgent legal access and how Environmental non-permit time is allocated during the week. Confirm whether the two Simple-only planners or restricted pools have relief coverage. Use the statutory 14-day minimum and the verified local clock for notice. Council and holiday effects can delay notice opening, handling, or sign-off under the applicable rules.
- **Rework.** A return targets a specific failed requirement. Repeat visits and appeals may have different durations and probabilities from first attempts. Model persistent complexity, withdrawal and escalation. Long-running cases remain in the system until a modeled disposition or the reporting cutoff.
- **Applicant and third-party response.** Document receipt, consultant reports, inspection readiness and no-shows have separate delay or competing-outcome models. Checker uptake, applicant effort and delay effects are pilot parameters. The effect ranges include low or zero benefit.
- **Cross-case dependencies.** Use project links when available. Architect, site and neighborhood proximity can support probabilistic grouping when links are missing. Include shared shocks within plausible groups and test weaker and stronger dependence, including its effect on the backlog tail.
- **Non-stationary demand.** Preserve historical season and weekday profiles in the reference forecast, with a range for growth, case mix and burst concentration. A rollout can change the timing and composition of submissions. Capacity and council availability vary on their own dated schedules.
- **Missing data.** Use explicit missingness indicators, bounds or multiple imputation. Test whether lifecycle coverage varies by team, severity or season. Reconcile weekend timestamps in the illustrative excerpt with the supplied rosters and check whether they are postings, exceptions or illustrative simplifications. Include a bounded emergency stream or reserve capacity if omitted emergency decisions displace ordinary work. Carry ambiguity about objections and uncoded rework into parameter and structural scenarios.

# 5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty treatment

- **Initialization and horizon.** For a live decision, initialize from actual open cases, queue positions, calendar commitments, notice and appointment deadlines, and retained work. Sample residual times conditional on elapsed time and completed effort when known. For a synthetic starting state, run at least one prior annual cycle under baseline rules and compare queue sizes and age profiles at the same calendar phase after additional cycles. If work accumulates from cycle to cycle, show that drift and use observed initialization or explicit finite-horizon scenarios. Run two post-rollout years to capture repeated peaks. Track initial backlog separately from post-rollout submission cohorts. Follow cohorts beyond the measurement window under continuing arrivals and capacity constraints, with a common reporting cutoff. Use time-to-event estimates with administrative censoring and account for withdrawals as a competing disposition. Report unresolved age profiles and restricted mean time in process to a common horizon. Report a percentile only where follow-up and the decision rate identify it.
- **Replications.** Start with 40 paired replications per configuration and use 100–200 as an initial production budget. Expand to a predefined precision target, such as a 95% interval half-width of 5 days for the overall p90 difference and 2 percentage points for material SLA differences. Allow more runs for scarce strata. Record the master seed, replicate, parameter draw and configuration. Use shared random inputs across policies for the same replicate, with independent replicate streams. Generate common demand and external shocks once, and key municipal service or response draws to stable cases, task occurrences and random input types so a changed branch does not shift every later draw.
- **Interval estimates.** Use paired replication-level differences or bootstrap entire matched replications for nonlinear statistics. Cases in the same replicate share queues and shocks. Hold model parameters fixed for Monte Carlo precision, then repeat over joint posterior draws or a hierarchical/block bootstrap of the fitting data for parameter uncertainty. Report stochastic and parameter contributions separately, with predictive ranges for workload and SLA risk under each stress. Provide simultaneous intervals or confirm finalist comparisons on fresh runs when screening many policies.
- **Tail and structural sensitivity.** Preserve mass at the ordinary notice minimum and model contested excess separately. Compare empirical resampling, censored survival fits and plausible heavier tails for responses, repeat work and contested resolution. Use external records or experts to bound sparse tails, and report sensitivity to those bounds. Vary service-time attribution, capacity, policy uptake, defect risk, rework probabilities, and shared-shock strength using coherent joint draws or explicit scenarios. Report where rankings reverse. Avoid trimming valid long cases or forcing unresolved ones to close at the horizon.

# 6. Validation strategy

| Validation | Evidence and checks |
|---|---|
| Face validity and verification | Walk complex traces through the model with reviewers, inspectors, legal staff and portal staff. Verify rule versions, simultaneous-event ordering, task eligibility, deadline clocks, route capacity and conservation of cases and staff-hours. Test no legal service in a blackout without an urgent exception, no unverified exemption, and no draft with a missing required result. |
| Event-log replay | Align observed traces to the process-tree projection, classify deviations such as unlogged redo steps and legitimate appeals, and check resource and priority rules against cases with good lifecycle data. Separately drive the baseline twin with historical submissions, initial WIP and dated calendars. Generate downstream behavior and compare it with observed outcomes without conditioning each replayed case on its realized future path. |
| Distributional validity | Compare submission interarrival and burst distributions, conditional routes and rework, applicant holds, and case-time and queue-time distributions by type, variant, district and season. Use survival curves that account for censoring, p50/p90 and age profiles, plus notice and travel diagnostics. Set tolerances before testing, for example 10% for common-stratum medians and 15% for p90 where data support that precision. Review systematic misses and the coverage of predictive intervals. |
| Resource utilization | Compare work, travel, absences and idle eligible hours by pool and week to roster, time-study, and booking evidence. Compare due-date pressure, queue ages and Monday or seasonal peaks. Sample completion-only teams to test the inferred split of work and wait. |
| Bottleneck validity | Check whether simulated queues form and clear in the same places and weeks as observed. Use historical staffing shocks where available and add small capacity increments to individual pools to check the direction and location of congestion changes. Validate the workload reconciliation before trusting these responses. |
| Out-of-sample validity | Hold out the latest contiguous months covering a peak and a shoulder if possible. Use rolling-origin folds across the 18 months for other seasonal contrasts. Fit on earlier data and compare arrival forecasts, route and delay distributions, and backlog against the held-out period. Check transfer to held-out architect or project groups where sample sizes permit. In a limited rollout measure uptake, risk, and behavior changes that historical replay cannot validate. |

# 7. Decision criteria

Agree on clocks, thresholds and implementation cost with the municipality before comparing runs. Use elapsed calendar days from submission to first decision as the common KPI, with separate intent-to-decision and final-disposition metrics where applicable. Report authority-controlled time separately if the SLA pauses for applicant holds. Include all submissions in cohort-based reporting and show withdrawals, appeals, cases censored before their SLA deadline and unresolved cases already overdue.

| Metric | Example selection rule |
|---|---|
| Median and tail duration | Seek at least a 5% median and 10% p90 reduction for targeted case types in the reference scenario. Require a paired interval supporting a tail improvement and check that p95 and long-running appeal cases show no material deterioration. |
| SLA by type | As provisional test deadlines use 30 days for Simple, 75 for New residential, 105 for Commercial and 180 for Complex variance. Seek at least a 5 percentage point improvement in targeted types below the approved compliance target, with no other type losing more than 2 points. Report absolute compliance and distance from the municipal target. |
| Fairness | Show raw and case-mix-standardized on-time rates and p90s by district and architect volume, including small practices and self-filers where identifiable. Report gaps with intervals and group counts. As a guardrail, allow at most a 2-point loss in any adequately measured group's on-time rate and at most a 3-point widening of the adjusted gap. Use pre-policy case attributes for adjustment and retain the raw differences. Check D3 first-case holds and D7 slot access explicitly. |
| Utilization and cost | Measure busy hours over eligible roster hours by skill and week, showing travel, overtime and restricted pools separately. Investigate repeated weeks above 90% busy time alongside rising waits. Compare staffing, outreach and checker costs and hours of applicant effort against incremental SLA misses averted. |
| Rework and decision quality | Track repeat tasks per 100 cases by stage, no-shows, reversals, withdrawals and appeals. Require all mandatory reviews and notice rules to be met. For D2, require audited evidence that material missed defects stay within a municipality-approved risk margin. For D5 and D8, track stale pre-checks and misclassified reviews. |
| Backlog and robustness | Track total and aged WIP by stage and type, peak backlog, and recovery time. Require improvement in targeted aged WIP and check that comparable end-of-season dates show no additional persistent buildup. For finalists, seek at least 90% of plausible parameter draws meeting the agreed guardrails and check that each designated stress stays within the allowed losses against its matched baseline. |

Choose among the feasible policies by reporting their trade-offs in cost, tail time, compliance and risk. If uncertainty spans the decision margins, identify the measurements that would change the choice and use a limited pilot to resolve them. First-draft rankings are conditional on calibration, credible inputs for policy effects and the tested scenario set.

def observed_intervals(rows):
    """Supply a start only for matched task occurrences with a verified finish.
    Supply readiness only when reconstructed from the actual prerequisites.
    """
    timing = rows.copy()
    timing["service_elapsed_s"] = np.nan
    timing["queue_wait_elapsed_s"] = np.nan
    if "start_timestamp" in timing:
        start = pd.to_datetime(timing["start_timestamp"], errors="coerce")
        service = (timing[END] - start).dt.total_seconds()
        timing["service_elapsed_s"] = service.where(service >= 0)
        if "ready_timestamp" in timing:
            ready = pd.to_datetime(timing["ready_timestamp"], errors="coerce")
            wait = (start - ready).dt.total_seconds()
            timing["queue_wait_elapsed_s"] = wait.where(wait >= 0)
    return timing


def mine_log(raw):
    events = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        raw.copy(), case_id=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=END
    )
    dfg, starts, ends = pm4py.discover_dfg(events)
    perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(events)
    submissions = events.loc[events[ACT].eq("Submit application"), [CASE, END]]
    arrival_times = submissions.groupby(CASE)[END].min().sort_values()
    tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(" ".join(TREE.split()))
    return {
        "log": events,
        "variants": pm4py.get_variants_as_tuples(events),
        "dfg": dfg,
        "start_activities": starts,
        "end_activities": ends,
        "performance_dfg": perf_dfg,
        "case_arrival_average_s": arrival_times.diff().dt.total_seconds().mean(),
        "observed_timing": observed_intervals(raw),
        "tree": tree,
    }

def route(case, active_policies):

        "D2" in active_policies
        and case.case_type == "Simple renovation"
        and case.value_eur is not None

    if case.environment:
        reviews.append("Environmental review")

        "notice": case.notice,
        "inspection": not exempt,

def run_experiments(engine, fitted, roster, snapshot, window, design,
                    replications=100, master_seed=7408):
    """design maps stress scenario to {policy ID: factor-level settings}.

    engine dispatches ready work, advances event time, separates work and
    external delays, handles joins and rework, and returns cohort metrics.
    Hold fitted parameters fixed here and call again for outer draws.
    """
    rows = []
    replicates = np.random.SeedSequence(master_seed).spawn(replications)
    for replicate, seed in enumerate(replicates):
        demand_seed, event_seed = seed.spawn(2)
        event_key = int(event_seed.generate_state(1, dtype=np.uint64)[0])
        for stress, policy_settings in design.items():
            inputs = generate_exogenous(
                window, fitted, stress, np.random.default_rng(demand_seed)
            )
            for policy_id, factor_levels in policy_settings.items():
                active = POLICIES[policy_id]
                calendars = build_calendars(
                    window, roster, stress, active, factor_levels
                )
                metrics = engine(
                    window=window,
                    snapshot=deepcopy(snapshot),
                    fitted=fitted,
                    exogenous=deepcopy(inputs),
                    calendars=calendars,
                    active_policies=active,
                    factor_levels=factor_levels,
                    route_case=partial(route, active_policies=active),
                    event_seed=event_key,
                )
                rows.append({
                    "replicate": replicate, "stress": stress,
                    "policy": policy_id, **metrics,
                })
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

Within the simulation engine, derive random inputs from the event seed and stable identifiers and run every policy within a replicate against the same underlying demand and shocks.
