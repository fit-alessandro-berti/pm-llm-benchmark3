# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

## Use cases distinguished

| Use case | What the model must do | Costlier error | Acceptable fitness/precision position |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Exploration / discovery** | Show plausible flow and support questions | Hiding real structure, such as rework, or implying a false norm | High fitness is essential. Low precision is tolerable only if the model is labelled non-normative and does not erase important behavior (F7 holds only partly). |
| **Conformance enforcement / audit** | Accept all policy-valid behavior and reject policy-forbidden behavior | False acceptance of forbidden behavior (audit finding). | **Simulation / play-out** | Generate realistic, policy-valid cases | Generating impossible cases, such as approval with no checks, which poisons the simulation | Precision matters most. Play-out from the model must be plausible. Frequencies need separate calibration. |
| **Stakeholder communication** | Be read *correctly* by compliance, operations, IT and the process owner | Being read confidently and wrongly | Comprehension must be measured as interpretation accuracy, not liking. |
| **Maintenance / governance** | Absorb policy changes cheaply and have clear ownership | Branch explosion, unowned duplicates | Structure size matters, but only alongside semantic fidelity. |

The log-based measures (fitness, precision, generalization, simplicity) all score a model against *observed* behavior. Enforcement is judged against *policy*, which is a different object. I therefore treat the metrics as evidence about the log-model relationship and not as evidence of compliance.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

## Strongest case for S

- **Stable under the split.** The holdout fitness of 0.981 is close to training and the model is cheap to run.
- **Honest about one unobserved attribute.** Because the external/internal flag is absent from the log, Supplier Review *must* be treated as optional at the control-flow level. S does that.
- **Accepts the valid probes.** It accepts B1, B2, B5 and B7, which are the valid unseen combinations (alternate check order, Supplier plus Auto Approval, Expedite with Supplier) that C rejects (F2, F4). It implements P5 (exactly one approval) correctly.
- **Tolerates missing events without false alarms.** It is easy to read and cheap to maintain.
- **Fit for a labelled exploration use** (F7).

## Strongest case against S

**"Robust to missing events" is the same property as "blind to missing events".** Optionalizing Credit Check and Technical Review means a dossier that skipped the credit check entirely is indistinguishable from one whose event was never logged. The model cannot detect the most important control failure. The full approval-without-checks path is accepted, 72% of its language violates P2 or P4, and the 0.584 precision does not reveal that (§2). S has no Correct Dossier and no loop, so rework is invisible even for exploration. Its rejection of B8 (repeated check) is an accident of omission, not a rule.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

## Strongest case for C

- **Auditability.** An explicit allow-list is easy to audit case by case, and nothing outside it is silently accepted.
- **Forbidden probes.** It rejects B3, B4 and B8, and accepts B1 and B7.
- **F8's closed-world posture** is a legitimate governance stance *if* the list is the approved policy. Under it, a rejection is an escalation rather than a verdict of fraud.
- **High precision is partly real.** On this log the model has almost no behavior beyond what occurs.
- **Stable semantics.** There are no silent transitions to reason about.

## Strongest case against C

- **It is not the policy.** P2 and P4 are *rules*, not variants. Model C rejects B2, a trace stakeholders explicitly label valid (Technical  Credit  Auto Approval). It also rejects B5, B6 and unlisted combinations such as `Technical, Credit, Supplier, Manager`. The F8 rule "unseen = noncompliant" contradicts stakeholders' own policy.
- **Branch enumeration.** The factors are: check order (2), Supplier/Expedite sequences (5), approval type (2), and correction scope (4 per cycle). That gives 20 correction-free branches, 80 more for exactly one correction, and 320 for exactly two, since each cycle multiplies by 4 (the cycle count is unbounded in reality). Model C has 8 branches and already misses most of the correction-free ones. C-style maintenance scales exponentially in the number of policy factors.
- **Unseen combinations.** Auto Approval exists only in the simplest branch. Reversed check order exists only in the no-Supplier, no-Expedite branch. Expedite before Supplier is absent, though P4 allows it.
- **Correction handling.** One branch repeats *both* checks in the original order. There is no way to say "only Technical Review" (F6) and no way to repeat corrections. The B6 outcome (reject) is therefore uninformative, because the model cannot represent the policy decision at all.
- **Holdout degradation.** The drop from 0.973 to 0.744 is consistent with memorization. The training fitness of 0.973 (not 1.0) shows it does not even cover its own training log.
- **Is precision 0.941 a memorization artifact?** Largely yes. The model was built *from* training variants, so precision on the training log is nearly guaranteed. Precision on the holdout and against a negative suite is unreported. The precision measure could remain high even if C were wrong about policy everywhere outside the 8 traces.
- **Duplicate labels and ownership.** Nine copies of Credit Check and Technical Review mean that a policy change ("Credit Check now requires X") needs edits in up to 9 places, with no link asserting they are the same control (F3). Alignments also become ambiguous, with many equal-cost optimal alignments, which hurts diagnostic stability and runtime.
- **Maintainability (F2, F3).** Adding one new legal combination means adding a branch, and the model has no way to state *why* it is legal.

# 7. Third Alternative

## Strategy

**A block-structured procedural backbone (expressed as a POWL partial order, with an equivalent process tree for cross-checking), plus an explicit policy layer for what the log cannot express.**

The backbone encodes only what P1–P7 and the log can support *structurally*. Everything attribute-dependent (Supplier Review obligation, recheck scope) is decided in a declarative post-hoc layer that returns explicit *pending* verdicts when the attribute is missing. It does not silently relax the model.

## 7.1 Compact formal description

With RD = Register Dossier, CC = Credit Check, TR = Technical Review, SR = Supplier Review, ER = Expedite Review, CD = Correct Dossier, MA = Manager Approval, AA = Auto Approval, RO = Release Order:

```
M3 = ->( RD,
         +(CC, TR),
         *( tau, ->( CD, X( CC, TR, +(CC, TR) ) ) ),
         +( X(SR, tau), X(ER, tau) ),
         X(MA, AA),
         RO )
```

As a POWL strict partial order: nodes {RD, CC, TR, LOOP, SR?, ER?, APPR, RO}; edges RDCC, RDTR, CCLOOP, TRLOOP, LOOPSR?, LOOPER?, SR?APPR, ER?APPR, APPRRO. SR? and ER? are unordered, so either order is allowed. The language is 20 traces with no correction and 80 with exactly one, in general unbounded in correction cycles.

**Language relations (derived by hand):** L(C)  L(M3). All 8 Model C traces are in M3. The correction-free part of L(M3)  L(S). M3 therefore sits between the two inputs on the correction-free part, and adds what neither has: legal correction variants.

## 7.2 Explicit semantics

1. **Mandatory checks.** CC and TR each occur in the first pass, in either order or concurrently. No path reaches approval otherwise. There is no optional CC or TR anywhere in the model.
2. **Optional Supplier Review.** It is optional at control-flow level *only because the attribute is missing*. The policy layer makes it required for `sourcing == "external"` and forbidden for `"internal"`. If `sourcing` is unknown, a trace without SR gets the verdict `PENDING`, not `CONFORMING`.
3. **Expedite Review.** It is optional, occurs at most once, and follows *all* checks, including rechecks. It precedes approval. It may come before or after SR (they are concurrent in the partial order), as P4 allows.
4. **Exactly one approval.** `X(MA, AA)` occurs once, after SR?/ER?, before RO. The eligibility of AA (Q4) is left to a future data rule. The model does not invent one.
5. **Correction and recheck.** The loop `*(tau, ->(CD, recheck))` is the *only* path that repeats a check. CD must precede every recheck (P7) and each CD must be followed by a non-empty recheck: CC only, TR only, or both (concurrent). The model accepts all three scopes. The policy layer verifies the scope when an `affected_checks` attribute exists. Without it B6 gets `PENDING` rather than `CONFORMING`. Corrections occur only after the first checks and before SR/ER (Q2), which is a deliberate conservative choice.
6. **Repeated check without CD** (B8) is structurally impossible, which implements P7 directly.

**Optionality and tau ledger** (governance artifact): the silent transitions are exactly (a) the loop's do-part, (b) SR optional, (c) ER optional. Any new optionality needs a signed justification.

## 7.3 PM4Py code for the main model

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

try:
    from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
        Transition, SilentTransition, OperatorPOWL, StrictPartialOrder)
    HAVE_POWL = True
except ImportError:          # older PM4Py without POWL
    HAVE_POWL = False

# Equivalent block-structured process tree (used as fallback and as a cross-check).
THIRD_TREE = (
    "->('Register Dossier',"
    " +('Credit Check','Technical Review'),"
    " *(tau, ->('Correct Dossier',"
    "      X('Credit Check','Technical Review', +('Credit Check','Technical Review')))),"
    " +(X('Supplier Review', tau), X('Expedite Review', tau)),"
    " X('Manager Approval','Auto Approval'),"
    " 'Release Order')"
)

def build_model_third_powl():
    T = lambda label: Transition(label=label)              # each occurrence is a fresh object
    def opt(x):                                            # the ONLY optional constructs
        return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[x, SilentTransition()])

    reg, cc, tr, rel = (T("Register Dossier"), T("Credit Check"),
                        T("Technical Review"), T("Release Order"))

    # Recheck after a correction: CC only | TR only | both (concurrent)
    both = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[T("Credit Check"), T("Technical Review")])  # no edge => concurrent
    recheck = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                           children=[T("Credit Check"), T("Technical Review"), both])
    redo = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[T("Correct Dossier"), recheck])
    cd = [n for n in redo.order.get_children() if False]   # placeholder to keep linters quiet
    corr = redo.children[0] if hasattr(redo, "children") else None
    return redo, recheck  # helper, replaced below
```

That draft is awkward because of object bookkeeping. This is the clean version to use (it supersedes the draft above):

```python
def build_model_third_powl():
    T = lambda label: Transition(label=label)
    def opt(x):
        return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[x, SilentTransition()])

    reg, cc, tr, rel = (T("Register Dossier"), T("Credit Check"),
                        T("Technical Review"), T("Release Order"))

    corr = T("Correct Dossier")
    both = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[T("Credit Check"), T("Technical Review")])
    recheck = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                           children=[T("Credit Check"), T("Technical Review"), both])
    redo = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[corr, recheck])
    redo.order.add_edge(corr, recheck)
    loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[SilentTransition(), redo])

    sup, exp = opt(T("Supplier Review")), opt(T("Expedite Review"))
    appr = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                        children=[T("Manager Approval"), T("Auto Approval")])

    root = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[reg, cc, tr, loop, sup, exp, appr, rel])
    for a, b in [(reg, cc), (reg, tr), (cc, loop), (tr, loop),
                 (loop, sup), (loop, exp), (sup, appr), (exp, appr), (appr, rel)]:
        root.order.add_edge(a, b)
    return root

model_third_tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(THIRD_TREE)
model_third = build_model_third_powl() if HAVE_POWL else model_third_tree

def to_petri(model):
    return pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)   # accepts POWL in recent PM4Py; otherwise use the tree

net3, im3, fm3 = to_petri(model_third)
```

```python
    case_attrs = case_attrs or {}
    F = []
    add = lambda sev, code, msg: F.append((sev, code, msg))
    idx = lambda a: [i for i, x in enumerate(acts) if x == a]

    if not acts or acts[0] != policy["first"]:  add("VIOLATION", "P1_FIRST", "first activity")
    if not acts or acts[-1] != policy["last"] or len(idx(policy["last"])) != 1:
        add("VIOLATION", "P1_LAST", "release not exactly once, last")

    ap = [i for i, a in enumerate(acts) if a in policy["exactly_one_of"]]
    if len(ap) != 1: add("VIOLATION", "P5_APPROVAL", "need exactly one approval")
    a_i = ap[0] if ap else len(acts)

    last_check = -1
    for c in policy["mandatory_before_approval"]:
        ii = idx(c)
        if not ii or ii[0] > a_i:
            add("VIOLATION", "P2_MANDATORY", f"{c} missing or after approval")
        else:
            last_check = max(last_check, ii[-1])
        for prev, cur in zip(ii, ii[1:]):
            if policy["repeat_requires_between"][c] not in acts[prev + 1:cur]:
                add("VIOLATION", "P7_REPEAT_NO_CORRECTION", f"{c} repeated without correction")

    ex = idx("Expedite Review")
    if len(ex) > policy["expedite"]["max"]: add("VIOLATION", "P4_EXPEDITE_COUNT", "expedite > once")
    for e in ex:
        if e < last_check or e > a_i:
            add("VIOLATION", "P4_EXPEDITE_ORDER", "expedite must follow all checks, precede approval")

    has_sr = "Supplier Review" in acts
    if has_sr and idx("Supplier Review")[0] > a_i:
        add("VIOLATION", "P3_SUPPLIER_AFTER_APPROVAL", "supplier review after approval")
    if policy["open_policy"]["Q1_supplier_after_checks"] and has_sr \
            and min(idx("Supplier Review")) < last_check:
        add("OPEN_POLICY", "Q1_SUPPLIER_ORDER", "supplier before a check: ruling needed")
    rule = policy["conditional"]["Supplier Review"]
    src = case_attrs.get(rule["attr"])
    if src == rule["required_if"] and not has_sr:
        add("VIOLATION", "P3_EXTERNAL_NO_SUPPLIER", "external order without supplier review")
    elif src == rule["forbidden_if"] and has_sr:
        add("VIOLATION", "P3_INTERNAL_WITH_SUPPLIER", "internal order with supplier review")
    elif src is None and not has_sr:
        add("PENDING", "P3_ATTR_UNKNOWN", "cannot judge missing Supplier Review; sourcing unknown")

    cds = idx("Correct Dossier")
    for k, i in enumerate(cds):
        if i < last_check and i < min([j for c in MANDATORY for j in idx(c)] or [0]):
            add("VIOLATION", "P6_CORRECT_BEFORE_CHECKS", "correction before any check")
        j, rechecked = i + 1, set()
        while j < len(acts) and acts[j] in MANDATORY:
            rechecked.add(acts[j]); j += 1
        if not rechecked and not policy["open_policy"]["Q3_empty_recheck_allowed"]:
            add("OPEN_POLICY", "Q3_EMPTY_RECHECK", "correction without recheck: ruling needed")
        if correction_scopes is not None and k < len(correction_scopes):
            if rechecked != set(correction_scopes[k]):
                add("VIOLATION", "P6_SCOPE_MISMATCH", f"rechecked {rechecked}, affected {correction_scopes[k]}")
        elif rechecked and rechecked != set(MANDATORY):
            add("PENDING", "P6_SCOPE_UNVERIFIED", "partial recheck; affected_checks attribute missing")
        if any(a in acts[i + 1:j] for a in ()): pass
    if any(acts[i] in ("Supplier Review", "Expedite Review") for i in range(min(cds, default=len(acts)))) and cds:
        pass  # Q2 handled structurally by the model; add explicit rule when the owner rules
    if len(cds) > policy["rework_warn_threshold"]:
        add("PENDING", "P6_REWORK_THRESHOLD", "excessive correction cycles")

    worst = max((SEV[s] for s, _, _ in F), default=0)
    verdict = {3: "VIOLATION", 2: "OPEN_POLICY", 1: "CONFORMING_PENDING_ATTRIBUTE", 0: "CONFORMING"}[worst]
    return verdict, F
```

The checker duplicates the structural rules deliberately (defense in depth, and a policy oracle independent of the net). Two lines are placeholders (marked `pass`) for the Q2 rule, which needs a ruling before it is coded. The oracle and model must be authored and reviewed by *different people* (§9).

## 7.5 Limitations caused by missing attributes

- **No `sourcing`.** Supplier Review obligation cannot be enforced. External orders with no Supplier Review are `PENDING`, not violations. Internal orders that performed Supplier Review also cannot be flagged. The 20-trace correction-free language and the optional SR? are a *control-flow approximation*.
- **No `affected_checks`.** The model accepts CC-only, TR-only and both rechecks on every correction (B6 is structurally accepted, verdict `PENDING`). A correction that affected TR but triggered a CC-only recheck is *undetectable*.
- **Open rulings.** The conservative choices for Q1 (SR after checks), Q2 (corrections only before SR/ER), Q3 (non-empty rechecks) and Q5 (no consecutive corrections, which would need `*(CD, tau)`) reject behavior stakeholders have not declared invalid. Each is an `OPEN_POLICY` finding and not a violation.
- **Auto Approval eligibility** (Q4) is not modeled.
- **Missing events are not repaired.** A dropped Credit Check event will appear as a violation. That is deliberate. The remedy is a data-quality investigation, not a looser model (§9 tests this).

## 9.1 Reproducible splits and preprocessing

```python
import hashlib, random, pandas as pd, pm4py

PREPROC_VERSION = "v1"   # publish the code and hash it with the log
def load_log(path):
    df = pm4py.read_xes(path)                       # or read_csv + format_dataframe
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id="case:concept:name",
                                activity_key="concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp")
    df = df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"], kind="mergesort")  # stable tie-break
    return df

def log_fingerprint(df):
    return hashlib.sha256(pd.util.hash_pandas_object(df[["case:concept:name","concept:name","time:timestamp"]],
                                                     index=False).values.tobytes()).hexdigest()

def hash_split(case_ids, salt, train_pct=80):
    bucket = lambda c: int(hashlib.sha256(f"{salt}|{c}".encode()).hexdigest(), 16) % 100
    train = {c for c in case_ids if bucket(c) < train_pct}
    return train, set(case_ids) - train

SEEDS = [11, 23, 47, 59, 71, 83, 97, 101, 113, 127]      # report the distribution, not one draw

def stratified_split(df, strata_cols, seed, frac=0.8):
    cases = df.drop_duplicates("case:concept:name")
    train = (cases.groupby(strata_cols, dropna=False, group_keys=False)
                  .apply(lambda g: g.sample(frac=frac, random_state=seed)))
    tr = set(train["case:concept:name"])
    return df[df["case:concept:name"].isin(tr)], df[~df["case:concept:name"].isin(tr)]

def variant_grouped_split(df, seed, frac=0.8):          # all cases of a variant go to the same side
    var = df.groupby("case:concept:name")["concept:name"].agg(tuple)
    uniq = sorted(set(var)); random.Random(seed).shuffle(uniq)
    tr_var = set(uniq[:int(frac * len(uniq))])
    tr = set(var[var.isin(tr_var)].index)
    return df[df["case:concept:name"].isin(tr)], df[~df["case:concept:name"].isin(tr)]
```

Three split families are run for each model: random-case (10 seeds), attribute-stratified, and variant-grouped (the unseen-variant stress test). A temporal split is added too. Report median and range across seeds.

## 9.3 Curated negative and positive suites

- Build a **positive suite** by enumerating policy-valid traces from the rules (not from the log), and a **negative suite** by mutating valid seeds and from expert curation:
  - delete each mandatory check, and delete both
  - Expedite before or between checks
  - duplicate check without a correction
  - double approval, and Release before approval
  - correction after approval
  - Supplier after approval
- The **oracle** is `check_trace`. Compliance must co-author it, and it must be reviewed by someone other than the model author (mitigating circularity).
- Each suite item has a class (`critical`, `ordinary`, `open_policy`). Open-policy items are reported separately and never counted as failures.

```python
from datetime import datetime, timedelta

def traces_to_df(traces, attrs=None):
    rows, t0 = [], datetime(2024, 1, 1)
    for k, tr in enumerate(traces):
        for j, a in enumerate(tr):
            rows.append({"case:concept:name": f"s{k}", "concept:name": a,
                         "time:timestamp": t0 + timedelta(minutes=j)})
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(pd.DataFrame(rows), case_id="case:concept:name",
                                  activity_key="concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp")

def accepts(traces, net, im, fm):
    diag = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(traces_to_df(traces), net, im, fm)
    return [abs(d["fitness"] - 1.0) < 1e-9 for d in diag]      # language membership, not partial credit

def confusion(suite, net, im, fm):       # suite: list of (trace, expected_accept, cls)
    acc = accepts([t for t, _, _ in suite], net, im, fm)
    out = {"critical_false_accept": 0, "false_reject": 0, "n": len(suite)}
    for (t, exp, cls), a in zip(suite, acc):
        if cls == "open_policy": continue
        if a and not exp and cls == "critical": out["critical_false_accept"] += 1
        if (not a) and exp: out["false_reject"] += 1
    return out
```

```python
def perturb(trace, kind, rng):
    t = list(trace); i = rng.randrange(len(t))
    if kind == "drop": del t[i]
    if kind == "dup":  t.insert(i, t[i])
    if kind == "swap" and len(t) > 1: j = min(i + 1, len(t) - 1); t[i], t[j] = t[j], t[i]
```

```python
from pm4py.objects.petri_net import semantics
from pm4py.algo.simulation.playout.petri_net import algorithm as pn_playout

def structural_checks(net, im, fm, cap=200_000):
    sound = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=False)
    seen, fired, stack = set(), set(), [im]
    while stack and len(seen) < cap:
        m = stack.pop()
```

## 9.9 Stakeholder comprehension protocol

- **Participants:** at least 6 per group (compliance, operations, IT architecture, analysts, process owner), recruited and stratified in advance.
- **Materials:** all models rendered in the same notation with the same legend, plus a one-page policy description for the third model.
- **Design:** within-subject, counterbalanced across model order (Latin square), with different but equivalent question sets per presentation to avoid learning effects.
- **Tasks:** 12 questions per model, with ground truth from the policy oracle. Examples: "Can approval happen before Credit Check?", "Can a Technical Review be repeated without Correct Dossier?", "Is a missing Supplier Review a violation?"
- **Measures:** *interpretation accuracy* (primary), time per question, confidence (Brier score), and Likert clarity (secondary, reported separately). A maintainer task ("add a rule that...") measures edit correctness and time.
- **Blinding and analysis:** scorers are blind to model identity. Pre-register the questions. Report bootstrap confidence intervals and the per-group breakdown.

## 9.10 Decision rule: reject despite good aggregate metrics

A model is **rejected for enforcement** if *any* of the following holds (thresholds are proposals):

1. Any false acceptance of a `critical` negative (missing mandatory check, approval before checks, Expedite before checks, double approval, repeat without correction).
2. Recall on policy-valid unseen-combination and stratified cells below 95% (a policy-valid class below 90% in any stratum).
3. Dropping a mandatory event leaves fitness at 1.0 in any perturbation run.
4. Verdicts flip under the cost sweep for critical items.
5. The net is unsound, or has dead transitions or unreachable required behavior.
6. Results are not reproducible (fingerprint mismatch, unpublished preprocessing, or seed-to-seed range beyond a pre-agreed tolerance).
7. Comprehension accuracy is below 80% in any stakeholder group, or the maintainer task fails.
8. Unresolved `OPEN_POLICY` items are more than a pre-agreed share of production traces.

A model is **rejected for exploration** if it hides an important behavior class (for example, rework) that appears in more than a pre-agreed share of cases.

# 11. Dissenting View

**Rebuttal.** The third alternative is a model-shaped policy that nobody has ratified. I chose Supplier Review after both checks, corrections only before Supplier and Expedite, and non-empty rechecks. Each choice rejects behavior stakeholders never declared invalid, which is the sin I charge Model C with, only less visibly. Its credibility rests on a hand-written oracle and negative suite, so the validation is partly circular. Splitting truth across a POWL model, a dictionary and a Python checker creates three places to update, which is exactly the maintainability problem F3 attacks. The attributes that would justify the design (`sourcing`, `affected_checks`) do not exist, so the headline gains are unverifiable today, and the `PENDING` verdicts may bury auditors in unresolved cases. A simpler route, Model S plus six DECLARE-style constraints and a correction loop, would probably match its negative-suite performance, be easier to explain (S scored 4.4), and avoid inventing ordering policy. A cautious auditor could also argue that Model C's closed-world posture is the only defensible stance until the policy is written down: a false rejection costs paperwork, while a false acceptance costs an audit finding.

**Evidence that would change my decision**

- **S plus declarative constraints wins.** If the §9 suite shows it matches the third model on critical negatives and unseen-combination recall, with higher comprehension accuracy and fewer maintenance edits, I would recommend it instead.
- **Owner rulings change the structure.** If the owner rules that Supplier Review may precede the checks, or that corrections happen after Expedite, I would restructure, and the partial-order form becomes more valuable.
- **Log evidence on corrections.** If more than a small share of real cases correct after Supplier or Expedite Review, or make a correction with no recheck, the conservative loop placement is wrong.
- **Attributes never arrive.** If `sourcing` and `affected_checks` cannot be captured, the extra machinery buys little, and a stricter allow-list such as a governed C variant might be the honest choice.
- **Stakeholder comprehension.** If accuracy on the third model is below the threshold in any group, I would simplify it or withdraw the compliance recommendation.
