## 1. Duplicate-handling decision tree

**Separate event identity, observation identity, and business abstraction level.** Matching labels, timestamps, or objects generates candidates; it does not establish duplication.

Apply these gates in order:

1. **Exact technical replay?** The same source record/version, unchanged business payload, and identical qualified E2O relations were ingested repeatedly. **Drop the redundant copy**, retaining its ingestion provenance. A payload hash alone is insufficient.
2. **Conflicting identity?** A supposedly immutable source key identifies incompatible records. **Quarantine for review**; investigate revisions, key reuse, or integration errors.
3. **Harmonization collision?** Source meanings differ despite identical integrated labels. **Retain both with refined labels or qualifiers.**
4. **Known distinct transactions?** Different transaction-defining objects and source evidence establish distinct occurrences. **Retain both unchanged.** Different object sets alone are not sufficient evidence either way.
5. **Same physical/business occurrence verified across systems?** **Merge attributes and compatible relations**, preserving every source observation. Conflicting attributes require field-level authority, not whole-system precedence.
6. **Derived summary or status observation?** Normally **retain both with refined labels or qualifiers**. Merge only in an explicitly chosen coarser business-event view, preserving derivation lineage.
7. **Unresolved candidate?** **Retain both unchanged**, flagged for review. Quarantine instead when identity or integrity conflicts make ordinary analysis unsafe.

No displayed pair is demonstrably an exact technical replay: all supplied source keys are distinct. Therefore, counts below start from **17 events and 44 listed E2O incidences**, without inventing additional duplicate rows.

## 3. Timestamp-tie ordering rules

Maintain an evidence-labelled precedence DAG and produce a deterministic linear extension only for serialization.

**Semantic constraints** include explicit domain precedence, verified message dependencies, and validated lifecycle rules on the *same relevant object*. Thus, e12 precedes e13 for M1; this does not establish authorization for M2. Source-specific meanings establish that acceptance and handover are distinct.

**Within-source sequence** supplies observation-order constraints within that source only. Retain sequence evidence from *all* merged observations: e04’s scanner sequence remains relevant after canonicalization into e03. Sequence order does not automatically establish cross-object physical causality.

**Ingestion time is technical evidence**, useful for latency diagnostics—not primary business ordering. For incomparable events, use stable canonical IDs as the deterministic fallback. Such fallback choices are not semantic constraints.

Reject cycles and conflicts with non-tied timestamps for review. Do not silently override them with ingestion order.

The following orders use source sequence as trustworthy observation order. They also assume **both listed captures contributed to e15**; remove those dependency edges when message lineage cannot support that assumption.

**10:00, after conservative merging:**
Constraints are `e03 ≺ e05`, `e03 ≺ e06`, and `e06 ≺ e07`. The selected extension is:

`e03, e05, e06, e07`

Alternatives include `e03, e06, e05, e07` and `e03, e06, e07, e05`. A confirmed “all constituent picks precede packing” rule would additionally impose `e05 ≺ e06`; do not silently infer it from labels.

**10:15:**
The explicit constraint is `e09 ≺ e10`. Select `e08, e09, e10`; `e09, e08, e10` is also admissible without a validated release-before-acceptance rule.

**12:00, after e16 merges into e11:**
Use `e12 ≺ e13 ≺ e14` from PSP observation order, `e11 ≺ e15` from ERP sequence, and the stated summary-contributor assumption. Both

`e11, e12, e13, e14, e15`

and

`e12, e13, e14, e11, e15`

are admissible. Invoice issuance before authorization is not supplied as a universal rule.

Adding microseconds **before** establishing these constraints merely fossilizes an arbitrary row order. Keep the DAG and original timestamps authoritative; a total order neither proves physical sequence nor eliminates possible concurrency.

## 4. Conservative cleaning pipeline

Archive the complete OCEL, validate identifiers and references, and inspect duplicate candidates before transforming anything.

Merge only e03/e04 and e11/e16. Retain the packing-status and invoice-balance observations, refining e07 and e15. Restore the distinction between warehouse release and carrier acceptance. Preserve original identifiers, integrated labels, timestamps, sources, and source keys.

Construct precedence from the original observations, then transfer it through the canonical-ID mapping. Store deterministic ranks separately. Create timestamp-adjusted adapters only for consumers that require unique timestamps.

The result contains **15 events and 39 E2O incidences**: removing redundant observations reduces incidences by three for e04 and two for e16. All eight objects remain.

## 5. Aggressive alternatives

**Evidence-based coarse projection — 14 events, 37 E2O incidences.**
Start with the verified merges, then represent e06/e07 as one packing transition. Their relation union is e06’s existing set. Retain both original observations outside the canonical event table. This is defensible for physical-fulfilment analysis, but hides ERP status propagation and its independently measurable reliability.

**Guarded mechanical PM4Py experiment — 13 events, 34 E2O incidences.**
To preserve the explicit constraints, exclude e05 and all payment events from mechanical merging. Apply the library to remaining equal-label/time groups. This merges e03/e04, e06/e07, e08/e09, and e11/e16, while retaining the original harmonized labels.

This branch deliberately demonstrates damage: release and acceptance disappear into one event; the packing observation layer disappears; e15 still resembles another capture. It is a **diagnostic dataset, not an approved cleaned log**.

An unguarded call would be expected to produce **10 events and 28 incidences** here, also collapsing the distinct picks and captures. That rejected counterfactual is not executed.

`have_common_object=True` cannot protect e13/e14: they share **O1 and V1**, although their transaction-defining Payment objects differ. PM4Py’s implementation also generates new IDs and retains one event row rather than performing source-authority reconciliation. ([GitHub][1])

## 6. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation

PM4Py’s drop function deduplicates relation rows by activity, timestamp, and object—not immutable source identity. Its sorting helper orders the events table; its timedelta helper adds index-based **milliseconds**, not microseconds. The wrapper below scopes deletion, restores stable IDs, and synchronizes relations. ([GitHub][1])

The block assumes the supplied identifiers and standard OCEL tables. Qualified-relation conflicts fail closed. It is source-checked, but was not integration-tested against an installed PM4Py package here.

```python
from copy import deepcopy
from itertools import product
import warnings
import pandas as pd
import networkx as nx
import pm4py

E, A, T = (ocel_raw.event_id_column, ocel_raw.event_activity,
           ocel_raw.event_timestamp)
O, OT, Q = (ocel_raw.object_id_column, ocel_raw.object_type_column,
            ocel_raw.qualifier)

RULE_VERSION = "ocel-clean-v1"
AUTHORITY = {"location": "WMS"}  # Add approved invoice-field authorities here.
raw_archive = deepcopy(ocel_raw)  # Persist ALL OCEL tables.
base = deepcopy(ocel_raw)

required = {E, A, T, "source", "source_event_key", "source_seq",
            "ingested_at", "payload_hash"}
assert required <= set(base.events.columns)

for old, current in [("original_eid", E), ("original_activity", A),
                     ("original_timestamp", T)]:
    if old not in base.events:
        base.events[old] = base.events[current].copy()

base.events[T] = pd.to_datetime(base.events[T], utc=True)
base.events["original_timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(
    base.events["original_timestamp"], utc=True)
base.events["source_seq"] = pd.to_numeric(base.events["source_seq"])
if Q not in base.relations:
    base.relations[Q] = None


def sync(x):
    """Synchronize denormalized relation fields; never infer new links."""
    ev = x.events.set_index(E)
    for col in [A, T, "original_activity", "original_timestamp"]:
        x.relations[col] = x.relations[E].map(ev[col])
    return x


def validate(x):
    assert x.events[E].notna().all() and x.events[E].is_unique
    assert x.objects[O].notna().all() and x.objects[O].is_unique
    assert x.events[T].notna().all()

    ei, oi = set(x.events[E]), set(x.objects[O])
    assert set(x.relations[E]) <= ei
    assert set(x.relations[O]) <= oi

    ev, ob = x.events.set_index(E), x.objects.set_index(O)
    for col in [A, T]:
        assert x.relations[col].eq(x.relations[E].map(ev[col])).all()
    assert x.relations[OT].eq(x.relations[O].map(ob[OT])).all()

    for name, cols, valid in [
        ("e2e", [E, E + "_2"], ei),
        ("o2o", [O, O + "_2"], oi),
        ("object_changes", [O], oi),
    ]:
        table = getattr(x, name, None)
        if table is not None and not table.empty:
            for col in cols:
                assert set(table[col]) <= valid


# Validate before synchronization could conceal input mismatches.
# Repeated event IDs fail closed: inspect technical replays/key conflicts first.
base.relations[T] = pd.to_datetime(base.relations[T], utc=True)
validate(base)
sync(base)


def candidates(x):
    """Candidate generation is NOT authorization to merge."""
    object_sets = x.relations.groupby(E)[O].agg(
        lambda s: tuple(sorted(set(s))))
    report = x.events[
        [E, A, T, "source", "source_event_key", "payload_hash"]
    ].copy()
    report["objects"] = report[E].map(object_sets)
    return report[
        report.duplicated([A, T], keep=False)
    ].sort_values([T, A, E])


candidate_report = candidates(base)
source_key_review = base.events[base.events.duplicated(
    ["source", "source_event_key"], keep=False)]
# Source-key reuse requires payload/version/relation checks, not blind deletion.
print(candidate_report.to_string(index=False))


def fresh():
    return {
        "ocel": deepcopy(base),
        "members": {e: [e] for e in base.events[E]},
        "audit": [],
    }


def merge_group(state, ids, rule, confidence, method="custom", subset=False):
    """Merge an explicitly selected group, retaining complete input snapshots."""
    x = state["ocel"]
    ids, canonical = sorted(ids), min(ids)
    rows = x.events.set_index(E).loc[ids].reset_index()
    rel = x.relations[x.relations[E].isin(ids)].copy()

    assert rows[T].nunique() == 1 and rows[A].nunique() == 1
    # This example has no supplied qualifiers. Never silently erase real roles.
    assert rel[Q].isna().all(), "Qualified E2O relations require review"

    signatures = {e: set(rel.loc[rel[E].eq(e), O]) for e in ids}
    union = set().union(*signatures.values())
    if method != "library":
        if subset:
            assert signatures[canonical] == union
        else:
            assert all(s == union for s in signatures.values())

    record = rows.set_index(E).loc[canonical].to_dict()
    record[E] = canonical
    conflicts = {}

    # Technical fields describe the representative, not the sole source.
    # Every contributing source record is retained in the audit/archive.
    technical = required | {
        "original_eid", "original_activity", "original_timestamp"
    }
    for col in rows.columns.difference(list(technical)):
        values = rows.loc[rows[col].notna(), [E, "source", col]]
        unique = {repr(v) for v in values[col]}
        if len(unique) == 1:
            record[col] = values.iloc[0][col]
        elif len(unique) > 1:
            conflicts[col] = values.to_dict("records")
            preferred = values[values["source"].eq(AUTHORITY.get(col))]
            record[col] = (
                preferred.iloc[0][col] if len(preferred) == 1 else pd.NA
            )

    result_rel = rel.copy()
    if method in {"drop", "library"}:
        part = deepcopy(x)
        part.events = rows.sort_values(E).reset_index(drop=True)
        part.relations = rel.sort_values([E, O]).reset_index(drop=True)

        if method == "drop":
            # Safe only on this verified equal-relation group.
            # Sorting puts the chosen canonical event first.
            assert all(s == union for s in signatures.values())
            part = pm4py.ocel_drop_duplicates(part)
            assert set(part.events[E]) == {canonical}
        else:
            # WARNING: destructive sensitivity experiment, not semantic approval.
            part = pm4py.ocel_merge_duplicates(
                part, have_common_object=True)
            assert len(part.events) == 1, "Unexpected library grouping; review"

        result_rel = part.relations.copy()
        assert set(result_rel[O]) == union

    # Restore a stable canonical ID, including after library-generated IDs.
    result_rel[E] = canonical
    result_rel = result_rel.drop_duplicates([E, O, Q])
    x.events = pd.concat([
        x.events[~x.events[E].isin(ids)],
        pd.DataFrame([record]),
    ], ignore_index=True)
    x.relations = pd.concat([
        x.relations[~x.relations[E].isin(ids)],
        result_rel,
    ], ignore_index=True)

    # Preserve native E2E references; collapsed native relations require review.
    if not x.e2e.empty:
        remap = {e: canonical for e in ids}
        for col in [E, E + "_2"]:
            x.e2e[col] = x.e2e[col].replace(remap)
        assert not x.e2e[E].eq(x.e2e[E + "_2"]).any(), "Collapsed E2E: review"

    originals = sorted({
        original for e in ids for original in state["members"][e]
    })
    for e in ids:
        del state["members"][e]
    state["members"][canonical] = originals

    state["audit"].append({
        "canonical_eid": canonical,
        "source_eids": originals,
        "rule": rule,
        "version": RULE_VERSION,
        "pm4py_version": getattr(pm4py, "__version__", "unknown"),
        "confidence": confidence,
        "input_events": rows.to_dict("records"),
        "input_relations": rel.to_dict("records"),
        "attribute_conflicts": conflicts,
        "method": method,
    })
    sync(x)
    validate(x)


def refine(state):
    x = state["ocel"]
    labels = {
        "e07": "Record ERP Packed Status",
        "e08": "Release from Warehouse",
        "e09": "Accept by Carrier",
        "e15": "Update Invoice Balance",
    }
    for eid, label in labels.items():
        mask = x.events[E].eq(eid)
        if mask.any():
            state["audit"].append({
                "canonical_eid": eid,
                "rule": "label-refinement",
                "version": RULE_VERSION,
                "before": x.events.loc[mask, A].iloc[0],
                "after": label,
            })
            x.events.loc[mask, A] = label
    sync(x)
    validate(x)


raw = fresh()

conservative = fresh()
merge_group(
    conservative, ["e03", "e04"],
    "reconciled-scan", "high", method="drop")
merge_group(
    conservative, ["e11", "e16"],
    "verified-invoice-issuance", "high")

# Independent coarse scenario; optional merging precedes label refinement.
evidence = deepcopy(conservative)
merge_group(
    evidence, ["e06", "e07"],
    "coarse-pack-projection", "conditional", subset=True)
refine(conservative)
refine(evidence)

aggressive = fresh()
# Hard guards preserve the distinct pick and every payment/summary event.
protected = {"e05", "e12", "e13", "e14", "e15"}
eligible = base.events[~base.events[E].isin(protected)]
warnings.warn(
    "Aggressive branch intentionally ignores known logistics semantics.")
for _, group in eligible.groupby([A, T], sort=True):
    if len(group) > 1:
        merge_group(
            aggressive, group[E].tolist(),
            "guarded-library-collision", "not semantically approved",
            method="library")


def ordered_views(state):
    x = deepcopy(state["ocel"])
    mapping = {
        raw_id: e
        for e, roots in state["members"].items()
        for raw_id in roots
    }
    times = x.events.set_index(E)[T].to_dict()
    graph, constraints = nx.DiGraph(), []
    graph.add_nodes_from(x.events[E])

    def edge(a, b, reason):
        u, v = mapping[a], mapping[b]
        constraints.append((a, b, u, v, reason))
        if u == v:
            # Only this explicitly coarsened observation dependency may contract.
            assert (a, b) == ("e06", "e07"), "Collapsed precedence: quarantine"
            return
        assert times[u] <= times[v], "Clock/ordering conflict: quarantine"
        if times[u] == times[v]:
            graph.add_edge(u, v)
        # For distinct timestamps, chronology already supplies the ordering.

    for a, b, reason in [
        ("e06", "e07", "derived packing observation"),
        ("e09", "e10", "known carrier precedence"),
        ("e12", "e13", "M1 authorization before capture"),
        # Explicit assumption: BOTH captures contribute to this ERP summary.
        ("e13", "e15", "assumed summary contributor"),
        ("e14", "e15", "assumed summary contributor"),
    ]:
        edge(a, b, reason)

    # Use every original source observation, including merged-away rows.
    # This establishes source observation order, not global physical causality.
    for source, group in base.events.groupby("source"):
        levels = [
            g[E].tolist()
            for _, g in group.groupby("source_seq", sort=True)
        ]
        for left, right in zip(levels, levels[1:]):
            for a, b in product(left, right):
                edge(a, b, "within-source sequence:" + str(source))

    # Interpret only an explicitly designated precedence qualifier.
    for rec in x.e2e.to_dict("records"):
        if rec[Q] == "precedes":
            edge(rec[E], rec[E + "_2"], "native precedes")

    ranks, order = {}, []
    for _, group in x.events.groupby(T, sort=True):
        sub = graph.subgraph(group[E])
        if not nx.is_directed_acyclic_graph(sub):
            raise ValueError("Precedence cycle: quarantine; do not force order")
        extension = list(nx.lexicographical_topological_sort(sub, key=str))
        ranks.update({e: i for i, e in enumerate(extension)})
        order.extend(extension)

    x.events["tie_rank"] = x.events[E].map(ranks)
    x = pm4py.ocel_sort_by_additional_column(
        x, "tie_rank", primary_column=T)
    assert x.events[E].tolist() == order

    x.relations["event_order"] = x.relations[E].map(
        {e: i for i, e in enumerate(order)})
    x.relations = x.relations.sort_values(
        ["event_order", O]).reset_index(drop=True)
    validate(x)

    # Disposable adapter for tools requiring unique timestamps; NOT KPI data.
    adapter = deepcopy(x)
    adapter = pm4py.ocel_add_index_based_timedelta(adapter)  # Order fixed first.
    adapter.events["adjusted_timestamp"] = adapter.events[T]
    adapter.relations["adjusted_timestamp"] = adapter.relations[T]
    assert adapter.events[T].is_monotonic_increasing

    # Guard the supplied second-resolution buckets against excessive offsets.
    if not adapter.events[T].dt.floor("s").eq(
            adapter.events["original_timestamp"].dt.floor("s")).all():
        raise ValueError(
            "Synthetic offsets too large; use original timestamp plus rank")
    validate(adapter)

    state["constraints"] = constraints
    state["timestamp_map"] = adapter.events[
        [E, "original_timestamp", "adjusted_timestamp", "tie_rank"]
    ].copy()
    return x, adapter

    assert sorted(

        for roots in states[name]["members"].values()

    ) == sorted(base.events[E])
    print(name, len(business_log.events), len(business_log.relations))
```

## 7. Sensitivity analysis

Use the same counting definitions across scenarios. Distinguish label-refinement effects from event-contraction effects by also comparing views projected back to the original activity vocabulary.

| Dataset              | Events / E2O | Order-flattened DFG                                                                       | Item-flattened DFG                                 | Payment-flattened DFG                           |
| -------------------- | -----------: | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Raw                  |      17 / 44 | Duplicate observations, summary-level repetitions, and harmonization collisions coexist   | I1 has duplicate pick observations                 | M1: authorization then capture; M2: one capture |
| Conservative         |      15 / 39 | Removes verified duplicate observations; separates logistics milestones and summaries     | Removes I1’s duplicate pick; preserves I2’s pick   | Unchanged                                       |
| Evidence-based merge |      14 / 37 | Additionally removes the separate ERP packing-status step                                 | Unchanged from conservative: e07 had no Item links | Unchanged                                       |
| Guarded aggressive   |      13 / 34 | Collapses release/acceptance and packing/status; retains misleading payment-summary label | Same as conservative under selected ordering       | Unchanged because payments are protected        |

**Concrete changing patterns.** Under the selected extensions:

* Order `Pick Item → Pick Item` falls from two occurrences to one. The remaining occurrence represents two different items, not proven rework.
* I1 `Pick Item → Pick Item` disappears entirely.
* Raw `Pack Package → Pack Package` becomes `Pack Package → Record ERP Packed Status`; the coarse projection removes that status step.
* Raw `Dispatch Package → Dispatch Package` becomes `Release from Warehouse → Accept by Carrier`; aggressive merging erases this distinction.
* `Accept by Carrier → Handover to Carrier` remains visible conservatively, but becomes coarse `Dispatch Package → Handover to Carrier` aggressively.
* Raw Order `Capture Payment → Capture Payment` occurs twice; conservative refinement leaves one cross-payment repetition followed by `Capture Payment → Update Invoice Balance`.
* A later raw `Capture Payment → Issue Invoice` can reflect duplicate e16, not a second issuance.

**Ordering sensitivity matters independently.** For the declared conservative constraints, there are \(3\times3\times4=36\) tied-block linearizations. Enumerate them and report necessary versus possible DFG edges. Without confirmed pick-before-pack semantics, I2 can exhibit either `Pick → Pack` or `Pack → Pick`. Linearization frequency is not a probability of physical order.

**OC-DFG metrics.** Compare event-pair counts, distinct-object counts, and event-pair–object counts separately; these are different PM4Py aggregations. ([GitHub][2]) For example, the Order pick self-loop loses an occurrence while its distinct-object support remains one Order. Item `Pick → Pack` still has two supporting Items under the selected orders.

**ET–OT weights.** PM4Py’s classic implementation counts relation rows for each activity/type pair. ([GitHub][3]) `Pick Item–Item` falls from 3 to 2; `Issue Invoice–Invoice` from 2 to 1. `Capture Payment–Payment` remains 2 across all four datasets. The Order weight for capture changes from 3 to 2 only when e15 is relabelled. Therefore, unchanged Payment weights do not certify unchanged semantics.

**OTG relations.** PM4Py’s OTG aggregates object-pair graphs, including interaction, descendants, cobirth, and codeath—not simply event frequencies. ([GitHub][4]) Distinct interaction pairs remain unchanged across these four guarded datasets: the contractions remove identical or subset relation sets. However, first/last-observation-based relations are order-sensitive. ([GitHub][5]) Placing e12 before invoice issuance can make V1 and M1 appear “coborn.” The rejected unrestricted payment merge would additionally invent direct M1–M2 co-participation.

**Lifecycle/performance.** Original-time Order span remains 24 hours. Item and payment observations tied within one second do not establish zero physical processing duration. Synthetic offsets manufacture positive intervals and scenario-dependent durations. Calculate performance from original times, and never interpret first/last observed events automatically as actual creation/termination.

## 8. Audit trail and reversibility

Use three linked, versioned structures.

**Immutable observations:** raw event-row ID, original eid, source and source key, original integrated activity, available native activity, timestamp, sequence, ingestion time, payload/hash, and complete attributes. Archive original relations separately, including qualifiers and relation-row IDs.

**Canonicalization decisions:** scenario, canonical eid, contributing source-event IDs, merge/refinement rule, reviewer or rule version, confidence, reason, evidence reference, per-field authority choice, retained values, and conflicts excluded from canonical scalar fields. “Excluded” must not mean destroyed.

**Ordering decisions:** original and adjusted timestamps, rank, predecessor/successor IDs, evidence class, assumptions, contracted observation dependencies, fallback choices, and software version.

Reconstruct the raw view from the immutable observations and original relation tables—not by exploding canonical relation unions, which cannot recover original ownership. Preserve untouched O2O and object-change tables as well.

`original_activity` in this snapshot preserves the **integrated** label. Lost native labels cannot be reconstructed exactly without source extracts or harmonization history.

## 9. Failure modes and safeguards

1. **Collapsing partial payments:** enforce explicit cannot-links and transaction-object checks; preserve e14 independently.
2. **Turning abstraction updates into false rework:** refine e07/e15 and distinguish observation-level from business-event analyses.
3. **Ordering by ingestion:** use evidence-labelled precedence first; reserve ingestion for technical diagnostics.
4. **Treating shared objects as identity proof:** O1 and V1 provide context, not transaction identity.
5. **Unioning incompatible E2O relations:** validate roles and cardinalities; never infer Payment links for e15 from its summary relationship.
6. **Changing durations through synthetic offsets:** isolate timestamp adapters and prohibit adjusted timestamps in performance calculations.
7. **Losing source-authoritative attributes:** apply field-specific authority; archive competing values rather than using global “ERP wins.”
8. **Applying duplicate functions globally:** inspect groups and scope operations. A broad drop operation can leave e05 linked only to I2 and e14 only to M2, stripping their shared context even when the events survive. ([GitHub][1])

A smaller log is not inherently a better log. The acceptance criteria are preserved transaction distinctions, traceable transformations, valid references, and conclusions that remain defensible across admissible interpretations.
