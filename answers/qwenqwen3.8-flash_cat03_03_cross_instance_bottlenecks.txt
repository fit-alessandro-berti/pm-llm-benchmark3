## 1. Interaction-graph description

A useful typed interaction graph treats events as connectors between object types rather than forcing all events into a Job-centric log. Important node types are **Job**, **Component**, **ComponentLot**, **Batch**, **Machine**, **Tool**, and **Shipment**. Important edge types include:

- `requires`: Job  Component, e.g., `J1 requires C1`.
- `contains`: ComponentLot  Component, e.g., `L7 contains C2` and `L7 contains C5`.
- `batches`: Batch  Job, e.g., `B1 batches J1, J2, J3`.
- `consolidates`: Shipment  Job, e.g., `S1 consolidates J1, J4`.
- `cured_in` / `processed_on` / `tested_on`: Job or Batch  Machine, e.g., `B1 cured_in OVEN-1`, `J4 processed_on CNC-2`, `J6 tested_on TEST-1`.

- `dispatches`: Shipment  time-bound completion, e.g., `S1 dispatches 19:25`.

Inferred edges include `J1 waits_for J3/B1`, `J2 waits_for J3/B1`, `J4 waits_for FIX-A`, `J5 blocked_by L7`, `J6 blocked_by CAL-3 recalibration`, and `J1 waits_for J4/S1`. These inferred edges are not separate events in the supplied logs; they are constructed from readiness, batch starts, tool holds, lot releases, and shipment dispatch times.

Three higher-order motifs are especially important:

1. **Batch synchronization motif**: `Job  Batch  Machine/Tool`, with `Job  Component  ComponentLot`. B1 shows J1, J2, J3, C1, C2, C3, OVEN-1, and RACK-7 joined by one `Batch Cure` event.
2. **Tool-machine-job motif**: `Job  Tool  Job` and `Job  Machine`. J3 holds FIX-A while J4 is at CNC-2; J4’s process event later relates both CNC-2 and FIX-A.
3. **Shipment-partner motif**: `Job  Shipment  Job`. J1 and J4 are independent jobs but become coupled through S1; J1’s packed state does not imply dispatch readiness.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

1. **J1 batch-synchronization wait in B1**  
   Calculation: `12:05 - 08:10 = 3 h 55 min`.  
   Counterfactual assumption: if J1’s readiness at 08:10 were trusted and J1 could have been cured individually or in an earlier batch without waiting for J3, this interval would be avoidable synchronization waiting. This is not proof: OVEN-1, RACK-7, or C1/L8 conditions might have constrained an earlier start.

2. **J2 batch-synchronization wait in B1**  
   Calculation: `12:05 - 08:25 = 3 h 40 min`.  
   Counterfactual assumption: if J2 could have been batched without waiting for J3, the wait would be reduced. Complication: J2’s component C2 belongs to L7, which was quarantined at 10:20 and released at 13:50, yet J2 entered B1 before release due to a supervisor override. Thus the 3 h 40 min may be partly lot-policy masking, not pure synchronization.

3. **Total B1 synchronization-induced job waiting**  
   Calculation: `J1 3 h 55 min + J2 3 h 40 min + J3 10 min = 7 h 45 min`.  
   Counterfactual assumption: if the three jobs could be cured independently with no additional oven or rack setup penalty, up to 7 h 45 min of aggregate job waiting could be removed. This is an upper-bound style decomposition, not a causal estimate, because batching may reduce setups or satisfy quality policy.

4. **J4 FIX-A-associated waiting at CNC-2**  
   Calculation: J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50; CNC-2 is idle 10:05–10:47; FIX-A is released at 11:40; J4 starts process at 11:45. A conservative tool-associated delay is `11:45 - 10:47 = 58 min`.  
   Counterfactual assumption: if FIX-A had been available when CNC-2 became idle at 10:47, and no other setup or material condition blocked J4, J4 could have started around 10:47. The full `11:45 - 09:50 = 1 h 55 min` wait at CNC-2 should not be attributed entirely to FIX-A.

5. **J4 synchronization wait in B2**  
   Calculation: `14:50 - 13:10 = 1 h 40 min`.  
   Counterfactual assumption: if J4 could have been cured individually at 13:10, this wait is synchronization-induced. However, RACK-7 is recorded as cleaning/quarantine until 14:35, so a more constrained counterfactual is earliest feasible start at 14:35; then only `14:50 - 14:35 = 15 min` is clearly additional synchronization beyond the rack constraint.

6. **J1 shipment-consolidation wait in S1**  
   Calculation: J1 packed at 13:30; S1 dispatches at 19:25; wait = `5 h 55 min`. If a standalone S1-like shipment could dispatch 15 minutes after packing, as observed after J4 packed at 19:10, consolidation-induced delay = `19:25 - 13:45 = 5 h 40 min`.  
   Counterfactual assumption: if J1 were allowed to ship alone after normal load/dispatch handling, the partner wait for J4 would be removed. The exact avoidable amount depends on shipment policy and transport cost.

7. **J6 CAL-3-associated delay**  
   Calculation: J6 ready at 16:05; J5 finishes test at 16:35; CAL-3 recalibrates 16:35–17:20; J6 starts at 17:25. Recalibration-associated delay = `17:25 - 16:35 = 50 min`. Total ready-to-start delay = `17:25 - 16:05 = 1 h 20 min`.  
   Counterfactual assumption: if CAL-3 did not require recalibration between J5 and J6, or if a second CAL-3 were available, J6 could have started shortly after J5 finished. TEST-1 being idle 16:35–17:25 supports a tool/calibration explanation rather than pure machine capacity, but it is still inferential.

## 4. What flattening misses

**Job flattening** would make visible each job’s activity sequence and median transitions such as `Stage Component -> Machine Process` at 1.1 h and `Pack -> Dispatch` at 1.1 h. It would hide or distort cross-job synchronization because a single `Batch Cure@12:05` is duplicated into J1, J2, and J3 logs. It also hides FIX-A waiting, L7 quarantine/release, and shipment partner waiting. A misleading conclusion is that no job exceeds 1.8 standard deviations above the mean, so there is no delay problem; if all six jobs are delayed by common batch or shipment policy, the distribution can look internally normal while absolute waiting is high.

**Machine flattening** would make visible CNC-2, OVEN-1, and TEST-1 events, utilization, and gaps. It would hide why OVEN-1 is idle 12:55–14:50 or why TEST-1 is idle 16:35–17:25. A misleading conclusion is that OVEN-1 at 44% utilization has abundant capacity. In fact, J4 may be ready at 13:10 but cannot start until 14:50 because of batch synchronization, rack cleaning, or J5 readiness. Low utilization can coexist with long job waiting when the machine is idle while waiting for a batch member, a tool, a lot release, or a shipment policy.

**Batch flattening** would make visible B1 and B2 starts, members, and oven/rack use. It would hide individual readiness differences and downstream shipment effects. A misleading conclusion is that batch cycle time is acceptable; B1 may have a normal cure duration while J1 and J2 wait almost four hours before cure.

**Tool flattening** would make visible FIX-A, RACK-7, and CAL-3 holds, cleaning, and recalibration. It would hide job-level consequences unless tool events are linked to jobs. A misleading conclusion is that FIX-A utilization is simply high; the operational problem is that J4 is at CNC-2 from 09:50 but cannot process until FIX-A is released at 11:40.

**ComponentLot flattening** would make visible L7 quarantine@10:20 and release@13:50, and L8 membership. It would hide inconsistent job linkage: the supplied Job flattening excludes lot events because they were extracted only from events directly related to the Job at source. A misleading conclusion is that lot events are irrelevant to jobs; they relate to J2 and J5 in the object-centric evidence, but J2 had an override.

**Shipment flattening** would make visible `Create Shipment -> Load -> Dispatch` with medians 0.8 h and 0.3 h. It would hide the fact that J1 waits from 13:30 packed until 19:25 dispatch because J4 is not packed until 19:10. A misleading conclusion is that shipment processing is fast and therefore not a bottleneck; the bottleneck is partner synchronization, not load/dispatch handling.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**Narrative A: B1 oven/rack batch**  
Objects: J1, J2, J3, C1, C2, C3, L7, L8, B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, S2.  
Temporal precedence: J1 is ready at 08:10, J2 at 08:25, J3 at 11:55, and the single `Batch Cure` occurs at 12:05. L7 is quarantined at 10:20 and released at 13:50, after B1 starts.  
Resource feasibility: The batch event links OVEN-1 and RACK-7, so oven and rack availability are jointly relevant. J1 and J2 could have been ready, but the batch cannot proceed as a synchronized group until J3 is ready and the oven/rack are available.  
Policy constraints: Batch synchronization appears to require all three jobs. A supervisor override allowed J2 to enter B1 before L7 release.  
Causal speculation: J1 and J2 waits are plausibly caused by waiting for J3 and batch policy, not by OVEN-1 capacity. The lot override complicates the story: J2’s path was not strictly blocked by L7, so lot release may be a confounder rather than the direct cause.

**Narrative B: FIX-A, CNC-2, J4, B2, S1**  
Objects: J3, J4, FIX-A, CNC-2, C4, B2, OVEN-1, RACK-7, S1, J1.  
Temporal precedence: J3 acquires FIX-A at 09:00 and releases it at 11:40. J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50. CNC-2 is idle 10:05–10:47. J4 starts `Machine Process` at 11:45. J4 is ready for B2 at 13:10. J1 is packed at 13:30, J4 at 19:10, and S1 dispatches at 19:25.  
Resource feasibility: CNC-2 is available, but J4’s process event relates to FIX-A, suggesting tool availability is necessary. No explicit `Wait for Tool` event exists.  
Policy constraints: The system may require FIX-A for J4’s CNC operation and may not record tool waiting as a job activity.  
Causal speculation: FIX-A contention plausibly delays J4 by about 58 minutes after CNC-2 idle time. That delay may propagate to B2 and S1, contributing to J1’s long packed wait, but the evidence does not prove a full causal chain because other setup or quality steps could intervene.

**Narrative C: L7, B2, CAL-3, TEST-1, J5, J6, S3**  
Objects: L7, C5, J5, J4, B2, OVEN-1, RACK-7, TEST-1, CAL-3, J6, S3.  
Temporal precedence: L7 is quarantined at 10:20 and released at 13:50. J4 is ready for B2 at 13:10; J5 at 14:40; B2 starts at 14:50. J5 and J6 are ready for final test at 16:00 and 16:05. J5 tests 16:10–16:35; CAL-3 recalibrates 16:35–17:20; J6 tests 17:25–17:50. S3 dispatches 35 minutes after its last job is packed.  
Resource feasibility: OVEN-1 is idle 12:55–14:50, but RACK-7 is cleaning/quarantine until 14:35. TEST-1 is idle 16:35–17:25, but CAL-3 is recalibrating.  
Policy constraints: Batch B2 appears to wait for J5; final tests require CAL-3; shipment S3 waits for its partner. Lot release may have gated J5.  
Causal speculation: J5’s progress may have been coupled to L7 release, while J6’s delay is plausibly associated with CAL-3 recalibration rather than TEST-1 capacity. The shipment rule then adds a further partner wait for S3.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

Observations that would weaken the top-ranked batch-synchronization explanation:

1. J1 or J2 readiness timestamps are wrong because C1/C2/C3 were not actually staged, inspected, or available at 08:10/08:25.
2. OVEN-1 or RACK-7 had explicit unavailable events before 12:05, showing the batch could not start even if J3 were ready.
3. B1 start was constrained by L8 or another component-lot hold not shown in the supplied lot events.
4. J1 and J2 would still wait almost as long if batch synchronization were removed, because downstream shipment or test constraints dominate.
5. B2 start at 14:50 is fully explained by RACK-7 cleaning until 14:35 plus J5 readiness at 14:40, leaving little avoidable synchronization.
6. Smaller or rolling batches increase total job waiting because extra setups consume OVEN-1 or RACK-7.
7. Supervisor overrides show that batch membership was not actually synchronized by readiness, e.g., J2 was forced into B1 for reasons unrelated to J3.
8. Tool events show FIX-A, RACK-7, or CAL-3 were available when jobs waited, contradicting the inferred resource coupling.

Additional fields or event types that would discriminate among hypotheses:

1. Explicit `Wait for Batch`, `Wait for Tool`, `Wait for Lot`, and `Wait for Shipment` events with reason codes.
2. Component staging-complete events linked to Job, Component, and ComponentLot.
3. Machine and tool availability calendars, including RACK-7 cleaning, CAL-3 calibration, and CNC-2 setup windows.
4. Batch policy attributes: minimum/maximum members, latest allowable start, required cure duration, and override reason.
5. Lot release decision events linking L7 to C2/C5, J2/J5, quality status, and authorizing user.
6. Shipment partner constraints: required-by date, customer delivery window, freight cutoff, and standalone shipment option.
7. Tool reservation or allocation events showing FIX-A and CAL-3 planned versus actual holds.
8. Quality inspection or quarantine events at the component level, not only lot level.
