# 1. Data audit and estimands

**Queueing quantities and units.** Opening hours are 07:00–19:00, i.e. 12 opening hours or 720 staff-minutes per FTE-day. `arrivals` and `completed` are daily counts during opening hours. Arrival intensity is therefore estimated as `arrivals/12` per opening hour; completion intensity as `completed/12` per opening hour. `mean_wip` is a 15-minute snapshot mean of cases in the system during opening hours. `median_service_min` and `p90_service_min` are active handling times excluding waiting. `staff_fte` is mean concurrently available full-time-equivalent capacity. A nominal capacity-pressure proxy is  
`U_med = arrivals × median_service_min / (staff_fte × 720)`  
and a tail-stress proxy is  
`U_p90 = arrivals × p90_service_min / (staff_fte × 720)`.  
These are not true utilization because mean service time is not supplied and FTE is not skill-adjusted.

**Key limitations and threats to naive inference.**
1. **Daily aggregation hides intraday queueing.** The hourly vectors show burstiness, but only for four representative days.
2. **Completions are not matched to same-day arrivals.** Carry-over across days contaminates any daily throughput interpretation.
3. **07:00 backlog is not supplied.** `mean_wip` snapshots during opening hours cannot be converted into a clean initial-condition queue length.
4. **Medians and p90s are not means.** Capacity pressure and Little’s Law require mean flow time, not median service time.
5. **Case-mix is non-stationary.** `complex_pct` jumps sharply around 2026-04-20; this may be a real mix shift, a coding change, or both.
6. **Outage and recovery are not normal regimes.** 2026-04-15 has a 180-minute outage; 2026-04-16–17 look like recovery/catch-up.
7. **Staffing intervention is temporally fuzzy.** It began during 2026-04-29 but exact time was not logged; only three post-intervention weekdays are observed.
8. **Daily points are serially dependent.** Weekday/weekend cycles, week-to-week drift, and regime changes violate independent-days assumptions.
9. **Peak concentration varies materially.** `peak_08_10_pct` ranges from about 17% on early weekends to 50% on 2026-04-27, so pooled peak assumptions are risky.
10. **No event-level log.** PM4Py-style process-mining metrics cannot be computed directly from these aggregates.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

A materially different segmentation is **intraday process-pattern segmentation**:

| Alternative regime | Dates/examples | Operational story |
|---|---|---|
| Smooth ordinary weekday | 2026-04-07 vector | Stable demand and completion profile; useful baseline |
| Outage day | 2026-04-15 vector | Midday completion collapse from 11:00–14:00 |
| Bursty post-mix weekday | 2026-04-21 vector | Arrival spikes at 10–11 and 13–14; high peak concentration |
| Weekend/light-load | 2026-04-25 vector | Low volume, flatter load, but possibly high p90 when complex mix is high |

This alternative is strong for **staffing within the day** and peak smoothing. It directly addresses the fact that two weekdays with similar daily totals can have very different hourly queue behavior. Its weakness is that it ignores the calendar-phase drivers: case-mix shift, outage recovery, and the staffing intervention. It also relies on only four representative hourly vectors, so it cannot establish a full stochastic model.

A second serious alternative is a **pure calendar-phase segmentation**: low-complex stable phase, outage/recovery phase, high-complex/backlog-building phase, and intervention phase. That captures non-stationarity better but would pool weekdays with weekends and therefore hide the large volume difference: weekdays average about 108 arrivals/day versus about 44.9 on weekends.

# 4. Pooled versus segmented statistics

Pooled summaries obscure behavior in at least three major ways.

**Weekday/weekend composition.** Pooled arrivals are 90/day, but weekdays average 108/day and weekends 44.9/day. A single pooled arrival rate is therefore a weighted average of two very different operating regimes.

**Complex-case mix.** Pooled complex mix is about 33.8%. But early baseline weekdays are about 20.9%, while high-complex pre-intervention weekdays are about 44.6%. Pooled service median is about 31 min, but the early regime is 25 min and the high-complex regime is 35 min.

**Outage and staffing intervention.** The pooled completion rate of 86/day hides the outage day at 58/day and recovery days at 120.5/day. Similarly, high-complex pre-intervention weekdays complete about 100.6/day with WIP 44.3, while the three intervention weekdays complete 121.0/day with WIP 37.0. The intervention looks beneficial locally, but it is confounded with backlog clearing and only three days are observed.

**Simpson’s-paradox-like reversal.** Pooled weekday/weekend comparison makes more staff appear associated with higher WIP because weekdays have both more staff and higher WIP. Within the high-complex weekday phase, increasing staff from about 5.93 to 6.83 FTE coincides with WIP falling from 44.3 to 37.0. Pooling reverses the apparent staffing–WIP relationship.

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

Using hourly counts as 12 equal bins, the Fano factor is `variance/mean`. For a Poisson process this is 1; values above 1 indicate overdispersion/burstiness.

| Representative day | Arrival mean/h | Arrival Fano | Arrival CV | Completion mean/h | Completion Fano | Completion CV | Note |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2026-04-07 | 8.42 | 2.96 | 0.59 | 8.25 | 1.56 | 0.44 | Ordinary weekday, moderately overdispersed |
| 2026-04-15 | 8.75 | 3.79 | 0.66 | 4.83 | 2.06 | 0.65 | Outage creates zero completions 11:00–14:00 |
| 2026-04-21 | 9.58 | 9.28 | 0.98 | 8.42 | 0.90 | 0.33 | Very bursty arrivals, smoother completions |
| 2026-04-25 | 4.25 | 1.18 | 0.53 | 3.58 | 0.72 | 0.45 | Weekend, closer to Poisson for arrivals |

Daily arrivals are also overdispersed: daily variance/mean is roughly 9.7. The daily lag-1 autocorrelation for arrivals is approximately **0.40**, indicating positive serial dependence. This is partly weekday/weekend cycling and partly phase drift.

Change-point evidence is strong around **2026-04-20** for complex mix, arrivals, WIP, and p90 service. The outage on **2026-04-15** is a clear one-day capacity break. A second plausible change point is **2026-04-29**, when staff increases and WIP begins to fall, but the exact intervention time is not logged. Four representative hourly vectors cannot establish a full stochastic model: they do not identify interarrival distributions, within-day dependence beyond 12 bins, or cross-day carry-over dynamics.

# 6. Little’s-Law applicability matrix

Little’s Law is `L = W`. Here `L` is `mean_wip` from opening-hours snapshots. `` must be a stable throughput proxy during the same window. `W` is mean flow time, not median service time. Using arrivals gives `W = L / (arrivals/12)` hours; using completions gives `W = L / (completed/12)` hours.

# 7. Operational implications

**Staffing.** Do not staff from the pooled 90 arrivals/day average. Weekdays need closer to the high-complex regime of 114 arrivals/day, while weekends are around 45 arrivals/day. However, high-complex weekends have p9083 min with only 2.5 FTE, so weekend understaffing may create tail delays even when daily volume is low.

**Backlog recovery.** Treat 2026-04-15–17 as an outage/recovery episode. The high completions on 2026-04-16–17 are consistent with backlog clearing and should not be used as evidence of a new steady-state capacity. Recovery staffing should be planned separately from normal demand.

**Peak smoothing.** The high-complex phase has `peak_08_10_pct` around 40–50%, and 2026-04-21 shows large intraday arrival spikes. Uniform staffing across 07:00–19:00 will under-serve 08:00–11:00 and some early-afternoon spikes.

**Data collection.** Collect timestamped arrivals, starts, completions, outage start/end, 07:00 backlog, exact intervention time, staff skill mix, and service time by complex/simple variant. With raw event data, PM4Py temporal features and case arrival/finish rates could replace these aggregate proxies.

**Policy that looks good pooled but risky segmented.** A uniform “about 5 FTE” policy looks adequate under the pooled average: pooled U_med0.75. But under segmentation, high-complex weekdays need about 6 FTE just to keep median-load near 0.93 and p90-load above 2, while weekends may need only 2.5–3 FTE by volume but still suffer p90 tails. Uniform pooling hides both the weekday overload and the weekend tail risk.

    "pooled all": daily["regime"].notna(),
    "R1 baseline weekdays": daily["regime"] == "R1 baseline weekdays",
    "R4 high-complex weekdays pre": daily["regime"] == "R4 high-complex weekdays pre",
    "R5 intervention weekdays": daily["regime"] == "R5 intervention weekdays",
    "R6 baseline weekends": daily["regime"] == "R6 baseline weekends",
}
little_rows = []
for name, mask in windows.items():
    g = daily[mask]
    L = g["mean_wip"].mean()
    lam_arr = g["arrivals"].mean() / OPEN_HOURS
    lam_comp = g["completed"].mean() / OPEN_HOURS
    little_rows.append({
        "window": name,
        "n": len(g),
        "mean_wip": L,
        "arr_per_h": lam_arr,
        "comp_per_h": lam_comp,
        "W_from_arrivals_h": L / lam_arr,
        "W_from_completions_h": L / lam_comp,
    })
little = pd.DataFrame(little_rows)
print("\nLITTLE'S LAW SELECTED WINDOWS")
print(little.round(2))
