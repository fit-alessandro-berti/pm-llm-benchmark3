# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

Organizational roles are defined not merely by the presence of an activity in a resource's event log, but by the interplay of four structural dimensions:
1. **Activity Frequency & Concentration**: Is the activity a primary throughput driver or an occasional occurrence?
2. **Case Complexity Distribution**: Under what case conditions (Low, Medium, High, Critical) does the resource execute the activity?
3. **Substitution Context**: Was the activity performed as part of routine baseline operations, or during a temporary disruption/surge?
4. **Organizational & Decision Authority**: Does the execution represent formal sign-off, delegated authority, or preliminary operational triage?

Below is a detailed analysis of core versus peripheral activities for each proposed role.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

Informal and shadow roles emerge organically within service operations to bypass structural bottlenecks, compensate for resource absences, and bridge functional silos. Rather than treating these behaviors as compliance breaches, process mining must evaluate both their operational benefits and systemic risks.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    SHADOW & BRIDGING TOPOLOGY                                      |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                                                                                    |
|    [ Front Office ] -------- (Informal Pricing Bridge) --------> [ Commercial / Pricing ]         |
|      R07_Giorgio (Senior FO)                                       R06_Fatima (Commercial)         |
|      * Benefits: Rapid front-line pricing; absorbs overflow.                                       |
|      * Risks: Duration doubles (38m -> 74m); margin leakage; lack of audit trail.                  |
|                                                                                                    |
|                                                                                                    |
|    [ Operational Silos ] <==== (Cross-Functional Hub) ====> [ Risk / Management / Tech ]           |
|      (FO / Tech / QA)                R13_Mateo                     (Erik / Hana / Chen)            |
|      * Benefits: Specialist shielding; noise reduction; rapid incident routing.                    |
|      * Risks: Filter failure (critical cases delayed >2d); unreviewed sign-offs (13 rechecks).      |
|                                                                                                    |
|                                                                                                    |
|    [ Incident Stream ] ------ (Specialist Risk Gatekeeper) ------> [ Secure Resolution ]           |
|                                      R05_Erik (Security)                                           |
|      * Benefits: High-rigor risk containment; zero-compromise security posture.                    |
|      * Risks: Severe capacity bottleneck; single point of failure; queuing latency.               |

+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1. `R07_Giorgio` as Commercial Shadow Role
- **Formal Status vs. Observed Reality**: Giorgio's formal title is Senior Service Agent in the Front Office team. However, event log analysis reveals that he performs 72 `A7 (Pricing Exception Review)` and 88 `A8 (Customer Negotiation)` events, exhibiting a 0.71 cosine similarity with Fatima and a 0.84 similarity with Amira. He holds formal pricing certification in addition to entitlement certification.
- **Substitution Dynamics**: During Weeks 3–4, when Fatima was absent for 6 working days, Giorgio absorbed the commercial workload, executing 31 A7 and 18 A8 events.
- **Operational Benefits**:
  - *Process Continuity & Velocity*: Prevents severe backlog accumulation in the commercial silo during specialist absence.
  - *First-Contact Resolution*: Allows complex front-office cases with minor pricing exceptions to be resolved immediately without initiating an asynchronous handoff to Commercial.
- **Operational Risks & Deficiencies**:
  - *Execution Inefficiency*: During Giorgio's substitution period, median pricing exception processing duration escalated from 38 minutes to 74 minutes (a 95% increase), demonstrating that shadow execution lacks the specialized fluency of the primary owner.
  - *Governance & Commercial Leakage*: Shadow pricing decisions risk inconsistent discount application, margin erosion, and unmonitored commercial concessions that bypass formal audit logs.

### 2. `R13_Mateo` as Cross-Functional Escalation Broker
- **Formal Status vs. Observed Reality**: Mateo holds the formal title of Incident Coordinator. Rather than executing a narrow set of tasks, Mateo exhibits the highest activity entropy across the entire event log, touching all 14 activities and engaging with 76 Critical and 148 High complexity cases (median complexity 0.77).
- **Observed Shadow Interventions**:
  - *Supervisory Approval Proxy (Week 5)*: During Hana's audit workshop, Mateo executed 21 `A9 (Supervisor Approval)` events. However, 13 of these were later flagged and rechecked by Hana upon her return.
  - *Security Triage Filtering (Weeks 7–8)*: During an incident surge in "Enterprise Security", Mateo actively intercepted incoming referrals, pre-filtering low-risk security requests to protect Erik's bandwidth.
- **Operational Benefits**:
  - *Specialist Shielding*: Buffers high-cost, capacity-constrained specialists (Erik, Hana) from operational noise, triage overhead, and false-positive escalations.
  - *Cross-Silo Lubrication*: Facilitates rapid case transfers between Front Office, Technical, and Quality teams on complex reopened requests (Sequence Fragment 5: Reopen → Mateo:Coordinate → Specialist → Hana:Approval/Close).
- **Operational Risks & Deficiencies**:
  - *Filter Failures & False Negatives*: In Weeks 7–8, Mateo's informal security filtering caused two critical security incidents to be misclassified, waiting >2 days for expert intervention.
  - *Compliance Violations & Rework*: Mateo lacks formal supervisory approval authority; 62% of his provisional approvals in Week 5 required post-hoc rework and verification, introducing compliance exposure and double-handling.

### 3. `R05_Erik` as Rare but Critical Specialist
- **Formal Status vs. Observed Reality**: Erik is a Security Specialist within the Risk team. His total event volume is minimal (167 total events; 96 A6 events), but his complexity profile is the most extreme in the organization (median complexity 0.86; 55 Critical and 82 High cases).
- **Operational Dynamics**: Erik is an organizational "island" in frequency space (cosine similarity with Mateo is only 0.39). He intervenes strictly in specialized sequences (Sequence Fragment 3: Register → Validate → Diagnose → Erik:Security Review → Mateo:Coordinate → Implement → Quality Check).
- **Operational Benefits**:
  - *High-Rigor Risk Containment*: Ensures regulatory compliance, vulnerability remediation, and enterprise risk mitigation on mission-critical cases.
- **Operational Risks & Deficiencies**:
  - *Single Point of Failure (SPOF)*: Erik has no designated peer backup in the Risk team. When load surges, security reviews become an acute operational bottleneck.
  - *Vulnerability to Upstream Triage*: Because Erik relies on upstream actors (Mateo, Chen, Dina) to route security cases to him, any upstream filtering breakdown directly compromises enterprise security posture.

### 4. Additional Shadow & Boundary Dimensions
- **`R14_Nora` (Automated Boundary & Exception Handoff)**: Nora operates as a high-volume intake buffer (760 A1, 610 A2), but her operational boundary is brittle. When validation fails or complexity exceeds standard rules, cases spill into human queues, creating hidden handoff latency.
- **`R11_Kai` & `R12_Lina` (The Temporary Workforce Downstream Shadow)**: Hired for seasonal surge support, Kai and Lina closed hundreds of simple cases but generated an unmonitored "rework shadow": 17 cases were reopened due to inadequate entitlement documentation, shifting an unexpected rework burden onto permanent staff (`R01`, `R02`, `R13`).
- **`R09_Ivan` & `R10_Julia` (Quality Gatekeeping & Feedback Bridge)**: Ivan and Julia act as the bridging mechanism between technical execution and organizational memory. Their 200 combined `A14 (Knowledge Base Update)` events transform isolated troubleshooting fixes into standardized operational knowledge.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

The Python implementation below demonstrates a complete, end-to-end process mining workflow using PM4Py. It performs standard role discovery and social network mining, while integrating advanced safeguards that incorporate case complexity, filter out temporary substitution anomalies, and prevent the misinterpretation of raw activity frequencies as formal organizational authority.

```python
"""
Advanced Organizational Role Discovery and Social Network Mining in PM4Py
Author: Process Mining & Organizational Analytics Team
"""

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from scipy.spatial.distance import cosine
from sklearn.cluster import AgglomerativeClustering
from sklearn.preprocessing import normalize


def load_and_preprocess_event_log(csv_path: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Loads and formats an event log dataframe with PM4Py standard attributes."""
    df = pd.read_csv(csv_path)

    # Standardize event log schema using pm4py.format_dataframe
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        timest_format="%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S",
    )

    # Ensure required contextual attributes are properly typed
    if "org:resource" not in df.columns:
        df["org:resource"] = df["resource"]

    df["case_complexity"] = pd.to_numeric(
        df["case_complexity"], errors="coerce"
    ).fillna(0.5)
    df["temporary_substitution"] = df["temporary_substitution"].astype(bool)

    return df


def discover_standard_roles_and_similarities(df: pd.DataFrame):
    """Executes standard PM4Py role discovery and activity similarity routines."""
    print("=== 1. Standard PM4Py Organizational Role Discovery ===")
    # Discover basic organizational roles based on activity-resource co-occurrence
    discovered_roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(df)
    for idx, role in enumerate(discovered_roles):
        print(
            f"Role {idx + 1}: Activities = {role.activities}, Resources = {role.resources}"
        )

    print("\n=== 2. Activity-Based Resource Similarity ===")
    # Computes cosine similarity matrix across normalized resource-activity execution vectors
    similarity_matrix = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(df)
    print(similarity_matrix)

    return discovered_roles, similarity_matrix


def discover_organizational_networks(df: pd.DataFrame):
    """Mines Handover of Work, Working Together, and Subcontracting networks."""
    print("\n=== 3. Organizational Social Network Mining ===")

    # Handover of Work Network (reveals operational routing and process handoffs)
    handover_network = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(df)

    # Working Together Network (reveals co-involvement within the same case)
    working_together_network = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(df)

    # Subcontracting Network (reveals delegation and temporary task outsourcing)
    subcontracting_network = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(df)

    return handover_network, working_together_network, subcontracting_network


def advanced_multidimensional_role_analysis(df: pd.DataFrame):
    """Integrates case complexity, filters temporary substitutions, and detects shadow/bridging roles.

    Avoids over-interpreting raw activity frequency as formal authority.
    """
    print("\n=== 4. Advanced Multi-Dimensional Role & Shadow Discovery ===")

    # A. Separate baseline behavior from temporary substitution episodes
    df_baseline = df[~df["temporary_substitution"]].copy()
    df_substitution = df[df["temporary_substitution"]].copy()

    # B. Compute resource activity profiles across complexity tiers
    resources = sorted(df["org:resource"].unique())
    activities = sorted(df["concept:name"].unique())

    # Build multi-dimensional feature tensor: (Resource x [Activities, Complexity, Reopen Touchpoints])
    profile_data = []
    for res in resources:
        res_df = df_baseline[df_baseline["org:resource"] == res]
        res_sub_df = df_substitution[df_substitution["org:resource"] == res]

        # Activity counts in baseline
        act_counts = res_df["concept:name"].value_counts().to_dict()
        act_vector = [act_counts.get(a, 0) for a in activities]

        # Complexity distribution
        median_complexity = (
            res_df["case_complexity"].median() if len(res_df) > 0 else 0.0
        )
        high_crit_share = (
            (res_df["case_complexity"] >= 0.7).mean()
            if len(res_df) > 0
            else 0.0
        )

        # Substitution volume share
        sub_events = len(res_sub_df)
        total_events = len(res_df) + sub_events
        sub_share = sub_events / total_events if total_events > 0 else 0.0

        # Entropy across activities (identifies cross-functional brokers)
        counts_arr = np.array(act_vector, dtype=float)
        if counts_arr.sum() > 0:
            probs = counts_arr / counts_arr.sum()
            entropy = -np.sum(probs * np.log2(probs + 1e-9))
        else:
            entropy = 0.0

        profile_data.append(
            {
                "resource": res,
                "total_events": total_events,
                "median_complexity": median_complexity,
                "high_crit_share": high_crit_share,
                "substitution_share": sub_share,
                "activity_entropy": entropy,
                "act_vector": act_vector,
            }
        )

    profile_df = pd.DataFrame(profile_data)

    # C. Identify Bridging Brokers and Shadow Roles algorithmically
    print("\n--- Structural Role Classification ---")
    for _, row in profile_df.iterrows():
        res = row["resource"]
        ent = row["activity_entropy"]
        comp = row["median_complexity"]
        sub = row["substitution_share"]

        # Classification heuristics combining process and organizational features
        if ent > 3.0 and comp > 0.65:
            classification = (
                "Cross-Functional Escalation Broker (High Entropy & High Comp)"
            )
        elif sub > 0.15:
            classification = (
                "Shadow / Frequent Substitute Asset (High Substitution Share)"
            )
        elif comp >= 0.80:
            classification = (
                "Specialized Risk / Governance Gatekeeper (Extreme Complexity)"
            )
        elif row["total_events"] > 800 and comp < 0.20:
            classification = "High-Volume Intake / Automation Tier"
        elif comp < 0.25:
            classification = (
                "Auxiliary / Entry-Level Support (Low Complexity Ceiling)"
            )
        else:
            classification = "Standard Specialized Operational Role"

        print(
            f"Resource: {res:12s} | Entropy: {ent:4.2f} | Med Comp: {comp:4.2f} | Sub Share: {sub:4.2f} | Inferred Role: {classification}"
        )

    return profile_df


# Illustrative execution pipeline
if __name__ == "__main__":
    # In practice, pass the path to the PM4Py event log CSV
    csv_file_path = "order_to_resolution_event_log.csv"

    try:
        log_df = load_and_preprocess_event_log(csv_file_path)

    except FileNotFoundError:

        )
```

### Two Critical Cautions Against Naïve Staffing Adjustments

#### Caution 1: Conflating Activity Co-occurrence with Formal Authority and Legal Competence
A major hazard of naive role discovery is observing a resource perform an activity and concluding they are fully interchangeable with the formal role owner. 
- *The Danger*: In the event log, `R13_Mateo` executed 44 `A9 (Supervisor Approval)` events, and `R07_Giorgio` executed 72 `A7 (Pricing Exception Review)` events. Treating informal triage or provisional coverage as proof of formal competence destroys process quality, violates regulatory segregation of duties, introduces compliance liability, and generates severe downstream rework.

#### Caution 2: Eliminating Bridging and Shadow Roles as "Inefficient Process Deviations"
In traditional lean or process optimization initiatives, analysts frequently target resources with dispersed activity profiles (such as `R13_Mateo` or `R07_Giorgio`) as redundant, unfocused, or non-compliant "process waste" because they do not conform to clean functional silos.
- *The Danger*: Restructuring the organization to force Mateo strictly into a narrow dispatching box and forbidding Giorgio from touching pricing exceptions would appear on paper to create clean, modular role boundaries.
- *The Consequence*: Eliminating these informal bridging mechanisms destroys the organization's adaptive capacity. Without Mateo filtering operational noise and routing complex reopened tickets, specialized bottlenecks (`R05_Erik`, `R08_Hana`, `R03_Chen`) would become overwhelmed with triage overhead. Without Giorgio absorbing commercial overflow, every commercial absence would result in complete gridlock. Shadow roles are the shock absorbers of real-world service operations; optimizing them away creates a brittle process prone to catastrophic failure under stress.
