## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

These months should not be treated as one continuous “after April the model failed” decline. The causes are layered, and their timing matters.

- **January to March**  
  Baseline. Monthly breach ~0.18; model risk 0.18–0.19; AUROC ~0.81; Brier ~0.13; ECE <0.03. The variant split is roughly constant: V1 ~45%, V2 ~35%, V3 ~20%. Mature variant breach shows V3 is the highest-risk group at ~0.30–0.32, but stable.

- **April**  
  This is a **data-pipeline incident**, not a sudden performance problem. `Initial Diagnosis Completed` was renamed and the feature pipeline only recognized old label; we see high unknown activity 17%, missing queue feature 14%, and a huge rise in mean predicted risk. The drop in AUROC to 0.67 and active Brier increase are artifacts of building the feature on the wrong event or not building at all. Any actual risk model likely was not tested in April.

- **May**  
  Partial recovery. The mapping patch is deployed, but not all derived features are recomputed. Unknown activity fell, but some April-like features are still contaminated. May is somewhat safer than April, but we still cannot trust April–May for retraining without a full repair and reprocessing.

- **June**  
  The jump in V2 share from 35% to 52% due to remote routing policy explains lower observed overall rate; V2 matured breach remains around 0.09–0.11. The main problem is mix shift, not a concept change. Predictions are slightly too high because the model was not immediately calibrated to the new V1/V2/V3 mix.

- **July-August–September**  
  This is where overlapping drift begins. In July the part-supplier issue starts; the `Order Part -> Part Received` median jumps from 62 h in Jun to 65 h in Jul. In August it becomes severe: 130 h. V3 matured breach rises from 0.35 in Jun to 0.46 in Jul and 0.64 in Aug. At the same time V3 share moved from 22% to 35%, increasing the impact of the bad cases. September adds Partner Z, which switches more cases to V3 (58% for Partner Z vs. 36% non-Partner Z) and brings older or more remote equipment. So August and September have three simultaneous layers: process drift (parts lead time), concept/are not fully separated (V3 rate jumps), and population shift (Partner Z enters).

- **October**  
  The degraded metrics may look like they are still bad, but the observed breach rate of 0.16 only appears to be lower because only 55% of labels have matured. Median label delay is 52 days. The observed breach rate is heavily censored; the low 0.16 should not be interpreted as “the model is better” or 0.16 is actual risk. October must be excluded from performance comparisons until outcome labels reach the same maturity as earlier months.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

### Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting

Compare each month’s observed breach rate at the same percentage of maturity — for example, at day 30, 55, 90, and mature. Use a fixed cut-off date, not moving cutoff.  
If the hypothesis is **label delay/censoring**, then October’s observed breach rate should increase as more labels arrive; fixed-vintage metrics should show October similar to or worse than September, not better.  
If the hypothesis is **a real drift “improvement”**, then October would still show underperformance even after maturity, and recall should not depend on age.

### Recompute after activity-label normalization and duplicate cleaning

Rebuild event-log features from the complete original event trace. Normalize `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` back to `Initial Diagnosis Completed`; remove only same-event, same-timestamp duplicates, and preserve `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` as a quality column rather than dropping it. Then recalculate features for April–May.

- If **data-pipeline failure** is the cause, April/Apr prediction distribution and ECE should return to almost Jan/Jan slope.
- If **process/concept drift** is the cause, correcting the pipeline will not fix Jun–Oct degradation, and April-unchanged features might still be high, but model behavior remains under-predict.

### Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source, month

Compute Brier, ECE, AUROC separately by month and variant snapshot (V1, V2, V3).

- If **V3-specific process drift** is happening, V3 will lose calibration in Aug–Sep while V1/V2 remain stable.
- If **population shift** is the issue, both Partner-Z and non-Z will degrade when segmented; how larger relationship may change to some but not all other strata.
- If **label delay** is the issue, because October’s Brier/ECE will be sensitive to whether only mature cases are included.

### Comparison of process variants and DFG / performance-DFG edges

Compare `Part Received` and `Remote Fix` before/after June and Aug–Oct. Compute performance DFG across months and variants.

- If **process drift** is present, the part-order group’s DFG edge grows while remote groups remain unchanged.
- If **concept drift** is the sole cause, same process variant still shows different breach rates even on similar traces and input conditions.

### Test of outcome relationship conditional on same controller path

Pick a common shared V3 trace, e.g., `Case Created  Triage  Initial Diagnosis Completed  Order Part  Part Received  Dispatch Technician  Repair Completed`. Stratify by month, source, and model features; compare a breach probability given identical paths except for calendar month.

- If **process drift** is explanatory, correcting for part-request date and lead time should fully account for the higher V3 breach.
- If **concept drift** is also present, V3 risk can still increase after controlling for elapsed time, device age, and region.

### Variant / population reweighting or transport-style checks

Use a standard dataset or feature histogram for old months (Jan–Mar) and reweight Sep data to match the old population distribution by variant, device age, distance, and source. If **population shift** only explains the reduction, then recalibrated model should perform like old months when operating on reweighted cases; if **concept drift** matters, reweighting old frequencies will not remove the breach-rate increase.

### Feature missingness and lineage audit

Log, for every event, source system, ingestion timestamp, rename status, duplicate flags, and whether the item is `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY`. Compare monthly counts.

- If **data-pipeline failure** explains April; unknown activity and feature missing identifiers should exactly align with the broken lab.
- If **planning drift** holds, unknown activity may return to normal, but missingness patterns are absent while metrics still degrade.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

Recommended staged response:

### Immediate containment (now)
- Do not trust Oct or Oct performance metrics. Remove Oct from decision alerts.
- Keep a baseline model from Jan-Mar trained or calibrated at that time, but do not automatically run from Apr/May contaminated output.
- For Aug-Sep pilot operations, add a temporary post-hoc risk adjustment for cases with order-part delay > or <= programme. Report uncertainty range rather than a single point.

### Time after recalibration (after normalization)
- First repair the data pipeline: map rename, remove duplicates, recompute all derived features for April–May and April-the records.
- Once repaired, fit recalibration only: use a logistic recalibrator on the corrected May–Sep scores separately for V1/V2/V3. Do not retrain if now split is so much of the error comes from missing/edited features.
- Training safety until this step: **safe**: Jan–Mar (mature, pre-incident); **conditionally safe**: May–Jun if fully repaired; **unsafe until repaired**: April if running in old rationale; **unsafe for final**:  Aug–Oct until label mature and supplier/transport features are added.

### Short-term feature repair
- Add `distance-to-technician`, `device_age`, `part lead time forecast`, `parts order fibre`, and `PartnerZ` indicator to the model if they are legally allowed.
- Include process variable coverage: whether the case is V1/V2/V3, and whether a part price has been ordered. Even if not available at time of diagnoses for V3, the part delay edge can be predicted using supplier features.

### Full retraining (after labels mature)
- Full retraining only after the Aug–Sep labels are fully usable and after the feature and event pipelines are normalized. It is fine, but not immediately; treat Aug–Sep as a high-signal dataset if matured, but as a future dataset if not.
- For October: even if pipeline fixed, keep it out of training until label maturity is >80–90% in the newest model.

### Segment-specific or variant-specific justification
- A variant-specific treatment is justified because each variant has different risk and different process driver. V2 comprises a low sustained risk of ~0.09–0.12; V3 now carries a high risk and supplier delay.  
- Start with a common model but with separate intercepts for V1/V2/V3 and Zone. If tests show V3 has a nonlinear effect from order delay, then build a mild variant-split. Do not over-split, since simple variant-specific models may overfit.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

Monitoring layer snapshot:  
- **Predictive monitor**: monthly AUROC, Brier, log-likelihood of predictions for full score and blank classes.
- **Calibration monitor**: ECE, calibration slope, reliability diagram also for variants.
- **Label-maturity monitor**: label age; percent matured; median and percentile label delay by month.
- **Data-quality monitor**: unknown activity share, missing feature line, duplicate event share, activity label version.
- **Process-variant monitor**: share of V1/V2/V3, mean and median duration of `Order Part -> Part Received`.
- **Performance-DFG monitor**: edge-level performance; extract DFG for each month, manually flag key delay.
- **Population monitor**: statistics for Partner Z share, rural distance, device age, coverage.
- **Intervention/deployment monitor**: mark Sep 1 Partner Z onboarding, Jun 6 routing policy, input supplier change; link key code events to drift plots.

**Alert dependencies**:
- Maturity monitor exception  alert to outcome issuance, not to the model retraining; it may also block offline evaluation.
- Data-quality anomaly hits  hold predictive alert; wait for recompute; do not force calibration change.
- A combination of data-quality and label-maturity alarms should not produce “retrain.” Only push model retrain when the model exceeds threshold after trace normalized, maturity, and reweighted. Otherwise fire false-positive model alarms.

```python
    # 1. Event-calendar cohort: cases/events active in calendar window for
    #    process-performance monitoring. A case can start earlier.
    calendar_log = pm4py.filter_time_range(
        df, start, end, mode="events"
    )

    # 2. Case-start cohort: cases whose first event is in the window,
    #    used for fair training/label-maturity comparisons.
    first_event = (
        df.sort_values("time:timestamp")
        .groupby("case:id:concept")["time:timestamp"]
        .first()
    )
    start_ids = first_event[
        (first_event >= start) & (first_event < end)
    ].index
    case_log = df[df["case:id:concept"].isin(start_ids)]

    summary = {"month_start": start_label, "month_end": end_label}

    # Process variants (only from canonical/repaired event logs)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(case_log)
    summary["variant_freq"] = {
        k: len(v) for k, v in variants.items()
    }

    # DFG / performance DFG for both cohorts
    for name, log in [("calendar", calendar_log), ("case_start", case_log)]:
        dfg = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
        perf_dfg = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)

        edge_key = ("Order Part", "Part Received")
        edge_perf = perf_dfg.get(edge_key)
        summary[f"{name}_part_to_received_median"] = edge_perf
        summary[f"{name}_number_events"] = len(log)

    # Temporal feature extraction, no model fitting
    # PM4Py has no drift detector; we only extract per-month numeric features.
    tmp = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
        df[df["case:id:concept"].isin(start_ids)]
    )
    summary["temporal_feature_columns"] = list(tmp.columns)

    return summary

# Example audit loop: 2026 Jan-Sep
for month in range(1, 11):
    end_month = month + 1
    if end_month <= 10:
        print(
            summarize_month(
                f"2026-{month:02d}-01",

            )
        )
```

The event-calendar cohort supplies current process performance. The case-start cohort supplies stable labels for model monitoring. They should not be compared with each other without a cohort label.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

### 1. Retraining on immature labels
If you retrain on September or October labels while only 82% and 55% are mature, the model will be trained to see only cases that completed quickly. Important: a long weeks resolution cases with breaches are exactly those that are missing. That will bias the model toward predicting low risk.
**Mitigation**: enforce a fixed label-maturity threshold. Do not use October until a 55-day/90-day fixed label. Build maturity curves and compare before/after filters.

### 2. Calibrating around a pipeline bug
If you calibrate the April–May model before fixing feature recomputation, the calibration will learn from a distorted map and incorrectly perform a blank `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` or wrong event marker. Future live events will not resemble that repaired input, and calibration will be worse.  
**Mitigation:** require a quality gate: zero residual unknown or missing feature for a month before feeding. Re-do calibration after duplicates and named normalization are complete.

### 3. Ignoring a real supplier-driven process shift
If we say “all models are broken”, we may continue using an old model for three months while V3 is in actual breach rate. Predictions under-forecast 0.64 matured V3 breach in Aug. This is not merely model repair; it may mean operational risk has moved materially.  
**Mitigation**: trigger process-focused action: surface the Order-to-Part median delay, add supplier delay as a feature, and use upstream lead time as a model input. Recheck after batch operation.

### 4. Treating Partner population shift as unfair model bias
Without split line, Partner Z appears as a new population with high breach and higher V3 share. If we consider it “covariate drift” and the model doesn’t have distance/device age, we might blame the model for being unfair when the true cause is omitted predictor and supply process. Or conversely, if we don’t track Partner Z, we will not explain September’s result.  
**Mitigation**: test Partner Z and non-Z separately; reweight; inspect the same covariates. Use model action calibration/egress report per partner. Add the omitted attributes and monitor for collider bias. Do not change the model decision by until model distinguish between Partner if a legitimate proxy and residual unmeasured variables.

### 5. Ignoring calendar/cohort mismatch  
If you put the copper-level performance metrics and outcome-based metrics into the same report, you might think October is a recovery when in fact it only has 55% of mostly missing labels.  
**Mitigation**: report by both “calendar month of event” and “case-start month” with explicit label-maturity setting. A predicted risk can be shown, but observed breach should always be marked “immature” if fewer than 90 days of full label age.
