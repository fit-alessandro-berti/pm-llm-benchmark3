## 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

**Resilience** in this case means adapting the process while preserving the purpose of the control. A resilient deviation may leave the baseline DFG, but it still answers the core questions: *Is this customer authorized? Is this the right product and lot? Was temperature/security maintained? Who released it? Who received it? Can we reconstruct what happened contemporaneously?*

**Concealed noncompliance** occurs when the process appears to recover operationally while the evidence of control has disappeared or is deferred into an invisible remediation queue. Throughput preservation alone is not resilience if critical controls vanish.

Using the DFGs and metrics:

- **True or defensible resilience**
  - **B, manual authorization checks**, preserved a critical control despite system loss.
  - **A, hospital-critical fast lane**, is defensible when it changes sequence and priority but still requires minimum authorization and product-specific checks.
  - **D, manual inventory snapshot check**, was reasonable early in T2 if used with a fresh, timestamped snapshot.
  - **E, manual pick lists**, were necessary to maintain physical flow when paired with signed, lot-level evidence and later reconciliation.
  - **F, temporary release control**, was directionally resilient for controlled substances because it replaced a lost system gate with human review.
  - **I and J, deferral and backlog prioritization**, are resilient when rules are explicit, risk-based, and fair.
  - **K, reconciliation**, is a necessary recovery control, though not a substitute for real-time control.

- **Risky shortcuts / concealed noncompliance**
  - **C, skipping authorization**, traded compliance for speed. Even if limited to ambient products, it normalized an authorization gap.
  - **G, dispatch without scan**, created the largest traceability break. The T2 metrics confirm this: traceability exceptions rose from 0.8% baseline to 9.5%.
  - **H, phone POD**, is acceptable only as a structured fallback for lower-risk products. As sole evidence for controlled substances or cold-chain deliveries, it is inadequate.
  - **D becomes risky over time**: a Day 0 snapshot is understandable; a Day 2 snapshot is stale and can produce phantom inventory and misallocation.
  - **F was incomplete**: focusing on controlled substances did not address cold-chain temperature documentation, which rose to 4.8%.
  - **L must not be treated as proof of compliance**. The T4 DFG shows “Invoice close  Audit remediation” for 2,300 cases, proving that operational close occurred before evidence was complete.

- **Ambiguous adaptations depending on risk appetite**
  - **A** is resilient if it is “hospital first with controls,” but concealed noncompliance if it becomes “hospital first regardless of controls.”
  - **I and J** are resilient if transparent and risk-based; they become unfair if informal and invisible.
  - **H** is acceptable for ambient orders with structured attestation, but not for controlled substances.
  - **K and L** are valuable recovery mechanisms, but if management counts them as equivalent to real-time compliance, they hide the true control failure.

The conformance numbers support this interpretation. T2 fitness dropped to 0.71 and fitting traces to 44%; that does not mean every deviation was bad. However, the move-on-log deviations and move-on-model deviations show that some adaptations removed controls rather than replacing them. By T4, fitness recovered only to 0.90 and fitting traces to 76%, below baseline, while the audit queue persisted. That is the signature of operational recovery with residual compliance debt.

## 4. Recovery process model

**Notation used below:** `` means sequence; `<decision>` means a branching decision; `||` means parallel activities; `[guard]` means a required condition before proceeding.

**High-level flow:**

`Monitor  <Trigger continuity mode?>  Incident declaration  Triage  Minimum-control gate  Manual or system execution  Parallel evidence capture  Dispatch/POD  Recovery mode  Reconciliation  Audit closure  Normal KPI reporting`

**A. Explicit trigger conditions**

Enter **Continuity Mode** if any of the following occur:

1. WMS, scanning, or electronic authorization is unavailable for more than 30 minutes.
2. Inventory synchronization variance exceeds 1%, or the manual inventory snapshot is older than 2 hours.
3. Conformance fitness falls below 0.90 or fitting-trace percentage below 75% on a rolling sample.
4. Traceability exceptions exceed 2× baseline, i.e. above approximately 1.6%.
5. Cold-chain documentation missing exceeds 1%, or controlled-substance dual-control missing exceeds 0.5%.
6. A cyber incident is formally declared affecting order-to-delivery systems, even before metrics degrade.

Escalate to **High-Risk Continuity Mode** if any affected order involves controlled substances, cold-chain products, recall-sensitive items, or hospital-critical delivery.

**B. Triage rules**

After `Receive order  Emergency/compliance triage`, assign a priority score:

`Priority = customer criticality + product risk + clinical urgency + order age + delivery-window risk`

Non-negotiable rules:

1. **Product risk can override customer priority.** A hospital-critical controlled-substance order still requires full controlled-substance controls.
2. **No order ships without an authorization evidence source.** Electronic or manually verified offline authorization must exist.
3. **High-risk products go to compliance hold if minimum controls cannot be met.**
4. **Aging boost applies:** orders waiting too long gain priority to avoid starvation of pharmacy-standard and wholesale-bulk segments.

Tiers:

- **Tier 0 — Safety/high-risk hold:** recall-sensitive orders with uncertain recall status, controlled substances without dual control, cold-chain orders without temperature evidence. Action: hold, remediate, QA release.
- **Tier 1 — Mission-critical:** hospital-critical orders with complete minimum evidence. Action: fast lane with compliance co-sign.
- **Tier 2 — Standard care:** pharmacy-standard orders, especially ambient or low-risk products. Action: process after Tier 1, batch where possible.
- **Tier 3 — Deferrable:** wholesale-bulk, ambient, non-recall-sensitive, no urgent clinical need. Action: defer with ETA and customer communication.

**C. Minimum controls by product/customer risk**

| Risk class | Minimum controls before dispatch | Prohibited shortcuts |
|---|---|---|
| **All orders** | Verified customer identity; product identity; quantity; lot where applicable; authorization evidence from electronic system or approved offline list; timestamped inventory source; picker/checker; release evidence; delivery evidence. | No shipment with unknown customer, unknown product, or undocumented authorization. |
| **Hospital-critical** | Priority handling plus all product-specific controls. Compliance co-signature for any deviation. Fast lane may reorder work but cannot remove controls. | No blanket waiver because the customer is a hospital. |
| **Ambient** | Offline authorization from versioned list; timestamped inventory snapshot; manual pick checklist; dispatcher handoff record; structured POD. | No assumed standing authorization; no routine dispatch without scan-equivalent record. |
| **Cold-chain** | Temperature logger or manual temperature log started before pick; temperature check at pack, dispatch, and delivery; QA review of temperature evidence. | No dispatch with missing temperature documentation; phone POD alone is insufficient. |
| **Controlled-substance** | Verified authorization; dual control at pick and pack; lot/serial capture; two-person signatures; secure courier chain of custody; restricted POD with recipient identity. | No skip authorization; no single-person release; no phone-only POD; no dispatch without scan-equivalent chain-of-custody. |
| **Recall-sensitive** | Recall-list check from authoritative offline source with timestamp; lot-level traceability; quarantine of suspect lots; QA release. | No dispatch if recall status is unknown. |
| **Wholesale-bulk** | Standard authorization and inventory controls; may be deferred under transparent rules; must not be canceled without approval and customer notification. | No invisible indefinite deferral. |

**D. Manual evidence capture**

During Continuity Mode, every case receives a manual evidence packet:

- Order ID, customer, product, quantity, lot/expiry/serial where applicable.
- Authorization source: electronic record, offline list version,/phone callback log, verifier name, timestamp.
- Inventory source: snapshot ID, snapshot time, person who checked availability.
- Pick evidence: picker, checker, time, location, lot selected.
- Product-specific evidence: temperature readings, dual-control signatures, recall-list check result.
- Pack/release evidence: packer, release approver, time.
- Dispatch evidence: courier, seal number where relevant, scan or scan-equivalent record.
- POD evidence: signature, photo, structured phone attestation, recipient role, timestamp.
- Deviation fields: what control was unavailable, who approved the workaround, and required remediation.

Rule: **for high-risk products, no required field means no ship.** For ambient emergencies, deviations can be conditionally accepted only with documented approval and an open remediation flag.

**E. Backlog prioritization**

When systems return:

`Deferred orders  Backlog scoring  <Minimum controls complete?>  Release to pick / Hold for remediation / Cancel with approval`

Backlog rules:

1. Hospital-critical and high-product-risk orders first.
2. Then pharmacy-standard orders with clinical impact or long age.
3. Then wholesale-bulk, with aging boost to prevent indefinite starvation.
4. Recall-sensitive or controlled orders do not move simply because they are old; they require control completion.
5. Cancellations require reason code, approver, and customer communication.
6. Backlog dashboards show count, oldest age, segment mix, and product-risk mix.

**F. Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow**

`System restored  Ingest manual packets  Three-way match: order record / inventory record / physical evidence  Exception queue by severity  Remediation  Compliance approval  Case close`

Severity rules:

- **Severity 1:** controlled-substance, cold-chain, recall-sensitive, or hospital-critical cases with missing evidence. Remediate within 24 hours; escalate to QA/compliance leadership.
- **Severity 2:** ambient hospital-critical or pharmacy-standard cases with missing POD/scan evidence. Remediate within 72 hours.
- **Severity 3:** low-risk bulk documentation gaps. Remediate within agreed SLA; cannot block all operations but must remain visible.

Important design rules:

1. Invoice close may be conditional, but the dashboard must show open remediation.
2. After-the-fact remediation must never silently convert an uncontrolled shipment into a “compliant” record.
3. Missing temperature, authorization, dual-control, or scan evidence must be classified as a control deviation, not inferred or reconstructed from assumption.
4. Recovery is complete only when conformance, traceability, and audit backlog return near baseline, not merely when cycle time normalizes.

## 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should accept temporary deviation from the baseline sequence when it preserves hospital supply and is supported by contemporaneous evidence: offline authorization, timestamped inventory checks, signed manual picks, structured POD, and transparent backlog scoring. It should not accept skipped authorization, routine dispatch without scan-equivalent evidence, phone-only POD for controlled or cold-chain products, informal prioritization that silently sacrifices pharmacies or bulk customers, or “operational recovery” that depends on a hidden audit-remediation tail. The recommendation would soften if evidence showed that temporary deviations retained equivalent real-time control completeness and produced no material increase in exceptions; it would tighten if future incidents show repeated missing authorization, missing temperature/controlled-substance evidence, rising audit queues after cycle times normalize, or worsening service inequity across customer groups.
