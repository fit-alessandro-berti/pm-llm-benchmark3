**Redesigned flow:** Intake -> Enhanced auto-screen (deployed in ~2 weeks) classifies cases into four triage lanes: (1) Complete + Low-risk -> Fast approval; (2) Incomplete + High digital literacy -> Evidence portal (targeting 30–45% reduction in repeated evidence loops); (3) Incomplete + Low digital literacy / unclear -> Human evidence request with shorter timer to reduce 26% second-request loops; (4) High-value / Premium / Unclear -> Mandatory Manual/Fraud review (hard R1/R2). Instead of universal pre-dispatch gates, a risk-based sampling model is used: quality inspectors verify part selection only for high-value and cases with prior evidence gaps; others proceed to dispatch with rapid close, saving the 6–18 h universal delay. Post-dispatch, a customer-feedback loop catches errors before they become 96 h complaints. Reopened claims are handled by manual review per R3, but only after first reopening to protect resources.

**Targets:** The 22% unclear/missing-evidence segment (140 h median, highest abandonment); the 26% repeated-evidence loops; and the manual-review bottleneck (7 reviewers).

**Mechanism:** This redesign attacks the bottleneck rather than the speed. By using the evidence portal and updated rules, it reduces the mean 34 h loop back to Manual eligibility review and lowers the 140 h unclear-case median. Dynamic triage prevents the 15–35% manual-inflow surge from overwhelming reviewers by routing only genuinely uncertain cases to full manual review. Sampling avoids adding universal delay, preserving speed for the 25% Standard low-risk cohort.

**Sacrifices / unresolved:** Sampling leaves residual risk: wrong replacements can still reach customers for unverified fast-track cases, and the long-tail complaint pattern (mean 96 h) may persist at a lower rate. If portal adoption is low among low-literacy Standard customers, abandonment may rise without cycle-time benefit. It does not fully enforce R1/R2 by structural design—only by rule updates—so alignment deviations could remain unless monitored.

6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A (Segment-Hardened Fast Track)**
- *Failure 1 — Manual queue explosion:* The updated auto-screen routes 15–35% more cases to Manual eligibility review. With 7 reviewers at 36 h/week, utilization crosses 82% rapidly; mean 7.5 h becomes nonlinear (potentially >36 h), increasing complaint/reopen via delay rather than quality. The 26% evidence-loop rate may worsen if reviewers are too busy to resolve evidence gaps quickly.
- *Failure 2 — Gate becomes ceremonial:* If the 6–18 h pre-dispatch quality gate is staffed by the same 2 inspectors used only during campaigns, it creates a new bottleneck; wrong replacements slip through if part-match logic is weak, preserving the 96 h complaint tail for a subset of cases.
- *Bottleneck migration:* The bottleneck shifts from “fast approval causing downstream complaints” to “manual eligibility review queue” and then to “pre-dispatch verification station.”

**Redesign B (Repair-First Fulfillment)**
- *Failure 1 — Repair capacity collapse:* Redirecting high-value cases from dispatch to repair overloads 3 technicians. Repair quote already suffers from part-price confirmation delays; mean 20 h to repair can balloon, pushing high-value median beyond 120 h and triggering service-credit exposure (premium expectations near 24 h).
- *Failure 2 — Quality check backlog:* Scaling quality check to all repaired high-value devices uses underused inspectors, but if reopened cases also surge (because repair takes longer), the 45-minute daily Escalation board is insufficient; reopened premium cases wait, and R3 compliance risks shift from “close before review” to “review before close but too late.”
- *Bottleneck migration:* The bottleneck moves from “replacement dispatch / wrong part selection” to “repair device / repair quote” and “quality inspection”; also to “escalation board” if reopened volume is not reduced proportionally.

**Redesign C (Evidence-Loop / Sampling)**
- *Failure 1 — Portal abandonment and hidden cost:* The portal reduces repeated evidence loops by 30–45%, but for the Standard low-risk segment (25% of cases, high tolerance for 72 h if visibility is good), low digital literacy may drive abandonment up; lost claim recovery may offset speed gains.
- *Failure 2 — Sampling misses slow-tail errors:* Because only high-value/uncertain cases get pre-shipment verification, wrong replacements in the fast-track standard cohort reach customers; the 96 h mean complaint pattern persists at a reduced rate, and compliance alignment (fitness 0.91) improves only marginally.
- *Bottleneck migration:* The bottleneck shifts from “manual review backlog” to “evidence-portal support / triage desk” and possibly to “post-dispatch customer service” because sampling delays detection.

7. Recommended experiment sequence

**Phase 1 — First pilot (Weeks 1–4): Redesign C elements on Standard low-risk accessory, complete-evidence sub-cohort**
- Deploy updated auto-screen rules (2-week development) and evidence portal for a 20% sample of intake, restricted to Standard low-risk cases with complete evidence.
- Monitor: (a) second-request evidence-loop rate (target: reduce 26% by at least 30%); (b) manual-inflow change (watch for >15% increase); (c) abandonment rate (stop if absolute increase >10%); (d) median cycle time (should hold near 13 h).
- *Stop/go:* Go only if manual reviewer utilization stays <82% and portal abandonment does not spike. If manual queue grows sharply, halt and add human-fallback triage before scaling.

**Phase 2 — Second pilot (Weeks 5–10): Redesign A rules + pre-dispatch gate on Premium high-value devices**
- Enforce R1 (Mandatory Manual/Fraud before Fast) and R2 (evidence complete) via routing engine; add 6–18 h part-match gate for this segment (14% share).
- Monitor: (a) alignment-derived R1/R2 violation rate on pilot traces (target: near-zero); (b) complaint/reopen rate (target: <10% vs baseline 22%); (c) p90 cycle time (watch for gate pushing >120 h); (d) manual review queue length.
- *Stop/go:* Go if complaints drop and compliance aligns; stop if p90 exceeds 120 h or reviewer queue exceeds 82% utilization, because the mechanism fails by delay, not quality.

**Phase 3 — Third pilot (Weeks 11–16): Redesign B repair-first pathway on Standard high-value devices with repairable defects**
- Route 30% of Standard high-value cases (21% share) to Repair quote -> Repair device -> Quality check, bypassing replacement dispatch; allocate 2 inspectors to this path.
- Monitor: (a) complaint/reopen (target: <7% vs 18% baseline); (b) repair rework rate (target: <8% vs 12.5%); (c) technician utilization (stop if >90%); (d) total cost per case including labor and error avoidance.
- *Stop/go:* Go if quality improves without technician backlog; stop if repair-method delay creates new service-credit costs exceeding EUR 410 replacement-error savings.

**Sequencing logic:** Start with C because it is lowest structural risk (rules + portal) and reveals the true manual-inflow elasticity. Use that elasticity data to calibrate A’s routing hardening. Only after resource capacity is known (from C and A pilots) do we test B’s fulfillment-method change, which is highest risk because it shifts volume from dispatch to repair.

**Learning real vs shifted:** Do not judge success by intake-to-approval cycle time alone. Monitor “time to final close including reopened loops” (current 96 h mean complaint tail), cost per case including service credits and reshipments (fast approval saves 31% pre-rework, 9% after), and conformance alignment (fitness / R1-R3 deviation rate) on pilot samples. Track resource utilization by activity—reviewers, fraud, repair techs, quality inspectors—to detect bottleneck migration before it becomes a customer-facing delay. Compare segment-level outcomes (Premium high-value vs Standard low-risk) rather than overall averages, because the contradiction is hidden inside aggregated frequency.
