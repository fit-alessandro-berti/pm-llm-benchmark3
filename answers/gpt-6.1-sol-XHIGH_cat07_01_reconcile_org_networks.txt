# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

The strongest provisional interpretation is that Mira coordinates a selective, complex stream of work between automation, adjusters, and specialists. This can coexist with valuable expertise, a fragile staffing arrangement, and discretionary routing that needs clearer controls. None of the networks independently establishes her formal authority, effectiveness, or responsibility for delays.

### Handover-of-work network

This network connects resources whose recorded events follow one another within a case, according to the chosen adjacency or succession definition. Mira’s high reported in-degree, out-degree, and betweenness indicate a broad position in recorded transitions. Bot_Rules → Mira is a prominent exception entry point; her outgoing links reach both teams’ adjusters and medical and legal specialists. Specialist → Mira returns suggest that she sometimes retains responsibility for moving cases onward after consultation.

This supports an exception-routing or coordination function. It does not prove that she personally communicates with every recipient, manages those resources, or causes their subsequent waiting time. An automated assignment recorded under her identity could produce similar edges. Sequential timestamps also do not establish an actual transfer when activities run in parallel. Betweenness measures shortest paths in a constructed graph, not the proportion of actual claims that require her.

Bots and vendors shape the graph: Bot_Rules distributes work, Bot_Ingest supplies an upstream entry point, and Vendor_Pay is largely a downstream endpoint. Mira’s significance should therefore be assessed in the complete network and in carefully defined human-resource views. Removing intermediary events can create inferred human-to-human transitions that were never directly recorded.

### Working-together network

This network describes shared case participation. It is undirected and does not establish simultaneous work, direct communication, or teamwork quality. The reported patterns suggest recurring North and South team relationships, while Mira shares fewer cases with each individual teammate. Her 22% overall coverage, combined with her concentration in reopened claims and complaints, is consistent with selective involvement outside the routine team cores.

High handover frequency and low shared-case strength can coexist because:

- Several transitions or returns can occur within one shared case, increasing handover counts without increasing the number of shared cases.
- Mira’s shared cases can be spread across many recipients, giving her considerable routing breadth without a strong relationship with any single resource.
- Different denominators, normalization, thresholds, and observation windows can make edge strengths incomparable.

However, **short events do not themselves reduce binary case co-occurrence**. One routing event still puts Mira in that case’s resource set. Disappearing for several events changes sequencing and duration, but it does not erase shared case participation. The supplied operational explanation is therefore incomplete unless the implementation uses duration weighting or another nonstandard definition.

There is also a concrete denominator problem: if the percentages refer to all claims, Alice–Carla cannot co-occur in 27% of them. Alice participates in only 665 cases, and Bot_Ingest alone establishes at least 4,100 distinct cases; the maximum all-case rate would be approximately 16.2%. The percentages may be conditional on a subset or use another normalization. Until clarified, the relative team pattern is suggestive, but the numerical comparison is provisional.

### Subcontracting network

The reported short-horizon A → B → A patterns capture a resource’s return after another resource acts. Mira → Omar → Mira and Mira → Priya → Mira fit consultation, specialist scheduling, or retained exception ownership. Her high subcontracting betweenness suggests a prominent position in the graph built from such return relationships.

“Subcontracting” here is a structural process-mining label. It does not establish a contractual relationship, hierarchical delegation, or control over the specialist’s professional judgment. The same motifs could represent clarification loops, rework, automatic callbacks, or recording conventions. Team-lead → adjuster → team-lead patterns show that return structures also occur in ordinary approval workflows.

The n=2 setting favors very short returns and can miss consultations with intervening activities. The evidence that Mira sometimes disappears for several events therefore matters: some of her coordination may be omitted. Bot_Rules → Mira → Bot_Rules could reflect a human review followed by a rules update. Temp_1 → Vendor_Verify → Temp_1 shows a similar structure involving external verification. Neither is proof of managerial delegation.

### Performance/time statistics

Mira’s 4.2-minute median service time suggests brief recorded actions; it does not demonstrate spare capacity or small organizational impact. Fragmented work, interruptions, and responsibility for unresolved exceptions may be poorly captured by event duration. Her exception exposure and high-variance cycle-time contribution also make raw comparisons with routine handlers misleading.

During her absence, exception-case 90th-percentile cycle time increased from 8.4 to 13.9 days: 5.5 days, or roughly 65%. Ordinary low-value claims were unaffected. This is meaningful evidence of selective dependency on her function, but it does not isolate her absence from changes in incoming complexity, staffing, specialist availability, or backlog.

Elapsed time between events is not automatically queue waiting time. Its interpretation depends on whether timestamps represent starts or completions, whether activities overlap, and whether recorded intervals include off-hours or external response time. The 42- and 51-minute specialist handover intervals cannot be reduced by subtracting specialist median service times: those are different statistics over potentially different observations.

Vendor_Verify’s 2.1-hour and Vendor_Pay’s 1.3-hour reported service times may include asynchronous turnaround. Their delays can dominate portions of end-to-end time even though they have little routing betweenness. The undefined “cycle-time contribution” measure must be clarified before attributing delay to a person; several resources may share the same long case.

### Role discovery or activity-profile similarity

Resource–activity profiles would probably distinguish Mira’s missing-information, reopening, complaint, and assignment work from specialist reviews and adjuster assessment/negotiation. Partial overlap with team leads, juniors, and temporaries is expected. This supports a coordination-oriented activity profile, rather than a conclusion that she belongs to a particular formal managerial category.

Similarity is sensitive to activity taxonomy, frequency normalization, rare activities, and clustering thresholds. Bot_Rules and a team lead can both perform “Triage Claim” without having equivalent authority. Likewise, Vendor_Verify and temporary staff share “Validate Coverage” without being interchangeable. Discovered roles describe recorded behavior, not qualifications, permissions, contractual status, or an organizational chart.

“Assign Specialist” appears in Mira’s profile and fragments but is absent from the initial activity catalogue. That mismatch needs resolution before treating her profile as a reliable role signature.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

These hypotheses overlap: a legitimate coordinator can also become a key-person dependency. Their plausibility should be updated with future evidence rather than reduced to one definitive label.

### Hypothesis A: Formal coordinator / exception desk

- **Mechanism:** Routing rules send exceptional claims to Mira, who requests clarification, assigns expertise, and returns claims to their normal handling path.
- **Supporting evidence:** The strong Bot_Rules → Mira link, relevant activities, and disproportionate participation in reopened claims and complaints fit a designated exception function. Specialist return patterns are consistent with retained coordination responsibility.
- **Contradicting or limiting evidence:** The event log cannot establish formal appointment, documented accountability, or authorized decision boundaries. The process owner’s “queue cleaner” description may acknowledge this function while disputing leadership status. Some recorded assignments may be automated.
- **Predictions:** Exception routing should follow reproducible criteria. A trained substitute should reproduce much of her activity and routing pattern. Case attributes and recorded reasons should explain a substantial share of specialist destinations; routine claims should continue largely outside the desk.

### Hypothesis B: Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency

- **Mechanism:** Difficult claims require Mira’s availability or tacit knowledge before progressing. Short decisions become a queueing constraint when nobody else can make them, even if her active processing time is modest.
- **Supporting evidence:** High routing and return-pattern centrality, concentration in hard cases, adjusters’ reliance on her knowledge, and the absence-period deterioration support dependence on her function.
- **Contradicting or limiting evidence:** Ordinary claims were unaffected, so the concern is selective rather than universal. There are no arrival rates, utilization measures, queue timestamps, or staffing records proving sustained overload. Downstream specialists or vendors may be the limiting capacity; an absence-related backlog is not automatically a permanent bottleneck.
- **Predictions:** Cases awaiting her routing decision should age during absences or demand peaks. Delay should appear before her next action, with a backlog-clearing pattern after return. Qualified backup coverage should reduce these delays, unless the constraint subsequently shifts to specialist capacity.

### Hypothesis C: Organizational boundary spanner or triage broker

- **Mechanism:** Mira connects otherwise separate teams, automation, temporary staff, and specialist disciplines, using knowledge of who can handle unusual cases.
- **Supporting evidence:** Her links span North and South adjusters and both specialists. High handover breadth alongside weak pairwise shared-case relationships fits brokerage across several groups. Adjusters’ description directly supports expertise in finding the appropriate destination.
- **Contradicting or limiting evidence:** A graph spanning resource names does not establish actual organizational boundaries or unique expertise. Mandatory workflow design could generate the same structure. Neither regional affiliations nor alternative routing paths are fully documented here.
- **Predictions:** Her involvement should be enriched in cross-region, reassigned, unusual, or multidisciplinary claims. Effective brokerage should predict appropriate first assignments and fewer avoidable specialist referrals after matching for complexity. Her bridging position should remain visible in graphs that explicitly account for bots and vendors.

### Hypothesis D: Quality gatekeeper or automation monitor

- **Mechanism:** Mira interprets ambiguous rule outcomes, detects missing information, and returns corrected cases to automated or human processing.
- **Supporting evidence:** Bot_Rules → Mira routing, missing-information work, reopenings, complaints, and 62 Bot_Rules → Mira → Bot_Rules patterns support a manual review interface.
- **Contradicting or limiting evidence:** Reopening and escalation activities may record problems detected elsewhere. The short return patterns cover only part of her work and do not demonstrate error prevention or improved quality. Unnecessary review could also create delay.
- **Predictions:** Logs should show identifiable rule triggers, clarification outcomes, and revised routing or screening results after her intervention. Appropriate interventions should reduce downstream correction or misrouting rates for comparable cases; duplicative interventions would add time without measurable quality benefit.

### Hypothesis E: Recording or network-construction artifact

- **Mechanism:** A shared account, automatically attributed assignments, inconsistent lifecycle handling, repeated events, or incompatible normalization exaggerates her personal centrality or understates shared-case participation.
- **Supporting evidence:** The co-case denominator inconsistency, activity-catalogue mismatch, and unclear graph definitions warrant this hypothesis. The listed Mira outgoing handover frequencies total 1,640, exceeding her 960 events. That is impossible for one immediate-successor edge per source event in the same extract, although broader succession counting or different extracts could explain it.
- **Contradicting or limiting evidence:** Reported reliance on her expertise and the selective absence-period deterioration make a purely computational explanation less persuasive. They still require corroboration and do not establish that every event represents her own action.
- **Predictions:** Harmonizing extracts, lifecycle representation, resource identities, and counting rules should materially alter the statistics if artifacts are important. Assignment audit data should distinguish personal actions from automatic or shared-account activity. Persistent brokerage and absence sensitivity after correction would strengthen the substantive hypotheses.

# 3. Risk assessment

- **Bottleneck risk:** Exception routing may delay claims when Mira is unavailable or demand exceeds coverage. The absence evidence makes this credible, but the location of delay remains uncertain. Distinguish waiting for a routing decision from waiting for Omar, Priya, an adjuster, or a vendor. Her coordination may also prevent a larger downstream bottleneck.

- **Coordination risk:** Concentrating routing knowledge in one person can create inconsistent substitutions, lost context, and difficult handoffs. Conversely, eliminating the desk can fragment ownership, increase formal escalations, and send unsuitable work to scarce specialists. Measure both coordination cost and avoided misrouting; high centrality alone establishes neither benefit nor harm.

- **Key-person risk:** Dependence on Mira’s tacit expertise threatens continuity during leave, turnover, or sudden workload spikes. The vulnerability may lie in knowledge, authorization, system access, or staffing coverage. A named backup is insufficient unless that person can actually execute the decisions and access the necessary information.

- **Compliance/control risk:** Detecting problems and selecting specialists may give Mira meaningful discretion. Potential concerns include inconsistent referral criteria, undocumented overrides, conflicts of interest, and unclear accountability. There is no direct evidence of wrongdoing or an actual segregation-of-duties violation. Her profile does not show settlement approval or payment execution. Assess applicable control requirements and decision rights before imposing additional approvals; use recorded reasons and proportionate independent review where warranted.

- **Workload fairness risk:** Event counts understate some forms of complex coordination and cannot establish equal workload. Comparing Mira’s short events with specialists’ lengthy reviews, bots’ throughput, or vendors’ turnaround would be misleading. Compare human workload using available working hours, complexity, active effort, interruptions, responsibility, and outcomes. Include temporary staff’s escalation needs and distinguish legitimate specialization from systematic concentration of difficult cases or unsupported delegation.

- **Data-quality risk:** Unclear denominators, lifecycle duplication, evolving flags, activity naming, and incomplete observation windows can produce false role and performance conclusions. Reconcile the direct-handover count issue and the co-case percentages before using them for staffing decisions. Check case-count percentile ordering and whether Bot_Ingest and Bot_Rules exception shares can differ as reported when they cover almost the same cases: an immutable case-level flag would make that discrepancy suspicious. Vendor_Pay’s nonzero betweenness alongside zero outgoing degree also needs explanation if both refer to the same directed graph. These inconsistencies identify measurement questions, not evidence of misconduct.

# 4. Further process-mining analyses

### Establish a consistent analytical log

Use `pm4py.format_dataframe` with explicit case, activity, and timestamp mappings. Establish whether each row is a lifecycle transition or one completed activity instance. Pair starts and completions using reliable instance identifiers where available; repeated activities make pairing by case and name alone ambiguous. Normalize timezones and retain a deterministic tie-breaker, while marking uncertain order among concurrent events.

Verify resource identity, automation attribution, the “Assign Specialist” taxonomy, flag semantics, and common observation windows. Keep full case histories where possible. Separate cases entering during the window from older cases observed during it, and record censoring at the window boundary. Document network direction, normalization, thresholds, succession horizon, and centrality weighting.

### Handover analysis

Recompute `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` on the validated representation, explicitly documenting immediate versus broader succession behavior in the installed implementation. Report raw transitions, distinct cases per edge, and destination probabilities conditional on the source. Compare Mira’s degree, strength, and betweenness across exception and ordinary claims, regions, and weeks.

Use `pm4py.discover_network_analysis` or pandas/NetworkX to annotate resource transitions with activity pairs and elapsed-time distributions. Separate Bot_Rules → Mira exception entry, Mira → specialist referrals, specialist returns, and adjuster reassignments. Compare the complete graph with an induced human-only graph; if intermediary events are skipped instead, label the resulting inferred edges explicitly.

Specify whether weighted betweenness uses weights as distances or converts interaction strengths into distances. Stronger interaction should not silently become a longer path. Bootstrap whole cases to assess centrality stability. Node-removal calculations can identify structural vulnerability, but cannot predict actual rerouting behavior or service capacity after an organizational change.

### Working-together analysis

Use `pm4py.discover_working_together_network`, then independently construct a binary case–resource matrix. For each pair, report shared cases, the fraction of all cases, Jaccard overlap, and the conditional fraction of Mira’s cases involving that counterpart. This directly resolves whether low reported strength reflects limited overlap or normalization.

Recompute within comparable exception cohorts and team/region subsets. Give each case appropriate weight rather than letting repeated events inflate shared-case participation. If start–complete intervals exist, analyze temporal overlap separately: shared case participation and concurrent work answer different questions.

### Subcontracting analysis

Use `pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network` with n=2 and sensitivity analyses at larger supported horizons, such as 3 and 5. Enumerate the underlying resource-return fragments to verify the implementation’s horizon convention and graph construction.

Compare distinct-case prevalence, repeat counts, elapsed return time, and subsequent outcomes. Distinguish completed consultation, clarification, unsuccessful referral, reopened work, and automation callbacks. A return to Mira followed by successful progression supports retained coordination; repeated returns without resolution suggest a different operational issue. Include the Bot_Rules and Temp_1/Vendor_Verify motifs in this comparison.

### Activity similarity and role discovery

Apply `pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` and `pm4py.discover_organizational_roles`. Inspect normalized activity profiles alongside raw volumes and test clustering sensitivity. Examine whether Mira’s profile stays distinctive after removing common activities and separating routine from exception cases.

Retain labels for human, automated, and vendor resources when interpreting clusters. If her role is unique, look for staff with partial overlap who might be trained as backups; similarity alone does not establish readiness or authorization. Check whether the profile changes during her absence as others perform portions of her function.

### Cohort and variant filtering

Use `pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values`, `pm4py.filter_variants`, and dataframe case/time selections to compare exception flags, priorities, amounts, regions, channels, reopening/complaint paths, and specialist-referral variants.

For a case-level exception cohort, select qualifying case IDs and retain their complete traces. Dropping only individual events changes adjacency and can manufacture apparent handovers or short return motifs. Likewise, document inferred edges created by activity filtering. If flags evolve during a case, distinguish the flag known at routing time from a later outcome flag; using the latter to explain earlier decisions can introduce hindsight bias.

Compare variants bypassing Mira with otherwise similar variants involving her. Treat differences as associations until case mix and selection into exception handling have been addressed.

### Service and waiting-time analysis

Where valid start and completion timestamps exist, use `pm4py.get_service_time` for supported activity summaries and pandas for resource- and cohort-level distributions. Use `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` for case-level measures after verifying its duration and overlap conventions. Do not interpret an unexplained residual as a particular person’s queue time.

Estimate waiting between an enabling or assignment event and the next activity start, distinguishing specialist queues, missing-information responses, external verification, and payment processing. Account for parallel work and business calendars; elapsed case duration cannot generally be allocated by summing all resource intervals. If only completion timestamps exist, report elapsed gaps and state that active service and waiting are not identifiable from them alone.

Report medians and tail percentiles, queue age, arrivals, completions, and unresolved work. Interpret vendor turnaround separately from human active effort, and compare human demand with available staffing hours rather than median duration multiplied by event count.

### Absence comparison and prospective validation

Analyze comparable periods before, during, and after Mira’s absence, using cases eligible for her function and ordinary claims as a possible comparison group. Match or adjust for pre-routing case attributes, demand, calendars, staffing, and specialist/vendor availability. A difference-in-differences approach is useful only if its assumptions, including comparable underlying trends, are credible.

Locate where queues accumulated and check whether timestamps for routing, specialist start, or other stages changed. Follow cases long enough to include delayed completions; relying only on completed claims can understate backlog. Report sample sizes and case-level bootstrap uncertainty for tail statistics.

Prospectively test documented routing plus trained backup coverage in a bounded pilot. Evaluate delay, referral appropriateness, specialist workload, complaints, and control exceptions together. Faster routing with greater downstream rework would not establish an improvement.

# 5. Recommendation

### Change immediately

Preserve the exception-coordination function while reducing its dependence on one person. Clarify Mira’s operational responsibilities, decision rights, routing criteria, and escalation boundaries with the process owner and relevant specialists. Capture reasons for unusual referrals and overrides in the workflow. Apply existing independent-review requirements to decisions that actually require them.

Start documenting tacit routing knowledge and training at least one designated backup with the necessary access and authority. Provide explicit absence coverage and ownership of unresolved exception queues. Agree referral and return expectations with specialists, temporary staff, and vendors so that coverage does not simply push unfiltered work downstream.

Validate the contradictory measurement definitions before using these dashboards for individual performance evaluation or structural staffing decisions. These steps address continuity and accountability without requiring a definitive formal role label.

### Monitor

Track exception queue size and age, routing turnaround, tail cycle time, repeated referrals, unresolved returns, specialist utilization, and vendor turnaround. Compare periods with Mira, with backup coverage, and with neither. Adjust comparisons for case mix and available staffing.

Monitor assignment appropriateness, reasons for overrides, complaint and reopening outcomes, and the share of difficult cases concentrated on each human resource. Review whether coordination prevents unnecessary escalation and whether proposed controls introduce avoidable waiting. Interpret centrality changes together with service and quality outcomes.

### Defer until more evidence is available

Do not remove Mira, centralize more decisions under her, declare her a formal team lead, or impose a universal extra approval step solely from these networks. Do not automate ambiguous exception routing merely because her recorded actions are short.

If management later chooses to redistribute or replace her role, first provide documented routing knowledge, qualified decision-makers, clear case ownership, access rights, coverage, and sufficient specialist capacity. Pilot the replacement and establish acceptable service, quality, and control outcomes before withdrawing the current function. The objective is reliable exception handling, regardless of which role title or staffing arrangement ultimately delivers it.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira appears to coordinate difficult claims across teams and specialists. Her high handover and return-pattern centrality suggests that many exceptions pass through her, while low working-together scores suggest limited shared case coverage. These measures describe different relationships; they do not establish managerial authority or poor performance. Short routing events alone cannot explain low case co-occurrence, and the reported counts and percentage denominators need checking.

Her absence coincided with exception-case delays, while ordinary claims were unaffected. This makes dependency on her coordination function a credible concern, although staffing and case complexity could also explain part of the change. Selecting specialists may involve valuable judgment and warrants documented rules and oversight; there is no evidence of misconduct.

Management should document routing decisions, train a backup, and monitor exception queues, specialist workloads, delays, and outcomes. Keep the coordination function while testing improvements. Any replacement must preserve her knowledge and routing capacity, with clear accountability and evidence that service quality and controls improve.
