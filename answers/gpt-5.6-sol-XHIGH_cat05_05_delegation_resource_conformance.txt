## 2. Rule Interpretation

Let \(H(e)\) be the canonical human attributed to event \(e\). It may be unknown where session evidence is insufficient.

### RR1 — Drafter/approver separation

For every case \(c\):

\[
H(Draft_c) \ne H(Approve_c)
\]

This is evaluated across aliases, not account strings. If either action cannot be reliably mapped to a human, the result is **indeterminate**, not an automatic pass.

### RR2 — Implementer/verifier separation

For every case \(c\):

\[
H(Implement_c) \ne H(Verify_c)
\]

The stated rule requires different humans but does not expressly require the verifier to hold the historical “Independent Verifier” role. Consequently, Eva’s post-2026-08-11 Quality Engineer status and Grace’s Site Director status do not themselves violate RR2. They may still expose a separate qualification or independence weakness that would require a qualification matrix or additional policy.

### RR3 — High-risk approval authority

For a non-emergency HIGH-risk approval at time \(t\), the actor must be:

1. a standing QM at \(t\); or
2. a QM delegate under a valid delegation.

A delegation \(d\) is valid only if:

\[
start_d \le t \le end_d
\]

and all of the following hold:

- the action and change scope are covered;
- any required principal-absence condition is established at \(t\);
- the evidence is authentic and supports creation before use;
- the delegation chain permits the delegation; and
- no prohibited subdelegation occurred.

A controlled record that appears only later can corroborate migration or reconstruction, but **cannot alone prove that the delegation existed before the event**. D-QM-01 is nevertheless supported by the signed PDF timestamped before the relevant approvals. It cannot cover CHG-507 because that approval preceded both the delegation interval and the PDF timestamp.

### RR4 — Emergency substitution

A HIGH-risk emergency change is a justified exception only if:

1. `emergency=true`;
2. a credible safety or emergency rationale exists;
3. the IC or a valid IC delegate approves before implementation;
4. no invalid subdelegation is used; and
5. a QM or valid QM delegate performs the retrospective review within two business days.

The supplied log contains emergency flags but not the safety rationales, so rationale credibility remains manual and unresolved.

I count the implementation date as day zero and use the next two site business days, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and site holidays. In the absence of a stated site timezone, the supplied UTC dates are used. Thus:

- CHG-505, implemented Tuesday 2026-08-11, was due by the end of Thursday 2026-08-13. Its Friday review was late.
- CHG-510, also implemented Tuesday, was reviewed Wednesday and was timely.

Using an exact same-time deadline on the second following business day would not change either conclusion.

### Principal acting while on leave

Annual leave supports an absence condition for a delegate but does not automatically terminate the principal’s standing authority. Asha may personally approve a case while on leave if she actually performed the action and no policy suspends her authority.

A one-off principal action also does not automatically revoke a non-exclusive delegation for the rest of the leave period. It may, however, require closer review of whether the principal was “absent” at a particular time. No Ben approval overlaps Asha’s stated VPN interval.

### RR5 — Identity evidence

- Personal account ownership is an initial attribution, subject to contradictory evidence.
- Service-account resource names are not human identities.
- Strong event-specific badge, token, and session evidence may map a service event to a human.
- Weak workstation association or room presence is insufficient by itself.
- Unknown attribution makes human-level SoD indeterminate.

### RR6 — Suspicion versus proof

Pair frequency, reciprocity, shared devices, leave conflicts, and concentrated handovers are screening indicators. They do not prove collusion, intent, or misconduct. Adjudication requires transaction-level authentication, assignment opportunity, communications, benefits, deviations from normal routing, and other corroboration.

def standing_roles_at(human, timestamp):
    # Pandas interval logic for historical roles.
    mask = (
        roles["human"].eq(human)
        & roles["start"].le(timestamp)
        & roles["end"].ge(timestamp)
    )
    return sorted(roles.loc[mask, "role"].tolist())

def principal_absence_status(principal, timestamp):
    mask = (
        absences["human"].eq(principal)
        & absences["start"].le(timestamp)
        & absences["end"].ge(timestamp)
    )
    if mask.any():
        return True
    # No record is treated as unknown rather than proof of availability.
    return None

def combine_conditions(conditions):
    if any(x is not None and not bool(x) for x in conditions):
        return False
    if any(x is None for x in conditions):
        return None
    return True

def delegation_details(event, delegated_role):
    actor = event["org:resource"]
    timestamp = event["time:timestamp"]

    # Pandas interval logic for candidate delegations.
    mask = (
        delegations["delegate"].eq(actor)
        & delegations["delegated_role"].eq(delegated_role)
        & delegations["start"].le(timestamp)
        & delegations["end"].ge(timestamp)
    )

    candidates = delegations.loc[mask]
    results = []

    for _, delegation in candidates.iterrows():
        scope_ok = event["scope"] == delegation["scope"]
        action_ok = (
            event["concept:name"] == delegation["allowed_activity"]
        )
        evidence_ok = bool(delegation["preexisting_evidence"])
        chain_ok = not bool(delegation["prohibited_subdelegation"])

        if delegation["requires_principal_absence"]:
            absence_ok = principal_absence_status(
                delegation["principal"], timestamp
            )
        else:
            absence_ok = True

        # The event CSV has no incident_id. D-IC-03/04 therefore cannot
        # establish the CI-44 restriction automatically.
        if delegation["required_incident"]:
            incident_ok = None
        else:
            incident_ok = True

        overall = combine_conditions([
            scope_ok,
            action_ok,
            evidence_ok,
            chain_ok,
            absence_ok,
            incident_ok,
        ])

        results.append({
            "id": delegation["id"],
            "valid": overall,
            "scope_ok": scope_ok,
            "action_ok": action_ok,
            "absence_ok": absence_ok,
            "incident_ok": incident_ok,
            "chain_ok": chain_ok,
        })

    return results

## 6. Control Improvements

1. **Delegation creation**
   - Require delegation approval and registration before use.
   - Use immutable signatures, hashes, creation timestamps, and migration lineage.
   - Reject retroactive creation except through a controlled exception process.

2. **Effective-time and scope recording**
   - Store start/end timestamps, timezone, delegated action types, controlled scope codes, incident IDs, and permitted risk levels.
   - Make scope hierarchical and machine-readable so `cooling_system` versus `production_equipment` is not a free-text judgment.
   - Record whether retrospective review is included separately from initial approval.

3. **Principal availability**
   - Integrate leave, on-call, outage, and formal-unavailability records.
   - Require a transaction-time availability determination where delegation depends on absence.
   - Define whether a principal’s one-off action suspends, coexists with, or terminates a delegation.

4. **Alias management**
   - Maintain one immutable employee identifier across all systems.
   - Feed all historical aliases to the process-mining and SoD engine.
   - Block four-eyes checks based solely on distinct account strings.

5. **Individual authentication**
   - Prohibit interactive approvals and verifications through shared service accounts.
   - Require transaction-bound FIDO/MFA and re-authentication for regulated signatures.
   - Separate workstation ownership from user identity and record session handoffs.

6. **Emergency review**
   - Require incident ID, emergency flag, safety rationale, IC decision, and implementation chronology before permitting the exception.
   - Calculate the two-business-day deadline automatically using the controlled site calendar and timezone.
   - Escalate missing, late, or unauthorized retrospective reviews before closure.

7. **Pair rotation and independence**
   - Introduce risk-based reviewer rotation or secondary sampling for highly concentrated pairs.
   - Preserve expertise-based assignment exceptions, but require documented justification.
   - Do not treat repeated pairing alone as misconduct.

8. **Network monitoring**
   - Normalize network rates for assignment opportunity, shift, cell, role, activity, and risk.
   - Monitor sudden changes, reciprocal approval concentration, self-loops after alias normalization, and use of unresolved service identities.
   - Route alerts to investigation rather than automatically labeling collusion.

9. **Evidential retention**
   - Retain delegation originals, signature validation, migration audit trails, IAM alias history, FIDO assertions, session logs, endpoint telemetry, badge records, workflow comments, and controlled role history.
   - Apply synchronized timestamps and preservation holds when credential compromise is alleged.
