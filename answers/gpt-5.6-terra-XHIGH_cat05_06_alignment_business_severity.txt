## 1. Materiality Framework

The supplied alignment is a structural diagnostic: it says how the observed trace was repaired to fit the payable-claim model. It does not by itself establish whether a control was performed, whether policy conditions were met, or whether a customer or financial loss occurred. The policy overlay and corroborating evidence therefore take precedence over raw alignment cost.

### Severity scale

| Severity | Meaning | Typical treatment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Negligible | Permitted silent routing, UI/transport noise, or an explained duplicate with no control or customer impact. | Tune logging/filtering; no case incident. |
| 2 — Limited | Model-scope, label-mapping, or evidence-integration weakness with no evidence of an incorrect payment decision. | Correct data/model design and sample-review affected cases. |
| 3 — Material control/evidence concern | Customer-notification evidence is unreliable, a required record is not demonstrable, or a potentially meaningful policy issue is contained or uncertain. | Timely remediation and targeted retrospective review. |
| 4 — Serious policy/control breach | Confirmed prohibited treatment or materially unauthorized approval route, even if the amount is below the P2 high-value threshold. | Expedited investigation, population scan, and preventive control repair. |
| 5 — Severe | Payment released before required approval, high-value/high-risk/bank-change control failure, suspected payment diversion, or a failure with major irreversibility. | Immediate incident handling, payment/beneficiary review, senior control-owner escalation. |

Severity considers:

- **Control objective:** Preventing unauthorized, fraudulent, or diverted payment is more material than exact event-label conformance.
- **Exposure:** Amount, fraud risk, beneficiary-bank change, and threshold conditions matter. A low-value bank-change payment may still be severe because it is a diversion indicator.
- **Reversibility and timing:** A payment accepted by a bank before approval is more serious than a later missing log event because recovery may be difficult.
- **Customer and regulatory harm:** Missing timely notification may create service, complaint, or regulatory-record risk even where payment controls were sound.
- **Recurrence:** A one-off mapping issue differs from 74 connector retries or four policy-ineligible Auto Approves.
- **Evidence reliability:** Missing, mutable, late, or uncorrelated evidence weakens assurance. Evidence weakness can itself be a control deficiency, but it is not proof that the activity never happened.
- **Model scope:** Denials and withdrawals should not be treated as payable claims merely because the current model has no exit branch for them.

### Confidence is separate from severity

Severity is the potential or confirmed business impact. Confidence is the strength of the conclusion.

For example:

- **CL-602** is severity 5 because EUR 480,000, HIGH risk, and a one-day bank change create severe exposure if the Fraud Screen was absent; confidence is only medium because a missing event does not prove that a fraud review did not occur.
- **CL-603** is severity 1 with high confidence because the duplicate message ID and retry transport evidence strongly explain the extra notification event.
- **CL-610** is severity 5 with high confidence because the recorded data conditions directly violate P1 and P2 despite a zero-cost synchronous alignment.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Raw alignment cost is the sum of supplied edit operations under a uniform technical cost of 1 for every visible log-only or model-only move. It measures structural distance from this particular model under this particular cost function. It does not measure probability of fraud, policy compliance, financial loss, customer harm, audit sufficiency, or evidence confidence.

1. **A low cost can conceal severe payment risk.**  
   CL-602 has raw cost 1 because only `Fraud Screen` is model-only. Yet it involves EUR 480,000, HIGH risk, and a beneficiary-bank change one day earlier. P2 makes the missing or unproven fraud-control evidence highly material. One edit is not one unit of risk.

2. **A moderate cost can hide an irreversible sequencing breach.**  
   CL-604 has raw cost 3, but the critical fact is not the count of moves. It is that the bank accepted payment at 10:03 and Manual Approve was recorded at 10:20. The payment-before-approval breach is severe because payment acceptance may be difficult to reverse. The later `M(Pay Claim)` is an alignment repair, not evidence of a second payment.

3. **Zero cost does not prove policy compliance.**  
   CL-609 and CL-610 are fully synchronous and have raw cost 0. The model permits Auto Approve, but P1 and P2 depend on amount, risk, and recent beneficiary-bank-change data that are not encoded in the tree. CL-610 is particularly serious because P2 explicitly requires Fraud Screen and Manual Approve for any recent bank change.

4. **High cost can be an artifact of scope rather than misconduct.**  
   CL-607 and CL-612 each have raw cost 4 because a payable-claim model inserts missing assessment/approval/payment steps after a denial or withdrawal. Neither alignment proves that approval or payment should have occurred. The cost mostly measures the gap between the model’s scope and the observed case type.

5. **Duplicated telemetry can inflate cost without business deviation.**  
   CL-601 has raw cost 3 solely from UI events. CL-603 has raw cost 1 from a retried notification with the same message ID. P5 says UI telemetry, autosave, retry, and transport events are not business activities unless they reveal a meaningful control issue.

6. **Missing core events can coexist with credible external evidence.**  
   CL-606 has `M(Assess Loss)` at cost 1, but a signed external-adjuster report predates Fraud Screen and Manual Approve. The issue is event capture and case correlation, not necessarily failure to assess the loss. Conversely, the two external-adjuster cases without stable document IDs deserve lower confidence than cases with strong linkage.

7. **Activity granularity and naming affect cost.**  
   CL-611 costs 2 because `Assess Damage` and `Assess Loss` are different labels. If they are semantically equivalent under an effective-dated approved mapping, the alignment cost reflects vocabulary drift, not two failed controls. If they are not equivalent, the same technical pattern may be more material. Cost cannot resolve that semantic question.

8. **Hidden moves are fitting model behavior, not hidden business noncompliance.**  
   CL-605 has a `T(tau,c=0)` routing transition. Silent Petri-net transitions are valid internal model routing and are not missing business activities. Zero cost is meaningful there, but only because the silent move represents permitted technical structure.

9. **Cost-function design is arbitrary and distinct from policy materiality.**  
   The configuration assigns the same cost to missing fraud evidence, a duplicate retry, UI autosave, a missing notification event, and a missing payment event. Business policy clearly does not assign them equal importance. Risk-aware monitoring may use weights or rule flags, but even weighted cost is still only a prioritization aid; it cannot replace evidence review, scope decisions, or data-time semantics.

In short:

- `S(A)` means the label and ordering fit the model; it does not prove data-aware policy eligibility.
- `L(A)` means an extra logged event exists; it does not mean the event was harmful.
- `M(A)` means no matching event was found in this log; it does not prove the activity did not occur elsewhere.
- `T(tau)` means allowed hidden routing; it is fitting.
- Raw cost is useful for finding structural patterns, but business risk requires policy facts, timestamps, evidence reliability, and scope interpretation.
