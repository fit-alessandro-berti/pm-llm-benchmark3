## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

All case facts, policy language, dates, and aggregate counts below come from the supplied review record. Where that record is silent, I treat the proposition as unproved rather than silently filling the gap. 

### Stipulated facts

**M0’s semantics are clear but limited.** For the approved branch, both `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` must finish before `Manual_Procurement_Approval`, which must finish before `Activate_Supplier`. The two checks are concurrent in the model, so either observed order is acceptable. The rejected branch also follows completion of both checks. `Notify_Requester` and then `Close_Case` follow either outcome.

**CS-9 is the strongest formally controlled source.** Since January 1, 2025, governed onboarding activations require:

* sanctions and quality review before activation;
* manual procurement approval before activation; and
* different humans as requester and manual approver.

The last requirement is invisible to M0.

**PD-3 and FD-7 conflict with the standard route but are not equivalent authorities.**

* PD-3 was a recorded steering-committee pilot decision for June 1 through July 31, 2026. It permitted a narrowly eligible auto-approval route and delayed quality review. It was never incorporated into CS-9 or M0, and the Compliance observer’s statements create a genuine authority ambiguity.
* FD-7 was a written emergency directive with an exact scope, exact effective interval, amount limit, monitoring requirements, and automatic expiry.

**The evidence system is known to be incomplete.** From July 1, only about 70% of CRM notifications were mirrored into the onboarding log, and notification missingness rose from 2% to 29%. A missing onboarding event after that date is therefore weak evidence that no notification occurred.

**M0 omits established operational behavior.** Forty-seven cases contain `Request_More_Info`; none activated before the requested evidence arrived. This supports the legitimacy of an information-gathering loop, although it does not prove that every request was necessary or timely.

### Supported propositions

1. **M0 is not a complete compliance specification.** It cannot decide risk, geography, group membership, amount limits, effective dates, business-day deadlines, actor identity, conflicts of interest, or exception authority.
2. **Some observed deviations are genuine model or data defects rather than misconduct.** SUP-704, SUP-707, SUP-711, and SUP-713 are the clearest examples.
3. **The operation nevertheless contains genuine material noncompliance.** SUP-703 and SUP-708 cannot plausibly be explained by model obsolescence alone.
4. **Informal normalization did not create a permanent auto-approval policy.** PD-3 expired on July 31, 2026, and no permanent amendment existed by August 2.
5. **Trace fitness cannot establish compliance.** SUP-712 fits M0 but violates CS-9’s four-eyes rule; SUP-715 fits M0 but raises a separate disclosure-control issue.

### Disputed propositions

| ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| PD-3 validly authorized departures from CS-9                  | **Disputed**                                       | A recorded steering decision and the observer’s “monitored exception” language support operational authority; non-incorporation and the observer’s later qualification undermine the claim that CS-9 was formally weakened. |

### Burden-of-proof approach

A raw M0 deviation is a **screening signal**, not the conclusion.

To classify a case as `NONCOMPLIANT`, the reviewer should establish:

1. the case falls within the rule’s defined scope;
2. the rule was effective and authoritative at the relevant event time;
3. sufficiently reliable evidence establishes the prohibited sequence, missing control, identity conflict, or disclosure failure; and
4. no valid exception covered the case.

A balance-of-evidence standard is sufficient for ordinary remediation. A high or critical finding should require clear, internally consistent evidence because of its disciplinary and regulatory consequences.

The operation bears the affirmative burden for an exception that weakens a preventive control. It should produce:

* the contemporaneous directive or decision;
* delegated authority to issue it;
* effective dates and timezone;
* a case-time eligibility snapshot;
* evidence of every compensating control; and
* proof that the exception had not expired.

A benign outcome is relevant to severity, not to whether a preventive rule was breached. Conversely, absence of an event is not sufficient proof of nonperformance when a documented collection failure plausibly explains the absence.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The strongest prosecution case is not that every M0 deviation is wrongdoing. It is that the operation repeatedly treated temporary or narrow exceptions as discretionary alternatives to a controlled standard.

### Formal authority and expiry matter

CS-9 is controlled and formally approved. PD-3 was not incorporated into it. The Compliance observer’s initial statement that the pilot could proceed as a monitored exception does not establish that she or the steering committee had authority to amend the standard, especially given her later statement that she had not approved weakening CS-9.

Even under the most favorable interpretation of PD-3, it was narrow and temporary. It applied only to LOW-risk DE suppliers already belonging to an approved corporate group, and only from June 1 through July 31, 2026. Continued use after expiry cannot be justified by saying that operations expected permanent approval.

The aggregate pattern is concerning:

* 14 of 312 auto-approved cases are clearly ineligible;
* 9 have missing eligibility attributes;
* six more used auto-approval in the first week of August after expiry.

The 289 apparently eligible cases do not neutralize these failures. They show that the route was operated at scale, making route authorization, eligibility controls, and expiry enforcement more—not less—important. 

### High-risk cases show substantive control failure

**SUP-703** is the clearest misuse of the pilot mechanism. It was a HIGH-risk new foreign supplier, yet the operator selected “pilot.” Quality review followed activation, manual approval was bypassed, and the first purchase was EUR 180,000. The case fails PD-3’s eligibility test regardless of the broader authority dispute. “Auto_Approve” operated as an uncontrolled override rather than as a tightly bounded exception.

**SUP-708** is independently severe. A HIGH-risk supplier was activated 40 minutes before sanctions clearance, and manual procurement approval also occurred after activation. There was no applicable pilot or emergency. The fact that no transaction occurred before clearance reduced realized harm, but the supplier had nevertheless been activated without two mandatory preventive controls. A rule designed to prevent exposure is not satisfied merely because the exposure happened not to produce a transaction.

### Expired emergency and pilot practices were normalized

**SUP-705** occurred on July 13, after FD-7 expired at 23:59 on July 12. Operations may be correct that flood recovery factually continued, but operational facts do not extend a directive that expressly expired automatically. The EUR 12,000 amount and retrospective checks mitigate severity; they do not supply legal authority.

**SUP-714** similarly used the pilot route on August 2, two days after PD-3 expired. Belief that permanent approval was imminent is not an effective policy instrument. The six post-pilot cases indicate a failed expiry control rather than a one-off misunderstanding.

### M0 also understates noncompliance

A literal control-flow analysis would miss some of the operation’s most important risks:

* **SUP-712** fits M0 perfectly but violates CS-9 because requester and approver are the same human behind two aliases.
* **SUP-715** fits M0, yet no conflict-of-interest disclosure was recorded despite a relationship that required disclosure.
* M0 cannot detect SUP-703’s risk, geography, group-membership, or amount context.
* M0 cannot verify the EUR 20,000 emergency cap, daily monitoring, pilot dates, or two-business-day deadlines.

This means the answer is not to stop enforcing M0. The answer is to enforce the substantive CS-9 controls through additional rule layers.

### Desirable behavior does not amend policy retroactively

`Request_More_Info` may be beneficial, and the observed 47 cases suggest it prevented activation before missing evidence arrived. That supports revising M0. It does not establish a general principle that any behavior stakeholders later regard as desirable was authorized when performed.

Likewise, the later-approved mapping from `QA_Clearance` to `Quality_Review` may establish semantic equivalence in SUP-713, but a general practice of retroactive relabeling would be dangerous without retained raw labels, mapping versions, and audit evidence.

### Prosecution’s necessary concessions

The prosecution should not treat the raw M0 deviation count as the number of violations:

* SUP-704 appears to be a valid emergency exception.
* SUP-707 exposes a missing loop in M0.
* SUP-711 is strongly explained by duplicate event ingestion.
* SUP-713 is strongly explained by an approved semantic label mapping.
* SUP-706 is affected by known notification collection failure.
* SUP-709 and SUP-710 cannot be judged confidently until scope and reactivation rules are fixed.

These defects reduce the evidentiary value of aggregate M0 nonconformance. They do not explain SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, or SUP-714.

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest defense is that M0 is being used as though it were a complete, timeless legal specification when it is actually a narrow control-flow skeleton for one standard onboarding route.

### M0 structurally cannot represent the governing reality

M0 contains only:

* a standard sequence;
* one activation branch;
* one rejection branch; and
* notification and closure.

It lacks:

* the time-bounded pilot route;
* the emergency route;
* the established information-request loop;
* abbreviated rejection screening;
* reactivation semantics;
* actor-identity and segregation-of-duties rules;
* amount and monitoring conditions;
* label equivalence;
* CRM-based notification evidence; and
* policy version or exception authority.

Consequently, raw conformance systematically mixes at least four different phenomena: misconduct, valid exceptions, scope mismatch, and collection error.

### Concurrency should not be mistaken for order violations

M0 correctly represents sanctions and quality review as concurrent. A trace with sanctions followed by quality, or quality followed by sanctions, is not nonconforming merely because one appears first in the event list. The relevant standard-route requirement is completion of both before activation.

The real conflict is not the order between the checks. It is that PD-3 and FD-7 intentionally permit quality review after provisional activation under narrower conditions. A single unversioned model cannot fairly evaluate both the standard and exception periods.

### The pilot was predominantly operated as a defined process

Of the 312 auto-approved cases, 289—approximately 92.6%—appear eligible, while 14—approximately 4.5%—are clearly ineligible and 9—approximately 2.9%—lack eligibility attributes. That distribution supports a defense that most pilot use followed a recognizable rule rather than representing arbitrary bypassing.

PD-3 was not merely an oral custom. It was recorded in steering-committee minutes and a follow-up email, and the Compliance observer initially wrote that it could proceed as a monitored exception. That is meaningful evidence of contemporaneous operational authorization, even though it does not establish a permanent amendment.

Thus, an eligible in-window case should not be treated like SUP-703. The correct distinction is between:

* an eligible, evidenced, in-window exception;
* an eligible case with incomplete prerequisite evidence;
* an ineligible case;
* an expired exception; and
* an entirely unauthorized bypass.

### SUP-704 demonstrates why literal M0 enforcement is misleading

SUP-704 activated before quality review and manual approval, so it plainly fails M0. Yet it appears to satisfy FD-7:

* activation on July 11, within the directive window;
* critical pump supplier;
* sanctions before activation;
* initial spend of EUR 18,000;
* daily monitoring;
* quality review and approval the next business day.

Calling this case materially noncompliant would punish conduct that followed the most specific contemporaneous emergency rule.

### Notification deviations are heavily contaminated by data migration

Notification missingness rose from 2% to 29% after the CRM migration, close to what would be expected when only approximately 70% of CRM notifications are mirrored. That is strong process-level evidence that many apparent missing notifications are extraction failures.

SUP-702 has a stable `notification_id`, making its notification evidence stronger than the onboarding trace. SUP-706 has weaker linkage, so it should remain indeterminate rather than be presumed compliant. But neither should be treated as equivalent to a proven failure to notify.

### `Request_More_Info` is a model omission, not prohibited behavior

SUP-707’s two requests obtained missing certificates before approval. Across all 47 such cases, none activated before the evidence arrived. The loop appears to strengthen rather than weaken the control objective.

M0’s inability to express repetition should not turn prudent evidence gathering into misconduct. The proper response is to add the loop and collect request–response linkage, not to suppress the activity to improve conformance scores.

### Case boundaries and semantic mappings are defective

**SUP-709:** CS-9 explicitly recognizes that rejected leads may use an abbreviated pre-screen, while the model’s case boundary is disputed. Requiring a full quality review before rejecting a lead that failed sanctions screening may be wasteful and may not have been intended.

**SUP-710:** A reactivation linked to an existing supplier and still-valid certification is not obviously equivalent to a new onboarding. The lack of a reactivation procedure is a governance defect. It prevents a confident compliance finding in either direction.

**SUP-711:** Two register rows with the same source transaction ID are much more plausibly one duplicated event than two business registrations.

**SUP-713:** Equivalent audit content is more probative than the literal activity label. Formal mapping approval one week later reflects taxonomy lag, not necessarily a missing quality control.

### Defense’s necessary concessions

Model obsolescence cannot become a blanket defense:

* SUP-703 was plainly outside the pilot’s eligibility.
* SUP-708 bypassed sanctions and manual approval without any exception.
* SUP-705 and SUP-714 lack contemporaneous extensions.
* SUP-712 violates an explicit resource rule even though M0 cannot detect it.
* SUP-715 cannot be excused merely because M0 lacks conflict-of-interest semantics; at minimum, the disclosure requirement still applies.
* Eligible pilot cases still require evidence of automated master-data validation and valid exception authority.

The defense therefore defeats indiscriminate M0 enforcement, but not targeted findings based on the substantive rules.

### System-level judgment

**Operational compliance is materially deficient, but the deficiency is concentrated rather than universal.**

The strongest evidence of a systemic compliance problem is the combination of:

* 14 clearly ineligible auto approvals;
* 9 auto approvals with missing eligibility attributes;
* six post-pilot auto approvals;
* normalization of emergency-style processing after expiry;
* a severe ineligible case, SUP-703;
* a severe sanctions bypass, SUP-708; and
* a hidden four-eyes failure, SUP-712.

These are not explained by CRM missingness or a missing loop. They demonstrate inadequate route authorization, eligibility enforcement, expiry controls, identity resolution, and exception governance.

**M0 is still valid as a standard-route skeleton, but invalid as the sole normative model.**

Its core standard route remains consistent with CS-9. It should not simply be discarded. However, it is materially incomplete because it cannot represent legitimate temporary overlays, common information cycles, rejected-lead scope, reactivation, data lineage, resource identity, or contextual rules.

The proper system conclusion is therefore:

> **Enforce CS-9’s substantive preventive controls immediately, while replacing literal single-model conformance with a versioned, layered compliance architecture.**

This means disabling expired routes at runtime, reviewing the 14 ineligible, 9 unknown, and six post-pilot cases, and prioritizing SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-712, and SUP-715 for remediation. At the same time, cases should not be charged merely because they contain a legitimate request loop, a demonstrable duplicate, an equivalent label, or a notification missing from a degraded mirror.

### Where I reject the prosecution

I reject any argument that the raw M0 deviation rate measures the operation’s misconduct rate. SUP-704, SUP-707, SUP-711, and SUP-713 are compelling counterexamples. SUP-706, SUP-709, and SUP-710 also cannot sustain confident misconduct findings on the supplied evidence.

I also reject a categorical view that PD-3 had no operational authority whatsoever. The recorded steering decision and monitored-exception language make eligible in-window cases potentially justified. They do not, however, eliminate the need to prove every pilot prerequisite.

### Where I reject the defense

I reject the claim that model obsolescence or beneficial outcomes legitimize unauthorized conduct. SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, and SUP-714 remain violations under any defensible reading of the evidence. SUP-715 also remains a disclosure-control failure unless evidence of disclosure is produced.

I reject the proposition that the flood emergency’s factual continuation automatically extended FD-7, and I reject the proposition that operational expectation of permanent auto-approval extended PD-3. Exceptions require authority, not only continuing business circumstances or stakeholder preference.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

A single POWL should not be asked to encode the entire norm. The revised architecture should separate behavioral possibilities from contextual permission.

| Layer                                    | Primary artifact                                   | Content                                                                                                                                |
| ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Scope and case taxonomy**           | Controlled policy and case master data             | Defines intake lead, governed onboarding, reactivation, withdrawal, rejection, and supplier lifecycle boundaries.                      |
| **2. Control-flow envelope**             | POWL                                               | Represents standard, auto, emergency/provisional, rejection, notification, closure, concurrency, and repeatable information requests.  |
| **3. Declarative obligations**           | DECLARE or log-skeleton-style constraints          | Represents universal precedence, response, terminal-outcome, and occurrence rules that cut across POWL branches.                       |
| **4. Data-aware eligibility and timing** | Pandas checks or a rule engine                     | Evaluates risk, country, group membership, effective dates, amount caps, business-day deadlines, and policy versions.                  |
| **5. Resource and conflict controls**    | Organizational master data plus pandas checks      | Resolves aliases, checks four-eyes separation, role authority, delegation, reporting lines, and disclosure obligations.                |
| **6. Policy-overlay governance**         | Controlled policy registry and runtime route guard | Determines which route is available, who authorized it, when it expires, and what compensating controls apply.                         |
| **7. Evidence and collection integrity** | Append-only event and lineage stores               | Preserves raw labels, stable IDs, source transactions, notification linkage, effective timestamps, record timestamps, and corrections. |

### Revised control-flow model

The POWL behavioral envelope should contain four exclusive outcome routes after registration.

**Standard manual route**

* `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` are concurrent.
* Zero or more `Request_More_Info` events may interleave before manual approval.
* Both checks and the information-request stage must finish before `Manual_Procurement_Approval`.
* Manual approval precedes activation.
* Activation precedes notification.
* Closure follows completion of the route.

**Low-risk auto route**

* `Sanctions_Check`, `Automated_Master_Data_Validation`, and `Auto_Approve` must precede activation.
* Quality review may occur before or after provisional activation.
* Notification may follow activation before the delayed quality review is complete.
* Closure waits for the quality obligation.
* Risk, country, approved-group status, pilot dates, and the two-business-day deadline are external predicates.

**Emergency/provisional route**

* Sanctions clearance always precedes activation.
* Activation may precede quality review and manual approval.
* Notification may follow provisional activation.
* Closure waits for the required retrospective controls.
* Criticality, emergency authority, spend cap, daily monitoring, and deadline are external predicates.

**Rejection route**

* Sanctions pre-screen is mandatory.
* Quality review is optional only when an approved rejected-lead scope rule permits abbreviated screening.
* Information requests may repeat before rejection.
* Rejection precedes notification, and notification precedes closure.

### Declarative constraints

The cross-route constraint layer should include at least:

* `precedence(Sanctions_Check, Activate_Supplier)` for every activation route;
* `precedence(Manual_Procurement_Approval, Activate_Supplier)` when `route=STANDARD`;
* `precedence(Auto_Approve, Activate_Supplier)` when `route=AUTO`;
* `response(Activate_Supplier, Quality_Review)` for auto and emergency routes, with a two-business-day temporal condition;
* `precedence(Notify_Requester, Close_Case)`;
* exactly one terminal business outcome per case: activation, rejection, withdrawal, or cancellation;
* every `Request_More_Info` must have a corresponding evidence-receipt event before activation or rejection;
* no unresolved information request at closure.

The current log does not contain a paired evidence-receipt activity. That event and a stable `request_id` should be added before claiming automated conformance to the last two constraints.

### Data-aware pandas or rule-engine checks

For every route decision, evaluate against an immutable case-time snapshot:

* risk level at decision time;
* supplier country;
* approved corporate-group membership and its effective date;
* case type;
* activation timestamp;
* applicable rule version;
* emergency or pilot eligibility;
* automated master-data validation result;
* cumulative spend before retrospective approval;
* quality-review business-day interval;
* presence and cadence of daily monitoring;
* whether the route was selected during its effective interval; and
* whether an extension existed before the original rule expired.

Historical checks must use the values effective at the decision time, not current master-data values.

### Resource and conflict-of-interest controls

The organizational layer should enforce:

* `requester_canonical_person_id != manual_approver_canonical_person_id`;
* approver role authorization at the approval’s effective time;
* delegation records containing delegator, delegate, scope, effective dates, and authorization evidence;
* alias resolution through a versioned identity table;
* relationship and same-reporting-line indicators;
* required conflict disclosure and disposition;
* no substitution of display names or email aliases for canonical person identity.

A spouse or reporting-line relationship need not automatically prohibit approval unless policy says so. The system should require disclosure, independent review where prescribed, and an auditable disposition.

### Policy-overlay governance

Every pilot or emergency overlay should be a controlled object with:

* unique `rule_version_id`;
* issuing authority and delegated power;
* signed or approved timestamp;
* effective-from and effective-to values;
* timezone and business calendar;
* exact scope predicate;
* overridden baseline rules;
* compensating controls;
* monitoring owner;
* expiry behavior;
* extension mechanism;
* revocation timestamp; and
* superseded or successor rule.

Route selection should be blocked automatically when the overlay expires. Extensions must be issued before or at the point they become effective; continued circumstances should not silently extend a directive. A proposed permanent auto route should remain unavailable until the permanent policy’s effective version is approved and deployed.

### Explicit scope rules

**Rejected leads:** Define whether the log begins at intake or after sanctions pre-screen. A defensible architecture would retain intake as a case type but allow a controlled abbreviated rejection route, avoiding the fiction that every failed lead must undergo full quality review.

**Reactivations:** Use a distinct `case_type=REACTIVATION` and a separate model or route. It should link to the prior supplier lifecycle and record which prior controls are being relied upon. Recommended controls include current sanctions screening, certification-validity evidence, and a documented risk reassessment. These are proposed controls because CS-9 presently supplies no reactivation procedure.

**Withdrawals:** The supplied materials do not define a withdrawal activity or procedure. The policy owner should approve a separate terminal withdrawal route rather than silently treating withdrawals as rejects or incomplete cases. At minimum, the route should record who withdrew, when, whether activation had already occurred, notification, and closure.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

PM4Py represents visible and silent POWL leaves with `Transition` and `SilentTransition`, exclusive choices and two-child loops with `OperatorPOWL`, and partial-order concurrency with unordered `StrictPartialOrder` nodes. Its loop semantics execute the first child and then either exit or execute the second child followed by another first-child iteration. ([GitHub][1]) The helper below also closes each relation transitively before validation, using the relation operation provided by the current PM4Py implementation. ([GitHub][2])

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def make_po(nodes, edges):
    """
    Construct a StrictPartialOrder and explicitly add its transitive closure.
    'edges' may contain only the immediate precedence relations.
    """
    model = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=nodes)
    for source, target in edges:
        model.order.add_edge(source, target)
    model.order.add_transitive_edges()
    return model


def zero_or_more(activity_label):
    """
    POWL approximation of zero or more occurrences.

    LOOP(tau, A) first executes a silent body and may then exit, or execute A
    and repeat the silent body. Its visible language is therefore A*.
    """
    return OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.LOOP,
        children=[
            SilentTransition(),
            Transition(label=activity_label),
        ],
    )


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# STANDARD CS-9 ROUTE
#
# Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review are unordered and may run concurrently.
# Both checks and any information-request cycles must finish before manual
# approval. Manual approval precedes activation; activation precedes notice.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

std_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
std_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
std_info_loop = zero_or_more("Request_More_Info")
std_manual = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
std_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
std_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

standard_route = make_po(
    nodes=[
        std_sanctions,
        std_quality,
        std_info_loop,
        std_manual,
        std_activate,
        std_notify,
    ],
    edges=[
        (std_sanctions, std_manual),
        (std_quality, std_manual),
        (std_info_loop, std_manual),
        (std_manual, std_activate),
        (std_activate, std_notify),
    ],
)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# LOW-RISK AUTO-APPROVAL ROUTE
#
# POWL expresses the possible control flow but not the PD-3 predicates.
# Quality_Review is intentionally unordered relative to activation, allowing
# either pre-activation review or review after provisional activation.
# Closure, defined at the root, still waits for the complete route.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

auto_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
auto_master_data = Transition(label="Automated_Master_Data_Validation")
auto_info_loop = zero_or_more("Request_More_Info")
auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
auto_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
auto_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
auto_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

auto_route = make_po(
    nodes=[
        auto_sanctions,
        auto_master_data,
        auto_info_loop,
        auto_approve,
        auto_activate,
        auto_quality,
        auto_notify,
    ],
    edges=[
        # Information requests must finish before the auto decision.
        (auto_info_loop, auto_approve),

        # Pilot prerequisites and the auto decision must precede activation.
        (auto_sanctions, auto_activate),
        (auto_master_data, auto_activate),
        (auto_approve, auto_activate),

        # Notification may occur before the delayed quality review.
        (auto_activate, auto_notify),
    ],
)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# EMERGENCY / PROVISIONAL ROUTE
#
# Sanctions clearance is the universal pre-activation condition.
# Quality and manual approval are deliberately unordered relative to
# provisional activation, allowing them to be retrospective.
# All route obligations must finish before the common Close_Case.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

emg_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
emg_info_loop = zero_or_more("Request_More_Info")
emg_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
emg_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
emg_manual = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
emg_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

emergency_route = make_po(
    nodes=[
        emg_sanctions,
        emg_info_loop,
        emg_activate,
        emg_quality,
        emg_manual,
        emg_notify,
    ],
    edges=[
        (emg_sanctions, emg_activate),

        # Any missing-information cycle must finish before retrospective
        # manual approval, but may occur after provisional activation.
        (emg_info_loop, emg_manual),

        # The requester may be told about provisional activation before all
        # retrospective controls have completed.
        (emg_activate, emg_notify),
    ],
)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# REJECTION ROUTE
#
# Sanctions pre-screen is mandatory. Quality review is optional to represent
# either abbreviated rejected-lead screening or a full review. The policy/data
# layer must determine when skipping quality is actually authorized.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

rej_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
rej_info_loop = zero_or_more("Request_More_Info")

rej_optional_quality = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[
        Transition(label="Quality_Review"),
        SilentTransition(),
    ],
)

reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
rej_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

rejection_route = make_po(
    nodes=[
        rej_sanctions,
        rej_info_loop,
        rej_optional_quality,
        reject,
        rej_notify,
    ],
    edges=[
        (rej_sanctions, reject),
        (rej_info_loop, reject),
        (rej_optional_quality, reject),
        (reject, rej_notify),
    ],
)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# TOP-LEVEL MODEL
#
# The XOR is a behavioral envelope. POWL itself cannot evaluate whether the
# auto or emergency route is legally available for a particular case.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

route_choice = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[
        standard_route,
        auto_route,
        emergency_route,
        rejection_route,
    ],
)

register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

root = make_po(
    nodes=[
        register,
        route_choice,
        close,
    ],
    edges=[
        (register, route_choice),
        (route_choice, close),
    ],
)

# Fail fast if any partial order is cyclic or not transitively closed.
root.validate_partial_orders()

if __name__ == "__main__":
    print(root)
```

The code is syntactically validated. Constructor execution was not locally testable because PM4Py was not installed in the available runtime, but the class names, constructors, loop semantics, ordering methods, and validation call follow the current official source.

### Important modeling limitation

The POWL is intentionally a **permissive behavioral envelope**. It cannot determine whether a case is LOW risk, German, linked to an approved group, within an effective window, below an amount cap, or covered by an authorized directive. Because identical activity labels appear in multiple branches, ordinary trace alignment alone may also be unable to determine which policy route was intended.

The `LOOP(tau, Request_More_Info)` construction permits zero or more visible requests. It does not prove that requested evidence was received, pair individual requests and responses, or prevent activation while one request remains unresolved. Those obligations require additional events and declarative/data checks.
