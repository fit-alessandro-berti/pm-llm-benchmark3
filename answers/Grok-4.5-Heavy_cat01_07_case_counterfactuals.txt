## 1. Feasibility model

```
Register ≺ Triage
Triage ≺ Identity Check
Triage ≺ Originals Receipt          [if originals required]
Originals Receipt ≺ Document Check  [if originals required]
Identity Check ∥ Document Check     [allowed when no originals required; Identity may still precede or parallel when originals required]
{Identity Check, Document Check} ≺ Assessment
Assessment ≺ Compliance Review      [mandatory if high-risk or high-value]
Compliance Review ≺ Decision        [if Compliance required]
Assessment ≺ Decision
Decision ≺ Notify

Optional / conditional arcs:
Triage ≺ Escalation Advice / Preliminary Compliance screen
(Assessment ∨ Compliance Review) ≺ Request More Info
Request More Info ≺ new Document Check ≺ new Assessment
new Assessment ≺ (new or reaffirmed Compliance Review)  [if high-risk/value]
new Assessment ≺ Decision

Prelim screen does not eliminate the final Compliance requirement.
```

```
1. High-risk or high-value cases require Compliance Review after the latest Assessment and before Decision.
2. Four-eyes rule: resource performing final Assessment ≠ resource performing final Compliance Review.
3. Preliminary compliance or escalation work is permitted but does not replace the final Compliance Review.
4. When originals are required, Originals Receipt must complete before Document Check; Identity Check may run earlier or in parallel.
5. After Request More Info, a new Document Check and new Assessment are mandatory before Decision; high-risk/value then require new or reaffirmed Compliance.
6. No resource may execute two service intervals that overlap in time.
7. Queue estimates from snapshots are forecasts, not deterministic waits; actual waits depend on arrivals, priorities and capacity.
8. Decision may be approve, approve-with-conditions, or reject. An intervention may authorize deciding on incomplete evidence only when explicitly specified (K-337); a discrepancy may not be silently ignored.
9. Workarounds for concurrency must be accompanied by real-time timestamps reflecting actual work intervals; batch or false concurrency is non-compliant for audit.
10. Only listed resources; C1 is the sole fully certified Compliance resource in the window; S1 advisory work may temporally overlap other activities.
```

K-104 alters timing (and lightly routing) by inserting early Escalation Advice and provisional C1 engagement; final control-flow and decision policy remain identical.  
K-219 alters routing (D2 replaces D1) and thereby timing and potential rework probability; decision policy is unchanged.  
K-337 alters the allowed decision policy (suppresses the second RMI, forces Decision on existing evidence with conditions or reject) and consequently removes later activities.  
K-411 alters concurrency and timing (both verification tasks created at Triage; workaround permits true parallel Identity + Document) with required real-time logging; decision policy is unchanged.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104
Factual mechanism: At Triage the sanctions ambiguity was noticed but not escalated. Identity and complex Document Check proceeded normally. Only at Assessment (A1) was the ambiguity rediscovered, triggering Escalation Advice then a full from-scratch Compliance Review by C1 that consumed most of Wednesday afternoon. The late discovery forced C1 to read evidence cold under residual queue pressure.

Minimal intervention: Immediately after Triage, transmit a preliminary escalation packet containing the sanctions note to S1 and place a provisional reservation on C1. S1 can perform advisory review concurrent with Identity/Document work; C1 can perform a preliminary screen on whatever evidence is already available. Final Compliance Review remains mandatory after the completed Assessment and is performed by C1 (different from A1).

Temporal propagation: Identity and Document Check timings are essentially unchanged because the intervention does not alter their queues or service. Assessment therefore still completes around Tue 19:10. However, because preliminary work has already been done, the final Compliance Review can start earlier (Tue evening or Wed morning instead of Wed 13:10) and is shorter (roughly 2–4 h instead of 4.5 h). Decision and Notify shift earlier by 0.5–1.5 days. The provisional C1 reservation itself may slightly lengthen waits for other cases already in the Compliance queue.

Process-feasibility check: All control-flow prerequisites hold (both checks before Assessment, final Compliance after latest Assessment and before Decision). Four-eyes rule is satisfied (A1 ≠ C1). High-risk/high-value rule is satisfied. Resource non-overlap is preserved provided the provisional slot is managed so C1 is not double-booked. Preliminary work does not replace the final review.

Best/base/worst case: Best — packet complete enough that final Compliance saves the full 3 h and starts immediately after Assessment, Decision Tue night. Base — 1 day cycle-time reduction, decision unchanged. Worst — incomplete packet yields negligible time saving or extra questions; or C1 reservation collides with the two urgent compliance cases noted in the Mon 16:00 snapshot, delaying this or other cases.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Discovery that the preliminary packet was systematically incomplete for sanctions issues (so C1 still needs full cold read); or S1/C1 capacity already saturated such that provisional reservation cannot be honored without violating other SLAs; or Assessment uncovers material new issues requiring complete re-work of the prelim.

### K-219
Factual mechanism: Triage routed to D1 because of difficult handwriting. Originals arrived Mon 17:30, but Document Check waited for D1 availability (long D1 queue); started Tue 09:30, completed accurately Tue 13:30 (4 h). No rework. Assessment by A2 Tue evening; Decision/Notify Wed morning. Cycle from Register ~2 days. Standard (handwritten) documents, no high-risk/value, originals required.

Minimal intervention: Assign Document Check to D2 as soon as Originals Receipt completes (Mon 17:30), instead of holding for D1. No change to other routing or policy.

Temporal propagation: Mon 16:00 snapshot D2 queue ~5 h. After Originals 17:30, D2 start feasible ~Mon 22:30 or more realistically early Tue (~08:00 after overnight evolution). D2 service time for standard+handwritten is longer and more variable than D1 (6–12 h plausible). Completion therefore Tue ~14:00–20:00 range. Assessment readiness advances relative to factual Tue 13:30 Doc end. Assessment queue Tue (A2 ~6–7 h growing) still constrains; A2 may start Tue evening or early Wed. If clean path, Decision possibly advances 4–12 h. However, elevated probability that D2 requests clarification on handwriting → Request More Info (external 8–36 h) → new Document Check + Assessment, shifting Decision later by 1–3 days and re-loading Doc/Assess queues.

Process-feasibility check: Originals Receipt ≺ Document Check satisfied. Identity already complete. No high-risk → no Compliance or four-eyes required. Distinct resources. Decision after Assessment. Intervention is pure routing/timing change; control-flow preserved. Possible induced rework loop remains compliant with rules.

Best/base/worst case: Best — D2 starts late Mon/early Tue, completes in ~6–8 h with no clarification request, Assessment starts ~Tue 16–18, Decision late Tue or early Wed; cycle-time reduction 6–14 h; same approve decision and quality. Base — D2 start Tue morning, 8–10 h service, complete mid-afternoon/evening, Assessment/Decision similar to factual or 2–6 h earlier; rework probability 20–35 %. Worst — D2 slow or flags issues, RMI issued Tue, external delay + re-Doc + re-Assess pushes Decision to Thu; cycle +1–2 days; higher chance of residual quality notes or appeal; D2 queue displacement for other cases.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Historical first-pass success rate of D2 on handwritten documents ≥90 % (low rework); measured D2 queue at Mon 17:30 substantially longer than 5 h forecast; Assessment capacity so constrained that earlier Doc completion yields negligible start-time gain; or external clarification responses systematically <8 h making rework net time-neutral.

### K-337
Factual mechanism: Supplier inconsistencies triggered two full rework loops. After Document Check 2 and Assessment 2 (Fri 08:00–10:30), a second Request More Info was issued; external response delayed over weekend until Mon 09:00, requiring Document Check 3 (Mon 13–16:30), Assessment 3 (Tue 08–10:30), then Compliance, Decision next Wed. Cycle time ~9 calendar days from Register. High-risk required Compliance after the latest Assessment.

Minimal intervention: After Assessment 2 completion (Fri 10:30), prohibit the second Request More Info loop. Require proceeding to Compliance Review then Decision using evidence already available. Decision must address the remaining discrepancy explicitly (approve with conditions or reject); cannot silently ignore. No Document Check 3 or Assessment 3.

Temporal propagation: Assessment 2 ends Fri ~10:30. C1 queue around Fri (extrapolating from Thu 08:00 snapshot C1:5 h and clearing trend) estimated 3–8 h forecast. Compliance can therefore start Fri ~13:00–18:00 or early Mon if residual backlog. Service time 3–6 h (evidence partially reviewed in Ass 2). Decision/Notify follows immediately after. Relative to factual (which waited external + Doc 3 + Ass 3 into Tue), the path advances by avoiding ~2.5–3.5 calendar days of external/weekend + rework activities. Assessment queue and later activities shift earlier by that margin; no new rework demand on D1/A1.

Process-feasibility check: Prerequisites hold: Identity + (Originals → Doc 2) before Ass 2; Ass 2 (latest) before Compliance; Compliance before Decision; Decision before Notify. Four-eyes satisfied (A1 performed Assessments, C1 Compliance). High-risk rule met. Resource exclusivity: C1 and M1 single-tasked. Intervention explicitly authorizes skipping further RMI and deciding on incomplete evidence, so policy change is allowed; remaining discrepancy is not ignored but handled in Decision.

Best/base/worst case: Best — residual inconsistency judged minor/non-material; Compliance completes Fri afternoon; approve-with-conditions Fri evening/Mon morning; cycle-time reduction 3.5–4.5 days; low appeal. Base — Compliance Fri/Mon; Decision Mon with conditions; reduction ~3–4 days; modest increase in conditions/appeal risk. Worst — C1 backlog higher than forecast or needs extra scrutiny of unresolved item (duration 6+ h into Mon/Tue); Decision rejects or imposes heavy conditions raising loss/appeal exposure; net cycle still shorter but quality/compliance defensibility degraded.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Discovery that the remaining inconsistency was material (e.g., valuation error exceeding thresholds making conditional approval non-compliant); C1 queue spike on Friday from other high-risk cases; policy interpretation forbidding any Decision on unresolved supplier totals for high-value cases; or external response arriving spontaneously earlier than assumed, making the second loop low-cost.

### K-411
Factual mechanism: Legacy system at Triage created only the Identity task; case lock prevented concurrent Document task ownership. Identity (I2 Tue 11:30–13:20) completed first; Document task then created and executed by D2 (15:00–19:30). Assessment therefore started only Wed 09:30 after both ready and after Assessment queue growth. No high-risk, standard digital documents, concurrent allowed in principle.

Minimal intervention: At Triage (Tue 09:35), explicitly create both Identity and Document verification tasks. Apply controlled workaround (documented) so I2 and D2 can acquire ownership and work concurrently. Enforce real-time timestamps (no later batch entry).

Temporal propagation: After Triage Tue 09:35, both tasks enter queues. Tue 08:00 snapshot: Identity I2 ~2 h, Document D2 ~4 h. Thus Identity starts ~Tue 11:30 (same), finishes ~13:00–15:00. Document starts earlier than factual (~Tue 13:30–14:00 after its queue) instead of 15:00, duration 4–8 h for standard, finishes ~Tue 17:30–22:00. Both complete by late Tue evening (vs factual Doc end 19:30). Assessment readiness advances by ~0–4 h. However Assessment queue (Tue 08 A2:6 h, noted growing) means Assessment start may still be Wed morning or only modestly earlier. Decision/Notify shift by the net readiness gain after queue wait. Simultaneous I2+D2 load is local and short.

Process-feasibility check: No originals required → concurrent Identity ∥ Document fully allowed before Assessment. Assessment after both complete. No Compliance needed. Resources: I2 and D2 distinct, no self-overlap. Workaround + real-time timestamps satisfy logging integrity; false concurrency avoided by requirement. Queue forecasts uncertain, so earlier readiness does not guarantee earlier Assessment start if congestion absorbs the gain.

Best/base/worst case: Best — Document queue clears faster, both finish mid-Tue afternoon; Assessment finds a slot Tue evening; Decision Wed morning; cycle reduction 4–8 h; no quality loss. Base — Document finishes late Tue; Assessment still Wed ~08–10 due to queue; modest 2–4 h overall save. Worst — Workaround logging incomplete creating audit issues; or Assessment congestion fully absorbs earlier readiness (zero net cycle gain); simultaneous verification load slightly delays other cases in I/D pools; or higher concurrent arrivals push Assessment further.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Snapshot or log showing Document queue was actually longer than 4 h on Tue; Assessment queue grew faster than observed; workaround technically impossible without violating case-lock integrity; or real-time timestamps reveal the factual sequential path was already near-optimal due to resource availability.

Applying any single intervention to 100 similar cases in one week produces interference far beyond local effects because resource pools are small and C1 is a singleton. For K-104-style early escalation + provisional C1 reservation: each case that reserves a C1 slot reduces available Compliance capacity for others. Snapshots show C1 already at 16–20 h early week; 100 reservations would create severe starvation. Other high-risk cases experience multi-day delays, bottleneck migration from Assessment to Compliance, and possible priority inversion. Preliminary work by S1 is overlapping-ok but still consumes advisory attention. Net system cycle time may worsen for the portfolio even if individual targets improve; incomplete packets waste reserved slots (nonlinearity). D1/D2 substitution (K-219 style): shifting 100 handwritten/standard cases to D2 frees D1 for complex corporate work, potentially shrinking D1 queues. However D2 is slower and has higher clarification probability on handwriting. Aggregate rework could generate 20–40 extra Document + Assessment loops, increasing load on both Doc and Assessment pools and offsetting gains. Weekend external responses for rework amplify variance. Bottleneck migrates toward D2 and Assessment. Suppressing second rework (K-337 style) on 100 high-risk cases frees substantial D1 and A1 capacity by eliminating late loops, improving throughput. Yet forcing decisions on incomplete evidence raises the rate of conditional approvals, rejects, and subsequent appeals or loss events. These appear outside the immediate process window but consume future Compliance/Assessment capacity and create compliance defensibility risk. External/weekend effects are reduced (positive), but quality variance increases nonlinearly with the severity of unresolved discrepancies. Parallelization (K-411 style): enabling concurrent Identity + Document for 100 cases raises instantaneous utilization of I and D pools without changing total work. Earlier readiness of many cases can surge the Assessment queue (already elevated mid-week), converting a verification bottleneck into an Assessment bottleneck. Real-time timestamp discipline prevents false concurrency claims, but any logging workaround failure creates audit exposure across the cohort. System-wide effect is modestly positive if Assessment capacity absorbs the earlier arrivals; otherwise gains are absorbed and non-target Assessment cases suffer. Cross-cutting: Assessment-queue bottlenecks appear in three of four interventions when readiness advances. Weekend and external-response effects matter most for rework-related policies. Timestamp behavior versus true concurrency is critical for audit validity under parallelization. Overall, scarce C1 and Assessment capacity are the primary interference channels; local cycle-time wins frequently displace congestion or risk onto non-target cases or later periods.

## 7. Final synthesis

K-411 emerges as the most robust intervention. It changes only concurrency and timing within already-permitted control-flow, leaves decision policy and quality requirements untouched, is highly reversible, and yields clean learning about true parallel ownership and Assessment-queue sensitivity. Local cycle-time gains are modest and often partially absorbed by downstream congestion, yet the absence of quality or compliance side-effects makes the intervention safe to scale once logging integrity is proven.

K-337 is the most dangerous despite its large potential speed-up. Suppressing the second rework loop explicitly alters the allowed decision policy: the process must now decide on incomplete evidence. Even when the residual discrepancy is judged minor, the shift from pure approve to conditional approve or reject raises appeal, loss-exposure and defensibility risks that materialize outside the original process window and are difficult to reverse. The local counterfactual therefore cannot be transported to a system-wide policy without accepting a material change in risk appetite.

The local case counterfactual is least transportable for K-104. A single provisional C1 reservation looks harmless and can shorten final Compliance, but the same policy applied to dozens of high-risk cases collides with the singleton nature of C1. Early-week snapshots already show 16–20 h Compliance queues; widespread reservation produces starvation, priority inversion and bottleneck migration that no single-case analysis can reveal. The interference is structural, not merely quantitative.

The most valuable additional data are therefore: (1) high-resolution, resource-level queue traces and actual start-time deviations from forecast for C1, A1/A2 and D1/D2; (2) first-pass success rates and clarification reasons by document type and resource (especially handwritten vs digital for D2); (3) precise timestamps of concurrent ownership attempts and any workaround flags; and (4) later appeal or reopen events linked to residual discrepancies. These observables would allow calibration of both the local temporal propagations and the nonlinear system-wide interference that currently limit confident policy transfer.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. Queue snapshots remain approximately valid forecasts for the single-case counterfactuals; actual waits deviate only modestly from the tabulated values. (temporal)  
2. Service-time ranges given in the guidance are representative of the resources’ true performance distributions under the observed case attributes. (temporal)  
3. C1 is the sole fully certified Compliance resource; no substitute reviewer can perform final Compliance Review in the relevant window. (resource)  
4. Preliminary escalation or compliance work does not legally or procedurally replace the mandatory final Compliance Review after the latest Assessment. (structural)  
5. Resources never execute overlapping service intervals for different cases; any apparent concurrency is either distinct resources or advisory overlap permitted for S1. (resource)  
6. Decision-makers will explicitly address residual discrepancies (conditions or reject) rather than silently ignore them when the second RMI is prohibited. (behavioral)  
7. First-pass Document Check quality and subsequent clarification probability differ materially between D1 and D2 on handwritten documents, elevating rework risk under the K-219 intervention. (quality)  
8. Scaling any intervention to 100 similar cases in one week produces non-linear interference through shared pools (especially C1 and Assessment) that cannot be obtained by simple multiplication of single-case effects. (interference)
