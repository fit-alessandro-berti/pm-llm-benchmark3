# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

**Assumption A1 — time origin.** Time starts at `Submit`. The SLA horizon is τ = 72 hours. For terminal rows, `duration_or_age_h` is the elapsed time from `Submit` to the observed terminal outcome. For `open` rows, it is age at the observation cutoff.

**Assumption A2 — no fabricated activity timestamps.** The prefixes give activity order, not activity times. Therefore `Escalate`, `Review`, `Rework`, and `AutoCheck` can support control-flow features, but not transition-time hazards unless exact event timestamps are later supplied.

Let `T` be time to the first observed internal terminal disposition, with cause `J ∈ {Resolve, Withdraw, Reject, Transfer}`. Open cases are right-censored at their observed age. `Transfer` is internally terminal but externally unresolved; it must be handled by sensitivity analysis.

Useful SLA estimands:

| Estimand                               | Definition                                                                                               | Best operational match                                                                            |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Resolution-by-SLA incidence**        | `F_resolve(72) = P(T ≤ 72, J = Resolve)`                                                                 | “What share of submitted cases are successfully resolved within the SLA?”                         |
| **No-resolution-by-SLA risk**          | `1 − F_resolve(72)`, under a chosen treatment of withdrawal, rejection, and transfer                     | Customer-facing SLA governance when success means `Resolve`, not merely closing the file.         |
| **Late-resolution incidence**          | `P(72 < T ≤ t*, J = Resolve) = F_resolve(t*) − F_resolve(72)` for a pre-specified follow-up horizon `t*` | Tail-remediation: cases that eventually resolve but only after breaching the 72-hour target.      |
| **No-terminal-disposition-at-72 risk** | `P(T > 72)` where any internal terminal status counts as terminal                                        | Workload and backlog management: “How many cases are still internally alive at the SLA boundary?” |
| **Cause-specific competing risks**     | `F_withdraw(72)`, `F_reject(72)`, `F_transfer(72)`, and corresponding hazards                            | Separating true resolution delay from withdrawal, rejection, and external transfer behavior.      |

The phrase “probability of breaching the SLA” is therefore not unique. A case can be “not resolved by 72” but also not “active at 72” if it was withdrawn, rejected, or transferred before 72.

| Example case                | Resolution/no-resolution-by-72 estimand                                                                                 | Late-resolution estimand                                                                                                     | No-terminal-disposition-at-72 estimand                                                                                                                | Competing-risk interpretation                                                                            |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **S03**, resolved at 76h    | Already known **not resolved by 72**; it breaches the resolution SLA.                                                   | Counts as a **late Resolve event** at 76h.                                                                                   | Counts as **still without terminal disposition at 72**, because terminal resolution occurs at 76h.                                                    | Cause `Resolve` at 76h; not censored.                                                                    |
| **S09**, open at 92h        | Already known **not resolved by 72**.                                                                                   | No late Resolve observed; right-censored at 92h for eventual resolution.                                                     | Counts as **still without terminal disposition at 72**.                                                                                               | Censored at 92h; `last_event_gap_h = 31` makes non-informative censoring questionable.                   |
| **S06**, withdrawn at 27h   | Under success-specific SLA, it is **not resolved by 72**; under an “any valid disposition” SLA, it closed before 72.    | Does not count as late resolution; withdrawal precludes observed internal Resolve.                                           | Does **not** count as active at 72, because it terminated at 27h.                                                                                     | Competing event `Withdraw` at 27h, not ordinary censoring.                                               |
| **S08**, transferred at 68h | Under internal-resolution SLA, no internal Resolve by 72. Under external-outcome SLA, status after transfer is unknown. | No observed late Resolve. Depending on assumption, transfer is a competing event, censoring time, or adverse non-resolution. | If `Transfer` is an internal terminal disposition, it is **not active at 72**. If the external process is in scope, activity after 68h is unobserved. | Transfer at 68h requires sensitivity analysis; treating it as ordinary censoring is a strong assumption. |

# 2. Event and censoring encoding

A clean design should keep separate encodings for different estimands rather than forcing all statuses into one binary variable.

| Observed status |                                        Event indicator(s) | Competing-risk code for first internal terminal disposition | Censoring rule                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Sensitivity alternatives                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------: | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `resolved`      |                     `E_resolve = 1`; `E_any_terminal = 1` | `1 = Resolve` at `duration_or_age_h`                        | Not censored. If duration ≤ 72, resolved by SLA; if duration > 72, observed late resolution and observed resolution-SLA breach.                                                                                                      | Can split `Resolve ≤ 72` and `Resolve > 72` for descriptive tail reporting, but survival encoding should keep actual event time.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| `withdrawn`     |                     `E_resolve = 0`; `E_any_terminal = 1` | `2 = Withdraw` at `duration_or_age_h`                       | Not censored under competing-risk analysis.                                                                                                                                                                                          | If the business says customer withdrawal removes the SLA obligation, treat it as a competing terminal closure. If success-specific service quality is the estimand, count it as non-resolution. Do not silently drop it.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| `rejected`      |                     `E_resolve = 0`; `E_any_terminal = 1` | `3 = Reject` at `duration_or_age_h`                         | Not censored under competing-risk analysis.                                                                                                                                                                                          | If rejection is a valid timely disposition, include it in “any terminal by 72.” If the SLA promises successful resolution, it remains a non-resolution competing event.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `transferred`   | Default: `E_resolve = 0`; `E_any_terminal = 1` internally | Default: `4 = Transfer` at `duration_or_age_h`              | Default: not censored for internal process analysis; terminal handoff observed.                                                                                                                                                      | **Transfer assumption 1 — competing terminal handoff:** treat as its own competing event. Good for internal case-management performance. **Transfer assumption 2 — right-censor at transfer:** valid only if post-transfer resolution would be independent of transfer conditional on covariates; doubtful here because transfers are P1/escalated-heavy. **Transfer assumption 3 — adverse or unresolved outcome:** count as non-resolution/breach if the organization remains accountable for external outcome. **Transfer assumption 4 — linked external follow-up:** replace transfer with external terminal outcome if later data become available. |
| `open`          |           `E_resolve = 0`; `E_any_terminal = 0` at cutoff | `0 = censored` at observed age                              | Right-censored for final terminal outcome. However, if age ≥ 72, the case is already known not to have resolved by 72 and already known to have no internal terminal disposition by 72. If age < 72, the horizon outcome is unknown. | Administrative censoring is plausible only if cutoff is unrelated to future resolution risk. Long gaps, such as S26 with age 104h and gap 72h, suggest possible informative censoring or stalled work. Use IPCW, joint models, or best/worst-case sensitivity if censoring is not ignorable.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |

The key distinction is:

`open` at 35h, such as S15, is right-censored before the SLA boundary and cannot be labeled as a breach.

`open` at 92h, such as S09, is right-censored for eventual outcome but already known to have missed the 72-hour resolution SLA.

`withdrawn` or `rejected` is not right censoring; it is an observed competing terminal event.

`transferred` is observed internally but unresolved externally, so its treatment depends on the decision boundary.

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

Analyzing only completed resolved cases would use the 13 `resolved` rows and would observe 4 late resolutions: S03 at 76h, S13 at 110h, S20 at 74h, and S28 at 88h. That describes late resolution **among observed resolved cases**, not the probability that a submitted case breaches the SLA.

It creates several biases.

| Bias source                       | Concrete evidence from the extract                                                                                                                                                                   | Why it matters                                                                                                                                                                              |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Survivor / completion bias**    | Open cases S09, S11, S25, and S26 are already older than 72h.                                                                                                                                        | Dropping them excludes cases that have already failed the resolution-by-72 target but have not yet reached a terminal outcome.                                                              |
| **Open cases below vs above 72h** | Below 72h: S10 at 47h, S15 at 35h, S24 at 67h, S29 at 51h, S31 at 23h. Above 72h: S09 at 92h, S11 at 81h, S25 at 79h, S26 at 104h.                                                                   | Below-72 open cases are not yet classifiable for the SLA horizon. Above-72 open cases are already observed SLA misses for resolution-by-72 but remain censored for final outcome.           |
| **Informative censoring risk**    | S11 has age 81h and last-event gap 55h; S25 has age 79h and gap 48h; S26 has age 104h and gap 72h.                                                                                                   | These do not look like random administrative censoring. Long inactivity may signal stalled work, missing updates, or lower chance of near-term resolution.                                  |
| **Competing risks**               | S05 and S23 are rejected early; S06, S12, S22, and S30 are withdrawn; S08, S21, and S32 are transferred.                                                                                             | Treating these as ordinary non-informative censoring would estimate a hypothetical world in which they could still resolve internally like similar active cases. That may be inappropriate. |
| **Cohort imbalance**              | Cohort A has 15 cases; cohort B has 17. B has 5 P1 cases versus A’s 3. AutoCheck appears in 2/15 A cases but 13/17 B cases, showing incomplete rollout/compliance rather than randomized assignment. | A crude A/B comparison mixes policy period, priority mix, actual AutoCheck exposure, escalation, and transfer behavior.                                                                     |
| **Transfer informativeness**      | All transferred cases are P1 and escalated: S08, S21, S32.                                                                                                                                           | If transfers are censored, censoring is concentrated in high-priority escalated cases, violating a simple independent-censoring story.                                                      |

A completed-resolved-only analysis can bias the tail risk downward by omitting unresolved old open cases. It can also bias comparisons unpredictably because withdrawn, rejected, and transferred cases are not missing at random; they are part of the process outcome structure.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

The primary analysis should be estimand-first.

For a **customer-facing successful-resolution SLA**, the most defensible primary estimand is the cumulative incidence of `Resolve` by 72 hours, with withdrawal, rejection, and transfer treated as competing outcomes under a stated transfer assumption. The primary report would include `F_resolve(72)` and its complement, plus the cumulative incidence of late resolution after 72.

For **workload management**, the better primary estimand is survival without any terminal disposition at 72: `P(T_any_terminal > 72)`. This asks how many cases remain internally alive at the SLA boundary, regardless of whether unresolved terminal outcomes are good or bad.

Method comparison for these data:

| Method                                                | Usefulness here                                                                                                                                       | Main caution                                                                                                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Kaplan-Meier for time to Resolve**                  | Only acceptable as a sensitivity analysis if non-Resolve terminal outcomes are explicitly treated as independent censoring.                           | Usually inappropriate here because withdrawal, rejection, and transfer are competing events, not random censoring.                                 |
| **Kaplan-Meier for time to any terminal disposition** | Good for `P(no terminal disposition by 72)`. Open rows remain censored; terminal rows are events.                                                     | This does not answer “successful Resolve by 72.” It treats rejection, withdrawal, transfer, and resolution all as terminal closure.                |
| **Aalen-Johansen / cumulative incidence**             | Best primary nonparametric estimator for `F_resolve(72)` and cause-specific cumulative incidences with competing terminal outcomes.                   | Needs a clear rule for transfer. With only 32 cases, uncertainty intervals would be wide.                                                          |
| **Cause-specific hazards**                            | Useful for modeling instantaneous transition risk to `Resolve`, `Withdraw`, `Reject`, or `Transfer`, especially with time-varying process covariates. | Cause-specific hazard coefficients do not directly equal absolute breach probabilities; they need conversion to cumulative incidence.              |
| **Subdistribution hazards / Fine-Gray**               | Useful if the decision target is direct modeling of cumulative incidence of resolution or non-resolution by 72.                                       | Interpretation is predictive/associational, not causal. Small sample size makes fitted covariate models unstable.                                  |
| **Restricted mean time unresolved or active**         | Useful operational summary: expected hours unresolved or active up to 72h or a longer fixed horizon.                                                  | Requires handling competing events consistently with the chosen business definition.                                                               |
| **Landmarking**                                       | Strong design for alerts at 24h, 48h, 60h, or after a process stage. Only information observed by the landmark is used.                               | Requires exact event timestamps to know which activities happened before each landmark. Prefix order alone is insufficient for time landmarks.     |
| **Multi-state model**                                 | Best process-aware structure: estimate transitions such as `Review → Rework`, `Review → Escalate`, `Escalate → Transfer`, `Escalate → Resolve`.       | With current data, only transition order is observed, not transition times. We can build the state graph but not reliable transition-time hazards. |

Recommended design:

Primary: **Aalen-Johansen cumulative-incidence analysis** for first terminal outcome from `Submit`, with causes `Resolve`, `Withdraw`, `Reject`, and `Transfer`. Report `F_resolve(72)`, `1 − F_resolve(72)`, `F_resolve(t*) − F_resolve(72)`, and cause-specific cumulative incidences. Use transfer-as-competing-terminal as the default internal-system analysis, then run transfer sensitivity.

Secondary: **Kaplan-Meier or restricted mean time for any terminal disposition**, because operations also need to know how much work remains active at 72h. This should be reported separately from successful resolution.

Exploratory: **stratify by priority and cohort**, but avoid strong causal claims. For cohort B, the policy change was not randomized and compliance was incomplete. Actual `AutoCheck` and `Escalate` exposure should be treated as process-time variables, not clean treatment assignment.

# 5. Process-aware features and stage hazards

The prefixes should be used in a time-aware way. Some fields are safe baseline covariates; others are post-start process history and can leak future information.

| Feature                          | How to derive from supplied data                                         | Type                                | Use                                                          | Leakage / bias risk                                                                                |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `priority`                       | Given at case start                                                      | Baseline                            | Stratification, calibration, baseline risk                   | Priority assignment mechanism may itself reflect case complexity.                                  |
| `cohort`                         | A vs B policy period                                                     | Baseline period indicator           | Intention-to-treat-style comparison of periods               | Not randomized; confounded by time, workload, compliance, and case mix.                            |
| Observed `AutoCheck` occurrence  | Prefix contains `AutoCheck`                                              | Time-varying process exposure       | Prediction after AutoCheck has occurred; compliance analysis | Leakage if used at `Submit`, because some cases only reveal AutoCheck later.                       |
| Current stage                    | Last observed nonterminal activity for open cases or stage at a landmark | Time-varying                        | Alerting and state-specific hazards                          | Must be measured only at the prediction time.                                                      |
| Number of `Review` visits so far | Count `Review` activities up to a landmark                               | Time-varying                        | Repeated review may indicate complexity or delay             | Full-prefix count leaks future reviews if used at baseline.                                        |
| `Rework` seen so far             | Prefix contains `Rework` before prediction time                          | Time-varying / post-treatment       | Stage hazard for rework loops and late resolution            | Full `rework_count` is post-outcome summary if used at case start.                                 |
| `Escalate` seen so far           | Prefix contains `Escalate` before prediction time                        | Time-varying / mediator             | Escalation-state hazards and transfer risk                   | The supplied `escalated` field is leakage-prone as a baseline predictor.                           |
| Path variant / prefix pattern    | Sequence such as `Submit>Screen>Review`                                  | Time-varying control-flow history   | Variant-level tail risk and conformance                      | Full terminal prefix contains outcome activity and must not be used for prediction before outcome. |
| `last_event_gap_h`               | Given for each row; meaningful for open rows as inactivity at cutoff     | Time-varying current-status feature | Stalled-case alerts; informative-censoring diagnostics       | It is measured at cutoff or terminal time, not necessarily available earlier.                      |
| Elapsed age                      | `duration_or_age_h` for open rows; current age in production             | Time-varying                        | Residual SLA risk                                            | For terminal rows, total duration is an outcome and cannot be used as a baseline predictor.        |

Observed transition skeleton from the supplied prefixes:

Submit → {Screen, AutoCheck}

Screen → {AutoCheck, Review, Escalate, Resolve, Reject, Withdraw}

AutoCheck → {Screen, Review, Escalate, Resolve}

Review → {Resolve, Reject, Withdraw, Rework, Escalate}

Rework → {Review, Resolve}

Escalate → {Review, Resolve, Transfer}

Resolve, Reject, Withdraw, Transfer → terminal states

A multi-state representation could use states such as `Submitted`, `Screened`, `AutoChecked`, `InReview`, `Escalated`, `Rework`, `Resolved`, `Rejected`, `Withdrawn`, and `Transferred`. With event timestamps, one could estimate transition intensities and state-occupancy probabilities at 72h. With only prefixes, one can estimate control-flow variants and transition frequencies but not transition-time hazards.

The immortal-time problem is central. A case must survive long enough and follow a path that allows `Escalate` or `Rework` to occur. Treating `escalated = yes` or `rework_count = 1` as if it were known at `Submit` assigns earlier event-free time to a future-defined group and can make escalation or rework look artificially protective or harmful. The safe alternatives are counting-process covariates, landmark models, or state-based models.

A simple observed horizon classification under the success-specific internal-resolution definition would label 9 cases as resolved by 72h, 18 as known not resolved by 72h, and 5 as censored before 72h if transfers are treated as competing or adverse. If transfers before 72h are instead censored, the “known not resolved by 72h” count falls to 16 and the censored-before-72 count rises to 7. These are not final model estimates, but they show why transfer treatment matters.

# 7. Operational interpretation

Survival outputs should become operational tools, not just retrospective scores.

For active cases, the useful alert quantity is conditional and history-aware: probability of no `Resolve` by 72h given the case’s current age, priority, cohort/policy period, current stage, observed prefix so far, review/rework history so far, escalation state so far, and current inactivity gap. The model should expose components: risk of late resolution, risk of transfer, risk of rejection/withdrawal, and probability of still being active at 72h.

Operational actions can be linked to the estimand:

| Output                                             | Example action                                                                                                               |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| High probability of no `Resolve` by 72h            | Expedite review, assign senior handler, or trigger supervisor check.                                                         |
| High probability of no terminal disposition by 72h | Add capacity, rebalance queue, or prioritize aging work-in-progress.                                                         |
| High transfer cumulative incidence                 | Audit transfer criteria, follow external outcomes, and decide whether transfer satisfies or violates governance obligations. |
| High risk after repeated `Review` or `Rework`      | Create specialist queue or require earlier escalation.                                                                       |
| Long inactivity gap in an open case                | Generate a stalled-case alert independent of the final survival model.                                                       |

SLA governance should report multiple curves or tables, not one ambiguous breach rate. Recommended dashboard components are:

Resolution by 72h.

No resolution by 72h.

Still active at 72h.

Late resolution after 72h.

Withdraw/reject/transfer cumulative incidence.

Open cases below 72h versus open cases already above 72h.

Do not present cohort or AutoCheck differences as causal effects. Cohort B is a policy-period indicator with incomplete compliance and non-random assignment. Actual `AutoCheck`, `Escalate`, and `Rework` are process events and may be mediators, responses to case complexity, or leakage if used incorrectly.

Calibration checks should compare predicted and observed 72h outcomes within risk bands, using censoring-aware methods when cases are open below 72h. Calibration and error rates should be checked separately by priority and cohort. Fairness checks should ask whether P1, P2, and P3 cases receive comparable calibration, whether false negative alert rates differ by priority, and whether improvements in one cohort are achieved by shifting cases into transfer, withdrawal, or rejection rather than resolving them.

# Cause codes for first internal terminal disposition:
# 0 censored/open, 1 Resolve, 2 Withdraw, 3 Reject, 4 Transfer competing, 5 Transfer adverse.
def encode_events(data, transfer_scenario):
    if transfer_scenario not in {"competing", "censor", "adverse"}:
        raise ValueError("transfer_scenario must be competing, censor, or adverse")

    encoded = data.copy()
    causes = []
    for status in encoded["observed_status"]:
        if status == "open":
            causes.append(0)
        elif status == "resolved":
            causes.append(1)
        elif status == "withdrawn":
            causes.append(2)
        elif status == "rejected":
            causes.append(3)
        elif status == "transferred":
            if transfer_scenario == "competing":
                causes.append(4)
            elif transfer_scenario == "censor":
                causes.append(0)
            elif transfer_scenario == "adverse":
                causes.append(5)
        else:
            raise ValueError(f"Unknown status: {status}")

    encoded["time"] = encoded["duration_or_age_h"]
    encoded["cause"] = causes
    encoded["event_any_terminal"] = (encoded["cause"] != 0).astype(int)
    encoded["event_resolve"] = (encoded["cause"] == 1).astype(int)
    return encoded

def classify_resolution_horizon(row, transfer_scenario):
    status = row["observed_status"]
    t = row["duration_or_age_h"]

    if status == "resolved":
        return "resolved_by_72" if t <= SLA else "known_not_resolved_by_72"

    if status in {"withdrawn", "rejected"}:
        return "known_not_resolved_by_72"

    if status == "open":
        return "known_not_resolved_by_72" if t >= SLA else "censored_before_72"

    if status == "transferred":
        if transfer_scenario == "censor" and t < SLA:
            return "censored_before_72"
        return "known_not_resolved_by_72"

    raise ValueError(f"Unknown status: {status}")

# Transparent Kaplan-Meier fallback for survival without any terminal disposition.
# No survival package is assumed. If using a library, lifelines.KaplanMeierFitter
# could replace this for ordinary KM; competing-risk CIFs require an Aalen-Johansen
# implementation such as scikit-survival or another validated survival package.
def km_survival_at(data, horizon):
    x = data[["time", "event_any_terminal"]].copy()
    event_times = sorted(x.loc[(x["event_any_terminal"] == 1) & (x["time"] <= horizon), "time"].unique())
    survival = 1.0
    for t in event_times:
        n_at_risk = int((x["time"] >= t).sum())
        d_events = int(((x["time"] == t) & (x["event_any_terminal"] == 1)).sum())
        if n_at_risk > 0:
            survival *= (1.0 - d_events / n_at_risk)
    return survival

# Simple Aalen-Johansen cumulative-incidence fallback for one cause.
def aj_cif_at(data, cause_code, horizon):
    x = data[["time", "cause"]].copy()
    event_times = sorted(x.loc[(x["cause"] != 0) & (x["time"] <= horizon), "time"].unique())
    survival_before_t = 1.0
    cif = 0.0
    for t in event_times:
        n_at_risk = int((x["time"] >= t).sum())
        d_all = int(((x["time"] == t) & (x["cause"] != 0)).sum())
        d_cause = int(((x["time"] == t) & (x["cause"] == cause_code)).sum())
        if n_at_risk > 0:
            cif += survival_before_t * d_cause / n_at_risk
            survival_before_t *= (1.0 - d_all / n_at_risk)
    return cif

# 9. Limitations and additional data

The strongest limitation is that the extract provides full prefixes but not timestamps for each activity. For process-aware survival, exact timestamps are needed for `Submit`, `Screen`, `AutoCheck`, `Review`, `Escalate`, `Rework`, `Resolve`, `Withdraw`, `Reject`, and `Transfer`. Without them, one can analyze case-level terminal times and control-flow variants, but not stage-specific transition hazards or proper time-varying covariates.

Highest-priority additional data:

| Missing data                                         | Why it matters                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Exact event timestamps for every activity            | Required for landmarking, time-varying escalation/rework features, transition hazards, and avoiding immortal-time bias.                                                               |
| External outcomes after `Transfer`                   | Needed to decide whether transfer is timely success, unresolved breach, censoring, or a separate accountable outcome.                                                                 |
| Observation cutoff date and case start dates         | Needed to distinguish administrative censoring from period effects, weekday effects, workload, and policy timing.                                                                     |
| Policy rollout compliance                            | Cohort B is not enough; actual eligibility, assignment, override, and compliance data are needed to evaluate automated screening and escalation policy.                               |
| Assignment mechanism and workload                    | Queue length, staff availability, reviewer assignment, and priority-routing rules may confound SLA tail risk.                                                                         |
| Time-varying workload and capacity                   | Tail risk may reflect congestion rather than process path alone.                                                                                                                      |
| Reason codes for withdrawal, rejection, and transfer | Needed to decide whether these are valid dispositions, customer-driven exits, quality failures, or process failures.                                                                  |
| Priority definition and changes over time            | Priority may be baseline severity, a triage result, or updated during the case.                                                                                                       |
| Case complexity and customer attributes              | Needed for fair stratification and to avoid attributing complex-case delays to cohort or process stage.                                                                               |
| Post-cutoff follow-up for open cases                 | Needed to reduce censoring uncertainty and validate whether long event gaps predict stalled outcomes.                                                                                 |
| SLA business rule                                    | Must state whether the SLA is “successful Resolve within 72h,” “any terminal disposition within 72h,” “internal handoff within 72h,” or “known final external resolution within 72h.” |

The present extract is sufficient for a defensible design, careful encodings, and descriptive sensitivity analysis. It is not sufficient for strong causal claims about cohort B’s policy change or for leakage-free stage-hazard modeling without additional timestamps.
