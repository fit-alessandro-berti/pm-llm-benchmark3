# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

## Material assumptions

1. A **case** is identified by `case:concept:name`; events have at least:
   - `concept:name`
   - `time:timestamp`
   - `org:resource`
2. Event timestamps are sufficiently ordered to evaluate sequence-based constraints. Equal timestamps require a deterministic tie-breaker, such as ingestion order.
3. “Accepted case” means a case containing `Activate Vendor`; “rejected case” means a case containing `Reject Vendor`.
4. The normal onboarding backbone requires:
   - all three preliminary checks before `Classify Risk`;
   - one of rejection, low-risk approval, or high-risk approval after classification;
   - activation only after the applicable approval;
   - first payment only after activation;
   - closure after rejection or first payment.
5. `Request Clarification` / `Receive Clarification` may repeat. The intended semantics are one outstanding clarification at a time: each request must be answered before a subsequent request or before a terminal outcome.
6. `Sanctions Escalation` is optional, but if it occurs it requires an `Obtain Legal Opinion`.
7. The business model contains no logged “emergency activation” flag and no logged evidence of joint external authorization. Therefore, activity-only conformance cannot prove that R6 has been met.
8. `Change Bank Details` is treated as a post-activation amendment. It may occur after initial onboarding closure only if the operational event stream remains associated with the same vendor/case. In a production design, vendor lifecycle management would preferably be modeled as a related case rather than appended to a closed onboarding trace.
9. Temporal-profile deviations are compliance warnings, not automatic rejections, because the stated distributions are expectations rather than hard deadlines.
10. PM4Py DECLARE support/confidence metadata are model metadata. They are not evidence that a normative requirement is optional.

## R5/R6 conflict

R5 and R6 cannot both be enforced as unconditional activity-only rules:

- **R5:** after `Sanctions Escalation`, `Obtain Legal Opinion` must precede `Activate Vendor` under all circumstances.
- **R6:** an emergency activation may precede the opinion if external joint authorization exists, with the opinion within 24 hours.

Two defensible resolutions are:

### Resolution A — Legal-first strict policy

Treat R5 as authoritative. `Activate Vendor` after `Sanctions Escalation` is prohibited until `Obtain Legal Opinion` has occurred.

- Declarative encoding: `Obtain Legal Opinion` must precede `Activate Vendor`.
- T6 fails because activation occurs at hour 13 before the opinion at hour 23.
- R6 is treated as a stakeholder proposal or exception process that is not approved for operational deployment.

**Risk:** this may block genuine urgent cases and create pressure for off-system workarounds.

### Resolution B — Authorized emergency exception policy

Treat R6 as an approved exception to R5. Activation before legal opinion is permitted only when:

1. an emergency flag is true;
2. joint external authorization evidence is present;
3. the legal opinion occurs within 24 hours after activation;
4. sanctions escalation has occurred and the normal escalation workflow is followed.

This requires a data/resource-aware validation layer because the current event log lacks the necessary attributes.

- T6 is conditionally compliant only if external evidence proves emergency authorization.
- T6 remains a data-evidence failure if the emergency authorization cannot be evidenced.

**Risk:** the event log alone cannot distinguish a justified emergency from an unauthorized policy breach. Accepting such traces without evidence weakens legal control.

## Chosen implemented policy

The implemented model uses **Resolution A: Legal-first strict policy**.

Thus, after `Sanctions Escalation`, legal opinion must occur before activation. R6 is retained as an external exception-policy validator and governance discussion, but it is not silently embedded into the ordinary activity-only model.

**Compliance risk of the choice:** valid emergencies such as T6 may be flagged as non-compliant by the deployed model even if an off-log authorization existed. This is deliberately conservative: the current log schema cannot substantiate the emergency exception.

# 2. Requirement-to-Formalism Mapping

| Requirement | Primary formalism | Exact encoding | Why this formalism | Redundancy or limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | DECLARE | `noncoexistence("Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor")` | Mutual exclusion is naturally declarative and applies regardless of branch structure. | Also structurally prevented by the procedural normal path; DECLARE catches malformed traces. |
| R2 | Procedural backbone plus DECLARE | Procedural exclusive choice among reject / manager route / compliance route; DECLARE pairwise noncoexistence between approvals and absence of approvals on reject is checked externally | Exact “accepted iff exactly one approval; rejected iff none” is easiest in procedural routing plus case-aware validation. | Pairwise DECLARE rules alone do not prove that one approval occurred for every activation. |
| R3 | Procedural backbone plus external sequence check | Process-tree loop approximates repeated cycles; external scan enforces one outstanding request and response before next request/final outcome | The exact “before next request and before final outcome” semantics is stateful and clearer in code. | `altresponse(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` is a useful declarative approximation but does not itself prohibit final outcomes while unresolved unless supplemented. |
| R4 | DECLARE | `response("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion")` | Eventual obligation is a standard response constraint. | Vacuously true when escalation never occurs. |
| R5 | DECLARE, authoritative under chosen policy | `precedence("Obtain Legal Opinion", "Activate Vendor")` | Precedence captures “if activation occurs, prior legal opinion must exist.” | This is stricter than necessary if legal opinion may occur for unrelated reasons; procedural/external context can refine it. |
| R6 | Resource/data-aware validation layer | Emergency flag, joint authorization reference, authorization identity/role, and 24-hour deadline check | Emergency authorization is absent from the activity labels and cannot be inferred from timestamps. | Under chosen strict policy it is not an accepted exception; it is retained for alternative policy evaluation. |
| R7 | DECLARE | `precedence("Run Sanctions Screening", "Sanctions Escalation")` | Simple ordering rule. | Also compatible with procedural design. |
| R8 | DECLARE | `noncoexistence("Enhanced Due Diligence", "Manager Approval")`; `noncoexistence("Compliance Approval", "Manager Approval")` | Pairwise incompatible activities fit noncoexistence. | EDD with Manager Approval is also structurally excluded in the backbone. |
| R9 | Resource/data-aware validation layer | Compare `org:resource` for `Verify Bank Account` vs applicable approval; compare `Capture Master Data` vs `Activate Vendor` | Requires event-resource values and route context. | `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` can help identify violations, but custom code is needed for the “applicable approval” condition. |
| R10 | Log Skeleton | `activ_freq` with `{"Activate Vendor": {0,1}, "Reject Vendor": {0,1}, "Release First Payment": {0,1}}` | Frequency bounds are a natural skeleton constraint. | Procedural choice also makes repeated terminal actions abnormal. |
| R11 | Resource/data-aware stateful validation layer | After each post-activation `Change Bank Details`: require later `Suspend Vendor`; require a subsequent `Verify Bank Account` before any later `Release First Payment` | This is an order-sensitive, repeated-event, post-activation obligation. | A plain process tree would need a separate lifecycle extension; DECLARE alone is insufficiently precise for repeated change/payment cycles. |
| R12 | Temporal profile | Activity-pair distributions in seconds, `zeta = 2.0` | Statistical timing expectations are not control-flow rules. | Timestamp quality, missing events, and multiple occurrences affect reliability. |

# 3. Procedural Backbone

## 1. Compact formal description

Let:

- `R = Receive Onboarding Request`
- `M = Capture Master Data`
- `S = Run Sanctions Screening`
- `B = Verify Bank Account`
- `C = Classify Risk`
- `Q = Request Clarification`
- `Aq = Receive Clarification`
- `E = Sanctions Escalation`
- `L = Obtain Legal Opinion`
- `D = Enhanced Due Diligence`
- `MA = Manager Approval`
- `CA = Compliance Approval`
- `RJ = Reject Vendor`
- `AV = Activate Vendor`
- `P = Release First Payment`
- `CL = Close Onboarding`

The canonical onboarding process is:

\[
R \rightarrow (M \parallel S \parallel B) \rightarrow
(Q \rightarrow Aq)^* \rightarrow C \rightarrow
\left(
RJ \rightarrow CL
\;\oplus\;
MA \rightarrow AV \rightarrow P \rightarrow CL
\;\oplus\;
(E? \rightarrow L? \rightarrow D \rightarrow CA \rightarrow AV \rightarrow P \rightarrow CL)
\right)
\]

The process tree intentionally does **not** encode all policy conditions:

- Legal escalation/activation precedence is authoritative in DECLARE.
- Exact clarification outstanding-state semantics is authoritative in the external sequence validator.
- Post-activation bank-detail changes are excluded from this onboarding tree and validated separately.
- The high-risk branch permits an optional escalation/opinion fragment structurally, but DECLARE and external policy checks decide whether the opinion is required and when activation is allowed.

## 2. PM4Py Python code

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator

# Visible labels, used exactly as specified
R = "Receive Onboarding Request"
M = "Capture Master Data"
S = "Run Sanctions Screening"
B = "Verify Bank Account"
C = "Classify Risk"
Q = "Request Clarification"
RC = "Receive Clarification"
SE = "Sanctions Escalation"
LO = "Obtain Legal Opinion"
EDD = "Enhanced Due Diligence"
MA = "Manager Approval"
CA = "Compliance Approval"
RV = "Reject Vendor"
AV = "Activate Vendor"
P = "Release First Payment"
CO = "Close Onboarding"


def leaf(label):
    return ProcessTree(label=label)


def op(operator, children):
    node = ProcessTree(operator=operator)
    node.children = children
    for child in children:
        child.parent = node
    return node


tau = ProcessTree(label=None)

# Receive Onboarding Request starts every case.
start = leaf(R)

# The three preliminary checks may execute concurrently.
initial_checks = op(
    Operator.PARALLEL,
    [leaf(M), leaf(S), leaf(B)]
)

# Zero or more clarification request/receive cycles.
# This is a block-structured approximation of the "one outstanding request"
# policy. Exact no-overlap and no-terminal-without-response semantics are
# enforced in the external validator.
clarification_cycle = op(
    Operator.SEQUENCE,
    [leaf(Q), leaf(RC)]
)
clarification_loop = op(
    Operator.LOOP,
    [clarification_cycle, tau]
)

classification = leaf(C)

# Rejection branch
reject_branch = op(
    Operator.SEQUENCE,
    [leaf(RV), leaf(CO)]
)

# Low-risk accepted branch
low_risk_branch = op(
    Operator.SEQUENCE,
    [leaf(MA), leaf(AV), leaf(P), leaf(CO)]
)

# High-risk branch. Escalation/legal opinion are structurally optional here;
# their true obligations are enforced in DECLARE and external policy checks.
optional_escalation_legal = op(
    Operator.XOR,
    [
        tau,
        op(
            Operator.SEQUENCE,
            [leaf(SE), leaf(LO)]
        )
    ]
)

high_risk_branch = op(
    Operator.SEQUENCE,
    [
        optional_escalation_legal,
        leaf(EDD),
        leaf(CA),
        leaf(AV),
        leaf(P),
        leaf(CO)
    ]
)

outcome = op(
    Operator.XOR,
    [reject_branch, low_risk_branch, high_risk_branch]
)

procedural_model = op(
    Operator.SEQUENCE,
    [
        start,
        initial_checks,
        clarification_loop,
        classification,
        outcome
    ]
)
```

## 3. Explanation

### Concurrency

`Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, and `Verify Bank Account` are placed under a parallel (`Operator.PARALLEL`) node. This permits all interleavings, while the sequence position before `Classify Risk` ensures all three complete before classification.

### Choices

The final XOR has three mutually exclusive normal outcomes:

1. rejection;
2. low-risk acceptance with `Manager Approval`;
3. high-risk acceptance with `Enhanced Due Diligence` then `Compliance Approval`.

This embodies the intended primary routing and prevents a normal trace from containing both rejection and activation.

### Clarification loops

The loop models zero or more sequential request/response cycles. It is deliberately a **block-structured approximation**:

- It supports repeated `Request Clarification`  `Receive Clarification` cycles.
- It prevents a request from being left unresolved in the normal tree.
- It does not independently model a clarification request interleaved with every possible pre-classification task.

The external clarification-state validator is authoritative for the precise R3 semantics.

### Terminal outcomes

The rejection branch ends with `Close Onboarding`. Accepted branches require:

`approval  Activate Vendor  Release First Payment  Close Onboarding`.

### Post-activation behavior

`Change Bank Details` and `Suspend Vendor` are deliberately excluded from the onboarding process tree because they describe a post-activation lifecycle amendment with repeated-event semantics. They are validated in the resource/data-aware layer. In a mature implementation, this should become a related vendor-maintenance process rather than a tail attached to a closed onboarding case.

| Template | Rule direction | Semantics |
|---|---|---|
| `init` | `Receive Onboarding Request` | The first event of every trace must be `Receive Onboarding Request`. |
| `response` | `Sanctions Escalation  Obtain Legal Opinion` | Every escalation must eventually be followed somewhere later by a legal opinion. This is not an immediate-next-event requirement. |
| `precedence` | `Run Sanctions Screening  Sanctions Escalation` | Every sanctions escalation must have an earlier screening event. The tuple is `(required predecessor, constrained event)`. |
| `precedence` | `Obtain Legal Opinion  Activate Vendor` | Every activation requires a prior legal opinion. This implements strict R5 and is intentionally conservative. |
| `precedence` | `Activate Vendor  Release First Payment` | Every first payment requires a prior activation. |
| `altresponse` | `Request Clarification  Receive Clarification` | Every request must be followed by a receive event before another request can activate a new response obligation. This is stronger than ordinary response and supports one-at-a-time cycles. |
| `noncoexistence` | `Reject Vendor  Activate Vendor` | The two activities cannot both occur anywhere in one trace. Unlike nonsuccession, order does not matter. |
| `noncoexistence` | `Enhanced Due Diligence  Manager Approval` | EDD and manager approval cannot co-occur in a trace. |
| `noncoexistence` | `Compliance Approval  Manager Approval` | Compliance and manager approvals cannot co-occur in a trace. |
| `nonsuccession` | `Reject Vendor  Manager Approval` | Once rejection occurs, manager approval may not occur later. This is directional and weaker than noncoexistence. |
| `nonsuccession` | `Reject Vendor  Compliance Approval` | Once rejection occurs, compliance approval may not occur later. |

Important distinctions:

- **Response**: if source occurs, target must occur later eventually.
- **Precedence**: if target occurs, source must have occurred earlier.
- **Alternate response**: each source occurrence must be followed by target before a new source occurrence can create another obligation.
- **Chain response** would require the target to be the immediate next event; it is intentionally not used because intervening activities are allowed.
- **Noncoexistence** forbids both activities anywhere in the same trace.
- **Nonsuccession** only forbids the second activity after the first; it does not forbid the reverse order.

# 5. Log-Skeleton Model

```python
log_skeleton = {
    "equivalence": set(),

    # Pair orientation: (earlier activity, later activity)
    "always_before": {
        ("Receive Onboarding Request", "Capture Master Data"),
        ("Receive Onboarding Request", "Run Sanctions Screening"),
        ("Receive Onboarding Request", "Verify Bank Account"),
        ("Capture Master Data", "Classify Risk"),
        ("Run Sanctions Screening", "Classify Risk"),
        ("Verify Bank Account", "Classify Risk"),
        ("Classify Risk", "Reject Vendor"),
        ("Classify Risk", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Classify Risk", "Enhanced Due Diligence"),
        ("Classify Risk", "Compliance Approval"),
        ("Manager Approval", "Activate Vendor"),
        ("Compliance Approval", "Activate Vendor"),
        ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"),
    },

    # Pair orientation: (earlier activity, later activity)
    "always_after": {
        ("Close Onboarding", "Reject Vendor"),
        ("Close Onboarding", "Release First Payment"),
    },

    "never_together": {
        ("Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor"),
        ("Enhanced Due Diligence", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Compliance Approval", "Manager Approval"),
    },

    "activ_freq": {
        "Receive Onboarding Request": {1},
        "Activate Vendor": {0, 1},
        "Reject Vendor": {0, 1},
        "Release First Payment": {0, 1},
    },

    # Pair orientation: (predecessor, immediate successor)
    "directly_follows": set(),
}
```

The orientations used above are explicit:

- `always_before(A, B)`: whenever both appear, `A` occurs before `B`.
- `always_after(A, B)`: whenever both appear, `A` occurs after `B`.
- `directly_follows(A, B)`: if populated, it would mean `B` immediately follows `A`.

No `directly_follows` constraints are included because concurrency and optional clarification events make immediate adjacency brittle and not semantically required.

Overlap with other layers:

- `Reject Vendor` / `Activate Vendor` is duplicated by DECLARE noncoexistence and procedural XOR routing.
- Approval-to-activation and activation-to-payment order overlaps with the procedural tree and DECLARE precedence.
- The skeleton intentionally does not encode legal-opinion precedence because the strict legal policy is represented authoritatively in DECLARE.
- The skeleton cannot express the conditional “applicable approval” or resource separation.

Potential disagreement:

- Skeleton `always_before` is generally evaluated only when both activities occur. Therefore, it is weaker than a mandatory procedural path and weaker than a declarative response/precedence combination.
- The `Close Onboarding` `always_after` pairs do not state that one of rejection or payment must exist; they only constrain ordering if both events occur.

# 6. Temporal Profile

```python
temporal_profile = {
    (
        "Request Clarification",
        "Receive Clarification"
    ): (
        48 * 3600,
        24 * 3600
    ),

    (
        "Sanctions Escalation",
        "Obtain Legal Opinion"
    ): (
        12 * 3600,
        6 * 3600
    ),

    (
        "Activate Vendor",
        "Release First Payment"
    ): (
        72 * 3600,
        36 * 3600
    ),
}

zeta = 2.0
```

With `zeta = 2.0`, the approximate warning intervals are:

| Pair | Expected interval |
|---|---:|
| Request Clarification  Receive Clarification | 0 to 96 hours |
| Sanctions Escalation  Obtain Legal Opinion | 0 to 24 hours |
| Activate Vendor  Release First Payment | 0 to 144 hours |

Temporal deviations are warnings rather than automatic control-flow failures because:

1. timing distributions describe typical behavior, not necessarily legal impossibility;
2. a delayed opinion can still be validly obtained before activation;
3. operational delays may be justified by documented circumstances;
4. timestamp quality, time zones, batching, and missing events can distort observed durations.

Under the strict legal policy, an activation before legal opinion is still a control-flow violation even if the opinion arrives within the 24-hour temporal-profile band.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

Required event attributes:

| Attribute | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `case:concept:name` | Case grouping |
| `concept:name` | Activity label |
| `time:timestamp` | Ordering and elapsed-time checks |
| `org:resource` | Four-eyes checks |
| `emergency_activation` | Boolean emergency flag for alternative R6 policy |
| `joint_authorization_id` | Evidence reference for joint authorization |
| `joint_authorizer_1`, `joint_authorizer_2` | Evidence that two distinct authorizers approved |
| `joint_authorization_timestamp` | Authorization timing |
| optionally `vendor_id` | Link post-onboarding bank changes to vendor lifecycle |

If `org:resource` is missing, R9 must be reported as **not evaluable / data-quality failure**, not silently passed. If emergency attributes are missing, an emergency exception cannot be proven.

```python
import pandas as pd

ACT = "concept:name"
CASE = "case:concept:name"
TS = "time:timestamp"
RES = "org:resource"

VERIFY_BANK = "Verify Bank Account"
MANAGER = "Manager Approval"
COMPLIANCE = "Compliance Approval"
CAPTURE = "Capture Master Data"
ACTIVATE = "Activate Vendor"
CHANGE_BANK = "Change Bank Details"
SUSPEND = "Suspend Vendor"
PAYMENT = "Release First Payment"
ESCALATE = "Sanctions Escalation"
LEGAL = "Obtain Legal Opinion"


def ordered_case_events(case_df):
    return case_df.sort_values([TS]).reset_index(drop=True)


def case_events(case_df, activity):
    return case_df[case_df[ACT] == activity]


def first_event(case_df, activity):
    x = case_events(case_df, activity)
    return None if x.empty else x.iloc[0]


def all_resources(case_df, activity):
    events = case_events(case_df, activity)
    if events.empty:
        return set()
    if RES not in events.columns or events[RES].isna().any():
        return None
    return set(events[RES].astype(str))


def validate_r9_four_eyes(case_df):
    """
    Returns a list of findings. Each finding has status:
    pass, fail, or not evaluated.
    """
    findings = []
    cdf = ordered_case_events(case_df)

    if RES not in cdf.columns:
        return [{
            "rule": "R9",
            "status": "not evaluated",
            "reason": "Missing org:resource attribute"
        }]

    # Capture Master Data performer must differ from Activate Vendor performer.
    capture_resources = all_resources(cdf, CAPTURE)
    activate_resources = all_resources(cdf, ACTIVATE)

    if activate_resources:
        if capture_resources is None or activate_resources is None:
            findings.append({
                "rule": "R9_capture_vs_activate",
                "status": "not evaluated",
                "reason": "Missing resource on Capture Master Data or Activate Vendor"
            })
        elif capture_resources & activate_resources:
            findings.append({
                "rule": "R9_capture_vs_activate",
                "status": "fail",
                "reason": "Same resource performed Capture Master Data and Activate Vendor",
                "overlap": capture_resources & activate_resources
            })
        else:
            findings.append({
                "rule": "R9_capture_vs_activate",
                "status": "pass"
            })

    # Verify Bank Account performer must differ from applicable approval performer.
    # Applicable approval is Manager Approval for low-risk route or Compliance Approval
    # for high-risk route.
    applicable_approval = None
    if not case_events(cdf, MANAGER).empty:
        applicable_approval = MANAGER
    elif not case_events(cdf, COMPLIANCE).empty:
        applicable_approval = COMPLIANCE

    if applicable_approval is not None:
        verify_resources = all_resources(cdf, VERIFY_BANK)
        approval_resources = all_resources(cdf, applicable_approval)

        if verify_resources is None or approval_resources is None:
            findings.append({
                "rule": "R9_verify_vs_approval",
                "status": "not evaluated",
                "reason": "Missing resource on Verify Bank Account or applicable approval"
            })
        elif verify_resources & approval_resources:
            findings.append({
                "rule": "R9_verify_vs_approval",
                "status": "fail",
                "reason": (
                    f"Same resource performed Verify Bank Account and "
                    f"{applicable_approval}"
                ),
                "overlap": verify_resources & approval_resources
            })
        else:
            findings.append({
                "rule": "R9_verify_vs_approval",
                "status": "pass"
            })

    return findings


def validate_r3_clarifications(case_df):
    """
    Exact one-outstanding-request interpretation:
    - Request opens an outstanding clarification.
    - Receive closes it.
    - A second request while open fails.
    - A terminal outcome while open fails.
    """
    cdf = ordered_case_events(case_df)
    outstanding = False
    findings = []

    terminal = {"Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor", "Close Onboarding"}

    for _, event in cdf.iterrows():
        activity = event[ACT]

        if activity == "Request Clarification":
            if outstanding:
                findings.append({
                    "rule": "R3",
                    "status": "fail",
                    "reason": "New clarification request before prior response"
                })
            outstanding = True

        elif activity == "Receive Clarification":
            if not outstanding:
                findings.append({
                    "rule": "R3",
                    "status": "warning",
                    "reason": "Clarification received without logged outstanding request"
                })
            outstanding = False

        elif activity in terminal and outstanding:
            findings.append({
                "rule": "R3",
                "status": "fail",
                "reason": f"Final outcome {activity} occurred with unanswered clarification"
            })

    if outstanding:
        findings.append({
            "rule": "R3",
            "status": "fail",
            "reason": "Case ended with unanswered clarification"
        })

    return findings


def validate_r11_bank_change(case_df):
    """
    For every Change Bank Details after activation:
      1. Suspend Vendor must eventually occur.
      2. Before each later Release First Payment, another Verify Bank Account
         must occur after the bank change.
    """
    cdf = ordered_case_events(case_df)
    findings = []

    activation_positions = cdf.index[cdf[ACT] == ACTIVATE].tolist()
    if not activation_positions:
        return findings

    first_activation_pos = activation_positions[0]

    for change_pos in cdf.index[cdf[ACT] == CHANGE_BANK].tolist():
        if change_pos <= first_activation_pos:
            continue

        later = cdf.loc[change_pos + 1:]

        if not (later[ACT] == SUSPEND).any():
            findings.append({
                "rule": "R11_suspend",
                "status": "fail",
                "reason": "Post-activation Change Bank Details was not followed by Suspend Vendor"
            })

        for pay_pos in later.index[later[ACT] == PAYMENT].tolist():
            between_change_and_payment = cdf.loc[change_pos + 1:pay_pos - 1]
            if not (between_change_and_payment[ACT] == VERIFY_BANK).any():
                findings.append({
                    "rule": "R11_reverify_before_payment",
                    "status": "fail",
                    "reason": (
                        "Release First Payment after Change Bank Details occurred "
                        "without a new Verify Bank Account"
                    )
                })

    return findings


def validate_emergency_exception_r6(case_df):
    """
    Alternative-policy validator only. It does not override strict R5 unless
    governance explicitly selects Resolution B.
    """
    cdf = ordered_case_events(case_df)
    findings = []

    escalation = first_event(cdf, ESCALATE)
    activation = first_event(cdf, ACTIVATE)
    legal = first_event(cdf, LEGAL)

    if escalation is None or activation is None:
        return findings

    # Only relevant where activation occurs after escalation but before legal opinion.
    if legal is not None and legal[TS] <= activation[TS]:
        findings.append({
            "rule": "R6_emergency_exception",
            "status": "pass",
            "reason": "No emergency exception needed; legal opinion precedes activation"
        })
        return findings

    required_columns = [
        "emergency_activation",
        "joint_authorization_id",
        "joint_authorizer_1",
        "joint_authorizer_2",
        "joint_authorization_timestamp"
    ]
    missing = [col for col in required_columns if col not in cdf.columns]
    if missing:
        findings.append({
            "rule": "R6_emergency_exception",
            "status": "not evaluated",
            "reason": f"Cannot prove emergency exception; missing attributes: {missing}"
        })
        return findings

    activation_row = activation
    if not bool(activation_row["emergency_activation"]):
        findings.append({
            "rule": "R6_emergency_exception",
            "status": "fail",
            "reason": "Activation before legal opinion is not marked as emergency"
        })
        return findings

    if pd.isna(activation_row["joint_authorization_id"]):
        findings.append({
            "rule": "R6_emergency_exception",
            "status": "fail",
            "reason": "Missing joint authorization evidence"
        })
        return findings

    a1 = activation_row["joint_authorizer_1"]
    a2 = activation_row["joint_authorizer_2"]
    if pd.isna(a1) or pd.isna(a2) or a1 == a2:
        findings.append({
            "rule": "R6_emergency_exception",
            "status": "fail",
            "reason": "Joint authorization does not identify two distinct authorizers"
        })
        return findings

    if legal is None:
        findings.append({
            "rule": "R6_emergency_exception",
            "status": "fail",
            "reason": "No legal opinion after emergency activation"
        })
        return findings

    elapsed = (legal[TS] - activation[TS]).total_seconds()
    if elapsed > 24 * 3600:
        findings.append({
            "rule": "R6_emergency_exception",
            "status": "fail",
            "reason": "Legal opinion arrived more than 24 hours after emergency activation",
            "elapsed_seconds": elapsed
        })
    else:
        findings.append({
            "rule": "R6_emergency_exception",
            "status": "pass",
            "reason": "Emergency authorization evidence present and legal opinion within 24 hours",
            "elapsed_seconds": elapsed
        })

    return findings
```

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

## Duplicated enforcement across layers

Several constraints are deliberately duplicated:

- `Reject Vendor` versus `Activate Vendor`:
  - procedural XOR;
  - DECLARE noncoexistence;
  - skeleton `never_together`.
- Approval/activation/payment order:
  - procedural sequence;
  - DECLARE precedence;
  - skeleton `always_before`.
- EDD and Manager Approval incompatibility:
  - procedural branch routing;
  - DECLARE noncoexistence;
  - skeleton `never_together`.

This redundancy is useful for diagnostics. A procedural failure indicates path deviation; a DECLARE failure identifies a policy relation; a skeleton failure indicates trace-level structural inconsistency.

## Constraints becoming inconsistent under loops or post-activation changes

Clarification cycles create repeated occurrences. A naive `response(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` can allow one late response to satisfy multiple request obligations. `altresponse` is better but still benefits from an explicit outstanding-request state machine.

Post-activation `Change Bank Details` complicates the original “first payment follows activation” backbone:

- R11 refers to a later `Release First Payment`, suggesting additional payment events may exist in a broader vendor lifecycle.
- R10 says `Release First Payment` occurs at most once.
- If a bank change occurs after the only first payment, the “verify before any later Release First Payment” clause may be vacuously true, while suspension remains mandatory.
- If future payments are modeled, they should use a different activity label such as `Release Payment`, leaving `Release First Payment` semantically unique.

## Vacuity in declarative rules

Many DECLARE rules are vacuously satisfied:

- `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` passes if no escalation occurs.
- `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` passes if no activation occurs.
- `noncoexistence(Manager Approval, Compliance Approval)` passes if neither approval occurs.

Therefore DECLARE must not be the sole model of mandatory routing. The procedural backbone and case-aware validations establish existence and path obligations.

## Missing case attributes

The event labels do not contain:

- risk classification result;
- accepted/rejected case flag;
- emergency indicator;
- joint authorization reference;
- authorizer identities and roles;
- vendor relationship identifiers;
- reason codes for rejection, suspension, or bank change.

Without these attributes, some policies can only be conservatively approximated. In particular, R6 cannot be validated from activity labels and timestamps.

## Timestamp-only limitations

Timestamps cannot prove:

- that an emergency was genuinely urgent;
- that authorization was joint and valid;
- that an opinion’s content was favorable;
- that the resource logged on the event actually performed the action;
- that clock timestamps are complete, synchronized, and non-manipulated.

Timing checks should account for time zones, business calendars, data-entry delay, batch imports, and duplicate events.

## Support/confidence are not normative truth

Setting `support = 1.0` and `confidence = 1.0` expresses a normative model declaration here. It does not mean:

- historical logs actually achieve 100% compliance;
- the policy is legally correct;
- the rule is more important than another rule;
- a PM4Py conformance result resolves a stakeholder conflict.

Governance must determine which policy source is authoritative before numeric metadata is treated as operational policy.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

Use the **ProcessTree** as the canonical representation of the stable onboarding control-flow backbone:

- receive request;
- parallel preliminary checks;
- optional clarification cycles;
- classification;
- exclusive rejection, low-risk, or high-risk outcome;
- activation, payment, and closure for accepted cases.

Use the **resource/data-aware policy layer** as the authoritative layer for conditions that cannot be reliably represented by activity labels alone:

- four-eyes segregation;
- emergency authorization;
- applicable approval determination;
- clarification outstanding-state enforcement;
- post-activation bank-change obligations;
- evidence completeness.

Use **DECLARE** as the authoritative activity-sequence policy layer for cross-cutting obligations, especially:

- escalation implies legal opinion;
- screening precedes escalation;
- strict legal opinion before activation under the chosen policy;
- incompatible approvals and outcomes.

Recommended conformance order:

1. **Data quality validation**
   - required case ID, activity, timestamp, resource, ordering;
   - emergency evidence fields when exception policy is selected.
2. **Procedural conformance**
   - identify deviations from the intended onboarding path.
3. **DECLARE conformance**
   - identify cross-cutting activity-sequence policy violations.
4. **Log-skeleton conformance**
   - identify frequency, co-occurrence, and ordering anomalies.
5. **Resource/data-aware checks**
   - segregation-of-duties, emergency authorization, post-activation changes.
6. **Temporal-profile monitoring**
   - produce warnings and trend analysis rather than automatic rejection.

Contradictory findings should be shown to users as separate, explainable findings rather than collapsed into one opaque score. For example:

- “Procedural path valid.”
- “Legal policy violation: activation occurred before legal opinion.”
- “Emergency exception cannot be substantiated because authorization attributes are missing.”
- “Timing normal: opinion arrived 17 hours after escalation.”

This preserves the distinction between control-flow nonconformance, missing evidence, resource-control failure, and statistical delay.
