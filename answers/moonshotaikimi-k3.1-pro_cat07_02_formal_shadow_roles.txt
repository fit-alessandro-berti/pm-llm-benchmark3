# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

Activity frequency is useful for identifying routine work, but three qualifications are essential:

- **Criticality can outweigh frequency.** Erik’s security reviews and the quality analysts’ KB work remain important even when other activities have higher counts.
- **Complexity distinguishes work within the same activity profile.** Chen and Dina can share a technical role without being interchangeable on critical cases.
- **Execution is not authority.** A6 and A9 are performed by several resources, so preliminary review, local approval, final approval and later rechecking must be distinguished.

For each proposed role:

- **Automated intake and precheck:** A1/A2 are core by design, but Nora’s A2 should be treated as a precheck until its output semantics prove otherwise. Its volume does not give it human entitlement or exception authority.
- **Front-office request resolution:** A1/A2/A4/A12 define the routine role. Technical, commercial and quality events are peripheral executions or handoff support. Amira’s more complex mix may reflect experience-based routing, but does not yet justify a separate formal role.
- **Seasonal standard-work support:** The temps’ observed core is narrow routine execution. Their high A2/A4/A12 counts describe workload, not proven entitlement competence. Their zero A13 counts are particularly weak quality evidence because fragment 6 shows other resources performing subsequent rework.
- **Senior front-office/commercial hybrid:** Giorgio’s front-office work and recurrent A8 negotiation are stable parts of a hybrid role. A7 combines apparent ongoing shadow work with a substitution spike. Technical activities and A9 remain peripheral or ambiguous; neither his pricing certification nor temporary substitution proves unrestricted final commercial or supervisory authority.
- **Technical diagnosis and implementation:** A3/A5/A10 are core for both Chen and Dina. A6, A13 and A14 are occasional security, escalation or learning contributions. Chen’s higher complexity and security adjacency justify different routing and supervision, even though the basic activity set is nearly identical.
- **Security exception specialism:** A6 is core because of its risk function and Erik’s case mix, not because it dominates his activity counts. His other technical and KB activities may be follow-through on the same security cases; they should not be used to reclassify him as a general technical analyst without case-level evidence.
- **Commercial exception handling:** Fatima’s A7/A8 work is core. Her miscellaneous front-office and technical events are peripheral, and her A9 events require confirmation as commercial recommendations or local approvals rather than final supervisory sign-off.
- **Supervisory approval:** Hana’s core is defined by approval and escalation accountability. Her rechecking of Mateo’s work is stronger authority evidence than the raw A9 count. Her other scattered activities do not make her a generalist.
- **Quality and knowledge management:** A11 and A14 are both core: operational quality control and organizational learning. A5/A10 and other events look like support or rework. A14 should not be demoted to peripheral work merely because it occurs less often than A11.
- **Cross-functional coordination:** Mateo’s core is visible primarily in sequence position, handovers and high-complexity case involvement, rather than in a modal activity. Direct task execution, preliminary A9 work and referral filtering are bounded coordination or backup activities, not blanket specialist or supervisory competence.

# 6. Operational interpretation

**Staffing and capacity**

- Maintain distinct capacity pools for routine front-office work, complex technical work, commercial exceptions, quality, security and coordination. Do not aggregate them merely because employees occasionally perform one another’s activities.
- Route critical technical work according to demonstrated capability: Chen’s profile should not make Dina appear unable to develop, but nor should their similarity make them immediately interchangeable.
- Protect security and approval capacity even when event volumes are modest. Erik’s staffing requirement should be driven by risk and critical demand, while Hana’s should reflect final decision workload and delegation control.
- Use Giorgio and Mateo as resilience assets, but avoid making either an undocumented single point of failure.

**Training and development**

- Give Kai and Lina targeted entitlement-note training, closure checklists and risk-based quality sampling. Assess improvement using first-time-right outcomes, not closure volume.
- Use Chen’s complex cases and security-adjacent work as a development path for Dina, with staged complexity and supervision.
- If Giorgio’s commercial backup is to continue, formalize its scope, pricing limits and escalation criteria. If Mateo retains approval-backup duties, train and log the distinction between preliminary and final decisions.
- Use Ivan and Julia’s KB work to convert recurring reopen and escalation causes into front-office and temporary-agent guidance.

**Risk control**

- Add explicit event states such as `precheck`, `preliminary`, `final`, `rechecked` and `delegated` where appropriate. This would prevent A2/A6/A9 labels from concealing different levels of accountability.
- For Enterprise Security referrals, define mandatory triggers for direct Erik review, critical-case overrides and maximum waiting times. Track both false escalations and delayed critical cases.
- Audit temporary-agent entitlement notes and subsequent reopens, while retaining surge capacity where quality is acceptable.
- Review Giorgio’s pricing turnaround using case-mix-adjusted service and waiting times. The 38-to-74-minute change should trigger investigation, not an automatic competence judgment.
- Monitor Nora’s precheck exceptions and maintain a tested manual registration/validation fallback.

**Resilience**

The process owner should document primary and backup coverage for Fatima, Hana and Erik; reserve coordination capacity for Mateo during incident surges; and test whether quality coverage actually works across Ivan’s and Julia’s different shifts. Role overlays can be formalized without eliminating their flexibility.

**Cautions before changing staffing**

1. **The log covers only ten weeks and includes absence, seasonal staffing and an Enterprise Security surge.** Permanent staffing changes require a longer window and sensitivity analysis across normal and exceptional periods.
2. **Event volume is not effort, utilization or replaceable capacity.** Nora’s automation events are not comparable to human work, and low-volume security or approval work can still be indispensable.
3. **Similar activity profiles do not establish equal competence or authority.** This applies especially to Chen/Dina, Giorgio/Fatima and Hana/Mateo.
4. **The operational episodes are not causal estimates.** The pricing-duration increase and the 17 reopened cases need denominators, case-mix controls and outcome audits before being used to reduce staff, remove backups or change individual responsibilities.
