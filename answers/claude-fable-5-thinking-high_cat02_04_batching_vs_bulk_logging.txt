# 3. Consequences for performance analysis

For each cluster: bias direction and mechanism on **waiting time** (arrival→start), **service time** (start→complete), **resource utilization**, and **bottleneck detection**.

- **G1.** If the batch is genuine, per-item metrics are approximately correct, but utilization computed as sum-of-item-service-times / elapsed-time overstates load fourfold (12 item-minutes packed into 3 wall-clock minutes on one fixture). Throughput per hour is fine; capacity headroom is not, unless the fixture is modeled as a 4-slot server. Waiting (2–6 min) reflects deliberate batch-forming delay: early arrivals wait for the fixture to fill, which is a policy cost, not congestion. Bottleneck analysis that ignores batch-forming will blame upstream flow for waiting the labeling cell itself causes.
- **G2.** Logged service times 8/14/21/27 min are increasingly inflated: each later item's "service" silently includes the queueing time behind its group-mates. Mean logged service ≈ 17.5 min vs. a plausible true ≈ 6–8 min per item. Waiting is correspondingly understated (it stops at the fictitious common start). Utilization of T-B looks like 4 parallel jobs (400% instantaneous) — impossible for one person. Bottleneck detection inflates the inspection step's service and hides queue growth in front of it.
- **G3.** The mirror image of G2: logged completes are pushed late to 11:30, so service times of early-started items (E01: 28 min) are inflated by post-work "sitting in the folder" time, and downstream waiting (from 11:30) is understated because items were actually finished earlier. Flow time into the *next* step gets misattributed: idle folder time is booked as review service. Utilization again shows impossible overlap for one reviewer.
- **G4.** Essentially unbiased if "start" means work start: waits (7–20 min, growing along the chain) correctly show batch queueing; services are a stable 4 min; utilization is a clean 100% busy period of 20 min. The one caveat: if the scanner auto-stamps start at the previous complete, "service" includes pick-up/walk time and true hands-on time is slightly smaller — a bounded, second-order bias.
- **G5.** With the resource trusted as one worker, utilization exceeds 100% (up to 4 concurrent intervals), service times are correct per case, and staffing analysis concludes an impossible super-worker. With the shared account acknowledged, utilization per analyst is simply unknown (bounded between 33% and 100% of the pool depending on how many were active). Bottleneck ranking by "resource load" is meaningless until identity is resolved.
- **G6 — why bulk logging creates both inflated waiting and near-zero service.** The only instant the system observes is the *entry* moment (17:00/17:01). Everything before it is folded into waiting: C01's wait is logged as 2 h 55 min even if the ticket was actually closed at 14:30 and merely typed up later. Simultaneously service collapses to zero because start and complete are the same keystroke. So the analysis reports a process that queues for hours and is then processed instantaneously — an artifact pair generated by a single mechanism (one observable timestamp per case). Consequences: waiting-time distributions get a fat right tail and a spike pattern at shift boundaries; service-time distributions get a spurious mass at zero; utilization shows T-F idle all afternoon then infinitely fast at 17:00; queue-length reconstructions show a phantom backlog that may never have existed physically. Any queueing model fitted to this (e.g. arrival/service rate estimation) is doubly corrupted.
- **G7.** Rounding to 15 min makes waits meaningless at the item level — four of five are negative, and the "true" waits are anywhere in roughly (−15, +15) min of the stated values. Service times all snap to exactly 15 min, destroying variance; utilization within the quarter-hour is unrecoverable. Bottleneck detection based on service-time variance or on short queues is blind here; only quarter-hour-aggregate throughput is trustworthy.
- **G8.** Waits of 38–65 min are real in total but causally heterogeneous: roughly 30–35 min is *blockage* (resource down for the safety check) and the remainder is *queue discipline* (items 2–5 waiting their turn in the serial chain). A bottleneck analysis that pools both will recommend adding triage capacity when the actual lever is downtime scheduling. Service (7 min) and utilization (busy 15:38–16:13) are trustworthy.

    return (a[0] < b[1]) and (b[0] < a[1])

    ivs = list(zip(sub["start"], sub["complete"]))
    pairs = list(itertools.combinations(range(len(ivs)), 2))

# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

**Resource IDs trusted vs. treated as shared accounts.**
- *Trusted:* G5 becomes a paradox — one analyst holding four open intervals, resolvable only as multitasking; utilization > 100% must then be read as interleaving intensity. G2 and G3 stay artifacts (one human cannot truly run four overlapping inspections/reviews), which *strengthens* the start-batching and bulk-submission diagnoses because genuine concurrency is off the table.
- *Shared:* G5 flips to ordinary parallel work by 2–3 people and stops being anomalous at all (except that concurrency 4 > 3 documented analysts still requires either one instance of multitasking or minute-level timestamp slack). If T-B or T-C were secretly shared, G2/G3's overlaps could be partially genuine, and the confidence in "artifact" would drop from ~85/70 to roughly 50 — the single strongest sensitivity in this whole analysis. G1 (machine) and G4/G8 (non-overlapping chains) are insensitive to this assumption.

**Timestamps exact vs. interval-censored by stated precision.**
- *Exact:* G1's second-level agreement is meaningful evidence of true simultaneity; G7's equalities would imply genuine simultaneous measurement — but then the negative waits become contradictions, so the "exact" reading is internally inconsistent for G7 and must be rejected there.
- *Interval-censored:* every 1-min-precision cluster (G2, G3, G5, G6) has ±30 s slack — immaterial for their diagnoses, which rest on multi-minute structure. G7's ±7.5-min censoring dissolves both the equalities and the negative waits: starts anywhere in [14:00, 14:15) and completes in (14:00, 14:15] admit a fully sequential, physically consistent schedule (e.g. serial 2–3-min measurements finishing just before each recorded_at). Censoring is therefore not a nuisance for G7 — it is the *resolution* of the contradiction. G6 under censoring still yields no service-time information; censoring cannot rescue a log whose only real observable is entry time.

**Assumption log.** (A1) All clocks except the flagged safety system are approximately synchronized — needed because cross-field comparisons (arrival vs. start) otherwise carry no meaning. (A2) `sequence_no` faithfully reflects source insertion order — needed to read G7's recorded_at staggering as an ordering signal. (A3) `queue_arrival` from the upstream system is accurate to its displayed minute — needed for all waiting-time statements. (A4) One row = one work item — needed to talk about per-item service at all.

**Same temporal pattern, multiple physical explanations (three-plus cases).**
1. *All-equal starts and ends* appears in G1 (genuine simultaneous machine batch), G7 (rounding artifact), and G6 (bulk data entry). Identical geometry, three different physical worlds; only fixture capacity, boundary-clustering plus negative waits, and the source name respectively break the ties.
2. *Common start with staggered ends* (G2) supports: start batching with serial execution; genuine parallel processing with different item difficulties; or a setup phase shared by all items followed by individual finishing — the log alone cannot separate them; the "begin group" button context does.
3. *Staggered starts with a common end* (G3) supports: bulk submission of finished work; one reviewer multitasking and finishing everything at once; or a downstream gate (e.g. a release meeting at 11:30) that legitimately synchronizes completions — the third reading would make the 11:30 completes *correct* and the process genuinely synchronized.
4. *Perfect back-to-back chains* (G4, G8) support: deliberate sequential batching; continuous one-piece flow that merely looks batch-like because arrivals bunched; or a logging protocol that defines each start as the previous complete. The first two differ in *intent* (a batching policy vs. incidental bunching), which no timestamp can reveal.

# 6. Data-collection redesign

Prioritized by diagnostic value per unit of implementation effort:

1. **Separate execution timestamps from recording timestamps everywhere** (fixes G6, G3, G2). Every event carries both `occurred_at` (when the work happened, entered or measured) and `recorded_at` (system write time). Bulk forms must ask "when did you actually close this?" instead of stamping submission time.
2. **Explicit group/batch identifiers** (fixes G1, G2, G6 interpretation). When a fixture, group button, or bulk form covers N items, emit a `batch_id` and a `batch_role` (member/anchor). This converts today's inference problem into a recorded fact and lets PM4Py-style detections be validated instead of trusted.
3. **Badge-level resource identity on shared accounts** (fixes G5). Keep the pool login if needed, but log the badge or SSO identity per event; retain it at least in pseudonymized form. Without it, utilization and staffing analytics for shared stations should be formally out of scope.
4. **Timestamp precision and clock metadata as first-class fields** (fixes G7, G8). Each source publishes its true granularity, its rounding rule (down/up/nearest), and a periodic clock-offset measurement against a reference (NTP drift log). The legacy MES's "undocumented rule" is the single cheapest documentation fix on this list.
5. **Pause/resume and blocking events** (fixes G8, improves G5). Record resource-unavailability intervals (safety checks, breaks, downtime) in the same log with the same clock, so waiting can be decomposed into blocked vs. queued time mechanically.
6. **Workstation capacity registry** (supports G1, G2). A static table of parallel capacity per station (fixture slots, bench positions) lets any concurrency observation be checked against physical possibility automatically.
7. **True per-item lifecycle on grouped interfaces** (fixes G2's residual ambiguity). Even with a "begin group" button, capture a lightweight per-item start (first touch/scan of that item), keeping the group button as a batch-level event rather than a per-item timestamp donor.
8. **Monotonic sequence numbers with source and clock lineage retained** (preserves what already works). `sequence_no` rescued the G7 diagnosis; guarantee its retention and document its semantics (insertion order per source) so future analysts can rely on it.

# 7. Final adjudication

**Interpretable with high confidence, usable largely as-is:** G4 (sequential batching; all four performance dimensions trustworthy, minor caveat on start semantics) and G1 (genuine simultaneous machine batch, *conditional* on a one-time confirmation that the fixture-of-four reading is correct; then model the fixture as a 4-slot server for utilization). G8's *service* segment (15:38 onward) is equally trustworthy.

**Usable only after correction or censoring:** G2 (reconstruct starts from the completion chain; then it becomes a G4-like sequential cluster), G3 (interval-censor completes to [next start, 11:30]; report waiting and service as ranges, not points), and G7 (interval-censor everything to ±15 min; keep for quarter-hour throughput only; ban item-level waits — four are negative — and service variance from any report).

**Exclude from queue analysis or restrict severely:** G6 — service times must be excluded outright (zero by construction), and waiting times reported only as upper bounds (arrival → 17:00 entry), explicitly labeled as right-censored by the bulk-entry practice. Publishing G6 waits as measurements would be the single most misleading number this dataset could produce.

**Require operational validation before any performance conclusion:** G5 (resolve who is behind `analyst_pool` — even a one-week badge sample would separate parallelism from multitasking) and G8's *waiting* decomposition (measure the repair-queue vs. safety-system clock offset; until then, "48 min median wait" cannot be split into downtime cost vs. queueing cost, and no capacity recommendation should be made). G2 and G3 would also benefit from a brief floor observation to confirm the single-worker assumption on which their artifact diagnoses rest.

The overarching adjudication principle applied here: PM4Py's batch categories correctly summarize timestamp geometry in all eight clusters, but in only three of the eight (G1, G4, G8) does that geometry survive contact with the independent evidence — recording lags, sequence numbers, impossible orderings, source names, and equipment context — as a description of physical work. Identical timestamps were never, on their own, evidence of simultaneous work; in this dataset they were more often evidence about the logging system than about the process.
