## 1. Materiality Framework

Process-alignment cost measures only the minimum control-flow edit distance between an observed event trace and a normative process tree under a uniform unit-cost function. Business materiality requires evaluating what a deviation means for financial integrity, fraud prevention, regulatory compliance, and customer protection.

### Dimensions of Business Severity

1. **Control Objective**: Whether the affected step is a preventive financial/fraud gate (`Fraud Screen`, `Manual Approve`, payment release ordering) versus an administrative, evidentiary, or non-control telemetry step.
2. **Amount and Financial Exposure**: Direct monetary loss at stake (e.g., EUR 480,000 vs. EUR 1,200), weighted alongside whether a small-ticket vulnerability is automatable at scale.
3. **Reversibility**: Funds already settled to a beneficiary bank (especially an account changed hours earlier) are largely irreversible; logging, mapping, or model-scope defects are fully reversible without financial outflow.
4. **Timing and Sequencing**: Whether authorization and fraud screening occurred *before* disbursement (Policy P3), whether bank-detail changes immediately preceded payout (account-takeover indicator), and whether claimant notification met the 24-hour SLA (Policy P4).
5. **Customer and Regulatory Harm**: Risk of wrongful payout to a fraudster (harming the legitimate policyholder), failure to maintain auditable anti-fraud/governance evidence, unfair claim handling, or notification spam/omission.
6. **Recurrence and Systemic Exploitability**: One-off human overrides vs. systemic rule-engine defects (4 unauthorized auto-approvals/month), integration gaps (9 external-adjuster cases/month), or connector storms (74 retry duplicates/month).
7. **Evidence Reliability**: Strength and immutability of corroborating records outside the core log (signed licensed-adjuster report with stable ID vs. mutable CRM text field vs. uncorroborated verbal assertion).
8. **Model Scope**: Whether the case actually belongs to the payable-claims lifecycle modeled by the process tree (Policy P6) or represents a legitimate non-payable exit (denial, withdrawal).

### Severity Scale (1–5)

| Severity | Label | Definition |
|---:|---|---|
| **5** | **Critical** | Active failure of a preventive fraud or payment-authorization control on an irreversible outflow, combining high financial exposure, premature payment (P3), or acute fraud indicators (e.g., recent bank change bypassed via Auto Approve). Requires immediate containment and investigation. |
| **4** | **High** | Material policy/authorization bypass (e.g., unauthorized Auto Approve violating P1 on a mid-sized claim) without immediate indicators of active account takeover, creating unapproved financial exposure and systemic control drift. |
| **3** | **Moderate** | Substantive operational, SLA, or auditability failure with bounded direct loss—such as missing corroboration for a high-value step (e.g., the 2 unlinked external-adjuster cases in the monthly pattern) or a confirmed >24-hour notification breach. |
| **2** | **Low** | Substantive business controls executed properly, but cross-system audit trails or linkage integrity are weakened (e.g., external evidence present with minor integration or mutability gaps). |
| **1** | **Informational / Non-Issue** | Technically compliant execution where alignment cost stems solely from UI telemetry, transport retries, ETL label drift, silent Petri-net routing transitions, or out-of-scope non-payable lifecycles. |

### How Confidence Differs from Severity

**Severity** measures the *potential business impact* if a deviation is real; **Confidence** (High / Medium / Low) measures *how certain we are about what actually happened* given the current evidence. The two are orthogonal:
- A **Severity 5 / Medium-High Confidence** case (such as CL-602) demands urgent action *despite* residual uncertainty over whether an informal fraud check occurred verbally, because the absence of auditable evidence on a EUR 480,000 high-risk payout is already a severe control failure.
- A **Severity 1 / High Confidence** case (such as CL-601 or CL-605) requires no operational intervention because we are highly certain no business rule was breached.
- A **missing event (`M(...)`) never proves an activity did not occur**; it proves only that the core log lacks a matching record. Confidence rises only when corroborated by independent artifacts (bank timestamps, decision-engine inputs, signed reports, or immutable cross-system keys).

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Raw alignment cost is a **syntactic distance metric** over control-flow labels under a chosen edit-cost function. Treating it as a proxy for business risk systematically misallocates audit and remediation effort, as demonstrated across the supplied cases:

1. **Uniform Cost-Function Design Flattens Control Criticality**: All visible log and model moves were configured uniformly at cost `1`. Consequently, omitting a mandatory anti-fraud check on a EUR 480,000 high-risk payout with a 1-day-old bank change (**CL-602**, `M(Fraud Screen)`, `raw_cost=1`) receives the exact same score as a benign notification retry (**CL-603**, `L(Notify Claimant)`, `raw_cost=1`). A single missing preventive gate can represent catastrophic loss, whereas multiple extra log events may represent zero harm.
2. **Data Conditions Are Invisible to Pure Control-Flow Trees**: The normative process tree allows `Auto Approve` unconditionally as a structural branch. Thus **CL-609** (EUR 18,000, MEDIUM risk, violating P1) and **CL-610** (EUR 4,500, bank changed 1 hour before payment, violating P1 and P2) both achieve `raw_cost=0`—a perfect technical fit that completely masks unauthorized payouts and an active account-takeover indicator.
3. **Model Scope Boundaries Artificially Inflate Cost**: Because M0 models only payable claims (P6), legitimate non-payable exits (**CL-607** denied pre-screen; **CL-612** claimant withdrawal) are forced to "pay" both for the unmodeled exit event and for every skipped downstream payable activity (`Assess Loss`, `Auto Approve`, `Pay Claim`, `Notify Claimant`), yielding the highest alignment costs in the portfolio (`raw_cost=4`) despite zero financial outflow or operational failure.
4. **Hidden/Silent Moves Have Zero Cost by Construction**: In **CL-605**, `T(tau,c=0)` is an internal routing transition introduced when converting the XOR choice `X(...)` into an accepting Petri net. It contributes `0` cost and is technically fitting—showing that non-synchronous alignment entries are not automatically deviations, just as synchronous zero-cost traces (CL-609, CL-610) are not automatically compliant.
5. **Activity Granularity and Label Renaming Multiply Edit Cost**: In **CL-611**, a pure taxonomy mismatch (`Assess Damage` instead of `Assess Loss`) costs `2` (`L` + `M`) because edit distance penalizes a substitution as a deletion plus an insertion. That harmless ETL lag scores twice the raw cost of **CL-602**'s EUR 480,000 missing fraud screen.
6. **Duplicated Telemetry and UI Noise Dominate Edit Counts**: Fine-grained UI events in **CL-601** (`Open Workbench`, `Autosave`, `Close Workbench`, `raw_cost=3`) and transport retries in **CL-603** (`raw_cost=1`, 74/month) accumulate log-move penalties unrelated to business control objectives. **CL-601**'s harmless UI telemetry reaches the same `raw_cost=3` as **CL-604**'s severe EUR 90,000 premature payment.
7. **Missing Core Events Can Mask Valid External Evidence**: In **CL-606** (`M(Assess Loss)`, `raw_cost=1`) and **CL-608** (`M(Notify Claimant)`, `raw_cost=1`), substantive work occurred outside the core system (a signed external adjuster report at 10:05; a CRM portal notice at +3 hours). Alignment cost cannot distinguish "activity never happened" from "activity happened in another system with intact or weakly linked evidence."
8. **Irreversibility and Timing Risk Are Non-Linear**: In **CL-604**, executing `Pay Claim` 17 minutes *before* `Manual Approve` (with no Fraud Screen) is an irreversible cash outflow violating P3. Even if an alternative model or lower-cost repair could align the swap cheaply, once funds settle externally, retroactive approval cannot undo credit or fraud exposure.
