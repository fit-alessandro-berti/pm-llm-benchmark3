## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional verdict: mainly confounded. Confidence: 72/100.**

Orion’s crude mean cycle time is 21.96 hours versus Nova’s 15.24, a +6.72-hour association. The strongest evidence for confounding is the routing pattern: Orion receives substantially more high-complexity, compliance-required, night, and high-load work. Moreover, the comparison reverses within every observed complexity stratum: Orion is faster by 2.14 hours for low, 2.21 for medium, and 3.43 for high complexity. Standardizing both groups to the pooled complexity distribution produces approximately 17.14 hours for Orion and 19.65 for Nova, an Orion-minus-Nova contrast of 2.51 hours.

The strongest evidence against a confident confounding-only conclusion is poor support: Orion has only one day/normal-load case, while several complexity-by-shift cells contain only one group. Complexity is coarse, routing may use unobserved case risk, and completed-case selection can bias either direction. Orion’s higher crude queue wait and rework rate could therefore still contain a harmful operational component.

The relevant total effect is the effect of routing an otherwise eligible case to Orion, allowing resulting queueing, reviewer mix, service, and rework to change. A direct effect holding those mechanisms fixed is largely a residual “group-label” effect and is not operationally compelling. Mediated effects through queueing, reviewer skill, review service, and potentially rework are more plausible; compliance requirement itself precedes assignment and is not an Orion-mediated variable.

Moving 50% may succeed if Nova has spare equivalent capacity, fail if the crude gap is case mix, or backfire by overloading Nova, weakening night/compliance coverage, and affecting quality.

Fact-supported arrows include complexity and operating conditions into routing, compliance requirement into performance, and queue/service/rework into cycle time. Orion’s night coverage and certified-reviewer mix support routing and skill pathways. Direct group-to-outcome arrows, unobserved-risk pathways, and group-dependent completion are plausible assumptions rather than established facts. The static DAG suppresses queue feedback: current routing changes each group’s future workload and waiting time, which then influences later routing decisions. It also compresses the time-varying `Review -> Request Info -> Review` loop into one rework node.

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0: Instrument and establish support | Capture all arrivals, routing scores/reasons, eligibility, queue state, event timestamps, reviewer, staffing, and censoring status before diversion | Measurement, residual confounding, completed-case selection | All newly classified cases | No operational reassignment; validate definitions and compliance conformance | Variant frequencies, directly-follows relations, event/queue durations, WIP at arrival, handoffs, open-case age, completion hazards | Establishes whether comparable Orion and Nova cases exist and separates queue, compliance, external, and service time | Do not start a routing experiment if key timestamps or eligibility are missing for more than a prespecified tolerance, or if no dual-eligible overlap exists |
| 1: Queue/compliance capacity crossover | Add or re-time certified night/compliance capacity, use aging-based release and WIP limits, without changing the group label | Queueing and compliance-related waiting | Randomized or staggered shift-week | Never bypass compliance; maintain certification and workload limits; monitor both queues for spillovers | Queue entry-to-start time, compliance interval, WIP, aged inventory, throughput, p50/p90 cycle time, conformance deviations | Tests whether the main lever is capacity and queue discipline rather than group identity | Roll back for any compliance breach, sustained backlog increase, or p90 cycle deterioration beyond a prespecified guardrail |
| 2: Small randomized routing pilot | Route approximately 10–15% of dual-eligible cases to Orion or Nova within blocks defined by complexity, compliance requirement, shift, and queue state | Total assignment effect under controlled saturation | Individual eligible case within monitored time blocks | Fixed probability; capacity caps; exclude cases lacking required certification; intention-to-treat analysis; independent quality audit | Cycle and completion hazard, decomposed waits, service, rework, variants, handoffs, queue spillovers, audited quality | Directly estimates a local policy effect while revealing queue, service, and rework mechanisms | Pause if either group’s WIP or p90 exceeds its guardrail for consecutive shifts, certification is violated, or audited quality shows credible deterioration |
| 3: Adaptive scale-up | Increase diversion in small steps only where the pilot shows benefit; otherwise deploy queue-state-aware routing or retain specialist allocation | System-level equilibrium and nonlinear congestion | Routing-policy block or service line | Preserve specialist capacity for difficult cases; monitor saturation and heterogeneous effects; maintain a holdout | Saturation-response curves, throughput, open-case age, variant drift, compliance time, rework, quality, group-specific WIP | Estimates whether effects persist when diversion changes both groups’ queues, unlike a static historical comparison | Return to the prior diversion level if marginal cycle improvement disappears, backlog transfers to Nova, or quality/coverage guardrails fail |

**Final recommendation.** Do not authorize an immediate 50% reassignment. The supplied data undermine the stated rationale: Orion’s higher overall mean reverses within every measured complexity level, while joint overlap by complexity and shift is extremely poor. Historical averages also cannot predict the equilibrium effect of transferring half the workload, because the transfer itself changes Nova’s queue, Orion’s queue, staffing pressure, and access to certified reviewers.

First instrument all arrivals and separate queueing, compliance, external-information waiting, and review service. In parallel, run a staggered night/compliance-capacity intervention, because the standardized descriptive difference is concentrated mainly in waiting rather than service. Then conduct a capped randomized pilot among dual-eligible cases, initially around 10–15%, stratified by case and operating conditions. Analyze all routed cases using intention-to-treat and time-to-completion methods, not completed cases alone. Scale only if Nova produces lower cycle time under comparable assignment without transferring backlog, increasing rework, breaching certification requirements, or degrading independently audited quality.

print("\n=== PM4Py frequency DFG comparison ===")
# Parse only activity sequences. No timestamps are created, so the DFGs below
# are frequency DFGs and must not be interpreted as performance DFGs.
dfgs = {}
for group in group_order:
    trace_strings = (
        df.loc[df["group"] == group, "trace"]
          .str.replace(">", ",", regex=False)
          .tolist()
    )
    event_log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(trace_strings)
    dfg, start_activities, end_activities = pm4py.discover_dfg(event_log)
    dfgs[group] = dfg

    print(f"\n{group} start activities: {dict(start_activities)}")
    print(f"{group} end activities: {dict(end_activities)}")
    print(f"{group} directly-follows counts:")
    for (source, target), frequency in sorted(dfg.items()):
        print(f"  {source} -> {target}: {frequency}")

all_edges = sorted(set().union(*(set(dfg.keys()) for dfg in dfgs.values())))
edge_rows = []
group_sizes = df["group"].value_counts()

for source, target in all_edges:
    row = {"relation": f"{source} -> {target}"}
    for group in group_order:
        count = dfgs[group].get((source, target), 0)
        row[f"{group}_count"] = count
        row[f"{group}_per_case"] = count / group_sizes[group]
    edge_rows.append(row)

edge_comparison = pd.DataFrame(edge_rows).set_index("relation")
print("\nDFG edge comparison, including frequencies per case:")
print(edge_comparison.round(3))

print(
    "\nDIAGNOSTIC ONLY: these summaries demonstrate association, overlap, and "
    "control-flow differences. They do not identify a causal resource-group effect."
)

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Uncertainty:** The routing engine may use risk information not represented by three-level complexity. **Most valuable addition:** a pre-assignment `routing_score/reason_code` field.
2. **Uncertainty:** Recorded waiting cannot be separated into group queueing, compliance waiting, and external-information delay. **Most valuable addition:** an event-level state-transition timestamp log.
3. **Uncertainty:** Orion’s group effect cannot be separated from individual reviewer skill. **Most valuable addition:** `reviewer_id` linked to a fixed certification roster.
4. **Uncertainty:** Completed-case conditioning may hide group-specific open or abandoned cases. **Most valuable design change:** retain an inception cohort of every arrival with status and censoring date.
5. **Uncertainty:** Appeals are too sparse and post-process dependent to establish quality safety. **Most valuable design change:** a blinded, standardized case-file quality audit.
