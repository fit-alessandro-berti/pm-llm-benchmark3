# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

Mira_Coordinator appears to handle exceptions across teams. She touches about 22% of all claims, 64% of reopened claims, and 71% of complaints. The networks are consistent with a resource who frequently determines or records the next step on a subset of claims, sometimes receives the case back, and has few recurring partners across the whole portfolio. That function could be part of a formal desk, depend on Mira's personal knowledge, or combine both.

## Handover-of-work network

In a directly-follows handover network, an edge records that one resource's event was followed by another resource's event on the same claim. Bot_Rules → Mira, the temporary-staff handoffs into Mira, and Mira's outgoing links to both teams' adjusters and to medical and legal specialists are consistent with an exception-routing hub. Her reported high out-degree and betweenness suggest broad reach and a position between groups in the aggregate graph. The bot-to-bot route and team lead-to-Vendor_Pay routes illustrate how automation and external providers can also shape the network's structure. Event succession alone cannot establish who initiated a transfer, who owned the work, or whether a path through Mira in the aggregate graph occurred on any individual claim.

## Working-together network

This network records whether two resources appear anywhere on the same claim. It has no direction and usually gives each pair one contribution per claim. It can reveal recurring pairs like Alice–Carla and Bruno–Diego, but cannot establish that people communicated, shared ownership, or worked simultaneously. Mira's partners vary by exception and team. A pair can account for a substantial share of her work while appearing on a small share of all claims. Repeated transfers within a claim can raise handover frequency without raising the pair's distinct-case count. A short routing event still counts toward co-occurrence. The explanation therefore depends on which claims and partners Mira touches, how often transfers repeat, and how the scores are normalized.

Every directly observed Mira-to-adjuster handover must occur on a claim where the two co-occur. The published figures need reconciliation before they can quantify this difference. Mira's six listed outbound counts sum to 1,640 and her five listed inbound counts sum to 1,580. Each total exceeds her 960 events, which is impossible for unweighted immediate predecessor and successor counts on the same event slice. If the working-together rates use the roughly 4,100 claims suggested by Mira's coverage, Alice–Carla's 27% would imply about 1,100 shared claims, above Alice's 665 total claims. Confirm the case denominator, observation window, event grain, and edge construction before comparing the strengths or using the centrality ranks for decisions.

## Subcontracting network

With an event lag of two positions, a Mira → Omar or Priya → Mira pattern records a specialist event followed by a return to Mira. The 126 medical and 98 legal patterns are consistent with a brief consultation followed by coordination, and fit the reported links in both directions. The recurring Bot_Rules → Mira → Bot_Rules pattern is consistent with a manual intervention followed by renewed automated processing. Mira's reported subcontracting betweenness describes her position in a graph built from such returns. Formal delegation, supervision, approval authority, and even the fact of an explicit request or response require evidence beyond the sequence. Log order, repeated review, or a required sign-off can produce the same pattern.

## Performance and time statistics

Mira's 4.2-minute median recorded service time is consistent with short recorded actions. The 42- and 51-minute mean gaps from Mira to medical and legal events are inter-event elapsed times. Depending on which lifecycle events were used, those gaps can include queueing, off-system work, specialist service, or delayed recording. They cannot be decomposed by subtracting the specialists' median service times. The six-day absence is stronger evidence of operational exposure than centrality alone. The exception-claim 90th percentile rose from 8.4 to 13.9 days while the ordinary low-value median held steady. One episode leaves open the contribution of arrival mix, concurrent staffing, specialist capacity, external providers, and claims still open at the end of the period.

## Role discovery or activity-profile similarity

Activity profiles could place Mira near staff who handle missing information or leads who handle reopening, while her complaint and specialist-assignment mix could make her profile distinctive. Similarity describes the activities recorded for a resource. It cannot establish job title, skill equivalence, authority, or ability to cover the desk. Compare Mira with other resources on the exception work they are eligible to receive. Check whether a role grouping changes when high-volume automation and external providers are considered separately. Confirm whether “Assign Specialist” is an actual activity, an alias, or shorthand in the scenario, since it is absent from the activity inventory.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

## Formal coordinator or exception desk

A defined desk may receive rule-engine referrals and temporary-staff escalations, request clarification, and dispatch cases using documented criteria. Bot_Rules → Mira, the incoming temporary-staff links, and the concentration in reopenings and complaints support this mechanism. The process owner's “queue cleaner” description fits an operational queue function, while adjusters' reliance on Mira's judgment suggests the procedure may leave meaningful choices to the operator. The log does not resolve how much is prescribed. If this mechanism dominates, future logs should show stable assignment rules for comparable cases, consistent triggers before desk work, and similar route choices and queue performance when a trained substitute covers the desk.

## Hidden bottleneck or key-person dependency

Exceptions may wait for a decision that Mira alone can make efficiently because of specialized knowledge, access rights, or limited backup coverage. Short service times can coexist with a long assignment queue if a scarce resource handles bursts of work. The absence-period tail increase and adjusters' description of Mira support dependence on her availability. A one-off absence cannot identify the constrained stage, and the unchanged routine median narrows the observed effect to the exception side. If this hypothesis holds, unresolved ready-to-route work and time to assignment should rise during comparable gaps in coverage, with the largest increase before specialist dispatch or redispatch. Trained backup coverage should reduce that increase even at similar incoming volume.

## Organizational boundary spanner or triage broker

Mira may use knowledge of team remit, specialist availability, and case history to move difficult claims between otherwise cohesive teams or functions. Referrals to both teams, the selective medical and legal loops, and the North and South co-case clusters fit that mechanism. The interactions could also be prescribed by Bot_Rules or a queue policy, and the log does not directly record the conversations or judgments behind them. If brokerage adds something beyond standard routing, Mira's next destinations should vary with the exception type, team boundary, or specialist availability beyond what static routing rules predict. In comparable cases with comparable coverage, timely broker involvement should be associated with fewer misroutes, repeat transfers, or formal escalations. Severe cases may both attract Mira and escalate more often, so raw outcome comparisons would confound that test.

## Event attribution or recording artifact

A shared queue account, proxy entry, batch posting, or lifecycle duplication could make multiple actors' actions appear under Mira's identifier or inflate apparent transfers and return patterns. The count inconsistencies and the activity-inventory mismatch justify checking these possibilities. They could also result from mixed reporting definitions, and an artifact alone is less consistent with both staff testimony and a measured exception backlog during the absence. If attribution drives much of the pattern, future logs with actor and assignee captured separately, work-item identifiers, and reliable event times should redistribute Mira's edges or materially reduce the return counts after lifecycle consolidation. Evidence of a real routing function and an artifact can coexist.

# 3. Risk assessment

- **Bottleneck risk** is greatest for exception queues and tail turnaround. Locate the delay before assigning it to Mira, specialist capacity, customer document returns, or vendor processing. The lead-to-payment gaps and Vendor_Verify's high waiting-time component make external stages relevant to the same end-to-end outcome.
- **Coordination risk** arises if cases lose context or ownership between Bot_Rules, the desk, adjusters, specialists, and providers. The operations manager's concern that removing the desk could increase formal escalations and specialist overload is plausible. Useful indicators include repeat transfers, rejected referrals, cases without a named owner, and changes in specialists' incoming case mix.
- **Key-person risk** concerns which decisions or permissions only Mira can exercise and what happens during leave or peak demand. Concentrated know-how and inadequate coverage call for different fixes from insufficient staffing across the entire desk.
- **Compliance and control risk** arises if one person can originate an exception and choose a specialist without the review or authorization the process requires. A recorded referral does not identify who originated the flag, which rule applied, or whether an independent review occurred. Trace the bot's rule decision, actor and assignee, routing reason, and any override or approval. The evidence raises a control-design question and does not establish misconduct.
- **Workload fairness risk** includes complex cases and coordination effort being concentrated with Mira or passed disproportionately to particular adjusters or specialists. Compare case mix, follow-ups, active effort, queue duty, and available staffed hours. The temporary workers' escalation patterns could reflect their assignment or authority limits, so event totals and case speed are insufficient for ranking individuals.
- **Data-quality risk** includes proxies or shared accounts, inconsistent flags, missing activities, duplicate lifecycle steps, same-time events, and incomplete bot or vendor milestones. Those issues can change both network paths and time attribution. Establish the provenance of the network tables and reconcile their totals before making staffing or control decisions.

# 4. Further process-mining analyses

1. **Prepare and segment the log.** Use `pm4py.format_dataframe` with the case, activity, and timestamp columns. Preserve raw lifecycle records and create a sequence view with a defined event grain, for example one completion per work item after reliable start/complete pairing. Check resource identities, timestamps, and ordering ties using a source sequence where available. Compare claim-level filtering on `exception_flag`, reopen/complaint membership, and variants using pandas or PM4Py filters while retaining the full traces in each cohort. Use the flag known at routing time for comparisons of routing decisions, and final claim status for descriptive profiles. Remove events only for explicitly defined projections, since dropping intervening events creates new adjacencies.
2. **Rebuild handovers by case and activity pair.** Use `discover_handover_of_work_network` for the graph and a within-case shift in pandas for auditable, immediate-transition counts and distinct-claim counts. Check how much of Mira's reach comes from Bot_Rules entry routes, temporary-staff referrals, repeated missing-information loops, and cross-team or specialist dispatch. Compare exception and routine cohorts, and distinguish observed human-to-human adjacencies from the projected next human event after dropping machine or vendor records. Report neighbor counts and frequency-weighted strengths separately. When computing shortest-path betweenness in NetworkX, document direction and the distance convention and test sensitivity to edge weights and rare ties.
3. **Rebuild co-case networks in the same cohorts.** Use `discover_working_together_network` and count pairs from each claim's set of distinct resources. Report shared-claim counts, rates over all eligible claims, and the fraction of Mira's claims shared with each partner. Compare overlap with the partner's prevalence in the same cohort, accounting for Bot_Rules' broad coverage and the team leads' more localized caseloads. Link the two networks through each pair's distinct shared claims and handovers per shared claim. This tests whether the whole-log result reflects partner dispersion, within-claim repetition, or the original scoring choices.
4. **Audit return patterns.** Use `discover_subcontracting_network` with the stated lag and explicitly enumerate A → B → A fragments with case IDs, activities, and lifecycle transitions. Report episodes and unique claims, plus return rates among eligible A → B handovers with sufficient follow-up. Test wider, clearly defined event and elapsed-time windows and check whether the sequence persists after pairing lifecycle events. Compare specialist review returns with Bot_Rules manual-exception returns, and use queue or work-item records, where available, to verify which returns correspond to actual referrals.
5. **Compare activity profiles.** Use `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` or `discover_organizational_roles` with a transparent resource-by-activity table. Compare raw and normalized profiles for routine and exception cohorts and by team and period. Check how results change when bots and vendors are grouped separately, and when coverage shifts to another staff member. Similar activity mix can identify backup candidates. Observed task eligibility, authorization, and successful coverage are the evidence for substitutability.
6. **Locate queues and evaluate coverage.** Use paired lifecycle timestamps with `get_service_time` for activity service summaries and `insert_case_service_waiting_time` for case-level interval measures. Use queue-entry or readiness, service-start, and completion records to measure separately time to desk assignment, time to specialist start, active review, and time for vendor verification or payment. Treat missing milestones as unobserved and account for overlapping work when attributing case time. Around the six-day absence, compare open work at the start and incoming exception claims with corresponding cohorts in matched periods on day of week, case type, priority, amount band, region, channel, arrivals, and staffing. Define the cohorts from desk eligibility and timing, independent of whether Mira ultimately touched the claim. Check vendor delays and pre-existing trends. Use queue age, backlog, throughput, and median and 90th percentile of cycle time. Include still-open claims through consistent follow-up or censoring-aware estimates. Compare a backup-coverage pilot using the same measures, with ordinary low-value claims as a reference for broader changes.

# 5. Recommendation

- **Change immediately.** Keep the desk operating, designate and cross-train an authorized backup, and pilot coverage with clear ownership of queued exceptions. Document referral criteria and decision rights. Record the flag source, routing reason, destination, and any override, with independent authorization for decisions that require it under existing rules. These steps preserve the coordination function and give management evidence about whether it depends on one person.
- **Monitor.** Review exception arrivals, backlog age, assignment delay, specialist and vendor queue times, referral returns, formal escalations, and median and tail turnaround during normal, absence, and backup periods. Track demand and workload by case mix and staffed capacity. Reconcile the network counts before using changes in their rankings as performance indicators.
- **Defer structural changes.** Wait for stage-level delay evidence and tested backup coverage before eliminating the desk, redistributing all referrals to specialists, hard-coding ambiguous routing decisions, or changing formal authority. Any eventual redesign should specify who or what will recognize exceptions, select an authorized destination, and take ownership when work comes back.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira appears to connect claims teams with specialists when cases need extra handling. She touches about one in five claims overall, nearly two thirds of reopened claims, and seven in ten complaints. Her contacts span several teams and types of work, so any one pairing may be uncommon across all claims. Some reported counts need to be reconciled before the network rankings are used for decisions.

During her six-day absence, typical turnaround for ordinary low-value claims held steady. For exception claims, the time within which 90% were completed rose from 8.4 to 13.9 days. That points to a coverage gap worth testing against staffing and claim mix. Keep the exception desk in place, train a backup, and document who can choose specialists and approve routing overrides. Track how long claims wait for assignment, specialist review, and outside providers so managers can see where delays build.
