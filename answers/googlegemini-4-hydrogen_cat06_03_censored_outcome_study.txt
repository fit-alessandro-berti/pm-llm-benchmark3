## 1. Target definition

### Primary Target Formulation: Decomposed Observed-Policy Multi-State and Conditional Time-to-Default Target
Predicting “90-day default for every application” as a single binary indicator is statistically ill-defined because rejected, withdrawn, transferred, and still-open applications never enter credit exposure (`Account Activated`). Rather than fabricating “non-default” labels for unactivated cases, the primary formulation decomposes the prediction problem at an early-warning landmark $t_p$ into two linked, observable quantities under the prevailing underwriting and operational policy:

1. **Component A — Competing Application Pathway ($Y_{\text{path}}, T_{\text{path}}$):**
   The first terminal/absorbing process state reached strictly after $t_p$:
   $$Y_{\text{path}} \in \{\text{Activated},\ \text{Rejected},\ \text{Withdrawn},\ \text{Transferred}\}$$
   with time-to-resolution $T_{\text{path}}$ measured from $t_p$, where still-open applications are treated as right-censored at the administrative cutoff.
2. **Component B — Conditional Time-to-Default Given Activation ($T_{\text{def}}, \Delta$):**
   Among applications that reach `Account Activated`, time from `Account Activated` to `Default Recorded`, evaluated over horizon $H = 90$ days with right censoring indicator $\Delta \in \{0, 1\}$ accounting for immature follow-up and delayed outcome feeds.

From these two components, the model outputs three operationally transparent quantities at landmark $t_p$:
- Activation propensity: $\hat{p}_{\text{act}}(t_p) = \Pr(Y_{\text{path}} = \text{Activated} \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_p})$
- Conditional 90-day default risk given activation: $\hat{r}_{90 \mid \text{act}}(t_p) = \Pr(T_{\text{def}} \le 90 \mid Y_{\text{path}} = \text{Activated}, \mathcal{F}_{t_p})$
- **Observed-policy joint default incidence**: $\hat{p}_{\text{joint}}(t_p) = \hat{p}_{\text{act}}(t_p) \cdot \hat{r}_{90 \mid \text{act}}(t_p) = \Pr(Y_{\text{path}} = \text{Activated},\ T_{\text{def}} \le 90 \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_p})$

### Specification of Elements
- **Prediction time ($t_p$):** Evaluated at a causal prefix landmark—either fixed prefix length $k$ (e.g., $k = 3$ events, typically around `Identity/KYC Check` or initial `Credit Review`) or fixed elapsed time $\tau$ (e.g., 24 hours after `Application Started`)—strictly prior to any credit decision (`Accepted`, `Rejected`, `Withdrawn`, `Transferred`).
- **Horizon:** Up to process resolution for $Y_{\text{path}}$ (or a 60-day application-resolution window), and $90$ days post-`Account Activated` for default.
- **Unit of analysis:** One credit application (`case:concept:name`).
- **Population:** All credit applications started in the observation window that remain active and pre-decision at landmark $t_p$.
- **Estimand:** Policy-conditional cumulative incidence of activation and conditional 90-day default probability given activation (and their joint product) under the realized underwriting, operational, and collection regime.

### Alternative Targets and Why They Answer Different Questions
1. **Activated-Only Conditional 90-Day Default (with Right Censoring):** Modeling only $\Pr(T_{\text{def}} \le 90 \mid \text{Activated}, \mathcal{F}_{t_p})$ answers: *“Among applicants who will ultimately be approved and activated, who defaults?”* It cannot be interpreted as application-level default risk at $t_p$ because it conditions on a future post-$t_p$ collider (`Activated`), distorting calibration when deployed on all incoming applications.
2. **Policy-Specific Counterfactual Default Risk ($\Pr(D_{90}^{\text{do(Activate=1)}} = 1 \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_p})$):** Answers: *“What would the 90-day default rate be if every applicant—including currently rejected or withdrawn ones—were activated?”* While this directly informs credit-policy expansion, it is **not non-parametrically identifiable** from historical observational logs because rejected cases experience zero credit exposure (violating positivity in deterministic reject regions) and requires strong untestable ignorability or reject-inference assumptions.

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

### Distinguishing the Five Non-Observation Mechanisms
1. **Ordinary Right Censoring:** Occurs in Component A for the 5,100 still-open applications (censored at `T_cutoff`), and in Component B for activated accounts whose follow-up duration $T_{\text{cutoff}} - t_{\text{act}}$ is shorter than 90 days without a prior default. Under non-informative administrative timing, standard survival likelihoods remain unbiased.
2. **Delayed Outcome Labels (7–35 Day Reporting Lag):** Because the external feed arrives 7–35 days after day 90, simply filtering on `label_available_at <= T_cutoff` creates **length and reporting-delay bias**—especially if defaults are reported at a different speed than non-defaults, or if recent cohorts appear artificially sparse. We therefore enforce an administrative censoring time $C_i = \max(0,\ \min(90,\ T_{\text{cutoff}} - L_{\text{buf}} - t_{\text{act}, i}))$ using a conservative lag buffer $L_{\text{buf}} = 35$ days (or explicitly estimate reporting-delay-adjusted exposure).
3. **Selection Through Activation:** Rejected (9,100) and withdrawn (5,700) cases have no credit exposure; their default outcome under activation does not exist in the observed regime. Treating them as censored non-defaults or dropping them without modeling Component A biases risk estimates when generalizing to early prefixes.
4. **Transfer as a Competing Event / Missing Follow-Up:** Transferred cases (2,900) leave the focal origination process. Even though ~60% can be linked to another platform ID, those cases undergo different underwriting, pricing, and servicing policies, while ~40% are unlinked. Pooling linked transfers introduces selection and policy-heterogeneity bias.
5. **Permanently Missing Outcome Feeds:** Partner-originated activated accounts exhibit elevated permanent non-reporting even well beyond $90 + 35$ days. This is **not** ordinary right censoring—it is missingness correlated with `source_system` and `case:channel`, and potentially unmeasured partner risk (MAR or MNAR).

### Primary Analysis Strategy
- **Component A (Competing Risks):** Fit cause-specific Cox / gradient-boosted hazard models (or Fine–Gray subdistribution models) for $\{\text{Activated}, \text{Rejected}, \text{Withdrawn}, \text{Transferred}\}$ on all active cases at $t_p$, treating the 5,100 still-open cases as administratively right-censored at $T_{\text{cutoff}}$.
- **Component B (Right-Censored Survival + IPCW for Missing Feeds):** On activated cases, model time-to-default $T_{\text{def}} \in (0, 90]$ using a survival model (e.g., time-varying Cox or discrete-time survival hazard). Exclude accounts activated within the last $L_{\text{buf}} = 35$ days from immature target confirmation unless censored at $C_i = 0$; for accounts past $90 + 35$ days whose feed is permanently missing, fit a missingness propensity model $\hat{\pi}_{\text{obs}}(X) = \Pr(\text{Feed Observed} \mid \text{Activated}, \text{Mature}, \text{Channel}, \text{SourceSystem}, X_{t_p})$ and apply stabilized Inverse Probability Weights $w_i = 1 / \hat{\pi}_{\text{obs}}(X_i)$ (with weight clipping, e.g., at the 99th percentile).

### Sensitivity Analyses
1. **Pattern-Mixture / Extreme Bounds for Permanently Missing Partner Feeds & Unlinked Transfers:** Recompute overall and partner-channel default rates assuming missing partner feeds default at $0.5\times$, $1.0\times$, $2.0\times$ the observed partner rate, plus worst-case Manski bounds (0% vs. 100% default on unlinked/missing feeds).
2. **Policy-Discontinuity Selection Sensitivity (July 1, 2025 Shift):** Near the credit-policy threshold change, borderline applicants who were rejected pre-July 1 may be activated post-July 1 (or vice versa). Compare conditional default curves in the local `risk_score` overlap region across regimes, and apply trimmed inverse-activation weights $\hat{p}_{\text{act}}^{-1}$ to bound selection sensitivity.
3. **Differential Feed-Lag Audit:** Among historical mature activations, test whether `label_available_at - (t_act + 90d)` differs systematically between `default_90d = 1` and `default_90d = 0`.

### Testability of Assumptions
- **Testable:** Covariate shift across the July 1 policy change and Oct 15 migration; feed-lag distributions and differential reporting delay between defaults and non-defaults on historical mature cohorts; dependence of missing outcome feeds on `case:channel`, `source_system`, and prefix covariates.
- **Partially Testable:** Ignorability of activation selection in the *overlap region* around the July 1 threshold shift; conditional independence of transfer linkage (~60% vs. ~40%) on observed prefix variables.
- **Fundamentally Untestable:** Counterfactual default rate of deterministically rejected cases (positivity violation); MNAR mechanisms in permanently missing partner feeds (unobserved partner quality); true outcome distribution of the 40% unlinked transfers.

## 4. Prefix extraction

### Causal Prefix Construction Rules
For each case, events are first canonicalized (mapping post-`2025-10-15` renamed activities to unified labels and deduplicating migration double-writes), then ordered deterministically by `(time:timestamp, canonical_activity_order, original_row_index)`. Two complementary prefix schemes are supported:

1. **Fixed Event-Count Prefixes ($k \in \{2, 3, 4\}$):**
   Retain strictly the first $k$ events $\{e_1, \dots, e_k\}$, setting the prediction landmark to $t_p = t(e_k)$. Any case that reached a terminal or decision event (`Accepted`, `Rejected`, `Withdrawn`, `Transferred`, `Account Activated`) at index $j \le k$ is **excluded** from the prediction risk set at landmark $k$, because its outcome decision has already occurred.
2. **Fixed Elapsed-Time Prefixes ($\tau \in \{4\text{h}, 24\text{h}, 72\text{h}\}$):**
   Retain all events satisfying $t(e) \le t_{\text{start}} + \tau$, setting $t_p = t_{\text{start}} + \tau$. Cases that terminated before $t_{\text{start}} + \tau$ are excluded from the risk set at $\tau$. This scheme mirrors real-time SLA triggers and avoids conditioning on pace of processing.

### Handling Edge Cases
- **Short Cases ($< k$ events):** Under fixed-$k$ extraction, standard `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(log, length=k)` returns all events of traces shorter than $k$. If a trace has $< k$ events because it terminated early (e.g., quick KYC rejection at event 2), leaving it in the $k=3$ dataset leaks its early termination. We therefore strictly require `prefix_length == k` *and* absence of any terminal/decision activity within those $k$ events. Traces shorter than $k$ only because they are still open at `T_cutoff` have not yet reached landmark $k$ and are scored at earlier landmarks ($k'<k$) or under elapsed-time $\tau$.
- **Repeated Document Loops:** `Credit Review -> Request Documents -> Credit Review` loops can repeat multiple times. We do **not** collapse these loops into a single event because loop frequency and duration carry genuine friction/risk signal. In fixed-$k$, loops consume prefix budget and act as a signal of stalled underwriting; in fixed-$\tau$, loop counts within $\tau$ hours capture early document insufficiency.
- **Activity Renaming & Duplicate Events (15 Oct 2025):** Apply a versioned canonical mapping dictionary prior to prefixing (e.g., mapping `KYC_Verification_v2` $\rightarrow$ `Identity/KYC Check`). Collapse consecutive identical `(case_id, canonical_activity, org:resource)` rows occurring within a migration-artifact window (e.g., $\Delta t \le 2\text{s}$ on/after `2025-10-15`), while keeping legitimate repeated reviews separated by real time.

| **Resource & Queue / WIP** | Historical-reference (strictly $< t_p$) | Distinct resources touched in prefix; count of resource handoffs; causal resource experience = count of events handled by `org:resource` strictly *prior* to $t(e_i)$; system WIP at $t_p$ = count of other open applications in `Credit Review` at $t_p$; rolling 7-day application inflow and median KYC duration prior to $t_p$. |
| **Training-Fold Process Structure** | Historical-reference (train prefixes only) | DFG transition probability / frequency of observed prefix edges from `pm4py.discover_dfg` on **training truncated prefixes**; prefix variant frequency / unseen-variant flag from `pm4py.get_variants` on **training truncated prefixes**. |
| **Cohort / Calendar Context** | Static / As-of $t_p$ | Policy regime indicator (`t_p >= 2025-07-01`), system migration indicator (`t_p >= 2025-10-15`)—used for regime-aware modeling, monitoring, and stratification. |

## 6. Leakage audit

| Feature or data element | Leakage or bias risk | Valid availability rule | Keep/repair/drop | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `final_limit` | Direct target/decision leakage: populated only after final credit decision, after $t_p$. | Available only post-`Accepted`/`Rejected`. | **Drop** | Never observed at early-warning landmark $t_p$; encodes underwriting outcome. |
| `decision_reason` | Direct decision leakage: populated at or after `Accepted`/`Rejected`. | Available only at decision timestamp ($> t_p$). | **Drop** | Directly reveals whether the case was accepted or rejected and why. |
| `collection_status` | Post-activation outcome leakage: updated after activation and directly reveals delinquency/default. | Available only post-`Account Activated` ($> t_p$). | **Drop** | Proxy/direct indicator of the target `default_90d` itself. |
| Future events (`Contract Signed`, `Account Activated`, `Collections Contact`, `Default Recorded`, post-$t_p$ reviews) | Temporal horizon leakage: directly reveal pathway and default outcome. | Strictly `event_time <= t_p`. | **Drop** (post-$t_p$ events) | Must truncate log strictly at $t_p$ before any feature or DFG/variant computation. |
| `documents_total` | Same-day snapshot leakage: nightly batch can include documents uploaded later on the same calendar day as $t_p$. | Only trust if `snapshot_date < date(t_p)` (prior-day EOD), or reconstruct from timestamped document events $\le t_p$. | **Repair or Drop** | Prior-day EOD snapshot is safe (`date < date(t_p)`); same-day snapshot leaks future intraday uploads. |
| Updated `risk_score` | Forward-update leakage if taking the case's final/latest `risk_score` from the end of the trace. | Use only `risk_score` value recorded on the latest event with `time:timestamp <= t_p`. | **Repair** | Prefix-valid `risk_score` at $\le t_p$ is an informative causal signal; post-$t_p$ updates are leakage. |
| Resource experience | Extraction-time look-ahead leakage: reflects tenure/volume accumulated after the event up to `2026-02-15`. | Reconstruct causally as cumulative events/cases handled by `org:resource` strictly before `time:timestamp` of that event. | **Repair** (or Drop raw) | Using extraction-time experience leaks future employee retention and volume into past predictions. |
| Label availability (`default_90d`, `label_available_at`, `data_extract_time`) | Target-maturity & backtest leakage: using labels in training whose `label_available_at > T_train_cutoff`, or treating `NaN` as non-default. | Only observe `default_90d` in training/evaluation at cutoff $T$ when `label_available_at <= T` and follow-up satisfies maturity/censoring rules. | **Repair** (audit/censor) | Prevents look-ahead into delayed outcome feeds and separates censoring from true `0`s. |
| Post-migration activity names & duplicates (`>= 2025-10-15`) | Concept/schema artifact & temporal leakage if model learns new activity names as time proxies. | Canonicalize activity names and deduplicate migration double-writes prior to splitting/prefixing. | **Repair** | Prevents spurious split across pre/post migration processes. |

## 7. Validation design

### Temporal Validation and Backtesting Scheme
We implement an **expanding-window temporal validation** ordered strictly by `case_start_time`, enforcing an **as-of label availability cutoff** (`label_available_at <= T_train_cutoff`) for each training fold so no model is trained on delayed outcomes that would not yet have arrived in production:

1. **Train Window (Pre-Policy Baseline):** Cases started `2024-01-01` to `2025-03-31`, evaluated as-of `T_train_cutoff = 2025-06-30` (ensures mature 90d+feed labels for early activations and proper administrative censoring for recent ones).
2. **Validation Window (Pre-Policy Maturity & Tuning):** Cases started `2025-04-01` to `2025-06-30` (evaluated using mature outcomes up to `2025-11-30` for tuning, or as-of `2025-09-30` in rolling real-time simulation).
3. **Temporal Stress Test 1 — Policy Change Regime:** Cases started `2025-07-01` to `2025-10-14` (tests robustness to the 1 July 2025 threshold shift, where both $\Pr(\text{Activated} \mid X)$ and conditional default calibration shift).
4. **Temporal Stress Test 2 — Post-Migration & Right Censoring:** Cases started `2025-10-15` to `2025-12-31`, evaluated against extraction `2026-02-15` (tests canonicalized activity mapping, deduplication, and censoring-aware evaluation on immature/open cases).

### Why a Random Case Split Is Misleading
A random trace split (such as `pm4py.split_train_test`) mixes pre- and post-July 2025 credit policies and pre- and post-October 2025 system migrations across train and test sets. This hides policy-induced concept drift, leaks future macro/queue conditions and resource-history distributions into training, artificially inflates discrimination, and completely masks calibration collapse across regime shifts.

### Evaluation Metrics, Calibration, Subgroups, and Drift Monitoring
- **Component A (Competing Pathways):** Cause-specific time-dependent AUROC, cause-specific AUPRC, and **IPCW-weighted Brier Score** (accounting for the 5,100 right-censored still-open cases).
- **Component B (Conditional 90-Day Default):** **Uno’s C-index (IPCW time-dependent concordance)**, **IPCW time-dependent AUROC at day 90**, and **Integrated Brier Score (IBS)** over $[0, 90]$ days, weighted for both administrative censoring and missing partner feeds.
- **Calibration & Decision Quality:** Expected Calibration Error (ECE), reliability curves, and calibration slope/intercept reported separately for $\hat{p}_{\text{act}}$, $\hat{r}_{90\mid\text{act}}$, and $\hat{p}_{\text{joint}}$ across pre- vs. post-July 2025 cohorts.
- **Subgroup & Shift Checks:** Disaggregated metrics across `case:channel` (specifically partner vs. direct channels, reporting missing-feed coverage alongside error bounds), `case:region`, `case:requested_amount` quartiles, and rework status. Monitor covariate drift (PSI on static/prefix features and variant distributions) and selection drift ($\hat{p}_{\text{act}}$ shift post-July 1).

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

### What the Study Can and Cannot Claim
- **Can claim:** Well-calibrated, leakage-free early-warning rankings and probability estimates for (1) application pathway completion and (2) conditional 90-day default given activation under the observed underwriting and operational regime, complete with censoring and missing-feed uncertainty intervals.
- **Cannot claim:** Causal estimates of what would happen if rejected or withdrawn applicants were forcibly approved; policy-invariant creditworthiness independent of granted `final_limit`, pricing, or collections intensity; or exact default rates on unlinked transfers and MNAR missing partner feeds beyond sensitivity bounds.

### Predictive Association vs. Selection Effects vs. Counterfactual Default Risk
- **Predictive association:** Statistical correlation between early process dynamics (e.g., KYC delays, document loops, early `risk_score`) and realized outcomes under existing operations.
- **Selection effects:** Conditioning on `Account Activated` creates collider/selection distortion—an application that barely passed a stricter post-policy underwriting screen differs systematically on unmeasured traits from one that passed an earlier lenient screen.
- **Counterfactual default risk:** $\Pr(D_{90}^{\text{do(Activate=1)}} = 1 \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_p})$ requires positivity and unconfoundedness (or randomized approval exploration / quasi-experimental discontinuities), which do not hold across the entire applicant pool.

### How Deployment Changes the Data-Generating Process (Feedback Loops)
1. **Underwriting / Approval Policy Feedback:** If underwriters or automated gates use the early-warning score to reject or reduce limits for flagged applicants, previously defaulted profiles shift into `Rejected` (unobserved default), degrading future calibration and shrinking overlap.
2. **Operational Triage & Rework Acceleration:** Routing high-risk prefixes to manual verification or triggering extra `Request Documents` loops alters prefix durations, resource assignments, and increases applicant `Withdrawn` rates (shifting competing risks).
3. **Pre-Delinquency Collections Intervention:** Using early-warning scores to trigger proactive reminders or early `Collections Contact` post-activation can prevent or delay `Default Recorded` beyond day 90, directly mutating the target definition.
4. **Channel / Broker Adaptation:** Partner channels aware of early flags may alter application timing or pre-screen submissions, inducing covariate and missingness shift.

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

# 1. Load event log & parse timestamps (no post-cutoff inspection)
df = pd.read_csv("applications_events.csv")
for col in ["time:timestamp", "label_available_at", "data_extract_time"]:
    df[col] = pd.to_datetime(df[col], utc=True, errors="coerce")

# 2. Harmonize post-2025-10-15 renamed activities & deduplicate migration double-writes
ACTIVITY_MAP = {
    "KYC_Verification_v2": "Identity/KYC Check",
    "Credit_Assessment_v2": "Credit Review",
    "Doc_Request_v2": "Request Documents",
    "Account_Active_v2": "Account Activated",
}
df["concept:name"] = df["concept:name"].replace(ACTIVITY_MAP)
df = df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", "concept:name"]).reset_index(drop=True)
prev_t = df.groupby(["case:concept:name", "concept:name", "org:resource"])["time:timestamp"].shift(1)
is_mig_dup = (df["time:timestamp"] >= "2025-10-15") & ((df["time:timestamp"] - prev_t).dt.total_seconds() <= 2)
df = df.loc[~is_mig_dup].copy()

# 3. Format with PM4Py standard event-log schema
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df, case_id="case:concept:name", activity_key="concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
)

def build_case_lifecycle(events_df: pd.DataFrame, as_of_cutoff: pd.Timestamp, lag_buffer_days: int = 35):
    """Build as-of cohort status, censoring, and label maturity strictly <= as_of_cutoff."""
    ev = events_df[events_df["time:timestamp"] <= as_of_cutoff].copy()
    start_t = ev.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min().rename("case_start_time")
    act_t = ev[ev["concept:name"] == "Account Activated"].groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min().rename("act_time")
    term_map = {"Account Activated": "Activated", "Rejected": "Rejected", "Withdrawn": "Withdrawn", "Transferred": "Transferred"}
    term_ev = ev[ev["concept:name"].isin(term_map)].sort_values("time:timestamp").groupby("case:concept:name").first()
    meta = ev.groupby("case:concept:name").agg(
        channel=("case:channel", "first"),
        source_system=("source_system", "first"),
        default_90d=("default_90d", "last"),
        label_available_at=("label_available_at", "max"),
    )
    cases = pd.concat([start_t, act_t, meta], axis=1)
    cases["pathway"] = term_ev["concept:name"].map(term_map).reindex(cases.index).fillna("Open")
    cases["censor_time_path"] = term_ev["time:timestamp"].reindex(cases.index).fillna(as_of_cutoff)
    # Maturity: label must have arrived by cutoff AND 90d + feed buffer check applies
    cases["feed_observed"] = cases["default_90d"].notna() & (cases["label_available_at"] <= as_of_cutoff)
    days_since_act = (as_of_cutoff - cases["act_time"]).dt.total_seconds() / 86400.0
    cases["followup_days_buffered"] = np.clip(days_since_act - lag_buffer_days, 0.0, 90.0)
    cases["comp_b_status"] = np.where(
        cases["pathway"] != "Activated",
        "Selectively_Unobserved_" + cases["pathway"],
        np.where(cases["feed_observed"], "Labeled_Mature",
                 np.where(days_since_act >= 90 + lag_buffer_days, "Missing_Feed_Mature", "Censored_Immature"))
    )
    return ev, cases

AS_OF_CUTOFF = pd.Timestamp("2026-02-15T23:59:59Z")
ev_asof, case_table = build_case_lifecycle(df, AS_OF_CUTOFF)
print("Cohort counts:\n", case_table["comp_b_status"].value_counts(dropna=False))

# 4. Temporal split by case_start_time (Note: pm4py.split_train_test is random and NOT used here)
train_ids = case_table.index[case_table["case_start_time"] < "2025-04-01"]
val_ids = case_table.index[(case_table["case_start_time"] >= "2025-04-01") & (case_table["case_start_time"] < "2025-07-01")]
test_policy_ids = case_table.index[(case_table["case_start_time"] >= "2025-07-01") & (case_table["case_start_time"] < "2025-10-15")]
test_mig_ids = case_table.index[case_table["case_start_time"] >= "2025-10-15"]

# 5. Fixed-k prefix extraction without future leakage (drop post-decision/outcome columns first)
LEAKY_COLS = ["final_limit", "decision_reason", "collection_status", "default_90d", "label_available_at", "data_extract_time"]
PRE_DECISION_STOP = {"Accepted", "Rejected", "Withdrawn", "Transferred", "Account Activated", "Collections Contact", "Default Recorded"}
PREFIX_K = 3

def extract_causal_prefixes(events_df: pd.DataFrame, case_ids: pd.Index, k: int) -> pd.DataFrame:
    sub = events_df[events_df["case:concept:name"].isin(case_ids)].drop(columns=[c for c in LEAKY_COLS if c in events_df.columns]).copy()
    # PM4Py prefix truncation: keeps up to k first events per trace
    pref = pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(sub, length=k)
    # Exclude short traces (< k) AND any trace that already hit a decision/terminal activity at or before k
    counts = pref.groupby("case:concept:name")["concept:name"].transform("count")
    has_terminal = pref["concept:name"].isin(PRE_DECISION_STOP).groupby(pref["case:concept:name"]).transform("any")
    return pref[(counts == k) & (~has_terminal)].copy()

train_pref = extract_causal_prefixes(ev_asof, train_ids, PREFIX_K)
val_pref = extract_causal_prefixes(ev_asof, val_ids, PREFIX_K)

# 6. Training-only PM4Py process-aware artifacts (never fit on validation/test or full traces)
train_dfg, train_starts, train_ends = pm4py.discover_dfg(train_pref)
train_variants = pm4py.get_variants(train_pref)

# 7. Assemble prefix feature table + causal resource experience (strictly <= t_p)
# Assumption: resource experience is reconstructed cumulatively from prior events in ev_asof, not extraction metadata.
ev_asof["causal_res_exp"] = ev_asof.groupby("org:resource").cumcount()
train_pref = train_pref.merge(ev_asof[["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", "concept:name", "causal_res_exp"]].drop_duplicates(),
                              on=["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", "concept:name"], how="left")

features = train_pref.groupby("case:concept:name").agg(
    t_pred=("time:timestamp", "max"),
    t_start=("time:timestamp", "min"),
    channel=("case:channel", "first"),
    req_amount=("case:requested_amount", "first"),
    risk_init=("risk_score", "first"),
    risk_latest=("risk_score", "last"),
    doc_loops=("concept:name", lambda s: int((s == "Request Documents").sum())),
    res_exp_mean=("causal_res_exp", "mean"),
)
features["elapsed_hours"] = (features["t_pred"] - features["t_start"]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0
features["risk_delta"] = features["risk_latest"] - features["risk_init"]

# 8. Automated leakage audit check
assert not any(c in features.columns for c in LEAKY_COLS), "Leakage column detected in feature matrix!"
max_ev_time = train_pref.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].max()
assert (max_ev_time <= features["t_pred"]).all(), "Post-landmark event found in prefix features!"
assert not train_pref["concept:name"].isin(PRE_DECISION_STOP).any(), "Post-decision event leaked into prefix!"
