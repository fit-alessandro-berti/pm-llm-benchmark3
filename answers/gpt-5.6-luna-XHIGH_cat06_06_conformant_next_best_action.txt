## 2. Counterfactual continuations

**1. If the new screen resolves the fuzzy match and a refreshed risk score falls below 70, the Rule 6 second-review trigger may end; the case still needs the applicable analyst decision and a valid approval before execution. If the score remains at least 70 or the match remains unresolved, S2 must complete the second review. The likely outcomes after that review are approval followed by execution, rejection, or a hold while a Suspicious Activity Report is filed. The stated 0.55–0.75 approval estimate is only observational and may not transport to this intervention; review duration and the new screening result are the principal uncertainties.

**2. C alone.** The validated evidence goes through the required analyst continuation, then S2—who must not be R17—independently reviews the evidence because the current score is 72 and the fuzzy match is unresolved. S2 may support approval, rejection, or a hold/report. Only the approval branch can lead to execution, and a report by itself does not authorize execution. The main uncertainty is whether the 35–75 minute review and 5–15 minute recording interval fit inside the remaining deadline.

**3. D sequentially.** In 15–30 minutes, the alternate screening may resolve the fuzzy match, leave it unresolved, or reveal a stronger match. A resolved match plus a refreshed score below 70 could remove the Rule 6 second-review trigger, subject to the ordinary analyst path. If either trigger remains, an independent S2 or S4 review is still mandatory. A stronger match makes rejection or a report-related hold more likely; neither a clear screen nor a filed report by itself authorizes execution. The uncertain S4 arrival time is the key intervention uncertainty.

**4. F.** If the evidence and policy threshold for rejection are documented as sufficient, reject in 10–20 minutes and close the execution branch. If that threshold is not satisfied, the action is not currently valid and the case must return to C/D/E rather than being rejected merely because the match is unresolved. The uncertainty is not the process rule but whether the available evidence meets the unstated rejection standard; false rejection could therefore be substantial.

**5. E.** Requesting evidence starts another Evidence Requested → Evidence Received → Evidence Validated → Analyst Review cycle. If the evidence resolves the match and materially lowers risk, the case may proceed to the applicable approval path; if not, it may be rejected or held/reporting may occur. Because the evidence may take 6–24 hours, the current deadline is almost certainly lost. A Suspicious Activity Report must not be disclosed to the customer.

## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

Conformance is a feasibility gate, not just a small negative term in an expected-value calculation. The immediate operational upside is €20,000–€35,000, while false rejection or prolonged hold could cost €15,000–€80,000. Those figures justify fast, information-preserving review, but they do not authorize an action that the process forbids.

The downside of unlawful execution is plausibly €100,000–€500,000 or more, with asymmetric regulatory, audit, customer, and reputational consequences that are not fully monetizable. That exposure dominates the direct deadline benefit as a risk-control argument, especially because the current risk score is still 72 and the fuzzy sanctions match remains unresolved. Thus A and B cannot be promoted by assuming that execution is likely to be profitable.

C offers the best balance among currently available conformant choices: it preserves a meaningful chance of timely execution while keeping the required independent control. D can improve information quality, but its sequential form consumes 15–30 minutes before review and can turn a potentially recoverable deadline into an almost certain miss. Running the screen in parallel improves its value, provided it does not delay S2. F becomes attractive only when the rejection threshold is established; otherwise it replaces uncertainty about an unlawful clear with uncertainty about an unsupported rejection. E is safest for evidence quality but is too slow for this deadline. G has no calculable benefit until it is genuinely enabled.

The 0.55–0.75 approval prediction should be used as a scenario range, not as a causal probability. The prompt expressly warns that intervention can change the estimate, and the stale 12-minute-old score, review-duration range, screening result, and S4 availability all widen uncertainty.

## 4. Conformance constraints

- **A:** Rule 6 requires Second Officer Review for a risk score of at least 70 or an unresolved fuzzy match; both conditions currently hold. Rules 8 and 9 also require a valid approval path before execution. Rule 10 cannot rescue A because neither director authorization nor an incident identifier exists. The current evidence cycle may also require a return to Analyst Review under Rule 5.
- **B:** R17 cannot be both the first analyst and the second officer. That violates Rule 7 even if the nominal review is recorded. Rules 6, 8, and 9 would still not be satisfied by the resulting execution.
- **C:** C is conformant if S2 independently reviews the current validated evidence, S2 is different from R17, any required post-evidence Analyst Review is completed, and approval precedes execution. The risk score and unresolved match make Rule 6 applicable.
- **D:** Alternate screening is permissible and may improve the evidence, but it is not a substitute for Rule 6's second review while the score is at least 70 or the match remains unresolved. If the new screening supports a refreshed score below 70 and resolves the match, the case follows the remaining ordinary analyst/approval rules; otherwise S2 or S4 must independently review.
- **E:** E follows Rule 5 and is conformant. After validation, the case must return to Analyst Review, and Rule 6 still applies if either trigger remains. No execution is permitted without the resulting valid approval path.
- **F:** Rule 8 permits rejection after the applicable review process, and Rule 6 specifically imposes the second review before approval or execution, not necessarily before a properly supported rejection. F is therefore usable only if the evidence and governing policy actually establish the documented rejection threshold; the prompt does not establish that fact. Any SAR information remains subject to Rule 11 and must not be disclosed to the customer.
- **G:** Rule 10 requires both Compliance Director authorization and a valid, created, and justified incident identifier. Neither condition currently holds, so G is not an enabled execution path. Even if enabled, the authorization's scope must be checked; it cannot be assumed to waive unrelated controls or the SAR non-disclosure rule.

Rule 3's Auto Clear route is unavailable because the case has a risk score far above 40 and an unresolved fuzzy match. The fact that the destination is an established supplier does not remove the explicit Rule 6 triggers.

## 5. Resource spillovers

S2 is immediately available only by interrupting P-901 for about 45 minutes, increasing P-901's breach probability by 0.12–0.28. That is a real cost, but it buys the only currently available path that can start the required review immediately. The choice should be recorded as a cross-case prioritization decision rather than treated as free capacity.

S4 avoids that spillover but is expected only in 35–65 minutes. Once S4 starts, the second review plus recording takes another 40–90 minutes at the stated bounds. If S4 is reliably available in **35 minutes**, running the alternate screen now and waiting for S4 can become the preferred implementation, because the screen should finish before or around arrival and P-901 is protected. It is still not guaranteed to meet the 90-minute deadline, so C/S2 remains the safer deadline-preserving fallback if S4's commitment or review duration is uncertain.

If S4 is not available until **65 minutes**, only about 25 minutes remain at the moment S4 arrives, less than the stated minimum 40 minutes for review and recording. Waiting for S4 should then fall below immediate C/S2, and the S2 interruption is justified if retaining a chance of timely execution is important. A sequential D→S4 plan also drops sharply in rank at that timing; D should be run only in parallel with an already-started conformant review, if at all.

The alternate vendor has only three expedited calls left today. One call is reasonable when its result can materially inform S2 or a refreshed risk assessment, but the quota should not be exhausted on repeated screens that cannot replace the mandatory review. Seeking G may consume 20–60 minutes of director time and the case's remaining decision window; it can be attempted without delaying C, but it must be abandoned as an execution basis unless both required conditions become real and documented.
