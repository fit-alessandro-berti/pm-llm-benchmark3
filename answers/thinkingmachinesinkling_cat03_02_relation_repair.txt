## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

**Hard invariants** (violating them makes the dataset structurally invalid, not just implausible):

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

### Bundle A: Conservative / minimum-change repair
**Philosophy**: Add only relations supported by direct event-object references (`scanner_ref`, `carrier_ref` with best match, event semantics). Leave ambiguous aggregations (invoice multi-order, payment settlement, delivery confirmation truncation) open. Do not use arithmetic equality to close financial loops.

- **ev08 and PK-8**: Accept `P1` (E2O `ev08`  `PK-8`). Accept `P2` (O2O `PK-8`  `IT-B1`) **provisionally**, because `Pack Item` semantics require a package partner and `PK-8` is the only plausible container given `scanner_ref`. We do not treat `P2` as independently confirmed.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y**: Accept `P3` (`ev10`  `DEL-Y`) because `N-421?` is the closest match (`DEL-Y`). Accept `P4` (`DEL-Y` carries `PK-8`) as the structural mirror, with explicit awareness that `P4` follows `P3`.
- **ev11 and the delivery objects**: **Leave unresolved** (`P5` and `P6` not added; `P7` rejected). The truncated `N-42*` and dual-package content of `ORD-A` (`IT-A1` in `PK-7`/`DEL-X`, `IT-A2` in `PK-8`/`DEL-Y`) make any exclusive or even dual assignment speculative. Per rule 5, the portal may have lost the carrier identifier entirely; forcing a link risks inventing false delivery confirmation.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B**: **Do not add** (`P8` and `P9` rejected for this bundle). The invoice amount discrepancy (`230` vs `ORD-A` `130`) is explained by fees/taxes or discounts. Adding `ORD-B` solely on arithmetic over-repairs the financial object graph.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44**: **Leave unresolved** (`P10` and `P11` not added; `P13` remains open). `PAY-2` is retained as an event-linked `Payment` with no `Invoice` relation, consistent with unapplied cash (rule 7). This avoids false financial closure.

**Coherence check (Bundle A)**:
- `ORD-A`: `Create`  `Add Item` (×2)  `Pack` (`PK-7`, `PK-8`)  `Dispatch` (`DEL-X`, `DEL-Y`)  `Confirm Delivery` (portal-only, no delivery object)  `Issue Invoice` (`INV-44`, `ORD-A` only)  `Record Payment` (`PAY-1` settles `INV-44`). Lifecycle is complete except for explicit delivery-event pairing and partial invoice/payment closure.
- `ORD-B`: `Create`  `Add Item`  `Pack` (`IT-B1` into `PK-8`)  `Dispatch` (`PK-8` via `DEL-Y`). After dispatch, `ORD-B` has no `Confirm Delivery`, no `Invoice`, and no `Payment`. This is a **truncated but not impossible** lifecycle: an order can be shipped and paid via a consolidated invoice (unobserved here) or remain open.
- `PK-8`: `Pack` (`IT-A2`, then `IT-B1`)  `Dispatch` (`DEL-Y`). Lifecycle is structurally sound if `P1`/`P2` hold.
- `DEL-Y`: `Dispatch` (`PK-8`)  `carries` (`PK-8`). Minimal but valid.
- `PAY-2`: Only `Record Payment` event. Open lifecycle—acceptable.

### Bundle B: Aggregated lifecycle / full-closure interpretation
**Philosophy**: Treat matching amounts, sequential references, and shared customer as sufficient to close loops, accepting the risk of arithmetic over-determination in exchange for fully connected object lifecycles.

- **ev08 and PK-8**: Same as Bundle A (`P1`, `P2`).
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y**: Same as Bundle A (`P3`, `P4`).
- **ev11 and the delivery objects**: **Accept both `P5` and `P6`** (`ev11` relates to both `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y`). Rationale: `ORD-A` spans both packages (`IT-A1` in `PK-7`/`DEL-X`, `IT-A2` in `PK-8`/`DEL-Y`). A portal confirmation for `ORD-A` plausibly acknowledges both deliveries together. We do **not** assign exclusively (`P7` rejected).
- **INV-44 and ORD-B**: **Accept `P8` and `P9`** (`ev12` includes `ORD-B`; `INV-44` bills `ORD-B`). Rationale: `230` equals the sum of both declared values; same customer; single invoice event suggests aggregation. We acknowledge this is heuristic, not proof.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44**: **Accept `P10` and `P11`** (`ev14` settles `INV-44`; `PAY-2` settles `INV-44`). Rationale: `100 + 130 = 230`; sequential bank references; both payments are the only ones in the log.

**Coherence check (Bundle B)**:
- `ORD-B` now connects to `INV-44` and, via `PAY-1`/`PAY-2`, participates in a fully paid invoice. However, `ORD-B` still lacks a direct `Confirm Delivery` event; only `DEL-Y` (via `PK-8`) is implicitly covered through `ev11`’s bulk delivery confirmation.
- `INV-44`: Billed to both orders (`ORD-A`, `ORD-B`); settled by both payments (`PAY-1`, `PAY-2`). Lifecycle is fully closed.
- `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y`: Both appear in `ev11`, giving them delivery-confirmation endpoints.
- **Risk**: The bundle relies on arithmetic closure (`P8`/`P9`/`P10`/`P11`) and bulk delivery (`P5`/`P6`) without direct event references. If any amount actually includes tax, the invoice-payment graph becomes false.

### Bundle C (brief variant): Financial open, delivery closed
Not required but noted for completeness: accept `P5`/`P6` (delivery closure) but reject `P8`/`P9`/`P10`/`P11` (keep invoice/payment minimal). This separates delivery ambiguity from financial ambiguity, yielding a “logistics-repaired, finance-conservative” hybrid. We omit full tabulation to avoid redundancy, but it demonstrates that the ambiguous dimensions are largely independent.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

**Orders**:
- `ORD-A`: In Bundle A, lifecycle runs `Create`  `Add Item` (×2)  `Pack` (`PK-7`, `PK-8`)  `Dispatch` (`DEL-X`, `DEL-Y`)  `Confirm Delivery` (`ORD-A` only, delivery objects unresolved)  `Issue Invoice` (`INV-44`, `ORD-A`)  `Record Payment` (`PAY-1`). This is a plausible, nearly complete lifecycle. The only gap is the missing delivery-object link in `ev11`, which is defensible under rule 5.
- `ORD-B`: `Create`  `Add Item` (`IT-B1`)  `Pack` (`PK-8`, via `P1`/`P2`)  `Dispatch` (`PK-8`/`DEL-Y`, via `P3`/`P4`). After dispatch, `ORD-B` has no `Confirm Delivery`, no `Invoice`, and `PAY-2` is not linked. This is a **truncated lifecycle**, not an orphan event sequence: the events exist and are ordered correctly, but later stages are missing. This is acceptable; we do not invent them.

**Items**:
- `IT-A1`: Full lifecycle within `ORD-A`  `PK-7`  `DEL-X`  `ORD-A` confirmation.
- `IT-A2`: Full lifecycle within `ORD-A`  `PK-8`  `DEL-Y` (Bundle A/B).
- `IT-B1`: `ORD-B`  `PK-8` (Bundle A/B)  `DEL-Y`. The item is never “lost”; it is delivered via `PK-8`. Only the `Confirm Delivery` event does not mention `ORD-B`.

**Packages**:
- `PK-7`: `Pack` (`IT-A1`)  `Dispatch` (`DEL-X`)  `carries` (`DEL-X`). Complete.
- `PK-8`: `Pack` (`IT-A2`, `IT-B1`)  `Dispatch` (`DEL-Y`)  `carries` (`DEL-Y`). In Bundle A, this creates a **one-to-many** `O2O` (`PK-8`  `IT-A2` observed; `PK-8`  `IT-B1` added). The cardinality is permitted (packages can contain multiple items). There is no contradiction with `gross_weight=2.9` because item weights are unknown.

**Deliveries**:
- `DEL-X`: `Dispatch` (`PK-7`)  `carries` (`PK-7`). Complete.
- `DEL-Y`: `Dispatch` (`PK-8`)  `carries` (`PK-8`). In Bundle A, `DEL-Y` participates only in `ev10` (and `P4`). It is not an orphan object because it has both an event (`ev10`) and an `O2O` relation (`P4`). Its lifecycle is minimal but valid.

**Invoices**:
- `INV-44`: `Issue` (`ev12`, `ORD-A`)  `bills` (`ORD-A`)  `settles` (`PAY-1`). In Bundle A, amount (`230`) exceeds linked order value (`130`). Per rule 6, this is consistent with adjustments or fees. No circular reasoning: `INV-44`  `ORD-A` is observed; we do not derive it from `E2O`.

**Payments**:
- `PAY-1`: `Record` (`ev13`)  `settles` (`INV-44`, observed `O2O`). Complete.
- `PAY-2`: `Record` (`ev14`), object `PAY-2` only. In Bundle A, this is an **open lifecycle** (unapplied cash). No orphan event (`ev14` exists), but the object has no `Invoice` link. This is explicitly allowed.

**Cardinality checks**:
- `ORD-A`  Items: 1:2 (valid).
- `INV-44`  Orders: 1:1 in Bundle A, 1:2 in Bundle B (rule 6 permits 1:many).
- `PK-8`  Items: 1:2 in Bundle A/B (valid for package content).
- `DEL-X`/`DEL-Y`  Packages: 1:1 (valid).

**Circular reasoning flag**: We explicitly avoid using `P4` (`DEL-Y` carries `PK-8`) to confirm `P3` (`ev10`  `DEL-Y`). In the audit, `P4` is noted as a structural mirror derived from `P3`. Similarly, `P11` (`PAY-2` settles `INV-44`) is derived from `P10`, not an independent check. The `OC-DFG` and `OTG` analyses treat these edges as single-source, not doubly confirmed.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

We compare the unrepaired log, Bundle A, and Bundle B across standard OCEL discovery artifacts.

### OC-DFG (Object-Centric Directly-Follows Graph)
- **Unrepaired**: The graph has a disconnected fragment for `IT-B1`: `Add Item` (`IT-B1`)  `Pack Item` (`IT-B1` only, no package)  gap  `Dispatch Package` (`PK-8`, no delivery). The `IT-A1` and `IT-A2` paths are intact (`Pack`  `Dispatch`  `Delivery` for `PK-7`; `Pack`  `Dispatch` only for `PK-8`). There is no `Confirm Delivery`  `Delivery` edge because `ev11` lacks delivery objects.
- **Bundle A**: The `IT-B1` path connects: `Pack Item` (`IT-B1`, `PK-8`)  `Dispatch Package` (`PK-8`, `DEL-Y`). This introduces the missing `Item`  `Package`  `Delivery` sequence for `ORD-B`’s item. The `Confirm Delivery` node (`ev11`) still connects only to `Order`; no `Delivery` node is added, so no false delivery-confirmation edge is invented.
- **Bundle B**: `Confirm Delivery` (`ev11`) now connects to `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y`. This creates `Dispatch Package`  `Confirm Delivery` edges for both deliveries, closing the package-delivery loop. The `Order`  `Invoice`  `Payment` chain also closes because `ORD-B` and `PAY-2` join the graph.

### OTG (Object Type Graph)
- **Unrepaired**: Edges present: `Order`–`Item`, `Item`–`Package` (`IT-A1`–`PK-7`, `IT-A2`–`PK-8`), `Package`–`Delivery` (`PK-7`–`DEL-X`), `Order`–`Invoice` (`ORD-A`–`INV-44`), `Invoice`–`Payment` (`INV-44`–`PAY-1`). `DEL-Y` is isolated; `IT-B1` lacks a `Package` edge; `ORD-B` lacks `Invoice`/`Payment` edges.
- **Bundle A**: Adds `Item`–`Package` (`IT-B1`–`PK-8`) and `Package`–`Delivery` (`PK-8`–`DEL-Y`). The graph becomes connected for all logistics objects, but financial nodes (`ORD-B`, `PAY-2`) remain peripheral.
- **Bundle B**: Adds `Order`–`Invoice` (`ORD-B`–`INV-44`), `Invoice`–`Payment` (`INV-44`–`PAY-2` via `O2O`), and `Order`–`Delivery` (indirectly via `ev11` linking `ORD-A` to both `DEL-X`/`DEL-Y`). The `OTG` becomes fully connected with no isolated nodes.

### ET–OT graph
- **Unrepaired**: `Pack Item` has heterogeneous object-type sets: `ev06` (`Item`, `Package`), `ev07` (`Item`, `Package`), `ev08` (`Item` only), `ev05` (`Order`, `Item`). `Dispatch Package` is heterogeneous: `ev09` (`Package`, `Delivery`), `ev10` (`Package` only).
- **Bundle A**: `Pack Item` becomes uniformly (`Item`, `Package`) for all instances; `Dispatch Package` becomes uniformly (`Package`, `Delivery`). This removes spurious event-type heterogeneity caused by missing relations.
- **Bundle B**: `Confirm Delivery` now includes `Delivery` type(s) in addition to `Order`; `Issue Invoice` includes two `Order` types; `Record Payment` (`ev14`) includes `Payment` + `Invoice` like `ev13`.

### Flattenings
- **Order flattening (`pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, ["Order"])`)**:
  - *Unrepaired*: `ORD-A` trace is rich; `ORD-B` trace ends abruptly after `Pack Item` (`ev08` has no package, `ev10` is not linked to `ORD-B`).
  - *Bundle A*: `ORD-B` trace now includes `Pack Item` (with `PK-8`) and, via package linkage, can be traced into `Dispatch` (`PK-8`/`DEL-Y`), though `ORD-B` itself is not in `ev10`. The trace is longer but still lacks `Confirm Delivery` and financial events.
  - *Bundle B*: `ORD-B` gains `Issue Invoice` and `Record Payment` links (through `INV-44`), making its trace structurally closer to `ORD-A`. Both orders now share `Confirm Delivery` via `ev11` (if we treat `ev11` as covering both via delivery links).

- **Package flattening (`["Package"]`)**:
  - *Bundle A* is critical: `PK-8` previously had `Pack Item` (`ev07`) and `Dispatch` (`ev10`) but no delivery partner. After `P3`/`P4`, `PK-8` has a complete `Pack`  `Dispatch`  `Delivery` (`DEL-Y`) lifecycle, matching `PK-7`.

- **Delivery flattening (`["Delivery"]`)**:
  - *Unrepaired*: `DEL-X` has `Dispatch` (`ev09`); `DEL-Y` has nothing.
  - *Bundle A*: `DEL-Y` gains `Dispatch` (`ev10`) and `carries` (`PK-8`). Its lifecycle is minimal but present.
  - *Bundle B*: `DEL-Y` (and `DEL-X`) also appear in `Confirm Delivery` (`ev11`), giving both deliveries a confirmation endpoint.

- **Invoice flattening (`["Invoice"]`)**:
  - *Bundle A*: `INV-44` has `Issue` (`ev12`), `Order` (`ORD-A`), `Settlement` (`PAY-1`). The missing `ORD-B` and `PAY-2` mean the invoice trace looks like a partial settlement.
  - *Bundle B*: `INV-44` now includes both orders and both payments, yielding a “full billing and settlement” trace. This may cause the miner to treat `INV-44` as always multi-order and always two-payment, which is a bias if the repair is wrong.

- **Payment flattening (`["Payment"]`)**:
  - *Bundle A*: `PAY-2` has only `Record Payment`. The flattening produces a one-event case.
  - *Bundle B*: `PAY-2` is linked to `INV-44`, pairing it with `PAY-1` in the same invoice case.

### Activity frequencies
- **Event-based**: Unchanged across bundles (same 14 events).
- **Unique-object-based**: Bundle A activates `DEL-Y` (now in `ev10` and `P4`); Bundle B additionally activates `ORD-B` in invoice/payment, and `DEL-X`/`DEL-Y` in `ev11`.
- **Total-object-based**: Same object counts, but relation density increases in Bundle B.

### Equivalent-execution clustering around Order
- In all bundles, `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` remain distinguishable because `ORD-B` never receives its own `Create`/`Add` sequence in `ev11` (which is tied to `ORD-A`). Even in Bundle B, `ORD-B` is linked to `INV-44` and `PK-8`, but its direct event trace (`ev02`, `ev05`, `ev08`) differs from `ORD-A`. Clustering algorithms (e.g., based on event sequences per order) will likely keep them separate. However, in Bundle B, financial clustering might merge them through the shared `INV-44` and payments, which could be misinterpreted as a single “multi-order invoice process” rather than two independent orders.

### Concrete edges / fragments
- **Appears in Bundle A**: `Pack Item` (`IT-B1`)  `Dispatch Package` (`PK-8`) via `P1`; `Package`  `Delivery` (`PK-8`  `DEL-Y`) via `P4`.
- **Appears in Bundle B (not in A)**: `Confirm Delivery`  `DEL-X`/`DEL-Y`; `Issue Invoice`  `ORD-B`; `Record Payment` (`PAY-2`)  `INV-44`; `Payment`  `Invoice` (`PAY-2`  `INV-44`).
- **Disappears / avoided in both bundles relative to aggressive repair**: No false `ev08`  `PK-7` (`P12` rejected); no false exclusive `ev11`  `DEL-X` (`P7` rejected).

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Forcing `ev11` exclusively to `DEL-X`** (`P7`) creates a false `Confirm Delivery`  `DEL-X` edge and leaves `DEL-Y` orphaned. Downstream, the `OC-DFG` would show `Confirm Delivery` always followed by `DEL-X`, biasing delivery-time analytics and hiding the `PK-8`/`DEL-Y` flow.

2. **Adding `ORD-B` to `INV-44` (`P8`/`P9`) based solely on `130 + 100 = 230`** invents a multi-order invoice pattern. If the `230` actually includes a `30` tax on `ORD-A` alone, the repair corrupts revenue attribution per order and makes `Bundle B`’s invoice flattening show false two-order traces.

3. **Linking `PAY-2` to `INV-44` (`P10`/`P11`) via arithmetic** creates false financial closure. If `PAY-2` is actually unapplied cash (e.g., a customer prepayment for a future order), the invoice `INV-44` will appear fully settled, hiding open-receivables state in payment-flattened analysis.

4. **Inventing `PK-8`  `IT-B1` (`P2`) without resolving scanner uncertainty** could fabricate a multi-item package. In package-level process discovery, this introduces a false “pack two items together” variant, affecting capacity or weight-based models, and the false `O2O` may be treated as independent corroboration of `P1` (circular reinforcement).

5. **Deriving `DEL-Y` carries `PK-8` (`P4`) from `ev10` (`P3`) and then using the `O2O` to “confirm” the dispatch assignment** creates self-fulfilling circular reasoning. The `OTG` will show a `Package`–`Delivery` edge, but its only evidential source is the `E2O` addition we made; treating it as a separate confirmation overstates confidence.

6. **Adding both `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y` to `ev11` (`P5` + `P6`) in Bundle B** invents a bulk-delivery-confirmation pattern. If most real-world confirmations reference a single delivery, the repaired log will bias clustering and discovery toward multi-delivery confirmation events, misrepresenting the standard process variant.
