## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

1. **Lexicographic Dominance of Regulatory and Non-Monetary Compliance Risk:**
   * The operational benefit of meeting P-884’s 90-minute deadline is bounded at **€20,000–€35,000**, and the cost of a prolonged hold or false rejection is bounded at **€15,000–€80,000**.
   * By contrast, the harm from an unlawful execution (violating sanctions or mandatory AML/four-eyes controls) is **€100,000–€500,000+**, compounded by **asymmetric non-monetary consequences**: strict-liability sanctions enforcement, banking license restrictions, correspondent banking de-risking, criminal/civil liability for officers, and severe audit findings.
   * Even if the observational probability of ultimate approval is $0.55\text{–}0.75$ (implying a $0.25\text{–}0.45$ chance that the payment is genuinely non-clearable or sanctioned), an expected monetary calculation on Action A or B would yield:
     $$\text{Expected Regulatory Harm} \ge 0.25 \times €100,000\text{ to }0.45 \times €500,000 = €25,000\text{ to }€225,000+ \text{ (plus non-monetary ruin)}$$
     which dominates the €20,000–€35,000 SLA benefit even before accounting for the **100% certainty** of committing a willful internal control breach. Therefore, **Actions A and B are strictly dominated** and inadmissible regardless of SLA pressure.

2. **Balancing Deadline Loss vs. False Rejection/Hold Harm (P-884):**
   * **Action F (Reject now)** trades a 100% certainty of avoiding regulatory fines for a 100% certainty of incurring false-rejection/relationship harm (€15,000–€80,000) whenever the transaction is actually legitimate ($p = 0.55\text{–}0.75$).
   * **Composite Plan D1** and **Action C** preserve the $0.55\text{–}0.75$ probability of a legitimate approval while still offering a realistic path to beat the 90-minute clock (saving €20,000–€35,000) and reducing false-rejection harm.
   * **Action E (Request more evidence)** sacrifices the 90-minute deadline (€20,000–€35,000 loss) and incurs temporary hold friction, but avoids permanent false rejection of a valid supplier payment and protects compliance. Thus, D1 and C dominate E on speed, while E dominates F on preserving customer value if a fast review cannot resolve the match.

3. **Observational vs. Interventional Transportability:**
   * The baseline SLA breach prediction ($0.48\text{–}0.64$) and approval prediction ($0.55\text{–}0.75$) are observational. Intervening via **Action D (alternate screening)** actively alters the information state: if the alternate vendor resolves the 0.46 fuzzy match, the second officer’s review time shifts toward the lower bound of the 35–75 minute range (or eliminates the second-review requirement if risk drops $<70$), substantially lowering the true interventional SLA breach probability compared to passive waiting.
