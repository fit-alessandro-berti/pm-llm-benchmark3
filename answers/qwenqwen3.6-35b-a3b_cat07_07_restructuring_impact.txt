# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1.  **Tail Latency:** Average cycle time may improve due to efficient simple cases, while P90/P95 worsens as high-complexity cases get stuck in central queues. `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg` must show distribution, not just mean.
2.  **High-Complexity Bottleneck:** Central routing concentrates complex cases. If central capacity is insufficient, tail latency for complex cases explodes, harming high-value customers. Monitor `case_complexity` vs `waiting_time`.
3.  **Local Knowledge Erosion:** Loss of `local_expertise_flag` may not immediately affect cycle time but will increase rework for cases requiring regional nuance. `rework_flag` correlation with `local_expertise_flag` is a critical signal.
4.  **Fairness Across Teams/Segments:** East may benefit while North/South degrade, or specific customer segments may face longer waits. Average masks distributional effects. Compare `team` and `customer_segment` performance in P90 and rework.
5.  **Learning & Skill Stagnation:** Local staff lose exposure to complex cases, reducing skill development. `pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` may show divergence between local and central tech skills over time, reducing resilience.
6.  **Accountability Diffusion:** Shared approval queues can lead to "tragedy of the commons" where no one feels responsible for decision quality. Increased `rework_flag` after approval or `reassignment_count` indicates accountability loss.
7.  **Exception Handling Rigidity:** Routing rules may block necessary exceptions, forcing workarounds that increase `reassignment_count` and handover noise. Monitor `handover_reason` for "Bypass" or "Escalation".

# 5. Pilot design

*   **Pilot Scope:** Implement central Technical Shared Service and Shared Approval Queue for **Team East** only. Keep North and South local. Centralize Legal/Risk/Quality for East.
*   **Comparison Group:** North and South teams serve as control group. East performance is compared to its own baseline and relative to North/South.
*   **Pre/Post Observation Window:**
    *   Pre: 3 months baseline data.
    *   Post: 3 months pilot execution.
    *   Analysis: Compare East pre/post, and East post vs North/South post.
*   **Segmentation:**
    *   By `team`: East vs North/South.
    *   By `case_complexity`: Simple vs High.
    *   By `required_specialty`: Legal/Risk vs Technical.
    *   By `customer_segment`: High-value vs Standard.
*   **Guardrail Metrics:**
    *   SLA miss rate for East must not exceed 12%.
    *   Rework rate for East must not exceed 12%.
    *   Central queue wait P95 must not exceed 10h.
    *   `local_expertise_flag` for East must remain > 0.5 for complex cases.
*   **Staff Feedback Collection:**
    *   Weekly surveys on workload, clarity, and customer issues.
    *   Bi-weekly focus groups with caseworkers and specialists.
    *   Track `handover_reason` and `reassignment_count` as proxies for friction.
*   **Rollback/Adjustment Triggers:**
    *   Immediate rollback if SLA miss rate > 15% for 2 consecutive weeks.
    *   Adjustment if rework rate increases > 10% vs baseline.
    *   Adjustment if `local_expertise_flag` drops below 0.3 for complex cases.
    *   Adjustment if staff feedback indicates severe accountability confusion.

# 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation: Proceed with Pilot First.**

The restructuring offers potential benefits in specialist utilization and standardization, but risks of central bottlenecks, local expertise loss, and fairness issues are significant. The asymmetric impact on East (currently overloaded) versus North/South (stable) suggests a one-size-fits-all rollout is premature.

**Conditional Logic:**
*   **Pilot East:** East's high utilization and slower cycles suggest it may benefit most from centralization. This tests capacity relief and central routing efficacy with minimal risk to stable teams.
*   **Monitor Indicators:** Focus on tail latency, rework for complex cases, local expertise trends, and fairness metrics. Do not rely on average cycle time.
*   **Rollback Triggers:** If guardrail metrics are breached, adjust routing rules or restore local specialist roles for East.

**What Management Should Not Infer:**
*   **Do not infer sustainability from early averages:** Early improvements may be due to simple cases; complex cases may lag.
*   **Do not assume utilization gains are permanent:** Centralization may initially boost utilization but could lead to burnout if queues grow.
*   **Do not ignore fairness:** East improvement does not justify North/South degradation. Fairness must be maintained.
*   **Do not overlook learning:** Loss of local expertise may not show immediately but will reduce long-term resilience.

# 9. Segmented Analysis (Example: East vs North, High Complexity)

print(f"East Local Expertise: {east_local_exp}")
