# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

All durations below are in minutes. The main finding is that **B most strongly controls fork-join completion**, despite A having the longest service time. Whether earlier join readiness produces earlier case completion depends on final-station dispatch and availability.

For case \(c\), let \(a_c\) be arrival, \(r_c\) fork release, \(s_{ci}\) branch start, \(e_{ci}\) branch completion for \(i\in\{A,B,C\}\), and \(f_c,g_c\) final start and completion. Define:

\[
\begin{aligned}
U_c &= r_c-a_c &&\text{upstream elapsed time},\\
Q_{ci} &= s_{ci}-r_c &&\text{branch queue waiting},\\
S_{ci} &= e_{ci}-s_{ci} &&\text{branch service interval},\\
L_{ci} &= Q_{ci}+S_{ci}=e_{ci}-r_c &&\text{release-to-branch-completion time},\\
J_c &= \max_i e_{ci} &&\text{join readiness},\\
D_c &= J_c-r_c=\max_i L_{ci} &&\text{elapsed fork-join stage},\\
W_{ci} &= J_c-e_{ci}=D_c-L_{ci} &&\text{branch synchronization waiting},\\
V_c &= f_c-J_c &&\text{downstream waiting},\\
S_{cF} &= g_c-f_c &&\text{final service interval},\\
T_c &= g_c-a_c=U_c+D_c+V_c+S_{cF} &&\text{total case flow time}.
\end{aligned}
\]

**Measurement assumptions.** The supplied start/end fields delimit each activity's execution interval, all three branches are required, and their recorded ends satisfy the join rule. Calling these intervals *active service* additionally assumes no unlogged interruptions or preemption. Calling \(Q_{ci}\) and \(V_c\) *queueing* assumes the corresponding work was otherwise eligible and waiting for execution. Without that assumption, they are measured waiting intervals whose causes remain unresolved. Upstream elapsed time cannot be split into preparation service and waiting from these fields.

The critical set is \(K_c=\{i:e_{ci}=J_c\}\). Retain every tied branch; when a single representative is needed, choose A before B before C. There are no observed ties in this dataset. With representative \(k_c\), a valid additive case decomposition is

\[
T_c=U_c+Q_{c k_c}+S_{c k_c}+V_c+S_{cF}.
\]

Other branches' queues, services, and synchronization waits are parallel diagnostics, not extra serial components. For **each** branch,

\[
Q_{ci}+S_{ci}+W_{ci}=D_c;
\qquad
\sum_i(Q_{ci}+S_{ci}+W_{ci})=3D_c.
\]

Thus summing branch timelines counts the same stage three times. Synchronization waits also overlap: in F02, C waits 35 minutes and A waits 10 minutes; both wait during 09:15–09:25. Their sum, 45 branch-minutes, counts that ten-minute interval twice. In F07, synchronization waits total \(25+0+52=77\) branch-minutes, exceeding the entire 75-minute stage. These sums can describe waiting deliverables, but cannot be added to elapsed flow time. They do not imply that branch resources remain occupied after completion.

# 3. Aggregate critical-path analysis

To measure influence rather than work content, define the exclusive excess

\[
G_{ci}=\left(L_{ci}-\max_{j\ne i}L_{cj}\right)^+.
\]

Under the assumption that other branches do not change, reducing branch \(i\)'s elapsed time by \(\delta_{ci}\ge0\) advances readiness by

\[
\Delta D_{ci}=\min(\delta_{ci},G_{ci}).
\]

This is zero for a noncritical branch or one member of a completion tie. \(G_{ci}\) is the largest possible readiness gain from accelerating that branch alone, even to instantaneous completion. It is a mathematical ceiling under fixed other branches, not a feasible redesign promise.

|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---:|
| A | 50 / 50 / 50 / 50 | 0 / 0 / 0 / 0 | 50 / 50 / 50 | 2/12 = 16.7% | 11.00 | 1.25 / 10 | 8.33 |
| B | 37.50 / 37.50 / 45 / 45 | 19.17 / 20 / 30 / 30 | 56.67 / 74 / 75 | 8/12 = 66.7% | 4.33 | 10.00 / 25 | 43.33 |
| C | 20.17 / 20 / 21.80 / 30 | 10.42 / 5 / 24 / 35 | 30.58 / 53 / 57 | 2/12 = 16.7% | 30.42 | 1.00 / 7 | 9.33 |

“Selected stage contribution” is \(\operatorname{mean}[D_c\mathbf{1}(k_c=i)]\). The three entries sum to 61 minutes because the stage is assigned to its selected critical branch. This is descriptive accounting, not the causal benefit of removing a branch.

**Influence ranking: B clearly first; A and C are close behind.** B's queue offsets its shorter service: its mean release-to-completion time exceeds A's by 6.67 minutes, and it controls eight joins. Its exclusive excess averages 10 minutes across all cases and 15 minutes conditional on its eight critical cases. B also controls both 75-minute stages, F07 and F12, whose total flows of 130 and 125 minutes are the two largest. Its 74-minute elapsed-time p90 nearly determines the observed stage tail.

A and C have equal observed critical frequency. A's average exclusive excess is slightly larger, 1.25 versus 1.00 minutes, while C's occasional critical stages contribute slightly more stage time in the descriptive allocation. There is no defensible strong population ranking between them from 12 cases. C's unusually late starts in F04 and F08 deserve targeted attention; its large mean synchronization wait otherwise indicates frequent early completion, not strong control of the join.

A's 50-minute service is the largest workload per case, but reducing it usually leaves B or C controlling completion. Conversely, accelerating B can matter without reducing any technical service work. After sufficient B improvement, A's unchanged 50 minutes becomes a floor in more cases, so benefits saturate.

The downstream interval is also substantial: mean 14.67 minutes, p90 19.8, maximum 20. Its cause is not established by these timestamps. For example, F03 is ready at 09:45 while its assigned F1 has no recorded work between 09:28 and 10:00. F08 waits for F2 until 11:05 while F1 has no recorded work between 10:40 and 11:10. These observations are incompatible with a fully observed, always-available, unrestricted, work-conserving pooled queue; they are consistent with holds, assignments, calendars, or omitted work. They do not prove which explanation is correct.

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

**Assumption for isolated branch changes.** Keep arrival, fork release, other branch intervals, and final service unchanged. A's service reduction moves its end earlier; B's queue reduction moves its start and end earlier by the same amount. Shared-resource feedback is excluded at this first stage because branch resources are not supplied.

Readiness gains are exact conditional calculations. Case-completion gains require a final-station model. Three interpretations are useful:

1. **Unchanged downstream interval:** move final start earlier by the readiness gain and retain each observed downstream wait. This gives a simple timing estimate, but overlapping final services must be checked.
2. **Preserved final appointments:** retain observed final starts and ends. Earlier branch completions merely increase downstream waiting; both branch interventions then yield zero completion savings.
3. **Resource-feasible illustrative replay:** treat each observed downstream interval as a case-specific eligibility lag \(h_c=V_c\). A case becomes eligible at \(J'_c+h_c\); two identical, initially available final resources process eligible cases FCFS, breaking eligibility ties by case ID and resource ties by resource number. Retain 20-minute nonpreemptive service and assume no other workload. This calibrated model reproduces every baseline final start/end, although resource assignments can change. It demonstrates possible capacity masking; it does not identify the true waiting mechanism.

The three primary counterfactuals below use the third interpretation. The final intervention removes the assumed holds and pools the two existing resources. The zero-wait timing ceiling and a third-server sensitivity are also reported.

**A service reduced by 20%.** Every A interval falls from 50 to 40 minutes, releasing 120 A service-minutes across the cohort under the service assumptions. Only F01 and F11 become join-ready earlier: F01 gains five minutes before B becomes critical, and F11 gains ten before A and B tie. The other ten cases have zero readiness gain because B or C remains critical. The unchanged-downstream-interval estimate would reduce mean flow to 114.00 minutes, with no tail improvement.

The replay exposes a further limitation for F11: its earlier join at 11:20 plus the retained 20-minute eligibility lag gives eligibility at 11:40. F09 is in service until 11:50 and F10 receives the other server at 11:40. F11 still starts at 11:50, so its ten-minute readiness gain becomes ten additional minutes of downstream waiting. Only F01's five-minute completion saving survives. Other dispatch rules could distribute this conflict differently; the zero gain for F11 is a replay result, not an observed causal fact. Faster A execution may lower workload or improve availability for other cases, but that feedback is outside the isolated calculation. Its mean synchronization wait actually rises from 11.00 to 19.75 minutes because A finishes earlier in mostly unchanged stages.

**B queue reduced by 50%.** The mean B queue falls from 19.17 to 9.58 minutes, with service unchanged. Queue reductions total 115 branch-minutes, but only 92.5 minutes reach join readiness because other branches take over. All eight originally B-critical cases gain; F01, F04, F08, and F11 gain zero because A or C remains critical. F02 and F06 become A/B ties; F05 and F10 become A-critical. B remains critical in F03, F07, F09, and F12.

For example, F07's B elapsed time falls from 75 to 60 minutes, so readiness improves by 15 rather than the 25-minute gap to A. F10's B elapsed time falls from 55 to 45, but A remains at 50; only five minutes are realized. In the chosen final replay, all readiness savings survive capacity checks. Mean flow falls by 7.71 minutes, p90 falls by 10 minutes, and maximum flow falls from 130 to 115. The cohort's last final completion moves from 12:40 to 12:25. With fixed final appointments, these completion gains would instead be zero.

Secondary effects include altered B-resource overlap and final arrival bunching. The assumed queue reductions are targets, not a demonstrated feasible schedule for unknown B resources. In the isolated arithmetic, mean A/C synchronization waits fall to 3.29/22.71 minutes, but B's rises from 4.33 to 6.21 as it becomes noncritical more often. More synchronization waiting at an accelerated branch need not mean worse case flow.

**Reduce final-station waiting.** Removing all observed downstream intervals gives a timing ceiling of 176 case-minutes saved, or 14.67 per case; all cases have positive potential because every observed interval is positive. Joins and branch synchronization waits are unchanged. However, immediate service at every join is infeasible with only two servers: F09 would run 11:15–11:35 and F10 11:20–11:40, leaving F11, ready at 11:30, to wait five minutes. The two-server FCFS replay therefore saves 171 minutes, averaging 14.25, with residual mean final queue \(5/12=0.42\) minutes.

Under the same no-other-work assumptions, a third server plus removal of holds achieves the full zero-wait ceiling. Its incremental benefit over pooling two servers is only five minutes in this cohort, entirely for F11; the last cohort completion remains 12:30. **Adding a third server while retaining the modeled eligibility holds gives zero benefit**, because the observed eligible-start schedule already fits two servers. Capacity alone therefore cannot be credited with eliminating all 176 observed waiting minutes.

These scenarios are alternative conditional models, not confidence bounds. They distinguish zero gains from branch noncriticality, gains absorbed downstream, and capacity-feasible gains. Combined interventions require another replay rather than adding their independently calculated savings.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

**1. Pilot a reduction in B waiting, with case-aware dispatch and verified resource availability.** First establish whether B waits arise from occupied machines, staffing, setup, batch releases, assignments, or other gates. Then test dispatch or availability changes against the 50% queue-reduction target; prioritize cases predicted to delay the join, with aging safeguards for other work. Monitor C's F04/F08-style late starts so administrative exceptions are not overlooked.

- **Throughput:** B service workload remains 450 minutes for these cases. Queue reduction alone does not increase physical service capacity or prove a throughput increase. Better dispatch may improve effective output if avoidable idle time exists; actual capacity relief requires evidence about resources and calendars.
- **Flow-time tail:** B controls the two longest cases, making it the strongest branch target. The isolated replay lowers p90 from 124.5 to 114.5 and maximum from 130 to 115 minutes. Check that improvement persists across days and case mixes.
- **Utilization:** Measure machine occupancy, setups, downtime, and staffed availability before adding capacity. Starting cases earlier changes concurrency even when service work is unchanged. Exact B utilization cannot be inferred from the supplied intervals.
- **New bottlenecks and synchronization:** A becomes limiting more often; final arrivals may bunch. A/C synchronization falls, while B can finish earlier and wait longer. Do not optimize synchronization totals in isolation.
- **Pilot first:** Log B eligibility, queue-entry and dispatch decisions, machine/team identity, and availability. Track queue distributions, join readiness, final waiting, completed cases per staffed hour, and test quality. Use a controlled rollout or comparable shifts to distinguish operational gains from changes in workload.

**2. Pilot join-triggered final dispatch and pooling of F1/F2 before purchasing another server.** Make actual join readiness visible, investigate holds and fixed assignments, and route eligible cases to an available qualified final resource when policy permits. Preserve necessary release controls; remove only avoidable scheduling delays.

- **Throughput:** At a constant 20 minutes per case, two continuously available identical servers have nominal capacity of six cases/hour. The listed forks occur every 15 minutes, corresponding to four cases/hour over this sequence. A third server would raise nominal capacity to nine/hour, but demand, qualifications, calendars, and unseen work determine whether it raises actual throughput. Faster completion of this cohort is not evidence of a sustained capacity increase.
- **Flow-time tail:** Removing holds and pooling two resources conditionally lowers mean flow to 101.00, p90 to 114, and maximum to 115. It does not change the branch stage; B's 75-minute stages remain. A third server adds only a five-minute saving for F11 in this replay and does not further reduce p90 or maximum.
- **Utilization:** Each recorded final resource supplies 120 service-minutes. Over the common 09:08–12:40 window, each has a cohort busy fraction of \(120/212=56.6\%\), assuming continuous availability and no other work. This is not measured total resource utilization. Pooling can redistribute idle time; adding capacity can lower utilization if demand is unchanged.
- **New bottlenecks and synchronization:** Removing final delays exposes the fork-join stage as the dominant remaining component. Branch synchronization is unchanged by final dispatch. Combining this redesign with B acceleration could create a different final arrival pattern and must be replayed jointly.
- **Pilot first:** Record actual join/eligibility signals, release-policy decisions, queue entries, resource qualifications and calendars, and all final workloads. Compare readiness-to-start tails, policy compliance, service quality, and output per staffed hour. Add capacity only if the full workload shows persistent capacity-induced waits after dispatch changes.

# 6. Reproducible Python

The following self-contained script embeds the supplied data and requires pandas and PM4Py. It calculates the baseline decomposition, preserves all critical ties, runs the three primary counterfactuals and two capacity sensitivities, and prints per-case and aggregate results. Counterfactual fractions are kept rather than rounded back to the one-minute observational grid. PM4Py service-time results are converted from seconds to minutes.

```python
from io import StringIO
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

RAW = """case arrival fork_release A_start A_end B_start B_end C_start C_end final_start final_end final_resource
F01 07:55 08:10 08:10 09:00 08:15 08:55 08:20 08:38 09:08 09:28 F1
F02 08:05 08:25 08:25 09:15 08:45 09:25 08:30 08:50 09:40 10:00 F2
F03 08:20 08:40 08:40 09:30 09:00 09:45 08:45 09:03 10:00 10:20 F1
F04 08:35 08:55 08:55 09:45 09:05 09:35 09:30 09:52 10:10 10:30 F2
F05 08:50 09:10 09:10 10:00 09:35 10:10 09:15 09:35 10:20 10:40 F1
F06 09:05 09:25 09:25 10:15 09:55 10:30 09:40 10:00 10:45 11:05 F2
F07 09:20 09:40 09:40 10:30 10:10 10:55 09:45 10:03 11:10 11:30 F1
F08 09:35 09:55 09:55 10:45 10:00 10:30 10:20 10:50 11:05 11:25 F2
F09 09:50 10:10 10:10 11:00 10:35 11:15 10:15 10:35 11:30 11:50 F1
F10 10:05 10:25 10:25 11:15 10:45 11:20 10:30 10:50 11:40 12:00 F2
F11 10:20 10:40 10:40 11:30 10:50 11:20 10:45 11:03 11:50 12:10 F1
F12 10:35 10:55 10:55 11:45 11:25 12:10 11:00 11:18 12:20 12:40 F2
"""
BRANCHES = ("A", "B", "C")  # Tie order is always A, then B, then C.
df = pd.read_csv(StringIO(RAW), sep=r"\s+")
time_cols = ["arrival", "fork_release", "final_start", "final_end"]
time_cols += [f"{i}_{edge}" for i in BRANCHES for edge in ("start", "end")]
for col in time_cols:
    df[col] = pd.to_datetime("2026-05-19 " + df[col],
                             format="%Y-%m-%d %H:%M")


def minutes(delta):
    return delta.dt.total_seconds() / 60.0


def decompose(source):
    x = source.copy()
    x["upstream_elapsed"] = minutes(x.fork_release - x.arrival)
    ends = [f"{i}_end" for i in BRANCHES]
    x["join_ready"] = x[ends].max(axis=1)
    x["fork_join_elapsed"] = minutes(x.join_ready - x.fork_release)
    for i in BRANCHES:
        x[f"{i}_queue"] = minutes(x[f"{i}_start"] - x.fork_release)
        x[f"{i}_service"] = minutes(x[f"{i}_end"] - x[f"{i}_start"])
        x[f"{i}_elapsed"] = minutes(x[f"{i}_end"] - x.fork_release)
        x[f"{i}_sync"] = minutes(x.join_ready - x[f"{i}_end"])
        assert (x[f"{i}_queue"] >= 0).all()
        assert (x[f"{i}_service"] >= 0).all()
        assert (x[f"{i}_sync"] >= 0).all()
        assert (abs(x[f"{i}_queue"] + x[f"{i}_service"]
                    + x[f"{i}_sync"] - x.fork_join_elapsed) < 1e-9).all()
    ties = x.apply(lambda r: tuple(i for i in BRANCHES
                                  if r[f"{i}_end"] == r.join_ready), axis=1)
    x["critical_branch"] = ties.map(lambda t: "|".join(t))
    x["critical_primary"] = ties.map(lambda t: t[0])
    x["downstream_queue"] = minutes(x.final_start - x.join_ready)
    x["final_service"] = minutes(x.final_end - x.final_start)
    x["total_flow_time"] = minutes(x.final_end - x.arrival)
    parts = x[["upstream_elapsed", "fork_join_elapsed",
               "downstream_queue", "final_service"]].sum(axis=1)
    assert (x.upstream_elapsed >= 0).all()
    assert (x.downstream_queue >= 0).all()
    assert (x.final_service >= 0).all()
    assert (abs(parts - x.total_flow_time) < 1e-9).all()
    return x


base = decompose(df)
case_cols = ["case", "upstream_elapsed"]
case_cols += [f"{i}_{kind}" for i in BRANCHES for kind in ("queue", "service")]
case_cols += ["join_ready", "fork_join_elapsed", "critical_branch"]
case_cols += [f"{i}_sync" for i in BRANCHES]
case_cols += ["downstream_queue", "final_service", "total_flow_time"]
view = base[case_cols].copy()
view["join_ready"] = view.join_ready.dt.strftime("%H:%M")
print("CASE DECOMPOSITION (minutes)")
print(view.to_string(index=False))

branch_rows = []
for i in BRANCHES:
    row = {"branch": i}
    for kind in ("service", "queue", "elapsed"):
        s = base[f"{i}_{kind}"]
        for label, value in (("mean", s.mean()), ("median", s.median()),
                             ("p90", s.quantile(.9)), ("max", s.max())):
            row[f"{kind}_{label}"] = value
    mask = base.critical_branch.str.split("|", regex=False).map(lambda t: i in t)
    row["critical_count"] = int(mask.sum())  # All tied branches count.
    row["critical_pct"] = 100 * mask.mean()
    row["sync_mean"] = base[f"{i}_sync"].mean()
    others = base[[f"{j}_elapsed" for j in BRANCHES if j != i]].max(axis=1)
    row["exclusive_excess_mean"] = (base[f"{i}_elapsed"] - others).clip(lower=0).mean()
    row["selected_stage_mean"] = base.fork_join_elapsed.where(
        base.critical_primary.eq(i), 0).mean()
    branch_rows.append(row)
print("\nBRANCH SUMMARY (linear-interpolated sample p90)")
print(pd.DataFrame(branch_rows).round(2).to_string(index=False))
aggregate = base[["upstream_elapsed", "fork_join_elapsed", "downstream_queue",
                  "final_service", "total_flow_time"]].agg(["sum", "mean", "max"])
aggregate.loc["p90"] = base[aggregate.columns].quantile(.9)
print("\nBASELINE AGGREGATES")
print(aggregate.round(2).to_string())


def replay_final(source, lag, servers=2):
    # Assumption: pooled, identical, nonpreemptive servers; no external work.
    # FCFS by join_ready + lag; equal release times break by case ID.
    # lag is an assumed eligibility hold, NOT an identified cause of waiting.
    x = source.copy()
    ready = x.join_ready + lag
    available = [ready.min()] * servers
    order = sorted(x.index, key=lambda k: (ready.loc[k], x.loc[k, "case"]))
    for k in order:
        resource = min(range(servers), key=lambda j: (available[j], j))
        start = max(ready.loc[k], available[resource])
        end = start + pd.Timedelta(minutes=float(source.loc[k, "final_service"]))
        x.loc[k, "final_start"] = start
        x.loc[k, "final_end"] = end
        x.loc[k, "final_resource"] = f"F{resource + 1}"
        available[resource] = end
    return decompose(x)


lag = base.final_start - base.join_ready
# Calibrated timing model: observed waiting is treated as an eligibility lag.
# This reproduces observed start/end times; assigned resources may differ.
```

# 7. Limits of inference

- **Causality and generalization:** Twelve cases on one day establish timing associations, not why B waits or how an intervention will change them. No controls, costs, demand history, or case-complexity information support a causal or economic estimate. The reported percentiles are descriptive sample values, not reliable population tail guarantees.
- **Join semantics and timestamp accuracy:** Join readiness is derived, not logged. It equals the latest recorded branch end only if all three ends are valid prerequisites and no extra checks, rework, transfer, or release gates intervene. One-minute precision limits distinctions near ties; fractional counterfactual results express arithmetic, not additional measurement precision.
- **Service and preemption:** Start-to-end intervals cannot identify hands-on work, interruptions, pauses, setups within the interval, or concurrent work by several people. If these occur, the reported service is interval residence time. Synchronization waiting concerns a completed branch's output and does not establish retained resource occupation.
- **Branch resource capacity:** No branch resource identifiers, calendars, competing cases, or skill constraints are supplied. Overlapping intervals establish that executions overlap, but do not identify machine counts, true utilization, service discipline, or whether earlier B starts are feasible. Under additional single-slot, uninterrupted-service assumptions, overlap can give capacity lower bounds; those assumptions are not established here.
- **Meaning of final waiting:** Positive `final_start − join_ready` identifies elapsed downstream waiting under the join rule, not its cause. Recorded idle gaps cannot exclude unlogged workload, breaks, qualifications, transport, appointment schedules, batching, or mandatory release policies. The two known resource labels do not prove unrestricted interchangeability or continuous availability.
- **Counterfactual scheduling:** The eligibility-lag replay is a transparent illustrative assumption calibrated to baseline times, not a discovered final scheduling policy. Preserved appointments can absorb all branch improvements; pooling and responsive dispatch can transmit them. Branch-resource and final-queue feedback can change other cases as well, so isolated arithmetic is not a full network simulation.
- **Throughput, costs, and feasibility:** The cohort's completion times and workload minutes do not identify long-run output, staffing needs, investment returns, or steady-state utilization. A faster assay may save work even with little flow benefit; a shorter queue may improve flow without saving work. Quality and mandatory controls must be measured in pilots before translating timing scenarios into an operational redesign.
