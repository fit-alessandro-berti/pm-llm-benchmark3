## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

**Supported by evidence**

- M0 is the current normative POWL model. It requires `Register_Supplier`, concurrent `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review`, an exclusive choice between `Manual_Procurement_Approval -> Activate_Supplier` and `Reject_Supplier`, then `Notify_Requester`, then `Close_Case`.
- CS-9 requires sanctions and quality review before activation; manual procurement approval before activation; and a different requester and manual approver. The resource rule is not encoded in M0.
- PD-3 is a formal steering-committee pilot decision, time-boxed to 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31. It is not incorporated into CS-9 or M0. It permits low-risk DE suppliers in approved corporate groups to use `Auto_Approve`, with quality review delayed up to two business days after provisional activation.
- FD-7 is a formal emergency directive, time-boxed to 2026-07-10 through 2026-07-12. It permits provisional activation before quality review and manual approval, with sanctions check before activation, a EUR 20,000 spend cap, daily monitoring, and retrospective quality/approval.
- N4 shows that notification logging degraded after the CRM migration: missing notification rose from 2% to 29%.
- N5 shows `Request_More_Info` is repeated in practice and considered desirable by compliance and operations, but M0 has no loop for it.
- The observed traces and aggregate statistics are stipulated as recorded.

**Disputed / unsupported propositions**

- Whether PD-3 is a valid normative exception for eligible cases or only an unauthorized weakening of CS-9.
- Whether the factual continuation of the flood emergency after FD-7 expiry creates continuing authority.
- Whether rejected leads and reactivations are inside M0 scope.
- Whether the CRM notification link in SUP-706 is sufficient evidence despite being a mutable text field.
- Whether the SUP-712 alias resolution is definitive for the resource rule.
- Whether the SUP-715 spouse/reporting-line relationship triggered a reportable conflict under the COI policy.
- Whether SUP-713’s retroactively approved label mapping should be accepted.

**Burden of proof**

For a case to be declared **NONCOMPLIANT**, the reviewer must establish: (1) an applicable normative rule within scope; (2) observed behavior inconsistent with that rule; and (3) no valid exception or data-quality explanation. For a case to be declared **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**, the operator must show: (1) a documented authority with an effective window; (2) eligibility snapshot satisfying the overlay; and (3) all overlay conditions met. When the evidence cannot distinguish a process violation from a logging/model defect, the appropriate default is **DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE** or **MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT**, not automatic noncompliance.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The strongest process-aware case is that the operation is materially noncompliant and that CS-9/M0 should be enforced until formally amended.

**Formal authority is on the side of CS-9.** CS-9 is controlled, formally approved, and effective from 2025 onward. PD-3 is not incorporated into CS-9 or M0. The compliance observer’s statement that the pilot “may proceed as a monitored exception” is not an amendment to CS-9; her later statement that she had not approved weakening CS-9 confirms that the baseline standard remains intact. A monitored exception is not the same as a normative replacement. Therefore, any case that uses `Auto_Approve` or delayed quality review must be justified by an exception, not by treating the exception as the new rule.

**Expired authority is not authority.** FD-7 expired automatically on 2026-07-12 at 23:59. SUP-705 was activated on 2026-07-13. Operations argues that the flood emergency still factually existed, but a formal control framework cannot depend on unrecorded factual assertions. If the emergency persisted, the operator should have obtained a written extension or a new directive before continuing to use emergency procedures. No such document exists, so SUP-705 is noncompliant.

**M0 hides serious deviations.** SUP-703 is not an edge case: a high-risk, new foreign supplier was activated without manual procurement approval and with quality review after activation. The first purchase was EUR 180,000. This is exactly the case CS-9 was designed to control. SUP-708 is more severe: a high-risk supplier was activated before the sanctions check. No emergency, no pilot, and no documented exception apply. SUP-712 fits M0 but violates the CS-9 resource rule because the requester and manual approver are the same human through aliases. SUP-715 fits M0 but involves an unreported conflict-of-interest relationship. Control-flow conformance does not make these cases safe.

**Normalization of exceptions is a systemic risk.** Aggregate evidence shows 312 cases used `Auto_Approve` in June and July. Of these, 14 were clearly ineligible and 9 had missing eligibility attributes. Six more auto-approval cases occurred in the first week of August after PD-3 expired. This pattern is not a model artifact; it is evidence that an exception was allowed to become normal practice. The defense says operations believed permanent approval was imminent; that belief is not policy.

**Desirable behavior does not retroactively amend policy.** `Request_More_Info` is desirable, and SUP-707 shows it was used safely: no supplier was activated before requested evidence arrived. But M0 cannot express it, and no formal amendment yet exists. The proper response is to revise the model through the governance process, not to treat every unmodeled desirable activity as automatically compliant.

**Prosecution concessions.** The prosecution acknowledges genuine model and data defects: M0 has no request loop, notification collection is incomplete after the CRM migration, `QA_Clearance` is a label rename, duplicate register events with the same source transaction ID are data errors, and the scope for rejected leads/reactivations is ambiguous. These defects should be fixed. But they do not excuse high-risk activations before sanctions, expired pilot/emergency authority, or resource/COI failures.

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest process-aware case is that M0 is obsolete, incomplete, incorrectly scoped, and contradicted by better evidence.

**M0 is a simplified model, not the operational process.** A POWL model is only as good as its activity set and scope. M0 has no `Request_More_Info` loop, no pilot route, no emergency route, no reactivation scope, and no handling for the CRM notification migration. The high deviation rate is therefore mostly a measure of model obsolescence, not business misconduct.

**Valid overlays exist.** PD-3 is a steering-committee decision recorded in minutes and email. It has an explicit effective window, explicit eligibility criteria, and explicit conditions. The compliance observer said the pilot could proceed as a monitored exception. SUP-702 is a low-risk DE supplier in an approved corporate group, within the PD-3 window, with quality review next business day and a stable CRM `notification_id`. That is a classic justified exception. SUP-704 is within the FD-7 window, involves a critical relief supplier, has sanctions before activation, spend of EUR 18,000 under the cap, daily monitoring, and retrospective quality/approval. The emergency directive expressly allowed this.

**Concurrency is not an ordering violation.** M0’s `StrictPartialOrder` for `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` has no edge between them. The real invariant is not that sanctions must occur before quality; it is that sanctions must occur before activation. SUP-708, where sanctions occurred after activation, is not defensible. But the defense can still argue that the model should encode the real invariant explicitly, rather than implying it through a monolithic `checks -> decision` structure.

**Missing loops are a model gap.** SUP-707 shows repeated `Request_More_Info` events, all before activation, and no activation before the requested evidence arrived. This is good operational behavior. M0 cannot express it, so the correct classification is `MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT`, not `NONCOMPLIANT`.

**Notification migration explains missing events.** The CRM migration raised missing notification from 2% to 29%. In SUP-702, the notification exists in CRM with a stable `notification_id`. In SUP-706, a CRM message exists at +3 hours, but the case link is a mutable text field. At worst, SUP-706 is data-quality indeterminate. It is not evidence that no notification occurred.

**Scope is ambiguous for rejected leads and reactivations.** SUP-709 is a lead rejected at sanctions pre-screen before quality review. CS-9 explicitly allows an abbreviated pre-screen process for rejected leads. The scope document is ambiguous about whether M0 begins before or after pre-screen acceptance. SUP-710 is a reactivation of a supplier closed six months earlier, with a prior quality certification still valid. No reactivation procedure exists in CS-9. Treating these as M0 violations assumes a scope that the normative materials do not prove.

**Label equivalence and duplicate records are data issues.** SUP-713’s `QA_Clearance` is the renamed `Quality_Review`; audit documents show equivalent content. SUP-711 has two `Register_Supplier` rows with the same source transaction ID, which is a migration duplication, not a second registration. Raw trace deviations of this kind do not establish process noncompliance.

**Defense concessions.** The defense acknowledges that not every deviation is a model defect. SUP-703 is a high-risk, ineligible supplier activated under a falsely applied pilot. SUP-708 is a high-risk activation before sanctions. SUP-714 is use of an expired pilot without permanent authority. SUP-712, if alias resolution is correct, is a segregation-of-duties failure. SUP-715, if the COI policy was triggered, is an undisclosed conflict. Those are genuine risks and cannot be normalized merely because operations wanted a permanent low-risk route or believed the flood emergency had not ended.

### System-level judgment

The operation is **materially noncompliant in aggregate**, but not because M0 is a perfect model. Genuine violations exist: SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714, and SUP-715. The pilot and emergency overlays are defensible only when documented, time-boxed, and correctly applied. The expired-pilot continuation and the post-expiry emergency continuation are not defensible.

M0 is **not valid as the sole normative control-flow model**. It is obsolete and incomplete. A revised architecture must include the request loop, the auto-approval route, the emergency route, explicit rejected-lead/reactivation scope, and data-collection improvements for notifications and actor identity. Enforcing M0 unchanged would punish model/logging defects as if they were operational failures, and would obscure the real high-risk violations.

### Where each side is rejected

**Prosecution rejection.** I reject the claim that M0/CS-9 should simply be enforced unchanged. M0 cannot express approved pilot/emergency behavior, `Request_More_Info`, label equivalence, notification migration, rejected-lead pre-screening, or reactivation. SUP-702 and SUP-704 are better explained as documented exceptions; SUP-706, SUP-707, SUP-709, SUP-710, SUP-711, and SUP-713 are primarily model/data/scope defects, not misconduct.

**Defense rejection.** I reject the claim that M0’s high deviation rate mostly reflects obsolescence and incomplete scope. That argument fails for SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714, and SUP-715. These cases involve ineligible high-risk activation, activation after an expired directive, sanctions check after activation, self-approval through aliases, use of an expired pilot, and an undisclosed COI. No model revision can make those behaviors compliant without new substantive policy and evidence.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The revision should be layered. A single POWL model cannot enforce data, resource, COI, and authority conditions.

### Layer 1: Control-flow model

The executable POWL approximation in Section 7 represents:

- Standard route: `Register_Supplier` -> concurrent `Sanctions_Check`, `Quality_Review`, repeatable `Request_More_Info` -> XOR(`Manual_Procurement_Approval -> Activate_Supplier`, `Reject_Supplier`) -> `Notify_Requester` -> `Close_Case`.
- Low-risk auto-approval route: `Register_Supplier` -> `Sanctions_Check` -> automated master-data validation -> `Auto_Approve` -> `Activate_Supplier` -> `Quality_Review` -> `Notify_Requester` -> `Close_Case`.
- Emergency/provisional route: `Register_Supplier` -> `Sanctions_Check` -> `Activate_Supplier` -> concurrent `Quality_Review` and `Manual_Procurement_Approval` -> `Notify_Requester` -> `Close_Case`.
- Pre-screen reject route: `Register_Supplier` -> `Sanctions_Check` -> `Reject_Supplier` -> `Notify_Requester` -> `Close_Case`.

### Layer 2: Data-aware eligibility, amount, and time rules

Use pandas/data checks, not POWL, for:

- Route eligibility: `auto_eligible = risk == "LOW" and country == "DE" and approved_group == True`.
- Effective windows: `PD-3_active`; `FD-7_active`; any future overlay.
- Spend caps: emergency initial spend <= EUR 20,000; cumulative emergency spend monitored.
- Time limits: `Quality_Review` within two business days after provisional activation in auto/emergency routes.
- Notification: a stable `notification_id` must exist before `Close_Case`.
- Rule/version IDs: every event should carry a `rule_version_id` so the route can be checked against the policy table.

### Layer 3: Resource and conflict-of-interest rules

- Maintain a canonical `actor_id` and an alias-resolution table.
- Require `requester_id != manual_approver_id` for the standard route.
- Record delegation evidence if another human acts on behalf of the named approver.
- Require COI disclosure and review before quality/approval for close personal or reporting-line relationships.
- Store COI declaration IDs on the case.

### Layer 4: Policy-overlay governance

| Overlay | Route | Effective window | Conditions | Authority snapshot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CS-9 baseline | Standard | Permanent | HIGH risk or not eligible for other routes; sanctions+quality before activation; manual approval before activation; different requester/approver | CS-9 version; formal change control |
| PD-3 | Low-risk auto-approval | 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31 | LOW risk, DE, approved corporate group; sanctions+master data before activation; quality within 2 business days; `Auto_Approve` instead of manual | PD-3 v1; steering-committee minutes; monitored exception |
| FD-7 | Emergency/provisional | 2026-07-10 00:00 to 2026-07-12 23:59 | Critical relief; sanctions before activation; spend <= EUR 20,000; daily monitoring; retrospective quality+manual approval within 2 business days | FD-7; no extension without written declaration |
| Proposed permanent low-risk route | Low-risk auto-approval | After formal CS-9 amendment | Same as PD-3, plus audit of eligibility snapshots and no sunset | CS-9 revision; formal approval |

Overlays must have explicit expiry and renewal paths. A monitored exception is not a permanent weakening.

### Layer 5: Explicit scope rules

- **Rejected leads:** if a `Register_Supplier` event is recorded and the lead is rejected before quality review, the pre-screen reject route applies. If rejection occurs before `Register_Supplier`, the case is outside M0.
- **Reactivations:** treat reactivation as a separate case type. Require sanctions and manual approval; require a new `Quality_Review` only if the prior certification is invalid or the risk profile changed. If M0 is intended to cover reactivation, that must be stated explicitly.
- **Withdrawals:** if a supplier withdraws before activation, record a terminal `Withdrawal` event outside the success paths. Withdrawals are not M0 violations.

### Formal mapping

- **POWL:** route topology, activity order, XOR route choice, request loop, notification before closure.
- **DECLARE/log-skeleton-style constraints:** precedence constraints such as `Sanctions_Check` before `Activate_Supplier`, `Manual_Procurement_Approval` before `Activate_Supplier` in the standard route, `Notify_Requester` before `Close_Case`, and `Quality_Review` within two business days after provisional activation.
- **Pandas/data checks:** risk, country, group eligibility, amount caps, rule/version IDs, notification IDs, actor identity, COI disclosure.
- **Organizational controls:** formal authority to create overlays, emergency extensions, COI policy, and exception governance.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

The following code constructs a revised POWL model. It does not read external files. It is a defensible approximation; POWL cannot by itself enforce data, amount, date, identity, or authority conditions.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# Common nodes
register = Transition("Register_Supplier")
notify = Transition("Notify_Requester")
close = Transition("Close_Case")

# --- Standard route ---------------------------------------------------
sanctions_std = Transition("Sanctions_Check")
quality_std = Transition("Quality_Review")

# Request_More_Info loop: LOOP(do=silent, redo=Request_More_Info, exit=silent)
request_enter = SilentTransition()
request_info = Transition("Request_More_Info")
request_exit = SilentTransition()
request_loop = OperatorPOWL(Operator.LOOP, children=[request_enter, request_info, request_exit])

# Sanctions, quality, and request loop are concurrent inside standard checks
standard_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions_std, quality_std, request_loop])

manual_approval_std = Transition("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
activate_std = Transition("Activate_Supplier")
standard_approve = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[manual_approval_std, activate_std])
standard_approve.order.add_edge(manual_approval_std, activate_std)

reject_std = Transition("Reject_Supplier")
standard_decision = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, children=[standard_approve, reject_std])

standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[standard_checks, standard_decision])
standard_route.order.add_edge(standard_checks, standard_decision)

# --- Low-risk auto-approval route -------------------------------------
sanctions_auto = Transition("Sanctions_Check")
master_data = SilentTransition()  # automated check; may not appear as a visible event
auto_approve = Transition("Auto_Approve")
activate_auto = Transition("Activate_Supplier")
quality_auto = Transition("Quality_Review")

low_risk_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions_auto, master_data, auto_approve, activate_auto, quality_auto])
low_risk_route.order.add_edge(sanctions_auto, master_data)
low_risk_route.order.add_edge(master_data, auto_approve)
low_risk_route.order.add_edge(auto_approve, activate_auto)
low_risk_route.order.add_edge(activate_auto, quality_auto)

# --- Emergency/provisional route --------------------------------------
sanctions_em = Transition("Sanctions_Check")
activate_em = Transition("Activate_Supplier")
quality_em = Transition("Quality_Review")
manual_approval_em = Transition("Manual_Procurement_Approval")

# Quality and retrospective manual approval may happen in any order after activation
post_activation_review = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[quality_em, manual_approval_em])

emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions_em, activate_em, post_activation_review])
emergency_route.order.add_edge(sanctions_em, activate_em)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(activate_em, post_activation_review)

# --- Pre-screen reject route ------------------------------------------
sanctions_rej = Transition("Sanctions_Check")
reject_rej = Transition("Reject_Supplier")

reject_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions_rej, reject_rej])
reject_route.order.add_edge(sanctions_rej, reject_rej)

# --- Route choice and top-level model ---------------------------------
route_choice = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.XOR,
    children=[standard_route, low_risk_route, emergency_route, reject_route]
)

revised_M = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, route_choice, notify, close])
revised_M.order.add_edge(register, route_choice)
revised_M.order.add_edge(route_choice, notify)
revised_M.order.add_edge(notify, close)
```

**Limitations of the code**

- POWL cannot express data guards. The XOR route choice is unconstrained in the model; a separate data layer must enforce risk, country, amount, date, and rule-version eligibility.
- `Master_Data_Validation` is modeled as a `SilentTransition` because it is automated and may not be visible in the event log. The data layer should still record an audit flag.
- The `Request_More_Info` loop uses `LOOP(do=silent, redo=Request_More_Info, exit=silent)`, which yields zero or more repetitions. If the installed PM4Py version uses a different LOOP child order, the children should be reordered accordingly.
- Duplicate labels across branches are intentional; `rule_version_id` and `overlay_id` in the event data are required to distinguish which route was actually authorized.

**Event/data-collection proposal**

The revised architecture requires the following data fields to make conformance decidable:

- **Stable notification IDs:** CRM notifications must be mirrored into the onboarding log with an immutable `notification_id` foreign key. Mutable text links are not sufficient.
- **Rule/version IDs:** every event should carry `rule_version_id` (e.g., `CS-9_v2`, `PD-3_v1`, `FD-7_v1`) so the route can be checked against the policy table.
- **Eligibility snapshots:** risk category, country, corporate group membership, and any other route criteria must be captured at case start.
- **Actor aliases and delegation:** maintain a canonical `actor_id` table; store alias resolution evidence and delegation records.
- **COI declarations:** store `coi_declaration_id` and reviewer/approver relationships.
- **Emergency/pilot authority:** store the directive ID, issuing body, effective timestamps, expiry timestamp, and spend cap.
- **Immutable timestamps:** distinguish `effective_timestamp` from `record_timestamp`; corrections must create new events, not edit old ones.

This revised model should not be deployed without the data layer. The data layer is what converts an over-approximate POWL skeleton into an enforceable normative architecture.
