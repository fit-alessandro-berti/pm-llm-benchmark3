# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

**System under review:** Custom-order dossier with 9 activities. Ground truth partially known but two relevant attributes are *missing from the discovery log*: `external/internal` governing Supplier Review (P3) and `affected_check` governing re-work (P6). Any model learned from control-flow alone is therefore necessarily under-specified. Evaluation must reward explicit handling of this uncertainty rather than silent optionalization.

**Intended uses distinguished:**

1.  **Exploration / Discovery:** Help analysts see what happens. High fitness (recall of observed behavior) is valued; low precision is tolerable *if* the permissiveness is documented as noise-tolerance, not as policy. Must not hallucinate policy.
2.  **Conformance Enforcement / Audit:** Decide valid / forbidden for compliance and for automated gating. Requires high precision against a *negative* suite, correct enforcement of mandatory orderings, and ability to reject suspicious re-work (P7). Low fitness on plausible but unseen valid combinations is a defect, not a feature.
3.  **Simulation / What-if:** Generates plausible futures for capacity planning. Needs soundness, appropriate optionalities, and correct conditional branching; over-permissive models produce impossible resource profiles, over-precise models produce over-confident forecasts.
4.  **Stakeholder Communication / Maintainability:** Ownership, impact analysis, and update cost. Block-structured, non-duplicated, explicitly constrained models dominate enumerated variant lists.

**Fitness/precision trade-off by use case:**

*   For exploration: `fitness  0.95` on training, `generalization` not rewarded if it comes from making everything optional. Precision `~0.5-0.7` can be acceptable if explained.
*   For enforcement: `precision` measured against *both* positive log *and* curated negative traces is the dominant metric; `fitness` must remain high on *all* policy-valid traces, including unseen combinations of known fragments. A model that achieves high precision by memorizing training variants (low holdout fitness) is unsafe. Simplicity and soundness are gate criteria, not tie-breakers.

No aggregate score is defensible here because the reported metrics measure orthogonal concerns on different distributions with unknown comparability (see §2, §6). Disaggregated reasoning is required.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

**Strongest case *for* S:**

*   Robust to log deficiencies explicitly acknowledged in F4/F6: missing `Supplier Review` events and missing `external/internal` attribute, missing `affected_check` attribute, and potential missing events due to logging gaps. By making every non-bookend activity optional and parallel, S never rejects a case because of a missing optional review or alternate check order, avoiding false non-compliance on the core mandatory pair (P2) in practice.
*   Predictable visualization: one parallel block + one choice = linear cognitive complexity, cheap alignment (0.018s), zero duplicates, trivial to update when a new activity appears.
*   High observed fitness retention trainholdout (0.9920.981) in the presence of novel holdout combinations suggests it will not block operations on legitimate but unseen orders (F2).
*   Honest about uncertainty: if used *explicitly as an exploratory map with disclosure* that it does not enforce policy, its low precision is a feature (F7) – it invites analyst-driven filtering rather than automated rejection.

**Strongest case *against* S:**

*   **It cannot enforce any policy**. The discovery team’s “robust to missing events” is achieved by making mandatory behavior optional, which conflates *noise tolerance for discovery* with *semantic correctness for enforcement*. For compliance (F1), it is unusable.
*   Silent-transition permissiveness masks missing-attribute problems instead of surfacing them for data-owner resolution.
*   It completely deletes `Correct Dossier` from the language, so correction/re-work (P6/P7) is invisible despite being a known audit risk.

**At least five concrete undesirable/ambiguous behaviors admitted (traces that S accepts but policy forbids or deems suspicious):**

1.  **Skips both mandatory checks:** `Register Dossier, Manager Approval, Release Order` (B3) – P2 violation (both Credit Check and Technical Review mandatory). S admits by taking `` for all four parallel branches. Intentional noise tolerance would be *allowing one* missing event with penalty, not zero-mandatory.
2.  **Skips one mandatory check:** `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Manager Approval, Release Order` and `Register Dossier, Technical Review, Auto Approval, Release Order` – P2 violation. Accidental semantic flaw: `X(Check, )` inside `+` makes each check independent; intended tolerance for *occasional* missing event becomes *systematic* optionalization.
3.  **Expedite before mandatory checks:** `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` (B4) – violates P4 ordering (`Expedite must follow both checks`). S admits because `Expedite Review` is concurrent with checks. This is accidental, not noise.
4.  **Unordered Manager Approval:** `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Release Order, Manager Approval` is *not* admitted (S at least enforces bookends), but `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Supplier Review, Expedite Review, Manager Approval, Auto Approval, Release Order` with *both* approvals is rejected, while `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order, Expedite Review` is rejected – so far so good. However S also admits:
    `Register Dossier, Supplier Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Auto Approval, Release Order` where Supplier Review precedes checks – policy does not explicitly forbid this but operationally Supplier Review should follow checks (P4 analogue). S admits any interleaving; ambiguity is accidental permissiveness, not documented tolerance.
5.  **Silent extra behavior hiding violations:** `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Credit Check, Manager Approval, Release Order` (B8) without `Correct Dossier` – P7 suspicious. S *technically* rejects duplicate due to at-most-once encoding, but a permissive alignment with log-move will still give high fitness (0.98) and may be *reported* as fitting, obscuring the suspicious pattern. The model gives no diagnostic for “repeat without correction.”
6.  **Bonus – correction invisibility:** `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Correct Dossier, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` (B6) – S cannot even parse `Correct Dossier`; alignment fitness treats it as noise. A model that silently deletes re-work cannot be used for audit of corrections (P6/F6).

**Intentional vs accidental:** Tolerating a missing Supplier Review (P3 without attribute) and tolerating alternate Credit/Technical order (P2) are *intentional* and defensible for exploration. Making Credit/Technical/Expedite all optional and unordered, and deleting `Correct Dossier`, are *accidental* semantic flaws that achieve robustness by abandoning policy.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

**Strongest case *for* C:**

*   Mirrors frequent variants verbatim, so training precision 0.941 is genuine on the positive log; for a tiny stable process with exhaustive sampling, enumeration would be audit-ready.
*   Correctly enforces bookends (P1) and at least a subset of correct orderings (CreditTechnical, Supplier before Manager, etc.) and rejects B3, B4, B8 on the surface.
*   Includes a concrete correction pattern (B6-like) and both approval types, which S lacks.
*   Low generalization (0.412) could be framed as conservatism (F8): “unseen = non-compliant until approved” – a defensible stance in highly regulated domains if paired with a rapid exception workflow.

**Strongest case *against* C (and why holdout degradation to 0.744 is diagnostic):**

*   **Branch enumeration is memorization, not learning.** X-of-8-sequences stores observed traces, not rules. Any unseen combination of *known* fragments is rejected:
    *   `Technical Review` before `Credit Check` with `Expedite Review` or `Supplier Review + Expedite Review` or `Auto Approval` in those orders is absent (B5, B2). F4 explicitly flags holdout cases combining Supplier Review + Auto Approval + alternate order – all legitimate under P2–P5. This explains the 23-point fitness drop. High training precision is therefore an *artifact* of enumerating positives, not of capturing policy.
*   **Duplicate labels (33) break governance:** Each activity appears in many branches, so petri conversion duplicates transitions. Impact analysis (“who owns Credit Check?”), patching (fix one branch, miss others), and diagnostic alignments become nondeterministic. F3 is decisive: maintainability failure.
*   **Correction handling is both incomplete and over-constrained:** Only one re-work pattern `Correct  Credit + Technical` (both) is allowed. Policy (F6, P6) requires `Correct  Credit only` *or* `Technical only` *or* `both` depending on affected check. C rejects `B6` (single Technical repeat) if it were valid, and in turn still cannot exploit the missing `affected_check` attribute to validate. It also misses loops with multiple corrections, Supplier Review after correction, Expedite interleavings, etc.
*   **Unseen-combination brittleness compounds with missing-event bias:** If Supplier Review events are missing in some external cases (F4), C will reject them as non-fitting, inflating false non-compliance, while S inflates fitness by ignoring the miss.
*   **Performance and soundness risk:** Enumerated trees converted to nets can be unsound or non-free-choice after unfolding; 0.44s median alignment suggests state explosion. Updating C for a new valid variant requires adding a 9th branch (and more duplicates), guaranteeing complexity growth O(variants).
*   **Positive-log-only precision (F5):** With no negative suite, 0.941 proves only “I don’t generate much beyond what I saw,” not “I reject what I should.” A flower model would have low precision; a memorizing model has high precision; both can be wrong.

In short, C overfits syntactically, under-generalizes semantically, and incurs architectural debt that S avoids – but S avoids it by underfitting.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

Ranked by *decision impact* (high  low). All are plausible given undisclosed seeds, preprocessing, costs, and missing attributes.

**1. Fitness rewarded by optionalizing mandatory behavior (CRITICAL)**
*Making Credit/Technical/Supplier/Expedite all `X(·,)` drives training/holdout fitness 1 irrespective of correctness.* This explains S’s 0.992/0.981. Governance risk: board sees “high fitness = good” and deploys a model that cannot enforce F1. **Impact: could certify a non-compliant model for enforcement.**

**2. Train/holdout leakage / split sensitivity (CRITICAL)**
Unpublished split, no seed, no stratification by variant or by `external` proxy. With only 8 enumerated variants in C vs many valid combinations, a random 80/20 split that by chance holds out the `TRCC + Supplier + Auto` combos (F4) produces the observed 0.744 drop without leakage. Conversely, if holdout was sampled *after* filtering rare variants, leakage inflates S. Without published cases, results are not reproducible and favor the model that happens to match the split’s composition.

**3. Positive-log-only precision (HIGH)**
Precision computed vs observed positives only (F5) penalizes any generalization beyond training, even policy-valid generalization. C’s 0.941 is therefore memorization premium; S’s 0.584 is not evidence of being “too permissive for enforcement” vs “correctly permissive for exploration” – distinction requires a curated negative suite. **Impact: high precision is misread as audit-ready.**

**4. Missing-event bias (HIGH)**
F4 notes Supplier Review events may be missing. Fitness aligns missing events as model-moves. S never incurs cost (activity optional), C always incurs cost (activity required on its branches). This systematically biases fitness in favor of S and biases precision against C, independent of policy. Without handling missing-event annotation, comparison is meaningless.

**5. Duplicate-label effects (HIGH)**
33 duplicates in C fracture state-space, make alignment disambiguation arbitrary, inflate petri simplicity penalty (0.281) and runtime (0.44s), and can distort `precision_alignments` because escaping edges are counted per duplicate transition. The reported 0 duplicate for S is trivially true but hides that silent transitions create related non-determinism. **Impact: precision/runtime not comparable across models.**

**6. Silent-transition effects (MEDIUM-HIGH)**
S uses 4× `` for optionality + 1× `` implied by approval choice? Actually 4 silents; C uses none (?) but its X-branches contain implicit silents after conversion. Alignment precision and fitness are highly sensitive to silent cost (typically 0). With zero cost, S can always “skip” mandatory checks for free, inflating fitness while hiding violations.

**7. Alignment-cost sensitivity (MEDIUM)**
Without documented `cost_log_move`, `cost_model_move`, `cost_sync`, any fitness/precision comparison is contingent. Standard PM4Py defaults (log 1, model 1, sync 0) treat skipping mandatory checks and skipping optional Supplier identically, which is policy-blind. A policy-aware cost (heavy penalty for skipping mandatory, light for optional) would collapse S’s fitness.

**8. Generalization rewarded by excessive permissiveness (MEDIUM)**
Token-based `generalization_tbr` rewards an underfit model (see table). S’s 0.907 is not evidence of “likely to handle future policy-valid cases correctly,” only that it rarely needs to visit new states because it has few constraints.

**9. Stakeholder-score methodology (LOW-MEDIUM)**
Informal 1–5 without task, N, or protocol. Confounds visual simplicity with correct comprehension. S’s 4.4 does not mean stakeholders understand that it permits B3/B4; C’s 1.9 reflects size, not lack of auditability per se. **Impact: should not influence enforcement gate, only communication plan.**

**10. Runtime confounding by model size (LOW)**
0.44s vs 0.018s largely measures branch enumeration and duplicate expansion, not inherent algorithmic inferiority of precise models. A well-constrained precise model (third alternative) can be as fast as S. **Impact: useful for ops planning, not for correctness judgment.**

# 7. Third Alternative

**Strategy chosen:** *Block-structured procedural backbone (POWL) plus DECLARE/resource/data constraints + explicit uncertainty handling and post-hoc policy checks.* This hybrid keeps a sound, maintainable backbone that enforces undisputed orderings (P1, P2 ordering flexibility, P4 precedence, P5 exclusive approval, P1 bookends) while leaving truly data-conditioned behavior (Supplier Review presence, selective re-check) optional *with an explicit flag* rather than silently optional.

Why not pure POWL, pure DECLARE, or attribute-conditioned family alone: Pure POWL cannot elegantly express “at least one of CC/TR after Correct” without blow-up; pure DECLARE loses stakeholder-intuitive sequence; attribute conditioning is ideal but attributes are missing, so we need a model that *documents* the missing-attribute limitation and enforces audit checks externally.

## 7.1 Compact formal description

```
model_third = LOOP(
  backbone_before_correction,
  sequence( Correct Dossier , recheck )
)

backbone_before_correction = StrictPartialOrder {
  nodes: {
    R  = Register Dossier,
    CC = Credit Check,
    TR = Technical Review,
    SR = Option(Supplier Review)   // X(SR, )
    ER = Option(Expedite Review)   // X(ER, )
    APP = XOR( Manager Approval , Auto Approval )
    REL = Release Order
  }
  edges:  // “a  b” means a must precede b if both occur
    R  CC, R  TR,
    CC  SR, TR  SR,
    CC  ER, TR  ER,
    CC  APP, TR  APP,
    SR  APP, ER  APP,  // if SR/ER occur, they precede approval
    APP  REL
  // No edge between CCTR (concurrent), no edge between SRER (concurrent, either order),
  // loop allows repetition
}

recheck = // selective repeat after Correct, at least one mandatory check
  XOR(
    CC,                     // only CC affected
    TR,                     // only TR affected
    AND(CC, TR)             // both affected, concurrent
  )
  // followed by optional SR/ER re-evaluation and re-approval? For minimal version,
  // recheck = recheck_mandatory_checks  APP'  (loop again or REL)
  // Full version allows SR/ER re-occurrence before re-approval.

Loop semantics:  LOOP(body, redo) = body ( redo body )*  – zero or more corrections.
If no correction occurs, trace ends at first REL. If correction occurs, at least one mandatory check must recur (enforced by recheck XOR).

DECLARE / post-hoc constraints (outside POWL, enforced as diagnostics):
  D1: ExactlyOne( Manager Approval , Auto Approval ) — already in POWL but also checked.
  D2: Not(skipped CC and skipped TR) — mandatory pair.
  D3: Precedence( {CC,TR}, ER ) — if ER occurs, both CC and TR must precede it.
  D3b: Precedence( {CC,TR}, SR ) — if SR occurs, both CC/TR precede it (operational interpretation).
  D4: Response( Correct Dossier , {CC,TR} ) — after Correct, at least one mandatory check before next approval/Release. Flag P7 violation: repeat of CC/TR without preceding Correct within same case (suspicious).
  D5: Data constraint future: *when* external/internal attribute available, SR required iff external==true. Similarly, Correctaffected_check attribute determines *which* recheck branch is allowed. Until then, system raises “attribute-missing” warning for manual review.

Uncertainty annotation: SR optionality and recheck selectivity are tagged `uncertainDueToMissingAttribute` so conformance dashboards distinguish “fitting because optional” from “fitting because attribute would have required it.”
```

**Semantics per requirement:**

*   **P1:** `R` minimal, `REL` maximal in PO + `APP  REL` ensures last.
*   **P2:** `RCC`, `RTR`, no edge between CC/TR  both mandatory, either order, concurrent. `CCAPP` and `TRAPP` ensures approval only after both.
*   **P3:** `SR` optional via `X(SR,)`. Edges `CCSR`, `TRSR`, `SRAPP` place it after checks, before approval if present. Missing `external`  optional with warning, not silently assumed internal.
*   **P4:** `ER` optional via `X(ER,)`, edges `CCER`, `TRER`, `ERAPP` enforce after checks, before approval. No edge `SRER` allows either order (covers F4, B5, A1).
*   **P5:** `APP = XOR(Manager, Auto)` enforces exactly one per iteration; final `APPREL` ensures approval before release.
*   **P6/P7:** LOOP with `recheck = XOR(CC, TR, AND(CC,TR))` allows single-check repeat (covers B6 selective re-work) and flags repeat without `Correct` via DECLARE D4 post-check. Multiple corrections allowed via loop. Limitation: without `affected_check`, system cannot auto-validate *which* single-check branch was correct – requires manual attestation or data enhancement.

## 7.2 Self-contained PM4Py code (POWL)

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.objects import Transition, SilentTransition, OperatorPOWL, StrictPartialOrder
from pm4py.objects.powl.objects import Operator

# --- helpers ---
def opt(node):
    """Optional wrapper: X(node, tau)"""
    tau = SilentTransition()
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[node, tau])

# --- atomic transitions ---
t_R   = Transition(label="Register Dossier")
t_CC  = Transition(label="Credit Check")
t_TR  = Transition(label="Technical Review")
t_SR  = Transition(label="Supplier Review")
t_ER  = Transition(label="Expedite Review")
t_MA  = Transition(label="Manager Approval")
t_AA  = Transition(label="Auto Approval")
t_REL = Transition(label="Release Order")
t_COR = Transition(label="Correct Dossier")

# for recheck (fresh instances to avoid identity sharing across PO)
t_CC2 = Transition(label="Credit Check")
t_TR2 = Transition(label="Technical Review")

# --- backbone PO before correction ---
# optional wrappers
opt_SR = opt(t_SR)
opt_ER = opt(t_ER)

# approval choice
app_choice = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[t_MA, t_AA])

# strict partial order: backbone
backbone = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[t_R, t_CC, t_TR, opt_SR, opt_ER, app_choice, t_REL])
# bookends & mandatory checks
backbone.order.add_edge(t_R, t_CC)
backbone.order.add_edge(t_R, t_TR)
# checks -> optional reviews -> approval
backbone.order.add_edge(t_CC, opt_SR)
backbone.order.add_edge(t_TR, opt_SR)
backbone.order.add_edge(t_CC, opt_ER)
backbone.order.add_edge(t_TR, opt_ER)
backbone.order.add_edge(t_CC, app_choice)
backbone.order.add_edge(t_TR, app_choice)
backbone.order.add_edge(opt_SR, app_choice)
backbone.order.add_edge(opt_ER, app_choice)
# approval -> release
backbone.order.add_edge(app_choice, t_REL)

# --- recheck PO (at least one of CC/TR, optionally both concurrent) ---
# XOR(CC, TR, AND(CC,TR))

# we wrap each alternative as a POWL node; simplest: keep XOR flat
xor_single = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[t_CC2, t_TR2, and_both])

# recheck approval (fresh)
t_MA2 = Transition(label="Manager Approval")
t_AA2 = Transition(label="Auto Approval")
app_choice2 = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[t_MA2, t_AA2])

# recheck sequence: recheck checks -> approval
# We need to ensure if SR/ER re-occur after correction they are optional before re-approval.
# Minimal recheck PO: checks -> approval
recheck_body = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[xor_single, app_choice2])
recheck_body.order.add_edge(xor_single, app_choice2)

# if you want to allow SR/ER after correction as well:
t_SR2 = Transition(label="Supplier Review")

opt_SR2 = opt(t_SR2)
opt_ER2 = opt(t_ER2)
# extend recheck_body to include optional reviews between checks and approval
recheck_full = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[xor_single, opt_SR2, opt_ER2, app_choice2])
recheck_full.order.add_edge(xor_single, opt_SR2)
recheck_full.order.add_edge(xor_single, opt_ER2)
recheck_full.order.add_edge(opt_SR2, app_choice2)
recheck_full.order.add_edge(opt_ER2, app_choice2)
# also keep precedence from xor_single -> app_choice2 transitively

# --- loop: backbone ( Correct -> recheck_full )* -> then final Release already inside backbone
# To avoid duplicating Release, we separate body without final Release for loop.
# Create body_no_release for looping: same PO but without t_REL
body_no_release = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[t_R, t_CC, t_TR, opt_SR, opt_ER, app_choice])
body_no_release.order.add_edge(t_R, t_CC)
body_no_release.order.add_edge(t_R, t_TR)
body_no_release.order.add_edge(t_CC, opt_SR)
body_no_release.order.add_edge(t_TR, opt_SR)
body_no_release.order.add_edge(t_CC, opt_ER)
body_no_release.order.add_edge(t_TR, opt_ER)
body_no_release.order.add_edge(t_CC, app_choice)
body_no_release.order.add_edge(t_TR, app_choice)
body_no_release.order.add_edge(opt_SR, app_choice)
body_no_release.order.add_edge(opt_ER, app_choice)

# redo = Correct -> recheck_full
redo_seq = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[t_COR, recheck_full])

# LOOP

# final model_third = loop -> Release Order
model_third = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[loop, t_REL])
```

## 7.4 Limitations caused by missing attributes

*   **Supplier Review:** Cannot decide if absence is compliant (internal) or non-compliant (external missing). Model marks absence as *uncertain-fit* rather than *compliant*; downstream requires case attribute enrichment or sampling audit. Simulations must be run under two scenarios: `P(external)=p` sensitivity.
*   **Affected-check:** Cannot auto-validate that a `Correct Technical Review only` trace (B6) was correct. Model accepts all three recheck branches; validation requires either (a) manual correction reason or (b) future log enrichment. Post-hoc check D4 only verifies *at least one* recheck, not *the right one*.
*   Both gaps mean conformance dashboards must show *three states*: `fit`, `uncertain (missing attribute)`, `violation`, rather than binary fit/non-fit. Neither S nor C exposes this.

**Do not solve missing data by silently treating every activity as optional** – done above via optional wrappers *tagged* as uncertain and via constrained recheck XOR that still enforces mandatory presence where policy is certain.

## 9.4 Missing-event and duplicate-event perturbation

*   **Missing-event:** Randomly delete 5%,10%,20% of `Supplier Review` events from training log copy; measure fitness variance. A correct model should show moderate fitness drop (detects missing mandatory if SR were mandatory) but S should be insensitive (diagnostic of over-permissiveness).
*   **Duplicate-event:** Duplicate `Credit Check`/`Technical Review` without inserting `Correct Dossier`; measure alignment cost and whether `flag_suspicious()` fires. Fitness alone should not be used.

```python
import random, copy
def perturb_missing(log, activity, p=0.1, seed=0):
    random.seed(seed)
    new_log = copy.deepcopy(log)
    for trace in new_log:
        trace[:] = [e for e in trace if not (e["concept:name"]==activity and random.random()<p)]
    return new_log
```

## 9.10 Decision rule for rejection despite good aggregate metrics

Reject a model if *any* of:
*   Accepts 1 hard-forbidden trace from curated negative suite (B3,B4) *or* false-accept rate >5% on negatives.
*   Holdout combination-fitness for known-policy-valid unseen combos (B2/B5 pattern) <0.90.
*   Unsound or contains dead transitions (except ).
*   Duplicate-label count >5 or requires manual branch sync for policy change.
*   Alignment fitness obtained by skipping mandatory checks at default cost but not at policy-aware cost (indicates cost sensitivity masking violation).

This rule would reject S (fails first condition) and C (fails second/fourth) despite their aggregate wins.

# 11. Dissenting View

**Dissent (197 words):**

The board should not retire either original. In a low-trust log with missing attributes and suspected missing events, Model S’s permissiveness is operationally safer than a precise model that blocks legitimate orders. Its 0.981 holdout fitness and 0.018s runtime mean customers are served; Model C’s 0.744 holdout fitness means one in four real future cases would be wrongly flagged non-compliant, creating backlog and workarounds that are themselves non-compliant. Operations (F2) and cost favor S. Audit risk can be mitigated off-model by adding two hard rules outside the miner – “require Credit and Technical before approval” and “require Expedite after checks” – exactly the post-hoc checks you propose, but without paying for a complex POWL rebuild. C’s high precision is not an artifact; it reflects that rare valid combos like B5 are genuinely rare and should require manager review, as the C advocate argues (F8). Simplicity and stakeholder comprehension (4.4 vs 1.9) predict adoption. Governance should approve S for both exploration *and* enforcement with external guardrails, archive C only, and defer the third model until data quality improves.

**What would change my decision:**

I would abandon this dissent if the curated negative suite shows S false-accepts >5% of hard-forbidden traces even with external guardrails (guardrails are not enforced in practice), or if temporal holdout proves S’s high fitness depends on Supplier-missing traces that later prove to be external orders audit samples flagged as non-compliant, or if the POWL+DECLARE third model meets the §10 thresholds *and* demonstrates equal or better runtime and comprehension than S in the preregistered user study. Conversely, if the unseen-combination tests prove B5-type cases are <1% and always manually reviewed anyway, C’s holdout drop becomes tolerable and its enumeration could be justified with tooling to manage duplicates. Concrete evidence of operational harm from false-accepts vs false-rejects – not aggregate scores – should decide.
