## 1. Feasibility model

Source basis: the uploaded case specification, including normative process rules, resource pools, queue snapshots, factual traces, and specified interventions. 

```text
Common skeleton:
Register < Triage

No-originals cases:
Triage < Identity Check
Triage < Document Check
Identity Check < Assessment
Document Check < Assessment
Identity Check || Document Check is allowed

Originals-required cases:
Triage < Identity Check
Triage < Originals Receipt < Document Check
Identity Check < Assessment
Document Check < Assessment

Low-risk and low-value finalization:
Assessment_final < Decision < Notify

High-risk or high-value finalization:
Assessment_final < Compliance Review_final < Decision < Notify

Rework loop:
Assessment_i or Compliance Review_i < Request More Info_i
Request More Info_i < Document Check_{i+1} < Assessment_{i+1}
If high-risk/high-value:
Assessment_{i+1} < Compliance Review_{i+1 or reaffirmed} < Decision

Preliminary work:
Triage < Escalation Advice_prelim
Triage < Compliance Screen_prelim
Compliance Screen_prelim may overlap with Identity Check and Document Check
Compliance Screen_prelim does not replace:
Assessment_final < Compliance Review_final
```

```text
Conditional and declarative constraints:
- Register must be first and Triage must immediately follow it in control-flow order.
- If originals_required = yes, Originals Receipt must precede every substantive Document Check.
- If originals_required = no, Identity Check and Document Check may run concurrently after Triage.
- Assessment may start only after both Identity Check and the relevant Document Check have completed.
- If high_risk = yes or high_value = yes, a final Compliance Review is mandatory after the latest relevant Assessment and before Decision.
- Four-eyes rule: resource(final Assessment) != resource(final Compliance Review).
- Preliminary compliance or escalation work may reduce later service time but cannot satisfy the final compliance obligation.
- Request More Info creates a new evidentiary cycle: new Document Check and new Assessment are required before Decision.
- Resource calendars are capacity constraints: one resource cannot perform two service intervals simultaneously.
- Queue estimates are uncertain forecasts; they constrain plausibility but do not imply deterministic start times.
- Under K-337 only, the intervention changes the allowed decision policy: after Assessment 2, the case must be decided using existing evidence, with rejection or approval-with-conditions allowed, but silent disregard of the discrepancy not allowed.
- Under K-411, timestamps must reflect actual concurrent work; later batch entry cannot be treated as evidence of true concurrency.
```

K-104 and K-411 mainly alter timing and work release: K-104 adds early escalation/compliance preparation and a provisional C1 reservation, while K-411 releases identity and document tasks concurrently despite the legacy lock. K-219 alters routing by moving the document check from D1 to D2. K-337 alters the allowed decision policy because it suppresses a second evidence-repair loop and forces a decision under unresolved discrepancy.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

Factual mechanism:
The factual path did not violate control flow, but it treated the sanctions ambiguity as a late discovery. Identity and Document Check could already proceed in parallel because no originals were required, so the major loss was not verification sequencing. The loss came from escalation and compliance being postponed until after A1 had already invested assessment effort. C1 then started much later and had to review evidence from scratch, while C1 was already a scarce resource with a large Monday and Tuesday backlog.

Minimal intervention:
The minimal change is not to skip any mandatory activity. It is to add a preliminary S1 packet immediately after Triage and to place a soft provisional C1 reservation keyed to expected post-assessment readiness. The original verification routing remains unchanged: I1 handles Identity Check, D1 handles complex corporate documents, A1 performs Assessment, and C1 still performs final Compliance Review after Assessment.

Temporal propagation:
Early S1 advice can convert a post-assessment ambiguity into a prepared compliance issue. C1 can pre-read sanctions context and evidence structure while Identity and Document Check run. This does not make the final review legally complete, but it can reduce final service time and reduce the chance that the case waits behind a newly formed compliance queue after A1 completes. The benefit is bounded because final C1 work cannot begin before the latest Assessment, and the case still waits for D1’s complex-document queue.

Process-feasibility check:
The counterfactual obeys `Register -> Triage`, allows concurrent Identity and Document Check because originals are not required, waits for both checks before A1, and keeps final C1 after A1. The four-eyes rule is preserved because A1 performs the final Assessment and C1 performs Compliance Review. Preliminary compliance is explicitly not counted as final compliance.

Best/base/worst case:
Best case: S1 resolves the ambiguity early, C1 pre-reads enough evidence, and the reserved slot closely follows A1 completion, saving perhaps 8-12 h. Base case: final C1 review is shorter but still occurs Wednesday, saving about 3-7 h. Worst case: the packet is incomplete, the reserved slot is mistimed, or urgent cases displace the reservation, giving little target-case gain and causing spillover delay elsewhere.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:
If event data showed that C1’s factual delay was caused by fixed working-hour availability rather than queue position, or that preliminary packets frequently change after Document Check, then early C1 preparation would have low value. Evidence of urgent compliance cases breaching deadlines due to reservations would also reverse a positive operational conclusion.

### K-219

Factual mechanism:
The factual route used D1 because handwriting was difficult. That protected accuracy but exposed the case to D1’s long queue. Since originals arrived Monday 17:30, Document Check could not validly start before then. After D1 completed Tuesday 13:30, the case waited for A2 and then for M1, closing Wednesday morning.

Minimal intervention:
The minimal change is to assign the document task to D2 immediately after Originals Receipt rather than wait for D1. Identity Check remains independent and already completes before Document Check readiness becomes decisive. The decision path remains low-risk/low-value, so no Compliance Review is added.

Temporal propagation:
D2’s shorter queue can move the first Document Check substantially earlier, even if D2’s service time is longer. Earlier document completion may place the case into the Assessment queue sooner. The gain survives only if D2 accepts the handwriting without clarification. If D2 requests clarification, the process does not merely pause; it creates a rework branch that requires a new Document Check and Assessment before Decision.

Process-feasibility check:
Originals Receipt still precedes Document Check. Identity Check and Document Check both complete before Assessment. No Compliance Review is required because the case is neither high-risk nor high-value. If clarification is treated as Request More Info, the new Document Check and new Assessment are required before Decision.

Best/base/worst case:
Best case: D2 starts shortly after originals plus its shorter queue, completes overnight or early Tuesday, and A2 can assess earlier, saving roughly 12-20 h. Base case: D2 completes before or near D1’s factual finish but Assessment remains congested, saving about 6-10 h. Worst case: D2 flags handwriting ambiguity, external clarification takes 8-36 h, and the returned work re-enters Documents and Assessment, producing a delay relative to the factual path.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:
If historical logs show D2 has a low clarification rate and acceptable error rate on handwritten standard documents, the D2 route becomes more attractive. If D2’s clarification rate is high or D1’s queue advantage for handwritten cases is substantial, the factual D1 routing is more defensible despite longer waiting.

### K-337

Factual mechanism:
This case’s long factual duration was driven by repeated evidence repair. After Assessment 2, one inconsistency remained. The second Request More Info crossed a weekend, so external response delay dominated the timeline. Document Check 3 and Assessment 3 then restored evidentiary completeness before C1 and M1 acted.

Minimal intervention:
The minimal intervention is exactly at Assessment 2: stop the second rework loop, create an unresolved-discrepancy decision record, and route the case to final Compliance Review. The intervention does not permit a normal unconditional approval that ignores the issue. It permits only a documented reject or approval with conditions.

Temporal propagation:
Suppressing Request More Info 2 eliminates the Friday-to-Monday external delay, Document Check 3, Assessment 3, and the associated queues. It moves the case from evidence collection into compliance/managerial risk disposition. The time saving is therefore mostly from avoiding external and weekend delay, not from faster internal service. If C1 cannot review until Monday, much of the weekend benefit is lost but the later Tuesday/Wednesday factual work is still avoided.

Process-feasibility check:
The case remains feasible only if final Compliance Review occurs after Assessment 2 and before Decision. The four-eyes rule is preserved with A1 as assessor and C1 as compliance reviewer. Because no second Request More Info is issued, no third Document Check or Assessment is required. The unresolved discrepancy must be visible in the decision rationale.

Best/base/worst case:
Best case: C1 reviews Friday and M1 decides Friday, saving roughly 100-115 h but possibly changing the decision to conditional approval or rejection. Base case: C1/M1 act Monday, saving roughly 45-70 h. Worst case: the internal case closes faster but a rejection is appealed or a conditional approval later produces loss exposure, making true end-to-end performance no better or worse.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:
If later data show that second supplier confirmations almost never change the decision, the intervention becomes safer. If the remaining discrepancy is predictive of fraud, loss, appeal, or compliance findings, then the speed benefit should be treated as a harmful truncation rather than an improvement.

### K-411

Factual mechanism:
The factual case was standard, digital, low-risk, and did not require originals, so Identity Check and Document Check were allowed to run concurrently. The legacy system instead released only the identity task. Document Check began after Identity Check completion, and the case reached Assessment after the assessment queue had grown.

Minimal intervention:
The minimal change is to create both verification tasks at Triage and permit I2 and D2 to work concurrently through a controlled workaround. The resources and substantive checks remain the same. The logging rule is part of the intervention because without real-time timestamps, the event log could show artificial concurrency rather than actual parallel execution.

Temporal propagation:
The direct saving is limited to the overlap between Identity Check and Document Check. Since D2 still has a queue and service time, the case may become assessment-ready only slightly earlier. If A2 is still the next bottleneck, earlier readiness reduces waiting only marginally. The intervention is operationally clean but cannot guarantee earlier final completion unless downstream capacity exists.

Process-feasibility check:
Because originals are not required, parallel Identity Check and Document Check are allowed. Both complete before Assessment. No Compliance Review is needed. Decision precedes Notify. Feasibility depends on real concurrency and auditable timestamps, not on after-the-fact batch entry.

Best/base/worst case:
Best case: parallel release lets D2 finish early enough to catch an earlier A2 or M1 slot, saving perhaps 6-12 h. Base case: it saves 1-4 h or mainly reduces latent waiting while Assessment still starts the next morning. Worst case: Assessment congestion absorbs all benefit, or workaround overhead and poor logging add audit risk without cycle-time gain.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:
If logs show that assessment start times are insensitive to verification readiness, cycle-time benefits would be minimal. If workaround timestamps are often back-entered or inconsistent, the intervention should be rejected until logging control is fixed.

For a 100-case weekly rollout, local counterfactual gains become interdependent. K-104 is the clearest interference risk: a single provisional C1 reservation can help one prepared case, but 100 similar reservations compete for the only fully certified compliance reviewer. If many packets are incomplete, C1 time shifts from final reviews to speculative pre-reading, and urgent cases may be displaced. A reservation policy would therefore need eligibility gates, release rules, and explicit measurement of non-target compliance delay.

K-219 creates substitution between D1 and D2. Moving standard handwritten cases to D2 can protect D1 for complex corporate documents, but only until D2’s shorter queue disappears. If D2’s clarification rate rises, the system creates extra external responses, second Document Checks, and new Assessments. The apparent document-queue improvement can migrate to Assessment or rework queues.

K-337 reduces internal loops but changes the risk profile. Across 100 cases, avoided rework may look like a large throughput gain, yet unresolved discrepancies may reappear as appeals, losses, complaints, or compliance findings. Weekend effects are especially important: suppressing a Friday rework request saves much more than suppressing a Tuesday request, so average effects are timing-dependent.

K-411 is operationally safer but may reveal bottleneck migration. Parallel verification makes cases assessment-ready earlier, but if A2/A1 remain congested, the assessment queue grows and final completion barely changes. Its main measurement hazard is timestamp behavior: batch-entered work can create false concurrency, overstating benefits and hiding simultaneous workload pressure.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is K-411. It corrects a release-timing defect in a case type where parallel Identity Check and Document Check are already allowed. It does not weaken evidence requirements, does not change decision criteria, and is easy to audit if real-time timestamps are enforced. Its benefit is not guaranteed to be large, because earlier verification readiness may simply expose Assessment as the next bottleneck, but the downside is limited and learnable.

The most dangerous intervention is K-337. It can produce the largest apparent internal cycle-time saving, especially when the avoided Request More Info would cross a weekend. However, it is not just an acceleration. It changes the evidentiary decision policy by forcing C1 and M1 to decide under a known unresolved discrepancy. That may be defensible for a documented rejection or tightly conditioned approval, but it can also shift work and risk into appeals, losses, or compliance exceptions.

The local counterfactual least transportable to a system-wide policy is K-104’s C1 reservation logic. In one case, early compliance preparation can reduce final waiting and scratch-reading. Across many cases, the same mechanism competes for the single certified C1 reviewer and may delay urgent non-target cases. The local gain is partly a queue-position gain, so broad rollout can cancel or reverse it.

The most valuable additional data are resource-calendared queues, true service intervals, reservation release times, rework reasons, external-response timestamps, and post-decision outcomes such as appeal, loss, conditions breached, and compliance exceptions. Without those, cycle-time improvements may be confused with displaced work or weakened quality.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **temporal** — Queue snapshots are treated as approximate forecasts of waiting time around the snapshot, not deterministic schedules.

2. **resource** — Resources can work the shown factual hours, including early/late service intervals, but still cannot overlap their own service intervals.

3. **structural** — Preliminary S1 or C1 work in K-104 can be added without changing the mandatory final high-risk/high-value compliance path.

4. **quality** — D1 has a meaningful quality advantage over D2 for difficult handwriting, even though the document set is otherwise standard.

5. **behavioral** — In K-219, D2 is more likely than D1 to request clarification rather than silently accept ambiguous handwriting.

6. **structural** — In K-337, suppressing Request More Info 2 means no new Document Check or Assessment is required, because no new evidence is requested.

7. **interference** — A C1 slot reserved for one preliminary compliance case can delay another urgent high-risk/high-value case.

8. **interference** — Making many K-411-style cases verification-ready earlier can migrate the bottleneck from Identity/Documents to Assessment rather than reducing final completion proportionally.
