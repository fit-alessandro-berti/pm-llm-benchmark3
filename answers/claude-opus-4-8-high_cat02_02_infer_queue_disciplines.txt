# 1. Queue-by-queue diagnosis

Convention used throughout: a "decision opportunity" is a moment when the serving resource becomes free (or a preemption occurs) and at least one eligible job is waiting. I reconstruct the *eligible waiting set* at each such moment (respecting arrival time, the ≤2-minute scan delay, skill, and resource availability) and compare the observed pick against the pick each candidate rule would make. I distinguish **observations** (what the log shows), **estimates/bounds** (what I infer with stated uncertainty), **hypotheses** (candidate disciplines), and **recommendations** (Section 6).

| queue | primary_discipline | secondary_or_hybrid_component | confidence_0_to_100 | strongest_supporting_episode | strongest_contradicting_episode | main_identifiability_problem |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| QA | FIFO | Appointments are order-consistent, so effectively inert; priority field is ignored | 82 | At 08:32 A02 (p3, arr 08:04) is chosen over A04 (**p1**, arr 08:13) — priority is not honored, arrival order is | A03 chosen at 08:20 over A02 — but A03's 08:20 appointment coincides with FIFO-adjacent order, so it is not a true violation | Appointment times fall in the same order as arrivals, so FIFO vs appointment-scheduling are observationally near-equivalent |
| QB | Preemptive priority (triggered by P1 arrivals) | FIFO baseline for the non-P1 backlog + strong "resume-the-interrupted-job" preference | 68 | B01 (p3) is preempted mid-service at 09:16 by the just-arrived B02 (**p1**) — a genuine preemption, not just reordering | At 10:28 B04 (p3, arr 09:35) is chosen over B06 (**p2**, arr 10:05) — priority is *not* applied to p2, revealing FIFO underneath | One interruption is labeled "dispatcher reassignment" (exogenous), so we cannot tell policy from ad-hoc override; scan delay clouds whether B05 (p1) was visible at 09:48 |
| QC | SPT/SEPT (on the visible estimate) | Deadline/priority *override when an SLA is endangered* (policy switch) | 66 | At 10:10 C02 (est 8) is chosen from a backlog of est {36,8,14,22}; at 10:18 C03 (est 14) is chosen over C05 (**p1**, deadline 10:50) — smallest estimate wins over both priority and deadline | At 10:54 C05 (est 40, **p1**, deadline already blown) is chosen over C06 (est 6) — the opposite of SPT | est_min may be noisy/strategic; the switch point (endangered SLA) means two policies coexist, so no single rule fits the whole trace |
| QD | Skill-based routing (per-skill resource pools) | Priority/SPT tie-break *within* a mechanical pool; appointment honored exactly for D06 | 80 | D05 (firmware+electrical) waits for D-F, "the only fully qualified resource," and D10 (electrical, **p1**) waits until a qualified resource frees at 12:16 — routing is skill-gated | D08 (**p1**, mech) at 11:35 jumps ahead of D09 (p3, mech, arr earlier) on D-M — a within-pool priority/SPT effect that pure skill routing does not predict | Each skill maps to essentially one feasible resource at a time, so skill routing, resource-availability constraints, and any secondary rule are hard to separate |

Confidences are deliberately moderate: every queue contains at least one episode that a single clean rule cannot explain.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

**QA — primary: FIFO.**
- Supporting: A02 over A04-p1 (08:32); A05 over A06-p2 (09:05); A07 over A08-p1 (09:32); A08 (p1) served dead last. Priority is repeatedly ignored → strong FIFO support.
- Contradicting: A03 chosen at 08:20 (has 08:20 appointment). **Explainable by constraint**: its appointment time coincides with its near-FIFO position; no undocumented exception needed. A03's own note "appointment arrived early" plus service exactly at 08:20 is equally consistent with FIFO and appointment scheduling.

**QB — primary: preemptive priority for p1 over a FIFO baseline.**
- Supporting: B02 preempts B01 at 09:16; B05 (p1) served immediately at 10:00; interrupted B01 always resumed first (09:31, 10:14).
- Contradicting: B04 (p3) before B06 (p2) at 10:28. **Requires the hybrid caveat**: it shows priority is *not* applied below p1, i.e. the queue is not clean preemptive priority. B01's second stop labeled "dispatcher reassignment" is an **exogenous override**, not policy; treat it as a data-flagged exception. Whether B05 was visible at 09:48 is a **data-quality** open question (scan delay), so the 09:48→B03 pick is not decisive either way.

**QC — primary: SPT with an SLA-endangered override.**
- Supporting: C02 (10:10) and C03 (10:18) are textbook SPT beating both priority (C03/C05) and EDD (C01/C05); C06 (11:34) is SPT.
- Contradicting: C05 at 10:54 (longest, p1, blown deadline) beats C06 (e6) — **explained by the documented policy switch** ("deadline already at risk when selected"). C04 over C06 at 10:32 and C01 over C07 at 11:40 — **partly explained** by draining the initial backlog with C01 held to the tail; residual risk is that est_min is noisy/strategic, so "SPT" may be operating on unreliable numbers.

**QD — primary: skill-based routing.**
- Supporting: D05 waits for the "only fully qualified resource"; D10 (p1) waits ~50 min purely for a qualified free resource; D07 forced onto D-X; D02/D03 forced onto D-F.
- Contradicting: D08 (p1) ahead of D09 within the mech pool — **explained by a within-pool secondary rule** (priority or SPT), not a violation of skill routing. D06 served exactly at its 11:30 appointment — an appointment overlay on the skill routing, again not a contradiction.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

**Episode 1 — QC at 10:32 (C04 chosen over C06).** Two strongest explanations:
- (a) *SPT on the initial backlog only* (newer C06 not yet folded into the sort): predicts C04 now, then C06 later.
- (b) *SPT with a scan-delay artifact* (C06 arrived 10:25 but not visible until as late as 10:27 — should be visible by 10:32, so this is weak) or *pure global SPT* which would instead predict C06.
Next-job prediction diverges at the *following* decision: under (a) after C04 the sort restarts and C06 (e6) is picked at 10:54 — but observed is C05, so (a) must be *combined* with the SLA override. Under a pure global-SPT (b) C06 would already have run at 10:32. **Discriminating observable**: the *dispatch-decision timestamp and the eligibility set the dispatcher actually saw* at 10:32 (did C06 appear in it?). If C06 was in the eligibility set and still skipped, SPT is not global and (a) is confirmed; if it was absent, the scan-delay data-quality story holds.

**Episode 2 — QB at 09:48 (B03 chosen while B05-p1 exists).** Two strongest explanations:
- (a) *Preemptive priority, but B05 not yet visible* (scan delay): the highest visible priority is B03 (p2), so B03 is correct; B05 then runs next at 10:00.
- (b) *FIFO-among-visible with a one-off dispatcher reassignment*: B03 happens to be picked and B05's later start is coincidental.
Under (a) the very next pick *must* be B05 the instant it becomes visible — which matches (10:00). Under (b) the next pick would follow arrival order (B04, arr 09:35, before B05). Observed next pick is B05 → favors (a). **Discriminating observable**: B05's actual *queue-entry/scan timestamp* (was it ≥09:48?) plus the *preemption/selection reason code* at 09:48. If B05 entered the visible set only at ~10:00, preemptive-priority (a) is confirmed and the "reassignment" label is orthogonal.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked most→least plausible:

1. **Skill/availability constraints masquerading as policy (QD, and B/C resource gaps).** Highly plausible and directly evidenced (D05, D10). Effect: removes most apparent QD "delays" from any dispatch rule; QD's real policy is only visible in the one contested mech-pool pick. Raises QD confidence in *skill routing* but lowers confidence in identifying its secondary rule.

2. **Policy switch under endangered SLA (QC).** Plausible and self-labeled (C05 note). Effect: means QC is genuinely two-regime; any single-rule diagnosis is wrong by construction. Strengthens the "SPT + urgency override" reading and caps QC confidence.

3. **Delayed arrival scans (≤2 min) creating phantom priority inversions (QB 09:48; QC 10:32).** Plausible data-quality effect. Effect: could fully explain the B05/B03 ordering as correct preemptive priority; could not explain C04>C06 (2 min is not enough by 10:32). Raises QB-priority confidence, leaves QC anomaly standing.

4. **Noisy or strategically inflated est_min (QC, QD).** Plausible; the prompt flags it. Effect: "SPT" may be operating on unreliable inputs, so QC could be mis-labeled if estimates were gamed; also confounds the D08-vs-D09 SPT-vs-priority split. Lowers precision of every SPT claim.

5. **Resume-cost / anti-fragmentation preference (QB).** Plausible: B01 is always resumed before a fresh job (09:31, 10:14) even when a higher-priority-than-p3 job waits. Effect: this is itself a mini-policy; without it QB looks like inconsistent priority. Explains the resume picks as rational, not violations.

6. **Undocumented one-off dispatcher override (QB "reassignment").** Least generalizable. Effect: if genuinely ad-hoc, it should be excluded from policy inference entirely; treating it as policy would over-fit.

# 6. Additional event data needed

Prioritized, tailored to the specific ambiguities above:

1. **Dispatch-decision timestamp + eligibility set actually seen at each decision.** Resolves QC 10:32 (was C06 visible?) and QB 09:48 (was B05 visible?). Highest value — it separates SPT-scope and scan-delay stories from real policy.
2. **Accurate queue-entry / scan timestamp per job (with scan latency).** Directly quantifies the ≤2-min delay effect on QB/QC inversions.
3. **Preemption/selection reason codes (structured, not free-text).** Distinguishes "urgent p1 arrival" (policy) from "dispatcher reassignment" (exogenous) in QB; tells us whether D08's jump was priority- or SPT-driven.
4. **Current estimated *remaining* time at each decision (not just initial est_min), plus realized vs estimated duration.** Tests whether QC's "SPT" is on remaining work and whether estimates are gamed (QC/QD).
5. **Resource state timeline (busy/idle/skill-available) at minute granularity.** Confirms QD routing is forced by availability, not chosen; validates D-F meeting and D-M/D-X windows.
6. **Appointment *tolerance window* (not just target time).** Distinguishes QA appointment-scheduling from FIFO (do early/late arrivals get served at appointment ± tolerance or in arrival order?), and validates D06's exact-minute honoring.
7. **Deadline/SLA breach flags and the trigger threshold for escalation.** Pins down QC's switch point (when does urgency override SPT?).
8. **Policy-version / configuration ID per queue and per time window.** Detects whether QC (or any queue) actually reconfigured mid-day rather than us inferring a switch.
9. **Priority reassignment history.** Checks whether priorities changed after arrival (would explain some QB/QC inversions without a policy change).

# 7. Final synthesis

- **Most identifiable: QA.** It is FIFO with high confidence: priority is ignored in two independent p1 cases, and the only non-FIFO-looking pick (A03) is appointment-order-consistent. The residual ambiguity (FIFO vs appointment scheduling) is benign because both predict the same sequence here.
- **Least identifiable: QC.** It genuinely mixes SPT with a deadline/priority override that activates when an SLA is endangered, and it runs on est_min values that may be noisy or strategic. No single rule fits the whole trace, and two picks (C04>C06, C01>C07) remain only partially explained. QD is *forced* by skills (easy to label "skill routing") but its secondary rule is under-identified; QC is the one where the *primary* label itself is unstable.
- **Most dangerous claim to infer from these data alone: "QB is preemptive priority."** The one clean preemption (B02 over B01) is seductive, but p2 is *not* prioritized over older p3 (B04>B06), one interruption is an exogenous "dispatcher reassignment," and a scan delay may explain the B05 timing. Asserting a clean preemptive-priority discipline would over-fit a single episode and mis-predict the non-p1 backlog behavior. The safer statement is: QB is a FIFO baseline with p1-triggered preemption and a resume-first preference — a hybrid — and it should not be operationalized as pure priority without the reason-code and scan-timestamp data in Section 6.

Cross-cutting caution consistent with the quality bar: not every FIFO violation is priority (QA proves priority is inert there; QB shows priority only at p1), and skill constraints, appointments, urgency, and shortest-job effects are frequently observationally equivalent (QD routing, QC's SPT-vs-EDD picks, QA's FIFO-vs-appointment order). Confidences above are capped accordingly.
