# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

The two submissions should not be judged as though one control-flow model must simultaneously serve discovery, enforcement, simulation, and communication. Those uses impose different loss functions and different tolerances for false acceptance and false rejection. The review below therefore treats the reported metrics as diagnostic evidence rather than as a leaderboard. 

| Intended use                   | Primary need                                                                                        | Acceptable fitness–precision trade-off                                                                                                                                 | Position on the submitted models                                         |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Exploratory process mining     | Reveal common behavior, combinations, and suspected data-quality problems                           | High fitness and broad behavioral coverage may justify moderate precision, provided the model is explicitly labeled non-normative                                      | Model S has value as an exploratory behavioral envelope                  |
| Compliance enforcement         | Reject hard policy violations while accepting approved combinations                                 | Mandatory controls cannot be optionalized merely to increase fitness. False acceptance of B3/B4 is more serious than moderate fitness loss                             | Neither submitted model is adequate                                      |
| Operational conformance triage | Separate clear violations, plausible data errors, and unresolved conditional cases                  | A three-way result—pass, fail, review—is preferable to forcing every case into conforming/nonconforming                                                                | The third alternative is designed for this use                           |
| Simulation                     | Generate behavior with valid control flow, data conditions, probabilities, durations, and resources | Soundness and behavioral validity are necessary but not sufficient; observed frequencies and missing events cannot be treated automatically as execution probabilities | None of the three models is simulation-ready without enrichment          |
| Stakeholder communication      | Make rules understandable and changeable                                                            | Simplicity matters, but a simple diagram must not communicate false policy semantics                                                                                   | Model S is understandable but misleading; Model C is detailed but opaque |

The following assumptions resolve only what is necessary:

1. **Standard process-tree semantics.** `->` denotes sequence, `+` parallel composition, `X` exclusive choice, and `tau` a silent activity. There are no implicit loops.
2. **Complete-trace acceptance.** A model accepts a probe only when the complete visible trace from Register Dossier through Release Order belongs to its language.
3. **Exact labels.** Labels in the probes match the leaves in the expressions exactly.
4. **Supplier scope.** P3 is interpreted as “external orders require Supplier Review; internal orders do not require it.” The supplied policy does not clearly say that Supplier Review is forbidden for internal orders.
5. **Latest-valid-check interpretation.** For enforcement, Expedite Review should follow the latest required versions of both mandatory checks. Thus, a subsequent correction should not occur after Expedite Review unless the expedite rule is clarified.
6. **Correction cycle.** Each Correct Dossier should be followed, before the next correction or approval, by a nonempty subset of `{Credit Check, Technical Review}`. The subset must equal the affected checks when that information is available.
7. **Missing affected-check data.** A trace such as B6 is not automatically passed or failed when the affected-check attribute is absent; it receives a review status.
8. **Observation versus execution.** An absent Supplier Review can reflect either nonexecution or a missing event. A control-flow model alone cannot identify which explanation is true.
9. **P7 enforcement.** A repeated mandatory check without an intervening correction is treated as a hard violation for compliance, although an exploratory analysis may instead flag it as suspicious.

More importantly, a weighted average could allow excellent runtime or simplicity to compensate numerically for accepting a trace that violates a mandatory control.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

## Strongest case for Model S

Model S has a coherent purpose **when narrowly framed as exploration**. Its visible language is approximately

[
R \cdot \operatorname{shuffle}(C?,T?,S?,E?) \cdot (M \oplus A) \cdot F,
]

where each selected review occurs at most once. This gives it several useful properties:

* It compactly represents both orders of Credit Check and Technical Review.
* It naturally combines Supplier Review, Expedite Review, and either approval without enumerating every combination.
* It can fit logs in which optional or poorly recorded review events are absent.
* It has one visible leaf per activity, low alignment runtime, high reported holdout fitness, and high stakeholder readability.
* It correctly enforces one approval choice, approval before release, and a fixed start and end.

If its purpose is to expose observed combinations, highlight missingness, or provide an upper behavioral envelope, its low precision may be an intentional choice rather than a defect.

## Strongest case against Model S

The central problem is that its “noise tolerance” has been encoded in the **model language**. A missing mandatory event could instead be represented by a log move during conformance checking. By putting `tau` beside Credit Check and Technical Review, S states that omitting them is normal model behavior.

Concrete undesirable or unresolved behavior admitted by S includes:

| Admitted trace or context                                                                                                        | Why it is undesirable or ambiguous                                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Register Dossier, Manager Approval, Release Order`                                                                              | Both mandatory checks are skipped; this is B3                                                      |
| `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Auto Approval, Release Order`                                                                   | Technical Review is skipped                                                                        |
| `Register Dossier, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order`                                                            | Credit Check is skipped                                                                            |
| `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order`                             | Expedite Review occurs before either mandatory check; this is B4                                   |
| `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Expedite Review, Technical Review, Auto Approval, Release Order`                                | Expedite occurs before both checks have completed                                                  |
| `Register Dossier, Supplier Review, Manager Approval, Release Order`                                                             | Supplier Review does not compensate for the missing mandatory checks                               |
| `Register Dossier, Supplier Review, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order`            | Expedite is early even though the eventual trace contains both mandatory checks                    |
| External-order context with `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Auto Approval, Release Order`                     | S accepts an external case without its required Supplier Review                                    |
| Internal-order context with `Register Dossier, Supplier Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` | S cannot determine whether the Supplier Review is unnecessary but tolerated or actually prohibited |

The first seven are direct language properties. The last two expose the missing data condition: the same visible trace can have different policy status depending on an absent attribute.

S is also **underpermissive in a different dimension**. It contains no Correct Dossier and no loops, so it rejects every legitimate correction and recheck pattern, including B6 when the correction affected Technical Review alone. It rejects B8, but only because no activity can repeat—not because it represents P7’s causal rule.

The distinction is therefore:

* **Intentional permissiveness:** broad combinations, optional Supplier Review, and tolerance of possibly missing review events for exploration.
* **Accidental semantic flaws:** optional mandatory checks, unconstrained Expedite ordering, absent correction behavior, and no conditional Supplier semantics.

Model S should not be described merely as “robust to missing events.” It is robust partly because it reclassifies potentially serious omissions as perfectly conforming behavior.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

## Strongest case for Model C

Model C is a defensible **temporary whitelist of eight known traces**. It has several genuine strengths:

* Every branch contains both Credit Check and Technical Review before approval.
* It rejects B3, B4, B8, double approvals, and arbitrary review ordering.
* Its training precision of 0.941 is consistent with a deliberately narrow model.
* Each branch can be reviewed individually and tied to a specific observed variant.
* Under a strict “deny until explicitly approved” governance philosophy, low holdout fitness can be interpreted as cases requiring review rather than cases the model should automatically accept.

If the eight branches had been explicitly approved as the complete authorized catalog, exact enumeration could be a legitimate policy implementation.

## Strongest case against Model C

The evidence does not show that the eight branches are a complete policy catalog. They appear to be frequent training variants encoded as policy. That causes several pathologies.

### Branch enumeration and unseen combinations

Independent rules have been multiplied into complete traces. A change to one dimension—such as allowing Auto Approval after Supplier Review—requires adding branches for every relevant check order, expedite choice, and correction pattern.

Model C rejects, among others:

* `Register Dossier, Technical Review, Credit Check, Auto Approval, Release Order` — B2.
* `Register Dossier, Technical Review, Credit Check, Supplier Review, Expedite Review, Auto Approval, Release Order` — B5.
* `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Supplier Review, Auto Approval, Release Order`.
* `Register Dossier, Technical Review, Credit Check, Expedite Review, Manager Approval, Release Order`.
* `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Expedite Review, Supplier Review, Manager Approval, Release Order`, even though P4 allows Expedite before Supplier.
* Every Supplier Review plus Auto Approval combination.
* Every Expedite Review plus Auto Approval combination.
* Every trace with two correction cycles.

These are not novel activities or necessarily novel rules. They are new products of already known choices.

### Duplicate labels and maintainability

The same business activity is represented by many distinct leaves. Consequently:

* A change to Credit Check semantics must be propagated across numerous branches.
* Transition-level diagnostics can split one business activity into many model nodes.
* Token-based generalization can penalize branch-specific transitions that receive little evidence.
* Impact analysis must determine which copies implement the same rule and which are intentionally different.
* Ownership and control documentation become attached to paths rather than reusable policy components.

The reported duplicate count of 33 is itself not reproducible from the expression under an obvious definition.

### Correction handling

The sole correction branch permits exactly:

`Credit Check, Technical Review, Correct Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval`.

It therefore assumes:

* one correction at most;
* both checks always repeat;
* Credit Check precedes Technical Review both times;
* only Manager Approval follows;
* no Supplier or Expedite Review participates.

That contradicts F6 and does not transparently represent the decision “which checks were affected?”

### Holdout degradation and possible memorization

The fitness decline from 0.973 to 0.744 is consistent with variant memorization. The high training precision is therefore double-edged: a model whose language closely resembles a finite list of training traces will naturally leave few escaping continuations, but that does not show that its restrictions correspond to policy.

The overfitting interpretation is strongly supported by the combination of:

* high training precision;
* low token-based generalization;
* severe holdout fitness loss;
* qualitative evidence of legitimate unseen combinations;
* branch enumeration rather than compositional rules.

It is not conclusively proven because the split, missing events, and cost settings are unknown. Nevertheless, “audit-ready” is not justified. An audit-ready model needs traceable policy rules, curated negative tests, reproducible settings, and explicit handling of unresolved data—not merely a close fit to frequent variants.

# 7. Third Alternative

## Formal design

The recommended third alternative is a **balanced POWL procedural backbone plus tri-state policy checks**.

Let:

* `R` = Register Dossier
* `C` = Credit Check
* `T` = Technical Review
* `S` = Supplier Review
* `E` = Expedite Review
* `D` = Correct Dossier
* `M` = Manager Approval
* `A` = Auto Approval
* `F` = Release Order

The intended structure is:

[
\begin{aligned}
INITIAL &= PO({C,T},\varnothing) \
CYCLE &= D,;,PO({C?,T?},\varnothing) \
CORE &= INITIAL,;,CYCLE^* \
PRE &= PO({CORE,S?,E?},{CORE \prec E}) \
THIRD &= R,;,PRE,;,X(M,A),;,F
\end{aligned}
]

The executable POWL backbone deliberately overapproximates two unresolved conditions:

1. `S?` is structurally optional because the external/internal attribute is unavailable.
2. Both rechecks inside a correction cycle are structurally optional, but an external rule requires a **nonempty** recheck set and compares it with the affected-check attribute when available.

Compliance is therefore not “zero-cost alignment alone.” It is:

[
\text{PASS} =
\text{fits procedural backbone}
\land
\text{no hard policy failure}
\land
\text{no unresolved required attribute}.
]

A structurally fitting case with missing external/internal or affected-check data is **REVIEW**, not PASS.

## Self-contained PM4Py construction and policy checker

```python
from __future__ import annotations

from dataclasses import asdict, dataclass
from typing import Any, Iterable, Mapping, Optional, Sequence

import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    OperatorPOWL,
    SilentTransition,
    StrictPartialOrder,
    Transition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Activity constants
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------

REGISTER = "Register Dossier"
CREDIT = "Credit Check"
TECHNICAL = "Technical Review"
SUPPLIER = "Supplier Review"
EXPEDITE = "Expedite Review"
CORRECT = "Correct Dossier"
MANAGER = "Manager Approval"
AUTO = "Auto Approval"
RELEASE = "Release Order"

CHECKS = frozenset({CREDIT, TECHNICAL})
APPROVALS = frozenset({MANAGER, AUTO})
ALL_ACTIVITIES = frozenset(
    {
        REGISTER,
        CREDIT,
        TECHNICAL,
        SUPPLIER,
        EXPEDITE,
        CORRECT,
        MANAGER,
        AUTO,
        RELEASE,
    }
)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Small POWL constructors
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------

def xor(*nodes):
    return OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, list(nodes))


def optional_activity(label: str):
    return xor(Transition(label), SilentTransition())


def partial_order(nodes, edges=()):
    """
    Build a strict partial order. Edges are pairs of child-node objects.
    Nodes without an ordering edge are concurrent.
    """
    result = StrictPartialOrder(list(nodes))
    for source, target in edges:
        result.order.add_edge(source, target)
    return result


def strict_sequence(*nodes):
    """
    Sequence represented as a transitively closed strict partial order.
    Adding every earlier->later edge avoids relying on implicit closure.
    """
    result = StrictPartialOrder(list(nodes))
    for i, source in enumerate(nodes):
        for target in nodes[i + 1 :]:
            result.order.add_edge(source, target)
    return result


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Procedural backbone
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------

# Mandatory initial checks; neither is ordered before the other.
initial_credit = Transition(CREDIT)
initial_technical = Transition(TECHNICAL)
initial_checks = partial_order([initial_credit, initial_technical])

# One correction cycle: Correct Dossier, followed by zero/one Credit
# recheck and zero/one Technical recheck. A post-hoc rule below rejects
# the empty subset and validates the affected-check condition.
correct_event = Transition(CORRECT)
credit_recheck = optional_activity(CREDIT)
technical_recheck = optional_activity(TECHNICAL)

correction_cycle = partial_order(
    [correct_event, credit_recheck, technical_recheck],
    edges=[
        (correct_event, credit_recheck),
        (correct_event, technical_recheck),
    ],
)

# POWL loop semantics are do, followed by zero or more redo/do cycles.
# A silent do-part therefore permits zero or more correction cycles.
zero_or_more_corrections = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.LOOP,
    [SilentTransition(), correction_cycle],
)

core = strict_sequence(initial_checks, zero_or_more_corrections)

# Supplier is conditionally mandatory, but the conditioning attribute is
# missing. It remains optional in the backbone and is checked separately.
supplier_optional = optional_activity(SUPPLIER)

# Expedite is optional but follows the entire mandatory-check/correction
# core. It is unordered relative to Supplier Review.
expedite_optional = optional_activity(EXPEDITE)

preapproval = partial_order(
    [core, supplier_optional, expedite_optional],
    edges=[(core, expedite_optional)],
)

approval_choice = xor(
    Transition(MANAGER),
    Transition(AUTO),
)

model_third = strict_sequence(
    Transition(REGISTER),
    preapproval,
    approval_choice,
    Transition(RELEASE),
)

# Validate the explicit partial-order relations and create an accepting net.
model_third.validate_partial_orders()
net_third, im_third, fm_third = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_third)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Declarative/data-aware policy layer
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------

POLICY_RULES = {
    "P1": "Register is the unique first activity; Release is the unique last activity.",
    "P2": "Credit and Technical are mandatory before approval and unordered.",
    "P3": "External cases require one Supplier Review; unknown scope is REVIEW.",
    "P4": "Expedite follows the latest required checks and precedes approval.",
    "P5": "Exactly one Manager or Auto Approval occurs.",
    "P6": "Each correction has a nonempty recheck subset matching affected checks.",
    "P7": "A mandatory check cannot repeat without a new correction.",
}


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Finding:
    rule: str
    level: str       # "FAIL" or "REVIEW"
    detail: str


def audit_trace(
    trace: Sequence[str] | Sequence[Mapping[str, Any]],
    *,
    case_attrs: Optional[Mapping[str, Any]] = None,
    affected_by_correction: Optional[Mapping[int, Iterable[str]]] = None,
) -> dict[str, Any]:
    """
    Tri-state semantic audit.

    Parameters
    ----------
    trace:
        Activity labels, or event dictionaries containing "concept:name".
    case_attrs:
        Expected keys when available:
          - external_sourcing: True / False / None
          - supplier_event_complete: True only when extraction completeness
            for Supplier Review is independently established.
    affected_by_correction:
        Mapping from zero-based correction occurrence to the set of checks
        affected by that correction, e.g.
            {0: {"Technical Review"}}
        Missing entries produce REVIEW, not PASS.

    Returns
    -------
    {
        "status": "PASS" | "FAIL" | "REVIEW",
        "findings": [...],
        "correction_segments": [...]
    }
    """
    attrs = dict(case_attrs or {})
    effects = {
        int(ordinal): frozenset(values)
        for ordinal, values in (affected_by_correction or {}).items()
    }

    labels = [
        event if isinstance(event, str) else str(event["concept:name"])
        for event in trace
    ]

    findings: list[Finding] = []

    def fail(rule: str, detail: str) -> None:
        findings.append(Finding(rule, "FAIL", detail))

    def review(rule: str, detail: str) -> None:
        findings.append(Finding(rule, "REVIEW", detail))

    unknown = sorted(set(labels) - ALL_ACTIVITIES)
    if unknown:
        fail("MODEL", f"Unknown activities: {unknown}")

    # P1: start/end and uniqueness.
    if not labels or labels[0] != REGISTER or labels.count(REGISTER) != 1:
        fail("P1", "Register Dossier must occur exactly once and be first.")

    if not labels or labels[-1] != RELEASE or labels.count(RELEASE) != 1:
        fail("P1", "Release Order must occur exactly once and be last.")

    # P5: exactly one approval.
    approval_positions = [
        i for i, label in enumerate(labels) if label in APPROVALS
    ]
    if len(approval_positions) != 1:
        fail(
            "P5",
            f"Expected exactly one approval, found {len(approval_positions)}.",
        )

    approval_index = (
        approval_positions[0] if approval_positions else len(labels)
    )

    if len(approval_positions) == 1:
        suffix = labels[approval_index + 1 :]
        if suffix != [RELEASE]:
            fail(
                "P1/P5",
                "Only Release Order may follow the selected approval.",
            )

    # P2: at least one initial occurrence of each mandatory check
    # before approval.
    positions_before_approval = {
        check: [
            i
            for i, label in enumerate(labels[:approval_index])
            if label == check
        ]
        for check in CHECKS
    }
    for check, positions in positions_before_approval.items():
        if not positions:
            fail("P2", f"{check} is missing before approval.")

    # P6/P7: segment checks by correction occurrences.
    initial_seen = {CREDIT: False, TECHNICAL: False}
    active_segment: Optional[dict[str, Any]] = None
    correction_ordinal = 0
    correction_segments: list[dict[str, Any]] = []

    def close_segment(end_index: int) -> None:
        nonlocal active_segment

        if active_segment is None:
            return

        observed = frozenset(active_segment["observed"])
        ordinal = int(active_segment["ordinal"])

        if not observed:
            fail(
                "P6",
                f"Correction {ordinal} has no mandatory-check recheck.",
            )

        expected = effects.get(ordinal)
        if expected is None:
            review(
                "P6",
                f"Correction {ordinal} has rechecks {sorted(observed)}, "
                "but the affected-check attribute is unavailable.",
            )
        else:
            illegal_expected = expected - CHECKS
            if illegal_expected:
                fail(
                    "P6",
                    f"Correction {ordinal} contains invalid affected checks: "
                    f"{sorted(illegal_expected)}.",
                )
            elif not expected:
                fail(
                    "P6",
                    f"Correction {ordinal} has an empty affected-check set.",
                )
            elif observed != expected:
                fail(
                    "P6",
                    f"Correction {ordinal}: observed rechecks "
                    f"{sorted(observed)} do not match affected checks "
                    f"{sorted(expected)}.",
                )

        correction_segments.append(
            {
                "ordinal": ordinal,
                "correct_index": active_segment["correct_index"],
                "end_index": end_index,
                "observed_rechecks": sorted(observed),
                "expected_rechecks": (
                    None if expected is None else sorted(expected)
                ),
            }
        )
        active_segment = None

    for index, label in enumerate(labels[1:approval_index], start=1):
        if label == CORRECT:
            if active_segment is not None:
                close_segment(index)

            if not all(initial_seen.values()):
                fail(
                    "P6",
                    "Correct Dossier occurred before both initial checks "
                    "were completed.",
                )

            active_segment = {
                "ordinal": correction_ordinal,
                "correct_index": index,
                "observed": set(),
            }
            correction_ordinal += 1
            continue

        if label not in CHECKS:
            continue

        if not initial_seen[label]:
            if active_segment is not None:
                fail(
                    "P2/P6",
                    f"The first {label} occurs only after a correction.",
                )
            initial_seen[label] = True
            continue

        # This is a repeat of a mandatory check.
        if active_segment is None:
            fail(
                "P7",
                f"Repeated {label} at position {index} has no preceding "
                "unconsumed Correct Dossier.",
            )
        elif label in active_segment["observed"]:
            fail(
                "P7",
                f"{label} repeats more than once in correction "
                f"{active_segment['ordinal']}.",
            )
        else:
            active_segment["observed"].add(label)

    close_segment(approval_index)

    # P4: zero or one Expedite; after the latest mandatory-check
    # occurrences and before approval.
    expedite_positions = [
        i for i, label in enumerate(labels) if label == EXPEDITE
    ]
    if len(expedite_positions) > 1:
        fail("P4", "Expedite Review occurs more than once.")

    if expedite_positions:
        expedite_index = expedite_positions[0]

        if expedite_index >= approval_index:
            fail("P4", "Expedite Review must precede approval.")

        if all(positions_before_approval[check] for check in CHECKS):
            latest_required_check = max(
                max(positions_before_approval[CREDIT]),
                max(positions_before_approval[TECHNICAL]),
            )
            if expedite_index <= latest_required_check:
                fail(
                    "P4",
                    "Expedite Review occurs before the latest required "
                    "versions of both checks are complete.",
                )

        if CORRECT in labels[expedite_index + 1 : approval_index]:
            fail(
                "P4",
                "A correction occurs after Expedite Review; under the "
                "conservative interpretation, expedite must follow the "
                "final correction cycle.",
            )

    # P3: attribute-conditioned Supplier Review.
    supplier_positions = [
        i for i, label in enumerate(labels) if label == SUPPLIER
    ]
    if len(supplier_positions) > 1:
        fail("P3", "Supplier Review occurs more than once.")

    if any(i >= approval_index for i in supplier_positions):
        fail("P3", "Supplier Review must precede approval.")

    external = attrs.get("external_sourcing")
    supplier_event_complete = attrs.get("supplier_event_complete") is True

    if external is True and not supplier_positions:
        if supplier_event_complete:
            fail(
                "P3",
                "External case has no Supplier Review in a source known "
                "to be complete.",
            )
        else:
            review(
                "P3",
                "External case lacks Supplier Review, but execution cannot "
                "be distinguished from a missing event.",
            )
    elif external is False and supplier_positions:
        review(
            "P3",
            "Supplier Review occurred for an internal case; the policy says "
            "it is not required but does not clearly say it is forbidden.",
        )
    elif external is None:
        review(
            "P3",
            "External/internal sourcing is unknown, so the conditional "
            "Supplier requirement cannot be decided.",
        )

    if any(finding.level == "FAIL" for finding in findings):
        status = "FAIL"
    elif any(finding.level == "REVIEW" for finding in findings):
        status = "REVIEW"
    else:
        status = "PASS"

    return {
        "status": status,
        "findings": [asdict(finding) for finding in findings],
        "correction_segments": correction_segments,
    }
```

The POWL construction uses the formal semantics that unconnected partial-order nodes may execute concurrently and that a two-child loop executes its first child and then zero or more repetitions of the second and first children. PM4Py’s current implementation supports `Transition`, `SilentTransition`, `StrictPartialOrder`, XOR/LOOP `OperatorPOWL` nodes, and conversion of a POWL model to an accepting Petri net. ([GitHub][1])

## Explicit semantics and limitations

**Mandatory checks.** The initial Credit Check and Technical Review are visible, mandatory, and unordered. They cannot be skipped with `tau`.

**Supplier Review.** It is optional only in the procedural backbone because the conditioning attribute is missing. The policy layer requires it for a known external case. Unknown scope or suspected missingness produces REVIEW.

**Expedite Review.** It is optional, follows the mandatory-check and correction core, precedes approval, and has no ordering edge relative to Supplier Review. Consequently, it may occur before or after Supplier Review.

**Approval.** The XOR structurally enforces exactly one of Manager Approval and Auto Approval.

**Corrections.** Zero or more correction cycles are supported. Each correction precedes its selected rechecks. The checker rejects an empty recheck set and any repeated mandatory check not attributable to a new correction. With an affected-check attribute, the selected set must match exactly. Without it, the case remains unresolved.

**Remaining limitations.**

* The generic model cannot prove whether Supplier Review was required without external/internal data.
* It cannot prove that the correct recheck subset was chosen without affected-check data.
* It cannot distinguish an omitted event from omitted work unless an independent extraction-completeness signal is supplied.
* The conservative “Expedite after the final correction” interpretation may be stricter than intended and needs owner approval.
* The procedural representation has one initial and one recheck transition for each mandatory-check label. This small amount of duplicate labeling should be documented when interpreting token-based metrics.
* The procedural backbone must never be deployed without the policy checker; otherwise a correction with no selected recheck would remain in the overapproximating language.

# 9. Validation Experiments

## Reproducibility package

Every result should be accompanied by:

* the raw-extract identifier and cryptographic hash;
* PM4Py and Python versions;
* the exact preprocessing source code and configuration;
* lifecycle and timestamp semantics;
* stable event tie-breaking;
* all case IDs assigned to training and holdout;
* random seeds;
* process-model serialization;
* alignment algorithm and cost maps;
* hardware and timeout settings;
* treatment of incomplete cases, duplicate events, and missing attributes.

Exact duplicate events should initially be **flagged, not silently deleted**. Deduplication should be a named sensitivity condition.

A minimal deterministic preprocessing skeleton is:

```python
import hashlib
import json
import platform
from pathlib import Path

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CASE = "case:concept:name"
ACTIVITY = "concept:name"
TIMESTAMP = "time:timestamp"


def preprocess(raw: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    required = {CASE, ACTIVITY, TIMESTAMP}
    missing = required - set(raw.columns)
    if missing:
        raise ValueError(f"Missing required columns: {sorted(missing)}")

    df = raw.copy()
    df["_source_row"] = np.arange(len(df), dtype="int64")
    df[TIMESTAMP] = pd.to_datetime(df[TIMESTAMP], utc=True, errors="raise")

    # Stable ordering for tied timestamps. Do not infer concurrency from an
    # arbitrary unstable input order.
    df = df.sort_values(
        [CASE, TIMESTAMP, "_source_row"],
        kind="mergesort",
    ).reset_index(drop=True)

    duplicate_key = [CASE, ACTIVITY, TIMESTAMP]
    df["_exact_duplicate_candidate"] = df.duplicated(
        duplicate_key, keep=False
    )

    return pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id=CASE,
        activity_key=ACTIVITY,
        timestamp_key=TIMESTAMP,
    )


def dataframe_hash(df: pd.DataFrame) -> str:
    hashed = pd.util.hash_pandas_object(
        df.sort_index(axis=1),
        index=True,
    ).values.tobytes()
    return hashlib.sha256(hashed).hexdigest()


def environment_manifest(df: pd.DataFrame, seed: int) -> dict:
    return {
        "dataframe_sha256": dataframe_hash(df),
        "seed": seed,
        "python": platform.python_version(),
        "pm4py": pm4py.__version__,
        "preprocessing": {
            "timezone": "UTC",
            "tie_breaker": "_source_row",
            "deduplication": "none; candidates flagged",
        },
    }
```

## Repeated case-level and attribute-stratified splits

At least five published seeds should be used. Splitting must occur by complete case before any variant-specific tuning.

The case strata should include, when available:

* external/internal/unknown sourcing;
* Manager versus Auto Approval;
* Supplier present/absent;
* Expedite present/absent;
* Credit-before-Technical versus Technical-before-Credit;
* zero, one, or multiple corrections;
* recheck subset;
* event-source or extraction-completeness status.

The missing sourcing attribute should be restored from the operational source before the primary P3 experiment. If restoration is impossible, “unknown” must be a separate stratum, and no unconditional Supplier-compliance claim may be made.

In addition to repeated random 80/20 splits, conduct:

1. a chronological holdout;
2. a combination holdout in which selected combinations are absent from training;
3. a source-system or location holdout if extraction pipelines differ;
4. a rare-route stress split that deliberately places Supplier-plus-Auto and selective corrections in holdout.

A split generator should publish the actual case IDs, not only the seed.

## Curated positive, negative, and conditional suite

The business-policy suite should include:

* valid: B1, B2, attribute-instantiated B5, B7;
* forbidden: B3, B4, B8;
* conditional: B6 with affected set `{T}`, `{C}`, `{C,T}`, and missing;
* double approvals;
* approval before one or both mandatory checks;
* Release before approval;
* correction with no recheck;
* repeated check twice after one correction;
* external case with no Supplier event under complete and incomplete extraction;
* Expedite after approval;
* correction after Expedite;
* two valid correction cycles.

For each trace, report:

* zero-cost procedural acceptance;
* alignment cost and move sequence;
* policy-check result;
* expected oracle status;
* false-accept, false-reject, or review error.

This is more decision-relevant than positive-log precision alone.

## Missing-event and duplicate-event perturbations

Start with curated valid traces and apply controlled perturbations:

* delete Supplier Review;
* delete Credit Check or Technical Review;
* duplicate a mandatory check without correction;
* duplicate a recheck within one correction cycle;
* delete Correct Dossier while retaining a recheck;
* delete an approval;
* create timestamp ties between C and T;
* swap only activities whose policy relation is concurrent;
* inject a second approval.

For each perturbation rate and type, measure separately:

* change in fitness;
* change in alignment moves;
* change in tri-state policy classification;
* whether a data-quality perturbation is misclassified as a policy pass;
* whether a hard violation is hidden by permissive model behavior.

Model S is expected to be particularly insensitive to deletion of mandatory checks, which is precisely the pathology the experiment should quantify rather than celebrate.

## Unseen-combination tests

Construct a factorial suite over:

* check order: `C,T` or `T,C`;
* sourcing: internal or external;
* Supplier position when external;
* Expedite absent, before Supplier, or after Supplier;
* approval type: Manager or Auto;
* correction count: zero, one, or two;
* affected subset per correction: `{C}`, `{T}`, or `{C,T}`.

Hold out complete combinations while retaining each constituent activity and rule in training. This directly tests compositional generalization:

* S will tend to accept combinations indiscriminately.
* C will tend to reject combinations not enumerated.
* The third model should accept only combinations satisfying its structural and conditional rules.

## Soundness, reachability, and separate metric reporting

```python
import statistics
import time

import pm4py
from pm4py.algo.conformance.alignments.petri_net import (
    algorithm as alignment_algorithm,
)


def evaluate_model(train_df, test_df, model):
    train_log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(train_df)
    test_log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(test_df)

    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)

    sound, soundness_diagnostics = pm4py.check_soundness(
        net,
        im,
        fm,
        print_diagnostics=False,
    )

    reachability_graph = pm4py.convert_to_reachability_graph(net, im, fm)

    train_fitness = pm4py.fitness_alignments(train_log, net, im, fm)
    test_fitness = pm4py.fitness_alignments(test_log, net, im, fm)

    train_precision = pm4py.precision_alignments(train_log, net, im, fm)
    test_precision = pm4py.precision_alignments(test_log, net, im, fm)

    train_generalization = pm4py.generalization_tbr(
        train_log, net, im, fm
    )
    test_generalization = pm4py.generalization_tbr(
        test_log, net, im, fm
    )

    simplicity = pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(
        net,
        im,
        fm,
        variant="arc_degree",
    )

    elapsed = []
    alignments = []
    for trace in test_log:
        started = time.perf_counter()
        result = alignment_algorithm.apply_trace(trace, net, im, fm)
        elapsed.append(time.perf_counter() - started)
        alignments.append(result)

    return {
        "sound": sound,
        "soundness_diagnostics": soundness_diagnostics,
        "reachable_states": len(reachability_graph.states),
        "reachable_transitions": len(reachability_graph.transitions),
        "train_alignment_fitness": train_fitness,
        "test_alignment_fitness": test_fitness,
        "train_alignment_precision": train_precision,
        "test_alignment_precision": test_precision,
        "train_token_generalization": train_generalization,
        "test_token_generalization": test_generalization,
        "simplicity_arc_degree": simplicity,
        "median_alignment_seconds": statistics.median(elapsed),
        "p95_alignment_seconds": (
            float(pd.Series(elapsed).quantile(0.95)) if elapsed else None
        ),
        "alignments": alignments,
        "net": net,
        "im": im,
        "fm": fm,
    }
```

PM4Py exposes POWL/Petri-net conversion, alignment fitness and precision, token-based generalization, simplicity, soundness analysis, and reachability-graph conversion through the APIs used in this skeleton. ([GitHub][2])

Soundness is a hard gate. Reachability analysis should additionally check:

* the final marking is reachable;
* no reachable dead marking other than the final marking exists;
* both approval alternatives are reachable;
* each optional route is reachable;
* one and two correction cycles are reachable;
* no execution reaches approval while an activated mandatory branch remains incomplete;
* the state space remains computationally manageable under the chosen conversion.

Metrics must remain disaggregated by model, seed, split, and stratum. Confidence intervals or empirical ranges across seeds should be reported rather than only one point estimate.

## Alignment-cost sensitivity

At minimum, compare:

1. uniform visible move costs;
2. high costs for missing mandatory checks and approvals;
3. lower cost for a missing Supplier event when extraction completeness is uncertain;
4. asymmetric costs for unexpected duplicate checks versus missing checks.

```python
    return alignments.apply_trace(
        trace,
        net,
        im,
        fm,
        parameters=parameters,
```

## Stakeholder comprehension protocol

Replace the informal 1–5 score with a documented, task-based study:

* recruit representatives from compliance, operations, audit, IT architecture, and process ownership;
* randomize model presentation order;
* conceal model identity and advocacy team;
* provide the same notation tutorial;
* ask participants to classify a balanced set of valid, forbidden, and conditional traces;
* ask them to explain why B3, B5, B6, and B8 are accepted or rejected;
* ask them to implement two policy changes, such as allowing Auto Approval after Supplier Review and adding a credit-only correction;
* measure classification accuracy, explanation accuracy, task time, change errors, confidence, and perceived difficulty separately;
* report results by stakeholder role and use an explicit scoring rubric.

A model should not receive a high comprehension score merely because participants feel confident. Incorrect confident answers are evidence of misleading simplicity.

## Rejection rule despite good aggregate metrics

A model is rejected for compliance deployment if any of these hard gates fails:

1. It is unsound, has an unreachable final state, or has reachable deadlocks.
2. It accepts B3, B4, B8, a double-approval trace, or another board-designated hard negative with zero deviation.
3. It rejects B1 or B2, or rejects attribute-consistent B5/B7 merely because a combination was unseen.
4. It automatically passes cases whose Supplier scope or affected-check set is unresolved.
5. Its conformance decision changes from pass to fail solely under reasonable alignment-cost choices when the underlying policy classification is unchanged.
6. Its results cannot be reproduced from the published split, preprocessing, model artifact, and cost configuration.
7. Its runtime or state-space growth exceeds the deployment service objective.
8. Stakeholders cannot classify hard probes or update a rule at a pre-registered minimum accuracy.

High average fitness, precision, simplicity, or comprehension cannot override these gates.

# 10. Governance Recommendation

**Model S may be retained only for exploration.** It should be labeled “descriptive permissive envelope—not a policy model.” Dashboards using it should display mandatory-check omissions and early Expedite events as explicit overlays rather than describing such cases as conforming. S must be removed from any compliance registry, automated approval control, or normative simulation.

**Model C should be retired as the enterprise conformance model.** Its eight branches may be archived as a historical training-variant catalog and regression-test source. It should not be represented as a complete approved process. Its branch enumeration, correction semantics, duplicate labels, and holdout degradation make continued extension increasingly risky.

**Neither original model should be used for compliance enforcement.** S has unacceptable false acceptances; C has unacceptable false rejections and no evidence that its whitelist is policy-complete.

**The third alternative is the preferred governance candidate**, with the following deployment conditions:

* external/internal sourcing is restored or unknown cases are routed to manual review;
* affected-check information is captured, or corrected cases remain review-only;
* the process owner approves the conservative Expedite-after-final-recheck interpretation;
* the procedural model and policy checker are versioned as one indivisible governed artifact;
* all hard probes and curated negatives pass;
* soundness and reachability checks pass;
* repeated-seed, temporal, stratified, and unseen-combination experiments show acceptable stability;
* alignment settings and preprocessing are published;
* stakeholder testing demonstrates correct understanding, not merely preference;
* every automated decision records model version, alignment diagnostics, policy findings, data-quality status, and final disposition.

For **simulation**, no current model should be approved. The third alternative could later provide the control-flow backbone, but only after adding attribute distributions, routing probabilities, correction probabilities, resource constraints, durations, and an explicit missing-event mechanism.

The practical governance disposition is therefore:

| Model             | Exploration                               | Compliance                           | Simulation | Governance action                                           |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| S                 | Yes, with warning and policy overlays     | No                                   | No         | Retain as a non-normative exploratory view                  |
| C                 | Limited use as historical variant catalog | No                                   | No         | Retire from active model registry; archive                  |
| Third alternative | Yes                                       | Candidate after hard-gate validation | Not yet    | Develop, validate, and govern as model-plus-checker package |

# 11. Dissenting View

## Strongest rebuttal

The board should reject the proposed hybrid and adopt Model C as a temporary default-deny whitelist. The third alternative spreads semantics across a POWL model, a procedural overapproximation, attribute lookups, and custom post-hoc code. That fragmentation creates a serious audit risk: an engineer may run only the Petri-net alignment, omit the checker, or deploy inconsistent versions of the two artifacts. The backbone itself admits a correction with no recheck and makes Supplier Review optional, so its safety depends on perfect integration rather than on the executable model. Model C, by contrast, is explicit: every automatically accepted trace is visible as a complete approved branch. Its high training precision is appropriate for a regulated process, and its low holdout fitness may be a feature rather than overfitting—unseen combinations should enter change control instead of being inferred as legitimate. False rejection causes review and delay; false acceptance can bypass a mandatory control. Branch duplication and runtime are manageable engineering costs compared with that asymmetry. The organization currently lacks the attributes needed to validate the hybrid’s conditional rules, so it would generate a large, subjective review population while appearing more sophisticated than the evidence warrants. A finite whitelist, supplemented by a formal process for adding approved branches, is therefore safer until policy and data collection mature.

## Evidence that would change the recommendation

I would move toward the dissenting position if the board established that the eight Model C branches are an intentionally approved and substantially complete whitelist rather than frequent variants; independently reviewed the held-out combinations and classified them as unauthorized; demonstrated zero false acceptance on a curated negative suite; showed adequate coverage of valid cases across sourcing, approval, and correction strata; and provided automated branch generation, duplicate-label traceability, and reliable change control.

Conversely, Model S could become an enforcement candidate only if the policy itself changed so that both mandatory checks could legitimately be skipped and Expedite could occur before them. Under the stated P2 and P4, no additional metric evidence can make S suitable for compliance.

The third model’s details should be revised if stakeholders clarify that Supplier Review is forbidden for internal cases, that corrections may occur after Expedite without re-expedite, or that some correction types legitimately require no mandatory-check recheck.
