# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

**Reconciliation in brief.** The three networks measure different things, so they need not agree. Handover and subcontracting are computed on event sequences and reward being *between* other resources. Working-together is computed on case co-membership and rewards *repeatedly sharing cases with the same partners*. Mira touches 22% of claims with about 1.06 events per case (960 events / 905 cases), spread over many partners. That profile produces high directed-flow centrality and thin co-occurrence ties at the same time. The "contradiction" is the signature of a broker with short touches, plus some amount of measurement artifact that has to be ruled out first.

### Handover-of-work network

- **Implies:** Mira sits in many directly-follows paths. She receives mainly from Bot_Rules (920), the temps (190, 170) and the specialists (160, 140), and sends to both specialists and all four adjusters. Her out-degree (0.82) is far above her in-degree (0.51), so she is more a distributor than a collector.
- **Implies:** she is a major source of work for adjusters, not only an exception router. Mira → Carla (350) exceeds Alice → Carla (320), and the listed edges into Elise and Farid (290, 280) correspond to nearly half of their distinct cases (610, 590). About 76% of her listed outflow goes to adjusters, not specialists.
- **Implies:** she is normally responsive. Elapsed times on edges into her are 3.5–12 minutes, and elapsed times on edges out of her (42 min, 51 min, 16–19 min) are close to the target's own median service time (37 min, 51 min, 17–24 min). This suggests the edge times mostly contain the target's processing, not a queue at Mira.
- **Does not imply:** authority, decision quality, workload, or delay. Betweenness (0.77) is a topological measure that is sensitive to edge thresholding and ignores volume. Bot_Rules has the second-highest betweenness (0.41) and nobody would call it a manager.
- **Caveat:** the listed edges out of Mira sum to 1,640 and those into her to 1,580, yet she has 960 events. Each event has at most one direct successor, so these cannot all be direct-succession counts on the same event set. Possible explanations are start and complete lifecycle events both being counted, a different time window, or an indirect-handover variant. Her centrality should be recomputed on a cleaned log before it is relied on.

### Working-together network

- **Implies:** she does not form a stable dyad with anyone. The teams show the opposite pattern: lead–adjuster and adjuster–adjuster pairs at 19–27%.
- **Implies:** her involvement is shallow per case. She "disappears from the case for several events", which fits one routing touch and not joint work.
- **Does not imply:** that she is unimportant or disconnected. Co-occurrence measures shared case membership, not influence on the path a case takes.
- **Caveat:** the normalization is unclear. Alice–Carla at 27% cannot be a share of all 4,100 cases (1,107 cases would exceed Alice's 665). Mira–Carla at 8% cannot be a pair-relative share either, because 8% of the pair's union is about 131 cases, fewer than the 350 handovers between them. If Mira's rates are shares of all cases, 8% is about 328 cases, which is roughly 36% of her own caseload and not low at all. Part of her "peripherality" may therefore be a denominator effect.
- **Caveat:** Bot_Ingest appears in every case but has a working-together weighted degree of 0.05. The metric is clearly not raw co-occurrence, so it also under-represents any resource that touches cases briefly.

### Subcontracting network

- **Implies:** Mira is the A in the most frequent A → B → A patterns (126 with Omar, 98 with Priya), and her subcontracting betweenness (0.69) is three times the next value. She delegates a question and gets the case back.
- **Implies:** she is not a strict gate. Only about 60% of Mira → Omar handovers (126 of 210) and 54% of Mira → Priya handovers (98 of 180) return to her within two events. About a third of medical reviews and a quarter of legal reviews do not arrive from her at all.
- **Implies:** she is a human-in-the-loop for the rules engine (Bot_Rules → Mira → Bot_Rules, 62 times, "Fraud Screen update").
- **Does not imply:** contractual subcontracting, supervision, or control over the specialist's conclusion. With n=2 the pattern also arises from ordinary request-and-reply loops, and it is sensitive to interleaved events and coarse timestamps.

### Performance/time statistics

- **Implies:** her own touch time is small. A rough proxy (960 events × 4.2 min over about 60 working days) gives around 1.1 hours of logged work per day, against about 6.8 hours for Carla and Diego. Either she is not capacity-constrained in logged terms, or most of her work (calls, investigation, deciding where a case should go) is not logged.
- **Implies:** her *availability* matters for exception cases. During her 6-day absence the P90 cycle time of flagged cases rose from 8.4 to 13.9 days. The increase of 5.5 days is close to the length of the absence, which fits cases waiting for her return instead of being rerouted. Ordinary low-value claims were unaffected.
- **Does not imply:** that she causes delay in normal operation. Her "high variance" cycle-time contribution is confounded by case mix: 48% of her cases are flagged against a baseline near 19% (Bot_Ingest's share across all cases).
- **Does not imply:** that she is the main time driver. Vendor_Verify (2.1 h), Vendor_Pay (1.3 h), Omar and Priya (37 and 51 min, "high" and "very high" contributions) dominate elapsed time on the paths she feeds.
- **Caveat:** the absence is a single episode. Seasonality, a batch of hard cases, or vendor delays in the same week could contribute.

### Role discovery or activity-profile similarity

- **Implies:** her activity profile is mixed and partly unique. She shares Request Missing Information with Temp_1, Elise and Farid, and Reopen Claim with Alice. Escalate Complaint and Assign Specialist appear only in her profile. A role-discovery run would probably put her in a singleton role or attach her weakly to the clerical or junior cluster.
- **Does not imply:** hierarchy or discretion. The same label can cover different work: a temp's Request Missing Information may be clerical, while hers may be diagnostic.
- **Caveat:** "Assign Specialist" is not among the 14 defined activities. It may be an ad hoc or renamed label used only by her, which would make her look more distinctive than she is.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

These hypotheses are not mutually exclusive. The evidence fits a combination, and I do not assign a single label.

### H1. Formal coordinator / exception desk

- **Mechanism:** Bot_Rules is configured to send non-standard cases to a designated desk. Mira dispatches them and the organization intends this.
- **Supporting evidence:** Bot_Rules → Mira is the largest human-bound edge (920), close to her 905 cases. She handles 64% of reopened claims and 71% of complaints. Her events are short. The process owner's "queue cleaner" description fits.
- **Limiting evidence:** only 48% of her cases are flagged, so about half of her work is not formally an exception. Conversely she sees only about 56% of flagged cases (roughly 434 of 780), so other exception routes exist. A pure desk would not be the largest direct work source for junior adjusters.
- **Predictions:** her volume tracks Bot_Rules routing volume and rule changes. Her choice of target is largely predictable from case attributes (case_type, amount_band, channel). A trained substitute performs similarly.

### H2. Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency

- **Mechanism:** routing knowledge for hard cases is tacit and held by one person. There is no fallback path, so her absence stalls flagged cases.
- **Supporting evidence:** the absence episode, including the match between the P90 increase and the absence length. Handover betweenness of 0.77 and subcontracting betweenness of 0.69 with no comparable second node. The adjusters' remark that she "knows where hard cases should go".
- **Limiting evidence:** in normal weeks she answers within minutes, so she is a single point of failure more than a congestion point. Median behaviour for flagged cases during the absence is not reported. Many specialist referrals bypass her. There is one absence only.
- **Predictions:** future absences or peaks reproduce the queue growth. Waiting time before her events rises non-linearly with weekly exception volume. Cases that bypass her in her absence show more rework or misrouting.

### H3. Organizational boundary spanner or triage broker

- **Mechanism:** Team North and Team South are closed clusters. Mira links them to the specialists, the temps and the rules engine, and translates a problem into the right destination.
- **Supporting evidence:** edges to both teams in similar volume (640 to North adjusters, 610 to South). High betweenness with low tie strength is the classic broker profile. She is the main return address for Omar and Priya (76–78% of her referrals send a handover back).
- **Limiting evidence:** co-occurrence with the team leads is low (5%, 4%), so she may bypass the leads instead of bridging the teams. Only one medical and one legal specialist exist, so "choosing the specialist" involves limited choice of person. The Omar–Priya tie (4%) shows little joint specialist work for her to coordinate.
- **Predictions:** cases she touches involve more distinct roles and both teams more often than comparable cases. Without her, cross-team or specialist cases show longer paths and more ping-pong handovers.

### H4. Informal dispatcher or shadow supervisor for juniors and temps

- **Mechanism:** she functions as the first line of help for temporary staff and as a de facto work allocator for adjusters, without the title.
- **Supporting evidence:** Temp_1 and Temp_2 send her 190 and 170 handovers against 495 and 470 cases (over a third), although only 13–14% of temp cases are flagged. No lead → junior edge appears among the top edges, while Mira → Elise and Mira → Farid do. Mira → Carla exceeds Alice → Carla.
- **Limiting evidence:** she performs no Approve Settlement. The edge table is truncated, so lead → junior edges may exist just below the cut. The edge counts carry the counting caveat from Section 1. The notes describe these edges as reassignment and rework, not first assignment.
- **Predictions:** temp → Mira volume falls as temps gain tenure or receive a checklist. A large share of juniors' Assess Damage events are directly preceded by a Mira event. Leads' triage volume does not rise when she is away.

### H5. Human-in-the-loop for the rules engine

- **Mechanism:** Bot_Rules cannot resolve some cases. Mira supplies the manual judgment and the bot re-screens.
- **Supporting evidence:** the Bot_Rules → Mira → Bot_Rules fragment (62). Bot_Rules has 4,750 events on 4,090 cases, and its exception share of 0.31 is only arithmetically possible if flagged cases receive almost two bot events each, which points to a re-screening loop.
- **Limiting evidence:** 62 is small relative to her 960 events. This explains one part of her role at most.
- **Predictions:** her volume and the loop frequency move with rule releases and threshold changes. Specific rule outcomes account for most Bot_Rules → Mira handovers.

### H6. Measurement or data artifact

- **Mechanism:** her centrality is inflated and her co-occurrence deflated by logging and metric choices.
- **Supporting evidence:** handover counts that exceed her event count, inconsistent working-together denominators, an undefined activity label, an exception share for Bot_Rules that is evidently event-weighted, and Vendor_Pay's out-degree of 0.00 although Close Claim should follow payment.
- **Limiting evidence:** the absence effect and the interview statements are independent of the SNA metrics and point the same way. An artifact alone cannot explain them.
- **Predictions:** after cleaning (complete events only, one window, a single normalization), her rank drops somewhat but she stays the top human broker. If she drops to the level of the team leads, H2 and H3 weaken considerably.

# 3. Risk assessment

1. **Data-quality gate (first).** Check lifecycle values, duplicate events and identical timestamps. Recount Mira's direct successors and compare with the 1,640 in the table. Check where and when exception_flag is set, and whether "Assign Specialist" maps to a defined activity.
2. **Handover-of-work.** Compute it separately for flagged and unflagged cases, with and without bots and vendors, and per week. Export `connections` to networkx and recompute betweenness on weighted and thresholded graphs. Then delete Mira's node and measure how many source–target pairs lose their only short path. That gives structural substitutability instead of a raw centrality number.
3. **Working-together.** Recompute with explicit denominators: share of all cases, share of each resource's own cases, and Jaccard. Repeat on flagged cases only. If Mira's ties become strong when normalized by her own caseload, the peripherality is largely a denominator effect.
4. **Subcontracting.** Compare n=2 with n=3 and n=4 to see whether specialist cases return to her later. Split by activity fragment. Compute the return rate and time-to-return per specialist, and what happens to the 40–46% that do not return.
5. **Similarity and roles.** Run `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` and `discover_organizational_roles` on the full log and on flagged cases, with and without the Assign Specialist label. Test whether Mira clusters with temps, juniors, leads or alone, and whether the answer is stable across the 12 weeks.
6. **Filtering.** Use `filter_event_attribute_values` on exception_flag, on activity (Reopen Claim, Escalate Complaint) and on `org:resource` to isolate cases with and without Mira. Use `filter_variants` on the top variants of each group. Use `pm4py.filter_time_range` for the absence window against matched weeks before and after, and compare variants to see who absorbed her work or whether cases simply waited.
7. **Service and waiting time.** Use `get_service_time` (with a start timestamp if available) per resource-filtered sub-log. Use `insert_case_service_waiting_time` to split case sojourn time into service and waiting. Use the `performance=True` link analysis to get edge-level waiting, and report medians and P90 instead of means. Attribute vendor and specialist waiting separately from waiting in front of Mira.
8. **Routing consistency (closely related tooling).** Fit a simple classifier (scikit-learn) predicting her target resource from case_type, priority, amount_band, region and channel. High predictability supports H1 and makes her rules documentable. Low predictability indicates tacit judgment, relevant to H2 and to the control concern.
9. **Outcome comparison.** Compare reopen rate, complaint rate and cycle time for flagged cases routed through Mira against similar flagged cases that bypassed her, matched on the case attributes. This is observational, so it bounds the selection effect without removing it.
10. **Load sensitivity.** Relate weekly exception arrivals to the waiting time before her events, and to Omar's and Priya's queues, to see where saturation actually starts.

# 5. Recommendation

**Change immediately (low cost, justified under every hypothesis):**

- Name and train at least one backup for the exception desk, with a defined rule for what Bot_Rules does when the desk is unstaffed.
- Have Mira and the backup write down the current routing criteria as a short playbook.
- Add a mandatory reason code to referral, reassignment and reopen events, and make Assign Specialist a defined activity. This addresses the control gap without implying misconduct.
- Introduce a light sample review of manual fraud-screen exceptions, and apply the same review to the team leads' combined triage, approval and payment-release steps.
- Fix the logging issues from the data-quality gate before any further network reporting.

**Monitor:**

- Queue age and P90 cycle time of flagged cases, weekly, and waiting time in front of Mira.
- Share of flagged cases, reopenings and complaints passing through her, and the distribution of her handovers across adjusters adjusted for case mix.
- Temp → Mira escalation rate, as an indicator of a training or instruction gap.
- Bot_Rules routing volume to the desk after each rule change, and the re-screening loop frequency.
- Omar's and Priya's queues, and Vendor_Verify and Vendor_Pay turnaround, so that their delays are not attributed to coordination.

**Do not change until more evidence is available:**

- Do not remove, downgrade or bypass the role. If it is ever removed, its function must be replaced by explicit routing rules in Bot_Rules, a staffed and rotated exception desk, and a defined escalation path to the specialists, and this should be piloted on a subset of cases first.
- Do not promote her to a formal team lead or restructure the teams on the basis of centrality scores.
- Do not automate her routing in Bot_Rules until the consistency analysis shows that her decisions are rule-like.
- Do not impose heavy segregation-of-duties controls, or draw conclusions about her conduct or performance, before the data-quality and outcome analyses are complete.

# 6. Short executive summary

In the data Mira does not behave like a team lead, but she appears to be more than a queue cleaner. Hard cases pass through her: she handles about a fifth of all claims but most reopenings and complaints, and she usually decides where those cases go next. That is why she looks central in "who passes work to whom" and marginal in "who works on the same cases". She touches many cases briefly instead of working a few cases in depth, so both pictures can be true. In normal weeks she appears to respond within minutes and is not what slows claims down. The main risk is dependence. When she was away for six days, difficult cases waited and the slowest took about five days longer, while routine claims were unaffected. Nothing indicates improper decisions, but the log does not record why she routes a case as she does. Train a backup now, document her routing rules and record referral reasons. Do not remove or redesign the role until the further analysis is done.
