## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

- **Compliance is a hard gate, not merely another expected-cost term.** The operational benefit of timely execution is €20–35k, while an unlawful execution has a plausible €100–500k loss plus sanctions, audit, licensing, and reputational consequences. Those outcomes are asymmetric and not accompanied by a probability estimate. Consequently, A and B are inadmissible; a favorable approval forecast, large invoice, or deadline cannot cure a missing mandatory review.
- **A lawful delay is preferable to an unlawful payment.** E certainly misses the deadline and risks €15–80k of customer/hold harm, but that harm is bounded and does not include the potentially much larger unlawful-execution tail. A and B have no permissible paid outcome.
- **F is control-effective but presently uncertain.** If the rejection threshold is genuinely met, F removes the execution tail immediately without consuming S2 or the screening quota. If the threshold is not met, it creates a false rejection of €15–80k and potentially an invalid adverse decision. That uncertainty keeps it below fully conformant second-review options. If the threshold were confirmed, F should be reranked substantially upward.
- **D versus C is primarily a resource-externality trade-off.**
  - D/S4 uses one scarce API call and has uncertain review turnaround, but it preserves S2 and avoids a known +0.12–0.28 breach-probability impact on another high-value case.
  - C provides an immediately available independent reviewer and has a stated +40–90 minute completion window, but externalizes delay risk to P-901.
  - Because P-901’s monetary value is not supplied, the 0.12–0.28 probability-point spillover cannot be converted reliably into money. It should not be treated as costless.
- **The customer-harm range should not automatically be added to the deadline-loss range.** For example, a late approval may produce the €20–35k operational loss and only part of the €15–80k customer harm; the estimates may overlap. They should be treated as separate dimensions unless their relationship is known.
- **The forecasts are not intervention effects.** The 48–64% SLA-breach probability describes “continue normal processing,” while the 55–75% approval probability is observational for approval after a conformant review. Screening may alter the case mix and approval probability; S2 interruption, reviewer identity, and waiting time may also change the outcome. Those ranges should therefore be used as priors, not precise estimates for C or D.
- **The 12-minute-old score is still the operative fact unless authoritatively updated.** It is already close to the 90-minute deadline, so the final decision should use the latest authoritative score and match status. A negative alternate screen does not by itself lower the risk score or remove the second-review trigger.
