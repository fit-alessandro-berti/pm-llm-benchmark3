# 1. Current-process diagnosis

The process-mining evidence reveals that the organization is not running a single warranty process, but a misaligned one-size-fits-all workflow in which **segment-blind automation** pushes high-risk claims through a fast-approval highway while **rework loops and waiting queues** choke manual and repair paths.

### Key Diagnostic Findings Across Process-Mining Artifacts
* **Performance and Waiting-Time Waste (Frequency & Performance DFGs):** The **Frequency DFG** shows 72% of claims (3,600/5,000) enter `Auto eligibility screen`, with 2,550 routed straight to `Fast approval` and 2,300 to `Dispatch replacement` in a median of roughly 6 hours (`Intake -> Auto` 0.5h, `Auto -> Fast approval` 0.4h, `Fast approval -> Dispatch` 1.8h, `Dispatch -> Close` 3.2h). By contrast, claims that fall out of automation suffer severe waiting times—not primarily processing time. The **Performance DFG** reveals massive handoff delays on `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` (34h mean / 88h p90), `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote` (22h mean / 60h p90, driven by waiting for part-price confirmation), and `Repair quote -> Customer approval` (30h mean / 80h p90). Crucially, **46% of all entries into `Manual eligibility review` (1,200 of 2,600) are rework or loopbacks**—890 returning from `Request missing evidence` and 310 returning from `Customer complaint/reopen`.
* **Quality and Rework Breakdown (Rework Statistics & Temporal Profile Deviations):** While `Fast approval` appears rapid, **Rework statistics** show it generates a **14.1% complaint/reopen rate** versus only **3.3%** on the `Repair quote -> Customer approval -> Repair device` path. **Temporal profile deviations** tie the long-tail `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` pattern (260 occurrences; 96h mean / 180h p90 delay) directly to **incorrect replacement part selection**, which costs an expected **EUR 410** on high-value devices versus **EUR 95** on low-risk accessories. Meanwhile, `Quality check` (2 inspectors, EUR 38/h) is underused—touching only 360 repaired cases (7.2% of volume) while catching a 12.5% repair rework rate on high-value devices—yet zero replacement dispatches pass through it.
* **Systemic Compliance Failure (Conformance & Alignment Diagnostics):** **Token-based replay** yields a log fitness of **0.91**, which masks a serious compliance gap: only **68% of traces fit the normative model**, meaning **32% of cases violate policy**. **Alignment diagnostics** and low model precision confirm three structural breaches:
  1. **R1 violations:** Outdated, segment-blind auto-screen rules send >EUR 400 high-value claims directly to `Fast approval` without `Manual eligibility review` or `Fraud/risk review`.
  2. **R2 violations:** `Dispatch replacement` occurs without confirmed evidence completeness for non-exempt segments (Standard accessories and High-value devices).
  3. **R3 violations:** In the Frequency DFG, 120 reopened claims route `Customer complaint/reopen -> Escalation board` and proceed directly to `Dispatch replacement` (65), `Repair device` (45), or `Claim denied` (10), bypassing mandatory `Manual eligibility review` before close or denial.
* **Severe Customer-Experience Polarization (Customer Segment Outcomes):** The **Unclear defect / missing evidence** segment (22% of volume) experiences a **140-hour median cycle time**, drives a **26% repeat evidence-request rate**, and accounts for the bulk of the **320 abandonments** at `Request missing evidence`. **Premium high-value device** customers (14%) face a lose-lose choice: a 22% complaint/reopen rate on the fast path or a 96-hour median wait on the reviewed/repair path (flagged as a temporal profile deviation). Conversely, **Premium low-risk accessory** customers (18%) actually thrive on the fast path (8h median, 6% complaints, R2-exempt) and face high service-credit exposure if pushed past 24 hours.

### Resolving the Core Contradiction
The apparent contradiction—that the most frequent path is simultaneously the "best" (fastest, cheapest) and the "worst" (most complaints, non-compliant)—is an **aggregation artifact caused by mixing incompatible risk segments on the same fast lane**:
* On upfront unit economics, `Fast approval` looks **31% cheaper**, but once reshipments, complaint handling, and service credits are counted, that advantage collapses to **9%**—and even that residual 9% ignores unpriced regulatory/compliance exposure from R1/R2 breaches and brand erosion.
* Moreover, fast-path speed is partly illusory: when a fast-approved claim fails, complaints surface **50–70 hours (median) to 150–180 hours (p90)** later, triggering another **31–42 hours (median)** to re-enter `Manual eligibility review` or the non-scalable daily 45-minute `Escalation board`.
* However, the slow reviewed path is **not universally superior**. For **Premium low-risk accessories** (18%), moving to the manual path increases median cycle time from 8h to 46h (triggering service credits) to cut complaints by only 3 percentage points on EUR 95 error items. For **Standard low-risk accessories** (25%), customers tolerate up to 72 hours with good visibility, making expensive manual reviews unnecessary as long as R2 evidence completeness is verified. The real value destruction happens because **High-value devices (35% of volume)** are leaking into an uncontrolled fast path while **Unclear defect cases (22%)** trap manual reviewers in endless evidence loops.

# 2. Redesign A: Risk-Segmented Auto-Triage & Redeployed-QC Pre-Dispatch Gate

### Redesigned Process Flow
1. **Segment- and Value-Aware Auto-Screen (2-week rule update):** Replace the flat `Auto eligibility screen` with tiered policy routing:
   * *Premium low-risk accessories (18%):* Retain straight-through `Auto screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement` (preserving <24h SLA and R2 exemption).
   * *Standard low-risk accessories (25%):* Require an automated mandatory-field/attachment completeness check inside `Auto screen` before `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement` (satisfying R2 without manual review).
   * *High-value devices >EUR 400 (35% — Premium & Standard):* Block direct `Auto screen -> Fast approval`. Route to `Manual eligibility review` (or `Fraud/risk review` when automated risk flags trigger) to strictly enforce **R1**.
2. **Pre-Dispatch Quality Gate Using Underused QC Capacity:** Insert a lightweight **Pre-Dispatch Verification Gate** between `Fast approval` (or post-review approval) and `Dispatch replacement` exclusively for **High-value devices** (and flagged complex accessories). Staff this gate by redeploying the **2 underused Quality Check inspectors (EUR 38/h)** to verify part/SKU compatibility against defect codes and confirm R2 evidence completeness before shipment.
3. **R3 Compliance Guardrail on Reopens:** Require any claim exiting `Customer complaint/reopen` toward the `Escalation board` to pass a mandatory `Manual eligibility review` sign-off (or embed one Manual reviewer directly into the daily 45-minute Escalation Board session) before dispatch, repair, or denial.

### Targeted Segments, Paths, and Activities
* **Segments:** High-value devices (35% of volume: 14% Premium, 21% Standard) and Standard low-risk accessories (25%, for R2 compliance).
* **Paths/Activities:** `Auto eligibility screen` routing rules, `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement` (stopping wrong-part dispatches), underutilized `Quality check` inspectors, and the non-compliant `Customer complaint/reopen -> Escalation board` bypass.

### Mechanism of Improvement
* Eliminates the primary driver of the **EUR 410 wrong-replacement cost** and the 18–22% high-value complaint rate by catching part-selection mismatches *before* dispatch using idle QC labor instead of waiting 70–96 hours for customer complaints.
* Directly closes **R1, R2, and R3 conformance gaps**, lifting fitting traces well above 68% within weeks using existing staff and low IT effort.
* Protects the 18% Premium low-risk segment from service-credit penalties by leaving its <24h fast lane intact.

### Deliberate Sacrifices and Unresolved Problems
* **Speed sacrifice on High-value devices:** Adding mandatory manual/risk review plus the 6–18h pre-dispatch gate lengthens upfront cycle time for High-value devices (especially challenging for Premium high-value expectations).
* **Capacity pressure:** Updating auto-screen rules increases `Manual eligibility review` inflow by an expected **15–35%**, risking queue blowups near the **82% utilization cliff** until downstream complaint reopens decline enough to offset it.
* **Unresolved upstream waste:** Does *not* fix the **Unclear defect / missing evidence** segment (22%, 140h median, 26% repeat requests, 320 abandonments) or the 22h waiting delay for part-price confirmation in `Repair quote`.

# 3. Redesign B: Guided Dual-Mode Intake, Automated Evidence Verification & Pre-Priced Repair Flow

### Redesigned Process Flow
1. **Dual-Mode Guided Intake & Evidence Portal:** Redesign `Intake claim` with a structured digital portal that dynamically requests defect-specific evidence (serial number validation, guided photo capture, symptom tree) *before* claim submission. Pair this with an explicit **assisted-intake fallback** (phone/agent-guided capture) triggered for customers who stall or exhibit low digital literacy, avoiding digital exclusion.
2. **Proactive Status Visibility & Automated R2 Verification:** Once structured intake completes, an automated validation layer stamps evidence completeness (closing R2 gaps for Standard accessories and high-value claims) and pushes proactive milestone updates—explicitly leveraging **Standard low-risk customers' 72-hour tolerance** to suppress anxiety-driven complaints.
3. **Pre-Priced Repair Catalog & Upfront Pre-Authorization:** Eliminate the waiting bottleneck in `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote` (22h mean / 60h p90 caused by waiting for part-price confirmation) by equipping the 3 technicians (EUR 45/h) with a **pre-priced standard parts/defect matrix**. Combine this with upfront customer repair pre-authorization within policy bounds at intake, compressing the **30h mean / 80h p90** `Repair quote -> Customer approval` wait and reducing the 70 approval-stage abandonments.
4. **Targeted Auto-Screen Exception Routing:** Keep fast-path automation for clean, verified low-risk claims while routing >EUR 400 claims to Manual/Fraud review using the manual capacity freed by slashing evidence-request loops.

### Targeted Segments, Paths, and Activities
* **Segments:** **Unclear defect / missing evidence** (22% of volume, 140h median), **Standard low-risk & Standard high-value** (46% total), and all repair-bound claims.
* **Paths/Activities:** `Intake claim`, `Request missing evidence <-> Manual eligibility review` loop (650 + 780 entries; 890 loopbacks; 320 abandonments), `Manual/Fraud -> Repair quote`, and `Repair quote -> Customer approval`.

### Mechanism of Improvement
* **Upstream error & rework prevention:** Cutting repeated evidence requests by **30–45%** directly attacks the 890 `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` loopbacks (34h mean / 88h p90 each) and frees substantial `Manual eligibility review` hours—creating organic capacity headroom below the 82% utilization limit.
* **Making the high-quality Repair path fast enough to scale:** Today, repair has a **3.3% complaint rate** (vs. 14.1% on fast approval), but is crippled by 52 hours of combined mean waiting across part-pricing and customer approval. Removing those non-value-adding waits makes verified repair competitive on speed and lowers wrong-part dispatch exposure without adding inspection gates.
* **Reducing wrong-part selection at the source:** Structured serial/defect capture gives fulfillment accurate SKU data upfront rather than catching mistakes post-approval.

### Deliberate Sacrifices and Unresolved Problems
* **Higher upfront IT investment and slower deployment:** Building a guided evidence portal, assisted fallback, and pre-priced part catalog takes significantly longer and costs more upfront than rule updates.
* **Abandonment risk trade-off:** Even with assisted fallback, stricter upfront evidence requirements risk higher early-stage abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers (shifting friction from mid-process to intake).
* **Unresolved control gaps:** Without a physical pre-dispatch inspection gate, human/system SKU errors in warehouse fulfillment can still slip through on high-value replacements; Monday/post-campaign `Fraud/risk review` spikes (2 specialists) remain structurally unaddressed.

# 4. Redesign C: Decoupled Dual-Track Operating Model with High-Value Case Ownership & Dynamic Resource Pooling

### Redesigned Process Flow
Rather than forcing five divergent segments through shared queues, split the warranty operation into **two structurally separate value streams** supported by cross-trained capacity pooling:
1. **Track 1 — Express Accessory Track (43% of volume: 18% Premium + 25% Standard low-risk accessories):**
   * Completely decouple low-risk accessories from `Manual eligibility review` and high-value queues.
   * *Premium low-risk (18%):* Zero-touch `Auto screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch` (<8h median, zero service credits, R2-exempt).
   * *Standard low-risk (25%):* Automated intake evidence check + batched fulfillment within a promised **48-hour SLA** (comfortably inside their 72h tolerance with status tracking), eliminating unnecessary manual reviews while meeting R2.
2. **Track 2 — High-Value Technical Assurance Track (35% High-value devices + escalated Unclear defects):**
   * *Premium high-value devices (14%) — End-to-End Case Ownership:* Replace the fragmented multi-handoff chain (`Intake -> Manual -> Fraud -> Quote -> Approval`, currently 96h median) with a **dedicated cross-trained specialist cell** empowered to execute eligibility review, risk-guideline check (R1), part-verified replacement or expedited repair authorization, and evidence confirmation (R2) in a single touch.
   * *Standard high-value devices (21%) — Verified Repair-First Default:* Shift default resolution away from error-prone fast replacement (18% complaints, EUR 410 error) toward the proven **Repair path** (3.3% complaints), using a mandatory pre-dispatch verification only when swap is unavoidable.
3. **Dynamic Cross-Trained Capacity Pooling & Escalation Redesign:**
   * Cross-train underused `Quality check` inspectors (EUR 38/h) and `Manual eligibility` reviewers (EUR 34/h) to flex into basic `Fraud/risk` triage during Monday/post-campaign spikes and absorb High-value pre-dispatch checks, preventing the 82% utilization cliff.
   * Refocus the scarce daily 45-minute `Escalation board` purely on systemic exception governance and VIP saves, strictly enforcing **R3** via mandatory case-owner/manual-reviewer sign-off before any reopen closure or denial.

### Targeted Segments, Paths, and Activities
* **Segments:** Structural separation across **all five segments**, with specific focus on breaking the speed-vs-quality deadlock for **Premium high-value devices (14%)** and eliminating cross-segment queue interference.
* **Paths/Activities:** Multi-handoff manual/fraud/quote chains, shared `Manual eligibility review` and `Fraud/risk review` bottlenecks, underused `Quality check` labor, and the non-scalable `Escalation board`.

### Mechanism of Improvement
* **Eliminates priority inversion and handoff delays:** Low-value accessory exceptions no longer consume reviewer hours needed for >EUR 400 claims. Single-touch case ownership for Premium high-value claims collapses inter-activity wait times (`Intake -> Manual -> Fraud -> Quote`, currently 40–60h+ of queuing) while preserving R1/R2 compliance and sub-6% complaint rates.
* **Structural bottleneck smoothing:** Dynamic pooling across Manual (7), QC (2), and Fraud (2) absorbs Monday/campaign surges without permanent headcount expansion.
* **Shifts volume to the highest-quality fulfillment mode:** Defaulting Standard high-value devices to verified repair exploits its ~4x lower complaint/reopen rate while shielding the operation from EUR 410 swap errors.

### Deliberate Sacrifices and Unresolved Problems
* **High organizational complexity and slower rollout:** Requires cross-training across four pay grades (EUR 34/h to EUR 52/h), new case-ownership workflows, and governance restructuring—high change-management risk and slow time-to-value.
* **Higher unit operating cost on Premium high-value claims:** Dedicated specialist case ownership increases direct labor cost per Premium high-value case relative to automated fast approval (justified only if rework, EUR 410 errors, and churn savings exceed labor cost).
* **Longer cycle time for Standard high-value claims:** Repair-first increases upfront turnaround for Standard high-value customers compared to fast swap, risking dissatisfaction if turnaround and communication are not tightly managed; also does not inherently modernize the customer-facing evidence portal for Unclear defect cases unless paired later with Redesign B.

Consequently, Redesigns A, B, and C occupy distinct non-dominated positions depending on executive weighting of near-term capex, compliance urgency, and Premium retention.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

### Redesign A: Risk-Segmented Auto-Triage & Redeployed-QC Pre-Dispatch Gate
* **Failure Mode 1 — Manual Review Queue Collapse (>82% Utilization Cliff):** Updating auto-screen rules increases `Manual eligibility review` inflow by 15–35% *immediately*, while the offsetting reduction in reopened complaints (310 cases) arrives only weeks later due to the 50–180h complaint lag. Pushing the 7 reviewers past 82% utilization triggers exponential queue growth, blowing SLAs for Premium high-value claims and driving anxiety-induced complaints.
* **Failure Mode 2 — Pre-Dispatch Gate Becomes a Rubber Stamp or Too Slow:** If the 2 QC inspectors face high swap volumes without clear part-matching checklists, either the gate drifts toward the upper **18-hour** delay bound (eroding customer satisfaction) or inspectors rush checks under backlog pressure, allowing EUR 410 wrong-part errors to leak through anyway.
* **Likely Bottleneck Migration:** Bottleneck shifts sharply from downstream `Customer complaint/reopen` and `Escalation board` **upstream into `Manual eligibility review` and `Fraud/risk review`** (especially on Mondays and post-marketing campaigns when the 2 Fraud specialists are already saturated), and secondarily to the **2 QC inspectors** during post-repair campaign peaks when repair QC and pre-dispatch swap checks collide.

### Redesign B: Guided Dual-Mode Intake & Pre-Priced Repair Flow
* **Failure Mode 1 — Disguised Abandonment Among Low-Digital-Literacy Customers:** The new evidence portal cuts repeated requests by 30–45%, but if customers with low digital literacy drop out *before* creating a formal claim record, abandonment rises invisibly outside the DFG—making internal cycle time and manual workload look artificially healthier while silently damaging customer trust and retention.
* **Failure Mode 2 — Stale Part-Pricing Catalog Creates Margin Leakage or New Disputes:** If the pre-priced repair matrix drifts out of sync with volatile component costs or omits newer device SKUs, technicians revert to manual price-confirmation waits (restoring the 22h/60h p90 delay), or upfront auto-quotes underprice repairs and create margin erosion or post-intake re-quotes (`Repair quote -> Complaint`, already 80 cases).
* **Likely Bottleneck Migration:** Eliminating the 52h combined wait in `Repair quote` and `Customer approval` increases conversion into actual repairs (cutting the 70 approval abandonments and making repair more attractive). This migrates the bottleneck **downstream to `Repair device` capacity and `Quality check`**, where high-value devices already suffer a **12.5% repair rework rate** (`Quality check -> Rework repair`, 14h mean delay), potentially overloading the 2 QC inspectors and repair benches.

### Redesign C: Decoupled Dual-Track & Dynamic Resource Pooling
* **Failure Mode 1 — Cross-Training Dilution and Quality Regression:** Stretching EUR 34/h manual reviewers and EUR 38/h QC inspectors across fraud detection (EUR 52/h skill set) and technical repair authorization can dilute specialist judgment—leading to either overly conservative denials/delays (spiking complaints) or missed fraud patterns during high-volume Monday/campaign surges.
* **Failure Mode 2 — Standard High-Value Customer Pushback Against Repair-First:** Defaulting Standard high-value claims (21%) to repair-first lowers wrong-part errors, but if customers expect rapid replacements (as competitors or past Fast-approval habits conditioned them to expect), repair turnaround times may trigger a surge in `Repair quote -> Customer complaint/reopen` and `Customer approval -> Claim abandoned`.
* **Likely Bottleneck Migration:** Concentrating end-to-end authority for Premium high-value devices (14%) in a dedicated specialist cell risks creating a **micro-bottleneck in the Premium cell** during defect spikes or absences (small-pool vulnerability), while shifting Standard high-value claims toward repair-first migrates pressure onto **Repair technicians (3 technicians) and parts availability**, potentially exposing the 12.5% high-value repair rework loop at higher volume.

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

Because exact ROI and behavioral elasticity (digital abandonment, cross-training efficiency, repair acceptance) are unknown, the sponsor should **not** commit blindly to a single full-scale transformation. Instead, execute a staged, evidence-gated sequence that de-risks capacity before increasing control load and tests upstream fixes before reorganizing staff.

### Stage 1 (First Pilot — Weeks 1–6): Targeted Redesign A Control Slice on Standard High-Value Claims
* **Scope:** Deploy updated segment-aware `Auto screen` rules and the **QC Pre-Dispatch Gate** (using idle QC inspector capacity) on a controlled **25–30% randomized cohort of Standard high-value devices** (leaving Premium low-risk untouched and avoiding an immediate shock to Manual review). Simultaneously enforce the **R3 Manual-review sign-off** inside the daily Escalation Board for all reopened claims.
* **Rationale (Why First):** Fast (2-week rule deployment), near-zero capex, immediately tests the real magnitude of Manual review inflow (+15–35% uncertainty) and measures true pre-dispatch gate duration (6h vs. 18h) and EUR 410 wrong-part catch rate before touching high-sensitivity Premium customers.
* **Metrics to Monitor:**
  * *Capacity/Speed:* `Manual eligibility review` weekly utilization (%), QC inspector utilization (%), pre-dispatch gate added hours (median & p90), end-to-end cycle time.
  * *Quality/Compliance:* Wrong-replacement rate caught pre-dispatch vs. post-dispatch, 21-day cohort complaint/reopen rate, R1/R2/R3 fitting-trace %.
* **Stop / Go Thresholds:**
  * *Go / Expand:* Pre-dispatch gate catches a meaningful share of SKU/evidence errors (driving a >=30–40% relative drop in cohort post-dispatch complaints), added gate delay stays within **6–12 hours median**, and Manual review utilization remains **below 80–82%**.
  * *Pause / Adjust:* Manual review utilization breaches **82–85%** for two consecutive weeks or gate wait exceeds **18 hours p90**—signaling that upstream loop reduction (Stage 2) must precede full Redesign A rollout.

### Stage 2 (Weeks 7–16): Redesign B Portal + Assisted Fallback on Unclear Defects & Pre-Priced Repair Pilot
* **Scope:** Launch the **Guided Evidence Portal** with mandatory **assisted-intake fallback** for an A/B cohort of the **Unclear defect / missing evidence** segment (22%), alongside a **pre-priced top-20 parts catalog** for the 3 repair technicians.
* **Sequencing Logic:** Stage 2 directly frees `Manual eligibility review` capacity (by attacking the 890 evidence loopbacks) so the organization can safely expand Stage 1's strict R1/R2 routing to *all* High-value devices (including Premium high-value) without crossing the 82% utilization cliff. It also compresses Repair path lead time before any shift toward repair-first.
* **Metrics to Monitor:**
  * *Upstream Flow:* Repeat `Request missing evidence` rate (target drop: 30–45%), intake-to-submission drop-off rate (specifically segmented by digital vs. assisted channel), `Request missing evidence -> Claim abandoned` rate.
  * *Repair Flow:* `Manual -> Repair quote` and `Repair quote -> Customer approval` waiting hours (median & p90), repair rework rate on high-value devices (watch for rise above 12.5%).
* **Stop / Go Thresholds:**
  * *Go / Scale:* Repeat evidence requests fall by **>=25–30%**, total abandonment (including pre-submission drop-offs) increases by **no more than 2–3 percentage points** (or declines overall), and `Manual -> Repair quote` median wait drops by **>=40%**.
  * *Stop / Redesign UX:* Pre-submission or missing-evidence abandonment rises materially among older/low-digital-literacy cohorts without assisted fallback uptake, or repair backlog pushes QC/repair rework above **15%**.

### Stage 3 (Weeks 17–28): Redesign C Structural Pilot — Premium High-Value Case-Ownership Cell & Monday Pooling
* **Scope:** Using the freed manual capacity and faster repair catalog from Stages 1–2, pilot a **dedicated Premium High-Value Case-Ownership Cell** (covering 50% of the 14% Premium high-value segment) paired with **cross-trained QC/Manual flex support for Monday/post-campaign Fraud spikes**. Decouple Standard low-risk accessories onto the automated 48h-SLA Express Track with proactive status updates.
* **Sequencing Logic:** Organizational restructuring (Redesign C) has the highest reversal cost; running it third ensures the team only cross-trains and builds Premium concierge cells once routing rules (A) and intake/pricing data hygiene (B) are proven, preventing the new cell from simply absorbing broken evidence loops.
* **Metrics to Monitor:** Premium high-value median & p90 cycle time (target moving from 96h toward <36–48h with <=5–6% complaints), Fraud/risk Monday queue p90, unit labor cost per Premium high-value case vs. rework/credit savings, Standard low-risk complaint rate under 48h batched SLA.
* **Stop / Go Thresholds:**
  * *Go / Institutionalize:* Premium high-value complaint/reopen rate stays at or below the reviewed benchmark (**~5–6%**) while cutting median cycle time by **>=40–50%** relative to the current 96h reviewed path, and Monday Fraud p90 wait drops noticeably without rising fraud leakage.
  * *Stop / Pivot:* Direct labor cost per Premium case rises disproportionately without measurable reductions in service credits, complaints, or churn, or cross-trained triage increases fraud false-negatives/denial disputes.

### How to Verify Improvements Are Real Rather Than Shifted Downstream
Because the current process hides failure in long-tail delays and silent dropouts, every pilot must enforce four anti-illusion measurement rules:
1. **Cohort-Based Lag Windows (Defeating the 50–180h Complaint Mirage):** Never evaluate a pilot on weekly throughput or immediate closure rates. Because `Fast approval -> Complaint` (50h median / 150h p90) and `Dispatch -> Complaint` (70h median / 180h p90) arrive days later, track fixed **entry cohorts for a minimum of 21 days post-closure/dispatch** (covering >p90 of the reopen tail plus downstream review) before declaring quality or cycle-time gains.
2. **Full Funnel & Pre-Intake Abandonment Accounting (Defeating Hidden Drop-Offs):** Measure total claim conversion from **initial customer contact/portal landing** through final resolution—not just `START -> Intake claim`. A drop in manual workload paired with a drop in submitted claims indicates shifted friction (silent abandonment), not genuine efficiency.
3. **End-to-End Conformance Replay & Post-Close Auditing:** Run token-based replay and alignment diagnostics on every pilot cohort to verify that **fitting-trace % rises above 68%** and that `Customer complaint/reopen after close` and R1/R2/R3 bypasses actually decline rather than migrating into informal workarounds (e.g., off-system goodwill credits or unlogged escalations).
4. **Total Rework-Adjusted Cost-to-Serve:** Evaluate every pilot on **fully loaded cohort cost**—direct labor across all roles (EUR 34–52/h) + auto-screen/portal operating cost + expected wrong-part reshipments (EUR 95 / EUR 410) + repair rework + service credits—rather than upfront processing cost, preventing a repeat of the current 31%-to-9% fast-path illusion.
