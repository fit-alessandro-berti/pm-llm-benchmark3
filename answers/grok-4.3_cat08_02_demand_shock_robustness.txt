**1. Stress scenarios**

- **S1 – Peak demand with Urgent/Complex-panel mix shift**: 20–25% volume increase, case mix becomes Routine 40%, Urgent 32%, Complex-panel 22%, Repeat 6%. Matches observed peak but with sustained mix pressure.
- **S2 – Assay-machine degradation**: M2 failure rate doubles (higher repeat probability) while M1 runs at 95%+ utilization; coincides with 15% volume increase.
- **S3 – Medical-validator and specialist absence**: One medical validator absent (utilization on remaining validators >100%) plus specialist unavailable every second Friday; volume at peak levels.
- **S4 – Courier arrival compression**: Two daily spikes collapse into one 10:00–12:00 window, creating 40% higher instantaneous intake; volume normal but pre-analytical and registration queues surge.

**2. Bottleneck-migration analysis**

In S1 the first bottleneck forms at Pre-analytical check (96% utilization, 9.5 h median queue) and immediately migrates to Technical validation (101%) because Urgent and Complex-panel cases require more Medical validation passes; the observed 54% of cases experiencing >12 h queues becomes chronic. Relieving Pre-analytical via I6 shifts load to Assay machines, where M2 already shows elevated repeats in peak DFG (1260 vs 1140).  

In S2, Assay run becomes the primary bottleneck (M2 degradation raises Repeat assay edges from 1140 to >1600). Technical validation queue grows because fewer valid runs reach it; the 22 h peak Medical validation median lengthens further as Repeat-after-failure cases re-enter the flow.  

In S3 the Medical validation station saturates first (93% normal peak rises above 110%); Hold for specialist review queue explodes to >48 h (already 36 h in peak data). Technical validators back up because they cannot release without medical sign-off, starving Release result.  

In S4, Registration clerks and Pre-analytical check queues spike within the compressed window; the 2.8 h registration queue observed in peak becomes 6–8 h. The bottleneck then migrates downstream to Test preparation and Assay run because batch-wait edges lengthen (14 h peak) when arrival rhythm is lost.

**3. Robust redesign package**

Core package: I6 (early pre-analytical triage), I7 (shift courier slots), I8 (automate registration for complete metadata), and selective I4 (cross-train one technical validator to accession correction, keeping one full-time on technical tasks).  

These are robust because they attack the two stations that appear in every scenario’s first or second bottleneck (Pre-analytical, Registration) without creating priority starvation. I6 reduces recollection loops (observed 760–780 in both periods) before they consume courier and registration capacity. I7 smooths the intake spike that drives the 9.5 h pre-analytical queue. I8 lowers registration utilization from 85% peak without shifting work to already overloaded Pre-analytical. The partial I4 relieves the 14 h accession-correction queue without removing a full technical validator. All four interventions remain beneficial when volume, mix, machine health, or validator availability vary.

**4. Scenario-specific policies**

- S1/S2 mix or machine stress: Activate controlled I1 (Urgent-first at preparation and assay) only when Urgent p90 cycle time exceeds 25 h or M2 utilization >95% for two consecutive days; deactivate when both metrics fall below thresholds for 48 h.  
- S3 validator absence: Activate I5 (evening medical-validation shift) when Medical validation median queue >12 h or >15% of Urgent cases exceed 18 h; deactivate when queue <8 h for three days.  
- S3/S2 combined: Activate I10 (Complex-panel overflow) when Hold for specialist review median queue >24 h; transport delay accepted only under this trigger.  
- S4 courier compression: Activate temporary I7 slot rebalancing plus I6 triage within 2 h when registration queue >4 h or courier intake >120 cases in any 3-hour window.

**6. Residual vulnerabilities**

Even after the package, Medical validation remains capacity-constrained under sustained S3 because no new medical validators are added. In addition, the redesign itself creates a new vulnerability: aggressive I6 triage plus I8 automation can mask chronic metadata-quality problems from ordering physicians, so that when automation coverage drops (system outage or new order types) the pre-analytical reject rate spikes faster than the current 7.5–11.8% rework baseline.

**7. Minimum monitoring dashboard**

Leading indicators (daily):
- Pre-analytical reject rate within 2 h of receipt (>12% = warning)
- Registration queue median (>3 h = warning)
- Assay M2 repeat rate (>15% of runs = warning)
- Medical validation queue median (>10 h = warning)
- Fraction of Urgent cases bypassing batch wait (<55% in peak = warning of priority-rule erosion)

Lagging outcome metrics (reviewed every 48 h):
- Urgent cases released >18 h
- Overall cases with any queue >12 h
- Rework/repeat percentage

These signals trigger the scenario-specific policies above.
