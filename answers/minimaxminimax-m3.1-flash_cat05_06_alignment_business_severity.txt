## 1. Materiality Framework

### 1.1 Eight assessment dimensions

| Dimension | What it captures | How it is applied here |
|---|---|---|
| **Control objective** | *Which* control failed: payment authorisation (P3), fraud prevention (P1/P2), audit evidence, customer communication (P4), model coverage | Weighted highest for payment authorisation and fraud prevention; lowest for taxonomy/noise defects. A bypass of a fraud control outranks a documentation defect of the same monetary size. |
| **Amount / exposure** | EUR at risk, plus the populationlevel exposure of the pattern | Used as a *modifier*, never as the severity itself (EUR 4,500 with a fraud indicator can outrank EUR 18,000 without one). |
| **Reversibility** | Can money or harm be undone (recall/clawback, corrected notice, reverification) | Irreversible payments (CL602, CL604, CL609, CL610) cannot be "fixed" by a better alignment; recoverable defects cannot be compared with them at equal weight. |
| **Timing** | Position of the deviation relative to the payment/decision, and the detection window | Payment before approval = worst case (P3). Notification at +3h (CL608) is inside P4's 24h window. |
| **Customer / regulatory harm** | Claimant impact, unfairtreatment/appeal risk, recordkeeping and regulatory exposure | Denied/withdrawn cases can carry appealnotification duties even though no money moved (CL607, CL612). |
| **Recurrence** | Frequency across the month and whether the mechanism is systemic | Monthly pattern evidence (74 / 9 / 4 cases) escalates a single lowvalue case (CL610) into a systemic finding. |
| **Evidence reliability** | Strength of the proof: independent system timestamps > signed documents > systemofrecord events > mutable free text > absence | Governs *confidence*, and also changes the control conclusion: an unevidenced control on a EUR 480,000 claim is itself an audit failure, independent of whether it was performed. |
| **Model scope** | Whether the normative tree even covers the population | Outofscope populations (denied, withdrawn) are scored as *modelcoverage* findings, not operational deviations (P6 dispute). |

### 1.2 Severity ladder (business severity, 1–5)

| Sev | Definition | Anchor cases |
|---|---|---|
| **5 – Severe** | Irreversible payment released while a required control was bypassed **or** absent, on a highvalue / highrisk case or with a fraud indicator; credible regulatory or customer impact | CL602, CL604 |
| **4 – Serious** | A required control (notably fraud screening / manual approval) bypassed with money already out and a live fraud indicator; exposure bounded | CL610 |
| **3 – Material** | Policy deviation with a completed payment but no fraud indicator and bounded exposure; or a controlobjective breach in a datadependent rule | CL609 |
| **2 – Moderate** | Evidence, linkage or modelcoverage weakness: control probably performed but not provable, or model blind spot; no demonstrated harm or exposure | CL606, CL607, CL608, CL612 |
| **1 – Minimal** | No business deviation: UI telemetry, retry duplicates, netconversion artefacts, label synonyms, fully compliant execution | CL601, CL603, CL605, CL611 |

### 1.3 Confidence is not severity

* **Severity** answers *"if the deviation is real, how bad is it?"* — a property of consequence.
* **Confidence** answers *"how sure are we that the deviation is real, and that our diagnosis of the cause is right?"* — a property of evidence.
* High severity with low confidence  escalate **investigation**, not remediation of a harm we have not proven (e.g. CL608: severity 2, confidence Moderate). Low severity with high confidence  fix the mechanism cheaply and stop (e.g. CL603).
* Thin evidence does **not** reduce severity; at high exposure it increases it, because an unverifiable control cannot be relied upon (CL602: High severity, High confidence that the control is *unevidenced*, only Moderate confidence about *why*).
* Confidence in the *alignment* and confidence in the *business explanation* are separate: CL606's alignment is certainly "missing" (High) while the signed report makes it certain the assessment happened (High), so the residual issue is linkage, not performance.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

> **Standing rule for this section:** a missing log event is never proof that the activity did not occur. It is proof that the **system of record does not evidence** the activity — which is itself a control finding when the activity is a required control.

**4.1 CL602 — missing Fraud Screen**
* **HA (logging gap):** screening was performed in a separate tool; the result was never written back to the claim. *Discriminating evidence:* fraudservice API/IT call logs for the claim ID; a screening result present in another system at a time before 10:xx payment release; any writeback job failures.
* **HB (control skipped):** no screening occurred; the approver substituted a verbal check. *Discriminating evidence:* the approver's action metadata (manual vs systemrecorded), claim freetext notes with timestamps, interview statement, and whether other claims from the same approver show the same gap (perapprover clustering).
* **HC (screening performed but on the wrong entity/amount):** screening covered a different claim ID or a prechange bank account. *Discriminating evidence:* screening request payload (claim ID, beneficiary account) versus the account actually paid.
* **Cannot conclude:** that fraud screening did not happen — only that it is unevidenced on the system of record.

**4.2 CL604 — payment 17 minutes before approval**
* **HA (genuine outofsequence execution):** payment was released before approval. *Evidence:* bank accept time vs core approval creation time, and the approval record's *creation* timestamp (not just the displayed approval time).
* **HB (clock/datapipeline skew):** the two timestamps come from systems with unsynchronised clocks or a delayed ingestion pipeline. *Evidence:* NTP/chrony offsets on both hosts; raw vs ingested event timestamps; crosscase comparison of paymentapproval deltas (a bimodal distribution points to skew).
* **HC (retrospective documentation):** approval actually happened verbally/at the time and was recorded afterwards. *Evidence:* record modification history, author device/session, presence of an attestation or esignature.
* **Cannot conclude:** that the case is compliant merely because an approval record exists — the ordering question is unresolved until creation times are checked.

**4.3 CL606 — no core Assess Loss event**
* **HA (outsourcedasusual, evidence capture gap):** the licensed adjuster's signed report *is* the assessment. *Evidence:* report ID, signature time 10:05, adjuster licence, report referenced in the claim; report predates screening/approval.
* **HB (assessment also performed internally but not logged):** duplicated effort. *Evidence:* internal assessment workproduct, reserve changes, adjuster invoice line items.
* **HC (unsupported/fabricated assessment):** in the 2 cases with no stable identifier, the document may not belong to the claim. *Evidence:* document hash and metadata chain of custody, adjuster statement, claim ID in document body, and — decisive — whether the adjuster was ever instructed on that claim.
* **Cannot conclude:** that the assessment was not performed; the signed report is positive evidence for CL606.

**4.4 CL603 — duplicate notification**
* **HA (connector retry on timeout, idempotent resend):** supported by transport retry logs and identical message IDs. *Evidence:* connector config (retry count, timeout, idempotency key), payload hash equality of the two events.
* **HB (deliberate renotification):** a supervisor triggered a resend after a complaint. *Evidence:* user/actor on the send, notificationtype code, campaign tags.
* **HC (messageID collision or mapping bug):** two distinct messages share an ID. *Evidence:* ID generation scheme, collision frequency for other message types.
* **Cannot conclude:** that the claimant was notified twice — the ID and payload indicate one message.

**4.5 CL611 — "Assess Damage" vs "Assess Loss"**
* **HA (pure naming drift):** same activity, different label across systems. *Evidence:* mappingtable version history (updated after the case), procedure text and UI screenshots, fieldlevel comparison of outputs (reserve, loss amount) set by each label.
* **HB (different scope):** damage assessment only; the loss determination was genuinely missing. *Evidence:* whether the label's payload includes loss/amount determination and feeds the reserve; whether the decision that was approved used a figure produced by it.
* **HC (interim process change):** the activity was renamed midquarter with an active migration. *Evidence:* release notes, change calendar.
* **Cannot conclude:** that the assessment step was skipped — the alignment's `M(Assess Loss)` is a vocabulary artefact here.

**4.6 CL609 / CL610 — policyconformant structure, policybreaching data**
* **HA (stale/thresholddrifted rule configuration):** the autoapprove rule was configured with the wrong limit (e.g., 50,000 instead of 5,000) or the P1/P2 conditions were never implemented. *Evidence:* rulesengine configuration and version **effective at the transaction date**, rule change history, comparison with the 4 monthly violations.
* **HB (documented override with a legitimate reason):** someone accepted a manual override. *Evidence:* override audit entries with actor, reason code and approver.
* **HC (data defect at decision time):** the risk score was defaulted to LOW/MEDIUM by fallback, or the 7day bankchange flag was not populated because the change was recorded in a different system (the same integration weakness as CL608). *Evidence:* riskscore provenance (model vs default vs manual), bankchange feed completeness for the decision window, and whether the change event existed anywhere at decision time.
* **Cannot conclude:** that a specific rule "allowed" it — and, conversely, that the absence of a violation flag means no violation: the cost function cannot see this class.

**4.7 CL607 / CL612 — outofscope populations**
* **HA (scope decision):** the model legitimately covers payable claims only, and these traces should be excluded from conformance metrics. *Evidence:* processowner's written scope statement (P6); prior conformance reporting that excluded them.
* **HB (model incompleteness):** the live process genuinely includes denial and withdrawal, so the model must be extended. *Evidence:* intake and closure procedure documents, CRM status history showing active Denied/Withdrawn outcomes, volume and staffing for those paths.
* **HC (notificationlabel ambiguity):** the notification did happen under a different name (e.g., "Send Denial Notice"/"Send Closure Letter"), so the `M(Notify Claimant)` may be an artefact. *Evidence:* CRM/portal outbound message inventory for the claim with claim ID and template code.
* **Cannot conclude:** that the claimants were never informed because no `Notify Claimant` event exists.

**4.8 CL608 — notification "missing" from core, present in CRM with a mutable link**
* **HA (notification sent and correctly attributed):** most likely, given a linked portal message at +3 h. *Evidence:* portal delivery/read receipts, message payload containing claimant identity and claim amount.
* **HB (message belongs to another claim — mislinked):** actual notification failure at EUR 250,000. *Evidence:* correlation of claimant identity + amount + timestamp across claims in the same CRM message batch; presence of a competing candidate claim.
* **HC (record created but never delivered):** a placeholder row. *Evidence:* connector delivery logs and portalside receipt (not just CRMside row).
* **Cannot conclude:** that notification occurred — only that a CRM record exists; the mutable field means the link itself is not durable evidence.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Alignment cost answers a narrow question — *how many visible moves are needed to make the log look like the model?* — under one uniform cost vector. Business risk answers a different question: *could a claimant, a regulator or the company be harmed, and can the money come back?* The supplied setup makes the gap measurable and, in places, **inverted**.

**1) The cost function has no business semantics at all.** Costs were configured uniformly at 1 for every visible log/model move, "with no businessspecific weighting". A missing Fraud Screen (CL602), a duplicate notification (CL603), a weak CRM linkage (CL608) and a missing assessment performed offsystem (CL606) therefore all cost exactly **1**, on exposures of EUR 480,000, EUR 3,200, EUR 250,000 and EUR 75,000 respectively. Cost measures *edit effort*, not *control importance*; the function cannot see which of the four is a fraud control, a customercontact control, or a duplicate bit.

**2) The model encodes structure, not data — so the cost function is blind to the most dangerous class.** The tree says "XOR between {Fraud Screen  Manual Approve} and {Auto Approve}". It has no amount, no risk band, no bankchange predicate. CL609 (EUR 18,000, MEDIUM risk, autoapproved) and CL610 (EUR 4,500, LOW risk but a bank change one hour before payment, autoapproved) are **perfectly synchronous alignments with raw_cost 0**, yet CL610 breaches both P1 and P2 and is the single clearest paymentdiversion profile in the sample (severity 4). Zero cost is a statement about the model, not about the claim. Conversely, the four monthwide cases with "fully synchronous Auto Approve path despite violating P1 or P2" are exactly the population that a costbased triage can never surface.

**3) Scope mismatch inflates cost on exactly the wrong population.** Denied and withdrawn cases account for most alignments with raw_cost  4. CL607 (denied prescreen) and CL612 (claimant withdrew) are charged 4 each: three modelonly steps that the model invented (Assess Loss, Auto Approve, Pay Claim) plus a logonly Deny/Withdraw event. No money moved in either case; exposure is zero. Because the model has no Deny or Withdraw branch, **legitimate business outcomes are structurally indistinguishable from skipped controls** — and the residual risk is masking, not the cost itself: any real deviation inside the denied/withdrawn population is drowned in model orphans. P6 records that this scope is disputed, which is why the "cost 4" signal is a modelling artifact pending a business decision.

**4) Hidden moves score zero by construction.** CL605 contains `T(tau,c=0)`, technically fitting and costfree. Here that is benign: EUR 800, LOW risk, legitimate autoapprove, with the hidden transition being the XORPetrinet routing artefact. But the general lesson is the opposite of reassuring: a silent move means the log cannot evidence *which* routing decision occurred, so zero cost can coexist with an unprovable branch selection — precisely the question an auditor asks about every autoapproved claim (CL609, CL610). Cost 0 is a property of the encoding, never evidence of compliance.

**5) Activity granularity and vocabulary manufacture cost.** CL611 records one assessment under two names ("Assess Damage" in core, "Assess Loss" in the procedure) and is charged 2 for a pair of mutually explanatory artefacts. CL601 is charged 3 for opening a workbench, an autosave and closing it. CL605's routing transition adds a third kind of invisible noise. All three inflate the cost baseline — and inflating the baseline is precisely how the CL602/CL604 class gets buried: a quarterly costranking report would put CL601 (severity 1) and CL607/612 (severity 2) above CL604 (severity 5). Under P5 the UI events are explicitly *not* business activities, so the cost function is scoring nonbusiness facts as if they were deviations.

**6) Duplicated telemetry and missingbutexternal evidence are mispriced in opposite directions.** CL603's cost 1 comes from a *duplicate* of a control that was performed correctly (a resent message), while CL606's cost 1 comes from a control that *was* performed but recorded elsewhere (a signed adjuster report) and CL608's cost 1 comes from a control performed in an allowed channel with only a mutable link. So a cost of 1 can mean "we did it twice", "we did it offsystem", "we did it in another system" or "we did not do it" (CL602). Cost carries no information about the *direction* of risk.

**7) Irreversibility is invisible to cost.** CL604's cost of 3 is repairable by a twomove alignment edit (insert Fraud Screen, reorder Payment after approval), which makes it look like the cheapest defect in the top quartile. But the bank had already accepted EUR 90,000 at 10:03 and the fraud screen never ran: P3 says a cheap repair does not reduce severity, because no realignment can unpay a claimant or supply the missing fraud assessment. Conversely CL612's cost of 4 is *irrepaired* by nothing because nothing happened. Cost measures how hard it is to make the log resemble the model — it has no term for money already transferred, for a missing fraud investigation, for a record that cannot be produced in an audit, or for a claimant who may never have been told their claim was denied.

**Consequence for management.** A costweighted view of this portfolio would order it as CL607/612  CL601/604  CL611  CL602/603/606/608  CL605/609/610. A riskweighted view orders it CL602, CL604 (severity 5)  CL610 (4)  CL609 (3)  CL606/607/608/612 (2)  CL601/603/605/611 (1). The two orderings disagree on the top, on the bottom, and on the middle. Cost is a *diagnostic* about where the model and the log disagree; severity is a *decision input* about where to send money, effort and accountability.

def build_tree():
    try:
        tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(TREE_STR)
        print("[model] parsed with pm4py.parse_process_tree")
        return tree
    except Exception as exc:
        print("[model] pm4py.parse_process_tree unavailable (%s); using explicit ProcessTree fallback" % exc)
        from pm4py.objects.process_tree.process_tree import ProcessTree, Operator
        reg  = ProcessTree(label="Register Claim")
        val  = ProcessTree(label="Validate Coverage")
        ass  = ProcessTree(label="Assess Loss")
        fraud= ProcessTree(label="Fraud Screen")
        man  = ProcessTree(label="Manual Approve")
        auto = ProcessTree(label="Auto Approve")
        pay  = ProcessTree(label="Pay Claim")
        notif= ProcessTree(label="Notify Claimant")
        seq_fm = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[fraud, man])
        xor    = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR,     children=[seq_fm, auto])
        return ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
                           children=[reg, val, ass, xor, pay, notif])

def build_xes(traces):
    out = ['<?xml version="1.0" encoding="UTF-8"?>',
           '<xes xmlns="http://www.xes-standard.org/" '
           'xmlns:xsi="http://www.w3.org/2001/XMLSchema-instance">',
           '  <log>',
           '    <string key="concept:name" value="Insurance claims conformance log"/>']
    for case, acts in traces.items():
        out.append('    <trace>')
        for i, act in enumerate(acts):
            # Synthetic, deterministic ordering timestamps: alignment does not depend on real
            # clock times, but CL-604's business severity DOES (payment 10:03 vs approval 10:20),
            # and that fact comes from the case narrative, not from this reconstruction.
            ts = (pd.Timestamp("2024-06-01 08:00:00") + pd.Timedelta(minutes=7 * i)
                  ).strftime("%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%S.000Z")
            out += ['      <event>',
                    '        <string key="concept:name" value="%s"/>' % act,
                    '        <string key="case_id" value="%s"/>' % case,
                    '        <date key="time:timestamp" value="%s"/>' % ts,
                    '      </event>']
        out.append('    </trace>')
    out += ['  </log>', '</xes>']
    return "\n".join(out)

def extract_alignments(diag):
    al = diag.get("alignments") if isinstance(diag, dict) else diag
    out = OrderedDict()
    for item in (al or []):
        if isinstance(item, tuple) and len(item) == 2:
            out[item[0]] = item[1]
        else:
            out[getattr(item, "case_id", None)] = getattr(item, "alignment", None)
    return out

def decode(alignment, case_id):
    """Return the PM4Py alignment as a list of (move_type, label) for comparison."""
    try:
        events = [e for e in log if e["case_id"] == case_id]
    except Exception:
        events = []
    seq = []
    for mv in alignment:
        mt = mv.move_type
        if mt == "L" and events and 0 <= mv.log_index < len(events):
            lbl = events[mv.log_index]["concept:name"]
        else:
            lbl = transition_names.get(mv.model_index, "model#%s" % mv.model_index)
        seq.append((mt, lbl))
    return seq

**How to read the outputs.** Section 5's comparison table makes the technical point explicit: PM4Py may return a different (equally optimal) alignment for ties such as CL604's unmatched `Pay Claim`, and it will express the XOR branch as invisible transitions — therefore the supplied notation governs the business reading. Section 6b is the quantitative demonstration of Section 6: raw cost and business severity are weakly, and at the extremes inversely, related. Section 7 is the working triage aid — the indicator flags are computed from case data, while `severity` and `confidence` remain analyst judgements in their own columns and are never overwritten by the code.
