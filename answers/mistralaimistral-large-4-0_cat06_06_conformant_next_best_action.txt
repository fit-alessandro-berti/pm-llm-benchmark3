## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

The central comparison is between bounded operational harm and asymmetric regulatory harm. The benefit of executing within the deadline is €20k–€35k. The harm from a false rejection or prolonged hold is €15k–€80k. Both are material but bounded. By contrast, unlawful execution carries a potential regulatory/compliance harm of €100k–€500k plus non-monetary consequences such as license risk, personal liability, and reputational damage. That asymmetry dominates any plausible deadline benefit.

This is why Actions A and B are not acceptable even if a forecast makes them look operationally attractive. The predicted deadline benefit is at most €20k–€35k, while the downside is an order of magnitude larger and includes non-monetary harm. Moreover, the prediction estimates are observational and may not transport unchanged under intervention. A favorable forecast cannot cure a mandatory control violation.

Among conformant actions, the ranking weighs:
- **Deadline loss** (€20k–€35k) against **false rejection/hold** (€15k–€80k). False rejection can be worse than a missed deadline, so rejecting early is risky when the probability of ultimate approval after conformant review is 0.55–0.75.
- **Conformance and optionality**: D+ preserves the possibility of approval while avoiding P-901 spillover. C is faster but externalizes risk to P-901.
- **Uncertainty**: S4 availability, review duration, and screening results are uncertain. Therefore, the recommendation uses a composite plan with a fallback rather than a single rigid action.
- **Dominance**: A and B are dominated by C/D because they risk severe regulatory harm for a small bounded benefit. E is dominated by D/C because it makes deadline miss nearly certain. F is dominated by D/C if second review can be completed, because F forgoes a 0.55–0.75 chance of approval. G is dominated by D/C unless the exception is actually enabled and strictly time-boxed.

## 4. Conformance constraints

- **Action A — Execute payment immediately**: violates Rule 6 because risk score is 72 (70) and the fuzzy sanctions match is unresolved, so Second Officer Review is required before Approve or Payment Executed. It also violates Rule 9 because Payment Executed is conformant only after a valid approval path. No valid approval path exists.
- **Action B — R17 self-certifies second review**: violates Rule 7 (four-eyes principle) because the first analyst and second officer must be different people. It also violates Rule 6 because the second review is not independent. This is nonconformant regardless of speed.
- **Action C — Interrupt P-901 for S2**: conformant if S2 independently reviews the evidence. S2 is a different person from R17, satisfying Rule 7. Rule 6 is satisfied by the second review. Rule 8 then permits Approve, Reject, or hold/SAR. Rule 9 permits execution only after that valid approval path.
- **Action D — Alternate screening then second review**: conformant now if the second review is performed by S4 (or by S2 if independent). The alternate screening is additional evidence; it does not substitute for the mandatory second review while the current conditions remain (Rule 6). Rule 5 permits evidence cycles, but the current evidence cycle is already complete; additional screening is supplementary.
- **Action E — Request additional customer evidence**: conformant now under Rule 5. The analyst may request evidence, and the cycle may repeat. However, it almost guarantees missing the 90-minute deadline.
- **Action F — Reject now**: potentially conformant only if the documented evidence threshold for rejection is satisfied. The prompt does not establish this. If rejection is treated as a final disposition after required reviews, then the mandatory second-officer review must also have occurred under Rules 6 and 8. Evidence required for F to become valid: a documented rationale that the unresolved fuzzy match cannot be cleared, the validated evidence does not mitigate the risk, the risk score remains 70, no further evidence can be obtained within the deadline, and—if required—second-officer review has occurred. Any SAR must not be disclosed to the customer (Rule 11).
- **Action G — Seek emergency override**: not currently enabled. Rule 10 permits an emergency manual override only when a Compliance Director authorizes it and a valid incident identifier is recorded. Neither condition is currently satisfied. Evidence required for G to become valid: actual Compliance Director authorization, a valid incident identifier, and a recorded justification. Even then, Rule 9 requires a valid approval path before execution.

**Why A and B cannot become recommended through a favorable numerical forecast**: The maximum predicted benefit of A or B is avoiding €20k–€35k of deadline loss. The potential regulatory harm is €100k–€500k plus non-monetary consequences. The asymmetry is so large that no plausible probability of regulatory harm makes A or B acceptable. More fundamentally, conformance is a constraint, not a utility tradeoff. Rules 6, 7, and 9 make A and B invalid regardless of any forecast.

## 5. Resource spillovers

**S2 versus S4**: S2 is immediately available but only by interrupting P-901, a high-value payment with a 70-minute deadline. Interrupting P-901 for 45 minutes increases its breach probability by 0.12–0.28. That is a real externalized cost. S4 is expected in 35–65 minutes and avoids P-901 spillover, but may arrive too late for P-884’s 90-minute deadline. The choice is therefore between speed with spillover (S2) and slower but cleaner processing (S4).

**Alternate-screening API quota**: There are three expedited API calls left today. Using one for P-884 reduces future capacity for other cases. However, it can run in parallel with waiting for S4 and may reduce uncertainty or reveal a false match, which could improve the second review. It should not be allowed to delay the second review.

**Risk of consuming time while waiting for an exception**: Action G may take 20–60+ minutes and fail. That could push P-884 past its deadline and force a worse fallback. Any exception attempt must be strictly time-boxed and must not displace the mandatory second-review path.

**If S4 becomes available in 35 minutes**: D+ becomes clearly dominant. S4 can start the second review early, giving a realistic chance to meet the 90-minute deadline without P-901 spillover. Ranking remains D+ #1, C #2.

**If S4 becomes available in 65 minutes**: D+ may miss the deadline. C may become #1 for P-884’s deadline, because S2 can start immediately. However, the P-901 spillover must be weighed. If P-901 harm is severe, D+ may still be #1 with an accepted risk of missing P-884’s deadline, or a composite plan should trigger C if S4 is not available by ~45–50 minutes.
