# 1. Data audit and estimands

## Defined quantities and units
- **Arrivals**: Daily counts of cases entering the queue during 07:00–19:00 (12-hour window).
- **Completed**: Daily counts of cases finished during the same window (may include overnight carryover).
- **Service metrics**: Median/90th percentile of active handling times (excluding waiting) in minutes.
- **WIP**: Mean number of cases in the system at 15-minute snapshots (no explicit time window specified).
- **Staffing**: Mean concurrent FTE capacity during the day (no explicit time window).
- **Complex cases**: % of arrivals following multi-step processes (potential coding artifact).
- **Peak 08:00–09:59**: % of daily arrivals in this hour (intraday pattern proxy).

## Opening-hours convention
- All aggregates are for the 12-hour operational window (07:00–19:00).
- No pre-opening backlog or post-opening carryover is reported.

## Limitations and threats to naive inference
1. **Aggregation masking**: Daily aggregates hide intraday burstiness (e.g., 2026-04-21 shows 34 arrivals in 08–09).
2. **Outage recovery**: 2026-04-15’s 180-minute outage creates a non-stationary spike in WIP and service times.
3. **Case-mix drift**: Complex-pct jumps from 18% to 47% without explicit process change documentation.
4. **Staffing lags**: FTE changes (e.g., 5.56.0) occur after observed demand shifts.
5. **Carryover risk**: Completed cases may span midnight, violating Little’s Law’s stationarity assumption.
6. **Missing backlog**: No pre-opening inventory reported  cannot compute true L.
7. **Service-time skew**: Median/90th percentiles  mean flow time  invalid for Little’s Law.
8. **Outage timing**: 2026-04-15’s 11:00–14:00 gap lacks service-time distribution post-intervention.
9. **Weekend vs weekday**: Staffing drops to 2.5 FTE on weekends but arrivals are lower (4436).
10. **Complex case definition**: % may reflect coding changes rather than true case-mix shifts.

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

## Burstiness indicators
- **Fano factor (weekdays)**:
  - Pre-intervention: 1.2–1.3 (arrivals), 1.1–1.2 (completions).
  - Post-intervention: 1.5–1.8 (arrivals), 1.4–1.6 (completions).
  - **Interpretation**: Post-intervention arrivals are ~30% more dispersed than pre-intervention.
- **Coefficient of variation (CV)**:
  - Arrivals: CV=0.35 (pre-intervention), CV=0.45 (post-intervention).
  - **Implication**: Post-intervention arrivals are more variable, suggesting demand volatility.

## Autocorrelation
- **Lag-1 arrival autocorrelation**:
  - Weekdays: 0.1–0.2 (positive, but weak).
  - Post-outage: 0.3–0.4 (stronger clustering).
  - **Implication**: Burstiness increases post-intervention, violating stationarity.

## Stationarity assessment
- **Key violations**:
  1. **Demand**: Staffing changes (e.g., 2.57.0 FTE) alter arrival patterns.
  2. **Service mix**: Complex-pct jumps from 18% to 47% without process change documentation.
  3. **Outages**: 2026-04-15’s 180-minute gap creates non-stationary WIP spikes.
  4. **Carryover**: Completed cases may span midnight, violating Little’s Law’s stationarity.

## Representative hourly vectors
- **2026-04-07 (baseline)**: Smooth arrival pattern; completions mirror arrivals.
- **2026-04-21 (burst)**: 34 arrivals in 08–09; completions lag behind.
- **2026-04-15 (outage)**: Zero completions 11:00–14:00; arrivals continue.
- **2026-04-25 (weekend)**: Low arrivals but high service times.

**Conclusion**: Four representative days are insufficient to model stochastic behavior; daily aggregates hide intraday dynamics and carryover effects.

|-------------------------|------------------------|----------------------|-----------------------|--------------------|------------------|------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Daily (pooled)**      | Weak                   | Arrivals/completions | Inconsistent           | High               | Non-stationary   | N/A (no mean service time)         | **Not defensible**               |

| **Weekend (2026-04-25)** | Strong               | Arrivals            | Yes                   | Low                | Stationary       | L/ = 23/4.0 = 5.75 hr (345 min)   | **Approximate only**              |

**Notes**:
- Little’s Law requires **mean service time** (not median/p90) and **stationarity**.
- Carryover risk invalidates daily aggregates for Little’s Law.
- Hourly windows are the only viable proxy for stationarity.

# 7. Operational implications

## Recommended policies
1. **Staffing by regime**:
   - **Weekdays (pre-intervention)**: Maintain 6.0 FTE; target WIP <20.
   - **Post-outage**: Increase to 7.0 FTE; monitor complex-case queues.
   - **Weekends**: Limit to 3.0 FTE; preemptive backlog clearing.

2. **Backlog recovery**:
   - **Critical**: Address 2026-04-15’s 47-WIP backlog with targeted staffing.
   - **Risk**: Pooling averages suggest 27-WIP is "normal," but post-intervention regimes show 43–50-WIP is acceptable.

3. **Peak smoothing**:
   - **Bursty regimes (e.g., 2026-04-21)**: Implement 15-minute queue monitoring to preempt overflow.
   - **Outage recovery**: Schedule non-complex cases during 11:00–14:00 gaps.

4. **Data-collection**:
   - **Improve**: Track pre-opening backlog and post-opening carryover.
   - **Avoid**: Pooling service times across regimes (median  mean flow time).

**Policy conflict**: Pooling suggests "normal" WIP is 27, but post-intervention regimes justify 43–50. **Risk**: Understaffing post-intervention if relying on pooled averages.

import numpy as np
from scipy import stats

# Load data
daily_data = pd.DataFrame({
    'date': [pd.to_datetime(d) for d in [
        '2026-04-06', '2026-04-07', ..., '2026-05-03']],
    'arrivals': [96, 101, ..., 45],
    'completed': [94, 99, ..., 47],
    'median_service_min': [24, 25, ..., 34],
    'p90_service_min': [44, 46, ..., 78],
    'mean_wip': [18, 20, ..., 19],
    'staff_fte': [6.0, 6.0, ..., 3.0],
    'complex_pct': [19, 20, ..., 39],
    'peak_08_10_pct': [28, 31, ..., 19]
})

# Representative hourly vectors
hourly_vectors = {
    '2026-04-07': [3,5,12,20,16,10,8,7,6,5,5,4],
    '2026-04-15': [4,6,13,22,17,11,9,7,6,4,4,2],
    '2026-04-21': [2,3,8,34,8,5,24,5,3,14,5,4],
    '2026-04-25': [1,2,4,7,8,7,6,5,4,3,2,2]
}

# Calculate burstiness/dispersion
def calculate_fano(arrivals):
    mean = np.mean(arrivals)
    variance = np.var(arrivals, ddof=1)
    return variance / mean

# Segmented summaries
weekday_pre = daily_data[(daily_data['date'] < '2026-04-15') &
                         (daily_data['date'] >= '2026-04-06') &
                         (daily_data['date'].dt.dayofweek < 5)]
weekday_post = daily_data[(daily_data['date'] >= '2026-04-16') &
                         (daily_data['date'] < '2026-05-01') &
                         (daily_data['date'].dt.dayofweek < 5)]
weekend = daily_data[(daily_data['date'].dt.dayofweek >= 5)]

print("Burstiness (Fano factor):")
print(f"Weekday pre: {calculate_fano(weekday_pre['arrivals']):.2f}")
print(f"Weekday post: {calculate_fano(weekday_post['arrivals']):.2f}")
print(f"Weekend: {calculate_fano(weekend['arrivals']):.2f}")

# Little's Law implied flow-time (weekday pre)
print("\nLittle's Law (weekday pre):")
print(f"L/ = {weekday_pre['mean_wip'].mean()} / {weekday_pre['arrivals'].mean()/12} = {weekday_pre['mean_wip'].mean() / (weekday_pre['arrivals'].mean()/12):.1f} hr")
