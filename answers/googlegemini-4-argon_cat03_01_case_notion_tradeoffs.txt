## 1. Analytical assumptions

### Core definitions
* **Case notion:** In traditional process mining, a case notion is a partitioning attribute that groups events into mutually exclusive, sequential traces. In an OCEL 2.0 context, selecting a single object type $OT$ as a case notion (flattening) creates one trace per object $o \in OT$, ordered chronologically by timestamp, containing only events $e$ where $(e, o) \in E2O$.
* **Convergence:** The phenomenon where a single event is linked to multiple objects of the selected case-notion type ($|\{o \in OT \mid (e, o) \in E2O\}| > 1$). Upon flattening on $OT$, that single operational execution is replicated across the trace of every linked object, inflating activity frequencies and creating parallel copies of the same work.
* **Divergence:** The phenomenon where an object of the selected case-notion type is associated with multiple instances of a subordinate or peer object type that progress independently. When flattening on the parent/host object type, events belonging to different sub-objects are interleaved into a single linear trace, generating artificial self-loops, false causal edges (e.g., an event for sub-object A appearing to cause an event for sub-object B), and combinatorial variant explosion.

### Treatment of copied shared events
Copied shared events are treated as **both evidence and duplication**, depending on the analytical perspective:
* From a **state-transition (entity-lifecycle) perspective**, a copied event is legitimate **evidence**. For example, event `e19` (`Issue Invoice`) genuinely changes the billing state of both `O2` and `O3`. Omitting `e19` from either trace would falsely suggest an unbilled order.
* From a **resource-capacity and activity-costing perspective**, a copied event is **duplication**. In `e16` (`Dispatch`), one truck (`D2`) departs once, yet flattening on `Order` registers three dispatches (`O1`, `O2`, `O3`), and flattening on `Item` registers three dispatches (`I12`, `I21`, `I31`). Treating these copies as independent executions corrupts activity counts, resource utilization, and bottleneck volume metrics.

### Permitted analytical transformations
By default, all primary analyses below strictly evaluate the raw $E2O$ relation set without silent propagation. Notice that the supplied log already contains substantial upstream $E2O$ propagation in the logistics and billing streams (e.g., `e16` explicitly links to `O1, O2, O3, I12, I21, I31, P2, P3, D2`), while omitting cross-stream links (e.g., billing events `e18–e22, e25–e26` do not link to `Item`, `Package`, or `Delivery`, and logistics events `e11–e17, e23–e24, e27` do not link to `Invoice` or `Payment`). 

As an **explicit analytical transformation**, we permit **controlled O2O graph propagation** only for targeted cross-functional queries—for instance, propagating `Invoice` (`V1–V3`) and `Payment` (`M1–M4`) events down to `Item` (`I11–I41`) via `V --bills--> O --contains--> I` to calculate item-level cash-to-cash cycle times. However, any such propagation must be flagged as a synthetic enrichment because it deliberately induces severe convergence (e.g., `e19` would be copied onto `I21`, `I31`, and `I32`).

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

### Distortion 1: Cross-order package batching inflates packing frequency and masks batch efficiency (`Item` and `Order` flattenings)
* **Affected flattenings:** `Order` (`O2, O3, O4`) and `Item` (`I21, I31, I32, I41`).
* **Cited identifiers:** Events `e13` (`Pack Package` for `O2, O3, I21, I31, P3`) and `e14` (`Pack Package` for `O3, O4, I32, I41, P4`).
* **Resulting distortion:** In reality, warehouse staff packed 4 packages (`P1–P4` in `e11–e14`). Flattening on `Order` copies `e13` into both `O2` and `O3`, and copies `e14` into both `O3` and `O4`. Flattening on `Item` copies `e13` into `I21` and `I31`, and `e14` into `I32` and `I41`. Consequently, the `Pack Package` activity node shows a frequency of **6** in both the `Order` DFG and the `Item` DFG—a **50% inflation** over the 4 physical packing operations. Furthermore, waiting time before packing (`Add Item -> Pack Package`) is distorted: in the `Item` flattening, `I21` (`e07` at 09:20 to `e13` at 10:12 = 52 min) and `I31` (`e08` at 09:22 to `e13` at 10:12 = 50 min) are treated as two independent queuing delays rather than a single synchronization wait to consolidate `P3`.
* **Evaluation:** **Harmful** for warehouse labor accounting and capacity modeling, as it overstates packing workload by 50%. **Informative** only when measuring customer-facing order/item touchpoints.

### Distortion 2: Cross-order package split-and-merge induces false sequential packing loops (`Order` flattening)
* **Affected flattening:** `Order` (`O1` and `O3`).
* **Cited identifiers:** `O1` with events `e11` (`Pack Package` on `P1`, 10:00) and `e12` (`Pack Package` on `P2`, 10:10); `O3` with events `e13` (`Pack Package` on `P3` shared with `O2`, 10:12) and `e14` (`Pack Package` on `P4` shared with `O4`, 10:20).
* **Resulting distortion:** Because `O1` is split across `P1` and `P2`, and `O3` is split across cross-order packages `P3` and `P4`, flattening on `Order` produces a DFG self-loop `Pack Package -> Pack Package` with a frequency of 2 (`e11->e12` in `O1` with a 10-minute duration; `e13->e14` in `O3` with an 8-minute duration). In `O3`, this divergence interacts directly with convergence: `O3` appears to undergo "rework" in packing, when in reality its two distinct items (`I31` and `I32`) were merely batched into two different cross-order packages (`P3` with `O2`, and `P4` with `O4`).
* **Evaluation:** **Harmful.** Traditional conformance checking will flag `Pack Package -> Pack Package` as packing rework or inefficiency, misdiagnosing deliberate multi-parcel splitting and cross-order consolidation.

### Distortion 3: Consolidated delivery `D2` triples transport counts and biases transit-time KPIs (`Order`, `Item`, and `Package` flattenings)
* **Affected flattenings:** `Order` (`O1, O2, O3`), `Item` (`I12, I21, I31`), and `Package` (`P2, P3`).
* **Cited identifiers:** Delivery `D2` carrying `P2` and `P3`; events `e16` (`Dispatch` at 11:05) and `e24` (`Confirm Delivery` at 16:00), both linked to `O1, O2, O3, I12, I21, I31, P2, P3, D2`.
* **Resulting distortion:** Physically, there are 3 deliveries (`D1, D2, D3`) with transit times (`Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery`) of:
  * `D1` (`e15->e23`): 3h 00m (180 min)
  * `D2` (`e16->e24`): 4h 55m (295 min)
  * `D3` (`e17->e27`): 22h 45m (1,365 min)
  * True mean delivery transit time across the 3 vehicles = $(180 + 295 + 1365) / 3 = \mathbf{613.3\text{ min}}$ (10h 13m).
  When flattened on `Package`, `D2`'s events (`e16, e24`) are duplicated across `P2` and `P3`, shifting the apparent mean transit time across the 4 packages to $(180 + 295 + 295 + 1365) / 4 = \mathbf{533.75\text{ min}}$ (a 13% downward bias) and inflating total dispatches from 3 to 4. In the `Item` flattening, `D2` is tripled (`I12, I21, I31`) and `D3` is doubled (`I32, I41`), inflating total dispatches by **100%** (from 3 to 6) and shifting mean transit time to $(180 + 3\times 295 + 2\times 1365) / 6 = \mathbf{632.5\text{ min}}$. In the `Order` flattening, `e16` and `e24` are tripled into `O1, O2, O3`, and because `O1` and `O3` have other deliveries (`D1` and `D3`), the DFG does not even preserve clean `Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery` edges (instead creating `Dispatch -> Dispatch` edges `e15->e16` in `O1` and `e16->e17` in `O3`).
* **Evaluation:** **Context-dependent to Harmful.** Counting 6 item deliveries is valid if measuring "percentage of line items delivered within 5 hours" (4 of 6 items = 66.7%, vs. 2 of 3 trucks = 66.7%), but **severely harmful** if used to estimate fleet emissions, carrier invoices, or truck dispatch frequency.

### Distortion 4: Consolidated invoicing `V2` creates illusory billing duplication and distorted invoice-to-pay lead times (`Order` flattening)
* **Affected flattening:** `Order` (`O2` and `O3`).
* **Cited identifiers:** Invoice `V2` billing `O2` and `O3`; event `e19` (`Issue Invoice` at 12:05) and payment events `e22` (`Record Payment` at 12:40) and `e25` (`Record Payment` at 2026-02-04 09:00).
* **Resulting distortion:** Flattening on `Order` copies `e19`, `e22`, and `e25` into both `O2` and `O3`. The `Order` DFG displays 4 `Issue Invoice` executions (`e18` for `O1`, `e19` twice for `O2/O3`, `e20` for `O4`) instead of the 3 invoices (`V1–V3`) actually generated, and 6 `Record Payment` executions instead of 4 (`M1–M4`). Moreover, if an analyst sums invoice values or counts billing transactions from the `Order`-flattened log, `V2` is double-counted.
* **Evaluation:** **Harmful** for Accounts Receivable workload and revenue aggregation; **informative** only for verifying that both `O2` and `O3` passed a billing milestone prior to `Close Order` (`e29, e30`).

### Distortion 5: Partial payments on consolidated invoice `V2` trigger false loops and interleave with delivery (`Invoice` and `Order` flattenings)
* **Affected flattenings:** `Invoice` (`V2`) and `Order` (`O2, O3`).
* **Cited identifiers:** Invoice `V2`, payments `M2` (`partially_settles`, `e22` at 12:40 on Feb 3) and `M3` (`completes_settlement`, `e25` at 09:00 on Feb 4), interleaved with `e24` (`Confirm Delivery` for `D2` at 16:00 on Feb 3) and `e27` (`Confirm Delivery` for `D3` at 10:00 on Feb 4).
* **Resulting distortion:** 
  1. In the `Invoice` flattening (`V2` trace: `<e19, e22, e25>`), divergence between `V2` and payments `M2, M3` creates a DFG self-loop `Record Payment -> Record Payment` (`e22->e25`, duration 20h 20m). Because the E2O activity name is simply `Record Payment` (the O2O qualifiers `partially_settles` and `completes_settlement` are invisible in standard event logs), a standard DFG cannot distinguish a planned two-instalment schedule from a duplicate payment error.
  2. In the `Order` flattening, the two payments are interleaved with physical delivery confirmations. In `O2` (`<..., e19, e22, e24, e25, e29>`) and `O3` (`<..., e19, e22, e24, e25, e27, e30>`), the DFG generates causal edges `Record Payment -> Confirm Delivery` (`e22->e24` twice, and `e25->e27` in `O3`) AND `Confirm Delivery -> Record Payment` (`e24->e25` twice). This creates a tight bi-directional cycle (`Record Payment <-> Confirm Delivery`) in the `Order` DFG, falsely implying that delivery confirmation and payment recording depend causally on each other inside a loop.
* **Evaluation:** **Harmful.** The `Record Payment <-> Confirm Delivery` ping-pong loop in the `Order` DFG is an artifact of interleaving two parallel streams (instalment billing on `V2` and physical transport on `D2/D3`) into a single linear sequence.

### Distortion 6: Near-singleton lifecycle truncation in `Payment` flattening destroys process structure (`Payment` flattening)
* **Affected flattening:** `Payment` (`M1–M4`).
* **Cited identifiers:** Objects `M1, M2, M3, M4` and events `e21, e22, e25, e26` (`Record Payment`).
* **Resulting distortion:** Because `M1–M4` are only linked to `Record Payment` events in the $E2O$ table (and not to `Issue Invoice` `e18–e20` or `Close Order` `e28–e31`), flattening on `Payment` yields 4 traces of length 1: `M1: <e21>`, `M2: <e22>`, `M3: <e25>`, `M4: <e26>`. Every trace has a throughput time of **0 seconds**, and the discovered DFG has **zero internal causal edges** (only `Start -> Record Payment` with weight 4 and `Record Payment -> End` with weight 4). Crucial payment performance metrics—such as invoice-to-payment time (`e18->e21` = 30m; `e19->e22` = 35m; `e19->e25` = 20h 55m; `e20->e26` = 21h 20m)—are completely erased unless `Issue Invoice` events are propagated across the `M --settles--> V` O2O relation.
* **Evaluation:** **Harmful (Analytical Trap).** Selecting `Payment` as a case notion without O2O relation propagation collapses a temporal lifecycle into isolated point-in-time timestamps.

### Distortion 7: Multi-item divergence within orders creates artificial sequential edges and masks concurrency (`Order` flattening)
* **Affected flattening:** `Order` (`O1` and `O3`).
* **Cited identifiers:** `O1` with `e05` (`Add Item` `I11`, 09:15) and `e06` (`Add Item` `I12`, 09:17); `O3` with `e08` (`Add Item` `I31`, 09:22) and `e09` (`Add Item` `I32`, 09:23); later `O1` deliveries `e23` (`Confirm Delivery` `D1`, 14:00) and `e24` (`Confirm Delivery` `D2`, 16:00).
* **Resulting distortion:** Flattening on `Order` forces concurrent or independent item/delivery events into strict total orders based on timestamps. In `O1`, trace `<e01, e05, e06, e11, e12, e15, e16, e18, e21, e23, e24, e28>` exhibits four distinct self-loops: `Add Item -> Add Item` (`e05->e06`), `Pack Package -> Pack Package` (`e11->e12`), `Dispatch -> Dispatch` (`e15->e16`), and `Confirm Delivery -> Confirm Delivery` (`e23->e24`). Worse, in `O3` (`<..., e24, e25, e27, e30>`), because `D2` is confirmed (`e24` at Feb 3 16:00) before `M3` is paid (`e25` at Feb 4 09:00), which occurs before `D3` is confirmed (`e27` at Feb 4 10:00), the two delivery confirmations for `O3` are split apart by a payment event. Thus, `O1` shows a `Confirm Delivery -> Confirm Delivery` self-loop, while `O3` shows `Confirm Delivery -> Record Payment -> Confirm Delivery`, exploding trace variants (all 4 orders `O1–O4` have **4 completely unique trace variants**, 100% variant diversity) despite following the exact same business rules.
* **Evaluation:** **Harmful.** Divergence turns simple parallel sub-object lifecycles into spaghetti loops and 100% distinct trace variants.

### Distortion 8: Cross-stream blindness in `Item` and `Package` flattenings hides unbilled/unpaid delivery risks (`Item` and `Package` flattenings)
* **Affected flattenings:** `Item` (`I11–I41`) and `Package` (`P1–P4`).
* **Cited identifiers:** Delivery confirmation `e24` (Feb 3 16:00, delivering `I12, I21, I31` in `P2, P3`) vs. payment completion `e25` (Feb 4 09:00, completing settlement `M3` for `V2` on `O2, O3`) and payment `e26` (Feb 4 09:30, settling `V3` on `O4` before `e27` confirms delivery of `P4/I41` at 10:00).
* **Resulting distortion:** Because `Item` and `Package` traces exclude `Invoice` and `Payment` events (`e18–e22, e25–e26`), all 6 `Item` traces and all 4 `Package` traces appear 100% identical in control-flow structure. The analyst cannot see that items `I21` (`O2`) and `I31` (`O3`) were delivered to the customer (`e24` at 16:00 on Feb 3) **17 hours before** their invoice `V2` was fully paid (`e25` at 09:00 on Feb 4), whereas `I11` and `I12` (`O1`) were fully paid (`e21` at 12:30) **before** delivery (`e23` at 14:00, `e24` at 16:00), and `I41` (`O4`) was paid (`e26` at 09:30 on Feb 4) **30 minutes before** delivery (`e27` at 10:00).
* **Evaluation:** **Harmful** for credit-control and compliance auditing (e.g., checking whether goods are released or delivered before full payment settlement).

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

### Recommended flattened logs and safe questions
Rather than discarding traditional process mining entirely, three domain-specific flattened logs should be materialized alongside object-centric models, while three should be avoided or restricted:
1. **Retain `Delivery`-flattened log (`D1–D3`):**
   * *Why:* It has **zero convergence** and **zero divergence** (`e15–e17`, `e23–e24`, `e27`).
   * *Safe questions:* What is the exact vehicle transit time distribution (`D1`: 3h, `D2`: 4h 55m, `D3`: 22h 45m)? How many physical carrier trips were executed (3)?
2. **Retain `Invoice`-flattened log (`V1–V3`):**
   * *Why:* It has **zero convergence** across `e18–e22, e25–e26`.
   * *Safe questions:* What is the time from invoice issuance to initial payment (`V1`: 30m, `V2`: 35m, `V3`: 21h 20m) and to final settlement (`V2` via `e19->e25`: 20h 55m)? Which invoices required multiple instalments (`V2`)?
3. **Retain `Package`-flattened log (`P1–P4`) with caution:**
   * *Why:* It has **zero divergence** and captures warehouse staging delay (`Pack Package -> Dispatch`: `P1` 60m, `P2` 55m, `P3` 53m, `P4` 55m) without duplication on the packing side.
   * *Safe questions:* How long do packed boxes wait on the loading dock before dispatch (`e11->e15`, `e12->e16`, `e13->e16`, `e14->e17`)? (Unsafe for transit time due to `D2` convergence on `P2, P3`).
4. **Restrict `Order` and `Item` logs; discard `Payment` log:**
   * `Order` should only be flattened to check boundary lead times (`Create Order -> Close Order`: `O1` 26h 00m, `O2` 26h 03m, `O3` 26h 01m, `O4` 26h 04m) or high-level milestone existence via log skeletons. `Payment` flattening is discarded as a singleton table.

### Questions requiring Object-Centric Process Mining (OCPM) artifacts
* **OC-DFG (Object-Centric Directly-Follows Graph):** Required to visualize the synchronization between physical fulfilment (`Order`, `Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`) and financial settlement (`Order`, `Invoice`, `Payment`) without creating the false `Record Payment <-> Confirm Delivery` loop seen in the `Order` DFG, and to compare **event frequency** (e.g., `Dispatch` = 3 unique events) against **object frequency** (e.g., `Dispatch` = 4 packages, 6 items, 3 deliveries).
* **OTG (Object-Type Graph) & Object Interaction Summary:** Required to audit cardinality constraints and business anomalies across O2O and co-occurrence links—specifically identifying that `Package` has a many-to-many relationship with `Order` (`P3` links to `O2, O3`; `O1` links to `P1, P2`), `Delivery` has a 1-to-many link to `Package` (`D2` carries `P2, P3`), and `Invoice` has a 1-to-many link to `Order` (`V2` bills `O2, O3`) and a 1-to-many link to `Payment` (`V2` settled by `M2, M3`).
* **ET–OT (Event-Type to Object-Type) Graph:** Required to diagnose scope truncation and convergence hotspots before flattening—immediately showing why `Payment` participates in only 1 activity (`Record Payment`), `Delivery` in 2 (`Dispatch`, `Confirm Delivery`), and `Order` in all 8 activities with variable cardinalities (1 per event in `Create Order`/`Close Order`, up to 3 per event in `Dispatch`/`Confirm Delivery`).
* **Equivalent-Execution Clustering (`cluster_equivalent_ocel`):** Only applicable if an analyst anchors on a sub-object like `Package` or `Invoice` to group structurally identical sub-executions (e.g., grouping single-payment invoices `V1` and `V3` separately from multi-payment invoice `V2`). Because all 4 `Order` subgraphs have distinct interaction topologies (`O1` splits into 2 packages/deliveries; `O2` merges with `O3` in package/delivery/invoice; `O3` bridges `O2` and `O4`; `O4` shares a package/delivery with `O3` but has its own invoice `V3`), clustering on `Order` yields 4 singleton clusters.

### Reconciling contradictory findings between flattened and object-centric views
When a flattened view contradicts an OC-DFG, apply a three-step reconciliation protocol:
1. **Frequency discrepancies (Convergence check):** Compare the flattened DFG activity count against the OC-DFG **Unique Events** metric. For example, when the `Order` DFG reports 5 `Dispatch` executions and the `Item` DFG reports 6, the OC-DFG confirms 3 unique `Dispatch` events (`e15, e16, e17`) operating on 3 `Delivery` objects. The OC-DFG Unique Events count is accepted as the true operational volume; the flattened counts are reclassified as "order-dispatch touches" (5) and "item-dispatch touches" (6).
2. **Loop and edge discrepancies (Divergence/Interleaving check):** When the `Order` DFG shows a self-loop (`Pack Package -> Pack Package`) or cross-stream cycle (`Record Payment <-> Confirm Delivery`), inspect the OC-DFG edges colored by object type. In the OC-DFG, `Pack Package` has **zero** self-loops along `Package` or `Item` paths, and `Record Payment` connects only via `Invoice`/`Order` while `Confirm Delivery` connects via `Delivery`/`Package`/`Item`/`Order`. Any edge present ONLY in the `Order` projection of the OC-DFG (and absent from `Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`, and `Invoice` projections) is diagnosed as an interleaving artifact of `Order` divergence rather than a direct operational handoff.
3. **Duration discrepancies (Weighting check):** Reconcile mean lead-time shifts (such as `Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery` equaling 613.3 min in the `Delivery` view vs. 533.75 min in the `Package` view) by distinguishing **vehicle-weighted transit time** (`Delivery` object path) from **parcel-weighted transit time** (`Package` object path). Both numbers are mathematically valid answers to different business questions.

## 5. Lead-object decision

### Selected Position: **C. Reject a single lead object for this process**

#### Decision criteria
A single lead object type is defensible for end-to-end process analysis only if it satisfies three criteria simultaneously:
1. **Event coverage completeness:** It connects (directly or via unambiguous 1-to-1 propagation) to all material process milestones without truncating upstream or downstream phases.
2. **Cardinal stability (Low Convergence/Divergence):** Its ratio of trace events to unique operational events stays near 1.0, and it does not interleave independent sub-object lifecycles into false causal loops.
3. **Topological decomposability:** Sub-graphs extracted around each instance of the lead object partition the process into loosely coupled, comparable process executions.

#### Strongest supporting evidence for rejecting a single lead object
No object type in the inventory satisfies more than one criterion:
* **Why `Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`, and `Payment` fail Criterion 1 (Coverage):** Flattening or anchoring on any of these five types discards between **55% and 87%** of the log's 31 events. Specifically, the physical hierarchy (`Item -> Package -> Delivery`) is completely disconnected from the financial hierarchy (`Invoice -> Payment`) in both the $E2O$ table and the direct $O2O$ graph, intersecting only through `Order`.
* **Why `Order` fails Criteria 2 and 3 (Stability and Decomposability):** Although `Order` covers all 31 events, it sits at the center of a **many-to-many bow-tie topology** with both logistics and billing:
  * *Logistics M:N tangle:* `O1` splits into `P1, P2` and `D1, D2`; `P3` merges `O2, O3`; `P4` merges `O3, O4`; `D2` merges `O1, O2, O3`; `D3` merges `O3, O4`. Consequently, `O1, O2, O3, O4` form **one single connected component** in the object interaction graph (`O1` connects to `O2, O3` via `D2` in `e16/e24`, and `O3` connects to `O4` via `P4/D3` in `e14/e17/e27`). You cannot isolate a single order's execution without cutting shared packages (`P3, P4`), shared deliveries (`D2, D3`), and a shared invoice (`V2`).
  * *Severe structural distortion:* Using `Order` as a single lead object inflates total event instances by **38.7%** (43 trace steps vs. 31 unique events), inflates `Dispatch` and `Confirm Delivery` counts by **66.7%** (5 vs. 3), fabricates 4 self-loops (`Add Item`, `Pack Package`, `Dispatch`, `Confirm Delivery`), and creates a false `Record Payment <-> Confirm Delivery` cycle (`e22->e24->e25->e27`).

#### Strongest counterargument
The strongest argument for **Position B (selecting `Order` as primary lead object with secondary lifecycles)** is that `Order` is the sole commercial contract binding the customer request (`e01–e04`), line items (`I11–I41`), physical fulfilment (`P1–P4`, `D1–D3`), and financial settlement (`V1–V3`, `M1–M4`) through to closure (`e28–e31`). Furthermore, `Order` is the only object type present in all 31 events, making it convenient for measuring overall customer wait time (`Create Order -> Close Order`, ~26 hours across `O1–O4`) and verifying via a log skeleton that no order closes before its items are delivered and its invoices are paid.

#### Why the counterargument is insufficient & residual risk
Elevating `Order` to "primary lead object" still privileges a customer-contract lens over warehouse, fleet, and accounting lenses, encouraging stakeholders to treat the `Order`-flattened DFG or `Order`-centered subgraphs as the master process map. Because all four orders (`O1–O4`) are entangled in a single connected component via `P3, P4, D2, D3, V2`, any `Order`-lead subgraph extraction (`cluster_equivalent_ocel` on `Order`) duplicates shared logistics and billing subgraphs across multiple order clusters.

**Residual risk of Position C:** Rejecting a single lead object requires maintaining a multi-object mental model (OC-DFG + coordinated views on `Order`, `Package`, `Delivery`, and `Invoice`). Business users accustomed to single-case KPIs ("What is our average case duration?") may initially struggle with the fact that the process has no single case count (4 orders, 6 items, 4 packages, 3 deliveries, 3 invoices, 4 payments) and that bottleneck duration depends on which object path is queried.

#### Conditions under which this decision would change
I would switch from **Position C** to **Position B (`Order` as primary lead object)** if and only if the business enforces **strict order-level encapsulation rules** in warehouse and billing operations—specifically:
1. Prohibiting cross-order packing (`P3, P4`) and cross-order invoicing (`V2`), reducing the `Order : Package` and `Order : Invoice` relationships from $M:N$ to strict $1:N$ hierarchies.
2. Recording `Dispatch` and `Confirm Delivery` at the `Delivery` manifest level without propagating those events onto individual `Order` objects, or treating `Delivery` as an external carrier batch while tracking order-level shipping milestones.

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

To ensure that analytical conclusions are robust against representational artifacts, we execute four structured sensitivity checks:

### Check 1: Removing or down-weighting copied shared events (Fractional Event Weighting)
* **Design:** For any event $e$ linked to $k = |\{o \in OT \mid (e, o) \in E2O\}|$ objects of the flattened type $OT$, assign each copied event instance in the flattened log a fractional weight $w(e, o) = 1/k$ instead of $1.0$.
* **Concrete application on this OCEL:**
  * In the `Order` flattening, `e16` and `e24` (`D2` events shared across `O1, O2, O3`) receive weight $1/3$ per order trace; `e13, e14, e17, e19, e22, e25, e27` receive weight $1/2$ per order trace; unshared events (`e01–e12, e15, e18, e20, e21, e23, e26, e28–e31`) retain weight $1.0$.
  * Summing fractional weights across all 4 `Order` traces restores exact true operational activity counts: `Pack Package` drops from $6 \to 4.0$ ($1 + 1 + 2\times 0.5 + 2\times 0.5$), `Dispatch` drops from $5 \to 3.0$ ($1 + 3\times \frac{1}{3} + 2\times 0.5$), `Issue Invoice` drops from $4 \to 3.0$ ($1 + 2\times 0.5 + 1$), and `Record Payment` drops from $6 \to 4.0$ ($1 + 2\times 0.5 + 2\times 0.5 + 1$).
  * Similarly, fractional weighting on `Package` (`e16, e24` weighted at $0.5$ each for `P2, P3`) restores the true mean `Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery` transit time from $533.75\text{ min}$ back to the true vehicle mean of $613.3\text{ min}$.
* **Validation criterion:** Any throughput volume or mean duration KPI that changes by more than 10% between unweighted ($w=1$) and fractionally weighted ($w=1/k$) flattened logs is flagged as convergence-sensitive and must be reported using the fractional/OC-DFG metric.

### Check 2: Comparing direct E2O relations with an explicitly propagated-relation variant
* **Design:** Construct an enriched OCEL variant (`ocel_propagated`) by propagating E2O links across the static O2O hierarchy for targeted pairs, and compare the resulting flattenings and OC-DFG against the baseline raw E2O log (`ocel`).
* **Concrete application on this OCEL:**
  1. *Propagating `Invoice` events (`e18–e20`) to `Payment` objects (`M1–M4`) via `M --settles--> V`:* `e18` links to `M1`; `e19` links to `M2, M3`; `e20` links to `M4`. Flattening on `Payment` now transforms 4 useless singleton traces (`<e21>`, `<e22>`, `<e25>`, `<e26>`) into 4 two-step traces (`<e18, e21>`, `<e19, e22>`, `<e19, e25>`, `<e20, e26>`), revealing payment tranche lead times (30m, 35m, 20h 55m, 21h 20m) at the cost of introducing convergence on `e19` (copied across `M2` and `M3`).
  2. *Propagating financial events (`e18–e22, e25–e26`) down to `Item` (`I11–I41`) via `O --contains--> I`:* Increases `Item` event coverage from 14 events (45%) to 21 events (68%), allowing verification of whether each item was paid before `Confirm Delivery`, but increases `Item` trace step duplication from 10 extra instances to **21 extra instances** (e.g., `e19, e22, e25` each get copied across 3 items: `I21, I31, I32`).
* **Validation criterion:** Relation propagation is accepted **only** for calculating cross-object cycle times (e.g., `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment` on `Payment` objects) and strictly rejected for activity frequency and control-flow discovery where it multiplies convergence.

### Check 3: Lead-object rotation (Cross-perspective stability check)
* **Design:** Systematically rotate the perspective across `Order`, `Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`, and `Invoice`, testing whether process bottleneck and conformance hypotheses hold across object boundaries.
* **Concrete application on this OCEL:**
  * *Bottleneck hypothesis:* Suppose the `Order` flattening flags `Record Payment -> Confirm Delivery` (`e25` at Feb 4 09:00 to `e27` at Feb 4 10:00 in `O3`) or `Confirm Delivery -> Record Payment` (`e24` at Feb 3 16:00 to `e25` at Feb 4 09:00 in `O2, O3`, a 17-hour gap) as the main process delay.
  * Rotating to `Delivery` (`D1–D3`) and `Invoice` (`V1–V3`) immediately falsifies the direct causal link: the overnight delay in physical shipping is inside `D3` (`Dispatch` `e17` at Feb 3 11:15 to `Confirm Delivery` `e27` at Feb 4 10:00 = **22h 45m**), while the overnight delay in billing is inside `V2` (`e22` at Feb 3 12:40 to `e25` at Feb 4 09:00 = **20h 20m**) and `V3` (`e20` at Feb 3 12:10 to `e26` at Feb 4 09:30 = **21h 20m**).
* **Validation criterion:** A causal edge or bottleneck is validated as operationally real only if it persists in the flattened log of the finest-grained object type that directly governs both activities (`Delivery` for `Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery`; `Invoice` for `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment`; `Package` for `Pack Package -> Dispatch`).

### Check 4: Comparing OC-DFG frequency metrics (Events vs. Unique Objects vs. Total Objects)
* **Design:** In the discovered OC-DFG, evaluate every activity and edge across three distinct counting semantics: (1) **Events** (number of unique event IDs), (2) **Unique Objects** (number of distinct objects of a given type or across all types participating in the activity/edge), and (3) **Total Objects** (total `(event, object)` pairs participating in the activity/edge).
* **Concrete application on this OCEL:**
  * For `Pack Package` (`e11–e14`): **Events** = 4; **Total Objects** = 16 (`e11`: 3, `e12`: 3, `e13`: 5, `e14`: 5). By object type: `Package` unique/total = 4/4 (ratio 1.0); `Item` unique/total = 6/6 (ratio 1.0, but 1.5 items per event); `Order` unique/total = 4/6 (`O1` counted twice in `e11, e12`; `O3` counted twice in `e13, e14`).
  * For `Dispatch` (`e15–e17`) and `Confirm Delivery` (`e23–e24, e27`): **Events** = 3; **Total Objects** = 19 (`e15`: 4, `e16`: 9, `e17`: 6). By object type: `Delivery` unique/total = 3/3; `Package` unique/total = 4/4; `Item` unique/total = 6/6; `Order` unique/total = 4/5 (`O1` in `e15, e16`; `O3` in `e16, e17`).
  * For `Record Payment` (`e21–e22, e25–e26`): **Events** = 4; **Total Objects** = 14 (`e21`: 3, `e22`: 4, `e25`: 4, `e26`: 3). By object type: `Payment` unique/total = 4/4; `Invoice` unique/total = 3/4 (`V2` participates twice in `e22, e25`); `Order` unique/total = 4/6 (`O2` and `O3` each participate twice in `e22, e25`).
* **Validation criterion:**
  * Whenever $\text{Total Objects}(OT) > \text{Unique Objects}(OT)$ for an activity (e.g., `Order` in `Pack Package` $6 > 4$, `Dispatch` $5 > 4$, `Record Payment` $6 > 4$; or `Invoice` in `Record Payment` $4 > 3$), **divergence/repetition** is occurring for that object type.
  * Whenever $\text{Total Objects}(OT) > \text{Events}$ for an activity (e.g., `Item` in `Pack Package` $6 > 4$ and `Dispatch` $6 > 3$; `Package` in `Dispatch` $4 > 3$; `Order` in `Issue Invoice` $4 > 3$), **convergence/batching** is occurring for that object type. Both ratios must be inspected before reporting any process metric.
