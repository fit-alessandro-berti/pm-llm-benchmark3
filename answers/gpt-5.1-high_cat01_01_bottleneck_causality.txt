## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional verdict:** mainly confounded. **Confidence:** 70.

The crude comparison shows Orion with a much longer mean cycle time than Nova (21.96 h vs 15.24 h). However, this reverses within complexity strata: for low, medium and high complexity, Orion’s mean cycle times are slightly shorter than Nova’s (7.5 vs 9.6; 17.2 vs 19.4; 31.6 vs 35.0 h). Similar reversals appear for mean queue wait, which is consistently lower for Orion at a given complexity. This strongly suggests that routing of harder, compliance-heavy, night-shift work into Orion is the primary driver of the crude gap, not inherently slower work by Orion.

There may still be a direct Orion effect operating through mechanisms such as higher reviewer skill, different staffing patterns at night, or stricter use of rework. Those mechanisms could plausibly reduce cycle time at fixed complexity while raising rework rates. The evidence against a strong harmful Orion effect is the absence of worse 30-day outcomes and the within-stratum advantage in both queue wait and cycle time.

The proposed 50% reassignment could therefore backfire. If Nova receives more high-complexity, night, compliance-required work without matching Orion’s staffing and expertise, its queues may lengthen and overall cycle time can increase, especially for difficult cases. At the same time, Orion might lose economies of specialization. A safer approach is to treat routing policy, queueing, and compliance capacity as levers, and test targeted changes rather than bluntly moving half of Orion’s work.

Final recommendation (120–200 words):

Management should not proceed with a blanket 50% reassignment of Orion cases to Nova based solely on the crude mean cycle-time difference. The available evidence points to strong case-mix and shift/load confounding and suggests that, within observed complexity strata, Orion is at least as fast and often slightly faster than Nova, especially through shorter queues. A naïve reassignment risks overloading Nova with high-complexity, night, compliance-heavy work without ensuring equivalent staffing and expertise, which could lengthen queues and potentially harm service for difficult cases while eroding Orion’s specialization benefits.

Instead, the organization should first solidify its understanding through enriched observational analysis and then run a carefully bounded randomized routing pilot in overlapping strata to learn about the true resource_group effect. In parallel, targeted interventions on night and compliance queues offer a safer way to improve performance, regardless of group labels. Subsequent routing-policy changes should be based on these findings, not on aggregated means alone.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. The true distribution of case risk within each coarse complexity band is unknown. A pre-routing numeric risk score per case, recorded before routing and compliance decisions, would greatly improve control of confounding by unobserved_case_risk.
2. The detailed evolution of queues over time (e.g., instantaneous queue lengths and waiting by step) is not observed. Capturing per-activity timestamps and queue state snapshots at key process points would distinguish pure queueing effects from fixed processing times.
3. Individual reviewer effects and skill differences are unmeasured. Recording reviewer identifiers for review activities, along with their certification and experience, would enable within-reviewer and mixed-effects analyses to separate skill from group labels.
4. Selection bias from excluding open or abandoned cases is unquantified. Extending the dataset to include all started cases with completion flags and censoring times would allow survival-style analysis and assessment of group-specific non-completion.
5. The 30-day quality outcome is coarse and may miss important quality trade-offs (e.g., payment accuracy, customer satisfaction). Adding richer quality fields such as error rates, customer complaints, and audit outcomes would clarify whether faster processing under different routing policies maintains or harms true quality.
