# 3. Risk assessment

## Bottleneck risk

**Severity: High for exceptions, low for standard flows.**

Mira is a bottleneck specifically for exception cases. Her absence data proves this conclusively: standard claims were unaffected, but exception-flag cases saw 90th-percentile cycle time increase by 65%. The risk is not that she is slow (her service time is 4.2 minutes) but that she is the *only* routing mechanism for exceptions. If she is absent, ill, or overloaded, exceptions have no fallback. With 920 handovers from Bot_Rules alone, even a modest increase in exception rate (e.g., during a catastrophe event or regulatory change) could overwhelm a single person.

However, this risk must be calibrated: her total case volume (22% of claims, ~905 distinct cases over 12 weeks) is manageable for one coordinator. The bottleneck risk materializes primarily under stress conditions.

## Coordination risk

**Severity: Moderate.**

The gap between Mira's handover centrality (0.82 out-degree, 0.77 betweenness) and her working-together centrality (0.16 weighted degree) indicates that she connects resources without co-occurring with them. This creates coordination risk: the resources she dispatches work to may not understand why they received a particular case, what Mira's assessment was, or what the expected outcome is. In process-mining terms, there is a "handover gap" — work transfers happen, but context transfer may not.

This risk is amplified by the low service time (4.2 minutes), which suggests Mira may not be documenting her routing rationale. If she makes a judgment that a case should go to Priya_Legal instead of Omar_Medical, that decision may not be visible in the event log, creating a coordination blind spot.

## Key-person risk

**Severity: High.**

Mira holds tacit knowledge about exception routing that is not formalized in Bot_Rules' rules engine. The absence data, the adjusters' testimonial, and the operations manager's warning all point to the same conclusion: her departure would create a knowledge vacuum. This is compounded by the fact that she has no obvious successor — no other resource shares her activity profile or network position.

The key-person risk is partially mitigated by the team leads (Alice, Bruno) who also handle some exception routing, but the data shows they handle routine team-based routing (Team North/South), not the cross-team exception routing that Mira performs.

## Compliance / control risk

**Severity: Moderate-to-high.**

The compliance reviewer's concern is valid and specific: Mira both detects problems (via exception routing from Bot_Rules, complaint escalations) and selects the next handler (assigning to specific adjusters or specialists). This is a dual-function role that in regulated processes is typically subject to segregation-of-duties controls. Without audit trails for her routing decisions, there is a risk that:

- Cases could be selectively routed away from rigorous review
- Specialist selection could be influenced by relationships rather than appropriateness
- Complaint escalations could be suppressed by routing them to low-priority paths

However, there is **no direct evidence of wrongdoing**. The compliance concern is structural (the role design creates opportunity), not evidential (we have observed misuse). The 48% exception rate on her cases may simply reflect that Bot_Rules sends her genuinely complex cases.

## Workload fairness risk

**Severity: Low-to-moderate.**

The working-together data shows that Mira interacts with all four adjusters (Carla 8%, Diego 7%, Elise 6%, Farid 6%) with rough parity, and with both team leads similarly. This suggests she is not systematically favoring one team. However, her subcontracting patterns show she routes to Omar_Medical (126) more than Priya_Legal (98), which may reflect case mix rather than bias.

The junior adjusters (Elise, Farid) have higher exception_flag rates (36%, 35%) than senior adjusters (Carla 33%, Diego 29%), which could indicate that Mira routes harder cases to juniors — or that juniors are assigned to cases that are already flagged. Without counterfactual analysis, this cannot be determined.

## Data-quality risk

**Severity: Moderate.**

Several data-quality concerns emerge:

1. Mira's activity "Assign Specialist" is not in the predefined activity list, suggesting either a logging inconsistency or that this activity was added ad hoc
2. Her very short service times (4.2 min median) raise questions about whether all her routing decisions are logged or whether some are system-level actions attributed to her
3. The subcontracting network with n=2 may overcount her re-engagement if the system automatically reassigns cases to her after specialist completion (process design artifact vs. human decision)
4. The low working-together rates could partly reflect cases that are still in-flight or that span beyond the 12-week observation window

# 5. Recommendation

## What should be changed immediately

**Document Mira's routing decisions.** The most urgent action is to create an audit trail for Mira's routing choices. When she assigns a case to a specialist or adjuster, the system should capture a routing rationale (even a simple dropdown: "medical complexity," "legal question," "fraud indicator," "missing documentation," "complaint escalation"). This addresses the compliance reviewer's concern about uncontrolled discretion without requiring process redesign. It also begins to externalize the tacit knowledge that makes Mira a key-person risk.

**Formalize the exception-routing rules.** Work with Mira to codify the decision rules she currently applies intuitively. Start with the top 5 exception patterns identified in the subcontracting network (medical, legal, fraud, missing-info, complaint). These rules can initially serve as guidelines for any backup coordinator and eventually be incorporated into Bot_Rules' rules engine to reduce Mira's single-point-of-failure status.

## What should be monitored

**Mira's queue depth and response time.** Track the number of cases awaiting Mira's routing action in real time. If the queue depth exceeds a threshold (e.g., 30 cases), trigger an alert for team lead backup. Monitor whether her median service time is increasing over time, which would indicate growing workload or complexity.

**Exception-case cycle time by absence.** Establish a baseline for exception-case 90th-percentile cycle time and monitor for drift. Any sustained increase should trigger investigation into whether Mira is overloaded or whether exception volume is growing.

**Specialist utilization balance.** Monitor whether Mira's routing to Omar_Medical and Priya_Legal is balanced relative to case need. If one specialist's utilization spikes while the other's drops, it may indicate routing bias or a shift in case mix.

**Working-together network drift.** Periodically recompute the working-together network. If Mira's co-occurrence rates with adjusters and team leads begin to increase, it may indicate her role is expanding into case ownership (increasing key-person risk). If they decrease further, it may indicate she is being bypassed (increasing exception-handling risk).

## What should not be changed until more evidence is available

**Do not remove Mira's role.** The absence data conclusively shows that her function is operationally critical for exceptions. Removing her without a fully operational alternative would increase cycle times, specialist overload, and complaint rates. The operations manager's warning is supported by the data.

**Do not automate her routing until rules are validated.** The temptation to replace Mira with an expanded Bot_Rules engine should be resisted until the routing rules she applies are documented, validated, and tested. Premature automation could replicate her biases or, worse, create routing rules that fail silently on edge cases she currently handles by judgment.

**Do not redistribute her cases to team leads.** Alice and Bruno already handle team-based triage and settlement approval. Adding exception routing to their workload would likely create a bottleneck at the team-lead level without the specialist-routing expertise that Mira has developed.

**Do not implement segregation-of-duties controls without understanding the impact.** The compliance reviewer's concern is valid, but splitting Mira's detection and routing functions into separate roles would add handoffs and cycle time. Before implementing such controls, quantify the compliance risk (how many cases could theoretically be misrouted) versus the operational cost (how much cycle time would increase with additional handoffs).

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira_Coordinator is the central exception-routing resource in our claims process. She touches only 22% of claims, but these are the hard cases: 64% of reopens and 71% of complaints flow through her. Network analysis shows she is the most connected resource in handover and delegation networks, yet she rarely co-occurs with other resources on the same case — meaning she routes work efficiently but doesn't linger on cases.

Her 6-day absence proved her operational importance: exception-case cycle times increased 65% at the 90th percentile, while routine claims were unaffected. The organization depends on her tacit knowledge of where complex cases should go, yet this knowledge is undocumented, creating key-person and compliance risks. She both detects exception conditions and selects the next handler, which concerns compliance reviewers.

We recommend three immediate actions: (1) capture routing rationale in the system to create an audit trail, (2) codify her routing rules into documented decision guidelines, and (3) establish a queue-depth monitoring alert. We do **not** recommend removing or automating her role until these foundations are in place, as her absence data shows the process cannot currently handle exceptions without her.
