## 1. Analytical assumptions

- **Case notion.** Flattening on an object type `T` creates one case per object of type `T`. A case’s trace is the sequence of events directly related to that object in the E2O relation, ordered by timestamp. A shared event is copied into every case of type `T` to which it is directly related.
- **Convergence.** In a flattened log, convergence means multiple cases share the same activity or event copy. I treat convergence as potentially inflated when the same physical event is copied into several cases, because the copies are not independent observations.
- **Divergence.** Divergence means multiple possible directly-follows paths from an activity. I treat divergence as potentially inflated when one object participates in several items, packages, deliveries, invoices, or payments, producing repeated activities that look like process branching but are often parallel or split fulfilment.
- **Copied shared events.** I treat copied shared events as **both** evidence of object involvement and duplication. They are evidence that, e.g., `O2` and `O3` both participated in `e13` Pack Package. They are duplication when counting how many times “Pack Package” occurred: `e13` is one physical event, not two.
- **Permitted analytical transformations.** I allow explicit relation propagation through O2O links as a separate variant, never silently. For example, propagate `Item  Order` via `contains`, `Package  Item` via `packs`, `Delivery  Package` via `carries`, `Invoice  Order` via `bills`, and `Payment  Invoice` via `settles`. I compare direct E2O flattening with this propagated variant. I also use OC-DFG, OTG, ET–OT, and object interaction summaries to avoid flattening where it distorts.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

1. **Cross-order package `P3` in Order flattening (`e13`).**  
   Affected flattening: Order.  
   `e13` Pack Package is directly related to `O2, O3, I21, I31, P3` and is copied into both `O2` and `O3` cases.  
   Distortion: Add Item  Pack Package appears in both `O2` and `O3`; Pack Package frequency is inflated from 4 to 6. The two order cases look as if two separate packing operations occurred, but it is one physical packing of `P3` containing items from both orders.  
   Effect: harmful for frequency and package-level analysis; informative for order involvement.

2. **Cross-order package `P4` in Order flattening (`e14`).**  
   Affected flattening: Order.  
   `e14` Pack Package is related to `O3, O4, I32, I41, P4` and is copied into `O3` and `O4`.  
   Distortion: in `O3`, `e13` Pack  `e14` Pack creates a Pack Package  Pack Package self-loop. This may be misread as rework or repeated packing, but `O3` actually has two distinct packages, `P3` and `P4`. `e14` is also duplicated across `O3` and `O4`.  
   Effect: harmful for DFG interpretation; informative for split shipments.

3. **Consolidated delivery `D2` in Order flattening (`e16`, `e24`).**  
   Affected flattening: Order.  
   `e16` Dispatch and `e24` Confirm Delivery are related to `O1, O2, O3, I12, I21, I31, P2, P3, D2` and are copied into `O1`, `O2`, and `O3`.  
   Distortion: in `O1`, `e15` Dispatch  `e16` Dispatch creates Dispatch  Dispatch; in `O3`, `e16`  `e17` also creates Dispatch  Dispatch. Dispatch frequency is inflated from 3 to 6; Confirm Delivery from 3 to 6. Performance bias: `O1`’s `e15e16` gap is 5 minutes and `O3`’s `e16e17` gap is 10 minutes, which are not meaningful processing times. The single consolidated delivery `D2` appears as three separate dispatch events across order cases.  
   Effect: harmful for transport analysis; informative for order involvement in `D2`.

4. **Consolidated invoicing `V2` in Order flattening (`e19`, `e22`, `e25`).**  
   Affected flattening: Order.  
   `e19` Issue Invoice is related to `O2, O3, V2` and copied to `O2` and `O3`; `e22` and `e25` Record Payment are related to `O2, O3, V2, M2/M3` and copied to `O2` and `O3`.  
   Distortion: Issue Invoice frequency is inflated from 3 to 4; Record Payment from 4 to 6. In `O2` and `O3`, Record Payment  Record Payment (`e22  e25`) appears, which could be misread as duplicate payment, but it is actually two instalments `M2` and `M3` for the same invoice `V2`. `O2` and `O3` also appear to have identical invoicing/payment behaviour, hiding that they share one invoice.  
   Effect: harmful for invoice-level analysis; informative for order-level payment exposure.

5. **Partial payments `M2`/`M3` in Payment flattening.**  
   Affected flattening: Payment.  
   `M2` (`e22`) and `M3` (`e25`) each become separate cases with a single Record Payment event.  
   Distortion: the `partial_settles` and `completes_settlement` qualifiers are lost unless retained as attributes. The relationship between `M2` and `M3` as two instalments of `V2` is lost; they appear as unrelated payments.  
   Effect: harmful for payment process analysis; informative only for payment event counts.

6. **Near-singleton lifecycle in Payment flattening.**  
   Affected flattening: Payment.  
   All four payments `M1–M4` have only one event, Record Payment.  
   Distortion: each payment case has a single event, so the DFG has no edges, no variants, and no performance. This is a near-singleton lifecycle that cannot answer any process question.  
   Effect: harmful as a process log; context-dependent if only payment frequency is needed.

7. **Item flattening lifecycle truncation.**  
   Affected flattening: Item.  
   Items `I11–I41` each have only Add Item  Pack  Dispatch  Confirm Delivery. For example, `I12` belongs to `O1` and is shipped in `D2` with `O2/O3`, but Item flattening for `I12` shows no Issue Invoice, Record Payment, or Close Order.  
   Distortion: the commercial lifecycle is truncated. Shared events `e16/e24` copied to `I12, I21, I31` also inflate Dispatch/Confirm Delivery frequencies.  
   Effect: harmful for end-to-end analysis; informative for logistics.

8. **Package flattening duplication of consolidated delivery `D2`.**  
   Affected flattening: Package.  
   `e16` Dispatch and `e24` Confirm Delivery are related to `P2, P3, D2` and copied into `P2` and `P3`.  
   Distortion: in Package flattening, `P2` and `P3` each have a Dispatch event at 11:05 and a Confirm event at 16:00, making it appear as two separate deliveries with identical timestamps. Actually `D2` is one consolidated delivery carrying both packages. This hides consolidation and inflates Dispatch/Confirm Delivery frequencies from 3 to 4.  
   Effect: harmful for transport analysis; informative for package involvement.

9. **Delivery flattening lifecycle truncation.**  
   Affected flattening: Delivery.  
   `D1–D3` each have only Dispatch  Confirm Delivery. There is no Pack, Invoice, Payment, or Close.  
   Distortion: for `D2`, the case hides that it carries `P2` and `P3` and involves `O1, O2, O3`. Performance: `D2` duration 4h55m and `D3` 22h45m are only dispatch-to-confirm durations, not end-to-end.  
   Effect: harmful for order lifecycle; informative for transport performance.

10. **Order flattening frequency inflation and self-loops.**  
    Affected flattening: Order.  
    The order log has 42 events versus 31 original events. Shared events `e13, e14, e16, e17, e19, e22, e24, e25` are copied.  
    Distortion: frequencies inflate as follows: Pack 46, Dispatch 36, Confirm Delivery 36, Issue Invoice 34, Record Payment 46. Self-loops appear: Add ItemAdd Item (`O1, O3`), PackPack (`O1, O3`), DispatchDispatch (`O1, O3`), Confirm DeliveryConfirm Delivery (`O1, O3`). These may be misinterpreted as rework or repeated activities, but they reflect multiple items/packages/deliveries per order.  
    Effect: harmful for DFG frequency and variant interpretation; informative for split shipments.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

**Flattened logs that should still be created, with safe questions:**

- **Order-flattened log.** Safe for order-centric lifecycle variants, split shipments (`O1` across `P1/P2` and `D1/D2`; `O3` across `P3/P4` and `D2/D3`), order-level waiting times, and order completion. Not safe for package/delivery/invoice/payment uniqueness, consolidated resource utilisation, or payment instalment semantics.
- **Item-flattened log.** Safe for item logistics tracking from Add Item to Confirm Delivery and logistics bottlenecks. Not safe for financial lifecycle or order close.
- **Package-flattened log.** Safe for package handling times and pack-to-dispatch performance. Not safe for order/financial questions or consolidation analysis.
- **Delivery-flattened log.** Safe for transport performance, e.g. dispatch-to-confirm durations for `D1`, `D2`, `D3`. Not safe for end-to-end analysis or consolidation details.
- **Invoice-flattened log.** Safe for invoice cycle time, payment terms, and instalment detection (`V2` with `M2`/`M3`). Not safe for logistics or order-level questions.
- **Payment-flattened log.** Only for payment event frequency/amount analysis. Not for process mining.

**Questions that require object-centric methods:**

- **Cross-order package consolidation.** Which orders/items share a package? Use OTG or object interaction summary. Example: `P3` packs `I21` and `I31` from `O2` and `O3`. Order flattening duplicates `e13`; Package flattening hides order composition. Use OC-DFG with object type Package or OTG to see O2O links.
- **Consolidated delivery `D2`.** Which packages/orders are in `D2`? Use OTG or object interaction summary. `D2` carries `P2` and `P3`; `P2` packs `I12` from `O1`, `P3` packs `I21/I31` from `O2/O3`. Delivery flattening hides this; Order flattening duplicates `e16/e24`.
- **Consolidated invoicing `V2`.** Which orders are billed by `V2`? Use ET–OT graph or object interaction summary. `V2` bills `O2` and `O3`. Invoice flattening hides order composition; Order flattening duplicates `e19/e22/e25`.
- **Partial payments.** Relationship between `M2`/`M3` and `V2`. Use ET–OT graph or object interaction summary. Payment flattening loses it; Invoice flattening shows two Record Payment events but not the partial/completion qualifiers unless attributes are retained.
- **End-to-end process across object types.** Use OC-DFG to see directly-follows relations per object type without flattening. Use OTG to see which object types interact. Use ET–OT to see which object types are involved in each activity. Use object interaction summary to summarise co-occurrence. Equivalent-execution clustering could be used exploratorily on Order cases, but because the lead-object decision rejects a single lead, it is not a primary method.

**Reconciling contradictory findings:**

- Order-flattened log shows Dispatch frequency 6 and Dispatch  Dispatch self-loops; Package-flattened log shows Dispatch frequency 4 and no self-loop; Delivery-flattened log shows Dispatch frequency 3 and no self-loop. Reconcile by returning to the OCEL: unique dispatch events are `e15`, `e16`, `e17` = 3. Order flattening inflates because `e16` is copied to `O1, O2, O3`. Use object-centric unique-event counts as the tie-breaker.
- Invoice-flattened log shows `V2` with two Record Payment events; Payment-flattened log shows `M2` and `M3` as separate singleton cases. Reconcile with ET–OT: `e22` relates to `M2` and `V2`; `e25` relates to `M3` and `V2`. The two payments are distinct events but the same invoice. Both views are correct at different levels; the object-centric graph shows the relationship.
- Order-flattened log shows `O3` with two Pack Package events; Package-flattened log shows `P3` and `P4` each with one Pack. Reconcile: `O3` has two packages, `P3` and `P4`, so two pack events are correct for `O3`; but `e13` and `e14` are shared with `O2` and `O4` respectively. Use OC-DFG to see unique pack events per package.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Decision: C. Reject a single lead object for this process.**

**Decision criteria:**

1. **Coverage:** Does the object type’s lifecycle span the whole process from order creation to close, including logistics and finance?
2. **Fidelity:** Does flattening on that type duplicate shared events or truncate lifecycles?
3. **Cross-object questions:** Can it answer questions about consolidation (`P3`, `P4`, `D2`, `V2`) and partial payments (`M2`/`M3`)?
4. **Interpretability:** Does the DFG/performance remain meaningful without excessive self-loops or frequency inflation?
5. **Robustness:** Are conclusions stable when changing the lead object?

**Strongest supporting evidence:**

Order flattening is the only flattening that spans Create Order to Close Order and captures split shipments (`O1`, `O3`) and shared `V2` payments. It preserves the most complete per-order lifecycle. However, it duplicates shared events (`e16/e24` copied to `O1/O2/O3`; `e13/e14`; `e19/e22/e25`) and creates self-loops and frequency inflation: Dispatch 36, Confirm Delivery 36, Pack 46, Record Payment 46. Item, Package, Delivery, Invoice, and Payment flattenings each truncate the lifecycle and cannot answer end-to-end questions. Payment is a near-singleton. Therefore no single object type is defensible as the sole lead for end-to-end analysis.

**Strongest counterargument:**

One could argue Order is the natural lead because it is the only object type with a full lifecycle, and all other objects are related to orders via O2O links. With explicit relation propagation, an Order-centric view could be enriched with package/delivery/invoice/payment details. However, propagation would still duplicate shared events and would not solve the problem that a single physical dispatch `e16` is one event but appears in three order cases. It would also require careful handling of many-to-many relationships such as `V2` billing `O2` and `O3`, and `D2` carrying `P2` and `P3`. So Order as primary lead plus secondary lifecycles (position B) is a possible compromise, but it still privileges Order and risks overweighting order-level metrics.

**Residual risk:**

Rejecting a single lead may fragment analysis and make it harder to produce a single end-to-end process view. Analysts may over-focus on object-centric graphs and miss order-level performance. Mitigation: use the Order-flattened log as the primary order-centric view with explicit caveats, and use object-centric methods for cross-object questions.

**Conditions under which I would change the decision:**

- If the business question were exclusively order-centric, e.g. “What is the order-to-close cycle time?”, I would select Order as lead (position A) with qualifications.
- If a new object type were introduced that is a true end-to-end case, e.g. a Shipment or Case object that uniquely represents the whole fulfilment, I would reconsider.
- If the process were simplified so that no cross-order packages, deliveries, or invoices existed, Order would be defensible. In this OCEL, cross-order consolidation is pervasive, so C is the most defensible position.

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

1. **Removing or down-weighting copied shared events.**  
   In the Order-flattened log, identify events that appear in multiple order cases: `e13` (`O2,O3`), `e14` (`O3,O4`), `e16` (`O1,O2,O3`), `e17` (`O3,O4`), `e19` (`O2,O3`), `e22` (`O2,O3`), `e24` (`O1,O2,O3`), `e25` (`O2,O3`). Create a deduplicated variant where each shared event is assigned to only one order, or down-weighted by `1/k` where `k` is the number of orders involved. Compare DFG frequencies: Dispatch should drop from 6 to 3 if deduplicated; Confirm Delivery from 6 to 3; Pack from 6 to 4; Record Payment from 6 to 4. This tests whether self-loops and frequency inflation disappear.

2. **Direct E2O relations versus explicitly propagated-relation variant.**  
   Create a propagated E2O variant where relations are propagated through O2O links: `Item  Order` via `contains` (`I11O1`, `I12O1`, `I21O2`, `I31O3`, `I32O3`, `I41O4`); `Package  Item` via `packs` (`P1I11`, `P2I12`, `P3I21,I31`, `P4I32,I41`); `Delivery  Package` via `carries` (`D1P1`, `D2P2,P3`, `D3P4`); `Invoice  Order` via `bills` (`V1O1`, `V2O2,O3`, `V3O4`); `Payment  Invoice` via `settles` (`M1V1`, `M2V2`, `M3V2`, `M4V3`). Then flatten on Order using propagated relations and compare with direct E2O Order flattening. Also compare Item flattening direct versus propagated: direct Item `I12` has only AddPackDispatchConfirm; propagated Item `I12` would also get Create Order `O1`, Issue Invoice `V1`, Record Payment `M1`, and Close Order `O1`. This tests how much lifecycle truncation is due to direct E2O versus O2O propagation.

3. **Changing the lead object.**  
   Repeat the same analysis for each object type as lead: Order, Item, Package, Delivery, Invoice, Payment. Compare DFG complexity, number of variants, lifecycle length, and duplication. For example, Order has 4 cases, 42 events, and self-loops; Item has 6 cases, 24 events, and a uniform sequence; Package has 4 cases, 12 events, and a uniform sequence; Delivery has 3 cases, 6 events, and a uniform sequence; Invoice has 3 cases, 7 events, and one variant with two payments; Payment has 4 cases, 4 events, and a singleton lifecycle. This tests whether conclusions about process variants and bottlenecks change with the lead object.

4. **Comparing frequency metrics based on events, unique objects, and total objects in the OC-DFG.**  
   In the OC-DFG, for each activity and object type, compute: (a) number of events of that activity; (b) number of unique objects of that type involved; (c) total object-event associations, i.e. the sum of objects per event. For Dispatch: events `e15,e16,e17` = 3; unique Orders involved = `O1,O2,O3,O4` = 4; total order-event associations = `e15O1`, `e16O1,O2,O3`, `e17O3,O4` = 6. This matches the Order-flattened Dispatch count of 6. Unique Packages involved = `P1,P2,P3,P4` = 4; total package-event associations = `e15P1`, `e16P2,P3`, `e17P4` = 4. This shows that Order-flattened frequency (6) is inflated relative to unique events (3) and unique orders (4). Use this to calibrate flattened metrics.

5. **Qualifier-aware payment analysis.**  
   Compare Invoice-flattened `V2` with and without retaining the `partial_settles` and `completes_settlement` qualifiers. If qualifiers are dropped, `V2`’s two Record Payment events look like duplicate payments; if retained, they are correctly interpreted as instalments. This tests whether event attributes are sufficient to preserve payment semantics.
