# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

Core activities are those with high frequency that also align with formal title/certifications, dominate the relevant sequence fragments, and match the resource’s typical case-complexity distribution. Peripheral activities are low-frequency, substitution-driven, or supporting only; they do not define the role’s organizational authority.

- **Automated Intake (Nora)**: Core is exclusively A1 + A2 (volume dominates and initiates nearly all simple paths). No peripheral activities. Authority is a hard system-level complexity gate (median 0.11).

- **Temporary Front-Office Support (Kai/Lina)**: Core is A1/A2/A4/A12 restricted to very-low-complexity cases. Peripheral volume is negligible. High frequency is offset by demonstrated competence limits (incomplete Validate notes causing 17 reopens). Authority is routine close only; the role is pure capacity, not expansion of permanent FO skill.

- **Permanent Front-Office Service Agents (Amira/Ben)**: Core remains A1/A2/A4/A12 with deeper entitlement validation. Peripheral work includes light A3/A5/A7/A8/A10 (Amira higher). Frequency is high, but complexity medians (0.29–0.34) and limited critical exposure distinguish them from both temps and hybrids. Authority is standard response and close, not exceptions or final approval.

- **Senior Hybrid Service Agent (Giorgio)**: Core is the FO set plus substantial A7 and A8 (dual certifications, meaningful volume even outside substitution windows). Peripheral includes occasional A9 and light technical work. Complexity median 0.58 shows absorption of harder FO + commercial cases. Authority on pricing is informal/secondary (duration rose sharply when he was sole performer).

- **Technical Solution Analysts (Chen/Dina)**: Core is A3/A5/A10 (configuration certification, dominant counts, sequence 2). Peripheral includes limited FO, A11/A14, reopens, and A6 (Chen markedly higher). Frequency profiles are nearly identical (sim 0.94), yet complexity and critical-case distributions differ systematically; Chen’s greater security-adjacent exposure indicates higher risk authority within the same nominal role.

- **Security Specialist (Erik)**: Core is A6 only. Peripheral activities appear only on the critical paths he already owns. Absolute frequency is low by design; complexity (0.86) and critical count (55) define the role. Authority on the security gate is absolute and non-substitutable without risk.

- **Commercial Specialist (Fatima)**: Core is A7 + A8. Peripheral includes light validation, close, and some A9. Complexity 0.67; formal authority on pricing exceptions still routes many cases onward to Hana for final approval.

- **Quality Gatekeepers (Ivan/Julia)**: Core is A11 + A14. Peripheral is late-stage technical work. Complexity mid-range; authority is post-implementation control and knowledge capture rather than case ownership.

- **Supervisor / Approver (Hana)**: Core is A9 (138 events). Peripheral includes reopen handling and limited negotiation. Complexity 0.79 with heavy critical load. Authority is final; Mateo’s preliminary approvals were systematically rechecked, proving the distinction.

- **Cross-functional Escalation Broker (Mateo)**: No single activity dominates by frequency. The “core” is the coordination pattern itself (high A13, multi-activity handovers, security filtering, preliminary A9). Individual activities are peripheral. Complexity 0.77 and critical volume confirm the bridging function; authority is routing, not final decision.

Distinguishing method therefore intersects frequency with complexity distribution, substitution performance (duration or rework), sequence position (gate vs. routine), and formal attributes. Frequency alone is insufficient.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

**R07_Giorgio – Commercial Shadow / Hybrid Senior Agent**  
Formal title is Senior Service Agent (Front Office). Dual certifications and activity profile allow him to absorb commercial work. During Fatima’s six-day absence he performed 31 A7 and 18 A8 events. Benefit: continuity and resilience for pricing exceptions. Risk: median handling time rose from 38 to 74 minutes, indicating lower efficiency or less specialized judgment. The shadow is not improper; it is a documented capacity buffer that should not be treated as permanent equivalence.

**R13_Mateo – Cross-functional Escalation Broker**  
He appears across nearly every activity class at moderate volume, owns most reopens, performs preliminary approvals, and filters security referrals. Benefit: protects scarce specialists (Erik) from low-risk noise and supplies rapid cross-team routing on complex or reopened cases; false-escalation complaints fell during the high-load window. Risk: two critical cases waited more than two days; 13 of 21 of his preliminary A9 events were later rechecked by Hana, confirming limited formal authority. He is a bridge, not a substitute supervisor. Over-reliance creates a coordination single point of failure.

**R05_Erik – Rare but Critical Specialist**  
Low absolute volume (96 A6) yet highest median complexity and 55 critical cases. Benefit: concentrated expertise that others cannot safely replicate. Risk: extreme specialization plus Mateo’s filtering both protects and potentially delays critical work; no obvious equivalent exists if Erik is unavailable. Volume-based methods undervalue him; risk-control staffing must treat the role as a protected scarce resource.

**Additional boundary and limited-competence roles**  
- Automation boundary (Nora) cleanly hands off low-complexity intake; any scope expansion requires explicit redesign.  
- Temporary workforce (Kai/Lina) is a pure capacity buffer whose competence ceiling is demonstrated by reopen rates; useful for volume spikes but unsafe beyond low-complexity routine work.  
- Quality gatekeepers (Ivan/Julia) close the loop on both technical and front-office paths and surface systemic defects (e.g., temp validation quality).

Shadow and bridging roles improve resilience when escalation protocols, duration/quality monitoring under substitution, and clear non-equivalence rules are maintained.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.algo.organizational_mining.roles import algorithm as roles_discovery

# Assume event_log_df contains the usual PM4Py columns plus
# case_complexity, temporary_substitution, escalation_level, customer_segment, etc.

# 1. Format the dataframe
log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    event_log_df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    resource_key="org:resource"
)

# Optional enrichment
# log = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(log)

# 2. Discover organizational roles (activity-based)
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log)
# or with explicit variant for control:
# roles = roles_discovery.apply(log, variant=roles_discovery.Variants.ACTIVITY_ROLE)

# 3. Activity-based resource similarity (cosine over normalized activity vectors)
similarity = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log)

# 4. Networks that surface cross-functional and bridging behaviour
handover_net = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log)
working_together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log)
# subcontracting_net = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(log)

# 5. Join role output with complexity and substitution indicators
resource_stats = (
    event_log_df
    .groupby("org:resource")
    .agg(
        median_complexity=("case_complexity", "median"),
        mean_complexity=("case_complexity", "mean"),
        n_events=("concept:name", "count"),
        n_critical=("case_complexity", lambda s: (s > 0.8).sum()),  # example threshold
        substitution_events=("temporary_substitution", "sum"),
        activity_profile=("concept:name", lambda x: x.value_counts().to_dict())
    )
    .reset_index()
)

# Map mined roles onto resources (exact structure depends on PM4Py version)
role_map = {}  # populate from roles result, e.g.
# for group in roles:
#     for r in group.resources:
#         role_map[r] = group.label or str(group.activities)
resource_stats["mined_role"] = resource_stats["org:resource"].map(role_map)

# Enrich further with formal title, tenure, certifications if available
# Then examine complexity distributions and substitution counts per mined_role

# Focused views on known substitution windows or flags
sub_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    log, "temporary_substitution", [True], level="event"
)
# or time-window filters for the documented absence periods

variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)
# service_times = pm4py.get_service_time(log, ...)  # compare durations under substitution

# 6. Guard-rails against over-interpretation of frequency clusters
# - Always re-join mined roles with case_complexity quantiles and formal attributes
# - High activity similarity does not imply identical authority or risk exposure
#   (inspect A9 counts, escalation_level, recheck patterns, critical-case share)
# - temporary_substitution performance must be judged by downstream metrics
#   (duration inflation, reopen rate, later rechecks), not merely by ability to execute the activity
# - Prefer complexity-stratified rediscovery:
#   high_c_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(log, "case_complexity", [0.6, 1.0], level="case")
#   roles_high = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(high_c_log)
# - Use handover / working-together degree to identify brokers versus pure specialists
# - Never treat the resulting clusters as the formal org chart; validate against titles, certifications and outcome data

print(resource_stats.sort_values("median_complexity", ascending=False))
```

The code is illustrative of the required PM4Py-style workflow; exact parameter names may vary slightly by library version, but the logical sequence (format → discover roles → similarity → networks → enrich with complexity/substitution → stratified re-analysis) prevents naïve frequency over-interpretation.

# 6. Operational interpretation

Management can use the discovered structure for:

- **Staffing**: Protect the rare critical specialist (Erik) from volume work; employ the broker (Mateo) as an intelligent filter during peaks while monitoring critical wait times. Restrict temporary agents strictly to low-complexity FO paths. Maintain Giorgio as a documented commercial buffer rather than a head-count substitute for Fatima. Keep quality gatekeepers after implementation so that reopen and knowledge-base loops remain intact.

- **Training**: Upskill permanent FO agents (especially Amira) on medium-complexity and light commercial work to reduce pressure on the hybrid shadow. Use the Chen–Dina complexity gap as a mentorship path. Explicitly document and rehearse substitution protocols for Giorgio (pricing) and Mateo (escalation/preliminary approval) so that temporary coverage degrades less.

- **Risk control**: Treat Erik and Hana as single points of failure; mandate rechecks of any preliminary A9 performed by Mateo. Monitor service-time inflation and reopen rates under substitution. Preserve the automation boundary at low-complexity intake only.

- **Resilience**: The hybrid shadow and the cross-functional broker supply flexibility for absences and load spikes. Quality gatekeepers close feedback loops. These mechanisms work only when accompanied by clear authority limits and outcome monitoring.

**Cautions about changing staffing solely on the basis of mined roles**  
1. Activity clusters and similarity scores do not equal formal authority or risk tolerance. Mateo can execute preliminary A9 events, yet many were later rechecked; promoting him (or expanding his approval rights) on volume or network centrality alone would remove a control point. Giorgio’s A7 volume during substitution does not confer permanent pricing-exception authority or equivalent negotiation outcomes.  
2. Temporary substitution performance and high profile similarity systematically overstate permanent competence and interchangeability. Temps appear almost identical to permanent FO agents yet generated 17 reopens from incomplete notes; Giorgio handled pricing but median duration nearly doubled. Decisions that treat them as fully equivalent (reducing Fatima capacity in reliance on Giorgio, or expanding temp scope) will increase rework, lengthen critical-path waits, and degrade quality. Always validate with complexity distributions, downstream metrics (reopens, rechecks, service times), and formal attributes before redesigning teams or head-count.

Ignoring case complexity when reallocating technical or security capacity risks either overloading high-skill resources with routine work or leaving critical paths under-protected.
