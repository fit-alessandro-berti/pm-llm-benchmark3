# 1. Materiality Framework

The severity of a case is evaluated against the following dimensions:

- **Control Objective**: Identifies the core purpose of the business rules and policies (e.g., fraud prevention, claim accuracy).
- **Financial Exposure**: The monetary impact of the observed deviation.
- **Reversibility**: Whether the issue can be easily mitigated or undone.
- **Timing**: Whether the deviation compromises time-sensitive commitments.
- **Customer/Regulatory Harm**: Harm caused to customers or violation of regulatory policies.
- **Recurrence Frequency**: Whether similar deviations occur repeatedly, amplifying systemic risk.
- **Evidence Reliability**: Confidence in log-event integrity or alternative evidence (e.g., external documents).
- **Model Scope**: Whether the alignment deviation surfaced a potential boundary issue in the normative process model.

**Confidence vs. Severity**:
Confidence reflects the reliability and completeness of evidence to evaluate business impact (e.g., actionable links, verbal cues). Severity quantifies the actual or potential damage caused by the deviation, irrespective of evidence reliability.

Severity is ranked between 1 (very low importance) to 5 (critical issue).

### CL-610 (Policy-Breaking Auto Approve)

  *Evidence*: Test transaction rules for bank-change detection.  

### External Adjuster Gaps

  *Evidence*: Confirm adjuster assignment protocol and linked evidence.  

### Retry Duplicate Notifications
- **H1**: Retry logic designed for fault tolerance caused redundant events.  
  *Evidence*: Simulate connector under retry-error conditions.

# 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

1. **UI Telemetry (CL-601)**: Raw cost reflects harmless UI noise irrelevant to business harm.  
2. **Policy Failures (CL-610)**: Zero-cost alignment masked substantive compliance failure violating P1.  
3. **Timing Race (CL-604)**: Cheap repair underestimates delayed fraud-screen risks.  
4. **External Evidence (CL-606)**: Missing-but-available adjuster records inflate raw cost without operational issue.  
5. **Retry Duplicates (CL-603)**: Repeated notifications add minor cost but no business consequences.

Alignment cost oversimplifies risk because:
- It treats all deviations equally, ignoring contextual severity.
- Silent moves (e.g., tau transitions) obscure subtle data-aware policy failures.
- External documentation or multichannel processes often bypass direct log matching.
- Events with harmless retries inflate raw_cost without process failure implications.

# 7. PM4Py Reproduction and Aggregation Code

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.importer import factory as pt_importer
from pm4py.objects.conversion.process_tree import factory as pt_converter
from pm4py.objects.log.util import log as log_utils
from pm4py.objects.log import obj as log_obj
from pm4py.algo.conformance.alignments import factory as align_factory
import pandas as pd

# Parse the process tree
process_tree_string = "-> ( 'Register Claim', 'Validate Coverage', 'Assess Loss', X ( -> ( 'Fraud Screen', 'Manual Approve' ), 'Auto Approve' ), 'Pay Claim', 'Notify Claimant' )"
process_tree = pt_importer.apply_string(process_tree_string)

# Convert to Petri net
petri_net, initial_marking, final_marking = pt_converter.apply(process_tree)

# Sample event log
data = [
    {"case": "CL-601", "activity": ['Register Claim', 'Validate Coverage', 'Assess Loss', 'Auto Approve', 'Pay Claim', 'Notify Claimant'], "amount": 1200, "risk": "LOW"},
    {"case": "CL-602", "activity": ['Register Claim', 'Validate Coverage', 'Assess Loss', 'Manual Approve', 'Pay Claim', 'Notify Claimant'], "amount": 480000, "risk": "HIGH"},
]
event_log = log_obj.EventLog([log_utils.convert_dict_to_event(log_case) for log_case in data])

# Perform alignment diagnostics
results = align_factory.apply(event_log, petri_net, initial_marking, final_marking)

# Summarize results
summary = []
for result in results:
    case_id = result['trace'].attributes['concept:name']
    moves = result['alignment']
    raw_cost = result['cost']
    summary.append({'case': case_id, 'moves': moves, 'raw_cost': raw_cost})

# Construct pandas dataframes
summary_df = pd.DataFrame(summary)
pattern_df = summary_df.groupby(['case'], as_index=False).agg({'raw_cost': 'sum', 'moves': list})
print(pattern_df)
```
