## 2. Process-model consistency checks

**Landmark and information-availability checks.** Identify the first `Coverage Check Completed` by event identity and reliable ordering—not the last occurrence or a retrospectively corrected trace position. Record the feature-read cutoff associated with that landmark.

For observed-state features, require both an eligible event/effective time and historical availability by `t0`. A backdated record received tomorrow was not available today. Historical recomputation may run now, but must use only source versions accessible then and transformations reproducible at the landmark. Point-in-time feature retrieval should look backward from each prediction timestamp, not toward the extraction date. ([Feast Documentation][2])

Concrete assertions include:

* No future queue snapshot or later-published snapshot can match; stale or missing snapshots remain explicitly missing.
* Future claims, later assignment versions, and later uploads cannot change the corresponding historical features.
* Every feature has traceable source records and a declared aggregation cutoff; a suffix such as `_at_t0` is not accepted as evidence.

**Executable normative model and pattern contract.** Encode the supplied rules as a guarded process model or state machine. Interpret the fast-track assessment and the shared assessment milestone as the same milestone, rather than requiring two assessments because it appears twice in the prose. Preserve fraud/evidence/assessment concurrency. Do not add unstated requirements, such as an investigation-completion event or appeal deadline.

Specify whether each arrow means **direct succession**, **eventual succession**, or succession within a projected branch. Fraud events may interleave between evidence activities without violating their branch order. A global direct-follows requirement would incorrectly reject such behavior.

**Prefix conformance, not premature completion checking.** Check whether the observed prefix can be extended to an accepted execution:

$$
p_{t_0}\in \operatorname{Pref}(L(M))
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists s:\;p_{t_0}s\in L(M).
$$

For an online claim, missing future assessment, decision, and closure events are not deviations. Prefix alignment should therefore end at a reachable intermediate state rather than penalize all unexecuted activities required for a complete case. PM4Py supplies alignment-based conformance diagnostics; the audit must select or implement the appropriate prefix acceptance semantics rather than assume ordinary complete-trace diagnostics provide them. ([PM4Py Documentation][3])

After the supplied prefix, the explanation validator should distinguish:

| Status at `t0`                             | Application to this process                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Observed                                   | Registration and the first completed coverage check.                                                                                     |
| Conditionally enabled next                 | A document request on the evidence route; assessment on an eligible fast-track route; investigation opening if its trigger is satisfied. |
| Future-possible, but not currently enabled | Receipt before a request exists, validation before receipt, settlement before assessment, and appeal before a qualifying decision.       |
| Impossible under the supplied model        | A genuine assessment occurring after terminal closure, absent a separately authorized reopening extension.                               |
| Unknown                                    | Trigger satisfaction, routing eligibility, or tied-event order where the required evidence is missing.                                   |

A high initial complexity rating does not itself prove which route will occur. Likewise, an enabled investigation is not an observed investigation.

**Same-timestamp ambiguity.** Use trusted source sequence or causal identifiers where available. Otherwise preserve a partial order. Evaluate admissible orderings against the **whole surrounding trace**, including decision prerequisites. Classify the result as necessarily conformant, necessarily nonconformant, or order-dependent.

For Z, do not arbitrarily reverse the two events and declare the case repaired: other events may still make the trace inconsistent. Conversely, the displayed reversal does not prove an actual business violation. A model-preferred ordering is not evidence of the actual ordering. Regardless of that resolution, these events remain outside the early prefix.

**Appeals and repeated decisions.** A trace containing

```text
... -> Settlement Decision Recorded [Partial]
    -> Appeal
    -> Settlement Decision Recorded [...]
    -> Claim Closed
```

can be legitimate. The second decision must not be rejected merely because its coarse label repeats. Check decision identifiers, outcomes, appeal links, and source event identities to distinguish reconsideration from duplicate ingestion. An appeal following only an Approve decision fails the supplied prerequisite. Y’s coarse full-trace string does not contain enough decision-outcome detail to certify this guard.

**Leakage and explanation regression tests.** For a fixed model and identical historically available prefix, appending, deleting, or changing future events must leave the feature vector, prediction, and explanation unchanged. Repeat this test with future records belonging to related claims and employees. Test both the model encoder and any surrogate sequence explainer: impossible motifs may originate in explainer perturbations rather than directly in model inputs.

Footprints and DFGs are useful screening tools for suspicious order relations. They cannot, by themselves, establish guarded appeal eligibility, cycle correspondence, or normative authorization. **A frequent DFG edge is an observation, not a business rule.**

Finally, a verified pre-`t0` violation is a different case from leakage: it may legitimately support a **prefix-conformance anomaly** feature. It must be described as a deviation, not as compliant process progress. None of the supplied future motifs becomes admissible through that exception.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

These classes overlap: for example, queue length is a process-state signal that may also encode unequal service access.

| Class                                      | Surviving or repairable signals                                                                                                              | Defensible interpretation                                                                              | Unsupported interpretation                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Defensible predictive signal               | Frozen initial complexity; repaired prior-claim history; reserve and channel after availability verification                                 | “These characteristics may help distinguish breach risk in clean, held-out landmark data.”             | “Complexity causes this amount of delay,” “prior claims imply difficult behavior,” or “the reserve proves fraud.”    |
| Process-state or congestion signal         | Prefix age; one-sided as-of queue measurement; historically known assignment, workload, and verified conformance flags                       | “The claim has already consumed time” or “it enters a measured workload context associated with risk.” | “Eighteen queued claims guarantees a breach,” or “moving this claim automatically removes the attributed risk.”      |
| Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review | Region, channel, prior history, team, and potentially reserve                                                                                | “The model may be exploiting associations with access, routing, case mix, or other characteristics.”   | “The claimant’s region intrinsically causes delay,” or “predictive accuracy makes the resulting treatment fair.”     |
| Drift/calendar proxy                       | Calendar week; policy and holiday context available at prediction                                                                            | “Risk estimates vary across periods that may differ operationally.”                                    | “Week 52 itself causes breaches,” or a separately identified holiday/policy effect without adequate comparison data. |
| Unusable until lineage is repaired         | Current risk score, nightly document count, extraction-date experience/history, uncertified queue values, reserve/channel without timestamps | “This feature design may be useful after historical reconstruction.”                                   | “A plausible name, sensible value, or frequent agreement with historical reality proves availability.”               |

**Predictive validity, causality, actionability, and fairness are separate standards.** An association can be useful for prediction without identifying the effect of an intervention. Moving from “this feature changes the model’s output” to “changing this feature would improve settlement time” requires an explicit intervention and additional causal identification assumptions or evidence. ([SHAP][4])

Congestion is potentially actionable at the operational level, but resource changes may affect other claims. Region is not an appropriate attribute to “change”; verified repair access or communication support may be actionable mechanisms. Those mechanisms are hypotheses here, not established explanations of any selected claim.

Two further boundaries matter:

**Document quantity is not evidence-cycle completion.** Registration attachments could produce a legitimate positive document count before any `Documents Requested` event. However, count alone does not establish completeness, validity, relevance, or reduced uncertainty.

**The target needs an explicit clock contract.** Confirm its start, calendar/business-day definition, settlement endpoint, and treatment of appeals and withdrawals. Prefix age remains valid without these details, but numerical remaining slack does not. An appeal occurring after the target’s stopping event could be post-outcome information rather than a cause of its breach.

The JSON below is a **qualitative audit reconstruction**, not a recomputed model explanation. Its directions retain the supplied signs only as provisional associations for temporally admissible inputs; clean-model signs may change. Uncertified queue values are withheld, not rejected as a feature concept. Channel and reserve receive the same treatment rather than being assumed available.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

**Priority 0 — Contain the defective output and define the prediction contract.** Stop presenting these scores as validated early warnings. Preserve the original inputs and explanations for forensic comparison. Document the exact landmark and target clock, including what constitutes settlement and how subsequent reconsideration affects the label. Separate prospective risk explanations from retrospective descriptions of completed cases.

**Priority 1 — Implement historical, versioned feature storage.** Store entity identifiers, values, effective times, first-available times, version/supersession history, source event identifiers, sequence information, and transformation versions. For each prediction, retain the actual feature vector, freshness indicators, model version, and explanation configuration.

Maintain historical versions for assignments, reserves, risk scores, and employee attributes. Apply availability-aware joins recursively: a historical risk-score timestamp is insufficient if its underlying calculation used future information. A backfill must not silently rewrite what was knowable at an earlier prediction.

**Priority 2 — Rebuild the complete training and serving pipeline from prefixes.** Recompute every training example at its own first coverage-check landmark. Audit sequence encoders, embeddings, aggregate tables, missingness indicators, and unranked inputs—not just the displayed features. Reconstruct related-claim history using then-known records and identity mappings. Fit preprocessing and feature selection only on the relevant training partition; otherwise leakage can remain after obvious columns are removed. ([Scikit-learn][5])

Preserve legitimate complexity, age, and repaired congestion information. Retain other baseline or proxy signals according to evidence and governance decisions rather than deleting them indiscriminately.

**Priority 3 — Make temporal and process tests release-blocking.** Automate suffix-invariance tests, future-related-record injection tests, one-sided snapshot checks, constant-prefix-pattern checks, appeal-guard tests, and tied-timestamp tests. Confirm that valid short prefixes pass without being forced to closure.

Maintain separate diagnostic outcomes for unavailable information, unknown provenance, genuine process deviations, and ordering ambiguity. Explanation perturbations must preserve feasible histories and cannot silently introduce future activities. A process-aware explanation should never describe a merely enabled activity as already performed.

**Priority 4 — Refit, reassess discrimination, and recalibrate.** Do not substitute zero for leaked fields in the existing model and call the result repaired. Retrain and evaluate using temporally ordered cohorts and labels sufficiently mature for the chosen outcome. Use separate data for calibration and final evaluation.

Report discrimination alongside reliability curves, Brier score or log loss, and threshold-specific performance. Calibration must be reassessed after changing the model; it cannot be inferred from the previous probabilities. Brier score and log loss also reflect more than calibration alone, so reliability assessment remains necessary. ([Scikit-learn][6])

No supplied data establish how much performance will fall, whether cases will change rank, or what their new probabilities should be.

**Priority 5 — Validate explanation stability and proxy-related harms.** Re-estimate explanations across bootstrap samples, model seeds, time windows, and defensible landmark-specific reference populations. Record changes in signs, rankings, and case narratives. Compare grouped attributions for related variables—such as region, channel, and repair access—rather than treating unstable individual credit allocations as distinct mechanisms.

Evaluate errors, calibration, alert rates, and intervention allocation across relevant groups and intersections, with uncertainty intervals where sample sizes permit. Measure the consequences of deployment: extra assistance and deprioritization have different fairness implications. Disaggregated performance is a central assessment tool, but choosing relevant harms and groups remains contextual. ([fairlearn.org][7])

Feature removal is one possible mitigation, not a substitute for this assessment. Operational interventions should target verified needs and respect process prerequisites; SHAP signs do not justify suppressing appeals, skipping required evidence, or opening investigations without a trigger.

**Priority 6 — Monitor schema, lineage, process, and outcome drift together.** Monitor renamed or duplicated coverage events, missing sequence indices, timestamp precision, changing feature definitions, snapshot freshness, late-arrival rates, and violations of supposedly frozen fields. Track routing, policy, calendar, and population changes alongside calibration and subgroup outcomes.

A nonzero observed appeal or closure motif at this landmark should trigger an immediate data/explanation investigation. Changing regional or calendar importance should trigger robustness review—not an automatic new causal story.

**Release criterion:** every displayed driver must have auditable landmark-time provenance, a process-consistent interpretation or explicitly identified deviation, and an explanation recomputed for the clean model. Operational recommendations additionally require evidence about the proposed action—not merely evidence that a feature predicts risk.
