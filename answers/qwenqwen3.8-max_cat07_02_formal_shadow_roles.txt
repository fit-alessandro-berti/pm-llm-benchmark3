# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

**R07_Giorgio — commercial shadow role**  
- Shadow nature: Giorgio is formally a senior service agent but performs substantial pricing and negotiation work, especially when Fatima is absent. His profile bridges front-office service and commercial exception handling.  
- Benefits: provides continuity during commercial specialist absence, reduces bottleneck risk, allows complex front-office cases to receive pricing attention without always waiting for Fatima, and builds organizational resilience through cross-training.  
- Risks: the substitution episode shows increased pricing-exception duration, suggesting lower specialist fluency. There is also risk of inconsistent pricing decisions, unclear approval thresholds, and role overload if Giorgio is expected to maintain both front-office volume and commercial exception work.  
- Interpretation: this shadow role is not inherently improper. It is adaptive, but it should be made explicit with defined pricing limits, escalation rules, and training.

**R13_Mateo — cross-functional escalation broker**  
- Bridging nature: Mateo connects technical, security, commercial, quality, front-office, and supervisory work. He appears in reopen, escalation, security filtering, and coordination fragments.  
- Benefits: reduces unnecessary specialist load, filters low-risk referrals, helps complex cases move across functional boundaries, and provides a single coordination point for high-complexity or reopened cases.  
- Risks: Mateo can become a hidden dependency. If his filtering criteria are not explicit, critical cases may be delayed, as seen with two critical security cases waiting more than two days. His preliminary A9 activity can also blur the boundary between coordination and supervisory authority.  
- Interpretation: Mateo is a broker, not a supervisor. Management should formalize escalation criteria, decision rights, and handoff points to Hana, Erik, Fatima, and technical resources.

**R05_Erik — rare but critical specialist / security boundary role**  
- Specialist nature: Erik is not a high-volume resource, but his A6 Security Review work is central to high-risk cases. He also bridges security risk and technical implementation when security concerns affect configuration or implementation.  
- Benefits: protects the organization from security failures, provides expert judgment on critical cases, and prevents inappropriate implementation of risky solutions.  
- Risks: low frequency can make him appear expendable or statistically unimportant in naïve mining. He is also a single point of failure. If Mateo filters too aggressively or Erik is unavailable, critical cases can stall.  
- Interpretation: Erik should be treated as a critical-control role, not a routine capacity resource. Staffing decisions should be based on risk exposure, not event count.

**Additional boundary and bridging roles worth recognizing**  
- **R14_Nora — automation boundary role**: Nora shapes the intake queue and performs entitlement precheck. Benefit: high-volume automation. Risk: if precheck errors are not visible, human agents may inherit incomplete or incorrect entitlement data. Nora should be monitored as a process boundary, not clustered as a human agent.  
- **R11_Kai and R12_Lina — temporary surge workforce role**: they provide seasonal capacity for simple cases. Benefit: absorbs volume. Risk: incomplete validation notes and reopens show that surge capacity without quality controls creates downstream cost.  
- **R09_Ivan and R10_Julia — quality gatekeeping role**: they bridge execution and control by checking work and updating the knowledge base. Benefit: reduces repeat errors and supports learning. Risk: if quality authority is unclear, they may become bottlenecks or their findings may be overridden without trace.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Load and format the event log
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE_ID = "case:concept:name"
ACTIVITY = "concept:name"
TIMESTAMP = "time:timestamp"
RESOURCE = "org:resource"

df = pd.read_csv("order_to_resolution_log.csv")

# PM4Py standard formatting
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id=CASE_ID,
    activity_key=ACTIVITY,
    timestamp_key=TIMESTAMP,
    resource_key=RESOURCE,
)

# Ensure useful case-level attributes are available.
# In many logs, case_complexity, customer_segment, product_line,
# temporary_substitution, and escalation_level are case attributes
# repeated on events. We keep them, but interpret carefully.
case_attributes = df.groupby(CASE_ID).agg(
    case_complexity=("case_complexity", "first"),
    customer_segment=("customer_segment", "first"),
    product_line=("product_line", "first"),
).reset_index()

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Separate automation boundary from human organizational mining
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Nora is a system boundary resource. Including her directly in
# human role discovery can distort activity-frequency clusters.
human_log = df[df[RESOURCE] != "R14_Nora"].copy()

# Keep Nora visible for boundary-role analysis.
automation_log = df[df[RESOURCE] == "R14_Nora"].copy()

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Discover organizational roles from human resources
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# The threshold controls granularity. A lower threshold creates more,
# finer roles; a higher threshold merges resources more aggressively.
# Do not choose threshold only to maximize cluster cleanliness.
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(human_log, threshold=0.30)

role_rows = []
for role_id, members in enumerate(roles):
    for resource in members:
        role_rows.append({
            "mined_role_id": f"role_{role_id:02d}",
            RESOURCE: resource,
        })

mined_roles = pd.DataFrame(role_rows)

# Add Nora manually as an automation boundary role, not a human role.
nora_role = pd.DataFrame([{
    "mined_role_id": "automation_boundary",
    RESOURCE: "R14_Nora",
}])

mined_roles = pd.concat([mined_roles, nora_role], ignore_index=True)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Activity-based resource similarity
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# PM4Py helper: similarity based on activity profiles.
sim_pairs = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(
    human_log,
    threshold=0.50
)

# Expected output is a list of tuples such as:
# (resource_1, resource_2, similarity)
similarity_df = pd.DataFrame(
    sim_pairs,
    columns=["resource_1", "resource_2", "activity_similarity"]
)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Manual activity-profile matrix for validation
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
resource_activity = (
    human_log
    .groupby([RESOURCE, ACTIVITY])
    .size()
    .unstack(fill_value=0)
)

# Row-normalize each resource's activity mix.
resource_activity_norm = (
    resource_activity
    .div(resource_activity.sum(axis=1), axis=0)
    .fillna(0.0)
)

# Cosine similarity over normalized activity profiles.
X = resource_activity_norm.values
norms = np.linalg.norm(X, axis=1, keepdims=True)
norms[norms == 0] = 1.0

cosine_matrix = X @ X.T / (norms @ norms.T)

activity_cosine = pd.DataFrame(
    cosine_matrix,
    index=resource_activity_norm.index,
    columns=resource_activity_norm.index
)

# Example: inspect pairs that may be overinterpreted.
pairs_to_check = [
    ("R03_Chen", "R04_Dina"),
    ("R09_Ivan", "R10_Julia"),
    ("R11_Kai", "R12_Lina"),
    ("R01_Amira", "R02_Ben"),
    ("R06_Fatima", "R07_Giorgio"),
    ("R08_Hana", "R13_Mateo"),
]

for r1, r2 in pairs_to_check:
    if r1 in activity_cosine.index and r2 in activity_cosine.columns:
        print(f"{r1} - {r2}: {activity_cosine.loc[r1, r2]:.3f}")

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Handover-of-work and working-together networks
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
handover_network = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
    human_log,
    threshold=0.10
)

working_together_network = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(
    human_log,
    threshold=0.10
)

subcontracting_network = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(
    human_log,
    threshold=0.10
)

def network_to_dataframe(network):
    rows = []
    for source, targets in network.items():
        for target, weight in targets.items():
            rows.append({
                "source_resource": source,
                "target_resource": target,
                "weight": weight,
            })
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

handover_df = network_to_dataframe(handover_network)
working_together_df = network_to_dataframe(working_together_network)
subcontracting_df = network_to_dataframe(subcontracting_network)

# Useful checks:
# - Who hands over to Hana for A9?
# - Who hands over to Erik for A6?
# - Does Mateo broker between technical, commercial, and supervisory roles?
print(handover_df.sort_values("weight", ascending=False).head(20))
print(working_together_df.sort_values("weight", ascending=False).head(20))

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 7. Join mined roles with case complexity exposure
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Use distinct cases per resource to avoid overcounting repeated events.
resource_cases = (
    df[[CASE_ID, RESOURCE]]
    .drop_duplicates()
    .merge(case_attributes, on=CASE_ID, how="left")
)

complexity_counts = (
    resource_cases
    .groupby([RESOURCE, "case_complexity"])
    .size()
    .unstack(fill_value=0)
)

# Add a critical-exposure indicator.
if "Critical" in complexity_counts.columns:
    complexity_counts["critical_case_count"] = complexity_counts["Critical"]
else:
    complexity_counts["critical_case_count"] = 0

# If a numeric complexity score exists, use it as an additional check.
if "case_complexity_score" in df.columns:
    complexity_score = (
        resource_cases
        .groupby(RESOURCE)["case_complexity_score"]
        .median()
        .rename("median_complexity_score")
    )
else:
    complexity_score = pd.Series(dtype=float, name="median_complexity_score")

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 8. Temporary substitution indicators
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
if "temporary_substitution" in df.columns:
    substitution_events = df[df["temporary_substitution"].astype(bool)].copy()
else:
    substitution_events = df.iloc[0:0].copy()

substitution_by_resource_activity = (
    substitution_events
    .groupby([RESOURCE, ACTIVITY])
    .size()
    .unstack(fill_value=0)
    .add_prefix("subst_")
)

substitution_by_resource = (
    substitution_events
    .groupby(RESOURCE)
    .size()
    .rename("temporary_substitution_event_count")
)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 9. Combine role assignments with complexity and substitution evidence
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
role_analysis = mined_roles.copy()

role_analysis = role_analysis.merge(
    complexity_counts,
    left_on=RESOURCE,
    right_index=True,
    how="left"
)

role_analysis = role_analysis.merge(
    complexity_score,
    left_on=RESOURCE,
    right_index=True,
    how="left"
)

role_analysis = role_analysis.merge(
    substitution_by_resource,
    left_on=RESOURCE,
    right_index=True,
    how="left"
)

role_analysis = role_analysis.merge(
    substitution_by_resource_activity,
    left_on=RESOURCE,
    right_index=True,
    how="left"
)

role_analysis["temporary_substitution_event_count"] = (
    role_analysis["temporary_substitution_event_count"]
    .fillna(0)
    .astype(int)
)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 10. Inspect variants, service time, and critical paths
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
variants = pm4py.get_variants(
    df,
    case_id=CASE_ID,
    activity_key=ACTIVITY
)

# Service and waiting time can reveal whether shadow substitution
# increases cycle time or waiting time.
df = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
    df,
    case_id=CASE_ID,
    timestamp_key=TIMESTAMP
)

# Depending on PM4Py version, inserted columns may include
# case_service_time and case_waiting_time.
if "case_service_time" in df.columns:
    service_by_case = (
        df.groupby(CASE_ID)["case_service_time"]
        .median()
        .rename("median_case_service_time")
    )
else:
    service_by_case = pd.Series(dtype=float, name="median_case_service_time")

# Filter critical cases and security-review events for separate review.
critical_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df,
    "case_complexity",
    ["Critical"],
    level="case"
)

security_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df,
    ACTIVITY,
    ["A6 Security Review"],
    level="event"
)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 11. Optional case clustering, used only for context
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Case clustering can help identify routine versus exceptional paths,
# but it should not be used alone to infer formal authority.
# API details vary by PM4Py version.
#
# clustered_log = pm4py.cluster_log(
#     df,
#     case_id=CASE_ID,
#     activity_key=ACTIVITY,
#     timestamp_key=TIMESTAMP,
# )

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 12. Interpretation guards
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Do not interpret mined roles as formal authority by themselves.
# Validate with:
#   1. case complexity exposure,
#   2. critical-case exposure,
#   3. temporary substitution episodes,
#   4. handover directionality,
#   5. approval/reopen authority,
#   6. HR/system permission data,
#   7. rare but high-impact activities such as A6 and A9.
#
# Similar activity profile != same authority.
# Temporary substitution != permanent competence.
# Low event count != low organizational importance.
```

# 6. Operational interpretation

Management should use the discovered structure as a decision-support map, not as an automatic organizational chart. The event log reveals work patterns, but formal authority, risk exposure, and contractual limits must be validated separately.

**Staffing implications**
- Keep permanent front-office agents focused on low and medium complexity cases, while reserving high-complexity hybrid cases for more experienced staff such as Amira or Giorgio.
- Treat Kai and Lina as surge capacity for simple, scripted work. Do not count them as equivalent to permanent front-office capacity for medium or high complexity cases.
- Maintain Chen and Dina as a technical pair, but route critical and security-adjacent cases preferentially to Chen until Dina’s critical-case competence is validated.
- Protect Erik’s capacity for critical security work. Do not absorb his time into routine technical work because his event count is low.
- Formalize Fatima’s commercial authority and define Giorgio’s delegated threshold. If pricing exceptions are growing, consider training a second formal commercial specialist rather than relying indefinitely on Giorgio’s shadow role.
- Recognize Mateo as an escalation coordinator. If escalation volume grows, add coordination support rather than forcing Mateo to become a full approver.

**Training implications**
- Train temporary agents specifically on complete Validate Entitlement documentation, because incomplete A2 notes are already linked to reopened cases.
- Cross-train Amira and Ben in triage and basic diagnostics, but avoid assuming that high standard-response volume qualifies them for technical or commercial exception work.
- Pair Dina with Chen for complex diagnostics and security-adjacent cases, with explicit sign-off criteria before she handles critical cases independently.
- Provide Giorgio with formal pricing-policy training, negotiation guidelines, and clear escalation thresholds if the organization wants to retain his shadow commercial role.
- Train Mateo on escalation criteria, security risk screening, and handoff discipline to reduce the chance that filtering delays critical cases.
- Train quality analysts not only on checking, but also on knowledge-base curation and reopen prevention.

**Risk-control implications**
- Define a RACI or authority matrix:
  - Nora performs automated precheck, not final accountable entitlement validation.
  - Front-office agents can close routine cases but cannot approve pricing or security exceptions.
  - Fatima owns commercial exceptions, with Giorgio as a bounded delegate.
  - Erik owns security risk decisions.
  - Hana owns final supervisory approval.
  - Mateo coordinates but does not finally approve unless explicit delegation exists.
  - Ivan and Julia can reject or flag work, but the escalation path for disagreements should be clear.
- Monitor substitution episodes as risk events. Track duration, rework, reopen rates, critical-case waiting time, and approval recheck rates during substitutions.
- Add controls for shadow work: pricing thresholds, preliminary approval limits, security filtering rules, and audit trails for Mateo’s and Giorgio’s interventions.
- Monitor Nora’s precheck quality. If automated entitlement precheck errors propagate, human agents will absorb hidden rework.

**Resilience implications**
- The process depends on several bridging roles: Giorgio for commercial backup, Mateo for escalation brokerage, and Erik for critical security judgment. These roles improve resilience but create hidden dependencies.
- Create documented backup arrangements for Fatima, Hana, and Erik. Rare critical roles are precisely the ones that need formal succession or on-call coverage.
- Keep quality analysts involved early in high-complexity cases, not only at the end, to reduce reopen loops.
- Use the knowledge-base role of Ivan and Julia to convert reopened cases and temporary-substitution failures into prevention rules.

**Cautions about changing staffing based only on mined roles**
1. **Do not reduce rare critical specialists because their event count is low.** Erik, Hana, and Fatima may appear statistically small, but their work controls high-risk outcomes. Cutting them based on frequency would confuse volume with importance.
2. **Do not promote or expand temporary or high-volume resources solely because they close many cases.** Kai and Lina close many simple requests, but the reopen evidence shows that volume without validation quality can increase downstream cost. Similarly, Giorgio’s shadow commercial work is valuable, but the increased duration during substitution shows that shadow performance is not proof of full specialist competence.
