**Cause of the drop versus real operational concern.** The measured collapse from 93% to 32% is overwhelmingly representational: rank 1, 2 and 4 are pure data-and-semantics mechanisms and, together, take Jun-Jul back to 89%, and rank 3 is a model-governance failure rather than a behavioral one. The residual operational concerns are the reverse order: the possibly bypassed compliance checks (~55 cases) and the unmodeled `Manual Override` matter most for risk even though they contribute least to the fitness number, and the unstratified high-value population is a live blind spot because the model cannot express the EUR 50,000 rule at all.

## 3. Causal-abductive explanation

```text
UI_release            -> recorded_labels, label_semantics_contract, client_rollout_lag
gateway_release       -> recorded_labels, technical_event_stream, resource_null_rate
ETL_change            -> recorded_order, recorded_timestamps, sequence_number_unused
policy_change         -> true_process, reviewer_behavior, model_governance_gap, high_value_flag_relevance
supervisor_memo       -> reviewer_behavior
interface_patch_0812  -> recorded_labels, override_logging_visibility
model_governance_gap  -> normative_model
normative_model       -> alignment_moves
true_process          -> reviewer_behavior, business_outcomes, logging_layer
reviewer_behavior     -> true_process, override_logging_visibility, compliance_check_execution
compliance_check_execution -> logging_layer, business_outcomes, incident_detection
logging_layer         -> recorded_labels, recorded_order, event_completeness
client_rollout_lag    -> recorded_labels
technical_event_stream-> recorded_labels, event_completeness
sequence_number_unused-> recorded_order
recorded_labels       -> alignment_moves
recorded_order        -> alignment_moves
event_completeness    -> alignment_moves, extraction_aggregation_defect
extraction_aggregation_defect -> measured_fitness, reported_variant_tables
alignment_moves       -> measured_fitness, deviation_diagnosis
measured_fitness      -> stakeholder_conclusions
business_outcomes     -> incident_detection, stakeholder_conclusions
incident_detection    -> confirmed_incidents
high_value_flag_relevance -> true_process, normative_model
override_logging_visibility -> recorded_labels
```

Four independent mechanisms landed inside a hundred-day window, and each of them acts on a different arrow into `alignment_moves`. The UI release and the gateway release changed `recorded_labels` while leaving `true_process` untouched, so the model demanded `Classify` and saw `Risk Screen` plus an extra token. The ETL change corrupted `recorded_order` for exactly the two activities whose timestamp source moved to ingestion time. The policy change altered `true_process` legitimately while `normative_model` stayed frozen, which converts lawful behavior into deviation. Because alignment cost is additive over moves, these effects compound multiplicatively in the fitting-trace percentage: a case can be non-fitting for a label reason, a technical-event reason and an ordering reason at once, which is why removing them one at a time yields the staircase 32, 58, 81, 89 rather than a single explanatory jump.

Unchanged approval, appeal and cycle-time figures do not prove the drop is a data error. Fitness is measured on every case, while outcome metrics are population averages: roughly 30 cases per period with a missing compliance check are one twentieth of the volume and cannot move a 3.7% appeal rate, and appeals lag decisions so recent periods are censored. Nor do flat outcomes rule out noncompliance. `compliance_check_execution` reaches `confirmed_incidents` only through `incident_detection`, which compliance itself declares incomplete, and a skipped control raises risk without necessarily producing a visible bad outcome in a small sample. Symmetrically, the fact that data repairs explain most of the number does not exonerate reviewers, because the repairs that fixed labels, technical events and ordering left the missing checks and the overrides completely untouched.

Governance, data-aware controls and re-baselining** (isolates whether the fixed pipeline and versioned model are trustworthy going forward) | Publish model v2 with the sign-off branch expressed as a data-aware constraint conditioned on amount, or as stratified models, and re-baseline conformance from the earliest period under the frozen v2 pipeline; back-test v2 against Apr-May and Jun-Jul to confirm it does not overgeneralize; add a canary conformance check to every release gate; monitor precision, deviation-class mix and event-completeness metrics as first-class KPIs alongside fitness; set up a periodic sampled provenance audit for missing-event classes | `pm4py.parse_process_tree` for the versioned tree, `pm4py.convert_to_petri_net`, `pm4py.write_pnml` for the versioned artifact, `pm4py.fitness_alignments` and `pm4py.precision_alignments` in regression tests, plus attribute-conditioned rule checks (LTL-style or log-skeleton) that the control-flow model cannot express | The complete history under the frozen pipeline, the versioned model artifacts, the release calendar, and the semantics dictionary from phase 1 | A stable, reproducible conformance baseline whose future movements can be attributed to behavior rather than to releases; and an explicit list of rules the control-flow model cannot enforce | Re-baselining can institutionalize a permissive model, so v2 must be justified by precision and by back-tests rather than by its fitness score. Freezing a pipeline built on unverified repairs would make an assumption invisible and permanent |

Each phase is deliberately ordered so that the cheapest, most decisive mechanism is eliminated first: phase 1 establishes what the log even means, so phase 2 is not measuring against a moving target; phase 2 strips every explanation that can be produced without human misbehavior, so phase 3's residual is small and worth auditing; phase 3 separates model debt from data debt from behavior; and only then does phase 4 change what is measured going forward. Simply re-running conformance at any point would produce a new number with the same unattributable cause.

## 6. PM4Py reproduction and model-comparison code

```python
"""
Conformance root-cause reproduction for the regulated claims process.

The supplied data are VARIANT AGGREGATES, not the original timestamped log, so the
numbers printed here will not equal the aggregate report exactly. The purpose is a
logically coherent, executable pipeline that isolates each candidate mechanism:
label change, technical event, ordering defect, and model obsolescence.
"""

from collections import Counter

import pm4py

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 0. Normative and candidate models
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------

NORMATIVE_TREE_STR = (
    "-> ( 'Receive', 'Classify', "
    "X ( * ( 'Standard Review', 'Request Info' ), "
    "-> ( 'Enhanced Review', 'Compliance Check' ) ), "
    "'Decision', 'Notify' )"
)

# Candidate v2: control-flow-only extension that permits an OPTIONAL sign-off
# between review and decision. X('Supervisor Sign-off', tau) = skippable activity.
CANDIDATE_TREE_STR = (
    "-> ( 'Receive', 'Classify', "
    "X ( * ( 'Standard Review', 'Request Info' ), "
    "-> ( 'Enhanced Review', 'Compliance Check' ) ), "
    "X ( 'Supervisor Sign-off', tau ), "
    "'Decision', 'Notify' )"
)


def tree_to_net(tree_str):
    tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_str)
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)
    return tree, net, im, fm


# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Variant tables, expanded into logs
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------

APR_MAY = {
    "Receive,Classify,Standard Review,Decision,Notify": 380,
    "Receive,Classify,Enhanced Review,Compliance Check,Decision,Notify": 180,
    "Receive,Classify,Standard Review,Request Info,Standard Review,Decision,Notify": 40,
}

JUN_JUL = {
    "Receive,Risk Screen,Queue Routed,Standard Review,Decision,Notify": 270,
    "Receive,Risk Screen,Queue Routed,Enhanced Review,Compliance Check,Decision,Notify": 145,
    "Receive,Risk Screen,Queue Routed,Standard Review,Notify,Decision": 70,
    "Receive,Risk Screen,Standard Review,Decision,Notify": 90,
    "Receive,Classify,Queue Routed,Standard Review,Decision,Notify": 30,
    "Receive,Risk Screen,Queue Routed,Enhanced Review,Decision,Notify": 25,
    "Receive,Risk Screen,Queue Routed,Manual Override,Standard Review,Decision,Notify": 20,
}

AUG_SEP = {
    "Receive,Risk Screen,Queue Routed,Standard Review,Decision,Notify": 210,
    "Receive,Risk Screen,Queue Routed,Enhanced Review,Compliance Check,Supervisor Sign-off,Decision,Notify": 150,
    "Receive,Risk Screen,Queue Routed,Enhanced Review,Compliance Check,Decision,Notify": 90,
    "Receive,Risk Screen,Queue Routed,Standard Review,Supervisor Sign-off,Decision,Notify": 60,
    "Receive,Risk Screen,Queue Routed,Standard Review,Notify,Decision": 45,
    "Receive,Risk Screen,Queue Routed,Enhanced Review,Decision,Notify": 30,
    "Receive,Risk Screen,Queue Routed,Manual Override,Standard Review,Decision,Notify": 35,
}


def expand(variants):
    """Variant table -> list of trace strings (one string per case)."""
    traces = []
    for trace, count in variants.items():
        traces.extend([trace] * count)
    return traces


def to_log(traces):
    """Trace strings -> event log object usable by the pm4py simplified interface."""
    return pm4py.parse_event_log_string(traces, sep=",")


# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Transparent normalization pipeline (each step is one hypothesis repair)
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------

LABEL_MAP = {"Risk Screen": "Classify"}       # UI release 4.2 renamed the screen
TECHNICAL_EVENTS = {"Queue Routed"}           # gateway event, not a business step
# 'Manual Override' is deliberately NOT in TECHNICAL_EVENTS: IT states it is a
# real user action, so removing it would hide behaviour instead of repairing data.


def apply_label_map(traces, mapping=LABEL_MAP):
    out = []
    for t in traces:
        out.append(",".join(mapping.get(a, a) for a in t.split(",")))
    return out


def drop_technical(traces, technical=TECHNICAL_EVENTS):
    out = []
    for t in traces:
        out.append(",".join(a for a in t.split(",") if a not in technical))
    return out


def reorder_by_source_sequence(traces):
    """
    Stand-in for ordering by the source-system sequence number that the ETL job
    stopped using on 2026-06-15. On the real log this is NOT a string rewrite:
    you would re-sort the dataframe by (case id, source sequence number), rebuild
    'time:timestamp', and re-run pm4py.format_dataframe(...). Here only the
    aggregate variants exist, so the known ingestion-time inversion
    ... Notify, Decision  ->  ... Decision, Notify
    is undone explicitly. Caveat: only 11 of 70 Jun-Jul inversions were confirmed
    by source screenshots; this repair assumes the rest share the same cause.
    """
    out = []
    for t in traces:
        acts = t.split(",")
        for i in range(len(acts) - 1):
            if acts[i] == "Notify" and acts[i + 1] == "Decision":
                acts[i], acts[i + 1] = acts[i + 1], acts[i]
        out.append(",".join(acts))
    return out


def normalize(traces, map_labels=True, remove_technical=True, fix_order=False):
    out = list(traces)
    if map_labels:
        out = apply_label_map(out)
    if remove_technical:
        out = drop_technical(out)
    if fix_order:
        out = reorder_by_source_sequence(out)
    return out


# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Diagnostics helpers
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------

def dfg_report(log, label, top=8):
    dfg, start_activities, end_activities = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
    print(f"\n[DFG] {label}")
    for (a, b), c in sorted(dfg.items(), key=lambda kv: -kv[1])[:top]:
        print(f"    {a} -> {b}: {c}")
    print(f"    start: {dict(start_activities)}")
    print(f"    end:   {dict(end_activities)}")


def deviation_profile(log, net, im, fm):
    """Aggregate move-on-log / visible move-on-model counts from alignments."""
    diagnostics = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(log, net, im, fm)
    moves_on_log, visible_moves_on_model = Counter(), Counter()
    for case in diagnostics:
        if case is None:
            continue
        for log_step, model_step in case["alignment"]:
            if model_step == ">>" and log_step != ">>":
                moves_on_log[log_step] += 1
            elif log_step == ">>" and model_step not in (">>", None):
                visible_moves_on_model[model_step] += 1
    return moves_on_log, visible_moves_on_model


def evaluate(traces, net, im, fm, label, show_dfg=False, show_precision=False):
    log = to_log(traces)
    if show_dfg:
        dfg_report(log, label)
    fitness = pm4py.fitness_alignments(log, net, im, fm)
    moves_on_log, moves_on_model = deviation_profile(log, net, im, fm)
    print(f"\n[ALIGN] {label}")
    print(f"    fitting traces %      : {fitness['percentage_of_fitting_traces']:.1f}")
    print(f"    average trace fitness : {fitness['average_trace_fitness']:.3f}")
    print(f"    log fitness           : {fitness['log_fitness']:.3f}")
    print(f"    top moves on log      : {moves_on_log.most_common(5)}")
    print(f"    visible moves on model: {moves_on_model.most_common(5)}")
    if show_precision:
        precision = pm4py.precision_alignments(log, net, im, fm)
        print(f"    precision (alignments): {precision:.3f}")
    return fitness


# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Main investigation
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------

def main():
    _, norm_net, norm_im, norm_fm = tree_to_net(NORMATIVE_TREE_STR)
    _, cand_net, cand_im, cand_fm = tree_to_net(CANDIDATE_TREE_STR)

    apr_may = expand(APR_MAY)
    jun_jul = expand(JUN_JUL)
    aug_sep = expand(AUG_SEP)

    print("=" * 78)
    print("BASELINE: Apr-May, raw log vs normative model")
    print("=" * 78)
    evaluate(apr_may, norm_net, norm_im, norm_fm, "Apr-May raw", show_dfg=True)

    print("\n" + "=" * 78)
    print("STAGED REPAIRS: Jun-Jul (isolate one mechanism per stage)")
    print("=" * 78)
    stages_jun_jul = [
        ("S0 raw log", jun_jul),
        ("S1 + label map Risk Screen->Classify",
         normalize(jun_jul, True, False, False)),
        ("S2 + remove technical event Queue Routed",
         normalize(jun_jul, True, True, False)),
        ("S3 + order by source sequence number",
         normalize(jun_jul, True, True, True)),
    ]
    for label, traces in stages_jun_jul:
        evaluate(traces, norm_net, norm_im, norm_fm, f"Jun-Jul {label}",
                 show_dfg=(label == "S0 raw log"))
    dfg_report(to_log(stages_jun_jul[-1][1]), "Jun-Jul fully normalized")

    print("\n" + "=" * 78)
    print("RESIDUAL DEVIATIONS after normalization (Jun-Jul): real-behaviour candidates")
    print("=" * 78)
    residual_log, residual_model = deviation_profile(
        to_log(stages_jun_jul[-1][1]), norm_net, norm_im, norm_fm)
    print(f"    moves on log (unexplained by data repairs): {dict(residual_log)}")
    print(f"    visible moves on model (missing/skipped)  : {dict(residual_model)}")
    print("    -> 'Manual Override' = real user action, model gap or workaround.")
    print("    -> 'Compliance Check' on model = either true bypass or missing event;")
    print("       alignments alone CANNOT separate these two.")

    print("\n" + "=" * 78)
    print("MODEL COMPARISON: Aug-Sep, normalized log, normative vs candidate-v2")
    print("=" * 78)
    aug_sep_norm = normalize(aug_sep, True, True, True)
    evaluate(aug_sep_norm, norm_net, norm_im, norm_fm,
             "Aug-Sep normalized vs NORMATIVE (no sign-off)",
             show_dfg=True, show_precision=True)
    evaluate(aug_sep_norm, cand_net, cand_im, cand_fm,
             "Aug-Sep normalized vs CANDIDATE-v2 (optional sign-off)",
             show_precision=True)

    # Sanity check: the candidate model must not be used to launder Jun-Jul,
    # where no sign-off existed. Equal fitness with LOWER precision there is the
    # signature of an overpermissive model rather than a better model.
    jun_jul_norm = normalize(jun_jul, True, True, True)
    evaluate(jun_jul_norm, norm_net, norm_im, norm_fm,
             "Jun-Jul normalized vs NORMATIVE (precision reference)",
             show_precision=True)
    evaluate(jun_jul_norm, cand_net, cand_im, cand_fm,
             "Jun-Jul normalized vs CANDIDATE-v2 (overgeneralization check)",
             show_precision=True)

    print("\n" + "=" * 78)
    print("EQUIVALENT PM4PY-NATIVE FILTERING (what to use on the real log)")
    print("=" * 78)
    raw_log = to_log(apply_label_map(jun_jul))
    filtered_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        raw_log, "concept:name", TECHNICAL_EVENTS, level="event", retain=False)
    print("    pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(..., retain=False) events: "
          f"{len(raw_log)} -> {len(filtered_log)}")
    print("    Technical-event removal must be a declared, versioned filter, not an "
          "ad-hoc cleanup.")

    print("\n" + "=" * 78)
    print("CAVEATS")
    print("=" * 78)
    for caveat in [
        "1. The candidate model only makes 'Supervisor Sign-off' OPTIONAL. Being "
        "control-flow only, it cannot express the real rule 'sign-off REQUIRED iff "
        "claim amount > EUR 50,000'. It therefore accepts sign-off on small claims "
        "AND accepts missing sign-off on high-value claims: the very violation the "
        "policy targets becomes invisible.",
        "2. Higher fitness for the candidate model is partly bought with precision: "
        "an optional branch enlarges the accepted language. Always report fitness "
        "AND precision, and check that the candidate does not also 'fit' Jun-Jul, "
        "where no sign-off policy existed.",
        "3. The correct fix is a data-aware / multi-perspective check (e.g. LTL or "
        "log-skeleton style rules conditioned on the high-value attribute, or two "
        "stratified models for high-value and normal claims), not a permissive "
        "control-flow model.",
        "4. Reordering here rewrites known ingestion-time inversions in aggregate "
        "variants. On the real log, re-derive order from the source sequence number; "
        "only 11 of 70 inversions were provenance-confirmed, so treat the rest as a "
        "hypothesis to be audited.",
        "5. 'Manual Override' is kept as a business activity. Deleting it because it "
        "is inconvenient would convert a possible control weakness into a clean chart.",
        "6. Absolute numbers here differ from the aggregate report because variant "
        "aggregates lack timestamps, resources, case attributes and incomplete cases.",
    ]:
        print(f"  - {caveat}")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

Executed against PM4Py 2.7.23, the script reproduces the qualitative staircase rather than the exact report figures: Jun-Jul fitting traces move 0.0% (raw) to 13.8% (label map) to 82.3% (technical-event removal) to 93.1% (source ordering), leaving exactly 20 `Manual Override` moves on log and 25 `Compliance Check` moves on model. For Aug-Sep the normalized log yields 55.6% fitting with precision 0.921 against the normative model, versus 89.5% fitting with precision 0.936 against the candidate. Crucially, on Jun-Jul the candidate model gives identical fitness (93.1%) but precision drops from 0.896 to 0.789, which is the fingerprint of an overpermissive model: it buys nothing where the added behavior never occurred, and only pays off where the behavior is genuinely present.

## 7. Provisional conclusion

**Primary explanation for the measured collapse.** The conformance drop is predominantly a measurement and governance failure, not a behavioral one. Three data-and-semantics mechanisms account for almost all of the Jun-Jul deterioration, and the staged repairs quantify each: renaming `Classify` to `Risk Screen` in UI release 4.2 (32% to 58% fitting), a gateway-emitted technical event `Queue Routed` that was never a business step (58% to 81%), and an ETL switch to warehouse-ingestion time for `Decision` and `Notify` that inverted 70 adjacent event pairs while the source sequence number sat unused (81% to 89%). Aug-Sep has a fourth, distinct cause: the EUR 50,000 `Supervisor Sign-off` policy created a legitimate new activity while the tree was never versioned, so lawful work scored as deviation (66% to 86% by editing the model alone). Confidence: high, roughly 85-90%. Each mechanism has an independent dated trigger, a repair confined to itself, and corroboration from stable outcome metrics plus an events-per-case rise almost entirely attributable to the technical event.

**Whether a real compliance problem remains.** Quite possibly; it is a separate question. Two residuals survive every data repair: 25 Jun-Jul and 30 Aug-Sep enhanced reviews with no recorded `Compliance Check`, and `Manual Override` in 20 then 35 cases. Alignments cannot distinguish a skipped control from an unlogged one, no audit sample exists, and compliance says incident detection is incomplete, so flat appeal rates are weak evidence: 30 cases cannot move a 3.7% rate. Confidence that a genuine control weakness exists: low to moderate, roughly 30-40%, and deliberately not lower given that the strictest control is the one going missing.

**Immediate actions.** IT: restore source-sequence ordering, publish an event-semantics dictionary with the label mapping and declared technical events, and determine whether the 55 missing checks exist in the compliance system. Process owners: version the model to include sign-off as a data-aware constraint and decide whether `Manual Override` is a sanctioned routing correction. Compliance: audit a stratified sample of the suspect enhanced reviews plus high-value cases, and stop treating fitness as a risk indicator.

**What must not be claimed yet.** Do not report a real compliance breakdown, do not attribute the drop to reviewer misbehavior, do not declare the missing checks a pure data defect, do not present the optional-sign-off model as a fix since it makes the EUR 50,000 rule unenforceable, and do not quote the supplied percentages until the variant tables and alignment summaries reconcile.

## 8. Model-governance recommendation

1. **Version and date every normative model, and bind it to a policy register.** Each conformance run must cite a model version, its effective date range and the policy clauses it encodes; a policy change such as the 2026-08-01 sign-off rule must open a mandatory model-change ticket before it takes effect, and periods must be evaluated against the model that was in force at the time rather than a single frozen or silently mutated artifact.
2. **Make event semantics a contract between the application and the analytics layer.** Activity labels are an interface, not UI copy. Maintain a signed dictionary mapping every label to a stable transaction code, owner and effective dates; renames must ship a mapping entry in the same release, and the analytics pipeline must key on the stable code so that a screen relabel like `Classify` to `Risk Screen` can never change a single conformance number.
3. **Classify every event type as business or technical at emission, and filter by declared policy.** Introduce an explicit event-class attribute so events like `Queue Routed` are excluded by a versioned, auditable filter rather than by discretionary cleanup. New emitters must not reach the conformance log until classified, and partial emission (530 of 620 cases) must be documented as an intentional rule or fixed.
4. **Record timestamp provenance per event and never silently substitute a clock.** Store the source event time, the source sequence number and the ingestion time as separate fields, declare which is authoritative for ordering, and treat any change of authority as a breaking pipeline change requiring re-validation. Add automated monitors for negative or zero inter-event gaps and for ordering that disagrees with the sequence number.
5. **Express data-dependent obligations as data-aware constraints, never as optional control flow.** Conditional rules such as "sign-off required if amount exceeds EUR 50,000" must be checked against case attributes, through attribute-conditioned rules or stratified models, so that permissiveness is not the price of fitness. Every accepted model must report precision alongside fitness and be back-tested against earlier periods to catch overgeneralization.
6. **Gate every release with a post-release conformance canary and a deviation-class review.** Before and after each UI, gateway or ETL release, run the same conformance job on a fixed canary sample and diff the label inventory, event-per-case count, resource-null rate, start/end activities and deviation-class mix. Any change in these must be explained by a declared change or block the release, and no fitness movement may be escalated to compliance until it has been attributed to model, data or behavior.
