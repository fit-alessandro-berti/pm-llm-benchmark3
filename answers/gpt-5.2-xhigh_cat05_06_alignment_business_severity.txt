## 1. Materiality Framework
Alignment diagnostics answer “how far is the recorded behavior from the control‑flow model under a chosen cost function.” Business materiality answers “what is the exposure if the deviation is real, how irreversible is it, and how reliable is the evidence.” These are related but not interchangeable.

### Severity scale (1–5)
- **1 (Benign / technical noise)**: No control objective is weakened; deviations are telemetry/retries/hidden routing; negligible exposure and no customer or regulatory harm.
- **2 (Minor)**: Primarily modeling/logging/labeling/scope issues; low exposure; reversible; limited audit impact.
- **3 (Moderate)**: Material evidence gaps or control-support weaknesses (auditability, traceability, notification proof) with non‑trivial exposure or recurrence, but no clear “pay without required authorization” event.
- **4 (Major)**: Clear policy‑overlay breach that weakens preventive controls (e.g., ineligible Auto Approve; missing key control evidence on high value/high risk) with meaningful exposure or systemic recurrence.
- **5 (Severe / critical)**: Control failure with high and/or irreversible exposure (especially **payment before required approval/fraud screening**) or major fraud-enablement conditions (high value/high risk/bank-change) coupled with missing/contradictory evidence.

### Materiality dimensions to consider
- **Control objective**: Authorization (Manual Approve), fraud prevention (Fraud Screen, bank-change safeguards), completeness/traceability (Assess Loss evidence), customer/regulatory communication (Notify within 24h).
- **Exposure**: Claim amount, risk rating, and **bank-change recency** (strong fraud indicator even for smaller amounts).
- **Irreversibility & timing**: A payment executed before approval (P3) is materially worse than a missing log event discovered later; recall/recovery probability matters.
- **Customer/regulatory harm**: Late/uncertain notification can create complaint or compliance issues; weak evidence can fail audits even if operations were correct.
- **Recurrence/systemic nature**: A single benign retry vs a recurring connector issue (74 cases) vs a policy‑gate failure (4 cases) imply different remediation priority.
- **Evidence reliability**: Immutable system-of-record events > mutable text links; “missing event” ≠ “activity not performed.”
- **Model scope & granularity**: Denied/withdrawn cases may be out of scope (P6); telemetry (P5) should not inflate materiality.

### Confidence vs severity
- **Severity** is the *impact if the deviation reflects reality* (exposure + control objective + irreversibility + recurrence).
- **Confidence** is the *probability that our interpretation is correct* given evidence quality and plausible alternative explanations (e.g., performed externally but not logged).
High severity can coincide with medium confidence (e.g., missing fraud-screen evidence in a high-value case), and low severity can have high confidence (e.g., duplicate notification retries).

## 3. Repeated Deviation Patterns

### Pattern A — Retry duplicates on notification connector
- **Evidence / frequency**: 74 cases show `L(Notify Claimant)` immediately after a synchronous notify with the **same message ID**; example CL-603.
- **Business risk**: Low direct harm if idempotent, but can inflate “multiple notifications,” confuse customers, distort SLA reporting, and mask true missing notifications.
- **Likely cause**: Connector retries are logged as business events rather than transport retries; lack of idempotency/de-duplication at ingestion.
- **Confidence**: High (same message ID + known retrying connector).
- **Recommended owner**: Integration/platform engineering + data engineering (event ingestion).

### Pattern B — Missing fraud-screen evidence in high-exposure cases
- **Evidence / frequency**: CL-602 has `M(Fraud Screen)` with extremely high exposure and bank change; CL-604 also shows missing fraud screen in the “repaired” model order.
- **Business risk**: Potential fraud enablement and regulatory/audit failure; highest priority because it involves core preventive control objectives (P2).
- **Likely cause**: Fraud screening performed outside core system (verbal/offline), logging omitted, label mismatch, or step bypassed; weak control evidence retention.
- **Confidence**: Medium–High (evidence gap is certain; operational reality must be tested).
- **Recommended owner**: Fraud ops + compliance + core claims product engineering.

### Pattern C — Pay-before-approval (order-of-operations control failure)
- **Evidence / frequency**: CL-604 has independent timestamps (bank acceptance 10:03 vs approval 10:20) consistent with P3 breach.
- **Business risk**: Severe and often irreversible; undermines authorization controls and can violate internal control frameworks and regulatory expectations.
- **Likely cause**: System gating defect, emergency override without audit trail, asynchronous posting/timestamp drift, or split systems with inconsistent clocks.
- **Confidence**: High for “payment preceded recorded approval”; medium for precise root cause.
- **Recommended owner**: Payments engineering + control owner (2nd line) + IT risk.

### Pattern D — External-adjuster assessments not appearing as `Assess Loss` in core log
- **Evidence / frequency**: Nine high-value cases have `M(Assess Loss)` plus external-adjuster documents; CL-606 is an example. Two cases lack stable document-to-case IDs.
- **Business risk**: Moderate: incomplete traceability of assessment; weakens defensibility in disputes and audits; can also distort throughput analytics.
- **Likely cause**: Integration gap (external adjuster system not emitting events), missing stable identifiers, or event mapping not updated for external channel.
- **Confidence**: Medium (documents exist, but correlation quality varies).
- **Recommended owner**: Vendor/integration team + data governance.

### Pattern E — Event-label mapping/taxonomy drift
- **Evidence / frequency**: CL-611 (`Assess Damage` vs `Assess Loss`), with mapping table updated after the case.
- **Business risk**: Low–moderate: primarily measurement/analytics risk; can cause false nonconformance and misdirect remediation.
- **Likely cause**: Uncontrolled naming across UI/procedure/logs; mapping versioning/backfill gaps.
- **Confidence**: High.
- **Recommended owner**: Data governance + process mining/model engineering.

### Pattern F — Out-of-scope (or missing-variant) denied/withdrawn lifecycle cases
- **Evidence / frequency**: Denied/withdrawn cases account for most raw_cost ≥ 4; examples CL-607 (deny) and CL-612 (withdraw). Scope disputed (P6).
- **Business risk**: Low direct financial exposure (no payment), but high “noise” that can drown real control failures; may hide whether notification obligations exist for non-payable outcomes.
- **Likely cause**: Model is intentionally “payable claims” only; event log includes broader lifecycle.
- **Confidence**: Medium (depends on governance decision).
- **Recommended owner**: Process owner + model owner + compliance (scope definition).

### Pattern G — Data-aware auto-approval eligibility failures (zero-cost policy failures)
- **Evidence / frequency**: Four cases in month are fully synchronous Auto Approve despite violating P1 or P2; examples CL-609 and CL-610.
- **Business risk**: Major: preventive control bypass at scale; can increase fraud and erroneous payments, especially when bank-change triggers are ignored.
- **Likely cause**: Eligibility rules not enforced at runtime, missing/late data (bank-change timestamp), risk score not consumed, or override paths misclassified as “auto.”
- **Confidence**: High for policy violation in the described cases; root cause requires testing.
- **Recommended owner**: Claims product engineering + fraud/risk analytics + controls owner.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

### CL-602 (missing fraud-screen evidence on EUR 480k, HIGH risk, bank change)
- **Hypothesis A (control bypass)**: Fraud Screen did not occur; manual approval proceeded without required screening.
- **Hypothesis B (off-system control)**: Fraud check occurred verbally or in a separate tool, but no durable artifact was retained/linked.
- **Hypothesis C (logging/mapping gap)**: Fraud Screen occurred but was logged under another event label or dropped by ETL.
- **Discriminating evidence/tests**:
  - Pull **fraud tooling** audit logs keyed by claimant/claim ID/time window; check for a screening record and outcome.
  - Verify whether “verbal check” has an **attestation** requirement; if so, check compliance records/call logs.
  - Compare ingestion pipelines: are Fraud Screen events present upstream but missing downstream? Look for correlated technical IDs and message loss.

### CL-604 (Pay Claim before approval; bank acceptance 10:03 vs approval 10:20)
- **Hypothesis A (true pay-before-approve)**: Payment was initiated/settled before authorization—gating failure or override.
- **Hypothesis B (timestamp artifact)**: Manual approval happened earlier but was recorded late (async commit, clock skew, batching).
- **Hypothesis C (two-stage payment)**: Pre-authorization/hold at 10:03 followed by final approval later, but logged as “Pay Claim.”
- **Discriminating evidence/tests**:
  - Inspect payment system **initiation vs settlement** timestamps and event types (initiate/authorize/settle).
  - Check approval event’s **creation time vs persisted time** and user session logs.
  - Audit for override flags, emergency workflows, or reconciliation jobs that backfill approvals.

### Pattern A / CL-603 (duplicate Notify Claimant with same message ID)
- **Hypothesis A (harmless retry)**: Connector retries delivery; downstream is idempotent; duplicates are only log artifacts.
- **Hypothesis B (duplicate customer touch)**: Both notifications were delivered (two portal posts/emails).
- **Discriminating evidence/tests**:
  - Check message delivery receipts in the notification/portal system keyed by message ID.
  - Validate idempotency behavior: does the connector use message ID as a de-dup key end-to-end?
  - Compare customer complaint rates and notification counts before/after connector retries.

### CL-608 (core missing Notify; CRM shows portal message at +3h but link is mutable)
- **Hypothesis A (notification occurred; correlation weak)**: Message was sent, but the linkage field can be edited, weakening audit proof.
- **Hypothesis B (notification failed)**: Message exists but is unrelated; linkage was added later to “paper over” a miss.
- **Discriminating evidence/tests**:
  - Require an **immutable portal message ID** with write-once linkage to claim ID; check if such an ID exists retroactively.
  - Use CRM field-level audit history to see if/when the link field was edited.
  - Cross-check recipient identity, timestamps, and content hashes between claim and portal message.

### Pattern D / CL-606 (missing Assess Loss in core; external adjuster report exists)
- **Hypothesis A (externalized step)**: Assessment was done by adjuster; no core event was designed to be emitted.
- **Hypothesis B (integration failure)**: An “assessment completed” callback exists but intermittently fails; documents show completion.
- **Hypothesis C (correlation instability)**: Document exists but cannot be reliably tied to the case (population includes two unstable IDs).
- **Discriminating evidence/tests**:
  - Check adjuster platform API callbacks and error logs for the case time window.
  - Verify presence of stable document identifiers and whether they are immutable and unique per case.
  - Compare the nine cases: do misses cluster by adjuster/vendor/version?

### CL-611 (Assess Damage vs Assess Loss)
- **Hypothesis A (pure taxonomy mismatch)**: Same business step, different label; mapping update confirms.
- **Hypothesis B (additional step)**: “Assess Damage” is a sub-activity or different granularity than “Assess Loss.”
- **Discriminating evidence/tests**:
  - Review procedure/UI definitions and mapping governance records to confirm semantic equivalence.
  - Check whether both labels ever co-occur in the same case; if so, they may be distinct.

### Pattern G / CL-609 & CL-610 (ineligible Auto Approve with raw_cost=0)
- **Hypothesis A (rule not enforced)**: Workflow routes to Auto Approve regardless of P1/P2 data.
- **Hypothesis B (data latency/misread)**: Bank-change or risk score wasn’t available at decision time; later data shows violation.
- **Hypothesis C (override misclassified)**: A human forced approval but system logged it as Auto Approve.
- **Discriminating evidence/tests**:
  - Inspect decision logs for the approval router: inputs (amount, risk, bank-change timestamp), rule outcome, and version.
  - Recompute eligibility from **data snapshots at decision time** (not after-the-fact).
  - Search for override flags, manual user actions, or UI paths that can trigger Auto Approve logging.

### Pattern F / CL-607 & CL-612 (deny/withdraw lifecycle vs payable model scope)
- **Hypothesis A (out of scope by design)**: Payable model is correct; denied/withdrawn should be filtered or modeled separately.
- **Hypothesis B (model incomplete)**: Denial/withdrawal are legitimate variants that should be included (incl. notification obligations).
- **Discriminating evidence/tests**:
  - Governance decision: define the population for compliance KPIs and control monitoring.
  - Validate whether notification SLA (P4) applies to denial/withdrawal and how it is evidenced.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk
Alignment cost is an optimization objective under a technical model; business risk is a function of exposure, control intent, timing, and evidentiary quality. The supplied cases show multiple failure modes of “raw_cost ⇒ severity”:

1. **Uniform move costs ignore control criticality**: Costs are configured as 1 per visible move (L/M). Missing a **Fraud Screen** in CL-602 adds only 1 cost unit, yet it undermines a key preventive control for a EUR 480k, HIGH-risk, bank-change case (P2). Severity is critical; raw_cost is small.
2. **Extra non-business events can inflate cost**: CL-601 has raw_cost=3 solely from UI telemetry (`Open Workbench`, `Autosave`, `Close Workbench`). Under P5 these are not business activities; severity is 1 despite higher cost than CL-602.
3. **Ordering/irreversibility is not priced appropriately**: CL-604’s raw_cost=3 treats a pay-before-approve incident as a few edits. Business impact is severe because payment acceptance before approval (P3) is hard to reverse and indicates a control gate failure.
4. **Policy overlay (data conditions) is invisible to control-flow conformance**: CL-609 and CL-610 have raw_cost=0 because the control-flow model allows Auto Approve. Yet both violate P1/P2 based on amount/risk/bank-change—real business exposure with zero alignment cost.
5. **Hidden moves (tau) affect technical fit but not business meaning**: CL-605 includes a silent `T(tau)` and still has raw_cost=0. Hidden routing can exist without implying any operational deviation; “zero cost” is not “zero risk,” and “tau present” is not “a step happened.”
6. **Missing events are ambiguous without evidence context**: CL-606 has raw_cost=1 for `M(Assess Loss)`, but an external adjuster report suggests the activity occurred; severity depends on traceability and correlation strength, not on the single missing event.
7. **Scope mismatch creates high costs with low risk**: CL-607 and CL-612 show raw_cost=4 because denial/withdrawal variants are not in the payable-claims model (P6). Business risk is low (no payment), but the technical cost is high.
8. **Activity granularity and label semantics distort cost**: CL-611’s raw_cost=2 arises from `Assess Damage` vs `Assess Loss` naming. This is primarily taxonomy/ETL quality, not a control breakdown; cost reflects naming, not harm.
9. **Evidence reliability is outside alignment cost**: CL-608 raw_cost=1 flags missing Notify in core, but the real issue is that the alternative evidence link is mutable—alignment cost cannot represent “audit-proof vs not audit-proof.”

In short: cost functions measure “distance to a behavioral model,” while business severity depends on **what control is weakened, how much is at stake, whether the harm is reversible, and how trustworthy the evidence is**.

def move_sequence(alignment: str):
    parts = [p.strip() for p in alignment.split("→")]
    acts = []
    for p in parts:
        m = re.search(r"\(([^,)]*)", p)
        acts.append(m.group(1).strip() if m else p)
    return acts

def occurs_before(alignment: str, a: str, b: str) -> bool:
    seq = move_sequence(alignment)
    return (a in seq) and (b in seq) and (seq.index(a) < seq.index(b))

def add_move_features(row):
    s = row["alignment"]
    return pd.Series({
        "n_S": len(re.findall(r"\bS\(", s)),
        "n_L": len(re.findall(r"\bL\(", s)),
        "n_M": len(re.findall(r"\bM\(", s)),
        "n_T": len(re.findall(r"\bT\(", s)),
        "uses_auto": bool(re.search(r"S\(Auto Approve", s)),
        "uses_manual": bool(re.search(r"S\(Manual Approve", s)),
        "has_M_fraud": bool(re.search(r"M\(Fraud Screen", s)),
        "has_S_fraud": bool(re.search(r"S\(Fraud Screen", s)),
        "has_M_assess": bool(re.search(r"M\(Assess Loss", s)),
        "has_M_notify": bool(re.search(r"M\(Notify Claimant", s)),
        "has_L_notify": bool(re.search(r"L\(Notify Claimant", s)),
        "has_L_telemetry": bool(re.search(r"L\((Open Workbench|Autosave|Close Workbench)", s)),
        "pay_before_manual": occurs_before(s, "Pay Claim", "Manual Approve"),
        "pay_before_auto": occurs_before(s, "Pay Claim", "Auto Approve"),
        "pay_before_fraud": occurs_before(s, "Pay Claim", "Fraud Screen"),
        "scope_variant": bool(re.search(r"(Deny Claim|Withdraw Claim)", s)),
    })
