## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

I use the following rules, classified by how much weight they carry when they conflict with each other.

**Hard invariants** (a repair that violates these is discarded outright):

- H1. Type discipline: an event may only be linked to object types the activity can legitimately touch. `Pack Item` touches Item and Package, not Order; `Record Payment` touches Payment and Invoice, not Order.
- H2. No invention: no new event, object, type or attribute. If a relation would need a missing object to make sense, it is left unresolved.
- H3. Temporal admissibility: an object may not participate in an event preceding its own creation.
- H4. O2O directionality: `DEL-X carries PK-7` is stored source→target with that qualifier; the reversed pair is never emitted as an independent fact.
- H5. No duplicate (event, object) pairs or (source, qualifier, target) triples.

**Strong defaults** (normally true, overridable by explicit contrary evidence):

- S1. `Create Order` → one Order; `Add Item` → one Order plus one Item. Both already hold, so no repair is warranted there.
- S2. `Pack Item` → one Item plus one Package. ev08 has an Item but no Package, and the scanner-outage note names a known missing-value mechanism rather than a new sub-process.
- S3. `Dispatch Package` → one Package plus one Delivery. ev10 has a Package but no Delivery.
- S4. `Record Payment` → exactly one Payment; normally one Invoice, but unapplied cash is an accepted real state.
- S5. A durable E2O addition should be mirrored by the corresponding O2O edge and vice versa — but the mirror is *the same* evidence expressed twice, never a second vote.

**Weak heuristics** (used to rank candidates, never to close a case alone):

- W1. Identifier similarity: `scanner_ref=PK8-2` resembles `PK8-1` (which appears on the PK-8 event) and the package id `PK-8`; `external_ref=N-421?` resembles `carrier_ref=N-421` on DEL-Y; `N-42*` on ev11 is a wildcard that matches both N-420 and N-421 and therefore discriminates nothing.
- W2. Amount coherence: 130 + 100 = 230 and 100 + 130 = 230. Suggestive, but rule 6 is explicit that fees, discounts and taxes break arithmetic; a total matching a sum raises confidence, it does not demonstrate that a billing or settlement relation exists.
- W3. Customer coherence: ORD-A, ORD-B, INV-44 and ev11's portal field all carry C17. This makes cross-order consolidation possible but says nothing about which consolidation happened.
- W4. Route coherence: both packages hint NORTH and both deliveries are NORTH, which *permits* any package-to-delivery assignment and therefore selects none.
- W5. Timestamp proximity: ev09 and ev10 share 10:00 exactly, equally compatible with two parallel single-package dispatches and with one bulk dispatch.
- W6. Weight: PK-7 = 3.1 with one known item, PK-8 = 2.9 with one or two. Packaging mass for a `GLASS-1` item plausibly dominates, so weight neither confirms nor refutes a second item in PK-8.

**Evidence against repairs** (facts I treat as active counter-evidence):

- A1. The literal `?` on ev10's `external_ref=N-421?` is a recorded uncertainty marker. It weakens exactly the identifier match that would otherwise settle ev10, so I cap ev10→DEL-Y well below certainty.
- A2. The `*` in ev11's `N-42*` is a truncation, and a truncation matching two carrier refs is evidence *against* unique assignment, not for it.
- A3. DEL-Y and PK-8 are under-connected. A lonely object is not evidence that a particular edge was lost; it is precisely the bait an eager repairer takes.
- A4. PAY-1 = 100 equals ORD-B's declared value and PAY-2 = 130 equals ORD-A's, while PAY-1 already settles INV-44. Two incompatible readings — one consolidated invoice paid in two instalments, or per-order settlement — fit the same numbers, so the amounts encode ambiguity rather than confirmation.

**Combination policy.** I start from cardinality gaps (S2–S4), which say *where* something is missing. Only then does identifier similarity rank candidates, discounted by explicit dirtiness markers (A1, A2). Time, route and customer act as filters that can eliminate candidates but never decide. Existing O2O structure supplies prior shape — PK-7/DEL-X shows what a complete shipment looks like — without assuming symmetry across objects. Where two candidates survive filtering with comparable support, the relation stays unresolved. Qualifier names for E2O additions (`package`, `delivery`, `order`, `invoice`) are my convention, since the source E2O table carries none.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A — conservative / minimum change.** Repair only the two rows where a documented failure mode explains a cardinality gap and where a single candidate clearly leads.

- *ev08 and PK-8*: add ev08→PK-8 and `PK-8 packs IT-B1` (R1, R2). This is the one place where the missing value has a named cause (scanner outage reconciled later) and the identifier series points at one package.
- *ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y*: add ev10→DEL-Y and `DEL-Y carries PK-8` (R3, R4). The `?` keeps confidence at roughly two-thirds, but DEL-Y is the only object whose carrier_ref matches the recorded string.
- *ev11 and the delivery objects*: no change. The wildcard matches both carrier refs, so I leave `Confirm Delivery` attached to ORD-A alone and record R6/R7/R8 as unresolved.
- *INV-44 and ORD-B*: no change. Rule 6 forbids treating the sum as decisive, and the invoice may legitimately bill ORD-A plus charges.
- *PAY-2 and INV-44*: no change; PAY-2 stays unapplied cash.

Lifecycle coherence: ORD-A runs create → add items → invoice → (confirm delivery, order-side) and is fully coherent. Both packages get pack-then-dispatch, and both deliveries now exist as dispatched shipments. The deliberate incoherences are visible and honest: ORD-B stops after `Add Item`, no delivery is ever confirmed at delivery level, and PAY-2 is a floating receipt. That is the intended shape of a conservative bundle — the gaps remain legible as gaps rather than being smoothed away.

**Bundle B — consolidated customer interpretation.** C17 placed two orders, they were packed across two packages, dispatched on one NORTH round with two carrier legs, acknowledged once in the portal, billed on one consolidated invoice, and settled in two instalments.

- *ev08 and PK-8*: as in Bundle A (R1, R2). PK-8 is a mixed-order package holding IT-A2 and IT-B1.
- *ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y*: as in Bundle A (R3, R4); two legs, one per package.
- *ev11 and the delivery objects*: add ev11→DEL-X, ev11→DEL-Y and ev11→ORD-B (R6, R7, R8) as a joint acknowledgement. I do not pick one delivery: the truncated `N-42*` is precisely the pattern a portal produces when it groups a round, and choosing DEL-X alone would rely on the wildcard's first match, which is an artefact of ordering rather than evidence. Under this bundle the multi-delivery reading is *stronger* than either single-delivery reading because it does not require discarding half of the wildcard's extension.
- *INV-44 and ORD-B*: add R9, R10. The 230 total is consistent with billing both orders; this is treated as corroboration of an already plausible consolidation story (same customer, same day, joint shipment), not as arithmetic proof.
- *PAY-2 and INV-44*: add R11, R12 — two instalments against one invoice.

Lifecycle coherence: every object type closes. ORD-B becomes create → add item → confirm delivery → issue invoice; INV-44 becomes issued then settled twice; both deliveries become dispatched then confirmed. The bundle is internally consistent, but it leans on one shared assumption — that this is a single consolidated customer episode — so its edges are correlated: if the consolidation reading is wrong, roughly six added relations fail together, not one.

**Bundle C — bulk-dispatch variant.** Same handling of ev08/PK-8 as Bundle A. For ev10 it takes R5 instead of R3/R4: the identical 10:00 timestamps and rule 4's bulk-dispatch allowance are read as one dispatch action, `external_ref=N-421?` is treated as a mis-keyed reference, and `DEL-X carries PK-8` is added. ev11 then resolves cleanly to DEL-X alone, ORD-B is billed as in Bundle B, and PAY-2 settles INV-44. Lifecycle check: this yields tidy Order, Invoice and Payment lifecycles but leaves DEL-Y as a complete orphan — an object with no event and no O2O edge. I regard that as the bundle's fatal weakness: it requires the recorded string `N-421` to be a coincidence that exactly names an otherwise unexplained object. I keep Bundle C on the table only as a documented rival, not as a recommendation.

**Selected bundle: A.**

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

**Order.** ORD-A: create (ev01) → add items (ev03, ev04) → confirm delivery (ev11) → issue invoice (ev12). Plausible, though `Confirm Delivery` preceding `Issue Invoice` should be noted as a deliver-then-bill policy rather than an anomaly. ORD-B: create (ev02) → add item (ev05), then nothing. Under Bundle A, ORD-B is an intentionally truncated lifecycle, not an orphan: it has events and an O2O edge to IT-B1. Both order-to-item edges have genuinely independent support (E2O on ev03–ev05 *and* pre-existing O2O rows in the source), which is the strongest evidence class in this log.

**Item.** All three items have add-then-pack lifecycles after R1. IT-B1's `Pack Item` participation was already present in the source E2O; only its package was missing, so the item lifecycle was never actually broken. Cardinality is clean: one order and one package per item.

**Package.** PK-7: pack (ev06) → dispatch (ev09), one item, one delivery. PK-8: pack (ev07), pack (ev08) → dispatch (ev10), two items, one delivery. The two-item PK-8 is a legitimate one-to-many, but it is the first cross-order structure in the log — PK-8 now bridges ORD-A and ORD-B — so it deserves a flag rather than silent acceptance.

**Delivery.** DEL-X: dispatch (ev09), carries PK-7 — supported independently by both E2O and O2O in the original data, the only shipment edge with that status. DEL-Y: dispatch (ev10), carries PK-8 — supported by a single piece of evidence duplicated across the two representations. Neither delivery has a delivery-level confirmation event in Bundle A, so both lifecycles end at dispatch. That is an accepted incompleteness, not a defect to repair.

**Invoice.** INV-44: issued (ev12), bills ORD-A, settled once by PAY-1 (ev13, plus O2O). Under Bundle A the invoice is under-settled at 100 of 230 and bills one order whose declared value is 130. This is visibly odd, and I prefer visible oddness to a repair that closes it on arithmetic alone.

**Payment.** PAY-1 is fully connected. PAY-2 participates in ev14 but has no invoice — an orphan *relation*, not an orphan object. There is no orphan event in Bundle A: every event retains at least one object.

**Circular reasoning.** Three places invite it, and I mark them explicitly. First, R2 exists only because R1 was assumed; if I later cited `PK-8 packs IT-B1` as O2O confirmation of ev08→PK-8, that would be one inference counted twice. R4 stands in exactly the same relation to R3. Second, in Bundle B, the ORD-B billing edge (R9/R10) and the ORD-B delivery edge (R8) are both fed by the assumption that this is one consolidated C17 episode; using either to support the other is circular, and their true joint probability is well below the product of their stated confidences suggests. Third, the 230 total is used twice in Bundle B — once to attach ORD-B to the invoice and once to attach PAY-2 to it — which are two conclusions from one number, and I do not treat the second as reinforced by the first.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**OC-DFG.** Unrepaired, the Package type shows `Pack Item → Dispatch Package` for both PK-7 (ev06→ev09) and PK-8 (ev07→ev10). Bundle A adds a `Pack Item → Pack Item` self-loop on the Package type (ev07 → ev08 within PK-8), lengthening PK-8's path to pack, pack, dispatch. On the Delivery type, the unrepaired log has a single `Dispatch Package` node touched by DEL-X; Bundle A doubles its object frequency without adding an edge, since a one-event lifecycle yields no directly-follows arc. Bundle B additionally creates the Delivery-type edge `Dispatch Package → Confirm Delivery` (10:00 → 14:00) for both deliveries, and on the Order type creates `Add Item → Confirm Delivery` and `Confirm Delivery → Issue Invoice` for ORD-B — a fragment that appears as a jump, because orders never participate in packing or dispatch.

**OTG.** Unrepaired object-type edges: Order–Item, Item–Package, Package–Delivery (PK-7/DEL-X only), Order–Invoice, Invoice–Payment. Bundle A adds no new *type* pair; it thickens Item–Package and Package–Delivery and makes the Item–Package edge span two orders. Bundle B adds a genuinely new Order–Delivery type edge via ev11. Anyone reading the OTG as a data model would then conclude that orders relate directly to deliveries — a structural claim resting on one truncated portal reference.

**ET–OT graph.** Unrepaired, `Dispatch Package` connects to Delivery in only one of its two events, and `Record Payment` connects to Invoice in only one of two — both look like partial-coverage anomalies. Bundle A repairs the first (Dispatch Package → Delivery becomes 2/2) and deliberately leaves the second at 1/2, which is exactly the diagnostic signal a data steward should still see. Bundle B creates the `Confirm Delivery` ↔ Delivery cell, which is absent in both the unrepaired log and Bundle A, and completes `Record Payment` ↔ Invoice.

**Flattenings.** Order: unrepaired and Bundle A give ORD-A a five-event case (ev01, ev03, ev04, ev11, ev12) and ORD-B a two-event case; Bundle B gives ORD-B a four-event case and duplicates ev11 and ev12 across both order cases. Package: Bundle A turns PK-8's case from two events into three, and that case now spans items from two orders, so per-order KPIs derived from a package flattening become ambiguous. Delivery: unrepaired yields one case (DEL-X); Bundle A yields two single-event cases; Bundle B yields two two-event cases with ev11 duplicated into both — one real acknowledgement becoming two observed `Confirm Delivery` occurrences. Invoice: unchanged in Bundle A; in Bundle B INV-44 gains a second `Record Payment`, producing a `Record Payment → Record Payment` loop. Payment: PAY-2 stays a one-event case under both bundles.

**Activity frequencies.** `Confirm Delivery` stays at one event under every bundle, but its unique-object count rises from one (ORD-A) to four in Bundle B (ORD-A, ORD-B, DEL-X, DEL-Y), and its total-object count rises correspondingly. `Pack Item` keeps three events; its unique Package count rises from two to two but its total object count rises from five to six under Bundle A. `Dispatch Package` keeps two events while unique Delivery objects go from one to two. Reporting by events versus by objects therefore diverges sharply after repair — a dashboard counting "deliveries confirmed" by object would show a fourfold jump in Bundle B from a single unchanged event.

**Equivalent-execution clustering around Order.** Unrepaired and under Bundle A, ORD-A and ORD-B fall into clearly different execution classes: ORD-A's connected component reaches items, packages, deliveries, the invoice and PAY-1, while ORD-B's component reaches only IT-B1 — Bundle A's PK-8 edges do quietly connect the two components through the shared package, which merges them into one large process execution under a connected-component extraction even though no order-level edge was added. That side effect is easy to miss and worth stating. Under Bundle B the two orders converge toward the same variant (create, add item(s), confirm, invoice) and would likely cluster together, differing only in item multiplicity, which is precisely the kind of artificial homogeneity a repair can manufacture.

import pandas as pd

events_orig, objects_orig = events.copy(), objects.copy()
relations_orig, o2o_orig = relations.copy(), o2o.copy()

           .merge(events[["ocel:eid", "ocel:timestamp", "ocel:activity"]], on="ocel:eid", how="left")
           .merge(objects[["ocel:oid", "ocel:type"]], on="ocel:oid", how="left"))

print("reversed-pair collisions:", len(rev[rev["ocel:qualifier"] == rev["ocel:qualifier_r"]]))

# cardinality re-check after repair
card = (relations_rep.merge(events[["ocel:eid", "ocel:activity"]], on="ocel:eid", how="left",

print(card[card["n"] > 1])
print("events with no object:", set(events["ocel:eid"]) - set(relations_rep["ocel:eid"]))

# older/newer releases may not accept o2o in the constructor; then assign afterwards:

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Attaching orders to packing and dispatch events.** Adding ORD-A to ev06/ev07 or ORD-B to ev08/ev10 makes Order lifecycles look continuous, but it fabricates the directly-follows edges `Add Item → Pack Item` and `Pack Item → Dispatch Package` on the Order type. Every subsequent order-level throughput and bottleneck analysis would then measure a process step that the source system never recorded against orders.

2. **Resolving ev11 to a single delivery.** Picking DEL-X because `N-42*` happens to sort to N-420 first converts a truncation artefact into a fact. The result is a Delivery-type edge `Dispatch Package → Confirm Delivery` for DEL-X and a permanently unconfirmed DEL-Y, which will read as a delivery failure or SLA breach that never occurred.

3. **Attaching ev11 to both deliveries and both orders at once.** The opposite excess duplicates one real acknowledgement into up to four flattened occurrences. Confirmation rates measured per object jump while the underlying event count is unchanged, and per-delivery cycle times are computed from a shared timestamp that may belong to only one of them.

4. **Closing the invoice on arithmetic.** Booking ev12→ORD-B and ev14→INV-44 because 130 + 100 and 100 + 130 both reach 230 hides the alternative that the invoice bills ORD-A plus 100 of fees or tax while PAY-1 is misapplied. If wrong, ORD-B is reported as billed and paid, disappears from receivables ageing, and a genuine unapplied-cash exception vanishes from the log — the exact anomaly the data was probably meant to surface.

5. **Adding both `DEL-X carries PK-8` and `DEL-Y carries PK-8` to hedge.** Hedging at the edge level rather than the bundle level produces a many-to-many shipment structure that no carrier operation supports. The OTG then shows Package–Delivery as many-to-many, and package-level cost or weight allocation double-counts PK-8 across two legs.

6. **Repairing ORD-B's lifecycle simply because it looks truncated.** A same-day order that has been created and picked but not yet shipped or billed at 16:00 is entirely normal. Completing it turns a legitimately in-progress case into a closed one and destroys the ability to measure work in progress; it also removes the only visible evidence that PK-8 mixes two orders, since the anomaly is what draws attention to the mixed package.

7. **Trusting O2O enrichment on the repaired log.** Running an enrichment routine after the additions returns the assumed edges as if they had been discovered from co-occurrence, giving a false impression that E2O and O2O evidence agree independently. In this dataset only the ORD-A/ORD-B-to-item edges and the PK-7/DEL-X edge have that status in the source data; every edge I add has exactly one underlying reason expressed in two places.
