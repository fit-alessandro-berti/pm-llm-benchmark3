The pilot separates mechanism failure from implementation failure by measuring actual task concurrency versus scheduled concurrency. If the treatment arm shows high scheduled overlap but low actual overlap, implementation failure (IT lock, training, routing) is the cause. If actual overlap is high but waiting/cycle time does not improve, mechanism failure (downstream bottleneck, dependency violation) is identified. Stratified analysis isolates the digital subgroup where the causal path is theoretically intact, while the physical subgroup tests whether conditional parallelization introduces administrative drag. Negative controls (non-verification cycle time components) verify that observed changes are not system-wide artifacts.

## 9. Recommendation

`conditional go`

Transfer the parallelization mechanism only to the digital-document subgroup (38% of volume) where statutory and IT constraints do not block execution. Do not copy Site A’s blanket rollout, cross-training model, or reliance on managerial huddles as primary levers. Begin with digital claims that bypass legal review to isolate the core concurrency effect without downstream flooding. Expected benefits include a 4–6 hour median cycle time reduction and a 10–15% drop in pre-assessment waiting for the eligible segment. Primary risks are bottleneck shifting to assessment and legal review, IT lock enforcement causing artificial sequentialization, and rework inflation from premature document handling. Expansion should be triggered only if the pilot demonstrates actual overlap >60%, stable rework (<18%), and p90 degradation <5 hours. Modification is warranted if assessment WIP consistently exceeds limits, requiring capacity scaling or triage rules. Abandonment is indicated if IT locks prevent concurrent task states, overlap metrics rely on back-entry, or appeal rates rise above 5.5%. The intervention must be treated as a conditional process redesign, not a direct copy, with strict guardrails on statutory prerequisites and downstream capacity.
