## 7. Final synthesis

The Early Escalation intervention (K-104) appears most robust. It respects the four-eyes rule and compliance mandates while smoothing the critical Compliance Review bottleneck. It mitigates risk by preparing evidence early without altering the final decision policy. Conversely, the Parallel Verification intervention (K-411) is most dangerous despite potential speed gains. By bypassing system locks, it risks audit failures and creates assessment queue congestion that displaces other low-risk cases.

A local case counterfactual is least transportable to a system-wide policy in K-337. Suppressing a rework loop for a specific high-risk case assumes the evidence is sufficient, which may not hold for the general population, increasing appeal risk and compliance defensibility issues.

Additional event data on C1 queue depth during high-risk windows and external supplier response times are most valuable. Understanding the correlation between supplier slowness and internal rework frequency would clarify if K-337's speed comes at a quality cost. Finally, real-time resource utilization logs are needed to validate if parallelization truly reduces total lead time or merely shifts congestion upstream to Triage and downstream to Assessment.

To ensure safety, pilot programs must track C1 wait times and decision appeal rates. Without this data, the assumption that 'faster is better' is false. The system is constrained by C1 capacity, not document processing. Therefore, optimizing around C1 is more valuable than optimizing around document resources.
