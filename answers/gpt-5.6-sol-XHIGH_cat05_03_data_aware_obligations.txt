## 1. Temporal Data Semantics

I use a per-case, event-time state reconstruction with the following semantics:

- **Event order:** events are ordered lexicographically by  
  \[
  (\texttt{time:timestamp},\ \texttt{event\_index})
  \]
  Thus, for equal timestamps, lower `event_index` occurs first.
- **Blank cells:** a blank means **no update**. It does not erase the prior value and is not interpreted as a low-risk/default value.
- **State at an event:** for each changing attribute—`amount`, `risk_level`, `jurisdiction`, `customer_type`, and relevant exemption reference—the state is the most recent nonblank value at or before that event. An update on the event itself is effective for evaluating that event.
- **No backward filling:** a value first observed after release is not copied backward. In particular, TX-305’s settlement amount is not treated as the release amount.
- **Final case attributes:** a final value is not assumed to have governed prior events. For TX-311, DE is the release-time jurisdiction, but the Enhanced_Review at the same timestamp occurred first and therefore saw US.
- **Material changes:**
  - Changes to jurisdiction, risk, or customer type are material when they actually change the prior state.
  - An amount increase greater than 20% relative to the amount at the latest Standard_Review or Enhanced_Review invalidates that review for amount-dependent obligations.
  - An amount increase crossing an activation or scope boundary, such as EUR 100,000, is also trigger-relevant.
  - A decrease does not by itself invalidate a review.
  - If the comparison amount is unknown, materiality is not safely decidable.
  - The initial transaction state is the baseline that a later review must follow.
- **Strict ordering:** “before” and “after” are strict. For example, R2 requires:
  \[
  Enhanced\_Review \prec Compliance\_Approve \prec Release\_Funds
  \]
- **Event time:** when the business event occurred in the log.
- **Effective time:** when a business fact legally or operationally applies. An update is effective at event time unless separate evidence establishes another effective time.
- **Record time:** when a fact was inserted into a database. A late record does not necessarily mean the underlying approval was late.
- **Evidence time:** when independent evidence was created or cryptographically fixed, such as a qualified signature timestamp or certificate creation time. Evidence time is used to assess whether approval existed before release.
- **Same-time external evidence:** if approval evidence and release have the same timestamp but no reliable sequence, “already valid” is not conclusively established.

This produces three-valued findings where necessary: true, false, or unknown/indeterminate.

Let \(e_1 \prec e_2\) mean:

\[
t(e_1)<t(e_2)
\quad\lor\quad
(t(e_1)=t(e_2)\land index(e_1)<index(e_2)).
\]

Let \(S(c,a,e)\) be the value of attribute \(a\) from the greatest event \(u\preceq e\) in case \(c\) having a nonblank update for \(a\). If no such event exists, \(S\) is unknown.

**R1**

```text
R1Trigger(c,r) :=
    Release(r)
    AND S(c,jurisdiction,r) != "DE"
    AND S(c,amount,r) >= 100000.

ValidERForR1(c,q,r) :=
    Enhanced_Review(q)
    AND q  r
    AND every known material change m relevant to the released state satisfies m  q
    AND NOT AmountInvalid(q,r).

R1Satisfied(c,r) :=
    NOT R1Trigger(c,r)
    OR exists q: ValidERForR1(c,q,r)
    OR exists x: ValidExemption(x,"R1",c,r).
```

A change on the release event is included in the release state; a prior review cannot be “after” that change.

**R2**

```text
R2Trigger(c,r) :=
    Release(r)
    AND (S(c,risk_level,r) = "HIGH"
         OR S(c,customer_type,r) = "PEP").

R2Satisfied(c,r) :=
    NOT R2Trigger(c,r)
    OR exists q,a:
         Enhanced_Review(q)
         AND Compliance_Approve(a)
         AND RelevantToCurrentRiskAndCustomer(q,r)
         AND q  a  r
         AND ApprovalCoversEnhancedPacket(a,q).
```

A Compliance_Approve known to cover only a standard packet does not satisfy `ApprovalCoversEnhancedPacket`.

**R3**

```text
R3Trigger(c,r,u) :=
    Release(r)
    AND UpdateList(u, S(c,jurisdiction,r))
    AND exists s:
          Sanctions_Screen(s)
          AND s  u  r
          AND no later screen exists between s and u.

R3Satisfied(c,r,u) :=
    NOT R3Trigger(c,r,u)
    OR exists s2:
         Sanctions_Screen(s2)
         AND u  s2  r
         AND ScreenUsedApplicableJurisdictionAndListVersion(s2,u).
```

**R4**

```text
LatestReviewBefore(c,u,q) :=
    q is the greatest Standard_Review or Enhanced_Review with q  u.

AmountInvalid(q,u) :=
    Known(S(c,amount,q))
    AND Known(S(c,amount,u))
    AND S(c,amount,u) > 1.20 * S(c,amount,q).

UnknownAmountInvalidity(q,u) :=
    amount at q is unknown
    OR an amount transition after q has an unknown comparison base.

A later qualifying review after u resets the comparison baseline.
A decrease alone does not satisfy AmountInvalid.
```

**R5**

```text
ValidExemption(x,rule,c,r) :=
    Waives(x,rule)
    AND ApprovedBy(x,"Head_Compliance")
    AND EffectiveFrom(x) <= time(r)
    AND time(r) <= Expires(x)
    AND ScopeJurisdiction(x) = S(c,jurisdiction,r)
    AND ScopeCustomerType(x) = S(c,customer_type,r)
    AND AmountWithinScope(x,S(c,amount,r))
    AND EvidenceSupportsApprovalExistingBefore(x,r)
    AND NOT RevokedBefore(x,r).
```

`RecordTime(x) > time(r)` does not itself make the exemption invalid. Conversely, a displayed pre-release approval time does not itself prove approval existed then.

**R6**

```text
If a trigger formula is neither provably true nor provably false,
or a required review/exemption scope cannot be validated because of
decisive unknown evidence, return INDETERMINATE unless independent
evidence resolves the issue.
```

For conjunctions and disjunctions, known values can resolve the trigger. For example, jurisdiction DE makes R1 false even if amount is unknown; customer type PEP makes R2 true even if risk is unknown.

**R7**

```text
State uses only updates u with u  evaluated event e.
No update u with e  u may affect S(c,a,e).
Equal timestamps are ordered by event_index.
An explicitly established effective time may replace event time for
that fact, but record time alone does not.
```

## 4. Ambiguities and Alternative Readings

- **TX-302**
  - The late 11:00 database insertion does not make EX-77 post-hoc because the qualified signature independently fixes approval at 09:05, before the 09:40 release.
  - If the 09:20 `Enhanced_Review` event were only a start event rather than completion, EX-77 would still justify release.
  - If both the signature and the review-completion evidence were invalidated, the case would become NONCOMPLIANT.
  - A classification scheme that treats an actually performed review as primary could use COMPLIANT rather than JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.

- **TX-305**
  - An immutable release payload showing amount below EUR 100,000 would establish that R1 was not triggered and would support COMPLIANT.
  - A payload showing EUR 150,000, plus evidence that the 11:15 review covered that amount and no material change followed, would also support COMPLIANT.
  - A payload or update history showing that the amount became EUR 150,000 after the 11:15 review, especially through a greater-than-20% increase, would support NONCOMPLIANT.
  - The next-day settlement amount alone cannot resolve these alternatives because it is reliable only at settlement time and is not immutably linked to the release payload.

- **TX-310**
  - Independent pre-release evidence—such as a Head_Compliance call recording, signed approval ledger, trusted timestamp, or contemporaneous workflow audit entry—would support JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.
  - Evidence disproving the alleged oral approval, or a policy requiring a qualified signature rather than oral approval, would make the case NONCOMPLIANT.
  - Accepting the requester’s testimony under a low evidentiary threshold could support an exception, but the requester’s conflict of interest materially lowers confidence.
  - The 09:20 Standard_Review cannot substitute for an Enhanced_Review.

- **TX-311**
  - `event_index=3` means the Enhanced_Review occurred before the DE update at `event_index=4`. Therefore the review saw US, not DE. Copying the final DE state backward would be temporally wrong.
  - Under the stated policy, this ordering does not create a violation because R1 is activated at release, when jurisdiction is DE.
  - Merely reversing the two same-time indices would change the state seen by the review but not the release-time R1 result: release would still be DE.
  - If separate effective-time evidence showed that the DE change was not effective until after release, the release jurisdiction would remain US. The logged Enhanced_Review would still appear structurally sufficient because it followed the EUR 115,000 update; evidence that the review was incomplete or did not cover EUR 115,000 would then be needed to make the result NONCOMPLIANT.
  - A materially different “latched obligation” interpretation—under which entering a foreign high-value state creates an obligation that survives a later change to DE, and every later jurisdiction change invalidates the review—could produce NONCOMPLIANT. That reading conflicts with R1’s explicit “At Release_Funds” trigger and R4’s selective invalidation approach.

- **TX-308**
  - R1 is active because “at least EUR 100,000” includes exactly EUR 100,000.
  - A broad reading that every amount change must be followed by a new review would make the case NONCOMPLIANT, but it conflicts with R4’s express statement that a decrease does not by itself invalidate a review.

- **TX-306**
  - The result assumes an increase of 4.55% does not require approval refresh. If a separate policy required approval after every amount update, the case could fail, but that requirement is not present in R1–R7.
  - A review-to-approval linkage showing that the approval covered a different packet would change the R2 conclusion.

- **TX-309**
  - The second increase is 22.88% relative to the EUR 118,000 amount at the latest Enhanced_Review. Comparing only to the initial amount would miss the review-reset semantics.
  - Evidence that the Enhanced_Review actually completed after the EUR 145,000 update, contrary to the logged ordering, could change the result to COMPLIANT.

    case_id=CASE,

)

df = (
    formatted_df
    .sort_values([CASE, TIME, "event_index"], kind="mergesort")

# Forward fill only within a case and only from past to future.
# No bfill is used, so future values cannot leak backward.

#
# Amount materiality includes:
#   * >20% relative to the immediately prior amount;
#   * >20% relative to the latest review amount;
#   * crossing from below 100,000 to at least 100,000.
# Decreases are not marked as invalidating.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
df["_material_known"] = False
df["_material_reason"] = ""
df["_uncertain_amount_change"] = False
df["_r2_state_change"] = False

    old = df.at[row_index, "_material_reason"]
    reasons = [] if not old else old.split("|")
    if reason not in reasons:
        reasons.append(reason)
    df.at[row_index, "_material_reason"] = "|".join(reasons)
    df.at[row_index, "_material_known"] = True

        "amount": np.nan,
        "risk_level": np.nan,
        "jurisdiction": np.nan,
        "customer_type": np.nan,
        "exemption_id": np.nan,
    }
    latest_review_seen = False
    latest_review_amount = np.nan

        row = df.loc[i]
        activity = row[ACT]

        if activity == "Create_Transaction":
            add_material_reason(i, "initial_baseline")
            df.at[i, "_r2_state_change"] = True

        if activity != "Create_Transaction":

                if pd.isna(old):
                    # Direction and percentage cannot be proved.
                    df.at[i, "_uncertain_amount_change"] = True
                else:
                    if new > old:
                        if old == 0 or new > 1.20 * old:
                            add_material_reason(i, "amount_gt20_from_previous")
                        if old < 100000 <= new:
                            add_material_reason(i, "amount_crossed_100k")

                if latest_review_seen:
                    if pd.isna(latest_review_amount):
                        df.at[i, "_uncertain_amount_change"] = True
                    elif new > 1.20 * latest_review_amount:
                        add_material_reason(i, "amount_gt20_from_latest_review")

        # Apply input updates to the previous-state tracker.
        for col in state_cols:
            if bool(row[f"_upd_{col}"]):
                prev[col] = row[f"_input_{col}"]

        # A review resets the R4 comparison baseline to state at that review.
        if activity in {"Standard_Review", "Enhanced_Review"}:
            latest_review_seen = True
            latest_review_amount = df.at[i, "amount"]

def last_activity(frame, activities):
    if isinstance(activities, str):
        activities = {activities}
    else:
        activities = set(activities)
    x = frame[frame[ACT].isin(activities)]
    return None if x.empty else x.iloc[-1]

def event_ref(row):
    if row is None:
        return None
    return f"{row[TIME].isoformat()}#{int(row['event_index'])}"

def tri_r1_trigger(amount, jurisdiction):
    # Conjunction can be disproved by either known false operand.
    if pd.notna(jurisdiction) and jurisdiction == "DE":
        return False
    if pd.notna(amount) and amount < 100000:
        return False
    if pd.notna(amount) and pd.notna(jurisdiction):
        return jurisdiction != "DE" and amount >= 100000
    return None

def tri_r2_trigger(risk, customer_type):
    # Disjunction is true if either known operand is true.
    if risk == "HIGH" or customer_type == "PEP":
        return True
    if pd.notna(risk) and pd.notna(customer_type):
        return False
    return None

## 6. Why Control-Flow-Only Conformance Is Insufficient

1. **Final-state leakage:** a trace model does not prevent an implementation from attaching TX-305’s later EUR 150,000 settlement amount to the earlier release or DE from TX-311 to the earlier Enhanced_Review.

2. **Conditional activation:** R1 applies only when release-time jurisdiction is not DE and amount is at least EUR 100,000. R2 depends on HIGH or PEP. A fixed activity sequence cannot faithfully express these data predicates.

3. **Material changes after review:** TX-309 contains an Enhanced_Review before release, but the later 22.88% increase invalidates it. A simple “Enhanced before Release” constraint incorrectly passes the case.

4. **Post-hoc versus late-recorded exemptions:** both EX-77 and EX-88 are recorded after release. Control flow sees the same `Release  Record_Exemption` pattern, but EX-77 has strong pre-release signature evidence while EX-88 has conflicting post-release certificate evidence.

5. **Missing data and three-valued logic:** control flow cannot distinguish “amount below threshold” from “amount unknown.” TX-305 must not be treated as low value merely because its event sequence contains an Enhanced_Review.

6. **Same-timestamp ordering:** PM4Py or a process model ordered only by timestamp may treat TX-311’s Enhanced_Review and jurisdiction update as simultaneous or reorder them. `event_index` establishes that the review saw US and the update to DE occurred afterward.

7. **Review type and review content:** a Standard_Review is not an Enhanced_Review. Likewise, an approval event may cover a standard packet rather than an enhanced packet, as in TX-307. Activity presence alone does not establish semantic adequacy.

8. **Quantitative comparisons:** R4 requires comparing the amount at the latest review with later amounts and applying a strict greater-than-20% test. Ordinary control-flow conformance does not retain the review-specific numeric baseline.

9. **Scope matching:** exemption validity depends on jurisdiction, customer type, amount ceiling, waived rule, effective date, expiry, approver, and evidence quality. The presence of an exemption event or ID does not prove any of those conditions.

10. **External list state:** R3 depends on the jurisdiction named in a sanctions-list update and the list version used by a later screen. The sequence of generic `Sanctions_List_Update` and `Sanctions_Screen` labels is insufficient without payload matching.

11. **Effective time versus event/record time:** an event trace normally exposes one timestamp. Exemptions, corrections, and list updates may have separate business-effective, record, and signature times, each with a different compliance role.

12. **Causality and linkage:** `Compliance_Approve` must approve the relevant Enhanced_Review. Mere temporal order does not prove that the approval and review concern the same packet, state snapshot, or rule version.

## 7. Recommended Logging and Control Improvements

- **Immutable release payloads**
  - Record an immutable `release_payload_id`, hash, amount, currency, FX rate/time if applicable, risk level, jurisdiction, customer type, PEP status, exemption ID, and sanctions-list version.
  - Cryptographically link settlement messages to the exact release payload rather than assuming the settlement amount equals the authorized amount.

- **Separate temporal fields**
  - Store `event_time`, `business_effective_time`, `record_time`, `ingestion_time`, and, where applicable, `signature_time` and certificate creation time.
  - Record the timezone and clock source.
  - Require a reason and authority for any effective time that precedes record time.

- **Stable event ordering**
  - Use an immutable case sequence number, not only a timestamp.
  - Retain source-system `event_index`, event UUID, source transaction ID, causal predecessor ID, and tie-order provenance.
  - Do not let PM4Py import or ETL sorting overwrite source ordering.

- **Explicit state-change events**
  - Log old value, new value, source, actor, reason, and provenance for amount, risk, jurisdiction, and customer-type changes.
  - Distinguish correction, restatement, and new business information.
  - Represent “unknown,” “not collected,” “feed unavailable,” and “not applicable” explicitly rather than overloading blanks.

- **Review snapshots**
  - Each Standard_Review and Enhanced_Review should reference an immutable reviewed-state snapshot containing amount, jurisdiction, risk, customer type, PEP findings, sanctions-list version, and rule version.
  - Log `review_started_at`, `review_completed_at`, result, reviewer, evidence hash, and review type.
  - Record whether the review remains valid after each subsequent material update.

- **Approval linkage**
  - `Compliance_Approve` should contain `approved_review_id`, `approved_snapshot_hash`, approval scope, conditions, approver authority, and completion/signature time.
  - Prevent approval of a standard packet from being treated as approval of an enhanced packet.

- **Structured exemption records**
  - Store jurisdiction scope, customer-type scope, amount minimum/maximum, currency, waived rule IDs, effective-from, expiry, revocation status, approver, approval event ID, signature time, record time, and evidence hash as separate fields.
  - Log the exemption version used by the release.
  - For outage backfills, log an outage incident ID and preserve the independently timestamped original approval evidence.

- **Sanctions controls**
  - Treat list updates as global, versioned events with added/removed jurisdictions or entities, effective time, publication time, and record time.
  - Every screen should log the screened jurisdiction/entity set, exact list version, result, and screen-completion time.
  - Block release automatically when the latest relevant list update postdates the latest matching screen.

- **Rule-version logging**
  - Record the policy/rule version evaluated at review, approval, exemption approval, and release.
  - Store machine-readable trigger results, required obligations, exemptions applied, and reasons for each pass, failure, or manual override.

- **Preventive controls**
  - Recalculate obligations immediately before release using the immutable release snapshot.
  - Block release when decisive state is unknown rather than defaulting it.
  - Automatically invalidate amount-dependent reviews after a qualifying increase and require a new review ID.
  - Require explicit manual adjudication and signed rationale for conflicted evidence such as EX-88.

- **Append-only audit design**
  - Never overwrite historical values. Use correction or supersession events with links to the corrected record.
  - Preserve original event, effective, record, and evidence timestamps so later database repairs cannot make post-hoc facts appear contemporaneous.
