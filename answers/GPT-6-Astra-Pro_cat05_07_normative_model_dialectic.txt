## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

**The evidence supports two simultaneous conclusions: there are material operational violations, and M0 is inadequate as a universal compliance evaluator.** Neither conclusion cancels the other.

### What M0 actually requires

Let \(R\) denote registration, \(S\) sanctions, \(Q\) quality, \(M\) manual approval, \(A\) activation, \(J\) rejection, \(N\) notification, and \(C\) closure. Reading the construction according to its stipulated intended semantics, M0’s complete visible language is:

$$
L(M0)=R\;(SQ\mid QS)\;(MA\mid J)\;NC.
$$

Thus, sanctions and quality may complete in either order; both must precede the decision; approval and rejection are exclusive; and notification precedes closure. Concurrency permits either observed ordering—it does not require simultaneous timestamps. M0 also requires quality review before rejection, although CS-9 permits abbreviated rejected-lead processing and leaves its relationship to M0 unclear.  

On exact, unnormalized activity labels, **only SUP-701, SUP-712, and SUP-715 fit M0**. That is a statement about these fifteen supplied traces, not an estimate of population compliance. Crucially, SUP-712 violates the human-separation rule despite fitting the model. 

### Established facts versus unresolved propositions

The controlled status of CS-9, the stated pilot and emergency windows, the CRM collection change, and the supplied case circumstances are stipulated evidence. PD-3’s authority to displace CS-9 is disputed: meeting minutes and an email can document an authorized exception, but the supplied material does not establish the relevant delegation, and the Compliance observer’s statements conflict. FD-7 is presented as an operative emergency directive without an equivalent authority dispute; I therefore treat it as facially effective within its stated scope and window. 

Important unproved propositions include whether SUP-702 completed automated master-data validation, whether SUP-704’s **total** pre-approval spend remained within the cap, whether SUP-706’s CRM message belongs to this case, and whether SUP-715’s required disclosure occurred through another channel. Complete pilot *eligibility* fields do not establish complete pilot *control execution*. Likewise, “no disclosure was recorded” establishes a record gap, not necessarily that no disclosure occurred.  

### Burden of proof

For **retrospective noncompliance**, I require an identifiable applicable obligation, sufficiently established scope and timing, and reliable evidence of contradiction. Missing events establish misconduct only when the relevant collection and linkage are sufficiently dependable. Findings about exposure, actual harm, and culpability must remain separate.

For **accepting an exception**, its proponent must establish competent authority, applicability at the relevant action time, eligibility, and satisfaction of the exception’s retained controls. Good intentions, favorable outcomes, or common practice are insufficient. However, unresolved exception authority should not automatically become a finding of deliberate employee misconduct.

For **live authorization**, uncertainty is treated more conservatively: an unverified exception does not unlock activation. This is different from declaring a historical violation proved.

The primary classifications below concern the dominant issue; they do not certify every unobserved obligation. In particular, SUP-704 can have a justified sequencing exception while its aggregate spend evidence remains incomplete.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

### A controlled baseline cannot be displaced by operational custom

CS-9 expressly requires sanctions and quality before activation, manual approval before activation, and different humans as requester and manual approver. PD-3 was not incorporated into CS-9 or M0, and the Compliance observer subsequently disputed having approved a weakening of CS-9. The strongest prosecution position is therefore not that “emails never authorize exceptions,” but that **the authority necessary to override these controls has not been established**. An operator selecting “pilot” is not an authorization decision. 

SUP-703 is especially difficult to defend. Its HIGH-risk, new foreign-supplier attributes contradict PD-3’s eligibility conditions independently of the authority dispute. It received automatic rather than manual approval and activated before quality review. The EUR 180,000 purchase increases exposure; it is **not** a breach of FD-7’s EUR 20,000 cap, because that cap belongs to a different exception. No actual loss is supplied.  

SUP-708 bypassed the sanctions-before-activation control for forty minutes and also received manual approval only after activation. No pilot or emergency applied. The absence of transactions before clearance mitigates realized exposure, but does not restore the preventive control that activation bypassed. 

### Expiry is an operational control, not an administrative footnote

SUP-705 entered the provisional pattern on July 13, after FD-7 expired. A continuing factual emergency can justify seeking renewed authority; it does not itself extend an automatically expiring directive. SUP-714 similarly used the pilot pattern on August 2, after PD-3 ended. Belief that permanent authorization was imminent is not permanent authorization. The six August auto-approval cases indicate that expiry was not reliably enforced operationally.   

This does **not** implicate SUP-704 merely because its retrospective work happened after expiry. A valid July 11 provisional activation can create obligations that remain due after July 12. Expiry blocks new use of the permission; it does not erase or necessarily invalidate follow-up on a valid earlier use.  

### Trace fitness conceals important violations

SUP-712 satisfies M0 but breaches the explicit different-human requirement once aliases are resolved. SUP-715 raises a separate disclosure concern: the relationship is not itself prohibited, but a disclosure obligation exists and no disclosure is recorded. A prosecution that treats these as equivalent “four-eyes violations” would overstate the evidence; nevertheless, both show why activity-sequence fitness is insufficient.  

Of the 312 June–July auto-approval cases, 14, or **4.49%**, are clearly pilot-ineligible; 289, or 92.63%, appear eligible; and nine, or 2.88%, lack eligibility attributes. These are eligibility categories, not compliance rates. Authority, master-data validation, timing, and other obligations remain separate questions. 

The prosecution must concede genuine defects: missing information-request behavior, incomplete notification collection, duplicated registration, equivalent labels, and ambiguous scope. Those defects weaken a blanket indictment based on M0’s deviation count; they do not excuse the independently supported violations above.

## 3. Defense Brief

### M0 confuses a standard route with the whole operational domain

M0 correctly models unordered checks, but has no information-request activity or repetition mechanism. SUP-707’s extra requests obtained missing certificates before approval. The aggregate evidence reports 47 cases with requests and no activation before the requested evidence arrived. This supports adding an evidence-completion mechanism rather than penalizing the activity’s mere presence. It does not prove that every conceivable information-request practice is harmless.   

The rejected-lead and reactivation examples also challenge the observation boundary. SUP-709 may be a legitimate abbreviated intake rejection or outside M0 altogether. SUP-710 concerns reactivation with still-valid quality certification; whether a new review event is required cannot be settled by simply counting events in the new case. The defense cannot claim that reactivation is automatically exempt, but the prosecution cannot silently assume that every reactivation is a fresh onboarding requiring newly performed quality work.  

### Better evidence can defeat an apparent trace violation

The notification gap is strongly consistent with a collection problem: missingness increased from 2% to 29% after a migration that mirrors approximately 70% of notifications. This supports investigation and evidence integration, not a conclusion that all missing notifications occurred.  

SUP-702 has a stable CRM notification identifier, whereas SUP-706 has a mutable link entered two days later. Those are materially different evidence strengths. Moreover, SUP-702 occurred on June 15: the July 1 migration cannot explain that June omission. The earlier collection path remains unspecified. Notification occurrence and ordering should be reconstructed from actual CRM timestamps, not by appending an inferred event immediately before closure.   

SUP-711’s duplicate is established as a migration artifact with the same source transaction ID. SUP-713 has audit evidence of equivalent review content despite a renamed label. Removing a proven duplicate or applying a justified semantic mapping is not the same as deleting genuine misconduct from a log. The late mapping approval in SUP-713 also differs from a late substantive policy waiver: it can recognize that the required work already occurred. 

### Exceptions can be legitimate without immediately rewriting the base model

The strongest defense of PD-3 is that the steering decision, follow-up email, and monitored-exception statement may document a competent temporary exception. A controlled standard need not necessarily be rewritten for every delegated exception. Whether the decision-makers possessed that authority remains unresolved.

SUP-702 meets the stated eligibility conditions and the supplied quality-review deadline. SUP-704 has substantially stronger exception evidence: an in-window critical supplier, sanctions before activation, monitoring, and next-business-day quality and approval. Nevertheless, SUP-702 still needs master-data-validation evidence, and SUP-704’s initial EUR 18,000 spend is not a complete pre-approval transaction ledger.  

The defense’s strongest sustainable claim is therefore **misclassification by an incomplete evaluator**, not general permission to disregard controls. It cannot explain away SUP-703’s ineligibility, SUP-708’s late sanctions check, SUP-712’s same-human approval, or the absence of applicable extensions for SUP-705 and SUP-714.

### System-level judgment

I reject the prosecution’s implication that every raw mismatch proves misconduct or that documentary exceptions are necessarily powerless unless incorporated into the base model. The immediate priorities are to investigate SUP-703 and SUP-708’s exposure, remediate SUP-712’s identity-based approval bypass, enforce expiry gates, and resolve PD-3’s authority. In parallel, repair notification linkage, preserve raw evidence, and introduce the information-request mechanism. These are complementary actions, not alternatives.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The revised evaluator should return a **multidimensional result**: scope determination, raw trace fit, evidence-normalized trace fit, policy applicability, data reliability, resource compliance, and outstanding obligations. A single fitness score cannot represent these judgments.

| Layer                           | Proposed design                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Appropriate implementation                                                                        |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Control flow**                | Standard concurrent checks; manual approval; separately selected pilot and provisional routes; repeatable requests; rejection; notification; closure after route obligations.                                                                          | POWL and route-specific Petri-net conformance.                                                    |
| **Cross-event obligations**     | Sanctions precede every activation; standard quality/manual approval precede activation; exceptional quality eventually completes; notification precedes closure; activation and terminal rejection are mutually exclusive within the modeled episode. | DECLARE/log-skeleton-style constraints, supplemented by explicit obligation state.                |
| **Eligibility, time and spend** | Risk, country, corporate-group membership, effective permission, master-data validation, business-day deadlines, and provisional spending.                                                                                                             | pandas/data checks or an operational rules engine over immutable snapshots and transaction joins. |
| **People and conflicts**        | Resolve actors to humans; enforce requester–approver separation; verify delegation; record disclosures and their disposition.                                                                                                                          | Identity data checks plus organizational authorization and conflict-management controls.          |
| **Authority and scope**         | Versioned policies, delegated exceptions, expiry, exception precedence, intake boundaries, reactivation rules, and withdrawal handling.                                                                                                                | Governed policy registry and approved process ownership—not process discovery alone.              |

These are proposed implementation choices. They preserve the source’s control requirements while separating matters M0 does not encode. 

### Scope and lifecycle rules

**Rejected leads:** Create an explicitly identified intake episode. A sanctions pre-screen failure may terminate through abbreviated rejection without quality review, once the scope owner formally approves that boundary. Do not make quality optional for all rejected onboarding cases.

**Reactivations:** Create a new episode linked to the existing supplier and prior certification. Require either a fresh review or an explicit assessment that prior evidence remains valid for the current supplier, scope, and circumstances. A prior event must not be copied into the new episode as though newly executed.

**Withdrawals:** Define early withdrawal as a non-activation outcome with notification and closure. Withdrawal after partial work needs cancellation semantics; withdrawal after activation needs deactivation or suspension and resolution of retained obligations. It must not become a shortcut around retrospective controls.

### Exception governance

A pilot or emergency must identify its authorizing body, delegated power, displaced rules, retained rules, eligibility predicate, entry window, obligation deadlines, and expiry behavior. PD-3 does not waive sanctions; FD-7 does not waive sanctions, spending controls, monitoring, or retrospective work. FD-7 specifies a two-business-day deadline for quality, **not an explicit two-business-day deadline for manual approval**. A new approval deadline may be proposed prospectively but must not be invented retrospectively. 

Evaluate permission at the relevant authorization/activation time, not merely the case’s registration date. Bind validly created follow-up obligations to the policy version that created them, so they survive the entry window’s expiry. A permanent auto-approval route remains a draft capability until a competent permanent amendment exists.  

Business-day calculations require an explicitly selected calendar and timezone. Operationalize the EUR 20,000 cap through a defined cumulative spend measure and a complete pre-approval ledger; an individual purchase amount is not sufficient.

### Data normalization without evidence laundering

Keep raw events immutable. Construct a separate canonical view with provenance-supported duplicate removal, versioned label mappings, and verified CRM-event linkage. Preserve uncertainty where links remain weak. Do not make notification optional merely to improve fit, and do not convert retrospective statements into contemporaneous evidence.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

### Model design and limitations

The code constructs a **candidate architecture**, plus separate profiles that an authorized policy evaluator must select. The broad candidate is **not** an authorization engine: allowing an emergency trace in its language does not authorize SUP-705 to use that route.

The information-request loop models zero or more request events. Receipt, validation, and resolution of individual requests remain external obligations; otherwise the model would invent historical receipt events. The pilot route explicitly includes master-data validation, so its absence in an old log is an evidence question, not something to silently fill.

Notification can follow provisional activation while quality or retrospective approval remains outstanding, but closure waits for all modeled route obligations. The emergency variant shown defers manual approval; quality may be early or retrospective. The reactivation variant covers validated reuse of prior quality evidence, and the withdrawal variant covers early withdrawal only. Adverse retrospective outcomes require a separate suspension/deactivation model rather than forced successful closure.

PM4Py’s documented implementation supports these POWL constructors, binary-loop semantics, partial-order validation, and conversion to a Petri net. The code stores transitive sequence relations explicitly. ([GitHub][1])

**Validation performed:** the script was syntax-checked, and its 22 witnesses passed an independent Petri-net semantics harness. Direct execution against PM4Py remains unverified because the dependency could not be installed in this environment. The script includes the corresponding PM4Py runtime tests.

```python
"""Prospective POWL candidates; not an authorization engine.
Requires PM4Py. No external data files are read.
"""
from collections import deque
from typing import Iterable

try:
    import pm4py
    from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
        POWL, Transition, SilentTransition,
        StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL,
    )
    from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator
    from pm4py.objects.conversion.powl import converter
    from pm4py.objects.petri_net import semantics
except ImportError as exc:
    raise SystemExit(
        "Install PM4Py in this Python environment: pip install pm4py"
    ) from exc


def activity(label: str) -> POWL:
    return Transition(label=label)


def sequence(*nodes: POWL) -> POWL:
    if not nodes:
        return SilentTransition()
    if len(nodes) == 1:
        return nodes[0]
    result = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    # Complete transitive relation, not merely adjacent cover edges.
    for i, source in enumerate(nodes):
        for target in nodes[i + 1:]:
            result.order.add_edge(source, target)
    return result


def parallel(*nodes: POWL) -> StrictPartialOrder:
    return StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))


def choice(*nodes: POWL) -> POWL:
    if not nodes:
        raise ValueError("A route selection must not be empty.")
    if len(nodes) == 1:
        return nodes[0]
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=list(nodes))


def requests() -> POWL:
    # LOOP(tau, Request) has visible language Request*.
    # Reply receipt/validation and outstanding requests are data rules.
    return OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.LOOP,
        children=[
            SilentTransition(),
            activity("Request_More_Info"),
        ],
    )


def standard_route() -> POWL:
    checks = parallel(
        activity("Sanctions_Check"),
        activity("Quality_Review"),
        requests(),
    )
    decision = choice(
        sequence(
            activity("Manual_Procurement_Approval"),
            activity("Activate_Supplier"),
        ),
        activity("Reject_Supplier"),
    )
    return sequence(
        checks, decision, activity("Notify_Requester")
    )


def pilot_route() -> POWL:
    activation = sequence(
        parallel(
            activity("Sanctions_Check"),
            activity("Master_Data_Validation"),
            requests(),
        ),
        activity("Auto_Approve"),
        activity("Activate_Supplier"),
    )
    quality = activity("Quality_Review")
    notify = activity("Notify_Requester")
    result = parallel(activation, quality, notify)
    result.order.add_edge(activation, notify)
    # Quality can precede/follow activation, but must precede closure.
    return result


def emergency_route() -> POWL:
    activation = sequence(
        parallel(activity("Sanctions_Check"), requests()),
        activity("Activate_Supplier"),
    )
    quality = activity("Quality_Review")
    retrospective = activity("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
    notify = activity("Notify_Requester")
    result = parallel(
        activation, quality, retrospective, notify
    )
    result.order.add_edge(activation, retrospective)
    result.order.add_edge(activation, notify)
    # Selected variant: manual approval is retrospective.
    # Quality can be completed early or retrospectively.
    return result


def prescreen_route() -> POWL:
    return sequence(
        parallel(activity("Sanctions_Check"), requests()),
        activity("Reject_Supplier"),
        activity("Notify_Requester"),
    )


def early_withdrawal_route() -> POWL:
    return sequence(
        activity("Withdraw_Request"),
        activity("Notify_Requester"),
    )


BUILDERS = {
    "standard": standard_route,
    "pilot": pilot_route,
    "emergency": emergency_route,
    "prescreen": prescreen_route,
    "early_withdrawal": early_withdrawal_route,
}


def new_episode(routes: Iterable[str]) -> POWL:
    names = tuple(routes)
    if not names or len(set(names)) != len(names):
        raise ValueError(
            "Supply a nonempty set of distinct route names."
        )
    unknown = set(names) - set(BUILDERS)
    if unknown:
        raise ValueError(f"Unknown routes: {sorted(unknown)}")
    # Every builder call creates fresh node instances.
    return sequence(
        activity("Register_Supplier"),
        choice(*(BUILDERS[name]() for name in names)),
        activity("Close_Case"),
    )


def reactivation_episode() -> POWL:
    # This profile covers authorized reuse of valid prior evidence.
    return sequence(
        activity("Register_Reactivation"),
        parallel(
            activity("Sanctions_Check"),
            activity("Validate_Prior_Quality_Evidence"),
            requests(),
        ),
        activity("Manual_Procurement_Approval"),
        activity("Activate_Supplier"),
        activity("Notify_Requester"),
        activity("Close_Case"),
    )


# Select profiles using validated policy/scope evidence, never best fit.
PROFILES = {
    name: new_episode([name]) for name in BUILDERS
}
PROFILES["reactivation"] = reactivation_episode()

# Broad architectural candidate: NOT an operational permission.
M1_CANDIDATE = choice(
    new_episode(BUILDERS),
    reactivation_episode(),
)

for model in [M1_CANDIDATE, *PROFILES.values()]:
    model.validate_partial_orders()


def accepts(
    petri_tuple,
    trace: list[str],
    max_states: int = 100_000,
) -> bool:
    """Exact complete-trace acceptance, including silent moves.

    No token insertion or alignment repair is performed.
    State-budget exhaustion raises an error, not a false violation.
    """
    net, initial, final = petri_tuple
    pending = deque([(initial, 0)])
    seen = set()

    while pending:
        marking, pos = pending.popleft()
        key = (
            pos,
            frozenset(
                (place, count)
                for place, count in marking.items()
                if count
            ),
        )
        if key in seen:
            continue
        seen.add(key)

        if len(seen) > max_states:
            raise RuntimeError(
                "Trace acceptance unresolved: state budget exceeded."
            )

        if pos == len(trace) and marking == final:
            return True

        for transition in semantics.enabled_transitions(net, marking):
            if transition.label is None:
                next_pos = pos
            elif (
                pos < len(trace)
                and transition.label == trace[pos]
            ):
                next_pos = pos + 1
            else:
                continue

            successor = semantics.execute(
                transition, net, marking
            )
            if successor is not None:
                pending.append((successor, next_pos))

    return False


# Synthetic witnesses, NOT reconstructed case evidence.
LABELS = dict(

def expand(symbols: str) -> list[str]:
    return [LABELS[symbol] for symbol in symbols.split()]


def main() -> None:
    candidate_net = converter.apply(M1_CANDIDATE)

    positive = [
        "R S Q M A N C",
        "R Q S M A N C",
        "R S I I Q M A N C",
        "R Q S J N C",
        "R S J N C",
        "R S D U A N Q C",
        "R Q S D U A N C",
        "R S A N M Q C",
        "R S A Q M N C",
        "R W N C",
        "K S V M A N C",
    ]
    negative = [
        "R Q A S M N C",       # Sanctions after activation.
        "R S D U A N C",       # Missing quality completion.
        "R S Q M A C",         # Missing notification.
        "R S U A Q N C",       # Missing master-data validation.
        "R S Q J A N C",       # Reject and activate.
        "R S A Q N C",         # Missing retrospective approval.
        "R S Q M A N C C",     # Duplicate closure.
        "R R S Q M A N C",     # Duplicate registration.
    ]

    for symbols in positive:
        if not accepts(candidate_net, expand(symbols)):
            raise AssertionError(
                f"Expected acceptance: {symbols}"
            )

    for symbols in negative:
        if accepts(candidate_net, expand(symbols)):
            raise AssertionError(
                f"Expected rejection: {symbols}"
            )

    standard_net = converter.apply(PROFILES["standard"])
    # Broad-candidate acceptance must not authorize these as standard.
    for symbols in [
        "R S A Q M N C",
        "R S J N C",
        "R S D U A N Q C",
    ]:
        if accepts(standard_net, expand(symbols)):
            raise AssertionError(
                f"Unauthorized standard-route behavior: {symbols}"
            )

    print("PM4Py version:", pm4py.__version__)
    print(
        "Passed 22 control-flow checks; "
        "eligibility/authority/identity are NOT certified."
    )


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

### Normalized policy-overlay tables

Separate policy versions from individual rule bindings and case-level assignments. The identifiers below are proposed registry identifiers; unspecified approval metadata must remain unknown rather than be fabricated.

**PolicyVersion**

| Proposed version ID    | Source-effective interval               | Authority treatment                                                               |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `CS9_2025`             | January 1, 2025 onward                  | Controlled, formally approved baseline.                                           |
| `PD3_2026`             | June 1–July 31, 2026                    | Historical pilot; power to override CS-9 disputed. No new entries after expiry.   |
| `FD7_2026`             | July 10, 2026 00:00–July 12, 2026 23:59 | Facially operative emergency directive within scope. No new entries after expiry. |
| `AUTO_PERMANENT_DRAFT` | No effective interval                   | Proposed only; never unlocks a route.                                             |

These intervals and authority distinctions derive from N1–N3 and the absence of a permanent amendment. Actual timezone, timestamp precision, authorizing identities, delegation references, and immutable record times require registry evidence.

Notification/closure rules remain linked to the model version. The conflict-disclosure rule must be linked to its actual policy version once obtained; its identity and effective dates are not supplied.

Use a separate **CasePolicyAssignment** relation containing `episode_id`, `route_id`, `policy_version_id`, `eligibility_snapshot_id`, `authority_evidence_id`, `decision_event_id`, `assignment_status`, `effective_at`, and `recorded_at`. Individual obligations belong in an **Obligation** relation with `obligation_id`, `trigger_event_id`, `rule_id`, `due_at`, `calendar_id`, `status`, and `completion_evidence_id`.

This prevents an operator’s “pilot” selection from becoming its own proof of eligibility or authority.

### Event and data-collection proposal

| Record family                     | Required fields and controls                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Canonical event**               | Immutable `event_id`, `episode_id`, `supplier_id`, canonical activity, original activity, lifecycle, outcome, source system, source transaction ID, immutable `effective_at`/occurrence time and `recorded_at`, timezone, source sequence, payload hash, mapping-version ID. Corrections create superseding records rather than overwriting timestamps. |
| **Notification**                  | Stable `notification_id` generated by CRM; immutable CRM event identity; recipient, message type, occurrence time, delivery outcome, and append-only episode linkage. Preserve the history of older mutable links.                                                                                                                                      |
| **Eligibility and authorization** | Risk/country/group-membership snapshot as known at the decision; provenance and validity times; model, rule and policy-version IDs; exception authority, signature and delegation evidence; provisional/standard activation mode.                                                                                                                       |
| **Actor identity and conflicts**  | Service or actor ID, canonical human ID where applicable, versioned alias mappings, actual decision-maker, delegator/delegate, delegation scope and interval, disclosure ID, review/disposition and recusal evidence.                                                                                                                                   |
| **Evidence requests and reuse**   | Stable request/document IDs, requested items, receipt and validation events, outstanding-obligation state, prior certification ID, validity scope, and explicit reuse assessment. Repeated requests are not deduplicated merely because their labels match.                                                                                             |
| **Transactions and monitoring**   | Linked transactions with event time, amount, currency and defined spend basis; cumulative provisional exposure; approval cutoff; monitoring-day evidence; deadline/calendar provenance.                                                                                                                                                                 |

For SUP-711, deduplication should use the proven migration lineage and an appropriate composite source key—not a global assumption that every shared transaction ID is a duplicate. For SUP-713, the mapping should preserve both the original label and the evidence establishing semantic equivalence. For SUP-706, unresolved linkage must remain an explicit uncertainty rather than a fabricated notification event.

**The revised system should reduce false allegations without reducing real controls.** Its success criterion is that legitimate evidence requests and verified data corrections stop generating misconduct findings, while ineligible auto-approval, expired permissions, late sanctions checks, and same-human approval remain visible and enforceable.
