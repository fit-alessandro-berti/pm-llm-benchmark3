## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts (drawn directly from the materials):**  
GPIP-4 (Source A) is a board-approved global policy effective 2026-01-01 covering all Northbridge entities and purchases. Local SOP DE-AAC-PROC-17 Rev 6 (Source B) is a controlled Aachen operating procedure effective 2025-03-01 limited to maintenance and production-support purchases. ECD-2 (Source C) is a time-bounded incident directive (2026-07-14 09:00–2026-07-18 18:00 CEST) jointly signed by the COO and Incident Commander and scoped to “critical sterilization-line lots.” The CFO email (Source D) is an ordinary, uncontrolled message sent 2026-07-15 07:20 CEST. The Chief Risk Officer was unavailable and issued neither a written A7 exception nor any later ratification of ECD-2. The organization has previously treated signed incident directives as temporary policy overlays, but not in a case involving new-supplier Compliance clearance. ERP outage ON-44 (2026-07-14 08:30–13:00 CEST) can produce timestamps lagging real events by up to 12 minutes; telephone recordings, signed PDFs, access logs and supplier acknowledgments therefore carry higher evidential weight for affected intervals. Incident Command members may make operational decisions; their charter is silent on budget-owner status. A6 states that later approval mitigates consequences but does not automatically cure a missing pre-commitment approval.

**Assumptions (explicit and defeasible):**  
1. Normative weight is multi-dimensional, not a single total order. Formal source rank (board policy > time-limited executive directive > controlled local SOP > ordinary email) interacts with specificity, temporal window, process purpose (integrity versus operational continuity) and reserved powers.  
2. ECD-2 does not constitute an A7 exception: A7 reserves written, scoped, time-limited exceptions to the Chief Risk Officer; C is silent on segregation of duties, Finance Controller approval, Compliance clearance and the EUR 100 000 threshold; and no CRO ratification occurred. Prior overlay practice is weak precedent for core integrity controls.  
3. “Critical sterilization-line lots” requires a sterilization or production-line nexus plus contemporaneous evidence of urgency. The Ops/IA contest is resolved by preferring the narrower reading wherever ambiguity would relax high-integrity controls (new supplier or high value); pure contingency items, cafeteria repairs and post-window recovery fall outside.  
4. Joint Incident Command authorization can serve as an operational/Plant substitute and, under D1, as a Finance/operational-approval proxy for amounts ≤ EUR 150 000 during the ECD-2 window. It does not convert IC members into independent budget owners for A2, nor does it waive A1 segregation of duties or A3 Compliance clearance.  
5. Rules are applied at the moment of the relevant act—especially Commit_to_Supplier. Post-commitment events may mitigate severity or corroborate a timely verbal act, but cannot cure a missing mandatory pre-commitment control (A6).  
6. The burden of proving an exception or full compliance rests on the actors claiming it. Ambiguity or absence of contemporaneous record favors non-compliance or indeterminacy rather than assumed compliance.  
7. Emergency necessity under B4 or C supports only timing and supplier-register deviations; it does not reach integrity controls (SoD, Compliance) without explicit higher authority.  
8. Evidential ranking: contemporaneous non-ERP records (phone, signed PDF, supplier acknowledgement, access logs) outrank delayed ERP timestamps during or after the outage. Verbal authorization recorded within the stated window (C3 four hours; B6 twenty-four hours) is acceptable when corroborated.

**Decision method:** For each case identify (i) the applicable temporal window and scope at the moment of commitment, (ii) the control dimension engaged (integrity/SoD, Finance threshold, Compliance, timing/PO order, supplier register, emergency definition), (iii) the highest-ranking non-defeated rule for that dimension, (iv) the quality of available evidence, and (v) a single primary classification drawn from the four permitted labels, recording residual uncertainty and the evidence that would reverse the conclusion.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

**P-502** – Alternative primary: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.  
Premise that must change: C2 + C4 are read as authorizing not merely the use of an unapproved supplier but also the dispensation of the entire A3 Compliance_Clearance process for new suppliers inside the critical window.  
Credibility: Moderate. C’s silence on Compliance and the explicit reservation of exceptions to the CRO under A7 make the reading strained; however, the operational purpose of “supplier-onboarding requirements” and the prior practice of treating incident directives as overlays give it some institutional plausibility. Weakness: no CRO ratification and A6’s anti-cure rule remain.

**P-503** – Alternative primary: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION (scope) or INDETERMINATE.  
Premise that must change: “critical sterilization-line lots” is given the broad Ops reading that includes backup-line contingency stock labelled “critical contingency” by Incident Command.  
Credibility: Moderate on operational grounds (Incident Command did label it). Weakness: three-day existing stock undercuts urgency; IA’s 24-hour view is more consistent with the contamination-containment purpose; SoD and missing Finance/Compliance still fail independently of scope.

**P-505** – Alternative primary: NONCOMPLIANT.  
Premise that must change: the ERP Commit timestamp of 18:06 is treated as conclusive and the nine-minute-fast possibility is disregarded, placing the act after ECD-2 expiry.  
Credibility: Low–moderate. The outage notice and the supplier acknowledgement at 17:59:31 supply stronger contemporaneous evidence; treating the lagged ERP stamp as decisive would invert the stated evidential ranking.

**P-507** – Alternative primary: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.  
Premise that must change: D1’s “all operational approvals” language, combined with C’s precedence over ordinary workflow, is read as also covering the Finance Controller requirement for amounts between 50 k and 150 k even when no separate Finance event exists.  
Credibility: Moderate. D is an interpretive Finance communication and the amount is inside the 150 k ceiling. Weakness: D3/D4 expressly disclaim waiver of essential integrity controls; A2 still requires an independent budget owner; no document equates IC members with Finance Controllers.

**P-508** – Alternative primary: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.  
Premise that must change: for amounts only marginally above 150 k, the combination of pre-commitment Compliance, Legal (D2) and IC joint authorization is treated as satisfying the functional purpose of A2, or D1 is read as extending a de-facto proxy slightly beyond the stated ceiling under “use judgment.”  
Credibility: Low–moderate. The 5 k overage is small and all other high-integrity controls were observed. Weakness: D1’s numeric ceiling is explicit; A2’s independent-budget-owner requirement is not met; self-limiting language in D4 cuts against expansion.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

**Distinctions observed**  
- Policy breach (missing pre-commitment control required by A): P-502 (Compliance), P-503 (SoD + Finance + Compliance), P-504 (supplier + authority), P-506 (post-window + incomplete A2), P-507 (Finance), P-508 (Finance).  
- Logging / recording defect only: P-507 (ERP authorization late, but signed PDF contemporaneous); P-505 (ERP lag, but supplier email decisive).  
- Late evidence of a timely act: P-502 (IC record 12:55 of 10:10 verbal); P-507 (PDF 12:40 of 08:55 authorization). These are acceptable under C3 when corroborated.  
- Inability to prove compliance: P-501 (no delegation record), P-503 (scope evidence thin), P-506 (no written extension).

**Immediate case-level remediation**  
- P-501, P-503, P-507, P-508: obtain retrospective independent budget-owner / Finance Controller sign-off and document the residual risk; escalate SoD conflict in P-503 to Compliance and Legal.  
- P-502: open a formal Compliance review of the new supplier; document why the late clearance does not cure the pre-commitment gap.  
- P-504: reverse or re-procure under normal controls; treat as non-emergency personal/site amenity spend.  
- P-505: file the supplier acknowledgement and outage note as the primary time evidence; close as compliant.  
- P-506: treat as ordinary post-incident recovery; complete missing Plant and independent budget approvals and issue a lessons-learned note on verbal-extension practice.

**Monitoring actions**  
- Freeze further use of ECD-2 language until CRO reviews residual cases.  
- Require dual contemporaneous recording (ERP + signed PDF or voice log) for any future emergency commitment.  
- Sample all ≥50 k commitments in the next 90 days for pre-commitment evidence completeness.  
- Log every instance in which IC members act as both operational authorizer and committer for SoD review.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Replacement hierarchy and exception clause (concise)**  

1. GPIP-4 remains the residual baseline for segregation of duties (A1), independent budget-owner + Finance Controller approval ≥ EUR 50 000 (A2), and Compliance clearance for > EUR 100 000 or any new supplier (A3).  
2. A time-limited incident directive jointly signed by the COO and Incident Commander may override ordinary local timing (Create_PO sequence) and approved-supplier-register requirements solely for purchases that are both (a) required for critical sterilization or production-line lots needed within 24 hours to contain an active contamination or safety event and (b) executed inside the directive’s stated window.  
3. Such a directive does **not** waive A1, A2 or A3 unless the Chief Risk Officer issues a concurrent or subsequent written, scoped, time-limited A7 exception that expressly names the controls being relaxed.  
4. The CFO may, by controlled memorandum, authorize Incident Command joint authorization to serve as the Finance Controller proxy for amounts ≤ EUR 150 000 inside an active ECD window; the independent-budget-owner requirement and all Compliance/SoD rules remain. Amounts > EUR 150 000 additionally require Legal clearance.  
5. Verbal authorizations are valid only if recorded (signed PDF, voice log or system entry) within four hours; contemporaneous non-system evidence prevails over lagged ERP timestamps during declared outages.  
6. No verbal or informal extension of an ECD window is effective; any extension requires the same formal signatures as the original directive.  
7. Safeguard against emergency normalization: every ECD-style directive must contain an automatic sunset no longer than five calendar days and a mandatory post-event CRO review of all commitments > EUR 50 000 executed under it. Local SOPs may add stricter operational evidence but may not weaken the above hierarchy.
