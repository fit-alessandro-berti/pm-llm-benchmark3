“Saturday completion” requires a favorable call-out **and** a short QA review that fits the Saturday window. Otherwise, an early weekend microbiology result waits for Monday QA.

## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

### Calendar and queue calculations used

- **Chemistry:** The analysts’ existing jobs finish at approximately 15:54–16:24 and 16:30–17:30. B-417 therefore cannot ordinarily start before about 16:00–17:30. Even a 1.5-hour B-417 service must start by **16:30** to finish by 18:00. This is a narrow favorable window; most of the chemistry work will spill to Monday.
- **Microbiology:** Friday 14:00 plus 46–54 hours gives Saturday 12:00 through Monday 04:00. Without weekend authorization, Monday 08:00 plus 0.5–1.5 hours gives a usable primary result at **Monday 08:30–09:30**.
- **QA working time:** Monday–Friday provides 12 working hours per day; Saturday provides four. Thus 4–12 query hours, 8–20 documentation-investigation hours, and potentially 32 combined working hours represent different fractions of elapsed time.
- **Parallel join:** For every ordinary path, the join is  
  \[
  J=\max(C_{\text{usable}},M_{\text{usable}}),
  \]
  not \(C+M\). Rework clocks begin only when their branch or QA predecessor becomes available.

## 4. Forecast summary

These are **planning quantiles**, not statistically calibrated percentiles: the future QA queue, arrival overdispersion, and upper QA-service tail are not sufficiently specified to fit a queueing distribution.

| Forecast level | Remaining time from Fri 18 Sept 15:30 | Approximate terminal clock interval |
|---|---:|---|
| **Plausible earliest completion** | About **22–26 h** | **Sat 19 Sept, approximately 13:30–16:30.** This is a low-probability stack: early primary readiness, favorable call-out, essentially no queue, and a short review fitting before Saturday QA closes. |
| **Central / P50-like** | About **68–93 h** | **Mon 21 Sept 11:30 – Tue 22 Sept 12:30.** The most likely normal outcome is a Monday test join followed by Monday afternoon or Tuesday morning QA acceptance and release. |
| **P80-like** | About **96–168 h** | **Tue 22 Sept 15:30 – Fri 25 Sept 15:30.** This includes delayed confirmation, a short chemistry spillover, documentation rework, congested QA, and the faster laboratory-investigation cases. |
| **P90-like / severe-tail** | About **168–360 h**, or **7–15 days** | **Fri 25 Sept 15:30 – Wed 7 Oct 15:30.** Laboratory investigations, a confirmatory culture, and QA backlog can compound. A still more severe tail beyond 15 days is not excluded. |

**Most plausible terminal events:** electronic release after normal or remediable QA acceptance, with Monday–Tuesday most likely and Saturday possible but uncommon. Batch rejection remains possible following a confirmed critical defect, but its probability cannot be inferred from the supplied contamination probabilities.

A useful planning shorthand is:

- Roughly **55–70%** of runs avoid a several-day laboratory investigation and finish by approximately Tuesday.
- Roughly **25–40%** finish Wednesday through Sunday because of queueing, confirmation, or working-time rework.
- A laboratory investigation is a **few-percent to roughly 10% compound tail**, not an independent add-on to every other uncertainty.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

| Rank | Uncertainty driver | Main category | Why it matters |
|---:|---|---|---|
| **1** | Actual usable primary microbiology result, including its timestamp and whether it is positive | **Path uncertainty, with calendar gating** | It can permit a rare Saturday release, leave a normal Monday join, or add 20–30 incubation hours plus another readout and investigation. It changes both the clock and the downstream path. |
| **2** | QA queue and effective workload at the future join | **Queue uncertainty** | Two reviewers can absorb a moderate Monday arrival stream, but bursty arrivals and several long reviews can move acceptance by most of a working day or more. |
| **3** | Unresolved temperature excursion and its induced dependence | **Dependence uncertainty; also observation uncertainty** | Confirmation can raise both branch-anomaly risks and independently raise the investigation probability. The supplied marginals do not quantify the resulting compound tail. |
| **4** | Chemistry dispatch order and spillover across the weekend | **Queue uncertainty, amplified by calendar constraints** | Missing the 16:30 Friday start-and-finish window shifts a major clock to Monday. Priority access Monday morning can partially offset, but not erase, that loss. |
| **5** | Duration and co-occurrence of QA review, query and investigation work | **Service-time uncertainty, with path overlap** | The 3.5-hour QA duration is only an 80th percentile, not a cap. Queries and investigations can also co-occur, so their duration distribution matters as much as their individual probabilities. |

## 6. Most forecast-changing next event

The single most informative next event is the **primary microbiology readout: its actual completion time, usability, and whether a contamination indication is present**.

- **Usable early Saturday result, no indication:** this creates a possible Saturday release if the short QA review fits; otherwise it advances the join to Monday. The central forecast moves roughly 1.5–2 days earlier than a routine Monday readout.
- **Usable Monday morning result, no indication:** the normal forecast remains Monday–Tuesday, with the join set by whichever of microbiology and resumed chemistry is later.
- **Positive indication without weekend call-out:** confirmation generally moves the usable result to Tuesday 08:30–17:00 and makes QA deviation handling more likely.
- **Positive indication with the early on-call variant:** confirmation readout may occur Monday morning, so chemistry can become the dominant clock again rather than microbiology.

**Secondary update trigger:** resolution of the temperature-excursion flag. If it is ruled out, the correlated rerun/contamination tail and the 30–55% abnormal-investigation regime become less relevant. If confirmed, the investigation allowance rises even without positive tests, and the upper forecast can move from the Monday–Tuesday central range into the late-September to early-October laboratory-investigation range.
