## 2. Rule Interpretation

- **RR1.** For each case, the sets of humans who drafted and approved must have empty intersection. One identified person in both sets establishes a breach at any risk level. With an unresolved actor, distinct account strings cannot establish separation.
- **RR2.** Apply the same test to implementers and verifiers. A reliable different-human match supports technical separation. RR2 specifies neither a verifier-only job title nor separation of approver and verifier. Skill, independent judgment, and influence over the verifier remain control questions.
- **RR3.** At each HIGH approval time, establish the human and either their standing QM role or a grant from the empowered principal. Check delegate, interval, activity, change scope, any required principal absence, and the authority for every link in a delegation chain. An emergency IC approval is evaluated through RR4. LOW approval has no QM test under these rules, but RR1 still applies.
- **Effective time and availability.** Record the signed/effective time separately from the database creation time. A later entry alone cannot establish that a grant existed when someone acted. An authentic, controlled, pre-event signed instrument can, even if migrated later. A principal may exercise her standing authority during leave if she personally acts and the role is in force. Establish the duration and scope of any return to duty before deciding its effect on an absence-conditioned grant. One VPN session neither identifies a signer conclusively nor establishes day-long availability. A weekend establishes the calendar day only.
- **RR4.** For the emergency route require HIGH risk, a credible emergency flag and safety rationale, approval by the IC or a valid in-scope IC delegate before implementation, and a subsequent review by the QM or a delegate empowered to **review this change**. I count from implementation, exclude its date, count Monday–Friday on the supplied no-holiday calendar, and allow through the end of the second business day, using UTC as the supplied clock. Both emergency changes were approved and implemented Tue Aug 11. Day one is Wed Aug 12 and day two Thu Aug 13, so a review on Fri Aug 14 is late. Counting from approval gives the same dates here. Timeliness, review authority and initial approval authority are separate tests.
- **RR5.** Preserve a distinction between account, attributed human, and evidence for that attribution. Credit a service-account action only when its session reliably binds it to a human who may perform the action. Where a mandatory fact is missing, report that the control cannot be certified and identify the fact that would resolve it.
- **RR6.** A directed handover measures succession within cases. Working together measures participation in the same cases. Neither establishes an agreement to circumvent controls. Assess concentration against eligible staffing and assignments, then corroborate with independent evidence of how the decisions were made.

## 6. Control Improvements

- **Delegations and role history.** Have the empowered principal sign a versioned grant before use. Capture principal, delegate, effective start/end in UTC, permitted activities (including approval versus retrospective review), controlled scope or incident ID, absence condition, and onward-delegation rights. Validate the chain at sign-off and reject a transfer prohibited by its parent. Preserve creation, signature, migration, revocation and effective times as distinct fields so an auditor can replay the decision.
- **Principal and delegate availability.** Maintain time-bound leave, on-call, emergency return and hand-back records. An urgent return by a principal should identify the cases and times affected and trigger re-evaluation of overlapping grants. Flag a delegate sign-off during sick leave for individual re-authentication and duty confirmation. Record an authorized replacement if needed.
- **Human identity and signatures.** Give each person one durable identifier with effective-dated account aliases. Enforce RR1/RR2 on that identifier when the user signs. Require individual, transaction-bound authentication on shared terminals, with a short inactivity lock. Where an integration posts as svc_qms, carry the signed human identity and session/transaction ID through to the event. Route unattributable signatures for resolution.
- **Emergency review.** Require an incident link, safety rationale and valid IC signature before emergency implementation. At implementation, calculate the due date from the site calendar and assign a QM or expressly authorized retrospective reviewer. Notify a backup before expiry and escalate missed or unauthorized reviews. Keep late remediation visible alongside the original due date.
- **Independent assignment and monitoring.** Rotate qualified approvers when the shift has coverage. Where it does not, use sampled second reviews from outside the cell. Monitor directed draft/approval handovers, reciprocal assignments, overrides and case co-participation against staffing-adjusted baselines. Have a reviewer examine substantive challenges and decisions before escalating a collusion allegation.
- **Evidential retention.** Preserve the original QMS signatures, document versions, identity crosswalk, role/leave snapshots, signed grants, incident records and relevant endpoint, VPN, badge and FIDO records. Synchronize clocks and retain transaction identifiers, access controls, hashes and chain of custody for a period that supports change-control investigations. This permits later reconstruction even when the user master has changed.
