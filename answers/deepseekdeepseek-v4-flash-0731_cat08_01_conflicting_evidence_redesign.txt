## 1. Current-process diagnosis

The current process is not one process; it is two conflicting processes competing for the same cases. The dominant fast path and the slower reviewed path produce different outcomes, but neither is universally better.

- The fastest, cheapest path is a quality and compliance trap. The frequency DFG shows **Auto eligibility screen  Fast approval: 2550** and **Fast approval  Dispatch replacement: 2300**. The performance DFG shows this path is very fast: median approval time **0.4h**, median dispatch time **1.8h**. But the rework statistics undercut that speed: **14.1% of Fast approval cases lead to Customer complaint/reopen**, versus **3.3%** for repair-route cases. Fast approval is **31% cheaper before rework**, but only **9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments, and service credits**. For Premium high-value devices, the fast path produces **22% complaint/reopen** versus **5%** on the reviewed/repair path.

- The fast path also violates core compliance rules. Conformance analysis shows **log fitness 0.91** and only **68% fitting traces**. The alignment diagnostics explicitly identify **Fast approval before Evidence completeness**, **Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices**, and **Customer complaint/reopen after close**. R1 and R2 are being ignored in many cases.

- The slower reviewed path is not automatically good. For low-risk accessories, the manual path reduces complaints, but it triples cycle time and can cause service-credit exposure. Premium low-risk accessory cases have median **8h** on the fast path versus **46h** on the manual path; if a case exceeds **24h**, service-credit exposure becomes high. For low-risk accessories, the manual path is not a safe default. For high-value devices, however, the reviewed/repair path is strongly better on quality and complaint outcomes.

- Evidence handling is a major source of waste and poor customer experience. The DFG contains a heavy loop: **Request missing evidence  Manual eligibility review: 890**, and **Manual eligibility review  Request missing evidence: 780**. The performance DFG shows this loop is slow: **mean 34h**, **p90 88h**. **26% of cases that enter Request missing evidence receive at least two evidence requests.** The unclear-defect/missing-evidence segment has a median cycle time of **140h** and the highest abandonment. **320 cases abandon directly from Request missing evidence**, and another **70 abandon after Customer approval**.

- Replacement and repair quality are not yet controlled. **Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen** has mean **96h** and p90 **180h**, linked to incorrect replacement part selection. Rework repair occurs in **12.5% of repaired high-value devices** but only **4% of repaired low-risk devices**. ## 2. Redesign A: Risk-Tiered Compliance Gate and High-Value Dispatch Safety Check

### Redesigned process flow

1. **Intake claim** captures segment, product value/risk tier, defect type, and evidence status.
2. **Auto eligibility screen** is updated to be segment-aware and compliance-aware:
   - Low-risk accessory with complete evidence  **Fast approval  Dispatch replacement**.
   - Mid-risk device with evidence complete and value below **EUR 400**  can use Fast approval.
   - Claim value **above EUR 400**, high-value device, or unclear defect  **Manual eligibility review**, not Fast approval.
   - Fraud indicator  **Fraud/risk review**.
3. Manual eligibility review or fraud/risk review is completed before any technical decision, enforcing R1.
4. For replacement approvals, a **pre-dispatch quality gate** is added before **Dispatch replacement**:
   - Automated part-compatibility check for low-risk devices.
   - Lightweight inspector check for high-value devices, using underused quality-check staff.
   - This adds **6–18 hours** for high-value dispatch, depending on resource design.
5. Repair routes remain unchanged: **Repair quote  Customer approval  Repair device  Quality check**.

### Segments, paths, and activities targeted

- **Premium high-value devices** and **Standard high-value devices**: currently suffer 22% and 18% complaint/reopen on the fast path.
- **All cases over EUR 400**: currently violating R1 by reaching Fast approval without manual/fraud review.
- **Dispatch replacement**: targeted to reduce wrong replacement selection and the **Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen** long tail.

### Mechanism of improvement

Redesign A prevents downstream failure instead of reacting to it. It enforces **R1 and R2** before Fast approval, and it catches wrong replacement selection before dispatch. The pre-dispatch gate specifically targets the alignment deviation **“Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices”** and the process-mining pattern **Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen: p90 180h**.

### Deliberate sacrifices and unresolved issues

- Redesign A deliberately sacrifices same-day replacement for high-value customers. This may increase Premium service-credit pressure because Premium customers expect completion within **24 hours**. The 6–18h gate makes that threshold hard to guarantee.
- It will increase manual review inflow, possibly by **15–35%**, because updated auto-screen rules will route more cases to Manual eligibility review. With only 7 reviewers, queue risk is real.

## 3. Redesign B: Evidence-First Intake with Assisted Digital Evidence Portal

### Redesigned process flow

1. **Intake claim** starts an automated evidence-need assessment based on defect type, product category, and claim value.
2. The customer receives a link to an **evidence portal** with a case-specific checklist: proof of purchase, photos, diagnostic logs, etc.

4. **Assisted call-back or in-person evidence collection** is available for customers with low digital literacy or for unclear-defect cases.

### Segments, paths, and activities targeted

### Mechanism of improvement

The evidence portal converts a broken asynchronous loop into a single front-loaded customer interaction. ## 4. Redesign C: Complaint/Reopen Closed-Loop with Root-Cause Triage and R3-Compliant Service Recovery

### Redesigned process flow

1. **Customer complaint/reopen** becomes a first-class process event, not simply a reroute back into Manual eligibility review.
2. A **reopen triage** captures:
   - original path,
   - product segment,
   - fulfillment type,
   - complaint reason: wrong replacement, delay, repair quality, eligibility dispute.
3. **All reopened claims pass Manual eligibility review before final close or denial**, satisfying R3. The review is scoped to the reopening reason and uses the existing claim file, so it is faster than a full re-review.
4. After manual review, decision tracks split:
   - **Wrong replacement or repair-quality issue**: issue correct replacement/repair immediately, including a compatibility check if replacement is involved. No new Customer approval loop.
   - **Delay or communication issue**: for Standard cases, provide status update and, if needed, a predefined minor service credit. For Premium or high-impact cases, route to the Escalation board with pre-filled options.
   - **Eligibility/fraud dispute**: full Fraud/risk review, then Manual eligibility review, then final denial if appropriate.
5. A **weekly root-cause review** of complaint codes feeds back into Auto-screen rules and the pre-dispatch gate. This closes the loop and reduces recurrence.

### Segments, paths, and activities targeted

- **Customer complaint/reopen**: **9.2% of all cases**, but **14.1% of Fast approval cases**.
- **Customer complaint/reopen  Manual eligibility review: 310** and **Customer complaint/reopen  Escalation board: 120**.
- **Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen**: long-tail mean **96h**, p90 **180h**.
- **Customer complaint/reopen after close**, which is an explicit conformance deviation.

### Mechanism of improvement

Redesign C treats complaints as a process with ownership, decision rules, and learning, rather than as random failures. It enforces R3 while avoiding a full re-review loop. It reduces Escalation board overload by delegating standard service-recovery cases to rule-based decisions. The root-cause review is designed to make the Auto-screen rules less “outdated” by using downstream complaint data to improve upstream routing.

### Deliberate sacrifices and unresolved issues

- Redesign C is reactive. It does not reduce the initial number of Fast approval mistakes unless the learning loop actually changes rules.
- It increases cost because every reopened case requires Manual eligibility review. If Fast approval continues to generate complaints at the current rate, the manual review queue will come under pressure.
- The Escalation board has only one daily slot. If the redesign does not successfully delegate Standard cases, the board remains a bottleneck.
- It does not solve evidence loops or pre-dispatch replacement errors unless combined with A or B.

## 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

### Redesign A

- **Failure mode 1:** Manual eligibility review becomes the new bottleneck. The updated Auto-screen rules increase manual inflow by **15–35%**. With only 7 reviewers and queue time growing sharply above **82% utilization**, high-value cases may wait so long that Premium customers receive service credits for missed **24-hour** expectations.
- **Failure mode 2:** The pre-dispatch gate is applied too broadly or becomes a full manual inspection. It could add **6–18 hours** to low-risk replacements that did not need it, creating complaints and negating the speed of the fast path.
- **Likely bottleneck migration:** Downstream “wrong replacement” failures move upstream to **Manual eligibility review** and the **pre-dispatch quality gate**.

### Redesign B

- **Failure mode 1:** Low digital-literacy customers abandon the portal. The **320 existing abandonments from Request missing evidence** could increase if the portal replaces a human interaction without an assisted fallback.
- **Failure mode 2:** Evidence quality stays poor. Customers upload irrelevant files, and manual reviewers still need to request additional information, so the repeated-evidence rate does not fall by the expected **30–45%**.
- **Likely bottleneck migration:** The bottleneck moves from Manual eligibility review to the **evidence portal / assisted channel**, specifically to the callback queue for unclear-defect and low-literacy cases.

### Redesign C

- **Failure mode 1:** Every reopened case must pass Manual eligibility review. If Fast approval continues to generate complaints, the reopened-case volume overwhelms the 7 manual reviewers, making complaint resolution slower than before.
- **Failure mode 2:** The Escalation board remains a one-slot-per-day bottleneck. If Standard cases are not delegated to rule-based service recovery, Premium cases will wait, and the strong customer-satisfaction effect of the board may be diluted.
- **Likely bottleneck migration:** The complaint tail moves from downstream **Customer complaint/reopen** to the **Manual eligibility review queue** and the **Escalation board queue**.

## 7. Recommended experiment sequence

### First pilot: A-lite on Standard high-value devices

Start with Redesign A, but only for **Standard high-value devices**, not Premium. This avoids the most sensitive **24-hour service-credit threshold** while still testing the highest compliance-risk area.

**Metrics to monitor:**
- % of high-value cases reaching Fast approval without Manual/Fraud review, i.e., R1/R2 violations. Target: near zero.
- High-value **Customer complaint/reopen** rate. Baseline: **18%** on the fast path, **6%** on the reviewed/repair path. Target: reduction of at least one-third in the pilot group.
- **Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen** frequency and p90 time.
- Manual eligibility review utilization. Stop if utilization exceeds **85–90%** for two consecutive weeks.
- Service-credit cost, replacement cost, and p90 cycle time for high-value cases.

**Stop/go:**
- **Go** if R1/R2 violations drop by more than **80%**, high-value complaint/reopen drops by at least **25–30%**, and manual review utilization stays below **85%**.
- **Hold/redesign** if manual review utilization exceeds **90%**, or if complaint/reopen does not improve after four weeks.

### Second pilot: Redesign B on unclear-defect/missing-evidence cases

Run Redesign B on the **22% unclear-defect / missing-evidence segment** after A-lite shows the new manual-review demand. The rationale is that B reduces manual review touches by **30–45%**, which creates capacity for A’s increased manual inflow.

**Metrics to monitor:**
- Repeated evidence request rate. Baseline: **26%** of evidence cases. Target: at least **30% reduction**, ideally **45%**.
- Time from intake to technical decision for unclear-defect cases. Baseline median: **140h**. Target: meaningful reduction, especially at **p90**.
- Abandonment rate in the evidence process. Baseline: **320 from Request missing evidence**. Target: no increase; if abandonment rises, strengthen assisted fallback.
- Manual review touches per completed case.

**Stop/go:**
- **Go** if repeated evidence requests fall by at least **30%** and abandonment does not increase by more than **10 percentage points**.
- **Stop/retune** if abandonment increases sharply or if the assisted channel becomes a new queue.

### Third pilot: Redesign C on residual complaint/reopen cases

Deploy Redesign C after A and B stabilize the upstream flow. This ensures the root-cause learning loop is fed by the revised process rather than by the old fast path.

**Metrics to monitor:**
- Overall complaint/reopen rate.
- Time from complaint/reopen to final close.
- % of reopened cases that pass Manual eligibility review before close/denial, i.e., **R3 compliance**. Target: near **100%**.
- Escalation board queue length and service-credit cost.
- Repeat reopen rate.

**Stop/go:**
- **Go** if reopened-case resolution time p90 drops by more than **30%**, R3 compliance reaches near **100%**, and repeat reopen rate falls.
- **Hold** if the Escalation board becomes the main bottleneck, and delegate more Standard cases to rule-based recovery.

### How to know improvements are real rather than shifted downstream

- Compare **end-to-end outcomes**, not just edge-level mean times: final close, complaint/reopen, abandonment, service credits, reshipments, rework repair.
- Use a **control group** in a different region or product family so seasonal and campaign effects can be separated.
- Re-run the same process-mining analysis after each pilot: compare the DFG, performance DFG, conformance fitness, precision, and alignment diagnostics. Look for new deviations such as **Evidence portal  Abandoned** or **Manual review  Complaint/reopen**.
- Track **long-tail p90** times, because a redesign can improve the median while worsening the worst customers, especially Premium customers.
- Monitor whether a problem disappears from one segment only to appear in another. For example, if A reduces Fast approval complaints but B increases abandonment, that is not a genuine improvement. The goal is to reduce **total** rework, complaints, compliance exceptions, and service credits across all segments, not to move them to a less visible part of the process.
