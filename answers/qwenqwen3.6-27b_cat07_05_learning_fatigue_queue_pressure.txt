# 2. Integrated explanation

The observed performance trajectory is not driven by a single mechanism but by a dynamic interplay of **case-mix selection**, **queue pressure**, and **resource experience**, modulated by **batching workarounds** and **automation interactions**.

**Early Shift: Complexity and Selection Dominance**
At the start of shifts, service times are high (18.8 min) and complexity is elevated (0.72). This is primarily a **case-mix effect**: backlogs from previous shifts contain complex, high-priority cases. **Selection bias** amplifies this: senior resources, who are more capable, intentionally take these complex cases early, which stabilizes error rates (3.4%) despite difficulty. The high service time reflects genuine processing needs, not inefficiency. Learning effects may exist but are confounded by the dropping complexity curve.

**Mid Shift: Stabilization and the "Sweet Spot"**
As the shift progresses (pos 6–50), complexity declines (0.61  0.50), queue pressure stabilizes (WIP ~30–39), and resources warm up. Service time drops to 12.9–13.6 min, and error rates reach their minimum (2.5–2.7%). This represents the optimal operating zone where **learning** and **favorable case-mix** align with manageable workload. The speed-up here is largely justified by lower complexity and stabilized queue conditions.

**Late Shift: Queue Pressure and Speed-Quality Tradeoff**
In the final shift positions (51–75 and 76+), queue pressure escalates dramatically (WIP 62  88), while complexity remains low (0.46  0.43). Service time accelerates to 10.8 min, but error rates spike to 7.9% and rework to 13.7%. This is a classic **queue pressure effect**: workers rush to reduce WIP, sacrificing accuracy. The speed-up is not learning; it is a **speed-quality tradeoff** induced by pressure. **Fatigue** may exacerbate errors, but the primary driver is pressure, as evidenced by the WIP-complexity table showing high error rates specifically under high WIP.

**Resource Heterogeneity and Batching**
Experience moderates these effects. Seniors maintain quality better under pressure (error 4.9% vs. 12.4% for Juniors at high WIP), but even seniors degrade. **Batching** distorts late-shift metrics: Juniors batch Document Review, and Quality Checks are deferred, creating artificial service time drops and error bursts. **Automation** introduces risk: Bot_Risk corrections increase under high WIP, suggesting bot outputs may be less reliable or harder to verify under pressure.

**Conclusion:** Service time falls due to complexity reduction (early) and rushing (late). Errors rise late due to queue pressure, not fatigue alone. Early errors are elevated by complexity. The system suffers from a **pressure-induced quality collapse** in late shifts, masked by batching and exacerbated by automation interactions.

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

**Worker-Level Conclusions are Misleading:** Raw error rates conflate individual performance with systemic factors. A junior with 12.4% error rate under high WIP is facing a **structural risk zone**, not necessarily incompetence. Penalizing workers based on unadjusted metrics is **unfair** and ignores **selection bias** (seniors take hard cases) and **queue pressure** (management-driven backlog).

**Selection Bias:** Seniors self-select complex cases early, making early-shift error rates appear better than they are for the population. Juniors may be assigned easier cases, masking their potential errors on complex work. Metrics must be **complexity-adjusted**.

**Queue Pressure and Management Responsibility:** Late-shift errors are driven by WIP buildup, often caused by demand peaks or handover inefficiencies. Blaming workers for errors under pressure shifts responsibility from **process design** to **individuals**. Management must address root causes of backlog.

**Automation Quality:** Bot_Risk corrections increase under pressure, suggesting bot outputs may be less reliable or harder to verify. Workers correcting bot errors are not "making errors"; they are **mitigating automation failures**. Error attribution must distinguish human vs. bot origins.

**Measurement Error:** Batching distorts service times; delayed QC masks errors. **Rework within 7 days** is a better quality indicator than immediate error flags. Metrics should account for **downstream corrections**.

**Punitive Use:** Using error rates for punishment creates a **chilling effect**, encouraging workers to hide errors or avoid complex cases, degrading overall quality. Performance management should focus on **support and process improvement**, not blame.

# 6. Monitoring dashboard specification

A process-mining dashboard should provide real-time, context-aware metrics to support decision-making and fairness.

1. **Complexity-Adjusted Service Time:** Mean service time normalized by `case_complexity_score` and `priority`. Reveals true efficiency independent of case mix.
2. **WIP-Stratified Error Rate:** Error rate by `wip_at_start` bins (Low/Med/High). Highlights pressure-induced quality risks.
3. **Queue Pressure Index:** Composite metric of `queue_length_at_start`, `wip_at_start`, and arrival rate. Triggers alerts when thresholds exceeded.
4. **Rework Rate within 7 Days:** Percentage of cases requiring rework, indicating downstream quality issues. More reliable than immediate error flags.
5. **Batching Detection Score:** Frequency of sequential batching events, especially for Document Review and QC. Identifies workarounds distorting metrics.
6. **Resource Experience vs. Complexity Match:** Ratio of complex cases assigned to each experience group. Monitors fairness and selection bias.
7. **Handover Quality Score:** Error rate and complexity of cases transferred between shifts. Assesses backlog management and shift transitions.
8. **Automation Correction Rate:** Percentage of Bot_Risk classifications corrected by humans, stratified by WIP. Monitors bot reliability under pressure.

**Dashboard Features:**
- **Real-time updates** with event log streaming.
- **Drill-down capabilities** to resource, activity, and shift level.
- **Contextual alerts** for high-risk zones (e.g., High WIP + High Complexity).
- **Fairness indicators** to ensure metrics are not used punitively.
- **Trend analysis** over time to evaluate intervention impact.

This dashboard enables **evidence-based management**, focusing on process improvement and worker support rather than blame.
