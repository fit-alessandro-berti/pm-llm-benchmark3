# Repair Analysis for OCEL 2.0 Dataset

## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

### Hard invariants
- **E2O reflexivity:** Every event must relate to at least one object of the type(s) implied by its activity.
- **O2O directionality:** A relation is directional; reversing it creates a different assertion.
- **Object type consistency:** An object has one immutable type (Order, Item, Package, Delivery, Invoice, Payment).
- **No duplicate E2O:** An event–object pair may appear at most once (though a qualifier may vary).

### Strong defaults
- `Create Order`  exactly one Order object (ev01, ev02 follow this; ev01 and ev02 do not clash).
- `Pack Item`  one Item, one Package (ev06, ev07 follow; ev08 is incomplete).
- `Dispatch Package`  one Package, one Delivery (ev09 matches, ev10 lacks a Delivery link).
- `Record Payment`  one Payment, normally one Invoice (ev13 follows; ev14 lacks an Invoice target).
- O2O relations reflect business structure: `contains`, `packs`, `carries`, `bills`, `settles`.

### Weak heuristics
- Matching **identifier prefix** (e.g., "N-42" in ev11 vs. "N-420", "N-421") suggests relatedness but does not determine identity.
- **Timestamp proximity** and **amount matching** (100, 130) suggest intent but do not prove causality.
- **Route matching** (NORTH) supports but does not enforce co-delivery.
- **Customer cohesion:** If ORD-A and ORD-B share a customer and invoice, they may be billed together, but arithmetic mismatch (100 + 130  230 exactly) allows multiple interpretations.

### Evidence against a repair
- **Cardinality violation:** If an item is packed into two packages (e.g., IT-A1 in both PK-7 and another), lifecycle becomes incoherent.
- **Delivery-to-order aliasing:** If ev11 confirms DEL-X when only DEL-Y was dispatched for ORD-B, causal flow breaks.
- **Invoice double-billing:** If PAY-2 (130) is matched to INV-44 (230), a partial-payment interpretation is forced; if also matched to a phantom invoice for ORD-B, object scope explodes.
- **Temporal inversion:** A delivery confirmation (ev11) before all dispatches (ev10 lacks a delivery) is plausible but weakens causal narrative.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

### **Bundle A: Conservative minimum-change repair**

**Objective:** Preserve existing relations, fill only high-confidence gaps, leave structural ambiguities unresolved.

- **ev08  PK-8:** **REJECT.** Domain rule 3 allows incomplete pack events. Mark as reconciliation-pending rather than forcing a package link.  
  - **Justification:** No explicit scanner_ref for PK-8 in ev08; domain semantics allow later reconciliation.

- **ev10  DEL-Y:** **PROVISIONAL (0.70).** Accept based on carrier_ref prefix match and route consistency, but flag as uncertain.  
  - **Justification:** "N-421?" suggests manual data entry; ev10 has no explicit confirmation. Alternative: leave dispatch unmatched to a delivery (orphan event).

- **ev11 confirmation:** **PRIMARY: DEL-X (0.80); SECONDARY: DEL-Y unresolved (0.40).**  
  - **Justification:** "N-42*" prefix closer to "N-420" (DEL-X); temporal alignment (both 10:00 dispatch). Do not force joint confirmation unless ORD-B  PK-8  DEL-Y chain is independently verified.

- **INV-44  ORD-B:** **REJECT.** Standard invoicing ties one invoice per order or explicitly groups them in event activity.  
  - **Justification:** `Issue Invoice` event (ev12) mentions only ORD-A; ORD-B would need a separate event or explicit mention. Arithmetic matching is weak heuristic (rule 6).

- **PAY-2  INV-44:** **REJECT.** No explicit E2O in ev14; allow as unapplied cash.  
  - **Justification:** PAY-2 amount (130) matches ORD-B.declared_value; PAY-1 (100) matches ORD-A. This mirrors amounts but does not prove intent. PAY-2 could be a deposit, advance, or partial payment for a future transaction.

**Bundle A lifecycle:** ORD-A is complete (created, items added, packed, dispatched, delivered, invoiced, partially paid). ORD-B is orphaned: items added and packed (via ev08 reconciliation flag), dispatched (to unknown delivery), never confirmed, never invoiced. PAY-2 is unapplied. This preserves data integrity but leaves business process incomplete for ORD-B.

### **Bundle B: Joint-order and payment interpretation**

**Objective:** Assume ORD-A and ORD-B are a single logical transaction, issued as one invoice and settled by two payments.

- **ev08  PK-8:** **ACCEPT (0.85).** Reconcile IT-B1 into PK-8; assume scanner outage delayed the E2O link.  
  - **Justification:** Weight 2.9 kg (PK-8) + 3.1 kg (PK-7) = 6.0 kg total, reasonable for two SKUs. If IT-B1 (cable) is light, it fits PK-8.

- **ev10  DEL-Y:** **ACCEPT (0.80).** Dispatch PK-8 via DEL-Y.  
  - **Justification:** Carrier ref "N-421" is distinct from "N-420"; bulk dispatch rule 4 permits. Both NORTH route.

- **ev11 confirmation:** **JOINT: DEL-X and DEL-Y (0.75).** The wildcard "N-42*" is intentionally ambiguous and refers to the NORTH route cohort.  
  - **Justification:** ev11 mentions portal_customer=C17 (generic); portal loses carrier detail in high-volume confirmations. Confirm both deliveries as a unit.

- **INV-44  ORD-B:** **ACCEPT (0.70).** Single invoice for both orders, issued after both are packed and dispatched.  
  - **Justification:** Same customer, same timestamp, single invoice object. Arithmetic 100 + 130 = 230 is corroborating (not proof). Standard in e-commerce for order batches.

- **PAY-2  INV-44:** **ACCEPT (0.75).** PAY-1 (100) + PAY-2 (130) settle INV-44 (230) fully.  
  - **Justification:** Total reconstruction; amounts partition cleanly. PAY-1 could settle ORD-A portion; PAY-2 settles ORD-B portion.

**Bundle B lifecycle:** ORD-A and ORD-B are co-shipped via DEL-X and DEL-Y on the same day, issued under one invoice and settled by two payments. This is a coherent, complete lifecycle. Risk: assumes that the data-quality glitches (missing E2O, wildcards) are cosmetic rather than structural gaps.

### **Bundle C: Partial-Invoice alternative (low confidence)**

**Objective:** Compromise between A and B; assume ORD-B exists but is invoiced separately (missing event).

- **ev08  PK-8:** **ACCEPT (0.80).** Same as B.
- **ev10  DEL-Y:** **ACCEPT (0.75).** Same as B.
- **ev11 confirmation:** **PRIMARY: DEL-X (0.85); SECONDARY: DEL-Y unresolved (0.35).**  
  - **Justification:** No joint confirmation; each order confirmed independently via its delivery.
- **INV-44  ORD-B:** **REJECT.** ORD-B is invoiced separately (missing event INV-45).
- **PAY-2  INV-44:** **REJECT.** PAY-2 settles ORD-B's missing invoice; PAY-1 settles INV-44 for ORD-A.

**Consequence:** Creates an implicit invoice object (INV-45) and implicit event (Issue Invoice for ORD-B). Less parsimonious but avoids forcing arithmetic reasoning. Not pursued further here due to invention of implicit objects.

Then uses `INV-44 bills ORD-B` to justify `PAY-2 settles INV-44`. **Flagged as circular reasoning in output; confidence set to 0.70–0.75 to reflect uncertainty.**
- **Bundle A:** Avoids this by leaving PAY-2 and INV-44  ORD-B unresolved. No circularity.

def validate_integrity(events_df, objects_df, relations_df, o2o_df, bundle_name=""):
    """Perform basic referential-integrity checks."""
    
    print(f"\n===== Integrity Check for {bundle_name} =====")
    
    # Check: All events in relations exist in events
    events_set = set(events_df['ocel:eid'].values)
    oids_in_relations = set(relations_df['ocel:eid'].values)
    orphan_events_in_relations = oids_in_relations - events_set
    if orphan_events_in_relations:
        print(f"WARNING: Events in relations but not in events table: {orphan_events_in_relations}")
    
    # Check: All objects in relations exist in objects
    objects_set = set(objects_df['ocel:oid'].values)
    oids_in_relations_obj = set(relations_df['ocel:oid'].values)
    orphan_objects_in_relations = oids_in_relations_obj - objects_set
    if orphan_objects_in_relations:
        print(f"WARNING: Objects in relations but not in objects table: {orphan_objects_in_relations}")
    
    # Check for duplicates in relations
    relations_dedup = relations_df[['ocel:eid', 'ocel:oid', 'ocel:qualifier']].drop_duplicates()
    if len(relations_dedup) < len(relations_df):
        print(f"WARNING: Duplicate E2O relations detected: {len(relations_df) - len(relations_dedup)} duplicates.")
    
    # Check: All objects in o2o exist in objects
    oids_in_o2o_1 = set(o2o_df['ocel:oid'].values)
    oids_in_o2o_2 = set(o2o_df['ocel:oid_2'].values)
    oids_in_o2o = oids_in_o2o_1 | oids_in_o2o_2
    orphan_objects_in_o2o = oids_in_o2o - objects_set
    if orphan_objects_in_o2o:
        print(f"WARNING: Objects in o2o but not in objects table: {orphan_objects_in_o2o}")
    
    # Check for duplicates in o2o
    o2o_dedup = o2o_df[['ocel:oid', 'ocel:oid_2', 'ocel:qualifier']].drop_duplicates()
    if len(o2o_dedup) < len(o2o_df):
        print(f"WARNING: Duplicate O2O relations detected: {len(o2o_df) - len(o2o_dedup)} duplicates.")
    
    print(f" Referential integrity check passed (with warnings noted above).")
    return True

    # For PM4Py OCEL constructor (version-dependent):

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Arithmetic-driven false causality:**  
   Matching PAY-2 (130) to ORD-B (100) and PAY-1 (100) to ORD-A (130) creates a spurious payment partition that may not reflect actual payment allocation. E.g., a single payment might cover multiple orders, or a payment might be held in suspense. Over-repair introduces phantom business logic not supported by event attributes.

2. **Wildcard prefix matching creates spurious delivery joins:**  
   Interpreting "N-42*" in ev11 as both DEL-X (N-420) and DEL-Y (N-421) forces a joint-confirmation semantics that isn't explicit in the data. A more likely scenario is that ev11 confirms one delivery, and the "*" is simply a data-entry artifact. Over-repair merges two independent logistics flows.

3. **Scanner-outage reconciliation becomes permanent packing:**  
   Domain rule 3 permits incomplete Pack Item events due to scanner outages, with reconciliation deferred. Over-repairing by permanently adding `PK-8 packs IT-B1` assumes the reconciliation is complete, but the original log provides no evidence that it happened. This creates false confidence in pack-level traceability.

4. **One invoice for two orders hides billing governance:**  
   In standard e-commerce, one invoice per order is the norm. Forcing INV-44 to bill both ORD-A and ORD-B because their declared values sum to the invoice total obscures whether this is a business rule (e.g., customer discounts, bundling) or a data anomaly. Over-repair erases the signal of an unusual invoice pattern.

5. **Circular arithmetic validation:**  
   Using total(PAY-1, PAY-2) = INV-44.total to infer that PAY-2 settles INV-44 is circular: if PAY-1 and PAY-2 are separate deposits, their sum could be coincidental. Then treating this inference as independent evidence that "the data is coherent" creates false confidence in Bundle B. Over-repair bootstraps a self-referential validation loop.

6. **Delivery orphaning as acceptable process variance:**  
   Bundle A leaves DEL-Y unconfirmed, suggesting that packages can be dispatched but never confirmed. While possible (e.g., failed delivery, customer pickup), normalizing this as an acceptable outcome masks potential process-control failures. Over-repairing by confirming DEL-Y forces a cleaner log but may hide real operational issues.

7. **Flattening amplifies unresolved cardinality:**
