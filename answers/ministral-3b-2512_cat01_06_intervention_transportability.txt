### **6. Candidate Site B intervention**
**Process Specification**:
- **Eligibility**: All cases where `Originals Receipt` is **not** required (38% digital claims).
- **Concurrent Tasks**:
  - `Identity Check` and `Document Check` may start **after** `Register` and **before** `Originals Receipt` (if applicable).
  - `Assessment` may start **after** both `Identity Check` and `Document Check` are complete.
- **Statutory Prerequisites**:
  - `Originals Receipt` must occur **before** `Document Check` if required (62% cases).
  - `Legal Review` is mandatory for 40% of cases and must follow `Assessment`.
- **Queue/WIP Controls**:
  - Cap WIP per reviewer (e.g., 3 tasks per identity/document reviewer).
  - Enforce `Originals Receipt` delay (18h median) as a sequential gate.
- **Resource Responsibilities**:
  - Identity reviewers: `Register`  `Identity Check`.
  - Document reviewers: `Originals Receipt`  `Document Check` (if applicable).
  - Assessors: `Document Check`  `Assessment`.
  - Legal reviewers: `Assessment`  `Legal Review` (with supplement loop).
- **Handling Legal Review**:
  - If `Legal Review` rejects or requests supplements, `Document Check` must restart.
- **Logging Requirements**:
  - Track timestamps for `Originals Receipt`, `Document Check` start/end, and `Assessment` delays.

### **9. Recommendation**
**`conditional go`**

**What to transfer**:
- The **core idea of parallel verification** for cases where `Originals Receipt` is not required (38% digital claims). This aligns with Site A’s evidence and avoids legal constraints.
- **Queue discipline improvements** (e.g., daily huddles) if Site B can replicate them.

- Copying the **full parallelization** for all cases, as it violates legal requirements for 62% of cases.
- Assuming **cross-trained reviewers** will scale to Site B’s siloed teams. Instead, **role-specific parallelism** (e.g., digital claims use cross-trained reviewers; physical claims use specialized teams).

**Subgroup entry**:
- **Pilot digital claims (38%) first**, then high-complexity cases (48%), to test robustness.
- **Monitor legal review bottlenecks** in the full rollout.

**Expected benefits**:
- **Faster cycle time** for digital claims (median reduction of 8–10h, p90 reduction of 15–20h).
- **Lower waiting time** before assessment (median reduction of 5–8h).
- **No increase in rework** for digital claims (Site A’s evidence suggests parallelism is stable).

**Expected risks**:
- **Tail delays may worsen** if legal review or assessment becomes the bottleneck (Scenario 3).
- **Capacity constraints** could overwhelm reviewers if WIP limits are not enforced.
- **Physical claims (62%) will not benefit**, requiring separate optimization.

**Evidence needed to expand**:
- **Pilot results**: If digital claims show a 10h median improvement with <15% rework increase, expand to high-complexity cases.
- **Legal review analysis**: If `Assessment  Legal Review` delay becomes the bottleneck, adjust the POWL to include legal review as a sequential gate.

**Evidence to abandon**:
- If the pilot shows **no improvement in median cycle time** for digital claims or **rework increases by >20%**, abandon parallelism for digital claims and focus on queue discipline or capacity scaling.

### **10. Assumption register**

      - *Why*: Site A’s improvement was driven by digital cases (38% of Site B).
      - *Test*: Pilot digital claims (38%) first; compare median cycle time vs. physical claims.
      - *Consequence*: If digital claims show no improvement, abandon parallelism.

2. **`Originals Receipt delay is deterministic`**
   - *Why*: Site B’s 18h median delay is legally enforced; parallelism must wait.
   - *Test*: Audit `OriginalsReceipt` timestamps; model delay as a sequential gate.
   - *Consequence*: If delays vary widely, add stochastic delays to the POWL.

3. **`Reviewers can handle parallelism without deadlocks`**
   - *Why*: Site A’s no-lock assumption may fail in Site B’s legacy system.
   - *Test*: Monitor task ownership logs; enforce WIP limits per reviewer.
   - *Consequence*: If deadlocks occur, add `lock` constraints to the POWL.

4. **`Legal review is a bottleneck only for 40% of cases`**
   - *Why*: Site B’s 40% legal cases may mask parallelism’s benefits.
   - *Test*: Compare `Assessment`  `Legal Review` delays vs. `Document Check`  `Assessment` delays.
   - *Consequence*: If legal review is slow, add it to the partial order.

5. **`Case complexity does not increase rework`**
   - *Why*: Site A’s complexity mix shifted post-intervention.
   - *Test*: Segment rework by complexity; ensure no increase in `Assessment`  `Reject` loops.
   - *Consequence*: If rework rises, parallelism may not be suitable for complex cases.

   - *Why*: Site A’s overlap metrics may reflect better logging.

7. **`Resource capacity is fixed and sufficient`**

   - *Test*: Compare reviewer WIP vs. capacity; add constraints to the POWL.
   - *Consequence*: If capacity is exceeded, add queue discipline or hire more reviewers.

   - *Why*: Data-dependent rules (e.g., "high-value cases require legal review") are not encoded.
   - *Test*: Model legal requirements as conditional gates in the POWL.
   - *Consequence*: If rules change, the model must be updated dynamically.
