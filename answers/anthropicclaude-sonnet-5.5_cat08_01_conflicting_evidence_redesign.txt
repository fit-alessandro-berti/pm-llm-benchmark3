# 2. Redesign A: Risk-tiered triage with a guarded fast lane and a review-first high-value lane

**Flow**
1. Intake  a **segment/value/defect classification step** (claim value vs EUR 400, device class, Premium/Standard, defect type, evidence-complete flag). This replaces the current outdated, non-segment-aware auto rules.
2. **Lane 1, low-risk accessories with complete evidence and no risk flag:** Auto eligibility  Auto approval  automated part-compatibility check (no human step)  Dispatch  Close, with post-dispatch sampling audit.
   - Premium uses the R2 exception.
   - Standard dispatches only when evidence completeness is auto-confirmed.
3. **Lane 2, high-value devices (>EUR 400 or device class high-value):** auto screen never approves. It triggers a **parallel combined review**: eligibility review and risk scoring run at the same time, with the fraud specialist pulled in only on risk triggers. Part-price confirmation and the replace-vs-repair recommendation are prepared concurrently. Then Technical decision  Fulfillment. R1 is satisfied by construction.
4. **Lane 3, unclear defect or missing evidence:** goes to the existing evidence loop, or to Redesign B if adopted.
5. **Queue policy:**
   - Borderline Premium accessory cases get a priority queue with a 24h timer.
   - Borderline Standard accessory cases go to a low-priority queue with proactive status messages, since ~52h is tolerable.
   - A reserved reviewer pool serves Premium high-value devices.

**Targets**
- Premium and Standard high-value devices (35% of cases), where fast-path complaints are 18–22% and R1/R2 are at risk.
- Accessories (43% of cases), where the fast path should be protected.
- The slow Manual review  Repair quote edge for Premium, through parallel part-price confirmation.

**Mechanism**
- Control intensity is matched to loss exposure: EUR 410 wrong-replacement cost and high compliance risk for high-value devices, EUR 95 for accessories.
- It keeps the 8h/13h accessory speed. For high-value devices it replaces "skip review" with "review that runs in parallel and is shorter than today's 96–120h reviewed path". I would treat 40–72h as a hypothesis to test, not a promise.
- It removes the R1 violations and the fast-path complaint excess where they are largest.

**Sacrifices and leaves unresolved**
- It deliberately gives up the 20–28h high-value fast path.
- Updated auto rules could raise manual inflow by 15–35%, which is about +210 to +490 cases on the 1,400 direct manual entries. That is dangerous near the 82% utilization cliff.
- It increases fraud specialist load.
- It does not fix the evidence loops or the wrong-part error beyond the automated compatibility check.
- It does not fix reopen handling.
- It creates a rule-governance burden, and mid-risk devices need an explicit lane assignment.

# 3. Redesign B: Evidence-first intake and closed-loop evidence resolution

**Flow**
1. Intake presents a **defect-type-specific evidence checklist** (photos, serial number, proof of purchase). This runs through the portal or an **assisted channel** (agent-guided upload, callback) for customers with low digital literacy.
2. An automatic **completeness check at intake** gates the case before any eligibility decision. Incomplete cases get **one consolidated request** with a deadline, reminders and a visible status tracker, not sequential piecemeal requests.
3. On evidence return, the case goes to an **automatic re-screen**, not to Manual eligibility review. Manual review happens only on rule failure or a risk trigger.
4. Unclear defects get a **short guided diagnostic triage** (structured symptom questions, technician-assisted for ambiguous cases) that assigns a defect class before eligibility is decided.
5. Downstream decision logic stays as today, but R1/R2 checks become explicit preconditions: no Fast approval without the evidence-complete flag.

**Targets**
- The unclear-defect/missing-evidence segment (22% of cases, median 140h, highest abandonment).
- The 1,430 evidence requests, including the 890 returns to manual review (mean 34h, p90 88h) and the 320 abandonments.
- The 26% of cases that request evidence repeatedly.

**Mechanism**
- Fewer, better-specified requests cut repeat loops. The portal evidence suggests a 30–45% reduction in repeated requests. On ~370 cases with two or more requests, that is on the order of 110–170 fewer, as a directional estimate.
- Replacing the return-to-manual-review hop removes the largest single wait in the process.
- It **frees reviewer capacity**: about a third of manual review entries are evidence returns. That headroom is what makes Redesign A's extra inflow survivable.
- It fixes the R2 "dispatch before evidence completeness" deviation upstream.

**Sacrifices and leaves unresolved**
- It does nothing about the 14.1% fast-path complaint rate, wrong-part errors or post-close reopens.
- The portal may **increase abandonment for low-digital-literacy customers**, and assisted channels cost money.
- Auto re-screen on return is faster but weaker than human review, which could let through poor evidence.
- High-value R1 compliance is only partly addressed.
- Benefits are concentrated in 22% of cases, so the effect on overall average cycle time will look modest even if the segment improves a lot.

# 4. Redesign C: Verify-before-ship and recover-after-close control layer

**Flow**
1. Front-end routing stays largely as today, including fast approval for low-risk cases.
2. After approval and before dispatch, a **replacement specification gate** is added:
   - **Automated** part-compatibility/serial-to-part validation for all replacements (minutes, no human).
   - **Human verification by the underused inspector pool** for high-value devices, run in parallel with pick/pack.
   - **Sampled** human checks for Standard accessories.
   - The gate also acts as a **hard stop on R1/R2 preconditions** (review flag present, evidence complete). Failures are routed back to review instead of shipping.
3. Dispatch  **provisional close**  delivery/fit confirmation and a proactive check-in  final close after confirmation or a window of roughly 7–8 days. That window is justified by the complaint lag (p90 150–180h).
4. **Reopen handling:** complaints are root-cause coded (wrong part, defect not resolved, evidence dispute) and go to a pre-populated Manual eligibility review (R3 satisfied). Bounded, pre-authorized remedies (for example, corrected-part reshipment up to a threshold) apply for clear wrong-part cases. The Escalation board is reserved for contested or high-value exceptions.
5. **Repair side:** for high-value repairs, the Quality check gets a strengthened checklist. Rework repair is 12.5% for high-value devices versus 4.0% for low-risk ones.

**Targets**
- The Dispatch  Complaint long tail (260 events, mean 96h) and the Fast approval  Complaint edge.
- The premature close that causes "complaint after close".
- The 310 reopens sent to manual review (mean 40h) and the 120 escalations (mean 54h).
- Standard high-value devices, where wrong replacement is expensive, and the Premium high-value R1/R2 exposure.

**Mechanism**
- It prevents the expensive error (wrong part) at the cheapest point, using idle inspector capacity. It does not add load to reviewers or fraud specialists.
- The gate cost of 6–18h is small compared with the 70–100h gap between the fast path and the reviewed path for high-value devices. It buys a large share of the complaint reduction while keeping a 26–38h-type cycle, which is hypothesis-level.
- Faster, delegated recovery shortens the pain of the failures that do occur.
- It turns hidden post-close failures into an explicit, measurable state.

**Sacrifices and leaves unresolved**
- Human gating on Premium accessories would risk the 24h threshold (8h + up to 18h), so it is automated-only there.
- It does not cure upstream decision quality: high-value fast approvals still happen and are caught late.
- It does not touch evidence loops.
- Provisional close lengthens measured cycle time and complicates KPIs.
- Delegated remedies need governance.
- Gate cost is added to the 9% residual savings.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A**
- *Failure 1:* the rule update floods manual review (+210 to +490 cases on the 1,400 baseline). Utilization passes the ~82% cliff, and the Premium accessory borderline queue breaks the 24h credit threshold.
- *Failure 2:* misclassification and gaming. Mid-risk devices fall between lanes, and claimants split or describe claims to fit Lane 1, which raises fraud exposure once rules become predictable.
- *Failure 3:* the reserved Premium pool starves Standard high-value cases, whose median was already 120h.
- *Bottleneck migration:* Manual eligibility review and the two Fraud/risk specialists (Monday and campaign peaks), then Repair quote part-price confirmation.

**Redesign B**
- *Failure 1:* low-digital-literacy customers drop out, so abandonment rises beyond the current 22% at request, or before the claim is even created. The latter is invisible in the event log.
- *Failure 2:* auto re-screen admits incomplete or weak evidence, which then shows up as later complaints or fraud.
- *Failure 3:* long checklists deter legitimate claims.
- *Bottleneck migration:* faster evidence resolution releases a wave of cases to decision steps at the same time, so Manual review, Fraud/risk review and Repair quote get spikes. The assisted channel and diagnostic triage staff also become bottlenecks.

**Redesign C**
- *Failure 1:* the gate becomes a rubber stamp or an 18h delay. Either it fails to reduce wrong parts, or it breaks Premium expectations.
- *Failure 2:* provisional close inflates work in progress and irritates customers with check-ins. Teams may also game the confirmation signal.
- *Failure 3:* delegated remedy authority is abused or inconsistent.
- *Bottleneck migration:* gate failures and R1/R2 hard stops route cases back to review, shifting load to Manual review and Fraud/risk. Inspector capacity collides with post-repair-campaign peaks, and the Escalation board slot stays a limit for contested cases.

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**Stage 0 (weeks 0–2): fix the evidence and add guardrails**
- Reconcile the log inconsistencies: dispatch in/out counts, manual review flows, and complaint event versus case counts.
- Characterize the missing mid-risk devices.
- Run the new auto-screen rules **in shadow mode** only, to measure the real inflow shift without changing routing.
- Apply an immediate compliance guardrail for high-value devices (no Fast approval without review). This is a control, not an experiment.

**Stage 1: first pilot, the verify-before-ship gate for Standard high-value replacements (C)**
- Why first:
  - Wrong replacement costs EUR 410.
  - Complaints are 18%, against 6% on the reviewed path.
  - The segment tolerates added hours better than Premium.
  - It uses idle inspector capacity and adds no load to manual review.
- Design: alternate by case or week against a holdout, with automated-only gating for accessories tested separately.

**Stage 2 (parallel, disjoint segments): evidence-first pilot (B)**
- Start with unclear-defect and Standard accessory cases, stratified by digital literacy and with an assisted-channel arm.

**Stage 3: live Redesign A, gated by manual-review headroom**
- Start only after B shows freed capacity and manual utilization sits comfortably below the 82% cliff.
- Ramp in steps: Lane 1 accessories first (Premium, then Standard), then the review-first high-value lane, increasing manual inflow in small increments.

**Stage 4: combine, then expand C to Premium high-value and to the reopen and escalation redesign, and re-mine.**

- Wait for complaint maturity. With p90 reopen lag of about 150–180h, no pilot can be judged before roughly 8+ days of follow-up per cohort.
- Track cohorts to final outcome, including abandonment and denial (treat abandonment as censored, not as a success), and measure cost per closed case including credits, reshipments and rework.
- Watch adjacent queues (manual review, fraud, quote, board) against a holdout, and control for Monday and campaign arrival effects.
- Re-mine the new log to check for new deviant variants, loop counts and precision, to confirm the model is not just producing new policy-violating paths.
- Report both **provisional** and **final** close times so redesign C does not look slower or faster just because of its definition.
- Weight outcomes by segment share. An average-cycle-time gain hidden by a high-value quality loss does not count.
