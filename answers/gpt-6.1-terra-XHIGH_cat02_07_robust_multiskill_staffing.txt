# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

Use elapsed time from arrival to completed Validation for the SLA clock; it continues through breaks, overnight and outages. A case is complete only after every required diagnostic branch and Validation finish. P1, P2 and P3 are separate denominators; the targets are respectively 90% within 120, 480 and 1,440 minutes. Report both the mean and p90/p95 of elapsed case time, by priority and type. Waiting means elapsed time excluding active work, with special care for parallel activities; define queue waiting separately as assignment/start minus queue entry.

For calculations, assume independent uniform draws within each ordinary or tail interval and independent tail indicators by activity and case. These are assumptions, not measured distributions. Thus mean base minutes for Intake, Mechanical, Electrical, Software and Validation are respectively **6.94, 26.30, 32.82, 38.50 and 15.74**. Bounds and tail dependence can materially change results. Priority and case type are assumed independent within each block; expected arrivals are fractional workloads, not a claim about a particular realization. For U1, increase P1 by eight percentage points and reduce P2 and P3 proportionally. For U2, interpret renormalization as keeping the block total fixed after doubling the E and ES weights; if instead those actual case counts double, total arrivals and overload are higher. No arrivals outside 08:00–20:00 are specified; unfinished cases wait until the next available shift. The starting backlog is assumed zero. Neither overnight arrivals nor weekend calendars are specified.

Treat handover, team transfer and parallel coordination as non-resource-consuming elapsed delays; setup and restart consume the assigned resource. Apply the 7-minute handover to each change along a case's realized task-to-task path, plus 5 minutes when crossing the mechanical versus electrical/software team boundary unless retained by one cross-skilled person. For parallel branches, explicitly record the handovers to and from both branches; the exact convention for simultaneous transfers needs agreement. A restart adds 10% of remaining active service, with at most one interruption per case. Shifts and breaks are hard stops; assume service can pause and resume without a restart penalty at a scheduled boundary, unlike a priority preemption. Overtime is optional and capped at 30 minutes per person, not assumed available as routine capacity.

Decisions are which qualified resource takes each stage, priority ordering, whether to reserve capacity, whether and when to preempt, and whether to run ME/ES branches in parallel. Estimate the finish of sequential and parallel options from current queue, remaining service, speed, transfer/setup and coordination delays; parallelize when it materially improves predicted completion or protects an SLA without exhausting scarce skills. Prediction is not a guarantee.

Two useful objectives are:

1. **SLA/tail objective:** minimize the worst scenario's weighted priority SLA shortfall, for example `max_s [10 max(0, .90 − P1_s) + 3 max(0, .90 − P2_s) + max(0, .90 − P3_s)]`, with p95 elapsed time as a tie-breaker. Report uncertainty for these rates rather than assuming them from capacity.
2. **Robustness/fairness objective:** lexicographically minimize worst-scenario P1 shortfall, then the maximum scenario backlog or p95, then the largest individual utilization above 90% in base and disparity in utilization across available staff. Include handover/setup burden and total overtime as costs. A weighted-sum alternative must state weights and may trade away P1 protection; no weights are given by the prompt.

# 3. Candidate policies

All policies use qualification, active shift and break checks. Within a priority class order by earliest SLA deadline then queue-entry time and case ID as a deterministic tie-break. Never interrupt Intake or Validation, and never preempt a case twice. Log every override and the prediction used for it.

**Specialization with fixed queues.** Intake: Ada, Ben, Deepa, Farah; Mechanical: Ada, Ben, Farah; Electrical: Chen, Deepa; Software: Chen, Emil; Validation: Ben, Deepa, Emil, Farah, subject to actual qualification and availability. Each resource serves the highest-priority eligible fixed queue; use a published fixed stage precedence when a person has multiple queues. No priority preemption: a P1 waits for the next free eligible person. Run both branches in parallel if both assigned queues can start immediately; otherwise sequentially only if a fixed-queue resource is qualified for both. Transfer cases through the fixed queues. If no designated eligible person is scheduled, leave the job queued and escalate; do not silently assign an unqualified person. This is predictable and reduces family switching, but has severe gaps, including late Mechanical.

**Full pooling.** Put all ready stages into one dispatcher and permit any qualified on-shift resource. Rank P1 before P2 before P3, then earliest deadline. Among resources choose the earliest predicted stage finish including multiplier, setup and handover, breaking ties by lower projected utilization and then name. For a ready two-branch case compare predicted case finish under parallel versus feasible sequential execution, including the extra 8-minute coordination and the transfers; choose the earlier option. If a P1 cannot start immediately and would otherwise miss its predicted deadline, preempt an eligible lower-priority diagnostic only when estimated P1 completion improves after accounting for the 10% remaining-work restart cost; select the interruption with the least penalty. Pooling has no protected capacity, so a burst of P1s may still wait behind non-preemptible stages and scarce skills.

**Hybrid with P1 reservation and controlled overflow.** Maintain the same ranked queues and predictions, with a rolling 15% of available on-shift minutes per resource protected from non-P1 allocation. The dispatcher may lend unused reserved capacity to a non-P1 only for work predicted to finish within 15 minutes or when no P1 is waiting and the resource can be reclaimed for a forecast P1; Intake and Validation cannot be reclaimed mid-task. Prefer a resource already holding the case if its predicted completion is within 10 minutes of the fastest option. Preserve Chen/Emil for Software and Deepa/Chen for Electrical when their projected skill backlog exceeds 30 minutes, unless a higher-priority SLA is at risk. Run branches sequentially on a qualified owner unless parallel execution improves predicted completion by at least 15 minutes or is necessary to protect a P1 deadline; require both resources to be actually available. Use the same controlled preemption rule as pooling. Open cross-skill overflow when a stage's predicted wait exceeds 30 minutes, a P1 is predicted late, or a designated resource is unavailable; close it after projected waits remain below 15 minutes for 30 minutes. Escalate unschedulable work or projected late P1 immediately.

The reservation is a dispatch control, not a promise that 15% of staff capacity suffices for P1, particularly under U1/U5. Practical implementation should also plan coverage after 16:00, including Mechanical after 18:00, and arrange surge/absence cover; routing cannot create missing skills.

# 4. Scenario comparison

The table is a **deterministic capacity approximation**, not a queue simulation or an SLA forecast. The code allocates expected work separately in each two-hour block, optimistically starting every block empty; it allows fractional work to be allocated across resources and ignores stage precedence, within-block arrival timing and backlog. It excludes overtime. Base-minute deficits cannot be translated directly into late cases. The 15% hybrid non-P1 cap is a coarse proxy for reservation, and estimated setup, retention and parallel fractions are illustrative assumptions, not measured policy results. The solver balances the highest modeled block utilization and can produce deceptively equal utilization. Coordination is assumed elapsed minutes per arriving case; it does not include queueing or active setup. The figures are sensitive to those assumptions and should not be interpreted as policy performance estimates.

`D` = summed unallocated base-equivalent minutes across independently solved blocks; `P1 D` = the P1 portion. `Max U` = maximum resource utilization across blocks; `spread` = worst within-block max-minus-min utilization among available resources, in percentage points, a crude fairness proxy. `C` = assumed coordination/transfer minutes per case. Mean delay, p90/p95 delay and P1/P2 SLA are **unknown (—)** in every row. Actual utilization, fairness and resilience depend on backlog, assignment sequence and shift coverage. # 5. Robust recommendation

Use the **hybrid as the primary candidate**, provisionally, with added late and surge coverage. It combines case ownership, skill protection and transparent overflow, and is the strongest candidate for the lexicographic P1/fairness objective; the table does not establish dominance in real mean, tail or SLA outcomes. In particular, the model reports the lowest hybrid deficit in Base/U2/U3/U4 but not U1/U5. The 15% reservation and switch thresholds are initial operating parameters to calibrate, not evidence-based guarantees. Add qualified Mechanical coverage beyond 18:00, and increase general and Electrical/Software capacity from 14:00 onward; quantify staffing using a queue simulation and actual arrivals. Account for handover, breaks and the 30-minute overtime cap in that roster.

Switch to a managed surge mode when any P1 is predicted to miss 120 minutes, projected skill backlog exceeds 60 minutes, or a critical skill has no available worker. Open all qualified overflow, summon prearranged cross-skilled cover, protect Intake and Validation as well as diagnostics, and explicitly prioritize P1 then earliest deadline. If capacity remains inadequate, escalate to incident command and communicate anticipated breaches; do not assume preemption fixes overload. Return to hybrid only after no predicted P1 miss and all skill backlogs below 30 minutes for 60 minutes. Track whether planned base utilization exceeds 90% for any individual and revise the roster rather than simply reassigning pressure.

Specialization may suit stable, low-load periods where measured setup costs and skill quality dominate and all queues have coverage. Full pooling may be preferable during a short heterogeneous burst with reliable predictions and enough general capacity, or when policy testing shows reservation causes unnecessary idle time. Neither is demonstrated superior without case-level validation.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

Service estimates can be gamed to win priority or avoid assignments; calibrate predictions against actual active time by skill, resource and complexity, audit overrides, and retain uncertainty intervals. Strict P1 priority can starve P3: monitor P3 age and approaching 24-hour deadlines, reserve planned catch-up capacity when P1 protection permits, and escalate sustained conflict rather than violating priority silently. Use training rotations and competency checks to prevent skill erosion; avoid repeatedly assigning slower staff only the undesirable cases. Compare fairness over whole shifts and over weeks, including breaks, overtime, case complexity and undesirable hours, not just instantaneous utilization.

Preemption may multiply restart work and destabilize the system; enforce one preemption per case in the case state, record remaining active time and added work, and review preemption rate and P1 time saved. Parallelism can create coordination and handover overhead, compete for scarce simultaneous staff and increase Validation bursts. Monitor both branch completion and join readiness; ensure Validation capacity is protected. Delays attributed to handovers must be measured, not conflated with queue waits. Keep rules, thresholds, tie-breaks and exception reasons visible to operators; test the ability to explain any assignment and provide an authorized manual override with audit trail.

# 7. Monitoring and process-mining design

Instrument every transition with case ID, activity instance and branch ID, case type, priority, arrival and absolute SLA deadline; event time and lifecycle type; queue-entry and ready times; assignment decision, candidates' eligible-resource set, availability and qualification snapshot, prediction and reason/tie-break; assigned resource, skill family, planned/actual service and speed assumption; actual start/end, pause/resume and reason, preemption count, remaining work and restart penalty; setup start/end and prior skill; handover origin/destination and delay, team transfer, parallel fork/join and coordination; completion, policy version, shift/break/overtime and scenario or shock flag. Keep immutable event IDs and timestamps with timezone; log reassignment and policy overrides. Record arrivals during outages as they occur if possible.

Monitor priority SLA attainment with confidence intervals and counts, p50/p90/p95 elapsed time, queue-entry-to-start by stage/skill and priority, branch join and Validation wait, active resource utilization and overtime, workload disparity, backlog age, handovers per case and delay, setups and preemption/restart costs, parallel share, predicted versus actual duration, skill coverage and unserved work. Review separately by time block, case type, priority and shock; use control limits calibrated on baseline history. Preserve censored/incomplete cases in operational dashboards and use an appropriate cohort or survival analysis instead of treating them as successful or dropping them.

With interval logs, PM4Py activity service-time distributions can show service drift, and a handover-of-work network can identify resource transitions and changes in their frequency. Compare filtered logs over time, shift, type and policy; assess directly-follows and discovered/conformance models for missing branches, unexpected routing or altered fork/join behavior. Compute queue-specific waits from explicit queue-entry and actual-start events, join wait from branch completion to Validation readiness, and backlog and utilization from time intervals outside the case-level PM4Py functions. PM4Py's case service/waiting measures describe the whole observed case between logged events and can repeat on each case row; they are not per-stage queues. Parallel overlap, pauses, unlogged arrival-to-first-start, and incomplete cases can make naive service sums or waiting interpretations misleading. A handover network also does not by itself measure the 7- or 5-minute delays or imply causation.

# 8. Reproducible Python

The following deterministic scaffold requires `pandas`, `numpy`, `scipy` and `pm4py`. It solves the approximate workload allocation for all policies/scenarios and prints compact comparisons. Its synthetic interval log is a tiny, deterministic simulated fixture to demonstrate PM4Py, not output from the allocation or a queue simulation. It writes `illustrative_interval_log.csv` in the working directory. The LP does not simulate case routing, actual priority queues, parallel execution, handover timings, pauses, shifts at sub-block resolution, backlog carryover, overtime or SLAs; policy-specific fractions are explicit assumptions and the fixture's sequential cases are illustrative only. Consequently its table supplies no mean/tail delay or SLA estimate. The fixture's inter-event gap includes handover and is not a queue-specific wait.

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
from scipy.optimize import linprog
import pm4py

# Uniform within each range; independently mixed ordinary/tail draws.
service = pd.DataFrame([
    ('Intake',5,8,10,14,.08), ('Mechanical',18,28,35,55,.15),
    ('Electrical',22,35,40,65,.18), ('Software',25,40,45,80,.20),
    ('Validation',10,18,22,35,.12)],
    columns=['skill','lo','hi','tail_lo','tail_hi','p_tail']).set_index('skill')
skills = ['Intake','Mechanical','Electrical','Software','Validation']
resources = ['Ada','Ben','Chen','Deepa','Emil','Farah']
speeds = pd.DataFrame([
    [1,1,1.35,np.nan,1.15], [1.10,1,np.nan,np.nan,1],
    [1.10,np.nan,1,1.25,1.10], [1,1.25,1,np.nan,1],
    [1.10,np.nan,1.30,1,1], [1,1.40,1.40,1.40,1.20]],
    index=resources, columns=skills)
# Times are minutes after midnight; breaks and shifts are half-open.
calendar = {'Ada':(480,960,[(735,765)]), 'Ben':(480,960,[(720,750)]),
    'Chen':(480,960,[(750,780)]), 'Deepa':(600,1080,[(840,870)]),
    'Emil':(720,1200,[(960,990)]), 'Farah':(480,720,[])}
demand = pd.DataFrame([
    (480,7,.10,.55,.35,.30,.25,.15,.20,.10),
    (600,9,.15,.55,.30,.25,.25,.15,.25,.10),
    (720,7,.12,.58,.30,.22,.28,.18,.20,.12),
    (840,10,.18,.52,.30,.20,.30,.18,.20,.12),
    (960,6,.10,.50,.40,.20,.25,.25,.15,.15),
    (1080,3,.05,.45,.50,.25,.20,.30,.10,.15)],
    columns=['start','arrivals','P1','P2','P3','M','E','S','ME','ES'])
scenarios = {'Base':(False,False,False,False,False),
    'U1':(True,False,False,False,False), 'U2':(False,True,False,False,False),
    'U3':(False,False,True,False,False), 'U4':(False,False,False,True,False),
    'U5':(True,False,True,False,True)} # surge, mix, absence, tail, outage
# Fixed diagnostic assignments; an unavailable assignment means unmet load.
fixed = {'Intake':{'Ada','Ben','Deepa','Farah'},
    'Mechanical':{'Ada','Ben','Farah'}, 'Electrical':{'Chen','Deepa'},
    'Software':{'Chen','Emil'}, 'Validation':{'Ben','Deepa','Emil','Farah'}}
# Fractions are explicit modeling assumptions, not observed policy outcomes.
policy = {'Specialist': {'setup_fraction':.10, 'handovers':1.5,
                        'retained':0., 'parallel':.6, 'restart_fraction':0.},
          'Pool': {'setup_fraction':.40, 'handovers':1.7,
                   'retained':.2, 'parallel':.6, 'restart_fraction':.20},
          'Hybrid': {'setup_fraction':.20, 'handovers':.8,
                     'retained':.6, 'parallel':.3, 'restart_fraction':.20}}

def overlap(a,b,c,d):
    return max(0., min(b,d)-max(a,c))

def available(r,start,absent):
    if absent and r == 'Chen': return 0.
    a,b,breaks=calendar[r]
    return overlap(start,start+120,a,b)-sum(overlap(start,start+120,c,d) for c,d in breaks)

def block_data(row, scenario):
    surge,mix,absent,tail,outage=scenarios[scenario]
    n=float(row.arrivals); p1=float(row.P1)
    if surge and row.start < 720:
        n *= 1.7; p1 += .08
    types=row[['M','E','S','ME','ES']].astype(float).copy()
    if mix and 720 <= row.start < 960:
        types[['E','ES']] *= 2
        types /= types.sum() # fixed total arrivals; other interpretation is possible
    st=service.copy()
    if tail: st.loc[['Mechanical','Electrical','Software'],'p_tail'] *= 2
    means=(1-st.p_tail)*(st.lo+st.hi)/2+st.p_tail*(st.tail_lo+st.tail_hi)/2
    counts=pd.Series({'Intake':n,'Mechanical':n*(types.M+types.ME),
        'Electrical':n*(types.E+types.ME+types.ES),
        'Software':n*(types.S+types.ES),'Validation':n})
    return n,p1,types,means,counts,absent,outage

def allocate(row, scenario, pol):
    n,p1,types,means,counts,absent,outage=block_data(row,scenario)
    cfg=policy[pol]; load=counts*means
    # Expected 5-minute setup per modeled switched diagnostic branch.
    setup = pd.Series(0., index=skills)
    for s in skills[1:4]:
        setup[s] = counts[s]*5*cfg['setup_fraction']
        load[s] += setup[s]
    # At most one interruption per case: 20% of P1 arrivals induce one; the
    # interrupted branch has half its service remaining on average. Spread the
    # resulting base-minute work across diagnostic skills in proportion to load.
    restart=n*p1*cfg['restart_fraction']*.1*.5*float(means[skills[1:4]].mean())
    load.loc[skills[1:4]] *= 1+restart/load.loc[skills[1:4]].sum()
    # Setup is five actual minutes, not multiplied by resource speed.
    def coefficient(r, s):
        return 1 + (speeds.loc[r,s]-1)*(load[s]-setup[s])/load[s] if load[s] else 1.
    pairs=[(r,s,p) for r in resources for s in skills for p in ['P1','other']
           if not pd.isna(speeds.loc[r,s]) and available(r,row.start,absent)>0
           and (pol!='Specialist' or r in fixed[s])]
    unmet=[(s,p) for s in skills for p in ['P1','other']]
    nvar=len(pairs)+len(unmet)+1; z=nvar-1
    # Minimize unmet base work, with a greater P1 penalty; then prefer lower
    # max utilization and faster assignments. This is one aggregate LP, not a
    # queue simulation and not an implementation of the full dispatch policy.
    cost=np.zeros(nvar)
    for i,(r,s,p) in enumerate(pairs): cost[i]=.001*coefficient(r,s)
    for j,(s,p) in enumerate(unmet): cost[len(pairs)+j]=1000 if p=='P1' else 100
    cost[z]=.1
    Aeq=[]; beq=[]
    for j,(s,p) in enumerate(unmet):
        v=np.zeros(nvar)
        for i,(r,ss,pp) in enumerate(pairs):
            if s==ss and p==pp: v[i]=1
        v[len(pairs)+j]=1; Aeq.append(v)
        beq.append(load[s]*(p1 if p=='P1' else 1-p1))
    Aub=[]; bub=[]
    for r in resources:
        cap=available(r,row.start,absent)
        if cap<=0: continue
        v=np.zeros(nvar)
        for i,(rr,s,p) in enumerate(pairs):
            if rr==r: v[i]=coefficient(r,s)
        # z bounds utilization; z <= 1 implements normal-shift capacity.
        v[z]=-cap; Aub.append(v); bub.append(0)
        if pol=='Hybrid':
            v2=np.zeros(nvar)
            for i,(rr,s,p) in enumerate(pairs):
                if rr==r and p=='other': v2[i]=coefficient(r,s)
            Aub.append(v2); bub.append(.85*cap)
    if outage and row.start==480:
        # 09:30-10:00 outage: no Intake can be processed then. Aggregate
        # limit, not exact sub-block scheduling, so it is optimistic.
        v=np.zeros(nvar)
        for i,(r,s,p) in enumerate(pairs):
            if s=='Intake': v[i]=coefficient(r,s)
        Aub.append(v)
        bub.append(sum(min(90,available(r,row.start,absent)) for r in resources
                       if not pd.isna(speeds.loc[r,'Intake'])))
    result=linprog(cost,A_ub=np.array(Aub),b_ub=np.array(bub),
        A_eq=np.array(Aeq),b_eq=np.array(beq),
        bounds=[(0,None)]*(nvar-1)+[(0,1)],method='highs')
    if not result.success: raise RuntimeError(result.message)
    x=result.x
    util={}
    for r in resources:
        cap=available(r,row.start,absent)
        if cap:
            util[r]=sum(x[i]*coefficient(r,s) for i,(rr,s,p) in enumerate(pairs) if rr==r)/cap
    deficit=sum(x[len(pairs)+j] for j in range(len(unmet)))
    p1def=sum(x[len(pairs)+j] for j,(s,p) in enumerate(unmet) if p=='P1')
    # Delays do not consume staff in this approximation. Independent of
    # dispatch, queueing, precedence and the feasibility of keeping a case.
    delay=n*(7*cfg['handovers']+5*(types.ME+types.ES)*(1-cfg['retained'])
             +8*(types.ME+types.ES)*cfg['parallel'])
    return {'deficit_base_min':deficit,'P1_deficit_base_min':p1def,
            'max_block_util':max(util.values(),default=0),
            'block_util_range':max(util.values(),default=0)-min(util.values(),default=0),
            'assumed_delay_min':delay,'arrivals':n}
```

# 9. Limits and validation plan

Expected counts, broad service ranges and marginal tail probabilities do not determine arrival clustering, priority/type correlation, within-range shapes, case-level complexity dependence, preemption opportunities, queueing, actual breaks, overtime, rework or future arrivals. The model does not establish P1/P2/P3 attainment, mean or p95 delay, daily individual utilization, actual fairness, or the effect of the outage. It cannot establish that any policy meets the targets, or that added cover of a particular size is sufficient. Uniform assumptions and the fixed-total U2 interpretation should be stress-tested against alternative plausible distributions, correlations and the increased-total interpretation. Accounting conventions for parallel handovers, who performs them, simultaneous transfers, and the meaning of team boundaries must be agreed before implementation.

First obtain timestamped historical arrivals, work intervals and resource calendars; validate completeness, eligibility and recorded pauses. Backtest a calibrated event-driven simulator against held-out periods on case elapsed-time distributions, SLA attainment, queues, resource utilization and handovers, including shifts, non-preemptible stages, correlated tails, parallel joins and backlog across days. Simulate repeated seeds and uncertainty over parameters; report confidence intervals and worst-scenario outcomes. Use paired arrivals and service draws across policies to reduce comparison noise, plus stress tests for absence and outages. Evaluate the 90% base fairness condition using full-shift individual capacity, not maximum block utilization.

Run a shadow dispatcher first and compare its proposed actions with current operations. Then pilot on a small, preselected time window with an operational owner, prearranged backup staff and conservative P1 protection; do not withhold resources from actual P1 cases for experimental balance. Compare matched periods or carefully randomized eligible non-P1 routing while retaining the same P1 safety rules, and capture all overrides. Set stop conditions before rollout: a predicted or actual P1 breach triggers immediate human review and fallback, and sustained excess P1 delay, unsafe workload, or loss of critical-skill coverage pauses the pilot. Review case counts and uncertainty before claiming a 90% target is met; expand only after the observed results and coverage plan support it.
