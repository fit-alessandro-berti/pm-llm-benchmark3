# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1.  **Tail latency and queue variability:** Average cycle time may improve due to East's improvement, while North/South experience longer 90th percentile delays due to central queue variability. `get_service_time` distributions will show heavy tails for central activities.
2.  **High-complexity case degradation:** Routing rules may send high-complexity cases to central queues where they wait longer. Filtering by `case_complexity` may reveal that complex cases have increased cycle times and rework, even if average improves.
3.  **Local knowledge loss and rework shift:** Rework may shift from specialist activities to local activities as local teams lose informal tech support. `get_rework_cases_per_activity` will show rework increasing in `Local Technical Assessment` or `Local Customer Contact`, invisible in aggregate rework rates.
4.  **Fairness and team divergence:** East may benefit significantly while North/South degrade. Average metrics mask this divergence. Filtering by `team` reveals that North/South staff face longer waits and higher reassignment, impacting morale and retention.
5.  **Learning and skill atrophy:** Junior staff in local teams may stop developing technical skills as hard cases are routed away. This is not directly visible in short-term logs but may manifest as increased `reassignment_count` or decreased `local_expertise_flag` over time.
6.  **Accountability and exception handling:** Shared queues may reduce accountability, leading to increased `reassignment_count` and `handover_reason`="clarification". Exception cases may be mishandled due to lack of local context, causing rework in `Implementation` or `Quality Check`.
7.  **Customer segment fairness:** Regional customer segments may face more handovers and rework due to loss of local accommodation. Filtering by `customer_segment` may reveal higher rework and SLA misses for specific segments, indicating fairness issues.
8.  **Resilience to absence:** Centralized specialists create single points of failure. Absence of one specialist may cause disproportionate delays across all teams. `discover_working_together_network` may show reduced redundancy, and queue wait times may spike during absences.

# 5. Pilot design

### Pilot scope
*   **Pilot team:** East Team (benefits from capacity relief; high current utilization).
*   **Control group:** North Team (stable performance; strong local expertise).
*   **Duration:** 6 weeks pre-observation, 6 weeks pilot, 4 weeks post-observation.
*   **Restructuring elements:** Implement central Technical Shared Service and shared approval queue for East only. North retains current structure.

### Comparison group
*   **East vs. North:** Compare metrics to isolate restructuring effects.
*   **Segmentation:** Analyze by `case_complexity`, `required_specialty`, `customer_segment`, and `priority`.

### Pre/post observation window
*   **Pre:** 6 weeks baseline data for East and North.
*   **Pilot:** 6 weeks implementation for East.
*   **Post:** 4 weeks stabilization and measurement.

### Segmentation
*   **Team:** East (pilot), North (control).
*   **Complexity:** High vs. Low complexity cases.
*   **Specialty:** Legal/Risk vs. Technical cases.
*   **Customer segment:** Regional segments requiring accommodation.

### Guardrail metrics
*   **Queue wait time:** Central queue median wait < 4.5h; 90th percentile < 8.0h.
*   **Reassignment count:** Increase < 10% vs. baseline.
*   **Rework rate:** Local rework increase < 2%; Specialist rework decrease > 10%.
*   **SLA miss rate:** Increase < 1% for East; North stable.
*   **Cycle time:** East 90th percentile cycle time < 20d; North stable.
*   **Handover reason:** "Bypass" or "escalation" < 5% of handovers.

### Staff feedback collection
*   **Surveys:** Weekly surveys on role clarity, workload, and satisfaction.
*   **Handover reason analysis:** Monitor `handover_reason` for qualitative insights.
*   **Focus groups:** Bi-weekly sessions with East and North staff to discuss challenges.

### Rollback or adjustment triggers
*   **Rollback:** If queue wait time > 6.0h for 3 consecutive days, or reassignment count > 20% increase, or SLA miss rate > 10%.
*   **Adjustment:** If local rework rises > 2%, introduce informal tech support channel. If role clarity issues detected, clarify ownership protocols.

# 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation: Pilot first, then conditional rollout.**

The restructuring offers potential benefits for East and specialist utilization, but carries significant risks for North/South, fairness, role clarity, and resilience. A full rollout is premature due to the countervailing risks and the potential for hidden degradation in tail latency and local expertise.

**Conditions for proceeding:**
1.  **Pilot success:** East must show improved cycle time and rework without guardrail breaches. North must remain stable.
2.  **Monitoring:** Continuous monitoring of leading indicators (queue wait, reassignment, handover reasons) and lagging indicators (rework, SLA, fairness).
3.  **Adjustments:** Implement adjustments based on pilot feedback, such as clarifying role ownership, providing informal tech support, or optimizing queue routing.
4.  **Fairness assurance:** Ensure that North/South do not experience degradation; consider hybrid models that retain some local expertise.

**What management should not infer from early averages:**
*   **Average cycle time improvement** does not guarantee tail latency improvement; 90th percentile may worsen.
*   **Specialist utilization increase** does not guarantee efficiency; it may indicate queue buildup or starvation.
*   **Nominal handover reduction** does not guarantee process stability; `reassignment_count` may rise, indicating hidden friction.
*   **Rework reduction in specialist activities** does not guarantee overall quality; rework may shift to local activities.
*   **East improvement** does not guarantee fairness; North/South may suffer, leading to morale and retention issues.

                                                             timestamp_key='time:timestamp',
                                                             clock_unit=pm4py.objects.log.clock.ClockUnit.HOURS)
    team_metrics['team'] = log_df.set_index('case:concept:name')['team']
    team_stats = team_metrics.groupby('team')['duration'].agg(['mean', 'median', 'quantile_90'])
    print("Team Stats:", team_stats)
