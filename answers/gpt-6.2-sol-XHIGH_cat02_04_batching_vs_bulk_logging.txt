# 3. Consequences for performance analysis

The raw comparisons use waiting = local start − upstream arrival, service = local complete − local start, and recording lag = database insertion − local complete. For performance interpretation, assume the clocks are comparable and each selected field represents the milestone being measured. The fragment does not establish that for every source. Treat intervals as half-open when measuring overlap. Summed item elapsed time measures item exposure to the activity, while the union measures logged time with at least one case open under the resource label. Actual utilization requires active time, resource capacity, and an observation or staffed-time denominator. These fragments do not establish a process-wide bottleneck or steady-state throughput.

- **G1.** Mean wait to the fixture cycle is **4.25 min**. Each item occupies the reported cycle for **3 min**, giving 12 item-minutes over 3 fixture-minutes. Using 12 as machine busy time would overstate utilization fourfold. If labels are applied serially inside the cycle, the recorded start can understate item wait to contact and the interval can overstate hands-on service. Count four outputs and one cycle. Model the common start/end and fixture capacity in resource conformance to avoid flagging normal fixture occupancy as a violation.
- **G2.** Mean wait to the group-open marker is **12.25 min**. Test group admission and item completion as distinct transitions. For illustration, continuous serial inspection beginning at 10:00 with immediate completion clicks would yield item service of 8, 6, 7, and 6 minutes. Those durations assume continuous work and immediate clicks. If common setup consumes labor, charge that effort once to the group.

def repeated_sizes(times):
    counts = times.value_counts().loc[lambda counts: counts.ge(2)]
    return ",".join(str(count) for count in sorted(counts, reverse=True)) or "–"


def overlap_counts(group):
    indexed = group[["start", "complete"]].reset_index(drop=True).rename_axis("row")
    pairs = indexed.reset_index().merge(
        indexed.reset_index(), how="cross", suffixes=("_a", "_b")
    )
    pairs = pairs.loc[pairs["row_a"] < pairs["row_b"]]
    later_start = pairs[["start_a", "start_b"]].max(axis=1)
    earlier_end = pairs[["complete_a", "complete_b"]].min(axis=1)
    return int(later_start.lt(earlier_end).sum()), len(pairs)


def logged_occupancy(group):
    boundaries = pd.concat([group["start"], group["complete"]]).drop_duplicates().sort_values()
    peak = 0
    busy = pd.Timedelta(0)
    for left, right in zip(boundaries.iloc[:-1], boundaries.iloc[1:]):
        active = int((group["start"].le(left) & group["complete"].gt(left)).sum())
        peak = max(peak, active)
        if active:
            busy += right - left
    return peak, busy.total_seconds() / 60

# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

**Trusting identity versus allowing shared accounts.** If each human resource label is one exclusive worker and each interval is continuous hands-on work, the peak of four intervals in each of G2, G3, and G5 contradicts that model. G2 and G3 have direct reasons to read their spans as group-open or release-tracked time. Allowing shared accounts weakens worker-level attribution for all human labels, although the evidence of sharing is specific to G5. # 6. Data-collection redesign

1. **Capture execution and logging milestones separately.** Automatically capture per-item and group starts, completions, and pause/resume events at the workstation where feasible. Distinguish ready, released to work, physically started, finished, administratively released, and submitted. Keep independent database insertion timestamps and preserve source event IDs and order through retries or bulk writes.
2. **Calibrate clocks and document timestamp precision.** Use a shared time basis and monitor source offsets and drift, especially across repair, safety, upstream queue, and database systems. Preserve raw source times and offsets alongside normalized times. Record clock domain, capture method, actual storage resolution, display precision, rounding direction, and known uncertainty for each lifecycle field. Mark inherited, backfilled, and manually entered timestamps.
3. **Identify who worked and what capacity was available.** Log badge or assignment identity separately from login, submitter, workstation, fixture, and lane. Record staffed capacity, parallel slots, changes in availability, and downtime reason and scope. Link pauses and resumes to the affected cases or capacity so open time, active person-time, and machine busy time can be measured separately.
4. **Identify operational groups explicitly.** Attach a group, folder, or cycle ID, item membership, admission order, and group setup or release events. Keep the database transaction or bulk-submission ID in a separate field. For the serial stations, capture dispatch decisions and queue snapshots at opening or capacity restoration to distinguish deliberate grouping from backlog draining.

G2’s common group-start marker and G5’s pooling of identities are also well supported. Individual hands-on intervals remain unobserved. G8’s safety explanation remains materially less certain than its serial drain.
