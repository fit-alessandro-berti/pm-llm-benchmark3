## 1. Materiality Framework

A business severity judgment for these alignments should not be derived from raw alignment cost. The cost function supplied is a uniform edit-distance weight: every visible log/model mismatch costs 1, hidden moves cost 0, and no business attribute is considered. That means a missing mandatory fraud screen on a EUR 480,000 high-risk claim can have the same technical cost as a duplicate notification retry or a missing UI telemetry event.

The framework below separates **technical fit**, **policy overlay**, **evidence reliability**, and **business materiality**.

### Severity scale

| Severity | Meaning | Typical examples in this case set |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Informational / harmless technical noise or fitting model behavior; no material control impact. | UI telemetry, hidden fitting transition, duplicate retry with same message ID, label mapping noise. |
| 2 | Low materiality but meaningful model, scope, or evidence gap; limited direct financial exposure. | Denied/withdrawn claims outside payable model, weak notification evidence where payment controls are otherwise sound. |
| 3 | Moderate control or evidence issue; policy violation or missing evidence with compensating factors or moderate exposure. | Unauthorized auto-approval of EUR 18,000 medium-risk claim, external assessment evidence not logged in core. |
| 4 | Serious control failure or high fraud exposure; payment may already be made, but amount or compensating evidence limits severity from becoming critical. | Auto-approval after recent beneficiary-bank change, even if amount is low. |
| 5 | Critical control failure: mandatory pre-payment control missing or bypassed on high exposure, payment irreversible or likely harmful. | Missing fraud screen on EUR 480,000 high-risk claim; payment before required approval on EUR 90,000 claim. |

### Materiality dimensions

1. **Control objective affected**
   - Payment authorization, fraud screening, and payment release are more material than notification timing or UI telemetry.
   - Pay-before-approval is severe because it defeats the purpose of the approval control.

2. **Amount and exposure**
   - High-value claims magnify control failures.
   - Low-value claims can still be severe if fraud indicators exist, for example a recent beneficiary-bank change.

3. **Reversibility**
   - A payment already accepted by the bank is harder to remediate than an approval recorded late but not yet paid.
   - Missing evidence can sometimes be cured retroactively; an irreversible fraudulent payment may not be recoverable.

4. **Timing and sequence**
   - Controls required **before** payment are more material when violated before payment.
   - A late approval after bank acceptance is not merely a logging issue; it may indicate a payment-release control failure.

5. **Customer and regulatory harm**
   - Notification failures may create regulatory or customer-communication risk, even if payment controls are intact.
   - Evidence-retention requirements matter: notification through portal/CRM is acceptable only if reliable linked evidence exists.

6. **Recurrence**
   - A one-off label mismatch is less severe than a recurring connector retry or repeated unauthorized auto-approval.
   - Monthly pattern evidence raises the priority of seemingly low-cost cases.

7. **Evidence reliability**
   - A missing event is not proof that an activity did not occur. It may indicate a logging gap, integration failure, external process, or weak linkage.
   - Mutable text fields are weaker evidence than immutable system logs, stable document IDs, or signed reports.

8. **Model scope**
   - If the model was designed only for payable claims, denied and withdrawn cases may produce high alignment cost without any operational failure.
   - Scope disputes should be resolved before treating such cases as deviations.

### Confidence versus severity

- **Severity** asks: *If the interpretation is correct, how bad is the business/control impact?*
- **Confidence** asks: *How reliable is the evidence supporting that interpretation?*

A case can be high severity but medium confidence, for example a missing fraud-screen event where the activity may have occurred outside the logged system. Conversely, a case can be high confidence but low severity, for example a clearly identified duplicate notification retry with the same message ID.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Alignment cost is a technical measure of edit distance under a chosen cost function. In this case set, all visible log/model moves were weighted equally at 1, hidden moves were weighted 0, and no data attributes were considered. That makes raw cost useful for diagnosing model fit, but insufficient for business risk prioritization.

1. **Cost-function design ignores business weighting**  
   CL-602 has raw cost 1 because only `Fraud Screen` is missing. CL-601 has raw cost 3 because of three UI telemetry events. Technically, CL-601 appears worse than CL-602. Businessly, CL-602 is potentially critical because a EUR 480,000 HIGH-risk claim with a recent bank change lacks fraud-screening evidence, while CL-601 is harmless.

2. **Cost cannot see data-aware policy conditions**  
   CL-609 and CL-610 have raw cost 0 because the control-flow path is fully synchronous. However, CL-609 violates P1 because EUR 18,000 and MEDIUM risk are not eligible for Auto Approve. CL-610 violates both P1 and P2 because a beneficiary-bank change occurred one hour before payment. These are zero-cost policy failures. Alignment cost alone would completely miss them.

3. **Hidden moves can be fitting and harmless**  
   CL-605 contains `T(tau,c=0)`, a hidden routing transition. It contributes zero cost and represents no business deviation. It is a model implementation detail. Treating hidden moves as suspicious by default would create false positives; treating them as automatically safe is also wrong if they hide meaningful control logic. Here, the hidden move is fitting and immaterial.

4. **Scope mismatch can create high cost without operational failure**  
   CL-607 and CL-612 have raw costs of 4 because the payable-claim model expects assessment, approval, payment, and notification after registration/validation. But CL-607 was denied and CL-612 was withdrawn. No payment was intended. The high cost reflects model scope, not financial loss or customer harm. These cases show that raw cost can measure the distance between reality and an incomplete model, not the severity of reality.

5. **Duplicated telemetry can create cost without business harm**  
   CL-603 has raw cost 1 because of a duplicate `Notify Claimant` event. The duplicate shares the same message ID and transport logs show a retry. This is technical noise, not a second business notification. The cost is informative because it identifies a connector issue, but it is not material customer harm.

6. **Missing events may reflect external evidence or logging gaps**  
   CL-606 has raw cost 1 because `Assess Loss` is missing from the core log, but an external signed adjuster report exists. CL-608 has raw cost 1 because core notification is missing, but a portal/CRM message exists. In both cases, the alignment cost understates the need to evaluate evidence reliability, but it also should not be treated as proof that the activity did not occur.

7. **Irreversibility and timing are not captured by edit distance**  
   CL-604 has raw cost 3, but the bank accepted payment before manual approval was recorded. That timing makes the case severe because the payment may be irreversible and the approval control was defeated. The alignment cost counts sequence repairs but does not capture the financial and control consequence of paying first.

8. **Activity granularity and label mismatch can inflate cost**  
   CL-611 has raw cost 2 because the log says `Assess Damage` while the model says `Assess Loss`. The substantive control appears to have occurred. The cost reflects naming/granularity mismatch, not a missing approval or payment failure.

In short: raw alignment cost answers “how far is the log from the model under this cost function?” It does not answer “how much money is at risk?”, “was a mandatory control bypassed?”, “is the payment reversible?”, “is the evidence reliable?”, or “is the case even within model scope?”
